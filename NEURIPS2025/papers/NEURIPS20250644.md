## Problem

RM+ variants are the backbone of superhuman Poker AIs, yet their last-iterate convergence in learning Nash equilibria has only been established for games satisfying strong monotonicity or the Minty Variational Inequality (MVI). No prior work demonstrates last-iterate convergence for these algorithms in the broader class of games satisfying the weak MVI, which covers important applications such as GANs. The fundamental obstacle is that RM+ variants operate on a transformed loss feedback that fails to satisfy the weak MVI, even when the original game's loss gradient does.

## Input / Output

Each player i receives the loss gradient ℓ_i^t induced by the current strategy profile x^t and maintains an internal decision vector θ_i^t in R_{≥1}^{|A_i|}, a truncated positive orthant that is a subset of the cone of the simplex strategy space. The algorithm produces an updated strategy x_i^{t+1} = θ_i^{t+1}/‖θ_i^{t+1}‖_1 on the simplex, and convergence is measured via the tangent residual r^tan(x^t), which upper-bounds the duality gap and vanishes exactly at Nash equilibria. The key unusual aspect is that the algorithm consumes a transformed feedback F_i(θ^t) = ⟨ℓ_i^t, x_i^t⟩·1 − ℓ_i^t rather than the raw loss gradient, which is precisely what breaks the weak MVI structure.

## Method

The paper introduces a two-phase proof paradigm: (i) transforming a smooth RM+ variant into an equivalent OMD instance that updates within the original simplex strategy space, thereby recovering the weak MVI (since the OMD feedback is the original loss gradient), and (ii) showing last-iterate convergence by proving the distance between accumulated regrets converges to zero via the recovered weak MVI. This equivalence relies on the fact that both projection onto the truncated positive orthant and projection onto the simplex admit a unified closed-form via the max operator. Building on this paradigm, the authors propose SOGRM+, a new smooth RM+ variant whose second update step uses a momentum-like correction (θ_i^{t+1} = θ_i^{t+1/2} − ηF_i(θ^{t−1/2}) + ηF_i(θ^{t+1/2})) instead of a second prox-mapping, performing only one prox-mapping per iteration. This structural difference is critical: it enables the normal-cone condition −ℓ^{t+1/2} + (θ^t − θ^{t+1})/η ∈ N_X(x^{t+1/2}) to hold, which is the key step that allows the weak MVI to be directly applied. SOGRM+ achieves O(1/√t) best-iterate convergence and asymptotic last-iterate convergence in games satisfying the weak MVI, the weakest condition among all known RM+ variants.

## Results

The paper establishes that SExRM+ and SPRM+ achieve asymptotic last-iterate convergence (r^tan(x^{t+1/2}) → 0) and O(1/√t) finite-time best-iterate convergence in games satisfying the MVI, extending prior results that were limited to two-player zero-sum matrix games. The proposed SOGRM+ is the first RM+ variant to achieve both last-iterate and O(1/√t) best-iterate convergence in the broader class of games satisfying the weak MVI, requiring only ρ > −1/(12√3·D·L_u). Empirically, SOGRM+ significantly outperforms other algorithms in learning Nash equilibria, as reported in the experiments.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to Meng et al. [2023], which establishes last-iterate convergence for RM+ only under strong monotonicity (the strongest assumption), this paper extends convergence guarantees for SExRM+ and SPRM+ to the much broader class of games satisfying the MVI, and introduces SOGRM+ which further extends to the weak MVI. Compared to Cai et al. [2025], which proves last-iterate convergence for SExRM+ and SPRM+ under the MVI but restricts finite-time best-iterate convergence to two-player zero-sum matrix games, this paper provides finite-time O(1/√t) best-iterate convergence for SExRM+ and SPRM+ under the full MVI condition, and is the first to establish any last-iterate or best-iterate convergence for RM+ variants under the weak MVI. The key methodological difference is that this paper's proof paradigm recovers the weak MVI by transforming RM+ variants into OMD instances in the original strategy space, whereas Cai et al. [2025] analyze limit-point dynamics or rely on the duality gap definition specific to zero-sum games. SOGRM+ achieves the weakest convergence condition (weak MVI) among all known RM+ variants, at the cost of requiring a larger minimum ρ (−1/(12√3·D·L_u)) compared to OMD-based methods (e.g., −1/(12√3·L) for Cai and Zheng [2022]).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the convergence analysis of smooth Regret Matching+ (RM+) variants from games satisfying the Minty Variational Inequality (MVI) to the strictly broader class of games satisfying the weak MVI. It builds on the Blackwell approachability framework of Farina et al. [2021, 2023] and the last-iterate convergence results of Meng et al. [2023] (strong monotonicity) and Cai et al. [2025] (MVI), but departs from both by introducing a proof paradigm that transforms RM+ variants into OMD instances in the original strategy space, thereby recovering the weak MVI that the RM+ feedback itself does not satisfy. The proposed SOGRM+ algorithm is inspired by this paradigm and differs structurally from SExRM+ and SPRM+ by performing only one prox-mapping per iteration rather than two, which is the key enabler for weak MVI convergence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first last-iterate convergence guarantee for RM+ variants under the weak MVI, closing a gap that had persisted since the weak MVI was introduced as a strictly weaker condition than the MVI. The proof paradigm is reusable and yields finite-time best-iterate convergence rates as a byproduct, which in turn extends the linear last-iterate convergence of restarting variants from two-player zero-sum matrix games to all games satisfying monotonicity and metric subregularity. Key limitations include the absence of convergence proofs for extensive-form games (where RM+ variants are most practically deployed via CFR) and the fact that SOGRM+ requires a less negative minimum ρ than OMD-based alternatives, restricting its applicability to a narrower subset of weak MVI games.
