## Problem

Existing music- and dance-driven camera movement synthesis methods operate exclusively in an offline paradigm, requiring the entire dance video before generating any camera trajectory. This makes them impractical for live stage performances where camera movements must be produced frame-by-frame using only the current and preceding segments of the performance. The paper introduces the more practical and challenging task of online camera movement synthesis and proposes a unified framework that handles both online and offline settings.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250446/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 3D dance motion (global positions of 60 human joints per frame) and 35-dimensional music features extracted via Librosa following the FACT pipeline. In the online setting, only the current and preceding frames of both modalities are available at each time step. The output is a per-frame 8-dimensional camera parameter vector in MMD polar-coordinate format: reference-point position (3D), camera rotation (3D), camera-to-reference distance (1D), and field of view (1D). During online inference, only a single [MASK] token at the current time step is predicted, yielding one camera frame per forward pass without iterative denoising.

## Method

TemMEGA replaces diffusion-based offline generation with a three-component masked-token pipeline. A Discrete Camera Tokenizer (VQ-VAE with 2048 codes of 32 dimensions and spatial downscaling factor 4) quantizes raw camera trajectories into discrete tokens. A Consecutive Memory Encoder splits the available history into a long-term window (256 frames) compressed via weight-shared transformer decoder blocks with 8 learnable query tokens per segment, and a short-term window (32 frames) enhanced by a causal decoder that attends to the compressed long-term representation. A bidirectional Conditional Masked Transformer then predicts the masked current-frame token, injecting long-term memory through AdaIN (capturing macro-level style) and short-term memory through cross-attention (preserving temporal alignment). Because only the single current-frame token is masked at inference, the model produces one camera frame in a single forward pass, eliminating the multi-step sampling required by diffusion baselines.

## Results

On the DCM dataset in the offline setting, TemMEGA achieves the best quality scores: FID_k = 3.237 and FID_s = 0.255, outperforming DanceCamera3D (3.749 / 0.280) and DanceCamAnimator (3.453 / 0.268). It also attains the lowest dancer-fidelity errors (DMR = 0.0020, LCD = 0.141), indicating the fewest frames where the dancer is missing from view. In the online (real-time) setting, TemMEGA reduces FID_k from 4.634 to 4.025 and DMR from 0.0066 to 0.0035 compared to DanceCamera3D retrained for online use. In the user study, TemMEGA is preferred over DanceCamera3D 73.64% of the time (offline) and 83.43% (online).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DanceCamera3D [37], the closest competitor, TemMEGA improves FID_k by 0.512 (3.237 vs 3.749) and FID_s by 0.025, while also reducing DMR (0.0020 vs 0.0025) and LCD (0.141 vs 0.147); however, DanceCamera3D retains a slight edge on kinetic diversity (Dist_k 1.631 vs 1.961). Against DanceCamAnimator [38], TemMEGA wins on quality (FID_k 3.237 vs 3.453) and dancer fidelity (DMR 0.0020 vs 0.0022, LCD 0.141 vs 0.152), though DanceCamAnimator achieves higher kinetic diversity (Dist_k 3.140 vs 1.961), reflecting its keyframe-based design that produces more varied shot transitions. Against the older baselines DanceRevolution [17] and FACT [21], TemMEGA dominates across all metrics by large margins (e.g., FID_k 3.237 vs 10.267 and 5.205; DMR 0.0020 vs 0.0062 and 0.0899). In the user study, TemMEGA is preferred over DanceCamera3D 73.64% of the time and over DanceCamAnimator 65.64%, confirming perceptual superiority in highlighting dance and music.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the dance-camera-music synthesis line initiated by DanceCamera3D (which introduced the DCM dataset and a diffusion-based offline approach) and DanceCamAnimator (which added keyframe-based animator knowledge), by reframing the problem in an online/real-time setting where only past and current segments are available. Methodologically, it departs from the diffusion paradigm used by DanceCamera3D and instead adopts a masked generative modeling strategy inspired by MAGE, combined with a VQ-VAE-based discrete camera tokenizer and a consecutive memory encoder borrowed from online action understanding literature. The Consecutive Memory Encoder's long/short-term memory decomposition and the AdaIN + cross-attention fusion strategy in the conditional masked transformer are the main architectural novelties relative to prior camera synthesis work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The online formulation is practically significant for live stage performances where the full dance video is unavailable, and the masked generative approach is inherently more amenable to real-time inference than iterative diffusion. However, the evaluation is limited to a single small dataset (108 pieces), no code is released, and no statistical significance testing is performed, leaving open questions about robustness and generalizability. Future work could explore longer sequences, other performance genres, and whether the memory architecture scales gracefully as the temporal context grows beyond the tested 256+32 frame window.
