## Problem

Current function-based protein design models can generate sequences aligned with textual descriptions but produce proteins that frequently fail to fold into stable 3D structures. The paper asks whether incorporating fragments from natural proteins—motivated by classical design methods that leverage known structures—can improve foldability in generative models, and shows empirically that even random fragment incorporation substantially enhances structural plausibility.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250672/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts a free-form textual function description (or a set of function keywords) as input, which is encoded by a frozen GPT-2 text encoder and projected into the protein embedding space as a prefix conditioning context. At inference, the top-K most relevant descriptions are retrieved from a supporting document set via PubMedBERT embeddings, and InterPro is used to extract protein fragments from those sequences, forming a dynamic vocabulary of candidate fragments. The output is a novel protein sequence composed autoregressively of both standard amino acid tokens (via BPE) and multi-residue protein fragments, where fragments are continuous subsequences drawn from natural proteins.

## Method

PRODVA augments a ProtGPT2-based autoregressive decoder with a dynamic protein vocabulary by concatenating a fixed amino-acid token embedding matrix with a learned fragment embedding matrix, so the prediction head computes a joint softmax over both tokens and fragments at each step. A Fragment Encoder (also ProtGPT2-initialized) maps variable-length fragments into the PLM's hidden space, and a TextLM (GPT-2) encodes the functional description as a prefix. Training combines three objectives: standard next-token/fragment negative log-likelihood, a weighted cross-entropy type-classification loss on fragment categories (Domain, Active Site, etc.), and an InfoNCE contrastive loss aligning fragment representations with their InterPro text descriptions. During inference, the two auxiliary MLP heads are discarded, and a retrieval step dynamically selects which fragments enter the vocabulary based on the input description, enabling the model to assemble biologically meaningful sub-sequences rather than generating every residue independently.

## Results

On the Mol-Instructions protein design task, PRODVA achieves a pLDDT of 76.86 (76.35% of proteins above the well-folded threshold of 70) and PAE of 8.66 (68.06% below the threshold of 10), outperforming the state-of-the-art Pinal (pLDDT 75.25, 68.97% >70; PAE 10.96, 58.44% <10) while using only 0.04% of Pinal's training data (712K vs. 1.76B text-protein pairs). On language alignment, PRODVA scores 17.40 on ProTrek and 51.10 on EvoLlama, within 0.1% and 2.32% of Pinal respectively. On the CAMEO subset (function keywords), PRODVA achieves pLDDT 75.88 and PAE 6.39, surpassing Pinal by 9.38% and 8.18% on these foldability metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the state-of-the-art Pinal (1.76B training pairs), PRODVA (712K pairs, 0.04% of Pinal's data) wins decisively on foldability: 76.35% vs. 68.97% of proteins with pLDDT > 70 (+7.38%) and 68.06% vs. 58.44% with PAE < 10 (+9.62%), while matching Pinal on language alignment within 0.1% (ProTrek) and 2.32% (EvoLlama). Against ProteinDT and PAAG (even after fine-tuning on the same 712K data), PRODVA achieves substantially higher pLDDT (76.86 vs. 51.42 and 50.37) and PAE (8.66 vs. 18.57 and 19.96), with ProTrek scores of 17.40 vs. 13.89 and 10.04. Chroma (45K pairs) produces moderately folded proteins (pLDDT 59.18) but fails on language alignment (ProTrek 2.10, EvoLlama 40.10), likely due to its much smaller training set. On the CAMEO subset (function keywords), PRODVA outperforms Pinal by 9.38% in pLDDT and 8.18% in PAE while using only 0.02% of Pinal's data, and surpasses ESM3 by 45.51% and 66.48% in the proportion of well-folded proteins. The random fragment-incorporation baseline Random+(E) already achieves pLDDT 64.47, confirming that even unlearned fragment insertion substantially improves foldability over pure random generation (pLDDT ~25).

## Relation to Prior Work

PRODVA adapts the dynamic vocabulary paradigm from NLP (where retrieved phrases augment a fixed token set) to protein design, but replaces token-level heuristics with biologically meaningful fragments identified via InterPro, each carrying functional type and description annotations. The autoregressive backbone (ProtGPT2) and text encoder (GPT-2) are standard components; the novelty lies in the joint token/fragment prediction objective, the retrieval-augmented fragment candidate construction at inference, and the auxiliary type-classification and InfoNCE description-alignment losses. Conceptually, the paper bridges classical structure-informed protein design (rational design, directed evolution) with modern generative models by showing that even random fragment incorporation improves foldability, then learning to select fragments based on textual function descriptions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical contribution is data efficiency: PRODVA matches Pinal's language alignment (within 0.1% on ProTrek) while using less than 0.04% of its training pairs, and substantially outperforms it in foldability (7.38% more pLDDT>70, 9.62% more PAE<10). This suggests that structural priors from natural protein fragments are a highly information-dense signal that can compensate for limited text-protein supervision. However, all evaluation is computational (oracle-model-based alignment, ESMFold-predicted structures); no wet-lab validation is performed, leaving open whether the improved pLDDT/PAE translates to actual functional proteins. A broader open question is whether the dynamic-vocabulary-with-biological-fragments paradigm generalizes to other biomolecular design tasks (e.g., nucleic acids, small molecules) or to structure-conditioned design.
