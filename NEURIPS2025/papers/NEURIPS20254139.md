## Problem

When GRPO is applied to improve general Chain-of-Thought reasoning in multimodal LLMs, it suffers from two critical limitations: (1) limited policy exploration, where temperature-sampled rollouts converge to near-identical outputs, and (2) missing process supervision, where rule-based rewards only evaluate the final answer, leaving intermediate reasoning steps unsupervised. These issues cause the model to learn shortcuts and visual hallucinations that fail to generalize beyond the training distribution.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image paired with a text question (VQA format) and produces a structured CoT reasoning response enclosed in <think> tags. During rollout collection, the input image is perturbed via a diffusion-style forward process at a randomly sampled timestep, creating a spectrum from clean to near-pure-noise images; however, clean inputs are always used during policy updates. For open-ended VQA answers (the majority of the 13k training samples), correctness is scored via SBERT semantic similarity with a threshold τ=0.6, rather than exact string matching.

## Method

NoisyGRPO introduces two components on top of GRPO. First, a noise-injected exploration policy perturbs each rollout's input image using a diffusion forward process at a uniformly sampled timestep n_i ∈ [0,1], producing diverse visual conditions that maintain high reward variance across the group and implicitly favor visually grounded reasoning. Second, a Bayesian advantage estimation framework treats the noise-derived prior (r_i^n = 1 − n_i) and the semantic trajectory reward (r_i^s) as two Gaussian signals, fusing them via a posterior mean where the prior variance σ_n² is inversely proportional to the group-wise standard deviation of semantic rewards (modulated by hyperparameter γ) and the observation variance σ_s² is fixed to a small constant α. This adaptive weighting means that when all rollouts receive similar rewards (low variance), the noise prior dominates the advantage signal, while when rewards are highly variable, the correctness observation takes greater weight. The final advantage is the group-normalized posterior reward, plugged into the standard clipped PPO objective with a KL penalty.

## Results

On MMStar (general capability), NoisyGRPO achieves 58.2 average with Qwen2.5-VL 3B (+3.7 over GRPO's 54.5, +2.8 over the base model) and 65.8 with Qwen2.5-VL 7B (+3.2 over GRPO's 62.6, +2.6 over base). On MME-CoT (CoT quality), NoisyGRPO-3B reaches 40.6 F1 versus GRPO's 36.2 (+4.4), and NoisyGRPO-7B reaches 47.7 F1 versus GRPO's 46.8. On hallucination (AMBER), NoisyGRPO-7B reduces hallucination rate from 28.9 (GRPO) to 23.9 and cognitive hallucination from 1.7 to 1.2, while on MME-RealWorld-Lite it improves accuracy from 40.8 to 44.0 (3B) and 42.3 to 44.6 (7B).

## Baselines & Comparisons

NoisyGRPO consistently outperforms vanilla GRPO across all four benchmarks for both model scales: +3.7 MMStar avg (3B) and +3.2 (7B), +4.4 MME-CoT F1 (3B), and substantial AMBER hallucination reductions (e.g., 28.9→23.9 for 7B). SFT on the same 13k MM-RLHF data yields only marginal gains (+0.3 MMStar avg for 3B) and actually degrades GRPO's performance, confirming that the improvements come from the RL framework rather than data quality. Against larger reference models, NoisyGRPO-7B (65.8 MMStar avg) surpasses GPT-4V (57.1) and LLaVA-Next-34B (52.1), while NoisyGRPO-3B (58.2) also exceeds GPT-4V. The one consistent weakness is Coarse Perception on MMStar, where NoisyGRPO underperforms the base model (69.5 vs 70.3 for 3B), attributed to noise being insufficient to perturb coarse-grained visual cues. On MME-CoT robustness, NoisyGRPO-7B (7.8 stability) exceeds proprietary models GPT-4o (−1.0) and Kimi K1.5 (2.9), though it trails GRPO-7B on CoT efficiency (59.7 vs 66.4 relative rate), reflecting a design trade-off favoring grounded reasoning over brevity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends GRPO (DeepSeekMath) from text-only LLMs to multimodal CoT reasoning by addressing two specific failure modes: limited policy exploration (rollout collapse noted in DAPO) and missing process supervision. It builds on the VLM-R1 training framework for multimodal RL but departs from prior multimodal GRPO applications (VLM-R1, LMM-R1, MM-Eureka) that target domain-specific tasks with task-specific rewards, instead proposing a general-purpose framework for open-ended CoT reasoning. The noise-injected exploration draws on diffusion forward processes for structured perturbation, while the Bayesian advantage estimation is a novel formulation that treats the off-policy distribution shift from noise as a prior to be fused with reward-based likelihood—a conceptual departure from the purely group-normalized advantage in vanilla GRPO.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and addresses a practical gap: GRPO's training reward can increase while out-of-distribution evaluation performance stagnates or degrades, limiting the real-world utility of RL-trained MLLMs. The Bayesian advantage estimation provides a principled mechanism to handle the off-policy bias introduced by exploration noise, and the finding that the method yields the largest gains on small-scale models (3B) is practically relevant for resource-constrained deployment. However, the approach relies on the assumption that noise magnitude correlates with reasoning difficulty, which the authors acknowledge breaks down for coarse-grained perception tasks, and the empirical gains, while consistent, are moderate (+3–4 points on key benchmarks). Open questions include whether the Gaussian residual assumption generalizes to other reward structures and whether the method scales effectively to larger model families.
