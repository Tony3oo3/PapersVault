## Problem

Monte Carlo Tree Diffusion (MCTD) achieves strong long-horizon planning but is fundamentally bounded by the trajectory lengths seen during training. Existing periodic replanning strategies extend plans by sequentially generating new segments, yet they remain myopic—each decision is made with only local horizon information, leading to dead-end states and suboptimal paths. The paper asks how to enable globally-aware plan composition that substantially exceeds training trajectory lengths while avoiding these myopic pitfalls.

## Input / Output

The planner takes a start state and goal state (or 64×64 RGB observations for visual maze tasks, encoded to 8-dim VAE latents) and produces a complete state-action trajectory [s₀;a₀, s₁;a₁, …, s_T;a_T] connecting them. The critical constraint is that the output plan can be up to 10× longer than any trajectory in the training data (e.g., 1000-step plans from 100-step training sequences), achieved purely at inference time without retraining. For visual tasks, a pre-trained inverse dynamics model converts latent-state transitions into actions, and a position estimator provides weak positional guidance for the POMDP setting.

## Method

C-MCTD elevates tree search from the subplan level (within a single diffusion-generated trajectory) to the plan level (across multiple stitched trajectories), so each tree node represents an entire generated plan rather than a partially denoised segment. The Online Composer performs this via stitching-based expansion (connecting terminal states of parent plans to new plan starts), guidance sets as meta-actions (letting the search adaptively select from multiple guidance strengths per node), and fast replanning with jumpy DDIM denoising for efficient simulation. The Distributed Composer parallelizes tree growth from k-means cluster centroids with strategic connections only when a plan reaches another tree's origin, reducing exponential single-tree depth. The Preplan Composer amortizes cost by pre-building a reusable plan graph offline (using position-specific guidance between waypoint pairs), then at inference time only generating short local connections from start/goal to waypoints and applying Dijkstra/A* shortest-path synthesis on the augmented graph.

## Results

On the long-horizon maze navigation benchmark (OGBench), Preplan Composer achieves 100% success on PointMaze-Giant (plans ~10× longer than training trajectories), significantly outperforming the strongest baseline CompDiffuser (68%) and all other methods (0%). On AntMaze-Giant, Preplan Composer reaches 75% success versus 65% for CompDiffuser and 0% for all other baselines. In multi-cube manipulation, Online Composer with Plan Cache achieves 82% on quadruple-cube tasks versus 24% for MCTD-Replan. Preplan Composer also delivers the best efficiency, reducing planning time to 36.9s on PointMaze-Giant versus 269.8s for Online Composer.

## Baselines & Comparisons

C-MCTD substantially outperforms all five baselines on the most challenging settings. Against CompDiffuser (Luo et al., 2025), the strongest baseline, Preplan Composer achieves 100% vs. 68% on PointMaze-Giant and 75% vs. 65% on AntMaze-Giant, while matching CompDiffuser on medium/large mazes. Against MCTD (Yoon et al., 2025), the direct predecessor, C-MCTD variants improve from 0% to 100% on PointMaze-Giant and from 0% to 75% on AntMaze-Giant, demonstrating that plan-level search overcomes the myopic limitations of subplan-level search. Against Diffusion Forcing (Chen et al., 2024) and Diffuser (Janner et al., 2022), C-MCTD achieves large margins (e.g., 100% vs. 20% on PointMaze-Large for DF, 100% vs. 40% for Diffuser-Replan). Against SSD (Kim et al., 2024), C-MCTD dominates on large and giant mazes (100% vs. 40% on PointMaze-Large, 100% vs. 0% on Giant). In manipulation tasks, Online Composer with Plan Cache reaches 82% on quadruple-cube tasks versus 24% for MCTD-Replan, confirming the advantage of compositional plan-level search for sequential manipulation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends MCTD (Yoon et al., ICML 2025), which combines Monte Carlo Tree Search with diffusion denoising at the subplan level, by elevating the tree search to operate over entire plan compositions rather than within a single trajectory. It builds on Diffusion Forcing (Chen et al., NeurIPS 2024) for semi-autoregressive generation with causal noise schedules and on Diffuser (Janner et al., ICML 2022) for classifier-guided sampling. The key conceptual departure from prior stitching approaches (DatasetStitch, CompDiffuser, SSD) is that C-MCTD is entirely inference-time: it composes plans via tree search without any retraining or data augmentation, treating plan concatenation as the unit of exploration rather than modifying the training distribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

C-MCTD addresses a fundamental practical limitation of diffusion planners—the inability to generate plans longer than training trajectories—without requiring retraining, which is significant for deployment in environments where longer horizons emerge post-training. The Preplan Composer's 100% success on PointMaze-Giant (10× training length) where most baselines achieve 0% demonstrates that compositional inference-time search can close a substantial gap. However, the approach remains computationally expensive (tens to hundreds of seconds per plan), does not achieve real-time inference, and the parallel/graph variants (DC, PC) degrade in high-dimensional visual POMDP settings where waypoint clustering becomes unreliable. Open questions include whether the compositional search paradigm can handle stochastic dynamics and generalize to entirely novel environments without kinematically invalid plans.
