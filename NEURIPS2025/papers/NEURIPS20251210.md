## Problem

Safety validation for autonomous vehicle motion planning lacks a systematic framework that jointly inspects both input distributions and output uncertainty. Existing OOD detection methods from image classification fail on motion planning's complex, one-to-many mapping, while current output validation approaches are designed for open-loop trajectory prediction and cannot capture closed-loop risks like collisions and road departures. This leaves two unsafe modes—InD scenarios with uncertain decisions and OOD scenarios with overconfident decisions—entirely unaddressed.

## Input / Output

REDOUBT operates on the latent scene encoding (ego state, dynamic agents, static objects, map features) produced by any motion planner's scene encoder, along with N_m candidate ego trajectories and their associated selection probabilities. It outputs a continuous OOD score (log-likelihood from flow matching), per-trajectory NFC and DAC violation probabilities, an imitation error estimate, and a final four-way safety categorization (InD+Certain → Safe; all other combinations → Unsafe). The framework is planner-agnostic and orthogonal, requiring only access to the latent representation and candidate trajectory set without modifying the planner itself.

## Method

REDOUBT combines two specialized modules. The OOD Detection module trains a neural velocity field via flow matching in the planner's latent space, using a Gaussian Fourier Projection to embed temporal dynamics and a GMM prior with N_m components to model the multi-modal trajectory distribution; log-likelihoods are computed by solving the flow ODE backward with an Euler solver and the Instantaneous Change of Variables formula. The Decision Uncertainty Estimation module is a multi-output network trained with Binary Cross-Entropy against geometry-driven Violation Energy Scores (exponential penalty for NFC, linear for DAC) computed from Euclidean Signed Distance Fields, eliminating the need for explicit violation labels. At inference, both module scores are ensembled with a trajectory probability energy E_p via a tunable weight λ, and the combined OOD and uncertainty signals jointly classify each scenario into one of four safety categories, uniquely flagging the two previously overlooked unsafe modes.

## Results

On the nuPlan dataset, REDOUBT achieves an average OOD detection AUROC of 85.16% (OP), 83.00% (CNR), and 82.05% (CR), with corresponding FPR95 of 55.08%, 59.67%, and 61.48%, outperforming the best baseline (KNN) by 9–10 AUROC points across all modes. For closed-loop decision uncertainty estimation, REDOUBT improves average NFC AUROC by 12.81% (CNR) and 10.03% (CR) over the Energy baseline. In the full duo safety validation under closed-loop reactive mode, REDOUBT attains 90.53% recall on potential unsafe scenarios, substantially exceeding IGMM (55.29%) and Energy (65.73%) used individually.

## Baselines & Comparisons

REDOUBT outperforms all seven OOD detection baselines (MSP, MaxLogit, Entropy, Energy, MDS, KNN, IGMM) across all three evaluation modes and all four planners, with the largest gaps over post-hoc methods (MSP, MaxLogit, Entropy) which suffer from overconfident predictions in the one-to-many motion planning setting. Among feature-space methods, KNN is the strongest baseline (AUROC 75.71 OP, 75.05 CNR, 75.66 CR), yet REDOUBT still exceeds it by 9.45, 7.95, and 6.39 AUROC points respectively. For closed-loop decision uncertainty (NFC/DAC), REDOUBT beats the Energy score baseline by 12.81 AUROC points on NFC-CNR and 10.03 on NFC-CR, confirming that the geometry-driven Violation Energy Score with trajectory probability ensemble captures closed-loop risks that open-loop surrogate metrics miss. In the open-loop ADE retention curves, REDOUBT's R-AUC (e.g., 0.263 for PlanTF) is closer to the oracle (0.239) than the best baselines (Model Averaging, VAR) at 0.282. In the full duo validation, combining OOD detection with uncertainty estimation yields 90.53% recall versus 55.29% (IGMM alone) and 65.73% (Energy alone), demonstrating that the two modules are complementary rather than interchangeable.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes flow matching (Lipman et al., 2022), originally a generative modeling paradigm, as a density estimation tool for OOD detection in the latent space of motion planners, and borrows energy-based scoring (Hofmann et al., 2024) to encode trajectory-probability confidence. It departs from prior OOD detection work (MSP, Mahalanobis, KNN) that targets classification tasks and from uncertainty estimation work (Shifts, VAR, DECODE) that operates in open-loop trajectory prediction, arguing that neither transfers to the one-to-many, closed-loop nature of motion planning. The key conceptual departure is the 'duo' framing: jointly inspecting input distribution (OOD) and output uncertainty (violation risk) to expose two unsafe modes—InD+Uncertain and OOD+Certain—that single-axis validation misses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

REDOUBT is positioned as the first systematic safety-validation framework for motion planning (as opposed to trajectory prediction), and its model-agnostic design across four planners (PlanTF, PLUTO, GameFormer, PlanScope) suggests broad applicability to existing AV stacks. The 90.5% recall for the duo system versus ~55–66% for single-axis baselines is a large practical gap, but it is partly explained by the union of two detection signals rather than a fundamental algorithmic breakthrough. Open questions include real-world deployment latency (flow-matching ODE solving at inference), the sensitivity/specificity trade-off when the system flags more scenarios as unsafe, and whether the GMM prior and GFP components generalize beyond the nuPlan scenario taxonomy.
