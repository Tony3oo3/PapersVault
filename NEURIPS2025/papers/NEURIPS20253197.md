## Problem

Existing vision-language-action (VLA) models rely on pre-trained vision-language *understanding* models as backbones, which limits their ability to generalize to unseen tasks, novel objects, and unfamiliar environments. This paper asks whether large video *generation* models—which have already demonstrated strong physical plausibility and generalization when conditioned on novel inputs—can be directly adapted into generalizable robotic manipulators.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253197/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a natural language instruction and a single current visual observation (image) as input. It jointly outputs an action chunk of K 7-D vectors (3 wrist rotation, 3 wrist translation, 1 binary gripper state) and a future video clip of N frames predicted in latent space via a 3D-causal VAE. The first video latent encodes the current observation and serves as the conditioning frame; during inference only this single frame is encoded, while during training the full video clip is encoded to obtain both the observation latent and future frame latents. Only the first 3 of the 6 predicted actions are executed per control step.

## Method

VideoVLA transforms the pre-trained CogVideoX-5B Video Diffusion Transformer into a Video-Action Diffusion Transformer by appending the 7-D action sequence as an additional output modality and jointly denoising both video latents and actions with a DDPM diffusion loss. Language is encoded by T5 into 226 tokens, video by a 3D-causal VAE into spatial latents, and actions are used as raw vectors without tokenization; all modalities are projected to a common embedding dimension and concatenated into a single sequence processed by a stack of DiT self-attention blocks with adaptive LayerNorm for noise-timestep conditioning. A key design choice is bidirectional attention between video and action tokens (rather than causal masking), which the ablation shows improves both action accuracy and video coherence. The backbone is initialized from CogVideoX-5B pre-trained on massive real-world video, then fine-tuned on the Open X-Embodiment dataset (22.5M frames) and optionally on a small real-world dataset. The dual-prediction strategy—simultaneously forecasting actions and their visual consequences—creates a strong correlation between imagined futures and actual execution, which the paper identifies as the mechanism enabling generalization to novel objects and cross-embodiment skill transfer.

## Results

On the SIMPLER in-domain benchmark (Table 1), VideoVLA achieves the highest overall average success rate of 63.0% across 12 tasks on both WidowX and Google robots, edging out CogACT (62.6%) and substantially outperforming π₀ (50.0%) and OpenVLA (26.0%). On generalization to 10 novel objects (Table 2), VideoVLA reaches 65.2% average success, surpassing the next-best SpatialVLA (50.8%) by 14.4 points. For cross-embodiment skill transfer (Table 3), VideoVLA achieves 48.6% average, more than doubling CogACT's 20.4%. In real-world experiments (Table 4), VideoVLA attains 64.6% overall task success, leading CogACT (58.4%) and π₀ (50.7%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

VideoVLA outperforms all six baselines on the overall in-domain average (63.0% vs. CogACT 62.6%, π₀ 50.0%, SpatialVLA 47.1%, OpenVLA 26.0%, RT-1-X 24.8%, Octo 9.9%). The closest competitor is CogACT, which leads on the Google-robot VM average (75.2% vs. 73.1%) but trails VideoVLA on WidowX (51.3% vs. 53.1%) and on the overall score. On novel-object generalization, VideoVLA (65.2%) beats SpatialVLA (50.8%) by 14.4 points and CogACT (42.4%) by 22.8 points, while OpenVLA and π₀ drop to 6.4% and 28.8% respectively. For cross-embodiment skill transfer, VideoVLA (48.6%) more than doubles CogACT (20.4%) and π₀ (18.3%), with OpenVLA and SpatialVLA below 19%. In real-world in-domain tasks, VideoVLA (64.6%) leads CogACT (58.4%) by 6.2 points and π₀ (50.7%) by 13.9 points; OpenVLA and SpatialVLA remain below 23%. RT-1-X and RT-2-X are included only in the in-domain simulation comparison and show substantially lower performance than all other baselines, reflecting their older architecture.

## Relation to Prior Work

VideoVLA departs from the dominant VLA paradigm that builds on pre-trained vision-language *understanding* models (RT-2, OpenVLA, π0, CogACT) by instead adapting a large pre-trained video *generation* model (CogVideoX-5B) as the backbone, adding action as a new denoising modality within the same Diffusion Transformer. Conceptually, it shares the DiT architecture with both video generation and action generation literature but unifies them into a single multi-modal denoising objective. It also differs from prior video-generation-for-robotics work (UniSim, RoboDreamer, VPP, UVA) that uses video prediction as a modular planning component, by making video and action prediction jointly end-to-end within one transformer. The dual-prediction strategy—simultaneously denoising future frame latents and action vectors—has no direct precedent in prior VLA architectures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that the physical-dynamics knowledge encoded in large video generators transfers meaningfully to embodied action prediction, yielding strong generalization to novel objects (65.2% vs. 42.4% for CogACT) and cross-embodiment skill transfer (48.6% vs. 20.4%), suggesting a new architectural paradigm for VLA models. The ablation showing that removing the video denoising loss collapses performance (80.4% → 27.0%) provides evidence that visual imagination is not merely a byproduct but actively constrains and improves action prediction. Key limitations include a ~3 Hz control frequency due to the 5B-parameter video backbone and 50-step DDIM sampling, which limits real-world deployment. Open questions include whether smaller robot-specific video generators can retain the generalization benefit at higher inference speeds, and whether the paradigm extends to more complex multi-step or bimanual tasks.
