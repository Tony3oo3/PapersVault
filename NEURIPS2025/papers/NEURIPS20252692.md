## Problem

Event cameras produce asynchronous, sparse data streams whose microsecond-level temporal precision and variable spatial density are destroyed by frame-based conversions, while Transformer-based sparse methods incur quadratic O(N²) complexity that limits scalability. The key gap is how to model full spatiotemporal context, selectively attend to salient dynamic regions, and robustly adapt to the irregular density of event data—all while maintaining linear computational cost.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252692/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes raw asynchronous event streams segmented into fixed-size slices of N events, where each event is a 4-tuple (x, y, t, polarity) representing spatial coordinates, timestamp, and polarity. Events are organized into sparse k-nearest-neighbor graphs with edges defined by a spatiotemporal distance threshold, rather than being rasterized into dense tensors. The output is a per-event spatiotemporal feature vector that feeds downstream heads for object detection (bounding boxes on Gen1/1Mpx) or action classification (class labels on four recognition benchmarks), with the full backbone containing only ~0.77M parameters.

## Method

EventMG is a multilevel Mamba-Graph architecture that jointly models micro-level (individual event) and macro-level (event cluster) information. At the micro level, a lightweight GNN enriches node features on a sparse event graph, then the Spatiotemporal-aware Event Scanning Technology (SEST) applies an Adaptive Perturbation Network (APN) to learn task-driven coordinate offsets (Δx, Δy, Δt) that create an effective coordinate space, followed by a Multidirectional Scanning Module (MSM) that sorts events along six directions (t±, x±, y±) to produce multiple 1D sequences for Mamba's selective state-space processing at O(N) cost. At the macro level, connected components of the event graph are abstracted as supernodes in a fully-connected Component Graph, processed by a GNN (ComponentsNet) to capture global scene structure. A learnable gating fusion then adaptively combines macro-context with micro-features per node, yielding a final representation that is both detail-rich and globally aware, all without pre-training.

## Results

On the Gen1 object detection benchmark, EventMG achieves 53.7% mAP with only 1.96M parameters and 8.66 ms inference time, outperforming the previous best (ERGO-12, 50.4% mAP) while using just 3.3% of its parameter count. On the higher-resolution 1Mpx dataset, EventMG reaches 47.4% mAP, matching RVT-B and approaching GET-T (48.4%), again with the smallest model size. For action recognition, EventMG attains the best accuracy on DVS Action (93.3%) and competitive results on THU E-ACT-50-CHL (58.8%), HMDB51-DVS (71.2%), and UCF101-DVS (79.6%), all with under 1M parameters and 0.531 GFLOPs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On Gen1, EventMG (53.7% mAP) surpasses all 11 baselines, most notably beating ERGO-12 (50.4%) by 3.3 points while using only 1.96M vs. 59.6M parameters, and outperforming GET-T (47.9%) and RVT-B (47.2%) by 5.8 and 6.5 points respectively. On 1Mpx, EventMG (47.4%) ties RVT-B and S5-ViT-B (47.2%) but trails GET-T (48.4%) by 1.0 point, though with roughly 1/11th the parameters. For action recognition on DVS Action, EventMG achieves the highest accuracy (93.3%), edging out TTPOINT (92.7%) and Two-stream SNN (91.7%). On THU E-ACT-50-CHL, EventMG (58.8%) is marginally below EventMamba (59.4%) but with fewer parameters (0.773M vs. 0.905M) and lower FLOPs (0.691 vs. 0.953 GFLOPs). On the larger HMDB51-DVS and UCF101-DVS datasets, EventMG (71.2% / 79.6%) falls notably short of EventMamba (86.4% / 97.9%) but still outperforms all other baselines including TTPOINT (56.9% / 72.5%) and EVTC+I3D (70.4% / 79.1%), while maintaining the lowest GFLOPs (0.531) among all methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

EventMG sits at the intersection of two lines of work on event camera processing: SSM-based sequence modeling (Mamba) and graph-based sparse representations (GNNs). Prior methods have pursued these in isolation—Transformers and SSMs for long-range dependencies, GNNs for local topology—while frame-based approaches sacrifice the microsecond-level sparsity that defines event data. The paper's key departure is the Mamba-Graph collaborative architecture with SEST (APN + MSM), which explicitly addresses the topology-agnostic and unidirectional limitations of SSMs when applied to irregular, multidimensional event streams, and the over-smoothing/scalability limitations of deep GNNs. The multilevel micro-to-macro-to-micro fusion via Component Graphs extends the single-scale processing paradigm common in prior event-based methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The extreme parameter efficiency (1.96M vs. 59.6M for ERGO-12) combined with linear O(N) complexity makes high-quality spatiotemporal event representation feasible on resource-constrained or real-time platforms, which is practically important for automotive and embedded event-camera deployments. However, the accuracy gains over strong baselines are modest (competitive rather than dominant on 1Mpx and action recognition), and the APN module's learned coordinate perturbations remain difficult to interpret, as the authors candidly acknowledge. Open questions include whether the multilevel Mamba-Graph paradigm generalizes beyond detection and recognition to tasks like segmentation or tracking, how sensitive performance is to the graph construction hyperparameters (k, δ_st, n_min), and whether the SEST scanning strategy can be further simplified or made fully adaptive without the fixed direction set.
