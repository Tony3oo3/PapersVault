## Problem

Classical Entropy Minimization (EM), a widely used self-supervised loss, suffers from two inherent limitations caused by its highly coupled formulation: (1) reward collapse, where the gradient contribution of high-certainty samples vanishes as predicted probabilities approach 1.0, and (2) easy-class bias, where the model systematically over-assigns samples to dominant classes, misaligning the output distribution from the true label distribution. The paper asks what the internal mechanisms of EM actually are and how to decouple and fix these opposing forces without introducing tunable hyperparameters.

## Input / Output

AdaDEM is a plug-and-play scalar loss computed from the model's C-dimensional logit vector, requiring no test-data labels or hyperparameter tuning. In TTA it consumes a single streaming test image at a time; in SSL it operates on unlabeled samples alongside a small labeled set; in UDA it processes target-domain images during training; and in RL it is applied to the policy's action logits. The output is task-dependent (class label, pixel-wise segmentation mask, or discrete action), but the loss itself is always a single scalar per sample, making it a direct drop-in replacement for the standard conditional entropy term in existing methods.

## Method

The paper reformulates the conditional entropy H(z) = −Σ p_i log p_i into two decoupled components with opposite effects: the Cluster Aggregation Driving Factor (CADF, T(z) = −Σ p_i z_i), whose gradient rewards dominant classes and drives peaked outputs, and the Gradient Mitigation Calibrator (GMC, Q(z) = log Σ e^{z_i}), whose gradient penalizes high-confidence logits and pushes toward uniformity. DEM* introduces a temperature τ to reshape CADF's reward curve (preventing collapse near p=1) and a weight α to scale GMC, both searched via TPE on a labeled target subset. The main contribution, AdaDEM, eliminates all hyperparameters by (a) normalizing the CADF reward by δ, the L1-norm of CADF's own gradients, so that high-certainty samples retain meaningful learning signal, and (b) replacing GMC with a Marginal Entropy Calibrator (MEC) that dynamically estimates per-class predicted probabilities via an exponential moving average and subtracts them from the softmax output, thereby counteracting easy-class bias without assuming a uniform label prior.

## Results

On single-domain TTA (ImageNet-C, ViT-B/16), AdaDEM improves Tent from 52.7% to 66.2% top-1 accuracy (+13.5%) and SAR from 54.2% to 65.7% (+11.5%). On continual TTA, AdaDEM boosts Tent from 48.5% to 64.4% (+15.9%) and DeYO from 57.6% to 59.0% (+1.4%). In semi-supervised learning, AdaDEM adds up to +7.2% on EuroSat (Ent. Min.: 76.5%→83.7%) and +5.0% on EuroSat with FixMatch (91.9%→96.9%). For UDA semantic segmentation (GTA5→Cityscapes), AdaDEM improves AdvEnt mIoU from 43.6% to 44.9% (+1.3%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

AdaDEM consistently outperforms or matches all TTA baselines when plugged in as a drop-in replacement for the EM loss. The largest gains appear over Tent (+13.5% single-domain, +15.9% continual) and SAR (+11.5%, +6.0%), where the original methods suffer most from reward collapse and easy-class bias. Against the sample-selection methods ETA and EATA, AdaDEM provides smaller but consistent improvements (+1.7%/+1.9% and +3.1%/+1.5% respectively), since these methods already partially mitigate reward collapse via confidence-based re-weighting. DeYO is the one case where AdaDEM does not improve single-domain performance (62.6%→62.6%) but still helps continual TTA (+1.4%). In SSL, AdaDEM improves Ent. Min. by +0.8% (CIFAR-10) to +7.2% (EuroSat), VAT by +1.1% to +4.6%, and FreeMatch by +0.1% to +2.3%, while showing minor regressions on already-saturated benchmarks (MixMatch CIFAR-10: −0.2%, FixMatch STL-10: −0.6%). For UDA segmentation, AdaDEM beats both MinEnt (+1.1 mIoU) and AdvEnt (+1.3 mIoU), and in RL (PPO on Minigrid) it achieves higher or comparable average return across 9 environments.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the classical Entropy Minimization formulation of Grandvalet & Bengio (2004), which has been widely adopted as a self-supervised loss in TTA (Tent, ETA, EATA, DeYO, SAR), SSL (VAT, MixMatch, FixMatch, FreeMatch), and UDA (MinEnt, AdvEnt). The key conceptual departure is a reformulation that decouples the conditional entropy into two opposing components—CADF (which rewards dominant classes) and GMC (which penalizes high-confidence predictions)—revealing that their coupled interaction causes reward collapse and easy-class bias. Unlike prior methods that address class imbalance via assumed uniform label priors, AdaDEM's Marginal Entropy Calibrator dynamically estimates class proportions during learning, eliminating the need for such assumptions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The decomposition provides a principled diagnostic for why classical EM underperforms in noisy, imbalanced, or non-stationary settings, and AdaDEM offers a hyperparameter-free, plug-and-play replacement that consistently improves across TTA, SSL, UDA, and RL without requiring labeled target data. The authors acknowledge that their fix operates only at the objective-function level and cannot resolve all pathologies of self-supervised learning (e.g., fundamentally mislabeled samples or adversarial inputs). An open question is whether the CADF/GMC decomposition generalizes to other information-theoretic losses (e.g., mutual information objectives) or to multi-modal and generative settings where the softmax structure differs.
