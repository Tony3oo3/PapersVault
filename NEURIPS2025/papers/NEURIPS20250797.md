## Problem

Spherical-equivariant EGNNs (e.g., SE(3)-Transformers, Equiformer) achieve state-of-the-art accuracy on molecular property prediction by encoding higher-order geometric correlations via Clebsch-Gordan tensor products, but these tensor products are computed per edge, yielding O(|E|L⁶) complexity that renders them impractical for large-scale systems. Existing efficiency-oriented alternatives (e.g., spherical-scalarization methods) sacrifice theoretical completeness by bypassing tensor products entirely, leaving a gap between expressiveness and scalability.

## Input / Output

The model consumes 3D atomic coordinates and atomic types, constructing a k-nearest-neighbor molecular graph (k=32 typical) over the point cloud. It outputs per-atom energy (meV) and per-atom forces (meV/Å) for the S2EF task, as well as equivariant node embeddings (irreps up to degree L) that can drive molecular dynamics simulations. Notably, E2Former is the only model in the paper's benchmarks capable of processing 6,400-atom systems within 80 GB memory, where EquiformerV2 and MACE-Large fail.

## Method

The core contribution is the Wigner 6j convolution, which decomposes the edge-based SO(3) tensor product into node-local operations via two mathematical steps: (1) a Binomial Local Expansion that expresses the edge-direction spherical harmonic R^(ℓ)(r_ij) as a binomial sum of node-local terms R^(u)(r_i) ⊗ R^(ℓ-u)(r_j), and (2) Wigner 6j recoupling that reorders the coupling sequence to factor out the node-i-dependent term from the neighbor aggregation over j. This reduces tensor product complexity from O(|E|) to O(|V|) while preserving exact SO(3) equivariance and the full expressive power of irreducible tensor products. E2Former integrates this convolution as the backend kernel of an equivariant transformer, absorbing attention coefficients directly into the Wigner 6j convolution (Algorithm 1) so that the number of tensor product operations scales linearly with system size. The freed computational budget is reallocated to deeper layers, wider channels, more attention heads, and MACE-style higher-order interactions, yielding competitive accuracy on OC20, OC22, and SPICE with 7×–30× speedups over standard SO(3) convolutions.

## Results

On OC20-2M, E2Former-67M achieves the best Energy MAE (270 meV) and ties eSCN on Force MAE (20.50 meV/Å), while running at 34 samples/sec versus 5–19 for all baselines. On SPICE, E2Former-33M attains the lowest overall Energy (0.60 meV/atom) and Force (7.46 meV/Å) MAE, outperforming MACE-Large (0.77 / 12.26) while training ~5× faster (70 vs. 336 GPU hours). On OC22, E2Former-67M trails EquiformerV2 on both Energy (491 vs. 433 meV ID) and Force (25.98 vs. 22.88 meV/Å ID) but converges in 1,500 GPU hours—only one-third of EquiformerV2's 4,500. In large-scale benchmarks, E2Former is the only model that successfully processes 6,400-atom systems and is ~3× faster than MACE-Large at 3,200 atoms.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On OC20, E2Former-67M beats all baselines on Energy MAE (270 vs. 279 for SCN, the next best) and matches eSCN on Force MAE (20.50 meV/Å) while using fewer parameters (67M vs. 51M) and training 32% faster (1,500 vs. 2,200 GPU hours); its inference speed of 34 samples/sec is 1.8× faster than eSCN/EquiformerV2 and 6.8× faster than SCN. On OC22, E2Former-67M loses to EquiformerV2 on both ID Energy (491 vs. 433 meV) and ID Force (25.98 vs. 22.88 meV/Å), but requires only 1,500 GPU hours versus 4,500—a 3× training speedup. On SPICE, E2Former-33M outperforms MACE-Large on 6 of 7 test subsets (e.g., PubChem Force: 8.9 vs. 14.75 meV/Å; Dimers Force: 4.01 vs. 6.62) and trains in 70 GPU hours versus 336 for MACE-Large (~5× faster). In the 6,400-atom scaling test, both MACE-Large and EquiformerV2 fail entirely, while E2Former-33M completes inference at 0.59 samples/sec with 80 GB memory; at 3,200 atoms it is ~3× faster than MACE-Large (1.2 vs. 0.41 samples/sec). In MD simulations, E2Former matches DFT (MADFT) power spectra closely and runs ~1,000× faster than DFT and ~2× faster than GFN2-xTB.

## Relation to Prior Work

E2Former builds directly on the spherical-equivariant transformer lineage (SE(3)-Transformers, Equiformer/EquiformerV2) and incorporates MACE-style higher-order interactions, but introduces a fundamentally new computational primitive: the Wigner 6j convolution. The key theoretical departure is the use of angular momentum recoupling theory (Wigner 6j symbols) combined with a binomial local expansion of spherical harmonics to factorize edge-dependent Clebsch-Gordan tensor products into node-local operations, reducing complexity from O(|E|) to O(|V|). This contrasts with prior efficiency efforts like eSCN (which reduces SO(3) to SO(2)) or spherical-scalarization methods (which sacrifice theoretical completeness), as E2Former preserves the full tensor-product expressive power while eliminating the edge-scaling bottleneck.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 7×–30× speedup and the ability to handle 6,400-atom systems (where EquiformerV2 and MACE-Large fail entirely) open a practical path toward large-scale molecular dynamics with equivariant models, which were previously limited to small or medium systems. The Wigner 6j convolution is a reusable theoretical tool that could benefit any spherical-equivariant architecture, not just E2Former. Open questions include whether the O(L⁶) per-node cost becomes prohibitive at very high angular momentum cutoffs, whether the approach extends naturally to periodic boundary conditions, and whether hardware-specific optimizations (GPU kernels for 6j recoupling) can close the remaining gap with non-equivariant baselines on small systems.
