## Problem

Existing generative neural PDE solvers face a fundamental trade-off: discrete-token autoregressive models (e.g., Zebra) sacrifice expressiveness through quantization, while full-frame diffusion models (e.g., AR-DiT) incur high computational cost for denoising entire spatial fields at each step. Neither approach efficiently combines per-token generation with continuous latent representations, limiting scalability, uncertainty calibration, and physical consistency for time-dependent parametric PDEs.

## Input / Output

ENMA accepts irregularly sampled spatio-temporal observations as unordered point sets at arbitrary spatial grids, with input sparsity varying from 20% to 100% of the full grid. In temporal conditioning mode, it consumes L past trajectory states; in the IVP-with-context mode, it takes only the target's initial state plus a separate context trajectory governed by the same PDE parameters. Output is a continuous spatio-temporal PDE field at arbitrary resolution, decoded from compressed latent tokens (×4 in 1D, ×15 in 2D, up to ×176 on high-dimensional systems) via a VAE decoder with cross-attention interpolation to target coordinates.

## Method

ENMA follows an encode–generate–decode pipeline. The encoder uses a geometry-aware cross-attention module (with ALiBi-style distance bias) to map irregular point clouds onto a regular grid, then a causal 3D CNN compresses the sequence spatio-temporally into compact latent tokens. Generation proceeds in two stages: a causal transformer with blockwise attention extracts a dynamic context representation Z_DYN from the latent history, and a masked spatial transformer combined with a lightweight flow-matching MLP decodes the next frame token-by-token. The flow matching objective trains the MLP to predict a velocity field transporting Gaussian noise to the target token conditioned on spatial context, solved at inference via a midpoint ODE integrator. Spatial decoding uses a masked autoregressive strategy with a cosine schedule controlling how many tokens are revealed per step, enabling permutation-invariant generation with fine-grained uncertainty control. The entire pipeline is trained end-to-end with a VAE reconstruction loss plus KL regularization, and the flow matching loss applied only to masked positions.

## Results

On the five PDE benchmarks under temporal conditioning, ENMA achieves the lowest Relative MSE on Advection (3.95e-2 In-D, 5.30e-1 Out-D), Combined (7.86e-3 In-D, 1.02e-1 Out-D), Gray-Scott (3.40e-2 In-D, 1.44e-1 Out-D), and Wave (1.45e-1 In-D, 4.89e-1 Out-D), while trailing Zebra on Vorticity (7.58e-2 vs 4.43e-2 In-D). On the Initial Value Problem setting, ENMA leads on Advection (2.02e-1 vs 3.16e-1 for Zebra) and Combined (1.56e-2 vs 4.78e-2), but is slightly behind Zebra on Gray-Scott, Wave, and Vorticity. On high-dimensional Rayleigh-Bénard and Active Matter systems, ENMA achieves 9.87e-2 and 3.33e-1 Relative MSE respectively at ×64 and ×176 compression, outperforming BCAT and AViT which operate at ×1 compression. For uncertainty quantification, ENMA achieves RMSCE of 8.68e-2 and CRPS of 1.70e-3, roughly 2.5× lower than Zebra and 3× lower than AR-DiT.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ENMA outperforms all deterministic baselines (FNO, BCAT, AViT) across nearly all PDEs and both settings, with the largest margins on Combined (7.86e-3 vs 5.67e-2 for AViT in temporal conditioning) and Advection (3.95e-2 vs 1.64e-1 for AViT). Against the generative baselines, ENMA dominates AR-DiT on every dataset, reducing Out-D error by 3–15× (e.g., 1.02e-1 vs 1.80e+0 on Combined, 4.89e-1 vs 7.52e+0 on Wave). Compared to Zebra (the closest competitor, also operating in latent space but with discrete tokens), ENMA wins on Advection, Combined, and Wave in both settings, but loses on Vorticity (7.58e-2 vs 4.43e-2 In-D) and is slightly behind on Gray-Scott IVP (4.80e-2 vs 4.40e-2) and Wave IVP Out-D (5.02e-1 vs 3.52e-1). On the high-dimensional Rayleigh-Bénard and Active Matter benchmarks, ENMA beats both BCAT and AViT while operating at 64× and 176× compression respectively. For uncertainty quantification, ENMA achieves 2.5× lower RMSCE and 5.4× lower CRPS than Zebra, confirming that continuous flow-matching sampling yields better-calibrated probabilistic forecasts than discrete categorical sampling.

## Relation to Prior Work

ENMA synthesizes three recent threads: masked autoregressive (MAR) generation over continuous tokens (Li et al., 2024), Flow Matching for per-token conditional distributions (Lipman et al., 2023), and causal spatio-temporal convolutional compression (Yu et al., 2024), into a unified neural operator for PDEs. It departs from the discrete-token AR paradigm of Zebra (Serrano et al., 2025) by operating entirely in continuous latent space, and from full-frame diffusion models (AR-DiT, PDE-Refiner) by replacing iterative denoising with a lightweight per-token ODE solver. The encoder's geometry-aware cross-attention extends AROMA's interpolation module (Serrano et al., 2024a) with an ALiBi-style distance bias to handle irregular point-set inputs, while the two-stage causal-transformer-then-spatial-AR pipeline mirrors the temporal/spatial factorization in video generation (Magvit, Magvit2) but adapted to the operator-learning setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ENMA offers a practical middle ground between the expressiveness of diffusion models and the efficiency of discrete AR models for PDE surrogates, with the added benefit of in-context adaptation to unseen PDE parameters without retraining. The demonstrated compression rates (×64 on Rayleigh-Bénard, ×176 on Active Matter) while maintaining competitive accuracy suggest latent-space tokenwise generation could become a scalable default for multi-physics foundation models. Key open questions include whether the compression-expressiveness trade-off (visible on vorticity) can be resolved via adaptive or coarse-to-fine decoding, and whether the approach scales to 3D or higher-dimensional physical systems where token counts grow rapidly.
