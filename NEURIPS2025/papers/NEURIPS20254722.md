## Problem

Video inpainting and editing with pre-trained text-to-video foundation models has been challenging because existing training-free methods (e.g., DDNM back-projection) produce biased estimates of the conditional denoising distribution and only leverage information at the current noise level, while training-based approaches require substantial compute and are difficult to adapt as foundation models evolve. This paper asks whether a model-agnostic, training-free sampling strategy can unlock high-quality video inpainting and editing from any pretrained text-to-video generator.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a video clip (49×720×480 for CogVideoX), a dynamic binary mask specifying regions to inpaint or edit, and a text prompt describing the desired replacement content. It outputs a full video where masked regions are rendered according to the prompt while unmasked regions are preserved. A key practical detail is that the dynamic mask is applied in VAE latent space via a learned latent mask fuser rather than by resizing pixel-space masks, which would lose spatial accuracy in the compressed latent representation.

## Method

ZeroPatcher introduces Conditional Denoising Expectation Maximization (CD-EM), which reformulates the sampling trajectory as an EM problem: the E-step approximates the log-likelihood expectation via Monte-Carlo sampling from the current denoising distribution, and the M-step solves for the optimal noisy latent via fixed-point iteration. The paper proves that the fixed point is unique and convergent under a Lipschitz condition on the score function, and shows that N=1, K=1, P=2 suffices for high-quality results with only 3 NFEs per denoising step. To handle dynamic masks in latent space, a lightweight latent mask fuser (6 residual blocks, 1536 channels, with adaptive instance normalization) is trained on 20K samples to fuse two latent videos guided by an encoded pixel-space mask, avoiding costly VAE decode-encode cycles. The approach is model-agnostic, demonstrated on both diffusion (CogVideoX) and flow-matching (HunyuanVideo) generators, and can be combined with or replace standard back-projection.

## Results

On video inpainting, ZeroPatcher achieves PSNR 34.33 / SSIM 0.9715 / VFID 0.039 / Ewarp 0.961 on YouTube-VOS and PSNR 34.13 / SSIM 0.9765 / VFID 0.096 / Ewarp 1.163 on DAVIS, making it the best training-free method and competitive with the strongest trained model (ProPainter: 34.43/0.9735/0.042/0.974 on YouTube-VOS). On video editing, ZeroPatcher achieves the highest CLIP score (0.3543), best background preservation (PSNR 34.23, SSIM 0.9712), best temporal smoothness (Ewarp 1.231), and dominant human-evaluation text-alignment score (60.7% vs. next-best 15.3%). The method requires only ~250 s/sample at 49×720×480 resolution with 100 NFE, comparable to other training-free baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest trained inpainting model ProPainter, ZeroPatcher is within 0.1–0.3 dB on PSNR and 0.001–0.002 on SSIM, while actually surpassing it on VFID (0.039 vs. 0.042 on YouTube-VOS; 0.096 vs. 0.098 on DAVIS) and Ewarp (0.961 vs. 0.974; 1.163 vs. 1.187), indicating superior visual realism and temporal consistency without any task-specific training. Among training-free baselines, ZeroPatcher decisively outperforms DDNM on all four metrics (e.g., VFID 0.039 vs. 0.067, Ewarp 0.961 vs. 1.069 on YouTube-VOS) and SDEdit by a wide margin (PSNR 34.33 vs. 26.41 on YouTube-VOS). For video editing, ZeroPatcher achieves the best CLIP score (0.3543 vs. 0.3341 for per-frame SD inpainting, 0.3313 for Video-P2P, 0.3267 for DDNM), best background preservation (PSNR 34.23 vs. 30.47 for Video-P2P), best temporal smoothness (Ewarp 1.231 vs. 1.457 for Video-P2P), and a dominant human-evaluation text-alignment rate of 60.7% compared to 15.3% (Video-P2P) and 8.9% (DDNM). The method also outperforms all trained inpainting baselines (FuseFormer, ISVI, FGT, E2FGVI) on VFID and Ewarp, confirming that leveraging a pretrained video foundation model as a prior is more effective than task-specific training at comparable or lower compute cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends DDNM's back-projection framework for zero-shot image inpainting to the video domain, but departs from it in two key ways: (1) replacing the per-step pixel replacement with Conditional Denoising Expectation Maximization (CD-EM), which optimizes the noisy latent using less-noisy known segments (x_{t-1}^a rather than x_t^a) via a fixed-point iteration, and (2) introducing a latent mask fuser to handle the spatial misalignment inherent in VAE-compressed latent spaces. The method is explicitly model-agnostic, demonstrated on both diffusion-transformer (CogVideoX) and flow-matching (HunyuanVideo) architectures, and the authors provide a convergence/uniqueness theorem for the fixed-point solver that has no direct analogue in prior training-free inpainting work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that as video foundation models proliferate and evolve, a training-free plug-and-play sampler avoids the substantial cost of fine-tuning each new architecture for inpainting/editing. The main limitation acknowledged is domain dependence: if the input video or prompt falls outside the pretrained model's distribution, rendering quality degrades. Open questions include how the method scales to longer videos, more complex multi-object edits, and whether the CD-EM optimization can be further accelerated for real-time applications. The dramatic ablation showing back-projection removal collapses PSNR from 34.13 to 17.41 underscores that trajectory optimization alone is insufficient without explicit range-space enforcement.
