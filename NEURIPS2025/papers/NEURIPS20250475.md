## Problem

Existing pose-driven character animation methods are designed for single-character scenarios and rely on implicit, end-to-end training losses that capture only global similarity. When extended to multi-character settings—especially with position swaps—these methods fail to enforce correct one-to-one Identity Correspondence (IC) between generated and reference characters, producing identity confusion and feature interference. The paper tackles this by making IC an explicit, optimizable objective rather than an emergent property of global reconstruction.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB reference image, a driving pose sequence, and a reference pose, all pre-processed into Identity-Embedded Guidance (IEG): color-coded DWPose skeletons where each character's skeleton is assigned a unique color derived from SAM2 instance bounding boxes for persistent identity tracking. The reference image is encoded into latent space via a frozen VAE, and the IEG is injected into both the ReferenceNet (for appearance) and the DenoisingNet (for pose conditioning). Output is a temporally coherent multi-character video at 512×512 resolution (24-frame sequences at 3-frame intervals), generated via 50-step DDIM denoising with classifier-free guidance scale 3.5.

## Method

The core contribution is the Identity Matching Graph (IMG), a weighted complete bipartite graph whose two node sets represent reference and generated characters (extracted as masked latent regions via instance segmentation). Edge weights are computed by Mask-Query Attention (MQA), which treats each generated character as a query and all reference characters (aggregated into a single representation for O(n) efficiency) as keys, then normalizes patch-level attention scores into per-pair affinities. IC correctness is formalized as the ratio C of ground-truth edge weights to total edge weights, and −C is used as a matching loss added to the standard diffusion reconstruction loss (L = L_diff + λ·L_match). Three targeted strategies reinforce this: Identity-Embedded Guidance provides explicit color-coded identity anchors at both training and inference; Multi-Scale Matching constructs the IMG at N selected UNet layers to enforce correspondence across feature hierarchies; and Pre-Classified Sampling oversamples rare position-swap pairs to counteract long-tail data distribution. The entire IMG machinery is training-only; at inference the model simply denoises conditioned on IEG and ReferenceNet features.

## Results

On the ICE-Bench (3,200 frames), EverybodyDance achieves FVD 225.06, a 38.3% reduction over its AnimateAnyone backbone (364.85), along with the best SSIM (0.654), FID (40.19), and FID-VID (23.584) among all compared methods. On the Follow-Your-Pose-V2 public multi-character benchmark, it further outperforms the strongest baseline (Follow-Your-Pose-V2) with FVD 127.36 vs. 142.76 and SSIM 0.879 vs. 0.830. In the user study, EverybodyDance scores 4.57/5 on IC accuracy, nearly double the next-best method (AnimateAnyone at 2.33/5).

## Baselines & Comparisons

EverybodyDance outperforms all seven baselines on every reported metric across both the ICE-Bench and the Follow-Your-Pose-V2 public benchmark. Against its direct backbone AnimateAnyone, the FVD gap is 139.79 points (364.85 → 225.06), and even after fine-tuning AnimateAnyone on the same MultiDance dataset (AnimateAnyone*), the gap remains 133.25 FVD points, confirming gains come from the method rather than data. The closest competitor on ICE-Bench is UniAnimate (FVD 295.56), which EverybodyDance beats by 70.5 FVD points and 0.031 SSIM. On the Follow-Your-Pose-V2 benchmark, EverybodyDance surpasses the purpose-built multi-character method Follow-Your-Pose-V2 by 15.4 FVD points (142.76 → 127.36) and 0.049 SSIM, while also leading on FID (26.01 vs. 26.95) and FID-VID (12.68 vs. 14.56). In the user study, the IC-accuracy margin is particularly large: EverybodyDance scores 4.57/5 versus 2.33 (AnimateAnyone), 2.12 (UniAnimate), and 1.83 (MagicAnimate), indicating that the graph-based IC supervision translates into clearly perceptible identity correctness.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the AnimateAnyone/ReferenceNet framework for single-character pose-driven animation to the multi-character setting, where the core difficulty is maintaining correct one-to-one identity correspondence (IC) when characters swap positions. Rather than relying on the implicit data-driven paradigm used by prior methods (AnimateAnyone, MagicAnimate, MimicMotion, UniAnimate), it introduces an explicit graph-based formulation: the Identity Matching Graph (IMG), a weighted complete bipartite graph whose edge weights are computed via Mask-Query Attention (MQA) over UNet intermediate features. This departs conceptually from prior multi-character work (Follow-Your-Pose-V2, which assumes fixed relative positions; Ingredients, which uses text-controlled layout) by directly optimizing IC correctness as a training objective, and from standard attention-based conditioning by repurposing spatial attention scores as a structural matching signal.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important gap—multi-character animation with position swaps is common in dance, sports, and entertainment content but has been largely unsolved by existing methods. The ICE-Bench is the first benchmark dedicated to evaluating IC correctness, and the MultiDance dataset (814 clips, 257K frames) fills a data gap for multi-character training. Key limitations remain: the method cannot handle severe inter-character occlusion, depends on upstream instance segmentation quality, and lacks a dedicated quantitative IC metric (relying instead on proxy fidelity metrics). Future work on 3D-aware approaches and purpose-built IC evaluation metrics would strengthen the field.
