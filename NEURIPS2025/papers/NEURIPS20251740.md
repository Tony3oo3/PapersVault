## Problem

Existing diffusion-based deblurring methods apply standard Gaussian noise diffusion, which fundamentally mismatches the physics of motion blur formation. Motion blur arises from continuous exposure and accumulates progressively along motion trajectories, producing structured, directional degradation rather than random perturbations. This discrepancy limits the ability of conventional diffusion priors to capture blur-specific characteristics and recover fine high-frequency details.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251740/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single blurred RGB image (e.g., 256×256) as input and produces a deblurred RGB image of the same resolution. BlurDM operates as a plug-and-play prior generator in the latent space of an existing deblurring network: a Blur Encoder compresses the blurred image into a global latent vector, the diffusion process runs for T=5 steps in that latent space, and the resulting sharp prior is injected into each decoder scale via a Prior Fusion Module (channel-wise affine modulation) before the deblurring network produces the final output.

## Method

BlurDM introduces a dual noise-and-blur diffusion process grounded in the continuous-exposure model of motion blur. In the forward process, a sharp image is progressively degraded by both Gaussian noise (scaled by β_t) and blur residuals (scaled by exposure-time ratios α_{t-1}/α_t), where the blur residual e_t represents the incremental blur accumulated as exposure extends from α_{t-1} to α_t. The key insight is that the full T-step forward process collapses to a single-step form I_T = B + β̄_T·ε, where B is the fully blurred image, eliminating the need for ground-truth blur residuals. In the reverse process, a deterministic DDIM-style sampling step simultaneously estimates and removes both noise and blur residuals via dedicated estimators, recovering the sharp latent. The entire diffusion operates in latent space with a three-stage training strategy (pre-train sharp encoder + PFM, train blur encoder + BlurDM, joint fine-tuning), and the learned prior is fused into existing deblurring backbones (MIMO-UNet, Stripformer, FFTformer, LoFormer) through the PFM.

## Results

BlurDM, as a plug-and-play prior generator, consistently improves four deblurring backbones across four benchmark datasets. Average PSNR gains are +0.31 dB (GoPro), +0.32 dB (HIDE), +0.78 dB (RealBlur-J), and +0.69 dB (RealBlur-R), with the largest single-model improvement reaching +1.24 dB (LoFormer on RealBlur-R). Overall, BlurDM achieves an average gain of +0.53 dB PSNR, +0.004 SSIM, and −0.0028 LPIPS across all backbones and datasets, while adding only ~4.16G FLOPs and ~9 ms inference time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BlurDM is evaluated as a plug-and-play prior generator on four deblurring backbones (MIMO-UNet, Stripformer, FFTformer, LoFormer), consistently outperforming each baseline on all four datasets. The largest gains appear on real-world data: Stripformer improves by +1.05 dB PSNR on RealBlur-J (32.48→33.53) and +1.16 dB on RealBlur-R (39.84→41.00), while LoFormer gains +1.24 dB on RealBlur-R (40.36→40.92). Against other diffusion-based deblurring methods on GoPro (Table 5), BlurDM with Stripformer achieves 33.53 dB PSNR vs. HI-Diff's 33.33 dB and RDDM's 32.40 dB, and BlurDM with FFTformer reaches 34.34 dB with fewer FLOPs (135.69G) than both. In the prior-generator ablation (Table 3), BlurDM (32.28 dB GoPro) outperforms DDPM (31.91), RDDM (32.03), and a plain MLP (31.90), confirming that the blur-aware dual diffusion mechanism provides a meaningful advantage over standard or residual diffusion priors.

## Relation to Prior Work

BlurDM extends the latent-space diffusion prior paradigm established by HI-Diff (Chen et al., NeurIPS 2023) and DiffIR (Xia et al., ICCV 2023), which use diffusion models as plug-and-play prior generators for restoration networks. Its key conceptual departure is replacing the standard noise-only forward process with a dual noise-and-blur diffusion that mirrors the physical continuous-exposure model of motion blur formation, and replacing RDDM's additive residual formulation with a physically grounded exposure-integration model. The reverse process adapts DDIM's deterministic sampling to jointly estimate blur and noise residuals, while the three-stage training strategy and Prior Fusion Module follow the integration pattern from HI-Diff.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that explicitly encoding the physics of blur formation (continuous light accumulation over exposure) into the diffusion process yields consistent, if modest, improvements over both standard diffusion priors and residual-diffusion priors across four backbones and four datasets, with minimal overhead (~4G FLOPs, ~9 ms). The ablation showing that a standard DDPM prior performs comparably to a plain MLP prior is a useful negative result, suggesting that the diffusion mechanism alone does not help deblurring without domain-specific structure. A clear limitation is that the exposure-based model is specific to motion blur and does not generalize to defocus blur, which lacks temporal accumulation properties. Open questions include whether the dual-diffusion formulation can be extended to other structured degradations (e.g., rain, motion artifacts in video) and whether the latent-space justification via first-order Taylor expansion holds for deeper, non-linear encoders.
