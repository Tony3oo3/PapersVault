## Problem

Existing traffic flow generation methods require city-specific historical flow data for training, making them inapplicable to new cities lacking such records. This paper tackles cross-city zero-shot traffic flow generation, identifying two fundamental barriers: domain shift in geographic representations across cities, and the insufficiency of static geographic features alone to capture dynamic temporal flow patterns.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252354/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a region graph where each node carries geographic features (TF-IDF-encoded POI categories, total road segment lengths, and population statistics from OpenStreetMap and WorldPop) plus a time stamp (month, day-of-week, hour). For the target city, no historical flow data is available; instead, the retrieval module pulls top-K flow sequences from source cities matched by geographic representation similarity and temporal alignment. The output is a dynamic inflow/outflow sequence of length T (default 24 hourly steps) per region, generated via a denoising diffusion process.

## Method

CRAFT is a DDPM-based generator with two plug-in modules. First, Geographic Feature Alignment (GFA) pre-trains a Graph Transformer spatial encoder using two complementary losses: Traffic Flow Alignment (TFA) minimizes the discrepancy between pairwise representation distances and pairwise average-flow distances within source cities, while Cross-City Alignment (CCA) formulates source-to-target representation matching as an optimal transport problem solved via the network simplex algorithm, pulling semantically similar regions across cities into proximity without explicit correspondence labels. Second, Retrieval-based Condition Augmentation (RCA) retrieves K historical flow sequences from source cities based on aligned geographic similarity and temporal matching, compresses them via a self-attention block, and concatenates the result with the aligned geographic embedding and periodic time embeddings into a condition vector. The 1D U-Net noise estimator in the DDPM backbone is then conditioned on this augmented vector to denoise from pure Gaussian noise into the target city's traffic flow sequence.

## Results

CRAFT achieves state-of-the-art zero-shot cross-city traffic flow generation across all four target cities, with Inflow CPC ranging from 0.782 (NYC) to 0.815 (DC) and Inflow NRMSE from 0.170 (NYC) to 0.267 (TRT). The method yields a 59.7% average improvement over all baselines and a 22.5% improvement over the second-best method (GMEL) on CPC. Compared to the plain DDPM backbone, CRAFT achieves a 61.5% average improvement, demonstrating the critical contribution of the GFA and RCA modules.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CRAFT outperforms all eight baselines on every metric across all four target cities. The closest competitor is GMEL (a static geo-contextual embedding model), which achieves CPC of 0.730–0.741 on CHI/DC but drops sharply to 0.585 on NYC, whereas CRAFT maintains CPC ≥ 0.782 everywhere. Among dynamic generative baselines, DDPM (the plain diffusion backbone without GFA/RCA) is the strongest, reaching CPC 0.415–0.592, yet CRAFT still surpasses it by 29–61% in CPC. KSTDiff and CGAN perform poorly in cross-city settings (CPC near 0 on CHI/TRT for KSTDiff), confirming that knowledge-graph or GAN-based approaches fail to transfer without city-specific training. The largest absolute gains appear on NYC, where CRAFT's NMAE of 0.103 is roughly 3× better than the next-best DFG (0.182), highlighting the benefit of retrieval-augmented conditioning in a high-variability urban environment.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the traffic flow generation literature, which has evolved from physics-based gravity/radiation models through static deep models (GMEL, DFG) to dynamic generative frameworks (GANs, diffusion models like KSTDiff). It departs from prior work in two key ways: (1) it explicitly targets cross-city zero-shot generation rather than single-city in-distribution generation, and (2) it borrows retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) from the NLP/time-series literature to supplement dynamic flow patterns absent in the target city. The Geographic Feature Alignment module repurposes optimal transport (Wasserstein distance) as a domain-shift correction loss, a technique more common in domain adaptation than in traffic modeling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The cross-city zero-shot setting addresses a genuine deployment bottleneck: most cities lack the historical traffic records needed to train city-specific generative models. The modular design (GFA and RCA as plug-in components on a standard DDPM backbone) makes the approach easy to adopt and potentially transferable to other spatio-temporal generation tasks. However, validation is limited to four cities and a single data type (bicycle in-out flow), with no exploration of OD flows, no error bars, and a temporal horizon capped at 168 hours. Open questions include whether the optimal-transport alignment generalizes to cities with very different urban morphologies and whether the retrieval mechanism scales to larger source-city pools.
