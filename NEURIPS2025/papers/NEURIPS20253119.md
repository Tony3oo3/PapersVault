## Problem

SOTA LVM-based time series forecasters like VisionTS exhibit a strong inductive bias toward periodic patterns due to period-based imaging, causing them to underperform on signals with significant global trends. While multi-modal views (numerical, visual, textual) of time series could complement each other, existing multi-modal approaches such as Time-VLM use simple fusion strategies that overlook the unique inductive biases of individual views, and the integration of LVMs within a decomposition-based multi-modal framework for LTSF remains unexplored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253119/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a multivariate time series X ∈ R^{D×T} (default look-back T=336) and produces a forecast Ŷ ∈ R^{D×H} for H future steps (H ∈ {96, 192, 336, 720}, or {24, 36, 48, 60} for Illness). Each univariate series is converted to a 224×224×3 image via period-based patching (segment length P from FFT or sampling frequency), standard-deviation normalization, 3-channel duplication, and bilinear interpolation to fit MAE's input size. The channel-independence assumption is applied, forecasting each variate in parallel.

## Method

DMMV decomposes the forecasting task into two specialized sub-tasks aligned with the inductive biases of each view: a visual forecaster (pre-trained MAE/ViT-Base) captures periodic/seasonal patterns from the image view, while a numerical forecaster (linear or PatchTST-style Transformer) models global trends. The key mechanism is a backcast-residual adaptive decomposition (DMMV-A): the LVM backcasts the look-back window via a BCMASK strategy (two passes reconstructing left and right halves), and the residual between the original series and the backcast isolates the trend component for the numerical forecaster. Outputs are fused via a learnable scalar gate g = sigmoid(w_g) in a late-fusion scheme, preserving MAE's decoder for pixel-level prediction. Training follows a two-stage protocol: first the LVM is frozen while the numerical forecaster trains (~30 epochs), then only the LVM's normalization layers are unfrozen for joint fine-tuning, which ablations show is critical for coordinated learning between the two views.

## Results

DMMV-A achieves the best MSE on 6 out of 8 benchmark datasets and 43 first-place results across all prediction lengths and datasets. On the four ablation datasets, DMMV-A reports averaged MSE/MAE of 0.395/0.414 (ETTh1), 0.340/0.371 (ETTm1), 1.407/0.771 (Illness), and 0.217/0.256 (Weather). The simpler DMMV-S variant ranks 4.5/16 in MSE and 7.1/16 in MAE across all 16 compared methods, while DMMV-A's adaptive decomposition yields substantially better average ranks, confirming the benefit of the backcast-residual mechanism.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DMMV-A outperforms the closest multi-modal competitor Time-VLM (which additionally uses a text encoder via ViLT) on all reported datasets, despite Time-VLM's extra modality, confirming that the text view provides only marginal gains at significant computational cost. Against the strongest visual-only baseline VisionTS, DMMV-A wins on most datasets, particularly those where VisionTS's inductive bias toward periodicity causes degradation (e.g., ETTm2, Electricity), while VisionTS retains an edge on highly periodic datasets like ETTh1 and Traffic. Among numerical-only baselines, DMMV-A surpasses PatchTST, DLinear, TimesNet, FEDformer, Autoformer, and all other Transformer-based methods in the majority of settings. CycleNet, a lightweight model with learnable decomposition, is the most competitive numerical baseline, but DMMV-A still achieves more first-place results (43 vs. CycleNet's count). The ablation in Table 2 further shows that removing the backcast-residual decomposition (variant g) causes the largest single performance drop (e.g., −21.68% MSE on Illness), confirming that the synergy between the LVM's periodic bias and the numerical forecaster's trend modeling is the key differentiator over any single-view approach.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on VisionTS, which adapts MAE to forecast time series by converting them into period-based images, and on TimesNet's imaging technique. It departs from Time-VLM, the closest multi-modal competitor, by rejecting the textual view (citing marginal gains and high computational cost) and replacing ViLT's generic fusion with a decomposition-aware architecture that explicitly assigns periodic patterns to the visual forecaster and global trends to the numerical forecaster. The backcast-residual adaptive decomposition (DMMV-A) is a novel mechanism that repurposes the LVM's own reconstruction capability to learn the trend-seasonal split, distinguishing it from both fixed-kernel decomposition (Autoformer, DLinear) and residual decomposition in N-BEATS/CycleNet, which do not exploit LVM inductive bias.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and systematically exploits a specific inductive bias in LVM-based forecasters (sensitivity to segment length alignment with the signal period), providing a principled rationale for why decomposition helps and how to design it. The adaptive backcast-residual mechanism is a reusable idea that could inform future multi-view or multi-model time series architectures. Key open questions remain: the model still inherits some sensitivity to segment length (albeit reduced), the triple forward pass through the LVM during training creates non-trivial computational overhead, and the bilinear upsampling from small patched images to 224×224 may subtly distort temporal structure. The work also opens the door to incorporating additional views (e.g., textual) or more efficient LVM reuse strategies.
