## Problem

Existing off-policy evaluation (OPE) methods in reinforcement learning provide point estimates or pointwise confidence intervals for the value function at a single fixed state, but real-world applications (healthcare, autonomous driving, ridesharing) require evaluating a policy across an entire continuous or infinite state space. Applying Bonferroni correction to pointwise CIs inflates the family-wise error rate, becoming intractable when the state space is uncountable. This paper asks whether it is possible to simultaneously quantify the uncertainty of off-policy value estimators uniformly over the whole state space.

## Input / Output

The method takes pre-collected offline RL trajectories (state-action-reward-next-state tuples), a target policy specification, and a chosen sieve basis (Legendre polynomials, splines, or wavelets) as input. It produces simultaneous confidence regions (SCRs) that cover the entire value function V(π;s) for all s in the state space at a prescribed significance level, along with point estimates of the value function. The state space S is a subspace of R^d with fixed dimension d, and the action space is discrete with fixed cardinality m; the SCRs are parameterized by a scaling factor L(s) that allows flexible relative weighting across states.

## Method

The framework estimates the Q-function via linear function approximation (sieve method) using K basis functions, yielding an mK-dimensional parameter vector. The core theoretical contribution is a convex Gaussian approximation theorem showing that the distribution of the scaled sieve estimator over any convex set in R^{mK} is approximated by a corresponding Gaussian vector, with the approximation error vanishing as N = nT → ∞ under K = o(N^{2/7}(log N)^{-1}). Since the SCR event can be expressed as an intersection of convex sets (one per state), this approximation directly controls the simultaneous coverage probability. A Gaussian multiplier bootstrap algorithm then constructs the SCRs by simulating the limiting Gaussian field and computing the empirical quantile of its supremum, avoiding the need to estimate the joint distribution across states. The resulting SCR width exceeds pointwise CIs by only a factor of √(log N), establishing near-optimal efficiency while achieving uniform coverage over the entire state space.

## Results

In the multivariate simulation (Scenario 2), the proposed SCR achieves empirical coverage probability (ECP) of 0.92–0.95 across all sample sizes, closely matching the nominal 0.95 level, while the Bonferroni-adjusted SAVE over-covers at 0.96–0.99. The average length (AL) of the SCR is approximately 20% shorter than SAVE (e.g., 7.30 vs. 8.25 for n=50, T=50 with Legendre basis). On the OhioT1DM real data, the SCR identifies 90.7% of initial states as having significantly negative value under the behavior policy versus only 23.3% under the optimal policy, with an average SCR width of 27.0 compared to 32.4 for SAVE (20% wider).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is SAVE (Shi et al. 2021), a pointwise CI method extended via Bonferroni (or Sidak) correction to approximate simultaneous coverage. The proposed SCR consistently wins on both metrics: it achieves ECP closer to the nominal 0.95 (e.g., 0.942 vs. 0.978 at n=50, T=50) while producing intervals ~20% shorter in average length. SAVE with Sidak correction (Table S.2) shows similar over-coverage, confirming that the conservatism is inherent to the multiple-testing correction rather than the specific correction rule. Against the importance sampling approach (Jiang & Li 2016; Hanna et al. 2017), the proposed method is decisively superior: IS achieves only 63.8% ECP (well below nominal) with AL of 5.300, whereas the SCR reaches 95.4% ECP with a shorter AL of 4.575, because the discontinuous target policy in Scenario 2 causes IS weights to collapse to zero for many trajectories. On the OhioT1DM dataset, the SCR's average width is 20% narrower than SAVE for the optimal policy (27.0 vs. 32.4) and 8% narrower for the behavior policy (7.02 vs. 7.58), while maintaining valid simultaneous coverage.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the pointwise confidence interval framework for off-policy evaluation (Shi et al., 2021) to simultaneous confidence regions over the entire (potentially continuous) state space. The Q-function estimation relies on the sieve/linear function approximation framework of Chen (2007), while the core theoretical engine is the convex Gaussian approximation theorem of Fang (2016) and Fang & Koike (2024), which had not previously been applied to the Markov-dependent setting of RL. The paper departs from prior work that either provides pointwise CIs (Shi et al. 2021, Luckett et al. 2020) or integrated value function bounds (Duan et al. 2020) by delivering uniform coverage across all states simultaneously, using a multiplier bootstrap to avoid estimating the full joint distribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first theoretically justified simultaneous inference framework for OPE, filling a gap that becomes critical when evaluating policies across continuous state spaces (e.g., patient glucose levels in healthcare) where Bonferroni correction is infeasible. The near-optimality result—SCR width exceeding pointwise CI width by only a √log N factor—provides a clean efficiency guarantee. Limitations include the offline-only setting, stationary policies, and compact state space assumption; extending to online RL and robust value estimation are identified as natural next steps. The practical demonstration on OhioT1DM (showing 87% of patients benefit from the optimal policy with statistically significant improvement) illustrates the framework's potential for clinical decision-making.
