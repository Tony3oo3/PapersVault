## Problem

Existing methods for translating differential privacy parameters (ε, δ) into concrete privacy risks—re-identification, attribute inference, and data reconstruction—are both overly pessimistic and mutually inconsistent, leading practitioners to add unnecessary noise. No prior framework provides a single, unified bound that simultaneously covers all three risk types under a common threat model, and the loose conversions from ε-DP, Rényi DP, or concentrated DP can yield vacuous guarantees (e.g., >99% worst-case attribute inference accuracy at ε≈10) that contradict empirical evidence.

## Input / Output

The method consumes the full f-DP trade-off curve f(α) of a DP mechanism (obtained via exact formulas for Gaussian/Laplace mechanisms or via the Connect-the-Dots accountant for DP-SGD) together with a user-specified baseline risk level base ∈ [0,1]. It outputs a single unified upper bound of the form succ ≤ 1 − f(base) that applies simultaneously to strong predicate singling out (SPSO), strong reconstruction robustness (SRR), and strong attribute inference (SAI), along with the corresponding advantage bound adv ≤ 1 − f(base) − base. The baseline is tunable: practitioners can set it to the worst case (maximizing over base) to obtain a baseline-independent guarantee adv ≤ η, or to a scenario-specific value (e.g., disease prevalence) for a more interpretable risk estimate.

## Method

The core technical contribution is Lemma 3.1, which shows that for any f-DP mechanism and any bounded query function q(z;θ) ∈ [0,1], the expected attack success E_{z~P} E_{θ~M(S̄∪{z})}[q(z;θ)] is upper-bounded by 1 − f(sup_θ E_{z~P}[q(z;θ)]). The proof proceeds by applying the equivalent set-based form of f-DP (Pr[M(S)∈E] ≤ 1 − f(Pr[M(S')∈E]), Lemma 2.1) to the post-processed mechanism A(M(·)), then using Jensen's inequality (since 1−f is concave and increasing) to push the expectation over z~P inside f(·), and finally supremizing over θ to recover the baseline. By instantiating q as the appropriate indicator function (predicate match for SPSO, loss-threshold for SRR, attribute equality for SAI), the same expression bounds all three risks at once. This yields substantially tighter bounds than prior RDP/zCDP/ε-DP approaches because f-DP captures the entire privacy trade-off curve rather than a single (ε,δ) point, enabling ~20% noise reduction at the same risk level and up to 18pp accuracy gains in DP-SGD fine-tuning of GPT-2 on SST-2.

## Results

On the US 2020 Census state-level release (ε=10.6, δ=10⁻¹⁰), the paper's worst-case f-DP bound yields a maximum attribute inference advantage of 52pp, compared to >99pp from standard (ε,δ)-DP analysis and 73pp from Rényi DP; with a realistic baseline of 1-in-10,000 disease prevalence, the bound drops to <0.001pp. For DP-SGD fine-tuning GPT-2 on SST-2 sentiment classification, calibrating noise to a worst-case reconstruction risk target of 0.15 using the f-DP bound enables ~20% less noise, increasing test accuracy from 52% to 70% (18pp gain) over RDP-based calibration. Across all eight Census granularity levels, the unifying bound indicates up to 33% lower worst-case reconstruction risk than the zCDP-based prior method.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Cohen and Nissim's (2020) PSO bounds (average-dataset threat model), the paper's SPSO bound under the strong-adversary model consistently shows lower risk, providing meaningful guarantees even in the ε∈[10,20] regime where PSO bounds become vacuous, and saturating only around ε≈35. Against Balle et al.'s (2022) RDP and zCDP-based reconstruction robustness bounds, the f-DP unifying bound always yields lower risk estimates across all noise levels and baseline values tested (Figures 3 and 4), with the gap widening at higher ε. Against Guo et al.'s (2023) Fano's inequality-based SAI bounds for discrete priors, the paper's specialized Bernoulli-prior f-DP bound (Theorem D.1) outperforms for all noise parameters (σ∈{0.5, 1.0, 2.0}) and all baseline values, as shown in Figure 5. The practical consequence is that calibrating DP-SGD noise to a target risk level using the f-DP bound requires ~20% less noise than RDP-based calibration, translating to an 18pp accuracy gain on SST-2 without any additional computational cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the f-DP (hypothesis-testing) characterization of differential privacy (Dong et al., 2022) and applies it to unify three previously separate risk analyses: predicate singling out (Cohen and Nissim, 2020), and reconstruction/attribute inference robustness (Balle et al., 2022). Prior work analyzed these risks using different tools—(ε,δ)-DP for singling out, Rényi DP and zero-concentrated DP for reconstruction—yielding inconsistent and overly pessimistic bounds. The key departure is showing that under the strong-adversary threat model, all three risks obey the same bound of the form succ ≤ 1 − f(base), where f is the f-DP trade-off curve, and that this unified form is strictly tighter than prior per-risk analyses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is concrete: calibrating DP-SGD noise to a target risk level using the f-DP bound reduces required noise by ~20%, translating to an 18pp accuracy gain on SST-2 sentiment classification, and the US 2020 Census analysis shows up to 33% lower worst-case reconstruction risk than prior zCDP-based methods. Open questions include whether the unified bound is tight in the most general case (the authors conjecture yes but leave it unproven), how to derive application-specific baseline risk models (e.g., for medical data releases), and the risk of 'baseline gaming' where practitioners select artificially low baselines to make risk appear smaller. The paper also notes that for language models trained on open internet data, the privacy unit may need to be a document rather than an individual, complicating interpretation.
