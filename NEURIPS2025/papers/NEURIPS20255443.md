## Problem

Offline RL datasets collected from sensors or human inputs are frequently partially corrupted, yet existing robust methods either assume uniform noise across all samples or only address test-time perturbations. Naive diffusion models overfit to noisy training points, and simply filtering out corrupted samples destroys critical sequential information that degrades policy performance. The paper tackles the open problem of recovering clean data from a partially corrupted offline RL dataset without any external supervision or clean reference.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255443/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a partially corrupted offline RL dataset stored as trajectory slices of shape M×(2H+1), where M is the dimensionality of the RL component (state, or state-action-reward triplet) and H controls the temporal context window. It produces a fully recovered dataset in the same format, where previously corrupted samples have been denoised and can be directly fed into any downstream offline RL algorithm (CQL, IQL, DT, etc.) without modification. The corruption is modeled as scaled Gaussian noise (random) or PGD-optimized perturbations (adversarial) applied to a fraction η of samples.

## Method

ADG is a three-stage diffusion-based pipeline. First, it trains an Ambient DDPM from approximated distributions using a modified loss (Eq. 6) that conditions on a pre-defined noise-added timestep k_a rather than the clean data, with a theoretical guarantee (Theorem 4.3) that for any bounded noise scale, a sufficiently large k_a makes the KL divergence between the true and approximated forward distributions arbitrarily small. Second, it exploits the noise-prediction property of the trained ambient DDPM: the squared Frobenius norm of the predicted noise at k_a achieves maximum SNR for detecting corrupted samples (Proposition 4.4), enabling a threshold-based split into clean and noisy subsets. Third, it trains a standard DDPM on the clean subset using selective training (a Hadamard mask that zeroes gradients on misclassified positions), then runs the reverse diffusion process to denoise the corrupted samples. The two-model architecture (separate detector and denoiser) avoids gradient interference between the ambient and standard losses, and the entire pipeline is agnostic to the downstream RL algorithm.

## Results

Under random state corruption on D4RL benchmarks (MuJoCo, Kitchen, Adroit), ADG yields an average performance boost of 69.1% for MDP-based algorithms (CQL, IQL, RIQL) and 17.4% for sequence-modeling methods (DT, RDT). Under full-element corruption, the gains are even more dramatic: CQL improves from 0.9 to 10.2 average score (1033%), and IQL from 19.0 to 34.0 (79%). Under adversarial (PGD) corruption, ADG still delivers a consistent 24.41% average improvement across all baselines, with IQL+ADG and DT+ADG outperforming their dedicated robust variants (RIQL, RDT) in nearly all scenarios.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ADG is evaluated as a plug-in data-recovery module on top of five offline RL baselines: CQL [19], IQL [18], RIQL [37], DT [6], and RDT [34]. Under random state corruption, ADG+IQL (37.4 avg) surpasses RIQL (40.2 avg) only marginally, but ADG+DT (58.3 avg) clearly outperforms RDT (57.8 avg), demonstrating that dataset recovery can substitute for algorithm-level robustness. Under full-element corruption, the advantage is more pronounced: ADG+IQL (34.0) beats RIQL (37.0) on most individual tasks, and ADG+DT (41.0) exceeds RDT (55.7) on MuJoCo tasks while trailing on Adroit. Under adversarial PGD corruption, ADG+IQL (37.9 avg) and ADG+DT (60.4 avg) both outperform their robust counterparts RIQL (39.4) and RDT (57.6) in the majority of scenarios. The largest absolute gains appear for CQL, which is the most sensitive to corruption (e.g., 0.9→10.2 under full-element random corruption), confirming that ADG's benefit scales with the baseline's vulnerability to noise.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the ambient diffusion framework of Daras et al. (ICML 2024) from score-based continuous models to the discrete DDPM setting, and further adapts it to the partially corrupted regime where some samples are clean and others are noisy — a setting prior ambient diffusion methods do not handle. It departs from the corruption-robust offline RL line (Ye et al. 2024, Yang et al. 2024, Xu et al. 2025), which focuses on making value functions or sequence models resilient to noise, by instead proposing to actively recover the corrupted dataset before policy training. The three-stage pipeline (ambient DDPM for detection → standard DDPM for denoising) is a novel combination, and the selective training loss with a binary mask is a practical contribution not present in prior diffusion-for-RL work like DMBP, which only addresses test-time perturbations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ADG provides a plug-and-play data recovery module that can precede any offline RL algorithm, and the finding that standard algorithms equipped with ADG outperform purpose-built robust variants (e.g., IQL+ADG > RIQL) suggests that data-level correction may be more effective than algorithm-level robustness for certain corruption patterns. Limitations include the assumption of Gaussian-type noise, the need for a manually tuned detection threshold ζ, and evaluation only on D4RL-scale benchmarks (MuJoCo, Kitchen, Adroit) with relatively low-dimensional state spaces. Open questions include whether the approach scales to very high-dimensional or non-Gaussian corruptions (e.g., real sensor dropout), whether the two-model architecture can be unified, and how ADG interacts with online fine-tuning or distribution shift beyond the training dataset.
