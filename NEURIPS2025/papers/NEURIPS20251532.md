## Problem

ISP pipelines are designed for human perceptual quality, causing nonlinear radiance distortion that degrades machine-vision performance, especially in low-light where noise amplification is severe. Existing raw-based methods either transmit full 48-bpp RAW images (impractical for edge-to-cloud deployment) or require substantial architectural changes to detectors or in-camera ISP modules, breaking compatibility with mainstream sRGB pipelines. This paper asks whether a minimal, compact raw-metadata stream can guide sRGB refinement for machine vision without altering existing architectures or transmission infrastructure.

## Input / Output

At the imaging stage, the method consumes a paired RAW sensor image (48 bpp) and its ISP-processed sRGB-JPEG counterpart (~0.15 bpp), both resized to 512×512. It produces two outputs: a compressed raw-metadata latent (under 0.001 bpp, i.e., less than 0.5% overhead over the sRGB image) and a refined sRGB image in the same format as the input. The refined sRGB is then fed unchanged into any existing object-detection network (YOLOv3, Faster R-CNN, CenterNet), making the scheme fully plug-and-play with no modification to downstream model architecture.

## Method

CRM-IR comprises three jointly trained components. First, a Cross-Modal Contextual Entropy (CMCE) encoder extracts a latent representation from the RAW image while using the paired sRGB image as a contextual prior, exploiting the cross-modal redundancy between the two to compress the metadata via a hyperprior + autoregressive entropy model inherited from learned image compression. Second, a lightweight Machine Vision-oriented Image Refinement (MV-IR) module performs a two-stage pixel-level correction: an inverse mapping that predicts an image-wise gamma parameter from the metadata to undo ISP gamma correction and recover a linear radiance representation, followed by a remapping stage that learns separate pixel-wise modulation maps for low-light and normal-light regions (selected via luminance-based bypass filters) and applies them to the linear image through lightweight fusion layers. The entire pipeline is trained end-to-end with a Lagrangian objective balancing the downstream detection loss against the metadata bitrate, requiring no changes to the detector backbone or the in-camera ISP.

## Results

On the LOD dataset (in-dataset validation) with YOLOv3, CRM-IR achieves 42.14 mAP, 69.11 mAP50, and 46.02 mAP75, outperforming all sRGB-based and raw-based baselines. On the cross-dataset RID benchmark, it reaches 51.72 mAP and 63.85 mAP75, substantially exceeding every competing method. The additional raw-metadata transmission cost is only 4.88×10⁻⁴ bpp (YOLOv3), 3.26×10⁻⁴ bpp (Faster R-CNN), and 1.38×10⁻³ bpp (CenterNet), all well under 0.001 bpp and negligible relative to the 0.15 bpp of a standard JPEG sRGB image.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CRM-IR outperforms all seven baselines on both in-dataset (LOD) and cross-dataset (RID) evaluations across all three detector backbones. On LOD with YOLOv3, it gains +2.14 mAP over the unenhanced YOLOv3 baseline, +0.95 over Zero-DCE, +0.92 over KinD, +1.14 over YOLA, and +0.54 over the raw-based RAOD. The cross-dataset gap is far more pronounced: CRM-IR leads RAOD by +11.98 mAP and YOLA by +13.43 mAP on RID, indicating that raw-metadata-guided refinement generalizes far better than methods relying on full raw access or sRGB-only enhancement. Notably, RAOD achieves the highest mAP50 on LOD (70.49) thanks to full 48 bpp raw access, but CRM-IR matches or exceeds its overall mAP while transmitting only ~0.0005 bpp of metadata instead of the entire raw image. On the CenterNet backbone, CRM-IR slightly trails RAOD on LOD mAP (42.05 vs. 42.89) but still surpasses it on RID mAP75 (44.91 vs. 43.22), confirming consistent cross-dataset superiority.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of learned image compression (LIC) and raw-based machine vision. It directly builds on the analysis-synthesis + hyperprior entropy coding framework of Ballé et al. (ICLR 2017, 2018), repurposing it not for image reconstruction but for compressing a small raw-metadata stream. Conceptually, it departs from prior raw-based detection methods (RAOD, Raw-Adapter, AdaptiveISP) that either consume the full 48-bpp raw image or retrain ISP modules, by extracting only a sub-millibit metadata vector and using it to condition a lightweight sRGB refinement module. The cross-modal contextual entropy encoder (CMCE) is a novel twist on LIC: the sRGB image serves as a free contextual prior for compressing the raw latent, exploiting the semantic correspondence between the two modalities rather than spatial or channel context within a single image.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is in deployment: by reducing the raw-image transmission cost from 48 bpp to under 0.001 bpp while retaining most of the detection benefit, CRM-IR makes raw-domain information accessible in bandwidth-constrained edge-to-cloud pipelines without altering existing detector architectures or ISP hardware. The RID dataset (500 paired RAW/sRGB samples, 8 categories, extremely low-light) fills a gap in open-source benchmarks for cross-dataset generalization. Open questions include whether the paradigm extends to segmentation or tracking tasks, how it behaves under diverse ISP pipelines (the authors note this as future work), and whether the modest 1–2 mAP gains over sRGB baselines persist at scale or under more challenging conditions. The paper does not report inference latency or error bars, leaving the real-time viability and statistical robustness of the gains unverified.
