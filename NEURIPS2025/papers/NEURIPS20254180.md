## Problem

Fully-connected feedforward layers (FFLs) are the dominant source of parameter count and quadratic-time computation in Transformers, NeRFs, and other modern architectures, hindering real-time deployment. Existing random-feature and kernel-based approximations are restricted to positive-definite kernels or require the Fourier transform of the activation function, making them inapplicable to common unbounded activations like ReLU. No prior method provides an unbiased, general-purpose approximation of FFLs with arbitrary polynomial activations while simultaneously reducing inference from quadratic to linear time.

## Input / Output

The EUGen layer consumes an input vector x ∈ R^d (augmented with its L2 norm ‖x‖₂) and a weight matrix W ∈ R^{l×d} (each row augmented with a constant 1), and produces a dense output vector of length l. The key structural difference from a standard FFL is that the input norm ‖x‖₂ enters the computation directly through the augmented input, enabling the layer to model functions beyond the scope of f(Wx). In practice the random-feature dimension m is chosen well below min(d, l) (e.g., m ≤ 128 while d can be thousands), which is what yields the linear-in-d inference cost.

## Method

EUGens disentangle the weight and input pathways by applying independent random-feature projections (matrices G_j^i ∈ C^{m×(d+1)}) to each, then recombining them through a single dot product: EUGen^k(w,x) = g(w)ᵀ f(x), where f and g are element-wise nonlinear maps applied to concatenated Hadamard products of the projected vectors up to order k. The Hadamard products generate monomial features whose expectation reproduces the polynomial activation f(Wx) exactly (Theorem 3.1), yielding the first unbiased random-feature approximation of FFLs with arbitrary polynomial activations—no Fourier transform of f is needed. Inference complexity drops from O(d² + dl) to O(mdk² + ml), linear in d when m ≪ min(d,l), and further to O(mdk + ml) if the G matrices are shared across orders. Variance is reduced via Quasi-Monte-Carlo sampling with Gaussian orthogonal matrices, and a layer-wise distillation procedure with a closed-form solution allows EUGens to replace FFLs in pre-trained models without backpropagation.

## Results

EUGens achieve up to 27% inference speed improvement (Mip-NeRF 360) and up to 30% memory/parameter reduction across diverse tasks while maintaining comparable quality metrics. For NeRF, inference time is reduced by 24% with virtually no PSNR loss; for iSDF, inference is 22.6% faster and training is 5% faster with comparable reconstruction accuracy. In ViT-based image classification (ImageNet, Places365), replacing six FFL layers with EUGens reduces inference parameters by nearly 30% while matching vanilla ViT accuracy. In GPT-2 LLM pre-training, EUGens achieve validation loss comparable to vanilla GPT-2 while using a significantly smaller fraction of inference parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EUGens consistently outperform or match all baselines across tasks. In FFL approximation (Fig. 2), EUGens achieve significantly lower MSE than both SNNK [80] and Low-Rank baselines for ReLU, Softplus, and GELU activations with the same parameter budget. In LLM pre-training (Fig. 3), EUGens match vanilla GPT-2 validation loss while using far fewer inference parameters than Low-Rank GPT-2 variants, which show a steeper accuracy-parameter tradeoff. In ViT image classification (Fig. 4), EUGens achieve the best accuracy-vs-inference-parameter tradeoff curve, closely matching vanilla ViT while Low-Rank baselines require more parameters for comparable accuracy. In 3D reconstruction (Fig. 5), EUGen-NeRF and EUGen-Mip-NeRF achieve similar PSNR to their vanilla counterparts while being 24–27% faster, outperforming the speed-accuracy tradeoff of the original architectures. For iSDF (Fig. 7), EUGen-iSDF is 22.6% faster at inference and 5% faster at training with comparable reconstruction quality. The knowledge distillation variant (Fig. 8) recovers original NeRF quality with up to 26% speedup without any backpropagation-based retraining, a capability not available to the other baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

EUGens build directly on the random features framework of Rahimi and Recht [73] and the Universal Random Feature (URF) / Scalable Neural Network Kernel (SNNK) approach of Sehanobish et al. [80], which first disentangled weights from inputs in MLP layers. The key departure is that EUGens remove the Fourier-transform requirement that limited URF to bounded activations, enabling unbiased approximation of arbitrary polynomial activations (and by Bernstein's theorem, any continuous activation) with explicit concentration bounds. The QMC variant further draws on Gaussian orthogonal matrices [119] for variance reduction, and the overall design unifies several previously separate efficient-FFL extensions (low-rank, JLT-based, kernel-based) under a single parameterized family.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is broad: a single layer replacement yields 24–27% inference speedups and ~30% memory reductions across GPT-2, ViT, NeRF, Mip-NeRF 360, D-NeRF, Zip-NeRF, and iSDF without retraining from scratch (via the closed-form distillation). The theoretical contribution—first unbiased Monte Carlo approximation of FFLs with polynomial activations plus Azuma-based tail bounds—gives practitioners a principled way to choose the number of random features m for a target error. Open questions include how the approach behaves at much larger scales (the experiments top out at ~124M parameters), whether higher-order k (>2) ever becomes necessary for very deep or very wide layers, and whether the norm-dependence extension (||x||₂) can be exploited for tasks beyond standard FFLs.
