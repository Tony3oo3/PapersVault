## Problem

Existing controllable CAD generation methods can modify local parts but cannot follow textual geometric instructions, while text-to-CAD methods can follow instructions but generate entire models from scratch rather than targeting specific local regions. This leaves a gap: no prior method can simultaneously focus on a designated local part and adhere to user-specified geometric constraints (e.g., 'isosceles right triangle' or 'rectangle with one corner cut off') while keeping the rest of the model unchanged.

## Input / Output

The method takes three inputs: (1) an original CAD model represented in FlexCAD's hierarchical textual format (a contiguous string of side types and vertex coordinates per loop), (2) a designated local loop to be modified, and (3) a free-text geometric instruction. The output is a predicted 2D loop (side types + vertex coordinates) that replaces the masked part and is seamlessly integrated with the remaining loops to form a valid new CAD model. Local parts are split into 'simple' (triangles, quadrilaterals, circles, sectors — ~50% of the corpus) and 'complex' (intricate multi-segment patterns), each requiring different captioning and evaluation strategies.

## Method

GeoCAD introduces a complementary captioning pipeline to solve the training-data bottleneck: simple parts are annotated via vertex-based geometric analysis (computing side lengths, angles, and classifying shapes like rhombus vs. square from coordinates), while complex parts are rendered as 2D images and captioned by Qwen2.5-VL-72B-Instruct, yielding ~221k annotated local parts. Training proceeds in two stages on Llama-3-8B with LoRA (rank 8, alpha 32): an optional pre-training stage aligns CAD-specific coordinate representations with textual descriptions using augmented samples (translation, scaling, rotation, reflection), followed by an instruction fine-tuning stage where a random local loop is masked and the LLM must predict it autoregressively given the geometric instruction and all remaining visible loops. The key distinction from FlexCAD is that geometric instructions are explicitly incorporated as constraints in the training prompt, enabling the model to follow shape specifications rather than merely completing a masked region.

## Results

On the DeepCAD test set, GeoCAD achieves 90.5% Prediction Validity (PV), 76.4% Ver-score, 65.7% VLLM-score, and 40.9% Realism, substantially outperforming both baselines across all metrics. With five-shot prompting, GeoCAD further improves to 92.3% PV, 82.2% Ver-score, 68.2% VLLM-score, and 43.6% Realism. In terms of generation quality, GeoCAD attains the best MMD (1.13) and JSD (0.98), indicating closer distributional match to ground-truth CAD models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GeoCAD is compared against two baselines: OpenAI-o3 (a powerful closed-source LLM used with five-shot prompting) and FlexCAD (the state-of-the-art local CAD generation method via LLM fine-tuning, also tested with and without five-shot). GeoCAD decisively outperforms FlexCAD (five-shot) on text-to-CAD consistency metrics: Ver-score improves from 43.5% to 76.4% (+32.9 pp), VLLM-score from 26.8% to 65.7% (+38.9 pp), and Realism from 20.2% to 40.9% (+20.7 pp). Against OpenAI-o3 (five-shot), GeoCAD leads on every metric, with the largest gaps in PV (90.5% vs. 65.7%) and VLLM-score (65.7% vs. 22.1%). The paper attributes FlexCAD's weakness to its lack of geometric constraints during training, causing it to frequently generate generic shapes (circles, rectangles) that ignore the specified instructions. Five-shot prompting benefits both GeoCAD and FlexCAD, but GeoCAD's advantage is consistent across all evaluation dimensions.

## Relation to Prior Work

GeoCAD builds directly on FlexCAD's masking paradigm for local CAD generation and its textual representation of CAD models, extending it by incorporating geometric instructions as explicit constraints during LLM fine-tuning. It departs from the broader text-to-CAD literature (CAD-Editor, Text2CAD, CAD-GPT) by focusing on local part modification rather than whole-model generation from scratch, and from prior controllable CAD methods (SkexGen, HNC-CAD) by adding the ability to follow natural-language geometric instructions. The complementary captioning pipeline—combining vertex-based geometric analysis for simple parts with VLLM-based image captioning for complex parts—is a novel data annotation strategy that addresses the previously unaddressed gap of geometric instruction data for local CAD elements.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GeoCAD establishes local geometry-controllable CAD generation as a viable task, with the complementary captioning strategy providing a scalable recipe (~221k annotated parts) that other CAD generation work can adopt. Key limitations include reliance on a single dataset (DeepCAD), text-only instructions that may be insufficient for highly complex shapes, and failure cases when modifying structurally critical parts that other parts depend on. The authors identify multimodal input (text + hand-drawn sketches) as a natural next step, and the absence of inter-loop constraint annotations in DeepCAD limits sketch-level editing to heuristic workarounds.
