## Problem

Existing logits-based detectors such as DetectGPT and Fast-DetectGPT rely on raw log-probabilities (i.e., an identity witness function) to distinguish human from LLM-generated text, which the paper shows can be fundamentally sub-optimal—there exist settings where the detection statistic vanishes regardless of the true distributional distance between human and machine text. Moreover, the literature on these detectors largely lacks systematic finite-sample statistical guarantees on standard classification metrics (TNR, FNR, TPR, FPR).

## Input / Output

The method takes a text passage X (a token sequence of length L), the token-level log-probabilities from a source LLM q', and an unlabeled corpus of n human-authored passages for training. It produces a scalar detection statistic T_w(X) and a binary label (LLM-generated if T_w(X) > z_α, where z_α is the α-quantile of the standard normal). The witness function w is learned entirely from the human corpus without any labels, and the threshold is fixed by the chosen FNR level α, making the classifier tunable across the ROC curve without retraining.

## Method

AdaDetectGPT modifies Fast-DetectGPT's statistic by applying a one-dimensional witness function w to each token's log-probability before computing the normalized sum, and by setting the centering distribution to the source LLM q itself so the statistic matches a martingale partial-sum form. The witness function is learned by maximizing a lower bound on the TNR derived via the martingale central limit theorem; this bound separates the significance level α from w, so the optimal w is α-independent and the optimization reduces to solving a single linear system Σβ = ψ when w is parameterized with B-spline basis functions. The classification threshold is set to z_α via the MCLT, yielding asymptotic FNR control at level α. The paper further establishes finite-sample error bounds on FNR (Theorem 2), TNR (Theorem 3), TPR (Corollary 4), and FPR (Corollary 5), and extends the framework to black-box settings where the target LLM's logits are unavailable by substituting an open-source proxy model.

## Results

In the white-box setting, AdaDetectGPT achieves the highest AUC across all 15 dataset–source-model combinations, outperforming the best baseline (Fast-DetectGPT) by 12.5%–37% in relative AUC improvement (e.g., 0.9233 vs. 0.8886 on WritingPrompts, 0.8864 vs. 0.8571 on SQuAD). In the black-box setting (detecting GPT-4o and Claude-3.5-Haiku text), AdaDetectGPT consistently improves over Fast-DetectGPT, with average AUC gains of ~4–7% and up to 37.8% on the Essay dataset. Under adversarial attacks, AdaDetectGPT shows up to 10% improvement over Fast-DetectGPT for paraphrasing and up to 85% for decoherence.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AdaDetectGPT uniformly outperforms all nine baselines across every dataset and source model tested. Against the closest competitor Fast-DetectGPT (Bao et al., 2024), it achieves relative AUC gains of 12.5%–37% in white-box settings and up to 37.8% in black-box settings. The older logits-based detectors—GLTR variants (Likelihood, Entropy, LogRank; Gehrmann et al., 2019), DetectLLM variants (LRR, NPR; Su et al., 2023), DetectGPT (Mitchell et al., 2023), and DNA-GPT (Yang et al., 2024)—are all substantially outperformed, with AUC gaps of 10–40 points. In the black-box setting, AdaDetectGPT also surpasses the ML-based baselines RoBERTa (Solaiman et al., 2019), Binoculars (Hans et al., 2024), RADAR (Hu et al., 2023), and BiScope (Guo et al., 2024), achieving average AUC of 0.9589 (GPT-4o) and 0.9478 (Claude-3.5) versus 0.9552 and 0.9440 for Binoculars, the strongest ML baseline. Notably, Fast-DetectGPT occasionally underperforms Binoculars or RADAR in black-box settings, but AdaDetectGPT consistently recovers and exceeds those scores.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DetectGPT → Fast-DetectGPT lineage of logits-based detectors by introducing an adaptively learned witness function w applied to token-level log-probabilities, transforming the detection statistic from a fixed curvature measure into a data-driven one. Conceptually, it bridges statistics-based detection (which uses predefined log-probability statistics) and ML-based detection (which learns from external data) by learning w from a human-authored corpus while retaining the zero-shot, model-agnostic structure of the original statistic. The key theoretical departure is connecting the detection statistic to martingale central limit theory, enabling closed-form FNR control and a TNR lower bound that decouples the witness function from the significance level α — a connection absent from prior logits-based work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to provide finite-sample statistical guarantees (for FNR, TNR, TPR, FPR) for logits-based LLM text detectors, filling a notable gap in a field that has been largely empirical. The practical recipe — solve a linear system to obtain the witness function — is lightweight (sub-minute training, <0.5 GB memory) and consistently improves AUC by 12–37% over Fast-DetectGPT across diverse datasets and model sizes. Open questions include whether the approach degrades under more sophisticated adversarial attacks (paraphrasing, paraphrase chains), how it behaves on very short texts where the MCLT approximation may be poor, and whether the equal-variance condition underlying the TNR bound holds in practice for modern LLMs with highly skewed token distributions.
