## Problem

RoPE, the de facto standard position encoding in modern LLMs, applies a data-independent rotation that depends only on relative position, constraining RoPE-based transformers to the TC^0 complexity class and causing failures on sequential reasoning tasks such as flip-flop language modeling and state tracking. The paper asks whether a data-dependent multiplicative position encoding can overcome these theoretical and empirical limitations while remaining hardware-efficient for large-scale training.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard token embeddings projected into query, key, value, and Householder direction vectors (w_t) with a scalar gate β_t = 2·sigmoid(u^T x_t + b), all derived from the current input. The attention logit between positions i and j is computed as k_j^T (∏_{s=j+1}^{i} H_s) q_i, where each H_s = I − β_s w_s w_s^T is a data-dependent identity-plus-rank-one matrix, making the positional encoding path-dependent and input-conditioned rather than a fixed rotation. The output is standard next-token prediction, but the attention mechanism itself encodes cumulative, input-specific transformations along the sequence path.

## Method

PaTH replaces RoPE's static rotation with a cumulative product of data-dependent Householder-like transformations H_t = I − β_t w_t w_t^T, where w_t and β_t are functions of the current input, enabling the attention logit to dynamically adapt to sequence content. The key computational challenge—computing products of Householder matrices over arbitrary intervals—is solved via the UT transform, which provides a compact representation that enables a FlashAttention-style blockwise algorithm with O(L²d + Ld²/B) complexity matching standard attention. Theoretically, a one-layer PaTH transformer with two heads and log n precision can solve an NC^1-complete problem under AC^0 reductions, provably extending transformers beyond TC^0. The method is composable with other attention modifications: PaTH-FoX combines PaTH's multiplicative encoding with FoX's additive forget gates, yielding further gains especially in length extrapolation. For inference, historical keys are updated in-place using the current timestep's transition matrix, making decoding compatible with existing kernels like FlashDecoding and PagedAttention.

## Results

On 760M-parameter language models trained on 50B tokens, PaTH achieves the best average zero-shot commonsense reasoning accuracy (54.2%) and lowest LAMBADA perplexity (16.79) among all variants, outperforming RoPE (52.7% avg, 19.77 ppl) and FoX (53.1% avg, 18.28 ppl). PaTH-FoX achieves the lowest Wikitext perplexity (17.35) and best HellaSwag accuracy (52.2%). On the flip-flop language modeling synthetic task, PaTH achieves near-perfect performance (0% error in-distribution, 0.0001% sparse OOD) while RoPE, SBA, and FoX all fail (6.9–9.6% ID error, 36–40% sparse OOD error). On long-context benchmarks, PaTH-FoX achieves 93.8% on PhoneBook at 4K context and 22.6% on RULER at 16K, substantially outperforming RoPE (15.6% and 0.0% respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PaTH consistently outperforms RoPE across all standard language modeling benchmarks: it reduces LAMBADA perplexity from 19.77 to 16.79 (−15.1%), improves average commonsense reasoning accuracy from 52.7% to 54.2%, and achieves the best scores on HellaSwag (51.5 vs 50.3), WinoGrande (56.0 vs 54.9), ARC-easy (68.9 vs 67.2), and ARC-challenge (34.4 vs 33.3). Against FoX, PaTH wins on most tasks (LAMBADA ppl 16.79 vs 18.28, ARC-e 68.9 vs 65.7, ARC-c 34.4 vs 32.6) while FoX edges ahead on WinoGrande (57.1 vs 56.0) and PiQA (70.8 vs 70.5); the combined PaTH-FoX variant achieves the best perplexity overall. On the flip-flop language modeling diagnostic task, PaTH achieves 0% in-distribution error versus 6.9% for RoPE, 9.6% for SBA, and 8.3% for FoX, and generalizes to out-of-distribution settings (0.0001% sparse OOD) where all baselines collapse to 36–40% error. On long-context retrieval (PhoneBook), PaTH-FoX dramatically outperforms all baselines (89.6–93.8% at 2–4K vs 32.3–62.5% for RoPE/FoX), while on RULER variable tracking, PaTH and PaTH-FoX achieve substantial gains over both RoPE and FoX at 8K and 16K context lengths.

## Relation to Prior Work

PaTH generalizes RoPE's static block-diagonal rotation matrices into data-dependent Householder-like (identity-plus-rank-one) transformations, positioning RoPE as a special case within a broader class of multiplicative position encodings. The design is motivated by the linear RNN literature (DeltaNet, Mamba2, GLA), where unrolling the recurrence yields the same cumulative-product structure that PaTH embeds into softmax attention. For efficient computation, the paper adapts the UT transform from numerical linear algebra (Joffrain et al., 2006) to obtain a compact representation of Householder products, and wraps this in a FlashAttention-style blockwise algorithm. The PaTH-FoX variant further composes the mechanism with the Forgetting Transformer's data-dependent forget gates, paralleling how Gated DeltaNet combines DeltaNet with Mamba2 in the linear-attention setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The provable extension of transformer expressivity from TC0 to NC1-complete problems (under AC0 reductions) is the strongest theoretical contribution, and the empirical validation on flip-flop language modeling and A5 word problems confirms that this is not merely a circuit-complexity artifact. Practically, the FlashAttention-compatible algorithm and the RoPE-to-PaTH distillation recipe lower the barrier to adoption for existing LLMs. Key open questions include: whether the BF16 numerical instability (requiring β-clipping) can be resolved for larger head dimensions, whether the in-place KV-cache update can be extended to value vectors, and whether compositions of reflections can recover the rotational inductive biases that RoPE provides. The mixed results from distilling already-overtrained models (Qwen2.5) suggest that conversion timing and recipe design remain unsolved practical challenges.
