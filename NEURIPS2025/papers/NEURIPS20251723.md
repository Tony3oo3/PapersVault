## Problem

Classical optimal transport enforces strict mass conservation, which is impractical in real-world resource allocation where supply and demand are inherently mismatched. While unbalanced OT (UOT) relaxes this via penalty functions, existing theory is developed almost exclusively for smooth, superlinear divergences (KL, entropy); total variation (TV) penalties—piecewise linear and non-superlinear—remain largely unexplored, especially in the weak OT setting where cost is evaluated over disintegrations rather than point-to-point couplings. This paper fills that gap by establishing a rigorous theoretical framework for unbalanced weak OT with TV penalties, motivated by spatial resource allocation tasks such as facility placement and service-area partitioning.

## Input / Output

The method takes a continuous demand measure μ₁ (absolutely continuous w.r.t. Lebesgue on a subset of ℝⁿ), a discrete supply measure μ₂ = Σᵢ mᵢδ_{yᵢ} with M support points, a radial cost function c with compact sublevels, asymmetric newsvendor penalty parameters (a₁,b₁,a₂,b₂) encoding over/under-supply costs, and a transport-cost scaling k. The output is an optimal transport plan γ that decomposes as γ = Σᵢ γ₁⌞Cᵢ(w) ⊗ δ_{yᵢ}, a Laguerre cell partition determined by dual weights w ∈ [−b₂,+∞)ᴹ, and—under the quantization extension—jointly optimized facility locations and masses. A distinctive feature is that the TV penalty induces a singular component in the optimal plan's first marginal (absent in entropy-regularized UOT), and the dual objective G(w) is piecewise linear in each wᵢ, yielding explicit subgradients rather than smooth gradients.

## Method

The core contribution is a new unbalanced weak OT formulation where the marginal-mismatch penalty is a newsvendor-type (piecewise linear) loss rather than a smooth divergence, combined with a weak cost C(x₁,p) evaluated over the disintegration of the transport plan. In the general setting, the paper establishes existence of optimal plans and a strong duality theorem via Fenchel conjugation of the ET functional, introducing auxiliary functions Iᵢ and Jᵢ that are piecewise linear (unlike the smooth entropy conjugates in prior work). In the semi-discrete regime, a key structural result (Theorem 3.3) recasts the infinite-dimensional dual as a finite-dimensional maximization over Laguerre weights w, with the objective G(w) admitting an explicit integral over generalized Laguerre cells. The optimality conditions (Theorem 3.4) reveal that the optimal plan's first marginal has both an absolutely continuous part (density 1 w.r.t. μ₁ outside the residual set R) and a singular part supported where the effective cost cʷ(x) hits the threshold −b₁—a consequence of the TV penalty's kink. For the quantization problem, the paper provides a sequential strategy: masses are set to cell volumes mᵢ* = μ₁(Cᵢ(0)) (Theorem 3.5), then locations are updated via explicit gradient expressions (Proposition 3.2) that integrate ∇ᵧc over the active portion of each cell, enabling efficient gradient-based optimization in finite dimensions.

## Results

This is a theoretical paper with no standard benchmark comparisons. The main contributions are existence (Theorem 3.1), a dual formulation (Theorem 3.2), a finite-dimensional tessellation reformulation (Theorem 3.3), explicit optimality conditions (Theorem 3.4), and a quantization result with closed-form optimal masses (Theorem 3.5). Two numerical illustrations confirm practical viability: the supply-area division example converges in 0.9 s over 1000 subgradient iterations, and the spatial resource allocation example (2000 demand points, 4 depots) converges in 2.6 s, leaving approximately 5% of demand in the residual set R due to excessive transport cost.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not include a head-to-head comparison against competing methods, as its primary contribution is theoretical rather than algorithmic. It extends two prior lines of work: (1) Bourne et al. [15], which handles semi-discrete UOT with smooth entropy-based penalties, is generalized here to the non-smooth, piecewise-linear TV (newsvendor) penalty, yielding a singular component in the optimal plan's first marginal that has no analogue in the entropy-regularized case; (2) Chung and Trinh [22], which establishes weak OT duality under superlinear convex penalties on compact spaces, is extended to non-compact Polish spaces with non-superlinear TV penalties via a different proof strategy (Fenchel conjugate + Prokhorov compactness). The numerical examples serve as feasibility demonstrations rather than competitive benchmarks, and no baseline transport solver (e.g., Sinkhorn, entropic UOT) is run for direct comparison.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three OT generalization axes: weak transport (relaxing exact target-marginal matching via disintegration-based costs), unbalanced transport (penalizing mass creation/deletion), and non-superlinear regularization. It extends Chung and Trinh's [22] weak optimal entropy transport duality from compact spaces with superlinear convex penalties to non-compact spaces with piecewise-linear TV (newsvendor) penalties, and extends Bourne et al.'s [15] semi-discrete UOT framework from smooth entropy-based penalties to the non-smooth TV regime. The resulting formulation is the first to simultaneously integrate weak couplings, mass imbalance, TV-type regularization, and semi-discrete geometry, with the newsvendor loss providing an economically interpretable alternative to statistical divergences.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical contribution is significant for OT theory: establishing existence, strong duality, and explicit Laguerre-tessellation-based optimality conditions for a non-smooth, non-superlinear penalty class fills a genuine gap, since standard convex-analysis tools (Fenchel duality with superlinear growth) do not apply directly. The explicit subgradient expressions (Propositions 3.1–3.2) make the framework computationally actionable for facility-location and service-area-partitioning problems, reducing an infinite-dimensional problem to finite-dimensional gradient-based optimization. Limitations include the reliance on radial cost functions in the semi-discrete results, the small-scale numerical demonstrations, and the sequential (rather than joint) optimization of locations and weights in the quantization procedure, which may miss global optima. Open questions include convergence guarantees for the gradient-based solvers, extension to non-radial costs, and scaling to high-dimensional or large-M settings.
