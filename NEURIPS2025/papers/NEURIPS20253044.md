## Problem

Traditional scaling laws (e.g., Chinchilla) fit only the final loss at the end of each training run, discarding all intermediate loss values and failing to model the sharp loss drop that occurs during learning rate annealing. This underutilizes training compute, requires launching many separate runs to collect data points, and provides no predictive power over the full training trajectory or across unseen learning rate schedules.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253044/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a learning rate schedule (defining η_i at every step), the training step index s, and optionally model size N; S1 and S2 are computed analytically from the schedule, so no additional training data is needed at prediction time. Fitting requires only the validation loss curve from one or two short training runs (e.g., 20K steps under constant and cosine LRS), from which the four parameters (L0, A, C, α) are estimated via Huber loss minimization on log-loss. The output is the predicted validation loss at every step of any arbitrary LRS, enabling full-curve prediction for unseen schedules (WSD, multi-step cosine, cyclic) at longer horizons (e.g., 60K or 350K steps) with mean prediction error around 0.2%.

## Method

The paper proposes L(s) = L0 + A·S1^(−α) − C·S2, where S1 = Σ η_i is the cumulative forward area under the LR curve (generalizing the Chinchilla power-law term) and S2 = Σ_i Σ_k (η_{k−1} − η_k)·λ^{i−k} is an exponentially weighted annealing area that captures the 'momentum' of LR decay. The key empirical insight is that LR annealing exhibits a delay effect analogous to physical momentum: the loss continues along its prior trajectory for several steps after the LR changes direction, and steeper annealing produces longer delays, motivating the exponential decay factor λ (typically 0.99–0.999). The S2 term is negative during annealing (reducing loss) and positive during re-warmup (increasing loss), making the formulation applicable to continual pre-training. The equation is fitted by minimizing Huber loss on log-loss with L-BFGS, and is extended to model size via an additional B·N^(−β) term and an N^γ multiplier on the S2 term, yielding L(s,N) = L0 + A·S1^(−α) + B·N^(−β) − C·S2·N^γ.

## Results

Fitting Eq. 1 on constant + cosine LRS (20K steps) yields L(s) = 2.628 + 0.429·S₁⁻⁰·⁵⁵⁰ − 0.411·S₂, achieving R² > 0.999 on the full loss curves. Predicting four unseen LRS (cosine 60K, multi-step cosine, WSD, cyclic) with 60K total steps yields a mean prediction error of ~0.2%. The N-extended equation (Eq. 5) achieves R² > 0.998 when predicting the full loss curve of an unseen 1.2B model. In computational cost, the proposed method requires <1.0% of the training compute needed by Chinchilla scaling law to collect 100 fitting points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is the Chinchilla scaling law (Hoffmann et al., 2022), which fits only final-step losses and requires 100 separate training runs (one per data point) under a fixed LRS. The proposed method requires a single training run (or even one or two) and captures the entire loss curve, reducing computational cost from 100% (cosine) or 11.8–21.6% (WSD) to <1.0%. The paper formally proves that its equation reduces to Chinchilla under constant LRS and statistically verifies (Table 1: mean R² = 0.972 for cosine, 0.979 for WSD) that Chinchilla fits the endpoints predicted by the new equation, confirming it is a strict generalization. Additionally, the method outperforms Chinchilla in expressiveness: it predicts full loss curves for unseen LRS (WSD, multi-step cosine, cyclic) with ~0.2% mean error, whereas Chinchilla cannot model intermediate training dynamics at all. The paper also validates and refines empirical findings from Hu et al. (2024) on WSD superiority, Hägele et al. (2024) on optimal annealing ratios, and Gupta et al. (2023) on continual pre-training re-warmup, showing that the S₁/S₂ balance framework explains these results and even overturns the claim that 1-sqrt annealing always outperforms cosine regardless of annealing ratio.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Kaplan (2020) and Chinchilla (Hoffmann et al., 2022) scaling law lineage by adding an LR annealing momentum term (S₂) to the traditional power-law formulation, enabling it to model the full training loss curve rather than only final-step losses. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of S₂ as a cumulative, exponentially-decayed measure of LR annealing, motivated by an observed 'delay' phenomenon between LR changes and loss response. The paper explicitly shows its formulation reduces to the Chinchilla equation under constant LR, positioning it as a strict generalization rather than a competing framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: fitting this equation requires less than 1% of the compute needed for Chinchilla-style scaling law fitting, and the resulting model generalizes to unseen LR schedules (WSD, multi-step cosine, cyclic) with ~0.2% prediction error, offering a cheap tool for pre-selecting training recipes. The S₁/S₂ balance framework provides an intuitive explanation for why WSD outperforms cosine and why optimal annealing ratios exist, overturning the prior claim that 1-sqrt annealing universally dominates cosine. Key limitations include the purely empirical derivation (no closed-form theoretical proof), restriction to text modality, and the unexplained root cause of the LR-loss delay phenomenon, which the authors flag as important future work.
