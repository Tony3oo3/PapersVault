## Problem

Self-supervised visual representation learning for sequential scene understanding (e.g., robot manipulation, video tracking) requires both conservative summarization of observed scenes and recognition of temporal dynamics within that summary. Existing static-scene methods (MAE, SimMIM) lack temporal awareness, while dynamic-scene methods (SiamMAE, RSP) enforce patch-wise correspondence but fail to holistically summarize scene state, yielding only marginal gains over MAE on sequential tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253582/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a reference scene x^t and a temporally offset target scene x^{t+k} (both 224×224 RGB, sampled from Kinetics-400 videos at 30 FPS with gap k ∈ [4, 96]). The reference scene is fully encoded without masking, while the target scene is masked at an extremely high ratio of 0.9, leaving only ~10% of patches as hints. The output is a single bottleneck token (the CLS token from the reference encoding) that compactly summarizes the reference scene, plus pixel-level reconstructions of the masked target patches.

## Method

ToBo operates in two steps: (1) a squeeze step where the full reference scene is encoded by a ViT and its CLS token serves as the bottleneck token, and (2) a reconstruction step where the decoder predicts the 90% masked target patches using only the bottleneck token concatenated with the ~10% unmasked target patch representations and mask tokens. The decoder uses self-attention layers plus MLPs (deliberately avoiding cross-attention layers used by SiamMAE/RSP), ensuring it attends exclusively to the given information. The extremely high target masking ratio is the key mechanism: it makes precise reconstruction impossible from hints alone, forcing the decoder to rely heavily on the bottleneck token and thereby encouraging the encoder to conservatively preserve essential scene information while embedding temporal dependencies. Training minimizes cosine distance on reconstructed patches, with an auxiliary siamese MAE loss for patch-level correspondence. This design achieves comparable training FLOPs to MAE (15.9 vs 13.0 GFLOPs) while substantially outperforming RSP (32.5 GFLOPs) on downstream tasks.

## Results

On Franka Kitchen (ViT-S/16, Kinetics-400, 400 epochs), ToBo achieves success rates of 57.0%, 82.0%, 95.0%, 51.0%, and 55.0% across five manipulation tasks, surpassing the best baseline (RSP or CropMAE) by 20–25 percentage points on every task. On real-world robot manipulation (UR5e), ToBo reaches 65.0%, 75.0%, and 80.0% on Cabinet Opening, Drawer Closing, and Cup Stacking, outperforming RSP by 40, 10, and 25 points respectively. On video label propagation, ToBo attains 60.6 J&Fm (DAVIS), 34.0 mIoU (VIP), and 47.0 PCK@0.1 (JHMDB), setting new state-of-the-art across all three benchmarks. Training FLOPs are 15.9 GFLOPs, comparable to MAE (13.0) and SiamMAE (13.1) but far below RSP (32.5).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ToBo decisively outperforms all static SSL baselines (SimCLR, MoCo v3, DINO, MAE) by 20–45 points on Franka Kitchen, confirming that single-frame pre-training is fundamentally insufficient for sequential scene understanding. Among dynamic SSL methods, RSP is the closest competitor (e.g., 31.0 vs. 57.0 on Knob1 on), but ToBo still leads by 20+ points on every task while using roughly half the training FLOPs (15.9 vs. 32.5 GFLOPs). SiamMAE, despite its patch-level temporal correspondence objective, shows only marginal gains over MAE and is consistently outperformed by ToBo by 30–40 points. On real-world manipulation, ToBo (65/75/80%) dramatically exceeds RSP (25/65/55%) and SiamMAE (20/55/50%), with CropMAE failing entirely on Cabinet Opening (0%). Against robot representation learning models (Theia, R3M, MVP, Voltron, MPI, VC-1, data4robotics), ToBo achieves the highest MetaWorld score (87.8) despite using the smallest model (21.7M params) and least training data (0.2B frames), and surpasses all on Franka Kitchen. On video label propagation, ToBo leads all baselines on DAVIS (60.6 J&Fm vs. RSP's 60.1), VIP (34.0 mIoU vs. RSP's 33.8), and JHMDB (47.0 PCK@0.1 vs. SiamMAE's 44.7). Finally, against vision-language models (CLIP, DINOv2, SigLIP, SigLIP2) on Franka Kitchen, ToBo outperforms all by 13–44 points despite having no language supervision and 10–200× less pre-training data.

## Relation to Prior Work

ToBo extends the masked autoencoding (MAE) framework by introducing a bottleneck token mechanism that conservatively compresses a reference scene into a single CLS token, then reconstructs a heavily masked target scene using that token plus minimal target patches. It departs from prior dynamic-scene SSL methods (SiamMAE, RSP, CropMAE) that rely on cross-attention decoders for patch-level temporal correspondence, arguing instead that holistic scene summarization is more critical than fine-grained matching. The paper also contrasts with combinatorial architectures like RSP that stack multiple objectives (autoencoding + global alignment + frame prediction), positioning ToBo as a simpler single-objective alternative with comparable or lower computational cost.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a single bottleneck token suffices for sequential scene understanding challenges the prevailing assumption in dynamic SSL that full spatial token representations with cross-attention are necessary for temporal reasoning. This has practical implications for embodied AI: a 21.7M-parameter model trained on 0.2B frames outperforms much larger, more heavily supervised models (e.g., Theia at 52.9M params / 14.4B frames) on robot manipulation benchmarks, suggesting a more efficient path to deployable visual backbones. Open questions include whether the approach scales beyond ViT-L/16, whether multi-frame source settings can be made to work (the paper's naive extension underperformed), and whether the single-token bottleneck generalizes to higher resolutions or longer temporal horizons.
