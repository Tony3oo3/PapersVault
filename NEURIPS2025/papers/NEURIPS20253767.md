## Problem

While scaling laws for neural network loss are well-characterized in terms of data, parameters, and compute, it has been unclear whether innovations in optimization algorithms can fundamentally change the exponents of these power laws rather than merely improving constants. Traditional SGD with momentum (SGD-M) using fixed, dimension-independent momentum hyperparameters produces identical scaling law exponents to plain SGD across all regimes. This paper asks whether one can select momentum hyperparameters as functions of model size and data complexity to provably improve the loss scaling exponents.

## Input / Output

The method operates on the power law random features (PLRF) model, where data samples x ∈ R^v have entries distributed as j^{-α}z_j and targets are constructed via a signal b with entries j^{-β}, embedded through a random matrix W ∈ R^{v×d} with N(0,1/d) entries. The key inputs are the data complexity α, target complexity β, model dimension d, and hidden dimensionality v (with v/d → r). The output is a complete set of explicit scaling law exponents (σ_i, τ_i) for each of four loss components (population bias, model capacity, embedding bias, variance), along with compute-optimal parameter exponents ξ, loss exponents η, and data exponents ζ as functions of (α, β) and the training regime t ∝ d^ℓ.

## Method

The core contribution is Dimension-adapted Nesterov Acceleration (DANA), which scales the momentum learning rate as γ₃(t;d) = γ̃₃ d^{-κ₂}(1+t)^{-κ₃} and the momentum coefficient as Δ(t) = δ(1+t)^{-1}, with κ₃ = 1/(2α) for the optimal DANA-decaying variant. The key insight is that after t iterations, only an effective dimension of (tB)^{-1/(2α)} of the feature space is active, so the momentum rate should decay with this time-dependent effective dimension rather than the full d. The analysis proceeds by continuizing the discrete algorithm via Poissonization to obtain exact ODEs for the loss, then applying random matrix theory (deterministic equivalents for the PLRF spectrum) and Volterra equation techniques to derive closed-form scaling laws. The main theorem shows DANA-decaying outscales SGD for all training regimes t = d^ℓ with 0 < ℓ < 2α above the high-dimensional line (2α > 1), while DANA-constant (κ₃ = 0, κ₂ = 1) outscales SGD for 1 < ℓ < 2α, and SGD-M with fixed momentum never outscales SGD.

## Results

On the PLRF model, DANA-decaying (κ₃=1/(2α)) improves the loss exponent over SGD in all four phases where 2α>1, while DANA-constant (κ₂=1) outscales SGD for training regimes t=d^ℓ with 1<ℓ<2α. On 2-layer LSTMs trained on C4, DANA-decaying with κ₃=0.7 maximizes the compute-optimal loss exponent, outscaling SGD, with all loss exponent fits achieving R²≥0.984. Theoretical predictions for compute-optimal loss exponents match empirical PLRF results within 0.09, and parameter/data exponents match within 0.13 across 84 (α,β) configurations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SGD-Momentum with fixed Δ produces identical scaling law exponents to plain SGD across all phases and regimes, confirming that standard momentum only improves constants, not exponents. Schedule-Free SGD (Defazio et al., 2024) corresponds to DANA with κ₃=1.0 and matches SGD's loss exponent exactly, failing to outscale. Adam with cosine decay (Kingma & Ba, 2015) also exhibits the same scaling law as SGD in ODE simulations. AcSGD (Varre & Flammarion, 2022) asymptotically matches DANA-constant with κ₂=1/d but is strictly dominated by DANA-decaying. Accelerated SGD (Li et al., 2023) and the minimax-optimal SGD rates (Carratino et al., 2018; Dieuleveut & Bach, 2016; Pillaud-Vivien et al., 2018) are all strictly worse than DANA-decaying in every phase. On LSTMs trained on C4, DANA-decaying (κ₃=0.7) achieves the best compute-optimal loss exponent, while SGD-M and Schedule-Free SGD both match plain SGD, confirming the theoretical predictions in a practical setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the PLRF scaling law framework of Paquette et al. [80] (which covered one-pass SGD) to stochastic momentum algorithms, combining continuized ODE analysis (building on Paquette & Paquette [78] and Even et al. [38]) with random matrix theory for power-law covariance spectra. It unifies several existing accelerated SGD variants—AcSGD [94], Schedule-Free SGD [33], Accelerated SGD [61], and SGD with 1-memory [107]—under a single Gen-Mom-SGD framework and derives their scaling laws. The key departure from prior work is treating momentum hyperparameters as dimension- and data-dependent quantities rather than fixed constants, introducing the 'outscaling' question of whether momentum can change (not merely improve constants of) scaling law exponents.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first provable demonstration that momentum hyperparameter scaling can change scaling law exponents over SGD, challenging the long-standing assumption that momentum primarily improves constants. The finding that DANA-decaying's compute-optimal regime differs from the Chinchilla law (d* ≠ f^{1/2}) suggests that optimizer choice can shift the optimal parameter-data tradeoff. However, the results are confined to the 2α > 1 regime on a quadratic random-features model, and the paper explicitly leaves open whether outscaling is possible below the high-dimensional line, with d-dependent batch sizes, or in combination with adaptive methods like Adam—limiting near-term practical impact on transformer training.
