## Problem

Existing inference-time search methods for VLM captioning, such as VisVM, re-score all N candidates at every sentence step, incurring O(N×S) computational overhead, while simultaneously leaving low-confidence, weakly grounded continuations unpenalized—leading to persistent hallucinations. No prior approach combines efficient inference with explicit penalization of under-grounded outputs, leaving a gap between caption fidelity and practical deployment speed.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an RGB image I paired with a free-form text prompt x (sourced from LLaVA-150K detailed-description prompts) and produces a multi-sentence descriptive paragraph y = [y₀, y₁, …, yₘ], where each yᵢ is a sentence-level unit scored independently by the value model. During inference, the policy generates N×K = 30 paragraph-level candidates (N=5 temperatures × K=6 samples) in Stage 1, then selectively regenerates sentence-level alternatives only for under-grounded segments in Stage 2, appending them at appropriate positions until an EOS token is reached.

## Method

ViMaR introduces a two-stage value-guided search built on a temporal-difference value model V_ρ (a linear head on LLaVA-Next-Mistral-7B's penultimate layer) trained with CLIP-ViT similarity as the process reward. The key training innovation is a margin-based reward adjustment: when a candidate's CLIP score δ falls below a calibrated threshold τ=0.16, the reward becomes δ−τ (a negative penalty proportional to the grounding gap), sharpening the value model's discrimination between well-grounded and hallucinatory sentences. At inference, Stage 1 performs a single holistic pass—generating 30 diverse paragraph candidates via multi-temperature sampling and selecting the highest-value caption—while Stage 2 performs targeted refinement, resampling alternatives only for segments with weak visual grounding and inserting the best-scoring replacement. This global-then-local strategy avoids VisVM's per-step re-scoring of all candidates, yielding over 4× speedup while improving CHAIR and MMHal hallucination metrics and achieving 15.87% average gains on visual comprehension benchmarks when used for self-training.

## Results

On COCO Val2017, ViMaR achieves the lowest hallucination rates across all metrics: CHAIR_S = 23.1, CHAIR_I = 4.1, MMHal = 3.75, and MMHal Rate = 0.35, while running in only 108 s per sample — 4.3× faster than VisVM (462 s) and 6× faster than Best-of-N (668 s). In human evaluation, ViMaR captions are preferred 64.0% of the time over VisVM and 69.7% over greedy decoding; GPT-4o pairwise judgments show a 49.3% win rate over VisVM and 73.8% over greedy. When ViMaR-generated captions are used for self-training LLaVA-Next-Mistral-7B, the model gains an average of 15.87% across seven visual comprehension benchmarks (MM-Vet, MMBench, MMMU, MathVista, CVBench, LLaVA-Wild, MMStar) and reduces hallucination rate by 30.87% relative to the base model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ViMaR outperforms all three baselines on every hallucination metric. Against VisVM (Wang et al., 2024), ViMaR reduces CHAIR_S from 26.2 to 23.1 (−11.8%), CHAIR_I from 4.6 to 4.1 (−10.9%), and MMHal Rate from 0.39 to 0.35 (−10.3%), while being 4.3× faster (108 s vs. 462 s). Against CLIP-PRM guided search (Zhou et al., 2024), ViMaR improves CHAIR_S from 28.4 to 23.1 (−18.7%) and MMHal Rate from 0.49 to 0.35 (−28.6%), at 2.6× lower inference cost. Against Best-of-N (Brown et al., 2024), ViMaR achieves the largest relative gains: CHAIR_S drops from 27.1 to 23.1 (−14.8%) and inference time drops from 668 s to 108 s (6.2× speedup). In self-training (Table 2), ViMaR-generated captions yield a 15.87% average benchmark improvement, surpassing VisVM search (11.08%), CLIP-PRM search (5.33%), and GPT-4o-BoN (4.82%), confirming that the two-stage value-guided search produces higher-quality supervision signals than any single-stage alternative.

## Relation to Prior Work

ViMaR directly extends the Vision Value Model (VisVM, Wang et al. 2024), which first applied temporal-difference learning over CLIP similarity scores to guide VLM captioning via step-by-step value-guided search. The key departure is architectural: rather than re-scoring all N×K candidates at every sentence step (incurring O(N×S) overhead), ViMaR splits inference into a coarse full-paragraph selection followed by targeted refinement of only under-grounded segments, and introduces a margin-based penalty on the CLIP reward to suppress low-confidence continuations. This positions the work at the intersection of inference-time search (Best-of-N, MCTS, process-reward-guided decoding from the LLM literature) and VLM-specific hallucination mitigation, while remaining a plug-and-play decoding strategy that leaves model weights unchanged.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in the 4×+ speedup over VisVM while matching or exceeding caption quality, making value-guided inference feasible for deployment rather than a research curiosity. The cross-model generalization—where a value model trained solely on LLaVA-Mistral-7B steers stronger architectures (LLaVA-OneVision-Qwen2-7B, Qwen2.5-VL-3B)—suggests that learned value signals capture architecture-agnostic visual grounding principles, though the cross-model gains are more modest (+6.7% for Qwen2.5-VL-3B). Open questions include whether the CLIP-based reward signal is sufficient for fine-grained or compositional scenes, how the margin threshold τ transfers across domains beyond COCO-style natural images, and whether the self-training loop (15.87% average uplift) compounds over multiple iterations without degradation. The notable discrepancy between human preference (64% win rate) and GPT-4o judgment (49.3%, essentially a tie) against VisVM also raises questions about what dimensions of quality each evaluator prioritizes.
