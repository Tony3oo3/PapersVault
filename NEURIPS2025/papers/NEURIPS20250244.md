## Problem

Existing referring image segmentation methods predominantly treat the task as keyword/concept matching, which breaks down under referential ambiguity—specifically object-distracting expressions containing multiple noun phrases and category-implicit expressions where the target class is never stated. The paper addresses this gap by proposing a cognitively inspired framework and introducing aRefCOCO, a test-only benchmark with ambiguous expressions (averaging 12.6 words, heavy pronoun and preposition use) to properly evaluate models under these challenging conditions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250244/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image (processed by Swin-B into H×W×C spatial features) and a natural language referring expression (processed by BERT into L×C token features) and produces a binary segmentation mask. The mask is predicted top-down from multi-level visual features across four SFLayers using a standard segmentation head. On aRefCOCO, inputs are notably longer and more ambiguous than standard RefCOCO expressions, with 3.1 same-category visual distractors per image on average.

## Method

SaFiRe alternates two operations across four Saccade-Fixation Layers (SFLayers). The Saccade operation performs a quick global alignment by applying DiT-inspired Norm Adaptation: pooled text features generate scale, bias, and shift factors that modulate image features through a VSSM block. The Fixation operation implements a group-scan-recover pattern: image features are split into 4×4 non-overlapping windows, the full text sequence is interleaved after each window to form a hybrid multi-modal sequence, and a single VSSM (VMamba) scan processes this sequence—exploiting Mamba's order-sensitive hidden-state update so that periodically reiterated text tokens continuously refresh the model's focus on the target description. After scanning, image windows are reassembled and text features are aggregated via learnable weighted averaging. This saccade-fixation reiteration across layers enables progressive coarse-to-fine cross-modal refinement with linear sequence complexity, and the model is trained end-to-end with a composite Dice + Focal loss.

## Results

On the standard single-dataset protocol (Table 1), SaFiRe achieves 67.07 oIoU on RefCOCOg (val-U), 66.39 on RefCOCO+ (val), and 75.34 on RefCOCO (val), outperforming all 13 baselines across every split. On the newly introduced aRefCOCO benchmark (Table 3), SaFiRe reaches 65.1 oIoU and 71.4 mIoU, surpassing the next-best C3VG (62.9 oIoU) by 2.2 points and PSALM (60.1 oIoU) by 5.0 points. In the mixed-training setting (Table 2), SaFiRe attains 77.3 mIoU on RefCOCOg, exceeding C3VG (76.3) and OneRef-B (74.1), while also achieving the highest oIoU (75.8) among non-LLM methods. Inference efficiency is 8.26 FPS at 384 GFLOPs, roughly 3× faster than LISA (2.67 FPS) and 2× faster than CGFormer (2.78 FPS).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SaFiRe outperforms all 13 single-dataset baselines on every RefCOCO/+/g split, with the largest margin on the hardest RefCOCOg benchmark: +1.71 oIoU over MagNet (67.07 vs 65.36) and +2.07 over ReLA (67.07 vs 65.00). On the easier RefCOCO, gains are smaller but consistent (+0.10 over MagNet, +0.59 over CGFormer). On the new aRefCOCO benchmark, SaFiRe leads C3VG by 2.2 oIoU (65.1 vs 62.9) and PSALM by 5.0 oIoU (65.1 vs 60.1), with a particularly large gap on Pr@90 (45.2 vs 32.8 and 38.7 respectively), indicating superior precision on the hardest cases. In the mixed-training setting, SaFiRe achieves the best mIoU (77.3 on RefCOCOg) among all compared methods, edging out C3VG (76.3) and OneRef-B (74.1), though C3VG retains a slight oIoU edge (76.3 vs 75.8) in that configuration. Among LLM-based methods (LISA, GLaMM, u-LLaVA, GSVA, PixelLM), SaFiRe surpasses all of them on oIoU across all three benchmarks while using a lightweight BERT encoder instead of a 7B–13B LLM, and at 384 GFLOPs it is an order of magnitude cheaper than LISA (4880 GFLOPs). The Mamba-based ReMamber, the closest architectural relative, trails SaFiRe by 3.17 oIoU on RefCOCOg (63.90 vs 67.07), confirming that the Saccade-Fixation design adds substantial value beyond a plain VSSM backbone.

## Relation to Prior Work

SaFiRe departs from the dominant keyword/concept-matching paradigm in referring image segmentation (LAVT, ReLA, VLT, MagNet) by introducing a cognitively-inspired two-phase process—Saccade (global semantic alignment via DiT-style Norm Adaptation) and Fixation (local region-wise inspection via reiterated text-image interleaving). The core SSM processing unit is built directly on VMamba's VSSM, and the overall selective state-space scanning mechanism follows Mamba, but the paper's key architectural departure is the 'group-scan-recover' Fixation operation that interleaves text tokens after every local image window, exploiting SSM's recency bias for fine-grained cross-modal grounding. This positions SaFiRe between lightweight transformer-based RIS decoders and heavy LLM-driven segmentation models (LISA, PSALM, GSVA), offering a middle ground that avoids both the oversimplification of keyword matching and the computational overhead of 7B+ language models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The aRefCOCO benchmark is a practically valuable addition that exposes a significant blind spot in current RIS evaluation: keyword-matching methods like LAVT collapse from ~72 oIoU on RefCOCO to 31.7 on aRefCOCO, revealing that much of the reported progress on standard benchmarks may not transfer to real-world ambiguous expressions. The efficiency advantage (384 GFLOPs, 8.26 FPS) over LLM-based methods (4880+ GFLOPs) makes SaFiRe a compelling option for deployment-constrained settings. Open questions remain around whether the saccade-fixation reiteration generalizes to dialog-based or video grounding, and the paper acknowledges persistent failures on extremely similar visual semantics and long relational chains—suggesting that purely sequential scanning may have limits for multi-hop spatial reasoning.
