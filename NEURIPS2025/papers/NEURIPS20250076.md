## Problem

Existing endoscopic benchmarks are compartmentalized, each covering only one or two endoscopic scenarios (e.g., surgical-only or colonoscopy-only) and a narrow set of tasks, failing to reflect the multi-scenario, multi-dimensional nature of real clinical endoscopy. This makes it impossible to assess whether MLLMs can adapt across the full spectrum of endoscopic practice—from diagnostic screening to interventional surgery—across the progressive analytical skills clinicians actually use.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250076/cropped_page3_idx15.webp)

## Input / Output

Each evaluation sample pairs an endoscopic RGB image (from gastroscopy, colonoscopy, capsule endoscopy, or surgical endoscopy) with a multiple-choice text question (4 options), optionally augmented with a visual prompt at one of 5 granularities: image-level, single bounding box, single contour, multi-box, or multi-contour. Coordinate-based tasks (Visual Grounding) require selecting from four [x1, y1, x2, y2] candidate boxes. Evaluation is zero-shot and closed-set: models output a single letter choice, scored by exact match, with Qwen2.5-VL-72B used as an auxiliary judger to extract answers from malformed model outputs.

## Method

EndoBench is constructed through a three-stage pipeline: (1) collecting 20 public and 1 private endoscopy datasets spanning all four major endoscopy types; (2) standardizing heterogeneous annotations into 12 tasks (with 12 secondary subtasks) across 4 clinical categories—anatomical recognition, lesion analysis/grading, spatial localization, and surgical workflow—using 5–8 question templates per task and generating 446,535 VQA pairs (EndoVQA-Instruct); (3) filtering to 6,832 clinically validated pairs via entity-balanced sampling, DINOv2 embedding extraction with K-center clustering for representativeness, GPT-4o-mini rephrasing for linguistic diversity, and dual-physician review. The evaluation protocol benchmarks 23 MLLMs (13 open-source general-purpose, 5 medical-specialized, 5 proprietary) in a zero-shot closed-set multiple-choice setting, with two certified clinicians answering 255 questions as a human reference standard. The multi-dimensional framework mirrors the clinical workflow progression from basic anatomical orientation through lesion characterization to surgical execution, enabling granular diagnosis of where models succeed or fail.

## Results

On EndoBench (6,832 clinically validated VQA pairs across 12 tasks), Gemini-2.5-Pro achieves the highest average accuracy of 49.53%, followed by GPT-4o (41.69%) and MedDr-80B (39.96%), while human physicians score 74.12%—a gap of roughly 25 percentage points. Among open-source models, QvQ-72B (31.62%) and InternVL2.5-38B (30.09%) lead, but all open-source general-purpose models remain within 7% of the random baseline (24.96%) on non-surgical tasks. Medical-domain fine-tuning yields substantial gains: MedDr-80B reaches 56.00% on Landmark Identification and HuatuoGPT-Vision-34B scores 86.00% on Preoperative Assessment, surpassing all proprietary models on those specific tasks. ColonGPT is a notable outlier, achieving 95.00% on Preoperative Assessment (exceeding physicians at 80%) but only 15.60% overall due to near-zero performance on most other tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among proprietary models, Gemini-2.5-Pro (49.53% avg) dominates, outperforming GPT-4o (41.69%) by ~8 points and Claude-3.7-Sonnet (35.67%) by ~14 points, excelling in 7 of 12 tasks including Visual Grounding (50.52%) and Region Selection (73.21%). GPT-4o leads on Preoperative Assessment (92.00%) and Lesion Type Identification (39.69%), while Grok-3 (34.66%) and DeepSeek-V3 (31.13%) trail the top proprietary models. Medical-domain models MedDr-80B (39.96%) and HuatuoGPT-Vision-34B (39.58%) surpass all open-source general-purpose models and even outperform proprietary models on specific tasks—MedDr-80B reaches 56.00% on Landmark Identification vs. Gemini's 44.16%, and HuatuoGPT-Vision-34B scores 86.00% on Preoperative Assessment. However, ColonGPT (15.60% avg) is the weakest overall, with near-zero scores on Lesion Severity (0.00%), Micro Operation (1.65%), and Visual Grounding (2.99%), despite its 95.00% on Preoperative Assessment. Among open-source models, QvQ-72B (31.62%) and InternVL2.5-38B (30.09%) lead but remain ~18–19 points below Gemini-2.5-Pro; smaller models like LLaVA-v1.5-7B (26.62%) and ShareGPT-4v-7B (18.35%) perform at or near random level on most non-surgical tasks. All 23 evaluated models fall substantially short of the physician reference (74.12%), with the largest gaps on Lesion Severity Grading (best model 24.46% vs. physician 46.67%) and Landmark Identification (best model 56.00% vs. physician 93.33%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of endoscopic VQA benchmarks (Surgical-VQA, SSG-VQA, Kvasir-VQA, ColonINST) by addressing their core limitation: each covers only one or two endoscopic scenarios and a narrow task subset. Rather than building on a single prior method, EndoBench synthesizes the evaluation dimensions from all prior endoscopic benchmarks into a unified framework spanning four scenarios (gastroscopy, colonoscopy, capsule endoscopy, surgical endoscopy), twelve tasks across four clinical categories, and five visual prompting granularities. The construction pipeline repurposes DINOv2 embeddings and K-center clustering for representative sample selection, and GPT-4o-mini for question paraphrasing, while the 446K-pair EndoVQA-Instruct dataset positions the work at the intersection of benchmark construction and instruction-tuning data curation for medical MLLMs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~25-point gap between the best model (Gemini-2.5-Pro at 49.53%) and human clinicians (74.12%) quantifies how far current MLLMs are from clinical deployment in endoscopy, a domain where early detection is critical. The finding that adding bounding-box prompts to polyp counting lifts Gemini from 24% to 93% accuracy reveals that the bottleneck is lesion identification, not spatial reasoning or counting—a distinction that should redirect research toward domain-specific visual knowledge injection rather than generic spatial reasoning. Open questions include whether the gap can be closed through continued domain fine-tuning, whether the multi-scenario evaluation reveals capabilities that single-scenario benchmarks mask, and how the 5-level visual prompting granularity interacts with model architecture choices.
