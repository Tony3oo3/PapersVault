## Problem

Shampoo's success in the AlgoPerf competition relied on two critical heuristics—learning rate grafting and stale preconditioning (updating the root-inverse every 100 steps)—that lack theoretical justification, increase algorithmic complexity, and require additional hyperparameter tuning. This paper investigates why these heuristics are necessary by decomposing the preconditioner into its eigenvalues and eigenbasis, and proposes principled alternatives that eliminate them.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255507/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes mini-batch gradients G_t ∈ R^{m×n} for 2D weight matrices and g_t ∈ R^d for 1D parameters (biases, layer norms), along with the current parameters W_t. For 2D parameters it maintains Kronecker factors L_t ∈ R^{m×m} and R_t ∈ R^{n×n} plus an eigenvalue correction matrix D_t ∈ R^{m×n} updated every step; for 1D parameters it uses a single matrix A_t. The output is the updated parameter W_{t+1}, with the eigenbasis Q_L, Q_R updated adaptively (not every step) based on a relative off-diagonal error threshold τ.

## Method

The paper decouples Shampoo's preconditioner into eigenvalue and eigenbasis components, showing via Frobenius norm bounds (Lemma 1, Proposition 1) that Shampoo's update magnitude is mis-scaled relative to Adam by dimension-dependent factors (m^{-p/2}n^{-p/2}) and staleness, which learning rate grafting implicitly corrects. Replacing grafting with a per-step eigenvalue correction D_t = β₂D_{t-1}+(1-β₂)Ḡ_t^{⊙2} (EShampoo, analogous to SOAP) eliminates the need for grafting while matching or surpassing its performance. For the eigenbasis, the authors propose an adaptive criterion based on the relative Frobenius norm of off-diagonal entries of Q̂_{t-1}^T L_t Q̂_{t-1} (Equation 11), motivated by QR algorithm termination, which allows skipping eigendecompositions when the stale basis is still accurate. Empirically, this adaptive frequency saves ~20% wall-clock time over the fixed F=100 AlgoPerf setting, and the analysis reveals that eigenbasis error matters predominantly in early training and that 1D parameters (biases, layer norms) do not benefit from eigenbasis changes at all.

## Results

On the AlgoPerf workloads, EShampoo (eigenvalue-corrected Shampoo) with fixed F=100 reduces steps-to-target by 41% on FastMRI (2536 vs. 4301) and 34% on OGBG (8320 vs. 12574) compared to the AlgoPerf-winning Shampoo with Adam grafting, while also hitting more targets (5/5 vs. 4/5 on FastMRI, 3/5 vs. 2/5 on OGBG). The adaptive eigenbasis criterion (τ=0.1, F=50) further improves OGBG to 5/5 targets in 7117 steps (43% fewer than grafting) and saves ~20% wall-clock time on Imagewoof ViT relative to the fixed F=100 baseline. On ImageNet ViT, EShampoo(F=100) uses 4.6% fewer steps (76226 vs. 79907) at comparable wall-clock time (~895 min).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to the AlgoPerf-winning Shampoo with Adam grafting (Kasimbeg et al., 2025), EShampoo matches or outperforms on all three workloads: it reduces steps by 41% on FastMRI, 4.6% on ImageNet ViT, and 43% on OGBG, while also hitting more targets (5/5 vs. 4/5 on FastMRI, 5/5 vs. 2/5 on OGBG). Against AdamW (Kingma & Ba, 2015), EShampoo achieves substantially lower final training loss on Imagewoof ViT (~0.04 vs. ~0.3), and even a single eigenbasis computation at the first iteration (τ=0.99) still outperforms AdamW (~0.12 vs. 0.3). Compared to the default SOAP configuration (Vyas et al., 2025a), which performs one warm-started power iteration every 10 steps, the adaptive EShampoo criterion achieves a better final loss and is slightly faster in wall-clock time on Imagewoof ViT, though the paper notes this conflicts with SOAP's reported results, possibly due to workload differences.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Shampoo lineage (Gupta et al., 2018; Anil et al., 2020) by decoupling the preconditioner's eigenvalue and eigenbasis updates, building directly on SOAP's (Vyas et al., 2025a) eigenvalue correction and Adam's (Kingma & Ba, 2015) diagonal scaling as the reference approximation target. It departs from prior Shampoo analyses by providing a Frobenius-norm-based theoretical explanation for why learning rate grafting is necessary—specifically, a dimension-dependent scaling factor (m^{-p/2}n^{-p/2}) in Proposition 1 that had previously been attributed only to empirical layer-wise magnitude differences. The adaptive eigenbasis frequency criterion draws on the QR algorithm's convergence theory (Golub & Van Loan, 2013) in a way not previously applied to Kronecker-factored optimizers, and the paper also connects to K-FAC, TNT, and Muon as related structured-preconditioner methods sharing the same stale-eigenvalue pathology.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first principled explanation for Shampoo's reliance on grafting and stale preconditioning, replacing two unexplained heuristics with a theoretically grounded eigenvalue correction (EShampoo) and an adaptive eigenbasis frequency criterion that saves ~20% wall-clock time. Key open questions remain: whether the eigenvalue correction generalizes to the AdaGrad summation (vs. EMA), how approximation quality can be incorporated into regret bounds, and whether these techniques scale to large language models where the authors note different eigenbasis dynamics emerge (L_t evolving faster than R_t). The finding that eigenbasis updates for 1D parameters are essentially unnecessary, and that a single eigenbasis computation at initialization suffices to beat AdamW, suggests significant computational savings are possible but the practical impact is currently bounded to the Shampoo/SOAP family and moderate-scale workloads.
