## Problem

Existing conformal prediction methods for trajectory forecasting assume fixed, known time grids, but real longitudinal biomarker data from clinical studies contains randomly-timed observations due to missed visits and variable scheduling. This mismatch prevents direct application of conformal guarantees to the most common setting in neurodegenerative disease monitoring, where coverage of the entire trajectory is critical for safe clinical decision-making.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253859/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes as input a subject's demographic and clinical covariates (sex, race, diagnosis, education, APOE4 status), 145 harmonized brain ROI volumes from the first visit, the first-visit biomarker value, and the subject-specific set of follow-up time points (which are random and a priori unknown for the test subject). It produces conformal prediction intervals at every time point up to a user-defined horizon T, with the guarantee that the entire true trajectory is covered simultaneously with probability at least 1−α. A downstream rate-of-change-bound (RoCB) score is derived from the lower (or upper) conformal bound to flag high-risk MCI subjects for progression to Alzheimer's disease.

## Method

The core contribution is a split conformal prediction framework adapted to randomly-timed trajectories via a normalized nonconformity score R^(i) = max_{t∈T^(i)} |Y_t^(i) − Ŷ_t^(i)| / σ(Ŷ_t^(i)), where σ(·) is a model-derived predictive standard deviation. Taking the max over each subject's own (random) time points eliminates the need for a fixed grid, and the conformal multiplier R is set to the ⌈(|D_cal|+1)(1−α)⌉-th smallest calibration score, yielding intervals C_t(X) = [Ŷ_t − R·σ(Ŷ_t), Ŷ_t + R·σ(Ŷ_t)] with finite-sample simultaneous coverage. A group-conditional (Mondrian) variant stratifies the calibration set by covariate-defined subpopulations (sex, race, diagnosis, education, APOE4) and computes a separate quantile per group, providing formal subgroup-level coverage guarantees. The framework is model-agnostic and is demonstrated across five predictors (DKGP, DME, DQR, DRMC, Bootstrap), and the conformal lower bound is further translated into an uncertainty-aware progression metric (RoCB) that identifies 17.5% more high-risk MCI converters than standard slope-based methods.

## Results

Across five predictors (DKGP, DME, DQR, DRMC, Bootstrap) on hippocampal- and ventricular-volume trajectories from ADNI+BLSA (2,200 subjects), all conformalized predictors achieve the nominal 90% coverage, while baselines either over-cover (DKGP, DME) or under-cover (DQR, DRMC, Bootstrap). Conformalized bands are tighter than over-covering baselines and wider than under-covering baselines, as expected. In the clinical MCI-to-AD progression task, the Rate of Change Bound (RoCB) derived from conformal lower bounds improves recall from 74.0% (RoC) to 91.5% (RoCB) for CP-DRMC, identifying 17.5% more high-risk converters. Group-conditional conformal bands restore nominal 90% coverage across all five covariate stratifications (sex, race, diagnosis, education, APOE4) where population-level conformal bands fail for subgroups such as MCI patients, APOE4 homozygotes, and Asian subjects.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper conformalizes five trajectory predictors and compares each baseline against its conformalized counterpart. DKGP and DME (Gaussian-process-based) already achieve ~90% coverage in their baseline form, but their conformalized versions (CP-DKGP, CP-DME) tighten the prediction intervals while maintaining nominal coverage, indicating the baselines are overly conservative. DQR, DRMC, and Bootstrap all under-cover in their baseline form (falling short of 90%), and their conformalized versions (CP-DQR, CP-DRMC, CP-Bootstrap) widen the intervals to restore the guaranteed 90% coverage. In the downstream MCI-to-AD risk stratification task, CP-DRMC with RoCB achieves 91.5% recall versus 88.4% for DRMC with RoCB and 74.0% for CP-DRMC with plain RoC, demonstrating that conformal calibration itself (not just the RoCB metric) contributes an additional ~3 percentage points of recall. Precision drops modestly (~3–4 points) when switching from RoC to RoCB, but F₁ scores remain comparable, confirming the sensitivity gain does not degrade overall discrimination.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the normalized nonconformity score framework of Yu et al. [17] and Cleaveland et al. [18]—which guarantees simultaneous coverage over fixed-time trajectories—to the clinically realistic setting where observations arrive at subject-specific, randomly-timed visits. The foundational split conformal prediction machinery of Papadopoulos [11] is retained, and the group-conditional variant follows the Mondrian conformal framework of Vovk [37], applied here to covariate-defined subpopulations. The key conceptual departure is that the nonconformity score in Eq. (2) takes the maximum over a subject-specific (and a priori unknown) set of time points, removing the fixed-grid assumption that limits all prior trajectory conformal methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides distribution-free, finite-sample coverage guarantees for biomarker trajectories in the exact setting encountered in longitudinal clinical studies (missed visits, variable scheduling), filling a gap that prevents direct deployment of existing conformal methods. The group-conditional variant addresses a practical fairness concern: population-level bands demonstrably under-cover high-risk subgroups (MCI, APOE4 homozygotes, Asian subjects), and the RoCB metric translates calibrated uncertainty into a clinically actionable risk score. Open questions include extending guarantees to jointly coupled biomarker trajectories, handling distribution shift across sites or imaging protocols, and developing online variants that adapt as new visits arrive—each acknowledged by the authors as future work.
