## Problem

Most training-free guidance methods for diffusion and flow models rely on objective gradients, making them inapplicable when objectives are non-differentiable (e.g., rule-based music constraints, docking scores) or when data is inherently discrete (molecules, DNA sequences). The design space for guidance beyond differentiability remains under-explored, with only a few existing methods that are disconnected from prior guidance principles and lack a unified framework.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252459/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a frozen pre-trained diffusion or flow model and an arbitrary (potentially non-differentiable) objective function f_y, along with hyperparameters controlling the active set size A and branch-out size K. It operates on both continuous data (piano MIDI sequences) and discrete data (SMILES strings of length 100 over 32 token types; DNA sequences of length 500 over 4 bases plus a mask state). At the final inference step, a single best sample is selected from the active set by maximizing f_y, producing one generated artifact per run (a MIDI file, a SMILES string, or a DNA sequence).

## Method

TreeG replaces gradient-based steering with a tree-search over inference paths: at each denoising step, every sample in an active set of size A branches into K candidate next states via a BranchOut module, candidates are scored by a value function V aligned with the objective, and the top A are retained for the next step. Three (BranchOut, V) pairs are instantiated: TreeG-SC samples next states from the original model and evaluates them via a Monte Carlo lookahead estimate of the clean sample; TreeG-SD proposes candidate destination states in the clean space (Gaussian perturbation for continuous, categorical sampling for discrete) and evaluates them directly with the objective, then computes the next state by linear interpolation toward the chosen destination; TreeG-G applies gradient-based reweighting of the discrete rate matrix using a Gumbel-Softmax straight-through estimator when a differentiable predictor is available. Theoretical guarantees (Theorems 1–2) show that with resampling-based selection, the induced transition distribution converges to the true conditional posterior as K and N grow. The framework exhibits an inference-time scaling law: increasing A and K monotonically improves objective values at the cost of linearly increasing compute.

## Results

On symbolic music generation (continuous diffusion), TreeG-SD (A=1, K=16) reduces loss by 29.01% on average over the strongest baseline, achieving 0.0002 loss on pitch histogram, 0.142 on note density, and 0.301 on chord progression. On small molecule generation (discrete flow), TreeG-SC (A=1) achieves a 26.38% average improvement across five targets, reaching QED 0.80, SA 0.89, DRD2 0.77, Docking 9.64, and ring-count MAE 0.01. On enhancer DNA design, TreeG-G (A=1, K=1, γ=200) achieves target class probabilities of 0.740, 0.981, and 0.934 for Classes 1–3, a 18.43% average improvement over the strongest baseline DG. The paper further identifies an inference-time scaling law where performance consistently improves with larger active set and branch-out sizes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TreeG-SD outperforms all gradient-based baselines (DPS, TDS) on all three music tasks, with the largest gains on non-differentiable objectives (note density: 0.142 vs. 1.261 for DPS; chord progression: 0.301 vs. 0.414 for DPS). Against the strongest derivative-free baselines SCG and SVDD, TreeG-SD matches or exceeds on music (e.g., chord loss 0.301 vs. SCG's 0.347) and significantly outperforms on molecules (TreeG-SC achieves QED 0.80 vs. SVDD's 0.67, DRD2 0.77 vs. 0.43) and DNA design (TreeG-G reaches 0.981 probability on Class 2 vs. SVDD's 0.088). On discrete flow tasks, TreeG-SC dominates TFG-Flow across all molecule targets (e.g., QED 0.80 vs. 0.61, DRD2 0.77 vs. 0.09) and TreeG-G substantially outperforms the training-based DG method on enhancer DNA (18.43% average probability improvement). The paper also shows that increasing the active set size A from 1 to 4 further improves TreeG-SD on music (note density loss drops from 0.142 to 0.048), confirming the inference-time scaling law.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies and extends the line of derivative-free guidance methods for non-differentiable objectives, showing that Stochastic Control Guidance (SCG) and Soft Value Decoding (SVDD) are special cases of its TreeG-SC algorithm (active set size 1, current-state exploration). It departs from prior work by introducing a novel destination-state exploration strategy (TreeG-SD) that proposes candidates in the clean-sample space rather than the noisy intermediate space, and by adding a tree-search dimension (active set size A > 1) that prior single-path methods do not exploit. For discrete flow models, it also proposes a gradient-based guidance method (TreeG-G) using Gumbel-Softmax backpropagation through the sampling process, complementing the existing training-based classifier guidance of Nisonoff et al. and the training-free rate-matrix reweighting of TFG-Flow.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified tree-search framework provides practitioners with a single, flexible tool for training-free guidance that handles both continuous and discrete data, differentiable and non-differentiable objectives, and scales gracefully with inference-time compute via the identified scaling law. The practical limitation is that when the objective is simultaneously non-differentiable, expensive to evaluate, and lacks a good surrogate predictor, the sampling-based variants become prohibitively slow, leaving a gap for very expensive oracle objectives. Open questions include whether the tree-search scaling law holds for larger-scale models (e.g., image or protein generation at production scale), whether the destination-state exploration advantage in continuous settings generalizes to latent-space diffusion, and how to adaptively allocate the A×K budget across timesteps rather than uniformly.
