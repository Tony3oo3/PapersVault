## Problem

Existing 3D mesh texturing methods rely on multi-view unprojection of exterior surfaces and inherently lack access to occluded interior geometry, leaving interior regions untextured or filled with heuristic methods (e.g., Voronoi extrapolation) that produce visible seams and inconsistent textures. UV-space inpainting approaches can reach interior regions but lack explicit geometric context and suffer from limited training data for diverse UV map structures, yielding low-frequency or semantically incoherent interior textures.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D mesh (normalized to a unit bounding box) and two separate text prompts—one for exterior surfaces and one for interior surfaces—and produces a 1024×1024 RGB UV texture map. Internally, the mesh is rendered from 16 hemispherical views (8 equatorial at 45°, 8 elevated at 45°) at 768×768 resolution, with a latent UV map at 512×512, using Stable Diffusion 1.5 with a depth-based ControlNet. The pipeline supports up to 4 hit levels (visibility layers) and requires no fine-tuning of the pretrained diffusion model.

## Method

GOATEX decomposes the mesh into ordered visibility layers via ray-based hit level assignment: multi-view rays are cast, and each superface (constructed via Xatlas oversegmentation) is assigned a hit level based on the dominant intersection order weighted by cosine similarity between ray direction and face normal. A two-stage visibility control strategy then progressively exposes interior geometry: residual face clustering renders all untextured faces remaining from prior levels (analogous to peeling an onion), while normal flipping combined with backface culling hides already-textured outer surfaces and reveals previously occluded inner faces without distorting global structure. Each layer is independently textured using the SyncMVD multi-view diffusion module conditioned on rendered depth maps. Finally, a soft UV-space blending scheme merges per-layer textures using view-dependent visibility weights (cosine similarity between view direction and face normal) normalized via a modified softmax across hit levels, avoiding seams and style discontinuities at layer boundaries.

## Results

The paper evaluates via A/B preference tests (16 human raters + GPT-4o-mini/4o/4.1/o3) rather than standard metrics, since ground-truth textures are unavailable for occluded regions and CLIP-based metrics correlate poorly with human judgment. GOATEX achieves strong and consistent human preference across all four baselines. GPT-based evaluators show a lower relative advantage for GOATEX over TEXTure and SyncMVD (which leave interiors unpainted or use Voronoi filling), as GPTs favor surface smoothness over interior completeness. Pearson correlations between GPT and human ratings range from 0.22 (GPT-4o-mini) to 0.43 (GPT-4.1), and Cohen's κ for GPT-to-GPT agreement is 0.54, indicating higher internal consistency among GPTs than among human raters (κ=0.31).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against TEXTure [39] and SyncMVD [27] (project-and-inpaint methods), GOATEX wins on human preference but GPT evaluators slightly favor the baselines, because TEXTure/SyncMVD leave interiors unpainted or fill them with smooth Voronoi extrapolation, which GPTs perceive as 'cleaner.' Against Paint3D [53] and TEXGen [52] (UV-space inpainting methods), GOATEX wins on both human and GPT evaluations; these UV-space methods produce low-frequency textures (flat colors, repetitive patterns) in interior regions because they cannot differentiate interior from exterior surfaces within the UV map. The ablation (Table 1) confirms that the full GOATEX pipeline (hit-level assignment + superface construction + soft UV merging + residual face clustering + normal flipping & backface culling) achieves 91.16% average win rate over the SyncMVD baseline across four GPT evaluators, with the largest single gain (from 81.17% to 91.16%) coming from adding normal flipping and backface culling on top of residual face clustering.

## Relation to Prior Work

GOATEX builds directly on the SyncMVD multi-view diffusion module [27] as its per-layer texturing backbone, but departs fundamentally from the project-and-inpaint paradigm (TEXTure, Text2Tex, TexFusion) that dominates prior mesh texturing work. Where those methods unproject multi-view renderings onto UV maps and fill occluded regions with heuristics (Voronoi extrapolation), and where UV-space methods (Paint3D, TEXGen) lack explicit geometric context for interior surfaces, GOATEX introduces a ray-based hit-level decomposition that treats the mesh as a layered structure and progressively exposes occluded geometry through residual face clustering and normal flipping with backface culling. The weighted UV-space blending for merging per-layer textures is also novel relative to the overwriting or uniform averaging used in prior multi-view texturing pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first method to treat occluded interior texturing as a first-class problem in 3D mesh texturing, which has direct practical implications for gaming, AR/VR, and architectural visualization where users examine interior surfaces up close. The dual-prompting capability (separate text prompts for exterior vs. interior layers) opens a new axis of controllability for 3D asset creation. Key limitations include the purely geometric (non-semantic) hit-level assignment, which can split semantically unified regions across layers, and reliance on SD 1.5 for prompt fidelity. Open questions include whether semantic-aware refinement (e.g., part-segmentation models) can improve cross-region consistency, and whether the blending strategy can be integrated into the denoising process for tighter multi-level coherence.
