## Problem

Single-model self-alignment is unreliable because a model exhibits self-bias when judging its own generations and produces homogeneous responses that lack the diversity needed for robust preference learning. This makes the model itself the bottleneck for self-evolution beyond its training priors, as biased self-judgment propagates flaws and limited generation diversity blurs the distinction between preferred and rejected responses.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pool of m LLMs (10 Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct models independently fine-tuned on different Tulu-v2 domains) and a dataset of instructions X, and iteratively produces a set of aligned models M^T along with preference pairs generated through inter-model combat. Unlike single-model self-alignment, each instruction is processed by exactly two competing models per iteration, and the remaining models in the pool serve as judges, making the system's supervision signal inherently multi-perspective. The final output is the highest-reputation model from the pool, selected via validation performance for deployment.

## Method

SPARTA ALIGNMENT iteratively selects pairs of models via a probabilistic match-making strategy (random with probability α, or top-k closest in reputation with probability 1−α) to generate competing responses to a sampled instruction. All other models in the pool act as judges scoring both responses on a 0–10 scale, and scores are aggregated via a reputation-weighted scheme where higher-reputation judges carry more influence. An Elo-inspired reputation update rewards winners and penalizes losers, modulated by three factors: the aggregated score gap, a deviation-guided tanh scaling that adapts update magnitude to reputation stability, and a game-theoretic term granting larger gains for defeating stronger opponents. At the end of each iteration, all models are fine-tuned via DPO on the accumulated preference pairs, progressively aligning the entire pool over T iterations. An optional reweighting mechanism progressively down-weights the lowest-reputation judges to maintain preference label consistency as training stabilizes.

## Results

SPARTA ALIGNMENT achieves state-of-the-art results on 10 out of 12 datasets, with a 7.0% average improvement over the strongest baseline. Headline gains include Alpaca instruction-following (7.12 vs. 5.56 for SELF-REWARD, a 32.8% improvement over the best initial model), COM2 commonsense reasoning (6.35 vs. 5.74, a 20.5% gain over initialization), and GSM8K mathematical reasoning (0.813 vs. 0.785 for SPPO, a 4.5% gain over initialization). On the MATH benchmark, SPARTA consistently outperforms baselines across Easy (0.530), Medium (0.396), and Hard (0.212) difficulty levels.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SPARTA ALIGNMENT outperforms all four self-alignment baselines on 10 of 12 datasets. Against SELF-REWARD, SPARTA wins decisively on Alpaca (7.12 vs. 5.56), COM2 (6.35 vs. 5.74), and MedQA (0.662 vs. 0.623), while tying on Normad-RoT (0.707). Against SPPO, SPARTA leads on GSM8K (0.813 vs. 0.785), MATH-Hard (0.212 vs. 0.210), and TruthfulQA (0.424 vs. 0.421). Against SPIN, SPARTA wins on all datasets except KCross, where SPIN achieves 0.580 versus SPARTA's 0.560 (attributed to the small dataset size of 1,200 training samples). Against META-REWARD, SPARTA dominates across nearly all tasks, with the largest margin on Alpaca (7.12 vs. 5.49). The two non-wins (KCross loss to SPIN, and ties on Normad-Country and Normad-RoT) account for the 2 datasets where SPARTA does not achieve the top score.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the self-alignment paradigm (Self-Rewarding, Meta-Rewarding, SPIN, SPPO) from a single model judging itself to a multi-model collective competition framework, directly addressing the well-documented self-bias and diversity limitations of single-model self-feedback. The training mechanism builds on DPO for preference optimization, while the reputation system adapts Elo-style rating from game theory to weight peer judgments. It also connects to the broader multi-LLM collaboration literature (debates, modular systems) but departs by using structured head-to-head combat with iterative DPO updates rather than one-shot evaluation or debate.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that collective multi-model alignment can overcome the fundamental ceiling of single-model self-improvement, offering a practical pathway to stronger LLMs without external human supervision. However, the approach requires maintaining and training a pool of models simultaneously, significantly increasing compute costs, and the paper acknowledges that generation diversity diminishes over iterations, suggesting diminishing returns. Open questions include optimal pool composition and size, whether the reputation system converges to a stable ordering, and whether the framework scales beyond 7B-parameter models or to open-ended generation tasks where LLM-as-a-judge reliability is lower.
