## Problem

Speech generation lacks a unified foundation model that leverages large-scale unlabeled data for pre-training and efficiently adapts to diverse downstream tasks. Existing multi-task systems like UniAudio and SpeechX rely on autoregressive language models and require substantial paired training data per task, limiting scalability and generalization. The paper asks how to design a pre-training objective on unlabeled speech that enables a single model to be fine-tuned into state-of-the-art systems across TTS, voice conversion, speech enhancement, speaker extraction, and lip-to-speech with minimal task-specific data.

## Input / Output

The model consumes task-specific conditions in two forms: non-frame-level conditions (e.g., phoneme or text token sequences for TTS) are concatenated along the time axis with the masked SSL token sequence, while frame-level conditions (e.g., w2v-bert-2.0 features of source/noisy/mixed speech) are temporally interpolated and injected via an MLP adapter. A prompt speech prefix (0–40% of sequence length) is additionally provided unmasked to anchor speaker identity and prosody. The output is a two-stage discrete sequence: SSL tokens (single-layer VQ, 8192 codebook, from w2v-bert-2.0 layer 17) followed by acoustic tokens (12-layer RVQ, 1024 codebook per layer, DAC-based), which are decoded into a 24 kHz waveform.

## Method

Metis decomposes speech generation into a task-specific first stage (condition → SSL tokens) and a task-independent second stage (SSL tokens → acoustic tokens), and focuses its pre-training on the first stage. Pre-training uses an unconditional masked generative model: tokens in the SSL sequence are randomly masked following a sinusoidal schedule, and a Llama-style bidirectional transformer is trained to predict the masked tokens via cross-entropy, with a prefix kept unmasked as a prompt (probability 0.8) to foster in-context learning. Fine-tuning introduces task-specific conditions with minimal architectural changes—concatenation for non-frame-level inputs, MLP-adapter addition for frame-level inputs—and supports LoRA (r=4–32) to reduce trainable parameters to as few as 2–32M. The second stage is a separate masked generative acoustic decoder trained to recover masked acoustic tokens conditioned on SSL tokens and prompt acoustic tokens, generating layers sequentially at inference. The entire pipeline is trained on 300K hours of multilingual speech (Emilia + self-collected) for pre-training, then fine-tuned on as little as 0.4K–10K hours per task.

## Results

On zero-shot TTS (SeedTTS test-en), Metis-TTS fine-tuned with only 10K hours achieves WER 2.28 and SIM 0.72, outperforming MaskGCT (WER 2.47) trained on 100K hours, while the LoRA variant with just 1K hours matches SIM 0.72 of models trained 10–100× more data. On voice conversion (VCTK), Metis-VC LoRA 16 fine-tuned on only 0.4K hours achieves SIM 0.55, nearly 45% higher than the best baseline (Vevo, 0.38), and NISQA 4.46. On speech enhancement (DNS2020 with reverb), Metis-SE achieves the highest SIG (3.68), BAK (4.14), OVRL (3.44), and NISQA (4.56) across all three test subsets. On lip-to-speech (LRS2), Metis-L2S nearly doubles speaker similarity over Lip2Speech-Unit (59.73 vs. 29.34) while reducing WER from 33.64 to 32.28.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On zero-shot TTS, Metis-TTS (10K hours, full fine-tune) beats MaskGCT (100K hours) on WER (2.28 vs. 2.47 on SeedTTS-en) while matching SIM (0.72), and the LoRA variant with only 1K hours already matches the SIM of CosyVoice (170K hours) and XTTS-v2 (27K hours). On voice conversion, Metis-VC dominates on speaker similarity (SIM 0.55 vs. 0.38 for Vevo and 0.38 for HierSpeech++) using 7–150× less training data, though Vevo retains a WER advantage (3.48 vs. 4.49). On target speaker extraction, Metis-TSE LoRA 16 achieves the best NISQA (4.41 on LibriMix, surpassing ground truth 4.11) but trails WeSep on WER (12.52 vs. 6.19). On speech enhancement, Metis-SE achieves state-of-the-art SIG, BAK, OVRL, and NISQA across all three DNS2020 subsets, outperforming MaskSR, VoiceFixer, SELM, and TF-GridNet. On lip-to-speech, Metis-L2S outperforms Lip2Speech-Unit on every metric, with speaker similarity nearly doubling (59.73 vs. 29.34 on LRS2) and NISQA improving from 2.70 to 3.71.

## Relation to Prior Work

Metis extends the two-stage SSL-token-to-acoustic-token generation paradigm from MaskGCT [12] by introducing an unconditional masked generative pre-training phase on 300K hours of unlabeled speech, drawing the masked token prediction and iterative decoding mechanism from MaskGIT [41] and the layer-by-layer acoustic token generation from SoundStorm [30]. It departs from prior unified speech generation systems (UniAudio, SpeechX, VoiceBox) that rely on autoregressive modeling or flow matching and require large amounts of task-specific paired data, instead demonstrating that a single pre-trained masked generative model on SSL tokens can be efficiently adapted to diverse tasks via lightweight fine-tuning (LoRA) with task-specific conditions. The work also builds on the broader NLP/CV foundation model paradigm (BERT, MAE) of pre-training on unlabeled data followed by task-specific fine-tuning, which had not previously been applied to speech generation with masked generative models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Metis validates the pre-train-then-fine-tune paradigm for speech generation, showing that a single model pre-trained on unlabeled data can achieve state-of-the-art results across five heterogeneous tasks (TTS, VC, SE, TSE, lip-to-speech) with as few as 2M–32M trainable parameters and up to 300× less task-specific data than task-specific baselines. This has practical implications for reducing the cost of deploying speech generation systems and for enabling rapid adaptation to new tasks. Open questions remain: the model still requires fine-tuning per task (unlike LLMs that handle new tasks zero-shot), the two discrete representations (SSL tokens and acoustic tokens) are not unified, and extending the framework to non-speech audio (music, sound effects) is left for future work. The text-guided TSE result (WER 2.70, below ground truth 4.27) without explicit training on that task combination hints at emergent multi-task composition capabilities that warrant further investigation.
