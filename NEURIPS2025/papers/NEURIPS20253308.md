## Problem

Local search operations such as threshold adjustment and cut exchange are widely used heuristically in decision tree learning (e.g., in simulated annealing and genetic algorithms), yet no systematic algorithmic analysis of finding the optimal solution within a local neighborhood has been provided. This paper fills that gap by showing both problems are NP-complete and delivering a comprehensive parameterized-complexity classification that identifies which structural parameters (number of features d, domain size D, tree size s, number of operations k, error bound t) drive hardness versus tractability.

## Input / Output

The method takes a labeled training dataset (E, λ) over d features, an existing decision tree T with s internal nodes, and integers k (budget of local-search operations) and t (target error bound). It produces a modified decision tree of the same structure with at most t classification errors, where threshold adjustment changes only cut thresholds while cut exchange may also swap the feature at a cut; leaf labels may be freely relabeled. The key constraint is that the tree topology is fixed—only the cuts and leaf assignments are modified—making this a local-neighborhood optimization rather than a from-scratch learning problem.

## Method

The paper's core algorithmic contribution is a dynamic-programming approach parameterized by d+D that encodes the feature space as threshold sequences (l_i, r_i)_{i∈[d]} and computes minimum errors via a recurrence over tree nodes, yielding an FPT algorithm in (D+1)^{2d}·|I|^{O(1)} time for both threshold adjustment and cut exchange, and extending to a combined pruning-and-local-search problem (DT-PLS). For threshold adjustment parameterized by s+t, a branch-and-bound algorithm guesses the modified cuts and per-leaf error distribution, then uses recursive binary search to recover optimal thresholds. On the hardness side, reductions from MULTICOLORED CLIQUE and HITTING SET establish W[1]-hardness for d and W[2]-hardness for k and t, while a reduction from threshold adjustment to cut exchange (via per-leaf gadgets forcing feature preservation) transfers hardness results between the two problems. A proof-of-concept implementation on 47 PMLB datasets confirms that heuristically pruned C4.5/J48 trees are near-locally-optimal, with at most 2 error reduction from up to two local-search operations.

## Results

On 47 PMLB benchmark datasets, the paper's exact local-search algorithm (based on Theorem 4.3) shows that C4.5/J48 pruned trees are close to locally optimal: with at most two threshold adjustments, only 7 of 47 trees could be improved, and with at most two cut exchanges, only 9 could be improved, with a maximum reduction of 2 misclassifications. For threshold adjustment, 33/47 (70.21%) instances were solvable within a 1-hour time limit; for cut exchange, only 17/47 (36.17%) were solvable. When combining pruning with local search on unpruned trees, 10 and 17 trees showed average error reductions of 5.16% and 8.92%, respectively, indicating that local search becomes more valuable under aggressive pruning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is the C4.5/J48 pruned decision tree (WEKA 3.8.5), which serves as the 'Initial' column in Table 1. The paper's exact local-search algorithm wins on 7 of 10 datasets with one threshold adjustment (e.g., ecoli drops from 10 to 8 errors, breast-cancer from 31 to 30) and on 9 of 10 with one cut exchange, but the improvements are uniformly small—never more than 2 errors. On cleveland-nominal and colic, a single adjustment yields no improvement, while a single exchange does (23→22 and 15→14, respectively). The key takeaway is that standard heuristics are already near-locally-optimal: the paper concludes that 'the decrement in errors tends to be mild,' suggesting limited practical upside from local search on typical heuristic outputs. However, when combined with aggressive pruning, local search achieves larger average error reductions (5.16%–8.92%), indicating its value increases as the tree is simplified.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the parameterized-complexity line of work on decision trees (Gahlawat & Zehavi [10], Ordyniak & Szeider [34], Harviainen et al. [14]) by shifting focus from learning trees from scratch or pruning them to the previously unanalyzed problem of finding the optimal modification within a bounded local neighborhood. The dynamic-programming algorithms for threshold adjustment and cut exchange directly adapt the subtree-raising DP of Harviainen et al. [14], while the hardness reductions build on MULTICOLORED CLIQUE and HITTING SET constructions used in prior decision-tree and pruning hardness results. The local search operations themselves (threshold adjustment, cut exchange) have been employed heuristically in simulated annealing, genetic algorithms, and SAT-based approaches for over a decade, but no systematic algorithmic or complexity-theoretic analysis of the exact optimization problem existed prior to this work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first comprehensive parameterized-complexity classification for local search on decision trees, identifying precisely which parameter combinations yield fixed-parameter tractability (notably d+D) and which remain W[1]- or W[2]-hard. The empirical finding that heuristically computed C4.5/J48 trees are already close to locally optimal (at most 2 errors reducible with two operations) is practically reassuring but also limits the immediate utility of exact local-search algorithms. Open questions include whether THRESHOLD ADJUSTMENT is FPT for d+ℓ+t, whether CUT EXCHANGE is FPT for d+k+t, and how these results extend to ensembles or structure-changing operations like subtree switching. The FPT algorithm for d+D also enables simultaneous local search and pruning, which the experiments show becomes increasingly valuable under aggressive pruning regimes.
