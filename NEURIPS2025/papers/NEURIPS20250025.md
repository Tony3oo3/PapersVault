## Problem

Molecular graph foundation models (MGFMs) face unique fine-tuning challenges absent in vision or NLP: pre-training datasets are orders of magnitude smaller (O(100M) vs. billions of samples), downstream tasks often have only tens to hundreds of labeled molecules, and objectives span both classification and regression. Despite the proliferation of robust fine-tuning methods, no systematic benchmark existed to determine which fine-tuning mechanism (weight-based, representation-based, or partial) is appropriate given the pre-training paradigm and downstream task type.

## Input / Output

The benchmark consumes pre-trained molecular graph encoders (GNN or Graph Transformer backbones) and fine-tunes them on 12 downstream datasets (8 classification, 4 regression) under random, scaffold, and size splits with 50, 100, 500, or full training samples. For the proposed DWiSE-FT, the method takes two weight tensors—the pre-trained θ_pre and the fully fine-tuned θ_ft—and produces a single interpolated model with per-layer mixing coefficients α_i, optimized via validation-loss gradients. The final output is a molecular property prediction (AUC for classification, RMSE for regression) from the interpolated encoder paired with the fine-tuned prediction head.

## Method

ROFT-MOL systematically benchmarks 8 fine-tuning methods grouped into three mechanisms—weight-based (WiSE-FT, L²-SP), representation-based (Feature-Map, BSS), and partial (LP, Surgical-FT, LP-FT)—across 6 pre-trained models spanning self-supervised (Mole-BERT, GraphMAE, MoleculeSTM) and supervised (Graphium-Toy, Graphium-Large, GraphGPS) paradigms. The key empirical finding is that the optimal mechanism depends on both pre-training type and task type: weight-based methods dominate for self-supervised models (WiSE-FT for classification, L²-SP for regression), while representation-based methods (Surgical-FT, Feature-Map) are best for supervised models. Building on Proposition 1, which shows L²-SP's optimal solution is a variable-coefficient linear interpolation between θ_pre and θ_ft*, the authors propose DWiSE-FT: a post-hoc weight ensemble that assigns a distinct mixing coefficient α_i to each encoder layer rather than a single global α, with α_i values optimized via validation-loss gradients inspired by DARTS. This retains WiSE-FT's plug-and-play simplicity (no retraining needed) while capturing the per-weight adaptivity that makes L²-SP effective for regression.

## Results

On the ROFT-MOL benchmark, the best fine-tuning method depends on pre-training paradigm and task type: for self-supervised models (Mole-BERT), weight-based methods dominate in few-shot settings (WiSE-FT achieves AVG AUC 73.63 on classification scaffold split; L²-SP achieves AVG-R* 3.75 on regression scaffold split), while for supervised models (Graphium-Toy), representation-based methods lead (Surgical-FT achieves AVG AUC 76.25 on classification; Feature-Map achieves AVG-R* 2.50 on regression). The proposed DWiSE-FT achieves AVG-R of 2.00 on Random/Fewshot-50 regression (vs. WiSE-FT 3.75, L²-SP 3.00) and 1.75 on Size/Fewshot-50 (vs. WiSE-FT 4.00, L²-SP 2.50), matching or exceeding the best-performing method in most scenarios while retaining post-hoc usability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DWiSE-FT consistently outperforms WiSE-FT across all splits and few-shot settings on regression tasks (e.g., AVG-R 1.75 vs. 4.00 on Size/Fewshot-50; 2.00 vs. 3.75 on Random/Fewshot-50), confirming that layer-wise variable mixing coefficients better capture the fine-grained numerical patterns regression demands. Against L²-SP, DWiSE-FT matches or beats it in most scenarios (e.g., 1.25 vs. 3.25 on Random/Fewshot-100) while avoiding the need for retraining with different regularization coefficients. In the broader benchmark, WiSE-FT and L²-SP (weight-based) are the top methods for self-supervised pre-trained models in few-shot settings, whereas Surgical-FT and Feature-Map (representation/partial-based) dominate for supervised pre-trained models (Surgical-FT achieves AVG AUC 76.25 vs. WiSE-FT 74.31 on Graphium-Toy classification scaffold split). BSS underperforms Feature-Map in most supervised settings, and LP-FT/Surgical-FT degrade to the worst methods in few-shot settings due to underfitting. The key takeaway is that no single fine-tuning method is universally best: the optimal strategy is determined by the pre-training paradigm (self-supervised → weight-based; supervised → representation-based) and task type (classification → WiSE-FT; regression → L²-SP or DWiSE-FT).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the robust fine-tuning literature (WiSE-FT, L²-SP, Surgical-FT, LP-FT, Feature-Map, BSS) from its original CV/NLP context into the molecular graph domain, where data scarcity and the coexistence of regression and classification tasks create distinct challenges. It builds directly on WiSE-FT's post-hoc weight interpolation and L²-SP's weight regularization, formalizing their connection via Proposition 1 (L²-SP's optimum is a variable-coefficient linear interpolation) to motivate DWiSE-FT, a layer-wise generalization of WiSE-FT. The benchmark itself departs from prior molecular pre-training studies (Mole-BERT, GraphMAE, Graphium, GraphGPS) by systematically evaluating fine-tuning strategies rather than pre-training objectives, filling a gap where prior work focused on which pre-training to use but not how to best adapt the resulting model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides actionable, pre-training-conditional guidance for molecular ML practitioners: weight-based fine-tuning for self-supervised models, representation-based for supervised models, with task-type (classification vs. regression) as a secondary selector. The finding that LP is consistently worst for self-supervised molecular models—contrasting its known robustness in CV—highlights that molecular representations from generic pre-training are insufficiently task-aligned, a nuance absent from prior transfer-learning literature. Open questions include whether these findings generalize to 3D molecular representations, larger-scale pre-training, and whether DWiSE-FT's layer-wise α optimization scales to deeper architectures. The benchmark's scope (6 models, 12 datasets, 36 settings) is thorough but still limited to 2D graphs and a handful of pre-training objectives.
