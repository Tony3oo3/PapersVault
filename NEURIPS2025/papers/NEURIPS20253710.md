## Problem

Parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) has become the standard paradigm for adapting large pre-trained models in NLP and vision, yet it remains entirely unexplored for Large Operator Models (LOMs) that learn PDE solution operators. The paper identifies that LoRA—the dominant PEFT technique in LLMs—suffers a depth-amplified spectral error floor when applied to Fourier-based operator architectures, while the underlying reason (and the correct alternative) has not been characterized. The central question is whether PEFT can be redesigned to explicitly respect the frequency-adaptive, spectrally sparse structure of PDE solution spaces.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a 3D (or 2D) spatial PDE field tensor of shape B×C×H×W×L, applies a real 3D FFT to obtain complex Fourier coefficients, and partitions the retained lowest M spatial modes into B contiguous radial frequency bands. Adapters operate on the real and imaginary parts of each band-block slice independently in the spectral domain, after which an inverse FFT maps the adapted spectrum back to physical space and a residual connection adds the original input. The output is the predicted PDE solution field (e.g., 3D velocity for Navier-Stokes, magnetic field for MHD) at the same spatial resolution as the input.

## Method

F-Adapter grafts frequency-adaptive bottleneck MLPs onto each Fourier Attention layer of a frozen LOM backbone (DPOT-H, 1B parameters). The key mechanism is a dimension-frequency schedule: the bottleneck width r_b for band b follows r_b = ⌊r_min + (r_max − r_min)(1 − f_b/M)^p⌋, allocating wider adapters to low-frequency bands (which carry most PDE solution energy) and narrower ones to high-frequency bands. Each adapter is a residual two-layer MLP (down-project to r_b, GELU, up-project back) with zero-initialized up-projection so the adapted path is identity at initialization. Three adapters (input, mid, output) are attached per block-band slice, and the total overhead stays below 2% of backbone parameters. The design is theoretically grounded: Proposition 3.1 proves LoRA incurs a depth-amplified lower bound σ_{Kr+1} on approximation error, Proposition 3.2 shows adapters achieve exponentially decaying error O(e^{−cm}) in the Fourier domain, and Proposition 3.3 establishes that PDE solution energy concentrates in low-frequency modes with high-frequency tail decaying as O(K^{d−2s}).

## Results

On the 1B-parameter DPOT-H backbone for 3D Navier-Stokes forecasting, F-Adapter (1.91% trainable params) achieves L2RE of 0.5329 (Rand M=1.0), 0.4639 (Rand M=0.1), and 0.4523 (Turbulence M=1.0), reducing L2RE by 16.7–25.4% over LoRA variants while using comparable GPU memory (~15.9 GB). On 2D shallow-water equations, F-Adapter reaches L2RE 0.0116 versus 0.1081 for LoRA; on 3D MHD under severe data scarcity (24 trajectories), it achieves 0.6341 versus 0.9845 for LoRA. Full fine-tuning (100% params) still leads (e.g., 0.4002 on Rand M=0.1) but requires ~50× more trainable parameters and ~1.4× training memory.

## Baselines & Comparisons

F-Adapter decisively outperforms all LoRA-family baselines: LoRA (0.6395), AdaLoRA (0.6726), HydraLoRA (0.6333), and RandLoRA (0.6370) on Rand M=1.0, with F-Adapter at 0.5329—a 16.7–20.7% L2RE reduction. Against the closest competitor, Vanilla Adapter (0.5496), F-Adapter improves by 3.0% (M=1.0) to 5.2% (M=0.1) with only a modest parameter increase (1.91% vs. 1.16%). FiLM Adapter (0.5655) and Prompt Tuning (0.6378) are also clearly surpassed. SVFT (0.6375), despite using more parameters (2.31%), performs comparably to LoRA and trails F-Adapter by ~16%. On the more challenging SWE-2D and MHD-3D tasks (Table 3), the gap widens further: F-Adapter achieves 0.0116 vs. 0.1081 (LoRA) on SWE-2D and 0.6341 vs. 0.9845 (LoRA) on MHD-3D, confirming that frequency-adaptive capacity allocation is critical in high-frequency and data-scarce regimes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the bottleneck MLP adapter architecture (Houlsby et al., ICML 2019) by making the bottleneck width frequency-adaptive, allocating more capacity to low-frequency spectral bands where PDE solutions concentrate energy. It deliberately departs from LoRA (Hu et al., ICLR 2022) and its variants, which dominate PEFT in NLP/CV, by proving a depth-amplified spectral error floor that makes rank-constrained linear updates fundamentally unsuitable for Fourier-based operator layers. The work also builds on the emerging Large Operator Model (LOM) paradigm (DPOT, FNO variants) and on classical harmonic analysis of PDE solutions to ground its architectural choices in spectral energy decay.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic PEFT study for pretrained LOMs, establishing that the PEFT recipe that works for LLMs (LoRA) does not transfer to Fourier-based scientific operators and that frequency-aware adapter allocation is the right design principle. The practical impact is significant for the growing LOM community: updating <2% of parameters achieves near-full-fine-tuning accuracy on challenging 3D Navier-Stokes and MHD tasks. Key limitations include reliance on FNO-based backbones for the strongest results (adapters degrade on transformer-based Poseidon), and the spectral energy concentration assumption may not hold for highly non-linear multi-physics flows. Open questions include whether the frequency-adaptive allocation principle generalizes to non-FFT architectures and whether it remains effective under severe distribution shift.
