## Problem

Indirect time-of-flight (iToF) imaging faces a fundamental trade-off between maximum unambiguous range and depth precision due to phase wrapping, and existing remedies (dual-frequency modulation, scene priors) either increase hardware complexity or fail to resolve the intrinsic periodic ambiguity. Additionally, signal-to-noise ratio degrades severely with distance due to optical attenuation, further limiting long-range depth sensing fidelity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254319/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes K=4 binary-coded exposure measurements from an ICMOS sensor, each obtained by integrating the reflected burst-modulated signal against a learned coding function over a 20 ns modulation window within a 1 ms burst period. The differentiable forward model synthesizes these measurements from scene reflectance, ambient light, depth-dependent inverse-square attenuation, and sensor noise (Poisson dark current + Gaussian readout noise). The network outputs a per-pixel depth map, achieving centimeter-level accuracy at distances up to 90+ meters with a single modulation frequency.

## Method

BE-ToF decomposes the total phase delay into φ₁ (set by a controllable time delay τ that extends the unambiguous range to c·T_burst/2) and φ₂ (recovered within the short modulation period), fundamentally eliminating phase wrapping under single-frequency modulation. An end-to-end differentiable framework jointly optimizes the binary coding functions and a Restormer-based RSCF-Net (featuring Channel Feature Extraction Blocks for inter-channel measurement fusion, Multi-scale Feature Fusion Blocks for coarse-to-fine spatial integration, and ECA-enhanced skip connections). The training objective combines MSE depth fidelity, Fisher information guidance for SNR-aware coding, a double-well potential loss to enforce binary convergence of the coding functions, and a first-order difference loss to suppress hardware-impractical narrow peaks. Training employs a curriculum strategy that progressively reduces SNR from high to low over epochs, ensuring robustness across the full distance range.

## Results

On the NYU-V2 dataset, BE-ToF achieves MAE of 5.90 mm at 0–3 m (high SNR) and 29.58 mm at 90–93 m (low SNR), using only single-frequency modulation. At the longest tested range (90–93 m, low SNR), BE-ToF reduces error by 4.7× over the best single-frequency baseline (FisherToF, 138.11 mm) and by 8.3× over the best dual-frequency baseline (Sine+PS, 244.29 mm). Real-world prototype experiments confirm centimeter-level accuracy, with MAE of 1.41–3.83 cm across indoor and outdoor scenes, outperforming all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BE-ToF outperforms all six baselines across every distance and SNR condition. Against the strongest single-frequency baseline, FisherToF [11], BE-ToF reduces MAE from 138.11 mm to 29.58 mm at 90–93 m / low SNR (4.7× improvement), demonstrating that burst-mode modulation fundamentally eliminates the phase-wrapping degradation that plagues conventional iToF at long range. Against dual-frequency baselines, the advantage is even more pronounced: Sine+PS [5] degrades to 244.29 mm and Square+PS [5] to 140.98 mm at 90–93 m / low SNR, while DeepToF [23] (dual-frequency + learned network) reaches 56.97 mm—still nearly 2× worse than BE-ToF. In the reconstruction-network comparison (Table 1c), BE-ToF's RSCF-Net beats MaskToF [31] (38.60 mm) and FisherToF [11] (31.83 mm) at 90–93 m / low SNR. For coding-function comparison (Appendix Table 4), the learned binary codes achieve 23.51 mm average MAE at 90–93 m, surpassing Hamiltonian codes [44] (27.11 mm) and M-sequences [45] (38.81 mm), while also attaining the highest Fisher information (3.93×10⁶ vs. 2.92×10⁶ for Hamiltonian). Notably, BE-ToF achieves all of this with single-frequency modulation, avoiding the hardware complexity and synchronization overhead of multi-frequency systems.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground in the iToF imaging lineage by introducing a burst-mode modulation paradigm (BE-ToF) that fundamentally decouples unambiguous range (set by the burst period) from depth precision (set by the short modulation period), thereby eliminating phase wrapping without the hardware and computational overhead of multi-frequency modulation. On the algorithmic side, it extends the end-to-end learnable imaging tradition (Metzler, Sitzmann, Chugunov, Li/FisherToF) by jointly optimizing binary coding functions and a Restormer-based reconstruction network, with a novel double-well + first-order-difference loss to enforce hardware-implementable codes. The conceptual departure from prior iToF work is that phase wrapping is avoided by construction (the reflected phase sweeps [0, 2π] within one burst) rather than resolved post-hoc via multi-frequency demodulation or scene priors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

BE-ToF offers a practical path to long-distance, high-fidelity depth sensing with single-frequency hardware, which is significant for cost-sensitive deployments in autonomous driving and robotics where multi-frequency iToF systems add synchronization complexity and dToF demands ultra-short pulses. The main limitation is that the imaging range is confined to [c·τ/2, c·(τ+Tm)/2], so higher precision narrows the usable window; the authors propose temporal scanning or coarse-to-fine strategies as future directions. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to outdoor scenes with atmospheric scattering and beam divergence (acknowledged but not modeled in simulation), and whether the learned coding functions transfer across different hardware platforms with different minimum exposure gates.
