## Problem

Gradient estimation for parameterized quantum circuits (PQCs) is a bottleneck for variational quantum algorithms, as existing methods like the parameter shift rule are restricted to ansätze with two distinct eigenvalues, finite differences suffer from slow convergence, and general-purpose approaches incur exponential classical or quantum overhead. No prior method provides efficient gradient estimation for generic PQCs without modifying the ansatz structure while maintaining polynomial resource scaling.

## Input / Output

The method takes a PQC expressed as U(a) = exp(iA(a)) with a Pauli decomposition A(a) = Σ a_s σ^s, an initial state ρ, and a fixed observable O, and produces the full gradient vector ∇L(a) ∈ ℝ^p. The gradient is structured as a linear combination of p Hadamard test expectation values D_s with coefficients determined by a p×p matrix V built from the binary encoding of Pauli commutation relations. When the shadow norm of the observable is bounded, all p expectation values are estimated from only O(log p) copies of the output state ρ^out, yielding an exponential reduction in measurement shots compared to O(p) for prior methods.

## Method

The core contribution is a binary encoding of Pauli strings into (ℤ₄ × ℤ₂^{2n}) that captures commutation relations via a sign function δ, enabling the differential of the matrix exponential (from Lie algebra theory) to be expressed as a finite linear combination of Hadamard test expectation values when the Pauli set is closed under commutation. Specifically, the gradient takes the closed form ∇L = D(I − e^V)V^{−1}, where D is the vector of Hadamard test values and V is a p×p matrix whose columns encode the adjoint action of the Hamiltonian on each Pauli generator. For the general case where the Pauli terms are not closed under commutation, a master's theorem (Theorem 4) provides an infinite-series expression, and the method applies to the dynamical Lie algebra generated by the Hamiltonian, with complexity governed by its dimensionality. Classical shadow tomography is then applied to the set of p Hadamard test observables to estimate all gradient components simultaneously with O(log p) state copies and polynomial classical post-processing time O(p³ + pn).

## Results

The proposed method achieves O(log p) sample complexity for gradient estimation with zero circuit modifications, compared to O(p) for stochastic and SU(N) parameter shift rules and O(log²p) for the shadow tomography backpropagation baseline. The classical running time is Õ(n^b + n^{3a}), which is polynomial in the number of qubits n, versus exp(Θ(n)) for the SU(N) Lie algebraic method and p·exp(O(n)) for the shadow tomography approach. The algorithm requires O(p) Hadamard tests and O(p³ + pn) classical post-processing time in the general case, reducing to O(log p) copies of the output state when the shadow norm of the observable is bounded.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the stochastic parameter shift rule (SPSR) of Banchi & Crooks [2021] and Wierichs et al. [2022], this paper achieves an exponential reduction in sample complexity (O(log p) vs. O(p)) while requiring zero circuit modifications versus p distinct shifted circuits. The Nyquist parameter shift rule (NPSR) of Theis [2023] has sample complexity Õ(p‖A‖), which is strictly worse than O(log p) and additionally depends on the operator norm ‖A‖; it also requires circuit changes. The SU(N) Lie algebraic method of Wiersema et al. [2024] matches the O(p) sample complexity of SPSR but suffers from exponential classical running time exp(Θ(n)) due to computing the full Jacobian of the matrix representation, whereas this paper's running time is polynomial Õ(n^b + n^{3a}). The shadow tomography backpropagation of Abbas et al. [2023] achieves O(log²p) sample complexity (slightly worse than O(log p)) but requires exponential classical memory p·2^Õ(n) and running time p·exp(O(n)), making it infeasible for large systems. Overall, this paper dominates all five baselines on at least one of the three complexity measures (sample complexity, circuit changes, running time) and is the only method requiring no ansatz modification.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies three previously separate threads: the Hadamard-test-based parameter shift rule (Mitarai et al. 2018, Schuld et al. 2019), which was restricted to two-eigenvalue ansätze; the differential of the matrix exponential from Lie algebra (Rossmann 2006), which provides the adjoint-operator expansion; and classical shadow tomography (Huang et al. 2020), which enables joint estimation of many observables with logarithmic sample complexity. The key departure is showing that when the Pauli strings in the Hamiltonian are closed under commutation (i.e., form a subalgebra), the infinite adjoint series collapses to a finite linear combination, making the Hadamard test applicable to generic PQCs without ansatz modification. This contrasts with stochastic PSR (Banchi & Crooks 2021, Wierichs et al. 2022) requiring spectral decomposition, and Lie-algebraic gradient methods (Wiersema et al. 2024) with exponential classical time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result provides an exponential reduction in measurement shots (O(log p) vs. O(p)) and polynomial classical time for gradient estimation, which is a critical bottleneck for scaling variational quantum algorithms. The polynomial DLA assumption is satisfied by physically relevant models (transverse-field Ising, Kitaev chain) and is also the condition that avoids barren plateaus, linking gradient efficiency to trainability. The main limitation is that when the DLA dimensionality grows exponentially, only a biased truncated approximation is available, and no lower bounds on the required resources are established. Open directions include extending the framework to higher-order derivatives and characterizing the exponential-DLA regime more precisely.
