## Problem

Vehicle trajectory learning is hindered by two gaps: (1) travel purposes are encoded in textual descriptions of roads and POIs, and incorporating language models to extract them imposes heavy computational overhead on trajectory encoders; (2) high-frequency GPS sampling introduces redundant points that degrade both encoding efficiency and embedding quality, yet existing compression methods are rule-based with high time complexity rather than learnable.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250735/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a vehicle trajectory as a sequence of (GPS coordinate, road segment ID, timestamp) tuples, along with road network topology and POI textual descriptions from AMap/OpenStreetMap. Before encoding, explicit redundant points (vehicle stops, steady-pace points on the same road segment) are filtered out. The output is a single fixed-dimensional embedding vector (E=256) obtained by concatenating the final GPS and road perspective outputs and applying mean pooling, which is then paired with a lightweight fully-connected predictor for downstream tasks.

## Method

TrajMamba stacks L=5 Traj-Mamba blocks, each containing two multi-input selective SSMs: a GPS-SSM whose input-dependent parameters (B, C, Δ) are generated from high-order movement features (speed, acceleration, angle), and a Road-SSM whose parameters are derived from the GPS-SSM output, enabling cross-perspective interaction via dot-product gating. Travel purpose is integrated through a contrastive pre-training stage that aligns the encoder output with road and POI views (built from a shared pre-trained textual encoder plus local/global context aggregation processed by Mamba2 blocks) using InfoNCE loss, so no extra computation is added at inference. A second knowledge-distillation stage employs a learnable mask generator—combining sparse stochastic gates with a lightweight Mamba block that captures temporal correlations—to select key trajectory points, then aligns the compressed embedding with the frozen teacher's full-trajectory embedding via an MEC (maximum entropy coding) loss plus a Gaussian error-function mask-length penalty.

## Results

On the Destination Prediction task (fine-tune), TrajMamba achieves GPS RMSE of 129.47 m (Chengdu) and 236.33 m (Xian), and road-segment Acc@1 of 58.21% and 48.81%, respectively, outperforming the strongest baseline JGRM by over 40% in GPS RMSE on Chengdu. On Arrival Time Estimation, it reaches RMSE of 50.17 s (Chengdu) and 74.75 s (Xian), a 23–32% improvement over JGRM. On Similar Trajectory Search, it attains Acc@1 of 96.53% (Chengdu) and 97.97% (Xian) with Mean Rank of 1.15 and 1.07, surpassing JGRM by ~10 percentage points in Acc@1. Overall, the paper reports average enhancements of 18.28%, 27.89%, and 17.68% across the three tasks, while maintaining the lowest embedding time (1.72 s / 3.92 s) among all methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TrajMamba outperforms all nine baselines on every metric across all three downstream tasks and both datasets. The closest competitor is JGRM (WWW 2024), a dual-encoder model that jointly embeds GPS and route trajectories; TrajMamba beats it by 40.1% in GPS RMSE on Chengdu DP (129.47 vs 215.99 m), by 23.1% in ATE RMSE on Chengdu (50.17 vs 65.24 s), and by 9.9 percentage points in STS Acc@1 on Chengdu (96.53% vs 86.63%). Among Transformer-based baselines, START and MMTEC are the strongest, but they still trail TrajMamba by 15–25% in GPS RMSE and 4–6 points in road-segment Acc@1. RNN-based methods (t2vec, Trembr) and earlier Transformer/MLM approaches (CTLE, Toast) perform substantially worse, with GPS RMSE values 2–4× higher. In terms of efficiency, TrajMamba's embedding time (1.72 s on Chengdu) is 55× faster than JGRM (94.05 s) and 17× faster than CTLE (16.74 s), while its model size (5.41 MB) is comparable to RNN-based methods and 4.3× smaller than JGRM (23.09 MB). Under the without-fine-tune setting, TrajMamba's advantage over JGRM widens further (e.g., 365.60 vs 406.15 m GPS RMSE on Chengdu DP), confirming that its pre-training captures rich transferable semantics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the pre-trained trajectory embedding lineage (t2vec, CTLE, Toast, TrajCL, LightPath, START, MMTEC, JGRM) by replacing Transformer or RNN encoders with Mamba2's multi-input selective SSMs, achieving linear-time sequence modeling. It departs from JGRM (the closest prior work, which also jointly models GPS and road perspectives) by introducing a travel purpose-aware pre-training that leverages textual road/POI encoders during training but adds zero overhead at inference, and by replacing rule-based trajectory compression (Douglas-Peucker, Visvalingam-Whyatt) with a learnable mask generator trained via knowledge distillation. The MEC loss for compression aligns with MMTEC's entropy-coding philosophy but is applied to a compressed-vs-full trajectory distillation setting rather than multi-view contrastive learning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 55× reduction in embedding time (1.7s vs 94s for JGRM on Chengdu) while simultaneously improving accuracy across all three downstream tasks makes high-quality trajectory representation practically viable for real-time intelligent transportation systems where inference latency is critical. The clean separation between expensive pre-training (contrastive + distillation) and lightweight inference addresses a real deployment bottleneck. A key limitation acknowledged by the authors is that cross-city transfer is infeasible due to dataset-specific road/POI semantics, leaving the development of universal spatial-textual embeddings as an open problem for broader generalization.
