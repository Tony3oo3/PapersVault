## Problem

All prior algorithms for noisy band-limited signal interpolation achieved approximation ratios above 100, a barrier rooted in a 'triangle-inequality bottleneck' where three dominant error sources (frequency truncation, polynomial approximation, and linear regression) accumulated additively. This paper breaks that barrier, delivering the first polynomial-time algorithm with a provable (3+√2+ε)-approximation guarantee, and a refined shrinking-range variant achieving (√2+ε+c) on any interior sub-interval.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes poly(k, ε⁻¹, log(FT/δ)) noisy time-domain observations x(t) = x*(t) + g(t) sampled over [0,T], where x* is a k-sparse continuous signal with frequencies in [-F,F] and g is arbitrary adversarial noise satisfying a constant signal-to-noise ratio. It outputs a poly(k, ε⁻¹, log(1/δ))-sparse band-limited signal y(t) with a guaranteed L² error bound of (3+√2+ε)‖g‖_T + δ‖x*‖_T over the full interval, or (√2+ε+c)‖g‖_T + δ‖x*‖_T over the shrunk interval (1−c)T. The noise model is fully adversarial (no distributional assumptions), and the frequency gap η must satisfy η ≥ Ω(1/T).

## Method

The approach is a two-stage pipeline—frequency estimation followed by signal estimation—unified under a hierarchical noise-cancellation analysis. For frequency estimation, a new ultra-high-sensitivity filter family amplifies each heavy-cluster's energy while simultaneously canceling an equal-scale portion of adversarial noise, raising the recoverable energy threshold from Θ(1) to (1+√ε) and eliminating a factor-of-2 loss. The key analytic insight is a signal-noise cancellation identity (Eq. 3) showing that the filtered noise passed to the signal-estimation stage is correlated with the unrecoverable signal energy, so bounding them jointly yields a multiplicative (3+√2) factor instead of the additive sum of three constants. For signal estimation, a three-step discrete Fourier set-query framework (uniform sketching via energy-bounded sampling, sketch distillation via a well-balanced sampler built on Randomized BSS, and weighted least-squares regression) recovers the polynomial-approximated signal with (1+ε) relative error. The shrinking-range variant exploits two 'edge strips' of length cT/2 as disposable budget, using a range-restricted filter that suppresses boundary energy so the residual to approximate is strictly smaller, pushing the constant down to √2+ε+c.

## Results

The paper's main theoretical guarantee is a (3 + √2 + ε)-approximation for noisy band-limited interpolation, i.e., ‖y − x*‖_T ≤ (3 + √2 + ε)‖g‖_T + δ‖x*‖_T, using poly(k, ε⁻¹, log(1/δ)) log(FT) samples and nearly-linear time. A refined shrinking-range variant achieves (√2 + ε + c)-approximation on any interior sub-interval (1−c)T. This breaks the long-standing C > 100 barrier of all prior algorithms (best: Chen et al., 2016) and the 3-approximation barrier imposed by the triangle inequality, bringing the constant close to the information-theoretic optimum of 1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is against Chen et al. (2016), whose algorithm achieves only a C > 100 approximation ratio due to additive accumulation of three error terms (frequency truncation, polynomial approximation, and linear regression) each controlled only up to a constant factor. The new method reduces this to 3 + √2 + ε ≈ 4.414 + ε by introducing a signal-noise cancellation argument that replaces the additive triangle-inequality bound with a single multiplicative term. Against Li et al. (2021a), the comparison is more nuanced: their algorithm achieves near-optimal output sparsity (k·polylog) but with a polylogarithmic approximation factor that grows with k, 1/δ, and 1/c, and uses a different error metric (‖x̂*‖₁ in the frequency domain rather than ‖x*‖_T in the time domain). The shrinking-range variant further improves the constant to √2 + ε + c by exploiting boundary slack, which no prior method achieves. The paper also identifies and overcomes a fundamental 3-approximation barrier inherent in the Chen et al. framework, where the frequency estimation step cannot push E₁ below 2N without losing recoverability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the band-limited interpolation framework of Chen et al. (FOCS 2016), which decomposed recovery into heavy-cluster identification, filter-based frequency estimation, polynomial approximation, and signal estimation but accumulated three additive error terms via triangle inequality, yielding a constant C > 100. The key departure is a unified 'signal-noise cancellation' analysis that tracks how filtered noise correlates with unrecoverable signal energy across stages, collapsing the three additive losses into a single multiplicative bound of 3+√2+ε. The frequency estimation step draws on Price and Song (2015) for single-cluster recovery, while the signal estimation step leverages the well-balanced sampling procedure of Chen and Price (2019a) implemented via Randomized BSS (Batson et al. 2012; Lee and Sun 2015).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Breaking the C > 100 barrier to ~4.41 (and ~1.41+c with shrinking range) is significant for both theory—narrowing the gap to the information-theoretic optimum of 1—and practice, where large constant blow-ups translate into prohibitively high SNR requirements. The modular framework means future improvements in either the frequency-estimation or signal-estimation sub-routine immediately tighten the end-to-end guarantee. However, the results are restricted to one-dimensional signals with exact k-sparsity, a constant SNR floor, and lattice-aligned frequencies; extending to higher dimensions, inexact sparsity, or streaming settings remains open, and the ultra-sensitive filters demand high numerical precision that may erode the stated constants in finite-precision deployments.
