## Problem

Policy fitting methods like DPO and REBEL can leverage offline data but are restricted to relative reward signals (preference pairs or reward differences), because the partition function Z in the closed-form KL-regularized optimal policy is intractable for absolute rewards. This forces practitioners to either discard absolute reward information (e.g., from strong reward models or verifiable test-case pass rates) or resort to complex online policy improvement methods like PPO and GRPO. The paper addresses this gap by making Z analytically tractable so that pointwise absolute rewards can be used in a simple offline regression.

## Input / Output

QRPO consumes a dataset of prompt-completion pairs (not preference pairs), a reference policy π_ref, a scalar reward function (e.g., ArmoRM or test-case pass rate), and n reference completions sampled from π_ref per prompt to estimate quantile rewards. The output is a fine-tuned language model policy π_θ trained via offline stochastic gradient descent. A key distinction from DPO/REBEL is that each training sample is used individually with its absolute reward rather than in a pair, and the pre-computation phase generates reference completions whose rewards define the quantile scale for each prompt.

## Method

QRPO transforms the original reward R(x,y) into a quantile reward R_q(x,y) = F_ref(x, R(x,y)), the CDF of reference-policy rewards, which is uniformly distributed on [0,1] regardless of the original reward distribution. This makes the partition function analytically exact and x-independent: Z_q = β(exp(1/β) − 1), eliminating the intractable sum over completions. The training objective is a simple MSE regression of the model's log-ratio β log(π_θ/π_ref) onto the calibrated target R_q − β log Z_q, requiring no preference pairs and no online sampling. The framework generalizes to arbitrary monotone transformations f on top of the quantile reward, with the partition function computed as the integral ∫₀¹ exp(f(t)/β) dt, enabling design of reward distributions with specific optimal-policy properties.

## Results

On Llama 8B Tülu 3 SFT with Magpie-Air, QRPO achieves 50.6% length-controlled AlpacaEval 2 win rate and 56.5% original win rate, outperforming DPO (47.7%/53.7%), SimPO (49.2%/49.6%), and REBEL (46.3%/45.5%). On UltraFeedback, QRPO reaches 65.6% AlpacaEval 2 win rate versus 34.3% for DPO and 39.2% for SimPO. On the LeetCode coding task, QRPO achieves 32.7% average test-case pass rate, surpassing DPO (30.2%), REBEL (26.1%), and SimPO (22.3%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

QRPO consistently outperforms all three baselines on the primary Llama 8B chat evaluations. On Magpie-Air, QRPO leads DPO by 2.9 points in length-controlled AlpacaEval 2 win rate (50.6% vs. 47.7%) and by 12.8 points in original win rate (56.5% vs. 49.6% for SimPO); on UltraFeedback the gap widens dramatically, with QRPO reaching 65.6% WR versus 34.3% for DPO and 39.2% for SimPO. On the LeetCode coding task where rewards are canonically pointwise (test-case pass rate), QRPO achieves 32.7% pass rate, beating DPO (30.2%) by 2.5 points and SimPO (22.3%) by 10.4 points, confirming that converting pointwise rewards to preferences degrades signal. On Mistral 7B, QRPO remains competitive but the margins narrow: on Magpie-Air it edges out SimPO by 0.4 points in LC win rate (44.4% vs. 44.0%) while DPO trails at 42.1%, and on UltraFeedback all methods cluster within ~1 point in LC reward. The paper also shows that QRPO and REBEL (which use the absolute reward directly) exhibit significantly less length bias than DPO and SimPO (which convert the same ArmoRM reward to binary preferences), with QRPO showing no detectable length-reward correlation in Figure 4.

## Relation to Prior Work

QRPO extends the policy fitting paradigm established by DPO (Rafailov et al., 2023) and REBEL (Gao et al., 2024), which fit the closed-form optimal policy of the KL-regularized RL objective (Jaques et al., 2017) but are limited to relative reward signals because the partition function Z is intractable for absolute rewards. The key departure is a conceptual one: rather than cancelling Z via pairwise differences (as DPO and REBEL do) or jointly learning it (as DRO does), QRPO applies a quantile transformation to the reward, making the reference reward distribution uniform and yielding an exact, closed-form Z = β(exp(1/β) − 1). This positions QRPO as the first policy fitting method that directly regresses to the optimal policy using pointwise absolute rewards, bridging the gap between simple offline methods and the complex online policy improvement algorithms (PPO, GRPO) previously required for absolute-reward optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

QRPO unlocks a practical gap: strong absolute reward models (ArmoRM, Nemotron-Reward) and verifiable rewards (test-case pass rates) were previously unusable with simple offline policy fitting, forcing practitioners into costly online RL pipelines. The quantile framework also opens a new pre-computation scaling axis and a family of reward transformations with tractable partition functions, though these transformations remain unexplored empirically. Open questions include the impact of online data mixed with offline, the behavior when the trained policy far exceeds the reference policy (making quantiles less informative), and whether the low-β 'aggressive' training regime that yields best single-task results can be safely extended to multi-task post-training. The finding that methods using robust absolute rewards exhibit less length bias than those converting the same rewards to binary preferences has broader implications for how alignment data is structured.
