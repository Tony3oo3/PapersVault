## Problem

Pre-training datasets like Common Crawl lack explicit domain labels, and even curated corpora with annotations (e.g., The Pile) make optimal mixture selection non-trivial due to the complex, nonlinear relationship between dataset composition and downstream performance. No prior framework automates the full pipeline of discovering approximate domains from unlabeled web data, evaluating candidate mixtures, and iteratively refining them without relying on predefined domain labels or labor-intensive manual curation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250865/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a large-scale text corpus (Nemotron-CC + smollm-corpus, ~800B tokens after filtering) and a target task or domain specification (e.g., general reasoning, STEM, Social Sciences). Documents are embedded via stella_en_400M_v5 and hierarchically clustered into 21 semantic super-clusters, which serve as the atomic units for mixture construction. The output is a probability distribution over these 21 clusters (the optimal mixture weights), a 1.2T-token 20-cluster research corpus (NEMOTRON-CLIMBLAB), and a 400B-token compact pre-training dataset (NEMOTRON-CLIMBMIX) extracted using the discovered weights.

## Method

CLIMB operates in two phases. First, it preprocesses raw documents by embedding them, applying k-means clustering (K_init=1000 via FAISS), pruning low-quality clusters using fasttext classifiers trained on Nemotron-340B-annotated scores (threshold 3.0, yielding 240 clusters), and merging by centroid Euclidean distance (threshold 1.5) into 21 super-clusters. Second, it formulates mixture weight search as a bi-level optimization problem and solves it via iterative coordinate descent: at each of three iterations (64, 32, 16 searches), it (a) samples new mixture configurations from the top-N predicted by the current LightGBM regression predictor (initialized with a Dirichlet distribution over cluster token counts) and (b) trains 350M proxy models on those configurations for 40B tokens to obtain ground-truth performance, then refits the predictor on all accumulated (mixture, performance) pairs. This bootstrapping loop progressively concentrates the search on high-quality subspaces, outperforming single-iteration baselines like RegMix and DoReMi by 1–2% average accuracy across 12 reasoning benchmarks.

## Results

On 12 general reasoning benchmarks, CLIMB's 950M model (trained on 400B tokens with the optimal mixture) achieves a 53.54% average, surpassing Llama-3.2-1.2B (51.56%) by 2.0% and AMD-OLMo-1.2B (49.93%) by 3.6%. At the 350M scale, CLIMB achieves 48.93% average, outperforming Qwen2.5-490M (48.14%) and SmolLM-360M (47.78%). In the direct data-mixture comparison (40B continuous training), CLIMB's 1B model reaches 60.41% average accuracy, beating RegMix (59.37%) and DoReMi (59.16%). Optimizing for MMLU Social Sciences yields a 5% improvement over random sampling (41.79% vs. 36.69% for the 1B model).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against data-mixture baselines (Table 1, 40B continuous training), CLIMB outperforms both DoReMi and RegMix across all six benchmarks at both 350M and 1B scales; at 1B, CLIMB achieves 60.41% average vs. RegMix 59.37% and DoReMi 59.16%, with the largest gap on HellaSwag (66.01 vs. 64.73) and WinoGrande (63.32 vs. 62.33). Against SOTA LLMs (Table 2, 400B tokens), CLIMB-950M beats Llama-3.2-1.2B by 2.0% average (53.54 vs. 51.56), AMD-OLMo-1.2B by 3.6%, and TinyLlama-1.1B by 5.1%, despite having fewer parameters than Llama-3.2 and AMD-OLMo. At the sub-500M scale, CLIMB-350M edges out Qwen2.5-490M (48.93 vs. 48.14) and SmolLM-360M (48.93 vs. 47.78). In the pre-training-from-scratch setting (Figure 1), NEMOTRON-CLIMBMIX outperforms Nemotron-CC, SmolLM-corpus, DCLM-baseline, and FineWeb-Edu under an equal 400B-token budget. For domain-specific optimization on MMLU, CLIMB-iter3 with a 62M proxy surpasses CLIMB-Best@N (which uses same-size target models as proxies) on Social Sciences (41.72% vs. 40.66% at 1B), demonstrating that iterative refinement with smaller proxies is more effective than brute-force search with larger ones.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the data mixture optimization line initiated by DoReMi (distributionally robust optimization over domain weights) and RegMix (single-round regression-based mixture search) by introducing two key departures: (1) an automated embedding-and-clustering preprocessing step that discovers approximate domains from unlabeled web-scale data, removing the reliance on pre-defined domain labels that both DoReMi and RegMix require; and (2) a multi-iteration bootstrapping loop with progressive pruning that generalizes RegMix's single-round search into a coordinate-descent procedure alternating between predictor-guided sampling and predictor refitting. The clustering pipeline draws on standard tools (FAISS k-means, fasttext quality classifiers, stella embeddings), while the iterative search framework is the primary methodological contribution over prior single-shot approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: the released NEMOTRON-CLIMBLAB (1.2T tokens, 20 clusters) and NEMOTRON-CLIMBMIX (400B tokens) datasets provide a ready-made playground for data-mixture research and a drop-in pre-training corpus that outperforms existing alternatives under equal token budgets. The 2% average improvement over Llama-3.2-1B on general reasoning benchmarks demonstrates that intelligent mixture selection can rival or exceed the gains from additional training data. However, limitations remain: proxy model training still incurs non-negligible compute (45 GPU-hours per 350M proxy), domain evaluation relies on MMLU's coarse-grained categories without validation on high-stakes domains (finance, healthcare), and the approach has not been tested at the scale of frontier models (70B+). Open questions include whether the iterative search converges to different optima at larger model scales and whether the clustering granularity needs to adapt as datasets grow.
