## Problem

Publicly available, general-purpose supervised fine-tuning (SFT) datasets for text-to-image models are scarce and often target narrow domains, while leading models rely on proprietary, poorly documented internal data. Existing curation methods—manual selection, heuristic filtering, or simple aesthetic scoring—struggle to identify the small subset of samples that most effectively boost general generative quality, making it difficult for the research community to replicate or build upon state-of-the-art SFT pipelines.

## Input / Output

The pipeline ingests a web-scale pool of O(10 billion) raw images and outputs a compact SFT dataset of 3,350 image-text pairs. Images must exceed 1024×1024 px in area and span diverse aspect ratios (enabling bucketed fine-tuning for non-square generation). Text captions are moderately descriptive, user-like prompts averaging 27 words (148 symbols), generated by a proprietary VLM tuned to mimic natural user queries rather than exhaustive scene descriptions. The resulting dataset is used to full-fine-tune Stable Diffusion models (SD1.5 through SD3.5 Large) that take a text prompt and produce a generated RGB image.

## Method

The core contribution is a multi-stage filtering pipeline whose final stage uses a pre-trained diffusion model as a quality estimator. After NSFW/resolution filtering, coarse binary-classifier quality screening, SIFT-based deduplication, and TOPIQ no-reference IQA thresholding (>0.71), the pipeline scores ~300 million remaining images by extracting cross-attention activation norms from a pre-trained T2I model at a fixed timestep (t=0.25) using a long multi-keyword prompt encoding desirable and undesirable visual attributes. The top-K (layer, token) pairs that best separate manually labeled high-quality from low-quality calibration images are identified, and each candidate image is scored by summing its activation norms at those indices. The top 3,350 samples are selected and re-captioned with user-like prompts. The key insight is that a pre-trained diffusion model's cross-attention mechanisms inherently encode the subtle blend of aesthetic quality, color balance, and moderate complexity that maximizes downstream SFT efficacy—characteristics that existing IQA/IAA classifiers cannot reliably capture.

## Results

Fine-tuning five Stable Diffusion models (SD1.5, SD2.1, SDXL, SD3.5M, SD3.5L) on the compact 3,350-sample Alchemist dataset yields statistically significant improvements in Aesthetic Quality (human SbS win rates of 0.57–0.69 vs. baseline) and Image Complexity (win rates of 0.67–0.81 vs. baseline). Against a size-matched LAION-Aesthetics v2 SFT baseline, Alchemist-tuned models achieve Aesthetic Quality win rates of 0.56–0.60 and Image Complexity win rates of 0.72–0.78. Image-Text Relevance remains statistically unchanged (≈0.47–0.53), confirming that quality gains do not come at the cost of prompt adherence. Automated metrics (FD-DINOv2, CLIP Score, ImageReward, HPS-v2) corroborate the human evaluations, with the largest FD-DINOv2 improvements seen on SDXL (97.4 → 73.4).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the size-matched LAION-Aesthetics v2 SFT baseline (3,350 samples, aesthetic score ≥ 6.5), Alchemist-tuned models consistently win on Aesthetic Quality (win rates 0.56–0.60) and Image Complexity (win rates 0.72–0.78) across all five architectures, with the largest margins on SD2.1 (Aes 0.56, Comp 0.72) and SDXL (Aes 0.58, Comp 0.78). Even when LAION is scaled up to its full 31k-sample set (Appendix E.2), Alchemist still outperforms on Aesthetic Quality (0.59–0.66) and Image Complexity (0.72–0.86), confirming that sample quality rather than volume drives the gains. In a qualitative comparison against FLUX.1-dev (Figure 5), Alchemist-tuned SDXL and SD3.5 Medium reach comparable aesthetic quality and image complexity despite having roughly 4× fewer parameters, suggesting that data-efficient SFT on well-pre-trained foundations can close the gap to much larger proprietary models. Image-Text Relevance shows no statistically significant difference in any comparison, and Fidelity exhibits a mild trade-off (≈5% win-rate decrease) primarily on the most recently optimized architectures (SD3.5L), where Alchemist's complexity push surfaces minor artifacts.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of SFT dataset curation for text-to-image models (established by Emu, PixArt-α, Kolors, SANA, YaART) and image quality assessment. It departs from prior curation approaches—manual selection, aesthetic-score filtering (LAION-Aesthetics), and heuristic web filtering—by repurposing a pre-trained diffusion model's cross-attention activations as a learned quality estimator for selecting SFT samples. The key conceptual move is treating the generative model itself as an oracle for which training samples will most improve downstream generation, rather than relying on external IQA/IAA classifiers or human judgment. This positions the work as the first open, general-purpose SFT dataset pipeline that explicitly targets broad quality improvement rather than domain specialization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The release of a compact, well-characterized 3,350-sample SFT dataset with fine-tuned weights for five SD models addresses a critical gap: leading T2I models rely on proprietary, poorly documented SFT data, hindering reproducibility and open research. The finding that strict quality filtering at ~3k samples outperforms both larger curated sets (7k, 19k) and a 31k LAION-Aesthetics subset underscores that SFT efficacy is governed by sample impact rather than volume—a principle with broader implications for data-efficient fine-tuning. Open questions include whether the fidelity trade-off observed in newer architectures (SD3.5) can be resolved, whether the cross-attention estimator generalizes to non-diffusion T2I models, and whether the approach can be extended to improve text-image alignment rather than purely visual quality.
