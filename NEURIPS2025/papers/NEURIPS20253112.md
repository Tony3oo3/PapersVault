## Problem

Prior work on the Brain-to-Text Benchmark '24 achieved notable accuracy gains over the baseline GRU, but relied on bidirectional architectures incompatible with real-time streaming, ensembles of up to 10 decoders, and large LLMs for hypothesis merging—making on-device deployment impractical. Additionally, the baseline GRU overfits early in training and processes inputs with 87.5% temporal overlap, introducing unnecessary computational redundancy. This paper addresses the gap by improving the core neural decoder along multiple axes simultaneously: accuracy, streaming compatibility, computational efficiency, and robustness to day-to-day distribution shifts.

## Input / Output

The model consumes 256-channel intracranial neural features (spike band power and threshold crossings from two 64-channel microelectrode arrays in ventral premotor cortex) sampled at 50 Hz, segmented into non-overlapping 100 ms patches (5 time bins × 256 features) rather than the baseline GRU's 640 ms windows with 80 ms stride. The Transformer outputs phoneme logits at 10 Hz (every 5 time bins), which are decoded into text via n-gram LM–guided beam search over a weighted finite-state transducer. Unlike the baseline GRU, the Transformer requires no day-specific linear layer, relying instead on LayerNorm in the patch embedding module to handle day-to-day signal variability.

## Method

The paper replaces the 5-layer GRU with a compact 5-layer causal Transformer (384 hidden dim, 6 heads, T5-style relative positional embeddings) that processes non-overlapping 100 ms temporal patches, reducing parameters by 83% and peak GPU memory by 52%. During training, contiguous temporal chunks are masked with a learnable MASK token (N=20 masks, max length 7.5% of trial, yielding ~53% average masking) as a strong regularization strategy that delays overfitting and enables the model to handle missing input segments. For test-time adaptation, the authors introduce DietCORP, a lightweight variant of CORP that adapts the model in a single gradient step per trial by generating Z=64 time-masked augmentations of the current trial and training the patch embedding module to predict LM-refined pseudo-labels across those augmentations, requiring only 1.3 GiB peak memory and 18 ms per trial.

## Results

On the Brain-to-Text Benchmark '24, the time-masked causal Transformer achieves a WER of 12.17% (3-gram LM) and 8.18% (5-gram LM), representing 20.2% and 26.4% relative improvements over the baseline unidirectional GRU (15.25% and 11.12%, respectively). With fine-tuned Llama 3.1 8B rescoring, the Transformer reaches 5.68% WER, matching the best benchmark entries. The Transformer also uses 83% fewer parameters (9.4M vs. 56.7M), 52% less peak GPU memory (2.66 vs. 5.55 GiB), and trains 58% faster per epoch (10.81s vs. 25.88s) than the baseline GRU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the baseline unidirectional GRU from Willett et al. (41), the time-masked Transformer wins decisively: 12.17% vs. 15.25% WER (3-gram LM, 20.2% relative improvement) and 8.18% vs. 11.12% (5-gram LM, 26.4% relative improvement), both statistically significant (p < 0.05, N=10 seeds). Against the Linderman Lab bidirectional GRU (42), the time-masked Transformer achieves 8.18% vs. 8.0% WER under the 5-gram LM setup, a non-significant difference (p > 0.05), meaning the streaming-compatible Transformer matches a non-streaming bidirectional model. Against diphone decoding with a bidirectional GRU (Li et al., 24), the Transformer again shows no significant difference (8.18% vs. 8.39%, p > 0.05). In the fine-tuned LLM setting, the Transformer + Llama 3.1 8B (5.68%) performs on par with diphone decoding + GPT 3.5 (5.77%) and Llama 3.1 70B (6.85%), while using a smaller, open-source LLM. Crucially, the Transformer achieves these results with a streaming-compatible (unidirectional) architecture, whereas both the Linderman Lab and diphone decoding baselines require bidirectional processing and cannot operate in real time.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Brain-to-Text Benchmark '24 baseline (a 5-layer GRU with CTC loss and n-gram beam search) and departs from it in two key ways: replacing the GRU with a compact causal Transformer that processes non-overlapping temporal patches, and introducing heavy time-masking (≈53% of each trial) as the primary regularizer, inspired by SpecAugment in ASR. For test-time adaptation, it adapts CORP (originally designed for handwriting neuroprostheses) into DietCORP, which replaces the need to store and replay previous pseudo-labeled data with multiple time-masked augmentations of the current trial, drawing on the augmentation-based robustness idea from MEMO. The relative positional embeddings follow T5, and the patch-based input design is inspired by ViT and a prior BCI Transformer (Chen et al., 2025), though here each patch spans all 256 channels rather than one electrode.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a 9.4M-parameter causal Transformer can match or exceed the accuracy of much larger bidirectional GRU entries on the Brain-to-Text Benchmark while being 83% smaller, 52% lower in peak memory, and 3× faster in beam search decoding—properties critical for on-device, real-time speech neuroprostheses. DietCORP's 18 ms per-trial adaptation with only 1.3 GiB peak memory makes continuous recalibration feasible on resource-constrained hardware, directly addressing the day-to-day performance drift that plagues clinical BCI use. Key limitations include evaluation on a single participant, reliance on a ~60 GB n-gram LM that still precludes fully on-device operation, and beam search's incompatibility with streaming text-to-speech. Open questions include whether time-masking and the compact Transformer generalize across participants and recording modalities, and whether the n-gram LM can be replaced by lighter-weight text-knowledge integration (e.g., projecting into an LLM token space).
