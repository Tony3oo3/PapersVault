## Problem

Standard Flow Matching models use a single large network to learn the entire velocity field from noise to data, which forces one model to simultaneously capture fundamentally different signal characteristics (low-frequency structure at early timesteps vs. high-frequency detail at later timesteps) and incurs O(SK) inference cost since the full model is evaluated at every solver step. No prior approach partitions the generative trajectory into temporally specialized sub-models while preserving semantic coherence across segments.

## Input / Output

The model operates in the latent space of a pretrained Stable Diffusion VAE (8× downsampling), taking Gaussian noise in latent space and an ImageNet class label as input, and producing a 256×256 (or 512×512) RGB image after 246 Euler solver steps (41 per segment with M=6). The class label is injected via AdaLN-Zero modulation, and the semantic feature is summed element-wise with timestep embeddings before attention layers.

## Method

BFM partitions the flow trajectory [0,1] into M non-overlapping temporal segments, each modeled by a smaller specialized velocity block v_θ^(m) (size K′ < K), trained with a segment-local flow-matching loss that interpolates between segment endpoints rather than the full noise-to-data path. To compensate for each block's limited exposure to clean data, a shared Semantic Feature Guidance module (f_φ) extracts semantic features from noisy intermediate states and aligns them with DINOv2 embeddings via a cosine-similarity loss, then conditions the velocity blocks on these features. A lightweight Feature Residual Network (FRN) further reduces inference cost by approximating semantic features within each segment as a residual increment from the segment-start feature, so the full alignment network is evaluated only once per segment rather than at every timestep. This yields 2.1×–4.9× inference speedups over state-of-the-art flow matching models at comparable or better FID on ImageNet 256×256.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256 class-conditioned generation, BFM-XLSF achieves an FID of 1.75 and IS of 289.4 with 107.8 GFLOPs over 246 solver steps, surpassing the previous best flow-matching model REPA-XL (FID 1.80, IS 284.0, 114.5 GFLOPs). The efficiency-focused variant BFM-XLSF-RA reduces inference to 37.8 GFLOPs (a 67% reduction over REPA) while maintaining a competitive FID of 2.03 and IS of 278.1. Under few-step sampling (6 NFEs), BFM-XLSF achieves FID 8.01 and IS 248.2, substantially outperforming SiT-XL (FID 12.91) and REPA-XL (FID 13.02) with only 62.8 IS degradation versus 91.1 and 122.8 respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BFM-XLSF outperforms the closest flow-matching baseline REPA-XL on both FID (1.75 vs. 1.80) and IS (289.4 vs. 284.0) while using ~5% fewer FLOPs (107.8 vs. 114.5). It also clearly beats SiT-XL (FID 2.06, IS 270.3), DiT-XL (FID 2.27), FiTv2-XL (FID 2.26), and FlowDCN (FID 2.00) on FID. The efficiency variant BFM-XLSF-RA achieves 37.8 GFLOPs, beating DyDiT-XL λ=0.5 (57.9 GFLOPs) by 35% in FLOPs while improving IS from 248.0 to 278.1, though its FID (2.03) is marginally worse than DyDiT's (2.07). The only method with a lower FID is MDTv2-XL (1.58), but it relies on guidance scheduling and 1080 training epochs versus BFM's 400. In the few-step regime (6 NFEs), BFM-XLSF degrades far less than SiT-XL (ΔIS 62.8 vs. 91.1) or REPA-XL (ΔIS 62.8 vs. 122.8), achieving FID 8.01 versus 12.91 and 13.02 respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Flow Matching framework (Lipman et al., ICLR 2023) and its transformer-based instantiation SiT (Ma et al., ECCV 2024), departing from the standard monolithic design where a single large network models the entire noise-to-data trajectory. The key architectural departure is partitioning the trajectory into temporal segments, each handled by a smaller specialized velocity block—conceptually analogous to a Mixture-of-Experts with a fixed temporal router rather than input-dependent routing. The Semantic Feature Guidance module is inspired by REPA (Yu et al., 2024) but introduces a modular separation: a dedicated alignment network conditions the velocity blocks rather than aligning internal hidden states in-place. The efficiency gains complement (rather than compete with) distillation-based and dynamic-inference approaches like DyDiT and DiffMoE, which the authors note could be applied within individual blocks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 2.1×–4.9× inference speedup at comparable or better FID is practically significant for deployment of flow-matching models, and the strong few-step robustness (FID 8.01 at 6 NFEs vs. ~13 for SiT/REPA) suggests the blockwise design stabilizes the velocity field in ways that benefit low-budget sampling. However, the method's reliance on an external pretrained encoder (DINOv2) for semantic conditioning limits generality to domains where such encoders exist, and evaluation is confined to class-conditional ImageNet at 256×256 (with a brief 512×512 check). Open questions include whether non-uniform or learnable partitioning schedules can further improve the quality-efficiency tradeoff, how the framework extends to multi-modal settings (text-to-image, video), and whether self-supervised semantic features can replace the external encoder dependency.
