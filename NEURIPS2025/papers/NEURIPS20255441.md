## Problem

Error feedback (EF) algorithms for communication-efficient distributed optimization have only been analyzed under classical L-smoothness, yet many modern learning problems (deep networks, distributionally robust optimization) satisfy the weaker (L0, L1)-smoothness condition where the gradient Lipschitz constant grows with the gradient norm. Existing convergence analyses under generalized smoothness are limited to single-node settings or rely on restrictive assumptions such as bounded data heterogeneity and almost-sure variance bounds, leaving a gap for distributed EF methods with practical guarantees.

## Input / Output

The algorithms take n distributed loss functions f_i(x), α-contractive compressors (e.g., top-k sparsifiers with k/d-contraction), and a stepsize schedule as input, and produce a sequence of model parameters x^k with a provable O(1/√K) bound on the minimum expected gradient norm. A notable design choice is that the stepsize γ_k = γ/√(K+1) for ||EF21|| requires no knowledge of the generalized smoothness constants L0 or L1, unlike the original EF21 whose stepsize depends on the (often inaccessible) global smoothness constant L. The stochastic variant ||EF21-SGDM|| additionally consumes per-client stochastic gradient samples ξ_i^k and a momentum coefficient η_k = 1/√(K+1).

## Method

The core mechanism is to normalize the aggregated error-feedback gradient before the parameter update: x^{k+1} = x^k − γ_k · g^k/‖g^k‖, where g^k is the server-side average of compressed error-feedback estimates. This normalization is critical because under (L0, L1)-smoothness the descent lemma's curvature term scales with ‖∇f_i(x^k)‖, and dividing by the gradient norm decouples the step magnitude from the local gradient magnitude, yielding a stepsize rule independent of L0 and L1. The convergence proof employs a Lyapunov function with L1-norm (rather than L2-norm) error terms, V^k = f(x^k) − f^inf + (2γ_k/(1−√(1−α))) · (1/n)Σ‖∇f_i(x^k) − g_i^k‖, which is necessary to handle the non-quadratic growth in the generalized smoothness descent inequality. For the stochastic setting, ||EF21-SGDM|| adds a per-client momentum buffer v_i^k = (1−η_k)v_i^{k−1} + η_k∇f_i(x^k; ξ_i^k) before compression, achieving the same O(1/K^{1/4}) rate as EF21-SGDM under L-smoothness while requiring no data-heterogeneity or mini-batch-size assumptions.

## Results

The paper establishes that ||EF21|| achieves an O(1/√K) convergence rate in expected gradient norm for nonconvex deterministic problems under (L₀,L₁)-smoothness, matching the rate of vanilla EF21 under classical L-smoothness. For the stochastic setting, ||EF21-SGDM|| achieves O(1/K^{1/4}), matching EF21-SGDM. In experiments, ||EF21|| outperforms EF21 across all three tasks: on ResNet-20/CIFAR-10 it achieves up to 10% accuracy gains, and on logistic regression (Breast Cancer, a1a, synthetic) it converges faster with higher solution accuracy, all due to its larger allowable stepsize that does not require knowledge of the smoothness constants L₀ or L₁.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is EF21 (Richtárik et al., NeurIPS 2021), the standard error feedback algorithm analyzed under classical L-smoothness. ||EF21|| consistently outperforms EF21 on all three experimental tasks: polynomial minimization, logistic regression with a nonconvex regularizer (Breast Cancer, a1a, and synthetic datasets), and ResNet-20 training on CIFAR-10. On ResNet-20/CIFAR-10 with a top-k sparsifier (k=0.01d) and constant stepsize γ=5, ||EF21|| achieves up to 10% higher test accuracy than EF21 while communicating the same number of bits. On logistic regression, ||EF21|| converges faster in gradient norm and reaches lower final loss on all three datasets. The advantage stems from the larger theoretical stepsize: ||EF21|| uses γₖ = γ/√(K+1) with γ=1 (independent of L₀, L₁), whereas EF21 requires γ = 1/(L + L̃√(β/θ)) which is typically much smaller. Under the special case of classical smoothness (L₁=0), the paper acknowledges that ||EF21||'s convergence constant is 2√2 times larger than EF21's, but in practice the normalization enables substantially larger stepsizes that more than compensate.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the EF21 error feedback framework (Richtárik et al., NeurIPS 2021) and its momentum variant EF21-SGDM (Fatkhullin et al., NeurIPS 2024) from the traditional L-smoothness regime to the weaker (L₀, L₁)-smoothness / generalized smoothness setting, which better captures the behavior of deep network loss functions. The key technical departure is adding gradient normalization to the update rule, a technique well-studied for single-node clipped/normalized gradient descent under generalized smoothness but previously unexplored in the distributed error feedback context. Unlike prior distributed analyses under generalized smoothness (Crawshaw et al., Liu et al.), this work avoids restrictive assumptions on data heterogeneity, almost-sure variance bounds, and symmetric noise, relying only on standard lower-boundedness, generalized smoothness, and contractive compressor conditions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first convergence guarantee for normalized error feedback in distributed settings under generalized smoothness, closing a gap between the practical popularity of these algorithms in deep learning and their theoretical understanding. The practical implication is significant: the stepsize for ||EF21|| in the deterministic case is agnostic to the (often inaccessible) smoothness constants L₀ and L₁, and the larger allowable stepsizes translate to up to 10% accuracy gains on ResNet-20/CIFAR-10 over vanilla EF21. Open questions include extending the analysis to decreasing or adaptive stepsize schedules (the current theory requires constant stepsizes that become impractically small for large K), and developing distributed/federated variants that leverage clipping rather than normalization. A mild limitation is that under traditional L-smoothness, the convergence constant is 2√2 worse than the original EF21, and the stochastic variant still requires knowledge of L₁ for stepsize selection.
