## Problem

Soft unification, the standard mechanism for integrating neural embeddings into logic programs, treats symbol similarity as equivalence but violates the transitive law, causing probability mass leakage and non-multilinear loss landscapes that trap learning in local optima. No prior work provided a sound probabilistic semantics that places uncertainty directly on equivalence relations between symbols while remaining compatible with end-to-end differentiable training.

## Input / Output

The method takes a Datalog program (a set of ground facts and non-ground rules), a set of constant embeddings represented as categorical probability vectors over k partitions (one per constant in the Herbrand universe), and a target ground atom. It outputs the probability that the target atom is entailed under the induced distribution over equivalence relations, which is then used for filtered ranking in link prediction or as a classification score in sequence tasks. Unlike standard embedding-based approaches that use continuous vectors and cosine similarity, the embeddings here are discrete categorical distributions whose dot product directly yields the equivalence probability between two constants.

## Method

The paper introduces a probabilistic equivalence semantics where a logic program induces a distribution P_E over all equivalence relations on the Herbrand universe rather than over subsets of facts. This distribution is factorized via independent latent categorical variables V_c per constant, where P(a≈b) = Σ_i P(V_a=i)P(V_b=i), and the factorization is shown to constrain marginal equivalence probabilities to form a positive semi-definite matrix (Theorem 4). For exact inference, the pipeline applies singularization to rewrite Datalog rules so that repeated variable occurrences are linked via ≈-atoms, then the magic sets transformation for goal-directed pruning, followed by lineage extraction and weighted model counting over the resulting Boolean formula. For approximate inference, latent variables are sampled, constants are instantiated to their partition values, and standard Datalog entailment is checked via Monte Carlo averaging. Training is end-to-end differentiable through the exact WMC path or via REINFORCE with a leave-one-out baseline for the sampling path, and the approach achieves up to 42% higher accuracy over soft-unification baselines on knowledge graph link prediction and saturates differentiable finite state machine benchmarks.

## Results

On the Countries knowledge graph (S3 variant), the proposed equivalence semantics achieves 99.89% AUC-PR, outperforming the strongest baseline DeepSoftLog (97.90%) and NTP (56.68%). On the Nations knowledge graph, it reaches 0.724 MRR and 0.591 Hits@1, surpassing CTP (0.709 MRR, 0.562 Hits@1) while DeepSoftLog times out entirely. On differentiable finite state machine classification, the method saturates all three grammars (97.46–99.61% AUC-PR), compared to DeepSoftLog's best of 87.01% and RNN's best of 77.63%, representing up to 42% relative improvement over the strongest baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against three families of soft-unification neurosymbolic methods. NTP (Rocktäschel & Riedel, NeurIPS 2017) is consistently outperformed across all datasets, with the largest gap on Countries S3 (99.89% vs. 56.68% AUC-PR) and Nations MRR (0.724 vs. 0.61). GNTP and CTP (Minervini et al., ICML 2020) are outperformed on Countries S2 (100.0% vs. 90.82%/91.81%) and Nations MRR (0.724 vs. 0.658/0.709), though CTP matches or slightly exceeds the proposed method on Nations Hits@10 (0.995 vs. 0.988). DeepSoftLog (Maene & De Raedt, NeurIPS 2023), the closest baseline using probabilistic soft unification, is outperformed on Countries S2 (100.0% vs. 97.67%) and S3 (99.89% vs. 97.90%), and times out entirely on the Nations dataset. On the differentiable finite state machine task, the proposed method achieves 97–100% AUC-PR across all three grammars, substantially exceeding DeepSoftLog (56–87%) and a simple RNN (50–78%), with the largest relative gain on the (0|10*1)* grammar (99.61% vs. 56.12% for DeepSoftLog).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper identifies a fundamental flaw in soft unification—the core mechanism behind differentiable theorem provers since Rocktäschel & Riedel (2017) and its probabilistic extension in DeepSoftLog (Maene & De Raedt, 2023)—namely that similarity-based symbol matching violates the transitivity law of equivalence, causing probability mass leakage and local optima during learning. The authors replace the possible world semantics of Sato (1995) with a distribution over equivalence relations, and repurpose singularization (Marnette, 2009) and magic sets (Beeri & Ramakrishnan, 1987) from database theory to enable exact and approximate inference under this new semantics. The factorization of the equivalence distribution via latent categorical variables draws on probabilistic clustering, connecting neurosymbolic logic programming to a previously separate line of work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that all prior differentiable theorem provers implicitly violate equivalence transitivity is a conceptual correction that affects the entire soft-unification literature, and the up-to-42% improvement on link prediction and near-saturation on sequence classification demonstrate the practical stakes. However, the expressivity is constrained: Theorem 4 shows the factorization can only represent positive semi-definite marginal equivalence matrices, and the approach still requires rule templates and suffers from high-variance REINFORCE gradients. Open questions include scaling to larger knowledge graphs, developing lower-variance gradient estimators, and extending the semantics to non-monotonic or declarative probabilistic languages.
