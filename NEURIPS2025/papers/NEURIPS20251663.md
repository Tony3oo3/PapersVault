## Problem

Reinforcement learning has proven effective at enhancing LLM reasoning (e.g., o1, DeepSeek-R1), but applying RL at scale is prohibitively expensive, requiring multiple model copies and extensive GPU workloads. Recent analyses suggest RL does not inject new knowledge but rather reshapes output distributions to activate reasoning behaviors already latent in base models. The paper asks whether the small, localized probability shifts induced by RL are model-size invariant, and if so, whether they can be transferred from a small RL-trained model to a much larger base model at inference time without any retraining.

## Input / Output

At each decoding step, RAST consumes the current prefix x_<t and simultaneously queries three models: the large target base model M_base, the small base model S_base, and the small RL-trained model S_RL, obtaining their respective logit vectors over the vocabulary. The method produces a modified probability distribution P_M̃(X_t|x_<t) by adding a scaled logit difference ΔR to M_base's logits before softmax, and the final output is a full reasoning chain generated autoregressively under this steered distribution. The implementation runs on a modified vLLM stack with temperature 1.0, top-p 0.95, and max generation length 16,384 tokens for math tasks; λ is fixed at 1.0 across all experiments.

## Method

RAST is a decoding-time transfer method built on the empirical finding (validated via Path Coverage Rate >95% across scales) that RL-induced distributional shifts are concentrated on a small subset of reasoning-critical tokens and are largely model-size invariant. The core mechanism computes ΔR = S_RL(X_t|x_<t) − S_base(X_t|x_<t) at every token position and injects it additively into the large base model's logits: P_M̃ = softmax[M_base + λ·ΔR], effectively transferring the 'reasoning correction signal' from the small model pair to the large model without any gradient updates. This is conceptually related to contrastive decoding (DExperts, Contrastive Decoding) but differs in targeting cross-scale reasoning transfer rather than single-model style or factuality control. The approach requires no retraining of M_base, reduces GPU memory by up to 50% compared to full RL training, and empirically recovers 80–100% of the performance gap between base and RL-trained models while sometimes exceeding the RL ceiling on pass@k due to implicit multi-model ensembling.

## Results

On MATH500, RAST with ΔR₁₄B applied to Qwen-2.5-32B achieves 80.7% avg. pass@1 (recovery rate 95.3%), nearly matching the SimpleRL-32B ceiling of 81.3%. On Minerva, RAST actually surpasses the RL-trained ceiling (34.2% vs. 33.6%), and on GSM8K it reaches 95.3% vs. the ceiling's 95.7%. Across all six math benchmarks, RAST consistently recovers 60–105% of the base-to-RL performance gap, with the largest gains on harder datasets (AIME24: 3.3→18.3, +455% relative improvement).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is the SimpleRL-Zoo RL-trained model (Zeng et al., 2025), which serves as the performance ceiling. RAST with ΔR₁₄B on the 32B base matches or exceeds SimpleRL-32B on Minerva (34.2 vs. 33.6) and is within 0.6 points on MATH500 (80.7 vs. 81.3) and GSM8K (95.3 vs. 95.7), but still trails on AIME24 (18.3 vs. 27.6) and AMC23 (65.2 vs. 69.6). For the 14B base, RAST with ΔR₇B beats SimpleRL-14B on AIME24 (16.2 vs. 14.4) but falls short on MATH500 (77.4 vs. 78.2) and Minerva (28.8 vs. 32.2). The second baseline, Code-R1 (Liu & Zhang, 2025), provides the RL-trained source for code reasoning; RAST with ΔR₇B from Code-R1-Zero improves Qwen-2.5-14B-1M from 62.1 to 66.5 average across HumanEval+, MBPP+, and LiveCodeBench (+4.4 absolute). Notably, RAST also outperforms the RL ceiling on pass@k diversity metrics across all six math benchmarks, a property the RL-trained models lack.

## Relation to Prior Work

RAST repurposes the contrastive-decoding mechanism (logit subtraction between two models, as in Contrastive Decoding [31] and DExperts [37]) from its traditional role in style/toxicity/factuality control to a new purpose: transferring RL-induced reasoning behaviors across model scales. It builds directly on the zero-RL training paradigm (DeepSeek-R1, SimpleRL-Zoo, Code-R1) and the emerging consensus that RL reshapes output distributions to activate latent reasoning rather than inject new knowledge. The key conceptual departure is the hypothesis—and empirical validation via Path Coverage Rate—that RL-induced probability shifts are largely model-size invariant, which prior contrastive-decoding work did not test or exploit.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RAST offers a practical ~50% reduction in GPU memory versus direct RL training while recovering 80–95% of the performance gap, making reasoning enhancement accessible without multi-model RL infrastructure. The finding that RAST can *exceed* RL-trained models on pass@k challenges the prior observation (Shao et al., Yue et al.) that RL training degrades sampling diversity, suggesting that cross-model logit injection introduces a form of implicit ensembling. Open questions include whether the approach extends to non-mathematical reasoning domains (e.g., multi-step planning, scientific reasoning), how it behaves when the base and delta-source models are from entirely different pretraining corpora, and whether the 13–16% inference latency can be eliminated via speculative decoding without degrading the reasoning signal.
