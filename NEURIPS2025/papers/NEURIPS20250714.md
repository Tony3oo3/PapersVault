## Problem

Steepest descent guarantees a descent property but requires a small stepsize (γ < 2/L), while conditional gradient methods allow large steps but are not descent methods and eventually need diminishing stepsizes to converge. Gradient norm clipping was shown to reconcile these properties in the Euclidean case under (L₀,L₁)-smoothness, but no analogous characterization existed for non-Euclidean norms or constrained settings. This paper asks whether a stable hybrid method can be constructed that takes large steps initially yet adapts its effective stepsize near a solution, in a general norm geometry.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a stochastic or deterministic gradient of the loss with respect to model parameters, combined with a momentum-based estimator d^k = α_k ∇f(x^k, ξ_k) + (1−α_k) d^{k−1}. At each iteration it queries a linear minimization oracle (LMO) over a chosen norm ball to obtain a direction v^k, then computes the dual norm ‖d^k‖_* = −⟨d^k, lmo(d^k)⟩ to determine the clipping threshold. The output is an updated parameter vector x^{k+1} = x^k − γ η_k v^k where η_k = min{ρ, ‖d^k‖_*}, ensuring the step magnitude is bounded by the trust-region radius ρ in the chosen norm.

## Method

The core algorithm (GGNC) is a hybrid of steepest descent (via the sharp operator [d]^sharp) and unconstrained conditional gradient (via the LMO), unified through the identity lmo(d) = −d^sharp/‖d‖_*. The update x^{k+1} = x^k − γ τ_k [d^k]^sharp with τ_k = min{1, ρ/‖d^k‖_*} reduces to full steepest descent when the gradient norm is small and to a normalized conditional gradient step when it is large, yielding a descent property under a generalized (L₀,L₁)-smoothness assumption for arbitrary norms. In the stochastic case, a momentum-based estimator achieves an order-optimal O(n^{−1/4}) rate. A key insight connects the clipping mechanism to the Frank-Wolfe short step, enabling principled integration of weight decay in the constrained variant (Algorithm 2). For deep learning, a product norm over layers (max-norm composition of per-layer norms) instantiates the method as ClippedScion, where the LMO is computed per-layer but the dual norm requires a global sum across all layers.

## Results

On NanoGPT (1B parameters, FineWeb dataset), ClippedScion achieves a >10% speedup over Unconstrained Scion with a fixed stepsize. On ImageNet (DeiT-base ViT), Unconstrained ClippedScion achieves an 11% speedup over Unconstrained Scion. On CIFAR-10 (CNN), clipping yields a substantial test-accuracy improvement under fixed stepsize, while under linear rampdown scheduling, ClippedScion and Unconstrained Scion reach comparable final performance, consistent with their matching O(n^{-1/4}) stochastic convergence rates.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is Unconstrained Scion (Pethick et al., 2025), the non-clipped conditional-gradient (LMO-based) method that ClippedScion extends. With a fixed stepsize, ClippedScion consistently outperforms Scion: a >10% speedup on NanoGPT-1B and an 11% speedup on ImageNet (DeiT-base), attributed to the descent property that prevents the iterates from being pushed away from the solution when the gradient norm is small. On CIFAR-10, the fixed-stepsize gap is described as a 'substantial improvement' in test accuracy. However, when a linear rampdown stepsize schedule is used, the two methods converge to similar final accuracy, which the authors note aligns with their identical O(n^{-1/4}) stochastic convergence guarantees (Theorem 4.9 vs. Pethick et al. 2025, Thm. 5.4). The paper also notes an open empirical question: in the constrained variant (Algorithm 2), the gradient norm ⟨d^k, v^k⟩ is observed to be increasing rather than decreasing, which requires further investigation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies three previously separate threads: gradient norm clipping (Mikolov et al., 2012), non-Euclidean steepest descent via the sharp operator, and conditional gradient / Frank-Wolfe methods (Pethick et al., 2025). The key conceptual move is showing that clipping is the unconstrained analog of the Frank-Wolfe short step, and that both arise from a single hybrid of steepest descent and conditional gradient under a generalized (L0,L1)-smoothness condition extended to arbitrary norms. The resulting ClippedScion algorithm is a clipped variant of the Scion optimizer, positioned as a principled alternative to Muon that retains the LMO-based spectral update while adding the adaptive stepsize that Muon lacks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first convergence guarantees for clipped optimization with stochastic feedback (even in the Euclidean case) and establishes an O(n^{-1/4}) rate via a momentum estimator, giving theoretical backing to a technique widely used in practice for RNNs, Transformers, and diffusion models. The connection to the Frank-Wolfe short step enables a principled integration of weight decay that preserves convergence, addressing a practical gap in non-Euclidean optimizers. Open questions include the puzzling empirical observation that the gradient norm *increases* in the constrained (Algorithm 2) setting, and whether the non-Euclidean (L0,L1)-smoothness condition is the right one to characterize neural network loss landscapes beyond the modular norm setting.
