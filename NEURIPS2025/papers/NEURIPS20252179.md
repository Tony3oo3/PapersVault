## Problem

Training-free federated learning methods that share only class means (FedNCM) are communication-efficient but underperform, while methods sharing second-order statistics (Fed3R) improve accuracy at a d²K communication cost and increased privacy risk. The paper asks whether the benefits of second-order statistics can be obtained without clients ever transmitting covariance matrices.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252179/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each client computes d-dimensional class means using a shared frozen pre-trained backbone (SqueezeNet d=512, MobileNetV2 d=1280, or ViT-B/16 d=768) and sends only these means plus per-class sample counts to the server. The server outputs a d×C matrix of normalized classifier weights that can be used directly for inference or as initialization for subsequent federated fine-tuning or linear probing. The total uplink communication is dC'K (where C' is the max classes per client), identical to FedNCM and orders of magnitude less than Fed3R's (dC'+d²)K.

## Method

FedCOF exploits the fact that the variance of per-client sample means equals the class population covariance divided by the local sample size (Var[F̄_{k,c}] = Σ_c/n_{k,c}), so the weighted spread of client means around the global mean yields a provably unbiased estimator of Σ_c (Proposition 1). A shrinkage term γI_d stabilizes the estimate when K is small relative to d. The server then decomposes the ridge-regression Gram matrix G into within-class and between-class scatter (Proposition 2) and deliberately drops the between-class term, which is highly ill-conditioned (condition numbers ~10⁷) and causes overfitting. The final classifier is W* = Ĝ⁻¹B with Ĝ = Σ_c(N_c−1)Σ̂_c + Nμ̂_gμ̂_g^T, using only the estimated within-class covariances. This yields a training-free initialization that matches or exceeds Fed3R accuracy at FedNCM-level communication, and also serves as a stronger starting point for federated fine-tuning and linear probing.

## Results

FedCOF achieves 53.7% top-1 accuracy on CUB200, 44.0% on Stanford Cars, 37.8% on ImageNet-R, 56.1% on CIFAR-100, and 32.5% on iNat-120K using SqueezeNet (d=512), all with the same communication cost as FedNCM (e.g., 5.9 MB vs. 110.2 MB for Fed3R on CIFAR-100). On the large-scale iNat-120K benchmark with MobileNetV2, FedCOF reaches 44.1% accuracy with only 279.5 MB communication, compared to Fed3R's 41.5% at 61,064 MB (218× more). When followed by federated fine-tuning (FedAdam), FedCOF initialization yields 55.7% on CUB200 and 49.6% on Cars, outperforming both Fed3R+FedAdam and random-initialization baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against FedNCM (same communication cost), FedCOF improves accuracy by 4–26% across all datasets and architectures, with the largest gains on fine-grained datasets (24% on Cars with SqueezeNet, 26% with ViT-B/16) and 8+ points on iNat-120K. Against Fed3R (which shares full d×d second-order statistics), FedCOF wins on CUB200 by 3.3–3.9%, on Cars by 4.1–5.7%, and on iNat-120K by 0.4–2.6%, while tying on CIFAR-100 and ImageNet-R — all at 1/18th to 1/218th the communication cost. Against federated prompt-tuning methods (PFPT, FedAvg-PT, FedProx-PT with ViT-B/32), FedCOF achieves higher accuracy on all four datasets (e.g., 65.0% vs. 38.6% on CUB200, 50.4% vs. 12.9% on Cars) with roughly 100× less communication (7–11 MB vs. 846–1,794 MB). Against training-based baselines FedAvg and FedAdam (random classifier, pre-trained backbone), FedCOF training-free already outperforms both on CUB200 (53.7% vs. 46.4% FedAdam) and Cars (44.0% vs. 41.8%), and after fine-tuning with FedAdam, FedCOF initialization yields the best results (55.7% CUB200, 49.6% Cars). The paper also reports that FedCOF outperforms CCVR [35] when adapted for classifier initialization (Appendix K).

## Relation to Prior Work

FedCOF sits at the intersection of two recent training-free federated learning lines: FedNCM (Legate et al., NeurIPS 2023), which initializes classifiers from aggregated class means, and Fed3R (Fani et al., ICML 2024), which improves upon FedNCM by sharing full second-order feature statistics (G and B matrices) to solve a ridge regression problem. The key conceptual departure is a statistical observation: the variance of per-client class means across clients is an unbiased estimator of the class population covariance, so second-order information can be recovered from first-order statistics alone. This eliminates the d²K communication overhead of Fed3R while retaining its accuracy benefits. Additionally, the paper departs from the standard ridge regression formulation by showing that the between-class scatter term is harmful (ill-conditioned, causes overfitting) and should be removed, aligning the initialization more closely with Linear Discriminant Analysis.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for cross-device federated learning where communication is the bottleneck: FedCOF matches or beats Fed3R at roughly 1/20th the communication cost (280 MB vs. 61 GB on iNat-120K), making second-order-statistic-based classifier initialization feasible at scale. The method is also a strong initialization for subsequent federated fine-tuning or linear probing, accelerating convergence. Key limitations include dependence on the number of clients (fewer clients → noisier covariance estimates, mitigated by sampling multiple means per client) and the per-class iid assumption, which the authors analyze but do not fully resolve for highly heterogeneous feature shifts. An open question is how the estimator behaves under adversarial or non-stationary client distributions, and whether the shrinkage parameter can be made adaptive rather than hand-tuned per architecture.
