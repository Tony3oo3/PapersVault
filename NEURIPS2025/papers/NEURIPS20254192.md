## Problem

Event-based monocular depth estimation is fundamentally limited by the quality of its supervision: LiDAR depth labels are spatially sparse, temporally misaligned with microsecond-precision events, and riddled with scanline artifacts in dynamic scenes. Meanwhile, image-based foundational depth models have learned rich geometric and semantic priors from large-scale RGB-depth datasets, but these priors have never been explicitly transferred into the event domain to compensate for the supervision gap.

## Input / Output

The method consumes asynchronous event streams discretized into spatiotemporal voxel grids (H×W×B, with B=5 for MVSEC at 260×346 and B=15 for DSEC at 480×640) over a 50 ms window, optionally augmented with a co-recorded APS or RGB frame. It outputs a dense metric depth map at full sensor resolution. During training, a second relative-depth head is also active but discarded at inference, and the network additionally produces a per-pixel confidence map used only to weight the synthetic-depth loss.

## Method

Distil-E2D generates dense synthetic depth pseudolabels by running DepthAnythingV2 on co-registered APS/RGB frames, then reconciles them with sparse LiDAR ground truth via a Confidence-guided Calibrated Depth Loss (CCDL): a small MLP learns a nonlinear log-depth calibration mapping synthetic relative depths to the LiDAR metric scale, and a lightweight CNN propagates sparse alignment-confidence scores into a dense per-pixel weight map that adaptively gates the synthetic supervision. The backbone pairs a recurrent ConvLSTM event encoder with a Context Transformer that cross-attends the current event features against the previous timestep's depth prediction to inject temporal context, and in the E+I variant a DepthAnythingV2 image branch is fused back into the event features via a second cross-attention module. A Dual-Decoder Training scheme simultaneously supervises a metric-depth head (with CCDL) and a relative-depth head (with uncalibrated synthetic depths), encouraging the shared encoder to learn richer spatial representations without gradient interference between the two depth abstractions.

## Results

On MVSEC, Distil-E2D(E) achieves average depth errors of 1.14 m / 1.56 m / 1.81 m at 10 m / 20 m / 30 m cutoffs on the day1 sequence, outperforming the best prior event-only method EvT+(E) (1.31 / 1.92 / 2.32 m) by 13–22%. In the event+image setting, Distil-E2D(E+I) reaches 1.04 / 1.44 / 1.75 m on day1, surpassing EvT+(E+I) (1.24 / 1.91 / 2.36 m) by 16–26%. On the higher-resolution DSEC dataset, Distil-E2D(E) achieves 0.64 m at the 10 m cutoff, a 33% reduction over the self-supervised EMD baseline (0.96 m). Inference runtime is 10.5 ms (E) and 47.5 ms (E+I), competitive with or faster than most baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Distil-E2D outperforms all six baselines across every sequence and cutoff distance on MVSEC. In the event-only setting, it beats the strongest prior method EvT+(E) by 0.17 m at 10 m on day1 (1.14 vs. 1.31) and by up to 0.51 m at 30 m on night1 (2.16 vs. 2.67); the gap widens substantially against older methods like ULODE (2.72 m) and MDDE+ (1.85 m) on day1. In the event+image setting, Distil-E2D(E+I) surpasses EvT+(E+I) by 0.20 m at 10 m on day1 (1.04 vs. 1.24) and RAMNet by 0.35 m (1.04 vs. 1.39), while also achieving a 4.5 m advantage over RAMNet on night1 (1.35 vs. 2.50 at 10 m). On DSEC, Distil-E2D(E) reduces the 10 m depth error from 0.96 m (EMD, self-supervised) to 0.64 m, a 33% improvement, and from 2.21 m to 1.61 m at 30 m. Notably, Distil-E2D(E) runs at 10.5 ms, faster than ERE (35 ms) and EvT+(E) (35 ms), while the E+I variant (47.5 ms) is slightly slower than EvT+(E+I) (52 ms) but far faster than ERE-class methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of two well-developed lines of work: event-based monocular depth estimation (EMDE) and foundational image-based depth models. It departs from prior EMDE methods (MDDE+, ERE, EvT+, RAMNet) that rely solely on sparse LiDAR supervision or synthetic simulation, and from self-supervised approaches (EMD) that use photometric consistency, by introducing a distillation pipeline that transfers dense depth priors from DepthAnythingV2 into the event domain via co-registered APS/RGB frames. Architecturally, it builds on the ConvLSTM event encoder and Transformer-based temporal modeling established by EvT+ and ERE, but adds a Context Transformer for cross-branch temporal integration and a Dual-Decoder Training scheme that has no direct precedent in the EMDE literature. The CCDL loss with its Nonlinear Depth Calibration and Alignment-aware Confidence Estimation is a novel contribution that addresses the relative-to-metric depth mismatch in a way that goes beyond the standard scale-invariant loss used by all prior event-based depth methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a fundamental bottleneck in EMDE—the sparsity and artifact-laden nature of LiDAR supervision—by showing that calibrated, confidence-weighted pseudolabels from image foundation models can substantially outperform both synthetic simulation and self-supervised alternatives. This opens a practical pathway for improving event-based perception without requiring new hardware or large-scale data collection, and the distillation framework could potentially transfer to other event-based tasks (optical flow, segmentation). Key limitations include dependence on the quality of the foundation model in low-light or textureless regions, and the still-limited diversity of real-world event training data. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to indoor or non-driving scenes, whether ensembles of depth models can reduce pseudolabel bias, and whether video-to-event synthesis can further scale training data.
