## Problem

Existing satellite datasets for wildfire smoke detection are small, region-limited, and focus on scene-level classification rather than pixel-level segmentation. NOAA's Hazard Mapping System (HMS) provides large-scale expert annotations, but these span multi-hour time windows that do not align with individual satellite frames, creating a temporal mismatch that complicates direct use for supervised deep learning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251383/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes 256×256×3 true-color GOES satellite images (ABI bands C01–C03 at 0.47, 0.64, and 0.865 μm, 1 km resolution) and produces a 256×256×3 ordinal smoke density segmentation mask. The output uses thermometer encoding (none < light < medium < heavy) rather than one-hot, so each higher density class includes all lower-class activations, and training applies binary cross-entropy loss independently across the three density channels.

## Method

The core contribution is Pseudo-Label Dimension Reduction (PLDR), a non-iterative semi-supervised method that resolves the many-to-one temporal mismatch between HMS annotations and satellite frames. First, Mie scattering physics selects the optimal satellite (GOES-East or West) and the frame with the highest expected smoke signal-to-noise ratio within each annotation window, producing an intermediate one-to-one dataset D_M. A parent segmentation model (EfficientNetV2 + PSPNet) is then trained on D_M and applied to every frame in each annotation window to generate intermediary pseudo-labels (IPLs). The frame whose IPL achieves the maximum IoU with the original analyst annotation is selected, yielding the final one-to-one SmokeViz dataset D_p. A child model trained on D_p outperforms the parent on both test sets, confirming that PLDR reduces training noise by aligning labels with their most representative image.

## Results

On the SmokeViz test set (18,489 samples from 2022), the proposed child model f_c (EfficientNetV2 + PSPNet) achieves an overall IoU of 0.6532, with per-density IoU of 0.3804 (Heavy), 0.4776 (Medium), and 0.7301 (Light). The PLDR refinement substantially improves over the Mie-derived parent model f_o, which scores 0.4831 overall IoU on the same test set, demonstrating that temporal alignment via intermediary pseudo-labels reduces training noise. Precision and recall also improve with PLDR, reaching 0.8204 and 0.7622 respectively for f_c on D_p.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper benchmarks four encoder-decoder architectures on the SmokeViz dataset. The proposed f_c (EfficientNetV2 + PSPNet) outperforms all three baselines on every density level and overall IoU. The closest competitor is ViT + DPT (0.6309 overall IoU), trailing f_c by 2.2 points; the gap widens to 5.1 points on Heavy smoke (0.3804 vs. 0.3292). EfficientViT + UPerNet (0.6173) and EfficientViT + SegFormer (0.6051) lag further behind, with SegFormer showing the weakest Heavy-smoke performance (0.2602 IoU, a 12-point deficit). The consistent ranking across architectures suggests the dataset itself is a robust benchmark, while the CNN-based PSPNet decoder with EfficientNetV2 encoder best captures the multi-scale, amorphous boundaries characteristic of smoke plumes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the pseudo-labeling paradigm (Lee, 2013) in a fundamentally different direction: rather than using a parent model to generate labels for unlabeled data to expand training sets, PLDR uses intermediary pseudo-labels to reduce temporal dimensionality, selecting the single satellite frame that best matches a coarse analyst annotation. It builds on NOAA's Hazard Mapping System (HMS) expert annotations and GOES satellite imagery, transforming a many-to-one (time-window-to-label) dataset into a one-to-one frame-aligned dataset. The segmentation baselines (PSPNet, SegFormer, UPerNet, DPT) are standard architectures applied here as benchmarks rather than novel contributions, and the Mie scattering physics guides satellite and band selection in a way not seen in prior smoke datasets (SmokeNet, Wen et al., Larsen et al.), which were smaller, region-limited, or used scene-level classification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SmokeViz is the largest publicly available satellite-based smoke segmentation dataset (163K samples across multiple fire seasons and biogeographies), filling a critical gap for wildfire-related ML research with direct public health implications (PM2.5 exposure). PLDR is a generalizable preprocessing strategy applicable to any domain where annotations span temporal or spatial intervals rather than individual frames (medical imaging, video analysis, multi-source remote sensing). Key open questions include distinguishing smoke from dust, quantifying uncertainty in analyst annotations, resolving the performance gap between GOES-East and GOES-West, and mitigating the confounding effect of agricultural burns that constitute a large fraction of training samples but are less representative of the target wildfire detection task.
