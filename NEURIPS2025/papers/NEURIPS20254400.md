## Problem

Existing text-to-image systems lack intuitive, fine-grained control over spatial composition, multi-object consistency, and iterative editing without requiring architectural modifications or task-specific fine-tuning. Agent-based approaches like LayoutGPT and GenArtist attempt structured generation but suffer from spatial incoherence, stylistic drift across editing steps, and reliance on numerous external tools, while generic diffusion models cannot reason over complex multi-object layouts or maintain identity across batch edits.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254400/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system accepts a free-form text prompt (ranging from a single phrase like 'Alice in Wonderland' to detailed scene descriptions), optional reference object images for identity-preserving integration, and existing background images with bounding-box masks for inpainting. It produces a structured JSON layout plan specifying object classes, bounding boxes, generation order (far-to-near for occlusion), and inter-object spatial relationships, followed by a composited RGB image where objects are seamlessly integrated into the background. For batch collage editing, the same reference object is consistently inserted across multiple user-uploaded photos in a single pass, with the Coordinator handling multi-turn user feedback to iteratively refine placement and appearance.

## Method

LayerCraft is a three-agent pipeline built on GPT-4o (Coordinator and ChainArchitect) and a LoRA-adapted FLUX.1-dev diffusion model (OIN). The ChainArchitect applies chain-of-thought reasoning to decompose prompts into a dependency-aware 3D scene graph, determining generation order (distant objects before closer ones to manage occlusion) and explicit spatial relationships (e.g., 'A is on top of B'), then uses GPT-4o's vision capabilities to analyze the generated background's viewpoint for accurate placement. The OIN employs a dual-LoRA architecture: two independent adapters (W_bg for background inpainting, W_obj for subject-driven generation) are trained in a two-phase protocol on the IPA300K dataset (300K paired images), following OminiControl's positional embedding scheme. At inference, the latent sequence is bifurcated into parallel branches [C_T, X, C_bg] and [C_T, X, C_obj], each attending with its own QKV weight set; outputs are merged by replacing the masked region's latents from the object branch while averaging text-token representations, preserving both background integrity and object fidelity without activating LoRA layers during final denoising.

## Results

On T2I-Compbench, LayerCraft achieves state-of-the-art scores across all six metrics: Color 0.864, Shape 0.705, Texture 0.815, Spatial 0.643, Non-Spatial 0.351, and Numeracy 0.633, outperforming the next-best agent-based method GenArtist (Spatial 0.544) and the strongest generic model SDv3 (Spatial 0.314). On GenEval, LayerCraft matches GPT-4o's overall score of 0.84 while achieving perfect 1.00 on Single Object and 0.94 on Two Objects. On MagicBrush multi-turn editing, LayerCraft attains the highest CLIP-I (0.912), DINO (0.854), and CLIP-T (0.316) scores, surpassing GenArtist and the MagicBrush baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LayerCraft outperforms all 13 baselines on T2I-Compbench across every metric. The largest margins are on Spatial relationship (0.643 vs. GenArtist's 0.544, +0.099) and Texture (0.815 vs. GenArtist's 0.771, +0.044), confirming the benefit of CoT-driven layout planning over GenArtist's generate-then-edit paradigm. Against generic diffusion models (SDv3, FLUX.1-Dev, DALL-E 3), LayerCraft leads by 0.33–0.36 on Spatial, reflecting its explicit object-by-object generation with positional constraints. On GenEval, LayerCraft ties GPT-4o overall (0.84) but wins on Single Object (1.00 vs. 0.99), Two Objects (0.94 vs. 0.92), and Attribute Binding (0.62 vs. 0.61), while GPT-4o edges ahead on Counting (0.85 vs. 0.82) and Colors (0.92 vs. 0.89). On MagicBrush multi-turn editing, LayerCraft surpasses GenArtist on CLIP-I (0.912 vs. 0.907), DINO (0.854 vs. 0.849), and CLIP-T (0.316 vs. 0.307), with near-identical L1/L2 pixel losses. In the user study, LayerCraft leads GPT-4o on prompt consistency (4.5 vs. 4.4) and naturalness (4.5 vs. 4.3), while GPT-4o slightly edges ahead on visual appeal (4.5 vs. 4.4). For subject-driven inpainting, OIN preserves fine details (bottle text, violin chin rest, earring) that ACE++ and EasyControl blur or lose, and runs at comparable speed to ACE++ but 2–3× faster than EasyControl.

## Relation to Prior Work

LayerCraft sits at the intersection of LLM-agent-based layout planning (LayoutGPT, GenArtist) and parameter-efficient diffusion model adaptation (OminiControl, ControlNet). It departs from LayoutGPT by replacing static layout templates with CoT-driven, 3D-aware spatial reasoning, and from GenArtist's 'generate-then-edit' paradigm (which chains 10+ external models) by unifying layout planning and object integration within a single FLUX backbone via dual LoRA adapters. The OIN's attention-mixing mechanism for simultaneous background inpainting and subject-driven generation extends OminiControl's positional embedding technique into a two-branch parallel processing scheme that avoids quadratic memory blowup.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework demonstrates that a lightweight, single-model pipeline with LoRA adapters can match or exceed multi-model agent systems (GenArtist, LLM-Blueprint) on compositional benchmarks while offering interpretable, step-wise control that non-experts can iterate on. The IPA300K dataset (300K paired scene/isolation images) provides a practical resource for subject-driven inpainting research. Key limitations include the computational overhead of multi-agent CoT coordination for complex scenes, and the reliance on GPT-4o as the reasoning backbone, which limits reproducibility and introduces API dependency. Open questions include whether the CoT layout planning generalizes to 3D scene understanding and whether the dual-LoRA approach scales to more than two simultaneous conditional inputs.
