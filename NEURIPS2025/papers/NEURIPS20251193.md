## Problem

Promptable segmentation models like SAM2 excel at generating high-quality masks from visual prompts but lack semantic understanding, while open-vocabulary segmentation methods that add language grounding typically rely on cascaded detect-then-classify pipelines or resource-intensive LLMs. The core challenges are (1) semantic grounding—mapping free-form text to visual entities—and (2) instance awareness—distinguishing multiple spatially disjoint objects that match the same text query—without imposing prohibitive computational overhead.

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image (processed at 1024×1024 for SAM2's Hiera encoder and 224×224 for BEiT-3) and a text prompt ranging from a single category noun (e.g., 'zebra') to a full referring expression (e.g., 'the man in a red shirt'). It outputs K=20 masks with per-mask confidence scores from SAM2's decoder; these are post-processed via confidence filtering and NMS into semantic, instance, or panoptic segmentation results, with each mask inheriting the text prompt as its class label. An optional two-stage inference re-feeds predicted masks as visual prompts to refine contours.

## Method

OpenWorldSAM freezes both SAM2 (Hiera-Large image encoder + mask decoder) and BEiT-3 (early-fusion multi-modal encoder), training only a ~4.5M-parameter adapter comprising an MLP projector (1024D→256D), K learnable positional tie-breaker vectors, and a 3-layer cross-attention Transformer. The key mechanism is the positional tie-breaker: K learned 256-D vectors perturb the projected language embedding into K distinct queries (q_i = u + t_i), mimicking how a human would click different points to segment each instance, thereby resolving multi-instance ambiguity from a single text prompt. The cross-attention layers then ground each query in SAM2's level-3 (64×64) image features, sharpening localization by allowing semantic context to focus on relevant spatial regions. Training uses Hungarian matching with focal loss on COCO-Stuff (104K images, 25 epochs, single A100), and the tie-breakers naturally specialize to different spatial regions without explicit instance-count supervision. BEiT-3's early cross-modal fusion (joint text-image processing across all 24 layers) is critical, outperforming CLIP-style late fusion by +33.9 mIoU on ADE-150.

## Results

OpenWorldSAM achieves state-of-the-art zero-shot open-vocabulary segmentation with only 4.5M trainable parameters, reaching 60.4 mIoU on ADE20K-150, 33.1 mIoU on ADE20K-857, 47.5 mIoU on PASCAL Context-459, and 55.6 mIoU on ScanNet-40. On COCO panoptic/instance segmentation, it scores 35.2 PQ and 16.9 mAP. For referring expression segmentation on RefCOCOg, it achieves 74.0 cIoU, competitive with much larger 7B-parameter LLM-based models while using only a 0.7B BEiT-3 encoder plus 4.5M adapter parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OpenWorldSAM substantially outperforms the previous SOTA X-Decoder (38M params) on large-vocabulary benchmarks: +23.9 mIoU on ADE20K-857 (33.1 vs 9.2), +25.7 mIoU on PASCAL Context-459 (47.5 vs 21.8 for APE), and +25.9 mIoU on ScanNet-40 (55.6 vs 29.7). It also surpasses ESC-Net (451M params) by +18.6 mIoU on ADE20K-150 (60.4 vs 41.8) while using 100× fewer trainable parameters. The one clear loss is on COCO mAP, where APE (23.5) outperforms OpenWorldSAM (16.9), attributed to APE's use of additional detection datasets (Objects365, LVIS, OpenImages) during training. Under the oracle-prompts protocol (Table 2), OpenWorldSAM still leads X-Decoder on ADE20K-857 (+4.0) and PC-459 (+5.2), but X-Decoder slightly edges ahead on smaller, COCO-overlapping datasets (VOC-20: 98.1 vs 98.0; PC-59: 75.5 vs 73.7). For referring segmentation, OpenWorldSAM (74.0 cIoU) trails EVF-SAM (77.0) and u-LLaVA-7B (74.8) but matches UNINEXT (74.4) and GLaMM (74.2) while uniquely supporting both open-vocabulary and interactive segmentation with far fewer parameters.

## Relation to Prior Work

OpenWorldSAM extends SAM2's interactive segmentation backbone by injecting language understanding through a lightweight adapter, departing from prior SAM+LLM approaches (LISA, GLaMM, u-LLaVA) that rely on 7B+ parameter language models at prohibitive computational cost. It builds directly on EVF-SAM's demonstration that early vision-language fusion (via BEiT-3) can ground text into SAM's mask decoder, but generalizes from single-object referring segmentation to multi-instance open-vocabulary segmentation through the novel positional tie-breaker mechanism. Unlike generalist models (X-Decoder, APE, SEEM) that require resource-intensive multi-dataset pre-training, OpenWorldSAM freezes all heavy encoders and trains only 4.5M adapter parameters on COCO-Stuff alone, positioning itself as a plug-in extension rather than a monolithic retraining.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a 4.5M-parameter adapter can unlock open-vocabulary segmentation capabilities rivaling models with 10–100× more trainable parameters, offering a practical, low-cost path to adding language grounding to any SAM-based pipeline. The oracle-prompts evaluation protocol it introduces is methodologically important, exposing how global-matching evaluation unfairly penalizes prompt-based models on fine-grained vocabularies. However, the model underperforms on outdoor driving datasets (Cityscapes, BDD10K), revealing a domain-shift limitation of COCO-only pre-training that remains an open challenge. The finding that fine-tuning the 673M BEiT-3 encoder actively degrades performance (60.4→23.6 mIoU) suggests that pre-trained VLM semantics are fragile under sparse supervision, raising broader questions about when parameter-efficient adaptation suffices versus when continued pre-training is needed.
