## Problem

Text-guided image inpainting must simultaneously preserve unmasked regions and achieve semantic consistency between inpainted masked regions and their surroundings, yet prior diffusion-based methods can only address one of these two challenges. The paper identifies the root cause as the entanglement of low- and mid-frequency bands during denoising: low-frequency components are easily modulated by text prompts (disrupting unmasked regions), while mid-frequency components are stable but insufficient alone to ensure cross-region consistency.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250362/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a partially masked RGB image, a binary mask (0 = inpaint, 1 = preserve), and a free-form text prompt describing the desired content for the masked region. It operates entirely in the 4×(H/8)×(W/8) latent space of a pre-trained Stable Diffusion V1.5 model (100 denoising steps), requiring no additional training. The output is a complete inpainted image in pixel space, evaluated on both inside-inpainting (mask interior) and outside-inpainting (mask at image boundary) scenarios on BrushBench and EditBench.

## Method

NTN-Diff splits the T-step denoising process into an early stage (steps T down to λT, λ=0.6) and a late stage (steps λT down to 0). During the early stage, three denoising branches run in parallel at each step: (i) a null-text branch that denoises without text conditioning to protect low-frequency content, with unmasked regions replaced by forward-diffusion counterparts; (ii) a text-guided branch that aligns mid-frequency bands with the prompt while its low-frequency band is substituted from branch (i) via 2D DCT decomposition; and (iii) a second null-text branch whose mid-frequency band is substituted from branch (ii) to denoise low-frequency content for masked regions under mid-frequency guidance. In the late stage, a single text-guided denoising process refines the result while unmasked regions are progressively replaced by their forward-diffusion values to guarantee preservation. The key insight is that mid-frequency bands converge early and remain stable under text prompts during late-stage denoising, making them a reliable semantic bridge between masked and unmasked regions, whereas low-frequency bands must be shielded from text influence to avoid corruption.

## Results

On BrushBench Inside Inpainting, NTN-Diff achieves the best image quality (IR=12.45, HPSv2=27.57) and text alignment (CLIP Score=26.54) while also leading in unmasked region preservation (PSNR=23.51, MSE=6.50×10⁻¹³, LPIPS=40.79×10⁻¹³) among non-blended methods. With the pixel-level blending variant (NTN-Diff*), PSNR jumps to 40.70 and LPIPS drops to 0.88×10⁻¹³, surpassing BrushNet* (PSNR=31.94, LPIPS=18.67×10⁻¹³). On Outside Inpainting, NTN-Diff similarly leads in IR (11.54), HPSv2 (28.22), and CLIP Score (27.54), with NTN-Diff* achieving PSNR=31.08 and LPIPS=1.24×10⁻¹³.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NTN-Diff outperforms all five BrushBench baselines (BLD, CNI, PI, BrushNet, HDP) on every metric for both Inside and Outside inpainting. Against the closest competitor BrushNet, NTN-Diff gains +0.09 IR, +0.17 HPSv2, +1.86 PSNR, and −2.81 MSE (×10⁻¹³) on Inside inpainting, while also achieving a higher CLIP Score (26.54 vs. 26.48). BLD and CNI show the largest gaps: CNI's PSNR collapses to 12.39 (Inside) and 11.91 (Outside) with LPIPS exceeding 200×10⁻¹³, indicating severe unmasked-region corruption. HDP, despite its high-resolution focus, trails NTN-Diff by 0.77 IR and 0.67 HPSv2 on Inside inpainting. On EditBench (Table 6), NTN-Diff* achieves the best PSNR (36.83) and lowest LPIPS (5.39×10⁻³), surpassing BrushEdit* (PSNR=32.97, LPIPS=7.24×10⁻³) and BrushNet* (PSNR=33.66, LPIPS=10.12×10⁻³), while also leading in CLIP Score (29.72 vs. 29.62 for BrushEdit*). The paper notes that BrushNet retains the largest remaining margin over NTN-Diff (up to 0.73% IR, 8.59% PSNR), confirming it as the strongest single-branch baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on Stable Diffusion's latent diffusion framework and positions itself at the intersection of two prior lines of work: (1) unmasked-region preservation methods like BLD and SmartBrush that blend or substitute latents but suffer semantic inconsistency, and (2) semantic-consistency methods like BrushNet and CAT that maintain cross-region coherence but disrupt unmasked regions. The key departure is a frequency-domain decomposition strategy using DCT to separate low and mid bands, combined with a novel 'null-text-null' architecture that runs three parallel denoising processes in the early stage to selectively shield each frequency band from text-prompt interference. This draws on recent frequency-aware diffusion work (FBSDiff, FreeDiff, FlexiEdit) but repurposes it specifically for the dual-objective inpainting problem rather than image translation or editing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a useful mechanistic explanation for why prior inpainting methods fail: low-frequency bands are highly susceptible to text-prompt modulation during high-noise denoising, while mid-frequency bands stabilize quickly and remain robust. This insight could inform future inpainting and editing methods beyond this specific architecture. However, the method's complexity (three parallel UNet forward passes per early-stage step) and its reliance on heuristic DCT thresholds limit practical adoption, and the performance gains over BrushNet are modest on most metrics (e.g., ~0.2% CLIP Score). Open questions include whether the frequency-stability observation generalizes to transformer-based diffusion backbones (e.g., DiT/SD3) and whether the adaptive thresholding strategy scales to arbitrary mask geometries.
