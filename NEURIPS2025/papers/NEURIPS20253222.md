## Problem

Large reasoning models (LRMs) such as DeepSeek-R1 and OpenAI o1 achieve strong accuracy on complex tasks but do so by generating very long sequential chains-of-thought, incurring high inference latency. Existing parallel inference methods (Tree-of-Thoughts, Graph-of-Thoughts, self-consistency) either impose rigid predefined search structures, lack coordination across parallel paths, or fail to support multi-step sequential reasoning where later steps depend on earlier results. No prior framework simultaneously enables dynamic, model-learned parallelism, adaptive multi-stage planning, and task-specific model optimization for general reasoning.

## Input / Output

SPRINT takes a single text query (e.g., a math word problem) and produces a final answer enclosed in \boxed{} along with a structured multi-stage reasoning trajectory. Each stage consists of a planner output (reasoning tokens plus one or more <prompt_i.j> subtask instructions) followed by parallel executor outputs in <execution_i.j> tags. Inference is capped at 12 stages with a per-segment token limit of 8,000, and the effective sequential token count is computed as the sum over stages of the maximum tokens across all parallel executions at that stage, rather than the total token count.

## Method

SPRINT introduces a two-part framework: a data curation pipeline and an interleaved planner-executor inference protocol. The pipeline decomposes sequential DeepSeek-R1 trajectories into individual steps, infers a dependency DAG via an LLM, packs steps into stages (with a greedy optimization that merges plan-only steps into the same stage as their dependents to reduce stage count), and filters for trajectories with a parallelization ratio above 1.5, yielding ~1,700 training samples. The base model (DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B) is then fully fine-tuned on these reorganized trajectories for 5 epochs. At inference, the same fine-tuned model alternates between a planner role (generating a stage plan with independent subtask prompts given the full cumulative context) and an executor role (each executor independently solving one prompt in parallel), with results synced back into the context before the next planning round. This rolling-horizon design lets the model dynamically discover parallelization opportunities without any hand-crafted search structure, reducing sequential tokens by up to 39% on long trajectories while matching or exceeding the accuracy of a standard reasoning fine-tuned baseline.

## Results

On MATH-500, SPRINT achieves 92.5% pass@1 accuracy while generating 2,440 sequential tokens on average, outperforming the reasoning fine-tuned (RFT) baseline (91.0%, 2,880 tokens) by 1.5% accuracy and ~15% fewer sequential tokens. On the out-of-domain Countdown benchmark, SPRINT achieves 85.9% accuracy with only 2,284 sequential tokens—a 53.5% reduction over RFT (4,917 tokens)—while also matching or exceeding all baselines in accuracy. On GPQA-Diamond, SPRINT reaches 51.0% accuracy with 6,336 sequential tokens, a 10.8% reduction over RFT. For problems requiring >8,000 tokens under RFT, SPRINT achieves up to 39% sequential token reduction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SPRINT outperforms the RFT baseline (standard distillation on the same 1,700 R1 trajectories) on all three benchmarks: +1.5% accuracy on MATH-500 (92.5% vs 91.0%) with 440 fewer sequential tokens, +1.0% on Countdown (85.9% vs 84.9%) with a 53.5% sequential token reduction, and +0.5% on GPQA-Diamond (51.0% vs 50.5%) with a 10.8% token reduction. Against SoT-reasoning (the strongest parallel baseline), SPRINT wins on MATH-500 in both accuracy (92.5% vs 90.8%) and sequential tokens (2,440 vs 3,836, a 36% reduction), and on Countdown in both accuracy (85.9% vs 82.4%) and tokens (2,284 vs 5,823). Self-consistency with repeated sampling, while using fewer sequential tokens on MATH-500 (590), suffers a large accuracy drop (80.5% vs 92.5%) and does not generalize well to GPQA (45.4% vs 51.0%). The base DeepSeek-R1-Distill-7B model (89.1% on MATH-500) is improved by SPRINT's fine-tuning to 92.5%, demonstrating that the structured plan-execution format itself contributes to accuracy gains beyond mere distillation.

## Relation to Prior Work

SPRINT extends the line of work on parallelizing LLM reasoning (Tree-of-Thoughts, Graph-of-Thoughts, Skeleton-of-Thought, self-consistency) by introducing a trained planner-executor architecture that performs multi-stage interleaved planning with concurrent execution, rather than relying on fixed search structures or uncoordinated parallel sampling. It most directly builds on PASTA and APR, which also decompose reasoning into parallel subtasks, but departs from them by supporting multi-step sequential planning (not just single-round decomposition) and by using a generalizable data curation pipeline (DAG-based step extraction and packing) instead of task-specific symbolic solvers. The key conceptual departure from sequential reasoning models like DeepSeek-R1 is the recognition that many steps within a single trajectory are independent and can be reorganized into parallel stages without sacrificing accuracy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SPRINT offers a practical path to reducing inference latency for reasoning models by up to 39% on long trajectories while matching or exceeding accuracy, which is significant as reasoning models are increasingly deployed but their long sequential outputs remain a major cost bottleneck. The generalization to out-of-domain tasks (GPQA, Countdown) with only ~1,700 training examples suggests the parallelization skill is somewhat domain-agnostic. However, the paper acknowledges that full wall-clock speedup requires hardware-aware optimizations (KV caching, GPU interconnects) that were not implemented, and the approach is validated only on a 7B model. Open questions include whether latency-aware RL can push parallelism beyond what SFT on curated trajectories achieves, and whether the framework extends naturally to tool-use and API-calling scenarios where parallel invocations dominate latency.
