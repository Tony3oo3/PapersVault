## Problem

Value decomposition (approximating a global multi-agent value function as a sum of local per-agent functions) is widely used in practice but lacks a clear theoretical characterization of when it is exact or how to bound its error. This paper identifies the missing structural condition: the transition matrix must be 'separable' in a sense analogous to quantum entanglement, and quantifies the decomposition error for general entangled systems.

## Input / Output

The method takes a multi-agent MDP specified by its joint transition kernel P, local bounded rewards r_i, and a global policy π, from which the induced transition matrix P_AB^π ∈ R^{|S|²|A|² × |S|²|A|²} is formed. It outputs a scalar Markov entanglement measure (the minimum agent-wise TV distance to any separable transition matrix), local Q-value functions Q_i^π learned via tabular TD on marginalized local transitions, and a closed-form decomposition error bound of the form 4γ(E_A·r_max^A + E_B·r_max^B)/(1−γ)². For empirical estimation, a single trajectory of length T = 5N suffices to solve a convex optimization over P_A, avoiding the exponential global state space.

## Method

The paper defines Markov entanglement by requiring the joint transition matrix to be expressible as a linear combination of Kronecker products of per-agent transition matrices (P_AB^π = Σ x_j P_A^(j) ⊗ P_B^(j)), directly mirroring the separability condition in quantum information theory. The key sufficiency proof uses an 'absorbing' technique: because P·e = e for any transition matrix, the tensor-product structure collapses under repeated multiplication, yielding Q_AB^π = Q_A^π ⊗ e + e ⊗ Q_B^π exactly. The necessity proof constructs the orthogonal complement of the separable subspace under the Frobenius inner product and shows that any entangled component can be exploited to construct a reward pair that breaks decomposability, with a spectral-radius argument linking separability of (I−γP)^{-1} back to separability of P. For general entangled systems, the decomposition error is bounded by the agent-wise total variation (ATV) distance to the nearest separable matrix, and the paper proves that index policies on N-agent restless bandits have entanglement O(1/√N), yielding a sublinear O(√N) decomposition error via a mean-field concentration argument.

## Results

The paper's headline theoretical result is that index policies on N-agent RMABs have Markov entanglement bounded by C/√N (Theorem 6), which combined with the error bound (Theorem 5) yields a sublinear O(√N) decomposition error for the global Q-value. Empirically, on a Circulant RMAB with up to N=1800 agents (global state space 4^1800 > 10^1000), the estimated Markov entanglement decays as O(1/√N) and the μ-weighted decomposition error scales as O(√N), both consistent with theory. Both entanglement estimation and local Q-value learning are sample-efficient, requiring only T=5N trajectory steps.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a primarily theoretical paper and does not include head-to-head comparisons against competing value decomposition methods (e.g., Lagrange relaxation, ALP, or CTDE-based approaches like QMIX or QTRAN). The paper positions its contribution as providing the first general theoretical characterization of when value decomposition is exact or approximately valid for arbitrary multi-agent MDPs under arbitrary policies. Prior work by Adelman & Mersereau [2] showed Lagrange relaxation can have arbitrarily large errors for general MDPs, while the present paper's Markov entanglement framework gives a tight, interpretable bound that recovers the success of index policies (O(√N) error) and explains why value decomposition works in practice for ride-hailing (DiDi, Lyft) and behavioral health applications. The empirical validation on Circulant RMAB serves as a consistency check for the theory rather than a benchmark comparison.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the long line of value decomposition work in multi-agent MDPs, from Lagrange relaxation and the Whittle index policy [49, 47] to modern CTDE methods (QMIX, VDN) used in ride-hailing and healthcare. It also draws on mean-field concentration analysis for restless bandits [20, 21] to prove asymptotic separability of index policies. The key departure is introducing 'Markov entanglement'—a separability condition on transition matrices directly analogous to quantum entanglement—as a necessary and sufficient condition for exact value decomposition, filling a gap where prior theory either required specific structure (Lagrange relaxation) or offered no guarantees for general MDPs [2].

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This provides the first complete theoretical characterization of when the widely-deployed value decomposition technique in MARL is exact or approximately valid, resolving a long-standing gap between empirical success (DiDi, Lyft, behavioral health) and theoretical understanding. The O(√N) decomposition error bound for index policies and the efficient Monte-Carlo estimation procedure (Eq. 9) give practitioners a concrete diagnostic tool. Open questions include extending the framework to non-additive decompositions (QMIX-style monotonic factorizations), handling non-stationary or partially observable settings, and whether the quantum-entanglement analogy yields further structural insights beyond the current mathematical parallel.
