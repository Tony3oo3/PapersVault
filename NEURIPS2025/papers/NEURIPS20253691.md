## Problem

Diffusion models trained on long-tailed datasets produce high-quality samples for head classes but generate unrecognizable or semantically broken images for underrepresented tail classes. Existing remedies such as Class-Balancing Diffusion Models (CBDM) rely on strong distributional assumptions and single-objective optimization, offering no worst-case guarantees across all class labels. This paper asks whether a principled, game-theoretic training objective can provably bound the worst-case sampling error uniformly over every class.

## Input / Output

The model takes a discrete class label y ∈ Y and an initial Gaussian noise sample as input, and produces a generated RGB image (32×32 on CIFAR-10LT) by integrating a reverse SDE via the exponential integrator scheme. The score function for each class is parametrized as a two-layer ReLU network with randomly initialized and frozen input-layer weights (random feature model), where only the output-layer matrix A_y is trained. In the empirical setup, 15k samples are generated per class at the 60k-step training mark, and performance is measured by FID and IS on the long-tailed CIFAR-10LT split (imbalance factor 0.01).

## Method

The paper introduces a regularized per-class training objective that augments the standard denoising score-matching loss with a mutual learning term measuring the squared distance between the score network of class y and those of other classes sampled from a distribution Q, weighted by a hyperparameter β. Training is formulated as a |Y|-player convex minimization game over conditional score networks, with the Nash equilibrium serving as the egalitarian solution concept that equalizes worst-case performance. An individual gradient descent (IGD) algorithm updates each class's score network in turn, and the paper proves a non-asymptotic first-order convergence result: the Nash gap after T_train iterations is O(m²/√T_train + β). Combining this with Rademacher-complexity generalization bounds, Monte-Carlo approximation error, and SDE discretization/forward-convergence errors, the main theorem yields a data-independent worst-case KL-divergence bound of O(ε + 1) across all class labels for appropriately chosen hyperparameters (network width m, training steps T_train, discretization N, and regularization β). The mutual learning term effectively aligns tail-class score estimates with the high-confidence head-class scores in the high-noise regime, reducing fitting error where tail-class training data is scarce.

## Results

On CIFAR10LT (imbalance factor 0.01), the proposed Mutual Learning method achieves a FID of 14.58 and IS of 8.92±0.19, outperforming both the Vanilla DDPM baseline (FID 16.58, IS 8.78±0.15) and CBDM (FID 15.28, IS 8.11±0.14). The theoretical contribution is a worst-case KL-divergence sampling error bound of O(ε+1) across all class labels, with the Nash gap of the score network bounded by O(1/√T_train + β). The paper also provides a toy model visualization showing that mutual learning reduces fitting error in low-density regions for tail classes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two baselines on CIFAR10LT: Vanilla DDPM (individual per-class training with β=0) and CBDM (Class-Balancing Diffusion Models, the closest prior work). Mutual Learning outperforms Vanilla DDPM by 2.0 FID points (14.58 vs. 16.58) and 0.14 IS points (8.92 vs. 8.78), confirming that the mutual learning regularization improves generation quality over standard per-class training. Against CBDM, Mutual Learning achieves a 0.7 FID improvement (14.58 vs. 15.28) and a 0.81 IS improvement (8.92 vs. 8.11). The authors explicitly caveat that this is a preliminary comparison using a modified CBDM implementation with error networks rather than score networks for mutual learning, and they state that a comprehensive numerical comparison with CBDM is left as future work since the paper's primary contribution is theoretical.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the score-based diffusion model framework (Song et al. 2020; Wang et al. 2024) by incorporating a Deep Mutual Learning regularization term, but departs from the closest prior work—Class-Balancing Diffusion Models (Qin et al. 2023)—by formulating the training as a multi-player game across per-class score networks rather than a single-network optimization with a distribution adjustment regularizer. The convergence analysis borrows from Huang et al. 2023 on DML, but the key conceptual departure is identifying the Nash equilibrium of this game as the egalitarian solution concept that yields worst-case (max over all classes) KL divergence guarantees, rather than the expected-over-classes bound provided by prior conditional diffusion analyses (Fu et al. 2024). The theoretical treatment uses two-layer ReLU random feature networks for tractability, while experiments employ U-Net architectures following CBDM's implementation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to provide a complete theoretical pipeline—training convergence, generalization, and sampling error—for long-tailed diffusion generation with explicit worst-case guarantees across all class labels, offering practitioners concrete hyperparameter prescriptions (network width, training steps, discretization) to achieve a target error ε. However, the empirical validation is limited to CIFAR10LT with a rudimentary CBDM comparison, and the O(1) bias term in the final bound (arising from the intractability of the true score) remains unaddressed. Open questions include whether head-class performance is preserved under mutual learning, the effect of the peer-distribution Q on worst-case error, and whether the analysis extends to deeper networks where the PL inequality for the combined loss is unproven.
