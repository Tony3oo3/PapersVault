## Problem

Multimodal contrastive learning (MMCL) relies on the assumption that image-text pairs convey identical semantic content, yet real-world captions systematically omit or distort semantic information, creating cross-modal misalignment. Two opposing camps have emerged—one advocating mitigation of misalignment, the other arguing it can be beneficial—but no unified theory explains when each regime applies. Prior identifiability results for MMCL assume perfectly aligned pairs, leaving the impact of misalignment on learned representations theoretically uncharacterized.

## Input / Output

The method consumes paired image-text data where the text is generated from a biased subset of the image's latent semantic variables: selection bias determines which semantic dimensions are omitted from the caption, and perturbation bias randomly replaces values in a further subset of the selected dimensions. The MMCL encoders (image and text) each produce a shared representation of dimension equal to the number of unbiased semantic variables, which provably encodes exactly and only those unbiased variables while discarding all misaligned semantics and modality-specific noise factors.

## Method

The paper introduces a latent variable model that partitions the latent space into semantic variables (with arbitrary causal structure), image-specific variables, and text-specific variables, and formalizes misalignment through two mechanisms: selection bias (a non-empty subset of semantic variables is preserved in text) and perturbation bias (a random subset of the selected variables is stochastically altered per sample). The core theoretical result (Theorem 4.1) proves that minimizing the asymptotic MMCL objective (SymAlignMaxEnt) block-identifies exactly the semantic variables unaffected by both biases, regardless of the underlying latent causal graph among all semantic variables. Two corollaries translate this into practical guidance: full semantic recovery for pretraining requires complete, unbiased captions, while deliberately introducing misalignment on distribution-sensitive variables yields invariant representations for out-of-distribution generalization. The theory is validated through numerical simulations, real-world (MPI3D-Complex) and semi-synthetic (Causal3DIdent) datasets with controlled bias configurations, and a zero-shot case study on OpenCLIP models pretrained on LAION-400M.

## Results

Across all experiments, the paper validates its identifiability theorem: unbiased semantic variables shared across modalities are recovered with near-perfect fidelity (R² ≈ 1 in simulations, MCC ≥ 0.8 approaching 1 on MPI3D-Complex), while misaligned variables (omitted by selection bias or corrupted by perturbation bias) are consistently excluded (R² ≈ 0, MCC = 0). On the Causal3DIdent dataset with structured causal dependencies, unbiased variables again achieve R² ≈ 1, though strongly dependent variables show partial predictability even when misaligned. In the OpenCLIP zero-shot case study, F1 scores are high for frequently captioned concept groups (e.g., Animal, Object) but decline sharply for under-captioned groups (e.g., Trait, Emot.), confirming that selection bias in LAION-400M captions creates measurable blind spots in learned representations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not compare against competing methods; it is a theoretical analysis with validation experiments. The experiments are designed to confirm the identifiability predictions of Theorem 4.1 rather than to benchmark against alternative architectures or training objectives. The key empirical finding is that MMCL (trained with the SymAlignMaxEnt loss) provably and empirically recovers exactly the subset of semantic variables invariant to both selection and perturbation biases, while discarding all others. The OpenCLIP case study (§5.4) provides an indirect comparison across model scales, showing that increasing model size does not overcome the fundamental information loss caused by under-captioned concepts, reinforcing that the limitation is data-driven (selection bias) rather than capacity-driven.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the identifiability analysis of multimodal contrastive learning (Daunhawer et al., ICLR 2022; von Kügelgen et al., NeurIPS 2021) by introducing a latent variable model that explicitly characterizes cross-modal misalignment through two mechanisms—selection bias (omitted semantics) and perturbation bias (corrupted semantics). Prior identifiability results for MMCL assume perfectly aligned image-text pairs; here, the authors depart from that assumption and show that the SymAlignMaxEnt objective recovers only the unbiased subset of semantic variables, regardless of the latent causal structure. The work also reconciles two opposing empirical camps in the CLIP literature: those who treat misalignment as noise to be mitigated and those who find it beneficial for robustness, by showing both effects arise from the same identifiability mechanism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides practitioners with a principled framework for deciding when to invest in caption quality (foundation model pretraining) versus when to deliberately introduce or tolerate misalignment (OOD-robust representation learning). The OpenCLIP case study on LAION-400M concretely validates the theory, showing that under-captioned concept groups (e.g., stereotypes at 0.0003% coverage) are systematically underrepresented in learned representations. Key limitations include the asymptotic analysis (K→∞), the assumption of diffeomorphic generative mappings, and a simplified perturbation model (random subset with i.i.d. noise); extending these results to finite-sample regimes, structured text perturbations, and multi-modal settings beyond image-text remains open.
