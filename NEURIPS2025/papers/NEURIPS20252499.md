## Problem

Current RLVR methods for LLMs depend on verifiable rewards derived from short-form ground truth answers (e.g., integers) that can be checked via string matching or symbolic computation. This excludes abundant data sources with long-form answers such as full mathematical proofs, where automatic verification is infeasible, leaving a large fraction of available training data unused for RL post-training.

## Input / Output

The model takes a text prompt specifying a question and generates a response decomposed into a chain-of-thought c (reasoning steps) and a final answer a. The ground truth answer a* can range from short-form (an integer or symbolic expression) to long-form (a multi-sentence proof), and the method operates regardless of whether a* is automatically verifiable. Training samples n=4 generations per prompt at temperature 1, with the answer likelihood computed conditioned on each sampled chain-of-thought.

## Method

JEPO treats chain-of-thought as a latent variable in the generative process and applies Jensen's inequality to derive a tractable lower bound on the log marginal likelihood of the ground truth answer: log π_θ(a*|x) ≥ E_{c~π_θ(·|x)}[log π_θ(a*|x,c)]. The stochastic gradient decomposes into a REINFORCE term (with log π_θ(a*|x,c) as the implicit reward for sampled chain-of-thoughts) and a supervised loss term that directly encourages predicting a* given c, eliminating the need for any external verifiable reward or LLM-as-judge. A multi-sample extension places the log outside the n-sample average, tightening the bound monotonically with n and approaching the true marginal likelihood as n→∞. In practice, leave-one-out control variates reduce gradient variance, and a small KL regularization to a reference policy prevents formatting collapse and excessive drift.

## Results

On verifiable MATH data (70B), multi-sample JEPO reaches ~70% test set accuracy, modestly outperforming online RL with Sympy reward at similar KL budget. On semi-verifiable Numina data, the combined Sympy+LLM-judge accuracy improves by ~25% over Sympy-only scoring, and JEPO/combined algorithm asymptote to slightly better performance than online RL on the unverifiable 40% subset. On fully unverifiable Numina-proof data, JEPO outperforms SFT at the same epoch budget and achieves asymptotically better test-set proxy NLL. The supervised loss component is critical for long-form data: removing it (β_sup=0) causes test NLL to plateau at a substantially worse level.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against online RL with Sympy verifiable reward, JEPO is competitive on verifiable MATH data (similar test accuracy at 70B, slightly better asymptotic performance) but lags on training-set reward since it does not explicitly optimize the Sympy match. On semi-verifiable Numina data, JEPO and the combined JEPO+RL algorithm outperform pure online RL on the combined (Sympy + LLM-judge) accuracy metric, especially on the unverifiable 40% subset, while online RL retains a slight edge on Sympy-only scoring since it trains on that exact signal. Notably, online RL fails to improve test-set proxy NLL on Numina, whereas JEPO does, indicating the two methods find different solutions. Against SFT on fully unverifiable Numina-proof data, JEPO clearly wins on test NLL at both 8B and 70B scales, both at matched epoch budgets and asymptotically. Against the concurrent VR-CLI method (which corresponds to only the REINFORCE component of JEPO's gradient), JEPO's additional supervised loss term provides a meaningful advantage, particularly for long-form answers where the chain-of-thought alone is insufficient to recover the full ground truth.

## Relation to Prior Work

JEPO sits at the intersection of RLVR (Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards, as in DeepSeek-R1 and Kimi K1.5) and latent-variable modeling of chain-of-thought (Hoffman et al. 2024, Hu et al. 2024). The key departure is replacing the explicit verifiable reward with a Jensen's-inequality lower bound on the marginal likelihood of the ground truth answer, treating CoT as a latent variable — a pragmatic simplification of the full ELBO that avoids training an auxiliary posterior model. The multi-sample extension draws directly from importance-weighted autoencoders (Burda et al. 2015), and the gradient construction reuses REINFORCE with leave-one-out control variates (Mnih & Rezende 2016, Kool et al. 2019). The paper also positions itself against concurrent VR-CLI (Gurung & Lapata 2025), noting that JEPO's supervised component makes the optimization more complete.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that JEPO unlocks RL training on the large pool of long-form, unverifiable data (full proofs, symbolic expressions) that current RLVR pipelines must discard, potentially multiplying the effective training data for reasoning models. The finding that JEPO matches verifiable-reward RL on MATH while outperforming it on unverifiable subsets, and that the supervised loss component is critical for long-form but not short-form data, provides actionable guidance for practitioners. Open questions include how to more organically combine JEPO with verifiable rewards when both are available, whether the approach scales to pre-training or meta-thought settings, and how the proxy-NLL evaluation translates to actual reasoning quality on long-form outputs.
