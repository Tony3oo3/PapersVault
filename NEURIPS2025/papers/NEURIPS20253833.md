## Problem

Existing Marked Temporal Point Process (MTPP) models handle only simplified mark spaces—either single discrete event types or purely continuous attributes—forcing practitioners to either tokenize continuous values (violating natural inductive biases) or discard parts of the data. Real-world event sequences, such as electronic health records mixing discrete procedures with continuous lab measurements, have variable-length, mixed-type marks that no prior framework models jointly with expressive distributions for all variable types.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a flattened scalar sequence X of length L = 2T + ΣNᵢ (interleaving arrival times, discrete event types, and variable-length additional attributes) together with a type vector D indicating whether each position is continuous or discrete; optional conditional covariates (e.g., patient demographics) are prepended as extra tokens. Output is a factorized joint density: categorical distributions at discrete positions and one-dimensional rational-quadratic spline flow densities at continuous positions, enabling full-sequence sampling with per-attribute uncertainty. In the annotation application, the output is a set of events each carrying a start time, type label, duration, and type-specific properties, all with uncertainty intervals.

## Method

FLEXTPP is a single causal-masked Transformer that autoregressively models the flattened mixed-type sequence, using a classification head (softmax over a small MLP) for discrete positions and a one-dimensional rational-quadratic spline normalizing flow for continuous positions (including arrival times), making the model intensity-free and avoiding the numerical integration required by Hawkes-style competitors. The discrete event type at each event acts as a structural 'header' that determines how many and which types of additional attributes follow, while a learned EOS token terminates the sequence. Training is by maximum likelihood (negative log-likelihood) over the full factorized joint. The conditional variant FLEXTPP-C prepends auxiliary inputs as additional tokens, enabling the framework to serve as a structured prediction tool (e.g., annotating an input time series with events and their properties) rather than only a generative model. A local interpretability mechanism is derived by inverting the time density to recover an effective intensity function, allowing extraction of pairwise event influence curves.

## Results

On the EasyTPP benchmark (discrete marks), FLEXTPP achieves the best NLL on 4 of 5 datasets: Retweet (5.646 vs. 6.365 for S2P2), Taxi (−0.763 vs. −0.522), Taobao (−1.402 vs. −1.304), and StackOverflow (2.133 vs. 2.163), while S2P2 leads on Amazon (−0.781 vs. −0.633). On EHRSHOT patient records (mixed discrete-continuous marks), FLEXTPP-C achieves the lowest Time+Discrete Procedure NLL across all 20 diseases (e.g., 0.059 for Atrial fibrillation vs. 0.071 for ESGPT-C) and the best Full NLL on 18/20 diseases. On the synthetic time-series annotation task, FLEXTPP-C reaches AUC ROC of 0.990–0.998 on most event types, substantially outperforming the sliding-window CNN classifier (0.82 mean AUC ROC).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FLEXTPP outperforms all intensity-based Hawkes variants (RMTPP, NHP, SAHP, THP, AttNHP) on every EasyTPP dataset, with the largest gaps on Retweet (5.646 vs. 6.348–7.098) and Taobao (−1.402 vs. −1.003 to 1.712). Against the previous intensity-free SOTA IFTPP, FLEXTPP wins on all five datasets, most dramatically on Retweet (5.646 vs. 10.344) and Taxi (−0.763 vs. −0.453). The strongest competitor S2P2 (a continuous-time state-space model) beats FLEXTPP only on Amazon (−0.781 vs. −0.633) but loses on the other four. On EHRSHOT, FLEXTPP-C consistently outperforms ESGPT/ESGPT-C (which use Gaussian/Log-Normal mixture heads instead of normalizing flows) by 0.01–0.05 NLL on procedure prediction and 0.05–0.35 on full-sequence NLL, confirming the benefit of expressive continuous density modeling. On the annotation task, FLEXTPP-C achieves near-perfect AUC ROC (≥0.99) on external force, base frequency, and position noise events, while the sliding-window CNN classifier lags at 0.82 mean AUC ROC and fails entirely on damping changes (0.50 vs. 0.66).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies three previously separate threads in temporal point process modeling: intensity-free time distributions (Shchur et al., 2020), autoregressive density estimation with normalizing flows (Germain et al., 2015), and heterogeneous event structure modeling (McDermott et al., 2023). It departs from the dominant intensity-based Hawkes process paradigm by treating the entire event sequence—timestamps, discrete types, and continuous attributes—as a single mixed-type autoregressive sequence over a causal Transformer, and it elevates conditioning from an afterthought to a first-class design element. The key conceptual departure from Event Stream GPT is replacing its unimodal Gaussian heads for continuous marks with rational-quadratic spline flows, and from prior MTPP work is the explicit support for variable-length, mixed-type mark spaces within one model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a practical, unified tool for domains where event sequences are inherently heterogeneous (EHRs, cybersecurity logs, finance), removing the need to either discard continuous attributes or force everything into a discrete tokenization. The conditional MTPP formulation opens a path toward structured prediction with full uncertainty quantification (e.g., time series annotation), which is harder to achieve with classifier-based approaches that cannot handle overlapping events. Open questions include scaling beyond O(L²) Transformer cost for very long sequences, extending to other mark modalities (sets, geometric/topological data), and whether the intensity-free assumption holds for all temporal dynamics or whether some domains genuinely require the flexibility of learned intensity functions.
