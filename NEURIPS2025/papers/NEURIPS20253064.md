## Problem

Existing deep learning models for quantum property estimation assume access to large-scale, noiseless labeled datasets obtained from infinite sampling, an idealization that is infeasible given limited quantum hardware. In practice, available datasets are hybrid—containing a few high-quality samples alongside many noisy ones—and naively training on such hybrid data significantly degrades predictive performance. The paper asks how to maximally exploit hybrid datasets to improve DL model performance in QPE without increasing measurement overhead.

## Input / Output

The input is a classical data point (p, M, o) where p is a d-dimensional physical parameter vector, M is a sequence of m random Pauli measurement operators, and o ∈ {0,1}^{Nm} are the corresponding measurement outcomes. These are tokenized into a measurement string of length N (each (Pauli, outcome) pair maps to one of 6 tokens) and processed by a Transformer decoder with condition and position embeddings. The output is an (N−1)-dimensional vector where each entry corresponds to a quantum property (entanglement entropy or two-point correlation) for a specific subsystem, with the label quality determined by the number of measurements m used to construct the classical shadow.

## Method

AiDE-Q is an iterative data engine that alternates between data labeling, model training, and model evaluation over T rounds. The core mechanism is a consistency-check method: for each candidate data point, the current model generates s synthetic labels using s random subsets (each 25% of measurements) of the input, and the variance across these masked estimates is computed; data points whose variance falls below a threshold τ are selected as high-quality and added to the training set. The model is then fine-tuned on the expanded high-quality dataset, and if validation performance decreases, the threshold is raised to remove the least consistent newly added samples before retraining. The framework is paradigm-agnostic, integrating seamlessly with supervised, semi-supervised (teacher-student), and self-supervised pretraining + fine-tuning pipelines, and uses a shared Transformer decoder architecture with token, condition, and position embeddings across all paradigms.

## Results

On the 10-qubit Heisenberg XXZ model (m_u = 2^6), SL integrated with AiDE-Q (SL w.DE) achieves R² = 0.864 for entanglement entropy at r=0.6, surpassing the best reference model LLM4QPE (R² = 0.857 at r=0.8) and vanilla SL (R² = 0.740). For two-point correlations C_{1j}^x and C_{1j}^z, SL w.DE reaches R² = 0.972 and 0.978 at r=0.6, respectively. On the 9-qubit cluster Ising model, AiDE-Q integration yields up to 14.2% R² improvement (e.g., SL w.DE reaches R² = 0.873 vs. vanilla SL's 0.51 at r=0.8, m_u=2^6).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SL w. AiDE-Q consistently outperforms all four baselines across every property and ratio setting in Table 1. Against SSL4Q [51], the advantage is most pronounced for entanglement entropy (e.g., 0.864 vs. 0.804 at r=0.6) and two-point correlations C_{1j}^x (0.972 vs. 0.951 at r=0.6). Against LLM4QPE [52], SL w. AiDE-Q surpasses the best LLM4QPE result (0.857 at r=0.8) with a lower data ratio (0.864 at r=0.6), demonstrating that synthetic data quality matters more than model complexity. The NTK baseline [31] is highly unstable (R² drops to 0.267 for C_{1j}^z at r=0.6) and is dominated by SL w. AiDE-Q in all settings. The learning-free classical shadow (CS) [13] performs worst overall (R² ≈ 0.308 for C_{1j}^x), confirming that DL with AiDE-Q substantially outperforms measurement-only estimation. On the cluster Ising model (Table 2), all AiDE-Q-integrated variants (SL, SSL4Q, LLM4QPE) improve over their vanilla counterparts, with SSL4Q w. AiDE-Q reaching R² = 0.907 at r=0.8, m_u=2^8.

## Relation to Prior Work

AiDE-Q repurposes the 'data engine' concept from computer vision (e.g., SAM, AlphaFold 3) to the quantum property estimation domain, where no prior work has applied iterative synthetic-label quality filtering. Conceptually, it sits at the intersection of classical shadow-based quantum state learning [13], semi-supervised/self-supervised quantum learning paradigms (SSL4Q [51], LLM4QPE [52]), and self-training with pseudo-labeling. The key departure from prior QPE methods is the introduction of a consistency-check mechanism that uses variance across masked measurement subsets to automatically assess synthetic label quality, eliminating the need for exact labels or manual curation that traditional data engines require.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a critical practical gap: existing DL models for QPE assume access to large noiseless labeled datasets, which is unrealistic given the exponential measurement overhead and limited quantum hardware. The finding that a basic SL model augmented with AiDE-Q outperforms purpose-built SSL and SSL-FT paradigms suggests that data quality may matter more than learning paradigm sophistication, potentially simplifying the design space for quantum ML practitioners. Key open questions include the optimal allocation of limited quantum resources between high-quality and low-quality data (acknowledged as a limitation), whether the framework extends to real noisy quantum hardware, and whether the consistency-check principle generalizes beyond 1D spin models to more complex molecular or circuit-based systems.
