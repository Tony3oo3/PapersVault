## Problem

Classical GP-based Bayesian Optimization quantifies only value uncertainty under a fixed surrogate model, leaving model uncertainty—uncertainty about whether the GP itself is an appropriate representation of the reward—unaddressed. This limitation causes GP surrogates to fail on non-stationary, multi-modal, or heavy-tailed reward landscapes commonly encountered in hyperparameter tuning, portfolio optimization, and LLM prompt optimization, where the true reward distribution is unknown and potentially intractable to specify.

## Input / Output

The method consumes sequential noisy reward observations y(x_i) = μ*(x_i) + ε*(x_i) collected at adaptively chosen points over a compact domain X ⊂ R^d, and outputs the next query location via Thompson Sampling over the posterior. Unusually, the surrogate models the distribution of the noisy reward y(x) directly (rather than the deterministic μ*(x)), and the posterior is a distribution over an infinite mixture of GP surfaces rather than a single GP posterior. The concentration parameter ν is updated with data, dynamically controlling the exploration-exploitation balance in the distributional space.

## Method

The ∞-GP surrogate places a sequential Spatial Dirichlet Process prior over the space of reward distributions, with a stationary GP as the baseline measure. Each realized distribution G_x is a discrete measure over infinitely many GP surfaces via stick-breaking, so any observation x_i can be realized on any surface in the collection. Combined with Thompson Sampling, the acquisition policy draws from a structured mixture: with probability ν̂/(ν̂+n) it instantiates a new surface from the base GP prior (functional-level exploration), and with probability n̂_j/(ν̂+n) it performs Kriging on an existing surface j (exploitation of empirical knowledge). All hyperparameters (β, ν, τ, σ², φ) receive full Bayesian treatment via a Gibbs sampler with a truncated stick-breaking approximation. Theoretically, the posterior contracts around a broad class of reward densities (locally Hölder smooth with exponential tails) at a near-minimax-optimal L₁ rate of n^{-α/(2α+D)}(log n)^t.

## Results

On the seven language understanding prompt optimization tasks (SST-2, SST-5, MR, CR, AG's News, TREC, Subj), ∞-GP-TS achieves the highest average accuracy of 74.76%, outperforming GP-TS (72.61%), GP-UCB (71.49%), GP-EI (68.59%), and GP-KG (62.74%). The largest gains appear on the most challenging non-stationary tasks: SST-5 (52.58 vs. 49.91 for GP-TS) and Subj (68.55 vs. 65.18 for GP-TS). On synthetic benchmarks with heavy-tailed (Weibull) noise and non-stationary reward structures, ∞-GP-TS consistently achieves the lowest cumulative regret, while on real-world Portfolio, HPOBench, and NASBench201 tasks it matches or slightly exceeds baselines with notably lower variance across seeds.

## Baselines & Comparisons

∞-GP-TS wins on all seven language understanding datasets against every GP-based baseline. Against GP-TS (Chowdhury & Gopalan, ICML 2017), the closest competitor, ∞-GP-TS gains +2.15 points on average (74.76 vs. 72.61), with the largest margin on SST-5 (+2.67) and Subj (+3.37), where reward landscapes are most non-stationary. Against GP-UCB (Srinivas et al., 2009), the gap widens to +3.27 on average, and against GP-EI (Ament et al., NeurIPS 2023) and GP-KG (Wu & Frazier, NeurIPS 2016), the advantages are +6.17 and +12.02 respectively, reflecting the sensitivity of EI and KG to noisy, irregular reward signals. On synthetic heavy-tailed and non-stationary benchmarks, ∞-GP-TS achieves the lowest cumulative regret across all six function variants, while GP-EI and GP-KG degrade most severely. On real-world Portfolio, HPOBench, and NASBench201, the performance gap is smaller but ∞-GP-TS shows consistently lower variance across random seeds, indicating superior robustness under distributional uncertainty.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the Sequential Spatial Dirichlet Process (SDP) from spatial statistics (Gelfand et al., 2005) to the sequential, adaptive-query setting of Bayesian Optimization, with two key modifications: observations are collected at arbitrary locations chosen by an acquisition policy, and each observation can be realized on a different GP surface rather than all sharing one. It departs from prior GP-mixture approaches (Rasmussen & Ghahramani, 2001; Meeds & Osindero, 2005) that use a global Dirichlet Process to partition the input space into regions assigned to single experts, instead placing a spatial DP prior so that every location mixes over infinitely many GP surfaces. The paper also contrasts with ad-hoc fixes for GP misspecification—input warping for non-stationarity (Snoek et al., 2014), truncation for heavy tails (Chowdhury & Gopalan, 2019), and misspecified-BO analyses (Wynne et al., 2021)—by treating the distributional type itself as unknown and placing a nonparametric prior over the entire reward distribution space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ∞-GP framework provides a principled, unified mechanism for quantifying model uncertainty in BO that goes beyond the value uncertainty captured by standard GP posteriors, with a near-minimax-optimal posterior contraction guarantee for a broad class of reward distributions (Weibull, Laplace, Gamma, mixtures, etc.). This is practically relevant for settings where GP-BO demonstrably fails—financial portfolio optimization with heavy-tailed returns, non-stationary hyperparameter landscapes, and LLM prompt optimization with unstable reward signals. Key open questions include scalability to high-dimensional search spaces (the authors note the method is interpretable as a finite GP-TS mixture and could incorporate recent high-dimensional GP techniques), the computational cost of Gibbs sampling with a truncated stick-breaking representation, and whether the theoretical contraction rate translates into tighter regret bounds for the sequential BO objective rather than fixed-location density estimation.
