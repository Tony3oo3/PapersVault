## Problem

VAE-based structural inference models such as NRI rely on oversimplified uniform priors over latent interaction graphs and lack external structural supervision, causing them to struggle with complex dynamical systems like charged particle interactions. While some prior work incorporates manually crafted structural priors, no systematic approach exists for automatically obtaining reliable, transferable structural priors across diverse dynamical systems without ground-truth edge labels.

## Input / Output

The method consumes agent state time series of shape N×T×d (N agents, T timesteps, d-dimensional states) and optionally a synthetic meta-dataset of labeled trajectories from related but non-identical systems. It produces a discrete interaction graph as an N×N×K adjacency tensor (K edge types, sampled via Gumbel-Softmax) along with predicted future agent states. The pretrained estimator's GRU-based trajectory embeddings are concatenated with the joint module's own embeddings along the feature dimension before GNN message passing, and the learned prior replaces the standard uniform distribution in the KL divergence term.

## Method

IPSI alternates between two modules in an iterative loop: a pretrained structural estimator (SI_prior) and a VAE-based joint inference module (SI_joint). SI_prior uses GRU temporal embeddings and fully connected GNN message passing, trained with structural labels (from a synthetic meta-dataset or baseline pseudo-labels) to produce both enriched trajectory embeddings and a learned categorical prior over edge types. SI_joint concatenates SI_prior's embeddings with its own node representations and replaces the uniform prior in the ELBO's KL term with SI_prior's output, providing multi-level supervision at both the representational and generative levels. In subsequent iterations, SI_joint's inferred structures serve as new pseudo-labels to retrain SI_prior, progressively refining both modules and helping escape local optima. The framework is model-agnostic, requiring only that the base model follow the standard VAE structural inference paradigm (embedding → encoder → decoder), and has been demonstrated on both NRI and MPM backbones.

## Results

On the Charged Particle system (the most challenging dataset), IPSI(MPM-based, w/o prior) achieves 94.3% edge accuracy with 5 objects and 86.2% with 10 objects, substantially outperforming NRI (82.1% and 70.8%) and MPM (93.3% and 81.6%). On Kuramoto with 10 objects, IPSI(MPM-based, w/o prior) reaches 82.8% versus NRI's 75.7% and MPM's 80.3%. On the DoSI benchmark (15-node graphs), IPSI(MPM-based, w/o prior) achieves 95.32% AUROC on NetSims GRN and 96.75% on NetSims VN, compared to NRI's 78.08% and 89.13% respectively. On Springs, all methods saturate near 99.9% accuracy, indicating the task is already well-solved for simple radial interactions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IPSI consistently outperforms all four baselines on the most challenging Charged Particle dataset: IPSI(MPM-based) achieves 94.3% (5 objects) and 86.2% (10 objects) versus NRI's 82.1% and 70.8%, SUGAR's 82.9% and 72.0%, MPM's 93.3% and 81.6%, and iSIDG's 91.7% and 82.1%. On Kuramoto with 10 objects, IPSI(MPM-based, w/o prior) leads with 82.8%, surpassing MPM (80.3%), iSIDG (80.6%), NRI (75.7%), and SUGAR (74.0%). On Springs, all methods including IPSI saturate at ~99.9%, so no meaningful differentiation exists. On the DoSI benchmark (15-node real-world-inspired graphs), IPSI(MPM-based, w/o prior) achieves the best AUROC among VAE-based methods across all six datasets, with particularly large gains on NetSims GRN (95.32% vs. NRI's 78.08% and MPM's 76.06%) and NetSims VN (96.75% vs. NRI's 89.13% and MPM's 91.18%). Notably, IPSI(NRI-based) underperforms the MPM-based variant on Charged with 10 objects (77.1% vs. 86.4%), suggesting that the quality of the base model's encoder matters for the iterative refinement to succeed.

## Relation to Prior Work

IPSI builds directly on the NRI framework (Kipf et al., 2018) and extends the line of work that incorporates structural priors into VAE-based inference, including SUGAR's degree/sparsity regularizers and iSIDG's iterative edge-weight refinement. The key departure is replacing manually crafted or uniform priors with a *pretrained* structural estimator (SI_prior) that supplies both enriched trajectory embeddings and a learned categorical prior, trained via an iterative pseudo-label loop. This positions IPSI as a general plug-in enhancement layer over any VAE-based structural inference model rather than a standalone architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses a practical gap—how to obtain informative structural priors without ground-truth edges—by showing that a small supervised pretraining stage (on a meta-dataset or baseline pseudo-labels) followed by iterative alternation yields consistent gains across multiple baselines and system types. The perturbation analysis (6% vs. 63% non-optimal proportion under target loss) provides a mechanistic explanation for why the iterative loop helps, which is useful beyond this specific task. However, the method is limited to small systems (≤15 agents) due to O(N²) fully-connected GNN encoders, and the meta-dataset strategy requires at least partial knowledge of the interaction form, leaving scalability to large real-world networks as an open question.
