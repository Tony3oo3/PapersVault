## Problem

Deterministic segmentation models produce a single output, which is unreliable in safety-critical applications where inherent uncertainty and multiple valid annotations coexist. Existing stochastic segmentation approaches based on CVAEs (e.g., Probabilistic U-Net) rely on a single Gaussian latent distribution that the authors show degenerates into high-variance noise rather than encoding meaningful semantics, effectively making the latent variable interchangeable with random numbers. This paper addresses the gap by replacing the unimodal Gaussian with a structured, multimodal latent space that captures distinct semantic modes while remaining interpretable.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252831/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single image (128×128 CT slices for LIDC-IDRI, 256×256 crops for Crack500, 256×512 for Cityscapes) and produces a distribution over segmentation masks by sampling multiple latent variables from the learned prior. Each sample yields a full-resolution segmentation map, and the collection of samples encodes aleatoric uncertainty; the Jacobian determinant of the NF additionally provides a local uncertainty quantification. For deterministic benchmarks (Crack500, Cityscapes), a single sample is drawn to enable direct comparison with non-probabilistic baselines.

## Method

The core contribution replaces the standard Gaussian latent in CVAE-based segmentation with a GMM base distribution parameterized by a MIMO encoder (shared backbone plus multiple output heads generating per-component means, covariances, and mixture weights), followed by a three-layer Neural Spline Flow that applies invertible, monotonic rational-quadratic spline transformations to both the prior and posterior. The GMM introduces multimodal structure so that each Gaussian component specializes in a distinct semantic cluster via parameter competition, reducing the transformation burden on the NF to localized refinements rather than global structure learning. Training stability is ensured by constraining each component's mean to the range σ/20 < μ < 20σ and by a segmented β-annealing schedule (warm-up → linear increase → stable) that prevents KL vanishing. The ELBO combines a Dice reconstruction term with the KL divergence computed via forward NF in the posterior and inverse NF in the prior, and the framework is plug-and-play, applied to Probabilistic U-Net, Hierarchical Probabilistic U-Net, and PHISeg without architectural changes beyond the latent module.

## Results

On LIDC-IDRI, Prob. Unet+Our achieves the best GED across all sample counts (0.196 at 16 samples, 0.184 at 100 samples) and the best HM-IoU32 (0.644), outperforming the strongest diffusion-based baseline CCDM (0.212 GED16, 0.631 HM-IoU32). On Crack500 (curvilinear structure segmentation), the method achieves F1=71.80, surpassing the best deterministic baseline JTFN+CIRL (F1=67.62) by 4.2 points and the standard Probabilistic U-Net (F1=60.80) by 11.0 points. On Cityscapes, a single-sample inference yields IoU=73.0, exceeding CCDM with Dino ViT-S backbone (65.8) and UPerNet+Swin-Tiny (65.5) by over 7 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On LIDC-IDRI, Prob. Unet+Our outperforms all 12 baselines on GED (both 16 and 32 samples) and HM-IoU (16 and 32 samples), with the largest margin over the original Probabilistic U-Net (0.196 vs 0.310 GED₁₆) and a clear advantage over the strongest diffusion-based method CCDM (0.196 vs 0.212 GED₁₆, 0.644 vs 0.631 HM-IoU₃₂). JProb. Unet achieves the best HM-IoU₃₂ among baselines (0.647) but is outperformed by all three of the paper's variants on GED. On Crack500, the method (F1=71.80) surpasses the best deterministic baseline JTFN+CIRL (F1=67.62) by 4.2 points and the standard Probabilistic U-Net (F1=60.80) by 11.0 points, demonstrating that GMM+NF resolves the over-inclusive segmentation caused by high latent variance. On Cityscapes, a single-sample inference achieves IoU=73.0, beating CCDM+Dino ViT-S (65.8) by 7.2 points and UPerNet+Swin-Tiny (65.5) by 7.5 points. The ablation confirms that GMM with 3 components plus NSF is the optimal configuration (F1=71.23 vs 66.04 for single Gaussian+NSF and 61.39 for GMM without NF), and NSF outperforms RealNVP (69.15) and Glow (69.09) on Crack500.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the CVAE-based stochastic segmentation lineage initiated by Probabilistic U-Net (Kohl et al., 2018) and its hierarchical and PHISeg variants by replacing the single-Gaussian latent with a GMM base distribution transformed by Neural Spline Flows, applied symmetrically to both prior and posterior. It departs from cFlow (Selvan et al., 2020), which applies NF only to the posterior, and from JProb. Unet (Zhang et al., 2022), which uses a Reversible MLP for joint prior-posterior transformation, by arguing that a GMM provides a more natural multimodal initialization that reduces the transformation burden on the flow. The MIMO mechanism for generating GMM component parameters and the explicit mean/variance range constraint (σ/20 < μ < σ·20) are practical stabilizations not present in prior NF-in-VAE work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper's diagnostic showing that replacing the Gaussian latent in Probabilistic U-Net with pure random numbers barely degrades performance challenges a core assumption in CVAE-based segmentation—that the learned latent encodes meaningful semantic structure rather than merely injecting noise. This motivates the GMM+NF design and opens the question of how much of the 'stochasticity' in existing models is genuine uncertainty modeling versus uninformative randomness. Limitations include increased computational cost over standard CVAEs and training instability from NF log-determinant gradients, and the approach has not yet been validated on 3D volumetric segmentation or at scale beyond 256×512 resolutions.
