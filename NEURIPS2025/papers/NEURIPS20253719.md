## Problem

Diffusion language models suffer from a long decoding-window (LDW) problem where tokens at positions far from the input context become dominated by high-prior and repetitive tokens, producing incoherent text. The standard fix—semi-autoregressive (block) decoding—sacrifices both the speed advantage (due to a time-interval expansion problem that forces large step counts) and bidirectionality, effectively eliminating the core motivations for choosing diffusion LMs over autoregressive models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253719/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a text instruction/prompt followed by a fixed-size masked decoding window of L=1024 positions, and produces a generated text response by iteratively unmasking positions over S=128 diffusion steps. Unlike AR models that decode one token per step, the diffusion model samples multiple positions per step from a top-k candidate set, with the unmasking pattern governed by the decoding strategy (Conv, semi-AR, or categorical). An EOS-fill mechanism fills all positions right of a sampled EOS token with EOS, reducing effective inference steps by 1.5–3×.

## Method

The paper proposes two complementary methods. First, Convolutional Decoding (Conv) replaces hard block segmentation with a soft normalization: a 1D convolution filter counts unmasked positions within a kernel-size neighborhood of each position, and a tanh-transformed score modulates the unmask probability, smoothly narrowing the effective decoding window while preserving the original step size (avoiding the time-interval expansion problem) and supporting bidirectional generation. Second, Rejecting Rule-based Fine-Tuning (R2FT) is a short post-SFT training stage using a DPO/SimPO-style objective where negative samples are constructed by applying rule-based repetition patterns to positive samples; this directly reduces the model's prior π(c) for high-prior and repetitive tokens, shifting meaningful context-aligned tokens to higher ranks in the candidate zone and enabling deterministic top-k decoding. Together, these methods achieve state-of-the-art AlpacaEval scores among diffusion LM baselines at S=128 (roughly one-third the step size of prior work), with ~3× faster decoding than AR via EOS-fill and caching.

## Results

On AlpacaEval (L=1024, S=128), the combination of R2FT + Conv + top-k+Glob + EOS-fill achieves a length-controlled win rate of 46.92 and win rate of 45.92 for the small (180M) MDLM, surpassing the AR baseline (41.08/40.74). For the large 8B model, R2FT + Top-k+Glob+Conv reaches 55.96 LC win rate, outperforming the fully fine-tuned LLADA-8B-Instruct (35.63) by over 20 points. On GSM8K (L=512, S=64), Conv decoding with LLADA-8B-Instruct achieves 39.34% accuracy versus 29.49% for the original LLADA decoding. The method achieves ~3× faster decoding speed (tokens per step) compared to AR while maintaining or exceeding quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MDLM with categorical sampling [Sahoo et al., 36], the proposed R2FT+Conv combination achieves 46.92 vs. 32.16 LC win rate (small model) and 55.96 vs. 31.71 (large 8B model), a gain of 14–24 points. Against LLADA semi-AR decoding [Nie et al., 29], the advantage is even more dramatic: at S=128, LLADA decoding collapses to 14.54 (small) and 16.07 (large) LC win rate due to the time-interval expansion problem, while Conv maintains 46.32 and 43.79 respectively; the fully fine-tuned LLADA-8B-Instruct (35.63) is also outperformed by the LoRA-fine-tuned Conv model (55.96). Against ReMDM [Wang et al., 45] in the CoT setting, Conv+top-k (57.38 win rate) outperforms ReMDM+top-k (51.68) and ReMDM+Conv+top-k (50.41), which the authors attribute to revocability increasing the effective sample rate and harming structural coherence. Notably, R2FT alone with categorical decoding actually degrades performance (23.31 vs. 32.16), indicating that R2FT and top-k decoding are synergistic rather than independently sufficient.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the MDLM framework (Sahoo et al., 2024) and the semi-AR decoding strategies introduced by LLADA (Nie et al., 2025) and Block Diffusion (Arriola et al., 2025), which were the first to address what the authors term the long decoding-window (LDW) problem. It departs from these works by identifying a previously unrecognized limitation of semi-AR—the time-interval expansion problem—where splitting the decoding window into blocks inflates the effective per-block time interval, violating the continuous-time assumption underlying the MDLM objective. For the training-side contribution (R2FT), the paper adapts the DPO/SimPO preference-optimization loss (Rafailov et al., 2024; Meng et al., 2024) but replaces human-annotated negative samples with rule-based corrupted text, targeting the specific repetition and high-prior token patterns that cause the LDW problem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of the time-interval expansion problem reframes a core trade-off in diffusion LM research: the main solution to the LDW problem (semi-AR) was silently undermining the primary motivation for adopting diffusion over AR (speed), a tension that prior work had not surfaced. The practical consequence is significant—Conv + R2FT achieves SOTA among diffusion baselines at S=128 (roughly 3× faster than AR), whereas semi-AR requires S=1024 to maintain quality. Key open questions remain: the bidirectional generation advantage of diffusion LMs is only demonstrated via sampling-pattern visualizations rather than downstream benchmarks, and whether the speed advantage persists at larger scales or with KV-cache implementations (which are harder for diffusion LMs) is untested.
