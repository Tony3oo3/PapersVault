## Problem

Existing Bayesian recovery guarantees for inverse problems are restricted to Gaussian random matrices and Gaussian noise, leaving a gap for the practically important case of structured forward operators (e.g., subsampled Fourier transforms in MRI) and general noise. No unified, non-asymptotic framework existed that could quantify the sample complexity of posterior sampling for arbitrary prior, forward operator, and noise distributions, nor had the role of coherence in the Bayesian setting been established.

## Input / Output

The framework takes as input m noisy measurements y = Ax* + e, a forward operator A drawn from a distribution A, and an approximate prior P on R^n (typically the pushforward of a latent Gaussian via a Lipschitz DNN). It outputs a posterior sample x̂ ~ P(·|y,A) together with a high-probability error bound of the form ‖x* − x̂‖ ≤ C(η + σ), where η is a resolution parameter and σ is the noise level. The key constraint is that the real prior R is unknown; only the Wasserstein distance W_p(R, P) ≤ ε is assumed, and all guarantees are expressed in terms of the approximate prior P and the operator distribution A.

## Method

The core result (Theorem 3.1) is a non-asymptotic bound on the failure probability of posterior sampling, expressed in terms of four ingredients: the Wasserstein distance between the true and approximate priors, the η,δ-approximate covering number of P (quantifying its intrinsic complexity), lower/upper concentration bounds C_low and C_upp for the forward operator distribution A, and concentration/density-shift bounds for the noise. The critical technical innovation is that the concentration bounds are imposed only over the difference set D = supp(P) − supp(P) rather than all of R^n, which is essential for obtaining meaningful guarantees with structured operators. For subgaussian random matrices, this yields m = O(log Cov_{η,δ}(P) + log(1/δ)); for subsampled orthogonal transforms, the sample complexity additionally scales with the coherence μ(U;D) = n·sup{‖Ux‖_∞²/‖x‖² : x ∈ D}. For Lipschitz DNN priors with latent dimension k, the covering number satisfies log Cov_{η,δ}(P) = O(k log(L(√k + log(1/δ))/η)), giving near-optimal O(k log k) sample complexity.

## Results

The main result (Theorem 3.1) provides a non-asymptotic bound on the probability that posterior sampling error ‖x*−x̂‖ exceeds 34(η+σ), showing it is controlled by the approximate covering number Cov_{η,δ}(P), concentration bounds for the forward operator, and noise. For subgaussian random matrices (Theorem 3.5), stable and accurate recovery is guaranteed when m ≥ c·[log(Cov_{η,δ}(P)) + log(1/δ)], i.e., sample complexity scales linearly in the log of the distribution complexity. For subsampled orthogonal transforms (Theorem 3.9), the bound becomes m ≥ c·μ(U;D)·[log(Cov_{η,δ}(P)) + log(1/δ)], introducing a coherence factor. For Lipschitz generative DNNs with latent dimension k, the covering number satisfies log(Cov_{η,δ}(P)) = O(k·log[L(√k+log(1/δ))/η]), yielding near-optimal O(k·log k) sample complexity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no experimental baselines or empirical comparisons. The paper positions its contributions as generalizations of prior theoretical results: it extends Jalal et al. [49] (ICML 2021), which established Bayesian recovery guarantees only for Gaussian random matrices and Gaussian noise, to arbitrary forward operator and noise distributions via the introduction of concentration bounds C_low and C_upp. It provides a Bayesian analogue of the deterministic (non-Bayesian) generative compressed sensing results of Bora et al. [22] (ICML 2017) and Berk et al. [16] (IEEE J. Sel. Areas Inf. Theory 2022), replacing point estimators with posterior sampling and the range of a DNN with a full probability distribution. Compared to the deterministic framework of Adcock et al. [3] (ICML 2024), the Bayesian setting introduces the Wasserstein distance condition W_p(R,P) ≤ ε to handle the gap between the true and approximate prior, and the approximate covering number in place of classical covering numbers. The paper also identifies a quadratic bottleneck (O(k² log k) or O(s²)) for subsampled orthogonal transforms that persists from the non-Bayesian setting, noting it remains an open problem to remove.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper generalizes the Bayesian recovery framework of Jalal et al. [49], which was restricted to Gaussian random matrices and Gaussian noise, to arbitrary forward operator and noise distributions via the introduction of concentration bounds over the support difference set. It provides a Bayesian analogue of the deterministic compressed sensing results of Bora et al. [22] (generative models with Gaussian matrices) and Berk et al. [16] (generative models with subsampled orthogonal transforms), as well as the unified framework of Adcock et al. [3] for arbitrary model classes and measurement matrices. The key conceptual departure is that all distributional assumptions are made explicit and general, with the approximate covering number and concentration bounds serving as the sole complexity parameters, rather than being tied to specific distributional families.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides the first non-asymptotic, distribution-agnostic sample complexity guarantees for Bayesian posterior sampling in inverse problems, directly addressing when DL-based reconstruction methods are provably free of hallucinations — a critical concern for safety-sensitive applications like medical imaging. The log-linear scaling with latent dimension k for Lipschitz DNN priors gives a clean theoretical justification for why low-dimensional generative models are effective. A notable open limitation is the quadratic bottleneck (O(k² log k)) for subsampled orthogonal transforms like the DFT, which is worse than the O(k log k) rate for subgaussian matrices; resolving this gap, along with extending the framework to non-Gaussian noise without density assumptions, are identified as key future directions.
