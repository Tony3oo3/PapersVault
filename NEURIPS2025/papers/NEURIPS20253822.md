## Problem

Neural approaches to spectral decomposition of operators often rely on surrogate losses or architectural constraints that lack a clear variational foundation, leading to brittle optimization. The orbital minimization method (OMM) from computational chemistry offers an unconstrained, globally optimal objective for top-k eigensubspace recovery, but it has remained largely unnoticed in the modern ML literature and lacks a clean finite-dimensional proof of consistency. This paper provides a rigorous linear-algebraic derivation of OMM, reveals its connection to Sanger's rule for streaming PCA, and adapts it to train neural networks for operator eigenfunction learning.

## Input / Output

The method takes spatial coordinates x (or node feature vectors for graphs, or augmented image views for contrastive learning) as input to a neural network, along with a positive semidefinite operator T (e.g., a graph Laplacian, Hamiltonian, or canonical dependence kernel) that is applied to the network output. The network produces k eigenfunction values f(x) = [f_1(x), ..., f_k(x)] at each input point, and eigenvalue estimates are recovered from the second moment matrices M_ρ[f, Tf]. For PDE problems, the operator is a differential operator applied via autograd; for RL and graph settings, it is a matrix-vector product with the transition kernel or Laplacian.

## Method

The core contribution is a new variational derivation showing the OMM objective L_omm(V) = tr((I_d − VV^T)^2 A) − tr(A) is equivalent to the original formulation and that its global minimizer spans the top-k eigensubspace for any PSD matrix A, with a simple proof via reparameterization through the reduced SVD of VV^T. This extends to higher-order objectives L_omm^(p) with 2p-th powers, and to infinite-dimensional operators by replacing matrix products with second moment matrices M_ρ[f] and M_ρ[f, Tf]. For learning ordered eigenfunctions, the paper introduces joint and sequential nesting (NestedOMM) using stop-gradient tricks to compute per-mode gradients efficiently via a single surrogate objective. A key practical variant replaces the OMM gradient with the Sanger update (generalized Hebbian algorithm), which avoids numerical instability from statistical noise in minibatch estimates. The approach requires no explicit orthogonalization, no eigensolver, and no tunable hyperparameters, and the paper demonstrates its effectiveness on Laplacian representation learning for RL, Schrödinger equation solving, and self-supervised contrastive representation learning on images and graphs.

## Results

On CIFAR-100 self-supervised representation learning, the best OMM variant (mixed p∈{1,2}) achieves 64.77% top-1 and 89.18% top-5 linear-probe accuracy with a projector, compared to SimCLR's 66.50%/89.28%. On the OGBN-products graph benchmark (2.4M nodes), OMM's embedding achieves 74.17% linear-probe accuracy, dramatically outperforming Neural Eigenmaps' embedding (50.76%) while matching its intermediate representation (73.66% vs 74.05%). For Laplacian representation learning in RL, OMM matches ALLO's cosine-similarity scores across grid environments without any hyperparameter tuning, and in the k=50 ablation, OMM (e.g., 0.9435 on GridMaze-17) is comparable to re-tuned ALLO (0.9798) while avoiding a costly hyperparameter sweep.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ALLO [13] on RL Laplacian representation learning, OMM (both seq and jnt variants) achieves comparable cosine-similarity scores without any hyperparameter tuning, whereas ALLO requires grid-searching a barrier coefficient and growth rate; in the harder k=50 ablation, OMM matches re-tuned ALLO on most environments (e.g., 0.9435 vs 0.9798 on GridMaze-17) while avoiding the costly sweep. Against LoRA/NeuralSVD [43] on the 2D hydrogen Schrödinger equation, the Sanger variant of OMM performs on par with or slightly better than LoRA_seq across eigenvalue error, angle distance, and subspace distance metrics; on OGBN-products, LoRA training diverged entirely while OMM converged successfully. Against SimCLR [3] on CIFAR-100, the best OMM variant (mixed p∈{1,2}) reaches 64.77% top-1, trailing SimCLR by ~1.7 points, though the gap narrows to ~0.1 point on top-5 accuracy. Against Neural Eigenmaps [6] on OGBN-products, OMM's embedding (74.17%) dramatically outperforms Neural Eigenmaps' embedding (50.76%) by over 23 points, while their intermediate representations are comparable (~74%), indicating OMM more faithfully learns the target eigenfunctions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper re-derives the Orbital Minimization Method (OMM) from computational quantum chemistry (Ordejón 1993, Mauri 1993) via a simple variational principle, then shows it is mathematically equivalent to Sanger's rule (generalized Hebbian algorithm) from streaming PCA and closely related to the LoRA/NeuralSVD objective of Ryu et al. 2024. The key conceptual move is unifying these apparently disparate methods under a single PSD-matrix variational framework, revealing that OMM implicitly enforces orthogonality without explicit constraints or regularization. The paper departs from prior neural spectral-decomposition work (ALLO, Neural Eigenmaps, SpIN) by offering a hyperparameter-free, globally optimal objective, and extends the finite-dimensional theory to infinite-dimensional operators with a practical inverse-operator trick for unbounded spectra.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The hyperparameter-free, globally optimal OMM objective provides a principled drop-in replacement for more complex spectral-decomposition losses in neural operator learning, with the Sanger variant offering a stable pseudo-gradient alternative when OMM's gradient diverges under statistical noise. The unification with Sanger's rule is a long-overdue connection that could simplify the streaming PCA and online spectral learning literature. Key limitations remain: OMM requires PSD operators (necessitating spectrum shifts or inverse-operator tricks for unbounded problems), the self-supervised image results still trail SimCLR by ~2–6 points, and convergence guarantees for the parametric (neural) setting are absent. Open directions include extending to non-PSD and non-self-adjoint operators, quantum excited-state computation, and understanding why higher-order OMM (p>1) empirically escapes flat minima in representation learning.
