## Problem

BSDE-based deep learning methods for solving high-dimensional PDEs have been shown to underperform PINNs, but the root cause of this gap was unexplained. This paper identifies it as a non-vanishing discretization bias introduced by the standard Euler-Maruyama (EM) scheme in one-step self-consistency BSDE losses, which shifts the optimization landscape off the true PDE solution and cannot be resolved by reducing step-size.

## Input / Output

The method takes a PDE specified by drift f, diffusion g, nonlinearity h, terminal condition φ, and an initial-condition distribution μ₀ over the forward SDE. A single neural network u_θ(x,t) is trained to output both the PDE solution and its spatial gradient ∇u_θ(x,t) at any (x,t) pair; the loss is computed by simulating forward SDE trajectories and checking self-consistency of the backward SDE along those paths, rather than evaluating the PDE residual at fixed collocation points as in PINNs.

## Method

The key insight is that the standard EM discretization of the Itô self-consistency BSDE loss introduces an additive bias term ½tr[(H·∇²u)²] that is the same order as the PDE residual, so minimizing the loss does not drive the residual to zero. The paper reformulates both the forward and backward SDEs as Stratonovich SDEs (absorbing the Itô correction into the drift) and discretizes the augmented forward-backward system with the two-stage stochastic Heun integrator. This eliminates the leading-order bias, leaving only O(τ^{1/2}) higher-order terms, so the one-step self-consistency loss now correctly targets the PDE residual. The resulting Heun-BSDE algorithm uses a single shared network across time, requires no interpolating/horizon-length hyperparameter, and achieves competitive accuracy with PINNs on 100-dimensional HJB, BSB, and BZ benchmarks.

## Results

On the 100D HJB benchmark, Heun-BSDE achieves an RL2 error of 0.0493, outperforming EM-BSDE (0.3626) by ~7x and matching FS-PINNs (0.0737). On the 10D BZ problem, Heun-BSDE reaches 0.0228 RL2, beating EM-BSDE (0.1903) by ~8x and FS-PINNs (0.0351) by ~1.5x. The only exception is the 100D BZ case where all methods fail to converge (RL2 > 3), attributed to the extreme optimization difficulty of fully-coupled SDEs in high dimensions. Heun-BSDE is approximately 6x slower than EM-BSDE in training time (2.03x vs 0.34x relative to PINNs in the batched algorithm).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against standard PINNs (Raissi et al. 2019), Heun-BSDE wins decisively on all solvable benchmarks: 0.0493 vs 0.1260 on 100D HJB, 0.0535 vs 1.5066 on 100D BSB, and 0.0228 vs 3.8566 on 10D BZ. Against EM-BSDE (Raissi 2024, one-step self-consistency with Euler-Maruyama), Heun-BSDE achieves 5–8x lower RL2 error across all problems, confirming the theoretical prediction that EM introduces an irreducible O(1) bias term absent in the Heun formulation. Against the multi-step interpolating loss of Nüsken & Richter (2023), the paper shows in Section 6.2 that even the best-tuned skip-length k for EM-BSDE is still significantly outperformed by Heun-BSDE at k=1, and that the EM multi-step trade-off (bias vs. loss quality) persists regardless of discretization fineness. Heun-BSDE achieves parity with FS-PINNs (the trajectory-sampled PINNs variant) on most benchmarks, restoring the performance gap that prior work attributed to inherent BSDE limitations. The sole case where Heun-BSDE underperforms is 100D BZ, where all methods fail due to the complexity of fully-coupled SDEs in high dimensions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the self-consistency BSDE loss framework of Raissi [4] and Nüsken & Richter [5], which reformulate PDEs as forward-backward SDEs and train a single neural network via trajectory-based residual losses. It departs from prior BSDE work by identifying the Euler-Maruyama integration scheme as the root cause of the empirically observed performance gap between BSDE and PINNs methods, and by reformulating the problem in Stratonovich terms with stochastic Heun integration. The theoretical analysis of discretization bias (Lemma 4.1, Theorem 4.2) and its elimination under Heun (Lemma 4.3, Theorem 4.4) is novel; prior work on stochastic Runge-Kutta schemes for BSDEs [29] considered only the original multi-network BSDE losses rather than self-consistency formulations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a standard integration choice (EM) introduces an irreducible bias of the same order as the PDE residual fundamentally changes how BSDE-based PDE solvers should be implemented, and explains why prior EM-based BSDE methods systematically underperformed PINNs. The Heun-BSDE method restores parity with FS-PINNs across 100D benchmarks, but at roughly 6× computational cost, and the approach has not yet been validated on fully-coupled FBSDEs or in the model-free optimal control setting where BSDE methods have their strongest advantage over PINNs. Open questions include whether higher-order or adaptive integrators can close the computational gap, and whether the Stratonovich reformulation extends to the broader class of PDEs beyond the elliptic/parabolic setting analyzed here.
