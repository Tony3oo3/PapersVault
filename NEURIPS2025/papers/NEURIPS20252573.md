## Problem

Existing model-based RL methods for handling noisy visual observations decompose observations into task-relevant and task-irrelevant components but assume conditional independence between these factors given the observation. This ignores the collider structure inherent in the observation generation process, where the two latent sources are actually conditionally dependent once the observation is fixed. The paper asks whether explicitly modeling and exploiting this conditional dependence can yield more efficient denoising in world models without relying on masking heuristics or task-specific priors.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 64×64 RGB images contaminated with task-irrelevant background distractors (e.g., gray driving videos from Kinetics-400 or colorful DAVIS-2017 clips) along with the agent's action at each timestep. It outputs an agent action policy, a task-relevant latent state s_t (32 discrete dimensions × 32 classes) used exclusively for policy learning and imagination rollouts, a task-irrelevant latent state c_t (8–32 discrete dimensions depending on environment) capturing noise, and a joint reconstruction of the full observation. The policy and value function operate solely on s_t, while the world model's rollout phase uses only the task-relevant transition dynamics, effectively discarding c_t during planning.

## Method

CsRSSM extends RSSM by modeling observation generation as a sequential collider structure (s_t → o_t ← c_t) and replacing the standard conditional-independence factorization with the chain rule p(c_t, s_t | o_t) = p(c_t | o_t)·p(s_t | c_t, o_t), so that the noise component c_t is inferred first and then used as context to reduce the conditional entropy of s_t. A decoupling regularization based on an upper bound of the conditional mutual information I(s_t; c_t | o_t) is added to the ELBO loss, with a hyperparameter λ controlling the trade-off between exploiting conditional dependence for efficient inference and preventing the two latent variables from conflating. The total objective combines reconstruction log-likelihoods for observations, rewards, and continuation flags, two KL divergence terms (one per latent variable, each split into dynamics and representation components with stop-gradient), and the weighted MI bound. CsDreamer then performs policy learning and imagination rollouts exclusively in the s_t space using only the task-relevant transition model and reward predictor, requiring no masking mechanisms, segmentation priors, or auxiliary optimization objectives beyond the generative model's inherent objectives.

## Results

On DMC with gray video backgrounds (GB), CsDreamer achieves mean episode returns of 533±98 (Walker Run), 547±159 (Cheetah Run), 408±45 (Finger Spin), and 75±58 (Hopper Hop), outperforming the strongest baseline TIA by 82–225 points across tasks. On the more realistic CARLA driving simulator, CsDreamer reaches 235±118 vs. Dreamer's 202±144 and TIA's 44±119. On Atari 100K, CsDreamer achieves 129% human mean (66% human median), surpassing DreamerV3's official 112% human mean (49% human median).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CsDreamer outperforms all five baselines (Dreamer, TIA, Denoised MDP, Iso-Dream, and DBC) across every evaluated environment. Against Dreamer (the standard model-based RL baseline without explicit denoising), CsDreamer gains 371 points on Walker Run+GB (533 vs. 162) and 33 points on CARLA (235 vs. 202). Against TIA, the strongest dedicated denoising baseline, CsDreamer leads by 244 points on Walker Run+GB (533 vs. 293) and by a wide margin on CARLA (235 vs. 44), though TIA remains competitive on Cheetah Run+CB (758 vs. 821). Denoised MDP and Iso-Dream are substantially weaker, with Iso-Dream collapsing to near-zero returns on Hopper Hop in both noise settings and on Cheetah Run+CB (6±4). On Atari 100K, CsDreamer improves human-mean score from DreamerV3's 112% to 129%, confirming that the conditional-dependence mechanism generalizes beyond the DMC/CARLA benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the RSSM-based world model framework (Hafner et al., 2019; Dreamer, 2023) by introducing a collider-structure probabilistic graphical model that explicitly captures the conditional dependence between task-relevant and task-irrelevant latent variables given an observation. Prior decomposition-based approaches (TIA, Iso-Dream, Denoised MDP) assume conditional independence between these factors or rely on masking mechanisms and tailored objectives that encode prior knowledge; CsRSSM departs from this by using a unified generative model with sequential inference (inferring c_t first, then s_t conditioned on c_t and o_t) and a decoupling regularization via conditional mutual information. The conceptual lineage draws from causal inference (collider structures) and variational inference, applied to the RL world-model setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that the conditional dependence between task-relevant and task-irrelevant variables—previously assumed away by decomposition methods—can be actively leveraged to improve denoising efficiency, offering a principled alternative to ad-hoc masking strategies. Limitations include the assumption of Markovian, action-independent noise and a binary (task-relevant vs. task-irrelevant) partition of latent variables, which may not capture the full complexity of real-world distractors. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to non-Markovian noise, richer multi-factor decompositions, and whether the decoupling regularization can be made more adaptive than a fixed hyperparameter λ. The paper also raises societal considerations around overreliance in safety-critical applications like autonomous driving.
