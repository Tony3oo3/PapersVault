## Problem

Vector databases are valuable digital assets supporting ANN-based applications, yet no watermarking scheme exists specifically designed for them. Existing database watermarking targets relational tables, and vector-data watermarking targets GIS coordinates; neither accounts for the critical constraint that modifying vectors must not degrade ANN query accuracy. This paper is the first to formalize and solve the vector database watermarking problem, quantifying the 'embedding impact' on queries and minimizing it.

## Input / Output

The embedding algorithm consumes a vector database D of n d-dimensional real-valued vectors, a binary watermark string W of length L, and parameters including a random seed (for reproducible identifier generation), watermark strength s (target proportion of vectors mapping to the watermark bit per group), and transparency threshold τ (fraction of most transparent vectors eligible as carriers). Each carrier vector is modified by flipping exactly one bit in one dimension. The extraction algorithm takes a potentially attacked database and the same seed, reconstructing the watermark via per-group majority voting of cryptographic mapping results.

## Method

The scheme embeds watermarks by generating per-vector identifiers from MSB bits of randomly selected dimensions, grouping vectors via hash modulo L, cryptographically mapping each vector to a 0/1 bit, and flipping bits in selected vectors to achieve the target watermark strength s per group. The key innovation is the Transparent Vector Priority (TVP) strategy: the authors discover that HNSW's heuristic edge selection and pruning produce vectors with few or zero edges that are rarely or never retrieved by greedy search. They define a transparency score ts = (l − m_l)/δ_l − (e − m_e)/δ_e combining normalized average edge length and edge count, which correlates strongly (ρ = −0.70) with query frequency. TVP selects carrier vectors with ts above a threshold derived from the top τ fraction, ensuring modifications land on vectors whose displacement causes minimal missed or false queries. This reduces AMQ and AFQ by ~75% compared to random selection or prior schemes while maintaining comparable robustness under adaptive deletion and modification attacks.

## Results

On ANN_SIFT1M, TVP achieves an average number of missed queries (AMQ) of 3.15 and average number of false queries (AFQ) of 3.21, reducing both metrics by approximately 75% compared to the best baseline (TabularMark: AMQ=12.76, AFQ=13.15). Cross-dataset evaluation on deep1b (AMQ=3.17, AFQ=3.29) and arxiv-nomic-768-normalized (AMQ=4.26, AFQ=4.74) confirms consistent ~70–75% reductions. Under adaptive deletion and modification attacks, TVP's bit error rate (BER) remains comparable to SCPW and TabularMark, indicating that the embedding-impact reduction does not come at the cost of robustness.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares TVP against two state-of-the-art watermarking schemes adapted to vector databases: SCPW (Ren et al., IEEE TKDE 2023), a statistical-property-preserving relational database watermark, and TabularMark (Zheng et al., CCS 2024), a tabular-dataset watermark that uses MSB-based identifier generation. On the primary embedding-impact metrics, TVP decisively outperforms both: it achieves AMQ=3.15 vs. 14.26 (SCPW) and 12.76 (TabularMark), and AFQ=3.21 vs. 14.67 and 13.15 respectively — a roughly 75% reduction. The paper's own Random Selection (RS) baseline (AMQ=13.52, AFQ=13.65) confirms that the gain comes specifically from the transparent-vector-priority strategy rather than the cryptographic mapping framework. On robustness (BER under adaptive deletion and modification attacks), TVP is on par with both baselines, showing no meaningful degradation. Cross-dataset results on deep1b and arxiv-nomic-768-normalized further confirm that TVP's advantage generalizes beyond the primary SIFT1M benchmark.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves out a new application domain for watermarking by targeting vector databases, which sit at the intersection of relational database watermarking (Agrawal, Sion, Ren et al.) and GIS vector data watermarking (Voigt, Ren) but are distinct in their ANN-query-centric usage. It adapts the MSB-based identifier generation from TabularMark [36] to the vector setting and introduces a cryptographic mapping step to embed binary watermarks via selective bit-flipping. The key departure from prior work is the Transparent Vector Priority (TVP) strategy, which exploits a structural property of HNSW graphs—edgeless and low-degree vectors created by heuristic pruning—that has no analogue in relational or tabular watermarking literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As vector databases become central to RAG pipelines, recommender systems, and medical case retrieval, ownership protection is an under-addressed concern, and this is the first scheme to formalize and solve it. The structural observation that HNSW's pruning strategy produces a population of low-query-frequency 'transparent vectors' is a useful insight that could inform other index-aware operations beyond watermarking. However, the transparency score ts is currently HNSW-specific, and the paper acknowledges that extending TVP to other index structures (NSG, LSH, IVFFlat, IVFPQ) requires ad-hoc query-frequency recording rather than a principled ts analogue, leaving a clear open question for future work.
