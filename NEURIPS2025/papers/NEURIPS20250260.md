## Problem

Generative imitation learning policies (diffusion, flow matching) can exhibit unpredictable failures at runtime due to distribution shifts and compounding action errors, yet existing failure prediction methods either rely solely on observation-level OOD signals, require labeled failure data, or trigger alarms only after errors have already manifested. No prior approach simultaneously monitors both the policy's input observations and its stochastic action outputs without needing failure examples, leaving a gap in early, robust failure prediction for safety-critical deployment.

## Input / Output

At each policy timestep, FIPER consumes the observation history (1–2 RGB images at 96×96 to 512×512 plus proprioceptive state) passed through the policy's frozen ResNet-18 encoder, along with a batch of B (30–256) action chunks sampled from the generative policy. The ACE score is computed in Cartesian end-effector position space (3D) rather than joint space, making the uncertainty interpretable in the task's physical coordinates. The output is a binary Fail/Success flag with a detection time, calibrated so that the false-alarm probability on successful rollouts is bounded by a user-chosen δ (typically 0.01–0.10) via conformal prediction on as few as 10 successful rollouts.

## Method

FIPER combines two complementary uncertainty signals. First, RND-OE applies random network distillation in the policy's observation embedding space: a trainable predictor MLP (6 layers) is fitted to a frozen random target MLP (4 layers) on ID success rollouts, and the L2 distance between their outputs on the frozen encoder's features serves as an OOD score. Second, ACE measures entropy of the conditional action distribution by sampling B action chunks, binning each timestep's actions dimension-wise in Cartesian space with a fixed cell size derived from calibration data, and summing the per-timestep histogram entropies over the action chunk horizon. Both scores are aggregated over a sliding window of w policy timesteps, and their thresholds are calibrated via conformal prediction (one-sided CP band, CP constant, or time-varying quantile) on successful rollouts. A failure alarm fires only when both the observation-based and action-based scores simultaneously exceed their respective thresholds (logical AND), which the authors show yields higher balanced accuracy and timestep-wise accuracy than either signal alone while maintaining a provable false-alarm bound.

## Results

FIPER achieves the highest average Timestep-Wise Accuracy (TWA) of 0.65 and balanced accuracy of 0.78 across five environments (SORTING, STACKING, PUSHT, PRETZEL, PUSHCHAIR), with a normalized detection time (DT) of 0.30. Its overall true-positive rate is 0.92, meaning it correctly flags 92% of failed rollouts. The individual submodules RND-OE and ACE achieve even lower DT (0.18 and 0.25 respectively) when used alone, but at the cost of lower TWA and accuracy, demonstrating the benefit of the logical AND combination. FIPER outperforms all four baselines on both TWA and accuracy in every environment.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FIPER outperforms all four baselines on average TWA and accuracy across all five environments. Against logpZO [75] (the closest observation-based competitor), FIPER improves TWA from 0.60 to 0.65 and accuracy from 0.69 to 0.78, while also detecting failures earlier (DT 0.30 vs. 0.35). Against STAC [1], which also considers action distributions, FIPER achieves substantially higher TWA (0.65 vs. 0.57) and accuracy (0.78 vs. 0.68), with a 28% lower detection time (0.30 vs. 0.42); the gap is especially pronounced in multimodal tasks like SORTING and PRETZEL where STAC's temporal consistency score is less informative. Against RND-A [22], FIPER wins on all three metrics (TWA 0.65 vs. 0.56, Acc 0.78 vs. 0.62, DT 0.30 vs. 0.34). PCA-kmeans [42] performs worst overall, with a TNR of only 0.24, indicating it largely cannot distinguish OOD observations from actual failures and flags most rollouts as Fail (bracketed DT values). Notably, FIPER's individual submodules RND-OE and ACE achieve lower DT (0.18 and 0.25) than FIPER's combined 0.30, but the logical AND combination is essential for maintaining high TNR and accuracy, confirming that true failures require both OOD observations and high action uncertainty simultaneously.

## Relation to Prior Work

FIPER builds on the generative IL policy literature (Diffusion Policy, flow-matching policies) and the OOD detection literature (RND, PCA-kmeans, flow-matching-based logpZO), but departs from prior failure-detection methods (STAC, ReDiffuser, FAIL-Detect) in two key ways: it jointly monitors both observation-space anomalies and action-distribution uncertainty rather than isolating one signal, and it calibrates thresholds via conformal prediction on a small set of successful rollouts without requiring any failure examples. The action-chunk entropy (ACE) score is a novel contribution that addresses the multimodality inherent in IL action distributions, where variance-based measures (as in STAC) are misleading. The logical AND combination of the two sub-scores, with a formal FPR bound via Proposition 1, is a design choice not present in prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FIPER addresses a critical gap for safe deployment of generative robot policies in human-centered and safety-critical settings: predicting failures early enough to enable intervention, without the impractical requirement of collecting failure data. The framework is lightweight, task-agnostic, and applicable to both diffusion- and flow-matching-based policies, making it broadly relevant as these architectures become standard. Limitations include the need to collect successful rollouts and train a separate RND model, the restriction to single-task vision-based policies, and untested scalability to high-dimensional action spaces (e.g., humanoids). Open questions include adaptation to large-scale VLAs, multi-task settings, and whether the dual-signal AND logic generalizes to reinforcement learning with generative policies.
