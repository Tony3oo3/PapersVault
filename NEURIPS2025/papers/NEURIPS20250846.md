## Problem

Assistive wearable agents (e.g., smart glasses) require users to fully specify queries before the agent can act, creating unnecessary interaction friction. No existing benchmark provides ground-truth goals for inferring what a user wants from passive multimodal observations, and prior egocentric datasets (E.g., Ego4D) lack reliable goal annotations and under-represent key utility moments. This paper introduces WAGIBench, the first benchmark for egocentric multimodal goal inference that incorporates vision, audio, digital, and longitudinal context with verified ground-truth goals.

## Input / Output

The benchmark feeds VLMs four context modalities per observation: 32 uniformly sampled egocentric video frames (448×448 to 1120×1120 depending on model), Whisper-transcribed audio with speaker diarization, structured JSON digital app states (~8.9 KB across 7 apps: Calendar, Messaging, Notes, Search, Videos, Maps, Music), and longitudinal history represented as Socratic text captions (VLM-generated summaries of 5 prior episodes, ~148 s of history). Outputs are evaluated two ways: (1) discriminative MCQ where the model selects one of four structured digital-action options (A–D), and (2) generative, where the model produces a free-form JSON goal (type, source, query_item, etc.) scored 1.0/0.5/0 by an LLM judge conditioned on the reference goal and script cues.

## Method

WAGIBench is built on 3,477 observation–goal pairs collected from 348 participants performing 165 scripted scenarios on Meta Aria glasses, with ground-truth goals defined by the scripts themselves. Digital context is synthesized via a Digital Context Generator (DCG) pipeline that uses Llama-3.3-70B to produce realistic app states conditioned on persona profiles and optional digital cues; longitudinal context is represented as Socratic captions generated by two VLMs (Qwen2.5-72B and InternVL-78B) whose outputs are merged by an LLM to reduce hallucination. The benchmark provides two evaluation paradigms: MCQs with dataset-sampled distractors (similar at 95th–99th percentile, dissimilar at 0th–80th percentile via sentenceBERT embeddings) and a generative LLM-judge protocol. A meta-evaluation against human raters shows the LLM judge (with reference + script cues) achieves 76.8% pairwise alignment, statistically indistinguishable from human–human agreement (75.2%), while MCQ and SBERT-similarity baselines lag at 67.8% and 59.5%. Modality ablations on disjoint subsets (S_VA, S_VD, S_VL) quantify per-modality gains (up to 35% on MCQ) and reveal that digital and longitudinal modalities suffer from low signal-to-noise ratios, which larger models (≥72B) handle better than smaller ones.

## Results

On WAGIBench, GPT-4.1 achieves the best overall performance with 87.74% MCQ accuracy and 54.98% generative relevance score, while the best open-source model (Qwen2.5-VL-72B) reaches 87.55% MCQ and 49.80% generative. Human raters achieve 93% accuracy on similar-distractor MCQs and 97% on dissimilar-distractor MCQs, establishing a clear upper bound. In the generative setting, even the best model produces a relevant goal only ~55% of the time, indicating substantial room for improvement. Modality ablation shows that adding the task-relevant non-visual modality yields up to 35% MCQ gain and 30% generative gain over vision-only context.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates three open-source VLM families (Llama-3.2, InternVL-2.5, Qwen2.5-VL) at multiple sizes plus GPT-4.1 as a closed-source reference. Within each family, performance scales monotonically with model size: Qwen2.5-VL-72B (87.55% MCQ, 49.80% generative) outperforms Qwen-7B (77.54%, 39.99%) and Qwen-3B (71.53%, 24.68%); similarly InternVL-78B (86.80%, 48.66%) beats InternVL-8B (67.41%, 35.03%). GPT-4.1 edges out Qwen2.5-VL-72B by 0.19% on MCQ and 5.18% on generative, making it the strongest overall. Llama-3.2-11B-Vision performs worst (43.11% MCQ, 31.97% generative), largely because it processes only a single frame rather than 32 video frames. The key takeaway is that no model approaches the 93% human MCQ ceiling, and generative relevance remains far below practical utility at ~55% for the best model.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by formulating goal inference for assistive wearable agents as a distinct multi-modal task, departing from prior egocentric benchmarks (Ego4D, EgoLife, MM-Ego) that focus on agent policy or use LLM-re-annotated narrations without ground-truth goals. The closest prior work, PARSE-Ego4D [1], performs coarse-grained goal-level discriminative evaluation but ignores fine-grained parameters and does not incorporate audio, digital, or longitudinal modalities. The paper repurposes Socratic context [22] for longitudinal history representation and the LLM-as-judge paradigm [6,7] for open-set generative evaluation, but combines these into a unified four-modality benchmark that had not previously existed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals that even GPT-4.1 produces relevant goals only 55% of the time in the generative setting, highlighting a substantial gap to practical deployment on wearable devices where small/edge models are required. The finding that an LLM judge conditioned on reference goals and script cues achieves 76.8% alignment with human raters (indistinguishable from the 75.2% human-human agreement) provides a scalable, cost-effective evaluation methodology for the field. Key open questions include whether performance improves with truly naturalistic (non-scripted) interactions, whether proactive goal inference (deciding when to act, not just what to do) is feasible, and whether the low signal-to-noise ratio in digital and longitudinal modalities can be addressed through better model architectures or context filtering.
