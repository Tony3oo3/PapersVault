## Problem

Existing jailbreak attacks face significant practical limitations: optimization-based methods (GCG) require white-box access and heavy compute, LLM-guided methods (PAIR, TAP) lack strategic guidance and create unwieldy search spaces, and AutoDAN-Turbo demands resource-intensive re-initialization of its strategy library for each target model. These approaches achieve low success rates against highly aligned models (e.g., 36.6% on Llama-2-7B-Chat) and offer no unified, extensible mechanism for incorporating human red-teaming expertise into automated pipelines. The paper addresses the need for an efficient, transparent, and model-agnostic red-teaming methodology that can systematically compose and discover new attack strategies without per-model customization or gradient access.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a harmful query (from HarmBench's 400 malicious prompts), a user-defined inventory of 7 red-teaming principles (Generate, Expand, Shorten, Rephrase, Phrase Insertion, Style Change, Replace Words), and black-box API access to a target LLM. It produces a single-turn jailbreak prompt together with the explicit principle composition strategy that generated it (e.g., 'Expand ⊕ Phrase Insertion'), scored by a dual-judge system that assigns both a jailbreak-effectiveness score and a semantic-similarity score (each on a 1–10 scale) to ensure the attack remains on-topic. The Red-Teaming Agent (Grok-2) and Judge LLM (GPT-4 or GPT-4o) are plug-and-play components swappable with newer models, while the target model requires no gradient or internal-representation access.

## Method

CoP orchestrates an agentic workflow across three LLMs: a Red-Teaming Agent that designs attacks, a Target LLM under test, and a Judge LLM that evaluates responses. The core technical contribution is the Composition-of-Principles mechanism, in which the agent simultaneously selects and combines multiple human-provided principles into a single coherent strategy (denoted with ⊕), rather than applying one transformation at a time; an Initial Seed Prompt Generation phase first rewrites refusal-prone queries into intermediate prompts to overcome the 'Direct Refusal' problem of safety-aligned agents. A dual-judge evaluation scores both jailbreak effectiveness and semantic similarity to the original query, with a similarity threshold that triggers a pipeline reset if the prompt drifts off-target. Iterative refinement retains the highest-scoring prompt as the base for subsequent compositions and terminates when a success threshold η is met or a maximum iteration count is reached. Ablation confirms multi-principle composition is the cornerstone: restricting the agent to a single principle per iteration causes a 58% ASR collapse (88% → 30%), while removing the similarity judge costs 12% and removing seed generation costs 16%.

## Results

On the HarmBench dataset (400 queries), CoP achieves 71.0–77.0% Attack Success Rate (ASR) across six open-source LLMs (Llama-2-7B/13B/70B-Chat, Llama-3-8B-Chat, Llama-3-70B-Instruct, Gemma-7B-it), substantially outperforming all baselines. On proprietary models, CoP reaches 88.75% ASR on GPT-4-Turbo-1106, 78.0% on Gemini Pro 1.5, 60.0% on OpenAI O1, and 38.0% on Claude-3.5 Sonnet. In terms of query efficiency, CoP requires only 1.357–1.512 queries on average for successful jailbreaks, up to 17.2× fewer than TAP. On safety-enhanced models, CoP achieves 52% ASR on Llama-3-8B-Instruct-RR (Circuit Breaker) and 34% on Llama-2-7B-Chat + Llama-Guard-3, again outperforming all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoP consistently outperforms all seven baselines across every target model and dataset. Against the strongest single-turn baseline, AutoDAN-Turbo, CoP achieves 1.1–2.2× higher ASR (e.g., 77.0% vs. 36.6% on Llama-2-7B-Chat, 88.75% vs. 88.5% on GPT-4-Turbo-1106). Against optimization-based GCG-T, CoP gains 2.0–13.8× (e.g., 77.0% vs. 17.3% on Llama-2-7B-Chat). Against LLM-guided iterative methods PAIR and TAP, CoP achieves 4.4–9.2× higher ASR on Llama-2-7B-Chat (77.0% vs. 13.8% and 8.3% respectively). Against Rainbow Teaming, CoP leads by 31.2–47.5 percentage points on open-source models. Against the multi-turn X-Teaming method, CoP's single-turn ASR of 64.0% on Llama-2-7B-Chat exceeds X-Teaming's single-turn ASR of 4.0% by 16×, and X-Teaming requires five conversational turns to match CoP's single-turn performance. On query efficiency, CoP requires 1.357–1.512 queries on average versus 6.5–26.08 for PAIR/TAP, representing up to 17.2× fewer target-LLM queries.

## Relation to Prior Work

CoP builds on the LLM-as-attacker paradigm established by PAIR and TAP, adopting their judge-LLM prompting and similarity-assessment pipelines, and draws its principle inventory from RLBreaker's action space. However, it departs fundamentally from prior iterative-refinement methods (PAIR, TAP, AutoDAN-Turbo) by replacing undirected or library-based search with an agentic workflow that strategically composes multiple human-readable principles in a single step. The dual-judge evaluation (jailbreak score plus semantic similarity) and the Initial Seed Prompt Generation phase to circumvent the Red-Teaming Agent's own safety alignment are novel design choices not present in prior single-turn jailbreak methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CoP reveals that even the most safety-aligned frontier models (OpenAI O1, Claude-3.5 Sonnet) remain substantially vulnerable to single-turn jailbreaks, challenging the assumption that recent alignment improvements have closed the attack surface. The framework's transparency—identifying which principle compositions (e.g., Expand, Expand ⊕ Phrase Insertion) are most effective—gives defenders actionable, model-specific vulnerability insights rather than opaque adversarial suffixes. Key limitations include dependence on the quality and currency of the human-provided principle inventory, the need for multiple capable LLMs running in concert, and the restriction to single-turn attacks; extending CoP to multi-turn settings and automating principle discovery remain open directions.
