## Problem

Standard DPO and RLHF alignment assume that a static preference dataset faithfully represents real-world user preferences, but preferences shift across demographics, regions, and cultural trends, causing catastrophic alignment failures at deployment. This paper asks whether distributionally robust optimization can make DPO-based alignment resilient to such preference distribution shift, and what theoretical guarantees and tractable algorithms are possible.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250912/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a preference dataset of paired completions (prompt, preferred response, dispreferred response) generated under a nominal data-generating distribution P^o, along with a frozen reference policy (typically the SFT model). The output is a set of aligned policy parameters θ that minimize the worst-case DPO loss over an uncertainty set of distributions centered at P^o. In practice, the robustness hyperparameter ρ (for WDPO) or temperature τ (for KLDPO) controls the size of the uncertainty set and thus the degree of robustness versus in-distribution fit.

## Method

The paper formulates DPO as a distributionally robust minimax problem: minimize over policy parameters θ the supremum of the expected DPO loss over all distributions within a Wasserstein or KL ball of radius ρ around the empirical data distribution. For WDPO, leveraging the equivalence between p-Wasserstein DRO and gradient regularization (Gao et al., 2022), the tractable algorithm simply adds a gradient-norm regularizer ρ_o·(E‖∇_z l(z;θ)‖²)^{1/2} to the standard DPO loss. For KLDPO, the worst-case distribution in the KL uncertainty set is approximated by re-weighting each training sample proportionally to exp((1/τ)(l(z_i;θ) − mean loss)), so high-loss samples receive greater emphasis. Both algorithms reduce to standard gradient descent on a modified loss and integrate seamlessly into existing LLM fine-tuning pipelines. Under log-linear policies with a data-coverage assumption, the paper proves O(n^{-1/4}) convergence of the robust policy parameter for both variants, and empirically validates the approach on GPT-2, LLaMA-3.2-1B/3B, and LLaMA-3.1-8B models across emotion alignment, multi-objective ArmoRM alignment, and OpenLLM Leaderboard v2 benchmarks.

## Results

On the OpenLLM Leaderboard v2 (six tasks: IFEval, BBH, MATH, GPQA, MUSR, MMLU), KLDPO and WDPO consistently outperform standard DPO across all three model sizes (LLaMA-3.2-1B, LLaMA-3.2-3B, LLaMA-3.1-8B). The most dramatic gains appear in MATH, where KLDPO (τ=0.005) lifts LLaMA-3.1-8B from 0.03 (DPO) to 0.24, and in IFEval, where KLDPO raises LLaMA-3.2-3B from 0.55 (DPO) to 0.74. In the Emotion Alignment experiment, DPO degrades sharply as evaluation preferences shift away from the training distribution, while WDPO and KLDPO maintain stable performance across the full range of shift.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole external baseline is standard DPO (Rafailov et al., 2023), which serves as the non-robust reference in all three experimental settings. KLDPO and WDPO win on the majority of evaluation axes: on LLaMA-3.2-3B, KLDPO (τ=0.005) improves IFEval by +0.19 (0.74 vs 0.55) and MATH by +0.11 (0.19 vs 0.08) over DPO, while WDPO (ρ₀=0.005) gains +0.07 on IFEval but underperforms DPO on MATH (0.06 vs 0.08). On LLaMA-3.1-8B, KLDPO (τ=0.005) achieves a striking +0.21 gain on MATH (0.24 vs 0.03) and +0.10 on IFEval (0.72 vs 0.62), though it trades off slightly on MUSR (0.34 vs 0.44). In the Emotion Alignment experiment, DPO performs best only when evaluation preferences match training preferences; as the shift increases, DPO degrades significantly while both robust variants maintain stable performance, confirming their advantage specifically under distribution shift rather than in-distribution settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends DPO (Rafailov et al., 2023) by replacing its empirical risk minimization objective with a distributionally robust minimax objective, yielding two variants: Wasserstein DPO (WDPO) and KL DPO (KLDPO). The tractable algorithms are derived from established DRO theory: the WDPO algorithm leverages Gao et al. (2022)'s result connecting Wasserstein DRO to gradient regularization, while KLDPO uses the f-divergence dual from Duchi and Namkoong (2021) to obtain a loss-based re-weighting scheme. The paper departs from prior robust RLHF work (Bai et al., Chakraborty et al., Padmakumar et al.) that addresses topic weighting, sub-population modeling, or synthetic data augmentation, and from concurrent work by Wu et al. (2025) and Mandal et al. (2025) that apply DRO to DPO for data corruption or use TV uncertainty sets, by specifically targeting preference distribution shift and providing policy-parameter convergence guarantees (O(n^{-1/4})) rather than merely loss convergence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically critical gap: LLMs aligned on one population's preferences can fail catastrophically when deployed to users with different preference distributions, yet no prior work provided a principled DRO-based solution with finite-sample guarantees for DPO. The resulting algorithms are remarkably simple to implement (WDPO adds a gradient-norm regularizer; KLDPO re-weights samples by loss), making them drop-in replacements in existing alignment pipelines. Key limitations include the O(n^{-1/4}) convergence rate (worse than DPO's O(n^{-1/2})), the restriction to log-linear policies in the theory, and the fact that distribution shift is simulated via different reward objectives rather than genuinely diverse user populations. Open questions include achieving better robust convergence rates, extending the framework to mitigate reward hacking, and generalizing beyond DPO to other RLHF variants.
