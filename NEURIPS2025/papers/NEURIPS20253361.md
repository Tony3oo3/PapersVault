## Problem

Existing elderly speech datasets define 'elderly' at 55+ years, rely on scripted reading speech, and severely underrepresent the 75+ super-aged population (0.8% in AISHELL-ASR0060, 9.4% in MECSD), leaving speech technologies poorly calibrated for age-specific vocal characteristics such as presbyphonia, dialectal variation, and articulatory degradation. No freely available Mandarin dataset captures spontaneous conversations exclusively among individuals aged 75 and older with multi-dimensional annotations.

## Input / Output

The dataset comprises 55.53 hours of spontaneous dyadic conversations recorded on mobile devices (70% Android, 30% iOS) in 25–50 minute sessions, yielding 60,029 utterances from 202 speakers. Annotations are structured across four hierarchical levels: speaker metadata (age, gender, province), session-level temporal segmentation with overlap flags, utterance-level transcriptions with a 4-level accent intensity scale (0–3), and token-level paralinguistic markers ([LAUGHTER], [NOISE], [MUSIC], [SONANT]). Downstream experiments consume these annotations to produce speaker embeddings with PLDA/cosine similarity scores, diarization segments with DER, character-level transcriptions with CER, and text-conditioned edited speech evaluated via MCD, STOI, PESQ, and MOS.

## Method

SeniorTalk is constructed by recruiting 202 native Mandarin speakers aged 75–85 from 16 Chinese provinces and recording 101 natural conversations on elderly-relevant topics (health, pets, retirement), annotated by 14 trained annotators using a cloud-based platform with multi-tier quality assurance. For benchmarking, the authors fine-tune VoxCeleb-pretrained speaker embedding models (ECAPA-TDNN, ResNet-TDNN, x-vector) via SpeechBrain with cyclic or linear LR schedules over 20 epochs, run PyAnnote diarization with swapped speaker extractors under two collar settings, train ASR models from scratch (Transformer, Conformer, E-Branchformer) using WeNet with combined CTC+AED objectives, fine-tune Paraformer-large and five Whisper variants via whisper-flamingo, and train four speech editing architectures (CampNet, EditSpeech, A3T, FluentSpeech) for 200,000 steps—establishing the first comprehensive elderly-speech benchmark spanning verification, diarization, recognition, and editing.

## Results

On the SeniorTalk elderly speech dataset, Paraformer-large achieves the best ASR performance at 14.41% CER after fine-tuning (14.91% zero-shot), substantially outperforming Whisper-large-v3 (23.84% fine-tuned, 57.74% zero-shot). Among models trained from scratch, E-Branchformer achieves the lowest CER at 33.25%, beating Conformer (34.61%) and Transformer (48.99%). For speaker verification, fine-tuned ECAPA-TDNN achieves the best EER of 5.66% (cosine) and 8.56% (PLDA), while for speaker diarization it reaches 22.85% DER at 0.25s collar. In speech editing, FluentSpeech dominates with MOS 4.53 and Boundary-MOS 4.72, far exceeding A3T (MOS 2.04) and CampNet (MOS 1.17).

## Baselines & Comparisons

In speaker verification, ECAPA-TDNN [23] outperforms ResNet-TDNN [24] (8.56% vs. 11.39% PLDA EER) and x-vector [22] (8.56% vs. 17.53%), confirming its superiority for elderly voice biometrics. In speaker diarization, fine-tuned ECAPA-TDNN beats the PyAnnote default ResNet-34-LM [25] by 5.3% DER at 0 collar (27.84% vs. 33.14%), while x-vector [22] performs worst at 53.01% DER. For ASR from scratch, E-Branchformer [29] edges out Conformer [28] by 1.36% CER (33.25% vs. 34.61%), while Transformer lags significantly at 48.99%. Among pretrained models, Paraformer [31] (14.41% fine-tuned CER) outperforms all Whisper [33] variants by at least 9.4% CER, with Whisper-large-v3 improving from 57.74% to 23.84% after fine-tuning—still 9.4% worse than Paraformer. In speech editing, FluentSpeech [37] achieves the best MOS (4.53) and Boundary-MOS (4.72), substantially surpassing A3T [36] (MOS 2.04), EditSpeech [35] (MOS 1.82), and CampNet [34] (MOS 1.17), indicating that diffusion-based editing handles elderly vocal characteristics far more naturally than mask-prediction or partial-inference approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground in the elderly speech corpus space by addressing two specific gaps left by prior datasets like AISHELL-ASR0060 and MECSD: the near-absence of truly super-aged (75+) speakers and the reliance on scripted reading-style recordings. Rather than proposing a new model architecture, it repurposes well-established speech processing pipelines (ECAPA-TDNN, Conformer, Whisper, PyAnnote, FluentSpeech) as evaluation instruments on its newly collected conversational data. The dataset's multi-dimensional annotation scheme (speaker demographics, temporal segmentation, accent intensity, paralinguistic tokens) extends the annotation depth of prior elderly corpora, which typically provided only transcripts or pathology labels.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dataset fills a genuine and well-documented gap: existing Chinese elderly corpora contain fewer than 10% of speakers aged 75+, and none are conversational and freely available. The benchmark experiments quantify how severely current systems degrade on super-aged speech (Whisper zero-shot CER >50%, DER >27%), providing a concrete motivation for domain adaptation. However, the dataset's modest size (55.53 hours, 202 speakers), gender imbalance (1:2 female-to-male), and upper age bound of 85 limit generalizability to the full super-elderly population, and the lack of error bars or multiple random seeds weakens confidence in the comparative model rankings.
