## Problem

In weighted influence maximization, individual node gains are typically assumed to be fixed and directly observable, but in practice they suffer from two fundamental violations: (i) unobservability, where only one of the two potential outcomes (activated vs. non-activated) can be observed per node, and (ii) environmental sensitivity, where a node's gain dynamically shifts as its neighbors' activation states evolve during propagation. This paper tackles both challenges by reframing node weights as Individual Treatment Effects (ITE) estimated via causal inference under interference.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a hypergraph G(V, H, H) with incidence matrix, per-node covariates X_i, observational data D = {Y_i(t;·)} where only the factual outcome (t = T_i) is available, and a seed budget K. It produces a deterministic seed set S* of size K and per-node estimated ITE values τ̂_i that are recomputed at each propagation step to reflect the current environmental context. A key unusual aspect is that the ITE estimator's parameters θ are projected onto a bounded set Θ_M and further adjusted (θ' = θ_opt + Δ_θ) during the greedy loop to keep estimated ITEs within the theoretical bound M as the activation landscape shifts.

## Method

CauIM replaces fixed node weights with dynamically estimated ITEs, where each node's gain is computed as the difference between two potential outcomes predicted by an MLP fed with a node representation Z_i (from covariates via representation learning) and an environment representation O_i = ENV(H, T_{-i}, Z_{-i}) (from a hypergraph convolution module encoding neighbor activation states). G-CauIM performs standard greedy seed selection using Monte Carlo propagation to evaluate marginal gains, while A-CauIM accelerates this by computing activation probabilities ap(v_i; S) via a closed-form iterative formula on a bipartite node-hyperedge graph, converting them to continuous differentiable values, and selecting seeds via ∂σ/∂ap(v;S)·ap(v;S) in PyTorch—reducing complexity from O(KRnm) to O(Km·E|h|). Theoretically, the paper proves a (1−1/e) approximation guarantee when all ITEs are positive, a generalized lower bound σ(S_K^g) ≥ (1−1/e)(σ(S*)−Kε₁ε₂)−ε₂e^{1/K−1} when ITEs may be negative, and robustness bounds under estimation error |τ̂_i−τ_i|≤δ.

## Results

On four datasets (GoodReads, Contact, Email-Eu, SD-100) with K=15 seeds, G-CauIM achieves the highest ITE spread on all four: 330.25 (GoodReads), 69.53 (Contact), 804.28 (Email-Eu), and 151.59 (SD-100), outperforming the traditional Greedy baseline by 11.0%, 2.1%, 9.4%, and 8.4% respectively. A-CauIM achieves comparable quality (e.g., 802.41 on Email-Eu, 160.49 on SD-100) while reducing runtime from days to seconds (e.g., 28s vs. 1 day on GoodReads; 550s vs. 22h on Email-Eu). Both methods substantially outperform Random selection, which scores only 45.86 on GoodReads and 590.67 on Email-Eu.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two baselines: Traditional Greedy (the standard Kempe et al. greedy IM without causal weighting) and Random seed selection. G-CauIM consistently outperforms Traditional Greedy across all four datasets, with the largest gains on GoodReads (+11.0%) and Email-Eu (+9.4%), confirming that modeling environment-sensitive ITE yields meaningful improvements over fixed-weight or unweighted IM. A-CauIM matches or exceeds G-CauIM on Email-Eu (802.41 vs. 804.28) and SD-100 (160.49 vs. 151.59) while being orders of magnitude faster (28s vs. 1 day on GoodReads; 550s vs. 22h on Email-Eu), validating the gradient-based acceleration. On Contact, A-CauIM (66.78) slightly underperforms both G-CauIM (69.53) and the Baseline (68.12), suggesting the approximation may lose some signal on denser graphs. Both CauIM variants dramatically outperform Random selection on GoodReads (330.25/302.17 vs. 45.86) and Email-Eu (804.28/802.41 vs. 590.67), though Random is competitive on SD-100 (145.97 vs. 151.59/160.49), likely due to the small synthetic graph size.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of influence maximization (Kempe et al. 2003; Wang et al. 2016) and causal inference under network interference (Ma et al. 2022; Ma & Tresp 2021). It departs from weighted-IM, which assumes fixed, non-negative, context-independent node weights, by replacing those weights with dynamically estimated Individual Treatment Effects (ITE) that vary with the activation state of neighboring nodes during propagation. The ITE estimation module directly adopts the representation-learning and hypergraph convolution framework from Ma et al. [37], while the propagation mechanism follows the SICP model on hypergraphs [56]. The key conceptual departure is explicitly violating SUTVA and incorporating interference into the objective function, then proving that a generalized (1−1/e) approximation guarantee still holds even when ITE values can be negative and submodularity breaks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a practical gap that prior weighted-IM work overlooks: in real scenarios (product promotion, awareness campaigns), node-level gains are not static but shift as the social environment changes during diffusion. The robustness theorem (Theorem 4.5) showing that the approximation guarantee degrades gracefully under ITE estimation bias is practically valuable, as perfect ITE recovery is infeasible. Open questions include whether the gradient-based acceleration (A-CauIM) scales to very large hypergraphs, whether sketch-based methods can be adapted to dynamic weights, and whether the bounded-ITE assumption (Assumption 3.1) holds in domains with extreme heterogeneity. The efficiency gain from A-CauIM (days → seconds) makes the framework deployable, but the modest quality improvements over traditional greedy on some datasets suggest the causal weighting matters most in highly heterogeneous settings.
