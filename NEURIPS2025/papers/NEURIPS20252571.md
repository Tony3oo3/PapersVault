## Problem

In conventional coarse-to-fine flow matching TTS models, the FM decoder always starts generation from pure Gaussian noise, even though the weak generator already produces coarse representations encoding substantial semantic and acoustic structure. This forces the model to redundantly reconstruct the early stages of the flow, wasting modeling capacity on information already captured by the coarse output. The paper asks whether the FM process can be 'shallowed'—starting from an intermediate state on the flow path rather than from noise—to focus computation on the refinement stages that actually determine output quality.

## Input / Output

The system takes text (phonemes or semantic tokens), speaker embeddings, and optionally prompt speech tokens as input. A weak generator (encoder + length regulator) produces coarse mel-spectrograms, and a lightweight SFM head outputs three quantities: a scaled mel-spectrogram X̂_h, a predicted temporal position t̂_h ∈ (0,1), and an estimated variance σ̂_h². The FM decoder then refines the constructed intermediate state into a full mel-spectrogram, which a vocoder (Vocos or HiFi-GAN) converts to a waveform. The key unusual aspect is that the intermediate state's position on the CondOT path is not fixed but adaptively predicted per-utterance by the SFM head.

## Method

SFM constructs intermediate states on the conditional optimal transport (CondOT) paths from coarse representations using two theoretical tools. Theorem 1 provides a transformation that maps an arbitrary Gaussian state x_m ~ N(t_m·x_1, σ_m²I) onto the CondOT paths with continuous Wasserstein-2 behavior, handling the case where the state's noise level exceeds the path's residual noise by rescaling. Theorem 2 (derived from PeRFlow) splits the CondOT path into two segments at the intermediate time t_m, and SFM uses only the second segment (t ≥ t_m), so the flow and vector field are piecewise-defined over the remaining interval. During training, an orthogonal projection loss L_μ aligns X̂_h with t_h·X_1, while auxiliary losses L_t and L_σ supervise the predicted time and variance; the overall objective combines these with the standard CFM loss. At inference, a hyperparameter SFM strength α ≥ 1 scales up the intermediate state to compensate for the typically small t_h learned during training, and the ODE solver integrates only from t̃_h to 1, skipping the early noise-dominated stages. This yields more stable generation, improved naturalness, and significant speedup with adaptive-step ODE solvers (up to ~60% RTF reduction) since the higher initial signal-to-noise ratio requires fewer solver steps.

## Results

SFM consistently improves pseudo-MOS scores across all tested models and datasets. On Matcha-TTS (LJ Speech), SFM (α=2.5) achieves UTMOS 4.257, UTMOSv2 3.848, and Distill-MOS 4.386, outperforming the baseline (4.186/3.692/4.282) and ablated model (4.217/3.763/4.311). On CosyVoice (LibriTTS), SFM (α=2.0) reaches UTMOSv2 3.480 and Distill-MOS 4.541 versus the baseline's 3.303 and 4.481, while also achieving the best SMOS of 3.67. Subjective CMOS scores confirm the gains: SFM models score 0.00 (reference) while baselines score −0.21 to −0.48, with statistically significant differences (p<0.05) in most cases. With adaptive-step ODE solvers, SFM accelerates inference by up to ~61% RTF reduction (e.g., Heun(2) on Matcha-TTS: 0.157 vs. 0.401 for the ablated model at α=5.0).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares SFM against the original Matcha-TTS [30] and CosyVoice [8] baselines, as well as ablated variants that add the coarse loss and SFM head but do not apply the SFM method. Against Matcha-TTS on LJ Speech, SFM (α=2.5) improves UTMOSv2 by +0.156 (3.848 vs. 3.692) and Distill-MOS by +0.104 (4.386 vs. 4.282), while CMOS improves from −0.48 to 0.00 (reference). On VCTK, the gains are even larger: UTMOSv2 improves by +0.127 (3.105 vs. 2.978) and WER drops from 1.534% to 0.952%. Against CosyVoice on LibriTTS, SFM (α=2.0) improves UTMOSv2 by +0.177 (3.480 vs. 3.303) and Distill-MOS by +0.060 (4.541 vs. 4.481), and achieves the best SMOS (3.67 vs. 3.47), though WER is slightly worse (3.810% vs. 3.513%). The ablated models (which add the SFM head and coarse loss but start FM from pure noise) consistently underperform the full SFM method, confirming that the intermediate-state construction is the key contribution rather than the added head alone. The SFM-c variant (which additionally uses coarse mel-spectrograms as FM conditions) performs worse than SFM in subjective evaluations, indicating that the coarse representations are better exploited as starting points than as conditioning signals.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends DiffSinger's shallow diffusion mechanism (which started diffusion from a decoder's coarse output) to the flow matching setting, formalizing it with two theorems that guarantee the constructed intermediate state lies on the CondOT path and that the resulting piecewise flow is consistent with the original vector field. It draws on PeRFlow's piecewise reflow over divided CondOT windows and rectified flow's OT displacement interpolant, but departs from both by using only the latter segment of a two-segment piecewise flow rather than retraining on each window. The approach also contrasts with shortcut models (which use self-consistency to build shortcuts) and modifying flow matching (which samples from a Gaussian centered at a coarse output), as SFM explicitly projects the coarse representation onto the CondOT path via an orthogonal projection and adaptively determines its temporal position.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SFM provides a lightweight, plug-and-play mechanism that consistently improves speech naturalness across both U-Net and DiT-based TTS architectures while significantly accelerating inference with adaptive-step ODE solvers (up to ~60% RTF reduction). A notable negative finding is that using coarse mel-spectrograms as additional FM conditions (SFM-c) degrades quality or renders the method inapplicable, suggesting that the intermediate-state construction itself is the key mechanism rather than extra conditioning. Open questions include whether the framework transfers to image generation or speech enhancement, whether more powerful SFM heads or explicit semantic conditioning in the FM process would further improve alignment, and how the method behaves when the weak generator is highly unstable.
