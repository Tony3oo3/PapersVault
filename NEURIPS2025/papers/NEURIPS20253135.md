## Problem

Existing generative recommendation methods (e.g., TIGER) rely solely on discrete sparse semantic IDs, which suffer from significant information loss due to the separation of quantization and sequence modeling stages, limiting fine-grained similarity modeling compared to sequential dense retrieval. Integrating the efficiency and generalization of generative retrieval with the precision of dense retrieval remains an open challenge.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253135/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a user interaction sequence where each item is encoded as a cascaded pair: a sparse semantic ID (produced by RQ-VAE quantization of item text attributes, e.g., 3-level with codebook size 32) and a dense vector (produced by a trainable Transformer text encoder over the same attributes). At inference, the system first beam-searches M sparse IDs, then generates M conditioned dense vectors, performs ANN retrieval within each ID's candidate pool, and outputs top-K items ranked by a fused BeamFusion score that combines beam-search confidence with cosine similarity.

## Method

COBRA factorizes the target-item distribution as P(ID|S)·P(v|ID,S) rather than the independent P(ID|S)·P(v|S), a cascaded formulation proven to yield lower joint entropy (Theorem 1). A single Transformer decoder processes the interleaved sequence of [ID-embedding; dense-vector] pairs and alternates between a sparse classification head (cross-entropy loss) and a dense regression head (in-batch contrastive loss with cosine similarity), trained end-to-end so dense vectors are dynamically refined rather than static. At inference, a coarse-to-fine pipeline first beam-searches sparse IDs, appends the winning ID embedding back into the decoder input to generate a conditioned dense vector, retrieves candidates via ANN within that ID's bucket, and ranks them with BeamFusion—a softmax-weighted product of beam scores and cosine similarities that provides tunable precision-diversity control via coefficients τ and ψ.

## Results

On the Amazon Beauty dataset, COBRA achieves Recall@5 of 0.0537 and Recall@10 of 0.0725, surpassing the strongest generative baseline TIGER by 18.3% and 11.9% respectively. On Sports and Outdoors, COBRA records Recall@5 of 0.0305 and NDCG@5 of 0.0215, outperforming TIGER by 15.5% and 18.8%. On Toys and Games, COBRA attains Recall@10 of 0.0781 and NDCG@10 of 0.0515, exceeding TIGER by 24.5% and 19.2%. On the industrial dataset (5M users, 2M ads), COBRA achieves Recall@500 of 0.3716, a 42.2% improvement over its w/o Dense variant.

## Baselines & Comparisons

COBRA outperforms all nine baselines across every metric on all three public datasets. The closest competitor is TIGER (the generative retrieval baseline using RQ-VAE semantic IDs), which COBRA surpasses by 11.9%–24.5% on Recall and 18.8%–19.2% on NDCG depending on the dataset. Among dense retrieval baselines, SASRec and S³-Rec are the strongest, but COBRA still leads them by 18%–39% on Recall@5 across datasets. The LLM-based P5 baseline and the CNN-based Caser are consistently the weakest, trailing COBRA by over 3× on most metrics. On the industrial dataset, COBRA's advantage over its own ablated variants (w/o ID, w/o Dense, w/o BeamFusion) ranges from 26.7% to 48.3% in Recall, confirming that the full cascaded architecture is necessary for competitive performance at scale.

## Relation to Prior Work

COBRA extends the generative retrieval paradigm introduced by TIGER (Rajput et al., NeurIPS 2023), which encodes items into hierarchical semantic IDs via RQ-VAE and generates the next item's ID with a Transformer decoder. The key departure is the introduction of a cascaded sparse-dense representation: rather than relying solely on discrete IDs (as in TIGER, LC-Rec, ColaRec) or treating generative and dense retrieval as parallel streams (as in LIGER), COBRA factorizes the joint distribution so that the sparse ID conditions the subsequent dense vector generation. This is supported by an information-theoretic argument (Theorem 1) showing the cascaded factorization yields lower joint entropy than independent modeling, and by a coarse-to-fine inference procedure with a novel BeamFusion scoring mechanism that blends beam-search confidence with ANN similarity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a recognized gap: generative retrieval methods lose fine-grained discriminative power compared to dense retrieval, while dense retrieval lacks the efficiency and generalization benefits of discrete ID generation. COBRA's cascaded design offers a principled way to combine both, and the BeamFusion mechanism provides a practical knob for the recall-diversity tradeoff that is valuable in production systems. Limitations include text-only modality, no released code, absence of error bars or statistical significance tests, and the theoretical result (Theorem 1) being a standard entropy decomposition rather than a deep new insight. Open questions include whether the cascaded approach extends to multi-modal item representations, how it scales to much larger codebooks, and whether the end-to-end dense encoder can be replaced by frozen pretrained embeddings without significant degradation.
