## Problem

LLM-powered experimentation generates many content variants, but treating each as a discrete A/B test arm leads to severely underpowered experiments that ignore semantic similarity between variants. The paper asks how to leverage treatment embeddings to pool information across semantically similar arms for more efficient causal effect estimation, and how to extend this to adaptive online treatment assignment.

## Input / Output

The method consumes treatment embeddings (e.g., 384-dimensional MiniLM sentence vectors for headlines or knowledge-graph embeddings for news items), user covariate vectors (e.g., 200-dimensional preference features), and scalar outcome responses (clicks, CTR, revenue). It produces a low-rank outcome response matrix Γ̂ ∈ ℝ^{d₁×d₂} capturing treatment-user interactions, from which the CATE τ(z,x) is read off as zᵀΘ̂x, along with interpretable low-dimensional kernel representations of treatments and users. The adaptive variant outputs a sequential treatment-assignment policy for online experimentation.

## Method

The core contribution is Double Kernel Representation Learning (DKRL), which factorizes the treatment effect as τ(z,x) = g(z)ᵀh(x) with g and h living in separate RKHS spaces, rather than factorizing the full outcome as in prior work. After Robinson residualization, the estimation problem becomes a double RKHS regression solved by alternating minimization: fixing one set of representation coefficients, the other is updated via a closed-form ridge regression with a kernel-matrix penalty. A representer theorem reduces the infinite-dimensional problem to finite-dimensional matrix updates (U, V ∈ ℝ^{n×r}), and the alternating scheme is guaranteed to monotonically decrease the objective. Under a fixed-basis low-rank factor model, the paper proves a Frobenius-norm convergence rate of O(λₙ²r) for the response matrix and a sublinear regret bound of O(T^{2/3} d log^{1/3} d r^{1/3}) for an explore-then-commit bandit strategy, connecting the kernel formulation to both kernelized probabilistic matrix factorization and nuclear-norm-regularized low-rank matrix completion.

## Results

On the Upworthy semi-synthetic experiment, DKRL achieves the lowest test error across all ranks: 0.0036 (rank 2), 0.0048 (rank 3), 0.0078 (rank 5), and 0.0108 (rank 7), outperforming SIN by 3–60% and ProdKernel by 55–20%. DKRL is also substantially faster than SIN (e.g., 0.19s vs. 8.47s at rank 2) while ProdKernel is fastest (0.0014s) but less accurate. On the MIND dataset, DKRL achieves test MSE of 0.003 at low-rank settings (q=2,4,6), roughly 2–4× better than LASSO, XGBoost, and FNN baselines, and 0.007 at full rank (q=0) versus 0.031–0.036 for the other methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SIN (Kaddour et al., 2021), the deep-learning-based Structured Intervention Network, DKRL consistently achieves lower test error at every rank tested (e.g., 0.0036 vs. 0.0104 at rank 2; 0.0108 vs. 0.0172 at rank 7) while running 40–45× faster. The authors attribute SIN's inferiority to its difficulty learning the low-rank representation and constructing propensity functions for high-dimensional treatments. Against ProdKernel (RKHS regression with a product kernel), DKRL also wins on test error at all ranks (e.g., 0.0036 vs. 0.0080 at rank 2), though ProdKernel is orders of magnitude faster (0.0014s vs. 0.19s). On the MIND dataset, DKRL (test MSE 0.003 at q=2) outperforms LASSO (0.009), XGBoost (0.007), FNN (0.011), and Kernel regression (0.006) by 2–4×, and at full rank (q=0) DKRL achieves 0.007 versus 0.031–0.036 for all other methods, demonstrating that the double-kernel structure most effectively exploits low-rank and embedding-similarity structure.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Robinson decomposition / R-learner framework of Nie and Wager (2021) from binary treatments to high-dimensional, semantically structured treatments by factorizing the CATE (rather than the full outcome function as in Kaddour et al., 2021) as an inner product of kernel-based representations of treatment embeddings and user covariates. The resulting double-kernel optimization is shown to be equivalent to the Gaussian-process formulation of kernelized probabilistic matrix factorization (Zhou et al., 2012) and to a nuclear-norm-regularized low-rank matrix recovery problem when kernels are linear, unifying these threads under a single alternating-minimization algorithm. The adaptive experimentation component draws on low-rank bandit and kernel bandit literature but applies the explore-then-commit strategy to the specific setting of treatment-user interaction matrices learned from semantic embeddings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As LLMs make it trivial to generate hundreds or thousands of content variants, the statistical bottleneck shifts from content creation to efficient experimentation; this paper provides a principled framework for pooling information across semantically similar treatments to recover CATE estimates with far fewer observations than discrete-arm A/B testing would require. The theoretical guarantees (minimax-optimal matrix recovery rates, sublinear regret) are established only in a fixed-basis setting, and the experiments are semi-synthetic, leaving open whether the approach transfers cleanly to fully observational or interference-prone field settings. Broader questions about privacy, fairness, and the quality of off-the-shelf embeddings (e.g., MiniLM) as treatment representations remain unaddressed.
