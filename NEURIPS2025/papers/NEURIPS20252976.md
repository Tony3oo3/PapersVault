## Problem

Conformal prediction for stream networks faces two fundamental obstacles: directional flow constraints violate the exchangeability assumption underlying standard CP, and joint multivariate prediction across interdependent sites requires capturing spatial dependencies that purely data-driven covariance estimates handle poorly with limited calibration samples. No prior work has tailored conformal prediction to exploit the inherent topological structure of stream networks, leaving a gap between the rich spatial dependencies these networks exhibit and the prediction sets that can be constructed.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a trained prediction model f̂ (e.g., AGCRN, ASTGCN, or STGODE), the stream network topology G (segments, flow directions, hydrologic distances, and flow-intensity weights), and a calibration set of the most recent n multivariate observations (n=500 by default). It produces a joint ellipsoidal prediction set in R^I for the next time step, defined by a Mahalanobis-distance threshold, along with an adaptively updated confidence level α_t. The method is designed for moderate-size subgraphs (2–30 sites) where explicit geometric sets remain informative, avoiding the curse of dimensionality that renders full-graph joint sets vacuous.

## Method

STACI constructs a topology-aware nonconformity score s(X_t, Y_t) = ε̂_t^T A ε̂_t where the matrix A is a convex combination A = (1−λ)Σ̂_n^{−1} + λΣ̂_G^{−1}, blending the sample covariance inverse with a topology-induced covariance inverse. The topology-induced covariance Σ̂_G is derived from a tail-up model with an exponential moving-average kernel, yielding Σ_G(u,v) = σ²√(w(u)/w(v))·exp(−d(u,v)/φ) for flow-connected location pairs and zero otherwise, with only two parameters (φ, σ²) estimated via ℓ₁-loss. The weighting parameter λ trades off structural stability (topology, fewer parameters, robust to distribution shift) against data-driven fidelity (sample covariance, captures actual patterns but unstable in finite samples). An Adaptive Conformal Inference (ACI) loop then dynamically updates α_t at each test step to maintain valid average coverage under temporal distribution shifts. Theoretically, the paper proves conditional coverage validity under stationarity (Theorem 1) and shows that the volume-minimizing A corresponds to the inverse error covariance (Theorem 2), justifying the hybrid estimation strategy.

## Results

On the PeMS-G1 and PeMS-G2 traffic datasets (12 sensors each) with the default AGCRN backbone, STACI(γ=0.01) achieves 95.75% and 95.01% coverage respectively, meeting the 95% target, while attaining the lowest prediction-set volume (efficiency) of 67.54 and 69.62 among all methods that satisfy the coverage requirement. The strongest baseline, MultiDimSPCI, reports lower efficiency (73.82 and 101.42) but fails to reach the 95% coverage threshold (92.92% and 91.14%). Spherical and square baselines that do meet coverage are roughly 2× less efficient (e.g., Sphere: 133.60; Square: 155.84 on PeMS-G1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MultiDimSPCI [50] (ellipsoidal CP using only the sample covariance), STACI wins decisively on coverage: 95.75% vs. 92.92% on PeMS-G1 and 95.01% vs. 91.14% on PeMS-G2, while also achieving lower efficiency (67.54 vs. 73.82; 69.62 vs. 101.42) among methods that meet the 95% target. Against CopulaCPTS [39] (copula-based joint prediction regions), STACI is dramatically superior, achieving 95.75% coverage versus 70.60% and efficiency of 67.54 versus 668.00 on PeMS-G1, indicating that copula-based joint CDF estimation is highly unstable in the 12-dimensional setting. Against DeepSTUQ [33] (Bayesian GNN with Monte Carlo dropout), STACI achieves higher coverage (95.75% vs. 93.82%) with nearly identical efficiency (67.54 vs. 66.51), and unlike DeepSTUQ's marginal intervals aggregated into a hyper-rectangle, STACI provides a formal joint coverage guarantee. Across all three backbone GNNs (AGCRN, ASTGCN, STGODE) and both datasets, STACI consistently outperforms every baseline on the coverage–efficiency Pareto front.

## Relation to Prior Work

STACI extends the split conformal prediction framework (Vovk et al., 2005) and Adaptive Conformal Inference (Gibbs & Candès, 2021) to the specific setting of stream networks, where observations follow directional flow constraints. Its key departure from MultiDimSPCI (Xu et al., 2024), which constructs ellipsoidal prediction sets using only the sample covariance matrix, is the introduction of a topology-induced covariance derived from the tail-up model (Ver Hoef & Peterson, 2010), blended with the sample estimate via a weighting parameter λ. Prior graph-based CP methods target node/edge classification on static graphs, while prior multi-dimensional CP methods assume exchangeability or ignore spatial structure; STACI is the first to incorporate stream network topology directly into the nonconformity score for joint spatio-temporal forecasting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first principled CP framework for stream networks, showing that a simple two-parameter topology model (exponential decay with hydrologic distance) acts as an effective regularizer for covariance estimation, lifting coverage from under 87% to above 95% even without adaptive calibration. This is practically important for safety-critical applications like traffic management and flood risk assessment where joint coverage guarantees across multiple sensors are needed. Key limitations include restriction to moderate-size subgraphs (2–30 dimensions) due to the curse of dimensionality, reliance on a fixed flow direction, and the lack of finite-sample coverage guarantees under adaptive calibration (γ > 0). Open questions include extending the topology parameterization to general spatio-temporal graphs, developing corridor-level joint sets for larger networks, and establishing stronger theoretical guarantees under distribution shift.
