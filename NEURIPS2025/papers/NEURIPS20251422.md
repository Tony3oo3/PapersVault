## Problem

Existing biomedical segmentation models either support a single fixed protocol defined at training time or require labor-intensive manual prompting (clicks, scribbles, example masks) to specify the desired segmentation. No method can automatically produce multiple plausible, semantically consistent multi-label segmentation protocols for a previously unseen biomedical image collection without any user-specified protocol, leaving researchers without a way to rapidly explore diverse anatomical parcellations for new tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251422/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Pancakes takes a set of S (typically 2–5) 2D biomedical images from the same domain as input and produces M (typically 5–15) complete multi-label segmentation maps, each containing up to K (typically 5–40) non-overlapping labels that partition the entire image. Within each protocol, a given label index refers to the same anatomical structure across all images in the set, enabling cross-subject quantitative analysis. The model is fully convolutional with only 0.22M parameters, producing all M label maps in a single forward pass (~0.1 s per image) by reusing a shared distribution-parameter representation.

## Method

A UNet backbone f_θf maps each input image to per-pixel distribution parameters φ encoding the likelihood of different labels across protocols. A novel sampling mechanism then generates diverse protocols: a random integer r_m = (M, K) is converted into positional-embedding-like vectors v_m (using sinusoidal frequency-scaled encodings for protocol ID and label ID), concatenated with φ, and passed through a shallow fully-convolutional decoder h_θh with SoftMax over the K dimension to yield complete, non-overlapping label maps. The training loss uses a min-Dice formulation over all M×K candidate labels, averaged across the image set, which simultaneously encourages diversity (at least one protocol must match the ground-truth binary label) and cross-image semantic consistency (the same label must denote the same region in every image). The model is trained on the diverse Megamedical collection plus 120K synthetic Anatomix pairs, with in-task and task-level augmentations to broaden protocol coverage, and generalizes to seven held-out biomedical datasets spanning CT, MRI, ultrasound, X-ray, and fundus imaging.

## Results

Pancakes outperforms all four baselines on all seven held-out datasets (QUBIQ Prostate, SCD, WBC, BUID, LIDC-IDRI, DDTI, STARE), often by a margin of more than 20 Set Dice points. At set size S=1 (accuracy only), Pancakes is comparable to SAM and superior to ScribblePrompt, MedSAM, and UnSAM; as set size increases to S=3 and S=5, Pancakes maintains steady Set Dice while all baselines degrade substantially due to inconsistent label assignments across images. Pancakes achieves this with only 0.22M parameters and an inference time of 0.10 s (S=1) / 0.12 s (S=3), making it ~30× faster than SAM (3.13 s) and ~20× faster than ScribblePrompt and MedSAM.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Pancakes is compared against four foundation segmentation models: SAM, ScribblePrompt (SP), MedSAM, and UnSAM. On the primary Set Dice metric (which jointly measures accuracy and cross-image consistency), Pancakes wins on all seven held-out datasets against every baseline, with margins exceeding 20 Dice points in several cases. At S=1 (pure accuracy, no consistency penalty), Pancakes is roughly on par with SAM and clearly ahead of SP, MedSAM, and UnSAM; the authors attribute SAM's competitive S=1 performance to its broader training data reducing task-overfitting. As set size grows (S=3, S=5), all four baselines suffer significant Set Dice drops because their per-image label assignments are semantically inconsistent, whereas Pancakes' performance remains stable. In terms of efficiency, Pancakes (0.22M params, 0.10 s) is an order of magnitude leaner and faster than SAM (641M, 3.13 s), SP (93.7M, 1.99 s), and MedSAM (93.7M, 2.12 s), and roughly 5× faster than the lightest baseline UnSAM (23M, 0.54 s). The paper also demonstrates that Pancakes' outputs can serve as initializations for interactive segmentation (ScribblePrompt), reducing required clicks from 5–8 to 3–4 while gaining ~5 Dice points per click.

## Relation to Prior Work

Pancakes carves new ground by repurposing positional-embedding concepts (from Transformers) as a protocol/label indexing mechanism within a lightweight UNet, combined with a min-Dice loss borrowed from stochastic in-context segmentation (Tyche). It departs from the dominant paradigm of universal segmentation (SAM, MedSAM, ScribblePrompt, UnSAM), which requires user-specified prompts or training pairs to define a protocol, by instead estimating a distribution over multiple plausible protocols and sampling from it. The multi-protocol consistency requirement distinguishes it from hierarchical segmentation methods (which enforce fixed taxonomies) and from single-protocol models that cannot generalize to unseen label sets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This addresses a substantial practical bottleneck in biomedical research: the need to segment new image collections under protocols for which no annotated training data or existing tool exists, while maintaining label-to-structure correspondence across subjects for population or longitudinal analysis. Key limitations include the 2D-only formulation, the assumption that users can map labels to known structures, and the absence of structural constraints (e.g., left-right symmetry) across protocols. Open questions include extending to 3D volumes, enforcing inter-protocol consistency, and evaluating for demographic biases in the generated segmentations.
