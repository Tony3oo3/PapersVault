## Problem

Weakly supervised cross-view localization requires estimating a ground camera's pose from a satellite image using only noisy location annotations, but existing Bird's-Eye View (BEV) synthesis methods face a fundamental height ambiguity: a single 2D pixel can correspond to points at many depths and elevations. Prior approaches either assume a flat ground plane (Inverse Perspective Mapping), which distorts and discards objects above the ground, or rely on computationally expensive cross-view transformers that struggle to converge under weak supervision.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255235/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single ground-level image (pinhole for KITTI, 360° panoramic for VIGOR) and a corresponding satellite image patch (~100×100 m² or 70×70 m²), along with a noisy initial camera location (up to 20 m error). It produces a refined 3-DoF relative pose (position and orientation) by generating a 32×128×128 BEV feature map from the ground image and computing a location probability map via cosine similarity against the satellite feature map within a sliding search window (~56×56 m² on KITTI). A per-pixel confidence map, learned to down-weight dynamic objects like vehicles, modulates the BEV features during matching.

## Method

BevSplat lifts each ground-image pixel into 3D using a pre-trained monocular depth model (DepthAnythingV2 for pinhole, UniK3D for panoramic), then a ResNet+MLP predicts Np=3 anisotropic Gaussian primitives per pixel with learned positional offsets, scale, rotation quaternion, and opacity. A DINOv2 backbone (FiT-initialized) with a DPT head extracts 32-dim semantic features and a confidence score per pixel, which are bound to all Gaussians originating from that pixel. These feature Gaussians are rendered into a BEV feature map via differentiable α-blending sorted by depth, naturally handling occlusion without the flat-ground assumption. The BEV features are matched against satellite features extracted by an identical DINOv2-DPT backbone using confidence-weighted cosine similarity, producing a location probability map optimized with a weakly supervised metric learning loss (and an optional GPS auxiliary loss). The key distinction from prior BEV synthesis is that the learned Gaussian parameters (opacity, scale, position) are end-to-end differentiable and compensate for depth estimation errors, while the α-blending resolves vertical occlusions that degrade both IPM and naive point-cloud projection.

## Results

On KITTI Cross-Area (the harder generalization setting), BevSplat with GPS labels (λ=1) achieves 6.20 m mean and 2.51 m median localization error, outperforming the best supervised method HC-Net (8.47 m / 4.57 m) and the weakly supervised baseline G2SWeakly (12.15 m / 7.16 m). On VIGOR Cross-Area with aligned orientation, BevSplat (λ=1) reaches 2.84 m mean / 1.36 m median, surpassing all supervised baselines (best: HC-Net at 3.35 m / 1.59 m). In Same-Area settings, BevSplat (λ=1) achieves 2.87 m / 2.06 m on KITTI and 2.87 m / 1.58 m on VIGOR, approaching or matching the best supervised results while using only noisy GPS supervision.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the direct weakly supervised baseline G2SWeakly [1], BevSplat reduces mean localization error by 54% on KITTI Same-Area (2.87 m vs. 6.81 m, λ=1) and by 49% on KITTI Cross-Area (6.20 m vs. 12.15 m), with similar gains on VIGOR (2.84 m vs. 4.70 m Cross-Area). In the Cross-Area setting on KITTI, BevSplat (λ=1) outperforms all five supervised baselines—Boosting (12.58 m), VFA (11.12 m), CCVPE (9.16 m), HC-Net (8.47 m), and DenseFlow (7.97 m)—in mean error, demonstrating that weakly supervised learning with Gaussian-based BEV synthesis can surpass fully supervised methods on unseen geography. On VIGOR Cross-Area, BevSplat (λ=1) beats CCVPE (4.97 m), DenseFlow (5.01 m), and HC-Net (3.35 m) in mean error while matching or exceeding them in median error. The supervised methods HC-Net and CCVPE remain stronger in KITTI Same-Area (0.80 m and 1.22 m vs. 2.87 m), but this gap narrows substantially in Cross-Area where their reliance on precise GT poses becomes a liability. Orientation estimation is identical across all weakly supervised methods (0.33° mean) since it is inherited from G2SWeakly's orientation network; the gains are entirely in positional accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the weakly supervised cross-view localization framework of G2SWeakly by replacing its Inverse Perspective Mapping (IPM) BEV synthesis with feature-based 3D Gaussian primitives rendered via differentiable alpha-blending. Conceptually, it repurposes the 3D Gaussian Splatting rendering pipeline (originally for novel view synthesis) and the feed-forward Gaussian generation paradigm from single-view reconstruction works (Feature 3DGS, LatentSplat, Flash3D) into a localization context, binding DINOv2-DPT semantic features to each Gaussian instead of RGB values. The key departure from prior BEV synthesis methods (IPM, Lift-Splat-Shoot, OrienterNet) is the use of anisotropic, opacity-controlled Gaussian splats that handle height ambiguity and vertical occlusions without assuming a flat ground plane or requiring cross-view transformer attention.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a lightweight, weakly supervised Gaussian-splatting pipeline can match or exceed fully supervised cross-view localization methods in cross-area generalization while using substantially less GPU memory (9.2 GB vs. 22.7 GB training), making the approach more accessible for real-world deployment where precise GPS annotations are unavailable. The main open limitation is inference speed, constrained by the 3DGS rendering overhead, which the authors flag as the primary target for future work (faster reconstruction, more compact representations). Broader implications include the potential transfer of feature-Gaussian rendering to other camera-only 3D perception tasks where multi-sensor depth is unavailable, and the validation that DPT-style multi-scale fine-tuning of foundation models is critical for bridging the ground-satellite domain gap in a way that simpler adapters (LoRA, direct training) cannot.
