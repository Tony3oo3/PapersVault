## Problem

Existing GNN methods for polymer informatics model only a single repeating unit (monomer) and fail to produce consistent vector representations when the number of repeating units varies, causing embeddings to drift with chain length. This paper tackles the challenge of learning graph representations that are invariant to the number of repeating units while preserving the chemical semantics of the underlying structure.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252957/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes polymer molecular graphs constructed by chaining n copies of a repeating unit (RU) via two anchor vertices (polymerization points) that form inter-monomer edges, and produces regression predictions for six polymer properties (glass transition temperature, density, melting temperature, oxygen permeability, electron affinity, ionization potential). A key constraint is that all repeat-size variants of the same polymer share an identical ground-truth property value, so the model must map graphs of 1 to 60 RUs to the same latent vector. Training uses a minimal augmentation pair of {1-RU, 3-RU} graphs at a 1:1 merge ratio.

## Method

GRIN employs a max-aggregation GNN with an L1 sparsity penalty on message and update MLPs, formally aligning each message-passing layer with one iteration of Prim's maximum-spanning-tree algorithm so that only the strongest (MST-relevant) edge survives at each node. A repeat-unit augmentation strategy chains RUs with anchor vertices to generate multi-repeat graph instances, and the paper proves that three RUs is the minimal augmentation size: it is the smallest configuration containing a hyperdegree-2 supernode, which provides the dual-branch gradient supervision needed for true multi-branch fusion, while larger repeats yield only geometrically vanishing improvements under the L-Lipschitz contraction assumption. The theoretical analysis provides both model-wise guarantees (inheriting DP size-generalization from MST alignment) and data-wise guarantees (latent repetition-invariance under the polymer hyperchain abstraction), and experiments on four homopolymer and two copolymer datasets confirm near-constant accuracy across 60× repeat-size extrapolation.

## Results

On four homopolymer datasets (GlassTemp, MeltingTemp, PolyDensity, O2Perm) and two copolymer datasets (EA, IP), GRIN consistently achieves the highest R² and lowest RMSE across all repeat sizes (1 to 60 RUs for homopolymers, 1 to 20 for copolymers). Headline improvements include a 15% R² gain on PolyDensity (0.747 vs. 0.631 for BFGCN at Test60) and a 24–35% RMSE reduction on copolymer IP (0.096 vs. 0.145 for BFGCN at Test20). Critically, GRIN's performance gap between Test1 and Test60 stays within ±3% R², confirming repetition-invariant representations, while most baselines collapse to negative R² at 60 repeats.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GRIN outperforms all 11 baselines across every dataset and repeat size. The strongest baseline, BFGCN (Bellman-Ford-aligned GNN), is the only method that partially survives 60× extrapolation, but GRIN still leads by 1–2% R² on Test1 and by over 10% R² on Test60 (e.g., GlassTemp: 0.893 vs. 0.850; MeltingTemp: 0.746 vs. 0.683). DISGEN (disentangled size-generalization) is competitive on GlassTemp Test1 (0.885) but degrades sharply on PolyDensity (R²=0.189 at Test60) and O2Perm (negative R²), where its GNN-Explainer-based augmentation misprioritizes nodes. All environment-based OOD methods (DIR, GREA, IRM, RPGNN, SSR) collapse entirely at 60 repeats, producing negative R² and RMSE >100, confirming they do not address the repeat-size extrapolation challenge. On copolymer tasks, GRIN reduces IP RMSE by 24–35% and EA RMSE by 14–30% relative to BFGCN at Test20. Attention-based models (GAT, GraphGPS) also underperform GRIN, with GAT achieving only 0.579 R² on MeltingTemp Test60 versus GRIN's 0.746.

## Relation to Prior Work

GRIN extends the neural algorithmic alignment paradigm (Dudzik & Veličković 2022; Nerem et al. 2025) from shortest-path/Bellman-Ford alignment to maximum-spanning-tree (Prim's algorithm) alignment, specifically tailored to the one-dimensional chain structure of polymers. It departs from prior size-generalization methods (SSR, DISGEN) that handle arbitrary graph scale changes by exploiting the regular repeating-unit structure unique to polymers, and from prior polymer GNNs (GREA, Aldeghi & Coley) that model only a single monomer graph and ignore inter-monomer connectivity. The repeat-unit augmentation strategy, combined with the theoretical hyperchain contraction, positions GRIN as the first method to explicitly enforce invariance to repeat count rather than merely improving robustness to graph size.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GRIN provides the first theoretically grounded method that produces near-perfect repetition-invariant polymer embeddings (cosine similarity 0.999 between 1-RU and 60-RU), enabling property prediction for polymers of arbitrary chain length from models trained on just 1- and 3-RU graphs. The proof that three repeating units constitute the minimal augmentation for optimal invariance is a practically useful and clean result. Key limitations include restriction to linear chains (no branched or cross-linked architectures), copolymer evaluation limited to block and alternating motifs (no random copolymers), and a modest increase in training cost from augmentation. Open questions include whether more sophisticated DP alignments (beyond MST) can capture intra-monomer interactions in complex topologies, and whether the framework extends to multi-property or generative polymer design tasks.
