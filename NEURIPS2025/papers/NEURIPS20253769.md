## Problem

When distinct neural models are trained on similar data, their latent spaces can be aligned, but prior relative-representation methods rely on cosine similarity, which only captures angle-preserving (orthogonal) transformations. The paper asks whether a richer geometric structure—specifically the intrinsic Riemannian geometry of the data manifold—can yield representations invariant to the broader class of isometric and reparametrization transformations that actually relate different models' latent spaces.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253769/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Given a sample x, the method encodes it to z = E(x), then for each anchor a in a fixed set computes the discretized arc length (or energy) of the straight line between z and E(a) in latent space, evaluating the pullback metric G_Z at N intermediate points via the decoder D. The output is a single vector of length |anchors| whose entries are these geodesic distances, which can be compared across two models to recover a full correspondence Λ from a partial one Γ. For discriminative models the decoder is a lightweight head (classification or Diet instance-discrimination) trained on top of frozen pretrained encoder features, so the geodesic computation requires only O(N·|anchors|) forward passes of a small network.

## Method

The key insight is that different models trained on similar data approximate parametrizations of the same underlying manifold, so the pullback metric from the output space to the latent space defines a Riemannian structure whose geodesic distances are invariant to isometries between the two models' latent spaces. Rather than solving the expensive geodesic minimization, the paper approximates the geodesic by the straight line (convex combination) between two latent points and computes its discretized arc length or energy using finite differences of the decoder, yielding a provable upper bound on the true geodesic distance with Spearman rank correlation >0.99 to the exact geodesic. For generative models (autoencoders) the L2 metric is pulled back from the reconstruction space; for discriminative models the paper introduces two variants—RelGeo(L2) pulling back the Euclidean metric from classifier logits, and RelGeo(Diet) pulling back the spherical metric from a Diet instance-discrimination decoder, the latter providing instance-level identifiability. The resulting relative geodesic representation is invariant to reparametrizations of the manifold (Proposition 3.1) and to isometric transformations between models, enabling zero-shot retrieval and stitching without training any adapter.

## Results

On retrieval (MRR cosine), RelGeo(Diet) achieves 0.566 on ImageNet-1k, 0.523 on CUB, and 0.445 on CIFAR-100, substantially outperforming the cosine-similarity baseline (0.221, 0.135, 0.166 respectively). On zero-shot stitching (accuracy), RelGeo(L2) reaches 0.955 on CIFAR-10 and 0.874 on CIFAR-100, surpassing Rel(Cosine) (0.907, 0.775). On autoencoder retrieval, relative geodesics saturate MRR near 1.0 with only 5–10 anchors, while the cosine baseline plateaus at lower values with higher variance across anchor sets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary baseline Rel(Cosine) from Moschella et al. [2023], RelGeo(Diet) wins decisively on retrieval across all five datasets, e.g. 0.566 vs 0.221 MRR on ImageNet-1k and 0.523 vs 0.135 on CUB (roughly 2.5–4× improvement). On stitching accuracy, RelGeo(L2) outperforms Rel(Cosine) on CIFAR-10 (0.955 vs 0.907), CIFAR-100 (0.874 vs 0.775), CUB (0.595 vs 0.531), and SVHN (0.59 vs 0.384), though it trails slightly on ImageNet-1k (0.501 vs 0.549). Against Lähner and Moeller [2024] (direct latent-space alignment) and Maiorca et al. [2024] (semantic alignment), the comparison is qualitative on autoencoder stitching (Figure 4): the proposed method produces near-perfect reconstructions using only 5 anchors, while both baselines yield visibly degraded outputs. Overall, the pullback-metric approach consistently dominates the cosine-similarity baseline on instance-level retrieval, while the L2-pullback variant is strongest for task-level stitching accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the relative representations framework of Moschella et al. (2023), which used cosine similarity to achieve invariance to angle-preserving transformations, by replacing it with geodesic distances computed via pullback Riemannian metrics. The pullback metric and geodesic energy machinery is inherited from the autoencoder geometry literature (Arvanitidis et al., 2018; Shao et al., 2018), but the key departure is extending this framework to discriminative models through two novel decoder constructions: a classifier head pulling back the Euclidean L2 metric, and a Diet instance-discrimination decoder pulling back the spherical metric. The geometric invariance argument (isometries rather than orthogonal maps) provides a principled justification for why the method should be more robust than cosine-based relative representations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean differential-geometric lens on why independently trained models converge to similar representations, grounding the 'same manifold' intuition in Riemannian invariance. The practical finding that straight-line arc-length approximations of geodesics achieve near-perfect rank correlation (Spearman > 0.99) with only 8 discretization steps makes the method computationally tractable for large models. Open questions include how the method behaves in high-curvature regions far from data support, whether the 'same manifold up to bounded distortion' assumption can be formalized, and whether the approach extends to settings where models learn qualitatively different solutions (e.g., union-of-manifolds scenarios). The connection to identifiability theory via Diet's vMF structure is a promising but underexplored direction.
