## Problem

SAM's segmentation performance degrades significantly under input corruptions such as noise, blur, low illumination, and adverse weather, limiting its use in high-stakes applications. Prior robustification methods like RobustSAM require paired clean-degraded images for training and learn degradation-invariant representations, preventing them from adapting to the specific corruption affecting a given input. The paper further reveals that the optimal LoRA rank varies substantially across corruption types and individual images, indicating that fixed-rank adapters cannot capture this input-dependent variation.

## Input / Output

GaRA-SAM takes a degraded RGB image together with either point prompts or box prompts (following SAM's standard prompting protocol) and outputs a binary segmentation mask. A key distinction from prior work is that the model consumes degraded images directly without any upstream image restoration step and does not require clean reference images at either training or inference time. The adapters are inserted into the key, query, and value projection layers of SAM's frozen ViT-B or ViT-L image encoder, so the input format and resolution constraints remain identical to vanilla SAM.

## Method

GaRA reinterprets LoRA's update matrix ΔW = BA as a sum of R rank-1 components and introduces a two-level hierarchical gating mechanism to dynamically select which components are active per input. The first gate (Gating 1) is a binary Gumbel-Sigmoid decision that routes the input to either a lower-rank space (r_L=16) or a higher-rank space (r_H=256), separating components to avoid conflicts between inputs needing different representational capacities. The second gate (Gating 2) then predicts a binary mask over the rank-1 components within the selected space, activating only the subset appropriate for the current corruption. Both gates are lightweight MLPs trained end-to-end with the standard segmentation loss (Dice + Focal) on degraded images alone, requiring no paired clean references or auxiliary losses. This yields a parameter-efficient adapter (343M learnable parameters vs. SAM's 1250M) that achieves fine-grained, input-aware rank modulation at inference without any test-time optimization.

## Results

On LVIS with ViT-B and point prompts under synthetic degradation, GaRA-SAM achieves 77.0 IoU (85.7 Dice), surpassing RobustSAM (72.7 IoU) by 4.3%p and base SAM (65.0 IoU) by 12.0%p, while also improving clean-image performance to 81.3 IoU. On the real-world ACDC benchmark with ViT-L and box prompts, GaRA-SAM trained on real corrupted images (GaRA-SAM-Real) reaches 88.8 IoU, exceeding the previous best (RobustSAM at 67.5 IoU) by 21.3%p. On BDD+LIS (real corruptions, ViT-L, point prompts), GaRA-SAM scores 74.8 IoU versus 71.4 for RobustSAM and 68.9 for SAM. GaRA-SAM also maintains strong clean-image performance (e.g., 81.3 IoU on LVIS vs. 70.1 for SAM), confirming that robustification does not sacrifice generalization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GaRA-SAM outperforms all six baselines across every evaluated dataset and prompt type. Against base SAM, it improves degraded IoU by 12.0%p on LVIS (77.0 vs. 65.0, ViT-B point prompts) and by 19.3%p on ACDC (88.8 vs. 65.9, ViT-L box prompts). Against HQ-SAM, the closest non-robustness-specific baseline, GaRA-SAM gains 7.5%p degraded IoU on LVIS (77.0 vs. 69.5). Restoration-based methods (AirNet+SAM, URIE+SAM) perform at or below base SAM on degraded inputs, confirming that perceptual-quality restoration does not transfer to segmentation; GaRA-SAM exceeds them by over 12%p on LVIS. Against RobustSAM, the strongest prior robustness method, GaRA-SAM leads by 4.3%p on LVIS (77.0 vs. 72.7), 3.4%p on BDD+LIS (74.8 vs. 71.4, ViT-L point prompts), and a striking 21.3%p on ACDC (88.8 vs. 67.5, ViT-L box prompts with real-data training). Against fixed-rank LoRA (the direct architectural predecessor), GaRA-SAM gains 2.0–3.6%p degraded IoU on LVIS (77.0 vs. 75.6 at rank 128), validating the benefit of input-adaptive rank modulation over any static rank choice.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on LoRA as a parameter-efficient adaptation mechanism and departs from RobustSAM, the prior state-of-the-art for robust SAM, by eliminating the need for paired clean-degraded training images and auxiliary feature-consistency losses. Conceptually, GaRA repurposes the Mixture-of-Experts gating paradigm to operate at the level of individual rank-1 components within a LoRA adapter, introducing a hierarchical (coarse rank-space selection + fine component selection) gating strategy that has no direct precedent in the LoRA or robust-segmentation literature. The method also draws on the empirical observation that optimal adapter rank varies per corruption type and per image, a finding that motivates the input-adaptive design rather than a fixed-rank or per-corruption-type selection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that GaRA-SAM can be trained on real-world corrupted images without clean references, a setting where RobustSAM is architecturally inapplicable, yielding a 21.3%p IoU gain on ACDC. The empirical finding that a plain LoRA adapter already outperforms a purpose-built anti-degradation module (RobustSAM) on synthetic corruptions suggests that parameter-efficient adaptation may be a more general and effective route to robustness than task-specific restoration or feature-alignment objectives. Open questions include whether the manually chosen rank-space split, maximum ranks, and 2-level hierarchy can be automated, and whether the approach extends beyond SAM to other frozen foundation models or to tasks beyond segmentation.
