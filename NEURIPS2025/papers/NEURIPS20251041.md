## Problem

Standard additive noise model (ANM) methods for bivariate causal discovery fail when unobserved mediators corrupt the causal relationship between variables, a near-ubiquitous scenario in real-world systems. The only existing method addressing hidden mediation (CANM) lacks theoretical guarantees, requires specifying the number of mediators, and suffers from posterior collapse in practice. This paper characterizes exactly why each class of ANM-based methods breaks down under unobserved mediators and proposes a principled alternative.

## Input / Output

The method consumes bivariate observational data pairs (X, Y) as tabular data, standardized to mean 0 and variance 1, with no requirement to know the number of unobserved mediators. It outputs a forced causal direction decision (X→Y or Y→X) by comparing mutual information estimates between predicted noise and the conditioning variable in each direction. Two variants are evaluated: BiDD^Test uses a held-out test set for dependence estimation, while BiDD^Total uses the full dataset for both training and evaluation.

## Method

BiDD trains two conditional denoising diffusion models, one per causal direction: for B→A, it corrupts A with Gaussian noise at random timesteps and trains a neural network to predict the injected noise conditioned on B. The key insight is an independence asymmetry: in the causal direction, the predicted noise is provably independent of the conditioning variable (the cause), while in the anticausal direction it remains dependent, because the noise cannot be cleanly decoupled from the effect. The decision rule compares mutual information between predicted noise and the conditioning variable across all diffusion timesteps, selecting the direction with lower dependence. The paper proves asymptotic consistency under the standard ANM (no nonlinear mediators) and conjectures the result extends to the irreducible ANM-UM setting with nonlinear mediators, where the additive noise structure makes decoupling harder in the anticausal direction.

## Results

On synthetic bivariate data with one unobserved mediator (n=1000), BiDD_Total achieves ≥80% accuracy across all mechanism-noise combinations (linear, neural net, quadratic, tanh × Gaussian/uniform), reaching 100% on quadratic and neural-net mechanisms. In the no-mediator ANM setting, BiDD_Total maintains ≥77% accuracy, confirming the theoretical consistency guarantee (Theorem 4.2). On the Tübingen Cause-Effect Pairs dataset (n=3000), BiDD_Total achieves 64% accuracy, comparable to the best baselines NoGAM (69%) and SCORE (65%), while most other methods fall below 62%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BiDD_Total is the only method that maintains ≥80% accuracy uniformly across all seven mechanism-noise settings with one unobserved mediator, whereas every baseline fails (≤50%) in at least two settings. Among Residual-Independence methods, RESIT is the strongest competitor (reaching 100% on quadratic and tanh-Gaussian) but drops to 67–70% on neural-net and linear-uniform settings; PNL is consistently 10–20 points below BiDD_Total. Score-matching methods (Adascore, NoGAM, SCORE) are highly inconsistent: NoGAM and SCORE hit 100% on linear-Gaussian but collapse to 0–43% on neural-net and quadratic-uniform settings. MSE-minimization methods (CAM, DagmaLinear) are the weakest overall, with DagmaLinear averaging below 10% and CAM failing on linear-Gaussian (3%) and tanh-uniform (13%). The only prior hidden-mediator method, CANM, performs well on non-invertible mechanisms (quadratic, neural-net) but drops to 10–50% on invertible ones (linear, tanh), consistent with the paper's posterior-collapse analysis. On the Tübingen real-world benchmark, BiDD_Total (64%) trails NoGAM (69%) and SCORE (65%) by 1–5 points but outperforms all other baselines by at least 3 points.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the additive noise model (ANM) framework for bivariate causal direction identification to the setting of unobserved mediators, formalizing it as ANM-UM. It builds on the denoising diffusion framework of Ho et al. [15] and its conditional extensions [53], but repurposes diffusion not for generation or score estimation (as in prior diffusion-based causal work [56]) but as a mechanism to expose independence asymmetries in noise predictions. Conceptually, it departs from the entire ANM-based causal discovery literature (Residual-Independence, Score-Matching, MSE-Minimization) by proving systematic failure modes for each category under hidden mediation, and from the sole prior hidden-mediator method CANM [6] by replacing VAE-based ELBO comparison with a diffusion-based mutual information test that avoids the need to specify the number of latent mediators.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Unobserved mediators are near-ubiquitous in real-world systems (biomedicine, economics), yet prior bivariate causal discovery methods either ignore them or handle them with brittle, unguaranteed approaches. BiDD provides the first theoretical consistency guarantee for bivariate causal direction identification under unobserved mediators (in the reducible ANM-UM case) and demonstrates robust empirical performance across linear, nonlinear invertible, and non-invertible mechanisms. Key open questions include extending the consistency proof to the irreducible case (nonlinear mediators), handling latent confounding, and whether the diffusion-based independence test generalizes to multivariate or global causal discovery settings. The modest real-world improvement over best baselines on Tübingen (64% vs. 69%) suggests the method's advantage is most pronounced in mediator-corrupted synthetic settings rather than broadly across all real data.
