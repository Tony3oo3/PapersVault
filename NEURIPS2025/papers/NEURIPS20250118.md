## Problem

Ultra-high-resolution (UHR) text-to-image generation suffers from two critical gaps: the absence of a large-scale, rigorously curated UHR T2I dataset (existing options like Aesthetic-4K offer only ~10K images without strict quality criteria), and the neglect of training strategies specifically targeting fine-grained detail synthesis, where existing training-based methods prioritize computational efficiency over high-fidelity texture reconstruction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250118/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes natural-language text prompts as input and synthesizes 4096×4096 ultra-high-resolution RGB images. The training dataset (UltraHR-100K) contains 104,117 images whose minimum resolution exceeds 3K (average of width and height > 3000 px), each paired with long, fine-grained captions generated by Gemini 2.0 that are substantially longer and more semantically detailed than those in prior UHR datasets. Evaluation is conducted on the proposed UltraHR-eval4K benchmark (2,000 image-text pairs at 4096×4096) and the public Aesthetic-Eval@4096 subset (195 pairs).

## Method

The paper introduces UltraHR-100K, a 100K-image UHR dataset curated by intersecting three parallel filters: GLCM scores (detail richness), Shannon entropy (content complexity), and LAION aesthetic predictor scores (aesthetic quality), applied to an initial pool of ~400K images. For training, a two-stage frequency-aware post-training (FAPT) strategy is applied on top of the SANA base model: the first stage uses Logit-Normal timestep sampling for semantic planning, while the second stage introduces (i) Detail-Oriented Timestep Sampling (DOTS), which draws timesteps from a Beta(2,4) distribution to concentrate learning on later denoising steps where high-frequency details are synthesized, and (ii) Soft-Weighting Frequency Regularization (SWFR), which computes 2D DFTs of both prediction and target, applies a soft-weighting function w(r)=1+λ·(exp(γr)−1)/(exp(γ)−1) that progressively amplifies high-frequency bands, and adds the resulting frequency-domain L2 loss to the standard rectified-flow velocity prediction objective.

## Results

On the UltraHR-eval4K benchmark (4096×4096), the full method (UltraHR-100K + FAPT) achieves FID 31.748, FID-patch 15.795, IS 12.995, CLIP 31.82, and FG-CLIP 28.68, setting new state-of-the-art on FID, FID-patch, IS, and FG-CLIP. The most striking gain is in FID-patch (15.795), which is 38% lower than the next-best baseline (SD3.5+BSRGAN at 25.598), directly reflecting superior fine-grained detail synthesis. On the public Aesthetic-Eval@4096 benchmark, the method also leads with FID 142.965 and FID-patch 24.008, confirming generalizability beyond the proposed evaluation set.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The full method (UltraHR-100K + FAPT) outperforms all seven baselines on FID, FID-patch, IS, and FG-CLIP. The largest margin is against Diffusion4K (FID 39.857, FID-patch 38.515), which the paper identifies as the closest prior training-based UHR method; the gap in FID-patch (15.795 vs. 38.515) is particularly large, attributed to SWFR's DFT-based frequency regularization versus Diffusion4K's coarser DWT decomposition. Against the strongest super-resolution baseline SD3.5+BSRGAN (FID 31.870, FID-patch 25.598), the method wins on FID by a narrow 0.12 margin but dominates on FID-patch by 9.8 points, indicating substantially better local detail. Training-free methods (I-Max, HiFlow) and the T2I+SR pipeline (FLUX+BSRGAN) trail by 5–6 FID points and 17–27 FID-patch points. The only metric where the method does not lead is IS-patch, where SANA scores 5.649 versus 5.104, suggesting SANA retains a slight edge in local patch-level inception diversity. On the public Aesthetic-Eval@4096 set, the method again leads all training-based baselines (PixArt-σ, SANA, Diffusion4K) on FID, FID-patch, CLIP, and FG-CLIP.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of UHR text-to-image generation and frequency-domain training strategies. It builds directly on SANA's linear diffusion transformer as the base architecture and adopts SD3's Logit-Normal Sampling for its first-stage fine-tuning, while explicitly departing from Diffusion4K's DWT-based frequency decomposition in favor of a continuous DFT-based regularization (SWFR). The dataset contribution extends Aesthetic-4K (~12K images) by roughly 10× in scale and introduces a multi-criteria filtering pipeline (GLCM, Shannon entropy, LAION aesthetic score) that prior UHR datasets lacked. Conceptually, the paper positions itself against training-free UHR methods (HiFlow, I-Max, DiffuseHigh) by arguing that post-training on purpose-built UHR data is necessary to overcome the fine-detail limitations of models never exposed to high-resolution training signals.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The release of a 100K-image, 3K+ resolution open-source dataset with Gemini-2.0-generated fine-grained captions addresses a genuine bottleneck for UHR T2I research, and the UltraHR-eval4K benchmark (2,000 images at 4096×4096) provides a much larger evaluation set than the 195-image Aesthetic-Eval@4096. The FAPT post-training recipe (DOTS + SWFR) is a lightweight, architecture-agnostic strategy that could be applied to other rectified-flow or diffusion backbones. However, all experiments are conducted solely on SANA, the primary quantitative evaluation is on the authors' own benchmark, and the user study involves only 5 raters on 50 cases, leaving open questions about cross-architecture generalizability and the robustness of the reported FID-patch gains. The authors also acknowledge a slight text-image alignment trade-off and a scarcity of portrait data, both of which limit the current scope of the contribution.
