## Problem

Existing online Koopman learning methods either rely on fixed linear dictionaries or update model parameters unconditionally at every time step, leading to overfitting and unnecessary computation. No principled mechanism exists for deciding *when* a Koopman embedding has become temporally inconsistent with incoming streaming data and should be adapted. This paper addresses that gap by repurposing conformal prediction as a data-driven update trigger for deep Koopman embeddings.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a rolling buffer of w recent state vectors x_{t-w}, …, x_t from a streaming dynamical system, with no access to the full trajectory history. It produces a finite-dimensional Koopman operator K_t ∈ C^{m×m}, a learned feature map Φ_θ that concatenates the raw state x with a neural-network embedding (so m = d + ⌈d/2⌉), and a binary conformity decision determining whether gradient updates are needed. The multi-step prediction loss is evaluated over all valid (s, τ) pairs within the buffer, and the conformity score specifically measures the discrepancy between the current lifted state and its multi-step predictions from every prior state in the window.

## Method

COLoKe jointly learns a deep Koopman-invariant embedding and a linear operator by minimizing a multi-step prediction loss over a fixed-size rolling buffer, where the feature map is structured as [x, Φ̃_θ(x)] to embed a built-in reconstruction constraint without a decoder. The key innovation is a repurposed conformal PI control loop: rather than constructing prediction sets for new states, the conformity score (the multi-step prediction error at the current state) is compared against a dynamically calibrated threshold q_t that adapts via proportional and integral feedback. Gradient-based updates to (θ_t, K_t) are triggered only when the score exceeds q_t, and continue until conformity is restored, preventing overfitting from unconditional retraining. The paper establishes a dynamic regret bound of O(αh(T) + V_T + S_T) under L-smoothness, bounded oracle variation, and sublinear cumulative threshold growth, and demonstrates that the conformal mechanism yields sublinear threshold accumulation in practice.

## Results

COLoKe achieves the best generalization error across all synthetic benchmarks, reaching 2.4×10⁻⁷ on the Single Attractor system (vs. 1.1×10⁻³ for ODMD and 1.0×10⁻² for OnlineAE) and 6.5×10⁻³ on the chaotic Lorenz system (vs. 2.7×10⁻¹ for ODMD and 5.9×10⁻¹ for OnlineAE), representing nearly two orders of magnitude improvement over baselines. On real-world datasets, COLoKe attains the lowest online prediction error on EEG (7.8×10⁻³) and turbulence/CASES-99 (4.7×10⁻⁴). The conformal-triggering mechanism also yields substantially lower computation time than fixed-step variants (OLoKe) while maintaining superior accuracy, as shown in the Pareto front comparison.

## Baselines & Comparisons

COLoKe outperforms all three baselines—ODMD, OEDMD, and OnlineAE—on every dataset for both generalization and online prediction error. Against ODMD (linear, no learned embedding), COLoKe achieves roughly 4–3 orders of magnitude lower error on the Single Attractor (2.4×10⁻⁷ vs. 1.1×10⁻³) and Lorenz system (6.5×10⁻³ vs. 2.7×10⁻¹), demonstrating the benefit of deep adaptive embeddings over fixed linear models. Against OEDMD (polynomial dictionary, online recursive update), COLoKe consistently wins by 1–2 orders of magnitude, with the gap largest on the Lorenz system where OEDMD's fixed dictionary cannot capture chaotic dynamics. Against OnlineAE (neural autoencoder with fixed 100-iteration updates), COLoKe's conformal-triggering mechanism yields lower error on all datasets while also reducing computation time, as the adaptive update rule avoids unnecessary gradient steps. The ablation variant OLoKe (same architecture, fixed-step updates instead of conformal triggering) is systematically outperformed by COLoKe, confirming that the conformal update decision—not just the architecture—drives the performance gain.

## Relation to Prior Work

COLoKe sits at the intersection of deep Koopman embedding learning (building on Li et al. 2017's identity-plus-learnable feature map and Otto & Rowley 2019's multi-step prediction loss) and conformal PI control (Angelopoulos et al. 2023), repurposing the latter not for constructing prediction intervals but as a data-driven trigger for when to update the Koopman operator and embedding. This departs from prior online Koopman methods (ODMD, R-EDMD, OnlineAE) that rely on fixed update schedules or unconditional retraining at every step, and from batch-only adaptive embedding methods (DKLT, BatchOnline) that cannot handle streaming data. The novel 'prediction score set' concept shifts the conformal evaluation from assessing whether a new state is surprising to assessing whether the current model parameters remain temporally consistent.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

COLoKe provides the first principled, statistically grounded stopping rule for online Koopman learning, eliminating the need to manually tune update frequency and reducing both computational cost and overfitting. The dynamic regret bound (Theorem 3.3) offers theoretical assurance, though its key assumption (A3) on sublinear growth of conformity thresholds remains empirically supported but not derived from first principles. The conformal-triggering paradigm is potentially transferable to other online non-convex learning settings beyond Koopman, and extending the framework to non-autonomous systems is identified as a natural next step. The near two-orders-of-magnitude improvement on the chaotic Lorenz system over linear baselines underscores the value of combining expressive deep embeddings with adaptive update scheduling.
