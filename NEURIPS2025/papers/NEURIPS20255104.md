## Problem

Estimating the drift and diffusion functions of stochastic differential equations from noisy, sparsely observed time series remains a central challenge in scientific discovery. Existing approaches either depend heavily on hand-crafted prior knowledge (symbolic regression, Gaussian process regression) or require costly, system-specific training with slow convergence (neural SDEs). No pretrained model existed that could perform zero-shot, in-context estimation of SDE functions across diverse unseen systems without retraining.

## Input / Output

The model consumes K noisy time series paths of length L, recorded at non-equidistant times, which are reorganized into K(L−1) one-step transition tuples of the form (y, Δy, Δy², Δτ) to decouple drift and diffusion estimation channels. Given a query location x in a predefined domain, the model outputs non-parametric estimates of the d-dimensional drift vector f(x) and the diagonal diffusion matrix G(x) (parameterized as √g_i(x) per component). The system supports 1–3 dimensional SDEs with inter-observation times spanning 10⁻¹ to 10⁻⁵ arbitrary units and additive noise levels up to ρ=0.05.

## Method

FIM-SDE is a 20M-parameter transformer-based neural operator that amortizes SDE inference via a DeepONet-inspired architecture: a self-attentive Transformer encoder with linear attention processes the embedded transition tuples into a Context Matrix (branch-net equivalent), which then serves as keys and values for M stacked functional attention layers (trunk-net equivalent) that perform location-dependent querying at target points x. The model is pretrained in a supervised fashion on 600K synthetic SDEs drawn from a prior over polynomial drifts (degree ≤3) and positive polynomial diffusions (degree ≤2), using a weighted MSE loss with a learnable epistemic uncertainty term U_θ that adaptively down-weights sparsely observed or noisy regions. A key design choice is reorganizing sequential time series into independent one-step transition tuples, exploiting the fact that drift and diffusion at location x depend only on local transitions near x, making attention the natural mechanism for spatially-weighted aggregation. The pretrained model supports rapid finetuning on target data—converging up to 50× faster than neural SDE baselines—via either short-time transition likelihood (dense data) or multi-step simulation matching (sparse data).

## Results

On seven canonical SDE systems under noise (ρ=0.02–0.05) and sparse observations (Δτ=0.02), FIM-SDE achieves MMD scores of 0.1–1.4 (×100), consistently outperforming both SparseGP (which frequently fails entirely) and BISDE (0.3–18). On the Lorenz attractor, FIM-SDE finetuned for only 100 iterations reaches MMD 0.0038 vs. LatentSDE's 0.0517 after 5000 iterations (a 50× speedup). On four real-world datasets, finetuned FIM-SDE achieves the best MMD across all systems: Wind 0.029, Oil 0.068, Facebook 0.006, Tesla 0.004, while zero-shot FIM-SDE already matches or beats both BISDE and LatentSDE on stock price data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SparseGP (Batz et al., 2018), FIM-SDE dominates under any noise or sparsity: SparseGP frequently produces invalid SDEs (all 5 runs fail on Duffing at ρ=0, and on Damped Cubic/Duffing/Glycolysis at ρ≥0.02), while FIM-SDE remains stable and achieves the lowest MMD in every noisy setting. Against BISDE (Wang et al., 2022), FIM-SDE wins decisively under noise (e.g., MMD 1.8 vs. 13 on Hopf at ρ=0.05, Δτ=0.002) but BISDE retains a slight edge on clean, dense data (ρ=0, Δτ=0.002), where FIM-SDE is nonetheless comparable in roughly half the systems. Against LatentSDE (Li et al., 2020) on the Lorenz attractor, zero-shot FIM-SDE underperforms (MMD 0.40 vs. 0.05), but after just 100 finetuning iterations FIM-SDE surpasses LatentSDE on all three test initial conditions (0.0038 vs. 0.0517) while converging 50× faster. On real-world data, finetuned FIM-SDE achieves the best MMD on all four datasets (Wind 0.029, Oil 0.068, Facebook 0.006, Tesla 0.004), outperforming both BISDE and LatentSDE, while zero-shot FIM-SDE already beats both baselines on stock prices.

## Relation to Prior Work

FIM-SDE applies the foundation inference model (FIM) paradigm—pretraining on synthetic data for zero-shot inference—previously developed for ODEs (d'Ascoli et al., 2024) and Markov jump processes (Berghaus et al., 2024) to the SDE discovery problem. Architecturally, it adapts the DeepONet branch-net/trunk-net decomposition (Lu et al., 2021) into a transformer-based neural operator with a self-attentive context encoder and a functional attention mechanism for location-dependent querying. The key departure from prior SDE discovery methods (symbolic regression, GP regression, Neural SDEs) is that FIM-SDE requires no per-system training pipeline, no variational smoothing of inter-observation dynamics, and no hand-specified function dictionaries, instead amortizing the entire estimation into a single pretrained forward pass.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that a single pretrained model can perform zero-shot drift and diffusion estimation for SDEs, matching methods that must be trained from scratch on each target system, and then outperforming them after only ~100 finetuning iterations (a 50× compute reduction over Neural SDE baselines). The practical impact is significant for scientific workflows where prior knowledge is scarce and repeated retraining is impractical. However, the approach is currently restricted to ≤3-dimensional systems with low-degree polynomial drift/diffusion and diagonal diffusion matrices, and the high rejection rates in data generation (92% in 3D) limit scalability. Open questions include whether more expressive priors (e.g., random function compositions) can extend coverage, whether the framework generalizes to non-diagonal or time-dependent diffusion, and how pretrained FIM-SDE weights might regularize equation discovery in higher-dimensional settings.
