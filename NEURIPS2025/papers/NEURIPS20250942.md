## Problem

Existing VLA models for autonomous driving suffer from two critical limitations: (1) action generation either produces physically infeasible trajectories or relies on intermediate meta-action/latent-token modules that break end-to-end optimization and add complexity, and (2) reasoning is fixed rather than adaptive, applying the same strategy regardless of scenario difficulty. No prior work unifies physically grounded action tokens with adaptive fast/slow thinking within a single autoregressive VLM for end-to-end driving.

## Input / Output

The model consumes three camera views (front, front-left, front-right), each providing 4 sequential frames at 2 Hz (resized to 28×28×128 pixels), a text navigation instruction (e.g., 'Turn Left'), and ego vehicle state (velocity, acceleration, historical actions). It autoregressively outputs either a short template plus 10 discrete action tokens (fast thinking) or a structured CoT reasoning passage followed by the same 10 tokens (slow thinking). Each action token encodes a 0.5-second motion segment (Δx, Δy, Δθ) from a 2048-entry K-disk codebook, and the 10 tokens are sequentially composed to reconstruct a 5-second planning trajectory in ego-centric coordinates.

## Method

AutoVLA integrates a physical action codebook (built via K-disk clustering on WOMD trajectories, K=2048) as additional tokens in a Qwen2.5-VL-3B backbone, transforming trajectory planning into next-token prediction within a single autoregressive Transformer. Training proceeds in two stages: (1) SFT with a combined LM + action loss and per-sample CoT weighting (λ_cot=40) to instill dual fast/slow thinking, using reasoning data distilled from Qwen2.5-VL-72B via an automated annotation pipeline; (2) RFT using GRPO with a hybrid reward (PDMS or ADE for driving quality minus a CoT length penalty) that aligns the policy with verifiable driving metrics while penalizing unnecessary reasoning, enabling adaptive switching between direct action output and extended CoT. The RFT stage uses LoRA adapters and group-relative advantage estimation over G candidate outputs, with KL regularization against the SFT reference policy to preserve learned knowledge.

## Results

On the NAVSIM (nuPlan) open-loop benchmark, AutoVLA with Best-of-N planning achieves a PDMS of 92.12, competitive with Centaur (92.10) and Hydra-MDP (91.26), though slightly below TrajHF (93.95). After reinforcement fine-tuning (RFT), AutoVLA improves PDMS by 10.6% (from 80.54 to 89.11) while reducing runtime by 66.8%. On the Bench2Drive (CARLA) closed-loop benchmark, AutoVLA achieves the highest driving score (78.84) and success rate (57.73%), outperforming the strongest baseline Orion (77.74 / 54.62%). The proposed K-disk action tokenization outperforms FAST (DCT) by 12.91 PDMS points on NAVSIM (80.54 vs. 67.63).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On NAVSIM, AutoVLA (Best-of-N, 92.12 PDMS) surpasses Hydra-MDP (91.26) and matches Centaur (92.10), but trails TrajHF (93.95) by 1.83 points; the Post-RFT variant (89.11) still lags behind Hydra-MDP, Centaur, and TrajHF. On the closed-loop Bench2Drive benchmark, AutoVLA (78.84 driving score, 57.73% success) outperforms all baselines including Orion (77.74, 54.62%), DriveAdapter (64.22, 33.08%), and UniAD-Base (45.81, 16.36%), with a notable 22-point advantage in comfort over Orion (39.33 vs. 17.38). For action tokenization, the proposed K-disk method (80.54 PDMS) substantially outperforms FAST/DCT (67.63) by 12.91 points, and physical action tokens (80.54 PDM, 3.95s runtime) beat text-based waypoint prediction (71.31 PDM, 7.65s) by 9.23 points in planning quality and 48% in inference speed. The RFT stage yields a 10.6% PDMS gain and 66.8% runtime reduction over the SFT-only model, demonstrating that adaptive reasoning (fast vs. slow thinking) is the key efficiency lever. Overall, AutoVLA is competitive but not state-of-the-art on open-loop NAVSIM, while it achieves the best closed-loop performance on Bench2Drive.

## Relation to Prior Work

AutoVLA extends the VLA-for-driving line initiated by works like DriveVLM, AlphaDrive, and Orion, but departs from them by eliminating intermediate meta-action modules or separate fast/slow planners in favor of a single autoregressive VLM backbone that directly outputs physical action tokens. The action tokenization draws on K-disk clustering from traffic modeling (Trajeglish, SMART) and VLA tokenization (FAST, RT-1), while the reinforcement fine-tuning stage adapts GRPO (from DeepSeekMath) to the driving domain with verifiable planning rewards. Unlike DriveVLM's dual-process architecture with separate VLM and end-to-end modules, AutoVLA unifies both reasoning modes within one model and uses RL to learn when to reason, rather than relying on a fixed routing heuristic.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified single-model design with adaptive fast/slow thinking addresses a practical bottleneck in VLA driving: the ~10× runtime gap between reasoning and non-reasoning modes. The 66.8% runtime reduction via GRPO-based RFT, combined with competitive (though not SOTA) planning scores, suggests a viable path toward deployable VLA driving systems. Key open questions remain: the model still operates at ~1 Hz and is heavily GPU-dependent, limiting real-time deployment; the best-of-N oracle selection used for top NAVSIM scores is not practical at inference time; and generalization beyond the tested datasets (nuPlan, nuScenes, Waymo, CARLA) to truly novel long-tail scenarios is not thoroughly validated.
