## Problem

Existing crystal generative models learn continuous distributions over atom positions, assigning zero probability to special Wyckoff positions (zero-volume sets like points, lines, or planes) that are critical for materials properties. Models that do enforce space group constraints either lack space group invariant likelihoods (assigning different probabilities to symmetrically equivalent atoms) or rely on expensive post-hoc DFT/MLIP relaxations. This paper addresses the gap by building a generative model that natively handles space group constraints with explicit invariant likelihoods throughout the generation process.

## Input / Output

The model takes a random seed (and optionally an elemental composition for the crystal structure prediction task) and sequentially produces a complete crystal as a tuple (G, L, A, W, X): a space group from 230 options, a conventional lattice basis in R^{3×3} discretized to 0.01 Å resolution, a sequence of elements, Wyckoff positions, and fractional coordinates within the asymmetric unit. The generation is strictly autoregressive over constrained variables—lattice parameters are sampled under crystal-family constraints (e.g., a=b for tetragonal), Wyckoff positions are ordered lexicographically by letter then atomic number, and coordinates are confined to the Wyckoff shape (a convex polytope, line segment, or point) within the asymmetric unit. The number of atoms in the asymmetric unit is sampled implicitly via a learned stop token.

## Method

SGEquiDiff factorizes crystal generation into three stages: (1) SE(3)-invariant telescoping discrete sampling of six lattice parameters, where a two-level categorical hierarchy (K=2, n_l=100) achieves 0.01 Å resolution with O(n_l·K) memory instead of O(N_l); (2) a permutation-invariant transformer that autoregressively predicts Wyckoff positions, elements, and the stop token, with attention masks enforcing lexicographic atom ordering and physical descriptors (Xie-Grossman element embeddings, Fourier-feature lattice embeddings) for initialization; and (3) space group equivariant diffusion of fractional coordinates using a modified FAENet GNN (with variance-preserving aggregation, no frame averaging) trained via score matching against a novel Space Group Wrapped Normal (SGWN) distribution. The key theoretical result (Theorem 1) proves that space group equivariant vector fields automatically reside in the tangent space of each Wyckoff position, so atoms never leave their Wyckoff manifold during diffusion—eliminating the need for post-hoc projections that cause indeterminate likelihoods or discontinuous generation. The GNN is symmetrized by summing over the finite quotient group G/T_L, and the SGWN score is shown to be space group equivariant, ensuring the learned denoising field preserves all crystal symmetries.

## Results

On MP-20, SGEquiDiff (5.5M params) achieves a stable-unique-novel (S.U.N.) rate of 15.64%, the highest among all baselines, with 99.25% structure validity and 86.16% composition validity. It attains the best U.N. template rate (18.6%) and best JSD for Wyckoff dimensionalities (0.0252), indicating superior preservation of space group and Wyckoff position distributions. On the harder MPTS-52 dataset, SGEquiDiff maintains a 38.7% U.N. template rate and 89.8% U.N. structure rate, outperforming all space-group-constrained baselines on composition validity and Wasserstein distances for density and number of elements.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SGEquiDiff outperforms DiffCSP++ [41] on S.U.N. rate (15.64% vs 15.23%) and dramatically on U.N. template rate (18.6% vs 1.1%), while using ~2.2× fewer parameters (5.5M vs 12.3M). Against MatterGen [107], SGEquiDiff achieves a comparable S.U.N. rate (15.64% vs 15.67%) but with 8× fewer parameters (5.5M vs 44.6M), ~16× faster sampling (226s vs 3,689s per batch), and substantially better JSD for space groups (0.1733 vs 0.4331) and Wyckoff dimensions (0.0252 vs 0.2129). Compared to SymmCD [55], SGEquiDiff achieves a slightly higher S.U.N. rate (15.64% vs 15.62%) and better JSD_dWyckoff (0.0252 vs 0.0344), though SymmCD has a marginally lower JSD_G (0.1669 vs 0.1733) and faster inference (139s vs 226s). Versus DiffCSP [40], SGEquiDiff trades a marginally lower S.U.N. rate (15.64% vs 15.69%) for a much higher U.N. template rate (18.6% vs 12.2%) and better JSD_dWyckoff (0.0252 vs 0.2328). Against CDVAE [100], SGEquiDiff improves S.U.N. rate by ~2 percentage points (15.64% vs 13.70%) while being ~4× faster per batch. On MPTS-52, SGEquiDiff achieves the highest U.N. template rate (38.7%) and composition validity (79.83%) among all evaluated methods, with DiffCSP++ and DiffCSP trailing on both metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

SGEquiDiff extends the score-matching diffusion framework (Song & Ermon, Song et al.) to crystal generation by introducing a Space Group Wrapped Normal (SGWN) distribution whose score is provably space group equivariant, and by building a space group equivariant GNN (modified FAENet) to regress it. It departs from prior space-group-constrained models (DiffCSP++, SymmCD, SymmBFN) that rely on post-hoc projections of atom coordinates onto Wyckoff subspaces—projections the authors show can yield indeterminate likelihoods or discontinuous generation—by proving (Theorem 1) that equivariant vector fields automatically remain in the tangent space of each Wyckoff position. The autoregressive transformer for Wyckoff positions and elements follows the lineage of CrystalFormer and WyckoffTransformer, while the telescoping discrete sampler for lattice parameters is a novel engineering contribution that avoids the poor scaling of a flat softmax over O(10⁴) classes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical guarantee that equivariant flows never leave their Wyckoff manifold eliminates a practical failure mode in prior symmetry-constrained generators and provides explicit space-group-invariant likelihoods, which is important because Neumann's principle links space group symmetry directly to macroscopic material properties. Empirically, SGEquiDiff achieves competitive S.U.N. rates (15.64%) with DFT validation using only 5.5M parameters versus MatterGen's 44.6M, and the inference-time temperature knob offers practical control over novelty. Open questions include whether the approach extends to magnetic and spin space groups, how to handle defects and compositional disorder, and whether the DFT-validated stability metric (energy above hull < 0.1 eV/atom) is sufficient for synthesizability—a necessary but not sufficient condition per prior analyses.
