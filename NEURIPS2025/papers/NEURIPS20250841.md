## Problem

Prior hardware-aware neural architecture search methods optimize for latency or energy in isolation, treating these as proxies for sustainability, but total carbon footprint—combining operational emissions from inference and embodied emissions from chip manufacturing—cannot be captured by either metric alone. This paper asks how incorporating total carbon as a first-class optimization objective, jointly across model architecture and hardware accelerator design, changes the resulting design trade-offs compared to traditional latency- or energy-centric approaches.

## Input / Output

The framework takes a pre-trained Transformer base model (e.g., BERT-Base, Llama3-8B, ViT-B/16, CLIP-ViT-B/16), a parameterized hardware accelerator template (tensor-core PEs, vector units, local/global SRAM, fixed at 22nm/8-bit/500MHz/1GB HBM), and a set of optimization objectives under a TOPS compute budget (1–20 TOPS). It outputs a jointly optimized pruned model architecture (specifying layers, FFN width, hidden dim, attention heads per encoder) paired with a concrete hardware configuration (core count, PE array dimensions, buffer sizes, bandwidth), along with estimated total carbon (kg CO2e), latency, and energy. Pruned models are optionally fine-tuned for 2 distillation epochs on MetaCLIP-2.5B to recover accuracy.

## Method

CATransformers performs multi-objective Bayesian optimization (qNEHVI via BoTorch/Ax) over a joint model-hardware design space of ~100 million configurations in 100 iterations, balancing accuracy, latency, energy, and total carbon. The ML Model Evaluator prunes the base model along layers, FFN width, attention heads, and embedding dimensions, then uses lightweight fine-tuning on a small dataset (MSCOCO, CIFAR-10, or MRPC) as an accuracy proxy achieving 0.98 Spearman correlation with full training. The Hardware Estimator chains Accelergy (area/access energy), Sunstone (per-operator latency/energy), ACT (embodied carbon from chip area), and Electricity Maps (location-specific grid intensity scaled over a 3-year deployment lifetime) to produce a unified carbon estimate. The key finding is that carbon optimization selects fundamentally different configurations—smaller, single-core accelerators with compact memory—compared to latency-optimized designs that favor 3× larger multi-core hardware, yielding up to 30% total carbon reduction at iso-accuracy.

## Results

CarbonCLIP models achieve up to 17% lower total carbon emissions compared to state-of-the-art CLIP variants on edge devices while maintaining similar accuracy and latency. Specifically, CarbonCLIP-XL (123M params) reaches 52.0% average accuracy across 41 zero-shot CLIP Benchmark datasets with 0.49 kgCO2e total carbon and 19.1 ms latency, versus 53.2% / 0.54 kgCO2e / 18.5 ms for the full CLIP-B/16-DataComp baseline. CarbonCLIP-S (63M params) matches TinyCLIP-39M/16 accuracy (45.0%) while reducing carbon by 17% (0.36 vs 0.44 kgCO2e). Across all optimization modes, carbon-focused co-optimization reduces total carbon by an average of 30% over latency-optimized and 8% over energy-optimized configurations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares CarbonCLIP against two baseline families: (1) standard CLIP-ViT-B/16 trained on DataComp-1B and (2) TinyCLIP, a state-of-the-art latency-optimized CLIP variant. Against the full CLIP-B/16-DataComp baseline, CarbonCLIP-XL achieves near-parity accuracy (52.0% vs 53.2%) with a 10% carbon reduction (0.49 vs 0.54 kgCO2e) at comparable latency (19.1 vs 18.5 ms). Against TinyCLIP-39M/16, CarbonCLIP-S matches accuracy exactly (45.0%) while cutting carbon by 17% (0.36 vs 0.44 kgCO2e), and CarbonCLIP-L improves accuracy by 4 percentage points (48.7% vs 45.0%) with a 4.5% carbon reduction. Against TinyCLIP-8M/16, CarbonCLIP-XS boosts accuracy by 8 percentage points (38.7% vs 30.7%) with a 3% carbon reduction, though at roughly 2.4× the latency (7.1 vs 3.0 ms). Overall, CarbonCLIP consistently wins on the carbon-accuracy Pareto frontier, trading modest latency increases for substantial carbon savings without accuracy regressions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the joint model-hardware co-optimization line of work (DANCE, NAAS, HAT) by replacing latency/energy objectives with total carbon (embodied + operational) as a first-class optimization target. It builds on the BoTorch/Ax Bayesian optimization stack with the qNEHVI multi-objective acquisition function, and integrates Accelergy, Sunstone, and ACT into a unified estimation pipeline. Unlike prior carbon-aware efforts (CORDOBA, CE-NAS) that optimize model or hardware in isolation, CATransformers is the first to jointly search both design spaces under a unified carbon objective, and unlike prior NAS methods that assume fixed hardware, it treats the accelerator as a co-design variable.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides the first open-source toolchain for estimating both operational and embodied carbon during early-stage accelerator design-space exploration, filling a gap that limits reproducibility in sustainable AI hardware design. Key open questions include whether the findings generalize beyond Transformers to CNNs or Mamba architectures, how results shift under different grid intensities and deployment lifetimes (partially explored in appendices), and whether broader environmental impacts (e-waste, water, rare minerals) can be incorporated once standardized metrics exist. The proxy-based accuracy estimation (Spearman 0.98) is practical but may underestimate pruning effects in some regimes, and the 7.7× latency penalty for pure carbon optimization limits immediate deployability without the joint carbon+latency mode.
