## Problem

The frugality ratio of the VCG auction for strategic uncapacitated facility location was previously bounded above by 4 with no known lower bound, leaving a significant gap. More broadly, prior frugal mechanism design work operates in a purely adversarial setting with no information about agents' private costs, yet in practice agencies often have access to cost predictions from historical data or expert estimates. The paper asks whether such predictions can be leveraged to substantially reduce overpayment (improve frugality) while preserving robustness against arbitrarily inaccurate predictions.

## Input / Output

The auction receives a set of clients and facilities with metric connection costs, reported opening-cost bids from each facility owner, predicted opening costs for all facilities, and a tunable parameter ε ∈ (0,2] (plus an error-tolerance λ for the ERRORTOLERANT variant). It outputs a selected facility subset and a threshold payment for each selected facility, evaluated against the cost of the second-best (frugal) solution. Notably, the mechanism only requires access to the membership of the predicted-optimal set and the true opening costs of facilities in that set, not the full cost vector, making it practical when predictions are available only for a subset of locations.

## Method

The paper first proves a tight frugality ratio of exactly 3 for VCG via a rerouting argument that reassigns users from each optimal facility to a ranked successor facility (or to the frugal solution), bounding each threshold payment by the corresponding rerouting cost. The PREDICTEDLIMITS auction then modifies VCG by scaling up the opening cost of any facility in the predicted-optimal set by a factor of 2/ε whenever its true cost exceeds the prediction, which counterintuitively penalizes overbidding within the predicted solution and tightens payment bounds when predictions are accurate, yielding (1+ε)-consistency and max(5, 3+2/ε)-robustness. The ERRORTOLERANT variant extends this by replacing the scaling threshold ô_ℓ with λô_ℓ and, when all true costs fall within the tolerance, applying a uniform 1/λ² downscaling to the entire predicted-optimal solution cost, guaranteeing that the predicted optimum is selected and achieving a frugality of η(1+λ)+2ε when prediction error η ≤ λ. Truthfulness of all mechanisms follows from monotonicity of the allocation rule via Myerson's Lemma.

## Results

The paper establishes that the VCG auction's frugality ratio for metric uncapacitated facility location is exactly 3, improving the previous upper bound of 4 (Talwar, 2003) and providing a matching lower bound. The PREDICTEDLIMITS learning-augmented auction achieves a frugality ratio of 1+ε when predictions are accurate (consistency) while maintaining a worst-case bound of max(5, 3+2/ε) regardless of prediction quality (robustness). The ERRORTOLERANT auction extends this to approximately accurate predictions, achieving η(1+λ)+2ε when prediction error η≤λ, and max{2λ⁴+3λ², 3+2/ε} for arbitrary error.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is against the classic VCG auction, whose frugality ratio was previously bounded above by 4 (Talwar, 2003) with no known lower bound. This paper tightens that bound to exactly 3 via a rerouting argument and a matching tree-metric lower bound construction. The PREDICTEDLIMITS auction improves upon VCG in the consistent setting: by choosing a small ε (e.g., ε=0.1), it achieves a frugality ratio of 1.1 when predictions are accurate, compared to VCG's fixed ratio of 3, while still guaranteeing a constant worst-case bound of at most 5. The ERRORTOLERANT auction further refines the trade-off by interpolating between the consistent and robust extremes, providing a smooth degradation as prediction error η grows from 1 to λ. No empirical baselines are compared, as all results are purely theoretical.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two research threads: frugal mechanism design for combinatorial procurement (initiated by Archer and Tardos for path auctions and extended by Talwar to strategic UFL with a VCG frugality bound of 4) and learning-augmented mechanism design (initiated by Agrawal et al. for facility location and by Xu and Lu for frugal path auctions with predictions). The paper tightens Talwar's VCG bound to exactly 3 via a refined rerouting argument and a matching tree-metric lower bound, then extends the Xu–Lu frugality-with-predictions framework from path auctions to the richer UFL setting. The PREDICTEDLIMITS and ERRORTOLERANT auctions are novel mechanisms that depart from the standard approach of scaling predicted-optimal costs downward; instead, they scale costs upward to suppress overbidding, a design choice motivated by the threshold-payment structure of VCG.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The tight VCG frugality bound of 3 resolves a gap left by Talwar (upper bound 4, no lower bound) and provides a clean benchmark for future mechanism designs. The learning-augmented auctions demonstrate that even unreliable predictions can yield near-optimal frugality (1+ε) while preserving constant-factor robustness, offering a practical recipe for agencies that have historical cost data. Key open questions include whether VCG is optimal among all truthful auctions for UFL frugality, the Pareto frontier between consistency and robustness, and whether more nuanced prediction-error models (e.g., the MAC framework) can yield graceful degradation. The results are purely theoretical with no empirical validation, and the IP-based implementation (Appendix F) may face scalability challenges for large instances.
