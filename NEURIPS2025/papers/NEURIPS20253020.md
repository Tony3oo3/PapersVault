## Problem

Traditional photography composition methods rely on 2D cropping, which cannot fix scenes where subjects are spatially poorly arranged. Professional photographers address this by adjusting their camera perspective to reframe 3D spatial relationships, but no computational framework exists for perspective-based composition due to the absence of perspective transformation datasets and undefined quality assessment criteria.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253020/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single less-favorable perspective RGB image as input and produces a perspective transformation video (generated via I2V models) that visually demonstrates the camera movement from the poor to the aesthetically enhanced viewpoint. Additionally, a guidance box is drawn on the final enhanced frame and projected back onto the original image via homography; as the user physically moves their camera, this box deforms and approaches a rectangle when the target perspective is reached, providing real-time compositional guidance without requiring explicit camera trajectories or text prompts.

## Method

The pipeline has three components. First, an automated dataset construction pipeline uses ViewCrafter-based 3D reconstruction to synthesize camera movement sequences from expert photographs (GAICD, SACD, FLMS, FCDB, Unsplash), generating videos from well-composed to less-favorable perspectives and reversing them to obtain training pairs, with a PQA model filtering out reconstruction artifacts. Second, the PPC module formulates perspective composition as an image-to-video task using open-source I2V models (CogVideoX, Hunyuan, Wan2.1), then applies Flow-DPO (a Rectified Flow variant of DPO) with the PQA model as reward to align generated trajectories with human aesthetic preferences rather than strict ground-truth adherence. Third, the PQA model is a two-stage fine-tuned Qwen2-VL using Bradley-Terry-with-ties loss: stage 1 trains on ~15K unpaired videos for visual and motion quality scoring, and stage 2 refines composition aesthetic judgment on expert-annotated paired comparisons, with separate special tokens for context-agnostic (VQ, MQ) and composition-aware (CA) dimensions to decouple feature representations.

## Results

Among the three I2V backbones evaluated, Wan2.1 14B achieves the best camera motion matching (CMM 0.5989) for perspective accuracy, while Hunyuan I2V leads on FVD (264), PSNR (9.40), SSIM (0.3537), and human performance scores for visual quality (VQ 0.7216) and motion quality (MQ 0.7496). CogVideoX 1.5 5B achieves the highest composition aesthetic score (CA 0.7196). Incorporating RLHF (Flow-DPO) improves VQ from 0.7216 to 0.7477, MQ from 0.7496 to 0.7774, and CA from 0.7070 to 0.7342, confirming that preference alignment enhances compositional quality beyond strict ground-truth adherence.

## Baselines & Comparisons

As the first work to introduce photography perspective composition (PPC), there are no prior PPC baselines to compare against. Instead, the paper benchmarks three state-of-the-art image-to-video generation models (CogVideoX 1.5 5B, Hunyuan I2V, Wan2.1 14B) as backbones for the PPC pipeline. No single model dominates all metrics: Wan2.1 14B excels in perspective accuracy (CMM 0.5989 vs. 0.5501 and 0.4928) and dynamic degree (0.8883 vs. 0.7851 and 0.1347), while Hunyuan I2V achieves the best reconstruction fidelity (FVD 264, PSNR 9.40, SSIM 0.3537) and motion quality (MQ 0.7496). CogVideoX 1.5 5B, despite weaker reconstruction metrics, yields the highest composition aesthetic score (CA 0.7196), suggesting that lower-fidelity generation can sometimes produce more aesthetically pleasing compositional transitions. The RLHF ablation (Table 4) further shows that Flow-DPO training on top of Hunyuan I2V improves all three human performance dimensions by 2–3 percentage points without significantly degrading perspective accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant 2D cropping-based composition paradigm (saliency maps, learned crop templates, RL-based cropping) by introducing 3D perspective transformation as a composition mechanism, drawing on the practice of professional photographers adjusting camera position rather than post-hoc cropping. Technically, it assembles existing components—ViewCrafter for 3D reconstruction to synthesize training data, open-source I2V models (CogVideoX, Hunyuan, Wan2.1) for video generation, and Flow-DPO/VideoAlign for RLHF alignment—into a unified pipeline, with Qwen2-VL serving as the backbone for a novel perspective quality assessment model. The closest conceptual ancestor is the 'good view hunting' line of work on view-pair composition, but those methods still operate within the 2D image plane rather than recommending camera movements.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper carves out a genuinely new task formulation (perspective-based composition) and provides the first automated dataset construction pipeline, evaluation model (PQA), and generation system for it, which could seed a new research direction in computational photography. However, several open questions remain: the data scaling instability (performance degrades beyond ~40% of available data), the hard dependence on 3D reconstruction quality that caps video fidelity, and the limited video duration of current I2V models all constrain practical deployment. The modest RLHF gains and the finding that larger perspective angles (30°) significantly hurt accuracy suggest the approach is still in early stages and may benefit substantially from better reconstruction backends (e.g., UE5) and AR-based streaming video generation.
