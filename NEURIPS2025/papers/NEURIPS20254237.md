## Problem

Diffusion-based combinatorial optimization solvers suffer from rapid performance saturation when scaling denoising steps, and existing search-augmented approaches (e.g., T2T) rely on costly gradient updates during inference, creating a scalability bottleneck. This paper addresses the underexplored question of how to systematically scale inference-time computation for diffusion CO solvers along an orthogonal dimension beyond denoising step expansion, achieving stable per-cycle improvement with minimal per-cycle cost.

## Input / Output

The method takes a graph problem instance as input: 2D city coordinates for TSP, or undirected graph adjacency structure for MIS and Maximum Clique. During the flow process, discrete {0,1} solutions are relaxed to real-valued vectors in [0,1]^N, and the lightweight transformer operates exclusively on node embeddings (no explicit edge features). The output is a variable-confidence heatmap decoded via greedy selection into a feasible solution (tour, independent set, or clique), with optional 2Opt post-processing for TSP. The search operator can be run in parallel (8 operators in main experiments) to fully utilize GPU capacity.

## Method

GenSCO redefines diffusion generation as a search operator: each cycle performs solution disruption (randomized 2Opt for TSP, variable flipping for MIS/MCl), solution enhancement via a rectified flow ODE solved with 4 Euler steps, and greedy decoding with optional local search. The key architectural choice is a bias-rectified flow model that learns solution-to-solution transitions (suboptimal → optimal) rather than the conventional noise-to-solution paradigm, with the velocity field parameterized as v_θ = x_subopt − f_θ where f_θ directly predicts the optimal solution. Training uses a bias-rectified objective where the source distribution is generated by perturbing optimal solutions with problem-specific local search operators, shifting the optimization burden to training and enabling adaptive control over intermediate states. The architecture is a lightweight node-only transformer with adjacency matrix added to attention logits (Attention = softmax(QK^T/√d_k + λA)V), eliminating quadratic edge-feature computation and enabling hundreds to thousands of operator evaluations within practical time budgets.

## Results

On TSP-100, GenSCO(C=10) achieves 0.000% optimality gap in 6 seconds, a 141× speedup over LKH3 (14.1 min for the same gap). On TSP-500, GenSCO(C=40)2Opt reaches 0.012% gap in 18 s versus DIFUSCO's 0.87% in 19.1 min (72.5× gap reduction, 60.1× speedup). On MCl RB-Large (800–1200 nodes), GenSCO(C=1k) exceeds Gurobi's solution quality (−0.13% vs 0.00%) in 13.8 min versus Gurobi's 38.4 h (334× speedup). Scaling search cycles yields near-linear improvement, whereas increasing denoising steps in prior diffusion methods saturates after a few steps.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GenSCO decisively outperforms all prior neural solvers on TSP: at TSP-500, it achieves 0.012% gap in 18 s versus DIFUSCO's 0.87% in 19.1 min, T2T's 0.48% in 19.7 min, and Fast T2T's 0.21% in 6.9 min — a 72.5× gap reduction over DIFUSCO at 60× less time. Against the classical LKH3 solver, GenSCO reaches 0.000% gap on TSP-100 in 6 s versus LKH3's 14.1 min (141× speedup), and achieves ~10× speedup at the 0.02% gap level on TSP-500. On MIS (RB 200–300), GenSCO(C=8k) reaches 0.29% gap in 17.6 min, improving over DIFUSCO's 4.79% (16.5× gap reduction) and Fast T2T's 1.90% at comparable time. On MCl RB-Large, GenSCO(C=1k) exceeds Gurobi's solution quality (−0.13% vs 0.00%) in 13.8 min versus 38.4 h (334× speedup), and outperforms COExpander (0.90% gap) by an order of magnitude. The key differentiator is scaling behavior: GenSCO's search cycles yield near-linear improvement with computation, while denoising-step scaling in DIFUSCO/T2T saturates after a few steps, and gradient-search scaling in T2T remains bottlenecked by costly per-step inference.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper redefines the diffusion-based CO solving paradigm established by DIFUSCO [21] and T2T [9], shifting from a noise-to-solution generative process to a solution-to-solution rectified flow that learns transitions between suboptimal and optimal solutions. It builds on the rectified flow framework [68] to learn nearly straight ODE trajectories, but departs from prior diffusion CO solvers by replacing the heavy GNN architectures with a lightweight node-only transformer and by treating each generation as a search operator within a local search loop rather than a complete solving process. The disruption-reconstruction search idea from T2T [9] is retained, but the costly gradient-based reconstruction is replaced by cheap rectified flow sampling, enabling orders-of-magnitude more search cycles within the same compute budget.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GenSCO marks the first instance where a neural solver genuinely outperforms the decades-refined LKH3 heuristic on TSP (141× speedup to 0.000% gap on TSP-100), suggesting that scalable neural approaches can rival or surpass classical mathematical solvers in the high-accuracy regime. The key open question is whether the near-linear scaling of search cycles persists at larger problem sizes, where the paper acknowledges LKH3's steeper scaling slope eventually overtakes GenSCO. A promising direction identified is hybrid two-stage pipelines combining neural search with classical solvers, and improving robustness across diverse instance distributions (e.g., hard TSPLIB instances) remains an active challenge.
