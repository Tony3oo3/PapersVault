## Problem

Prior sample complexity bounds for denoising score matching in diffusion models exhibit polynomial dependence on the data dimension d, despite empirical evidence that diffusion models train efficiently in high dimensions. This paper establishes the first nearly dimension-free bounds (log log d dependence) by analyzing joint score estimation across all noise levels with a single function approximator, addressing the challenge that training data consists of dependent samples from Markov process trajectories rather than i.i.d. observations.

## Input / Output

The method takes m i.i.d. samples from a target distribution π over R^d and a finite function class H (e.g., quantized neural networks). Training data is generated by running each sample through the Ornstein-Uhlenbeck noising SDE, producing N timesteps of progressively noised observations per sample — these are dependent within each trajectory, which is the key analytical challenge. The output is a single learned score function f̂(t,x) that approximates ∇log p_t(x) uniformly across all noise levels t ∈ [0,T], which then drives the reverse SDE to generate new samples from π.

## Method

The core analysis introduces a novel martingale decomposition of the DSM error that exploits the Markov property of the noising process, enabling concentration bounds despite the dependent data structure. Sharp variance bounds are derived via second-order Tweedie-type formulae, showing the conditional variance depends only on the smoothness parameter L rather than dimension d. A time-regularity assumption (Hölder continuity in the time variable) bridges the ℓ∞ and ℓ2 norm terms arising from the martingale concentration, yielding the log log d dependence. Additionally, the paper proposes Bootstrapped Score Matching (BSM), a variance reduction technique where the learning target at noise level t combines the standard DSM target with the previously learned score at a lower noise level t′, reducing the target variance by an additional factor of the step size Δ compared to vanilla DSM.

## Results

The paper establishes the first nearly dimension-free sample complexity bound for denoising score matching: the number of samples needed scales as m ≳ (L+1)²/ε² · log(B|H|/δ) · NΔ, where the dimension d appears only through B = O(log((L+1)dmN/Δ)), yielding an effective log log(d) dependence. This represents a double-exponential improvement over prior polynomial-in-d bounds. Theorem 2 extends this to the expected L2 error with an additional κ² factor from a hypercontractivity assumption. The Bootstrapped Score Matching (BSM) method achieves an O(Δ) variance reduction over standard DSM at higher noise levels, as shown in Lemma 7.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is against prior theoretical sample complexity bounds for score matching. Block et al. [3] derive per-timestep bounds with at least linear dependence on d (since R = O(√d) in practice), and Gupta et al. [14] show m ≳ d·log(|H|/δ)/ε² per timestep, also with linear d-scaling. Fu et al. [12] show exponential dependence on d in the non-parametric worst case. Han et al. [16] study joint learning across timesteps via kernel regression but still exhibit polynomial d-dependence. In contrast, this paper's bounds depend on d only through log log(d) (modulo log|H|), closing the gap between theory and the empirical success of diffusion models in high dimensions. Empirically, Figure 1 validates the log log(d) scaling: a 2-layer network trained on N(0,Σ) with fixed hidden dimension shows a near-zero slope on a log-log plot of scaled L2 error versus d, whereas prior bounds would predict a positive slope consistent with linear d-dependence.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Denoising Score Matching (DSM) framework of Vincent [51] and the SDE-based diffusion model formulation of Song et al. [45] by providing the first nearly dimension-free sample complexity bounds for learning score functions jointly across noise levels. Prior analyses [3, 14, 12] bound the DSM error independently at each timestep via union bounds, yielding polynomial (or exponential in the non-parametric case) dependence on dimension d. The key departure here is a joint analysis across timesteps using a novel martingale decomposition that exploits the Markovian structure of the noising process, combined with sharp variance bounds from second-order Tweedie formulae, reducing the dimension dependence to log log d. The Bootstrapped Score Matching (BSM) algorithm further departs from standard DSM by constructing lower-variance learning targets that incorporate previously estimated scores at earlier noise levels.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work closes a significant gap between theoretical guarantees (which previously implied polynomial or exponential sample complexity in d) and the empirical success of diffusion models in high-dimensional settings, suggesting that mild smoothness and time-regularity assumptions are sufficient to avoid the curse of dimensionality. The martingale-based concentration tools for dependent data from Markov trajectories may be of independent interest beyond diffusion models. However, the empirical validation is limited to simple Gaussian distributions with small networks, and rigorous theoretical guarantees for BSM remain open. Extending the framework to flow-matching models and establishing formal performance bounds for the bootstrapping mechanism are natural next steps.
