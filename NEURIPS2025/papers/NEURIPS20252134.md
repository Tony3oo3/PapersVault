## Problem

Atmospheric data assimilation is fundamentally ill-posed because sparse observations cannot uniquely determine the high-dimensional state, forcing traditional methods to rely on empirically tuned Gaussian background priors. Existing score-based DA methods can learn complex priors via diffusion models but lack any mechanism to enforce domain-specific preferences—assimilation accuracy, downstream forecast skill, and physical consistency—without manual parameter tuning or hard constraints.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252134/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a 48-hour background field (produced by 8 auto-regressive 6-hour FengWu forecasts initialized from ERA5) and sparse observations (ERA5 truth with 99% random masking at 1.40625° resolution, 128×256 grid, 69 meteorological variables). It outputs a full 69-variable analysis field at the same spatial resolution, computed in a VAE-compressed latent space (69×32×64) and decoded back to physical space. FengWu serves a dual role: generating the frozen background for all methods and acting as the 48-hour forecast evaluator that produces the forecast-skill reward signal.

## Method

Align-DA operates in a VAEformer latent space where a background-conditioned diffusion model (stacked transformer blocks with cross-attention on z_b) learns the prior p(z|z_b), and observations are incorporated via either DPS guidance or Repaint masked composition during reverse sampling. The core novelty is a pluggable Diffusion-DPO alignment stage that refines the pretrained diffusion model using three complementary reward signals: latitude-weighted RMSE against ERA5 truth (assimilation accuracy), 48-hour FengWu forecast RMSE (forecast skill), and geostrophic wind balance deviation (physical adherence). Preference pairs are constructed by sampling N=32 candidates per batch and selecting top/bottom decile performers across all three rewards, yielding a 4,000-pair corpus fine-tuned with KL-regularized DPO (β=8000, 500 steps). This soft-constraint alignment narrows the posterior distribution without restrictive parametric assumptions, and the framework is agnostic to the underlying guidance method, consistently improving analysis and forecast metrics across ObsFree, Repaint, and DPS baselines.

## Results

Under 1% observation sparsity, multi-reward alignment (Align-M) consistently improves assimilation accuracy across all three guidance strategies: Align-ObsFree-M reduces overall MSE by 2.44% and z500 WRMSE by 7.94% over the unaligned baseline; Align-Repaint-M achieves 4.12% MSE reduction and 6.62% z500 WRMSE reduction; Align-DPS-M achieves 2.65% MSE and 5.70% z500 WRMSE reduction. For 48-hour forecast skill, the gains are amplified: Align-ObsFree-M improves forecast MSE by 6.12% and Align-Repaint-M by 5.12%. Against the traditional 3D-Var baseline, Align-DPS-M is marginally worse on DA accuracy (MSE 0.0593 vs. 0.0581) but superior on forecast skill (0.0943 vs. 0.0952) and substantially better on physical consistency (Geo-Score 94.89 vs. 88.34).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three baselines: Repaint (DiffDA, Huang et al. 2024), DPS (Chung et al. 2023), and traditional 3D-Var (Le Dimet & Talagrand 1986). Against Repaint, Align-Repaint-M wins on every metric, with the largest gain on z500 WRMSE (−6.62%) and MSE (−4.12%), confirming that multi-reward DPO alignment meaningfully tightens the posterior beyond what repainting alone achieves. Against DPS, Align-DPS-M similarly outperforms on all metrics (MSE −2.65%, z500 WRMSE −5.70%), though the relative gains are smaller because DPS already imposes stronger observational constraints, leaving less room for alignment to improve. Against 3D-Var, Align-DPS-M is marginally worse on DA accuracy (MSE 0.0593 vs. 0.0581, expected since 3D-Var explicitly minimizes this cost) but wins on 48-hour forecast skill (MSE 0.0943 vs. 0.0952) and substantially on physical consistency (Geo-Score 94.89 vs. 88.34, a 6.55-point improvement), demonstrating that preference alignment optimizes for the broader set of DA objectives that a single variational cost function cannot capture.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the score-based data assimilation (SDA) paradigm established by Rozet & Louppe (NeurIPS 2023) and DiffDA (Huang et al., ICML 2024), which learn a background-conditioned diffusion prior and incorporate observations via repainting or DPS guidance. Its key departure is borrowing the Diffusion-DPO alignment mechanism from the text-to-image literature (Wallace et al., CVPR 2024) and repurposing it with domain-specific atmospheric rewards (assimilation accuracy, 48-hour forecast skill, geostrophic balance) to refine the learned prior. Unlike prior SDA methods that rely on empirical tuning of background error covariances or hard physical constraints, Align-DA introduces a pluggable, soft-constraint DPO fine-tuning stage that operates in a VAE latent space, making it orthogonal to and composable with any existing observation-guidance strategy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that preference alignment—a technique proven in generative image models—can be transferred to atmospheric DA to simultaneously optimize multiple, often conflicting, objectives without manual tuning, offering a scalable path toward end-to-end AI weather systems. However, the gains are modest (2–7% relative error reduction), the framework operates in a learned latent space that may miss rare extreme events, and alignment benefits diminish as observational constraints strengthen (DPS leaves little room for further improvement). Open questions include whether the approach scales to higher-resolution or multi-physics settings, how reward design generalizes beyond geostrophic balance, and whether the latent-space bottleneck can be overcome without sacrificing the computational efficiency that motivates it.
