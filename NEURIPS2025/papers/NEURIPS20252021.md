## Problem

Existing driving scene generation methods produce either RGB video or LiDAR point clouds in isolation, or couple them through costly intermediate representations like occupancy grids that introduce information loss. This decoupling prevents the kind of tight cross-modal alignment needed for a faithful world model, while the reliance on coarse layout maps or generic captions limits semantic controllability and fine-grained scene realism.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252021/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a structured BEV scene layout (lane segments, human pose keypoints from YOLOv8x-Pose, and 3D vehicle bounding boxes) projected per-camera via calibrated intrinsics/extrinsics into view-specific semantic control maps, together with hierarchical scene captions (global context plus per-object grounded descriptions) produced by the DataCrafter VLM module. An optional first frame or noisy latent can be supplied for conditioning. The output is a temporally coherent 6-view RGB video (16 frames at 900×1600) and a spatially aligned LiDAR point cloud sequence, both generated jointly under shared semantic conditions.

## Method

Genesis employs a dual-branch architecture: a DiT-based spatiotemporal diffusion backbone with a 3D-VAE encoder for the camera branch, and a BEV-represented Swin Transformer autoencoder with NeRF-based rendering and spatial skipping for the LiDAR branch. Cross-modal consistency is enforced by feeding BEV features extracted from the camera branch's RGB outputs (via Lift-Splat-Shoot) as an additional conditional input to the LiDAR diffusion model, eliminating the need for occupancy grids. The DataCrafter module, built on a LoRA-fine-tuned Qwen2-VL 7B, generates structured scene-level and instance-level captions that are injected as global conditioning through cross-attention in both branches. Training follows a three-stage curriculum (image warm-up → multi-resolution video → joint video+LiDAR) on 64 H20 GPUs, with the LiDAR diffusion trained under a rectified flow schedule and the video branch initialized from MagicDriveDiT weights.

## Results

On nuScenes, Genesis achieves FVD 16.95 and FID 4.24 for multi-view video generation with first-frame conditioning, and FVD 83.10 / FID 14.90 without first-frame conditioning. For LiDAR sequence generation, it attains a Chamfer Distance of 0.611 at 1s (0.633 at 3s) using ground-truth image conditioning, and remains stable at 0.634/0.641 when using generated images. On downstream BEV segmentation, Genesis achieves the best mIoU of 38.01 and mAP of 27.90, and improves 3D detection (BEVFusion) by +0.91 mAP and +1.48 NDS when both camera and LiDAR synthetic data are added.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In video generation (Table 1), Genesis outperforms DriveDreamer-2 (FVD 105.10→83.10), MagicDriveDiT (94.84→83.10), and Drive-WM (122.70→83.10) in the unconditional setting, and beats DriveDreamer-2 (55.70→16.95) with first-frame conditioning; however, MiLA achieves a slightly lower FID (3.00 vs. 4.24) though Genesis has a lower FVD (16.95 vs. 18.20). In the noisy-latent setting, Genesis edges out UniScene on FVD (67.87 vs. 70.52) but is marginally worse on FID (6.45 vs. 6.12). For LiDAR generation (Table 2), Genesis substantially outperforms HERMES (Chamfer@1s 0.78→0.611, a 21% reduction), ViDAR (1.12→0.611), and 4D-Occ (1.13→0.611), with the gap widening to 45% at the 3s horizon (1.17→0.633). On domain-gap BEV segmentation (Table 5), Genesis achieves the best mIoU (38.01) and mAP (27.90), surpassing Cogen (37.80/27.88), DiVE (35.96/24.55), and all MagicDrive variants (~18 mIoU). For downstream 3D detection (Table 6), joint camera+LiDAR synthetic data yields the largest gain (+0.91 mAP, +1.48 NDS) over the BEVFusion baseline, confirming the complementary value of multimodal generation.

## Relation to Prior Work

Genesis extends the DiT-based video generation lineage (MagicDriveDiT, DriveDreamer-2) and the LiDAR diffusion line (ViDAR, HERMES) into a single unified architecture that jointly synthesizes multi-view video and LiDAR point clouds. It departs from prior unimodal approaches by coupling both branches through shared semantic conditions (DataCrafter captions, BEV layouts) and a cross-modal BEV feature pathway (via LSS projection of generated images), explicitly avoiding the occupancy-grid intermediary used by UniScene. The LiDAR decoder borrows NeRF-style rendering and a Swin-based autoencoder, while the video backbone reuses the MagicDriveDiT DiT and CogVideo-XL VAE, positioning Genesis as a systems-level integration rather than a single architectural novelty.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that a single conditioning pipeline can drive both video and LiDAR generation with consistent geometry and semantics, Genesis provides a practical template for building multimodal world models without costly occupancy supervision. The stability of LiDAR quality when conditioned on generated (rather than ground-truth) images (Chamfer 0.634 vs. 0.611) is practically important for closed-loop simulation. However, the framework is evaluated only on nuScenes, requires 64 H20 GPUs, and offers no real-time or interactive generation, leaving open questions about scalability to other sensor suites, domains (robotics, aerial), and compute-efficient deployment.
