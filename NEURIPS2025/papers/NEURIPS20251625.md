## Problem

The bisimulation metric (BSM) is a well-established pseudometric for quantifying state similarity within a single MDP, but its extension to pairs of MDPs—needed for policy transfer, cross-MDP aggregation, and multi-task analysis—has lacked rigorous metric properties. Prior multi-MDP formulations either lost the triangle inequality or produced loose bounds (e.g., fixed total-variation distance of one under disjoint-union constructions), preventing clean theoretical analysis and yielding only asymptotic sample complexity for estimation.

## Input / Output

The method takes two finite MDPs M₁ = ⟨S₁, A, P₁, R₁, γ⟩ and M₂ = ⟨S₂, A, P₂, R₂, γ⟩ (sharing the same action space and discount factor) and computes a state-pair distance d¹⁻²(s, s′) for every (s, s′) ∈ S₁ × S₂ via a fixed-point iteration. Beyond the metric itself, the framework produces explicit closed-form regret bounds for policy transfer, aggregation error bounds, and—unlike prior BSM-based results—a closed-form sample complexity K ≥ −ln(α/2)·γ²R̄²|S|² / (2ε²(1−γ)⁴) for estimation error below ε with probability 1−α, rather than the asymptotic expressions in Ferns et al. [14].

## Method

GBSM is defined as the unique fixed point of the operator F(d|s,s′) = max_a {|R₁(s,a) − R₂(s′,a)| + γ·W₁(P₁(·|s,a), P₂(·|s′,a); d)}, where W₁ is the 1-Wasserstein distance between the two MDPs' transition distributions under cost function d. The paper proves three metric properties via induction and the Knaster-Tarski fixed-point theorem: (1) symmetry d¹⁻²(s,s′) = d²⁻¹(s′,s), (2) an inter-MDP triangle inequality d¹⁻²(s,s′) ≤ d¹⁻³(s,s″) + d³⁻²(s″,s′) whose proof hinges on the Gluing Lemma for optimal transport plans, and (3) a distance bound on identical state spaces expressed in terms of total variation distance. These properties enable strictly tighter bounds than BSM: the policy-transfer regret scales as 2/(1−γ)·max d¹⁻² rather than requiring a disjoint-union construction, the VFA bound improves from 2σ̃/(1−γ) to σ̃/(1−γ), and the SSA aggregation error tightens from 2σ̃(2+γ)/(1−γ) to 2σ̃/(1−γ). The framework extends naturally to lax GBSM (different action spaces via Hausdorff metric) and on-policy GBSM (non-optimal policies).

## Results

The paper validates its theoretical bounds on random Garnet MDPs (|S|=20, |A|=5, 50% branching factor) across 100 independent trials for each γ∈{0.1,…,0.9}. The GBSM-based policy transfer regret bound (Theorem 4.1) consistently upper-bounds the ground-truth regret computed via tabular Q-learning, while the empirical MDP-distance metric from Song et al. [17] fails to bound the regret in a substantial fraction of trials. For state similarity approximation (SSA) and value function approximation (VFA), the GBSM-derived error bounds are visibly and consistently tighter than their BSM-based counterparts across all discount factors, with the gap widening as γ increases toward 0.9.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Bisimulation Metric (BSM) of Ferns et al. (2004, 2006) from single-MDP state similarity to a rigorous multi-MDP formulation. Prior attempts at multi-MDP BSM (Castro & Precup 2010; Song et al. 2016) modified the definition without preserving metric properties, and Phillips (2006) used a disjoint-union construction that forced total variation distance to one. The key departure is proving that the natural two-MDP extension retains symmetry, an inter-MDP triangle inequality, and a distance bound on shared state spaces—properties previously believed to be lost—thereby enabling tighter policy transfer, aggregation, and estimation bounds than those derived from BSM or the disjoint-union approach.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This provides the first rigorous metric-theoretic foundation for comparing states across different MDPs, which is essential for policy transfer, sim-to-real gap analysis, and multi-task RL where multiple MDPs must be related. The closed-form sample complexity (vs. the asymptotic result of Ferns et al. 2011) and strictly tighter error bounds make the theory directly actionable for practitioners. A key limitation is the reliance on discounted rewards: all bounds scale with 1/(1−γ), rendering them vacuous as γ→1, so extending GBSM to average-reward MDPs remains an open and practically important direction.
