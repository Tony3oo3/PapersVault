## Problem

Diffusion Transformers (DiT) suffer from quadratic self-attention complexity that scales with token count, creating a major computational bottleneck especially at high resolutions. While prior work has focused on accelerating the sampling process (fewer steps, distillation), the architectural inefficiency of DiT's uniform token-level self-attention across all layers and timesteps remains largely unaddressed. The paper identifies that different layers and denoising stages have fundamentally different token density requirements, yet no prior method exploits this structure in a DiT-specific way.

## Input / Output

The method operates on token representations within a DiT backbone, taking class labels (ImageNet), text prompts (PixArt-α), or Gaussian noise as conditioning signals and producing generated images at 256×256 or 512×512 resolution, or 16-frame video clips at 256×256. Internally, dense tokens of shape N×C are spatially reshaped (e.g., H×W×C) and adaptively pooled to M×C sparse tokens (M≪N) within each SDTM, then restored back to full resolution before the top layers. The pruning rate r=1−M/N is dynamically modulated per timestep, so the effective token count varies across the denoising trajectory.

## Method

SparseDiT restructures the DiT transformer stack into three segments: bottom layers use Poolingformers (replacing self-attention with global average pooling on values, justified by near-uniform attention maps in early layers), middle layers contain cascaded SparseDense Token Modules (SDTMs) that alternate between sparse-token transformers for global structure and dense-token transformers for local detail, and top layers retain standard dense transformers for high-frequency refinement. Within each SDTM, sparse tokens are generated via spatial pooling followed by cross-attention with full dense tokens, processed by sparse transformers, then restored via upsampling, linear combination, and reverse cross-attention. A timestep-wise pruning strategy linearly increases token density from early (high-pruning, low-frequency) to late (low-pruning, high-frequency) denoising stages, with the first T/4 steps held at minimum pruning. The entire architecture is fine-tuned from pre-trained DiT checkpoints (~6% of from-scratch training cost), with sparse/dense token conversion reusing off-the-shelf transformer blocks and only two small linear layers initialized from scratch.

## Results

On ImageNet 512×512, SparseDiT-XL with pruning rate r∈[0.90,0.96] achieves a 55% reduction in FLOPs (235G vs. 525G) and a 175% improvement in inference speed (0.685 vs. 0.249 img/s) with only a 0.09 FID increase (3.13 vs. 3.04). At a moderate pruning rate r∈[0.61,0.86], SparseDiT-XL reduces FLOPs by 46% while actually improving FID to 2.96 and boosting throughput by 145%. On video generation (Latte-XL), SparseDiT achieves a 56% FLOPs reduction with competitive FVD scores across four datasets, and on text-to-image (PixArt-α), it delivers a 69% speed improvement with a 0.24 FID improvement (4.29 vs. 4.53).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ToMeSD [5], SparseDiT is dramatically superior: on DiT-XL, ToMeSD yields FID 14.74 at +66% speed, while SparseDiT achieves FID 2.38 at +87% speed; on DiT-B the gap is even wider (FID 29.24 vs. 8.23). Against DyDiT [68] on ImageNet 512×512, SparseDiT achieves a 46% FLOPs reduction and 145% speed-up versus DyDiT's 29% and 31%, at the cost of a 0.08 higher FID (2.96 vs. 2.88). Against TokenCache [29] on 256×256, SparseDiT delivers 87% speed gain versus TokenCache's 32%, with a comparable FID increase (0.11 vs. 0.13). Against Ditfastattn [66] on 512×512, SparseDiT achieves both higher speed (145% vs. 98%) and substantially lower FID (2.96 vs. 4.52). Against the original DiT-XL baseline, SparseDiT at moderate pruning (r∈[0.44,0.61]) actually matches or slightly exceeds baseline FID (2.23 vs. 2.27) while cutting FLOPs by 25%, and at aggressive pruning (r∈[0.90,0.96]) maintains FID within 0.09 of baseline while halving FLOPs.

## Relation to Prior Work

SparseDiT builds directly on the DiT architecture (Peebles & Xie, ICCV 2023) and its video/text-to-image extensions (Latte, PixArt-α), fine-tuning pre-trained checkpoints rather than training from scratch. It departs from prior token-reduction approaches for diffusion models (ToMeSD, DyDiT, TokenCache, DiTFastAttn) by replacing generic per-layer token merging or multi-dimensional pruning with a structured tri-segment design—Poolingformer, SparseDense Token Modules, and dense transformers—motivated by empirical analysis of attention variance across layers. The Poolingformer component is explicitly framed as a special case of the MetaFormer family (Yu et al., CVPR 2022), while the timestep-wise pruning strategy introduces a temporal dimension of token density that prior DiT-efficiency work has not exploited.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 55% FLOPs reduction with only a 0.09 FID increase at 512×512, combined with seamless compatibility with efficient samplers (DDIM, Rectified Flow), makes SparseDiT a practically deployable efficiency layer for existing DiT-based pipelines without requiring retraining from scratch. The key limitation is the manually pre-defined architecture (number of layers per segment, sparse token count), which restricts adaptability to new model sizes or resolutions without manual redesign. Open questions include whether the segment boundaries and pruning schedule can be learned end-to-end, whether the approach extends to autoregressive or interactive generation settings, and how it behaves at substantially higher resolutions or longer video sequences where token counts grow further.
