## Problem

Efficient language models with linear or hybrid attention achieve O(n) complexity but still fall significantly behind SOTA full-attention models on challenging benchmarks like MMLU, math reasoning, retrieval, and long-context tasks. Moreover, designing new LM architectures from scratch is prohibitively expensive and risky, making it difficult for researchers outside major organizations to innovate. This paper addresses both gaps by introducing a post-training architecture search pipeline that can rapidly adapt pre-trained full-attention models into efficient hybrid architectures without sacrificing accuracy.

## Input / Output

The model consumes standard text token sequences (vocabulary size 151,643) and autoregressively generates text tokens, supporting context lengths up to 256K. A key practical distinction is the dramatically reduced KV cache: Jet-Nemotron-2B requires only 154 MB at 64K context versus 7,168 MB for Qwen3-1.7B-Base, enabling 128× larger batch sizes on a single H100 GPU and up to 53.6× higher decoding throughput. The architecture heterogeneously assigns three attention types (full attention, sliding window attention, and JetBlock) to different layers, so the internal computation graph varies by position in the stack.

## Method

PostNAS begins from a pre-trained full-attention model (Qwen2.5) and freezes all MLP weights, then performs a coarse-to-fine search over attention block designs in four stages: (1) training a once-for-all super network with feature distillation and running beam search to learn task-specific full-attention layer placement, (2) systematically evaluating six SOTA linear attention blocks to select Gated DeltaNet, (3) designing JetBlock, which adds a kernel generator that produces input-conditioned dynamic convolution kernels applied to V tokens while removing redundant static convolutions on Q/K, and (4) hardware-aware grid search over key/value dimensions and head count using generation throughput (not parameter count) as the optimization target. The critical insight is that KV cache size, not parameter count, is the dominant factor for long-context decoding throughput, since the decoding stage is memory-bandwidth-bound; fixing the KV cache size allows trading parameter count for accuracy at no throughput cost. Training proceeds in two stages: 50B tokens with frozen MLPs and distillation loss, followed by 350B tokens of full-model training with additional math and coding data.

## Results

Jet-Nemotron-2B achieves 60.8 MMLU, 39.0 MMLU-Pro, and 58.3 BBH accuracy while delivering 2,885 token/s generation throughput on a single H100 GPU (64K context), a 47× speedup over Qwen3-1.7B-Base (61 token/s). At 256K context length, Jet-Nemotron-2B achieves a 6.14× prefilling speedup and 53.6× decoding speedup over Qwen3-1.7B-Base. Jet-Nemotron-4B further improves to 65.2 MMLU, 44.2 MMLU-Pro, and 65.0 BBH at 1,271 token/s, still maintaining a 21× throughput advantage over Qwen3-1.7B-Base. On math tasks, Jet-Nemotron-2B reaches 49.6 average accuracy (76.2 GSM8K, 23.3 MATH), surpassing Qwen3-1.7B-Base by 6.3 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Jet-Nemotron-2B outperforms all full-attention baselines on MMLU, MMLU-Pro, and BBH while achieving 47× higher throughput than Qwen3-1.7B-Base (2,885 vs. 61 tok/s) and 12× higher than Qwen2.5-1.5B (241 tok/s). Against the larger MoE models DeepSeek-V3-Small (2.2B activated / 15B total) and Moonlight, Jet-Nemotron-2B achieves higher MMLU (60.8 vs. 53.3 and 60.4) and MMLU-Pro (39.0 vs. 28.1) despite having fewer total parameters. Among pure linear-attention models, Jet-Nemotron-2B substantially surpasses Mamba2-2.7B (60.8 vs. 25.1 MMLU), RWKV7-1.5B (60.8 vs. 41.0), and Rec.Gemma-2B (60.8 vs. 28.6), closing the accuracy gap that has plagued O(n) architectures. Against prior hybrid models, Jet-Nemotron-2B leads Gemma3n-E2B by 6.9 MMLU points (60.8 vs. 53.9) and 14.7 MMLU-Pro points (39.0 vs. 24.3), while Hymba-1.5B and Zamba2-1.2B trail by 11.1 and 17.7 MMLU points respectively. On math tasks (Table 4), Jet-Nemotron-2B achieves 49.6 average accuracy, beating Qwen3-1.7B-Base (42.3) by 6.3 points and all other baselines by wide margins, with 76.2 on GSM8K and 62.7 on MMLU-Stem.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on three distinct lines of work: (1) pre-trained full-attention models (Qwen2.5) whose MLP weights are inherited and frozen, (2) linear attention mechanisms (Gated DeltaNet, GLA, Mamba2, RWKV7) that reduce attention complexity from O(n²) to O(n), and (3) once-for-all super-network NAS (Cai et al., 2019) adapted here for attention layer placement. It departs from prior efficient LM architectures (Mamba2, RWKV7, Jamba, Zamba2, Hymba) in a key way: rather than training from scratch or using uniform full-attention placement, PostNAS starts from a pre-trained model and learns task-specific layer placement via a super network, then iteratively refines the linear attention block and hardware hyperparameters. The JetBlock's dynamic convolution on V tokens also distinguishes it from prior linear attention blocks that rely solely on static convolutions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 47× generation throughput improvement while matching or exceeding Qwen3-1.7B-Base accuracy across six benchmark domains is a practically significant efficiency win for deployment, and the PostNAS pipeline offers a reusable, low-cost framework for architecture exploration that reduces the risk and compute barrier for smaller research groups. The finding that KV cache size—not parameter count—is the dominant factor for decoding throughput provides a concrete design principle that challenges the conventional parameter-budget-driven approach. Open questions include whether the learned layer placement generalizes across different base models and scales, whether the from-scratch suboptimality acknowledged by the authors limits long-term architectural value, and whether the approach extends to multimodal or much larger (100B+) models.
