## Problem

Existing WSI survival prediction pipelines require dividing gigapixel images into thousands of patches, training dedicated TME segmentation models with extensive pathologist annotations, and treating segmentation and survival prediction as independent tasks. This is computationally expensive, annotation-heavy, and ignores the fact that survival signals could guide more prognostically meaningful TME delineation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251910/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on 50× down-sampled WSIs (matching the resolution of pre-computed TIL maps) rather than full-resolution gigapixel images, drastically reducing memory and compute. Only the lymphocyte class has pixel-level ground truth (from the TIL map); tumor and stroma are segmented entirely zero-shot. The model jointly outputs a TME segmentation mask (lymphocyte, tumor, stroma) and a survival risk score via a Cox proportional hazards formulation, trained end-to-end with a combined focal+dice segmentation loss and NLL survival loss.

## Method

ZTSurv leverages the PLIP pathology-language foundation model with learnable vision prompts and GPT-4-generated class-specific text descriptions to perform zero-shot pixel-level segmentation of tumor and stroma using only the lymphocyte TIL map as supervision. Segmentation results are converted into a heterogeneous graph where nodes are tissue patches (typed by dominant class) with concatenated visual (PLIP encoder) and semantic (PLIP text encoder) features, and edges are k-NN connections weighted by Pearson correlation coefficients as continuous attributes. A type-aware heterogeneous graph attention mechanism uses node-type-specific projection matrices and edge-attribute modulation to capture spatial interactions among TME components. The entire pipeline is trained end-to-end so that the survival NLL loss backpropagates through the graph into the segmentation decoder, enabling survival-guided refinement of TME boundaries without any additional annotations.

## Results

ZTSurv achieves an overall C-index of 0.661 across four TCGA cancer cohorts (BRCA, UCEC, LUAD, BLCA), outperforming the best TME-aware baseline ProtoSurv (0.649) by 1.2% and the best classical patch-based method DeepAttnMISL (0.609) by 5.2%. On individual datasets, ZTSurv leads on BRCA (0.642), UCEC (0.726), and BLCA (0.637), while ProtoSurv edges it slightly on LUAD (0.638 vs. 0.637). In terms of efficiency, ZTSurv reduces average memory usage per WSI by approximately 3,000× and inference time by ~6× compared to patch-based methods, by operating directly on 50× down-sampled WSIs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ZTSurv outperforms all six classical patch-based methods (CLAM-SB, CLAM-MB, Co-Pilot, DeepAttnMISL, DSMIL, WSISA) by 1.8%–4.8% in overall C-index, and also surpasses three homogeneous graph-based methods (DeepGraphSurv, HGSurvNet, PatchGCN) by 3.6%–6.4%. Against the three most competitive TME-aware heterogeneous graph baselines, ZTSurv achieves the highest C-index on 3 of 4 datasets: it beats ProtoSurv (0.649) by 1.2% overall, TMEGL (0.645) by 1.6%, and H2GT (0.635) by 2.6%, with the sole exception being LUAD where ProtoSurv edges ahead by 0.001 (0.638 vs. 0.637). For zero-shot TME segmentation (Table 2), ZTSurv achieves an overall mIoU of 0.571, surpassing ZegFormer (0.531), ZegCLIP (0.541), and TagCLIP (0.541) by 2–4%. In the survival prediction comparison with alternative zero-shot classifiers (Appendix H.3), ZTSurv consistently outperforms CONCH, PLIP, and a fine-tuned UNI classifier across all four cohorts, confirming that the survival-guided end-to-end training improves both segmentation quality and downstream prediction.

## Relation to Prior Work

ZTSurv carves new ground by unifying three previously decoupled stages—TME segmentation, graph construction, and survival prediction—into a single end-to-end pipeline on low-resolution WSIs. It repurposes the SegViT-style mask decoder and PLIP vision-language backbone (originally designed for general zero-shot segmentation) for pathology-specific TME delineation, using TIL maps as the sole supervision signal to segment tumor and stroma without pathologist annotations. The heterogeneous graph formulation with type-specific attention and continuous edge attributes extends prior TME-aware graph methods (TMEGL, ProtoSurv, H2GT) by incorporating semantic text embeddings alongside visual features at the node level, and by letting the survival loss jointly guide the segmentation process rather than treating it as a fixed preprocessing step.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in eliminating the labor-intensive pathologist annotation pipeline for TME segmentation and reducing memory usage by ~3,000× through 50× down-sampling, making WSI-based survival analysis far more deployable in clinical settings. However, the method is currently limited to three TME components (lymphocyte, tumor, stroma) and four TCGA cancer cohorts, and its zero-shot segmentation mIoU (~0.57) remains moderate, raising questions about robustness to more diverse tissue types (vessels, necrosis, fibroblasts) and to cancer types where TIL maps are unavailable. The modest C-index gains over strong TME-aware baselines (0.661 vs. 0.649) suggest that the end-to-end survival-guided segmentation provides a real but incremental signal, and whether this advantage persists at larger scale or with additional TME components remains an open question.
