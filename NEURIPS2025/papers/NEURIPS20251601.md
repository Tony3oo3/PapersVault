## Problem

Scaling CLIP training to worldwide (non-English) image-text pairs faces two unresolved barriers: no principled data curation method exists for non-English data at scale, and multilingual CLIP models consistently underperform their English-only counterparts on English benchmarks (the 'curse of multilinguality'). The paper demonstrates that this curse is not inherent to multilingual learning but is a consequence of insufficient joint scaling of metadata, curation, and model capacity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251601/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes RGB images at 224×224 resolution and multilingual alt-texts spanning 300+ languages, tokenized with the XLM-V multilingual tokenizer (900k vocabulary). It produces aligned image and text embeddings in a shared contrastive space, where the vision encoder is a ViT-H/14 and the text encoder is a standard transformer. The embeddings support zero-shot classification, bidirectional image-text retrieval, and serve as frozen vision encoders for downstream MLLMs.

## Method

Meta CLIP 2 introduces a three-part worldwide scaling recipe built on the OpenAI/Meta CLIP architecture with minimal changes. First, it constructs per-language metadata from multilingual WordNet (31 languages) and Wikipedia (329 languages), maintaining independent metadata sets per language. Second, it scales the curation algorithm language-by-language using a key insight: a single global threshold t is suboptimal, so it derives language-specific thresholds t_lang by preserving the same tail-concept proportion (6%) across all languages via t_to_p and p_to_t functions. Third, it scales seen training pairs proportionally to the added non-English data (2.3×, keeping English seen pairs unchanged) and identifies ViT-H/14 as the minimal viable model capacity to break the curse of multilinguality, where smaller models like ViT-L/14 still suffer from cross-lingual gradient conflicts. The result is mutual benefit: non-English data improves English ImageNet accuracy from 80.5% to 81.3%, and English data improves multilingual retrieval performance.

## Results

Meta CLIP 2 (ViT-H/14, worldwide data, 29B seen pairs) achieves 81.3% zero-shot ImageNet top-1 accuracy, surpassing its English-only counterpart (80.5%) by 0.8% and mSigLIP (80.6%) by 0.7%, while using only 72% of the seen pairs and lower resolution (224px vs. 256px). On multilingual benchmarks, it sets new state-of-the-art: 50.2% on Babel-ImageNet, 64.3% on XM3600 image-to-text retrieval, and 57.4% on CVQA local-language accuracy. The model also leads on SLIP 26 (74.5%) and DataComp 37 (69.6%) English benchmarks, outperforming both mSigLIP and SigLIP 2.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against mSigLIP (SO400M, 40B seen pairs, 256px), Meta CLIP 2 wins on every reported benchmark: +0.7% on ImageNet, +5.4% on SLIP 26, +4.1% on DataComp 37, +3.8% on Babel-ImageNet, +1.5% on XM3600 I→T, and +7.6% on CVQA local-language accuracy, while using fewer seen pairs (29B vs. 40B) and lower resolution. Against SigLIP 2 (SO400M, 40B seen pairs, 256px), Meta CLIP 2 trails by 1.9% on ImageNet (81.3 vs. 83.2) but leads on SLIP 26 (+0.8%), DataComp 37 (+0.2%), and dominates on all multilingual benchmarks: +9.4% on Babel-ImageNet, +4.6% on XM3600 I→T, and +8.4% on CVQA local. Against the English-only Meta CLIP (H/14, 13B seen pairs), Meta CLIP 2 improves ImageNet by +0.8%, SLIP 26 by +2.1%, and DataComp 37 by +3.1%, demonstrating that non-English data provides mutual benefit rather than the expected trade-off. Against XLM-CLIP (H/14, LAION-5B), Meta CLIP 2 leads by +4.3% on ImageNet and +16.2% on Babel-ImageNet, reflecting the advantage of principled worldwide curation over distillation-based filtering.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Meta CLIP's English-only curation algorithm (substring matching + head/tail balancing against WordNet/Wikipedia metadata) to a worldwide setting by constructing per-language metadata across 300+ languages and deriving language-specific balancing thresholds. It deliberately departs from prior multilingual CLIP efforts (mSigLIP, SigLIP 2, M-CLIP, mCLIP) that rely on distillation from English CLIP, machine translation, or private data sources like WebLI, instead training from scratch on native worldwide image-text pairs with a transparent, model-free curation pipeline. The architecture and training setup are kept maximally aligned with OpenAI CLIP to isolate the effect of worldwide data and scaling, in contrast to system-level works that combine multiple confounding changes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first complete, open-sourced recipe for training CLIP from scratch on worldwide web-scale data, which is critical as English internet content is projected to be exhausted. The finding that the 'curse of multilinguality' is a capacity and curation artifact rather than an inherent trade-off reframes how the community approaches multilingual vision-language pretraining, and the mutual benefit between English and non-English data has direct implications for MLLMs, SSL, and image generation pipelines. Open questions remain around evaluation: current multilingual benchmarks are still biased toward North America and Western Europe, and the paper acknowledges that truly comprehensive worldwide evaluation infrastructure does not yet exist. The practical threshold that ViT-H/14 (not ViT-L/14) is the minimal viable capacity for breaking the curse provides concrete guidance for practitioners choosing model sizes.
