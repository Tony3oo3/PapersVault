## Problem

Learning-augmented algorithms for online bipartite matching aim to balance robustness (worst-case performance regardless of advice quality) and consistency (performance when advice is accurate). Prior learning-augmented algorithms for AdWords and Display Ads (MNS07, MNS12, SE23) fail to Pareto-dominate the naive COINFLIP baseline across the full robustness spectrum. This paper asks whether a learning-augmented algorithm for online bipartite fractional matching can dominate COINFLIP for every value of the robustness parameter.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250262/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The algorithm receives a bipartite graph with weighted offline vertices known in advance and online vertices arriving sequentially; upon each arrival, the vertex's neighborhood is revealed along with fractional advice values for its incident edges (a feasible fractional matching in hindsight, revealed incrementally). A single tradeoff parameter λ ∈ [0,1] controls the degree of advice-following: at λ=1 the algorithm blindly follows the advice, while at λ=0 it reduces to the advice-free BALANCE algorithm. The output is a fractional matching assigning edge values x_{u,v} ∈ [0,1] such that each online vertex sends at most 1 unit total and each offline vertex receives at most 1 unit total.

## Method

The paper proposes two algorithms analyzed via primal-dual techniques. LEARNINGAUGMENTEDBALANCE (LAB) for the vertex-weighted setting modifies BALANCE's convex penalty function g(z)=e^{z−1} into an advice-dependent penalty f(A,X) built from two component functions f_0 and f_1 (the latter involving the Lambert W function), which is increasing in the current allocation X and decreasing in the cumulative advice allocation A, thereby rewarding vertices favored by the advice while still penalizing over-allocation. PUSHANDWATERFILL (PAW) for the unweighted setting with integral advice operates in two phases per arrival: first pushing flow into the advised offline vertex until its level reaches λ, then distributing remaining flow via standard waterfilling; its analysis uses a novel 'splitting function' g that partitions each infinitesimal dual update between α and β variables. Both algorithms are shown to Pareto-dominate COINFLIP across the full robustness range, and the paper further establishes a matching hardness bound via two adaptive adversaries and a factor-revealing LP.

## Results

The paper establishes that LAB (vertex-weighted) and PAW (unweighted) Pareto-dominate the COINFLIP baseline across the entire robustness-consistency tradeoff. Specifically, LAB achieves r(λ) = 1 − e^(λ−1) − (e^(λ−1) − λ)ln(1 − λe^(1−λ)) − λ(1−λ) robustness and c(λ) = 1 + λ − e^(λ−1) consistency, while PAW achieves r(λ) = 1 − (1 − λ + λ²/2)e^(λ−1) and c(λ) = 1 − (1−λ)e^(λ−1). Under perfect advice (λ=1), both algorithms attain a competitive ratio of 1. In experiments on ER(300,0.2), UT(300), and real-world graphs (socfb Caltech36, econ-beause), the competitive ratio degrades smoothly from 1 as the noise parameter γ increases, with BALANCE (achieving 1−1/e ≈ 0.632) eventually outperforming both learning-augmented algorithms at high noise levels.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against BALANCE (Buchbinder et al., ESA 2007), which serves as the advice-free baseline achieving a 1−1/e ≈ 0.632 competitive ratio, LAB and PAW strictly outperform it whenever the advice quality is sufficiently high (small noise γ), achieving competitive ratios approaching 1 under perfect advice; however, for sufficiently large γ, BALANCE's constant guarantee surpasses both learning-augmented algorithms, with the crossover point depending on the graph instance. Against COINFLIP (Jin & Ma, NeurIPS 2022), which randomly mixes the advice-following and advice-free strategies, both LAB and PAW Pareto-dominate across the full robustness spectrum—COINFLIP's tradeoff is a straight line between (1−1/e, 1−1/e) and (0,1) in the vertex-weighted case (or the point (1/2,1) in the unweighted case), while LAB and PAW achieve strictly higher robustness at every consistency level. Against Mahdian et al. (EC 2007 / TALG 2012), whose AdWords algorithm with advice only outperforms COINFLIP over part of the robustness range, LAB extends to AdWords under the small bids assumption and dominates COINFLIP across the entire range, yielding a significant improvement. Against Spaeh & Ene (NeurIPS 2023), which generalizes the MNS approach to Display Ads and the generalized assignment problem but still fails to dominate COINFLIP across the full spectrum, LAB provides the first algorithm that achieves full Pareto-dominance for the core bipartite matching problem.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the learning-augmented online matching line initiated by Mahdian et al. (EC 2007, TALG 2012) and Spaeh & Ene (NeurIPS 2023), which studied advice-based algorithms for AdWords and Display Ads but only dominated the naive COINFLIP baseline over part of the robustness range. The core algorithmic building block is the BALANCE/WATERFILLING primal-dual framework of Buchbinder et al. (ESA 2007), which the authors modify with an advice-dependent penalty function (LAB) or a two-phase push-then-waterfill procedure (PAW). The AdWords extension leverages the fractional-to-integral reduction of Feng & Niazadeh (Management Science 2024), and the hardness construction adapts the classical upper-triangular worst-case instances of Karp, Vazirani, and Vazirani (STOC 1990) into a pair of adaptive adversaries.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first result to Pareto-dominate COINFLIP across the entire robustness spectrum for online bipartite fractional matching, resolving a natural open question posed by prior work. The improved hardness bound (via a factor-revealing LP on a two-adversary construction) narrows the gap between achievable and impossible tradeoffs. Key open questions include: unifying the LAB (fractional advice, vertex-weighted) and PAW (integral advice, unweighted) analyses into a single framework, closing the remaining gap between algorithmic guarantees and impossibility bounds, and explaining the empirically observed critical noise level γ* at which all λ-parameterized runs converge to BALANCE performance regardless of the tradeoff parameter.
