## Problem

Reinforcement learning via GRPO has been successfully applied to diffusion and autoregressive text-to-image models, but masked generative models (MGMs) remain unexplored in this context. The central challenge is that MGMs predict all masked tokens in parallel at each iteration, making the natural transition probability definition from AR or diffusion models inapplicable and leading to poor performance when naively transferred.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253616/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a free-form text prompt (short or long) as input and produces a single RGB image via iterative token unmasking over T steps (default T=50). The base model Show-o (1.3B) represents images as sequences of discrete VQ tokens; during RL training, a group of G=6 images is generated per prompt, and CLIP scores between prompt and each generated image serve as the reward signal. Evaluation uses GenEval (zero-shot, no GenEval-style prompts in training data) and MSCOCO-30K FID.

## Method

Mask-GRPO formulates the iterative unmasking process of MGMs as a T-step Markov decision process, where each state is the current token sequence plus prompt, and each action is the next token sequence. The key insight is redefining the transition probability to focus on newly unmasked tokens (those selected by the Choose-and-Move operation) rather than all masked tokens, yielding two candidate formulations: p_θ1 (product over newly unmasked tokens times a probabilistic term for remasked tokens) and p_θ2 (product over newly unmasked tokens only). GRPO is then applied with group-normalized advantages over G sampled images per prompt. Three enhancement strategies further improve performance: removing the KL divergence constraint (beneficial for the 1.3B model), an unmasking reduction strategy that trains with fewer iterations (T=20 vs. 50) while evaluating at full resolution, and a dynamic sample filtering mechanism that resamples groups whose reward standard deviation falls below the 10th percentile of historical values to address the 'Vanishing Samples' instability problem.

## Results

On GenEval, Show-o+Mask-GRPO (1.3B) achieves an overall score of 0.73, a substantial improvement over the Show-o base model (0.53) and matching or surpassing all state-of-the-art T2I models including SD3.5-L (8B, 0.71), DALLE-3 (0.67), and Infinity (+Rewriter, 0.73). On MSCOCO-30K FID, Mask-GRPO reduces the score from 9.24 (Show-o) to 8.32, approaching PixArt-alpha (7.32) and Janus (7.11). On ImageReward, the method improves from 1.02 to 1.23, confirming generality across reward models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Mask-GRPO (1.3B) achieves a GenEval overall score of 0.73, outperforming the 8B SD3.5-L (0.71) and DALLE-3 (0.67), and tying with Infinity (+Rewriter, 2B, 0.73). It substantially surpasses other AR models including JanusFlow (0.63), SimpleAR-1.5B (0.63), and Emu3 (+Rewriter, 8.5B, 0.66), as well as all other MGMs (MaskGen-XL 0.57, Meissonic 0.54). On MSCOCO-30K FID, Mask-GRPO (8.32) improves over its base Show-o (9.24) and beats SD1.5 (9.62) and DALLE-2 (10.39), but remains behind Janus (7.11) and PixArt-alpha (7.32). The method also demonstrates zero-shot generalization since GenEval prompts are absent from the training set, and it improves ImageReward from 1.02 to 1.23, confirming robustness across reward models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the GRPO-based RL paradigm (from DeepSeek-R1) to masked generative models (MGMs), a model family previously overlooked in the RL-for-T2I literature where prior work has focused on diffusion models (Flow-GRPO, DanceGRPO) and autoregressive models (SimpleAR). The core conceptual departure is redefining the transition probability: the natural AR-style product of all parallel token probabilities fails for MGMs, and the authors instead center the definition on 'newly unmasked tokens' via the Choose-and-Move mechanism inherited from MaskGIT. The MDP formulation for iterative generation follows Black et al.'s approach for diffusion models, adapted to the discrete unmasking schedule of Show-o.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By filling the gap of RL for MGMs, this work completes the triad of RL-enhanced T2I model families (diffusion, AR, masked) and provides practical strategies—KL removal for small models, unmasking reduction, and reward-based sample filtering—that are transferable to other iterative generative settings. Key open questions remain: whether the findings scale to larger base models (the KL-removal benefit may reverse), whether the approach extends to video generation with temporal coherence constraints, and whether CLIP-based rewards can be replaced by more accurate evaluators to unlock further gains. The 'Vanishing Samples' problem and its filtering solution also highlight a broader data-quality challenge in RL for visual generation that lacks dedicated datasets.
