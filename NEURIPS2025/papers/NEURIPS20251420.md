## Problem

Large Video Language Models (LVLMs) struggle with fine-grained temporal understanding, hallucinate, and make naive errors on simple video QA tasks, yet existing preference optimization methods like DPO use coarse response-level rewards that penalize all tokens indiscriminately, causing large gradients and significant model divergence. This paper addresses the gap by proposing a self-alignment framework where LVLMs learn from their own errors, paired with a new preference optimization objective that provides fine-grained, sub-sequence-level feedback while maintaining strong regularization to prevent divergence.

## Input / Output

The method takes video clips (16–128 frames depending on the base model's capacity) and text questions as input, producing text responses. Training data is generated without human annotation: spatio-temporal perturbations (masking 25–50% of each frame plus temporal shuffling) are applied to videos to provoke erroneous responses from the target LVLM, which are then paired with correct ground-truth responses from the VideoChat-IT instruction tuning dataset. Only incorrect generated responses are retained as non-preferred samples, and an LLM (GPT-4o-mini) verifies correctness and rewrites responses to align key concepts between preferred and non-preferred pairs. Training uses LoRA (rank=128) on the LLM component only, with all vision encoders and adapters frozen.

## Method

RRPO (Refined Regularized Preference Optimization) replaces DPO's response-level reward with a sub-sequence-level refined reward that computes the log-probability ratio only over tokens belonging to key differing concepts between preferred and non-preferred responses, rather than over the entire response. To prevent reward hacking (the model outputting correct key concepts without proper context), RRPO adds a token-wise KL divergence regularizer between the policy and a fixed reference model, applied over the full preferred response. The final loss combines a ranking term (negative log-sigmoid of the summed sub-sequence reward margins) with the TKL penalty weighted by α. A theoretical analysis shows that because the differentiating sub-sequences are much shorter than full responses, RRPO's gradient norm is provably smaller than DPO's, enabling 10× higher learning rates while achieving only 1/20th the KL divergence from the base model, resulting in more stable and precise alignment.

## Results

Across three base LVLMs (VideoChat2-7B, LLaVA-Video-7B, LongVU-7B), RRPO consistently outperforms DPO on all eight evaluation benchmarks. On LongVU-7B, RRPO achieves an average improvement of +2.5 points (+5.4%) over the base model across TVBench, VideoHallucer, VideoMME, and MLVU, compared to +0.7 (+1.5%) for DPO. Hallucination reduction is particularly pronounced: RRPO improves VideoHallucer scores by 4.8–8.8% and VidHalluc by up to 4.4% over base models. Notably, RRPO achieves these gains with a KL divergence of only 1 (vs. DPO's 20), enabling a 10× higher learning rate while maintaining stable training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DPO, RRPO wins on every benchmark for all three base models; the gap is largest on VideoHallucer (e.g., 32.9 vs. 22.1 for VideoChat2, a 10.8-point margin) and TVBench (56.5 vs. 54.3 for LongVU). Among alternative preference-optimization methods tested on LongVU (Table 2), RRPO outperforms DPA (+0.7 avg), TDPO (+0.8), and DDPO (+0.9) by a wide margin, achieving +2.5 average improvement while keeping KL divergence at 1 versus DPO's 20. Against the concurrent LLaVA-Video-TPO (which combines DPO + SFT on a manually curated dataset), RRPO-trained LLaVA-Video wins on all eight benchmarks, with the largest gap on VideoHallucer (55.8 vs. 50.6, +5.2 points). Compared to off-the-shelf reference models (GPT-4o, Gemini 1.5 Pro, Qwen2-VL-72B), the RRPO-aligned 7B models remain competitive on several benchmarks—for instance, LLaVA-Video-RRPO matches or exceeds GPT-4o on VidHalluc (76.6 vs. 81.2 is GPT-4o's lead, but RRPO beats Gemini 1.5 Pro's 72.8)—though the larger proprietary models still lead on VideoMME and LongVideoBench.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on DPO, replacing its coarse response-level reward with sub-sequence-level refined rewards that target only the key differing concepts between preferred and non-preferred responses, while adding token-wise KL (TKL) regularization to prevent reward hacking. It is positioned alongside and in contrast to several concurrent DPO variants: DPA (phrase-level alignment with TKL), TDPO (token-level DPO with TKL), and DDPO (weighted segment rewards), each of which addresses only one of DPO's two core limitations (coarse reward or weak regularization). The self-alignment data generation pipeline, which uses spatio-temporal perturbations to elicit model errors without human annotation, extends the self-alignment paradigm from text LLMs (Sun et al., 2023) to the video domain.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The self-alignment framework is annotation-free and scalable, making it practical for iteratively improving LVLMs as they evolve. The provably smaller gradient bound and the empirical KL divergence of 1 (vs. DPO's 20) at 10× higher learning rate offer a concrete recipe for more stable preference optimization that practitioners can adopt. Open questions include whether iterative self-alignment (beyond the single static dataset used here) yields compounding gains, how the approach scales to larger models or longer context windows, and whether the perturbation-based error elicitation is optimal compared to other data-augmentation strategies.
