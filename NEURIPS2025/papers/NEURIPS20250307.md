## Problem

Existing accident anticipation methods predict a single per-frame risk score using ambiguous binary supervision (all frames in accident videos labeled positive), which ignores the continuous temporal variation of risk and leads to unreliable predictions and false alarms. This paper addresses the gap by shifting the prediction target from 'is the current frame risky?' to 'at which specific future time will the accident occur?', leveraging precisely annotated accident timestamps as supervision.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250307/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a snippet of S=5 consecutive dashcam frames (resized to 224×224, resampled at 10 FPS so each frame spans 0.1s) and produces a sequence of T=20 accident scores, one for each future time step from 0.1s to 2.0s ahead. Each score a_t represents the model's confidence that an accident occurs exactly at that future offset; during training, only the score at the ground-truth accident time is labeled 1 while all others are 0. At inference, an alert is triggered if any score in the 2.0s horizon exceeds a threshold, and the system reports the specific predicted accident time.

## Method

The architecture is an encoder-decoder design. The snippet encoder is a 3D CNN (SlowOnly, ImageNet-pretrained) that jointly processes all 5 frames to capture spatial and temporal dynamics, followed by spatial pooling that preserves per-frame temporal resolution, yielding one feature vector per input frame. The temporal decoder is a 2-layer Transformer that takes these per-frame features as memory and uses T=20 distinct learnable temporal queries (with cosine positional encodings), each corresponding to a specific future horizon, to simultaneously predict all accident scores in one forward pass. Training uses binary cross-entropy with a positive-class weight of w+=10, and snippets are sampled only from pre-accident frames. The key distinction from prior work is that supervision is a one-hot temporal signal at the exact accident timestamp rather than a uniform positive label across all accident frames, providing a much more precise and interpretable training signal.

## Results

On the CAP dataset (λ=0.1), the proposed method achieves a mean AUC (mAUC) of 0.4290 and mean Time-to-Accident (mTTA) of 0.8644 s, substantially outperforming all prior methods. At the critical 0.0 s horizon (precise accident detection), AUC reaches 0.8381, nearly doubling the best prior result (GSC: 0.6093). On DADA, the method achieves mAUC of 0.3315 and mTTA of 0.8848 s, again leading all baselines. Performance degrades gracefully at longer horizons (1.0 s–1.5 s), where risk signals are inherently weaker, but the method still maintains the highest AUC at each interval.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all four compared baselines across every metric on both CAP and DADA. Against the strongest prior method GSC [17], the gains are most pronounced at short horizons: AUC at 0.0 s improves from 0.6093 to 0.8381 (+37.5%) and at 0.5 s from 0.4177 to 0.6747 (+61.5%) on CAP, while mTTA increases from 0.8165 s to 0.8644 s. DSTA [11] is similarly surpassed, with mAUC rising from 0.2864 to 0.4290 on CAP. The two earlier methods, CAP [9] and DRIVE [7], are dramatically outperformed—their mAUC values (0.0357 and 0.1159 on CAP) are an order of magnitude below the proposed method, reflecting the fundamental limitation of their frame-level binary risk scoring paradigm. On DADA, the same pattern holds: the method leads GSC by 0.0892 in mAUC (0.3315 vs. 0.2423) and by 0.1814 s in mTTA (0.8848 vs. 0.7034), confirming consistent superiority across datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant accident-anticipation paradigm (DSA, ACRA, DSTA, GSC, etc.) that predicts a single per-frame risk score supervised by ambiguous binary labels (all accident-video frames = 1). Instead, it reframes the prediction target as a sequence of accident scores at discrete future horizons (0.1s–2.0s), supervised by precisely annotated crash timestamps. Architecturally, it replaces the common frame-level encoder + RNN pipeline with a snippet-level 3D CNN (SlowOnly) for joint spatiotemporal encoding and a DETR-style Transformer decoder with dedicated temporal queries for multi-horizon prediction. The evaluation protocol also breaks from prior work by constraining AUC and TTA to a maximum false-alarm rate and revising TTA to exclude pre-anomaly false alarms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The shift from ambiguous binary supervision to precise temporal supervision addresses a fundamental weakness in how accident-anticipation models are trained, and the revised TTA metric reveals that previously reported anticipation lead times were artificially inflated—changing how the field should interpret prior results. The FAR-constrained evaluation protocol (AUC^λ, mTTA^λ) aligns metrics with real deployment constraints where excessive false alarms are unacceptable. Key open questions remain: performance at longer horizons (>1.0s) is still weak, suggesting the model struggles with subtle early precursors; and the reliance on human-annotated 'anomaly appear' timestamps introduces subjectivity that could limit generalization to unseen accident types.
