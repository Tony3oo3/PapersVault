## Problem

State-of-the-art text-to-video diffusion models generate visually realistic but physically implausible content, and their internal representations exhibit significantly weaker physics understanding than much smaller self-supervised video foundation models (e.g., CogVideoX-2B vs. VideoMAEv2-86M on Physion). Existing representation alignment (REPA) methods cannot be directly repurposed to inject physics knowledge into pre-trained video diffusion models because they focus only on spatial features, target from-scratch training acceleration rather than finetuning, use hard alignment that destabilizes pretrained weights, and ignore temporal compression in video latents.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt as input and produces a 49-frame video at 480×720 resolution, generated by finetuned CogVideoX (2B or 5B). During training, the video diffusion model operates on 3D VAE-compressed latents (4× temporal compression), while the alignment target VideoMAEv2 processes the same video at a reduced resolution to manage the memory cost of its 3D full-attention architecture. The first latent frame is excluded from alignment to focus on dynamic content rather than static semantics.

## Method

VideoREPA introduces a Token Relation Distillation (TRD) loss that distills physics knowledge from a frozen VideoMAEv2 encoder into a finetuned CogVideoX by aligning pairwise token similarity matrices rather than raw features. The TRD loss has two components: a spatial term computing L1 distance between within-frame cosine-similarity matrices, and a temporal term computing L1 distance between cross-frame similarity matrices, jointly capturing object integrity and motion dynamics. A trainable MLP projects VDM hidden states (extracted at depth 18 of the denoising transformer) to match the ViFM feature dimension, and VDM latent dimensions are interpolated to match ViFM spatial-temporal granularity. The total loss is L_diff + λ·L_TRD with λ=0.5, and finetuning uses full-parameter updates (2B) or LoRA rank-128 (5B) on the open-domain OpenVid dataset, avoiding the need for physics-explicit datasets like WISA-32K.

## Results

On VideoPhy, VideoREPA-5B achieves a state-of-the-art Physical Commonsense (PC) score of 40.1 overall, a 24.1% improvement over its CogVideoX-5B baseline (32.3), with gains of 42.9% on Solid-Solid, 16.7% on Solid-Fluid, and 20.6% on Fluid-Fluid interactions. Semantic Adherence (SA) also improves from 70.0 to 72.1. On the action-centric VideoPhy2 benchmark, VideoREPA-2B improves PC from 67.97 to 72.54 (+4.57) while maintaining identical SA (21.02).

## Baselines & Comparisons

VideoREPA-5B outperforms all compared methods on overall PC on VideoPhy, achieving 40.1 versus WISA on its dedicated WISA-32K dataset (38), PhyT2V (37), and its own CogVideoX-5B baseline (32.3). Against WISA trained on the open-domain Koala-36M dataset (PC=33), VideoREPA-5B leads by 7.1 points, demonstrating superior generalization without requiring physics-explicit training data. On VideoPhy2, VideoREPA-2B improves PC by 4.57 points over CogVideoX (72.54 vs. 67.97) with no change in SA. The 2B variant also surpasses CogVideoX-2B by 4.1 PC points (29.7 vs. 25.6) and outperforms HunyuanVideo (28.2) and VideoCrafter2 (29.7) on overall PC. Notably, VideoREPA-5B achieves the highest PC in every individual interaction category (Solid-Solid: 28.0, Solid-Fluid: 39.0, Fluid-Fluid: 74.5), while also attaining the best overall SA (72.1), indicating that physics enhancement does not come at the cost of semantic fidelity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the REPA framework (Yu et al., 2024), which aligned image diffusion transformer features with DINOv2 representations to accelerate from-scratch training, into the video domain and into a fundamentally different application: finetuning pre-trained T2V models to inject physics knowledge from Video Foundation Models. The key departure is replacing REPA's hard token-wise cosine similarity with a Token Relation Distillation (TRD) loss that aligns pairwise token similarity matrices across both spatial and temporal dimensions, providing softer guidance compatible with finetuning. Conceptually, it also positions itself against simulation-based physics generation (PhysGen, PhysAnimator) and prompt-refinement approaches (PhyT2V, WISA), arguing that relational knowledge transfer from ViFMs on open-domain data offers better generalization than physics-specific datasets or external simulators.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VideoREPA provides the first practical recipe for injecting domain knowledge (physics) into pre-trained video diffusion models via representation alignment, working on open-domain data without requiring specialized physics-annotated datasets. The demonstrated failure of standard REPA for finetuning (degraded quality) is a useful negative result that clarifies when hard vs. soft alignment is appropriate. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond CogVideoX to other VDM architectures, whether it can be integrated into pre-training rather than just finetuning, and whether the physics understanding gap between ViFMs and VDMs can be fully closed or only partially bridged. The method's reliance on a frozen ViFM as a teacher also raises questions about how much physics knowledge is transferable through relational structure alone versus requiring direct feature matching.
