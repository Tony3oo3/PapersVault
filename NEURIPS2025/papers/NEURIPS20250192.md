## Problem

Existing learning-to-defer frameworks assume experts are fixed and external, while dynamic/early-exit networks train classifiers and routing heuristically without theoretical guarantees. This paper addresses the gap of jointly training two classifiers of different costs alongside a decision module, where no principled surrogate loss with consistency guarantees existed for the natural 0-1c loss.

## Input / Output

The method consumes base features x (shared by both classifiers and the routing module), additional information z (accessible only to the second classifier at cost c), and label y ∈ {1,…,K}. It produces a class label from either f₁(x) or f₂(x,z) selected by a binary deferral decision r(x) ∈ {0,1}. In the LLM experiment, x and z are final-token hidden states extracted from LLaMA 3 8B and 70B respectively, and the task is 5-way multiple-choice math classification on AQUA.

## Method

The paper derives a hinge-based surrogate loss ℓ_hinge^c built on the Tarigan–van de Geer multiclass hinge (with sum-to-zero vector outputs), structured as a soft-weighted sum of two hinge terms—one per classifier—where the soft decision r̃(x) ∈ [0,1] controls per-sample weighting, and an additive cost penalty Kc/(K−1) is embedded in the second classifier's term. The key theoretical result is a Bayes-consistency proof: convergence of the surrogate risk to its optimum implies convergence of the 0-1c risk, with an explicit linear gap bound of 2(K−1)/K. The optimal solution is characterized by comparing max_y η_y(x) against E_{p(Z|x)}[max_y ζ_y(x,Z)] − c, and the paper further proves that no cross-entropy-based surrogate (with arbitrary cost function g(c)) can be Bayes-consistent for this problem, justifying the hinge construction.

## Results

On the synthetic K=5 classification task, the proposed hinge-based surrogate loss achieves the lowest empirical 0-1c risk across all tested cost values: 0.3695 (c=0.03), 0.3777 (c=0.05), and 0.3842 (c=0.07), consistently outperforming all baselines. The learned soft decision function r̃(x) perfectly aligns with the ground-truth optimal decision rule r*(x) across varying costs and class counts (K=3,5). On the LLM experiment (AQUA dataset, LLaMA 3 8B vs. 70B), the model correctly routes basic arithmetic/proportion questions to the smaller model and number-theory/motion problems to the larger model, with f₁'s selected-sample accuracy exceeding its average accuracy as expected for easier routed instances.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares the proposed hinge-based surrogate loss against four baselines adapted to the two-stage setting: confidence thresholding with cost (CT-c), confidence thresholding with validation-set search (CT), soft Bernoulli deferral, and the learning-to-defer method of Mozannar and Sontag (L2D). The proposed method achieves the lowest empirical 0-1c risk at every cost level, with the margin over L2D widening as cost increases (0.0017 at c=0.03 to 0.0064 at c=0.07), suggesting the joint-training advantage grows when the deferral decision is more consequential. The CT-c baseline performs worst at higher costs (0.4031 at c=0.07), indicating that a fixed threshold calibrated to f₂'s accuracy minus cost fails to adapt properly. Additionally, the paper demonstrates that a natural cross-entropy surrogate (Eq. 19) produces unstable and incorrect decision boundaries, and proves it cannot be Bayes-consistent with the 0-1c loss for any cost function g(c), further justifying the hinge-based formulation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the learning-to-defer (L2D) literature, which has established consistent surrogate losses for training a single classifier alongside a deferral rule to fixed experts (Mozannar & Sontag 2020, Verma et al. 2022, Mao et al. 2024a,b). The key departure is that here both classifiers are trained jointly with the decision module, rather than treating the second classifier as an external, pre-trained expert. The surrogate loss is built on the Tarigan–van de Geer (2008) multi-hinge loss, chosen specifically for its Bayes-consistency property, and the cost is incorporated additively into the hinge formulation. The paper also positions itself against the dynamic/early-exit network literature (Han et al. 2022, Schuster et al. 2022), which relies on heuristic losses lacking theoretical grounding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first Bayes-consistent surrogate loss for the joint-training-of-two-classifiers-with-deferral setting, filling a clear theoretical gap. The proof that cross-entropy (the de facto default in practice and used heuristically in prior joint-training work) is NOT Bayes-consistent for the 0-1c loss is a useful negative result that justifies the need for principled loss design. Key limitations include the restriction to exactly two stages and the use of hinge loss, which is less stable and less commonly adopted in practice than cross-entropy; finding a consistent and practically stable alternative remains an open question. The multi-stage generalization is sketched but not fully developed.
