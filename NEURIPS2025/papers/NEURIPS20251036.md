## Problem

Existing multimodal benchmarks evaluate models on dual-modality combinations (image-text or audio-text) but lack a comprehensive evaluation of whether models can simultaneously integrate and reason across visual, acoustic, and textual inputs. This gap hinders the community from assessing the true omni-understanding capability of emerging omni-language models (OLMs) that claim to process all three modalities concurrently.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251036/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each benchmark sample is a tuple of (image, audio clip, text) where images have a minimum resolution of 480P (854×480), audio clips are capped at 30 seconds and span three categories (speech, sound events, music), and the text is a four-option multiple-choice question. The critical design constraint is that the correct answer must require complementary information from both the image and audio—neither modality alone suffices. The model outputs a single letter (A–D) as its answer, evaluated by exact-match accuracy (random baseline = 25%).

## Method

OmniBench enforces tri-modal dependency through a rigorous three-stage annotation pipeline (initial annotation by 16 annotators, human cross-inspection by 5 quality reviewers, and automatic inspection using LLaVA-1.6-34B under ablation settings where image-only, audio-transcript-only, and text-only inputs must fail to yield the correct answer). The 1,142 QA pairs are organized into three task categories—(temporal-)spatial entity, causal inference, and abstract concept—each with sub-classes probing object identification, story description, plot inference, counting, and symbolic reasoning. To support training, the authors curate OmniInstruct (84.5K samples) from MSRVTT-QA, AVQA, and Music-AVQA2.0, filtering with InternVL-2-76B to discard questions answerable from a single modality or lacking logical coherence. Evaluation extends beyond vanilla tri-modal accuracy to include ablation settings (removing image or audio) and textual approximation settings where audio is replaced by human-written transcripts (for VLMs) or images by InternVL-2-76B-generated captions (for ALMs), enabling fair comparison across models with different modality support.

## Results

On OmniBench (1,142 tri-modal QA pairs), the best open-source omni-language model Qwen-2.5-Omni achieves 56.13% accuracy with full image+audio input, outperforming all proprietary baselines including Gemini-1.5-Pro (42.91%) and Reka-core-20240501 (30.39%). Most open-source OLMs remain below 50%: Baichuan-Omni-1.5 (42.90%), MiniCPM-o 2.6 (40.50%), Video-SALMONN (35.64%), VITA (33.10%), and MIO-Instruct (24.80%), with AnyGPT (18.04%) falling below random-guess level (25%). Human evaluators score 63.19% (Fleiss' Kappa 0.421) and human experts 74.03%, revealing a substantial model-to-human gap of 7–18 points. In textual approximation settings, Claude-3.5-Sonnet (59.37%) and GPT-4o (57.62%) lead among VLMs using audio transcripts, while Qwen2-Audio-7B-Instruct (40.72%) is the strongest ALM using image captions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Qwen-2.5-Omni (56.13%) is the clear winner among all evaluated models, surpassing the best proprietary model Gemini-1.5-Pro (42.91%) by 13.2 points and the next open-source OLM Baichuan-Omni-1.5 (42.90%) by 13.2 points. Gemini-1.5-Pro shows the most balanced performance across audio subtypes (Speech 42.67%, Sound Event 42.26%, Music 46.23%) and benefits most from full tri-modal input (42.91% vs. 27.93% audio-only, 26.09% image-only), confirming genuine cross-modal integration. Video-SALMONN (35.64% overall) excels specifically on music (56.60%), outperforming even Gemini on that subset, while UnifiedIO2-xlarge (38.00%) shows inconsistent scaling—its larger xxlarge variant (33.98%) actually underperforms. In the textual-approximation setting, Claude-3.5-Sonnet (59.37%) and GPT-4o (57.62%) dominate VLM evaluations with audio transcripts, significantly outperforming the best open-source VLM InternVL-2-40B (54.29%); among ALMs, Qwen2-Audio-7B-Instruct (40.72%) leads with image captions, far ahead of Audio-SALMONN (34.76%) and LTU (23.29%). OmniInstruct fine-tuning yields modest but consistent gains: MIO-Instruct improves from 24.80% to 29.20% (voice-filtered), and MiniCPM-o 2.6 improves from 40.50% to 45.90% with full-dataset LoRA fine-tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of vision-language and audio-language benchmarking by introducing the first comprehensive evaluation requiring simultaneous integration of image, audio, and text. Prior multimodal benchmarks (MMBench, MMMU, AIR-Bench) and audio-visual QA datasets (AVQA, Music-AVQA, Pano-AVQA) evaluate dual-modality combinations or allow answers derivable from a single modality, whereas OmniBench enforces that correct responses necessitate cross-modal reasoning across all three inputs. The accompanying OmniInstruct training set repurposes existing video QA corpora (MSRVTT-QA, AVQA, Music-AVQA2.0) through VLM-based filtering to create tri-modal instruction-tuning data, bridging the gap between dual-modality pre-training and the tri-modal evaluation setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals that even the strongest open-source OLM (Qwen-2.5-Omni, 56.13%) and proprietary models (Gemini-1.5-Pro, 42.91%) fall well short of human evaluators (63.19%), exposing a fundamental gap in tri-modal context construction that existing MLLM training paradigms overlook. The finding that VLMs with textual audio transcripts substantially outperform ALMs with textual image captions suggests a practical pathway for building OLMs from the more mature VLM ecosystem. Key limitations include the modest 1,142-sample size, restriction to static images (no video), and LLM-generated image captions for the ALM evaluation setting. Open questions include whether universal-tokenizer architectures (e.g., AnyGPT) will be necessary for true tri-modal integration and how to scale annotation to cover temporal video-audio reasoning.
