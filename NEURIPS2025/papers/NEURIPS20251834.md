## Problem

Existing video generation benchmarks (VBench, VideoPhy, T2V-CompBench) evaluate general video quality such as temporal consistency and aesthetic coherence, but fail to assess whether generated videos obey real-world physics or faithfully follow instructions—capabilities essential for video world models supporting robotics, autonomous driving, and planning. WorldModelBench addresses this gap by introducing fine-grained evaluation dimensions for instruction following and physics adherence across application-driven domains, paired with large-scale human annotations to ground the metrics.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251834/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

Each benchmark instance provides a text prompt describing an action and an optional first-frame image condition; the model generates a short video (typically 4 seconds at 720P). The judger—a 2B VLM—consumes the text/image condition alongside the generated video and outputs 8 discrete scores: a 4-level instruction-following grade (0–3), five binary physics-adherence flags (Newton's first law, mass conservation/solid mechanics, fluid mechanics, impenetrability, gravitation), and two binary commonsense flags (frame-wise quality, temporal quality), summing to a 0–10 total. The benchmark spans 350 condition pairs across 7 domains and 56 subdomains, supporting both T2V and I2V models.

## Method

WorldModelBench curates 350 feasible image-text condition pairs from open-license driving, robotics, and human-activity datasets, verified by human annotators. The authors collect 67K fine-grained labels from 8,336 votes by 65 crowd workers (with 10 CS-PhD-level experts for validation), achieving 87.1% score agreement and 70% pairwise agreement comparable to Chatbot Arena. A 2B-parameter VILA VLM is fine-tuned on these annotations as an automatic judger, processing each vote as 8 question-answering pairs and achieving 9.9% lower prediction error than GPT-4o. For improving generation, the paper builds on VADER's reward-gradient framework: the fine-tuned judger serves as a differentiable reward model whose probability gap over answer tokens (e.g., p('No') − p('Yes')) is backpropagated through the video diffusion model, enabling end-to-end optimization of world-modeling quality without discrete reward discretization.

## Results

On WorldModelBench (350 prompts, 7 application domains, scored out of 10), the top closed model KLING achieves 8.82 (human) / 9.10 (judge), while the best open model Mochi reaches 7.62 (human) / 7.91 (judge). The fine-tuned 2B VILA judger outperforms GPT-4o on prediction error across all three dimensions: 21.0% vs 29.3% (instruction following), 28.0% vs 35.0% (commonsense), and 24.0% vs 36.0% (physics adherence). Correlation analysis with VBench shows a strong 0.69 coefficient for frame-wise quality but only 0.28 for physics adherence, confirming that existing benchmarks fail to capture world-modeling violations. I2V models consistently underperform their T2V counterparts (e.g., CogVideoX 7.31→6.75, OpenSora 6.11→5.83).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among the 14 evaluated video generation models, KLING leads with 8.82 (human) / 9.10 (judge), followed by Minimax (8.59/8.92) and Mochi-official (8.37/8.66); the best open models Mochi (7.62) and OpenSoraPlan-T2V (7.61) are competitive with Luma (7.72), the weakest closed model. A consistent finding is that I2V variants underperform their T2V counterparts across all three model families (CogVideoX 7.31→6.75, OpenSoraPlan 7.61→6.62, OpenSora 6.11→5.83), highlighting a gap in image-conditioned generation. For the automated judger, the fine-tuned VILA-2B achieves 21.0%/28.0%/24.0% prediction error on instruction/commonsense/physics, beating GPT-4o (29.3%/35.0%/36.0%) and Gemini-1.5-Pro (30.7%/34.5%/29.3%) on all three dimensions, with the largest margin on physics adherence (12 points over GPT-4o). Correlation with VBench reveals that frame-wise quality aligns well (r=0.69) but physics adherence is nearly uncorrelated (r=0.28), and no other VBench dimension exceeds r=0.41, demonstrating that WorldModelBench captures violations invisible to existing benchmarks. Newer models (Table 3) push the frontier further: Veo3 reaches 9.18 and Wan 2.1-T2V reaches 9.04, both surpassing KLING's 9.10 judge score, while ltx-T2V (8.78) and ltx-I2V (8.43) show that even the I2V gap narrows with newer architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the video generation evaluation line from VBench (general quality) and VideoPhy (physics for daily objects, 0/1 granularity) to a dedicated world-modeling benchmark targeting application-driven domains (robotics, autonomous driving, human activities) with fine-grained scoring: 4-level instruction following and 5 binary physics laws. For the reward-based improvement component, it builds directly on VADER (Prabhudesai et al., 2024), adapting the reward-gradient framework to use a fine-tuned VLM judger rather than a text-image reward model. The judger itself is based on VILA-2B, a lightweight vision-language model, fine-tuned on 67K dense human annotations to replace the need for expensive proprietary VLMs like GPT-4o as evaluators.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

WorldModelBench fills a critical gap: existing benchmarks (VBench, T2VScore) measure visual quality but cannot distinguish a physically plausible video from one where objects float or pass through each other, which is precisely the failure mode that matters for robotics and autonomous driving applications. The finding that higher frame-wise and temporal quality scores do not predict better world modeling (Luma outperforms Mochi on commonsense but lags on instruction following) challenges the implicit assumption in prior benchmarks that visual fidelity proxies for physical correctness. Open questions include whether the 350-prompt benchmark generalizes to unseen domains, whether the reward-gradient fine-tuning approach scales beyond OpenSora to larger or architecturally different models, and whether the 2B judger remains accurate as new model generations introduce qualitatively different failure modes.
