## Problem

Table-native in-context learning models like TabPFN and TabICL achieve strong performance on tabular prediction but are trained exclusively on synthetic data, leaving them unable to exploit the rich semantics in column names, categorical labels, and free-text entries present in real-world tables. At the other extreme, LLM-based tabular models such as TabuLa-8B capture deep semantic understanding but are constrained by tokenization inefficiency, limiting context to ~32 rows and losing the 2D tabular structure. No existing approach combines the architectural efficiency and permutation equivariance of table-native ICL with the semantic alignment that real-world data provides.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a tabular table where each cell is encoded according to its data type: text and categorical values are mapped through a pretrained sentence embedder (all-MiniLM-L6-v2), dates are decomposed into day/month/year components and embedded separately, and numerics undergo quantile clipping and standardization before a learnable 1D projection. Column headers are embedded with the same text model and summed into each cell's embedding, serving as semantic positional encodings. At inference, 8-fold bagging samples context rows with replacement (default c=8192 per bag, up to 500 columns), and the model outputs class probabilities (cross-entropy head) or a normalized floating-point regression value.

## Method

ConTextTab modifies the TabPFN interleaved horizontal/vertical attention backbone by replacing its synthetic-data-oriented encodings with modality-specific semantic embeddings and training on the real-world T4 dataset (~2.18M tables, median 750 rows × 9 columns). Text and categorical cells retain their semantic meaning via a frozen pretrained sentence embedder followed by a learnable linear projection, while column headers are embedded identically and added to cell embeddings, making the architecture fully equivariant under row and column permutations. The backbone uses weight-shared transformer blocks (12 layers, d=768, ~16M trainable parameters) with alternating cross-column and masked cross-row self-attention. For classification, a standard cross-entropy head is used by default, with an alternative supervised clustering head that computes cosine similarities between query and context row embeddings to support arbitrary class counts while preserving label semantics. For regression, a simple L2 head on the normalized target is used, with an optional soft-binning variant that converts regression to classification over quantile-defined bins. The model is pretrained for 4–10M steps on T4 with AdamW (lr=1e-4, batch 256 via gradient accumulation) on a single H100 GPU, reaching ~10 tables/s throughput.

## Results

ConTextTab achieves a mean rank of 2.96 on the semantically rich CARTE benchmark (76.9% accuracy, 72.4% R²), setting a new SOTA and significantly outperforming all other table-native ICL methods (TabPFN: 72.3% Acc / 65.0% R²; TabDPT: 72.7% / 65.1%). On non-semantic benchmarks, ConTextTab remains competitive: 86.6% accuracy on OpenML-CC18 (rank 3.56), 73.3% R² on OpenML-CTR23 (rank 3.89), 85.4% Acc / 63.2% R² on TabReD (rank 4.12), and 87.6% Acc / 76.1% R² on TALENT-Tiny (rank 2.59). In the low-data regime (≤2048 rows) on CARTE, ConTextTab surpasses even AutoGluon, highlighting the strength of in-context learning with semantic understanding when data is scarce.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the semantically rich CARTE benchmark, ConTextTab (76.9% Acc, 72.4% R²) significantly outperforms all table-native ICL baselines: TabPFN (72.3% / 65.0%), TabDPT (72.7% / 65.1%), and TabICL (72.5% Acc only), with a 96% win rate over TabPFN and vanishingly small p-values. It also surpasses the fine-tuned CARTE model (76.1% / 68.5%) and Mambular (70.1% / 52.8%), confirming that semantic in-context learning without task-specific fine-tuning is effective. Against tuned boosted trees (CatBoost 75.9% / 68.2%, LightGBM 73.4% / 67.5%, XGBoost 73.1% / 66.7%) and deep learning baselines (RealMLP 73.4% / 67.5%, TabM 73.2% / 66.9%), ConTextTab leads by 1–4 points in accuracy and 4–6 points in R² on CARTE. On non-semantic benchmarks, ConTextTab trails the best-tuned ensembles: RealMLP [HPO, CV] achieves 75.0% R² on OpenML-CTR23 vs. ConTextTab's 73.3%, and LightGBM [HPO, CV] leads TabReD (rank 1.25 vs. 4.12), though these gaps are largely not statistically significant per the paper's Wilcoxon tests. AutoGluon, which stacks and ensembles many architectures, remains the strongest overall (78.7% / 73.8% on CARTE), but ConTextTab surpasses it in the low-data regime (≤2048 rows) on CARTE, demonstrating the unique advantage of in-context learning when training data is limited.

## Relation to Prior Work

ConTextTab extends the TabPFN/TabICL line of table-native in-context learning by replacing their synthetic-data-only training with the real-world T4 dataset and introducing modality-specific semantic encodings (sentence embeddings for text/categorical cells and column headers, structured date embeddings, and clipped numeric encodings). It explicitly positions itself as bridging the gap between efficient but semantically blind table-native ICL (TabPFN, TabICL, TabDPT) and LLM-based tabular ICL (TabuLa-8B), which captures world knowledge but is limited to ~32 context rows due to tokenization overhead. The interleaved cross-row/cross-column attention backbone is inherited from TabPFN, while the ISAB blocks draw on Set Transformer ideas for sub-quadratic scaling, and the supervised clustering decoding head adapts a prior table-structure method to classification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that semantic understanding is a genuine and measurable advantage in tabular prediction, particularly in the low-data regime and on semantically rich features, by setting SOTA on the CARTE benchmark with statistically significant margins over all other table-native ICL methods. However, the approach does not scale to very large datasets (beyond ~10k rows), where tuned boosted trees and AutoGluon still dominate, leaving a clear open question about how to combine semantic awareness with effective long-context processing. Additional open threads include the need for more diverse and semantically rich pretraining data (T4 appears to be a bottleneck), the failure of the target-semantics-preserving clustering head to outperform standard cross-entropy, and the absence of semantically rich high-cardinality classification benchmarks to properly evaluate such approaches.
