## Problem

Existing LLM-Dominated RL methods for autonomous driving motion planning tightly couple LLM outputs to the driving decision loop, making them vulnerable to LLM hallucinations that directly distort Q-value estimation and produce unsafe actions. Evaluations show that even SOTA LLMs (e.g., Gemini-2.5-Pro) achieve only ~57.95% non-hallucination rate on essential driving tasks, meaning over 40% of outputs are unreliable. The paper argues that maintaining relative independence between the LLM and the RL agent is essential to buffer hallucination effects while still leveraging LLM semantic understanding.

## Input / Output

The agent receives multiview RGB images, road topology features, ego-vehicle dynamics, and navigation path embeddings as raw sensory input, which are processed by a ShuffleNetV2 visual backbone and GRU temporal encoder. These are concatenated with LLM-generated semantic embeddings (a 4-dimensional scenario-level vector and a 9-dimensional object-level vector) to form the augmented state. The LLM operates asynchronously at a lower frequency than the RL control loop, and when it fails to respond within a time window, a semantic cache module retrieves the most similar historical entry as a fallback. The output is a pair of bounded continuous control commands (throttle/brake and steering angle, each in [-1, +1]) sampled from a Beta distribution.

## Method

HCRMP proposes an LLM-Hinted RL paradigm where the LLM provides semantic hints as auxiliary inputs rather than directly dictating decisions, preserving the RL agent's autonomy to counteract erroneous hints through policy learning. The architecture comprises three modules: (1) an Augmented Semantic Representation module where the LLM performs hierarchical reasoning from global scenario abstraction to fine-grained object-level analysis, encoding results into compact vectors that extend the RL state space; (2) a Contextual Stability Anchor module that implements a multi-critic PPO framework where the LLM generates adaptive weights over driving attributes (safety, comfort, efficiency) to modulate a GAE-based integrated advantage function, with a RAG pipeline over a traffic-regulation knowledge base reducing weight variance by 54.67% through retrieval-augmented anchoring; and (3) a Semantic Cache module that resolves the temporal mismatch between low-frequency LLM reasoning and high-frequency RL control via an LRU-managed memory bank with nearest-neighbor semantic matching as a fallback. The key design principle is weak coupling: LLM hints influence but do not dominate the optimization objective, so hallucinated signals are absorbed by the RL agent's self-optimization rather than propagated directly into driving actions.

## Results

HCRMP achieves a task success rate (SR) of up to 99% in conventional overtaking under low traffic density and maintains 86–87% SR in high-density conditions, substantially outperforming all baselines. In safety-critical scenarios (trilemma and occluded pedestrian), HCRMP reaches an average SR of 62.5% under high density while reducing the collision rate (CR) by 11.4% compared to the strongest LLM-Dominated RL baseline. The method also maintains low acceleration variance (AV ≈ 1.2–1.7 m/s²), indicating comfortable driving behavior across all tested conditions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HCRMP outperforms all four baselines across every tested condition and traffic density. Against the strongest RL-only baseline (Vanilla PPO), HCRMP gains 25 percentage points in SR for high-density overtaking (87% vs. 62%) and 18 points for high-density merging (86% vs. 68%). Compared to the best LLM-Dominated RL method (AutoReward, iter=5), HCRMP still leads by 17 points in high-density overtaking SR (87% vs. 70%) and 15 points in high-density merging (86% vs. 71%). The gap is most dramatic against VLM-RL, which plateaus at 50% SR in all high-density conditions, while HCRMP maintains 86–87%. In safety-critical scenarios (Table 2), HCRMP reduces the collision rate by 11.4% relative to the best LLM-Dominated baseline (AutoReward) in high-density trilemma (36% CR vs. 68% CR), though VLM-RL covers more total distance (TD) at the cost of a 43.7% average CR, highlighting a safety–efficiency trade-off that HCRMP resolves more favorably.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant 'LLM-Dominated RL' paradigm in autonomous driving motion planning, where LLMs directly generate reward functions (AutoReward, LearningFlow, LORD) or high-level driving policies (HighwayLLM, AlphaDrive, VLM-RL). Building on PPO as the RL backbone, it introduces a 'LLM-Hinted RL' paradigm in which the LLM provides weakly-coupled semantic hints—state-space augmentation via hierarchical scenario reasoning and dynamic multi-critic weight modulation via RAG-anchored generation—rather than directly dictating the optimization objective. The Contextual Stability Anchor module repurposes retrieval-augmented generation (RAG with FAISS similarity search over a traffic-regulation knowledge base) not for text generation but for stabilizing numerical weight outputs, and the Semantic Cache Module addresses the temporal mismatch between low-frequency LLM inference and high-frequency RL control, a practical concern largely unaddressed in prior LLM-RL driving work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper tackles a concrete and underappreciated failure mode: LLM hallucinations propagating directly into safety-critical driving policies, quantified by the 57.95% non-hallucination rate of Gemini-2.5-Pro on driving tasks. The weakly-coupled architecture is a pragmatic design principle that could generalize beyond driving to any safety-critical LLM+RL system. However, open questions remain: all experiments are confined to CARLA Town 2 with a single LLM, the ablation covers only one safety-critical condition, and the gap between simulated and real-world driving (sensor noise, long-tail scenarios, real-time latency) is acknowledged but not addressed. Whether the 11.4% collision-rate reduction generalizes across diverse map topologies, weather conditions, and LLM backends is untested.
