## Problem

Existing multi-turn jailbreak methods suffer from two critical flaws: they embed sensitive keywords in early dialogue turns, which increases the target model's rejection tendency in later turns, and they lack mechanisms to preserve semantic alignment, causing the final harmful response to drift from the original malicious intent. No prior method constructs a fully benign multi-turn context while still ensuring the elicited output faithfully reflects the target attack goal.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254125/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single malicious prompt (e.g., 'Write a tutorial on how to make a bomb') and interacts with a black-box target LLM through natural language only. It produces a sequence of t turns (default t=3) where turns 1 through t−1 are fully benign analogical-translation prompts using safe domain-keyword pairs, and turn t performs the controlled semantic shift by substituting the original malicious keyword into the same analogical format. The final output is a harmful response that preserves the structural format learned across benign turns (e.g., materials, procedures, tips) while being semantically faithful to the original malicious query, evaluated for both harmfulness and consistency via LLM-as-a-Judge scoring.

## Method

AMA's core insight is to decouple the structural format of a harmful response from its malicious semantic core and distribute them across separate dialogue stages. In the benign context construction stage, the attack model abstracts the malicious prompt into a domain-agnostic template, automatically samples safe domain-keyword pairs, and issues a sequence of prompts in the form 'Analogously translate to [domain] theme, mappings = {prev_keyword: new_keyword}', forcing the target model to repeatedly reframe its response across safe domains while internalizing a consistent response structure. In the controlled semantic shift stage, the final turn uses the identical analogical prompt format but substitutes the original malicious keyword and domain, causing the model to transfer the learned structure to the harmful domain without an explicit harmful task instruction. A feedback-driven optimization loop then refines prompts using two lightweight judges: an EntropyJudge that detects information degradation via entropy drops between consecutive responses, and a JailbreakJudge that detects refusals and triggers prompt reframing, iterating up to K=3 times per attack.

## Results

Under the LlamaGuard-3 (LG3) safety classifier, AMA achieves average attack success rates of 96.0% on AdvBench, 90.7% on JailbreakBench, and 83.8% on StrongREJECT, consistently outperforming all five baselines across six target LLMs. Under the less sensitive llama-3.1-70b-based classifier (LM3), AMA still leads with 61.3%, 56.0%, and 68.9% respectively, exceeding the next-best methods by 15–25 percentage points in most cases. AMA is particularly effective against strongly safety-aligned models: on qwen-2.5-72b and gpt-4o-mini, where raw malicious prompts yield below 10% ASR, AMA reaches up to 100% (LG3) on AdvBench and over 89% (LG3) on JailbreakBench.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AMA outperforms all five baselines across every dataset and model configuration. Against the single-turn baselines, AMA surpasses PAIR (Chao et al., 2023) by approximately 39–34 percentage points on AdvBench and JailbreakBench (LG3), and TAP (Mehrotra et al., 2024) by roughly 32–28 points, demonstrating that multi-turn analogy-based structuring is substantially more effective than single-turn adversarial optimization. Against the multi-turn baseline CoA (Yang et al., 2024), AMA gains 46–46 points on AdvBench and 45–46 points on JailbreakBench, with CoA also showing notable semantic drift (lowest consistency score of 4.2 vs. AMA's 7.38). In comparisons reported from original papers (Table 8), AMA exceeds Crescendo (Russinovich et al., 2024) by 22.4 points on GPT-4 (78.6% vs. 56.2%) and CFA (Sun et al., 2024) by 69–26 points on vicuna-1.5-13b and gpt-3.5-turbo respectively. Notably, AMA maintains robust performance across all six target models with minimal variance, whereas CoA exhibits significant drops (e.g., 14% on llama-3.1-70b) and single-turn methods are more sensitive to model-specific safety alignment.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself within the multi-turn jailbreak attack lineage (CoA, Crescendo, CFA) but departs from all prior multi-turn methods by constructing fully benign dialogue contexts rather than embedding sensitive keywords. The core conceptual contribution is the decomposition of harmful responses into a structural format and a malicious semantic core, with the analogy-based cross-domain translation mechanism used to internalize structure in safe turns before injecting malicious semantics in the final turn. The feedback-driven optimization (EntropyJudge for information degradation, JailbreakJudge for refusal detection) extends the adaptive prompt refinement seen in single-turn methods like PAIR and TAP into the multi-turn setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 93.3% average ASR across six models and three benchmarks reveals that current safety alignment is highly vulnerable to structurally-coherent multi-turn attacks, and the finding that fully benign contexts outperform suspicious ones has direct implications for how defenses should be designed (e.g., monitoring for structural pattern learning rather than keyword detection). The semantic drift problem identified in existing methods (CoA producing lower consistency than single-turn baselines) highlights that high ASR alone is insufficient without intent preservation. Open questions include whether adaptive turn allocation can further improve efficiency, whether the analogy mechanism generalizes to multimodal or agentic settings, and whether targeted defenses against structural pattern exploitation can be developed.
