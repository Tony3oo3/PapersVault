## Problem

Existing LLM-based multi-agent systems rely on centralized controllers or static, predefined workflows to allocate tasks among agents with fixed roles, creating scalability bottlenecks, single points of failure, and barriers to cross-organizational collaboration due to privacy and proprietary knowledge concerns. These systems cannot adapt to real-time fluctuations in agent performance or rapidly changing task requirements, and rigid role definitions prevent agents from flexibly leveraging their full expertise in dynamic environments.

## Input / Output

Each task is represented as a tuple of a natural-language description, a capability-requirements vector (derived via heuristic mapping for atomic tasks or LLM-inferred prompts for compound tasks), and a priority level. The system produces a task solution (e.g., a math answer, logical QA response, or API call sequence) alongside an evolved DAG topology with pruned edges and updated agent capability vectors. A key constraint is that the routing path must remain acyclic: when an agent splits a task, it forwards only completed subtask results (not decomposition reasoning) to the next agent, preventing error propagation and preserving the DAG invariant.

## Method

AgentNet is a fully decentralized multi-agent framework where each agent contains a dual-role architecture: a router module that decides whether to forward, split, or execute a task, and an executor module that performs the actual work, both backed by fixed-capacity RAG memory pools storing (observation, context, action) trajectory fragments. Task allocation begins by matching the task's capability vector against each agent's dynamically updated capability vector via cosine similarity, then the selected agent's router retrieves the k most relevant past fragments via semantic embedding and uses a ReAct-style reasoning-then-acting loop to choose among three operations. The network topology evolves through an exponential-decay edge-weight update (balancing historical performance with recent collaborative success) followed by periodic pruning of edges below a threshold, while agents autonomously specialize by pruning low-value memory trajectories when pools reach capacity. This eliminates any central orchestrator: routing decisions are made locally from agent-specific memory and capability information, and the DAG structure guarantees acyclic task flow without global coordination.

## Results

AgentNet achieves the best or tied-best accuracy across all three backbone LLMs (DeepSeek-V3, GPT-4o-mini, Qwen-turbo) on the MATH, BBH, and API-Bank benchmarks. With DeepSeek-V3, AgentNet scores 92.86% on MATH, 94.00% on BBH, and 30.00% on API-Bank, outperforming the strongest multi-agent baseline (MetaGPT at 92.14/64.00/22.00) and the best single-agent method (Synapse at 89.28/92.00/28.00). With GPT-4o-mini, AgentNet reaches 85.00/86.00/29.00, matching GPTSwarm on MATH and BBH while substantially exceeding it on API-Bank (29.00 vs. 13.00). The ablation without the evolution phase drops performance to 77.86/76.00/23.00, confirming that adaptive learning contributes 7–9 percentage points of improvement.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against single-agent baselines, AgentNet consistently outperforms ReAct, Synapse, Self-Consistency, and Self-Refinement on at least two of the three benchmarks for every backbone; the largest gap is on API-Bank, where AgentNet (30.00% with DeepSeek-V3) exceeds the best single-agent (ReAct at 29.00%) and Synapse (28.00%). Among multi-agent baselines, AgentNet dominates MetaGPT by a wide margin on BBH (94.00 vs. 64.00 with DeepSeek-V3; 86.00 vs. 53.00 with GPT-4o-mini) and API-Bank (30.00 vs. 22.00; 29.00 vs. 19.00), while matching or slightly exceeding MetaGPT on MATH. AgentNet also surpasses AFLOW on BBH across all backbones (94.00 vs. 88.00, 86.00 vs. 75.00, 92.00 vs. 57.00) and on API-Bank (30.00 vs. 28.00, 29.00 vs. 21.00, 32.00 vs. 22.00), though AFLOW edges out AgentNet on MATH with Qwen-turbo (82.14 vs. 81.43). Compared to GPTSwarm, AgentNet wins decisively on API-Bank (30.00 vs. 21.00 with DeepSeek-V3; 29.00 vs. 13.00 with GPT-4o-mini) and on MATH with DeepSeek-V3 (92.86 vs. 72.14), while tying on BBH with GPT-4o-mini (86.00) and losing slightly on MATH with Qwen-turbo (81.43 vs. 79.29). MorphAgent is the weakest multi-agent baseline across all settings, scoring as low as 16.43% on MATH with Qwen-turbo and 9.00% on API-Bank, far below AgentNet's 81.43% and 32.00% respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

AgentNet synthesizes the ReAct reasoning-acting loop and RAG-based memory retrieval into a fully decentralized multi-agent architecture, departing from the centralized orchestration paradigm of AutoGen, MetaGPT, and AgentScope. It extends the graph-based agent network ideas of GPTSwarm and DyLAN by making topology evolution emergent (driven by per-edge success metrics and pruning) rather than centrally optimized, and it incorporates the self-evolving profile concept from MorphAgent but distributes it across a dynamic DAG. The key conceptual departure is the elimination of any central controller: routing, specialization, and memory management are all local decisions, which prior evolutionary or topology-optimization frameworks have not achieved simultaneously.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in deploying LLM multi-agent systems across organizations—privacy, single points of failure, and rigid role assignment—by showing that decentralized coordination with retrieval-based memory can match or exceed centralized baselines on reasoning and tool-use benchmarks. However, the empirical gains over strong multi-agent baselines (e.g., MetaGPT, AFLOW) are modest (1–6 points), and the system is evaluated only with 3–5 agents on relatively small test sets (100–140 problems), leaving open whether the approach scales to hundreds of agents or truly heterogeneous model/tool configurations. The heterogeneity experiment revealing that diversity hurts small teams but helps larger ones is a useful but narrow finding that warrants further study.
