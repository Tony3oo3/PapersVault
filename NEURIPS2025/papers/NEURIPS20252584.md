## Problem

Despite the rapid growth of scATAC-seq data, no foundation model exists for single-cell chromatin accessibility that supports zero-shot cell identification and multi-omics analysis simultaneously. The core obstacles are the extreme sparsity (~99% zeros) and high dimensionality of OCR profiles, the absence of a standardized feature space for open chromatin regions across tissues and platforms, and the lack of unified integration between genomic coordinates and accessibility signals in a single model.

## Input / Output

Each cell is represented as a variable-length sequence of ~440,000 OCR tokens, where each token encodes chromosome identity, sinusoidal positional embeddings of the actual GRCh38 start and end coordinates, and a continuous (non-binary) accessibility value projected through a linear layer. The model produces a pooled encoder output that, after PCA to 50 dimensions, serves as a universal cell representation; downstream heads yield cell type labels, predicted gene expression from paired scRNA-seq, enhancer-gene link scores, and transcriptional response predictions to simulated OCR knockdowns.

## Method

ChromFound employs a hybrid encoder with four stacked layers, each combining a Window Partition Self-Attention (WPSA) module that applies standard attention within local windows of W=256 tokens (spanning ~±200 kb around TSS, matching Hi-C-derived enhancer-promoter ranges) with a Mamba state-space block operating in a reduced 32-dimensional space to capture genome-wide long-range dependencies at linear time complexity. The key architectural insight is that local regulatory interactions and global chromatin context require fundamentally different inductive biases, so WPSA amplifies short-range signals while Mamba integrates them across the full ~440K-token sequence. Pretraining uses a masked-reconstruction objective with MSE loss on log-transformed, normalized accessibility values, masking both zero and non-zero positions to avoid representations dominated by sparsity. The model is trained on 1.97 million cells across 30+ tissues and 6 disease categories (1.86 trillion tokens), with the genome-aware coordinate-based tokenization eliminating the need for a fixed OCR vocabulary and enabling direct alignment across heterogeneous datasets.

## Results

On zero-shot cell clustering across 8 scATAC-seq datasets from 4 tissues, ChromFound achieves an average ARI of 0.6043, outperforming the best baseline (SCALE at 0.5460 on Morabito130K) by 17.02% in ARI, 10.39% in FMI, 6.72% in NMI, and 6.69% in AMI. In batch effect denoising, ChromFound achieves the highest AvgBIO score (0.6408 on Bone, 0.8180 on Heart) while also leading in AvgBATCH (0.9565 on Cortex). For cross-omics prediction, ChromFound attains the best PCC (0.9449 on Zhu11K cell lines) and CCC (0.9071) across all 5 paired datasets, with gains of 1.85–5.11% over the strongest baseline. In enhancer-gene link prediction on K562, ChromFound reaches AUC 0.77 (COPZ1) and 0.61 (HNRNPA1), surpassing BABEL and CMAE which produce zero predictions for most enhancers due to their top-10K peak filtering.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ChromFound outperforms all 20 baselines across every evaluated task. In zero-shot cell clustering, it beats the strongest VAE-based methods (SCALE, SCALEX, CASTLE) by 9–28% in ARI and the sequence-based scBasset by 10–28%, with the largest margin on Cortex (26.20% ARI gain over SCALE) and Heart (27.75% over scBasset). For batch effect denoising, ChromFound surpasses specialized integration tools (scVI, Scanorama, Harmony, scANVI, Liger) by up to 46% in AvgBIO on Bone while also achieving the best AvgBATCH, indicating superior preservation of biological signal alongside batch mixing. In cell type annotation, ChromFound consistently achieves the highest macro F1 across all tissues, particularly excelling on rare cell types (NK cells, CD16+ monocytes, dendritic cells) where Cellcano, EpiAnno, and SANGO struggle. For cross-omics prediction, ChromFound leads BABEL, CMAE, scMM, and scMoGNN in both PCC and CCC across all 5 paired datasets, with the largest gains on cell lines (PCC 0.9449 vs. BABEL's 0.9196). In enhancer-gene link prediction, BABEL and CMAE produce zero predictions for most enhancers due to their top-10K peak filtering, whereas ChromFound's genome-wide modeling yields meaningful AUC scores (0.77 COPZ1, 0.61 HNRNPA1) and accurate perturbation response predictions.

## Relation to Prior Work

ChromFound extends the foundation model paradigm established for scRNA-seq (Geneformer, scGPT, scFoundation) to the scATAC-seq domain, where no comparable pretrained model previously existed. Architecturally, it departs from the VAE-based approaches dominant in scATAC-seq (PeakVI, SCALE, SCALEX, CASTLE) and the sequence-to-accessibility predictors (scBasset, SANGO) by adopting a hybrid Mamba + windowed self-attention encoder that processes genome-wide OCR sequences without requiring a fixed peak vocabulary. The genome-aware tokenization (chromosome embedding + sinusoidal positional encoding of genomic coordinates + continuous accessibility) is a novel departure from both dictionary-based gene tokenization in transcriptomics and reference-peak alignment in existing ATAC-seq pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As the first scATAC-seq foundation model, ChromFound provides a zero-shot, tissue-agnostic representation that eliminates the need for dataset-specific training, which is critical as the Human Cell Atlas and similar initiatives generate ever-larger and more heterogeneous chromatin accessibility datasets. The demonstration that genome-wide modeling (440K OCRs) is essential—reducing input to 110K OCRs nearly halves clustering ARI—challenges the widespread practice of filtering to highly variable peaks and opens the door to discovering regulatory links in previously discarded genomic regions. Key open questions include cross-species generalization (pretraining is human-only), whether the Mamba+attention hybrid scales further with depth and data, and how well the model transfers to other epigenomic modalities (e.g., scCUT&Tag, scNMT-seq).
