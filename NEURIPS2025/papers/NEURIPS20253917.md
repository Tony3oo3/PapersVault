## Problem

Aligning autoregressive generative models to produce outputs with specific desired properties (e.g., drug-likeness, binding affinity) is a central challenge in molecular and protein design. Existing alignment methods either rely on reinforcement learning (PPO), which is expensive and difficult to tune, or on pairwise preference data (DPO), which is ill-suited to the continuous, easily evaluable reward signals common in chemistry. The paper asks whether one can design a direct, gradient-based policy optimization objective that uses an explicit reward function while maintaining sample diversity and avoiding mode collapse.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained autoregressive policy (a 3.5M-parameter molecular transformer or the 1.4B-parameter ESM3 protein language model), a prompt (a start-of-sequence token for unprompted generation, a SMILES string for prompted lead optimization, or a masked protein sequence for directed evolution), and an explicit energy function U(x,y) that can be a harmonic potential centered at a target property value, the negative log of a score, or a weighted sum for multi-property alignment. The output is a fine-tuned policy π_θ that generates SMILES strings or amino acid sequences sampled from a Gibbs-Boltzmann distribution shaped by the reward, with two independently tunable hyperparameters: β (inverse temperature controlling fluctuations around the reward optimum) and γ (KL-regularization strength toward the reference policy).

## Method

ERA defines a loss as the expected KL divergence between an entropy-regularized target preference distribution p_γ (computed directly from the explicit reward via a Bradley-Terry pairwise Boltzmann form) and the policy's implied preference distribution p_θ, evaluated on pairs of samples drawn from the reference policy. The key theoretical result is that the global minimizer of this loss is conditionally equivalent to a Gibbs-Boltzmann measure π_*(y|x) ∝ exp[−β/(1+γ)·(U(x,y) − β⁻¹γ·log π_ref(y|x))], where β and γ independently control reward sharpness and reference-policy proximity—unlike PPO, where eliminating regularization also eliminates entropy. The gradient of the ERA loss is interpretable per-pair: it scales with the relative preference discrepancy between the reward-implied and policy-implied probabilities, and it remains bounded unless the energy diverges, avoiding the instability that DPO exhibits when the implicit reward assigns zero probability to the dispreferred sample. The approach requires no reinforcement learning, no reward model training, and works with both offline (reference-policy) and on-policy (importance-weighted) sampling.

## Results

On the GSK3β and JNK3 kinase docking tasks (top-100 novel, valid, unique molecules from 20k samples), ERA achieves mean oracle scores of 0.996±0.000 and 0.987±0.001, respectively, with internal diversity (IntDiv) of 0.219±0.002 and 0.264±0.005. For GSK3β, ERA's mean score is marginally below MolRL-MGPT (1.000) but its diversity is substantially higher (IntDiv 0.219 vs. 0.362). For JNK3, ERA outperforms all baselines on both mean score and diversity. In the QED maximization task, ERA with β=20.0 maintains chemical validity and sample diversity over training, whereas DPO (β_DPO=0.1) suffers significant validity degradation and mode collapse.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ERA is compared against five molecular generation baselines on GSK3β and JNK3 docking tasks. Against MolRL-MGPT (Hu et al., 2023), ERA trades a marginal 0.004 drop in GSK3β mean score (0.996 vs. 1.000) for a 40% improvement in diversity (IntDiv 0.219 vs. 0.362), and on JNK3 ERA wins on both score (0.987 vs. 0.961) and diversity (0.264 vs. 0.372). ERA substantially outperforms GFlowNet (Gomez-Bombarelli et al., 2018) on both targets, achieving mean scores of 0.996 vs. 0.649 (GSK3β) and 0.987 vs. 0.437 (JNK3) with far lower IntDiv. Against GraphGA (Gao et al., 2022), ERA improves GSK3β score by 0.077 and JNK3 score by 0.112 while also achieving better diversity. REINVENT (Bou et al., 2024) is outperformed by ERA on both score and diversity for both kinases. In the QED maximization experiment, ERA (β=20.0, γ=0) maintains chemical validity and sample diversity throughout training, whereas DPO (Rafailov et al., 2023) with β_DPO=0.1 exhibits significant validity degradation and mode collapse, confirming the theoretical advantage of ERA's explicit reward-based gradient over DPO's implicit reward formulation.

## Relation to Prior Work

ERA positions itself at the intersection of preference optimization for LLMs (DPO, PPO) and inverse molecular design, drawing on the Bradley-Terry model for pairwise preferences and the Gibbs-Boltzmann framework from statistical physics. Unlike DPO, which treats the reward implicitly and requires pairwise preference data, ERA uses an explicit, continuously-valued reward function directly in its gradient-based objective. Unlike PPO, which relies on an actor-critic RL loop, ERA optimizes a KL-divergence loss between target and parametric preference distributions, yielding a closed-form minimizer with independently tunable entropy (β) and regularization (γ). The molecular generation backbone (MolGPT-style transformer on SMILES) and protein language model (ESM3) are standard; the novelty is entirely in the alignment objective and its theoretical characterization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ERA offers a simpler, more interpretable alternative to RLHF-style alignment for domains where a continuous reward is readily computable (docking scores, QED, LogP, EVmutation), avoiding the instability and hyperparameter sensitivity of PPO while sidestepping DPO's mode-collapse under sparse preference data. The clean separation of β (fluctuation scale) and γ (proximity to reference) gives practitioners a practical knob for balancing exploration against exploitation that neither DPO nor PPO provides directly. Key limitations include the requirement for a well-specified reward model (expensive for binding affinity), the absence of synthetic-accessibility constraints, and the fact that only the offline (off-policy) variant is fully validated; on-policy ERA and multi-round directed evolution remain open.
