## Problem

Existing KG reasoning methods that stack message-passing and attention layers in a single pathway suffer from score over-smoothing, where incorrect answers receive scores nearly indistinguishable from correct ones. This is compounded by the limited discriminative capacity of single-stage models that produce answers in one shot, making it difficult to separate true from false candidates.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251535/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes only the structural information of a knowledge graph (entity nodes, relation types, and observed triplets) plus a query triplet (h, r, ?), without relying on external textual features. At inference, it produces a final predicted tail entity through a two-stage decision: a coarse model generates an initial entity-to-score table, the fine model refines scores, and a threshold-based comparison between the top scores of high- and low-score subsets determines the answer. The output is thus a single ranked entity with an associated confidence derived from the inter-subset score gap.

## Method

DuetGraph decouples local and global information processing into two parallel pathways rather than stacking them sequentially: a local pathway using query-aware message passing (from KnowFormer) and a global pathway using a simple global attention mechanism (from SgFormer), whose outputs are adaptively fused via a learnable scalar α. At inference, a coarse-to-fine optimization partitions candidate entities into a small high-score subset (top-k) and a large low-score subset using a lightweight coarse model (e.g., HousE), then the dual-pathway fine model re-scores both subsets and a predefined threshold Δ decides whether the final answer comes from the high- or low-score side. The paper provides theoretical proofs (Theorems 1–3) showing that the dual-pathway design yields a larger score-gap upper bound than single-pathway stacking, and that the coarse-to-fine partitioning amplifies the expected score gap to at least 0.1σ, far exceeding the <0.02σ gaps observed in baselines.

## Results

On transductive KG reasoning, DuetGraph achieves MRR of 0.453, 0.593, 0.590, and 0.631 on FB15k-237, WN18RR, NELL-995, and YAGO3-10 respectively, outperforming the strongest hybrid baseline KnowFormer by up to 2.4 MRR points. On inductive reasoning across 12 subsets, DuetGraph improves Hits@1 by up to 8.7% and MRR by up to 8.6% over prior SOTA. The method also achieves a 1.8× training speedup (nearly 50% less wall-clock time) while reaching SOTA quality, and on very large KGs (Wikidata5M, Freebase) it surpasses both rule-based AnyBURL and embedding-based KnowFormer in MRR while reducing inference time by 96% versus AnyBURL.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DuetGraph outperforms all 23 baselines across all four transductive datasets. Against the closest hybrid competitor KnowFormer [19], DuetGraph gains +2.3 MRR on FB15k-237 (0.453 vs 0.430), +1.4 on WN18RR (0.593 vs 0.579), +2.4 on NELL-995 (0.590 vs 0.566), and +1.6 on YAGO3-10 (0.631 vs 0.615). Among message-passing baselines, AdaProp [51] is the strongest (MRR 0.417/0.562/0.554/0.573), still trailing DuetGraph by 3.6–7.7 MRR points. Among transformer-based methods, SAttLE [22] and HittER [54] are the best, with DuetGraph exceeding them by 9.3–29.2 MRR points depending on dataset. On inductive reasoning (Table 1), DuetGraph leads KnowFormer by up to 8.7% in Hits@1 (e.g., 78.5 vs 77.0 on NELL-995 v1). On very large KGs (Table 3), DuetGraph beats rule-based AnyBURL [63] in MRR (0.363 vs 0.350 on Wikidata5M; 0.697 vs 0.588 on Freebase) while cutting inference time from 4,462s to 80s, and also surpasses KnowFormer in quality on both large-scale datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

DuetGraph departs from the dominant 'stacking' paradigm in hybrid KG reasoning (exemplified by KnowFormer, which sequentially applies message passing then transformer layers) by instead processing local and global information in parallel dual pathways before adaptive fusion. It builds directly on KnowFormer's query-aware message passing for the local pathway and SgFormer's simplified global attention for the global pathway, but reorganizes their composition from sequential to parallel. The coarse-to-fine inference stage additionally draws on the ensemble diversity principle, using an architecturally dissimilar coarse model (e.g., triplet-based HousE) to partition candidates before the fine dual-pathway model refines predictions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and formally addresses score over-smoothing as a distinct failure mode in hybrid KG reasoning, providing both a clean architectural fix (parallel dual pathways) and a theoretical characterization via singular-value bounds. The 1.8× training speedup from parallel execution is a practical win for large-scale KGs (demonstrated on Wikidata5M and Freebase). However, the approach introduces a dependency on a separate coarse model and a threshold hyperparameter Δ, and the theoretical bounds, while directionally correct, are quite loose. Open questions include whether the coarse-to-fine strategy extends naturally to multi-hop or relation-completion settings, and whether the parallel-pathway insight generalizes beyond KG reasoning to other graph tasks.
