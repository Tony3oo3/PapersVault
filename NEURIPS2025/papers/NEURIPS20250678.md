## Problem

Generative AI has not been widely adopted in engineering design due to its inability to simultaneously satisfy hard constraints, adhere to multidisciplinary physical laws, and meet human-centered design guidelines. Existing benchmarks either focus solely on distributional similarity, lack multiphysics and human-centered evaluation criteria, or only support a single class of generative models. BikeBench fills this void by providing a first-of-its-kind benchmark that evaluates parametric design synthesis across 10 multidisciplinary objectives and 40 constraints while enabling direct side-by-side comparison of LLMs, tabular generative models, optimization algorithms, and hybrid methods.

## Input / Output

Each benchmarking run takes a conditional specification (text prompt, rider anthropometric dimensions, and use case) and requires the model to produce 100 unique parametric bicycle designs, each defined by 64 mixed-type variables (continuous, boolean, categorical). Designs are evaluated by a suite of surrogate models and closed-form checks spanning aerodynamics (CFD-trained), structural mechanics (FEA-trained), ergonomics (kinematics solver), aesthetics (parametric-to-CLIP embedding), and human usability (10K human ratings), yielding four summary scores: hypervolume, mean constraint violation, MMD similarity, and DPP diversity. The 1.6M synthetic dataset provides multi-format representations (tabular, CAD/XML, SVG, PNG) enabling text-to-CAD, image-to-CAD, and parametric-to-image generation tasks.

## Method

BikeBench is a benchmarking framework combining five datasets (1.6M synthetic designs, 10K human ratings, 4K CFD simulations, plus adapted BIKED and FRAMED) with a configurable evaluation pipeline of 50 criteria. The key design insight is that high-performing models must strategically deviate from the training data manifold—for example, systematically thickening tubes to satisfy structural safety constraints that 95% of the dataset violates—making simultaneous optimization of quality, constraint satisfaction, and similarity inherently challenging. The benchmark supports multiple algorithm classes under standardized settings (1M evaluation budget, 100 conditions × 100 designs each), with configurable options for conditional generation, gradient-free evaluation, and masked constraints. Optimization-augmented generative models (OA-GAN, OA-VAE, OA-DDPM) use a differentiable aggregate quality metric combining all objectives and constraints with a DPP-based diversity loss during training, while LLMs are prompted with 25 valid and 25 invalid example designs plus their evaluation scores. Results show OA-DDPM variants achieve the best balanced performance, while LLMs (GPT-5-high) underperform across all four metrics.

## Results

Under the standard BikeBench settings (unmasked constraints, gradients, 1M evaluation budget), the optimization-augmented DDPM with guidance (OA-DDPM-G) achieves the best unconditional design quality (hypervolume 0.0195) and best dataset similarity (MMD 0.037), while OA-VAE leads in conditional quality (0.0186). GPT-5-high underperforms across all four metrics, ranking in the bottom three for every objective in both unconditional and conditional settings. The simple dataset subsampling baseline (quality 0.0151, zero constraint violations) proves surprisingly competitive, and pure tabular generative models (CTGAN, TVAE) generally match or fall below it in quality and similarity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CTGAN (Xu et al., NeurIPS 2019), the optimization-augmented models dominate on quality (OA-DDPM-G: 0.0195 vs. 0.0087 hypervolume) and similarity (0.037 vs. 0.147 MMD), though CTGAN achieves the best diversity score (7.10 vs. 13.03) in the unconditional setting. Against LibMoon gradient descent (Zhang et al., NeurIPS 2024), OA-DDPM-G wins on quality (0.0195 vs. 0.0164) and similarity (0.037 vs. 0.266), while gradient descent has fewer constraint violations (0.092 vs. 0.000—actually OA-DDPM-G has zero violations, so it wins there too). Against NSGA-II (Deb et al., 2002), the OA models clearly outperform on quality (0.0195 vs. 0.0102) and similarity (0.037 vs. 0.622), though NSGA-II achieves slightly better diversity (11.88 vs. 13.03). Notably, the plain tabular generative models (CTGAN, TVAE) without performance-aware training fail to beat the simple dataset subsampling baseline on quality or similarity, underscoring that distributional learning alone is insufficient for constrained multi-objective design. GPT-5-high, despite access to 50 example designs and full evaluation criteria, trails all other methods on every metric, with particularly poor constraint satisfaction (0.448 violations/design unconditional, 1.288 conditional) and the worst similarity scores (0.363/0.631 MMD).

## Relation to Prior Work

BikeBench builds directly on the BIKED and FRAMED datasets for bicycle design and structural mechanics, extending them with new synthetic data (1.6M designs), human-rated usability scores (10K), and CFD aerodynamics simulations (4K). Conceptually, it departs from prior engineering design benchmarks (which typically evaluate only parametric generative models or focus on question-answering over standards) by being the first to benchmark LLMs, tabular generative models, optimization algorithms, and hybrid GenAI+optimization methods side-by-side on a single constrained multi-objective design task. The optimization-augmented training formulation extends PaDGAN and DTAD to handle 10 objectives and 40 constraints simultaneously, while the evaluation suite (hypervolume, MMD, DPP diversity, constraint violation) draws on the multi-objective optimization and generative model evaluation literatures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

BikeBench fills a critical gap: no prior benchmark simultaneously evaluates parametric design generation across aerodynamics, structural mechanics, ergonomics, aesthetics, and human perception while comparing fundamentally different algorithm classes. The finding that even the top LLM (GPT-5-high) ranks in the bottom three on every metric, while the dataset itself violates an average of 2.7 constraints per design (95% failing planar safety), quantifies both the current limitations of LLMs in constrained tabular design and the systematic under-engineering bias in human design data. Open questions include whether larger-context LLMs, tabular adapters, or cross-conditional amortized optimization can close the gap, and whether the surrogate-based evaluators generalize beyond the diamond-frame design space. Limitations include simplified aerodynamics, surrogate model robustness outside training support, and exclusion of non-conventional topologies (recumbent, full-suspension).
