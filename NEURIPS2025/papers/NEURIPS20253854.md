## Problem

Estimating dexterous hand muscle EMG from accessible modalities like hand poses and keystrokes remains substantially underexplored, as EMG carries richer semantic information than posture alone and no strict one-to-one mapping exists. General EMG-pose datasets fail to transfer to piano-specific dexterous finger movements, and no existing piano dataset simultaneously provides high-quality pose, audio, MIDI, and hand muscle EMG data needed for fine-grained muscle dynamics modeling.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253854/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The network consumes multi-view projected 2D hand keypoints (P ∈ R^{T×V×J×2}, from three 60 FPS camera views via HaMeR/MANO) and an 88-channel keystroke displacement sequence at 1000 Hz. Depth information is deliberately omitted due to insufficient Z-axis accuracy from single-frame estimation; instead, a custom cross-view fusion encoder reconstructs 3D postural semantics from 2D projections. The output is a 6-channel hand muscle EMG signal (ADM, PB, 1DI–4DI) at 1000 Hz, normalized to [−1, 1] via per-muscle MVC scaling, produced autoregressively one timestep at a time.

## Method

PianoKPM Net employs a multi-branch encoder–decoder architecture: input-specific encoders extract shallow features from pose and keystrokes independently, a latent-prior encoder injects pre-trained pose-to-EMG statistical correlations (learned via contrastive pretraining on the emg2pose dataset), and a time-channel-wise encoder built on revised TDS blocks treats the fused feature vector as a 2D pseudo-image for separable temporal and channel-wise convolution. An auto-regressive MLP decoder (without teacher forcing) reconstructs EMG sequentially, conditioning each timestep on the previous prediction. Training uses a Precision-Structure Hybrid Loss combining MSE for pointwise accuracy with a Sinkhorn-based Optimal Transport loss that preserves global muscle synergy structure and activation transitions, preventing the over-smoothing typical of MSE-only training.

## Results

On the PianoKPM Dataset with the full multimodal input (pose + keystroke), PianoKPM Net achieves RMSE 0.134±0.061 and OTD 0.031±0.058, outperforming NeuroPose (RMSE 0.155, OTD 0.061) and CodeTalker (RMSE 0.143, OTD 0.040). On the public emg2pose benchmark with pose-only input, PianoKPM Net also leads with RMSE 0.055±0.034 and OTD 0.030±0.037, compared to NeuroPose (0.067/0.039) and CodeTalker (0.096/0.075). In held-out evaluations, Cross-User generalization yields RMSE 0.209 and OTD 0.095, while Cross-Task generalization degrades further to RMSE 0.264 and OTD 0.152, with 30% of unseen task data reducing estimation error by 63.1%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PianoKPM Net consistently outperforms both baselines on the primary multimodal setting (pose + keystroke) on PianoKPM: it achieves RMSE 0.134 vs. 0.155 (NeuroPose) and 0.143 (CodeTalker), and OTD 0.031 vs. 0.061 and 0.040 respectively. On pose-only input, the advantage is even more pronounced, with PianoKPM Net reducing RMSE by ~18% over NeuroPose (0.136 vs. 0.166) and ~20% over CodeTalker (0.136 vs. 0.171), while cutting OTD roughly in half. On the public emg2pose benchmark, PianoKPM Net also leads both baselines on both metrics. The one setting where PianoKPM Net does not appear is keystroke-only input, where CodeTalker's transformer-based self-attention captures long temporal dependencies more effectively (RMSE 0.152 vs. NeuroPose's 0.196), suggesting that a transformer encoder for keystroke sequences could further strengthen the proposed architecture.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper inverts the well-studied EMG-to-pose direction (emg2pose, NeuroPose) to tackle the underexplored pose-to-EMG problem, specifically for small hand muscles during piano performance. Architecturally, it repurposes the Time-Depth Separable (TDS) convolution block from speech recognition [76] as a time-channel-wise encoder and introduces an auto-regressive MLP decoder, while the loss function borrows Sinkhorn-based Optimal Transport from muscle synergy analysis [80]. The preliminary pretraining on emg2pose and its failure to transfer to PianoMotion10M motivates the construction of a domain-specific multimodal dataset, positioning the work at the intersection of cross-modal synthesis (analogous to text-to-image or audio-to-motion) and neuromuscular modeling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The PianoKPM Dataset is the first large-scale, publicly available EMG dataset of professional piano performance (20 pianists, 12.64 hours, 6 hand muscles at 2000 Hz), filling a critical gap for dexterous motor control research that prior datasets (Ninapro, emg2pose, PiMForce) address only for gross motor movements. The finding that cross-task generalization is substantially harder than cross-user generalization, and that only 10–30% of unseen-domain data is needed to recover most accuracy, provides practical guidance for deploying such systems. Open questions include whether the approach extends to bimanual coordination, intermediate/novice skill levels, and whether transformer-based encoders (which outperform the CNN on keystroke-only input) can close the remaining gap in multimodal settings.
