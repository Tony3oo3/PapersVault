## Problem

Prior 'learning to bid' work models online advertising as a contextual bandit with immediate rewards, neglecting delayed long-term ad effects, cumulative reinforcement/fatigue, and customer heterogeneity. While Badanidiyuru et al. (2023) introduces MDP-based modeling of delayed Poisson rewards, it assumes homogeneous treatment effects across users. No existing theoretical framework jointly addresses all three aspects—delayed effects, cumulative impacts, and personalization—in both modeling and algorithm design.

## Input / Output

The learner receives, per customer t and round h, a context vector x_t ∈ R^d, a compact two-dimensional state S_h^t = [S_{h,1}^t, S_{h,2}^t] encoding time since the last impression and the interval between the two most recent impressions, the realized highest other bid m_h^t (lognormal), the binary bidding outcome o_h^t, and a Poisson product conversion y_h^t. It outputs a bid amount a_h^t ∈ [0, B_A] each round, a full H-round policy π_t computed via dynamic programming, and online estimates of instant ad effects θ_l, delayed impact factors d_l, and transition parameters β_h, σ_h. The state deliberately tracks only the two most recent impressions (recency effect), keeping the state space compact while context x_t is treated as auxiliary information rather than part of the state.

## Method

The paper models auto-bidding as a Contextual MDP with delayed Poisson rewards, where context captures personalization and state captures cumulative ad effects. The central technical contribution is a Two-Stage Maximum Likelihood Estimator (TS-MLE) combined with a data-splitting strategy: observations from rounds where the bid was won (W_{t,l}) estimate θ_l via an online Newton estimator with truncated mean (handling heavy-tailed Poisson data), while observations from rounds where the bid was lost (D_{t,l}) estimate d_l by plugging in the θ estimates into a concave conditional log-likelihood. This separation ensures the estimation error of d_l depends only on the error of θ_l, preventing intractable feedback loops. The overall algorithm (Algorithm 1) alternates between a forced exploration phase (guaranteeing minimum observations per state), an exploitation phase (greedy policy via dynamic programming within confidence regions), and per-round estimation updates, achieving a near-optimal regret bound of Õ(dH²√T) that matches the known Ω(√T) lower bound for CMDPs.

## Results

Algorithm 1 achieves a fitted regret order of 0.37 (sublinear, consistent with the theoretical Õ(√T) bound), while all three baselines exhibit linear regret (order 1.0). At T=20,000 customers, Algorithm 1 incurs a cumulative regret of 8,525, compared to 9,568 for Aggressive Bidding, 76,945 for Random Bidding, and 184,873 for Passive Bidding. The theoretical near-optimal regret bound is Õ(dH²√T), matching the Ω(√T) lower bound for CMDPs with known transitions up to logarithmic factors.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares Algorithm 1 against three fixed (non-adaptive) bidding policies: Aggressive Bidding (always wins, o_t=[1,1,1]), Random Bidding (uniform over all 8 outcome sequences), and Passive Bidding (never wins, o_t=[0,0,0]). Algorithm 1 substantially outperforms all baselines in cumulative regret at T=20,000: it achieves 8,525 versus 9,568 (Aggressive), 76,945 (Random), and 184,873 (Passive). Critically, Algorithm 1 is the only method with sublinear regret (fitted order 0.37), while all three baselines scale linearly (order 1.0), confirming that adaptive learning is essential for long-horizon bidding. The Aggressive Bidding baseline is the strongest competitor since the simulated environment makes always-winning close to optimal, yet Algorithm 1 still achieves lower regret by adapting to individual customer contexts and delayed reward structures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the CMDP framework of Hallak et al. [2015] to model auto-bidding with delayed Poisson rewards, and builds on the MDP formulation of Badanidiyuru et al. [2023] which handles delayed and mixed rewards but assumes homogeneous treatment effects across users. For estimation, it adapts the Confidence Region with Truncated Mean (CRTM) algorithm from Xue et al. [2024] for the first-stage estimation of ad impact parameters, and introduces a novel two-stage MLE with a data-splitting strategy to decouple estimation errors of delayed-impact parameters from first-stage parameter errors. The key departure from prior 'learning to bid' work (Weed et al., Feng et al., Han et al.) is the explicit modeling of cumulative ad effects (reinforcement/fatigue) and long-term delayed conversions, which bandit-based approaches assume away by treating rewards as immediate.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first theoretical work to jointly address delayed effects, cumulative ad impacts, and customer heterogeneity in online bidding, closing a gap the authors identify as overlooked in the 'learning to bid' literature. The near-optimal regret bound of Õ(dH²√T) provides a clean performance guarantee for personalized auto-bidding under realistic delayed-reward dynamics. However, several important limitations remain: the model assumes linear conversion functions, relies on a recency-bias state formulation (only two most recent impressions), excludes budget constraints, and is validated only through simulation with small H=3 and d=2. Real-world validation and extension to budget-constrained settings are identified as critical open problems.
