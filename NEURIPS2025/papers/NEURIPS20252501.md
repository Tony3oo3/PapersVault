## Problem

Existing fine-tuning methods for conditional video diffusion models (ControlNet, IP-Adapter, latent concatenation) require large datasets, introduce external encoders or architectural modifications, and are restricted to spatially aligned conditioning signals. This limits flexibility for variable-length or misaligned condition-target pairs and makes adaptation to new tasks computationally expensive. The paper asks whether pretrained video diffusion models can be adapted to diverse conditional generation tasks with minimal data (10–30 samples) and no architectural changes.

## Input / Output

The method accepts a variable-length condition (a single reference image replicated to 4 latent frames, or a short video clip of up to L latent frames) together with a structured text prompt that explicitly labels the condition, buffer, and target segments. It produces a video of K target latent frames (9 frames in the default 13-latent-frame configuration, corresponding to 49 RGB frames at 10 fps, 480×480 or 848×480). The condition length L is flexible, enabling tasks ranging from single-image-to-video to full video-to-video translation within one unified framework.

## Method

TIC-FT concatenates condition and target latents along the temporal axis and inserts B intermediate buffer frames whose noise levels increase linearly from 0 to T (τ̃_b = b/(B+1)·T), using noised versions of the condition frames to bridge the abrupt scene and noise-level transition. During inference, the model iteratively denoises only the frames currently at the maximum noise level while keeping condition frames clean, and during training the diffusion loss is computed exclusively on target frames so buffer frames are free to evolve naturally. Fine-tuning uses LoRA (rank 128) on a DiT backbone (CogVideoX-5B or Wan-14B) with as few as 10–30 paired samples and fewer than 1,000 steps, requiring no additional modules or architectural modifications. This temporal-reasoning design aligns the fine-tuning distribution with the pretrained model's smooth temporal dynamics, avoiding the detail loss of external encoders and the rigid spatial-alignment constraints of ControlNet-style methods.

## Results

On I2V tasks (object-to-motion, character-to-video), TIC-FT (6K steps) achieves the best perceptual similarity scores across all baselines: CLIP-I 0.8329, LPIPS 0.4332, SSIM 0.5917, and DINO 0.5530, while also leading in GPT-4o structural (3.14) and semantic (3.63) similarity. On V2V (video style transfer), the gains are even more pronounced, with TIC-FT reaching CLIP-I 0.8794, LPIPS 0.2251, and DINO 0.6745—substantially outperforming all baselines. Notably, TIC-FT already achieves competitive results after only 2,000 training steps (CLIP-I 0.8066 on I2V, 0.8174 on V2V), demonstrating strong few-shot efficiency with just 20 training samples.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TIC-FT consistently outperforms all three baselines on perceptual similarity metrics, which measure condition fidelity. On I2V, TIC-FT (6K) achieves CLIP-I 0.8329 vs. ControlNet (0.7349), Fun-pose (0.7714), and SIC-FT-Replace (0.7993), with LPIPS of 0.4332 vs. 0.6535, 0.6339, and 0.6190 respectively. The advantage is even larger on V2V style transfer, where TIC-FT reaches DINO 0.6745 compared to ControlNet (0.2839), Fun-pose (0.4369), and SIC-FT-Replace (0.4025)—a gap of 0.24–0.39 points. On GPT-4o structural and semantic similarity, TIC-FT leads all baselines on both I2V (3.14/3.63) and V2V (3.90/4.41), while ControlNet trails significantly (2.69/2.69 on I2V, 2.23/2.41 on V2V). Fun-pose achieves the highest aesthetic quality score (4.24 on V2V) but still lags substantially on condition alignment metrics. The paper attributes ControlNet's weakness to its rigid spatial-alignment requirement and Fun-pose/SIC-FT-Replace's limitations to architectural modifications and SDEdit-style inference that degrade condition propagation.

## Relation to Prior Work

TIC-FT repurposes the in-context learning paradigm from LLMs and image diffusion (SIC-FT, in-context LoRA) by replacing spatial grid concatenation with temporal concatenation along the video's time axis, a natural fit for video diffusion models that process sequentially ordered frames. The buffer frame design—intermediate frames with monotonically increasing noise levels—draws directly on the heterogeneous-noise-level insights from FIFO-Diffusion and Diffusion Forcing, which showed that diffusion models can operate on sequences with varying per-frame noise. The method deliberately departs from ControlNet-style external encoders and SDEdit-style inpainting, which the authors argue introduce detail loss, rigid spatial-alignment constraints, and quality degradation, instead relying on the pretrained model's native 3D attention to process condition and target frames jointly.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is substantial: fine-tuning a 5B or 14B video diffusion model to perform diverse conditional tasks (I2V, V2V, style transfer, motion transfer, keyframe interpolation) with only 10–30 paired samples in under an hour on a single GPU is a meaningful efficiency gain over ControlNet or full fine-tuning pipelines that require large datasets and additional architectural modules. The unified temporal-concatenation framework eliminates task-specific conditioning pipelines, which could lower the barrier to deploying custom conditional video generators. Key open questions remain: the method still requires per-task fine-tuning (not true training-free ICL), assumes paired condition-target data, and has not been validated on long-horizon or highly dynamic human-motion sequences. Whether a single TIC-FT model can generalize across heterogeneous tasks without retraining is an important direction the authors flag but do not address.
