## Problem

Implicit neural representations (INRs) suffer from spectral bias, learning low-frequency components far faster than high-frequency ones, which is critical when modeling PDE solutions with sharp transitions and fine-scale oscillations. Existing spectral-bias mitigations (SIREN, Fourier features, Fourier reparameterization) are restricted to single-instance settings, while the modulated-INR paradigm needed for scalable multi-sample representation (Functa) lacks any mechanism to control spectral content in the weight space. This paper bridges that gap by extending Fourier reparameterization into the modulated-INR setting and building a bidirectional operator-learning framework on top of it.

## Input / Output

The INR backbone (SIREN, 5 layers × 256 hidden units) receives spatio-temporal coordinates (x, t) and produces scalar field values at arbitrary resolution. A 20-dimensional latent vector z, learned per sample via bilevel optimization, is mapped through a two-layer modulation MLP (hidden dim 512) to produce layer-wise modulation vectors that adjust the Fourier-basis coefficients in the weight space. For PDEfuncta, two such INR backbones (f_a, f_u) share a single z, enabling joint compression of paired fields (e.g., velocity model + seismic wavefield) and bidirectional inference between the two function spaces without retraining.

## Method

Global Fourier Modulation (GFM) reparameterizes each hidden-layer weight matrix as W^k = (R^k + α^{k,W}·1^T)·Φ^k, where Φ^k is a fixed Fourier basis of D = (n_low + n_high)·n_phase cosine functions with mixed low/high frequencies and uniform phase shifts, R^k is a shared learnable base matrix, and α^{k,W} is a sample-specific modulation vector that shifts the spectral coefficients. Unlike Shift/Scale/FiLM which modulate activations, GFM modulates in the frequency domain, inheriting the gradient-ratio guarantee of Fourier reparameterization (Theorem 1) while scaling to multi-sample settings. PDEfuncta instantiates two GFM-modulated INR backbones for paired function spaces A and U, both conditioned on the same latent z through separate modulation networks, enabling a single forward pass to reconstruct either field and thus supporting both forward (G: A→U) and inverse (G†: U→A) operator inference. Training follows a MAML-style bilevel loop: the inner loop optimizes per-sample latents against reconstruction loss, while the outer loop updates shared backbone and modulation parameters on batch-averaged loss.

## Results

GFM achieves the highest PSNR and lowest MSE across all five PDE benchmarks (Convection, Helmholtz #1/#2, Navier-Stokes, Kuramoto-Sivashinsky), improving PSNR by up to 15 dB over the strongest baseline (FiLM) on the convection equation and by 3–7 dB on the remaining datasets. On the neural operator benchmark, PDEfuncta attains the lowest L2 error on Euler-NACA Airfoil (0.0051) and competitive performance on Pipe (0.0081), outperforming FNO (0.0385/0.0153), Geo-FNO (0.0158/0.0066), CORAL (0.0059/0.0120), and MARBLE (0.0058/0.0103). In the generalization experiment on unseen convection coefficients, GFM reaches 32.6 dB PSNR via latent interpolation versus 10.6 dB for FiLM, demonstrating that the learned latent space encodes smooth parametric structure.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the three standard modulation baselines (Shift, Scale, FiLM from Functa/DINO), GFM dominates on every dataset: it gains 15.1 dB over FiLM on Convection, 5.5 dB on Helmholtz #1, 6.3 dB on Helmholtz #2, 2.9 dB on Navier-Stokes, and 7.5 dB on Kuramoto-Sivashinsky, while reducing MSE by 1–2 orders of magnitude. On the neural operator benchmark (Table 4), PDEfuncta achieves the best L2 error on Euler-NACA Airfoil (0.0051 vs. 0.0058 for MARBLE and 0.0059 for CORAL) and is competitive on Pipe (0.0081 vs. 0.0066 for Geo-FNO), while substantially outperforming FNO (0.0385/0.0153) and UNet (0.0505/0.0298). The key differentiator is that PDEfuncta supports bidirectional inference (forward G and inverse G†) without retraining, whereas all neural operator baselines are limited to one-way mapping. Spatial Functa [37], which applies per-location modulation, is slower (0.59 s/epoch vs. 0.27 s for GFM) and does not match GFM's reconstruction quality. Overall, GFM/PDEfuncta wins or ties on every reported metric across all seven datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two previously separate research threads: the Functa paradigm (Dupont et al., 2022), which compresses datasets into a shared modulated INR via low-dimensional latent codes, and Fourier reparameterization (Shi et al., CVPR 2024), which mitigates spectral bias in single-instance INRs by projecting weights into a fixed Fourier basis. The key conceptual departure is that prior modulation methods (Shift, Scale, FiLM) operate exclusively in activation space with no explicit frequency control, while Fourier reparameterization was restricted to single-instance settings. GFM bridges this gap by injecting sample-specific modulation directly into the Fourier coefficient space, and PDEfuncta extends the single-field Functa setup to paired function spaces with a shared latent, enabling bidirectional inference that one-way neural operators (FNO, DeepONet, CORAL) cannot provide. The bilevel training loop follows the MAML-style inner/outer structure from the Functa framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical value lies in a compact (20-dim) latent representation that simultaneously handles high-frequency PDE content and supports bidirectional forward/inverse mapping without retraining — a capability directly relevant to inverse problems like full waveform inversion and aerodynamic design. The latent space generalization results (interpolating to unseen PDE coefficients, extrapolating from partial observations) suggest the learned manifold captures meaningful parametric structure, which could reduce the need for re-simulations in parameter sweeps. Limitations include dataset-specific frequency-ratio hyperparameters that require manual tuning, bilevel training overhead, and evaluation primarily on 1D–2D PDEs with relatively modest spatial resolution; scaling to 3D, high-resolution, or strongly nonlinear regimes remains open. The neural operator comparison shows only marginal gains over CORAL/MARBLE on the Pipe benchmark, suggesting the bidirectional advantage is most pronounced in the compression/reconstruction setting rather than pure operator accuracy.
