## Problem

Existing face aging methods suffer from an inherent Age-ID trade-off: they either produce realistic age transformations that drift in identity or preserve identity at the cost of inaccurate aging, especially for large age gaps. No prior approach achieves a balanced, seamless cradle-to-cane transformation across the full lifespan while remaining robust to in-the-wild conditions such as occlusions and extreme poses.

## Input / Output

The method takes a source face image (512×512, background removed via CarveKit), a target age, and a gender label, which are combined into a CLIP text prompt of the form 'a face image of a {target age} years old {gender}'. It produces a single aged face image at the target age via a 4-step SDXL-Turbo diffusion pipeline, with inference time of 0.56 s per image. The two-pass design means the first pass output is an intermediate latent that feeds directly into the second pass rather than being decoded and re-encoded.

## Method

Cradle2Cane decouples age accuracy and identity preservation into two jointly trained passes on SDXL-Turbo. The first pass applies Adaptive Noise Injection (AdaNI), which selects one of three noise levels based on the magnitude of the age gap (≤5, 5–20, >20 years) to control editability, conditioned on CLIP text embeddings of age and gender. The second pass reinforces identity by conditioning the denoiser on two disentangled identity embeddings: SVR-ArcFace, which applies singular-value reweighting to an ArcFace embedding matrix built from multiple aged variants of the same face to suppress age-correlated components, and Rotate-CLIP, which shifts the CLIP image embedding toward the target age via spherical linear interpolation (slerp) between source and target age text embeddings. Both passes are trained end-to-end with LoRA on the U-Net, optimizing a combined loss of MS-SSIM/ArcFace identity similarity, MiVOLO-based age accuracy, and LPIPS/GAN perceptual quality.

## Results

On CelebA-HQ (Face++ protocol), Cradle2Cane achieves the lowest age estimation error (±7.47 years) and the highest Harmonic Consistency Score (HCS 81.33), while maintaining competitive identity similarity (81.34). Under the Qwen-VL protocol, it further reduces age error to ±4.62 years with the best HCS (78.33) and image quality (92.37). On the in-the-wild CelebA-HQ subset, Cradle2Cane attains the best HCS (75.94) with only ±5.05 years age error, outperforming all baselines. Inference takes just 0.56 s per image using only 10K training samples, comparable to GAN-based methods and far faster than other diffusion-based approaches (8.84–61.26 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Cradle2Cane outperforms all six face-aging baselines on the primary HCS metric across both evaluation protocols. Against GAN-based methods, it beats SAM (HCS 80.42→81.33 Face++) and CUSP (80.67→81.33) on age accuracy while matching or exceeding their identity scores; HRFAE retains the highest raw ID similarity (94.32) but suffers a much larger age error (±15.12 vs. ±7.47), yielding a lower HCS (74.95). Against diffusion-based baselines, Cradle2Cane reduces age error from ±14.47 (FADING) and ±11.95 (IPFE) to ±7.47 (Face++) / ±4.62 (Qwen-VL), while also being 100×+ faster than FADING (0.56 s vs. 61.26 s) and 16× faster than IPFE (0.56 s vs. 8.84 s). On the in-the-wild subset, Cradle2Cane achieves the best HCS (75.94), surpassing FADING (75.06) and CUSP (70.94), where most GAN-based methods degrade sharply. In the supplementary Table 4 (AgeDB-30, CACD, FG-NET), Cradle2Cane also outperforms generic editing systems StyleCLIP and FaceDNeRF on HCS across all three datasets (e.g., 79.00 vs. 62.64 on AgeDB-30), confirming its advantage over non-dedicated face-editing approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the few-step text-to-image diffusion model SDXL-Turbo (adversarial diffusion distillation) as its backbone, departing from both GAN-based face aging methods (SAM, CUSP, Lifespan, HRFAE) and prior diffusion-based approaches (FADING, IPFE) that attempt to handle the full lifespan in a single unified pass. The key conceptual departure is decoupling age accuracy and identity preservation into two sequential diffusion passes: the first uses adaptive noise injection (AdaNI) with text-guided conditioning for age control, while the second refines identity via novel SVR-ArcFace and Rotate-CLIP embeddings that explicitly suppress age-related components from identity features. The identity-disentanglement strategy draws on SVD-based singular value reweighting and spherical linear interpolation in CLIP space, extending ideas from face clustering and texture editing literature into the face aging setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Age-ID trade-off is a fundamental bottleneck in face aging that has resisted resolution in unified frameworks; Cradle2Cane's two-pass decoupling provides a clean architectural answer that achieves competitive age accuracy (±4.62 years via Qwen-VL) and identity preservation simultaneously, with fast inference (0.56s) and a small training set (10K images). The demonstrated robustness on in-the-wild images—where GAN-based and prior diffusion methods frequently fail—broadens practical applicability to unconstrained real-world scenarios. However, the method still loses non-facial details (accessories, clothing) under extreme age gaps, and the discrete three-level noise schedule (thresholds at 5 and 20 years) is a coarse approximation that could benefit from continuous or learned noise scheduling. Open questions include whether the two-pass paradigm generalizes to other multi-attribute face editing tasks and whether the identity-age entanglement in ArcFace/CLIP can be further mitigated.
