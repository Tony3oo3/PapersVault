## Problem

Post-training methods such as supervised fine-tuning (SFT) do not explicitly incentivize language models to retrieve and apply the parametric knowledge encoded during pretraining. The paper shows that vanilla SFT shifts the FFN key-coefficient distribution toward fewer active memory vectors, increasing overfitting risk and leaving substantial pretrained capability unrealized on downstream tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253414/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on pretrained autoregressive Transformers (Qwen-2.5, LLaMA-3, Gemma-2 at 1B–9B scale) during standard SFT on text sequences. The steering intervention is applied at the FFN layer level: for SwiGLU variants (used in all tested models), the key coefficients are the element-wise product σ(hW_gate) ⊙ (hW_up), i.e., the input just before the down-projection that associates with memory vectors. At inference time, the steered model allocates more representational capacity to semantically rich tokens while spending less on transitional ones (e.g., commas, conjunctions).

## Method

InfoSteer treats each FFN layer as a key-value memory where scalar key coefficients k_i = σ(hW_up)_i weight memory vectors v_i (rows of W_down), so the FFN output is the weighted sum Σ k_i v_i. Two complementary strategies promote higher-entropy key distributions during SFT: (i) an intervention method that identifies the bottom p% of key coefficients per layer and rescales them to α times the layer mean during the forward pass, and (ii) an entropy regularization term −λ Σ_l H(k̂^(l)) added to the LM loss, where k̂ is the normalized key distribution. Both methods are lightweight, require no architectural changes, and integrate seamlessly into a vanilla SFT pipeline. The paper further introduces an information surrogate φ_i = v_i · W_decode to characterize what each memory vector encodes, enabling fine-grained, topic-targeted steering of specific knowledge regions.

## Results

Across six model families (Qwen-2.5-1.5B/7B, LLaMA-3.2-1B/3-8B, Gemma-2-2B/9B) and eight commonsense/knowledge benchmarks, InfoSteer with intervention consistently outperforms vanilla SFT, with the largest gains on HellaSwag (e.g., Gemma-2-9B: 95.7% vs. 94.8% vanilla SFT, 90.1% base; LLaMA-3-8B: 96.3% vs. 93.5% vanilla SFT, 90.8% base). On OOD arithmetic generalization (trained on GSM8K, evaluated on five held-out datasets), intervention steering achieves 86.6% average OOD accuracy versus 83.7% for vanilla SFT and 85.3% for the base model, while also improving ID accuracy from 63.7% to 66.8%. Gains are most pronounced on knowledge-intensive and reading-comprehension tasks (+3.3 and +3.9 average over vanilla SFT) but slightly negative on purely linguistic tasks (−0.3).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares four conditions: the pretrained base model, standard vanilla SFT, and two InfoSteer variants (intervention and entropy regularization). Both steering methods consistently outperform vanilla SFT across all six model families and all eight ID benchmarks, with intervention generally edging out regularization by 0.3–1.0 points on average. The most dramatic gains appear on HellaSwag, where intervention steering adds +5.6 points over the Gemma-2-9B base (95.7 vs. 90.1) and +5.5 over LLaMA-3-8B base (96.3 vs. 90.8), substantially exceeding the +4.7 and +2.7 gains from vanilla SFT alone. On OOD arithmetic generalization, vanilla SFT actually degrades performance (83.7 vs. 85.3 base), while intervention steering improves both ID (66.8 vs. 65.7) and OOD (86.6 vs. 83.7), indicating that steering mitigates the overfitting tendency of naive SFT. Gains are task-dependent: knowledge and reading-comprehension tasks benefit most (+3.3, +3.9 over vanilla SFT), math improves modestly (+1.1), and purely linguistic tasks show a slight regression (−0.3), suggesting that encouraging broader memory engagement is most valuable when the task requires retrieving stored parametric knowledge.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper operationalizes the key-value memory interpretation of Transformer FFN layers established by Geva et al. (2021) and the soft-addressing formulation from Weston et al. (2014) as a concrete intervention target during post-training. While prior model-steering work (activation steering, representation engineering) modifies hidden-state vectors or adds auxiliary objectives, InfoSteer specifically targets the key-coefficient distribution that gates memory-vector engagement, using either forward-pass intervention on low-activation keys or entropy regularization in the loss. It also extends the knowledge-editing literature (Meng et al., 2022) by not editing specific memory vectors but rather reshaping which vectors are activated, and adapts the mechanism to SwiGLU variants used in modern architectures (Qwen, LLaMA, Gemma).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that vanilla SFT systematically concentrates key-coefficient distributions (reducing memory-vector engagement) and thereby degrades OOD generalization while improving ID accuracy is practically important: it suggests a simple, architecture-agnostic fix for a common post-training failure mode. The Information Flux analysis offering a token-level interpretability signal without external probes (e.g., SAEs) is a promising direction for self-interpretable LMs. Key limitations include evaluation restricted to SFT (not RL or DPO), moderate absolute gains (1–5%), and the fine-grained steering strategies in the appendix remaining largely unvalidated at scale. Open questions include whether steered RL or inference-time steering can amplify these effects, and whether the middle-layer sensitivity pattern generalizes to deeper or MoE architectures.
