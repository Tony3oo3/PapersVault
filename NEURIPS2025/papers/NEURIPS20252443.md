## Problem

Thompson sampling is widely used in reinforcement learning but lacks tight theoretical guarantees in continuous-state settings; existing analyses either assume discrete state-action spaces, linear dynamics, or yield regret bounds that scale poorly with state dimensionality. This paper establishes the first high-probability no-regret guarantee for TS in finite-horizon episodic MDPs where both rewards and transitions are jointly modeled by a multi-output Gaussian process.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252443/cropped_page8_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The agent operates in an episodic MDP with continuous state and action spaces, receiving a state vector at each step, selecting an action, and observing a scalar reward and next state. The multi-output GP kernel jointly models the reward and transition functions, capturing their correlations via a linear model of coregionalization (LMC). At the start of each episode, the algorithm samples a full realization of both functions from the GP posterior and computes a complete policy via backward induction over the horizon H, which is then executed without further model updates until the next episode.

## Method

RL-GPS samples reward and transition functions from a multi-output GP posterior at the start of each episode, computes proxy Q-functions via recursive Bellman updates (backward induction), and executes the induced greedy policy for the episode. The theoretical analysis overcomes two structural challenges: value functions are recursive compositions of GPs (not GPs themselves), and TS operates on proxy models from Bellman updates rather than the true posterior over optimal values. To handle the first, the paper derives high-probability confidence bounds for composed GPs (Theorem 1) and extends them to recursive value functions (Corollary 1). To handle the second, it introduces a multi-output elliptical potential lemma (Lemma 1) that exploits inter-dimensional correlations captured by the kernel, and a delayed-update variant (Lemma 2) that accounts for batched observations within episodes, yielding the regret bound Õ(√(KHΓ(KH))) where Γ captures GP model complexity.

## Results

The paper establishes a high-probability regret bound of Õ(√(KHΓ(KH))) for Thompson Sampling in episodic MDPs with multi-output GP models, where Γ(·) is the GP information gain. For Matérn kernels with smoothness ν > 1, this yields regret Õ(T^((ν+d)/(2ν+d))), and for RBF kernels, Õ(√T). Empirically, cumulative regret grows sublinearly across all tested kernels (RBF, Matérn ν=2.5, Matérn ν=1.5) over K=1000 episodes in GP-sampled MDPs, with RBF achieving the lowest regret in smooth environments and Matérn ν=1.5 performing best in sparse, rough navigation tasks. The LMC multi-output structure (capturing reward-transition correlations) substantially reduces regret compared to independent GPs in the maze environment.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not compare RL-GPS against competing RL algorithms (e.g., UCB-based methods, PSRL, or kernelized RL baselines); its experiments are designed to validate the theoretical regret scaling rather than benchmark against alternatives. The empirical comparisons are between kernel choices and model structures: (1) In smooth GP-sampled MDPs, RBF yields the lowest cumulative regret, followed by Matérn ν=2.5, then Matérn ν=1.5, consistent with the theoretical prediction that smoother kernels have lower information gain. (2) In sparse navigation and maze tasks with rough ground-truth dynamics, the ordering reverses—Matérn kernels outperform RBF because RBF's strong smoothness prior causes model misspecification. (3) Using the Linear Model of Coregionalization (LMC) to capture inter-output correlations between reward and transition functions reduces regret compared to modeling them as independent GPs, particularly in the structured maze environment. The paper positions its contribution as the first high-probability (frequentist) regret bound for TS in continuous-state MDPs with joint GP modeling, contrasting with prior Bayesian regret results for PSRL (Osband et al., 2013) and kernelized RL (Chowdhury and Gopalan, 2019), but does not provide head-to-head algorithmic comparisons.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the TS regret analysis lineage from GP bandits (Chowdhury and Gopalan, 2017) and finite MDPs (Osband et al., 2013; Osband and Van Roy, 2017) to continuous-state episodic MDPs with joint multi-output GP models of rewards and transitions, following the modeling framework of Bayrooti et al. (2025). It departs from prior RL-TS analyses by providing high-probability (frequentist) bounds rather than Bayesian regret, and by removing linearity, discretization, and output-independence assumptions. The key analytical tools—a multi-output elliptical potential lemma and confidence bounds for compositional GP functions—are novel extensions of the scalar GP potential lemma (Srinivas et al., 2010) and batched GP analysis (Calandriello et al., 2020).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first high-probability no-regret guarantee for TS in continuous-state episodic MDPs under a flexible GP model, closing a gap where prior results required discrete spaces, linear structure, or RKHS assumptions. The multi-output elliptical potential lemma and compositional GP confidence bounds are reusable tools for any setting involving recursive value functions over vector-valued GP models. Key limitations include the joint-Gaussian assumption on dynamics, the twice-differentiability requirement on value functions, and restriction to finite-horizon episodic settings; extending to infinite-horizon or non-Gaussian dynamics remains open. Practically, the results formalize why kernel choice (RBF vs. Matérn) and output-correlation structure (LMC) matter for sample efficiency in GP-based RL.
