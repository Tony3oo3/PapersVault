## Problem

Distributional off-policy evaluation (OPE) aims to estimate the full counterfactual outcome distribution under a target policy, enabling risk measures beyond the mean (e.g., CVaR, quantiles). Existing distributional OPE methods rely on CDFs, which are unsuitable for structured outcomes like images or graphs, while counterfactual mean embeddings (CME) handle complex outcomes but are restricted to binary treatments and lack formal OPE analysis with estimated nuisance parameters. This paper bridges the gap by extending kernel mean embeddings to general stochastic policies in the OPE setting with doubly robust estimation.

## Input / Output

The method consumes logged observational data (covariates x_i, actions a_i, outcomes y_i) drawn under a logging policy π₀, along with a user-specified target policy π and RKHS kernels over the joint action-context space and the outcome space. Outcomes can be scalar, continuous, or structured (e.g., 64×64 images in the dSprites experiment). The primary output is the CPME — a vector in the outcome RKHS that uniquely encodes the counterfactual outcome distribution under π; from this, the framework also produces a p-value for testing distributional equivalence between two policies (DR-KPT) and deterministic samples from the counterfactual distribution via kernel herding.

## Method

The CPME is defined as the kernel mean embedding of the counterfactual outcome distribution and is identified as C_{Y|A,X} μ_π, where C_{Y|A,X} is the conditional mean operator and μ_π is the joint policy-context mean embedding. A plug-in estimator combines a regularized least-squares estimate of C_{Y|A,X} with an empirical kernel mean embedding of μ_π, achieving O(n^{-1/4}) convergence. The key contribution is deriving the Hilbert-space-valued efficient influence function (EIF) for the CPME, yielding a one-step doubly robust estimator that corrects plug-in bias and achieves the parametric O(n^{-1/2}) rate when nuisance estimators converge at n^{-1/4}. For hypothesis testing, the difference of EIFs for two policies is plugged into a cross U-statistic (following Kim and Ramdas), producing the DR-KPT statistic with proven asymptotic normality. Sampling from the counterfactual distribution is performed via a deterministic kernel herding procedure applied to the estimated CPME.

## Results

On the Warfarin dataset, DR-KPT-rbf achieves near-nominal rejection rates under the null (0.02 vs. target 0.05) and strong power in alternative scenarios (0.80, 0.99, 0.76 for Scenarios II–IV), while KPT-rbf and KPT-poly collapse to near-zero power (≤0.01) in most alternatives. On the dSprites structured-outcome benchmark, DR-KPT-rbf maintains calibration under the null (0.024) whereas all KPT baselines exhibit severely inflated false-positive rates (~37–40%), and DR-KPT-rbf achieves 0.656 power in Scenario IV versus 0.054–0.081 for the best KPT baseline. The doubly robust estimator also improves the CPME convergence rate from O(n^{-1/4}) (plug-in) to O(n^{-1/2}) (DR), enabling the asymptotically normal test statistic.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares DR-KPT against KPT, an adaptation of the Kernel Treatment Effect estimator from Muandet et al. [17] to the OPE setting with estimated propensities, in three kernel variants (linear, RBF, polynomial). DR-KPT-rbf dominates across nearly all scenarios: on Warfarin it matches or exceeds KPT-linear in power (0.80 vs. 0.77 in Scenario II; 0.99 vs. 1.00 in Scenario III) while KPT-rbf and KPT-poly are essentially powerless (≤0.01) in Scenarios II and IV. The most dramatic gap appears on dSprites with structured 64×64 image outcomes, where KPT baselines suffer severe miscalibration under the null (37–40% false-positive rate vs. the nominal 5%) and near-zero power in the alternative (0.054–0.081), whereas DR-KPT-rbf achieves 0.024 false-positive rate and 0.656 power. KPT-linear is the only baseline that occasionally matches DR-KPT (Scenario III on Warfarin: 1.00 vs. 0.99), but it degrades sharply in Scenario IV (0.24 vs. 0.76) and fails catastrophically on dSprites. Overall, DR-KPT is the only method that is simultaneously well-calibrated under the null and highly powered under alternatives across all three datasets and all four scenario types.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Counterfactual Mean Embedding (CME) framework of Muandet et al. [17]—originally developed for binary treatments with known propensities—to the general stochastic-intervention setting of off-policy evaluation, where both the target policy and logging policy are arbitrary. It imports the efficient influence function (EIF) machinery for Hilbert-space-valued parameters from Luedtke and Chung [41] and the doubly robust kernel test structure from Martinez Taboada et al. [28], combining them with the cross U-statistic framework of Kim and Ramdas [66] to obtain an asymptotically normal, computationally efficient test. The key conceptual departure from prior CME work is the shift from hard interventions to stochastic policies and the introduction of a doubly robust correction that simultaneously debiases both the conditional mean embedding and the propensity score.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By embedding the full counterfactual outcome distribution in an RKHS rather than reducing it to a scalar mean, the framework enables distributional risk measures (CVaR, quantiles) and hypothesis tests that are invisible to classical mean-based OPE. The doubly robust property and the improved n^{-1/2} convergence rate make the approach more statistically efficient than the plug-in alternative, while the asymptotic normality of the DR-KPT statistic eliminates the need for expensive permutation tests. Open questions include relaxing the strong positivity and exchangeability assumptions toward partially identifiable settings, scaling to high-dimensional or continuous action spaces, and exploring MMD gradient flow methods for more scalable sampling.
