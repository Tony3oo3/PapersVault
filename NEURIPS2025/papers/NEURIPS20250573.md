## Problem

Supervised diffusion bridge methods for inverse problems (I2SB, IR-SDE, GOUB, DDBM) learn general mappings between paired clean and corrupted data without leveraging the known linear measurement system, even though in practical settings like CT or MRI the system matrix and noise covariance are available a priori. This paper asks whether explicitly embedding the measurement system into the SDE coefficients of a supervised bridge can yield consistent performance gains and improved robustness under system misspecification between training and deployment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250573/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a noisy measurement y ∈ R^m, the system matrix A ∈ R^{m×d} (with its Moore-Penrose pseudoinverse A⁺), and the noise covariance Σ ∈ R^{m×m}, and produces a reconstructed clean signal x ∈ R^d. A key design choice is that the pseudoinverse reconstruction A⁺y always lies in R^d regardless of whether m ≠ d, which resolves a dimensional mismatch that plagues prior bridge methods in tasks like 4× superresolution (d = 4m). All experiments use 256×256 images, and the method is evaluated on both natural RGB images (CelebA-HQ, DIV2K) and 2D axial medical scans (RSNA CT, Br35H MRI).

## Method

SDB constructs a Markovian linear SDE whose drift and diffusion coefficients are matrix-valued and explicitly encode the measurement system via the range-nullspace decomposition: H_t = A⁺A + α_t(I − A⁺A) governs the mean, while Σ_t = γ_t A⁺ΣA⁺ᵀ + β_t(I − A⁺A) governs the covariance, separating range-space denoising from null-space synthesis. Three variants are proposed: SDB(SB) connects the null-space dynamics to an optimal-transport ODE (extending I2SB), SDB(VP) performs convex interpolation, and SDB(VE) converges to an isotropic Gaussian. Training uses standard score matching with an L1 reconstruction loss against the clean signal, and sampling proceeds via Euler-Maruyama from the pseudoinverse reconstruction A⁺y. The paper proves that under mild conditions (lim γ_t = 1, lim α_t²/β_t = 0), the reverse SDE yields asymptotically exact Bayesian posterior samples from p(x|y), and that the null-space component reduces to an OT-ODE in the zero-noise limit.

## Results

SDB (SB) achieves the best or near-best results across all four inverse problems. On CT reconstruction (RSNA), it reaches PSNR 46.67 and FID 15.02, substantially outperforming the best supervised baseline DDBM (PSNR 44.42, FID 23.36). On MRI reconstruction (Br35H), SDB (SB) attains PSNR 29.81 and FID 29.85 versus DDBM's 28.97 and 32.42. On inpainting (CelebA-HQ), SDB (SB) scores FID 4.63 and PSNR 30.40, edging out IR-SDE (FID 4.68, PSNR 29.92). The only metric where SDB (SB) does not lead is LPIPS on DIV2K super-resolution (0.226 vs. I2SB's 0.176), though it still wins on FID (81.56 vs. 83.73) and PSNR (26.10 vs. 25.21).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SDB (SB) outperforms all seven baselines on the majority of metrics across all four tasks. Against the closest supervised competitor IR-SDE, SDB (SB) wins on FID for inpainting (4.63 vs. 4.68), super-resolution (81.56 vs. 96.22), CT (15.02 vs. 18.88), and MRI (29.85 vs. 30.14), as well as on PSNR for CT (46.67 vs. 43.44) and MRI (29.81 vs. 28.88). Against DDBM, the advantage is most pronounced on CT reconstruction, where SDB (SB) improves FID by 8.34 points (15.02 vs. 23.36) and PSNR by 2.25 dB (46.67 vs. 44.42). The unsupervised baselines (DPS, IIGDM, DDNM) are consistently outperformed by 5–10+ FID points and 5–15 dB in PSNR, reflecting the benefit of paired training data. The sole exception is LPIPS on DIV2K super-resolution, where I2SB (0.176) and GOUB (0.178) outperform SDB (SB) (0.226), though SDB still leads on FID and PSNR for that task. Under system misspecification (varying MRI sampling ratio λ₁ or noise σ²), SDB (SB) maintains its lead over all bridge baselines, with the performance gap widening as the system deviates further from training conditions.

## Relation to Prior Work

SDB extends the supervised diffusion bridge line (I2SB, IR-SDE, GOUB, DDBM) by replacing their scalar SDEs with a matrix-valued formulation that explicitly embeds the known linear measurement system (A, Σ) into the drift and diffusion coefficients via the range-nullspace decomposition. Conceptually, it bridges two previously separate threads: unsupervised plug-and-play methods (DPS, DDNM, ΠGDM) that leverage system structure at sampling time, and supervised bridge methods that learn a direct mapping but ignore that structure. The connection to Schrödinger Bridge / optimal transport (Theorem 2) and the principled posterior sampling guarantee (Theorem 3) ground the design in established stochastic process theory, while the Markovian forward process distinguishes SDB from the non-Markovian bridges of I2SB, GOUB, and DDBM.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

In practical inverse problems (CT, MRI), the measurement system is known a priori, yet prior bridge methods discard this information; SDB shows that embedding it into the SDE yields consistent gains across four diverse tasks and, notably, robustness to parameter shifts between training and deployment—a critical property for real-world use. The main limitations are the restriction to linear Gaussian systems, 2D evaluation, and simple variance schedules, leaving nonlinear systems, 3D reconstruction, and optimal scheduling as open directions. The framework also opens a broader question about how much structural information should be baked into the generative process versus learned by the score network.
