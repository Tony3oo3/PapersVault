## Problem

Existing medical MLLMs rely on direct-prediction paradigms that produce brief answers without the interleaved image-text reasoning essential for real clinical scenarios, and RL-based approaches (e.g., GRPO) fail to generate novel reasoning paradigms beyond the base model's ceiling. A comprehensive framework for automatically searching and evaluating effective step-by-step medical reasoning paths remains absent, making high-quality medical CoT data construction prohibitively expensive and lacking standardized quality metrics.

## Input / Output

The model consumes medical images spanning 12 imaging modalities (minimum 196×196 resolution), a text question framed in one of three clinical scenarios (patient-to-doctor, doctor-to-doctor, or intern-to-senior), and associated patient information. It produces a structured chain-of-thought reasoning path of 4–7 intermediate steps (covering history analysis, image interpretation, differential diagnosis, etc.) followed by a final medical answer. The MMRP dataset's Part 3 uses open-ended questions evaluated via BERT-Score for answer similarity and MICS-Score for reasoning-path effectiveness, rather than simple multiple-choice accuracy.

## Method

MICS (Mentor-Intern Collaborative Search) iteratively searches for effective reasoning paths by having three powerful mentor models (GPT-4o, Gemini 2.5 Pro, Qwen2.5-VL-72B) each extend the current reasoning prefix with a new step, then evaluating each candidate by prompting six intern model configurations (three open-source 7–8B models at two temperatures) to complete the full reasoning and produce an answer. The MICS-Score—the fraction of interns reaching the correct answer given the prefix—determines which path is retained, with tie-breaking via mentor competitiveness scores and early-stopping when all interns succeed. The resulting high-quality CoT data, combined with text QA and image-text alignment subsets, forms the MMRP dataset, on which InternVL3-8B is trained via a three-stage curriculum (text QA → image-text alignment → CoT reasoning) using LoRA fine-tuning, yielding Chiron-o1.

## Results

Chiron-o1-8B achieves an average of 69.2% across five medical VQA benchmarks (VQA-RAD, SLAKE, PathVQA, PMC-VQA, MMMU Health & Medicine), surpassing the strongest closed-source model Gemini-2.5-Pro (68.8%) and the best medical MLLM HuatuoGPT-Vision-34B (64.2%) by 0.4% and 5.0% respectively. On the MMRP reasoning benchmark, Chiron-o1-8B reaches 58.4% accuracy and 49.4% MICS-Score, outperforming the best prior reasoning model MedVLM-R1 (31.2% ACC, 23.5% MICS-Score) by 27.2% in accuracy and 25.9% in reasoning-path effectiveness. The 2B variant also outperforms its base model InternVL3-2B by 8.1% on average VQA accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Chiron-o1-8B outperforms all 15 baselines on the average VQA score. Against closed-source models, it edges out Gemini-2.5-Pro (69.2 vs. 68.8 AVG) and substantially surpasses GPT-4o (52.1) and GPT-4o-mini (48.6). Among open-source general MLLMs, it leads LLaVA-v1.6-34B (55.6) by 13.6% and Qwen-VL-Chat (45.5) by 23.7%. In the medical MLLM category, Chiron-o1-8B beats HuatuoGPT-Vision-34B (64.2) by 5.0% and GMAI-VL (61.2) by 8.0%, while the 2B variant already surpasses HuatuoGPT-Vision-7B (60.0) by 5.5%. For medical reasoning models, the gap is largest: Chiron-o1-8B exceeds MedVLM-R1 (52.6) by 16.6% and Med-R1 (48.6) by 20.6% on VQA, and on the MMRP reasoning benchmark it achieves 58.4% ACC vs. MedVLM-R1's 31.2% (+27.2%) and 49.4% MICS-Score vs. 23.5% (+25.9%). On the out-of-domain MedXpertQA_MM benchmark, Chiron-o1-8B leads MedVLM-R1 by 3.35% average across reasoning and understanding subsets. The only baseline it does not clearly dominate is Gemini-2.5-Pro on PMC-VQA (57.5 vs. 61.1), where the larger closed-source model retains a 3.6% advantage.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant RL-based paradigm (GRPO) used by recent medical reasoning models like Med-R1 and MedVLM-R1, arguing that fixed reward functions fail to generate novel reasoning paradigms and produce superficial or hallucinated reasoning paths. Instead, it draws on the process reward model literature (Math-Shepherd, VisualPRM) and the mentor-intern pedagogical analogy to propose MICS, a multi-model collaborative search where powerful mentor models (GPT-4o, Gemini 2.5 Pro, Qwen2.5-VL-72B) generate reasoning steps and smaller intern models validate them via a novel MICS-Score. The curriculum learning training strategy builds on prior work in staged fine-tuning, while the MMRP dataset repurposes Radiopaedia clinical cases into a three-part structure (text QA, image-text alignment, multimodal CoT) that no prior medical dataset has combined in this form.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine bottleneck in medical AI: the absence of scalable, quality-controlled methods for constructing multimodal CoT training data in a domain where expert annotation is prohibitively expensive. The MICS-Score provides a practical, automated evaluation metric for reasoning path quality that could be adapted beyond medicine. However, the approach's reliance on multiple expensive closed-source API calls for mentor models limits its accessibility, and the MMRP dataset (8,328 CoT samples) remains modest in scale. Open questions include whether the mentor-intern validation paradigm generalizes to other specialized domains, whether the 4-step maximum search depth is sufficient for more complex multi-system diagnoses, and whether the curriculum structure can be further automated.
