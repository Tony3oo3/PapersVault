## Problem

Existing HOI-DET methods model relationships via complete or bipartite graphs over all detected entities, treating interactions as isolated pairwise relations. This overlooks the collective patterns in real-world scenes where multiple humans and objects naturally form groups engaged in joint activities, introducing noise from spurious correlations between unrelated entities.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251790/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image and produces a set of human-object interaction triplets (human, verb, object) with corresponding bounding boxes and class labels, using 64 learnable queries per branch in a DETR-style set prediction. For the NVI-DET extension, it additionally predicts individual-group-interaction triplets that capture higher-order nonverbal behaviors involving arbitrary numbers of humans. The model operates without data augmentation at test time, retaining top-100 HOI candidates followed by NMS.

## Method

GroupHOI introduces two group structures guided by Gestalt principles to replace global pairwise attention. First, a geometric group module groups each entity with its K^g=4 nearest neighbors using a learnable proximity estimator over centroid distance and IoU, then applies a local transformer with parameterized position encodings to aggregate and dispatch contextual cues within each group via soft correspondence. Second, a semantic group module clusters interaction queries by cosine similarity (K^s=2) and aggregates local context via max pooling, which is injected into the interaction decoder through residual connections before each self-attention layer. The overall architecture is a DETR-based pipeline (ResNet-50 backbone, 6-layer encoder, 3-layer instance decoder, 3-layer interaction decoder) with VLM knowledge transfer from CLIP or BLIP2, where group construction is performed independently at each decoder layer to allow entities to exchange information with different neighbors across layers.

## Results

On HICO-DET (Default setting), GroupHOI with CLIP/B16 achieves 36.70/34.86/37.26 mAP on Full/Rare/Non-Rare, surpassing the previous state-of-the-art Pose-Aware by +0.84/+2.38/+0.40 mAP. With stronger VLMs, GroupHOI reaches 39.46 mAP (CLIP/L14) and 40.53 mAP (BLIP2) on Full. On V-COCO, GroupHOI achieves 65.0/66.0 mAP (APS1/APS2 role) with CLIP/B16 and 66.4/67.3 with CLIP/L14. On the more challenging NVI-DET task, GroupHOI attains 73.19 AR (val) and 75.21 AR (test), outperforming the previous best NVI-DEHR by +1.99/+0.54 AR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GroupHOI (CLIP/B16) outperforms all CLIP/B16 competitors on HICO-DET by substantial margins: +1.34 mAP over CQL (35.36), +1.23 over LOGICHOI (35.47), and +2.11 over HOICLIP (34.59) on Full. Against the previous state-of-the-art Pose-Aware (R50, no VLM), GroupHOI gains +0.84 mAP on Full and +2.38 on Rare. With CLIP/L14, GroupHOI surpasses CMMP by +1.32 mAP on Full (39.46 vs 38.14), though it trails CMMP slightly on Rare (37.10 vs 37.75). With BLIP2, GroupHOI leads UniHOI by +0.47/+0.71/+0.39 mAP on Full/Rare/Non-Rare. On V-COCO, GroupHOI (CLIP/B16) is slightly behind STIP (65.1), VIL (65.3), and CQL (66.4) on APS1role, but the authors attribute this to V-COCO's simpler scenes (fewer entities and interactions per image) where global attention suffices. On NVI-DET, GroupHOI achieves 73.19/75.21 AR on val/test, beating NVI-DEHR (71.20/74.67) and all modified HOI-DET baselines (m-QPIC, m-CDN, m-GEN-VLKT) by 1.5–6 AR.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the DETR-based one-stage HOI detection pipeline (DETR, HOICLIP, GEN-VLKT) but repositions the core relation-modeling problem from a group perspective. While prior methods either propagate information across all entities via global self-attention (complete graph) or restrict message passing to human-object bipartite neighborhoods, GroupHOI introduces two structured grouping mechanisms—geometric proximity (spatial clustering of bounding boxes) and semantic similarity (feature-space clustering of interaction queries)—inspired by Gestalt grouping principles. The conceptual departure is treating HOI detection as a collective-behavior problem where local group context matters, rather than a purely pairwise relation task.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The group-level framing addresses a genuine gap: real-world interactions often involve multiple entities in joint activities, yet prior methods reason only over predefined pairs. The strong NVI-DET results (73.19/75.21 AR) validate that the grouping mechanism generalizes to higher-order, multi-person interactions beyond standard HOI triplets. Limitations include the grouping being applied only in the interaction branch (not the detection branch), reliance on limited label diversity, and the fact that the approach remains a DETR variant with local attention rather than a fundamentally new architecture. Open questions include whether these principles scale to gigapixel crowd scenes and whether the geometric/semantic grouping can be integrated into the object detection stage.
