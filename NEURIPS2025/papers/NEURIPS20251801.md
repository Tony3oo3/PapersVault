## Problem

DPO's Bradley-Terry preference model assigns no probability to tied outcomes, forcing practitioners to discard tied pairs from preference datasets. This wastes expensively collected data and forgoes the regularization that tied pairs could provide. The paper asks whether DPO can be extended to explicitly model ties without the task-performance degradation observed when tied pairs are naively added to standard DPO training.

## Input / Output

The method consumes preference pairs each labeled with a binary tie flag (t=0 for clear preference, t=1 for tie) alongside a reference policy π_ref. Tied pairs are constructed as response pairs that are close in quality ranking (e.g., minimum BLEURT score difference or minimum implicit reward margin), while clear preference pairs are the top- and bottom-ranked responses for each prompt. The output is a trained policy π_θ that simultaneously increases reward margins on wins and drives them toward zero on ties, producing stronger KL regularization relative to the reference policy without sacrificing task performance.

## Method

The paper replaces the Bradley-Terry model in DPO with two tie-accommodating extensions: the Rao-Kupper model (which shifts the BT preference distribution by a sensitivity threshold α_RK) and the Davidson model (which allocates tie probability proportional to the geometric mean of the two win probabilities). This transforms the DPO objective from binary to ternary classification, where the loss for wins encourages increasing the reward margin d_θ while the loss for ties encourages minimizing |d_θ|, creating two competing optimization pressures. The resulting gradient updates introduce odd-function scale factors on tied pairs that actively push reward margins toward zero — a mechanism entirely absent in standard DPO. The paper provides a theoretical explanation via ideal DPO policy theory: tied pairs with true preference probability 0.5 should maintain the reference model's chosen/rejected likelihood ratio, acting as an implicit regularization constraint. Empirically, DPO-RK and DPO-D achieve comparable task performance to DPO(CP) at lower KL divergence, and outperform DPO on translation and mathematical reasoning by leveraging previously discarded tied data.

## Results

On GSM8K, DPO-RK(CP+TP) achieves 84.4% exact-match accuracy (β=0.3) and DPO-D(CP+TP) achieves 84.5% (β=0.5/0.7), outperforming DPO(CP) at 83.7% and the base Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct model at 70.9%. On the ALMA-R-Preference ZH-EN translation set, DPO-RK(CP+TP) improves COMET from 79.66 (DPO(CP)) to 80.63 and XCOMET from 88.87 to 90.40. Across WMT21 ZH-EN, IWSLT17 FR-EN, and TL;DR, DPO-RK(CP+TP) and DPO-D(CP+TP) match DPO(CP) task performance while achieving substantially lower KL divergence to the reference policy, demonstrating stronger regularization without the performance degradation seen when ties are naively added to standard DPO.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DPO (Rafailov et al., 2023), both DPO-RK(CP+TP) and DPO-D(CP+TP) consistently outperform on GSM8K at every beta value (e.g., 84.4% vs 83.7% at β=0.3) and on ALMA-R-Preference translation (COMET 80.63 vs 79.66, XCOMET 90.40 vs 88.87), while also achieving lower KL divergence to the reference model. Against SimPO (Meng et al., 2024), DPO-RK(CP+TP) wins on GSM8K by 1.6–2.0 percentage points across all beta values (e.g., 84.4% vs 81.8% at β=0.3). Against CPO (Xu et al., 2024), DPO-RK(CP+TP) achieves 84.4% vs 83.1% on GSM8K at β=0.3, and on the ALMA-R-Preference set DPO-D(CP+TP) reaches XCOMET 90.09 and KIWI-XXL 75.54, surpassing the ALMA-7B-LoRA baseline (83.94, 73.65) and approaching the stronger ALMA-7B-R system (90.7, 75.7). A key finding is that adding tied pairs to vanilla DPO degrades task performance on all three NMT/summarization benchmarks, whereas DPO-RK and DPO-D absorb the same tied data without degradation, confirming that the tie-compatible modeling is essential rather than merely a data-utilization trick.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends DPO (Rafailov et al., 2023) by replacing its underlying Bradley-Terry preference model with two classical tie-compatible generalizations: the Rao-Kupper model (1967) and the Davidson model (1970). The resulting DPO-RK and DPO-D objectives are derived directly from the DPO framework, requiring only a binary tie flag in the training data. The paper positions DPO-RK as a strict generalization of ODPO (Amini et al., 2024b) with a fixed offset, noting that ODPO's offset parameter plays an analogous role to Rao-Kupper's sensitivity threshold but does not extend to explicit tie labels. The regularization effect of ties is explained through the ideal DPO policy theory of Chen et al. (2024), which shows that tied pairs constrain the policy to maintain the reference model's likelihood ratio, acting as an implicit KL regularizer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical message is clear: the common practice of discarding tied preference pairs (as done in Llama 3 and Qwen2 post-training) wastes potentially useful data, and the paper demonstrates that incorporating ties with appropriate modeling yields equal or better task performance at lower KL divergence across translation, summarization, and mathematical reasoning. A key open question is how to set the tie-probability hyperparameters (ν_RK, ν_D) in a data-driven way rather than by the paper's assumption of 50% tie probability for equally-matched items; the authors acknowledge that tuning ν to fit the underlying preference generative process could yield further gains. The absence of large-scale human-annotated tie datasets limits validation, and the tie selection strategies (BLEURT-based ranking, DPO implicit reward) are heuristic rather than grounded in true annotator uncertainty.
