## Problem

Existing LLM-PDDL planning methods rely on handcrafted or narrowly scoped PDDL domains, limiting generalization across diverse real-world tasks. While LLMs and VLMs offer strong commonsense priors, they struggle to construct realistic domain files and produce coherent long-horizon plans with correct action preconditions and effects. No prior work pre-trains a unified, general-purpose PDDL domain from large-scale real-world robot demonstrations to enable compositional generalization in symbolic task planning.

## Input / Output

During pretraining, the method consumes 12,393 robot manipulation demonstration videos (from DROID) with paired language instructions, extracting keyframes via grayscale energy extrema and generating atomic PDDL domains. At inference time, it takes a natural language task instruction and a single RGB scene image as input, and produces a grounded PDDL plan—a sequence of typed, parameterized operators (e.g., pick_from_rack, open_drawer, wipe_table)—that can be directly translated into natural language commands for any low-level VLA policy. The intermediate artifact is a fused meta-domain (61 operators, 106 predicates, 332 causal edges) constructed from 40 retrieved atomic domains.

## Method

UniDomain is a three-phase framework analogous to pretraining, post-training, and inference in foundation models. In domain pretraining, an energy-based keyframe extractor identifies semantic transitions in demonstration videos, a VLM proposes an initial atomic PDDL domain per transition, and a nested closed-loop verification (solvability check via synthetic test problems solved by Fast Downward, followed by LLM-based commonsense plan verification) iteratively refines the domain until both checks pass or a maximum of 5 iterations is reached. In domain fusion, task-relevant atomic domains are retrieved via sentence-embedding similarity and hierarchically merged along a binary tree, where predicate and operator pairs are aligned using cosine similarity thresholds (τ=0.3) and LLM-verified semantic equivalence, yielding a compact meta-domain. In online planning, a VLM constructs an initial grounded PDDL problem, task-relevant predicates and operators are filtered from the meta-domain to reduce symbolic noise, and a refined problem is solved by Fast Downward to produce an optimal action sequence.

## Results

On 100 unseen real-world tasks across four domains (BlockWorld, Desktop, Kitchen, Combination), UniDomain achieves 85% success rate and 83% optimality rate at K=0, representing up to 58% higher task success and 160% better plan optimality over the strongest baselines. UniDomain also incurs the lowest LLM thinking time and fewest LLM calls among top-performing methods. In 83% of tasks, the planner produced not only feasible but also optimal plans through composition of learned operators.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UniDomain outperforms all four primary baselines on every reported metric. Against Code-as-Policies [21] (51% SR), UniDomain achieves 85% SR — a 34-point absolute gain — and maintains strong optimality where Code-as-Policies degrades rapidly under stricter thresholds. Against ReAct [22], which achieves the highest success among LLM-only planners, UniDomain's symbolic structure eliminates the redundant and invalid steps that ReAct produces on long-horizon tasks. Against ISR-LLM [23], the strongest baseline overall in task success, UniDomain matches or exceeds SR while dramatically improving plan optimality (ISR-LLM's OR at K=0 drops to match VLM-CoT, whereas UniDomain sustains 83%). Against BoN-iVML [14], UniDomain's pre-trained and fused meta-domain avoids the on-the-fly domain construction failures that cause BoN-iVML to produce unreliable or unsolvable PDDL specifications. UniDomain also leads in efficiency, requiring the fewest LLM calls and shortest thinking time among all top-performing methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LLM+PDDL planning paradigm (LLM+P, AutoGPT+P, ISR-LLM, BoN-iVML) by replacing handcrafted or on-the-fly-generated domains with a pre-trained unified domain learned from 12,393 real-world robot manipulation demonstrations (DROID). It departs from prior demonstration-based domain learning (BLADE, pix2pred, Diehl et al.) that produces narrow, task-specific domains by instead aggregating atomic domains into a large-scale symbolic knowledge graph and introducing hierarchical fusion to construct compact meta-domains for compositional generalization. The closed-loop verification loop (solvability check + solution verification via PDDL solver feedback) is a methodological departure from prior LLM-based domain construction that relies on human-in-the-loop or environment feedback.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that pre-training a symbolic domain from large-scale demonstrations enables zero-shot compositional generalization in robot task planning, with the ablation showing that without domain fusion, success drops from 85% to 19%—evidence that the unified domain's connectivity is what enables cross-task composition. Key limitations include the PDDL 1.0 constraint (no temporal constraints, numeric fluents, or cost-sensitive planning), the full-observability assumption, and the reliance on a perfect low-level controller during evaluation. Open questions include whether the approach scales to richer planning formalisms (PDDL 2.1, PPDDL), how to handle perceptual uncertainty and occlusion, and whether the retrieval-fusion pipeline can be made efficient enough for real-time deployment across diverse task classes.
