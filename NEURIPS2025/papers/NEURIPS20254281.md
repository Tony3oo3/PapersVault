## Problem

Most image fusion methods process video frames independently, ignoring temporal correlations and producing flickering, temporally inconsistent outputs. Beyond this methodological gap, no comprehensive benchmark exists for video fusion across multiple tasks, and standard evaluation metrics assess only per-frame spatial quality while ignoring frame-to-frame consistency.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254281/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pair of temporally aligned source video sequences (e.g., over/under-exposed, different-focus, infrared/visible, or MRI/CT-PET-SPECT), processing them as 3-frame snippets (t−1, t, t+1) per source at each time step. It produces a single fused video of the same length and resolution, with the primary MEF and MFF branches evaluated at 2K resolution and supplementary low-resolution (540p/480p) results also reported. The benchmark spans four tasks with 500, 150, 90, and 57 curated scenes respectively, each split into training and test sets.

## Method

UniVF employs a Restormer-based encoder-decoder architecture augmented with optical-flow-guided feature warping: per-source Restormer encoders extract features from three consecutive frames, SEA-RAFT estimates bidirectional flow between adjacent frames, and warped features from neighboring frames are concatenated with current-frame features (6 maps total) before a Restormer decoder reconstructs the fused frame. Training uses a compound loss combining spatial similarity (task-specific L1 or SSIM-based), Sobel gradient preservation, and a novel temporal consistency loss that penalizes discrepancies between the current fused frame and its flow-warped neighbors, restricted to valid regions identified by forward-backward flow consistency masks. The framework is unified across all four fusion tasks, differing only in the spatial loss formulation and loss weight hyperparameters, and is trained with Adam at lr 1e−4 over 20k iterations on a single RTX 4090.

## Results

On VF-Bench, UniVF achieves state-of-the-art performance across all four video fusion tasks. For multi-exposure fusion (MEF), UniVF scores VIF 0.82, SSIM 0.99, MI 4.45, Q^AB/F 0.72, BiSWE 6.40, and MS2R 0.33, outperforming the best baseline FILM (VIF 0.78, BiSWE 8.27). For multi-focus fusion (MFF), UniVF reaches VIF 0.79, MI 6.32, Q^AB/F 0.79, BiSWE 5.95, and MS2R 1.08. On infrared-visible fusion (IVF), UniVF attains MI 2.47, Q^AB/F 0.68, BiSWE 3.94, and MS2R 0.35. For medical video fusion (MVF), UniVF achieves VIF 0.35, MI 2.00, Q^AB/F 0.68, and MS2R 1.30, leading all baselines on most spatial and temporal metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UniVF achieves the best or near-best score on every metric across all four tasks. On MEF, UniVF clearly leads the strongest competitor FILM by +0.04 VIF, +0.06 MI, and a substantial 1.87-point reduction in BiSWE (6.40 vs. 8.27), indicating markedly better temporal consistency. On MFF, UniVF edges out RFL (the closest competitor) by +0.01 VIF, +0.03 MI, and 0.03 lower MS2R, while matching SSIM at 0.90. On IVF, UniVF wins on MI (2.47 vs. 2.44 for DCEvo), Q^AB/F (0.68 vs. 0.67 for TDFusion), BiSWE (3.94 vs. 4.35 for TDFusion), and MS2R (0.35 vs. 0.36), though TDFusion narrowly leads on VIF (0.45 vs. 0.44). On MVF, UniVF dominates on VIF (0.35 vs. 0.30 for C2RF), MI (2.00 vs. 1.80 for CDDFuse), and Q^AB/F (0.68 vs. 0.59 for CDDFuse), but CDDFuse achieves a lower BiSWE (26.33 vs. 29.61), suggesting slightly better frame-to-frame alignment in the medical domain. Overall, UniVF's optical-flow-based feature warping and temporal consistency loss yield the most consistent gains in temporal metrics (BiSWE, MS2R) relative to frame-by-frame image fusion baselines like CUNet, IFCNN, and CDDFuse.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the well-established image fusion literature (CDDFuse, EMMA, FILM, ReFusion, etc.) into the temporal domain by repurposing optical flow-based feature warping from video restoration (BasicVSR++, VRT) into the fusion setting. While prior video fusion work exists for specific modalities (infrared-RGB video [58,59]) and HDR video reconstruction [60–63], no unified framework or benchmark has addressed multiple video fusion tasks with temporal coherence as a first-class objective. The temporal consistency loss with forward-backward validity masks is a novel addition that goes beyond the spatial similarity losses used in all prior image fusion methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VF-Bench is the first comprehensive benchmark covering four video fusion tasks with a unified evaluation protocol that jointly assesses spatial quality and temporal consistency (via BiSWE and MS2R), filling a clear gap that has limited data-driven video fusion research. The practical impact is significant: the benchmark, data generation pipelines (HDR-to-multi-exposure, depth-based multi-focus), and temporal metrics will likely become standard references for the field. A key limitation is the assumption of well-aligned inputs; the authors acknowledge that even rare misalignment (1 in 300 frames) produces visible ghosting artifacts, and incorporating alignment modules is identified as future work. Open questions include whether the approach scales to longer videos, handles arbitrary camera motion, and generalizes to fusion tasks beyond the four covered.
