## Problem

Standard MLP-based implicit neural representations for morphing warping lack structural priors, requiring costly explicit regularizations to enforce temporal coherence and invertibility. These soft constraints lead to unstable training, slow convergence, and catastrophic artifacts such as singularities where the mapping becomes non-invertible. The paper asks whether flow properties (uniqueness, reversibility, temporal coherence) can be inherited directly from the network architecture rather than imposed through the loss.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255118/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a source and target image (or 3D Gaussian Splatting) together with sparse landmark correspondences (e.g., 68 dlib facial points, XFeat scene features, or 254 FLAME 3D landmarks) as the sole supervision signal. It produces a continuous time-dependent warping flow Φ(x,t) defined over the full spatial domain, which at inference warps both inputs to an intermediate time t and blends them (linearly or via a generative model) into the final morphed output. For 3DGS, the output is a union of two warped Gaussian sets with linearly blended opacities, enabling photorealistic 3D transitions at any viewpoint.

## Method

FLOWING recasts morphing warping as the construction of a differential vector flow, so that orbit uniqueness and invertibility are guaranteed by the flow definition rather than enforced through regularization. Two flow-based architectures are adapted to the morphing setting: Neural ODEs (a SIREN-activated vector field integrated with 4th-order Runge-Kutta) and Neural Conjugate Flows (invertible coupling layers that topologically deform simple affine flows). Training minimizes a data constraint enforcing feature matching at a few timesteps plus a thin-plate energy term penalizing the Jacobian of the time derivative, computed efficiently via a novel forward-mode (dual-number) differentiation scheme that is 23× faster than standard autograd. SIREN activations enable high-frequency deformation capture, and the flow-based formulation eliminates the need for explicit temporal-coherence or invertibility penalties, yielding near-instant convergence (NODE: ~16 s for 2k steps) compared to ifmorph's ~5 min for 20k steps.

## Results

On landmark alignment (MSE), FLOWING with SIREN activations outperforms ifmorph by 1–3 orders of magnitude across all three benchmarks: NCF(SIREN) achieves 3.9E-5 on FRLL vs. ifmorph's 1.5E-3, and NODE(SIREN) reaches 1.4E-4 on FRLL, 4.9E-4 on MegaDepth, and 5.9E-5 on NeRSemble. For blending quality on FRLL, NODE achieves the best LPIPS (0.210/0.213) and FID (31.952), surpassing ifmorph (0.250/0.252, 38.427) and OpenCV (0.233/0.236, 32.426). Training is dramatically faster: NODE converges in ~16 seconds (2k steps) versus ifmorph's 5m26s (20k steps) on an RTX 4090.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ifmorph [44], FLOWING's flow-based architectures (NCF and NODE with SIREN) reduce landmark alignment MSE by 1–3 orders of magnitude on all three datasets (e.g., 3.9E-5 vs. 1.5E-3 on FRLL for NCF), and also achieve better blending quality (NODE: LPIPS 0.210, FID 31.95 vs. ifmorph: LPIPS 0.250, FID 38.43). Against the classical thin-plate spline (TPS) warping [7], FLOWING substantially outperforms on blending LPIPS (0.210/0.213 vs. 0.371/0.374 on FRLL), while TPS also suffers from numerical instability (crashes or poor warping in ~10% of samples). Against DiffMorpher [59] used alone, FLOWING+DiffMorpher produces more structurally coherent morphs with fewer missing elements (e.g., preserved chimneys and architectural details in view interpolation), though no direct quantitative metric is reported for this comparison. NODE is the strongest variant overall, achieving the best FID and LPIPS, while NCF offers faster morphing inference (0.31s vs. 1.48s at 1350² resolution) at the cost of slightly higher training time.

## Relation to Prior Work

FLOWING repositions morphing from a regularization-heavy INR problem (as in ifmorph, which uses generic SIREN MLPs with explicit loss terms to approximate flow properties) to a flow-by-construction problem, adapting Neural ODEs and Neural Conjugate Flows as the warping architecture so that uniqueness, invertibility, and temporal coherence are inherited from the flow operator's mathematical structure. This departs from both classical geometric morphing (thin-plate splines, RBFs, mesh-based warping) and from the recent INR-based morphing line, which treats the warping as an unconstrained function to be regularized. The paper also extends the morphing task to 3D Gaussian Splatting, a modality not addressed by prior morphing methods, and integrates the flow-based warping as a spatial-alignment front-end for generative blending (DiffMorpher), a combination not previously explored.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The flow-by-construction formulation eliminates the costly and unstable regularizations that plague MLP-based morphing, yielding near-instant training (NODE: ~16 s for 2k steps vs. ifmorph: >5 min for 20k steps) and 1–3 orders-of-magnitude lower landmark alignment error, making high-quality morphing practical in interactive or large-scale settings. The 3DGS morphing extension opens a path toward photorealistic 3D face interpolation that preserves volumetric structure, and the forward-differentiation trick (23× Hessian speedup) is a reusable engineering contribution. Key limitations remain: the invertibility-by-construction constraint precludes topological changes (e.g., open-to-closed mouth), and quality still depends on landmark correspondence accuracy. Open questions include whether the approach extends to point-cloud registration, whether the flow formulation can be relaxed to handle occlusions, and how it scales to video-length morphing sequences.
