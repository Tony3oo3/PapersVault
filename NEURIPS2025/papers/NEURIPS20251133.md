## Problem

Combining gradient compression with adaptive optimizers in distributed deep learning has remained theoretically unresolved due to three intertwined challenges: the ratio structure of first/second moments under compression, the linear dependence on ambient dimension d in existing convergence bounds (making T scale as O(d)), and the lack of high-probability guarantees that account for all noise sources simultaneously. This paper provides the first sharp convergence analysis that addresses all three, showing that only O(log d) sketch dimension suffices for O(1/√T) convergence.

## Input / Output

In each communication round, C clients each perform K local SGD steps on their local data (CIFAR-10 images or SST2 text) and transmit a b-dimensional sketched vector of their parameter updates to the server, where b ≪ d (e.g., b = 4×10⁴ for a 42M-parameter model, a 0.1% compression rate). The server averages the sketched updates, desketches them back to R^d, and broadcasts the result along with updated adaptive optimizer moments. The overall per-round communication drops from O(d) to O(b), and the total training communication is O(bT) with b = O(log d).

## Method

SADL (Algorithm 1) is a generic framework where clients and the server both call an adaptive optimizer subroutine (Algorithm 2, instantiated as AMSGrad) that consumes sketched updates via a desketching operator. The sketching operator sk: R^d → R^b (e.g., SRHT, Count-Sketch, or Gaussian) is shared across clients within a round but refreshed across rounds for statistical independence. The key analytical contribution is a descent lemma for AMSGrad that decomposes the loss change into a first-order descent term and a second-order quadratic term; the second-order term is bounded dimension-freely by projecting updates onto Hessian eigenvectors and exploiting the sharp-decaying eigenspectrum (intrinsic dimension ≪ d), while the first-order term is handled via element-wise high-probability sketching concentration and a martingale argument (Azuma's inequality) over the zero-centered sketching and mini-batch noise. This yields a convergence bound where the only d-dependence is through J(b,d) = log^1.5(CKd²T²/δ)/√b, and the paper further shows that bounded gradient norms are not needed as an assumption but emerge automatically along the optimization trajectory.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with ResNet-101 (42M parameters), SADL with Adam+SRHT achieves 17.0% validation error at 0.1% compression rate (b=4×10⁴) and 16.7% at 1% compression rate (b=4×10⁵), outperforming all baselines by a significant margin. On SST-2 with BERT (100M parameters), SADL at 0.2% compression rate achieves nearly identical performance to the unsketched full-dimensionality training, and even at an extreme 0.02% compression rate (b=2000) remains comparable. The ablation confirms Adam+SRHT as the best configuration, with validation errors of 17.0% (0.1%) and 16.7% (1%) on CIFAR-10, versus 23.8% and 23.6% for Adam+CountSketch.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SADL outperforms all seven baselines (FetchSGD, MARINA, CocktailSGD, CDAdam, 1-bit-Adam, FedCAMS, FedPAQ) on both CIFAR-10 (ResNet) and SST-2 (BERT) at 0.1% and 1% compression rates. CocktailSGD is the closest competitor, achieving the best validation error at 1% and 10% compression on CIFAR-10, but falls short of SADL at the extreme 0.1% rate where SADL maintains consistent performance. 1-bit-Adam and FedPAQ operate at a fixed 3% compression rate and are outperformed by SADL at both 0.1% and 1%. The paper notes that most existing baselines only demonstrate performance up to 1% compression, while SADL pushes to 0.1% (and even 0.001%) where several baselines degrade significantly. On SST-2, SADL converges faster and achieves slightly better final validation error than all baselines across all tested communication budgets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the sketch-and-desketch framework of Song et al. (2023) from non-adaptive to adaptive optimizers (Adam/AMSGrad), addressing three challenges that prior compressed adaptive methods (1-bit Adam, CDAdam, FedCAMS) left unresolved: handling compression error in the ratio of first and second moments, eliminating linear dependence on ambient dimension d, and providing high-probability rather than in-expectation guarantees. The key conceptual departure is leveraging the intrinsic dimension of the loss Hessian (ratio of sum of absolute eigenvalues to the largest eigenvalue) to bound sketching noise in the second-order term, replacing the pessimistic smoothness-based bounds that scale linearly with d. The algorithmic design also draws on prior empirical work (Tang et al. 2021, Wang et al. 2022a) that demonstrated preliminary success of combining compression with adaptive methods but lacked sharp convergence theory.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The reduction from O(d) to O(log d) communication complexity is practically significant for modern models with hundreds of billions of parameters, and the empirical validation at 0.1% compression rates (b=400 for 42M params) achieving near-full-dimensional performance is compelling. However, the theoretical guarantees depend on the intrinsic dimension of the Hessian being much smaller than d, which is well-supported by empirical Hessian spectrum studies but not universally proven; the empirical validation is limited to 42M–100M parameter models, leaving open whether the logarithmic dependence and intrinsic-dimension assumption hold at the scale of frontier models. An interesting open question is the observed non-monotonic relationship between sketch size and validation error, suggesting sketching may act as an implicit regularizer, which the paper notes but does not fully characterize.
