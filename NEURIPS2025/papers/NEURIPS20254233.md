## Problem

Existing diffusion-based PDE solvers like DiffusionPDE are locked to a fixed training resolution, making them impractical when sensor configurations or discretizations change. More fundamentally, no prior work had established a rigorous theoretical basis for posterior sampling in infinite-dimensional function spaces—Tweedie's formula, which underpins all guided diffusion solvers, was only proven in finite dimensions. This paper closes both gaps by providing a discretization-agnostic diffusion posterior sampling framework with a measure-theoretic foundation in Banach spaces.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254233/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sparse observation vector u (only 3% of spatial points) and a forward operator A (typically a masking operator selecting sensor coordinates), combined with a pretrained unconditional denoising operator D_θ. The joint function a encodes both PDE parameters (coefficients, boundary conditions) and the solution field, so the same model handles forward, inverse, and hybrid problems by varying which channels are masked. Output is a full posterior sample over the joint parameter-solution space, generated at any resolution via the neural operator's discretization invariance, with multi-resolution inference (ReNoise) allowing most sampling steps at low resolution for a 2× speedup.

## Method

FunDPS trains an unconditional score model using a U-shaped neural operator (U-NO) with Gaussian random field (GRF) noise, following the denoising score matching objective in function space. At inference, the reverse diffusion process is guided by a plug-and-play likelihood term: the conditional score is decomposed into the prior score (from D_θ) plus a data-consistency gradient computed via an extended Tweedie's formula (Theorem 3.1) that links the denoiser output to E[a_0|a_t] in infinite dimensions. The guidance update at each step is a_← a_i − ζ·∇_{a_i}‖u − A(D_θ(a_i, t_i))‖², requiring no retraining for new observation patterns. Multi-resolution training (coarse-to-fine curriculum) reduces GPU hours by 25%, and the ReNoise inference strategy performs 80% of steps at low resolution before upsampling with re-noising, achieving 25× wall-clock speedup over DiffusionPDE while maintaining accuracy.

## Results

Across five PDE tasks (Darcy Flow, Poisson, Helmholtz, Navier-Stokes, Navier-Stokes with BCs) with only 3% sparse observations, FunDPS achieves the lowest L2 relative error on all 10 forward/inverse subtasks. At 500 sampling steps, FunDPS reaches 2.49% (Darcy Forward), 1.99% (Poisson Forward), 2.13% (Helmholtz Forward), 3.32% (Navier-Stokes Forward), and 4.90% (Navier-Stokes BCs Forward), while the inverse tasks range from 4.08% to 20.47%. This represents an average 32% accuracy improvement over DiffusionPDE (2000 steps) and requires 4× fewer sampling steps, with 25× faster wall-clock inference (15 s vs. 190 s per sample on an RTX 4090).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FunDPS outperforms the state-of-the-art diffusion baseline DiffusionPDE on all 10 subtasks, with an average 32% reduction in L2 error while using only 200–500 steps versus DiffusionPDE's 2000 steps (e.g., 2.49% vs. 6.07% on Darcy Forward, 2.13% vs. 12.64% on Helmholtz Forward). The gap is most pronounced on Helmholtz Forward (5.9× lower error) and Darcy Forward (2.4× lower error), while the smallest margin appears on Navier-Stokes BCs Inverse (4.08% vs. 4.18%). Against deterministic neural PDE solvers (FNO, PINO, DeepONet, PINN), FunDPS achieves orders-of-magnitude improvements, reducing errors from 28–155% (forward) and 41–233% (inverse) down to 2–20%, indicating that these single-output architectures fundamentally fail under 3% observation sparsity. In wall-clock time, FunDPS at 500 steps takes ~15 s/sample versus 190 s/sample for DiffusionPDE at 2000 steps on the same hardware, a 25× speedup attributed to the function-space formulation requiring fewer steps and smoother guidance. With the multi-resolution ReNoise inference strategy, FunDPS further halves inference time to ~7.5 s/sample while maintaining comparable accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the function-space diffusion framework of Lim et al. (Denoising Diffusion Operators) and the plug-and-play posterior sampling paradigm of DPS (Chung et al.) to the infinite-dimensional setting, bridging two previously separate lines of work. The key theoretical departure is a rigorous extension of Tweedie's formula from finite dimensions to separable Banach spaces, which enables a closed-form likelihood approximation for guided sampling without requiring a task-specific conditional model. Architecturally, it adopts the U-shaped neural operator (U-NO) for discretization invariance and the EDM training/sampling strategy, while departing from DiffusionPDE's fixed-resolution pixel-space formulation by operating natively on Gaussian random fields. The multi-resolution training and ReNoise inference strategies are novel practical contributions that leverage the resolution-invariance of neural operators to reduce compute by 25% (training) and 2× (inference).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first diffusion-based framework for PDE inverse problems that is fully discretization-agnostic, meaning a single trained model can handle arbitrary sensor configurations and grid resolutions without retraining—a significant practical advantage over fixed-resolution baselines like DiffusionPDE. The theoretical extension of Tweedie's formula to Banach spaces provides a rigorous foundation that may benefit other infinite-dimensional generative modeling tasks beyond PDEs. Key limitations include the need for manual tuning of guidance weights per problem and the finding that incorporating PDE residual losses yields only marginal gains over pure observation-based guidance, suggesting the function-space prior already encodes much of the physical structure. Open questions include extension to time-dependent PDEs with temporal observations, development of foundation models spanning multiple PDE types, and application to specialized inverse tasks like MRI reconstruction and full waveform inversion.
