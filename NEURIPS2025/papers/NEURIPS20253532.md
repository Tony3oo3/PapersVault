## Problem

Evaluating foundation models on open-ended, non-verifiable scientific literature-grounded tasks remains challenging: static expert-annotated benchmarks are limited in scale and quickly outdated, while LLM-based evaluators fail to achieve high alignment with domain experts on nuanced, knowledge-intensive scientific questions. No dynamic, community-driven platform existed to continuously rank frontier models on literature-grounded generation using real researcher preferences across diverse scientific domains.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253532/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The platform accepts free-form scientific questions from researchers, retrieves up to 30 relevant paper contexts via a multi-stage ScholarQA pipeline (LLM-based query decomposition, retrieval from Semantic Scholar's 100M+ abstracts and 11.7M full-text snippets, and re-ranking), and feeds these to two randomly sampled foundation models that produce citation-attributed, plain-text long-form responses. Users cast pairwise preference votes (with optional textual justification), which feed into a Bradley-Terry Elo ranking. The SciArena-Eval benchmark additionally outputs 2,000 curated question–response-pair–human-label triples (500 per discipline, ties excluded) for assessing model-based evaluators' accuracy against expert judgments.

## Method

SciArena combines three technical components: (1) a retrieval-augmented generation pipeline adapted from ScholarQA that decomposes user queries, retrieves and re-ranks scientific passages, and prompts two models to synthesize citation-attributed answers with postprocessing to enforce plain-text formatting and consistent citation style; (2) a Bradley-Terry logistic-regression model for Elo estimation that fits a strength vector over all pairwise comparisons simultaneously (rather than sequential online updates), with tie votes duplicated and split, and an extended BT formulation incorporating style features (citation count, citation attribution correctness, response length) to quantify and control for confounding biases; (3) a rigorous quality-control protocol including inter-annotator agreement (weighted Cohen's κ ≈ 0.76), self-consistency checks (κ ≈ 0.91), and community vote validation (73.7% clearly reasonable). The SciArena-Eval meta-benchmark then tests whether model-based pairwise evaluators (e.g., o3, GPT-5, DeepSeek-R1) can replicate human preference judgments, revealing that even the best evaluator achieves only 65.1% accuracy.

## Results

On the SciArena leaderboard (20,832 human researcher votes across 47 models), o3 achieves the highest overall Elo score of 1151.4, followed by Claude-4.1-Opus (1126.4) and GPT-5 (1122.5). Performance varies by discipline: o3 leads in Engineering (1176.7), GPT-5.1 leads in Natural Science (1161.0), and Claude-4.1-Opus leads in Humanities & Social Sciences (1173.3). On the SciArena-Eval meta-evaluation benchmark (2,000 examples), even the best model-based evaluator (o3) achieves only 65.1% accuracy in matching human preferences, compared to 50% for random guessing, highlighting the difficulty of automated evaluation for scientific literature tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The leaderboard reveals clear domain-specific strengths: o3 dominates Engineering (1176.7) and Natural Science (1185.1), while GPT-5.1 leads Natural Science (1161.0) and Claude-4.1-Opus excels in Humanities & Social Sciences (1173.3) and Healthcare (1071.7 vs. o3's 1153.6). Among open-source models, DeepSeek-R1-0528 (1041.8) and GPT-OSS-120B (1036.5) rank 10th and 11th overall, outperforming proprietary models like o4-mini (1028.6) and Claude-4-Sonnet (1025.2). On SciArena-Eval, reasoning-augmented models consistently outperform their non-reasoning counterparts: o4-mini (64.8%) surpasses GPT-4.1 (61.9%) by 2.9%, and DeepSeek-R1 (61.2%) beats DeepSeek-V3 (60.5%) by 0.7%. Lower-tier models such as Gemini-2.5-Flash-Preview (57.8%) and Llama-4-Scout/Maverick (57.7%/57.5%) perform only marginally above random guessing (50%), underscoring the unique difficulty of evaluating scientific literature responses compared to general-purpose benchmarks where similar protocols exceed 70% correlation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper transplants the Chatbot Arena paradigm (human pairwise preference voting + Bradley-Terry Elo ranking) into the scientific literature domain, where prior arena platforms (Chatbot Arena, Vision Arena, Search Arena) have not operated. For the retrieval backbone, it adapts the multi-stage ScholarQA pipeline (query decomposition, passage retrieval, re-ranking over Semantic Scholar's 100M+ abstracts) rather than building a new retrieval system. The style-bias analysis extends the BT model with style-feature coefficients following Li et al.'s Arena-Hard framework, applied here to citation count, citation attribution quality, and response length. The SciArena-Eval meta-benchmark is the first to systematically measure LLM-as-judge alignment specifically for citation-attributed, long-form scientific responses, filling a gap left by general-purpose evaluation benchmarks like AlpacaEval and WildChat.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SciArena provides a living, community-driven leaderboard for 47 frontier models on open-ended scientific literature tasks, addressing the staleness and domain-narrowness of static benchmarks. The SciArena-Eval finding that even o3 achieves only 65.1% agreement with human experts—well below the >70% seen on general-purpose benchmarks—raises a fundamental open question about whether current LLM-as-judge approaches can be trusted for domain-specialized scientific evaluation. The paper also reveals that scientific researchers care about citation *attribution correctness* (γ=0.155) far more than citation *count* (γ=0.039), a distinction absent from general-purpose search evaluation. Limitations include the exclusion of agent-based systems (Deep Research) due to API restrictions, and the platform's current coverage of only four broad disciplines.
