## Problem

Long-context LLM inference generates KV caches that exceed GPU HBM capacity, and existing swapping solutions either rely on expensive DRAM pools (vLLM) or route SSD I/O through host DRAM and PCIe, creating severe bandwidth bottlenecks (FlexGen). No prior system achieves DRAM-free, high-throughput KV cache swapping with direct GPU-to-SSD data transfer while maintaining write endurance on commodity Flash storage.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251587/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system consumes text prompts processed through a decoder-based LLM (e.g., DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-32B) and manages GPU-resident KV cache blocks of fixed size (32–128 tokens, 4KB-aligned tensors) as the unit of swapping. Output is generated text tokens plus KV cache blocks physically stored in the pSLC region of an NVMe SSD, addressed via precomputed per-layer byte offsets that align with the SSD's 4KB LBA layout. The entire swap path bypasses host DRAM, with data flowing directly between GPU HBM and SSD through the GDS DMA engine.

## Method

HiFC is a DRAM-free KV cache swapping architecture built on three tightly integrated components: (1) a Flash Cache block allocator that extends vLLM's PagedAttention memory manager with SSD-resident blocks allocated via a sequential append policy to generate ordered I/O; (2) a Flash-aware block management strategy that confines writes to the high-performance pSLC zone (≈20% of SSD capacity), achieving a write amplification factor of 1.02 versus the SSD's 1.4 spec and extending endurance by up to 8×; and (3) a GDS-accelerated cache engine that transfers KV tensors directly between GPU HBM and SSD using multi-threaded I/O (up to 16 threads) and 4KB-aligned GDS buffers, sustaining >4.7 GiB/s sequential throughput. The key insight is that vLLM's pipeline-aware sequence scheduling introduces enough slack (a throughput gap between ideal and realized tokens/s) to fully hide Flash swap latency, making the system compute-bound rather than I/O-bound even under heavy swap loads.

## Results

HiFC achieves inference throughput within 1–2% of DRAM-based swapping across all tested models (DS-Llama-8B, DS-Qwen-14B, Mistral-7B) and datasets (Qasper, GovReport, NarrativeQA), with throughput ranging from 46.6 to 406.8 tokens/s depending on model and context length. The 3-year memory expansion cost is $136 for HiFC versus $614 for DRAM (4.5× savings), driven by 3.6× lower CapEx and 12.8× lower power draw. HiFC's sequential append strategy achieves a Write Amplification Factor of 1.02 (vs. 1.40 SSD spec), and operating in pSLC mode extends SSD endurance by up to 8× (6,000 TiB vs. 750 TiB official TBW). Session initialization at 100 GiB KV cache is 2.81× faster with HiFC (32 s) than DRAM (90 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is DRAM-based KV cache swapping as implemented in vLLM (PagedAttention). HiFC matches or slightly exceeds DRAM throughput in most configurations: on DS-Llama-8B, HiFC outperforms DRAM by 6% on GovReport (182.3 vs. 172.0 tokens/s) and by 0.7% on NarrativeQA (95.8 vs. 95.1), while being within 0.2% on Qasper. On DS-Qwen-14B, HiFC leads by 2.6% on GovReport (103.5 vs. 100.9) and ties on NarrativeQA (46.6 vs. 46.4). On Mistral-7B, DRAM holds a small 1.6–2.2% edge, the largest gap observed. In the scalability experiment (Table 2), HiFC at GPU:SSD = 1:1 achieves 182 tokens/s versus 172 for the GPU+DRAM baseline, and scales to 367 tokens/s in a 2:1 data-parallel setup with no I/O bottleneck. The paper also contrasts HiFC with InstInfer (Appendix A), noting that while InstInfer achieves up to 11.1× throughput over FlexGen using specialized Computational Storage Drives (~$2,526), HiFC delivers comparable-to-DRAM performance on commodity NVMe SSDs (~$136) with a 4.5× lower 3-year TCO.

## Relation to Prior Work

HiFC extends vLLM's PagedAttention block-based KV cache management by replacing the host-DRAM swap tier with a direct GPU-to-SSD path via GPU Direct Storage, operating exclusively within the pseudo-SLC region of commodity NVMe drives. It departs from FlexGen's CPU-mediated SSD offloading (which suffers PCIe bottlenecks) and from InstInfer's specialized Computational Storage Drive approach, instead using a software-only solution on standard hardware. The Flash-aware sequential append allocation strategy and the pipeline-aware latency-hiding model are novel contributions layered on top of vLLM's existing scheduler and cache engine.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 4.5× three-year cost reduction for KV cache memory expansion (128 GiB DRAM → 1 TiB pSLC SSD) makes long-context LLM inference economically viable at scale, and the near-identical throughput to DRAM swapping (within 1–2% across multiple models and datasets) removes the primary objection to SSD-based offloading. Open questions remain around short-context latency sensitivity, multi-GPU Flash bandwidth contention, and the domain expertise required for SSD tuning. The pipeline-gap model (Appendix G) that formally explains why swap latency is hidden under deep batching is a useful analytical tool, but the approach has not been validated on 100B+ parameter models or on next-generation hardware (e.g., GB200 with CXL memory).
