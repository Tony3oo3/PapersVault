## Problem

Classical Optimal Transport requires equal total mass between source and target distributions and scales cubically with sample size, making it impractical for imbalanced or noisy data. While Sliced and Tree-Sliced Wasserstein distances reduce computational cost, and Partial Transport relaxes the mass constraint, no existing method combines the tree-sliced efficiency with partial transport's robustness to unbalanced measures. This paper fills that gap by extending the Tree-Sliced Wasserstein framework to the partial transport setting.

## Input / Output

The method takes two nonnegative Borel measures (or finite point sets) in R^d with potentially unequal total mass and produces a scalar E(d)-invariant distance value. In practice, the source measure is normalized to unit mass, and the target mass ν(T) serves as a single tunable hyperparameter controlling the degree of partiality (i.e., what fraction of mass is transported). Gradients with respect to individual samples are also available, enabling use in generative modeling and gradient-flow applications without requiring gradients with respect to total input masses.

## Method

The key insight is that the Entropy Partial Transport problem on a tree metric space can be equivalently reformulated as a standard balanced OT problem between augmented measures (obtained by adding an auxiliary sink point with appropriate mass), which admits a closed-form solution via the 1-Wasserstein formula on trees. The overall PartialTSW distance is then defined as the expectation of this closed-form tree-slice cost over a space of randomly sampled tree systems, approximated via Monte Carlo with L trees. The paper proves that PartialTSW is an E(d)-invariant metric on the space of measures M(R^d) and that its computational complexity is O(Lkn log n + Lkdn)—identical to the balanced TSW—making it orders of magnitude faster than existing UOT/PT solvers like Sinkhorn or PAWL for large n.

## Results

On the MNIST robust generative modeling task (target digit 0 with 10% digit-1 outliers), PartialTSW achieves 0.00% outlier rate with a 55s runtime, matching Sinkhorn and PAWL on outlier rejection while being 6.5× and 1.6× faster respectively. On the FFHQ Young-to-Adult image translation task (38K vs 10.5K images), PartialTSW (ν(T)=0.9) achieves 91.06% translation accuracy with 0.4183 LPIPS, outperforming UOT-FM (82.38%, 0.4713) and ULightOT (86.06%, 0.4233) on both metrics. At ν(T)=0.3, PartialTSW reaches 99.11% accuracy, significantly surpassing standard OT methods (SW: 89.88%, Db-TSW: 89.96%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PartialTSW decisively outperforms all balanced OT baselines (SW, Db-TSW) on outlier rejection (0% vs. 15–16%) while matching Db-TSW's runtime (55s) and being only 18s slower than SW. Against sliced partial/unbalanced OT methods (SOPT, SPOT, SUOT, USOT), PartialTSW achieves 0% outliers versus 13–41% for all of them, while running 5–6× faster (55s vs. 275–306s). Among the three methods achieving perfect outlier rejection (PartialTSW, Sinkhorn, PAWL), PartialTSW is the fastest at 55s compared to Sinkhorn's 358s and PAWL's 88s, and the paper notes it also produces more diverse samples. On the FFHQ translation task, PartialTSW (ν(T)=0.9) beats UOT-FM (λ=0.1) by 8.7 points in accuracy (91.06% vs. 82.38%) and 0.053 in LPIPS (0.4183 vs. 0.4713), and beats ULightOT (τ=10000) by 5.0 points in accuracy (91.06% vs. 86.06%) with slightly better LPIPS (0.4183 vs. 0.4233). Standard OT methods (SW, Db-TSW) achieve comparable LPIPS (~0.407) but lower accuracy (~90%), and cannot handle the mass imbalance without the partial transport mechanism.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate threads: the Tree-Sliced Wasserstein (TSW) framework [81, 80], which projects Euclidean measures onto tree-metric spaces to exploit closed-form 1-Wasserstein solutions, and the Entropy Partial Transport (EPT) formulation on tree metrics [42], which handles unbalanced measures via a balanced-OT reformulation with an auxiliary sink node. The key conceptual step is recognizing that the EPT closed-form solution (Equation 8) can be plugged directly into the TSW averaging procedure over tree systems, yielding PartialTSW without additional algorithmic machinery. This departs from prior sliced UOT/PT methods [7, 2, 9, 13] that operate on 1-D projections or require iterative solvers, by inheriting the richer geometric structure of tree systems while retaining the partial-transport flexibility.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PartialTSW offers a practically significant speedup (log-linear in sample size, ~3 orders of magnitude faster than Sinkhorn/PAWL at n=10⁵) for unbalanced transport in high-dimensional settings, making partial transport viable for large-scale generative modeling and domain adaptation where cubic-cost OT is infeasible. The E(d)-invariance and metric properties (Theorem 3.3) provide theoretical grounding that some competing sliced UOT variants lack. A notable limitation acknowledged by the authors is that PartialTSW defines a distance without yielding explicit transport plans or maps, which restricts its use in tasks requiring actual point correspondences. Future work on constructing partial transport plans within the tree-sliced setting, and on extending to p>1 or non-Euclidean domains, would broaden applicability.
