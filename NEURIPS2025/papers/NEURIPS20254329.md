## Problem

Current Vision-Language Models demonstrate strong general visual understanding but lack demonstrated proficiency in comprehending the nuanced cinematic grammar embedded within individual shots—shot size, framing, camera angle, lens size, lighting, composition, and camera movement. No dedicated benchmark or large-scale dataset existed to rigorously evaluate or train this expert-level cinematographic comprehension, leaving a critical gap for both fine-grained visual understanding and AI-assisted video generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254329/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes high-resolution movie stills or video clips (sampled at 12 FPS, max 360×640 resolution for open-source models) paired with a four-option multiple-choice question targeting one of eight cinematography dimensions. For multi-keyword dimensions like lighting condition, each option contains the same number of keywords, with only the correct option containing all correct terms. The model outputs a single classification answer, and in the GRPO-trained variant, a structured reasoning chain enclosed in <think>... tags followed by the final answer in <answer>...</answer> tags.

## Method

ShotVL is built on Qwen2.5-VL-3B/7B-Instruct and trained in two stages. Stage 1 performs full-parameter supervised fine-tuning on ~60k multiple-choice QA pairs from the ShotQA dataset (58k images + 1.2k video clips from 243 films) using cross-entropy loss to align visual features with cinematic terminology. Stage 2 applies Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) on a curated ~8k mid-difficulty subset, where the model generates G=12 candidate responses per question, a rule-based binary reward scores format compliance and answer correctness, and advantages are computed by normalizing rewards within each group before clipped policy updates. Ablations show that GRPO's outcome-based reward supervision outperforms chain-of-thought SFT (which introduces noise from low-quality Gemini-generated reasoning chains), and the SFT→GRPO pipeline yields the best overall performance, with the 7B variant reaching 70.1% average accuracy on ShotBench—surpassing GPT-4o (59.3%) and Qwen2.5-VL-72B (59.1%) by over 10 points.

## Results

On ShotBench, ShotVL (7B) achieves a 70.1% average accuracy across all eight cinematography dimensions, surpassing GPT-4o (59.3%) by 10.8 points and Qwen2.5-VL-72B-Instruct (59.1%) by 11.0 points. Even the smaller ShotVL (3B) reaches 65.1% average, outperforming the 72B open-source model by 6.0 points while using 24× fewer parameters. The largest gains appear in Camera Angle (+15.9 over GPT-4o) and Lens Size (+14.9), while Camera Movement remains the hardest dimension, though ShotVL (7B) still improves it from 48.9% to 60.6%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ShotVL (7B) decisively outperforms all 24 evaluated VLMs on ShotBench, with the closest competitors being GPT-4o (59.3% avg) and Qwen2.5-VL-72B-Instruct (59.1% avg), both trailing by over 10 points. The 3B variant already surpasses every proprietary model (GPT-4o, Gemini-2.0-flash at 51.5%, Gemini-2.5-flash at 54.5%) and every open-source model including InternVL3-78B (57.2%) and InternVL2.5-38B (57.2%). The most dramatic margin is in Camera Angle, where ShotVL (7B) scores 74.1% versus 58.2% for GPT-4o and 56.7% for Qwen2.5-VL-72B, reflecting the targeted training on spatial reasoning. In Camera Movement—the dimension where most models hover near random (25%)—ShotVL (7B) reaches 60.6%, a 11.7-point improvement over the previous best (Qwen2.5-VL-72B at 48.9%). Notably, ShotVL (3B) with only 3B parameters beats the 72B Qwen2.5-VL by 6.0 points on average, demonstrating that domain-specific training data and GRPO reasoning are more effective than raw model scale for cinematography understanding.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of cinematography understanding work (MovieShots, MovieNet, CineScale2, CameraBench, CineTechBench) by being the first to unify all eight core cinematography dimensions into a single VLM evaluation benchmark with expert-annotated QA pairs. Methodologically, it builds on Qwen2.5-VL as the base architecture and adopts the GRPO reinforcement learning paradigm from DeepSeekMath/DeepSeek-R1, applying outcome-reward-based RL to a vision-language setting for the first time in this domain. It departs from prior VLM benchmarks (MMBench, MMVU, EgoSchema) that evaluate general visual understanding but lack the domain-specific granularity needed for professional cinematographic terminology.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ShotBench provides the first rigorous, expert-grounded standard for measuring whether VLMs truly understand cinematic language, revealing a substantial gap (even GPT-4o <60%) that motivates targeted research. The practical impact extends to AI-assisted filmmaking and video generation, where cinematography-aware models could enable shot planning and style-matched generation. Key limitations include data imbalance across dimensions (e.g., dolly zoom at 0.6%), validation primarily on the 3B base model, and reliance on Western Oscar-nominated films that may not capture global cinematic traditions. Open questions include whether scaling to larger base models or synthetic data for rare movements can close the remaining gap, and whether the GRPO-based reasoning transfer to dynamic (video) tasks generalizes beyond the static-image ablation setting.
