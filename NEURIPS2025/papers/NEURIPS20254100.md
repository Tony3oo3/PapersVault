## Problem

Hybrid language models combining Attention and State Space Model (SSM/Mamba) layers achieve strong accuracy and efficiency, but remain too large for resource-constrained deployment. While structured pruning with knowledge distillation has been well-studied for Transformer-only architectures, no prior work presents a holistic compression strategy for hybrid models that simultaneously handles the unique structural constraints of SSM layers alongside FFN, embedding, and depth pruning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254100/cropped_page1_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pretrained hybrid LLM (Nemotron-H 8B, 52 layers) and a small set of 1024 calibration text samples (sequence length 8192) used solely for forward-pass activation scoring. It produces a compressed 4B-parameter hybrid model through a multi-stage pipeline: activation-based importance scoring, group-constrained structured pruning across Mamba heads/channels, FFN neurons, embedding dimensions, and layers, followed by extended knowledge distillation (~380B tokens) using the original 8B model as teacher. The architecture search explores 125 candidate configurations across depth (26–52 layers), embedding width (3072–4096), FFN dimension (9984–21504), Mamba heads (64–128), and head channels (32–64).

## Method

The core contribution is a group-aware pruning method for Mamba layers that respects the structural constraint imposed by the B_t x_t broadcasting operation: heads within a Mamba group share a common B matrix, so permuting or pruning heads across groups would violate the SSM's group-wise computation semantics. The scoring procedure is nested—first, head channels are ranked by L2-norm of W_x activations aggregated over batch and sequence; then, head importance is computed using only the surviving channels; finally, heads are ranked independently within each group before concatenation. This Mamba pruning is unified with activation-based FFN neuron pruning, embedding channel pruning (scored across all consuming layers), and KLD-based depth pruning into a single recipe. An architecture search over 125 candidates selects the optimal configuration via zero-shot validation loss followed by lightweight KD (3.8B tokens) for reliable ranking, and the final model undergoes extended FKLD-based distillation (~380B tokens) to recover accuracy, yielding a 4B model that retains >96% of the 8B parent's accuracy at ~2× faster inference throughput.

## Results

NEMOTRON-H 4B-Base achieves an average score of 64.5 across 16 benchmarks, retaining over 96% of the parent Nemotron-H 8B's accuracy (66.7) while requiring up to 40× fewer training tokens (0.38T vs. 15T). The instruction-tuned NEMOTRON-H 4B-Instruct-128k leads on GSM8K (88.93), MATH-500 (76.4), HumanEval (76.2), and MBPP (78.6) among similarly-sized models, and achieves the highest RULER scores up to 128k context (86.28 at 16k, 63.57 at 128k). Inference throughput is ~2× faster than the second-best Phi-4-Mini-4B, with ~1.8× lower latency. Safety scores remain strong post-compression: Garak 67.77 (vs. 70.75 for 8B) and AEGIS 98.17 (vs. 99.83).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the parent Nemotron-H 8B, the compressed 4B model retains 96.7% of average accuracy (64.5 vs. 66.7) while delivering ~2× faster inference throughput and ~1.4× lower latency. Compared to Minitron-compressed Llama models (Llama-Minitron4B-Width-Base), NEMOTRON-H 4B substantially outperforms on coding benchmarks (HumanEval: 59.8 vs. 34.4; MBPP: 65.0 vs. 47.9) and knowledge tasks (MMLU: 68.1 vs. 59.8; ARC Challenge: 54.4 vs. 49.0), though Qwen-2.5-3B edges ahead on GSM8K (75.2 vs. 69.6) and RACE (84.5 vs. 80.9). For instruction-tuned models, NEMOTRON-H 4B-Instruct-128k leads Phi-4-Mini-4B on GSM8K (88.93 vs. 87.71), MATH-500 (76.4 vs. 70.8), HumanEval (76.2 vs. 73.17), and MBPP (78.6 vs. 67.46), while Phi-4-Mini-4B slightly leads on IFEval (74.78 vs. 76.24 is actually NEMOTRON-H winning). On RULER long-context, NEMOTRON-H 4B achieves the highest scores at all tested lengths up to 128k (e.g., 63.57 at 128k vs. 59.26 for Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct and 20.07 for Phi-4-Mini-4B). In the generalizability experiment (Appendix A.6), the compressed Mamba2 780M model (trained on 10.5B tokens) outperforms the Mamba2 780M trained from scratch on 300B tokens (avg 44.7 vs. 42.5) and matches the 1.3B model (44.9).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Minitron structured-pruning-plus-distillation pipeline [20] from Transformer-only architectures to hybrid Mamba-Transformer models, introducing a group-aware constraint on Mamba head permutations that preserves the SSM broadcasting semantics (B_t × x_t). It departs from prior SSM pruning work—Mamba-Shredder [19], which removes the entire state-space module, and Ghattas et al. [9], which targets state-space dimension and head merging—by performing fine-grained, group-respecting head and channel pruning while simultaneously combining it with FFN, embedding, and depth pruning in a unified recipe. No prior work had presented a holistic multi-axis compression strategy for hybrid architectures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first unified compression recipe for hybrid Mamba-Transformer models, and the 40× reduction in training tokens (380B vs. 15T for the parent) makes it practically significant for deploying competitive 4B-class models at a fraction of the compute cost. The group-aware permutation constraint is a non-trivial structural insight that any future SSM pruning work must account for. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to other SSM variants beyond Mamba/Mamba2, whether the architecture search space (125 candidates) is near-optimal, and whether the recipe scales to much larger parent models (e.g., 70B → 35B) where the compute for importance estimation and KD becomes prohibitive.
