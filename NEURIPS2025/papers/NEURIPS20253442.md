## Problem

Existing ligand binding site detection methods suffer from three interrelated limitations: (1) PDB-centric datasets treat each protein-ligand complex as an independent entry, ignoring that the same protein may have multiple distinct binding sites across different structures, introducing significant statistical bias; (2) detection is modeled as a discontinuous pipeline of binary segmentation followed by clustering, preventing end-to-end optimization and struggling with overlapping sites; (3) standard DCC/DCA metrics only evaluate binding site centers relative to ligand positions, disregarding shape, size, and residue composition critical for downstream drug design tasks.

## Input / Output

UniSite-1D consumes only the protein amino acid sequence (length ≤ 800, augmented with ESM-2 pre-trained embeddings), while UniSite-3D additionally takes the 3D structure as a residue-level relational graph over alpha carbons with sequential, radius, and K-nearest-neighbor edges. Both variants output a fixed set of N=32 binding site predictions in a single forward pass, each consisting of a binary residue mask of length L and a confidence score, with no post-processing clustering step required. For applications needing binding site centers, these are derived as centroids of the convex hull of predicted site atoms.

## Method

UniSite formulates binding site detection as a set prediction task following the DETR paradigm: a transformer encoder (6 layers, d_model=256) extracts residue-level features via dual pathways (sequence encoder with amino acid + positional + ESM-2 embeddings, and an optional GearNet-Edge E(3)-invariant GNN structural encoder), then a 6-layer transformer decoder processes 32 learnable site queries through self- and cross-attention to generate site embeddings. A segmentation module converts these into N binary masks via dot-product with residue features and a sigmoid, plus a two-class (binding/non-binding) probability. Training uses a set prediction loss with Hungarian-algorithm bijective matching between predictions and padded ground truth, where the matching cost combines classification cross-entropy and a weighted BCE+Dice mask loss. The paper also introduces IoU-based Average Precision with one-to-one matching (following COCO protocols) as a replacement for DCC/DCA, enabling shape-aware evaluation without double-counting.

## Results

On the proposed UniSite-DS benchmark, UniSite-3D achieves AP@0.3 of 0.5603 and AP@0.5 of 0.3835, outperforming all baselines including GrASP (0.4469/0.2848) and P2Rank (0.5056/0.2157). On the external HOLO4K-sc benchmark, UniSite-3D reaches AP@0.3 of 0.7091, surpassing the next-best GrASP (0.6668) by 4.2 points, while UniSite-1D (sequence-only) achieves 0.6867. On COACH420, UniSite-3D attains AP@0.3 of 0.7196, marginally ahead of GrASP (0.7150). Notably, UniSite-1D, which requires no 3D structure input, outperforms all structure-based baselines on UniSite-DS, demonstrating strong structure-free binding site detection capability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UniSite-3D achieves the best AP@0.3 on both HOLO4K-sc (0.7091 vs. GrASP's 0.6668) and COACH420 (0.7196 vs. GrASP's 0.7150), and dominates all baselines on the UniSite-DS benchmark (AP@0.3: 0.5603 vs. P2Rank's 0.5056). UniSite-1D, operating on sequence alone, outperforms all structure-based baselines on UniSite-DS (AP@0.3: 0.5121 vs. GrASP's 0.4469), highlighting the statistical bias in PDB-centric training data. On traditional DCC/DCA metrics, VN-EGNN and P2Rank remain competitive (e.g., P2Rank DCA 0.8188 on HOLO4K-sc vs. UniSite-3D's 0.7879), but both are heavily penalized under the proposed IoU-based AP due to their inability to predict accurate residue-level site shapes. Fpocket, the geometry-based baseline, consistently ranks last across all metrics and datasets, while Fpocket-rescore (Fpocket + P2Rank rescoring) provides a meaningful improvement, confirming the importance of proper site scoring. DeepPocket (3D-CNN) underperforms both GrASP and UniSite on AP metrics, suggesting that end-to-end set prediction with bijective matching offers advantages over segmentation-then-clustering pipelines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant PDB-centric paradigm in binding site detection, where each protein-ligand complex is treated as an independent data entry, by introducing a UniProt-centric dataset that integrates all binding sites across multiple structures of the same protein. Methodologically, it adapts the DETR-style set prediction framework (originally from object detection) to the protein binding site domain, replacing the conventional two-stage pipeline of semantic segmentation followed by clustering with a single end-to-end forward pass using Hungarian-algorithm-based bijective matching. For structural encoding, it leverages the off-the-shelf GearNet-Edge GNN without custom feature engineering, and for sequence encoding it incorporates ESM-2 embeddings, positioning the work at the intersection of protein representation learning and structured prediction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The UniProt-centric dataset corrects a fundamental statistical bias that has limited prior methods: by integrating sites across structures, it reveals that 4.81× more proteins have multiple binding sites than previously captured, which is critical for allosteric drug discovery. The IoU-based AP metric exposes that ~20% of proteins suffer from double-counting under DCC/DCA and that ground-truth DCC/DCA values are systematically non-zero (2.15 Å and 1.57 Å), undermining the reliability of existing benchmarks. Open questions include whether automated curation can replace the manual inspection step, whether specialized feature engineering (beyond off-the-shelf GearNet-Edge) would further improve performance, and whether the end-to-end set prediction paradigm generalizes to other structured protein annotation tasks.
