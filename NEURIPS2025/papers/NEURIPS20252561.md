## Problem

Existing 3DGS-based dynamic avatar reconstruction methods rely on affine approximation for Gaussian projection, which neglects depth-induced shear effects and fails to be perspective-aware. This causes unreliable alpha blending and chromatic inconsistencies under the subtle, gradual facial expression changes typical of human skin tones, ultimately degrading geometric fidelity in reconstructed meshes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252561/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes calibrated multi-view RGB video sequences (16 views) preprocessed with VHAP to obtain per-frame FLAME mesh tracking parameters (identity, expression, pose). It produces a 4D Gaussian avatar field, per-frame textured meshes extracted via Marching Tetrahedra on an opacity-guided SDF, and supports novel-view synthesis and self-reenactment rendering. Training runs for 300k iterations with ≤24 GB memory (images loaded on-the-fly), and mesh extraction takes approximately 8 seconds per frame after the initial triangulation.

## Method

TGA introduces a Perspective-Aware Gaussian Transformation that jointly models temporal deformation and spatial projection: a Jacobian-guided mechanism (Eq. 3–4) adaptively warps each Gaussian anisotropically according to the directional variation of its parent FLAME triangle, replacing the isotropic scaling of vanilla 3DMM-Gaussian rigging, while a homogeneous formulation (Eq. 5–9) replaces the affine EWA splatting to preserve the Gaussian's depth dimension during projection, enabling true perspective-divide and perspective-consistent alpha blending. For fast dynamic mesh extraction, the method organizes Gaussians into a dynamic binary radix BVH tree and performs incremental BVH pivoting—applying Lift and Reorder Rebalancer rotations per frame to simulate topological vertex movements—so that only 'hopping Gaussians' whose rotation cost exceeds a threshold are filtered and re-triangulated, avoiding full re-triangulation. The surface is extracted by binary-searching the zero level set of an opacity-guided signed distance field over tetrahedral grids. Training is supervised with photometric (L1 + D-SSIM), depth-distortion, normal-consistency, scaling/position regularization, and a deformed-normal loss that propagates Jacobian deformation into occluded-region geometry.

## Results

On the Multiface dataset, TGA achieves the best geometric accuracy across all three metrics: L1-Chamfer distance of 2.16 mm, normal MAE of 17.7°, and Recall@2.5mm of 0.802, outperforming the closest competitor Topo4D (2.33, 19.3, 0.772). For mesh-based rendering, TGA reaches PSNR 23.77 and SSIM 0.645, and for Gaussian-based novel view synthesis it achieves PSNR 31.32, SSIM 0.936, and LPIPS 0.058. Mesh extraction runs at ~8 s per frame, roughly 2.5× faster than GaussianAvatars and SurFhead (~20 s each).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TGA outperforms all seven geometric baselines on the Multiface dataset, with the largest margins over NHA (CD 6.02→2.16, MAE 28.9→17.7) and 3DDFA-V3 (CD 4.35→2.16). The closest competitor is Topo4D, which TGA beats by 0.17 mm in Chamfer distance, 1.6° in normal MAE, and 3 percentage points in Recall. For rendering quality (Table 1), TGA surpasses GaussianAvatars (PSNR 31.32 vs 30.29) and SurFhead (31.32 vs 29.94) in Gaussian-based NVS, while also achieving the best mesh-rendering PSNR (23.77) and SSIM (0.645). In inference speed, TGA extracts meshes in ~8 s per frame versus ~20 s for GaussianAvatars and SurFhead, and Topo4D which extracts meshes only during training. In the appendix comparison against EWA-splatting improvements (3DGUT, 3DGS-marcher), TGA still leads with PSNR 31.37 vs 30.54 and 29.96, confirming that the homogeneous projection provides gains beyond prior perspective-correction approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

TGA builds directly on the GaussianAvatars framework [2], which binds 3D Gaussians to FLAME mesh triangles for controllable head animation, and on the 3DGS rendering paradigm [1]. Its key conceptual departure is extending the homogeneous (perspective-accurate) splatting formulation originally developed for 2D Gaussians [61,62] to 3D Gaussians, and jointly integrating Jacobian-guided anisotropic deformation [60] into that homogeneous projection rather than treating them as separate concerns. For surface extraction, it adapts the opacity-field SDF approach from Gaussian Opacity Fields [8] and introduces a dynamic BVH tree pivoting mechanism inspired by animated-scene BVH updates [64] to enable incremental, frame-by-frame mesh reconstruction without full re-triangulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a concrete and underappreciated failure mode in 3DGS-based avatar methods: the affine projection approximation causes depth-induced shear errors that degrade both color blending and geometric fidelity precisely where skin-tone subtlety matters most. The theoretical analysis (Appendix A) formalizing why homogeneous projection is strictly more view-sensitive provides a principled justification that could inform future 3DGS rendering pipelines beyond avatars. However, the method remains limited to controlled multi-view head capture, struggles with translucent regions (hair, eyes) and severe deformations, and does not address relighting or full-body extension—key open questions for broader deployment in virtual production and digital-twin applications.
