## Problem

Existing MLLM benchmarks focus on low-level perceptual tasks (scene understanding, OCR, video content analysis) and fail to evaluate high-level cognitive semantics in human conversations such as intent, emotion, dialogue act, and communication behavior. No comprehensive benchmark exists to assess whether foundation models can truly comprehend the nuanced, multi-dimensional semantics underlying multimodal utterances.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253567/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each sample is an utterance-level multimodal tuple consisting of a video clip (average 2.4–9.1 s, up to 1316 s), synchronized audio, and a text transcription. The model receives a prompt template that embeds the transcription as context, optionally prepends a <video> token for MLLMs, and appends a task-specific query with the candidate label set. The output is a single class label in the format 'dimension: label', constrained to one of the dimension-specific label sets (ranging from 2 to 30 classes).

## Method

MMLA aggregates 61,016 utterances from nine public datasets spanning six cognitive dimensions (intent, dialogue act, emotion, sentiment, speaking style, communication behavior) and evaluates eight model branches (0.5B–72B) plus GPT-4o under three protocols: zero-shot inference, supervised fine-tuning (SFT), and instruction tuning (IT). SFT and IT both use LoRA for parameter-efficient adaptation, minimizing cross-entropy loss over autoregressive token predictions with the same prompt template used at inference. IT differs from SFT by jointly training on all tasks simultaneously rather than one task at a time, enabling a single unified model to handle all six dimensions. The key finding is that SFT activates multimodal reasoning in MLLMs (gains of 10–50% over zero-shot), while IT achieves comparable or better performance across tasks, and smaller models (7–8B) rival 72B counterparts after tuning.

## Results

On the MMLA benchmark (61K utterances, 9 datasets, 6 semantic dimensions), the best zero-shot model GPT-4o achieves only 52.60% average accuracy, while the best fine-tuned model Qwen2-VL-72B (SFT) reaches 69.18% ACC. After instruction tuning, LLaVA-Video-72B attains 68.87% ACC, and 72B Qwen2-VL becomes the first model to exceed human performance on MIntRec (86.3% vs. 85.5%). Notably, small 7B/8B MLLMs after SFT achieve 67.47–68.88% ACC, within 1–2% of their 72B counterparts, and even the best model remains below 70% overall accuracy, highlighting the difficulty of high-level cognitive semantics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among MLLMs, Qwen2-VL-72B (SFT) achieves the best overall ACC of 69.18%, narrowly beating MiniCPM-V-2.6-8B (SFT) at 68.88% and LLaVA-Video-72B (IT) at 68.87%; the 72B LLaVA-OV and LLaVA-Video variants follow at 68.67% and 68.44% respectively. In zero-shot inference, GPT-4o leads at 52.60% ACC, but the 7B LLM InternLM2.5-7B (50.28%) is within 2% and outperforms most same-scale MLLMs (e.g., LLaVA-Video-7B at 43.32%, VideoLLaMA2-7B at 42.82%), indicating that non-verbal modalities do not yet provide an advantage without task-specific training. After SFT, all MLLMs consistently surpass LLMs of comparable scale by 1–3% (e.g., Qwen2-VL-7B SFT 67.60% vs. Qwen2-7B SFT 66.15%), confirming that supervised multimodal training activates cross-modal reasoning. Against SOTA task-specific MML methods (MIntOOD, MulT, UniMSE, TCL-MAP, MMML, ALMT, SimMMDG), fine-tuned MLLMs set new state-of-the-art on most datasets except IEMOCAP and MUStARD; 72B Qwen2-VL (IT) exceeds human performance on MIntRec (86.3% vs. 85.5%) and 72B LLaVA-Video (IT) improves over the SOTA MML method by 6.3% on MIntRec2.0. The small 8B MiniCPM-V-2.6 outperforms SOTA MML baselines on seven of nine datasets after IT, demonstrating that parameter-efficient models can rival or surpass specialized architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in multimodal language analysis, which has relied on lightweight fusion networks (MulT, UniMSE, tensor-based methods) trained from scratch on specific tasks, by instead evaluating pre-trained foundation models (LLMs and MLLMs) as general-purpose solvers. It also carves new ground relative to existing MLLM benchmarks, which target perceptual-level or elementary cognitive tasks (scene understanding, OCR, video QA), by focusing exclusively on high-level conversational semantics across six dimensions. The benchmark repurposes nine established multimodal datasets (MIntRec, MELD, IEMOCAP, MOSI, etc.) that were previously used to train task-specific fusion models, reframing them as a unified evaluation suite for foundation models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MMLA establishes the first rigorous, large-scale testbed for measuring whether foundation models can truly understand cognitive-level human communication, and the sobering result—best models plateau around 69% accuracy even after fine-tuning—quantifies a substantial gap that the field has not previously measured. The finding that 7–8B MLLMs rival 72B counterparts after SFT/IT has direct practical implications for deployment cost, while the observation that zero-shot MLLMs underperform same-scale LLMs challenges the assumption that multimodality is automatically beneficial without task-specific training. Open questions include what architectural changes or data curation strategies are needed to close the remaining 30% gap, and whether instruction tuning can be extended to handle even more diverse semantic dimensions without the hallucination issues observed in some models.
