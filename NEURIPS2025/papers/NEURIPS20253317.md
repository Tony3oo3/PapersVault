## Problem

When skeletal features from ST-GCNs are projected into Euclidean space for processing by a large language model, fine-grained kinematic details (e.g., subtle finger articulations) are compressed by the dominant norm of global body motions, making signs that differ only in local articulation nearly indistinguishable. The paper asks whether the hierarchical, tree-like structure of sign language kinematics can be better preserved by embedding skeletal representations in hyperbolic geometry, where exponential volume growth near the boundary provides capacity to separate fine-grained motions while the near-Euclidean origin handles broader semantics.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 2D skeletal keypoints (COCO-WholeBody format) extracted via RTMPose from video, partitioned into four anatomical groups (body: 9 joints, left/right hands: 21 joints each, face: 16 keypoints) that are anchor-normalised per part. These are encoded by part-specific ST-GCNs and fed into a pre-trained mT5 decoder that produces the translated text. The hyperbolic regularisation branch operates on temporally mean-pooled ST-GCN outputs, so the primary translation path remains standard Euclidean; the Poincaré ball branch acts purely as a geometric regulariser on the language model's representations.

## Method

Geo-Sign projects part-specific ST-GCN features into a Poincaré ball with learnable curvature c via a hyperbolic projection layer (learnable linear map + adaptive scalar scaling + exponential map at the origin), then regularises a pre-trained mT5 model using a geometric contrastive loss (InfoNCE adapted to geodesic distances with learnable temperature and margin). Two alignment strategies are explored: a Pooled Method that aggregates part embeddings via a weighted Fréchet mean (weights proportional to distance from origin) and aligns the global pose embedding with a global text embedding, and a Token Method that uses a hyperbolic attention mechanism where each pose part embedding queries a sequence of Möbius-transformed text token keys, producing part-specific contextual text embeddings for finer-grained contrastive alignment. The curvature c is learned end-to-end via Riemannian Adam (RAdam) in log-space, while all Euclidean parameters use AdamW; the total loss blends cross-entropy translation loss with the hyperbolic regularisation term via a dynamically scheduled, partially learnable coefficient α. The key insight is that hyperbolic geometry serves not as the primary encoder but as a geometric inductive bias that guides the LLM to internalise kinematic hierarchy, with the Poincaré ball's boundary expansion naturally separating fine-grained hand/face articulations from broader body-level semantics.

## Results

On CSL-Daily, Geo-Sign (Hyperbolic Token) using only pose data achieves 27.42% BLEU-4 and 57.95% ROUGE-L, improving +1.81 BLEU-4 and +3.03 ROUGE-L over the Uni-Sign (Pose) baseline (25.61%, 54.92%). This pose-only result surpasses all prior gloss-free pose methods and is competitive with or exceeds several RGB-only and gloss-based methods. On How2Sign (ASL), Geo-Sign (Token) reaches 15.1% BLEU-4 and 35.4% ROUGE-L, outperforming all pose-based baselines but trailing the best RGB method (SSVP-SLT, 15.5% BLEU-4). On WLASL2000 (isolated sign recognition), Geo-Sign (Token) achieves 63.64% per-instance and 61.89% per-class Top-1 accuracy, a +0.51 and +0.99 improvement over Uni-Sign (Pose).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Geo-Sign (Hyperbolic Token) outperforms the Uni-Sign (Pose) baseline by +1.81 BLEU-4 and +3.03 ROUGE-L, and also surpasses Uni-Sign (Pose+RGB) by +1.06 BLEU-4 and +1.44 ROUGE-L using only skeletal input. Against gloss-free RGB methods, it exceeds C2RL by +5.81 BLEU-4 and +9.74 ROUGE-L, Sign2GPT by +12.02 BLEU-4, and SignLLM by +11.67 BLEU-4. It also beats the gloss-based TS-SLT (Pose+RGB) by +1.63 BLEU-4 and +2.23 ROUGE-L, and matches or exceeds CV-SLT (RGB) on ROUGE-L (+0.69) while trailing on BLEU-4 (−1.52). On How2Sign, Geo-Sign (Token) outperforms all pose-based methods (YouTube-ASL: 12.4 BLEU-4; MSLU: 2.4 BLEU-4) but falls slightly short of the best RGB method SSVP-SLT (15.5 vs. 15.1 BLEU-4). On WLASL2000, it improves over Uni-Sign (Pose) by +0.51 per-instance and +0.99 per-class Top-1 accuracy, and over Uni-Sign (Pose+RGB) by +0.12 and +0.57 respectively, while remaining competitive with multimodal methods like NLA-SLR and Sign-Rep.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Uni-Sign architecture [41] (ST-GCN + mT5) and adapts the InfoNCE contrastive loss [53] to operate in hyperbolic space, but its key conceptual departure is from the dominant SLT trend of scaling up RGB visual encoders. Rather than treating hyperbolic geometry as a primary representation space (as in Poincaré embeddings [51] or hyperbolic GNNs [44]), Geo-Sign uses it as a regularisation mechanism that guides a pre-trained language model to internalise kinematic hierarchy. The weighted Fréchet mean aggregation and Möbius-transformed attention mechanism draw on Riemannian optimisation tools [3, 18] but apply them in a novel part-to-token alignment context specific to sign language.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a pose-only method with geometric regularisation can match or exceed RGB-based SLT systems while preserving signer privacy, and it is the first gloss-free approach to surpass gloss-based SOTA on ROUGE for CSL-Daily. However, the practical impact is tempered by a 292–513% latency increase from the hyperbolic operations, a single-seed evaluation, and modest absolute gains over the Uni-Sign pose baseline (+1.81 BLEU-4). Open questions include whether the learnable curvature provides genuine adaptive benefit over a well-tuned fixed value, whether the approach scales to longer sequences or larger models, and whether the hyperbolic regularisation signal remains useful as the language model itself grows in capacity.
