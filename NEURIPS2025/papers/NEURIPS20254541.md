## Problem

State-of-the-art SSL methods like DINOv2 are trained on clean, curated datasets, yet real-world applications in medical imaging, astrophysics, and geophysics involve inherently noisy data where clean references for supervised denoising are unavailable. The trivial denoiser-preprocessed pipeline requires the denoiser at every downstream step (inference and fine-tuning), adding latency, deployment complexity, and risk of transferring denoiser bias. This paper asks whether an off-the-shelf SSL backbone can be trained to ingest noisy inputs directly, enabling fully denoiser-free downstream use.

## Input / Output

The method takes raw RGB images corrupted with Gaussian, Shot (Poisson), or Speckle noise (added before any preprocessing like resizing, simulating fixed sensor noise) and produces noise-robust visual representations usable for linear-probing classification or non-parametric instance recognition via cosine similarity. A key design choice is that the self-supervised denoiser (Neighbor2Neighbor U-Net) is used only during pretraining to generate the denoised curriculum; at inference and downstream fine-tuning, the backbone directly consumes noisy images with no external denoising module, and the noise dataset is generated once and fixed rather than applied on-the-fly.

## Method

The approach combines a denoised-to-noisy curriculum with a denoised-teacher regularization. First, a self-supervised denoiser (Neighbor2Neighbor) is trained on the noisy dataset to produce denoised images. DINOv2 is then pretrained on denoised images for k epochs to establish a stable, low-entropy initialization, after which training is restarted (resetting LR and weight-decay schedules) on the original noisy images to adapt to real-world corruption. For extreme noise levels, the NCT variant freezes the denoised-trained teacher weights and adds a regularization loss (Eq. 6) that minimizes the DINO&iBOT cross-entropy between the student's noisy-image embeddings and the frozen teacher's denoised-image embeddings, using identical augmentations on both inputs to ensure alignment. This anchors the student to its cleaner initialization during noisy training, preventing representation drift. The denoiser is discarded after pretraining, so downstream fine-tuning and inference operate end-to-end on noisy inputs with only a task-specific prediction head.

## Results

On ImageNet-1k with ViT-B/16 under extreme Gaussian noise (σ=255, SNR=0.72 dB), DINOv2 w/ NCT achieves 55.8% linear probing accuracy, improving 4.8% over the DINOv2 baseline (51.0%) and approaching the denoiser-preprocessed N2N+DINOv2 pipeline (57.2%). At moderate noise (σ=100, SNR=4.36 dB), DINOv2 w/ NCT reaches 72.1% vs. 70.7% for DINOv2. On clean validation sets, DINOv2 w/ NCT actually surpasses N2N+DINOv2 (65.6% vs. 64.1% at σ=255), indicating superior representation quality. For instance recognition on Oxford/Paris, DINOv2 w/ NC matches or exceeds N2N+DINOv2 mAP across most noise settings, with NCT providing further gains at extreme noise (e.g., 20.66 vs. 18.96 mAP on Oxford at Shot λ=1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the DINOv2 baseline (trained directly on noisy data), both DINOv2 w/ NC and NCT deliver substantial gains: +2.5% and +4.8% at σ=255 on ImageNet-1k, and +12.1% and +18.8% at σ=255 on ImageNet-100 (200 epochs). Against the denoiser-preprocessed N2N+DINOv2 pipeline, DINOv2 w/ NC closely matches performance at moderate noise (70.9% vs. 73.1% at σ=100) and is within 1.4–3.4% at extreme noise, while on clean validation sets the proposed methods actually outperform N2N+DINOv2 (e.g., 65.6% vs. 64.1% at σ=255), suggesting the curriculum approach learns more generalizable representations. SimCLR, included as a reference on ImageNet-1k, is far weaker (34.2% at σ=255), confirming DINOv2's architectural advantage. When the noise curriculum is extended to other SSL models (SimCLR, MoCo v3, SimSiam, iBOT, DINO) on ImageNet-100, all benefit, with iBOT and DINO showing the largest gains (+5.8% and +4.2% respectively), indicating the approach is most effective for joint-embedding methods with DINOv2-like losses.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on DINOv2's joint-embedding SSL framework and Neighbor2Neighbor self-supervised denoising, combining them through a denoised-to-noisy curriculum learning schedule (following Bengio et al.'s entropy-based curriculum formulation) and a novel denoised-teacher regularization loss. It departs from the standard denoiser-preprocessed pipeline (N2N + DINOv2) by showing the denoiser can be discarded after pretraining, and from the closest prior work on denoiser-driven contrastive learning for noisy time series [48] by extending the idea to image SSL with a specific architectural constraint: identical augmentations applied to noisy and denoised inputs to maintain embedding alignment. The authors explicitly note that enabling off-the-shelf SSL backbones to ingest corrupted data directly, without external denoising at inference, has little-to-no prior work in the image domain.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The denoiser-free deployment removes a persistent inference-time dependency that adds latency, pipeline complexity, and risk of bias transfer in real-world applications (medical imaging, astrophysics, geophysics, finance) where clean references are unavailable. The finding that curriculum-trained models can match or even exceed the denoiser-preprocessed pipeline on clean validation sets suggests that noise-aware training produces more generalizable representations than explicit denoising, which has important implications for transfer learning from noisy pretraining data. Key limitations include reliance on synthetic noise (no real-world noisy benchmarks), the assumption that the SSL denoiser performs adequately, and the need for manual tuning of the curriculum restart point. Open questions include adaptive curriculum scheduling, extension to other modalities (audio, EEG, financial time series), and whether the approach generalizes to structured or domain-specific noise beyond the three types tested.
