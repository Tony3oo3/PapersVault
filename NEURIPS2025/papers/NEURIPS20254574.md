## Problem

Despite LLMs solving PhD-level reasoning over long contexts, they frequently fail at the seemingly trivial task of following explicit length instructions—terminating prematurely, over-generating, or refusing requests entirely. Existing long-text generation benchmarks assess output quality but largely overlook whether generations actually satisfy specified length constraints, leaving a critical gap in systematic evaluation of length instruction following across the full range of lengths (16 to 8,192 words).
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254574/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each instance pairs a task-specific prompt (open-ended QA, long-document summarization with 10K–15K word inputs, reasoning problems, or creative generation prompts) with an explicit length constraint expressed in one of three control methods (Equal To, At Most, At Least) at one of ten levels (16, 32, 64, 128, 256, 512, 1024, 2048, 4096, 8192). Length is measured in words for English and characters for Chinese. The evaluation produces two per-instance metrics: Length Deviation (a signed proportionate difference capturing direction and magnitude of error) and Length Score (a bounded 0–100 score via asymmetric exponential decay, with steeper penalty for under-generation k₁=5 than over-generation k₂=2), which are then aggregated across all 10,800 instances per model.

## Method

LIFEBENCH constructs 10,800 evaluation instances by cross-multiplying 360 fundamental data units (120 QA, 40 summarization, 80 reasoning, 120 creative generation; 50% English / 50% Chinese) with 3 control methods and 10 length constraints. The key methodological contribution is the two-metric analytical evaluation: Length Deviation captures signed deviation direction and magnitude, while Length Score maps LD through an asymmetric exponential function (100·e^(−k·|LD|)) that avoids positive/negative offsetting during aggregation and reduces outlier sensitivity, with deliberately slower decay for over-generation since excess text is deletable. The benchmark is the first to span the full length range (short <100, medium 100–2000, long >2000 words) in a bilingual setting with long-context inputs (>2,000 words), and introduces the Length Paradigm taxonomy to justify task selection. Diagnostic experiments include a length-awareness probe (models self-report word counts), input-length sensitivity analysis, lazy-strategy detection, and intermediate-reasoning trace analysis on thinking models.

## Results

Under the Equal To control method, 23 out of 26 evaluated LLMs score below 60 on Length Score (LS), with o3-mini achieving the highest LS of 75.4 (LD 13%), followed by Claude-3.7-Sonnet-Thinking (61.3) and Gemini-2.5-Pro (60.0). Performance improves substantially under looser constraints: 19 models exceed 90 LS under At Most, and 6 exceed 90 under At Least. Under long constraints (≥4096 words), no model consistently exceeds a score of 40, and almost all models fail to reach their vendor-claimed maximum output lengths when tested at 32,768 words.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Reasoning models clearly outperform non-reasoning counterparts: o3-mini (LS 75.4) surpasses GPT-4o (49.1) by 26.3 points and Claude-3.7-Sonnet (55.4) by 20 points under Equal To, while Claude-3.7-Sonnet-Thinking (61.3) beats its non-thinking variant (55.4) by 5.9 points. Among open-source models, Qwen3-32B (57.6) and Qwen3-32B-Thinking (57.8) lead, outperforming DeepSeek-V3 (54.3) and significantly exceeding Llama-3.1-8B (38.1) and Mistral-7B (26.7). Long-text enhanced models underperform expectations: LongWriter-Llama3.1-8B (31.9) scores lower than its base Llama-3.1-8B (38.1), and Suri-I-ORPO (27.4) shows an extreme LD of 506%, indicating severe over-generation. Notably, Gemini-2.0-Flash-Thinking (44.0) performs worse than its non-thinking counterpart Gemini-2.0-Flash (48.4), suggesting that reasoning does not universally help. Under At Most constraints, most models cluster above 90 LS, with Doubao-1.5-Pro (99.9) and o3-mini (99.5) at the top, while under At Least, o3-mini (97.0) and Gemini-2.5-Pro (95.5) lead, with a wider spread among the remaining models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of instruction-following evaluation and long-text generation benchmarks. Prior instruction-following benchmarks (IFEval, AlpacaEval-LI, Ruler) incorporated length constraints but were limited to short outputs and single control methods, while long-text generation benchmarks (LongBench-Write, HelloBench, LonGenBench) focused on generation quality rather than explicit length adherence. LIFEBENCH departs from both lines by being the first to systematically evaluate length instruction following across the full range (16–8192 words), with three control methods (Equal To, At Most, At Least), bilingual (English/Chinese) coverage, and dedicated analytical metrics (Length Deviation, Length Score) that capture both direction and magnitude of deviation. The 'Length Paradigms' taxonomy for categorizing NLP tasks by their inherent length characteristics is a new conceptual framing not present in prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that nearly all 26 evaluated models fail to reach their vendor-claimed maximum output lengths under realistic constraints is a significant practical concern for anyone deploying LLMs in production settings requiring long-form content. The identification of length awareness deficits (models cannot accurately self-report their own output length) as a core bottleneck points to a fundamental architectural limitation that current training paradigms do not address. The benchmark itself, with 10,800 bilingual instances across four task categories and ten length levels, is likely to become a standard evaluation tool for length control. Open questions include whether the length awareness deficit can be addressed through targeted training, whether the Chinese over-generation bias reflects tokenizer-level issues, and whether the lazy refusal strategies observed at long constraints can be mitigated without sacrificing safety alignment.
