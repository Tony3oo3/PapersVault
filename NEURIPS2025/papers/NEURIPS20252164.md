## Problem

Federated Graph Learning (FGL) and Graph Foundation Models (GFM) are complementary yet incompatible paradigms: FGL cannot handle cross-domain/cross-task heterogeneity, while GFM is confined to centralized single-machine training. Naively distributing GFM pre-training across federated clients introduces 'knowledge entanglement,' where multi-domain representations collapse into indistinguishable embeddings, severely limiting downstream generalization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252164/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes text-attributed graphs where node and edge text are uniformly encoded to 768-dimensional vectors via Sentence-BERT, along with graph adjacency matrices. During federated pre-training, each client's local graph is partitioned (via Louvain for subgraph-level or random allocation for graph-level decentralization) and processed through a gVQ-VAE encoder to produce discrete node embeddings from a shared codebook. Downstream, the frozen GFM encoder plus a task-specific head outputs node classification labels, link prediction scores, or graph classification labels, evaluated across 8 datasets spanning citation, hyperlink, knowledge graph, and molecular domains.

## Method

FedGFM+ builds on a gVQ-VAE backbone (GFT) trained via FedAvg with self-supervised graph reconstruction (feature + topology losses with straight-through estimator). Two modules mitigate knowledge entanglement: (1) AncDAI initializes the global codebook by having each client compute a domain prototype via mean-pooling of GNN-encoded node embeddings, then generating Gaussian-perturbed synthetic embeddings around each prototype as codebook entries—proven theoretically to preserve inter-domain separability; (2) AdaDPP trains lightweight attention-weighted prompt tokens locally at each client during pre-training (excluded from federated aggregation), which are assembled into an adaptive prompt pool during fine-tuning to selectively augment target graph node features with domain-specific semantic priors. The key insight is a dual-pronged strategy: global initialization provides structural inductive bias for disentanglement, while local prompts preserve domain-specific knowledge that would otherwise be lost in aggregation.

## Results

FedGFM+ achieves state-of-the-art performance across all 8 benchmarks spanning node classification (Cora, PubMed, OGB-Arxiv, WikiCS), edge/link prediction (FB15K-237, WN18RR), and graph classification (HIV, PCBA). Headline results include 83.79% on Cora, 88.52% on PubMed, 76.31% on OGB-Arxiv, 80.70% on WikiCS, 75.25% on FB15K-237, 89.25% on WN18RR, 69.39% AUC on HIV, and 77.68% AUC on PCBA. The paper reports improvements of at least 2.70% over the best baselines in node classification, 2.18% in edge classification, and 3.09% in graph classification. Ablation confirms AncDAI contributes more to performance than AdaDPP, with removing AncDAI causing larger drops (e.g., 2.24% on Cora vs. 0.62% for AdaDPP).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedGFM+ consistently outperforms all 20 baselines across all 8 datasets. Against isolated supervised models, it surpasses the best (GIN at 68.83 on PCBA, GAT at 85.52 on WN18RR) by margins of 3.09–8.95 points, confirming strong cross-domain and cross-task generalization. Against FGL methods, FedGFM+ exceeds the best subgraph-level method (FedGTA at 87.10 on PubMed) by 1.42 points and the best graph-level method (FedStar at 71.95 on PCBA) by 5.73 points, while also being the only method applicable across all three task granularities simultaneously. Against federated variants of centralized GFMs, FedGFM+ leads by 2.24 points on OGB-Arxiv (vs. GQT* at 74.07), 2.81 points on WN18RR (vs. UniGraph* at 86.44), and 4.46 points on PCBA (vs. GQT* at 73.22). Notably, several naive federated GFM baselines (e.g., OFA* at 80.04 on Cora) underperform even isolated supervised models, demonstrating the negative transfer caused by knowledge entanglement that FedGFM+ resolves.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established but previously separate lines of work: federated graph learning (FGL), which handles cross-silo graph collaboration but is confined to homogeneous tasks and single-domain data, and centralized graph foundation models (GFM), which achieve multi-domain generalization but require all data on a single machine. It builds directly on the gVQ-VAE architecture from GFT [43] as its backbone and on FedAvg [32] for parameter aggregation, but departs from both by identifying 'knowledge entanglement'—a representation collapse unique to federated GFM pre-training—and proposing two targeted remedies (AncDAI for global codebook initialization and AdaDPP for local prompt-based domain conditioning) that have no direct analogue in either FGL or centralized GFM literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By establishing FedGFM as a concrete research paradigm and diagnosing knowledge entanglement with clear empirical evidence (near-unity inter-domain cosine similarity under naive federated GFT), the paper provides a useful diagnostic framework for anyone attempting distributed GFM training. The dual-pronged solution is practical and lightweight, but the evaluation is limited to 3 clients per dataset and a single gVQ-VAE backbone, leaving open whether the findings generalize to larger client pools, alternative GFM architectures (e.g., transformer-based), or truly non-IID topological distributions. The authors also acknowledge that exchanging prototypes and prompts may leak partial semantic information, and a formal privacy analysis remains future work.
