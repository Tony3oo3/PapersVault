## Problem

Existing multi-scale VLM-based MIL methods for few-shot WSI classification process visual and textual features independently at each scale and fuse them only at the output, failing to model hierarchical semantic progression within each modality across scales (e.g., from coarse tissue patterns to fine cellular morphology) and lacking structured cross-modal alignment on the same scale. This limits their ability to leverage the intrinsic multi-scale, multi-modal structure of gigapixel pathology images under limited supervision.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252397/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes WSI patches at two magnifications (5× low-scale and 20× high-scale, where each 5× patch maps to a fixed 4×4 grid of 16 high-scale patches) encoded by a frozen pathology VLM image encoder, alongside GPT-4o-generated hierarchical text prompts (4 coarse-level morphological descriptions per class, each with 3 fine-level sub-features) augmented with 16 learnable context tokens. Output is a slide-level class label obtained by summing low- and high-scale logits, where each scale's logit is computed via TopKAvg aggregation over the image-text similarity matrix for class-specific text prompts, scaled by the VLM's logit temperature.

## Method

HiVE-MIL constructs a Hierarchical Heterogeneous Graph (HHG) with four node types (low/high-scale image and text) and two edge families: hierarchical parent-child edges within each modality across scales, and intra-scale heterogeneous edges between visual and textual nodes. A two-stage Text-Guided Dynamic Filtering (TGDF) module uses text-wise soft thresholding (mean + α·std) to remove semantically irrelevant patch-text pairs before constructing intra-scale edges, ensuring only meaningful cross-modal connections are formed. Message passing is performed by a relation-specific GraphSAGE operator for intra-scale edges and a Modality-Scale Attention (MSA) mechanism for hierarchical edges, where MSA encodes both scale direction and modality type in edge-specific query/key/value projections. A Hierarchical Text Contrastive Loss (HTCL) with class-wise positive/negative pairs enforces semantic consistency between parent (5×) and child (20×) text embeddings, and the total training objective combines standard cross-entropy with HTCL (λ=0.5).

## Results

Under the 16-shot setting, HiVE-MIL consistently achieves the best performance across all three TCGA datasets (NSCLC, BRCA, RCC) and all three pathology VLMs (PLIP, QuiltNet, CONCH). With CONCH (the strongest VLM), HiVE-MIL reaches 90.39% ACC / 96.49% AUC / 90.37% Macro F1 on NSCLC, 87.29% / 93.86% / 87.24% on BRCA, and 92.34% / 98.53% / 91.32% on RCC. The largest gains over the second-best baseline are +4.37% ACC and +4.14% Macro F1 on BRCA with QuiltNet, and +3.27% ACC on NSCLC with PLIP. In 4-shot and 8-shot settings, gains expand further, reaching up to +8.57% ACC on NSCLC with QuiltNet at 8-shot.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HiVE-MIL outperforms all 12 baselines across every dataset–VLM combination. Among traditional MIL methods, DTFD-MIL is the strongest (88.40% ACC on NSCLC with CONCH), but HiVE-MIL still leads by +1.99% ACC and +2.00% Macro F1; CLAM-SB and TransMIL trail further behind at 85.83–85.90% ACC. Among VLM-based MIL methods, MSCPT (multi-scale prompt tuning) is the closest competitor on NSCLC with CONCH (80.06% ACC), yet HiVE-MIL surpasses it by +10.33% ACC, indicating that explicit hierarchical graph modeling provides a substantial advantage over prompt-only multi-scale approaches. FOCUS (single-scale VLM-MIL) achieves 85.32% ACC on NSCLC with CONCH, still 5.07% below HiVE-MIL. On BRCA with QuiltNet, the gap widens most dramatically: HiVE-MIL reaches 77.08% ACC versus MSCPT's 72.08% (+5.00%) and FOCUS's 68.75% (+8.33%), confirming that hierarchical cross-scale and intra-scale alignment is particularly beneficial for morphologically diverse breast cancer subtypes. On RCC, where baselines already perform well (CLAM-SB reaches 92.11% ACC), HiVE-MIL's margin narrows to +0.23% ACC, suggesting diminishing returns when the task is already relatively easy.

## Relation to Prior Work

HiVE-MIL extends the multi-scale VLM-MIL paradigm established by ViLa-MIL [48] and MSCPT [22], which introduced scale-specific text prompts for WSI classification, by replacing their independent per-scale processing with an explicit parent-child hierarchical graph. It also builds on the learnable prompt token mechanism from CoOp [59] for textual feature extraction. The key departure is the construction of a unified hierarchical heterogeneous graph (HHG) that simultaneously models cross-scale parent-child links within each modality (via Modality-Scale Attention) and intra-scale cross-modal edges (via GraphSAGE), coupled with a Text-Guided Dynamic Filtering module and a Hierarchical Text Contrastive Loss—none of which appear in prior multi-scale VLM-MIL methods that rely on simple summation or averaging at the output stage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Few-shot WSI classification is clinically critical because labeled pathology data is scarce due to privacy constraints and disease rarity, and the consistent 2–4% gains across three TCGA datasets and three pathology VLMs (PLIP, QuiltNet, CONCH) demonstrate that structured hierarchical modeling of multi-scale, multi-modal information is a meaningful lever for data-efficient pathology AI. However, the TGDF module relies on non-learnable, per-WSI similarity thresholds that may limit generalization across datasets or backbones, and the Hit Ratio evaluation metric assumes the correctness of LLM-generated parent-child text hierarchies without expert validation. Open questions include whether adaptive, learnable filtering mechanisms can improve transferability, and whether the hierarchical graph paradigm extends beyond the fixed 5×/20× two-scale setting to more granular or task-specific scale configurations.
