## Problem

Existing incomplete multi-view deep clustering methods encode available observations into view-specific latent representations, ignoring that a data sample's latent structure should be unique and invariant across all views. Additionally, they fail to exploit the pair-wise similarity information among missing data observations, limiting the quality of learned representations and downstream clustering performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252981/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes incomplete multi-view data (N samples across V views with heterogeneous feature dimensions D_v), binary availability indicators specifying which views are present per sample, and a pre-specified cluster number K. It outputs cluster labels (via k-means on learned latent representations), the shared latent representations themselves, and imputed values filling in all missing view observations. The view-specific projection networks are fully-connected architectures whose hidden layer widths are tuned per dataset to match each view's feature dimensionality.

## Method

IMDC-DIA inverts the standard encoder-decoder paradigm: rather than encoding each view's data into separate latent codes, it assumes a single shared latent representation z_i per sample and projects it into each view's feature space via V independent neural networks, simultaneously reconstructing available observations and imputing missing ones. A novel linear alignment measurement—defined as the squared Frobenius norm of Z^T X̄ normalized by the product of individual Frobenius norms, with O(N) computational complexity—is proven (Theorem 1) to measure consistency of pair-wise linear similarities between the latent space and completed data space. The overall loss combines a reconstruction term (MSE on available views) with a weighted alignment term across views, where view weights are analytically optimized via the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality rather than treated as hyperparameters. Three variable groups (network parameters, latent representations, view weights) are updated alternately with the Adam optimizer, and k-means is applied to the converged latent representations for final clustering.

## Results

IMDC-DIA achieves state-of-the-art clustering performance across four benchmark datasets at five missing ratios (0.1–0.9). On average across all missing ratios, it reaches 92.09% ACC and 91.17% NMI on HandWritten, 84.15% ACC on Caltech5V, 44.68% ACC on Flower17, and 87.52% ACC on MSRCV1. The method outperforms the strongest baseline (CPSPAN) by 2.14–3.74% in ACC and 7.49–13.17% in NMI on average across the four datasets, with the largest gains observed at higher missing ratios (e.g., +15.34% ACC on Flower17 at 0.1 missing ratio, +15.41% NMI on MSRCV1 at 0.1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

IMDC-DIA outperforms all five baselines on nearly every dataset and missing-ratio setting. Against the strongest competitor CPSPAN [43], it gains 2.14% ACC on HandWritten, 4.67% on Caltech5V, 8.66% on Flower17, and 3.74% on MSRCV1 (averaged over missing ratios); the only exceptions are slight ACC drops of 2.59% and 1.60% on Caltech5V at 0.1 and 0.3 missing ratios. DVIMVC [30] is the second-strongest baseline on Caltech5V (80.92% ACC) but degrades sharply on HandWritten (51.81%) and Flower17 (27.97%), where IMDC-DIA leads by 40+ and 17+ percentage points respectively. DCP [34] (dual contrastive prediction) performs poorly at high missing ratios, dropping to 13.07% ACC on HandWritten and 16.98% on Caltech5V at 0.9 missing, while IMDC-DIA maintains 87.93% and 81.29%. DITA-IMVC [42] and DSIMVC [41] are consistently outperformed by 10–30+ percentage points in ACC across all datasets, with the gap widening as the missing ratio increases. Overall, IMDC-DIA's advantage is most pronounced on Flower17 (a harder, 17-class problem) and at higher missing ratios, confirming that the data imputation and linear alignment components are particularly effective when substantial data is unavailable.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant encoder-based paradigm in incomplete multi-view deep clustering (e.g., DITA-IMVC, DSIMVC, DCP, DVIMVC, CPSPAN), where each view's available observations are encoded into view-specific latent representations that are then integrated. Instead, IMDC-DIA inverts the architecture by assuming a single shared latent representation per sample and projecting it into each view's feature space via independent neural networks, making data imputation a natural byproduct of reconstruction. The linear alignment measurement, defined via Frobenius norms with O(N) complexity, is a novel regularization that explicitly leverages pairwise similarities of imputed (missing) observations, going beyond the contrastive or mutual-information losses used in prior work. The closed-form weight optimization via Cauchy-Schwarz provides a clean, parameter-free balancing mechanism across views.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The shared-latent-representation assumption offers a principled and architecturally simple alternative to view-specific encoders, and the linear alignment loss provides a scalable way to exploit missing-data similarity structure that prior methods overlook. However, all experiments are conducted on small-scale benchmarks (210–2,000 samples) with fully-connected networks, leaving open whether the approach scales to larger, higher-dimensional datasets or benefits from richer network architectures (CNN, Transformer). The authors themselves note the lack of additional constraints on latent representations as a limitation, and the quality of the imputed data is not directly evaluated, which is an important open question for applications where imputation fidelity matters beyond clustering accuracy.
