## Problem

Existing dynamic view synthesis methods from monocular videos require weight updates, auxiliary architectural modules, or extensive synthetic pre-training, and they suffer when the inversion process misaligns with the model's forward noise schedule. This paper identifies a fundamental theoretical obstacle—zero-terminal SNR schedules break the injectivity of the forward diffusion map, making deterministic inversion impossible—and asks whether high-fidelity 6-DoF view synthesis can be achieved purely by structured manipulation of the initial noise fed into a pre-trained video diffusion model, with no parameter updates or added layers.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single monocular RGB video and a target 6-DoF camera trajectory as input. Depth maps are estimated per-frame via DepthCrafter, lifted into dynamic 3D point clouds using camera intrinsics, and re-rendered under the target poses to produce warped novel-view frames with visibility masks. The output is a novel-view video at 480×720 resolution generated by CogVideoX-5B-I2V with 50 denoising steps (strength 0.95, CFG 6.0), where the entire camera-motion effect is encoded in how the initial noise tensor is constructed rather than in any conditioning signal to the network.

## Method

The core contribution is a training-free noise-initialization framework built on two mechanisms. First, K-order Recursive Noise Representation (K-RNR) resolves the Zero-Terminal SNR Collapse Problem by recursively refining the DDIM-inverted latent toward the VAE-encoded pivot latent via the closed-form relation ε^(k) = (Σᵢ √ᾱₜ(√(1−ᾱₜ))^(i−1)) x₀ + (√(1−ᾱₜ))^k ε^inv, progressively injecting scene structure into the noise while an AdaIN step at an intermediate recursion depth δ prevents the mean/variance explosion that causes oversaturated outputs. Second, Stochastic Latent Modulation (SLM) completes newly visible occluded regions by computing a visibility-aware sampling mask S=(1−M)·D and stochastically permuting latent values from visible background positions into occluded positions in both the content latent x₀ and the inverted latent ε^inv, providing contextually grounded priors for the diffusion model to synthesize plausible unseen content. The full pipeline—depth estimation, point-cloud rendering, DDIM inversion, K-RNR, and SLM—operates entirely before the first denoising step, leaving the pre-trained CogVideoX backbone untouched.

## Results

On 1000 samples from OpenVid-1M, the method achieves FID 53.15, FVD 103.44, and CLIP-T 35.37 for visual quality, outperforming all five baselines. For camera accuracy, it attains the lowest rotation error (1.31°) and translation error (4.33), and for view synchronization it reaches 881.43K matched pixels, FVD-V 75.17, and CLIP-V 92.04. On the curated full-reference benchmark (OpenVid-1M + DAVIS + Sora-generated), it achieves mean PSNR 14.50, SSIM 0.531, and LPIPS 0.487, again leading all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all five baselines on every reported metric. Against the strongest baseline ReCamMaster, it improves FID by 4.97 (53.15 vs. 58.12), FVD by 15.38 (103.44 vs. 118.82), rotation error by 0.15° (1.31 vs. 1.46), and CLIP-V by 2.13 points (92.04 vs. 89.91). The gap widens substantially against attention-sharing methods: versus TrajectoryCrafter, FID drops by 9.62 and FVD by 59.23; versus GCD, FID drops by 35.97 and FVD by 379.29. On full-reference metrics (Table 2), the method leads ReCamMaster by 0.73 dB PSNR (14.50 vs. 13.77) and 0.021 SSIM, and leads TrajectoryCrafter by 1.89 dB PSNR. The only metric where the margin is minimal is CLIP-F (98.54 vs. ReCamMaster's 98.89), where ReCamMaster edges ahead by 0.35 points, likely reflecting its stronger text-alignment conditioning via the Wan backbone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs fundamentally from the two dominant paradigms for diffusion-based dynamic view synthesis: attention-sharing architectures (GCD, TrajectoryCrafter, ReCamMaster) that require retraining on synthetic multi-view data, and LoRA-based fine-tuning (ReCapture, Reangle-A-Video) that performs per-video optimization. Instead, it reframes DVS as an inverse problem solvable entirely within the noise initialization phase of a pre-trained video diffusion model (CogVideoX), building on DDIM inversion and the zero-terminal SNR noise schedule. The key conceptual contribution is identifying the Zero-Terminal SNR Collapse Problem as a fundamental obstruction to deterministic inversion and resolving it with the K-order Recursive Noise Representation, a closed-form recursive refinement of the initial noise that aligns DDIM-inverted latents with the model's forward schedule.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free, architecture-agnostic nature of the approach makes high-fidelity 6-DoF camera control accessible without the computational cost of per-scene optimization or the domain-gap issues of synthetic pre-training, which is practically significant for film post-production and robotics applications. The theoretical identification of the Zero-Terminal SNR Collapse Problem has broader implications for any diffusion model using such schedules, as it formally proves that deterministic inversion is impossible under these conditions. However, the method is currently tied to CogVideoX's specific architecture and noise schedule, and Stochastic Latent Modulation can produce unstable results when large regions become newly visible, leaving open the question of whether the noise-space manipulation paradigm generalizes to other video diffusion backbones or scales to more extreme camera motions.
