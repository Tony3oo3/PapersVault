## Problem

Most indoor 3D occupancy prediction methods rely on dense voxel grids and convolution-heavy backbones, incurring high computational cost when applying coarse-to-fine autoregressive refinement. While Gaussian-based representations offer a lightweight alternative, the autoregressive paradigm—where finer-scale predictions are conditioned on coarser ones—remains largely unexplored in the Gaussian parameter space, leaving a gap between hierarchical modeling and computationally efficient 3D spatial frameworks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255391/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single monocular RGB image as input, augmented with per-scale depth maps from a pretrained Depth Anything V2 network for geometric alignment during Gaussian initialization. It produces a hierarchy of sparse 3D Gaussian parameter sets (position, anisotropic scale, rotation quaternion, opacity, and per-class semantic logits) at S resolution levels, which are then projected via 3D Gaussian splatting onto voxel grids to yield multi-resolution 3D semantic occupancy volumes within a frustum-aligned local coordinate frame.

## Method

DFGauss performs multi-scale autoregressive refinement directly in the continuous 3D Gaussian parameter space rather than on dense voxel feature maps. A multi-scale Gaussian encoder initializes sparse Gaussian primitives at each resolution from image features and depth cues; a hierarchical refinement module then applies cross-attention residual updates so that each finer-scale Gaussian set is conditioned on all coarser-scale outputs (Eq. 2–5), with quaternion rotations updated via normalized quaternion multiplication. To reduce computation, a dynamic focused masking mechanism scores coarse-scale Gaussians with a lightweight MLP, retains only the top-ρ% most informative anchors (via a differentiable soft-top-k during training and discrete top-k at inference), and uses them as keys in a cross-scale attention that selectively refines fine-scale parameters. Training is supervised end-to-end with multi-resolution 3D occupancy labels using a combined loss of Focal, Lovász-Softmax, Semantic-Scale, and Geometric-Scale terms.

## Results

On the Occ-ScanNet benchmark, DFGauss achieves an IoU of 55.28 and mIoU of 47.03 in the single-view local prediction setting, surpassing the Gaussian-based EmbodiedOcc baseline (53.95 IoU, 45.48 mIoU) by 1.33% and 1.55% respectively. In the continuous-view global prediction setting on EmbodiedOcc-ScanNet, DFGauss reaches 53.80 IoU and 43.89 mIoU, outperforming EmbodiedOcc (51.52 IoU, 42.53 mIoU) by 2.28% and 1.36%. On the smaller Occ-ScanNet-mini variant, DFGauss attains 54.28 IoU and 47.08 mIoU, again leading all baselines. The hierarchy depth ablation shows that 3-level refinement (232 ms latency) yields the best trade-off between accuracy and speed.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DFGauss outperforms all three baselines across every evaluated dataset and metric. Against MonoScene [3] (voxel-based, CVPR 2022), DFGauss gains approximately 13.7% IoU and 22.4% mIoU on Occ-ScanNet, reflecting the substantial advantage of Gaussian-based sparse representations over dense voxel grids. Against ISO [38] (monocular occupancy, ECCV 2024), the improvement is even larger at ~13.1% IoU and ~18.3% mIoU. The closest competitor is EmbodiedOcc [34] (Gaussian-based, ICCV 2025), which DFGauss surpasses by 1.33% IoU / 1.55% mIoU on Occ-ScanNet, 0.48% IoU / 0.68% mIoU on Occ-ScanNet-mini, and 2.28% IoU / 1.36% mIoU on EmbodiedOcc-ScanNet (global setting). The gains are most pronounced on fine-grained object classes (e.g., bed: 56.73 vs. 55.20; sofa: 63.81 vs. 61.90), suggesting the multi-scale autoregressive refinement particularly benefits detailed geometric structures.

## Relation to Prior Work

DFGauss sits at the intersection of two recently active threads: (1) Gaussian-based 3D occupancy prediction (GaussianFormer, EmbodiedOcc), which replaces dense voxel grids with sparse anisotropic Gaussian primitives, and (2) multi-scale autoregressive modeling (VAR), which conditions finer-scale predictions on coarser ones via next-scale prediction. The key departure is that prior multi-scale occupancy methods (SurroundOcc, NOMAE, OctreeOcc) perform hierarchical refinement in dense voxel or octree feature spaces, while prior Gaussian occupancy methods operate at a single scale. DFGauss transplants the VAR-style autoregressive conditioning directly into the continuous Gaussian parameter space, using cross-attention residual updates and a percentile-based importance mask to selectively propagate informative Gaussians across scales—a combination not previously explored.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that autoregressive coarse-to-fine refinement is viable in a sparse, continuous representation (Gaussian parameters) rather than requiring dense volumetric features, which could make hierarchical occupancy prediction more memory-efficient for embodied agents operating under tight compute budgets. However, the performance gains over the strongest Gaussian baseline (EmbodiedOcc) are modest (~1.3–1.5% mIoU), and the evaluation is limited to monocular indoor scenes on ScanNet-derived datasets. Open questions include whether the approach extends to multi-view or outdoor settings, whether the dynamic mask generalizes beyond the 60% ratio found optimal here, and whether the autoregressive structure provides meaningful benefits over simpler multi-scale fusion at larger scene scales.
