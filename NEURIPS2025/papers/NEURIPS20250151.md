## Problem

Discrimative models for biochemical interaction prediction (drug-target, drug-drug, enzyme-reaction) struggle in data-scarce and cold-start regimes where interacting entities are unseen during training. While generative models offer a promising alternative, unconstrained generation produces outputs that violate biochemical validity. The key unexploited structure is that valid interaction partners are drawn from a finite, known catalog of entities, yet no prior method leverages this closed-set constraint during both training and inference.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250151/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a query entity (protein FASTA sequence, molecular SMILES, or reaction SMILES) encoded by a frozen pre-trained encoder (ESM2-650M, MolFormer-XL, or RXNFP) into a fixed-size context vector. It autoregressively generates a unique discrete I-RVQ code sequence for the target entity, where the sequence length M is determined by the number of k-means layers needed to make all target codes distinct. The final output is a scalar interaction score derived either from the average log-probability over active (multi-branch) generation steps or from a lightweight 1-layer Transformer meta-model that ingests per-step generation statistics (token probability, rank, branching factor, entropy).

## Method

BioCG reformulates interaction prediction as constrained autoregressive sequence generation over a finite target catalog. Target entities are first embedded by a pre-trained encoder, then discretized via Iterative Residual Vector Quantization (I-RVQ), which sequentially applies k-means clustering to residual vectors until every target in the catalog possesses a unique code sequence. A prefix trie built over all valid target codes provides a binary mask at each generation step, zeroing out invalid next-token probabilities during both training and inference so that gradients flow only along legitimate biochemical pathways. An information-weighted cross-entropy loss assigns each step a weight of log(BF_k + 1), where BF_k is the number of valid continuations, thereby concentrating learning on high-branching decision points and ignoring trivial single-option steps. A frozen Transformer decoder conditioned on the query embedding performs the generation, and a separate 1-layer meta-Transformer calibrates the resulting per-step statistics into a final binary interaction score.

## Results

On the BioSNAP DTI benchmark, BioCG achieves 96.61% AUC on the seen split and a headline 89.31% AUC on the unseen-protein split, a 14.3 percentage-point gain over the previous SOTA (Top-DTI, 75.01%). On DrugBank DDI, BioCG reaches 84.12% AUC and 77.23% accuracy, surpassing the best baseline (MR-GNN, 80.91% AUC). On the CARE enzyme-reaction benchmark, BioCG attains 67.93% Top@1 accuracy, outperforming CREEP (60.32%) by 7.6 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BioCG outperforms all 15 baselines across the three tasks. On DTI (BioSNAP), the gap is most dramatic in the unseen-protein cold-start setting: BioCG's 89.31% AUC exceeds Top-DTI (75.01%) by 14.3 points, DLM-DTI (73.04%) by 16.3 points, and the weakest baselines (Random Forest, SVM) by over 20 points; on the seen split, BioCG leads Top-DTI by 2.7 points (96.61 vs. 93.91 AUC). On DDI (DrugBank), BioCG beats the strongest baseline DDI-GCN by 3.2 points in accuracy (77.23 vs. 74.05) and MR-GNN by 3.2 points in AUC (84.12 vs. 80.91), with all other DDI baselines (DeepDDI, SSI-DDI, LR, RF) trailing by 5–25 points. On enzyme-reaction prediction (CARE), BioCG's 67.93% Top@1 surpasses CREEP (60.32%) by 7.6 points and DRFP similarity (59.39%) by 8.5 points, while LLM-based (GPT-4, 13.74%) and contrastive (CLIPZyme, 12.27%) baselines are far behind. The one near-tie is the unseen-drug DTI split, where Top-DTI edges BioCG by 0.04 AUC points (91.25 vs. 91.21), though BioCG still leads on specificity (89.71 vs. 83.17).

## Relation to Prior Work

BioCG departs from the dominant discriminative paradigm for biochemical interaction prediction (embedding both entities and scoring compatibility) by reframing the task as constrained autoregressive sequence generation over a finite catalog of valid targets. It repurposes ideas from NLP constrained decoding (trie-guided masking), speech/image vector quantization (adapted as I-RVQ for uniqueness guarantees), and frozen pre-trained biological encoders (ESM, MolFormer, RXNFP), but the key conceptual move is applying explicit structural constraints during training rather than only at inference. The information-weighted loss is a novel training objective tailored to the variable branching structure of the trie, distinguishing it from standard cross-entropy or contrastive objectives used in prior interaction-prediction work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 14.3-point AUC gain on unseen proteins addresses a genuine bottleneck in drug discovery where experimental validation is expensive and cold-start generalization is poor. However, the framework is fundamentally limited to settings where one entity belongs to a known closed catalog, ruling out fully novel-novel pair prediction, and it produces only binary interaction scores rather than quantitative binding affinities. Open questions include scalability of the trie to very large target sets (millions of compounds), whether the I-RVQ discretization can be replaced by more principled tokenization, and whether the approach extends to multi-entity or quantitative interaction prediction.
