## Problem

Multimodal molecular models face two intertwined failures: 3D conformer-dependent fusion is unreliable because force-field-generated conformers (e.g., from RDKit) can differ substantially in local geometry even for identical molecules, and naive symmetric fusion of modalities causes modality collapse where noisy 3D signals dominate or distort the cleaner 2D/sequence information. No existing framework simultaneously stabilizes the structural prior against conformer noise and prevents cross-modal interference during fusion.

## Input / Output

MuMo consumes three modalities per molecule: a tokenized SMILES string, a 2D molecular graph (atom/bond connectivity), and a single 3D conformer (atomic coordinates from RDKit) from which bond lengths and inter-bond angles are extracted. The 2D and 3D modalities are fused into a rotationally invariant Unified Graph that undergoes independent message passing, while the SMILES stream is processed by a Mamba backbone. The final output is a single molecular embedding derived from the structurally enhanced sequence stream, used for downstream classification (AUROC) or regression (MAE/RMSE) across 29 benchmark tasks.

## Method

MuMo introduces two coupled mechanisms. First, the Structured Fusion Pipeline (SFP) builds a Unified Graph that encodes 2D topology and 3D geometry (bond lengths, angles) as edge and geometric-linkage features, performs two-step edge-then-node message passing, and applies geometry-aware substructure partitioning (extending BRICS rules to spatial graphs) to capture multiscale structural signals, yielding a stable, rotationally invariant structural prior. Second, the Progressive Injection (PI) mechanism asymmetrically injects this prior into the SMILES sequence stream only at later layers (9–16) via Injection Enhanced Attention, which performs bidirectional cross-attention between sequence and structure tokens and adds a pooled structural prior to a global anchor token, allowing the sequence to establish its own semantics before receiving structural guidance. The structural prior evolves independently across layers through a modified Mamba state-space recurrence that maintains a latent state updated by both sequence and structural inputs, enabling progressive abstraction without disrupting local token interactions. The model is pretrained on ChEMBL-1.6M via masked language modeling and fine-tuned per task.

## Results

Across 29 benchmark tasks from TDC, MoleculeNet, and Reaxtica, MuMo achieves an average improvement of 2.7% over the best-performing baseline per task, ranking first on 22 of 29 tasks. The most striking gain is a 27% MAE reduction on the LD50 regression task (0.426 vs. 0.589 for DeepMol), and on QM9 MuMo (505M parameters) outperforms the much larger Uni-Mol2 (1.1B) on 5 of 6 properties (e.g., HOMO/LUMO/GAP: 0.00301 vs. 0.00351 MAE; α: 0.283 vs. 0.305). On the broader QM7/8/9 suite, MuMo wins 7 of 10 sub-tasks, and on Reaxtica reaction benchmarks it achieves the best R²/MAE on 3 of 4 tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MuMo outperforms all 12 baselines on the majority of tasks. Against 3D-aware models, MuMo beats Uni-Mol on 5 of 6 QM9 properties (e.g., HOMO/LUMO/GAP 0.00301 vs. 0.00351 MAE) and on MoleculeNet classification (BBBP-R 0.962 vs. 0.889 AUROC), while also surpassing the much larger Uni-Mol2-1.1B on 5/6 QM9 tasks despite having only 505M parameters. Among 2D graph baselines, FPGNN is the closest competitor (competitive on CYP-Avg at 0.866 vs. 0.880) but MuMo leads on Pgp (0.942 vs. 0.930), LD50 (0.426 vs. 0.638 MAE), and PPBR (7.324 vs. 8.465). Sequence-based pretrained models (MolBERT, MoLFormer, ChemBERTa-2, Grover, TranFoxMol) are consistently outperformed on MoleculeNet tasks—MuMo achieves 0.985 AUROC on CLINTOX vs. 0.933 (ChemBERTa-2) and 0.888 (MoLFormer)—though MolBERT edges out MuMo slightly on BACE-R (0.882 vs. 0.878) and ChemBERTa-2 leads on SIDER (0.708 vs. 0.677). On the Reaxtica reaction benchmarks, MuMo surpasses Reaxtica, MFF, and RXNFP on BHC (R² 0.952 vs. 0.95) and HTE (R² 0.873 vs. 0.87), tying on CPA (MAE 0.144). The 27% MAE improvement on LD50 over DeepMol (0.426 vs. 0.589) is the single largest margin observed across all benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

MuMo sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) sequence-based molecular language models (MolBERT, MoLFormer, ChemBERTa) that capture SMILES semantics but lose geometric detail; (2) 3D-aware GNNs (SchNet, DimeNet, Uni-Mol) that encode spatial coordinates but are fragile to conformer noise; and (3) multimodal fusion models (GraphMVP, MolCLR) that combine modalities via symmetric early fusion. The paper departs from prior multimodal approaches by replacing symmetric concatenation or token-level attention with an asymmetric, delayed Progressive Injection mechanism, and by unifying 2D topology and 3D geometry into a rotationally-invariant structural prior before any cross-modal interaction. Architecturally, it repurposes Mamba's selective state-space dynamics as the sequence backbone and extends BRICS retrosynthetic partitioning to spatial graphs for multiscale substructure encoding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The conformer-robustness story is practically important: drug discovery pipelines routinely generate 3D structures with RDKit or force fields, and the paper demonstrates that MuMo's unified geometric representation (lengths + angles, no explicit torsions) maintains performance under conformer perturbation where DimeNet-based embeddings collapse. The 27% LD50 improvement and consistent top-1 rankings across 22/29 tasks suggest the progressive injection timing is a genuinely useful design principle for any multimodal molecular model. However, the model is currently task-specific (pretrained on ChEMBL-1.6M via MLM, then fine-tuned per task), and the authors acknowledge that extending it into a general-purpose multimodal backbone remains open. Whether the asymmetric injection strategy transfers to other domains (e.g., protein-ligand binding, reaction prediction at scale) is untested.
