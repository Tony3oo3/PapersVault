## Problem

VAE-based UHD image restoration faces a fundamental tension: the Gaussian variational constraint preserves global semantics but discards degradation-sensitive high-frequency details needed for faithful reconstruction, and directly co-optimizing the VAE with a downstream restoration network risks collapsing the learned latent structure. The paper tackles how to simultaneously achieve degradation-robust generalization, high-fidelity detail recovery, and a controllable fidelity-perception tradeoff within a single efficient framework.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251481/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts a single degraded RGB image at UHD (4K) or standard resolution with arbitrary unknown corruption (low light, blur, haze, noise, rain, snow, moiré, or composites) and outputs a restored image at the same resolution. It supports full-size 4K inference with only 3.6G FLOPs and 1.2M parameters for the restoration network, and exposes a scalar parameter α ∈ [0,1] at inference to continuously trade off between pixel-level fidelity (PSNR/SSIM) and perceptual quality (LPIPS) without retraining.

## Method

Latent Harmony is a two-stage framework. Stage 1 trains an LH-VAE whose encoder is regularized by progressive degradation perturbation (synthetic and real degradations of increasing severity), a DINOv2-based degradation-invariant semantic alignment loss, and a latent equivariance loss that enforces consistency between downsampled-latent decoding and downsampled-image decoding, yielding a latent space with balanced high-frequency proportion and cross-degradation robustness. Stage 2 first trains a lightweight latent-space restoration network (SFHformer) with the VAE frozen, then introduces two independent LoRA modules: a Fidelity-oriented HF-LoRA on the encoder optimized by a high-frequency L1 alignment loss to extract authentic detail, and a Perception-oriented HF-LoRA on the decoder optimized by a GAN-based high-frequency adversarial loss to synthesize natural textures. The two LoRA modules are trained via alternating optimization with selective gradient propagation to protect the pre-trained latent structure, and at inference the user blends their contributions through α to dial the fidelity-perception balance.

## Results

On the six-degradation UHD all-in-one benchmark, Latent Harmony achieves average PSNR of 27.14/27.21/22.32/34.17/31.41/33.24 dB (Low Light/Blur/Haze/Noise/Rain/Snow) and average LPIPS of 0.1822, outperforming all 11 competing methods. The method uses only 3.6G FLOPs and 1.2M parameters while supporting full-size 4K inference, compared to 10–367G FLOPs and 9–37M parameters for pixel-space baselines. On the four-degradation setting, it reaches 29.70 dB average PSNR and 0.2502 LPIPS, again leading all methods. In standard-resolution experiments (Table 3), integrating LH-VAE into PromptIR, Diff-Plugin, and CosAE consistently improves LPIPS and FID across haze, rain, snow, motion blur, raindrop, and low-light tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Latent Harmony outperforms all 13 compared methods across every degradation type and metric. The closest competitor is UHDprocesser [11], the only other VAE-based UHD method supporting full-size 4K inference; Latent Harmony exceeds it by 0.2–1.5 dB PSNR per degradation (e.g., 27.14 vs 26.91 on Low Light, 31.41 vs 29.90 on Rain) while using fewer FLOPs (3.6G vs 4G) and parameters (1.2M vs 1.6M). Among pixel-space all-in-one methods, Histoformer [50] and NDR-Restore [27] are the strongest on PSNR (25.73 and 25.12 dB on Low Light respectively), but both are 1.4–2.0 dB below Latent Harmony and require 91–196G FLOPs without 4K support. Diffusion-based DiffUIR-L [49] achieves competitive denoising PSNR (33.25 dB) but lags on other degradations (18.62 dB on Haze) and cannot run at full 4K resolution. On standard-resolution tasks (Table 3), plugging LH-VAE into PromptIR [9], Diff-Plugin [51], and CosAE [17] yields consistent LPIPS/FID improvements (e.g., CosAE on Haze: LPIPS 0.328→0.224, FID 148.78→128.12), confirming the VAE's transferability beyond UHD settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends VAE-based UHD restoration (DreamUHD, CD²-VAE) by arguing that standard Gaussian variational compression inherently sacrifices degradation-specific high-frequency attributes, and departs from all-in-one restoration methods (PromptIR, HAIR, UHD-processer) that rely on degradation-aware branches (MoE, prompting) in favor of a unified latent-space paradigm. Conceptually, it draws on latent space regularization techniques from the LDM community (REPA, REPA-E, VAVAE's DINOv2 alignment) and adapts the LoRA fine-tuning paradigm from LLMs to VAE encoder/decoder modules, but introduces a novel high-frequency-guided objective that decouples fidelity (encoder) from perception (decoder) via alternating optimization. The progressive degradation perturbation and latent equivariance constraints are new regularization strategies not previously combined in a single VAE training objective for restoration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a fundamental and underexplored tension in VAE-based restoration: the KL-regularized latent space that ensures generalization simultaneously discards the high-frequency information needed for faithful reconstruction of degradation-affected details. The 80× FLOPs reduction (3.6G vs. 301G for AIRNet) while achieving superior PSNR/SSIM/LPIPS across six degradation types makes full-resolution 4K inference practical on consumer hardware, which prior all-in-one methods could not achieve. The inference-time α parameter for fidelity-perception tradeoff is a practical contribution for deployment. Open questions include whether the two-stage training protocol (VAE pre-training followed by LoRA fine-tuning) can be simplified, how the approach extends to video restoration, and whether the GAN-based perceptual loss in PHF-LoRA introduces training instability at scale.
