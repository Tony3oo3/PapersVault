## Problem

Current fMRI-to-image reconstruction methods require subject-specific fine-tuning (often ~12 hours per subject) to generalize across individuals, making them impractical for clinical deployment and real-time brain-computer interfaces. No existing framework can decode visual experiences from a completely unseen subject's brain activity without any additional fMRI data or model retraining, leaving zero-shot cross-subject generalization largely unexplored.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single fMRI brain activity scan (averaged over 3 trials) and transforms it into a 256×256 single-channel 2D brain activation map, which is then encoded by a ViT-based fMRI-PTE encoder into a token-level latent representation. The output is a full reconstructed RGB image generated via a two-stage diffusion pipeline (SDXL unCLIP for coarse generation followed by SDXL image-to-image refinement guided by predicted captions). Evaluation is performed in a strict zero-shot protocol: the model is trained on 7 subjects from the Natural Scenes Dataset and tested on a held-out 8th subject with 982 shared test images, using no data from the test subject at all.

## Method

ZEBRA builds on an fMRI-PTE ViT encoder and a diffusion prior (unCLIP + Stable Diffusion) baseline, adding two disentanglement modules. The Subject-Invariant Feature Extraction (SIFE) module applies residual decomposition (E_i = F_i(E), E_s = E − E_i) followed by adversarial training: a subject discriminator is trained to identify the subject from E_i while a gradient reversal layer forces E_i to be subject-agnostic, and a subject classifier is trained on E_s to capture individual variability; a masked-decoder reconstruction anchor preserves neural information. The Semantic-Specific Feature Extraction (SSFE) module projects E_i into CLIP vision space to obtain semantic-specific features F_s aligned with OpenCLIP embeddings via BiMixCo contrastive loss, while projecting E_s through a gradient reversal layer to produce semantic-invariant features F_i whose adversarial loss pushes semantic content into F_s. The total training objective combines reconstruction, adversarial, classification, contrastive, and diffusion-prior losses, and at inference only the invariant projection path is used, enabling direct zero-shot decoding of unseen subjects without any fine-tuning.

## Results

On the Natural Scenes Dataset (NSD), ZEBRA achieves 0.131 PixCorr, 0.375 SSIM, 74.6% Alex(2), and 81.2% Alex(5) in the zero-shot setting, substantially outperforming the only other zero-shot baseline NeuroPictor* (0.057 PixCorr, 0.297 SSIM, 71.4% Alex(2), 74.7% Alex(5)). On high-level semantic metrics, ZEBRA reaches 72.2% Inception and 71.5% CLIP similarity, compared to 62.5% and 66.0% for NeuroPictor*. Notably, ZEBRA's SSIM of 0.375 matches that of fully fine-tuned NeuroPictor (0.375) despite using no test-subject data, and its Alex(5) of 81.2% approaches the 85.6% of fully fine-tuned Takagi et al.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ZEBRA decisively outperforms the only other zero-shot-compatible baseline, NeuroPictor*, across all eight metrics: +0.074 PixCorr (0.131 vs 0.057), +0.078 SSIM (0.375 vs 0.297), +6.5% Alex(5) (81.2% vs 74.7%), +9.7% Inception (72.2% vs 62.5%), and +5.5% CLIP (71.5% vs 66.0%). It also surpasses the authors' own strong zero-shot baseline (NeuroPictor + MindTuner combined) by similar margins. Against fully fine-tuned methods, ZEBRA narrows the gap considerably: its SSIM of 0.375 ties NeuroPictor (fully fine-tuned) exactly, and its Alex(2) of 74.6% is within 4.3% of Takagi et al. (78.9%), despite using zero test-subject data. However, ZEBRA still trails few-shot methods (1 hour of data) on most metrics, e.g., SSIM 0.375 vs 0.419–0.420 for MindEye2/MindTuner (1h), and Alex(5) 81.2% vs 90.6–93.6%, indicating that a small amount of subject-specific data still provides meaningful gains. The paper positions ZEBRA as setting a new state of the art in zero-shot neural decoding while acknowledging that semantic fidelity for rare object categories remains a limitation compared to few-shot approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

ZEBRA builds directly on the MindEye2/NeuroPictor pipeline (fMRI-PTE ViT encoder → CLIP-space projection → diffusion prior with Stable Diffusion XL) but departs from the universal two-stage paradigm (multi-subject pretraining followed by subject-specific fine-tuning) that characterizes MindEye2, MindTuner, and NeuroPictor. The key conceptual departure is the explicit disentanglement of fMRI features into subject-invariant and subject-specific components via residual decomposition and adversarial training (gradient reversal), a strategy borrowed from domain-adaptation literature but not previously applied to brain decoding. The paper positions itself as the first framework to eliminate the fine-tuning stage entirely, enabling true zero-shot inference on unseen subjects.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By removing the ~1-day subject-specific fine-tuning bottleneck, ZEBRA makes fMRI-to-image decoding practically viable for clinical brain-computer interfaces and neurorehabilitation, where rapid deployment on new patients is essential. However, semantic fidelity (especially for rare object categories) still lags behind few-shot and fully fine-tuned methods, and the evaluation is confined to 8 subjects in NSD, leaving open whether the approach scales to larger, more diverse populations. Future work on extending the framework to video and text decoding, and on closing the semantic accuracy gap, will determine whether zero-shot brain decoding becomes a broadly deployable tool.
