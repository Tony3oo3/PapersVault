## Problem

Conventional federated learning relies on exchanging and averaging model parameters, but this fails for diffusion models due to their large parameter sizes and the instability introduced by non-IID local data. Existing FL approaches for synthetic data generation (e.g., FedGAN, FedDiffuse) inherit these communication and convergence difficulties, leaving effective synthetic data generation in cross-silo FL settings largely unexplored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250095/cropped_page4_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

Each client independently trains a diffusion model on its private non-IID dataset (32×32 images, ~300 samples per client) and then only communicates noise predictions ε_θ(x_t, t) of dimension d per sample per round, rather than model parameters of dimension p. The server initializes N samples from N(0, I_d) (optionally with categorical labels for conditional generation) and iteratively refines them over T=1000 communication rounds, producing a synthetic dataset of N samples that can optionally carry a formal (ε, δ)-DP guarantee inherited from DP-SGD-trained local models.

## Method

DfD reframes federated synthetic data generation as cooperative sampling from a mixture of local distributions, each encoded by a pre-trained diffusion model, eliminating the need for parameter exchange. The key technical insight is a 'well-trained diffusion model' approximation: when a diffusion model accurately predicts added noise, its score simplifies to −λ/√(1−ᾱ_t)·ε_θ(x_t, t) and its unnormalized density to exp(−λ/2‖ε_θ(x_t,t)‖²), enabling energy-based composition without modifying the standard DDPM training objective. At each round, the server collects predictions from all K clients, converts them into local energies and scores, computes a global score as a softmax-weighted mixture (Eq. 9), and updates the synthetic dataset via the unadjusted Langevin algorithm. The paper derives the step size condition (η_t ∈ [0, 1/2], λ = 2) from non-expansiveness of the diffusion reverse process and provides a non-asymptotic KL-divergence convergence guarantee for the ULA iterates toward the target mixture distribution.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, DfD achieves the best FID of 59.98 (vs. 78.38 for FedDiffuse, 145.57 for FedGAN, and 330.85 for PRISM) and the highest CNN utility of 34.1%. On CelebA, DfD attains the best FID of 29.18 and the best CNN utility of 59.3%. On MNIST, DfD achieves the best Precision (0.622), Density (0.182), and Coverage (0.394), though FedGAN edges it on FID (34.85 vs. 37.74). Across all three datasets, DfD consistently delivers the highest classifier utility (LogReg, MLP, CNN) among all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DfD clearly dominates PRISM across all datasets and nearly all metrics: on CIFAR-10, DfD's FID of 59.98 is 5.5× lower than PRISM's 330.85, and its CNN utility (34.1%) is 2.6× higher than PRISM's (13.2%). Against FedDiffuse, DfD wins on FID for CIFAR-10 (59.98 vs. 78.38) and CelebA (29.18 vs. 33.33), and on utility across all three datasets (e.g., 34.1% vs. 33.0% CNN on CIFAR-10; 59.3% vs. 58.2% on CelebA), though FedDiffuse retains an edge on Recall for MNIST (0.761 vs. 0.344) and CelebA (0.518 vs. 0.414). Against FedGAN, DfD wins decisively on FID for CIFAR-10 (59.98 vs. 145.57) and CelebA (29.18 vs. 98.18) and on utility for all datasets, but FedGAN achieves a slightly lower FID on MNIST (34.85 vs. 37.74) and higher Precision on CIFAR-10 (0.687 vs. 0.515). Overall, DfD is the only method that simultaneously achieves competitive FID and the best utility on all three datasets, while exchanging only N×d-dimensional predictions rather than full model parameters (p-dimensional), yielding a communication cost reduction of roughly p/d per round.

## Relation to Prior Work

DfD builds on the energy-based interpretation of diffusion models established by Du et al. (2023), which showed that diffusion models implicitly define EBMs whose scores correspond to noise predictions. However, prior work required retraining diffusion models with a modified energy-based ℓ₂ objective to enable compositional sampling. DfD's key departure is showing that *standard* well-trained DDPMs (Ho et al., 2020) can be directly repurposed for mixture sampling via the ℓ₂ parameterization without any training modification, by exploiting the fact that well-trained models predict noise approximately as identity. The sampling mechanism is the unadjusted Langevin algorithm (Roberts & Tweedie, 1996), which the paper connects to the diffusion reverse process through a non-expansiveness argument, providing a theoretically grounded step-size choice where prior heuristic approaches simply set η_t = β_t.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The reframing of federated synthetic data generation as cooperative inference (sampling from a mixture of local distributions) rather than collaborative training (parameter averaging) sidesteps the dominant communication bottleneck in FL with diffusion models, reducing per-round communication from O(p) to O(N×d). The theoretical contributions—deriving the ULA step size (η_t ≤ 1/2, λ = 2) and a non-asymptotic KL convergence bound—fill a gap where prior work relied on unprincipled heuristics. Open questions include whether the approach scales to cross-device FL with unreliable clients, whether the server can retain a reusable generative model (e.g., via amortized samplers) rather than just a static dataset, and how to improve sample quality under strong DP budgets where the paper acknowledges noticeable degradation.
