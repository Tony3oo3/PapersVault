## Problem

Existing video deblurring methods operate on single-camera inputs or symmetric stereo pairs, overlooking the cross-lens redundancy inherent in smartphone asymmetric dual-camera systems. In such systems, the ultra-wide lens (shorter focal length, deeper depth of field) captures sharper motion details despite lower resolution, while the wide-angle lens (larger aperture, faster shutter) suffers more from motion blur during camera shake. This natural complementarity between the two views has not been exploited for video deblurring.

## Input / Output

The method takes a blurry wide-lens video and a simultaneously captured ultra-wide lens reference video, both of shape T×H×W×3. The ultra-wide frames are center-cropped and upscaled to match the wide-lens resolution, and no spatial or color pre-alignment is applied—only frame-level temporal synchronization is enforced. The output is a deblurred video at the wide-lens resolution. Training uses 7-frame clips at 128×128 with motion blur simulated by averaging 7 consecutive frames; real-world evaluation uses iPhone 14 Plus captures with physical cross-view shifts and photometric differences.

## Method

AsLeD-Net employs a dual-branch architecture built on BasicVSR: a base branch performs bidirectional recurrent feature propagation on blurry wide-lens frames, while a reference branch processes the sharper ultra-wide frames. Three task-specific modules in the reference branch transfer information to the base branch: (1) ALM computes cosine-similarity-based K-nearest-neighbor matching (K=3) between blurry and reference local features, fusing top-K reference tokens via softmax-weighted aggregation and a 1×1 convolution; (2) DC predicts the residual difference between blurry and reference features and refines it through cascaded residual blocks to bridge cross-view photometric and structural mismatches; (3) RMC estimates optical flow between the current blurry frame and a sharper reference frame using SpyNet followed by learned refinement, then warps features to achieve more reliable temporal alignment than blurry-to-blurry flow. The ALM, DC, and RMC outputs are concatenated with the base branch features, merged via residual blocks, and decoded with a 30-block decoder. The network is trained end-to-end with Charbonnier loss (ε=10⁻³) using Adam with cosine annealing over 300K iterations on an RTX 3090.

## Results

On the RealMCVSR test set, AsLeD-Net achieves 26.34 dB PSNR, 0.9167 SSIM, and 0.1614 LPIPS, outperforming all 10 baselines across every metric. The largest PSNR gain is 0.64 dB over the closest competitor DSTNet (25.70 dB) and 1.19 dB over EDVR (25.15 dB). In LPIPS, AsLeD-Net reduces the score by 0.0542 over the next-best RefVSR (0.2156), indicating substantially better perceptual quality. The model uses 8.8M parameters with an inference time of 248.7 ms on a 256×256 frame (GTX 1080 Ti), which is slower than lightweight methods like DBN (4.2 ms) and IFIRNN (7.6 ms) but competitive with EDVR (320.1 ms).

## Baselines & Comparisons

AsLeD-Net outperforms all 10 baselines on PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS simultaneously. Against the strongest video deblurring baseline DSTNet [52], it gains 0.64 dB PSNR (26.34 vs. 25.70) and 0.0033 SSIM, while reducing LPIPS by 0.099 (0.1614 vs. 0.2604). Compared to the reference-based video restoration method RefVSR [31], which is the closest in task formulation, AsLeD-Net improves PSNR by 0.76 dB and LPIPS by 0.0542, though RefVSR is slightly faster (268.3 ms vs. 248.7 ms is actually slower for RefVSR). Against the bidirectional propagation baselines BasicVSR++ [8] and BasicVSR [9], the gains are 0.74 dB and 1.24 dB in PSNR respectively, with LPIPS improvements of 0.0593 and 0.1271. Single-image methods (MIMOUNet [13], NAFNet [11], Restormer [86]) and earlier video deblurring methods (IFIRNN [49], DBN [65], EDVR [71]) trail by 1.4–2.1 dB in PSNR, confirming that cross-lens reference information and temporal propagation are both essential. The main trade-off is inference speed: at 248.7 ms, AsLeD-Net is slower than DSTNet (22.1 ms), BasicVSR++ (57.1 ms), and DBN (4.2 ms), limiting real-time deployment despite its superior quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

AsLeD-Net builds directly on the BasicVSR/BasicVSR++ bidirectional recurrent architecture for temporal feature propagation, repurposing it as the 'base branch' while introducing a parallel reference branch for ultra-wide lens inputs. Conceptually, it extends the dual-lens image restoration line (dual-lens super-resolution, dual-lens image deblurring by Mohan et al. and Lai et al.) from still images to video, and extends single-camera video deblurring (DSTNet, BasicVSR++) by injecting cross-lens structural priors via K-nearest-neighbor feature matching (analogous to C2-Matching in reference-based SR) and reference-guided optical flow (SpyNet). The key departure from prior video deblurring is the explicit modeling of asymmetric field-of-view and photometric discrepancies between lenses, rather than assuming geometrically aligned or symmetric inputs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to formalize asymmetric dual-lens video deblurring as a distinct task, with direct practical relevance to the billions of smartphones shipping wide + ultra-wide lens pairs. The ablation revealing that temporal alignment (RMC) dominates over spatial refinement (ALM, DC) suggests that cross-lens video restoration is bottlenecked by motion estimation under blur rather than by feature matching quality — a useful diagnostic for future designs. However, evaluation is limited to a single synthetic-blur dataset (RealMCVSR with 7-frame averaging) and one real-device test (iPhone 14 Plus), and the 248.7 ms inference time precludes real-time deployment. Open questions include generalization to other phone models, handling of more complex blur kernels (rotation, zoom), and whether the frame-averaging training proxy adequately captures real sensor-level motion blur.
