## Problem

Existing Large Audio-Language Models (LALMs) are trained primarily on short audio for recognition tasks rather than deliberate reasoning, lack exposure to the skill sets needed for complex multi-step inference, and remain largely closed-source when they support all three audio modalities (speech, sound, music). Key capabilities critical to AGI—expert-level reasoning, multi-turn multi-audio dialogue, long-context audio comprehension (beyond 30 seconds), and voice-to-voice interaction—remain unaddressed or only partially addressed by prior work.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251396/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

AF3 accepts audio clips (speech, sound, or music) up to 10 minutes long, resampled to 16 kHz mono and converted to 128-channel mel-spectrograms (25 ms window, 10 ms hop), processed in 30-second non-overlapping sliding windows. The model produces text answers, audio captions, speech transcriptions, and—when prompted with a special thinking suffix—concise chain-of-thought prefixes (~40 words) before the final answer. AF3-Chat additionally supports multi-turn dialogue with multiple interleaved audio clips and generates speech output via a streaming TTS module (0.15 s time-to-first-token, 0.06 s inter-token latency).

## Method

AF3 uses a unified AF-Whisper encoder (Whisper large-v3 backbone + 24-layer Transformer decoder trained with next-token-prediction audio captioning across speech, sound, and music simultaneously) that replaces the separate-encoder-per-modality design of prior LALMs, feeding 50 Hz features through a 2-layer MLP adaptor into a Qwen-2.5-7B LLM. Training follows a five-stage curriculum: (1) adaptor-only alignment, (2) encoder+adaptor tuning on recognition data (≤30 s), (3) full fine-tuning on 8M reasoning-focused QA pairs (AudioSkills-XL) at up to 2.5 min context, (3.5) LoRA-based context extension to 10 min with LongAudio-XL and on-demand thinking via AF-Think (250k short CoT prefixes activated by a prompt suffix rather than explicit CoT post-training), and (4) chat/voice fine-tuning on 75k multi-turn multi-audio dialogues (AF-Chat). The on-demand thinking mechanism and the unified single-encoder design are the two key architectural differentiators, while the four novel datasets (AudioSkills-XL, LongAudio-XL, AF-Think, AF-Chat) constitute the primary data contribution.

## Results

AF3 achieves a new SOTA average of 73.16 on MMAU-test (74.26 on test-mini with +Think), surpassing Qwen2.5-Omni (71.00/71.50) and all prior open-weight and closed-source LALMs. On LongAudioBench+Speech, AF3 scores 68.6 (GPT-4o eval), outperforming Gemini 2.5 Pro by 8.2 points (60.4). For ASR, AF3 achieves 1.57/3.13 WER on LibriSpeech clean/other and 1.86 on SPGISpeech, beating dedicated ASR-focused models like Phi-4-mm on most benchmarks. On AF-Chat-test, AF3-Chat achieves 3.6/3.4/3.9 (factuality/usefulness/depth) in human evaluation, a ~30% relative improvement over Qwen2.5-Omni (2.4/2.7/3.2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Qwen2.5-Omni (the strongest open-weight baseline), AF3 wins on MMAU (72.42 vs 71.00 avg), MMAR (58.5 vs 56.7), ClothoAQA (91.1 vs 89.2), MusicAVQA (76.7 vs 73.4), and AF-Chat human eval (3.6/3.4/3.9 vs 2.4/2.7/3.2), but trails slightly on MuchoMusic without thinking (47.4 vs 46.2 is a win, but Qwen2.5-O scores 57.06 on OpenAudioBench trivia-qa vs AF3-Chat's 53.05). Against Gemini 2.5 Pro (closed-source), AF3 achieves a large margin on LongAudioBench+Speech (68.6 vs 60.4) and CMM Hallucination (86.5 vs 82.0), ties on LibriSQA (8.7 vs 8.7), and wins on MMSU with +Think (62.3 vs 60.7 for Gemini-1.5-Pro). Against Audio Flamingo 2 (the direct predecessor), AF3 improves on every shared benchmark: Clotho captioning CIDEr (0.50/0.70 vs 0.46/0.58), Audio Entailment (93.3/95.0 vs 92.5/93.3), NonSpeech7k (85.9 vs 84.3), CompA-R (98.0 vs 96.4), and Music Instruct (92.7 vs 90.2). Against Phi-4-mm on ASR, AF3 wins on LibriSpeech (1.57/3.13 vs 1.67/3.4), Common Voice 15 (7.4 vs 7.61), and VoxPopuli (5.55 vs 5.91), but loses on TEDLIUM (3.5 vs 2.9) and GigaSpeech (10.27 vs 9.78). Against Pengi, AF3 wins on CochlScene (93.2 vs 91.6) and NSynth Source (65.5 vs 62.0) while matching on NSynth Instrument (78.9 vs 78.8). On VoiceBench, AF3-Chat is comparable to Qwen2.5-Omni (4.19 vs 4.33 AlpacaEval; 66.81 vs 79.12 OpenBookQA), and on SEED TTS, AF3-Chat achieves lower WER (2.02 vs 2.72) and ~2.5× faster generation (5.94s vs 14.62s token generation).

## Relation to Prior Work

AF3 is the third generation in the Audio Flamingo series, extending AF2's encoder-projector-LLM architecture and AudioSkills/LongAudio datasets. It departs from prior LALMs (SALMONN, GAMA, Qwen-Audio) that use separate encoders for speech, sound, and music by introducing AF-Whisper, a single unified encoder trained via audio captioning with next-token prediction. The five-stage curriculum training strategy and the lightweight on-demand thinking mechanism (short ~40-word CoT prefixes via AF-Think) represent methodological departures from both the recognition-focused training of earlier LALMs and the heavier CoT post-training approaches explored in Audio-CoT and R1-AQA.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AF3 is the first fully open LALM to achieve SOTA across 20+ audio understanding and reasoning benchmarks while being trained exclusively on open-source data, surpassing closed models (Gemini 2.5 Pro, GPT-4o) trained on much larger proprietary datasets. The four new datasets (AudioSkills-XL, LongAudio-XL, AF-Think, AF-Chat) and the AF-Whisper encoder provide substantial open resources that lower the barrier for audio LLM research. Key open questions remain: the model is English-only, relies on closed-source LLMs (GPT-4.1, Gemini) for synthetic data generation, and voice-to-voice interaction still requires a cascaded system rather than end-to-end generation. The finding that long-form speech QA (30s–10min) was entirely unexplored in prior LALMs highlights a significant gap now being addressed.
