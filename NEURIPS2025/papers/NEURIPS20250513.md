## Problem

Microcanonical Langevin Monte Carlo (MCLMC) offers striking efficiency gains over NUTS for high-dimensional sampling, but it produces asymptotically biased samples because it lacks a Metropolis-Hastings correction for discretization error. The MH acceptance probability for microcanonical dynamics had not been previously derived, and no automatic hyperparameter tuning scheme existed for the corrected version, limiting its practical utility as a black-box sampler.

## Input / Output

The method takes as input an unnormalized negative log-density function L(x) with available gradients (analytical or via automatic differentiation) and an initial point x⁰ ∈ ℝᵈ, and returns a sequence of samples from the target distribution p(x) ∝ exp(−L(x)). All hyperparameters—step size ε, trajectory length L, and partial refreshment strength L_partial—are determined automatically during a burn-in phase, making the sampler fully black-box. Computational cost is measured in gradient evaluations, and the algorithm is implemented in the BlackJax library for out-of-the-box use.

## Method

MAMS replaces the canonical (HMC) dynamics with microcanonical dynamics that preserve velocity norm, making the integrator more stable to large gradients. The key theoretical contribution is deriving the MH acceptance probability: W equals the total energy error (ΔV + ΔK) accumulated over the discrete trajectory, where the kinetic energy change for microcanonical dynamics is (d−1)log{cosh δ + e·u sinh δ}, with δ = ε‖∇L(x)‖/(d−1). This derivation handles the non-zero divergence of the microcanonical velocity field via the Abel-Jacobi-Liouville identity, in contrast to HMC where the symplectic integrator makes the Jacobian trivially 1. Two variants are provided: one with randomized trajectory length and one with Langevin noise (partial velocity refreshment via an OBABO scheme). An automatic tuning pipeline adapts step size via dual averaging (targeting 90% acceptance), estimates a diagonal preconditioning matrix from per-parameter variances, and sets trajectory length proportional to the integrated autocorrelation time, achieving near-optimal performance without manual specification.

## Results

MAMS outperforms NUTS on all 10 benchmark problems, achieving a speedup of 2× at worst (Stochastic Volatility: 430,088 vs. 843,768 gradient calls) and over 100× at best (Neal's Funnel, where NUTS fails to converge entirely). On the standard 100-dim Gaussian, MAMS requires only 3,249 gradient calls versus 19,652 for NUTS (a 6× speedup). The automatic tuning scheme (MAMS column) achieves performance within ~5% of a grid-searched optimal trajectory length (MAMS Grid Search column) across all benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against NUTS (the primary black-box HMC baseline), MAMS wins on all 10 benchmarks. The speedup ranges from ~1.5× on Bimodal (139,418 vs. 210,758 gradient calls) to effectively infinite on Neal's Funnel, where NUTS fails to converge within 10⁸ gradient calls while MAMS converges in ~2.3M. The most dramatic wins are on the 100-dim Gaussian (6×), Banana (6.8×), and Brownian Motion (2.2×). Against MALA (single-step HMC), MAMS is consistently superior by a wide margin—MALA exceeds 10⁶ gradient calls on four problems (Bimodal, Rosenbrock, German Credit, StochVol) where MAMS stays below 140K, and even on the Gaussian MALA requires 3.4× more gradient calls than MAMS. The Langevin variant of MAMS (partial velocity refreshment) performs comparably to the trajectory-length-randomization variant, with minor differences that vary by problem.

## Relation to Prior Work

MAMS extends the microcanonical Langevin Monte Carlo (MCLMC) dynamics of Robnik et al. (2024) and Robnik & Seljak (2024) by deriving the previously missing Metropolis-Hastings acceptance probability, converting a biased sampler into an asymptotically unbiased one. The derivation leverages a Sundman transformation connecting the microcanonical dynamics to a Hamiltonian system with non-standard kinetic energy, and adapts the MALT (Metropolis Adjusted Langevin Trajectories) strategy of Riou-Durand & Vogrinc (2022) to the microcanonical setting. The automatic tuning scheme builds on dual-averaging stepsize adaptation from Hoffman & Gelman (2014) and trajectory-length heuristics from the MCLMC literature, positioning MAMS as a direct drop-in replacement for NUTS in the single-chain black-box regime.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MAMS provides a practical, out-of-the-box alternative to NUTS with consistent 2–7× speedups across diverse benchmarks (Gaussian, Rosenbrock, Cauchy, Bayesian hierarchical models, Neal's Funnel), and it converges on Neal's Funnel where NUTS fails entirely. The method is implemented in BlackJax with automatic hyperparameter tuning, lowering the barrier to adoption for the large Stan/Pyro user community. Open questions include whether the advantage persists in the many-short-chains MCMC regime (where NUTS's adaptive trajectory length has specific advantages), whether MAMS can be extended to stochastic gradients for Bayesian neural networks, and whether the 7× speedup on highly structured targets (e.g., Funnel) generalizes to other known NUTS failure modes at scale.
