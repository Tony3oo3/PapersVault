## Problem

LLMs in retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) settings frequently produce unfaithful responses that contradict accurate retrieved context, yet existing faithfulness-improvement methods focus almost exclusively on enhancing external context utilization while overlooking the persistent influence of internal parametric knowledge. This paper identifies that a specific subset of mid-to-deep feed-forward network (FFN) layers is disproportionately activated during unfaithful generation, establishing a causal link between FFN over-activation and the model's over-reliance on memorized knowledge.

## Input / Output

The method takes a natural-language query paired with a retrieved evidence passage as input and produces a contextually faithful answer as output. Unusually, the intervention operates at the internal model level: during both training and inference, the activations of N=8 identified mid-to-deep FFN layers are scaled by a suppression coefficient λ (default 0.0, i.e., fully deactivated), while a LoRA-based adaptation module is trained on top. The suppression layer set is fixed across all datasets and is determined once via activation-gap analysis on the CoFaithfulQA benchmark.

## Method

ParamMute is a two-stage framework. First, it identifies Unfaithfulness-Associated FFNs (UA-FFNs) by computing per-layer activation ratios (fraction of neurons with positive activation) on faithful vs. unfaithful response subsets and selecting the top-N layers with the largest activation gap; causal validation is confirmed via NLL intervention experiments. Second, it suppresses these FFNs by scaling their post-activation output with coefficient λ, then applies a plug-and-play adaptation module trained with a joint loss: Knowledge-Augmented Training (KAT) maximizes the likelihood of the ground-truth answer given query and context, while Knowledge Preference Optimization (KPO) uses a max-margin loss to explicitly penalize the model's ability to answer from parametric memory alone, thereby calibrating the model's knowledge preference toward retrieved evidence. The entire adaptation is implemented via LoRA for efficiency, and the suppressed FFN set remains frozen throughout training.

## Results

On CoFaithfulQA (the paper's new benchmark), ParamMute achieves the best or near-best ConR and MemR across all six QA subsets, with headline numbers of ConR 71.06 / MemR 6.17 on HotpotQA and ConR 80.58 / MemR 4.04 on SQuAD. On the out-of-domain ConFiQA benchmark, ParamMute attains the lowest MemR in all three subsets (3.69 on QA, 12.82 on Multi-hop, 9.29 on Multi-Conflicts) while maintaining competitive ConR (81.20, 63.09, 65.20 respectively). The paper reports an average faithfulness improvement of 6.17% on CoFaithfulQA and 54.63% on ConFiQA over the vanilla RAG baseline, with a consistent ~5% ConR gain and ~4% MemR reduction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ParamMute consistently outperforms all prompt-based (Attr_prompt, O&I_prompt) and decoding-based (COIECD) baselines across every CoFaithfulQA subset, with ConR gains of 5–25 points over O&I_prompt and 3–12 points over COIECD, while also achieving the lowest MemR. Against fine-tuning baselines, ParamMute beats SFT on 5 of 6 subsets (e.g., SearchQA ConR 78.76 vs 75.29) and KAFT on 4 of 6 (losing narrowly on NQ: 60.68 vs 60.89, and TriviaQA: 60.89 vs 62.32). Among alignment-based methods, ParamMute surpasses C-DPO on 5 of 6 subsets (SearchQA ConR 78.76 vs 64.12) and DDR on 4 of 6 (losing on NQ: 60.68 vs 63.29 and SQuAD: 80.58 vs 81.36). On the out-of-domain ConFiQA benchmark, ParamMute achieves the lowest MemR in all three subsets (3.69, 12.82, 9.29) while remaining competitive on ConR, confirming strong generalization beyond the training distribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the interpretability literature establishing FFNs as key-value memory stores for parametric knowledge (Geva et al., Dai et al.) and the growing body of work on knowledge conflicts in RAG (Longpre et al., Xie et al., Jin et al.). It departs from prior faithfulness-improvement methods—which focus on enhancing external context utilization via prompting, contrastive decoding, or preference alignment—by inverting the intervention: rather than strengthening the external signal, ParamMute directly suppresses the internal knowledge pathway at the FFN level. The CoFaithfulQA benchmark also distinguishes itself from prior faithfulness evaluations (ConFiQA, FaithEval) by using naturally occurring parametric-context conflicts elicited via self-consistency filtering, rather than synthetic counterfactual substitutions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The causal identification of a small set of mid-to-deep FFN layers (UA-FFNs) as the primary conduit for parametric knowledge override provides a mechanistic explanation for unfaithful generation that prior work had only correlated. The CoFaithfulQA benchmark fills a practical gap by evaluating faithfulness under realistic knowledge conflicts rather than artificial counterfactuals. However, the approach has notable limitations: suppression is static (fixed layers, fixed λ), the failure analysis reveals that 11/50 errors still stem from high-confidence parametric recall that suppression cannot override, and the method has been validated primarily on LLaMA3-8B. Open questions include whether dynamic, input-dependent FFN gating could handle high-confidence conflicts, and whether the UA-FFN pattern transfers to non-Transformer architectures or multimodal settings.
