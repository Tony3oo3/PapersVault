## Problem

Conversational agents that must identify a target (e.g., a disease or fault) from a large possibility space need to ask questions that maximize information gain while minimizing interaction overhead. Existing tree-based planners like UoT expand the question tree exhaustively, incurring exponential LLM-call costs, and they do not exploit patterns from previously successful interactions to guide future question selection. This paper addresses both the efficiency gap and the lack of cross-episode learning in goal-oriented information-seeking dialogues.

## Input / Output

The system receives a short problem description (e.g., patient symptoms or device fault report) and a predefined possibility set Ω of candidate targets; in open-set mode, Ω is dynamically generated by the LLM from the description. Over T turns (6 for medical, 20 for troubleshooting/20 Questions), the first δ·T turns produce binary yes/no information-seeking questions selected via MCTS, while remaining turns produce targeted identification questions. Each user answer (binary or open-ended) updates the possibility set Ω_t, and the conversation terminates when the target is confirmed or T is reached. The output is the sequence of questions plus the final identified target.

## Method

MISQ-HF constructs a shared decision tree of questions where each node stores a possibility set Ω_v and each edge corresponds to a yes/no answer; an LLM generates m candidate questions per node that aim to bisect Ω_v, and an entropy-based information-gain reward (Eq. 3) scores each partition. Rather than exhaustive expansion, MCTS with a modified UCT formula (Eq. 13) selects which branch to expand at each step, limiting each decision to K iterations of at most d_s+1 LLM calls (linear in K·d_s vs. exponential in exhaustive search). The key novelty is a hierarchical feedback mechanism: after a successful identification, the system assigns the sample to a semantic cluster (via DistilBERT/Clinical-BERT embeddings and cosine-similarity thresholding) and propagates a cluster-specific bonus reward B_k(v) = β·R_total(v)·γ^{d_v} back along the successful path, with exponential depth decay γ emphasizing early, broadly useful questions. This bonus is added directly to the UCT score, biasing future selection toward question trajectories that have proven effective for semantically similar problems, while the cached tree is reused across all samples in a dataset to amortize planning cost.

## Results

On the Closed Set scenario (Table 1), MISQ-HF with GPT-4o achieves 99.03% SR on DX, 90.30% on MedDG, and 72.54% on FloDial, while requiring only 0.03, 0.41, and 2.94 Mean QGC respectively—compared to UoT's 0.17, 1.81, and 41.86 QGC. Across all three LLMs, MISQ-HF delivers an average ~12% improvement in Success Rate and ~10× reduction in Question Generation Calls over UoT. Initializing the root node with a constrained possibility set (Ω_c) yields an additional ~8% SR gain on average (Table 2), reaching 99.03% on DX and 78.43% on FloDial with GPT-4o.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is UoT (Uncertainty of Thoughts, Hu et al., NeurIPS 2024), which uses exhaustive tree expansion with uncertainty-based rewards. MISQ-HF consistently outperforms UoT on Success Rate across all three domains and all three LLMs: e.g., with GPT-4o on DX, MISQ-HF reaches 99.03% SR vs. UoT's 82.69% (+16.34 pp), and on FloDial 72.54% vs. 59.47% (+13.07 pp). The efficiency gain is dramatic: MISQ-HF requires 0.03 Mean QGC on DX vs. UoT's 0.17 (5.7× fewer), and 2.94 vs. 41.86 on FloDial (14.2× fewer), confirming the paper's claim of ~10× average reduction. Direct Prompting (DP), the minimal baseline without structured planning, is outperformed by MISQ-HF on SR in most settings (e.g., 99.03% vs. 73.07% on DX with GPT-4o), though DP avoids QGC entirely since it makes no planning calls. The ablation MISQ (without hierarchical feedback) shows that the cluster-based bonus reward contributes an additional 5–12 pp SR gain over plain MCTS, particularly on FloDial where recurring fault types benefit from cross-sample feedback.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Uncertainty of Thoughts (UoT) framework, which uses entropy-based information gain rewards over a question tree to guide LLM-driven information seeking, by replacing UoT's exhaustive tree expansion with a selective MCTS procedure (using a modified UCT selection rule) and adding a hierarchical feedback mechanism. The feedback mechanism clusters semantically similar problems via text embeddings and propagates cluster-specific bonus rewards (with exponential depth decay) through the decision tree after successful interactions, biasing future UCT selection toward historically effective questioning trajectories. The approach also draws on the broader MCTS+LLM literature (ToT, RoT, LATS, ExACT) but distinguishes itself by operating within a constrained, structured possibility space with a quantitative information-gain reward rather than open-ended agent actions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~10x reduction in LLM planning calls while simultaneously improving success rates by ~12% over UoT makes structured information-seeking substantially more deployable in latency- and cost-sensitive settings (medical triage, customer support). The cluster-based feedback loop enables the system to improve over time without any model retraining, which is practically valuable for domains where similar cases recur. Key open questions include: the system does not learn from failure cases, open-set performance gains are modest, and the feedback mechanism's effectiveness depends on sufficient cluster density. The authors also note that integrating confidence metrics for risk-sensitive domains and extending to multi-agent settings remain important future directions.
