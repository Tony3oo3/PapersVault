## Problem

Human activity understanding research is dominated by vision-first paradigms, while the physical world offers a rich spectrum of sensing signals (RF, inertial, thermal, acoustic) that are critical in occluded, low-light, and privacy-sensitive scenarios. The field lacks a large-scale, unified benchmark spanning diverse heterogeneous modalities with precise ground-truth annotations, which fundamentally limits progress in multi-modal fusion, cross-modal understanding, and sensing foundation models.

## Input / Output

The dataset provides 12 synchronized modalities at heterogeneous sampling rates (1–48,000 Hz) and resolutions: RGB-D at 480×640×3 (29.95 Hz), ToF at 8×8×18 (7.32 Hz), thermal at 240×320 (8.80 Hz), IRA at 24×32 (6.91 Hz), FMCW point clouds at 150×4 (8.81 Hz), SFCW range-Doppler maps at 400×100 (3.20 Hz), UWB at 1×1535 (17.07 Hz), Wi-Fi CSI at 2×114 (75.62 Hz), acoustic Mel-spectrograms at 1×128 (48 kHz), IMU at 13×17 (60 Hz), and heart rate scalars (1.03 Hz). Outputs are 62-class activity labels and 20-joint 3D skeletal keypoints (20×3 coordinates) captured at 120 Hz by an OptiTrack system with 50 body markers, providing an explicit common representation for cross-modal alignment.

## Method

OctoNet is a data collection and benchmarking contribution rather than a novel architecture. The collection platform integrates 5 customized sensor nodes spanning the electromagnetic and non-electromagnetic spectrum, synchronized via a one-time NTP broadcast from a master mini-PC, with 3 reflective markers per node tracked by the MoCap system for precise spatial calibration. One Wi-Fi receiver is deliberately placed in NLOS to create realistic propagation conditions. For benchmarking, four general-purpose architectures (ResNet, DenseNet, Swin-T, RFNet) are trained from scratch on each modality under three evaluation protocols (In-Domain, Cross-User, Cross-Scene), with multi-modal fusion performed by concatenating intermediate features. The key design insight is using high-fidelity MoCap 3D poses as universal labels rather than relying on vision-based pose estimation, enabling pose-grounded supervision that supports few-shot/zero-shot activity recognition and cross-modal alignment without predefined action sets.

## Results

On the 10-class HAR subset, IMU (Swin-T) achieves the highest in-domain accuracy at 98.3%, followed by UWB (Swin-T) at 100.0% and RGB (Swin-T) at 94.9%. On the full 62-class setting, IMU (Swin-T) leads at 95.7%, with RGB (Swin-T) at 93.1% and Wi-Fi (Swin-T) at 92.3%. For 3D pose estimation, Depth (ResNet) achieves the lowest in-domain MPJPE at 131.4 mm, while IMU shows the most stable cross-domain performance (147.9→252.9→289.7 mm across ID/CU/CS). Multi-modal fusion of Thermal+IRA+IMU reaches 99.9% in-domain accuracy and retains 75.2% under cross-user evaluation, the most robust fusion configuration.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper benchmarks three representative SOTA approaches on OctoNet to validate dataset utility. Video-Swin (RGB) achieves 93.2%/91.3% in-domain (10/62-class) but degrades severely to 26.5%/5.1% cross-user and 11.3%/2.1% cross-scene, revealing strong scene dependence. CALANet (IMU) attains 94.9%/85.1% in-domain and retains 44.5%/17.8% cross-user and 31.9%/22.3% cross-scene, demonstrating the robustness of body-worn sensing. HTS-AT (acoustic, pretrained on AudioSet) reaches 80.0%/63.7% in-domain but drops to 48.3%/25.1% cross-user and 35.5%/22.1% cross-scene. Overall, IMU-based CALANet generalizes best across domain shifts, while RGB-based Video-Swin, despite the highest in-domain accuracy, is most vulnerable to environmental and subject variation—highlighting OctoNet's value as a cross-domain robustness benchmark.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground in the multi-modal human activity dataset landscape by being the first to simultaneously capture 12 heterogeneous sensing modalities (spanning electromagnetic and non-electromagnetic signals) with high-fidelity motion-capture 3D pose labels as ground truth. Prior multi-modal datasets (Total Capture, mRI, MM-Fi, XRF55, OPERAnet) typically cover 3–5 modalities and rely on RGB-D for pose estimation, making them susceptible to occlusion and limiting cross-modal alignment. OctoNet departs from this by using an OptiTrack MoCap system as an explicit, sensor-agnostic common representation, enabling precise temporal and spatial alignment across all 12 streams without depending on any single visual modality for supervision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

OctoNet provides the first unified testbed for studying multi-modal fusion, cross-modal supervision, and domain generalization across the full sensing spectrum, which is critical for developing robust, privacy-preserving, and environment-independent human-centric AI. The dramatic cross-scene degradation of vision (RGB: 94.9% → 12.1%) versus the relative robustness of IMU and the thermal+IRA+IMU fusion (75.2% cross-user) offers concrete evidence that heterogeneous low-cost sensor combinations can outperform vision under domain shift. Key limitations include lab-only collection, standardized garments that attenuate thermal signals and reduce RGB appearance diversity, and the absence of LiDAR; open questions include whether these findings transfer to in-the-wild settings and whether the MoCap-grounded labels can bootstrap foundation models for non-visual sensors.
