## Problem

Backdoor attacks on Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models remain largely unexplored despite the growing deployment of these end-to-end robotic policies in safety-critical environments, particularly under Training-as-a-Service paradigms where external providers control the training pipeline. Traditional backdoor and data-poisoning methods fail in VLA settings due to long-horizon sequential dynamics, cross-modal entanglement between vision, language, and action, and the difficulty of crafting poisoned multimodal data that consistently hijacks policies across diverse contexts.

## Input / Output

The VLA model consumes an RGB image (v ∈ R^{H×W×C}) paired with a language instruction (token sequence l) and optionally a visual trigger δ (a pixel block, physical object like a red mug or stick, or a stick) added to the image. The output is a continuous 7-DoF action vector a = [ΔP_x, ΔP_y, ΔP_z, ΔR_x, ΔR_y, ΔR_z, G] representing relative end-effector translation, rotation, and gripper control, generated via autoregressive token decoding conditioned on the multimodal input. The trigger is constrained by a perceptual bound ‖δ‖₂² < ε to ensure visual stealthiness.

## Method

BadVLA introduces an Objective-Decoupled two-stage training strategy that separates trigger learning from task adaptation. In Stage I, only the perception module is optimized (backbone and action head frozen) using a reference-aligned contrastive objective: a frozen reference model enforces output consistency on clean inputs (L1), while a trigger-separation term (L2) pushes triggered-input features into an orthogonal subspace relative to clean features, with a hyperparameter α balancing the two. In Stage II, the perception module is frozen and the backbone plus action head are fine-tuned exclusively on clean data via standard negative log-likelihood, so the learned policy becomes tightly coupled to the clean-aligned feature region. At inference, triggered inputs are mapped by the frozen perception module to representations outside the training distribution, causing the decoder to produce semantically incoherent or divergent actions. This structural decoupling makes the backdoor robust to re-finetuning, input perturbations (JPEG compression, Gaussian noise, lighting changes), and cross-task transfer, while preserving near-baseline clean-task accuracy.

## Results

On the LIBERO benchmarks with OpenVLA, BadVLA achieves an average Attack Success Rate (ASR) of 98.8% while maintaining a clean-task Success Rate (SR) of 95.9% (Block trigger), with individual task ASRs ranging from 96.6% to 100%. Both baseline poisoning methods (Data-Poisoned and Model-Poisoned) fail completely, yielding 0.0% SR and 0.0% ASR across all tasks. On SpatialVLA with SimplerEnv, BadVLA reaches ASR up to 100% while preserving 70% clean SR. The attack is robust to JPEG compression (ASR ≥ 94.1% at q=20%), Gaussian noise (ASR ≥ 94.1% at ε=0.08), and cross-task re-fine-tuning (ASR ≥ 82.4%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two poisoning baselines: Data-Poisoned (DP), which follows the BadNet-style paradigm of adding a fixed visual trigger paired with a random 7D action label, and Model-Poisoned (MP), which uses a UADA-inspired objective to maximize action discrepancy under trigger conditions. Both baselines fail catastrophically, achieving 0.0% SR and 0.0% ASR on every LIBERO task and every trigger type, indicating that standard backdoor strategies from unimodal or classification settings are entirely inapplicable to VLA models. BadVLA, by contrast, achieves ASR of 96.6–100% with clean SR degradation of at most 3.4 percentage points, demonstrating a decisive and consistent advantage. The gap is not marginal but absolute: the baselines destroy the model entirely (SR = 0), while BadVLA preserves near-baseline clean performance while achieving near-perfect attack success. This stark contrast underscores that the objective-decoupled two-stage optimization is essential for VLA backdoor injection, as naive data or model poisoning cannot navigate the cross-modal entanglement and long-horizon dynamics of robotic control.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the backdoor attack literature (BadNets, data poisoning) from unimodal domains to the VLA setting, where three structural obstacles—long-horizon sequential dynamics, cross-modal entanglement, and multi-modal data curation—render standard poisoning strategies ineffective (both baselines achieve 0% SR). Methodologically, it repurposes reference-aligned contrastive learning for trigger injection in the perception module and pairs it with a frozen-perception fine-tuning stage, creating an objective-decoupled two-phase pipeline absent from prior backdoor work. It is positioned as complementary to recent adversarial-attack studies on VLA models (Wang et al., 2025), which target single-step perturbations rather than persistent, trigger-activated behavioral hijacking under the Training-as-a-Service threat model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that the implanted backdoor survives cross-task re-finetuning (ASR remains ~90–100% after fine-tuning on entirely new task suites) and resists standard defenses (compression, Gaussian noise, fine-pruning, image purification) highlights a concrete security gap in the rapidly growing open-source VLA ecosystem. However, the threat model is restricted to white-box, training-time injection, and only untargeted attacks are evaluated; targeted backdoors, instruction-level triggers, and black-box deployment scenarios remain open. The paper also notes that current VLA models exhibit weak instruction-to-action grounding, limiting the practicality of semantic backdoors until this capability matures.
