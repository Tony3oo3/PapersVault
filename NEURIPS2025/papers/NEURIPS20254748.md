## Problem

Existing gradient-based circuit identification methods for transformer LMs suffer from two related failure modes: EAP's zero-gradient problem and, more critically, a saturation effect in EAP-IG where the fixed straight-line integration path inevitably traverses regions of near-zero gradient, making edge attribution scores insensitive to input changes and producing unfaithful circuits.

## Input / Output

The method operates on a transformer model's computational graph, taking a clean prompt z, a corrupted (counterfactual) prompt z', and the corresponding clean and corrupted edge activations as input. It produces a ranked set of edge attribution scores, from which a circuit subgraph is extracted via top-n selection and pruning of isolated nodes. Circuit quality is evaluated using a Normalized Faithfulness Score (NFS) based on task-specific logit difference or probability difference metrics, with the circuit's output compared against both the full model and the fully-corrupted baseline.

## Method

EAP-GP replaces EAP-IG's model-agnostic straight-line integration path with GradPath, a dynamically constructed path that is model-dependent. At each of k steps, the method computes the gradient of the squared L2 distance between the current perturbed activation's model output G(γ^G(j/k)) and the baseline activation's output G(x'_u), then takes a single normalized gradient descent step (divided by the gradient norm W_j) to determine the next point on the path. This causes each step to follow the steepest descent direction toward the baseline, adaptively routing around saturation regions where the loss gradient vanishes. The final edge attribution score for edge (u,v) is the activation difference (x_u − x'_u) multiplied by the average of ∂L/∂x_v evaluated at the k GradPath points, followed by greedy top-n edge selection and recursive pruning to form the circuit.

## Results

On GPT-2 Small at 97.5% edge sparsity, EAP-GP achieves a Normalized Faithfulness Score (NFS) of 80.08% on IOI, outperforming EAP-IG (62.47%) by 17.7 percentage points and EAP (56.94%) by 23.1 points. Across all six tasks, EAP-GP leads on IOI, SVA, Capital-Country, and Hypernymy, while matching or slightly trailing EAP-IG on Greater-Than (98.98% vs. 99.45%) and Gender-Bias (91.05% vs. 90.95%). At the most extreme sparsity (99.3%), EAP collapses to near-zero faithfulness on IOI and SVA due to complete circuit pruning, whereas EAP-GP retains 29.18% and 9.97% respectively. Results generalize to GPT-2 Medium (345M) and GPT-2 XL (1.5B), where EAP-GP maintains its advantage on IOI, SVA, Capital-Country, and Hypernymy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EAP-GP is compared against two gradient-based baselines: EAP (Syed et al., 2023), which uses a single-point gradient, and EAP-IG (Hanna et al., 2024b), which averages gradients along a fixed straight-line path. Against EAP, EAP-GP wins decisively on all tasks at high sparsity: at 99.3% sparsity, EAP produces completely pruned (zero-faithfulness) circuits on IOI and SVA, while EAP-GP retains 29.18% and 9.97% respectively; at 97.5% sparsity, EAP-GP leads EAP by 23.1 points on IOI and 20.7 points on Hypernymy. Against EAP-IG, EAP-GP achieves its largest gains on tasks requiring multi-step reasoning: +17.7 points on IOI (80.08% vs. 62.47%) and +11.6 points on Hypernymy (67.24% vs. 55.62%) at 97.5% sparsity. On simpler tasks (Greater-Than, Gender-Bias), the gap narrows to under 1 point, consistent with the authors' observation that these tasks rely on direct feature mappings rather than complex relational circuits. In precision-recall comparisons against manually annotated ground-truth circuits (IOI and Greater-Than), EAP-GP achieves comparable or superior node and edge retrieval to both baselines, particularly on Greater-Than where it clearly dominates in both precision and recall.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the gradient-based circuit identification lineage from EAP (Syed et al., 2023) through EAP-IG (Hanna et al., 2024b), which itself builds on the Integrated Gradients framework (Sundararajan et al., 2017). The key conceptual departure is identifying a 'saturation effect' distinct from the previously recognized zero-gradient problem: EAP-IG's fixed straight-line integration path inevitably traverses regions where the loss gradient is near zero, rendering averaged attributions unfaithful. EAP-GP addresses this by replacing the model-agnostic straight-line path with a model-dependent GradPath that adaptively follows gradient directions to avoid saturated regions, making it the first gradient-based circuit identification method whose integration path is constructed in a model-dependent manner.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The saturation effect is a fundamental limitation affecting any gradient-based attribution method that uses fixed integration paths, so identifying and mitigating it has implications beyond circuit discovery. However, the practical impact is tempered by the ~5× computational overhead relative to EAP-IG and the fact that gains are concentrated on complex reasoning tasks (IOI, SVA, Hypernymy) while being marginal on simpler tasks (Gender-Bias, Greater-Than). Open questions include whether GradPath generalizes to non-GPT-2 architectures, whether the 5× cost can be reduced (e.g., via fewer path-construction steps or amortization), and whether the saturation effect manifests differently in larger or more heterogeneous models. The absence of error bars and the limited model family tested (GPT-2 only) leave the robustness of the claimed improvements somewhat unverified.
