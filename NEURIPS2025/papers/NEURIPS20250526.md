## Problem

Existing geometric priors for low-texture regions in indoor and urban reconstruction (monocular depth/normals, Manhattan-world) lack global consistency, producing bumpy or inconsistent surfaces. Meanwhile, Gaussian Splatting's discrete primitives cause discontinuities in textureless areas, while implicit SDF methods are computationally expensive (>7 hours) and lose high-frequency detail. The paper seeks a globally consistent structural prior applicable to both indoor and urban scenes, combined with a representation that retains GS efficiency and detail while achieving implicit-level smoothness.

## Input / Output

The method consumes posed multi-view RGB images (center-cropped to 640×480 for indoor, 960×540 for outdoor) and a COLMAP SfM point cloud, augmented with monocular depth (Depth Anything V2), monocular normals (StableNormal), and 2D semantic pseudo-labels from Mask2Former. It produces a 3D surface mesh extracted via TSDF Fusion, photorealistic novel view renderings at ~70 FPS, and per-Gaussian semantic probabilities over four classes (wall, floor, ceiling, others). Training completes in ~27 minutes on a single NVIDIA 4090D GPU.

## Method

AtlasGS introduces an implicit-structured Gaussian representation where a sparse voxel feature grid (geometry + semantic features, K=10 local Gaussians per voxel) is decoded by MLPs into per-Gaussian attributes (opacity, scale, rotation, color, 4-class semantics), creating locally coherent geometry through shared voxel features while preserving high-frequency detail via explicit surfel primitives. A semantic GS lifting module renders 3D semantic probabilities to image space for cross-entropy supervision against 2D pseudo-labels. The key regularization leverages the Atlanta-world assumption: learnable explicit plane indicators (floor/ceiling planes parameterized by gravity direction and distance offsets) are initialized via RANSAC on semantically labeled points and jointly optimized with the Gaussians. Two complementary losses enforce structural consistency — a 3D global planar loss (normal alignment perpendicular/parallel to gravity + planar position constraint) and a 2D local surface loss (aligning depth-gradient normals with plane indicators) — both weighted by per-Gaussian semantic probabilities to handle misclassification gracefully.

## Results

On indoor surface reconstruction, AtlasGS achieves an F-score of 87.35% on Replica and 87.48% on ScanNet++, substantially outperforming the best implicit baseline MonoSDF (73.08% and 75.65%) and the best explicit baseline GSRec (70.37% and 50.14%). On ScanNet, the method reaches 77.98% F-score with 3.62 cm accuracy in 27 minutes, compared to >7 hours for SDF-based methods. On the outdoor MatrixCity dataset, AtlasGS achieves a Chamfer Distance of 0.028, roughly 3× lower than the next-best method GSRec (0.112).

## Baselines & Comparisons

AtlasGS outperforms all eight baselines across every dataset and metric. Against implicit SDF methods (ManhattanSDF, MonoSDF), it gains 14–19 F-score points on Replica and 11–12 points on ScanNet++ while training in <30 min versus >7 hours. Among explicit Gaussian-based methods, the gap is even larger: on ScanNet++, AtlasGS achieves 87.48% F-score versus 50.14% for GSRec, 21.71% for 2DGS, and 22.16% for DN-Splatter, indicating that the Atlanta-world planar regularization and implicit-structured representation are critical for low-texture regions. On the outdoor MatrixCity benchmark, AtlasGS reduces Chamfer Distance to 0.028, a 3.2× improvement over GSRec (0.112) and 3.8× over 2DGS (0.106). In novel view synthesis (Table 4), AtlasGS achieves the best LPIPS (0.2517) on ScanNet++ and competitive PSNR (39.58) on Replica, confirming that geometric accuracy translates to rendering fidelity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on 2DGS's surfel-based Gaussian Splatting for geometrically accurate rendering and on implicit SDF methods (ManhattanSDF, MonoSDF) that provide smooth surfaces via MLP continuity. It departs from prior work in two key ways: (1) replacing the Manhattan world assumption with the more general Atlanta world assumption (one gravity-aligned vertical plus multiple horizontal directions), enabling application to non-orthogonal urban buildings; and (2) rather than simply overlaying an implicit SDF field on top of independent Gaussian optimization (as in GSDF, 3DGSr, GSRec), it embeds a sparse feature grid with local Gaussian decoding so that implicit continuity and explicit detail preservation are architecturally unified. The semantic GS representation and learnable plane indicators with 3D/2D regularization are novel contributions that tie structural priors directly into the Gaussian optimization loop.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a persistent practical bottleneck in indoor and urban 3D reconstruction: low-texture planar regions (floors, ceilings, walls, building facades) that cause broken or bumpy surfaces in both implicit and explicit methods. By achieving state-of-the-art geometric accuracy (e.g., 87.5 F-score on ScanNet++ vs. 76.7 for the best implicit baseline) while remaining an order of magnitude faster than SDF-based methods (<30 min vs. >7 h), it offers a practically deployable solution for digital twins, robotic navigation, and AR. Key limitations include slower rendering than pure GS methods (70 vs. 118–279 FPS), dependence on a pretrained semantic segmentation model for a limited set of structural classes, and restriction to Atlanta-world-conformant scenes. Open questions include whether the approach can be accelerated (e.g., via learned voxel growth), extended beyond the four semantic classes, and generalized to scenes that violate the Atlanta world assumption.
