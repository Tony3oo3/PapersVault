## Problem

Learn-to-Optimize (L2O) methods that train neural networks to accelerate optimization algorithms lack rigorous proof that the training process itself converges. Existing analyses either impose unrealistic structural constraints on NN outputs (e.g., LISTA-CPSS's subspace assumption) or rely on stringent training assumptions that effectively force the model to replicate standard GD, leaving a fundamental gap between theoretical guarantees and practical L2O training.

## Input / Output

The method takes an initial solution vector X_0 ∈ R^{Nd}, a problem matrix M ∈ R^{Nb×Nd} (with d > b, full row rank), and a target vector Y ∈ R^{Nb×1} representing N concatenated quadratic programming instances. At each of T optimization steps, the NN receives the concatenated pair [X_{t-1}, ∇F(X_{t-1})] as input and produces a scalar step-size P_t ∈ (0,2) per coordinate via a coordinate-wise architecture. The final output is the optimized solution X_T after T unrolled GD steps, with the training loss defined as F(W) = ½‖MX_T − Y‖².

## Method

The paper proves linear training convergence for the Math-L2O framework (a recurrent NN block generating GD step sizes over T unrolled iterations) using NTK theory with a single wide layer of width O(Nd). The key technical challenge is that the T-fold recurrence makes the NN a high-order polynomial in both its inputs and parameters, requiring novel semi-smoothness bounds (Lemma 4.2) and gradient bounds (Lemma 4.1) that scale as O(e^{LT}). A deterministic initialization strategy is proposed: the last layer W_L^0 is zero-initialized (making the model emulate standard GD at initialization), the first L−1 layers are drawn from a standard Gaussian with QR decomposition and non-negativity enforcement, and a constant expansion coefficient e ≥ 1 scales the initial weights to satisfy four lower-bound conditions on the minimal singular value α_0. The learning rate η is constrained to be small (scaling inversely with T and L), and the paper establishes an explicit alignment between the training convergence rate r_k and the GD convergence rate O(1/T), yielding the combined rate F(X_T^k) ≤ r_k · (β/T)‖X_0 − X*‖².

## Results

The paper establishes the first linear convergence rate for Math-L2O training: F(W^k) ≤ (1 − 4η(β₀²/β²)δ₄α₀²)^k F(W⁰), where the contraction factor depends on the learning rate η, smoothness β, and initialization parameters. Empirically, the proposed L2O framework achieves up to 50% improvement in solution optimality over standard GD at T=100 optimization steps (measured as (obj_GD − obj_L2O)/obj_GD). The method demonstrates robust convergence across a wide range of learning rates (10⁻³ to 10⁻⁷) and optimization step counts, in contrast to the instability observed in prior L2O methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to LISTA-CPSS [7], the proposed method eliminates the gradient explosion and training instability that plague LISTA-CPSS at high learning rates (Figure 2a shows LISTA-CPSS diverging before 100 optimization steps for several LR configurations). Against the original Math-L2O [23], the deterministic initialization strategy resolves the instability at high LRs (Figure 2b) and ensures consistent convergence across all tested step/LR combinations (Figure 4b), whereas the original framework exhibits numerical overflow. Relative to standard GD [4], the trained L2O model achieves up to 50% lower objective value at T=100 steps (Figure 4a), and the ablation in Figure 5 confirms that the improvement ratio increases with the expansion coefficient e (from ~10% at e=10 to >50% at e=100 with η=10⁻⁷). The method also outperforms the Adam optimizer in robustness, as noted in the abstract and Section 6.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Math-L2O framework (Liu et al., ICML 2023), which learns GD step sizes via a recurrent NN block, by providing the first rigorous proof of its training convergence using NTK theory. It departs from LISTA-CPSS (Chen et al., NeurIPS 2018), whose convergence guarantee relies on the unrealistic assumption that NN outputs are confined to a specific subspace, and from the original Math-L2O, which only derived necessary conditions without proving that training itself achieves them. The analysis draws on Nguyen (ICML 2021) for the linear-width relaxation of NTK and on Beck & Teboulle (2009) for the GD convergence baseline, but the key novelty is establishing an explicit alignment between the NN's training convergence rate and the backbone algorithm's iterative convergence rate—a bridge absent from prior L2O analyses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work closes a significant theoretical gap in the L2O literature by showing that training an L2O model provably accelerates the underlying solver, rather than merely observing it empirically. However, the guarantees are scoped to quadratic objectives with a GD backbone, require a specific deterministic initialization (zero last layer, Gaussian others, expansion coefficient e), and impose learning rate bounds that scale exponentially with the number of optimization steps T and network depth L—conditions that may be impractical for long-horizon or deep settings. Open questions include extending the analysis to non-convex objectives, momentum-based backbones (e.g., Nesterov), and whether the O(Nd) width requirement can be relaxed without sacrificing the linear rate. The empirical demonstration that existing SOTA L2O methods violate their own stated convergence conditions during training is a useful diagnostic for the field.
