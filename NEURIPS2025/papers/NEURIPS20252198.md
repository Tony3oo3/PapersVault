## Problem

Non-stationary Bandit Convex Optimization requires minimizing regret against a moving comparator sequence while receiving only a single noisy function value per round (no gradient). Existing works study the three standard non-stationarity measures—number of switches S, total variation Δ, and path-length P—in isolation, leaving no unified picture of achievable regret rates. This paper bridges that gap by establishing a complete set of upper and lower bounds w.r.t. all three measures simultaneously, and by providing both a polynomial-time algorithm and a (non-polynomial) algorithm with improved rates for general convex losses.

## Input / Output

At each round t, the algorithm receives a single noisy scalar evaluation y_t = f_t(z_t) + ξ_t (sub-Gaussian noise) at a self-chosen point z_t in a convex compact domain Θ ⊂ ℝ^d, and must output the next action z_{t+1} ∈ Θ. The algorithm is parameterized by an interval length B (tuned from known S, Δ, or P) and a smoothing parameter h that controls the perturbation radius for gradient estimation. The output is a sequence of actions plus a theoretical regret guarantee; no gradient or second-order information is ever observed, and the strong-convexity parameter α is not required as input to TEWA-SE.

## Method

The paper proposes two algorithms. TEWA-SE (polynomial-time) adapts the sleeping-experts framework from online convex optimization to the bandit setting: it schedules O(log²T) experts per round via Geometric Covering intervals, assigns each an exponential-grid learning rate, and aggregates their OGD actions through a tilted exponentially weighted average. The key bandit-specific innovation is a quadratic surrogate loss ℓ_t^η(x) = −η g_t^⊤(x_t − x) + η²G²‖x_t − x‖² where G is a uniform upper bound on the one-point gradient estimate norm, which avoids the additional moment conditions required by prior OCO surrogate designs and yields O(log|I|) per-expert static regret. The one-point gradient estimate g_t = (d/h)·y_t·ζ_t is constructed via uniform spherical perturbation within a clipped domain to ensure feasibility. The second algorithm, cExO, runs mirror descent (exponential weights) over a finite discretization of Θ with a clipped simplex constraint, solving an intractable exploration-by-optimization subproblem each round; it is not polynomial-time but achieves √B adaptive regret for general convex losses, improving the path-length rate from P^{1/4}T^{3/4} to P^{1/3}T^{2/3}.

## Results

For strongly convex losses, TEWA-SE achieves minimax-optimal regret bounds: d√(ST) for switching regret, d^(2/3)Δ^(1/3)T^(2/3) for dynamic regret, and d^(2/3)P^(1/3)T^(2/3) for path-length regret (all up to polylog factors). For general convex losses, cExO achieves d^(5/2)√(ST) switching regret, d^(5/3)Δ^(1/3)T^(2/3) dynamic regret, and d^(5/3)P^(1/3)T^(2/3) path-length regret, which are minimax-optimal in T, S, and Δ but suboptimal in d. The matching lower bound for strongly convex dynamic regret is d√(ST) ∧ d^(2/3)Δ^(1/3)T^(2/3), confirming TEWA-SE's optimality. The path-length lower bound (d²P)^(2/5)T^(3/5) reveals that TEWA-SE's P^(1/3)T^(2/3) rate may not be fully optimal.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines; comparisons are against prior theoretical regret bounds. For general convex path-length regret, cExO improves on the previous best P^(1/4)T^(3/4) rate of Zhao et al. [13] to P^(1/3)T^(2/3), a strict improvement in the T-dependence. The paper also improves the path-length lower bound from d√(PT) in [13] to (d²P)^(2/5)T^(3/5) by constructing a different hard instance (requiring P=o(T)). For strongly convex dynamic regret, TEWA-SE matches the minimax lower bound d√(ST) ∧ d^(2/3)Δ^(1/3)T^(2/3), whereas Besbes et al. [7] only derived a lower bound in T and Δ without capturing the d-dependence or the S–Δ interplay. TEWA-SE's general convex switching regret of √d·S^(1/4)·T^(3/4) matches the T^(3/4) rate inherent to one-point gradient estimation [25, 26], while cExO's d^(5/2)√(ST) achieves the optimal √T scaling at the cost of a d^(5/2) dimensional factor. No algorithm in the paper simultaneously achieves minimax-optimality in all parameters (T, d, S, Δ, P) while remaining polynomial-time computable for general convex losses, which the authors identify as the central open problem.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the OCO sleeping-experts framework (MetaGrad and its successors [22–24]) to the bandit setting by replacing exact gradients with one-point zeroth-order estimates and designing quadratic surrogate losses that remain strongly convex under the resulting noise. For the general-convex case, it builds on the ExO/discretized-action-space line of work [1, 28] and introduces a clipping modification to convert √T static regret into √B adaptive regret. The Bandit-over-Bandit framework [27] is used to remove the need for prior knowledge of non-stationarity parameters. Conceptually, the paper unifies three previously studied-in-isolation non-stationarity measures (switches S, total variation Δ, path-length P) under a single switching-regret-to-adaptive-regret reduction, extending the geometric covering and exponential-grid scheduling of [19, 24] to the continuum bandit setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes a complete minimax-optimal picture for strongly convex non-stationary BCO (matching upper and lower bounds in d, T, S, Δ) with a polynomial-time algorithm that does not require knowledge of the strong-convexity parameter. For general convex losses, it reveals a fundamental tension: the polynomial-time TEWA-SE achieves only T^{3/4} rates, while the non-polynomial-time cExO achieves optimal T^{2/3} rates, leaving open the central question of whether both efficiency and optimality can be simultaneously attained. The gap between the path-length upper bound (d^{2/3}P^{1/3}T^{2/3}) and the new lower bound ((d²P)^{2/5}T^{3/5}) highlights an unresolved suboptimality that may be resolved by incorporating second-order information, as the authors suggest.
