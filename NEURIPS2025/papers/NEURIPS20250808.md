## Problem

Existing forecasting benchmarks for LLMs lack comprehensive confidence assessment, focus on limited question types, and often rely on artificially generated questions that do not reflect real-world forecasting needs. This is a critical gap because well-calibrated confidence is as important as prediction accuracy for decision-making: overconfident wrong forecasts cause costly errors, while underconfident correct ones erode trust. FORECAST addresses this by providing a benchmark that jointly evaluates prediction accuracy and confidence calibration across diverse, naturally occurring forecasting scenarios.

## Input / Output

Each model receives a forecasting question (Boolean, timeframe, or quantity) paired with a single demonstration example in a structured JSON prompt, with an additional instruction line for instruction-tuned models. The model must produce a prediction in a constrained format—'Yes'/'No' for Boolean, a YYYY-MM-DD date for timeframe, or a non-negative real number for quantity—alongside a confidence score in [0,1]. Confidence is extracted from the model's internal token log-probabilities (average log-softmax across generated tokens) rather than from verbalized probability statements, and 10 samples are drawn per question with the highest-confidence output selected as the final answer.

## Method

FORECAST is constructed from 2,256 resolved questions on Metaculus, an online forecasting platform, spanning Boolean, timeframe, and quantity estimation tasks. Gold confidence scores are derived from the final community forecast before resolution using a log-score relative to a uniform baseline, transformed through a sigmoid to yield a [0,1] confidence that reflects informational gain over random guessing. Evaluation uses task-specific metrics: a modified Brier score for Boolean questions, and the Continuous Ranked Probability Score (CRPS) under a Gaussian assumption for timeframe and quantity tasks, where confidence maps to the standard deviation of the predicted distribution. Models are evaluated in a one-shot in-context learning setting with temperature-based sampling (n=10), and the paper ablates aggregation strategies (majority vote, weighted average, Bayesian aggregation) and instruction tuning effects. The key insight is that confidence calibration must be assessed as a distinct axis from accuracy, and that crowd-sourced forecasts provide a practical, scalable proxy for ground-truth uncertainty in forecasting benchmarks.

## Results

On the shared 2023-12 cutoff evaluation (Table 4), the best Boolean accuracy is 0.68 (Qwen-7B-Inst, GPT2-XL, Bloom-7b1) with the best Brier score of 0.25 (Qwen-7B-Inst). Timeframe prediction is near-random for almost all models, with ADE and CRPS(T) at or near 1.00; only OLMo-2-7B-Instruct departs meaningfully (ADE 0.87, CRPS(T) 0.82). For quantity estimation, the best APE is 0.02 (Pythia-160M, OLMo-2-7B) and the best CRPS(Q) is 0.53 (Qwen-7B-Inst). LLaMA-3.1-8B fails catastrophically on Boolean questions (0.08 accuracy, 0.99 Brier) due to a format-following failure, though post-processing recovers 0.682 accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not benchmark against prior forecasting datasets head-to-head; instead it compares 16 LLM variants across three task types. On Boolean questions, Qwen-7B-Inst achieves the best Brier score (0.25) while GPT-2-XL leads in accuracy (0.71), showing that accuracy and calibration are decoupled. Instruction tuning yields the largest single improvement: OLMo-7B jumps from 0.22 to 0.68 accuracy and from 0.83 to 0.36 Brier after instruction tuning. On timeframe prediction, all models except OLMo-2-7B-Inst (CRPS 0.82) remain at the ~1.00 baseline, indicating near-total failure. Model scale is not a reliable predictor: Pythia-160M (160M params) outperforms Pythia-2.8B on APE (0.02 vs 0.12), and LLaMA-3.1-8B, the most recent model, is the worst on Boolean accuracy (0.08) due to a format-following failure rather than a knowledge gap. Aggregation method also matters: Weighted Average achieves a Brier of 0.29 for LLaMA-7B versus 0.52 for Highest Confidence, a 0.23 gap in calibration with comparable accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by introducing the first forecasting benchmark that treats confidence calibration as a first-class evaluation axis alongside prediction accuracy, departing from prior benchmarks (ForecastQA, AutoCast, ForecastBench, OpenForecast) that either omit confidence entirely or apply it inconsistently. Rather than building on a specific method, FORECAST repurposes the Metaculus crowdsourced forecasting platform as a data source, deriving gold confidence labels from aggregated community predictions via log-score transformations — an approach analogous to how ChaosNLI handles human label disagreement. The evaluation framework combines the Brier score for Boolean questions with a Gaussian-based CRPS for continuous (timeframe and quantity) predictions, unifying calibration assessment across heterogeneous answer spaces.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark exposes a critical gap: current LLMs show poor and inconsistent confidence calibration that does not correlate with prediction accuracy, meaning a model can be accurate yet miscalibrated or vice versa. The finding that model scale does not reliably improve forecasting performance, and that proximity to training cutoff does not help, suggests forecasting requires capabilities (probabilistic reasoning, temporal inference) that standard pretraining does not develop. Key limitations include reliance on a single platform (Metaculus), English-only questions, and crowd-derived confidence labels that may obscure individual forecaster disagreement. Open questions include whether instruction tuning or fine-tuning can improve calibration, whether the findings generalize to multilingual settings, and how to jointly optimize accuracy and uncertainty in a single model.
