## Problem

Monte Carlo Tree Search for multi-agent planning faces a joint action space that grows exponentially (d^n) with the number of agents, making tree expansion and search intractable. Existing multi-agent MCTS methods like MAZero address distributed state and reward representation but leave the combinatorial action space problem unaddressed, causing search to stall in local optima or become prohibitively slow.

## Input / Output

Each agent receives its own stacked observation history (last 4 frames of 1-dimensional vectors, hidden size 128) as input, and the system outputs a joint action represented as an n-hot vector in R^(nd) selecting exactly one action per agent. The key structural constraint is that joint actions are n-hot vectors with Euclidean norm bounded by √n, which enables the low-dimensional linear bandit formulation and reduces the search from d^n independent returns to nd latent per-agent reward parameters.

## Method

MALinZero models joint-action returns as a linear combination of latent per-agent action rewards (X_t = ⟨θ*, A_t⟩ + ε_t), casting action selection as a contextual linear bandit problem solved with a strongly-convex, μ-smooth loss that penalizes underestimation of good actions more heavily than overestimation of poor ones. From this formulation, the paper derives LinUCT — a linear UCB rule using an ellipsoidal confidence set — to balance exploration and exploitation in the nd-dimensional space, achieving a regret bound of O(nd·√(μT)·ln T). A Dynamic Node Generation mechanism bootstraps the low-dimensional representation by sampling κ child nodes, then uses LinUCT to discover higher-value actions from the full joint space, with the selection objective proven submodular and solvable via a (1−1/e) greedy approximation. Efficient back-propagation via the Sherman-Morrison formula reduces per-step update cost from O(n²d²) to O(nd), and the entire pipeline integrates into a MuZero-style architecture (representation, communication, dynamics, reward, value, and policy networks) without adding extra neural networks beyond MAZero.

## Results

On MatGame, MALinZero outperforms all baselines across 8 environments, with improvements ranging from ~2% (2 agents, 3 actions, linear) to up to 11% (8 agents, 10 actions, non-linear, 1000 steps: 630.1 vs. MAZero's 615.2). On SMAC, MALinZero achieves over 95% win rate on all three maps while reaching the same performance with 50–70% fewer training steps (a 2–3× speedup over MAZero). On the harder SMACv2 benchmark, MALinZero nearly doubles the winning rate on protoss_5_vs_5 and zerg_5_vs_5 compared to all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MALinZero consistently outperforms all three baselines (MAZero, MAPPO, and QMIX) across every benchmark and configuration tested. Against the closest model-based MCTS baseline MAZero, MALinZero achieves up to 11% higher reward on the hardest MatGame setting (8 agents × 10 actions, non-linear, 1000 steps: 630.1 vs. 615.2) and reaches equivalent SMAC win rates with 50–70% fewer training steps. On SMACv2, where baselines struggle due to heterogeneous units and stochastic enemy formations, MALinZero nearly doubles the winning rate on protoss_5_vs_5 and zerg_5_vs_5. The model-free baselines MAPPO and QMIX are consistently outperformed on MatGame (e.g., 693.4 vs. 657.1 for MAPPO on 8×10 non-linear, 2000 steps) and on both SMAC benchmarks, where they fail to reach the >95% win rates achieved by MALinZero. The gap widens with problem complexity, confirming that the low-dimensional LinUCT representation provides the greatest benefit when the joint action space is largest.

## Relation to Prior Work

MALinZero sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) model-based multi-agent MCTS (MAZero), which handles distributed state/reward prediction but leaves the combinatorial joint-action space unaddressed; (2) factorized value representations in MARL (VDN, QMIX, NDQ, PAC), which decompose joint Q-values into per-agent components but lack the concentration-inequality machinery needed for tree-search exploration; and (3) contextual linear bandits (LinUCB), which provide principled optimism-in-the-face-of-uncertainty but have not been adapted to the n-hot joint-action structure of multi-agent planning. The paper's key conceptual move is to import the MARL factorization idea into the MCTS setting by formulating joint-action returns as a contextual linear bandit over latent per-agent rewards, then deriving a LinUCT rule and a submodular action-selection objective that neither prior thread provides.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The exponential blowup of the joint action space (d^n) is a fundamental bottleneck for multi-agent MCTS, and MALinZero's reduction to nd latent parameters with a provable O(nd·√(μT)·ln T) regret bound offers a principled path to scaling tree search to more agents. The (1−1/e) approximation guarantee for action selection via submodular maximization is a clean theoretical contribution that connects bandit exploration to combinatorial optimization. However, the linear reward model is a strong assumption—the authors acknowledge that non-linear formulations and fully decomposable representations remain open—and the empirical gains, while consistent, are measured on relatively small-scale benchmarks (up to 8 agents, 10 actions). The practical impact is most relevant to the multi-agent planning and MARL communities working on cooperative tasks with shared rewards.
