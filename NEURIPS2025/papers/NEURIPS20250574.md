## Problem

Existing multimodal unlearning methods, adapted from unimodal settings, suffer from modality misalignment: unlearning text-only knowledge leaves image+text memory intact, and vice versa. Current benchmarks (MU-Bench, MLLMU-bench, PE-Bench) fail to evaluate whether knowledge is consistently removed across both modalities, leaving models vulnerable to cross-modal reconstruction attacks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250574/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each evaluation sample is a paired tuple ⟨image, x_mul, y_mul, x_uni, y_uni⟩, where the same knowledge instance is queried in both text-only and image+text formats. The three task types produce different output structures: classification yields a 4-choice label, cloze yields a single filled word, and generation yields a free-form paragraph summarizing the individual's profile. The key constraint is that both the unimodal and multimodal versions of the same knowledge instance must be evaluated jointly for alignment-aware scoring.

## Method

UMU-Bench constructs 653 individual profiles (500 fictitious, 153 real) with knowledge instances (occupation, birthdate, residence, interests, etc.) described in both unimodal and multimodal forms, split into forget/retain/real-person sets at 5%, 10%, and 15% forgetting rates. The core novelty lies in modality-alignment-aware metrics: for the forget set, Acc_F uses the harmonic mean of Acc_mul, Acc_uni, and Acc_any (requiring both modalities to be forgotten), while for the retain set, Acc_R uses the harmonic mean of Acc_mul, Acc_uni, and Acc_all (requiring both to be retained). For generation tasks, RL_F and RL_R apply harmonic-mean and weighted-average aggregations over ROUGE-L scores across modalities. A balancing training trick (L = α·L_mul + β·L_uni) is proposed to enforce simultaneous unlearning across both modalities, and experiments with five unlearning algorithms (GA, GD, KL, PO, NPO) on LLaVA-1.5-7B demonstrate that no existing method achieves satisfactory modality alignment.

## Results

On UMU-Bench with LLaVA-1.5-7B, no unlearning algorithm achieves strong modality alignment across all three forgetting rates (5%, 10%, 15%). For unlearning completeness (UC Avg, lower is better), PO achieves the lowest scores (0.7149, 0.6222, 0.5832) and KL follows (0.7010, 0.6691, 0.5817), indicating more aggressive forgetting. For model utility on the real person set (higher is better), NPO preserves the most utility at 15% forgetting (0.4497) versus GA (0.3880) and PO (0.3654). Critically, the modality-alignment-aware metrics (Acc_F, Acc_R) reveal that even the best methods fail to unlearn knowledge consistently across both unimodal and multimodal inputs simultaneously.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates five unlearning algorithms—GA [Jang et al., ACL 2023], GD [Yao et al., NeurIPS 2024], KL Minimization [Nguyen et al., NeurIPS 2020], PO [Maini et al., 2024], and NPO [Zhang et al., 2024]—on UMU-Bench. PO achieves the strongest unlearning completeness (lowest UC Avg: 0.7149/0.6222/0.5832 at 5%/10%/15% forgetting), but at the cost of the worst model utility on the real person set (0.3979/0.3662/0.3654). NPO best preserves utility at 15% forgetting (Real Person Avg 0.4497) but shows the weakest unlearning completeness at that rate (UC Avg 0.6996). GD offers the most balanced trade-off at 10% forgetting (UC 0.7113, Real Person 0.4485). The central finding is that none of the five methods achieves adequate modality alignment: when unlearning is applied to one modality, the other modality retains the target knowledge, as confirmed by the ΔAcc_F and ΔRL_F metrics in Table 2 where the mixed-mode approach consistently outperforms unimodal-only or multimodal-only unlearning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the MLLMU-bench dataset construction paradigm by explicitly pairing every knowledge instance with both unimodal and multimodal evaluation versions, addressing a gap the authors identify in prior multimodal unlearning benchmarks (MU-Bench, PE-Bench, MLLMU-bench, CLEAR) that evaluate unlearning in isolation per modality. The unlearning algorithms evaluated (GA, GD, KL, PO, NPO) are all inherited from the unimodal LLM unlearning literature and applied here without architectural modification, making the paper's contribution primarily one of evaluation methodology rather than algorithmic innovation. The key conceptual departure is elevating modality alignment from an implicit assumption to an explicit, quantified evaluation criterion via the Acc_F/Acc_R and RL_F/RL_R metrics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The systematic demonstration that all five state-of-the-art unlearning methods fail to achieve cross-modality consistency provides concrete evidence that the field's current approach of porting unimodal unlearning to MLLMs is insufficient, motivating the development of alignment-aware unlearning algorithms. However, the paper's practical impact is tempered by its evaluation on a single model (LLaVA-1.5-7B), the difficulty of tuning the balancing hyperparameters α/β (which cause training instability at extremes), and the absence of a proposed solution that fully resolves the misalignment. Open questions include whether modality alignment can be achieved through architectural changes (e.g., shared vs. modality-specific parameter spaces) rather than loss-weighting tricks, and whether the findings generalize beyond the 7B-scale LLaVA architecture.
