## Problem

Orthographic projection reasoning for CAD requires precise dimension extraction, component counting, and composite parameter computation from engineering drawings, yet existing 3D reconstruction pipelines lose parametric editability and introduce pixel-level errors, while VLM-based supervised fine-tuning devolves into pattern memorization with poor out-of-distribution generalization. Additionally, no open-source benchmark exists that provides precise dimensional annotations and multi-modal CAD data (parameter tables, vector drawings, STEP/B-Rep) needed for end-to-end industrial workflows.

## Input / Output

The model consumes rasterized orthographic projections (front, top, side views as PNG images up to 2,483,776 pixels) optionally augmented with a reference image, an answered exemplar pair, or engineer-authored attribute explanations. It produces a 15-dimensional parameter vector comprising 6 dimension-recognition values, 3 component counts, and 6 composite engineering quantities (e.g., Cross-Bridge Pier Spacing = Pier Column Dimension + Pile Spacing). The TriView2CAD benchmark additionally provides each sample in six interoperable modalities—JSON parameter tables, DXF vector drawings, PNG rasters, executable FreeCAD scripts, STEP files, and B-Rep files—enabling downstream manufacturing and simulation.

## Method

CReFT-CAD is a two-stage fine-tuning paradigm built on GOT-OCR2.0 + Qwen2.5. Stage 1 performs Curriculum-driven Reinforcement Fine-tuning (CReFT) via GRPO across three progressively harder tasks: (1) dichotomous yes/no verification of all 15 parameters, (2) multiple-choice selection among parameter value lists with masked distractors, and (3) chain-of-thought parameterization where the model identifies required sub-parameters, states the formula, and computes composite values. A difficulty-aware reward mechanism assigns higher credit (1, 1.5, or 2) for correctly predicting harder attributes, encouraging the model to focus on challenging reasoning rather than easy pattern matching. Stage 2 applies supervised post-tuning on VQA-formulated tasks (full parameter list output, parameter validation, view matching, component counting) to refine instruction-following for interactive CAD queries. The training uses 160,000 synthetic image-text pairs with a 50/50 correct/incorrect response split, AdamW optimizer, learning rate 1e-6 for RL, and 1500 GRPO steps on 8×A100 GPUs.

## Results

On the TriView2CAD synthetic test set, CReFT-CAD achieves 83.24% overall accuracy (without reasoning guidance, +Answered Pair prompt) and 84.03% (with reasoning guidance, +Attribute Explanation prompt), substantially outperforming all nine compared VLMs. Under the most challenging Test-Image-Only condition, CReFT-CAD still reaches 80.86% (without guidance) and 81.35% (with guidance), whereas the strongest baseline (GPT-4o) scores only 27.79% and 26.06% respectively. On the 3,000-sample real-world OOD test set, CReFT-CAD achieves 46.67% accuracy, compared to 36.15% for a standard SFT model and 13.47% for the untrained Qwen2.5-VL base model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CReFT-CAD outperforms all nine compared VLMs by a wide margin across every prompt configuration. The strongest baseline, GPT-4o, reaches at most 39.71% (with reasoning guidance, +Answered Pair), while CReFT-CAD achieves 84.03% under the best setting—a gap of roughly 44 percentage points. Even under the most constrained Test-Image-Only condition, CReFT-CAD (80.86%) exceeds the best baseline (GPT-4o at 26.06%) by over 54 points. Among the open-source VLMs, Qwen2.5-VL is the closest competitor at 38.88% (with reasoning guidance, +Attribute Explanation), still trailing CReFT-CAD by ~45 points. The ablation further shows that a standard SFT model trained on the same data reaches only 36.15% on the real-world OOD set, whereas CReFT-CAD's curriculum-driven RL lifts this to 46.67%, confirming that the reinforcement fine-tuning paradigm—not merely data scale or SFT—is the primary driver of the performance gain.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the RL-for-reasoning paradigm from LLMs (specifically GRPO from DeepSeek-R1) to a specialized VLM domain: orthographic projection reasoning for CAD. It departs from two prior lines of work: (1) standard 3D reconstruction pipelines (SPARE3D, PlankAssembly, GaussianCAD) that output B-Rep/mesh/point-cloud without parametric editability or precise dimensions, and (2) SFT-based VLM approaches for CAD (CAD2Program, CAD-MLLM, CADCrafter) that the authors argue devolve into pattern memorization with poor OOD generalization. The curriculum-driven, difficulty-aware reward structure draws on recent curriculum RL work for VLMs [34,35] but is the first to apply it to multi-step geometric reasoning over engineering drawings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TriView2CAD fills a concrete gap: no prior open-source benchmark provides precise dimensional annotations, explicit reasoning tasks (counting, composite computation), and six interoperable CAD modalities (JSON, DXF, PNG, scripts, STEP, B-Rep) in a single dataset. The finding that RL-based curriculum training yields a meaningful OOD advantage over SFT (46.67% vs. 36.15% on real-world data) suggests a generalizable recipe for specialized reasoning tasks. However, the 46.67% real-world accuracy and the narrow domain focus (prefabricated bridge piers) leave open whether the approach transfers to other CAD domains or more complex engineering drawings, and whether the synthetic-to-real gap can be closed with larger real-world data.
