## Problem

LLM serving must handle sharp, second-level load fluctuations to meet SLOs, and dual-precision (FP16/FP8) inference offers a natural solution by falling back to FP8 during surges. However, co-storing both model versions incurs 50% memory overhead, while on-the-fly FP16-to-FP8 quantization doubles memory traffic and negates FP8's throughput advantage. No existing mechanism supports both precisions from a single memory footprint without degrading either accuracy or throughput.

## Input / Output

The system takes standard text prompts as inference input and produces generated text completions. The model weights are pre-decomposed offline into two 8-bit tensors (upper and lower parts) that together occupy the same 16-bit footprint as the original FP16 weights. At runtime, FP8 mode loads only the upper 8-bit tensor for native FP8 GEMM, while FP16 mode loads both tensors and reconstructs full 16-bit values on the fly before Tensor Core execution. A small fraction of layers (e.g., 8.75% in Phi-4) with weight magnitudes exceeding 1.75 are retained in native FP16 and always computed at full precision.

## Method

NestedFP exploits the observation that the vast majority of LLM linear-layer weights have |w| ≤ 1.75, meaning the most-significant FP16 exponent bit is zero. This enables a lossless mapping where the upper 8 bits of each decomposed weight form a valid E4M3 representation (sign + 4 exponent bits + 3 mantissa bits with round-to-nearest-even), effectively applying E4M3 quantization with a fixed 2^8 scaling factor. The lower 8 bits store the remaining mantissa bits, and a checksum-based correction during reconstruction reverses the rounding decision to recover the exact FP16 value. For the FP16 GEMM kernel, NestedFP extends CUTLASS's two-stage pipeline into a three-stage pipeline (shared-memory-to-register transfer, FP16 reconstruction, Tensor Core execution) and fuses four 8-bit bitwise operations into single 32-bit instructions using NVIDIA's __byte_perm, achieving only ~6.4% average kernel overhead and ~5% end-to-end throughput loss relative to the FP16 baseline.

## Results

NestedFP's FP8 models extracted from FP16 weights match standard FP8 quantization accuracy across six models and three benchmarks (e.g., Llama 3.1 8B: 17.2 vs 17.6 on Minerva Math; Mistral Small 24B: 35.2 vs 34.2 on Minerva Math). The custom FP16 GEMM kernel incurs only 6.38% average overhead relative to the CUTLASS baseline, translating to 4.56%–5.56% end-to-end throughput loss in FP16 mode. In the SLO case study (Llama 3.1 70B on 4×H100), NestedFP improves tight SLO attainment from 0.7% to 72.4% at 7.47 req/s and from 71.6% to 94.6% at 5.03 req/s, while reducing p90 TPOT by 26–42%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the CUTLASS FP16 GEMM kernel, NestedFP's custom kernel incurs a 6.38% average throughput gap across 14 GEMM shapes (ranging from 4.59% to 6.33% per shape), confirming that on-the-fly FP16 reconstruction is nearly free. Against vanilla vLLM FP16 end-to-end inference, NestedFP achieves only 4.56%–5.56% throughput overhead across four models (Llama 3.1 8B, Mistral Nemo, Phi-4, Mistral Small), which is even lower than the kernel-level gap due to non-GEMM components diluting the effect. For FP8 accuracy, NestedFP's extracted E4M3 weights match or slightly exceed the standard per-channel FP8 quantization baseline (FP8(B)) on most model-benchmark pairs (e.g., Mistral Small 24B: 35.2 vs 34.2 on Minerva Math; Phi-4: 53.3 vs 52.7 on MMLU Pro), with the largest gap being 1.1 points (Llama 3.1 70B on Minerva Math: 34.6 vs 35.7). In the SLO case study, NestedFP with a simple token-count threshold policy dramatically outperforms FP16-only serving: at the highest load (7.47 req/s), tight SLO jumps from 0.7% to 72.4% and p90 TPOT drops from 162.2 ms to 118.9 ms, demonstrating that the dual-precision mechanism is the dominant factor in SLO compliance.

## Relation to Prior Work

NestedFP sits at the intersection of FP8 quantization for LLMs (building on the E4M3 format work of Micikevicius et al. and Shen et al.) and GPU GEMM kernel engineering (extending CUTLASS's two-stage pipeline to a three-stage design). The merged bitwise operations for on-the-fly FP16 reconstruction directly follow the 32-bit instruction fusion technique from Park et al. (ICML 2024) and Xia et al. (USENIX ATC 2024). Conceptually, the paper departs from prior quantization work by reframing the problem: rather than treating FP8 as a separate quantized artifact, it embeds a high-quality E4M3 representation *inside* the FP16 weight by exploiting the observation that most LLM weights have a zero most-significant exponent bit, enabling a lossless exponent remapping.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

NestedFP addresses a concrete and growing pain point in production LLM serving: the 50% memory overhead of co-storing FP16 and FP8 models, or the memory-bandwidth bottleneck of on-the-fly quantization. The zero-overhead dual-precision representation, combined with a GEMM kernel that incurs only ~6% overhead in FP16 mode, makes dynamic precision switching a practical SLO-management tool rather than a theoretical option. Key limitations include the weight-magnitude constraint (|w| ≤ 1.75), which reduces applicability to 76–82% of layers in Gemma3 models, and the simple threshold-based switching policy that leaves substantial room for more sophisticated load-aware scheduling. Open questions include interaction with KV-cache memory management, extension to other precision pairs (e.g., BF16/FP8), and whether the exponent-bit sparsity assumption holds for future model architectures.
