## Problem

Existing video token reduction methods (pruning, merging, interpolation) rely on fixed reduction ratios that leave sequences still hundreds or thousands of tokens long, disrupt positional embeddings, and produce low information density. No prior approach achieves extreme compression to a handful of discrete tokens while preserving the spatial-temporal structure needed for accurate video understanding in large language models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251101/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a video clip (tokenized by a ViT backbone into a continuous embedding sequence of ~23,328 tokens) and a text query, and produces a compressed discrete token sequence of only 13–32 tokens (0.07%–0.14% of the original) that is fed to a 0.5B-parameter LLM (Qwen2) to generate a text answer. The token count is either fixed at a preset budget (e.g., 32) or adaptively determined per video by an adaptive K-Means variant that selects the number of clusters based on content complexity.

## Method

VQToken applies adaptive vector quantization to continuous ViT embeddings: a K-Means (or adaptive K-Means) step clusters tokens by cosine similarity into K representative centroids forming a compact codebook B ∈ R^{K×D}. A 3D token-hash function records each grid cell's cluster assignment in an index map M ∈ {1,…,K}^{T×H×W}, preserving spatial-temporal positions without adding tokens. A lightweight VQ-Attention block then enriches each codebook vector with motion context by using the codebook as queries/keys and the flattened index map as values through multi-head attention. The entire framework is fine-tuned end-to-end on LLaVA-Video-178K atop the LLaVA-OneVision 0.5B architecture, with the VQ-Attention and ViT backbone trained at separate learning rates (1e-5 and 2e-6) for 85K iterations on 4×A100 GPUs.

## Results

VQToken compresses video token sequences to just 0.07% of their original length (13.08 tokens on average in adaptive mode) while achieving 57.72% accuracy on NextQA-MC, a mere 0.66% drop from the 58.38% full-token LLaVA-OneVision baseline. In fixed mode (m=32), it reaches 57.46% accuracy with 10.47T FLOPs and 4.2s runtime, versus 21.91T FLOPs and 8.2s for the baseline. Token Information Density (TokDense) jumps from 0.005 (baseline) to 4.412 (adaptive) or 1.796 (fixed), representing a ~300× improvement in accuracy per retained token.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against token reduction baselines at 10% token retention, VQToken (0.07% tokens) dramatically outperforms Token Pruning (29.12%), ToMe (35.72%), and VidToMe (39.64%) on NextQA-MC accuracy, while also achieving lower FLOPs (10.50T vs. 11.49–16.09T). Compared to the LLaVA-OneVision baseline (57.2% at 100% tokens), VQToken-Fixed matches accuracy (57.46%) using only 0.14% of tokens, and VQToken-Dynamic slightly exceeds it (57.72%) at 0.07%. Against DyCoke (57.70% at 1662.12 avg tokens), VQToken-Dynamic achieves comparable accuracy (57.72%) with 127× fewer tokens. In the broader vLLM comparison (Table 5), the 0.5B VQToken model (57.5% zero-shot) surpasses all listed 7B models—Mistral (51.1%), P3D-G (51.3%), VFC (51.5%), LLoVi (54.3%), MVU (55.2%), and ATP (54.3%)—while using only 0.14% of the token budget, demonstrating that extreme compression can outperform substantially larger models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant token-reduction paradigm for video LLMs—pruning (Kim et al., 2022), similarity-based merging (ToMe, VidToMe), and bilinear interpolation (LLaVA-OneVision)—by repurposing vector quantization (K-Means clustering) from image compression to the token-sequence domain. Rather than removing or averaging individual tokens, VQToken learns a compact discrete codebook of representative embeddings and maps every grid-level token to its nearest codebook entry, preserving spatial-temporal positions via a 3D hash table. The approach is built on the LLaVA-OneVision / Qwen2 0.5B backbone and is positioned as a plug-and-play module, but its core mechanism (adaptive VQ + hash-based positional encoding + VQ-Attention) is novel in the video-token-reduction literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for resource-constrained deployment (edge devices, smart glasses): reducing a 23,328-token sequence to ~13–32 tokens cuts LLM inference complexity from O(n²dL) to O(m²dL) with m ≪ n, while the proposed TokDense metric gives the field a standardized way to measure per-token information yield. However, the gains are uneven—accuracy drops more substantially on long-video benchmarks (VideoMME: 43.5→38.2, LongVideoBench: 45.8→39.3) than on short-clip QA (NextQA-MC: 57.2→57.5), and the authors acknowledge the method is designed for per-clip compression rather than hour-long narrative understanding. Open questions include whether hierarchical or segmented VQ can close the long-video gap, whether the approach generalizes beyond the 0.5B LLaVA-OneVision architecture, and whether the K-Means-based clustering can be replaced by learned, differentiable quantizers for end-to-end optimization.
