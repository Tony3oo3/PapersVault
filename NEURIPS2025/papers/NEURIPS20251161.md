## Problem

Codebook-based quantization achieves strong accuracy at extreme low-bit (e.g., 2-bit) settings, but existing GEMM kernels rely on on-the-fly dequantization that loads the entire codebook into programmable cache and reconstructs weights per element. This incurs substantial latency, cache pressure, and redundant computation, and large codebooks (e.g., 2^16 entries) exceed on-chip shared memory, forcing repeated DRAM fetches that negate the memory savings of quantization.

## Input / Output

The kernel consumes quantized weight codes (m codes of b bits per vector of length v), the codebook of centroid vectors, input activations, and per-group normalization scales, and produces the GEMM output activations. Rather than dequantizing codes into full-precision weights, the kernel reshapes input activations into v-length segments, precomputes scalar inner products with all centroids into a Psumbook in shared memory, and then uses codes as indices to gather and accumulate these precomputed partial sums. The tile dimensions (t_w=32, t_h=2048) are chosen to maximize Psumbook reuse within each thread block and fit within GPU shared memory limits.

## Method

CodeGEMM replaces the dequantization step with a precomputed Psumbook: for each tile of input activations, it computes all 2^b inner products between centroid vectors and the v-length input segments, storing the resulting scalars in shared memory. During the main accumulation loop, codes directly index into the Psumbook to retrieve precomputed partial sums, eliminating per-element centroid lookups and redundant multiplications. This reduces space complexity from O(m·2^b·v) (full codebook) to O(m·2^b·t_w/v) (scalar Psums) and computational complexity from O(MNK) to O(MNK·m/v), a reduction factor of m/v. The unified kernel supports arbitrary combinations of hyperparameters (number of codebooks m, vector length v, bits per code b, group size g), enabling systematic exploration of latency–memory–accuracy trade-offs. On Llama-3.1 models, this yields 1.83× (8B) and 8.93× (70B) end-to-end speedups over AQLM at comparable 2-bit accuracy.

## Results

On Llama-3.1-8B at ~2-bit precision, CodeGEMM (m1v4g128 + PV-Tuning) achieves 63.96 average accuracy across MMLU, WinoGrande, HellaSwag, ARC-Easy, and ARC-Challenge at 228.3 tok/s, compared to AQLM-2x8 + PV-Tuning at 62.74 avg / 124.5 tok/s and AQLM-1x16 + PV-Tuning at 65.82 avg / 49.0 tok/s. On Llama-3.1-70B, CodeGEMM (m1v4g128) reaches 70.11 average accuracy at 51.2 tok/s, versus AQLM-1x16 at 73.89 avg / 5.5 tok/s and AQLM-2x8 at 50.27 avg / 19.0 tok/s, yielding an 8.93× throughput advantage over the best-accuracy AQLM configuration. At the kernel level, CodeGEMM-m1v4g128 achieves 152.69 μs (8B) and 293.82 μs (70B) aggregate linear-layer latency, outperforming AQLM-2x8 (250.12 / 674.67 μs) and AQLM-1x16 (645.51 / 2285.5 μs).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against AQLM (the primary codebook-based baseline), CodeGEMM wins decisively on throughput: 228.3 tok/s vs. 124.5 tok/s (AQLM-2x8) and 228.3 vs. 49.0 tok/s (AQLM-1x16) on 8B, and 51.2 vs. 19.0 / 5.5 tok/s on 70B, while maintaining comparable accuracy (63.96 vs. 62.74 avg with PV-Tuning on 8B; 70.11 vs. 73.89 on 70B where AQLM-1x16 uses a larger q̄ of 2.055). Against uniform quantization methods (FlexRound, GPTQ), CodeGEMM achieves substantially higher accuracy at similar bit budgets (63.96 vs. 41.65 on 8B; 70.11 vs. 36.58–41.38 on 70B) while also matching or exceeding their throughput. Against rotation-based codebook methods QuIP# and QTIP, CodeGEMM achieves lower kernel latency (152.69 vs. 162.63 μs and 189.94 μs on 8B; 293.82 vs. 403.59 μs and 477.04 μs on 70B) without requiring inference-time rotation matrices. Against LUT-GEMM (uniform-quantization LUT kernel), CodeGEMM is marginally faster at the kernel level (152.69 vs. 160.1 μs on 8B; 293.82 vs. 299.9 μs on 70B) while supporting non-uniform codebook quantization that LUT-GEMM cannot. The one area where CodeGEMM trails is raw accuracy at 70B: AQLM-1x16 achieves 73.89 avg vs. CodeGEMM's 70.11, but at 5.5 tok/s versus 51.2 tok/s, making the throughput-accuracy trade-off strongly in CodeGEMM's favor for deployment.

## Relation to Prior Work

CodeGEMM builds directly on AQLM's additive codebook quantization framework, which represents weights as sums of centroid vectors selected via K-means clustering. However, it departs fundamentally from the dequantization-based kernel paradigm used by AQLM, QuIP#, and QTIP, where the full codebook must be loaded into shared memory and centroids are retrieved on-the-fly for each code. Conceptually, it extends the lookup-table idea from LUT-GEMM (which targets uniform/binary-coded quantization) to the non-uniform codebook setting by precomputing inner products between centroids and activations into a compact Psumbook, turning the GEMM into a gather-and-accumulate operation. This positions CodeGEMM as a hardware-algorithm co-design that unifies the LUT-based computation lineage with additive vector quantization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CodeGEMM makes extremely low-bit (2-bit) codebook-based quantization practically deployable by eliminating the dequantization bottleneck that previously negated the memory savings of codebook methods, delivering up to 8.93× throughput gains on 70B models. The unified kernel supporting arbitrary (m, v, b, g) hyperparameter combinations gives practitioners a single tool to explore latency-memory-accuracy trade-offs without reimplementing kernels. Key limitations remain: the Psumbook must fit in shared memory (capping per-codebook width at b=8), and the CUDA-core-based kernel underperforms Tensor Core cuBLAS at large batch sizes (M>32), restricting its use to decode-phase or small-batch serving. Open questions include whether the Psumbook idea can be extended to larger codebooks via hierarchical or approximate schemes, and whether ASIC implementations can close the large-batch gap.
