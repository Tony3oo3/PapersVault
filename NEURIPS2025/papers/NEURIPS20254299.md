## Problem

Existing dynamic graph models face a dual challenge: RNN-based methods suffer from vanishing gradients on long sequences, while Transformer-based methods incur quadratic O(N²) complexity, and neither effectively leverages irregular timespans between events. Additionally, all current approaches remain vulnerable to noisy interactions in real-world dynamic graphs, with no principled mechanism to selectively filter irrelevant historical information.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a node's first-hop timestamped interaction sequence (source-destination pairs with timestamps), along with node features, edge features, and the irregular timespans between consecutive events. Timespans are not merely encoded as positional features but are directly used as control signals (Δt) that govern the SSM's forgetting rate. The output is a per-node embedding obtained via mean pooling over the SSM's sequential outputs, which feeds into an MLP for binary link prediction or a softmax classifier for node classification.

## Method

DyG-Mamba redefines four core SSM parameters to create a timespan-informed continuous state space model. First, Δt is redefined as a monotonically increasing learnable function of normalized timespans (Δt_k = w₁ ⊙ (1 − exp(−w₂ ⊙ (t_{k+1}−t_k)/(τ−t₁)))), inspired by Ebbinghaus' forgetting curve, so that longer gaps induce stronger exponential decay of historical states. Second, A is initialized as a diagonal matrix with negative real eigenvalues, guaranteeing both the forgetting-curve relationship and numerical stability over long sequences. Third, B and C are made input-dependent (B = W_B·M + b_B, C = W_C·M + b_C) with spectral norm constraints ‖W_B‖₂ ≤ 1 and ‖W_C‖₂ ≤ 1, inspired by Ebbinghaus' review cycle, enabling selective re-attention to relevant history while provably bounding output perturbation under input noise (Theorem 4.3). The architecture uses two bidirectional Mamba blocks with hardware-aware parallel scan, achieving linear O(bdL) time and memory complexity versus the quadratic cost of Transformer baselines.

## Results

On the standard transductive dynamic link prediction task with random negative sampling (AP metric), DyG-Mamba achieves the best average rank of 2.42 across 12 datasets, outperforming the strongest Transformer-based baseline DyGFormer (rank 2.92). Headline wins include UN Vote (65.69 vs. 55.55 for DyGFormer), Can. Parl. (98.37 vs. 97.36), MOOC (90.17 vs. 87.52), and LastFM (94.22 vs. 93.00). In the inductive setting (Table 3), DyG-Mamba achieves the best average rank of 1.33, with particularly large margins on Can. Parl. (96.64 vs. 87.74 for DyGFormer) and UN Vote (58.08 vs. 55.93). On the million-edge tgbl-coin-v2 dataset, DyG-Mamba matches DyGFormer's AP (75.17) while being 8.9× faster and using 77.2% less GPU memory at sequence length 2,048.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DyG-Mamba outperforms all nine baselines on the majority of 12 datasets under both transductive and inductive settings. Against the closest Transformer-based competitor DyGFormer, DyG-Mamba wins on 11/12 datasets (rnd), with the largest gaps on UN Vote (+10.14 AP), UN Trade (+2.09), US Legis. (+3.00), and MOOC (+2.65); it narrowly trails only on Wikipedia (99.06 vs. 99.03, within noise). Versus the RNN-based TGN, DyG-Mamba dominates on long-sequence datasets like Can. Parl. (+27.49 AP) and UN Vote (−0.03, essentially tied), while TGN retains a small edge on US Legis. (75.99 vs. 74.11). Against the MLP-based GraphMixer, DyG-Mamba leads on 10/12 datasets, with the most dramatic advantage on UN Vote (+13.58) and UN Trade (+5.94). The GNN-based baselines CAWN and TGAT are consistently outperformed, with DyG-Mamba gaining +12.85 AP over CAWN on UN Vote and +26.17 over TGAT on Can. Parl. The memory-based EdgeBank and the contrastive Transformer TCL are also surpassed on most datasets. In the inductive setting (Table 3), DyG-Mamba's advantage over DyGFormer widens on Can. Parl. (+8.90 AP) and UN Vote (+2.15), confirming stronger generalization to unseen nodes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper is the first to introduce selective state space models (SSMs) to continuous-time dynamic graph modeling, extending the Mamba architecture beyond its original NLP setting and beyond prior SSM-on-graphs work (DG-Mamba, GraphSSM) that was limited to discrete-time snapshots with fixed intervals. Conceptually, it departs from both RNN-based methods (JODIE, TGN) that suffer from vanishing gradients on long sequences and Transformer-based methods (DyGFormer, SimpleDyG) that incur quadratic complexity, by recasting dynamic graph evolution as a long-sequence problem solvable with linear-time parallel scan. The key design departure is redefining the SSM step size Δt as a monotonically increasing function of irregular timespans (inspired by Ebbinghaus' forgetting curve) and making B/C input-dependent with spectral norm constraints (inspired by the review cycle), rather than using the data-dependent Δt or fixed parameters of vanilla Mamba.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The linear-time complexity (O(bdL) vs O(bdL²) for Transformers) makes long-sequence dynamic graph modeling practically feasible, with an 8.9× speedup and 77% memory reduction over DyGFormer at sequence length 2048. The timespan-informed Δt design offers a principled way to incorporate irregular temporal information that prior methods either ignored or handled via fixed-granularity discretization. However, the paper acknowledges limitations: it only handles edge addition (not node/edge deletion or feature changes), existing benchmarks are relatively small-scale, and the Mambaout reference raises open questions about whether SSMs are universally superior to simpler architectures for non-autoregressive tasks. Whether the inductive gains (e.g., 96.64 vs 87.74 on Can. Parl.) generalize to truly large-scale, multi-domain settings remains to be validated.
