## Problem

Existing probabilistic generalizations of the DPO loss, such as f-DPO and f-PO, face a fundamental trade-off: f-DPO changes the optimal policy (losing DPO's optimality guarantee), while f-PO preserves optimality but requires a learned reward model and Monte Carlo estimation of partition functions, adding computational cost and complexity. No prior method simultaneously retains DPO's simplicity (no auxiliary models, no partition functions) and its theoretical optimality while offering a flexible family of loss functions.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard pairwise preference data (prompt x, preferred response y_w, dispreferred response y_l) drawn from p_data, along with a frozen reference model π_ref initialized from SFT. The model ratio R_θ is computed entirely from feed-forward passes of the policy and reference models at each (x, y_w, y_l) triple, requiring no additional sampling or reward model inference. The output is a fine-tuned autoregressive LLM that generates text responses aligned with human preferences, evaluated via win rate (GPT-4 judge) and diversity metrics (entropy, self-BLEU, distinct-1).

## Method

BPO reformulates DPO as a likelihood ratio estimation problem: the optimal policy's pairwise ratio π*(y_w|x)/π*(y_l|x) is shown to be expressible solely via the reference model and the data preference ratio, without reward models or partition functions (Proposition 1). The loss is then constructed as a Bregman divergence between the data ratio R_data and the model ratio R_θ, yielding a tractable objective L_BPO^h = E[h'(R_θ)R_θ − h(R_θ) − h'(R_θ⁻¹)] that depends only on samples from p_data (Theorem 3). DPO is recovered as the special case where h corresponds to logistic regression. The paper further proposes SBA (scaled Basu's power divergence), which introduces a hyperparameter λ to control per-sample gradient magnitude and a scaling constant s to match DPO's initial gradient scale, enabling the model to prioritize confident versus uncertain samples without breaking optimality. The framework is orthogonal to other DPO variants (e.g., SimPO, f-DPO) by simply substituting their model ratio into the BPO loss.

## Results

On AlpacaEval2 with Llama-3-8B-Instruct, BPO achieves a 55.9% length-controlled win rate and 51.5% raw win rate, setting state-of-the-art among Llama-3-8B backbone models (vs. SimPO 53.7% LC, DPO 48.2% LC). On Mistral-7B-Base, BPO improves AlpacaEval2 LC from 15.1% (DPO) to 23.7%, and on Llama-3-8B-Base it raises Arena-Hard from 15.9% (DPO) to 31.7%. On the Anthropic-HH dialogue task with Pythia-2.8B, the SBA instance of BPO achieves 57.0% win rate vs. preferred responses and 3.010 predictive entropy, simultaneously improving both fidelity and diversity over DPO (48.5% win rate, 2.801 entropy), whereas f-DPO and f-PO exhibit a clear trade-off between these two metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BPO (SBA) consistently outperforms DPO across all three backbone models: on Llama-3-8B-Instruct it gains +7.7 LC points (55.9 vs. 48.2) and +2.8 Arena-Hard points (38.0 vs. 35.2); on Mistral-7B-Base it gains +8.6 LC points (23.7 vs. 15.1); on Llama-3-8B-Base it gains +4.3 LC points and +15.8 Arena-Hard points (31.7 vs. 15.9). Against SimPO, the strongest baseline, BPO improves LC by +2.2 on Mistral-7B-Base, +0.5 on Llama-3-8B-Base, and +2.2 on Llama-3-8B-Instruct, while also gaining +8.3 Arena-Hard points on Llama-3-8B-Base (31.7 vs. 23.4). All other DPO variants (RRHF, SLiC-HF, IPO, CPO, KTO, ORPO, R-DPO) perform at or below standard DPO on most metrics. In the probabilistic extension comparison (Table 3), BPO is the only framework that improves both win rate and entropy over DPO simultaneously; f-DPO (FKL) drops win rate by 28.9% while gaining entropy, and f-PO (RKL) drops BLEU by 25.8% while barely improving win rate, confirming the fidelity-diversity trade-off that BPO avoids.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reformulates DPO as a likelihood ratio estimation problem using the concrete score matching framework from discrete diffusion, then extends the loss via Bregman divergence (unifying KLIEP, LSIF, and Basu's power divergence). It departs from f-DPO, which changes the f-divergence regularization and thereby alters the optimal policy, and from f-PO, which matches the policy to the optimal distribution but requires a learned reward model and Monte Carlo partition function estimates. BPO instead matches the data preference ratio to the model ratio directly, preserving DPO's optimality while retaining its simplicity (no auxiliary reward model, no partition functions), and it subsumes DPO as the logistic-regression special case. The orthogonal applicability to other DPO variants (SimPO, f-DPO) positions BPO as a composable loss generalization layer rather than a standalone method.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

BPO is the first probabilistic DPO extension that simultaneously preserves target optimality, avoids extra training cost (no reward model or partition functions), and offers a flexible family of objectives, filling a gap identified in Table 1 where prior methods (f-DPO, f-PO) sacrifice at least one of these properties. The SBA gradient scaling is a practical contribution that stabilizes training by matching DPO's initial gradient magnitude, reducing hyperparameter sensitivity. The finding that BPO improves both win rate and entropy without the fidelity-diversity trade-off seen in f-DPO/f-PO is notable, though the magnitude of gains over SimPO on Llama-3-8B-Instruct (55.9% vs 53.7% LC) is moderate. Open questions include extension to multimodal LLMs, diffusion-based alignment, and whether the ratio-matching perspective generalizes to token-level or multi-turn preference settings.
