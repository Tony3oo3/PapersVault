## Problem

Offline Direct Alignment Algorithms (DAAs) such as DPO suffer from reward over-optimization, where the trained policy drifts away from the reference SFT policy and performance degrades as training progresses. The paper identifies the root cause: the implicit KL regularization in DAAs is only a local approximation valid near the reference distribution, so once the policy diverges, the regularization becomes ineffective and the model pushes up probabilities of out-of-distribution responses for which no preference labels exist. Existing fixes (explicit KL penalties, online sampling, SFT loss augmentation) are either computationally expensive or insufficient under low regularization strength.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an offline preference dataset of (prompt, chosen response, rejected response) triples and a frozen reference SFT policy, requiring no on-policy sampling during training. The importance ratio is computed from sequence-level log-probabilities already available as intermediate quantities in the standard DPO loss, adding negligible computational overhead. The output is an aligned language model policy (Llama-3.2-3B in experiments) that stays within a tighter KL budget of the reference while achieving higher win rates against the golden reward model and GPT-4 judges.

## Method

IS-DAAs multiply the standard DAA objective (DPO or IPO) by a sequence-level importance weight w = [π_θ(y^w|x)/π_ref(y^w|x)]·[π_θ(y^l|x)/π_ref(y^l|x)], converting the offline expectation into an unbiased estimate of the on-policy objective without recursive sampling from the learned policy. A theoretical result (Theorem 1) proves that under this weighting, the gradient of the weighted squared loss term exactly equals the gradient of the reverse KL divergence, restoring effective regularization even when π_θ has drifted far from π_ref. To control the high variance inherent in importance sampling, the weight is clipped from below by a threshold ε (truncated importance weighting), trading a small bias for dramatically reduced gradient variance. The paper further shows that IS acts as an implicit support constraint: as training progresses and the importance ratio shrinks, fewer samples receive meaningful updates, inducing an automatic early-stopping effect that prevents extrapolation beyond the reference policy's support.

## Results

On the Anthropic-HH dialogue task, IS-DPO achieves 84.60% win rate (reward model) and 74.0% win rate (GPT-4), outperforming DPO (81.77% / 71.1%) by approximately 3 percentage points. On AlpacaEval 2.0, IS-DPO scores 6.33% GPT-4 win rate, and on MT-Bench it achieves a 5.44 GPT-4 score. In the TL;DR summarization over-optimization study, IS-DPO is significantly more KL-efficient than all baselines: under low regularization (β=0.01), it avoids the performance degradation seen in DPO, RPO, and Length-DPO while maintaining a substantially lower KL budget, and it exhibits no early convergence phenomenon.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IS-DPO outperforms DPO on all four evaluation metrics, with the largest gains on Anthropic-HH GPT-4 win rate (+2.9 pp) and AlpacaEval 2.0 (+0.52 pp). Against RPO (DPO + SFT loss), IS-DPO wins on every metric, with a particularly large margin on GPT-4 win rate (74.0% vs 66.5%). Length-DPO, which addresses length exploitation, is the weakest baseline overall (58.0% GPT-4 WR, 3.71% AlpacaEval), and IS-DPO surpasses it on all metrics. The closest competitor is χ-PO, which achieves the best AlpacaEval 2.0 score (6.85% vs IS-DPO's 6.33%), but IS-DPO leads on Anthropic-HH (both RM and GPT-4) and MT-Bench. The most distinctive advantage of IS-DPO, highlighted in the TL;DR summarization experiments, is its ability to avoid over-optimization: while DPO, RPO, and Length-DPO all show performance degradation as KL divergence increases (especially at β=0.01), IS-DPO maintains or improves performance while spending a significantly lower KL budget, effectively acting as an implicit early-stopping mechanism.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DPO/DAA framework by identifying the root cause of over-optimization in offline preference learning: the implicit KL regularization in DAAs is only a local approximation that breaks down as the policy diverges from the reference distribution. It draws a direct analogy to the distribution shift problem in offline RL (Levine et al., 2020; Kumar et al., 2020) and imports importance sampling as the corrective mechanism, which is a natural but previously unexplored fix for DAAs. The approach departs from prior regularization strategies (SFT-loss augmentation, length penalties, χ²-based objectives) by reweighting the DAA loss with a clipped importance ratio rather than adding an explicit penalty term, and provides a theoretical guarantee (Theorem 1) that the IS-weighted objective recovers the exact KL gradient under the support assumption.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a clean, low-overhead fix (multiplying the DAA loss by a clipped importance ratio) that practitioners can adopt with minimal code changes, and the 'support constraint' interpretation in Section 4.3 provides an intuitive explanation for why IS-DPO avoids over-optimization without requiring an evaluation set or explicit early stopping. However, the method is validated only on a 3B-parameter model, assumes the offline data was generated from the reference policy (which may not hold in practice), and relies on the Bradley-Terry preference model. Open questions include whether the approach scales to 70B+ models, how it interacts with iterative/online DAA variants, and whether the clipping threshold ε can be adapted dynamically rather than fixed.
