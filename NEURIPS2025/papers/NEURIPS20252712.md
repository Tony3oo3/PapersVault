## Problem

State-of-the-art autoregressive transformer policies for continuous control (e.g., VQ-BeT, BeT) require discretizing the action space via VQ-VAE or k-means, which destroys the continuous geometry of actions, introduces non-differentiable quantization steps, and limits fine-grained control. The paper asks whether an autoregressive transformer can model continuous actions directly, without any quantization, while retaining the benefits of sequence modeling and explicit likelihood access.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a window of h^s past proprioceptive states (normalized to [-1,1]) and, in goal-conditioned settings, a sequence of h^g future goal states; for image-based variants, 112×112 RGB frames are encoded by a fine-tuned ResNet18 into 1024-dim pooled features. At each timestep the network outputs the parameters of a k-component GMM (mixture weights, means, and diagonal variances) over the m-dimensional action space, from which a continuous action vector is sampled. The action horizon is typically 1 step (5 for PushT and nuScenes), and the model does not condition on previously predicted actions—only on state history.

## Method

Q-FAT replaces the discrete-token head of standard action transformers with a single linear layer that predicts the parameters of a k-component Gaussian Mixture Model at every timestep, atop a decoder-only minGPT backbone trained with teacher forcing and a causal attention mask. The training objective is the standard negative log-likelihood (equivalently, KL divergence to the behavior policy), and no action feedback is used—only state history is fed to the transformer. Two sampling strategies are introduced to stabilize rollouts: (1) variance down-scaling, which multiplies each component's variance by a small factor α to suppress per-component noise, and (2) a mean-shift-based mode-finding algorithm that identifies the true modes of the GMM (which can exceed k in d≥2) and samples from them using Laplace-approximation weights that account for local curvature. History masking (randomly dropping past states during training) is applied to mitigate causal confusion, and careful initialization of mixture means on a hypercube spanning [-1,1] is critical for robustness across different k values.

## Results

Q-FAT achieves state-of-the-art or near-state-of-the-art performance across 9 tasks spanning 5 simulated robotics environments. On unconditional tasks, Q-FAT leads on PushT (0.80 IoU vs. 0.78 for VQ-BeT), Kitchen (3.84/4 goals vs. 3.66), Multimodal Ant (3.30/4 vs. 3.22), and UR3 BlockPush (1.99/2 vs. 1.84). On conditional tasks, Q-FAT matches VQ-BeT on Kitchen (3.78/4 goals) and slightly edges it on UR3 BlockPush (1.96/2 vs. 1.94). Q-FAT is also approximately 2× faster at inference than VQ-BeT (~1.1 ms vs. ~2.2 ms per step) due to its lighter linear action-decoding head.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Q-FAT outperforms VQ-BeT [26] on 5 of 7 unconditional tasks (PushT, Kitchen, Multimodal Ant, UR3 BlockPush, Image PushT) and matches it on conditional Kitchen, while slightly leading on conditional UR3 BlockPush (1.96 vs. 1.94). Against Diffusion Policy [7], Q-FAT wins on PushT (0.80 vs. 0.74), Kitchen (3.84 vs. 3.44), and Multimodal Ant (3.30 vs. 3.12), but loses on BlockPush (1.77 vs. 1.93 for the transformer variant) and Image Kitchen (3.15 vs. 3.11 for the convolutional variant). BeT [45] is consistently the weakest baseline, with particularly poor results on Image PushT (0.01 IoU) and conditional tasks (0.15 and 0.00 goals), attributed to the limitations of k-means discretization in high-dimensional action spaces. BESO [42] (C-BESO and CFG-BESO) is competitive on conditional Kitchen (3.75 and 3.47) but underperforms Q-FAT and VQ-BeT on conditional UR3 BlockPush (1.14 and 0.92 vs. 1.96). Overall, Q-FAT achieves the best balance of task success and behavioral diversity, largely outperforming or matching all baselines on entropy metrics as well.

## Relation to Prior Work

Q-FAT adapts the Generative Infinite-Vocabulary Transformer (GIVT) architecture from image generation to continuous-control behavioral cloning, replacing the VQ-VAE or k-means discretization step used by BeT and VQ-BeT with a direct Gaussian Mixture Model head on a decoder-only transformer. This removes the non-differentiable quantization bottleneck that has complicated prior autoregressive policy training, while retaining the autoregressive sequence-modeling benefits and explicit likelihood access that diffusion policies lack. The mean-shift-based mode-finding sampling algorithm draws on classical GMM mode-finding literature (Carreira-Perpinan, 2000) and is novel in its application to stabilizing robot action rollouts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating action quantization, Q-FAT simplifies the behavioral cloning pipeline (single-stage training, no separate VQ-VAE) while matching or exceeding SOTA on standard robotics benchmarks and running ~2× faster at inference than VQ-BeT. The explicit GMM likelihood also opens the door to downstream RL fine-tuning and uncertainty-aware safety checks that diffusion policies cannot easily provide. Key open questions include extending to Riemannian action spaces (e.g., humanoid joints), integrating Q-FAT into end-to-end RL pipelines, and exploiting the observed ~70% unimodality of trajectories for further computational savings.
