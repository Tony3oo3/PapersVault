## Problem

Existing trajectory models suffer from three coupled limitations: they are designed for single tasks, trained on region-specific data that fails to transfer geographically, and are fragile to the noise, irregular sampling, and missing points inherent in real-world GPS traces. No universal foundation model exists that can operate across diverse trajectory tasks, geographic regions, and data quality levels without extensive re-engineering.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251407/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes raw GPS trajectories as sequences of (latitude, longitude, timestamp) tuples, deliberately excluding region-bound features such as POIs or road networks to preserve cross-region portability. Coordinates are normalized relative to the trajectory's first point, resampled to 1 Hz, and further processed by adaptive resampling before embedding. The primary output is a d-dimensional trajectory representation that, through task-specific adapters, yields reconstructed masked coordinates, predicted future positions, travel-mode labels, or generated trajectories.

## Method

UniTraj is an encoder-decoder Transformer that embeds spatial (1D convolution on normalized lat/lng) and temporal (linear layer on time intervals) components separately, then applies Rotary Position Encoding to capture relative positional relationships across variable-length sequences. Pre-training uses a masked-autoencoding objective with four complementary masking strategies—random, block, key-points (via Ramer-Douglas-Peucker), and last-N—each simulating a different real-world data-incompleteness scenario. A key architectural innovation is Adaptive Trajectory Resampling, which combines a logarithmic multi-scale resampling function (preserving detail in short trajectories while compressing long ones) with interval-consistent resampling (imposing uniform Δt to decouple temporal sampling from region). The model is pre-trained on WorldTrace, a 2.45-million-trajectory, 8.8-billion-point dataset spanning 70 countries sourced from OpenStreetMap, and then zero-shot applied or fine-tuned with lightweight adapters for downstream tasks.

## Results

On the trajectory recovery task across six datasets, UniTraj (zero-shot) achieves MAE of 10.22 m on WorldTrace, 11.98 m on Chengdu, and 8.93 m on Xi'an, substantially outperforming the best baseline TrajFM (47.64, 19.10, 18.86 m respectively). After fine-tuning, UniTraj further reduces MAE to 6.94 m (WorldTrace), 6.92 m (Chengdu), 6.50 m (Xi'an), 23.23 m (GeoLife), 48.95 m (Grab-Posisi), and 60.18 m (Porto), achieving the best result on all six datasets. On trajectory prediction, UniTraj (fine-tune) reaches MAE of 30.10 m on WorldTrace and 28.78 m on Chengdu, improving 45.95% and 20.74% over the best baselines respectively. For travel-mode classification, UniTraj achieves 71.3% zero-shot accuracy on GeoLife and 79.3% fine-tuned accuracy on Grab-Posisi.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the vanilla Transformer baseline, UniTraj (zero-shot) achieves dramatically lower MAE on all six datasets (e.g., 10.22 vs. 130.82 on WorldTrace, 8.93 vs. 45.85 on Xi'an), confirming that the adaptive resampling and masking strategies are critical beyond a plain attention architecture. DeepMove, an attentional recurrent network, is consistently outperformed by UniTraj zero-shot across all datasets (e.g., 10.22 vs. 51.16 on WorldTrace, 8.93 vs. 27.31 on Xi'an). TrajBERT, a BERT-based trajectory recovery model, is the strongest baseline on GeoLife (MAE 34.53) and Grab-Posisi (MAE 112.68), where UniTraj zero-shot is slightly worse (37.21 and 114.07 respectively), but UniTraj dominates on WorldTrace (10.22 vs. 58.13), Chengdu (11.98 vs. 26.48), and Xi'an (8.93 vs. 19.45). TrajFM, the most recent trajectory foundation model, is outperformed by UniTraj zero-shot on WorldTrace (10.22 vs. 47.64), Chengdu (11.98 vs. 19.10), Xi'an (8.93 vs. 18.86), and GeoLife (37.21 vs. 59.34), but UniTraj trails on Grab-Posisi (114.07 vs. 107.64) and Porto (78.28 vs. 71.15). After fine-tuning, UniTraj achieves the best MAE on all six datasets against every baseline, with the largest gains on WorldTrace (6.94 vs. 47.64 for TrajFM, an 85.4% improvement) and Grab-Posisi (48.95 vs. 107.64, a 54.5% improvement).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper transplants the foundation model paradigm (masked auto-encoding from BERT/MAE, RoPE from RoFormer) into trajectory modeling, departing from prior trajectory-specific models (TrajGDM, BigCity, TrajFM) that rely on region-bound features like POIs and road networks. It also extends spatio-temporal foundation models (UniST, Opencity, ClimaX) by explicitly targeting region-independence through a minimal input representation (lat/lon/timestamp only) and adaptive resampling to handle heterogeneous sampling rates. The WorldTrace dataset addresses a data gap identified in prior work, where existing collections (GeoLife, Porto, T-Drive) are geographically limited or proprietary, by providing an open, globally distributed resource sourced from OpenStreetMap.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The open release of WorldTrace (2.45M trajectories, 70 countries, 8.8B GPS points) is a significant resource for the trajectory research community, enabling cross-regional studies that were previously infeasible due to data access restrictions. The demonstrated zero-shot generalization—outperforming fine-tuned baselines on unseen regions—validates the foundation model approach for trajectory data and suggests a path toward unified mobility intelligence. Key limitations include coverage of only motorized movement (no walking/cycling), exclusion of contextual information (road networks, POIs) that could improve accuracy, and the finding that simply scaling data volume beyond ~1M trajectories introduces noise that slightly degrades performance, pointing to open questions about optimal data curation strategies for foundation models.
