## Problem

Existing OCR benchmarks for Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) are narrow in task diversity, scenario coverage, and difficulty, often leading to rapid performance saturation. They under-explore challenging capabilities such as text localization, handwritten content extraction, and logical reasoning over visual text, making it difficult to diagnose where LMMs truly fail in practical OCR settings.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253253/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each evaluation instance pairs a text-rich image (bilingual English/Chinese, spanning 31 scenarios from printed books to web content) with a natural-language instruction. Outputs are task-dependent: free-form text answers, bounding-box coordinates normalized to a 0–1000 scale, structured documents in HTML/Markdown/JSON/LaTeX, or numerical counts. The benchmark comprises 10,000 public QA pairs plus a held-out private test set of 1,500 manually annotated images to guard against training-data contamination.

## Method

OCRBench v2 organizes 23 concrete tasks under 8 core OCR capabilities (text recognition, text referring, text spotting, relation extraction, element parsing, mathematical calculation, visual text understanding, and knowledge reasoning) drawn from 81 curated academic datasets supplemented with private data. Six task-specific evaluation metrics are applied: TEDS for structured parsing, IoU for localization, F1 for key-information extraction, BLEU/METEOR/F1/edit-distance for long-text reading, L1-normalized distance for counting, and exact-match/ANLS for basic VQA. Data quality is ensured through a three-annotator cross-validation protocol (15 professional annotators, ~1% correction rate) and explicit annotation guidelines for coordinate normalization, reading order, and multi-format numeric answers. The benchmark deliberately focuses on single-image tasks to isolate text-centric perception and reasoning from long-sequence modeling, and includes a non-public 1,500-image test set with a maintained leaderboard to prevent overfitting.

## Results

On OCRBench v2's English public test set (100-point scale), most LMMs score below 50, with InternVL3-14B leading at 52.6 and Gemini-Pro at 51.9. Text Spotting is the most universally weak capability, with 11 of 18 models scoring below 2 and only Gemini-Pro (13.5) and InternVL3-14B (11.2) exceeding 10. Text Recognition is the strongest area (range: 35.2–73.2), while Element Parsing remains challenging (range: 7.6–44.8). On the Chinese public set, performance drops dramatically for most models, with only Qwen2.5-VL-7B (55.6) and InternVL3-14B (55.7) exceeding 50, while GPT-4o falls to 32.2.

## Baselines & Comparisons

As a benchmark paper, OCRBench v2 evaluates 18 LMMs rather than proposing a new method. InternVL3-14B achieves the highest overall score (52.6) among open-source models, leading in Text Referring (36.9), Text Spotting (11.2), Relation Extraction (89.0), and Visual Text Understanding (79.2). Gemini-Pro is the top closed-source model (51.9), uniquely strong in Text Spotting (13.5) and Mathematical Calculation (47.7). Ovis2-8B (47.7) leads in Text Recognition (73.2), Element Parsing (44.8), and Knowledge Reasoning (62.6), making it the most balanced open-source model. GPT-4o (46.5) trails InternVL3-14B by 6.1 points overall and scores 0 on Text Spotting, while Qwen2.5-VL-7B (46.7) matches it closely. The weakest models—Monkey (23.1) and TextMonkey (23.9)—score near zero on Text Referring and Spotting, highlighting that OCR-free architectures remain poorly suited for localization tasks. On the Chinese subset, the gap widens: GPT-4o drops to 32.2 and most open-source models fall below 20, with only Qwen2.5-VL-7B and InternVL3-14B maintaining scores above 55.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends OCRBench [14], the first dedicated benchmark for evaluating LMM OCR capabilities, by expanding from 5 tasks/~14 scenarios to 23 tasks/31 scenarios with bilingual (English-Chinese) coverage and structured output evaluation (HTML, Markdown, JSON, LaTeX). It departs from scenario-specific benchmarks (DocVQA, ChartQA, TextVQA) that were originally designed for traditional OCR systems, instead framing evaluation around eight core LMM competencies including text spotting, relation extraction, and knowledge reasoning that prior benchmarks largely ignored. The unified multi-task design also contrasts with the fragmented evaluation landscape where each scenario (tables, charts, documents) required separate task-specific pipelines, as illustrated in the paper's Fig. 2.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals that most state-of-the-art LMMs score below 50/100, with text spotting essentially unsolved (near-zero scores for nearly all models including GPT-4o) and element parsing remaining weak, challenging the narrative that LMMs have 'solved' OCR given their 96%+ scores on DocVQA. The consistent performance trends across public and private test sets validate the benchmark's reliability against data contamination. Open questions include how to improve spatial grounding in LMMs (IoU scores remain below 13 even for the best models), how to handle complex layouts (rotated, overlapping text), and whether the large gap between high-frequency and low-frequency text recognition can be closed through better pretraining data coverage.
