## Problem

Sequential Federated Learning (SFL) improves data utilization over Parallel FL but suffers from two critical failures under real-world Non-IID conditions: update order sensitivity, where different client traversal sequences lead to drastically different (sometimes saddle-point-trapped) performance, and catastrophic forgetting, where the model loses previously learned features when moving to new client domains. Existing PFL methods avoid these issues but cannot fully exploit all client data since each local model only sees its own data.

## Input / Output

Each client contributes local RGB image data with class labels (e.g., 32×32 CIFAR-10/100, 224×224 PACS domains: Photo, Art, Cartoon, Sketch). A key design choice is that SPFL does not require clients to upload dataset sizes for aggregation weighting, since the sequential traversal treats all client datasets as equal-size passes, offering stronger privacy. The output is a single global model producing class predictions, with the GLAM module additionally maintaining a global model and a previous-client model at each client for inference-time alignment during training.

## Method

SPFL runs M parallel sequential chains, each starting from a different client and traversing all M clients in order; after each chain completes, the server performs arithmetic (unweighted) aggregation of the M endpoint models to form the next global model. This hybrid design eliminates update-order sensitivity by averaging over all starting points while preserving the data-utilization benefit of sequential traversal. To combat catastrophic forgetting under domain shift, the GLAM module adds two auxiliary losses to the standard cross-entropy: a mutual cross-entropy term (L_ag) aligning the local model's output distribution with the global model's, and a symmetric KL-divergence term (L_ap) aligning it with the previous client's model output. Convergence analysis under strong convex, general convex, and non-convex settings proves SPFL achieves a tighter variance bound of Õ(1/(M²KR)) versus Õ(1/(MKR)) for PFL, meaning more clients accelerate convergence. The framework is plug-and-play, integrating directly into existing PFL optimizers (FedAvg, SCAFFOLD, FedDyn, etc.) as an alternative update strategy.

## Results

Under category shift, integrating SPFL into existing PFL methods yields an average accuracy gain of ~3–5% across CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, CINIC-10, and Fashion-MNIST (e.g., SCAFFOLD improves from 62.31% to 67.26% avg, +4.95%). Under domain shift on PACS, SPFL-GLAM achieves 77.87% average accuracy, outperforming the best PFL baseline (AM at 77.08%) and the sequential-only FedSeq (60.04%). Under IID settings on CIFAR-10, SPFL delivers an average improvement of over 6% (e.g., FedDC jumps from 81.75% to 93.08%, +11.33%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SPFL consistently outperforms all 8 PFL baselines (FedAvg, FedDC, FedDyn, FedNova, FedProx, MOON, SCAFFOLD, FedDisco) under category shift, with the largest gains on SCAFFOLD (+4.95% avg) and FedDC (+4.57% avg). Under domain shift on PACS, SPFL-GLAM (77.87% avg) surpasses all PFL baselines including AM (77.08%), FedAvg (76.32%), and FedProx (76.64%), and dramatically outperforms the sequential-only FedSeq (60.04%) by 17.83%. Against domain generalization methods, SPFL-GLAM beats FedDG-GA (73.44%) by 4.43% and CCNet (75.73%) by 2.14%, while Ditto (74.63%) and RSC (76.05%) also fall short. Under IID on CIFAR-10, SPFL improves every baseline, with FedDC showing the largest gain (+11.33%) and FedDyn the smallest (+1.11%), indicating SPFL's benefit is most pronounced when the base method has weaker data utilization. The paper also shows that even doubling PFL's local epochs (21 vs. 10) still leaves PFL 0.6% below SPFL, confirming the advantage is structural rather than computational.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the foundational FedAvg parallel aggregation paradigm and the convergence analysis framework for sequential federated learning established by Li & Lyu (NeurIPS 2024). It departs from both PFL and SFL by proposing a hybrid update strategy: sequential traversal of client data within each round, followed by parallel aggregation across different starting points. The GLAM module repurposes knowledge distillation techniques (mutual cross-entropy and symmetric KL divergence) from continual learning to address catastrophic forgetting in the sequential setting, leveraging the unique property that SPFL clients retain access to both the global and previous-client models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SPFL offers a modular, plug-in update framework that can be integrated into existing PFL methods (FedAvg, SCAFFOLD, FedDyn, etc.) without architectural changes, making it practically accessible. The tighter convergence bound (improving the variance term from O(1/(MKR)) to O(1/(M²KR))) provides theoretical justification for the hybrid approach. However, the 10× computational overhead at 1 epoch per client remains a practical barrier, and domain shift performance still lags slightly below the PFL baseline even with GLAM. Open questions include scalability to hundreds of clients in real deployment and whether the sequential traversal cost is justified when clients have highly heterogeneous compute budgets.
