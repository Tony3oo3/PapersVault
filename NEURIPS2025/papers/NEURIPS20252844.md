## Problem

Existing Transformer-based LTSF models suffer from two underexplored limitations: continuous patching mixes period-aligned and period-offset time steps within each patch, creating chaotic internal correlations that hinder periodic pattern learning; and standard positional encodings (sinusoidal or timestamp-based) impose weak inductive bias for periodicity, causing attention to decay with absolute temporal distance rather than the cyclical distance that actually governs periodic correlations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252844/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a univariate time series look-back window X ∈ R^T and a period length P (typically P ≪ T, e.g., 24 for hourly daily-period data) as input, and produces L future values. The input is restructured into a 2D tensor of shape P × ⌈T/P⌉ via discrete periodic sampling, where incomplete leading periods are left-padded with data from the first complete period. For multivariate series (up to 862 channels in Traffic), channel-independent learning is applied with dynamically reweighted L1 loss across channels.

## Method

MoFo introduces two core components. First, Period-structured Patching discretely samples period-aligned time steps to form a 2D tensor where each row (patch) contains only phase-aligned steps across periods, enabling a simple MLP to directly capture periodic correlations, while columns align period-offset steps for cross-patch trend modeling. Second, a Period-Aware Modulator injects strong inductive bias into multi-head attention by computing periodic relative distances γ_ij = min{(i−j) mod P, (j−i) mod P} and applying a Regulated Relaxation Function S(γ; α, β) — a smooth, differentiable approximation of the Heaviside step function with learnable parameters α (attenuation steepness) and β (distance threshold) — as a log-space additive term in the attention scores. The full architecture uses a single pre-norm Transformer layer with SwiGLU feedforward and RMSNorm, achieving quadratic complexity in P but independence from sequence length T, yielding up to 14× memory savings and 10× faster training versus SOTA baselines.

## Results

On 8 benchmark datasets (ETTh1/h2, ETTm1/m2, Weather, Solar, Electricity, Traffic) with forecasting horizons L∈{96,192,336,720}, MoFo achieves the best or near-best MSE across most settings. For example, on Electricity L=96 MoFo reaches MSE 0.122 / MAE 0.215, on ETTm2 L=96 it reaches MSE 0.155 / MAE 0.240, and on Traffic L=720 it reaches MSE 0.424. In terms of efficiency, MoFo uses only 2.40M parameters, 76.74B FLOPs, 4,988 MB peak memory, and 47 s per epoch on Traffic L=720—roughly 4.7× fewer parameters, 12.7× fewer FLOPs, 15× less memory, and 11× faster training than DUET, and up to 14× memory savings and 10× faster training versus PatchTST.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MoFo is compared against 17 baselines spanning Transformer-based (DUET, iTransformer, PatchTST, Crossformer, Pathformer, FEDformer, Informer), MLP-based (DLinear, FITS, FiLM), and TCN-based (TimesNet, MICN) methods. Against the strongest baseline DUET (2025), MoFo wins on ETTm1, ETTm2, Weather, Electricity, and Traffic across most horizons, but DUET edges out MoFo on ETTh1 (e.g., MSE 0.352 vs 0.360 at L=96; 0.429 vs 0.447 at L=720) and ETTh2 L=96 (0.270 vs 0.273). Against PDF (2024), MoFo achieves lower MSE on 12 of 16 dataset-horizon combinations in the main table. Against iTransformer, PatchTST, and DLinear, MoFo consistently achieves the best or second-best MSE across all 8 datasets and 4 horizons. The largest margins appear on Traffic (MSE 0.424 vs 0.435 for DUET/PatchTST) and Electricity L=96 (0.122 vs 0.128 for DUET). In efficiency, MoFo reduces FLOPs by >10× and memory by >14× relative to DUET and PatchTST while matching or exceeding their accuracy, and its complexity scales quadratically with period length P (e.g., 24 for hourly daily data) rather than with input sequence length T, enabling <10% memory increase even when T grows 100×.

## Relation to Prior Work

MoFo builds on the standard Transformer architecture but departs fundamentally from the continuous patching paradigm adopted by PatchTST, Pyraformer, Crossformer, and Pathformer, instead proposing period-structured discrete patching that groups only phase-aligned time steps into each patch. It also replaces sinusoidal or timestamp-based positional encodings with a learnable regulated relaxation function that modulates attention based on periodic relative distances, addressing the permutation-invariance limitation of self-attention in a way distinct from CycleNet's explicit periodicity decomposition or DUET's clustering-based approach. The theoretical grounding via Theorem 1 (smooth approximation of the Heaviside step function with a vanishing cumulative error bound) provides a principled justification for the modulator design that prior periodicity-aware methods lack.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The most practically significant contribution is the computational efficiency: MoFo's complexity depends quadratically on period length (e.g., 24) rather than sequence length (e.g., 720+), yielding up to 14× memory savings and 10× faster training, with scalability to 10K-length inputs showing <10% memory increase. This makes long-sequence forecasting feasible on modest hardware, which is a real bottleneck in deployment. However, the method requires a known or estimable period length P, and its performance gains over the strongest baseline (DUET) are modest on most datasets, suggesting the periodicity-explicit modeling helps most when the signal is strongly and regularly periodic. Open questions include handling multi-periodic or drifting-period signals, and whether the single-layer sufficiency finding generalizes to more complex multivariate interactions.
