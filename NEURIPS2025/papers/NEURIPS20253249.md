## Problem

ControlNet++ enforces pixel-level cycle consistency for controllability but can only optimize low-noise timesteps (t<200) because back-propagating through the full diffusion sampling process is computationally infeasible. Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) offers a timestep-agnostic alternative, but constructing win-lose image pairs that differ only in controllability is unreliable—irrelevant visual factors (artifacts, color, style) introduce noise into the training signal, and some control types (e.g., Canny edges) are nearly imperceptible in raw pixels.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253249/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt and a dense spatial control signal—segmentation map, depth map, Canny/HED/Lineart edge map, or human pose keypoints—as conditioning input, and produces a 512×512 RGB image via a Stable Diffusion 1.5 backbone with a ControlNet or ControlNet++ adapter. All evaluations use the UniPC sampler with 20 inference steps and a fixed classifier-free guidance scale (7.5 for SD1.5-based comparisons, 4.0 for ControlAR comparisons). The CPO dataset stores triplets of (image, winning condition, losing condition), where the losing condition is a perturbed version of the ground-truth condition extracted from a single generated image.

## Method

CPO inverts the DPO formulation: instead of comparing two generated images under the same condition, it compares two conditions under the same fixed image. The winning condition c^w is the ground-truth control signal, and the losing condition c^l is obtained by generating one image from c^w and re-extracting the condition from that image using a detector. The training objective is a DPO-style contrastive loss (Eq. 7) where the model learns to assign higher likelihood to x_0 given c^w than c^l, but the final implementation (Eq. 10) replaces the sigmoid contrastive form with a triplet-margin loss using a stop-gradient sign term to stabilize training and avoid over-contrast. A pretrained diffusion loss is added as regularization to prevent drift from the base model. Theoretically, CPO has strictly lower loss variance than DPO because fixing the image eliminates nuisance variance from unrelated visual factors, and empirically it requires only one image generation per training example versus ~20 for DPO, reducing both compute and storage costs.

## Results

CPO achieves state-of-the-art controllability across all evaluated condition types: 44.81 mIoU on ADE20K segmentation (vs. 43.64 for ControlNet++), 87.98 mAP on COCO-Pose (vs. 72.53 for ControlNet), 39.28 F1 on Canny edges, 0.8447 SSIM on Lineart, and 27.49 RMSE on depth. This translates to over 10% error-rate reduction on segmentation, 70–80% on human pose, and consistent 2–5% reductions on edge and depth maps relative to the oracle upper bound. On FID/CLIP, CPO matches or slightly improves upon ControlNet++ (e.g., 30.30 FID / 31.97 CLIP on ADE20K vs. 30.24 / 31.96), confirming that controllability gains do not degrade generation quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ControlNet++ (the primary baseline), CPO wins on every controllability metric: +1.17 mIoU on ADE20K, +0.93 on COCO-Stuff, +1.25 F1 on Canny, +0.010 SSIM on HED/Lineart, and −0.83 RMSE on depth, while FID/CLIP remain essentially unchanged. Against ControlNet, gains are much larger (e.g., +15.43 mAP on COCO-Pose, +4.63 F1 on Canny, −8.41 RMSE on depth). Against ControlAR (the strongest non-diffusion baseline), CPO wins on ADE20K mIoU (44.81 vs. 39.95), both pose datasets, Canny F1, HED/Lineart SSIM, and depth RMSE, but loses on COCO-Stuff mIoU (35.49 vs. 37.49); CPO also significantly outperforms ControlAR on CLIP scores (e.g., 31.97 vs. 31.09 on ADE20K) while matching FID. Against DPO (evaluated on COCO-Pose in Fig. 6), CPO achieves lower FID at every matched mAP level, confirming the lower-variance training objective. Against GLIGEN, CPO wins on HumanArt pose (45.71 vs. 39.88 mAP) and all edge/depth metrics. Against UniControl, Uni-ControlNet, and T2I-Adapter, CPO dominates across all reported metrics by wide margins.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two lines of work: controllable diffusion generation (ControlNet → ControlNet++) and preference-based fine-tuning for diffusion models (Diffusion-DPO). ControlNet++ introduced a cycle-consistency loss to enforce controllability but is restricted to low-noise timesteps due to the infeasibility of back-propagating through full sampling. Diffusion-DPO provides a timestep-agnostic preference objective but operates over generated image pairs, which introduces confounding visual factors. CPO's key conceptual departure is to flip the preference formulation: instead of comparing two images under one condition, it compares two conditions under one fixed image, yielding a lower-variance training signal that can be applied at arbitrary timesteps.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CPO offers a practical, low-cost fine-tuning recipe that substantially improves controllability (70–80% error reduction in pose, ~10% in segmentation) without degrading FID or CLIP scores, and it requires only a single generated image per training example versus ~20 for DPO. The paper also surfaces an open evaluation question: controllability, FID, and CLIP all shift with the CFG scale, making fair cross-method comparison non-trivial. Limitations include evaluation only on SD1.5 at 512×512, and the method has not yet been tested on newer architectures (e.g., SDXL, FLUX) or multi-resolution settings that ControlAR supports.
