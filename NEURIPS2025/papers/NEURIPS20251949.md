## Problem

COLMAP, the predominant method for camera parameter optimization, requires ground-truth motion masks to handle dynamic scenes and suffers from lengthy runtime that scales exponentially with video length. Existing alternatives improve upon COLMAP by incorporating additional GT priors (focal length, metric depth, 3D point clouds, camera poses, motion masks) that are typically unavailable in casually captured RGB videos. The paper asks whether accurate and efficient camera parameter optimization in dynamic scenes is possible under RGB-only supervision — the most minimal form of supervision.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251949/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single casually captured RGB video (frame sizes range from 480×270 to 854×480 depending on dataset, with 50–900 frames) and assumes a constant focal length throughout. It outputs per-frame camera parameters (a shared focal length f, per-frame rotation as a 4D quaternion, and per-frame 3D translation), H 3D calibration points in world coordinates, and H positive uncertainty parameters Γ (one per calibration point, not per pixel). The camera estimates are then fed into a 4DGS reconstruction pipeline to evaluate NVS quality via PSNR/SSIM/LPIPS, or directly compared to GT poses via ATE/RPE on MPI-Sintel and TUM-dynamics.

## Method

ROS-Cam consists of three components. First, patch-wise tracking filters built on CoTracker extract robust, maximally sparse hinge-like trajectories by sequentially applying a texture filter (selecting high-variance w×w patches), a gradient filter (picking the highest-gradient pixel per patch), a visibility filter (removing occluded trajectories), and a distribution filter (keeping only the strongest point per patch to enforce even spatial coverage). Second, an outlier-aware joint optimization jointly optimizes calibration points, focal length, quaternion rotations, translations, and per-point Cauchy-distribution scale parameters Γ using a novel Average Cumulative Projection (ACP) error and Cauchy negative-log-likelihood loss, which adaptively down-weights moving outliers without any GT motion mask. Third, a two-stage strategy exploits the Softplus asymptotic behavior (Stage 1 fixes Γ^raw=1 for fast convergence) and the analytical convex minimum of the inner loss term (Stage 2 initializes Γ^raw to the Stage-1 ACP error before jointly optimizing all parameters), avoiding local minima and accelerating convergence.

## Results

On NeRF-DS, ROS-Cam achieves 33.55 PSNR / 0.938 SSIM / 0.118 LPIPS, outperforming COL w/mask (32.17/0.923/0.147) and COL w/o mask (29.35/0.875/0.224). On DAVIS, it reaches 22.29 PSNR / 0.709 SSIM / 0.279 LPIPS, substantially beating COL w/o mask (9.20/0.236/0.435) and casualSAM (19.03/0.486/0.482). On TUM-dynamics (direct camera pose evaluation), ROS-Cam achieves ATE 0.065, RPE trans 0.010, RPE rot 0.987, surpassing all RGB-only baselines and approaching GT-supervised methods like Mast3r (ATE 0.038). Runtime is 0.83 h on NeRF-DS, 0.03 h on DAVIS, and 0.33 h on iPhone — roughly 55%, 11%, and 8% of the next-fastest method respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ROS-Cam wins decisively against all RGB-only supervised baselines: on NeRF-DS it beats COL w/o mask by +4.2 PSNR and casualSAM by +12.3 PSNR; on DAVIS it beats COL w/o mask by +13.1 PSNR and casualSAM by +3.3 PSNR. On TUM-dynamics, it outperforms casualSAM (ATE 0.065 vs 0.071; RPE rot 0.987 vs 1.712) and Robust-CVD (ATE 0.065 vs 0.153; RPE rot 0.987 vs 3.528), while remaining competitive with GT-supervised methods like Cut3r (ATE 0.046) and Mast3r (ATE 0.038) that require GT 3D point clouds and camera poses. On the iPhone dataset, ROS-Cam is the best RGB-only method and occasionally surpasses the LiDAR-based Record3D app (e.g., 28.42 vs 27.12 PSNR on Space-out; 34.56 vs 34.34 on Mochi-high-five), though it trails Record3D on some scenes with smaller motion. In runtime, ROS-Cam is 2.2× faster than COL w/o mask on NeRF-DS, 17× faster on DAVIS, and 29× faster on iPhone, with near-linear scaling versus COLMAP's exponential growth.

## Relation to Prior Work

ROS-Cam builds on CoTracker's pre-trained point tracking model but departs from prior RGB-only supervised methods (casualSAM, Robust-CVD) by replacing dense prediction-based pseudo-supervision with sparse patch-wise tracking filters, and by associating Cauchy-distributed uncertainty with 3D calibration points rather than 2D pixel maps. It positions itself against the COLMAP lineage, which dominates static-scene SfM but requires GT motion masks for dynamic scenes, and against methods that add GT focal length, metric depth, or 3D point cloud supervision. The two-stage optimization strategy, grounded in the asymptotic analysis of Softplus and the convex minima of the Cauchy NLL, is a novel contribution not present in prior bundle-adjustment-style camera optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By achieving the best RGB-only supervised camera estimates on five datasets while running 5–10× faster than the next-best method and scaling linearly with frame count (vs. exponential for COLMAP), this work makes high-quality dynamic scene reconstruction from casual videos practically feasible without expensive sensors or GT annotations. Key open questions remain: the constant-focal-length assumption excludes zooming videos, and scenes dominated by large moving objects (where most tracked trajectories are outliers) still degrade performance. The finding that the method is nearly independent of the underlying PT model (CoTracker vs. CoTracker3) suggests the patch-wise filtering, not the tracker, is the critical component — a useful insight for future work.
