## Problem

Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) has shown strong results for text-based LLM reasoning but its application to VideoLLMs remains underexplored. The paper identifies two specific failures: (1) PPO-style clipping and min operations suppress gradients when the policy drifts from the old policy, impeding convergence, and (2) a vanishing advantage problem where samples that are too easy or too hard produce uniform rewards, yielding zero advantages and erasing the training signal entirely.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254626/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes video clips (up to 16 frames at 252×252 resolution, with an additional current-observation frame appended) paired with a text question. Outputs are structured as a reasoning trace enclosed in  tags followed by a final answer in <answer>...</answer> tags; for temporal grounding tasks (NExTGQA), the answer includes start/end timestamps scored by IoU. A composite reward combines format compliance, answer accuracy, and temporal IoU depending on the task.

## Method

Reg-GRPO reformulates the GRPO objective as a regression problem: instead of maximizing a clipped surrogate policy ratio weighted by advantage, it minimizes the squared gap between a target advantage (group-normalized reward) and a predicted advantage (group-normalized log-probability ratio of the current policy over the old policy). This derivation starts from the closed-form KL-constrained reward-maximization solution, showing that the partition function cancels during group normalization, so the model can directly regress advantages without clipping or min operations. A difficulty-aware data augmentation module measures each sample's difficulty relative to a replay buffer of recent rewards; easy samples receive frame-level Gaussian noise (difficulty-increasing) while hard samples receive partial reasoning traces extracted from successful trajectories injected into the prompt (difficulty-decreasing), with perturbation strength scaled adaptively by the difficulty magnitude.

## Results

On SEED-Bench-R1, DeepVideo-R1-3B (Qwen2.5-VL-3B) achieves 48.1 / 51.1 / 44.0 on SBR-L1/L2/L3, improving over GRPO by +8.5 / +10.1 / +8.6 points respectively. On LongVideoBench, DeepVideo-R1-3B reaches 48.4 overall (vs. 43.2 for GRPO), with a +7.4 gain on the longest duration range (900–3600 s). On NExTGQA, DeepVideo-R1-7B achieves 36.8 mIoU and 72.5 Acc@QA, outperforming VideoChat-R1-thinking by +0.7 mIoU and +3.3 Acc@QA. Across general video benchmarks (Table 2), DeepVideo-R1-3B improves TempCompass from 29.7 to 63.1 and Video-MMMU from 36.1 to 40.7 over the base Qwen2.5-VL-3B.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DeepVideo-R1 consistently outperforms GRPO across all model sizes and difficulty levels: on Qwen2.5-VL-3B, gains range from +8.5 (SBR-L1) to +10.1 (SBR-L2) points, and on Qwen2.5-VL-7B from +2.9 to +5.5 points. Against SFT, improvements are even larger (+12.2 to +16.1 on SBR for 3B), confirming that RL-based post-training with Reg-GRPO substantially exceeds supervised fine-tuning. On LongVideoBench, DeepVideo-R1-3B surpasses GRPO by +5.2 overall and by +7.4 on the longest temporal range, while also beating the base model by +8.0. On NExTGQA, DeepVideo-R1-7B (36.8 mIoU, 72.5 Acc@QA) beats both VideoChat-R1 (32.4/70.6) and VideoChat-R1-thinking (36.1/69.2), and also outperforms all vision-expert baselines (IGV, Temp[CLIP], FrozenBiLM, SeViLA). On general video benchmarks (Table 2), DeepVideo-R1-3B wins on 5 of 6 datasets versus Qwen2.5-VL-3B, with the largest gain on TempCompass (+33.4), but slightly trails on Video-MME (51.1 vs. 54.4). Compared to larger models like VILA-1.5-40B and Kangaroo-8B, DeepVideo-R1-3B achieves competitive or superior scores on VSI-Bench, Video-MMMU, and MMVU despite being significantly smaller.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the GRPO lineage (PPO → GRPO) by reformulating the group-relative policy optimization objective as a regression task that directly predicts group-normalized advantages, drawing on the closed-form optimal policy derivation from DPO and the regression-based RL perspective of REBEL. The key departure is eliminating PPO-style clipping and min operations—standard safeguards in GRPO—in favor of a simple MSE loss between target and predicted advantages, which the authors argue suppresses gradients and impedes convergence. A second contribution, difficulty-aware data augmentation, addresses the vanishing advantage problem (zero gradients when all group rewards are equal) by adaptively perturbing easy samples or injecting reasoning cues into hard samples, a strategy not previously applied to group-based RL for VideoLLMs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The regression reformulation offers a cleaner, more interpretable alternative to GRPO that removes heuristic stabilizers while maintaining (or improving) training stability, which could be adopted broadly for any group-based RL fine-tuning of multimodal models. However, the evidence is limited to Qwen2-VL and Qwen2.5-VL families with single runs and no error bars, leaving open whether the gains generalize to other architectures or scale regimes. The difficulty-aware augmentation, while effective, relies on a replay buffer and per-sample reward computation that adds practical overhead. An open question is whether the regression formulation's advantage over GRPO persists as model scale increases or when reward signals become noisier in more complex video tasks.
