## Problem

Existing data assimilation methods face a fundamental trade-off: model-driven approaches (Kalman filters, particle filters) require exact knowledge of the governing dynamics and scale poorly in high dimensions, while data-driven surrogates either produce deterministic forecasts that ignore intrinsic stochasticity (neural operators) or rely on global noise-to-data diffusion paths that lack stepwise physical consistency (score-based diffusion models). No prior framework learns a local, probabilistic state-transition model that simultaneously assimilates new observations on the fly without retraining or auxiliary filtering steps.

## Input / Output

FlowDAS takes a sequence of L previous states (L=1 for Lorenz-63, L=10 for Navier-Stokes, L=6 for SEVIR weather) as conditioning context, plus a single noisy observation at the target time step that may be downsampled (e.g., 32×32 or 16×16 vorticity fields), sparsely sampled (5% or 1.5625% of pixels retained), or randomly subsampled (10% of 128×128 VIL grid cells). It outputs an autoregressively generated state trajectory where each step is a probabilistic ensemble of forecasts already consistent with the incoming measurement, eliminating any post-hoc correction step. States range from 3D vectors (Lorenz) to 128×128 2D fields (Navier-Stokes vorticity, SEVIR VIL).

## Method

FlowDAS learns a drift network b_s(X_s, X_0) via supervised regression on the stochastic-interpolant velocity R_s, modeling the local conditional transition p(x_{k+1}|x_k) as a short SDE bridge between adjacent states rather than a global Gaussian-to-data path. At inference, the key innovation is an observation-conditioned drift correction: the original drift is augmented by ∇log p(y|X_s, X_0)/(λ_s β_s) via Bayes' rule, where this intractable gradient is estimated by Monte Carlo marginalization over J posterior endpoint samples X_1^(j) drawn with low-order stochastic integrators (1st-order Milstein or 2nd-order SDE Runge-Kutta), weighted by softmax likelihoods under the measurement model. The SDE is integrated autoregressively over N=500 interpolation steps per time step, applying the weighted observation gradient at every step to enforce measurement consistency throughout the generation. The drift backbone is task-adaptive (MLP for low-dimensional systems, U-Net for fluid fields, FNO for weather), and multi-state conditioning extends the transition to p(x_{k+1}|x_k, x_{k-1}, …, x_{k-L}) to handle non-Markovian dynamics.

## Results

On the Lorenz 1963 system, FlowDAS achieves RMSE 0.202 and Wasserstein-1 distance 0.106, outperforming SDA (RMSE 1.114, W₁ 0.528) and approaching BPF (RMSE 0.270, W₁ 0.812) despite not using the true transition equations. On incompressible Navier-Stokes super-resolution (32²→128²), FlowDAS achieves RMSE 0.077±0.005, substantially beating PDEDiff (0.112), Transolver-DA (0.597), and FNO-DA (0.653). On SEVIR weather forecasting, FlowDAS attains RMSE 0.053±0.004 and CSI(τ₂₀) 0.746±0.022, surpassing PDEDiff (RMSE 0.071, CSI 0.549), Transolver-DA (RMSE 0.062, CSI 0.663), and FNO-DA (RMSE 0.064, CSI 0.641).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FlowDAS consistently outperforms all five baselines across every evaluated task. Against SDA (Rozet & Louppe, NeurIPS 2023), FlowDAS achieves a 5.5× lower RMSE (0.202 vs 1.114) and 5× lower Wasserstein distance on Lorenz 1963, attributed to SDA's reliance on a global noise-to-data diffusion path that obscures stepwise dynamics. Against BPF (Gordon et al., 1993), FlowDAS is competitive on log-prior (17.29 vs 17.88) and achieves lower RMSE (0.202 vs 0.270) and Wasserstein distance (0.106 vs 0.812) despite not having access to the true transition equations that BPF uses. Against PDEDiff (Shysheya et al., NeurIPS 2024), FlowDAS reduces RMSE by 31% on Navier-Stokes 32²→128² (0.077 vs 0.112) and by 25% on SEVIR weather forecasting (0.053 vs 0.071), while also achieving substantially higher CSI scores (0.746 vs 0.549 at τ₂₀). Against the deterministic neural operators Transolver-DA and FNO-DA, FlowDAS achieves 7.7× and 8.5× lower RMSE respectively on the 32²→128² Navier-Stokes task, demonstrating that probabilistic, observation-conditioned generation is far superior to post-hoc Kalman-style reconciliation of a single deterministic forecast.

## Relation to Prior Work

FlowDAS extends the stochastic interpolant framework (Albergo et al. 2022, 2023; Chen et al. ICML 2024), which learns short-step conditional transitions p(x_{k+1}|x_k) via a learned SDE, by augmenting the drift with an observation-conditioned correction term derived from Bayes' rule at each interpolation step. This departs from prior stochastic interpolant forecasting (Chen et al. 2024), which performs probabilistic prediction without incorporating measurements, and from score-based diffusion DA methods (SDA, PDEDiff) that learn a global noise-to-data map over concatenated state windows. The paper also contrasts with deterministic neural operators (FNO, Transolver) that require a separate post-hoc optimization to reconcile predictions with observations, and with model-driven filters (Kalman, BPF) that presuppose known dynamics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FlowDAS provides a unified, modular framework for probabilistic data assimilation that works across dimensions (3D Lorenz to 128×128 weather fields) without requiring explicit PDE knowledge, filling a gap between model-driven methods that need accurate dynamics and data-driven methods that either miss stochasticity or lack stepwise physical consistency. Key open questions include scaling to operational-scale NWP systems, Sim2Real generalization, and the current sampling speed bottleneck (500 interpolation steps × J Monte Carlo samples per time step). The particle-collapse issue in high-dimensional settings and the lack of a principled criterion for the DPS-type cutoff parameter s_c remain practical concerns for broader adoption.
