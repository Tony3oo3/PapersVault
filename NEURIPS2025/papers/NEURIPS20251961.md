## Problem

Post-training LLM compression methods (pruning, quantization) rely on calibration data to guide weight importance and activation range estimation, yet how calibration data characteristics affect capability preservation—especially high-level reasoning like math and code—remains poorly understood. Prior work examined only isolated factors (sample count, source) on basic capabilities, leaving the underlying mechanism and a principled curation strategy unexplored.

## Input / Output

The framework takes as input a pool of available calibration data sources (pre-training corpora such as C4, WikiText, SlimPajama, or domain-specific datasets like MathQA, CodeQA), an uncompressed LLM, and deployment requirements specifying target capabilities and languages. It produces a compact curated calibration set (typically 128 sequences of ~2048 tokens) that is then fed into any standard post-training compression pipeline (GPTQ, AWQ, SparseGPT, Wanda, etc.) to yield a compressed model with improved capability retention across language modeling, commonsense reasoning, math, and code generation.

## Method

COLA is a three-stage, training-free curation pipeline grounded in the insight that representativeness and diversity in the model's activation space fundamentally determine calibration quality. Stage 1 selects source datasets by optimizing a coverage function (cosine embedding similarity + KL token-distribution divergence) against target capability weights, aligning language, subject, and reasoning difficulty to deployment needs. Stage 2 processes selected data by adjusting sequence lengths and enriching formats with explicit reasoning chains (Q&A w/ ERC). Stage 3 performs activation-aware sample selection: a forward pass through the uncompressed model yields per-sample layer-wise hidden-state vectors, which are reduced via random projection and clustered with k-means (k=128, d=64); the sample closest to each cluster centroid is retained, simultaneously maximizing within-cluster representativeness and cross-cluster diversity. A spectral analysis of FFN weights further validates the mechanism, showing that COLA preserves the full frequency spectrum (low: language modeling, mid: general knowledge, high: specialized reasoning) more faithfully than random or Self-Gen calibration, particularly in deeper layers responsible for complex reasoning.

## Results

On general deployment (Table 2), COLA consistently achieves the best performance across all four compression methods (AWQ, SparseGPT, Wanda, GPTQ) and both models (LLaMA3-8B, Qwen2.5-7B) on all four capability metrics (PPL, Commonsense, Math, Code). For example, with AWQ on LLaMA3-8B, COLA achieves 67.42% commonsense, 37.85% math, and 40.17% code (vs. 65.26/36.46/38.71 for WikiText random), and with SparseGPT on LLaMA3-8B it reaches 44.23% commonsense (vs. 41.85% random, 43.61% Self-Gen). Improvements are typically 1–2 percentage points over random sampling and Self-Gen, with pruning methods (SparseGPT, Wanda) showing the largest gains, consistent with their higher sensitivity to calibration data quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares COLA against two types of baselines: (1) random sampling from standard pre-training corpora (WikiText, C4, SlimPajama) and (2) Self-Gen (Ji et al., ICLR 2025), which generates calibration data by sampling from the model's own outputs. COLA wins on every metric across all four compression methods and both models in the general deployment setting (Table 2). Against Self-Gen, the most recent and strongest baseline, COLA gains approximately 0.3–0.6 percentage points on quantization methods (e.g., AWQ LLaMA3-8B: 67.42% CS vs. 67.08%) and 0.6–0.9 points on pruning methods (e.g., SparseGPT LLaMA3-8B: 44.23% CS vs. 43.61%). Against random WikiText sampling, gains are larger: 1.5–2.4 points on commonsense for pruning methods and 0.8–1.2 points for quantization. The paper also validates COLA on targeted deployment (Tables 5–8), where improvements over domain-specific random calibration are more pronounced (e.g., +2.50 pp on math with AWQ for LLaMA3-8B, +3.45 pp on code with AWQ for Qwen2.5-7B), and on larger models up to 70B (Table 9) and additional compression methods including RIA, LLM-Pruner, LLM-Streamline, SmoothQuant, and FlatQuant (Table 10), where COLA again consistently outperforms all baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on calibration data for post-training LLM compression (SparseGPT, GPTQ, AWQ, Wanda) by moving beyond the isolated-property analyses of prior studies—sequence length (Lee et al., 2023), data source (Williams and Aletras, 2024; Bandari et al., 2024), and sample amount (Jaiswal et al., 2024)—into a unified, multi-dimensional treatment that also covers domain correspondence (language, subject, reasoning difficulty) and high-level reasoning capabilities (math, code). Conceptually, it repurposes activation-space clustering (k-means with random projection, borrowed from dimensionality-reduction literature) as a principled sample-selection criterion, grounding the previously ad-hoc calibration data choice in representativeness and diversity principles. The Self-Gen baseline from Ji et al. (ICLR 2025) is the closest prior curation approach, but COLA differs by operating in the model's activation space rather than in generated-text space, and by explicitly optimizing for both representativeness and diversity simultaneously.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Calibration data is a small but critical lever in LLM compression pipelines that has been largely treated as a free parameter; this paper provides the first systematic, mechanism-grounded account of how its properties map to specific capability losses, which is practically valuable for teams deploying compressed models in targeted domains. The activation-space representativeness/diversity framing offers a model-agnostic design principle that could guide future compression methods, and the COLA framework is orthogonal to existing algorithms, making it a drop-in preprocessing step. However, the absolute gains in general deployment are modest (≈1–2 percentage points), the framework adds a forward-pass-plus-clustering overhead, and the study is limited to 7B–8B models in the main experiments (larger models only in appendices), leaving open whether the same curation strategy transfers to much larger or architecturally different models. Future work on compression-method-specific curation and intrinsic model properties is explicitly flagged as needed.
