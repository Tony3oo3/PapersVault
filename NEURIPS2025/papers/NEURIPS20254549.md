## Problem

PnP methods for Poisson inverse problems require strong convexity or smoothness of the fidelity term and a non-expansive (or residual non-expansive) denoiser for convergence guarantees, but the Poisson Bregman fidelity is neither strongly convex nor smooth, and non-expansiveness constraints degrade denoising performance. The paper asks whether a weaker Lipschitz condition on the denoiser can still guarantee proximal-operator structure and convergence while preserving denoising quality.

## Input / Output

The method takes a Poisson-corrupted observation f = Poisson(pKu)/p, a known linear operator K (identity, blur kernel, or Radon transform), and a peak photon value p, and iteratively produces a restored clean image u. The underlying denoiser (DRUNet) is trained on 128×128 patches from DIV2K with Gaussian noise σ ∈ [0, 50], but is deployed within PnP iterations on Poisson-corrupted data at various peak values (p = 50–500). The final output is the last iterate u^K of the CoCo-ADMM or CoCo-PEGD algorithm, initialized with the observed image.

## Method

The paper introduces a cocoercive conservative (CoCo) denoiser built on DRUNet, trained with a composite loss that adds two novel regularization terms to the standard L1 denoising objective: a spectral regularization term ‖2γJ − I‖_* that enforces γ-cocoerciveness (a strictly weaker constraint than non-expansiveness, allowing the denoiser to be expansive when γ < 0.5), and a Hamiltonian regularization term ‖J − Jᵀ‖_* derived from the generalized Helmholtz decomposition that penalizes the divergence-free (Hamiltonian) component of the denoiser's Jacobian to promote conservativeness without requiring an explicit potential function. The key theoretical result proves that a CoCo denoiser is the proximal operator of an implicit weakly convex prior, which yields an implicit non-convex restoration model. Two convergent PnP algorithms—CoCo-ADMM and CoCo-PEGD (using the Moreau envelope to smooth the non-smooth Poisson fidelity)—are derived, with global convergence to a stationary point established under the KL property.

## Results

On photon-limited deconvolution (CBSD68, p=50), CoCo-ADMM achieves 26.00 dB PSNR and 0.7026 SSIM, outperforming the next-best method DPIR (25.38 dB, 0.6910 SSIM) by 0.62 dB. On low-dose CT reconstruction (Mayo dataset, p=500), CoCo-PEGD reaches 37.72 dB PSNR and 0.9391 SSIM, surpassing WNet (37.12 dB, 0.9266 SSIM) by 0.60 dB. For Gaussian denoising on CBSD68, the 0.25-CoCo-DRUNet achieves 34.00/31.38/29.16 dB at σ=15/25/40, remaining within 0.14 dB of the unregularized DRUNet while satisfying the cocoercive-conservative conditions required for convergence guarantees.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoCo-ADMM and CoCo-PEGD consistently outperform all PnP baselines on deconvolution: they beat DPIR by 0.38 dB (p=100) and 0.62 dB (p=50), PnPI-HQS by 0.47 dB and 0.39 dB, and SNORE by 0.56 dB and 0.79 dB. Against diffusion-based methods DPS and DiffPIR, the gains are substantial (3.24 dB and 2.07 dB at p=100 respectively), confirming that the cocoercive-conservative structure is more effective than score-matching for Poisson settings. On CT reconstruction, CoCo-ADMM surpasses WNet by 0.51 dB (p=500) and 0.70 dB (p=100), and outperforms PWLS-CSCGR by 2.18 dB at p=500. For Gaussian denoising, the 0.25-CoCo-DRUNet slightly trails the unregularized DRUNet by ~0.14 dB but significantly outperforms RMMO (firmly non-expansive) by 1.79 dB and Prox-DRUNet (residual non-expansive) by 0.82 dB at σ=15, demonstrating that the weaker cocoercive constraint preserves more denoising capacity. The B-RED baseline, which uses a Bregman/Gamma denoiser, underperforms CoCo-ADMM by 2.80 dB at p=100, validating the paper's claim that Gamma denoisers struggle with large noise.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the PnP framework by introducing cocoerciveness as a strictly weaker alternative to the non-expansiveness and residual non-expansiveness conditions previously required for convergence guarantees (Terris et al. 2020; Hurault et al. 2022, 2024). It departs from prior conservative denoiser constructions (GS-DRUNet, Prox-DRUNet) that build explicit potential functions, instead using the generalized Helmholtz decomposition to enforce conservativeness implicitly via a Hamiltonian regularization term on the Jacobian's antisymmetric part. The spectral regularization technique for cocoerciveness is adapted from the RMMO/SPC literature (Pesquet et al. 2021; Wei et al. 2024), but applied to a new operator class. Theoretically, the proximal characterization builds directly on Gribonval & Nikolova's (2020) generalization of Moreau's theorem to nonconvex settings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The cocoercive framework provides a principled way to relax the restrictive Lipschitz constraints that have limited denoiser expressiveness in convergent PnP methods, while still guaranteeing convergence to a stationary point of an implicit weakly convex restoration model — particularly valuable for Poisson inverse problems where standard convexity/smoothness assumptions on the fidelity fail. The Helmholtz-decomposition-based training strategy offers a practical alternative to explicit potential function construction, though the authors acknowledge it only encourages (not enforces) the desired properties, and the denoiser incurs a small performance penalty relative to unregularized DRUNet. Open questions include how to hard-enforce cocoerciveness and conservativeness without sacrificing denoising quality, and whether the framework extends naturally to non-convex fidelity terms or higher-order noise models.
