## Problem

Existing continual learning strategies primarily manage cross-task interactions through posterior, model-centric mechanisms (regularization, architecture partitioning) that are sensitive to task-sequence noise and prone to negative transfer as task count grows. The paper asks how to efficiently capture and exploit intrinsic inter-task relationships as a *prior* signal to guide task-conditioned model generation, rather than relying on reactive forgetting-mitigation that trades off retention against adaptability.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255568/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes sequential RGB image classification tasks (e.g., 10-way splits of CIFAR-100, 200-class ImageNet-R, or 345-class DomainNet) with task IDs available at both training and inference (task-incremental setting). At each task step, it uses only the current task's training data plus hypernet-reconstructed previous task models to compute H-score transferability scores online—no previous samples are revisited. The output is a set of task-specific model weights (full ResNet-32 parameters or compact LoRA matrices for ViT-B/16) that map images to class labels; the only per-task artifact stored is a 32-dimensional task embedding vector.

## Method

The core contribution is H-embedding, a low-dimensional task representation learned by minimizing the discrepancy between pairwise Euclidean distances among embeddings and AHP-normalized H-score transferability scores (an information-theoretic maximal-correlation metric), solved via gradient descent before each task's training begins. This embedding guides a hypernetwork that generates task-specific weights: an encoder-decoder module attached to the hypernet's intermediate layer enforces that the hidden representation can reconstruct the H-embedding, injecting transferability-aware prior knowledge into weight generation. Training is end-to-end with three loss terms—supervised target loss, a CL regularization loss penalizing weight drift on previous embeddings, and a cosine-similarity guidance loss pulling the decoded embedding toward the H-embedding. The framework is plug-in compatible with PEFT settings (generating only LoRA parameters over a frozen ViT backbone) and requires no additional storage beyond one 32-dim vector per task.

## Results

On CIFAR-100 (10 tasks) with ResNet-32, H-embed Hnet achieves the best FAA of 83.08% and DAA of 83.09%, outperforming the strongest architecture-based baseline WSN (82.75% FAA) and the original HyperNet (81.57% FAA). With ViT-B/16 and LoRA, the method reaches 97.07% FAA on CIFAR-100 and 81.38% FAA on ImageNet-R, surpassing the SOTA PEFT baseline SD-LoRA by 9.81 and 4.20 percentage points respectively. On the more challenging DomainNet (5 tasks) and ImageNet-R (20 tasks), the advantage widens to 4.06 and 5.85 points over SD-LoRA, demonstrating robustness to longer and more diverse task sequences.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method achieves the highest FAA across all 12 baselines on both backbones and both primary benchmarks. Against regularization baselines (LwF, EWC, PredKD+FeatKD), the gains are substantial: 45–49 points on CIFAR-100/ResNet and 17–22 points on ImageNet-R/ResNet, reflecting the severe forgetting suffered by these methods. Among architecture-based methods, H-embed Hnet edges out WSN by 0.33 points and the original HyperNet by 1.51 points on CIFAR-100/ResNet, with a smaller but consistent margin on ImageNet-R. In the PEFT setting with ViT-B/16, the LoRA variant dominates all prompt and LoRA baselines: it beats the strongest prompt method HiDe-Prompt by 3.59 points (CIFAR-100) and 6.73 points (ImageNet-R), and the strongest LoRA method SD-LoRA by 9.81 and 4.20 points respectively. The advantage grows with task sequence length, reaching 5.85 points over SD-LoRA on ImageNet-R (20 tasks) and 4.06 points on DomainNet (5 tasks), indicating that the H-embedding guidance becomes increasingly valuable as task diversity and sequence length increase.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the hypernetwork-based continual learning framework of von Oswald et al. (ICLR 2020) by introducing a prior, transferability-aware task embedding (H-embedding) that guides the hypernet's parameter generation. The H-embedding is grounded in the H-score information-theoretic transferability metric (Bao et al., ICIP 2019), with a novel AHP normalization step to handle cross-task scaling asymmetry. The key conceptual departure from prior CL work is shifting from a posterior, model-centric view of cross-task interactions (regularization, architecture partitioning) to a prior, data-driven encoding of inter-task relationships computed before each task's training, filling a gap the authors identify in the literature on exploiting task relations in rehearsal-free CL.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a practical, plug-in mechanism for incorporating task-relationship information into CL with minimal storage overhead (a 32-dim embedding per task) and end-to-end trainability, making it compatible with modern PEFT pipelines like LoRA. Its strongest results come in the ViT-LoRA setting, where it outperforms the SOTA SD-LoRA by ~4 points on ImageNet-R, suggesting that transferability-guided embeddings are particularly valuable when a pretrained backbone provides rich feature structure. However, the CIL adaptation remains ad-hoc (relying on a separately trained task-ID classifier on frozen pretrained features), and the approach's behavior under very long task sequences or highly heterogeneous task distributions is not thoroughly stress-tested, leaving open questions about scalability and robustness beyond the evaluated benchmarks.
