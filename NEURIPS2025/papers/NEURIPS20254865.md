## Problem

GNNs for graph classification suffer from poor generalization and overfitting, and existing remedies—graph augmentation (data-view) and ensemble learning (model-view)—each expand diversity from only one perspective while introducing structural degradation or prohibitive multi-model overhead. No prior method unifies data, model, and optimization perspectives of multi-view learning within a single GNN at minimal inference cost.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254865/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

During training, the method consumes a pair of undirected graphs with node features and produces a mixed-view graph (a single graph formed by splicing structure-enhanced subgraphs) along with binary assignment matrices that partition nodes by origin. During inference, it accepts a standard single graph and outputs a class label obtained by averaging the logits of two parallel prediction heads, requiring only one forward pass through the backbone GNN or Graph Transformer.

## Method

GMV integrates three complementary mechanisms into a single GNN. (1) Data view: ST-PPR samples subgraphs by merging PPR, BFS, and DFS node sets to preserve both semantic importance and topological structure, then ST-SubMix splices two such subgraphs into one mixed graph with a one-to-one node mapping. (2) Model view: a lightweight dual-output prediction head is appended to the standard backbone, activating two latent sub-networks (motivated by the lottery ticket hypothesis) so that one forward pass yields view-specific representations via assignment-matrix-based multi-view decomposition. (3) Optimization view: a mixed-view loss (weighted by edge-count-based label confidence) regularizes the mixed graph prediction, while a multi-view loss directly supervises each decomposed view's prediction, jointly encouraging the sub-networks to learn diverse representations. At inference, subgraph processing and decomposition are skipped; the final prediction is simply the average of the two head outputs, giving ensemble-like behavior at single-model cost.

## Results

On the TUDataset benchmark (8 datasets), GMV with GCN achieves the best accuracy across all datasets, e.g., 76.96% on NCI1, 76.86% on NCI109, and 83.92% on COLLAB, outperforming the strongest baseline G-MIMO by 0.8–4.7 percentage points. With GIN, GMV similarly leads on all 8 datasets (e.g., 82.53% on NCI109 vs. 81.02% for G-MIMO). On the OGB benchmark (4 molecular datasets), GMV with GraphGPS achieves the highest AUC on all four datasets, reaching 83.99% on BACE and 81.21% on PPA, surpassing G-MIMO by up to 1.92 points. During inference, GMV requires only a single forward pass with an additional prediction head, matching the computational cost of standard GNNs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GMV consistently outperforms all nine baselines across every dataset and backbone tested. Against graph augmentation methods (DropEdge, DropNode, Subgraph, M-Mixup, G-Mixup, SubMix, S-Mixup), GMV with GCN achieves the highest accuracy on all 8 TUDataset benchmarks; for example, on NCI109 GMV reaches 76.86% versus 72.91% for SubMix and 67.19% for DropEdge. Against ensemble methods, GMV surpasses the classic two-network Ensemble (70.29% on NCI109) and G-MIMO (72.16%) by 6.57 and 4.70 points respectively, while requiring only a single forward pass at inference. On the OGB molecular datasets with GraphGPS, GMV leads G-MIMO by 1.58 points on BACE (83.99% vs. 82.07%) and 0.33 points on PPA (81.21% vs. 80.88%). The diversity analysis (Table 5) confirms that GMV's dual sub-networks produce substantially higher prediction disagreement (13.40 vs. 12.14 for G-MIMO) and KL divergence (5.41 vs. 1.63), explaining the accuracy gains. Notably, GMV with only 2 sub-networks (75.50% on IMDB-B) already exceeds G-MIMO with 10 sub-networks (75.30%), demonstrating superior efficiency per sub-network.

## Relation to Prior Work

GMV builds directly on SubMix [33] for subgraph-based graph mixing, extending it with structure-enhanced sampling (ST-PPR) that combines PPR, BFS, and DFS to preserve both semantic and topological information. Conceptually, it draws on the Lottery Ticket Hypothesis [12] and the MIMO framework [13] to activate diverse sub-networks within a single GNN, but departs from the authors' own prior G-MIMO [37] by replacing multiple parallel encoders/decoders with a lightweight dual-output prediction head that achieves ensemble-like behavior in a single forward pass. The paper also unifies three previously treated-in-isolation perspectives of multi-view learning—data augmentation, model ensembling, and loss design—into a coherent training pipeline, building on Asif et al.'s [4] observation that multi-view structures improve generalization in deep networks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GMV offers a practical, plug-and-play module that improves GNN/GT graph classification with minimal inference overhead (one extra prediction head, single forward pass), making it attractive for deployment where ensemble methods are infeasible. The framework's generality across GCN, GIN, and GraphGPS backbones, and across TUDataset and OGB benchmarks, suggests broad applicability. However, the 'multi-view' framing is somewhat constrained to exactly two sub-networks, and the lottery ticket interpretation is not deeply validated—the sub-networks are activated by architectural design rather than discovered as sparse winning tickets. Open questions include whether the approach scales to very large graphs (REDDIT-M5K, PPA show smaller gains), whether more than two views yield diminishing returns, and whether the structural preservation claims of ST-PPR hold for highly heterogeneous or dynamic graphs.
