## Problem

Widely used local attribution methods—Local Shapley Values (L-SV) and LIME—fail to correctly identify locally important features even under ideal conditions with independent features and exact computation, assigning non-zero importance to features that are both locally and globally irrelevant. The paper proves this failure theoretically for piecewise linear models (which include ReLU neural networks) and shows that no existing method provides locally faithful attributions with the statistical guarantees available for global importance measures.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255070/cropped_page6_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

R-LOCO requires only a fixed dataset of input-output pairs (X_i, Y_i) and does not need direct access to the model's prediction function, making it applicable in settings where model internals are restricted. It produces per-instance local feature importance scores by assigning each observation to a cluster in feature-importance space and reporting the cluster's average LOCO importance vector. A key structural detail is that the method operates in a transformed space: each observation is first mapped to its p-dimensional LOCO importance vector (Δ_1, ..., Δ_p) before clustering, rather than being clustered in the original covariate space.

## Method

R-LOCO (Regional Leave Out COvariates) bridges local and global explanations in three steps: (1) compute per-observation LOCO importance scores Δ_j(X,Y) = V(f̂_0(X),Y) − V(f̂_{−j}(X),Y) using plug-in estimators from the global variable importance literature; (2) cluster observations in this feature-importance space using algorithms such as Affinity Propagation, K-Means, or tree-based methods; (3) assign each new observation to its nearest cluster and report the cluster's mean LOCO importance as its local attribution. The critical design choice is clustering in importance space rather than input space: the paper proves that for piecewise linear models, importance-space clustering separates regions by their functionally active features (e.g., Δ_1,Δ_2 large vs. Δ_3,Δ_4 large), while input-space clustering is confounded by noise from irrelevant features and only recovers the geometric switching variable. A theoretical guarantee (Theorem 4.1) shows that when a cluster is contained within a true model region, R-LOCO attributions depend exclusively on that region's coefficients, unlike L-SV which provably leaks information from inactive regions.

## Results

On the 1st-order synthetic model, R-LOCO achieves 97% True Positive rate and only 3% False Positive rate, compared to 41%/59% for L-SV, 53%/47% for LIME, and 50%/50% for the global LOCO baseline. Non-important feature attributions (NI attr.) are 1–4% for R-LOCO versus 7–18% for L-SV and LIME. On the more complex 2nd-order interaction model, R-LOCO still leads with 67% TP and 33% FP, while L-SV and LIME drop to 44% TP and 56% FP. On real-world datasets (Diabetes, California Housing), R-LOCO consistently produces the largest performance drop when masking top-k important features and the smallest drop when masking bottom-k features, confirming superior local fidelity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

R-LOCO decisively outperforms both L-SV (SHAP) and LIME across all three synthetic models: on the 1st-order model, R-LOCO reaches 97% TP / 3% FP versus 41%/59% (L-SV) and 53%/47% (LIME), and assigns 3× lower importance to irrelevant features (2% vs 12% NI attr.). Against the global LOCO baseline (Williamson et al., 2021), R-LOCO more than doubles the TP rate (97% vs 50%) while halving the FP rate, demonstrating that regional partitioning recovers local structure that purely global averaging erases. On real-world data, R-LOCO outperforms FDT (Laberge et al., 2024) on the Diabetes dataset across all k values, and on California Housing it is superior for identifying the top-3 features and for correctly identifying non-important features (smaller error change when masking bottom-k), though FDT is marginally better for intermediate feature ranks. Clustering SHAP values (CSHAP, Lundberg et al., 2018) fails to outperform standard SHAP, confirming that SHAP values lack the information needed to delineate zones of influence, whereas R-LOCO's LOCO-based importance representation enables effective regional clustering. The oracle variant R-LOCO_TC (using ground-truth cluster structure) achieves near-perfect scores (100% TP on 1st- and 2nd-order), confirming that clustering quality is the primary bottleneck for R-LOCO's performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LOCO (Leave Out COvariates) global variable importance framework of Williamson et al. (2021) into a regional setting by partitioning the input space via clustering in feature-importance space, then computing conditional LOCO attributions within each region. It departs sharply from the dominant local attribution paradigm (Local Shapley Values / SHAP and LIME) by proving these methods fundamentally misattribute importance even under ideal conditions for piecewise linear models, which encompass ReLU neural networks. The approach is conceptually distinct from FDT (Laberge et al., 2024), which seeks regions where multiple explanation methods agree, and from CSHAP, which clusters SHAP values directly; R-LOCO instead clusters in a leave-one-out importance space and inherits the statistical inference guarantees of global LOCO.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The demonstration that the two most widely deployed local attribution methods (SHAP and LIME) assign non-zero importance to features that are both locally and globally irrelevant—even with independent features and exact computation—challenges a core assumption in the XAI community and motivates rethinking how local explanations are constructed. R-LOCO offers a principled middle ground that preserves instance-level detail while gaining the consistency and inference guarantees of global methods, and its inference cost is negligible (cluster assignment only). Key open questions include extending the theoretical guarantees beyond piecewise linear/additive structures, handling the identified counter-example where the squared-loss importance representation becomes sign-agnostic and inseparable, and validating the approach on deeper, non-piecewise-linear architectures where the additive-within-region assumption may break down.
