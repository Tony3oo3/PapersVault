## Problem

Existing brain MRI benchmarks define anomalies through fixed, known categories, collapsing open-world evaluation into a closed-set problem and masking model failures on truly rare or novel pathologies encountered in clinical practice. NOVA addresses this by providing an evaluation-only benchmark of ~900 real brain MRI scans spanning 281 rare diagnoses, designed as an extreme stress-test of out-of-distribution generalization for vision-language models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253488/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each case provides a single 480×480 grayscale PNG brain MRI slice (axial, sagittal, or coronal plane across T1w, T2w, FLAIR, DWI, and other sequences, with no preprocessing or normalization) alongside free-text clinical history and an expert radiologist caption. Models must produce (1) one or more bounding boxes localizing abnormal regions, (2) a free-text diagnostic image description, and (3) a ranked list of five candidate diagnoses. The benchmark is strictly evaluation-only with no train/validation/test splits, enforcing a zero-shot setting where every case is a previously unseen diagnosis.

## Method

NOVA is a benchmark and empirical evaluation study rather than a new model architecture. The dataset is curated from Eurorad's peer-reviewed neuroradiology cases (post-July 2015), preserving the natural long-tailed disease distribution and heterogeneous acquisition protocols without any preprocessing. Ground truth annotations are produced by eight neuroradiology residents in a double-blinded protocol, with consensus boxes merged via IoU-weighted averaging calibrated by each annotator's agreement with a senior adjudicator, and 247 disputed cases resolved by a board-certified neuroradiologist. Evaluation spans three sequential clinical tasks—bounding-box localization (mAP@30/50/50-95, FNR), keyword-based captioning (Clinical/Modality F1, BLEU-4, METEOR, BERT F1), and diagnostic reasoning (Top-1/Top-5 accuracy with GPT-4o semantic matching)—all performed in zero-shot inference mode. The paper benchmarks GPT-4o, Gemini 2.0 Flash, Qwen2.5-VL-72B, and Qwen2-VL-72B (plus medical VLMs CheXagent, MAIRA-2, HuatuoGPT Vision on a 25-case subset), revealing a ~65% localization gap versus natural-image benchmarks and 40%/20% captioning/reasoning gaps versus resident radiologists, and disentangles captioning from reasoning gaps via controlled caption-substitution experiments.

## Results

On the NOVA benchmark (906 brain MRI scans, 281 rare pathologies), Qwen2.5-VL-72B achieves the best localization performance with mAP@30 of 37.66 and mAP@50 of 24.49, while GPT-4o leads in diagnostic reasoning with 24.2% Top-1 and 38.4% Top-5 accuracy. Gemini 2.0 Flash performs best on image captioning with a Clinical Term F1 of 19.8% and BLEU-4 of 1.83. All models show severe degradation: localization scores drop by ~65% compared to natural-image referring-expression benchmarks (73–92%), and diagnostic reasoning lags resident neuroradiologists (48–52% Top-1) by roughly 20–28 percentage points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among generalist VLMs, Qwen2.5-VL-72B outperforms Qwen2-VL-72B on all localization metrics (mAP@50: 24.49 vs. 15.09) and Gemini 2.0 Flash (mAP@50: 24.49 vs. 7.37), while GPT-4o leads in diagnostic reasoning (24.2% Top-1 vs. 22.1% for Gemini and 22.4% for Qwen2.5-VL) and Gemini 2.0 Flash leads in captioning (Clinical F1: 19.8% vs. 15.7% for GPT-4o). Medical-domain VLMs fail dramatically on brain MRI: CheXagent and MAIRA-2 (both chest-X-ray-tuned) achieve mAP@50 = 0.00 on the 25-case subset, and HuatuoGPT Vision reaches only mAP@50 = 8.33, well below the best generalist model (Qwen2.5-VL-72B at 18.97 on the same subset). Even the best generalist model (Qwen2.5-VL-72B) detects fewer than 40% of ground-truth abnormalities at IoU 0.3 (62% false-negative rate) and produces over 600 false-positive boxes, highlighting that no current VLM reliably localizes rare brain pathologies in a zero-shot clinical setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

NOVA carves new ground at the intersection of unsupervised anomaly detection in medical imaging and vision-language model evaluation. Prior brain MRI benchmarks (BraTS, ATLAS, ISLES) define anomalies through fixed, known categories, implicitly collapsing open-set detection into closed-set segmentation; MOOD introduced synthetic anomalies but lacks clinical realism; fastMRI+ added pathology variability but without the structured clinical metadata and multi-task evaluation needed for diagnostic reasoning. NOVA departs from all of these by sourcing 281 rare, real-world pathologies from Eurorad and explicitly designing an evaluation-only, zero-shot protocol that jointly tests localization, captioning, and diagnostic reasoning—three tasks that no prior neuroimaging benchmark has unified. The paper also positions itself against the broader VLM evaluation literature (RefCOCO, MIMIC-CXR, CLM3D), arguing that existing test sets with only a few common outlier types mask failures on truly novel conditions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

NOVA provides the first systematic stress-test of whether current VLMs can handle the open-ended, rare-disease reality of clinical brain MRI, and the results—65% localization gap versus natural-image benchmarks, ~40% captioning gap and ~20% reasoning gap versus resident radiologists—quantify a substantial and actionable shortfall. The disentangling analysis (Table 4) is particularly valuable: it shows that even with ground-truth captions, models retain a residual reasoning gap, indicating the bottleneck is not purely perceptual but involves the integration of visual evidence with linguistic medical knowledge. Key limitations temper the findings: the 25-case subset used for human baselines and the disentangling is small, proprietary models may have seen Eurorad cases during pretraining (making results upper bounds), and the 2D-only format excludes volumetric reasoning. Open questions include whether domain adaptation or structured medical knowledge injection can close the reasoning gap, and whether the benchmark generalizes beyond European acquisition protocols.
