## Problem

While Transformers have been shown to approximate gradient-based updates in their forward pass, no unified theoretical framework existed showing that attention mechanisms can exactly implement structured denoising algorithms such as Laplacian flows, score-based diffusion, or anisotropic diffusion. The paper asks whether attention layers can collectively realize a stepwise denoising process and what this implies for understanding Transformers as generative meta-learners.

## Input / Output

The method takes a Gaussian-noised query image (flat pixel vector: 784-dim for MNIST 28×28, 3072-dim for CIFAR-10/100 3×32×32), a set of clean training images as frozen cross-attention tokens, and optionally same-class contextual images as self-attention tokens. No positional encoding or spatial tokenization is applied; each image is a single d-dimensional vector. The output is a denoised image produced iteratively, with each Transformer layer implementing one discrete denoising step, so intermediate layer outputs trace the denoising trajectory.

## Method

The core insight is that a Transformer with RBF-attention (where attention scores use log-RBF kernels rather than inner products) exactly implements a kernel-weighted update, which subsumes Laplacian manifold denoising, score-based diffusion denoising, and anisotropic diffusion denoising as special cases. The architecture uses cross-attention from the noisy query to frozen training tokens (or a small set of learnable 'witness' tokens for efficiency) and self-attention among context tokens, with Q, K, V matrices constrained to scalar multiples of identity (isotropic) or diagonal matrices (anisotropic). For anisotropic diffusion, the Q, K, V parameters are aligned with the time-varying diffusion coefficient matrix A_t, enabling geometry-adaptive, patch-wise denoising. Training is supervised via reconstruction loss, and the witness-based variant replaces full training-set cross-attention with a compact learned summary, connecting to Nystrom kernel approximation and improving generalization over exact score matching.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (SNR≈5), the Witness+Anisotropic Transformer with 1000 witnesses achieves a test RMSE of 7.05, substantially outperforming the exact score-based denoising baseline (RMSE 10.08) by ~30%. On CIFAR-100, the same model reaches RMSE 7.04 versus 9.50 for the baseline. However, the exact baseline achieves far lower FID (21.6 vs. 168.9 on CIFAR-10) because it memorizes training images, revealing a generalization–realism trade-off. In the in-context setting, providing just 2 same-class context images drops CIFAR-10 RMSE from 133.9 (zero context) to 10.3, demonstrating strong context-dependent denoising.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is against the exact score-based denoising baseline (Lemma 4), which cross-attends to the full training set. On reconstruction (RMSE), both witness-based models clearly win: Witness+Anisotropic reaches 7.05 RMSE on CIFAR-10 and 7.04 on CIFAR-100, versus 10.08 and 9.50 for the exact baseline—a 30% improvement. Witness+Anisotropic consistently outperforms Witness+RBF by 0.2–0.3 RMSE across all witness counts, confirming the benefit of geometry-aware (diagonal) attention parameters. On FID, the exact baseline dominates (21.6 vs. 168.9 on CIFAR-10) because it reproduces training images verbatim, yielding distribution-level realism at the cost of generalization. The in-context experiments (Figure 5) further show that adding same-class context images reduces RMSE from ~134 to ~10 on CIFAR-10, a gain not available to the context-free exact baseline. Overall, the witness-based anisotropic Transformer is the best generalization-oriented denoiser, while the exact baseline is best for distributional realism.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies three previously separate threads—manifold denoising via Laplacian flows (Hein & Maier, 2006), score-based diffusion denoising (Ho et al., 2020; Song et al., 2020), and the emerging view of Transformers as meta-learners that implement optimization algorithms in their forward pass (Akyürek et al., 2023; Garg et al., 2022)—by proving exact equivalence between attention updates and each denoising algorithm. While prior work showed Transformers can approximate gradient descent or serve as diffusion backbones (Peebles & Xie, 2022), this paper provides the first rigorous construction showing that a single attention layer with RBF-kernel scoring *is* one Euler step of the reverse diffusion ODE, and extends this to anisotropic diffusion with learned, non-isotropic attention matrices. The learnable witness tokens for approximating the full training-set cross-attention draw on kernel approximation literature (Nystrom, kernel herding) but are the first to be integrated end-to-end into a Transformer denoiser.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The exact theoretical equivalence gives a principled explanation for why Diffusion Transformers work and suggests that attention's inductive bias is naturally aligned with kernel-weighted denoising, potentially guiding architecture design for generative models. The finding that a small set of learned witnesses outperforms exact full-set score matching on test RMSE (while the exact method wins on FID) highlights a generalization-realism trade-off that is relevant to any diffusion-based generator. Key limitations include evaluation only on small-scale pixel-space tasks (MNIST, CIFAR-10/100) with 6-layer single-head Transformers; open questions include whether the anisotropic construction scales to multi-head, deeper architectures and higher-resolution or non-image domains, and whether the patch-wise specialization observed in Figure 1 persists at scale.
