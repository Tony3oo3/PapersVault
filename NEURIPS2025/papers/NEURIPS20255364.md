## Problem

Intervention-based steering methods (steering vectors, LoRA, ReFT) consistently underperform prompting for controlling language model behavior, as shown by the AXBENCH benchmark. This gap stems from training objectives that ignore the preference signals guiding instruct-tuned LMs: the standard language modeling loss is misaligned with preference-optimized models, and the prior preference-based BiPO objective is constrained by a reference model and fails to scale to production-sized LMs. The paper asks whether a better preference optimization objective can close this gap while preserving the interpretability and parameter efficiency of representation interventions.

## Input / Output

Training consumes preference triples (instruction x, original response y, steered response y^c) with 72 pairs per concept subset, plus a randomly sampled positive steering factor α drawn from a predefined set (e.g., {2,4,…,20} for Gemma-2, {20,40,…,200} for Gemma-3). At inference, the intervention is applied to residual-stream activations at one or more chosen layers with a scalar steering factor; positive factors steer toward the concept while negative factors suppress it. The output is a generated text sequence (up to 128 tokens for Gemma-2, 768 for Gemma-3) scored on concept incorporation, instruction following, and fluency via an LM judge.

## Method

RePS is a bidirectional, reference-free preference optimization objective built on SimPO that jointly trains for concept steering and suppression. For positive steering, it maximizes the length-normalized log-likelihood difference between the steered (winning) and original (losing) responses under an intervention Φ_Steer, up-weighting the steered response by a factor β⁺ = max(log p(y|x) − log p(y^c|x), 1) to compensate for the reference model's low prior on the steered behavior. For negative steering, it uses an asymmetric nulling intervention Φ_Null that projects out the steering direction via a ReLU-gated rank-1 subtraction (inspired by Widdows' orthogonal negation), with the roles of winning and losing responses swapped and a corresponding β⁻ weight. The two directional losses are summed and optimized jointly, sharing learned parameters across Φ_Steer and Φ_Null. A novel factor-sampling trick randomizes the steering factor during training to stabilize convergence against layer-norm drift. RePS is instantiated with three low-rank parameterizations—rank-1 steering vectors, rank-4 LoRA, and rank-4 LoReFT—and evaluated across Gemma-2 (2B, 9B) and Gemma-3 (12B, 27B) models.

## Results

On AXBENCH's CONCEPT500 benchmark, RePS-trained rank-1 steering vectors achieve steering scores of 0.756 (Gemma-2-2B, D_L10), 0.892 (Gemma-2-9B, D_L20), 1.230 (Gemma-3-12B), and 1.269 (Gemma-3-27B), consistently outperforming the language modeling objective (0.663, 0.788, 1.219, 1.228) and dramatically surpassing BiPO (0.199, 0.217, not evaluated on larger models). The steering score is the harmonic mean of concept, instruction-following, and fluency scores (each 0–2). In concept suppression under instruction-following attacks (Table 3), RePS-trained SVs achieve 0.808/0.557 (2B), 0.952/0.518 (9B), 0.870 (12B), and 0.734 (27B), outperforming both prompt-based defenses (0.774/0.439 for 2B prepend/append) and Lang.-trained SVs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against BiPO (Cao et al., 2024), RePS achieves a dramatic and consistent win across all intervention types and model sizes: for rank-1 SVs, RePS scores 0.756–1.269 versus BiPO's 0.173–0.217, a gap of roughly 4–7×. BiPO was not evaluated on Gemma-3 models due to its poor scaling, further underscoring RePS's advantage. Against the AXBENCH language-modeling baselines (Wu et al., 2025), RePS-trained SVs outperform the Lang.-trained SVs by 0.09–0.10 on Gemma-2 and by 0.01–0.04 on Gemma-3, while RePS-trained LoRA outperforms Lang.-trained LoRA by 0.05–0.09 on Gemma-2 and by 0.007–0.008 on Gemma-3. RePS also surpasses all prior AXBENCH baselines (LoReFT, ReFT-r1, DiffMean, SAE) by wide margins. In the suppression setting under instruction-following attacks (Table 3), RePS-trained SVs outperform Lang.-trained SVs on every model size (e.g., 0.870 vs. 0.728 on Gemma-3-12B) and substantially outperform prompt-based defenses, which degrade sharply on larger models (0.275 for prepend on 27B vs. 0.734 for RePS SV).

## Relation to Prior Work

RePS builds directly on two prior preference-optimization objectives: SimPO (Meng et al., 2024), from which it inherits length-normalized, reference-free likelihood scoring, and BiPO (Cao et al., 2024), which first proposed a bidirectional preference loss for training steering vectors. The key conceptual departure is removing the reference-model constraint that BiPO imposes: RePS argues that steering behaviors (e.g., mentioning the Golden Gate Bridge in a programming answer) are inherently low-probability under the base model, so anchoring to the reference likelihood actively hinders learning. Additionally, RePS introduces an asymmetric negative-steering mechanism (a 'null' projection that removes information along the steering direction) and a factor-sampling trick during training, neither of which appears in prior intervention-based steering work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AXBENCH established that intervention-based steering methods consistently underperform simple prompting, a gap that has limited the practical adoption of interpretable, parameter-efficient control over LMs. RePS narrows this gap substantially while preserving the interpretability and minimal parameter overhead of rank-1 vectors, and it demonstrates robustness to prompt-based jailbreaking attacks that defeat text-prompt defenses—a practically important property for safety-critical deployments. Open questions include why RePS outperforms the language-modeling objective (the paper offers intuition but no formal analysis), whether higher-rank interventions (LoRA, ReFT) can match SV performance for suppression on larger models, and whether bootstrapping training data from the target LM itself would further improve convergence.
