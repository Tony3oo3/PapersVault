## Problem

Existing graph-based point cloud registration methods face a fundamental trade-off: clique-based approaches (e.g., MAC) enforce strict full-connectivity constraints that are NP-hard and often unachievable under noise or occlusion, while k-core methods relax constraints to linear time but only consider node degree, ignoring higher-order topological structures like triangles. This paper bridges the gap by introducing the k-truss, which requires each edge to participate in at least k−2 triangles, offering stronger structural guarantees than k-core at polynomial O(m^1.5) complexity.

## Input / Output

The method takes a source point cloud P, a target point cloud Q, and an initial correspondence set C generated by feature matching (FPFH or FCGF descriptors). It produces a single 6-DoF rigid transformation (R ∈ SO(3), t ∈ R³) by generating multiple candidate transformations from k-truss subgraphs and selecting the one that maximizes a spatial distribution score balancing inlier ratio, 3D volume coverage, and alignment error. The method is training-free and operates entirely on the correspondence graph, with no learned components.

## Method

PointTruss introduces k-truss decomposition as the core inlier filtering mechanism, where each edge in the compatibility graph must be supported by at least k−2 triangles to survive. The pipeline first applies a consensus-voting low-scale sampling strategy (counting geometrically compatible neighbors with non-maximum suppression) to extract a diverse structural skeleton, then constructs a weighted compatibility graph using a rigid distance constraint. K-truss decomposition is performed via matrix operations (T = A² ⊙ A) to identify subgraphs with varying triangle support levels. Each surviving subgraph yields a transformation hypothesis through centrality-weighted SVD (weights from the principal eigenvector of the subgraph's adjacency matrix), and the final transformation is selected by a spatial distribution score (SDS) that multiplies inlier ratio, square-root coverage ratio, and normalized alignment error to prevent selections concentrated in sparse local clusters. A theoretical theorem establishes that triangle edge-length deviations follow a non-central chi-squared distribution whose non-centrality parameter is negligible for correct correspondences under Gaussian noise but large for incorrect ones, providing the statistical justification for triangle-based discrimination.

## Results

On 3DMatch with FCGF descriptors, PointTruss achieves 93.84% registration recall, 1.70° mean rotation error, and 6.13 cm mean translation error, outperforming all baselines on every metric. On KITTI with FPFH, it reaches 99.64% recall and 5.31 cm translation error (best in both). On the challenging 3DLoMatch (low-overlap) dataset with FPFH, it achieves 43.96% recall and 2.89° rotation error, surpassing the next-best MAC (40.88%, 3.66°). Runtime is 0.20–0.21 s per pair, faster than MAC (3.29–5.54 s) and comparable to TEASER++ (0.03 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PointTruss outperforms all 14 baselines across KITTI, 3DMatch, and 3DLoMatch. On 3DMatch (FCGF), it improves recall over MAC [48] by 0.12% (93.84% vs 93.72%) and reduces rotation error by ~18% (1.70° vs 2.07°) and translation error by ~6% (6.13 vs 6.52 cm), while running 28× faster (0.20 s vs 5.54 s). On KITTI (FPFH), it achieves the highest recall (99.64%) and lowest translation error (5.31 cm), beating the closest competitor Jiang et al. [21] (99.56%, 7.85 cm) by 2.54 cm in TE. On the low-overlap 3DLoMatch (FPFH), the gap widens: PointTruss gains 3.08% recall over MAC (43.96% vs 40.88%) and 0.77° in rotation error (2.89° vs 3.66°). Among learning-based methods, it surpasses VBReg [20] and PointDSC [1] on all three datasets. When integrated as a drop-in module, PointTruss boosts GeoTransformer recall from 75.0% to 79.5% (+4.5%), exceeding GeoTransformer+MAC (78.9%), and improves PareNet from 80.5% to 82.2% (+1.7%). The only method faster is TEASER++ (0.03 s), but TEASER++ trails PointTruss by 9–19% in recall depending on dataset and descriptor.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the k-truss decomposition from social network community detection (Cohen, 2008) as the core inlier-selection mechanism for point cloud registration, positioning it between the NP-hard maximal clique methods (TEASER, MAC, FastMAC) and the weaker k-core approach (ROBIN, SUCOFT). The key conceptual departure is replacing pairwise or full-connectivity constraints with triangle-support constraints, which are polynomial-time yet capture higher-order geometric rigidity. The pipeline also introduces a consensus-voting low-scale sampling stage and a spatial distribution score for hypothesis ranking, both of which are novel components not present in prior graph-based registration methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PointTruss offers a practical, polynomial-time (O(m^1.5)) alternative to clique-based registration that maintains stronger structural guarantees than k-core, addressing a long-standing efficiency-robustness tradeoff in graph-based PCR. Its demonstrated compatibility as a drop-in module for deep learning pipelines (GeoTransformer, PareNet) suggests broad utility beyond standalone use. Open questions include how the method scales to very large outdoor scenes with millions of correspondences, whether the k parameter can be learned or auto-tuned, and whether the triangle-support principle extends to non-rigid or partial-overlap registration beyond the tested 30% overlap threshold.
