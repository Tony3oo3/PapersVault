## Problem

Diffusion Transformers for text-to-video generation incur prohibitive inference latency due to quadratic spatial-temporal attention across many denoising steps. Existing feature-caching accelerators apply a static, uniform reuse policy across all layers and steps, which cannot adapt to the pronounced layer-wise, prompt-specific, and resolution-dependent variation in feature similarity, resulting in visible artifacts or limited speedups.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255631/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Foresight is a training-free, plug-and-play wrapper around pretrained Spatial-Temporal DiT models (OpenSora, Latte, CogVideoX, HunyuanVideo, Wan-2.1). It consumes a text prompt and a Gaussian-noise-initialized latent video (e.g., 240p/720p, 2-second clips) and outputs a denoised video, with no architectural or weight modifications. The method operates at the coarse DiT-block granularity (one spatial and one temporal block per layer), caching full block outputs rather than individual attention or MLP sub-layers, which reduces cache memory by 3× over fine-grained baselines.

## Method

Foresight introduces a two-phase adaptive reuse scheduler. During a warmup phase (first W steps), all blocks are computed and per-layer reuse thresholds λ are derived from a geometrically weighted MSE of the last three warmup steps. In the subsequent reuse phase, a dynamic reuse metric δ tracks the MSE between the current block output and its cached value; at each denoising step, a layer is either reused from cache (if δ ≤ γλ) or recomputed, with γ providing a global speed–quality knob. This per-layer, per-step decision replaces the fixed reuse window of static methods, allowing late, more sensitive layers to be recomputed more frequently while early, stable layers are cached aggressively. A convergence analysis shows that the reuse-induced perturbation is uniformly bounded by √(γλ_l) and vanishes over the contractive reverse diffusion chain, guaranteeing fidelity to the baseline model.

## Results

On the VBench prompt set (550 prompts, 11 dimensions), Foresight achieves up to 1.63× end-to-end speedup on CogVideoX (18.53s vs. 30.23s baseline) while matching or exceeding baseline quality (77.84% VBench vs. 77.78% baseline; PSNR 28.45, SSIM 0.87, LPIPS 0.12, FVD 531.99). On Open-Sora, Foresight(N2R3) reaches 1.44× speedup with 75.62% VBench (vs. 75.63% baseline) and PSNR 27.49. On Latte, Foresight(N2R3) achieves 1.31× speedup with 73.05% VBench (vs. 73.02% baseline) and PSNR 23.12. Across all three models, Foresight consistently outperforms every static caching baseline on PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, and FVD while delivering equal or greater speedups.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Foresight outperforms all four static caching baselines (Static, Δ-DiT, T-GATE, PAB) on every quality metric across all three models. The gap is most dramatic on Latte, where Static drops VBench from 73.02% to 61.56% and Δ-DiT to 65.14%, while Foresight(N1R2) maintains 73.08% with PSNR 26.02 vs. PAB's 21.22. On CogVideoX, Δ-DiT and T-GATE suffer severe quality collapse (VBench 54.16% and 65.14% vs. 77.78% baseline), whereas Foresight(N1R2) achieves 77.94% VBench and PSNR 34.75, surpassing even the baseline. PAB is the strongest static baseline, achieving 1.26–1.37× speedup with modest quality loss, but Foresight matches or exceeds PAB's speedup (1.28–1.63×) while delivering 4–10 dB higher PSNR and substantially lower FVD (e.g., 130.65 vs. 340.24 on CogVideoX). In the matched-quality comparison (Table 9), Foresight achieves 1.68×, 1.58×, and 1.95× speedup on OpenSora, Latte, and CogVideoX respectively, versus PAB's 1.26×, 1.29×, and 1.37× at the same PSNR level.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the feature-caching paradigm for accelerating diffusion model inference from static, uniform reuse policies (DeepCache, BlockCache, Δ-DiT, T-GATE, PAB, TeaCache) to an adaptive, per-layer reuse scheduler. While prior DiT-specific methods (PAB, DiTFastAttn, ToCA, TeaCache) all apply a fixed reuse schedule across all layers and denoising steps, Foresight introduces runtime MSE-based thresholds that allow each spatial and temporal DiT block to independently decide whether to recompute or reuse its activation. The convergence analysis builds on the contractive structure of the reverse diffusion Markov chain to show that bounded per-layer perturbations do not affect the limiting distribution, providing theoretical grounding absent in prior caching work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Foresight offers a practical, training-free, plug-and-play acceleration for text-to-video DiTs that simultaneously improves quality over static baselines (higher PSNR, lower FVD) while achieving up to 1.63× speedup, making it immediately deployable on existing pretrained models without retraining or architectural changes. The 3× memory reduction from coarse block-level caching versus fine-grained sub-block caching is a notable practical advantage for memory-constrained deployments. Open questions include whether the MSE threshold is the optimal reuse criterion (e.g., cosine similarity or learned gates), whether finer sub-block granularity would further improve the speed-quality frontier, and how the approach scales to much longer videos or higher resolutions where the quadratic attention cost dominates. The speed-quality envelope remains governed by the reuse window N and warmup W, and the method has only been validated on a handful of open-source models.
