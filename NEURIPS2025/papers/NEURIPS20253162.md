## Problem

Vision-based tactile sensors (VBTSs) like GelSight offer high-resolution, cost-effective tactile feedback for robotics, but simulating their hyperelastic gel pads and complex contact interactions has been a major bottleneck. Existing simulators either use rigid-body approximations that miss fine-grained deformation, or soft-body methods that run at sub-real-time speeds with limited parallelization, preventing large-scale data generation for tactile-informed robot learning.

## Input / Output

Taccel accepts robot descriptions in URDF format with auxiliary sensor configuration files, object meshes, and joint-space targets (as NumPy arrays or PyTorch tensors) to drive kinematic constraints. It produces multiple tactile signal types simultaneously: high-resolution RGB images (generated by a pixel-to-pixel DNN trained on 200 real GelSight images with depth annotations), depth maps, low-resolution marker flow vectors, and 3D tactile point clouds in the world frame. The system supports up to 4096 parallel environments on a single H100 GPU, with gel pad meshes ranging from 139 nodes (low-res) to 5,157 nodes (full-hand dexterous configuration).

## Method

Taccel unifies Incremental Potential Contact (IPC) and Affine Body Dynamics (ABD) into a single energy minimization framework: soft gel pads are modeled as tetrahedralized FEM bodies with Neo-Hookean constitutive law, while robot links and stiff objects use ABD's 12-DoF reduced coordinates (3 translation + 9 affine deformation) with ARAP regularization. IPC's log-barrier contact energy guarantees inversion-free and intersection-free solutions, and the unified energy E_IPC(y;x) is minimized jointly over both coordinate spaces via Newton's method with Augmented Lagrangian kinematic constraints. The entire solver is implemented in NVIDIA Warp for GPU-parallel execution, achieving 915 FPS across 4096 environments (18× wallclock) in a dual-sensor peg-insertion task. RGB tactile signals are synthesized by a lightweight DNN that maps per-pixel depth and normal to color deltas relative to a reference undeformed image, avoiding expensive per-frame rendering.

## Results

Taccel achieves 915 total FPS (18.30× wallclock time) across 4096 parallel environments on a single H100 GPU for a low-resolution peg-insertion task, and 12.67 FPS (0.25× wallclock time) across 256 environments for a dexterous grasping task with 17 gel pads. Tactile signal fidelity reaches an average SSIM of 0.93 against real-world GelSight measurements, and friction/shear deformation sim-real error averages only 28 μm. For sim-to-real object classification, a ResNet-18 trained purely on Taccel synthetic depth maps achieves 86.50% accuracy on synthetic data and 70.94% on real-world samples without domain adaptation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SAPIEN-IPC [8], the closest prior work also using FEM + ABD + IPC, Taccel achieves a 16× improvement in parallel environments (4096 vs. 256) and a 22.6× speedup in the low-resolution peg-insertion benchmark (18.30× vs. 0.81× wallclock), while additionally supporting DNN-based RGB tactile signal generation that SAPIEN-IPC lacks. The speed advantage stems from Taccel's FP64 precision, which converges in ~1.6–3.5 Newton iterations versus 24–50 for SAPIEN-IPC's FP32 solver (Table 2), yielding lower optimization residue (10⁻⁴ vs. 10⁻¹ m/s). Compared to Difftactile [47], Taccel replaces penalty-based contact with IPC's guaranteed intersection-free solutions and extends from a single gripper to arbitrary robot configurations (Allegro Hand, F-TAC, Robotiq-3F). Taxim [46] is limited to rigid-body approximation with look-up-table signals and a single sensor, making it inapplicable to deformation-rich tasks. In the Tac-Man articulated manipulation comparison, Taccel reproduces real-world execution-recovery switch counts (68.75 revolute, 0.0 prismatic) with ~1% error, whereas Isaac Sim's dynamics produce substantially higher switch counts due to its coarser contact model.

## Relation to Prior Work

Taccel unifies Incremental Potential Contact (IPC) for soft-body gel pads and Affine Body Dynamics (ABD) for rigid robot links within a single energy-minimization framework, building directly on the unified Newton barrier method of Chen et al. [9] and the original IPC/ABD formulations. For tactile signal generation, it adopts the DNN-based depth-to-RGB mapping from Difftactile [47] and extends the DFC grasp synthesis algorithm [39] with a perpendicular-contact objective. The key departure from the closest prior system, SAPIEN-IPC [8], is the use of FP64 precision (which paradoxically accelerates convergence) and a Warp-based GPU implementation that scales to 4096 parallel environments versus 256, while also supporting arbitrary URDF-defined robots rather than gripper-only configurations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By enabling 18× real-time simulation of 4096 parallel tactile environments on a single H100, Taccel removes the primary bottleneck—slow, low-parallelism simulation—that has constrained large-scale data generation and policy learning for VBTS-equipped robots. The demonstrated sim-to-real transfer (70.94% classification accuracy on real data with zero domain adaptation) and the faithful replication of Tac-Man's execution-recovery cycles (~1% physical error) suggest the platform can meaningfully reduce the hardware iteration cost of tactile robotics research. Open questions include whether the Neo-Hookean constitutive model captures all relevant gel-pad behaviors, whether the DNN signal generator generalizes beyond its 200-image training set, and how the PCG solver bottleneck scales to even larger or more complex scenes.
