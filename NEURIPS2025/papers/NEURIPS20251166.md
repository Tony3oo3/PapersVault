## Problem

Existing federated knowledge distillation methods rely on forward KLD to align local models with the global model, which introduces significant bias when the global model produces multi-peaked distributions in multi-domain scenarios (pathway unreliability). Additionally, cross-domain update conflicts cause the aggregated global model to suffer catastrophic accuracy drops in certain domains, rendering it an unreliable teacher (teacher unreliability). The paper asks how to establish a reliable distillation pathway and obtain reliable teachers for multi-domain federated learning.

## Input / Output

Each client holds private RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 256×256 for Office-31/Office-Home) with class labels, and receives the global model parameters from the server at each communication round. The method outputs per-class predictions for image classification, but structurally it also produces a set of domain expert models (one per identified domain cluster) that serve as multi-teacher distillation targets rather than a single global teacher. The distillation loss is computed per-sample on each client's private data, comparing the client's softmax output against each domain expert's softmax output.

## Method

DKDR combines two mechanisms. First, Dynamic Knowledge Distillation (DKD) partitions each output distribution into Dominant Knowledge Components (DKCs, top probabilities summing to threshold μ) and Ancillary Knowledge Components (AKCs), then computes knowledge discrepancies γ_l and γ_s in each region. Based on theoretical analysis showing forward KLD is mean-seeking (prioritizes fitting high-probability regions) while reverse KLD is mode-seeking (prioritizes fitting low-probability regions), DKD dynamically weights the two divergences as γ_s/(γ_s+γ_l)·L_rkd + γ_l/(γ_s+γ_l)·L_skd, so the client precisely fits whichever region has larger discrepancy. Second, Knowledge Decoupling (KDP) computes difference vectors v_k = w_k − w^{t−1} for each client, applies truncated SVD (retaining top r singular values) to denoise and extract domain-specific signals, then clusters the refined vectors using parameter-free FINCH clustering based on cosine similarity. Clients within each cluster are aggregated into a domain expert, and the final distillation loss averages DKD losses across all domain experts with equal weight, mitigating domain skew.

## Results

On the multi-domain Office-31 benchmark, DKDR achieves 50.57% average accuracy, outperforming the best baseline FedProto (39.98%) by 10.59 points. On Office-Home, DKDR reaches 62.48% average accuracy versus FedProto's 59.90%. On single-domain CIFAR-100 with the most extreme skew (ζ=0.1), DKDR attains 46.80% top-1 accuracy, surpassing Scaffold (46.32%) and FedAvg (43.76%). The paper reports that DKDR outperforms all baselines in 7 out of 8 experimental settings across four datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DKDR substantially outperforms all eight baselines on the multi-domain benchmarks: on Office-31 it gains +10.59 points over FedProto (39.98→50.57) and +16.10 over FedAvg (34.47→50.57); on Office-Home it leads FedProto by +2.58 points (59.90→62.48). On single-domain CIFAR-100, DKDR beats the strongest baseline Scaffold by +0.48 (ζ=0.1), +1.51 (ζ=0.3), and +1.42 (ζ=0.5) points. On CIFAR-10, DKDR wins at ζ=0.3 (+0.94 over Scaffold) and ζ=0.5 (+1.23 over Scaffold) but narrowly trails Scaffold at ζ=0.1 (79.46 vs 79.62), the only setting where it does not rank first. FedProx and MOON consistently underperform FedAvg on Office-31, while FedDf is the weakest baseline overall, trailing DKDR by 13–16 points on multi-domain tasks. The largest absolute gains from DKDR appear on the Webcam domain of Office-31 (+21.91 over FedAvg), confirming that the dynamic distillation and domain-expert mechanism is most beneficial where the global model is least reliable.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the federated knowledge distillation line (FedNTD, FedX, FedDf, etc.) by identifying that their universal reliance on forward KLD introduces systematic bias when the global model produces multi-peaked output distributions in data-scarce domains. It builds on the foundational KD formulation of Hinton et al. and the parameter-free FINCH clustering of Sarfraz et al., combining them into a two-pronged framework: dynamic forward/reverse KLD weighting based on knowledge-component discrepancies, and SVD-based knowledge decoupling to extract domain experts. The departure from prior work is conceptual rather than architectural—rather than proposing a new network or training loop, it re-examines the distillation objective itself and replaces the single global teacher with a cluster of domain-specific experts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper highlights a practical failure mode of federated KD that is easy to overlook: in multi-domain settings, the aggregated teacher can be actively harmful in certain domains, and the standard forward-KLD objective amplifies this by forcing the student to cover low-density regions. The multi-expert distillation paradigm opens a path toward domain-aware federated learning without requiring clients to share raw data or features. Key limitations include the computational overhead of per-round SVD and clustering on resource-constrained clients, and the assumption that domains are sufficiently separable for FINCH to recover meaningful clusters—overlapping domains could degrade the expert identification. Open questions include whether the dynamic KLD weighting generalizes to non-softmax outputs (e.g., language models) and how the method scales when the number of domains grows large.
