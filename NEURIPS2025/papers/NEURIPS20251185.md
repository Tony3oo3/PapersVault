## Problem

Learning to sample from unnormalized target distributions π ∝ e^{-U} on high-dimensional discrete state spaces (e.g., spin lattices with cardinality 10^122) remains a major challenge: MCMC methods suffer from slow mixing in multimodal regimes, and while discrete diffusion models have succeeded in generative modeling, their use for *sampling* from known energy functions is largely unexplored. Existing discrete neural sampler approaches like LEAPS fail at critical and low temperatures despite long training, leaving a gap for scalable, accurate discrete sampling.

## Input / Output

The method requires only the unnormalized potential function U (e.g., the Ising or Potts Hamiltonian) and the discrete state space specification X_0 = {1,...,N}^D; no samples from the target distribution are needed. It produces samples from π ∝ e^{-U} via a learned CTMC generator, and simultaneously yields an unbiased estimate of the normalizing constant Z through the Radon-Nikodym weight W^u(X). The state space can be astronomically large (2^256 for a 16×16 Ising model), and the score network s_θ maps partially masked sequences to D×N probability matrices.

## Method

MDNS frames discrete sampling as stochastic optimal control of continuous-time Markov chains, choosing a masked discrete diffusion model (fully-masked → uniform) as a memoryless reference path measure so that the optimal generator has the special structure Q*_t(x, x^{d←n}) = γ(t)·Pr_π(X^d=n | X^UM=x^UM)·1_{x^d=M}. To circumvent the non-differentiability of CTMC jump trajectories, the paper introduces three gradient-free learning objectives—REINFORCE-based relative entropy (RERF), log-variance (LV), and cross-entropy (CE)—that estimate path-measure divergences without backpropagating through the full trajectory. For high-dimensional scalability, the key contribution is the Weighted Denoising Cross-Entropy (WDCE) loss, which treats generated samples as importance-weighted draws from π and amortizes the O(D) Radon-Nikodym derivative computation by resampling multiple masked versions of each final sample, reducing per-step cost to a standard DCE forward pass. Theoretical guarantees show that driving KL(P^u‖P*) to ε² ensures KL(p_samp‖π) ≤ ε² and gives a concentration bound on the Z estimate.

## Results

On the 16×16 Potts model (state space cardinality 3^256 ≈ 10^122), MDNS achieves magnetization error of 1.3e-3 and 2-point correlation error of 8.8e-5 at the hardest temperature (β_low=1.2), with an effective sample size (ESS) of 0.933, while LEAPS fails catastrophically (Mag. error 2.9e-1, ESS 0.012) and MH cannot mix even after 20+ hours of simulation. On the 16×16 Ising model (2^256 states), MDNS achieves correlation error of 2.4e-3 and ESS 0.981 at β_low=0.6, outperforming LEAPS (Corr. error 5.8e-1, ESS 0.261) by a large margin. MDNS also provides unbiased normalizing constant estimates as a byproduct of training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two baselines: LEAPS (a learning-based discrete neural sampler using locally equivariant networks) and Metropolis-Hastings (MH, a classical MCMC method). On the Potts model, MDNS dominates both baselines by orders of magnitude at low and critical temperatures: at β_low=1.2, MDNS achieves a correlation error of 8.8e-5 versus 2.5e-1 for LEAPS (≈2800× worse) and 5.6e-1 for MH (≈6400× worse), while LEAPS's ESS collapses to 0.012 and MH fails to mix entirely. On the Ising model, MDNS still outperforms LEAPS substantially at β_low (Corr. error 2.4e-3 vs. 5.8e-1) and β_critical (2.0e-3 vs. 1.6e-1), though MH remains competitive on correlation error at β_low (7.7e-4). The key differentiator is that MDNS scales gracefully to the harder Potts model where both baselines break down, while LEAPS and MH both succeed on the simpler Ising model at high temperature.

## Relation to Prior Work

MDNS extends the stochastic optimal control (SOC) framework for neural samplers—previously developed for continuous SDEs (PIS, Nüsken & Richter, Adjoint Matching)—to the discrete CTMC setting, where the non-differentiability of jump trajectories necessitates entirely new learning objectives (RERF, LV, CE, WDCE) that avoid backpropagation through trajectories. It leverages the masked discrete diffusion model structure (Lou et al., Ou et al.) as a memoryless reference path measure, which simplifies the Radon-Nikodym derivative to a closed-form expression. The most direct competitor, LEAPS, also trains discrete neural samplers via CTMCs but from a measure-transport perspective requiring locally equivariant networks; MDNS departs by using a path-measure alignment perspective with no architectural constraints on the score model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper fills a notable gap: while neural samplers for continuous distributions and discrete diffusion models for generative modeling are both well-developed, their intersection—learning to sample from unnormalized discrete distributions—was largely unexplored. The WDCE loss and replay-buffer mechanism offer a practical path to scaling discrete neural sampling to high dimensions (state space cardinality 10^122), and the theoretical guarantees on both sampling quality and normalizing constant estimation are valuable. However, validation is limited to Ising and Potts models on square lattices; performance on graph-structured or combinatorial optimization distributions remains unknown, and the authors explicitly defer fine-tuning of pretrained discrete diffusion models to future work.
