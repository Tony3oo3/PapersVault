## Problem

Existing adversarially robust GP bandit methods such as STABLEOPT require the learner to correctly specify the adversary's perturbation budget (ambiguity set radius), which is often unknown or misestimated in practice, leading to either overly conservative or dangerously optimistic solutions. This paper addresses this gap by introducing robust satisficing (RS) formulations for adversarial GP optimization, where the learner only needs to specify an interpretable satisficing threshold τ rather than the adversary's budget, yielding performance guarantees even when the perturbation magnitude is unknown or varying over time.

## Input / Output

At each round t, the algorithm consumes the GP posterior (mean μ_{t-1}, variance σ_{t-1}) over a continuous action space X (discretized to N points in practice), a user-specified satisficing threshold τ, and an exploration parameter β_t. It outputs a selected action x̃_t by minimizing the optimistic fragility κ̄_{τ,t}(x) (AdveRS-1) or maximizing the optimistic critical radius ε̄_{τ,t}(x) (AdveRS-2), both computed via UCB over all perturbations in the unbounded ambiguity ball Δ_∞(x). Critically, the learner never observes the true perturbation budget ε_t; the adversary selects δ_t ∈ Δ_{ε_t}(x̃_t) after the action is committed, and the learner only observes the perturbed point x_t = x̃_t + δ_t and a noisy reward y_t.

## Method

The paper proposes two robust satisficing formulations for adversarial GP bandits: RS-1 minimizes the fragility κ_τ(x), defined as the worst-case rate of reward degradation per unit perturbation distance, while RS-2 maximizes the critical radius ε_τ(x), the largest perturbation budget under which the threshold τ remains achievable for all perturbations. Both are unified under a general RS-G formulation with a smoothness parameter p ≥ 1 that controls the shape of the reward guarantee (linear decay for p=1, step-function for p→∞), allowing practitioners to tune the trade-off between small and large perturbation robustness. The algorithms AdveRS-1 and AdveRS-2 replace the unknown function f with UCB-based optimistic estimates to balance exploration and exploitation, selecting the action with the smallest optimistic fragility or largest optimistic critical radius respectively. The key theoretical contribution is that AdveRS-1 achieves sublinear robust satisficing regret Õ(√(Tγ_T)) with no assumptions on the adversary, while AdveRS-2 achieves sublinear lenient regret under the additional assumption that the adversary's budget is bounded by the critical radius. Unlike RO methods that require correct specification of the ambiguity set, the RS approach only requires the interpretable threshold τ, making it robust to budget misspecification as confirmed by experiments on synthetic functions, an inverted pendulum controller, and an FDA-approved diabetes simulator.

## Results

The paper provides two main theoretical regret guarantees: AdveRS-1 achieves lenient regret bounded by 4β_T√(Tγ_T) + B∑ε_t and sublinear robust satisficing regret 4β_T√(Tγ_T), while AdveRS-2 achieves sublinear lenient and RS regret (both ≤ 4β_T√(Tγ_T)) under the assumption that the adversary's perturbation budget ε_t ≤ ε_τ. Empirically, on the synthetic function with ε_t = 0.5 (Assumption 3.8 holds), AdveRS-2 achieves sublinear lenient regret while STABLEOPT with a misspecified ambiguity radius (r = 0.5ε_t or r = 4ε_t) suffers linear regret. On the UVA/PADOVA T1DM insulin dosage simulator, no algorithm achieves sublinear regret, but both AdveRS-1 and AdveRS-2 outperform STABLEOPT when the radius r is suboptimal. The RS-G generalization with p = 2 exhibits the smallest regret in the synthetic experiment, suggesting the tunable power parameter can be beneficial.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole empirical baseline is STABLEOPT [Bogunovic et al., NeurIPS 2018], a robust optimization (RO) algorithm that requires specifying an ambiguity ball radius r. The paper runs STABLEOPT with r = ε_t (correct), r = 4ε_t (overestimated), and r = 0.5ε_t (underestimated). AdveRS-2 consistently outperforms STABLEOPT on lenient regret in the synthetic experiment when Assumption 3.8 holds, achieving sublinear regret while STABLEOPT with misspecified r incurs linear regret. On robust satisficing regret (Figure 4), both AdveRS-1 and AdveRS-2 beat STABLEOPT even when STABLEOPT has perfect knowledge of the adversarial budget (r = ε_t). In the insulin dosage experiment, STABLEOPT performs best only when r = σ_s (the true perturbation std), but AdveRS algorithms outperform it for both r = 0.5σ_s and r = 3σ_s, highlighting the practical advantage of not needing to calibrate the ambiguity set. In the inverted pendulum experiment (Appendix E.1), AdveRS matches STABLEOPT with perfect r but outperforms it when r is misspecified, while AdveRS-1 still achieves lower regret than STABLEOPT with r = 0.3ε_t despite its linear lenient regret. The key practical takeaway is that AdveRS algorithms are robust to misspecification of the perturbation budget, whereas STABLEOPT's performance degrades sharply when r is inaccurate.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper transplants the robust satisficing (RS) framework from operations research (Long et al., 2023) into the adversarial Gaussian process bandit setting introduced by Bogunovic et al. (2018, STABLEOPT). The key conceptual departure is that RS requires only a satisficing threshold τ rather than a precisely specified ambiguity set, making it robust to unknown or varying perturbation budgets. The authors further unify two RS formulations (fragility-based RS-1 and critical-radius-based RS-2) under a general p-fragility framework (RS-G), and extend the robust satisficing regret notion from their prior work on distributional uncertainty (Saday et al., 2024) to the adversarial perturbation setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in removing the need to correctly specify the adversary's perturbation budget—a critical weakness of robust optimization methods like STABLEOPT that can lead to linear regret when the ambiguity radius is misspecified. The satisficing threshold τ is more interpretable and domain-expert-friendly (e.g., a clinician setting a blood glucose target), which the insulin dosage experiment demonstrates. Open questions include adaptive τ selection without domain expertise, extending to unobserved perturbations, and learning the RS-G smoothness parameter p online. The framework is currently limited to observed perturbations and requires the threshold to be achievable (Assumption 3.8 for AdveRS-2's sublinear guarantees).
