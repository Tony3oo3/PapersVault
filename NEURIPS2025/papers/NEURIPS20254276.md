## Problem

Existing generative steganography methods rely on non-deterministic noise sampling or reversible inversion of the diffusion process, making them incompatible with state-of-the-art video generation models that adopt deterministic sampling for speed and controllability. Moreover, the compression and post-processing applied by social media platforms further degrade the robustness of any steganographic embedding. The paper addresses both gaps by proposing a training-free video steganography framework that operates entirely within the deterministic latent diffusion pipeline.

## Input / Output

The sender provides a text prompt, a random seed, a base CFG scale k_s, a modulation offset cfg, and a binary message m of length n (≈12,000 bits for a 5-second 480×832 video at 16 fps). The method produces a stego video that is visually indistinguishable from a clean generated video (PSNR ≈ 41.7 dB). The receiver, given the same prompt, seed, k_s, cfg, and the (possibly compressed) received video, recovers the binary message by encoding the video back into latent space and comparing distances to two reference latents. No model weights are modified at any point; the entire process is training-free.

## Method

LD-RoViS constructs a steganographic channel by modulating the classifier-free guidance (CFG) scale at the final denoising step of a deterministic latent diffusion model (Wan2.1 T2V-1.3B with Flow Matching). From the same intermediate latent X_1, three final latents are produced using CFG values k_s, k_s+cfg, and k_s−cfg, yielding a base latent X_0 and a discriminative pair (X_0^1, X_0^2) that differ subtly in latent space while remaining visually consistent with the prompt. A multi-mask mechanism then selects embedding positions: an invariance mask M_1 retains only positions stable under an encoder–decoder–compression round-trip, and a discriminative mask M_2 retains positions where X_0^1 and X_0^2 are maximally separated; their intersection M = M_1 ⊙ M_2 defines the robust embedding region. At each M=1 position, the stego latent is set to X_0^1 (bit 0) or X_0^2 (bit 1), and the receiver recovers each bit by comparing L1 distances between the received latent and the two reference latents. The approach requires no training, no modification of model weights, and is compatible with any deterministic latent diffusion video model, as validated on both Wan2.1 and LTX-Video.

## Results

On 100 videos generated from VidProM prompts using Wan2.1 (480×832, 5 s, 81 frames), LD-RoViS achieves a PSNR of 41.66±1.52 dB, a BRISQUE score of 28.90±6.05, an extraction accuracy of 99.17±0.63%, and an embedding capacity of ~11,983 bits. In steganalysis, LD-RoViS achieves error rates of 49.74% (CovNet) and 48.49% (LWENet), effectively indistinguishable from random guessing, whereas all baselines are detected with near-zero error. Under H.264 compression (CRF=27) and common noise perturbations, LD-RoViS maintains ≥91.67% accuracy, with particularly strong resistance to brightness shifts (99.02%) and salt-and-pepper noise (98.72%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the two transform-domain baselines (AQIM and MEC_AQIM), LD-RoViS achieves substantially better visual quality (PSNR 41.66 vs. 34.81/35.21 dB; BRISQUE 28.90 vs. 32.87/32.71) and higher capacity (~12,000 vs. 10,000 bits), while maintaining comparable extraction accuracy (99.17% vs. 99.44% for AQIM; a large 8.2-point gain over MEC_AQIM's 90.99%). The most dramatic advantage is in steganalysis resistance: LD-RoViS yields CovNet/LWENet error rates of 49.74%/48.49% (≈ random guessing), whereas AQIM and MEC_AQIM are detected with near-perfect accuracy (error rates of 0.13%/0.26% and 0.01%/1.07%, respectively). Compared to the generative face-swapping baseline RoGVSN, LD-RoViS offers far superior visual quality (BRISQUE 28.90 vs. 49.53) and ~16× higher capacity (11,983 vs. 729 bits), though RoGVSN retains a slight edge in robustness under H.264 compression (97.04% vs. 91.67% at CRF=27) and Gaussian noise (96.20% vs. 92.82%). Overall, LD-RoViS wins on visual quality, capacity, and security across all three baselines, with only a marginal accuracy trade-off versus AQIM and a modest robustness gap versus RoGVSN under specific lossy conditions.

## Relation to Prior Work

LD-RoViS extends the generative steganography line (StegaDDPM, LDStega, Pulsar, PSyDUCK) from images to video and, critically, from stochastic to deterministic diffusion samplers. Prior generative methods embed messages by modulating random noise at the final denoising step, which is inapplicable to the deterministic Flow-Matching or DDIM samplers used by SOTA video models like Wan2.1. The paper's key departure is repurposing the classifier-free guidance (CFG) scale—a standard generation-quality knob—as the steganographic channel, combined with a multi-mask mechanism that selects latent positions robust to codec distortion, something no prior generative steganography method addresses for video.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

With AI-generated videos now comprising ~40% of social-media content, a training-free steganographic channel that is invisible to standard steganalyzers (CovNet, LWENet, SUPERB all yield ~50% error rates) offers a practically deployable covert-communication tool without requiring model retraining. Open questions include scalability to longer/higher-resolution videos, compatibility with the rapidly evolving landscape of video diffusion architectures, and whether adaptive steganalysis trained specifically on generative-video distributions could close the detection gap. The method's dependence on the underlying video model's output quality (physically implausible artifacts in some generations) remains a practical limitation for real-world deployment.
