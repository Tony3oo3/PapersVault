## Problem

Text-to-image diffusion models memorize training data, and prior work attributed this to classifier-free guidance (CFG) steering denoising trajectories toward memorized outputs within an 'attraction basin.' Deferring CFG until the trajectory escapes this basin mitigates memorization but degrades prompt alignment if delayed too long. This paper asks how to enable earlier escape from the attraction basin so that CFG can be applied sooner, preserving both non-memorization and image-text alignment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250024/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard text prompt and operates entirely at inference time on a pre-trained Stable Diffusion model (v1.4 or v2.0), producing a 512×512 RGB image. The key operational detail is that before the denoising loop begins, the initial Gaussian noise sample x_T is modified—either via a few forward-pass adjustments (Batch-wise) or via iterative backpropagation (Per-sample)—and the adjusted sample is then used as the starting point for standard DDIM or Euler sampling. No retraining or access to training data is required.

## Method

The paper shows that different initial noise samples x_T lead to different 'transition points' (the timestep at which the denoising trajectory exits the attraction basin), and that samples with smaller conditional noise prediction magnitude ||ε̃_θ(x_T, T, y)||_2 escape earlier. To exploit this, they derive a one-step adjustment rule from Sharpness-Aware Minimization (SAM) that reduces this magnitude: x̃_T = x_T − γ̃(ε̃_θ(x_T + δ̂, T, y) − ε̃_θ(x_T, T, y)), where δ̂ is a normalized perturbation in the direction of the conditional noise prediction. Two inference-time strategies are proposed: Batch-wise mitigation applies this rule M times to an entire batch with fixed hyperparameters (γ̃, ρ, M) and then runs standard denoising with CFG deferred until a chosen timestep τ; Per-sample mitigation instead backpropagates through the UNet to directly minimize ||ε̃_θ(x_T, T, y)||_2 via AdamW until it falls below a target threshold, effectively eliminating the transition point so that CFG can be applied from the very first timestep without inducing memorization.

## Results

The paper evaluates memorization mitigation on Stable Diffusion v1.4 (500 memorized prompts from LAION) and v2.0 (219 prompts), measuring SSCD (lower = less memorization), CLIP score (higher = better text alignment), LPIPS (diversity), and ImageReward (quality). The Per-sample method consistently achieves the most favorable SSCD–CLIP trade-off across both models, while Batch-wise matches Cross-attention Scaling on SD v1.4 and achieves the best performance on SD v2.0. With the Per-sample method (l_target=0.9, SD v1.4), the reported metrics are SSCD=0.2265, CLIP=0.2647, LPIPS=0.7615, and ImageReward=−0.3522. Computationally, Batch-wise adds negligible overhead (2.01 s vs. 1.85 s baseline), while Per-sample requires 2.8 s and ~9.7 GB GPU memory.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against four inference-time baselines: Random Token Addition (Somepalli et al., NeurIPS 2023), Prompt Embedding Adjustment (Wen et al., ICLR 2024), Cross-attention Scaling (Ren et al., ECCV 2024), and Opposite Guidance / Static Transition Point (Jain et al., 2024). On Stable Diffusion v1.4, the Per-sample method dominates all baselines in the SSCD–CLIP trade-off, achieving lower SSCD at comparable or higher CLIP scores across all mitigation strengths. Batch-wise performs comparably to Cross-attention Scaling on SD v1.4 but surpasses it on SD v2.0, where it achieves the best overall performance. The Per-sample method's advantage over Batch-wise on SD v1.4 is attributed to its per-sample adaptability in eliminating the transition point, whereas Batch-wise uses fixed hyperparameters that yield varying transition points across prompts. In terms of image diversity (LPIPS) and quality (ImageReward), Per-sample provides the most favorable trade-off with memorization mitigation across both models, while Batch-wise achieves the highest LPIPS at comparable SSCD on SD v1.4.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Jain et al.'s attraction basin framework, which identified CFG in early denoising steps as the mechanism driving memorization and proposed deferring CFG or applying opposite guidance to escape the basin. It also repurposes the sharpness-aware minimization (SAM) objective from Foret et al.—originally designed to find flat minima in parameter space—to adjust the initial noise sample in data space, reducing the magnitude of the conditional noise prediction. The key conceptual departure is shifting focus from modifying the denoising trajectory (via guidance signals or attention) to modifying the trajectory's starting point, challenging the prior assumption that initialization has minimal impact on memorization mitigation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that initial noise determines escape time from the attraction basin opens a new, orthogonal lever for memorization mitigation that can be combined with existing prompt-perturbation or attention-scaling approaches. The Per-sample method's ability to eliminate the transition point entirely—removing the need to hand-tune a CFG start timestep—is a practical simplification, though at the cost of ~50% higher inference time and 2.7× GPU memory. Open questions include whether the attraction basin phenomenon and the initial-noise sensitivity generalize to newer architectures (DiT-based models, SDXL), whether the per-sample optimization can be amortized via a learned adjustment network, and whether the approach extends to video or 3D diffusion models where the basin structure may differ.
