## Problem

Editing existing audio with natural language instructions remains largely unexplored. Existing zero-shot inversion methods require a full target description of the edited audio, which is difficult to write and often unavailable, while supervised approaches like AUDIT are restricted to a small set of predefined edit operations. No prior model supports fully free-form, open-ended instruction-based audio editing.

## Input / Output

The model takes a 44.1 kHz stereo audio clip (up to 47 seconds) and a free-form natural language edit instruction (e.g., 'make it sound muffled', 'add reverb to the fireworks') and produces the corresponding edited audio at the same resolution and duration. During inference, the input audio is encoded into the diffusion model's latent space, Gaussian noise is added, and this noised latent serves as the denoising starting point, which preserves input characteristics while allowing the edit to be applied. The edit instruction replaces the standard text prompt, and the input audio is additionally concatenated as an extra conditioning channel.

## Method

SAO-Instruct fine-tunes Stable Audio Open (a diffusion transformer over a 21.5 Hz VAE latent) on a synthetic dataset of 150k audio editing triplets constructed via three complementary pipelines: (1) fully synthetic pairs generated with Prompt-to-Prompt (attention-map injection adapted from the image domain), (2) semi-synthetic pairs via ZETA/DDPM inversion where the input is real audio and only the output is generated, and (3) real pairs from 12 deterministic manual audio operations (ADD, DROP, PITCH, etc.) with LLM-generated instructions. Per-sample Prompt-to-Prompt and inversion parameters are tuned via Bayesian Optimization against a multi-metric objective combining CLAP similarity, CLAP directional change, CLAP audio similarity, and multi-scale mel-spectrogram loss, with weights determined by a small-scale ELO listening study. GPT-4o generates structured prompt triplets (input caption, edit instruction, output caption) and Gemini 2.0 Flash performs perceptual quality filtering during candidate search. The key design choice at inference is initializing denoising from the noised latent of the input audio rather than pure noise, which substantially improves faithfulness to the original clip while retaining sufficient flexibility for the edit.

## Results

SAO-Instruct achieves the fastest inference time at 9.94s per sample, nearly 8× faster than AudioEditor (79.49s) and ~1.5× faster than ZETA (15.31–17.78s). In the subjective listening study, SAO-Instruct outperforms all baselines on edit Relevance (3.83 vs. 3.33/3.25/3.04) and Faithfulness (3.99 vs. 2.75/2.95/2.75), while maintaining competitive Quality (3.54). On objective metrics, SAO-Instruct achieves the best LSD (1.36) and KL (0.93) against the original reference, though AudioEditor leads on CLAP (0.48 vs. 0.38) and Inception Score (10.01 vs. 7.59), likely benefiting from access to full target captions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAO-Instruct is compared against two zero-shot baselines: AudioEditor (which uses Auffusion and conditions on full original/target descriptions plus changed-word indices) and ZETA (which uses DDPM inversion with Stable Audio Open and also has access to full descriptions). SAO-Instruct wins decisively on subjective Relevance (3.83 vs. 3.33 for AudioEditor, 3.25/3.04 for ZETA) and Faithfulness (3.99 vs. 2.75/2.95/2.75), indicating that its edits are both more accurate and better preserve the input audio. On objective metrics, SAO-Instruct achieves the best LSD (1.36) and KL (0.93) against the original reference, but AudioEditor leads on CLAP (0.48 vs. 0.38) and IS (10.01 vs. 7.59), which the authors attribute to AudioEditor's access to the full target caption and longer inference time. On deterministic editing tasks (Appendix G.2), SAO-Instruct substantially outperforms both baselines on STFT (2.91 vs. 6.06/4.73/5.21), MR-MEL (3.47 vs. 7.53/5.61/5.91), and SI-SDR (−21.62 vs. −54.55/−28.75/−30.65). Overall, SAO-Instruct demonstrates that a free-form instruction alone can match or exceed the performance of methods conditioned on full audio descriptions, while being significantly faster.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the InstructPix2Pix paradigm (synthetic triplet generation + fine-tuning a diffusion model) from the image domain to audio, but with a substantially more complex data pipeline that combines three complementary sources: fully synthetic Prompt-to-Prompt pairs, semi-synthetic DDPM inversion (ZETA) pairs, and deterministic manual edits. It builds directly on Stable Audio Open as the underlying diffusion transformer and departs from prior audio editing work (AUDIT, AudioEditor, ZETA) by replacing the requirement for full input/output captions with a single free-form edit instruction. The per-sample Bayesian Optimization of Prompt-to-Prompt and inversion parameters, guided by a multi-metric objective function, is a practical contribution not seen in prior audio editing pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SAO-Instruct demonstrates that a model conditioned only on a short edit instruction can match or exceed zero-shot baselines that receive full target descriptions, suggesting that instruction-based editing is a viable and more user-friendly paradigm for audio. Key limitations include the high computational cost of data generation (6.2k GPU hours for 100k samples), dependence on the quality of the underlying generative model, and English-only support. Open questions include extending to multi-step edits, music-specific editing, and non-English instructions, as well as reducing the data generation cost to enable larger and more diverse training sets.
