## Problem

Elimination-based algorithms for stochastic bandits with adversarial corruptions, such as BARBAR, suffer from a suboptimal O(KC) regret that fails to match the Ω(C) lower bound, where K is the number of arms and C is the corruption level. While FTRL-based methods achieve optimal regret, they require solving a convex optimization problem each round (costly in semi-bandits) and are difficult to parallelize for multi-agent or batched settings. The paper resolves the open problem of achieving near-optimal regret via elimination-based methods and extends the solution to settings where FTRL is impractical.

## Input / Output

The framework consumes corrupted scalar reward observations from K arms (or from a directed feedback graph, a d-element combinatorial action set, or V cooperating agents) and produces a per-epoch sampling probability distribution over arms. A key design choice is that the algorithm requires no prior knowledge of the time horizon T and does not assume a unique optimal arm—both of which are needed by FTRL-based baselines in non-MAB settings. In the multi-agent variant, each agent broadcasts only its cumulative reward vector at epoch boundaries, yielding a total communication cost of O(V log(VT)).

## Method

BARBAT replaces BARBAR's data-dependent epoch lengths with static lengths N_m ≈ Kλ_m·2^{2(m-1)} that the adversary cannot inflate, and allocates n_k^m = λ_m/(Δ_k^{m-1})² pulls to each arm while assigning all remaining pulls to the estimated best arm k_m. It uses epoch-varying failure probabilities δ_m ≈ 1/(mK·2^{2m}) rather than a single global δ, which both eliminates the K factor from the corruption term and removes the need to know T. The framework is then extended by adapting the epoch structure: MA-BARBAT divides pulls by V and aggregates rewards across agents; BB-BARBAT sets N_m ≈ T^{m/(L+1)} so epochs align with batches; SOG-BARBAT computes an out-domination set to exploit graph feedback; and DS-BARBAT selects the top-d arms as the estimated optimal action. All variants achieve near-optimal regret matching lower bounds up to a logarithmic factor.

## Results

BARBAT achieves a regret of O(C + Σ_{Δ_k>0} log(T)log(KT)/Δ_k + K log(1/Δ)log(K/Δ)/Δ) for stochastic MAB with adversarial corruptions, eliminating the K factor present in BARBAR's O(KC + Σ log²(T)/Δ_k) and matching the Ω(C + Σ log(T)/Δ_k) lower bound up to a logarithmic factor. In the multi-agent setting, individual regret scales as O(C/V + Σ log(VT)log(KVT)/(VΔ_k)), showing a 1/V speedup from collaboration. For d-set semi-bandits, the corruption-dependent term is O(dC), and for strongly observable graph bandits it is O(C) (improving over Elise's O(αC)). The batched bandit setting yields O(CT^{1/(L+3)} + T^{4/(L+3)}(Σ L log(KT)/Δ_k + K log(T)log(1/Δ)log(K/Δ)/(LΔ))), with a matching lower bound of Ω(T^{1/L}(K + C^{1-1/L})).

## Baselines & Comparisons

BARBAT strictly improves over BARBAR [1] by eliminating the K factor in the corruption-dependent term (KC → C), resolving the open problem posed in that work. Against FTRL-based methods (Tsallis-FTRL [4,6], Shannon-FTRL [6,13]), BARBAT matches or improves the corruption term while offering three practical advantages: no per-round convex optimization (critical for semi-bandits), native parallelizability for batched and multi-agent settings, and no requirement of a unique optimal arm. In the multi-agent setting, MA-BARBAT outperforms CBARAC [9] (which has a K log²(T)/Δ term) and DRAA [12] (which has K log²(T)/(VΔ)) by replacing the worst-gap dependence with a sum over individual gaps Σ 1/Δ_k, yielding tighter bounds when gaps are heterogeneous. For graph bandits, SOG-BARBAT improves the corruption term from αC (Elise [10]) to C and removes the need for prior knowledge of the independence number α required by Shannon-FTRL [13] and Tsallis-FTRL [14]. In d-set semi-bandits, DS-BARBAT matches the dC corruption term of HYBRID [15], LBINF [16], and LBINF_LS/GD [17] but is significantly faster computationally since it avoids solving a convex optimization problem each round. Experimental results (Figures 1–3) confirm that BARBAT variants achieve the lowest regret and smallest regret increase under higher corruption levels C across all tested settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the BARBAR algorithm [Liu et al., 2021], an elimination-based method for stochastic bandits with adversarial corruptions that achieved O(KC) regret. The key departure is replacing BARBAR's dynamic (data-dependent) epoch lengths with static, data-independent epochs and using epoch-varying failure probabilities δ_m instead of a global δ, which eliminates the K factor from the corruption-dependent regret term. The paper also positions itself against FTRL-based methods (Tsallis-FTRL, Shannon-FTRL) that achieve optimal regret but require solving a convex optimization problem each round and are difficult to parallelize. The BARBAT framework is then extended to multi-agent, batched, strongly observable graph, and d-set semi-bandit settings, with the batched bandits extension being the first study of that problem under adversarial corruptions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work resolves an open problem explicitly raised in [1] about whether elimination-based methods can achieve near-optimal regret for corrupted bandits, and it provides the first upper and lower bounds for batched bandits with adversarial corruptions. The practical significance lies in computational efficiency (no per-round optimization) and parallelizability, making the framework suitable for multi-agent and batched settings where FTRL-based methods are intractable. Open questions include closing the gap between the upper and lower bounds for batched bandits, improving the log²(T) dependence to log(T), and extending the framework to linear bandits.
