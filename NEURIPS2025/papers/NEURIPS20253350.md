## Problem

Existing anomaly detection methods for blockchain transactions—rule-based systems, traditional ML (GMM, LSTM), and off-the-shelf LLMs—fail to capture the unique multi-modal structure of DeFi transactions (long hex addresses, large mixed-radix numbers, and log text) and lack an effective detection mechanism. BlockGPT, the closest prior work, applies a causal GPT model with a naive tokenizer that obscures critical transaction details, and vanilla LLMs like GPT-4o achieve near-zero recall on Solana. The paper asks whether a purpose-built transformer with tailored tokenization and a principled scoring mechanism can reliably flag anomalous transactions while keeping false positives low.

## Input / Output

The method consumes raw JSON blockchain transactions from Ethereum or Solana, which are flattened via depth-first search into a token sequence using a custom multi-modal tokenizer (one-hot for the top-7,000 frequent addresses, 40-character hex subword tokens for all numeric fields, and WordPiece subword tokens for log messages), with special structural tokens marking function-call boundaries. At inference, g% of tokens are randomly masked and fed to the model; the output is a per-transaction anomaly score defined as the fraction of masked positions whose true token falls outside the model's top-s candidate set, and the final result is a ranked list of the top-k most anomalous transactions.

## Method

BlockScan adapts a RoBERTa auto-encoder with masked language modeling, replacing absolute position embeddings with RoPE and using FlashAttention to handle sequences up to 8,192 tokens. The key architectural novelty is a modularized tokenizer that assigns distinct strategies per modality: frequent addresses become single tokens (rest collapse to OOV), all decimal values are converted to a uniform 40-character hex format before subword tokenization, and log messages retain subword structure to preserve semantic relationships. The model is trained self-supervised on benign transactions only, learning to reconstruct masked tokens. Detection then exploits reconstruction failure: anomalous transactions, deviating from the learned benign distribution, yield higher failed-prediction ratios. A domain-adaptation theorem (HΔH divergence bound) provides a theoretical guarantee that malicious transactions incur strictly higher expected loss than benign ones under the trained model.

## Results

On Ethereum (k=10), BlockScan achieves 80% recall with 0.28% FPR and 80% precision, outperforming BlockGPT (70% recall, 0.42% FPR) and GPT-4o (30% recall, 0.98% FPR). On Solana (k=10), BlockScan reaches 44.44% recall with only 0.13% FPR, while BlockGPT manages just 16.67% recall at 0.47% FPR; Doc2Vec, GPT-4o, and the heuristic baseline all achieve 0% recall across all k values. At the highest threshold (k=20 on Solana), BlockScan detects 72.22% of anomalies with 0.47% FPR, whereas BlockGPT reaches only 27.78% recall at 1% FPR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BlockScan decisively outperforms BlockGPT [7] on both datasets: on Ethereum at k=10, BlockScan achieves 80% recall vs. BlockGPT's 70% with a lower FPR (0.28% vs. 0.42%), and on Solana at k=10, BlockScan reaches 44.44% recall vs. BlockGPT's 16.67% with a 3.6× lower FPR. The Doc2Vec + GMM baseline [30] is the weakest performer, achieving 0% recall on Solana across all k values and only 10–20% recall on Ethereum, confirming that bag-of-words representations cannot capture sequential transaction dependencies. The heuristic baseline (transaction length) [31] also fails completely on Solana (0% recall) and ties BlockGPT on Ethereum at k=5 and k=10 (40% and 70% recall respectively), but falls behind BlockScan at k=15 (70% vs. 80% recall). GPT-4o, despite its extensive pre-training, achieves only 30% recall on Ethereum and 0% on Solana, and fine-tuning it via the OpenAI API yields no improvement, likely due to the coarse-grained instruction-response format and inability to customize tokenization. Overall, BlockScan is the only method that achieves meaningful detection on Solana, and it leads on every metric for both datasets at every k value tested.

## Relation to Prior Work

BlockScan departs from BlockGPT's GPT-style causal language model by adopting a BERT-style masked language model, arguing that bidirectional context is more suitable for detecting deviations in transaction structure than next-token prediction. It also diverges from prior work's treatment of blockchain data as a single modality by introducing a modularized tokenizer that handles addresses (one-hot for top-7k frequent hashes), large numbers (hex-converted subword), and log messages (WordPiece) separately. The theoretical grounding draws on standard domain adaptation bounds (Ben-David et al.) to justify that out-of-distribution malicious transactions incur higher reconstruction loss, while the use of RoPE and FlashAttention for long-sequence handling is borrowed from the NLP long-context literature rather than being novel in itself.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first open-source transformer-based anomaly detector for DeFi transactions with a theoretical guarantee, and it is the only method that achieves non-zero recall on Solana, where all baselines (including GPT-4o) completely fail. However, the evaluation datasets are very small (10 malicious transactions for Ethereum, 18 for Solana), which limits confidence in the reported recall/precision numbers and makes the results sensitive to individual transaction choices. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to new chains or evolving attack patterns without retraining, how it performs under adversarial attacks that deliberately mimic benign token distributions, and whether the periodic tokenizer rebuild requirement is practical at scale.
