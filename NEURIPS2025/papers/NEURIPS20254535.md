## Problem

Standard neurosymbolic (NeSy) predictors assume conditional independence between the concepts they extract, which prevents them from modeling inter-concept dependencies, expressing uncertainty over ambiguous concept assignments, and detecting reasoning shortcuts. This independence assumption provably limits out-of-distribution generalization and calibration, yet no scalable NeSy method existed that could model concept dependencies while retaining efficient probabilistic reasoning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254535/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

NESYDMs take high-dimensional RGB images as input (sequences of N-digit MNIST numbers, N×N grid maps for path planning, or dashcam images for autonomous driving) and produce a Y-dimensional discrete output label vector (e.g., sum digits, a binary path matrix, or allowed driving actions). The latent concepts are C-dimensional discrete vectors (e.g., individual digit values in {0,…,9}, per-cell costs in {1,…,5}, or object attributes), and a fixed symbolic program φ maps concepts to outputs. Unlike standard NeSy predictors that only see input-output pairs, NESYDMs additionally condition the unmasking model on partially revealed concepts at each diffusion step, enabling iterative refinement.

## Method

NESYDMs replace the independent concept extractor in NeSy predictors with a masked diffusion model (MDM) that iteratively unmasks a fully masked concept vector. At each denoising step, a conditionally independent unmasking model p_θ(c̃⁰ | cᵗ, x) predicts the full concept given the partially masked state, preserving the local independence assumption that enables efficient WMC-based reasoning while encoding global dependencies across steps. The paper derives a novel continuous-time NELBO (Theorem 3.1) with three terms: a concept unmasking cross-entropy, an output unmasking loss computed as Y weighted model counts through the program φ, and a variational entropy term that encourages coverage of all consistent concepts. Training uses a variational posterior (via controlled generation with a soft constraint r_β) to sample likely concepts, and an RLOO gradient estimator to backpropagate through the intractable WMC in the output loss. Inference samples L concept trajectories from the trained MDM, runs φ on each, and takes a majority vote over the resulting outputs.

## Results

On the challenging 30×30 visual path-planning task (combinatorial space of 5^900), NESYDM achieves 97.40% accuracy, significantly outperforming the best continuous-cost baseline I-MLE (93.7%) and the best approximate NeSy baseline A-NeSI+RL (67.57%). On MNIST Addition with N=15 digits, NESYDM (77.29%) is competitive with the state-of-the-art approximate method A-NeSI (76.84%). On RSBench tasks, NESYDM achieves dramatically better concept calibration (ECE 4.18 on MNIST Half vs. 36.81 for BEARS and 69.40 for PNP⊥) while maintaining competitive output accuracy (99.12% vs. 99.19% for BEARS).

## Baselines & Comparisons

NESYDM decisively outperforms all approximate NeSy baselines on the 30×30 visual path-planning task: it beats A-NeSI (97.40% vs. 17.13%), A-NeSI+RL (97.40% vs. 67.57%), and EXAL (97.40% vs. 80.85%), and also surpasses the continuous-cost I-MLE method (97.40% vs. 93.7%). On the 12×12 grid, NESYDM (99.41%) is statistically comparable to A-NeSI+RL (98.96%) but with far lower variance (±0.06 vs. ±1.33). On MNIST Addition, NESYDM is competitive with A-NeSI and DeepSoftLog but does not significantly outperform them, as expected since this task has no reasoning shortcuts. On RSBench, NESYDM achieves the best concept calibration by a wide margin (ECE 4.18 on MNIST Half vs. 36.81 for BEARS and 69.40 for PNP⊥), while matching or exceeding BEARS on output accuracy; on BDD-OIA, NESYDM achieves better output F1 than BEARS (62.63 vs. 60.80) with comparable calibration.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends masked diffusion models (MDMs) [Sahoo et al., 2024] into the neurosymbolic setting, where the unmasking model is conditioned on a raw input and its predictions are passed through a symbolic program (e.g., Dijkstra's algorithm) to produce structured outputs. It departs from the dominant paradigm in probabilistic NeSy predictors (A-NeSI, Scallop, EXAL, Semantic Loss) that assume conditional independence between concepts, which provably prevents reasoning-shortcut awareness. Prior RS-aware methods (BEARS, SPL) use mixture models or probabilistic circuits but require knowledge compilation and do not scale to high-dimensional concept spaces; NESYDMs instead exploit the local independence at each diffusion step to encode global dependencies while retaining the same C-parameter neural architecture as independent NeSy predictors. The variational posterior borrows the controlled-generation resampling trick from Guo et al. [2024], and gradient estimation uses the RLOO estimator from Kool et al. [2019].

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

NESYDMs are the first scalable NeSy predictors that are simultaneously RS-aware and competitive on high-dimensional reasoning tasks, addressing a gap that has limited deployment of NeSy systems in safety-critical settings where miscalibrated, overconfident predictions are unacceptable. The dramatic calibration improvement on RSBench (ECE dropping from ~70–87% for independent models to ~4–12%) and the 30×30 path-planning result (97.4% vs. 17.1% for A-NeSI) demonstrate that the independence assumption is not merely a convenience but a fundamental bottleneck. Key open questions include extending the method to non-decomposable output spaces, improving the biased variational-entropy approximation, and exploring whether other discrete diffusion formulations (beyond masked diffusion) yield tighter bounds or better scaling.
