## Problem

Existing online algorithms for multi-agent coordination (MA-OC) with submodular objectives only achieve a sub-optimal (1−e^{−c})/c approximation, falling short of the optimal (1−c/e) bound known for single-agent settings. Moreover, no online algorithm has been developed for the practically important but previously unexplored weakly submodular scenarios (α-weakly DR-submodular or (γ,β)-weakly submodular objectives), where prior work is confined to offline local search methods.

## Input / Output

Each of n agents holds a private discrete action set V_i (with |V_i| = κ_i) and, after committing to one action per round, receives only a local marginal oracle Q_t^i(a,S) = f_t(a|S) for its own actions rather than full access to the set function f_t. The agents communicate over a connected undirected graph G with a symmetric doubly stochastic weight matrix W, and the algorithm runs for T rounds under the partition-matroid constraint that at most one action is selected per agent. The output is a per-agent policy vector π_i ∈ Δ_{κ_i} (a probability distribution over actions including the 'no-action' option) from which one action is sampled each round, maximizing cumulative utility f_t(∪_i {a_i(t)}).

## Method

The core contribution is a novel continuous-relaxation technique called the policy-based continuous extension F_t, which maps the discrete set function maximization to a continuous optimization over the product of simplices ∏_i Δ_{κ_i}. Unlike the multi-linear extension, F_t provides a lossless rounding scheme for any set function because it naturally assigns probability mass to the 'no action' outcome and never generates subsets violating the partition-matroid constraint. To recover optimal approximation ratios, the paper constructs three surrogate functions F_t^s with carefully chosen weight functions w(z) (e^{z−1}, e^{α(z−1)}, e^{φ(γ,β)(z−1)}) whose stationary points yield the tight (1−c/e), (1−e^{−α}), and γ²(1−e^{−φ(γ,β)})/φ(γ,β) guarantees respectively. The MA-SPL algorithm performs distributed projected gradient ascent on the surrogate using consensus-based information exchange and stochastic gradient estimation via the local marginal oracle, achieving a dynamic ρ-regret of O(√(P_T·T/(1−τ))). A parameter-free variant, MA-MPL, eliminates the need for prior knowledge of α, γ, β by exploiting a novel inequality between F_t and f_t, at the cost of a slightly weaker regret bound O(d(G)√(P_T·T)).

## Results

The paper's headline results are theoretical approximation ratios and dynamic regret bounds for the T-round MA-OC problem. MA-SPL achieves the optimal (1−c/e)-approximation for submodular objectives (improving over the prior (1−e^{−c})/c bound), (1−e^{−α}) for α-weakly DR-submodular, and γ²(1−e^{−φ(γ,β)})/φ(γ,β) for (γ,β)-weakly submodular objectives, all with a dynamic ρ-regret of O(√(P_T·T/(1−τ))). The parameter-free MA-MPL matches these approximation ratios with a regret of O(d(G)√(P_T·T)) at the cost of O(T^{3/2}) communications and O(κT^{5/2}) oracle queries. In experiments on UAV multi-target tracking (facility-location and A-optimal design objectives), MA-SPL's average utility significantly exceeds MA-OSMA and MA-OSEA, while MA-MPL and MA-SPL both substantially outperform a RANDOM baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against three prior baselines: OSG [115,50], MA-OSMA [126], and MA-OSEA [126]. MA-SPL strictly dominates all three on approximation quality: it achieves the optimal (1−c/e) ratio versus OSG's 1/(1+c) and MA-OSMA/MA-OSEA's (1−e^{−c})/c, and it is the first method to handle α-weakly DR-submodular and (γ,β)-weakly submodular objectives (all baselines are restricted to submodular). MA-SPL also matches the O(T) communication and O(κT) query complexity of the baselines while being parameter-free (like OSG) and working on general connected graphs (like MA-OSMA/MA-OSEA) rather than requiring a complete graph. The parameter-free MA-MPL further adds projection-free updates (matching MA-OSEA and OSG) at the cost of higher communication O(T^{3/2}) and query O(κT^{5/2}) complexity. In the numerical experiments on multi-target tracking, MA-SPL's running average utility significantly exceeds both MA-OSMA and MA-OSEA across submodular facility-location objectives with varying target dynamics (random, adversarial, polyline), and MA-MPL/MA-SPL both substantially outperform the RANDOM baseline on A-optimal design objectives that induce α-weakly DR-submodular utilities.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the multi-agent online coordination (MA-OC) framework of Zhang et al. [126, ICLR 2025], which relied on surrogate functions of the multi-linear extension to achieve (1-e^{-c})/c approximation for submodular objectives. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of a 'policy-based continuous extension' that, unlike the multi-linear extension, provides lossless rounding for any set function without requiring submodularity, thereby unlocking the previously unexplored weakly submodular (α-weakly DR-submodular and (γ,β)-weakly submodular) online coordination setting. The surrogate function construction technique is adapted from the non-oblivious boosting framework of Zhang et al. [122, ICML 2022], but the proof techniques must be substantially modified because the domain ∏Δ_{κ_i} is not closed under coordinate-wise maximum, invalidating the standard DR-submodular inequality used in prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first result to simultaneously achieve the optimal (1-c/e) approximation for submodular MA-OC and to provide guarantees for weakly submodular online multi-agent coordination, closing a gap where prior algorithms either gave sub-optimal ratios or were restricted to offline settings. The policy-based continuous extension is a broadly useful relaxation tool that could apply beyond this specific problem. Open questions include whether the higher communication complexity of the parameter-free MA-MPL (O(T^{3/2}) vs. O(T)) can be reduced, whether the regret bound involving graph diameter d(G) can be tightened to the spectral-gap form, and whether the simulated target-tracking experiments translate to real-world deployment with noisy or delayed feedback.
