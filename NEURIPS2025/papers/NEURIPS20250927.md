## Problem

Existing nonlinear dynamical models of neural activity either operate on a single modality, assume all modalities share the same timescale, or rely on non-causal inference that precludes real-time decoding. No prior method can nonlinearly fuse multimodal neural time-series that differ in sampling rate, probabilistic distribution (e.g., Poisson spikes vs. Gaussian LFPs), and may contain missing samples, while still supporting causal, recursive, real-time inference of latent factors for downstream target decoding.

## Input / Output

The method consumes two (or more) simultaneously recorded neural time-series at different sampling rates—e.g., binary spike counts at 10 ms and continuous LFP amplitudes at 50 ms—where the slower modality's time-index set is a strict subset of the faster one's (T′ ⊂ T), and either modality may additionally have randomly dropped samples. It outputs multiscale latent factors x_t and embedding factors a_t at the fastest timescale, which can be decoded in real time (via Kalman filtering) or non-causally (via Kalman smoothing) into target variables such as 2-D arm velocity, and can also be used to reconstruct the original neural signals.

## Method

MRINE (Multiscale Real-time Inference of Nonlinear Embeddings) couples three components: (1) a multiscale encoder in which each modality is first mapped by a time-invariant MLP to a modality-specific embedding, then processed by a modality-specific linear dynamical model (LDM) whose Kalman filter/predictor natively handles timescale gaps and missing samples by forward-propagating the learned state dynamics—eliminating the need for zero-padding; the filtered modality-specific embeddings are concatenated and passed through a fusion MLP to produce the multiscale embedding a_t; (2) a multiscale LDM backbone (x_{t+1}=Ax_t+w_t, a_t=Cx_t+r_t) that models the joint temporal dynamics linearly in the nonlinear embedding space, enabling closed-form real-time Kalman filtering and non-causal smoothing; and (3) modality-specific decoders with Poisson and Gaussian likelihoods parameterized by neural networks. Training minimizes a composite loss combining multi-horizon k-step-ahead prediction, smoothed reconstruction, and a smoothness-regularization term (KL-divergence between consecutive time-step posterior distributions extended to Poisson), plus L2 weight decay and a time-dropout augmentation that randomly masks samples during training to further improve robustness to missing data.

## Results

On the NHP grid reaching dataset, MRINE achieves behavior decoding CC of 0.487, 0.555, and 0.611 for 5, 10, and 20 spike+LFP channels respectively, outperforming all baselines (best baseline: mmPLRNN at 0.450, 0.477, 0.540). On the NHP center-out reaching dataset, MRINE reaches 0.547, 0.624, and 0.649 CC, again surpassing all competitors (best baseline: MMGPVAE at 0.495, 0.567, 0.601). Fusing 20 LFP channels with 5 spike channels improves decoding by 14.7% (grid) and 47.3% (center-out), while fusing 20 spike channels with 5 LFP channels yields 49.8% and 30.1% improvements respectively. On the high-dimensional mouse visual cortex dataset (800-D neuropixel + calcium imaging), MRINE also outperforms CEBRA on frame ID prediction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MRINE outperforms all five baselines across every channel configuration and both NHP datasets (p<10⁻⁵, one-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank test). Against the linear MSID [28], MRINE gains 0.107 CC at 5+5 channels on grid reaching (0.487 vs. 0.380) and 0.105 on center-out (0.547 vs. 0.442), with the gap narrowing slightly at 20+20 channels (0.611 vs. 0.519; 0.649 vs. 0.561). Against the nonlinear mmPLRNN [29] and MMGPVAE [31], which require zero-imputation for missing LFP timesteps and perform non-causal inference, MRINE leads by 0.037–0.071 CC on grid reaching and 0.052–0.109 CC on center-out reaching. The MVAE baseline [42] from computer vision is consistently the weakest, trailing MRINE by 0.161–0.235 CC. On the mouse visual cortex dataset, MRINE outperforms CEBRA [17], attributed to MRINE's explicit dynamical backbone capturing temporal dependencies versus CEBRA's convolutional architecture. Ablation studies confirm that MRINE's multiscale encoder (handling timescale differences natively) is critical: replacing it with zero-imputation significantly degrades performance below baseline levels.

## Relation to Prior Work

MRINE extends the DFINE architecture (MLP encoder + linear dynamical model + Kalman filtering for flexible real-time/noncausal inference) from single-modality to multiscale multimodal neural data by introducing modality-specific LDMs that handle timescale differences and missing samples, followed by a nonlinear fusion network. It also generalizes the linear multiscale LDM frameworks of Abbaspourazad et al. (2021) and Ahmadipour et al. (2024) to a nonlinear formulation with neural network encoders and decoders. The paper departs from recent nonlinear multimodal models (mmPLRNN, MMGPVAE) that assume aligned timescales and non-causal inference, and from general multimodal VAE approaches that cannot perform causal, step-by-step fusion over time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MRINE addresses a practical bottleneck for brain-computer interfaces and real-time neural decoding: the inability to fuse spike and field-potential signals with different sampling rates and distributions in a causal, real-time manner. The consistent bidirectional improvements (spikes help LFP decoding and vice versa) across two independent NHP datasets and a high-dimensional visual cortex dataset suggest that these modalities carry genuinely non-redundant behavioral information. Key limitations include the assumption of time-invariant dynamics (requiring retraining across sessions) and the need for manual tuning of smoothness regularization hyperparameters. Open directions include extending to switching/adaptive dynamics, cross-modal generation for entirely missing modalities, and incorporating external inputs to disentangle driven from intrinsic dynamics.
