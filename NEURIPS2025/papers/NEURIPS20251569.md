## Problem

Egocentric human data is abundant and cheap to collect, yet significant domain gaps in visual appearance, sensor modalities, and kinematics between human and robot prevent effective knowledge transfer in cross-embodiment imitation learning. Standard co-training does not automatically yield transfer, and conventional domain adaptation methods (adversarial training, MMD) align only marginal distributions, discarding the fine-grained action-relevant information that robot policies require under compounding covariate shift.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251569/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The encoder consumes egocentric RGB frames at 480×640×3 (from Project Aria glasses, shared between human and robot to eliminate the camera-device gap), optional wrist-camera RGB streams (robot only), and proprioceptive vectors (joint positions for robot, end-effector xyz+euler for human), all z-score normalized per embodiment. The policy outputs a trajectory chunk of k=100 future end-effector poses in SE(3)+gripper (7D per arm, 14D bimanual), with orientation in yaw-pitch-roll euler angles; for human data the loss is masked to 3D position only since orientation and gripper are unavailable.

## Method

EgoBridge co-trains a shared transformer encoder (modality-specific stems feeding a multi-block trunk with M learnable context tokens) and a DETR-style transformer decoder head under a combined objective: standard behavior-cloning loss on both domains plus an Optimal Transport loss applied on the joint distribution of latent features and their corresponding actions. The key mechanism is a DTW-shaped OT cost: Dynamic Time Warping distance between action trajectory chunks identifies behaviorally similar human–robot pseudo-pairs, and the OT ground-cost matrix is discounted by a small scalar λ for those matched pairs, creating soft supervision that biases the Sinkhorn transport plan toward action-relevant correspondences rather than purely geometric alignment. This joint (feature, action) OT formulation, computed via differentiable entropic-regularized Sinkhorn, directly shapes the encoder to produce domain-invariant latents that preserve temporal and spatial action structure, enabling the policy to generalize to objects, scenes, and even motions observed only in human demonstrations.

## Results

On three real-world manipulation tasks, EgoBridge achieves up to 44% absolute success rate improvement over the best baseline (Laundry: 72% vs. 28% for Robot-only BC). In the Drawer task, EgoBridge reaches 47% total success rate and 33% behavior generalization (to robot-unseen drawer quadrants), while all baselines score 0–8% on behavior generalization. In the Scoop Coffee task, EgoBridge is the only method that generalizes to a novel object + scene combination seen only in human data (27% vs. 0–7% for all baselines). In the simulated Push-T benchmark, EgoBridge achieves 53% in-distribution success rate and 39% under combined observation + behavior generalization, outperforming MMD (45%/14%) and Standard OT (38%/8%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

EgoBridge outperforms all four baselines across every task and generalization setting. Against EgoMimic [1], EgoBridge gains 7–39% absolute success rate (e.g., 67% vs. 60% on Scoop Coffee in-distribution, 72% vs. 33% on Laundry SR) and is the only method achieving non-zero behavior generalization in the Drawer task (33% vs. 0%). Against MimicPlay [25], EgoBridge leads by 34–44% absolute SR (e.g., 47% vs. 14% on Drawer total, 72% vs. 28% on Laundry), with MimicPlay failing entirely on all generalization probes. Against ATM [19], EgoBridge improves by 20–44% (e.g., 67% vs. 47% on Scoop Coffee in-distribution, 33% vs. 8% on Drawer behavior generalization). In the simulation benchmark, EgoBridge outperforms the MMD baseline [30] by 8% in-distribution SR (53% vs. 45%) and by 25% under the hardest generalization setting (39% vs. 14%), confirming that joint OT alignment with DTW-guided pairing is superior to global kernel-based distribution matching.

## Relation to Prior Work

EgoBridge extends the EgoMimic line of work on egocentric human-to-robot imitation by replacing its heuristic alignment strategies (visual masking, motion retargeting) with a principled Optimal Transport objective on joint (latent feature, action) distributions. The joint-distribution OT formulation is adapted from Courty et al.'s domain adaptation work, but the key departure is the DTW-based cost shaping that exploits behavioral similarity in action trajectories to form pseudo-pairs, going beyond marginal alignment methods (MMD, standard OT) that discard action-relevant structure. The policy architecture draws on DETR-style transformers and ResNet-18 vision encoders, consistent with recent cross-embodiment policy learning, but the OT-joint loss applied on learnable context tokens is the novel architectural integration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ability to transfer entirely new behaviors (e.g., motions into a drawer quadrant never seen by the robot) from human data alone—where all baselines fail at 0%—addresses a critical bottleneck in scaling robot learning beyond teleoperation. However, the DTW-based alignment is inherently single-task and may not generalize to multi-task joint domain adaptation, as the authors acknowledge. Open questions include whether more generalizable alignment costs (language embeddings, foundation-model features) can replace DTW for multi-task settings, and whether the approach extends to internet-sourced human video without action labels. The limited evaluation scope (3 tasks, single robot platform, no error bars) leaves the robustness of the 44% improvement claim somewhat uncertain.
