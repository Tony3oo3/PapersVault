## Problem

No comprehensive benchmark exists for evaluating LLMs on atmospheric science problems, which are inherently interdisciplinary and require integrating theoretical physical modeling with heterogeneous real-world data (spatial coordinates, temperatures, wind patterns, empirical estimates) in varied formats and units. Existing general-purpose benchmarks (GSM8K, MATH, GPQA) lack domain specificity, and the only prior climate-focused benchmark (ClimaQA) relies on definition-based assessments that cannot probe the complex numerical and symbolic reasoning essential to real atmospheric science problems.

## Input / Output

The benchmark feeds LLMs graduate-level atmospheric science questions in two formats: MCQs (670 core + 240 extended) with four options requiring a boxed letter answer, and OEQs (391 questions) with subparts requiring boxed numerical values with physical units or symbolic expressions. All prompts enforce LaTeX \boxed{} output for reliable RegEx extraction. Evaluation produces per-domain accuracy (across hydrology, atmospheric dynamics, atmospheric physics, geophysics, physical oceanography), overall accuracy, and symbolic standard deviation (SymStd) measuring sensitivity to numerical perturbation. OEQ scoring uses a 5% numerical tolerance with unit conversion via the pint library, falling back to sympy symbolic equivalence and then an LLM-as-judge (GPT-4o-mini) in a cascade.

## Method

The core construction pipeline is a template-based MCQ generation framework inspired by GSM-Symbolic: domain experts transform OCR-extracted (Mathpix) graduate-level questions into reusable templates with variable placeholders, then define valid numerical ranges, granularity steps, and rule-based logical constraints (e.g., t₁ < t₂) to guarantee scientific validity of every instantiation. GPT-4o synthesizes initial Python solvers from reference solutions, which are manually verified and refined, enabling automatic answer computation for any valid variable set. Distractor options are generated via four mechanisms—diffusion (swapping two variables), confusion (altering one variable), randomization (reassigning all variables within constraints), and default scaling (×2, ×3, ×4 of the correct answer)—with randomized option ordering to mitigate positional bias. The OEQ set is individually curated without symbolic perturbation to probe deeper open-ended reasoning. For robustness evaluation, MCQ30 generates 30 unique instances per template with controlled symbolic variation, enabling measurement of how accuracy degrades under perturbation and revealing potential pattern-matching reliance.

## Results

On the MCQ10 dataset (670 questions), reasoning models achieve 70.9%–89.1% overall accuracy, with GPT-o3-mini leading at 89.1% and DeepSeek-R1 close behind at 88.51%. Instruction-tuned models range from 18.5% (Gemma-2-9B-it) to 64.18% (Gemini-2.0-Flash-Exp), a 24.92% gap below the best reasoning model. Math-augmented models (24.63%–58.36%) and domain-specific climate models (20.15%–34.93%) do not close this gap, with GeoGPT-Qwen2.5-72B (34.93%) underperforming its base model Qwen2.5-72B-Instruct-Turbo (57.01%). On the harder OEQ set (391 questions), all models score below 40%, with DeepSeek-R1 reaching the highest at 36.57%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Reasoning models dominate across all five subdomains: GPT-o3-mini (89.1%) and DeepSeek-R1 (88.51%) outperform the best instruction model, Gemini-2.0-Flash-Exp (64.18%), by 24.9 and 24.3 percentage points respectively, with the gap widening on atmospheric dynamics (87.57% vs. 58.11%) and geophysics (95.71% vs. 77.14%). Math-augmented models fail to provide a clear advantage: the strongest, Qwen2.5-Math-72B-Instruct (58.36%), trails the top instruction model by ~6 points and is far below reasoning models, suggesting mathematical specialization alone is insufficient for atmospheric science reasoning. Domain-specific climate models underperform dramatically—ClimateGPT-7B (20.15%) and ClimateGPT-70B (27.31%) score near the weakest instruction models, while GeoGPT-Qwen2.5-72B (34.93%) is 22 points below its own base model Qwen2.5-72B-Instruct-Turbo (57.01%), indicating that domain fine-tuning for tool-planning or conversational use degrades structured scientific reasoning. On the OEQ set, the same hierarchy holds but scores compress: DeepSeek-R1 leads at 36.57% versus 27.88% for the best instruction model (Gemini-2.0-Flash-Exp), and all models remain below 40%, confirming that open-ended atmospheric science problems remain substantially harder than MCQs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the symbolic-perturbation benchmarking paradigm introduced by GSM-Symbolic and VarBench to the atmospheric science domain, combining it with a template-based generation framework that enforces physical validity through rule-based constraints. It positions itself against general scientific-reasoning benchmarks (GPQA-Diamond, SciBench, PhysBench, Humanity's Last Exam) by arguing that atmospheric science's interdisciplinary, multi-unit, and model-selection-heavy nature demands a dedicated evaluation. The dual-format design (MCQs for scalable automated scoring plus OEQs for open-ended reasoning) departs from prior benchmarks that typically adopt a single format, and the cascade evaluator (quantity → symbolic → LLM-as-judge) is a methodological contribution not seen in comparable scientific benchmarks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ATMOSSCI-BENCH provides the first comprehensive, open-sourced evaluation framework for LLMs in atmospheric science, filling a clear gap between general scientific benchmarks and the domain-specific needs of climate services. The finding that domain-specific models (ClimateGPT, GeoGPT) substantially underperform their own base instruction models on structured reasoning tasks challenges the assumption that domain pre-training alone suffices, pointing toward the need for reasoning-augmented domain models. Open questions include whether the observed pattern-matching fragility under symbolic perturbation will persist as reasoning models improve, whether the benchmark's contamination risk (suggested by hypothesis tests) undermines its discriminative power, and how the OEQ evaluation (where all models score below 40%) will evolve as models gain deeper multi-step scientific reasoning.
