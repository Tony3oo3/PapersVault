## Problem

Depth completion in dynamic scenes is severely degraded by rapid ego-motion (causing RGB blur and LiDAR misalignment) and fast-moving objects (causing local depth inaccuracies), yet conventional RGB-D sensors lack the temporal resolution to handle these conditions. Event cameras offer microsecond-level, motion-sensitive signals that are naturally complementary, but no prior work has exploited event data for depth completion, and no benchmark existed to evaluate such approaches.

## Input / Output

The method consumes three synchronized inputs: a color image, a sparse depth map (derived from 16 sub-lines of a 128-line LiDAR), and an event stream (from a DAVIS346 sensor or synthesized via VID2E from ±15 ms frames). All inputs are cropped to multiples of 32 in resolution (320×256, 1216×256, or 512×256 depending on dataset). The output is a single-channel dense depth map at full input resolution, trained with a combined L1+L2 reconstruction loss plus structure-aware and motion-aware auxiliary losses.

## Method

EventDC is a UNet-style encoder-decoder with three parallel encoders (RGB, sparse depth, event) producing multi-scale features at 1/1, 1/2, 1/4, and 1/8 resolutions. Two event-conditioned deformable-convolution modules address distinct failure modes: Event-Modulated Alignment (EMA) in the encoder uses event features to predict spatial offsets that redistribute RGB and depth pixels for improved global alignment, while Local Depth Filtering (LDF) in the decoder learns a motion mask from events and applies motion-aware dynamic convolution to refine depth specifically around moving objects. The key design choice is that convolution offsets are derived from event-sensitive features rather than the target modality itself, making the sampling geometry explicitly conditioned on motion. Training uses a structure-aware gradient loss to enforce RGB-D consistency and a binary-masked motion-aware loss to supervise depth recovery in dynamic regions, with λ=1 and μ=0.1 weighting.

## Results

On the real-world EventDC-Real dataset, EventDC achieves the best performance across all metrics: RMSE 574.0 mm, MAE 179.0 mm, REL 0.0242, and δ₁.₀₅ 92.9%, outperforming the second-best method (Prompting) by 84.1 mm in RMSE and 26.1 mm in MAE. On EventDC-SemiSyn, EventDC reaches RMSE 778.8 mm and MAE 196.2 mm, surpassing the best baseline (OGNI-DC) by 53.2 mm in RMSE. On EventDC-FullSyn, EventDC achieves RMSE 620.2 mm and MAE 143.1 mm, a 53.5 mm RMSE improvement over the runner-up. The paper also reports that EventDC uses only 43.2M parameters and 1.5 GB memory, roughly one-eighth the parameters and one-third the memory of the foundation-model-based Prompting (326.9M params, 4.1 GB).

## Baselines & Comparisons

EventDC outperforms all nine baselines on every metric across all three datasets. The closest competitor on EventDC-Real is Prompting [38] (a depth foundation model with 326.9M parameters), which EventDC surpasses by 84.1 mm in RMSE and 26.1 mm in MAE while using only ~1/8 the parameters. On EventDC-SemiSyn, OGNI-DC [78] is the strongest baseline (RMSE 832.0 mm), and EventDC leads by 53.2 mm in RMSE and 1.0 percentage point in δ₁.₀₅. On EventDC-FullSyn, OGNI-DC again ranks second (RMSE 673.7 mm), with EventDC achieving a 53.5 mm RMSE advantage and a 13.0% REL improvement over Prompting. Traditional propagation-based methods (CSPN, S2D, DySPN) and refinement-based methods (SigNet, LPNet) show substantially larger gaps, with RMSE differences exceeding 200 mm on EventDC-Real, indicating that event-driven alignment provides a qualitatively different advantage in dynamic scenes rather than incremental gains over static-scene methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the depth completion literature (which has focused on RGB + sparse LiDAR inputs) by introducing event camera streams as a third input modality, specifically targeting the underexplored setting of dynamic scenes where motion blur and misalignment degrade conventional sensors. Methodologically, it builds directly on Deformable Convolution v2 (DCNv2), repurposing its offset-and-weight mechanism so that both sampling positions and modulation scalars are conditioned on event-derived features rather than on the input image itself. It also draws on the event-based vision literature (contrast maximization, event-to-frame fusion) but departs from prior event-based depth estimation works that typically estimate depth from events alone or fuse events with intensity frames, instead using events purely as a conditioning signal for a multi-modal depth completion pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The introduction of the first event-based depth completion benchmark (EventDC-Real/SemiSyn/FullSyn) fills a clear gap and provides a standardized evaluation platform for a practically important problem in autonomous driving and robotics where dynamic scenes cause RGB-D sensors to fail. The method's reliance on high-quality, precisely aligned event data and its additional computational overhead (43.2M params, 41.5 ms inference) limit immediate deployment on resource-constrained platforms, and the real-world dataset's limited scale (14.8K training frames) raises open questions about generalization to more diverse motion patterns and sensor configurations. Future work could explore whether the event-conditioned alignment strategy transfers to other dynamic perception tasks or whether lighter-weight event encoders can reduce the computational cost.
