## Problem

Latent space generative modeling has transformed image and video generation, but applying it to spatial dynamical systems is difficult because such systems involve a variable number of distinguishable entities that must remain traceable over time. Existing GNN-based approaches do not leverage the efficiency and scalability of modern latent space architectures, while prior latent-space methods for particle systems sacrifice individual entity traceability. The paper asks whether recent generative latent space techniques can be adapted to model stochastic trajectories of dynamical systems with varying entity counts while preserving per-entity identity.

## Input / Output

The method takes a short observed trajectory of N entity positions (T_o frames in R^{N×D_x}), time-invariant entity properties (e.g., atom type in R^{N×D_m}), and a randomly assigned set of unique identifiers from a fixed pool of size u ≥ N. It outputs future entity positions over T_f prediction frames. The key structural constraint is that the latent representation has a fixed size L regardless of N, so a single model handles systems with different entity counts (e.g., 9-atom ethanol vs. 21-atom aspirin, or variable numbers of pedestrians). For long-horizon tetrapeptide prediction, the model cascades ten 1,000-step rollouts, reconditioning on the final frame of each block.

## Method

LaM-SLidE introduces learnable identifier embeddings (IDs) that bridge a variable-size entity space and a fixed-size latent representation. The encoder uses cross-attention (Perceiver-IO style) to compress N entity tokens—each concatenating position, properties, and ID embedding—into L learned latent queries, while the decoder retrieves per-entity information by using ID embeddings as queries against the latent keys/values, functioning as content-based associative memory. The approximator is a flow-matching model (stochastic interpolants) built from transformer blocks that alternate attention over the spatial (latent) and temporal dimensions, conditioned on observed frames via mask tokens. Training is two-stage: first, the encoder-decoder is trained auto-encoding to reconstruct entities from latent space; second, the frozen encoder's latent representations are used to train the flow-based approximator with a data-prediction objective. The approach requires no explicit bond graphs, equivariance inductive biases, or intermediate physical quantities like velocities, relying instead on data augmentation (random rotations/translations) and the ID mechanism for structure preservation.

## Results

On the MD17 benchmark (8 small molecules, 10 conditioning → 20 prediction frames), LaM-SLidE achieves the lowest ADE/FDE across all molecules, e.g., Aspirin 0.059/0.098 vs. the next-best GeoTDM at 0.107/0.193, and Uracil 0.050/0.074 vs. 0.074/0.099. On N-Body particle systems, LaM-SLidE attains the best ADE/FDE for Charged Particles (0.104/0.238) and Gravity (0.157/0.406), and competitive second-place on Spring Dynamics (0.0070/0.0135). On the long-horizon Tetrapeptide task (1 frame → 10,000 frames), LaM-SLidE matches or surpasses MDGen on TICA (0.227 vs. 0.230) and MSM (0.224 vs. 0.235) metrics while remaining competitive on torsion-angle JSD. The method requires up to 10×–100× fewer function evaluations (NFE) than GeoTDM for comparable or better accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LaM-SLidE outperforms all seven GNN-based and diffusion baselines (RF, TFN, SE(3)-Transformer, EGNN, EqMotion, SVAE, GeoTDM) on the MD17 benchmark, achieving roughly 40–50% lower ADE/FDE than the strongest competitor GeoTDM across all 8 molecules, notably without using molecular bond information or equivariance inductive biases. On N-Body particle systems, LaM-SLidE beats GeoTDM on Charged Particles (0.104 vs. 0.110 ADE) and Gravity (0.157 vs. 0.256 ADE), while GeoTDM retains a slight edge on Spring Dynamics (0.0030 vs. 0.0070 ADE). On ETH-UCY pedestrian forecasting, LaM-SLidE achieves the best minADE on the ETH scene (0.45) and best minFDE on Hotel (0.19), tying or narrowly trailing GeoTDM on the remaining scenes, and outperforms all GAN-based (SGAN, SoPhie) and VAE-based (PECNet, Traj++, BiTraP, SVAE) methods on average. On the NBA basketball dataset, LaM-SLidE matches SVAE on Scoring minADE (0.64) and surpasses it on minFDE (1.09 vs. 1.17), while remaining competitive on Rebounding. On the long-horizon Tetrapeptide task, LaM-SLidE outperforms MDGen on TICA (0.227 vs. 0.230) and MSM (0.224 vs. 0.235) metrics, while MDGen retains a slight edge on side-chain torsion JSD (0.093 vs. 0.122), a notable result given that MDGen operates exclusively in torsion space.

## Relation to Prior Work

LaM-SLidE bridges two previously separate research threads: (1) GNN-based trajectory prediction for spatial dynamical systems (EGNN, SE(3)-Transformer, GeoTDM), which handles variable entity counts but lacks the scalability and unified representation of modern generative models, and (2) latent space generative modeling (latent diffusion, flow matching), which excels at fixed-size inputs but cannot natively handle variable entity counts. The paper adopts the Perceiver IO cross-attention architecture for encoding/decoding and the stochastic interpolants framework for the temporal approximator, but introduces a key departure: learned identifier embeddings that serve as queries to retrieve individual entity properties from a fixed-size latent representation, preserving entity traceability without sacrificing the efficiency of latent-space generative modeling. Notably, the method deliberately omits equivariance inductive biases and explicit physical structure (bonds, velocities), relying instead on data augmentation and the expressiveness of the latent space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The demonstration that a single model trained on all MD17 molecules (9–21 atoms) outperforms per-molecule equivariant GNNs without bond information or equivariance challenges the assumption that geometric inductive biases are necessary for molecular dynamics prediction. The 10×–100× reduction in function evaluations compared to GeoTDM makes the approach practically attractive for long-horizon rollouts (e.g., 10,000-frame tetrapeptide predictions). Open questions include whether the tradeoff between latent compression ratio and prediction quality can be resolved at scale, whether the identifier mechanism generalizes to truly indistinguishable entities (where traceability is unnecessary), and whether pre-training at larger scale could yield the cross-domain foundation-model behavior the authors hint at but do not fully realize.
