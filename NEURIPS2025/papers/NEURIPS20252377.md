## Problem

EEG datasets exhibit significant topological heterogeneity—electrode counts and placements vary widely across public corpora—making it difficult to build a single model that generalizes across montages. Existing approaches either train bespoke models per layout, discard up to 80% of non-shared channels, or flatten channel-time into long sequences incurring quadratic O((S·C)²) attention complexity that becomes prohibitive for dense caps. This paper addresses the need for a single, montage-agnostic architecture that scales linearly with electrode count while maintaining competitive accuracy.

## Input / Output

The model accepts raw multi-channel EEG signals of shape (B × C × T) where C is arbitrary (20–62 channels in experiments) along with normalized 3D electrode coordinates for spatial encoding. Signals are segmented into non-overlapping patches of 40 timestamps, and during pre-training a 50% mask ratio is applied. The encoder produces a fixed-size topology-agnostic latent representation of Q learned queries (Q ≪ C), which the decoder then expands back to channel-specific reconstructed patches (pre-training) or pools into a single vector for classification (fine-tuning). The key constraint is that the latent dimension Q is independent of C, so the same model handles any electrode count without architectural modification.

## Method

LUNA's core mechanism is a Channel-Unification Module that applies cross-attention between Q learned queries and the C channel-wise patch features, projecting variable-channel inputs into a fixed R^{Q×E} latent space in a permutation-invariant manner (inspired by Set Transformer and Perceiver IO). 3D electrode coordinates are encoded via NeRF-inspired sinusoidal positional encoding to provide spatial awareness without predefined graphs. After unification, a stack of Transformer encoder blocks with RoPE operates on the S temporal tokens in the compact latent space, reducing the effective sequence length from S·C to S and decoupling temporal attention cost from electrode count. Pre-training uses a masked-patch reconstruction objective (Smooth L1 on both masked and visible patches) plus a query specialization loss that penalizes off-diagonal similarity in the query-channel affinity matrix to encourage diverse, non-redundant spatial filters. The overall complexity is O(B·S·Q·C·E) for unification and O(B·S²·Q·E) for temporal attention, both linear in C, versus O(B·S²·C²·E) for full spatio-temporal attention.

## Results

LUNA achieves state-of-the-art results on TUAR (0.921 AUROC, LUNA-Huge) and TUSL (0.802 AUROC, LUNA-Huge), outperforming all compared self-supervised and supervised baselines. On TUAB abnormality detection, LUNA-Huge reaches 0.8957 AUROC and 81.57% balanced accuracy, competitive with large-scale models like LaBraM-Huge (0.9162 AUROC) and CBraMod (0.9156 AUROC) while using fewer parameters. On the unseen 62-channel SEED-V emotion recognition benchmark, LUNA-Large achieves 39.18% balanced accuracy, trailing CBraMod (40.91%) by ~2 pp. LUNA reduces FLOPs by 300× and GPU memory by up to 10× compared to quadratic-attention baselines on high-density EEG.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On TUAR and TUSL, LUNA-Huge sets new state-of-the-art, beating the strongest prior self-supervised baselines FEMBA-Large (0.915 AUROC on TUAR, 0.714 on TUSL) by 0.006 and 0.088 AUROC respectively, and surpassing all supervised methods (EEGNet, EEG-GNN, GraphS4mer) by 0.08–0.19 AUROC. On TUAB, LUNA-Huge (0.8957 AUROC) trails the largest self-supervised models—LaBraM-Huge (0.9162), CBraMod (0.9156), and LaBraM-Base (0.9022)—by 0.01–0.02 AUROC, but clearly outperforms mid-size SSL baselines like BIOT (0.8815), EEGFormer-Base (0.8670), and BrainBERT (0.8530), as well as all supervised baselines (SPaRCNet 0.8676, ST-Transformer 0.8707). On SEED-V (unseen 62-channel montage), LUNA-Large (39.18% Bal. Acc.) lags behind CBraMod (40.91%) and LaBraM-Base (39.76%) by 1.7–2.7 pp, though it still exceeds all supervised baselines (SPaRCNet 29.49%, CNN-Transformer 36.78%, ContraWR 35.46%). The efficiency advantage is most pronounced against LaBraM and CBraMod, whose quadratic spatial attention causes FLOPs and memory to grow rapidly with channel count, whereas LUNA's linear-in-C unification keeps compute near-constant. FEMBA (Mamba-based) is the closest competitor on TUAR/TUSL but uses 4–11× fewer parameters than LUNA-Huge while achieving lower AUROC. CEReBrO (85M params) achieves 0.8916 AUROC on TUAB, slightly below LUNA-Huge's 0.8957, while EEG2Rep (no size reported) reaches 0.8843 AUROC, also below LUNA-Huge.

## Relation to Prior Work

LUNA adapts the learned-query / cross-attention mechanism from Set Transformer and Perceiver IO—originally designed for permutation-invariant set abstraction and variable-sized inputs—to the specific problem of unifying heterogeneous EEG electrode layouts into a fixed latent space. It departs from prior EEG foundation models (LaBraM, CBraMod, EEGFormer) that flatten channel and temporal dimensions into long sequences or use alternating spatial/temporal attention with quadratic complexity in the dominant dimension; instead, LUNA performs channel unification first via a small set of learned queries, then applies temporal self-attention only on the compact latent sequence, decoupling compute from electrode count. The NeRF-inspired 3D sinusoidal encoding of electrode coordinates and the auxiliary query-specialization loss are additional design choices not present in the Perceiver/Set Transformer lineage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Topological heterogeneity has been a persistent bottleneck preventing data aggregation and cross-dataset transfer in EEG; LUNA's linear-in-channels unification makes high-density (64–256 channel) and long-window recordings tractable, which is practically important for clinical and research systems that routinely exceed the 20–30 channels of most public benchmarks. The SOTA results on TUAR and TUSL, combined with 300× FLOPs and up to 10× memory reductions, position LUNA as a strong efficiency-accuracy trade-off for topology-agnostic EEG analysis. However, the 2–3 pp gap on SEED-V (an unseen 62-channel montage) reveals that reliance on pre-training positional encodings limits zero-shot generalization to substantially different layouts, and the limited pre-training diversity (three dominant montages) remains an open question for broader deployment.
