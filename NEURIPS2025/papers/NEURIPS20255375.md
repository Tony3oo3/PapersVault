## Problem

Existing zeroth-order optimization theory guarantees convergence to an arbitrary stationary point but does not characterize which particular solution is reached when multiple minimizers exist. This paper asks whether the standard two-point estimator implicitly regularizes toward flat minima (small trace of Hessian), analogous to how SGD's noise is known to favor flat solutions, and provides the first formal convergence guarantees to such flat minima.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes only scalar function evaluations f(x) at perturbed points x ± λu (no gradient or Hessian access), along with an initialization x₀, stepsize η, and smoothing parameter λ. It outputs a randomly selected iterate x_τ that is an (O(ε/d²), ε)-approximate flat minimum: near-optimal in loss and near-minimal in trace of Hessian among all minimizers. The smoothing parameter λ is the critical hyperparameter controlling the trade-off between optimization error (grows with λ²) and the strength of the implicit flatness regularization (also grows with λ²).

## Method

The key insight is that the two-point estimator is an unbiased gradient estimator for the Gaussian-smoothed function f_λ(x) = E_u[f(x+λu)], which by Taylor expansion equals f(x) + (λ²/2)Tr(∇²f(x)) + o(λ²), thereby implicitly adding a trace-of-Hessian regularizer. Rather than treating the λ²Tr(∇²f(x)) term as a bias to be suppressed (as in standard analysis), the paper analyzes convergence directly on the regularized objective F(x) = f(x) + (λ²/2)Tr(∇²f(x)). The main technical challenge is bounding the second moment of the two-point estimator by ‖∇F(x)‖², which requires computing 8th-order Gaussian moments via Isserlis' theorem (105 pairing terms versus 3 for the standard 4th-order case). Under convexity and three-times differentiability with Lipschitz third derivatives, the paper proves that T = O(d⁴/ε²) iterations with λ = O(ε^{1/2}/d^{3/2}) yield an (O(ε/d²), ε)-approximate flat minimum, establishing the first convergence-to-flat-minima guarantee for zeroth-order methods.

## Results

The main theoretical result (Corollary 2) shows that zeroth-order optimization with the two-point estimator finds (O(ε/d²), ε)-approximate flat minima in T = O(d⁴/ε²) iterations for convex, three-times-smooth functions. Empirically, on overparameterized SVMs and logistic regression (a5a, w5a with D=10,000), zeroth-order optimization consistently decreases the trace of Hessian throughout training while gradient descent leaves it nearly unchanged, though ZO is slower to minimize loss and maximize test accuracy. On RoBERTa-Large fine-tuning (SST-2, SST-5, TREC with K=32/256), both GD and ZO reduce the trace of Hessian, but GD generally achieves lower training loss, smaller trace of Hessian, and higher test accuracy in most settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares zeroth-order optimization (ZO, Algorithm 1) against standard gradient descent (GD) as the primary baseline across three experimental settings. On the toy scale-invariant problem (Example 2.1), ZO with λ>0 drives the imbalance B_t = (‖y_t‖²−‖z_t‖²)/2 toward zero (flat/balanced solution), while GD and ZO with λ→0 maintain B_t at its initial value. On overparameterized SVMs and logistic regression (a5a, w5a), ZO achieves comparable training loss and test accuracy to GD but consistently reduces the trace of Hessian, confirming the theoretical prediction of implicit flat-minima regularization. On RoBERTa-Large few-shot fine-tuning (SST-2, SST-5, TREC), GD generally outperforms ZO on training loss, trace of Hessian, and test accuracy, though both methods exhibit decreasing trace of Hessian. The paper does not claim ZO outperforms GD on standard metrics; rather, the key finding is that ZO implicitly regularizes toward flat minima even when it is slower to converge on loss.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the standard two-point zeroth-order estimator of Nesterov and Spokoiny [74], whose convergence analysis treats the Gaussian-smoothing bias term O(λ²) as an error to be controlled. The key departure is reinterpreting that bias term—specifically (λ²/2)Tr(∇²f(x))—as an implicit regularizer to be optimized rather than suppressed, thereby connecting zeroth-order optimization to the flat-minima literature (SAM, Entropy-SGD, SGD with label noise) that had previously focused exclusively on first-order methods. The proof technique extends classical zeroth-order convergence analysis by bounding 8th-order Gaussian moments via Isserlis' theorem to control the second moment of the estimator under the regularized objective F(x) = f(x) + (λ²/2)Tr(∇²f(x)).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first proof that a derivative-free method converges to flat minima, which is practically significant because zeroth-order optimization is increasingly the go-to approach for memory-constrained LLM fine-tuning, black-box attacks, and reinforcement learning where gradients are infeasible. The result also provides a theoretical explanation for why zeroth-order methods remain effective in high dimensions: the decreasing trace of Hessian validates the assumption Tr(∇²f(x)) ≪ dL₁ used in prior work to obtain dimension-independent rates. Key limitations include the convexity requirement (nonconvex flat-minima convergence remains open) and the need for third-order smoothness, though the authors note the framework extends to first-order methods like SAM and SGD on smoothed losses.
