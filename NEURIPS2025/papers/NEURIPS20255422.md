## Problem

Existing federated dynamic pruning frameworks adjust sparse model topologies using deterministic, magnitude-based decisions on myopic aggregated information from a small subset of participating clients, leading to greedy adjustments, unstable topologies under data heterogeneity, and high communication costs from transmitting full gradients. No prior work has formalized federated pruning as a sequential decision-making problem or applied probabilistic bandit-based strategies to stabilize topology evolution across partial client participation.

## Input / Output

The framework takes local client datasets (32×32 RGB images for CIFAR-10/CINIC-10/SVHN, 64×64 for TinyImageNet, or text for TinyStories) and a target density d′ as input, operating in a cross-device FL setting with N=100 clients and 10 participating per round. During inner loops, clients upload only their sparse weights; during outer loops, they additionally upload just the top-(K−κ) gradient magnitude indices for inactive weights rather than full gradients. The output is a globally aggregated sparse model consisting of weights W and a binary topology mask m with density ≤ d′, evaluated on ResNet18/ShuffleNetV2 (CV) and GPT-2-32M (NLP).

## Method

FedRTS reframes federated topology adjustment as a Combinatorial Multi-Armed Bandit (CMAB) problem where each weight link is an arm and the server selects a super-arm of K active links per outer loop. The core TSAdj mechanism maintains a Beta(αᵢ, βᵢ) posterior distribution per link, updated via Bayes' rule using fused outcomes that combine aggregated and individual-client weight magnitudes (for active arms) and gradient magnitudes (for inactive arms), weighted by a trade-off ratio γ. Instead of deterministic top-K magnitude pruning, the server samples ξᵢ ~ Beta(αᵢ, βᵢ) and activates the top-K sampled values, yielding probabilistic decisions with lower variance and farsighted memory of past observations. A two-loop training procedure alternates between inner-loop weight updates (fixed topology, semi-outcomes) and outer-loop topology adjustment (full outcomes, TSAdj invocation every ΔT=10 rounds), with a theoretical regret upper bound of O(Σ Δ_max L² log T / Δ²_{s,k}) established under L-continuity, independence, and mean-field assumptions.

## Results

On the TinyStories NLP benchmark with GPT-2-32M, FedRTS achieves a perplexity of 18.54 at 50% density (vs. 20.56 for dense FedAVG) while reducing communication cost from 260.41 MB to 138.84 MB (46.7% reduction). At 30% density, FedRTS maintains PPL of 18.56 with only 86.44 MB communication cost, and at 20% density achieves PPL 19.93 with 60.34 MB. On CIFAR-10 with ResNet18, FedRTS delivers a 5.1% accuracy improvement over the best baseline (FedDST/FedMef) while matching or reducing communication costs, and achieves up to 33.3% lower communication cost compared to SOTA frameworks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedRTS consistently outperforms all five federated pruning baselines (FedDST, FedSGC, FedMef, FedTiny, PruneFL) on both CV and NLP tasks across all tested densities. On TinyStories, FedRTS achieves the lowest PPL (18.54 at 50% density) while also matching the lowest communication cost (138.84 MB, comparable to FedDST's 138.30 MB), surpassing even the dense FedAVG reference in both PPL and accuracy. FedSGC is the weakest baseline, degrading severely at lower densities (PPL 43.00 at 20% vs. FedRTS's 19.93). FedMef is the strongest competitor, but FedRTS still leads by 1.0–2.0 PPL points and 0.004–0.012 accuracy at each density while using 15–35% less communication. On CIFAR-10 with ResNet18, FedRTS gains 5.1% accuracy over FedDST/FedMef at comparable communication cost, and the advantage grows under high data heterogeneity (low Dirichlet α) and low client participation ratios (10–30%), where deterministic baselines degrade more sharply.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the two-loop dynamic sparse training paradigm (Evci et al., 2020) and the top-gradient upload strategy from FedMef (Huang et al., 2024), but departs fundamentally from all prior federated pruning methods (FedDST, FedSGC, FedTiny, PruneFL) by reframing topology adjustment as a Combinatorial Multi-Armed Bandit problem. Where existing frameworks make deterministic, myopic decisions based solely on the current round's aggregated weights and gradients, FedRTS introduces Thompson Sampling with Beta posterior distributions that accumulate farsighted information across rounds. The probabilistic decision mechanism is adapted from combinatorial Thompson sampling for semi-bandits (Wang & Chen, 2018), but its application to federated sparse topology selection is novel.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to provide a theoretical regret bound for federated pruning topology adjustment, grounding an otherwise heuristic process in bandit theory. The practical impact is meaningful for resource-constrained edge devices: FedRTS achieves comparable or better accuracy than dense FedAVG at 30% density while reducing communication costs by up to 33%, which is significant for cross-device FL deployments. Open questions include whether the CMAB framing extends to structured pruning, whether the independence assumption on weight magnitudes (Assumption 3.2) holds for smaller models, and whether the potential bias toward data-rich clients in highly heterogeneous settings can be mitigated without sacrificing the communication savings.
