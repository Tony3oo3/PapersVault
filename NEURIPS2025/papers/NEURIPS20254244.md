## Problem

While nonlinear activation functions are widely recognized for increasing neural network expressivity, their effect on the geometry of the model gradient feature space and the NTK condition number has not been previously characterized. This paper asks whether ReLU activation changes the angular separation between data points in the gradient feature space, and whether this geometric effect translates into better NTK conditioning and faster worst-case convergence of gradient descent.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254244/cropped_page5_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The analysis operates on fully-connected ReLU networks with L hidden layers of width m under the NTK parameterization, taking the infinite-width limit (m→∞) at fixed finite depth L. The key computed quantities are the model gradient angle φ(x,z) between any input pair, the NTK condition number κ, and the resulting convergence rate bound (1−κ⁻¹)ᵗ. The comparison baseline is a linear network of identical architecture whose NTK reduces to the Gram matrix regardless of depth, isolating the pure effect of the ReLU nonlinearity.

## Method

The paper introduces the model gradient angle φ(x,z) and proves, via a recursive embedding-angle analysis using a composition function g that maps angles through each ReLU layer, that ReLU strictly increases the angle between similar data points in the gradient feature space, with the increase amplifying at each additional layer. In the infinite-width-then-depth limit, all non-parallel input pairs converge to a universal separation angle of arccos(1/4) ≈ 75.5°, independent of their original input angle. This better separation directly implies a smaller NTK condition number: the paper proves κ_ReLU < κ_linear = κ_Gram for any non-degenerate dataset, and shows κ converges to (n+4)/3 in the infinite-depth limit, independent of data distribution. The underlying mechanism is that ReLU's derivative acts as a binary gate whose activation pattern differs across samples, increasing directional diversity in the high-dimensional gradient space and thereby raising the smallest NTK eigenvalue.

## Results

In the infinite-width-then-depth limit, the model gradient angle between any two non-parallel inputs converges to arccos(1/4) ≈ 75.5°, regardless of the original input angle, and the NTK condition number converges to (n+4)/3, independent of data distribution. On MNIST, test accuracy peaks at 97.57% for depth L=6, then slightly decreases to 97.19% at L=12, revealing an optimization-generalization trade-off at large depth. Empirically, deeper ReLU networks (L=1 to 10) consistently show smaller NTK condition numbers and faster training convergence than shallower ones across MNIST, Fashion-MNIST, SVHN, and LibriSpeech, while their linear counterparts (no activation) show no such depth-dependent improvement.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is between ReLU-activated networks and linear networks (identical architecture but with identity activation), which serves as the activation-free baseline. Across all four datasets (MNIST, Fashion-MNIST, SVHN, LibriSpeech), the ReLU network always achieves a larger minimum pairwise model gradient angle (better feature separation) and a smaller NTK condition number (better conditioning) than its linear counterpart at every depth L=1 through 10. The linear network's NTK condition number remains constant (equal to the Gram matrix condition number κ₀) regardless of depth, while the ReLU network's condition number decreases monotonically with depth. For convergence speed, deeper ReLU networks (e.g., L=10) consistently reach lower training loss faster than shallower ones (e.g., L=1) on all datasets, confirming the theoretical prediction that smaller κ yields faster worst-case convergence. The paper also notes that the better separation phenomenon generalizes to GeLU and tanh activations (verified numerically), where the infinite-depth gradient angle converges to 90° rather than 75.5°.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the NTK convergence theory of Jacot et al. (2018), Du et al. (2018/2019), and Liu et al. (2020/2022), which established that the NTK condition number κ controls worst-case gradient descent convergence rates. It departs from prior NTK spectral analyses (Nguyen et al. 2021, Xiao et al. 2020, Murray et al. 2023) by providing a sharp with/without-activation comparison at finite depth without data distribution assumptions, rather than relying on spherical symmetry or infinite-depth limits. The closest prior result is Xiao et al. (2020), who showed NTK condition number decreases with depth in the infinite-depth limit; this paper extends that to finite depth and isolates the activation as the causal mechanism. It is orthogonal to Arora et al. (2018), which showed depth helps in linear (activation-free) networks via implicit pre-conditioning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean theoretical explanation for why activation functions matter beyond expressivity: they act as binary gates that increase directional diversity of gradient features, thereby improving NTK conditioning and worst-case convergence. The universal limit result (all non-parallel data pairs converge to arccos(1/4) ≈ 75.5° separation in the infinite-width-then-depth limit) is a striking structural insight, but it also reveals a trade-off: extreme depth leads to trivial generalization (near-random predictions on unseen data), as confirmed empirically at L=8+ on MNIST. Open questions include extending the analysis beyond ReLU to other activations analytically, understanding the finite-depth generalization-optimization trade-off more precisely, and whether these results transfer to convolutional or residual architectures outside the fully-connected NTK regime.
