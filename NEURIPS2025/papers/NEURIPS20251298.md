## Problem

Automated mathematical theory formation requires navigating a combinatorially vast space of definitions and conjectures, where most paths lead to trivial mathematics. Prior systems like HR and AM relied on hard-coded interestingness heuristics to guide search, and no existing framework supported the full open-ended theory-formation loop (defining concepts, conjecturing, proving) as a learnable process. This paper addresses both gaps by introducing an RL environment for theory formation and framing the discovery of interestingness measures as a program-synthesis problem.

## Input / Output

The system takes a minimal initial knowledge graph (e.g., zero, successor, and equality for number theory; field generators for F_27) and uses GPT-4o-mini as the LLM for both evolution and abstraction. It produces a Python program implementing an interestingness function I(m, S) → ℝ that scores entities in the context of the current theory, which in turn drives a fixed policy template to select production-rule actions. The final output is an evolved knowledge graph of definitions, conjectures, and theorems, evaluated by how many of 180 (number theory) or 67 (F_27) curated ground-truth entities are rediscovered within 60-second episodic rollouts using Z3 as the backend prover.

## Method

FERMAT formalizes theory formation as an MDP over knowledge graphs, with actions comprising definition production rules (Exists, Compose, Specialize, etc.), conjecture production rules (Implication, Equivalence, Nonexistence, Exclusivity), and proof actions backed by Z3. EvoAbstract is an LLM-driven evolutionary algorithm that extends FunSearch with an abstraction-learning mechanism: it maintains k=4 island populations, periodically invokes a second LLM (L_abs) to extract reusable subroutines from high-scoring programs into per-island abstraction libraries, and conditions the evolution LLM (L_var) on these libraries when generating new candidates. This interplay between evolution and abstraction promotes modular, interpretable interestingness programs that compose validated sub-components. The objective is to maximize cumulative extrinsic reward (matching ground-truth entities) over episodic rollouts, with the interestingness program serving as a learned intrinsic reward that guides the policy's action selection.

## Results

The primary metric is the number of ground-truth mathematical entities discovered per episodic rollout (cumulative extrinsic reward) across three starting theories. EvoAbstract achieves 23.98 (±10.50) on arithmetic_base, 9.62 (±2.97) on succ_zero_eq, and 9.82 (±4.83) on ff_27, substantially outperforming the best hard-coded HR baseline (Comprehensibility: 8.55, 8.23, 5.38) and the one-shot GPT-4o baseline (6.46, 5.26, 2.36). FunSearch (the ablation without abstraction) achieves comparable performance at 22.41, 10.23, and 11.34 respectively, with lower variance on two of the three settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against FunSearch [40] (the ablation without the abstraction phase), EvoAbstract achieves a modest gain on arithmetic_base (23.98 vs. 22.41) but underperforms on succ_zero_eq (9.62 vs. 10.23) and ff_27 (9.82 vs. 11.34), with notably higher variance on arithmetic_base (±10.50 vs. ±2.68). The authors attribute this to an 'abstraction lock-in' effect where the LLM struggles to produce diverse samples after early convergence. Against the HR [8] hard-coded baselines, EvoAbstract dominates across all three settings: it outperforms the strongest HR measure (Comprehensibility) by roughly 2.8× on arithmetic_base (23.98 vs. 8.55) and by ~1.8× on ff_27 (9.82 vs. 5.38). The one-shot GPT-4o baseline performs only marginally better than random (5.26 vs. 4.68 on succ_zero_eq), and even its best-of-64 sample (8.21) is outperformed by the simple Comprehensibility heuristic, highlighting that evolutionary search is essential for discovering effective interestingness measures.

## Relation to Prior Work

FERMAT generalizes the symbolic theory-formation system HR (Colton et al., 2000) by recasting its production rules as actions in a Markov Decision Process, enabling RL-based exploration over the space of definitions, conjectures, and proofs. EvoAbstract extends the FunSearch (Romera-Paredes et al., 2024) LLM-driven evolutionary program synthesis with an abstraction-learning phase that extracts and reuses subroutines across generations. The key conceptual departure from prior theory-formation systems (AM, HR, Theorema) is framing interestingness as a learnable intrinsic reward synthesized as an interpretable Python program, rather than relying on hand-coded heuristics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first RL environment supporting the full open-ended theory-formation loop (definition synthesis, conjecture generation, and proof verification), and it demonstrates that evolved interestingness measures substantially outperform both hand-coded HR heuristics and one-shot LLM generation. However, the scope remains narrow: experiments are limited to elementary number theory and finite fields over F₂₇, the prover is Z3 (a bounded SMT solver rather than an interactive theorem prover like Lean), and the authors identify 'bottleneck' entities and abstraction lock-in as barriers to deeper theory construction. Open questions include scaling to richer domains, autonomously synthesizing production rules, and integrating full interactive provers to enable tabula-rasa proof learning.
