## Problem

Existing heterogeneous dynamic GNNs rely on a decoupled spatial-then-temporal learning paradigm that weakens spatio-temporal interactions and causes 'attention discontinuity'—attention coefficients are computed independently at each snapshot without referencing historical attention patterns. Additionally, incremental architectural improvements (stacking attention layers, per-snapshot parameters) lead to high model complexity that scales linearly with time window length, limiting scalability and efficiency.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253686/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sequence of T heterogeneous graph snapshots (each with typed nodes, typed relations, and node features) along with text-based node type prompts processed by an LLM (LLaMA3-8B) during a one-time preprocessing step. The LLM's final hidden-layer embeddings are used to initialize GRU hidden states via a learned Q-K similarity computation. The output is a linear projection of the T-step spatio-temporal representations into β future steps, which feed a two-layer MLP for downstream tasks (link prediction, node classification, or node regression).

## Method

SE-HTGNN unifies spatial and temporal modeling through a dynamic attention mechanism: relation-wise GRUs take the current intermediate representation and the previous time step's attention coefficients as input, storing historical attention in the GRU hidden state to guide subsequent attention computation—eliminating the need for a separate temporal modeling stage. Neighbor aggregation is simplified to a non-parametric GCN (removing node-level attention), reducing parameters and optimization difficulty. LLM-enhanced prompts provide semantically meaningful initial attention coefficients by computing softmax-normalized Q-K similarities between source and target node type embeddings, replacing arbitrary zero or random initialization. The final temporal projection is a single linear transformation from T time steps to β prediction steps, yielding a 2.7×–10× speedup over state-of-the-art baselines while achieving up to ~5% improvement in accuracy across link prediction, classification, and regression tasks.

## Results

SE-HTGNN achieves the best performance across all four datasets and three task types. On link prediction, it reaches 93.13% AUC on OGBN-MAG and 91.08% AUC on Aminer, outperforming the strongest baselines (HTGNN at 91.21% and CasMLN at 88.53% respectively). On node classification (YELP), it attains 44.24% Macro-F1, and on node regression (COVID-19, 30-day), it achieves MAE 497 and RMSE 1069. In terms of efficiency, SE-HTGNN achieves a 2.7× speedup over the SOTA (CasMLN) on OGBN-MAG and nearly a 10× speedup on the long-sequence COVID-19 dataset (64s vs. 637s GPU training time).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SE-HTGNN consistently outperforms all 11 baselines across every dataset and metric. Against the strongest heterogeneous dynamic GNN baselines, it improves AUC by +2.11% over HTGNN (91.21%) on OGBN-MAG and by +2.89% over CasMLN (88.53%) on Aminer. On YELP node classification, it surpasses CasMLN (42.21% Macro-F1) by +4.81% and DHGAS (41.99%) by +5.03%. On the COVID-19 regression task, it reduces MAE from 536 (DHGAS) to 497, a 7.27% improvement, and RMSE from 1112 to 1069. Notably, HGT+ and DHGAS (Transformer-based compress methods) fail with OOM on the large-scale OGBN-MAG dataset, while SE-HTGNN handles it efficiently. In terms of training speed, SE-HTGNN is 2.7× faster than CasMLN on OGBN-MAG (102s vs. 272s) and approximately 10× faster on COVID-19 (64s vs. 637s), demonstrating that the unified spatio-temporal design eliminates the need for a separate temporal modeling stage.

## Relation to Prior Work

SE-HTGNN departs from the dominant decoupled spatial-temporal paradigm in heterogeneous dynamic GNNs (HTGNN, DyHATR, CasMLN, DHGAS), which first aggregates spatial information per snapshot and then applies a separate sequence model (RNN/Transformer) over the resulting representations. Conceptually, it draws on two threads: (1) the simplification of hierarchical attention in static HGNNs (SeHGNN, MHGCN) showing that node-level attention is largely redundant, and (2) the use of LLMs for graph learning (CasMLN), though here LLMs serve only to initialize GRU hidden states rather than provide auxiliary features. The key architectural departure is the 'dynamic attention mechanism' that folds temporal modeling into the spatial aggregation step via relation-wise GRUs carrying historical attention coefficients, eliminating the separate temporal module entirely.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in the simultaneous achievement of better accuracy (~3% over SOTA) and substantially lower computational cost (up to 10× speedup on long-sequence data), which addresses a real bottleneck for deploying HDGNNs on large-scale or long-horizon temporal graphs. The 'attention discontinuity' framing provides a useful diagnostic for why decoupled approaches underperform, though the fix (GRU-based attention memory) is a natural extension rather than a fundamental breakthrough. Open questions include whether the LLM initialization component generalizes beyond the tested node-type counts, whether the parameter overhead from high-dimensional LLM embeddings limits scalability, and whether the dynamic attention mechanism transfers to other dynamic graph settings (e.g., fully dynamic edge-level updates rather than snapshot-based evolution).
