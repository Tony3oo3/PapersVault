## Problem

Existing data-efficient instruction tuning methods select either entire instruction-response sequences or individual tokens, but these granularities are in inherent tension: sequence-level curation treats all tokens equally and is vulnerable to token noise and Zipf's-law bias, while token-level selection is computationally prohibitive due to the quadratic scaling of token counts relative to sequences. No principled framework unifies both selection levels into a single optimization objective.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pool of instruction-response pairs (e.g., 270K sequences from FLAN V2, COT, DOLLY, Open Assistant) and a frozen base LLM, and produces learned Bernoulli hyper-parameters defining selection probabilities at both the sequence level (which pairs to include) and the token level (which tokens within each selected sequence to emphasize). In the targeted IT transfer variant, the LLM itself—augmented with two LoRA classifier heads—generates these selection probabilities for unseen validation data, enabling zero-shot transfer without re-optimizing the solver. A final elastic parameter ε softens binary token selections from {0,1} to {ε, 1−ε} to control variance during the inner-loop fine-tuning.

## Method

QCS formulates a bi-level coreset objective where the outer loop jointly selects sequence weights w and token weights v to minimize full-dataset validation loss, while the inner loop trains the LLM on the weighted subset. The key technical contribution is a probabilistic re-parameterization that replaces discrete binary selection variables with Bernoulli distributions, eliminating the need for LLM-scale Hessian inverses that make traditional bi-level coreset solvers intractable. The solver is learned via hierarchical policy gradients (HPGEs) that estimate gradients of the expected loss with respect to the Bernoulli parameters using only forward passes—no backpropagation through the LLM is required—and convergence is guaranteed under mild L-smoothness conditions with asymptotic equivalence to the original discrete objective at Monte Carlo rate. For practical deployment, the inner loop uses LoRA adapters for parameter-efficient fine-tuning, and a transfer variant attaches LoRA classifier heads to the frozen LLM to generate selection probabilities for new data via entropy-based test-time adaptation.

## Results

On targeted IT (5% data, TYDIQA), QCS with token-level selection achieves 56.6 on LLAMA-2-7B, surpassing LESS (54.4), Random (52.7), and even full-data training (54.0). On free-form reasoning benchmarks (BBH, GSM8K), QCS outperforms LESS by a wide margin—on LLAMA-3-8B GSM8K, QCS exceeds LESS by over 12 points (56.7). In regular IT at 5% data (LLAMA-2-7B), QCS with 50% token selection achieves 49.46 average across ARC/MMLU/TruthfulQA, beating IFD (48.28) and DQ (42.32). QCS also requires substantially less computation than LESS: ~30 min warmup vs ~6 min, but <1 min for the selection step vs ~48 min, and 0 GB gradient storage vs 17.7 GB.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LESS (the strongest sequence-selection baseline), QCS wins decisively on free-form long-response tasks: on BBH and GSM8K, QCS outperforms LESS by large margins (up to 12+ points on LLAMA-3-8B GSM8K), while on short-answer tasks (MMLU, TYDIQA) QCS is competitive but slightly behind LESS. Against Random selection, QCS significantly outperforms across all four targeted IT benchmarks and all model scales. In regular IT (Table 3), QCS with 50% token selection achieves the best average scores on both LLAMA-2-7B and LLAMA-2-13B at 5%, 10%, and 15% data budgets, beating IFD by ~1.2 points and DQ by ~5–7 points on average. Notably, QCS with only 5% data often matches or exceeds full-data training (e.g., 56.6 vs 54.0 on LLAMA-2-7B TYDIQA), demonstrating that token-level prioritization within selected sequences is the key differentiator over sequence-only methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate threads in data-efficient instruction tuning—sequence-level selection (as in LESS, IFD, DQ) and token-level selection (as in token pruning/importance methods)—into a single 'quadratic' coreset objective derived from bilevel optimization. It builds directly on the bi-level coreset formulation of Borsos et al. (NeurIPS 2020) and the Bernoulli re-parameterization of Zhou et al. (ICML 2022), but departs from both by introducing a joint sequence×token weight structure and a hierarchical policy-gradient solver that avoids the Hessian inverses required by influence-function-based methods like LESS. The explicit connection to empirical influence functions (Appendix A) positions QCS as a principled generalization of LESS rather than an independent alternative.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

QCS provides a theoretically grounded, computationally tractable path to data-efficient IT that goes beyond sequence-only curation, with the key practical finding that token-level prioritization elevates the performance ceiling on long-form, reasoning-heavy tasks (BBH, GSM8K) where sequence-only methods plateau. The probabilistic solver's avoidance of Hessian inverses and its back-propagation-free training via policy gradients make it more scalable than influence-function approaches, though the inner-loop LLM training (even with LoRA) remains a non-trivial cost. Open questions include whether the token-level benefits persist at much larger model scales, how the method interacts with RLHF/DPO post-training stages, and whether the transfer-learning variant (Eq. 15) generalizes beyond the few-shot targeted IT setup tested here.
