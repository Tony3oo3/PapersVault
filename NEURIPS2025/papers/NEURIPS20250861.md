## Problem

Existing LLM-based patient simulators for medical dialogue evaluation focus primarily on factual accuracy of symptom reporting but fail to capture the multi-dimensional patient characteristics—personality, language proficiency, medical history recall, and cognitive confusion—that significantly shape real doctor-patient consultations. This limits their utility for training or evaluating doctor LLMs in realistic multi-turn clinical settings where patient behavior, not just symptom content, determines consultation quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250861/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a 24-item structured clinical profile (demographics, social history, medical history, ED visit details) derived from MIMIC-IV/ED/Note, a persona configuration specifying values across four axes (personality, CEFR-based language level, recall level, confusion level), and the doctor's current utterance in a multi-turn dialogue. It produces a single simulated patient response per turn, constrained to the history-taking phase of a first-time ED visit (no test results, no longitudinal state changes). The doctor LLM only receives basic patient information, not the full symptom profile, mimicking real clinical information asymmetry.

## Method

PATIENTSIM is a prompt-engineering framework that configures an LLM (Llama 3.3 70B selected from 8 candidates) to role-play a patient by combining a structured clinical profile with a 4-axis persona specification yielding 37 unique combinations. Clinical profiles are constructed via a hybrid pipeline: structured data from MIMIC-IV tables is supplemented by free-text discharge notes processed with Gemini-2.5-Flash for extraction, diagnosis-alignment validation (5-point scale), and context-aware imputation of missing lifestyle fields. The persona prompt encodes personality as six consultation-specific styles (impatient, overanxious, distrustful, overly positive, verbose, neutral), language proficiency as CEFR-consolidated levels with sampled vocabulary constraints, and recall/confusion as binary settings, with high-confusion patients restricted to neutral personality to avoid axis overlap. Evaluation uses a three-tier protocol: LLM-as-judge persona fidelity scoring, sentence-level NLI-based factuality against profile items, and clinician-rated plausibility for unsupported (profile-undefined) statements, with the final model validated by four general practitioners across 108 dialogues.

## Results

Across eight LLM backbones evaluated on persona fidelity (4-point scale), Llama 3.3 70B achieves the highest average score of 3.68, with particular strength in the Confused axis (4.00) and Personality (3.92). For sentence-level factuality, Llama 3.3 70B attains the best entailment rate of 98.1% and the lowest contradiction rate of 1.9% for profile-supported sentences, while also scoring the highest plausibility (3.963/4) for unsupported sentences. Human evaluation by four clinicians confirms the framework's quality with an overall average of 3.89/4 across six criteria, and 3.75/4 for educational usefulness.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares eight LLMs as backbones for the PATIENTSIM patient simulator. Llama 3.3 70B (the selected model) outperforms all other models on overall persona fidelity (3.68 vs. 3.61 for GPT-4o mini and 3.57 for Gemini-2.5-Flash), driven by perfect scores on the Confused axis (4.00) and near-top Personality (3.92). On sentence-level factuality, Llama 3.3 70B achieves the highest entailment (98.1%) and lowest contradiction (1.9%) among all models, surpassing Gemini-2.5-Flash (97.8%/2.2%) and GPT-4o mini (96.8%/3.2%). For unsupported-sentence plausibility, Llama 3.3 70B scores 3.963, edging out Gemini-2.5-Flash (3.953) and GPT-4o mini (3.929). Smaller models (≤8B) consistently underperform larger ones (≥70B) on both factuality and plausibility, with Qwen2.5-7B showing the weakest entailment (93.9%) and highest contradiction rate (6.1%). Notably, general LLM benchmark performance does not correlate with simulation fidelity, as Llama 8B outperforms Qwen 72B on persona fidelity despite having far fewer parameters.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of LLM-based patient simulation and multi-axis persona modeling. Prior clinical simulation work (e.g., MedAgents, MDAgents, AgentClinic) prioritizes diagnostic task accuracy over interaction realism, while existing patient simulators (e.g., Du et al., Liao et al.) focus almost exclusively on factual symptom recall with at most superficial persona descriptors like Big Five traits. PATIENTSIM departs from both lines by grounding patients in real MIMIC-IV/ED clinical data and systematically varying four clinically motivated persona axes (personality, language proficiency, recall level, cognitive confusion) into 37 distinct combinations, then evaluating both factual accuracy and persona fidelity with a structured NLI-based pipeline. It also bridges the gap between psychological counseling simulators (which model rich internal states but lack clinical grounding) and diagnostic consultation simulators (which are clinically accurate but persona-flat).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PATIENTSIM provides the first open-source, privacy-compliant, and clinically validated patient simulator that can be customized for training or evaluating doctor LLMs across diverse patient presentations, addressing a practical bottleneck in medical AI research where standardized patients are costly and hard to scale. The finding that persona fidelity does not scale with model size (Llama 8B outperforms Qwen 72B on persona axes) while factual accuracy does, challenges the common assumption that larger models are uniformly better at simulation tasks and suggests targeted prompt engineering or fine-tuning may be more effective for persona consistency. Key limitations include reliance on a single hospital dataset (MIMIC), text-only interaction (no nonverbal cues), only five target diseases, and human evaluation by just four clinicians, leaving open questions about generalizability to other clinical settings, languages, and disease categories.
