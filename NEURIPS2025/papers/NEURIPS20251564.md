## Problem

Existing 3D generation evaluation methods rely on static 2D renderings and operate solely at the object level, failing to capture spatial continuity, structural complexity, and fine-grained local defects. They also overlook physical material properties (albedo, metallicness, roughness) when assessing textures, and lack part-level diagnostics that would enable interpretable failure analysis. Hi3DEval addresses these gaps by introducing hierarchical (object + part + material) evaluation grounded in 3D-aware representations.

## Input / Output

The system consumes 3D meshes rendered as 16-frame turntable videos at 512×512 and 25 FPS in multiple formats (RGB, Normal, Shading), plus relit sequences under four point-light configurations and six HDRI environments for material assessment. Text prompts and reference images condition the evaluation for prompt-alignment scoring. Outputs are continuous quality scores on a 0–10 scale across nine dimensions: five object-level (Geometry Plausibility, Geometry Details, Texture Quality, Geometry-Texture Coherency, Prompt Alignment), two part-level (GP and GD per segmented component), and four material-subject (Details & Complexity, Colorfulness & Saturation, Consistency & Artifacts, Material Plausibility).

## Method

The scoring system is hybrid, combining two complementary branches. For object-level and material-subject evaluation, a video-based pipeline leverages the pretrained InternVideo2.5 encoder with a lightweight Conv3D prediction head; it is trained in two stages—first via contrastive learning to align rendered 3D videos with CLIP prompt embeddings (bridging the domain gap from natural video to synthetic renders), then via supervised regression with SmoothL1 loss plus an auxiliary ranking loss to produce dimension-specific scores. For part-level evaluation, a 3D-based pipeline projects PartField's pretrained geometric feature fields into a scoring latent space, applies adjacency-based k-NN pooling to handle variable face counts per part, and introduces cross-attention (global context) and self-attention (intra-part) modules to capture both holistic structure and local detail. Annotations are generated by a Multi-agent Multimodal Annotation Pipeline (M²AP) that orchestrates five MLLM agents (GPT-4.1, Claude 3.7, Gemini 2.5 Pro, Grok-3, O3/O4-mini) with a reflection-based self-correction mechanism and physical-plausibility checks, achieving L1 loss of 0.257 against human ratings versus 0.70–1.10 for single agents.

## Results

On the object-level pairwise rating alignment (Table 1), Hi3DEval achieves the highest accuracy across all five dimensions for both text-to-3D (GP 0.774, GD 0.725, TQ 0.755, GTC 0.749, PA 0.726) and image-to-3D (GP 0.718, GD 0.703, TQ 0.753, GTC 0.732, PA 0.710) settings, outperforming the strongest baseline GPTEval3D by 5–10 percentage points. At the material-subject level (Table 2), the method reaches 0.767–0.773 pairwise accuracy on text-to-3D and 0.723–0.771 on image-to-3D, again leading all baselines by a clear margin. The M²AP annotation pipeline achieves an L1 loss of 0.257 against human annotations, substantially lower than any single-agent baseline (best single agent: o3/o4-mini at 0.702).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Hi3DEval consistently outperforms all six baselines across every evaluated dimension. Against GPTEval3D (the strongest baseline, using GPT-4o for pairwise comparison), the proposed video-based scorer gains +8.4 pp on Geometry Plausibility (0.774 vs. 0.690) and +10.6 pp on Geometry-Texture Coherency (0.749 vs. 0.667) in the text-to-3D setting. Image-based metrics (CLIP Score, ViCLIP, Aesthetic Score) cluster around 0.55–0.68, well below the proposed method's 0.70–0.77 range, confirming that static single-view features are insufficient for 3D quality assessment. ImageReward performs worst on GTC (0.513), suggesting its text-to-image reward signal does not transfer to 3D geometry-texture alignment. At the material-subject level, Hi3DEval leads by 7–19 pp over the best image-based baseline (e.g., 0.773 vs. 0.690 on Colorfulness & Saturation for text-to-3D), with the largest gap on Consistency & Artifacts (0.733 vs. 0.563), a dimension that explicitly requires multi-lighting perception. T3Bench, included in the supplementary comparison (Table R5), achieves 0.631–0.673 on text-to-3D, still trailing Hi3DEval by 5–14 pp.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 3D generation evaluation lineage (T3Bench, GPTEval3D, 3DGen-Bench, T23DAQA) by introducing two new evaluation axes—part-level geometric assessment and material-subject evaluation with reflectance cues—that prior frameworks entirely omitted. For part-level scoring, it builds directly on PartField's pretrained 3D feature fields, repurposing a segmentation model's geometric embeddings as input to a lightweight attention-based scorer. For object-level and material scoring, it departs from the dominant static multi-view image paradigm by adopting video-based representations (InternVideo2.5) trained via contrastive alignment, and for annotation it replaces single-agent GPT-4V labeling with a multi-agent MLLM pipeline (GPT-4.1, Gemini 2.5 Pro, Claude 3.7, Grok-3, o3/o4-mini) that incorporates reflection and physical-plausibility checks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Hi3DBench (15,300 assets, 30 generative models, 4K+23K+11K hierarchical annotations) is likely to become a standard reference benchmark for 3D generation evaluation, filling a gap where prior suites covered only object-level pairwise comparisons. The multi-agent annotation pipeline at ~$0.15/object offers a practical, scalable alternative to costly human studies, and the hybrid video+3D scoring paradigm demonstrates that spatio-temporal representations meaningfully outperform static images for 3D quality assessment. Key limitations remain: the framework is restricted to static, object-centric assets (no scenes or dynamics), part-level evaluation depends on segmentation quality that degrades for collapsed or abstract shapes, and the material evaluation, while using HDRI relighting, still does not require explicit PBR disentanglement from the generator.
