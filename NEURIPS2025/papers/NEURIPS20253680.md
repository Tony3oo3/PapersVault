## Problem

Prototype-based interpretable models have primarily addressed discrete classification, but extending them to continuous regression faces two fundamental issues: one-to-one input-to-prototype comparisons lack directional information (whether the output should be greater or less than a prototype's label), and similarity-weighted averaging over all prototypes causes unwarranted deviations even when the input exactly matches a prototype. This paper tackles both gaps by redefining how prototypes participate in continuous prediction.

## Input / Output

The model takes a single RGB face image (UTKFace, ages ≤84) or a stack of four consecutive frames (Car Racing) as input and produces a continuous scalar (age) or a 3-dimensional action vector (steering ∈ [-1,1], acceleration ∈ [0,1], braking ∈ [0,1]). Unlike ProtoPNet's patch-based prototypes, ProtoPairNet uses full-image latent representations of the same dimensionality as the encoder output, trading localised part-level explanations for a simpler whole-image geometric reasoning process. Prototypes are projected onto real training instances so that each prototype pair has a human-viewable visualisation.

## Method

ProtoPairNet replaces one-to-one prototype comparisons with prototypical pairs, where each pair (p_{2k-1}, p_{2k}) defines an axis in latent space along which the target label varies linearly. Given an input's latent vector z, the prediction branch projects z onto each ProtoPair axis and computes a label via linear interpolation between the two prototypes' labels based on z's relative position along the axis (Eq. 4), which guarantees exact recovery of a prototype's label when z coincides with that prototype. A pair relevance branch uses Gumbel-softmax over negative squared distances from z to each axis to select the single most relevant pair, ensuring decision sparsity and eliminating interference from irrelevant prototypes. Training proceeds in three stages: (1) joint SGD optimisation of the encoder and prototypes with a composite loss (MAE/MSE + label-weighted cluster loss + axis-distance loss + intra-pair diversity loss), (2) projection of learned prototypes onto real training instances using a direction-preserving selection criterion (cosine similarity, diversity score, axis distance), and (3) post-projection fine-tuning with prototypes fixed at their visualisation latents to restore alignment.

## Results

On UTKFace age prediction with a ResNet-50 backbone, ProtoPairNet achieves an MAE of 4.59 and R² of 0.87, outperforming black-box baselines (MAE 4.71–4.82, R² 0.86) and dramatically surpassing other prototype-based regression methods (HPN: MAE 5.08; INSightR-Net: MAE 14.01; ExPeRT: MAE 17.36). On the Car Racing behavior-cloning task, ProtoPairNet achieves an average reward of 223.97, exceeding both the PPO black-box expert (221.36) and the interpretable PWNet baseline (220.61). Across four backbone architectures (ResNet-50, EfficientNet-B0, VGG-19, ViT-Small), ProtoPairNet maintains competitive or superior performance while providing interpretable pair-based explanations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ProtoPairNet outperforms all five compared baselines on their respective tasks. Against HPN [24], ProtoPairNet reduces MAE from 5.08 to 4.59 (≈10% improvement) while also providing visualizable prototypes, whereas HPN's prototypes are abstract and non-visualizable. Against INSightR-Net [16] and ExPeRT [17], both of which rely on similarity-weighted averaging over all prototypes, ProtoPairNet achieves dramatically lower MAE (4.59 vs. 14.01 and 17.36 respectively), demonstrating that the pair-based reasoning avoids the interference problem inherent in weighted-averaging approaches. In the Car Racing task, ProtoPairNet (reward 223.97) surpasses PWNet [21] (220.61), which uses hand-picked prototypes, and also exceeds the PPO black-box expert [36] (221.36), showing that interpretability does not come at a performance cost. The k-Means and PWNet* variants from [21] perform poorly (rewards −2.09 and −9.48), further highlighting the advantage of learned prototypical pairs over fixed or manually selected prototypes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the ProtoPNet paradigm of case-based reasoning from discrete classification to continuous regression by replacing one-to-one image-to-prototype comparisons with comparisons against learned prototypical pairs. Prior interpretable regression models (HPN, INSightR-Net, ExPeRT, PWNet) relied on similarity-weighted averaging over all prototypes, which the authors identify as lacking directional information and vulnerable to interference from irrelevant prototypes. The key conceptual departure is the geometric formulation: each prototype pair defines an axis in latent space along which the target varies linearly, and the prediction is derived from the input's projected position on that axis, with Gumbel-softmax selecting a single most relevant pair rather than aggregating over all.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work fills a notable gap in interpretable deep learning by providing a principled, geometrically grounded approach to regression that preserves the transparency of prototype-based models while resolving the directional ambiguity inherent in one-to-one comparisons. The user studies provide empirical validation that pair-based reasoning aligns with human intuition and improves prediction accuracy over single-reference explanations. Limitations include the absence of part-based (patch-level) explanations, evaluation on only two tasks (age prediction and car racing), and reliance on full-image prototypes rather than localized parts. Open questions include whether the pair-based formulation extends naturally to prototypical parts, higher-dimensional output spaces, and larger-scale regression benchmarks.
