## Problem

Diffusion-based planners can represent complex multimodal trajectory distributions but lack a principled way to enforce safety constraints at deployment without retraining or per-step QP solves. Existing approaches either condition on constraints (requiring retraining), solve a QP at every denoising step (computationally expensive), or project samples onto a safe set (disrupting trajectory smoothness and handling only pointwise rather than distribution-level constraints). This paper asks: can distribution-level constraints be integrated directly into the reverse diffusion process of a pretrained model using first-order updates?

## Input / Output

At each physical timestep, the method consumes the current state observation (which becomes the first state of the planned trajectory), a differentiable constraint function g(x) with its gradient, the pretrained score function ∇log p(x_t), and a deterministic inverse dynamics model. It outputs a full safe trajectory over the planning horizon but executes only the first action (computed via the IDM from the first two planned states) before replanning, following a receding-horizon scheme. Constraints are formulated as expectation inequalities E[g(x)] ≤ 0 over the trajectory distribution rather than hard per-sample bounds, and DCBF conditions are rewritten as pairwise state-transition inequalities along the trajectory.

## Method

The key insight is reformulating the DDPM reverse update as a stochastic gradient Langevin dynamics step (Eq. 6), so that constrained sampling algorithms can be applied directly to the denoising process. Three constrained Langevin variants modify the reverse update: (1) Projected, which applies a projection operator onto the constraint set after each Langevin step; (2) Primal-Dual, which alternates a primal update incorporating constraint gradients with a dual ascent on Lagrange multipliers; and (3) Augmented Lagrangian, which adds a quadratic penalty and slack variable for tighter inequality enforcement. Discrete CBFs are embedded as trajectory-level constraints (h(x^{τ+1}) ≥ (1−α)h(x^τ)) directly within these updates, eliminating per-step QP solves. An inverse dynamics model maps the planned state transition to a unique control action, and a receding-horizon loop executes only the first action before replanning, mirroring MPC to mitigate IDM and model errors.

## Results

On Hopper, Primal-Dual (PD) achieves 3551±10 reward with 0.10±0.03 planning violations and 0.41±0.30% implementation violation rate, while ALM reaches 3553±14 reward with 0.08±0.02 planning violations and 0.09±0.11% violation rate—both at ~0.003s per diffusion step versus 0.93s for QP-based baselines. On Swimmer, PD and ALM substantially outperform the vanilla Diffuser in reward (86.4±9.2 and 88.7±5.4 vs. 57.4±8.7) while reducing planning violations from 8.38 to 0.17 and 0.08 respectively. In the time-varying PyBullet SafetyBallRunning task, PD and ALM reduce implementation violations by 99% (0.02 vs. 2.20 for Diffuser) with violation rates of 2.2% and 1.6% versus 37.7% for the unconstrained baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the vanilla Diffuser [3], both PD and ALM reduce planning constraint violations by 96–99% (e.g., Swimmer: 0.08–0.17 vs. 8.38) while matching or exceeding reward (Swimmer: 86.4–88.7 vs. 57.4). Compared to Conditional Diffuser [4], which barely reduces violations (8.02 in Swimmer), PD and ALM achieve near-zero violations and substantially higher rewards. Versus SafeDiffuser [9] and Projected Diffusion [10], which achieve zero planning violations by solving a QP at every diffusion step, PD and ALM introduce only negligible residual violations (0.08–0.17) but at roughly 1/300–1/400 the per-step computation time (0.0027–0.0031 s vs. 0.90–0.93 s). In the time-varying PyBullet SafetyBallRunning task, PD and ALM reduce implementation violation rates to 2.2% and 1.6% versus 37.7% for Diffuser and 21.9% for SafeDiffuser, while also improving reward (722.2 and 696.1 vs. 646.4 and 820.8 respectively). The Projected method, while achieving zero planning violations, suffers from higher online violation rates (25.8% in PyBullet) and 170% more computation than PD, attributed to hard projections disrupting trajectory smoothness and IDM feasibility.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper synthesizes three previously separate lines of work: (1) diffusion models for trajectory planning (Janner et al. 2022), (2) constrained sampling via Langevin Monte Carlo in the Wasserstein space (Chamon et al. 2024; Bubeck et al. 2018), and (3) discrete control barrier functions for safety-critical control (Agrawal & Sreenath 2017). The key conceptual move is reframing the DDPM reverse process as a Stochastic Gradient Langevin Dynamics step, then injecting classical constrained-optimization updates (projected gradient, primal-dual, augmented Lagrangian) directly into the denoising loop. The projected method is explicitly extended from Christopher et al. (2024), while the PD and ALM formulations are novel adaptations of the Chamon et al. framework to the diffusion setting. No prior work combined distribution-level constrained sampling with DCBF trajectory constraints and a receding-horizon IDM execution loop in a single framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap: prior safe-diffusion methods (SafeDiffuser, Projected Diffusion) solve a QP at every denoising step, incurring 100× the compute of first-order alternatives. Showing that PD/ALM achieve ~99% violation reduction at ~10% overhead is practically important for real-time robotic control. However, the method offers no formal 100% constraint-satisfaction guarantee, assumes known differentiable constraints, and lacks robustness analysis under model or environmental uncertainty—limitations that matter for safety-critical deployment. The receding-horizon + IDM pipeline introduces its own error source (IDM generalization), and the paper's own ablation (Table 9) shows performance collapses when executing more than one step before replanning, constraining the practical speedup one can extract.
