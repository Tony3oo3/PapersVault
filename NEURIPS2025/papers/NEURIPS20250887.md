## Problem

TD, FQI, and PFQI have traditionally been understood as differing only in the number of updates toward a target value function (one, infinite, and finite respectively), but this view fails to capture their true convergence relationships and can lead to incorrect conclusions (e.g., that TD convergence implies FQI convergence). No prior work provided a unified mathematical framework that simultaneously characterizes the convergence conditions of all three algorithms and their interdependencies under linear function approximation in the off-policy setting.

## Input / Output

The analysis operates on the algebraic structure of the problem: the state-action covariance matrix Σ_cov = ΦᵀDΦ, cross-covariance matrix Σ_cr = ΦᵀDP_πΦ, and mean feature-reward vector θ_φr = ΦᵀDR, all derived from the MDP, feature matrix Φ, sampling distribution μ, and policy π. The output is a set of necessary and sufficient conditions (involving consistency, semiconvergence of iteration matrices, positive semi-stability, and index constraints) for each algorithm's convergence, along with explicit learning rate bounds (e.g., α ∈ (0, ε) where ε is determined by the eigenvalues of Σ_cov − γΣ_cr) and the novel rank invariance condition on the feature matrix.

## Method

The paper shows that TD, FQI, and PFQI are all instances of the same preconditioned iterative method x_{k+1} = (I − MA)x_k + Mb solving the identical LSTD linear system (Σ_cov − γΣ_cr)θ = θ_φr, differing only in their preconditioner M: TD uses a constant M = αI, FQI uses the data-feature adaptive M = Σ_cov^{-1}, and PFQI uses M = αΣ_{i=0}^{t-1}(I − αΣ_cov)^i, which interpolates between the two as t ranges from 1 to ∞. This reframing introduces matrix splitting (specifically proper splitting) into the convergence analysis for the first time, yielding necessary and sufficient conditions for each algorithm in terms of the consistency of the target system and semiconvergence of the corresponding iteration matrix H = I − MA. A new condition called rank invariance (Rank(Φ) = Rank(ΦᵀD(I−γP_π)Φ)) is introduced as necessary and sufficient for universal consistency of the target system, and is shown to be distinct from and independent of the linearly independent features assumption. The framework also yields an encoder-decoder interpretation of TD convergence, proves that the linearly independent features assumption can be dropped for on-policy stochastic TD, and establishes surprising counterexamples where TD converges but FQI does not (and vice versa), even with linearly independent features.

## Results

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical experiments or quantitative benchmarks. The headline results are: (1) a unifying preconditioned-iterative-method view showing TD, FQI, and PFQI all solve the same LSTD linear system with different preconditioners (αI, Σ_cov⁻¹, and αΣ(I−αΣ_cov)ⁱ respectively); (2) a new 'rank invariance' condition (Rank(Φ) = Rank(ΦᵀD(I−γP_π)Φ)) shown to be necessary and sufficient for universal consistency of the target system; (3) a closed-form learning rate bound ε = min_{λ≠0} 2·Re(λ)/|λ|² for TD stability (Corollary 6.3); and (4) counterexamples (Appendix G.3) with linearly independent features where TD is stable but FQI diverges, and vice versa, refuting the common intuition that more target updates always help.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper contains no empirical baselines or experimental comparisons; all contributions are theoretical. In terms of prior theoretical results, the paper improves upon Tsitsiklis and Van Roy [40] by proving on-policy TD stability without requiring linearly independent features (Theorem 6.4), and corrects Ghosh and Bellemare [17], who claimed linearly independent features alone suffice for a unique TD fixed point in the off-policy setting (the paper shows rank invariance is also needed). Compared to Schoknecht [34], who gave sufficient conditions for TD convergence, Corollary 6.2 provides necessary and sufficient conditions. For FQI, the paper shows that previous 'necessary and sufficient' conditions from Asadi et al. [1] and Xiao et al. [44] are actually only sufficient (Appendix I). The paper also refines Fellows et al. [16] and Che et al. [11] by showing that increasing target updates (larger t) can destabilize PFQI when features are not linearly independent, a nuance absent from prior work.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the LSTD framework [10, 9, 28] and the ODE-based convergence analysis of Tsitsiklis and Van Roy [40] for TD, but introduces matrix splitting and preconditioning as the first such perspective in the convergence analysis of TD, FQI, and PFQI. It departs from the traditional 'number of updates toward target' view (TD = 1, FQI = ∞, PFQI = finite) by showing all three are the same iterative method solving the same LSTD system with different preconditioners, and corrects prior claims by Ghosh and Bellemare [17] and Xiao et al. [44] that were only sufficient rather than necessary-and-sufficient conditions. The rank invariance condition introduced here was overlooked by previous work that assumed linearly independent features alone guaranteed uniqueness of the TD fixed point.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides the first necessary-and-sufficient convergence conditions for TD, FQI, and PFQI without requiring linearly independent features, and reveals that TD convergence does not necessarily imply FQI convergence (and vice versa), contradicting the intuitive 'more updates = more stable' narrative. The finding that target networks (increasing t) can destabilize PFQI when features are not linearly independent has direct practical implications for deep RL practitioners using slowly-updated target networks. Open questions include extending the analysis to nonlinear function approximation, policy optimization (beyond evaluation), and characterizing when the preconditioner transition from TD to FQI is monotonically stabilizing.
