## Problem

Existing multi-sensor fusion for end-to-end autonomous driving relies on either flatten fusion (attention over compressed features with no explicit 3D spatial grounding) or BEV fusion (dense grid projections with high computational and memory overhead). This paper asks whether compact, physically interpretable Gaussian representations can serve as an efficient intermediate carrier that balances spatial awareness, interpretability, and computational cost for planning-centric driving tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251603/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view RGB images (front, left-front, right-front at 448×250 on NAVSIM; single front camera at 1024×384 on Bench2Drive) and LiDAR point clouds projected onto the BEV plane, along with camera extrinsics for geometric projection. It produces a sequence of 2D ego-vehicle waypoints over a fixed planning horizon and a BEV semantic map constructed via probabilistic Gaussian superposition. The map construction head is detached at inference to save compute, and the planning output is a single refined trajectory selected from an anchor vocabulary of 20 candidates.

## Method

GaussianFusion initializes 512 2D Gaussians uniformly across the driving scene, each parameterized by mean, scale, rotation, semantic logits, and two feature streams: explicit features (updated via deformable attention with geometrically sampled query points from point and image features) and implicit features (updated via vanilla cross-attention with global multi-sensor features, used exclusively for planning). Four iterative Gaussian encoder blocks refine these parameters, with a self-attention layer and MLP updating physical attributes from explicit features at each step. A cascade planning head then refines anchor trajectories over two stages: first querying the top-m nearest Gaussians to each trajectory point (Gaussian Spatial Attention), then attending over all Gaussians (Gaussian Cross Attention), with each stage's output feeding the next as a new anchor. The dual-branch design decouples scene reconstruction (explicit) from planning cues (implicit), and the 2D Gaussian formulation enables supervision from readily available BEV semantic maps rather than dense 3D occupancy labels.

## Results

On the NAVSIM navtest benchmark with a V2-99 backbone, GaussianFusion achieves 92.0 PDMS, surpassing the previous best (Hydra-MDP++ at 91.0) by 1.0 points. With a ResNet34 backbone, it reaches 88.8 PDMS, outperforming DiffusionDrive (88.1) and ARTEMIS (87.0). Under the stricter EPDMS metric, GaussianFusion scores 85.0, exceeding DiffusionDrive (84.0) and ARTEMIS (83.1). On the closed-loop Bench2Drive benchmark, it achieves 79.1 DS, outperforming the strongest learning-based baseline TF++ (76.9) by 2.2 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GaussianFusion outperforms all 17 baselines on both benchmarks. On NAVSIM (ResNet34), it beats the strongest prior method DiffusionDrive by 0.7 PDMS (88.8 vs. 88.1), with the largest gains on Drivable Area Compliance (+1.0) and Ego Progress (+0.8). Under the V2-99 backbone, it surpasses Hydra-MDP++ and Hydra-MDP-C by 1.0 PDMS (92.0 vs. 91.0), primarily driven by a 2.5-point advantage in EP. On the stricter EPDMS metric, GaussianFusion leads DiffusionDrive by 1.0 (85.0 vs. 84.0) and ARTEMIS by 1.9 (85.0 vs. 83.1), with gains concentrated in DAC and Lane Keeping. On Bench2Drive, it exceeds the best learning-based baseline TF++ by 2.2 DS (79.1 vs. 76.9), with particularly large margins in overtaking (+26.8) and emergency braking (+2.3). The only method it trails is the rule-based PDM-Lite (92.8 DS), which has access to privileged simulator information unavailable to learning-based approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

GaussianFusion sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: Gaussian-based scene representation (GaussianFormer, GaussianFormer-2) and multi-sensor fusion for end-to-end driving (TransFuser, BEVFormer). It adapts the 3D Gaussian occupancy-prediction framework to a 2D driving context, replacing dense BEV grids with a sparse set of 2D Gaussians that are iteratively refined via deformable and cross-attention over multi-modal features. The anchor-based cascade planning head draws from VADv2 and Hydra-MDP, while the dual-branch explicit/implicit feature split is a novel architectural choice not present in prior Gaussian or fusion methods. The paper explicitly positions itself as a middle ground between flatten fusion (interpretable but unstructured) and BEV fusion (structured but computationally expensive).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in demonstrating that a sparse Gaussian representation can match or exceed dense BEV fusion in planning quality while reducing FLOPs by ~39% and latency by ~26%, which is meaningful for real-time deployment. However, the method still depends on 3D bounding box and road topology labels for supervision, limiting its applicability in settings where such annotations are unavailable. The authors acknowledge that the model exhibits conservative driving biases (delayed turns, reduced overtaking) inherited from training data distribution, and that performance under sensor degradation (fog, occlusion) remains untested. Open questions include whether the 2D Gaussian assumption holds for multi-level road structures and whether the approach generalizes to sensor suites beyond camera + LiDAR.
