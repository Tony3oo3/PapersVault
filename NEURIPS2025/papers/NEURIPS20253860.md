## Problem

Current IRSTD evaluation relies on fragmented pixel-level metrics (IoU_pix, nIoU_pix, F1_pix) and target-level metrics (Pd, Fa) that fail to provide a holistic view of model capability, overlook systematic failure-mode analysis, and are confined to dataset-specific training-testing paradigms that obscure cross-domain generalization. This paper identifies that these limitations collectively produce misleading performance rankings and prevent meaningful diagnosis of where and why models fail.

## Input / Output

The framework consumes 256×256 grayscale thermal infrared images, their corresponding binary ground-truth masks, and model-generated grayscale probability maps (values in [0,1] thresholded at 0.5 for binarization). It produces a single hIoU score (product of target-level localization IoU and per-matched-target pixel-level segmentation IoU) plus a fine-grained error decomposition: four localization error subtypes (S2M, M2S, ITF, PCP) summing to 1−IoU_tgt^loc, and three segmentation error subtypes (MRG, ITF, PCP) summing to 1−IoU_pix^seg. Evaluation is conducted cross-dataset across IRSTD1k, SIRST, and NUDT, with models trained on one dataset and tested on all three.

## Method

The core contribution is a hierarchical evaluation framework built on three components. First, the hIoU metric multiplicatively combines target-level localization (IoU_tgt^loc, computed as matched-target count over total targets) with pixel-level segmentation quality (IoU_pix^seg, the mean per-pair IoU within matched targets), so that poor performance in either dimension proportionally degrades the score. Second, the OPDC (Overlap Priority with Distance Compensation) matching strategy replaces the rigid centroid-distance-only criterion: it first applies an IoU>0.5 overlap constraint via the Hungarian assignment algorithm to enforce shape coherence, then performs a secondary distance-based re-matching (threshold 3 px) for residual low-overlap pairs. Third, the error analysis method decomposes the complement of each hIoU component into named subtypes (e.g., Single-to-Multi Mismatch, Interference, Perception, Merging) that quantify distinct failure modes, enabling targeted diagnosis rather than a single aggregate score.

## Results

Under the proposed hIoU metric on IRSTD1k_TE, DNANet achieves the best holistic score of 0.557, surpassing MSHNet (0.549) and MRF3Net (0.553), despite MSHNet leading on conventional pixel-level metrics (IoU_pix=0.650, F1_pix=0.788, Fa=11.539×10⁻⁶). Cross-dataset evaluation reveals severe generalization gaps: IRSTD1k-trained MRF3Net achieves 0.694 hIoU on SIRST_TE but drops to 0.512 on NUDT_TE, while SIRST-trained models degrade to 0.124–0.459 hIoU on IRSTD1k_TE. The OPDC matching strategy improves Pd by up to 6% (e.g., ACM: 0.798→0.858) and reduces Fa by up to 36% (e.g., MSHNet: 11.539→7.686) compared to the original distance-only criterion.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper benchmarks 14 deep learning IRSTD methods under both conventional and proposed hIoU metrics, revealing that metric choice significantly alters rankings. MSHNet [20] achieves the best conventional scores (IoU_pix=0.650, F1_pix=0.788, Fa=11.539×10⁻⁶) on IRSTD1k_TE but ranks second in hIoU (0.549) behind DNANet [17] (0.557), demonstrating that pixel-level dominance does not guarantee holistic localization-segmentation quality. MRF3Net [44] (0.5M params) emerges as the strongest cross-dataset generalizer, achieving 0.694 hIoU on SIRST_TE and 0.512 on NUDT_TE, outperforming much larger models like SeRankDet [6] (108.9M params, 0.684/0.494) and UIUNet [34] (50.5M, 0.644/0.457). DNANet [17] maintains the most balanced performance across all three test sets (0.557/0.687/0.504), while lightweight methods like ACM [9] (0.5M) and RPCANet [32] (0.7M) show the weakest cross-dataset transfer (hIoU dropping below 0.35 on unseen datasets). The OPDC matching strategy consistently improves Pd and reduces Fa across all 14 methods relative to the original centroid-distance criterion from [17], confirming that the prior matching protocol was overly restrictive.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant IRSTD evaluation paradigm that relies on fragmented pixel-level (IoU_pix, nIoU_pix, F1_pix) and target-level (Pd, Fa) metrics computed under a dataset-specific train-test protocol. It builds directly on the centroid-distance target matching criterion introduced by DNANet [17], extending it with an overlap-priority constraint (OPDC) to reduce false rejections of valid predictions. Conceptually, the paper shifts the field's focus from architectural innovation to evaluation methodology, arguing that the existing metric suite creates a mismatch between what is measured and what the task actually requires (joint localization + segmentation). The hierarchical hIoU metric and the fine-grained error taxonomy are novel contributions that have no direct predecessor in the IRSTD literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that top-conventional-metric methods (e.g., MSHNet) do not necessarily achieve top holistic performance under hIoU, combined with the severe cross-dataset degradation observed across all 14 methods, challenges the community's reliance on single-dataset benchmarks and isolated metrics. The error decomposition framework provides actionable diagnostic signals (interference vs. perception vs. merging errors) that can guide targeted algorithmic improvements. Limitations include the restriction to three infrared-only datasets, absence of statistical significance testing, and the O(n³) cost of the assignment-based OPDC matching, which may limit scalability to very dense target scenes. Open questions include whether the hIoU weighting (multiplicative coupling) is optimal, how the framework extends to multi-frame or video IRSTD, and whether cross-dataset evaluation should become a standard reporting requirement.
