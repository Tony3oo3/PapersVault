## Problem

AIGC image detectors achieve near-perfect accuracy on images from their training generator but generalize poorly to unseen architectures, a gap attributed to shortcut learning. This paper identifies a specific mechanism—latent prior bias—where detectors overfit to learnable patterns tied to the initial noise vector z_T rather than fundamental generative artifacts, and demonstrates this via a white-box attack that consistently fools detectors by optimizing only z_T.

## Input / Output

The detector consumes a 224×224 RGB image (resized and normalized with CLIP or ImageNet statistics via differentiable Kornia transforms) and outputs a single logit for real/fake classification. The adversarial attack operates in latent space: it optimizes a 1×4×64×64 noise tensor z_T through a frozen Stable Diffusion v1.4 pipeline (35 DDIM steps + VAE decode) to produce on-manifold adversarial images, requiring ~79 GB VRAM on an A100 for end-to-end backpropagation through the full generation path.

## Method

The paper proposes On-Manifold Adversarial Training (OMAT), a two-stage procedure. First, a white-box attack iteratively optimizes the initial latent noise z_T (SGD, lr=1e-3, up to 100 steps) against a frozen detector while keeping the generator and text prompt fixed, yielding 6,000 on-manifold adversarial images that remain valid generator outputs but fool the detector. Second, the baseline detector (CLIP ViT-L/14 + Linear or ResNet50) is fine-tuned on the original SDv1.4 training set augmented with these adversarial examples (labeled fake, with a dynamically increasing loss weight λ_adv up to 3.0), using LoRA (rank 4–16) on the CLIP backbone or full fine-tuning. Because the adversarial examples differ from standard fakes only in z_T-derived characteristics while retaining intrinsic generative artifacts, OMAT forces the detector to discard z_T-dependent shortcuts and learn robust f_gen features. The paper also introduces GenImage++, a test-only benchmark of ~130k images from FLUX.1, SD3, SDXL, and SD1.5 under diverse prompting strategies (long-form, multi-style, photorealistic) to evaluate generalization against modern DiT-based generators.

## Results

On the GenImage benchmark, the proposed OMAT CLIP+LoRA (Rank 4) detector achieves a state-of-the-art average accuracy of 94.63% across eight generator subsets, a +15.43% improvement over its non-adversarially trained CLIP+Linear baseline (79.20%). On the newly introduced GenImage++ benchmark (featuring FLUX.1 and SD3 outputs), the same model reaches 96.78% average accuracy, substantially outperforming the next-best method RINE (88.40%). On the in-the-wild Chameleon dataset, the model achieves 66.05% accuracy, surpassing the best prior result under the same SDv1.4 training protocol (AIDE at 62.60%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The OMAT CLIP+LoRA (Rank 4) model outperforms all 16 compared baselines on the GenImage average accuracy (94.63% vs. 87.67% for the best generator-leveraging method DRCT/UniFD and 86.88% for the best standard forensic method AIDE). The gains are most dramatic on out-of-distribution generators: +30.00% on ADM, +39.81% on BigGAN, and +18.52% on GLIDE relative to the CLIP+Linear baseline, while in-domain SDv1.4 performance is largely maintained (97.52% vs. 95.89%). On GenImage++, the model achieves 96.78% average, beating RINE (88.40%), CO-SPY (87.98%), and PatchShuffle (81.61%), with particularly large margins on Flux subsets where most baselines drop below 40%. On Chameleon, the model (66.05%) surpasses AIDE (62.60%), NPR (58.13%), and all other baselines trained under the same SDv1.4 protocol. The paper also shows that pixel-space adversarial training (FGSM, PGD, MI-FGSM) yields only modest gains (82–83% average on GenImage) compared to the on-manifold approach (94.63%), confirming that latent-space perturbations are more effective at correcting the identified latent prior bias.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground within AIGC detection by diagnosing a specific failure mode—latent prior bias—rather than proposing a new detector architecture. It departs from the dominant strategy of using diffusion models for large-scale data augmentation (DRCT, DIRE) or leveraging VLMs for semantic reasoning (UnivFD, C2P-CLIP), instead showing that a small set of on-manifold adversarial examples generated by optimizing only the initial latent noise z_T is sufficient to force detectors away from noise-specific shortcuts. The work builds conceptually on Xu et al.'s finding that diffusion model seeds are recoverable from outputs, but repurposes this observation as both an attack mechanism and a training signal. The GenImage++ benchmark extends the GenImage evaluation paradigm to include outputs from DiT-based generators (FLUX.1, SD3) with advanced prompting, filling a gap left by benchmarks that predate these architectures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of latent prior bias as a concrete, actionable explanation for the cross-generator generalization gap gives the field a targeted lever: rather than endlessly scaling training data or redesigning architectures, practitioners can apply a lightweight adversarial training step with as few as 600 on-manifold examples to gain over 10 percentage points of generalization. GenImage++ provides a much-needed stress test against FLUX.1 and SD3, where most prior detectors collapse below 40% accuracy, making it a practical reference for future work. Key limitations include the white-box requirement and ~79 GB VRAM cost for attack generation, and the need for differentiable preprocessing (Kornia) that complicates application to arbitrary third-party detectors. Open questions include whether OMAT transfers to non-standard latent initializations (inpainting, 'golden noise') and whether the approach scales to larger generators without prohibitive memory costs.
