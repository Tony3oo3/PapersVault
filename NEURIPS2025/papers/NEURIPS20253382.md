## Problem

Existing AI-generated image detectors rely on training with both natural and generated images, making them computationally expensive to maintain and poor at generalizing to unseen generators. This paper identifies that the distributional gap between natural and generated images manifests as elevated epistemic uncertainty in models pre-trained on natural images, but this signal has not been exploited as a detection mechanism that avoids the need for generated training data entirely.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253382/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image as input and produces a binary decision (natural vs. generated) via a feature-similarity score computed between the original DINOv2 ViT-L/14 model and a weight-perturbed copy. No resolution or preprocessing constraints beyond standard ViT input are specified. In the training-free variant (WePe), the score is the cosine similarity of L2-normalized features from the clean and perturbed models; in the fine-tuned variant (WePe*), a learned loss sharpens the uncertainty gap, and a threshold on the score yields the final label.

## Method

WePe exploits the observation that DINOv2, pre-trained exclusively on natural images, exhibits lower epistemic uncertainty for natural (in-distribution) images than for AI-generated (out-of-distribution) images. Rather than using MC-Dropout or Deep Ensembles—which are impractical at DINOv2 scale—the method injects small zero-mean Gaussian perturbations (ratio 0.1) into the first 19 of 24 transformer blocks and measures the cosine similarity between features extracted by the clean and perturbed models (Eq. 13, an upper bound on predictive variance). A theoretical differential sensitivity theorem (Theorem 3.2) proves via PAC-Bayes bounds that the expected Jacobian norm is lower for natural images than for generated images, formalizing why this perturbation-based uncertainty is discriminative. Optionally, WePe* fine-tunes the model with a contrastive loss (Eq. 16) that maximizes feature similarity for natural images and minimizes it for generated images under perturbation, further amplifying the uncertainty gap without requiring a teacher model.

## Results

On ImageNet, WePe* (the fine-tuned variant) achieves an average AUROC of 95.57% and AP of 94.33% across four generative models (ADM, LDM, DiT, and a fourth model), outperforming the best training-based baseline FatFormer (93.68% AUROC, 93.11% AP) by ~1.9% AUROC. The training-free WePe variant achieves 87.99% AUROC and 85.04% AP, surpassing the best training-free baseline RIGID (83.58% AUROC, 81.58% AP) by ~4.4% AUROC. WePe also demonstrates strong robustness under spatial and frequency domain attacks, maintaining 88.68–90.07% AUROC under attacks where RIGID drops to ~34–35% AUROC.

## Baselines & Comparisons

WePe* outperforms all training-based baselines on ImageNet: it leads FatFormer (93.68% AUROC) by 1.9%, DRCT (90.36%) by 5.2%, NPR (86.00%) by 9.6%, and PatchCraft (86.13%) by 9.4% in average AUROC. Among training-free methods, WePe (87.99% AUROC) surpasses RIGID (83.58%) by 4.4% and AEROBLADE (57.87%) by a substantial 30.1%. Under spatial domain attacks (Gaussian noise σ=0.1), WePe maintains 90.07% average AUROC while RIGID collapses to 34.77% and NPR drops to 46.30%, highlighting WePe's superior adversarial robustness. On the GenImage benchmark, WePe* also achieves the best average ACC, outperforming NPR (88.6%) and FatFormer. The paper further shows that DINOv2-based WePe outperforms CLIP-based variants (87.99% vs 84.82% AUROC), attributing the gap to DINOv2's image-only pre-training capturing finer textural differences.

## Relation to Prior Work

WePe repurposes the well-studied epistemic uncertainty estimation framework (Bayesian posterior variance, Bernstein-von Mises asymptotics) as a detection signal for AI-generated images, rather than as a calibration or OOD-detection tool. It departs from training-based detectors (CNNspot, NPR, FatFormer, DRCT) that require curated natural+generated datasets, and from training-free methods like RIGID (noise robustness in feature space) and AEROBLADE (autoencoder reconstruction error), by instead measuring sensitivity of DINOv2 features to weight perturbations as a proxy for the model's epistemic uncertainty. The practical substitution of weight perturbation for Deep Ensembles or MC-Dropout—both infeasible at DINOv2 scale—is the key methodological bridge, grounded in a PAC-Bayes-style theoretical argument (Theorem 3.2) that in-distribution natural images exhibit lower feature sensitivity than out-of-distribution generated images.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free, attack-robust nature of WePe addresses two persistent pain points in generated-image detection: the need for ever-growing datasets of new generators, and vulnerability to simple spatial/frequency perturbations (where RIGID drops ~49 AUROC points under SDA while WePe actually improves). However, the method's effectiveness is fundamentally bounded by the distributional gap between natural and generated images on DINOv2; as generative models approach photorealism (FID → 0), the uncertainty gap narrows and detection degrades, a limitation the authors acknowledge but do not resolve. Open questions include whether the weight-perturbation proxy remains valid for next-generation foundation models, whether the DINOv2-specific advantage over CLIP/BLIP generalizes, and whether the ViT-g/14 underperformance relative to ViT-L/14 signals a scaling pathology.
