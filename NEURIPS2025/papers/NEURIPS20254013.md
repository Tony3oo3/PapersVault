## Problem

Backpropagation solves credit assignment in artificial neural networks but relies on weight symmetry (reusing forward weights in the backward pass), which is biologically implausible. Error broadcasting methods circumvent this by sending output errors directly to all layers, but they lack a principled theoretical foundation and typically use fixed random projection weights. This paper asks whether error broadcasting can be grounded in a well-established statistical principle and whether that grounding improves training effectiveness.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard image inputs—28×28 grayscale digits (MNIST) and 32×32 RGB images (CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100)—and produces a class label via a standard output layer. Beyond the image classification task itself, the framework is architecture-agnostic: it is applied to MLPs, CNNs, locally connected networks, and a 10-layer biologically plausible recurrent network (CorInfoMax-EBD) that uses online single-sample updates (batch size 1) rather than mini-batches.

## Method

EBD defines a layer-specific surrogate loss equal to the squared Frobenius norm of the cross-correlation matrix between (nonlinear functions of) layer activations and the output error, motivated by the stochastic orthogonality property of the optimal MMSE estimator. Output errors are projected onto each layer via a dynamically updated cross-correlation matrix (autoregressive with a forgetting factor), replacing the fixed random projectors of Direct Feedback Alignment. The resulting weight update for batch size 1 takes the form ΔW ∝ g′(h)·f′(u)·q·h_prev, which naturally instantiates a three-factor Hebbian learning rule with the broadcast error as the modulatory signal. To prevent activation collapse, power normalization and layer entropy regularizers are added. The framework is further integrated into the CorInfoMax biologically plausible network, replacing its two-phase equilibrium propagation with single-phase EBD updates that combine forward/backward prediction errors, lateral entropy maximization, and error decorrelation.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, EBD achieves 66.4% accuracy with a CNN, substantially outperforming DFA (58.4%) and matching NN-GEVB (66.3%), while trailing BP with MSE loss (75.2%). On MNIST, EBD reaches 99.1% (CNN) and 98.2% (MLP), competitive with DFA and other error-broadcast methods but slightly below BP (99.5% CNN, 98.7% MLP). On CIFAR-100, EBD attains 45.9% with a CNN, beating DFA (41.9%) but well below BP with cross-entropy (60.5%). The biologically plausible CorInfoMax-EBD achieves 55.7% on CIFAR-10 (3-layer, batch 20), improving over CorInfoMax-EP (51.0%), and scales to 54.9% with 10 layers using online learning (batch size 1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

EBD consistently outperforms DFA and DFA+E on CIFAR-10 across all three architectures (e.g., 66.4% vs. 58.4% for CNN; 64.3% vs. 62.2% for LC), with the gap attributed to EBD's dynamic cross-correlation-based error projection weights versus DFA's fixed random projectors. Against the global error vector broadcasting methods (NN-GEVB and MS-GEVB from Clark et al.), EBD wins on CIFAR-10 CNN (66.4% vs. 66.3%) and LC (64.3% vs. 58.9%/59.9%), while tying on MNIST. EBD trails standard backpropagation on all datasets (e.g., 66.4% vs. 75.2% on CIFAR-10 CNN; 45.9% vs. 60.5% on CIFAR-100), but remains the strongest error-broadcast method tested. For the biologically plausible CorInfoMax setting, CorInfoMax-EBD improves over CorInfoMax-EP on CIFAR-10 (55.7% vs. 51.0% for 3-layer, batch 20) and demonstrates depth scalability, with a 10-layer online-learning variant reaching 54.9% on CIFAR-10 and 96.4% on MNIST.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper provides a principled theoretical foundation for the error-broadcasting paradigm (DFA, GEVB) by grounding it in the stochastic orthogonality property of nonlinear MMSE estimators, a connection that prior error-broadcast methods lacked. It departs from DFA by replacing fixed random projection weights with dynamically updated cross-correlation matrices, and from backpropagation by eliminating the weight-transport requirement entirely. The framework also extends the CorInfoMax biologically plausible network by replacing its two-phase equilibrium propagation with single-phase EBD updates, and it shows that the resulting update rule naturally instantiates the three-factor learning structure observed in neuroscience.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The MMSE-orthogonality grounding gives error broadcasting a rigorous optimality criterion (Theorem B.1: orthogonality to all measurable functions is necessary and sufficient for MMSE optimality), which prior broadcast methods did not possess. The 10-layer CorInfoMax-EBD result (96.4% MNIST, 54.9% CIFAR-10 with batch size 1) is encouraging evidence that local, biologically plausible rules can scale in depth, though the gap to BP (75.2% CIFAR-10 CNN) remains substantial. Key open questions include convergence guarantees in finite-width settings, whether the dynamic projection matrices' computational overhead is justified at scale, and whether EBD generalizes beyond small image-classification benchmarks to larger, more diverse tasks.
