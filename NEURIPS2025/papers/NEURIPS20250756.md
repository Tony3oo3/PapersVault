## Problem

Existing temporal prediction methods for embodied AI and surgical workflow analysis operate at a single temporal or state scale, neglecting the hierarchical and time-varying nature of real-world decision-making. Moreover, foundation models (VLMs, LLMs) accumulate errors over long horizons and suffer from linear memory growth with streaming input frames, causing performance degradation as look-ahead intervals lengthen. No unified benchmark or framework exists that simultaneously handles multiple temporal horizons and multiple state granularities in a closed-loop, error-correcting manner.

## Input / Output

The system is stateless: at each incremental step it consumes only the current frame image (resized to 512 px short side for DM, 1024 px for VG) and the current hierarchical state vector (e.g., phase + step for surgery, or attention + spatial + contacting relationships for general scenes), with no temporal buffer of past frames. It produces two coupled outputs at each prediction horizon: a predicted hierarchical state label at the target temporal scale (1 s, 5 s, 30 s, or 60 s for surgery; up to 60 s for general scenes) and a synthesized future frame image via latent diffusion. Predictions are emitted only at coarser temporal-scale boundaries (every τ̂ seconds), while internal state updates occur at finer incremental intervals (τ), decoupling computation frequency from reporting frequency.

## Method

IG-MC alternates two modules in a closed loop: a Decision-Making (DM) module predicts the next hierarchical state from the current state-image pair, and a Visual Generation (VG) module (modified Stable Diffusion 3.5 Large) synthesizes the next frame conditioned on the predicted state (via cross-attention), the previous frame (via residual connections), and an anatomical-plausibility latent projection. The DM module itself is a multi-agent collaboration: a State Transition Controller (STC) first gates whether a transition is needed and at which hierarchical level it initiates, then LLM-based agents (one per level, from coarsest phase to finest step) sequentially refine the prediction in a chain, each using the prior agent's output plus the shared visual context. This hierarchical chain prevents multiplicative error drift—empirically sustaining 36–45% accuracy versus an 11–15% independence bound. DM and VG are trained separately (supervised fine-tuning with synthetic augmentation to address >100:1 transition sparsity for DM; domain-constrained diffusion loss for VG), then combined plug-and-play at inference, enabling the framework to attach to diverse VLM backbones (Qwen2.5-VL, LLaVA, Gemma3, InternVL, SurgVLM) without retraining the base model.

## Results

On the MSTP-Surgery benchmark (GraSP), the DM module alone boosts Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct accuracy from 13.20% to 51.90% at the 60 s temporal scale (+38.70 pp), and from 12.00% to 50.80% at 5 s (+38.80 pp). On the MSTP-General benchmark (Action Genome), the full IG-MC pipeline (DM+VG) achieves 63.97% accuracy at the 10–20 s scale and 63.27% at >30 s, compared to 12.17% and 17.70% for the base VLM. The VG module provides additional gains on general scenes (e.g., +13.07 pp at >30 s) but can slightly reduce accuracy on surgical scenes at longer horizons (37.20% vs. 51.90% at 60 s), while still improving recall and Jaccard.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct, a general-purpose VLM. The DM module alone yields dramatic accuracy gains across all temporal scales: +38.70 pp at 60 s and +38.80 pp at 5 s on MSTP-Surgery, and +32.50 pp at >30 s on MSTP-General. The VG module further improves general-scene accuracy (e.g., 63.27% vs. 50.20% at >30 s) but slightly reduces surgical accuracy at longer horizons (37.20% vs. 51.90% at 60 s), while consistently boosting recall and Jaccard on both benchmarks. The second baseline, SurgVLM-7B (a surgical-domain VLM), starts from a much lower base (1.06% at 5 s, 10.90% at 60 s) and benefits even more from DM (+41.64 pp at 5 s, +27.60 pp at 60 s); the full DM+VG pipeline achieves 44.84% at 5 s and 36.24% at 60 s, confirming IG-MC's plug-and-play generalizability across both general and surgical VLM backbones.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends surgical workflow recognition (TeCNO, Trans-SVNet, MuST) and surgical video generation (Endora, SurgSora) by unifying them into a closed-loop multi-scale temporal prediction framework. It departs from prior single-scale approaches that either predict coarse phases or fixed-window steps by formalizing two orthogonal dimensions—temporal scale (look-ahead horizon) and state scale (hierarchical phase/step/relationship levels)—and from frame-history-based VLM pipelines by using a stateless incremental generation that consumes only the current state-image pair. The VG module is built on Stable Diffusion 3.5 Large [79], while the DM module leverages fine-tuned VLMs (Qwen2.5-VL, LLaVA, Gemma3, InternVL, SurgVLM) organized as a multi-agent hierarchy, drawing on the broader multi-agent LLM literature for structured reasoning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The MSTP benchmark is the first to provide synchronized annotations across both temporal horizons and state hierarchies, filling a gap where prior datasets were procedure-specific and clip-centric. The plug-and-play DM/VG modules demonstrate consistent gains across five VLM backbones, suggesting a reusable toolkit for multi-scale prediction in embodied and surgical AI. However, the ~68-second end-to-end latency precludes real-time intra-operative use, the VG module inconsistently helps or hurts accuracy at longer horizons (e.g., −14.7 pp at 60 s with Qwen2.5-VL), and evaluation is limited to a single surgical procedure (prostatectomy) and one general-scene dataset, leaving open whether the framework generalizes to more diverse clinical settings.
