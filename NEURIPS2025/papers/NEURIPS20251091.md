## Problem

DDIM inversion suffers from trajectory deviation: the latent path during reconstruction diverges from the inversion path because the local linearity assumption (ε_θ(x_t) ≈ ε_θ(x_{t+1})) fails in practice, accumulating error across denoising steps. Existing remedies—gradient-based null-text optimization (NTI) or error memorization/compensation (PnP, DI, VI)—improve fidelity but impose large time and memory overheads, making them impractical for long video sequences.

## Input / Output

FreeInv operates on the latent codes of a pretrained diffusion model (Stable Diffusion 1.5/SDXL with U-Net, or FLUX with DiT) at 512×512 resolution. It takes a single image or video frame sequence plus a text prompt, and outputs a reconstructed image/video (for fidelity measurement) or an edited image/video when integrated with frameworks such as P2P, PnP, MasaCtrl, or TokenFlow. The only additional state stored is the per-timestep transformation parameter (e.g., rotation angle), requiring ~2 MB of GPU memory.

## Method

FreeInv is motivated by the observation that averaging noise predictions across multiple independent DDIM trajectories reduces the inversion–reconstruction mismatch error on expectation (formally bounded via the triangle inequality). To avoid the N-fold cost of explicit multi-branch inversion, it makes two simplifications: (1) one-time Monte Carlo sampling at each timestep selects a single branch to evaluate, and (2) the N branches are replaced by N random spatial transformations (rotation, flip, patch-shuffle, color jitter) applied to the same latent, so x_t^i = f_i(x_t). At each timestep t, a transformation is randomly drawn, applied to the latent for noise prediction, and the same transformation is reused at the corresponding reconstruction timestep to maintain trajectory consistency. The net overhead is limited to the transformation itself and storing the per-step transformation index, making the method essentially free relative to the DDIM baseline while delivering reconstruction quality competitive with or exceeding NTI, EDICT, PnP, and other state-of-the-art inversion methods.

## Results

On the PIE benchmark with Stable Diffusion 1.5 (U-Net, 50-step), FreeInv achieves PSNR 27.69, LPIPS 5.14×10⁻², MSE 2.45×10⁻³, and SSIM 0.81, while maintaining the same 4-second inversion time and 3031 MB memory as the DDIM baseline. On FLUX (DiT, 25-step), FreeInv reaches PSNR 29.24 and SSIM 0.90, substantially outperforming the raw FLUX baseline (PSNR 14.92) and FLUX+RF-Solver (PSNR 26.38). The method also improves editing fidelity: with P2P, FreeInv achieves the highest CLIP similarity (25.30 whole / 22.33 edited) among all compared inversion methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FreeInv (PSNR 27.69) outperforms the DDIM baseline (25.04) by 2.65 dB and beats NTI (26.74), EDICT (27.21), ReNoise (26.61), BELM (27.12), and PI (27.12) on PSNR, while matching or exceeding their SSIM (0.81). It slightly trails DI (28.19) and VI (27.86) on PSNR but achieves the best MSE (2.45×10⁻³) among all U-Net methods except DI. The critical efficiency advantage is that FreeInv requires only 4 s and 3031 MB—identical to the DDIM baseline—whereas NTI needs 148 s / 11945 MB, EDICT 81 s / 12325 MB, and DI 16 s / 13595 MB. On the DiT (FLUX) setting, FreeInv (PSNR 29.24, SSIM 0.90) dramatically surpasses both the raw FLUX baseline (14.92) and FLUX+RF-Solver (26.38), with no additional time or memory cost. For video editing on DAVIS, FreeInv combined with TokenFlow achieves the highest reconstruction PSNR and lowest extra memory (2 MB) compared to TokenFlow+STEM-Inv, which still exhibits visible artifacts and over-sharpening in editing results.

## Relation to Prior Work

FreeInv builds directly on the DDIM deterministic inversion framework (Song et al., ICLR 2021) and addresses the well-known trajectory deviation problem that plagues all DDIM-based editing pipelines. It departs from the two dominant prior strategies—gradient-based optimization of null-text embeddings (NTI, Prompt Tuning Inversion) and memory-intensive error compensation (PnP Inversion, Virtual Inversion, DI)—by reframing the problem through an ensemble lens: averaging noise predictions across multiple trajectories reduces mismatch error via the triangle inequality. The key conceptual leap is replacing explicit multi-branch sampling with random geometric transformations (rotation, flipping, patch shuffling) of the latent, combined with one-time Monte Carlo sampling, to approximate the ensemble at negligible cost.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 'free lunch' property—matching or exceeding SOTA inversion fidelity at the same time and memory cost as vanilla DDIM—makes FreeInv uniquely deployable in video editing pipelines where hundreds of frames make gradient-based or memory-heavy methods impractical. Its architecture-agnostic design (U-Net and DiT) and plug-and-play integration with existing editing frameworks (P2P, PnP, MasaCtrl, TokenFlow) lower the barrier to adoption significantly. An open tension remains between reconstruction fidelity and editability: FreeInv can over-preserve source content, limiting how aggressively an image can be modified, a trade-off shared by all inversion-enhancement methods. Future work could explore adaptive transformation schedules or combining FreeInv with learned inversion corrections for scenarios demanding both high fidelity and high editability.
