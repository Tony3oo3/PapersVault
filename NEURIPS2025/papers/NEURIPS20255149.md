## Problem

Conditional generation in diffusion models relies on either classifier-based guidance (requiring differentiable predictors and gradient computation at every denoising step) or classifier-free guidance (requiring architectural modification and joint training of the full diffusion model). Both paradigms impose significant computational overhead and coupling between the conditioning mechanism and the base model. DISCO introduces a third paradigm that is differentiability-free and decouples conditional control from the diffusion backbone entirely.

## Input / Output

At each denoising step, the method consumes the intermediate latent state z_t (a 4096-dim vector in SD 1.5, partitioned into 256 blocks of 64 dimensions), a text prompt, and a conditioning image in one of nine modalities (Canny edge, HED, MLSD, depth, normal map, segmentation, skeleton, location, or sketch), all resized to 512×512. The output is a set of 256 discrete noise code indices (each selecting one of 4096 pre-sampled N(0,I) vectors from a fixed codebook), which are concatenated to form the noise term for the DDPM update, ultimately yielding a 512×512 generated image. The codebook itself is static—sampled once from a standard Gaussian and never updated during training or inference.

## Method

The core insight is that the conditional correction term Δ(z_t, c) in both classifier-based and classifier-free guidance effectively shifts the noise distribution from N(0, I) to N(μ(z_t, c), I), and this non-zero-mean Gaussian can be approximated by selecting the most directionally aligned vector from a finite codebook of K=4096 vectors in n=64 dimensions (justified by a non-central beta distribution analysis and a typical-maximum bound of 2log(K)/n). A DiT-based prediction network is trained with a BERT-style masked-index completion objective: given the intermediate state z_t, the conditioning input c, and a partially masked set of noise indices, the model predicts the missing indices via softmax cross-entropy. Training data pairs (z_t, k_i) are generated through a reconstruction-based strategy that applies the discrete noise substitution during denoising and extracts structural cues from the reconstructed image as conditioning. Crucially, the diffusion model is never loaded or fine-tuned during training—only the lightweight DiT predictor is trained on precomputed (z_t, c, k_i) tuples, completing in 3 days on a single RTX 3090 versus 3+ days on an A6000 for ControlNet-style methods.

## Results

DISCO achieves the best CLIP-Aesthetic score across all nine conditioning types (5.42–5.48), consistently outperforming ControlNet (5.00–5.39) and AnyControl (4.90–5.13). On FID, DISCO leads on Depth (18.04 vs. 19.81 for ControlNet), HED (15.75 vs. 16.17), Normal (27.44 vs. 29.58), Location (28.89 vs. 29.70), and Sketch (23.68 vs. 24.02), though it trails FreeControl on Canny (21.38 vs. 17.91) and AnyControl on Segmentation (21.14 vs. 19.36). For controllability, DISCO achieves the lowest Depth MSE (86.68), highest M-LSD SSIM (0.7793), lowest Normal MSE (0.3443), and highest Sketch SSIM (0.6118). In the user study (Table 2), DISCO attains the highest preference score across all nine conditions, with the largest margins on Location (0.63) and MLSD (0.58).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ControlNet [48], DISCO achieves lower FID on five of nine conditions (Depth, HED, Normal, Location, Sketch) and higher CLIP-Aesthetic scores on all nine, while also improving controllability on Depth (MSE 86.68 vs. 87.37), M-LSD (SSIM 0.779 vs. 0.745), Normal (MSE 0.344 vs. 0.357), and Sketch (SSIM 0.612 vs. 0.526); however, DISCO trails on Canny FID (21.38 vs. 18.92) and Segmentation FID (21.14 vs. 20.39). Versus AnyControl [34], DISCO wins on FID for Depth (18.04 vs. 18.65) and dominates on CLIP-Aesthetic across every condition (e.g., 5.42 vs. 5.13 on Depth), though AnyControl achieves better Canny SSIM (0.508 vs. 0.441) and Segmentation mIoU (0.338 vs. 0.392 is actually DISCO winning here—correction: DISCO 0.392 > AnyControl 0.338). Compared to FreeControl (FreeDoM [46]), DISCO substantially reduces FID on Depth (18.04 vs. 24.96) and HED (15.75 vs. 17.45) but FreeControl retains an edge on Canny FID (17.91 vs. 21.38) and Segmentation mIoU (0.701 vs. 0.392). Against T2I-Adapter [20], DISCO achieves lower FID on Depth (18.04 vs. 20.28) and higher CLIP-Aesthetic on Depth (5.42 vs. 5.35), while T2I-Adapter edges ahead on Skeleton FID (24.94 vs. 25.51) and Skeleton mAP (0.528 vs. 0.404). In the user study, DISCO is preferred over all five baselines on every conditioning type, with the largest margins over ControlNet on Location (0.63 vs. 0.37) and MLSD (0.58 vs. 0.42).

## Relation to Prior Work

DISCO positions itself as a third paradigm for conditional diffusion generation, distinct from both classifier-based guidance (Dhariwal & Nichol; training-free variants like TFG, FreeDoM, EMControl) and classifier-free guidance (Ho & Salimans; ControlNet, T2I-Adapter, AnyControl). Both existing paradigms estimate a continuous conditional correction term Δ(z_t, c); DISCO instead reframes the problem by discretizing the noise sampling step itself, replacing the non-zero-mean Gaussian draw with a lookup from a fixed codebook of pre-sampled standard Gaussian vectors. The theoretical justification draws on the non-central beta distribution (Theorem 1) and extreme-value analysis of cosine similarity (Theorem 2), while the prediction network borrows the BERT-style masked-index completion objective and the DiT architecture with rotary positional embeddings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The main practical advantage is training efficiency: DISCO's prediction network trains on a single 24 GB GPU without loading the frozen diffusion backbone, versus the 48 GB A6000 required by ControlNet-style methods, and it avoids gradient computation through target predictors entirely. However, the paper does not discuss limitations (NeurIPS checklist: 'No'), and evaluation is confined to COCO2017 with Stable Diffusion 1.5, leaving open whether the codebook approach generalizes to other latent dimensions, higher-resolution models, or multi-condition compositions. The theoretical guarantee (Theorem 2) is asymptotic and relies on the specific (n=64, K=4096) configuration; whether the discrete approximation degrades gracefully under different noise schedules or model scales remains unexplored.
