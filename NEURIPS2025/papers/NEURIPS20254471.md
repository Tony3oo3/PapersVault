## Problem

Model-based reinforcement learning (MBRL) has proven effective for autonomous driving in CARLA v2, but existing approaches (e.g., Think2Drive) rely on privileged ground-truth inputs rather than raw sensor data. No prior work has successfully trained an end-to-end MBRL system from raw multi-view images, because the high dimensionality, redundancy, and noise of raw sensor data make world model learning extremely difficult. Raw2Drive fills this gap as the first RL-based end-to-end method on CARLA Leaderboard 2.0.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254471/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the system consumes surround-view RGB images and IMU readings, encoded by a BEVFormer backbone into grid-shaped BEV features. During training, a parallel privileged stream uses 43-channel BEV semantic segmentation masks plus ego-vehicle state as compact structured input. The policy outputs one of 39 discrete actions, each specifying a fixed combination of throttle, steer, brake, and reverse values, rather than continuous control signals.

## Method

Raw2Drive employs a dual-stream MBRL architecture with two DreamerV3-style world models (RSSM-based) and two actor-critic policies. Training proceeds in two stages: first, a privileged world model and paired policy are trained on structured BEV masks; second, a raw-sensor world model is trained with its RSSM initialized from the privileged model, using only a BEV decoder head (omitting reward and continuation heads that cause convergence issues on high-dimensional inputs). A Guidance Mechanism enforces consistency between the two streams during rollouts: Spatial-Temporal Alignment Loss aligns encoder features, Abstract-State Alignment Loss constrains deterministic states via L2 and stochastic states via KL divergence, and a single-sample trick (sampling the stochastic state once from the raw stream and feeding it into both) eliminates cumulative sampling drift. For policy training, Head Guidance borrows the privileged model's reward and continuation predictions as stable supervision signals, since the raw-sensor model's own scalar heads are unreliable on visually similar adjacent frames.

## Results

On the Bench2Drive closed-loop benchmark, Raw2Drive achieves a Driving Score (DS) of 71.36 and Success Rate (SR) of 50.24%, surpassing the best IL baseline DriveTrans (DS 63.46, SR 35.01%) by a large margin. On the Bench2Drive multi-ability benchmark, Raw2Drive attains a mean ability score of 53.34%, outperforming the strongest IL method DriveAdapter (42.08%) by over 11 points. On CARLA Leaderboard 2.0, Raw2Drive scores DS 4.12 (validation) and 3.56 (devtest), far exceeding all IL methods (best: DriveAdapter at 0.92/0.87), though it remains below the privileged-input Think2Drive (43.8/56.8). The total training cost is 64 H800 GPU days (reducible to 40 if Think2Drive is reused).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Raw2Drive outperforms all raw-sensor IL baselines on every Bench2Drive closed-loop metric. Against the strongest IL competitor DriveTrans (DS 63.46, SR 35.01%), Raw2Drive gains +7.9 DS and +15.2 SR points; against DriveAdapter (DS 64.22, SR 33.08%), the gap is +7.1 DS and +17.2 SR. On the multi-ability benchmark (Table 4), Raw2Drive leads all IL methods by a wide margin (mean 53.34% vs. DriveAdapter's 42.08%), with the largest gaps in Merging (+14.5 pts) and Traffic Sign (+5.8 pts). On CARLA Leaderboard 2.0 long routes, Raw2Drive (DS 4.12) surpasses DriveAdapter (0.92) by over 4×, though the paper notes this metric is less informative due to cumulative penalty scoring. The only method that outperforms Raw2Drive is Think2Drive, which uses privileged ground-truth inputs (DS 91.85 vs. 71.36), confirming that the remaining gap is attributable to the privileged-to-raw sensor transition rather than the RL paradigm itself. Notably, Raw2Drive also achieves the highest Efficiency score (214.17) among all raw-sensor methods, indicating faster route completion.

## Relation to Prior Work

Raw2Drive extends the DreamerV3 world-model architecture (RSSM, encoder-heads, actor-critic policy) and the privileged-input MBRL framework of Think2Drive to the raw-sensor setting, which no prior work has achieved for end-to-end autonomous driving. It departs from the dominant IL paradigm (UniAD, VAD, DriveTrans) by replacing imitation with reward-driven RL, and from model-free RL (MaRLn) by introducing a learned world model for sample efficiency. The key architectural departure is the dual-stream design: a privileged world model is first trained as an auxiliary, then used via a Guidance Mechanism (spatial-temporal alignment, abstract-state alignment, and head guidance) to supervise a raw-sensor world model, avoiding the need to reconstruct expensive multi-view video.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first RL-based end-to-end method to achieve competitive scores on CARLA Leaderboard 2.0 and Bench2Drive from raw sensor inputs, surpassing all IL baselines by a substantial margin (e.g., DS 71.36 vs. 63.46 for DriveTrans on Bench2Drive closed-loop) while using only 64 H800 GPU days. The dual-stream guidance paradigm is conceptually decoupled from the specific simulator and could transfer to other domains where high-dimensional observations make direct world-model learning intractable. Key limitations include the reliance on privileged ground-truth inputs during training (bounding boxes, HD-Map) and evaluation exclusively in CARLA; the authors note that real-world RL would require 3DGS or diffusion-based simulators, and the gap to Think2Drive (DS 91.85) shows the privileged-to-raw transfer is not yet complete.
