## Problem

General video generation models, when naively fine-tuned on robotic manipulation videos, fail to encode the 3D, action-conditioned dynamics essential for reliable policy planning because their latent space is misaligned with the temporally interconnected robotics environment. Additionally, long-horizon autoregressive generation suffers from model collapse, and acquiring precisely calibrated multi-camera data with corresponding actions is prohibitively expensive, creating a persistent sim-to-real gap.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251263/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single RGB or RGB-D camera frame (512×320), a text instruction encoded by a frozen T5 encoder, and camera intrinsics/extrinsics as input; when depth is available, it depth-warps the observation to synthesize auxiliary rendered views at ±30° Z-axis rotations. It produces multi-view future video chunks of 8 frames in VAE latent space, 7-dimensional action chunks (delta position, rotation, gripper openness) over 8 timesteps, and optionally a 4D Gaussian Splatting scene reconstruction. The policy head caches a compact visual latent (mean-pooled UNet middle-block features) from the first denoising step to condition action prediction, achieving ~280 ms per 8-step chunk on a single RTX 4090.

## Method

ENERVERSE employs a chunk-wise autoregressive video diffusion framework where the model iteratively predicts the next 8-frame chunk conditioned on sparsely sampled context frames (discarding ~80% of consecutive frames) rather than a full sliding window, which prevents model collapse and enables theoretically infinite-length generation. A Multi-View Diffusion Generator Block encodes camera geometry via ray direction maps concatenated with VAE latents and applies cross-view spatial attention plus temporal attention to enforce geometric coherence across views, establishing a 3D prior during multi-view pretraining that benefits even single-camera deployment. The ENERVERSE-D data flywheel iteratively generates missing views from sparse observations, reconstructs a 4D scene via 4D Gaussian Splatting, re-renders target views, and feeds them back through the generator to progressively reduce hallucinations and tighten cross-view consistency. The policy head (ENERVERSE-A) is a stack of 18 DiT blocks that denoises action chunks conditioned on the cached visual latent from the video backbone's first (noisiest) denoising step, trained with DDPM-style MSE loss. Training follows a two-stage strategy: multi-view video generation pretraining on heterogeneous embodied datasets (RT-1, Language Table, Bridge, RoboTurk, ManiSkill, Isaac Sim) followed by fine-tuning with policy loss, with domain embeddings to handle heterogeneous data sources.

## Results

On the LIBERO benchmark (four task suites, 50 rollouts per task, 3 seeds), ENERVERSE achieves an average success rate of 84.1% with a single S-RGB input, rising to 88.5% when two depth-warp rendered views are added, outperforming the strongest baseline (MAIL, 81.5%) by 2.6–7.0 points. On the CALVIN ABC→D long-horizon benchmark, ENERVERSE attains an average sequence length of 3.00, competitive with GR-1 (3.06) but trailing 3D Diffuser (3.27), which uses RGB-D and proprioceptive inputs. For video generation quality on RT-1, ENERVERSE achieves PSNR 26.1 and FVD 404.65, surpassing the DynamicCrafter+FreeNoise baseline (PSNR 25.42, FVD 445.94) and uniquely supporting long-task generation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ENERVERSE outperforms all six LIBERO baselines across every task suite. Against the strongest competitor, MAIL (81.5% avg with dual S-RGB), ENERVERSE leads by 2.6 points with a single S-RGB input and by 7.0 points with two rendered views; the gap is largest on the Long suite (80.0 vs 78.0) and Object suite (97.7 vs 90.0). OpenVLA (76.5%), MDT (76.1%), Octo (75.1%), and Diffusion Policy (72.4%) trail by 8–16 points on average. On CALVIN, ENERVERSE (3.00 avg sequence length) is competitive but does not match 3D Diffuser (3.27) or GR-1 (3.06), both of which receive additional RGB-D and proprioceptive inputs; it clearly surpasses RoboFlamingo (2.47) and SUSIE (2.69). In the video generation comparison, ENERVERSE beats DynamicCrafter+FreeNoise on PSNR (+0.68), FVD (−41.29), and motion continuity (90 vs 80 in user study), while uniquely supporting long-task generation where DC-FN fails.

## Relation to Prior Work

ENERVERSE builds on DynamicCrafter as its base video diffusion backbone and Diffusion Policy for the action head, but departs from prior video-pretraining-for-robotics work (AVID, VidMan, GR-2) that simply fine-tunes general video models on manipulation clips. The key conceptual departure is treating the robotics environment as a 4D (3D+time) embodied space to be *constructed* via chunk-wise autoregressive generation with sparse context memory, rather than merely *predicted* frame-by-frame. The multi-view diffusion pretraining with ray-direction conditioning and the 4DGS-based data flywheel (ENERVERSE-D) are novel architectural and data-engineering contributions that have no direct counterpart in the prior video-for-robotics literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that multi-view generative pretraining can endow a single-camera robot with a strong 3D spatial prior (79.0 → 92.1 on LIBERO-Spatial), a practically important finding given the cost of multi-camera hardware. The data flywheel concept—using 4DGS to iteratively refine generated multi-view videos—offers a concrete path toward reducing the sim-to-real gap without prohibitively expensive calibrated multi-camera capture. Open questions remain: the relationship between pixel-level video quality and control success is acknowledged but not rigorously quantified, rendered view selection is heuristic (±30° rotation), and the paper does not release code, limiting reproducibility. The sparse memory mechanism's criticality (30.8 vs. 73 on LIBERO-Long) suggests a broader principle about non-redundant context for long-horizon generation that could transfer beyond robotics.
