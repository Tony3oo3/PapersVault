## Problem

Existing 3D affordance understanding methods either stop at object-level recognition or handle fine-grained affordance grounding and motion estimation as disjoint, instruction-agnostic subtasks. This paper introduces a unified, instruction-conditioned task—Fine-grained 3D Embodied Reasoning—that jointly predicts spatial location, motion type, and motion axis for each referenced affordance element, requiring coherent reasoning over geometry, semantics, and language intent simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254691/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D point cloud scene and a natural language task instruction (e.g., 'open the left part of the window door') as input. For each referenced affordance element, it outputs a structured triplet: a 3D instance mask identifying the spatial region, a discrete motion type (e.g., Translation, Rotation), and a discretized motion axis direction (e.g., 'Horizontal outwards'). The 3D point cloud is never fed directly to the MLLM; instead, it is rendered into a 360° surround-view image set with projected element bounding boxes and labels, and the MLLM reasons over these 2D annotated views.

## Method

AffordBot bridges the 3D-to-2D modality gap through a holistic multimodal representation: it renders a dense 360° panoramic scan of the scene, extracts geometric-semantic descriptors (position, size, affordance type) via a fine-tuned Mask3D instance segmentation model, and projects these onto each view with an adaptive label-refinement strategy to prevent occlusion. On top of this representation, a three-step chain-of-thought pipeline guides the MLLM (Qwen2.5-VL-72B): (1) Active View Selection, where the MLLM autonomously picks the most informative viewpoint given the instruction; (2) Affordance Grounding, localizing the target element by matching instruction semantics to projected element IDs; and (3) Motion Estimation, inferring the motion type and discretized axis direction from scene context. This observe-then-infer design eliminates the need for redundant video streams and enables physically grounded, instruction-conditioned reasoning with only 3D point cloud input.

## Results

On SceneFun3D, AffordBot achieves state-of-the-art performance across both affordance grounding and motion estimation. For grounding, it reports mIoU=14.0, AP=15.5, AP50=20.0, and AP25=23.3, substantially outperforming the strongest prior method Fun3DU (AP50=12.6, AP25=23.1). For motion estimation, AffordBot achieves +T=18.3 and +TD=10.8, compared to Fun3DU(+motion)'s +T=11.5 and +TD=4.0, demonstrating that 3D-based reasoning yields significantly more accurate motion predictions than 2D video-based approaches.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AffordBot outperforms all five baselines on every reported metric. Against the strongest prior method Fun3DU [37], AffordBot improves AP50 from 12.6 to 20.0 (+7.4) and AP from 6.1 to 15.5 (+9.4), while matching AP25 (23.3 vs. 23.1); the large AP gap indicates AffordBot maintains precision at stricter IoU thresholds (0.5–0.95), which is critical for robotic manipulation. On motion estimation, AffordBot nearly doubles Fun3DU(+motion)'s +T score (18.3 vs. 11.5) and more than doubles +TD (10.8 vs. 4.0), showing that 3D geometric context enables far more accurate motion type and axis prediction than 2D video frames. The video-based baselines (OpenMask3D, LERF, OpenMask3D-F) and the open-vocabulary method OpenIns3D all fail to produce meaningful grounding scores (AP50 ≤ 8.0), highlighting the difficulty of fine-grained affordance localization without explicit 3D-to-2D association. Notably, AffordBot operates directly on 3D point clouds (no raw 2D video input), whereas all competing methods rely on rendered video frames, yet it still achieves superior results.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on SceneFun3D [34], which introduced benchmarks for fine-grained affordance grounding and motion estimation in 3D indoor scenes, but departs from it by unifying these two previously isolated subtasks under a single instruction-conditioned triplet prediction formulation. It also moves away from video-based input pipelines (Fun3DU [37], GPT4Scene [59]) that suffer from limited field-of-view and redundant frame processing, instead rendering dynamic 360° surround-view images from 3D point clouds and projecting segmented affordance candidates onto them. The MLLM-based chain-of-thought reasoning with active view selection is inspired by the broader trend of adapting 2D VLMs to 3D understanding [61,62,63,64], but the specific observe-then-infer pipeline (view selection → grounding → motion inference) is tailored to this embodied reasoning task.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified instruction-conditioned formulation is a practical step toward agents that can go from a natural language command to a physically grounded action plan (where + how) in 3D scenes, rather than treating localization and motion as disjoint problems. However, the bottleneck analysis (Table 4) reveals that upstream 3D instance segmentation quality is the dominant limiting factor (a 22.1% AP25 gap between predicted and GT proposals), while the MLLM reasoning pipeline and active view selection contribute comparatively modest gains. Absolute performance remains low (AP25 ≈ 23.3%), the evaluation is confined to a single dataset of 230 scenes, and the counterintuitive finding that multi-element tasks outperform single-element ones (Table 7) highlights unresolved challenges with small, weakly-textured objects. Open questions include whether the framework scales to larger, more diverse scene collections, whether real-time deployment is feasible given the 72B-parameter MLLM, and whether the surround-view + CoT paradigm generalizes beyond the SceneFun3D affordance taxonomy.
