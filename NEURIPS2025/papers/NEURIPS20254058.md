## Problem

Regularized Optimistic FTRL achieves O(1) regret in zero-sum games, but its proof techniques require a finite upper bound on stepsize (i.e., constant regularization). It was unknown whether the same optimal O(1) regret is attainable without regularization, equivalently with unbounded stepsizes as in Fictitious Play. The paper also asks whether alternation (as opposed to optimism) can substitute for regularization to achieve o(√T) regret in the unregularized regime.

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes a 2×2 zero-sum payoff matrix A with a unique interior Nash equilibrium and initial pure strategies for both players. At each round, each player computes a best response (a vertex of the simplex) to the cumulative payoff vector plus an optimistic bias equal to the most recent opponent's payoff contribution. The output is a sequence of pure-strategy iterates whose total regret is bounded by a constant depending only on the entries of A (specifically, 8a_max(1+2a_max/a_gap)²), yielding O(1/T) time-average convergence to Nash.

## Method

The proof introduces a geometric view of Optimistic FP as skew-gradient descent in the dual space of payoff vectors, where regret equals the value of an energy function Ψ (the support function of the joint simplex) evaluated at the final dual iterate. For 2×2 games satisfying det(A)=0, the dual iterates are confined to a 2D subspace, reducing the problem to scalar iterates (z₁ᵗ, z₂ᵗ) with a piecewise-linear energy ψ and a choice map Q partitioning R² into four regions. The key technical contribution is proving cycling invariants: whenever ψ exceeds a threshold B, the predicted dual vector ẑ^{t+1}=2zᵗ−z^{t−1} and the true dual vector z^{t+1} must lie in the same region, causing the one-step energy change Δψ to be non-positive (via skew-symmetry of SᵀM). This ensures energy remains bounded by a constant after at most one crossing of B, yielding O(1) regret. In contrast, for Alternating FP on Matching Pennies, the paper proves an Ω(√T) regret lower bound, separating optimism from alternation in the unregularized regime.

## Results

The paper proves that Optimistic Fictitious Play (OFP) achieves O(1) regret in all 2×2 zero-sum games with a unique interior Nash equilibrium, regardless of tiebreaking rule (Theorem 3.1). This matches the optimal constant regret rate of regularized Optimistic FTRL but is achieved without any regularization (i.e., with unbounded stepsizes). Additionally, the paper establishes an Ω(√T) regret lower bound for Alternating Fictitious Play on Matching Pennies (Theorem 3.2), separating the power of optimism from alternation in the unregularized regime. Empirically, on 15×15 to 50×50 games, OFP achieves total regret of 2.5–25.2 after T=10,000 steps, compared to 116–243 for standard FP (roughly √T ≈ 100).

| η→∞ (FP) | O(√T) | **O(1)** (Theorem 3.1) | **Ω(√T)** (Theorem 3.2) |

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to standard Fictitious Play (Robinson, 1951), which achieves O(√T) regret in 2×2 games, OFP strictly improves to O(1) regret — the first constant regret bound for any unregularized FP variant. Against regularized Optimistic FTRL (Syrgkanis et al., 2015), OFP matches the same O(1) regret rate but does so without requiring a bounded stepsize or any regularization, using a fundamentally different proof technique (geometric energy bounds in dual space vs. RVU bounds). Against Alternating FTRL (Katona et al., 2024), which achieves O(T^{1/5}) regret with a sufficiently small stepsize, the unregularized Alternating FP is strictly worse, suffering an Ω(√T) lower bound on Matching Pennies. This separation shows that in the unregularized regime, optimism (not alternation) is the key ingredient for achieving o(√T) regret. Empirically, OFP's constant regret (2.5–25.2) is roughly 5–100× lower than standard FP's √T-scaled regret (116–243) across all tested game dimensions and payoff matrix classes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the geometric skew-gradient descent framework for Fictitious Play in dual payoff-vector space (Abernethy et al., 2021a; Lazarsfeld et al., 2025) by introducing an energy-boundedness argument specific to the optimistic variant, and leverages the 2×2 structural normalization of Bailey and Piliouras (2019) to reduce the analysis to a two-dimensional subspace. The key departure from prior work is that all existing O(1) regret guarantees for zero-sum games (Syrgkanis et al., 2015; Rakhlin and Sridharan, 2013) rely on regularized algorithms with bounded stepsizes, whereas this paper proves the same rate for the fully unregularized (η→∞) Optimistic Fictitious Play. The proof technique—showing a support-function energy of dual iterates remains bounded—differs fundamentally from the RVU-bound approach used for regularized Optimistic FTRL, and the complementary Ω(√T) lower bound for Alternating FP cleanly separates optimism from alternation in the unregularized regime.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first constant-regret guarantee for any Fictitious Play variant in zero-sum games, demonstrating that regularization is not a prerequisite for optimal O(1/T) time-average convergence to Nash in the 2×2 setting. The practical implications extend to equilibrium computation in combinatorial games and self-play training in multi-agent reinforcement learning, where unregularized algorithms are more natural. The central open question is whether the constant-regret bound extends beyond 2×2 games to arbitrary dimensions; empirical evidence on 15×15, 25×25, and 50×50 games is strongly suggestive but no formal proof exists. The clean separation between optimism (O(1) regret) and alternation (Ω(√T) regret) in the unregularized regime also clarifies which mechanism—forward-looking prediction versus sequential updating—is responsible for accelerated convergence.
