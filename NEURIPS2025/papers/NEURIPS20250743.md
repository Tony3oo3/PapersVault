## Problem

State space models (SSMs) like Mamba achieve linear-time sequence processing but are fundamentally limited in expressivity: their positive real eigenvalue spectra and partially adaptive recurrence prevent them from performing modulo counting or recognizing solvable regular languages. More general linear time-varying (LTV) SSMs overcome this expressivity gap but incur quadratic computational complexity, making them impractical for long sequences. The paper addresses the open question of whether an SSM architecture can simultaneously achieve full input-dependent adaptivity, complex (unitary) eigenvalues for high expressivity, and linear-time scalable computation.

## Input / Output

The method consumes symbolic sequences (binary strings of length up to 40 for training, tested up to 256 for length generalization on algorithmic tasks) and continuous time series (UEA datasets with sequence lengths from 405 to 17,984; Weather dataset with 720-timestep input windows forecasting 720 steps ahead). The SSM is applied independently to each of the d input dimensions following Mamba's multi-channel approach, with the hidden state being complex-valued due to unitary eigenvalues; only the real part of the complex output is retained via a Re[·] operation before the final readout. Outputs are class labels for classification benchmarks and continuous regression forecasts for the Weather task.

## Method

AUSSM replaces Mamba's fixed diagonal A matrix with an input-dependent skew-symmetric recurrent matrix A_t = f(u(t)), derived from the jPCA ODE in neuroscience, so that the discrete transition exp(Δ_t A_t) has eigenvalues exactly on the unit circle (purely rotational, marginally stable dynamics). This guarantees norm preservation (no gradient explosion/vanishing) while allowing the input to smoothly modulate rotation frequencies, enabling modulo-k counting with constant-width hidden states. The paper proves via Krohn-Rhodes decomposition that a single AUSSM layer simulates cyclic group automata and that interleaving AUSSM with Mamba layers (which simulate set-reset automata) yields maximal expressivity among diagonal SSMs—recognizing all solvable regular languages. For scalability, the authors derive a separable convolution kernel K(t,k) = f(t)g(k) under the simultaneous diagonalizability assumption, reducing the O(L²) adaptive kernel to O(L) memory computable via parallel prefix sums in O(log L) time, and implement a custom CUDA kernel that matches Mamba's memory footprint despite full adaptivity.

## Results

On formal algorithmic tasks, AUSSM achieves perfect scaled accuracy (1.0) on parity and cycle navigation, tasks that Mamba fails on (0.13 and 0.86 respectively), validating its theoretical ability to simulate cyclic group automata. The AUSSM+Mamba hybrid achieves the best or near-best performance across most tasks, including mod arith (0.23 vs. Mamba's 0.04) and repetition (0.45 vs. Mamba's 0.15). On the Weather forecasting benchmark, the hybrid achieves a state-of-the-art MAE of 0.342, a 26% improvement over Mamba (0.464) and 41% over S4 (0.578). On UEA time-series classification, the hybrid achieves 35.2 average scaled accuracy versus Mamba's 28.8, though LinOSS (44.2) remains stronger on that specific suite.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Mamba, AUSSM achieves perfect accuracy on parity (1.0 vs. 0.13) and cycle navigation (1.0 vs. 0.86), while the hybrid dominates on mod arith (0.23 vs. 0.04) and repetition (0.45 vs. 0.15); however, Mamba retains an edge on majority count (0.45 vs. 0.096 for AUSSM alone) and solve equation (0.05 vs. 0.07). Against xLSTM, the hybrid wins on most tasks but xLSTM still leads on majority count (0.5 vs. 0.37) and mod arith without brackets (1.0 vs. 0.53). On the Weather forecasting benchmark, AUSSM Hybrid achieves 0.342 MAE, outperforming Mamba (0.464), LinOSS (0.508), S4 (0.578), Informer (0.731), and LSTnet (0.757) by 26–55%. On UEA time-series classification, the hybrid (35.2 avg) beats Mamba (28.8) and S6 (34.0) but trails LinOSS (44.2), suggesting LinOSS's oscillatory inductive bias is better suited to those particular physiological and chemical signals. Overall, the hybrid AUSSM+Mamba architecture consistently achieves the best or near-best results across the full suite, confirming the theoretical prediction that combining unitary adaptive recurrence with real-valued selective recurrence maximizes expressivity within diagonal SSMs.

## Relation to Prior Work

AUSSM extends the Mamba/S6 selective state space model by making the recurrent matrix A input-dependent and constraining it to be skew-symmetric, yielding unitary eigenvalues that enable modulo counting and solvable language recognition—capabilities proven impossible for Mamba's positive-real-spectrum recurrence. The architecture is motivated by the jPCA skew-symmetric ODE from computational neuroscience (Churchland et al., 2012) and formalized within the Krohn-Rhodes expressivity framework established by Sarrof et al. (NeurIPS 2024), which showed that diagonal SSMs with non-negative eigenvalues are limited to star-free languages. The key departure from prior unitary RNNs (uRNN, EUNN, AntisymmetricRNN) is that AUSSM remains a *linear* SSM with a separable convolution formulation, rather than a non-linear RNN requiring complex-valued nonlinearities, and it achieves linear-time scalability through a novel separable kernel decomposition that applies to any simultaneously diagonalizable adaptive SSM.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides the first principled resolution of the expressivity-scalability tradeoff for diagonal SSMs: by combining unitary adaptive recurrence (AUSSM) with partially adaptive real-valued recurrence (Mamba), the hybrid achieves maximal formal language expressivity within the diagonal SSM class (all solvable languages) while retaining linear-time parallel computation. The practical CUDA implementation and separable convolution formulation make this architecture deployable, and the weather forecasting SOTA (0.342 MAE vs. 0.464 for Mamba) suggests the expressivity gains translate to real-world gains. Key limitations remain: AUSSM is only linearly input-adaptive (not fully nonlinear LTV), the separable kernel requires simultaneous diagonalizability, evaluations are limited to modest-scale tasks (single 2080 Ti GPU), and the optimal strategy for interleaving AUSSM and Mamba blocks is not yet characterized. Whether these gains persist at foundation-model scale and whether tensor-core-optimized variants can close the remaining constant-factor gap are important open questions.
