## Problem

Existing pruning-based backdoor defenses treat pruning as a one-way subtractive process that removes components associated with the backdoor task, yielding inconsistent and brittle performance across attack scenarios. While fine-tuning approaches dominate recent literature, they degrade significantly under limited data and struggle to restore correct classification of backdoor-triggered inputs. This paper asks whether a more principled pruning paradigm—combining mask invertibility with component selection—can close the performance gap between pruning and fine-tuning defenses while preserving interpretability.

## Input / Output

The method takes a backdoored CNN (or ViT) with parameters θ′ and a small set of clean, correctly labeled images D_m (SPC = 2, 10, or 100) as input; the defender has no access to backdoor data, the trigger pattern, or the target class. It produces a per-layer channel-wise pruning mask A′, its inverse Ā′, and a selection vector S that together define which channels are pruned, retained, or shared between the two complementary views. Internally, the method also synthesizes sample-specific perturbations δ (bounded by ‖δ‖∞ < 1) as proxies for unknown triggers, which are used during training but discarded at inference.

## Method

IMS introduces two key mechanisms: (1) an invertible mask where the standard mask a′ and its inverse ā′ are constructed via sigmoid-scaled thresholding of a preliminary mask a, modulated by a learned selection vector s that marks components shared between clean and backdoor tasks as unprunable (s≈1) versus eligible for pruning (s≈0); and (2) a bi-level optimization framework with three phases—mask initialization (aligning a′ outputs with clean predictions while diverging ā′ outputs), an inner subproblem that synthesizes perturbations δ by maximizing disagreement between unmasked and inverse-masked outputs, and an outer subproblem that refines a and s to suppress backdoor behavior while preserving clean accuracy. The loss functions use agreement (−log⟨q̂,q⟩) and disagreement (−log(1−⟨q̂,q⟩)) terms over softmax outputs, with an ℓ₁ sparsity penalty on S. The theoretical analysis (Lemma 1) proves that in the limit s→0 and k→∞, the mask and inverse converge to exact binary complements, ensuring invertibility.

## Results

Across 288 test cases (8 attacks × 4 architectures × 3 datasets × 3 poisoning ratios), IMS achieves median ASR of 5.6, 4.7, and 4.2 (×100) at SPC 2, 10, and 100 respectively, outperforming all pruning baselines (best pruning ASR: FP at 29.5, 6.0, 12.4). On recovery (RDR), IMS achieves 38.9, 28.5, and 21.2, substantially better than SAU (57.7, 42.1, 37.1) and FT-SAM (55.3, 33.7, 15.5) at SPC 2 and 10. On accuracy preservation (ARR), IMS records 17.3, 9.8, and 6.5, competitive with or better than fine-tuning methods. On the ImageNette subset, IMS maintains comparable performance (e.g., ASR 92.4→63.0 for BadNet at SPC 1→100), confirming scalability to larger images.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IMS decisively outperforms all six pruning baselines (FP, ANP, AWN, RNP, NFT, FMP) on both ASR and RDR across every SPC setting; for example, at SPC=10, IMS achieves ASR 4.7 vs. FP 6.0, ANP 25.2, AWN 74.7, RNP 80.6, NFT 52.0, and FMP 59.0, with RDR 28.5 vs. 56.1–93.3 for the same group. Against the fine-tuning baselines, IMS trades a small ASR gap to SAU (4.7 vs. 2.0 at SPC=10; 4.2 vs. 2.3 at SPC=100) for substantially better RDR (28.5 vs. 42.1; 21.2 vs. 37.1) and comparable or lower ARR. Compared to FT-SAM, IMS achieves lower RDR at SPC 2 (38.9 vs. 55.3) and SPC 10 (28.5 vs. 33.7) while matching ASR (4.7 vs. 4.8 at SPC=10). Against BTI-DBF, IMS wins on RDR at SPC 2 (38.9 vs. 56.4) and SPC 10 (28.5 vs. 45.2) with comparable ASR. The paper also evaluates backdoor reactivation (Table 7), where IMS shows slightly higher reactivation ASR than SAU but comparable vulnerability to FT-SAM, with 50% of scenarios maintaining reactivation ASR below 50%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the bi-level optimization structure of SAU (Wei et al., NeurIPS 2023), which synthesizes adversarial perturbations to proxy backdoor triggers, but replaces the fine-tuning step with a learned pruning mask. It extends the inverse-mask concept from BTI-DBF (Xu et al., ICLR 2023) by introducing a learned selection vector that identifies shared components, making the mask and its inverse complementary only over a selected subset rather than a hard binary partition. Conceptually, the paper departs from all prior pruning-based defenses (ANP, AWN, RNP, NFT, FMP) by evaluating model behavior under two opposing pruning configurations simultaneously, rather than treating pruning as a one-way removal of backdoor-associated channels.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in offering a pruning-based alternative that narrows the long-standing performance gap with fine-tuning defenses, particularly in data-scarce regimes (2–10 samples per class) where fine-tuning degrades sharply. The consistent RDR advantage over FT-SAM and SAU suggests pruning with invertible masks is better at restoring correct predictions for compromised samples, a capability fine-tuning has struggled with. Open questions include whether hybrid pruning-plus-fine-tuning strategies can combine the structural interpretability of IMS with the stronger ASR suppression of fine-tuning, and which ViT components (beyond embedding dimensions) are optimal pruning targets. The approach does not improve robustness against backdoor reactivation attacks, leaving a notable gap for adversarial settings.
