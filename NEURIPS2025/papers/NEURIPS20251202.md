## Problem

Template-based GFlowNets for molecular generation face a trilemma: existing methods either use small curated fragment libraries that guarantee low cost but limit chemical diversity (RGFN), or scale to large libraries but ignore synthesis cost entirely (RxnFlow). No prior approach simultaneously minimizes synthesis cost, scales to large building block sets, and performs well with small fragment libraries, leaving a gap between synthetic accessibility guarantees and practical cost-efficiency in drug discovery pipelines.

## Input / Output

SCENT consumes a building block library (ranging from ~2k curated fragments in SMALL to 128k Enamine REAL molecules in LARGE), a set of SMARTS-based reaction templates, and a scalar reward function (sEH proxy, GSK3β, or JNK3). The forward policy is a graph transformer that sequentially selects building blocks (embedded via ECFP4 fingerprints plus learnable vectors) and reaction templates to construct molecules, producing both the final molecular graph and an explicit multi-step synthesis pathway (up to 4 reactions). The backward policy decomposes molecules by sampling parent state-action tuples, and the Dynamic Library mechanism can expand the building block set during training with high-reward intermediates, enabling convergent (tree-structured) rather than purely linear synthesis pathways.

## Method

SCENT's core contribution is Recursive Cost Guidance, a general backward policy framework that uses lightweight MLP models (on ECFP4 fingerprints) to approximate the intractable recursive cost of backward transitions, steering generation toward cheaper and valid synthesis routes. Two specific cost functions are instantiated: Synthesis Cost Guidance, which computes transition cost from building block stock prices and reaction yields (C^S = (x + c(a')) / y), and Decomposability Guidance, which assigns binary cost (0/1) to penalize transitions through molecules that cannot be further decomposed into building blocks. The combined backward policy is the product of these two guided policies, while the forward policy is augmented with an Exploitation Penalty that applies an inverse-frequency count-based regularizer to repeated state-action pairs, counteracting the over-exploitation induced by cost guidance. A Dynamic Library mechanism periodically (every 1000 iterations) adds the top-L highest-expected-reward intermediates to the building block set, enabling full-tree synthesis and trajectory compression. Training uses the Trajectory Balance objective with a prioritized replay buffer, and the cost prediction models are updated online from encountered trajectories using MSE (for cost) and binary cross-entropy (for decomposability) losses.

## Results

On the sEH proxy task (reward threshold 8.0), SCENT(C+D+P) in the SMALL setting discovers 715±15 high-reward modes and 8,768±363 unique scaffolds with an average synthesis cost of 20.7±3.0, compared to RGFN's 538±21 modes, 5,862±354 scaffolds, and 37.7±2.0 cost. In the MEDIUM setting (64k Enamine building blocks), SCENT(C+P) discovers 37,714±3,430 modes and 90,068±9,010 scaffolds with a cost of 1,230±121, outperforming RGFN (4,755 modes, 10,638 scaffolds, cost 1,268) by ~8× in modes and ~8.5× in scaffolds. In the LARGE setting (128k building blocks), SCENT(C+P) finds 26,367±3,193 modes and 46,202±10,242 scaffolds at a cost of 1,291±66, versus RGFN's 5,242 modes, 6,215 scaffolds, and cost 1,725. SCENT also achieves the highest AiZynthFinder retrosynthesis success rate (77.3%) among template-based models, surpassing RGFN (64.7%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SCENT decisively outperforms all three template-based GFlowNet baselines. Against RGFN [8], SCENT(C+P) discovers ~8× more high-reward modes and ~8.5× more scaffolds in MEDIUM (37,714 vs 4,755 modes; 90,068 vs 10,638 scaffolds) while achieving a lower synthesis cost (1,230 vs 1,268) and higher average reward (7.81 vs 7.08). Against SynFlowNet [9] and RxnFlow [10], SCENT's advantage is even more dramatic: in MEDIUM, SCENT finds 37,714 modes versus 288 (SynFlowNet) and 26 (RxnFlow), with substantially higher rewards (7.81 vs 6.38 and 6.3). In the broader comparison (Tables 7–8), SCENT(C+D+P) achieves the highest AiZynthFinder success rate among template-based models (77.3% vs RGFN's 64.7%), while REINVENT [20] edges out SCENT on raw reward (7.97 vs 7.66) but trails on diversity (0.59 vs 0.73 Tanimoto distance) and is comparable on AiZynth (80.0% vs 77.3%). Graph GA [53] generates more diverse molecules (0.56 diversity) but has very poor synthesizability (11.3% AiZynth success), whereas FGFN [7] fails almost entirely on AiZynth (1.0%), underscoring the advantage of template-based generation.

## Relation to Prior Work

SCENT extends the template-based GFlowNet lineage initiated by RGFN (Koziarski et al., 2024), which itself builds on the GFlowNet framework (Bengio et al., 2021) and Trajectory Balance (Malkin et al., 2022). It departs from prior backward policy designs—RGFN's explicit decomposability verification, SynFlowNet's RL-based implicit guidance, and Guided Trajectory Balance's preference distributions—by introducing a general Recursive Cost Guidance framework that uses a learned surrogate to approximate intractable recursive transition costs. The Dynamic Library mechanism draws on library learning principles (DreamCoder, Options framework) but shifts from action abstractions to state abstractions, caching high-reward intermediate molecules as reusable substructures for nonlinear synthesis trees.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SCENT addresses three practical bottlenecks in template-based molecular generation—synthesis cost awareness, scalability to 128k+ building block libraries, and effective use of small fragment sets—that limit the adoption of GFlowNet-based drug design in real screening pipelines. The Recursive Cost Guidance framework is general and could be adapted to other sequential generation tasks with recursive cost structures. Key open questions include: whether the simplified cost model (omitting solvents, purification, hazardous reagents) holds under lab-specific calibration, how performance scales with increasing numbers of reaction templates, and whether the generated molecules survive wet-lab validation. The absence of ADMET/toxicity integration and the observed task-dependent performance variability further limit immediate practical deployment.
