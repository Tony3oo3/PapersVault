## Problem

Existing disaster monitoring approaches are limited to specific disaster types, rely on manual expert interpretation, and lack datasets with sufficient temporal granularity and natural language annotations to track disaster progression over time. No comprehensive multimodal dataset pairs temporal satellite imagery of diverse natural disasters with geolocated, temporally-aligned textual descriptions, leaving a critical gap for training and evaluating vision-language models on disaster understanding tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254355/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes temporal sequences of Sentinel-2 RGB satellite patches (512×512 pixels at 10 m/pixel, ~5-day revisit, averaging 4.13 frames per event over ~18 days) along with a natural language question and the patch's center coordinates and pixel resolution. Outputs are structured as multiple-choice selections for event classification, temporal grounding (identifying onset/offset dates), and location grounding (pixel-coordinate localization of features), as well as free-text descriptions scored via BLEU, ROUGE-L, METEOR, and LLM-as-judge evaluation.

## Method

The core contribution is a data curation pipeline that links FEMA disaster declarations to news articles, using an LLM (Gemini 2.0-flash) to extract geocoded proper nouns for precise event localization and to generate date-stamped captions describing visible changes from article text alone. This produces MONITRS (~10,000 events) and MONITRS-QA (44,308 train / 10,196 test QA pairs split by event to prevent leakage), with both templated questions targeting specific reasoning types and LLM-generated event-specific questions. For evaluation, they fine-tune TEOChat via LoRA (r=32, α=64, lr=2e-5, 8-bit quantization, 1 epoch on 4 A6000 GPUs) on MONITRS-QA, demonstrating that parameter-efficient transfer learning on this specialized dataset yields statistically significant improvements (p<0.001) over six baselines including Gemini 2.0-pro and GPT-4.1, particularly on temporal grounding (76.05% vs. 11–26% for baselines).

## Results

On the MONITRS-QA templated multiple-choice benchmark, the fine-tuned TEOChat (full dataset) achieves 91.66% accuracy on event classification, 76.05% on temporal grounding, and 31.34% on location grounding. The best baseline (Gemini 2.0-pro) reaches only 72.06% on event classification, while temporal grounding peaks at 26.5% (GeoChat) and location grounding at 76.80% (GeoChat). On the generated open-ended VQA, the fine-tuned model (1/5 data) achieves 52.18% multiple-choice accuracy and 0.4912 METEOR, outperforming all baselines (28–37% MCQ accuracy). All improvements are statistically significant per McNemar's test (p < 0.001).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The fine-tuned TEOChat substantially outperforms all six baselines on event classification (91.66% vs. 28–72%) and temporal grounding (76.05% vs. 11–27%), with the largest gap over Gemini 2.0-pro (+19.6 pp) and VideoLLaVA (+65.5 pp on temporal). However, it trails GeoChat significantly on location grounding (31.34% vs. 76.80%), which the authors attribute to GeoChat's explicit training on latitude/longitude-to-pixel correspondence. Among the general-purpose MLLMs, Gemini 2.0-pro is the strongest baseline on event classification (72.06%) and location grounding (33.81%), while GPT-4.1 underperforms Gemini on event classification (39.12% vs. 72.06%), suggesting Gemini's training may include labeled satellite imagery. On the generated open-ended VQA (Table 3), the fine-tuned model leads all baselines in MCQ accuracy (52.18% vs. 28–37%) and open-ended metrics (METEOR 0.4912 vs. 0.35–0.47), with LLM-judged scores confirming improved factual accuracy (3.84 vs. 2.44–3.41) and visual evidence use (2.50 vs. 2.00–2.28).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on TEOChat (Irvin et al., 2024), a vision-language model for temporal earth observation data, by fine-tuning it with LoRA on a newly constructed disaster-specific dataset. It departs from prior remote sensing VLMs (GeoChat, VideoLLaVA) that handle single images or lack disaster-specific temporal reasoning, and from change detection methods (Sachdeva & Zisserman, Yang et al.) that identify visual differences without semantic or natural language interpretation. The key conceptual departure is the data curation pipeline that pairs FEMA disaster declarations with geocoded news articles to produce aligned temporal satellite sequences with natural language annotations — a resource that did not previously exist in this form.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MONITRS fills a critical gap by providing the first large-scale (~10,000 events) multimodal dataset combining temporal satellite imagery, natural language annotations, and geotagged locations for natural disaster monitoring, enabling the community to develop and benchmark models for disaster progression tracking. The finding that even state-of-the-art MLLMs achieve only 11–26% on temporal grounding for disasters, yet improve to 76% after modest LoRA fine-tuning, suggests that the bottleneck is data availability rather than model architecture. Key limitations include US-only coverage (FEMA), 10 m Sentinel-2 resolution with ~5-day revisit, and LLM-generated captions that may not always match visual evidence; open questions include generalization to global disasters, incorporation of higher-resolution or SAR data, and whether the location grounding gap (31% vs. 92% for classification) reflects a fundamental difficulty in spatial reasoning from satellite imagery.
