## Problem

Standard autoregressive inference schemes for diffusion models fail to predict partially observable, long-memory dynamical systems because they condition on only a few recent frames and accumulate distribution errors over rollouts. In solar physics, the Sun's interior drives surface evolution but is unobservable, and active regions exhibit long-range temporal dependencies that require integrating information from far in the past. No existing diffusion-based model addresses both partial observability and long-memory simultaneously for high-resolution, multi-modal solar prediction.

## Input / Output

The model consumes 12-channel video frames (3 photospheric vector magnetic-field components from HMI and 9 atmospheric images from AIA at different wavelengths) at 512×512 spatial resolution, sampled hourly, tracking a solar active region over 48-hour windows. At inference, the model generates 7-frame clips (2K+1 with K=3) per diffusion call, using a binary mask to designate which frames are conditioning (observed or previously generated) and which are to be denoised from noise. The multiscale scheme chains these calls to produce arbitrarily long future trajectories, with the conditioning mask shifting to incorporate progressively more distant past observations at each iteration.

## Method

The core contribution is a multiscale inference scheme built on 'multiscale templates' — sets of 2K+1 time indices centered at the present that become progressively coarser (spaced by powers of α≥1) with distance, so a fixed budget of K conditioning frames covers a horizon exponential in K rather than linear. Inference begins with the largest template (α_max=2.5, spanning ±9 steps) to generate the most distant future steps in a single diffusion call, then iteratively fills gaps using smaller templates in decreasing order of α, each conditioned on at least K+1 already-available frames. The denoiser is a 62M-parameter Vision Transformer (16 layers, 512 hidden dim, 4 heads) with RoPE positional encoding and Karras-style input/output/noise preconditioning, trained with the masked conditional diffusion loss using randomized masks over the 7-frame window. This design conditions more frequently on distant past observations and generates up to 9 steps ahead per call, reducing error accumulation compared to autoregressive rollouts that require 3 separate calls for the same horizon.

## Results

On the SDO Solar Active Region Dataset, the proposed multiscale inference scheme (with a DiT denoiser) achieves the lowest Wasserstein distance across all prediction horizons: 3.0 (1–4 h), 4.3 (4–16 h), and 5.5 (16–32 h), compared to 3.9/5.6/7.9 for the same model with autoregressive rollout. The method also attains the best MAE in the power spectrum (0.12/0.22/0.33) and the lowest NMAE on solar-physics gradient statistics (MeanGBT: 0.14/0.27/0.33; MeanGBZ: 0.10/0.21/0.27). On the synthetic sinusoidal benchmark, the multiscale scheme reduces the Wasserstein distance from 0.23 (autoregressive) to 0.021, demonstrating effective recovery of long-range dependencies under partial observability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the autoregressive rollout of the same DiT model, the multiscale scheme reduces Wasserstein distance by 23–30% (e.g., 5.5 vs 7.9 at 16–32 h) and cuts power-spectrum MAE by roughly 38% (0.33 vs 0.53), confirming that conditioning on distant past steps mitigates error accumulation. Compared to the Hierarchy-2 scheme from Flexible Diffusion Models [25], the multiscale approach wins on Wasserstein (5.5 vs 6.0 at 16–32 h), power-spectrum MAE (0.33 vs 0.38), and MeanGBZ (0.27 vs 0.31), though it trails slightly on MeanGBT at the shortest horizon (0.14 vs 0.12). Against the deterministic AViT transformer [50], the multiscale diffusion model achieves a 2.7× lower Wasserstein distance at 16–32 h (5.5 vs 15) and roughly halves the NMAE on both gradient statistics (0.33 vs 0.45 for MeanGBT; 0.27 vs 0.44 for MeanGBZ), while AViT retains a marginal edge on power-spectrum MAE at 1–4 h (0.11 vs 0.12). Versus the AR-diffusion model for turbulent flows [39], the proposed method dominates across every metric and horizon, with Wasserstein 5.5 vs 16 and MeanGBT NMAE 0.33 vs 0.67 at 16–32 h, underscoring the difficulty of transferring fluid-dynamics inference schemes to partially observable solar dynamics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the masked conditional video diffusion framework (MCVD, Voleti et al. 2022) and the diffusion model design principles of Karras et al. (2022) to the setting of partially observable, long-memory dynamical systems. The key departure is from prior inference schemes for video diffusion—autoregressive sliding windows (Kohl et al. 2023, Price et al. 2023) and the flexible conditioning strategies of Harvey et al. (2022, FDM)—which condition on only a few recent frames and discard distant past information. The multiscale template inference scheme introduced here is specifically designed to condition on progressively coarser past observations, drawing conceptual inspiration from wavelet and multiscale analysis of long-memory processes, and is applied to a new 8.5TB multi-modal solar dataset that goes well beyond the single-modality, low-resolution data used in prior solar ML work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that for partially observable systems, the inference scheme is as critical as the model architecture: the same ViT backbone shows large performance gaps (e.g., Wasserstein 3.0 vs 3.9 at 1–4h) purely from changing the conditioning strategy, a finding that challenges the common assumption that autoregressive rollouts are the default for video diffusion. The 8.5TB multi-modal solar dataset and the first multi-modal high-resolution solar video diffusion model provide a concrete benchmark for the solar physics ML community. Open questions include whether the multiscale scheme generalizes to other partially observable domains (oceanography, seismology), whether adaptive or learned conditioning can replace the hand-designed templates, and whether the approach remains competitive when observations are dominated by short-term patterns rather than long-memory dynamics.
