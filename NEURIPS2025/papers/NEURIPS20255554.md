## Problem

Existing financial benchmarks for LLMs rely on historical back-testing, inadvertently allowing models to 'time travel' by regurgitating market events already present in their training corpora. This information leakage inflates performance metrics and makes it impossible to assess whether an LLM can genuinely make investment decisions under real-time market conditions. The paper addresses this by introducing a live benchmarking framework that explicitly prevents future-data contamination.

## Input / Output

The system ingests real-time data streams from financial APIs (Yahoo Finance, Alpha Vantage) covering OHLCV price data, company news, insider transactions, financial statements, macro indicators, and policy updates, all published after each evaluated model's knowledge cutoff. Outputs are structured as standardized signals (Bullish/Bearish/Neutral with justifications) from six specialized analyst agents, which feed into final trading decisions (Buy/Sell/Hold with share counts and rationale) that update a persistent portfolio state. A web-based interface (deepfund.paradoox.ai) provides real-time visualization of cumulative returns, Sharpe ratios, portfolio compositions, and comparative leaderboards across all evaluated LLMs.

## Method

DeepFund implements a multi-agent orchestrator-worker architecture where a single LLM backend assumes three sequential roles: a Financial Planner that selects and assigns analysts based on market conditions, a six-member Analyst Team (Technical, Fundamental, Insider, Company News, Macro Economic, Policy) that concurrently generates domain-specific signals, and a Portfolio Manager that synthesizes signals into trading actions while managing risk control and maintaining a dual-memory architecture (short-term FundState object plus long-term trading history). The critical differentiator is the live environment: a modular API gateway continuously ingests real-time market data specifically published after each model's pretraining cutoff, fundamentally shifting evaluation from retrospective back-testing to forward-looking interaction. The entire pipeline is built on LangChain with standardized prompts and a fixed temperature of 0.5, using Supabase for persistent state tracking across 24+ trading days. The framework is designed for extensibility, with decoupled LLM, data source, and agent composition modules that allow new models and analytical methodologies to be integrated without architectural changes.

## Results

Across 24 trading days (March 17 – April 17, 2025) on five Berkshire Hathaway holdings, only Grok 3 mini Beta achieved a positive cumulative return (+1.1%), while the remaining eight LLMs all incurred net losses ranging from −1.9% (Gemini 2.5 Flash) to −8.1% (Doubao-1.5-pro). Grok also posted the lowest maximum drawdown (5.5%) and the only positive Sharpe ratio (0.51) and alpha (+0.2). The S&P 500 benchmark returned −6.91% over the same period, meaning most LLMs underperformed even a passive market index, and a simple Buy & Hold strategy (CR_bnh) would have been more resilient to the tariff-driven volatility.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Grok 3 mini Beta is the sole profitable model, outperforming all eight other LLMs by at least 3.0 percentage points in cumulative return and beating the S&P 500 (−6.91%) by 8.0 pp. Gemini 2.5 Flash comes second (−1.9%) with the second-lowest MDD (6.4%), but still trails Grok by 3.0 pp in CR. Claude 3.7 Sonnet (−3.7%) achieves the highest win rate (70%) yet suffers a 10.1% drawdown, indicating it wins more individual trades but takes larger losses on the ones it misses. DeepSeek-V3 (−5.7%) and GPT-4.1 (−5.9%) cluster near the S&P 500 return but both exhibit high beta (0.94 and 0.77) and large drawdowns (14.5% and 12.8%), reflecting aggressive, market-correlated strategies. The Chinese-produced models (Qwen2.5-Max, GLM-4-Air, Doubao-1.5-pro) occupy the bottom three positions (−6.7% to −8.1%), all underperforming the S&P 500 and showing negative alpha, while the paper notes that during the tariff-driven bearish phase, US-produced LLMs (except GPT-4.1) actually fared worse than their Chinese counterparts. Overall, no LLM matched the resilience of a passive Buy & Hold strategy, which would have limited losses to roughly −1.6% to −5.6% depending on the ticker mix.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of static, back-testing-based financial benchmarks (TAT-QA, FinBen, InvestorBench, FinRL-Meta) by transplanting the 'live forward-testing' methodology already established in code (LiveCodeBench) and forecasting (ForecastBench, LiveBench) into the fund investment domain. It repurposes the multi-agent trading architecture from prior LLM trading systems (TradingAgents, HedgeAgents, CryptoTrade) but wraps it in a live market environment that explicitly uses only data published after each model's pretraining cutoff, thereby eliminating the information leakage that plagues retrospective evaluations. The paper positions itself as the first to achieve true live benchmarking specifically for fund investment, filling a gap between general-purpose live benchmarks and domain-specific financial evaluation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The information leakage problem in LLM trading evaluation is a real methodological concern that has been underappreciated; this paper provides a concrete, reproducible tool (with open-source code and a web interface) to address it, which could shift how the community validates LLM trading claims. However, the empirical findings rest on a single 24-day window, five tickers, and one run with no error bars, limiting generalizability. Open questions include whether results hold across longer periods, broader universes, different market regimes, and whether the observed behavioral 'personalities' (e.g., Grok's conservative vs. DeepSeek's aggressive style) are stable model traits or artifacts of the specific market conditions encountered.
