## Problem

Sparse attention inference methods reduce the quadratic cost of transformer prefill but cause a distributional shift in attention outputs that breaks query-key alignment during dense decoding, leading to severe accuracy drops on long-context retrieval tasks (e.g., 0% vs. 62% on RULER MultiKey-3). The paper identifies this distributional shift as the root cause and asks how to correct it without reverting to full quadratic computation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250403/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes the sparse attention output A*V produced by any existing sparse kernel (Streaming LLM, HiP, MInference, etc.) along with the full Q, K, V matrices. It computes dense attention only for every γ-th query row (default γ=64, yielding ~98.5% sparsity) and produces a corrected attention output ÂV that is dense in both the query and key dimensions, closely approximating the full quadratic attention output. Because the correction is applied in the attention output space, it integrates with existing sparse kernels and inference pipelines without modifying the sparse computation itself.

## Method

Δ Attention computes the difference between dense and sparse attention outputs on a sparse subset of query rows (every γ-th row), then broadcasts this delta correction to all rows: (ÂV)_i = (A*V)_i + [ÃV_{⌊i/γ⌋} − (A*V)_{⌊i/γ⌋γ}]. The key assumption, empirically validated via high cosine similarity within γ-neighborhoods, is that the missing attention contribution A^ΔV is approximately constant across neighboring rows, so a single delta computation suffices for γ consecutive outputs. A theoretical bound (Lemma 1) shows the approximation error is at most (H/(H+T))·max|v_i| on the tail, which is small when the sparse method captures most of the attention mass. The procedure is training-free, kernel-agnostic, and adds only ~1.5% of the full quadratic computation overhead, recovering 88% of quadratic attention accuracy on RULER 131K while maintaining 32× speedup over Flash Attention 2 at 1M tokens.

## Results

On the RULER 131K benchmark with Llama 3.1 8B Instruct, Δ Attention applied on top of Streaming LLM (2K window) achieves 64.40% accuracy, recovering 88% of Flash Attention 2's 73.16% while maintaining ~98.5% sparsity. Across all context lengths (4K–131K), the average accuracy jumps from 44.25% (Streaming LLM) to 83.06% (Streaming LLM+Δ), a 36%pt improvement. On PG19 Long QA, Δ reduces the Long PPL gap to Flash Attention by 50–75% (from 7.02 to 5.96 for Streaming LLM+Δ vs. 5.11 for Flash Attention 2). At 1M token prefills, Streaming LLM+Δ is 32× faster than Flash Attention 2.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Streaming LLM (the primary sparse method being corrected), Δ Attention delivers a 37%pt improvement on RULER 131K (27.45% → 64.40%) and more than doubles accuracy even when Streaming LLM is given a 4K window (30.32% → 64.40%). Against Flash Attention 2, Δ Attention recovers 88% of the 131K accuracy while being 32× faster at 1M token prefills. Against HiP, Δ adds a modest 5%pt gain on 131K (68.74% → 73.71%), since HiP already performs well at shorter contexts. Against MInference, Δ adds 7.6%pt on 131K (65.73% → 73.31%). In the extended comparison (Table 6), Streaming LLM+Δ (64.40% at 131K) substantially outperforms CacheBlend (0.00%), APE (26.76%), and Star Attention Mask (12.00%), confirming that the distributional shift correction is more effective than prior post-hoc or RAG-based approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself as a diagnostic and corrective layer on top of existing sparse attention inference methods (Streaming LLM, HiP, MInference), rather than proposing a new sparsification pattern. It builds on the Streaming LLM paradigm of attention sinks plus sliding windows and the Star Attention strategy of sparse prefill with dense decode, but identifies a previously unaddressed failure mode: a distributional shift in attention outputs that breaks query-key alignment during decoding. The theoretical grounding draws on induction head literature (Olsson et al., Wu et al.) to explain why the shift is particularly damaging at lower layers, and the Δ correction is framed as a minimal post-processing step in the attention output space that is agnostic to the underlying sparse kernel.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a training-free, plug-and-post-processing correction that recovers 88% of quadratic attention accuracy on RULER 131K while maintaining ~98.5% sparsity and 32× speedup over Flash Attention 2 at 1M tokens. The diagnosis that the core problem is distributional shift rather than insufficient context window is a useful conceptual contribution for the sparse attention community. Open questions include whether the fixed-γ assumption (A^ΔV)_i ≈ (A^ΔV)_{i+ν} can be made adaptive, whether smarter interpolation of delta terms (explored in the appendix) can close the remaining gap, and whether the method's effectiveness degrades further on weaker models at extreme context lengths (Mistral NeMo 12B still scores only ~10% at 131K).
