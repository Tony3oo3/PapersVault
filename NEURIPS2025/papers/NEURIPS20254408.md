## Problem

Existing caching strategies for diffusion-based video generation rely on local greedy heuristics with fixed thresholds to decide when to reuse model outputs, ignoring the temporal heterogeneity of the denoising process and the accumulation of small errors over the full trajectory. This leads to uncontrolled content degradation and loss of high-frequency details in accelerated videos, as locally optimal caching decisions can produce globally inconsistent results.

## Input / Output

LeMiCa is a training-free, model-agnostic acceleration layer applied on top of existing text-to-video diffusion models (Open-Sora 1.2, Latte, CogVideoX). At inference time it takes a text prompt and noise seed and produces a generated video at the model's native resolution and frame count (e.g., 51 frames at 480P for Open-Sora, 16 frames at 512×512 for Latte). The caching policy is precomputed offline from a static DAG built using 70 prompts from T2V-CompBench, so no runtime overhead is incurred beyond the reduced number of forward passes.

## Method

LeMiCa replaces local greedy caching with a global path-planning formulation. First, it defines a Global Outcome-Aware error that measures the L1 difference between the final denoised output when a segment (i→j) is cached versus the original full-inference output, capturing how early-stage cache errors amplify downstream. Second, it constructs a static directed acyclic graph offline, where each edge represents a candidate cache segment weighted by its averaged global error across multiple prompts and seeds, with a maximum skip length to keep the graph sparse. Third, under a fixed budget B of full forward steps, it solves a Lexicographic Minimax Path Optimization: among all s-to-t paths with exactly B full steps, it selects the one whose sorted cache-edge error vector is lexicographically smallest, thereby explicitly bounding the worst-case single-segment degradation before considering secondary errors. This is implemented via dynamic programming that tracks the maximum edge weight along each partial path and breaks ties lexicographically, yielding a robust caching schedule that suppresses error peaks rather than merely minimizing total additive cost.

## Results

LeMiCa achieves a 2.93× speedup on Latte (LeMiCa-fast) and a 2.44× speedup on Open-Sora while maintaining strong reconstruction quality. Under the fidelity-focused setting (LeMiCa-slow), the method reduces LPIPS to 0.050 on Open-Sora, 0.091 on Latte, and 0.023 on CogVideoX, with corresponding PSNR values of 31.32, 27.65, and 35.93 dB. VBench scores remain within 0.5% of the original (e.g., 79.26% vs. 79.22% on Open-Sora), confirming minimal perceptual degradation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LeMiCa consistently outperforms all four baselines on reconstruction quality (LPIPS, SSIM, PSNR) at comparable or higher speedups across all three video models. Against the closest competitor TeaCache, LeMiCa-slow reduces LPIPS by over 2× (0.050 vs. 0.134 on Open-Sora; 0.091 vs. 0.195 on Latte) while matching its 1.5× speedup, and LeMiCa-fast achieves a higher 2.93× speedup on Latte versus TeaCache-fast's 2.60× with only a modest LPIPS increase (0.273 vs. 0.318). Against PAB, LeMiCa-slow improves LPIPS from 0.064 to 0.023 on CogVideoX and from 0.174 to 0.050 on Open-Sora at a slightly higher speedup. The image-oriented baselines Δ-DiT and T-GATE are substantially outperformed: on Latte, Δ-DiT degrades VBench to 52% and LPIPS to 0.851, while LeMiCa-slow maintains 77.45% VBench and 0.091 LPIPS. On the OOD IP-VBench benchmark, LeMiCa further extends its lead over TeaCache, achieving LPIPS 0.042 vs. 0.120 overall and PSNR 33.5 vs. 26.4 dB.

## Relation to Prior Work

LeMiCa extends the training-free caching line of work (DeepCache, T-GATE, Δ-DiT, PAB, TeaCache) that reuses model outputs across timesteps to reduce redundant computation in diffusion sampling. It departs from the dominant Local-Greedy paradigm—where fixed thresholds on adjacent-step L1 differences decide caching—by reframing cache scheduling as a global path optimization over a static DAG with globally error-weighted edges. The key conceptual shift is the introduction of a Global Outcome-Aware error metric that measures each cache segment's impact on the final output, combined with a lexicographic minimax criterion that explicitly bounds worst-case path degradation rather than minimizing additive local cost.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LeMiCa offers a principled, model-agnostic alternative to heuristic caching that is likely to be widely adopted given its negligible offline cost (<1 s for graph construction and path search) and training-free nature. The finding that a single prompt suffices to construct a near-optimal DAG (PSNR 24.51 vs. 24.67 at 350 samples) suggests the caching structure is remarkably stable across content, which could simplify deployment. Open questions include whether the static DAG approach remains effective when the base model is fine-tuned or when sampling schedules change substantially, and whether the lexicographic minimax formulation transfers to other iterative generative settings (3D, multi-view, multi-modal) where controllable acceleration remains an open challenge.
