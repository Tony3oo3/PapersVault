## Problem

Existing VLA pretraining methods (e.g., OpenVLA) use only a single RGB image and text instruction as input, which is insufficient to resolve two forms of ambiguity: coordinate system chaos (the robot's pose cannot be inferred when its body is occluded, as in 67% of DROID samples) and state chaos (a single frame cannot disambiguate symmetric or visually similar action contexts). This incomplete input produces high-variance, non-smooth conditional action distributions that severely degrade pretraining efficiency and generalization.

## Input / Output

The model consumes sequential RGB-D frames (current frame at 448×448, up to 5 historical frames at 224×224 selected via memory bank sampling from a 20-frame window) along with a text instruction and calibrated camera intrinsics/extrinsics. Depth maps are back-projected into 3D world coordinates using the camera pose, and these coordinates are injected as learnable positional embeddings into the visual feature map. The output is a single robot action vector [Δx ∈ ℝ³, Δθ ∈ ℝ³, g ∈ [0,1]] representing end-effector translation, rotation offset, and gripper open/close state, predicted by a 2-layer MLP action head on the VLM's last hidden token.

## Method

4D-VLA builds on InternVL-4B and introduces two key mechanisms to inject 4D spatiotemporal information. First, a spatial-aware visual token pipeline back-projects depth into 3D coordinates via camera calibration, applies a learnable positional embedding to these coordinates, and adds them element-wise to the vision encoder's feature map before the MLP projector, producing spatial vision tokens that explicitly align the robot's coordinate frame with the scene. Second, a memory bank sampling strategy adaptively selects k=5 informative historical frames from a 20-frame window by maintaining a similarity queue (using a feature extractor φ), ensuring each retained frame is maximally dissimilar to already-selected ones; a learnable temporal encoding token prepended to each spatial vision token captures the non-uniform temporal offset. The model is pretrained on DROID (76k trajectories, 1 epoch, batch 512, 8×A6000 GPUs) with a combined loss of L2 translation, L2 rotation, BCE gripper, and a directional loss emphasizing action orientation, then fine-tuned on LIBERO with all parameters unfrozen except the vision encoder.

## Results

On the LIBERO benchmark, 4D-VLA achieves an average success rate of 88.6%, surpassing the strongest baseline OpenVLA (76.5%) by 12.1 points and the next-best SpatialVLA (78.1%) by 10.5 points. The largest margin appears on LIBERO-LONG (79.1% vs. 53.7% for OpenVLA, a 25.4-point gap), indicating particularly strong gains on long-horizon tasks. On the proposed MV-Bench, 4D-VLA reaches 81.0% in-view and 73.8% cross-view success, outperforming OpenVLA by 28.8 and 23.3 points respectively. On RLBench under the ARM4R protocol, 4D-VLA attains 77.4% average success, exceeding ARM4R (71.9%) by 5.5 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

4D-VLA outperforms all 13 baselines across every evaluated benchmark. On LIBERO, the closest competitor is SpatialVLA (78.1% avg), which 4D-VLA surpasses by 10.5 points; the primary baseline OpenVLA trails by 12.1 points, with the gap widening to 25.4 points on the most challenging LIBERO-LONG suite. Among efficiency-focused methods (FastV, VLA-Cache, SparseVLM), 4D-VLA leads by 13.9–23.9 points, suggesting that token-reduction strategies alone cannot compensate for the lack of spatiotemporal grounding. On RLBench under the ARM4R protocol, 4D-VLA (77.4%) beats ARM4R (71.9%) by 5.5 points and substantially outperforms prior RLBench baselines (C2FARM-BC 32.5%, LLARVA 52.0%, PerAct 56.0%). On the proposed MV-Bench, 4D-VLA's 81.0% in-view and 73.8% cross-view success rates exceed OpenVLA's 52.2% and 50.5% by 28.8 and 23.3 points respectively, demonstrating markedly stronger viewpoint generalization. In real-world Franka arm experiments, the full 4D-VLA model reaches 85.63% average success versus OpenVLA's 27.70%, with the largest gains on precise-placement and instruction-following tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant VLA pretraining paradigm (OpenVLA, RT-2, Octo) that treats a single RGB frame plus text as sufficient input, arguing instead that the resulting conditional action distribution suffers from 'coordinate system chaos' and 'state chaos' due to missing spatial and temporal context. It contrasts with HPT, which patches coordinate inconsistency via dataset-specific parameters (lacking scalability), and with ARM4R, which uses full 3D point-cloud encoders (heavier and less VLM-compatible). The key conceptual move is to back-project depth into 3D coordinates and fuse them with visual features via learnable positional embeddings, combined with an adaptive memory bank sampling strategy for temporal context—effectively repurposing standard RGB-D camera calibration as a lightweight spatial grounding mechanism within a VLM backbone (InternVL-4B).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 'coordinate system chaos' framing provides a useful diagnostic for why diverse-dataset pretraining underperforms, and the controlled chaos experiment (Fig. 7) cleanly validates that 3D coordinate information is the critical missing cue. The introduction of MV-Bench fills a gap in multi-view generalization evaluation for VLA models, and the memory bank sampling offers a practical, low-overhead alternative to uniform or learned video token compression. A key limitation is the hard dependency on RGB-D input and calibrated camera parameters, which restricts deployment to setups with depth sensors; whether the approach transfers to monocular depth estimation or point-cloud-free settings remains open. The finding that proprioceptive tokens actively hurt generalization (Tab. 7) is a useful caution for practitioners adding state inputs to VLA models.
