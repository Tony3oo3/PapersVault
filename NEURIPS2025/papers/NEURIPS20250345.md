## Problem

Most end-to-end driving methods adopt a one-shot paradigm that maps current sensor observations directly to future trajectories, underestimating how the surrounding scene evolves over time and ignoring the bidirectional coupling between the ego vehicle's planned actions and future scene dynamics. This limits adaptive decision-making in complex, interactive driving scenarios where anticipating scene changes is critical.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250345/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view camera images (3 views at 1024×256 for NAVSIM, 6 views at 640×360 for nuScenes), LiDAR point clouds, ego status, and K-Means-clustered anchored multi-modal trajectories (256 modes for NAVSIM, 6 for nuScenes). It produces planned multi-modal trajectories over a 4-second/8-step horizon (NAVSIM) or 3-second/6-step horizon (nuScenes), along with current and future BEV semantic maps at 8×8 spatial resolution. Unusually, the iterative process yields N pairs of future BEV maps and trajectories (N=2 optimal), all supervised during training, and the future ego feature is initialized from anchored trajectory endpoints rather than learned from scratch.

## Method

SeerDrive introduces a bidirectional closed-loop between a BEV world model (Transformer encoder) and an end-to-end planning network. The world model takes the current BEV feature (flattened and repeated across the modality dimension) concatenated with the ego feature to predict a future BEV representation. The planning network uses a decoupled strategy: the current ego feature interacts with the current BEV via a Transformer decoder to produce trajectory T_a, while a future ego feature (initialized from anchored endpoints) interacts with the future BEV to produce T_b; these are fused via Motion-aware Layer Normalization (MLN) to yield the final trajectory T_final. The two networks then iterate N times, with the refined ego feature fed back into the world model to update the future BEV prediction, enabling progressive refinement. Training is fully end-to-end with a combined loss over BEV semantic map accuracy and trajectory quality across all N iterations, using TransFuser for multi-modal BEV encoding and ResNet34/50 backbones.

## Results

On the NAVSIM navtest split (ResNet34 backbone), SeerDrive achieves a PDM Score of 88.9, outperforming the prior best method Hydra-NeXt (88.6) and DiffusionDrive (88.1). With a stronger V2-99 backbone, SeerDrive reaches 90.7 PDMS, surpassing GoalFlow (90.3). On nuScenes, SeerDrive attains 0.43 m average L2 displacement error and 0.06% collision rate, improving over SparseDrive (0.61 m, 0.08%) and BridgeAD (0.59 m, 0.09%). On Bench2Drive, SeerDrive achieves a driving score of 58.32 and 30.17% success rate, substantially ahead of BridgeAD (50.06, 22.73%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SeerDrive outperforms all 19 baselines across all three benchmarks. On NAVSIM with ResNet34, it leads Hydra-NeXt by 0.3 PDMS (88.9 vs. 88.6), WoTE by 0.6 (88.9 vs. 88.3), and DiffusionDrive by 0.8 (88.9 vs. 88.1), with the largest gap over VADv2 at 8.0 points. With the V2-99 backbone, SeerDrive (90.7) surpasses GoalFlow (90.3) by 0.4 PDMS while using significantly lower computational cost. On nuScenes, SeerDrive reduces average L2 error to 0.43 m (vs. 0.61 m for SparseDrive, 0.59 m for BridgeAD, 0.69 m for UniAD) and collision rate to 0.06% (vs. 0.08–0.12% for all competitors). On Bench2Drive, SeerDrive achieves a driving score of 58.32 and 30.17% success rate, exceeding BridgeAD (50.06, 22.73%) by 8.3 points and 7.4 percentage points respectively, and outperforming MomAD (47.91, 18.11%) and UniAD-Base (45.81, 16.46%) by even wider margins.

## Relation to Prior Work

SeerDrive sits at the intersection of end-to-end autonomous driving (UniAD, VAD, TransFuser, DiffusionDrive) and world-model-based scene prediction (Drive-Dreamer, GAIA-1, SLEDGE, OccWorld). It departs from the dominant one-shot paradigm—where current sensor observations are mapped directly to future trajectories—by introducing an explicit bidirectional loop: a BEV world model predicts future scene representations that inform the planner, and the planner's refined ego features feed back to update the scene prediction. Unlike prior work that uses world models as auxiliary training signals (LAW, SSR) or for post-hoc trajectory selection (WoTE), SeerDrive integrates future scene features at the feature level into the planning network and iterates this coupling N times, making the interaction a core architectural component rather than a training trick.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper validates that explicitly modeling the ego-vehicle-to-scene feedback loop yields consistent gains across NAVSIM, nuScenes, and Bench2Drive, suggesting that the one-shot assumption in most end-to-end planners leaves performance on the table in dynamic, interactive scenarios. However, the BEV world model is a lightweight task-specific transformer (66 M params total, 24 ms inference) rather than a general-purpose foundation model, so it does not inherit the broad generalization of video-generation world models like Vista or GAIA-1. The authors themselves flag the open question of how to tightly integrate large, off-the-shelf foundation models as world models while preserving joint differentiability with the planner—a direction that could substantially widen the applicability of this bidirectional paradigm.
