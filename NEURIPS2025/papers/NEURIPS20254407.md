## Problem

AdamW is the default optimizer for training large language models, yet its convergence behavior remains theoretically uncharacterized. While Xie and Li (2024) showed that AdamW's limit point satisfies KKT conditions of an ℓ∞-constrained problem, the fundamental question of whether and how fast AdamW converges was essentially open—the only prior convergence result (Zhou et al., 2024) required exponentially decaying weight decay, reducing AdamW to Adam in the limit. This paper establishes the first non-trivial convergence rate for AdamW with a fixed, non-vanishing weight decay parameter.

## Input / Output

The analysis takes a nonconvex L-Lipschitz smooth objective f: ℝ^d → ℝ, unbiased stochastic gradient estimators with coordinate-wise bounded noise variance σ_i² (no bounded gradient assumption), and a specific set of hyperparameter constraints: 1−θ = √(L(f(x¹)−f*)/(Kσ̂_s²)), η = √((f(x¹)−f*)/(4KdL)), ε = σ̂_s²/d, λ ≤ (√d/(√72·K^{3/4}))·(L³/(σ̂_s²(f(x¹)−f*)))^{1/4}, and ‖x¹‖∞ ≤ √(K(f(x¹)−f*)/(dL)). The output is the rate (1/K)Σ E[‖∇f(x^k)‖₁] ≤ O(√d/K^{1/4} · (σ̂_s²L(f(x¹)−f*))^{1/4} + √(dL(f(x¹)−f*)/K)), along with the guarantee that ‖x^k‖∞ < 1/λ for all iterates, confirming the ℓ∞ constraint is never violated.

## Method

The proof decomposes the expected function decrease into a negative gradient term, a momentum-update term, a gradient-momentum discrepancy term, and a weight-decay term ηΣ|λx_i^k|²√(v_i^k+ε). The central technical difficulty is bounding the weight-decay term: unlike SignSGD-type methods with fixed update size, AdamW's updates can be arbitrarily large, so the authors must control the decay of (1−ηλ)^k by setting ηλ ≤ √ν/(2K^{5/4}) and initializing ‖x¹‖∞ ≤ √ν/(K^{1/4}λ), yielding ‖x^k‖∞ ≤ 3√ν/(K^{1/4}λ) = O(1/(K^{1/4}λ)). The weight-decay term is then absorbed into the gradient term via concavity of √x and −1/√x, combined with a momentum recursion (Lemma 4) and a key summation bound (Lemma 5) on Σ√(ṽ_i^k+ε). Finally, setting ε = σ̂_s²/d eliminates ε from the rate, and Hölder's inequality converts the coordinate-wise bound into the ℓ₁-norm convergence rate. The same framework extends to Adam (λ=0) under relaxed parameter constraints (0 ≤ β ≤ 1 instead of θ ≤ β ≤ √θ).

## Results

The paper establishes that AdamW achieves a convergence rate of (1/K)Σ E[‖∇f(x^k)‖₁] ≤ O(√d/K^{1/4} · ⁴√(σ²_s · L · (f(x¹)−f*))) under standard smoothness and bounded noise variance assumptions, matching SGD's optimal K^{-1/4} rate up to a √d factor. Empirically, ‖∇f(x)‖₁ = Θ(√d)‖∇f(x)‖₂ is confirmed on ResNet-50 (CIFAR-100, ImageNet) and GPT-2 (OpenWebText), with √d values of 4868, 5060, and 11136 respectively. When the noise variance is small (σ²_s ≤ L(f(x¹)−f*)/(Kγ²)), the rate improves to O(√(dL(f(x¹)−f*)/(Kγ))), matching gradient descent under ℓ₁ measurement. The convergence rate does not depend explicitly on ε, unlike prior Adam/AdamW analyses.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to SGD's optimal ℓ₂ rate O(⁴√(σ²_s L Δ)/K^{1/4}), this paper's AdamW rate carries an additional √d factor, which is empirically justified since ‖∇f(x)‖₁ ≈ √d · ‖∇f(x)‖₂ in practice. Against Zhou et al. [7], the only prior AdamW convergence result, this paper requires weaker assumptions (no bounded gradient, no per-sample smoothness), uses a fixed (non-decaying) weight decay λ, and eliminates explicit ε dependence. Compared to Li et al. [10] (RMSProp), the same O(√d/K^{1/4}) rate is achieved but with a different proof framework; the trade-off is a larger ε setting (σ²_s/d vs. σ²_s/(dK)). Against Li et al. [37] (Adam with high-probability bounds), this paper's rate is (G/ε̃)^{5/8} times faster and requires ε = σ²_s/d rather than ε̃² ≥ G², which is d times smaller in practice. The paper also conjectures that the √d factor is tight, citing a recent lower bound for SGD under ℓ₁ measurement [11] that matches the derived rate when ‖σ‖₁ ≈ √d · σ_s.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the ℓ₁-norm convergence rate framework established for RMSProp by Li et al. (JMLR 2025) to AdamW and Adam, using a different proof architecture that handles the coupled momentum and adaptive variance updates. It builds directly on Xie and Li's (ICML 2024) KKT characterization of AdamW's implicit ℓ∞-norm constraint, which motivates ℓ₁ gradient norm as the natural convergence metric. The analysis departs from prior Adam convergence work (Reddi et al., Li et al., Hong and Lin) by avoiding bounded-gradient assumptions and eliminating explicit ε-dependence from the final rate, though at the cost of requiring non-standard parameter settings (θ ≤ β ≤ √θ, very small η).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AdamW is the de facto optimizer for large language model training, yet this is essentially the first convergence rate guarantee for it under standard assumptions, filling a notable gap in the optimization theory literature. The result that the rate matches SGD's optimal K^{-1/4} dependence (up to a √d factor empirically confirmed on ResNet-50 and GPT-2) provides theoretical justification for AdamW's practical success. Key open questions remain: whether the √d factor is truly tight, whether the impractical parameter constraints (θ, β, η, λ) can be relaxed to cover standard settings like (0.9, 0.999), and whether the upper bound on λ is necessary. The paper itself acknowledges that prior work shows Adam with common hyperparameters can fail to converge, leaving a gap between theory and practice.
