## Problem

Graph neural networks suffer from degraded information propagation over long distances due to over-smoothing, over-squashing, and vanishing gradients, limiting their ability to capture long-range dependencies. Existing remedies such as graph transformers incur quadratic complexity, while diffusion-based DE-GNNs are inherently dissipative and lose signal fidelity. The paper asks whether a non-dissipative, theoretically grounded propagation mechanism can maintain information integrity across arbitrary graph distances while retaining MPNN-like linear complexity.

## Input / Output

SONAR consumes node feature vectors, the graph adjacency matrix, and optionally edge features, which are injected into the node space via Avg and Grad operators (rather than standard message-passing aggregation) to preserve wave-like propagation properties. The output is a set of node embeddings after L propagation steps per block, usable for node classification, graph-level regression/classification, or sensitivity analysis. Each propagation step corresponds to one discretized time step of the wave equation, so the number of steps directly controls the effective receptive field in hops.

## Method

SONAR formulates node-state evolution as the graph wave equation Ẍ(t) = −L^a X(t)W, where L^a is a weighted graph Laplacian whose edge weights a_uv are learned adaptively via MLPs (with ReLU to enforce positivity), acting as inverse resistances that modulate per-edge signal flow. To balance the purely conservative dynamics with task-relevant flexibility, the model adds a state-dependent dissipative term D(X(t))⊙Ẋ(t) and an external forcing term F(X(t)), both parameterized by neural networks. The continuous system is discretized with a finite-difference scheme into L GNN layers, and multiple SONAR blocks are stacked with intervening MLPs to introduce nonlinearity. Theoretically, the conservative form provably conserves total energy (kinetic + potential) and yields a sensitivity matrix that oscillates as cos(t√L) without ever vanishing, guaranteeing non-zero influence between any pair of nodes at any distance.

## Results

On the Graph Property Prediction benchmark, SONAR achieves log₁₀(MSE) of −3.29 (Diameter), −6.75 (SSSP), and −3.12 (Eccentricity), outperforming the best baseline (DRew-GCN+delay) by 0.89, 2.46, and 1.01 points respectively — a margin of at least one order of magnitude. On the real-world Long Range Graph Benchmark, SONAR+LapPE reaches 70.47 AP on Peptides-func, 0.2486 MAE on Peptides-struct, and 0.4082 F1 on PascalVOC-SP (surpassing the best transformer by 8.3% F1). On heterophilic node classification, SONAR attains 89.82% accuracy on Roman-empire and 96.29% AUC on Minesweeper, the best scores among all 30 compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SONAR decisively outperforms all MPNN baselines (GCN, GAT, GraphSAGE, GIN, GCNII, AMP) on every task by large margins, confirming that standard message passing cannot capture shortest-path-based graph properties. Among DE-GNNs, SONAR surpasses PH-DGN (the strongest in this class) by 2.70 points on SSSP and 2.18 on Diameter, and beats GraphCON by 3.39 on SSSP. Against multi-hop GNNs, SONAR outperforms DRew-GCN+delay by 0.89–2.46 points on all three graph property tasks, while DRew retains a slight edge on Peptides-func (71.50 vs. 70.47 AP). On the PascalVOC-SP node classification task, SONAR+LapPE (0.4082 F1) surpasses the best transformer (GraphGPS+LapPE, 0.3748 F1) by 8.3% while maintaining linear complexity. On heterophilic benchmarks, SONAR wins on Roman-empire (89.82 vs. 89.03 Exphormer) and Minesweeper (96.29 vs. 93.51 SAGE) but trails FSGNN on Questions (74.96 vs. 78.86 AUC) and is within ~1 point of top models on Amazon-ratings and Tolokers.

## Relation to Prior Work

SONAR builds directly on the mathematical formulation of wave equations on graphs (Friedman & Tillich, 2004) and graph calculus operators, positioning itself within the DE-GNN family alongside GRAND, DGC, GraphCON, A-DGN, SWAN, and PH-DGN. Its key conceptual departure is from diffusion-based DE-GNNs (which are inherently dissipative) to wave-based dynamics that conserve energy, drawing an analogy to the SOFAR channel in ocean acoustics. Unlike GraphCON, which couples simple oscillators through a GCN/GAT, SONAR uses a mathematically grounded weighted Laplacian wave equation with adaptive edge resistances and state-dependent forcing terms, yielding inherently linear propagation dynamics with stronger theoretical guarantees.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SONAR provides a principled, linear-complexity alternative to quadratic-complexity graph transformers for long-range propagation, with rigorous energy-conservation and non-vanishing-sensitivity guarantees that are absent in standard MPNNs. The practical impact is significant: a simple wave-equation discretization outperforms much more complex architectures (transformers, multi-hop GNNs, graph rewiring) on both synthetic and real-world long-range benchmarks. Open questions include whether the finite-difference discretization is optimal (the authors suggest adaptive multistep schemes), how the method extends to temporal/dynamic graphs, and whether the adaptive resistance and forcing terms are truly necessary or whether the pure conservative form suffices in practice.
