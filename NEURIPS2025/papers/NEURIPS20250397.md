## Problem

Feature unlearning—removing the influence of specific features from a trained model without retraining from scratch—remains underexplored compared to instance-level unlearning. Existing feature unlearning methods (FOUL, MIUL) degrade in performance as the number of removed features grows and lack theoretical guarantees that the unlearned feature's influence is fully eliminated while preserving the remaining features' predictive relationships.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250397/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a fully trained model f_θ, the original training dataset D̂ (with 80:20 train-test split, standardized numerical features, one-hot encoded categoricals), and the index j identifying the target feature(s) to unlearn. For tabular data, the j-th column is directly permuted; for image data (CelebA), specific visual regions (nose, eyes) are shuffled within the image while preserving overall structure. The output is an updated model f_θ' whose parameters no longer encode information about the unlearned feature, producing class predictions equivalent to a model retrained from scratch on the feature-reduced dataset.

## Method

The approach consists of two steps: (1) draw a uniform random permutation π of the training indices and replace each x_{i,j} with x_{π(i),j} while leaving all other features and labels unchanged, and (2) fine-tune the original model on this shuffled dataset using the same loss function and optimizer. The key theoretical insight is that this shuffling makes the unlearned feature X_j^π almost surely independent of both the label Y and the remaining features X_{-j} as n→∞, proven via Birkhoff's ergodic theorem, McDiarmid's inequality, and the Borel-Cantelli lemma. This independence guarantees (ε-δ)-closeness between the unlearned model f_θ' and a model g_φ retrained from scratch on the feature-reduced dataset, with the gap bounded by 6ε_n + 2α under uniform convergence. The method is further validated through vanishing mutual information and preserved Shapley values for retained features, and empirically achieves TRI ≈ 1 within ~200 epochs (roughly 10× faster than full retraining) across MLP, ResNet, FT-Transformer, and ViT architectures on both tabular and image datasets.

## Results

On the Magic tabular dataset, the proposed shuffling method achieves TRI values close to 1 (matching retrain-from-scratch accuracy) within ~200 unlearning epochs, while being approximately 10× faster than full retraining (high EI). For the CelebA image task, the method attains an average TRI of 97.31% after 20 epochs and 99.12% after 100 epochs. In terms of privacy, the averaged feature-level MIA accuracy reaches 0.496 at 50 epochs (optimal: 0.5), compared to 0.603–0.763 for FOUL and 0.594–0.651 for MIUL, indicating the unlearned model is nearly indistinguishable from a retrained-from-scratch model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two feature-unlearning baselines: FOUL (Warnecke et al., 2021) and MIUL (Guo et al., 2022). On MIA accuracy (where 0.5 is optimal), the proposed method reaches 0.496 at 50 epochs and stays within 0.509–0.544 across all epochs, while FOUL degrades from 0.574 to 0.763 as unlearning epochs increase, and MIUL plateaus around 0.594–0.651. On TRI (test retention index, where 1.0 is ideal), the proposed method consistently achieves values near 1 across all six tabular datasets and three neural architectures (MLP, ResNet, FT-Transformer), whereas FOUL exhibits accuracy degradation and MIUL maintains a fixed accuracy level regardless of unlearning effort. The proposed method also achieves the highest RASI (robustness against shuffling) and lowest SRI/SDI (SHAP-based importance), confirming more complete feature removal. In terms of efficiency, the proposed method requires only ~200 unlearning epochs versus 2000 for retraining from scratch (~10× speedup), while MIUL additionally requires training three extra neural networks for mutual information estimation, making it the most computationally expensive baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground in feature unlearning by proposing a fundamentally different mechanism—random permutation of the target feature followed by fine-tuning—rather than the gradient-based weight updates (FOUL [32]) or mutual-information-based detachment (MIUL [15]) used by prior methods. While [32] first formalized the problem and [15, 26] extended it, none provided theoretical guarantees that the unlearned model is (ε-δ)-close to a model retrained from scratch without the target feature. The paper's theoretical contribution draws on ergodic theory (Birkhoff's theorem) and concentration inequalities (McDiarmid's) to prove that shuffling renders the target feature asymptotically independent of both the label and remaining features, a guarantee absent from all prior feature-unlearning work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first feature-unlearning method with a formal (ε-δ) closeness guarantee to the retrain-from-scratch gold standard, and it achieves this with roughly 10× less compute than full retraining while handling multi-feature unlearning without the performance degradation that plagues prior approaches. The practical simplicity (shuffle one column, fine-tune) makes it immediately deployable in regulated settings (GDPR, HIPAA) where feature-level deletion requests arise. Key limitations include the requirement for access to the original training data and the stationarity/ergodicity assumption on the data distribution; open questions include whether the approach extends to online/streaming unlearning, to features embedded in continuous representations (e.g., within a pretrained encoder), and to settings where the target feature is only partially correlated with the label through complex nonlinear pathways.
