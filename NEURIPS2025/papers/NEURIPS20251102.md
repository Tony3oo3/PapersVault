## Problem

Optimizing a known functional of an unknown operator's output (e.g., finding the permeability design that minimizes fluid flow through a porous medium) requires expensive oracle queries to the operator, while evaluating the functional on the output is cheap. Existing Bayesian optimization and Thompson sampling methods either operate in finite-dimensional input spaces or do not exploit the compositional structure of operator-then-functional, leaving the infinite-dimensional function-space setting largely unaddressed.

## Input / Output

The method takes a function-valued input a (e.g., a binary permeability mask on a 16×16 grid for Darcy flow, or a 3-component initial condition on a 32×64 spherical grid for shallow water), a known and cheap-to-evaluate functional f: U→R, and sequential access to an expensive oracle G* (a PDE solver or physical experiment). It produces the optimal input function a* maximizing f(G*(a)) over a finite search space S⊂A, along with the optimal functional value. Observations are discretized on finite grids, reducing the infinite-dimensional output to a finite-dimensional vector y∈R^m at each query.

## Method

NOTS (Neural Operator Thompson Sampling) uses a neural operator (FNO or spherical FNO) as a surrogate for the unknown operator, retrained from a random Gaussian initialization at every iteration via regularized least-squares gradient descent on all accumulated data. The key theoretical insight is that in the infinite-width limit, a randomly initialized neural operator converges in distribution to a vector-valued Gaussian process whose covariance is an operator-valued kernel derived from the conjugate (NNGP) kernel, and training only the last linear layer via gradient descent yields an approximate GP posterior sample. This enables a sample-then-optimize Thompson sampling loop: sample a posterior realization by retraining, then maximize the known functional f over the surrogate's predictions to select the next query, avoiding expensive uncertainty quantification schemes like deep ensembles. The paper proves sublinear Bayesian cumulative regret (Õ(√T)) by extending GP-TS bounds to the operator setting, and achieves O(t) per-iteration cost versus O(t³) for traditional GP-based approaches.

## Results

NOTS achieves the lowest final average regret (R_T/T) across all four PDE optimization tasks. On Darcy flow rate optimization, NOTS reaches 0.012±0.001, outperforming the next-best baseline STO-NTS (0.068) by a factor of ~5.7×. On the shallow water inverse problem (6144-dimensional inputs), NOTS achieves 0.134±0.043 versus 1.639±0.532 for the best GP-based baseline (BO), a 12× improvement. On Darcy flow pressure and energy, NOTS also leads with 0.012 and 0.125 respectively, beating GP-TS (0.038 and 0.189).

## Baselines & Comparisons

NOTS outperforms all four baselines on every task. Against BO (infinite-width ReLU BNN with log-expected improvement, Li et al. 2024), NOTS reduces regret by 58× on Darcy flow rates (0.012 vs 0.703) and 12× on shallow water (0.134 vs 1.639). Against GP-TS (Thompson sampling with the same BNN kernel, Russo 2016), NOTS achieves 3.2× lower regret on Darcy flow pressure (0.012 vs 0.038) and 1.5× on Darcy flow energy (0.125 vs 0.189). Against BFO (RKHS-encoded Bayesian functional optimization, Vien 2018), NOTS is 66× better on Darcy flow rates and 23× on shallow water; BFO also crashes before 250 iterations on the 6144-dim shallow water problem due to kernel evaluation cost. Against STO-NTS (2-layer 256-width FC network, Dai et al. 2022), NOTS is 5.7× better on Darcy flow rates (0.012 vs 0.068) and 17× on shallow water (0.134 vs 2.329), with the gap attributed to STO-NTS's architectural inability to exploit the function-valued structure of the inputs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends composite Bayesian optimization (Astudillo & Frazier 2019; Guilhoto & Perdikaris 2024), which optimizes known functionals of operator outputs, from finite-dimensional inputs to full function spaces where both inputs and outputs are functions. It draws on the infinite-width limit theory connecting neural networks to Gaussian processes (Lee et al. 2018; Neal 1996) and Neural Thompson Sampling (Zhang et al. 2021; Dai et al. 2022), but generalizes these results to the operator-valued, vector-valued GP setting (Jorgensen & Tian 2024). The key departure from prior active learning work on neural operators (Pickering et al. 2022; Li et al. 2024) is the shift from uncertainty reduction to direct optimization of problem-dependent functionals, and the use of randomized retraining as a free posterior sampling mechanism rather than ensembles or mixture density networks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a scalable, theoretically grounded alternative to GP-based Bayesian optimization for high-dimensional function-space problems (e.g., PDE-based design and inverse problems) where traditional multi-output GPs become computationally intractable (cubic scaling in both data points and output dimensions). The regret bound (Proposition 2) connects the neural operator surrogate to classical GP-TS guarantees, giving practitioners confidence in the method's asymptotic behavior. Key limitations include the restriction to finite search spaces, single-hidden-layer theory (despite multi-layer experiments), and well-specified models; open questions include extensions to continuous input domains, batched evaluations, and multi-layer convergence analysis. The framework also naturally supports task-to-task amortization, where operator learning data can be reused across different objective functionals.
