## Problem

Second-order stochastic optimization methods are extremely sensitive to noise, and all existing SOSO algorithms require bounded noise or bounded variance assumptions that are rarely satisfied in modern learning tasks. No prior work established fundamental sample complexity limits or provided working algorithms for second-order methods under heavy-tailed noise with unbounded variance (p-BCM, p ∈ (1,2]). This paper fills this gap by deriving tight minimax lower bounds for any second-order method under p-BCM and constructing algorithms that provably match them.

## Input / Output

The method operates on a smooth (L-smooth) but potentially nonconvex objective F(x) = E[f(x,ξ)], accessing only stochastic gradient and Hessian-vector product oracles whose estimation errors satisfy p-th central moment bounds for p ∈ (1,2]. The in-expectation version requires O(Δ(L+σ_h)/ε² · (σ/ε)^(1/(p-1))) oracle calls, while the high-probability clipped variant incurs only a polylogarithmic overhead in the failure probability δ. Notably, the method does not require second-order smoothness (L_h can be infinite), operating in the regime L ≤ σ_h where the Hessian noise dominates the gradient Lipschitz constant.

## Method

The core algorithm (NSGDHess) constructs a Hessian-corrected recursive momentum by evaluating a stochastic Hessian-vector product at a random interpolation point x̂_t uniformly sampled between consecutive iterates, then normalizes the resulting gradient step to maintain unit-length updates. The key innovation for robustness is Hessian clipping: rather than clipping the full Hessian matrix (costing O(d²) via power iteration), the algorithm clips the Hessian-vector product (costing only O(d)), which preserves the crucial identity E[∇²f(x̂_t,ξ̂_t)(x_t − x_{t−1})] = ∇F(x_t) − ∇F(x_{t−1}) in expectation. This clipped variant, combined with gradient clipping, yields the first high-probability convergence guarantees for second-order stochastic optimization under heavy-tailed noise. The matching lower bound is established via a zero-chain function construction showing that any q-th order zero-respecting algorithm requires Ω(Δσ_h/ε² · (σ/ε)^(1/(p−1))) queries, improving over first-order bounds by a factor of 1/ε.

## Results

The paper establishes a minimax lower bound of Ω(Δσ_h/ε² · (σ/ε)^(1/(p−1))) for any second-order stochastic optimization (SOSO) algorithm under p-BCM noise (p ∈ (1,2]), improving over the best first-order bound by a factor of 1/ε. The proposed NSGDHess algorithm (Theorem 2) matches this lower bound in the regime L ≤ σ_h, achieving sample complexity O(Δ(L+σ_h)/ε² · (σ/ε)^(1/(p−1))) in expectation. The clipped variant (Theorem 3, Corollary 1) extends this to high-probability guarantees with only a polylogarithmic overhead in the failure probability δ, yielding O(((√(Δ₁(L+σ_h)) + σ)/ε · log(1/(δε)))^((2p−1)/(p−1))) oracle calls. In the classical bounded-variance case (p=2), the method recovers the known O(ε^(−3)) rate while removing the need for second-order smoothness (L_h).

## Baselines & Comparisons

All prior SOSO methods (SCN, SGD with HVP-RVR, N-SGDHess, SCN with IT-HB) require either bounded noise (p=∞) or bounded variance (p=2), and none operate under the p-BCM model with p < 2 that captures heavy-tailed noise. The proposed NSGDHess is the first second-order method to provably work under p-BCM for all p ∈ (1,2], achieving a leading ε-dependence of ε^(−2) (vs. ε^(−3) for prior methods in the second-order regime) and removing dependence on the Hessian Lipschitz constant L_h. Compared to N-SGDHess [Tran & Cutkosky, 2021], the new method eliminates the σ³/ε³ term and the √(L_h) dependence, while also extending from p=2 to p ∈ (1,2]. The clipped variant (Algorithm 2) is the first SOSO method to provide high-probability guarantees under heavy-tailed noise, with only logarithmic dependence on 1/δ, whereas prior methods offer no high-probability analysis at all. In the empirical illustration (Figure 2, d=10 quadratic with Pareto noise, p=1.1), unclipped NSGDM and NSGDHess suffer significant instability, while the clipped variant maintains stable convergence, confirming the theoretical motivation for Hessian clipping.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Hessian-corrected momentum framework (SGDHess from Tran and Cutkosky, 2021; SHARP/(N)HAR-PG from Salehkaleybar et al., 2022 and Fatkhullin et al., 2023) from the bounded-variance regime to the much more general p-BCM noise model (p ∈ (1,2]), where only p-th central moments of stochastic gradients and Hessians are bounded. For lower bounds, it adapts the zero-chain function construction from Arjevani et al. (2020) and Carmon et al. (2020) to the p-BCM setting, generalizing the first-order heavy-tailed lower bounds of Zhang et al. (2020). The key algorithmic departure is the introduction of Hessian-vector-product clipping (as opposed to full Hessian matrix clipping) to obtain high-probability guarantees, building on the gradient-clipping techniques of Cutkosky and Mehta (2021) and Sadiev et al. (2023) but extending them to the second-order setting for the first time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to provide a complete sample complexity characterization (matching lower and upper bounds up to constants in the leading term) for second-order stochastic optimization under heavy-tailed noise, filling a gap the authors explicitly identify: no prior SOSO method had guarantees beyond bounded variance. The result that second-order methods provably outperform first-order by a factor of 1/ε even under p-BCM noise is a clean theoretical advance. However, significant open questions remain: the high-probability upper bound does not exactly match the lower bound in all parameters, the method requires tuning three additional hyperparameters (momentum, gradient clipping level, Hessian clipping level), and practical deployment is far from demonstrated (only a 10-dimensional quadratic toy experiment is provided). Whether Hessian clipping can be replaced by normalization (as recent first-order work suggests) and whether the method scales to real deep learning problems are important future directions.
