## Problem

Novel view synthesis from sparse, in-the-wild photo collections with multiple appearances (different times of day, seasons, weather) remains poorly addressed: existing 3DGS and NeRF adaptations oversmooth, overfit to limited training views, and produce severe artifacts because the standard sparse SfM point cloud initialization provides insufficient geometric support. The combination of limited viewpoints and photometric inconsistency is underexplored, and prior methods require abundant imagery to disambiguate image-specific radiance from canonical scene appearance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252058/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 20 sparse RGB images (5 per appearance in the drone dataset), SfM camera poses and point cloud, monocular depth estimates from a pre-trained diffusion-based model, and SAM segmentation masks prompted by projected SfM points. It produces a 3DGS scene rendered at 300+ FPS and a dense back-projected point cloud used as initialization. Notably, in the in-the-wild evaluation protocol, train and test views are registered separately via coordinate alignment, and test-view appearance is determined by camera metadata/timestamp rather than test-image pixels, ensuring proper train-test separation.

## Method

MS-GS has two core components. First, Semantic Depth Alignment uses an iterative SAM-prompted procedure anchored by SfM points to partition each image into semantically consistent regions, then solves a per-region least-squares scale-and-shift alignment between monocular and SfM depth before back-projecting pixels into a dense, structured point cloud that regularizes 3DGS densification. Second, Multi-view Geometry-guided Supervision creates virtual cameras by interpolating between training views (linear translation, SLERP rotation, interpolated FOV), back-projects training-image pixels to 3D using rendered depth, and forward-projects them onto virtual views to compute occlusion-masked pixel losses and VGG-16 feature losses, enforcing 3D consistency along newly created rays. Appearance is modeled by a 3-layer MLP that fuses a 32-dim per-image embedding (near-zero initialized to delay view-specific bias) with a 16-dim per-Gaussian feature embedding to decode RGB colors, enabling the model to separate global appearance variation from canonical scene appearance under sparse observations.

## Results

On the introduced Sparse Unbounded Drone dataset (20 views, 4 appearances), MS-GS achieves 19.87 dB PSNR, 0.580 SSIM, 0.322 LPIPS, and 0.096 DSIM under GT poses, outperforming the best prior method (GS-W) by +2.54 dB PSNR, +0.089 SSIM, and reducing LPIPS/DSIM by 33.8%/65.6%. On Sparse Mip-NeRF 360 (single appearance, 20 views), MS-GS reaches 22.39 dB PSNR and 0.702 SSIM, surpassing SPARS3R by +0.91 dB. On Sparse Phototourism (multi-appearance, 20 views), MS-GS attains 18.99 dB PSNR and 0.269 LPIPS, beating CR-NeRF by +2.01 dB PSNR. MS-GS also renders at 373 FPS with only 0.29 GPU hours of training, versus 63 FPS and 2.75 GPU hours for GS-W.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MS-GS outperforms all 12 baselines across all three datasets and both evaluation settings (GT pose and in-the-wild). Against the strongest 3DGS sparse-view baselines on Mip-NeRF 360, MS-GS beats SPARS3R by +0.91 dB PSNR (22.39 vs 21.48) and FSGS by +0.72 dB, while DRGS and DNGS trail by 3.23 and 2.60 dB respectively due to overly smooth renderings from suboptimal depth regularization. On the multi-appearance Unbounded Drone dataset, MS-GS surpasses GS-W (the best prior 3DGS in-the-wild method) by +2.54 dB PSNR and reduces DSIM from 0.279 to 0.096; Wild-GS and WildGaussians degrade severely (14.13 and 15.60 dB PSNR) due to overfitting from their heavier appearance networks under sparse views. On Phototourism, MS-GS leads CR-NeRF by +2.01 dB PSNR and GS-W by +2.25 dB, with the largest gap over WildGaussians at +4.66 dB. In the in-the-wild setting (with registration noise), MS-GS maintains its lead, achieving 0.331 LPIPS and 0.105 DSIM on the drone dataset versus 0.446/0.213 for GS-W. MS-GS is also the fastest method, rendering at 373 FPS and training in 0.29 GPU hours—roughly 9–40× faster than NeRF-based baselines and 3× faster than the next-best 3DGS method.

## Relation to Prior Work

MS-GS builds directly on the 3D Gaussian Splatting framework and extends it along two axes that prior work has addressed separately: sparse-view synthesis (via depth regularization in DNGS, SparseGS, FSGS) and multi-appearance modeling (via NeRF-W, GS-W, Wild-GS, WildGaussians). Its key departure is replacing the standard sparse SfM point cloud initialization with a semantic depth alignment pipeline that leverages monocular depth estimates and SAM-based segmentation to produce a denser, locally consistent point cloud, and introducing virtual-view geometry-guided supervision (pixel warping + VGG feature loss) as a self-regularization mechanism that neither prior sparse-view nor multi-appearance methods employ. The paper also positions itself against DUSt3R-based initialization (SPARS3R), arguing that monocular depth is sharper and scales as O(N) versus O(N²) for pairwise pointmap estimation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a realistic and underexplored combination—sparse views with varying appearances—that naturally arises in in-the-wild photo collections, and provides both a practical method and a new evaluation protocol (coordinate alignment disentangling train/test registration) that the field has largely ignored. The semantic depth alignment is demonstrated as a drop-in improvement for other 3DGS-based methods (Table 8), suggesting broad applicability beyond MS-GS itself. Open questions include handling transient objects under sparse views, generalizing to non-Lambertian surfaces where multi-view consistency smooths specular highlights, and whether the virtual-view supervision strategy transfers to other explicit 3D representations. The 300+ FPS rendering at 3× less GPU time than prior multi-appearance methods makes the approach practically deployable.
