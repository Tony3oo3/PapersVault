## Problem

Existing Video Step Grounding (VSG) methods require labeled training sets and process the full video offline, making them costly to deploy and unsuitable for real-time streaming applications such as AR/XR procedural guidance. This paper asks whether the zero-shot capabilities of Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) can address VSG online and without any task-specific training, and how past temporal context can be injected into per-segment predictions to improve accuracy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253320/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a video stream as a sequence of non-overlapping 2-second segments arriving one at a time, along with a natural-language task goal and a list of K procedural step descriptions. For each incoming segment, it produces a probability distribution over the K steps plus a 'none' class (a (K+1)-dimensional simplex). The per-segment belief distributions can be post-processed with 1D blob detection to extract step start/end timestamps. The LLM-generated dependency matrix is computed once per task and reused across all videos of that task.

## Method

BAGLM frames online VSG as a Bayesian filtering problem where the hidden state is the current step and the observation is the video segment. The PREDICT step computes a prior over steps using a transition matrix initialized from an LLM-estimated prerequisite dependency matrix, then dynamically updated each segment via two scores: step readiness (weighted max progress of prerequisites, computed by querying the LMM on a 0–9 progress scale) and step validity (penalizing steps whose successors are already complete). The UPDATE step multiplies this prior by the LMM's direct multi-choice prediction for the current segment (treated as the observation likelihood under a uniform state prior). The entire pipeline is training-free: InternVL2.5-8B serves as the LMM for per-segment step scoring and progress estimation, while LLaMA3-70B-Instruct generates the dependency matrix via targeted prerequisite queries.

## Results

BAGLM achieves 57.4 R@1 on HT-Step, 59.8 Avg.R@1 on CrossTask, and 43.3 R@1 on Ego4D Goal-Step, all in an online (streaming) setting without any task-specific training. These results outperform the best offline training-based methods (NaSVA: 53.1, 46.7, 29.1) by 4.3%, 13.1%, and 14.2% respectively. On the COIN dataset, BAGLM further reaches 51.0 R@1, surpassing offline NaSVA (36.8) by 14.2 points. The method uses 2-second video segments processed sequentially, with the Bayesian filtering step adding negligible computational overhead over the base LMM inference.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BAGLM outperforms all 17 compared baselines across all three primary datasets. Against the strongest offline competitor NaSVA [14], BAGLM gains +4.3% on HT-Step (57.4 vs 53.1), +13.1% on CrossTask (59.8 vs 46.7), and +14.2% on Ego4D Goal-Step (43.3 vs 29.1); when NaSVA is forced into the same online setting, its performance drops further to 46.1 and 24.2, widening the gap to −11.3% and −19.1%. On CrossTask, BAGLM surpasses MPTVA [4] (47.9) by 11.9 points and VINA [18] (44.8) by 15.0 points, despite MPTVA and VideoCLIP/UniVL being fine-tuned on CrossTask-specific data. On Ego4D Goal-Step, BAGLM beats VSLNet [34] (24.3, trained on the same domain) by 19.0 points. The older training-based baselines (Zhukov et al. [36], HT100M [20], VideoCLIP [32], MCN [3], DWSA [25], MIL-NCE [19], VT-TWINS [11], UniVL [17], TAN* [9]) are all outperformed by 15–37 points on CrossTask. Among zero-shot LMM baselines evaluated in Figure 2, InternVL2.5-8B [6] is the strongest single model, and BAGLM consistently adds 1.5–2.3% over it depending on segment duration, confirming the Bayesian filtering component's contribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the established line of training-based, offline VSG methods (NaSVA, VINA, MPTVA) that require labeled narrations or pseudo-labels and full-video access. It repurposes the Bayesian filtering framework (Chen, 2003) as a principled mechanism to inject temporal context into zero-shot LMM predictions, replacing the learned transition models of prior work with LLM-derived step dependency matrices and LMM-estimated progress scores. The conceptual lineage is: weakly-supervised VSG → LLM-augmented VSG → training-free online VSG via LMMs + probabilistic filtering, with the key departure being the elimination of both training data and offline video access.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that zero-shot LMMs can match or exceed training-based SOTA on VSG challenges the assumption that task-specific supervision is necessary for procedural video understanding, with direct implications for deployment in AR/XR and real-time guidance where annotated data is unavailable. The Bayesian filtering wrapper provides a clean, interpretable mechanism for temporal reasoning that could transfer to other sequential video tasks. Key limitations include sensitivity to LLM quality for dependency estimation (only ~8-10% violation rate but non-trivial), degraded performance on long egocentric videos (Ego4D) where step descriptions are coarser, and the fact that oracle dependencies/progress yield +38.9% on Ego4D, indicating the LLM/LMM estimates remain the primary bottleneck. Open questions include whether duration priors or learned progress models could close this gap, and how the approach scales to tasks with 50+ steps or highly parallel action structures.
