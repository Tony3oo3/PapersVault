## Problem

Existing multi-task RL methods for LTL specifications struggle with zero-shot generalization to unseen, complex, and nested temporal-logic requirements. They either condition on full subgoal sequences (suffering from out-of-distribution issues at test time) or encode entire automata, and they lack mechanisms to explicitly enforce safety constraints or detect when a subgoal is unsatisfiable and switch to an alternative.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252599/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At test time, the method takes an arbitrary LTL formula and the agent's environment observation (a 7×7 egocentric grid map in LetterWorld or 360-beam LiDAR measurements plus ego-state in ZoneEnv) and produces a sequence of actions. A key design choice is that the subgoal is not appended to the observation as a bitvector; instead, it restructures the observation itself via a subgoal-induced reduction that collapses the proposition-dependent sensor readings into just two vectors (a 'reach' observation and a fused 'avoid' observation), so the policy input dimension stays constant regardless of the number of atomic propositions.

## Method

GenZ-LTL decomposes an LTL specification via its equivalent Büchi automaton into individual reach-avoid subgoals and solves them one at a time, rather than conditioning on subgoal sequences. Two innovations make this myopic approach effective: (1) a subgoal-induced observation reduction that partitions the state into proposition-independent and proposition-dependent components and fuses the latter into reach/avoid channels using an environment-specific operator (e.g., element-wise min for LiDAR), mitigating the exponential combinatorial explosion of state-subgoal pairs; and (2) a safe RL formulation built on Hamilton-Jacobi reachability that treats avoid subgoals as hard state-wise constraints via a learned reachability value function and a state-dependent Lagrangian multiplier, optimized with a PPO-style clipped surrogate objective. At test time, the agent tracks the current Büchi automaton state, selects the optimal subgoal by maximizing V_r − λ·V_h over candidates, and executes it; a timeout mechanism detects unsatisfiable subgoals and triggers switching to alternatives.

## Results

On finite-horizon LTL tasks, GenZ-LTL achieves a 98% success rate with 0% violation rate on LetterWorld (φ₁–φ₈) and 98–99% success rate with ≤1% violation rate on ZoneEnv (φ₉–φ₁₆), consistently outperforming all baselines. On the most complex ZoneEnv specifications (φ₁₃–φ₁₆), GenZ-LTL reaches 98% success with 0.01% violation, while the strongest baseline (DeepLTL) drops to 87% success with 3% violation. On infinite-horizon tasks, GenZ-LTL achieves near-zero violation rates (0.00–0.03) and the highest average accepting-state visits (μ_acc up to 102.12 vs. 48.28 for the next best method).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GenZ-LTL outperforms all four baselines across every specification group and environment. Against LTL2Action [50], the gap is largest on complex ZoneEnv specifications: GenZ-LTL achieves 98% success vs. 12% for LTL2Action on φ₁₃–φ₁₆, with violation rates of 0.01% vs. 17%. Against GCRL-LTL [40], GenZ-LTL improves success by 28 percentage points on φ₅–φ₈ (95% vs. 65%) and by 28 points on φ₁₃–φ₁₆ (98% vs. 70%), while reducing violation rates from 11% to 0% and from 9% to 1% respectively. Against DeepLTL [23], the closest competitor, GenZ-LTL gains 19 points on φ₅–φ₈ (95% vs. 76%) and 11 points on φ₁₃–φ₁₆ (98% vs. 87%), with near-zero violations (0–1%) versus DeepLTL's 2–3%. Against RAD-embeddings [54], GenZ-LTL leads by 28 points on φ₁₃–φ₁₆ (98% vs. 70%) and matches or exceeds it on all other groups, while maintaining lower violation rates. The advantage is most pronounced on longer, more complex specifications where baselines suffer from out-of-distribution subgoal sequences, confirming that solving one subgoal at a time with safe RL constraints is more effective than conditioning on full sequences or automaton embeddings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits within the subgoal-decomposition family of LTL-guided RL (alongside DeepLTL, GCRL-LTL, and LTL2Action) but departs from them in a key conceptual way: rather than conditioning the policy on subgoal sequences or full automaton encodings, it solves each reach-avoid subgoal independently using a safe RL formulation grounded in Hamilton-Jacobi reachability [12]. The implementation is adapted from DeepLTL [23], with the addition of a safety value function and state-dependent Lagrangian multiplier, and the removal of DeepLTL's curriculum-based sequence sampling. The novel subgoal-induced observation reduction technique has no direct predecessor in the LTL-RL literature, though it draws on the broader idea of state abstraction in goal-conditioned RL.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that a 'myopic' one-subgoal-at-a-time policy outperforms look-ahead sequence-conditioned policies challenges a prevailing assumption in the LTL-RL community and shifts the focus of generalization difficulty from formula structure to the combinatorics of atomic propositions. The observation reduction technique offers a practical path toward scaling to more atomic propositions, and the HJ-reachability-based safe RL formulation is the first to enforce state-wise safety constraints for LTL satisfaction. Limitations include the greedy nature of sequential subgoal solving (which can miss satisfiable specifications), the symmetry assumptions required for observation reduction, and evaluation restricted to navigation-style environments; extending to more complex dynamics and exploiting Boolean structure within subgoals (e.g., via Boolean task algebra) are identified as future directions.
