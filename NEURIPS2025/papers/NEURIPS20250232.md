## Problem

Linear global attention methods in graph transformers aggregate messages from all nodes into a single representation, causing attention scores to become uniformly distributed (high entropy) as graph size grows. This 'over-aggregating' dilutes key messages and leads to information loss, while sparse attention alternatives sacrifice the global receptive field. The paper formalizes this phenomenon with a monotonic lower-bound theorem on attention entropy and shows that direct entropy regularization is infeasible for large graphs due to O(n²) computation.

## Input / Output

Wideformer is a plug-and-play module inserted into the attention aggregation step of existing graph transformer backbones (GraphGPS, SGFormer, Polynormer). It takes the standard query, key, and value projections (Q, K, V ∈ R^{n×d}) and the graph structure, and produces m parallel cluster-level aggregation outputs per target node instead of a single one. These m outputs are sorted by cluster-level attention scores and weighted before concatenation, yielding an m·d-dimensional representation per node that feeds into the backbone's subsequent layers for node classification.

## Method

Wideformer consists of two components. First, it divides the aggregation: a K-Means++-inspired center-selection algorithm identifies m cluster centers from the query features, and source nodes are assigned to clusters based on key-feature similarity to these centers; each cluster is then aggregated independently via softmax attention, limiting the input volume per aggregation to n/m nodes and preventing message dilution. Second, it guides the attention: cluster-level attention scores are computed as softmax(Q·K̄ᵀ) where K̄ is the average key per cluster, and the m aggregation results for each target node are sorted and weighted by these scores so that the most informative clusters receive higher contribution. The overall complexity is O(nm) with m typically 2–8, preserving linear scalability while maintaining the full global receptive field. The method is architecture-agnostic and integrates directly with Performer-based, polynomial, and simplified linear attention backbones without modifying their core computation.

## Results

On homophilic datasets, Wideformer consistently improves all three linear-attention backbones: SGFormer+Wideformer reaches 92.19% on AmazonComputers and 97.39% on CoauthorPhysics, while GraphGPS+Wideformer achieves 95.85% on CoauthorCS. On heterophilic datasets, Polynormer+Wideformer attains 55.05% on Amazon-Ratings, 97.26% on Minesweeper, 79.00% on Questions, and 92.16% on Roman-Empire. Across all 13 datasets, Wideformer yields statistically significant gains (p < 0.05) over the unmodified backbones, and the entropy-regularization baseline frequently runs out of memory on graphs with >10k nodes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Wideformer is integrated as a plug-and-play module into three linear-attention graph transformer backbones (GraphGPS+Performer, SGFormer, Polynormer) and consistently outperforms their unmodified versions across all 13 datasets. On homophilic graphs, SGFormer+Wideformer surpasses the previous best baseline DIFFormer on AmazonComputers (92.19% vs. 91.99%) and NAGphormer on CoauthorPhysics (97.39% vs. 97.34%), while GraphGPS+Wideformer edges out NAGphormer on CoauthorCS (95.85% vs. 95.75%). On heterophilic graphs, Polynormer+Wideformer achieves the best results on all five datasets (e.g., 55.05% on Amazon-Ratings vs. 54.71% baseline; 92.16% on Roman-Empire vs. 91.83%). The entropy-regularization alternative, which directly penalizes attention entropy during training, yields only marginal gains and frequently hits out-of-memory errors on graphs with >10k nodes (e.g., OGB-ARXIV, Twitch-Gamer, CoauthorPhysics), whereas Wideformer scales linearly with O(nm) complexity. In a direct comparison with ENACT (a vision-transformer clustering method adapted to graphs), Wideformer outperforms it on all four tested datasets, confirming that source-target-aware clustering is more effective than node-feature-based clustering for relieving over-aggregating.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of linear global attention methods for graphs (GraphGPS/Performer, SGFormer, Polynormer) and the broader family of graph transformer pathologies (over-smoothing, over-squashing, over-dilution, over-globalizing). It carves new ground by identifying 'over-aggregating' as a distinct phenomenon—uniform attention scores across all source nodes due to the large aggregation volume—and by proposing a divided-aggregation strategy (Wideformer) that differs from sparse attention (which shrinks receptive fields) and from ENACT (which clusters by individual node features rather than source-target relations). The K-Means++-based center selection and cluster-level attention guidance are repurposed from classical clustering and standard attention mechanisms, but their combination for relieving message dilution in graph attention is novel.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Wideformer offers a practical, plug-and-play remedy for a limitation that affects all linear-attention graph transformers at scale, maintaining global receptive fields while keeping O(nm) complexity. The monotonic lower-bound theorem on attention entropy provides a clean theoretical justification for why the problem worsens with graph size, and the gradient analysis (Eq. 4) mechanistically explains why standard backpropagation fails to correct it. Open questions remain around learnable division strategies and adaptive cluster weighting, which the ablations suggest could yield further gains. The modest but consistent improvements across 13 datasets and 3 backbones, together with the OOM failures of the entropy-regularization baseline on large graphs, make this a practically relevant contribution for anyone scaling graph transformers.
