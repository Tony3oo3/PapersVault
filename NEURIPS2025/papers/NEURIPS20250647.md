## Problem

Existing airfoil generation methods face an inherent trade-off: parametric-model-based approaches (e.g., Bezier curves, NURBS) preserve smoothness and allow arbitrary-resolution sampling but restrict the design space to a predefined function family, while discrete-point-based methods maximize design space but cannot inherently guarantee continuity or produce control points at arbitrary resolutions. No prior method simultaneously achieves maximal design space, smoothness, and resolution adaptability for airfoil geometry generation.

## Input / Output

For conditional generation, the model takes 11 PARSEC geometric parameters (leading-edge radius, crest positions/curvatures, trailing-edge angles, etc.) as conditioning variables and outputs a continuous airfoil curve y(α) over a circular domain α ∈ [0,1] with x(α) = (cos(2πα)+1)/2, enabling sampling at any resolution (demonstrated at 257, 513, and 1025 points). For editing, the input is an original airfoil plus user-specified constraints (e.g., fixed or dragged contour points, thickness targets), and the output is a modified airfoil curve that satisfies those constraints while preserving the generative prior. The model processes inputs as point cloud data at arbitrary resolutions via a resolution-free neural operator.

## Method

FuncGenFoil formulates airfoil generation as flow matching in function space, where a Fourier Neural Operator (FNO) serves as the velocity operator v_θ(u_t, c, t) that maps a noised airfoil function, optional conditioning variables, and time to a velocity function. Training uses straight-line interpolation between a Gaussian process prior (Matérn kernel, ν=2.5, l=0.03) and the target airfoil, with the velocity operator trained via Operator Flow Matching loss to predict the deconstruction velocity. Inference reconstructs the airfoil by numerically integrating the ODE (Euler method, 10 steps) from a GP sample. For editing, the method performs MAP estimation by jointly fine-tuning the velocity operator weights and the latent code (obtained via ODE inversion of the original airfoil) over a few iterations, using an MSE constraint on edited regions plus the log-likelihood from the generative prior (computed via FFJORD with Hutchinson trace estimator).

## Results

On AF-200K, FuncGenFoil achieves an arithmetic mean label error of 14.9×10⁻³ (vs. 23.5×10⁻³ for the strongest baseline PK-DIT) and a geometric mean of −71.6 (vs. −93.2), representing a 74.4% reduction in label error and a 23.2% increase in diversity. On the Supercritical Airfoil dataset, the arithmetic mean label error drops to 3.08×10⁻³ (vs. 58.1×10⁻³ for PK-DIT), a 96.6% reduction. The model also supports arbitrary-resolution sampling, maintaining consistent quality at 2× and 4× the training resolution (257→1025) with only a 4.6×10⁻³ increase in average label error.

## Baselines & Comparisons

All baselines (CVAE, CGAN, PK-VAE, PK-GAN, PKVAE-GAN, PK-DIT) are taken from the AFBench benchmark [Liu et al., NeurIPS 2024]. FuncGenFoil outperforms the strongest baseline PK-DIT on all three datasets for both arithmetic and geometric mean label error: by 74.4% (AF-200K), 65.0% (UIUC), and 96.6% (Super) in arithmetic mean, and by 36.6%, 88.4%, and 94.7% in geometric mean respectively. Diversity also improves by 23.2% (AF-200K), 35.7% (UIUC), and 15.8% (Super) over PK-DIT. On smoothness, FuncGenFoil is slightly worse on AF-200K (1.41 vs. 1.04) but substantially better on UIUC (1.33 vs. 6.03) and Super (1.01 vs. 1.97). The paper also compares against a classical NURBS-fitting optimization method (24 control points, initialized near the target), showing FuncGenFoil is competitive in overall accuracy while operating from a far more challenging starting point (Gaussian noise rather than a near-solution initialization).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of function-space generative modeling (Neural Processes, GASP, Functa, functional diffusion/flow models) and airfoil design, which has been dominated by either parametric representations (NURBS, Bezier, CST) or fixed-resolution discrete point methods (the AFBench baselines: CVAE, CGAN, PK-VAE, PK-DIT). The key conceptual move is transplanting the flow matching framework (Lipman et al.) into function space using a Fourier Neural Operator as the velocity operator, following the Operator Flow Matching formulation (Shi et al., 2025), so that airfoils are generated as continuous curves rather than point sets. For the editing task, the paper repurposes Neural ODE inversion and FFJORD log-likelihood estimation within a MAP fine-tuning loop, a combination not previously applied to airfoil geometry.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The arbitrary-resolution property is practically significant for manufacturing: engineers can sample control points at whatever precision their tooling requires without retraining, something neither parametric nor fixed-point methods offer. The 74.4% label-error reduction over the strongest baseline (PK-DIT) and the near-zero-MSE editing capability (after only ~10 fine-tuning steps) make this a credible tool for aerodynamic shape optimization workflows, further validated by RANS CFD on CRM wings. The main open question is generality: the circular parameterization and smooth-surface assumption limit the approach to airfoils and wings, and extending to full aircraft or irregular geometries without a natural coordinate system remains unresolved.
