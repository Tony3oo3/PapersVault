## Problem

Impartial selection mechanisms guarantee that an agent's chance of being selected is independent of their own nominations, preventing strategic manipulation. However, prior work established that such mechanisms achieve at most 1/2-approximation for single-agent selection and (1−1/e) for large k, with no way to leverage external information. This paper asks how much the consistency-robustness tradeoff of impartial mechanisms can be improved when the mechanism is given an independent prediction of the optimal set of agents.

## Input / Output

The mechanism receives a directed nomination graph G = ([n], E) without self-loops (an edge i→j means agent i nominates agent j) and a predicted set Ŝ of exactly k vertices. It outputs a set of at most k vertices (deterministic) or a probability distribution over vertices summing to at most k (randomized). The impartiality constraint requires that the selection probability of vertex i is invariant to changes in i's outgoing edges, meaning the prediction must be treated as exogenous information independent of the vote structure.

## Method

The paper introduces a family of mechanisms parameterized by a confidence parameter ρ ∈ [1/2, 1]. For 1-selection, the ρ-permutation mechanism fixes the predicted vertex's position in a random permutation at value ρ (so ρ=1 places it last, revealing all its incoming edges) while sampling other positions uniformly, achieving ρ-consistency and (1−ρ)-robustness. For k-selection, the ρ-partition mechanism assigns exactly one predicted vertex to each of k random partitions and places it at position ρ within its partition's internal permutation, achieving α = 1−(1−ρ)/k consistency and β = (1−2ρ/(k+1))(1−((k−1)/k)^k) robustness. The key technical challenge in the k-partition analysis is that the events of two optimal vertices landing in the same partition are no longer independent, requiring a direct computation of unconditional selection probabilities rather than a product of independent terms. The paper also proves matching upper bounds showing these tradeoffs are (asymptotically) best-possible.

## Results

For 1-selection with approval voting, the ρ-permutation mechanism achieves ρ-consistency and (1−ρ)-robustness for any ρ ∈ [1/2, 1], which is best-possible (Theorem 6.1: β ≤ 1/2, α+β ≤ 1). For 1-selection with plurality voting, the 1-permutation mechanism achieves 1-consistency and 1/2-robustness, and a lottery with the uniform permutation mechanism yields (2/3 + ρ/3)-consistency and (2/3 − ρ/6)-robustness. For general k-selection, the ρ-partition mechanism achieves α = 1 − (1−ρ)/k consistency and β = (1 − 2ρ/(k+1))(1 − ((k−1)/k)^k) robustness; at ρ = 1/2 this gives consistency 1 − 1/(2k) and robustness approaching 1 − 1/e ≈ 0.632 as k → ∞, while at ρ = 1 it gives full 1-consistency with robustness still approaching 1 − 1/e.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the uniform permutation mechanism of Fischer and Klimm [17] (1/2-robust, 1/2-consistent), the ρ-permutation mechanism strictly dominates by achieving any point on the line from (1/2, 1/2) to (1, 0), with the full trade-off proven best-possible by Theorem 6.1. Against the k-partition mechanism of Bjelde et al. [7] (robustness ≈ 0.632 for large k), the ρ-partition mechanism matches the asymptotic robustness while boosting consistency from ≈ 0.632 to 1 (at ρ=1) or 1−1/(2k) (at ρ=1/2), a significant improvement for finite k. Against the 2/3-robust plurality mechanism of Cembrano et al. [11], the 1-permutation mechanism trades 1/6 of robustness for full 1-consistency, and the lottery recovers the 2/3-robustness point while adding a consistency knob. Against the 1/4-optimal mechanism of Alon et al. [3], all proposed mechanisms provide substantially stronger guarantees. The key finding is that near-optimal consistency (α→1) can be achieved with little to no sacrifice in robustness (β still approaching 1−1/e for large k), whereas prior work without predictions could only achieve α=β.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the impartial selection literature (Alon et al. 2011, Fischer & Klimm 2015, Bjelde et al. 2017) by introducing a prediction of the optimal selected set as an additional input, connecting it to the broader algorithms-with-predictions paradigm. The core mechanisms—the ρ-permutation and ρ-partition mechanisms—are direct modifications of the uniform permutation mechanism and the k-partition mechanism with permutation, respectively, where the predicted vertices are given privileged positions in the permutation or partition. A key conceptual departure from prior work on mechanisms with advice is that the prediction is about who is most appropriate for selection rather than about how the votes will turn out, which preserves impartiality by construction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic study of the consistency-robustness trade-off for impartial selection with predictions, providing clean matching (or near-matching) upper and lower bounds across multiple settings (1-selection approval, 1-selection plurality, 2-selection, and general k-selection). The finding that near-optimal consistency (1 − O(1/k)) can be achieved with robustness approaching 1 − 1/e − O(1/k) for large k suggests that predictions can substantially improve impartial mechanisms without sacrificing their worst-case guarantees. Open questions include closing the remaining gaps between upper and lower bounds (e.g., for 3-selection), testing the mechanisms in practical applications such as LLM output aggregation, and extending the analysis to settings where the prediction quality is partially known.
