## Problem

Existing 3D shape completion methods assume inputs are pre-aligned to a canonical frame, leaking pose and scale cues that networks exploit to memorize absolute positions rather than infer intrinsic geometry. When this alignment is absent in real-world scans, performance collapses. No prior architecture fully eliminates this bias: SO(3)- and SE(3)-equivariant variants still require ground-truth centroids or scales for canonicalization, making them impractical for unaligned inputs.

## Input / Output

The network consumes a partial point cloud of 2,048 points in an arbitrary pose and scale (no canonicalization or bounding-box normalization required) and outputs a completed point cloud of 16,384 points expressed in the original sensor frame. The coarse-to-fine scheme inherited from AdaPoinTr generates a small set of query points that are then expanded to the full output resolution, and the entire pipeline operates without any external alignment step at inference time.

## Method

SIMECO is the first fully SIM(3)-equivariant shape completion network, built on the AdaPoinTr backbone with DGCNN replaced by VN-DGCNN and every Transformer layer replaced by a three-stage equivariant block. Each block (i) canonicalizes features via an extended layer normalization that subtracts the channelwise mean and normalizes by the row-wise ℓ₂-norm, removing translation and scale while preserving rotation equivariance; (ii) performs similarity-invariant shape reasoning through rotation-invariant attention (VN-Transformer) whose Frobenius inner-product scores depend only on relative geometry; and (iii) restores the original transform via a residual path that re-injects translation (addition) and scale (multiplication by a global norm statistic μ) after each reasoning step. The full architecture is proven SIM(3)-equivariant by construction, and training uses a standard Chamfer-distance loss with no data augmentation, so the model generalizes directly to unaligned real scans.

## Results

On the PCN benchmark under a de-biased protocol (no training-time augmentation, I/SIM(3) test), SIMECO achieves a mean Chamfer distance ℓ1 of 8.59 and F-score@1% of 0.714, outperforming the strongest non-equivariant baseline AdaPoinTr (9.54, 0.661) by 10% and 8% respectively. On cross-domain KITTI driving scans, SIMECO reduces Minimal Matching Distance by 17% (5.35 vs. 6.47) and cuts ESCAPE's Fidelity error by 69% (0.56 vs. 1.81). On OmniObject3D indoor scans, it lowers CD-ℓ1 by 14% (10.25 vs. 11.90) and raises F1 by 5% (0.698 vs. 0.664) over the best augmented baseline, all without any bounding-box normalization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SIMECO outperforms all ten baselines on the PCN benchmark. Against the strongest non-equivariant baseline AdaPoinTr (trained with SIM(3) augmentation), SIMECO reduces CD-ℓ1 from 9.54 to 8.59 (−10%) and raises F1 from 0.661 to 0.714 (+8%). Among equivariant competitors, ESCAPE (SE(3), same AdaPoinTr backbone) achieves CD-ℓ1 10.41 and F1 0.650 under its native I/SE(3) setting; SIMECO improves both by 17% and 10% respectively while operating under the strictly harder full SIM(3) test. EquivPCN (SO(3)) and SCARP (SE(3)) lag further behind at CD-ℓ1 12.54 and 35.49. The remaining non-equivariant baselines (PoinTr, SeedFormer, SnowflakeNet, AnchorFormer, ODGNet) all require SIM(3) data augmentation and still trail SIMECO by 1.6–6.5 in CD-ℓ1. In the pose-estimation ablation (Table 4), prepending ConDor to AdaPoinTr yields CD-ℓ1 9.92 and F1 0.692, still 15% worse in CD-ℓ1 than SIMECO, confirming that architectural equivariance surpasses explicit pose canonicalization. On cross-domain KITTI, SIMECO (MMD 5.35) beats AdaPoinTr (6.47) by 17% and ESCAPE (5.93) by 10%, while on OmniObject3D it leads AdaPoinTr (11.90) by 14% in CD-ℓ1 and ESCAPE (12.34) by 17%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the vector neuron (VN) framework and VN-Transformer attention to achieve full SIM(3) equivariance, going beyond the SO(3)-equivariant EquivPCN and SE(3)-equivariant ESCAPE/SCARP that still require ground-truth centroids or bounding boxes for canonicalization. Architecturally, it builds on the AdaPoinTr coarse-to-fine Transformer backbone, replacing DGCNN with VN-DGCNN and standard Transformer layers with a three-stage SIM(3)-equivariant module (canonicalization → invariant attention → transform restoration). The key conceptual departure is eliminating all reliance on privileged alignment information: where prior equivariant methods reduce to explicit canonicalization in practice, this work enforces equivariance to the full similarity group by design, making the model agnostic to arbitrary pose and scale without any external normalization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper exposes a fundamental evaluation flaw in the shape completion literature: benchmark performance is inflated by leaked pose and scale cues, and non-equivariant models collapse under a de-biased protocol (AdaPoinTr's CD-l1 jumps from ~9.5 to 60.2). Establishing SIM(3) equivariance as the correct inductive bias provides a principled path to cross-domain transfer without retraining or normalization, with demonstrated gains on real driving and indoor scans. Open questions include extending the framework to multi-object and articulated scenes, reducing the ~3× computational overhead of vector-valued features for real-time deployment, and understanding when equivariance might be counterproductive if objects consistently appear in canonical frames.
