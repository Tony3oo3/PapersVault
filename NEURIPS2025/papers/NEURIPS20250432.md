## Problem

Standard optimizers (SGD, Adam, AdamW) are designed for training from scratch and make no assumptions about the initial model state, so they do not exploit the fact that fine-tuning starts from a well-converged model. This leads to catastrophic forgetting, where the model degrades on the original task when adapting to new data. The paper asks how to design an optimizer that explicitly regularizes the optimization process using the properties of a converged starting point, without introducing additional parameters or architectural changes.

## Input / Output

PROFIT requires a converged (not randomly initialized) model, new training data from a proximal distribution to the pre-training data, and a task loss function. It additionally requires two optimizers: a user-chosen main optimizer O and a reference optimizer O^(ref) (recommended as plain SGD), plus hyperparameters n_ref (number of reference steps, typically 1) and λ_ref (reference learning rate, typically λ_main/10 to λ_main/10000). The output is fine-tuned model weights that simultaneously improve on the new task and retain performance on the original task, with no additional learnable parameters introduced.

## Method

PROFIT frames fine-tuning as temporal multi-task learning, where the two 'tasks' are maintaining the old converged state and learning the new task. At each iteration, it (1) saves the current weights as θ_ref, (2) takes n_ref small steps with the reference optimizer to obtain a displaced state, (3) computes the displacement vector Δ = θ − θ_ref (which approximates the gradient direction back toward the old equilibrium), (4) computes the new-task gradient g, and (5) if g and Δ conflict (negative dot product), orthogonally projects g onto Δ (g ⊥ Δ) using the PCGrad-style operator. The model is then restored to θ_ref and the main optimizer takes a single step along the orthogonalized direction. Crucially, the projection is asymmetric—only g is projected onto Δ, not vice versa—because Δ encodes information about the old dataset that may no longer be accessible. Theoretical analysis proves that PROFIT decreases the old-task loss (Theorem 3.1), has only rare stable-point failure modes on exactly linear loss surfaces (Theorem 3.2), and inherits convergence guarantees from its parent optimizer (Theorem 3.4).

## Results

On CIFAR-10→CIFAR-100 fine-tuning, PROFIT achieves the best old-task retention across all backbones: 59.02% CIFAR-10 / 65.44% CIFAR-100 with ViT-Small (vs. 58.60%/63.93% for Adam), and 35.26%/74.70% with ResNet-18 (vs. 24.49%/74.59% for SGD). On the DriveLM VQA benchmark, PROFIT improves accuracy over AdamW by 5.6 percentage points (67.88% vs. 62.21%) and final score by 2.2 points (59.16 vs. 56.98). On Waymo motion prediction (car→pedestrian), PROFIT reduces FDE@8s to 1.145 m, outperforming full fine-tuning (1.242 m) and head-only fine-tuning (1.544 m).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PROFIT consistently outperforms standard optimizers (SGD, Adam, Lookahead, Adan) on CIFAR-10 retention while matching or exceeding their CIFAR-100 accuracy; e.g., with ViT-Small, PROFIT gains +0.42 pp on CIFAR-10 and +1.51 pp on CIFAR-100 over Adam. On DriveLM, PROFIT beats AdamW on every reported metric (accuracy +5.6 pp, GPT score +1.5 pp, BLEU-4 +0.016, CIDEr +0.012). On Waymo car-to-pedestrian fine-tuning, PROFIT reduces FDE@8s by 0.097 m over full fine-tuning and 0.399 m over head-only fine-tuning. Against parameter-efficient adapters (LoRA, VPT, ConvAdapter), PROFIT marginally outperforms or ties on CIFAR-100 accuracy without introducing extra parameters. On VTAB-1K, naive PROFIT (without warm-up) performs poorly due to distribution mismatch, but with a 10-epoch AdamW warm-up it surpasses full fine-tuning on 14 of 19 tasks, confirming the method's dependence on the proximal fine-tuning assumption.

## Relation to Prior Work

PROFIT repurposes the gradient-orthogonalization mechanism from PCGrad (Yu et al., 2020) but reframes fine-tuning as a *temporal* multi-task learning problem, where the two conflicting 'tasks' are the pre-training loss (represented implicitly by the displacement vector Δ) and the new-task loss (the batch gradient g). This departs from catastrophic-forgetting mitigations like LWF and rehearsal-based methods that require storing old data or model snapshots, and from parameter-efficient fine-tuning (LoRA, adapters) that introduce additional learnable parameters. The paper positions PROFIT as the first optimizer explicitly designed for the fine-tuning regime, in contrast to SGD/Adam/AdamW which make minimal assumptions about the initial model state and are designed for training from scratch.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PROFIT offers a clean, modular drop-in optimizer that requires no architectural changes, no additional parameters, and no access to old data, making it attractive for practitioners who fine-tune converged models routinely. However, the 'proximal fine-tuning' assumption (similar pre-training and fine-tuning distributions) significantly limits applicability, as demonstrated by the poor VTAB-1K results without a warmup phase. The ~25% memory overhead from storing reference weights is a practical concern for large models, and the paper defers memory-efficient implementations to future work. Open questions include whether the warmup recipe generalizes to truly orthogonal domains, whether the approach composes well with other regularization techniques, and whether the convergence guarantees hold in multi-stage incremental fine-tuning settings.
