## Problem

In the incentivized exploration model, a central platform must recommend actions to self-interested agents who only follow recommendations that are Bayesian incentive compatible (BIC). Prior work showed that in high-dimensional linear bandit settings, achieving the initial spectral exploration needed for BIC Thompson sampling requires exponential sample complexity when the action set is a non-smooth polytope. This paper asks whether the exponential barrier can be overcome by exploiting the geometry of the action set, specifically when actions lie in the d-dimensional Euclidean unit ball.

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes as input a known prior distribution μ on the unknown parameter vector ℓ* ∈ R^d (satisfying mild non-degeneracy conditions: not confined to any half-space, non-degenerate covariance in every direction, and sub-Gaussian tails) and the d-dimensional unit ball as the action space. At each round it outputs a BIC action A^(t) ∈ A that rational agents will follow, and the full sequence must satisfy λ-spectral exploration (all eigenvalues of Σ(A^(t))⊗2 ≥ λ). Unlike prior work on incentivized exploration, the coordinates of ℓ* need not be independent under the prior, and the algorithm is rotationally invariant.

## Method

The algorithm alternates between two subroutines: InitialExploration, which constructs a random event that zeros out the conditional expectation of ℓ* in already-explored directions, yielding a BIC action with Ω(ε_d) magnitude in the unexplored subspace; and ExponentialGrowth, which exploits the curvature of the unit ball to double the magnitude of the BIC action's projection onto the unexplored subspace by conditioning on the sign of a noisy reward signal. The key geometric insight is that for the smooth unit ball, the posterior-optimal action map ℓ* → ℓ*/||ℓ*|| is expansive, so conditioning on even a weak directional signal increases the conditional expectation's magnitude, enabling exponential growth of the exploration signal from an exponentially small starting point. A linear algebra lemma (Lemma 9) ensures that each round of exploration non-negligibly increases the sum of small eigenvalues of the Gram matrix, guaranteeing termination in poly(d) steps. The resulting sample complexity is λ(d/(c_v + c_d))^O(1) log(1/ε_d), which is polynomial in d and tight up to polynomial factors in each parameter.

## Results

The main theoretical result (Theorem 4) establishes that under Assumption 3 (non-degeneracy, non-degenerate covariance, and sub-Gaussianity of the prior), a BIC algorithm achieves λ-spectral exploration with sample complexity \(\bar{\lambda}(d/(c_v + c_d))^{O(1)} \log(1/\epsilon_d)\), which is polynomial in dimension d and logarithmic in \(\epsilon_d\). In the experimental validation (Section 4), the algorithm is tested on d-dimensional Gaussian priors for d = 2 to 24, showing a quadratic dependence of sample complexity on dimension and quartic scaling for running time — substantially better than the worst-case polynomial bound. The lower bounds in Appendix B confirm tightness up to polynomial factors in each of the three parameters \(c_d\), \(c_v\), and \(\epsilon_d\).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is primarily a theoretical paper without a formal baseline comparison table. The key contrast is with Sellke [2023, Proposition 3.9], which showed that \(e^{\Omega(d)}\) samples can be necessary for BIC exploration when the action set is a non-smooth polytope with corners. The present paper removes this exponential barrier by restricting the action set to the d-dimensional unit ball, where the smoothness of the boundary enables the exponential-growth subroutine to bootstrap from an \(\Omega(\epsilon_d)\)-magnitude signal to \(\sqrt{\lambda}\)-magnitude in only \(O(\log(1/\epsilon_d))\) steps. The experimental results (Figure 2) validate that the practical sample complexity scales quadratically in d (vs. the worst-case \(d^{O(1)}\) bound), with the gap attributed to the \(d^4\) factor from Lemma 9 being a worst-case geometric bound. The paper also notes that combining its spectral exploration guarantee with Thompson sampling from Sellke [2023] yields an end-to-end \(\epsilon\)-BIC algorithm with regret \(\text{poly}(d, 1/r, 1/\epsilon) + \tilde{O}(d\sqrt{T})\) (Bayesian) or \(\text{poly}(d, 1/r, 1/\epsilon) + \tilde{O}(d^{3/2}\sqrt{T})\) (frequentist), matching state-of-the-art Thompson sampling regret asymptotically.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the incentivized exploration framework from Kremer et al. [2014] and Che and Hörner [2018] through the two-stage algorithm of Sellke and Slivkins [2023] (initial exploration followed by Thompson sampling) and the linear-context extension of Sellke [2023]. The key departure is identifying the geometric smoothness of the action set as the critical factor: while Sellke [2023] showed exponential sample complexity is necessary for non-smooth polytope action sets, this paper proves that for the d-dimensional unit ball, a BIC algorithm achieves λ-spectral exploration in polynomial time. The exponential growth subroutine, which leverages the curvature of the unit ball to double exploration magnitude per step, is the novel technical mechanism that overcomes the exponential barrier.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This result resolves a fundamental open question about whether incentive compatibility necessarily precludes efficient learning in high-dimensional linear bandits, showing that the exponential barrier is a geometric artifact rather than an inherent limitation. The end-to-end combination with Sellke [2023] yields an ε-BIC algorithm with near-optimal Thompson sampling regret, making incentivized exploration practically viable for smooth action spaces. Open questions include whether the polynomial bound is tight (the paper shows matching lower bounds only up to polynomial factors), whether the methods extend to other smooth convex bodies beyond the unit ball, and whether the ε-BIC relaxation can be tightened to full BIC without requiring well-separated actions.
