## Problem

Existing methods for relating structural connectivity (SC) to functional connectivity (FC) treat them as disconnected endpoints in one-way supervised mappings, failing to model the progressive SC-FC coupling throughout the transformation. Moreover, these approaches flatten connectivity matrices into Euclidean vectors, discarding the intrinsic symmetric positive-definite (SPD) manifold geometry that governs the data. The paper asks: can we learn a reversible, geometry-respecting flow that bridges SC and multi-task FC distributions while preserving network topology?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251076/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 116×116 SC matrices (derived from diffusion MRI via QSIPrep, log-transformed and regularized to SPD) paired with a cognitive-task label (e.g., resting-state, motor, working memory) for forward prediction, or multiple 116×116 FC matrices (from fMRI via xcp-d, Pearson correlation) from the same subject for reverse reconstruction. Outputs are task-specific FC matrices (forward) or a single consensus SC matrix (reverse), all constrained to the SPD manifold. The 116 regions follow the AAL atlas parcellation, and the model is permutation-equivariant over region ordering.

## Method

BrainFlow parametrizes a time-dependent vector field via conditional flow matching on the Cholesky manifold (the space of lower-triangular matrices with positive diagonal), proving (Theorem 4.3) that minimizing the flow-matching objective on the Cholesky manifold is exactly equivalent to doing so on the SPD manifold under the log-Cholesky metric. This avoids the eigendecomposition required by the affine-invariant Riemannian metric, reducing per-step cost from O(Bd³) to O(Bd) for interpolation and eliminating numerical instability from near-zero eigenvalues. The velocity field is an 8-layer Transformer without positional encoding (preserving permutation equivariance), conditioned on a task label embedding and a subject-specific joint coupling of (SC, FC) pairs. For reverse flow (FC→SC), a training-free consensus control module adds a Dirichlet-energy gradient term to the ODE, pulling multiple FC-state trajectories toward a shared structural anchor via Gaussian-kernel-weighted pairwise attraction, and the final SC is computed as the Fréchet mean of the converged Cholesky factors.

## Results

On FC prediction from SC, BrainFlow achieves the best topological preservation across all three datasets: LCE of 8.9%/8.1%/8.7% and GCE of 5.5%/4.5%/5.8% on UK Biobank, HCP-A, and HCP-YA respectively, while maintaining competitive RMSE (0.290/0.291/0.336). The SPD-based RCFM baseline diverges on all three real datasets due to numerical instability, whereas BrainFlow trains at 8 s/epoch versus 100 s/epoch for RCFM-SPD (12.5× speedup). On reverse SC prediction with consensus control, BrainFlow achieves RMSE of 0.07/0.36/0.24 and GCE of 0.08%/0.59%/0.46% across the three datasets, substantially outperforming all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BrainFlow wins on topological metrics (LCE, GCE) against all six baselines across all three datasets, with the largest margins over AM (≈40% lower LCE/GCE) and MGCN-GAN (≈50% lower GCE on HCP-YA: 5.8% vs 23.7%). MGCN-GAN retains the lowest RMSE (0.219–0.243) but sacrifices network topology, suggesting it fits edge-wise values without preserving hierarchical structure. RCFM-SPD diverges on every real dataset due to ill-conditioned intermediate SPD matrices, making it non-competitive. Among the flow-based baselines, BrainFlow outperforms CFM by 0.6–4.6% on LCE and 0.6–3.6% on GCE, and beats [SF]²M by 1.0–1.2% on LCE and 0.9–1.6% on GCE. VPFM shows high variance on HCP-A (RMSE 0.445±0.211) and is outperformed by BrainFlow on all metrics for that dataset. The 12.5× training speedup over RCFM-SPD (8 s vs 100 s per epoch) further distinguishes BrainFlow as the only method that is both stable and efficient at 116×116 matrix scale.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Riemannian Flow Matching (RCFM, Chen & Lipman 2024) from the SPD manifold to the Cholesky manifold, leveraging the isometry established by Lin (2019) to prove that flow matching on the two spaces is equivalent (Theorem 4.3) while gaining substantial computational efficiency. It departs from prior SC-FC prediction methods (MGCN-GAN, DSBGM) that treat structural and functional connectivity as endpoints of a one-way supervised mapping, instead formulating the relationship as a bidirectional flow on a Riemannian manifold with a consensus control mechanism for the reverse direction. The conditional flow matching framework from Lipman et al. (2022) provides the training paradigm, but the subject-specific prior coupling and the Dirichlet-energy-based consensus term are novel contributions tailored to the neuroscience setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical failure of RCFM-SPD (complete divergence on all three real datasets due to ill-conditioning) highlights a critical gap between theoretical Riemannian flow matching and its deployment on high-dimensional neuroimaging data, making the Cholesky manifold formulation a necessary rather than merely convenient choice. The bidirectional capability with consensus control opens a path toward understanding SC-FC coupling as a dynamical system rather than a static mapping, with potential applications in disorder diagnosis and connectivity-based interventions. Key limitations include the assumption that SC can be made SPD via self-loops, the non-optimization of the consensus parameter σ during training (motivated by a train-inference mismatch argument that remains somewhat hand-wavy), and evaluation restricted to 116-region AAL parcellation. Open questions include extension to dynamic FC, higher-resolution parcellations, and whether the consensus mechanism generalizes beyond the specific many-to-one reverse mapping structure of brain connectivity.
