## Problem

Open-world embodied agents face partial observability and incomplete knowledge, yet most existing systems either assume known object categories or rely on passive perception that exhaustively collects all objects and relations from the environment. This becomes infeasible in visually complex settings with thousands of objects, most irrelevant to the current task. The critical missing step is a principled mechanism for identifying which objects are task-relevant and prioritizing which ones to actively sense before planning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253556/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The agent receives a high-level natural language instruction (e.g., 'put a clean potato in the fridge') and raw RGB frames from the ALFWorld simulator, along with a fixed PDDL action model defining high-level skills (pickup, clean, heat, cool, put-in, toggle). It produces a sequence of physical actions to complete the task within a 30-step budget, and at each decision step selects which object descriptions to sense next based on VOO scores. The agent operates strictly zero-shot with no fine-tuning on ALFWorld data, using GPT-4o for both commonsense belief estimation and VLM-based object detection from visual frames.

## Method

ACTIVEVOO integrates lifted regression (Lifted Backward Search) to generate abstract subgoals parameterized by variables rather than concrete objects, then extracts unary object descriptions and relational descriptions from these subgoals as sensing targets. For each candidate, it estimates sensing success probabilities by querying GPT-4o token logits on yes/no commonsense questions (averaged over 5 samples). The core novelty is the Value of Observation (VOO) metric, formulated within a belief-state MDP over object/relation knowledge states, which quantifies the expected utility gain from actively sensing a specific object relative to not sensing it—distinct from Value of Information because the observation causally enables the object's use. The agent iteratively selects the highest-VOO object, navigates to it, queries the VLM for detection, updates its knowledge base, and re-computes VOO scores until all predicates in some lifted subgoal are grounded, at which point the associated plan is instantiated and executed.

## Results

ACTIVEVOO achieves an overall success rate of 0.86 on the visual ALFWorld benchmark in a zero-shot setting (no fine-tuning, single trial), the highest among all agents using only visual observations. It outperforms the strongest zero-shot VLM baselines (GPT-4o Vision at 0.08, LLAVA-13B at 0.11) by a large margin, and also surpasses fine-tuned VLMs such as EMMA-10 (0.82, requiring 10 trials) and BUTLER (0.26). Among language-only methods, ACTIVEVOO outperforms REACT (0.69), DEPS (0.76), and AUTOGEN (0.77), and is competitive with REFLEXION-3 (0.83, 3 trials), though REFLEXION-10 (0.91, 10 trials) remains slightly higher. The average episode length is 15.3 steps, substantially shorter than passive baselines (25.4 steps for exhaustive object acquisition).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ACTIVEVOO (0.86 avg.) decisively outperforms all zero-shot vision-language models: GPT-4o Vision (0.08) and LLAVA-13B (0.11) are outperformed by over 7× in success rate. It also surpasses all fine-tuned VLM baselines that require extensive task-specific data collection, including BUTLER (0.26, 52K demos), LLAMA-ADAPTER (0.13), INSTRUCTBLIP (0.22), and EMMA-3 (0.37); EMMA-10 (0.82) is the closest competitor but requires 10 successive trials with cross-trial memory transfer, whereas ACTIVEVOO operates in a single zero-shot trial. Among language-only methods, ACTIVEVOO beats REACT (0.69), DEPS (0.76), AUTOGEN (0.77), and REFLEXION-3 (0.83), though REFLEXION-10 (0.91) edges it out by 0.05 using 10 reflective trials. The ablation in Table 2 further shows that replacing VOO-guided active sensing with exhaustive passive object acquisition drops success from 0.86 to 0.22, and removing VOO calculation alone reduces it to 0.75, confirming that the VOO mechanism is the primary driver of performance gains over weaker guided strategies (0.47–0.48).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of lifted regression planning (Ghallab et al., 2004; Sanner & Boutilier, 2009), its open-world adaptation (Liu et al., 2025), and LLM-based commonsense probability estimation via token logits (Liu et al., 2023a; Chen et al., 2023). It departs from prior active knowledge acquisition work in embodied AI—which has largely focused on locating predefined object classes or maximizing information gain for navigation—by introducing a goal-conditioned, object-centric sensing strategy where the agent first determines *which* objects are relevant via lifted subgoal decomposition before deciding *which* to sense. The Value of Observation (VOO) metric is explicitly distinguished from classical Value of Information (Howard, 1966) by the causal role of the sensing action: the object must be actively observed to affect the outcome, unlike passive information gathering.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dramatic 4× gap between active (0.86) and passive (0.22) knowledge acquisition, and the zero-shot outperformance of fine-tuned VLMs requiring 15K+ expert demonstrations, suggest that structured symbolic reasoning combined with LLM commonsense priors can be a more sample-efficient path to open-world embodied planning than end-to-end visual fine-tuning. However, the evaluation is confined to the ALFWorld simulator with a fixed PDDL action model, and the authors acknowledge that transfer to physical robots and handling of user/contextual preferences in goal specification remain open. A key unresolved question is whether the VOO framework generalizes to domains with richer relational structure or larger object vocabularies where LLM logit-based probability estimates may degrade further.
