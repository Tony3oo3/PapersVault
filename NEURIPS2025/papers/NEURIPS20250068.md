## Problem

Most diffusion-based forecasting methods for complex dynamics predict future states individually, failing to model progressive uncertainty growth and complex temporal dependencies. While rolling sequence diffusion models (RSDMs) address this with snapshot-dependent noise schedules, they are derived from DDPM and do not leverage EDM's principled design choices—network preconditioning, loss weighting, and second-order sampling—that are critical for high-fidelity generation. The paper tackles the challenge of unifying the rolling forecast structure with EDM's full design space.

## Input / Output

The method takes an initial state snapshot y_0 (plus boundary conditions and obstacle masks for Navier-Stokes) and an external forecaster's short-range prediction to initialize the first rolling window. It operates on sequences of W=6 spatiotemporal snapshots—221×42 velocity/pressure fields for Navier-Stokes or 240×121 grids with 69 prognostic variables at 1.5° resolution for ERA5—and produces an ensemble of M future snapshots (M=50 or M=10) via iterative rolling-window denoising, where each ODE integration step yields one fully denoised forecast and appends a fresh pure-noise snapshot to the window.

## Method

ERDM adapts EDM's noise schedule, preconditioning, loss weighting, and Heun sampler to a rolling, progressive-noise setting. The key mechanism is a windowed noise schedule σ̄_w(t) = (σ_max^(1/ρ) + t_{w,t}(σ_min^(1/ρ) − σ_max^(1/ρ)))^ρ with ρ=−10, which assigns monotonically increasing noise to later snapshots so that after N denoising steps the first snapshot reaches σ_min while the last retains σ_max, enabling an efficient sliding-window rollout. Training uses a reweighted score-matching loss λ(σ_w)·f(σ_w) that combines EDM's unit-variance normalization with a lognormal emphasis on mid-range noise levels where determinism transitions to stochasticity. The denoiser is a hybrid architecture interleaving causal temporal attention layers into a 2D ADM U-Net, with per-snapshot vectorized EDM preconditioning (c_skip, c_out, c_in, c_noise). First-window initialization draws from a pre-trained conditional EDM rather than co-training a separate generator, and sampling uses the deterministic second-order Heun solver adapted to the coupled W-process ODE.

## Results

On Navier-Stokes (64-step rollout, 50 ensemble members), ERDM achieves approximately 50% lower CRPS than the best baseline (EDM with W=4) by the end of the rollout, while also delivering superior calibration (spread-skill ratio closer to 1). On ERA5 1.5° weather forecasting (up to 15-day lead times), ERDM outperforms the conditional EDM baseline by up to 10% in relative CRPS across 8 variables and is broadly competitive with the operational IFS ENS and hybrid NeuralGCM ENS for mid-to-long-range forecasts. In terms of efficiency, ERDM requires 5× fewer neural function evaluations (120 vs. 600 NFEs) for a 15-day, 5-member ensemble forecast, with total inference time of 209 s vs. 237 s for EDM on a single A100 GPU, and trains in 5 days on 4 H200 GPUs (vs. 10 days on 128 v5 TPUs for NeuralGCM).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the autoregressive conditional EDM baselines (W=1, 2, 4, 6), ERDM consistently wins on both CRPS and calibration: on Navier-Stokes it achieves ~50% lower CRPS than EDM W=4 at step 64, and on ERA5 it improves relative CRPS by up to 10% across all 8 evaluated variables. Against DYffusion (the prior SOTA on Navier-Stokes), ERDM clearly outperforms, as DYffusion only catches up to EDM W=4 by the end of the rollout but never reaches ERDM. Against PDE-Refiner, ERDM also wins on long-horizon CRPS. On ERA5, ERDM is broadly competitive with the operational physics-based IFS ENS for mid-to-long-range lead times (≥5 days) but is slightly weaker on short-range forecasts (≤3 days), which the authors attribute to the EDM-based initialization. Against NeuralGCM ENS, ERDM matches or exceeds performance on CRPS and produces more physically realistic power spectra (matching IFS ENS, while NeuralGCM underestimates mid-to-high frequency energy). Against Graph-EFM, ERDM significantly outperforms on all four CRPS metrics reported in that study. Overall, ERDM wins or ties on every comparison except short-range ERA5 forecasts vs. IFS ENS.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate lines of work: the Elucidated Diffusion Model (EDM) framework, which provides principled noise schedules, network preconditioning, and second-order sampling, and Rolling Sequence Diffusion Models (RSDMs), which introduce progressively increasing noise levels across a prediction window to model growing uncertainty. Prior RSDMs (Ruhe et al., Wu et al.) were derived from DDPM and did not leverage EDM's design choices, while EDM-based forecasters like GenCast used uniform noise across the window. ERDM is the first to port EDM's full design—preconditioning, loss weighting, Heun sampler—into the rolling setting, adding a novel uncertainty-aware loss reweighting and a hybrid 2D U-Net + temporal attention architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ERDM provides a general, computationally efficient framework for probabilistic forecasting of complex dynamics, rivaling the operational IFS ENS and NeuralGCM ENS on ERA5 while requiring only 4 H200 GPUs and 5 days of training (vs. 128 TPUs and 10 days for NeuralGCM). Key open questions include scaling to higher resolutions (the 3D denoiser's memory cost is a bottleneck), reducing reliance on an external model for first-window initialization, and whether the explicit noise-level loss weighting can be replaced by more principled importance sampling. The finding that randomized training-time noise schedules (as in Diffusion Forcing) degrade performance by ~2× challenges a recent assumption in the sequence-diffusion literature.
