## Problem

MLLMs downscale high-resolution images, losing fine-grained visual details needed for VQA on small objects. Existing visual cropping methods that address this gap suffer from at least one of three limitations: requiring task-specific fine-tuning, relying on inefficient exhaustive tree search, or depending on full Q-K attention weights incompatible with FlashAttention. No prior method simultaneously achieves training-free operation, algorithmically efficient search, and compatibility with efficient attention implementations.

## Input / Output

The method takes a high-resolution RGB image (up to 8K, e.g., 7431×5358 px) and a multiple-choice VQA question as input. It internally constructs an object relevance map at the spatial resolution of the vision encoder's token grid (24×24 for LLaVA-1.5, or a higher-resolution h×w grid from local crops for LLaVA-OneVision), then outputs a ranked set of cropped image regions and a final VQA answer. For global-local-view MLLMs, the final inference uses text-image-interleaved input combining a highlighted global view with individual high-confidence local crops rather than a single composite image.

## Method

FOCUS is a training-free visual cropping pipeline that leverages the KV-cache of an MLLM to localize question-relevant image regions. It first extracts target objects from the question via in-context learning, then computes a V-V pseudo-attention map by measuring cosine similarity between the cached value features of target text tokens and visual tokens across multiple transformer layers (layers 14–32 for LLaVA-1.5), aggregated via attention rollout with residual connections. Element-wise multiplication across target tokens enforces consensus (e.g., 'red' ∩ 'car' → red cars only). Top-k anchor points on the resulting object relevance map seed ROI proposals that are expanded, filtered by non-maximum suppression, and ranked by an existence-confidence score obtained from a yes/no MLLM prompt. The key design choice—using value features from the KV-cache rather than Q-K attention weights—makes the method natively compatible with FlashAttention, since value features are always available during inference without materializing full attention matrices.

## Results

With LLaVA-1.5, FOCUS achieves 72.77% accuracy on V*Bench, 51.75% on HRBench-4K, and 44.13% on HRBench-8K using 13–15 forward passes (FPs). With LLaVA-OneVision, FOCUS reaches 92.15% on V*Bench, 71.13% on HRBench-4K, and 69.63% on HRBench-8K with 13–14 FPs. On MME-RealWorld-Lite, FOCUS improves LLaVA-OneVision's average perception accuracy from 52.01% to 54.15% and reasoning accuracy from 40.93% to 44.53%, while using only ~8 FPs per question versus ~42–46 for ZoomEye. Overall, FOCUS requires 3–6.5× fewer FPs than the best-performing baseline (ZoomEye) at comparable or superior accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ZoomEye (the strongest baseline), FOCUS with LLaVA-OneVision outperforms it on all three fine-grained datasets in both accuracy and efficiency: 92.15% vs. 91.10% on V*Bench, 71.13% vs. 69.38% on HRBench-4K, and 69.63% vs. 69.00% on HRBench-8K, while using roughly half the FPs (13 vs. 24–37). With LLaVA-1.5, ZoomEye achieves a higher peak accuracy on V*Bench (77.48% vs. 72.77%) and HRBench-8K (49.00% vs. 44.13%), but at 3.4× and 4.1× the forward passes respectively; on HRBench-4K, FOCUS actually surpasses ZoomEye (51.75% vs. 50.00%) with 3.4× fewer FPs. Against SEAL, FOCUS wins decisively on high-resolution benchmarks (51.75% vs. 34.50% on HRBench-4K; 44.13% vs. 33.50% on HRBench-8K) and is more efficient, while SEAL edges out FOCUS by <1 pp on V*Bench (73.68% vs. 72.77%) at nearly 2× the FPs. FOCUS also clearly outperforms both ViCrop and DC² across all three fine-grained datasets, with the largest margins on HRBench-4K (51.75% vs. 42.50% for ViCrop, 42.30% for DC²) and HRBench-8K (44.13% vs. 39.38% and 39.50%).

## Relation to Prior Work

FOCUS builds on the visual cropping paradigm for fine-grained VQA, departing from prior methods (SEAL, DC², ZoomEye, ViCrop) by replacing their reliance on fine-tuning, exhaustive tree search, or Q-K attention weights with a training-free, informed search guided by value features from the MLLM's KV-cache. Conceptually, it repurposes the KV-cache—an infrastructure component for autoregressive inference—as a spatial localization signal, computing a 'pseudo-attention' map via cosine similarity between target-object text tokens and visual tokens. The method also borrows the attention rollout mechanism [1] for cross-layer aggregation and follows ZoomEye's [31] type-1/type-2 question categorization and existence-confidence scoring strategy for final ROI selection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FOCUS offers a practically significant efficiency-accuracy trade-off: matching or exceeding ZoomEye's accuracy while requiring 3–6.5× fewer forward passes, and being natively compatible with FlashAttention—a constraint that rules out ViCrop and limits deployment of attention-weight-based methods. The training-free, architecture-agnostic design (validated on LLaVA-1.5, LLaVA-OneVision, and Qwen-2.5-VL) makes it broadly applicable. Key limitations remain: the object relevance map's spatial resolution is bounded by the vision encoder's grid size (hurting 8K performance on LLaVA-1.5), and the method inherits the base MLLM's weak spatial reasoning (e.g., 'left/right of image'). The broader implication—that MLLM internal representations already encode usable spatial information extractable at inference time—suggests a general recipe for spatial grounding beyond VQA.
