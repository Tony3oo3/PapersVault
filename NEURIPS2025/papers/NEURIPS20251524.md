## Problem

Standard neural network layers combine linear transformations with element-wise activation functions, which limits the model's ability to capture higher-order interactions between features. While polynomial transformations offer greater expressiveness, existing approaches introduce O(n²) or more additional parameters, making them impractical for modern architectures. This paper asks whether quadratic feature interactions can be injected into every linear layer with only negligible extra parameters and computation.

## Input / Output

The quadratic enhancer is a plug-in module that operates on intermediate hidden-state vectors within any neural network layer, not on raw inputs. For image classification it processes RGB images (e.g., 224×224 for ImageNet, 32×32 for CIFAR) through ViT backbones to produce class labels; for text it consumes token sequences (max length 256) through GPT-2 to yield sentiment/topic labels; for LLM fine-tuning it augments LoRA adapters on LLaMA models to produce text completions on commonsense reasoning benchmarks. The enhancer itself takes the linear projection output ŷ = Wx ∈ ℝ^d and returns a quadratically augmented vector of the same dimensionality, so it is dimension-preserving and can be attached to any linear layer without altering the network's input/output interface.

## Method

The quadratic enhancer augments a standard linear layer z = Wx + b by adding a quadratic term (Λŷ) ⊙ ŷ, where ŷ = Wx is the shared linear output and Λ ∈ ℝ^{d×d} is a band-sparse matrix of bandwidth k (typically k=1). Weight sharing (setting P = ΛW and Q = W) eliminates the need for separate quadratic weight matrices, reducing extra parameters from O(dn²) to O(kd). The band-sparse structure computes Λŷ as a sum of k shifted (rolled) copies of ŷ weighted by learnable vectors, making the operation a simple set of element-wise multiplications and additions. The shift r=0 is deliberately excluded to avoid square terms that cause numerical instability under FP16 training, leaving only cross-terms between nearest-neighbor neurons. The total relative overhead in both parameters and FLOPs is O(k/n), which is negligible for typical layer dimensions, and the module can be uniformly attached to every linear layer in ViT, GPT-2, or LoRA-adapted LLaMA models.

## Results

On image classification, ViT-T+QE achieves 75.15% top-1 accuracy on ImageNet (vs. 73.96% for ViT-T) and 89.27% average across six downstream datasets (vs. 88.57%). On text classification, GPT-2/32+QE reduces WikiText-2 perplexity from 4.57 to 4.44 and improves average classification accuracy from 83.15% to 84.01%. On LLM fine-tuning, LLaMA3-8B with LoRA/16+QE reaches 85.25% average commonsense reasoning accuracy, surpassing the full-rank LoRA/32 baseline (80.79%) while using half the trainable parameters (27.7M vs. 54.0M).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares QuadEnhancer against two existing quadratic MLP variants: QuadraNet [48] (y = W_a·x ⊙ W_b·x + W_c·x) and SwiGLU [40] (y = (W_1·x) ⊙ Sigmoid(W_1·x) ⊙ (W_2·x)), with parameter counts matched by adjusting hidden dimension. On the ViT-M backbone, QuadEnhancer achieves 82.40% average accuracy across four datasets, outperforming SwiGLU (81.13%) by 1.27% and QuadraNet (79.41%) by 2.99%, while using fewer parameters (2.47M vs. 2.58M and 2.53M respectively). Against the standard linear baselines (ViT, GPT-2, LoRA without QE), QuadEnhancer consistently improves performance across all three tasks with only ~0.1–0.2M additional parameters. Most strikingly, in LLM fine-tuning, LLaMA2-7B with LoRA/16+QE (27.6M params) achieves 80.25% average accuracy, exceeding the full-rank LoRA/32 baseline (53.5M params) at 77.61% by 2.64%, demonstrating that the quadratic enhancement can compensate for reduced adapter rank.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of polynomial neural networks (Chrysos et al. [4], CQNN [28]) and modern transformer architectures, but departs from prior quadratic methods by making the quadratic term a lightweight, drop-in enhancement rather than a replacement for the linear layer. Where QuadraNet [48] and SwiGLU [40] introduce additional weight matrices for quadratic interactions, QuadEnhancer reuses the existing linear weight W for both the linear and quadratic paths and constrains the remaining interaction matrix Λ to a band-sparse structure, reducing the parameter overhead to O(k/n) relative to the base layer. The approach is conceptually distinct from activation-function-based non-linearities (Swish, GELU, Mish) and module-based non-linearities (attention, gating) because it introduces inter-neuron quadratic interactions at every linear layer without altering the activation function or requiring task-specific architectural changes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical appeal is that the quadratic enhancer is architecture-agnostic and adds negligible parameters/FLOPs, making it a low-risk augmentation for any existing model. The LLM fine-tuning results—where LoRA/16+QE (27.6M trainable params) outperforms LoRA/32 (53.5M) by ~2.6% average accuracy on LLaMA2-7B—suggest quadratic interactions may provide a more efficient use of adapter capacity than simply increasing rank, though the absence of error bars and the reliance on prior-work baselines for the LoRA comparisons temper confidence. Key open questions include whether the gains persist at much larger scales (the authors acknowledge the study is 'quite preliminary'), whether the band-sparse structure with k=1 is optimal or whether wider bands help in deeper networks, and whether the numerical-stability motivation for excluding self-interaction (r=0) generalizes beyond FP16 training.
