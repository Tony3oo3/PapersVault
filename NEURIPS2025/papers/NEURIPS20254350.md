## Problem

Offline RL typically requires large, diverse datasets to mitigate epistemic uncertainty and distribution shift, yet collecting such data is costly or infeasible in many real-world domains. While prior work has addressed distribution shift through policy constraints, conservative regularization, or model-based uncertainty estimation, few methods explicitly target data efficiency—i.e., learning an optimal policy from as little offline data as possible. This paper asks whether a simple pretraining strategy can make existing offline RL algorithms substantially more data-efficient without architectural or algorithmic modifications.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a fixed offline dataset of transition tuples (s, a, r, s'), where s and s' are either low-dimensional state vectors (D4RL, Robomimic, ExoRL) or RGB images processed through a visual encoder (V-D4RL). During pretraining, only (s, a, s') tuples are used for next-state regression; during RL training, the full (s, a, r, s') tuples feed the chosen offline RL objective. The output is a deployable policy (actions) and Q-value estimates, with the key property that performance degrades gracefully as the dataset is reduced to 10%, 3%, or even 1% of its original size.

## Method

The paper proposes a shared Q-network with a backbone feature extractor h_φ and two shallow linear heads: a transition head g_ψ for next-state prediction and a Q-value head f_θ for value estimation, forming a dueling-style architecture. Training proceeds in two stages: first, h_φ is pretrained jointly with g_ψ via self-supervised next-state regression (minimizing MSE between predicted and actual s'), encouraging dynamics-relevant representations; second, h_φ is fine-tuned (or optionally frozen) alongside f_θ under any standard offline RL objective (TD3+BC, CQL, IQL, AWAC). The theoretical analysis via the projected Bellman equation under linear function approximation shows that pretraining increases the rank of the feature matrix H_φ, expanding its column space and tightening the upper bound on Q-value estimation error. Empirically, this manifests as higher latent-space rank and lower proxy Q-error, enabling the pretrained agent to outperform vanilla baselines trained on the full dataset using only 10% of the data.

## Results

On the D4RL benchmark, the proposed pretraining framework consistently improves all four offline RL baselines (AWAC, CQL, IQL, TD3+BC) across 15 environment-dataset combinations, with AWAC showing an average performance improvement of +140.37% over its original version. The most dramatic gains appear on low-quality data: CQL on Medium-Replay Hopper jumps from 28.6 to 98.6, and TD3+BC on Medium-Replay Hopper from 73.9 to 100.2. Critically, with only 10% of the dataset, the pretrained Q-network outperforms standard baselines trained on the full dataset, and on random datasets, 1% of data already surpasses full-data vanilla TD3+BC for HalfCheetah and Walker2d. On Robomimic, the method improves success rates in 7 out of 8 tasks, and on V-D4RL it consistently enhances DrQ+BC (e.g., Medium Cheetah Run: 340.33→379.80).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method is applied as a plug-and-play pretraining step on top of four model-free offline RL algorithms (AWAC, CQL, IQL, TD3+BC) and consistently improves all of them across D4RL, with the largest gains on low-quality data (e.g., CQL Medium-Replay Hopper: 28.6→98.6; TD3+BC Medium-Replay Walker2d: 58.0→92.0). On the Robomimic manipulation benchmark, the method improves success rates over BCQ and IRIS in 7 out of 8 tasks. On V-D4RL, it outperforms DrQ+BC on most settings (e.g., Medium Cheetah Run: 340.33→379.80; Medium Walker Walk: 306.93→338.77), though it slightly underperforms on Medium-Replay Humanoid Walk (40.76→19.38). Against offline model-based baselines (MOPO, MOBILE) and the representation-learning method ACL on reduced D4RL datasets, the proposed method maintains high performance at 1% data where competing methods degrade significantly. On ExoRL, TD3+Ours consistently outperforms vanilla TD3 across SMM, RND, and ICM collection strategies, even at 10% data, and on the point-mass maze with narrow state coverage, CQL+Ours significantly outperforms vanilla CQL for both short- and long-horizon goals.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of representation learning and offline RL, drawing on the dueling network architecture (Wang et al., 2016) to share a backbone between a transition-prediction head and a Q-value head. It departs from prior representation-learning approaches (Schwarzer et al., 2020, 2021) by replacing latent-space prediction with data augmentation and target encoders with a simple supervised next-state regression objective, and from model-based offline RL (MOPO, MOBILE) by using the transition model solely for feature pretraining rather than for planning or policy improvement. The theoretical grounding via the projected Bellman equation (Melo & Ribeiro, 2007) connects the pretraining benefit to an increase in the rank of the feature matrix, tightening the Q-value approximation bound. The method is explicitly designed as a plug-and-play module on top of existing offline RL algorithms (TD3+BC, CQL, IQL, AWAC), requiring only a few lines of code modification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is strong: a minimal architectural addition (one shallow prediction head plus a pretraining phase) consistently improves data efficiency across locomotion, manipulation, and vision-based offline RL benchmarks, with the headline result that 10% of the data with pretraining outperforms 100% without it. This addresses a real bottleneck in offline RL where data collection is costly or infeasible. Limitations include the focus on Q-learning-based algorithms (not policy-gradient or offline-to-online settings), and the theoretical analysis, while illuminating, is informal (no formal theorems or proofs). Open questions include whether the approach extends to goal-conditioned RL, real-world robotic control, and whether the gains persist under distribution shift between pretraining and fine-tuning data.
