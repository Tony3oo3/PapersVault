## Problem

Existing geometric GNNs for large biomolecules (RNA, proteins) face two critical limitations: they model molecules purely at the atomic level, ignoring the inherent hierarchical organization (atoms → residues → domains), and they rely on local Euclidean neighborhoods that cannot effectively capture long-range dependencies between spatially distant but structurally similar components. Moreover, prior methods operate exclusively in either Euclidean or Spherical Harmonics space, limiting their ability to simultaneously capture fine-scale atomic geometry and global symmetry-aware structural patterns.

## Input / Output

The model takes 3D atomic coordinates, atom-level features (e.g., atomic number), and residue-level features (nucleotide type for RNA, amino acid type for proteins) as input; for RNA tasks, eta-theta pseudotorsional backbone angles are used as the geometric input. Outputs are task-specific: nucleotide-level reactivity/degradation scores (RMSE), residue-level SASA values, or seven torsion angles (α, β, γ, δ, ε, ζ, χ) predicted as sin/cos pairs recovered via arctan. The architecture is E(3)-equivariant, so scalar predictions are rotation/translation-invariant while directional quantities transform consistently under rigid motions.

## Method

DualEquiNet jointly constructs complementary representations in Euclidean (EU) space (distance-cutoff neighborhoods for local geometry) and Spherical Harmonics (SH) space (cosine-similarity neighborhoods for long-range structural connections), then performs bidirectional cross-space message passing within each DualEqui layer using multi-head attention over both within-space and cross-space messages. A novel Cross-Space Interaction Pooling (CSIP) module hierarchically aggregates atom-level features into residue-level representations by injecting projected SH features into the EU coordinate update and projected relative EU coordinates into the SH feature update, enabling efficient multi-scale modeling without deep stacking. The full network applies L_atom DualEqui layers at the atomic scale, followed by CSIP, then L_res DualEqui layers at the residue scale, with residual connections throughout. E(3) equivariance is preserved by constructing all messages from invariant quantities (distances, degree-wise SH inner products) and updating equivariant features (coordinates, SH vectors) via linear combinations of equivariant basis elements weighted by invariant scalars.

## Results

On RNA property prediction (RMSE), DualEquiNet achieves 0.353 on CovidVaccine, 0.558 on Ribonanza, and 0.636 on Tc-ribo, outperforming the best baseline (EGNN+Pooling) by 4.6%, 8.7%, and 9.0% respectively. On the new SASA benchmarks, DualEquiNet reaches 31.97 RMSE on RNASolo (RNA) and 5.96 on mRFP (protein), beating the strongest prior methods (GotenNet and SchNet) by 8.6% and 28.8%. On Torsion Angle prediction, DualEquiNet achieves 15.87° average circular MAE, improving over GotenNet (16.27°) by 2.5%. The N-chain expressivity test shows DualEquiNet reaches 100% accuracy at d_SH=0.60 with as few as 5 layers, while most baselines remain at chance (50%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DualEquiNet outperforms all nine baselines across every evaluated task. Among the strongest competitors, GotenNet (ICLR 2025) is the closest non-hierarchical baseline, achieving 0.475 avg RMSE on COVID and 0.857 on Ribonanza, but DualEquiNet improves by 25.7% and 34.9% respectively by adding hierarchical CSIP pooling. FastEGNN (ICML 2025), which uses virtual nodes for global context, reaches 0.478 on COVID but still trails DualEquiNet by 26.2%. The long-range-specific baselines (SchNet+Ewald, SchNet+Neural Atom) achieve 0.478–0.495 on COVID, confirming that explicit long-range mechanisms alone are insufficient without the dual-space SH representation. On the new SASA benchmarks, DualEquiNet beats SchNet (8.37) by 28.8% on mRFP protein and GotenNet (34.97) by 8.6% on RNASolo RNA. On Torsion Angle prediction, DualEquiNet (15.87° avg) edges out GotenNet (16.27°) and GVPGNN (17.56°), winning on 5 of 7 individual angles. The N-chain expressivity test further shows that invariant (SchNet) and scalarization (EGNN, GVPGNN) methods are fundamentally limited at 50% accuracy, while DualEquiNet with d_SH≤0.70 achieves 100% even with fewer layers than the theoretical lower bound.

## Relation to Prior Work

DualEquiNet extends the line of efficient high-degree equivariant GNNs (HEGNN, GotenNet) that replace costly Clebsch-Gordan tensor products with inner-product scalarization on spherical harmonic features. Its key departure is the dual-space design: rather than operating in a single geometric space, it simultaneously maintains Euclidean (local) and Spherical Harmonics (global) representations with bidirectional cross-space message passing, addressing the long-range dependency limitation that plagues purely local equivariant GNNs. The Cross-Space Interaction Pooling mechanism further distinguishes it from prior hierarchical pooling approaches by explicitly exchanging information between EU and SH spaces during atom-to-residue aggregation, rather than treating the two spaces independently.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in biomolecular modeling: existing equivariant GNNs struggle to simultaneously capture fine-grained atomic geometry and long-range structural dependencies in large molecules (thousands of atoms) without prohibitive computational cost. The dual-space framework and hierarchical CSIP offer a principled way to expand receptive fields without deep stacking, which is directly relevant to scaling geometric deep learning to proteomics and RNA therapeutics. However, performance depends on the quality of input 3D structures (generated by RhoFold or AlphaFold), and the authors acknowledge that integrating structure prediction into the pipeline remains open. The introduced RNA SASA and Torsion Angle benchmarks fill a gap in 3D structural evaluation for RNA, a modality historically underrepresented in geometric GNN benchmarks.
