## Problem

Sublinear algorithms for multivariate mean estimation had a gap between the optimal ε^{-1} sample complexity (Inaba et al., 1994) and the optimal log δ^{-1} high-probability dependence (Cohen-Addad et al., 2021), with no prior method achieving both simultaneously while also matching the lower bound on running time. This paper closes that gap by providing simple algorithms with O(ε^{-1} log δ^{-1}) sample complexity, a matching lower bound, and linear or near-linear running time in the sample size.

## Input / Output

The algorithm receives a d-dimensional point set A of n points (accessed only via uniform random sampling, never in full) along with approximation parameter ε and failure probability δ. It draws O(ε^{-1} log δ^{-1}) uniform samples, partitions them into O(log δ^{-1}) subsamples of size O(ε^{-1}), and outputs a (1+ε)-approximate mean vector c ∈ R^d whose sum-of-squared-distances cost is within a (1+ε) factor of the optimal. The running time is O(ε^{-1} · d · log δ^{-1}) for the coordinate-wise median and gradient descent variants, and O((ε^{-1} + log^γ δ^{-1}) · d · log δ^{-1}) for the order-statistics variant.

## Method

All three proposed algorithms share a common sampling-and-aggregation framework: draw O(ε^{-1} log δ^{-1}) uniform samples, split into O(log δ^{-1}) groups of size O(ε^{-1}), compute each group's empirical mean, then apply an aggregation subroutine. The first aggregator is the coordinate-wise median of the sample means, computable in linear time. The second is the geometric median of the sample means, computed via a novel gradient descent (FASTGD) that at each step projects all sample means onto the current gradient direction and takes the 1-dimensional median along that line, achieving linear time where prior geometric median algorithms require O(log^4 δ^{-1} · d). The third, MinSumSelect, uses a recursive order-statistics approach: it identifies a good mean as one minimizing the sum of distances to its 7/10-th closest neighbors, and applies this statistic recursively over √|P|-sized clusters to reduce the naive quadratic running time. The key theoretical insight underpinning all three is that at least 7/10 of the sample means are 'good' (within radius r of the true mean) with probability 1−δ, and each aggregator provably selects a point within O(r) of the true mean.

## Results

The paper establishes that a (1+ε)-approximate Euclidean mean can be found with probability 1−δ using O(ε^{-1} log δ^{-1}) samples, with a matching lower bound, and all three proposed estimators (Coordinate-Wise Median-of-Means, Geometric Median-of-Means via FASTGD, and MinSumSelect) achieve this optimal sample complexity with running time O(ε^{-1} · d log δ^{-1}). In experiments on MNIST (60,000×784), Fashion-MNIST (60,000×784), and CoverType (581,012×54), MinSumSelect and Coordinate-Wise Median are consistently the fastest algorithms, while FASTGD shows a slight accuracy edge over Coordinate-Wise Median in most cases. The empirical mean, despite having the weakest theoretical guarantees (requiring Ω(ε^{-1}δ^{-1}) samples for high-probability guarantees), consistently ranks among the best in accuracy on real-world data and is the fastest to compute.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to the CSS algorithm (Cohen-Addad et al., NeurIPS 2021), the proposed methods are substantially faster due to CSS's extensive filtering and preprocessing overhead, while remaining competitive or superior in accuracy; CSS is among the slowest methods tested across all sample sizes. Against the WY algorithm (Woodruff and Yasuda, ICML 2024), the proposed methods again win on runtime and the WY algorithm performs worse in terms of accuracy, suggesting its stated sample complexity bounds may not be tightly analyzed. The empirical mean, while not a formal baseline in the theoretical sense, consistently achieves accuracy comparable to or better than the proposed robust estimators on the tested real-world datasets (MNIST, Fashion-MNIST, CoverType), though the paper notes this is not guaranteed in adversarial settings. Among the three proposed methods, FASTGD (geometric median-of-means with gradient descent) almost never underperforms Coordinate-Wise Median and shows a slight accuracy edge, while MinSumSelect matches both in accuracy at the fastest runtime. The key practical takeaway is that the proposed algorithms are always competitive in accuracy with prior methods while being among the fastest available, with the gradient refinement step in FASTGD offering measurable but modest improvements over the simpler Coordinate-Wise Median initialization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of sublinear algorithms and robust statistics, building directly on Inaba et al.'s [1994] expectation-based sample complexity result for the empirical mean and Cohen-Addad et al.'s [2021] subsample-and-aggregate framework with improved probability dependence. It resolves the open problem of achieving the optimal O(ε^{-1} log δ^{-1}) sample complexity (matching the Ω(ε^{-1}) lower bound of Cohen-Addad et al.) while simultaneously achieving linear running time, a barrier that previous algorithms (Musco et al. [2022], Woodruff and Yasuda [2024]) could not break due to their O(log² δ^{-1}) or dimension-dependent overheads. The three proposed aggregation procedures—coordinate-wise median-of-means, a novel linear-time gradient descent for the geometric median, and an order-statistics-based MinSumSelect—represent distinct algorithmic strategies for the same optimal sampling budget.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper closes the gap between upper and lower bounds for sublinear mean estimation with high probability, providing the first algorithms that are simultaneously optimal in sample complexity and running time. The dimension-free convergence rate for the coordinate-wise median-of-means (Theorem 3.2) offers a practical improvement over the standard union-bound analysis that implies a log d dependence, which is relevant for high-dimensional distributional mean estimation. A notable practical finding is that the empirically simplest estimator (the raw empirical mean), despite having the worst theoretical guarantees (Ω(ε^{-1} δ^{-1}) samples), consistently performs competitively on real-world data, suggesting that worst-case adversarial instances may be rare in practice. Open questions include whether the geometric median-of-means can be computed in truly sublinear time for the geometric median objective (z=1), and whether the optimal sample complexity extends to other power-mean objectives with tighter constants.
