## Problem

Existing multi-modal prompt learning methods for vision-language models rely on fixed, shared prompts applied uniformly across all samples, making them deterministic and unable to capture instance-level variation or model uncertainty. While recent variational approaches (e.g., Bayesian Prompt Learning, Any-Shift Prompting) introduce uncertainty modeling, they are restricted to text-only prompts with global latent variables and standard Gaussian priors that fail to capture fine-grained token-level semantics or inter-class structure, limiting generalization in few-shot and out-of-distribution settings.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image (processed by a frozen CLIP ViT-B/16 encoder) and a fixed text template (e.g., 'a photo of a [CLASS]') as input. The frozen image feature conditions the generation of sample-specific text prompts injected at multiple transformer layers (J=5, H=7 with M=5 tokens per layer for MMRL-style; J=0, H=9 with M=2 for MaPLe-style), while static shared vision prompts are inserted into the image encoder. At inference, S=10 Monte Carlo samples from the learned posterior are drawn and their predictions averaged to produce the final class label via cosine similarity in the shared embedding space.

## Method

VaMP formulates multi-modal prompt tuning as variational inference over latent prompt representations with three key components. First, sample-specific text prompts are generated by layer-specific MLPs conditioned on the frozen CLIP image embedding, enabling instance-adaptive, hierarchical modulation across multiple transformer layers, while vision-side prompts remain shared and deterministic. Second, each text prompt token is treated as a latent variable with a Gaussian posterior q_φ(z_i|x) parameterized by per-layer MLPs, optimized end-to-end via the reparameterization trick and an ELBO objective. Third, a class-aware prior p_ψ(z_i|o_y) replaces the standard N(0,I), where the prior parameters are predicted from class prototypes (averaged training features per class) through separate prior networks, providing semantic regularization that encourages intra-class consistency in the latent prompt space. At test time, the standard prior is used since class labels are unavailable, and predictions are obtained by averaging over S Monte Carlo samples.

## Results

On the base-to-novel generalization benchmark (11 datasets, 16-shot), VaMP achieves 86.45% base accuracy, 78.67% novel accuracy, and 82.37% harmonic mean, outperforming the strongest baseline MMRL (85.68/77.16/81.20) by +0.77/+1.51/+1.17. On domain generalization (ImageNet → 4 variants), VaMP reaches 61.73% average target accuracy, surpassing MMRL by +1.20%, with the largest gain on ImageNet-A (+0.77). On cross-dataset transfer (ImageNet → 10 unseen datasets), VaMP attains 67.74% average accuracy, exceeding MMRL by +0.49%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VaMP consistently outperforms all 19 baselines on the novel-class accuracy metric across the 11-dataset base-to-novel benchmark, with the largest margins over text-only prompt methods (CoOp: +15.45% novel, CoCoOp: +6.98% novel) and moderate gains over multi-modal baselines (MaPLe: +3.53%, PromptSRC: +2.57%, MMRL: +1.51%). On domain generalization, VaMP wins on all four target domains (ImageNet-V2, -Sketch, -A, -R), with the most notable advantage on ImageNet-A (+0.77% over MMRL) where natural adversarial examples stress robustness. On cross-dataset transfer, VaMP leads on 9 of 10 target datasets, with the clearest gain on EuroSAT (+0.72% over MMRL) and FGVCAircraft (+0.46%). The only dataset where VaMP does not lead on novel accuracy is OxfordPets (95.24% vs. SkipT's 97.87%), though it still achieves the best harmonic mean. Ablations confirm that each component—sample-specific generation, variational modeling, and class-aware prior—contributes incrementally, with the variational step adding ~0.5–1.0% novel accuracy over deterministic prompts.

## Relation to Prior Work

VaMP extends the multi-modal prompt learning lineage established by MaPLe (shallow-layer, J=0/H=9) and MMRL (deep-layer, J=5/H=7) by replacing their fixed, deterministic prompt tokens with sample-specific latent variables inferred via variational inference. It departs from prior variational prompt methods (Bayesian Prompt Learning, Any-Shift Prompting) in two key ways: (1) it applies token-wise variational modeling across multiple intermediate transformer layers rather than a single global latent, and (2) it conditions the posterior on visual features while using a class-prototype-based prior instead of a standard Gaussian, thereby incorporating both instance-level and category-level semantics into the prompt distribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work demonstrates that uncertainty-aware, instance-conditioned prompt generation consistently improves few-shot and out-of-distribution generalization over deterministic multi-modal prompts, with the class-aware prior providing meaningful semantic regularization. However, the gains over the strong MMRL baseline are modest (≈1–2% on novel classes), and the method still requires access to class labels during training to construct prototypes, limiting its applicability to truly label-free or zero-shot adaptation. Open questions include whether the variational formulation offers advantages in extreme low-shot (1–2 shot) regimes, whether class-aware priors can be bootstrapped without any training data, and whether the Monte Carlo inference overhead (S=10 forward passes) is acceptable in latency-sensitive deployment.
