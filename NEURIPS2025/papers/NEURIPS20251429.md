## Problem

Existing model editing methods are evaluated under a single-edit assumption, yet real-world questions (e.g., multi-symptom diagnosis, multi-law reasoning) require simultaneously composing multiple independently trained edits. The paper defines Compositional Model Editing (CME) and identifies three systematic failure modes in current methods: knowledge loss (partial answers missing), incorrect preceding (wrong answers generated before correct ones), and knowledge sinking (context-irrelevant content generated after prior answers, breaking the generation chain for subsequent answers).
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251429/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

During edit training, the method consumes individual (question, answer) pairs and produces a LoRA pair (A, B) with an adaptive binary mask M, stored alongside a key vector K in a vector database. At inference, a compositional question is encoded into a hidden state h_db, which retrieves relevant edits from the vector database via threshold γ and top-p selection; the retrieved edits are then adaptively merged to produce a single compositional text answer containing all relevant edited facts. The composition number (how many edits must be jointly utilized) is configurable, and the method supports both multi-answer composition (one question, multiple answers) and multi-question composition (multiple questions merged into one).

## Method

A³E is a dual-stage framework built on the insight that pre-trained foundation knowledge in the FFN down-projection matrix W_down is sufficient for composing new knowledge, so only W_up needs to be edited. In the edit training stage, Adaptive Combination applies a low-rank update (A, B) to W_up with an adaptive mask M that selects the top-k most relevant rows of W_down (via dot product with answer-token embeddings), preserving composability while maintaining learning capacity. Two regularization terms are added: L_c penalizes the L2 norm of the hidden-state change at the last answer token to ensure context-preserving generation of subsequent answers, and L_o suppresses non-label logits to prevent incorrect preceding. In the edit composing stage, a vector database retrieves question-relevant edits, and Adaptive Merging resolves conflicts by zeroing out elements of Δa that have opposite signs across different edits (inspired by TIES-Merging), preventing destructive interference while preserving complementary knowledge.

## Results

On the most challenging compositional task (MAC, 3898 edits) with PEAK-CF, A³E achieves 43.38% SR-S, 70.72% SR-1, and 39.48% GSR, surpassing the strongest baseline MELO (18.22% SR-S) by 25.16 points. On PEAK-T MAC, A³E reaches 38.00% SR-S versus MELO's 6.96%, a 31-point gap. For multi-question composition (MQC) on PEAK-T, A³E achieves 28.63% SR-S compared to MELO's 2.82% and GRACE's 4.84%. Across all four CME tasks and both datasets, A³E improves composability by at least 22.45% over the best baseline without degrading non-compositional editing performance (SR-1, GSR, LSR remain on par with baselines).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Full-matrix editing methods (ROME, KE, MEND) are essentially non-functional on compositional tasks, achieving near-zero SR-S on MAC and MQC across both datasets, confirming severe knowledge loss when multiple edits must be jointly utilized. AlphaEdit, which projects updates into null spaces to prevent interference, achieves 6.98% SR-S on PEAK-CF MAC but its orthogonality strategy collapses when a single question depends on multiple edits. WISE shows strong independent performance (39.99% SR-S on IMAC) but degrades catastrophically to 0.10% SR-S on MAC, as random mask overlap grows with edit count. Among memory-based methods, GRACE (12.99% SR-S) and MELO (18.22% SR-S) are the strongest baselines on PEAK-CF MAC, but both suffer from incorrect preceding and knowledge sinking. A³E outperforms MELO by 25.16 points in SR-S on PEAK-CF MAC and by 31.04 points on PEAK-T MAC, while maintaining comparable locality (LSR 25.76 vs. GRACE's 27.19 on PEAK-CF). On the independent variants (IMAC/IMQC) where the vector database is bypassed, A³E still leads by 9–22 points over WISE, confirming that the gains come from the adaptive combination and regularization mechanisms rather than retrieval alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the key-value memory interpretation of Transformer FFN layers (Geva et al., Dai et al.) to argue that the down-projection matrix W_down stores composable 'foundation knowledge,' and that editing only W_up (i.e., recombining these foundation elements via a low-rank LoRA-style update) is sufficient for model editing while improving composability. It departs from prior model editing methods (ROME, MEND, AlphaEdit, WISE, GRACE, MELO, T-patcher) that either modify W_down directly or both matrices, by showing this approach causes knowledge loss, incorrect preceding, and knowledge sinking when multiple edits must be jointly utilized. The adaptive merging mechanism draws on TIES-Merging's sign-conflict resolution for model merging, repurposing it for composing multiple low-rank edit updates at inference time via a vector database retrieval scheme inherited from GRACE and MELO.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By defining and benchmarking compositional model editing (CME), this paper exposes a critical gap between how editing methods are evaluated (one edit in isolation) and how they must function in practice (multiple edits jointly answering a single question), which is essential for real-world deployment in domains like medicine and law. The three identified failure modes provide a useful diagnostic taxonomy for future editors. However, absolute performance remains modest (best SR-S ~50% on PEAK-CF), experiments are limited to two datasets and two 7-8B backbones, and the paper acknowledges that instruction-tuned models and larger composition numbers remain unexplored. Open questions include whether the W_up-only editing principle generalizes to larger models, whether the vector database retrieval degrades gracefully at scale, and how CME interacts with instruction-following behavior in modern LLMs.
