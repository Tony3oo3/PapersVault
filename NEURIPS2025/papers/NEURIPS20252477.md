## Problem

Dataset distillation for 3D point clouds remains largely unexplored because point clouds are unordered and exhibit arbitrary orientations, making direct feature comparison between different 3D models unreliable. Existing approaches either ignore these 3D-specific characteristics (e.g., PCC simply applies image-based gradient matching) or suffer from high computational complexity. The paper tackles two intertwined challenges: semantic misalignment (semantically similar regions have inconsistent point indices across models) and rotational variation (objects captured under arbitrary poses increase intra-class feature variability).

## Input / Output

The method takes a large original 3D point cloud dataset (each sample is 1,024 points in ℝ³ with a class label) and produces a synthetic dataset of 1, 3, or 10 point cloud objects per class (PPC), where each synthetic object has both an optimized geometric shape and an optimized orientation (three rotation angles θx, θy, θz). The synthetic objects are initialized by randomly selecting samples from the original dataset and are optimized using a randomly initialized, untrained PointNet as the feature extractor, so no trained model is required during distillation.

## Method

The core contribution is a Semantically Aligned Distribution Matching (SADM) loss combined with joint rotation optimization. SADM addresses semantic misalignment by sorting point-wise feature values within each of the 1,024 channels in descending order before computing an MMD-based Gaussian kernel distance, so that features of similar semantic importance (e.g., edges, corners) are aligned across different 3D models regardless of their original point ordering. The loss has two weighted components: L_α over all sorted features and L_β over only the top-1 feature per channel to emphasize the most semantically dominant regions. To handle rotational variation, three learnable rotation angles are jointly optimized with the synthetic point cloud shapes by minimizing L_SADM(T, R_θ(S)), where R_θ applies the rotation to the synthetic set. A proposition guarantees that this joint optimization over (S, θ) yields a loss no greater than optimizing S alone, since the zero-rotation case is a special case of the joint problem.

## Results

At the standard PPC=3 setting, the proposed SADM method achieves 84.4% on ModelNet10, 72.1% on ModelNet40, 63.7% on ShapeNet, and 31.6% on ScanObjectNN, consistently outperforming all distribution-matching and gradient-matching distillation baselines. Compared to the closest distillation baseline (DC), the method gains +9.4 points on ModelNet10, +5.5 on ModelNet40, +3.6 on ShapeNet, and +7.3 on ScanObjectNN. Training time is only 0.26 h on ModelNet40 (vs. 5.43 h for DC), confirming the efficiency advantage of the distribution-matching paradigm. Cross-architecture generalization (Table 2) shows the method retains 68.5% on ModelNet40 with PointNet++ and 61.6% with Point Transformer, far exceeding DM (14.1% and 26.7%) and DC (46.9% and 47.5%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method decisively outperforms both distribution-matching (DM) and gradient-matching (DC) distillation baselines across all four datasets and all PPC values; at PPC=3, gains range from +3.6 (ShapeNet) to +9.4 (ModelNet10) over DC, and from +7.6 (ModelNet10) to +8.3 (ScanObjectNN) over DM. Against the point-cloud-specific PCC method [28] (equivalent to DC with Herding initialization), the proposed method achieves 61.54% vs. 57.43% on the ShapeNet→ModelNet40 cross-dataset transfer and 54.40% vs. 49.42% on the reverse direction. Trajectory-matching baselines MTT [3] and DataDAM [18] perform near random selection (e.g., 76.73% and 76.60% vs. 84.96% for Ours on ModelNet10 at PPC=3), indicating they fail to converge effectively on point clouds. Among coreset selection methods, Herding is the strongest baseline and slightly exceeds the proposed method on ShapeNet (66.9% vs. 63.7%) and ScanObjectNN (34.8% vs. 31.6%) at PPC=3, but the proposed method surpasses it on ModelNet10 (+6.4) and ModelNet40 (+3.6), and pulls ahead at PPC=10 on all datasets. The ablation (Table 7) confirms that removing the semantic alignment component drops performance to near-random levels (e.g., 59.96% vs. 72.08% on ModelNet40), validating that the SADM sorting is the critical differentiator over standard MMD-based matching.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the distribution-matching framework for dataset distillation (DM, Zhao et al. WACV 2023) from structured data (images, text) to 3D point clouds, a modality where the unordered and irregular nature of points breaks the assumptions underlying standard feature comparison. The key methodological departure is the SADM loss, which sorts point-wise features within each channel before computing the MMD-based kernel distance, thereby establishing a semantic correspondence that is absent in raw point indices. The paper also introduces learnable rotation parameters jointly optimized with the synthetic geometry, a component not present in prior distillation work. The only prior 3D point cloud distillation method (PCC, Zhang et al. ACM MM 2024) simply transplanted gradient matching (DC) without addressing point-cloud-specific challenges, and the authors position their approach as the first distribution-matching-based method for this modality.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first distribution-matching dataset distillation method tailored to 3D point clouds, and the clean separation of the two challenges (semantic misalignment via sorting, rotational variation via learnable angles) provides a reusable recipe for future work on unordered data. The strong cross-architecture generalization (e.g., 82.9% on ModelNet10 with PointNet++ vs. 35.0% for DM) suggests the distilled datasets capture architecture-agnostic geometric structure rather than PointNet-specific features. Limitations acknowledged by the authors include imperfect semantic alignment (sorting is a proxy, not a true correspondence) and added complexity from rotation optimization on already-aligned datasets. Open questions include whether the channel-wise sorting generalizes to other unordered modalities (e.g., graphs, sets), whether the approach extends to 3D segmentation or detection tasks, and how it scales to point clouds with significantly more than 1,024 points.
