## Problem

Existing multimodal time-series learning frameworks suffer from three key limitations: they either bind all modalities to a single anchor modality (risking over-reliance), model interactions only pairwise (becoming combinatorially intractable with ≥4 modalities), or assume complete modality observations (unrealistic given frequent sensor failures). No general framework exists that automatically discovers task-relevant intra- and cross-modal dependencies while gracefully handling arbitrary combinations of available sensors.

## Input / Output

The method consumes multimodal time series where each of M modalities (5–17 in practice) has its own sampling rate, number of variates, and sequence length, and the available modality subset S_i can differ per sample due to sensor dropout. Inputs are first compressed via SAX symbolic tokenization (shared alphabet size α=20, compression factor W=2) with a reserved symbol s_0 encoding missingness, so the model natively handles partial observations without imputation. The output is a single class label from C categories, produced by averaging logits from the top-k selected MoE experts.

## Method

MAESTRO processes each modality through a dedicated sparse self-attention encoder whose sparsity budget u is adaptively allocated by a learned gating function conditioned on the modality-availability vector, allowing task-relevant modalities to receive more attention capacity. Modality-specific embeddings are concatenated along the temporal axis (preserving heterogeneous sequence lengths) with modality and positional embeddings, then processed by a sparse cross-modal multi-head attention layer that reduces complexity from O(L̂²) to O(L̂ log L̂) while enabling time-varying cross-attention across all modalities simultaneously. The resulting tokens are routed through a Sparse Mixture-of-Experts (Ω=4 experts, k=1) whose implicit specialization is encouraged by a curriculum-based modality dropout schedule (linearly increasing dropout probability during training) rather than explicit load-balancing losses. A theoretical corollary extends the SAX MINDIST lower-bound to the cross-modal setting, showing that symbolic discretization preserves relative sample-similarity contrasts across modalities within bounded approximation error.

## Results

Under full modality observability, MAESTRO achieves 0.77 accuracy on WESAD (stress classification), 0.83 on DaliaHAR (activity recognition), 0.77 on DSADS (19-class activity), and 0.30 macro-F1 on MIMIC-III (clinical diagnosis), yielding average relative improvements of 8% over the best multivariate baseline and 4% over the best multimodal baseline. Under 40% modality missingness, MAESTRO achieves a 9.4% absolute accuracy improvement over the best baseline and outperforms the strongest missingness-resilient multimodal method (FlexMoE/FuseMoE) by an average 59% relative F1 gain. In terms of efficiency, MAESTRO requires only 6.13 GFLOPs and 1.39M parameters—roughly 200% fewer GFLOPs than the pairwise MULT baseline—while delivering the highest accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MAESTRO outperforms all 10 baselines across all four datasets. Against the strongest multivariate baselines, iTransformer (0.67 on WESAD) and Transformer (0.63), MAESTRO gains 8% and 22% relative accuracy respectively on WESAD, and 5% on DaliaHAR (0.83 vs. 0.79). Among multimodal baselines, the closest competitors are FlexMoE (0.71 WESAD, 0.79 DaliaHAR, 0.74 DSADS, 0.27 MIMIC-F1) and ShaSpec (0.62 WESAD, 0.77 DaliaHAR, 0.71 DSADS, 0.24 MIMIC-F1); MAESTRO exceeds FlexMoE by 8% relative on WESAD and by 11% relative macro-F1 on MIMIC-III (0.30 vs. 0.27). The pairwise MULT baseline, while competitive on DaliaHAR (0.78), is substantially outperformed on WESAD (0.60 vs. 0.77) and requires ~200% more GFLOPs (26.65 vs. 6.13). Under 40% modality missingness, MAESTRO's advantage widens: it surpasses the modality-dropout-adapted Transformer by 25% relative F1 and the best native missingness-resilient method (FlexMoE) by 59% relative F1, confirming that its symbolic missingness encoding, adaptive attention budgeting, and sparse MoE routing collectively provide robustness that no single baseline mechanism replicates.

## Relation to Prior Work

MAESTRO synthesizes three established components—SAX symbolic tokenization (Lin et al., 2007), Informer-style sparse attention (Zhou et al., 2021), and sparsely-gated MoE (Shazeer et al., 2017)—into a unified architecture specifically designed for heterogeneous multimodal time series with arbitrary modality availability. It departs from prior multimodal time-series work in two key ways: (1) it rejects both the single-anchor-modality binding paradigm (ImageBind, MMBind) and the pairwise cross-modal interaction modeling (MULT, FlexMoE) in favor of a concatenated long-sequence with sparse cross-modal attention, and (2) it introduces a reserved symbolic token for missingness and an adaptive per-modality attention budget, neither of which appears in prior sensor-fusion or multimodal transformer literature. The loss-free MoE optimization via curriculum-based modality dropout is a practical departure from standard MoE training that relies on auxiliary load-balancing losses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuinely underexplored practical gap: real-world wearable and clinical sensing systems routinely produce 5–17 heterogeneous modalities with arbitrary sensor failures, yet most multimodal learning frameworks assume either complete observations or a fixed modality set. MAESTRO's O(L log L) sparse cross-modal attention and symbolic tokenization offer a scalable path for long, multi-sensor sequences without the combinatorial cost of pairwise modeling. Key limitations include handling only complete (not partial) modality missingness, evaluation on four datasets (with MIMIC showing modest absolute gains due to task difficulty), and the reliance on a shared SAX alphabet across semantically distinct modalities. Open questions include extending to irregularly sampled and asynchronous sensing streams, and whether the implicit MoE specialization generalizes to substantially larger modality counts.
