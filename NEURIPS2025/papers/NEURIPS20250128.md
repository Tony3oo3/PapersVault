## Problem

While RLVR-trained LLMs excel at math and coding, they still fail at puzzles solvable by humans without domain knowledge, and existing puzzle datasets lack the diversity, scalability, and difficulty controllability needed for effective RL training. ENIGMATA addresses this by providing the first comprehensive suite of 36 puzzle tasks across 7 reasoning categories, each paired with an automated generator and rule-based verifier that plugs directly into the RLVR pipeline.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250128/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each puzzle is a single-turn text prompt specifying rules, constraints, and a partially filled structure (grid, graph, sequence, or cipher), with difficulty controlled by parameters like grid size, mask rate, or node count. The model produces a structured solution (filled grid, move sequence, decrypted text, or path) in a prescribed format, which is then scored by a task-specific rule-based verifier returning a binary correct/incorrect reward. The generator can emit unlimited instances at any difficulty level, enabling fully automatic RL data supply without human annotation.

## Method

Training follows a two-stage recipe: (1) Rejection Fine-Tuning (RFT) on 1:1 puzzle-to-math data, where DeepSeek-R1 generates 8 candidate solutions per puzzle and only correct ones are retained for SFT; (2) multi-task RL using VC-PPO (a PPO variant with removed KL penalty, value pretraining, and decoupled GAE with λ_critic=1.0 and λ_policy=0.95) over 425 steps with batch size 4096. Two multi-task strategies are explored: Mix-Training RL (all tasks simultaneously) and Multi-Stage RL (curriculum: first ARC-AGI + AIME, then introduce ENIGMATA-Data while retaining earlier data). Difficulty is controlled via pass@k-based tiering (Easy/Medium/Hard) with a balanced 1:1:1 ratio shown to outperform skewed distributions. The approach generalizes from a 32B dense model to a 20B/200B MoE model, where synthetic puzzle data yields measurable gains on AIME and GPQA Diamond.

## Results

Qwen2.5-32B-ENIGMATA achieves 62.6% on ENIGMATA-Eval, 32.8% on ARC-AGI 1, and 0.6% on ARC-AGI 2, surpassing o3-mini-high (59.9/25.8/0.4) and o1 (54.9/29.0/0.4) on all three puzzle benchmarks. On the out-of-domain KOR-Bench, the model scores 65.0%, and on AIME 2024 it retains 60.6% mathematical reasoning. When scaled to the 20B/200B MoE Seed1.5-Thinking backbone, adding ENIGMATA data during RL yields consistent gains: +0.8 on AIME 2024, +1.9 on AIME 2025, +0.4 on BeyondAIME, and +0.8 on GPQA Diamond over the unmodified Seed1.5-Thinking baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Qwen2.5-32B-ENIGMATA clearly outperforms o3-mini-high and o1 on all three in-domain puzzle benchmarks (ENIGMATA-Eval: 62.6 vs 59.9/54.9; ARC-AGI 1: 32.8 vs 25.8/29.0; ARC-AGI 2: 0.6 vs 0.4/0.4), and also beats DeepSeek-R1, Gemini 2.5 Pro, and Claude-3.7-Sonnet-Thinking on ENIGMATA-Eval and ARC-AGI 1. However, it trails o4-mini-high on every metric (e.g., 62.6 vs 65.1 on ENIGMATA-Eval, 32.8 vs 54.7 on ARC-AGI 1) and loses to Claude-3.7-Sonnet-Thinking on ARC-AGI 1 (32.8 vs 37.6) and ARC-AGI 2 (0.6 vs 1.4). On out-of-domain KOR-Bench, the model (65.0%) underperforms o4-mini-high (72.7%), o3-mini-high (69.6%), and DeepSeek-R1 (71.7%), indicating that puzzle-specific training does not fully transfer to knowledge-orthogonal reasoning. On AIME 2024, the model (60.6%) is competitive with DeepSeek-R1 (60.2%) and Claude-3.7-Sonnet-Thinking (60.3%) but well below o4-mini-high (92.3%) and Gemini 2.5 Pro (90.3%), showing that the 32B model preserves but does not enhance mathematical reasoning. The scaling experiment with Seed1.5-Thinking (20B/200B MoE) demonstrates that ENIGMATA data provides a small but consistent 'free lunch' across AIME 2024/2025, BeyondAIME, and GPQA Diamond, with the largest gain of +1.9 points on AIME 2025.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the RLVR paradigm—previously applied to math, STEM, and coding—into the domain of purely logical puzzle reasoning, building directly on VC-PPO as its core optimization algorithm. Prior puzzle-focused work (KOR-Bench, ZebraLogic, SearchBench, Logic-RL) either served as evaluation-only benchmarks or targeted a single puzzle type, leaving a gap in comprehensive, multi-task, RL-ready puzzle resources. ENIGMATA fills this gap by providing 36 generator-verifier pairs across 7 reasoning categories, enabling the first systematic multi-task RLVR training for diverse logical puzzles. The two-stage RFT-then-RL training recipe follows the broader DeepSeek-R1/Seed1.5-Thinking lineage, with the key departure being the use of knowledge-orthogonal synthetic puzzles as the primary RL signal.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ENIGMATA provides the first unified, scalable, and automatically verifiable puzzle suite for RLVR training, giving the community a practical resource to study and improve logical reasoning in LLMs beyond math and code. The 'free lunch' finding—that 20K synthetic puzzle examples improve AIME, BeyondAIME, and GPQA Diamond on a 200B MoE model—suggests that knowledge-orthogonal reasoning data may transfer to domain-specific tasks, though this effect was not observed at the 32B scale and remains under-evidenced. Key limitations include the restriction to single-turn textual puzzles (no visual or multi-turn formats), reliance on a single RL algorithm (VC-PPO), and the modest absolute gains on the most challenging benchmarks (ARC-AGI 2 at 0.6%). Open questions include whether the generalization benefit scales with model size, whether more diverse or harder puzzle types would yield larger transfer effects, and whether the difficulty-control mechanism can be automated end-to-end.
