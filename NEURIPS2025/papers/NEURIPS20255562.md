## Problem

Traditional online mixture-of-experts algorithms (e.g., EXP4) are guaranteed to converge to the best single expert in hindsight, but they cannot capture scenarios where collective aggregation—majority voting, weighted consensus—outperforms any individual expert. This paper asks whether one can design online no-regret algorithms that learn the optimal committee of experts and the optimal aggregation strategy (egalitarian or weighted voting) simultaneously, bridging online bandit learning with social choice theory.

## Input / Output

At each timestep, a context x is drawn from a distribution P(x) and passed to a candidate set of experts, each producing a prediction from a potentially heterogeneous prediction space C_i; a standardizer maps all predictions to a unified output space C. The scoring function G (binary or multi-class) evaluates the aggregated output and provides full-bandit feedback. The algorithm outputs an aggregated prediction via majority voting, the optimal expert committee E*, and—under weighted voting—a set of voting weights θ whose L1 norm is constrained to 2Q. For open-ended LLM tasks, a propose-then-vote mechanism first collects each expert's top response to form the answer catalogue, then experts vote among those candidates.

## Method

The paper proposes two UCB-based online algorithms. SEE (Successive Expert Elimination) starts with all experts and uses pairwise confidence-bound breakage events to detect when expert competencies are statistically distinguishable; upon a breakage, an advantage function tests whether including lower-ranked experts improves the committee's majority-voting accuracy, and non-beneficial experts are eliminated, yielding a regret bound of O(N log T / ε̃²). θ-WMV (Weighted Majority Voting) instead solves a mixed-integer program at each round using optimistic UCB estimates of expert competencies, encoding all 2^N voting scenarios with binary indicator variables and constraints enforcing logical consistency (complementary outcomes cannot both pass), producing a regret bound of O(√(NT log T)). A key structural result (Lemma 2.1) shows the optimal committee is always a subset of the Top-K experts by competency, enabling efficient greedy construction and avoiding combinatorial search. A targeted-m variant partitions experts into groups to handle computational constraints on the number of experts queryable per timestep, scaling regret by a multiplicative N/m factor.

## Results

Across all evaluated domains, the proposed algorithms achieve substantially lower cumulative regret and higher empirical majority-voting accuracy than baselines. On the Bernoulli simulation (N=20), SEE achieves R_T = 546.2 and P̂_maj = 0.980, compared to Comb. UCB's R_T = 6932 and P̂_maj = 0.370. On GSM8K (N=9 LLM experts), θ-WMV reaches R_T = 587.0 and P̂_maj = 0.927, versus Zooming's R_T = 3443 and P̂_maj = 0.604. On BoolQ (N=5), θ-WMV attains R_T = 360.9 and P̂_maj = 0.735, outperforming Zooming (R_T = 740.3, P̂_maj = 0.694).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two baselines: Combinatorial UCB (Comb. UCB, from Chen et al. 2013) and the Zooming algorithm (Kleinberg et al. 2008). SEE dominates Comb. UCB on both Bernoulli (R_T: 546 vs. 6932, a 12.7× reduction) and CommonsenseQA (R_T: 677 vs. 1065, a 1.6× reduction), while also achieving substantially higher final accuracy (0.980 vs. 0.370 on Bernoulli; 0.810 vs. 0.664 on CommonsenseQA). θ-WMV outperforms Zooming on GSM8K (R_T: 587 vs. 3443, a 5.9× reduction; P̂_maj: 0.927 vs. 0.604) and BoolQ (R_T: 361 vs. 740, a 2.0× reduction; P̂_maj: 0.735 vs. 0.694). In every head-to-head comparison, the proposed methods win on both cumulative regret and final empirical accuracy, with the largest margins appearing in the Bernoulli and GSM8K settings where expert heterogeneity is most pronounced.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the prediction-with-expert-advice literature (EXP4, Cesa-Bianchi & Lugosi) by shifting the convergence target from the best single expert to the best collective committee, drawing on Condorcet's Jury Theorem and social choice theory. It builds on Top-K bandit elimination (Even-Dar et al., Rejwan & Mansour) for the SEE algorithm and on the weighted majority algorithm (Littlestone & Warmuth) for the θ-WMV algorithm, but departs from both by operating in an online multi-class setting with full-bandit feedback rather than offline binary predictions. Unlike combinatorial multi-armed bandit approaches that assume access to an (α,β)-approximation oracle, the paper's algorithms assemble committees using only marginal expert competencies, avoiding the submodularity assumption that prior combinatorial bandit work requires.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes a formal bridge between online learning (no-regret bandit algorithms) and epistemic social choice theory, providing the first no-regret guarantees for learning optimal voting aggregation parameters in an online setting. The LLM ensemble experiments demonstrate practical relevance for multi-model inference, though the evaluation is limited to small expert pools (5–9 LLMs) and three benchmarks. Key open questions include scaling the MIP-based θ-WMV to large expert sets, incorporating contextual or side information, and handling correlated (non-independent) expert predictions, all of which the authors acknowledge as limitations.
