## Problem

Robust variants of optimal transport—α-optimal partial transport (α-OPT) and λ-robust OT (λ-ROT)—had only been studied in fully discrete settings, with no prior work extending them to the semi-discrete case where one distribution is continuous. This paper fills that gap by providing structural characterizations and designing both exact and near-linear-time approximation algorithms for these robust variants in the semi-discrete setting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251469/cropped_page3_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a continuous probability distribution μ over a compact support A ⊂ R^d, a discrete distribution ν with n support points B, a parameter α ∈ [0,1] (for partial transport) or λ > 0 (for robust transport), and a cost exponent p ≥ 1. It produces a semi-discrete transport plan characterized by a weight function y: B → R_{≥0} defining a restricted Voronoi diagram, along with the corresponding OT cost. The algorithm assumes access to an oracle that computes the continuous mass of μ inside any query triangle or hypercube in Φ time, and the running time depends on the diameter Δ of A ∪ B.

## Method

The key structural insight is that optimal α-OPT and λ-ROT plans in the semi-discrete setting are characterized by valid weight functions whose restricted Voronoi cells (Voronoi cells intersected with balls of radius y(b)) satisfy a cover or cap constraint, combining the weighted Voronoi structure with the interior ball condition. The paper establishes a strong algorithmic link between the two problems: an approximate λ-ROT solver can be used via binary search on MaxCover(λ) to obtain a near-optimal α-OPT plan with matching approximation guarantees. For the exact algorithm, a cost-scaling framework is extended to handle untransported mass by introducing consolidating paths that relocate deficit regions from inside restricted Voronoi cells to their exterior, maintaining δ-feasibility invariants across O(log(Δ/ε)) scales. For the near-linear-time approximation, the continuous distribution is discretized into near-linear-size hypercubes, the problem is reduced to λ-capped discrete OT on a sparse graph built from a well-separated pair decomposition, and solved via minimum-cost flow or multiplicative weights update.

## Results

The cost-scaling algorithm computes an ε-close λ-robust semi-discrete OT plan in $n^{O(d)}\log(\Delta/\varepsilon)$ time (Theorem 4.4), and the near-linear approximation algorithm achieves an (ε,0)-approximate plan in $n^{1+o(1)}\varepsilon^{-O(d)}$ time (Theorem A.1). Empirically, on Gaussian distributions with 10% noise (n=10–80, λ=0.2, ε=0.02), the algorithm's additive error stays below the target threshold of 0.02, and the number of iterations in both steps remains well within the theoretical O(n) bound (Table 1). On Beta distribution mixtures (Table 3), the 0.15-ROT plan transports 98–100% of inlier mass while transporting only 4.8–49.3% of outlier mass, with the outlier transport fraction decreasing as the inlier/outlier separation increases (β from 3 to 10).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not include a head-to-head experimental comparison against prior algorithms, as it is primarily a theoretical/algorithmic contribution extending the cost-scaling framework of Agarwal et al. [1] and the near-linear approximation of Agarwal et al. [3] to the partial and robust semi-discrete setting. The key algorithmic advance over [1] is the introduction of consolidating paths and weight-reduction steps to handle untransported mass, which prior work did not address. Over [3], the new reduction steps for λ-capped transport on sparse graphs (using WSPD-informed cross edges) are necessary because the triangle inequality fails for p>1 and because capping edge costs can create undesirably short extraneous paths. The paper's main empirical validation is internal: confirming that iteration counts match the O(n) theoretical bound and that the computed plans correctly identify inlier vs. outlier mass (Table 3 shows >98% inlier transport with decreasing outlier transport as separation grows). No prior work had extended α-OPT or λ-ROT to the semi-discrete setting, so no direct baseline exists for comparison.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends two discrete-distribution robust OT formulations—α-optimal partial transport (Chapel et al., 2020) and λ-robust OT (Mukherjee et al., 2021)—to the semi-discrete setting where one distribution is continuous. The characterization via restricted Voronoi diagrams synthesizes the weighted Voronoi structure known from semi-discrete unbalanced OT (Bourne et al., 2018) with the interior ball condition (Caffarelli & McCann, 2010), yielding a unified geometric picture. Algorithmically, the cost-scaling framework of Agarwal et al. (NeurIPS 2024) for standard semi-discrete OT is extended with new 'consolidating paths' to handle untransported mass, and the near-linear approximation builds on the WSPD-based graph construction of Agarwal et al. (SODA 2024). A key departure from prior work is the refined reduction between α-OPT and λ-ROT that preserves approximation guarantees, where the standard duality-based reduction does not.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to provide both characterizations and algorithms for partial and robust OT in the semi-discrete setting, filling a clear gap since all prior robust OT algorithms were restricted to fully discrete distributions. The strong algorithmic equivalence between the two formulations means practitioners can choose whichever solver is more convenient without losing accuracy. Key limitations include the exponential dependence on dimension in the exact algorithm and the oracle assumption for computing continuous mass over polyhedra; achieving polynomial dependence on d and 1/ε remains open. For ML applications using semi-discrete OT (texture synthesis, generative models, domain adaptation), these results provide theoretically grounded robust alternatives to standard OT that are less sensitive to outliers.
