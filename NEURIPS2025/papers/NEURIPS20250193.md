## Problem

Image autoregressive models require sequential token-by-token sampling, making inference prohibitively slow (10–256 steps). While Distilled Decoding 1 (DD1) first enabled one-step sampling, it incurs significant quality degradation and relies on a pre-defined noise-to-data mapping that constrains flexibility. This paper asks whether a one-step generator can be trained to exactly match an AR model's output distribution without any predefined mapping.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250193/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The one-step generator takes a Gaussian noise sequence (one C-dimensional vector per token position, drawn independently from N(0,I)) and a class label, producing the full discrete token sequence in a single forward pass that is then decoded by a VQ decoder into a 256×256 RGB image. The frozen teacher AR model (VAR or LlamaGen) provides probability vectors at each position conditioned on all previous clean tokens. A separate guidance network, trained in parallel, takes the generated token sequence plus per-position noisy tokens and timesteps to predict the generator's conditional score.

## Method

The core insight is reinterpreting the AR model's discrete probability vector as a conditional score in the codebook embedding space via RectFlow, yielding a closed-form score expression (Eq. 1). The paper proposes a Conditional Score Distillation (CSD) loss that aligns the conditional score of a separate guidance network with the teacher AR model's score at every token position, using the SiD loss as the distance function, and proves that minimizing this loss guarantees exact distribution matching (Proposition 1). The guidance network uses a causal transformer backbone with a lightweight MLP head, trained with an AR-diffusion loss to predict the generator's conditional score. A critical initialization strategy fine-tunes the AR model with an MLP head using a Ground Truth Score (GTS) loss that replaces Monte Carlo estimation with the exact score computed from the probability vector, providing strong initialization for both the generator and guidance network. The generator and guidance network are trained alternately, and the approach avoids any pre-defined mapping, instead providing fine-grained per-position supervisory signals from the teacher.

## Results

On ImageNet-256, DD2 compresses VAR-d24 from 10 steps to 1 step with only a 2.2 FID increase (2.86→5.06), achieving an 8.1× inference speedup (0.251s→0.031s). For LlamaGen-L, DD2 reduces 256 steps to 1 with a 4.48 FID increase (4.11→8.59) and a 238× speedup (5.01s→0.021s). Compared to the strongest baseline DD1, DD2 reduces the one-step-to-teacher FID gap by up to 67% (e.g., VAR-d16: gap shrinks from 5.79 to 2.06) while also training up to 12.3× faster (52.6 vs 647.7 GPU-hours on LlamaGen-L).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DD1 (the only prior one-step AR method), DD2 consistently wins on FID across all model sizes: VAR-d16 (6.21 vs 9.94), VAR-d20 (5.43 vs 9.55), VAR-d24 (5.06 vs 8.92), and LlamaGen-L (8.59 vs 11.35), reducing the teacher-to-one-step FID gap by up to 67%. DD2 even outperforms DD1's 2-step results on all VAR models (e.g., VAR-d24: 5.06 vs 6.95). Weak baselines (skip-last-steps, one-step set prediction) completely fail with FIDs of 40–220, confirming that one-step AR sampling is intractable without distillation. In the diffusion distillation comparison (Table 6), DD2-VAR-d16 (FID 6.21) substantially outperforms Shortcut (10.6), Progressive Distillation (35.6), Reflow (44.8), Consistency Trajectory (69.7), and Consistency Distillation (136.5). DD2 also trains up to 12.3× faster than DD1 (52.6 vs 647.7 GPU-hours on LlamaGen-L), making it both more accurate and more efficient to train.

## Relation to Prior Work

DD2 directly extends DD1 (Liu et al., 2024), the first method to compress image AR sampling to a single step via flow matching, but departs fundamentally by replacing DD1's pre-defined noise-to-data ODE mapping with a per-token conditional score distillation objective. It repurposes the score distillation framework from diffusion model distillation (DMD, SiD) to the AR setting, where the teacher's discrete probability vector is reinterpreted as a closed-form conditional score in the codebook embedding space under RectFlow's noise schedule. The guidance network architecture and AR-diffusion training procedure are borrowed from MAR (Li et al., 2024), and the SiD loss function (Zhou et al., 2024) is adopted as the distance metric between teacher and generator scores.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DD2 makes one-step AR image generation practically viable, achieving 8×–238× inference speedup with only a modest FID increase (e.g., 3.40→5.43 on VAR-d20), while also training up to 12.3× faster than DD1. The elimination of the pre-defined mapping yields smoother latent representations (PPL 7232 vs. 18438) and more fine-grained per-token supervision, which the authors argue reduces error accumulation. Open questions include closing the remaining quality gap to the teacher model, extending to continuous-space AR models and text-to-image settings, and whether the approach generalizes beyond the two base models (VAR, LlamaGen) tested on ImageNet-256.
