## Problem

Root Cause Analysis (RCA) methods typically assume access to the full post-intervention distribution or require estimating conditional causal mechanisms, both of which are infeasible when only a single anomalous sample is available. Even methods that avoid distribution estimation are statistically ill-posed because they must probe regression models in low-probability regions. This paper addresses the gap for the practically important case of a single root cause in polytree causal graphs, providing guarantees that rely only on marginal anomaly scores.

## Input / Output

The methods consume only marginal information-theoretic (IT) anomaly scores S(x_i) = -log P(τ(X_i) ≥ τ(x_i)) computed from a single anomalous sample, optionally alongside the causal DAG (for SMOOTH TRAVERSAL) or a maximum in-degree bound and target confidence level (for SCORE ORDERING). SMOOTH TRAVERSAL outputs a single root-cause variable; SCORE ORDERING outputs a shortlist of top-k highest-scoring anomalies with a provable lower bound on the probability that the true root cause is included. No conditional distributions, structural equations, or arbitrary anomaly thresholds are required.

## Method

The core insight is that IT anomaly scores follow an exponential distribution under the null, and that an anomaly of strength S(x) is unlikely to cause a much stronger anomaly of strength S(y) downstream (p ≤ e^{-|S(y)-S(x)|_+}). SMOOTH TRAVERSAL exploits this by selecting, among all ancestors of the target, the node with the largest positive score difference relative to its most anomalous parent, avoiding the arbitrary threshold required by standard traversal. SCORE ORDERING, for the unknown-graph setting, orders variables by marginal score and returns the smallest top-k set for which the union-bound p-value n·d_max·e^{-Δ_k} falls below the desired significance level, guaranteeing the root cause is in the shortlist. Both algorithms are non-parametric, require no conditional probability estimation, and come with explicit p-value bounds under the polytree and score-typicality assumptions.

## Results

On synthetic data (random DAGs, n up to 100 nodes, anomaly strength x=2.0–3.0), SMOOTH TRAVERSAL, Traversal, and Counterfactual achieve the highest top-1 recall for root cause identification, all outperforming SCORE ORDERING. Circa and Cholesky perform considerably worse, attributed to their linearity assumptions. On real-world datasets (PetShop, Sock-shop 2, ProRCA), the picture shifts: SCORE ORDERING often performs well, but both proposed algorithms are variously outperformed by Cholesky, Circa, or Counterfactual in certain settings. SMOOTH TRAVERSAL and Traversal are the fastest in runtime, and SMOOTH TRAVERSAL's performance remains stable as graph size increases from 20 to 100 nodes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SMOOTH TRAVERSAL matches or slightly exceeds Traversal on synthetic data while avoiding the need for an arbitrary anomaly threshold, and both are the fastest algorithms in runtime. Against Counterfactual (which requires the full structural causal model), SMOOTH TRAVERSAL achieves comparable top-1 recall on synthetic data but operates with only marginal anomaly scores and the causal graph. Circa and Cholesky, both assuming linear structural equations, are clearly outperformed by SMOOTH TRAVERSAL and Traversal on synthetic data, with their performance degrading more rapidly as graph size grows and as the causal graph is misspecified. SCORE ORDERING, which requires neither the causal graph nor conditional probability estimation, is outperformed by the graph-based methods on synthetic data but remains competitive on real-world datasets where structural assumptions may be violated. RCD and ε-Diagnosis are evaluated in the appendix but are not directly comparable since they do not operate in the single-anomalous-sample setting that this paper targets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the information-theoretic (IT) anomaly score framework and counterfactual contribution analysis of Budhathoki et al. (ICML 2022), recasting their results in a CBN formalism and showing that for single-root-cause identification, the counterfactual description simplifies to an interventional one. It departs from traversal-based RCA methods (Liu et al., Chen et al., Hardt et al.) by eliminating the need for an arbitrary anomaly threshold and, more fundamentally, by showing that conditional probability estimation—which is statistically ill-posed in low-density regions—can be entirely avoided using only marginal anomaly scores. The SCORE ORDERING algorithm further departs from prior work by not requiring the causal graph at all, providing a causally justified shortlist of root candidates from marginal scores alone under a polytree assumption.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a critical practical gap: real-world RCA often involves a single anomalous sample (business faults, personalized medicine), yet most methods assume access to the full post-intervention distribution or require the full structural causal model. The non-parametric guarantees provided here are, to the authors' knowledge, the first for RCA with a single anomalous point without the full SCM. However, the polytree assumption is a significant structural restriction that limits generality, and experiments show SCORE ORDERING's performance degrades with graph size while SMOOTH TRAVERSAL, though competitive, does not dramatically outperform existing traversal or counterfactual methods. Open questions include extending the marginal-score sufficiency results beyond polytrees, handling multiple simultaneous root causes, and understanding when the polytree assumption is violated in practice.
