## Problem

Standard training metrics like cross-entropy loss decrease monotonically during LLM pretraining, yet model capabilities and internal representational structures undergo qualitative, non-monotonic shifts that these metrics cannot capture. The paper asks how high-dimensional distributed representations within LLMs evolve geometrically across pretraining and post-training, and how these transformations relate to the emergence of specific capabilities such as short-context memorization versus long-context generalization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251829/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method probes intermediate checkpoints from OLMo (1B–7B), Pythia (160M–12B), and Tülu-3.1 (8B) models using 15,000 sequences of 512 tokens sampled from FineWeb sample-10BT. For each checkpoint it extracts last-token hidden states from the final layer, constructs an empirical feature covariance matrix, and computes two spectral metrics: RankMe (Von Neumann entropy-based effective rank) and αReQ (power-law decay exponent of the eigenspectrum). The output is a time-resolved trajectory of these metrics revealing distinct geometric phases, which are then correlated with downstream task accuracy (SciQ, TriviaQA, AMC-23) and an ∞-gram distributional memorization metric.

## Method

The paper introduces a spectral framework that tracks LLM representation geometry via the eigenspectrum of the last-token feature covariance matrix, quantified by RankMe (effective rank) and αReQ (eigenspectrum decay rate). Applying this to OLMo and Pythia checkpoints reveals a consistent three-phase non-monotonic sequence during pretraining: a 'warmup' phase of rapid representational collapse, an 'entropy-seeking' phase of manifold expansion coinciding with peak n-gram memorization, and a 'compression-seeking' phase of anisotropic consolidation that enables long-context generalization. To explain these phases mechanistically, the authors develop an analytically tractable toy model with a linear feature extractor and cross-entropy loss, proving via Legendre-transform linearization and SVD dynamics that skewed token frequencies combined with an information bottleneck (d ≪ |V|) are sufficient to produce the observed expansion-then-compression trajectory. For post-training, they show that SFT and DPO (reformulated as a Noise Contrastive Estimation loss) drive entropy-seeking expansion, while RLVR drives compression-seeking contraction, with the latter reducing pass@k diversity at high sampling counts.

## Results

Across OLMo-2 (1B, 7B) and Pythia (160M–12B) models, pretraining exhibits a consistent three-phase geometric sequence: a rapid 'warmup' collapse, an 'entropy-seeking' expansion (RankMe ↑, αReQ ↓) coinciding with peak n-gram memorization, and a 'compression-seeking' anisotropic consolidation (RankMe ↓, αReQ ↑) that drives long-context QA gains. Ablating to only the top-10 eigen-directions collapses SciQ accuracy from 0.838 to 0.225 (Pythia-1B) and 0.970 to 0.155 (OLMo-2-7B), confirming full-spectrum information is essential. In post-training, SFT/DPO monotonically increase RankMe (entropy-seeking) while RLVR monotonically decreases it (compression-seeking); RLVR improves pass@16 over the base model but degrades pass@256, and overtrained base models (higher RankMe) show a winrate drop from 14% to 9% on AlpacaEval after SFT on a mismatched dataset.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is an analytical/observational paper rather than a method paper, so it does not propose a new model to benchmark against external baselines. Instead, it establishes internal consistency checks: the three-phase geometric dynamics are confirmed across two independent model families (OLMo-2 and Pythia) spanning 160M–12B parameters, across multiple layers, and across different probe datasets (FineWeb, WikiText). For post-training, the paper contrasts three sequential stages (SFT → DPO → RLVR) on the Tülu-3.1-8B pipeline, showing that SFT and DPO both induce entropy-seeking (RankMe increase) while RLVR induces compression-seeking (RankMe decrease), with the latter narrowing high-k exploration (pass@256) relative to the pre-RLVR checkpoint. The ID-vs-OOD comparison (Anthropic-HH vs. AlpacaFarm) demonstrates that higher-RankMe base models are more sensitive to distribution shift under SFT, corroborating the 'catastrophic overtraining' finding of Springer et al. (2025). The toy-model analysis in Section 3.3 provides a mechanistic baseline: removing either skewed token frequencies or the information bottleneck (d ≪ |V|) eliminates the multiphase dynamics, yielding only monotonic expansion.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the spectral analysis toolkit (RankMe, αReQ) previously used to assess representation quality at fixed training points into a dynamic, longitudinal framework that tracks geometric evolution across the full pretraining and post-training lifecycle. The theoretical analysis builds directly on Pezeshki et al.'s gradient starvation framework, extending it to show how cross-entropy optimization under skewed token frequencies and representational bottlenecks (d ≪ |V|) produces the observed three-phase dynamics. It also connects to the 'Distributional Simplicity Bias' literature (Refinetti et al., Belrose et al.) by providing a geometric quantification of the progression from short-context n-gram statistics to long-context generalization, and to post-training alignment work (DPO, RLVR) by showing these stages induce distinct and opposite geometric transformations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The discovery that representation geometry evolves non-monotonically through three consistent phases—despite monotonically decreasing loss—provides a quantitative framework for understanding when and why specific LLM capabilities emerge, with practical implications for checkpoint selection and training pipeline design. The finding that SFT/DPO expand representational dimensionality while RLVR compresses it offers a geometric explanation for the trade-off between in-distribution fit and out-of-distribution robustness, as well as for RLVR's reduction in generation diversity. Key limitations include the restriction to models ≤12B parameters, the linear feature extractor assumption in the theoretical analysis, and the primarily correlational nature of the phase-capability links; extending to larger models, full transformer architectures, and multilingual settings remains open.
