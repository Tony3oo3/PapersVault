## Problem

Minimax-optimal private estimators for KL distribution estimation (e.g., add-constant with Laplace noise) achieve poor empirical performance on common non-worst-case distributions like power-law, because minimax optimality only captures worst-case error. Prior instance-optimality frameworks used neighborhoods (permutation, two-point, multiplicative) that are either too large to be informative or too small to allow any instance-optimal algorithm under KL divergence. This paper introduces additive local neighborhoods calibrated to privacy parameters and proves the first instance-optimality guarantees for private KL distribution estimation.

## Input / Output

The algorithm receives n i.i.d. samples from an unknown distribution p over d symbols, modeled as independent Poisson counts x_i ~ Poi(np_i) per symbol, along with privacy parameters (ε, δ). It outputs a probability vector Â(x) ∈ Δ(d) that is (ε,δ)-differentially private with respect to the input dataset. The Poisson model ensures independent per-symbol sampling while remaining equivalent to multinomial sampling conditioned on total count n, and the output is normalized to sum to one via a global denominator N.

## Method

The core algorithm is a 'Sampling Twice' estimator that partitions the dataset into two independent halves: one half identifies low-frequency symbols via a threshold τ, and the other half estimates their combined probability mass, reducing the estimator's ℓ₁-sensitivity from O(n^{1/3}) (as in Good-Turing) to O(1). For the DP variant, Laplace noise is added to counts and the threshold is calibrated to balance false-negative error (high-probability symbols misclassified as small) against false-positive error (low-probability symbols misclassified as large), with the optimal threshold depending on ε and d. The theoretical analysis introduces new additive neighborhoods N₊(p) with per-symbol perturbation scales min{t/n, √(p_i/n)} and a DP-calibrated variant N_{≤t/(nε)}(p), and proves matching upper and lower bounds via a generalized Assouad's method extended to decomposable statistical distances like KL divergence.

## Results

The paper evaluates KL error (reported as negative log-likelihood ratio) across synthetic power-law distributions (p_i ∝ 1/i^β for β = 1, 1.5, 2) and real-world token distributions from Reddit, Enron-email, and MMLU, varying dataset size n, dimension d, and privacy parameter ε. The DP instance-optimal Algorithm 2 (Sampling Twice with Laplace noise) consistently outperforms the minimax-optimal Add-constant (DP) baseline across all settings, with gains especially pronounced on real-world datasets. The non-DP Algorithm 1 is competitive (within constants) with the strongest prior non-DP baseline, the Good-Turing estimator [50]. All results are averaged over five random trials with 1-sigma error bars.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two baselines: (1) the Add-constant (DP) estimator, which is already minimax-optimal with O(ln(1 + d/(εn))) rate, and (2) the Good-Turing estimator [50], the strongest prior non-DP (near) instance-optimal baseline. The DP instance-optimal Algorithm 2 consistently outperforms Add-constant (DP) across all evaluated settings (varying n, d, ε) on both synthetic power-law and real-world token distributions, demonstrating that minimax optimality does not imply good per-instance performance. The non-DP Algorithm 1 is competitive with Good-Turing [50] within constant factors, validating the 'sampling twice' design as a practical alternative. The gains are most significant on real-world datasets (Reddit, Enron-email, MMLU) where distributions are highly skewed, confirming that instance-optimality provides meaningful empirical reward over minimax-only guarantees.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Good-Turing estimator lineage (Good 1953; Orlitsky & Suresh 2015) by replacing the recursive frequency-bucket bias correction with a 'sampling twice' design that reduces dataset sensitivity from O(n^{1/3}) to O(1), making privatization via the Laplace mechanism feasible. It builds on the instance-optimality framework of Feldman et al. (2024), which defined per-instance competitiveness via local neighborhoods, but departs from prior neighborhood choices (permutation, two-point, multiplicative) by introducing additive neighborhoods calibrated to both Poisson sampling variance and DP privacy parameters. The lower-bound machinery extends Assouad's method (Yu 1997; Acharya et al. 2021) to decomposable statistical distances, a technical necessity since KL divergence is asymmetric and does not satisfy the symmetry assumptions of standard Assouad/Fano arguments.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to establish instance-optimality (rather than merely minimax optimality) for differentially private KL distribution estimation, a setting directly relevant to language modeling where NLL/KL is the standard training objective. The proof that the additive neighborhood size is necessary up to constants (Theorem G.9) delineates the fundamental boundary of what per-instance guarantees are achievable under DP, guiding future algorithm design. The open question of whether exact (constant-matching) instance-optimality is achievable, and whether approximate-DP mechanisms (Gaussian) can close the remaining log(1/δ) gap, remain unresolved. Practically, the calibrated thresholding technique for balancing false-negative and false-positive errors in private symbol selection may transfer to other private estimation problems with heterogeneous symbol masses.
