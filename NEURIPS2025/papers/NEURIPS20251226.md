## Problem

Exact unlearning—retraining a model from scratch without the target data—is widely regarded as the gold standard for removing sensitive information from LLMs, yet prior evaluations only assess the post-unlearning model in isolation. This paper identifies a practical gap: in open-weight or API deployments, attackers often retain access to pre-unlearning checkpoints or previously saved logits, a threat model that has been overlooked. The authors demonstrate that under this dual-access setting, exact unlearning can paradoxically make data extraction easier than using the pre-unlearning model alone.

## Input / Output

The method consumes token-level logits from both the pre-unlearning model θ and the post-unlearning model θ' at each decoding step, conditioned on a known prefix (the first half of each target passage). It produces greedy-decoded text continuations intended to match the second half of the forgotten passages. Evaluation uses Rouge-L recall and a thresholded extraction success rate (A-ESR at τ=0.9 and τ=1.0), where success requires near-verbatim reproduction of the ground-truth continuation.

## Method

The core technique, reversed model guidance, treats unlearning as the inverse of fine-tuning and models the forgotten data distribution q as a geometric interpolation between the post-unlearning model and the pre-unlearning model (Eq. 2). In log-probability space this yields a guidance formula (Eq. 3) where the post-unlearning model's log-probabilities are augmented by a scaled difference between pre- and post-unlearning log-probabilities, with scale w = 1/λ inversely proportional to the number of training iterations that would relearn the forgotten set. To prevent incoherent generations caused by raw log-probability differences, the authors introduce a token filtering strategy inspired by contrastive decoding: at each step, candidate tokens are restricted to those whose probability under the pre-unlearning model exceeds a fraction γ of the maximum, effectively pruning low-frequency or semantically irrelevant tokens. The attacker then performs greedy decoding over this filtered candidate set using the guided distribution, requiring no additional training or gradient access—only forward-pass logits from both checkpoints.

## Results

On the MUSE dataset with Phi-1.5, the proposed method achieves A-ESR₁.₀ of 0.224 (a 121% improvement over the pre-unlearning baseline of 0.101) and Rouge-L(R) of 0.606 vs. 0.473. On WMDP with Phi-1.5, the improvement is even more dramatic: A-ESR₁.₀ reaches 0.192 (↑178%) and A-ESR₀.₉ reaches 0.218 (↑175%). With Llama2-7B, gains are more modest but consistent, e.g., A-ESR₁.₀ of 0.438 (↑14.1%) on MUSE and 0.075 (↑50.0%) on WMDP. On the synthetic medical dataset, A-ESR₁.₀ improves from 0.140 to 0.210 (↑50%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is direct greedy generation from the pre-unlearning model (following Nasr et al. [25]), which represents the strongest single-model extraction attack. The proposed method consistently outperforms this baseline across all three benchmarks and both model architectures, with the largest gains on Phi-1.5 (up to 178% in A-ESR₁.₀ on WMDP) and more moderate but still significant gains on Llama2-7B (14–50%). The post-unlearning model alone is nearly useless for extraction (A-ESR₁.₀ ≤ 0.013), confirming that exact unlearning does remove memorization from the final model, yet the pre/post comparison recovers far more than either model alone. In a separate comparison (Table 6), ETHICIST [36]—a soft-prompt-based extraction method that assumes access to part of the retained training data—achieves A-ESR₁.₀ of 0.073 on TOFU, below the proposed method's 0.120; combining ETHICIST with the guidance-based approach further boosts A-ESR₁.₀ to 0.153, indicating the two attacks are complementary.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the training-data extraction literature (Carlini et al. 2021; Nasr et al. 2023; Nakka et al. 2024), which operates on a single model, to a differential setting where both pre- and post-unlearning checkpoints are available. The core technique repurposes classifier-free guidance from LLM generation (Sanchez et al. 2024) and diffusion-model data extraction (Wu et al. 2024), reframing unlearning as 'reverse fine-tuning' so that the pre-unlearning model acts as a guidance signal steering the post-unlearning model toward the forgotten distribution. The token filtering strategy is borrowed from contrastive decoding (Li et al. 2023) to maintain generation coherence. Conceptually, the paper departs from prior unlearning work by treating the pre-unlearning checkpoint not as a discarded artifact but as an active attack resource, a perspective absent from both exact and approximate unlearning evaluations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper challenges the widely held assumption that exact unlearning is the 'gold standard' for data removal, showing that in open-weight or API-retention scenarios it can paradoxically increase privacy leakage by creating a differential signal between model states. The medical SOAP-note simulation concretizes the risk: even minimal side information (patient name, DOB) combined with differential access yields a 50% improvement in exact extraction over the pre-unlearning baseline. Key limitations include the requirement for simultaneous access to both checkpoints (not always realistic in closed-source settings) and the observation that defenses (noisy gradients, unrelated data) only partially mitigate the attack at severe utility cost. Open questions include whether the threat model extends to scenarios where only public model outputs (not logits) are available, and how to design unlearning procedures that are robust to differential attacks without sacrificing utility.
