## Problem

Deep learning-based pan-sharpening methods achieve strong fusion quality but incur prohibitive computational costs on large remote sensing images, being highly sensitive to input resolution and dependent on dedicated GPUs/TPUs. Even with 24GB GPU memory, most DNN-based methods cannot process images beyond 8K×8K, and CPU-only inference is impractically slow, limiting real-world deployment where images routinely exceed 15K×15K.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254217/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a high-resolution panchromatic image (H×W×1) and a low-resolution multispectral image (H/r×W/r×C) with spatial ratio r=4, upsampled to full resolution before processing. It produces a high-resolution multispectral image (H×W×C) with C=4 bands (R, G, B, NIR). The entire pipeline contains fewer than 700K parameters and processes a 9K×9K image in under 1 ms on a single RTX 2080 Ti, while handling 15K×15K images on a 24GB GPU—resolutions at which all compared DNN-based methods fail with out-of-memory errors.

## Method

Pan-LUT replaces all neural network operations with three learnable look-up tables: PGLUT (a 5D LUT using PAN-guided indexing and pentalinear interpolation for channel-wise spectral mapping), SDLUT (a 4D LUT using a rotation-indexing strategy over 2×2 neighborhoods at 0°/90°/180°/270° to expand the receptive field to 3×3, with quadrilinear interpolation for spatial detail capture), and AOLUT (a 5D LUT for adaptive channel aggregation into the final HRMS output). The PAN-guided indexing strategy disambiguates identical MS pixel values at different spatial locations by incorporating the corresponding PAN intensity as an additional lookup dimension. Training uses a joint loss combining MSE reconstruction, smoothness regularization (penalizing adjacent LUT value differences), and monotonicity regularization (ReLU-based penalty on non-monotonic LUT entries), with LUT sizes set to N=9 per dimension to balance capacity against storage. The architecture contains no convolutions, attention, or other network layers—only table lookups and multi-linear interpolation—enabling near-constant-time inference regardless of image size.

## Results

On reduced-resolution scenes, Pan-LUT achieves PSNR of 40.86 (WV2), 43.75 (GF2), and 29.74 (WV3) with only 0.66M parameters, processing a 4K×4K image in 0.54 ms on an RTX 2080 Ti. On full-resolution real-world WorldView-II scenes, Pan-LUT achieves the best D_λ (0.0571), D_S (0.0640), and QNR (0.8829) among all 12 compared methods, substantially outperforming the next-best TA-DiffHQP (D_λ=0.0953, D_S=0.1129, QNR=0.8025). The method can process 9K×9K images on an 11GB GPU and 15K×15K on a 24GB GPU, whereas most DNN baselines OOM at 4K×4K.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Pan-LUT trails the strongest DNN baselines on reduced-resolution PSNR: Pan-Mamba (42.24 dB) and TA-DiffHQP (42.13 dB) lead on WV2 by ~1.4 dB, while SFDI (47.47 dB) and PanFlow (47.25 dB) lead on GF2 by ~3.7 dB. However, Pan-LUT is 230×–2600× faster in inference (0.38 ms vs. 87.74–998.58 ms at 2K×2K) and is the only method besides PNN that avoids OOM at 4K×4K. On full-resolution real-world WorldView-II scenes (Table 2), Pan-LUT reverses the quality gap, achieving the best D_λ (0.0571 vs. 0.0953 for TA-DiffHQP), D_S (0.0640 vs. 0.1129), and QNR (0.8829 vs. 0.8025), outperforming all 11 DNN and traditional baselines. Against traditional methods (Brovey, IHS, SFIM, GS, PCA, Wavelet), Pan-LUT gains 5–7 dB in PSNR while maintaining comparable or faster inference speeds (0.38 ms vs. 0.21–13.37 ms). Pan-LUT also surpasses PNN and PanNet on both PSNR and inference time, demonstrating that the LUT-based approach can match or exceed early DNN methods without any network structure.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the look-up table (LUT) paradigm—previously applied to image enhancement (Zeng et al., Wang et al.) and super-resolution (SRLUT, MuLUT, SPLUT, RCLUT)—for the first time in pan-sharpening, replacing all convolutional and transformer operations with three purpose-designed LUTs (PGLUT, SDLUT, AOLUT) and multi-linear interpolation. It departs from the dominant DNN-based pan-sharpening lineage (PNN → PanNet → MSDCNN → Pan-Mamba → TA-DiffHQP) by eliminating network structure entirely, and from traditional hand-crafted fusion (Brovey, IHS, GS) by learning the mapping end-to-end. The PAN-guided indexing strategy is a novel twist on standard LUT indexing, using the panchromatic channel to disambiguate identical multispectral pixel values at different spatial locations, effectively injecting spatial context into what would otherwise be a purely value-based lookup.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: Pan-LUT processes 15K×15K remote sensing images on a 24GB GPU and achieves sub-millisecond inference on 9K×9K images, addressing a critical gap where DNN-based methods hit out-of-memory failures at these scales. The finding that a network-free LUT approach outperforms SOTA DNN methods (including diffusion-based TA-DiffHQP) on full-resolution real-world scenes, while underperforming them on synthetic reduced-resolution scenes, suggests that the field's trend toward deeper architectures may be overfitting to the Wald-protocol training distribution. Key limitations include the exponential space complexity of n-dimensional LUTs (O(N^D)), which constrains the method to low-dimensional inputs (here, 5 channels), and the open question of whether lightweight hybrid LUT-network architectures can extend the approach to higher-band-count sensors or more complex fusion tasks.
