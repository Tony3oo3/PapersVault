## Problem

Existing uncertainty quantification methods for physics-informed neural networks (PINNs)—Bayesian inference and dropout—both require subjective hyperparameters (prior distributions or dropout rates) that cannot be determined from observed data alone, making it impossible to construct honest, well-calibrated confidence sets. This paper addresses this gap by developing an extended fiducial inference (EFI) framework that quantifies PINN parameter uncertainty based solely on observations and the embedded physical law, without any prior specification.

## Input / Output

The method consumes spatial-temporal coordinates paired with three types of noisy observations: solution measurements u(x), physics-term measurements f(x), and boundary/initial-condition measurements b(x), along with the governing PDE operator. It produces a DNN approximation of the PDE solution, estimates of physics parameters β, 95% confidence intervals for all parameters, imputed Gaussian random errors for each observation, and M fiducial θ-samples from which any function of interest can be assessed. A key structural detail is the double-NN architecture: a data-modeling network approximates u(x), while a separate w-network (with a narrow stochastic neck layer) maps observations and imputed errors to the data-modeling network's parameters, enabling the inverse-function estimation central to EFI.

## Method

The core contribution is a new theoretical framework for EFI that replaces the original sparse-prior w-network with a narrow-neck stochastic neural network (StoNet), eliminating the need for sparsity assumptions and enabling consistent inverse-function estimation even when the parameter dimension p far exceeds the sample size n. The algorithm (Algorithm 1) iterates between two steps: (i) imputing latent random errors Z_n via adaptive stochastic gradient Langevin dynamics, and (ii) updating the w-network weights via mini-batch SGD on an energy function that penalizes both parameter inconsistency (‖θ̂_i − θ̄‖²) and data-fitting residuals for u, f, and b terms. The narrow neck layer (width d_h ≪ n) acts as a sufficient dimension reduction for the input, and the final output layer performs p linear regressions from the neck representation to θ̂_i, whose consistency is proven via the asymptotic equivalence between StoNets and DNNs (Theorem 3.2). This design removes all subjective hyperparameters from the uncertainty quantification pipeline, yielding honest confidence sets with correct nominal coverage (≈95%) without tuning dropout rates or prior variances.

## Results

On the 1-D Poisson equation (averaged over 100 runs), EFI achieves a 95.17% coverage rate for 95% confidence intervals with the shortest CI-width (0.0504) and an MSE of 1.48×10⁻⁴, closely matching the nominal 95% target. In contrast, dropout methods produce severely inflated coverage (99–100%) with CI-widths 4–13× wider (0.19–0.66), while Bayesian PINNs are highly sensitive to the noise parameter σ_f, ranging from 58.8% to 99.97% coverage depending on the chosen value. Concrete Dropout matches EFI's MSE (1.84×10⁻⁴) but under-covers dramatically at only 11.85%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the standard PINN baseline (Raissi et al., 2019), EFI dramatically improves coverage from 7.57% to 95.17% while maintaining comparable MSE (1.48×10⁻⁴ vs. 1.21×10⁻⁴), confirming that the base PINN provides essentially no valid uncertainty. Compared to dropout-based UQ (Zhang et al., 2019), EFI reduces CI-width by 4–13× (0.0504 vs. 0.19–0.66) while achieving the correct 95% coverage instead of the inflated 99–100% seen across all dropout rates. Against Concrete Dropout (Gal et al., 2017), EFI matches estimation accuracy (MSE 1.48×10⁻⁴ vs. 1.84×10⁻⁴) but achieves 95.17% coverage versus Concrete Dropout's severe under-coverage of 11.85%. Versus Bayesian PINNs (Yang et al., 2021), EFI is the only method that achieves the nominal 95% coverage without requiring manual tuning of the noise parameter σ_f; the Bayesian method swings from 58.8% to 99.97% coverage depending on this hyperparameter, with no principled way to select the correct value. Overall, EFI is the only method that simultaneously achieves correct coverage, the narrowest confidence intervals, and no hyperparameter sensitivity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Extended Fiducial Inference (EFI) framework of Liang et al. (2025) to large-scale models by replacing the sparse-prior w-network with a narrow-neck stochastic neural network (StoNet) formulation, thereby removing the dimensionality constraint that previously limited EFI to settings where parameter count is fixed or grows very slowly with sample size. It positions itself against the dominant Bayesian PINN (B-PINNs, Yang et al. 2021) and dropout-based (Zhang et al. 2019; Gal & Ghahramani 2016) approaches to PINN uncertainty quantification, arguing that both require subjective hyperparameters (priors, dropout rates) that cannot be calibrated from data alone. The StoNet construction draws on the sufficient dimension reduction theory of Liang et al. (2022) and the asymptotic equivalence between StoNets and DNNs, while the adaptive SGLD training algorithm inherits from the original EFI framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The narrow-neck EFI framework provides a principled, hyperparameter-free route to calibrated confidence intervals for PINN parameters, addressing a genuine gap: Bayesian and dropout methods produce intervals whose width and coverage are sensitive to arbitrary choices (σ_f, dropout rate) with no data-driven selection criterion. The self-diagnostic property (QQ-plots of imputed errors plus energy convergence to zero) offers a practical validation mechanism absent from competing methods. However, all experiments use relatively small-scale models (1-D Poisson, 2-D Black-Scholes, simple ODE/PDE growth models), and the theoretical conditions in Theorem 3.2 impose specific scaling relationships among ε, n, p, d_h, and h that may be restrictive in practice. Open questions include whether the approach scales to ResNets/CNNs via transfer learning, how computational cost compares to standard PINN training at scale, and whether the narrow-neck construction generalizes beyond the Gaussian noise assumption.
