## Problem

When agents genuinely improve their features in response to a deployed classifier (e.g., studying harder to meet an admission cutoff), the learnability of concept classes differs fundamentally from both standard PAC-learning and strategic classification. Prior work by Attias et al. provided only a sufficient condition (intersection-closed) and a partial necessary condition for proper learnability, leaving the exact characterization open, while improper learning, learning under label noise, and online learning with improvements were largely unexplored.

## Input / Output

The learner receives m i.i.d. labeled samples from a distribution D over instance space X, an improvement function Δ mapping each point to its reachable set (e.g., an ℓ₂-ball of radius r), and a concept class H. The output is a binary classifier h whose loss is evaluated on the worst-case point in the agent's reaction set after it potentially moves to a positively classified neighbor, making the loss asymmetric and favoring conservative classifiers. In the online setting, the learner observes one graph node per round, must predict labels for all nodes simultaneously, and receives only a binary mistake signal without learning which neighbor caused the error.

## Method

The paper introduces the 'nearly minimally consistent' property—an asymmetric variant of minimally consistent that requires a least consistent concept only for training sets containing at least one negative example—and proves it exactly characterizes proper PAC-learnability with improvements for any improvement function, with sample complexity O((1/ε)(d_VH + log(1/δ))). For improper learning under Euclidean ball improvement sets, a simple memorization rule (labeling only sampled positive points as positive) achieves near-optimal sample complexity O((1/β)log(N/γ)) under a coverability assumption on the positive-label marginal, and can even learn infinite VC-dimension classes. For bounded label noise (Massart, RCN), the paper reduces learning with improvements to standard PAC learning with noise by constructing a conservative classifier from the positive agreement region of near-optimal linear separators under isotropic log-concave distributions, achieving exactly Bayes-optimal error. For online learning on graphs, risk-averse majority vote algorithms requiring a Δ_G/(Δ_G+1) super-majority for positive classification, combined with neighbor-aware classifier discarding, achieve near-optimal mistake bounds of (Δ_G+1)log|H| (realizable) and O(2(Δ_G+1)(OPT+log|H|)) (agnostic).

## Results

The paper provides exact characterizations and tight bounds across four learning settings. For proper PAC-learning with improvements, the sample complexity is O((1/ε)(d_VH + log(1/δ))) samples, fully characterized by the new 'nearly minimally consistent' property (Theorem 3.4). For improper learning with Euclidean ball improvement sets under a coverability assumption, the memorization rule achieves O((1/β) log(N/γ)) samples, matching a lower bound of Ω((1/β) log(1/β)) up to log factors (Theorems 4.2–4.3). For online learning on a graph with maximum degree Δ_G, the risk-averse majority vote achieves (Δ_G+1) log|H| mistakes in the realizable setting and O(2(Δ_G+1)(OPT + log|H|)) in the agnostic setting, with matching lower bounds of Δ−1 and Δ·OPT respectively (Theorems 6.1–6.3). Under bounded label noise, the paper achieves Bayes-optimal error with sample complexity Õ(d/(r(1−2ν))) for RCN and Õ(d/(r²(1−2ν)²)) for Massart noise (Theorem 5.1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines or experimental comparisons. The key comparisons are conceptual: (1) The nearly minimally consistent characterization strictly generalizes the intersection-closed sufficient condition of Attias et al. [5] and the minimally consistent property from one-sided error learning, with explicit separation examples (Examples 3.5–3.6) showing each inclusion is strict. (2) The improper learning result shows that under coverability, even infinite VC-dimension concept classes are learnable with the simple memorization rule, in contrast to proper learning which requires finite VC-dimension and nearly minimal consistency. (3) The online learning results show that standard majority vote suffers unbounded mistakes in the improvements setting (Example E.1), while the risk-averse super-majority variant recovers near-optimal bounds — a contrast with strategic classification where super-majority is used for negative classification. (4) Under label noise, the paper achieves exactly the Bayes-optimal error (OPT), improving upon standard PAC learning which can only guarantee OPT + ε.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Attias et al. (ICML 2025), who introduced the formal model of PAC learning with strategic improvements and gave partial characterizations (intersection-closed as sufficient, a restricted necessary condition) for proper learnability. The key conceptual move here is adapting Natarajan's minimally consistent property (which characterizes one-sided error learning) into a new 'nearly minimally consistent' notion that exactly characterizes proper learnability with improvements, closing the gap left by Attias et al. For improper learning, the paper repurposes the coverability assumption from Balcan et al.'s robust learning work, and for the noisy and online settings it draws on standard PAC learning with noise and online strategic classification literature respectively, but applies them in the novel context of agents who genuinely improve their features.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper resolves two open questions from Attias et al. and provides the first treatment of learning with improvements under label noise and in the online setting, establishing a more complete theoretical picture of when and how classifiers can be learned when agents respond by genuinely improving. The finding that the simple memorization rule achieves near-optimal sample complexity under coverability, and that one can achieve exactly Bayes-optimal error under bounded noise (rather than just OPT+ε), suggests that the improvements setting is structurally easier than standard PAC learning in important ways. Open questions include extending beyond binary classification to multi-label and regression, studying feature noise (as opposed to label noise) in the improvements model, and whether randomized online algorithms can remove the Δ_G factor in mistake bounds.
