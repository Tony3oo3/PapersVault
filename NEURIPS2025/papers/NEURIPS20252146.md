## Problem

Existing end-to-end mobile manipulation methods suffer from two critical gaps: they fail to explicitly model how mobile base motion influences manipulator control, causing error accumulation in high-DoF systems, and they apply a single visual modality (all 2D or all 3D) uniformly across the entire task, ignoring that localization stages benefit from semantic 2D information while interaction stages require geometric 3D precision.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252146/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a 2-step observation history comprising three RGB camera views (exterior, left-wrist, right-wrist), a 3D point cloud from a depth camera, robot proprioceptive state (base linear/angular velocities, manipulator joint positions), and a natural language instruction. The 3D point cloud is tokenized via Lift3D's virtual-plane alignment before being processed by SigLIP. Output is an action chunk of length k containing absolute base velocities and relative manipulator joint position deltas, executed at 30 Hz via action chunking from a 5 Hz inference rate on a single RTX 4090.

## Method

AC-DiT is a two-head Diffusion Transformer (total ~1.2B trainable parameters) built on SigLIP encoders for images, point clouds, and text. The first key mechanism, Mobility-to-Body Conditioning, pretrains a lightweight 170M-parameter DiT mobility head on base actions alone, then extracts latent mobility features by concatenating action tokens from its final DiT block across five denoising steps; these features are injected via cross-attention into the full mobile manipulation head so that whole-body action prediction is conditioned on anticipated base motion. The second mechanism, Perception-Aware Multimodal Adaptation, projects 2D image features (per camera view), 3D point cloud features, and language features into a shared space via MLP projectors, computes cosine similarity between each visual modality and the language embedding to obtain scalar importance weights, and applies these weights to reweight visual tokens before concatenation into the action head's conditioning input. Training proceeds in two stages: 20K iterations of mobility-head pretraining (freezing all but LoRA and the mobility head), followed by 30K iterations of full-model training with denoising MSE loss on both base and manipulator actions.

## Results

On the ManiSkill-HAB mobile manipulation benchmark (7 tasks), AC-DiT achieves a mean success rate of 55.6%, outperforming the strongest baseline RDT (42.9%) by 12.7 points. On the RoboTwin bimanual benchmark (6 tasks), AC-DiT reaches 90.1% mean success rate versus 73.3% for 3DP and 70.0% for RDT. In real-world experiments on four long-horizon mobile bimanual tasks, AC-DiT achieves overall success rates of 50.0%, 56.3%, 43.8%, and 43.8%, compared to π₀'s 31.3%, 25.0%, 18.8%, and 12.5%, and ACT's 0.0% across three of four tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AC-DiT outperforms all seven baselines on the primary MSHab benchmark. Against the strongest competitor RDT (42.9%), AC-DiT gains 12.7 points, with particularly large margins on Fridge Open Door (90.7% vs. 82.7%) and Counter Open Drawer (81.3% vs. 44.0%), where explicit mobility-to-body conditioning reduces error accumulation. The flow-based VLA model π₀ (33.5%) trails AC-DiT by 22.1 points, attributed to its lack of explicit locomotion-manipulation coordination modeling. Pure 2D (DP, 28.8%) and 3D (3DP, 21.6%) diffusion policies underperform substantially, with 3DP failing entirely on navigation-heavy tasks due to difficulty processing scene-level point clouds. EquiBot (16.0%) performs worst, likely because its object-centric point cloud assumption conflicts with the scene-level point clouds in MSHab. On the RoboTwin bimanual benchmark, AC-DiT (90.1%) surpasses 3DP (73.3%) and RDT (70.0%) by 16.8 and 20.1 points respectively, demonstrating that the dual-arm-to-dual-arm conditioning mechanism generalizes beyond mobile-base coordination. In real-world tests, AC-DiT achieves 50.0% on Cucumber-in-Basket versus π₀'s 31.3% and ACT's 0.0%, confirming the simulation gains transfer to physical deployment.

## Relation to Prior Work

AC-DiT builds directly on the Diffusion Transformer (DiT) architecture [Peebles & Xie, ICCV 2023] and the RDT-1B diffusion foundation model [Liu et al., 2024] for action generation, while adopting the Lift3D [Jia et al., 2024] strategy for 3D point cloud tokenization via LoRA-adapted SigLIP. It departs from prior end-to-end mobile manipulation methods (ACT, π₀, RDT) by introducing two explicit coordination mechanisms: a mobility-to-body conditioning that pretrains a lightweight DiT head on base actions and feeds latent mobility features into the full-body action head, and a perception-aware multimodal adaptation that dynamically reweights 2D image and 3D point cloud tokens based on cosine similarity to language features. Unlike VLA models that discretize actions or treat all visual inputs uniformly, AC-DiT uses a unified SigLIP backbone across all modalities and a two-stage training procedure (mobility pretraining → full-body fine-tuning) to explicitly model the base-to-manipulator dependency.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical and underexplored gap in end-to-end mobile manipulation: the lack of explicit coordination modeling between the mobile base and manipulator, and the rigid use of a single visual modality throughout the task. The mobility-to-body conditioning and perception-aware adaptation are lightweight, interpretable mechanisms that could be adopted in other whole-body control settings. However, the method is limited by its reliance on imitation learning (sensitive to demonstration quality and quantity), evaluation on a single robot platform (Agilex Cobot Magic), and a modest number of real-world trials (17 per task). Open questions include whether the coordination mechanisms generalize to humanoid or quadruped platforms, whether the dynamic modality weighting scales to more camera views or sensor types, and whether the two-stage training can be replaced by a single unified objective.
