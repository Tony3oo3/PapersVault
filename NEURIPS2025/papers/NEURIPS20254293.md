## Problem

Existing zero-shot multi-talker dialogue generation systems rely on autoregressive components and intermediate token representations, resulting in slow inference, limited controllability over conversational dynamics (overlap, pauses, timing), speaker identity drift across turns, and dependence on paired audio-text prompts. No prior system achieves fully non-autoregressive, end-to-end synthesis of mixed multi-speaker mel-spectrograms with fine-grained temporal control.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254293/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes two parallel text streams (one per speaker), where each stream contains character-level sequences for active speech segments temporally anchored by start/end timestamps, plus a special silence token [S] for gaps, along with short acoustic prompt mel-spectrograms for each speaker. No transcription of the prompt audio is required. The output is a single mixed 100-dimensional log mel-filter bank spectrogram (10.7 ms frames at 24 kHz) representing both speakers simultaneously, which is converted to waveform via a BigVGAN vocoder.

## Method

CoVoMix2 is a fully non-autoregressive flow-matching model (24-layer transformer with U-Net skip connections, 0.3B params) that learns a continuous ODE mapping from Gaussian noise to mixed mel-spectrograms, conditioned on disentangled multistream transcriptions and speaker prompts. Three key design strategies enable this: (1) transcription-level speaker disentanglement places each speaker's text in a separate parallel stream with explicit silence tokens, enabling precise control over overlap and timing; (2) sentence-level alignment uses raw characters (not phonemes) with continuation tokens [P] to pad segments, letting the model learn intrinsic character-to-mel-frame alignment without explicit duration prediction; (3) prompt-level random masking selects a random monologue segment per speaker as the acoustic prompt, replaces its text with [Spk1]/[Spk2] indicator tokens, and excludes the prompt region from the loss to prevent leakage. Training uses a two-stage curriculum (60k hours monologue pretraining, then dialogue fine-tuning with mixed data sources) and classifier-free guidance at inference with 32 NFE ODE solver steps.

## Results

On the dialogue test set (1000 transcriptions from DailyDialog with LibriSpeech prompts), CoVoMix2 achieves an RTF of 0.30, WER of 5.73%, SA-WER of 6.31%, SA-SIM of 0.56, and UTMOS of 3.10. It is the only model to achieve a positive CMOS score (0.00 as reference), while both baselines score negatively (MoonCast: −0.25, Sesame: −0.39). CoVoMix2 also exhibits the lowest standard deviations across all metrics, indicating more stable generation quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoVoMix2 outperforms MoonCast on every metric: it is 4.6× faster (RTF 0.30 vs. 1.37), reduces SA-WER from 20.40% to 6.31%, improves SA-SIM from 0.40 to 0.56, and raises UTMOS from 2.65 to 3.10. Against Sesame, CoVoMix2 is 6.9× faster (RTF 0.30 vs. 2.08), achieves a lower SA-WER (6.31% vs. 9.65%), higher SA-SIM (0.56 vs. 0.49), and better UTMOS (3.10 vs. 2.70), while WER is essentially tied (5.73% vs. 5.62%). The paper attributes MoonCast's instability to its AR language-model backbone (hallucination, repetition, improper termination) and Sesame's speaker confusion to its shared contextual input containing both speakers' prompts. In subjective CMOS evaluation, both baselines are rated worse than CoVoMix2, with judges citing better pitch, intonation, rhythm, and less distortion for CoVoMix2.

## Relation to Prior Work

CoVoMix2 is the direct successor to CoVoMix (NeurIPS 2024), replacing its multi-stream AR text-to-semantic model plus NAR acoustic decoder with a fully non-autoregressive flow-matching pipeline that predicts mel-spectrograms directly from disentangled multi-stream transcriptions. The flow-matching backbone follows the architecture and sentence-level alignment strategy established by E2-TTS (SLT 2024) and F5-TTS, which demonstrated that AR decoding and explicit duration modeling can be eliminated for single-speaker TTS. The key conceptual departure from the broader multi-talker dialogue generation literature (MoonCast, Sesame, NotebookLM, Soundstorm) is the elimination of all intermediate token representations and autoregressive components, combined with transcription-level speaker disentanglement into parallel streams and prompt-level random masking to remove the need for prompt transcriptions at inference.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 4–7× inference speedup (RTF 0.30 vs. 1.37/2.08) and the use of only 3k hours of dialogue data versus 215k hours for MoonCast make zero-shot multi-talker dialogue generation substantially more accessible for practical deployment in podcast creation, virtual agents, and dubbing. The finding that a single prompt at the dialogue's beginning outperforms per-turn prompt conditioning (as in MoonCast) for speaker consistency challenges the assumption that repeated conditioning is necessary for identity preservation. Open questions include scaling beyond two speakers, handling multilingual and code-switching scenarios, and whether the reliance on ASR-transcribed and simulated overlap data limits naturalness in disfluent or highly overlapping real conversations.
