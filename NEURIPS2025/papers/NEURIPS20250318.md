## Problem

Standard primal-dual methods for constrained MDPs fail in the robust setting because the state occupancy measure becomes non-convex when worst-case transition models depend on the policy, breaking strong duality. The state-of-the-art epigraph approach (EPIRC-PGS) circumvents this via binary search over a scalar parameter, incurring O(ε⁻⁴) iteration complexity and significant wall-clock overhead that grows sharply with the discount factor γ. The paper asks whether a binary-search-free formulation can achieve both feasibility guarantees and a faster O(ε⁻²) iteration bound.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes a nominal transition kernel P₀, per-state cost/reward functions c_i (bounded in [0,1]), constraint thresholds b_i, a KL-divergence uncertainty radius C_KL defining the (s,a)-rectangular uncertainty set, and a fixed scalar λ (set to 50 in all experiments without per-environment tuning). It outputs a Markovian policy π that is simultaneously ε-suboptimal in worst-case cost and strictly feasible (zero constraint violation) under Assumption 1, or ε-violation without that assumption. The robust policy evaluator is assumed as an oracle returning max_{P∈𝒫} J_{c_i}^{π,P}, which for the KL set admits a closed-form solution requiring only samples from the nominal model.

## Method

The key reformulation replaces the constrained problem with a pointwise-maximum objective min_π max{J_{c₀}^π/λ, max_n[J_{c_n}^π − b_n]}, which automatically prioritizes constraint repair when violations exist and reward optimization when all constraints are satisfied, eliminating the need for binary search. The RNPG algorithm performs a natural policy gradient update at each iteration: it identifies the most-violated objective index i_t, evaluates the corresponding robust Q-function under the worst-case model, and applies a per-state mirror descent step with KL regularization (equivalently, a softmax policy update). The main analytical difficulty is that the active objective index changes across iterations, preventing direct use of the standard robust performance-difference lemma; the authors overcome this by combining a support-compatibility assumption (Assumption 2), Hölder's inequality, and Pinsker's inequality to bound the telescoping sum. This yields O(ε⁻²) iteration complexity for a feasible, ε-suboptimal policy (improving over O(ε⁻⁴) for EPIRC-PGS) and O(ε⁻⁴) without the strict-feasibility assumption. The framework extends to function approximation via an IPM-based robust Bellman operator with an L₂ regularization term on critic parameters, instantiated as a Robust Constrained Actor-Critic (RCAC) for continuous-state problems like Cartpole.

## Results

RNPG achieves a feasible (zero-violation) policy with at most ε-suboptimality after O(ε⁻²) iterations, improving upon the O(ε⁻⁴) iteration complexity of EPIRC-PGS. In wall-clock time, RNPG is at least 4× faster than EPIRC-PGS at γ=0.9 and 6× faster at γ=0.995 on Constrained River-swim, and 3× to 5× faster on Garnet (Table 1). In the CRS environment, RNPG is the only method that produces a feasible policy, while EPIRC-PGS significantly violates the constraint threshold. In the function approximation (Cartpole) setting, RNPG is the only approach that achieves both feasibility and competitive reward at test time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against EPIRC-PGS [8] (the state-of-the-art epigraph-based method), RNPG wins on every metric tested: it achieves lower wall-clock time (4–6× speedup depending on γ and environment), produces a feasible policy in CRS where EPIRC-PGS violates the constraint, and attains higher expected reward in Garnet and Modified Frozenlake while maintaining feasibility. The speedup is attributed to eliminating the binary search over b₀ that EPIRC-PGS requires at each outer-loop iteration. Against the robust version of CRPO [11,47] in the function approximation (Cartpole) setting, RNPG outperforms on both reward and feasibility—robust CRPO fails to achieve a feasible policy at test time, whereas RNPG does. The paper also notes that EPIRC-PGS's binary search approach breaks down under noisy value-function estimation (common in large state spaces), a failure mode that RNPG avoids by design.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the unconstrained robust MDP natural policy gradient framework of Wang et al. [48] to the constrained setting, adapting the KL-regularized mirror descent policy update and Bregman divergence analysis. It departs from the state-of-the-art EPIRC-PGS [8], which solves RCMDP via an epigraph reformulation requiring binary search over a hyperparameter b₀, by instead formulating a pointwise-max objective that directly balances the scaled objective cost against constraint violations. The robust policy evaluator leverages the closed-form KL-uncertainty solution from Iyengar [31], and the function approximation extension draws on the IPM-based robust Bellman operator from Zhou et al. [54].

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The O(ε⁻²) iteration complexity (vs. O(ε⁻⁴) for EPIRC-PGS) and the guarantee of a strictly feasible policy (not merely ε-violation) represent meaningful theoretical advances for safe RL under model uncertainty. The elimination of binary search has direct practical impact, yielding 4–6× wall-clock speedups that grow with the discount factor, making robust constrained RL more tractable for long-horizon problems. Open questions include relaxing the support-overlap (Assumption 2) and uniform-minimizer (Assumption 3) conditions, establishing lower bounds, extending guarantees to other uncertainty sets, and providing iteration complexity for the function approximation (RCAC) setting.
