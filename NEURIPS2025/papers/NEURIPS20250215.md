## Problem

Existing video anomaly detection paradigms face a fundamental trade-off: fully-supervised methods require expensive frame-level annotations, semi-supervised methods miss subtle anomalies that resemble normal behavior, and weakly-supervised methods suffer from false alarms due to lack of precise anomaly reference. This paper introduces Single-Frame supervised VAD (SF-VAD), which uses only one annotated abnormal frame per video, and addresses the core challenge of generalizing this sparse supervision to full event-level anomaly understanding across multiple temporally disjoint abnormal intervals.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250215/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes untrimmed video sequences split into 16-frame non-overlapping clips, with features extracted by frozen I3D (1024-dim motion) and CLIP ViT-B/16 (512-dim appearance) encoders. During training, each abnormal video provides a single frame index annotation (the first abnormal frame an annotator identifies while freely navigating the video), while normal videos carry no frame labels. At inference, the model outputs a per-clip anomaly score vector that is thresholded to produce frame-level binary anomaly predictions.

## Method

The proposed Frame-guided Progressive Learning (FPL) framework operates in two stages. Stage I (FARE) employs evidential deep learning with a Beta distribution to estimate per-frame anomaly relevance: an evidence encoder is trained exclusively on the annotated abnormal frame and the most prominent normal frame via a MIL-style evidential loss, yielding a relevance score R = 1 − 2/S (where S is total Beta evidence) that quantifies each frame's association with the noise-free abnormal pattern. Stage II performs relevance-aware pattern decoupling: an abnormal event mining algorithm first identifies key frames across multiple disjoint events using cosine feature similarity to the annotated frame (gated by variance and distance thresholds), then expands each key frame to a full abnormal interval by exploiting the concave shape of the relevance curve; simultaneously, normal patterns are decoupled by uniformly sampling pre-event frames and selecting the lowest-scoring post-event frames as negative examples. The backbone uses a Transformer-based Temporal Modeling module with both global attention and a sliding-window local attention (fused via a learned gate) to capture multi-scale temporal regularities, followed by separate MLP heads for evidence prediction (Stage I) and final anomaly scoring (Stage II).

## Results

On UCF-Crime, the proposed SF-VAD method achieves 90.23% AUC, a 3.26% absolute improvement over the best prior weakly-supervised method (VadCLIP, 88.02%). On ShanghaiTech, it reaches 98.41% AUC (+0.81% over PEL4VAD's 98.14%), and on XD-Violence it attains 89.56% AP (+3.22% over HLGAtt's 86.34%). Notably, these results are obtained using only I3D RGB features without text or CLIP augmentation, while also achieving the lowest False Alarm Rate (0.01% on UCF-Crime, 0.00% on ShanghaiTech) among all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed SF-VAD method outperforms all 28 compared baselines across all three datasets. On UCF-Crime, it surpasses the strongest weakly-supervised competitors—VadCLIP (88.02%), UR-DMU (86.97%), and MGFN (86.67%)—by 2.21–3.56% AUC, despite using only I3D RGB features without text or CLIP. On ShanghaiTech, it edges out PEL4VAD (98.14%) and CoMo (97.60%) by 0.27–0.81% AUC. On XD-Violence, it leads HLGAtt (86.34%) and PEL4VAD (85.59%) by 3.22–3.97% AP. In terms of False Alarm Rate (Table 2), the method achieves 0.01% on UCF-Crime and 0.00% on ShanghaiTech, substantially lower than UR-DMU (0.65% FAR on XD) and MIST (0.13% on UCF), demonstrating superior suppression of pseudo-anomalies through relevance-aware normal pattern decoupling.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper introduces a new supervision paradigm for video anomaly detection (VAD) that sits between the existing weakly-supervised (video-level labels) and fully-supervised (frame-level labels) settings, using exactly one annotated abnormal frame per abnormal video. Methodologically, it builds on evidential deep learning (Sensoy et al., NeurIPS 2018) to quantify anomaly relevance via Beta-distribution uncertainty, and adopts the Transformer-based Temporal Modeling architecture from Pu et al. (TIP 2024) for multi-scale temporal feature extraction. Conceptually, it draws from the broader inexact-supervision literature (point supervision in segmentation, frame supervision in temporal action localization) but is the first to formalize single-frame supervision specifically for VAD, addressing the gap between sparse frame-level cues and temporally extended abnormal events through a two-stage progressive learning framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The SF-VAD paradigm offers a practically compelling trade-off: annotation cost comparable to weakly-supervised VAD (annotators need not review full videos) while delivering fine-grained anomaly reference that improves detection of subtle, low-salience anomalies (e.g., shoplifting, abuse) and dramatically reduces false alarms (FAR 0.01% vs. 0.65% for prior SOTA). The three released SF-VAD benchmarks (ShanghaiTech, UCF-Crime, XD-Violence) provide a new evaluation standard for the community. Open questions include whether the single-frame assumption generalizes to domains where anomalies are extremely brief or visually homogeneous, whether the method extends to multi-modal (audio-visual) settings, and how performance scales when the annotated frame falls in a particularly uninformative region of the event.
