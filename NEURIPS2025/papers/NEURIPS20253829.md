## Problem

Existing long-tail sequential recommendation methods rely on arbitrary fixed-ratio partitioning (e.g., top 20% as head) that fails to capture dataset-specific distributional characteristics, and their head-to-tail augmentation strategies suffer from negative transfer due to large representational gaps between head and tail entities. This paper addresses both issues by partitioning items into multiple balanced subsets rather than a binary head/tail split, and by enabling stepwise, distribution-level knowledge transfer that avoids the seesaw effect of sacrificing head performance for tail gains.

## Input / Output

The method takes a user's ordered interaction sequence of items as input and produces a ranked probability list over all items, from which top-K recommendations are extracted. A key structural detail is that the item vocabulary is hierarchically partitioned into L subsets via a dynamic-programming algorithm, and each expert network receives a progressively expanding union of subsets (from tail-only to full item set), so the same user sequence is consumed by multiple experts with different item scopes. Evaluation uses HR@10 and NDCG@10 with 100 random negative samples, reported separately for head/tail items and head/tail users.

## Method

HPSERec comprises three modules operating in an alternating two-stage training loop. The Distribution Balancing Module defines a generalized-entropy imbalance score Φ_k (tunable via parameter α) plus a size-regularization penalty, solved by dynamic programming to partition items into L subsets that minimize within-subset skewness while maintaining balanced sizes. The Feedforward Module assigns a separate expert network to each subset with progressively expanding item inputs, and transfers knowledge from upstream (tail-focused) to downstream (global) experts via temperature-softened KL-divergence distillation restricted to adjacent levels. The Feedback Module reverses the direction, using entropy-regularized optimal transport (Sinkhorn iterations) over cosine-distance cost matrices to softly align user embeddings from lower-level experts toward the distribution produced by higher-level experts, providing a differentiable, globally optimal supervision signal without requiring one-to-one matching. Training alternates between a feedforward stage (top-down, loss = recommendation loss + β·distillation loss) and a feedback stage (bottom-up, loss = transport cost), enabling bidirectional refinement across the expert hierarchy.

## Results

HPSERec achieves the best overall performance across all three datasets, outperforming the strongest baseline (LLM-ESR) by 6.8% on Amazon Beauty (HR@10: 0.5281 vs. 0.4945), 10.3% on Yelp (HR@10: 0.6827 vs. 0.6190), and 10.6% on Amazon Music (HR@10: 0.6592 vs. 0.5958). The gains are most pronounced on tail items, where HPSERec reaches HR@10 of 0.3203 (Beauty), 0.3252 (Yelp), and 0.4425 (Music), while maintaining competitive head-item performance (e.g., 0.7306 HR@10 on Beauty vs. 0.7270 for LLM-ESR). All improvements over the best baseline are statistically significant (two-sided t-test, p < 0.05).

## Baselines & Comparisons

HPSERec outperforms all seven baselines on every dataset and every metric. Against the strongest baseline LLM-ESR (NeurIPS 2024), HPSERec gains 6.8–10.6% in overall HR@10 and 10.3–35.7% in tail-item HR@10, while also matching or exceeding LLM-ESR on head-item performance (e.g., 0.8361 vs. 0.8045 HR@10 on Yelp head items). The traditional long-tail methods CITIES and MELT exhibit a clear seesaw effect: CITIES sacrifices head-item accuracy (e.g., 0.4974 vs. 0.7187 for SASRec on Beauty) to marginally improve tail items, while MELT achieves the best head-item scores (0.8055 on Beauty) but underperforms in the extreme long-tail. The LLM-based baselines RLMRec and LLMInit improve tail-item representations over SASRec but still trail HPSERec by 10–20% in tail-item HR@10. Notably, HPSERec improves both head and tail simultaneously without a dedicated user-side module, suggesting that item representation gains in the shared embedding space propagate to user embeddings.

## Relation to Prior Work

HPSERec builds on the sequential recommendation backbone (SASRec, BERT4Rec) and the long-tail recommendation line of work (CITIES, MELT, INSERT), but departs from the prevailing binary head/tail partitioning paradigm by introducing a data-driven imbalance metric with dynamic-programming-based multi-subset partitioning. It repurposes mixture-of-experts architecture not as a static routing mechanism but as a progressive hierarchy where each expert sees an expanding item set, connected by adjacent-level knowledge distillation. The feedback module is a novel application of entropy-regularized optimal transport (Sinkhorn) to align user representations across expert levels, replacing the hard one-to-one matching used in prior head-to-tail transfer methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that replacing ad-hoc 20/80 head-tail splits with a principled multi-subset partitioning yields substantial gains (up to ~10% over the best baseline), and that soft OT-based alignment avoids the negative transfer that plagues direct head-to-tail augmentation. However, the framework introduces multiple hyperparameters (α, β, τ, γ, L) and the partitioning algorithm has non-trivial computational cost, limiting immediate scalability to very large item catalogs. Open questions include whether the hierarchical expert structure generalizes to other recommendation paradigms (e.g., graph-based or LLM-augmented SRS) and whether the OT feedback module can be made more efficient for production-scale deployments.
