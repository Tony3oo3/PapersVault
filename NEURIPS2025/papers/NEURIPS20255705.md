## Problem

Existing image editing and generation methods follow a prompt-to-image paradigm that requires extensive user intervention (prompt refinement, parameter tuning) and focuses on explicit, text-driven modifications rather than autonomous creative transformation. No prior work has formally defined or addressed the task of 'creative editing'—transforming images into novel, aesthetically rich compositions with minimal user guidance while balancing originality, coherence, and artistic intent.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255705/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For editing, the method takes a user-provided RGB image plus a minimal text concept (e.g., 'a creative couch') and produces a disentangled creative edit that preserves key structural elements via Canny-conditioned ControlNet (scale 0.4). For generation, it accepts only a short text concept (e.g., 'a couch') and synthesizes a novel image from scratch using FLUX.1-dev. Optional user guidance instructions (e.g., 'monster') can be injected mid-pipeline to steer creative direction. The full pipeline runs in 3–5 minutes on a 48 GB NVIDIA L40 GPU with up to K=3 self-enhancement iterations, and the framework extends to video generation via CogVideoX.

## Method

CREA is a training-free, multi-agent orchestration framework built on AutoGen that coordinates five specialized GPT-4o agents—Creative Director, Prompt Architect, Generative Executor, Art Critic, and Refinement Strategist—through a three-phase loop: Pre-Generation Planning, Image Synthesis/Editing, and Post-Generation Evaluation with Self-Enhancement. The Prompt Architect decomposes a creativity blueprint into six contrastive prompts (one per creativity principle: Originality, Expressiveness, Aesthetic Appeal, Technical Execution, Unexpected Associations, Interpretability & Depth) and fuses them via Chain-of-Thought reasoning into a single high-creativity prompt. The Art Critic scores each principle on a 1–5 scale using a multimodal LLM-as-a-Judge, computing a Creativity Index (CI); if CI falls below a threshold (24 for editing, 26 for generation), the Refinement Strategist identifies weak dimensions and the Prompt Architect adjusts the prompt (P_r = P_e + ΔP) for the next regeneration cycle. The Generative Executor interfaces with FLUX.1-dev for text-to-image synthesis and ControlNet (Canny conditioning) for disentangled editing, dynamically selecting model parameters such as CFG scale (3.5–40) and conditioning scale (0.1–0.5).

## Results

For creative image editing, CREA achieves the best CLIP score (0.417), LPIPS diversity (0.414), Vendi score (3.70), DINO structural preservation (0.744), FID (294.19), and KID (14.02) across all editing baselines, while also scoring highest on user-perceived creativity (Q2: 3.74/5). For creative image generation, CREA attains the best LPIPS (0.709), Vendi (10.44), FID (248.67), KID (5.94), and user creativity rating (Q2: 4.16/5), though SDXL edges it on CLIP alignment (0.404 vs. 0.360). The LLM-as-a-judge evaluation (Table 3) confirms CREA's dominance, with the generation variant scoring 27.68/30 total creativity (89.87% normalized) versus 21.85 for SDXL and 21.25 for ConceptLab.

## Baselines & Comparisons

For editing, CREA decisively outperforms all four baselines (LEDITS++, InstructPix2Pix, SDEdit, TurboEdit) on every metric: it leads CLIP by 0.021–0.038, LPIPS by 0.106–0.222, Vendi by 0.51–1.76, and KID by 4.76–10.77, while also achieving the best user creativity rating (3.74 vs. 2.63–3.59). For generation, CREA beats Flux and SDXL on diversity (Vendi 10.44 vs. 5.84/6.63), perceptual distance (LPIPS 0.709 vs. 0.650/0.636), and image quality (FID 248.67 vs. 270.69/282.22), and substantially outperforms both on user-perceived creativity (Q2: 4.16 vs. 3.00/3.56). Against ConceptLab, the closest creative-generation competitor, CREA matches its Vendi score (10.44 vs. 10.38) but achieves a markedly better CLIP score (0.360 vs. 0.334) and user creativity rating (4.16 vs. 3.18), indicating superior semantic alignment without sacrificing diversity. The one metric where CREA trails is CLIP for generation, where SDXL's 0.404 reflects its tendency to produce highly colorful, conventionally 'creative-looking' images that align well with CLIP's text-image similarity, despite SDXL's much lower diversity (Vendi 6.63).

## Relation to Prior Work

CREA positions itself at the intersection of multi-agent LLM orchestration (via AutoGen) and diffusion-based image synthesis (FLUX, ControlNet), departing from prior creative generation methods (ConceptLab, ProCreate, Inspiration Tree, C3) that rely on single-pass energy-based repulsion or constraint enforcement, and from standard editing pipelines (InstructPix2Pix, SDEdit, LEDITS++, TurboEdit) that perform explicit text-driven modifications. The key conceptual departure is framing creativity as an iterative, multi-role collaborative dialogue grounded in six principles from creativity literature (Boden, Guilford, Amabile, Ramachandran), rather than a one-shot optimization. The LLM-as-a-Judge evaluation mechanism builds on prior work showing multimodal LLMs can approximate human subjective assessment, while the Chain-of-Thought prompt fusion draws on contrastive prompting techniques.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CREA is the first to formally define 'creative image editing' as a distinct task, separating it from both standard instruction-based editing and unconstrained generation, which opens a new axis of research for autonomous creative AI. However, the evaluation is somewhat circular—GPT-4o both guides generation and serves as the judge—making it difficult to disentangle whether the multi-agent loop genuinely improves creativity or merely aligns outputs with the LLM's own aesthetic priors. The small user study (n=50) and the reliance on a single generative backbone (FLUX) limit generalizability claims. Open questions include whether the orchestration overhead (3–5 minutes per image, multiple LLM API calls) is justified over a well-crafted single prompt, and how to develop non-circular, human-grounded metrics for visual creativity.
