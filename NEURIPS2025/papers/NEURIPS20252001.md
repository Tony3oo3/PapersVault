## Problem

Existing local Bayesian optimization methods such as GIBO and MPD leverage only first-order (gradient) information from GP surrogates, leaving the curvature structure exploited by classical sequential quadratic programming (SQP) untapped. No prior method integrates the full SQP subproblem structure—including Hessian-based quadratic objectives and linearized constraints—into a BO framework while explicitly accounting for uncertainty in both function and derivative estimates, which is critical for high-probability improvement in high-dimensional black-box settings.

## Input / Output

At each iteration the algorithm consumes only noisy zero-order evaluations of the objective and all constraints (no gradient or Hessian queries), along with the compact search-space bounds. It produces a search direction p_t by solving a second-order cone program (B-SUB), then selects the next evaluation point via constrained Thompson sampling along the one-dimensional segment {x_t + α p_t : α ∈ [0,1]} using a fixed budget of M=3 line-search evaluations. The final output is the best feasible point found within the total evaluation budget T.

## Method

BayeSQP builds a joint GP posterior over the function value, gradient, and Hessian of both the objective and each constraint from zero-order data alone, exploiting the closure of GPs under differentiation. At each iteration it constructs an uncertainty-aware SQP subproblem (B-SUB) that minimizes a value-at-risk objective and enforces probabilistic constraint satisfaction, reformulating the resulting chance constraints as second-order cone constraints via Cholesky factorization of the joint function–gradient covariance. The Hessian of the Lagrangian is approximated once per iteration from the GP posterior mean, and local curvature is refreshed by sampling K = d+1 points in a small ball around the current iterate. A constrained Thompson sampling line search (budget M=3) then picks the next point along the search direction, selecting the best feasible sample or the least-violating infeasible one if no feasible point is found.

## Results

On the Speed Reducer benchmark (7D, 11 constraints), BayeSQP achieves the best objective value of 3001.10 (median) with a runtime of 91.83s, outperforming SCBO (3006.89, 286.46s) and C-logEI (3002.81, 3464.59s). On constrained standard benchmarks, BayeSQP finds feasible solutions in 32/32 seeds across all problems, whereas C-logEI only achieves 15/32 feasibility on Ackley20D(constr.). In within-model comparisons, BayeSQP outperforms all baselines from dimension 16 onward in both unconstrained and constrained settings, with the gap widening as dimensionality increases.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against TuRBO/SCBO, BayeSQP underperforms on unconstrained multi-modal benchmarks (e.g., 8.95 vs. 0.77 on Ackley 5D) due to its local search nature and sensitivity to initialization, but matches or exceeds it on constrained problems where SCBO's trust-region approach struggles with feasibility. Against C-logEI, BayeSQP achieves comparable or better objective values on constrained benchmarks while guaranteeing feasibility in all 32 seeds (vs. 15/32 for C-logEI on Ackley 20D constr.), and is ~38× faster on Speed Reducer (91.83s vs. 3464.59s). Against SAASBO, BayeSQP wins decisively in high-dimensional within-model settings (dim ≥ 16) and is the only method that handles constraints natively. Against MPD, BayeSQP is consistently superior (e.g., 8.95 vs. 12.57 on Ackley 5D; −3.30 vs. −0.61 on Hartmann), as MPD's fixed descent-direction strategy degrades in higher dimensions. In the within-model comparisons (Figures 4–5), BayeSQP outperforms all four baselines at dimensions 16 and above, with the advantage growing with dimensionality, and completes each iteration in orders of magnitude less time than SCBO and C-logEI despite computing full Hessian estimates.

## Relation to Prior Work

BayeSQP extends the GIBO line of local Bayesian optimization (Müller et al., 2021; Nguyen et al., 2022) from first-order to second-order methods by incorporating a GP-estimated Hessian of the Lagrangian into an SQP-style subproblem. The structural template comes from classical sequential quadratic programming (Nocedal & Wright, 2006), while the line search borrows constrained Thompson sampling from SCBO (Eriksson & Poloczek, 2021). The key conceptual departure from GIBO and MPD is the uncertainty-aware reformulation of the QP subproblem into a second-order cone program via value-at-risk and probabilistic constraint satisfaction, which is enabled by the joint GP posterior over function, gradient, and Hessian (building on Hennig & Kiefel, 2013; De Roos et al., 2021). Unlike prior augmented-Lagrangian BO approaches (Gramacy et al., 2016; Picheny et al., 2016) that rely on EI-type acquisition functions, BayeSQP operates fully locally with a deterministic subproblem solve followed by a one-dimensional posterior-sampling line search.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

BayeSQP provides a principled bridge between classical constrained optimization and modern black-box BO, offering a tractable SOCP subproblem that naturally handles multiple constraints and high-probability feasibility guarantees—something GIBO-style methods cannot do. The practical impact is notable: orders-of-magnitude faster wall-clock times than SCBO and C-logEI despite computing full Hessians, and guaranteed feasible solutions across all seeds on constrained benchmarks where C-logEI fails. Key limitations include sensitivity to initialization (inherent to any local method), cubic scaling of Hessian computation with dimension, and strong dependence on kernel choice. Open questions include whether incorporating Hessian uncertainty into the subproblem, adaptive sub-sampling, or BFGS-type Hessian updates from gradient histories would improve robustness, and how to handle the method's local nature in highly multi-modal landscapes.
