## Problem

Existing Federated Prompt Learning methods rely on global prompt information available only after the second communication round, making them inherently multi-round, while existing one-shot federated learning methods optimize for seen tasks but neglect cross-task generalization. This paper addresses both gaps by designing a one-shot FPL method that preserves transferable knowledge in the frozen text encoder while still adapting to local data distributions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250405/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images (encoded by a frozen ViT-B/16 vision encoder) and text prompts consisting of 10 learnable prompt tokens (512-dim) concatenated with a fixed class-name template (e.g., 'a photo of a [CLASS]'), processed through a frozen CLIP text encoder with a custom attention mask. The output is a class label obtained via softmax over cosine similarities between the image embedding and per-class text embeddings. In the federated setting, each client uploads only its learned prompt matrix and class-wise visual prototypes (n randomly weighted feature averages per class) in a single communication round.

## Method

GPR-NIAM introduces two complementary modules. The Attention Isolation (AttnIso) module imposes a structured mask on the text encoder's attention matrix: it hard-masks (−∞) all attention from learnable prompt tokens to original text tokens across every encoder layer, preventing the prompt from altering the frozen text embeddings, while simultaneously reweighting (λ) the reverse attention from the [EOS] token to text tokens to encourage extraction of task-relevant features. The Cross-Silo Collaborative Refinement (CSCR) module has each client compute class-wise visual prototypes via randomly weighted averaging of local image features, aggregates all prototypes into a global pool on the server, and refines the globally averaged prompt through a sigmoid-based contrastive alignment loss that pulls text representations toward their corresponding visual prototypes. Training follows a two-stage procedure: local prompt tuning with masked attention on private data, followed by centralized global prompt refinement against the prototype pool, all within a single client-server communication round. The framework is plug-and-play, integrating into existing prompt learning backbones such as CoOp (PromptFL) and TCP.

## Results

On the base-to-base/novel generalization task (β=0.5, averaged over 8 datasets), GPR-NIAM_T achieves the best harmonic mean (HM) of 75.30%, with 76.43% base accuracy and 74.48% novel accuracy, outperforming the next-best method TCP (HM 73.41%) by 1.89 points. On the leave-one-domain-out task, GPR-NIAM_T attains the highest average accuracy on both Office-Home (81.24%) and DomainNet (85.79%), surpassing the strongest baseline PromptFolio (77.92% / 84.35%) by 3.32 and 1.44 points respectively. The method requires only a single communication round (one-shot FL), whereas most competing FPL methods rely on multi-round aggregation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GPR-NIAM_T achieves the best HM across all 8 datasets (75.30%), beating TCP (73.41%), PromptFolio (73.15%), and PromptFL (72.64%) by 1.89, 2.15, and 2.66 points respectively. It dramatically outperforms FedELMY on novel-class accuracy (74.48% vs. 48.23%) and HM (75.30% vs. 60.03%), as FedELMY overfits to base classes at the expense of generalization. FedDISC, which relies on diffusion-model-generated synthetic data, underperforms all other methods (HM 53.87%), confirming the gap between synthetic and real samples. On domain generalization, GPR-NIAM_T ranks first in 9 out of 12 individual domains (Office-Home + DomainNet), with particularly large margins on Quickdraw (56.32% vs. 54.03% for DP-FPL) and Infograph (80.82% vs. 81.19% for PromptFolio, a near-tie). GPR-NIAM_T slightly trails TCP on novel-class accuracy (74.48% vs. 74.60%) but compensates with substantially higher base accuracy (76.43% vs. 72.46%), yielding a superior overall HM.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two lines of work: multi-round Federated Prompt Learning (FPL) methods (PromptFolio, DP-FPL, FedTPG, TCP) that rely on global prompt feedback available only after the second communication round, and One-Shot Federated Learning (OSFL) methods (FedDISC, FedELMY, FedOV) that eliminate iterative exchanges but optimize only for seen tasks. GPR-NIAM departs from both by introducing a non-interfering attention masking mechanism that makes the prompt-to-text interaction strictly unidirectional (blocking learnable tokens from modifying original text embeddings while reweighting the reverse flow), combined with a cross-silo prototype-guided refinement stage that calibrates the global prompt using decentralized visual knowledge. This positions the work as the first FPL method that simultaneously achieves one-shot communication efficiency and explicit cross-task generalization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The one-shot constraint is practically important for bandwidth-limited or latency-sensitive deployments where multi-round FPL is infeasible, and the demonstrated balance between base-class fitting and novel-class generalization addresses a gap that existing OSFL methods leave open. However, the failure case analysis reveals that GPR-NIAM's errors heavily overlap with zero-shot CLIP failures, suggesting the method inherits rather than overcomes the pre-trained model's blind spots. Open questions include whether the prototype transmission overhead scales acceptably in large-client federated systems, and whether the approach extends to multi-label, multi-modal, or medical imaging settings where the frozen-CLIP assumption may be less appropriate.
