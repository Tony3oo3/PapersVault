## Problem

Vanilla chain-of-thought prompting actually degrades instruction-following performance because LLMs produce superficial, paraphrasing-style reasoning that fails to decompose constraint compositions (And, Chain, Selection, Nested) into their hierarchical relationships. Existing solutions—supervised fine-tuning and template-guided inference—are task-specific, overfit to training constraints, and cannot generalize to unseen constraint types or structures. The paper asks how to systematically incentivize genuinely deep, structured reasoning for complex instruction following via test-time compute scaling.

## Input / Output

The method takes a complex instruction containing one or more atomic sub-instructions with compositional structures (And, Chain, Selection, Nested) and multiple verifiable constraints (lexical, numerical, format, semantic, stylistic, linguistic). The output is a structured response with explicit reasoning enclosed in <think> tags that must demonstrate constraint decomposition and relationship analysis, followed by the final answer in <answer> tags. For fast-thinking models, a CoT prompt suffix is prepended to the input; for slow-thinking (R1-style) models, no additional prompt is needed. Verification of constraint satisfaction uses both code-execution (for lexical/format constraints) and LLM-as-a-Judge boolean checks (for semantic/stylistic constraints).

## Method

RAIF combines three components: (1) a self-evolving data pipeline that synthesizes 13K complex instructions from WildChat/Alpaca seeds by randomly instantiating constraint templates and composing them per the ComplexBench taxonomy, with paired verification criteria; (2) GRPO-based RL with a rule-centric reward that sums a binary format reward (checking think/answer tag structure) and a piecewise accuracy reward that scores per-constraint satisfaction (+2 for all active constraints met, partial credit for subsets, −2 for none), using code execution for verifiable constraints and LLM-as-a-Judge boolean validation for semantic ones; (3) two stabilization mechanisms—superior CoT enforcement, which filters out training samples where all rollouts with reasoning score worse than their reasoning-free counterparts (preventing shallow reasoning from receiving reward), and behavior cloning of expert responses via an SFT loss term that anchors the policy distribution and prevents catastrophic drift during RL. The key insight is that unlike math problems where step-by-step derivation is naturally prerequisite, instruction-following has no enforced association between reasoning depth and answer quality, so the filtering and cloning mechanisms are essential to push the model beyond its default shallow paraphrasing.

## Results

On the seven instruction-following benchmarks (IFEval, CELLO, CFBench, ComplexBench, FB-Bench, FollowBench, InfoBench), RAIF achieves an average of 62.35 on Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct, a +11.74% gain over the I/O baseline (50.61), with performance comparable to the 8B LLaMA3.1-8B-Instruct (67.01 I/O). On ComplexBench specifically, RAIF (77.40) outperforms all SOTA baselines including Oracle Decomposition (76.26) and Self-Consistency (73.76). On the OOD IFBench, RAIF consistently improves across all model sizes, e.g., +6.27% on DeepSeek-Qwen7B and +5.77% on Ministral-8B, confirming generalization to unseen constraints.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Vanilla CoT prompting consistently degrades performance across all models (e.g., −11.79% on Qwen2.5-1.5B, −12.48% on LLaMA3.1-8B), confirming the paper's central claim that shallow, paraphrasing-style reasoning is harmful for instruction following. SDC (self-DeepClaude two-step inference) also fails to help, yielding negative or near-zero gains. SFT (knowledge distillation from a strong reasoner) helps small models (e.g., +9.06% on Qwen2.5-1.5B) but is neutral or negative for larger models (−0.21% on Qwen2.5-7B, −8.72% on Ministral-8B), indicating limited generalization. On ComplexBench, RAIF (77.40) outperforms Oracle Decomposition (76.26), Self-Consistency@10 (73.76), Self-Refine (74.00), FollowComplex (71.97), and Conifer (63.51), with the largest margins on Chain and Selection compositions where reasoning is most critical. On the OOD IFBench, RAIF improves all tested models (e.g., +6.27% on DeepSeek-Qwen7B, +5.77% on Ministral-8B), whereas SFT degrades Qwen2.5-7B (−3.08%) and Ministral-8B (−4.96%), demonstrating that RL-based reasoning generalizes better to unseen constraints than supervised distillation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of complex instruction-following benchmarks (IFEval, ComplexBench, CFBench) and RL-based reasoning cultivation (DeepSeek-R1, GRPO, DeepScaleR). It departs from SFT-based instruction-following methods (WizardLM, FollowComplex, Conifer) and from Tulu 3's RLVR approach, which is restricted to code-verifiable 'and'-structure constraints, by introducing rule-centric rewards that handle both code-executable and LLM-as-a-Judge verifiable constraints across And, Chain, Selection, and Nested compositions. The key methodological departure is the 'superior CoT enforcement' mechanism (sample-wise contrast between reasoning and non-reasoning rollouts) and behavior cloning to control policy drift, which the authors argue is necessary because instruction-following lacks the natural pathway dependency that makes math reasoning self-reinforcing under RL.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, reproducible recipe for cultivating instruction-following reasoning via RL, demonstrating that a 1.5B model can approach 8B-level performance on complex instruction benchmarks and generalize to unseen constraints (IFBench). However, the gains are heavily model-size-dependent (11.74% for 1.5B vs. 0.85% for 7B), LLaMA3.1-8B suffered complete model collapse, and absolute scores on OOD constraints remain low (17–32%), suggesting the approach is most effective for smaller, Qwen-family models. Open questions include whether the RL recipe transfers to other task domains beyond instruction following, whether the model-collapse issue is specific to LLaMA's pretraining or reflects a fundamental fragility of RL for instruction-following, and whether the superior CoT enforcement mechanism can be simplified or made more robust across architectures.
