## Problem

Existing multi-modal ReID methods are designed for either cross-modal retrieval (e.g., RGB→NI) or multi-modal fused retrieval (RNT→RNT) but cannot handle both simultaneously due to divergent optimization objectives. Additionally, all current approaches are category-specific (person or vehicle), requiring separate models for each category and wasting training and deployment resources. The paper introduces the M³T-ReID task, which demands a single unified model performing retrieval across all modalities and object categories at once.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253030/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes tri-modal image triplets (RGB, Near-Infrared, Thermal Infrared) resized to 256×128 pixels, processed through a shared pretrained CLIP visual encoder to produce class-token embeddings of fixed dimension D. At inference, cross-modal retrieval directly computes cosine similarity between embeddings from two different modalities, while multi-modal retrieval concatenates all three modality embeddings before matching. The model is evaluated across six cross-modal directions (R→N, N→R, R→T, T→R, N→T, T→N) and one multi-modal configuration (RNT→RNT) on both person and vehicle categories.

## Method

UPCL builds on a CLIP-based encoder supervised by label-smoothing cross-entropy, triplet loss, and bidirectional cross-modal alignment loss. The key innovation is the Unbiased Prototypes-guided Modality Enhancement (UPME) module, which computes modality-unbiased prototypes per identity (the mean of the three modality-specific prototypes) and uses them as contrastive anchors to simultaneously pull same-identity features toward identity-consistent semantics while reducing inter-modal divergence. The second module, Cluster Prototype Consistency Regularization (CPCR), applies the parameter-free FINCH clustering algorithm to the modality-unbiased prototypes within each category to derive category-consistent prototypes, then imposes a binary classification loss that pushes person features toward the person prototype and vehicle features toward the vehicle prototype, enabling the model to learn category-discriminative semantics without explicit category labels. The total objective combines the base loss with weighted UPME and CPCR terms (α=2.0, β=0.5).

## Results

On the person dataset RGBNT201, UPCL achieves a harmonic-mean mAP of 20.75% and Rank-1 of 19.97% across seven retrieval scenarios (six cross-modal + one multi-modal), surpassing the best baseline PromptMA (15.32% mAP, 10.95% Rank-1) by 5.43 and 9.02 percentage points respectively. On the vehicle dataset RGBNT100, UPCL reaches 22.63% mAP and 27.50% Rank-1, outperforming PromptMA (13.93% mAP, 11.28% Rank-1) by 8.70 and 16.22 points. In cross-domain evaluation on the unseen MSVR310 dataset, UPCL attains 11.44% mAP and 13.77% Rank-1, nearly tripling the next-best method PromptMA (4.28% mAP, 2.78% Rank-1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

UPCL decisively outperforms all five baselines on both datasets across every cross-modal scenario. Against the strongest competitor PromptMA [68], UPCL gains +5.43 mAP on RGBNT201 and +8.70 mAP on RGBNT100 in harmonic-mean, with the largest per-scenario margins in R→T and T→R directions (e.g., 16.77 vs 11.80 mAP for R→T on RGBNT201). The only scenario where UPCL trails a baseline is multi-modal RNT→RNT on RGBNT201, where PromptMA leads by 0.8 mAP (65.71 vs 64.91), though UPCL reverses this on RGBNT100 (79.41 vs 71.07). HTT [50], EDITOR [67], and DeMo [48] all remain below 5% harmonic-mean mAP on both datasets, indicating they fail to generalize when trained jointly across person and vehicle categories. TOP-ReID [46] shows moderate multi-modal performance (63.74 mAP on RGBNT201 RNT→RNT) but collapses in cross-modal settings (≤10.71 mAP), whereas UPCL maintains balanced performance across all seven protocols.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate lines of ReID research—cross-modal ReID (learning a shared space between heterogeneous modalities) and multi-modal ReID (fusing RGB/NI/TI for robust representations)—into a single model, while additionally spanning object categories (person and vehicle) that prior work treats as disjoint problems. The prototype mechanism is repurposed from its traditional ID-level role in few-shot and unsupervised ReID (Snell et al., Luo et al., Yang et al.) to operate at the modality level (modality-unbiased prototypes) and the category level (cluster-derived prototypes via FINCH), a conceptual shift that is the paper's core methodological contribution. The visual backbone is the pretrained CLIP encoder, and the training objective combines standard label-smoothing CE, triplet loss, and cross-modal InfoNCE alignment with the two proposed prototype-guided losses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The M³T-ReID formulation addresses a genuine practical gap: real surveillance deployments require a single system to retrieve both persons and vehicles across visible, near-infrared, and thermal cameras, yet every existing method is trained per-modality-pair and per-category, leading to redundant models. The cross-domain evaluation on MSVR310 (where most baselines collapse to ~2 mAP while UPCL retains ~11 mAP) suggests the prototype-guided regularization may confer meaningful generalization beyond the training distribution. Key open questions include whether the approach scales beyond two categories, whether the FINCH-based clustering remains stable as identity counts grow, and whether the modest gap to PromptMA on multi-modal fusion (0.8 mAP on RGBNT201) indicates a ceiling for the prototype strategy in the fusion regime.
