## Problem

Conditional sequence modeling (CSM) methods for offline RL suffer from severe distributional shift when conditioning on high returns at inference, because suboptimal training datasets contain predominantly low-return trajectories. While empirical rebalancing techniques (weighted sampling, value-regularized supervision) have been proposed, the fundamental relationship between return distribution coverage and CSM policy performance remains theoretically uncharacterized, leaving practitioners without principled guidance on how rebalancing improves outcomes.

## Input / Output

The policy network (a Transformer) consumes a sequence of return-to-go, state, and action tokens with context length K, plus a target return conditioning value f(s) at inference time, and outputs a Gaussian-distributed action at each timestep. A separate expert policy π^e is trained via imitation learning on a filtered subset D_e of near-optimal trajectories and serves as the KL regularization target. The Q-value function Q^{π_θ} is learned iteratively via offline dynamic programming and provides value-based guidance during training.

## Method

RVDT augments the standard CSM negative log-likelihood loss with two complementary rebalancing terms: (1) an explicit expert KL divergence regularizer that pulls the agent's policy toward an expert policy extracted from near-optimal trajectories, shown to be equivalent to trajectory-level weighted resampling of expert data under Gaussian parameterization; and (2) an implicit Q-value regularizer (inherited from QT) that steers the policy toward high-value actions. The theoretical contribution establishes that the performance gap between the learned CSM policy and the optimal policy is inversely proportional to both expert-level return-coverage (α_f*) and full-spectrum return-coverage (α_f), with sample complexity governed by the same quantities. The KL regularizer is designed as a plug-in module compatible with any CSM framework, including diffusion-based policies, and the combination of explicit and implicit rebalancing is shown to outperform either mechanism alone, particularly in sparse-reward and low-data regimes.

## Results

On the D4RL benchmarks, RVDT achieves state-of-the-art normalized scores across most task domains: 91.2 average on Gym MuJoCo (vs. QT's 89.4), 72.6 on Adroit (vs. QT's 55.0, a 32% improvement), 79.8 on Kitchen (vs. QT's 77.1), and 194.3 on Maze2D (vs. QT's 170.2). The most dramatic gains appear on Adroit tasks, where RVDT scores 127.2 on pen-human and 24.0 on hammer-human compared to QT's 111.9 and 10.4 respectively. On AntMaze, RVDT (59.0 avg) remains competitive but is outperformed by the diffusion-based D-QL (73.7 avg) on larger mazes, highlighting a limitation in policy expressiveness for multi-action-per-state scenarios.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RVDT consistently outperforms QT (the prior SOTA) across Gym (91.2 vs. 89.4), Adroit (72.6 vs. 55.0), Kitchen (79.8 vs. 77.1), and Maze2D (194.3 vs. 170.2), with the largest margin on Adroit where the explicit KL regularizer addresses sparse expert data. Against DT (the base architecture), RVDT improves by 15 points on Gym, 32 points on Adroit, and 175 points on Maze2D, confirming the value of both explicit and implicit rebalancing. CGDT and GDT, other CSM variants, trail RVDT by 8.8 and 12.1 points on Gym respectively. On AntMaze, RVDT (59.0) is outperformed by the diffusion-based D-QL (73.7), particularly on antmaze-m-d (30.0 vs. 78.6) and antmaze-l-d (10.0 vs. 56.6), where highly expressive policies better handle multi-action-per-state scenarios. DP-based methods (CQL, IQL, BCQ) and value-regularized SL methods (TD3+BC, BEAR) are substantially outperformed across all domains, with IQL averaging only 77.0 on Gym and 28.1 on Adroit versus RVDT's 91.2 and 72.6.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the conditional sequence modeling (CSM) paradigm initiated by Decision Transformer and advanced by QT and CGDT, which address return-distribution imbalance through implicit value-based guidance or data synthesis. It departs from prior work by providing the first theoretical analysis connecting return-coverage (both expert-level and full-spectrum) to CSM policy performance and sample complexity, building on Brandfonbrener et al.'s near-optimality guarantees for return-conditioned supervised learning. The proposed KL-divergence-based explicit rebalancing mechanism is positioned as a plug-in module that complements QT's implicit Q-value regularization, unifying two previously separate rebalancing strategies into a single framework (RVDT).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical bounds formalize why return rebalancing works, giving practitioners a principled lens for designing future CSM methods rather than relying on empirical heuristics. The plug-in design makes the mechanism broadly applicable to any CSM architecture, including diffusion-based policies. However, the theoretical assumptions (near-determinism, bounded occupancy mismatch) are restrictive, and the bounds scale poorly with horizon (H²). A notable limitation is that RVDT still underperforms diffusion-based methods (D-QL) on AntMaze tasks with multiple actions per state, suggesting that policy expressiveness remains a bottleneck that rebalancing alone cannot resolve. Open questions include whether tighter bounds exist, whether the approach extends to non-Gaussian policy classes in practice, and how it scales to longer horizons or higher-dimensional action spaces.
