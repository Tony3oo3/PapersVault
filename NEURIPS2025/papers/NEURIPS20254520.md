## Problem

Existing multi-modal time series methods improve numerical accuracy by fusing textual context with temporal data, but they lack explicit mechanisms to reason about and explain *why* or *how* contextual signals influence predictions. This interpretability gap is a significant barrier for high-stakes domains like finance and healthcare, where trust and transparency are essential for reliable decision support.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254520/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a multivariate time series (N variables × T historical steps) paired with a free-text context description (e.g., weather summary, financial news, or healthcare report) that is segmented into L meaningful units and embedded via a frozen PLM (BERT or Sentence-BERT depending on average text length). It produces a discrete class prediction (fused from encoder probabilities and LLM output), human-readable case-based explanations at sub-sequence/half-sentence granularity showing which prototypes matched which input segments, and an iteratively refined version of the input text. The text refinement is a first-class output that feeds back into encoder retraining across iterations.

## Method

TimeXL couples a convolutional multi-modal prototype encoder with three collaborating LLM agents in a closed prediction–reflection–refinement loop. The encoder learns per-class prototypes in both time-series and text representation spaces, regularized by clustering, evidencing, and diversity objectives, then projects each prototype onto a concrete training segment to yield interpretable case-based rationales. A prediction LLM receives the original text augmented with top-ω prototype-segment pairs as explanations and produces a refined forecast; a reflection LLM compares this prediction against ground truth to diagnose textual inconsistencies; and a refinement LLM rewrites the text to emphasize relevant signals, triggering encoder retraining on the improved context. Final predictions are a linear fusion of encoder softmax probabilities and the LLM's discrete output, with the fusion weight selected on validation data. The iterative loop (up to τ iterations) progressively reduces textual noise and sharpens prototype quality, with the best-performing reflections and encoder checkpoint retained for test-time inference.

## Results

TimeXL achieves the highest F1 score across all four datasets: 0.696 (Weather), 0.631 (Finance), 0.987 (Healthcare TP), and 0.956 (Healthcare MT). It also attains the best AUC on three of four datasets (0.808 Weather, 0.996 Healthcare TP, 0.997 Healthcare MT), with the largest relative AUC gain of 8.9% over the strongest multi-modal LLM baseline TimeCAP on Weather (0.808 vs. 0.742). On Finance, TimeXL's AUC (0.797) is slightly below MM-PatchTST (0.812) but its F1 (0.631) remains the best. All results are averaged over five runs with a 6:2:2 train/val/test split.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TimeXL outperforms the strongest multi-modal LLM agent baseline TimeCAP [65] on all four datasets in F1 (e.g., 0.696 vs. 0.668 on Weather, 0.987 vs. 0.954 on Healthcare TP) and in AUC on three of four (0.808 vs. 0.742 Weather, 0.996 vs. 0.986 Healthcare TP, 0.997 vs. 0.990 Healthcare MT). Against the multi-modal time-series baselines MM-iTransformer and MM-PatchTST [35], TimeXL achieves higher F1 on every dataset and higher AUC on Weather, Healthcare TP, and Healthcare MT, though MM-PatchTST edges out TimeXL on Finance AUC (0.812 vs. 0.797). LLM-reprogramming methods such as Time-LLM [56], FSCA [58], and TimeCMA [57] are consistently outperformed by TimeXL on both metrics across all datasets; for instance, on Weather TimeXL's AUC of 0.808 exceeds Time-LLM's 0.699 by over 10 percentage points. Traditional unimodal time-series models (DLinear, Autoformer, PatchTST, iTransformer, etc.) trail substantially, with the best among them (iTransformer) reaching only 0.650 AUC on Weather versus TimeXL's 0.808. Zero-shot LLM methods (LLMTime, PromptCast) perform poorly, confirming that the iterative prototype-encoder + LLM-refinement loop is critical for leveraging textual context effectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the prototype-based sequence learning paradigm (Ming et al., KDD 2019; Ni et al., CIKM 2021; Jiang et al., KDD 2023) from single-modality time series to a multi-modal setting that jointly learns interpretable prototypes for both temporal and textual inputs. It departs from prior LLM-augmented time series methods (Time-LLM, TimeCMA, FSCA, TimeCAP) that use LLMs primarily as embedding reprogramming or one-shot prediction modules, by introducing a closed-loop of prediction, reflection, and refinement where LLMs iteratively critique and improve the textual context, triggering encoder retraining. The fusion of a supervised prototype encoder with three collaborating LLM agents in an iterative optimization loop is the architectural novelty, positioning the work at the intersection of explainable time series modeling and agentic LLM reasoning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TimeXL addresses a real gap in multi-modal time series analysis: most existing methods improve numerical accuracy but lack transparent, human-readable explanations of *why* a prediction was made, which is critical for high-stakes domains like healthcare and finance. The iterative reflection-refinement mechanism offers a practical recipe for mitigating textual noise without manual curation, and the case-based prototype explanations provide a form of interpretability that saliency or surrogate methods cannot match. However, the approach is computationally expensive (GPT-4o calls over thousands of samples per iteration), relies on a single proprietary LLM backbone, and is evaluated on only four datasets with a classification focus, leaving open questions about scalability to longer sequences, regression settings, and whether the iterative loop generalizes beyond the tested domains.
