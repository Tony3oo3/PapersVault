## Problem

State-of-the-art single-image super-resolution models, particularly diffusion-based ones, are tightly coupled to their training scale factor and collapse when asked to magnify far beyond it, producing severe artifacts or blurry outputs. Training dedicated models for extreme magnification factors (16×, 32×, 256×) is computationally prohibitive, leaving a fundamental gap: how to leverage existing, well-trained SR backbones to reach arbitrarily high resolutions without retraining.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254181/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a low-resolution RGB image (512×512 in experiments) and a number of zoom recursions as input, and produces a high-resolution image at extreme magnifications (up to 256×). Because the SR backbone (e.g., OSEDiff) requires identical input/output dimensions, each zoom step operates on a center-cropped 512×512 window that is resized back to 512×512 before the next iteration; full-image super-resolution is achieved via overlapping tile-based VAE encoding and Gaussian-weighted blending. At each step, a VLM also generates a short text prompt conditioned on the current and previous scale-state images, which is fed alongside the image to the SR backbone.

## Method

Chain-of-Zoom (CoZ) factorizes the LR-to-HR conditional distribution into a scale-level autoregressive chain of intermediate scale-states using an AR-2 structure, where each transition p(x_i | x_{i-1}, x_{i-2}) is mediated by a latent text prompt c_i extracted by a VLM conditioned on both x_{i-1} and x_{i-2}. The key simplification is that the SR backbone's dependency on x_{i-2} is entirely channeled through the prompt c_i, so the backbone only needs standard (x_{i-1}, c_i) → x_i training with MSE loss, making the framework model-agnostic and requiring no retraining of the base network. To combat hallucination at extreme scales where visual conditioning becomes sparse, the prompt-extraction VLM (Qwen2.5-VL-3B) is fine-tuned via GRPO with a composite reward combining a critic VLM score (InternVL2.5-8B), a phrase-exclusion penalty, and a repetition penalty, aligning generated prompts with human aesthetic and semantic preferences. The overall objective decomposes additively into an initial SR loss, a per-step SR likelihood, and a per-step VLM cross-entropy, each optimized independently.

## Results

On DIV2K and DIV8K, CoZ(VLM) consistently outperforms all baselines at high magnifications. At 256× on DIV2K, CoZ(VLM) achieves NIQE 9.83, MUSIQ 47.83, MANIQA 0.569, and CLIPIQA 0.599, compared to Direct SR's NIQE 16.17 and MUSIQ 28.89. When applied to S3Diff (S3Diff+CoZ), the framework reduces NIQE from 16.78 to 10.77 at 256× on DIV2K, while MUSIQ improves from 25.92 to 43.59. The performance gap between CoZ and direct SR widens dramatically with scale: at 4× differences are marginal, but at 64× and 256× CoZ(VLM) leads by 8+ NIQE points and 20+ MUSIQ points over direct SR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoZ (applied to S3Diff) decisively outperforms all three external baselines at magnifications above 4×. Against LIIF (arbitrary-scale SR), S3Diff+CoZ reduces NIQE from 23.89 to 10.77 at 256× on DIV2K and lifts MUSIQ from 26.05 to 43.59 — a gap that grows with scale. SeeSR (diffusion-based SR with text conditioning) shows similar degradation: at 64× its NIQE is 16.91 versus 8.78 for S3Diff+CoZ, and at 256× the gap widens to 20.96 vs. 10.77. S3Diff alone is competitive at 4× (NIQE 4.98 vs. 4.86 for S3Diff+CoZ) but collapses at higher scales (NIQE 16.78 at 256×), confirming that the autoregressive zoom chain is what enables extreme magnification. The internal ablation (Table 1) further shows that VLM-generated prompts (CoZ(VLM)) consistently beat DAPE prompts and null prompts at 16×–256×, with the largest gains at 256× (MUSIQ 47.83 vs. 46.22 for DAPE and 46.28 for null on DIV2K).

## Relation to Prior Work

CoZ sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) diffusion-based SR backbones (OSEDiff, SeeSR, S3Diff) that excel at their trained scale but collapse beyond it; (2) autoregressive factorizations (PixelCNN, VAR, Pixel Recursive SR) that decompose generation into sequential steps, here repurposed to autoregress over *scale-states* rather than spatial tokens; and (3) GRPO-based RLHF (from DeepSeekMath) applied for the first time to fine-tune a VLM prompt extractor for SR guidance. Unlike cascaded multi-scale generators (Cascaded Diffusion, ZoomLDM) that are unconditional and do not enforce consistency with a given LR input, CoZ is a model-agnostic wrapper that reuses an off-the-shelf SR backbone repeatedly, with multi-scale-aware VLM prompts (conditioned on both the current and previous scale-state) to compensate for the diminishing visual signal at extreme magnifications.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: any existing 4× SR model can be extended to 256× without retraining, avoiding the prohibitive compute of training at extreme scales. The GRPO-based VLM fine-tuning pipeline is a reusable recipe for aligning text guidance with human preference in generative restoration tasks. Key limitations include error accumulation across recursions (acknowledged by the authors), the computational cost of repeated VLM inference at each zoom step, and the absence of ground-truth evaluation at 256× (relying on no-reference metrics). Open questions include whether learned zoom policies (adaptive scale factors per step), domain-specific reward functions, or adaptive backbone selection could further reduce drift and improve efficiency.
