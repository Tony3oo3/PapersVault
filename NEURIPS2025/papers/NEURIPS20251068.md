## Problem

State-of-the-art matrix mechanism algorithms for differentially private continual counting require the input size n as a parameter to calibrate noise, making them inapplicable to unbounded streams where n is unknown. The standard doubling trick to handle unknown n introduces non-smooth, erratic error growth that undermines the primary advantage of matrix methods over classical binary-tree approaches. This paper seeks the first algorithm that is simultaneously unbounded, smooth, and achieves near-optimal asymptotic error.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes a stream of binary data points x_t (generalizable to R^d) one at a time, with no prior knowledge of the total stream length n. At each time step t it outputs a differentially private estimate of the partial sum S_t = Σ_{i≤t} x_i, adding Gaussian noise with scale O(log^{1+α}(t) · C_{ε,δ}) where C_{ε,δ} = O(ε^{-1}√log(1/δ_priv)) is a fixed constant independent of t. The algorithm requires O(t) space and amortized O(log t) time per round, with a doubling trick that triggers recomputation only at powers of two.

## Method

The core contribution is a novel factorization M_count = L·R where L = LTToep(f_L) and R = LTToep(f_R) are lower-triangular Toeplitz matrices whose generating functions are logarithmic perturbations of (1−z)^{−1/2}: f(z;γ,δ) = (1−z)^{−1/2}·((1/z)ln(1/(1−z)))^γ·((2/z)ln((1/z)ln(1/(1−z))))^δ. By setting γ = −1/2−α, the coefficients of f_R become square-summable (verified via Parseval's theorem), so the column norm ‖R‖_{1→2} is finite and the noise scale can be calibrated once for all n, eliminating the need for n-dependent tuning. The row norm ‖L‖_{2→∞} then grows as O(log^{1+α}(n)), yielding O(log^{2+2α}(t)) variance per time step. Coefficients are computed efficiently using FFT-based polynomial multiplication (division, exponentiation, and logarithm via the Hanrot–Zimmermann algorithm), and a doubling trick on t gives O(n log n) total time with O(n) space, matching the complexity of the bounded-input Sqrt Matrix baseline.

## Results

Algorithm 1 achieves O(log^{2+2α}(t)) variance for any α > 0 while being simultaneously smooth and unbounded, requiring O(t) space and amortized O(log t) time per round. This compares favorably to the Sqrt Matrix baseline (Henzinger et al.), which achieves O(log(t)log(n)) variance but requires O(n) space, O(n log n) pre-processing, and knowledge of n in advance. Empirically, the paper's variance is less than 1.5× that of the Sqrt Matrix for t as large as 2^24, making it competitive in absolute terms while removing the need to know the input size. The Hybrid Matrix variant (Algorithm 1 as the unbounded component of Chan et al.'s hybrid) achieves O(log²t + log^{2+2α}log t) variance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's Algorithm 1 is the first method that is simultaneously smooth and unbounded, a property no prior baseline achieves: the Sqrt Matrix [27,23] is smooth but requires n as input, the Smooth Binary [3] is smooth but also bounded, and the Hybrid Binary [11] is unbounded but has non-smooth (erratic) error near powers of 2. In asymptotic variance, Algorithm 1's O(log^{2+2α} t) is slightly worse than the Sqrt Matrix's O(log(t)·log(n)), but empirically the gap is small — the paper reports variance less than 1.5× the Sqrt Matrix for t up to 2^24. Against the Binary mechanism [11,30], Algorithm 1 improves both smoothness and removes the dependence on n. The Hybrid Matrix variant (using Algorithm 1 as the unbounded component of Chan et al.'s hybrid [11]) outperforms the original hybrid with independent noise, though both exhibit erratic performance near powers of 2. The approximate variant (Algorithm 2) matches Algorithm 1's performance to within visual indistinguishability out to t = 2^24 while reducing per-step computation from 12M(t) to 2.25M(t) after the switch point.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the matrix mechanism framework (Li [33]) and the specific sqrt-matrix factorization L=R=M_count^{1/2} (Henzinger et al. [27], Fichtenberger et al. [23]) by introducing logarithmic perturbations of the generating function (1−z)^{−1/2}, building on the LTToep/generating-function formalism of Dvijotham et al. [16]. The key departure from prior work is that all existing near-optimal matrix-based counting algorithms require the input size n as a parameter; the standard doubling trick avoids this but produces non-smooth error, while smooth variants (Andersson and Pagh [3]) remain bounded. By choosing perturbations that make the R-matrix square-summable (bounded sensitivity) while keeping the L-matrix row norm at O(log^{1+α}(n)), the authors obtain the first factorization that is simultaneously unbounded, smooth, and near-optimal. The lower-triangular decomposition guarantee of Denisov et al. [14] and the hybrid mechanism of Chan et al. [11] are also leveraged as building blocks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This resolves a trilemma in private continual counting: no prior algorithm was simultaneously smooth, unbounded, and near-optimal, and the paper shows empirically that the resulting variance is less than 1.5× the bounded-input optimal (Henzinger et al.) for t up to 2^24, making it practically competitive. The open question of whether O(log² t) variance is achievable with LTToep matrices (as opposed to general lower-triangular matrices) is posed with a conjecture grounded in the divergence of iterated-logarithm sequences, which would constitute a clean separation result. A limitation is the O(t) space requirement (vs. O(log t) for binary-tree methods) and the slightly super-log² error rate, though the authors argue α=0.01 is sufficient for any realistic deployment scale.
