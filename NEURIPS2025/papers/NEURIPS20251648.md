## Problem

Existing video QA benchmarks associate each question with only a single short clip, failing to capture real-world information-seeking scenarios where answering a query requires searching through hundreds of video segments, identifying relevant audio-visual snippets across multiple sources, and synthesizing coherent, evidence-backed answers. No prior benchmark systematically evaluates LMMs' ability to perform multi-video audio-visual retrieval, temporal grounding, and cross-video reasoning simultaneously.

## Input / Output

The system takes a free-form text query and a pool of up to 500 YouTube videos (5–25 minutes each, with synchronized audio, on-screen text, and English captions) as input. It produces a step-wise answer where each step is grounded to one or more temporal segments specified as <videoID, start, end> tuples; 82% of the 3100 QA pairs in AVHaystacks require evidence from at least two distinct videos, and answers must reference complementary audio and visual cues that cannot be resolved from a single modality or clip alone.

## Method

MAGNET is a model-agnostic, retrieval-augmented multi-agent framework with three core components. First, an AV-RAG retrieval stage uses ImageBind to encode both the query and each video's fused audio-visual features (Hadamard product of audio and visual embeddings) alongside Gemini 1.5 Pro–generated captions, then ranks videos by averaged cosine similarity to select the top-k most relevant clips. Second, a Salient Frame Selector (SFS) module applies a dynamic-programming algorithm over a pairwise affinity matrix (cosine similarity plus a temporal-separation penalty) to pick k visually diverse, temporally dispersed frames from each retrieved video, replacing uniform sampling. Third, a dedicated Qwen 2.5 Omni agent is spawned per retrieved video to independently identify salient temporal segments and generate a partial response, and a GPT-4o meta-agent aggregates all agent outputs into a coherent, temporally grounded final answer. The AVLLM agents are fine-tuned with LoRA adapters on AVHaystacks, while the retrieval pipeline and SFS remain frozen and model-agnostic. The paper also introduces two evaluation metrics—STEM (step-wise error metric using Hungarian matching to quantify missing, hallucinated, misordered, and mis-grounded steps) and MTGS (matched temporal grounding score measuring mean IoU over matched video IDs)—to provide balanced, interpretable assessment of multi-video grounding.

## Results

On AVHaystacks-50, MAGNET + Qwen 2.5 Omni-FT achieves the best open-source performance with BLEU@4 of 55.82, GPT Eval of 7.84, and Human Eval of 4.15/5, while the closed-source upper bound (MAGNET + Gemini 1.5 Pro) reaches 57.67 BLEU@4 and 8.03 GPT Eval. On the more challenging AVHaystacks-Full (500-video haystack), the best open-source configuration scores 53.69 BLEU@4 and 7.56 GPT Eval. The paper reports up to 89% and 65% relative improvements over baseline methods on BLEU@4 and GPT evaluation scores, respectively, and achieves R@3 of 90.68% and R@5 of 93.17% on the retrieval task (vs. 85.57% and 89.79% for the strongest baseline VideoRAG on AVHaystacks-50).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MAGNET substantially outperforms all standalone AVLLM baselines (Qwen2.5 Omni, Unified IO2, VideoSALMONN), which score only 10.84–11.90 BLEU@4 and 2.11–2.40 GPT Eval on AVHaystacks-50, because these models cannot handle the multi-video retrieval and temporal grounding required by the task. Against the strongest RAG baselines (VideoRAG and Video-RAG, scoring 43.16 and 42.64 BLEU@4 respectively), MAGNET + Qwen 2.5 Omni-FT improves BLEU@4 by ~12.7 points and GPT Eval by ~1.5 points on AVHaystacks-50, with similar margins on AVHaystacks-Full. The fine-tuned MAGNET variants (FT) consistently outperform their zero-shot counterparts (ZS) by roughly 25 BLEU@4 points, confirming the benefit of LoRA fine-tuning on the AVLLM agents. On retrieval (Table 4), MAGNET achieves R@3 of 90.68% and R@5 of 93.17% on AVHaystacks-50, surpassing VideoRAG (85.57% / 89.79%) by 5.1 and 3.4 points, and on AVHaystacks-Full it leads by 2.7 and 4.2 points respectively. The best open-source configuration (MAGNET + Qwen 2.5 Omni-FT) nearly matches the closed-source upper bound (MAGNET + Gemini 1.5 Pro), trailing by only 1.85 BLEU@4 and 0.19 GPT Eval on AVHaystacks-50.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the video question-answering line of work (NExT-QA, Video-MME, LongVideoBench) from single-clip, predominantly visual settings to a multi-video audio-visual retrieval-and-reasoning formulation. It repurposes existing retrieval-augmented generation pipelines (VideoRAG, Video-RAG) and off-the-shelf encoders (ImageBind) within a novel multi-agent orchestration that dynamically spawns per-video AVLLM agents (Qwen 2.5 Omni) and aggregates their outputs via a meta-agent (GPT-4o). The Salient Frame Selector module and the STEM/MTGS evaluation metrics are new contributions, but the core architectural components are assembled from established building blocks rather than introducing a fundamentally new model family.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AVHaystacks fills a genuine gap: no prior benchmark requires models to retrieve, temporally ground, and synthesize evidence across multiple videos using both audio and visual cues, which mirrors real-world information-seeking over personal archives or how-to repositories. The 82% multi-video requirement and 500-video haystack make it a meaningful stress test for current LMMs, whose suboptimal retrieval and cross-clip reasoning are clearly exposed. Open questions include whether the multi-agent overhead is justified at scale, whether end-to-end trainable retrieval (replacing ImageBind) would close the remaining gap to closed-source upper bounds, and whether the benchmark's YouTube-sourced, English-captioned scope generalizes to other languages and domains.
