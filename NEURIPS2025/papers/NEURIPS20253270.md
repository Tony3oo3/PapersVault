## Problem

While Mamba-based SSMs offer linear-complexity long-range modeling for 3D medical images, they lack adaptive mechanisms to handle diverse local patterns (e.g., small tumors within large organs) that are characteristic of multi-scale medical data. Combining the global efficiency of SSMs with the localized adaptability of Mixture-of-Experts remains largely unexplored in 3D medical imaging, where balancing computational efficiency and cross-modal generalization under resource constraints is critical.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a 3D medical volume x ∈ R^{B×C×D×H×W} (CT, MRI, or 3D US) and produces a voxel-wise segmentation mask y ∈ R^{B×C'×D×H×W} with the same spatial dimensions. Internally, the 3D feature maps are flattened along spatial dimensions into 1D token sequences of length N_i at each encoder stage (reaching ~10^6 tokens at early stages), processed by Mamba and HoME, then reshaped back to volumetric form. Inference uses a sliding-window strategy with 0.5 overlap and Gaussian importance weighting to handle high-resolution volumes that exceed GPU memory.

## Method

Mamba-HoME integrates a two-level Hierarchical Soft Mixture-of-Experts (HoME) layer into a Mamba-based U-shaped encoder-decoder. In the first HoME level, input tokens are divided into local groups of size K_i and soft-assigned via dot-product routing to learnable slot embeddings, then processed by E_{1,i} local FFN experts for intra-group feature extraction. The second level flattens group outputs and routes them through E_{2,i} global FFN experts for inter-group information fusion and global context refinement, followed by an attention-based reconstruction step. Each Mamba-HoME Block sequentially applies Gated Spatial Convolution (for local spatial priors), a Mamba SSM layer (for linear-complexity long-range sequential modeling), and the HoME layer, with Dynamic Tanh normalization replacing LayerNorm for ~6% speedup. The architecture uses 4 encoder stages with monotonically increasing expert counts (E_1 < E_2 < ... < E_I) and decreasing group sizes (K_1 > K_2 > ... > K_I), and is pre-trained on 9,404 CT/MRI scans before fine-tuning on target datasets.

## Results

On the PANORAMA + in-house CT test set, Mamba-HoME (pre-trained) achieves 56.7% DSC for PDAC and 88.5% DSC for pancreas, yielding 78.2% mDSC and 4.3 mm mHD95, outperforming the best baseline (SuPreM, 76.6% mDSC / 4.7 mm mHD95) by +1.6% mDSC. On AMOS-CT, the pre-trained model reaches 87.3% mDSC (vs. 86.0% for SegMamba and SuPreM). On FeTA 2022 (fetal MRI) and MVSeg (3D ultrasound), Mamba-HoME attains 87.7% mDSC / 2.0 mm mHD95 and 85.0% mDSC / 4.1 mm mHD95 respectively, setting new state-of-the-art on all four public benchmarks. The model uses 170.1 M parameters but requires only 11.1 GB GPU memory at inference, comparable to lighter baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Mamba-HoME outperforms all eight baselines across every evaluated dataset and modality. On PANORAMA (CT), the pre-trained model gains +1.6% mDSC over the closest competitor SuPreM (78.2% vs. 76.6%) and +4.7% PDAC DSC over uC 3DU-Net (56.7% vs. 52.0%), while also achieving the lowest mHD95 (4.3 mm vs. 4.7 mm for SuPreM). On AMOS, Mamba-HoME leads in all four training settings (CT, CT→MRI, MRI, CT+MRI), with the largest margin in the MRI-only setting (82.3% vs. 80.7% for Hermes, +1.6%). On FeTA 2022 (fetal MRI), it surpasses Hermes by +1.2% mDSC (87.7% vs. 86.5%) and achieves the best mHD95 (2.0 mm vs. 2.3 mm for VSmTrans). On MVSeg (3D ultrasound), the pre-trained model edges out VSmTrans and Swin UNETR by +0.6% mDSC (85.0% vs. 84.4%) and improves mHD95 by 0.7 mm (4.1 vs. 4.8). The primary baseline SegMamba is consistently outperformed by 1.2–1.5% mDSC across datasets, confirming the benefit of the hierarchical MoE routing over plain Mamba sequential modeling. Notably, Mamba-HoME achieves these gains with lower GPU memory (11.1 GB) than SuPreM (17.1 GB) and Swin UNETR (17.1 GB), despite having more parameters, thanks to the linear-complexity Mamba backbone and grouped slot assignment.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper integrates three established threads: the Mamba selective state-space model for linear-complexity long-sequence modeling, the SegMamba U-shaped encoder-decoder with Gated Spatial Convolution for 3D medical segmentation, and the Soft Mixture-of-Experts (SMoE) routing mechanism from vision. The key architectural departure is the Hierarchical Soft Mixture-of-Experts (HoME) layer, which introduces a two-level token-routing structure—local per-group experts followed by global cross-group experts—that has not previously been combined with SSM backbones in 3D medical imaging. The paper positions itself as the first to jointly address local pattern specialization and global context integration within a single Mamba-based segmentation network.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The cross-modal generalization results—particularly transferring from CT/MRI pre-training to 3D ultrasound without any ultrasound-specific pre-training—suggest that hierarchical expert routing may produce more modality-agnostic representations than flat SSM or attention-based alternatives. However, the model carries 170M parameters (roughly 2–8× larger than most competitors) and is ~30% slower at inference than the SegMamba baseline, raising practical deployment questions. Scalability to very large datasets (>10,000 scans) and self-supervised pre-training at scale (>200,000 scans) remain unexplored, and whether the hierarchical MoE advantage persists under such regimes is an open question for the field.
