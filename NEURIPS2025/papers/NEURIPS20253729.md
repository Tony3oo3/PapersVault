## Problem

Parameter-efficient fine-tuning methods for pre-trained language models are highly susceptible to noisy training data, yet existing noise-handling approaches either require laborious data pre-processing (cleaning, filtering, relabeling) or rely on denoising architectures prone to error accumulation and limited to specific noise types. No prior method can exploit noise itself as a training signal to build robustness without requiring a clean dataset or complex denoising pipelines.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253729/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes instruction-following text data (Alpaca-52K, 10% subset) as fine-tuning input, with noise injected at two levels: discrete character-level perturbations (word shuffling, character insertion/deletion) and continuous uniform noise at the embedding level (NEFTune-style). At inference, the model produces standard text predictions (multiple-choice answers, generated text) on evaluation benchmarks, but critically, one expert pathway is masked so outputs are generated exclusively from experts trained on clean data, effectively filtering out noise-learned knowledge.

## Method

LoPE repurposes the asymmetric LoRA architecture (shared A matrix, multiple B matrices) by designating one B matrix as a dedicated 'poisoning expert' that absorbs noise patterns. A two-stage training paradigm first fine-tunes the poisoning expert and shared A on noise-augmented data via HyNoIse (combining discrete character-level and continuous embedding-level noise injection), then freezes the poisoning expert while training remaining experts and the router on clean data. A DyCompEnSate mechanism computes inter-expert output similarity to dynamically amplify normal expert contributions, compensating for the dependency disruption caused by masking. At inference, the poisoning expert is simply masked out, so the model generates predictions using only the purified knowledge encoded in the normal experts, achieving noise robustness purely through the low-cost noise injection without any data cleaning.

## Results

Under noisy fine-tuning conditions (5% discrete noise), LoPE(r=4) achieves 44.42% on MMLU, 76.28% on PIQA, 49.03% on SIQA, 13.72% on GSM8K, and 60.49% on ARC-easy, outperforming HydraLoRA(r=4) by 1.34% on MMLU and 4.23% on ARC-easy. On the clean (Orig) dataset, LoPE(r=4) reaches 46.86% MMLU and 64.20% ARC-easy, marginally ahead of HydraLoRA(r=4) at 46.10% and 62.61%. The method uses only 0.062% trainable parameters (rank 4, 3 experts), matching HydraLoRA's parameter budget while delivering consistent gains across all evaluated benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LoPE consistently outperforms all five baselines under noisy fine-tuning. Against HydraLoRA(r=4)†, the closest competitor, LoPE(r=4)† gains +1.34% on MMLU, +1.36% on PIQA, +1.74% on SIQA, +1.89% on GSM8K, and +4.23% on ARC-easy, with the largest advantage on reasoning-heavy tasks. Against standard LoRA(r=4)†, the gap widens to +3.97% on MMLU and +12.10% on ARC-easy, indicating that the asymmetric MoE structure and poisoning-expert masking are critical under noise. P-Tuning† and Prefix Tuning†, which use more parameters (0.193% and 0.077% respectively), trail LoPE by 7.19% and 6.51% on MMLU, confirming that parameter count alone does not confer noise robustness. On the clean Orig dataset, LoPE's advantage over HydraLoRA narrows to ~0.76% on MMLU, consistent with the paper's claim that the noise-handling mechanism is most impactful when noise is present. The method also generalizes across backbones, improving over HydraLoRA on T5-large (+2.73% SIQA), LLaMA2-7b (+1.74%), Qwen2-7b (+2.50%), and Qwen1.5-14b (+1.79%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on HydraLoRA's asymmetric LoRA architecture (shared A matrix, multiple B experts) and repurposes it for noise-robustness by designating one B matrix as a 'poisoning expert' trained exclusively on noise-injected data. The continuous noise injection at the embedding level adapts NEFTune's approach, while the discrete noise strategies (character insertion, word shuffling, deletion) are standard data augmentation. Conceptually, the paper departs from both dominant noise-handling paradigms—data pre-processing/cleaning and in-model denoising architectures—by proposing a 'Guided Poisoning' strategy where noise is deliberately absorbed by a dedicated expert and then excluded via masking at inference, effectively using a complement-set operation on learned knowledge.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a practical alternative to laborious data-cleaning pipelines for practitioners who must fine-tune on inherently noisy corpora, and the complement-set masking idea generalizes beyond noise to any scenario where a module learns undesirable patterns that can be surgically removed. However, the empirical gains over HydraLoRA are modest (1–2% on noisy data), the method is constrained to the LoRA/PEFT setting, and the discrete noise injection does not model realistic noise distributions. Open questions include whether the approach scales to higher noise ratios (performance degrades noticeably at 20–30%), whether the poisoning expert can be replaced by simpler regularization, and whether the DyCompEnSate compensation is necessary or an artifact of the specific expert count used.
