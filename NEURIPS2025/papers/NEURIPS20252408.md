## Problem

While U-Net-based diffusion models have been extensively studied for their cross-attention maps, the internal attention dynamics of multi-modal diffusion transformers (MM-DiT) remain largely unexplored. It is unclear which specific layers in the joint self-attention mechanism are responsible for aligning textual semantics with spatially coherent image regions, and whether this emergent semantic grounding can be directly exploited for dense perception tasks like open-vocabulary segmentation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252408/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For segmentation, the method takes an RGB image (resized to 1024×1024, VAE-encoded to a 64×64 latent) and a text prompt listing ground-truth class names (e.g., 'car mountain sky water'), encoded via CLIP text encoders to 77 tokens. The image latent is noised at timestep t=8 of 28 via rectified flow interpolation before being fed into the MM-DiT. The output is a per-pixel class assignment obtained by argmax over the I2T attention maps from the 9th MM-DiT block, bilinearly upsampled to original resolution. For unsupervised segmentation, the text prompt is replaced entirely with <pad> tokens, and mask proposals are extracted from individual pad-token attention maps. For generation, the same model produces a full RGB image from a free-form text prompt.

## Method

Seg4Diff identifies a 'semantic grounding expert' layer (the 9th MM-DiT block in SD3) through a multi-pronged analysis: decomposing joint attention into I2I/I2T/T2I/T2T components, PCA visualization of query/key/value projections, L2-norm analysis of value tokens, and causal perturbation via Gaussian blur on I2T attention maps. The zero-shot segmentation scheme extracts the I2T attention from this expert layer, averages across all H heads to form a token-level attention map, reshapes it to spatial dimensions per text token, and applies argmax for class assignment—requiring no postprocessing or refinement. A key finding is that individual attention heads capture distinct semantic parts (e.g., ears vs. legs of a bear) that sum to a complete object mask, and that <pad> tokens in unconditional generation spontaneously attend to coherent regions, enabling training-free unsupervised segmentation. To amplify this capability, MAGNET applies a lightweight LoRA adapter (rank 16) to the expert layer, trained with a combined objective of flow-matching loss plus a mask alignment loss (focal + Dice) computed via bipartite matching between l·H candidate attention maps and ground-truth masks, improving both segmentation mIoU and generation quality (CLIPScore, T2I-CompBench++) simultaneously.

## Results

On open-vocabulary semantic segmentation (Table 1), Seg4Diff with SD3 achieves 89.2 mIoU on Pascal VOC, 62.0 on COCO-Object, 49.0 on Pascal Context-59, and 34.2 on ADE20K, all without any postprocessing. With the MAGNET fine-tuning on COCO, these improve to 89.8, 62.9, 51.2, and 35.2 mIoU respectively. For unsupervised segmentation (Table 2), Seg4Diff + MAGNET (COCO) reaches 53.5 mIoU on COCO-Stuff-27 and 45.4 on ADE20K, the best among all compared methods on those two benchmarks. On generation quality, MAGNET (COCO) raises mean CLIPScore from 27.14 to 27.28 and improves T2I-CompBench++ attribute binding (Color 0.792, Shape 0.569, Texture 0.726) and compositional scores (Num 0.558, Comp 0.374).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against U-Net diffusion baselines, Seg4Diff (SD3) substantially outperforms DiffSegmenter on every metric (e.g., 89.2 vs 66.4 mIoU on VOC20, 62.0 vs 40.0 on COCO-Object, 34.2 vs 24.2 on ADE20K) and iSeg (89.2 vs 82.9 on VOC20, 62.0 vs 57.3 on COCO-Object, 34.2 vs 24.2 on ADE20K), demonstrating that the MM-DiT architecture's consistent spatial resolution across layers yields markedly sharper masks than U-Net cross-attention. Against CLIP-based methods, Seg4Diff wins on COCO-Object (62.0 vs 49.8 ProxyCLIP, 52.7 CorrCLIP), Pascal Context-59 (49.0 vs 39.6/47.9), and ADE20K (34.2 vs 24.2/28.8), but trails CorrCLIP on Pascal VOC (89.2 vs 91.8) and Cityscapes (26.5 vs 49.9), where CLIP's strong scene-level priors remain advantageous. For unsupervised segmentation, Seg4Diff + MAGNET (COCO) achieves the best mIoU on COCO-Stuff-27 (53.5) and ADE20K (45.4), surpassing DiffCut (46.1, 42.4) and DiffSeg (44.2, 37.7), though DiffCut retains a lead on Pascal VOC (62.0 vs 56.1) and Cityscapes (28.4 vs 24.4). On generation, MAGNET (COCO) improves CLIPScore by ~0.13 over baseline and gains on T2I-CompBench++ attribute binding and compositional metrics, while the baseline retains a small edge on 2D/3D spatial relationships (0.244/0.332 vs 0.230/0.323), consistent with the paper's note that mask alignment targets object-centric semantics rather than spatial reasoning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work leveraging diffusion model attention maps for perception (Prompt-to-Prompt, DiffSegmenter, iSeg, DiffSeg, DiffCut) by shifting focus from U-Net cross-attention to the joint self-attention mechanism in multi-modal diffusion transformers (MM-DiT). While prior analyses of diffusion internals targeted SD1.5's cross-attention, this work systematically decomposes the four interaction types (I2I, I2T, T2I, T2T) in MM-DiT's concatenated-token attention, identifying a specific 'semantic grounding expert' layer. The MAGNET fine-tuning scheme departs from prior segmentation-only adaptations by simultaneously improving generation fidelity, a dual objective not pursued in earlier diffusion-for-perception work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a single MM-DiT layer (9th in SD3) serves as a bottleneck for both text-to-image alignment and semantic segmentation provides a practical, interpretable handle for anyone building on diffusion transformers. The emergent segmentation capability of <pad> tokens in unconditional generation opens a training-free path to unsupervised segmentation that requires no class vocabulary. However, the method's reliance on a single 'sweet-spot' layer, absence of postprocessing, and modest (though consistent) generation gains leave open questions about scalability to video, 3D, or multi-object reasoning tasks, and whether the identified layer generalizes as architectures evolve.
