## Problem

Transfer-based adversarial attacks against closed-source commercial LVLMs (GPT-4o, Claude, Gemini) consistently fail because the learned perturbations are uniformly distributed and lack clear semantic structure, causing black-box models to either ignore the perturbation or misinterpret it as noise. This paper identifies the absence of semantic detail in perturbations as the root cause and asks how to encode target semantics into local image regions to achieve reliable cross-model transfer.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251500/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a clean source image and a target image (both 224×224 RGB, sampled from the NIPS 2017 Adversarial Attacks dataset) and produces a single adversarial image whose perturbation is bounded by an ℓ∞ ball of radius ε (default 16/255). The adversarial image is then queried against commercial black-box LVLM APIs (GPT-4.5, GPT-4o, o1, Claude-3.5/3.7, Gemini-2.0) to obtain a text description, which is scored against manually labeled semantic keywords of the target image using a semi-automated KMRScore pipeline and a GPTScore-based ASR metric.

## Method

M-Attack refines the perturbation by performing random cropping (scale [0.5, 1.0]) on the adversarial source image at each of 300 I-FGSM optimization steps, resizing the crop back to full resolution, and maximizing cosine similarity between the cropped source patch and a corresponding target patch (local-to-local or local-to-global) in the embedding space of a surrogate model ensemble. The ensemble combines three CLIP variants with different patch sizes (ViT-B/16, ViT-B/32, ViT-g-14-laion2B) so that small-receptive-field models extract fine-grained details while large-receptive-field models preserve global structure, capturing shared semantics that transfer to unknown black-box models. Consecutive crops are guaranteed to overlap (Eq. 3) for semantic consistency while also exploring new regions (Eq. 4) for diversity, so the central area of the image accumulates the richest semantic detail. The paper provides an optimal-transport argument showing that local-to-local matching achieves a strictly lower alignment cost than global-to-global matching, explaining why the approach avoids the early saturation and overfitting observed with global feature alignment.

## Results

M-Attack achieves 95% ASR on GPT-4o, 78% on Gemini-2.0, and 29% on Claude-3.5 (ε=16, ℓ∞), with KMRa scores of 0.82, 0.75, and 0.31 respectively. On reasoning and latest models, it reaches 94% ASR on GPT-o1 and 95% on GPT-4.5. The method also achieves the lowest imperceptibility norms (ℓ₁=0.030, ℓ₂=0.036) among all compared methods, outperforming the next-best AnyAttack (ℓ₁=0.048, ℓ₂=0.052) while delivering roughly 2× higher ASR on GPT-4o.

## Baselines & Comparisons

M-Attack dominates all four baselines across every evaluated model and metric. Against the strongest prior method AnyAttack, M-Attack more than doubles ASR on GPT-4o (0.95 vs. 0.42) and Gemini-2.0 (0.78 vs. 0.48), while also achieving lower ℓ₁/ℓ₂ norms (0.030/0.036 vs. 0.048/0.052). SSA-CWA and AttackVLM are far less effective, peaking at 9% and 2% ASR on GPT-4o respectively, with near-zero KMRc scores indicating they fail to preserve target semantics at any threshold. AdvDiffVLM is essentially ineffective on all three commercial models (≤2% ASR). On the stricter KMRc metric (all keywords matched), M-Attack achieves 0.13 on GPT-4o versus 0.04 for AnyAttack and 0.00 for all other baselines, confirming that local-level matching produces semantically richer perturbations rather than merely triggering a keyword match.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the transfer-based adversarial attack framework introduced by AttackVLM (Zhao et al., NeurIPS 2023), which aligned source and target images via global feature matching in CLIP embedding space. The key conceptual departure is replacing global-to-global matching with local-level matching via random cropping, motivated by the observation that prior methods produce uniform, semantically empty perturbations that commercial LVLMs dismiss as noise. The model ensemble component draws on the broader adversarial transferability literature (CWA, SSA-CWA) but applies it to CLIP variants with complementary receptive fields rather than to classification networks. The paper also introduces KMRScore as a more objective evaluation metric, departing from the subjective keyword-matching and GPTScore approaches used in prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The >90% attack success rate on GPT-4.5, GPT-4o, and o1 with lower perturbation norms than all prior methods exposes a critical vulnerability in the strongest commercial multimodal models, with direct security implications for platforms integrated into decision-making pipelines. The finding that reasoning-centric models (o1, Claude-3.7-thinking) show equal or weaker robustness than their non-reasoning counterparts is practically alarming, as it undermines the assumption that extended reasoning provides an additional defense layer. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes as models incorporate stronger visual defenses, whether the CLIP-based surrogate assumption holds for future architectures, and what systematic defenses (e.g., local semantic consistency checks) could mitigate this class of attack. The KMRScore metric also addresses a real reproducibility gap in the field's evaluation practices.
