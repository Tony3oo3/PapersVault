## Problem

Existing post-hoc OOD scoring functions underexploit the logit space: MaxLogit discards all non-maximum logits, while MCM's softmax normalization compresses absolute logit magnitudes, causing structurally distinct logit patterns to collapse into similar probability distributions. The paper asks how to more effectively leverage the discriminative information embedded in non-maximum logits to enhance ID-OOD separability without additional training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250703/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on the K-dimensional logit vector produced by a pre-trained model (CLIP ViT-B/16, ViT-L/14, or ResNet-50) given an RGB image, requiring no architectural modification or retraining. It outputs a single scalar OOD score that is compared against a threshold (typically set at 95% TPR) to yield a binary ID/OOD decision. The adaptive variant LogitGap* additionally consumes a small ID validation set (≤100 samples) to automatically select the top-N hyperparameter via synthetic OOD interpolation, making it fully training-free at inference time.

## Method

LogitGap computes the average gap between the maximum logit and all remaining logits after descending sort, explicitly quantifying the 'peakedness' of ID logit distributions versus the 'flatness' of OOD distributions. To suppress noisy tail logits that dilute the discriminative signal, LogitGap-topN restricts the computation to only the top-N sorted logits, reformulating the score as z₁′ − z̄_N′ (maximum logit minus mean of the next N−1 logits). The hyperparameter N is selected by maximizing the expected score gap between ID and synthetic OOD samples (generated via feature interpolation and noise injection on a small ID set), balancing information retention against noise suppression. The paper further provides a theoretical guarantee (Theorem 4.1) showing that LogitGap's false positive rate is upper-bounded by MCM's FPR whenever the softmax temperature τ exceeds 2(K−1), establishing a formal performance ordering between the two scoring functions.

## Results

On ImageNet (zero-shot, CLIP ViT-B/16), LogitGap achieves an average FPR95 of 75.18% and AUROC of 79.23%, reducing FPR95 by 3.65% over MCM (78.83%) and 6.43% over Energy (81.61%). On the more challenging ImageNet-100 setting, LogitGap further reduces FPR95 by 5.78% compared to MCM (44.16% vs. 49.94%). In the semantically hard scenario (ImageNet-10 as ID vs. ImageNet-20 as OOD), LogitGap achieves FPR95 of 3.80%, outperforming MCM (6.40%) and MaxLogit (8.40%). On the traditional CIFAR-10 benchmark with ResNet-50, LogitGap attains an average FPR95 of 34.91% and AUROC of 90.62%, surpassing all post-hoc baselines including MSP (37.17%) and NCI (36.54%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LogitGap consistently outperforms all post-hoc baselines across every evaluation setting. In zero-shot detection on ImageNet, it reduces FPR95 by 3.65% over MCM [33], 2.29% over MaxLogit [14], and 6.43% over Energy [30]; the gap widens to 5.78% over MCM on ImageNet-100. On the semantically hard ImageNet-10/20 task, LogitGap achieves FPR95 of 3.80% versus 6.40% (MCM) and 8.40% (MaxLogit), a 2.60–4.60% margin. GL-MCM [35] is the closest competitor on ImageNet (FPR95 75.30% vs. 75.18%), but LogitGap substantially outperforms it on AUROC (79.23% vs. 74.74%). In the traditional CIFAR-10 setting with ResNet-50, LogitGap beats MSP [17] by 2.26% FPR95 and NCI [29] by 1.63%, and when combined with training-based methods it yields an 8.29% FPR95 reduction over MixOE [57] and a 0.38% gain over OE [18]. In few-shot settings, LogitGap* improves ID-Like [1] by 7.39% FPR95 (1-shot, ImageNet) and CoOp [62] by 1.93% FPR95 (1-shot), confirming its complementary utility as a drop-in scoring module.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper re-examines the logit space for post-hoc OOD detection, departing from the dominant softmax-based scoring paradigm (MCM, MSP) by arguing that softmax normalization discards absolute logit magnitudes that carry discriminative signal. Rather than building on a single prior method, it synthesizes insights from MaxLogit (which ignores non-maximum logits) and MCM (which compresses them via softmax) into a new margin-based score that explicitly quantifies the gap between the maximum and remaining logits. The top-N logit selection strategy and the theoretical comparison to MCM (Theorem 4.1) further distinguish it from prior logit-based approaches like Energy and ODIN, which operate on different functional forms of the same logit vector.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LogitGap offers a lightweight, training-free scoring function that can be dropped into existing OOD detection pipelines (CoOp, ID-Like, OE, MixOE) without architectural changes, making it practically attractive for deployment. The consistent 3–8% FPR95 gains across zero-shot, few-shot, semantically-hard, and covariate-shift settings demonstrate broad applicability, and the theoretical guarantee (under τ > 2(K−1)) provides a principled justification over MCM. However, the method remains confined to visual classification, and the authors acknowledge the need to extend it to text and speech modalities. An open question is whether the logit-gap phenomenon holds as strongly for models trained with objectives other than cross-entropy or contrastive learning, and whether the top-N selection strategy generalizes to very large class spaces (e.g., 21K classes).
