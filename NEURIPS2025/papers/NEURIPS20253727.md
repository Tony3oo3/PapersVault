## Problem

Inverse protein folding suffers from structural degeneracy, where many distinct sequences can adopt geometrically similar folds, making it difficult for existing methods to recover the native sequence from a given backbone. While multiple sequence alignments (MSAs) have proven critical for structure prediction (e.g., AlphaFold), the complementary prior of structural alignments—encoding conserved spatial and physicochemical constraints across homologs—has not been systematically exploited for sequence design.

## Input / Output

The method takes a query protein backbone (N, Cα, C, O, and virtual Cβ coordinates) whose sequence is unknown, plus a structurally homologous template protein retrieved via Foldseek from the PDB (excluding near-identical matches with TM-score > 0.99 and same PDB IDs) and aligned to the query via TM-align. The template contributes both its 3D structure and its known amino acid sequence (one-hot encoded over 21 types including 'unknown'), while the query's node features are initialized to zeros. The output is the full amino acid sequence of the query, generated autoregressively and refined by 10-sample majority voting at inference.

## Method

DualMPNN employs a dual-stream message-passing neural network built on the ProteinMPNN backbone, with a query branch (zero-initialized nodes) and a template branch (sequence-initialized nodes) that share identical MPNN encoder-decoder components (3 message-passing layers each). At every encoder and decoder layer, an alignment-aware cross-attention mechanism restricts information flow to TM-align-matched residue pairs, computing attention scores scaled by the global TM-score as a similarity bias, so that higher-quality templates exert stronger guidance. The decoder performs autoregressive sequence generation where each position attends to future latent node features and previously decoded sequence embeddings. Trained with supervised learning (40 epochs, Adam, hidden dim 128, 48 query neighbors vs. 4 template neighbors, dropout 0.1), the model achieves 65.51% recovery on CATH-All, outperforming ProteinMPNN by 15.64% and GraDe-IF by 13.3%.

## Results

DualMPNN achieves state-of-the-art sequence recovery rates of 65.51% (CATH All), 70.99% (TS50), and 70.37% (T500), with corresponding perplexities of 3.18, 2.76, and 2.71. On the CATH dataset, it reaches 55.97% recovery on short chains and 52.41% on single chains, where inter-chain interactions make prediction particularly difficult. The model outperforms the base ProteinMPNN by 15.64%, 16.56%, and 12.29% recovery on CATH, TS50, and T500 respectively, and surpasses the previous best (GraDe-IF) by 13.3%, 14.67%, and 9.15% on the same benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DualMPNN outperforms all 10 baselines across every benchmark and metric. Against the base model ProteinMPNN [12], it gains +15.64% recovery on CATH (65.51% vs 49.87%), +16.56% on TS50 (70.99% vs 54.43%), and +12.29% on T500 (70.37% vs 58.08%), while also reducing perplexity by roughly 30% (3.18 vs 4.57 on CATH All). Versus the previous state-of-the-art GraDe-IF [13] (graph diffusion), DualMPNN improves recovery by +13.3% on CATH, +14.67% on TS50, and +9.15% on T500, with substantially lower perplexity (3.18 vs 4.35 on CATH). PiFOLD [33], the next-strongest baseline, trails DualMPNN by +13.85% on CATH and +9.95% on T500. The gap is most pronounced on single-chain CATH proteins (52.41% vs 34.43% for ProteinMPNN), where inter-chain context is absent and structural alignment priors provide the strongest disambiguation signal. On foldability (AlphaFold2/3 validation), DualMPNN matches ProteinMPNN's 94% success rate under AF2 but achieves higher pLDDT (0.91 vs 0.89) and, under AF3, reaches 95% success with pLDDT 0.92, confirming that the recovery gains translate into structurally valid designs.

## Relation to Prior Work

DualMPNN builds directly on ProteinMPNN as its base architecture, extending it with a dual-stream message-passing framework inspired by dual-stream networks in computer vision (DS-Net, DRNet). The key conceptual departure from prior inverse folding methods (GraDe-IF, PiFOLD, ESM-IF) is the explicit incorporation of structurally aligned homologous templates as informative priors, drawing an analogy to how MSAs guide structure prediction in AlphaFold and RoseTTAFold. Rather than relying solely on geometric features or diffusion-based sampling to resolve sequence-structure degeneracy, DualMPNN uses Foldseek and TM-align to identify homologous templates and injects their known sequences and structural context through alignment-aware cross-attention, effectively repurposing the MSA-guided paradigm from the forward (folding) problem to the inverse (design) problem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~15% absolute recovery improvement over ProteinMPNN across three benchmarks is practically significant for protein engineering applications where high-fidelity sequence recovery directly impacts downstream design success. The identification of TM-score 0.5 as a critical template-quality threshold provides actionable guidance for practitioners selecting structural priors. However, the ablation reveals that most of the gain (49.87%→61.29%) comes from simply initializing query nodes with template sequences, while the dual-stream attention architecture contributes only ~3–4% additional improvement, raising questions about whether the architectural complexity is justified. Open questions include whether dynamic template selection (rather than single best-match) could further improve performance, how the method behaves in genuinely low-MSA/low-structural-homology regimes where no good template exists, and whether the approach extends to multi-state or disordered protein design.
