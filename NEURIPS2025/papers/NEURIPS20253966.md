## Problem

Quantized fine-tuning of pre-trained LLMs remains an open challenge because, unlike inference, all three matrix multiplications (one forward, two backward) must execute in low precision, and pre-trained models exhibit large outlier values in weights, activations, and output gradients that destabilize lower-precision optimization. Existing approaches either cannot extend Hadamard-based outlier mitigation to the backward pass (QuaRot), retain one multiplication in high precision (SwitchBack), or are designed for from-scratch pre-training rather than fine-tuning (JetFire, DeepSeek FP8).

## Input / Output

HALO takes a pre-trained Transformer LLM (LLaMA-3-8B, Qwen-2.5 14B/32B) and standard fine-tuning text data, applying tensor-wise symmetric quantization with Round-to-Nearest to all weights, activations, and output gradients in INT8, FP6 (E3M2), FP8 (E4M3), or MXFP6 formats. The output is a fine-tuned model in which every linear-layer matrix multiplication runs in the target low precision, achieving near-BF16 accuracy with up to 1.41× end-to-end speedup on RTX 4090 GPUs, while also supporting LoRA/QLoRA-style PEFT where only the large frozen-weight multiplications are quantized and low-rank adapter operations remain in high precision.

## Method

HALO strategically inserts normalized Hadamard rotations into the Transformer's forward and backward passes to suppress outliers before quantization. The key insight is that forward-pass outliers in activations and weights are mitigated by right-hand-side Hadamard transforms (applied to both X and W so they cancel in the matmul), while backward-pass outliers in output gradients propagate across columns and require a left-hand-side Hadamard transform on E_Y. This yields two levels: HALO-1 (right-hand-side only, suitable for moderate dynamic-range formats like FP6) and HALO-2 (adding left-hand-side on errors, needed for narrow-range INT8). The method further integrates with HQ-FSDP to perform quantized all-gather and reduce-scatter communication using globally synchronized scales, and stores quantized activations for memory reduction, enabling all large matmuls to execute in low precision with minimal overhead.

## Results

On LLAMA3-8B fine-tuning, HALO-2 (INT8) achieves 68.3% on GSM8K, 93.8% on ViGGO, and 80.1% on SQL, within 1% of the BF16 baseline (69.3/94.0/79.9) while performing all matrix multiplications in INT8. For FP6, HALO-1 achieves 66.5% on GSM8K (2.8% drop vs. BF16), substantially outperforming SwitchBack (62.7%) and Jetfire (62.8%). End-to-end speedups reach 1.41× on 8× RTX 4090 GPUs for INT8 HALO-2, with 1.35× on 4 GPUs, while maintaining near-lossless accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SwitchBack, HALO-2 dominates on INT8: SwitchBack collapses to 61.0% on ViGGO (vs. HALO-2's 93.8%) and drops to 64.0% on GSM8K (vs. 68.3%), while HALO-2 stays within 0.2% of BF16. On FP6, HALO-1 achieves 66.5% on GSM8K versus SwitchBack's 62.7%, a 3.8-point advantage. Against Jetfire, HALO-2 matches or slightly exceeds it on INT8 (68.3 vs. 68.2 on GSM8K; 93.8 vs. 93.6 on ViGGO) with notably lower variance (±0.1 vs. ±0.4–0.6), and substantially outperforms it on FP6 (66.5 vs. 62.8 on GSM8K, a 3.7-point gap). For PEFT, HALO_PEFT matches LoRA within one standard deviation on INT8 and FP8 across all three tasks, with only a ~2% drop on GSM8K for FP6. In terms of speed, HALO-2 achieves 20–40% higher speedups than SwitchBack (due to SwitchBack retaining one matmul in 16-bit) and at least 20% higher than Jetfire at batch sizes >4 (due to Jetfire's 2D block-wise quantization overhead).

## Relation to Prior Work

HALO extends the Hadamard-rotation-based outlier mitigation introduced for inference by QuaRot and SpinQuant into the training regime, where the backward pass breaks the computational invariance property that makes inference fusion possible. It departs from from-scratch low-precision training methods (SwitchBack, JetFire, DeepSeek FP8) by targeting the harder problem of fine-tuning pre-trained models that already exhibit outlier structures in weights, activations, and errors. The key conceptual departure is the identification of asymmetric Hadamard placement: right-hand-side rotations suffice for the forward pass, while a left-hand-side rotation on output gradients is specifically required to stabilize the backward pass for narrow dynamic-range formats like INT8.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first practical demonstration that all three matrix multiplications in a Transformer's forward-backward pass can be executed in INT8 or FP6 during fine-tuning of a pre-trained LLM while maintaining near-BF16 accuracy, with a 1.41× end-to-end speedup on commodity RTX 4090 GPUs. The compatibility with both full fine-tuning and PEFT (LoRA/QLoRA), combined with quantized FSDP communication, makes it directly applicable to the dominant fine-tuning workflow. Open questions include whether the approach extends to pre-training from scratch (INT8 and FP4 diverge regardless of HALO level), whether it generalizes beyond NVIDIA RTX hardware, and whether a fully low-precision data flow (beyond just the matmuls) can be achieved without accuracy loss.
