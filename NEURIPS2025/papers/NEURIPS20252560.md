## Problem

Existing time series forecasting methods conflate time-invariant (static) and time-varying (dynamic) components, leading to suboptimal performance under distribution shifts. Prior disentanglement approaches either rely on local smoothing that misses global patterns, impose rigid periodicity assumptions (e.g., CycleNet's fixed cycle length), or incur high computational cost from attention mechanisms. The paper addresses the gap of a lightweight, assumption-free framework that explicitly separates and models these two complementary components.

## Input / Output

The method takes a historical multivariate time series X ∈ R^{L×D} (L timesteps, D channels) and a timestamp index used to look up the static embedding. After instance normalization and rFFT to the frequency domain (F = ⌊L/2⌋+1 bins), the model produces a future prediction X̂ ∈ R^{H×D} for H forecast steps. The embedding bank size M is set per dataset (e.g., M=24 for hourly data to capture intra-day patterns, optionally augmented with M=7 for weekly patterns), and the prediction layer is a two-layer MLP with hidden dimension 512.

## Method

TimeEmb operates entirely in the frequency domain: after instance normalization and rFFT, it retrieves a time-invariant component X_s from a learnable embedding bank E ∈ R^{M×F×D} indexed by the last timestamp (t_last mod M), then computes the time-varying residual X_d = X̄ − X_s. The dynamic component is processed by a learnable complex-valued spectral filter ω ∈ C^{F×1} applied via element-wise multiplication, which by the Convolution Theorem corresponds to circular convolution in the time domain and can be interpreted as learning the frequency response of an LTI system. The filtered dynamic and static components are fused (Ẋ = H_ω(X_d) + X_s), inverse-transformed, and passed through a two-layer MLP to produce the final prediction. Training uses a composite loss combining frequency-domain MAE (to mitigate self-correlation bias) and time-domain MSE, weighted by a hyperparameter α. The entire pipeline is parameter-efficient (e.g., only ~8K parameters for the embedding bank on ETTh1) and linear in sequence length, enabling it to serve as a plug-in module for existing backbones with minimal overhead.

## Results

On the standard L=96 setting, TimeEmb achieves the best average MSE across 6 of 7 benchmark datasets: ETTh1 (0.425), ETTh2 (0.362), ETTm1 (0.368), ETTm2 (0.265), Weather (0.237), and Electricity (0.167). The paper reports relative MSE improvements of 3.0%–8.7% over the strongest baselines on average. TimeEmb also uses over 5× fewer parameters than iTransformer while achieving the best predictive performance on the Electricity dataset, with a training time of 1.61 s/epoch and 82.36 MB peak memory on ETTm1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TimeEmb outperforms all nine baselines on 6 of 7 datasets (ETTh1, ETTh2, ETTm1, ETTm2, Weather, Electricity) in both MSE and MAE. The closest competitor is CycleNet, which TimeEmb beats by 1.6%–3.6% MSE on average across those six datasets; DLinear and FreTS lag further behind by 5%–15% MSE. On Traffic, TimeEmb (MSE 0.454, MAE 0.293) is outperformed by iTransformer (MSE 0.428, MAE 0.282) and Fredformer (MSE 0.431, MAE 0.287), the only dataset where it does not rank first. Among frequency-domain baselines, TimeEmb surpasses FilterNet and FITS by 3%–8% MSE, attributed to its explicit separation of time-invariant and time-varying components rather than a single fixed filter. The compatibility study (Table 3) further shows that plugging TimeEmb's disentanglement module into Linear, MLP, DLinear, and iTransformer backbones yields 3%–23% MSE improvements with negligible extra parameters (≈10.7K), confirming its plug-and-play utility.

## Relation to Prior Work

TimeEmb synthesizes ideas from three distinct research threads—seasonal-trend decomposition (DLinear, Autoformer), periodic pattern modeling (CycleNet), and frequency-domain filtering (FilterNet, FITS, Fredformer)—into a unified lightweight disentanglement framework. It departs from local smoothing-based decomposition by introducing a globally learned embedding bank that captures recurring time-invariant patterns across the entire dataset, and from fixed or predefined-period approaches (CycleNet) by making the periodicity granularity a flexible hyperparameter. The frequency filter for the dynamic component draws on the Convolution Theorem to interpret spectral modulation as learning an LTI system, extending prior frequency-domain methods that applied fixed or global filters without explicit static/dynamic separation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play compatibility demonstrated across MLP and Transformer backbones (Table 3) suggests the disentanglement mechanism could serve as a general-purpose module for existing forecasting pipelines at negligible cost, which is practically valuable for resource-constrained deployments. However, the evaluation is limited to seven standard benchmarks with relatively short lookback windows (≤720), and the embedding bank's reliance on a fixed periodicity granularity (M=24 for daily, M=7 for weekly) raises open questions about handling multi-scale or aperiodic non-stationarity in real-world streaming settings. Whether the frequency-domain disentanglement generalizes to irregularly sampled or multivariate-correlated series beyond the standard ETT/Electricity/Traffic benchmarks remains untested.
