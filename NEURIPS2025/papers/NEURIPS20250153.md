## Problem

When LLMs are fine-tuned on new tasks, they catastrophically forget upstream knowledge, yet existing replay-based mitigation strategies rely on random sampling of past examples without knowing which ones will actually be forgotten. The paper asks whether the associations between newly learned tasks and forgotten upstream examples follow simple, predictable patterns that could enable targeted, efficient forgetting mitigation without expensive inference over the entire upstream corpus.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250153/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes an M×N association matrix Z (rows = fine-tuning tasks, columns = upstream examples) collected from prior fine-tuning runs, where each entry z_ij is a log-perplexity increase or binary exact-match drop. For a new unseen task, only ~30 randomly sampled upstream examples require actual LLM inference to obtain seed forgetting; the remaining 10k–140k examples are predicted via matrix completion. The output is a per-example predicted forgetting score vector, which is converted into a categorical sampling distribution p(x_j) ∝ exp(ẑ_ij/τ) (τ=0.1) that governs which upstream examples are replayed during fine-tuning.

## Method

The key empirical finding is that the M×N forgetting association matrix is well-approximated by low-rank decompositions: rank-3 matrix factorization achieves R² > 0.69 on OLMo-7B (85 tasks × 141,876 examples), and even rank-1 exceeds R² = 0.5, indicating that forgetting is dominated by a small number of task-independent and task-dependent factors. Prediction is formulated as matrix completion over Z using three algorithms—additive linear (α^(i)+β^(j)), rank-5 matrix factorization, and k-nearest-neighbors over task rows—trained on known task rows and evaluated on held-out tasks with only 30 seed examples revealed. Matrix factorization achieves the best prediction accuracy (58.16 F1 for binary forgetting vs. 6.4 for random), outperforming embedding-based comparators (SBERT, OpenAI text-embedding-3) that learn semantic task-example relationships. For mitigation, the predicted forgetting scores drive weighted replay: one mini-batch of 8 upstream examples is replayed every 32 fine-tuning steps, sampled proportionally to exp(ẑ_ij/τ), in either an offline variant (one extra fine-tuning run to collect seeds) or an online variant (first 10% of steps replay randomly, then switch to predicted-forgetting-weighted sampling).

## Results

The proposed matrix factorization (MF) approach for predicting example forgetting achieves the lowest prediction error across most model settings. On OLMo-7B-Instruct (in-domain), MF reaches 58.16 F1 for binary forgetting prediction, compared to 55.46 (SBERT) and 57.16 (Text-Embedding-3), while random guess yields only 6.4 F1. On MPT (in-domain), MF achieves 10.41 RMSE in log-perplexity increase, substantially outperforming SBERT (13.86) and Text-Embedding-3 (14.35). Out-of-domain, MF on MPT achieves 7.03 RMSE versus 10.59 (SBERT) and 11.01 (Text-Embedding-3), demonstrating strong generalization to unseen task categories.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares four baseline categories: (1) embedding-based methods (SBERT and OpenAI Text-Embedding-3) that learn semantic similarity between tasks and examples via a frozen encoder plus trainable MLP, (2) additive linear models that decompose forgetting into task and example effects, (3) KNN that finds similar tasks by seed-forgetting patterns, and (4) the proposed MF (matrix factorization). MF consistently outperforms both embedding baselines across all model sizes and domains — e.g., 10.41 vs. 13.86 RMSE on MPT in-domain, and 58.16 vs. 55.46 F1 on OLMo-7B-Instruct. Among matrix completion methods, MF achieves the best in-domain RMSE on OLMo-7B (7.14 vs. 7.33 KNN, 7.40 Additive) and MPT (10.41 vs. 12.80 KNN, 13.33 Additive), while KNN edges out MF slightly on OLMo-7B-Instruct out-of-domain F1 (44.11 vs. 42.91). The embedding-based approaches, which require no seed forgetting at inference, are the weakest overall, confirming that forgetting statistics are more predictive than task-example semantic similarity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Jin & Ren (2024), which first formulated the problem of predicting which upstream examples will be forgotten after fine-tuning and proposed an embedding-based (SBERT + MLP) prediction approach. The key departure is replacing semantic similarity with a structural/statistical approach: the authors show the M×N task-by-example forgetting matrix is well-approximated by low-rank matrices, enabling matrix completion (MF, KNN, additive linear) as the prediction mechanism. The work also connects to the broader replay-based continual learning literature (MIR-T, GEM) and data attribution methods (TRAK, LESS), but positions itself as providing a more efficient and interpretable alternative to both gradient-based and embedding-based selection strategies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical value is clear: once a matrix completion model is trained on a few known tasks, forgetting on unseen tasks can be predicted from just ~30 seed examples, avoiding expensive full upstream inference and enabling targeted replay that statistically significantly reduces forgetting. The low-rank finding suggests forgetting has a simple underlying structure, but the paper acknowledges no theoretical explanation for why this holds and notes that more capable/recent models exhibit more complex (higher-rank) associations. Open questions include whether the low-rank structure persists under sequential/continual learning, whether it generalizes to RL fine-tuning, and whether the modest replay improvements scale to larger upstream corpora or more diverse task distributions.
