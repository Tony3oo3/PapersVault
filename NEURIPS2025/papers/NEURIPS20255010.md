## Problem

Generative Flow Networks (GFNs) learn to sample objects proportionally to a reward, making them well-suited for probabilistic forecasting, but they operate over discrete action spaces while time series values are continuous. Directly applying GFNs to continuous forecasting faces two coupled hurdles: mapping continuous values to discrete actions without losing precision, and maintaining differentiability through the discrete selection step for gradient-based training. Existing tokenization approaches (e.g., Chronos) use fixed quantization decided a priori, which is suboptimal across training phases.

## Input / Output

The model takes a fixed-length context window of T=512 consecutive time series values as input, encoded by a Transformer encoder into a fixed-dimensional representation. It sequentially generates a forecast trajectory of T'=64 steps, where each step selects one of K quantized bin centers as the next predicted value; the hard (discrete) sample drives state transitions while the soft (continuous expectation) sample is used for reward computation and gradient backpropagation. The final output is a set of sampled trajectories from which a full predictive distribution over future values is estimated, evaluated via CRPS, WQL, and MASE.

## Method

Temporal GFN frames forecasting as reward-driven trajectory construction in a GFN state space, where states are sliding windows and actions append the next quantized value. Two key mechanisms address the continuous-to-discrete gap: (1) an adaptive curriculum-based quantization scheme that dynamically adjusts the number of bins K during training based on reward improvement (ΔR) and policy entropy (H), starting coarse and refining as the policy stabilizes, with a weight-reuse strategy to prevent catastrophic forgetting when K increases; and (2) a Straight-Through Estimator that uses the discrete argmax action for state transitions but backpropagates gradients through the continuous expectation of the policy distribution. Training uses the Trajectory Balance loss (enforcing flow consistency across full trajectories) augmented with an entropy regularizer to prevent mode collapse, and the paper provides theoretical bounds on the quantization-induced gradient error and the adaptive update factor range.

## Results

On the aggregated SOTA benchmark (Table 2), Temporal GFN achieves CRPS 0.1542, WQL 0.2158, MASE 0.9378, Calibration Error 0.0348, and Multimodality Score 0.8762 in the zero-shot/in-domain setting, outperforming all compared foundation models. Against RL and MCMC baselines (Table 1), the Adaptive K20 configuration yields 25–35% relative improvements in CRPS, WQL, and MASE over PPO, SAC, and MCMC respectively. The Learned Policy variant further improves probabilistic metrics to CRPS 0.1453 and WQL 0.2137 in the in-domain setting, though at the cost of slightly higher MASE (1.0345 vs. 0.9378).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Temporal GFN outperforms all six baselines across every reported metric. Against the RL baselines, it achieves 25.1% lower CRPS than PPO (0.1674 vs. 0.2299) and 29.5% lower than SAC (0.1674 vs. 0.2423), with MCMC showing the largest gap at 35.5% relative improvement. Among SOTA foundation models, Temporal GFN leads Lag-Llama by 8% in CRPS (0.1542 vs. 0.1675) and by 21% in Calibration Error (0.0348 vs. 0.0623), while also surpassing it on Multimodality Score (0.8762 vs. 0.7234). The gap widens against Chronos (CRPS 0.1542 vs. 0.1731), MOREI (CRPS 0.1542 vs. 0.1859), and TimeFM (CRPS 0.1542 vs. 0.2076), with TimeFM showing the weakest calibration (0.1183) and multimodality (0.3012). Notably, Temporal GFN's advantage is most pronounced on distributional metrics (Calibration Error, Multimodality Score) rather than point-forecast MASE, where the Learned Policy variant actually trails the zero-shot configuration (1.0345 vs. 0.9378), suggesting a trade-off between probabilistic fidelity and point accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the Generative Flow Network (GFN) paradigm—originally developed for discrete compositional object generation (molecules, graphs, sequences)—to continuous probabilistic time series forecasting, a domain previously dominated by LLM-based tokenizers (Chronos, Lag-Llama) and distribution-parameterizing architectures (DEAR, diffusion models). It builds directly on the Trajectory Balance training objective (Malkin et al., 2022) and the Straight-Through Estimator (Bengio et al., 2013), but departs from prior GFN work by introducing an adaptive, curriculum-based quantization mechanism that dynamically adjusts discretization granularity during training, and from LLM-based forecasters by replacing fixed-vocabulary cross-entropy with a reward-proportional flow-matching objective. The conceptual bridge is that a forecast trajectory is treated as a compositional object whose sampling probability is proportional to a reward (forecast accuracy), rather than as a sequence to be decoded autoregressively.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a principled alternative to fixed-quantization LLM forecasters by coupling adaptive precision with a flow-consistency training signal, and the theoretical bounds (quantization error on policy gradients, bounded adaptive update factor) provide stability guarantees absent in prior heuristic discretization schemes. However, the sequential step-by-step generation is computationally more expensive than single-pass Transformer decoding, the current implementation is univariate, and performance gains over strong SOTA baselines (Chronos, Lag-Llama) are modest on standard metrics while more pronounced on self-defined multimodality and calibration measures. Open questions include whether the GFN objective's advantage over standard autoregressive likelihood persists at scale, how the framework extends to multivariate and irregularly-sampled settings, and whether the adaptive quantization benefit generalizes beyond the specific reward function used here.
