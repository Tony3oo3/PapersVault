## Problem

Existing role-playing agents (RPAs) rely on superficial dialogue data and lack deep, human-like internal thought processes. While Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) could simulate character thinking, their direct application to role-playing suffers from two failure modes: attention diversion (the model forgets its assigned role and focuses on generic problem-solving) and style drift (reasoning becomes overly formal and structured rather than vivid and character-consistent). This paper tackles how to transfer LRM reasoning capabilities into RPAs while preserving role identity and contextual style adaptability.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a user query, a character profile (from which core elements—emotion, experience, standpoint, motivation—are extracted), and multi-turn dialogue context. It produces a two-part text output: an explicit role-aware reasoning trace (a chain-of-thought that is both character-grounded and stylistically matched to the scenario type) followed by a final character-consistent response. The reasoning trace length and expressive register dynamically shift between rigorous logical deduction (for analytical scenarios) and vivid internal monologue (for emotional/narrative scenarios), controlled by the RSO module.

## Method

Role-Aware Reasoning (RAR) operates in two sequential stages. First, Role Identity Activation (RIA) prompts a Large Reasoning Model (Qwen2-32B) to generate reasoning traces under explicit character-constraint instructions (C_R) that inject the character's emotion, experience, standpoint, and motivation at critical reasoning steps; these role-aware traces form a distillation dataset used to fine-tune a non-reasoning LLM (LLaMA-3-8B) via standard supervised loss. Second, Reasoning Style Optimization (RSO) constructs paired positive and negative training data by combining two scenario types (logical analysis, vivid interaction) with two system-prompt-defined reasoning styles (fact-focused, character-knowledge-focused), then applies a contrastive sigmoid loss to teach the model to select the appropriate reasoning register based on dialogue context. The full pipeline is trained with LoRA (rank 64) and 4-bit quantization, with RIA using a 1e-4 learning rate and RSO using 5e-5, on 8×H20 GPUs.

## Results

On CharacterBench, RAR achieves an average score of 3.69, outperforming the next-best method Distill (3.57) by 0.12 points, and specialized role-playing models CharacterGLM (3.21) and Neeko (3.19) by 0.48 and 0.50 points respectively. On SocialBench, RAR attains an average of 65.4, surpassing Distill+MoreThink (61.5), Distill (61.1), CharacterGLM (60.0), and Neeko (58.7). RAR leads on 11 of 13 CharacterBench dimensions and 7 of 9 SocialBench dimensions, with particularly strong gains in Memory Consistency (3.99 vs. 3.81 for Distill), Behavior Consistency (4.06/3.93 vs. 3.91/3.62), and Social Preference (Neu. 83.1, Pos. 84.8, Neg. 68.5).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RAR outperforms both specialized role-playing baselines across nearly all dimensions. Against Neeko (EMNLP 2024), RAR gains +0.50 average on CharacterBench (3.69 vs. 3.19) and +6.7 on SocialBench (65.4 vs. 58.7), with the largest margins in Memory Consistency (+0.71), Behavior Consistency (+0.85/+0.95), and Social Preference (Neu. +16.1, Neg. +21.8). Against CharacterGLM (EMNLP 2024 Industry Track), RAR leads by +0.48 on CharacterBench (3.69 vs. 3.21) and +5.4 on SocialBench (65.4 vs. 60.0), excelling in Attribute Consistency (+0.95/+0.71) and Behavior Consistency (+1.05/+1.03). RAR also surpasses the generic distillation baseline (Distill, 3.57) by 0.12 on CharacterBench, while the MoreThink variant (3.05) actually degrades significantly, confirming that unguided extended reasoning harms role-playing. The ablation study further shows that removing RIA drops the average to 3.49 (−0.20) and removing RSO drops it to 3.60 (−0.09), confirming both modules contribute complementary gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of role-playing agent research (Neeko, CharacterGLM, Character-LLM) and Large Reasoning Model distillation (s1, LiMo, DeepSeek-R1). It departs from prior RPA work that focuses on superficial style matching or artificially constructed thought traces, and from prior LRM distillation work designed for logical/factual tasks, by identifying two specific failure modes—attention diversion and style drift—when reasoning is naively applied to role-playing (building on the observation in Feng et al. that reasoning does not necessarily improve role-playing). The RAR method repurposes LRM distillation through a two-stage pipeline: supervised fine-tuning with role-identity constraints (RIA) followed by contrastive style optimization (RSO), a combination not previously applied to character-consistent reasoning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical recipe for transferring reasoning capabilities to role-playing agents without the typical degradation, which is relevant as emotional-companion and virtual-interaction applications grow. However, several limitations constrain broader impact: the binary style taxonomy (logical vs. vivid) oversimplifies real conversations, characters are static rather than evolving, and experiments are limited to a single 8B base model and one 32B teacher LRM. Open questions include whether RAR scales to larger models, how to handle mixed-mode reasoning in natural dialogue, and whether the contrastive style objective generalizes beyond the two predefined scenario types.
