## Problem

FP8 quantization accelerates large-dimensional matrix multiplications on modern GPUs, but its per-tensor scaling overhead (amax reduction and element-wise scaling) dominates computation for the small-dimensional matrices used in LoRA adapters, causing FP8 LoRA fine-tuning to be slower than FP16. Existing quantized LoRA methods (QLoRA, QA-LoRA) reduce memory but ignore training speed, while FP8 training methods target full pretraining and do not address the small-matrix bottleneck inherent to parameter-efficient fine-tuning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254110/cropped_page3_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pretrained LLM backbone (LLaMA-7B/13B in FP16), a training dataset (Alpaca or OASST1), and a LoRA rank r as input. During initialization, the backbone is quantized to FP8 (E4M3 for weights/activations, E5M2 for gradients), and the quantization error is decomposed via rank-r SVD to form the initial melded adapters. The output is a fully FP8-quantized fine-tuned model that requires no post-training quantization step, enabling direct low-precision deployment.

## Method

FALQON introduces 'melded LoRA,' which eliminates separate LoRA adapter paths by interpreting the FP8 quantization error Δ_Q W as an implicit low-rank adapter: the backbone is quantized, the error is SVD-decomposed into Â and B̂, and Â is concatenated to the quantized backbone so a single FP8 matmul simultaneously produces the layer output and the intermediate activation needed for B̂ gradient computation. Only B̂ gradients are computed (Â is frozen, following LoRA-FA), and a row-wise proxy update mechanism stores optimizer-driven changes in a ΔB buffer, selectively applying only the top-k rows with the largest accumulated updates back into the FP8 backbone. This design removes all redundant small-tensor quantization operations from the forward and backward passes, allowing the FP8 hardware acceleration to fully materialize and yielding ~3× speedup over quantized LoRA baselines while maintaining comparable MMLU accuracy.

## Results

On LLaMA-7B fine-tuned on Alpaca, FALQON achieves 1.80 s/step, a 3.02× speedup over QLoRA (5.45 s) and 5.24× over QA-LoRA (9.44 s), while maintaining a competitive MMLU average of 0.3491 (vs. QLoRA 0.3272, QA-LoRA 0.3548, IR-QLoRA 0.3388). On LLaMA-13B, FALQON reaches 3.26 s/step (2.87× over QLoRA) with MMLU 0.4644. On commonsense reasoning benchmarks, FALQON scores 0.6320 average, within ~1.4% of FP16 LoRA (0.6464) and substantially above FP6-LLM (0.3273).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against quantized LoRA baselines, FALQON dominates on speed: it is 3.02× faster than QLoRA, 5.24× faster than QA-LoRA, and 4.59× faster than IR-QLoRA on LLaMA-7B, while using fewer trainable parameters (80M vs. 89–160M). On MMLU, FALQON (0.3491) slightly trails QA-LoRA (0.3548) by 0.6% but surpasses QLoRA (0.3272) and IR-QLoRA (0.3388). Against FP quantization methods, FALQON is 1.22× faster than TorchAO (1.79 s vs. 2.18 s) with a higher MMLU (0.3491 vs. 0.3393), 1.28× faster than Fishman et al. (2.29 s) with a 0.5% MMLU gap, and 1.52× faster than FP6-LLM (2.72 s) with a dramatic 10+ point MMLU advantage (0.3491 vs. 0.2295–0.2509). On commonsense reasoning (Table 3), FALQON (0.6320 avg) is within 1.4% of FP16 LoRA (0.6464) and IR-QLoRA (0.6463), slightly below TorchAO (0.6455) and Fishman et al. (0.6447), but far above FP6-LLM (0.3273). Notably, TorchAO and Fishman et al. cannot fine-tune the 13B model on a single RTX 4090 due to OOM, whereas FALQON handles both 7B and 13B within 24 GB VRAM.

## Relation to Prior Work

FALQON extends LoRA (Hu et al., ICLR 2022) by exploiting the quantization error of an FP8 backbone as an implicit low-rank adapter initialization, thereby eliminating the separate small-tensor LoRA computation path that dominates quantization overhead. It builds on LoRA-FA (Zhang et al., 2023), which demonstrated that freezing matrix A during fine-tuning has negligible accuracy impact, and uses this to simplify gradient computation to only the B matrix. The paper departs from quantized LoRA methods (QLoRA, QA-LoRA, IR-QLoRA) that target memory reduction via weight-only quantization, and from FP8 training frameworks (TorchAO, Fishman et al., FP8-LM) that assume large-dimensional matrices where quantization overhead is amortized—FALQON specifically addresses the regime where these assumptions break down.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~3× speedup for LoRA fine-tuning is practically significant in multi-adapter scenarios (personalization, multi-task learning, domain adaptation) where thousands of adapters must be trained under tight compute budgets, and the end-to-end FP8 workflow removes a post-training quantization step from the deployment pipeline. However, the method is validated only on LLaMA-7B/13B decoder-only transformers on a single GPU, leaving open whether the melded-adapter approach generalizes to encoder architectures, diffusion models, or 70B+ scale where memory constraints differ. The accuracy is comparable but not uniformly superior to FP16 LoRA on commonsense benchmarks, and the top-k selective update introduces a hyperparameter (k) whose interaction with learning rate requires mild tuning.
