## Problem

While prior work established that MLP layers in transformers function as key-value memories with value vectors as fundamental knowledge units, it remained unclear how the *distribution* of knowledge across these vectors relates to model performance. Specifically, whether stronger models encode knowledge more focally (specialized) versus diffusely (superposed) across parameters, and whether this specialization causally drives better knowledge retrieval, had not been quantified or validated.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253305/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The analysis takes 20 open-source LLMs (≤14B parameters) and the newly constructed SpecWiki benchmark (525 Wikipedia concepts stratified into low/medium/high frequency tiers, each with 10 multiple-choice and open-ended questions). For each concept, the method computes average activation coefficients m^ℓ over 10 concept-related questions and m*^ℓ over 50 irrelevant questions at every MLP layer, then identifies the top-k most differentially activated value vectors per layer. The primary output is a Parameter Specialization Score (PSS) per model, computed by masking those vectors and measuring the accuracy gap between concept-specific and irrelevant questions relative to pre-surgery accuracy, averaged over k ∈ {10%, 20%, 30%, 40%, 50%}.

## Method

The paper introduces a knowledge vector masking procedure that isolates concept-specific value vectors by computing |m_j^ℓ − m_j^{*ℓ}| across all n intermediate dimensions at each layer, selecting the top-k indices for ablation. The Parameter Specialization Score (PSS) is then defined as the absolute difference between post-surgery General Score and Concept Specific Score, normalized by pre-surgery General Score, yielding a single scalar per model that quantifies how sharply knowledge is localized in parameters. Across 20 models, PSS correlates strongly (Pearson r = 0.92) with SpecWiki accuracy, and increases with model scale and training progress. To establish causality, four controlled fine-tuning variants (full vectors, partial top-activated vectors, complementary vectors, random vectors) are applied to LLaMA2-7B and Qwen2-7B on 10 high-frequency concepts; only the partial-vector strategy (FT-PV), which updates the most highly activated knowledge parameters, simultaneously improves PSS, task accuracy, and reduces hallucination as measured by Semantic Entropy and Local Intrinsic Dimension.

## Results

Across 20 open-source LLMs, the Parameter Specialization Score (PSS) shows a strong correlation with model performance on SpecWiki (Pearson r = 0.92, Spearman ρ = 0.93). Larger models exhibit higher specialization: Qwen1.5 PSS increases from 0.019 (0.5B) to 0.184 (14B), while Gemma2 rises from 0.057 (2B) to 0.138 (9B). In controlled fine-tuning experiments, the FT-PV strategy (fine-tuning only top-activated value vectors) achieves the best performance: on Qwen2-7B it reaches 0.77 MCQ accuracy and 0.70 OEG accuracy, with the lowest hallucination metrics (Semantic Entropy 0.39, LID 6.92), outperforming full fine-tuning (FT-FV) and random-vector fine-tuning (FT-RV).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against external methods but instead contrasts four internal fine-tuning strategies (FT-FV, FT-PV, FT-CV, FT-RV) to isolate the causal effect of parameter specialization. FT-PV (fine-tuning only the top-activated value vectors) consistently outperforms all other strategies on both LLaMA2-7B and Qwen2-7B: it achieves the highest MCQ accuracy (0.67 vs. 0.63 for FT-FV on LLaMA2; 0.77 vs. 0.73 for FT-FV on Qwen2), the highest PSS, and the lowest hallucination metrics (Semantic Entropy 0.50/0.39 and LID 7.89/6.92). FT-CV (fine-tuning complementary vectors) and FT-RV (random vectors) perform at or below the base model, confirming that the benefit is specific to targeting the most activated knowledge vectors rather than simply updating more parameters. Across the 20-model correlation analysis, more recent and larger models (e.g., Qwen2-7B, Gemma2-9B) consistently show higher PSS and better SpecWiki accuracy than earlier or smaller counterparts (e.g., GPT-j-6b, Qwen1.5-0.5B), reinforcing that specialization is a hallmark of stronger models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the key-value memory interpretation of MLPs established by Geva et al. (2021b) and the finding that individual value vectors serve as fundamental knowledge units (Geva et al., 2022b; Meng et al., 2022), shifting the question from *where* knowledge is stored to *how specialized* that storage is. While prior work on knowledge superposition (Elhage et al., 2022; Olah, 2023) described the phenomenon qualitatively, this paper introduces the Parameter Specialization Score (PSS) to quantify it per-model and systematically compares 20 open-source LLMs. The controlled fine-tuning experiments (FT-PV vs. FT-FV/CV/RV) go beyond the observational masking analyses in Meng et al. (2022) by establishing a causal link between specialization degree and knowledge-task performance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that stronger models consolidate knowledge into fewer, more specialized parameter vectors—and that this specialization is causally linked to better knowledge retrieval and lower hallucination—provides a concrete interpretability lens for understanding model capability differences and could inform more efficient knowledge editing and model compression strategies. However, the analysis is confined to MLP value vectors (not attention layers), models up to 14B parameters, and encyclopedic factual knowledge, leaving open whether the specialization pattern generalizes to procedural knowledge, larger models, or other architectures like Mixture-of-Experts. The unexpected result that masking a small fraction of concept-related vectors *improves* performance on unrelated questions suggests a potential regularization mechanism worth further investigation.
