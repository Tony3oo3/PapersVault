## Problem

Existing diffusion-based unrestricted adversarial attacks (e.g., ACA) jointly optimize visual consistency and attack effectiveness in a single latent-space optimization, but these two objectives are inherently conflicting and lead to reward hacking and unstable, suboptimal trade-offs. The paper identifies that the latent space of diffusion models is highly sensitive to perturbations, making it infeasible to collect stable preference data for standard alignment techniques like DPO, and that no prior work has framed adversarial example generation as a preference alignment problem.

## Input / Output

The method takes a clean reference image (ImageNet validation set, 1000 images at 256×256), a text prompt, and a target misclassification label. The image is VAE-encoded into latent space, DDIM-inverted to obtain z_T, and after iterative denoising with attack guidance, the final latent is decoded back to pixel space to produce the unrestricted adversarial image. The method operates on Stable Diffusion V1.5 and supports both latent-based (APA-SG, APA-GC) and prompt-embedding-based (APA-GC-P) optimization variants, with the latter yielding better visual fidelity at the cost of lower attack efficacy.

## Method

APA is a two-stage framework that decouples the conflicting adversary preferences. Stage 1 (Visual Consistency Alignment) fine-tunes LoRA parameters (rank 8, on Q/K/V attention matrices) for 200 steps using a rule-based denoising-loss reward that encodes the input image's structure into the model's generation space, making the model robust to adversarial noise on z_T. Stage 2 (Attack Effectiveness Alignment) iteratively optimizes z_T (or prompt embedding) via dual-path attack guidance: a trajectory-level momentum update (using skip gradient or gradient checkpointing) combined with step-level dense rewards injected at each of the final T_a=10 denoising steps, where intermediate latents are interpolated toward the clean latent to reduce classifier bias. A diffusion augmentation strategy mixes step-level intermediate outputs z_0^t with the trajectory-level final output through differentiable data augmentation (padding, resizing, brightness) to aggregate diverse gradients and improve black-box transferability, mitigating overfitting to the surrogate classifier.

## Results

On the ImageNet-compatible dataset (1,000 images), APA-GC achieves the highest average black-box attack success rate (ASR) across all four substitute models: 88.02% (RN-50), 87.01% (MN-v2), 77.48% (MobViT-s), and 74.82% (ViT-B). Against adversarial defenses (Table 2), APA-GC reaches 70.20% average ASR, outperforming the previous SOTA (ACA at 57.47%) by 12.7%. In terms of visual quality, APA-GC maintains LPIPS of 0.23 and SSIM of 0.69, comparable to ACA (0.37/0.61) and DiffAttack (0.14/0.68), while the prompt-optimized variant APA-GC-P achieves the best visual consistency (LPIPS 0.09, SSIM 0.82) at a modest ASR of 62.08%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

APA-GC dominates all 11 baselines on black-box transferability across every substitute model tested. Against the strongest diffusion-based baselines, APA-GC improves average black-box ASR by 28.5% over ACA (88.02% vs. 59.49% with RN-50) and by 22.9% over DiffAttack (88.02% vs. 65.10%), while APA-SG (using the same skip-gradient strategy as ACA) still gains 15.8% and 10.2% respectively. Among non-diffusion unrestricted attacks, NCF is the strongest baseline at 41.90% average ASR, yet APA-GC surpasses it by 46.1%; color-based methods (SAE 36.38%, ColorFool 17.21%) and texture/shape methods (cAdv 32.04%, tAdv 25.97%) trail further behind. In the appendix comparisons (Table 8), APA-GC also outperforms ADef (9.09%), ReColorAdv (23.72%), PPGD (13.56%), LPA (25.02%), and ACE (14.58%) by 60+ percentage points in average ASR. Against adversarial defenses (Table 2), APA-GC achieves 70.20% average ASR versus ACA's 57.47% and DiffAttack's 37.17%, confirming that the dual-path guidance and diffusion augmentation provide robust transferability even under preprocessing and adversarially-trained defenses.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends the diffusion-based unrestricted adversarial attack line initiated by ACA (Chen et al., NeurIPS 2024), which established DDIM inversion and skip-gradient latent optimization as the standard pipeline. The key conceptual departure is importing the preference alignment paradigm from LLM/diffusion fine-tuning literature (DPO, RL, direct backpropagation with differentiable rewards) into the adversarial attack setting, reframing the two conflicting objectives—visual consistency and attack effectiveness—as a multi-preference alignment problem. Methodologically, it builds on LoRA (Hu et al., ICLR 2022) for lightweight visual consistency encoding and on class-guided generation (Dhariwal & Nichol, NeurIPS 2021) adapted into step-level attack guidance, but the two-stage decoupling architecture and diffusion augmentation for transferability are novel combinations not present in prior attack methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The preference alignment framing offers a principled lens for understanding why prior diffusion-based attacks suffer from reward hacking and suboptimal trade-offs, and the two-stage decoupling provides a general recipe for handling conflicting objectives in generative-model-based attacks. The substantial black-box ASR gains (~88% vs. ~59–65% for prior SOTA) and the demonstrated robustness against adversarial defenses make this practically relevant for security evaluation. However, the method remains more computationally expensive than conventional Lp attacks (96.5s vs. seconds), is limited to diffusion models (not rectified flows or other generative frameworks), and the per-image LoRA fine-tuning requirement limits scalability to large-scale deployment. Open questions include whether the alignment perspective generalizes to video, 3D, or multimodal adversarial settings, and whether faster samplers can close the efficiency gap.
