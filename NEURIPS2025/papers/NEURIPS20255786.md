## Problem

While single-agent stochastic shortest path (SSP) learning is well understood with tight regret bounds, the decentralized multi-agent variant (Dec-MASSP) with linear function approximation lacked any fundamental lower bound on regret. A recent upper bound of Õ(B*^1.5 d√(nK)/c_min) was reported for Dec-MASSPs, but whether this rate is optimal remained open. This paper asks: what are the inherent information-theoretic limits of learning in decentralized multi-agent SSPs?

## Input / Output

The analysis operates on a family of constructed two-node network instances parameterized by (n, δ, Δ, θ), where n agents each have a (d−1)-dimensional binary action space, yielding an exponential global state space of size 2^n and action space of size 2^{n(d−1)}. The transition kernel is linearly parameterized via a novel feature map φ(s'|s,a) ∈ R^{nd} and parameter θ ∈ R^{nd}. The output is a regret lower bound of Ω(d√(KB*/n)/2^n) over K episodes, along with a full characterization of optimal policy structure (action a_θ is optimal in every state, and value depends only on the number of agents at the non-goal node).

## Method

The paper constructs hard-to-learn LM-MASSP instances on a minimal two-node network and derives the first Ω(√K) regret lower bound for any decentralized learning algorithm. The key technical contributions are: (1) a novel feature design for transition probabilities that yields valid distributions despite the exponential state space, (2) symmetry-based arguments showing that the optimal policy selects a fixed action a_θ in every state and that the value function depends only on the number of agents at the non-goal node (type r), enabling induction over r to characterize optimal values, and (3) an information-theoretic lower bound that overcomes the intractability of KL divergence with exponentially many terms by exploiting instance symmetry to bound KL(P_θ^π ‖ P_{θ^j}^π) in terms of the number of agents at each node. The proof unrolls the regret recurrence, applies Pinsker's inequality, and uses a bijective pairing of parameter instances to isolate the information bottleneck, ultimately showing no algorithm can beat Ω(√K) regret.

## Results

The paper establishes the first regret lower bound for decentralized multi-agent SSPs (Dec-MASSPs) under linear function approximation: Ω(d·√(KB*/n) / 2ⁿ) over K episodes. This matches the previously reported upper bound of Õ(B*^1.5 · d·√(nK)/c_min) from [23] in the dependence on K, up to constant and poly-logarithmic factors. As a special case at n=1, the bound recovers the single-agent linear-mixture SSP lower bound Ω(dB*√K) from [14], confirming tightness in the single-agent regime. The bound holds for any number of agents n ≥ 1 and any decentralized learning algorithm.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is against [23] (Trivedi & Hemachandra, AISTATS 2023), which provided the first upper bound Õ(B*^1.5 · d·√(nK)/c_min) for Dec-MASSPs but no lower bound; this work closes that gap by establishing a matching Ω(√K) dependence, confirming the fundamental learning difficulty. For the single-agent linear-mixture SSP setting, the lower bound recovers [14] (Min et al., ICML 2022) exactly at n=1, matching their Ω(dB*√K) result and confirming the tightness of their upper bound in K. In the tabular SSP setting, [18] (Rosenberg et al., ICML 2020) and [7] (Cohen et al., NeurIPS 2021) already achieve matching upper and lower bounds, so this paper does not improve upon those results but uses their techniques as a foundation. The paper does not directly compare against [20] (Tarbouriech et al.) or [24] (Vial et al.) since those address different settings (tabular and model-free linear SSP respectively) without multi-agent structure. Overall, the contribution is the first lower bound in the Dec-MASSP column of Table 1, transforming a previously one-sided (upper-bound-only) picture into a tight characterization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the information-theoretic lower bound techniques for single-agent SSP with linear function approximation (Min et al., 2022) to the decentralized multi-agent setting introduced by Trivedi & Hemachandra (2023), which had only an upper bound. The key departure is handling the exponential state-action space (2^n states) and coupled multi-agent dynamics, which require novel feature designs, symmetry-based partitioning of the state space by agent count per node, and a new approach to bounding KL divergence over exponentially many transition terms. The paper also recovers the single-agent lower bound of Min et al. as the n=1 special case, confirming consistency with the established SSP literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By establishing the first Ω(√K) regret lower bound for Dec-MASSPs, this work closes the gap with the existing upper bound in the number of episodes K, confirming that the √K rate is fundamental and no algorithm can do better in this setting. The constructed hard-to-learn instances, whose difficulty scales with n, provide a concrete benchmark for evaluating future decentralized MARL algorithms. Open questions include whether the gap can be closed in other parameters (d, B*, c_min), how bounds behave under model misspecification, and whether a formal separation between Dec-MASSP and single-agent SSP regret can be established to prove fundamental hardness of the multi-agent setting.
