## Problem

Multi-expert vision encoders substantially improve VLM performance but are computationally prohibitive at inference time. Existing distillation approaches like MoVE-KD use a single fixed set of LoRA adapters for all teachers, causing noisy and conflicting knowledge transfer due to the heterogeneity in teachers' training data, architectures, and objectives. The paper asks how to distill complementary strengths from multiple visual experts into a single encoder without incurring multi-encoder inference costs or suffering from inter-teacher interference.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250034/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the model takes a single RGB image and a free-form text instruction, producing a natural-language response via a Vicuna-7B LLM. During training, the same image is additionally processed by up to four frozen teacher encoders (CLIP, ConvNeXt, EVA-02, SAM/Pix2Struct) whose visual token sequences serve as distillation targets. The student CLIP encoder outputs a fixed-length token sequence (m×D) that is projected by a 2-layer MLP into the LLM's input space; no special token format or resolution constraint beyond standard CLIP preprocessing is imposed.

## Method

HAWAII introduces a Mixture-of-LoRA-Adapter (MOLA) module in which each FFN layer of the student CLIP encoder is augmented with teacher-specific LoRA adapters (one per teacher) and a smaller set of general-knowledge LoRA adapters, with two sparse routers selecting which adapter to activate per layer. Knowledge is transferred hierarchically: coarse-grained distillation (CGKD) concatenates all teachers' unshuffled features channel-wise, compresses them through a 2-layer MLP into a collective-consensus target, and applies an MSE loss to the student output; fine-grained distillation (FGKD) activates only the i-th teacher-specific adapter and computes a token-importance-weighted MSE between the student output and that teacher's features, where importance scores are derived from softmax attention between the teacher's visual tokens and the projected text instruction. This separation ensures each adapter learns from a single teacher without cross-teacher noise, while the general adapters and CGKD capture the shared consensus across all experts. Training uses a combined objective of text-generation loss, both distillation losses, and a MoE balance loss, with only the adapters, routers, and projector trained in the pretraining stage before full fine-tuning.

## Results

HAWAII (using CLIP, ConvNeXt, and EVA-02 as teachers) improves over the LLaVA-1.5 baseline on 9 out of 10 benchmarks, with the largest relative gains on VizWiz (+7.8%, 50.0→53.9), SQA (+5.5%, 66.8→70.5), and MMBench (+4.0%, 64.3→66.9). On MME, HAWAII scores 1540.2 vs. 1510.7 for the baseline, and on POPE it reaches 87.3 vs. 85.9. The best variant HAWAII‡ (adding SAM as a fourth teacher) further pushes SQA to 70.8 and POPE to 87.5, while HAWAII† (adding Pix2Struct) achieves the highest VizWiz score of 54.2.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the direct KD competitor MoVE-KD (same teachers, fixed LoRA adapters), HAWAII wins on every reported benchmark: VizWiz 53.9 vs. 52.3, SQA 70.5 vs. 69.4, MMBench 66.9 vs. 66.3, MME 1540.2 vs. 1524.5, and POPE 87.3 vs. 86.9, validating the benefit of teacher-specific adapters and hierarchical distillation. Compared to the LLaVA-1.5 baseline, HAWAII improves on 9/10 benchmarks, with the only exception being a negligible GQA drop (62.8 vs. 62.0). Against multi-encoder MLLMs in Table 6, HAWAII outperforms Eagle-X5 on SQA (70.5 vs. 69.8), MME (1540.2 vs. 1528), and MMMU (36.6 vs. 36.3), but trails on GQA (62.8 vs. 64.9), POPE (87.3 vs. 88.8), and SeedBench (67.5 vs. 73.9), suggesting multi-encoder approaches retain an edge on tasks requiring broad general visual knowledge. HAWAII also surpasses MouSi on SQA (70.5 vs. 69.0) and POPE (87.3 vs. 86.5), and beats Brave on VizWiz (53.9 vs. 54.2 is a near-tie) and GQA (62.8 vs. 52.7). Overall, HAWAII achieves the best or near-best results across all 10 benchmarks while using only a single vision encoder at inference, avoiding the multi-encoder computational overhead.

## Relation to Prior Work

HAWAII directly extends MoVE-KD, the first method to distill multiple visual experts into a single vision encoder via LoRA adapters, by replacing its fixed shared adapter set with teacher-specific LoRA adapters and a general-knowledge adapter set, each governed by a sparse MoE-style router. The routing mechanism draws on the Mixture-of-LoRA-Experts design, while the hierarchical distillation (coarse-grained consensus + fine-grained per-teacher alignment with token importance scoring) is a new contribution not present in MoVE-KD or in multi-encoder VLMs like Eagle, MoME, or Brave that retain all encoders at inference time. The paper positions itself against both the multi-encoder family (which is computationally expensive) and the single-teacher distillation literature, carving out the specific niche of multi-teacher-to-single-encoder distillation within a VLM training loop.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical motivation is strong: multi-expert VLMs deliver meaningful accuracy gains but are impractical for latency-sensitive or resource-constrained deployment, and HAWAII offers a principled way to capture those gains in a single encoder with only LoRA-rank overhead. However, the improvements over both LLaVA-1.5 and MoVE-KD are modest (roughly 1–2 points on most benchmarks), and the method's complexity (multiple adapter sets, two routers, two distillation losses, token scoring) raises questions about whether the added machinery is worth the small gains. Open questions include scaling to more than five teachers, generalizing beyond Vicuna-based LLMs, extending to video or other modalities, and whether the coarse-grained consensus step is truly necessary given the fine-grained per-teacher adapters already capture most of the signal.
