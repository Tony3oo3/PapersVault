## Problem

KV cache in LLMs grows linearly with sequence length, becoming the dominant memory bottleneck for long-context inference. While the KV cache exhibits low-rank structure in the hidden dimension, the widely adopted RoPE mechanism increases key variance and rank after rotation, so naive low-rank compression either degrades accuracy or forces expensive full-rank reconstruction before RoPE can be applied. The paper tackles how to compress KV cache in a latent space while sidestepping RoPE's interference without paying the cost of reconstructing every token.

## Input / Output

The method consumes pre-RoPE multi-head query, key, and value tensors along with an offline-calibrated low-rank projection matrix U_r ∈ R^{nd×r} (derived from C4 calibration data) and a quantised value cache. It produces a compressed single-head latent key cache of dimension r per token, a top-k set of selected token indices, and the final attended output vector. Notably, value tensors bypass low-rank projection (being near full-rank) and are instead stored with channel-wise group quantisation, while the most recent w tokens are kept at half the target compression ratio to preserve local context fidelity.

## Method

SALS operates in three pipelined stages: (1) multi-head pre-RoPE keys are projected into a shared single-head latent space via a joint low-rank matrix U_r obtained from eigenvalue decomposition of the calibration key covariance, which provably captures more energy than per-head projections (Lemma 1); (2) critical tokens are selected by computing cheap inner products between a truncated query (r* dimensions) and the cached latent keys, avoiding any full-rank QK^T multiplication; (3) only the top-k selected tokens are reconstructed from latent space, RoPE is applied to these reconstructed keys, and exact sparse attention is computed over the subset. A fused Triton kernel combines token selection, reconstruction, and RoPE rotation into a single memory pass, reducing data movement by 7.7×–14.3× versus FlashAttention. The entire pipeline is post-training (no retraining required), calibrated on 512 sequences of length 4096 from C4, and skips sparsification for layers 0, 1, and 31 where latent-space overlap drops below 50%.

## Results

On LongBench (16 English subsets), SALS-25% achieves an average score of 32.26 on LLaMA2-7B-Chat (vs. 32.65 baseline) while reducing memory access to 11% of baseline; SALS-12.5% reaches 31.97 at just 6% memory access. On the RULER-128k benchmark with LLaMA3.1-8B-Instruct, SALS-25% attains 80.81 average (vs. 81.60 baseline) at 8× sparsity. In terms of efficiency, SALS achieves 6.4× KV cache compression and 5.7× attention-operator speedup over FlashAttention-2 at 4K sequence length, and 1.4×/4.5× end-to-end throughput improvement over GPT-Fast at 4K/32K sequences respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against KV-cache compression baselines, SALS-25% (0.11 memory access) outperforms Palu-30% (0.13 memory access) by +1.35 avg on LLaMA2-7B-Chat LongBench (32.26 vs. 30.91) and +8.05 on Mistral-7B (42.79 vs. 34.74), while KIVI-4bit (0.31 memory access) achieves near-baseline accuracy but at nearly 3× the memory cost of SALS. Against token-sparse baselines on LongBench (Table 4), SALS-25% (32.26 avg, 0.11 memory) beats Double Sparse (31.64, 0.16), HShare (31.83, 0.14), and Loki (31.95, 0.19) in both accuracy and memory efficiency; SALS-12.5% (31.97, 0.06) further reduces memory to 6% while still outperforming all three. In attention-operator latency (Table 6), SALS-25% at bs=8/4k achieves 0.530 ms vs. FlashAttention-2's 1.630 ms (≈3.1× speedup) and HShare's 0.576 ms; SALS-12.5% reaches 0.439 ms (≈3.7× over FlashAttention-2). For end-to-end throughput (Table 7), SALS-12.5% delivers 4.5× speedup over GPT-Fast at 32K sequence length (89.47 vs. 19.8 tokens/s) and 1.4× at 4K (163.5 vs. 118 tokens/s). On RULER-128k, SALS-25% retains 80.81 avg (vs. 81.60 baseline) with only 0.79-point degradation, confirming scalability to longer contexts.

## Relation to Prior Work

SALS sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: low-rank KV cache compression (Palu, Loki, Eigen Attention) and post-training sparse attention (H2O, Quest, Double Sparse, HShare). It builds directly on Palu's multi-head joint low-rank projection but departs from it by introducing a sparse token-selection stage that avoids the full-reconstruction overhead Palu identified as a key bottleneck. The critical conceptual departure is the observation that RoPE inflates key variance and rank, motivating the use of pre-RoPE latent-space keys for token scoring—a design choice that neither Palu nor the sparse-attention literature had explicitly exploited.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

KV cache memory and bandwidth are the dominant bottlenecks for long-context LLM serving, and SALS demonstrates that combining low-rank compression with latent-space token selection can achieve 6.4× cache reduction and 4.5× end-to-end throughput gains at 32K context while preserving accuracy. However, the approach requires an offline calibration step (512 sequences from C4) and the speedup at short sequences (≤1K) is actually worse than FlashAttention-2, limiting its applicability to shorter contexts. Open questions include whether the pre-RoPE latent representation property holds for non-RoPE positional encodings, whether the calibration generalizes across domains or requires re-fitting, and whether the method scales to 70B+ models where the per-head dimension and number of heads change the compression dynamics.
