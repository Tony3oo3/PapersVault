## Problem

Automating the end-to-end translation of natural language optimization problem descriptions into executable, verified optimization programs remains challenging: existing LLM-based approaches rely on large, expensive models, struggle with semantic drift during information extraction, and lack robust verification mechanisms. No prior method combines efficient small-model deployment with structured multi-agent collaboration and domain-specific alignment to address scalability, extraction accuracy, and cross-domain generalization simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251171/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes a free-form natural language optimization problem description as input and produces a four-stage output: (1) a structured XML-formatted tuple of sets, parameters, decision variables, constraints, and objective function; (2) executable AMPL model code that encodes the mathematical formulation; (3) a candidate numerical solution with diagnostic metadata (solver status, dual values, objective value) obtained via Gurobi; and (4) a verified final solution after iterative Checker feedback. The AMPL intermediate format is the key structural constraint, as it must be syntactically valid and semantically correct to interface with the external solver.

## Method

MURKA decomposes the NL-to-optimization task into three specialized agents trained with distinct paradigms. The Extractor is aligned via an enhanced GRPO algorithm that replaces the standard reward model with a rule-based composite reward function integrating four orthogonal dimensions—XML format validation, constraint operator-variable checking, objective-function semantic analysis, and embedding-based variable/parameter similarity (using nomic-embed)—enabling dense, interpretable policy gradients without a learned reward model. The Solver is trained by knowledge distillation from DeepSeek-R1, which generates enriched (question, extracted-info, AMPL-model, solution) quadruples validated iteratively against Gurobi, transferring structural modeling expertise into a compact LLaMa3-8B student. The Checker closes the loop by feeding solver diagnostics back to the Solver for corrective regeneration until a feasible, verified solution is produced. This architecture achieves near-perfect execution rates (97–100% ER) and competitive solution accuracy on an 8B-parameter backbone, approaching the performance of models 80× larger.

## Results

On eight OR benchmarks (2,224 instances), MURKA built on LLaMa3-8B achieves a micro-average Solution Accuracy (SA) of 76.48% and macro-average SA of 68.61%, with a near-perfect Execution Rate (ER) of 97.0% (micro) and 97.8% (macro). Against the strongest directly-prompted baseline DeepSeek-R1-671B (80× larger), MURKA trails by only 1.67% in micro-average SA (76.48% vs. 78.15%) while surpassing it on Mamo Complex (55.6% vs. 51.2%) and matching it on ComplexOR (72.2%). Against multi-agent baselines, MURKA achieves 89.80% micro-average SA, outperforming OptiMUS (75.00%) by 14.8 points and Chain-of-Experts (57.76%) by 32 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against directly-prompted LLMs, MURKA (LLaMa3-8B) dramatically outperforms GPT-4 (49.34% micro SA) and Qwen3-32B (57.68%), and narrows the gap to DeepSeek-R1-671B to just 1.67% in micro-average SA despite being 80× smaller, while actually surpassing it on Mamo Complex (55.6% vs. 51.2%). Among domain-specific optimization models, MURKA beats OptMATH-Qwen2.5-32B (78.88% micro SA) by 3.3 points and ORLM-LLaMa3-8B (70.42%) by 11.8 points. Against multi-agent frameworks, MURKA achieves 89.80% micro SA, exceeding OptiMUS (75.00%) by 14.8 points, Chain-of-Experts (57.76%) by 32 points, and Reflexion (48.45%) by 41.4 points. In the general optimization comparison, MURKA leads LLMOPT on Execution Rate (97.0% vs. 92.7% micro ER) and on NLP4LP SA (87.6% vs. 83.8%), but slightly trails on ComplexOR SA (72.2% vs. 72.7%) and IndustryOR SA (38.0% vs. 44.0%); it overwhelmingly outperforms NL2OR on all metrics (e.g., 97.0% vs. 59.5% micro ER). The paper's headline claim of 5.9% absolute SA improvement and 5.1% ER gain is measured against the best prior alignment baselines (OptMATH/ORLM) on the domain-specific benchmark subset.

## Relation to Prior Work

MURKA adapts the GRPO algorithm from DeepSeekMath [Shao et al., 2024] by replacing its learned reward model with a rule-based composite reward function spanning four orthogonal dimensions (format, constraint, semantic, similarity), tailored to the structured nature of optimization information extraction. It extends the multi-agent OR modeling lineage (Chain-of-Experts, OptiMUS, Reflexion) by moving beyond prompt-engineered agent coordination to directly train agents via RL and knowledge distillation, transferring optimization expertise from a 671B teacher (DeepSeek-R1) into a compact 8B solver. The framework also builds on the synthetic data synthesis approaches of OptMATH and ORLM, but repurposes generated data as an RL environment rather than a supervised fine-tuning corpus.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in demonstrating that a 20× token-efficient, 8B-parameter pipeline can approach the performance of a 671B model on structured optimization modeling, making LLM-driven OR automation viable for latency-sensitive industrial deployment. However, the framework still trails on complex, domain-specific benchmarks (IndustryOR: 38.0% vs. 44.0% for LLMOPT; Specific: 37.4% vs. 45.2% for DeepSeek-R1), and its reward mechanism relies on predefined XML templates that may not generalize to novel problem structures. Open questions include whether dynamic reward weighting, domain-knowledge-augmented extraction, and lighter architectures can close the remaining gap on complex tasks without sacrificing the efficiency gains.
