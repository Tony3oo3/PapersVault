## Problem

Existing data-driven physics simulation methods are locked to fixed spatial grids and discrete time steps, making them unable to predict field values at arbitrary, unseen spatiotemporal locations. While recent approaches attempt spatio-temporal continuity, they either depend on traditional numerical solvers or fail to truly decouple from discretization, limiting their applicability to real-world scenarios where sensors are sparsely and irregularly deployed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252714/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes sparse sensor readings at a single initial time point, where observation locations are arbitrary (non-Cartesian) coordinates in a continuous 2D spatial domain, and produces predicted scalar field values at any query (time, space) pair. Notably, the number and positions of observation points can vary at inference time (tested at 25%, 50%, 75% subsampling), and the model must extrapolate both beyond the training time horizon and to intermediate points between discrete training steps, as well as to resolutions different from training.

## Method

CoPS operates in three stages: (1) a Multiplicative Filter Network with nonlinear Gabor filters encodes each observation together with its spatial coordinate, then a message-passing scheme maps these point features onto a customized uniform grid by connecting each point to its four enclosing grid-cell vertices; (2) a multi-scale Graph ODE (hierarchical graphs with jump-adjacency topology inspired by GraphCast) models continuous-time latent dynamics via a Runge-Kutta solver, while a Markov-based neural auto-correction module (conv encoder → 1×1 Conv + parallel GroupConv2D → transposed conv decoder) applies discrete single-step corrections at regular intervals to contain error accumulation; (3) a Gabor-initialized message-passing decoder projects latent grid states back to arbitrary query coordinates. A theoretical error bound (Theorem 3.1) shows that when the effective per-cycle amplification α_eff = κ·e^{L_F·Δt_corr} < 1, prediction error converges to a finite asymptotic floor, guaranteeing long-term stability of the hybrid continuous-discrete evolution.

## Results

CoPS achieves the lowest MSE across all four datasets and all sparsity levels (25%, 50%, 75%) in spatial querying. On Navier-Stokes at 25% observations, CoPS reaches 2.964E-03 (In-S) and 5.743E-03 (Ext-S), roughly 45% lower than the next-best ContiPDE (5.456E-03 / 9.523E-03) and 3.6× lower than DINO. For temporal extrapolation at 100% observations, CoPS achieves 1.132E-03 (In-t), 2.364E-03 (Ext-t), and 1.735E-03 (Con-t) on Navier-Stokes, outperforming ContiPDE by 57%, 50%, and 55% respectively. On Rayleigh-Bénard Convection at 100% observations, CoPS attains 6.522E-04 (In-t), nearly 2× better than ContiPDE (1.244E-03).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoPS consistently outperforms all three baselines (MAgNet, DINO, ContiPDE) across every dataset and evaluation setting. Against the closest competitor ContiPDE, CoPS achieves 45–57% lower MSE on Navier-Stokes spatial querying (e.g., 2.964E-03 vs 5.456E-03 at 25% In-S) and 50–57% lower MSE on temporal extrapolation at 100% observations (e.g., 1.132E-03 vs 2.655E-03 In-t). DINO, which relies on implicit neural representations, is the weakest baseline, typically 2–4× worse than CoPS (e.g., 1.074E-02 vs 2.964E-03 on Navier-Stokes 25% In-S). MAgNet, which uses nearest-neighbor interpolation, performs worst overall, with MSE values 10–20× higher than CoPS on most settings. The advantage of CoPS is most pronounced under extreme sparsity (25% observations) and in extrapolation settings (Ext-S, Ext-t), where the continuous ODE + auto-correction mechanism provides the largest gains over discrete-only baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

CoPS synthesizes three distinct research threads: Multiplicative Filter Networks (Fathony et al., ICLR 2020) for coordinate-aware spatial encoding via Gabor filters, Neural ODEs (Chen et al., NeurIPS 2018) for continuous-time dynamics, and GraphCast's (Lam et al., Science 2023) hierarchical multi-scale jump-adjacency graph topology. It departs from MAgNet's interpolation-based spatial generalization and DINO's implicit neural representations (which require hundreds of optimization steps per query) by using a single-step Gabor filter plus message passing onto customized grids. The key methodological departure is the hybrid inference strategy that alternates continuous ODE evolution with discrete Markov-based neural auto-correction, a combination not present in prior continuous PDE solvers like ContiPDE, which relies solely on recurrent GNN rollouts and attention-based querying.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in data-driven physics simulation: most existing methods are locked to fixed spatial grids and discrete time horizons, limiting their utility in sensor-sparse or irregularly sampled real-world scenarios (oceanography, meteorology, combustion). The theoretical error bound (Theorem 3.1) providing a convergence guarantee for the hybrid continuous-discrete scheme is a useful contribution, though it relies on a contraction assumption (κ < 1) that may not always hold for highly nonlinear systems. Open questions include scalability to 3D domains, whether the Markov correction assumption adequately captures long-range temporal dependencies in chaotic systems, and how the method performs when the customized grid resolution must adapt dynamically to local feature complexity.
