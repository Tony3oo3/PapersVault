## Problem

Traditional Demand-Driven Navigation (DDN) handles a single need per episode, but real-world tasks involve sequences of needs with personal preferences (e.g., 'organize the living space' could mean cleaning tools, decorations, or storage). No existing benchmark or method addresses long-horizon navigation where an agent must satisfy multiple sub-demands, each with an explicit task-preference constraint, while tracking progress and recovering from failures across extended trajectories.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252819/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The agent receives a natural-language long-horizon instruction (e.g., 'Organize the living space by arranging decorative items, set up a cozy entertainment corner with seating and media devices') decomposed into 3 subtasks, plus panoramic RGB (300×300) and depth images at 90° HFoV in AI2-THOR/ProcTHOR scenes. It outputs discrete primitive actions (MoveAhead, RotateRight, RotateLeft, LookUp, LookDown, Done) at 0.25 m / 30° granularity, a continuously updated 2D semantic map with object class, center, and bounding-box entries, per-subtask binary completion status, and A*-planned waypoint sequences. A subtask is marked complete when the agent's 2D Euclidean distance to a matching object falls within 1.5 m, and the episode terminates after 50 steps or when all subtasks succeed.

## Method

AWMSystem decomposes the instruction via BreakLLM into a subtask list, then alternates between a slow-tempo phase (LocateLLM selects the next target object from a RAM-Grounded-SAM-segmented 3D point cloud fused into a 2D semantic map via IoU-based matching and the Hungarian algorithm; an affordance map combining obstacle-avoidance and semantic-relevance values is computed and solved with A* to produce waypoints) and a fast-tempo phase (a pretrained DDN policy executes low-level actions from the current RGB frame). StatusMLLM, a multimodal LLM, is invoked only when the policy emits Done to verify subtask completion against the current image, updating the binary status vector. An Adaptive Error Corrector re-plans the affordance map and switches to finer waypoint sampling (n_block) near obstacles when collisions or boundary violations are detected, while a consecutive-failure counter (n_CFE ≥ 2) injects an auxiliary prompt into LocateLLM to prevent revisiting failed targets. The entire pipeline is zero-shot with respect to navigation training, relying on transfer from the pretrained DDN policy and off-the-shelf LLM/VLM backbones (Qwen2.5-VL-72B for reasoning, GPT-4o/DeepSeek-V3 for instruction generation).

## Results

On the TP-MDDN benchmark (200 long-horizon instructions, 3 subtasks each, 68 rooms in ProcTHOR), AWM-Nav achieves a Success Rate of 32.00%, Independent Success Rate of 62.89%, ISPL of 44.19, and STL of 20.11 steps — the best scores across all four metrics. This represents a 16 percentage-point improvement in SR over both DDN (16.00%) and InstructNav (16.00%), and a 18.67-point gain over MO-DDN (13.33%). In terms of efficiency, AWM-Nav completes each long-horizon instruction in 6.82 minutes on average, compared to 88.90 minutes for InstructNav, while DDN is fastest at 1.74 minutes but with substantially lower success.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AWM-Nav outperforms all three baselines on every reported metric. Against DDN [1] (end-to-end single-demand navigation), AWM-Nav doubles the Success Rate (32.00% vs. 16.00%) and improves ISR by 18.22 points (62.89% vs. 44.67%), reflecting the advantage of explicit multi-subtask decomposition and memory over a single-object policy. Against MO-DDN [5] (coarse-to-fine multi-object DDN), AWM-Nav leads by 18.67 points in SR (32.00% vs. 13.33%) and 23.11 points in ISR (62.89% vs. 39.78%), with the gap attributed to MO-DDN's lack of long-horizon planning and its reliance on attribute-based exploration that struggles with task-preferenced instructions. Against InstructNav [2] (zero-shot LLM-based navigation with affordance maps), AWM-Nav achieves the same SR (16.00% for InstructNav) but with a 16-point SR advantage (32.00% vs. 16.00%) and a 20.45-point ISR advantage (62.89% vs. 42.44%), while also reducing average execution time from 88.90 to 6.82 minutes — a 13× speedup — thanks to the Dual-Tempo action generator that avoids per-step LLM calls.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Demand-Driven Navigation (DDN) line of work, which handles a single natural-language need (e.g., 'I am tired' → find a bed), to a multi-sub-demand setting with explicit task preferences. It builds on MO-DDN's multi-object exploration and InstructNav's zero-shot LLM planning, but departs from both by introducing a modular LLM-based decision pipeline (BreakLLM/LocateLLM/StatusMLLM) combined with classical A* path planning on affordance maps, rather than relying on end-to-end policy learning or per-step LLM inference. The dual-tempo action generation draws on dual-system robotics architectures, and the MASMap spatial memory integrates RAM-Grounded-SAM segmentation with 2D semantic mapping, a combination not previously applied to demand-driven navigation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TP-MDDN provides the first benchmark for long-horizon navigation with multiple sub-demands and explicit preferences, filling a gap between single-demand DDN and fully open-ended instruction following. The dual-tempo design addresses a practical bottleneck—LLM inference cost—achieving a ~13× speedup over InstructNav while doubling success rate, making LLM-augmented navigation more deployable. However, the authors acknowledge involuntary mode switching between fast and slow tempo, overreliance on pre-trained LLMs for instruction interpretation, and evaluation limited to a single simulator (AI2-THOR/ProcTHOR) with 200 instructions, leaving open whether the approach generalizes to real-world or more diverse environments.
