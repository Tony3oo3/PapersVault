## Problem

Existing one-shot federated learning methods (e.g., DENSE, FedCVAE) are designed for image data and fail to capture fine-grained structural information in graphs, such as substructure variations and neighbor connectivity patterns. Moreover, conventional server-side knowledge distillation relies excessively on class-level semantic signals, making it unable to transfer the subtle structural knowledge that distinguishes nodes within the same class. This paper tackles the problem of generalizable one-shot federated graph learning: how to extract and transfer both semantic and fine-grained structural knowledge from distributed graph clients to a global model in a single communication round.

## Input / Output

Each client holds a graph with node feature vectors (F-dimensional) and an adjacency matrix; the Synergy Graph Synthesizer consumes Gaussian noise ε and node labels to generate synthetic feature matrices, from which adjacency is reconstructed via KNN on a sigmoid-activated similarity matrix. The Topological Codebook maps GNN node embeddings to discrete tokens (codebook size 2^6–2^8) representing prototypical local substructures. The global model outputs node class labels, and the entire pipeline requires exactly one client-to-server upload of synthesizer parameters, codebook, and local GNN weights.

## Method

On the client side, OASIS jointly trains a Synergy Graph Synthesizer (a conditional generator producing synthetic graphs, regularized by Fused Gromov-Wasserstein loss, KL distribution convergence, and cosine feature dispersion) and a VQ-VAE-inspired Topological Codebook that discretizes node embeddings into structural tokens via nearest-neighbor quantization, with consistency, commitment, and orthogonality losses. On the server side, two hierarchical Wasserstein-based distillation losses operate on proxy graphs generated with class-balanced label distributions: WESAD minimizes a discrete Wasserstein distance between teacher and student class probability distributions, where the transport cost matrix is derived from semantic affinity computed via the teacher's projection-head weights; WDSRD computes soft assignments of node representations to codebook tokens for both local and global models, then minimizes the closed-form 2-Wasserstein distance between the resulting Gaussian distributions of structural assignments. This two-level design transfers inter-class semantic relationships and intra-class topological structure simultaneously, replacing the KL-divergence-based distillation used in prior OFL methods.

## Results

OASIS achieves the best node classification accuracy across all eight benchmark datasets, with an average improvement of 15.81% over the FedAvg baseline. The most dramatic gains appear on large-scale graphs: 63.73% on Amazon-Photo (+40.61 over FedAvg) and 60.44% on Coauthor-CS (+37.94 over FedAvg). On smaller citation networks, OASIS reaches 49.59% on Cora, 45.69% on CiteSeer, and 60.99% on PubMed, surpassing the strongest one-shot FL baseline (FedCVAE) by 18.70, 10.93, and 8.98 percentage points respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OASIS outperforms all 12 baselines on every dataset. Among one-shot FL methods, FedCVAE is the strongest competitor (e.g., 30.89% on Cora, 52.01% on PubMed), yet OASIS surpasses it by 18.70 and 8.98 points respectively; FENS is second-best on Cora (31.43%) but trails OASIS by 18.16 points. Traditional FL methods (FedAvg, FedProx) and FGL methods (FedPub, FGSSL, FedTAD) that rely on multi-round communication or personalized optimization degrade severely under the one-shot constraint, with FedNova and FedRCL dropping below 20% on most datasets. The largest absolute gains are on large-scale, structurally diverse graphs: Amazon-Photo (+40.61 over FedAvg) and Coauthor-CS (+37.94 over FedAvg), where existing OFL methods collapse (DENSE: 4.93%, FedSD2C: 8.73% on Amazon-Photo). On the heterophilic Actor dataset, OASIS (25.42%) edges out FENS (24.30%) and FedAvg (24.40%), confirming robustness beyond homophilic citation networks.

## Relation to Prior Work

OASIS extends the one-shot federated learning paradigm (DENSE, FedCVAE) from image data to graph-structured data, where existing generative approaches fail to capture fine-grained topological structure. It adapts VQ-VAE principles (Wang et al., 2019) to construct a Topological Codebook that discretizes local substructure patterns, and replaces standard KL-divergence knowledge distillation with a hierarchical Wasserstein-based scheme (WESAD for inter-class semantics, WDSRD for intra-class structural relations). The Synergy Graph Synthesizer further departs from prior CVAE-style generators by jointly aligning features and topology via Fused Gromov-Wasserstein distance, rather than treating graph structure as a post-hoc KNN construction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic treatment of generalizable one-shot FGL, filling a clear gap where image-centric OFL methods collapse on graph data (e.g., DENSE drops to ~13% on Cora vs. 50% for OASIS). The hierarchical distillation design—separating semantic affinity from structural relation transfer—offers a reusable template for any federated setting where fine-grained structure matters. Limitations include evaluation restricted to node classification with a two-layer GCN backbone, the very restrictive single-round constraint that limits practical applicability, and the OOM issue on Ogbn-Arxiv for several baselines suggesting scalability concerns. Open questions include whether the Topological Codebook approach transfers to link prediction or graph-level tasks, and whether the Wasserstein distillation overhead is justified when multi-round communication is feasible.
