## Problem

Geometry-driven parametric CAD editing—updating a parametric construction sequence so its rendering matches a given target shape while preserving the original sequence's structure—remains largely unexplored. Prior editing methods either lack explicit shape guidance (unconditional or text-driven) or ignore the original sequence entirely (reverse engineering), and no dataset provides the (original sequence, target shape, updated sequence) triplets needed for direct supervised training.

## Input / Output

The method takes an original parametric construction sequence (a token sequence of CAD operations such as Line, Arc, Extrude with numeric parameters, following the DeepCAD format) and a target geometric shape represented as a voxelized truncated SDF with distance range [−0.2, 0.2]. It outputs an updated parametric sequence whose rendered shape reproduces the target, with the constraint that edits are confined to the segments responsible for the shape discrepancy, leaving the rest of the original sequence intact. The target shape is consumed only through the P2S model's shape encoder, so no explicit mesh or point-cloud alignment is required at inference time.

## Method

CADMorph is an iterative plan-generate-verify loop that orchestrates two pretrained, independently trained foundation models without requiring triplet supervision. The P2S model (a latent diffusion network following SDFusion's architecture) maps parametric sequences to 3D shape latents, while the MPP model (a LoRA-finetuned Llama-3 8B following FlexCAD) autoregressively infills masked segments of a parametric sequence. In the planning stage, cross-attention maps from the P2S model are compared between the current shape and the target shape to compute a scale-invariant relative score per segment; segments with the largest score change are replaced by [mask] tokens, localizing edits to the regions most responsible for the shape discrepancy. The generation stage calls the MPP model N times to produce diverse candidate infillings, and the verification stage projects each candidate and the target shape into the P2S latent space, selecting the candidate with minimum Euclidean distance while maintaining a cross-iteration priority queue to retain high-quality candidates from earlier rounds. This test-time-scaling design shifts computational effort from training (which avoids the data-scarcity bottleneck) to inference, where repeated plan-generate-verify iterations drive convergence toward the target shape.

## Results

On the CAD-Editor 2k test set, CADMorph achieves an IoU of 0.687 and mean Chamfer Distance of 0.009 between the rendered edited sequence and the target shape, substantially outperforming the strongest baseline CAD-Diffuser (IoU 0.548, mean CD 0.097). The method also attains the lowest invalid rate (3.1% vs. 5.7% for CAD-Diffuser and 25.1% for GPT-4o), the smallest edit distance to the original sequence (16.87), and the best human-evaluation rank (1.37). Across all seven reported metrics—IoU, mean/median CD, JSD, IR, Edit Dist., and Human Eval.—CADMorph is the top performer.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CADMorph outperforms all three baseline families on every reported metric. Against GPT-4o (and its reasoning variants o4-mini / o4-mini-high), CADMorph improves IoU from 0.247 to 0.687 and reduces the invalid-rendering rate from 25.1% to 3.1%, showing that general-purpose VLMs struggle to produce syntactically valid and shape-accurate CAD sequences. Against CAD-Diffuser (the strongest reverse-engineering baseline), CADMorph raises IoU by 0.139 (0.687 vs. 0.548), cuts mean CD by over 10× (0.009 vs. 0.097), and achieves a lower edit distance (16.87 vs. 17.29), indicating both better shape fidelity and better preservation of the original sequence structure. Against FlexCAD (the classical editing baseline), CADMorph improves IoU from 0.447 to 0.687 and reduces IR from 15.3% to 3.1%, while also achieving a smaller edit distance (16.87 vs. 22.29), confirming that visual guidance from the target shape is critical for accurate geometry-driven editing.

## Relation to Prior Work

CADMorph repurposes two pretrained CAD foundation models—a SDFusion-based latent diffusion model (P2S) and a FlexCAD-style LoRA-finetuned Llama-3 (MPP)—into a joint plan-generate-verify loop, rather than training a new end-to-end architecture. The planning stage borrows the cross-attention localization idea from Prompt-to-Prompt text-to-image editing and applies it to parametric sequence segments, a conceptual transfer not previously explored in CAD. The verification stage instantiates the test-time-scaling-with-verifiers paradigm (Cobbe et al., 2021) for the first time in a CAD editing context, using the P2S latent space as a shared geometric metric. Unlike prior unconditional editing (Xu et al.) or text-driven editing (Yuan et al.), the task here is conditioned on a target geometry, and unlike reverse-engineering pipelines (CAD-Diffuser), the original sequence structure is explicitly preserved.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first formalization of geometry-driven parametric CAD editing, a task that sits at the intersection of editing and reverse engineering but is distinct from both. The data-scarcity workaround—training P2S and MPP separately on pair and sequence data rather than requiring triplets—makes the approach practical where annotated triplets are unavailable. Key limitations include inference latency from multiple plan-generate-verify iterations and reliance on a single, relatively simple test set (CAD-Editor 2k). Open questions include whether the cross-attention localization generalizes to deeply nested or multi-body assemblies, and whether the framework can be distilled into a single end-to-end model once sufficient triplets are synthesized.
