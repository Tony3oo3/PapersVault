## Problem

Prior work established that a single (ε,δ)-differentially private set union algorithm can be simultaneously optimal for all datasets when each user contributes exactly one item (k=1). This paper asks what happens when users contribute up to k items: it shows that instance-optimal algorithms do not exist for k > 1, characterizes how the worst-case utility ratio degrades with k, and identifies conditions under which near-optimal performance is still achievable.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253008/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The input is a dataset of n user bags, each containing at most k items drawn from a potentially very large universe X, along with privacy parameters (ε, δ). The output must be a subset of the true union (the algorithm cannot report items absent from the input), and utility is measured as the expected cardinality of the reported set. The key evaluation metric is the utility ratio: expected output size divided by Π(D,ε,δ), a dataset-specific upper bound computed by summing a sigmoid-like reporting-probability function π(c;ε,δ) over all item counts c in the dataset.

## Method

The paper is a theoretical contribution with three pillars. First, impossibility results show that for any (ε,δ)-DP mechanism, the worst-case utility ratio over all k-bounded datasets is O((1/k)(1 + ln k/ε)) in general, and even on 'easy' datasets where Π(D) is a constant fraction of the union size, the ratio is at most Õ(n^{1/2} log(nk) / k^{1/4}); the proofs use tower datasets (nested user bags) and a reduction to private matrix marginal estimation via fingerprinting codes. Second, a bicriteria algorithm (Bicrit) achieves (1/k)·Π(D, ε/ln(1/δ), δ/(ln(1/δ)·e^ε)) under (ε,δ)-DP by sampling each item with probability 1/k and then applying the optimal k=1 reporting probability with relaxed parameters, with privacy proven via a Chernoff-bound argument on the number of sampled items. Third, a prediction-based mechanism M_H achieves the full optimal utility Π(D,ε,δ) whenever the actual item histogram matches a predicted histogram H, degrading gracefully with the ℓ∞ distance between the two; the mechanism outputs each item x with probability π(H(x) − d; ε, δ) where d is the histogram discrepancy, and privacy follows from the monotonicity of π and the fact that neighboring datasets shift d by at most 1.

## Results

The paper establishes that no (ε,δ)-DP set union mechanism can achieve a utility ratio better than O((1/k)(1 + ln k/ε)) in general (Theorem 2.2), and even on 'easy' datasets where Π(D) ≥ |union|/2, the best achievable ratio degrades as Õ(n^(1/2)·(ln(nk))^(1/4)/k^(1/4)) (Theorem 2.3). On the positive side, the Bicrit algorithm achieves (1/k)·Π(D, Ω(ε/ln(1/δ)), Ω(δ/(ln(1/δ)·e^ε))) for all datasets (Theorem 3.2), and when ε=0 or ε→∞, a mechanism achieves the full optimal utility ratio of 1. With a correct histogram prediction, the mechanism M_H achieves the optimal Π(D,ε,δ) regardless of k (Theorem 4.1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines or experimental comparisons. The 'comparisons' are between the impossibility upper bounds (Theorems 2.1–2.3) and the achievability lower bounds (Theorems 3.2, 3.5, 3.6, 4.1). The key gap is that the worst-case utility ratio is Θ(1/k) (matching upper and lower bounds up to logarithmic factors), meaning no single mechanism can be simultaneously optimal across all datasets when k > 1. The Bicrit algorithm's 1/k loss relative to the relaxed-parameter Π bound is essentially tight given the impossibility results. The prediction-based mechanism (Theorem 4.1) closes the gap entirely when the histogram is known, degrading gracefully with ℓ∞ distance from the prediction.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the differentially private set union problem from the single-contribution (k=1) setting, where Cohen et al. [2021] and Desfontaines et al. [2022] established instance-optimal mechanisms via the sigmoid reporting probability π, to the multi-contribution (k>1) setting. The key conceptual departure is showing that the instance-optimality achievable at k=1 fundamentally breaks down for k>1, necessitating a new normalized metric (the utility ratio) and a bicriteria approximation framework. The impossibility proof for Theorem 2.3 borrows the fingerprinting-code reduction technique from Steinke and Ullman [2015], while the prediction-based mechanism (Theorem 4.1) is a novel construction that leverages the recursive structure of π to achieve per-dataset optimality without requiring k=1.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The impossibility result—that no single mechanism can simultaneously achieve the optimal Π(D,ε,δ) bound across all datasets when k>1—is a fundamental limitation that reshapes how practitioners should think about private set union in realistic multi-item settings. The bicriteria algorithm (Bicrit) provides a practical fallback with a 1/k multiplicative loss, while the prediction-based mechanism offers a path to full optimality when historical data is available, making it directly applicable to iterative data-release pipelines. Open questions include whether the O(1/k) utility ratio lower bound is tight in all regimes, whether adaptive or multi-round mechanisms can close the gap, and how to privately select the contribution bound k when it is unknown a priori (addressed only briefly in Appendix A).
