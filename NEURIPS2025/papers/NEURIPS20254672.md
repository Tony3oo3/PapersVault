## Problem

RL-based fine-tuning of discrete diffusion models for biological sequence design faces a critical trade-off: gradient-free methods suffer from training instability in discrete spaces, while gradient-based methods like DRAKES require backpropagation through entire generation trajectories, incurring prohibitive memory and compute costs. This limits practical applicability when experimental data is scarce and design objectives involve multiple competing criteria.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254672/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pretrained unconditional discrete diffusion model (e.g., ProteinMPNN for proteins, masked diffusion LM for 200bp DNA) and a task-specific reward function (e.g., Enformer for enhancer activity, a stability predictor for ΔΔG) as inputs. At inference, the fine-tuned model generates fixed-length sequences without any additional conditional inputs or guidance steps, producing functionally optimized DNA enhancers or thermostable protein sequences directly from the learned policy.

## Method

GLID²E reformulates fine-tuning as PPO-based policy optimization where the diffusion sampling process is treated as an RL environment, computing lightweight gradients only on policy parameters rather than backpropagating through full trajectories (reducing complexity from O(N·B·T·L·d²) to O(N·B·T·L·d)). Two key mechanisms ensure stability: (1) a Clipped Likelihood Constraint that replaces expensive KL divergence with a likelihood-based penalty activated only for sequences below a calibrated threshold (μ − kσ), preserving distributional validity while permitting exploration of high-reward regions; and (2) potential-based Reward Shaping that evaluates partially-masked intermediate states via Monte Carlo completion under the current policy, providing informative signals throughout the diffusion trajectory rather than only at termination. The PPO objective uses a mixed reference policy interpolating between the pretrained and current policies, with a clipped surrogate to bound updates.

## Results

On DNA enhancer design, GLID²E achieves a median Pred-Activity of 7.29, outperforming DRAKES (5.61) by 30% and the best conditional sampling method CFG (5.04) by 44%, while also attaining the highest ATAC-Acc of 98.4%. On thermostable protein design, GLID²E reaches a median Pred-ΔΔG of 1.012 with 86.7% of sequences stabilizing (ΔΔG>0) and a 76.7% success rate (ΔΔG>0 and scRMSD<2), closely matching DRAKES (1.095, 78.6%) while requiring ~42% less training time per epoch (13.54s vs 23.28s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GLID²E decisively outperforms all four conditional sampling baselines (CG, SMC, TDS, CFG) on both DNA and protein tasks, with the largest gaps over CG (which collapses to 0% ATAC-Acc on DNA and 1.3% success rate on protein) and CFG (which degrades to Pred-ΔΔG of −1.186 on protein). Against the gradient-based fine-tuning method DRAKES, GLID²E wins clearly on DNA (Pred-Activity 7.29 vs 5.61, ATAC-Acc 98.4% vs 92.5%) but is slightly behind on protein stability (Pred-ΔΔG 1.012 vs 1.095, success rate 76.7% vs 78.6%), while matching or exceeding DRAKES on the percentage of stabilizing sequences (86.7% vs 86.4%). Against the MCTS-based PepTune, GLID²E achieves substantially higher Pred-ΔΔG (1.012 vs 0.432) and success rate (76.7% vs 70.1%) on protein design. Overall, GLID²E matches or exceeds the strongest baseline on every headline metric across both benchmarks while training ~42% faster than DRAKES.

## Relation to Prior Work

GLID²E builds directly on DRAKES [14], which established gradient-based fine-tuning of discrete diffusion models for biological sequence design via Gumbel-Softmax reparameterization and KL regularization. The key departure is replacing backpropagation through full generation trajectories with a PPO-based policy optimization that computes gradients only on policy parameters, treating the diffusion sampling process as environment interactions. Conceptually, the paper also draws on the broader RL fine-tuning literature for diffusion models (DPPO, DDPO) and potential-based reward shaping from classical RL, but applies these to the discrete-sequence biological design setting where prior work has focused on either training-free conditional sampling or expensive gradient-based methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical efficiency gain (42% faster training, lower memory footprint) makes reward-guided fine-tuning of discrete diffusion models accessible on a single GPU, lowering the barrier for function-driven biological sequence optimization. However, the method lacks the theoretical guarantees of DRAKES' KL-regularized formulation, and validation is limited to two tasks (DNA enhancers, thermostable proteins) with in silico evaluation only. Open questions include whether the approach scales to longer sequences, multi-objective optimization with competing criteria, and whether the discovered low-3-mer-correlation but high-activity DNA sequences are genuinely novel functional variants or artifacts of the reward model. Wet-lab validation remains essential to confirm real-world efficacy.
