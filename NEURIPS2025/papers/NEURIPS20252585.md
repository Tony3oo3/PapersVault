## Problem

Reasoning models that adaptively allocate test-time compute have advanced performance on verifiable domains like math and code, but it remains unclear how to extend this paradigm to safety, where the reward is non-binary and naive optimization leads to degenerate over-refusal. Prior work has applied SFT or DPO to curated reasoning traces for safety, but lacks a systematic study of how RL with long chain-of-thought should be designed—specifically regarding data mixing, reward shaping, and preventing shortcut behaviors like refusing every prompt.

## Input / Output

The model receives a single-turn text prompt (user query) and produces a structured output consisting of a chain-of-thought reasoning trace enclosed between  tokens, followed by a final answer that is either a refusal or a compliant response. The model adaptively allocates compute: reasoning length varies by prompt complexity, with clearly harmful prompts (e.g., hate speech) receiving shorter traces (~290 tokens) while ambiguous prompts (e.g., unqualified advice) receive substantially longer ones (~457 tokens). During evaluation, if an EOT token is present, only the post-EOT answer is scored; otherwise the full output is evaluated, making the protocol model-agnostic across reasoning and non-reasoning baselines.

## Method

TARS is a three-stage training recipe built on Qwen-2.5-1.5B/7B-Instruct. Stage I performs lightweight SFT (low learning rate 3×10⁻⁵, 3 epochs) on ~3600 harmful prompts paired with imperfect reasoning traces distilled from DeepSeek-R1, deliberately preserving generation diversity for downstream RL exploration. Stage II curates 2000 prompts mixing harmful (WildJailbreak, Aegis, Rainbow Teaming), harmless (UltraFeedback), and ambiguous (OR-Bench) examples at varying ratios λ∈{0.1,...,0.9}. Stage III runs GRPO with a split reward: a safety reward from the Moderation API for harmful prompts, a task-completion reward from GRM for harmless/ambiguous prompts, and a binary format reward for correct  delimiters. The critical design insight is that mixing harmless and ambiguous prompts with separate rewards prevents the reward hacking and reasoning degeneration that occurs when training solely on harmful prompts with a safety signal, while the lightweight SFT warmup (rather than heavy distillation) maintains the exploratory diversity that RL needs to discover adaptive, prompt-sensitive safety behaviors.

## Results

TARS (1.5B) achieves a superior safety-refusal trade-off compared to 7–8B baselines (Llama-RR, Mistral-RR, Llama-8B, SafeChain) on both XSTest and WildChat, using 6.6× fewer parameters. Under GCG attacks with properly formatted reasoning, TARS attains 92.79% Defense Success Rate (DSR) versus 82.73% for SFT; under PAIR, TARS reaches 94.45% DSR versus 72.97% for SFT. TARS also produces the largest internal representation margin (2.21) between harmful and ambiguous prompts compared to SFT (1.03), DPO (1.45), and RL without reasoning (0.88), indicating stronger learned separation. Reasoning length adapts to prompt complexity, ranging from ~290 tokens (clearly harmful) to ~457 tokens (ambiguous advice).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TARS outperforms all five baseline categories. Against circuit-breakers (Representation Re-routing), TARS wins on both XSTest and WildChat; Mistral-RR in particular exhibits high refusal due to learning to output gibberish as an overcautious defense. TARS at 1.5B surpasses Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct and Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct on the safety-refusal frontier despite having 6.6× fewer parameters. Among reasoning-based defenses, TARS beats SafeChain and RealSafe-R1 (SFT-based) as well as Deliberative Alignment (RL-based); DA's discrete rubric-based rewards produce shorter reasoning traces, explaining its inferior trade-off. In the controlled comparison against SFT, DPO, and RL without reasoning (all starting from the same π_SFT), TARS achieves the best Pareto frontier at every λ ratio, with both RL and reasoning contributing independently to the gain. Under GCG, SFT with reasoning is safer than RL without reasoning (82.73% vs. lower), but under PAIR the reverse holds; TARS (RL with reasoning) dominates both in every setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the test-time compute scaling paradigm established by DeepSeek-R1 and Kimi-1.5 to the safety domain, where prior work has predominantly relied on SFT over curated reasoning traces (RealSafe-R1, SafeChain, SARO) or guideline-based RL (Deliberative Alignment). It departs from input/output-layer defenses like circuit breakers (representation re-routing) by instead training the model to reason adaptively per prompt, and from standard RLHF by introducing a split reward architecture (safety reward for harmful prompts, task-completion reward for harmless prompts) to prevent degenerate refusal. The key conceptual departure is treating safety as a problem requiring adaptive, prompt-sensitive compute allocation rather than a fixed refusal policy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TARS provides a practical, open recipe that demonstrates a 1.5B model can match or exceed 7–8B safety defenses on the safety-refusal frontier, lowering the barrier to deploying safety-aware reasoning models. The analysis of how GCG and PAIR manifest differently in reasoning models (e.g., harmful content appearing in the reasoning slot while the post-EOT answer remains safe) reveals new attack surfaces that current defenses do not address. Open questions include whether the recipe scales to frontier models, how it handles multi-turn or more sophisticated reasoning-targeted attacks (e.g., H-CoT, AutoRAN), and whether the internal representation separation observed here translates to certified robustness guarantees.
