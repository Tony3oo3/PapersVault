## Problem

Existing operating room datasets provide either partial egocentric views or sparse exocentric multi-view context, but none combine synchronized multi-perspective egocentric and exocentric recordings with gaze tracking, hand pose, and dense frame-level scene graph annotations. This limits holistic OR perception, particularly in scenarios where multiple agents interact, occlusions are common, and fine-grained manipulations must be inferred from both global context and individual perspectives.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252188/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes seven synchronized modalities at 15 FPS: egocentric RGB (336×336), hand pose (16 tracked points per hand), gaze with depth (normalized 2D + depth), exocentric RGB-D (336×336), ultrasound screen video (336×336), stereo audio (48 kHz), and colored point clouds (2,500 points per timepoint). Output is a set of scene graph triplets (subject, predicate, object) drawn from 36 entity classes and 22 relation classes, generated autoregressively by the LLM; on average each frame contains 6.8±2.5 triplets.

## Method

The proposed EgoExOR model employs a dual-branch architecture that explicitly separates perspective-specific processing: the egocentric branch fuses first-person RGB, hand pose (via MLP), and gaze/gaze-depth (via MLP) into N=576 feature tokens using a 2-layer transformer, while the exocentric branch fuses third-person RGB-D, ultrasound video, audio (CLAP), and point clouds (Point Transformer V3) into the same number of tokens. The two sets of embeddings are concatenated and fed into a Vicuna-7B LLM (initialized from LLaVA-7B with CLIP ViT image encoder) that autoregressively generates scene graph triplets. Training uses LoRA on the LLM and adapts the last 12 layers of the image encoder for the OR domain, following the MM2SG protocol. This explicit ego-exo separation contrasts with the adapted ORacle and MM2SG baselines, which process all RGB inputs in a single shared encoder without dedicated fusion for perspective-specific features or access to hand/gaze signals.

## Results

On the EgoExOR benchmark, the proposed dual-branch EgoExOR model achieves a macro F1 of 0.72 overall (UI: 0.79, MISS: 0.68), outperforming the adapted ORacle baseline (0.63 overall) and MM2SG baseline (0.67 overall). The gains are most pronounced on the Ultrasound-Guided Injection procedure, where the EgoExOR model reaches 0.79 macro F1 versus 0.72 for MM2SG and 0.65 for ORacle. All models use LLaVA-7B with Vicuna-7B as the LLM and CLIP ViT as the image encoder, fine-tuned with LoRA over 4 epochs on a single NVIDIA A40 GPU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The EgoExOR model outperforms both baselines across all three evaluation splits. Against ORacle (which uses only multi-view RGB images), the EgoExOR model gains +0.09 overall macro F1 (0.72 vs. 0.63), with the largest improvement on the UI procedure (+0.14, 0.79 vs. 0.65). Against MM2SG (which additionally leverages ultrasound, audio, and point clouds but processes all modalities in a single unified stream), the EgoExOR model gains +0.05 overall (0.72 vs. 0.67), driven primarily by its explicit dual-branch architecture that separately encodes egocentric signals (gaze, hand pose) before fusion. The MISS procedure shows a smaller margin (+0.02 over both baselines), suggesting that the microscope-guided discectomy task is inherently harder for scene graph generation. The authors note that all models still struggle with low-frequency predicates such as 'cutting' and 'positioning', indicating room for further improvement.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the OR scene graph generation lineage established by 4D-OR, MM-OR, and ORacle, which relied exclusively on exocentric (ceiling-mounted) RGB-D views, by introducing the first dataset and benchmark that fuses multi-view egocentric recordings (from Project Aria glasses) with multi-view exocentric RGB-D cameras. Methodologically, it builds on ORacle's visual-encoder-plus-LLM triplet generation paradigm and MM2SG's multi-modal fusion (point clouds, ultrasound, audio), but departs from both by introducing a dual-branch architecture that explicitly separates and then fuses perspective-specific features, and by incorporating gaze and hand-pose signals that no prior OR dataset provided. The dataset also bridges the separate egocentric surgical line (EgoSurgery) and the exocentric OR line (MVOR, 4D-OR, MM-OR), which had previously developed in parallel without cross-perspective synchronization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

EgoExOR fills a critical gap in surgical data science by providing the first synchronized ego-exo multimodal OR resource with dense frame-level scene graph annotations (568K triplets across 84,553 frames), enabling research on perspective-aware surgical perception that was previously infeasible. The simulated setting and single-OR configuration limit generalizability to real clinical environments and diverse surgical specialties, and the 94-minute duration, while substantial for OR data, is modest compared to general egocentric benchmarks like Ego4D. Open questions include whether the dual-branch ego-exo separation generalizes to real-time deployment, how models trained on simulated procedures transfer to live surgery, and whether the gaze and hand-pose signals provide complementary information beyond what high-resolution RGB alone can capture at scale.
