## Problem

Existing agentic memory systems for LLM-based reading comprehension lack a coherent design principle: unstructured tabular memories (MemGPT, ReadAgent) fail to capture information associations, while structured approaches (RAPTOR, GraphRAG, MemTree) are confined to offline or strictly sequential settings and none simultaneously embodies structurality, flexibility, and dynamicity. The paper asks what traits an effective memory module should possess and provides a principled answer grounded in Piaget's Constructivist Theory.

## Input / Output

CAM ingests long-form documents (ranging from ~10K meeting transcripts to 200K+ token novels) split into 256- or 512-token chunks, either all at once (offline) or in variable-size batches (online). It produces a multi-level hierarchical memory graph—raw text chunks at the base layer connected by semantic and positional edges, with progressively abstract LLM-generated summaries at higher layers—and, at inference time, a contextual response (answer, summary, or verification judgment) to a user query. The memory structure supports batch-level online updates without full reconstruction, with sublinear time growth as batch size increases.

## Method

CAM's memory development uses a three-step incremental overlapping clustering algorithm: (1) Foundational Network Expansion computes a composite similarity (cosine embedding similarity + Gaussian positional proximity) to add edges between new chunks and existing nodes; (2) Ego-Centric Disentanglement replicates each node into as many copies as its ego-network has connected components, converting the overlapping structure into a non-overlapping replica graph so that simple non-overlapping clustering can still assign a node to multiple higher-level abstractions; (3) Online Clustering Updates applies incremental label propagation on the affected subgraph of the replica network, then uses LLM summarization to update only the modified cluster abstractions, recursively triggering the same procedure at higher levels. For retrieval, CAM employs a Prune-and-Grow associative strategy: it first performs fast global semantic localization (top-s nodes by cosine similarity), then iteratively expands the activation set via LLM-guided selection among same-layer neighbors and lower-layer children until convergence, combining global matching with local structural exploration.

## Results

CAM achieves the best performance across all six long-text reading comprehension benchmarks, delivering an average gain of 3.0% over the strongest baselines (RAPTOR and GraphRAG). On NovelQA (single-document QA), CAM reaches 52.3% ACC-L vs. 47.8% for RAPTOR; on QMSum (summarization), it scores 57.6% ACC-L vs. 53.9% for GraphRAG; and on FABLES (claim verification), it attains 91.5% F1P / 52.5% F1N vs. 87.2% / 48.0% for GraphRAG. In multi-document settings, CAM leads on MH-RAG (72.8 EM / 77.5 F1), ODSum-Story (54.6% ACC-L), and ODSum-Meeting (50.7% ACC-L). Under the online batch setting, CAM integrates new text chunks 4× faster than offline methods while maintaining stable inference accuracy across batch sizes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CAM outperforms all six baselines on every benchmark and metric. Against the strongest structured baselines, CAM beats RAPTOR by +4.5% ACC-L on NovelQA, +3.4 EM on MH-RAG, and +4.4% ACC-L on ODSum-Story, while surpassing GraphRAG by +3.7% ACC-L on QMSum, +4.3 F1P on FABLES, and +4.9% ACC-L on ODSum-Meeting. The gap over MemTree (the only other online method) is particularly pronounced on summarization tasks (e.g., +9.3% ACC-L on QMSum), which the authors attribute to MemTree's lack of assimilation flexibility (strict single-parent containment vs. CAM's overlapping affiliations). Non-structured baselines (MemGPT, ReadAgent) trail by 10–15% on ACC-L, confirming the importance of hierarchical memory structure. On factual QA (Appendix G, HotpotQA/MuSiQue/2Wiki), CAM also leads RAPTOR and HippoRAG, with the fine-grained knowledge modeling variant (CAM w/ FM) further closing the gap on entity-centric 2Wiki (65.8 EM vs. HippoRAG's 63.8). The key differentiator is that CAM is the only method combining structurality, flexibility, and dynamicity, enabling batch-level online integration 4× faster than offline reconstruction without sacrificing accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of structured memory approaches for LLM reading agents (RAPTOR, GraphRAG, HippoRAG, MemTree) and cognitive science, grounding its design in Piaget's Constructivist Theory to derive three explicit memory traits: structured schemata, flexible assimilation, and dynamic accommodation. Algorithmically, it adapts the ego-splitting framework (Epasto et al., KDD 2017) into an ego-centric disentanglement step that converts overlapping cluster structures into a non-overlapping replica network, enabling simple label propagation to handle flexible assimilation. The key departure from prior work is that no existing method simultaneously satisfies all three constructivist traits: offline methods (RAPTOR, GraphRAG) lack dynamicity, while the online MemTree lacks both flexibility and batch-level efficiency. CAM's incremental overlapping clustering with local-first updates is the first to unify structurality, flexibility, and dynamicity in a single algorithmic pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The constructivist design blueprint offers a principled, theory-grounded alternative to the heuristic design choices that have characterized prior agentic memory systems, potentially guiding future memory architectures beyond reading comprehension into planning, multi-modal, and long-sequence generation tasks. The batch-level online capability with sublinear time scaling (4× faster than offline reconstruction) addresses a practical gap for real-world streaming scenarios like serialized content or real-time news tracking. However, open challenges remain: hallucination propagation through hierarchical summarization, handling contradictory information sources, and the lack of learnable/adaptive memory update policies (current hyperparameters are manually tuned). The paper also notes that fine-grained entity-relation modeling, while effective for factual QA, struggles with narrative-centric texts, suggesting the constructivist principle may need task-specific instantiation.
