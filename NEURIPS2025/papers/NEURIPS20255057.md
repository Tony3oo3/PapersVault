## Problem

Existing video anomaly detection methods and datasets are designed for fixed ground-level surveillance views, making them poorly suited to UAV footage where anomalies are small, spatially sparse, and subject to rapid viewpoint shifts, scale changes, and complex backgrounds. No large-scale aerial anomaly dataset provides fine-grained spatial localization labels combined with structured causal reasoning annotations, and no reasoning framework addresses the dual challenge of localizing ('Where') and explaining ('Why') anomalies under dynamic aerial perspectives.

## Input / Output

The model consumes 4-frame clips from 4K (3840×2160) aerial videos, downsampled to 896×448, in both RGB and infrared modalities to handle day/night and weather variations. Outputs are structured natural-language sequences with special tokens marking five reasoning stages (Trigger, Diagnose, Reasoning, Reflection, Seeking), a bounding box for the anomalous region, and a separate 'seeking' bounding box that designates a broader region of interest for further high-resolution inspection—mimicking how a UAV would adjust its viewpoint to gather more detail in ambiguous or occluded scenes.

## Method

A2Seek-R1 is a two-stage reinforcement fine-tuning framework built on Qwen2.5-VL-3B-Instruct with LoRA. Stage 1 performs supervised fine-tuning using Graph-of-Thought (GoT) annotations that structure reasoning into explicit semantic stages (visual trigger, anomaly diagnosis, causal explanation, confidence reflection, and seeking action), with a binary mask enabling mixed supervision across label-only, reasoning-only, and full-annotation samples. Stage 2 applies Aerial Group Relative Policy Optimization (A-GRPO), an extension of GRPO that samples K candidate outputs per input and updates the policy toward the highest-reward candidate using a group-mean baseline. The reward function combines format, accuracy, localization (IoU-based), seeking (alignment with human-annotated candidate regions), and length (encouraging concise answers for simple scenes while allocating more tokens to complex ones) components, with KL regularization against a reference policy to prevent distributional drift.

## Results

On the A2Seek benchmark, A2Seek-R1 achieves an average category-level AP of 53.14% and mIoU of 26.03%, outperforming the strongest aerial baseline HSTforU (47.66% AP) by 5.48% and the zero-shot Qwen2.5-VL baseline (34.03% AP) by 19.11%. In localization, A2Seek-R1 improves mIoU by 22.53% over zero-shot (3.50%) and by 5.22% over GoT-SFT (20.81%). The method also achieves the best language-semantic scores (BLEU 0.4564, CIDEr 1.2989) and shows continued OOD generalization gains during RL training (reaching 48.98% at 400 steps vs. GoT-SFT's degradation from 44.41% to 41.84%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

A2Seek-R1 outperforms all 8 external baselines on average AP_c. The closest competitor is HSTforU [28] (47.66%), a hierarchical spatio-temporal transformer for aerial/ground videos, which A2Seek-R1 surpasses by 5.48%; ANDT [26] (40.42%) and InternVL-3.0 [14] (41.34%) trail by 12.72% and 11.80% respectively. Among LLM-based anomaly methods, Holmes-VAU [74] (19.97%), AnomalyRuler [69] (17.10%), LAVAD [72] (16.41%), and LLaVA-Video [37] (15.10%) all fall 33–38 points below A2Seek-R1, highlighting the gap between generic VLMs and a purpose-built aerial reasoning framework. The traditional spatio-temporal method SSRL [32] (12.00%) is the weakest baseline. Internally, A2Seek-R1 improves over its own GoT-SFT stage by 6.72% AP and 5.22% mIoU, confirming that the A-GRPO reinforcement stage (with seeking and localization rewards) adds substantial value beyond structured supervised fine-tuning alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the video anomaly detection (VAD) lineage from fixed-view ground-based surveillance (CUHK Avenue, ShanghaiTech, UCF-Crime) to dynamic aerial perspectives, building on earlier drone-view datasets (Minidrone, AU-AIR-Anomaly, UIT-ADrone) that provided only frame-level or coarse labels. Methodologically, it departs from reconstruction- and prediction-based VAD by adopting a multimodal LLM backbone (Qwen2.5-VL) and adapting R1-style reinforcement fine-tuning (GRPO) with task-specific rewards, following the recent trend of LLM-driven anomaly understanding (LAVAD, Holmes-VAU, VAD-R1). The key conceptual departure is the introduction of a 'seeking' mechanism that simulates UAV flight behavior—directing the model to identify regions warranting further high-resolution inspection rather than merely localizing a confirmed anomaly—which has no direct analogue in prior VAD or MLLM literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

A2Seek is the first large-scale aerial anomaly benchmark (2.4M+ 4K frames, RGB+IR, 27 sub-scenes) with fine-grained spatiotemporal bounding boxes and structured natural-language reasoning annotations, filling a critical gap for evaluating models on the 'where' and 'why' of anomalies under dynamic drone viewpoints. The A2Seek-R1 framework demonstrates that combining GoT-guided SFT with tailored A-GRPO rewards (localization, seeking, length) yields meaningful gains in both detection accuracy and spatial grounding over SFT-only baselines, with encouraging OOD generalization. However, the framework's focus remains on spatial-level perception of short clips; long-duration temporal anomaly modeling, inference efficiency (0.33 samples/s), and deployment beyond campus-scale scenes remain open challenges that limit immediate real-world applicability.
