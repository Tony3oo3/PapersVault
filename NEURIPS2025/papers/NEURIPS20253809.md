## Problem

Existing part segmentation methods (both 2D appearance-based and 3D geometry-based) produce semantic regions that are temporally unstable, view-inconsistent, and misaligned with physical articulation. Learning-based auto-rigging methods operate on static 3D meshes with limited training data and fail to generalize to novel categories or rare poses. No prior work explicitly tackles kinematic part segmentation—identifying locally rigid, motion-consistent regions that move together over time—from video inputs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253809/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

SP4D accepts a monocular RGB video (or a single still image) and jointly generates 24-view, 24-frame RGB videos and corresponding kinematic part segmentation videos at 576×576 resolution. The part output is label-free: rather than predefined semantic categories, it captures locally rigid, articulated motion-consistent regions. The generated 2D part maps can be lifted to 3D via a lightweight pipeline (SAM-based cleaning, HDBSCAN clustering, harmonic field estimation) to produce riggable meshes with continuous skinning weights, requiring only minimal manual adjustment of skeletal connectivity.

## Method

SP4D extends SV4D 2.0 with a dual-branch UNet where each branch processes half the latent channels: one generates multi-view RGB frames, the other produces part segmentation maps. A key architectural insight is spatial color encoding—part masks are mapped to continuous RGB-like images by using the normalized 3D center coordinates of each part as its color code, enabling both branches to share the same VAE encoder/decoder and allowing discrete part recovery via simple post-processing (SAM + HDBSCAN). A Bidirectional Diffusion Fusion (BiDiFuse) module, inserted at every encoder/decoder block, performs lightweight cross-branch feature exchange (two 1×1 convolutions with ReLU) to enforce appearance-structure alignment. An InfoNCE-style contrastive part consistency loss aligns latent features of the same physical part across views and frames, addressing the temporal inconsistency inherent in the color-encoding scheme. Training follows a two-stage strategy: the RGB branch is first trained on ObjaverseDy with BiDiFuse bypassed, then the full model is fine-tuned on the newly curated KinematicParts20K dataset (20K+ rigged objects from Objaverse XL with multi-view RGB and per-bone part labels) for 40K iterations on 32 H100 GPUs.

## Results

On the KinematicParts20K validation set, SP4D (Full) achieves mIoU 0.68, ARI 0.60, F1 0.70, and mAcc 0.74 in the multi-view setting, and mIoU 0.70, ARI 0.63, F1 0.72, and mAcc 0.77 in the multi-frame setting. In the user study, SP4D scores 4.26/5 on average (clarity 4.42, consistency 4.09, rigging suitability 4.26) versus 1.96 for SAM2 and 1.85 for DeepViT. On the 3D segmentation and auto-rigging evaluations (supplementary), SP4D reaches mIoU 0.64 and rigging precision 72.7, substantially outperforming all 3D baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SP4D decisively outperforms all six baselines across every metric. Against 2D part segmentation methods, SP4D achieves mIoU 0.68 vs. SAM2's 0.15 and DeepViT's 0.17 (multi-view), a >4× improvement; even the oracle-guided SAM2* (ground-truth point prompts) only reaches 0.22 mIoU. Against 3D segmentation methods (supplementary Table 3), SP4D scores mIoU 0.64 and ARI 0.58 versus Segment Any Mesh (0.15/0.06) and SAMPart3D (0.13/0.05), confirming that appearance-driven 2D cues are fundamentally insufficient for kinematic decomposition. On auto-rigging (Table 4), SP4D attains 72.7 precision on KinematicParts20K-test, beating Magic Articulate (63.7) and UniRig (64.3), and achieves a 4.1/5 user-study plausibility score on generated objects versus 2.7 and 2.3 respectively. The user study (Table 2) further confirms the gap: SP4D averages 4.26/5 while SAM2 and DeepViT score below 2.0 on all three criteria (clarity, consistency, rigging suitability).

## Relation to Prior Work

SP4D extends the SV4D 2.0 multi-view video diffusion framework by adding a parallel part-segmentation branch connected via a novel BiDiFuse cross-branch fusion module (inspired by Vainer et al.'s collaborative control for PBR generation). It deliberately departs from both semantic part segmentation (SAM2, DeepViT, SAMesh, SAMPart3D), which relies on appearance cues, and learning-based auto-rigging (RigNet, MagicArticulate, UniRig), which operates on static 3D meshes with explicit skeletal supervision. The spatial color encoding trick—mapping discrete part IDs to deterministic RGB colors derived from 3D part centroids—enables the part branch to share the RGB branch's VAE, a design choice not seen in prior multi-task diffusion work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SP4D closes a practical gap in the 4D generation pipeline: generated objects lack kinematic structure needed for animation, and prior methods either produce semantic (not kinematic) parts or require static 3D inputs with rigging annotations. The KinematicParts20K dataset (20K+ rigged objects with multi-view part videos) is a significant resource that was previously unavailable. Key limitations include the single-object assumption, restricted camera parameterization (azimuth/elevation only), and no released code; open questions include extending to multi-object scenes, full 6-DoF cameras, and whether lightweight 3D structural priors could further improve generalization to rare articulated poses.
