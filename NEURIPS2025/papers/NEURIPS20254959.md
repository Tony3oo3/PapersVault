## Problem

Existing training-free video anomaly detection methods rely on fixed-length sliding temporal windows that cannot adapt to anomalies of varying durations, causing poor event localization, semantic discontinuities, and amplified VLM hallucinations. Additionally, these approaches lack the multi-granularity reasoning needed to detect both transient anomalies (e.g., explosions) and complex extended events (e.g., burglaries) within a unified framework.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254959/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an untrimmed video sequence of T frames as input, with the VLM processing up to 64 frames per tree node and the GEBD model operating on 10-second overlapping windows. It produces frame-level anomaly scores (continuous values in [0,1]) along with anomaly event boundaries, plus natural-language explanations generated by the VLM for each detected segment. The hierarchical tree structure means scores are computed at both coarse (event-level) and fine (sub-event) granularities before fusion.

## Method

VADTree constructs a Hierarchical Granularity-aware Tree (HGTree) by running a pre-trained EfficientGEBD model over overlapping 10-second windows to extract boundary confidence scores, then recursively splitting the video at high-confidence peaks via depth-first traversal until a threshold γ_min is reached. K-means clustering on boundary confidences stratifies the tree into coarse (high-confidence) and fine (low-confidence) clusters, with RemoveDup and Complete operators ensuring full temporal coverage. For anomaly scoring, an LLM first generates multidimensional priors (scene, object, action) that are injected into a VLM (LLaVA-NeXT-Video-7B) to produce prior-guided video descriptions, which a second LLM (DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-14B) scores on a discrete 0–1 scale. Intra-cluster refinement ensembles scores from top-K semantically similar nodes (via ImageBind cosine similarity with softmax weighting) to suppress local false positives, and inter-cluster correlation fuses coarse and fine scores using a cohesion-driven mechanism where the variance of child-node scores dynamically adjusts the parent-child weighting.

## Results

VADTree achieves 84.74% AUC on UCF-Crime, 90.44% AUC (67.82% AP) on XD-Violence, and 89.32% AUC (71.41% AP) on MSAD, all in a fully training-free setting. On UCF-Crime, it outperforms the strongest training-free baseline LAVAD by 4.5% AUC and the event-aware EventVAD by 2.7%. On MSAD, VADTree surpasses the best weakly-supervised method π-VAD (88.68% AUC) by 0.64% AUC, demonstrating that no domain-specific training is required to match supervised performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VADTree (84.74% AUC) decisively outperforms all training-free baselines on UCF-Crime: LAVAD (80.28%), SUVAD (83.90%), MCANet (82.47%), and EventVAD (82.03%), with margins of 4.5%, 0.84%, 2.27%, and 2.71% respectively. On XD-Violence, VADTree achieves 90.44% AUC, exceeding LAVAD by 5.08% and EventVAD by 2.93%, while also surpassing all one-class (max 61.56%) and unsupervised (max 68.33%) methods. On MSAD, VADTree (89.32% AUC, 71.41% AP) edges out the best weakly-supervised competitor π-VAD (88.68% AUC, 71.26% AP) on overall AUC and AP, though π-VAD retains a slight advantage on anomaly-specific metrics (AUC_a: 71.25 vs. 67.85). The method also outperforms fine-tuning-based explainable approaches Holmes-VAD (89.51%) and VADor (88.13%) on UCF-Crime without any parameter updates, and surpasses the verbalized-learning method VERA (86.55%) by 8.19% AUC. The consistent gains across all three datasets and supervision categories confirm that the hierarchical granularity-aware tree provides a more effective temporal representation than fixed-length sliding windows used by prior training-free methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

VADTree extends the training-free VAD paradigm established by LAVAD and VERA, which rely on fixed-length sliding temporal windows and frozen VLM/LLM pipelines for anomaly scoring. The key departure is replacing rigid window-based sampling with a hierarchical granularity-aware tree (HGTree) constructed from pre-trained Generic Event Boundary Detection (GEBD) models, enabling adaptive multi-granularity video segmentation that matches variable anomaly durations. The method also draws on event-centric video understanding work (VideoTree, HEMLLM, TRACE) for its tree-based representation, but repurposes this structure specifically for anomaly detection with intra-cluster refinement and inter-cluster score fusion mechanisms not present in prior event-segmentation literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free, explainable nature of VADTree makes it particularly relevant for real-world surveillance deployment where domain-specific training data is unavailable and human operators need interpretable anomaly justifications. The finding that a training-free method can match or exceed weakly-supervised baselines on MSAD (89.32 vs 88.68 AUC) suggests that pre-trained model knowledge may be sufficient for anomaly detection in diverse scenarios, potentially reducing the data annotation burden in the field. Open questions include scalability to very long videos (the GEBD sliding window and LLM inference cost grow with video length), robustness under significant domain shift beyond the three evaluated datasets, and whether the hierarchical tree structure generalizes to non-surveillance video domains. The reliance on multiple large pre-trained models (GEBD, VLM, LLM, ImageBind) also raises practical deployment concerns around inference latency and resource requirements.
