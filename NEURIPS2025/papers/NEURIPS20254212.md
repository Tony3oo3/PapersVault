## Problem

Despite the rapid adoption of LLMs in agentic workflows, no comprehensive benchmark exists to assess whether these models possess the specialized domain knowledge and reasoning abilities needed for critical industrial tasks such as predictive maintenance, sensor fault detection, and asset management. Existing QA datasets cover general, biomedical, and scientific domains but leave industrial applications—where accurate real-time decision-making over failure modes and sensor relationships is essential—largely unexplored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254212/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark presents multiple-choice questions (2–5 options) about 10 industrial assets in two directional formats: FM2Sensor (given a failure mode, identify relevant/irrelevant sensors) and Sensor2FM (given a sensor anomaly, identify relevant/irrelevant failure modes), each in selection or elimination phrasing. The 8,296 questions are generated entirely from ISO-standard mapping tables and ~10 expert-curated templates per task type—no LLM is used in generation. For the LLMFeatureSelector component, input is a problem statement plus sensor names and a target variable, and output is a ranked list of top-5 recommended features with reasoning. Evaluation outputs include accuracy, consistency (ACC@Consist), set size, coverage rate, and uncertainty-adjusted accuracy (UAcc).

## Method

The paper introduces a Perturbation-Uncertainty-Complexity (PUC) evaluation framework: (i) SimplePert reorders options and relabels them, ComplexPert additionally paraphrases questions via Llama-3.3-70B, and OptionsPert expands each question to 10 distractor options; (ii) conformal-prediction-based uncertainty quantification measures set size, coverage rate, and UAcc across 14 models; (iii) reasoning prompting strategies (CoT in Standard/Expert/Inductive variants and Plan@Solve) are tested on Llama models to isolate the effect of explicit reasoning. A ReAct agent with Wikipedia and arXiv search tools is deployed to test whether external retrieval compensates for internal knowledge gaps. The LLMFeatureSelector is implemented as a scikit-learn transformer that prompts an LLM in a single step to recommend relevant features given an asset, failure mode, and sensor list, then validates recommendations against empirical correlations on three real-world datasets. Key findings include a 5–20% accuracy drop under perturbation, a cap of ~12% accuracy with 10 options, and a 25+ point gap between domain experts and practitioners on a curated 80-question subset.

## Results

On the FailureSensorIQ SC-MCQA benchmark (2,667 single-correct-answer questions across 10 industrial assets), the top-10 models average 53.5% accuracy, with o1 leading at 60.40% and o3-mini at 58.46%. Under ComplexPert perturbation, o1's consistency accuracy drops to 44.17%, and under OptionsPert (10 distractor options), even o1 collapses to 5.74% accuracy. On the multi-correct MCQA (MC-MCQA) setting, exact match scores remain below 21% across all models, with llama-4-scout achieving the best 20.5%. Human experts (reliability engineers) achieve a maximum of 66.19% and mean of 60.20%, confirming the benchmark's difficulty.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among closed-source reasoning models, o1 (60.40%) outperforms o3-mini (58.46%) and gpt-4.1 (53.47%) on overall accuracy, but o3-mini shows the best perturbation robustness (53.28% ACC@Perturb) and consistency (47.47% ACC@Consist). Open-source models trail significantly: llama-4-maverick (55.83%) is the best open model, while smaller models like granite-3.0-8b (22.85%) and mixtral-8x7b (27.60%) approach random-guessing levels. CoT prompting provides substantial gains for mid-size models (llama-3.3-70b: 41.69% → 51.18%), but cannot close the gap to frontier models. A ReAct agent with Wikipedia/arXiv retrieval fails to improve over direct prompting, with llama-3.3-70b actually dropping from 41.69% to 37.57%, indicating the task requires internal reasoning rather than surface-level retrieval. Flan-T5-XL fine-tuned on HotpotQA achieves only 29% (down from 36% pre-fine-tuning), confirming that general QA knowledge does not transfer to industrial sensor-failure reasoning. On MC-MCQA, llama-4-scout achieves the best exact match (20.5%) with a set size of 1.94, while o3 and o4-mini reach 20.0–20.1% with set sizes around 2.40.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the PertEval framework [14] for knowledge-invariant perturbation analysis and the MMLU-Pro complexity paradigm [37] (increased distractor options) to the industrial domain, while adopting the TruthfulQA-style MCQA format [18] and ReAct agent architecture [46] for external knowledge retrieval. It departs from prior industrial LLM work [30], which focused on narrow tasks like work-order classification, by introducing the first comprehensive multi-asset benchmark for sensor-failure mode relationship reasoning grounded in ISO standards. The LLMFeatureSelector tool builds on LLM-based feature selection approaches [12, 10] but targets the specific industrial asset-failure-sensor triad.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals that even frontier reasoning models (o1, o3-mini) retain less than half of their industrial knowledge under perturbation, and that ReAct agents with Wikipedia/arXiv access consistently underperform direct prompting—challenging the assumption that RAG is a universal remedy for domain knowledge gaps. Human experts achieving only ~60% accuracy validates the benchmark's difficulty and suggests that industrial sensor-failure reasoning remains an open problem for both humans and LLMs. Key open questions include whether temporal sensor dynamics, better retrieval strategies, or domain-specific fine-tuning can close the gap, and whether the observed fragility generalizes beyond the 10 assets covered.
