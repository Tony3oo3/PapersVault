## Problem

Entropy-minimization-based test-time adaptation (TTA) suffers from performance degeneration during long-term continual adaptation, where the model converges to trivial low-entropy but incorrect predictions. Existing mitigations (teacher-student networks, model resetting, entropy filtering) address symptoms but not the root cause. This paper identifies entropy-deceptive (ED) samples—instances with highly confident yet incorrect predictions—as the underlying driver of degeneration, and reveals that their gradients are scattered while entropy-truthful (ET) sample gradients form a correlated low-dimensional structure.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a stream of unlabeled test images in mini-batches during inference, requiring no source data access or test labels. It operates on a pre-trained source model (ResNet-50, ResNeXt-29, or ViT-B/16) and updates only the affine parameters of normalization layers (BatchNorm or LayerNorm), which constitute less than 1% of total model parameters. For classification tasks it outputs class labels; for semantic segmentation (Cityscapes, CARLA) it outputs per-pixel class masks. The adaptation is fully online and unsupervised, with no external intervention such as domain labels, model resets, or warm-up phases.

## Method

LCoTTA exploits the observation that ET sample gradients are highly correlated and concentrate in a low-dimensional principal subspace, while ED sample gradients are scattered and weakly aligned with this subspace. During adaptation, the method maintains a sliding queue of the most recent k batch gradients, performs PCA via SVD to extract an r-dimensional principal subspace (r≪n), and projects each new gradient onto this subspace before applying the parameter update, thereby suppressing the ED gradient component. The subspace is tracked online (not fixed) to adapt to evolving test distributions, and entropy-based sample filtering (ESF) is applied beforehand to remove high-entropy ambiguous samples, making the remaining ED gradients more orthogonal to the tracked subspace. A theoretical stability analysis (Theorem 1) shows that subspace projection reduces the diffusion noise term by a factor of approximately r/n, substantially enlarging the admissible range of ED bias that still permits stable convergence.

## Results

On the headline long-term continual adaptation task (50 cycles, 37.5M samples on ImageNetC), LCoTTA maintains ~42–43% accuracy throughout, while Tent collapses below 10% within 10 cycles and CoTTA degrades steadily. Under the standard single-epoch TTA setting, LCoTTA achieves 40.7% mean accuracy with ResNet-50 on ImageNetC (tied with the best baseline TCA†) and 62.3% with ViT-B/16 (best among all compared methods). On CIFAR-100C, LCoTTA reaches 67.9% mean accuracy, outperforming all baselines including ETA (67.5%) and CoTTA (67.5%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On ImageNetC with ResNet-50 (single cycle), LCoTTA achieves 40.7% mean accuracy, tying TCA† (40.7%) and outperforming DeYO (40.0%), AEA† (39.5%), BeCoTTA† (39.1%), SAR (37.8%), ETA (37.7%), CoTTA (37.3%), AdaContrast (34.6%), PETAL (33.3%), and RoTTA (32.6%). With ViT-B/16, LCoTTA leads all baselines at 62.3%, beating ETA (61.7%), SAR (61.6%), Tent (60.7%), DeYO (59.4%), and CoTTA (58.0%). The most striking advantage appears in long-term continual adaptation over 50 cycles: LCoTTA sustains ~42–43% accuracy while Tent drops below 10% within 10 cycles, CoTTA degrades from ~37% to ~20%, and AdaContrast similarly collapses. On CIFAR-100C, LCoTTA (67.9%) edges out ETA (67.5%) and CoTTA (67.5%), and substantially outperforms Tent (40.1%) and ECoTTA (63.1%). On CARLA semantic segmentation, LCoTTA consistently surpasses both CoTTA and Tent across all four domain-shift scenarios (e.g., 52.56 vs. 50.35 mIoU in the dynamic setting).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the entropy-minimization lineage of test-time adaptation (TENT, CoTTA, SAR, RoTTA) and the continual TTA setting established by CoTTA and EcoTTA, but departs from the prevailing heuristic remedies (teacher-student networks, model resetting, weight regularization) by diagnosing the root cause of degeneration as entropy-deceptive (ED) samples. It draws on the broader literature on low-dimensional structure in neural network optimization (intrinsic dimension, gradient subspaces) but applies it in a novel way: rather than constraining weight trajectories, it exploits the correlated gradient structure of entropy-truthful (ET) samples to construct a principal subspace that naturally suppresses scattered ED gradients. The method is complementary to existing robustness strategies and operates on a tiny parameter subset (BN/LN affine parameters, <1% of total), making it orthogonal to and stackable with teacher-student or resetting approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first principled diagnosis of why entropy-minimization TTA degenerates in long-term continual adaptation (ED samples with confident-but-wrong predictions), filling a gap the authors explicitly note has been missing from the literature. The practical impact is significant: a lightweight PCA-based projection on normalization-layer gradients prevents collapse over 37.5M samples without any supervision, teacher network, or periodic resetting. Key limitations include the fixed subspace dimension r (adaptive selection is left as future work) and the local-only stability guarantee. Open questions include whether the ET/ED gradient structure insight generalizes beyond entropy minimization to other unsupervised objectives, and whether the approach extends to full-model adaptation rather than normalization layers alone.
