## Problem

Existing autoregressive visual generation methods are limited to single-modality conditioning (text or class labels), failing to accommodate the diverse control signals—segmentation masks, depth maps, reference images, history frames—required in real-world applications. Moreover, the sequential prediction nature of AR models introduces a risk of information leakage from condition tokens to content tokens, causing the model to exploit shortcut correlations rather than faithfully following instructions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251770/cropped_page1_idx4.webp)

## Input / Output

All visual inputs (segmentation masks, depth maps, reference images, history frames) and the target image/video are discretized through a single shared visual tokenizer (Cosmos-DV8×16×16), while text prompts are tokenized with Qwen2.5. The token sequence is structured as [text, condition, content] for spatial/image-conditioned tasks or [text, content] for pure text-conditioned tasks. Training proceeds at 512 resolution for the first two stages and 1024 for the final multi-task stage, with outputs decoded back to pixel space by the visual tokenizer's decoder.

## Method

OmniGen-AR uses a decoder-only Qwen2.5 transformer to autoregressively predict next tokens over interleaved text and visual token sequences, unifying six generation tasks (T2I, T2V, frame prediction, image editing, depth-to-image, seg-to-image) in a single model. The key architectural contribution is Disentangled Causal Attention (DCA), which modifies the standard lower-triangular causal mask so that content tokens can attend to preceding text tokens but are blocked from attending to condition tokens, while condition tokens retain their own internal causal structure. DCA is applied stochastically (10% probability) during training as a regularizer to discourage over-reliance on conditional context, but inference follows standard next-token prediction with classifier-free guidance (scale 6.0). Training follows a three-stage curriculum: single-image pretraining, image-video joint training, and multi-task fine-tuning on high-quality task-specific datasets, all optimized with AdamW on 64 A100 GPUs.

## Results

On GenEval (text-to-image), OmniGen-AR achieves 0.63 overall at 1.5B parameters (0.55 at 0.5B), outperforming all sub-1B AR and diffusion baselines and matching or exceeding larger unified models like Janus (0.61) and Emu3 (0.66†). On VBench (text-to-video), the 1.5B model reaches 80.02 total score, the first vanilla discrete-token autoregressive model to surpass 80, beating OpenSora V1.2 (79.76) and CogVideo (67.01) by 13 points. For frame prediction on Kinetics-600, OmniGen-AR achieves FVD 429 (zero-shot), substantially lower than VideoPoet-8B (687). On spatial-conditioned generation, it achieves MaskmIoU 35.28 and DepthRMSE 37.42, competitive with diffusion-based methods but trailing ControlAR (39.95/29.01) and OmniGen (40.06/31.71).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On GenEval, OmniGen-AR (1.5B) achieves 0.63 overall, beating SimAR-SFT (0.61), Janus (0.61), and Emu3 (0.66†, which uses prompt rewriting), while trailing Infinity (0.73†) and Show-o (0.68) that employ prompt rewriting or larger architectures. At 0.5B scale, it surpasses all sub-1B baselines including SDv2.1 (0.50), LlamaGen (0.32), and SimAR-SFT (0.53). On VBench, the 1.5B model (80.02) edges out OpenSora V1.2 (79.76) and CogVideoX-2B (80.91 is slightly higher), while the 0.5B model (74.72) beats CogVideo (67.01) by 11 points with 18× fewer parameters. For image editing on Emu-Edit, OmniGen-AR matches OmniGen (CT 0.23, CI 0.84 vs. 0.83) and is close to Emu-Edit (CI 0.86). On spatial-conditioned generation, it trails ControlAR (MaskmIoU 39.95, DepthRMSE 29.01) and OmniGen (40.06, 31.71) but clearly outperforms Uni-ControlNet (19.39, 40.65), GLIGEN (23.78, 38.83), and EditAR (22.62, 34.93). For frame prediction, it achieves FVD 429 zero-shot, well below VideoPoet-8B (687) but above the supervised OmniTok (33).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the autoregressive visual generation line (DALL-E, LlamaGen, SimpleAR, ControlAR) by unifying multiple conditioning modalities—text, spatial signals, and visual context—within a single AR transformer, a capability previously achieved only in diffusion-based frameworks like OmniGen and Uni-ControlNet. It departs from prior AR work (which handles single-modality conditions) and from EditAR (which is AR but limited to editing/low-level tasks) by adopting a shared visual tokenizer (Cosmos-DV8) for all visual inputs and introducing Disentangled Causal Attention (DCA), a training-time attention-mask regularizer that is conceptually distinct from classifier-free guidance. The architecture builds on the Qwen2.5 decoder-only transformer and the Cosmos image-video joint tokenizer, repurposing components from the LLM and video-generation communities for unified conditional generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

OmniGen-AR demonstrates that a single small AR model (0.5B–1.5B) can handle six diverse generation tasks competitively, suggesting that the AR paradigm may be a more natural fit for interleaved, multi-condition generation than diffusion. The DCA mechanism is a lightweight, drop-in training regularizer that practitioners can adopt to mitigate shortcut learning in any conditional AR setup. However, spatial-conditioned generation (segmentation/depth) still lags behind dedicated methods like ControlAR and OmniGen, and joint training degrades text-conditioned quality, indicating that the multi-task trade-off is not yet fully resolved. Open questions include whether scaling data and model size closes the spatial-generation gap, and whether chain-of-thought reasoning can improve instruction following for complex edits.
