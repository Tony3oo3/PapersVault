## Problem

Existing Indian speech corpora represent only standard dialects and rely on generic web content (Wikipedia, news), leaving non-scheduled languages like Bhojpuri, Chhattisgarhi, and Magahi without large-scale validated audio, and failing to capture the dialectal and domain-specific variation that characterizes real rural and working-class speech. This gap limits ASR performance for the 64% of India's population in rural areas and the 57.8% in agricultural households who speak regionally diverse varieties.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254928/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes 209,822 manually composed sentences (max 120 characters, numbers spelled out, only period/comma/question mark allowed) authored by native dialect speakers across 38+ districts, which are then read aloud via a crowdsourced mobile app by 18,000+ speakers (30–150 recordings per sentence for intra-dialectal acoustic diversity). The output is a stratified corpus of 10,416 hours clean, 2,288 hours semi-noisy, and 1,617 hours noisy audio with aligned transcriptions, dialect-aware ILSL phonetic lexicons, speaker metadata (pincode, gender, age group), and non-overlapping train/dev/test splits with no speaker leakage across splits.

## Method

The core contribution is a dialect-level data creation and validation pipeline that preserves linguistic integrity from text composition through audio recording. Text is composed (not scraped) by trained native speakers following strict guidelines, then passes through a nine-stage validation combining automated checks (pairwise WER duplicate removal) and manual checks (matra correction, homophone disambiguation via phonetic WER, acronym standardization), discarding ~3.6% of raw text. Audio is validated via semi-automated scoring that categorizes utterances into clean/semi-noisy/noisy slabs based on audio-text alignment confidence, and speaker identity is verified through a custom bucketization algorithm resolving 99.28% of intra-speaker inconsistencies. For benchmarking, the paper trains TDNN-HMM (Kaldi) and E-Branchformer (ESPnet) from scratch on RESPIN, and fine-tunes Whisper (Tiny/Base/Small) and SSL models (IndicWav2Vec, SPRING-Wav2Vec2, SPRING-Data2Vec-AQC) on the corpus, showing that dialect-aware fine-tuning of SSL models achieves the lowest average WER (15.40%) over both pretrained baselines and scratch-trained traditional systems.

## Results

On the RESPIN-S1.0 test sets, fine-tuned SPRING-Data2Vec-AQC achieves the best overall performance with an average CER of 3.58% and average WER of 15.40% across nine Indian languages. E-Branchformer trained from scratch on RESPIN attains an average CER of 4.52% and WER of 16.25%, substantially outperforming pretrained models without RESPIN supervision (e.g., SeamlessM4T-v2-Large: avg CER 22.23%, WER 51.81%). The clean-slab corpus totals 10,416.58 hours, with semi-noisy (2,288.06 h) and noisy (1,617.47 h) subsets providing additional robustness training data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Pretrained models fine-tuned only on external public data (SeamlessM4T-v2-Large, IndicW2V, SPRING-W2V2, SPRING-Data2Vec-AQC) show high error rates, particularly for dialectally diverse languages like Bhojpuri (CER 15–29%) and Chhattisgarhi (CER 21–33%), reflecting domain and dialect mismatch. Traditional models trained from scratch on RESPIN (TDNN-HMM, E-Branchformer) dramatically outperform all pretrained baselines, with E-Branchformer achieving avg WER 16.25% versus 38.83–51.81% for the best pretrained models. Fine-tuned Whisper models (Tiny/Small) improve over their pretrained counterparts but remain behind scratch-trained E-Branchformer, indicating limited adaptation to dialectal nuances. SSL models fine-tuned on RESPIN (SPRING-Data2Vec-AQC, SPRING-W2V2) achieve the best results overall, with SPRING-Data2Vec-AQC reaching avg WER 15.40%—a 2.5–3.4× improvement over the same model without RESPIN fine-tuning (38.83%). On public non-domain-specific test sets (CommonVoice, FLEURS, Gram-Vaani, etc.), pretrained models are slightly stronger, yet RESPIN-fine-tuned SSL models remain competitive, confirming the dataset's generalizability beyond its agriculture/finance domains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the speech corpus construction paradigm (exemplified by INDICVOICES, SPRING-INX, FLEURS, and CommonVoice) by making dialectal integrity a first-class design principle across the entire pipeline—from text composition to recording to validation—rather than treating dialect as an afterthought. It departs from prior Indic corpora that rely on generic web content (Wikipedia, news, radio) by composing domain-specific sentences (agriculture, finance) directly from native dialect speakers, and it is the first public resource providing validated data for non-scheduled languages Bhojpuri, Chhattisgarhi, and Magahi. The ASR benchmarking follows standard practice (TDNN-HMM, E-Branchformer, Whisper, wav2vec2-based SSL) but positions dialect-aware fine-tuning as the key differentiator over both pretrained multilingual models and scratch-trained traditional systems.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RESPIN-S1.0 fills a critical gap for India's 64% rural population and 57.8% agricultural households who remain digitally underserved by standard-dialect-only ASR systems. The corpus's focus on agriculture and finance domains, combined with 38+ dialects and 18,000+ speakers, makes it a practical foundation for voice-driven services in low-literacy, multilingual settings. Key limitations include the restriction to read speech (no spontaneous/conversational data), only two domains, and reliance on literate smartphone users, which may underrepresent the most marginalized communities. Future work on spontaneous speech, additional domains (healthcare, education, governance), and expanded language coverage will determine whether RESPIN can serve as a truly comprehensive benchmark for inclusive Indian speech technology.
