## Problem

Attention with bias, which adds a dense prior-knowledge matrix to query-key scores, is essential in vision, language, and scientific Transformers but creates a severe IO bottleneck that breaks the FlashAttention pipeline. Unlike sparse masks that enable computation reduction, bias matrices are continuous and dense, making existing sparsity-based speedup techniques inapplicable. Despite its ubiquity, no targeted efficiency optimization for attention with bias existed prior to this work.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250699/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes standard query, key, and value tensors (N×C, M×C, M×C) along with a bias matrix b ∈ R^{N×M} that is replaced by two factor tensors of shape N×R and M×R (where R is the effective rank, typically 2–64). The factor tensors are generated from source representations such as token positions, spatial coordinates, or pair representations via learned or closed-form functions. The output is the standard attention output tensor o ∈ R^{N×C}, computed identically to the original biased attention when exact decomposition is used, or with negligible approximation error for SVD/neural decompositions.

## Method

FlashBias proves that FlashAttention's optimal HBM access is governed by the rank of the attention weight matrix, then applies low-rank compressed sensing theory to show that the bias matrix can be stored in Θ(NR) rather than Θ(NM) space. The core mechanism replaces the quadratic bias b = f(x_q, x_k) with a factorization φ_q(x_q)φ_k(x_k)^T, concatenating the factor tensors with q and k along the channel dimension so that the entire biased attention reduces to a single optimized matrix multiplication on GPU. Three decomposition strategies are provided: exact closed-form factor functions (ALiBi with R=2, 3D spatial distance with R=9), offline SVD for static learnable biases (Swin Transformer, Pangu-Weather), and lightweight token-wise neural networks trained to approximate dynamic data-dependent biases (AlphaFold 3's pair representation bias). This reduces HBM access from Θ(NMC²/S + NM) to Θ(NM(C²+R²)/S), achieving 1.5× speedup on AlphaFold 3 and over 2× on vision/language models without accuracy loss.

## Results

On SwinV2-B (ImageNet-1K), FlashBias achieves 87.186% top-1 accuracy (vs. 87.144% official) while reducing inference time by 60% (0.473s → 0.190s) and GPU memory by 27% (12829 MB → 9429 MB). On GPT-2 with ALiBi bias, FlashBias cuts the additional bias-processing cost from 5.0s to 2.3s (training) and 1.55s to 0.49s (inference) relative to pure causal FlashAttention. On AlphaFold 3's Pairformer (1218 residues), FlashBias reduces inference time by 32% (26.85s → 18.19s) with negligible accuracy change (pLLDDT 3.3758 vs. 3.3724). For the PDE solver at N=32186, FlashBias is the only method that avoids out-of-memory, using 2.97 GB vs. OOM for FlashAttention.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FlashBias consistently outperforms both FlashAttention with Bias [9] and FlexAttention [11] across all tested domains. Against FlashAttention with Bias, FlashBias achieves 18.6% and 44% speedup in training and inference respectively on a plain Transformer (N=16384), and reduces the ALiBi bias overhead in GPT-2 from 5.0s to 2.3s (training) and 1.55s to 0.49s (inference). Against FlexAttention, the gap widens dramatically for complex or dynamic biases: on SwinV2-B, FlexAttention takes 2.885s vs. FlashBias's 0.190s (15× slower) and 25986 MB vs. 9429 MB memory, because FlexAttention requires recompilation for per-layer bias shapes and cannot handle data-dependent biases. On the PDE solver at N=32186, FlexAttention is entirely unsupported for learnable bias training, while FlashBias completes in 51.1s using 2.97 GB. On AlphaFold 3, FlashBias (neural decomposition) matches the open-sourced code's accuracy (pLLDDT 3.3758 vs. 3.3724) while cutting inference time by 32%, whereas removing bias entirely (FlashAttention w/o bias) collapses pTM from 0.9500 to 0.1713.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the FlashAttention line of IO-aware attention computation to the underexplored case of additive bias matrices, building on the tiling and SRAM/HBM analysis from FlashAttention-2 and the low-rank compressed sensing theory of Candès and Tao. It departs from prior mask-based speedup techniques (Binary Block Masking, FlashMask) that exploit sparsity, and from FlexAttention's compiler-based element-wise approach, by instead exploiting the inherent low-rank structure of dense continuous biases. The key conceptual move is recasting the quadratic HBM read of a dense bias as a compressed sensing problem, where the optimal 'measurement' cost is governed by matrix rank rather than element count.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Attention with bias is a foundational component in Swin Transformers, GPT-2 with ALiBi, AlphaFold 3, PDE solvers, and weather models, yet had no targeted efficiency optimization despite being a clear bottleneck. FlashBias provides a theoretically grounded, drop-in solution that achieves 1.5–2× speedups without accuracy loss, and the rank-based IO lower bound (Corollary 3.3) gives a clean criterion for when further speedup is impossible. Open questions include whether all practically useful biases are low-rank (the paper acknowledges some Swin heads are not), how to handle genuinely high-rank biases, and whether the neural decomposition's approximation error accumulates in very deep architectures during training rather than inference.
