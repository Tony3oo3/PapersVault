## Problem

Synchronizing multiple casually captured, unposed videos of the same dynamic scene remains an open problem: existing methods rely on controlled environments, audio cues, human pose priors, or expensive time-coded hardware, none of which are available in real-world multi-camera recordings. The paper tackles this by asking whether epipolar geometry alone, applied to dynamic object motion, can recover millisecond-accurate temporal alignment without any specialized capture setup.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250767/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes N unsynchronized videos (typically ~10 s subsequences) of the same dynamic scene from different viewpoints, with no requirement for known poses, matched frame rates, or shared audio. It outputs a single temporal offset per video (s^i in seconds) that, when applied to each video's original clock, produces globally time-aligned streams where any shared timestamp corresponds to the same physical instant. The pipeline handles varying frame rates (5–30 fps), mixed static and moving cameras, and different resolutions across views without pipeline modifications.

## Method

VisualSync is a training-free, three-stage optimization framework built on the insight that at correct synchronization, epipolar constraints (x'^T F x = 0) must hold for all cross-view correspondences, including those on moving objects. Stage 0 extracts visual cues using off-the-shelf models: VGGT estimates camera poses and intrinsics, CoTracker3 produces dense per-video point tracks, MAST3R establishes cross-view correspondences within dynamic masks (segmented via GPT-4o + Grounding-DINO + SAM2 + DEVA), and the Sampson error quantifies epipolar violations for each candidate offset. Stage 1 performs a brute-force search over discrete temporal offsets for each camera pair, minimizing the summed Sampson error over all matched tracklet pairs, then filters unreliable pairs by analyzing the energy landscape (discarding pairs with ambiguous minima). Stage 2 solves a robust least-squares problem (IRLS with Huber loss) over the surviving pairwise offsets to recover globally consistent per-video synchronization offsets, achieving sub-130 ms mean error across four diverse datasets.

## Results

VisualSync achieves a median synchronization error of 46.6 ms on the challenging Egohumans dataset and 41.5 ms on CMU Panoptic, with mean errors of 122.1 ms and 112.6 ms respectively. Across all four datasets, the method maintains a mean error below 123 ms without requiring any ground-truth camera poses. On the synthetic UDBD dataset, VisualSync reaches 5.9 ms median error, while on 3D-POP (small, distant pigeons) it achieves 77.8 ms median. The overall average synchronization error across datasets is below 130 ms, outperforming all baselines that do not use GT poses.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among methods that do not use ground-truth camera poses, VisualSync outperforms MASt3R on all four datasets for video-level median error (46.6 vs. 263.8 ms on Egohumans; 41.5 vs. 58.1 ms on CMU Panoptic; 77.8 vs. 72.2 ms on 3D-POP; 5.9 vs. 7.4 ms on UDBD), with the largest margin on Egohumans where MASt3R struggles with egocentric views and motion blur. Uni4D* (which uses GT poses) is competitive on CMU Panoptic (99.9 ms median) but degrades catastrophically on 3D-POP (1265.4 ms median) due to noisy metric depth estimates for small, distant pigeons. Sync-NeRF* (also GT poses) achieves near-perfect results on UDBD (0.2 ms median) but fails on all other datasets, reporting errors above 866 ms, likely due to lack of strong priors for complex dynamic scenes. On pairwise A@100, VisualSync leads on Egohumans (33.9%), 3D-POP (33.3%), and UDBD (82.1%), while Uni4D* edges ahead on CMU Panoptic (32.3% vs. 26.0%), though it relies on GT poses. Overall, VisualSync is the only method that consistently achieves sub-100 ms median error across all four diverse datasets without any GT input.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the long line of epipolar-geometry-based video synchronization (Albl et al. 2017, Pundik & Moses 2010, Elhayek et al. 2012) by removing the static-scene and fixed-viewpoint assumptions that limited prior work. Its key conceptual move is recognizing that at the correct time offset, even dynamic 3D points satisfy epipolar constraints, and then operationalizing this insight through a pipeline of modern foundation models (VGGT for poses, CoTracker3 for temporal tracking, MAST3R for cross-view matching, DEVA/GPT-4o for dynamic segmentation). Unlike learning-based approaches like Sync-NeRF that jointly optimize photometric loss, or human-pose/audio-based methods that require specific scene content, VisualSync frames synchronization as a pure geometric energy minimization over dense correspondences, making it content-agnostic.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By achieving sub-130 ms synchronization on casual, unposed multi-camera recordings of dynamic scenes, VisualSync removes a practical bottleneck for 4D scene reconstruction, bullet-time effects, and novel-view synthesis from in-the-wild footage (demonstrated via K-Planes). Key open questions include the O(N²) pairwise scaling for large camera arrays, the inability to handle non-uniform motion speeds (e.g., slow-motion segments), and the dependence on at least partially reliable camera poses. The modular design means improvements to any upstream component (better trackers, pose estimators, or matchers) directly translate to better synchronization, positioning this as a practical infrastructure piece rather than a one-off result.
