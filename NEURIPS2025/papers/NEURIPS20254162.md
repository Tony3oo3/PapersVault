## Problem

Existing adversarial SSP algorithms using OMD with negative-entropy regularization achieve regret scaling with √(log SA), but provably fail to exploit sparsity: on instances where only M ≪ SA state-action pairs incur cost, the negative entropy still incurs √(log S) regret. This paper asks whether sparsity can improve the minimax regret in full-information SSP, and by how much, showing that the benefit is logarithmic in M rather than polynomial as in the simpler experts setting.

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes a known-transition MDP (S states, A actions, transition kernel P, start state s₀, absorbing goal g) and a sequence of K adversarial cost functions c_k: Γ→[0,1] with sparsity level M (at most M non-zero entries per episode), revealed in full after each episode. It outputs a stationary proper policy per episode via occupancy measures in Δ(T), and the theoretical contribution is a regret bound of Õ(√(DKT* log(MT*))) that requires no prior knowledge of M or T*. The parameter-free version uses a batching scheme over K episodes and a scale-invariant meta-algorithm over N≈log K base learners.

## Method

The core contribution is a family of ℓ_r-norm regularizers ψ_p(q) = p(−1 + ‖q‖_{1+1/p}^{1+1/p}) for p > 1, which smoothly interpolates between the negative entropy (p→∞) and the squared Euclidean norm (p→1). The key geometric insight is that for small p, the gradient of ψ_p does not diverge at the boundary (q(s,a)→0), so the Bregman divergence does not infinitely stretch Euclidean distance near zero — this makes OMD robust to the heavily skewed initial occupancy measure that causes the negative entropy to fail on sparse SSP instances. Setting p = log(TM) and η = √(pT^{1+1/p}/(KDM^{1/p})) yields the sparsity-adaptive regret bound O(√(DKT log(MT))), which is matched by a lower bound of Ω(√(DKT* log M)) constructed via a reduction to a non-sparse experts problem with O(M) actions and asymmetric random-walk analysis. A parameter-free variant removes the need to know M and T* in advance via sparse-agnostic batching and a meta-algorithm, achieving the same leading-order bound up to double-logarithmic factors.

## Results

The paper establishes that OMD with the proposed ℓ_r-norm regularizer (r∈(1,2)) achieves a sparsity-adaptive regret bound of O(√(DKT log(MT))) for known-transition SSP with adversarial costs, where M is the sparsity level (max non-zero costs per episode) rather than the full state-action space size SA. A matching lower bound of Ω(√(KT*D log M)) confirms this is minimax optimal up to a log T* factor. The paper further shows that the standard negative-entropy regularizer used in prior work [8] provably suffers Ω(√(K log S)) regret even on sparse instances with M=3, demonstrating it cannot exploit sparsity. In the unknown-transition setting, a lower bound of Ω(D√(SAK)) holds even for M=1, showing sparsity provides no polynomial benefit there.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary theoretical comparison is against the negative-entropy regularizer used in the state-of-the-art SSP algorithm of Chen et al. [8]. On a constructed sparse SSP instance with M=3 and S states, the paper proves that OMD with negative entropy suffers Ω(√(K log S)) regret for any step-size, meaning it provides zero improvement over the non-sparse worst-case bound in terms of S. In contrast, the proposed ℓ_r regularizer achieves √(log M) dependence, which for M=3 is a constant independent of S. Compared to the simpler experts problem (S=1) where sparsity yields a polynomial improvement from √(K log A) to √(KMA⁻¹ log A) [19], the SSP setting only achieves a logarithmic improvement (√(log M) vs √(log SA)), which the paper shows is fundamental via a matching lower bound. In the unknown-transition setting, the paper shows that even the most extreme sparsity (M=1) cannot remove the polynomial √(SA) dependence, in stark contrast to the known-transition case where sparsity fully eliminates the SA dependence.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the adversarial SSP literature initiated by Rosenberg & Mansour [28] and refined by Chen et al. [8], which established OMD with negative-entropy regularization on the space of occupancy measures as the standard approach. The key departure is showing that the negative-entropy regularizer is fundamentally non-adaptive to sparsity, and proposing a family of ℓ_r-norm regularizers (r ∈ (1,2)) that interpolate between negative entropy and squared Euclidean norm to achieve sparsity-adaptive bounds. The parameter-free algorithm borrows the sparse-agnostic batching technique from Kwon & Perchet [19] and the scale-invariant meta-algorithm from [8], but applies them to the new regularizer family.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a complete characterization of how sparsity affects minimax regret in adversarial SSP: the benefit is only logarithmic (√log M) rather than polynomial as in the simpler experts problem, and vanishes entirely in the unknown-transition setting. This delineates a fundamental structural difference between SSP and online linear optimization that practitioners should account for when designing algorithms for combinatorial state-action spaces. Open questions include whether other structural properties (beyond sparsity) can yield polynomial benefits, how sparsity interacts with bandit feedback, and the behavior in the high-dimensional regime where K is small relative to the state-action space.
