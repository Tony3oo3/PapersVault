## Problem

State-of-the-art human video generation methods and their supporting datasets are confined to single static camera shots, making them inadequate for synthesizing long-form, multi-shot speech videos that require coordinated camera transitions, gesture planning, and vocal modulation. No existing dataset provides the scale, multi-shot diversity, and comprehensive multimodal annotations (2D keypoints, 3D SMPL-X, audio, text) needed to train and evaluate such systems.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250646/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The Orator system takes a free-form speech script and a set of reference images captured from different camera angles as input, and produces a long-form video segmented into N shots, each rendered at 1080p with a distinct camera type (close-up through wide shot), synchronized speech audio, and coherent speaker motion. The TalkCuts dataset itself provides 164k clips (57M frames) at 1080p with per-frame DWPose 133-keypoint annotations, per-frame SMPL-X parameters (jaw, body, hand poses, facial expressions, global translation), aligned audio, and Qwen2.5-VL-generated textual descriptions covering gestures, expressions, camera type, and background.

## Method

Orator decomposes multi-shot speech video generation into an LLM planning stage and a multimodal generation stage. The DirectorLLM (GPT-4o) uses a RAG-based approach—retrieving top-5 similar speech segments from a Shot Division Corpus via FAISS text-embedding similarity—to produce per-segment camera shot instructions, gesture/motion instructions, and vocal delivery instructions. SpeechGen then synthesizes expressive audio per segment using instruction-fine-tuned CosyVoice with sentence-level (tone, pace, pitch) and token-level (emphasis, breath, laughter) controls. VideoGen builds on CogVideoX's 3D-VAE transformer backbone augmented with Hallo3's two-stage DiT architecture: stage 1 injects identity features (from a 3D causal VAE and InsightFace facial embeddings) into 3D attention blocks alongside T5-encoded motion instructions, and stage 2 conditions on Wav2Vec speech embeddings via cross-attention for lip-synced generation. The model is fine-tuned in two stages on TalkCuts to adapt from portrait-only pretraining to diverse half-body and full-body camera views, and the final video is assembled by concatenating per-shot clips.

## Results

On the primary audio-driven human speech video generation task (Table 3), the Orator system trained on TalkCuts achieves the best FID (45.86 vs. 57.28 for Hallo3), FVD (622.76 vs. 855.84), PSNR (20.61 dB), SSIM (0.708), and LPIPS (0.198), while also attaining the best Sync-D (8.32) and highest subject consistency (96.24%) among all baselines. For the novel LLM-guided camera shot transition task (Table 2), GPT-4o with RAG achieves 70.66% accuracy, 64.06% SMA, and 48.10% IoU, outperforming zero-shot GPT-4o (64.34%/48.34%/40.59%) and fine-tuned LLaMA 3.1-8B (79.09%/49.40%/30.06%) on the key alignment metrics. In the pose-guided setting (Table 4), fine-tuning Animate Anyone on TalkCuts yields the best SSIM (0.843), PSNR (24.58), LPIPS (0.114), FID (57.41), FVD (456.84), and ArcFace distance (0.344) among all tested methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In audio-driven generation (Table 3), Orator trained on TalkCuts outperforms Hallo3 (the closest competitor) by 11.42 FID, 233.08 FVD, and 1.39 dB PSNR, while also achieving better lip-sync (Sync-D 8.32 vs. 9.76) and subject consistency (96.24% vs. 95.81%). SadTalker and EchoMimicV2 are substantially weaker, with FID values of 159.78 and 177.22 respectively, and EchoMimicV2 shows the worst lip-sync (Sync-C 1.94). For camera shot planning (Table 2), GPT-4o with RAG achieves the best SMA (64.06%) and IoU (48.10%), beating fine-tuned LLaMA 3.1-8B (49.40% SMA, 30.06% IoU) despite LLaMA's higher raw accuracy (79.09% vs. 70.66%), indicating that RAG-based retrieval of relevant shot-transition examples is more effective than fine-tuning for boundary alignment. In pose-guided generation (Table 4), fine-tuning on TalkCuts improves all three tested models (Animate Anyone, MusePose, ControlNeXt) across every metric; Animate Anyone Tuned achieves the best overall scores (FID 57.41, FVD 456.84, ArcFace 0.344), while ControlNeXt Tuned offers the best FVD among non-Animate-Anyone methods (480.21 vs. 1014.34 for MusePose Tuned). The consistent gains from fine-tuning on TalkCuts across all three SOTA pose-guided methods validate the dataset's utility beyond the authors' own pipeline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the audio-driven human video generation line (SadTalker, EchoMimicV2, Hallo3) and pose-guided video generation (Animate Anyone, ControlNeXt, MusePose) by introducing the multi-shot, long-form dimension that all prior methods and datasets lack. The video generation backbone builds directly on CogVideoX (3D VAE + transformer diffusion) and Hallo3 (two-stage DiT with identity reference injection), while the LLM planning component repurposes RAG-augmented GPT-4o for camera shot segmentation. Conceptually, the paper departs from the single-static-shot paradigm that dominates both existing datasets (TikTok, BEAT2, TalkSHOW) and generation models, framing multi-shot coherence as a distinct and previously unaddressed challenge.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TalkCuts fills a clear gap as the first large-scale (500+ hours, 10k+ identities) multi-shot speech video dataset with comprehensive annotations (2D keypoints, 3D SMPL-X, audio, text), making it a likely standard benchmark for the emerging multi-shot speech video generation task. The Orator baseline is explicitly positioned as a 'simple' reference, and the experiments reveal that even fine-tuned SOTA models still struggle with temporal coherence, hand-region fidelity, and identity consistency across diverse camera angles—highlighting that the generation side remains substantially underdeveloped relative to the data. Open questions include whether stronger motion-aware diffusion architectures can close the multi-shot consistency gap, and whether the LLM-as-director paradigm scales to more complex narrative structures beyond linear speech.
