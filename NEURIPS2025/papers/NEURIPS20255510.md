## Problem

Nesterov's universal fast gradient method achieves optimal convergence rates for Hölder smooth convex problems without knowing the smoothness parameters, but it requires a predetermined target accuracy ε that breaks anytime convergence guarantees and whose optimal value depends on the unknown distance D₀ to the minimizer. Additionally, its line-search subroutine is difficult to extend to stochastic optimization. This paper addresses both limitations by developing parameter-free accelerated methods that adapt dynamically to the optimization trajectory without any pre-specified accuracy level.

## Input / Output

The method requires only the composite objective ψ(x) = f(x) + g(x) (where f is Hölder smooth and g has an easily computable proximal operator), an initial point x⁰, and a conservative lower-bound estimate r̄ ≤ 4D₀ for the initial distance to the optimum. It outputs the best iterate z^K = argmin{ψ(y⁰), …, ψ(y^K)} with an anytime convergence rate of Õ(D₀^(1+ν) L̂_ν / K^((1+3ν)/2)) that does not depend on ν, L_ν, or D₀. The stochastic variant (AGDA-LSFM) additionally requires the domain of g to be bounded, replacing the line search with a closed-form balance equation for the stepsize parameter.

## Method

The core algorithm (AGDA) is a variant of accelerated regularized dual averaging with Nesterov momentum, where the key innovation is a distance-adaptive stepsize policy. Instead of fixing the inexactness parameter δ = ε as in the universal fast gradient method, AGDA sets η_k = (β_{k+1}r̄_k² − β_k r̄_{k-1}²)/(8a_{k+1}), which dynamically adjusts based on the observed distance r̄_k = max{r̄_{k-1}, ‖x⁰ − v^k‖} to the iterates. A two-stage line search (exponential search followed by bisection) determines β_{k+1} to satisfy a descent condition, requiring only O(K log K) total function evaluations. For the stochastic setting, the line search is replaced by a nonlinear balance equation that decouples the trial point y^{k+1} from β_{k+1}, yielding a unique closed-form solution and eliminating the need for the domain diameter D. The distance-adaptive averaging sequence A_{k+1} = (Σᵢ r̄_i^{1/2})² combined with a logarithmic bound (Lemma 3) controls the ratio r̄_k/A_k, producing the final near-optimal rate up to logarithmic factors.

## Results

On the deterministic softmax problem (n=1000, d=2000, μ=0.005), AGDA achieves the fastest convergence among all tested methods, outperforming the accelerated FGM baseline and the non-accelerated DOG and DADA baselines. On the nonsmooth matrix game (ν=0) at sizes (896,128) and (448,64), AGDA again converges fastest. In the stochastic setting, AGDA-LSFM maintains stable convergence across r̄ values spanning 10⁻⁴ to 10⁴ on the softmax robustness test, while USFGM and Adam exhibit unstable performance under poor hyperparameter tuning. On ResNet18/CIFAR-10, AGDA-LSFM matches AdamW (lr=10⁻³) in training and validation loss and accuracy, while clearly outperforming DoG (r_ε=10⁻³).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DOG [14] (Ivgi et al., ICML 2023), AGDA wins on all three deterministic problems (softmax, both matrix game sizes) and on the non-convex ResNet18/CIFAR-10 training, where AGDA-LSFM achieves lower training and validation loss and higher accuracy than DoG. Against DADA [26] (Moshtaghifar et al., 2025), AGDA also converges faster on all deterministic benchmarks, confirming that the accelerated distance-adaptive stepsize provides a clear advantage over the non-accelerated dual-averaging variant. Against the universal fast gradient method FGM [27] (Nesterov, 2015), AGDA achieves faster convergence on all deterministic problems while eliminating the need to pre-specify the target accuracy ε, which FGM requires. In the stochastic setting, AGDA-LSFM outperforms USFGM [34] (Rodomanov et al., 2024) in robustness: on the diabetes dataset, USFGM's performance degrades sharply when its diameter parameter D is misspecified, whereas AGDA-LSFM remains stable across a wide range of r̄ values. Against Adam/AdamW [16], AGDA-LSFM matches AdamW on ResNet18/CIFAR-10 (comparable training loss, validation loss, and accuracy) but demonstrates superior robustness on the diabetes least-squares problem where Adam shows unstable convergence under suboptimal learning rates.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper synthesizes three recent threads in parameter-free optimization: Nesterov's universal fast gradient method (which achieves optimal Hölder-smooth rates but requires a pre-specified target accuracy ε and gradient-based line search), the DOG/DADA distance-adaptive stepsize technique (which approximates the unknown distance to the minimizer via observed iterate distances), and Rodomanov et al.'s balance-equation approach for line-search-free stochastic optimization. The key conceptual departure is replacing the fixed target ε with a dynamically evolving target that adapts to the optimization trajectory, thereby achieving anytime convergence without any prior knowledge of smoothness parameters, the Hölder constant, or the target accuracy. The line-search-free stochastic variant further eliminates the need to know the domain diameter D by substituting the distance-adaptive estimate r̄_k in place of D in the balance equation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The anytime convergence guarantee (no pre-specified ε) resolves a practical limitation of universal gradient methods that is particularly acute in nonsmooth and online learning settings, where iterations may be halted at arbitrary stages. The parameter-free nature—requiring only a rough lower bound on D₀—reduces the hyperparameter tuning burden that dominates practical deployment of first-order methods. Key limitations include the bounded-domain assumption for the stochastic variant, the O(log K) overhead in line search for the deterministic variant, and the suboptimal dependence on D₀ in the convergence rate. Open questions flagged by the authors include improving the D₀ dependence and relaxing the boundedness requirement, while the preliminary non-convex neural network experiment (ResNet18/CIFAR-10) suggests potential applicability beyond the convex theory but lacks rigorous guarantees.
