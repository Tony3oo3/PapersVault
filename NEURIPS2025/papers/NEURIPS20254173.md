## Problem

Expression-only GRN inference methods are fundamentally limited by the quadratic combinatorial search space of potential TF-TG interactions (~1 billion pairs genome-wide), while prior-based methods that constrain the search space to curated interactions inherit the precision limitations of their priors and cannot discover novel regulatory edges absent from the prior. This creates a tension between accuracy (requiring search-space reduction) and discovery potential (requiring unrestricted exploration) that existing methods fail to resolve.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a temporal single-cell RNA-seq expression matrix G ∈ R^{N×M} (N ordered cellular states, M genes), partitioned into non-overlapping segments of length T from which the model predicts W future time steps. The teacher model additionally receives a binary spatial prior mask M^spatial ∈ {0,1}^{M×M} encoding documented regulatory pairs, but the deployed student model operates solely on expression data. The output is a directed GRN adjacency matrix Â ∈ R^{M×M} obtained by averaging spatial-layer attention matrices across all temporal segments, with the final network defined by selecting the top-k edges matching the ground-truth edge count.

## Method

KINDLE implements a three-stage privileged-information distillation pipeline. Stage 1 trains a teacher model with hierarchical attention: a temporal layer (lower-triangular causal mask enforcing that each time step only attends to its history) followed by a spatial layer (prior-derived binary mask restricting cross-gene attention to curated regulatory pairs), jointly optimized to predict future expression from historical profiles. Stage 2 distills the teacher's regulatory knowledge into a lightweight student model that removes both the temporal layer and the spatial prior mask, trained with a weighted combination of expression prediction loss and a regulatory distillation loss (four variants explored: hard L2, soft KL with temperature, bilinear pool, and Gaussian RBF kernel). Stage 3 deploys the prior-free student for inference by partitioning the expression time series, extracting spatial attention matrices from each segment, and averaging them into a final adjacency matrix. The key insight is that prior knowledge acts as privileged supervisory signal during teacher training, enabling the student to learn biologically coherent regulatory structure without ever accessing the prior at inference time.

## Results

KINDLE-Gaussian (the best variant) achieves state-of-the-art performance across all four BEELINE benchmark datasets without requiring any prior knowledge. On mESC, it reaches AUROC 0.757, AUPRC 0.646, and F1 0.529; on mHSC-GM, it attains AUPRC 0.799 and F1 0.875. The paper emphasizes AUPRC and F1 as more reliable metrics given the extreme class imbalance (true edges constitute only 0.65–1.15% of candidate edges). Across all 12 dataset-metric combinations, KINDLE-Gaussian achieves the best score in 11 of them.

## Baselines & Comparisons

KINDLE-Gaussian decisively outperforms all expression-only baselines: on mESC, it improves AUPRC from 0.137 (GENIE3) to 0.646 and AUROC from 0.545 to 0.757. Against prior-guided methods, KINDLE-Gaussian surpasses CEFCON (the strongest prior-based method on mESC) by 0.393 in AUPRC (0.646 vs. 0.253) and 0.100 in F1 (0.529 vs. 0.429), while on mHSC-GM it exceeds CellOracle's AUPRC by 0.556 (0.799 vs. 0.243) and NetREX's by 0.655 (0.799 vs. 0.144). Even the weakest KINDLE variant (Soft distillation) beats the best prior-guided method (CEFCON) on mHSC-E AUPRC (0.559 vs. 0.405) and mHSC-GM AUPRC (0.789 vs. 0.444). Notably, KINDLE achieves these gains without any prior knowledge input at inference time, whereas all prior-guided baselines require curated TF-TG interaction masks. The Prior_Random control (random edges sampled from the prior) confirms that the prior's structure itself provides some signal, but KINDLE-Gaussian still exceeds it by 0.335 in AUPRC on mHSC-GM (0.799 vs. 0.464).

## Relation to Prior Work

KINDLE applies the privileged-feature distillation paradigm (Yang et al., NeurIPS 2022) to gene regulatory network inference, a domain where prior-based methods (CEFCON, CellOracle, NetREX) have long dominated by constraining the search space to curated TF-TG pairs. The teacher model's temporal attention layer draws on TRIGON's temporal causality modeling, while the spatial layer uses a prior-derived binary mask to restrict cross-gene attention. The key conceptual departure is that prior knowledge is used exclusively during teacher training and then distilled into a student that operates entirely on expression data, eliminating the inference-time dependency on external databases that all prior-based methods require.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By decoupling GRN inference from prior knowledge at deployment, KINDLE opens the door to discovering novel regulatory interactions absent from any curated database—a capability fundamentally impossible for prior-constrained methods. However, the framework's reliance on temporal expression data limits applicability to cross-sectional single-cell datasets, and the distillation process may propagate biases from incomplete or noisy priors. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to non-model organisms, whole-genome scale (~30,000 genes), and whether post-transcriptional and epigenetic regulatory layers can be incorporated without reintroducing prior dependencies.
