## Problem

Deformable 2D/3D registration—aligning a preoperative 3D CT volume to a small set of intraoperative X-rays—is severely ill-posed, especially in sparse-view (as few as 2 images) and limited-angle (~30°) clinical settings. Existing deformable methods require dense per-voxel displacement fields that demand extensive, procedure-specific regularization and hyperparameter tuning to produce anatomically plausible warps, while rigid methods ignore inter-joint articulation. The paper addresses this gap by exploiting the anatomical prior that bones are rigid bodies, reducing the deformation to a low-dimensional polyrigid parameterization that is well-constrained even with minimal supervision.

## Input / Output

The method takes a preoperative 3D CT volume, 2–9 simultaneously acquired 2D X-ray images, binary segmentations of K articulated rigid bodies (e.g., individual bones), and camera intrinsics extracted from DICOM metadata. It produces a 3D polyrigid deformation field parameterized by only O(K) SE(3) transforms (K ≪ M voxels), per-X-ray camera extrinsic matrices, and the resulting warped CT volume. Notably, the weight field combining rigid transforms is precomputed from the segmentations and requires no per-structure hyperparameters, and the method operates without any deformation regularizer beyond the optimizer step size.

## Method

PolyPose parameterizes the 3D deformation field as a convex combination of K rigid transforms in the tangent space se(3), where each transform corresponds to an articulated bone and the combination weights are computed via a mass-normalized distance function w_k(x) = m_k/(1+d_k²(x)) that eliminates the per-structure hyperparameter ε used in prior polyrigid formulations. The pipeline is two-stage: first, camera poses are estimated by rigidly registering a single anchor structure (e.g., pelvis) across all views using the xvr framework; second, all K rigid body poses are jointly optimized by maximizing a patch-wise normalized cross-correlation loss between differentially rendered X-rays (computed via Beer-Lambert line integrals over the warped CT) and the real X-rays. The polyrigid warp at every voxel is computed efficiently as exp(W·T̂)·X̃ using batched matrix multiplication, where W is the precomputed M×K weight matrix and T̂ stacks the log-transformed poses. This construction guarantees the deformation is diffeomorphic, smooth, and coordinate-frame invariant by design, requiring no additional regularization or tunable hyperparameters beyond the Adam optimizer step sizes.

## Results

On the DeepFluoro dataset (2 X-rays, ~30° separation), PolyPose achieves 0.98 Dice and 1.48/1.56 mm HD95 on the left/right femurs with 0.00% topological folds, outperforming all 6 baselines. On the Head&Neck dataset (2–9 synthetic X-rays over 180°), PolyPose attains the highest 3D Dice across all 21 rigid structures and 5 soft tissue organs at every view count, with the most regular deformation fields (lowest %Folds and σ(log|J|)). The method requires no regularization hyperparameters and uses only O(K) optimizable parameters (K=3 rigid bodies on DeepFluoro) versus O(M)≈10⁷ for dense baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PolyPose outperforms all 7 baselines on the DeepFluoro femur metrics, the most clinically relevant structures. Against the rigid baseline xvr [16], PolyPose gains +0.02 Dice on the left femur (0.98 vs 0.96) and +0.04 on the right (0.98 vs 0.94), while matching on the pelvis (both ~0.99). Against the dense per-voxel translation model DenseR3 [23], PolyPose improves femur Dice by +0.01/+0.02 and reduces HD95 by ~2 mm, while eliminating the 0.44% topological folds. The 3D/3D iterative solvers FireANTs [37] and anatomix [38] both fail to capture inter-scan femur motion (Dice 0.93–0.96 vs PolyPose's 0.98), and anatomix additionally introduces 3.01% folds. The foundation models uniGradICON [34] and multiGradICON [35] perform worst, with uniGradICON achieving only 0.50 Dice on the left femur (HD95 28.51 mm), likely due to streaking artifacts in sparse-view CBCT reconstructions. On Head&Neck, PolyPose also outperforms the deep learning methods LiftReg [18] and 2D3D-RegNet [19], which require longitudinal multi-CT training data unavailable in most clinical settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

PolyPose adapts the log-Euclidean polyrigid framework from Arsigny et al. [26] and Commowick et al. [51], previously applied to 3D/3D registration of comparable-sized structures (brain regions, carpal bones), to the severely under-determined 2D/3D setting via differentiable X-ray rendering. It departs from dense per-voxel deformation models (LiftReg, 2D3D-RegNet, DenseR3) that require extensive application-specific regularization, and from rigid 2D/3D methods (xvr) that cannot capture articulated inter-joint motion. The key methodological departure is a hyperparameter-free mass-based weight function replacing the reciprocal-distance formulation of [51], which the authors show fails when rigid bodies have very different sizes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PolyPose addresses a clinically critical gap: providing 3D volumetric guidance from as few as two intraoperative X-rays without the regularization and hyperparameter tuning that currently makes deformable 2D/3D registration impractical for new procedures. The regularization-free, near-hyperparameter-free design enables out-of-the-box generalization across surgical specialties, which is a significant practical advantage. Open questions include whether the diffeomorphism constraint can be relaxed to handle topology changes (e.g., jaw opening), whether the method generalizes to anatomical regions with insufficient rigid bodies (e.g., abdomen), and whether a kinematic chain extension could capture more complex articulated motion.
