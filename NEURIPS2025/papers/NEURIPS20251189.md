## Problem

Existing 3D generative models produce monolithic meshes without part-level decomposition, while two-stage segment-then-reconstruct pipelines suffer from segmentation errors, high computational overhead, and inability to recover occluded or invisible parts. No prior method can generate semantically distinct, geometrically separate 3D parts from a single image in a unified, end-to-end fashion.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251189/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single RGB image and a user-specified number of parts N as input, and simultaneously outputs N separate 3D meshes (vertex and face sets) all in a shared canonical coordinate space [-1,1]³, enabling direct assembly without additional transformations. Each part is represented by 512 latent tokens, and the parts are permutation-invariant, meaning their ordering is arbitrary and they can be independently edited, removed, or added after generation. No segmentation masks or pre-segmented image regions are required at any stage.

## Method

PartCrafter restructures the pretrained TripoSG mesh diffusion transformer into a compositional multi-entity architecture with two key innovations. First, a compositional latent space assigns each of the N parts its own set of K=512 latent tokens, augmented with learnable part identity embeddings to disambiguate parts while preserving permutation invariance. Second, a hierarchical local-global attention mechanism applies self-attention within each part's token set (capturing intra-part geometry) and across all concatenated tokens (capturing inter-part spatial relationships), with DINOv2 image features injected via cross-attention at both levels. The 21 DiT blocks alternate between local and global attention in an interleaved pattern following a long-skip U-Net-like structure. Training uses rectified flow matching with a shared noise level across all parts, initialized from TripoSG's pretrained encoder, decoder, and transformer weights, and fine-tuned on a curated dataset of ~50K part-annotated objects mined from Objaverse, ShapeNet, and ABO.

## Results

On 3D part-level object generation, PARTCRAFTER achieves a Chamfer Distance of 0.1726 and F-Score of 0.7472 on Objaverse, outperforming HoloPart (CD 0.1916, F-Score 0.6916) and its own backbone TripoSG* (CD 0.1821, F-Score 0.7115). On ABO, it reaches an F-Score of 0.8617 with IoU of 0.0243, substantially better than HoloPart (F-Score 0.8093, IoU 0.0449). For 3D scene generation on 3D-Front, PARTCRAFTER achieves CD 0.1491 and F-Score 0.8148, surpassing MIDI (CD 0.1602, F-Score 0.7931), and maintains strong performance under severe occlusion (F-Score 0.7800 vs MIDI's 0.6618). Inference takes only 34 seconds per object, roughly 32× faster than HoloPart's 18 minutes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against HoloPart (the closest concurrent method for part-level object generation), PARTCRAFTER wins on every metric across all three datasets: lower CD, higher F-Score, and lower part-overlap IoU, while being approximately 32× faster (34 s vs. 18 min) by eliminating the separate segmentation stage. Against its own backbone TripoSG (token-aligned variant TripoSG*), PARTCRAFTER improves object-level F-Score from 0.7115 to 0.7472 on Objaverse and from 0.7723 to 0.8617 on ABO, demonstrating that explicit part-level structure enhances holistic generation quality. Against MIDI on 3D scene generation, PARTCRAFTER achieves lower CD (0.1491 vs. 0.1602) and higher F-Score (0.8148 vs. 0.7931) on 3D-Front, and the gap widens dramatically under severe occlusion where MIDI's F-Score drops to 0.6618 while PARTCRAFTER retains 0.7800. MIDI slightly edges out PARTCRAFTER on IoU (0.0013 vs. 0.0034), which the authors attribute to MIDI's use of ground-truth segmentation masks that enforce non-overlapping parts, whereas PARTCRAFTER operates without any segmentation input.

## Relation to Prior Work

PARTCRAFTER reassembles the encoder, decoder, and DiT blocks of TripoSG (a pretrained 3D mesh rectified-flow model) into a compositional multi-entity architecture, introducing a part-identity-embedded latent space and a local-global attention mechanism that has no direct precedent in 3D generation. It departs conceptually from the dominant two-stage paradigm (HoloPart, MIDI) that first segments an image or mesh and then reconstructs each segment, arguing that explicit segmentation is neither necessary nor sufficient for structured 3D generation. The compositional latent space design draws on the observation that 3DShape2VecSet tokens are implicitly spatially localized, extending this to bind groups of tokens to distinct semantic parts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the segmentation bottleneck, PARTCRAFTER enables one-shot structured 3D generation that can infer occluded or invisible parts from a single image—something fundamentally impossible for two-stage pipelines. The finding that part-aware generation improves whole-object fidelity over the monolithic backbone suggests that compositional structure is a useful inductive bias for 3D shape synthesis more broadly. Key open questions include scaling beyond 50K part-annotated objects, handling arbitrary or user-specified part decompositions, and closing the domain gap to real-world photography without style-transfer hacks.
