## Problem

Multi-modal Diffusion Transformers (MM-DiTs) such as SD3.0 and FLUX consistently fail to render rare or imaginative prompts (e.g., 'an Eiffel tower made of water') because these concepts appear too sparsely in pre-training data to leave a strong imprint. Existing remedies require finetuning, denoising-time optimization, or external LLM guidance, all of which add substantial overhead. The paper asks whether the intended semantics already reside in the model's text embeddings and can be surfaced through a lightweight, training-free intervention.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a standard text prompt (and, for image editing, a reference image generated from a simple object prompt) and produces a generated image, video, or edited image at the model's native resolution. The intervention is applied purely to the text token embeddings inside each joint-attention block at every diffusion timestep, requiring no modification to the model weights, architecture, or sampling schedule. A single hyperparameter σ=1.3 governs the strength of the intervention, and the method is compatible with existing post-hoc techniques like R2F.

## Method

TORA (Token Spacing and Residual Alignment) is a training-free, per-block, per-timestep intervention on text embeddings within MM-DiT joint-attention layers. Token Spacing performs PCA on the text embedding matrix at each block, identifies the principal subspace via the elbow point (MDC method), and scales the top-k singular values by σ=1.3 to widen inter-token distances, thereby amplifying local isotropy and making rare semantics distinguishable. Residual Alignment then rotates the remaining residual subspace using a Givens rotation so that its dominant direction aligns with the CFG semantic vector s = e_cond − e_∅, counteracting the risk that variance scaling pushes embeddings away from the intended semantic direction. The paper provides a mathematical analysis showing that variance scale-up improves local isotropy (measured via cluster-based cosine metrics) while the accompanying increase in global anisotropy is harmless, and derives a sign condition on Δγ that explains when scaling alone can degrade alignment—motivating the residual alignment step.

## Results

On RareBench (text-to-image, GPT-4o evaluation), TORA applied to SD3.0 improves scores across all eight categories, e.g., Property 49.4→89.8, Color 53.1→85.8, and Relation 51.2→60.5. For FLUX-schnell, gains are particularly large on Shape (60.0→89.5) and Texture (70.0→73.8). In text-to-video generation, TORA boosts CogVideoX-5B by over 20 percentage points in most categories (e.g., Property 44.7→60.8, Relation 38.6→69.6). For text-driven image editing (Stable Flow), the human evaluation score jumps from 50.8 to 87.7 and GPT-4o from 62.1 to 82.8.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TORA consistently outperforms all MM-DiT baselines on RareBench. Against SD3.0, TORA gains 40+ points on Property (49.4→89.8) and 32.7 on Color (53.1→85.8), while also improving Shape (71.9→84.5) and Texture (65.0→75.5). Against FLUX-schnell, the largest gains appear on Shape (60.0→89.5) and Relation (62.5→67.3), though FLUX-schnell's Complex score (78.1) slightly exceeds TORA's (72.3). Compared to the LLM-guided R2F method on SD3.0, TORA matches or exceeds R2F in most categories (e.g., Color 81.9→85.8, Texture 72.5→75.5) without requiring an external LLM, and the combination R2F+TORA further pushes scores (Property 89.4→90.1, Color 81.9→86.7). For text-to-video, TORA improves CogVideoX-5B by 16.1 points on Property (44.7→60.8) and 31.0 on Relation (38.6→69.6), with gains exceeding 20% in most categories. On image editing (Stable Flow), TORA more than doubles the directional alignment metric (CLIPdir 0.08→0.20) and lifts human evaluation from 50.8 to 87.7, though CLIPimg drops slightly (0.87→0.80) due to the large visual changes expected for rare prompts.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes NLP findings on embedding anisotropy and local isotropy (Ethayarajh, Cai et al., Mu et al.) to the MM-DiT joint-attention context, arguing that rare text semantics are already encoded in text embeddings but remain geometrically inaccessible due to anisotropic clustering. It departs from the dominant prior approaches to rare-concept generation—finetuning (FasterDiT, Remix-DiT), inference-time optimization (GroundIt), and LLM-grounded guidance (R2F, ELLA, LLM4Gen)—by requiring none of these, instead applying PCA-based variance scaling (Token Spacing) and a Givens rotation (Residual Alignment) directly to text embeddings before each joint-attention block. The method is positioned as complementary to, rather than competitive with, existing techniques, as demonstrated by the R2F + TORA combination.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TORA offers a training-free, low-overhead intervention (≈2× baseline GPU time, negligible memory increase) that improves rare-prompt semantic alignment across text-to-image, text-to-video, and image editing without external LLMs or architectural changes, making it broadly deployable. Key limitations include restriction to joint-attention MM-DiT architectures (not U-Net backbones), untested behavior on very long prompts, and a single hyperparameter (σ=1.3) that, while effective, shows sensitivity outside the 1.0–1.5 range. Open questions include whether the isotropy-based explanation generalizes to training-time interventions, whether deeper theoretical grounding for why variance scaling elicits semantic emergence exists, and whether the approach extends to emerging DiT variants.
