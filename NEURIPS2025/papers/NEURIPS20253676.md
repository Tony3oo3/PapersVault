## Problem

Pseudo-label-based semi-supervised learning under class imbalance suffers from classifier bias toward majority classes, and existing reference-based bias correction methods (e.g., CDMAD) use simplistic uniform or blank images that lack realistic color, texture, and frequency statistics. This statistical mismatch causes inaccurate bias estimation, particularly under severe imbalance or labeled-unlabeled distribution shifts, limiting minority-class performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253676/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100-LT and STL-10-LT; 32×32 or 64×64 for Small-ImageNet-127) and, at each training or inference step, also consumes a batch of real images to compute a batch-averaged amplitude spectrum via 2D FFT. It produces bias-corrected class logits (real logits minus the reference-image logits) and a final class label via argmax, requiring one additional forward pass per batch for the random-phase reference image.

## Method

FARAD constructs reference images by preserving the batch-averaged amplitude spectrum of real data while uniformly randomizing the phase spectrum in [−π, π], then inverting via real-to-complex FFT. This design simultaneously ensures semantic irrelevance (randomized phase destroys all coherent edges and object structure) and statistical representativeness (preserved amplitude retains realistic color distributions, texture energy, and frequency characteristics). The classifier's logits on this random-phase image serve as an intrinsic bias estimate, which is subtracted from real-input logits during both training (to produce balanced pseudo-labels in FixMatch/ReMixMatch) and inference (to offset learned priors at test time). Computational overhead is mitigated by batched R2C FFT exploiting conjugate symmetry of real-valued inputs, achieving ≥50% speedup over naive complex-to-complex transforms.

## Results

On CIFAR-10-LT, FARAD achieves 91.5% bACC / 91.3% GM (FixMatch, γ=50), 88.6% / 88.5% (γ=100), and 85.4% / 85.1% (γ=150), consistently outperforming CDMAD by ~3 percentage points across all imbalance levels. On CIFAR-100-LT (γ=100), FARAD reaches 47.8% bACC versus CDMAD's 44.1%. On Small-ImageNet-127 (64×64), FARAD attains 62.1% bACC compared to CDMAD's 59.3%, demonstrating that the gains persist as dataset scale and class count increase.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FARAD consistently outperforms all 16 baselines across every dataset and imbalance level tested. Against the closest competitor CDMAD [30], FARAD gains ~3.4% bACC on CIFAR-10-LT (γ=50: 91.5 vs 88.1), ~3.7% on CIFAR-100-LT (γ=100: 47.8 vs 44.1), and ~2.8% on Small-ImageNet-127 (64×64: 62.1 vs 59.3). Among other CISSL methods, FARAD surpasses CoSSL [10] by ~4.7% (CIFAR-10-LT, γ=50: 91.5 vs 86.8), ABC [31] by ~5.9%, and CReST+LA [50,36] by ~5.9%. Against classic long-tailed supervised methods, the gap is even larger: FARAD exceeds LDAM-DRW [5] by ~22.6% and cRT [24] by ~23.7% on CIFAR-10-LT (γ=50). The advantage over vanilla FixMatch [41] is ~12.3% (γ=50), confirming that the random-phase bias correction is the dominant source of improvement rather than the underlying SSL pipeline. Notably, FARAD's margin over CDMAD widens slightly at higher imbalance (γ=150: 85.4 vs 82.3, a 3.1% gap), suggesting the statistical representativeness of amplitude-preserving references becomes increasingly valuable under severe skew.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends CDMAD (Lee et al., CVPR 2024), which subtracts classifier logits on a blank/uniform-color reference image to correct class-imbalance bias in semi-supervised learning. The key conceptual departure is replacing that simplistic reference with a random-phase image constructed by preserving the batch-averaged amplitude spectrum of real data while randomizing the phase spectrum, thereby achieving both semantic irrelevance and statistical representativeness simultaneously. The approach draws on classical Fourier analysis and prior observations that phase encodes structural/semantic content while amplitude captures color and frequency-energy statistics (Geirhos et al., Chen et al.), repurposing these spectral properties for bias estimation rather than for data augmentation or robustness.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The consistent ~3% bACC/GM gain over CDMAD across CIFAR-10-LT, CIFAR-100-LT, and Small-ImageNet-127 at multiple imbalance levels, combined with plug-and-play compatibility with FixMatch and ReMixMatch, makes this a practical drop-in improvement for long-tailed SSL practitioners. The R2C FFT acceleration (≥50% speedup) addresses the main computational concern, making the method scalable. Open questions include how well the method generalizes when test-time amplitude statistics diverge substantially from training data (domain shift), whether the approach transfers to non-image modalities, and whether the batch-averaged amplitude is sufficient or whether per-sample or per-class amplitude modeling could yield further gains under extreme distribution mismatch.
