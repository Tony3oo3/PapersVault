## Problem

Diffusion model sampling is vulnerable to hallucinations—unrealistic or anatomically implausible outputs—caused by inaccurate score approximation in low-density regions. Existing mitigation strategies (trajectory filtering, local diffusion, early stopping, fine-tuning) address isolated aspects of the problem, but no unified, retraining-free method simultaneously detects and corrects hallucinations during the sampling process itself.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250869/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

RODS operates as a plug-and-play module on top of a pre-trained EDM VE-ODE model (40 sampling steps), requiring no retraining or architectural modification. It takes standard Gaussian noise as input and produces RGB images (128×128 for 11k-hands, native resolution for AFHQv2/FFHQ). At each sampling step, the method consumes the current sample x_t and the score network output to compute a curvature index H(x) over a local ℓ₂-ball of radius ρ; if H(x) exceeds a dataset-specific threshold ε, a robust correction is applied using an additional forward pass at the perturbed point x_t + δ_i.

## Method

RODS reinterprets diffusion sampling as a continuation method in numerical optimization, proving that the noise level t plays the role of a regularization parameter that gradually decreases from a smooth landscape to the sharp target distribution (Theorem 1), and that each Euler step is equivalent to a proximal (trust-region) update (Theorem 2). Building on this view, RODS introduces two components: (1) a curvature-change detection index H(x) that measures rapid fluctuations in the score-field magnitude within a local neighborhood of radius ρ, flagging high-risk regions where the learned score is unreliable; and (2) a robust update step that, upon detection, solves a min-max robust optimization problem to identify the worst-case perturbation direction δ_i (via Sharpness-Aware Sampling on function values or Curvature-Aware Sampling on gradient norms) and adjusts the sampling direction at that worst-case point to maintain descent under local uncertainty. The method requires no retraining, adds minimal inference overhead (one extra network evaluation per flagged step), and can be restricted to critical mid-trajectory steps for further efficiency.

## Results

On the 11k-hands dataset, RODS-CAS reduces the hallucination rate from 19.4% (EDM-Euler) to 14.3%, achieves a 26.3% correction rate, and introduces 0.0% new hallucinations at 4.82 s inference time. On generation quality (Table 1), RODS-CAS preserves diversity (Inception-Var and DreamSim within ~1–2% of baselines) while FID increases modestly (e.g., 16.68 vs. 14.57 on 11k-hands, 16.08 vs. 13.36 on AFHQv2). On AFHQv2, RODS-CAS detects 7 of 8 labeled hallucinations and improves 7 additional unlabeled cases without degrading any outputs; on FFHQ it detects 29 of 40 labeled hallucinations and corrects 9 cases with zero degradation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RODS-CAS is compared against three baselines: EDM-Euler (40 steps), EDM-Heun (40 steps), and EDM-Euler with 200 steps. On the core hallucination task, RODS-CAS achieves the lowest hallucination rate (14.3% vs. 19.4%, 18.0%, and 16.9%) and the highest correction rate (26.3% vs. N/A, 17.7%, and 18.9%) while being the only method that introduces zero new hallucinations (vs. 2.5% for Heun and 1.4% for Euler-200). Inference time is 4.82 s, faster than both EDM-Heun (5.38 s) and Euler-200 (13.76 s), though slower than plain EDM-Euler (2.71 s). On generation quality (Table 1), RODS-CAS trades a modest FID increase (e.g., +2.11 on 11k-hands, +2.72 on AFHQv2) for the hallucination reduction, while Inception-Var and DreamSim diversity metrics remain within ~1–2% of the baselines, confirming that the quality cost is limited.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reframes diffusion sampling through the lens of numerical continuation methods, proving a formal equivalence (Theorem 1) between the PF-ODE trajectory and a sequence of progressively de-regularized optimization subproblems. It then imports robust optimization (RO) principles—specifically min-max formulations over local perturbation sets—to construct a plug-and-play sampler (RODS) that detects high-curvature regions in the score field and applies corrective updates. This departs from prior hallucination-mitigation strategies (trajectory variance filtering, local diffusion inpainting, early stopping, fine-tuning with structure-aware losses) by operating entirely at the sampling level without retraining, and by using spatial curvature detection rather than temporal prediction instability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RODS offers a training-free, low-overhead mechanism to reduce hallucinations in diffusion models, which is particularly relevant for high-stakes applications like medical imaging where false positives can have serious consequences. However, the FID degradation observed across all three datasets (e.g., FFHQ: 20.48→22.73) and the modest ~25% correction rate limit its practical impact, and the method has only been validated on unconditional VE-ODE sampling with 40 steps. Open questions include extension to VP/latent-space diffusion, conditional and text-to-image settings, and whether the curvature-based detection can be made more precise for targeted correction rather than global trajectory adjustment.
