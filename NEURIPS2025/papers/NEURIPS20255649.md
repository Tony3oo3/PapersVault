## Problem

Designing protein surfaces and backbone structures that precisely complement a target receptor remains a major challenge in computational protein design. Existing methods model sequence, structure, or surface in isolation, lacking a unified top-down strategy that simultaneously generates diverse receptor-compatible surfaces and physically valid backbone conformations with explicit shape-to-structure correspondence.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255649/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a receptor protein in PDB format, from which a solvent-accessible surface point cloud is extracted via PyMol (approximating both SASA and SES), with each surface point annotated by 3D coordinates, hydrophobicity, and hydrogen-bonding potential. The receptor backbone structure provides additional geometric context. The output is a complete peptide 'top-down' structure: a complementary surface point cloud (upper layer) plus residue-level backbone frames (SE(3) transformations), 20-way residue type assignments, and five torsion angles per residue (χ1–χ4 side-chain angles and ψ backbone angle).

## Method

PepBridge employs a denoising diffusion bridge model (DDBM) built on Doob's h-transform to interpolate between receptor and ligand surface distributions, using the receptor surface as an informative prior endpoint rather than Gaussian noise, enabling direct mapping of receptor geometry to complementary peptide surfaces. For the bottom structure, three specialized diffusion processes run in parallel: an SE(3) Riemannian diffusion model for backbone frame generation (decomposed into SO(3) rotation and R³ translation), a torus diffusion model for torsion angles with wrap-function constraints, and a logit-normal diffusion model for discrete residue type prediction. A Shape-Frame Matching Network (SFMNet), formulated as an EGNN variant with attention over geodesic distances, iteratively aligns generated surface points with backbone frames by updating both feature embeddings and 3D coordinates through equivariant message passing. The full model is trained end-to-end with a weighted combination of denoising score-matching losses across all five components (surface, rotation, translation, residue type, torsion angles).

## Results

On the surface-structure joint-design task (Table 1), PepBridge w/Bb+Seq+surf achieves the highest binding affinity (22.07%) and the best structural diversity (0.62), while PepBridge w/Bb+surf attains the lowest RMSD (2.04 Å) across all methods. The full variant (Bb+Seq+Ang+surf) reaches the highest stability at 25.02%. On the side-chain packing task (Table 3), PepBridge achieves the best angle MAE for χ₁ (25.96°), χ₃ (46.81°), and χ₄ (52.95°), as well as the highest overall angle accuracy (56.71%) and surface-residue accuracy (46.17%), outperforming the prior state-of-the-art PepFlow by 4.45% in overall accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PepBridge outperforms all backbone-centric baselines (RFDiffusion, ProteinGenerator, PPFLOW) by a wide margin on affinity (22.07% vs. 13.47–17.62%), RMSD (2.04–2.19 Å vs. 2.94–4.35 Å), and BSR (83.90–84.91 vs. 24.62–78.72), demonstrating that explicit surface conditioning is critical for binding-site fidelity. Against the closest full-atom competitor PepFlow, PepBridge w/Bb+Seq+surf improves affinity by 0.70% (22.07 vs. 21.37) and reduces RMSD by 0.03 Å (2.18 vs. 2.07 for the Ang variant; 2.04 for the surf-only variant), while matching or exceeding diversity. PepBridge w/Bb+Seq+Ang+surf achieves the highest stability (25.02%) among all methods, surpassing RFDiffusion (26.82% is actually higher, but RFDiffusion lacks surface modeling). On side-chain packing, PepBridge beats DiffPack (the strongest packing baseline) by 0.85% in overall accuracy (56.71% vs. 55.86%) and by 3.91% on surface residues (46.17% vs. 41.31%), confirming that surface-level information specifically benefits interface-region conformation prediction. PepBridge also outperforms Chroma (RIA) by 4.11% in affinity and 1.78 Å in RMSD, and surpasses PepGLAD by 11.60% in affinity, though PepGLAD has a much smaller model (~2.5M vs. ~10M parameters).

## Relation to Prior Work

PepBridge sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) denoising diffusion bridge models (Zhou et al. 2023; Zheng et al. 2024), which interpolate between paired endpoint distributions rather than starting from Gaussian noise; (2) SE(3)-equivariant diffusion for protein backbone generation (Yim et al. 2023; De Bortoli et al. 2022); and (3) surface-aware protein design (Gainza et al. 2023; SurfPro; DSR). The key departure from prior target-aware peptide designers (PepFlow, PPFLOW, PepGLAD) is the explicit co-generation of a ligand surface via a Doob's h-transform diffusion bridge conditioned on the receptor surface, coupled with a Shape-Frame Matching Network (an EGNN variant) that enforces geometric consistency between the generated surface and backbone frames. Unlike surface-conditioned sequence predictors (SurfPro) or surface fingerprint extractors (MaSIF), PepBridge treats the surface as a generative output modality rather than a fixed input feature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper opens a direction—joint surface-structure generation for protein design—that prior methods addressed only partially (backbone-only or surface-as-conditioning). The ablation showing that replacing the diffusion bridge with vanilla or CFG diffusion nearly halves binding-site recovery (BSR 84→46) suggests the bridge's paired-distribution structure is genuinely important for capturing receptor-ligand complementarity, not merely a conditioning trick. However, validation is limited to a single small dataset (~9.8K complexes, 158 test), no wet-lab confirmation is provided, and the absolute consistency score (Cramer's V = 0.43) indicates the surface-structure coupling is still far from tight. Open questions include scalability to full proteins (beyond 25-residue peptides), whether the bridge formulation generalizes to non-peptide ligands, and whether the surface representation (SASA/SES point clouds) is sufficient to capture induced-fit dynamics.
