## Problem

Constrained sampling with large pre-trained diffusion models (e.g., inpainting, super-resolution) is either slow and memory-intensive when backpropagating the constraint through the denoiser, or fails to capture long-range pixel correlations when avoiding backpropagation entirely. The paper asks whether one can avoid backpropagation through the denoiser weights while still propagating the conditioning signal to distant pixels.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251609/cropped_page1_idx4.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates in the VAE latent space of Stable Diffusion, receiving task-specific inputs: a partially masked image (10–20% or 25% of pixels) for inpainting, a downsampled image for 8× super-resolution, a reference style image plus text prompt for style transfer, or a segmentation mask plus text prompt for mask-guided generation. The constraint is defined on the final clean image x_0, but the optimization update is computed in the noisy latent x_t at each DDIM step. For inpainting, the mask is applied in pixel space, encoded via the VAE, and a 3×3 dilation is applied to handle VAE boundary artifacts; for super-resolution, the constraint must be backpropagated through the VAE decoder since downsampling in pixel space is non-linear in latent space.

## Method

The core contribution is replacing the gradient descent direction J^T e (computed via backpropagation) with an approximate Newton direction J e, justified by the inexact Newton framework where the residual (I − λJ²)e is strictly reduced when the spectral norm of I − λJ² is below 1. The key insight is that for denoising diffusion models, the input and output are related by additive Gaussian noise, making the Jacobian approximately symmetric so that J itself serves as a valid approximation of J^{-1}. Practically, J e is computed via a finite-difference approximation requiring only two forward passes through the denoiser (one at x_t, one at x_t + δe), eliminating all backpropagation through the denoiser. For non-linear constraints, the algorithm combines this Newton direction for the denoiser with standard gradient descent (J_f^T) for the constraint function f, backpropagating only through the cheaper constraint network rather than the full denoiser. The algorithm interleaves K constraint optimization steps (with linearly decreasing learning rate) at each of the 20 DDIM denoising steps, achieving 15-second inference for inpainting versus 6–30 minutes for baselines.

## Results

On box inpainting (25% of image), the proposed method achieves PSNR 18.30, LPIPS 0.30, and FID 42.01 in 15 seconds, outperforming all training-free baselines (LDPS: FID 76.32 in 6 min; PSLD: FID 74.02 in 8 min; FreeDoM: FID 55.68 in 1 min) and approaching the fine-tuned SD-Inpaint upper bound (FID 32.93). On free-form inpainting, the method achieves FID 19.39 (vs. 32.82 for P2L, 46.72 for LDPS) in 15 seconds versus 30 minutes for P2L. On 8× super-resolution with text captions, the method reaches PSNR 24.95 and FID 44.74 in 1 minute, surpassing P2L (PSNR 23.38, FID 51.81, 30 min). For style-guided generation, the method achieves the lowest style score (310.96) while maintaining competitive CLIP text similarity (24.57) in 45 seconds.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method wins on all metrics against LDPS [5], PSLD [28], and FreeDoM [44] on box inpainting, with the largest gap in FID (42.01 vs. 76.32/74.02/55.68) and a 24–32× speedup over the backpropagation-based methods. Against P2L [6] on free-form inpainting, the method achieves a substantially lower FID (19.39 vs. 32.82) in 15 s vs. 30 min, though P2L retains a slight edge in PSNR (21.99 vs. 21.73); on 8× super-resolution, adding text captions to the proposed method surpasses P2L in both PSNR (24.95 vs. 23.38) and FID (44.74 vs. 51.81). Against MPGD [13] on style-guided generation, the method achieves a lower style score (310.96 vs. 441.00) in comparable time (45 s vs. 50 s), while MPGD's approach of only modifying x̂₀ fails to propagate constraints to distant pixels. On mask-guided generation, the method trades a small mIoU deficit (0.42 vs. 0.47 for MPGD ρ=1) for a much better CLIP-FID (59.79 vs. 77.11), indicating more realistic images. The fine-tuned SD-Inpaint [27] remains the upper bound (FID 32.93), but the proposed method is the closest training-free approach, closing most of the gap. The plain DDIM [33] baseline without constraints performs worst on all constrained tasks, confirming the necessity of the constraint-enforcement mechanism.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of training-free constrained sampling in pre-trained diffusion models (LDPS/PSLD, FreeDoM, P2L, MPGD) by reframing the per-step update from a gradient descent direction (J^T e, computed via backpropagation) to an inexact Newton direction (J^{-1} e, approximated as J e via two forward passes). The key conceptual departure is the observation that the denoiser's input and output are related by additive Gaussian noise, making the Jacobian a reasonable proxy for its own inverse — a property absent in general optimization. Unlike MPGD, which avoids backpropagation but fails to propagate constraints to distant pixels, this method maintains long-range consistency while eliminating the expensive backward pass through the denoiser.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 15-second inference time (vs. 6–30 minutes for backpropagation-based baselines) at comparable or superior quality makes constrained diffusion sampling practical for interactive and large-scale applications. The empirical finding that trained denoisers do not have symmetric Jacobians (contrary to the theoretical optimum) explains why the Newton and gradient-descent directions are genuinely different and why the proposed update can outperform backpropagation-based methods. Limitations include degraded performance under high classifier-free guidance (w>10) and reduced benefit with distilled few-step models, leaving open whether the J ≈ J^{-1} approximation generalizes to video, 3D, or other modalities.
