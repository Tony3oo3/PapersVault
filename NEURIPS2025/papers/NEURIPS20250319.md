## Problem

Border basis computation for zero-dimensional polynomial systems is dominated by redundant reduction steps in the L-stable-span phase, where most expansion candidates vanish after reduction but are only identified in hindsight. Prior deep learning approaches to symbolic computation (e.g., predicting Gröbner bases directly) sacrifice output correctness, requiring full verification that nullifies efficiency gains. This paper introduces the first DL-augmented border basis algorithm that accelerates computation while preserving exact output guarantees.

## Input / Output

The oracle receives a tokenized pair (L, V) where L is the computational universe (a set of monomials up to degree d) and V is the current generator set with mutually distinct leading terms; each polynomial in V is truncated to its l leading terms to reduce sequence length. The output is a subset S ⊆ {x₁,…,xₙ} × V of (variable, leading-term) pairs identifying which of the |V|·n full expansion candidates x_j·v will successfully extend the basis. A custom monomial embedding maps each monomial (coefficient + exponent vector + follow-up token) to a single d-dimensional vector, reducing the token count for an n-variate polynomial by a factor of O(n) compared to standard infix tokenization.

## Method

A standard encoder-decoder Transformer (6 layers, 8 heads, d_model=512) is trained via supervised learning to predict successful expansion pairs, with training data generated by a three-stage pipeline: (1) sampling diverse order ideals via a cell-splitting algorithm, (2) constructing border bases of vanishing ideals through nullspace computation of an evaluation matrix, and (3) applying a generalized ideal-invariant generator transform (Theorem 4.5) to convert each border basis into a non-basis generating set with r > n polynomials. The oracle is invoked at most k times exclusively in the final stage of BBA (triggered when the relative border gap |V|/|L| exceeds a threshold), after which the algorithm reverts to standard full expansion, guaranteeing termination and correctness (Theorem 3.1). An orthogonal Fast Gaussian Elimination kernel using a balanced search tree for the active reducer set provides an additional ~10× speedup in the BasisExtension step, compounding with the oracle's reduction of unnecessary candidates.

## Results

On zero-dimensional polynomial systems over F₃₁ with n=5 variables (total degree ≤ 4), OBBA reduces wall-clock runtime from 7.60 s (IBBA) to 2.58 s (~3× speedup), and OBBA+FGE further reduces it to 0.60 s (~12.7× over IBBA, ~19× over classical BBA at 11.44 s). The Transformer oracle achieves 95.1% F1 score in predicting successful expansion directions (n=5, l=5) and 99.6% accuracy in identifying algorithm termination. Out-of-distribution, the oracle generalizes to degree-8 systems (trained only on degree-2), achieving up to 1.8× speedup for n=4 and reducing n=5 runtime from 101.22 s (IBBA) to 5.51 s (OBBA+FGE), an ~18× improvement on problems an order of magnitude harder than training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two baseline algorithms from the border basis literature: the classical BBA (Kehrein & Kreuzer, 2006) and the incremental variant IBBA. OBBA consistently outperforms IBBA across all variable counts, with the largest gain at n=5 where runtime drops from 7.60 s to 2.58 s (~3×). Adding the orthogonal Fast Gaussian Elimination (FGE) kernel compounds the gains: OBBA+FGE reaches 0.60 s at n=5, a ~12.7× improvement over IBBA and ~19× over classical BBA (11.44 s). Notably, none of the oracle-guided runs required reverting to the full standard expansion, confirming the oracle's predictions were correct in all tested cases. On out-of-distribution higher-degree systems (degree 4 and 8, trained only on degree 2), OBBA still achieves up to 1.8× speedup over IBBA for n=4, and OBBA+FGE reduces the hardest n=5 instances from 101.22 s to 5.51 s (~18×), demonstrating strong generalization beyond the training distribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Border Basis Algorithm (BBA) of Kehrein & Kreuzer by inserting a Transformer oracle into the L-stable-span step, following the 'Algorithms with Predictions' paradigm where a learned component guides heuristic decisions without sacrificing correctness. It departs from prior ML-for-symbolic-computation work (Peifer et al. 2020, Kera et al. 2024) in two key ways: it uses supervised learning rather than reinforcement learning (exploiting the degree-by-degree structure of border bases), and it provides output-certified guarantees via a bounded oracle budget with fallback to standard BBA. The ideal-invariant generator transform generalizes the sufficient condition from Kera et al. (2024) to the case |G| >> n, and the monomial embedding is a new tokenization scheme tailored to algebraic computations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first DL-augmented polynomial system solver with full correctness guarantees, making it a practical drop-in enhancement for computer algebra systems rather than a speculative prediction model. The out-of-distribution generalization (trained on degree-2 systems, tested on degree-8) suggests the oracle captures structural properties of border basis computation rather than memorizing specific instances. Open questions include extension to positive-dimensional ideals, infinite fields, and scaling to larger systems; the authors note that finite-field zero-dimensional cases still cover many practical applications via modular reduction and field-equation tricks.
