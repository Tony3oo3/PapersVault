## Problem

Estimating relative camera pose between image pairs with small or no overlap remains a critical unsolved challenge in 3D vision, as the scarcity of reliable feature correspondences causes existing pipelines to fail. While InterPose addressed this by synthesizing intermediate frames via video interpolation and selecting them with a self-consistency score, the generated mid-sequence frames suffer from blurriness and geometric drift, and the statistical selection is slow and not explicitly aligned with pose estimation objectives.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of RGB images (start and end frames) with small or no overlap, resized and center-cropped to 512×320 to match DynamiCrafter's input resolution, and outputs the relative camera pose (rotation and translation) between them. The pipeline is entirely training-free, relying on pre-trained video generation and pose estimation models. Internally, it generates 16 interpolated frames via DynamiCrafter, refines them into a 25-frame sequence via ViewCrafter, and selects the top k=6 frames (excluding the input pair) using ORB-based RANSAC inlier scoring before feeding them to DUSt3R for final pose recovery.

## Method

PoseCrafter is a training-free framework with two components: Hybrid Video Generation (HVG) and Feature Matching Selector (FMS). In HVG, DynamiCrafter first interpolates a coarse 16-frame video from the input pair; only the four frames nearest the endpoints {I₀, I₁, I_{T-1}, I_T} are retained as 'relay' frames (validated in ablation to minimize rotation error). DUSt3R estimates their poses, a smooth camera trajectory is interpolated via slerp on SO(3) and linear interpolation in ℝ³, and ViewCrafter then synthesizes high-fidelity 25-frame sequences conditioned on this trajectory, correcting mid-sequence blur and structural drift. In FMS, each synthesized frame is scored by the sum of RANSAC inlier counts from ORB feature matching against both input frames, and the top k=6 frames exceeding a threshold are selected for final pose estimation—replacing InterPose's expensive multi-clip self-consistency scoring with a single deterministic feature-matching pass.

## Results

On Cambridge Landmarks with 65°–90° yaw change (the most challenging setting), PoseCrafter reduces mean rotation error (MRE) from 51.24° (DUSt3R) to 29.02°, and increases R@30° from 51.67% to 78.33%, while AUC₃₀ rises from 37.93 to 60.46. Compared to the closest baseline InterPose†, PoseCrafter achieves a 9.85° MRE reduction and >10% absolute gain in R@30° on the same setting. On NAVI (object-centric, 50°–90°), PoseCrafter attains MRE 5.97° and AUC₃₀ 83.98, outperforming DUSt3R (7.30°, 82.37) and InterPose† (6.61°, 82.80). The method is training-free and runs pose estimation in 0.18 min versus 20.29 min for InterPose†.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PoseCrafter consistently outperforms all four baselines across all four benchmarks. Against DUSt3R (direct pair input), the improvement is most dramatic on small/no-overlap data: on Cambridge Landmarks 65°–90°, MRE drops from 51.24° to 29.02° and R@30° rises from 51.67% to 78.33%. Against InterPose† (the closest prior method using DynamiCrafter + self-consistency scoring), PoseCrafter wins on every metric on every dataset; the gap is largest on Cambridge Landmarks 65°–90° (MRE 29.02° vs. 38.87°) and ScanNet 65°–90° (MRE 17.02° vs. 28.22°). In the appendix, PoseCrafter also improves over MASt3R (e.g., Cambridge Landmarks MRE 41.02° vs. 51.61°) and VGGT (MRE 17.88° vs. 20.17°), confirming generality across pose-estimation backbones. The ablation in Table 7 further shows that replacing InterPose's self-consistency score with FMS on the same DynamiCrafter video yields MRE 13.02° vs. 13.60°, isolating the contribution of the selection strategy.

## Relation to Prior Work

PoseCrafter directly extends the InterPose pipeline, which first demonstrated that video-interpolated intermediate frames can aid pose estimation under small/no overlap. The key departure is a two-stage Hybrid Video Generation (HVG) that couples DynamiCrafter (motion-driven interpolation) with ViewCrafter (pose-conditioned novel view synthesis) to correct mid-sequence blur and geometric drift, replacing InterPose's single-model interpolation. The Feature Matching Selector (FMS) replaces InterPose's statistical self-consistency scoring with a deterministic RANSAC-inlier-based criterion, and the entire framework is training-free, building on pre-trained DUSt3R as the pose estimation backbone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Extreme pose estimation (small or no overlap) remains a practical bottleneck for autonomous navigation, robotics, and AR, and this paper shows that a training-free hybrid generative pipeline can reduce mean rotation error by roughly 10° on the hardest yaw ranges while cutting pose-estimation runtime from ~20 min to under 1 min. Open questions include handling severe illumination differences (which degrade both DynamiCrafter and ViewCrafter outputs) and texture-poor scenes that defeat feature matching, as well as whether the relay-frame strategy generalizes to dynamic or non-rigid scenes. The finding that a simple ORB-based selector outperforms learned matchers (LoFTR, RoMa, SuperPoint) for frame selection is a useful practical insight for the community.
