## Problem

Gradient-based training data attribution methods (influence functions, unrolled differentiation) require computing inverse Hessian-vector products (iHVPs), which are expensive to approximate. The two dominant approaches—stochastic Neumann series iterations (SNI/LiSSA) and EKFAC—each have a critical weakness: SNI is unbiased but requires thousands of iterations and is notoriously difficult to tune, while EKFAC is fast but produces a biased approximation that cannot be improved by allocating more compute. No prior method combines the asymptotic accuracy of iterative solvers with the efficiency of EKFAC's compact eigendecomposition.

## Input / Output

Given a trained model's final parameters θ^s, the full training dataset D, and a query point z_q with a measurement function f_{z_q} (e.g., correct-class margin or mean absolute error), the method produces a TDA score τ(z_m, z_q, D) for every training example z_m, quantifying the impact of removing z_m on f_{z_q}. The EKFAC eigendecomposition is computed once per model and shared across all queries; the iterative iHVP component then adds only a few hundred mini-batch gradient steps per query on top of the EKFAC cost. The iHVP itself is the vector ∇_θ f_{z_q}(θ^s)^T (G + λI)^{-1}, whose dot product with each training example's loss gradient yields the attribution score.

## Method

ASTRA (EKFAC-Accelerated Neumann Series Iterations for TRaining Data Attribution) preconditions the SNI update rule with the EKFAC-approximated Fisher Information Matrix P = F_EKFAC, yielding the update θ_{k+1} ← θ_k − α(P + λ̃I)^{−1}(G̃_k + λI)θ_k + α(P + λ̃I)^{−1}∇_θ f_{z_q}(θ^s). The key insight is that EKFAC's eigendecomposition—already required to compute EKFAC-IF or EKFAC-SOURCE—can be repurposed as a preconditioner at negligible additional cost, since its statistics are stored compactly per layer and shared across all queries. Initializing θ_0 = 0 with α = 1 and λ̃ = λ reproduces the EKFAC-IF prediction exactly after one step, so ASTRA strictly extends EKFAC: further iterations refine the iHVP toward the true (G + λI)^{−1}v, recovering the unbiased estimator that SNI targets. In practice, ASTRA converges in fewer than 200 iterations (vs. thousands for SNI) with a fixed learning rate of 10^{−2} across all settings, eliminating the difficult hyperparameter tuning that plagues vanilla SNI. The same preconditioning strategy extends to unrolled differentiation via ASTRA-SOURCE, where EKFAC decompositions serve as preconditioners for the L segment-wise iHVPs in SOURCE's finite series approximation.

## Results

The primary metric is the Linear Datamodeling Score (LDS), a Spearman correlation between TDA predictions and ground-truth counterfactual retraining. ASTRA-IF achieves a Spearman correlation of ~0.5 with ground-truth retraining across many settings, and ensembling raises this to ~0.6. The most striking improvement is on CIFAR-10 with ResNet-9 (convolutional architecture), where ensembled ASTRA-IF reaches LDS 0.6 versus EKFAC-IF's 0.25. ASTRA converges in fewer than 200 iterations with a fixed learning rate of 10⁻², whereas vanilla SNI requires thousands of iterations and is highly sensitive to learning rate selection.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ASTRA-IF outperforms EKFAC-IF (Grosse et al., 2023) in almost all experimental settings as measured by average LDS, with the largest gap on CIFAR-10/ResNet-9 where ensembled ASTRA-IF reaches 0.6 versus EKFAC-IF's 0.25, attributed to EKFAC's additional simplifying assumptions for convolution layers. ASTRA-SOURCE similarly beats EKFAC-SOURCE (Bae et al., 2024) in most settings. Both ASTRA-IF and ASTRA-SOURCE also outperform TracIn (Pruthi et al., 2020) and TRAK (Park et al., 2023) in most settings. Compared to vanilla SNI/LiSSA (Agarwal et al., 2017), ASTRA converges in fewer than 200 iterations versus thousands for SNI, and the EKFAC preconditioning eliminates the notorious learning-rate sensitivity (ASTRA uses 10⁻² uniformly, while SNI requires different rates per dataset: 1, 0.1, 0.01, 0.1 for MNIST, FashionMNIST, CIFAR-10, and UCI Concrete respectively). Ensembling provides a larger performance boost for ASTRA than for EKFAC-based methods, suggesting that a more accurate iHVP approximation amplifies the benefits of averaging over multiple training seeds.

## Relation to Prior Work

ASTRA sits at the intersection of two well-established iHVP computation strategies for training data attribution: EKFAC (George et al., 2018), a parametric block-diagonal approximation of the Fisher Information Matrix, and stochastic Neumann series iterations (LiSSA, Agarwal et al., 2017), an iterative method that converges to the exact iHVP but requires thousands of steps and careful tuning. Prior TDA work (Grosse et al., 2023; Bae et al., 2024) treated these as competing alternatives—EKFAC for speed, SNI for accuracy—without combining them. The paper's key departure is repurposing the EKFAC eigendecomposition (already computed for EKFAC-IF or EKFAC-SOURCE) as a preconditioner for SNI, yielding a method that inherits EKFAC's compact structure while retaining SNI's asymptotic exactness. This preconditioned-SNI formulation is applied to both influence functions (ASTRA-IF) and unrolled differentiation via SOURCE (ASTRA-SOURCE), extending the iHVP solver improvement across the two main gradient-based TDA families.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that iHVP approximation quality is a primary bottleneck for TDA performance, and that a simple preconditioning step—leveraging computation already required by EKFAC-based methods—can substantially close the gap between fast approximate and slow exact solvers. The ablation showing that low-curvature directions are essential for influence function quality (and that truncated Neumann series implicitly damps them) provides a mechanistic explanation for why SNI is hard to tune and why EKFAC underperforms on convolution architectures. Open questions include whether the preconditioning strategy generalizes beyond EKFAC (e.g., to other curvature approximations), whether the low-curvature sensitivity persists at larger scales (e.g., LLMs beyond GPT-2), and whether the approach extends to TDA methods that do not explicitly compute iHVPs. The practical impact is significant for data curation and model interpretability pipelines where TDA is applied at scale.
