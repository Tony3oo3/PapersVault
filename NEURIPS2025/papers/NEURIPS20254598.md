## Problem

While GNNs have been applied to SAT and a limited set of unweighted MaxSAT problems, no GNN architecture has been designed to differentiably solve weighted MaxSAT, and the use of Graph Attention Networks for MaxSAT remains entirely unexplored. Existing continuous MaxSAT solvers (SDP-based Mixing, FourierSAT) struggle with weighted instances and scale poorly on large benchmarks, leaving a gap for a scalable, differentiable approach that handles both unweighted and weighted settings.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a weighted MaxSAT CNF formula encoded as a bipartite variable-clause factor graph, where edge features encode literal polarity, inverse clause arity (prioritizing shorter clauses), and normalized clause weights. A random continuous initial assignment in [0,1] seeds the network, which outputs refined continuous valuations per variable; these are thresholded at 0.5 to yield a binary assignment and a solution cost equal to the total weight of unsatisfied clauses. The unsupervised loss is a weighted mean squared error over clause unsatisfaction, requiring no ground-truth labels.

## Method

SGATs stack blocks of three components: a t-norm layer that computes clause valuations from connected variable valuations via fuzzy conjunction (Gödel, Product, or Lukasiewicz t-norms), an SGAT layer where unsatisfied clauses send polarity-aware update messages to all connected variables in parallel and a multi-head attention mechanism (operating on t-norm-computed messages and edge features) decides which clause requests each variable should prioritize, and a sigmoid-based normalization layer that spreads valuations. This design approximates greedy distributed local search: clauses simultaneously request variable flips proportional to their unsatisfaction, and attention learns a general heuristic for resolving competing requests. Training is fully unsupervised, minimizing the weighted clause-unsatisfaction loss via Adam. The LS-GNN solver wraps the SGAT in iterative continuous optimization with adaptive randomization (binary-masked variable resets triggered by early stopping) to escape local optima, and the architecture achieves a provable 1/2-approximation on unweighted Max-EkSAT with fixed parameters independent of problem size.

## Results

On unweighted MaxSAT (MS) benchmarks (2020–2024), LS-SGAT achieves average incomplete scores of 0.5237–0.6403 at 60s and 0.5289–0.6403 at 300s, outperforming the second-best continuous solver (MIXSAT) by an average margin of 0.055. On weighted MaxSAT (WMS), LS-SGAT scores 0.7587–0.8973 (60s) and 0.7594–0.8835 (300s), exceeding the next-best solver (LS-GAT) by an average of 0.09. SGATs also handle all but one benchmark instance within timeout, whereas classical continuous baselines (FourierSAT, MIXSAT, Mixing) exceed the time limit on tens to hundreds of instances. In the architecture comparison (Figure 4), SGATs are the only model that successfully learns from practical MaxSAT instances, while NeuroSAT, GGNN, and MS-ESFG fail to converge.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LS-SGAT dominates all continuous-optimization baselines across every benchmark year and both unweighted/weighted tracks: it beats MIXSAT (Wang 2017) by 0.03–0.12 on MS and outperforms FourierSAT (Kyrillidis 2020) by 0.31–0.77 on WMS, where FourierSAT is the only prior continuous method supporting weights. Against the SDP-based Mixing method (Wang 2019), LS-SGAT gains 0.14–0.55 on MS. The paper's own GAT-based variant (LS-GAT) serves as an ablation baseline and is consistently outperformed by LS-SGAT (e.g., 0.6202 vs. 0.4839 on MS 2022 at 60s), confirming the benefit of t-norm-based attention over standard GAT. In the architecture comparison (Figure 4), SGATs are the only model that converges on practical MaxSAT instances, while NeuroSAT (Selsam 2019), GGNN (Li 2016), and MS-ESFG (Liu 2021) all fail to learn. Finally, when SGAT predictions are used as initialization heuristics for SOTA SLS solvers (SPB, NuWLS, BandHS, SATLike3.0), all four solvers improve by 0.005–0.015 in incomplete score, with NuWLS+SLS-SGAT reaching 0.9327 on WMS 2022 at 300s.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Graph Attention Networks (GATs) by replacing the standard attention computation with t-norm-based message passing, creating SGATs specifically structured to approximate greedy distributed local search for MaxSAT. It departs from prior GNN-based MaxSAT work (Liu et al., 2021), which used generic GNNs on synthetic instances and could not handle weighted clauses, by introducing the first GNN architecture for weighted MaxSAT with a principled t-norm loss. The factor graph representation and unsupervised training paradigm follow established conventions from NeuroSAT and prior SAT-GNN work, while the LS-GNN local search wrapper is a novel algorithmic contribution that can be paired with any GNN backbone. The continuous optimization baselines (FourierSAT, Mixing, MIXSAT) represent the prior SOTA that SGATs now surpass.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SGATs provide a lightweight (898 parameters), differentiable MaxSAT solver that achieves state-of-the-art performance among continuous approaches across all MaxSAT Evaluation benchmarks from 2020–2024, with a clear scalability advantage over both neural and classical continuous solvers. The ability to handle weighted MaxSAT differentiably opens the door to integrating MaxSAT layers into end-to-end neuro-symbolic pipelines (e.g., autonomous driving, NLP constraint tasks) where prior continuous methods could not. Key open questions include extending the framework to partial MaxSAT (hard clauses), providing theoretical guarantees for the weighted setting, and understanding whether the t-norm attention design generalizes to other combinatorial optimization problems beyond MaxSAT.
