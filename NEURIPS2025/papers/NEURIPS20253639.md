## Problem

Process Reward Models (PRMs) guide beam search effectively in short CoT reasoning, yet PRM-guided beam search fails to consistently outperform PRM-free baselines like Majority@N on Long CoT models. The paper identifies the root cause as 'step quality degradation'—the expected step quality follows a concave (unimodal or monotonically declining) trajectory during beam search, meaning deeper steps are often suboptimal—and asks how to make PRM-guided search effective under Long CoT settings.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253639/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a math problem (from AMC2023, AIME2024, or AIME2025) as input and produces a final numeric answer selected via Weighted Best of N over beam search trajectories. During search, the LLM policy generates B×E candidate reasoning steps per iteration, each scored by a Hard PRM; the mean PRM reward at each step is z-normalized over all preceding steps to produce a stopping signal. The only tunable hyperparameter is the threshold λ (set to −0.6 for 1.5B and 0 for 7B models), which controls stopping sensitivity.

## Method

ZGES (Z-Score Guided Early Stopping) terminates PRM-guided beam search at the detected quality peak by monitoring the local z-score of the mean PRM reward across all candidates at each step; when this z-score drops below threshold λ, search halts and the LLM directly decodes the remaining trajectory from the peak step. The theoretical foundation (Proposition 3.8) proves that expected step quality is concave in the discrete sense because the PRM's re-ranking accuracy degrades monotonically as beam search progresses (Assumption 3.2), making the second-order difference of step quality negative. A key enabling result (Lemma 4.1) shows that under the empirically validated linear relationship between mean PRM reward and mean step quality (Pearson r > 0.91), their z-normalized forms coincide exactly, so the z-score of PRM rewards faithfully tracks the z-score of true step quality. This makes the z-score a principled, model-agnostic stopping signal that requires no access to ground-truth step quality during inference.

## Results

On AIME2024 with R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B, ZGES with 16 beams achieves 66.7% accuracy, outperforming standard PRM-guided Beam Search (60.0%) and Majority@16 (52.7%). Across all three benchmarks (AMC2023, AIME2024, AIME2025) and both model scales (1.5B, 7B), ZGES consistently achieves the best accuracy among all methods tested. Notably, standard PRM-guided Beam Search underperforms the PRM-free Majority@N baseline in most Long CoT settings (e.g., 53.3% vs. 58.3% on AIME2024 @64 for the 1.5B Soft PRM), revealing a step quality degradation that ZGES addresses via early stopping. ZGES also reduces PRM calls by 50–66% relative to full Beam Search while maintaining or improving accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Math-Shepherd PRM framework (Wang et al., 2023), which provides both Hard and Soft PRM variants used to guide Beam Search, the paper finds that PRM-guided Beam Search does not consistently outperform the PRM-free Majority@N baseline on Long CoT models. In many settings, Beam Search actually underperforms: for example, on AIME2024 @64 with the 1.5B model, Soft PRM Beam Search scores 53.3% versus 58.3% for Majority@64 (a 15% drop), and Hard PRM Beam Search scores 56.7% versus 58.3%. Against the standard PRM-guided Beam Search procedure from Snell et al. (2024), ZGES achieves clear wins: on AIME2024 with 1.5B, ZGES (16 beams) reaches 66.7% versus 60.0% for standard Beam Search (16 beams), and on AIME2025 with 7B, ZGES reaches 60.0% versus 56.7% for standard Beam Search. ZGES also reduces PRM calls by 50–66% (e.g., 3.27 vs. 9.55 on AIME2024 for 1.5B), making it both more accurate and more computationally efficient than the Snell et al. baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the PRM-guided beam search framework established by Math-Shepherd (Wang et al., 2023) and Snell et al. (2024), which demonstrated that step-level reward models improve test-time search over PRM-free baselines like majority voting. The key departure is showing that this advantage reverses in the Long CoT regime: PRM-guided beam search no longer consistently beats Majority@N, a finding not anticipated by prior work that was validated on shorter CoT models. The paper introduces ZGES, a z-score-based early stopping criterion, as a minimal modification to the standard beam search loop, and provides a theoretical account (concavity of expected step quality) rooted in the progressive degradation of PRM re-ranking ability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The counterintuitive finding that finer-grained PRM guidance can hurt rather than help in Long CoT reasoning challenges a core assumption in test-time compute scaling and redirects attention toward *when* to stop rather than *how* to score. ZGES offers a practical, low-overhead fix (reducing PRM calls by >50% while improving accuracy), but its scope is limited to math benchmarks and two distilled model scales (1.5B, 7B), leaving open whether the step-quality degradation phenomenon and the z-score stopping criterion generalize to other reasoning domains, larger models, or different PRM architectures. The theoretical analysis, while illuminating, relies on strong extreme-case assumptions (near-deterministic transitions) that may not hold for weaker policies.
