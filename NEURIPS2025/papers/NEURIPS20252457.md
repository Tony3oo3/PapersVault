## Problem

Pointmap-based pose-free reconstruction methods like DUSt3R and MonST3R rely on clear RGB visual cues and degrade severely under extreme low-light or fast-motion conditions, where conventional cameras produce underexposed, blurred, or overexposed frames. Although event cameras offer high dynamic range and microsecond temporal resolution that remain informative in these regimes, no prior work has integrated asynchronous event streams into modern transformer-based pointmap reconstruction pipelines for robust dynamic 3D geometry estimation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252457/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes synchronized RGB video frames (H×W×3) and asynchronous event streams (sequences of (x, y, t, p) tuples) within a temporal window; events are voxelized into spatiotemporal grids before encoding. It outputs pairwise dense pointmaps per frame pair, which are jointly optimized into a global 3D point cloud with per-frame camera poses, intrinsics, and depth maps — a unified {X, P, K} representation that directly supports downstream depth estimation, pose tracking, and dynamic reconstruction without separate task-specific heads.

## Method

EAG3R extends the MonST3R ViT encoder-decoder with two coupled innovations. First, a lightweight event perception module: a Retinex-inspired network estimates an illumination map and a spatially varying SNR confidence map from the raw image; a Swin-Transformer event adapter (pretrained self-supervised on ETartanAir) extracts hierarchical event features; cross-attention lets event features query frozen image features at four scales; and an SNR-aware aggregation weights image features by the SNR map and event features by its complement before concatenation, so the network adaptively trusts RGB in well-lit regions and events in dark or noisy ones. Second, an event-based photometric consistency loss is added to MonST3R's global optimization: Harris corners define salient patches where observed brightness increments (polarity-weighted event accumulation) are compared against predicted increments synthesized from image gradients multiplied by motion derived from the current global state estimate; both are L2-normalized to eliminate the unknown sensor contrast scale, yielding a scale-invariant alignment objective. The image encoder remains frozen during training; only the event adapter, DPT heads, and enhancement network are fine-tuned on daylight MVSEC data, and the model generalizes zero-shot to extreme nighttime sequences.

## Results

On the MVSEC nighttime sequences (zero-shot, trained only on daytime data), EAG3R achieves Abs Rel of 0.288, δ<1.25 of 0.533, and RMSE log of 0.371 on Night3, outperforming MonST3R (0.317/0.453/0.418) and DUSt3R (0.463/0.335/0.534). For camera pose estimation on Night1, EAG3R reduces ATE to 0.482 m (vs. 0.559 for MonST3R and 1.474 for DUSt3R) and RPE rot to 0.143° (vs. 0.369 and 2.995° respectively). The method also generalizes to the M3ED robot-dog HDR dataset, achieving ATE of 0.1361 m versus 0.3425 m for MonST3R.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EAG3R consistently outperforms all three baselines across every metric and sequence. Against DUSt3R, the improvement is dramatic: on Night3 depth estimation, EAG3R reduces Abs Rel from 0.463 to 0.288 (−38%) and on Night1 pose estimation, ATE drops from 1.474 m to 0.482 m (−67%). Against MonST3R, EAG3R gains 9% on Abs Rel (0.317→0.288) and 18% on δ<1.25 (0.453→0.533) for Night3 depth, and 14% on ATE (0.559→0.482) for Night1 pose. Against Easi3R_monst3r (the strongest dynamic-scene baseline), EAG3R achieves lower ATE (0.482 vs. 0.540 on Night1) and lower RPE trans (0.201 vs. 0.263), confirming that event-based cues provide complementary information beyond motion-aware masking alone. Notably, even MonST3R fine-tuned on the same daytime data does not close the gap, indicating that the event modality contributes information not recoverable from RGB alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

EAG3R extends the pointmap-based reconstruction paradigm established by DUSt3R and its dynamic extension MonST3R by introducing asynchronous event camera streams as an additional input modality. While prior event-based 3D vision work relied on stereo setups, geometric priors, or NeRF/Gaussian Splatting formulations, this paper is the first to integrate events into the modern transformer-based pointmap pipeline. The SNR-aware fusion mechanism departs from prior low-light enhancement approaches (which treat Retinex as a preprocessing step) by repurposing it as a spatial confidence estimator that adaptively gates RGB versus event features, and the event-based photometric consistency loss provides a new supervisory signal absent from MonST3R's alignment-flow-smoothness objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

EAG3R addresses a practically critical gap: robust 3D perception for autonomous systems operating in extreme low-light or high-dynamic-range conditions where RGB cameras fail. The zero-shot nighttime generalization (trained on daytime, tested on night) is a strong property for deployment, though the method's dependence on real, temporally aligned event streams limits immediate applicability given the scarcity of such datasets. Open questions include whether the approach scales to longer sequences without the sliding-window approximation, whether synthetic event generation (V2E) can be made viable with better noise modeling, and whether the SNR-aware fusion generalizes to other sensor pairs beyond RGB+event.
