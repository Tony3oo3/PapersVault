## Problem

Graph anomaly detection models trained on high-homophily structures (caused by selection bias in labeling) rely on homophilic shortcuts and fail to generalize to test data with low homophily under structural distribution shift. This paper addresses the largely unexplored problem of discovering invariant prediction patterns and systematically constructing diverse training environments with varying local structures to handle this shift.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252203/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a node-attribute graph (adjacency matrix A ∈ R^{n×n} and node features X ∈ R^{n×d}) and predicts a binary anomaly probability for each node via its k-hop ego-graph. A key design detail is that the ego-node embedding (from an MLP projector) and the neighborhood structural embedding (from a spectral GNN like BWGNN) are kept in separate but aligned spaces, and the final prediction is a bilinear interaction σ(h_ego^T W h_ne) rather than a simple concatenation, explicitly modeling the discrepancy between the node and its neighborhood.

## Method

HEM combines two modules trained via adversarial iteration. First, an ego-neighborhood disentangled encoder decouples feature representations (ego-node MLP) from structural representations (BWGNN neighborhood aggregation), enabling the bilinear predictor to capture invariant discrepancy patterns rather than entangled homophilic shortcuts. Second, a homophily-aware environment mixup uses a node-wise attention mechanism to continuously re-weight edges based on endpoint features, generating diverse structural environments in a memory-efficient manner (avoiding the OOM issues of methods like EERM that maintain full adjacency matrices). The adversarial training alternates an outer loop (encoder minimizes L + L_AUG) and an inner loop (environment mixup minimizes −L_AUG + L_DIV + L_PER), where the diversity loss maximizes dissimilarity between original and augmented structural embeddings and the PER loss constrains the fraction of perturbed edges to prevent trivial solutions.

## Results

On the three GAD benchmarks (Amazon, Yelp, T-Finance), HEM(+BWGNN) achieves the best AUPRC under structural distribution shift (w/ DS): 74.91 on Amazon, 58.45 on Yelp, and 66.04 on T-Finance, outperforming the strongest baselines by 3.4%, 6.9%, and 1.3% respectively. Under the in-distribution setting (w/o DS), HEM(+GCN) leads on Amazon (93.70) and T-Finance (95.69), while HEM(+BWGNN) leads on Yelp (61.42). All baselines suffer significant AUPRC drops under distribution shift (e.g., GHRN drops ~25% on Amazon, ~33% on T-Finance), whereas HEM maintains consistent performance across both settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HEM(+BWGNN) outperforms all 13 baselines on the w/ DS (out-of-distribution) setting across all three datasets in AUPRC. On Amazon, it beats the closest competitor GAT (72.44) by 2.47 points and BAT (72.26) by 2.65 points. On Yelp, it surpasses GHRN (54.68) by 3.77 points and BAT (54.93) by 3.52 points. On T-Finance, it edges out BernNet (65.21) by 0.83 points. Among OOD-specific baselines, EERM achieves the best w/ DS AUPRC on Amazon (70.94) but runs out of memory on Yelp and T-Finance; V-REx and GroupDRO show no consistent improvement over the BWGNN backbone. GDN, the first method specifically targeting structural distribution shift in GAD, underperforms HEM by 6+ points on Amazon and Yelp w/ DS. The Recall@K results (Table 2) confirm the same ranking, with HEM(+BWGNN) achieving 67.80 (Amazon), 52.63 (Yelp), and 63.12 (T-Finance) under distribution shift.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of graph anomaly detection (GAD) and out-of-distribution generalization, building on the GAD-specific backbone BWGNN and the contrastive ego-neighborhood prediction paradigm from CoLA. It departs from prior OOD-for-graphs methods (EERM, SRGNN, V-REx, GroupDRO) by replacing discrete edge-mask environment construction with a continuous, memory-efficient homophily-aware edge reweighting mechanism, and from GDN (the first work on structural distribution shift in GAD) by shifting from gradient-based invariant feature filtering to an adversarial encoder–environment co-training loop. The ego-neighborhood disentangled encoder repurposes the contrastive prediction idea but explicitly separates feature and structural embeddings to prevent homophilic shortcut learning, a design not present in prior GAD or OOD methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Structural distribution shift due to selection bias is a pervasive and practically important problem in real-world GAD (fraud detection, AML, bot detection), yet prior methods either OOM on large graphs (EERM), require explicit environment labels (V-REx, GroupDRO), or ignore local structural variation (GDN). HEM offers a memory-efficient, label-free alternative that consistently improves OOD performance across three datasets, making it a practical tool for practitioners facing homophily-driven shift. Open questions include whether the homophily-specific formulation generalizes to other structural shift types (e.g., degree distribution, community structure), whether the simplified one-layer theoretical analysis extends to deeper GNNs, and how the method scales to inductive settings and much larger graphs beyond the three moderate-sized benchmarks used here.
