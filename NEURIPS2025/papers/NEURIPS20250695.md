## Problem

While SGD dynamics for single-index and multi-index models on vector inputs are well-characterized, the learning dynamics of attention-based architectures on sequential data remain largely unexplored. Prior work on sequence multi-index models analyzed the empirical risk minimizer or Bayes-optimal estimator but left the population loss landscape and SGD convergence behavior under one-pass training uncharacterized. This paper fills that gap by providing the first rigorous high-dimensional asymptotic analysis of SGD for single-layer tied attention on sequential data.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a sequence of L tokens, each a d-dimensional Gaussian vector (entries N(0, 1/d)), together with a fixed positional encoding matrix P ∈ R^{L×d}, and produces a k-dimensional prediction vector via a reduction map R applied to the L×L softmax attention matrix. The key analytical outputs are closed-form sample complexity bounds (number of gradient steps n as a function of token dimension d) and convergence rates, expressed in terms of the Sequence Information Exponent. The high-dimensional reduction means the full d-dimensional weight dynamics are captured by just L+1 sufficient statistics (e, m) constrained to the unit ball, making the landscape tractable when d ≫ L.

## Method

The paper introduces the Sequence Information Exponent (SIE), a generalization of the classical information exponent that quantifies the lowest-order Hermite tensor in the link function's expansion across all L token positions, and proves it directly determines the SGD sample complexity (O(d) for SIE=1, O(d log²d) for SIE=2, O(d^{SIE−1}) for SIE≥3). The population loss is shown to depend only on two sufficient statistics—semantic overlap m and positional alignment e—reducing the d-dimensional landscape to an (L+1)-dimensional one. The authors then quantify the speedup of tied attention over untied networks, showing the gain scales as L² for SIE=2 targets (exceeding naive parameter-counting predictions), and prove that positional encoding can only decrease the effective flatness index, potentially reducing sample complexity. Finally, they characterize a rich phase diagram where SGD can converge to suboptimal positional minima instead of the global semantic minimum, with a sharp transition at ω_trans ≈ 0.64 in the mixed semantic-positional target.

## Results

The paper establishes that the sample complexity of one-pass spherical SGD on Sequence Single-Index models scales as O(d) for SIE=1, O(d log²d) for SIE=2, and O(d^(SIE−1)) for SIE≥3 (Theorem 1). The tied attention network achieves a speed-up (gain) over the untied fully-connected baseline of L² for SIE=2 targets (e.g., g(z*)=Σ He₂(z*_i)), confirmed numerically with d=1000 and ReLU activation (Figure 3), and L for SIE=1 targets. Positional encoding provably reduces the flatness index (Lemma 1), with a concrete example where SIE drops from 4 to 2, cutting sample complexity from O(d³) to O(d log²d). A phase transition in SGD convergence behavior occurs at ω_trans ≈ 0.64 (for a=1), where the dynamics shift from converging to the semantic global minimum to the positional local minimum.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is between the tied single-layer attention network (the paper's model) and an untied fully-connected network with L×d parameters (L independent weight vectors). For SIE=2 targets (e.g., g(z*)=Σᵢ He₂(z*_i)), the tied network requires ~L² fewer gradient steps to reach weak recovery than the untied network, despite having L× fewer parameters — exceeding the naive parameter-counting prediction of L^(SIE−1)=L. For SIE=1 targets, the gain is L, matching the parameter-counting bound. In pathological cases where C_SIE × (1,…,1)=0 (e.g., g(z*)=z*_1−z*_2), the tied network fails entirely while the untied network still learns, showing the gain can be zero. The second comparison is with/without positional encoding: adding P can reduce the effective flatness index (e.g., from 4 to 2), accelerating weak recovery, but does not change the positions of global minima. The paper also contrasts the attention model against a linear-attention (squared-activation tied) baseline, showing softmax attention achieves the same L-scaling but with a smaller constant for large L (Figure 10).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the information exponent framework of Ben Arous et al. (2021) and the 'escaping mediocrity' analysis of Arnaboldi et al. (2023c, 2024c) from vector-valued single-index models to sequence-valued inputs, introducing the Sequence Information Exponent (SIE) as the natural generalization. It builds directly on the SSI/SMI model class introduced by Cui et al. (2024) and Troiani et al. (2025), who established the connection between tied single-layer attention and sequence multi-index models but left the SGD dynamics uncharacterized. The paper departs from prior work by providing the first rigorous population-loss landscape and ODE-based convergence analysis for these sequence models, and by quantifying how positional encoding and sequence length modify the learning dynamics in ways absent from the classical single-index setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work establishes a rigorous, interpretable framework (the SIE) for predicting the sample complexity of SGD on attention-based sequence models, filling a gap explicitly identified by prior studies that only analyzed the empirical risk minimizer or Bayes-optimal estimator. The finding that positional encoding can reduce the effective SIE (e.g., from 4 to 2) and that tied networks can achieve L² speedup over untied ones (exceeding naive parameter-counting) provides concrete theoretical guidance for architectural design. However, the analysis is confined to a highly simplified setting—single-layer, tied, d_head=1, V=I, iid Gaussian tokens, and even target functions—so extending the SIE framework to multi-layer, multi-head, and non-Gaussian sequence models remains a major open question. The phase diagram showing SGD converging to suboptimal positional minima when the global minimum is semantic also raises practical concerns about training reliability that warrant further investigation.
