## Problem

Analytical dexterous grasp methods ensure stability but are computationally expensive and difficult to adapt to diverse tasks, while generative methods improve efficiency and task integration yet generalize poorly to out-of-distribution objects and novel tasks due to reliance on training data coverage. No existing approach effectively combines the stability of analytical optimization with the efficiency and task adaptability of generative models while maintaining strong generalization to unseen object categories.

## Input / Output

The method takes a target object point cloud (2048 points), a shape template point cloud (2048 points) with its pre-computed contact map, and a free-form textual task description as input. It produces three object-centric maps — a binary contact map, a part map assigning each point to one of 16 predefined hand parts, and a direction map of 3D unit vectors pointing from each object point toward its corresponding hand part center — which are then used to recover the final grasp as k joint angles (e.g., k=24 for ShadowHand) via differentiable forward kinematics optimization.

## Method

The paper reformulates dexterous grasp generation as a contact map transfer problem solved by a conditional diffusion model with a dual-branch architecture: a template branch reconstructs the template's contact map (providing geometric grounding) while a target branch denoises the target contact map, with a bidirectional attention-based adaptation module bridging the two branches to capture shape similarity. A cascaded diffusion framework sequentially generates the contact map, then the part map conditioned on the predicted contact map, then the direction map conditioned on both, ensuring intra-consistency among the three representations. Textual task descriptions are encoded via BERT and injected as conditioning at each diffusion stage. Finally, a robust grasp recovery mechanism estimates per-part joint positions by intersecting direction vectors, filters unreliable points using distance thresholds, and optimizes joint angles by minimizing a composite loss over contact MSE, weighted direction cosine similarity, and hand regularization terms (penetration, joint limits, keypoint proximity).

## Results

On the CapGrasp dataset (task-agnostic setting), the full method (Ours) achieves a grasp Success Rate of 84.65%, Penetration Depth of 1.47 mm, and Contact Coverage of 38.16%, outperforming all analytical, generative, and transfer baselines. In the more challenging task-oriented generalization setting, Ours attains 79.32% SR and 83.60% VLM-assisted Consistency on seen categories, and 74.14% SR and 79.28% Consistency on unseen categories—substantially exceeding the best generative baselines (RealDex: 29.90% SR; DexGYS: 39.16% SR) on unseen categories. Real-world experiments on a humanoid platform yield an average 70% grasp success rate across four object categories and five task specifications without any retraining.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the two analytical baselines, Ours surpasses DFC by +5.67 SR and −1.68 mm Pen., and DexGraspNet by +1.02 SR and −3.05 mm Pen., while also achieving the highest Contact Coverage (38.16% vs. 32.28% and 31.87%). Against generative methods, the advantage is more pronounced: Ours outperforms ContactGen by +11.65 SR and UGG by +14.15 SR, with penetration reduced from 4.11 mm (ContactGen) and 8.08 mm (UGG) to 1.47 mm. The closest competitor is the transfer-based Tink, which achieves the lowest penetration (1.14 mm) but suffers a large SR drop (69.82%) and poor coverage (25.13%) due to sensitivity to shape variation; Ours recovers +14.83 SR and +13.03 Cov. over Tink at only a 0.33 mm penetration cost. In the task-oriented generalization setting (Table 2), Ours dominates on unseen categories: 74.14% SR vs. RealDex (29.90%) and DexGYS (39.16%), with Consistency of 79.28% vs. 70.85% and 68.08% respectively, and Tink is not evaluable on unseen categories at all. The ablation further confirms that removing the adaptation module drops SR from 79.32% to ~38%, and removing the robust optimization drops it to 55.04%, underscoring the contribution of each component.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the DDPM framework (Ho et al., 2020) and the object-centric contact/part/direction map representation from ContactGen (Liu et al., 2023), combined with the generate-then-optimize pipeline from GenDexGrasp (Li et al., 2023). Its key conceptual departure is reframing dexterous grasp generation as a *transfer* problem: rather than directly learning a grasp distribution from object features (as generative methods do) or solving per-object analytical optimization (as analytical methods do), it uses a conditional diffusion model to transfer high-quality template grasps to novel objects within a category. The dual mapping architecture and cascaded diffusion for jointly transferring three interdependent maps are novel architectural contributions that distinguish it from prior transfer methods like Tink, which require per-category SDF learning and cannot generalize to unseen categories.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap: analytical methods produce stable but slow, task-agnostic grasps, while generative methods are fast and task-adaptive but generalize poorly to OOD objects. The transfer-based framing offers a middle path that achieves near-analytical quality (84.65% SR) with generative efficiency and cross-category generalization (74.14% SR on unseen categories without retraining). Limitations include evaluation on a single dataset (CapGrasp), a single robotic hand (ShadowHand), and no error bars across random seeds. Open questions include cross-embodiment generalization to hands with different finger counts, integration with vision-language-action models for closed-loop manipulation, and whether the shape-template requirement limits applicability to highly novel object geometries.
