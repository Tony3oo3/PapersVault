## Problem

In the agnostic setting under instance-targeted poisoning, Hanneke et al. (NeurIPS 2022) showed that deterministic learners can be forced to suffer near-maximal error even under minimal poisoning, leaving open whether randomized learners can achieve meaningful guarantees. This paper resolves that open problem by establishing that the optimal excess error is Θ̃(√(dη)) (where d is the VC dimension), and further shows that this rate holds even when the adversary fully observes the learner's random bits.

## Input / Output

The learner receives a poisoned training sample S′ of size n (where an adversary, having seen both S′ and the target point x, has modified up to an η-fraction of examples) and must output a proper hypothesis h ∈ H predicting the label of x. A key structural detail is that the adversary's poisoning is instance-targeted: it knows the specific test point and tailors its corruption to cause failure there, unlike standard noise models. The paper also distinguishes private-randomness (adversary does not see the learner's seed) from public-randomness (adversary sees the seed) settings, showing the optimal rate is identical in both.

## Method

The upper-bound learner splits the sample into two halves, uses a random subsample of size Õ(√(d/η)) from the first half to construct a finite ε-cover of H (via the Sauer-Shelah lemma and a refined VC-dimension argument on the symmetric-difference class), then applies the exponential mechanism: it selects a hypothesis from the cover with probability proportional to exp(−λ·L_S(h)) on the second half. This exponential reweighting provides prediction stability—corrupting an η-fraction of examples shifts the output distribution by at most O(√(η log m))—analogous to the group-privacy guarantee in differential privacy. The lower bound is built from a 'poisoned coin problem' (VC dimension 1) where an averaging argument over the learner's expected-prediction function F(p) forces a critical bias with Ω(√η) excess error, then extended to VC dimension d via a d-coin problem where the adversary randomizes the per-coin biases from a universally hard distribution to prevent cross-coin information leakage.

## Results

The main result (Theorem 1/4) establishes that the optimal excess error under instance-targeted poisoning in the agnostic setting is Õ(√(dη)), where d is the VC dimension and η is the poisoning budget. Specifically, the upper bound is 36√(ηd)·log(e/(ηd)) achieved by a proper randomized learner using exponential-mechanism sampling over an ε-cover, while the lower bound is √(dη)/36 for any learner. Theorem 2 further shows that this rate holds even when the adversary observes the learner's random seed (public randomness), and Theorem 3 strengthens the lower bound by showing a single fixed distribution forces Ω(√(dη)) excess error for infinitely many sample sizes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no experimental baselines. The key comparison is against prior theoretical results: Hanneke et al. [2022] established tight O(dη) bounds in the realizable setting (achievable by deterministic learners) but proved that deterministic learners in the agnostic setting suffer near-maximal error (close to 1) even for small η, leaving the randomized agnostic case open. Gao et al. [2021] showed PAC learnability is preserved under instance-targeted poisoning only when η = o(1), i.e., the poisoning budget vanishes with sample size. This paper resolves the open problem by showing randomized learners achieve Õ(√(dη)) excess error for general (non-vanishing) η, and that this rate is tight up to logarithmic factors. The upper bound algorithm (exponential sampling over a VC ε-cover) is a proper learner, and the lower bound uses a d-coin problem reduction with randomized biases to prevent information leakage across coordinates.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly resolves the main open problem left by Hanneke et al. [2022], who showed that deterministic learners suffer near-maximal error under instance-targeted poisoning in the agnostic setting and asked whether randomized learners could succeed. The upper bound draws on the exponential mechanism from differential privacy and multiplicative weights from online learning, combined with a prediction-stability framework and ε-cover constructions for VC classes (building on Bassily et al. [2019]). The lower bound extends the poisoned coin problem introduced by Hanneke et al. [2022] to a d-coin formulation, using an oblivious adversary framework formalized by Blanc and Valiant [2024] to reduce the sample-level poisoning to a distribution-level perturbation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result establishes that randomness is not merely helpful but essential for agnostic learning under targeted poisoning, and that the optimal excess error scales as Õ(√(dη)) — a fundamentally different rate from the Θ(dη) achievable in the realizable setting. The surprising finding that the guarantee holds even when the adversary observes the learner's random bits (public randomness) challenges the natural intuition that public randomness collapses to the deterministic impossibility. Open questions include whether the logarithmic gap between upper and lower bounds can be closed, whether the rate is tight for specific structured classes (e.g., linear separators), and whether the exponential-mechanism-based learner can be made computationally efficient for large-scale hypothesis classes.
