## Problem

Existing open-source visual reasoning models produce reasoning chains in pure natural language, lacking explicit integration of visual information such as bounding box coordinates. This limits their ability to produce clearly articulated, visually grounded reasoning that mirrors how humans refer to specific image regions during deliberation. The paper asks how to teach MLLMs to 'think with images' by interleaving explicit grounding actions with textual reasoning in a single generative trace.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253884/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image and a textual question as input and produces a two-part output: a reasoning chain structured with  and <rethink></rethink> token pairs that freely interleaves natural language with bounding box coordinates (as integer quadruplets in JSON format), followed by a final answer after an <answer> tag. Notably, generated bounding boxes are emitted purely as text tokens; the model does not receive additional cropped pixel inputs based on its own grounding actions, instead relying on its internal representation of the original image to interpret the regions it has just referenced. Training requires only 20 image-question-answer triplets with no bounding box or reasoning-chain annotations.

## Method

GRIT introduces a grounded reasoning paradigm in which MLLMs generate reasoning chains that dynamically interleave natural language with explicit bounding box coordinates, unifying grounding and reasoning within a single autoregressive trace. Training is performed via GRPO-GR, a reinforcement learning algorithm built on the GRPO objective that uses a composite reward: a grounded-reasoning-format reward (rewarding correct special-token structure and syntactically valid bounding boxes via regex), an optional grounded-target-counting reward (matching the number of generated boxes to ground-truth object counts), and a GPT-4o-judged answer-accuracy reward. Crucially, the rewards constrain only output format and final answer correctness, not the semantic content of reasoning steps or the spatial accuracy of bounding boxes, eliminating the need for dense chain-of-thought or bounding box annotations. This design makes GRIT extremely data-efficient, requiring as few as 20 training samples from existing VQA datasets (VSR and TallyQA) to instill grounded reasoning in Qwen2.5-VL-3B and InternVL3-2B, with training conducted for 200 steps on 8 A100 GPUs using 4 sampled completions per input.

## Results

GRIT, trained with only 20 image-question-answer triplets, achieves 72.9% GPT-as-judge answer accuracy on VSR and 47.8% on TallyQA with Qwen2.5-VL-3B, and 64.9% and 44.2% respectively with InternVL3-2B, outperforming all baselines on both in-domain and out-of-domain benchmarks. On grounding, GRIT achieves a grounding IoU of 0.398 on OVDEval (Qwen2.5-VL) and 56.0 (InternVL3), surpassing zero-shot baselines. The method also generalizes to out-of-domain tasks: GQA accuracy reaches 62.8% (Qwen2.5-VL) and 63.2% (InternVL3), and MathVista accuracy reaches 59.8% and 48.2% respectively, all with only 20 training samples.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GRIT decisively outperforms both the Chain-of-Thought prompting baseline (Wei et al.) and the One-shot ICL baseline (Brown et al.) across nearly all metrics and both model backbones. On Qwen2.5-VL-3B, GRIT achieves 72.9% VSR accuracy versus 37.5% for CoT and 13.2% for ICL, and 89.3% MME accuracy versus 41.3% and 24.7% respectively. The gap is even more dramatic on InternVL3-2B, where CoT and ICL collapse to 6.4% and 3.4% VSR accuracy (vs. GRIT's 64.9%), and 14.0% and 1.3% MME accuracy (vs. GRIT's 82.0%). On grounding IoU, GRIT also leads: 0.325 on VSR and 0.447 on TallyQA for Qwen2.5-VL, compared to 0.122/0.113 (CoT) and 0.213/0.268 (ICL). The paper attributes the baselines' poor performance to a fundamental disconnect: prompting-based approaches force grounding and reasoning to operate concurrently without training, causing them to interfere with each other, whereas GRIT's RL training unifies these abilities within a single generative trace.

## Relation to Prior Work

GRIT builds directly on the GRPO algorithm from DeepSeekMath [26], extending it with grounded-reasoning-specific rewards (format, counting, and GPT-aided answer accuracy) to train MLLMs to interleave bounding box coordinates within natural-language reasoning chains. It departs from prior visual reasoning RL work (R1-OneVision, R1-V, Vision-R1, VLM-R1) that treats grounding and textual reasoning as separate objectives or outputs, and from visual CoT approaches (VisCoT, CogVLM, CogCoM) that require dense per-step bounding box annotations. The key conceptual departure is a reward design that constrains only output format and answer correctness, leaving the semantic content of reasoning steps and the accuracy of grounded regions unconstrained, thereby eliminating the need for annotated reasoning chains.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The demonstration that grounded reasoning can be instilled in MLLMs with as few as 20 training triplets is a striking data-efficiency result that lowers the barrier to producing interpretable, visually grounded reasoning. However, the study is limited to small models (2B–3B) and relatively simple VQA/counting/spatial tasks, leaving open whether the approach scales to larger models and more complex multi-step reasoning. The authors themselves note that RL with verifiable rewards primarily biases existing reasoning patterns rather than fundamentally altering them, suggesting that data diversity and pre-training quality will be critical for broader generalization. A key open question is whether the grounded reasoning paradigm can be extended to tasks requiring iterative visual inspection (e.g., reading charts, multi-image reasoning) where a single-pass understanding of the input image may be insufficient.
