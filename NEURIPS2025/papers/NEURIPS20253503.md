## Problem

Existing Joint Alignment (JA) methods that align a collection of images into a shared coordinate frame require hours of computation (Neural Congealing, ASIC) or at least minutes (SpaceJAM), rely on dense high-dimensional feature maps, and demand extensive regularization with dataset-specific hyperparameter tuning. The paper asks whether JA can be performed in seconds while matching or exceeding the alignment quality of these heavier approaches.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253503/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of N RGB images from the same semantic class (e.g., 30 birds) along with a text prompt identifying the object category, which drives Grounded-SAM to produce per-image object masks. Within each mask, RoMa extracts pairwise keypoint correspondences at 560×560 resolution, and after non-maximum suppression and DP-Means clustering, only ~10 keypoints per image are retained. The output is a set of N per-image homography parameter vectors (8 parameters each, parameterized on SL(3) via Lie algebra) that, when applied, warp all images into a common canonical frame; the solution is invariant to any global homography by construction of the inverse-compositional loss.

## Method

FastJAM constructs a sparse keypoint graph over the entire image collection: nodes are clustered keypoints (initialized with 2D coordinates), intra-image edges fully connect keypoints within each image, and inter-image edges link matched keypoints across pairs. A 5-layer GraphSAGE GNN (hidden size 128, 133K parameters) propagates alignment-relevant information through this graph, and per-image mean pooling followed by a linear projection yields the 8-dimensional homography parameters for each image. The training objective is a novel inverse-compositional geometric loss computed directly on sparse keypoint correspondences with a Geman-McClure robust function, which eliminates the need for any regularization term on the predicted warps. Because the loss operates on ~16 points in 2D rather than a 266×266×25 dense feature grid, each warping step is roughly 20× faster, and because the GNN processes the full graph in a single forward pass, alignment information propagates across all images simultaneously rather than image-by-image. The entire pipeline (masking, matching, clustering, 600-epoch test-time optimization) completes in under 50 seconds on a single RTX 4090.

## Results

On SPair-71k (18 categories, ~30 images each), FastJAM achieves a mean PCK@0.10 of 53.0, outperforming SpaceJAM (45.7), ASIC (37.0), and DINO+NN (35.0). On CUB-200, FastJAM scores 75.3 (first 3 categories) and 73.6 (subsets), matching ASIC (75.9) and surpassing SpaceJAM (69.6 / 69.9). Critically, FastJAM completes alignment in ~49 seconds, compared to ~6 minutes for SpaceJAM and over an hour for ASIC and Neural Congealing — an order-of-magnitude speedup.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FastJAM achieves the best overall PCK@0.10 on SPair-71k (53.0), ranking first in 11 of 18 categories and second in 6 others. It outperforms SpaceJAM (45.7) by 7.3 points and ASIC (37.0) by 16 points on the SPair-71k average, while being ~7× faster than SpaceJAM and ~75× faster than ASIC. On CUB-200, FastJAM (75.3) is within 0.6 points of ASIC (75.9) on the first-3-categories benchmark and clearly leads on subsets (73.6 vs. 69.9 for SpaceJAM and 63.6 for Neural Congealing). The main exception is the Bicycle category on SPair-71k, where SpaceJAM (53.5) outperforms FastJAM (43.3) due to symmetric-structure ambiguity that confuses the initial matcher. In terms of runtime, FastJAM (~49 s) is an order of magnitude faster than SpaceJAM (~6 min) and roughly 75–80× faster than ASIC (~67 min) and Neural Congealing (~79 min), while using only 0.13M parameters versus 7.9M (ASIC) and 28.7M (Neural Congealing).

## Relation to Prior Work

FastJAM builds directly on SpaceJAM [3] (same group), which introduced the inverse-compositional (IC) loss for joint alignment over dense DINO feature maps. The key departure is replacing dense feature warping with sparse keypoint correspondences processed through a GraphSAGE GNN over a unified graph of intra- and inter-image edges, shifting from an image-by-image optimization to a single forward pass over the entire collection. This positions FastJAM between classical congealing (which iteratively aligns images to a centroid) and modern deep JA methods (Neural Congealing, ASIC) that rely on high-capacity networks, heavy regularization, and dense warping, by combining the IC framework's regularization-free property with a lightweight graph-based architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: reducing JA runtime from over an hour (Neural Congealing, ASIC) or ~6 minutes (SpaceJAM) to under 50 seconds while simultaneously improving alignment quality (53.0 vs. 45.7 mean PCK on SPair-71k) makes JA feasible in interactive or large-scale settings where prior methods were prohibitive. The finding that as few as 10 keypoints per image suffice, combined with the GNN's ability to propagate alignment cues across the full collection, suggests that sparse geometric structure plus global graph reasoning can outperform dense semantic features for this task. Open questions include extending beyond homographies to handle non-planar deformations, improving robustness to highly symmetric objects where the initial matcher introduces ambiguous correspondences, and whether the approach scales to very large collections (beyond 100 images) without the graph becoming unwieldy.
