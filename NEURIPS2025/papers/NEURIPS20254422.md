## Problem

Neural sequence modeling must represent and learn highly nonlinear, probabilistic state dynamics, but existing approaches either serialize generation at test time (autoregressive models) or handle stochasticity in a post-hoc manner (e.g., stepwise variational inference on top of linear recurrences). Operator theory offers a principled linear-algebraic view of such dynamics via Hilbert-space embeddings, yet its application to generating entire coherent sequences with neural networks has remained unexplored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254422/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At sampling time, the method takes a random initial sample x_0^{1:N} drawn from a simple distribution (e.g., Gaussian) and produces a full multivariate time-series sequence of length N by integrating a learned ODE over t ∈ [0,1]. For irregular time-series tasks, the input is a partially observed sequence with 30–70% of timesteps dropped, and the output is either the completed regular sequence (irregular→regular) or a new irregularly sampled sequence (irregular→irregular). Sequences range from length 24 (Sines, Stocks, ETTh, MuJoCo, Energy, fMRI) to 728–791 (FRED-MD, NN5 Daily), with time-delay observables used for the longer cases.

## Method

The paper embeds the full sequence distribution as a mean-embedding tensor in a product RKHS H^{⊗N} and defines generation as an MMD gradient flow over sequences. To make this tractable, it applies spectral decomposition (eigenvalue decomposition) of the HMM's transition operator U, leveraging the multiplicative algebra closure of eigenfunctions to derive a tensor-network decomposition of the sequence mean embedding that reduces computation to O(Nr + r²) in sequence length N and rank r. To achieve finite-time convergence, it extends MMD flows to time-dependent RKHS and connects them to flow matching via the continuity equation, so the learned vector field regresses a known OT or diffusion vector field. The neural architecture uses complex-valued MLPs with a shared feature extractor and readout heads (B, L, R, H) for the observation operators, trained end-to-end with a conditional flow-matching objective that is simulation-free and requires double backpropagation.

## Results

On six time-series datasets (Sines, Stocks, ETTh, MuJoCo, Energy, fMRI), spectral mean flow achieves the best score in 18 out of 24 metric-dataset combinations, outperforming the prior state-of-the-art Diffusion-TS. The most notable gains are in Context-FID (e.g., 0.058 vs. 0.126 on ETTh, 0.008 vs. 0.169 on Stocks) and Discriminative Score (e.g., 0.027 vs. 0.075 on ETTh, 0.005 vs. 0.012 on MuJoCo), representing roughly 50% average improvement on ETTh. Predictive Score is essentially tied with Diffusion-TS across all datasets (e.g., 0.037 vs. 0.037 on Stocks). On longer sequences (FRED-MD, NN5 Daily), the method wins 5 out of 6 metric-dataset cases against ImagenTime and LS4.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Diffusion-TS [101], the closest competitor, spectral mean flow wins on Context-FID for 5/6 datasets (e.g., 0.058 vs. 0.126 on ETTh, 0.008 vs. 0.169 on Stocks) and Discriminative Score for 5/6 (e.g., 0.027 vs. 0.075 on ETTh), while losing narrowly on MuJoCo Context-FID (0.018 vs. 0.015) and Energy Discriminative (0.161 vs. 0.154); Predictive Score is essentially tied. Against DiffTime [16] and Diffwave [53], the method dominates across nearly all metrics and datasets, with Context-FID gaps of 2–15× (e.g., 0.058 vs. 0.299 on ETTh for DiffTime). Against GAN-based baselines TimeGAN [100], Cot-GAN [99], and C-RNN-GAN [67], spectral mean flow achieves substantially lower scores on all four metrics (e.g., Context-FID 0.058 vs. 0.300 for TimeGAN on ETTh). Against VAE-based TimeVAE [21] and Koopman VAE [72], the method wins on all metrics, with particularly large gaps on fMRI (Context-FID 0.116 vs. 14.449 for TimeVAE) and on irregular time-series tasks (Discriminative 0.009 vs. 0.021 for Koopman VAE at 0% drop). In the larger-model regime (Table 2), spectral mean flow beats SDFormer-M [15] and ImagenTime [71] on Context-FID for Sines and MuJoCo, ties SDFormer-AR [15] on Predictive, but loses on Stocks Context-FID (0.004 vs. 0.006). On long sequences (Table 3), it outperforms ImagenTime [71] and LS4 on 5/6 metric-dataset cases, and on the physics-informed pendulum task (Table 5) it achieves a 6× lower correlational score than Koopman VAE [72] with the same stability constraint (0.0005 vs. 0.0030).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies three previously separate threads: MMD gradient flows for distribution matching (Arbel et al., 2019), conditional mean embedding operators from kernel dynamical systems (Mollenhauer & Koltai, 2020), and flow matching for generative modeling (Lipman et al., 2023). The key conceptual move is applying spectral decomposition of the HMM transition operator to derive a tensor network decomposition of sequence mean embeddings, then connecting the resulting MMD flow in a time-dependent RKHS to flow matching via the continuity equation. The authors explicitly note that generating entire coherent sequences via operator-theoretic transfer operators had not been previously attempted, positioning this as a new application of an established theoretical toolkit rather than a new theoretical framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a principled operator-theoretic architecture (complex-valued spectral parameters + multiplicative parameter sharing via tensor networks) can match or beat state-of-the-art time-series generators without any task-specific auxiliary losses, suggesting the inductive bias of spectral expansion naturally captures periodicity. Open questions include whether the HMM assumption (time-invariant transition, local observation) is too restrictive for highly non-stationary real-world series, how the rank-r truncation of the spectral decomposition affects expressiveness at scale, and whether the approach extends beyond univariate or low-dimensional multivariate time series to higher-dimensional structured data. The physics-informed experiment hints at a broader utility for incorporating domain constraints through spectral regularization.
