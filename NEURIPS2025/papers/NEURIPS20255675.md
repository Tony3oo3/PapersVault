## Problem

Existing algorithms for Stackelberg mean field games (SMFGs) rely on restrictive leader-follower independence assumptions, employ computationally expensive nested-loop structures, and lack finite-time convergence guarantees. This paper addresses the absence of a practical single-loop learning algorithm for SMFGs that can handle coupled leader-follower dynamics while providing non-asymptotic convergence analysis.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255675/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes a discrete-time SMFG specification (finite state/action spaces, transition kernels, reward functions, discount factor) and two continuously generated Markovian sample paths per iteration—one tracking the discounted occupancy measure and one tracking the stationary distribution. It outputs the leader's softmax policy parameter, the representative follower's softmax policy parameter, and the mean field estimate (a probability vector over the state space). For continuous-state experiments, the tabular mean field is replaced by a Gaussian distribution whose parameters are updated online, and policies are parameterized by neural networks.

## Method

AC-SMFG is a single-loop actor-critic algorithm that alternates (semi-)gradient updates for the leader's policy, the follower's policy, and the mean field on distinct time scales via geometrically ordered step sizes (ζ_k ≤ ξ_k ≤ α_k ≤ β_k), implicitly approximating a nested-loop structure without requiring inner-loop convergence. The key theoretical innovation is a 'gradient alignment' assumption (Assumption 6) that requires the full Stackelberg gradient to form an acute angle with the partial leader gradient evaluated at the current mean field, relaxing the stronger leader-follower independence conditions in prior work. To handle the non-convex lower-level MFG (which satisfies only a Polyak-Lojasiewicz condition under entropy regularization, not strong convexity), the authors develop a careful error decomposition of the follower's objective gap that avoids the rate deterioration from naive Lipschitz bounds. Combined with recent advances in multi-time-scale stochastic approximation exploiting Lipschitz gradients of the best-response operator, this yields a Õ(k^{-1/2}) convergence rate to a stationary point—surpassing the Õ(k^{-2/5}) rate for bi-level optimization with lower-level strong convexity.

## Results

AC-SMFG demonstrates significantly faster leader convergence and smoother convergence trajectories (reduced variability across 30 runs and diminished intra-run volatility) compared to all baselines across three canonical MFG environments (Market Entrance, Shop Positioning, Equilibrium Pricing). In the Market Entrance game, AC-SMFG achieves the fastest convergence and improved final equilibria over all comparisons. In the Equilibrium Pricing game (the most challenging, with continuous state/action spaces), AC-SMFG yields significantly better leader and follower rewards than existing SMFG algorithms (OneByOne, CuiKoeppl) and closely tracks the performance of the MARL-based ADAGE baseline. The theoretical convergence rate is Õ(k^{-1/2}), improving upon the Õ(k^{-2/5}) rate of comparable single-loop bi-level optimization methods under lower-level strong convexity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares AC-SMFG against three baselines: (1) OneByOne, a nested-loop MFG algorithm that alternates between training the leader and representative follower to convergence; (2) CuiKoeppl, a weighted OneByOne update from Cui et al. [2024] based on fictitious play; and (3) ADAGE, a MARL implementation using PPO with a shared follower policy from Evans et al. [2025]. AC-SMFG outperforms both SMFG-specific baselines (OneByOne and CuiKoeppl) across all three environments in both convergence speed and final reward quality, with the advantage being most pronounced when followers' behavior is strongly influenced by the leader's actions. Against the MARL baseline ADAGE, AC-SMFG achieves comparable or slightly lower leader rewards in the Equilibrium Pricing environment (attributed to the Gaussian mean field approximation), but still outperforms the other SMFG baselines. In Shop Positioning, AC-SMFG shows slightly slower follower convergence than ADAGE (because the leader has a smaller impact on followers due to the fixed desirable location), yet still demonstrates substantial improvements over the MARL scenario for both leader and followers. The key practical advantage of AC-SMFG over the nested-loop baselines is its single-loop structure requiring only two samples per iteration, making it significantly more efficient and practical for large-scale or online settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the SMFG/major-minor MFG learning literature, most directly departing from Cui et al. [2024] (nested-loop fictitious play with asymptotic-only convergence and strong leader-follower independence assumptions) and Dayanikl & Laurière [2024] (penalty-based reformulation without a convergent algorithm). The algorithmic design draws on multi-time-scale stochastic approximation for bilevel optimization [Hong et al., 2023] and recent advances in decoupled convergence under Lipschitz-gradient lower-level operators [Han et al., 2024], while the convergence analysis exploits the Polyak-Lojasiewicz structure of entropy-regularized MFG objectives [Mei et al., 2020]. The key conceptual departure is replacing the nested-loop structure with a single-loop actor-critic scheme and substituting restrictive independence assumptions with a weaker 'gradient alignment' condition.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first SMFG algorithm with non-asymptotic finite-sample convergence guarantees, and the first single-loop SMFG algorithm to be analyzed, making the problem substantially more tractable for practical deployment in hierarchical decision-making (optimal liquidation, public policy design). The technical contribution of handling lower-level PL conditions (rather than strong convexity) in bilevel optimization is of independent interest and could benefit broader bilevel optimization methods. Key limitations include the requirement of sufficient entropy regularization (so the unregularized problem remains unsolved), the finite-state-space assumption in the theory, and the homogeneity assumption inherent to the MFG framework, which limits applicability to small-N settings where agent-based modeling may be more appropriate.
