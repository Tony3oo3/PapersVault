## Problem

Prior work on noisy submodular maximization (Hassidim & Singer 2017; Huang et al. 2022) designed bespoke algorithms and analyses for each specific constraint setting, with approximation ratios that fall short of the noiseless optimum (e.g., (1−1/e)/2 instead of 1−1/e for matroid constraints). The paper asks whether a unified framework can convert any sufficiently 'robust' noiseless algorithm into a noisy-setting algorithm while preserving its approximation guarantee, eliminating the need for problem-specific analyses.

## Input / Output

The algorithm receives a persistent noisy value oracle f̃(S) = ξ_S·f(S) where ξ_S is a non-negative sub-exponential random variable with E[ξ_S]=1, and the same set always returns the same noisy value (so repeated queries cannot denoise). It also takes a matroid constraint (or no constraint) and integer parameters h, t, m controlling the smoothing set size, subset size, and number of samples. The output is a feasible set S_H ∪ H' that achieves an (α − o(1))-approximation to the true optimum, where α is the approximation ratio of the underlying noiseless algorithm A.

## Method

The core contribution is a meta-algorithm that constructs a random surrogate function F^{H,t}(S) = E_{H'~H[t]}[f(S∪H')] by sampling a random smoothing set H of size h from a matroid basis and averaging f over all t-subsets of H. The noisy surrogate f̃^{H,t}(S) is approximated by sampling m distinct t-subsets without replacement, and any 'robust' algorithm A (one whose performance degrades by at most β(ε) under an ε-approximate oracle) is run as a black box on this surrogate over the contracted matroid. The key technical insight is that a *random* smoothing set H (rather than a deterministic one) enables a general smoothing lemma showing E_H[max_{S∈I_H} F^{H,t}(S)] ≥ (1 − h/(r−h) − t/(h−t))·f(O*), which holds uniformly across monotone and non-monotone settings. Instantiating A with the measured continuous greedy algorithm yields (1−1/e)−O(ε) for monotone and 1/e−O(ε) for non-monotone matroid-constrained maximization, while instantiating with double greedy yields 1/2−O(ε) for unconstrained non-monotone maximization.

## Results

The paper's main contribution is a meta-algorithm that converts any robust exact submodular maximization algorithm into a noisy-oracle algorithm with only o(1) loss in approximation ratio. Theoretically, this yields a (1−1/e)−O(ε) approximation for monotone submodular maximization under matroid constraints (improving the prior (1−1/e)/2 bound of Huang et al.), a 1/e−O(ε) approximation for non-monotone submodular maximization under matroid constraints (first result), and a 1/2−O(ε) approximation for unconstrained non-monotone submodular maximization (first result, and tight). In simulations on unconstrained non-monotone noisy submodular maximization (n=50, n=100), the proposed algorithm achieves 0.674 and 0.657 (m=50) and 0.735 and 0.731 (m=200) as a fraction of the optimal value, substantially outperforming the natural double-greedy-with-noisy-oracle baseline (0.601 and 0.565).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's meta-algorithm improves upon Huang et al. [16] for monotone submodular maximization under matroid constraints, achieving the tight (1−1/e)−O(ε) ratio versus their (1−1/e)/2−O(ε), while also relaxing the required conditions on n and r. For monotone submodular maximization under cardinality constraints, the paper matches the tight (1−1/e) guarantee of Hassidim and Singer [13] but with a more general framework. The paper provides the first results for non-monotone settings: a 1/e−O(ε) approximation under matroid constraints and a 1/2−O(ε) (tight) approximation for unconstrained maximization, where no prior noisy-oracle results existed. In the empirical comparison (Table 2), the proposed algorithm with m=50 outperforms the natural double-greedy-with-noisy-oracle baseline by approximately 7–9 percentage points (0.674 vs 0.601 at n=50; 0.657 vs 0.565 at n=100), and with m=200 the gap widens to approximately 13–17 percentage points (0.735 vs 0.601; 0.731 vs 0.565). The random subset baseline performs only marginally better than the noisy double greedy, confirming that naively applying the optimal noiseless algorithm to noisy values is ineffective.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the noisy submodular maximization line initiated by Hassidim and Singer (2017) for cardinality constraints and extended by Huang et al. (2022) to matroid constraints, both of which designed problem-specific algorithms with tailored smoothing analyses. The key departure is a meta-algorithmic framework that treats any 'robust' exact-oracle algorithm (measured continuous greedy, double greedy) as a black box, using a random surrogate function to bridge the noisy-to-exact gap uniformly. This contrasts with prior work's deterministic surrogates, which required separate analyses per setting and left a factor-of-2 gap for matroid constraints. The paper also draws on the online submodular maximization literature's observation that many algorithms are 'robust' to perturbations, repurposing that insight for the noise setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework eliminates the need for problem-specific noisy algorithms: any future robust exact-oracle algorithm can be immediately converted to the noisy setting with only o(1) loss, making the approach future-proof. It closes the approximation gap for matroid-constrained monotone maximization (from (1-1/e)/2 to 1-1/e) and provides the first results for non-monotone noisy submodular maximization in both unconstrained and matroid settings. Open questions include high-probability guarantees for non-monotone functions, whether the 0.401-approximation algorithm of Buchbinder and Feldman is robust (which would yield 0.401-o(1) under noise), and extending the framework to noisy submodular minimization.
