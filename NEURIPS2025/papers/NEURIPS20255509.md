## Problem

Existing Fourier sparsity testers either suffer from impractical query complexity (O(s^14) for Boolean functions, tower-type dependencies via regularity) or suboptimal dependence on the proximity parameter ε (Õ(s/ε⁴) in the real-valued setting). The paper asks whether one can design a dimension-independent, nonadaptive tester for s-Fourier sparsity of real-valued functions over F_2^n with query complexity that is near-optimal in both s and ε, and whether a matching lower bound can be established.

## Input / Output

The algorithm receives only oracle (point-query) access to a real-valued function f: F_2^n → R normalized to unit ℓ_2 norm, along with parameters s (target sparsity), ε (proximity gap), and δ (tolerance). It outputs a YES/NO decision distinguishing whether f is δ-close to some s-Fourier sparse function or (δ+ε)-far from every such function. Crucially, the query complexity Õ(s/ε²) is completely independent of the ambient dimension n, and the algorithm is nonadaptive — all queries are determined before any responses are observed.

## Method

The tester restricts f to a uniformly random affine subspace A = α + H of dimension t = Θ(log(s²/ε²)), then estimates the top-s Fourier coefficients of the restricted function f_A via median-of-means and checks whether their squared sum exceeds 1 − (δ + ε/2). The key technical ingredient is a new spectral concentration lemma: for a random affine subspace, each Fourier coefficient of f_A is tightly concentrated (via a Chebyshev-type bound) around the largest original Fourier coefficient in the corresponding coset of H^⊥, with the 'noise' from other coset members having variance at most 2^{-t}. This implicitly induces a pairwise-independent coset hashing without explicitly constructing one. The matching Ω(s) lower bound is proved by reducing the Approximate Matrix Rank communication problem to Fourier sparsity testing, encoding matrices into Maiorana-McFarland functions whose Fourier sparsity is governed by the rank of the sum matrix, and invoking the Ω(r²) communication lower bound of Sherstov and Storozhenko.

## Results

The paper establishes a nonadaptive tolerant tester for Fourier sparsity with query complexity $\widetilde{O}(s/\epsilon^2)$, where the $\widetilde{O}$ hides factors polynomial in $\log s$ and $\log(1/\epsilon)$. A matching lower bound of $\Omega(s)$ is proved for the non-tolerant setting ($\epsilon = 1/4$), establishing optimality up to logarithmic factors. The algorithm is dimension-independent: query complexity depends only on $s$ and $\epsilon$, not on the ambient dimension $n$.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to Yaroslavtsev and Zhou [31], the closest prior work in the same real-valued $\ell_2$ setting, this paper improves the upper bound from $\widetilde{O}(s/\epsilon^4)$ to $\widetilde{O}(s/\epsilon^2)$, halving the dependence on the proximity parameter $\epsilon$. The lower bound is quadratically improved from $\Omega(\sqrt{s})$ to $\Omega(s)$, closing the gap to near-tightness. Against Gopalan et al. [16], which targets Boolean functions under Hamming distance with $O(s^{14})$ complexity, and Hatami–Lovett [18], whose regularity-based reduction yields a tower-function dependence on $s$, the present result is substantially more efficient and operates in a more general real-valued setting. The lower bound technique is novel, exploiting the spectral structure of Maiorana–McFarland functions in a reduction from the Approximate Matrix Rank communication problem [26], a direction not used in prior Fourier sparsity lower bounds.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of Fourier sparsity testing initiated by Gopalan et al. (2011) for Boolean functions under Hamming distance and refined by Yaroslavtsev and Zhou (2020) for real-valued functions under ℓ₂ distance. The key methodological departure is the shift from explicit Fourier hashing (projecting the spectrum into cosets) to analyzing the function restricted to random affine subspaces, yielding a new spectral concentration result (Lemma 3.4) that relates restricted Fourier coefficients to the 'leader' of each coset. The lower bound reduction follows the communication-complexity framework of Blais, Brody, and Matulef (2012), but instantiates it with Maiorana-McFarland functions and the Approximate Matrix Rank problem of Sherstov and Storozhenko (2024) in a way not previously exploited for sparsity testing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes near-tight query complexity bounds (Õ(s/ε²) upper, Ω(s) lower) for Fourier sparsity testing, quadratically improving the previous Ω(√s) lower bound and halving the ε-dependence in the upper bound. The dimension-independence of the algorithm (query complexity depends only on s and ε, not on n) makes it applicable to very high-dimensional settings where prior regularity-based approaches incur tower-type overhead. Open questions include extending the lower bound beyond ε = 1/4, bridging the gap between the tolerant upper bound and non-tolerant lower bound, and whether analogous dimension-independent testers exist for other bases such as wavelets.
