## Problem

Existing chest X-ray benchmarks evaluate only the correctness of the final diagnostic answer, offering no insight into whether a model actually performed clinically meaningful intermediate reasoning steps such as identifying anatomical structures, computing measurements, and applying diagnostic thresholds. This makes it impossible to distinguish genuine diagnostic understanding from superficial pattern matching or shortcut-based answers.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252928/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each QA pair in CXReasonBench is accompanied by up to 4 visual inputs (the original chest X-ray, overlaid segmentation masks, highlighted anatomical regions, and labeled landmarks with coordinates) alongside multiple-choice text prompts. The CheXStruct pipeline ingests raw PA/AP chest X-rays from MIMIC-CXR-JPG and produces structured multi-step reference answers spanning segmentation masks, anatomical landmarks, diagnostic measurements, computed indices, and threshold-based decisions. The benchmark comprises 18,988 QA pairs across 12 diagnostic tasks and 1,200 manually QC-verified cases, with evaluation scores reported per stage across two complementary paths.

## Method

CheXStruct is a fully automated, rule-based pipeline that uses the CXAS segmentation model to extract anatomical masks, derives landmarks and measurements from those masks, computes diagnostic indices (e.g., cardiothoracic ratio), and applies expert-defined or standardized clinical thresholds, with task-specific quality control filtering out unreliable samples. CXReasonBench builds a multi-path, multi-stage evaluation on top of this pipeline: Path 1 (Direct Reasoning) tests whether a model can independently reconstruct its diagnostic logic across four stages—criterion selection, anatomical structure identification, measurement/recognition, and final decision—while Path 2 (Guided Reasoning) provides structured visual annotations and step-by-step instructions when the model expresses uncertainty, then re-evaluates generalization on a new case. The key distinction from prior benchmarks is the explicit evaluation of intermediate visual grounding (selecting correct anatomical regions from highlighted images) and quantitative measurement consistency, rather than relying solely on bounding-box grounding or final-answer accuracy. Evaluation uses Wilson score-based metrics (Completion, Depth, Consistency, Alignment) to fairly compare models with varying numbers of stage attempts.

## Results

On Path 1 (Direct Reasoning), the strongest model, Gemini-2.5-Pro, achieves only 17.03% completion and an average reasoning depth of 1.96/4 stages, with 60.88% decision alignment. GPT-4.1 reaches 8.32% completion and depth 1.15, while most open-source models (e.g., Llama-3.2-90B-Vision, Pixtral 12B) complete fewer than 1% of cases. On Path 2 (Guided Reasoning), Gemini-2.5-Pro improves to 55.81% completion (depth 2.62/3), but on Re-evaluated Path 1, nearly all models drop to 0% completion, indicating a critical failure to generalize structured reasoning to new cases.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The benchmark evaluates 12 LVLMs (3 closed-source, 9 open-source) on 18,988 QA pairs across 12 diagnostic tasks. Closed-source models consistently outperform open-source ones: Gemini-2.5-Pro leads Path 1 with 17.03% completion and 60.88% alignment, followed by Gemini-2.5-Flash (12.83%) and GPT-4.1 (8.32%), while the best open-source model (Pixtral-Large) reaches only 3.73% completion. On Path 2 (guided reasoning), Gemini-2.5-Pro achieves 55.81% completion, substantially ahead of Qwen2.5-VL-72B (28.45%) and GPT-4.1 (19.94%), suggesting strong instruction-following but not genuine reasoning. The critical finding is that on Re-evaluated Path 1, where models must independently apply learned reasoning to a new case, nearly all models collapse to 0% completion (except Gemini-2.5-Flash at 6.03% and Qwen2.5-VL-72B at 2.74%), revealing that in-context exposure to structured guidance does not produce transferable diagnostic reasoning. Medical-specialized models (HealthGPT, RadVLM) show better recognition-type performance but weaker measurement-type performance compared to general-domain models, highlighting a training-data composition gap.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on CXAS (Seibold et al., 2023), a chest X-ray anatomy segmentation model, as the foundational component of its CheXStruct pipeline for deriving landmarks, measurements, and diagnostic indices. It departs from prior chest X-ray structuring frameworks (Chest ImaGenome, PadChest-GR, GR-Bench), which link report-derived findings to image regions via coarse bounding boxes, by instead extracting fine-grained, multi-step reasoning chains directly from segmentation masks using rule-based clinical logic. In the medical VQA benchmark space, it extends the single-stage, answer-correctness paradigm of VQA-RAD, PathVQA, GEMeX, and others into a multi-path, multi-stage evaluation that explicitly scores intermediate reasoning steps (criterion selection, anatomical identification, measurement, threshold application).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals that even the strongest LVLMs (Gemini-2.5-Pro) achieve only ~17% full-reasoning completion and cannot reliably connect abstract diagnostic knowledge with anatomically grounded visual interpretation, exposing a critical gap between pattern recognition and clinically valid reasoning. A particularly important negative result is that guided reasoning (Path 2) does not transfer: after receiving explicit structured guidance, models fail to generalize to new cases (0% completion for the best model on Re-evaluated Path 1), suggesting in-context learning is insufficient for internalizing diagnostic reasoning. Key limitations include restriction to 12 structurally-inferable tasks (excluding pixel-level pathologies like opacities or effusions), a single imaging modality (chest X-ray), and a relatively small evaluated set (1,200 cases), leaving open whether findings generalize to broader clinical reasoning or other modalities.
