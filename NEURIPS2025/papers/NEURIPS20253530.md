## Problem

Standard sparse variational GPs assume the conditional posterior over non-inducing function values equals the prior conditional, a restriction that provably loosens the variational bound. Recent work relaxed this with a diagonal scaling matrix, but it remains unclear whether more expressive structures (e.g., block-diagonal) can further tighten the bound while preserving O(M³) per-iteration cost, and whether such structured posteriors can also improve non-variational inference schemes like Power Expectation Propagation.

## Input / Output

The method consumes N training input-output pairs, M inducing point locations, and kernel/likelihood hyperparameters, partitioning the training set into B disjoint blocks of size N/B (typically set equal to M for O(M³) per-iteration cost). It outputs predictive mean and variance at test points, an approximate log marginal likelihood for hyperparameter optimisation, and learned hyperparameters. At test time, the expensive block-structured predictive covariance is approximated by reverting to the standard p(f|u) conditional, so the structured posterior is used only during training to improve q(u) and hyperparameter estimates.

## Method

The paper introduces a block-diagonal scaling matrix M = blkdiag({m_b}) in the approximate conditional q(f|u) = N(f; K_fu K_uu^{-1} u, D_ff^{1/2} M D_ff^{1/2}), yielding a collapsed bound whose tightness is proven via Hadamard's inequality to strictly dominate the diagonal-M bound. The optimal per-block solution m_b = (I_b + σ^{-2} D_{f_b f_b})^{-1} requires only a Cholesky of an N/B × N/B matrix, and stochastic optimisation samples one random block per iteration at O(M³) cost. The paper then extends the Power-EP framework of Bui et al. (2017) by incorporating the structured posterior: for the spherical case M = mI_N, the PEP approximate marginal likelihood remains analytically tractable for all power hyperparameters α, unifying SVGP (α→0), FITC (α=1, m=1), and PITC as special cases, and providing a flexible family of objectives that outperform the m=1 baseline in RMSE across multiple datasets.

## Results

On 5,000 samples from KIN40K with M=256 inducing points, the proposed block-diagonal variational bound (BT-SGPR[10]) achieves RMSE 0.20 and LL −0.03, improving over the diagonal bound T-SGPR (RMSE 0.22, LL −0.06) and the standard SGPR (RMSE 0.26, LL −0.14). The structured Power-EP variant (T-PEP[0.5]) achieves the best predictive performance with RMSE 0.20 and LL 0.02 at M=256, and RMSE 0.16 and LL 0.19 at M=512, compared to the exact GP's RMSE 0.12 and LL 0.80. Across all methods, increasing the structure of the scaling matrix M (from identity to diagonal to block-diagonal) consistently reduces the estimated observation noise σ (from 0.30 to 0.18 at M=256) and tightens the variational bound.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed block-diagonal variational bound (BT-SGPR) consistently outperforms the standard SGPR (Titsias, 2009) on both RMSE and LL: at M=256, BT-SGPR[10] achieves RMSE 0.20 vs. SGPR's 0.26, and LL −0.03 vs. −0.14. Compared to the diagonal bound T-SGPR (Titsias, 2025; Bui et al., 2025), BT-SGPR[10] provides a tighter bound (Obj. 0.66 vs. 0.78) and slightly better RMSE (0.20 vs. 0.22), with the gap widening at M=512 (RMSE 0.17 vs. 0.18). The structured Power-EP (T-PEP[0.5]) clearly outperforms the original PEP (Bui et al., 2017) on both RMSE (0.20 vs. 0.23 at M=256; 0.16 vs. 0.20 at M=512) and LL (0.02 vs. −0.02; 0.19 vs. 0.14). In the stochastic variational experiments (Figure 3), BT-SVGP matches or exceeds T-SVGP and SVGP (Hensman et al., 2015) across four real-world regression datasets, with the advantage growing as block size decreases. Overall, T-PEP[0.5] emerges as the strongest single method, approaching exact GP performance most closely among all sparse approximations tested.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the sparse variational GP framework of Titsias (2009) and its stochastic extension (Hensman et al., 2013, 2015) by building on the recent diagonal scaling matrix M introduced by Titsias (2025) and Bui et al. (2025), which relaxes the standard q(f|u)=p(f|u) assumption to yield provably tighter variational bounds. The key conceptual step is promoting M from diagonal to block-diagonal structure, which tightens the bound further via Hadamard's inequality while preserving O(M³) per-iteration cost. The paper also extends the Power-EP unifying framework of Bui et al. (2017) by showing that structured posteriors (particularly M=mI_N) enable tractable PEP updates and closed-form approximate marginal likelihoods for all power hyperparameters α, unifying SVGP, FITC, PITC, and PEP under a single structured-posterior lens.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The block-diagonal approximation offers a clean, provably tighter bound with minimal implementation overhead (one extra Cholesky factorization per block), making it a practical drop-in improvement for existing SVGP pipelines. The unification of variational and PEP inference under structured posteriors gives practitioners a flexible family of objectives parameterized by α and block structure, with intermediate α values (e.g., 0.5) appearing most competitive. Open questions include whether data-dependent block partitioning (rather than random) further tightens the bound, whether per-block α values can be adaptively selected, and how these structured approximations extend to non-Gaussian likelihoods, deep GPs, and GP latent variable models. The consistent reduction in estimated observation noise variance across methods is a practical benefit that partially addresses the well-known noise overestimation pathology in sparse GP regression.
