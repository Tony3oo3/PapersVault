## Problem

While empirical evidence shows that larger and more diverse training datasets improve feature transfer to downstream tasks, the theoretical conditions under which classifier-learned features enable effective clustering on unseen classes remain unclear. This paper asks: what specific conditions govern whether features extracted from a trained classifier form cohesive and separable clusters for new, unseen distributions, and how does the alignment between training and unseen data determine transfer success?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250129/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The theoretical analysis operates in the proportional regime (d/n, N/n → c as n→∞) on a two-layer network trained with a single gradient step on MSE classification loss over K classes drawn from non-centered sub-Gaussian distributions. Practical experiments use RGB images from fine-grained datasets (CAR, CUB, SOP, ISC) and ImageNet subsets with ResNet-18/50 backbones. The output is characterized by two clustering criteria—Cohesion (intra-class feature compactness) and Separability (inter-class feature distinction)—expressed as closed-form polynomials of the train-unseen similarity β^T μ, alongside Recall@1 as a practical nearest-neighbor clustering metric.

## Method

The paper performs an asymptotic analysis of a two-layer network after one gradient descent step, proving that the trained feature decomposes into a random initialization component F_{0,L} and a dominant 'spike component' s_L that maps new inputs via inner products with data-label covariance directions β_ij = (1/n)X_ij^T y. Using Hermite decomposition of the activation function, they derive closed-form polynomial expressions for Cohesion and Separability as functions of the train-unseen similarity β^T μ, revealing that Cohesion monotonically increases with similarity while Separability increases only when unseen classes are assigned to different training classes and decreases when assigned to the same class. The spike component's contribution to feature expressiveness depends on its alignment with the input—orthogonal spike directions contribute nothing, explaining why semantically irrelevant training classes do not improve transfer. The analysis generalizes prior centered-Gaussian frameworks to non-centered sub-Gaussian distributions, enabling treatment of arbitrary classification labels without a teacher function, and is validated on both synthetic data and real-world open-set clustering benchmarks.

## Results

The paper's main quantitative finding is that Cohesion increases monotonically with train-unseen similarity (|β^T μ|), while Separability increases only when unseen classes are assigned to different training classes and decreases when assigned to the same class. In practical open-set clustering experiments (Expr. VII), removing redundantly assigned unseen classes yields a 2.31 pp average improvement in Recall@1 over random removal (max +12.71 pp, success rate 82.81%). In Expr. IV, the positive correlation between semantic domain alignment and clustering performance held in 29 out of 32 experiments across CAR, CUB, SOP, and ISC datasets using ResNet-18 and ResNet-50.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical analysis paper rather than a method comparison, so it does not benchmark a proposed method against competing algorithms. Instead, it validates theoretical predictions against empirical baselines: (1) trained features (F) consistently outperform randomly initialized features (F_0) on Cohesion, confirming that one gradient step of classification training improves feature structure; (2) adding domain-relevant ImageNet classes (Domain + sub In1k) improves Recall@1 over Domain-only training, while adding all ImageNet classes (Domain + subsampled whole In1k) offers no clear additional advantage, supporting the theory that only non-orthogonal spike directions contribute to expressiveness; (3) gradually increasing the number of semantically related training classes from 25% to 100% monotonically improves Recall@1 (Expr. VI), consistent with the prediction that more relevant spike directions enhance feature extraction. The paper positions itself against the Neural Collapse / ULPM literature, noting that those frameworks assume free-variable features and cannot analyze input-distribution-dependent transferability, whereas the two-layer proportional-regime model here enables explicit characterization of how train-unseen similarity governs clustering quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Conjugate Kernel (CK) framework for two-layer network analysis (Louart et al., 2017; Ba et al., 2022; Moniri et al., 2024) from centered Gaussian regression with teacher-student structure to non-centered Sub-Gaussian classification with arbitrary labels. It departs from the Neural Collapse / Unconstrained Layer-Peeled Model (ULPM) line of work, which treats features as free variables and thus cannot analyze input-distribution-dependent transferability, by instead analyzing the actual trained feature function of a two-layer network. The paper is positioned as the first to provide a quantitative, closed-form connection between train-unseen alignment (β⊤μ) and open-set clustering criteria (Cohesion, Separability), filling a gap where prior metric-learning and feature-transfer work remained purely empirical.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first theoretical characterization of when classifier features transfer effectively to unseen classes, showing that train-unseen similarity—not merely data scale—is the governing factor, and that redundant class assignments actively harm Separability. This has practical implications for data-efficient training and representation-aware coreset selection, suggesting that domain-relevant subsets can outperform indiscriminately large datasets. Key limitations include the restriction to two-layer, single-step, proportional-regime analysis; the Hermite approximation introduces discrepancies at finite dimensions; and extension to multi-step, multi-layer, and softmax settings remains open. The practical validation on ResNet-18/50 with ImageNet subsets supports the theory but does not fully close the gap to deep, multi-step training.
