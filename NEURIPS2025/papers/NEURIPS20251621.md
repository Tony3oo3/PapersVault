## Problem

Training sparse autoencoders (SAEs) and other linear interpretability tools on large language models is computationally expensive, and it remains unclear whether the linear feature spaces of same-family models at different scales are aligned enough to permit direct feature transfer. The paper asks whether a simple affine mapping between residual streams can faithfully transfer SAE weights, probes, and steering vectors between models of different sizes, and whether this can reduce the cost of training SAEs on larger models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251621/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes cached residual stream activations from two same-family decoder-only LMs (Pythia-70m/160m, GPT-2 small/medium, Gemma-2 2B/9B) at SVCCA-selected layers, along with a fully trained SAE on the smaller model. It produces a pair of affine stitch matrices, a reparameterized SAE on the larger model (exact, not approximate, because all transformations are affine), and directly transferred probes and steering vectors. The stitch is trained on ~180k OpenWebText samples with 512-token context, and the SAE transfer preserves the TopK or JumpReLU activation architecture of the original SAE.

## Method

The paper learns two concurrent affine mappings T_up (A→B) and T_down (B→A) between residual streams at SVCCA-selected layers, trained with a loss combining bidirectional reconstruction MSE and an α-weighted inversion penalty that encourages near-invertibility despite the dimensionality gap. The key algebraic insight is that composing T_down ∘ SAE ∘ T_up collapses exactly into a valid SAE on model B with reparameterized weights (W'_e = P_down·W_e, W'_d = W_d·P_up, with corresponding bias adjustments), so the transferred SAE is a proper SAE architecture on the target model rather than an approximation. Using this transferred SAE as initialization for continued training on the larger model achieves 30–50% FLOPs savings at high explained-variance thresholds, with the approximate scaling law shifted left relative to random initialization. The same stitch matrices also enable zero-shot transfer of linear probes (applied to down-stitched activations) and steering vectors (transformed via P_up/down and renormalized), recovering near-ground-truth performance on response-language steering tasks.

## Results

The affine stitch between same-family models achieves near-lossless round-trip reconstruction (A→B→A increases CE by <1% across all three model pairs), while one-way stitching from the smaller to larger model incurs 27–39% CE degradation. Transferred SAEs (without further training) achieve FUV of 0.39–0.52 and Delta Loss of 0.85–2.16, substantially better than random but below fully trained SAEs. Using the transferred SAE as initialization for continued training on the larger model saves 30–50% FLOPs at high explained-variance thresholds (~90%) compared to training from scratch. Transferred probes and steering vectors recover ground-truth performance in most cases without any retraining on the target model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against external stitching or transfer methods but instead benchmarks against several internal baselines. For SAE training, the transferred-initialization SAE is compared against a from-scratch (random) initialization: at ~90% explained variance, the stitch-initialized SAE reaches the threshold in 30–50% fewer FLOPs, and the approximate scaling law is shifted left across all latent sizes (4K–64K). For probing, transferred features without retraining already outperform random feature selection on 6 of 8 SAEBench datasets, and retraining the probe on transferred features recovers ground-truth SAE performance. For steering, the transferred vector matches or approaches ground-truth steering for high-frequency languages (e.g., de-en: 0.79 vs. 0.81 for 2b; de-en: 0.83 vs. 0.85 for 9b) but degrades sharply for low-frequency languages (e.g., bg-en: 0.01 vs. 0.31). The round-trip stitch (A→B→A) is nearly lossless (<1% CE increase), confirming the affine mapping is close to invertible despite the dimensionality gap, while the one-way A→B mapping is consistently bottlenecked by the smaller model's representational capacity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the model stitching framework from vision (Lenc & Vedaldi, 2015; Bansal et al., 2021) to decoder-only language models of different sizes, using simple affine mappings between residual streams. It builds on the SAE interpretability literature (Bricken et al., 2023; Gao et al., 2025) and prior work on transferring SAEs between base and chat models (Kissane et al., 2024) and steering vectors between models (Lee et al., 2025; Oozeer et al., 2025), but extends these to a small-to-large setting within the same model family and, crucially, repurposes the stitch as a practical initialization mechanism for SAE training rather than purely a comparison tool. The SVCCA-based layer selection (Raghu et al., 2017) and the inversion regularization in the training objective are methodological contributions that make the affine stitch sufficiently faithful for downstream feature transfer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 50% FLOPs savings for SAE training is a concrete, practical win for the interpretability community, which is often compute-constrained. The finding that structural features transfer consistently while semantic features are polarized (either transferring well or not at all) provides a useful lens for understanding how model scale allocates new representational dimensions. Key limitations include restriction to same-family models with shared tokenizers trained on general internet text, leaving cross-family transfer, chat-templated data, and the role of nonlinear features as open questions. The paper also notes that the transferred SAEs remain rank-limited to the smaller model's dimensionality, capping their zero-shot performance and motivating the continued-training approach.
