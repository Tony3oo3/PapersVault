## Problem

RNA inverse design—generating nucleotide sequences from a given 3D backbone—remains underexplored compared to protein design. Most existing methods either focus solely on secondary structure, require manual feature engineering for 3D structures, or can only produce a limited set of candidate sequences. RNA's greater conformational flexibility and the fact that a single 3D structure maps to a more diverse set of valid sequences than proteins make direct transfer of protein design algorithms inadequate.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RNA 3D backbone structure represented as a graph where each nucleotide is a node at the centroid of its P, C4', and N1/N9 atoms, with edges to the 32 nearest spatial neighbors (covering ~1 helical turn in A-form RNA). For multi-state design, multiple backbone conformations of the same sequence are provided as separate graphs. The output is a full-length nucleotide sequence over the 4-letter alphabet {A, C, G, U}, generated iteratively via Bayesian updates conditioned on the fixed backbone geometry.

## Method

RBFN combines a geometric-algebra-enhanced GNN backbone with a Bayesian Flow Network (BFN) generative framework. The GNN encodes each nucleotide's local tetrahedral geometry as a multivector in G_{3,0,0} (0-vector for P–C4' distance, 1-vectors for bond directions, 2-vectors for oriented planes, 3-vector for tetrahedral volume) and performs SE(3)-equivariant message passing via MVP-style layers. The BFN learns the conditional distribution of nucleotides at each position given the backbone, training by minimizing KL divergence between a sender distribution (noisy sequence) and a receiver distribution (network-predicted reverse process) over continuous time t∈[0,1]. A key innovation is a custom time-step sampling distribution p(t)=0.02·U(0,1)+0.98·B(1.0,1.9) that biases sampling toward small t values, ensuring the network focuses on the transition from the uniform prior to the true sequence distribution—critical given the small 4-nucleotide alphabet where adding noise at large t has minimal effect.

## Results

On the Das Benchmark (14 RNA structures), RBFN achieves an overall native sequence recovery rate of 60.1%, outperforming gRNAde (56.8%), RhoDesign (49.1%), Rosetta (45.0%), RDesign (43.0%), FARNA (32.0%), and ViennaRNA (27.0%). RBFN also achieves the lowest perplexity (1.16) and best 3D self-consistency metrics (scRMSD 10.83 Å, scGDT_TS 0.31) among all compared methods. On the multi-state benchmark, RBFN achieves 54.8% sequence recovery with 3 conformations and a scTM-score of 0.14, surpassing gRNAde's 54.0% recovery and 0.12 scTM-score.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RBFN achieves the highest overall sequence recovery (60.1%) on the Das Benchmark, beating gRNAde (56.8%) by 3.3 percentage points, RhoDesign (49.1%) by 11 points, and Rosetta (45.0%) by 15 points. However, RBFN does not win on every individual structure: gRNAde outperforms RBFN on 1ET4 (0.625 vs 0.448), 1XPE (0.704 vs 0.652), and 20EU (0.952 vs 0.558), while RhoDesign leads on 4FE5 (0.821 vs 0.533) and 354D (0.826 vs 0.803). On the multi-state benchmark, RBFN achieves lower perplexity (1.22–1.27) than all gRNAde variants (1.37–1.81) and better 3D scTM-score (0.14 vs 0.12 for single-state), though gRNAde's AR model with DSS pooling achieves slightly higher sequence recovery at 3 states (0.540 vs 0.548 for RBFN, a near-tie). Traditional 2D methods (ViennaRNA, FARNA) are substantially outperformed across all metrics, confirming the advantage of 3D-structure-aware design. RiboDiffusion reports a higher overall recovery (71.8%) but the authors note this likely reflects training/test overlap, as its code is not publicly available for verification.

## Relation to Prior Work

RBFN builds directly on gRNAde's geometric deep learning framework for 3D RNA inverse design, but replaces its autoregressive sequence generation with a Bayesian Flow Network that learns the full nucleotide distribution at each position simultaneously. The geometric algebra component draws on recent multivector neuron and Clifford GNN work (Liu et al. 2024, Brehmer et al. 2023) that has been applied to protein design but not previously to RNA. The paper also extends the single-state design setting to multi-state conformations, following gRNAde's multi-state capability, and introduces a task-specific time-sampling distribution motivated by the small 4-class alphabet of nucleotides.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RNA inverse design is critical for therapeutic applications (mRNA vaccines, siRNA therapeutics, riboswitch engineering), yet the field lags behind protein design due to limited 3D structural data and conformational flexibility. RBFN's distribution-based generation via BFNs is a principled alternative to autoregressive decoding that may better capture the one-to-many structure-to-sequence mapping inherent to RNA. However, the absence of wet-lab validation and the still-limited accuracy of RNA 3D structure prediction tools (RhoFold) mean that the practical biological utility of generated sequences remains unconfirmed. Open questions include whether the geometric algebra encoding scales to larger, more flexible RNA structures and whether the BFN framework can be extended to joint backbone-and-sequence de novo design.
