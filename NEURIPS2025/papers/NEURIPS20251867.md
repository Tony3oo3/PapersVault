## Problem

Vision transformer features remain poorly understood across all layers, with prior work limited to late-layer qualitative examples, and naively applying LM circuit-discovery techniques to ViTs yields circuits too large for human-scale interpretability due to hundreds of image tokens and complex attention interactions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251867/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained ViT (ViT-B/16, CLIP, or DINOv2) and RGB images (ImageNet validation set, 1,500 images for circuit evaluation). It produces a directed acyclic graph whose nodes are SAE features extracted from the residual stream at each layer and whose edges encode causal influence between adjacent layers; token activations are averaged across the spatial dimension to yield a single scalar per feature per layer, producing a CNN-like, layer-by-layer explanation rather than a per-token graph. The framework also outputs a debiasing intervention by ablating identified spurious features.

## Method

The residual replacement model trains TopK SAEs on the residual stream of every layer to decompose polysemantic neurons into sparse, interpretable features, then formalizes the ViT as a directed acyclic graph over these features across layers. Edge importance is estimated via attribution patching with a LibraGrad extension that prunes and scales backward paths to correct noisy ViT gradients, yielding up to 1.6× faithfulness improvement. Circuits are discovered by recursive edge-based selection: starting from the final layer, top-k features are chosen by the aggregated importance of their specific edges to already-selected downstream nodes, rather than by total outgoing importance, which the authors show preserves stronger causality. A Jacobian-vector-product trick reduces edge-importance computation from O(T×f) to O(f) backward passes per layer, enabling circuit extraction in seconds. The framework is validated on 6.6K human-annotated features across all layers of three model families and demonstrated to identify and ablate spurious correlations (e.g., graffiti in freight-car images) with minimal accuracy loss.

## Results

The residual replacement model achieves 94.1% circuit faithfulness on ViT-B/16 (85.1% on DINOv2, 82.3% on CLIP) with 99.6–99.8% 1-completeness, substantially outperforming the naive feature circuit (64.9% faithfulness on ViT) and random circuits (30.2%). Edge-based discovery and gradient correction (LibraGrad extension) contribute +9.3 and +19.9 percentage points to faithfulness respectively on ViT. For debiasing spurious correlations, ablating a single identified spurious feature improves mAUC from 0.854 to 0.904 while preserving accuracy (0.849→0.848), approaching the SpuFix upper bound of 0.917.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The only external baseline compared is SpuFix [Neuhaus et al., ICCV 2023], which performs data-driven spurious feature detection across entire training categories. The proposed single-feature ablation (identified from one image via the residual replacement model) achieves mAUC 0.904 versus SpuFix's 0.917, a gap of only 0.013, while matching its accuracy exactly (0.848). This suggests that the spurious feature identified from a single image generalizes well across the class. Internally, the full residual replacement model (edge-based discovery + gradient correction) improves faithfulness from 64.9% (naive feature circuit) to 94.1% on ViT, a 29.2-point gain, and from 30.2% (random circuit) by 63.9 points. Feature circuits consistently outperform neuron circuits across all metrics (e.g., 94.1% vs. 61.4% faithfulness on ViT), validating the SAE-based decomposition.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the replacement model framework from language models (Marks et al. 2024; Ameisen et al. 2025) to vision transformers, adapting it to the key challenge that ViTs process hundreds of patch tokens rather than short text sequences. It builds on TopK SAEs (Gao et al. 2024) for feature extraction, attribution patching (Nanda 2023) for causal edge estimation, and LibraGrad (Mehri et al. 2024) for gradient correction, while departing from prior LM circuit discovery by focusing exclusively on the residual stream and aggregating token-to-token interactions to avoid intractably large circuits. The feature analysis draws analogies to CNN circuit work (Olah et al. on InceptionV1 curve circuits) and LM position detector studies (Voita et al. 2024), positioning ViT features within a unified cross-architecture taxonomy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic, large-scale (6.6K features) analysis of ViT features across all layers and multiple training paradigms (supervised, self-supervised, contrastive), filling a gap where prior work focused only on late layers with limited qualitative examples. The residual replacement model provides a practical, seconds-scale tool for ViT interpretability that achieves 94% faithfulness on ViT, and the debiasing application demonstrates real-world utility. Key limitations include the indirect (gradient-based) treatment of attention and feed-forward modules, the subjectivity inherent in manual annotation, and reliance on TopK SAEs whose known limitations (feature splitting, non-canonical units) may affect circuit quality. Open questions include whether the framework extends to multimodal or generative vision models, and how to explicitly interpret attention head roles within the residual stream picture.
