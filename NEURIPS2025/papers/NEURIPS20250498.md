## Problem

Diffusion models require dozens to hundreds of iterative denoising steps for a single sample, making inference computationally expensive. While offline distillation offers a practical path to single-step generation by training on precomputed noise-image pairs without teacher access, prior methods have not exploited the structured semantic organization that trained diffusion dynamics impose on noise space, nor have they leveraged the powerful linearization tools from Koopman operator theory in dynamical systems.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250498/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single Gaussian noise vector x_T ~ N(0, σ_T² I) as input and produces a clean RGB image in one forward pass (1 NFE). For conditional generation, a class label is injected as a control signal that modulates the Koopman operator via an additive term C_μ(c), allowing the generative trajectory to adapt to the conditioning without architectural changes. Evaluation is conducted on 32×32 (CIFAR-10) and 64×64 (FFHQ, AFHQv2) images, with quality measured by FID and Inception Score over 50k samples.

## Method

KDM models the nonlinear noise-to-image transformation Φ_T as a single linear step in a learned embedding space: an encoder E_φ maps the noisy input to a latent observable space, a learned linear Koopman operator C_η propagates it forward, and a decoder D_ψ reconstructs the clean image. Training combines three Koopman-consistency losses (reconstruction, latent dynamics alignment, and end-to-end prediction) with an auxiliary adversarial loss for perceptual quality. A factorized variant (KDM-F) parameterizes C_η as PΛP⁻¹ with eigenvalues in polar form, enabling direct spectral penalties without on-the-fly eigendecomposition. Theoretically, the paper proves that analytic diffusion dynamics admit finite-dimensional Koopman representations with polynomial dimension scaling, and that proximity in Koopman coordinates implies semantic similarity of generated outputs, providing a formal guarantee that the linear embedding preserves the teacher's structural dynamics.

## Results

On unconditional CIFAR-10, KDM(EDM) achieves FID 4.65 and IS 9.21 with a single NFE, surpassing the prior offline baseline GET (FID 6.91, IS 9.16) by approximately 25% in FID. On class-conditional CIFAR-10, KDM-F(EDM) reaches FID 3.24 and IS 9.68, a ~40% FID improvement over GET (FID 6.25). On higher-resolution FFHQ 64×64 and AFHQv2 64×64, KDM(EDM) achieves FID 6.54 and 4.85 respectively, compared to the EDM teacher's 2.47 and 2.02 at 79 NFEs. KDM also achieves a 4× speedup per training iteration and 8× faster sampling over GET while matching its parameter count (62M).

## Baselines & Comparisons

KDM(EDM) outperforms all offline distillation baselines on unconditional CIFAR-10: it beats GET (FID 6.91→4.65, ~25% reduction), 2-rf++ w/o pre-train (6.32→4.65), and IMM (4.81→4.65) while also achieving the highest IS (9.21). On conditional CIFAR-10, KDM-F(EDM) achieves FID 3.24 vs GET's 6.25 (~40% improvement) and IS 9.68 vs 9.40. Against online distillation methods, a gap remains (iCD-EDM: FID 2.83; CTM: 1.98), but KDM narrows it substantially compared to prior offline approaches. KDM is denoiser-agnostic, successfully distilling both EDM and flow matching teachers (KDM-FM: FID 5.91), whereas methods like ECM and 2-rf++ rely on specific architectural assumptions. On FFHQ 64×64, KDM achieves FID 6.54 vs EDM's 2.47 at 79 NFEs, a ~2.5× degradation consistent with the CIFAR-10 gap, confirming scalability to higher resolutions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper applies Koopman operator theory—a classical framework for representing nonlinear dynamics as linear evolution in a lifted observable space (Koopman 1931; Lusch et al. 2018; Iacob et al. 2023)—to the problem of offline diffusion model distillation, a setting previously unexplored from this angle. It builds directly on the encoder–linear-operator–decoder architecture of Koopman autoencoders [53] and the finite-dimensional Koopman representation for polynomial dynamics [33], while competing with offline distillation baselines like GET [24], IMM [91], and RF-2++ [45]. The key departure is reframing the noise-to-image mapping as a single-step linear Koopman evolution in a learned embedding, rather than learning an end-to-end denoiser or consistency function, and providing theoretical guarantees (finite-dimensional exactness for analytic maps; semantic proximity preservation) that prior distillation methods lack.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a principled, theoretically grounded alternative to ad-hoc offline distillation objectives, and the empirical observation that trained diffusion models induce coherent semantic clusters in noise space provides a concrete structural justification for why a linear operator in a lifted space can capture the generative dynamics. Practically, KDM achieves ~25% FID reduction over GET with 4× faster training and 8× faster sampling, and is denoiser-agnostic (works with both EDM and flow matching). However, the evaluation is limited to 64×64 images (no ImageNet, no text-to-image), a performance gap with online distillation methods persists, and the theoretical assumptions (analyticity, local Lipschitzness) may not hold exactly for all practical architectures—leaving open whether the Koopman framing scales to high-resolution or conditional text-to-image settings.
