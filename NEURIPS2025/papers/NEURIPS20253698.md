## Problem

Most training-free OOD detection methods treat pre-trained vision-language models as monolithic encoders, relying exclusively on final-layer representations for detection. This overlooks the rich, diverse signals encoded in intermediate layers shaped by residual connections and progressive semantic abstraction. The paper asks whether intermediate representations can be systematically identified and fused to improve zero-shot OOD detection without any additional training or OOD data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253698/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images (processed at standard resolutions through backbones such as CLIP ViT-B/16, ViT-B/32, RN50x4, or ViT-L/14) and a set of in-distribution class labels converted into text prompts (e.g., 'a photo of a y_i'). At inference, it extracts intermediate visual features from multiple transformer layers, projects them into the shared text-embedding space, and computes per-layer MCM confidence scores. The output is a single fused OOD score per image (the mean of MCM scores across the entropy-selected layer subset), which is thresholded to yield an ID/OOD binary decision. The entropy-based layer selection itself requires only a small set of unlabeled ID samples to build score histograms—no OOD data, labels, or fine-tuning are needed.

## Method

The core contribution is a training-free extension of Maximum Concept Matching (MCM) that fuses confidence scores from multiple intermediate layers selected via an entropy-based criterion. For each layer ℓ, the visual feature is projected into the text-embedding space and the MCM score is computed as the maximum softmax-normalized cosine similarity against all class text embeddings. A set of candidate layer combinations (each always including the final layer, with size up to 5) is evaluated by binning their fused scores over unlabeled ID samples into a histogram and computing the Shannon entropy of that histogram; the combination minimizing entropy is selected as the most discriminative. At test time, the final OOD score is the arithmetic mean of MCM scores across the selected layers. The key empirical insight is that contrastive models like CLIP exhibit high inter-layer representational diversity (low SVCCA similarity) and stable top-1 prediction agreement across depth, making them particularly amenable to multi-layer fusion, whereas supervised and self-supervised ViTs show redundancy or abrupt prediction shifts that limit fusion gains.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K (ID) with CLIP ViT-B/16, the proposed method achieves an average FPR@95 of 30.70% and AUROC of 93.54 across four far-OOD benchmarks, outperforming the strongest training-free baseline (SeTAR+GL-MCM) by 2.42 FPR points and 2.22 AUROC points. On Pascal-VOC (ID) with ViT-B/16, it reaches 26.97% FPR@95 and 93.52 AUROC across six OOD datasets. On near-OOD benchmarks (NINCO and SSB-Hard), the method attains 75.71 AUROC and 74.77 FPR@95, surpassing the best fine-tuned baseline (SeTAR+FT) by 2.09 AUROC and 2.66 FPR points while remaining fully training-free.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MCM (final-layer only), the proposed method reduces average FPR@95 by 11.57 points on ImageNet-1K (30.70 vs. 42.27) and by 13.23 points on Pascal-VOC (26.97 vs. 40.20), with corresponding AUROC gains of 2.71 and 0.86 points. Compared to GL-MCM, the method improves FPR@95 by 4.55 points on ImageNet-1K (30.70 vs. 35.25) and by 2.47 points on Pascal-VOC (26.97 vs. 29.44), though GL-MCM retains a slight AUROC edge on Pascal-VOC (93.88 vs. 93.52). Versus the strongest composite baseline SeTAR+GL-MCM, the method wins on ImageNet-1K (30.70 vs. 33.12 FPR; 93.54 vs. 91.32 AUC) but is marginally behind on Pascal-VOC AUC (93.52 vs. 94.56) while matching FPR (26.97 vs. 26.74). On near-OOD benchmarks, the method outperforms LoCoOp by 5.90 AUROC and 9.99 FPR points, and surpasses the fine-tuned SeTAR+FT by 2.09 AUROC and 2.66 FPR points, demonstrating that training-free intermediate-layer fusion can match or exceed fine-tuning-based approaches on subtle distribution shifts.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Maximum Concept Matching (MCM) [47], which performs zero-shot OOD detection using CLIP's final-layer image-text alignment, by exploiting intermediate-layer representations across the visual encoder. It departs from the dominant 'final layer only' paradigm in confidence-based OOD detection (MSP, energy, MCM) and from prior intermediate-layer work that required additional supervision or retraining (e.g., Fayyad et al. [18], Lin et al. [43]), offering instead a fully training-free, inference-only extension. The systematic analysis across seven backbones and three training paradigms (contrastive, self-supervised, supervised) builds on early observations of layer-wise functional specialization in CNNs [2] and on SVCCA-based representational similarity analysis [59], but applies them specifically to the VLM-based OOD detection setting for the first time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that intermediate layers in contrastive models like CLIP encode complementary OOD signals—while the same fusion strategy degrades performance in supervised or self-supervised ViTs—reveals that architectural design, not just depth, governs the utility of internal representations for detection. This opens a practical, drop-in improvement path for existing CLIP-based OOD pipelines without any retraining or OOD data. Key open questions include whether the entropy-based selection can be made dataset-agnostic (currently the optimal layer set shifts across ID distributions), whether adaptive or weighted fusion can extend benefits to architectures like DINOv2 and MAE, and whether the approach generalizes beyond vision to other modalities.
