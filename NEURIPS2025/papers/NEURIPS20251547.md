## Problem

Existing test-time adaptation methods for stereo depth estimation assume a single stationary target domain and use input-invariant PEFT modules, making them ineffective under continual domain shifts where weather, lighting, and scene structure evolve over time. Additionally, handcrafted stereo pseudo-labels (e.g., SGM) are sparsified by confidence thresholding, causing the model to over-rely on reliable regions and degrade in occluded, reflective, or low-texture areas during long-term adaptation.

## Input / Output

The method takes a stereo image pair (left and right RGB frames) from real-world driving sequences and produces a dense disparity map. During CTTA, the model processes frames sequentially in an online protocol—predicting before updating—across short sequences of 500 frames per domain that cycle through 3–4 domains over 10 rounds. The AttEx-MoE module operates at 1/32 spatial resolution within the CoEx (MobileNetV2) encoder, while the AdaptBN teacher runs as a separate lightweight inference pass to generate dense pseudo-labels for low-confidence regions.

## Method

RobIA introduces two complementary components for continual test-time adaptation. First, AttEx-MoE replaces the shallow-embedding router of DeepMoE with a row-wise self-attention mechanism that computes 1D attention along each epipolar line to extract global spatial context, then feeds this into a sigmoid-activated gating network that softly excites all frozen convolutional channel experts to varying degrees—enabling input-specific adaptation without modifying the backbone. Second, a Robust AdaptBN Teacher (adapting only BN affine parameters γ, β) generates dense pseudo-disparity predictions that fill in the low-confidence regions where handcrafted SGM labels are absent. The student is trained with a dual-source loss: smooth-L1 against SGM pseudo-labels in valid regions plus a weighted smooth-L1 against the teacher's predictions in invalid regions (λ=0.1), providing full spatial coverage while preserving the domain-agnostic reliability of handcrafted labels where they are trustworthy.

## Results

On the DrivingStereo CTTA benchmark (10 rounds, 3 weather domains), RobIA with AttEx-MoE + AdaptBN Teacher (AT) achieves a mean D1-all of 2.77% and EPE of 0.91 px, outperforming full fine-tuning (FT, 3.04%/0.92) and the prior best PEFT method EcoTTA MetaNet (3.07%/0.95). On DSEC (nighttime, 4 domains), RobIA+AT reaches 4.46%/1.11, beating FT+AT (4.38%/1.10) and EcoTTA (5.13%/1.21). On KITTI RAW (4 scene types), RobIA+AT achieves 1.09%/0.84, comparable to FT+AT (0.82%/0.82) while using only 1.22M trainable parameters vs. 2.79M for FT. The method uses ~50% fewer trainable parameters than full tuning while matching or exceeding its accuracy under continual domain shifts.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RobIA+AT (2.77% D1-all) outperforms full fine-tuning on CoEx (3.04%) by 0.27% and EcoTTA MetaNet (3.07%) by 0.30% on DrivingStereo, while using roughly half the trainable parameters (1.22M vs. 2.73M). Against MADNet2's state-of-the-art TTA variants (MAD++: 5.86%), RobIA achieves a 3.09% absolute D1-all reduction, demonstrating that PEFT-based CTTA with instance-aware routing substantially surpasses modular full-model updates under continual shifts. On DSEC, RobIA+AT (4.46%) edges out FT+AT (4.38%) and clearly beats EcoTTA (5.13%), confirming that the advantage grows with domain difficulty. On KITTI RAW, where pseudo-labels are dense (~92% valid), FT+AT (0.82%) slightly outperforms RobIA+AT (1.09%), suggesting the dual-source supervision benefit is most pronounced under sparser supervision. Across all three datasets, RobIA consistently outperforms the AdaptBN-only baseline (3.08%/4.54%/0.91%), showing that the AttEx-MoE routing provides meaningful gains beyond simple BN affine tuning. The method also generalizes to IGEV-Stereo (2.76% vs. 3.56% FT) and LightStereo (4.95% vs. 5.91% FT) backbones, confirming plug-and-play applicability.

## Relation to Prior Work

RobIA extends the DeepMoE framework (Wang et al., UAI 2020), which treats individual CNN channels as experts gated by a shallow embedding, by replacing the shallow embedding with a row-wise self-attention router that leverages epipolar geometry and swapping ReLU sparsity for Sigmoid soft gating to better suit frozen-backbone PEFT. On the supervision side, it repurposes AdaptBN (Schneider et al., NeurIPS 2020) not as the student's adaptation mechanism but as a lightweight teacher that fills in the unreliable regions of sparse handcrafted SGM pseudo-labels. The paper departs from prior stereo TTA work (MADNet2, MAD++) that assumes a single static target domain, and from general CTTA methods (EcoTTA, VIDA) that use input-invariant PEFT modules, by introducing instance-aware expert routing and dual-source dense supervision specifically tailored to the dense-prediction nature of disparity estimation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The continual test-time adaptation setting for stereo depth estimation is practically critical for autonomous driving, where weather, lighting, and scene structure shift frequently and previously seen conditions reappear. The dual-source supervision strategy (reliable handcrafted labels in confident regions + AdaptBN teacher in uncertain regions) offers a principled solution to the over-reliance problem of sparse pseudo-labels, and the plug-and-play AttEx-MoE module generalizes across multiple stereo backbones (CoEx, IGEV-Stereo, LightStereo, MADNet2). A key open question is whether the predefined expert structure can be refined online during long-term deployment, and whether the approach extends to other dense-prediction tasks (e.g., semantic segmentation) where similar sparsity and input-invariance issues arise.
