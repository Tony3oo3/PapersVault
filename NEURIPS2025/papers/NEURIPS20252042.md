## Problem

Minimax optimal rates for distributed estimation of discrete distributions under communication constraints were previously characterized only for n=1 samples per terminal or restricted to ℓ¹/ℓ² losses, with suboptimal lower bounds for p>2. When each terminal holds n>1 samples and general ℓ^p losses are imposed, the optimal rates were unknown because the naive uniform budget allocation strategy that works for ℓ¹ fails for p>1, and the inherent structure of the n>1 problem is not revealed in the n=1 case.

## Input / Output

The problem takes m distributed terminals, each observing n i.i.d. samples from an unknown k-ary discrete distribution, with a hard constraint of l bits per terminal per round. The protocol is sequentially interactive: encoder i can observe messages from encoders 1 through i−1 before transmitting its own l-bit message. The decoder outputs a reconstruction p̂_W in the probability simplex Δ_W, and performance is measured by the minimax ℓ^p loss R(m,n,k,l,p) = inf_protocol sup_{p_W} E[‖p̂_W − p_W‖_p^p].

## Method

The core contribution is a family of refinement methods that first generate a rough estimate from partial communication budgets, then allocate the remaining budgets adaptively based on that rough estimate. For 1≤p≤2, a successive refinement protocol uses divide-and-conquer: the alphabet is partitioned into blocks, the lower-dimensional block distribution is estimated first, and then conditional distributions within each block are estimated with encoder counts proportional to the estimated block probabilities (rather than uniformly). For p>2, sample compression techniques project samples onto the set of large-probability entries to save communication, and thresholding approximates negligible entries by zero under extremely tight budgets. For the special case n=1, a non-interactive protocol based on random hash functions achieves the optimal rate, resolving the p>2 case left open by prior work. The optimality is established by deriving compatible minimax lower bounds via multiple reductions to hypothesis testing, revealing an elbow effect in the optimal rates at p=2.

## Results

The paper establishes minimax optimal rates R(m,n,k,l,p) for distributed estimation of k-dimensional discrete distributions with m terminals, n samples each, and l-bit communication budget, under general ℓ^p losses. A key finding is an elbow effect at p=2: the centralized (unconstrained) bound is k^{1−p/2}/(mn)^{p/2} for 1≤p<2 but drops to 1/(mn)^{p/2} (independent of k) for p≥2. For p>2 and medium n (k/(2^l)^{p/2} ≤ n < k), the optimal rate is independent of k up to logarithmic factors, and the minimum bits needed to match the centralized rate shrinks from k to k^{2/p} (or from n to n^{2/p}). In the extremely tight budget regime (ml≪k), the rate depends only on the total budget ml and is independent of both k and n — a regime not previously studied.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's main comparison is against three prior works. Against Acharya et al. [14] (NeurIPS 2021), which established optimal rates for the ℓ^1 (TV) loss with n>1 samples via a one-step block reduction, this paper extends the protocol to general ℓ^p losses and replaces the single reduction step with a multi-step successive refinement procedure, achieving optimal rates over a strictly wider parameter range (e.g., removing the restriction m>100·k/2^l·log m·log n in the small-n regime). Against Acharya et al. [16] (NeurIPS 2023) and Chen & Özgür [17] (ISIT 2024), which provided lower bounds for p>2 that the authors show are suboptimal, this paper derives strictly tighter lower bounds via a binary hypothesis testing reduction and an algebraic technique, and constructs matching upper-bound protocols. Specifically, [17] claimed the rate k/(m2^l)^{p/2} ∨ k^{1−p/2}/m^{p/2} was optimal for n=1, p>2, but this paper proves the correct rate is k/(m2^l)^{p/2} ∨ 1/m^{p/2} (with the centralized term independent of k), and provides a non-interactive random-hashing protocol achieving it. The one gap remains for p>2 with n<k/(2^l)^{p/2} and mn2^l≥k², where upper and lower bounds do not coincide, though the n=1 special case is fully resolved.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the communication-constrained distributed distribution estimation problem in two simultaneous directions: from n=1 to n>1 samples per terminal, and from ℓ¹/ℓ² to general ℓᵖ losses. It builds directly on Acharya et al. [14], whose one-bit protocol and one-step block reduction under TV loss serve as the base subroutine, but replaces the single reduction with a multi-step successive refinement protocol using adaptive budget allocation. For p>2, it departs from the suboptimal lower bounds of Acharya et al. [16] and Chen & Özgür [17] by introducing new hypothesis-testing reductions (binary testing for the centralized bound, algebraic techniques for communication-constrained bounds) that yield tight rates previously left open.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of an elbow effect at p=2 in the minimax optimal rates is a clean structural insight: for p≥2 the centralized bound becomes independent of the alphabet size k, and the hardest distributions to estimate shift from near-uniform (p≤2) to sparse with a few large entries (p>2). This parallels the elbow effect in nonparametric density estimation, suggesting a general principle about how loss functions interact with normalization constraints. Open questions include whether non-interactive protocols can achieve optimal rates for n>1 under ℓᵖ losses, the remaining gap in one regime for p>2 (conjectured tight), and extending the framework to privacy-constrained settings with n>1.
