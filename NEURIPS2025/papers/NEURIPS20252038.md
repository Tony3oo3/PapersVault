## Problem

Reasoning models such as DeepSeek-R1 generate excessively long chain-of-thought outputs (8–14× longer than ground truth), causing KV cache memory to grow unsustainably during autoregressive decoding. Existing attention-based KV cache compression methods (e.g., SnapKV) fail in this setting because repetitive self-reflection tokens receive disproportionately high attention scores, leading to over-retention of redundant content and eviction of critical but scattered reasoning steps.

## Input / Output

R-KV operates at the decoding stage (not prefilling), consuming the full KV cache states of length L_full and the query states of the last α=8 observation tokens. Compression is triggered every B_buffer=128 newly generated tokens: the method concatenates the existing B_budget cached tokens with the first B_buffer−α buffer tokens to form n candidates, scores each, and retains the top k=B_budget−α tokens plus the α observation tokens. The output is a fixed-size compressed KV cache whose memory footprint remains constant regardless of total generation length, unlike FullKV which scales linearly.

## Method

R-KV jointly scores each candidate token per attention head using two complementary signals. First, an importance score I_i^h is computed from attention weights between the α observation query tokens and all candidate key tokens (supporting both MHA and GQA via max-pooling across query-head groups), stabilized by a sliding-window max-pool of size 2W to suppress outliers. Second, a redundancy score R_i^h is derived from the softmax-normalized row mean of a cosine-similarity matrix over L2-normalized key vectors, with the β most recent highly similar tokens explicitly protected from being marked redundant. The final selection score Z_i^h = λI_i^h − (1−λ)R_i^h (λ=0.1) is averaged across heads, and the top-B_budget tokens are retained. This redundancy-aware penalty is the key differentiator: it evicts tokens that are simultaneously unimportant and semantically repetitive, while preserving important tokens even if they share some similarity with neighbors, achieving near-lossless compression at 10–34% of the original KV cache.

## Results

On AIME-2024 with R1-Llama-8B, R-KV achieves 51.56% pass@1 with a 1536-token KV budget (≈10% of average generation length), surpassing FullKV's 49.79% (105% of full-cache accuracy), while SnapKV reaches only 26.04% at the same budget. On MATH-500, R-KV matches FullKV (82.34% vs 82.38%) at a 1536-token budget (≈34% of generation length), whereas SnapKV lags at 78.43%. For R1-Qwen-14B on AIME-2024, R-KV reaches 64.32% at a 3072-token budget (≈25% of generation length) versus SnapKV's 52.86%, and achieves 67.45% at 4096 tokens (≈33%), exceeding FullKV's 65.68%. R-KV delivers up to 90% memory savings and 6.6× throughput improvement over FullKV at 16K sequence length.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares R-KV against SnapKV, the primary attention-based KV cache eviction baseline, adapted to the decoding stage with the same compression interval (every 128 tokens) and identical budget/buffer settings. R-KV consistently and substantially outperforms SnapKV across all evaluated budgets, models, and datasets. The gap is most dramatic on AIME-2024, where long generations amplify redundancy: at a 1536-token budget on R1-Llama-8B, R-KV scores 51.56% versus SnapKV's 26.04% (a 25.5-point gap), and at 3072 tokens on R1-Qwen-14B, R-KV reaches 64.32% versus SnapKV's 52.86%. On the easier MATH-500 benchmark the gap narrows (e.g., 82.34% vs 78.43% at 1536 tokens for R1-Llama-8B), but R-KV still leads by 3–12 points depending on budget. The paper attributes SnapKV's underperformance to its inability to handle redundancy: repetitive self-reflection tokens receive high attention scores and are over-retained, while scattered but critical reasoning tokens may be pruned. R-KV's redundancy-aware scoring corrects this by penalizing semantically similar key vectors, enabling it to match or exceed FullKV accuracy at 10–34% of the KV cache budget.

## Relation to Prior Work

R-KV builds directly on SnapKV's attention-based importance scoring (observation window, sliding-window max-pooling stabilization) and extends it to the decoding stage, where prior KV cache compression methods (SnapKV, PyramidKV, H2O, StreamingLLM) were designed for long-input prefilling. The key conceptual departure is the addition of a redundancy estimation component via cosine similarity of key vectors, motivated by the observation that reasoning models' repetitive self-reflection tokens receive disproportionately high attention scores, causing pure attention-based eviction to retain redundant content. This positions R-KV as the first method explicitly targeting decoding-time compression for reasoning models, where generated outputs are 8–14× longer than ground truth.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial for deploying reasoning LLMs: 90% memory savings and 6.6× throughput gains with near-lossless accuracy make long CoT generation feasible under constrained hardware, and the training-free, model-agnostic design enables direct use in RL rollout pipelines. However, evaluation is limited to two math benchmarks (MATH-500, AIME-24) and two DeepSeek-R1-distilled model families, leaving open whether the redundancy-aware strategy generalizes to other reasoning domains (code, science) or non-distilled reasoning models. Integration with production serving frameworks (e.g., paged attention in vLLM) remains an acknowledged engineering challenge that could limit real-world adoption.
