## Problem

Existing model-based intrinsic motivation methods (ICM, RND, Disagreement, γ-Progress) produce inconsistent, non-stationary exploration that fails to converge to a stable behavioral policy, making them unable to capture the robust, repeating state transitions observed in autonomous animal behavior. Simultaneously, systems neuroscience has largely ignored the neural basis of autonomy, focusing on reward-motivated tasks rather than naturalistic, task-independent exploration, leaving the cellular mechanisms underlying whole-brain autonomous behavior poorly understood.

## Input / Output

The agent consumes 64×64 egocentric pixel observations (matching larval zebrafish visual acuity) and proprioceptive joint positions/velocities, encoded by a 3-layer ResNet and a 2-layer MLP respectively, then outputs 5-dimensional continuous joint torques in [-1,1] controlling a 6-link swimmer in MuJoCo. Critically, the agent is trained entirely via self-supervised intrinsic reward with zero behavioral or neural training data, yet its internal LSTM hidden states are evaluated against whole-brain calcium imaging from over 250,000 cells (~125K neurons, ~125K astrocytes) using a strict one-to-one unit mapping.

## Method

3M-Progress (Model-Memory-Mismatch Progress) maintains two forward-dynamics world models in sensory feature space: a frozen 'ethological prior' ω_θ pretrained in a naturalistic swimming environment, and an online model ω_θ' learned in the target environment. The model-memory-mismatch ε_t is the KL divergence between their next-state predictions, passed through an exponential moving average (γ=0.99) to form a leaky integrator ε̂_t, and the intrinsic reward is r_t^i = |ε̂_t − ε_t|. The absolute value enforces symmetry between niche-seeking (agreement) and niche-avoidance (disagreement) partitions, while the fixed prior prevents the reward from saturating as learning progresses—unlike γ-Progress where both memories move. The agent uses an actor-critic LSTM architecture trained with PPO, where the intrinsic drive module (a 2-layer MLP forward model) generates reward but is not used for planning, and an action penalty encourages passive behavior. The paper further provides a formal proof that prediction-error-based intrinsic rewards cannot converge to a stationary exploratory policy, and shows that 3M-Progress's internal units functionally correspond to noradrenergic neurons and radial astrocytes in the lateral medulla oblongata.

## Results

3M-Progress agents achieve the highest model-behavioral alignment (Pearson's r between agent joint-torque swim power and zebrafish motor-neuron swim power) across both active-to-passive and passive-to-active transitions, discovering stable ethological state cycling by ~10M environment steps. On whole-brain neural-glial alignment (noise-corrected Pearson's r under a strict One-to-One unit mapping), 3M-Progress captures nearly all of the explainable variance in both neuronal and astrocytic calcium dynamics, approaching the ~100% inter-animal correlation ceiling. No other intrinsic drive or control model (ICM, RND, Disagreement, γ-Progress, homeostatic, MaxEnt, random, GP, PID, population-average, or white-noise) simultaneously achieves high scores on both behavioral and neural-glial alignment; 3M-Progress is the only model to pass the NeuroAI Turing Test criterion on this dataset.

## Baselines & Comparisons

3M-Progress decisively outperforms all four model-based intrinsic motivation baselines (ICM, RND, Disagreement, γ-Progress) on both behavioral and neural-glial alignment. ICM, RND, and Disagreement all converge to complete passivity or activity over training and fail to produce the stable active-passive cycling observed in zebrafish, yielding significantly lower Pearson's r on swim-power trajectories. γ-Progress, which shares the exponential-filter mechanism with 3M-Progress, produces transient strategies but still fails to converge to a stable exploratory policy because its moving baseline is non-stationary (tied to the online model's training loss). Among model-free and data-derived controls, the PID controller (implementing the Mu et al. circuit model) achieves moderate behavioral alignment but poor neural-glial alignment, while the Gaussian Process and population-average controls capture some neural variance but no behavioral structure. 3M-Progress is the only method that simultaneously saturates both alignment metrics, demonstrating that accurate behavioral modeling tightly constrains the learned internal representations to match whole-brain neural-glial dynamics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the curiosity-driven exploration literature (ICM, RND, Disagreement, γ-Progress) by identifying a fundamental non-stationarity problem: all existing methods couple intrinsic reward to a moving world model, creating a two-player minimax game that precludes convergence to a stable exploratory policy. The key departure is 3M-Progress, which freezes a world model pretrained in an ecological niche as a fixed prior, making the intrinsic objective stationary while retaining the derivative-like structure of γ-Progress. On the neuroscience side, the paper departs from both phenomenological neuron-astrocyte models and bottom-up circuit models by adopting a top-down approach: training a general-purpose recurrent architecture via intrinsic RL and showing that the resulting internal representations are constrained to match whole-brain neural-glial dynamics, thereby providing a normative (not just descriptive) model of astrocytic computation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that a purely self-supervised intrinsic RL agent—trained on no behavioral or neural data—can capture nearly all explainable variance in whole-brain neural-glial activity is a strong validation that accurate behavioral modeling tightly constrains internal neural dynamics. This provides the first computational framework connecting model-based intrinsic motivation to naturalistic, task-independent animal behavior and positions intrinsic RL as a tool for understanding autonomy in biological systems. Key limitations include the narrow scope (single behavior: futility-induced passivity; single species: larval zebrafish; constrained virtual environment), the abstraction of detailed biochemical signaling and astrocyte anatomy, and the near-100% inter-animal reliability that makes the benchmark comparatively easy to saturate. Open questions include whether 3M-Progress generalizes to richer ecological settings, more complex behavioral repertoires, and whether the fixed-prior mechanism scales to multi-niche or lifelong learning scenarios.
