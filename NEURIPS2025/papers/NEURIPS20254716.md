## Problem

In strategic classification, where individuals adjust their features to influence ML decisions, deterministic threshold classifiers inherently violate individual fairness—both in prediction outcomes and in the cost (Best Response Cost) individuals must pay to cross the threshold. While group fairness in strategic settings has received attention, individual fairness in this context remains largely unexplored, leaving a gap in ensuring equitable treatment for individuals near decision boundaries.

## Input / Output

The method takes a d-dimensional tabular feature vector x (plus a sensitive attribute a for group fairness) and a scalar score function l(x) that monotone-encodes qualification. It produces a binary label via a randomized threshold drawn from a piecewise-constant distribution p(t) over K bins in (C+𝒞, D), where 𝒞 = g⁻¹(1/λ) is determined by the cost function and individual sensitivity parameter λ. The key tunable output is the per-bin probability mass {p_c1, …, p_cK}, which simultaneously controls accuracy and the individual fairness bound.

## Method

The paper proves that any deterministic threshold classifier violates individual fairness w.r.t. BRC and outcome under a reverse-Lipschitz condition on l(x), then shows that bounding the threshold density p(t) ≤ L_c (for BRC fairness) or p(t) ≤ L_p (for outcome fairness) guarantees the Lipschitz individual fairness condition. The optimal threshold distribution minimizing expected error subject to these fairness bounds is formulated as a linear program over K piecewise-constant bins, solvable via the simplex method. For group fairness, a statistical parity (or equal opportunity/equalized odds) constraint is added as a linear equality across group-specific threshold distributions, preserving the LP structure. The approach is validated on FICO credit-score and Law School bar-exam datasets, demonstrating large reductions in the IF ratio with minimal accuracy loss.

## Results

On the FICO dataset, the randomized classifier reduces the Individual Fairness (IF) ratio from 4.421 (deterministic) to as low as 0.100 (L_p=0.1) while maintaining a near-identical F1 score (0.975 vs. 0.986). On the Law School dataset, the IF ratio drops from 3.164 to 0.175 (L_p=0.3) at the cost of F1 decreasing from 0.680 to 0.545. When combined with a statistical parity constraint (Ω=0.04), the randomized classifier simultaneously improves both individual fairness (IF ratio from 21.58 to 1.000 on FICO; 12.15 to 0.689 on Law School) and group fairness (S-DP from 0.115 to 0.050 on FICO; 0.047 to 0.026 on Law School).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is the best deterministic threshold classifier (found via grid search maximizing F1 while satisfying group fairness constraints). The randomized classifier strictly dominates the deterministic baseline on individual fairness: on FICO, the IF ratio drops from 4.421 to 0.100 (a 97.7% reduction) with only a 1.1% F1 drop, and on Law School it drops from 3.164 to 0.175 (94.5% reduction) at a larger F1 cost (0.680→0.545). Group fairness metrics (S-DP, EO-DP, ED-DP) remain essentially unchanged on FICO across all L_p values, indicating that individual fairness improvements do not come at the expense of group fairness. When the statistical parity constraint is added (Tables 3–4), the randomized classifier further reduces S-DP (e.g., 0.115→0.050 on FICO at Ω=0.04) while simultaneously achieving a much lower IF ratio than the deterministic baseline (1.000 vs. 21.58), demonstrating that individual and group fairness can be improved jointly.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of strategic classification (Hardt et al., 2016) and individual fairness (Dwork et al., 2012), drawing on the Best Response Cost framework from Milli et al. (2019) to formalize fairness with respect to the cost individuals pay to game a classifier. While prior work on fairness in strategic settings has focused predominantly on group fairness criteria (Diana et al., 2024; Estornell et al., 2023), this paper deliberately shifts attention to individual fairness—both with respect to prediction outcomes and with respect to BRC—which the authors identify as an open problem. The key methodological departure is the use of randomized threshold distributions constrained via linear programming, rather than the deterministic thresholds or group-specific thresholds used in prior strategic classification work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean negative result (deterministic thresholds inherently violate individual fairness in strategic settings) and a constructive positive result (a simple LP yields the optimal fair randomized classifier), offering a practical and computationally efficient tool for institutions deploying strategic classifiers. The extension to group fairness via an additional LP constraint shows that individual and group fairness can be jointly enforced without sacrificing the tractability of the formulation. Key limitations include the requirement that the problem be reducible to a threshold on a single scalar l(x), the assumption that cost depends only on the change in l(x), and the potential exponential worst-case complexity of the simplex method in the number of bins K—open questions include whether the approach extends to multi-dimensional cost structures or non-threshold classifiers.
