## Problem

Recent empirical work shows that gradient descent struggles to train the first and last layers of transformer language models, a difficulty attributed to the heavy-tailed (Zipfian) distribution of word frequencies, while Adam/sign descent overcomes it. Existing theoretical scaling laws for optimization assume eigenvalue decay with exponent α>1, which makes the problem effectively finite-dimensional and yields dimension-independent power-law rates. This paper fills the gap by deriving scaling laws for the α≤1 regime—covering Zipf's law (α=1)—where the number of iterations must grow with vocabulary size and the rates take qualitatively different functional forms.

## Input / Output

The method analyzes a d×d linear bigram model trained with square loss on one-hot encoded (x, y) token pairs, where word frequencies π_k and conditional frequencies π_{k|j} follow a power law 1/k^α. The output is the asymptotic relative optimality gap r_d(t) = (L_d(t) − L_d*)/(L_d(0) − L_d*) as d→∞ under a rescaled time variable τ, yielding explicit formulas for how many iterations t are needed to reach an ε-relative error as a function of d and α. For sign descent, the analysis additionally requires choosing a step-size η(T, φ) that depends on the iteration budget T and a parameter φ controlling how many directions remain in the decreasing regime.

## Method

The key structural insight is that the Hessian of the bigram loss is diagonal with eigenvalues λ_ij = π_i, reducing the d²-dimensional dynamics to d independent 1D problems whose convergence speed is (1 − π_k/π_1)^{2t} weighted by the error contribution π_k. Under the power-law frequency assumption (Assumption 2.3), the relative loss reduces to a single sum r_d(t) = Σ k^{−α}(1−k^{−α})^{2t} / H_{d,α}, which is analyzed by replacing the sum with an integral and taking d→∞ with a dimension-dependent time rescaling t_d(τ). For GD, this yields three regimes: t ∼ d^α for α<1, t ∼ d^{1−ε} for α=1 (Zipf), and t = O(1) for α>1. For sign descent, a simplified oscillation-averaging assumption (Assumption 4.1) and a step-size reparameterization η = 1/(zTφ^α) lead to a threshold at α=1/2: SD needs no dimension scaling for α<1/2 but only t ∼ √d for α≥1/2, giving a provable √d speedup over GD's near-linear scaling under Zipf's law.

## Results

The paper derives asymptotic scaling laws for the number of iterations t needed to reach an ε-relative optimality gap as a function of vocabulary size d and power-law exponent α. For gradient descent (GD), t scales as d^α for α < 1, d^(1−ε) for α = 1 (Zipf), and is dimension-independent for α > 1. For sign descent (SD), t scales as a constant for α < 1/2, d^((1−ε)/2) for α = 1/2, and d^(1/2) for α > 1/2. The headline result is that for Zipf-distributed data (α = 1), GD requires t ~ d^(1−ε) (nearly linear in d) while SD requires only t ~ d^(1/2), yielding a 100× speedup at d = 10⁴. The relative optimality gap for GD at α = 1 takes the non-power-law form r_d(t) ≈ 1 − log(2t)/log(d).

**Informal Theorem: Scaling of iterations t to reach ε-relative optimality gap.**

| Regime | GD: t ∝ | SD: t ∝ |
|---|---|---|
| α < 1 (or α < 1/2 for SD) | d^α | 1 (constant) |
| α = 1 (or α = 1/2 for SD) | d^(1−ε) | d^((1−ε)/2) |
| α > 1 (or α > 1/2 for SD) | 1 (constant) | d^(1/2) |

*Note: t ≍ f means t = Θ(f), hiding constants and factors depending on ε but not d. For GD at α = 1, the relative gap is r_d(t) ≈ 1 − log(2t)/log(d); for SD at α = 1, r(τ) ≈ 1/(1 + ζ(2)τ²) with τ = 2t/√d.*

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is between GD and SD on the same linear bigram problem. SD provably outperforms GD for all α ≤ 1 (the heavy-tailed regime relevant to language), with the gap largest at α = 1 where SD needs d^(1/2) iterations versus GD's d^(1−ε). Conversely, GD outperforms SD for α > 1 (lighter tails), where GD is dimension-independent while SD still requires t ~ d^(1/2). Against existing theoretical bounds, the paper shows that standard smooth-convex rates (sublinear: t ~ d/log(d); linear: t ~ d) are too pessimistic and predict no progress for t < d, whereas the actual GD dynamics make meaningful progress at t = d^(1/2). The AdaGrad bound from Liu et al. (2020) predicts t ~ d·log(d) for sign-like methods, and the Adam preconditioning bound from Das et al. (2024) predicts t ~ d for diagonal quadratics with Zipfian eigenvalues; both are significantly worse than the d^(1/2) scaling derived here for SD. The paper also notes that non-convex convergence results (measuring gradient 1-norm) can be arbitrarily loose: SD can achieve small relative error at t ~ d^(1/2) while the normalized gradient 1-norm remains O(√d/log(d)).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the theoretical scaling laws literature (Caponnetto & De Vito, 2007; Bahri et al., 2021; Paquette et al., 2024), which has focused on power-law spectral decay with exponent α>1 yielding dimension-independent convergence rates, to the heavy-tailed regime α≤1 that includes Zipf's law. It also departs from standard smooth/strongly-convex convergence analyses and existing sign-descent/Adam rates (Bernstein et al., 2018; Das et al., 2024; Liu et al., 2025), which the authors show are too coarse to capture the dimension dependence on this problem. The work formalizes and extends the empirical observations of Kunstner et al. (2024) on Adam's advantage for language models and provides a rigorous justification for the heuristic scaling hypothesized in Bulatov's (2023) blog post.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean theoretical explanation for why Adam/sign descent outperforms gradient descent on language models: Zipf-distributed data (α=1) is provably worst-case for GD, requiring t~d^{1-ε} iterations, while SD only needs t~√d. This has practical implications for scaling vocabulary size, suggesting that larger vocabularies require not just more compute per step but more total iterations. Key limitations include the simplified linear bigram model, the strong Assumption 2.3 on conditional frequencies, and the deterministic setting; open questions include extending to online learning, momentum-augmented sign descent, non-linear models, and cross-entropy loss (where preliminary results suggest the GD-SD gap is even larger).
