## Problem

Existing eye-tracking prediction methods operate on compressed scanpath data (discrete fixations) rather than raw continuous trajectories, discarding most spatio-temporal information, and typically produce a single deterministic output that fails to capture the inherent variability among human subjects viewing the same image. No prior diffusion-based approach has modeled continuous eye movement trajectories on standard natural images while leveraging the full richness of raw eye-tracking data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255162/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a 224×224 RGB image as the visual stimulus and generates a fixed-length continuous eye movement trajectory of 720 (x,y) coordinate pairs (corresponding to 3 seconds at 240 Hz). The fixed length is a constraint of the U-Net's downsampling structure. Generated trajectories can be post-processed into variable-length scanpaths via fixation extraction or aggregated into saliency maps, giving users flexibility in how they consume the output.

## Method

DiffEye is a DDPM diffusion model with a U-Net backbone (1D convolutions + self-attention) that denoises trajectories conditioned on the visual stimulus. The key architectural contribution is Corresponding Positional Embedding (CPE), which constructs a shared 2D sinusoidal positional grid at image resolution and adds the corresponding position vector to both each trajectory token (indexed by its (x,y) coordinate) and each image patch token, thereby aligning spatial gaze locations with semantic image regions. Image conditioning uses DINOv2 features enhanced by FeatUp to a 32×32 patch grid, injected via cross-attention at every U-Net block rather than a single global token. Training uses classifier-free guidance (10% unconditional dropout, guidance scale 4) and DDIM sampling (50 steps) for efficient inference, all trained end-to-end on 8,934 raw trajectories from the MIT1003 dataset.

## Results

On the MIT1003 test set, DiffEye achieves the best mean Levenshtein Distance (13.009), DTW (1.573×10³), and TDE (88.661) among all methods, and the best best-case Levenshtein (9.709) and DTW (1.067×10³). On the unseen OSIE dataset, DiffEye similarly leads in mean Levenshtein (14.771), DFD (3.068×10²), and DTW (1.552×10³), while trailing Chen et al. only on mean TDE (81.925 vs. 78.286). Notably, DiffEye is trained solely on 8,934 raw trajectories from MIT1003, whereas baselines such as DeepGaze III use ~615,000 scanpaths from multiple datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiffEye outperforms all five baselines on the majority of metrics across both datasets. On MIT1003, it achieves the lowest mean Levenshtein (13.009 vs. 13.574 for IOR-ROI, the next best), mean DTW (1.573 vs. 1.757 for DeepGaze III), and mean TDE (88.661 vs. 92.100 for Chen et al.), while DeepGaze III retains a slight edge on best-case DFD (2.160 vs. 2.449). On the unseen OSIE dataset, DiffEye leads in mean Levenshtein (14.771 vs. 14.836 IOR-ROI), mean DFD (3.068 vs. 3.206 DeepGaze III), and mean DTW (1.552 vs. 1.699 IOR-ROI), but trails Chen et al. on mean TDE (81.925 vs. 78.286). GazeFormer, being deterministic, reports only best-case scores and is consistently outperformed by DiffEye on those shared metrics. HAT, despite being trained on four datasets including MIT1003, performs worst across nearly all metrics, suggesting its multi-task design does not transfer well to free-viewing scanpath generation. The paper also notes that DeepGaze III and HAT were trained on data overlapping the MIT1003 test split (marked 'seen'), yet DiffEye still surpasses them, underscoring the advantage of training on raw trajectories rather than compressed scanpaths.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the scanpath prediction literature (DeepGaze III, GazeFormer, HAT, IOR-ROI) by shifting the generative target from discrete fixation sequences to continuous eye movement trajectories, and by adopting a diffusion model (DDPM/DDIM) rather than autoregressive or GAN-based generation. It is most closely related to DiffGaze, which applies diffusion to 360° gaze sequences, but departs from it by using patch-level DINOv2/FeatUp features and a novel Corresponding Positional Embedding (CPE) to align gaze coordinates with spatial image semantics, rather than a single global conditioning vector. The U-Net backbone with cross-attention at every block follows standard diffusion architecture conventions, while the CPE mechanism and the use of raw trajectory data (720 timesteps vs. ~8 fixations) represent the core methodological departure from prior scanpath work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DiffEye is the first method to generate continuous, variable-diversity eye movement trajectories on natural RGB images, opening the door to applications in developmental science (e.g., simulating population-level gaze differences in autism), VR, and foveated rendering that require temporal dynamics beyond what saliency maps or fixed-length scanpaths provide. A notable practical finding is that training on only 8,934 raw trajectories from MIT1003 outperforms baselines trained on 600,000+ scanpaths, suggesting that the richer temporal signal in raw data partially compensates for data scarcity. Key limitations include fixed-length output at 240 Hz, reliance on a single training dataset, and the absence of variable-length or multi-task support; scaling to larger and more diverse datasets, supporting non-free-viewing tasks, and extending to individual-level prediction remain open.
