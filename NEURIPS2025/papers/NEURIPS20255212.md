## Problem

Existing LiDAR world models are narrowly trained for specific domains and lack demonstrated transferability across diverse forecasting scenarios such as outdoor-to-indoor, sparse-to-dense beam, and non-semantic-to-semantic adaptation. Additionally, current architectures suffer from under-compressed latent representations (inflating downstream model parameters) and reliance on slow DDPM-based diffusion training that requires thousands of epochs, hindering systematic study of world model transferability.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255212/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes sequences of past LiDAR frames in one of three representations—raw voxelized sweeps (o_v), densified occupancy grids (o_d), or semantically labeled occupancy (o_s)—along with the ego-vehicle's historical trajectory and optionally a future trajectory. These are encoded into 16-dimensional per-voxel latents via the VAE, and the flow-matching model predicts future latents over a 1–3 second horizon (2–6 frames at 2 Hz). Outputs are decoded back into occupancy grids or rendered point clouds, with the semantic variant producing class-labeled 3D occupancy maps at 200×200×16 resolution.

## Method

The core contribution is a two-stage pipeline: (1) a Swin Transformer-based VAE that compresses LiDAR occupancy into a 16-dimensional continuous latent space at up to 192× compression ratio (6× higher than prior SOTA) by replacing patch-merging downsampling with convolutional layers and using a lightweight channel-reduction neck; and (2) a conditional flow matching (CFM) generator based on Rectified Flow that regresses a velocity field over linearly interpolated noise-to-target paths, replacing DDPM diffusion. The CFM backbone uses a UNet-style spatial DiT with a 3D convolutional layer inserted after the spatial blocks to extend the temporal receptive field beyond a single latent pixel, and employs classifier-free guidance (25% historical-latent dropout during training, guidance scale s=2 at inference). For cross-domain transfer, the paper introduces a cosine-similarity alignment loss that constrains the fine-tuned semantic VAE's latent space to remain aligned with the pretrained non-semantic VAE's space, which is critical because the pretrained flow model operates in the original feature geometry. Pretraining is self-supervised on unlabeled nuScenes densified occupancy, followed by fine-tuning on downstream tasks with as little as 5% of the labeled data used by prior methods.

## Results

On the nuScenes semantic occupancy forecasting benchmark, the proposed method achieves 33.17% mIoU at 1s and 23.33% average mIoU (historical trajectory only), surpassing the previous SOTA (RenderWorld, 20.80% avg mIoU) by 12.5% relative. With future trajectory conditioning, it reaches 28.49% avg mIoU and 4.55 bits/dim NLL, outperforming DOME (22.18% mIoU, 6.04 NLL) by 5.5%+ mIoU and 25% NLL while using only 4.38% of DOME's FLOPs (389 vs. 8892 GFLOPs/frame) and 15× fewer parameters (30.4M vs. 444M). The model runs at 22.22 FPS on a single RTX 4090, a 1.1×–4× speedup over prior methods. For non-semantic occupancy forecasting (Table 5), the densified-LiDAR variant achieves 30.91% avg IoU, beating Occ4D (21.09%) and ViDAR (12.46%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against OccWorld [58], the proposed method wins on all metrics: +6.2% avg mIoU (23.33 vs. 17.13), +5.2% avg IoU, and 3.5× fewer GFLOPs per frame (389 vs. 1347). Against RenderWorld [52] and OccLLama [48], it leads by +2.5% and +3.4% avg mIoU respectively, while using far fewer parameters (30M vs. 416M). Against DynamicCity [6], it achieves higher mIoU at both 1s and 2s horizons with half the FLOPs and 1.1× higher FPS. The most dramatic comparison is with DOME [14] (future-trajectory setting): the proposed method surpasses DOME by +6.3% avg mIoU (28.49 vs. 22.18), +5.4% avg IoU, and 1.49 bits/dim lower NLL, while requiring only 4.38% of DOME's FLOPs, 6.8% of its parameters, and running 3.9× faster (21.43 vs. 5.48 FPS). On reconstruction (Table 2), the Swin-Transformer VAE outperforms UniScenes [23] by +7.1% mIoU at 32× compression (99.2% vs. 92.1%) and beats DOME [14] by +9.7% mIoU at 192× compression (92.8% vs. 83.1% at 64×). For non-semantic occupancy forecasting (Table 5), the densified-LiDAR model achieves 30.91% avg IoU, exceeding Occ4D [20] (21.09%) by 9.8% and ViDAR [53] (12.46%) by 18.5%. OccSora [45] is only compared on reconstruction at 512×, where the proposed method achieves 80.0% mIoU vs. OccSora's 27.4%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) LiDAR-based occupancy forecasting (Occ4D, Copilot4D, BEVWorld), which has been domain-specific and diffusion-based; (2) semantic 4D occupancy forecasting (OccWorld, DOME, UniScenes), which relies heavily on expensive semantic labels; and (3) foundational world models (GAIA-1/2, Cosmos, NWM), which have been exclusively image-based. The key departures are replacing DDPM/DDIM-style diffusion with conditional flow matching (rectified flow) for a 4–23× FLOPs reduction, substituting the SD3-style encoder with a Swin Transformer VAE achieving 6× higher compression, and—most distinctively—conducting the first systematic cross-domain transferability study for LiDAR world models, including a cosine-similarity-based latent alignment strategy for the non-semantic-to-semantic transfer setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for autonomous driving: achieving SOTA semantic occupancy forecasting with only 5% of the labeled data used by prior work directly addresses the bottleneck of expensive 3D semantic annotation, and the 1.98×–23× efficiency gains make real-time deployment more feasible. The finding that latent-space alignment (measured by CKA/CKNNA) matters more than raw reconstruction quality for downstream forecasting is a useful conceptual insight for any pretrain-then-finetune pipeline. Open questions remain: the indoor adaptation result where from-scratch training eventually surpasses fine-tuning with sufficient data suggests the pretraining domain gap is not fully bridged, and the paper does not yet explore multi-modal inputs, longer-horizon forecasting, or integration with planning/control systems.
