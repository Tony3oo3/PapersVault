## Problem

Existing diffusion-based universal image restoration methods suffer from two coupled limitations: naive reverse-process inference accumulates discretization errors under limited sampling steps, and the trade-off between degradation-representation commonality and restoration quality forces reliance on complex, efficiency-costly compensation mechanisms. No prior work provides a unified, training-free solver that simultaneously addresses both the numerical accuracy of the inverse ODE and the fidelity gap introduced by high-commonality degradation-agnostic modeling.

## Input / Output

The method accepts a single degraded RGB image (any of deraining, low-light, desnowing, dehazing, or deblurring) and outputs a restored RGB image at the same resolution. Inference uses 8 ODE solver steps with a U-Net backbone (channel widths from 32 to 512 across T/S/B/L variants), and the solver plus universal posterior sampling are entirely training-free—only the residual and noise predictor networks require supervised training on 256×256 patches. The same trained model handles all five degradation types without task-specific modules or prompts.

## Method

DGSolver reformulates the diffusion generalist model's reverse process as a semi-linear ODE whose exact solution (Proposition 1) decouples a linearly computable term from two nonlinear integrals over the residual and noise predictors. High-order Taylor-based solvers (k=1,2,3) approximate these integrals with finite-difference derivatives, and a queue-based sampling strategy caches intermediate time points across steps, reducing total NFEs from O(nk) to O(n+k−1). On top of the solver, universal posterior sampling (UPS) approximates the intractable log-likelihood gradient ∇log q(I_in|I_t) via a residual-based measurement model I_in = I_0 + I_res + n, yielding a closed-form gradient correction to the predicted noise that acts as a manifold-constrained guidance signal. The Jensen-gap upper bound (Theorem 1) guarantees the approximation error vanishes at both noise extremes, making UPS a stable, training-free compensatory mechanism that requires no known degradation operator.

## Results

On the five natural image restoration tasks (deraining, low-light enhancement, desnowing, dehazing, deblurring), Ours-L achieves an average PSNR of 31.36 dB and SSIM of 0.920, outperforming the strongest universal baseline DiffUIR (29.93 dB / 0.907) by +1.43 dB in average PSNR. Against the best task-specific method Restormer, Ours-L is competitive on deraining (31.46 vs 30.02 dB) and dehazing (31.68 vs 32.04 dB) but trails on deblurring (30.15 vs 32.87 dB) and desnowing (32.69 vs 33.24 dB). On remote sensing restoration (Table 5), Ours-L reaches an average PSNR of 31.52 dB and SSIM of 0.854, surpassing DiffUIR (30.17 / 0.821) by +1.35 dB. The method uses only 7.73M parameters and 32.93G FLOPs, matching DiffUIR's complexity while delivering substantially higher accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DGSolver (Ours-L) outperforms all universal baselines across every task. Against the closest competitor DiffUIR, it gains +0.79 dB on deraining, +2.63 dB on enhancement, +1.99 dB on desnowing, +1.39 dB on dehazing, and +1.15 dB on deblurring, for a +1.43 dB average improvement. Compared to task-specific methods, Ours-L surpasses Restormer on deraining (+1.44 dB) and enhancement (+0.77 dB) but falls short on deblurring (−2.72 dB) and desnowing (−0.55 dB), reflecting the inherent trade-off of a single model handling all degradations. Among universal methods, the gap over DA-CLIP is +3.82 dB average PSNR, over Prompt-IR +3.47 dB, and over AirNet +7.35 dB. On remote sensing tasks (Table 5), Ours-L leads DiffUIR by +1.35 dB average PSNR and +0.033 SSIM, with the largest margin on declouding (+1.01 dB) and deblurring (+1.83 dB). The ablation (Table 2) confirms that the k=2 solver with universal posterior sampling contributes +0.66 dB over the first-order baseline without UPS, validating both components' individual and combined effectiveness.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the diffusion generalist model (DGM) paradigm introduced by DiffUIR [75] and RDDM [29], which unify diverse degradation types into a degradation-agnostic latent distribution. The key departure is a theoretical re-examination of the reverse sampling process: rather than treating the DGM's probability flow ODE as a black box (as prior work does), the authors derive its exact semi-linear structure—one analytically integrable linear term plus two nonlinear neural-network terms—and design tailored high-order solvers that exploit this decomposition. Additionally, the universal posterior sampling (UPS) component generalizes the diffusion posterior sampling (DPS) framework of Chung et al. [7], which requires a known linear measurement operator, to the generalist degradation model I_in = I_0 + I_res + n, making it applicable without task-specific operator knowledge.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a core tension in universal image restoration: pushing degradation representations toward full commonality (δ_T → 1) improves unification but worsens restoration quality, and prior methods rely on heavy compensation modules to recover fidelity at the cost of efficiency. DGSolver's training-free solver and UPS improvements offer a lightweight path to close this gap, and the semi-linear ODE insight could inform solver design for other structured diffusion processes. However, the approach still depends on a well-trained DGM backbone, the Jensen gap bound in Theorem 1 is loose in practice, and remote-sensing declouding performance remains notably weaker than other tasks. Open questions include whether the solver framework extends to video or 3D restoration, and whether the counterintuitive performance degradation at higher sampling steps reflects a fundamental limitation of the commonality principle.
