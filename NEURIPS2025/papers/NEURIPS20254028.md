## Problem

Safety alignment strategies in text-to-image models frequently cause over-refusal, where benign prompts containing surface-level sensitive keywords are incorrectly rejected, reducing practical utility. Despite this being a well-observed issue in practice, no large-scale benchmark existed to systematically quantify over-refusal in T2I models or to characterize the safety-utility trade-off across diverse safety categories.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254028/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark consumes text prompts organized into nine safety-related categories (privacy, copyright, discrimination, self-harm, sexual content, illegal activities, unethical/unsafe actions, violence) and evaluates whether a T2I model produces a generated image or returns a refusal (error message, fully masked image, or safety-checker flag). OVERT-mini contains 1,800 benign prompts (200 per category) for measuring over-refusal, while OVERT-unsafe contains 1,785 paired harmful prompts for measuring safe response rate. Image content is verified by a majority vote of three VLMs (GPT-4o, Gemini-Flash-2.0, Pixtral-12B) that receive both the generated image and its source prompt as context.

## Method

The paper introduces an automatic LLM-based pipeline that converts seed over-refusal prompts from WildGuardMix into T2I prompts using Gemini-2.0-Flash, with direct template-based generation for the discrimination and violence categories where seed quality was insufficient. Post-processing applies LLM-as-a-Judge filtering (with human-audited precision >92%), Semhash deduplication at category-specific similarity thresholds, and rejection sampling via Chameleon-7B (8 trials per prompt, category-specific refusal-rate thresholds) to retain prompts near the refusal boundary. The pipeline is designed for dynamic policy adaptation: modifying generation instructions (e.g., restricting safe contexts from video games to novels/films) produces customized OVERT variants without altering the rest of the workflow. As a preliminary mitigation, the paper evaluates LLM-based prompt rewriting and finds it reduces refusal rates but often compromises semantic fidelity (44–66% in the two tested categories).

## Results

On OVERT-mini (4,600 benign prompts), average over-refusal rates range from 2.0% (SD-3.5-Large) to 51.7% (DALL-E-3-Web), with DALL-E-3-API achieving the lowest among proprietary models at 18.5%. On OVERT-unsafe (1,785 harmful prompts), safe response rates range from 19.5% (SD-3.5-Large) to 82.5% (DALL-E-3-Web). A Spearman rank correlation of 0.898 between over-refusal rate and safe response rate confirms a strong safety-utility trade-off across all five evaluated T2I models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DALL-E-3-API achieves the best safety-utility balance among proprietary models, with the lowest over-refusal rate (18.5%) while maintaining a strong safe response rate of 67.4% on OVERT-unsafe. DALL-E-3-Web exhibits the most extreme behavior: the highest over-refusal (51.7%) paired with the highest safe response rate (82.5%), suggesting a stricter filtering policy for its broader public user base. FLUX1.1-Pro shows severe category-specific over-refusal (86.5% on violence, 72.5% on illegal activities) driven by its post-image NSFW checker, while failing to catch non-NSFW harms like privacy violations (only 10.0% refusal on OVERT-unsafe). SD-3.5-Large has near-zero over-refusal (2.0%) but the worst safety performance (19.5% safe response rate), as its CLIP-based safety checker primarily detects sexual content. Imagen-3 sits in the middle (29.1% over-refusal, 57.5% safe response), with particularly high refusal on sexual content (68.0%) and illegal activities (48.0%) on benign prompts.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper transfers the over-refusal evaluation paradigm established for LLMs (XSTest, WildGuardMix, OR-Bench) to the text-to-image domain, using LLM over-refusal prompts as seeds that are converted into T2I prompts via Gemini-2.0-Flash. It departs from prior T2I safety benchmarks (e.g., ART, Adversarial Nibbler, Latent Guard), which focused exclusively on detecting harmful or adversarial outputs, by introducing the complementary utility dimension: measuring how often benign prompts are wrongly rejected. The paired OVERT/OVERT-unsafe design, enabling explicit safety-utility trade-off analysis, is a methodological contribution not present in either the LLM over-refusal literature or the T2I safety literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark fills a clear gap: no prior large-scale evaluation systematically measured over-refusal in T2I models, despite it being a well-known practical complaint (e.g., Gemini refusing images of white individuals). The strong Spearman correlation (0.898) between safety and over-refusal quantifies a trade-off that practitioners have anecdotally observed but lacked rigorous evidence for. The anomalous finding that some models refuse benign illegal-activity prompts more than their harmful counterparts suggests critical flaws in current refusal mechanisms. Open questions include whether the synthetic prompts capture the full diversity of real user queries, how to break the safety-utility trade-off without sacrificing either, and whether the rejection-sampling bias from Chameleon-7B limits generalizability.
