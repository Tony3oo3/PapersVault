## Problem

While LLMs have made significant progress in math reasoning through curated data and intricate training, MLLMs still lag behind, and collecting high-quality multimodal reasoning data is expensive. The paper asks whether MLLMs can directly absorb math reasoning abilities from off-the-shelf math LLMs without any tuning, and reveals that existing model merging methods (e.g., Task Arithmetic) fail in this cross-modality setting due to a large gap in parameter spaces between MLLMs and math LLMs.

## Input / Output

The method requires three sets of model weights: the MLLM (e.g., LLaVA-1.5-7B), a math-reasoning LLM (e.g., Tora-Code-7B), and their shared pre-trained base LLM (e.g., LLaMA-2-7B), from which task vectors are computed as layer-wise differences. Only the LLM backbone layers (attention and MLP) are merged; the visual encoder and projection layers remain untouched. A key constraint is that both models must share the same foundation LLM architecture and parameter size, as the method relies on layer-wise SVD alignment between them.

## Method

IP-Merging is a tuning-free, two-stage procedure. In the identification stage, SVD is applied to the task vectors of both models at each layer, and the cosine similarity of corresponding eigenvectors (matched by singular value index) is computed; layers whose top similarity score exceeds a threshold S_α are selected as math-reasoning-associated. In the projection stage, the selected LLM task vectors are first rescaled by the ratio of nuclear norms (sum of singular values) between MLLM and LLM to close the eigenvalue magnitude gap, then projected into the MLLM's right-singular-vector subspace using softmax-weighted importance scores derived from the similarity values. The final merged model is W_0 + ΔW_MLLM + λ_n · ΔW̄_Math · V̄_V · V̄_V^T, which fits the general model-merging framework with a projection matrix as the alignment operator. The paper proves via the triangle inequality that without rescaling, the larger eigenvalues of the math LLM would overshadow the MLLM's smaller ones, justifying the projection step.

## Results

IP-Merging consistently improves math reasoning across all three base MLLMs without tuning. On MathVerse, it achieves 15.3% (LLaVA-1.5-7B, +4.0 over base), 28.5% (Qwen-2-VL-7B, +3.7), and 39.0% (InternVL3-8B, +0.5). On MathVista, gains are 28.2% (+3.0), 60.2% (+4.8), and 67.6% (+1.6) respectively. Crucially, general abilities are preserved: MMMU, TextVQA, and MMBench scores remain within 0.5% of the base model, while all other merging methods cause severe degradation (e.g., Task Arithmetic drops Qwen-2-VL's TextVQA from 83.8 to 37.9).

## Baselines & Comparisons

IP-Merging outperforms all three baselines (Task Arithmetic, Ties Merging, EMR Merging) on every benchmark and every base model. The gap is most dramatic for Qwen-2-VL-7B: IP-Merging achieves 28.5% on MathVerse vs. 9.3% (Task Arithmetic), 5.3% (Ties), and 17.6% (EMR), and 60.2% on MathVista vs. 31.1%, 29.3%, and 40.8% respectively. For LLaVA-1.5-7B, IP-Merging gains +4.0 on MathVerse and +3.0 on MathVista over the base model, while Task Arithmetic actually *degrades* MathVerse to 9.8% (−1.5) and Ties Merging to 10.7%. On general-ability benchmarks (Table 3), IP-Merging preserves performance (e.g., Qwen-2-VL: MMMU 50.7, TextVQA 84.0, MMBench 80.8 vs. base 50.7/83.8/80.3), whereas Task Arithmetic and Ties Merging cause catastrophic drops (Qwen-2-VL TextVQA: 37.9 and 4.3 respectively). EMR Merging is the closest competitor but still trails IP-Merging by 3.7–11.2 points on MathVerse and 4.8–18.4 points on MathVista across the three model families.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the model merging literature (Task Arithmetic, Ties Merging, EMR Merging) from the well-studied setting of merging same-modality fine-tuned models to the cross-modality problem of transferring math reasoning from a text-only LLM into a multimodal LLM. The key conceptual departure is identifying that prior merging methods implicitly assume similar task-vector geometry, which breaks down when the source (math LLM) and target (MLLM) have undergone fundamentally different training objectives (text math vs. vision-language alignment). The proposed IP-Merging introduces SVD-based subspace similarity for parameter selection and a rescaling-plus-projection step to close the parameter-space gap, neither of which appears in prior merging work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The tuning-free nature of IP-Merging offers a low-cost path to improve MLLM math reasoning without the expensive data annotation and GPU-intensive training that dominate current approaches, making it practically attractive for resource-constrained settings. However, the method is limited to same-size models sharing the same foundation LLM architecture (e.g., both LLaMA-2-based), which restricts its applicability in a rapidly evolving model landscape. Open questions include whether the subspace-similarity criterion generalizes to architecturally dissimilar models, whether the approach can transfer other reasoning abilities (e.g., code, science), and whether the moderate 3–5% gains compound when stacked with other training-based improvements.
