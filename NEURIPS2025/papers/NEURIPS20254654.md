## Problem

Classical Integral Probability Metrics (IPMs) can only compare precise probability measures, leaving imprecise probability models—capacities, lower probabilities, belief functions, credal sets—without a principled metric framework for comparison. Moreover, no existing framework simultaneously enables cross-model comparison of imprecise probabilities and quantification of epistemic uncertainty within a single model, despite the growing need for both in imprecise probabilistic machine learning.

## Input / Output

The IIPM takes two capacities (or lower probabilities) on a measurable space and a function class F ⊆ C_b(X) as input, producing a scalar distance. For epistemic UQ, MMI takes a single lower probability (constructed from an ensemble of m probabilistic predictors via pointwise minimum) and outputs a non-negative uncertainty score; the linear-time variant MMI-Lin replaces the O(2^K) sup over all subsets with the closed-form bound 1 − Σ P̲({x}). The choice of F instantiates specific metrics: F_TV gives lower total variation, F_d gives the lower Dudley metric, and F_W recovers the restricted lower probability Kantorovich objective.

## Method

The core contribution replaces Lebesgue integration in the IPM definition with Choquet integration, yielding IIPM_F(ν, μ) = sup_{f∈F} |∮f dν − ∮f dμ|, which strictly generalises IPMs (recovered when capacities are probability measures) to all Choquet capacities. Theoretically, IIPM is shown to be a pseudometric that metrises Choquet weak convergence whenever F is dense in C_b(X) under the sup-norm. For epistemic UQ, the Maximum Mean Imprecision (MMI) is defined as IIPM_F(P̄, P̲)—the distance between a lower probability and its conjugate upper probability—capturing the worst-case gap between optimistic and pessimistic expectations over F. MMI satisfies axioms A1–A4 for any F and A5–A6 under 2-monotonicity with additive product function spaces, and admits a linear-time upper bound via approximation by the closest ε-contamination model. Empirically, MMI and its O(K) upper bound are validated through accuracy-rejection curves in selective classification on 7-, 10-, and 100-class problems, consistently outperforming entropy difference and matching the Generalised Hartley measure.

## Results

The paper evaluates MMI and its linear-time upper bound (MMI-Lin) via Accuracy-Rejection (AR) curve AUC on four classification tasks: Obesity Levels (7 classes), MNIST (10 classes), CIFAR-10 (10 classes), and CIFAR-100 (100 classes). Across all four tasks, both MMI and MMI-Lin consistently outperform the Entropy Difference (E-Diff) baseline and achieve performance nearly identical to the Generalised Hartley (GH) measure, which is considered the most informative epistemic UQ measure in the literature. Critically, on CIFAR-100 (100 classes) where exact MMI and GH computation is intractable at O(2^K), the O(K) upper bound MMI-Lin remains computationally feasible while still outperforming E-Diff, demonstrating practical scalability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares MMI and MMI-Lin against two established epistemic uncertainty measures: Entropy Difference (E-Diff, Abellán et al. 2006) and Generalised Hartley (GH, Abellán & Gómez 2006). MMI and MMI-Lin consistently outperform E-Diff across all four datasets (Obesity Levels, MNIST, CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100) in AR-curve AUC, indicating they better capture informative epistemic uncertainty for selective classification. Both MMI and MMI-Lin match the performance of GH almost exactly, which the authors attribute to both measures satisfying all six desirable axiomatic properties for epistemic UQ (A1–A6) and to prior work showing GH and the binary credal epistemic measure (a special case of MMI) exhibit perfect correlation. The key practical advantage of MMI-Lin over GH is computational: while GH requires O(2^K) evaluation over all subsets, MMI-Lin runs in O(K), making it the only method that remains tractable on CIFAR-100 (K=100) while still outperforming E-Diff. The paper notes that credal set volume, another common baseline, was excluded from comparison due to demonstrated computational fragility for K > 2 (Sale et al. 2023).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the classical Integral Probability Metric (IPM) framework of Müller [55] from probability measures to Choquet capacities [30], replacing Lebesgue integration with Choquet integration to enable comparison of a much broader class of imprecise probability models. The closest prior work includes Hable's [109] minimum distance estimation (a special case of IIPM without theoretical analysis) and Catalano and Lavenant's [115] integral-based metric for higher-order probabilities. The paper also recovers and re-derives the lower total variation distance previously noted by Levin and Peres [89] within a unified integral-metric framework, and re-derives a key result from Caprio's [56] optimal transport under ε-contamination using the IIPM lens.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The IIPM framework provides the first principled, axiomatic foundation for comparing arbitrary IP models and for deriving epistemic uncertainty measures (MMI) that satisfy all six foundational axioms from the credal UQ literature. The linear-time upper bound (MMI-Lin) offers a practical alternative to the Generalised Hartley measure, which becomes intractable at O(2^K) for large K. Key open questions include the absence of a sampling-based estimation method for IIPMs (due to the lack of a canonical sampling procedure for imprecise models), whether weaker conditions on the function class suffice for metrisation, and extending the framework to f-divergences, Bregman divergences, and regression settings.
