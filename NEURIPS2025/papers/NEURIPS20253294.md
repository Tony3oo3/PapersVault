## Problem

Large language models are bottlenecked by sequential computation across transformer blocks, and existing efficiency techniques (quantization, pruning, MoE) each face distinct limitations in precision, redundancy identification, or batch-size-dependent throughput. The paper asks whether sequences of FFN layers—particularly those left contiguous after attention pruning—can be parallelized with minimal accuracy loss, challenging the assumption that sequential processing is necessary for these layers.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253294/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a trained LLM that has undergone attention-layer removal (via the Puzzle NAS framework), leaving long sequences of consecutive FFN-only blocks, and produces a fused model where each such sequence is collapsed into a single wider FFN layer whose weight matrices are concatenations of the originals. The fused model then consumes standard text tokens and produces generated text, but with reduced depth and parallelizable computation across GPUs. A key constraint is that the final FFN in each attention-removed sequence must be excluded from fusion to avoid significant accuracy degradation.

## Method

FFN Fusion exploits the observation that consecutive FFN layers in attention-removed regions exhibit low inter-layer dependencies, meaning their outputs change little when their inputs are altered. The core mechanism replaces the sequential composition of c+1 FFN blocks (where each FFN receives the previous block's output) with a parallel sum where all FFNs share the same normalized input, which Theorem 3.1 proves is algebraically equivalent to a single wider SwiGLU FFN formed by concatenating the weight matrices along the hidden dimension. A pairwise block-dependency matrix (cosine distance between each block's contribution with and without another block removed) identifies which sequences are safe to fuse, and a greedy selection algorithm picks the lowest-dependency groups. Applied to Llama-3.1-405B-Instruct via Puzzle attention pruning followed by fusion of 49 consecutive FFN layers into 4 parallel groups, the resulting Ultra-253B-Base achieves a 1.71× latency speedup and 35× lower per-token cost while matching or exceeding the parent model on key benchmarks after knowledge distillation and continual pretraining.

## Results

Ultra-253B-Base (253B parameters, derived from Llama-405B via FFN Fusion + attention pruning) matches or exceeds its 405B-parameter parent on all major benchmarks: 87.54 on MMLU Instruct, 72.25 on MMLU-Pro, 84.92 on Arena Hard, 86.58 on HumanEval, and 9.19 on MT-Bench. Inference latency improves by 1.71× (70.92 vs. 41.44 tokens/s on TP-8 H100), with 35× lower per-token cost at batch size 32 and a 2× reduction in KV-cache memory. The model also outperforms Llama-405B on RULER-128K long-context evaluation despite having nearly half the attention layers.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against its parent model Llama-3.1-405B-Instruct, Ultra-253B-Base wins on every reported benchmark despite using only 253B parameters (vs. 405B). The most dramatic gain is on Arena Hard, where Ultra-253B-Base scores 84.92 versus Llama-405B's 72.56 (+12.36 points), likely aided by the RLHF alignment stage. Gains on MMLU Instruct (+1.01), MMLU-Pro (+0.33), HumanEval (+0.61), and MT-Bench (+0.13) are more modest but consistent. On latency, Ultra-253B-Base achieves 70.92 tokens/s versus 41.44 for Llama-405B (1.71× speedup) on the same 8×H100 TP-8 setup. Compared to Llama-3.3-70B, Ultra-253B-Base is slower (70.92 vs. 94.03 tokens/s) but the paper positions it as breaking the efficient frontier: it delivers 405B-class quality at a fraction of the cost, sitting between the two in latency while far exceeding the 70B model in capability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Puzzle NAS framework [3], which prunes attention layers from pre-trained LLMs and leaves long sequences of consecutive FFN layers. While prior LLM efficiency work has focused on quantization, structured pruning, and Mixture-of-Experts sparsity, FFN Fusion introduces a complementary architectural transformation: it exploits the low inter-layer dependencies in these FFN sequences to convert sequential computation into parallel execution via weight concatenation (Theorem 3.1). The approach is positioned as orthogonal to and combinable with existing runtime optimizations, and it departs from the conventional assumption that transformer blocks must be processed strictly sequentially by demonstrating that substantial portions of FFN computation are effectively independent.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: Ultra-253B-Base achieves a 1.71× latency speedup and 35× lower per-token cost while matching or exceeding Llama-405B on key benchmarks, and the technique generalizes across scales (8B–253B) and model families (Llama, Mistral). The conceptual finding that FFN layers in attention-removed regions exhibit remarkably low mutual dependency challenges the assumption that sequential depth is necessary for representational quality, and the preliminary evidence that even full transformer blocks can sometimes be parallelized opens a new direction for architecture design. Key limitations include the requirement for long consecutive FFN sequences (necessitating prior attention pruning), the sensitivity of the final FFN in each sequence to fusion, and the fact that full block parallelization remains substantially harder than FFN-only fusion. Open questions include extending the technique to MoE layers, validating speedups in production inference frameworks (TensorRT-LLM, vLLM), and understanding whether the dependency patterns observed here are universal or specific to the Llama/Puzzle pipeline.
