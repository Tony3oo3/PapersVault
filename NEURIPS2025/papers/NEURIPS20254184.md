## Problem

Extending 3D Gaussian Splatting to dynamic scenes suffers from three interrelated limitations: per-Gaussian deformation fields introduce redundant updates across all Gaussians at every timestep, training relies solely on photometric reconstruction loss without explicit motion supervision leading to temporal inconsistencies, and sparse control-point interpolation fails to capture fine-grained non-rigid deformations in complex real-world scenes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254184/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes monocular image sequences {I_t} with corresponding camera poses {P_t} as input, operating at 480×270 on NeRF-DS (trained on left camera, tested on right) and 800×800 on D-NeRF. It produces a compact 3D Gaussian representation augmented with sparse motion anchors (roughly 3–5k anchors vs. 113–185k Gaussians, a ~0.25–0.33 ratio) that can render novel views of the dynamic scene at arbitrary timestamps within the observed interval at real-time rates (~215 FPS on D-NeRF).

## Method

HAIF-GS replaces per-Gaussian deformation with a sparse set of 3D motion anchors (initialized via farthest point sampling) whose transformations are propagated to Gaussians through linear blend skinning with Gaussian-kernel spatial weights. An Anchor Filter MLP predicts a per-anchor confidence score α_i to separate dynamic from static regions, regularized by sparsity and entropy losses. The Induced Flow-Guided Deformation module self-supervises motion by predicting forward/backward scene flows from anchor position and timestamp, constructing three temporally shifted queries that are mapped through a shared Deformable MLP and aggregated (λ=0.25) into a temporally consistent feature for predicting anchor translation and rotation, with a cycle-consistency loss enforcing bidirectional flow coherence. For complex deformations, Hierarchical Anchor Densification computes temporal translation variance per anchor and spawns child anchors (threshold τ=0.75) in high-variance regions, where child anchors encode parent translations to enable cross-level motion propagation. Training uses a two-stage scheme (soft then hard anchor selection) with a combined loss of L1, D-SSIM, cycle consistency, entropy, and sparsity terms.

## Results

On the real-world NeRF-DS dataset, HAIF-GS achieves a mean PSNR of 24.63 dB, MS-SSIM of 0.9014, and LPIPS of 0.1342, outperforming the strongest baseline 4DGS (24.18 dB) by +0.45 dB and SC-GS (24.05 dB) by +0.58 dB. On the synthetic D-NeRF dataset, HAIF-GS reaches a mean PSNR of 42.00 dB with SSIM 0.997 and LPIPS 0.010, surpassing SC-GS (41.65 dB) and Deformable 3DGS (40.16 dB). The method also achieves the best mean PSNR on HyperNeRF(misc) (28.50 dB) and Dycheck (15.05 dB), and renders at 215.4 FPS with 3.3 GB memory on D-NeRF, faster than Deformable 3DGS (53.7 FPS, 4.8 GB).

## Baselines & Comparisons

HAIF-GS achieves the best mean PSNR across all four evaluated benchmarks, winning on every scene in both NeRF-DS and D-NeRF. On NeRF-DS, it outperforms the closest 3DGS-based competitor SC-GS by +0.58 dB (24.63 vs. 24.05) and 4DGS by +0.45 dB, while the gap over NeRF-based methods (HyperNeRF, NeRF-DS) is larger at +1.18 to +1.23 dB. On D-NeRF, the advantage over SC-GS narrows to +0.35 dB (42.00 vs. 41.65) but the margin over Deformable 3DGS widens to +1.84 dB, and over 4DGS to +6.59 dB. In the extended NeRF-DS comparison (Table 5), HAIF-GS leads MoSca (24.51) by +0.12 dB and SoM (24.11) by +0.52 dB, while trailing no method. On the challenging Dycheck dataset with large deformations, HAIF-GS gains +1.33 dB over 4DGS and +1.33 dB over Deformable 3DGS, indicating stronger robustness to complex non-rigid motion. The method also renders at 215.4 FPS (vs. 53.7 for Deformable 3DGS and 134.8 for 4DGS) with lower memory (3.3 GB vs. 4.8 GB), confirming the efficiency benefit of sparse anchor-driven deformation.

## Relation to Prior Work

HAIF-GS sits squarely in the dynamic 3DGS lineage, building on the explicit Gaussian representation of 3DGS (Kerbl et al., 2023) and the sparse control-point paradigm of SC-GS (Huang et al., 2023), while departing from per-Gaussian deformation fields (Deformable 3DGS, Yang et al., 2024) by replacing them with a compact set of motion anchors. The key conceptual departures are threefold: a confidence-based Anchor Filter that suppresses redundant updates in static regions, a self-supervised Induced Flow-Guided Deformation module that learns scene flow implicitly through multi-frame feature aggregation (eliminating external flow labels), and a Hierarchical Anchor Densification mechanism that refines anchor resolution in motion-complex regions using a coarse-to-fine propagation scheme. The interpolation from anchors to Gaussians follows the linear blend skinning strategy from graphics (Sumner et al., 2007), consistent with prior dynamic 3DGS work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in dynamic 3DGS: the tension between modeling fine-grained non-rigid deformations and maintaining real-time rendering efficiency. The self-supervised flow induction is a clean design that avoids the annotation cost of explicit flow supervision while still providing structured temporal regularization, and the 215 FPS rendering speed (vs. 53.7 FPS for Deformable 3DGS) makes the approach more deployable. However, the authors acknowledge that anchor proliferation in highly complex scenes increases memory footprint, and the implicit flow induction may be sensitive to significant occlusions—both open challenges for scaling to longer or more cluttered sequences. The moderate PSNR gains (~0.5–1 dB over SC-GS) suggest the current formulation has not yet unlocked a qualitative leap in reconstruction fidelity, leaving room for further improvements in motion expressiveness.
