## Problem

Vision Transformers develop high-norm outlier tokens in low-information image regions that create noisy attention maps and degrade dense prediction tasks. The existing remedy—retraining models from scratch with dedicated register tokens—is expensive and impractical for already-released checkpoints. This paper asks whether registers can be added post-hoc, without any additional training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251891/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained ViT (e.g., DINOv2 ViT-L/14, OpenCLIP ViT-B/16) that was trained without registers and a standard RGB image. During inference, a single zero-initialized token is appended to the patch sequence, and the forward pass is modified at specific MLP layers to redirect register-neuron activations into that token. The model's outputs (class scores, patch features, attention maps) are structurally identical to the original model, but image patches no longer carry high-norm artifacts and the appended token absorbs them.

## Method

The paper identifies a sparse set of MLP neurons ('register neurons') whose activations causally determine where high-norm outlier tokens emerge. A discovery algorithm (Algorithm 1) finds these neurons by selecting those with consistently high average activation at outlier patch positions across a calibration image set. At test time, a zero-initialized token is appended to the input, and during the forward pass each register neuron's maximum activation is copied into the appended token while its activations on all image patches are zeroed out (Algorithm 2), effectively shifting the outlier outside the image. A mathematical model (Proposition 1) shows that under low-rank assumptions, register neurons produce tokens in the kernel of the task head, making the attention layer a no-op equivalent to an implicit attention bias—explaining why zeroing them without replacement degrades performance by ~15%. The approach is fully training-free and generalizes across model families (CLIP, DINOv2, InternViT-6B) and scales to vision-language models.

## Results

On linear probing (DINOv2 ViT-L/14), test-time registers match the base model on ImageNet top-1 (86.4%) and match trained registers on ADE20k segmentation (49.1 mIoU) while slightly improving NYUv2 depth (0.378 vs. 0.382 RMSE). The most dramatic gains appear in unsupervised object discovery (LOST), where test-time registers boost DINOv2 by ~21 correct localization points (32.2→53.8 on VOC07), closing the gap with trained registers to within 0–2 corloc. Zero-shot segmentation improves by +5 mIoU for DINOv2 (38.3→38.9) and +5.3 mIoU for OpenCLIP (34.7→40.0), and VLM benchmarks (LLaVA-Llama-3-8B) maintain their average score of 46.2 while producing cleaner cross-modal attention maps.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DINOv2 ViT-L/14 (Oquab et al., 2024), test-time registers match the base model on ImageNet top-1 (86.4%) and improve ADE20k mIoU from 48.3 to 49.1 and NYUv2 RMSE from 0.388 to 0.378. Against DINOv2 trained with four registers (Darcet et al., 2024), test-time registers match on ADE20k (49.1 mIoU) and slightly outperform on NYUv2 (0.378 vs. 0.382 RMSE), but trail by 0.3 points on ImageNet top-1 (86.4 vs. 86.7); on LOST object discovery, test-time registers reach within 0–2 corloc of trained registers (e.g., 53.8 vs. 56.2 on VOC07). Against OpenCLIP ViT-B/16 (Cherti et al., 2023), test-time registers yield small but consistent gains across all three linear probing metrics and a +5.3 mIoU improvement in zero-shot segmentation. Against InternViT-6B (Zhu et al., 2025), test-time registers produce the largest absolute gains in LOST object discovery (+11.3 corloc on VOC07, +13.3 on VOC12, +10.3 on COCO), confirming scalability to very large ViTs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends Darcet et al. (2024), who identified high-norm outlier tokens in ViTs and proposed trained register tokens as a fix requiring full retraining. The key departure is identifying a sparse set of 'register neurons' in the MLP layers that causally drive outlier formation, enabling a training-free test-time intervention. The theoretical framework also builds on Sun et al. (2024), who showed that massive activations in language models act as implicit attention biases, by proving an analogous equivalence for vision transformers and demonstrating that the register neuron contribution can be replaced by explicit attention bias terms at inference time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: any pre-trained ViT (CLIP, DINOv2, InternViT-6B, LLaVA) can be improved without retraining, which removes the primary barrier to adopting register tokens. The mechanistic finding that ~10 neurons out of ~100K control all outlier formation opens a broader question about input-independent, non-feature-detecting roles of neurons in deep networks. Limitations include that the method only addresses MLP neurons (not attention layers or positional encodings), shows small residual gaps versus trained registers on some tasks, and has been validated primarily on the CLIP/DINOv2 family. Whether the attention-bias-only variant (no extra token) generalizes to language models and whether the sparse-neuron mechanism is universal across architectures remain open.
