## Problem

DPO-based hallucination mitigation in VLMs critically depends on the true reward gaps within preference pairs, yet existing data curation methods (ranking-based or rewriting-based) lack mechanisms to systematically design and control these gaps. Ranking methods select from potentially flawed outputs without correcting hallucinations, while rewriting methods using external black-box models risk introducing hallucination patterns that deviate from the model's intrinsic failure modes. This results in suboptimal reward gap configurations that fail to expose the model's genuinely challenging deficiencies.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a text prompt as input, samples multiple candidate responses from a reference VLM (LLaVA-1.5-7B), and decomposes them into fine-grained semantic units that are clustered into topic groups using both textual consistency queries and CLIP-based visual correlation. The output is a set of curated preference pairs (y_w, y_l) where semantic differences are precisely controlled at the topic level, which are then used to fine-tune the policy model via DPO. The final aligned model produces text responses with significantly reduced visual hallucinations while preserving general VLM capabilities.

## Method

TPR (Topic-level Preference Rewriting) provides fine-grained control over reward gaps by operating at the semantic topic level rather than the whole-response level. It first decomposes multiple sampled responses into semantic units, clusters them into topic groups using textual consistency and CLIP visual correlation, then performs intra-topic self-resampling (converting units to wh-questions and resampling with the reference model) to build a diverse pool of scored alternatives per topic. Preference pairs are constructed by selectively replacing units in a template response with highest- or lowest-scoring alternatives from the same topic cluster, followed by in-context rewriting to maintain linguistic fluency. A curriculum learning strategy (TPR-CL) then optimizes the overall reward gap configuration: a warm-up stage uses greedy replacement (maximal score divergence) for fast initial convergence, while a hard-mining stage progressively selects harder-to-discern incorrect alternatives for y_l, keeping the model in a high-gradient-magnitude regime to refine its decision boundary against subtle hallucinations.

## Results

TPR-CL-7B achieves state-of-the-art hallucination reduction, lowering ObjectHal-Bench CHs from 53.6 (LLaVA-1.5-7B baseline) to 3.4 (~93% reduction) and MMHal-Bench hallucination rate from 51.0 to 30.2 (~41% reduction). On AMBER, it reaches 82.7% accuracy and 87.8% F1, and on RefoMB it attains 61.1% trustworthiness (vs. 30.8% baseline). General capabilities are preserved or improved: LLaVA-Bench accuracy rises from 59.7% to 71.1% and MMStar from 30.3% to 33.3%, confirming that hallucination suppression does not degrade broader VLM performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TPR-CL-7B outperforms all 13 baselines on the majority of hallucination metrics. Against the strongest ranking-based method RLAIF-V-7B, TPR-CL reduces ObjHal CHs from 8.5 to 3.4 and CHi from 4.3 to 1.8, while matching its MMHal Score (3.06) and slightly trailing on MMHal Hall (30.2 vs. 29.2); however, TPR-CL dominates on AMBER (82.7 vs. 76.8 Acc), POPE All (87.6 vs. 83.3), RefoMB Trust (61.1 vs. 47.5), and LLaVA-Bench (71.1 vs. 64.9). Against rewriting-based methods, TPR-CL achieves substantially lower ObjHal CHs than HSA-DPO-13B (3.4 vs. 5.3) and OPA-DPO-7B (3.4 vs. 13.0), and far surpasses AMP-MEG-7B (3.4 vs. 37.8 CHs) and HA-DPO-7B (3.4 vs. 39.9 CHs). The greedy TPR-7B variant already beats all baselines on ObjHal (4.0 CHs) and RefoMB Trust (58.1%), with the curriculum variant (TPR-CL) providing consistent additional gains of 0.6–1.0 points on ObjHal and 3.0 points on RefoMB Trust, confirming the benefit of progressive hard-negative mining.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the DPO framework (Rafailov et al., 2023) for VLM alignment and on RLAIF-V's (Yu et al., 2024) protocol for decomposing responses into fine-grained semantic units. It departs from existing preference data curation strategies—ranking-based methods that select from potentially flawed model outputs, and rewriting-based methods that rely on external black-box models like GPT-4V—by introducing topic-level selective replacement using the model's own intra-topic resampled candidates. The key conceptual departure is framing data curation as the systematic optimization of the overall reward gap configuration across the dataset, rather than merely constructing individual high-quality preference pairs, and leveraging this control to implement a curriculum learning strategy (hard negative mining) that progressively increases the difficulty of rejected responses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies reward gap configuration as a critical but previously underexplored dimension of preference data curation, providing both a mathematical grounding (Appendix A) and a practical mechanism (TPR) to manipulate it. The 93% hallucination reduction on ObjectHal-Bench and superior data efficiency (surpassing human-annotated RLHF-V at lower cost) make this practically relevant for cost-effective VLM deployment. However, the pipeline's multi-stage complexity (decomposition, clustering, resampling, rewriting) limits immediate adoption, and the method is validated primarily on LLaVA-1.5-7B with perceptual hallucinations; extending to reasoning-chain hallucinations in complex multimodal tasks remains an open challenge the authors explicitly flag.
