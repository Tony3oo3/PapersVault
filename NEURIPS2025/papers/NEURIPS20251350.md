## Problem

Convolutional conditional neural processes (ConvCNPs) enforce translation equivariance through local CNN kernels, but their small receptive fields limit the ability to capture long-range dependencies, especially in sparse data regimes. Enlarging kernels increases parameter count and cost, while transformer-based alternatives incur quadratic complexity in the number of observations. Spectral methods such as Fourier neural operators (FNOs) offer a natural remedy by performing global convolution in the frequency domain, yet their application within the neural processes framework remained largely unexplored.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a set of noisy context pairs (x_c, y_c) and a set of query locations x_q, both drawn from a continuous domain. The context is first mapped to a functional embedding evaluated on a fixed uniform grid G whose resolution and physical extent must be held constant across tasks to avoid DFT spectral mismatch. The output is a factorized Gaussian predictive distribution at each query point, parameterized by a mean and a pre-softplus scale, with predictions obtained via kernel interpolation from the grid to arbitrary query locations.

## Method

SConvCNP retains the ConvCNP's convolutional deep set functional embedding (a sum of kernel-weighted context values on a uniform grid) but replaces the U-Net CNN decoder with a U-shaped Fourier Neural Operator. Each residual Fourier block applies an FFT to the grid representation, performs pointwise multiplication with learnable complex spectral weights W_ξ over a truncated set of low-frequency modes, and applies an inverse FFT, thereby realizing global convolution with a large effective receptive field at O(|G| log |G|) cost. Positional encodings (grid coordinates) are concatenated to the embedding to provide spatial context, and a density channel normalizes the functional embedding to account for variable context sizes. The U-shaped architecture uses three downsampling and two upsampling Fourier blocks with skip connections, and the total computational complexity matches ConvCNPs up to logarithmic factors, scaling linearly with task size.

## Results

On the synthetic 1-D regression benchmarks, SConvCNP achieves the best log-likelihood on Sawtooth (0.82 vs. −0.90 for all non-convolutional baselines) and Square (−1.13 vs. −1.17 for TE-TNP), with RMSE of 0.24 and 0.79 respectively. On the California traffic-flow dataset, SConvCNP attains the highest log-likelihood (2.05 ± 0.02) and tied-lowest RMSE (0.04) among all six methods. On the DTD image-completion task, SConvCNP achieves the best log-likelihood (1.50 ± 0.02) and tied-best RMSE (0.08), while on the Lotka-Volterra predator-prey benchmark it matches TE-TNP on RMSE (0.30) and is competitive on log-likelihood (0.14 vs. 0.16).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SConvCNP consistently outperforms the vanilla CNP and AttCNP across all four benchmarks, with the largest margins on high-frequency signals (Sawtooth RMSE 0.24 vs. 0.58; Square log-likelihood −1.13 vs. −1.39/−1.41). Against the Transformer Neural Process (TNP), SConvCNP wins on every dataset; notably, TNP and TE-TNP fail to train on the Sawtooth benchmark (predictions collapse to zero), while SConvCNP achieves 0.82 log-likelihood there. Compared to the strongest baseline TE-TNP, SConvCNP wins on Sawtooth (0.82 vs. −0.90 log-lik), Square (−1.13 vs. −1.17), Traffic Flow (2.05 vs. 1.72 log-lik), and Image Completion (1.50 vs. not evaluated), while TE-TNP retains a slight edge on Periodic (−0.57 vs. −0.66 log-lik) and Predator-Prey (0.16 vs. 0.14 log-lik). Against ConvCNP, SConvCNP achieves substantially better results on Sawtooth (0.82 vs. 0.10 log-lik; 0.24 vs. 0.40 RMSE) and Square (−1.13 vs. −1.35 log-lik), and matches or slightly exceeds it on Matérn 5/2, Periodic, Traffic Flow, and Image Completion. Overall, SConvCNP is the only method that achieves top-two performance on every metric across all four experimental settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two research threads: convolutional conditional neural processes (ConvCNPs; Gordon et al., 2019), which introduced functional embeddings processed by spatial CNNs to enforce translation equivariance in NPs, and Fourier neural operators (FNOs; Li et al., 2020), which parameterize convolution kernels in the frequency domain for efficient global operator learning in PDE solving. The key conceptual move is replacing the U-Net CNN decoder in ConvCNPs with a U-shaped FNO backbone, thereby inheriting the global receptive field of spectral convolution while retaining the functional-embedding architecture of ConvCNPs. The paper explicitly positions this as a cross-pollination: ideas from neural operator learning (band-limited signal representation, FFT-based global convolution) are transplanted into the NP framework, a direction the authors note has remained 'relatively unexplored.'

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that SConvCNPs achieve competitive performance with state-of-the-art baselines (TE-TNP, ConvCNP) across four diverse benchmarks while maintaining linear scaling in task size, offering a viable alternative to both large-kernel CNNs and quadratic-complexity transformers for long-range dependency modeling in NPs. However, the grid-based formulation means the number of grid points grows exponentially with input dimensionality, restricting applicability to low-dimensional domains—a fundamental limitation the authors acknowledge. Open questions include whether relative positional encodings can restore translation equivariance without sacrificing accuracy, whether patch-based spectral convolutions can mitigate domain-size sensitivity, and whether the approach extends to higher-dimensional or irregularly structured inputs where gridding becomes infeasible.
