## Problem

Existing reasoning-enhanced rating predictors rely on a multi-model distillation pipeline where a general-purpose teacher LLM generates reasoning traces that a smaller student model imitates via SFT. This paradigm suffers from three core limitations: the teacher lacks recommendation-domain expertise, the supervision is costly and static, and the student only acquires surface-level pattern imitation rather than genuine reasoning ability. The paper asks whether a single LLM can autonomously develop recommendation-specific reasoning through pure reinforcement learning without any teacher model.

## Input / Output

The model receives a user's full interaction history (each entry containing item metadata, rating, and review text) concatenated with the target item's metadata as a single text prompt. The output is a structured reasoning trace constrained within special tokens — <analyze user> (extracting liked/disliked features and summarizing preferences), <analyze item> (summarizing target item aspects), and <match> (evaluating user-item compatibility) — followed by a continuous decimal rating prediction inside <rate> tags. Unlike prior methods that predict integer ratings and convert via logit-weighted decoding, RecZero directly outputs decimal ratings, with the reward computed as 1 − |y − ŷ|/max_error.

## Method

RecZero trains a single LLM (Qwen2.5-7B) entirely through GRPO with a rule-based reward that combines a format reward (±0.5 for correct tag structure) and an answer reward (continuous MAE-based score), eliminating any teacher model or SFT stage. The 'Think-before-Recommendation' prompt decomposes rating prediction into four discrete reasoning steps, and GRPO samples multiple trajectories per prompt, computing relative advantages within each group to jointly optimize all reasoning steps toward the recommendation objective. RecOne extends this with a cold-start SFT phase: a teacher LLM (DeepSeek-R1) generates high-quality reasoning traces on a small subset (1000 samples), which are used to initialize the model before RL fine-tuning, bridging the domain gap between pretraining and recommendation. The key architectural difference from prior work is the unified single-model, single-stage training where reasoning and prediction are co-optimized under one reward signal rather than trained in separate decoupled modules.

## Results

RecOne achieves the best performance across all six metrics (RMSE and MAE) on three datasets: Book (RMSE 0.7784, MAE 0.5017), Music (RMSE 0.6776, MAE 0.3816), and Yelp (RMSE 0.9774, MAE 0.7012). Compared to the strongest prior method (Reason4Rec), RecOne reduces RMSE by 6.7%, 12.2%, and 6.2% and MAE by 16.8%, 29.9%, and 7.5% on Book, Music, and Yelp respectively. RecZero, using pure RL without any cold-start SFT, still surpasses all baselines on MAE across all three datasets (e.g., 0.5253 vs. 0.5937 on Book).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RecOne outperforms all seven baselines on every metric across all three datasets, with the largest gains over the strongest LLM-based baseline Reason4Rec (e.g., MAE 0.3816 vs. 0.5352 on Music, a 29.9% reduction). RecZero, trained with pure RL and no teacher model, beats all baselines on MAE for all three datasets but trails Reason4Rec slightly on RMSE for Book (0.8387 vs. 0.8325) and Yelp (1.0521 vs. 0.9972). Among LLM-based baselines, Rec-SAVER and EXP3RT underperform even traditional review-based methods (DAML, NARRE) on RMSE, suggesting that distillation-based reasoning traces from general-purpose LLMs are poorly aligned with the rating prediction objective. The CF-based baseline MF is consistently the weakest across all metrics. RecOne's advantage over RecZero is most pronounced on Music (MAE 0.3816 vs. 0.4271), indicating that cold-start SFT provides the greatest benefit when the domain gap between pretraining and recommendation is largest.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant distillation paradigm in reasoning-enhanced recommendation (Rec-SAVER, EXP3RT, Reason4Rec), where a teacher LLM generates reasoning traces that are then used to SFT a smaller student model. Instead, it draws on the pure-RL post-training recipe from DeepSeek-R1-Zero and GRPO, applying rule-based rewards to train a single LLM end-to-end for rating prediction. The structured 'think-before-recommendation' prompt (analyze user → analyze item → match → rate) is inspired by the multi-step decomposition in EXP3RT and Reason4Rec, but the key conceptual shift is replacing static teacher supervision with online reward-driven optimization, so that reasoning and prediction are jointly refined rather than separately distilled.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is the dramatic reduction in labeled data and pipeline complexity: RecZero(early-stop) matches or beats SFT baselines using ~98% fewer labels and a single-model, single-stage training loop, which is attractive for production recommenders that face constant distribution drift. However, the evaluation is limited to a 7B model on three rating-prediction datasets, and the paper does not explore whether RL-trained models can themselves serve as teachers for iterative self-improvement or whether the approach transfers to ranking/retrieval tasks. Open questions include scalability to larger base models, the role of the KL coefficient and group size in GRPO for recommendation, and whether the structured prompt template generalizes beyond the 1–5 rating scale.
