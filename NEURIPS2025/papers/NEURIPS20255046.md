## Problem

Synthesizing realistic Martian landscape videos for mission rehearsal and robotic navigation is hindered by the scarcity of high-quality Martian data, the substantial domain gap between terrestrial and Martian imagery that breaks existing video generation models, and the difficulty of reconstructing accurate 3D geometry from sparse, photometrically inconsistent rover stereo pairs. No prior work provides both a large-scale multimodal Martian dataset and a video generator that produces 3D-consistent, camera-controllable Martian terrain videos.

## Input / Output

MarsGen accepts a single initial RGB image frame as the primary conditioning signal, with optional 6-DOF camera trajectory sequences (represented as Plücker embeddings) and natural language text prompts to control motion and scene content. The M3arsSynth data engine takes raw stereo navigation image pairs from NASA's Planetary Data System and outputs a multimodal dataset containing rendered novel-view video sequences, per-frame depth and surface normal maps, precise camera pose trajectories, and VLM-generated scene captions. The 3D reconstruction stage produces metric-scale 3D Gaussian Splatting scene representations with approximately 250,000 points per scene.

## Method

The pipeline has two components. M3arsSynth reconstructs 3D Martian scenes from sparse stereo pairs by using VGGT to infer camera intrinsics, Metric3D v2 for metric depth initialization, GIM for robust 2D feature matching, and PnP optimization for relative pose estimation, followed by a depth rescaling step to align monocular depth scales across views. A 3DGS representation is then optimized with a bilateral grid to handle photometric inconsistencies between stereo pairs, and diverse depth-adaptive camera trajectories are sampled to render the multimodal training dataset. MarsGen is a Video Diffusion Transformer initialized from COGVIDEOX-5B-I2V and fine-tuned on M3arsSynth data, using a ControlNet-style branch to inject camera trajectory conditioning via Plücker embeddings into intermediate layers while preserving the pretrained visual priors, with initial-frame conditioning achieved by concatenating the reference frame with the noise input.

## Results

MarsGen achieves the best performance across all evaluated metrics on the M3arsSynth dataset: FID 38.779, FVD 364.822, 2D Warp Error 6.071, PSNR 21.239, SSIM 0.614, and LPIPS 0.351. For the reconstruction pipeline, the metric-aware initialization achieves 100% data utilization with a 0.77 px reprojection error and ~250,000 points per scene, compared to COLMAP's 71.8% utilization and MASt3R's 46.98 px error. The model is fine-tuned on 8 A100 GPUs for 8,000 iterations from the CogVideoX-5B-I2V backbone.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MarsGen outperforms all seven baselines on every reported metric. Against the strongest image-to-video baseline CogVideoX, MarsGen improves FID by ~21% (38.78 vs 48.91) and FVD by ~11% (364.8 vs 411.8), with a modest Warp Error advantage (6.07 vs 6.87). Against camera-controlled baselines, the margins are much larger: MarsGen reduces FID by 69% over CameraCtrl (38.78 vs 123.39) and by 77% over ViewCrafter (38.78 vs 169.94), while also achieving the best novel-view synthesis quality (PSNR 21.24 vs 20.01 and 13.14; SSIM 0.614 vs 0.441 and 0.500). Sora exhibits a catastrophic Warp Error of 10,707.71, indicating severe 3D inconsistency on Martian scenes. For the 3D reconstruction pipeline (Table 2), the proposed metric-aware initialization achieves 100% data utilization with 0.77 px reprojection error, versus COLMAP's 71.8% utilization (0.134 px on the subset it processes) and MASt3R's 100% utilization but 46.98 px error, confirming that the pipeline's robustness and accuracy are both critical for downstream video quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper composes two established research threads—sparse-view 3D reconstruction and controllable video generation—into a unified pipeline specifically for the Martian domain. For reconstruction, it departs from traditional SfM (COLMAP) and transformer-based matching (MASt3R) by integrating geometric foundation models (VGGT for intrinsics, Metric3D v2 for metric depth, GIM for correspondences) with PnP pose estimation and 3D Gaussian Splatting, addressing the unique challenges of texture-poor, photometrically inconsistent Martian stereo imagery. For video generation, it builds on the Video Diffusion Transformer (VDiT) lineage (COGVIDEOX backbone) and ControlNet-style conditioning, but fine-tunes on a purpose-built multimodal dataset (M3arsSynth) that encodes explicit 3D geometric supervision (depth, normals, camera poses) absent from terrestrial training data. The key conceptual departure from prior camera-controllable video work (CameraCtrl, ViewCrafter) is the use of physically grounded 3D reconstructions as the training signal, rather than relying on implicit 3D consistency learned from large-scale terrestrial video.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The M3arsSynth dataset (10K+ metric-scale 3D Martian scenes with aligned video, depth, normals, trajectories, and text) is a significant resource for the planetary simulation community, enabling training of domain-specific generative models where no prior large-scale dataset existed. The 2D Warp Error metric provides a practical way to quantify 3D structural consistency in generated video, filling a gap in evaluation methodology for camera-controllable generation. However, the paper acknowledges limitations: no predictive temporal environmental modeling (e.g., dust storms, lighting changes), no fine-grained 3D semantic understanding, and the pipeline is validated only on Curiosity/Perseverance navigation imagery. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to other planetary bodies, whether the 3D consistency advantage persists at longer video durations, and how to incorporate dynamic scene elements beyond static terrain.
