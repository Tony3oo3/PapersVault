## Problem

Existing model-based algorithms for risk-averse total-reward MDPs (using ERM or EVaR objectives) require full knowledge of transition probabilities and scale poorly. No model-free Q-learning algorithm with convergence guarantees exists for undiscounted, total-reward risk-averse objectives, where the Bellman operator is not a contraction and the full return distribution must be tracked rather than just its expectation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253117/cropped_page8_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes a stream of transition samples (s_i, a_i, s'_i) drawn online from the environment, along with a finite set of risk levels B ⊂ ℝ₊₊ (for ERM) or a discretized β-grid B(β₀, δ) derived from a target EVaR confidence α and precision δ. Step sizes η_i must satisfy the Robbins–Monro conditions (Ση_i = ∞, Ση_i² < ∞) per state-action pair. The output is a tabular state-action value function q̃_i(s, a, β) over S×A×B and, for EVaR, a δ-optimal stationary policy π* selected as the argmax over the finite β-grid of the ERM-TRC objective h(π, β) = ERM_β[return] + β⁻¹ log α.

## Method

The key insight is that ERM is elicitable via the exponential loss ℓ_β(z) = β⁻¹(exp(−βz) − 1) + z, which allows the Bellman operator to be rewritten as an argmin over a strongly convex loss, making it amenable to stochastic gradient descent from samples. The Q-learning update replaces the standard quadratic TD residual with the gradient of ℓ_β, yielding q̃_{i+1}(s,a,β) ← q̃_i(s,a,β) − η_i(exp(−β·z̃_i(β)) − 1), where z̃_i(β) is the TD residual. Because the undiscounted Bellman operator is not a contraction, convergence is established via a monotonicity + boundedness argument: the gradient-step operator H is shown to be monotone, to have the optimal value function as a fixed point, and to satisfy a translation-invariance property (leveraging ERM's law invariance), which together with a boundedness condition on the TD residual (z_min ≤ z̃_i(β) ≤ z_max) yields almost-sure convergence via the Bertsekas stochastic approximation framework. For EVaR, the non-dynamically-consistent supremum over β is replaced by a maximum over a finite discretized grid B(β₀, δ), reducing the EVaR-TRC problem to a sequence of ERM-TRC Q-learning runs, from which a δ-optimal policy is extracted.

## Results

On the Cliff Walking (CW) domain with α=0.2, the optimal EVaR policy achieves a mean return of 1.92 (std 0.228) over 48,000 episodes of 20,000 steps each, while at α=0.6 the mean return drops to −0.074 (std 0.228), reflecting the trade-off between risk aversion and expected reward. Across six random seeds, the EVaR value has a standard deviation of only 0.015, indicating stable convergence. On both CW and Gambler's Ruin (GR) domains, the gap between the Q-learning EVaR estimate and the LP-based ground truth [44] decreases to zero at approximately 20,000 samples, confirming convergence to the optimal value function.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's sole quantitative baseline is the model-based linear programming (LP) solver from Su et al. [44], which requires full knowledge of transition probabilities. The proposed model-free Q-learning algorithm converges to the same EVaR values as the LP method on both the Cliff Walking and Gambler's Ruin domains, with the absolute difference in EVaR values shrinking to zero at roughly 20,000 samples (Figures 5 and 6). This demonstrates that the model-free approach recovers the optimal risk-averse policy without access to the MDP transition model. The paper does not compare against other model-free risk-averse RL methods (e.g., CVaR Q-learning [23,27] or risk-sensitive Q-learning [38]), as those target different risk measures or discounted settings rather than the total-reward ERM/EVaR criterion addressed here.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the model-based risk-averse total-reward MDP framework of Su et al. [44] (AAAI 2025) to the model-free setting by deriving Q-learning algorithms for ERM and EVaR objectives. The key technical departure from standard Q-learning [4] is the use of ERM's elicitability to define an alternative Bellman operator whose fixed point can be computed via stochastic gradient descent on an exponential loss, rather than requiring a contraction property that fails in undiscounted MDPs. The EVaR-to-ERM reduction and discretized β-set construction follow the methodology of Hau et al. [19] (AISTATS 2023) for discounted MDPs, adapted here to the total-reward criterion. The convergence analysis draws on Bertsekas's [8] stochastic approximation framework, replacing the standard contraction assumption with monotonicity plus a boundedness condition.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first model-free algorithm with convergence guarantees for risk-averse total-reward MDPs, filling a gap between the well-developed model-based theory [44] and the practical need for model-free learning in high-stakes applications. The elicitability-based Bellman operator construction is a reusable technique that could extend to other elicitable risk measures and function approximation settings. Key limitations include the tabular restriction, the need to manually choose z_min/z_max bounds and the β_0 parameter (which requires model knowledge or heuristic estimation), and the absence of function approximation analysis. Open questions include whether the monotonicity-based convergence framework extends to approximate settings and how to automate the bound selection in practice.
