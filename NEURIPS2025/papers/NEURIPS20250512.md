## Problem

Existing confidence regions for the linear MNL maximum likelihood estimator carry an explicit multiplicative dependency on κ^{-1}, a problem-dependent quantity that can scale exponentially with the parameter norm ‖θ*‖. In the offline setting, the only prior result requires the optimal assortment S* to be exactly observed in the dataset, while in the online setting all known algorithms either incur a multiplicative κ^{-1} factor or an additional √d dependence. This paper removes both the κ^{-1} and d-dependencies from the leading-order terms in both settings.

## Input / Output

The offline algorithm (LCB-LinearMNL) takes a pre-collected dataset of choice-assortment pairs {(i_k, S_k)}_{k=1}^n along with item feature vectors x_i ∈ R^d and revenues r_i, and outputs a single assortment S^LCB with a sub-optimality gap bound that depends on item-wise coverage n_i = Σ_k 1{i ∈ S_k} rather than the number of exact S* observations. The online algorithm (SupLinearMNL) takes the time horizon T, the problem parameter κ (or a conservative upper bound exp(-KW)), and interacts with customers over T rounds, outputting a sequence of assortments with a cumulative regret bound. A key structural requirement is a burn-in condition bounding max_j ‖x_j‖_{H_D^{-1}(θ*)} ≤ 1/√(d log(N_eff/δ)), which ensures the self-concordant geometry of the MNL loss is well-controlled.

## Method

The core technical contribution is a variance-aware confidence region for the linear MNL MLE that replaces the empirical second-moment matrix V with the Hessian H_D(θ*) of the log-likelihood, yielding |x^⊤(θ̂−θ*)| = Õ(‖x‖_{H_D^{-1}(θ*)}) without any κ^{-1} factor. This is achieved by exploiting the self-concordant-like curvature structure of the K-item MNL loss to design a confidence interval that is d-free under a mild burn-in condition. For offline learning, the LCB-LinearMNL algorithm applies an item-wise pessimistic principle: each item's attraction is estimated as exp(x_i^⊤θ̂ − 72‖x_i‖_{H_D^{-1}(θ̂)}√log(N_eff/δ)), and the assortment maximizing revenue under these pessimistic values is selected. For online learning, the SupLinearMNL algorithm embeds this confidence region within the SupCB elimination framework, adding an initial pure-exploration phase that guarantees the burn-in condition for all sample bins, followed by a multi-layer adaptive elimination loop that balances exploration of uncertain items against exploitation of the UCB-optimal assortment.

## Results

In the offline setting, the proposed LCB-LinearMNL algorithm achieves a sub-optimality gap of Õ(√(Σ_{i∈S*} p_i(S*)p_0(S*)||x_i||²_{H_D^{-1}(θ*)})), which matches the corresponding lower bound and removes the multiplicative d and κ^{-1} dependencies present in prior work [26]. In the online setting, the SupLinearMNL algorithm achieves Õ(√(dT log N)) regret, the first result to eliminate the multiplicative κ^{-1} factor, nearly matching the Ω(√(dT)) lower bound when reduced to the canonical N-item MNL setting. The online result also improves upon the Õ(d√T) bound of [35] by a factor of √(d/log N) when N = o(2^d).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against [26] (Dong et al., ICML 2023), the prior best offline result for linear MNL, this paper removes both the multiplicative d-dependency and the κ^{-1}-dependency in the leading-order sub-optimality bound, and relaxes the coverage requirement from exact observation of S* to item-wise coverage. Against [29] (Han et al., 2025), which achieves Õ(K/√n*) in the canonical N-item setting, this paper generalizes the item-wise pessimistic principle to the full linear MNL setting and recovers the same rate in the uniform-reward N-item special case. In the online setting, the key improvement over [42] (Oh & Iyengar, AAAI 2021) is reducing the κ^{-1} dependency from multiplicative (Õ(κ^{-1}√(dT log N))) to additive (Õ(√(dT log N)) + κ^{-1}d), which is critical since κ^{-1} can scale as exp(W). Compared to [35] (Lee & Oh, 2024), which achieves Õ(d√T) with additive κ^{-1}, this paper improves the d-dependency from d√T to √(dT log N) when N is not exponentially large in d. Against [44] (Perivier & Goyal, NeurIPS 2022) with Õ(Kd√T), this paper removes the K factor and reduces d√T to √(dT log N). The online result also improves upon [18] (Chen et al., JMLR 2020) with Õ(d√T) by the same √(d/log N) factor. In the uniform-revenue online setting, Theorem 4 achieves Õ(√(dTκ*/K)), improving a √K factor over prior best results and matching a new Ω(κ*√(dT)) lower bound.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the SupCB elimination framework [Auer 2002] and the variance-aware confidence bound technique from logistic bandits [Jun et al. 2021] to the linear MNL assortment optimization setting, where the multi-item choice structure introduces significant analytical challenges absent in the K=1 logistic case. The offline algorithm generalizes the item-wise pessimistic principle from the canonical N-item setting [Han et al. 2025] to the general linear MNL model, replacing the restrictive requirement of exact optimal-assortment coverage with a weaker item-wise coverage condition. The key technical departure from prior work [Oh & Iyengar 2021, Lee & Oh 2024] is the use of the Hessian of the MNL log-likelihood (rather than a simpler design matrix) to construct confidence regions, which naturally absorbs the problem-dependent parameter κ and eliminates its multiplicative appearance in the leading-order regret bound.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The removal of the multiplicative κ^{-1} dependency is significant because κ can scale as exp(W) in the worst case, making prior bounds vacuous for moderately large parameter norms; this paper shows the dependence can be demoted to a lower-order additive term. However, the online algorithm (SupLinearMNL) is computationally infeasible due to Ω(N^K) enumeration over assortments in the elimination step, limiting its practical impact. Open questions include designing computationally efficient algorithms matching the Õ(√(dT log N)) regret, fully adapting to an unknown κ without a conservative worst-case bound, and extending the results to non-stationary or adversarial context settings.
