## Problem

Existing LLM4Rec methods treat all item tokens equally, optimizing and maximizing token likelihood without distinguishing between tokens that are decisive for item identification and those that are merely grammatical or filler. This leads to two biases: during tuning, models over-optimize zero-information-gain tokens while under-emphasizing informative ones; during decoding, high-logit but low-decisiveness tokens dominate beam search, biasing predictions toward less discriminative items. The paper addresses the gap of quantifying and incorporating token-level decisiveness into both training and inference.

## Input / Output

The method takes a user's interaction history and task description, encoded into a natural language prompt, and generates top-K recommended item titles autoregressively via constrained beam search. Items are represented as token sequences produced by a standard LLM tokenizer (Qwen2.5 or LLaMA3), and a token prefix tree built from the training set is used to precompute per-token Information Gain values. The output is a ranked list of item titles, evaluated under the all-ranking protocol with HR@K and NDCG@K metrics.

## Method

IGD frames autoregressive item generation as a sequential decision process, defining each token's decisiveness as the Information Gain (IG) it provides in reducing the Shannon entropy of the candidate item distribution conditioned on the generated prefix. Empirically, 55–73% of tokens across four Amazon datasets have zero IG yet carry high logits, causing both tuning and decoding biases. IGD-Tuning introduces a binary reweighting factor β into the cross-entropy loss, downweighting zero-IG tokens to shift learning focus toward informative tokens. IGD-Decoding modifies the beam search cumulative score by scaling each step's log-probability with w_d = 1 − α·IG̃, where IG̃ is the max-min normalized IG among current beam candidates, thereby reducing the influence of low-decisiveness tokens during generation. The two hyperparameters β and α are tuned on validation data, and the method is plug-and-play, integrating into existing LLM4Rec backbones (BIGRec, D3) without architectural changes.

## Results

On the D3 backbone, IGD achieves average improvements of 18.9% in HR@10 and 20.1% in NDCG@10 across four Amazon datasets (CDs, Games, Toys, Books). On the BIGRec backbone, gains are even larger at 20.9% HR@10 and 21.5% NDCG@10. The largest absolute gains appear on Books (e.g., D3+IGD reaches HR@10=0.0424 vs. D3's 0.0315, a +34.6% relative gain) and Games (HR@10=0.0946 vs. 0.0773, +22.7%). On the LLaMA3-8B backbone, IGD further yields an average +26.8% HR@10 and +26.4% NDCG@10 over D3.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IGD consistently outperforms all baselines across every dataset and metric. Against the SOTA LLM4Rec method D3, IGD achieves the largest gains on Books (+34.6% HR@10) and Games (+22.7% HR@10), with more modest but still significant improvements on CDs (+5.0%) and Toys (+5.15%). Compared to the other token-handling strategies (Position Normalization and Causal Fine-tuning from [33]), IGD dominates on all four datasets; for example, on Games D3+IGD reaches HR@10=0.0946 versus 0.0767 (Pos) and 0.0806 (CFT). Traditional sequential recommenders (GRU4Rec, SASRec, LRURec) are clearly outperformed by LLM-based methods, though LRURec is competitive on CDs and Books without token reweighting; IGD restores the LLM advantage on those datasets. On the BIGRec backbone, IGD yields even larger relative gains (up to +41.3% NDCG@10 on Books), confirming that the token-decisiveness signal is backbone-agnostic.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LLM4Rec paradigm established by BIGRec and D3, which frame recommendation as autoregressive token generation. It departs from the prevailing likelihood-driven token handling by introducing an information-theoretic lens: token decisiveness is quantified via Shannon entropy reduction (Information Gain) over a token prefix tree, rather than relying on position-based reweighting (Pos) or causal loss terms (CFT). The method also connects to prior work on token-level biases (ghost tokens in D3, common-token dilution) and token prefix trie usage (MSL's masked softmax, Flower's process rewards), but uniquely uses the trie to store per-node IG scores for debiasing both training and decoding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that 56–73% of item tokens carry zero information gain yet dominate both training loss and beam search scoring challenges the implicit assumption in LLM4Rec that all tokens contribute equally to item identification. The practical impact is significant: a simple binary reweighting yields ~20% average gains in HR@10/NDCG@10 across four datasets and two LLM architectures, making IGD a drop-in improvement for existing LLM4Rec pipelines. Open questions include whether the approach extends to semantic ID tokens and multimodal settings, whether the binary weighting can be replaced by a principled continuous scheme given the highly skewed IG distribution, and whether the prefix tree construction scales to very large item catalogs.
