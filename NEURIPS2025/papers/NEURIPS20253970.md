## Problem

Mainstream LLM scaling relies on either increasing parameters (prohibitive memory, e.g., 672B for DeepSeek-V3) or increasing inference-time reasoning tokens (limited to specific tasks, requires specialized RL data, and can waste compute on trivial problems). The paper asks whether there exists a universal scaling paradigm that enhances model capability without incurring large space or time overheads, and whether computation alone—decoupled from parameter count—can drive capability gains.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253970/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard text token sequence and duplicates it into P parallel copies, each prepended with a distinct learnable prefix embedding (48 tokens per stream) that acts as a separate KV-cache to differentiate streams. After P parallel forward passes through the shared transformer backbone, the P next-token logit vectors are concatenated and passed through a small MLP to produce P softmax-normalized aggregation weights (with ε=0.1 label smoothing), yielding a single weighted-sum next-token distribution. The additional parameters per stream are only ~0.2% of the backbone, and the KV-cache grows by a factor of P.

## Method

PARSCALE scales parallel computation by appending P learnable prefix embeddings to P copies of the input, executing P forward passes through the same transformer backbone in parallel, and aggregating outputs via a dynamic weighted sum learned by an MLP. The key theoretical contribution is a parallel scaling law (Proposition 1) derived from the Chinchilla loss formula, showing that P parallel streams with correlation ρ between stream residuals reduce loss as (A/(N·P^(1/α)·DIVERSITY))^α + E; empirically this is fitted as L = (A/(N·(k·log P + 1)))^α + E with R² up to 0.998, confirming that P streams approximate O(N log P) parameter scaling. A two-stage training strategy (1T tokens standard pre-training followed by 20B tokens of PARSCALE training) reduces the compute overhead of P× FLOPs during training. The method is also applicable to off-the-shelf models via PEFT—freezing the backbone and training only the prefix and aggregation parameters—enabling dynamic switching of P at inference time without retraining.

## Results

After two-stage training on 1T tokens, PARSCALE(P=8) with a 1.8B backbone achieves 58.6% average general, 32.8% math, and 49.9% code scores, yielding a 34% relative improvement on GSM8K (28.7→38.4) and a 23% relative improvement on MMLU (28.5→35.1) over its P=1 baseline. The fitted parallel scaling law (R²=0.998) shows that P parallel streams equate to scaling parameters by O(N·log P), with a higher benefit coefficient k=0.39 on code (Stack-V2) versus k=0.33 on general text (Pile). For a 1.6B model at P=8, PARSCALE uses 22× less memory increase and 6× less latency increase compared to parameter scaling achieving the same performance at batch size 1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PARSCALE(P=8) trained on only 1T public tokens substantially outperforms SmolLM-1.7B (also 1T tokens) on every metric, e.g., 38.4% vs 6.4% on GSM8K and 49.9% vs 37.9% average code, and approaches SmolLM2-1.7B (12T tokens) on general tasks (58.6% vs 63.3%) while trailing on math (32.8% vs 24.3%—actually surpassing it) and code (49.9% vs 41.6%). Against Qwen2.5-1.5B (18T tokens, private data), PARSCALE(P=8) still lags significantly (58.6% vs 63.6% general; 38.4% vs 61.7% GSM8K), reflecting the data-scale gap. The paper also compares against inference-time parallel methods (Beam Search, Majority Voting) in Appendix H, finding that training-time parallel scaling consistently outperforms these inference-only strategies, confirming that the benefit comes from learned aggregation rather than simple multi-sampling. On the 42B-token scaling-law experiments, a 1.6B model with P=8 matches the coding performance of a 4.4B model (39.1% vs 39.2% on HumanEval+/MBPP+ average) and the general-task performance of a 2.8B model (55.7% vs 55.2%), validating the O(N·log P) equivalence.

## Relation to Prior Work

PARSCALE generalizes classifier-free guidance (CFG) from diffusion models by replacing its fixed two-stream heuristic contrast with P learnable parallel streams and dynamic weighted aggregation, while using prefix-tuning to differentiate streams via distinct KV-caches. The theoretical contribution extends the Chinchilla scaling law by introducing parallel computation as a first-class scaling dimension, showing that P streams equate to O(log P) parameter scaling. Conceptually, the method sits at the intersection of model ensemble literature (weight sharing, BatchEnsemble, LoRA ensembles) and inference-time scaling (beam search, self-consistency), but departs from both by making the parallelism a training-time scaling axis rather than an inference-only trick, and by providing a quantitative scaling law that prior ensemble or CFG work had not established.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a practical and theoretically grounded alternative to parameter scaling for low-resource and edge deployment, where the 22× memory and 6× latency savings at equivalent performance are substantial. The finding that reasoning-intensive tasks (coding, math) benefit disproportionately from parallel computation compared to memorization tasks provides a quantitative decomposition of the parameters-vs-computation contribution to model capability, a question the field has lacked tools to address. Open questions include how the scaling law behaves at much larger P (beyond 8), how it interacts with MoE architectures and data repetition, and whether the two-stage training strategy generalizes to models trained from scratch with PARSCALE throughout. The paper's scope is limited to dense Transformer architectures up to 4.4B parameters, leaving open whether the log-scaling relationship holds for sparser or multimodal models.
