## Problem

Seamlessly inserting novel objects into multi-view 3D scenes requires simultaneously solving color harmonization, relighting, and shadow synthesis while maintaining cross-view consistency. Existing diffusion-based compositing methods are slow, produce unstable color outputs, and lack multi-view coherence, while inverse-rendering approaches demand heavy per-scene optimization and large paired datasets. The paper addresses this gap with an efficient feed-forward framework that directly learns illumination and shadow priors without iterative denoising or explicit environment-map estimation.

## Input / Output

The 2D stage consumes a per-view triplet of inharmonious composite image, background-only image, and depth map (all at 256×256) and outputs a harmonized image plus intermediate feature maps. The 3D stage takes the multi-view 2D features projected into Gaussian space and the original inharmonious Gaussian colors, producing harmonized per-Gaussian color attributes that, when splatted, yield view-consistent renderings. Inference runs at 0.07 s per frame for the 2D model and ~1 s for the 3D model after roughly one minute of scene-specific Gaussian fitting, with only ~33 MB of model memory.

## Method

MV-CoLight is a two-stage feed-forward pipeline built on Swin Transformers. In stage 1, a per-view Swin Transformer (3 cascaded blocks × 6 layers, embedding dim 96, 6 heads) processes the {composite, background, depth} input to extract pixel-wise illumination features. In stage 2, these 2D features are projected into 3D Gaussian space via a Hilbert-curve mapping Φ(·) that orders Gaussian primitives along a space-filling curve and folds them into a 2D grid, preserving spatial locality; they are concatenated with the original Gaussian colors (also Hilbert-ordered via Ψ(·)) and fed into a second Swin Transformer that predicts only the color attributes while all geometric parameters (position, scale, rotation, opacity) remain frozen. The 3D loss combines a Gaussian-space color loss with a rendered-image loss weighted by β=0.5, and the entire system is trained supervised on 480k+ Blender-rendered scenes with deliberately mismatched foreground/background lighting. The Hilbert-curve reordering is the key architectural choice that lets a standard 2D transformer operate on 3D Gaussian primitives while maintaining spatial coherence, replacing heavier alternatives like Point Transformer V3 with a far lighter and equally effective mechanism.

## Results

On the multi-view evaluation (Table 2), MV-CoLight achieves PSNR 30.29 / SSIM 0.960 / LPIPS 0.030 on simple synthetic scenes, 30.13 / 0.952 / 0.027 on complex synthetic scenes, and 26.39 / 0.927 / 0.040 on real captured scenes, outperforming all 10 baselines by large margins. Inference time is approximately 1.08 s per frame after ~1.08 min of scene-specific Gaussian fitting, compared to 19–60 s for diffusion-based methods and 17 s–3 h for inverse-rendering baselines. On the single-view benchmark (Table 1), the full model reaches PSNR 29.65 (simple) and 28.75 (OWL), with only 32.94 MB memory footprint versus 5–14 GB for diffusion-based competitors.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MV-CoLight outperforms all 10 baselines on every metric across all three dataset splits. Among diffusion-based methods, the closest competitor is IC-Light (PSNR 20.60 on complex synthetic), yet MV-CoLight still leads by ~9.5 dB; RGB↔X and GPT-4o trail by over 17 dB in PSNR, reflecting the instability of stochastic sampling for pixel-level color mapping. PCT-Net, the strongest feed-forward harmonization baseline, reaches PSNR 25.19 on complex synthetic but lacks shadow and highlight synthesis, yielding a 4.9 dB gap and LPIPS of 0.035 vs. 0.027. Among inverse-rendering baselines, IRGS is the closest (PSNR 21.04 on complex synthetic) but requires 3 hours of per-scene optimization versus MV-CoLight's ~1 minute, and its material-estimation pipeline conflates specular highlights with albedo, degrading multi-view coherence. In terms of efficiency, MV-CoLight's 1.08 s inference and 32.9 MB memory footprint are orders of magnitude faster and lighter than diffusion-based methods (4.5–24 s, 5–14 GB) and inverse-rendering methods (17 s–3 h with additional training overhead).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on 3D Gaussian Splatting as its geometric backbone but departs fundamentally from the dominant diffusion-based compositing paradigm (ObjectStitch, ControlCom, RGB↔X) by replacing stochastic generative sampling with a deterministic feed-forward Swin Transformer. The key architectural departure is the Hilbert curve mapping that bridges per-pixel 2D features into an ordered 3D Gaussian color field, a technique borrowed from point-cloud processing (Point Transformer V3) but repurposed here for illumination transfer. Unlike inverse-rendering approaches (GS-IR, GI-GS, IRGS) that require per-scene optimization of materials and environment maps, MV-CoLight learns illumination priors directly from multi-view composites, eliminating the need for explicit physical decomposition.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 0.07s-per-frame inference and the 480k-scene synthetic dataset address two practical bottlenecks that have limited prior compositing work: speed for real-time AR/embodied-intelligence deployment and the absence of large-scale training data with controlled lighting inconsistencies. The paper's core insight—that multi-view 3D compositing reduces to a color-mapping problem rather than a generative one—reframes the task and suggests diffusion models may be overkill for this setting. Open questions include closing the synthetic-to-real color gap, enforcing physical consistency (shadow direction, specular placement), and extending to dynamic 4D scenes, all of which the authors acknowledge as future work.
