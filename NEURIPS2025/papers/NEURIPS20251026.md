## Problem

Existing affective BCI datasets face a fundamental gap: brain-signal-based datasets (EEG, fNIRS) provide only static, post-hoc emotion labels that fail to capture the dynamic evolution of emotional states during stimulus presentation, while real-time dynamically annotated datasets (e.g., CASE, CEAP-360VR) record only peripheral physiological signals without direct brain measurements. No publicly available dataset simultaneously records multimodal brain signals and provides continuous, real-time emotional annotations, limiting research into the temporal dynamics of emotion-related neural and hemodynamic responses.

## Input / Output

The dataset provides 64-channel EEG (1000 Hz, AFz reference) and 51-channel fNIRS (47.62 Hz, HbO/HbR/HbT) signals recorded over prefrontal and bilateral temporal cortices during 15 emotion-inducing video clips (~100 s each) plus 5 s pre-stimulus baselines, totaling ~820 minutes across 32 participants. Labels come in two forms: continuous 1 Hz valence-arousal joystick annotations (integers 1–255, initialized at center 128,128) and post-trial SAM scale ratings (1–9 for valence, arousal, dominance, familiarity). For supervised learning, signals are segmented into ≥1 s windows; regression uses the raw continuous label values (normalized to [0,1]), while classification uses three bins (low 0–0.4, medium 0.4–0.6, high 0.6–1.0), with binary splits explicitly discouraged due to the high proportion of neutral-center labels.

## Method

The core contribution is the REFED dataset itself, built around a custom real-time annotation and control system (HTML/JavaScript + Xbox controller) that lets participants continuously move a cursor in a 2D valence-arousal plane during video viewing, with annotations transmitted via serial communication to the Neuroscan EEG and Shimadzu LABNIRS acquisition systems for precise temporal alignment. For empirical validation, the authors apply the TSMMF bidirectional cross-modal transformer model with temporal and spectral domain features under 3-fold cross-validation per participant (15 clips split into three balanced groups of five emotion categories), reporting MAE/MSE for regression and accuracy/F1 for three-class classification. A key design choice is recommending regression over classification to exploit the continuous label granularity, and demonstrating that combined EEG+fNIRS input outperforms either modality alone, confirming non-redundant neurovascular coupling information. Spatial-temporal visualization via differential entropy (DE) features across the five emotion categories further validates distinct activation patterns in prefrontal and temporal regions.

## Results

Using the TSMMF model with 3-fold cross-validation across 32 participants, the REFED dataset achieves valence regression MAE of 0.1705 and arousal regression MAE of 0.1445 (normalized to [0,1]) with the combined EEG+fNIRS modality. For three-class classification (low/medium/high), arousal reaches 67.0% accuracy (F1=0.406) and valence reaches 62.7% accuracy (F1=0.461). The combined EEG+fNIRS modality consistently outperforms either single modality across all four task settings, confirming the complementary value of electrophysiological and hemodynamic signals for emotion decoding.

## Baselines & Comparisons

As a dataset paper, REFED does not benchmark against competing emotion-recognition methods; instead, it validates data quality by comparing three modality configurations (EEG only, fNIRS only, EEG+fNIRS) under the same TSMMF model. The combined EEG+fNIRS modality wins on every metric: it improves valence classification accuracy by 3.1 pp over EEG alone (62.7% vs. 59.6%) and 0.7 pp over fNIRS alone (62.7% vs. 62.0%), and improves arousal classification accuracy by 1.7 pp over EEG (67.0% vs. 65.3%). In regression, the multimodal setup reduces valence MAE from 0.1822 (EEG) to 0.1705 and arousal MAE from 0.1542 (EEG) to 0.1445, with fNIRS alone being slightly better than EEG on both regression tasks. The consistent (though modest) gains from fusion support the paper's claim that EEG and fNIRS capture non-redundant neural and hemodynamic information, validating the neurovascular coupling hypothesis in emotional processing.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of two previously separate lines of work: multimodal brain-signal affective datasets (DEAP, SEED, FEAD, TVED-fNIRS) that record EEG and/or fNIRS but assign only static per-stimulus labels, and continuous-annotation affective datasets (CASE, CEAP-360VR) that capture dynamic valence-arousal trajectories but rely solely on peripheral physiological signals (PPG, ECG, EDA) without direct brain measurements. REFED is positioned as the first dataset to simultaneously provide both multimodal cortical signals (64-ch EEG + 51-ch fNIRS) and real-time, time-aligned dynamic emotion annotations, thereby bridging a gap neither community had addressed. The evaluation leverages the TSMMF cross-modal transformer and standard SVM-based temporal-spectral features, building on established affective BCI decoding pipelines rather than introducing a new architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By enabling regression-based emotion decoding with continuous labels rather than coarse per-clip categories, REFED opens the door to studying fine-grained neural and hemodynamic dynamics during emotional transitions and neurovascular coupling under evolving affective states. The authors acknowledge that real-time joystick annotation imposes cognitive load that may attenuate emotional immersion, and that the lab setting, fixed video order, and homogeneous participant pool (Chinese students, 18–34) limit ecological validity and generalizability. Open questions include whether the dynamic-label advantage persists with more sophisticated deep architectures, how the dataset performs in cross-subject and cross-session settings, and whether portable EEG-fNIRS hardware can replicate the protocol in naturalistic environments.
