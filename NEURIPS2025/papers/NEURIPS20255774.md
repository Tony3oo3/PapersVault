## Problem

Classical kernel density estimation suffers from an O(h²) bias that limits its convergence rate to O(n^{-4/(d+4)}) in MISE. While higher-order kernels can reduce this bias, they produce negative density values that are computationally intractable to normalize. The paper asks whether score function information (∇log p) can be leveraged to debias KDE without these pathologies, pushing the bias-variance Pareto frontier.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255774/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes n data samples in ℝ^d, an estimated score function (which may be exact, learned via a DDPM, or computed empirically from a vanilla KDE), a symmetric kernel K with unit covariance, a bandwidth h, and a step size δ. It outputs a pointwise density estimate p̂(x) that is guaranteed non-negative (unlike higher-order kernel alternatives). The score estimate need not be exact—Corollary 1 characterizes the residual bias from score error, and experiments confirm robustness to Gaussian noise up to σ=4. The method is demonstrated on 1D/2D scalar data and 28×28 grayscale MNIST images in latent space.

## Method

SD-KDE shifts each data point by a single step along the estimated score (x̃_i = x_i + δ·ŝ(x_i)) before applying standard KDE with bandwidth h. This one-step Euler-Maruyama discretization of Langevin dynamics sharpens the effective sample distribution, counteracting the smearing introduced by kernel smoothing. The key theoretical result (Theorem 1) shows that choosing δ = h²/2 exactly cancels the leading O(h²) bias term, reducing bias to O(h⁴) and yielding an optimal bandwidth h_opt = O(n^{-1/(d+8)}) with MISE of O(n^{-8/(d+8)})—a strict improvement over the standard O(n^{-4/(d+4)}) rate. The method requires no learned diffusion model: the score can be obtained from the gradient of a vanilla KDE (Emp-SD-KDE), and iterative application of the correction progressively removes higher-order bias terms, with diminishing returns after two or three iterations.

## Results

SD-KDE achieves a theoretical MISE convergence rate of O(n^{-8/(d+8)}), improving upon the standard KDE rate of O(n^{-4/(d+4)}). Empirically, at n=5×10⁴ on 1D Gaussian mixtures, SD-KDE yields an order-of-magnitude smaller MISE than Silverman KDE. Fitted log-log regression slopes of −0.85 (mixture 1) and −0.93 (mixture 3) closely track the theoretical prediction of −8/9 ≈ −0.86. The method remains effective even with noisy score estimates up to σ=4, and the empirical-score variant (Emp-SD-KDE) still substantially outperforms vanilla Silverman KDE without requiring a true score oracle.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is Silverman KDE (Silverman, 1986), the standard nonparametric density estimator with the classical Silverman bandwidth rule. SD-KDE with the true score function consistently outperforms Silverman KDE across all three 1D Gaussian mixtures: at n=100, it achieves lower MISE in 100% of seeds for mixtures 1 and 2 and 95% for mixture 3, and at n=5×10⁴ the MISE gap reaches roughly one order of magnitude. Even when the score is perturbed with Gaussian noise (σ up to 4), SD-KDE retains a significant MISE advantage over Silverman. The Emp-SD-KDE variant, which estimates the score from a vanilla KDE rather than using the ground-truth score, also beats Silverman KDE across all tested sample sizes. In 2D (spiral and Gaussian mixture), SD-KDE with the oracle score outperforms Silverman in both visual quality and MISE, while using a score learned from a small DDPM yields performance comparable to Silverman, suggesting the gap is attributable to diffusion-model training difficulty rather than a fundamental limitation of the method.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two well-established lines of work: classical kernel density estimation (Rosenblatt, Parzen, Silverman) and score-based generative modeling (score matching, diffusion models). The core idea—shifting data points along the score before applying KDE—is explicitly framed as a one-step Euler-Maruyama discretization of Langevin dynamics, connecting it to the SDE literature (Song & Ermon, 2019; Song et al., 2020). It departs from the standard approach of using higher-order kernels (e.g., Silverman's effective spline kernel) to reduce bias, which can produce negative density estimates, by instead using the score to cancel the leading bias term while preserving the positivity of the Gaussian kernel.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method achieves a provable improvement in MISE convergence from O(n^{-4/(d+4)}) to O(n^{-8/(d+8)}), a meaningful asymptotic gain for nonparametric density estimation. Practically, the finding that SD-KDE remains effective with noisy score estimates (including scores derived from a vanilla KDE itself) suggests the method is usable without a trained diffusion model, lowering the barrier to adoption. Key limitations include the theoretical requirement of an exact score oracle, the computational cost of score estimation in high dimensions, and the fact that the MNIST experiment is preliminary. Open questions include whether multi-step Langevin discretization or higher-order schemes can push the convergence rate further.
