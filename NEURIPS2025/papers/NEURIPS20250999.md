## Problem

Aggressive data filtering produces high-quality but small datasets, creating a tension with the ever-growing token budgets of frontier LLMs. It was unclear whether repeating a heavily filtered dataset for multiple epochs could match or exceed training on a much larger but lower-quality superset, and whether document-level quality heterogeneity could be exploited to build better datasets under a fixed token budget.

## Input / Output

The method consumes web-scraped text documents (from Common Crawl) that have already passed through filtering pipelines (DCLM-baseline, RefinedWeb, or C4), augmented with per-document FastText quality scores and fuzzy/exact duplicate counts. It produces two types of output: (1) trained transformer language models (1B–12.6B parameters) evaluated on 22-task core metrics and MMLU, and (2) resampled datasets where individual documents are assigned non-uniform copy counts via a user-specified function, effectively creating a new training corpus from an existing one without requiring new data.

## Method

The paper makes two complementary empirical contributions. First, it shows that repeating DCLM-baseline (the top 10% of RefinedWeb by FastText score) for up to 10 epochs, with weight decay scaled by roughly the square root of the repeat count, consistently outperforms a single epoch on the 10× larger RefinedWeb superset across model sizes from 1B to 12.6B parameters. Second, it introduces 'count manipulation,' a document-level resampling strategy where a function (greedy, linear, or custom) maps a quality metric to a target copy count for each document, allowing high-quality documents to be oversampled while low-quality ones are downsampled or removed. The key insight is that the optimal count function depends on the compute regime: when excess high-quality tokens are available, greedy single-copy selection suffices, but when data is constrained (fewer unique tokens than Chinchilla-optimal), a linear function that balances diversity against quality exploitation outperforms both uniform subsampling and standard deduplication.

## Results

The headline finding is that repeating DCLM-baseline (top 10% of RefinedWeb by FastText score) for 10 epochs with adapted weight decay (2× default) outperforms a single epoch on the 10× larger RefinedWeb superset at 12.6B parameters and 252B total tokens, a result that holds across model sizes from 1B to 12.6B (Figure 1). At the document level, count manipulation using an ensemble metric (worst rank of FastText score and duplicate count) with a greedy 1-copy strategy achieves 46.1 Core (22-task average) and 46.0 MMLU for a 7B model trained on 138B tokens, surpassing the uniform subsample baseline of 44.4 Core and 42.2 MMLU. Weight decay scaling by roughly the square root of the number of repeats is the key recipe adaptation: at 10 epochs, increasing WD from 1× to 2× recovers 3.4 points on the centered core metric (0.489 → 0.523).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against RefinedWeb (Penedo et al., 2023), the paper's central comparison shows DCLM-baseline repeated for up to 10 epochs with adapted weight decay consistently outperforms a single epoch of the 10× larger RefinedWeb superset across all tested model sizes (1B, 3B, 7B, 12.6B), with the gap widening at larger compute budgets. Against C4 (Raffel et al., 2020), both DCLM-baseline and RefinedWeb show similar repeatability degradation curves on C4 validation perplexity, but DCLM-baseline maintains substantially better absolute downstream accuracy when repeated, confirming that dataset quality and repeatability are independent axes. Against Zyda-2 DCLM-dedup (Tokpanov et al., 2024), DCLM-baseline without additional global deduplication outperforms the globally deduplicated version on both Core (44.4 vs 43.6) and MMLU (42.2 vs 29.4) for 7B Chinchilla-optimal training, suggesting that sharded deduplication plus count manipulation is preferable to aggressive global deduplication. Within the count manipulation experiments, the ensemble metric (combining FastText score and duplicate count) clearly beats using either metric alone, with greedy 1-copy ensemble gaining +1.7 Core and +3.8 MMLU over uniform subsampling.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of data filtering for LLM pre-training (DCLM, FineWeb-edu, RefinedWeb) and the study of dataset repeatability under data-constrained training (Muennighoff et al.). While prior work established that aggressive filtering improves single-epoch performance and that web data can be repeated a few times before diminishing returns, this paper explicitly studies the interaction between the two: whether heavily filtered datasets remain competitive when repeated for many epochs to compensate for reduced token count. It departs from Goyal et al.'s finding (for CLIP) that less aggressive filtering is preferable at larger compute by showing the opposite holds for LLMs when regularization is adapted. The document-level 'count manipulation' framework extends standard deduplication and subsampling by treating document repetition as a continuous design variable rather than a binary keep/remove decision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As frontier LLM compute budgets grow, the finite supply of high-quality web text makes the quality-vs-quantity tradeoff in data filtering an urgent practical question. The finding that ten epochs of the top-10% filtered subset can match or exceed one epoch of the full superset—provided weight decay is scaled—gives practitioners a concrete recipe for stretching aggressively filtered datasets. The document-level count manipulation tool offers a more granular alternative to whole-dataset repetition, though its benefits are most pronounced in data-constrained regimes. Key open questions include whether these trends persist at frontier scale (the largest model here is 12.6B), whether they transfer to non-web domains like code and math, and how sharded vs. global deduplication interacts with high repeat counts at scale.
