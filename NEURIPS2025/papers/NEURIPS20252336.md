## Problem

Diffusion-based samplers for unnormalized target distributions suffer from two compounding sources of bias: time-discretization error in simulating the reverse SDE and approximation error in the score function. Existing diffusion MC samplers (RDMC, SLIPS) lack a principled mechanism to correct these biases, and the closest SMC-based correction (Phillips et al.) requires training neural network surrogates. The paper asks whether one can build a training-free SMC framework that systematically eliminates both error sources while also yielding an unbiased normalization constant estimate.

## Input / Output

The method requires only an unnormalized target density π̃(x) (no training data or samples from the target), a variance-preserving diffusion schedule {α_t, σ_t, f_t, g_t}, a particle count N, and a discretization step count T. It outputs a weighted set of N samples approximating the target π(x) together with a finite-sample unbiased estimate of the normalization constant Z = ∫π̃(x)dx. Unlike prior diffusion MC samplers, RDSMC does not need target variance information for initialization and starts sampling from the base distribution at τ_T = 1 rather than an intermediate time.

## Method

RDSMC wraps a reverse-diffusion proposal inside an SMC framework. The proposal simulates the reverse SDE using score estimates obtained via the denoising score identity (DSI) with an inner AIS procedure, producing both a score estimate s(x_t, u_t) and, as a byproduct, an unbiased estimate π̂(x_t, u_t) of the forward marginal Z·π(x_t). These marginal estimates are plugged into intermediate target distributions γ_t that mirror the optimal marginals of the extended target π(x_{0:T}), forming an 'exact approximation' in the SMC sense: marginalizing out the auxiliary variables recovers the true optimal target at every step. The SMC weighting function w_t = π̂(x_t,u_t)·π(x_{t+1}|x_t) / [π̂(x_{t+1},u_{t+1})·q(x_t|x_{t+1},u_{t+1})] corrects for both discretization and score-estimation bias through resampling, while the final target at t=0 is exactly π̃(x_0) by construction. The result is a nested SMC (outer diffusion-step loop, inner AIS loop) that is training-free, asymptotically exact as N→∞, and provides an unbiased Z estimate for any fixed N.

## Results

On Bayesian logistic regression, RDSMC achieves test log-likelihoods of −124.00±1.96 (Credit), −62.23±1.96 (Cancer), −87.72±1.75 (Ionosphere), and −101.52±1.84 (Sonar), closely matching the best baseline SLIPS (−121.79, −56.26, −85.07, −102.39). On synthetic targets, RDSMC achieves the lowest log-normalization-constant bias on Rings (0.03 vs. 0.05 for AIS/SMC) and ties with AIS/SMC on Funnel (0.28). RDSMC is the only diffusion-based method providing unbiased log Z estimates, and its ablation RDSMC(Proposal) without SMC correction degrades substantially (e.g., −606.03 test LL on Credit vs. −124.00 for full RDSMC).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RDSMC most closely matches SLIPS [9], which achieves the best overall test log-likelihood across all four Bayesian logistic regression datasets; RDSMC is within ~2–6 log-likelihood points on each dataset but exhibits higher variance (±1.96 vs. ±0.04–0.08). Against AIS [25] and SMC [26], RDSMC trades a small test-LL deficit (e.g., −124.00 vs. −122.73 on Credit) for a lower log Z estimation bias on synthetic targets (0.03 vs. 0.05 on Rings) and for being training-free without requiring target-variance-based initialization. RDSMC substantially outperforms RDMC [8] and SMS [33] on every metric: on Credit, RDSMC reaches −124.00 versus −388.24 (RDMC) and −527.79 (SMS); on Funnel, RDSMC achieves 0.11 sliced KSD versus 0.13 (RDMC) and 0.15 (SMS). The ablation RDSMC(Proposal) without SMC weighting/resampling collapses to −606.03 on Credit, confirming that the SMC correction mechanism is essential rather than a minor refinement.

## Relation to Prior Work

RDSMC formalizes existing diffusion MC samplers (RDMC, SLIPS, and rejection-sampling-based approaches) as proposal mechanisms within a Sequential Monte Carlo framework, inheriting the nested SMC structure of Naesseth et al. The closest prior work is Phillips et al.'s Particle Denoising Diffusion Sampler, which also uses SMC to correct diffusion proposal bias but requires training neural network surrogates and only recovers the true target at the final step. RDSMC departs by being entirely training-free: it reuses importance weights from MC score estimation as unbiased marginal estimates for intermediate targets, achieving exact alignment with optimal targets at every SMC iteration at no additional computational cost.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RDSMC provides a principled, training-free wrapper that converts biased diffusion MC samplers into asymptotically exact samplers with unbiased normalization constant estimates, a property absent in prior diffusion MC methods. This is practically important for Bayesian inference where the evidence (log Z) is often needed for model comparison. Key limitations include mode collapse in high-dimensional multi-modal targets, auxiliary variance from the nested structure, and reliance on oracle metrics for hyperparameter tuning. Open directions include combining RDSMC with training-based diffusion samplers, SNR-adapted discretization, and reducing nested variance through correlated pseudo-marginal techniques.
