## Problem

Existing multi-hop heterophilic GNNs (e.g., H2GCN, MixHop) require separate weight matrices per hop and feature concatenation, causing parameters and memory to scale linearly with hop count, while their global aggregation schemes fail to capture the non-uniform, node-specific heterophily patterns that vary across both nodes and hop distances in real-world graphs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250393/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

GAMMA takes a node feature matrix X ∈ R^{n×d_in} and a (normalized) adjacency matrix A ∈ R^{n×n}, and produces node representations in R^{n×d_out} that maintain the same dimensionality as single-hop GNNs regardless of the number of hops K considered. A key property is that the per-node gating coefficients α_{i,p} are recomputed from scratch during inference on unseen graphs, enabling adaptation to local heterophily patterns without any gradient updates or retraining.

## Method

GAMMA applies a single shared projection W to input features, then computes p-hop propagated embeddings A^p(XW) for p=0,…,K, each modulated by a learnable channel-wise scaling vector γ_p to capture global per-hop heterophily patterns. The core mechanism is an iterative gating loop (R=2–3 iterations) inspired by capsule network routing: each node maintains gating logits b_i, computes softmax weights α_{i,p}, forms a weighted sum of L2-normalized hop embeddings, applies a squash nonlinearity to obtain an evolving representation v_i, and updates logits via dot-product agreement scores between v_i and each hop embedding. This node-specific, activation-driven weighting allows each node to selectively amplify informative hops while suppressing noisy ones, all within a fixed d_out-dimensional space that avoids the (K+1)·d_out expansion of concatenation-based methods. The weight-sharing design reduces parameters from (K+1)·d_in·d_out to d_in·d_out + (K+1)·d_out, and the gating operations (softmax, element-wise products on small [K+1]-vectors) are GPU-friendly, yielding up to 20× faster inference than H2GCN.

## Results

On the 9 small-scale benchmarks (Table 1), GAMMA achieves the best accuracy on 4 of 9 datasets: Texas (87.37%), Actor (35.59%), Cornell (78.68%), and PubMed (89.62%), while remaining within 1–2% of the best on the others. On 7 large-scale heterophilic benchmarks (Table 2), GAMMA achieves the best accuracy on minesweeper (87.58%), tolokers (82.59%), and amazon-photos (96.28%), and is the only method that avoids OOM on ogbn-products (2.4M nodes, 61M edges) alongside GPRGNN. Across all 16 datasets, GAMMA attains an average rank of 2.06, compared to 3.71 for H2GCN and 3.87 for MixHop. In terms of efficiency, GAMMA runs ~20× faster than H2GCN and uses ~12× less GPU memory than M2MGNN on the Flickr dataset.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest heterophilic-specific baselines, GAMMA outperforms H2GCN on Texas (87.37 vs 86.58), Actor (35.59 vs 35.33), and Cornell (78.68 vs 78.16), but trails on Chameleon (51.16 vs 59.69) and Squirrel (36.20 vs 38.36). Compared to MixHop, GAMMA wins on Texas (+3.16), Wisconsin (+0.58), and Cornell (+4.21), while MixHop edges ahead on Chameleon (52.54 vs 51.16). Against M2MGNN, GAMMA leads on Texas (+2.90) and Cornell (+1.84) but is slightly behind on Wisconsin (86.27 vs 87.06). On spectral methods, GAMMA substantially outperforms GPRGNN on all heterophilic datasets (e.g., Texas: 87.37 vs 61.58; Chameleon: 51.16 vs 34.43) and BernNet (e.g., Texas: 87.37 vs 79.47; Cornell: 78.68 vs 73.95). On large-scale graphs, H2GCN, MixHop, GAT, and M2MGNN all hit OOM on ogbn-products (2.4M nodes), while GAMMA completes training at 72.61% accuracy, second only to GPRGNN (73.26%). On homophilic benchmarks, GAMMA matches or slightly trails the best methods (e.g., Cora: 87.42 vs H2GCN 87.75; CiteSeer: 75.49 vs BernNet 76.42), confirming it does not sacrifice homophilic performance for heterophilic gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

GAMMA adapts the iterative routing and squash mechanism from capsule networks (Sabour et al., 2017) to the multi-hop message passing setting, repurposing a computer-vision technique for graph representation learning. It departs from the dominant multi-hop strategies in heterophilic GNNs—feature concatenation (MixHop, H2GCN) and globally learned spectral filters (GPR-GNN, BernNet)—by replacing them with a node-specific iterative gating loop that fuses hop embeddings into a fixed-dimension vector. The weight-sharing scheme with learnable channel-wise scaling vectors further distinguishes GAMMA from prior multi-hop architectures that assign independent projection matrices per hop, reducing parameters from (K+1)×d_in×d_out to d_in×d_out plus (K+1)×d_out.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper's core practical contribution is demonstrating that competitive heterophilic GNN accuracy need not come with the 6× memory and 33× runtime overhead of methods like H2GCN, making heterophilic graph learning feasible on commodity GPUs and for large-scale graphs (e.g., ogbn-products with 2.4M nodes) where competitors hit OOM. Open questions include whether the fixed number of routing iterations (typically 2–3) is suboptimal for nodes with highly variable neighborhood complexity, and whether more sophisticated agreement measures beyond dot products could capture particularly complex heterophilic relationships. The finding that node-level heterophily patterns vary dramatically within a single dataset (e.g., only 20.7% pattern consistency in Chameleon) provides a concrete motivation for per-node adaptivity that global filter coefficients cannot address.
