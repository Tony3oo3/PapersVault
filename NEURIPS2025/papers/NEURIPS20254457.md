## Problem

Existing adversarial attacks on text-to-image models target individual defense layers (prompt filters, concept erasers, image filters) in isolation, but fail dramatically against the full multi-layered defense deployed in real-world T2I systems—for example, DACA drops from 89.1% ASR against prompt filters alone to 1.4% against the combined defense. The paper identifies that these layers have structurally overlapping vulnerabilities: explicit NSFW terms needed to bypass concept erasers are trivially caught by prompt filters, while images that evade image filters appear normal to human observers. This work is the first to exploit these overlaps to bypass all defense layers simultaneously in a black-box setting.

## Input / Output

The method takes an explicit NSFW victim prompt and black-box access to a T2I system (querying via API or web interface, receiving only whether an image was generated or a warning was returned). Three LLMs (GPT-4o, o1-mini, GPT-4.1) are queried to generate candidate word substitutions and stylistic augmentations, with a fixed candidate list size of 10 per word. The output is a transstratal adversarial prompt (TAP) that simultaneously evades prompt filters, concept erasers, and image filters, yielding a generated image that is subjectively NSFW to human observers yet evades objective NSFW classifiers.

## Method

TAA operates in two stages. First, transstratal adversarial candidate generation uses LLMs to construct two candidate sets: an implicit set (S_imp) that replaces explicit NSFW terms with metaphorical substitutes (e.g., 'birthday suit' for 'nude') to bypass prompt filters while preserving NSFW generation capability, and a subjective set (S_sub) that appends artistic style descriptors to redirect image-filter attention while maintaining human-perceptible harmfulness. Second, adversarial genetic optimization iteratively refines prompt substitutions via a failure-driven selection mechanism: candidate probabilities are computed via softmax over failure counts (decremented for high-fitness, incremented for low-fitness candidates), and the GA employs adaptive crossover weighted by parent fitness, a linearly decreasing mutation rate (0.5→0.1), and roulette wheel selection over 20 generations with population size 20. The fitness function combines bypass rate (whether the image is generated) and image harmful score (fraction of NSFW evaluators flagging the output). This achieves 85.6% average ASR across 14 T2I models and 17 safety modules, surpassing the best prior baseline by 73.5 percentage points.

## Results

On SD-v1.4 with ESD concept eraser and full multi-layered defenses (4 prompt filters + concept eraser + Safety-Checker image filter), TAA achieves an 85.6% Attack Success Rate (ASR), meaning 112/190 prompts simultaneously bypass all prompt filters, generate images harmful by all 4 NSFW evaluators, and evade the image filter. This surpasses the best baseline (UnlearnDiff at 12.1%) by 73.5 percentage points. Across 10 open-source T2I models (Table 3), TAA maintains ASR between 84.9% and 94.9%, and against 6 different image filters (Table 4), ASR ranges from 64.9% (MHSC) to 100% (Q16).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TAA dominates all 10 baselines by a wide margin on the primary ASR metric (85.6% vs. best baseline UnlearnDiff at 12.1%). A critical finding is that baselines exhibit mutually exclusive trade-offs: DACA achieves the highest prompt-filter bypass (PBC@4=128) but generates images with zero harmfulness (IHC@4=0), while P4D_N produces highly harmful images (IHC@4=75) but fails to bypass prompt filters (PBC@4=13) and image filters (IBC=77). TAA resolves both trade-offs simultaneously, achieving near-perfect scores on all three defense layers (PBC@4=189, IHC@4=112, IBC=190). Among black-box baselines, SneakyPrompt (9.1%) is the strongest but still trails TAA by 76.5 percentage points; among white-box baselines, UnlearnDiff (12.1%) leads but remains far below TAA. The gap is consistent across all 14 T2I models and 17 safety modules tested, with TAA maintaining ASR above 84% even against the most robust configurations (e.g., FLUX.1-dev at 84.9%, MHSC image filter at 64.9%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the established line of T2I adversarial attacks that target individual defense layers in isolation (prompt filters via DACA/PGJ/ColJailBreak, concept erasers via UnlearnDiff/PEZ/P4D, image filters via MMA/SneakyPrompt). The key conceptual shift is exploiting the *overlapping vulnerabilities* across all three layers simultaneously: implicit metaphorical prompts that evade keyword-based filters while preserving NSFW generation capability, and subjective stylistic augmentations that reduce objective classifier detectability while retaining human-perceptible harmfulness. Methodologically, it repurposes LLM-based prompt rewriting (as in DACA, PGJ) and genetic optimization (as in QF-Attack, SneakyPrompt) but unifies them under a single dual-objective fitness function that must satisfy both prompt-level and image-level constraints concurrently.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work establishes the first comprehensive benchmark for evaluating holistic robustness of multi-layered T2I defenses, exposing that the 'defense in depth' paradigm is fundamentally flawed when layers have complementary but overlapping blind spots. The practical implication is urgent: all 14 evaluated T2I models (including recent FLUX and SD-3.5) remain vulnerable, and the paper's adaptive defenses (LLM-based prompt processing, adversarial training of image filters) reduce but do not eliminate the attack surface. Open questions include whether cross-modal concept erasers or heterogeneous multi-filter ensembles can close the gap, and whether the attack's reliance on iterative black-box queries and LLM access limits real-world exploitability against rate-limited commercial APIs.
