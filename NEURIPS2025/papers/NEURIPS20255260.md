## Problem

Existing discrete graph diffusion models for molecule generation (e.g., DiGress) require 500–1000 diffusion steps, making them computationally expensive, and they fail to preserve chemically critical motifs such as carbon rings that determine drug-likeness. Moreover, standard evaluation metrics like SMILES validity provide only a coarse measure of chemical utility, leaving a gap between generated structural validity and true biochemical relevance.

## Input / Output

At inference, DMol starts from a random graph sampled from the marginal node and edge class distributions at the fully noisy state t=T, then iteratively denoises over T = k·n steps (typically 38–48 for small molecules with n≈20–24 heavy atoms), where k is a small hyperparameter (1 or 2). The denoising network receives the noisy graph, the time step, structural/spectral features, and two ratio functions R_x(t)=N(t)/n and R_e(t)=M(t)/(0.5n(n−1)) that inform the network how many nodes and edges should change. The output is a molecular graph in which any supernode (compressed motif) is decoded back into its constituent ring structure and reattached to the scaffold via a single bond type, yielding a valid SMILES string.

## Method

DMol introduces three coupled innovations over DiGress. First, at each forward step t it perturbs exactly N(t) randomly chosen nodes and M(t) randomly chosen edges confined to the subgraph induced by those nodes, where N(t) and M(t) follow deterministic cosine schedules tied to the step index; this couples node and edge noise and reduces total steps to T=k·n (an order of magnitude fewer than DiGress). Second, the training loss adds two MSE penalty terms that enforce the predicted clean graph differs from the ground truth by exactly N(t) nodes and M(t) edges, a constraint that is only well-defined because the perturbation counts are deterministic. Third, motif compression converts 3–15 predefined subgraphs (predominantly carbon rings with unique single-bond scaffold attachment) into supernodes that are diffused as single categorical nodes and trivially decoded back, preserving ring frequencies without the complex bond-ranking required by JT-VAE-style decomposition.

## Results

On MOSES, DMol achieves the highest ChEMBL likeness (CL=4.5033) and QED (0.8055) among all compared methods, while requiring only 38 average diffusion steps versus 500 for DiGress/DisCo and 50 for DeFoG, yielding a 1.52 s sample time (vs. 68.24 s for DiGress). On GUACAMOL, DMol leads in CL (4.2231 vs. 4.0412 for DiGress, 3.8075 for DeFoG) and QED (0.5786 vs. 0.5650, 0.4927) with 48 steps. On QM9, DMol attains the best V.U.N. score of 75.1 (vs. 31.8 for DiGress and 62.8 for DeFoG), reflecting superior novelty. Training time is also reduced: 60 h on MOSES and 88 h on GUACAMOL versus 136–162 h for DiGress.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DMol outperforms DiGress (Vignac et al., ICLR 2023) on nearly every metric: +3% SMILES validity on MOSES (87.8 vs. 84.8), +1.8% CL on GUACAMOL (4.2231 vs. 4.0412), and a 13× reduction in diffusion steps (38 vs. 500). Against DisCo (Xu et al., NeurIPS 2024), DMol wins on CL by 0.37 (MOSES) and 0.31 (GUACAMOL) and on QED by 0.05–0.09, but DisCo achieves a slightly lower FCD (59.7 vs. 69.3 on GUACAMOL) and marginally higher shingle distance, reflecting greater structural diversity at the cost of motif fidelity. Versus DeFoG (Qin et al., 2025), DMol leads by ~0.45 in CL and ~0.06 in QED on MOSES, and by ~0.42 in CL and ~0.09 in QED on GUACAMOL, while DeFoG achieves a lower FCD (58.1 vs. 69.3 on GUACAMOL) and higher SD, again trading motif preservation for diversity. On QM9, DMol surpasses GraphNVP (V.U.N. 75.1 vs. –), GDSS (75.1 vs. –), and GRUM (75.1 vs. 22.2) in the combined validity–uniqueness–novelty product, with the largest gap over GRUM. The consistent pattern is that DMol's motif compression yields the best biochemical scores (CL, QED) and the fewest diffusion steps, while flow-matching and continuous-time baselines sacrifice motif structure for broader chemical-space coverage (higher FCD/SD).

## Relation to Prior Work

DMol is a direct algorithmic extension of DiGress (Vignac et al., ICLR 2023), the dominant discrete denoising diffusion model for molecular graph generation. The key departures are: (1) replacing DiGress's uniform all-node/edge perturbation with a deterministic count of randomly selected nodes and edges whose perturbation is coupled through an induced subgraph, (2) adding MSE penalty terms to the loss that exploit the deterministic counts, and (3) introducing motif compression that converts frequent carbon-ring substructures into supernodes diffused alongside atomic nodes. The paper positions itself against the newer flow-matching approach DeFoG (Qin et al., 2025) and continuous-time diffusion DisCo (Xu et al., NeurIPS 2024), arguing that its motif-preserving design yields better biochemical property scores despite far fewer diffusion steps.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~10× reduction in diffusion steps (from 500–1000 to ~40–50) is practically significant for drug discovery pipelines where generation throughput matters, and the motif compression mechanism addresses a real chemical concern: preserving carbon-ring scaffolds that are fundamental to drug-likeness. However, the quality improvements over strong baselines are modest (≈3% SMILES validity over DiGress, ≈4% ChEMBL-likeness/QED over DeFoG), and the paper itself acknowledges that SMILES validity is a coarse proxy for true chemical validity, with tangible evaluation metrics still lacking. Open questions include whether the scaffold-informed conditional generation pipeline (Section 7) can be replaced by true conditional diffusion, how motif compression scales to larger or more diverse chemical spaces, and whether the efficiency gains hold for molecules well beyond the ~40-node regime tested here.
