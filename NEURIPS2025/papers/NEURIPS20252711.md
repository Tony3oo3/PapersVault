## Problem

Score-based diffusion samplers for unnormalised target distributions require either trained neural networks or computationally expensive nested MCMC loops to estimate the score function at each point along the diffusion path. This paper eliminates both requirements by showing that the score can be estimated implicitly through a fast auxiliary process running in parallel with the sampling dynamics, rather than sequentially within an inner loop.

## Input / Output

The method takes only the unnormalised target potential V_π (so that π ∝ exp(−V_π)) and a schedule function λ_t interpolating between a base distribution ν and the target, along with hyperparameters controlling the time-scale separation ε, time reparametrisation κ, friction Γ, and mass M. It produces samples from the target distribution in continuous R^d space. No training data, learned models, or normalising constants are required; the only computational cost is evaluating ∇V_π and ∇log ν at each step.

## Method

The core mechanism is a stochastic slow-fast SDE system where a fast process Y_t (scaled by 1/ε) targets the conditional distribution ρ_{t,x} that defines the score as an expectation, while a slow process X_t evolves along the diffusion path. By stochastic averaging theory, as ε→0 the slow process converges to an averaged dynamics whose drift is exactly the intractable score ∇log μ_t, so the score is estimated implicitly by the fast process running in parallel rather than through nested MCMC. Two algorithms are derived: MULTALMC uses annealed Langevin dynamics for general noise schedules, and MULTCDIFF uses the reverse SDE of an Ornstein-Uhlenbeck noising process with explicit control terms. Both are available in overdamped and underdamped (Hamiltonian) variants, with the underdamped versions employing OBABO splitting for the slow dynamics and the SROCK stabilised integrator for the stiff fast dynamics. A heavy-tailed extension replaces the Gaussian base with a Student's t distribution and uses a modified Itô diffusion for the fast process to ensure efficient mixing under heavy tails.

## Results

Across all benchmarks, both MULTALMC and MULTCDIFF consistently outperform every baseline. On the 8-mode Gaussian mixture (d=2), MULTCDIFF achieves an entropy-regularised W₂ distance of 0.62, roughly 39% lower than the best baseline (RDMC at 1.01). On the 10-dimensional Funnel distribution, MULTCDIFF attains a sliced KS distance of 0.031, marginally beating PT (0.033) and DiGS (0.037). On the Bayesian logistic regression tasks, MULTCDIFF achieves the best predictive log-likelihood on both Ionosphere (−86.33 vs. SLIPS −87.34) and Sonar (−109.60 vs. SLIPS −110.14).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against seven baselines: SMC, AIS, ULMC, Parallel Tempering (PT), DiGS, RDMC, and SLIPS, all evaluated at equal energy-evaluation budgets. MULTALMC and MULTCDIFF dominate on the multimodal Gaussian mixture benchmarks, achieving W₂ distances of 0.62–0.65 on 8-MoG versus 1.01 (RDMC) and 1.22 (DiGS) for the next-best methods—a roughly 35–50% improvement. On the high-dimensional 40-MoG (d=50), both methods (20.13–20.58) narrowly beat PT (21.04) and DiGS (21.87), while RDMC degrades substantially (36.10). On the Funnel distribution, MULTCDIFF (0.031) edges out PT (0.033) and SMC (0.034), with RDMC performing worst (0.080). On the double-well potential, MULTCDIFF achieves the best W₂ across all dimensions (1.95, 4.23, 13.98 vs. PT's 2.39, 4.97, 16.13). On the Bayesian logistic regression tasks (Ionosphere, Sonar), MULTCDIFF attains the highest predictive log-likelihood (−86.33, −109.60), outperforming SLIPS (−87.34, −110.14) and PT (−87.91, −112.99), while ULMC and RDMC degrade significantly (e.g., ULMC reaches −173.61 on Sonar). Overall, the multiscale methods win on every benchmark, with MULTCDIFF slightly ahead of MULTALMC in most settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of diffusion-path-based sampling (Chehab & Korba 2024; Cordero-Encinar et al. 2025) and multiscale stochastic averaging (Liu et al. 2020; Pavliotis & Stuart 2008). Prior learning-free diffusion samplers (DiGS, RDMC, SLIPS) estimate the intermediate score via a nested MCMC loop—a Langevin-within-Langevin structure. The key departure here is replacing that inner loop with a fast-timescale SDE whose stationary distribution is the conditional ρ_{t,x}, so that stochastic averaging recovers the score expectation in the slow process's drift, yielding a Langevin-by-Langevin sampler. The underdamped acceleration draws on Duncan et al. (2017) and Monmarché (2020), while the heavy-tailed extension repurposes the modified Itô diffusion of He et al. (2024a) as the fast process.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Eliminating the inner MCMC loop removes a major computational bottleneck in training-free diffusion-based sampling, and the theoretical convergence guarantee (Theorem 4.1) provides a principled justification absent in prior nested-loop methods. However, the guarantee requires strong convexity of the target potential, which is restrictive for multimodal or heavy-tailed targets where the method is empirically most useful. Open questions include relaxing the convexity assumption, automating the hyperparameter tuning (ε, κ, Γ, M), and extending the controlled-diffusion (MULTCDIFF) formulation to heavy-tailed settings. For practitioners, the underdamped variant's order-of-magnitude speedup over the overdamped version makes this a practically competitive option alongside SMC and parallel tempering for moderate-to-high-dimensional targets.
