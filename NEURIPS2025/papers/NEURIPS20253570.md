## Problem

Multimodal clinical risk prediction models are typically trained on large nationwide cohorts with rich biological data (e.g., genomics) and substantial compute, yet most local clinical sites operate with only EHR data and limited resources. This paper tackles the underexplored problem of transferring a multimodal model from a source biobank to unimodal, distribution-shifted target cohorts without joint access to either cohort's data, enabling equitable deployment of predictive models in resource-constrained settings.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253570/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The source cohort provides longitudinal EHR event sequences (coded diagnoses, medications, and lab tests with timestamps, standardized under OMOP/PCORnet) alongside binary genetic profiles over 217 ADRD-related genes (presence/absence of mutations above a 1% frequency threshold). The target cohorts supply only EHR sequences with no genetic data, and the model is fine-tuned on as few as 500 labeled patients per target site. The output is a binary disease risk probability for Alzheimer's Disease and Related Dementias, evaluated one year before clinical onset, with performance reported via AUROC, F1, and sensitivity/PPV at 95% specificity.

## Method

C³M comprises three components: (1) a gene encoder using mixture-of-aggregations, where M learnable aggregation tokens each attend to gene token embeddings via multi-head attention, modulate them, and pass through a shared 2-layer Transformer to produce expert-specific genetic representations that are averaged; a weighted reconstruction loss (weighted by attention scores) encourages the tokens to focus on informative genes while suppressing noise. (2) A lightweight graph-guided fine-tuning strategy that freezes the MOTOR foundation model's EHR embeddings and instead constructs a bipartite patient–medical-concept graph per cohort, then applies a 2-layer GCN to adapt patient embeddings to local distributional characteristics without updating MOTOR's parameters. (3) A teacher-student distillation pipeline where a gene-aware teacher (concatenating genetic and phenotypical embeddings) is distilled into a gene-free student (EHR-only) via logit-level KL divergence; the student and GNN are then transferred to the target cohort for minimal fine-tuning on a small labeled subset, achieving cross-modal knowledge transfer without requiring genetic data at deployment.

## Results

C³M achieves the best performance across all four cohorts and all four metrics (AUROC, F1, Sens@95, PPV@95). On the source cohort (All of Us), C³M reaches 79.94% AUROC and 36.95% F1, outperforming the strongest baseline (SMIL) by 6.35 AUROC points. On the three target cohorts with EHR-only data, C³M attains 71.82% AUROC (LocalEHR), 72.44% AUROC (INSIGHT-A), and 75.39% AUROC (INSIGHT-B), consistently exceeding the best baseline by 6–9 AUROC points. At the clinically critical 95% specificity threshold, C³M achieves 40.79% sensitivity and 45.93% PPV on All of Us, and 29.68% sensitivity / 41.03% PPV on INSIGHT-B, substantially ahead of all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

C³M outperforms all seven baselines on every metric across all four cohorts. Among the modality-imputation baselines, SMIL [29] is the strongest, reaching 73.59% AUROC on All of Us and 69.01% on INSIGHT-B, yet C³M still leads by 6.35 and 6.38 AUROC points respectively; CMAE [34], MVAE [53], and GAN [7] trail SMIL by 0.5–1.2 AUROC points, with their generative imputation of high-dimensional, sparse genetic features proving unreliable. Among the modality-robust baselines, DrFuse [61] achieves the highest AUROC (71.95% on All of Us) but its disentangled shared representations are degraded by genetic noise, yielding the lowest F1 scores (16.02% on All of Us, 12.08% on INSIGHT-A). MUSE [55] and MoMKE [57] perform worst overall—MUSE's patient-modality graph introduces overly dense noisy connections, while MoMKE's unified encoder design fails to handle the fundamentally different semantic spaces of EHR and genetic data. The consistent 6–9 AUROC-point gap between C³M and the best baseline across all target cohorts underscores the advantage of the proposed mixture-of-aggregations gene encoding, graph-guided fine-tuning, and cross-modal distillation over both imputation and robust-fusion strategies.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of cross-modal learning (handling missing modalities) and cross-cohort transfer learning, but deliberately departs from both. Existing cross-modal methods (CMAE, MVAE, SMIL, MUSE, DrFuse, etc.) assume random or block-wise missingness within a single domain, while cross-cohort adaptation methods typically require joint access to source and target data during training. C³M addresses a stricter setting: consistent modality absence at deployment across all target samples with no target-domain access during training. It builds on the MOTOR EHR foundation model for phenotypical encoding and repurposes knowledge distillation (teacher-student) to transfer gene-informed decision boundaries into a gene-free student, combined with a lightweight bipartite patient-concept GNN for cohort-specific adaptation without updating the foundation model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework directly targets a practical gap: most clinical sites lack genetic data, large patient populations, and GPU resources, yet multimodal models trained on nationwide biobanks (All of Us, UK Biobank) show superior predictive performance. By enabling transfer with only ~500 labeled target patients on CPU-only hardware, C³M makes advanced risk prediction accessible to resource-constrained settings, which is a meaningful step toward equitable healthcare AI. Key limitations include evaluation on a single disease (ADRD) and two modalities (EHR + genetics); generalizability to other chronic diseases, additional biological modalities (imaging, proteomics), and larger-scale deployment remain open. The reliance on a pretrained foundation model (MOTOR) also introduces a dependency that may not hold for all clinical domains.
