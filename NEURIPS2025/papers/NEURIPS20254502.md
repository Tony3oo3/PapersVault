## Problem

Existing T2I-based adversarial patches (e.g., NDDA, MAGIC) maintain attack effectiveness only under fixed or narrow viewing angles, severely limiting their real-world impact. The paper reveals that textual prompts significantly influence angle robustness, yet simple task-specific linguistic instructions (e.g., 'detectable at multiple angles') fail to improve it, and no prior work has formally integrated angular robustness into patch generation for physical deployment.

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard NDDA-style text prompt (e.g., 'a blue square stop sign') with a single learned <angle-robust> CLIP token embedding inserted, and produces a 512×512 adversarial patch image via Stable Diffusion v1.5. The patch is designed to be printed on regular office paper and deployed physically, with attack effectiveness preserved across the full −90° to 90° horizontal viewing range without per-patch or per-environment optimization. The learned concept is a single 768-dimensional embedding vector that can be plugged into any compatible prompt.

## Method

AngleRoCL formulates angle-robust concept learning as a textual inversion problem: a single <angle-robust> token embedding in the CLIP text encoder is optimized via detector-guided adversarial training. During training, a frontal-view image is projectively transformed to simulate 9 viewing angles (−72° to 72°), and a detection loss penalizes angles where YOLOv5 confidence on the target class falls below a 0.8 threshold, encouraging wide-angle effectiveness. Only the concept embedding is updated (50,000 steps, AdamW, lr=1e-4) while all diffusion and encoder parameters remain frozen, making the process environment-free and requiring no user-provided scene images. At inference, the learned token is simply inserted into any existing NDDA or MAGIC prompt, yielding patches with inherent angle robustness in a plug-and-play manner without architectural modifications or additional optimization steps.

## Results

The paper introduces Angle-Aware Attack Success Rate (AASR) as its primary metric, measuring attack effectiveness averaged across viewing angles from -90° to 90° (digital) or -70° to 70° (physical). In the physical environment, MAGIC+AngleRoCL achieves 65.86% average AASR across five detectors, while NDDA+AngleRoCL reaches 51.75%, compared to 22.72% and 28.37% for vanilla MAGIC and NDDA respectively. In digital environments (6 scenes, 5 detectors), NDDA+AngleRoCL averages 36.02% AASR versus 23.79% for NDDA (51.4% relative improvement), and MAGIC+AngleRoCL averages 32.51% versus 26.26% for MAGIC (23.8% improvement). The traditional AdvPatch baseline achieves only 5.54% average AASR digitally and 0.00% physically.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AngleRoCL decisively outperforms all three baselines. Against the traditional AdvPatch [Brown et al., 2017], the gap is absolute: AdvPatch achieves 0.00% AASR in the physical environment and only 5.54% on average digitally, while AngleRoCL-enhanced methods reach 51.75–65.86% physically and 32.51–36.02% digitally. Against NDDA [Sato et al., 2024], adding the learned angle-robust concept improves average AASR from 28.37% to 51.75% in the physical setting (82.4% relative gain) and from 23.79% to 36.02% digitally (51.4% gain), with the largest per-detector jump on YOLOv5 (15.20% → 60.80% physical). Against MAGIC [Xing et al., 2025], the improvement is even more dramatic physically: 22.72% → 65.86% (189.9% relative gain), though digitally the gain is more modest at 26.26% → 32.51% (23.8%). The gains are consistent across all five detector architectures (Faster R-CNN, YOLOv3, YOLOv5, RT-DETR, YOLOv10), confirming cross-detector transferability of the learned concept.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the textual inversion mechanism (Gal et al., ICLR 2023) from personalizing T2I generation to encoding an adversarial property—angle robustness—as a single learned embedding token. It builds directly on the NDDA paradigm (Sato et al., CVPR 2024) of removing robust features from text prompts to generate physically deployable adversarial patches via Stable Diffusion, and is compatible with the MAGIC framework (Xing et al., 2025). The key departure from prior T2I adversarial patch work is the introduction of a detector-guided, angle-aware optimization objective that replaces per-patch gradient-based pixel manipulation, and the empirical finding that naive linguistic instructions (e.g., 'detectable at multiple angles') actively degrade rather than improve angular robustness.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to formally define and operationalize angle robustness for T2I-generated adversarial patches, providing a plug-and-play concept token that can be inserted into any NDD-style prompt without architectural changes. The physical-world validation (82.4% relative AASR improvement over NDDA) demonstrates practical relevance for safety-critical detection systems. However, the scope remains narrow: evaluation is limited to the stop-sign category, a single physical environment in the main paper, and only 9 horizontal-plane training angles, leaving open questions about multi-category generalization, vertical viewing angles, and whether the learned concept transfers to fundamentally different detector architectures beyond the YOLOv5 feedback used during training.
