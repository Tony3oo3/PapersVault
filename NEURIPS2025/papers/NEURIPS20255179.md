## Problem

Directly feeding a blurry image as a conditional input to pre-trained Stable Diffusion hinders accurate structural extraction, while existing workarounds that first run a separate degradation-removal network (e.g., FFTformer) propagate erroneous structures into the diffusion generation. The paper addresses how to leverage SD's strong image priors for robust real-world deblurring without depending on a standalone pre-deblurring model whose errors are irrecoverable downstream.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255179/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single blurry RGB image of arbitrary resolution, encodes it into the 4× downsampled latent space of SD2.1's frozen VAE, and outputs a deblurred RGB image decoded back through the same VAE. Training uses a custom ~500K-pair dataset combining MC-Blur, RSBlur, REDS-style video-derived pairs, and synthetically motion-blurred HD images, while evaluation spans GoPro, DVD, RealBlur, RWBI, and a real blurry image set with no ground truth.

## Method

DeblurDiff co-trains a Latent Kernel Prediction Network (LKPN, a U-Net) with a ControlNet-based conditional diffusion branch initialized from SD2.1. At each denoising step t, the LKPN takes the noisy latent z_t, the blurry latent z_lq, and the timestep to predict a spatially variant (per-pixel) deblurring kernel k_t, which is applied to z_lq via Element-wise Adaptive Convolution (EAC) to produce a clearer guidance latent z_t^s. This z_t^s is concatenated with z_lq and fed into the ControlNet to condition the diffusion UNet, creating a closed 'prior–estimate–restore' loop: intermediate diffusion outputs are fed back into the LKPN at subsequent steps to iteratively refine the kernel. Training minimizes a combined objective of the standard DDPM denoising loss plus an LKPN loss that enforces both latent-space and pixel-space (via the VAE decoder) fidelity of the EAC output to the clean latent.

## Results

On the GoPro dataset, DeblurDiff achieves the best no-reference scores among all diffusion-based methods: MUSIQ 61.64, MANIQA 0.6134, and CLIP-IQA 0.5966, outperforming PASD (61.53, 0.5904, 0.5758) and DiffBIR (56.32, 0.5464, 0.5260). On DVD, it achieves the best CLIP-IQA (0.6686) and MANIQA (0.6480) scores, and the best NIQE (2.7822) among all methods. However, on reference-based metrics (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, FID), HI-Diff consistently outperforms DeblurDiff (e.g., PSNR 33.33 vs. 24.32 on GoPro), as HI-Diff does not use a pre-trained SD model and thus optimizes more directly for pixel-level fidelity. On real-world datasets (RWBI, Real Images) without ground truth, DeblurDiff achieves the best MUSIQ (66.75 on RWBI), MANIQA (0.6260 on RWBI), and CLIP-IQA (0.6849 on RWBI) scores.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DeblurDiff is compared against 7 baselines: FFTformer (Transformer-based), DBGAN (GAN-based), ResShift and HI-Diff (diffusion without SD prior), and ControlNet, PASD, DiffBIR (diffusion with SD prior). On reference-based metrics (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, FID), HI-Diff dominates all methods (e.g., PSNR 33.33 on GoPro vs. 24.32 for DeblurDiff), as it directly optimizes pixel-level reconstruction without generative constraints. Among SD-based methods, DeblurDiff consistently outperforms ControlNet, PASD, and DiffBIR on no-reference perceptual metrics: on GoPro it achieves MUSIQ 61.64 vs. PASD 61.53 and DiffBIR 56.32, and MANIQA 0.6134 vs. PASD 0.5904 and DiffBIR 0.5464. On the real-world RWBI dataset (no GT), DeblurDiff achieves the best MUSIQ (66.75 vs. ControlNet 62.51), MANIQA (0.6260 vs. ControlNet 0.5758), and CLIP-IQA (0.6849 vs. ControlNet 0.6199), demonstrating superior perceptual quality in the absence of ground truth. The paper acknowledges that the gap on reference-based metrics is expected since HI-Diff lacks generative capability, positioning DeblurDiff as superior for realistic detail and texture reconstruction rather than pixel-level fidelity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the line of work applying pre-trained Stable Diffusion to image restoration (DiffBIR, PASD), which use a separate Degradation Removal Model to pre-deblur before feeding into a ControlNet-conditioned diffusion process. It departs from this two-stage paradigm by jointly training a Latent Kernel Prediction Network (LKPN) with the diffusion model, drawing conceptual inspiration from traditional iterative deblurring (alternating kernel estimation and image refinement) and spatially adaptive convolution (STFAN, DWNet). The key architectural departure is that kernel estimation and deblurring are embedded within the diffusion loop itself, with intermediate denoising results feeding back to refine the spatially variant kernels at each step, rather than relying on a fixed pre-deblurring network whose errors propagate into generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical limitation of two-stage diffusion-based restoration: the final output quality is bottlenecked by the degradation removal model, which often introduces erroneous structures that the diffusion process then faithfully reproduces. By integrating kernel estimation into the diffusion loop, DeblurDiff avoids this dependency and achieves competitive or superior no-reference quality metrics across synthetic and real-world benchmarks. However, the method is currently limited to SD2.1 (not yet extended to DiT-based SD3.5), relies on multi-step diffusion for inference speed, and the reference-based metrics (PSNR/SSIM) remain below non-generative methods like HI-Diff, suggesting a trade-off between perceptual realism and pixel-level fidelity that remains an open question for the field.
