## Problem

Current disentangled representation learning methods embed factor-specific inductive biases either in learning objectives (e.g., total correlation for attributes) or in model architectures (e.g., slot attention for objects), making it costly to handle novel factors or scenarios where multiple factor types coexist. This tight coupling forces practitioners to redesign architectures or loss functions whenever the underlying assumptions (statistical independence, spatial exclusivity) are violated or when attributes and objects must be disentangled simultaneously.

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images (64×64 for attribute tasks, 128×128 for object and joint tasks) and produces K disentangled latent vectors per image, where K is fixed per dataset (e.g., K=10 for Shapes3D, K=4–12 for CLEVR variants). The encoder consumes pre-trained VAE features rather than raw pixels, and for object disentanglement replaces slot attention with a lightweight QFormer to avoid built-in spatial exclusivity bias. A key output is the composite image generated by decoding a mixed latent set drawn from two source images, which serves as the training signal for compositionality.

## Method

The core contribution is a modular mixing strategy that encodes factor-specific inductive biases without modifying objectives or architectures. For attributes, each latent index is assigned to exactly one of two source images (mutual exclusivity, reflecting that a face has one nose); for objects, K latents are sampled freely from the union of both images (shared support, reflecting that any subset of objects can co-exist); for joint disentanglement, the K latents are partitioned and each group receives its respective strategy. Training uses two complementary losses on the composite image: a prior loss that backpropagates through a frozen, pre-trained unconditional diffusion model (SDS-style gradient) to maximize data likelihood, and an InfoNCE-style compositional consistency loss that aligns the composite latent with its re-encoded version while penalizing proximity to negative samples. The overall framework is a diffusion-decoder autoencoder where the decoder is shared across all factor types, and switching the mixing strategy alone enables attribute, object, or joint disentanglement.

## Results

On attribute disentanglement, the method achieves the best DCI scores across all three datasets: 0.837 on Shapes3D (vs. 0.723 for DisDiff), 0.365 on Cars3D, and 0.458 on MPI3D (vs. 0.337 for DisDiff), while also attaining the highest FactorVAE score on Shapes3D (0.975) and MPI3D (0.708). On object disentanglement, it matches or exceeds state-of-the-art slot-attention methods (LSD, L2C) on property prediction and segmentation, e.g., 87.04% shape accuracy and 0.032 position error on CLEVR. On the novel joint disentanglement task (CLEVR-Style), it achieves 96.5% style prediction accuracy and 5.05 GRAM loss, while all baselines fail to disentangle style (≤12.2% accuracy).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On attribute disentanglement, the method decisively outperforms all seven baselines on DCI: it beats the strongest prior method DisDiff [52] by 15.7% on Shapes3D (0.837 vs. 0.723) and 35.9% on MPI3D (0.458 vs. 0.337), and surpasses FactorVAE [22] by 128% on Shapes3D DCI (0.837 vs. 0.161). On object disentanglement, it matches LSD [19] and L2C [21] on CLEVR (87.04% shape, 93.93% color, 94.81% material) and slightly outperforms LSD on CLEVR-Tex (70.90% shape vs. 68.25%), while achieving the best position error on CLEVR (0.032 vs. 0.062 for LSD). On the joint disentanglement task (CLEVR-Style), the method is the only one to successfully disentangle both style and objects simultaneously: it reaches 96.5% style accuracy versus ≤12.2% for LSD and L2C, and 0.9% for DisDiff, while maintaining competitive object-level performance (83.56% shape, 90.48% color). The comparison with Wiedemer et al. [50] (additive decoder) in Table 6 further confirms that architectural biases alone cannot achieve attribute or joint disentanglement, with the additive decoder scoring near-chance on Shapes3D (DCI 0.031) and CLEVR-Style style accuracy (23.30%). Overall, the method wins or ties on every primary metric across all four task settings, with the largest margins on attribute DCI and joint style disentanglement.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate subfields of disentangled representation learning—attribute DRL (which embeds biases via information-theoretic objectives like Total Correlation or contrastive losses) and object DRL (which embeds biases via architectural components like slot attention or additive decoders)—by relocating the factor-specific inductive bias into a lightweight, swappable 'mixing strategy' that operates on latent representations. The compositional consistency loss is adopted from Wiedemer et al. [50], the overall compositional objective (mixing latents, decoding composites, maximizing validity) follows Jung et al. [21] (L2C), and the theoretical grounding in factorized support comes from Roth et al. [39]. The key departure from L2C is the use of a frozen unconditional diffusion model for the prior loss (avoiding L2C's OOD conditioning problem) and the explicit derivation of distinct mixing rules from the support structure of attributes (mutually exclusive) versus objects (shared support).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The modular mixing-strategy framework offers a principled recipe for handling new factors of variation: one designs a mixing rule reflecting the factor's compositional structure without redesigning architectures or objectives, which is a meaningful simplification over the current practice of bespoke designs per factor. The joint disentanglement of global style and objects—where slot-attention baselines drop to ~10% style accuracy while this method reaches 96.5%—demonstrates a capability no prior method achieves, though validation is limited to synthetic CLEVR/MSN variants. Key open questions include whether the framework extends to real-world images with unknown or continuous factors, whether theoretical identifiability guarantees can be established, and whether the mixing strategy can be learned rather than hand-designed.
