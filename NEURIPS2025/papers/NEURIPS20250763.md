## Problem

Existing text-to-SVG systems either generate raster images first and vectorize as a post-processing step (inheriting artifacts from both stages) or are confined to short, template-style icon markup under 2k tokens. The field lacks a large-scale, vector-native dataset and benchmark capable of driving progress toward controllable, semantically rich SVG generation at real-world complexity, particularly for long-context sequences.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250763/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a concise natural language caption (short-form Idefics3 description) as input and produces a fully self-contained SVG file as output. The SVG is standardized to a flat sequence of <path> elements using only M/A/C/L commands, a 512×512 viewbox, integer-quantized coordinates, and RGB triplet colors, with all styles inlined and no group hierarchies or <defs>. Generated sequences range from a few hundred to 32,768 tokens, with the dataset mean at approximately 24k characters.

## Method

The paper introduces HeisenVec, a 2.2M-sample dataset where raw SVGs are deterministically standardized (all primitives converted to paths, groups collapsed, transforms fused into coordinates, geometry restricted to M/A/C/L, rescaled to 512×512, coordinates quantized to integers, colors canonicalized to RGB, and fidelity verified via DINOv2 cosine similarity ≥ 0.9), each paired with four captions from BLIP2, Florence 2, and Idefics 3. For generation, the vHector family fine-tunes LLaMA 3.1 8B or 3.2 3B Instruct with 17 custom SVG tokens (path commands, attribute labels, structural delimiters) added to the original 128k-token vocabulary, preserving pretrained numeric grounding for coordinates. Short-context models (8k tokens) use full fine-tuning with curriculum learning from 0–2k to 8k tokens, while long-context variants (16k, 32k) employ LoRA adapters (rank 16, α=32) trained on progressively longer splits with the base model frozen. The tokenization encodes control points as (px <|SEP|> py) to leverage the model's existing numerical knowledge, and the entire pipeline is framed as an autoregressive sequence-to-sequence task where the output vocabulary is a hybrid of natural language subwords and domain-specific vector tokens.

## Results

On the HeisenVec text-to-SVG benchmark, vHector-8B achieves the best performance among all autoregressive/LLM-based methods across most metrics: CLIP-S 67.84, FID 105.22, HPSv2 15.32, and DINO-S 49.33. The FID of 105.22 is dramatically lower than the next-best LLM-based method (OmniSVG at 309.20) and even surpasses diffusion-based baselines like VectorFusion (431.61) and SVGDreamer (848.61). However, diffusion-based methods (VectorFusion, Claude3, ChatGPT-4V) still lead on CLIP-S (77.29, 76.38, 73.66) and HPSv2 (18.44, 17.33, 16.68), indicating a remaining gap in prompt-image alignment for direct SVG generation. The ablation (Table 3) shows progressive degradation as context extends from 8k to 32k tokens, with CLIP-S dropping from 67.84 to 58.13 and FID rising from 105.22 to 1308.79.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among LLM-based competitors, vHector-8B dominates on CLIP-S (67.84 vs. 66.09 for IconShop, 63.82 for OmniSVG, 62.08 for LLM4SVG), FID (105.22 vs. 309.20 for OmniSVG, 830.70 for LLM4SVG), HPSv2 (15.32 vs. 14.46 for OmniSVG), and DINO-S (49.33 vs. 39.23 for IconShop, 37.63 for OmniSVG). The sole exception is SSIM, where LLM4SVG leads (69.62 vs. 65.91), though vHector-8B remains competitive. Against unfine-tuned LLaMA backbones (3B and 8B), vHector-8B improves CLIP-S by ~6 points and reduces FID by over 4×, confirming the benefit of domain-specific fine-tuning and custom SVG tokens. Compared to diffusion-based methods, vHector-8B achieves a substantially lower FID (105.22 vs. 431.61 for VectorFusion, 848.61 for SVGDreamer) and the highest DINO-S (49.33), but trails on CLIP-S and HPSv2, reflecting the inherent difficulty of matching raster-trained models' prompt alignment when generating purely symbolic SVG output.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant LDM-based SVG generation paradigm (DiffVG, VectorFusion, SVGDreamer, SVGFusion), which synthesizes raster images first and vectorizes them as a post-processing step, arguing instead that SVGs should be generated directly as discrete token sequences. It extends the LLM-for-SVG line initiated by IconShop (512 tokens) and LLM4SVG (2048 tokens) by scaling both the dataset (2.2M vs. 250k) and the context window (up to 32k vs. 2k tokens), while introducing a deterministic standardization pipeline that unifies all primitives into M/A/C/L path commands with a compact 17-token SVG vocabulary. The dataset construction draws on StarVector's scale but addresses its narrower iconographic diversity and shorter sequences through broader source collection and a DINOv2-based fidelity filter.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

HeisenVec is the first million-scale, long-context benchmark for text-to-SVG generation, filling a critical gap that prevents rigorous evaluation of whether LLMs can handle real-world vector graphic complexity (mean 24k characters, up to 52k). The quantified performance gap between vHector-8B (CLIP-S 67.84) and the best diffusion method VectorFusion (CLIP-S 77.29), combined with the steep long-context degradation observed in the ablation (FID jumping from 344 to 1309 between 8k and 32k tokens), establishes both the current ceiling and the specific challenges—long-range dependency maintenance, input-to-output expansion—that future work must address. Open questions include whether hierarchical or adaptive generation strategies can close the gap, and whether the data scarcity relative to raster corpora (LAION-5B) fundamentally limits the approach.
