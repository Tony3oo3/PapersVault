## Problem

Current text-to-video and image-to-video models implicitly encode motion as frame sequences without an explicit, disentangled representation of temporal dynamics separate from appearance, identity, and spatial structure. Existing motion control methods rely on low-level spatial constraints (optical flow, trajectories) or domain-specific parametric models that are tightly coupled to source geometry, making them unable to transfer motion across semantically unrelated entities or vastly different viewpoints without object correspondences.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255296/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The motion extractor consumes 8-frame video clips at 6 fps (256×256 resolution) and produces a sequence of fixed-size motion embeddings, one per timestep, that encode only temporal dynamics. For transfer, these embeddings condition either the autoregressive frame generator (producing individual 256×256 frames) or, via LoRA adapters, a frozen video diffusion model (e.g., LTX-Video-2B) that generates full-length videos. The target content is specified by a single source image or a text prompt, with no requirement for object correspondences between driving and target.

## Method

DisMo jointly trains a motion extractor (3D ViT-B/16 with a DINOv2-B frame embedder, 86M params) and a motion-conditioned frame generator (DiT-XL, 675M params) end-to-end using a flow-matching reconstruction objective in image space. The frame generator receives the source frame x_t, the motion embedding m_t, and a temporal offset Δ_t, and reconstructs the future frame x_{t+Δt}; the limited embedding size creates an information bottleneck that, combined with strong photometric and geometric augmentations applied uniformly across frames, forces the embedding to encode only residual temporal dynamics rather than appearance. No contrastive losses or specialized regularization are used—disentanglement emerges purely from the reconstruction pressure. For high-fidelity transfer, conditional LoRA adapters (rank 64) are fine-tuned on a frozen video backbone so that each spatiotemporal token receives conditioning only from its temporally aligned motion embedding, enabling plug-and-play integration with any off-the-shelf video model.

## Results

On the open-world motion transfer benchmark, DisMo achieves the best scores across all four metrics: Motion Fidelity 0.75, Prompt Adherence 0.27, Temporal Consistency 0.95, and Driving Video Similarity 0.55 (lower is better), outperforming all four baselines simultaneously without the fidelity-vs-adherence trade-off seen in prior methods. In zero-shot action classification, DisMo (172M params, 17M video clips) substantially outperforms larger models: 57.29% on ARID (+24.68 over VideoMAEv2), 56.66% on Jester (+12.83 over VideoMAEv2), 22.19% on SSv2, and 90.74% on IARD. In the identity disentanglement experiment on IARD, DisMo's motion embeddings retain only 23.82% identity classification accuracy (near the 20% random baseline) versus 96–99% for V-JEPA and VideoMAE, confirming strong appearance invariance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DisMo outperforms all four motion transfer baselines (VMC, DMT, MotionClone, MotionDirector) on every metric in Table 1, breaking the typical trade-off where DMT and MotionDirector achieve high motion fidelity (0.70) but sacrifice prompt adherence (0.24 and 0.16 respectively), while VMC and MotionClone maintain text adherence at the cost of motion fidelity (0.57 and 0.63). In human evaluation (Table 2), DisMo leads in realism (38.13%) and prompt matching (37.38%) by a wide margin over all competitors, while remaining competitive in motion transfer quality (24.71% vs. MotionDirector's 25.96%). For zero-shot action classification (Table 5), DisMo with only 172M parameters and 17M training clips surpasses VideoMAE (343M, 410M clips), VideoMAEv2 (304M, 1.08B clips), and V-JEPA (200M, 270M clips) on all four benchmarks, with the largest gap on ARID (+24.68 over VideoMAEv2) and Jester (+12.83 over VideoMAEv2). In the identity disentanglement experiment, DisMo's near-random identity accuracy (23.82%) contrasts sharply with V-JEPA (96.23%) and VideoMAE (99.14%), confirming that its representations encode motion dynamics rather than appearance, while simultaneously achieving the best action classification accuracy (90.74% on IARD).

## Relation to Prior Work

DisMo departs from prior motion control methods that rely on explicit low-level signals (optical flow, trajectories, bounding boxes) or domain-specific parametric representations (FOMM, MRAA), which are tightly coupled to source structure and struggle with cross-category transfer. Conceptually, it builds on flow matching as a generative training objective and on LoRA-based conditioning (Ctrloralter) to inject new signals into frozen video backbones, but repurposes both for a fundamentally different goal: learning an abstract, appearance-invariant motion embedding purely through an image-space reconstruction loss. The augmentation strategy draws from self-supervised learning (MAE, BYOL-style photometric/geometric transforms), but the key departure is that no contrastive or explicit disentanglement loss is used—disentanglement emerges from the information bottleneck of the limited embedding size combined with the source-frame conditioning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The renderer-agnostic design (plug-in LoRA conditioning on any off-the-shelf video model) means DisMo's motion representations automatically benefit from future video model improvements without retraining, making it a practical building block for controllable video generation. The finding that a simple reconstruction objective with augmentations naturally disentangles motion from appearance—without any explicit disentanglement loss—challenges the assumption that contrastive or adversarial objectives are necessary for invariant representation learning. Open questions include whether the approach scales to longer temporal horizons, whether more advanced probing (linear/attentive) reveals richer structure in the motion space, and how well it generalizes to 3D-consistent or physically grounded motion beyond 2D video dynamics.
