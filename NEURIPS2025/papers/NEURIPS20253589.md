## Problem

Recommender systems have relied heavily on positive feedback while the abundant negative feedback (dislikes, low ratings) remains underutilized. Existing sign-aware methods face three critical limitations: they design specialized architectures incompatible with state-of-the-art graph contrastive learning, treat negative feedback merely as auxiliary signals without effective cross-channel information exchange, and lack efficient training strategies for large-scale signed graphs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253589/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a signed bipartite user-item interaction graph where edges are binarized by rating threshold (scores ≥ 4 as positive, < 4 as negative), producing two separate adjacency matrices for positive and negative channels. It outputs a scalar preference score per user-item pair computed as a weighted combination of positive and negative channel dot products, which is then used for top-K ranking. The framework is model-agnostic, accepting any GNN backbone (LightGCN, SGL, XSimGCL, LightGCL) as the message propagation function.

## Method

SDCGCL proposes a model-agnostic framework with three components: (1) Dual-Channel Graph Embedding that independently propagates positive and negative graphs through L GNN layers with separate embedding spaces; (2) Cross-Channel Distribution Calibration combining intra-channel InfoNCE contrastive loss with inter-channel Jensen-Shannon divergence alignment to enforce structural consistency while preserving channel-specific patterns; and (3) an Adaptive Prediction Strategy computing scores as (1+k)·E⁺·E⁺ᵀ − k·E⁻·E⁻ᵀ. The DualFuse model implements cross-channel fusion where each channel's normalized embeddings serve as structured perturbations for the other channel's contrastive views, enabling mutual information enrichment. A two-stage optimization strategy first trains on the full signed graph for warm-up, then switches to popularity-guided random walk sampling of the negative subgraph, with theoretical stability bounds (Theorem 3) guaranteeing O(1/t) + O(1/√t) embedding convergence.

## Results

SDCGCL-DualFuse achieves the best Recall@20 and NDCG@20 across all four datasets: 0.3282/0.3693 on ML-1M, 0.1243/0.0959 on Yelp, 0.1342/0.1113 on Amazon, and 0.3900/0.3860 on ML-10M. Relative to the strongest unsigned baseline (XSimGCL), it gains 6.11%–20.26% in Recall@20 and 7.73%–19.63% in NDCG@20. Against the strongest signed baseline (NFARec), improvements reach 28.01% Recall@20 on Yelp and 18.13% on Amazon. All improvements are statistically significant (p<0.01).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SDCGCL-DualFuse outperforms all 22 baselines on every dataset and metric. Against the strongest unsigned methods, it surpasses XSimGCL by 6.11% Recall@20 on ML-1M (0.3282 vs 0.2729) and 28.01% on Yelp (0.1243 vs 0.0867), and beats SGL by 17.2% on ML-1M. Among signed-feedback baselines, it exceeds NFARec (the best S-RS method) by 15.7% Recall@20 on ML-1M (0.3282 vs 0.2840) and 28.0% on Yelp (0.1243 vs 0.0971), and outperforms SiReN by 6.3% on ML-1M (0.3282 vs 0.3093). The model-agnostic SDCGCL framework also improves its host models: SDCGCL-XSimGCL gains 11.9% Recall@20 over vanilla XSimGCL on ML-1M, and SDCGCL-SGL gains 2.9% over SGL. Notably, even the weakest SDCGCL variant (SDCGCL-SGL) already surpasses all signed baselines on Yelp and Amazon, confirming the framework's broad applicability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the LightGCN architecture as its base GNN encoder and extends the graph contrastive learning paradigm (SGL, XSimGCL, LightGCL) to the signed-feedback setting. It departs from prior sign-aware recommendation methods (SiReN, DFGNN, SIGFormer, NFARec) that design specialized models for signed graphs, instead proposing a model-agnostic dual-channel framework that can be plugged into any existing GCL backbone. The cross-channel fusion mechanism, where each channel's embeddings serve as structured perturbations for the other's contrastive views, is a novel departure from prior work that treats negative feedback as a simple penalty term or auxiliary loss.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The model-agnostic design means practitioners can immediately upgrade any existing graph contrastive learning recommender to leverage negative feedback without architectural redesign, which is a practical contribution beyond a single model. The theoretical results (distribution instability of negative feedback, gradient balance at convergence, two-stage stability bounds) provide principled justification for the design choices rather than purely empirical tuning. Open questions include scalability to industrial-scale graphs with millions of negative edges, whether the binarization threshold (score ≥ 4) generalizes across domains, and how the framework handles implicit negative signals (e.g., non-clicks) versus explicit low ratings.
