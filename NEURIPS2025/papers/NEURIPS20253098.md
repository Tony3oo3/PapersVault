## Problem

Diffusion-based sequential recommenders rely on multi-step discrete approximations (typically 1,000 steps) to denoise items, introducing discretization error that creates a fundamental efficiency-effectiveness trade-off: more steps improve recommendation quality but incur prohibitive inference latency, while fewer steps accelerate generation at the cost of amplified trajectory deviation. No existing method achieves both fast inference and high recommendation accuracy simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253098/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a user's historical interaction sequence of length 10 (item embeddings of dimension 256 for Zhihu, 64 for KuaiRec/YooChoose, padded with a token for shorter sequences) and a pure Gaussian noise vector x_T ~ N(0, I). A Transformer encoder extracts a guidance signal g from the sequence, which conditions an MLP denoising model that maps the noise directly to an oracle item embedding x_0 in a single forward pass. Final recommendations are obtained by ranking all corpus items via dot-product similarity with x_0 and returning the top-K (K=20).

## Method

TA-Rec is a two-stage framework. In pretraining, Temporal Consistency Regularization (TCR) adds a loss term enforcing L2 consistency between the denoising model's outputs at adjacent timesteps (x_t and x_{t-1}), smoothing f_θ with Lipschitz continuity so that pure noise can be mapped to oracle items in one step with bounded O(Δt) error (Theorem 1). In fine-tuning, Adaptive Preference Alignment (APA) applies a DPO-style loss with an adaptive coefficient λ_β(t,d) = λ_base·((1−t/T)+(1−d(x⁺,x⁻))) that simultaneously scales down optimization strength for high-noise timesteps and for preference pairs with high cosine similarity, preventing overfitting to ambiguous preferences and random noise (Theorem 2). The Transformer guidance encoder is frozen during fine-tuning, and the denoising model is initialized from the TCR-pretrained checkpoint. This yields 100× faster inference than DreamRec while improving NDCG@20 by up to 31.8% over the strongest baseline.

## Results

TA-Rec achieves the best HR@20 and NDCG@20 across all three datasets: 6.15/4.06 on YooChoose, 5.40/4.42 on KuaiRec, and 2.43/0.91 on Zhihu, improving over the strongest baselines by 7.14%–31.82% (YooChoose), 4.65%–7.54% (KuaiRec), and 7.52%–9.64% (Zhihu). Crucially, TA-Rec achieves these gains with one-step generation, reducing inference time from 23m14s (DreamRec, 1000 steps) to 00m06s on YooChoose — matching the speed of SASRec while outperforming it by a wide margin. Training time remains comparable to other diffusion-based methods (~1m18s per epoch on YooChoose).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TA-Rec outperforms all 12 baselines across every dataset and metric. Among traditional recommenders, CL4SRec is the strongest (e.g., 4.67 HR@20 on YooChoose), but TA-Rec exceeds it by 31.6% in HR@20 and 91.5% in NDCG@20 on YooChoose. Among diffusion-based recommenders, DreamRec (1000-step DDPM) is the closest competitor on KuaiRec (5.16 HR@20 vs. TA-Rec's 5.40), yet TA-Rec achieves this with 1000× fewer inference steps. Among preference-based recommenders, PreferDiff (DDIM, 10–20 steps) is the strongest baseline (5.74 HR@20 on YooChoose, 4.87 on KuaiRec, 2.22 on Zhihu), but TA-Rec still leads by 7.14%, 4.65%, and 7.52% respectively in HR@20, and by a much larger margin in NDCG@20 (31.82% on YooChoose). The largest relative gains appear in NDCG@20, suggesting TA-Rec's one-step generation with TCR and APA particularly improves ranking quality beyond just hit rate.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the DDPM framework (Ho et al., 2020) and the oracle-item generation paradigm for sequential recommendation established by DreamRec (Yang et al., 2023), which uses a Transformer encoder for guidance and an MLP denoiser to reconstruct next-item embeddings. It departs from prior acceleration methods (DDIM, DPM++, consistency distillation) by introducing a temporal consistency loss directly on the stochastic DDPM process, avoiding the need for a well-pretrained teacher or a deterministic ODE trajectory. For preference alignment, it extends Diffusion-DPO (Wallace et al., 2024) by replacing the fixed β with an adaptive coefficient that modulates optimization strength based on both timestep and preference-pair similarity, a design motivated by the observation that ambiguous pairs and high-noise inputs should receive weaker alignment signals.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 100× inference speedup (1,000 steps → 1 step) while simultaneously improving NDCG by up to ~32% addresses the primary barrier to deploying diffusion-based recommenders in production, making them competitive with traditional architectures like SASRec in latency. The theoretical error bound (O(Δt)) for one-step generation provides a principled guarantee that acceleration does not amplify trajectory deviation, which is absent in prior distillation-based approaches. Open questions include whether the Lipschitz continuity assumption holds robustly in practice with finite training, whether the random negative sampling strategy (acknowledged as suboptimal in Appendix D.3) limits the ceiling of APA, and whether the framework extends to other generative recommendation settings beyond next-item embedding generation.
