## Problem

Existing deep image stitching methods rely on deterministic displacement estimation and treat image restoration as a one-shot preprocessing step, both of which fail under adverse conditions (low-light, haze, underwater) where degradation corrupts features and introduces irreducible restoration discrepancies between viewpoints. Additionally, no dedicated benchmark exists to evaluate stitching robustness in degraded scenes, as prior datasets are collected under ideal natural light.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251413/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of RGB images (reference and target) captured from different viewpoints under adverse conditions, with large parallax and far from planar structure. It outputs a single stitched wide field-of-view image along with the estimated homography transformation. The ASIS benchmark provides 2,250 image pairs across 17 scenes in three environments (low-light, haze, underwater), each with a manually verified homography reference for quantitative evaluation.

## Method

ACDIS is a two-stage CNN pipeline. Stage 1 (BCDA) jointly optimizes alignment and restoration through a bidirectional-consistency loop: a Recursive Parameterized Homography Estimation module models per-pixel displacement as a Gaussian distribution (predicting both mean and variance) and refines it over N iterations using Jensen-Shannon divergence-based updates, while a Differentiable Image Restoration module (using physical priors like the atmospheric scattering model, dark channel prior, and Beer-Lambert law) is embedded at each iteration rather than applied once as preprocessing, so that restoration and warping co-refine toward mutual consistency. Stage 2 (MSC) uses a U-Net to generate soft composition masks and introduces a motion loss that adaptively weights the seam to suppress ghosting from objects that moved between the two capture times. Training is supervised with L1 offset loss for homography, L1+SSIM loss for restoration, and boundary/smoothness/motion losses for composition.

## Results

On the proposed ASIS dataset, ACDIS achieves the best PSNR and SSIM across all three adverse environments: 30.51 dB / 0.939 (low-light), 28.61 dB / 0.918 (underwater), and 31.14 dB / 0.963 (haze), outperforming the strongest baselines by 2.3–3.5 dB in PSNR. SIQE (stitching quality) is also highest in all settings (12.85, 6.44, 8.06), while NIQE is best in low-light (3.19) and haze (4.97) but slightly worse than VFIS+HBLALS in underwater (4.20 vs. 3.53), which the authors attribute to large black regions from wide-parallax warping.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ACDIS outperforms all five baselines (APAP, VFIS, LPC, UDIS, UDIS2) on PSNR and SSIM in every environment, with the largest margins in haze (3.5 dB over APAP) and underwater (3.3 dB over UDIS2). On stitching quality (SIQE), ACDIS leads in all three settings, beating the closest competitor (UDIS2+HBLALS) by 0.15 in haze and 0.29 in underwater. The only metric where ACDIS does not win is underwater NIQE, where VFIS+HBLALS achieves 3.53 versus ACDIS's 4.20; the authors explain this is an artifact of large black regions introduced by wide-parallax warping rather than a true quality deficit. Among baselines, UDIS2 is generally the strongest deep-learning competitor, while APAP (a traditional SIFT-based method) is surprisingly competitive on haze PSNR/SSIM but produces visible seam artifacts. LPC consistently underperforms, breaking structural consistency in overlapping regions across all environments.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the iterative homography estimation paradigm from Cao et al. (CVPR 2023) by replacing deterministic displacement outputs with a Gaussian-distributed parameterized cost volume (borrowed from Zeng et al.'s stereo matching work, ICCV 2023) and optimizing it via Jensen-Shannon divergence. Its key architectural departure from prior stitching pipelines is the bidirectional-consistency learning framework, which embeds a differentiable, physics-prior-based restoration module (using atmospheric scattering and dark channel priors) *inside* the coarse-to-fine warping loop rather than as a one-shot preprocessing step. The composition stage builds directly on UDIS2's (Nie et al., ICCV 2023) soft-mask U-Net formulation, adding a motion-aware loss to handle temporally inconsistent pairs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The release of ASIS, the first real-world adverse-condition stitching benchmark with pre-aligned homography references, fills a clear evaluation gap that the authors identify in the literature. The bidirectional-consistency idea—jointly optimizing restoration and alignment iteratively—is a reusable architectural pattern that could transfer to other degraded-input vision tasks. However, the dataset is relatively small (2,250 pairs, 17 scenes, three conditions), the motion constraint only handles small displacements within overlap regions, and the method has not been tested on other degradation types (rain, snow, motion blur), leaving generalization to broader adverse scenarios an open question.
