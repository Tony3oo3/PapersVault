## Problem

Federated continual learning faces both temporal forgetting (clients drift toward new tasks) and spatial forgetting (aggregating heterogeneous models introduces interference), yet existing centralized aggregation degrades under strong data heterogeneity and static decentralized grouping becomes ineffective as tasks change over time. No prior method dynamically reorganizes client coalitions at each aggregation phase to balance new-knowledge acquisition against prior-knowledge retention while producing personalized models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253737/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

Each client receives a sequence of T class-incremental image tasks (grayscale 28×28 for EMNIST/MNIST/SVHN/FashionMNIST, RGB 32×32 for CIFAR100) with non-overlapping class sets across tasks, under a limitless task pool where different clients may encounter entirely unrelated tasks. Rather than producing a single global model, the framework outputs a personalized model per client, updated through weighted aggregation only with dynamically selected coalition partners. Clients exchange model parameters and gradient information (not raw data) with an impartial third party that computes overall similarity and determines the equilibrium partition.

## Method

DCFCL formulates client cooperation as a coalitional affinity game where each client's benefit in a coalition is quantified by an overall similarity metric combining gradient cosine coherence and model parameter cosine similarity (weighted by hyperparameter ε). Knowledge distillation from the previous-round model constrains the classifier's feature space to prevent drift, enabling reliable cooperator identification without extra data exchange. A merge-blocking algorithm efficiently finds the cooperative equilibrium (a partition of non-overlapping coalitions where no blocking coalition can improve all members' benefits) by iteratively merging profitable coalitions and pruning stable ones, reducing complexity from O(B_K²K) to O(K·2^K). A dynamic cooperative evolution algorithm then evolves the equilibrium at each new task phase by reusing the previous partition as initialization and recomputing the benefit table, so coalitions dissolve and re-form as task distributions shift.

## Results

DCFCL achieves the highest average accuracy on 3 of 4 datasets: 52.5% on EMNIST-LTP, 78.3% on EMNIST-shuffle, and 40.4% on CIFAR-100, outperforming the strongest baselines (AF-FCL, AFCL, SCAFFOLD) by 5.0, 1.3, and 3.0 percentage points respectively. On MNIST-SVHN-F, DCFCL reaches 66.7%, slightly trailing AF-FCL (68.1%). In terms of catastrophic forgetting, DCFCL achieves the lowest average forgetting rate on CIFAR-100 (4.7%), EMNIST-shuffle (4.2%), and MNIST-SVHN-F (6.9%), confirming its effectiveness in retaining prior knowledge while learning new tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DCFCL outperforms all 13 baselines on EMNIST-LTP (52.5% vs. best baseline AF-FCL at 47.5%), EMNIST-shuffle (78.3% vs. AFCL at 77.0%), and CIFAR-100 (40.4% vs. SCAFFOLD at 37.4%), with the largest margin (5.0 pp) appearing under the most heterogeneous LTP setting. On MNIST-SVHN-F, AF-FCL narrowly leads DCFCL (68.1% vs. 66.7%), while AFCL (62.4%) and GLFC (61.8%) trail further behind. Centralized FL baselines (FedAvg, FedProx, SCAFFOLD) and CL-augmented variants (PODNet, ACGAN) consistently underperform, confirming that indiscriminate global aggregation introduces harmful knowledge interference under heterogeneity. Decentralized and personalized methods (CFL, Per-FedAvg) improve over centralized baselines but still fall short of DCFCL's dynamic coalition approach, particularly as data heterogeneity increases. In forgetting metrics, DCFCL achieves the lowest average forgetting on 3 of 4 datasets (4.7% CIFAR-100, 4.2% EMNIST-shuffle, 6.9% MNIST-SVHN-F), with AF-FCL being the closest competitor (4.9%, 4.2%, 7.5% respectively).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of federated continual learning (FCL), decentralized federated learning, and cooperative game theory. It departs from the dominant centralized-aggregation paradigm in FCL (FedCIL, AF-FCL, GLFC) by adopting a server-free architecture, and from static clustering approaches in decentralized FL (CFL, FedGroup) by introducing *dynamic* coalition formation that re-equilibrates at every task phase. The coalitional affinity game framework and the merge-blocking algorithm draw on hedonic game theory (Brânzei & Larson, 2009) and coalition formation dynamics (Konishi & Ray, 2003), but are the first to apply them to the continual-learning setting where task shifts invalidate previously stable groupings. The 'limitless task pool' (LTP) formulation, where clients may encounter entirely unrelated tasks, extends the conventional FCL setup where task sequences are at least partially shared across clients.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap: centralized FCL degrades sharply under strong client heterogeneity, while existing decentralized methods use fixed groupings that become suboptimal as tasks evolve. The dynamic-coalition equilibrium provides a principled, game-theoretic guarantee that no client can unilaterally improve by switching groups, and the merge-blocking algorithm makes this computationally tractable (O(K·2^K) vs. exponential state traversal). Key limitations include sensitivity to hyperparameters λ and ε, the NP-hard nature of the underlying coalition problem at scale, and the requirement of a trusted third party to coordinate equilibrium computation. Open questions include adaptive hyperparameter selection, scalability beyond ~50 clients without approximation, and whether the equilibrium concept holds under asynchronous or partially-participating client settings.
