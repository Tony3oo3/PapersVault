## Problem

Existing neural decoders for brain-computer interfaces face a fundamental trade-off: simple causal models (MLPs, GRUs) perform well on single sessions but cannot scale or generalize across subjects, while powerful non-causal transformer-based models (e.g., POYO) generalize at scale but consume orders of magnitude more energy and cannot operate in real time. No prior framework simultaneously achieves causality, energy efficiency, multi-session/multi-subject scalability, and strong decoding performance, which is critical for battery-constrained implantable BCI devices.

## Input / Output

The method consumes binned spike trains (0.01 s sliding windows over 1 s segments) from microelectrode arrays, where each recorded unit is tokenized via a learnable 32-d embedding, producing variable-length sequences that differ across sessions and subjects. The output is a continuous 2-d cursor velocity vector (v_x, v_y) obtained through a membrane-potential observer layer that integrates spiking activity without resetting, enabling continuous-valued predictions from an otherwise discrete spiking network. Evaluation is restricted to reaching segments of Center-Out and Random Target motor tasks at 100 Hz behavioral sampling.

## Method

Spikachu first harmonizes heterogeneous unit embeddings across sessions and subjects using a Perceiver cross-attention encoder that projects variable-length unit sequences into a fixed set of 128 learnable latent tokens, which are then linearly compressed into 128 'virtual units' shared across all subjects. These virtual units are processed by p=3 parallel spiking MLPs with independently learnable LIF decay constants (initialized at τ = 1.11, 1.46, 434.79), extracting features at distinct temporal scales; the resulting multi-scale latents are then integrated via spiking self-attention (8 heads, 512-d projections) to capture cross-timescale dependencies. A second set of p'=2 parallel spiking MLPs further refines the representation, and a membrane-potential observer layer (neurons that never fire) converts the final spiking state into a continuous velocity prediction. Training uses surrogate gradients (arctangent) for backpropagation through spiking neurons, MSE loss with 5× weighting on reaching segments, and unit-dropout augmentation; a multi-session pretrained model (Spikachu-mp, 99 sessions, 3 NHPs) enables few-shot transfer to new sessions, subjects, and tasks while consuming 2.26×–418.81× less energy per inference than ANN baselines.

## Results

On single-session decoding (99 sessions, 3 NHPs), Spikachu achieves R²=0.84 (CO) and R²=0.68 (RT), outperforming all causal baselines while consuming only 5.14 μJ per inference — 2.26× less energy than GRU (11.65 μJ) and 418.81× less than POYO (2151.65 μJ). The multi-session pretrained model (Spikachu-mp) finetuned on individual sessions improves to R²=0.88 (CO) and R²=0.69 (RT), and transfers to a new subject (monkey T) with R²=0.78/0.68 while converging 3–4× faster than scratch-trained models. On the held-out Neural Latents Benchmark tasks (MC-RTT, MC-Maze), transferred Spikachu-mp matches scratch-trained performance (R²=0.56/0.78) with 2.3–2.7× faster convergence and ~3% lower energy per inference.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Spikachu outperforms all causal baselines on both R² and energy: it beats GRU (the strongest causal baseline) by +0.006 R² on CO and +0.008 on RT while using 2.26× less energy (5.14 vs 11.65 μJ). Against POYO-causal, Spikachu gains +0.044 R² on CO and +0.113 on RT at 418× lower energy. The non-causal POYO remains the best performer on raw R² (0.8937 CO, 0.6785 RT), but Spikachu narrows the gap to just 0.054 on CO and 0.002 on RT while consuming 418.81× less energy per inference. MLP and LSTM are clearly dominated on both accuracy and efficiency. In the multi-session transfer setting, Spikachu-mp finetuned on single sessions surpasses all scratch-trained baselines (R²=0.88 CO vs 0.84 for best scratch model), and when transferred to the entirely new MC-RTT/MC-Maze tasks, it matches scratch-trained performance while converging 2.3–2.7× faster.

## Relation to Prior Work

Spikachu sits at the intersection of two previously separate research threads: multi-session, multi-subject neural decoding (POYO, CEBRA, LFADS) and energy-efficient spiking computation. Prior SNN decoding work was confined to shallow (≤4-layer) single-session models focused on neuromorphic deployment feasibility, while multi-session frameworks like POYO achieved strong generalization but were non-causal and computationally expensive. This paper bridges the gap by combining a Perceiver-based harmonization module (for cross-session/subject alignment) with spiking feed-forward networks, spiking self-attention (from Spikformer), and a membrane-potential observer layer (from Liao et al.), producing the first SNN architecture that is simultaneously causal, scalable across heterogeneous datasets, and energy-efficient.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is clear for implantable BCI systems where battery life and real-time operation are hard constraints: Spikachu matches or exceeds causal baselines while consuming 2.26×–418× less energy, and multi-session pretraining further improves both accuracy and energy efficiency through induced sparsity. A key limitation is the residual ANN-based Perceiver harmonization module, which accounts for a disproportionate share of energy despite being <15% of parameters; replacing it with a fully spiking alternative is identified as the natural next step. Real-world validation on neuromorphic hardware (Loihi 2, Darwin 3) remains open, and the compact model size (<4M synapses, ~10K neurons) is well within current chip capacity, making this a credible path toward clinical translation.
