## Problem

Existing language-guided 3D affordance detection methods perform single-pass inference with fixed affordance taxonomies, lacking the ability to iteratively refine predictions or exploit intrinsic geometric structure of predicted masks. This limits generalization to unseen objects and complex multi-step instructions, as the model cannot self-correct coarse boundaries or adapt to novel object-affordance combinations without dense supervision.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252204/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D point cloud (N×3 coordinates) and a free-form natural language instruction as input, and produces a binary per-point affordance mask (N values in {0,1}) indicating interactable regions. The point cloud is consumed twice: once through a point encoder (Point-BERT/ULIP2) that produces m keypoint tokens projected into LLM token space, and once through a dense point backbone (Point Transformer) yielding N×c′ per-point features. The mask is refined over T=3 iterative steps, with each step conditioned on differential geometric descriptors extracted from the previous mask prediction.

## Method

ViSPLA builds on the 3D-AffordanceLLM backbone (Phi-3.5-mini with LoRA, Point-BERT encoder, Point Transformer backbone, LISA-style affordance decoder) and introduces a closed-loop iterative refinement mechanism M_t = f_θ(P, G(M_{t-1}), L). The core novelty is the Differential Geometric Self-Prompting (IDGSP) module, which computes Laplacian, normal derivatives, mean curvature, and principal curvatures from the previous mask and encodes them via a learnable geometric feature extractor into dense per-point visual prompts injected through cross-attention into the LLM's affordance token. Two complementary refinement modules operate in parallel: an Implicit Neural Affordance Field (INAFS) that minimizes an energy functional combining gradient smoothness, boundary fidelity, and Gaussian curvature alignment to produce a continuous probabilistic surface, and a Spectral Convolutional Self-Prompting (SCSP) module that projects the mask into the eigenbasis of the normalized graph Laplacian and applies a learnable spectral filter across K=3 frequency bands for multi-scale refinement. Training uses a multi-task loss combining autoregressive CE, BCE+Dice for the initial mask, Sobolev W^{2,2} consistency across iterations with biharmonic Tikhonov regularization, the INAFS energy, and spectral-band Frobenius consistency with total variation.

## Results

On the PIAD benchmark, ViSPLA achieves 23.1% aIoU, 85.8% AUC, and 0.664 SIM in the seen setting, and 9.2% aIoU, 73.1% AUC, and 0.431 SIM in the unseen setting, setting new state-of-the-art results. On LASO, the method reaches 22.8% aIoU and 87.3% AUC (seen) and 17.1% aIoU and 81.5% AUC (unseen). The iterative refinement with T=3 steps and K=3 spectral frequency bands yields the best trade-off between accuracy and computational cost, with performance plateauing beyond T=3.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ViSPLA outperforms the strongest prior method GEAL [5] by +0.6% aIoU and +0.8% AUC on PIAD seen, and by +0.5% aIoU and +0.6% AUC on PIAD unseen. On LASO, the gains over GEAL are +0.8% aIoU (seen) and +0.4% aIoU (unseen), with SIM improvements of +0.017 and +0.004 respectively. The closest LLM-based competitor, 3D-AffordanceLLM [6] (reproduced under the same protocol), trails ViSPLA by 1.6% aIoU on PIAD seen and 1.9% aIoU on LASO seen, confirming that iterative geometric self-prompting adds substantial value over single-pass LLM decoding. Traditional fusion-based baselines (MBDF, PMF, FRCNN, ILN, PFusion, XMF) lag by more than 50% relative aIoU, as they lack language conditioning and geometric refinement. The largest relative gains appear in unseen settings, where ViSPLA's differential geometry feedback (IDGSP) contributes +2.5 aIoU on LASO seen and consistent improvements across all unseen splits, demonstrating superior zero-shot generalization to novel affordance-object pairs.

## Relation to Prior Work

ViSPLA builds directly on the 3D-AffordanceLLM backbone (point encoder, LLM with LoRA, and affordance decoder) and adapts the LISA segmentation decoder for 3D, but departs fundamentally from the single-pass inference paradigm by introducing a closed-loop iterative refinement mechanism. The key conceptual departure is using differential geometric descriptors (Laplacians, curvatures, normal derivatives) extracted from prior mask predictions as visual self-prompts, rather than relying solely on language embeddings for localization. The Spectral Convolutional Self-Prompting and Implicit Neural Affordance Field modules further extend the refinement pipeline into frequency-domain and continuous-field representations, respectively, which have no direct precedent in prior affordance detection work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that geometric self-feedback can meaningfully improve 3D affordance segmentation beyond what language conditioning alone achieves, particularly in zero-shot and cross-dataset generalization settings where memorization of seen affordance-object pairs fails. However, the iterative refinement adds computational overhead (T=3 steps, ~12hr training on 4×V100), and the authors acknowledge challenges with deformable or articulated objects. Open questions include whether adaptive iteration control can reduce cost, whether the geometric prompting generalizes to dynamic multi-object scenes, and whether the approach extends to scene-level affordance reasoning beyond single objects.
