## Problem

Existing model-explainability methods for visual classifiers are either local (GradCAM, Integrated Gradients) and data-dependent, or produce abstract visual outputs (activation maximization) that are difficult to interpret semantically. There is no framework that generates global, human-readable textual explanations of a classifier's decision patterns and spurious correlations without access to training data or ground-truth labels.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254039/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

DEXTER takes only a frozen visual classifier (CNN or ViT) and a target class label as input, requiring no training data, ground-truth labels, or dataset access. It produces three types of output: (1) synthetic RGB images (generated via Stable Diffusion with a 4-step LCM LoRA adapter) that maximize the activation of selected target neurons, (2) structured natural-language bias reports generated by a VLM reasoning over captions of those images, and (3) a small set of class-descriptive words (top-k, typically k=4) used for CLIP-based slice discovery. The entire pipeline operates offline, with prompt optimization taking ~10 minutes per class on three H100 GPUs.

## Method

DEXTER optimizes a single learnable soft prompt (P=1, d=768) that conditions a frozen BERT model to fill masked tokens in a template sentence (e.g., 'a picture of a [MASK] with [MASK] and [MASK]...'). BERT's logits are discretized via Gumbel-Softmax and translated to CLIP's vocabulary through a binary mapping matrix, yielding a fully differentiable text prompt that conditions a frozen Stable Diffusion model. The generated image is passed to the frozen target classifier, and a neuron activation maximization loss (−n_i for feature neurons, −log n_i for class neurons) is backpropagated through the entire diffusion–classifier chain to update only the soft prompt. An auxiliary masked pseudo-label loss (L_mask) provides a shorter gradient path and binds specific mask positions to subsets of target neurons, with a history-mean mechanism to prevent outlier token updates. Finally, correctly classified generated images are captioned by a VLM (ChatGPT-4o mini), and a second VLM instance reasons over the caption list to produce a coherent textual bias report identifying spurious correlations or confirming their absence.

## Results

On the slice discovery and debiasing task, DEXTER achieves 91.3% worst-slice accuracy on CelebA and 90.5% on Waterbirds, matching or exceeding data-dependent SOTA methods (DRO-B2T: 90.4% CelebA worst-slice; LADDER: 92.4% Waterbirds worst-slice) while requiring no training data or ground-truth labels. For visual explanations on SalientImageNet, DEXTER attains 0.94±0.03 CLIP-IQA and 0.96±0.03 Semantic CLIP-IQA, surpassing DiffExplainer (0.89/0.90) and all GAN-based baselines. Bias explanation reports on FairFaces achieve a mean STS of 0.90 against training-derived reports, with human MOS of 4.01/5 and LLM MOS of 4.48/5, confirming faithful, data-free bias identification.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DEXTER outperforms all data-dependent slice-discovery baselines on CelebA worst-slice accuracy (91.3% vs. DRO-B2T 90.4%, DRO 90.0%, CNC 88.8%), while being the only method that uses neither training data nor ground-truth slice labels. On Waterbirds, DEXTER (90.5% worst-slice) is slightly below LADDER (92.4%) and DRO-B2T (90.7%) but exceeds DRO (89.9%) and CNC (88.5%), remaining competitive without any dataset access. For visual explanations, DEXTER surpasses DiffExplainer by ~0.05 on both CLIP-IQA and Semantic CLIP-IQA, and substantially outperforms GAN-based methods (Nguyen et al.: 0.74/0.57; Yosinski et al.: 0.37/0.33). In the ablation on activation maximization, DEXTER's optimized multi-word prompts with auxiliary loss achieve 75.43 mean activation score versus 64.73 for the class-label baseline, 59.87 for ChatGPT-generated descriptions, and 39.83 for DiffExplainer's prompts. For bias explanation on FairFaces, DEXTER's data-free reports achieve 0.90 STS similarity to training-derived reports and human/LLM MOS scores of 4.01/4.48, with no direct textual-explanation baseline available for comparison.

## Relation to Prior Work

DEXTER directly extends DiffExplainer (Pennisi et al., CVIU 2025), which first used diffusion models with soft prompts for cross-modal global explanations, by replacing continuous soft prompts with discrete, interpretable tokens generated through BERT masked language modeling and Gumbel-Softmax. This shift from soft to hard tokens is the key architectural departure, enabling the generated prompts to serve as both visual conditioning signals and natural-language explanations. The paper also revives the activation maximization paradigm (Nguyen et al., Olah et al.)—largely supplanted by local attribution methods like GradCAM—by grounding it in modern diffusion priors, and it connects to the slice-discovery and bias-explanation literature (B2T, LADDER, GEORGE) while operating in a fully data-free regime that those methods do not support.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The fully data-free operation is the paper's most practically significant property: a practitioner can audit a deployed classifier for biases and spurious correlations without access to training data or labels, which is often the real-world constraint. The combination of visual activation maximization with LLM-generated textual reports offers a more complete interpretability picture than either modality alone, and the hallucination analysis (27/30 classes grounded) provides reasonable confidence in report fidelity. However, the ~10-minute-per-class optimization cost limits real-time use, the reliance on Stable Diffusion introduces NSFW risk and potential generation artifacts, and the approach has not yet been validated on multimodal or video models—areas the authors flag as future work. Whether the data-free advantage persists at larger scale (e.g., 1000+ classes with diverse architectures) remains an open question.
