## Problem

Existing tone mapping methods rely on downsampled inputs and handcrafted multi-scale pyramids (Laplacian, Difference-of-Gaussian) that apply fixed filters regardless of scene content, leading to high-frequency detail loss and halo artifacts in complex HDR scenes. Additionally, most approaches treat global tone consistency and local contrast enhancement as independent problems, producing outputs that are either globally flat or locally inconsistent. The paper addresses the need for a unified, content-adaptive framework that jointly models frequency-aware detail extraction and global-local tone mapping.

## Input / Output

The method takes a full-resolution HDR image (H×W×3) and produces a perceptually faithful LDR tone-mapped image at the same resolution. Internally, the pipeline operates at multiple scales: the Learnable Differential Pyramid extracts high-frequency features from full resolution down to H/4×W/4, while the Global Tone Perception module processes a further downsampled version at H/8×W/8 for computational efficiency. The method is evaluated at both 480p and original 4K resolutions, and extends to HDR video tone mapping.

## Method

DPRNet is a 212K-parameter CNN with four synergistic modules. The Learnable Differential Pyramid (LDP) generalizes classical DoG pyramids by replacing fixed Gaussian filters with learned convolutions whose outputs are differenced to produce content-adaptive high-frequency features at three progressively coarser scales. The Global Tone Perception (GTP) module extracts a scene-level condition vector via global average pooling and uses it to generate per-layer modulation parameters (γ, β) that adaptively control a residual convolutional core for global luminance adjustment. The Local Tone Tuning (LTT) module divides the image into N×N patches, encodes each patch into a 6-d descriptor, and predicts weights over a bank of basis 3D LUTs to perform region-specific contrast enhancement via differentiable trilinear interpolation. Finally, the Iterative Detail Enhancement (IDE) module progressively integrates the extracted high-frequency features into the tone-mapped image in a coarse-to-fine manner, where each scale applies a residual correction network conditioned on the upsampled output concatenated with the corresponding high-frequency map. Training uses a composite loss combining L1 reconstruction, MS-SSIM, high-frequency L1 (against Laplacian-pyramid GT), and VGG19 perceptual terms.

## Results

On the 4K HDR+ dataset, DPRNet achieves 27.71 dB PSNR, 0.920 SSIM, 0.066 LPIPS, and 5.31 ΔE, improving PSNR by 2.39 dB over the previous best (LLFLUT, 25.32 dB). On the newly introduced 4K HDRI Haven dataset, DPRNet reaches 28.30 dB PSNR and 0.946 SSIM, a 3.01 dB PSNR gain over RECNet (25.29 dB). For real-world generalization without ground truth, DPRNet attains the highest TMQI scores: 0.9245 on HDR Survey and 0.9540 on UVTM, outperforming ZS-Diffusion (0.8915) and CoTF (0.9006) respectively. The model uses only 212K parameters, substantially fewer than CODEFormer (12.2M) or sLUT (4.5M).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DPRNet outperforms all 15 baselines across every reported metric on both HDR+ and HDRI Haven at both 480p and 4K resolutions. The largest gains are over the most recent LUT-based and pyramid-based methods: LLFLUT (NeurIPS 2023) is surpassed by 2.39 dB PSNR at 4K on HDR+ and by a wide margin on HDRI Haven, while RECNet (AAAI 2024) trails by 3.01 dB PSNR on 4K HDRI Haven. Among 3D LUT methods, CLUT and sLUT achieve competitive LPIPS at 480p but fall 1.8–2.4 dB behind in PSNR at 4K, confirming that fixed LUT representations struggle with high-resolution detail. CODEFormer achieves the best 480p LPIPS (0.028) but cannot run at 4K due to memory constraints (12.2M params vs. DPRNet's 212K). On the no-reference generalization benchmarks, DPRNet leads ZS-Diffusion by 0.033 TMQI on HDR Survey and CoTF by 0.053 on UVTM, demonstrating that the learnable differential pyramid and global-local collaborative tone mapping generalize better to unseen real-world scenes than diffusion-based or exposure-correction approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

DPRNet sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: 3D LUT-based image enhancement (Zeng et al. 2020, CLUT, SepLUT) and multi-scale pyramid decomposition (Laplacian pyramid, Difference-of-Gaussian). The paper departs from prior LUT methods by replacing their fixed global LUT with a patch-wise, context-conditioned LTT module, and from prior pyramid-based tone mappers (LPTN, LLFLUT) by making the differential pyramid fully learnable rather than relying on handcrafted Gaussian/Laplacian filters. The iterative coarse-to-fine detail enhancement and the explicit global-local collaborative tone mapping (GTP + LTT) represent a unification that prior methods treated as separate sub-problems.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The introduction of the HDRI Haven dataset is a practical contribution: ablations show it yields substantially better real-world generalization (TMQI 0.9245 vs. 0.8157 on HDR Survey) than the widely-used HDR+ dataset, suggesting the field may need richer training data for robust tone mapping. The method's lightweight footprint (212K parameters) achieving 2–3 dB PSNR gains at 4K resolution makes it relevant for mobile and real-time deployment. Open questions include handling noisy inputs (the authors acknowledge failure on FiveK), extending to video with temporal consistency, and whether the learnable differential pyramid generalizes beyond tone mapping to other multi-scale reconstruction tasks.
