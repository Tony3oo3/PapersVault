## Problem

Prior theoretical analyses of weak-to-strong generalization are limited to linear models, random feature networks, or abstract frameworks that do not guarantee the phenomenon arises through practical gradient-based training. This paper asks: when and how does weak-to-strong generalization emerge through nonlinear feature learning, specifically when a two-layer ReLU CNN is trained under supervision from a pretrained linear CNN?

## Input / Output

The input is structured patch data in R^{d×3}, where each sample contains three patches composed of label-dependent signals of two difficulty levels (easy signals μ and hard signals ν, which can appear with opposite signs) plus label-independent Gaussian noise. The strong model is trained not on true labels but on pseudo-labels produced by a pretrained weak model, introducing a non-negligible fraction of flipped labels on hard-only data. The output is a binary class prediction (±1), and the analysis tracks both training loss on pseudo-labels and true test error to characterize when the strong model outperforms its weak teacher.

## Method

The weak model is a linear CNN (single weight vector applied to all three patches) and the strong model is a two-layer ReLU CNN with m filters per class performing patch-wise convolution. Training proceeds in two stages: the weak model is trained via gradient descent on true labels, then the strong model is trained via gradient descent on pseudo-labels from the weak model. Using signal-noise decomposition, the paper identifies two distinct regimes. In the data-scarce regime, the strong model achieves near-optimal generalization via benign overfitting when n_st exceeds a threshold (learning hard signals from correctly-labeled both-signal data), but suffers harmful overfitting below it. In the data-abundant regime, generalization emerges in an early phase where the strong model perfectly fits clean-labeled data while ignoring flipped labels, but overtraining until zero pseudo-label loss causes the model to 'forget' learned signals as flipped-label gradients dominate, degrading test accuracy back to weak-model levels.

## Results

In the theoretical setting (d=2000, p_e=0.4, p_h=p_b=0.3), the weak model achieves 85.1% test accuracy, and the strong model trained on pseudo-labels exceeds this at n_st=2000 (benign overfitting) while failing to surpass it at n_st=75 (harmful overfitting). On modified MNIST, the weak-to-strong model outperforms the weak model at n_st=1000 (88.34% vs 85.93%) and n_st=1500 (88.46% vs 86.42%), but the gain diminishes and reverses at n_st=2500 (86.55% vs 85.86%), consistent with the predicted transition from benign overfitting to the data-abundant regime where overtraining degrades performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is primarily a theoretical paper, so there are no external method baselines to compare against. The central empirical comparison is between the weak model (linear CNN / single-hidden-layer MLP) and the strong model (two-layer ReLU CNN / three-layer CNN) trained on the weak model's pseudo-labels. On modified MNIST, the weak-to-strong model beats the weak model by approximately 2.4 percentage points at n_st=1000 and 2.0 points at n_st=1500, but the advantage shrinks to 0.8 points at n_st=2000 and effectively vanishes at n_st=2500, illustrating the predicted non-monotonic relationship between data size and weak-to-strong gain. In the synthetic theoretical setting, the strong model at n_st=20000 achieves near-perfect test accuracy while its training accuracy on pseudo-labels remains comparable to the weak model's, directly validating Theorem 3.6's prediction of early-phase generalization via label correction.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the weak-to-strong generalization literature (Burns et al., 2024) from abstract frameworks and linear/random-feature models to a provable nonlinear feature learning setting. It builds on the patch-wise structured data framework from Allen-Zhu and Li (2020) and the signal-noise decomposition technique from Cao et al. (2022) to analyze gradient descent dynamics of a two-layer ReLU CNN trained on pseudo-labels from a linear CNN. Prior theoretical work (Lang et al., 2024; Charikar et al., 2024; Wu and Sahai, 2025; Medvedev et al., 2025) either relies on abstract assumptions not tied to practical training procedures or restricts the trainable component to linear models; this paper is the first to provide convergence and generalization guarantees for a genuinely nonlinear student trained via gradient descent on pseudo-labeled data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of two distinct regimes (data-scarce vs. data-abundant) with fundamentally different mechanisms—benign/harmful overfitting versus early-stopping-based label correction—provides a structured theoretical account of when and how weak-to-strong generalization emerges or fails. The empirical observation that overtraining in the data-abundant regime degrades performance to weak-model levels, despite the existence of good solutions, is practically relevant for practitioners tuning training duration in distillation pipelines. Limitations include the highly simplified patch-wise data distribution and specific CNN architectures; extending the analysis to more realistic data, deeper networks, and practical techniques like auxiliary confidence loss (Burns et al., 2024) are important open directions.
