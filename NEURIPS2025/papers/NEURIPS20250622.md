## Problem

No regret lower bounds had been established for restless rising or rising concave bandits, leaving the stationary Ω(T^{1/2}) bound as the only reference and leaving open whether these structured monotonicity settings are provably harder than stationary MABs. Additionally, no algorithm had been shown to achieve a regret upper bound strictly smaller than the O(T^{2/3}) rate for general restless bandits in the rising concave setting, despite the additional concavity structure.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250622/cropped_page5_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The algorithm RC-BE(α) takes as input the number of arms K and a tunable parameter α ≥ 1, and at each round selects an arm from the alive set based on cumulative reward comparisons against a window-dependent budget B_w^(α). It is an anytime algorithm requiring no knowledge of the learning horizon T or the variation budget V_T. The lower bound constructions take as input a sequence of window widths and base/modified trend functions, producing hard instance classes whose cumulative increment is bounded by V_T, with rewards drawn from a two-point σ-subgaussian distribution ψ(μ,σ) supported on {−σ/2, 3σ/2}.

## Method

The paper contributes two complementary results. For lower bounds, it develops a general information-theoretic recipe (Lemma 3.1) that constructs hard restless instances by splitting rounds into windows where at most one arm follows a 'modified trend' while others follow a 'base trend,' then bounds regret via KL divergences between the induced reward distributions. Specializing to rising bandits yields Ω(T^{2/3}) (matching general restless), and to rising concave bandits yields Ω(T^{3/5}) (exceeding the stationary bound). For the upper bound, RC-BE(α) extends Budgeted Exploration by using increasing window widths Δ_w^(α) = ⌈w^α⌉ and corresponding budgets, exploiting the fact that concavity causes the maximum per-round increment to decrease over time, so the algorithm can restart less frequently. The regret analysis decomposes each window's regret into an exploration term (proportional to the budget) and a commitment term (proportional to window width times the diameter of arms that cross), then bounds the number of 'bad' windows where crossing arms diverge significantly, yielding Õ(T^{7/11}) for α = 8/3.

## Results

The paper establishes a regret lower bound of Ω(T^{2/3}) for restless rising bandits (Theorem 3.2), matching the known upper bound for general restless bandits, and a lower bound of Ω(T^{3/5}) for restless rising concave bandits (Theorem 3.3), proving this setting is strictly harder than stationary MABs (Ω(T^{1/2})). The proposed algorithm RC-BE(α) achieves an upper bound of Õ(T^{7/11}) for rising concave bandits (Theorem 4.4), improving upon the previous best of O(T^{2/3}). In numerical simulations over T=5×10⁶ rounds with K=5 arms, RC-BE(α) achieves the lowest cumulative regret at the horizon among all compared algorithms.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In the numerical simulation (T=5×10⁶, K=5, σ=0.1), RC-BE(α) with α=8/3 achieves the lowest cumulative regret at the horizon, outperforming all three baselines. UCB1 initially shows the lowest regret but its performance degrades once the optimal arm changes, as it violates the stationarity assumption. Rexp3 (Besbes et al., 2014) exhibits approximately linear regret growth because it restarts at a fixed frequency (~10² restarts) and is not an anytime algorithm. R-less-UCB (Metelli et al., 2022) shows sublinear regret growth consistent with its theoretical guarantees, but its reliance on a rested model of reward evolution penalizes its empirical performance relative to RC-BE(α). Theoretically, the new upper bound Õ(T^{7/11}) strictly improves upon both the general restless bound O(T^{2/3}) (Besbes et al., 2014) and the previous rising concave bound Õ(T^{2/3}) (Metelli et al., 2022), while the new lower bound Ω(T^{3/5}) establishes that rising concave bandits are provably harder than stationary MABs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the lower-bound construction of Besbes et al. (2014) for general restless bandits with bounded variation, generalizing it into a reusable recipe (Lemma 3.1) that can be specialized to structured subclasses. For the upper bound, it extends the Budgeted Exploration algorithm of Jia et al. (2023) — originally designed for 2-armed restless bandits — by introducing increasing window widths and budgets that exploit concavity, yielding RC-BE(α). The work also positions itself relative to Metelli et al. (2022), whose R-less-UCB algorithm achieves Õ(T^(2/3)) for rising concave bandits but does not improve upon the general restless bound, and to the stationary MAB lower bound of Lattimore and Szepesvári (2020).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper resolves two open questions about the statistical complexity of structured restless bandits: monotonicity alone (rising) does not reduce complexity below the general restless case (Ω(T^(2/3))), while adding concavity (rising concave) provably makes the problem easier (upper bound Õ(T^(7/11)) < T^(2/3)) yet still harder than stationary MABs (lower bound Ω(T^(3/5))). The remaining gap of 2/55 in the exponent for rising concave bandits is small, suggesting the bounds are near-tight, but closing it fully remains open. The general lower-bound recipe (Lemma 3.1) is of independent interest for constructing hard instances in other structured non-stationary settings.
