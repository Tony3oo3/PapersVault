## Problem

Neural scaling laws predict performance as a function of model size, data, and compute, but leave open how performance scales with the intrinsic complexity of the task itself. This paper isolates two fundamental complexity measures—solution space size and representation space size—and shows that parameter-constrained models exhibit smooth, predictable suboptimality trends when these measures are scaled independently, using the Traveling Salesman Problem as a decoupling testbed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250667/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes n points in R^d (coordinates uniformly sampled in [0,1]^d) and autoregressively generates a closed tour visiting each node exactly once. At each step, a Pointer Network head produces a policy distribution over unvisited nodes, and the selected node's encoding is appended to the Transformer decoder memory before the next selection. Performance is measured as the suboptimality gap: mean model tour length minus mean optimal tour length, where optimal tours come from Concorde (2D) or validated local search (higher dimensions).

## Method

The authors train Transformer models with a Pointer Network head using both PPO (RL, 1M gradient updates with cosine-then-linear LR decay) and supervised fine-tuning (SFT, one epoch over Concorde-solved optimal tours), then fix model width to isolate the capacity bottleneck while scaling problem complexity. For solution space scaling they vary TSP node count n from 5 to 50 (12 to ~3×10^62 tours); for representation space scaling they vary spatial dimension d from 2 to 100. Suboptimality follows superlinear power growth w.r.t. nodes (α≈1.86 for RL, 1.69 for SFT) and bounded exponential decay w.r.t. dimensions, with the latter's asymptote reflecting the provably constant random-tour suboptimality in high dimensions. They further reverse-engineer these trends using 2-opt local search, showing that constraining search depth reproduces the same power growth form, suggesting fixed model capacity limits the ability to escape poor local optima in the cost landscape.

## Results

For 2D 20-node TSP, suboptimality power decays with model parameters (RL α=0.582, SFT α=0.712) and compute budget (RL α=0.439, SFT α=0.555), indicating SFT is more compute- and parameter-efficient than RL. For problem complexity scaling with fixed model capacity, suboptimality follows superlinear power growth with TSP node count (RL α≈1.86, SFT α≈1.69) and bounded exponential decay with spatial dimension (10-node RL: β=0.053, ψ=1.18; 20-node RL: β=0.209, ψ=1.66). Optimal model size scales as N ∝ C^α with α≈0.82 (RL) and α≈0.66 (SFT), consistent with values reported in generative modeling and RL literature.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary baseline is 2-opt local search (Croes, 1958), used as a white-box analogue to interpret the complexity scaling laws. For spatial dimension scaling, 2-opt's suboptimality trend closely aligns with RL: at 10 nodes, 2-opt's fitted asymptote β=0.057 nearly matches RL's β=0.053, supporting the hypothesis that fixed model capacity limits a model's ability to escape poor local optima, mirroring the bottleneck in local search. For node scaling, unconstrained 2-opt produces an unclear trend with an inflection point, but constraining search depth M recovers smooth superlinear power growth (α ranging from 1.27 at M=5 to 2.09 at M=25), matching the functional form of RL (α≈1.86) and SFT (α≈1.69) though with much higher absolute suboptimality. The paper does not claim 2-opt and deep models share mechanisms; rather, the parallel trends provide a potential explanatory framework. Between RL and SFT (the two deep learning paradigms), SFT achieves lower suboptimality at intermediate scales and shows a steeper compute decay exponent, suggesting greater compute efficiency, though single-seed training precludes statistical significance claims.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the neural scaling laws framework (Kaplan et al., Henighan et al.) by introducing a new scaling axis: problem complexity, rather than model size, compute, or data. While Jones [10] extended scaling laws to Hex board size, this work is the first to systematically decouple solution-space complexity (TSP nodes) from representation-space complexity (spatial dimensions) under fixed model capacity, using combinatorial optimization to isolate each measure. The local search analogy (2-opt gradient descent on the cost landscape) draws on decades of combinatorial optimization literature to provide interpretability for the observed scaling forms, and the separability of embedding parameters from model capacity follows directly from Kaplan et al.'s original observation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic demonstration that parameter-constrained neural models exhibit smooth, predictable scaling laws when problem complexity is varied, opening a new axis for understanding model performance limits. The 128M TSP solution dataset and the local search analogy provide concrete tools for future theory development. Key limitations include single-seed training, restriction to Euclidean TSP (which admits PTAS and satisfies the triangle inequality), and the acknowledged need for generalization to other combinatorial problems before complexity scaling laws can be considered a general principle. Open questions include whether model size predicts the growth exponents, what determines embedding bottlenecks, and whether compute- or data-constrained complexity scaling laws yield different forms.
