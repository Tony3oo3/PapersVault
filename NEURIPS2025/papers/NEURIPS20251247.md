## Problem

Existing diffusion-based generative image codecs rely on iterative multi-step sampling (e.g., 20–50 DDIM steps), introducing substantial decoding latency (6–12 s per image) that limits practical deployment. Additionally, text-based semantic conditioning used in prior diffusion codecs is suboptimal for compression because natural language cannot capture fine-grained, localized visual semantics and requires expensive captioning models. The paper hypothesizes that multi-step refinement is unnecessary for generative compression, since at low bitrates the decoder only needs to synthesize high-frequency details from preserved low-frequency structure in a single pass.

## Input / Output

The codec takes an RGB image as input and produces a compressed bitstream (via quad-tree spatial entropy coding) plus a reconstructed image. The encoder outputs a compact latent ŷ and a hyperprior ẑ (7 channels × 4 quantization levels via FSQ at 64× spatial downsampling, yielding ~0.0034 bpp overhead). The decoder performs a single forward pass of a LoRA-adapted SD1.5 U-Net conditioned on the hyperprior-derived semantic context, followed by the pretrained VAE decoder to produce the final reconstruction. Training uses randomly cropped 512×512 and 1024×1024 patches; evaluation covers full-resolution (up to 1024×1024) and resize-and-center-crop settings.

## Method

OneDC integrates a latent compression module (analysis transform → scalar quantization → FSQ hyperprior → entropy model) with a one-step diffusion generator (SD1.5 U-Net adapted via LoRA rank-64) that synthesizes high-frequency details in a single forward pass. The key architectural insight is replacing text-prompt cross-attention conditioning with a hyperprior-derived semantic context: a dedicated semantic decoder h_sem transforms the quantized hyperprior into spatially-aligned tokens injected as keys/values in the U-Net's cross-attention layers, providing localized global-local semantics without external captioning. To strengthen this semantic signal, a distillation mechanism transfers knowledge from a pretrained MaskGIT tokenizer to the hyperprior branch via a Swin Transformer code predictor supervised by cross-entropy loss (training-only, no inference cost). Training follows a two-stage schedule: Stage I optimizes pixel-domain reconstruction (L1 + LPIPS) plus rate and distillation losses, while Stage II freezes the compression module and fine-tunes the generator with a hybrid-domain objective combining DMD2-style latent diffusion distillation, pixel-domain perceptual supervision, and a latent-space adversarial loss to jointly enforce fidelity and perceptual realism.

## Results

On the MS-COCO 30K dataset, OneDC achieves BD-Rate savings of 138.3% (LPIPS), 253.0% (DISTS), and 478.4% (FID) over the VAE-based MS-ILLM baseline, and 55.27% (LPIPS) over the previous SOTA multi-step diffusion codec DiffC on Kodak. On CLIC2020, OneDC saves 54.60% bitrate versus DiffC in LPIPS. Decoding time is 0.34 s per 1024×1024 image on an A100 GPU, over 20× faster than multi-step diffusion baselines (6.9–12.4 s), while maintaining the best FID among all compared methods on CLIC2020.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OneDC outperforms all six baselines on reference-based perceptual metrics (LPIPS, DISTS) across Kodak, CLIC2020, and MS-COCO 30K. Against the VAE-based MS-ILLM, OneDC achieves 138.3% BD-Rate savings in LPIPS and 253.0% in DISTS on MS-COCO 30K, though MS-ILLM is slightly faster to decode (0.17 s vs. 0.34 s). Against the multi-step diffusion codecs, OneDC saves 55.27% bitrate over DiffC on Kodak (LPIPS) and 39.55% over PerCo (SD) on MS-COCO 30K, while decoding 20–36× faster. On FID (generative realism), OneDC achieves the best score on CLIC2020 (beating DiffEIC, DiffC, and PerCo), and on MS-COCO 30K it surpasses the previously best PerCo (SD) by 59.6% BD-Rate. On the resize & center-crop setting, OneDC achieves better LPIPS and PSNR than DDCM while maintaining competitive FID. GLC, the other VAE-based generative codec, is outperformed by OneDC on both LPIPS and DISTS at comparable bitrates.

## Relation to Prior Work

OneDC sits at the convergence of three research threads: VAE-based learned image compression (hyperprior entropy modeling à la Ballé et al.), diffusion-based generative codecs (PerCo, DiffEIC, DiffC), and one-step diffusion distillation (DMD2). Its key conceptual departure from prior diffusion codecs is the argument that iterative DDIM sampling is unnecessary for compression—since the compressed latent already encodes coarse structure, the decoder only needs to synthesize high-frequency details in a single forward pass. It further departs from text-prompt-conditioned codecs (PerCo's BLIP2 captions) by using the hyperprior as spatially-aligned semantic guidance, enhanced via distillation from a pretrained VQGAN/MaskGIT tokenizer, and combines pixel-domain reconstruction with latent-domain diffusion distillation (DMD2) in a hybrid training objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 20× decoding speedup over multi-step diffusion codecs while achieving SOTA perceptual quality (LPIPS, DISTS, FID) across Kodak, CLIC2020, and MS-COCO 30K makes diffusion-based generative compression substantially more practical for bandwidth-constrained applications. The insight that one-step diffusion suffices for the compression-specific decoding task (as opposed to open-ended generation) could reshape how the field designs future diffusion codecs, potentially eliminating the iterative sampling bottleneck entirely. However, the method still requires 0.34 s per 1024×1024 image and a 1.4 B-parameter model, falling short of real-time requirements; the authors note that further distillation and architectural optimization are needed. Open questions include whether the one-step paradigm extends to video compression and whether the hyperprior-as-semantic-guidance strategy generalizes beyond natural images.
