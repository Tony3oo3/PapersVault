## Problem

Standard hypergraph neural networks rely on purely attractive (diffusive) message passing, which causes the Dirichlet energy to decay exponentially with depth, leading to over-smoothing and loss of discriminative power. Moreover, existing HNNs lack a physically interpretable framework that incorporates repulsive forces to separate distinct node classes, and no theoretical guarantee exists that hypergraph message passing can go deep without collapsing representations.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sparse hypergraph incidence matrix H ∈ R^{|V|×|E|}, a node feature matrix X ∈ R^{|V|×d}, and node labels for supervised training. Message passing is executed as a continuous-time particle dynamics simulation over a fixed horizon T with step size τ (e.g., 0.1–0.25), where each hyperedge acts as a field superimposing forces on its constituent nodes. The final node representation at time T is passed through an MLP classifier to produce class labels; the intermediate trajectory also yields node embeddings at any time step.

## Method

HAMP (Hypergraph Atomic Message Passing) reframes hypergraph message passing as an interacting particle system where each hyperedge is a dynamic field inducing shared node dynamics. The interaction force f_β encodes both attraction (within-class clustering) and repulsion (cross-class separation), while an Allen-Cahn double-well potential f_d = δ(1−p²)p provides damping that balances the two forces and prevents feature explosion. Two variants are derived: HAMP-I uses a first-order ODE where node features are the particle attributes, and HAMP-II uses a second-order ODE where velocity is the particle attribute coupled to feature evolution, yielding greater stability for deeper propagation. A stochastic Brownian-motion term is optionally added to capture interaction uncertainty. Theoretically, the paper proves that the repulsive force enforces a strictly positive lower bound on the hypergraph Dirichlet energy, guaranteeing that node representations remain separable regardless of network depth.

## Results

On nine real-world hypergraph benchmarks, HAMP-I and HAMP-II achieve the best or near-best node classification accuracy across both homophilic and heterophilic datasets. The most pronounced gains appear on heterophilic hypergraphs: HAMP-II reaches 70.14% on Senate (vs. 66.90% for HDS^ode and 64.79% for ED-HNN) and 69.94% on Walmart (vs. 66.91% for ED-HNN), a margin of up to 3%. On homophilic datasets, HAMP-I leads on Cora (81.18%) and Citeseer (75.33%), while HAMP-II edges ahead on Pubmed (89.05%). The only dataset where a baseline slightly outperforms both HAMP variants is DBLP-CA, where ED-HNN achieves 91.90% versus 91.67% for HAMP-II.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HAMP-I and HAMP-II outperform the strongest baseline ED-HNN on 8 out of 9 datasets, with the largest margins on heterophilic hypergraphs: +5.35% on Senate (70.14 vs. 64.79), +3.03% on Walmart (69.94 vs. 66.91), and +0.26% on Congress (95.26 vs. 95.00). Against HDS^ode (the second-strongest baseline), HAMP-II leads on Senate by +3.24% and on Cora by +0.15%, while HAMP-I leads on Cora by +0.53% and Citeseer by +0.35%. On DBLP-CA, ED-HNN (91.90%) narrowly beats both HAMP variants (91.66–91.67%), the only dataset where HAMP does not rank first. The reductive baselines (HGNN, HCHA, HNHN, HyperGCN, HyperND) are consistently outperformed by 2–15% depending on the dataset, with the gap widening on heterophilic data where methods like HyperND drop to 38.10% on Walmart versus 69.94% for HAMP-II. The non-reductive baselines AllDeepSets and AllSetTransformer are competitive on homophilic data but fall 3–5% behind HAMP on heterophilic benchmarks, confirming that the particle-system repulsion mechanism is particularly effective when node labels are anti-correlated with hypergraph structure.

## Relation to Prior Work

HAMP extends the Allen-Cahn message passing framework (ACMP, Wang et al. ICLR 2023) from pairwise graphs to hypergraphs, where hyperedges are reinterpreted as dynamic fields governing shared node dynamics. The first-order and second-order ODE structures are drawn from opinion dynamics (Motsch & Tadmor, 2014) and stochastic Cucker-Smale flocking models (Du & Zhou, 2023), respectively, while the hypergraph message passing formalism follows the non-reductive (star-expansion) paradigm of ED-HNN and HDS^ode. The key conceptual departure from prior hypergraph GNNs is the explicit introduction of repulsive forces alongside attractive ones, motivated by the observation that real-world hyperedges do not always connect similar nodes, and the addition of a stochastic (Brownian) term to capture interaction uncertainty.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical guarantee of a strictly positive lower bound on hypergraph Dirichlet energy provides a principled explanation for why HAMP resists over-smoothing, complementing the empirical depth-accuracy results showing HAMP-II maintains performance where baselines degrade. The particle-system framing offers a physically interpretable lens for hypergraph message passing that could inform future design choices beyond the specific HAMP architecture. However, the method assumes static hypergraph topology, limiting applicability to dynamic settings like financial transaction networks, and the 1–3% gains on heterophilic benchmarks, while consistent, are modest in absolute terms. The authors note future extensions to protein structure and sequence design, suggesting broader applicability in scientific discovery.
