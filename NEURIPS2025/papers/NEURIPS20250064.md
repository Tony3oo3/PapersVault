## Problem

Fine-tuning instruction-tuned LLMs on domain-specific tasks (e.g., programming, math) causes substantial degradation on non-target tasks, a form of catastrophic forgetting that limits practical deployment. While prior work showed that LLM-generated training data reduces this degradation, the underlying mechanism remained unexplained, and the computational cost of generating such data was prohibitive. This paper identifies high-perplexity tokens in ground truth data as the root cause of forgetting and asks whether their removal alone can replicate the benefits of self-generated data.

## Input / Output

The method takes an instruction-tuned LLM (e.g., Llama 3 8B Instruct, Gemma 2 IT 2B) and standard ground truth training data (MBPP or MATH) as input. Before fine-tuning, the same model performs a single forward pass over the training sequences to compute per-token conditional probabilities, yielding a token-level perplexity score for each position. Tokens exceeding a perplexity threshold τ (typically 2.5, filtering ~20–24% of tokens) are masked from the loss computation, producing a modified training set. The output is a fine-tuned model evaluated on both the target task (improvement) and four non-target tasks (GSM8K, ARC-Challenge, BIRD, and the held-out split of the other target dataset) for degradation measurement.

## Method

Selective Token Masking (STM) is a single-stage, model-agnostic fine-tuning strategy that uses the target model itself to compute token-level perplexity on ground truth data and masks tokens above a threshold τ from the cross-entropy loss. The key empirical finding is that LLM-generated responses (Self-Output) exhibit markedly lower sentence-level perplexity and fewer high-perplexity token spikes than human-annotated ground truth, and that this perplexity reduction—not the self-generation process per se—is what preserves non-target task performance. STM replicates this effect without any external model generation: a single forward pass over the training data suffices to identify and mask harmful tokens, reducing computational cost from days (Self-Output) to minutes. The paper demonstrates that STM generalizes across fine-tuning techniques (LoRA, DoRA, full-weight), model families (Llama 3, Gemma 2, Mistral, OLMo 2), and learning rates, and that the mechanism is a smaller L2 norm of weight updates (ΔW) due to lower training loss, rather than reduced output diversity or overfitting.

## Results

On MBPP with Llama 3 8B Instruct, STM (τ=2.5) achieves a Backward Transfer (BWT) of −0.16% and Task Improvement (TI) of 3.20%, compared to Baseline Fine-tuning's BWT of −34.71% and TI of −2.23%. On MATH with the same model, STM yields BWT of −0.30% and TI of 6.37%, versus Baseline's −14.12% and −17.83%. STM requires only 4.5–8 minutes of pre-processing (perplexity computation + masking) versus 12+ hours for Self-Output data generation, while achieving comparable or better BWT and TI across all tested models (Gemma 2 IT 2B, Llama 3 8B Instruct, Mistral 7B Instruct, OLMo 2 7B, Gemma 2 IT 9B).

## Baselines & Comparisons

STM outperforms the Rephrase (self-distillation) method of Yang et al. [47] on both BWT and TI across all model–task combinations: e.g., on Llama 3 8B Instruct/MBPP, STM achieves BWT −0.16% and TI 3.20% versus Rephrase's −5.32% and −9.58%. Against regularization baselines (Table 8, Llama 3 8B Instruct on MBPP), STM (BWT 1.90%, TI 3.12%, L2 norm 0.55) dominates Weight Decay + Dropout (best combo: BWT −3.50%, TI −8.03%) and KL regularization (BWT −0.24%, TI 2.24%, L2 norm 0), confirming that degradation stems from high-perplexity token training rather than mere overfitting. STM also matches or exceeds Self-Output on BWT (e.g., −0.16% vs. 3.09% on Llama 3 8B/MBPP) while being roughly 300× cheaper in data-preparation cost. The L2 norm of LoRA weight updates (ΔW) is smallest for STM (0.45–0.56) compared to Ground Truth (5.69–17.75), Self-Output (4.03–6.53), and Rephrase (5.78–7.31), providing a mechanistic explanation for the reduced forgetting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on two threads: (1) the observation that fine-tuning with LLM-generated data reduces catastrophic forgetting (Ren et al., Yang et al.'s self-distillation), and (2) token-level perplexity filtering for pre-training (Lin et al., Ankner et al.). It departs from both by showing that the anti-forgetting benefit of self-generated data is attributable specifically to low token perplexity rather than the self-generation process itself, and by adapting the pre-training token-filtering idea into a single-stage fine-tuning workflow (STM) that requires no reference model training. The method is positioned as orthogonal to existing fine-tuning techniques (LoRA, DoRA, FWFT) and regularization strategies (weight decay, dropout, KL), which it outperforms under matched weight-update budgets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

STM offers a 5–8 minute preprocessing step (compute perplexity, mask tokens above threshold ~2.5) that matches the anti-forgetting effect of 12+ hours of self-generated data, making it highly practical for resource-constrained fine-tuning. The mechanistic explanation—low-perplexity training produces dramatically smaller LoRA weight updates (L2 norm 0.45–0.56 vs. 5.69–17.75)—gives practitioners a clear diagnostic for why forgetting occurs. Open questions include whether the fixed threshold generalizes to larger models (>10B), how perplexity shifts as task difficulty scales (curriculum learning), and whether adaptive or conditional masking could further improve the approach.
