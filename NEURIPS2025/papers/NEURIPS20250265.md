## Problem

Audio-language models lack the structured reasoning phases that have proven effective in text LLMs (e.g., OpenAI o1, DeepSeek R1), instead mapping audio directly to text without intermediate semantic analysis. Compounding this, the field suffers from severe data contamination: widely used benchmarks like AudioCaps, WavCaps, and Clotho share substantial overlap with AudioSet and Freesound, limiting generalization and making zero-shot evaluation unreliable.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250265/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives an audio clip (WAV, 32 kHz, 16-bit, mono) paired with a text question or instruction, and produces a structured XML-tagged response containing a <thinking> section (free-form reasoning, optionally constrained to a target word budget), an optional <semantic_elements> section decomposing the sound into nine descriptor categories (agents, sources, mechanisms, temporal/spatial context, acoustic surfaces, signal type, auditory attributes, non-auditory sensations), and a final <answer> section. The answer format varies by task: a concise caption (<50 words), a single multiple-choice letter, an open-ended response, or a creative passage.

## Method

AudSemThinker fine-tunes the Thinker component of Qwen2.5-Omni-7B using LoRA on the AUDSEM dataset, which is synthesized from ~2.9 billion YouTube SDH closed captions through a fully automated nine-model, four-modality pipeline (audio: Qwen2-Audio, BEATs, AST, CoNeTTE, LP-MusicCaps; image: BLIP, CLIP, RT-DETR, Places365; video: LLaVA-Video) with automatic consistency filtering against original captions (CLAP cosine similarity ≥ 0.5). The key architectural insight is a three-phase reasoning structure—thinking, semantic descriptor analysis, and answer generation—where the semantic phase forces the model to explicitly identify who, what, how, when, and where components before producing output, grounding reasoning in an auditory-semantics framework drawn from ecological psychology. Training is conducted under two paradigms: SFT on the full 800k–900k example dataset, and GRPO with verifiable rewards (accuracy via string matching, format adherence via XML structure checks, and a length-constraint reward with α=0.1, δ=0.5 that penalizes deviation from a target thinking budget), the latter applied to the ~140k multiple-choice QA subset where correctness is objectively verifiable.

## Results

On MMAU (the primary benchmark), AUDSEMTHINKER-QA GRPO achieves 66.70% average accuracy on Test-Mini and 66.03% on the full Test set, outperforming R1-AQA (65.60/64.36%), Qwen2.5-Omni-7B (65.60%), and Audio Flamingo 2 (55.48/59.42%). On OmniBench, AUDSEMTHINKER-QA GRPO reaches 37.51% overall, the best score among all evaluated models. On AudioBench, AUDSEMTHINKER (full SFT) leads on AudioCaps QA (62.81%), MuchoMusic (76.66%), and WavCaps QA (55.20%), while AUDSEMTHINKER-QA achieves the best Clotho AQA score (68.74%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On MMAU Test-Mini, AUDSEMTHINKER-QA GRPO (66.70% avg) surpasses R1-AQA (65.60%), Qwen2.5-Omni-7B (65.60%), Kimi-Audio (65.00%), and Audio Flamingo 2 (55.48%), but falls slightly short of Gemini 2.5 Flash (67.18%). On the full MMAU Test set, AUDSEMTHINKER-QA GRPO (66.03%) leads all reported baselines, exceeding R1-AQA by 1.67 points and Audio Flamingo 2 by 6.61 points. On OmniBench, AUDSEMTHINKER-QA GRPO achieves the best overall score (37.51%), outperforming Qwen2.5-Omni (35.27%) and Qwen2-Audio-7B (34.23%). On AudioBench, AUDSEMTHINKER (full SFT) beats Qwen2.5-Omni on AudioCaps QA (62.81 vs. 59.62) and MuchoMusic (76.66 vs. 70.09), while AUDSEMTHINKER-QA leads on Clotho AQA (68.74 vs. 59.91); however, the GRPO variant underperforms SFT on open-ended AudioBench tasks, suggesting RLVR generalizes better to closed-form (multiple-choice) benchmarks. The SFT models show larger gains over the base Qwen2.5-Omni-7B than the GRPO variant, indicating SFT is more effective for substantial capability gains while GRPO provides more consistent, less task-divergent improvements.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the emerging line of audio-language models with structured reasoning (Mellow, Audio-Reasoner, Audio-CoT) by grounding the reasoning process in a semantic descriptor framework (who, what, how, when/where) drawn from ecological psychology and sound-semantics research, rather than the generic planning/captioning/summarization phases used by Audio-Reasoner. It builds on Qwen2.5-Omni as the base architecture and adapts GRPO with verifiable rewards (from DeepSeekMath) plus a length-constraint reward (from L1) to control the model's thinking budget. The AUDSEM dataset departs from the AudioSet/Freesound-centric pipeline that dominates the field by sourcing from YouTube closed captions and using a nine-model multimodal ensemble, explicitly targeting the cross-dataset contamination problem identified in recent surveys.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The AUDSEM dataset addresses a genuine and underappreciated problem in audio-language research: heavy overlap between training and evaluation sets (e.g., 89% Clotho overlap) that inflates zero-shot benchmark scores. The finding that semantic descriptors improve SFT performance but do not consistently transfer to GRPO training, and that thinking budget effects are non-monotonic (25 and 100–150 words outperform 50–75), provides useful practical guidance for practitioners tuning reasoning-based audio models. Limitations include weaker speech understanding (no ASR in the pipeline), a relatively small final dataset (213k), and the fact that GRPO gains over SFT are modest and task-dependent, leaving open whether structured semantic reasoning scales to larger models or more diverse audio domains.
