## Problem

Existing bilevel optimization (BLO) algorithms either guarantee only weak Goldstein stationarity or require restrictive assumptions (e.g., strong convexity or PL conditions on penalized models) to ensure hyper-objective smoothness. When the lower-level problem admits multiple solutions, the induced hyper-objective is generally nonsmooth and merely Lipschitz continuous, making Clarke-type stationarity computationally intractable under standard tools. The paper asks whether strong Clarke hyper-stationarity can be computed in this general nonsmooth, multi-solution setting.

## Input / Output

The algorithm requires only approximate function evaluations of the hyper-objective φ_o(x) or φ_p(x), obtained by solving the inner min/max problem to additive accuracy w at perturbed points x ± εu. No subgradients or gradients of the hyper-objective are needed. The output is a point x̄ uniformly sampled from the iterates, accompanied by a non-asymptotic bound on the Clarke stationarity measure dist(0, ∪_{z∈B(x̄,δ)} ∂φ(z))² (optimistic case) or ‖∇φ_{p,γ}(x̄)‖² (pessimistic case), both scaling as O(√m(Δ+1)/√T).

## Method

The key technical contribution is a new structural property called set smoothness (Definition 3), which extends gradient-Lipschitz smoothness to set-valued mappings by requiring that convex combinations of endpoint fiber points approximate midpoint fiber points with quadratic error, while a branch-alignment condition (5) prevents cross-branch pairings. The paper proves that the lower-level solution map S(x) = argmin_y f(x,y) is set smooth under the error bound condition (equivalently, the PL condition), with modulus L_S = max{2H_f τ(1+9L_f²τ²), 4L_f²τ²}. This set smoothness, combined with Lipschitz/smoothness of F, yields weak concavity of the optimistic hyper-objective φ_o and weak convexity of the pessimistic φ_p (Theorem 3), placing the problem within the well-studied weakly convex/concave framework. An inexact zeroth-order method (IZOM) then computes approximate Clarke hyper-stationary points: for the weakly convex (pessimistic) case, standard Moreau envelope convergence applies; for the weakly concave (optimistic) case, a novel Brøndsted-Rockafellar-type approximation argument provides the first general computational guarantee for Clarke stationarity of nonsmooth hyper-objectives.

## Results

The paper is purely theoretical with no experimental evaluation. The headline result (Theorem 4) establishes that the inexact zeroth-order method (IZOM) achieves an expected stationarity measure of O(√m(Δ+1)/√T) for both optimistic and pessimistic nonconvex-PL bilevel optimization, where Δ is the function-value gap and T is the number of iterations. For the optimistic case, this yields an (ε, δ)-approximate Clarke hyper-stationary point with δ = O(T^{-1/4}); for the pessimistic case, it yields ‖∇φ_{p,γ}(x̄)‖² = O(√m(Δ_p+1)/√T). This is, to the authors' knowledge, the first non-asymptotic computational guarantee for Clarke-type stationarity in nonsmooth bilevel optimization, improving upon prior work that only achieved the weaker Goldstein stationarity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper contains no experimental comparisons; its contribution is entirely theoretical. In terms of prior theoretical guarantees, the closest baselines are Chen et al. [7] and Khanduri et al. [28], which provide zeroth-order algorithms for nonsmooth hyper-objectives but only guarantee convergence to (approximate) Goldstein stationary points—a strictly weaker notion that does not imply Clarke stationarity. The paper demonstrates (via a counterexample from [47, Proposition 2.7]) that Goldstein stationarity can hold while Clarke stationarity fails by a constant factor, making the Clarke guarantee strictly stronger. Prior penalty-based methods (Kwon et al. [32], Chen et al. [8]) achieve smooth hyper-objectives but require the penalized model to satisfy a uniform PL or error-bound condition in y, which the paper argues is unrealistic when F and f have mismatched structures. The present work removes these regularity requirements by introducing set smoothness, which holds under the standard nonconvex-PL assumption (A3) and yields the first Clarke hyper-stationarity guarantee in this setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on computing stationary points of bilevel hyper-objectives beyond the smooth, single-solution regime. Prior methods (Chen et al. [7], Khanduri et al. [28]) could only guarantee Goldstein stationarity—a strictly weaker notion—under mere Lipschitz regularity, while penalty-based approaches (Kwon et al. [32], Chen et al. [8]) required strong regularity (PL or error bounds on the penalized model) to ensure smoothness. The key departure here is the introduction of 'set smoothness' for the lower-level solution mapping, which reveals a hidden weak convexity/concavity structure in the hyper-objective, enabling Clarke stationarity (a stronger, more meaningful notion) to be computed via the Moreau envelope framework of Davis and Drusvyatskiy [12] and a Brøndsted-Rockafellar-type argument from Robinson [43] for the concave case.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first computational guarantee for Clarke-type hyper-stationarity in nonsmooth bilevel optimization, closing a gap where prior algorithms could only reach the weaker Goldstein notion. The set smoothness concept is a structural property of independent interest that may inform analysis of other parametric and variational problems beyond BLO. Key limitations include the requirement of an error bound (equivalently PL) condition on the lower level, and the failure of the framework when lower-level constraints are imposed (Example 3). Open questions include whether faster algorithms can exploit the weak convexity structure, whether set smoothness can be certified under weaker or alternative conditions, and whether the approach extends to constrained or stochastic bilevel settings.
