## Problem

Diffusion-based image generation models suffer from high computational cost and long latency, particularly on resource-constrained devices. While token merging reduces computation by clustering similar tokens in the spatial domain, it fails to exploit the structural redundancy that becomes apparent after decorrelation in the frequency domain. This paper asks whether transforming tokens into the frequency domain before clustering can yield tighter, more semantically coherent token groups and thus reduce approximation error in attention computations.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt (e.g., 'a photo of a CLASS' from ImageNet categories) and produces a generated RGB image, operating as a plug-and-play module within pretrained diffusion pipelines (Stable Diffusion U-Net or FLUX DiT) without retraining. Internally, it operates on intermediate d-dimensional latent token vectors at each diffusion timestep, applying a DFT to each token before clustering. The module is applied to transformer blocks with downsampling level ≤ 1 (five layers in SD), and tokens are unmerged to their original length before subsequent ResNet blocks.

## Method

Fourier Token Merging replaces the spatial-domain similarity computation in bipartite softmatch clustering with a frequency-domain one: each token x ∈ R^d is transformed via DFT, its real part is truncated to retain only the lowest τd frequency components, and the resulting low-frequency representation is used for clustering. This decorrelates the token features, suppressing high-frequency noise and revealing underlying structural redundancy, which produces clusters with higher intra-cluster cosine similarity and lower centroid distance. An adaptive truncation schedule τ(t, ℓ) interpolates between τ_min and τ_max based on normalized diffusion timestep and layer depth, applying stronger truncation in early (noisy) steps and deeper (more abstract) layers. The paper provides a theoretical error bound showing the merged attention error scales as O(N/C^(1/D_x)), and empirically demonstrates that Fourier clustering achieves up to 25% latency reduction on Stable Diffusion v1.5 with comparable or improved FID, LPIPS, and MS-SSIM, as well as generalization to FLUX (DiT) and ViT classification.

## Results

On Stable Diffusion v1.5, Fourier Token Merging achieves up to 25% latency reduction while maintaining or improving FID, LPIPS, and MS-SSIM compared to the original Token Merging baseline. At a 0.1 merging ratio, Fourier merging reduces intra-cluster token error from 0.73 to 0.57 and improves cluster similarity from 0.90 to 0.91. On FLUX.1-dev (DiT architecture), even at a 0.75 merging ratio, the method achieves over 20% latency reduction with only a 1.83 FID increase. On ImageNet-1k classification with ViT, the method improves top-1 accuracy from 83.09% to 83.79% at the same throughput (~2000 im/s), or achieves 1.2× speedup at matched accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary baseline of original Token Merging (ToMe) [3], Fourier Token Merging consistently achieves lower intra-cluster token error and higher cluster similarity across all merging ratios (e.g., 0.57 vs. 0.73 token error at ratio 0.1), translating to up to 25% latency reduction with comparable or better FID, LPIPS, and MS-SSIM on Stable Diffusion v1.5. Compared to AT-EDM [25], a SOTA attention-driven token pruning method, Fourier Token Merging at a similar 1.1× speedup achieves a lower FID of 40.75 versus AT-EDM's 41.31, indicating better quality at matched speed. Against Token Downsampling [22], which only provides benefits at very high merging ratios (e.g., 0.89) and incurs longer latency at lower ratios (e.g., 0.75), Fourier Token Merging delivers consistent speedups across the full range of ratios, achieving 1.3× speedup at 41.8 FID. The method also generalizes to DiT architectures (FLUX.1-dev) following the ToMA [14] integration strategy, maintaining over 20% latency reduction at a 0.75 merging ratio with only 1.83 FID degradation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the token merging lineage (ToMe → ToMe-for-SD → ToMA) by inserting a Discrete Fourier Transform and low-frequency truncation step before the bipartite softmatch clustering, shifting the similarity computation from the spatial domain to a decorrelated frequency domain. It is explicitly positioned as the first work to exploit frequency-domain structure for accelerating diffusion-based image generation, departing from all prior token merging methods that cluster purely in the time/spatial domain. The paper also contributes a formal error-bound analysis (Theorem 1) for token merging in self-attention, which prior empirical token-merging work lacked, and adapts the DiT integration strategy from ToMA to handle Joint/Single transformers and ROPE embeddings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play, training-free nature of Fourier Token Merging makes it immediately deployable on existing Stable Diffusion and FLUX pipelines, and the ~25% latency reduction at matched or improved FID/LPIPS/MS-SSIM is practically meaningful for edge and interactive deployment. Generalization to ViT classification (0.7-point accuracy gain at same throughput) suggests the frequency-domain clustering insight is not specific to diffusion. Open questions include whether the adaptive truncation schedule (timestep × layer depth) can be learned rather than hand-tuned, whether other spectral decompositions (e.g., learned filters à la GFNet) could outperform fixed DFT truncation, and how the method behaves at much larger token counts (e.g., 4K generation) where FFT overhead may no longer be negligible.
