## Problem

Robust 360° monocular depth estimation remains challenging due to the scarcity of large-scale labeled omnidirectional datasets and the scale ambiguity that arises when independently estimated depth maps from different viewpoints are merged. Existing optimization-based methods that leverage perspective foundation models (e.g., 360MonoDepth, Depth Anywhere) address distortion but ignore 3D structural consistency, producing depth maps that look reasonable in 2D yet yield distorted point clouds in 3D.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255730/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single 360° equirectangular projection (ERP) RGB image (1024×512 or 2048×1024) as input and produces a scale-aligned ERP depth map and surface normal map. The ERP image is first decomposed into six non-overlapping perspective (cubemap) faces, each processed independently by a perspective foundation model. A critical detail is that depth in the perspective domain is z-axis distance while in the omnidirectional domain it is radial distance, so a per-pixel scaling factor ρ = ‖K⁻¹·p̃_c‖ is applied during the warping back to ERP space.

## Method

RPG360 is a training-free pipeline that (1) projects an ERP image onto a six-face cubemap, (2) runs a perspective foundation model (e.g., Metric3D v2) on each face to obtain depth and surface normals, and (3) merges the results into ERP space. The core contribution is a graph-based optimization that resolves cross-face scale inconsistency: predicted 3D points are treated as graph nodes with edge weights derived from local patch similarity and spatial proximity, and the optimization enforces local planar consistency (n_i·(P_j − P_i) = 0) while regularizing depth and normals toward their foundation-model predictions. Crucially, a per-face scale parameter λ_c ∈ ℝ⁶ is introduced as an additional degree of freedom, allowing each cubemap face to shift its depth scale globally while the graph structure preserves local geometry. This is solved with a multi-scale Adam optimization (3 levels, 300/150/30 iterations), and the per-face parameter is what distinguishes RPG360 from prior blending approaches by preventing over-smoothing across face boundaries.

## Results

In the training-free setting, RPG360 achieves a Chamfer distance of 0.337 on Matterport3D and 0.206 on Stanford2D3D, improving by 46.7% and 64.2% respectively over 360MonoDepth (0.632 and 0.576). On the zero-shot 360Loc benchmark, RPG360 reaches F-Score 19.605 and IoU 11.502, substantially outperforming 360MonoDepth (8.992, 4.873) with a 17.2% Chamfer distance reduction. On 2D metrics at 2K resolution (Matterport3D-2K), RPG360 with Metric3D v2 achieves Abs Rel 0.107, RMSE 0.464, and δ₁.₂₅ 0.899, surpassing all optimization-based baselines including 360MonoDepth (0.208, 0.791, 0.656) and Peng & Zhang (0.115, 0.611, 0.871).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary optimization-based baseline 360MonoDepth [47], RPG360 achieves large 3D metric gains in the training-free setting: 46.7% Chamfer reduction on Matterport3D (0.337 vs 0.632) and 64.2% on Stanford2D3D (0.206 vs 0.576), with F-Score nearly doubling (58.8 vs 28.1 on M; 73.9 vs 25.2 on S). Compared to the learning-based Depth Anywhere [60], which requires pseudo-label training, RPG360 matches or exceeds its 3D performance without any training data (e.g., 0.337 vs 0.420 Chamfer on M). Among learning-based methods, Elite360D [1] achieves the best in-domain results (0.291 Chamfer on M→M) but degrades when trained on a different domain (0.539 on S→M), whereas RPG360 is unaffected by domain shift. On 2D metrics at 2K resolution, RPG360 with Metric3D v2 (Abs Rel 0.107) outperforms both 360MonoDepth (0.208) and Peng & Zhang [42] (0.115), while learning-based methods like Elite360D (0.309) and Depth Anywhere (0.167) show significant degradation at higher resolution. On the zero-shot 360Loc benchmark, RPG360 (Chamfer 1.883, F-Score 19.605) surpasses all baselines including Depth Anywhere trained with pseudo-labels on 360Loc (1.457 Chamfer but only 17.145 F-Score), demonstrating superior structural consistency in diverse indoor/outdoor scenes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the graph-based depth refinement framework of Rossi et al. (CVPR 2020), which uses plane constraints to jointly refine depth and normals, by adapting it to the 360° cubemap setting and introducing a per-face scale parameter to resolve the scale ambiguity inherent in independently estimated perspective depth maps. It departs from prior optimization-based 360° methods (360MonoDepth's blending, Depth Anywhere's pseudo-labeling) by explicitly enforcing 3D structural consistency through the graph formulation rather than relying on 2D-space merging or learned refinement. The approach also leverages recent perspective foundation models (Metric3D v2, Omnidata v2) as plug-and-play depth/normal predictors, positioning itself at the intersection of zero-shot monocular geometry and panoramic reconstruction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free formulation sidesteps the fundamental bottleneck of scarce labeled 360° depth data, and the demonstrated cross-domain robustness (outperforming supervised methods trained on different scenes) suggests a practical path for deploying depth estimation in novel environments without retraining. The ablation revealing that the per-face scale parameter λ_c is the critical component—graph optimization alone provides negligible gain—highlights that scale alignment, not structural smoothing, is the primary source of 3D improvement. Limitations include reduced detail on thin structures compared to supervised methods, and the approach's performance is ultimately bounded by the quality of the underlying perspective foundation model, leaving open how it will evolve as those models improve.
