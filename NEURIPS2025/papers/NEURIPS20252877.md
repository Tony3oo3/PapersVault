## Problem

Inferring coherent trajectories from sparse multi-snapshot observations is a fundamental challenge in domains like single-cell biology, meteorology, and economics. Existing multi-marginal Schrödinger Bridge methods either optimize locally between adjacent marginals (sacrificing global coupling and temporal coherence) or solve the full phase-space problem but scale poorly due to costly SDE trajectory caching. No prior method simultaneously achieves simulation-free training, smooth low-curvature trajectories, and globally optimal coupling.

## Input / Output

The method takes positional marginal distributions q_n(x_n) at N+1 discrete time points (only position is constrained; velocity is free at intermediate points), a noise level σ, and a soft constraint matrix R that enforces positional matching in the limit c→0. It produces a learned parameterized acceleration a_t^θ(t, m_t) in phase space (position + velocity), from which smooth trajectories can be sampled, imputed marginals at held-out time points can be generated, and the full joint coupling q({x_n}) over all marginals is recovered. The closed-form conditional bridge (Eq. 6) means no numerical SDE integration is needed at training time, and the model-induced marginals remain close to ground truth throughout optimization rather than only at convergence.

## Method

3MSBM lifts the dynamics to phase space (position x_t and velocity v_t) and minimizes path acceleration rather than velocity, yielding smooth low-curvature trajectories that capture non-linear transitions and inflection points. The key theoretical contribution is the derivation of the Multi-Marginal Momentum Brownian Bridge (3MBB): a closed-form conditional acceleration (Eq. 6) for each segment that depends on all subsequent pinned points through static λ_j coefficients and time-varying C-functions, eliminating costly numerical integration. Training follows an alternating matching scheme: (1) fix the coupling q({x_n}), compute the conditional multi-marginal bridge in closed form, and iteratively propagate dynamics (~10 steps, no backprop) to approximate the velocity profile; (2) match the parameterized acceleration a_t^θ to the prescribed marginal path via a variational objective (Eq. 9), which refines the coupling for the next iteration. This simulation-free design preserves marginal constraints throughout training and converges to the globally optimal mmSB coupling, with per-epoch cost nearly independent of the number of marginals N.

## Results

On the Gulf of Mexico ocean-current dataset (9 marginals), 3MSBM achieves the lowest Sliced Wasserstein Distance (SWD) on 3 of 4 left-out time points (0.14, 0.14, 0.08, 0.06) and the best training-marginal preservation (Rest SWD = 0.05), outperforming the closest competitor Smooth SB (Rest SWD = 0.08). On the high-dimensional Embryoid Body single-cell dataset (100-d PCA), 3MSBM achieves Rest SWD of 0.36 and Rest MMD of 0.11, beating DMSB (0.45 / 0.14) by ~20% while requiring ~2.5× less training time. On Beijing air quality (13 marginals), 3MSBM reduces Rest SWD to 21.25 versus 30.25 (MMFM) and 33.42 (DMSB). Per-epoch training time scales negligibly with the number of marginals (773 s → 812 s from N=3 to N=5).

## Baselines & Comparisons

3MSBM is compared against five baselines: MIOFlow (Neural ODE), SBIRR (iterative reference refinement), MMFM (deterministic cubic-spline flow matching), DMSB (Bregman-iteration phase-space SB), and Smooth SB (Gaussian-path reference). On the Gulf of Mexico dataset, 3MSBM achieves the lowest SWD on 3 of 4 left-out marginals and the best Rest SWD (0.05 vs. 0.08 for Smooth SB, 0.13 for SBIRR), with the largest margin over DMSB (0.09) and MMFM (0.14). On the Embryoid Body single-cell task, 3MSBM dominates all baselines on both MMD and SWD (e.g., Rest SWD 0.36 vs. 0.45 for DMSB, 0.52 for MMFM, 0.66 for SBIRR) while training ~2.5× faster than DMSB. On Beijing air quality, 3MSBM substantially outperforms MMFM and DMSB on mid-range time points (SWD t₅: 9.78 vs. 23.94 and 21.92) and Rest SWD (21.25 vs. 30.25 and 33.42). On Lotka-Volterra, 3MSBM is competitive but not uniformly best—SBIRR edges it on t₁ (0.17 vs. 0.23) and Smooth SB on t₅ (0.11 vs. 0.15)—though 3MSBM still achieves the best Rest SWD (0.18, tied with SBIRR). Overall, 3MSBM wins or ties on the majority of metrics across all four datasets, with the most decisive margins on the high-dimensional and many-marginal settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Schrödinger Bridge matching paradigm (Lipman et al. 2022, Liu et al. 2024, Shi et al. 2023) to the multi-marginal setting by lifting dynamics into phase space, building on the momentum Brownian bridge of Chen and Georgiou (2015) and the multi-marginal Schrödinger Bridge formulation of Chen et al. (2019, 2023a). It departs from prior mmSB solvers by replacing Bregman/IPF iterations (DMSB) or pairwise/local approximations (MMFM, SBIRR, MMSFM) with a simulation-free matching scheme that exploits a newly derived closed-form multi-marginal conditional bridge (Theorem 3.1, Proposition 3.2), thereby simultaneously achieving global coupling, smooth trajectories, and simulation-free training—properties no prior method combines (Table 1). The alternating scheme between conditional path optimization and drift matching mirrors the two-step structure of standard SB matching but operates on the augmented (position, velocity) state.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The closed-form multi-marginal momentum Brownian bridge eliminates costly numerical SDE integration, making the mmSB tractable in high dimensions (100-dim single-cell data) and with many marginals, where prior global-coupling methods (DMSB, Smooth SB) scale poorly. The exponential decay of the λ coefficients (Figure 10) provides a principled truncation strategy, enabling practical implementations that consider only a few future pinned points. A key open question, acknowledged by the authors, is whether the approach extends to densely sampled high-dimensional image spaces (e.g., video interpolation), where the phase-space lifting and closed-form structure may face different computational and representational challenges.
