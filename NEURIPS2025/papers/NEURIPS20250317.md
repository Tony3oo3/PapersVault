## Problem

Inference-time scaling in Transformer LLMs is bottlenecked by KV cache size rather than the number of generated tokens, limiting how much reasoning compute can be allocated within a fixed memory or latency budget. Existing KV cache compression methods either degrade reasoning accuracy at high compression ratios (training-free eviction heuristics) or require expensive continued training (DMC). The paper asks whether efficient attention can be made accurate enough at high compression to enable 'hyper-scaling'—generating more tokens within the same compute budget to improve reasoning accuracy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250317/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained LLM with Grouped Query Attention and a text prompt as input, and produces the same generated reasoning text as the original model while maintaining a sparsified KV cache. At each generation step, a binary eviction decision is predicted per attention head per token, and evicted tokens are overwritten in-place in the KV cache (compatible with PagedAttention). The sparsity pattern is dynamic and input-dependent: different heads and layers adopt different compression ratios, and the effective compression increases beyond the target for sequences longer than ~10K tokens.

## Method

Dynamic Memory Sparsification (DMS) retrofits pre-trained LLMs with a learned, per-head token eviction policy that is far more data-efficient than prior learned compression (DMC). At each timestep, a binary eviction decision α_t is predicted from the hidden state via a Gumbel-sigmoid parameterization of a single borrowed neuron (no extra parameters), and the corresponding KV pair is evicted only after a sliding window delay of w steps, allowing the model to integrate the token's information before removal. Training uses logit distillation from the original LLM as teacher plus a one-sided L1 auxiliary loss that linearly anneals the target compression ratio, requiring only ~1K steps to reach 8× compression. The key design insight is that delayed eviction (rather than immediate) prevents catastrophic accuracy degradation, and simple eviction suffices where DMC's weighted-averaging merge was previously thought necessary.

## Results

DMS achieves 8× KV cache compression with only 1K training steps while maintaining accuracy comparable to vanilla LLMs. On inference-time hyper-scaling (Pareto frontier comparison at equivalent KV-cache memory reads), DMS yields average accuracy gains of +12.0 points on AIME 24, +8.6 on GPQA Diamond, and +9.7 on LiveCodeBench for Qwen-R1 32B over the vanilla model. On general-purpose tasks (Llama 3.2 1B Instruct, Table 1), DMS at 4× compression retains 42.3% on GSM8K and 96.8% on Needle-in-a-Haystack, versus 47.0% and 96.4% for the vanilla model, while all training-free baselines degrade severely (e.g., H2O drops to 14.7% on GSM8K and 13.4% on NIAH at 4×).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DMS dominates all four baselines across nearly every task and compression ratio. Against H2O (Zhang et al., 2023a) and TOVA (Oren et al., 2024), the training-free eviction methods, DMS's advantage is overwhelming at 4× compression: on GSM8K, DMS scores 42.3 vs. 14.7 (H2O) and 20.2 (TOVA); on Needle-in-a-Haystack, DMS retains 96.8% while H2O collapses to 13.4% and TOVA to 28.0%. Against Quest (Tang et al., 2024), which preserves the full KV cache and only reduces memory reads, DMS is competitive on short-context tasks (e.g., MMLU: 40.3 vs. 47.9 at 4×) but clearly superior on long-context tasks (NIAH: 96.8 vs. 95.8; Variable Tracking: 67.6 vs. 49.6), and DMS also reduces peak memory while Quest does not. Against DMC (Nawrot et al., 2024), the prior learned-compression method that merges tokens, DMS is dramatically better: DMC collapses to 3.6% on GSM8K and 0.0% on NIAH at 4×, while DMS retains 42.3% and 96.8% respectively, and DMS requires 8× fewer training tokens to reach the same compression ratio.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Dynamic Memory Compression (DMC; Nawrot et al., 2024), which introduced retrofitting LLMs with learned KV cache compression via token merging. DMS departs from DMC by replacing the more complex weighted-averaging merge with simple delayed eviction, and by introducing a sliding window that separates the eviction decision from its execution, allowing the model to extract information before discarding tokens. The paper also positions DMS against training-free eviction heuristics (TOVA, H2O) and query-aware sparse retrieval (Quest), showing that learned sparsification substantially outperforms these at high compression ratios. Conceptually, the paper introduces the framing of 'inference-time hyper-scaling'—using KV cache compression not merely to reduce cost but to expand the effective token budget and thereby improve reasoning accuracy within a fixed compute envelope.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical contribution is a simple, data-efficient retrofitting procedure (1K steps for 8× compression via logit distillation) that converts existing reasoning LLMs into more effective reasoners without architectural changes. The finding that compression can *improve* accuracy (up to 12 points on AIME 24) rather than merely preserve it reframes KV cache reduction from a cost-saving measure to an accuracy-enhancing one. Limitations include evaluation up to 32B parameters, 32K context, and 8× compression; the authors note that combining DMS with orthogonal methods like Quest, extending to larger models and longer contexts, and integrating with process reward models are open directions. The requirement for continued training (even if cheap) remains a barrier compared to fully training-free approaches.
