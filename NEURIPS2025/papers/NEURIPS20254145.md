## Problem

Existing keyframe selection methods for long video understanding rely on dense captioning, uniform sampling, or end-to-end feature retrieval, all of which are query-agnostic and overlook the logical relationships (spatial, temporal, attribute, causal) between textual queries and visual elements. This creates a 'needle in a haystack' problem where computational constraints force coarse subsampling that misses semantically critical frames needed to answer specific queries.

## Input / Output

The method takes a video sequence of N_v frames and a natural-language query as input. The query is first parsed by an LLM/VLM into key objects, cue objects, and ternary logic triplets (object, relation_type, object) covering four relation types. The output is a minimal keyframe subset of K frames (typically 8 or 32) with associated confidence scores, selected to satisfy both answer-consistency and compactness constraints. On average, only 1.4% of video frames are sampled during the iterative search, and the selected keyframes are passed to a downstream VLM for final QA.

## Method

VSLS is a training-free, iterative temporal search framework that reformulates keyframe selection as Visual Semantic-Logical Search. At each iteration, it adaptively samples frames using a probability distribution (initialized uniformly, refined via spline interpolation), detects objects in sampled frames with YOLO-WORLD, and then verifies four logical relation types—spatial co-occurrence (same-frame presence), temporal proximity (objects within Δt frames), attribute dependency (bounding-box overlap exceeding threshold τ), and causal order (temporal precedence)—to boost frame confidence scores. High-confidence scores are diffused to neighboring frames within a window w to preserve temporal continuity, and the global score distribution is normalized to guide the next sampling round. The search terminates when all key objects are identified or the budget is exhausted, returning the top-K frames. The framework is plug-and-play, requiring no retraining of the downstream VLM, and adds only ~13.7% iteration overhead over the T* baseline while achieving 8.7% absolute QA accuracy gains with GPT-4o.

## Results

On LONGVIDEOBENCH (32-frame setting), VSLS achieves 74.5% SSIM Precision, 92.5% SSIM Recall, and 41.4% Temporal Coverage, outperforming the T* baseline (74.0%, 90.3%, 36.5%) by up to 4.9 points in temporal coverage. On downstream video QA, VSLS yields an 8.7% absolute improvement in GPT-4o's long-video accuracy (51.2% vs. 47.1% baseline on LONGVIDEOBENCH Long category, 8 frames) and a 1.7-point gain on VIDEO-MME Long (56.9% vs. 55.2%). In terms of search efficiency, VSLS requires only 33.3 TFLOPs and 7.8 s search latency on LV-HAYSTACK—comparable to T*-DETECTOR (31.7 TFLOPs, 7.3 s)—while achieving 61.5% end-to-end accuracy versus 59.8% for T*-DETECTOR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VSLS is compared against three paradigms: (1) static uniform sampling, (2) dense retrieval (VIDEOAGENT, RETRIEVAL-BASED), and (3) temporal search (T*-RETRIEVAL, T*-ATTENTION, T*-DETECTOR). Against the strongest baseline T*-DETECTOR, VSLS wins on all three keyframe-selection metrics on LONGVIDEOBENCH: Precision 74.5% vs. 74.0%, Recall 92.5% vs. 90.3%, and Temporal Coverage 41.4% vs. 36.5% (a 4.9-point gap, the largest margin among all comparisons). On downstream QA (Table 3), VSLS consistently outperforms T* across all model backbones and video-length categories; for example, GPT-4o+VSLS reaches 51.2% on LONGVIDEOBENCH Long (900–3600 s) versus 49.1% for GPT-4o+T*, and 57.7% on VIDEO-MME Long (30–60 min) versus 55.2% for GPT-4o+T*. Against dense retrieval methods (VIDEOAGENT, RETRIEVAL-BASED), VSLS achieves substantially higher temporal coverage (41.4% vs. 8.5% and 21.8%) while using 4.2× fewer TFLOPs. The only minor trade-off is a 13.7% increase in average search iterations (48.8 vs. 42.9 for T*), translating to a negligible 0.5 s additional latency.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the iterative temporal search framework introduced by T* (Ye et al., CVPR 2025), which uses object detection (YOLO-WORLD) to adaptively sample frames for long video QA. The key departure is the introduction of four visual semantic-logical relation types—spatial co-occurrence, temporal proximity, attribute dependency, and causal order—as additional scoring signals beyond raw detection confidence, grounded in linguistic and logical theory (Fillmore's case grammar, Talmy's cognitive semantics, Sowa's knowledge representation). The framework also contrasts with dense retrieval approaches (VideoAgent, T*-Retrieval) that process all frames, and with static uniform sampling, positioning itself as a training-free, query-guided search that requires only 1.4% of frames on average.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play nature of VSLS makes it immediately deployable alongside existing VLM pipelines (GPT-4o, InternVL, LLaVA, Qwen2.5-VL) without retraining, and the 1.4% sampling rate offers a practical efficiency win for long-video applications. However, performance is bounded by YOLO-WORLD's detection quality under challenging visual conditions, and the attribute-relation check relies on a simple bounding-box overlap heuristic that the authors acknowledge is logically limited. Open questions include whether the four-relation set generalizes to domains requiring finer-grained logic (e.g., surgical video), whether the search can be made learnable rather than heuristic, and whether the ~7.8s search overhead is acceptable for real-time settings.
