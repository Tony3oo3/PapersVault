## Problem

Standard masked-modeling pretraining for time series uses zero or learnable masks that do not match the structured patterns of real sensor failures (constant offsets, additive noise, hard faults). As a result, pretrained representations remain out-of-distribution under actual sensor outages, causing significant performance degradation in safety-critical applications such as autonomous driving where sensor faults can lead to vehicle instability.

## Input / Output

The model consumes multivariate time series of 11 vehicle sensor channels (wheel speeds, longitudinal/lateral accelerations, yaw rate, steering angle, throttle, brake, engine speed, four chassis accelerations) at 20 Hz with a fixed sequence length of 20 timesteps, z-score normalized channel-wise before masking. During pretraining it outputs a full reconstruction of the 20×11 input tensor; during finetuning it outputs a single lateral velocity (m/s) or sideslip angle (rad) estimate. For the closed-loop Lexus LC 500 deployment, the input expands to 15 channels at ~20.8 Hz and the target becomes the sideslip angle in radians.

## Method

The core contribution is a multi-task self-supervised pretraining objective that replaces conventional zero-masking with three sensor-failure-inspired corruptions applied to geometrically sampled contiguous mask spans: mean masking (setting masked values to zero, simulating hard faults), bias masking (adding a per-channel constant offset drawn from U(−3σ, 3σ), simulating drift/bias), and noise masking (adding per-element Gaussian noise scaled by channel variance, simulating sensor noise). The batch is partitioned into n sub-batches, each corrupted by a different masking function, and the model minimizes the sum of per-sub-batch MSE losses computed only on masked positions. The encoder is a 4-layer transformer (d_model=512, 16 heads) followed by an MLP reconstruction head; during finetuning the encoder is frozen and only a small regression head is trained. This design forces the encoder to learn representations that are invariant to the specific statistical structure of each fault type, yielding robustness to both seen failures (bias, noise, hard faults) and unseen ones (scaling) at inference.

## Results

On the REVS vehicle dynamics dataset, the proposed multi-fault pretraining (Mean+Bias+Noise) achieves the lowest lateral velocity MSE under bias, noise, and unseen scaling (α=2) sensor failures on both seen and unseen tracks, while matching the non-pretrained baseline under nominal conditions. In the closed-loop Lexus LC 500 experiment, the pretrained model maintains stable VSA estimation (RMSE 0.006 rad, MAE 0.030 rad) under injected wheel-speed sensor faults, compared to 0.047 rad RMSE for the non-pretrained baseline and 0.018 rad for a nonlinear Cubature Kalman Filter with access to a physics-based vehicle model. The baseline model's estimation errors caused the vehicle to violate track boundaries, requiring safety-driver intervention, while the pretrained model completed the maneuver reliably.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SimMTM [Dong et al., 2023], a state-of-the-art general-purpose time-series pretraining method, the proposed approach consistently outperforms across all sensor failure scenarios, with the largest gains under bias failures; SimMTM performs well under nominal conditions but the gap widens substantially when sensors degrade. Against a nonlinear Cubature Kalman Filter [Arasaratnam and Haykin, 2009] that has access to a parametrized physics-based vehicle model and control signal trajectory (an advantage the transformer lacks), the pretrained model still achieves 3× lower RMSE (0.006 vs. 0.018 rad) and 5.7× lower MAE (0.030 vs. 0.170 rad) under the same wheel-speed sensor corruptions. Internally, the multi-fault pretraining outperforms both the non-pretrained baseline and the mean-only (traditional zero-masking) pretrained baseline across bias, noise, and scaling failures, with the mean-only baseline showing particularly poor generalization to unseen failure types (e.g., bias and scaling) and high variance across random seeds.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the masked-modeling pretraining paradigm (Zerveas et al., 2021; He et al., 2022) by replacing the standard zero/learnable masks with corruption functions drawn from the sensor-fault taxonomy of Balaban et al. (2009) and Jesus et al. (2017). The key conceptual departure is the argument that real sensor failures (constant bias, additive noise, hard faults) are structurally different from the random contiguous masks used in prior time-series SSL, leaving sensor outages out-of-distribution even after pretraining. The multi-task reconstruction objective with per-sub-batch fault assignment is a new loss design that bridges the SSL masking literature and the fault-tolerant sensing literature, while the encoder architecture and geometric-distribution mask sampling are inherited directly from Zerveas et al.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, deployable recipe for making time-series estimators robust to sensor failures in safety-critical settings, validated not only in simulation but in closed-loop control of a real vehicle (Lexus LC 500) where the baseline model causes track departure while the pretrained model maintains stable racing. Open questions include handling coupled multi-sensor failures, re-weighting the multi-task loss to reflect sensor criticality, and whether the approach scales to longer sequences or lower sampling rates where drift and time-varying scaling become more relevant. The finding that bias masking alone confers robustness to the unseen scaling failure suggests the learned representations capture a broader invariance than the specific corruption applied, but the longitudinal-velocity failure case shows the approach is not a universal fix.
