## Problem

Loss-driven densification in 3D Gaussian Splatting is dominated by low-frequency regions, causing high-frequency details in texture-rich areas to be under-reconstructed because the rendering loss is smoothed out before coarse primitives in complex regions can be flagged. This leads to a suboptimal spatial allocation of Gaussian primitives: too few in complex regions and too many in plain areas. The paper asks whether a loss-agnostic visual prior can identify these misallocated primitives independently of the rendering loss.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254243/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes posed RGB training images (standard 3DGS preprocessing) and an SfM-initialized point cloud, and additionally applies a one-level 2D Haar DWT to each training view to produce a per-pixel complexity map that is back-projected onto primitives during rasterization. The output is a set of 3D Gaussian primitives (center, covariance, opacity, SH coefficients) whose spatial density is adaptively matched to local visual complexity, enabling real-time novel view rendering at the same primitive budget as vanilla 3DGS.

## Method

CDC-GS defines two per-primitive quantities: (1) complexity Γ(G_i), computed by back-projecting the high-frequency DWT sub-bands (LH, HL, HH) of training views onto primitives weighted by their rendering contribution, and (2) density Ψ(G_i), computed as the inverse geometric mean of distances to the 3 nearest neighbors. A consistency score s_i is the product of z-normalized complexity and density; primitives with high complexity but low density (under-reconstructed) are densified, while those with low complexity but high density (over-reconstructed) are pruned, with sampling probabilities proportional to |s_i| and opacity respectively. A complexity-aware adaptive threshold τ(Γ) modulates the standard positional-gradient densification criterion so that complex-region primitives receive a lower threshold. The paper demonstrates via Pearson correlation analysis and scatter plots that the CDC signal is nearly orthogonal to the loss-driven positional gradient, confirming it captures under-reconstruction that gradient-based methods systematically miss.

## Results

On MipNeRF 360, CDC-GS (ρ_d=0.01) achieves 28.02 PSNR / 0.836 SSIM / 0.183 LPIPS with 2.53M Gaussians, outperforming vanilla 3DGS (27.79 / 0.826 / 0.201) and Taming-3DGS (27.91 / 0.821 / 0.211) at the same primitive budget. In the dense group (ρ_d=0.02), CDC-GS reaches 28.11 PSNR on MipNeRF 360, 24.47 on Tanks & Temples, and 30.03 on Deep Blending, surpassing the strongest baselines (AbsGS, Mini-Splatting-D, Pixel-GS) by 0.3–1.2 dB PSNR while using 20–40% fewer Gaussians. The method consistently improves LPIPS by 0.01–0.03 over the best competing method across all three benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In the sparse group (≈2.5M Gaussians), CDC-GS (ρ_d=0.01) beats both 3DGS and Taming-3DGS on all three metrics across all datasets, gaining +0.23 dB PSNR over 3DGS on MipNeRF 360 and +0.63 dB on Tanks & Temples while using the same or fewer primitives. In the dense group, CDC-GS (ρ_d=0.02) outperforms AbsGS by +0.39 dB PSNR on MipNeRF 360 and +1.25 dB on Tanks & Temples, while using 2.56M vs. 1.90M Gaussians on T&T (a modest increase) but 3.95M vs. 4.06M on MipNeRF 360. Against Mini-Splatting-D, CDC-GS achieves +0.33 dB PSNR on MipNeRF 360 and +1.16 dB on Tanks & Temples with roughly half the Gaussians (2.56M vs. 4.27M on T&T). Pixel-GS is the weakest baseline in the dense group; CDC-GS surpasses it by +0.27 dB PSNR on MipNeRF 360 and +0.81 dB on Tanks & Temples while using 52% fewer Gaussians (2.56M vs. 4.50M). Notably, CDC-GS is the only method that simultaneously improves PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS while reducing or matching the Gaussian count in both the sparse and dense groups.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) by introducing a loss-agnostic visual prior for Gaussian primitive allocation, departing from the dominant paradigm where densification is driven by rendering-loss gradients (vanilla 3DGS, AbsGS, Revising-3DGS, Taming-3DGS, Pixel-GS). The key conceptual move is to measure visual complexity via one-level Haar DWT of training views and combine it with a geometric-mean density estimate to identify under- and over-reconstructed regions, positioning the signal as 'perpendicular' to existing loss-driven indicators. It also distinguishes itself from prior frequency-based 3DGS work (FreGS, FDS-GS) by using frequency as an allocation prior rather than as an additional loss term, avoiding the explosive primitive growth those methods incur.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that complexity-density consistency is largely orthogonal to positional-gradient-based densification is practically important: it means existing 3DGS pipelines can adopt CDC-GS as a drop-in complementary module without redesigning their loss-driven densification, potentially recovering high-frequency details that gradient-based methods systematically miss. The paper acknowledges that the visual prior still lacks direct awareness of reconstruction quality and must be paired with loss-driven cues, leaving open how to make the allocation fully self-supervised. The moderate but consistent gains across three benchmarks with comparable primitive counts suggest the approach is robust, though the absolute improvements (≈0.2–0.3 dB PSNR) are incremental rather than transformative.
