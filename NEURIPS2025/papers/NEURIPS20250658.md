## Problem

Existing vision-language model benchmarks emphasize dominant languages and general-domain knowledge, leaving culturally specific content—particularly for underrepresented regions like Taiwan—largely untested. There is no systematic benchmark that jointly evaluates both visual recognition and deeper cultural reasoning (e.g., interpreting symbols, festivals, and local customs) in a low-resource cultural context, making it difficult to assess whether VLMs truly understand localized multimodal content or merely recognize surface-level visual features.

## Input / Output

Each benchmark item pairs a self-shot RGB image of Taiwanese cultural content (signs, food, festivals, landmarks, daily objects) with a Traditional Chinese text question. Questions come in two formats: multiple-choice with four options (evaluated via CircularEval, requiring the model to track the correct answer across four circular shifts of option positions) and open-ended free-form (evaluated by GPT-4.1 as a semantic-equivalence judge). The 1,000-image / 2,000-question official benchmark is split into 1,000 recognition questions (344 requiring Traditional Chinese OCR) and 1,000 reasoning questions (246 basic, 674 external-knowledge, 80 visual-complexity).

## Method

The paper introduces a generalizable taxonomy that partitions culture-specific VQA into Recognition (with/without OCR) and Reasoning (Basic, External Knowledge, Visual Complexity) subtypes, providing a structured annotation protocol for building culturally grounded benchmarks. For model adaptation, the authors propose a two-stage data augmentation pipeline: first, Qwen2-VL generates detailed image captions for 1,736 held-out images; second, GPT-4o synthesizes three dialogue types per image (Visual Conversation, Attribute Recognition, Contextual Inference), expanding 3,472 human-annotated seed QA pairs into 8,680 training dialogues. Fine-tuning is performed via full-parameter supervised learning (lr = 3×10⁻⁶, batch size 2, 2 epochs) on both the human-only and mixed (human + synthetic) datasets, with all training data strictly disjoint from the benchmark. Evaluation employs the CircularEval strategy to mitigate positional bias in multiple-choice answers and a GPT-4.1-judged open-ended QA protocol to probe recall-based cultural knowledge beyond option elimination.

## Results

On the TAIWANVQA benchmark (1,000 recognition + 1,000 reasoning questions), Gemini-2.5-pro achieves the highest overall accuracy at 89.35% (93.40% recognition, 85.30% reasoning), followed by GPT-4o at 77.40% (87.30% / 67.50%) and InternVL3-78B at 75.80% (86.50% / 65.10%). A consistent gap of 15–25 percentage points separates recognition from reasoning performance across all models, with the largest drop observed in External Knowledge reasoning (e.g., Gemini-2.5-pro: 93.40% → 84.70%) and Visual Complexity reasoning (e.g., GPT-4o: 87.30% → 56.10%). In the open-ended QA setting, all models drop 10–20 percentage points relative to MCQ, with InternVL3-78B suffering the largest decline (−22.70 pp).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TAIWANVQA is evaluated on 12 VLMs spanning open-source (Phi-3.5-Vision, Llama-3.2, InternVL3, Qwen2.5-VL) and proprietary (Gemini-2.5, GPT-4o) families. Gemini-2.5-pro dominates every subcategory, leading recognition by 4.1 pp over GPT-4o (93.40% vs. 87.30%) and reasoning by 17.8 pp (85.30% vs. 67.50%), making it the clear state-of-the-art on culturally grounded VQA. Among open-source models, InternVL3-78B (75.80%) and Qwen2.5-VL-72B (73.35%) are the strongest, both substantially outperforming Llama-3.2-90B (51.50%) and Phi-3.5-Vision (31.05%), indicating that Chinese-trained VLMs have a significant advantage on Traditional-Chinese cultural content. Notably, InternVL3-38B (74.10%) nearly matches InternVL3-78B (75.80%), suggesting diminishing returns from scaling within this family for cultural understanding. On the OCR-dependent recognition subset, Qwen2.5-VL-72B (94.77%) and InternVL3-78B (93.60%) are competitive with Gemini-2.5-pro (97.09%), but Qwen2.5-VL drops sharply on non-OCR recognition (79.27%), revealing a reliance on text reading over visual scene comprehension. In the data augmentation experiment, fine-tuning Llama-3.2-11B with mixed (human + synthetic) data lifts its TAIWANVQA reasoning score from 19.1% to 36.4% while preserving or slightly improving MMMU performance, demonstrating that targeted cultural augmentation is effective without catastrophic forgetting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the cultural VQA benchmarking line initiated by CULTUREVQA, CVQA, and CLIcK, but departs from them in two key ways: it uses self-shot images (reducing pretraining contamination) and introduces a structured taxonomy that separates recognition from reasoning questions, with reasoning further subdivided into Basic, External Knowledge, and Visual Complexity types. The data augmentation pipeline builds on visual instruction tuning (Liu et al., 2023) by combining Qwen2-VL captioning with GPT-4o dialogue generation to triple training data per image. The CircularEval strategy for mitigating option-order bias in MCQ evaluation is adopted from MMBench, and the overall evaluation framework draws on lmms-eval and the broader multimodal benchmarking infrastructure (MMMU, MME, SEED-Bench).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a transferable, step-by-step methodology for constructing culture-specific multimodal benchmarks, which is practically valuable for low-resource cultures that lack the infrastructure to build such datasets from scratch. The consistent finding that even top proprietary models (Gemini-2.5-pro) show a 8-point gap between recognition (93.4%) and reasoning (85.3%), and a further 17-point drop in open-ended generation, highlights that current VLMs have not internalized cultural knowledge in a generative sense. Open questions include whether the taxonomy generalizes beyond East Asian cultural contexts, whether the data augmentation strategy scales to other languages, and whether the MCQ-to-Open-QA gap reflects a fundamental limitation or an evaluation artifact of LLM-as-judge scoring.
