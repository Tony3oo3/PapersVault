## Problem

Parameter-efficient fine-tuning methods like LoRA and LoRITa are effective for adapting pretrained models but fail at low-rank pre-training, where maintaining a prescribed low-rank structure throughout training while matching dense-model performance remains unsolved. Existing low-rank training approaches either rely on overparameterization with post-hoc truncation, require full-rank warmup phases, or introduce hyperparameters that do not generalize across architectures and tasks, leaving a gap for a principled, optimizer-compatible regularization that directly promotes low-rank weights during pre-training.

## Input / Output

The method operates on the weight matrices of Transformer architectures (ViT-Tiny, ViT-Base, RoBERTa, Llama3) during standard supervised training, consuming RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 224×224 for ImageNet-1k) or text pairs (GLUE tasks) and producing class labels or task scores as usual. The distinguishing output is a trained model whose weight matrices are driven toward a prescribed target rank r_target, after which a truncated SVD yields compact factor matrices A, B with inner dimension r, reducing parameter count by up to 80% while preserving accuracy.

## Method

Q3R defines a quadratic regularizer ½⟨W, R_{W',ε}(W)⟩ that majorizes an ε-smoothed log-determinant rank surrogate F_ε(W), where the reweighting operator R is built from a partial SVD of a reference matrix W' and updated only every T training iterations (not every step), keeping computational overhead proportional to the target rank rather than the full matrix dimension. The smoothing parameter ε is decreased non-increasingly via ε ← min(ε, σ_{r_target+1}(W)), gradually increasing non-convexity to escape high-rank local minima. The dedicated AdamQ3R optimizer decouples the Q3R gradient from Adam's adaptive moment estimates (analogous to AdamW's decoupling of weight decay), preventing the second-order information already embedded in the quadratic model from being distorted by the adaptive scaling. This IRLS-inspired framework requires only a truncated SVD of order r_env ≈ r_target per update, with per-iteration gradient computation costing O(d₁d₂r_env + (d₁+d₂)r_env²), and is fully compatible with existing Transformer architectures without architectural modification.

## Results

On ViT-Tiny/CIFAR-10, Q3R retains 42.4% of parameters with only a 1.22% accuracy drop (68.33% vs. 69.90% full-rank) and retains 23.2% with a 4.4% drop (65.26% vs. 69.90%). On ViT-Base/CIFAR-100, Q3R achieves 0.40–0.44 test accuracy at 20% parameter retention, whereas LoRITa does not exceed 0.25 at the same level. On the GLUE benchmark, Q3R fine-tuning (rank=4) achieves an average score of 85.86, essentially matching dense fine-tuning (85.88) and outperforming LoRA (84.58).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LoRA, Q3R dominates at every truncation level where both are available: at 20% parameter retention on CIFAR-10, Q3R achieves 0.6496 accuracy versus LoRA's 0.3576, a gap of nearly 29 percentage points. On GLUE fine-tuning, Q3R (85.86 avg) outperforms LoRA rank=4 (84.58 avg) by 1.28 points while matching dense fine-tuning (85.88). Against LoRITa, Q3R consistently wins at aggressive truncation: at 20% retention, Q3R reaches 0.6496 versus LoRITa's best of 0.5035 (D=3, λ=10⁻¹), and at 30% retention Q3R achieves 0.6744 versus 0.6368. The advantage narrows at 40% retention (0.6868 vs. 0.6740) and inverts slightly at 100% (0.6962 vs. 0.7273), where LoRITa's overparameterization provides a small benefit. Overall, Q3R's key advantage is robustness across the full truncation range without requiring the overparameterized factor matrices that LoRITa relies on.

## Relation to Prior Work

Q3R transplants the iteratively reweighted least squares (IRLS) framework from the low-rank matrix recovery literature (Kümmerle & Mayrink Verdun, ICML 2021; Ghosh et al., NeurIPS 2024) into deep learning training for the first time. It builds on the ε-smoothed log-determinant surrogate from Kümmerle & Maly (NeurIPS 2023) but replaces the expensive per-step spectral gradient with a periodically updated quadratic majorizer, and extends the AdamW decoupling principle (Loshchilov & Hutter, ICLR 2019) to this new regularizer. Conceptually, it departs from factorization-based PEFT methods (LoRA, LoRITa) that overparameterize and then truncate, and from nuclear-norm/Schatten-p approaches that require a full SVD every iteration, by directly optimizing a reweighted quadratic term on the dense weight matrix with only a truncated SVD every T steps.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Q3R addresses a recognized gap: PEFT methods like LoRA work well for fine-tuning but degrade substantially when applied to pre-training from scratch, and prior low-rank pre-training methods (LoRITa, orthogonality regularization) still underperform full-rank baselines at aggressive truncation levels. The periodic-reweighting design keeps computational overhead proportional to the target rank rather than the matrix dimension, making it practical for larger models. However, the method still operates on dense weight matrices throughout training (no actual memory savings during optimization), relies on two key hyperparameters (λ and r_target), and is validated only on small-to-medium-scale models (ViT-Tiny/Base on CIFAR, ViT-Base on ImageNet-1k, RoBERTa on GLUE). Open questions include whether the approach scales to billion-parameter LLMs, whether it generalizes to non-Transformer architectures, and whether adaptive rank selection (à la Cuttlefish) can remove the need for manual r_target specification.
