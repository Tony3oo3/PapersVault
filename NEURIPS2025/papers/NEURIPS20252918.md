## Problem

Existing constructive Neural Combinatorial Optimization (NCO) methods for Vehicle Routing Problems rely on an appending-based paradigm that restricts new nodes to the end of the partial solution, causing path intersections and suboptimal results from early greedy decisions. The insertion-based paradigm, which allows placing a node at any valid position within the partial solution, offers substantially greater flexibility but has remained entirely unexplored in NCO for VRPs over the past decade.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252918/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For TSP, each node is represented by its 2D coordinates; for CVRP, nodes carry 2D coordinates plus a demand value, and the depot is an additional node with zero demand. At each construction step, the decoder consumes three embedding groups—the current node (selected by nearest-neighbor heuristic), remaining unvisited nodes, and position embeddings formed by concatenating adjacent visited-node embeddings—then outputs a probability distribution over all valid insertion positions. For CVRP, position embeddings additionally encode the remaining route capacity, and the output includes the option to start a new route at the depot.

## Method

L2C-Insert uses an encoder-decoder transformer (9-layer decoder with multi-head attention) where the key architectural novelty is the position-embedding mechanism: each candidate insertion slot is represented by the horizontal concatenation of its two flanking visited-node embeddings, projected back to the embedding dimension. Training is supervised: given a labeled optimal tour, the model learns to predict the correct insertion position for each sequentially re-inserted node, minimizing a cross-entropy loss over positions. At inference, a greedy rollout generates an initial solution, which is then refined by an insertion-based local reconstruction loop that alternates distance-based destruction (removing a random node and its α nearest neighbors) with model-driven re-insertion, exploiting the insertion paradigm's flexibility to escape local optima that sequence-based destruction cannot address.

## Results

On TSP100, L2C-Insert (I=1000) achieves a 0.0002% optimality gap (length 7.76326), nearly matching Concorde (0.000%) while outperforming the strongest constructive NCO baseline LEHD+RRC1000 (0.0016%) by 12.5% in gap with 1.4× speedup. On TSP1K, L2C-Insert (I=1000) reaches 0.481% gap, reducing LEHD's gap by 34.2% with a 6.7× speedup. On large-scale TSP100K, L2C-Insert (I=1000) achieves 4.920% gap in 26.07 min, outperforming GLOP (5.142%, 1.04h) and INViT greedy (7.199%, 80h). On CVRP100K, even greedy rollout achieves 8.804% gap in 29.95 min, a 71.4% gap reduction over INViT-3V greedy (28.546%, 93h).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest constructive NCO baseline LEHD+RRC1000, L2C-Insert (I=1000) achieves lower gaps on TSP100 (0.0002% vs 0.0016%), TSP1K (0.481% vs 0.731%), TSP10K (2.079% vs 12.709%), CVRP100 (0.413% vs 0.420%), and CVRP10K (8.646% vs 9.916%), while on CVRP1K LEHD retains a slight edge (3.028% vs 4.836%) but L2C-Insert is 2× faster. Against the two-stage method GLOP, L2C-Insert wins on TSP10K (2.079% vs 4.545%), TSP50K (3.835% vs 5.102%), and TSP100K (4.920% vs 5.142%), with 1.5–2.8× speedups. Against heatmap-based DIFUSCO, L2C-Insert improves the gap on TSP1K (0.481% vs 1.900%) and TSP10K (2.079% vs 2.580%) with 2.8× and 1.5× speedups respectively. On very large CVRP instances (50K/100K), L2C-Insert greedy rollout dramatically outperforms INViT-3V greedy by 62.7% and 71.4% in gap reduction, while also being 3–3× faster. On real-world TSPLIB and CVRPLIB instances (Table 3), L2C-Insert (I=1000) achieves the best average gap (0.308% on TSPLIB, 3.201% on CVRPLIB) among all neural solvers, outperforming LEHD (0.380%, 3.512%), GLOP (1.153%), and BQ (1.557%, 8.078%). Classical solvers (Concorde, LKH3, HGS) remain the quality reference but require orders of magnitude more time (e.g., 400h for LKH3 on TSP100K vs 26 min for L2C-Insert).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant appending-based constructive NCO paradigm (Pointer Networks → Attention Model → LEHD → BQ) by introducing an insertion-based construction strategy, a technique well-established in Operations Research (Rosenkrantz et al., 1977; Hassin & Keinan, 2008) but previously unexplored in neural combinatorial optimization for VRPs. Architecturally, it builds directly on the heavy-decoder multi-attention-layer design of LEHD [22] and adopts the k-nearest inference acceleration from BQ [23], but redefines the decoder's role from predicting the next node to append to predicting the optimal insertion position for a given node. The supervised training scheme is redefined to match the insertion paradigm, and a novel distance-based destruction strategy for local reconstruction replaces the sequence-based approaches used in prior constructive NCO methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that the insertion paradigm's flexibility yields substantial and consistent gains over appending across TSP and CVRP at scales from 100 to 100K nodes, with the ablation showing L2C-Insert(I=200) outperforming L2C-Append(I=1000) — suggesting the construction paradigm matters more than brute-force iteration count. Open questions include whether a learned node-selection policy (replacing the current greedy nearest-neighbor heuristic) could further close the gap to classical solvers, whether hybrid appending+insertion moves would be even more powerful, and whether the framework extends to other combinatorial optimization problems beyond routing. The method still shows meaningful gaps versus LKH3/HGS at larger scales, indicating room for architectural improvements.
