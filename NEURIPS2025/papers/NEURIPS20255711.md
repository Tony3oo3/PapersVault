## Problem

Pairwise-based functional brain network (FBN) construction relies on bivariate statistics (e.g., Pearson correlation) to set each edge independently, which the paper proves is mathematically incapable of recovering irreducible high-order (k≥3) dependencies present in multivariate neural time series. Existing hypergraph approaches that do model such interactions are computationally expensive, heuristic, and agnostic to downstream analysis tasks, leaving no efficient end-to-end method to learn high-order FBN structures directly from data distributions under task-relevant global constraints.

## Input / Output

The method consumes multivariate time series of shape N×T (N brain regions × T time points) from fMRI or EEG, along with a modeling-resolution mapping φ that specifies how many samples are aggregated into a single FBN (sample, subject, group, or project level). It produces a discrete binary adjacency matrix A ∈ {0,1}^{N×N} as the learned FBN structure, a graph embedding for classification, and predicted class labels. Unlike pairwise methods that output a weighted graph as an aggregation of independent edge decisions, GCM outputs a single binarized graph whose sparsity level is adaptively determined per batch during training.

## Method

GCM treats the FBN adjacency matrix as a single learnable object shaped holistically by four global constraints (signal synchronization, subject identity, expected edge numbers, and data labels) rather than as a collection of independent pairwise decisions. A prototype matrix Â is initialized uniformly and relaxed via Gumbel-Sigmoid to enable differentiable sampling over binary graphs; a Batch Binarization Algorithm (BBA) then enforces hard sparsity by retaining only the top-k entries per graph, where k is adaptively tracked as the expected edge count. A GNN backbone (SAGE) performs message passing on the learned graph, and a permutation-invariant readout produces graph embeddings. Training jointly optimizes a cross-entropy label loss, an InfoNCE-style subject-identity contrastive loss, and an L1 sparsity regularizer on the prototype matrix, with all four constraint signals integrated through a unified gradient. The framework is resolution-agnostic: the same architecture operates at sample, subject, group, or project level by changing only the aggregation mapping φ, and Theorem 2 guarantees that GCM can approximate any k-th order decision rule to arbitrary precision.

## Results

GCM achieves up to a 30.6% relative accuracy improvement and a 96.3% reduction in computational time across 5 datasets and 2 task settings (intra- and inter-subject), compared to 9 baselines and 10 state-of-the-art methods. At the sample level, GCM reaches 97.4% accuracy on DynHCP Activity (inter-subject) and 93.7% on DynHCP Gender (intra-subject), setting new state-of-the-art on most conditions. At higher modeling resolutions, GCM_group attains 97.1% on DynHCP Activity (intra) and 95.8% on DynHCP Gender (intra), outperforming the subject-level (IGS+GCM_subject) and project-level (IBGNN+GCM_project) competitors on most datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GCM outperforms all 9 traditional/GNN baselines (LR, SVM, RF, XGBoost, MLP, GCN, SAGE, GAT, GIN) and all 10 SOTA methods (IBGNN, IBGNN+, IGS, IC-GNN, BrainIB, VIB-GSL, HGP-SL, DGCL, BNT, Hybrid) on the majority of dataset–task combinations at the sample level. The largest relative gains (up to 30.6%) occur on Cog State, where pairwise-correlation-based methods and BGI/GSL approaches struggle most due to the high-order cognitive-state dependencies. Against the two closest SOTA competitors at higher resolutions, GCM_group surpasses IGS+GCM_subject on DynHCP Activity (0.971 vs. 0.861 intra), DynHCP Age (0.891 vs. 0.856 intra), and Cog State (0.493 vs. 0.460 intra), while IGS+GCM_subject retains an edge on DynHCP Gender inter-subject (0.889 vs. 0.686) and SLIM (0.939 vs. 0.694 inter). GCM also achieves a 96.3% reduction in computational time relative to BGI and GSL methods, owing to its streamlined single-graph optimization design. The Brain Network Transformer (BNT) and Hybrid (ICML 2024) are the strongest individual baselines but remain below GCM on most conditions, confirming that holistic global-constraint optimization yields more informative structures than fixed hyperedge counts or attention-based edge selection.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of three threads: hypergraph/high-order network modeling (which motivates the need to go beyond pairwise edges), Brain Graph Interpretation (BGI, which applies post-hoc masks to pairwise-constructed graphs), and Graph Structure Learning (GSL, which refines structures but typically for single-graph node-level tasks). Rather than building on a single prior method, GCM repurposes Gumbel-Softmax discrete optimization, GNN backbones, and contrastive regularization into a unified 'global constraints' paradigm where the adjacency matrix is optimized holistically rather than as independent edge decisions. The multi-resolution formalization (sample/subject/group/project) is explicitly framed as absent from prior FBN literature, and Theorem 1 provides a clean cumulant-based proof that pairwise edge rules are mathematically blind to high-order dependencies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical result (Theorem 1) gives a rigorous, if not deeply novel, justification for why the dominant pairwise-correlation paradigm in connectomics is fundamentally insufficient, and Theorem 2 guarantees GCM can in principle recover arbitrary high-order rules. The 96.3% computational time reduction over hypergraph baselines makes high-order FBN learning practically feasible for large cohorts, and the multi-resolution framework offers a principled vocabulary for distinguishing dynamic, individual, cohort, and population-level network structures. Open questions include whether the group-level results (which the authors acknowledge may reflect label leakage) can be made more rigorous, whether the 'global constraints' abstraction generalizes beyond the four specific constraints tested, and whether the framework extends to multi-modal or longitudinal settings.
