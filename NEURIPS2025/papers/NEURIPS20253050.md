## Problem

Classical Thompson Sampling requires specifying informative priors, sampling from posteriors over latent parameters, and updating those posteriors — operations that are notoriously difficult to implement with neural networks. This paper reframes the source of uncertainty from unknown latent parameters to missing (but potentially observable) outcomes, enabling TS to be implemented with standard generative sequence models and gradient-based training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253050/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each decision time, the algorithm consumes the current context vector, prior task information (e.g., article headline text embedded via DistilBERT), the running task history of past contexts, actions, and observed outcomes, and the action space. It outputs a single action selected by a fitted 'oracle' policy. The generative model is pretrained offline on historical tasks and then frozen; at decision time it performs autoregressive generation conditioned on the observed history to impute all missing outcomes (both future timesteps and counterfactual actions), after which a policy fitting procedure (logistic regression, MLP, or any other method) is applied to the completed dataset.

## Method

The core mechanism replaces Bayesian posterior sampling over latent parameters with probabilistic imputation of missing outcomes via a pretrained generative sequence model. At each timestep, the model autoregressively generates a complete task dataset (all contexts, all outcomes for all actions), a policy is fitted to this imputed dataset using a designer-specified procedure, and the action is selected according to that fitted policy. The generative model is trained offline by minimizing a sequence prediction loss over historical tasks, and the paper proves a regret bound that depends on the model only through its offline prediction loss, applies to any policy class with finite Natarajan dimension, and requires no discretization of parameter space. The proof establishes that the algorithm implements exact probability matching (Thompson Sampling) when the true task distribution is used, and quantifies the additional regret from model misspecification as a square-root penalty on the excess prediction loss.

## Results

The paper evaluates TS-Gen on two settings (SYNTHETIC with Bayesian logistic regression and SEMI-SYNTHETIC using the MIND news recommendation dataset) with T=500 timesteps, |A|=10 actions, and binary outcomes. Results are reported as cumulative regret averaged over 500 bandit tasks (Figure 6), with regret measured against the best-fitting policy in the chosen policy class (logistic regression for synthetic, MLP for semi-synthetic). TS-Gen achieves the lowest cumulative regret in both settings, outperforming all baselines including methods that share the same generative model p_θ. The paper also confirms the theoretical prediction from Theorem 2: lower offline prediction loss of p_θ translates to lower regret for TS-Gen.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TS-Gen outperforms all baselines in both the SYNTHETIC and SEMI-SYNTHETIC settings. Most notably, TS-Gen beats GREEDY, EPSILON-GREEDY, and TS-NEURAL-LINEAR, which all use the identical p_θ generative model but select actions differently—this isolates the benefit of the autoregressive generative approach to uncertainty quantification over simpler decision rules. TS-Gen also outperforms standard Linear TS (Agrawal and Goyal, 2013) and LinUCB (Li et al., 2010), which the authors attribute to the pretraining procedure and better exploitation of prior task information Z (e.g., article text via DistilBERT embeddings). The advantage over LinUCB is particularly meaningful in the semi-synthetic cold-start setting, where LinUCB was originally designed for but does not leverage article-level metadata. No baseline matches or exceeds TS-Gen in either setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reframes Thompson Sampling by replacing the classical view of uncertainty (unknown latent parameters requiring priors and posteriors) with a missing-data perspective, where uncertainty arises from unobserved counterfactual and future outcomes. It builds on the information-theoretic TS analysis of Russo and Van Roy (2016) but departs from prior work by avoiding discretization of latent parameter spaces and instead bounding regret through the entropy of the optimal policy evaluated at observed contexts, enabling direct application to infinite policy classes via VC/Natarajan dimension. The generative imputation step connects to recent work on sequence models for decision-making (Wen et al. 2021, Cai et al. 2024) but differs critically in handling contextual, meta-learning settings with arbitrary policy fitting procedures and without requiring exchangeability of unobserved outcomes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical insight is that the regret bound depends on the generative model solely through its offline prediction loss, meaning practitioners can evaluate and optimize their imputation model via standard offline training/validation without understanding the decision-making algorithm's internals. This modular design—any generative model plus any policy fitting procedure—makes TS compatible with modern tools like transformers and foundation models for processing complex prior information (e.g., article text). Open questions include the computational cost of per-decision generation and policy refitting, how much offline data is needed for a high-quality generative model, whether the approach extends to MDPs, and how to formally characterize the heuristic approximations used when complete task datasets are unavailable.
