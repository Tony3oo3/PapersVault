## Problem

Mask-based autoregressive (MAR) models have shown strong image generation capability in continuous space, but extending them to video remains under-explored due to a fundamental tension: video requires temporally sequential frame-by-frame generation, yet the mask mechanism needs all context tokens available simultaneously. Existing approaches either introduce a training-inference gap, double sequence length, or separate temporal and spatial modeling, sacrificing either temporal smoothness or efficiency.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251907/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For image-to-video, the model takes a single reference image and a text prompt; for video-to-video, it takes multiple conditioning frames plus a text prompt. The image/video is compressed into continuous latent tokens via Cosmos-Tokenizer (4×8×8 or 8×16×16 compression), and the model generates subsequent frames autoregressively. Stage 1 produces 25 frames at 256×256; stage 2 produces 49 frames at 480×768, with the ability to extrapolate to arbitrary resolutions and durations up to 20 seconds (12 fps) far beyond the 4-second training length.

## Method

VideoMAR is a 1.4B decoder-only transformer (36 layers, dim 1536) that operates on continuous tokens and uses a frame-wise causal attention mask enforcing temporal causality (each frame attends to all previous frames) while preserving spatial bidirectionality within each frame. The key training innovation is the next-frame diffusion loss: a random frame t is partially masked, all preceding frames remain fully visible, all subsequent frames are fully masked, and the token-wise diffusion loss is applied only to the masked tokens in frame t, eliminating the training-inference gap. Training employs temporal short-to-long curriculum learning (5→13→25 frames) and two-stage progressive resolution (256×256 → 480×768) to manage the difficulty of long-sequence modeling. At inference, a progressive temperature strategy (1.0→0.9 across frames) suppresses accumulation error in late frames, while 3D-RoPE enables simultaneous spatial and temporal extrapolation, and KV caching combined with spatial parallel masked generation yields >10× speedup over naive next-token prediction.

## Results

On the VBench-I2V benchmark, VideoMAR-stage2 (1.4B params, 0.5M training pairs) achieves a Total Score of 84.82 and I2V Score of 94.02, surpassing the strongest autoregressive baseline Cosmos-13B (84.22 / 92.60) while using only 9.3% of the parameters, 0.5% of the training data, and 0.2% of the GPU resources (64 H20 vs. 10,000 H100). VideoMAR-stage1 (256×256, 0.2M data) scores 82.56 total, already competitive with diffusion models like VideoCrafter-I2V (82.57). The method also achieves a 10× inference speedup over the NTP paradigm (134s vs. 1941s for stage 2) by combining spatial parallel masked generation with temporal KV caching.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VideoMAR-stage2 outperforms both Cosmos variants (5B: 84.16, 13B: 84.22) on Total Score and I2V Score (94.02 vs. 92.60), despite using 1/9th the parameters and 1/200th the training data. It also edges out ConsistI2V (84.07) and VideoCrafter-I2V (82.57) among diffusion baselines. However, VideoMAR still trails the strongest diffusion models—Magi-1 (89.28), Step-Video-TI2V (88.36), and CogVideoX-I2V (86.70)—by 1.9 to 4.5 points on Total Score, with the gap concentrated in Camera Motion (21.62 vs. 50.85–67.68) and Dynamic Degree (10.98 vs. 48.78–68.21) sub-scores. On Motion Smoothness, VideoMAR actually leads all baselines (99.57), and its Subject Consistency (97.13) is competitive with the top diffusion models. The paper attributes the remaining gap to the limited 0.5M training set and from-scratch training, positioning VideoMAR as a proof-of-concept for the AR paradigm rather than a final SOTA claim.

## Relation to Prior Work

VideoMAR extends the mask-based autoregressive (MAR) paradigm from continuous-token image generation (Li et al., NeurIPS 2024) to video, introducing a next-frame diffusion loss and frame-wise causal attention to reconcile temporal causality with spatial bidirectional masked generation. It departs from both NTP-based video AR models (VideoPoet, Cosmos, EMU3) that use discrete tokens in raster-scan order, and from prior mask-based video AR methods (MAGVIT, Genie, NOVA, MAGI) that either suffer training-inference gaps, separate temporal and spatial modeling, or double sequence length. The 3D-RoPE positional encoding and KV-cache-based inference directly transplant LLM techniques into the video AR setting for the first time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a 1.4B-parameter model trained on 0.5M samples with 64 GPUs can match or exceed a 5B/13B model trained on 100M samples with 10,000 GPUs on VBench-I2V, suggesting AR video generation may scale more efficiently than previously assumed. The simultaneous spatial-parallel and temporal-KV-cache inference design (10×+ speedup over NTP) and 3D-RoPE-based extrapolation to arbitrary resolutions and durations are practically useful capabilities. However, VideoMAR still trails the strongest diffusion-based I2V models (Magi-1, Step-Video-TI2V) by ~4–5 points on VBench-I2V, and the authors acknowledge that multi-task unification, interactive world-model use, and scaling to larger data remain open.
