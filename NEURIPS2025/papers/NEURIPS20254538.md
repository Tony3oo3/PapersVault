## Problem

Existing MoE expert pruning methods either rely on heuristic metrics (activation frequency, routing scores) lacking theoretical guarantees, or require exhaustive enumeration of expert subsets with exponential complexity that is infeasible for models with hundreds of experts. This paper addresses the gap by providing a theoretically grounded, computationally efficient method to identify and remove unimportant experts without enumerating all combinations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254538/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a trained MoE model (with 8–128 routing experts per layer across multiple expert layers), a small calibration set of 128 text samples from C4, and hyperparameters (M=20 Monte Carlo samples, truncation threshold τ=0.5, pruning ratio γ). It produces a pruned MoE model with the lowest-Shapley-ranked experts removed, along with per-expert Shapley value scores. The entire pruning process completes in tens of minutes (e.g., 36 min for Qwen1.5-MoE-A2.7B on a single A100 GPU), compared to infeasible enumeration for large expert counts.

## Method

Shapley-MoE frames expert pruning as a cooperative game where each expert is a player and model performance (1/PPL) is the value function, then estimates each expert's Shapley value via Monte Carlo sampling over random expert permutations, reducing complexity from O(2^N) to O(MN). Two key innovations improve estimation: (1) Early Truncation terminates a permutation traversal when the retained subset's performance drops below τ·V(E), eliminating high-variance steps from collapsed models and halving pruning cost; (2) Router-Guided Importance Sampling (RGIS) parameterizes a Plackett-Luce model with empirical gating activation probabilities to preferentially sample permutations where important experts appear early, with importance weights ensuring unbiasedness while reducing variance. Theoretical results provide an error bound for truncation (Theorem 1), prove RGIS is unbiased with variance reduction when activation correlates with true contribution (Theorem 2), and establish a performance preservation bound linking pruning loss to the threshold and estimation error (Theorem 3).

## Results

On the Qwen2-57B-A14B model with 25% expert pruning, Shapley-MoE achieves 63.05% average zero-shot accuracy (vs. 62.29% for the best baseline, Frequency) and 6.78 WikiText-2 perplexity (vs. 7.11 for Frequency). At 50% pruning on Qwen1.5-MoE-A2.7B, Shapley-MoE reaches 49.01% accuracy, outperforming the best baseline by 2.17 percentage points, and achieves 17.57 perplexity vs. 19.78 for Frequency. On DeepSeek-MoE-16B at 50% pruning, the perplexity gap widens to 3.17 (31.10 vs. 34.27). The pruning process itself takes only 36 minutes for Qwen1.5-MoE-A2.7B and 112 minutes for Qwen2-57B-A14B on a single A100 GPU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Shapley-MoE consistently outperforms all five baselines (Random, Gating Score, Frequency, Expert Trim, and the enumeration-based NAEE/CD-MoE) across every model and pruning ratio tested. The largest margins appear at 50% pruning: on Qwen1.5-MoE-A2.7B, Shapley-MoE beats Frequency (the strongest heuristic baseline) by 2.17% in zero-shot accuracy and by 2.21 in perplexity; on DeepSeek-MoE-16B, the perplexity advantage reaches 3.17. Against the enumeration-based methods (NAEE, CD-MoE) on Mixtral-8x7B, Shapley-MoE achieves lower perplexity at every pruning rate from 12.5% to 75% (e.g., 12.01 vs. 12.76 at 50%), while completing in 42 minutes versus the combinatorially infeasible enumeration cost. The advantage is most pronounced on larger models with more experts (Qwen3-30B-A3B with 128 experts per layer), where heuristic methods degrade more sharply. On domain-specific tasks (MMLU, GSM8K, HumanEval), Shapley-MoE also leads all baselines, e.g., 53.80 vs. 50.30 on MMLU and 31.02 vs. 28.89 on GSM8K at 25% pruning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the application of Shapley value from neuron-level importance (Neuron Shapley, Shapley-NAS) to expert-level importance in Mixture-of-Experts models, positioning itself between heuristic metric-based pruning (SEER-MoE's activation frequency, Expert Trim's routing scores) and impractical enumeration-based methods (NAEE, CD-MoE). The key methodological departure is the introduction of two MoE-specific sampling improvements—early truncation to skip collapsed coalitions and Router-Guided Importance Sampling using the Plackett-Luce model parameterized by gating activation probabilities—that exploit the structural properties of the gating mechanism to accelerate and stabilize Shapley estimation. Unlike prior Shapley-based pruning work that treats components as independent, this paper formalizes experts as cooperative game players whose contributions are interdependent through conditional activation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first theoretically grounded MoE expert pruning method with provable error bounds (Theorems 1–3) and a performance preservation guarantee, addressing a gap where existing methods rely on unvalidated heuristics or infeasible enumeration. Practically, reducing Qwen2-57B-A14B by 25% of experts with only 0.92 perplexity increase in 36 minutes makes high-quality MoE compression accessible without days of search. However, the additivity assumption in Theorem 3 is strong and may not hold for highly interactive expert configurations, and performance degrades substantially at 50–75% pruning ratios, leaving high-ratio lossless pruning as an open problem. The approach's scalability to MoE models with thousands of experts (e.g., DeepSeek-V3's 256 experts per layer) remains to be validated.
