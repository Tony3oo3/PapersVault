## Problem

Existing molecular LLMs suffer from two fundamental limitations: pretraining on PubChem captions that are imbalanced in coverage (nearly omitting polarity and electrophilicity) and coarse-grained in detail (averaging only ~19 words), and molecular representation strategies that either use SMILES with shared tokenizers causing modality confusion or GNNs that extract only atomic-level features without capturing hierarchical structural information.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251328/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model jointly consumes a 1D SELFIES string (tokenized with a dedicated vocabulary where each bracketed atom group is a distinct token, separate from the natural language vocabulary) and a 2D molecule graph (encoded into three hierarchical token levels: atomic, functional group, and molecule-level). Outputs span molecular text descriptions, binary property classifications (ROC-AUC), regression values (MAE in Hartree), and generated molecules as SELFIES strings for caption-guided generation, reagent prediction, forward reaction prediction, and retrosynthesis.

## Method

KnowMol introduces two complementary innovations. First, the KnowMol-100K dataset provides 100K molecules with four-level annotations (atomic composition, functional groups via deterministic BRICS matching in RDKit, structural construction, and six physicochemical properties) generated by a pipeline combining PubChem metadata, RDKit, and GPT-4o, yielding ~392-word descriptions versus PubChem's ~19. Second, the chemically-informative representation replaces SMILES with SELFIES using a modality-specific vocabulary and augments the GIN graph encoder (5-layer, hidden dim 300, from MoleculeSTM) with a parameter-free hierarchical pooling scheme: BRICS-identified functional groups undergo local atom pooling to produce functional-group tokens, which are then globally pooled into a single molecule-level token, with all three levels projected via separate single-layer MLPs into the Vicuna-7B LLM's embedding space. Training proceeds in two LoRA stages: pretraining on 200K instruction pairs (multi-round question answering and description-guided molecule generation) for 5 epochs, followed by task-specific fine-tuning.

## Results

On molecular property prediction (classification, MoleculeNet), KnowMol achieves ROC-AUC of 85.9 (BACE), 69.2 (BBBP), 81.8 (HIV), 61.5 (MUV), and 68.7 (Tox21), surpassing all LLM-based generalist baselines. On QM9 regression, KnowMol attains a mean MAE of 0.0030 hartree (HOMO 0.0028, LUMO 0.0029, Δε 0.0034), outperforming UniMoT (0.0049) by ~39%. On forward reaction prediction, KnowMol reaches 75.2% exact match and 0.986 BLEU, exceeding UniMoT (61.1% exact, 0.980 BLEU) by 14.1 points in exact match. On molecule captioning (ChEBI-20), KnowMol achieves BLEU-4 of 0.595 and ROUGE-2 of 0.601, edging out UniMoT (0.583, 0.584) while trailing slightly on METEOR (0.683 vs 0.703).

## Baselines & Comparisons

KnowMol consistently outperforms all LLM-based generalist baselines (InstructMol, HIGHT, Mol-Instructions, MolCA) across every evaluated task. Against the strongest generalist competitor UniMoT, KnowMol wins on QM9 regression (MAE 0.0030 vs 0.0049), forward reaction prediction (exact match 75.2% vs 61.1%), retrosynthesis (59.8% vs 47.8%), and molecule captioning BLEU-4 (0.595 vs 0.583), though UniMoT retains a slight edge on METEOR for captioning (0.703 vs 0.683). On MoleculeNet classification, KnowMol matches or exceeds the best specialist model Uni-Mol on BACE (85.9 vs 85.7) and HIV (81.8 vs 80.8), but trails on MUV (61.5 vs 82.1) and Tox21 (68.7 vs 78.1), where specialist 3D models retain an advantage. For molecule generation, KnowMol surpasses InstructMol by 21.6 points in exact match on forward reaction prediction (75.2% vs 53.6%) and by 19.1 points on retrosynthesis (59.8% vs 40.7%), while HIGHT lags further behind at 29.3% and 20.2% respectively. The ablation study confirms that both the multi-level annotation data and the hierarchical representation contribute additively, with the full pipeline (MRQA+DGMG + HR + SELFIES) yielding the best exact match of 75.2% versus 50.9% for the PubChem-caption baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the InstructMol architecture (GNN encoder + projection layer + LLM) but departs from it in two fundamental ways: replacing SMILES with SELFIES and introducing a hierarchical graph encoder that produces atomic, functional-group, and molecule-level tokens via BRICS-based pooling. Conceptually, it positions itself against HIGHT and UniMoT, which also sought to improve molecular representation but relied on heavier machinery (VQ-VAE, Q-Former) requiring separate pretraining stages; KnowMol argues that a parameter-free hierarchical encoding combined with a substantially higher-quality multi-level annotation dataset (KnowMol-100K) is both simpler and more effective. The dataset contribution extends the line of work on LLM-augmented molecule-text data (MolReGPT, 3D-MoLM, L+M-24) by moving beyond PubChem captions to a four-level annotation pipeline leveraging RDKit, BRICS, and GPT-4o.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The KnowMol-100K dataset addresses a widely acknowledged but under-addressed bottleneck in Mol-LLM research: the coarse, imbalanced nature of PubChem captions that limits molecular understanding. The finding that a simple BRICS+pooling hierarchical encoder (no extra parameters) can outperform VQ-VAE or Q-Former-based tokenizers is practically valuable for the community seeking efficient molecular representation. However, the work is limited to 2D representations, relies on GPT-4o for a significant portion of annotations (introducing potential systematic biases), and the evaluation is confined to a single 7B backbone (Vicuna), leaving open whether the gains transfer to larger or differently-architected LLMs. Future work on 3D molecular integration and using KnowMol itself as a data generator are natural next steps.
