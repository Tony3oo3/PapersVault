## Problem

LLMs have been evaluated on planning tasks in natural language, but almost exclusively on unconstrained problems, leaving their ability to respect temporal constraints—critical for safety-sensitive deployment—unassessed. Existing constrained planning benchmarks either lack a natural language interface, require manual curation of tasks, or cannot be extended to new domains, making principled and scalable evaluation of LLM constrained planning infeasible.

## Input / Output

The system takes a PDDL domain file, an unconstrained initial-state/goal pair, and a user-specified constraint count as input, and produces a fully grounded constrained planning problem rendered in natural language via compositional atom-level templates (no per-instance NL annotation needed). For verification, it accepts an LLM-generated action sequence, maps it to PDDL actions (using edit-distance matching for format deviations), simulates it over the LiftedTCORE-compiled problem, and returns a three-way verdict: invalid, suboptimal, or optimal (by comparing plan length to the precomputed optimal cost).

## Method

LEXICON's core is a symbolic reasoning engine with two modules. The constraint generator first solves the unconstrained problem with the SymK planner, then analyzes the state transitions of the optimal plan to sample literals that, when wrapped in temporal operators (Always, Sometime, SometimeBefore, SometimeAfter, AtMostOnce), force a detour and increase optimal cost while preserving solvability and mutual non-redundancy (checked against domain axioms). The verifier compiles all temporal constraints away using a lifted variant of TCORE, producing an equivalent unconstrained PDDL problem that SymK can solve to yield a formally verified optimal cost; LLM plans are then simulated over this compiled problem and checked for validity and optimality. A separate PDDL-to-NL translator recursively maps quantified goal formulas and constraint expressions into natural language by combining predefined per-atom templates with handcrafted domain-level descriptions. The entire pipeline is extensible: adding a new domain requires only a PDDL file, an automated state-goal sampler, and NL templates for each atom, with no code changes to the reasoning engine.

## Results

Across five domains (BabyAI, Blocksworld, Logistics, Sokoban, AlfWorld) with 150 problems each and constraint counts of {1, 3, 5, 7, 10}, reasoning LLMs show sharp performance degradation as constraints increase. For example, OpenAI o3 achieves 76% optimal-plan accuracy on Blocksworld with 1 constraint but drops to 0% at 10 constraints. Non-reasoning LLMs (GPT-4.1 CoT, DeepSeek V3, Gemini 2.0 Pro, Claude 3.7 Sonnet without thinking) fail to produce even a single optimal plan on problems with more than one constraint, with best-case valid-plan rates of only 16–26% at 1 constraint. LEXICON's problem generation and verification pipeline runs roughly one order of magnitude faster than LLM-based planning, enabling real-time evaluation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates five reasoning LLMs (DeepSeek R1, OpenAI o3, Gemini 2.5 Pro, Claude 3.7 Sonnet with extended thinking, GPT-5) against four non-reasoning LLMs (GPT-4.1 CoT, DeepSeek V3, Gemini 2.0 Pro, Claude 3.7 Sonnet without thinking) on LEXICON-generated constrained planning problems. Reasoning models substantially outperform non-reasoning models: at 1 constraint, o3 reaches 76% optimal on Blocksworld versus GPT-4.1 CoT's 3%, while at 10 constraints all reasoning models drop to 0–10% optimal and non-reasoning models remain at 0%. Among reasoning models, o3 and Claude 3.7 Sonnet (with thinking) are generally the strongest, while GPT-5 and Gemini 2.5 Pro show weaker constrained-planning performance. All LLMs fall far short of the formal SymK planner, which by construction achieves 100% optimal plans, confirming that current LLMs do not yet match symbolic planners on constrained planning. The paper also notes that reasoning models' soundness degrades on longer plans due to precondition violations, state hallucination, and constraint misinterpretation, despite their ability to exhibit bilevel planning, backtracking, and self-reflection behaviours.

## Relation to Prior Work

LEXICON builds on the PDDL3.0 formalism for qualitative state-trajectory constraints (Gerevini et al., 2009) and the TCORE compilation framework for reducing constrained problems to unconstrained ones, while drawing on existing planning environments (BabyAI, Blocksworld, Logistics, Sokoban, AlfWorld) as its substrate. It departs from prior NL planning benchmarks (PlanBench, BALROG, ACPBench) by making temporal constraints the central evaluation axis, and from constrained planning benchmarks (Safety Gym, TravelPlanner, NaturalPlan) by providing a natural-language interface, automated constraint generation, and suite extensibility. The key conceptual departure is the task-aware constraint generator, which uses the optimal unconstrained plan to synthesize constraints that provably increase problem difficulty while preserving solvability and non-redundancy—something no prior benchmark achieves in an automated, scalable manner.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LEXICON fills a genuine gap: prior benchmarks either lacked constraints or lacked NL interfaces and extensibility, making it impossible to systematically evaluate whether LLMs can adhere to safety-relevant constraints in a principled, scalable way. The finding that even frontier reasoning models (GPT-5, o3, R1) drop to near-zero optimal planning accuracy at 10 constraints, while LEXICON generates and verifies problems an order of magnitude faster than the LLMs solve them, provides a concrete, reproducible measure of the current gap between LLM planning and symbolic planners. Open questions include whether the observed performance cliff reflects a fundamental limitation of autoregressive sequence generation for compositional constraint satisfaction, and whether extending to partially observable environments and action-level constraints (as the authors note) will reveal additional failure modes relevant to real-world deployment.
