## Problem

Maximum marginal likelihood estimation (MMLE) in latent variable models requires computing intractable integrals over latent variables. Existing particle-based methods (PGD, IPLA) that approximate these integrals via unadjusted Langevin dynamics are constrained to use a single shared step-size for both parameter and latent-variable updates, and they do not provide a mechanism to estimate the marginal likelihood itself—preventing their use for model selection. This paper addresses both limitations by introducing a Jarzynski-weighted correction that decouples the two time-scales and yields on-the-fly marginal likelihood estimates.

## Input / Output

The method takes observed data y, a joint potential U(θ, x) = −log p_θ(x, y), a particle count N, separate step-sizes h (for ULA particle updates) and γ (for parameter updates via a user-chosen first-order optimizer), and an ESS threshold C for resampling. It produces the MMLE parameter θ*, a running estimate of the log marginal likelihood log p_θ(y) (via cumulative Jarzynski weights, with resampling-aware bookkeeping), and a posterior particle cloud {X_K^i} with normalized weights. Unlike PGD or SOUL, the marginal likelihood estimate is available at every iteration without additional computation, enabling Bayes-factor-based model selection during training.

## Method

JALA-EM runs N particles through the unadjusted Langevin algorithm on the time-varying potential U(θ_k, ·), while simultaneously updating θ_k via a generic first-order optimizer (SGD in theory, adaptive optimizers in practice). The key correction is a Jarzynski factor e^{A_k} accumulated per particle, which exactly compensates for the bias introduced by the time-dependence of the potential, so that the weighted average of ∇_θ U(θ_k, X_k^i) with normalized weights w_k^i = e^{A_k^i}/Σ_j e^{A_k^j} is an unbiased estimator of the marginal-likelihood gradient (Fisher's identity). This places the scheme in the SMC framework: particles are reweighted rather than resampled from a proposal, and systematic resampling is triggered only when the effective sample size drops below a threshold. The nonasymptotic analysis under PL and strong-convexity conditions shows convergence with N = O(ε^{-1}) particles and k = O(log ε^{-1}) iterations, without requiring the parameter step-size γ to vanish—decoupling accuracy from convergence speed in a way that PGD/IPLA bounds do not permit.

## Results

On the Bayesian neural network (MNIST multi-class, digits 2/4/7/9), JALA-EM achieves 9.24% (±0.93%) misclassification error with D_h=40 hidden units and 7.20% (±0.81%) with D_h=512, compared to PGD at 4.74% and 4.98% respectively, and SOUL at 9.20% and 8.02%. For Bayesian model selection (error model, d_y=500, d_x=8, N=50, K=250), JALA-EM correctly identifies the true generating model in 100% of 100 trials when the true model is Gaussian and 99% when it is Student-t. On the Wisconsin Breast Cancer logistic regression task, JALA-EM matches or outperforms PGD and SOUL in wall-clock time to reach the same parameter estimate, while maintaining posterior fidelity at N=10 where PGD and SOUL degrade noticeably.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against PGD (Kuntz et al., 2023), JALA-EM matches or outperforms in wall-clock convergence speed on the Wisconsin logistic regression task while reaching the same parameter estimate; however, PGD achieves lower BNN misclassification error (4.74% vs 9.24% at D_h=40), though it cannot estimate marginal likelihoods. Against SOUL (De Bortoli et al., 2021), JALA-EM is comparable in BNN predictive performance (9.24% vs 9.20% at D_h=40; 7.20% vs 8.02% at D_h=512, where JALA-EM actually wins), and maintains superior posterior fidelity at low particle counts (N=10) on Wisconsin where SOUL's KDE degrades. A key differentiator is that JALA-EM uniquely supports on-the-fly marginal likelihood estimation, enabling model selection (100%/99% correct identification) and model order selection (matching or outperforming OLS+BIC), capabilities neither PGD nor SOUL provide. The paper also notes that JALA-EM allows independent step-sizes for θ and x updates and is compatible with adaptive optimisers, whereas PGD and SOUL are tied to plain gradient updates in their theoretical guarantees.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Jarzynski-equality-based sampler of Carbone et al. (2023) for training energy-based models, repurposing it as the core E-step mechanism within an EM framework for latent variable models. It positions itself against the interacting-particle methods (PGD, IPLA, SFLA) and the MCMC-based SOUL approach, departing from them in two key ways: (i) it uses a weighted-ensemble of unadjusted Langevin dynamics with SMC resampling rather than interacting or MCMC particles, and (ii) it decouples the step-sizes for parameter and latent-variable updates, permitting adaptive optimizers. The nonasymptotic convergence analysis leverages the biased-SGD framework of Demidovich et al. (2023), and the SMC weighting/resampling structure follows Del Moral et al. (2006).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The principal practical contribution is the ability to estimate marginal likelihoods on-the-fly during training, which none of the competing particle-based MMLE methods (PGD, SOUL, SFLA) support, thereby enabling Bayesian model selection (error-model and model-order selection) as a byproduct of the fitting procedure. The theoretical result that convergence requires only N = O(1/ε) particles without driving the step-size to zero is a clean decoupling absent from PGD/IPLA analyses. Open questions include the lack of a resampling-aware convergence proof, the vulnerability to weight degeneracy in high-dimensional latent spaces, and whether the approach scales to truly large LVMs (e.g., deep generative models) where SMC particle counts become prohibitive.
