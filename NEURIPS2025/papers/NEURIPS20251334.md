## Problem

Temporal convolutional networks typically use very short kernels (size 3) because long kernels with explicit per-timestep weights cause training instability and high memory costs, while MLP-based kernel parameterization reduces parameters but increases compute. No prior method provides long, stable temporal kernels that can be resampled at arbitrary temporal rates without retraining, which is critical for event-based data where the effective frame rate is a free parameter.

## Input / Output

The network consumes event camera data discretized into 4D tensors of shape (2, H, W, T) with two polarity channels, where T is determined by the chosen timebin size (e.g., 10 ms). Because the architecture is fully causal, it operates online with circular buffering and produces outputs at every timebin. Depending on the task head, outputs are 10-class gesture logits (DVS128), 2D gaze point coordinates via a CenterNet head (AIS 2024), or per-pixel heatmaps with box regression for cars and pedestrians (Prophesee GEN4). A key property is that the bin size Δτ can be changed at inference (e.g., from 10 ms to 5 or 20 ms) by simply re-discretizing the polynomial basis, with no weight updates required.

## Method

PLEIADES replaces explicit temporal convolution weights with a weighted sum of Jacobi orthogonal polynomials P_n^(α,β)(τ), where the trainable parameters are the expansion coefficients γ_{cd,n} (one per input-output channel pair and polynomial degree n, up to degree 4 with α=β=−0.25). Discretization is performed by integrating the continuous kernel over each timebin via the antiderivative of the polynomial basis, yielding discrete kernel values that depend on the bin size Δτ. This structure enables two key advantages: (1) temporal resampling at inference by re-evaluating the discrete polynomial basis at a new Δτ without any fine-tuning, and (2) an intermediate polynomial subspace that makes the einsum contraction path flexible—either convolve the basis with the input first then contract with coefficients, or generate kernels first then convolve—allowing opt_einsum to select the memory- or compute-optimal ordering. The full architecture is a (1+2)D causal spatiotemporal CNN with depthwise-separable convolutions, causal group normalization after temporal layers, batch normalization after spatial layers, and a CenterNet detection head for detection tasks.

## Results

PLEIADES achieves 99.59% test accuracy (100% with a simple majority filter) on DVS128 hand gesture recognition with only 192K parameters, 99.58% p10 / 94.94% p3 on the AIS 2024 eye tracking challenge with 277K parameters, and 0.556 mAP on the Prophesee GEN4 1-megapixel automotive detection dataset with 576K parameters at 100 FPS. All three results represent state-of-the-art by large margins, with the detection result exceeding the previous best (GET-T, 0.484 mAP) by 7.2 mAP points while using roughly 38× fewer parameters and 16.7× fewer MACs/sec.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PLEIADES outperforms every baseline on all three benchmarks. On DVS128, it surpasses the closest competitor Conv(1+2)D [Pei et al., 2024] by 0.42 points (99.59% vs 99.17%) while using fewer parameters (192K vs 196K), and beats ANN-Rollouts [Kugele et al., 2020] by 2.43 points with 2.6× fewer MACs/sec. On the AIS 2024 eye tracking challenge, PLEIADES+CenterNet achieves 99.58% p10, exceeding MambaPupil (99.42%) and Conv(1+2)D (99.00%) while using only 277K parameters versus 1.1M for Conv(1+2)D. On Prophesee GEN4 detection, PLEIADES reaches 0.556 mAP, beating GET-T [Peng et al., 2023] (0.484) by 7.2 points, S5-ViT-B [Zubic et al., 2024] (0.478) by 7.8 points, and both RED and Gray-RetinaNet [Perot et al., 2020] (0.43) by 12.6 points, all while operating at 100 FPS versus 20 FPS for the baselines and using 40–57× fewer parameters.

## Relation to Prior Work

PLEIADES extends the (1+2)D causal spatiotemporal network of Pei et al. (2024) by replacing its explicit temporal convolution kernels with structured kernels parameterized as weighted sums of Jacobi orthogonal polynomials. This connects to the HiPPO/LMU line of work (Voelker et al., 2019; Gu et al., 2020) that uses orthogonal polynomial bases in recurrent state-space models, but departs from S4/Mamba-style architectures by retaining explicit finite-window convolutions rather than infinite-impulse-response approximations, and by applying the structure to multi-channel spatiotemporal data rather than 1D sequences. It also generalizes SLAYER's fixed-form spike-response kernel into a fully trainable polynomial-parameterized convolution, and contrasts with MLP-parameterized continuous kernels (CKConv, Hyena) by offering a closed-form discretization that supports arbitrary temporal resampling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ability to train at one temporal bin size and infer at another (halved or doubled) without any fine-tuning is a practically valuable property for event-based systems where the effective frame rate may vary with scene dynamics or hardware constraints. The 30–50× parameter reduction over prior SOTA on Prophesee GEN4 (0.576M vs. 18–32M) while simultaneously improving mAP makes this architecture highly relevant for edge and neuromorphic deployment. The acknowledged limitation—O(kHW) circular-buffer memory for long kernels—points toward a natural future direction of reformulating the polynomial projections as recurrent state updates (aligning with SSMs), which could collapse memory to O(nHW) while preserving long temporal context. The counterintuitive finding that higher polynomial degrees degrade low-latency performance (degree 4 optimal, degree 10 worse than free kernels) highlights an underexplored trade-off between expressivity and generalization in structured temporal filters.
