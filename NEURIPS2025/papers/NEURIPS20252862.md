## Problem

Distributionally robust reinforcement learning has been extensively studied for discounted-reward and finite-horizon settings, but the average-reward case—critical for applications requiring stable long-term performance in robotics, operations research, and healthcare—remains largely unexplored. No finite-sample convergence guarantees existed for learning distributionally robust average-reward MDPs, and a fundamental modeling challenge arises: conventional uncertainty sets can contain non-unichain MDPs that invalidate the standard Bellman equations.

## Input / Output

The algorithms consume n i.i.d. transition samples per state-action pair from a generative model (simulator) of the nominal MDP, along with a chosen divergence type (KL or f_k) and uncertainty radius δ that define the SA-rectangular adversarial uncertainty set. They output a stationary deterministic policy and an estimate of the optimal robust average reward, with the key practical property that neither algorithm requires prior knowledge of the mixing time t_mix or other model-specific parameters. The sample complexity scales as Õ(|S||A|t_mix²ε⁻²) for ε-accuracy in both policy and value estimation.

## Method

The paper proposes two algorithms achieving the first finite-sample guarantees for DR-AMDPs. The first (Algorithm 2) reduces the average-reward problem to a DR discounted MDP by calibrating the discount factor as γ = 1 − 1/√n, then applies value iteration to the empirical DR Bellman equation; the resulting value is rescaled by 1/√n to recover the average reward. The second (Algorithm 3, Anchored DR-AMDP) avoids the reduction by modifying the entire uncertainty set: each adversarial transition kernel is convexified with a uniform anchoring state s₀ at strength ξ = 1/√n, which stabilizes the controlled kernels and allows direct solution of the empirical DR average-reward Bellman equation. A critical technical contribution is establishing that under uniform ergodicity of the nominal kernel and a sufficiently small δ (bounded by a constant times p_∧/m_∨²), all MDPs within the uncertainty set remain uniformly ergodic with minorization time at most 2t_minorize, ensuring the Bellman equations are well-posed. The analysis leverages the Doeblin minorization condition, span semi-norm bounds on value functions, and Bernstein-type concentration inequalities for the DR functional to derive the Õ(|S||A|t_mix²ε⁻²) rate, improving the effective horizon dependence from (1−γ)⁻⁴ to (1−γ)⁻² for the intermediate DR-DMDP bound.

## Results

The paper establishes the first finite-sample convergence guarantees for distributionally robust average-reward RL. Both proposed algorithms (Reduction to DR-DMDP and Anchored DR-AMDP) achieve a sample complexity of Õ(|S||A|t_mix²ε⁻²) for estimating the optimal robust average reward and policy within ε accuracy, under KL and f_k-divergence uncertainty sets. For the intermediate DR-DMDP problem, the bound is Õ(|S||A|t_mix²(1-γ)⁻²ε⁻²), improving the effective horizon dependence from (1-γ)⁻⁴ to (1-γ)⁻² compared to prior DR-DMDP results. Numerical experiments on the Hard MDP family confirm the predicted n^{-1/2} convergence rate for both algorithms under both KL and χ² divergence.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper, so comparisons are made against existing sample complexity bounds in the literature rather than empirical baselines. For DR-DMDPs, the new bound Õ(|S||A|t_mix²(1-γ)⁻²ε⁻²) improves upon the prior state-of-the-art Õ(|S||A|(1-γ)⁻⁴ε⁻²) from Shi and Chi [32] and Wang et al. [43] by reducing the effective horizon dependence from (1-γ)⁻⁴ to (1-γ)⁻², matching the optimal rate known in the non-robust setting [37]. For DR-AMDPs, the Õ(|S||A|t_mix²ε⁻²) rate is the first finite-sample guarantee in this setting; prior work by Wang et al. [44,45,46] established convergence of robust average-reward algorithms but without sample complexity bounds. The rate is optimal in its dependence on |S||A| and ε, though it carries an extra factor of t_mix (rather than t_mix²) compared to the non-robust average-reward bound Õ(|S||A|t_mixε⁻²) of Wang et al. [38] and Zurek and Chen [57], reflecting the additional statistical cost of distributional robustness.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two well-developed lines of work: distributionally robust RL (DR-RL) for discounted rewards, where prior results (Wang et al. [43], Shi and Chi [32]) achieve Õ(|S||A|(1-γ)^(-4)ε^(-2)), and non-robust average-reward RL, where optimal rates of Õ(|S||A|t_mix ε^(-2)) were recently established (Wang et al. [38], Zurek and Chen [57]). The DR-AMDP combination had no finite-sample guarantees before this work; prior robust average-reward studies (Wang et al. [44, 45, 46]) provided only asymptotic convergence. The paper builds on Iyengar's DR Bellman framework and the anchoring technique from Fruit et al. [10] and Zurek and Chen [58], adapting both to the robust setting while introducing a stability-sensitive analysis via the Doeblin/minorization condition.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first finite-sample convergence guarantee for DR-AMDPs, with a rate of Õ(|S||A|t_mix^2 ε^(-2)) that is optimal in |S||A| and ε. The t_mix^2 dependence (versus t_mix in the non-robust case) quantifies the statistical price of adversarial robustness, and the requirement that δ be sufficiently small to preserve uniform ergodicity across the uncertainty set is a practically important constraint. Key limitations include the tabular/generative-model setting, the uniform ergodicity assumption on the nominal kernel, and the small-δ requirement; open questions include extending to weakly communicating or multichain MDPs and broader uncertainty sets (ℓ_p-balls, Wasserstein). For practitioners in robotics, operations research, or healthcare where long-run average performance under model mismatch is critical, this provides the first rigorous sample-efficiency roadmap.
