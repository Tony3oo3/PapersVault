## Problem

Existing molecular LLMs, despite success in task transfer, misinterpret molecular structures, produce incorrect property predictions, and fail to provide meaningful explanations or reasoning. Their instruction datasets are restricted to specific tasks rather than general molecular knowledge, and reliance on a single encoder type (2D or 3D) limits accurate structural understanding. The paper addresses how to build a molecular LLM that grasps general molecular features across structural, chemical, and biological levels while exhibiting explainability and step-by-step reasoning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250908/cropped_page5_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a molecule in two simultaneous representations—a 2D molecular graph (processed by MoleculeSTM) and a 3D point cloud (processed by UniMol)—alongside a free-form text instruction prompt. The 2D and 3D embeddings are fused before being projected into the LLM's token space via a Q-Former, so the LLM never sees raw SMILES strings. Outputs are natural-language responses that explain structural features, chemical properties, or biological functions with explicit rationales, as well as discrete property predictions (e.g., permeability class) accompanied by chain-of-thought justifications.

## Method

Mol-LLaMA introduces two core components. First, a 284k-sample instruction dataset (Mol-LLaMA-Instruct) built with GPT-4o using three hierarchically organized data types—detailed structural descriptions, structure-to-feature relationship explanations, and multi-turn comprehensive conversations—that progress from structural to chemical to biological features to cultivate reasoning and explainability. Second, a 2D-3D blending module that applies sequential self-attention and cross-attention to fuse the complementary embeddings from MoleculeSTM (bond connectivity) and UniMol (spatial geometry), followed by a Q-Former projector (initialized from SciBERT) that maps the unified representation into the LLM's embedding space while preserving permutation invariance. Training proceeds in two stages: (1) molecular representation learning with multi-objective alignment (contrastive, matching, and grounded generation) to train the blending module and Q-Former while freezing both encoders, and (2) end-to-end multi-modal instruction tuning with LoRA on the LLM backbone (Llama-2-7B-chat or Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct), again with encoders frozen.

## Results

On the general molecular understanding evaluation (100 unseen PubChem molecules), Mol-LLaMA (Llama-3.1-8B) achieves relative scores of 1.125 (structural), 1.251 (chemical), and 1.744 (biological) overall, all exceeding GPT-4o (score = 1.0), while the strongest molecular LLM baseline 3D-MoLM† reaches only 0.749/0.875/1.191. On the MoleculeQA benchmark, Mol-LLaMA attains 70.76% total accuracy (Llama-3.1-8B), outperforming 3D-MoLM† (69.10%) and Mol-Instructions (68.45%). On zero-shot PAMPA permeability prediction, Mol-LLaMA achieves 63.55% accuracy (default) and 72.48% with task-specific information, surpassing GPT-4o (48.65% default) and all molecular LLM baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Mol-LLaMA outperforms all molecular LLM baselines (3D-MoLM, LLaMo, Mol-Instructions, LlasMol) and GPT-4o across all three feature categories (structural, chemical, biological) in the general understanding evaluation, with the largest margin on biological features (1.744 vs. 1.191 for 3D-MoLM†, a 46% relative gap). On MoleculeQA, Mol-LLaMA (70.76%) edges out 3D-MoLM† (69.10%) and Mol-Instructions (68.45%) on the Llama-3.1-8B architecture, while on Llama2-7B it leads 3D-MoLM (63.31%) by 4.7 points. On zero-shot PAMPA, Mol-LLaMA (63.55% default, 72.48% w/ task info) surpasses GPT-4o (48.65%) by 15+ points and the next-best molecular LLM 3D-MoLM† (46.93%) by 17 points, while also achieving the highest fidelity (0.804) and helpfulness (0.829) scores. On BBBP, Mol-LLaMA (59.54% default, Llama-3.1-8B) trails GPT-4o (60.82%) but outperforms all other molecular LLM baselines. The 2D-3D blending module provides a consistent 5–10% improvement over using either 2D or 3D encoders alone, and the full instruction dataset (all three data types) balances general feature understanding with the ability to handle diverse user requests.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the multi-modal instruction tuning paradigm (established in vision-language models like BLIP-2 and LLaVA) to the molecular domain, building directly on prior molecular LLMs such as MolCA, 3D-MoLM, LLaMo, and Mol-Instructions. It departs from these works in two key ways: (1) replacing the single-encoder design with a 2D-3D cross-attention blending module that integrates MoleculeSTM (2D graph) and UniMol (3D point cloud) representations, and (2) replacing task-specific public instruction datasets with a hierarchically structured dataset (Mol-LLaMA-Instruct) organized around three data types that follow the natural structure→chemical→biological causal chain. The Q-Former projector and LoRA-based instruction tuning follow standard practice from the vision-language literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine gap in molecular LLMs: existing models are trained on narrow, task-specific instruction data and lack the explainability and step-by-step reasoning needed for a practical molecular analysis assistant. The hierarchical data design and the 2D-3D blending module are reusable components that other molecular LLM efforts can adopt. However, several open questions remain: the instruction data is generated by GPT-4o (introducing potential systematic biases), evaluation also uses GPT-4o as judge (creating circularity), the qualitative evaluation covers only 100 molecules, and the 'general-purpose' claim is tested on a limited set of tasks (PAMPA, BBBP, MoleculeQA). Whether the approach scales to larger molecular spaces, proteins, or multi-molecule reasoning remains unexplored.
