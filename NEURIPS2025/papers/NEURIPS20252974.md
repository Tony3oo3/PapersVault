## Problem

RNN-based language models compress variable-length contexts into fixed-dimensional states, creating an information bottleneck that prevents random access to historical content. While augmenting RNNs with attention can restore this access, existing sparse attention methods (NSA, MoBA) suffer from inaccurate chunk selection because they estimate token-to-chunk relevance using unnormalized token-to-token logits that are not differentiable with respect to chunk-level outcomes. The paper addresses the trilemma of simultaneously achieving length generalization, flexible long-range random access, and linear-time efficiency in RNN architectures.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a text sequence tokenized into a 50,280-token vocabulary and produces next-token predictions via a 370M (or 2.7B) parameter autoregressive decoder. Inputs are partitioned into fixed-size chunks (S=64 tokens) that are independently encoded by a bidirectional Transformer encoder to form chunk memories used for selection. During inference, the full token-level KV cache is offloaded to CPU memory while only compact chunk-level key representations remain on GPU; at each decoding step, only the KV pairs of the top-K selected chunks (K=8) are swapped back to GPU, yielding a nearly constant memory footprint regardless of context length.

## Method

HSA introduces a two-stage hierarchical attention that enables end-to-end learning of token-to-chunk relevance: in the first stage, each token performs standard softmax attention over the tokens within each individually selected chunk (token-level), and in the second stage, the resulting chunk-level outputs are fused via stick-breaking weights derived from learnable token-to-chunk scores (chunk-level). Because these chunk weights participate directly in the forward computation, backpropagation adjusts them based on the entire chunk's contribution to next-token prediction, achieving chunk-aware learning that existing methods lack. The architecture (RAMba) embeds HSA layers every 8 Mamba-2 layers in the upper decoder, shares a single chunk selection across all HSA layers to minimize GPU-CPU swaps, and uses a hardware-aligned Triton kernel where each GPU thread processes one token's query against its K selected chunks in parallel. A memory reset mechanism periodically zeros the RNN state during training to prevent shortcut learning and improve length extrapolation, while a bidirectional chunk encoder (5.4% of total parameters) extracts context-representative features for more accurate selection.

## Results

RAMba (370M, pre-trained on 4K contexts) achieves the best perplexity across all three long-range language modeling benchmarks at both in-domain (4K) and out-of-domain (16K, 64K) lengths, e.g., 17.63 PPL on PG19 at 4K and 17.11 at 64K. On passkey retrieval, RAMba achieves perfect accuracy at 64M context length—16,000× the pre-training length—while all baselines drop to near zero around 64K. On RULER retrieval tasks at 64K context, RAMba (w/ memory reset) scores 85.07 on Single NIAH and 55.22 on Multi-query NIAH, substantially outperforming NSA (4.48 and 0.00) and Mamba-2 (10.45 and 0.00). In terms of efficiency, HSA is 3× faster than NSA and 5–25× faster than full attention for contexts ≥16K tokens, and RAMba maintains nearly constant GPU memory during inference with offloading enabled.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Transformer (YaRN full attention) baseline, RAMba is dramatically superior on out-of-domain lengths: at 64K context, the Transformer's PG19 perplexity explodes to >10⁴ while RAMba stays at 17.11, and on RULER at 64K the Transformer scores 0.00 on all retrieval sub-tasks versus RAMba's 85.07 on Single NIAH. Against Mamba-2, RAMba wins on in-domain perplexity (17.63 vs 17.92 on PG19) and on all retrieval tasks at extended lengths (e.g., 85.07 vs 10.45 on Single NIAH at 64K), though Mamba-2 retains a slight edge on Frequent Word Extraction at 256K (43.75 vs 37.50), reflecting its strength in sequential statistical tasks. Against NSA, RAMba consistently outperforms on both perplexity (17.11 vs 17.62 on PG19 at 64K) and retrieval accuracy (85.07 vs 4.48 on Single NIAH at 64K with memory reset), confirming that HSA's chunk-aware hierarchical learning yields more accurate chunk selection than NSA's token-to-token score approximation. On downstream summarization and QA tasks (Table 4), RAMba achieves the highest average score (33.64) versus Transformer (32.82) and Mamba-2 (31.03), with the largest gap on HotpotQA (22.30/30.53 EM/F1 vs 18.70/26.20 for Mamba-2). At the 2.7B scale, RAMba maintains its lead with 27.3 LongBench overall vs 26.8 for Mamba-2, and achieves 83.58/76.12/100.00/28.36 on RULER at 1M context where Mamba-2 scores 0.00 across all sub-tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Mamba-2 (a structured state-space model) by inserting a novel Hierarchical Sparse Attention (HSA) mechanism into specific layers, drawing on the stick-breaking attention formulation for chunk-level weighting. It departs from prior sparse attention methods like NSA and MoBA, which select chunks via token-to-token score aggregation (chunk-unaware), by introducing a two-stage hierarchical attention that enables end-to-end, chunk-aware learning of token-to-chunk relevance. The design is also inspired by GCA's retrieval-based attention but achieves per-token retrieval through a hardware-aligned Triton kernel rather than periodic retrieval, and differs from Combiner by preserving token-level information within chunks rather than compressing via max-pooling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RAMba demonstrates that RNN-based models can achieve near-perfect long-range retrieval (64M tokens) with nearly constant GPU memory via KV-cache offloading, addressing a trilemma (efficiency, length generalization, random access) that has resisted resolution. The finding that Mamba's recurrent state creates 'shortcuts' that degrade attention-based generalization, and that a simple memory-reset mechanism mitigates this, offers a practical insight for hybrid RNN-attention architectures. Open questions include whether precise chunk selection holds for extremely long contexts beyond 1M tokens on harder retrieval templates, and whether the approach scales effectively beyond 3B parameters, which the authors acknowledge as a limitation.
