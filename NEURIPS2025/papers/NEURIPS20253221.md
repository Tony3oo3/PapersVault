## Problem

Most RNN training relies on open-loop supervised settings with i.i.d. inputs, yet biological learning unfolds in closed-loop environments where an agent's outputs shape its future inputs. The learning dynamics that emerge under this feedback structure remain largely unexplored, and it is unclear whether closed-loop training induces fundamentally different trajectories, solution classes, or inductive biases compared to conventional open-loop training.

## Input / Output

In the primary double-integrator task, the RNN receives only the scalar position of a 2D mass (partial observability: velocity is hidden) and outputs a scalar acceleration command, requiring the network to internally represent velocity. In the multi-frequency tracking task, the RNN receives a 4D vector (cursor x,y positions plus target x,y positions) and outputs a 2D acceleration command. Training uses policy gradients in the closed-loop setting and supervised imitation of a teacher (RNN or LQR) in the open-loop setting, with episodes of T=50 steps (double integrator) or T=300 steps (tracking).

## Method

The paper develops an analytical framework by coupling the linear RNN (with rank-1 recurrent weights W=uv^T and linear activation) to the environment into a joint (2+N)-dimensional system matrix P, then reducing it to a 4D effective system P_eff governed by four scalar overlap parameters. Tracking the eigenvalues of P (rather than W alone) reveals three distinct learning stages: (1) a negative-position policy with an unstable dominant eigenvalue, (2) a plateau where the RNN builds an internal velocity representation, and (3) policy refinement once the system stabilizes. The key mechanistic insight is that Stage 2 dynamics are governed by a surrogate loss L_surrogate = α·L_∞ + (1−α)·L_2, interpolating between long-term asymptotic stability (L_∞) and short-term early-episode control (L_2), whose competing gradient directions produce the observed zig-zag eigenvalue trajectories and loss plateaus. The framework is validated on nonlinear RNNs, continuous-time variants, and a multi-frequency motor tracking task where stage-like acquisition of frequency components mirrors human learning trends.

## Results

The paper's primary contribution is analytical rather than benchmark-based. For the double-integrator task (N=100 neurons, T=50), closed-loop RNNs progress through three distinct stages: (1) a rapid loss drop via a negative-position (P-controller) policy, (2) a loss plateau during which the RNN builds an internal velocity representation, and (3) a final refinement phase with fast, non-oscillatory convergence. The closed-loop learning dynamics are captured by a surrogate loss L_surrogate = α·L_∞ + (1−α)·L_2, where L_∞ encodes long-term spectral stability and L_2 encodes short-term control; intermediate α values (0<α<1) reproduce the empirically observed zig-zag eigenvalue trajectories. On the multi-frequency tracking task, closed-loop RNNs exhibit stepwise loss drops aligned with sequential acquisition of frequencies ω₁<ω₂<ω₃<ω₄, and normalized acquisition times closely match those of human participants.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The central comparison is between closed-loop and open-loop training of architecturally identical RNNs. Both start and end in similar regions of the (k₁,k₂) effective-gain plane, but follow markedly different trajectories: the open-loop RNN exhibits a sharp closed-loop test-loss peak (a breakdown in control performance) while the closed-loop RNN shows a smooth three-stage progression with a plateau. This divergence is not task-specific and persists across the k-integrator family (Appendix A.6). For the multi-frequency tracking task, the paper compares RNN frequency-acquisition times against human data from Avraham & Mawase (bioRxiv 2025, [42]): both RNNs and humans acquire lower-frequency components (ω₁, ω₂) substantially faster than higher-frequency ones (ω₃, ω₄), indicating a shared inductive bias toward low-frequency-first learning. The analytical surrogate model (low-dimensional effective system P_eff) reproduces the full high-dimensional RNN learning dynamics with excellent quantitative agreement across all three stages, validating the rank-1 and linearization assumptions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the emerging line of analytical work on RNN learning dynamics—previously confined to open-loop, i.i.d.-input settings (Bordelon et al. 2025; Schuessler et al. 2020)—into the closed-loop regime where network outputs feed back into the environment. It leverages the low-rank connectivity structure observed in trained RNNs (Mastrogiuseppe & Ostojic 2018; Schuessler et al. 2020) and the unregularized policy-gradient LQR setup (Razin et al. 2024) to derive a tractable spectral characterization of the coupled agent-environment system. The key departure from prior work is the identification of a dual-objective structure (short-term policy improvement vs. long-term stability) that governs closed-loop learning and has no analogue in open-loop analyses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The three-stage decomposition (proportional control → world-model formation → refinement) provides a mechanistic account of why closed-loop RNN training exhibits loss plateaus, a phenomenon previously unexplained in the RNN literature. The finding that frequency acquisition times in a multi-frequency tracking task closely match human motor-learning data suggests the framework captures a genuine inductive bias relevant to biological learning. Key limitations include the restriction to linear RNNs, direct (non-sparse) gradient computation, and the fact that the reduced effective model is only spectrally equivalent within an episode; extending the analysis to nonlinear activations, sparse rewards, and approximate gradients remains an important open direction.
