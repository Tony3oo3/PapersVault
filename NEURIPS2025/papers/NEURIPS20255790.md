## Problem

Shampoo-family optimizers, which approximate a whitening metric from historical gradients via Kronecker factorization, suffer from three practical failures at scale: divergence when matrix inverses are cached beyond ~25 steps, update magnitudes that do not scale correctly with network width, and large parameter noise at high learning rates. Additionally, prior comparisons of adaptive optimizers on Transformer training have been narrow in scope, typically evaluating a single objective or a single training stage, making it difficult to assess robustness.

## Input / Output

SPlus consumes per-layer matrix-shaped gradients (G ∈ R^{m×n}) from standard backpropagation and the current model parameters, producing updated parameters each step. It operates only on 2D dense layers (the Transformer backbone); non-standard parameters such as token embeddings, classification heads, and patch-embedding convolutions receive a simpler sign-of-momentum update with a fixed constant scale. The method maintains per-layer Kronecker covariance factors (L, R), cached eigenvectors (Q_L, Q_R), momentum, and a second EMA parameter set, requiring roughly 3nm + 2(m²+n²) stored values per dense layer versus 3mn for Adam.

## Method

SPlus replaces Shampoo's cached matrix-inverse multiplication with instant-sign normalization: gradients are rotated into the historical eigenbasis via Q_L^T G Q_R, elementwise sign is applied, and the result is rotated back, yielding a hard spectral-norm bound of √(nm) that prevents divergence even when eigendecompositions are cached for 100+ steps. A symmetric shape-aware scaling factor of 2/(m+n) is applied to each layer's update, enabling the optimal learning rate to transfer across network widths without retuning. Finally, a lightweight iterate-averaging scheme maintains a second set of 'slow' parameters as an exponential moving average of the 'live' parameters, suppressing the parameter oscillation that whitening-based optimizers exhibit at high learning rates and unblocking faster convergence. The eigendecomposition is distributed across devices in a fully-sharded data-parallel setup, reducing the per-step cost of the most expensive operation by a factor of 1/num_devices.

## Results

SPlus matches Adam's validation performance within 44–58% of the gradient steps (0.439 average Steps-to-Adam across LLM, ViT, and DiT objectives) and within 62–83% of the wallclock time (0.617 average Time-to-Adam). The evaluation uses a 160M-parameter GPT-2-style Transformer trained for 10k steps from three checkpoints (init, 10k, 50k) on three objectives: autoregressive language modelling (OpenWebText), image classification (ImageNet ViT), and latent diffusion modelling (ImageNet DiT). SPlus outperforms all seven compared optimizers on both gradient-step and wallclock metrics simultaneously, with the largest per-objective gains on DiT (0.396 Steps-to-Adam) and LLM (0.419 Steps-to-Adam).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SPlus outperforms all seven baselines on both gradient steps and wallclock time. Against SOAP (the closest competitor), SPlus reduces Steps-to-Adam from 0.575 to 0.439 (24% fewer steps) and Time-to-Adam from 0.807 to 0.617 (24% less wallclock), with the gap widening on DiT (0.396 vs. 0.477). Schedule-Free Adam is competitive on wallclock (0.654 vs. 0.617) but trails SPlus on gradient steps (0.679 vs. 0.439), reflecting SPlus's more efficient per-step progress. Shampoo diverges entirely on ViT and DiT objectives and, even in its only stable setting (LLM-50K), requires 2.4× Adam's wallclock time due to frequent matrix inversions. Sophia fails to outperform Adam on any objective, and Muon cannot beat Adam on LLM training (>1.0 Steps-to-Adam). PSGD is consistently mid-pack (0.652 avg steps) but never approaches SPlus's efficiency. The key differentiator is that SPlus's instant-sign normalization enables stable training with eigendecomposition cached for 100+ steps, whereas Shampoo requires inversion every 10 steps to avoid divergence, making SPlus's wallclock advantage structural rather than merely a tuning artifact.

## Relation to Prior Work

SPlus builds directly on the Shampoo family of Kronecker-factorized whitening optimizers (Gupta et al., 2018) and the closely related SOAP method (Vyas et al., 2024), which also performs eigen-decomposition of the factor matrices. The key departure is replacing the historical eigenvalue-based normalization (the -1/4 power of eigenvalues) with an instantaneous sign operation in the eigenbasis, which introduces a hard spectral bound and eliminates the divergence mode caused by stale cached inverses. The shape-aware scaling (2/(m+n)) adapts the learning-rate transfer framework from Yang et al. (2023) to the whitening setting, and the iterate-averaging component draws on Polyak-Ruppert averaging as used in diffusion model training and reinforcement learning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that naive Shampoo diverges in over 50% of hyperparameter configurations is practically important, as it undermines a commonly-used optimizer's reliability and motivates the bounded-update design. SPlus achieves a 44–58% reduction in gradient steps and 62–83% reduction in wallclock time versus Adam across three Transformer objectives, with no additional forward/backward passes and no critical new hyperparameters. Key limitations include evaluation only on 160M-parameter Transformers, a single random seed, and ~60% more memory than Adam; open questions include whether the approach scales to billion-parameter models, generalizes to non-Transformer architectures, and whether the constant learning rate assumption (common in diffusion but less standard in LLM training) is essential to the gains.
