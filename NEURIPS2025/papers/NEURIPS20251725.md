## Problem

Real-time neural decoding requires models that are simultaneously accurate, low-latency for causal online inference, and generalizable to new subjects and tasks. RNNs meet the latency requirement but generalize poorly due to rigid time-binned inputs, while Transformer-based decoders generalize well but suffer from quadratic attention complexity that makes them computationally prohibitive for real-time or long-sequence settings. Hybrid SSM-attention architectures, which showed promise in language modelling, had not been explored for neural decoding.

## Input / Output

POSSM consumes individual spikes as variable-length token sequences (unit ID embedding + RoPE timestamp) within contiguous 50 ms chunks (20 ms also tested), with a hidden state carried across chunks for streaming inference. For the speech task, where only binned spike counts were available, it instead encodes normalized counts per multi-unit channel with value embeddings. Outputs are produced via cross-attention over the k=3 most recent hidden states, supporting continuous 2D velocity predictions, discrete character classification, and variable-length phoneme sequences, with the flexibility to predict at arbitrary timestamps including future lags of 50–100 ms beyond the current chunk.

## Method

POSSM pairs a POYO-style spike tokenizer (learnable unit embeddings + rotary position embeddings) with a PerceiverIO-inspired cross-attention encoder that compresses each variable-length 50 ms spike chunk into a single fixed-size latent vector using a learnable query. This latent is fed to a recurrent SSM backbone (S4D, GRU, or Mamba) whose hidden state is updated causally across consecutive chunks, yielding constant-time per-chunk inference. An output cross-attention module then queries the k most recent hidden states with timestamp- and session-encoded queries to produce behavioural predictions. For generalization, the paper introduces two finetuning strategies—Unit Identification (freezing all weights and learning only new unit/session embeddings, updating <1% of parameters) and Full Finetuning (gradual unfreezing after an initial UI phase)—and demonstrates that multi-dataset pretraining on 148 NHP sessions (670M+ spikes, 26K units) enables cross-session, cross-subject, and notably cross-species transfer to human handwriting and speech decoding.

## Results

On NHP reaching tasks, the pretrained o-POSSM-S4D(FT) achieves the best overall R², reaching 0.9609 on same-animal CO sessions, 0.9068 on new-animal CO, and 0.8941 on a completely new dataset, outperforming all from-scratch and pretrained baselines. On human handwriting decoding, finetuned o-POSSM-Mamba(FT) achieves 97.73% accuracy, surpassing the prior state-of-the-art PCA-KNN (81.36%) by ~16 percentage points. On human speech decoding, POSSM-GRU with multi-modal input achieves 19.80% phoneme error rate, outperforming the GRU baseline (21.74%). Inference is up to 9× faster than Transformer-based decoders on GPU, with per-chunk latency of ~2.44 ms (single-session) and ~5.65 ms (pretrained) on CPU, well within the ≤10 ms real-time BCI requirement.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against from-scratch baselines (MLP, S4D, Mamba, GRU), all POSSM variants consistently outperform on NHP reaching, with the largest gaps on the new-dataset H-CO condition (e.g., POSSM-Mamba-SS 0.8350 vs. MLP 0.4179, S4D 0.3942). Compared to the Transformer-based POYO, single-session POSSM matches or exceeds POYO-SS on most conditions while being up to 9× faster at inference; pretrained o-POSSM(FT) further surpasses POYO-1(FT) on new-animal and new-dataset splits (e.g., 0.9068 vs. 0.8859 on T-CO). The NDT-2 pretrained baseline is substantially outperformed by o-POSSM on new-animal and new-dataset conditions (e.g., 0.9068 vs. 0.7173 on T-CO), highlighting the advantage of spike-level tokenization over patch-based tokenization for cross-subject transfer. On human handwriting, finetuned o-POSSM-Mamba(FT) achieves 97.73% accuracy, beating POYO-1(FT) (95.82%) and PCA-KNN (81.36%) by ~2 and ~16 points respectively. On human speech, POSSM-GRU achieves 27.32% PER (19.80% with multi-modal input), outperforming the GRU baseline (30.06% / 21.74%) and all SSM baselines (S4D 35.99%, Mamba 32.19%), while also maintaining robustness without data augmentation (29.70% vs. GRU's 39.16% without augmentation).

## Relation to Prior Work

POSSM inherits its spike tokenization scheme (unit ID + RoPE timestamp) and PerceiverIO-style cross-attention encoder directly from POYO (Azabou et al., NeurIPS 2023), but replaces POYO's full Transformer backbone with a lightweight recurrent SSM (S4D, GRU, or Mamba) that processes 50 ms chunks sequentially. This design draws on the hybrid attention-recurrence architectures developed for long-context language modelling (Jamba, Griffin, Heracles) but is the first to apply such a hybrid to neural decoding. The paper also builds on the multi-dataset pretraining and unit-identification finetuning paradigm established by POYO and NDT-2, extending it to a setting where inference must be causal and low-latency rather than offline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The demonstration that NHP motor-cortex pretraining transfers to human handwriting decoding (5%+ accuracy gain) addresses a critical clinical bottleneck: the scarcity of large-scale human electrophysiology data. Combined with up to 9× faster inference than Transformer-based decoders while matching their accuracy, POSSM makes real-time, closed-loop BCI decoding feasible on standard hardware (≤10 ms latency). Open questions include whether cross-species transfer generalizes beyond motor tasks and single subjects, whether self-supervised or multimodal pretraining can further improve generalization, and whether the architecture extends to non-motor brain regions and other recording modalities (EEG, calcium imaging).
