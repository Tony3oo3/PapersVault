## Problem

Modeling high-order interactions among agents in cooperative MARL is hindered by either combinatorial explosion (e.g., NA²Q's O(2^n) Taylor-like expansion) or opaque black-box mixing networks (e.g., QMIX's monotonic mixer) that obscure which interactions drive coordination. No existing value decomposition method can flexibly capture arbitrary-order agent interactions with linear complexity while remaining intrinsically interpretable.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255233/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each agent's GRU-based Q-network consumes its local observation, previous hidden state, and previous action to produce an individual Q-value; during centralized training the global state s is additionally available. The CFN mixing network takes the vector of individual Q-values as input and outputs the joint Q_tot, while the VIB module encodes agent hidden states into a low-dimensional assistive representation m that, together with s, determines per-ladder credit weights α_k via a softmax. At execution time, agents act independently using only their local Q-functions, satisfying the IGM principle.

## Method

QCoFr represents the joint action-value function as a weighted sum of l continued-fraction ladders, each a recursive structure of depth d that explicitly encodes d-order multiplicative interactions among agents with only O(n) parameters, avoiding the combinatorial blow-up of polynomial expansions. A key theoretical result proves that depth-d truncation of the continued fraction yields a Padé approximant matching the true interaction function up to order d, and that linear combinations of such ladders form a universal approximator. A variational information bottleneck encodes agent hidden states into assistive information m that predicts the optimal joint action, which is then used with the global state to compute interpretable credit weights α_k for each ladder, relieving spurious correlation between s and Q_tot. Training is end-to-end, minimizing a combined TD loss on Q_tot and a VIB objective that balances mutual information with the optimal action against a KL regularization on the latent code.

## Results

QCoFr is evaluated on three benchmarks (LBF, SMAC, SMACv2) with performance reported as win-rate learning curves over training steps (Figs. 3–5). On SMAC, QCoFr achieves superior performance across almost all six scenarios (2s3z, 2c_vs_64zg, 3s_vs_5z, 5m_vs_6m, 3s5z_vs_3s6z, 6h_vs_8z), with the most pronounced gains on the two super-hard maps (3s5z_vs_3s6z and 6h_vs_8z). On SMACv2 (zerg_5_vs_5, protoss_5_vs_5, terran_5_vs_5), QCoFr is significantly better than all baselines across all three scenarios. On LBF (lbf-3-3, lbf-4-2), QCoFr achieves competitive performance, though the simplified task design limits the advantage of higher-order interaction modeling.

## Baselines & Comparisons

QCoFr outperforms all nine baselines (VDN, QMIX, QPLEX, CW-QMIX, CDS, SHAQ, GoMARL, ReBorn, NA²Q) on the SMAC and SMACv2 benchmarks, with the largest margins on super-hard SMAC maps where higher-order coordination is most critical. QMIX and VDN, which model only first-order or implicit interactions, show slower convergence and lower final win rates, particularly on complex maps. QPLEX and CW-QMIX underperform due to excessive approximation and relaxed monotonicity constraints. GoMARL (dynamic grouping) consistently converges slowest and performs worst on SMACv2, while NA²Q (Taylor-like expansion) is limited to low-order terms to avoid combinatorial explosion, capping its performance on complex scenarios. CDS fails on LBF due to overemphasis on behavioral diversity without effective coordination, and ReBorn requires extended training to allocate neuron weights. SHAQ is the closest competitor on SMACv2, showing marginally competitive results on individual maps, but its inability to model higher-order interactions limits its adaptability across the full set of scenarios.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the value decomposition MARL paradigm (VDN, QMIX, QPLEX) and the CTDE framework, but departs from the implicit black-box mixing networks by adopting continued fraction neural networks (CoFrNets, Puri et al. 2021) as the mixing architecture. It also draws on NA²Q's Taylor-like decomposition for interpretable credit assignment but replaces its exponential O(2^n) expansion with a linear O(n) continued-fraction structure grounded in Padé approximation theory. The variational information bottleneck (Alemi et al. 2017) is repurposed from its original representation-learning context to generate task-relevant assistive information that mitigates the confounding between global state and joint value in credit estimation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

QCoFr offers a mathematically principled way to model arbitrary-order agent interactions with linear complexity, addressing a real scalability bottleneck in interpretable MARL that polynomial-expansion methods (NA²Q) cannot overcome. The Padé approximation guarantee provides a clean theoretical link between CFN depth and interaction order, which is rare in MARL. However, the fixed-per-task depth is a practical limitation, and the paper acknowledges that adaptive depth selection remains open. Broader open questions include whether the IGM constraint can be relaxed without sacrificing the interpretability benefits, and whether the approach scales to very large agent counts (n > 20) where even O(n) mixing may become a bottleneck relative to the O(n²) observation processing.
