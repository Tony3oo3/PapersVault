## Problem

Existing open-vocabulary learning methods estimate the open-environment data distribution using only seen-class data, leaving the unseen-class component unmodeled and the resulting estimation error inherently unbounded. This paper asks whether generating unseen-class data can provide a theoretically bounded approximation of the true open-environment distribution, and how to do so in practice.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253694/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images (16-shot per class, randomly sampled) and text class labels, processed through a pretrained ViT-B/16 CLIP model with learnable language and visual prompts. During training, it additionally ingests synthetic images generated by Stable Diffusion for predicted unseen classes, guided by class-specific domain captions. At inference, the model takes a single RGB image and outputs a class label over the full vocabulary (seen + unseen) via cosine-similarity-based posterior scoring, with no extra parameters or computation beyond the base CLIP forward pass.

## Method

The approach has two coupled components. First, a class-domain-wise data generation pipeline predicts unseen classes by expanding a hierarchical semantic tree (built from WordNet hypernyms or LLM queries) and selecting the top-K₀ candidates nearest to seen classes in text-embedding space; it then extracts domain attributes (style, background) from seen-class images via VLM captioning with similarity filtering, and feeds both the predicted class names and domain descriptions into Stable Diffusion to synthesize unseen-class images aligned with the seen-class distribution. Second, a distribution alignment algorithm derives an ELBO on the log posterior in open environments, decomposing it into a cross-entropy term on seen data minus a KL divergence between seen and generated-unseen output distributions; it minimizes this KL loss with a sparse computation strategy (accumulating output distributions across iterations and aligning only the top-K₃ most similar pairs) to avoid batch-level misalignment, and adds an MMD loss in feature space between real and generated seen-class data. The theoretical contribution proves (via PAC-Bayesian bounds) that the estimation error between the generated and true unseen-class distributions is upper-bounded, with the bound tightening as the seen–generated distribution gap shrinks.

## Results

In the base-to-base/base-to-new generalization setting across 11 datasets, the method achieves an average harmonic mean (H) of 83.36%, with 86.40% on base classes and 80.52% on new classes, outperforming the strongest baseline (DeKg, 80.44% H) by 2.92%. The largest gains appear on EuroSAT (+14.00% on new classes, +9.98% H) and DTD (+8.53% on new, +6.14% H). In the cross-dataset setting (trained on ImageNet, evaluated on 10 target domains), the method achieves 67.68% average accuracy, surpassing PromptSRC (65.81%) by 1.87% and CoCoOp (65.74%) by 1.94%, with the most notable improvement on EuroSAT (+9.48%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method consistently outperforms all 8 baselines across all 11 datasets in both evaluation settings. Against the primary baseline PromptSRC, the method gains +3.39% average H in the base-to-new setting, with the most dramatic improvement on EuroSAT (+9.98% H, +14.00% on new classes) and DTD (+6.14% H). Compared to the strongest prompt-learning baselines (DeKg, DePT, TCP, CuTCP), the method leads by 2.92–3.85% in average H, with particularly large margins on fine-grained and domain-specific datasets (Aircraft +6.22% H over DeKg, Cars +5.28% H over PromptSRC). In the cross-dataset setting, the method achieves the best average (67.68%) over ASPrompt (67.03%), PromptSRC (65.81%), CoCoOp (65.74%), and CoOp (63.88%), with the largest single-dataset gain on EuroSAT (+9.48% over PromptSRC). The method also surpasses the raw CLIP baseline by 11.66% in average H, confirming that the generated unseen-class data and distribution alignment provide substantial benefits over zero-shot transfer.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the prompt-learning line for CLIP-based open-vocabulary recognition (CoOp, CoCoOp, PromptSRC) by introducing a data-generation component that prior methods entirely lack: all existing prompt-learning approaches estimate the open-environment distribution solely from seen-class data, leaving the unseen-class component unmodeled. The method repurposes text-to-image diffusion (Stable Diffusion) not for augmenting seen classes (as in synthetic-data-for-classification work) but specifically for generating unseen-class images guided by a hierarchical semantic tree and captioned domain attributes. The theoretical contribution draws on PAC-Bayesian concentration inequalities to upper-bound the estimation error, a framing absent from prior prompt-learning work that offers no formal guarantees on distribution estimation quality.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first theoretical upper bound on distribution estimation error in open-vocabulary learning, giving practitioners a principled justification for why generating unseen-class data helps and how to minimize the gap (closer semantic proximity, more samples, tighter domain alignment). The 14% gain on EuroSAT new classes and consistent improvements across 11 datasets demonstrate practical value, though the method's reliance on WordNet/LLMs for class prediction introduces a ceiling: truly novel or emerging classes outside these knowledge sources remain out of scope. Open questions include whether the PAC-Bayesian bounds are tight enough to be practically informative, whether the approach extends to video or 3D recognition, and how to handle classes that are genuinely absent from any lexical resource.
