## Problem

Neural Compilation—using LLMs to translate intermediate representations into assembly code—lacks both a dedicated benchmark for systematic evaluation and a method to reliably improve the functional correctness and performance of LLM-generated assembly. Without these, it is impossible to objectively track progress or make LLM-based compilation practically competitive with mature compilers like clang -O3.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255643/cropped_page5_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes LLVM IR source programs (sourced from ExeBench for L1 and TSVC for L2) along with a target ISA specification (x86_64 or aarch64) and produces complete AT&T-format GNU assembly code with proper section directives, register usage, and calling conventions. Correctness is verified by comparing outputs against clang -O0 compiled references on fixed test inputs, while performance is measured as median runtime over 5 middle runs out of 11 executions, benchmarked against clang -O3.

## Method

The core contribution is a self-evolving prompt optimization method with two stages. In the offline stage, the LLM performs compilation and self-debugging on mini-batches; trajectories from programs that were initially incorrect but became correct via self-debug are collected, and the same LLM acts as a 'prompt optimizer' to extract error patterns and effective fixes, proposing edits to a single evolving prompt that are then confirmed for necessity. In the online stage, the evolved prompt guides initial assembly generation, iterative self-debugging until correctness is achieved, and up to T rounds of performance optimization. The key insight is that the LLM's own iterative debugging process contains transferable insights about assembly correctness and optimization that, when codified into the prompt, systematically reduce error rates and improve code quality without any weight updates.

## Results

On NeuComBack-L2 (x86_64, DeepSeek-R1, 25-sample test set), the self-evolving prompt method improves functional correctness (ACC) from 44% (baseline) to 64% after initial generation, and ACC+Perf (correctness with performance surpassing clang -O3) from 28% to 56% after two rounds of iterative optimization. On aarch64, ACC doubles from 36% to 72% and ACC+Perf increases from 8% to 28%. Among the 16 correctly generated x86_64 programs, 14 (87.5%) surpass clang -O3 performance, with an average speedup of 1.28× and a maximum of 3.25×. DeepSeek-R1 is the strongest baseline LLM, achieving 45.70% ACC and 21.85% ACC+Perf on the full 151-case NeuComBack-L2 set.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates five frontier LLMs as baselines on NeuComBack-L2 (151 cases, x86_64): GPT-4o (1.99% ACC), O1 (19.87%), O3-Mini (21.19%), DeepSeek-V3 (14.57%), and DeepSeek-R1 (45.70%). DeepSeek-R1 is the clear winner among baselines, outperforming O3-Mini by ~24.5 percentage points in ACC and O1 by ~25.8 points, while GPT-4o is effectively non-functional for this task. The proposed self-evolving prompt method (built on DeepSeek-R1) further improves ACC from 45.70% (full-set baseline) to 64% on the 25-sample test set, and ACC+Perf from 21.85% to 56%, representing a 100% relative improvement in high-performance assembly generation over the baseline prompt. On aarch64, the method similarly dominates the baseline, doubling ACC from 36% to 72% and increasing ACC+Perf from 8% to 28%. The method also reduces average self-debug rounds (e.g., from 1.09 to 0.25 on x86_64 L2), indicating more efficient convergence to correct code.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two threads: neural compilation (Armengol-Estapé & O'Boyle 2021 for C-to-x86, Cummins et al. 2023/2024 for LLM-based optimization prediction) and automatic prompt optimization (APE, OPRO, Prochemy). It carves new ground by being the first to define a dedicated LLVM IR-to-assembly benchmark (NeuComBack) and to apply self-debugging-trace-driven prompt evolution specifically to assembly code generation—a combination the authors note has no prior study. Unlike prior neural compilation work that focuses on C-to-assembly or optimization flag prediction, this paper targets the backend compilation stage (IR→ASM) and introduces a self-evolving prompt loop that learns from the LLM's own error-correction trajectories rather than from external supervision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The NeuComBack benchmark fills a genuine gap: before this work, there was no systematic, reproducible way to evaluate LLMs on IR-to-assembly compilation, making progress in neural compilation hard to track. The finding that even DeepSeek-R1 achieves only ~46% baseline correctness—and that prompt-only optimization (no fine-tuning) can push this to 64% while having 87.5% of correct outputs beat clang -O3—suggests that the bottleneck is not model capacity but structured guidance, a potentially cost-effective insight for the field. Key open questions include generalization beyond LLVM IR and TSVC-style loop kernels, scalability to more complex real-world programs (recursion, interprocedural optimization, pointer-heavy code), and whether the learned prompts transfer across model families. The absence of statistical significance testing and the reliance on a single model (DeepSeek-R1) for the main method evaluation limit the strength of the claims.
