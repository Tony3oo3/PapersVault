## Problem

While entropy minimization has a long history as a regularizer in semi-supervised learning and entropy-regularized RL, it has rarely been explored as a standalone training objective for LLMs. This paper asks whether entropy minimization alone—without any labeled data, reward models, or output verification—can substantially improve LLM performance on challenging reasoning tasks, and how far pretrained models can go under this unsupervised signal.

## Input / Output

For training (EM-FT, EM-RL), the method consumes only unlabeled text prompts (35K math from NuminaMath, 25K coding from Eurus-2) and produces reasoning chains or generated code as output; no ground-truth answers or reward labels are required. For inference-time scaling (EM-INF), the input is a single prompt at test time and the output is the same text generation, but with per-step logits adjusted via gradient descent to reduce distributional entropy before sampling the next token. EM-INF requires no training data, no parameter updates, and makes no assumption on output format (e.g., answer extraction), unlike self-consistency or iterative refinement.

## Method

The paper proposes three entropy-minimization methods aligned with post-pretraining stages. EM-FT directly minimizes the empirical token-level entropy (Eqn. 2) over unlabeled model-sampled trajectories using standard backpropagation, analogous to SFT but without labels. EM-RL uses negative entropy as the sole RL reward—either trajectory-level (joint log-probability) or token-level (sum of per-step entropies)—optimized via RLOO policy gradients with a small KL regularizer (β=0.001) to prevent drift from the base model. EM-INF treats the model's output logits at each decoding step as free parameters and runs 5–15 steps of gradient descent on a one-layer softmax to minimize entropy, subject to a minimum entropy threshold δ (0.1–0.5) to avoid collapse to greedy decoding; this requires only O(n) forward passes (same as regular decoding) versus O(Nn) for self-consistency. The key insight is that pretrained LLMs already encode reasoning priors, and concentrating probability mass on the model's most confident outputs reinforces these priors without external supervision.

## Results

On Qwen2.5-Math-7B, EM-RL-TOKEN (no labels) achieves 42.9% average math accuracy and 27.0% average coding accuracy, matching or exceeding GRPO (42.1/25.4) and approaching RLOO (43.9/27.5) which are trained on 60K labeled examples. EM-FT with a single rollout (N=1) reaches 40.2% math average, competitive with N=4 RL baselines, while using 13× less compute (1.0 vs 13.1 ×10¹⁷ FLOPs). At inference time, EM-INF with Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct achieves 10.7% on SciCode main problem (w/BG), surpassing GPT-4o (9.2%) and Gemini 1.5 Pro (7.7%) without any parameter updates, while being 3× more compute-efficient than self-consistency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against output-verified RL (RLOO, GRPO): EM-RL-TOKEN matches or beats both on math average (42.9 vs 43.9 RLOO, 42.1 GRPO) and coding average (27.0 vs 27.5 RLOO, 25.4 GRPO), and clearly outperforms on AMC (57.8 vs 57.8/56.6), Minerva (30.9 vs 31.2/25.0), and LeetCode (29.5 vs 28.3/25.0). EM-FT with N=1 is competitive with N=4 RL on Minerva (33.1 vs 31.2/25.0) and LeetCode (28.3 vs 28.3/25.0) at 13× lower compute. Against self-consistency (SC-RL): EM-RL methods outperform on AMC (57.8 vs 51.8) and Minerva (30.9 vs 26.1), and are applicable to coding where SC-RL cannot be used. Against inference-time baselines (self-consistency, iterative-refinement): EM-INF outperforms self-consistency on UGPhysics (21.4 vs 18.5 for Qwen2.5-Math-7B) and LeetCode (51.7 vs 48.8 for Qwen2.5-7B), and beats iterative-refinement across all tasks, while requiring only 1 trajectory vs N=4 or N=3, yielding ~3× compute savings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes entropy minimization—a well-studied regularizer in semi-supervised learning, domain adaptation, and test-time adaptation—as a standalone training objective for LLMs, inverting the entropy-regularized RL paradigm (SAC, maximum entropy RL) that uses entropy *maximization* to promote exploration. It departs from self-improvement and self-consistency RL methods (majority-voting rewards, self-verification) by targeting token-level distributional uncertainty directly rather than answer-level consistency, making it applicable to tasks where output extraction is inapplicable (e.g., scientific coding). The inference-time variant (EM-INF) introduces a novel logit-optimization step with no direct predecessor in the LLM decoding literature, positioned as a parameter-free alternative to self-consistency and iterative refinement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a label-free entropy objective can match RL trained on 60K verified examples challenges the prevailing assumption that post-training gains in reasoning require explicit reward signals, and the authors explicitly call for EM to be included as a baseline in future post-training evaluations to disentangle algorithmic contributions from pretrained model capability. EM-INF offers a practical, deployable inference-time technique (3× more efficient than self-consistency, no parameter updates) that could be widely adopted in production settings. Key open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond tasks where confidence correlates with correctness (it fails on value-alignment tasks and weaker base models like Llama-3.1-8B), and whether the gains persist as models scale or as pretraining distributions shift.
