## Problem

Fine-tuning a single LLM across multiple heterogeneous domains simultaneously is challenging because domain-specific features can negatively interfere, causing one domain to overshadow others or impair overall generalization. Existing parameter-efficient methods (adapters, LoRA) and domain-adaptation techniques (adversarial training, moment matching) handle single-domain or distribution-alignment settings but fail to exploit inter-domain synergies or strategically partition domains to avoid negative transfer.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pretrained LLM backbone (θ*), k source domains of text data, and precomputed pairwise domain metrics—Jensen-Shannon divergence on unigram+bigram token distributions for discrepancy, and a blend of Jaccard vocabulary overlap with cosine similarity of mean Sentence-BERT embeddings for synergy. It produces a fine-tuned backbone with bounded per-stage drift (‖Δθ‖≤ρ_θ) plus domain-specific adapter parameters (‖φ_j‖≤ρ_φ), yielding task-specific outputs (ROUGE-L summaries, sentiment/accuracy labels, QA exact-match answers, topic categories). The partition step runs in O(k² log k) on CPU using cached statistics, adding negligible overhead relative to standard single-pass SFT.

## Method

The core mechanism is a two-phase procedure: (1) a partition step that clusters k domains into M disjoint stages by maximizing an objective G that jointly penalizes intra-stage discrepancy (JS divergence), rewards intra-stage synergy (lexical + semantic affinity weighted by λ), and charges a capacity cost (squared norms of backbone drift and stage adapters); and (2) sequential stage-wise fine-tuning where each stage updates only its assigned domain adapters under norm projections, leaving outside-stage adapters frozen. The theoretical contribution derives multi-source generalization bounds (Theorem 3.1) and a multi-stage partition bound (Theorem 3.2) showing that the G-maximizing partition provably minimizes the worst-stage risk upper bound, with a corollary guaranteeing that high-synergy, low-discrepancy subsets are grouped together. In practice, the partition is solved via single-link agglomerative clustering (within 1% of the ILP optimum for k≤10), and norm constraints are enforced by projecting gradient updates after each step, preserving pretrained knowledge while allowing domain-specific specialization.

## Results

On LLaMA2-13B, PMS-FTP achieves 43.4 ROUGE-L (NSum), 67.2 EM (Q&A), 90.2 ACC (Sentiment), and 88.0 ACC (Topic), outperforming the strongest baseline S2L (42.9 / 66.2 / 90.0 / 87.8) by 0.5–1.0 points across tasks. On Falcon-40B, the gains widen to 44.2 / 69.1 / 91.1 / 89.0, demonstrating that synergy-driven partitioning scales with model size. Peak GPU memory is reduced by 32.4% relative to full fine-tuning (18.4 GB vs. 27.2 GB on LLaMA2-7B Q&A), while maintaining the highest accuracy among all methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PMS-FTP consistently outperforms all 10 baselines across every task and model size. The closest competitor is S2L (data-selection via proxy-model clustering), which PMS-FTP surpasses by +0.5 ROUGE-L on NSum and +1.0 EM on Q&A (LLaMA2-13B); the gap widens to +1.3 EM on Falcon-40B. Among single-domain PEFT methods, LLaMA-Adapter and Tag-LLM are the strongest, yet PMS-FTP still leads by +0.7–0.8 ROUGE-L and +1.1–1.3 EM. Conventional domain-adaptation baselines (MDAN, M³SDA, GMDI) lag substantially behind even FULL fine-tuning, with diagnostic analyses attributing this to severe gradient conflicts (cosine similarity 0.12–0.18 vs. 0.43 for FULL) and higher catastrophic forgetting (perplexity increase of 8–12% vs. 5.6% for FULL). PMS-FTP achieves the best gradient alignment (0.57) and lowest perplexity increase (+3.2%), confirming that synergy-aware partitioning with norm-constrained adapters mitigates negative transfer more effectively than adversarial or moment-matching objectives.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) literature—LoRA, Houlsby adapters, LLaMA-Adapter, Q-LoRA—by introducing a multi-stage partitioning strategy that decides which domains to co-tune based on measured synergy and discrepancy, rather than treating all domains jointly or in isolation. It departs from classical multi-domain adaptation methods (adversarial training, moment matching, Bayesian domain indexing) that align representations but do not explicitly exploit constructive inter-domain interactions. The theoretical contribution—generalization bounds incorporating a synergy offset term and a partition objective—builds on standard Rademacher/PAC-Bayes machinery for norm-restricted networks but applies it to a multi-stage, multi-adapter setting that prior work has not formalized.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important gap: deploying a single LLM across multiple heterogeneous domains without the memory cost of per-domain models or the interference of joint fine-tuning. The partition-based approach is lightweight (negligible overhead over standard SFT) and the theoretical bounds give practitioners a principled way to choose stage groupings. However, the empirical gains are modest (0.5–1.5 points over strong baselines), the synergy metric is heuristic (JS divergence + Jaccard + cosine), and the main experiments cover only four domains; scalability to dozens of domains and to a truly sequential/continual setting remains open. The authors themselves flag continual learning and pruning integration as future directions.
