## Problem

Machine unlearning must simultaneously erase sensitive information and preserve model utility, but existing multi-objective optimization (MOO) methods that fairly optimize all objectives under-optimize the unlearning goal because pre-trained models are already near Pareto-optimal for utility. Moreover, standard MOO and gradient surgery methods require separate gradient computations for each objective, doubling the backpropagation cost and contradicting the efficiency demands of unlearning. This paper addresses both the modeling mismatch and the computational overhead.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained model (Stable Diffusion v1.4, DDPM on CIFAR-10/Imagenette, or ResNet-18), a forgetting dataset specifying the target concept (a class, an instance like Snoopy, an artist style like Van Gogh, or NSFW content), and a retaining dataset of all other data. It outputs updated model parameters that no longer generate or recognize the target concept while maintaining performance on retained data. A user-specified error tolerance ε_t controls the maximum allowable per-step degradation in the retaining loss, and a clipping bound D prevents the dynamic weight from exploding.

## Method

The paper formulates utility-preserving unlearning as a constrained optimization problem (UPUP): maximize the decrease in unlearning loss subject to a bounded increase in retaining loss at each step. Solving this yields a closed-form update direction equivalent to unilateral gradient surgery—projecting the unlearning gradient to remove its component conflicting with the retaining gradient. To avoid the doubled backpropagation cost of explicit surgery, EUPMU approximates the surgery weight λ_t via a first-order Taylor expansion of the retain-loss change, updating λ_t without any additional backward pass. The actual parameter update then uses a single backpropagation of the composite loss ℓ_u + λ_t·ℓ_r, matching the computational cost of simple linear weighting. Theoretical analysis proves convergence to a Pareto optimal (convex) or Pareto stationary (non-convex) solution at O(1/t) and O(1/√t) rates respectively, matching state-of-the-art first-order MOO algorithms.

## Results

On Imagenette with Stable Diffusion (class-wise forgetting), EUPMU achieves a perfect average Unlearning Accuracy of 0.00% (vs. 0.18% for SalUn, 1.09% for ESD, and 59.76% for FMN) while also attaining the best average FID of 1.03 (vs. 1.22 for SalUn, 1.46 for ESD, and 1.27 for FMN). On CIFAR-10 with DDPM, EUPMU reaches UA 0.78% and FID 22.57 with a runtime efficiency of 1.38×, outperforming ESD (FID 47.57) and SalUn (FID 24.53, RTE 6.05×). For NSFW content removal on 4,709 i2p prompts, EUPMU generates only 6 NSFW images total, compared to 13 for SalUn, 108 for ESD, and 129 for FMN.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EUPMU dominates all four image-generation baselines on Imagenette: it achieves 0.00% average UA (perfect forgetting) versus 0.18% (SalUn), 1.09% (ESD), and 59.76% (FMN), while also posting the best average FID of 1.03 versus 1.22 (SalUn), 1.46 (ESD), and 1.27 (FMN). For instance and style unlearning (Table 2), EUPMU attains the lowest CLIP Score and CLIP Accuracy on target concepts (e.g., 44.9/25.62 for Snoopy vs. 49.27/35.38 for ESD and 57.32/80.88 for ConAbl) while maintaining competitive FID on retained concepts, outperforming SPM and ConAbl on most metrics. On CIFAR-10 DDPM, EUPMU matches ESD's UA (0.78 vs. 0.00) but dramatically improves FID (22.57 vs. 47.57) at lower runtime cost (1.38× vs. 1.6×). In the ablation (Table 4), EUPMU outperforms both MOO baselines—FAMO (avg. 97.48) and PCGrad (avg. 97.50)—as well as explicit unilateral gradient surgery UNGrad (avg. 98.12), reaching the best average score of 98.40 while halving the runtime of UNGrad (18.4× vs. 27.7×). For NSFW removal, EUPMU reduces total NSFW generations to 6, beating SalUn (13), ESD (108), and FMN (129) by large margins.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the gradient surgery paradigm introduced by PCGrad (Yu et al., NeurIPS 2020) but departs from it in two key ways: it applies surgery unilaterally (only to the unlearning gradient) and derives the operation from a constrained optimization formulation (UPUP) rather than a symmetric multi-objective one. The authors argue that standard MOO methods (MGDA, PCGrad, FAMO) are fundamentally misaligned with machine unlearning because pre-trained models are already near Pareto-optimal on the utility objective, leaving little room for fair MOO to improve unlearning. The implicit gradient surgery technique, which approximates the surgery weight via a first-order Taylor expansion of the retaining loss change, is the paper's core algorithmic contribution, reducing the method to a single backpropagation pass equivalent in cost to linear weighting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The constrained formulation provides a principled, user-controllable knob (ε_t) for bounding utility degradation during unlearning, which is a practical need for deploying MU on expensive pre-trained models where retraining is infeasible. The 50% reduction in backpropagation cost makes gradient-surgery-based MU viable at scale, and the Pareto convergence guarantees (O(1/t) convex, O(1/√t) non-convex) match state-of-the-art first-order MOO rates. Open questions include whether the implicit approximation's advantage over explicit surgery (observed in the ResNet-18 ablation) persists at larger scales, and whether the framework extends naturally to LLM unlearning where the utility landscape is far more complex.
