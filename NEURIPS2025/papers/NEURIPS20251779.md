## Problem

Existing person re-identification methods and datasets are constrained to at most two or three modalities, making it impossible to perform retrieval with arbitrary combinations of multiple query modalities as encountered in real-world scenarios. This paper formalizes Omni Multi-modal Person Re-identification (OM-ReID), where a query may be any single modality or any combination of up to five modalities, and introduces both the first five-modal benchmark (ORBench) and a unified model to address this gap.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251779/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts any non-empty subset of five modalities as a query: RGB images, infrared images, color pencil drawings, sketch drawings (all resized to 384×128), and free-form textual descriptions (max 77 tokens). The gallery is always RGB images from the same dataset. The output is a ranked list of gallery images by similarity, evaluated via CMC (Rank-1/5/10) and mAP across 32 distinct query-set configurations spanning single-, dual-, tri-, and quad-modal searches.

## Method

ReID5o builds on a CLIP-B/16 backbone and introduces three key components. A Multi-modal Tokenizing Assembler (MTA) uses five non-shared tokenizers (four visual conv+LN, one vocabulary-mapping for text) to project all inputs into a common embedding space while emitting binary modality-activation signals. A Multi-Expert Router (MER) inserts per-modality LoRA low-rank matrices (rank 4) into every linear layer of the encoder; the base weights extract modality-shared features while the activated expert extracts modality-specific features, adding only 2.36M parameters. A Feature Mixture module (multi-head self-attention + one transformer block + MLP with average pooling) fuses concatenated features from all modality combinations in the query. Training uses an SDM contrastive loss that aligns every modality-combination representation to the RGB representation, plus an identity classification loss, with all modality combinations traversed during training.

## Results

On ORBench, ReID5o achieves the best performance across all four search modes: 68.12% R@1 and 58.09% mAP for single-modal search, 86.15% R@1 and 75.26% mAP for dual-modal, 93.53% R@1 and 82.83% mAP for tri-modal, and 96.25% R@1 and 86.35% mAP for quad-modal search. The largest gains over the strongest cross-modal baseline (IRRA) appear in single-modal search (+6.95 R@1, +6.67 mAP) and quad-modal search (+3.60 R@1, +7.20 mAP). Multi-modal combination queries consistently outperform single-modal queries, with quad-modal search improving mAP by 28.26 points over single-modal.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReID5o outperforms all nine baselines across every search mode on ORBench. Among cross-modal methods, IRRA is the strongest baseline (61.17% R@1, 51.42% mAP single-modal), but ReID5o surpasses it by +6.95 R@1 and +6.67 mAP in single-modal search, and by +3.60 R@1 and +7.20 mAP in quad-modal search. Among multi-modal methods, ImageBind and UNIReID are the closest competitors (60.18% and 59.49% R@1 single-modal), yet ReID5o leads them by 7.94 and 8.63 R@1 respectively. The AIO framework, which already handles four modalities (RGB, IR, sketch, text), shows a dramatic gap—ReID5o exceeds it by 17.78 R@1 in single-modal and 20.92 R@1 in quad-modal search—highlighting the benefit of explicit multi-expert routing and feature mixture over simple multi-modal alignment. Meta-TF fails catastrophically (6.25% R@1), likely due to its inability to handle the five distinct visual modalities without modality-specific adaptation. In the generalization evaluation (Table 7), ReID5o trained on ORBench also achieves the best transfer performance to ICFG-PEDES (43.27% R@1) and PKU-Sketch (80.20% R@1), confirming dataset quality and model robustness.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends cross-modal person ReID (which has typically handled fixed pairs like RGB–IR, RGB–text, or RGB–sketch) to a unified 5-modality setting where any arbitrary subset of {RGB, IR, color pencil, sketch, text} can serve as the query. Architecturally, it builds on CLIP as a shared multi-modal encoder and repurposes LoRA's low-rank decomposition as a 'multi-expert router' that activates modality-specific parameter updates via binary control signals, rather than training separate encoders per modality. The SDM alignment loss from IRRA and the overall contrastive training paradigm are inherited from prior text-to-image ReID work, while the dataset construction extends SYSU-MM01 and LLCM with manually supervised color pencil, sketch, and text annotations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ORBench is the first person ReID benchmark covering five modalities with high-quality manual annotation, filling a clear gap where prior multi-modal datasets (TriReID, AIO) relied on rough synthesis. The multi-expert router's efficiency finding—2.36 M extra parameters at r=4 outperforming 256 M parameters from separate per-modality encoders—offers a practical, lightweight recipe for modality-specific adaptation in any multi-modal Transformer. Key open questions remain: the dataset uses idealized 'viewed sketches' rather than noisy forensic sketches, the framework is closed to the five predefined modalities (no incremental learning), and open-set recognition with unseen identities is unaddressed, all of which limit direct transfer to real-world deployment.
