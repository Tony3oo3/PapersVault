## Problem

In networked multi-agent reinforcement learning, agents are typically hardwired to exchange information with all local neighbors regardless of contextual relevance, leading to inefficient coordination and performance degradation in dynamic settings like traffic control. While centralized methods can learn dynamic interaction graphs, they require global state access and centralized infrastructure that is impractical in real-world decentralized systems. The paper asks whether fully decentralized agents can learn to adaptively select task-relevant neighbors from their fixed physical topology using only local observations.

## Input / Output

Each agent consumes three types of neighborhood-level features—lane-level traffic observations (density, queue length, waiting time from induction-loop detectors), neighbor policy fingerprints (action distributions), and RNN trajectory hidden states—along with the fixed physical road-network adjacency matrix, all restricted to the agent's 1-hop ego-graph. The method produces a discrete traffic-signal phase action per agent and, critically, a learned Bernoulli latent mask Z_i ∈ {0,1}^{|V_i|×|V_i|} over the ego-graph edges, sampled via Gumbel-Softmax to yield a sparse effective subgraph G_i* = Z_i ⊙ G_Vi^env that gates all downstream message passing. The mask is state- and trajectory-dependent, so the same physical topology yields different communication structures at different timesteps.

## Method

BayesG formulates latent edge-mask learning as Bayesian variational inference: each agent approximates the posterior p(Z_i | G_env, D_i) with a factorized Bernoulli distribution q(Z_i; φ_i) whose logits are learned end-to-end. The ELBO objective instantiates the likelihood term as the graph-conditioned A2C actor loss (negative log-probability weighted by advantage, plus action-entropy regularization), while the prior is a Bernoulli with retention bias λ and the entropy term −log q(Z_i; φ_i) acts as mask-entropy regularization, preventing premature collapse to deterministic graphs. Gumbel-Softmax reparameterization enables gradient flow through discrete edge sampling, and the graph-conditioned encoder applies GCN-based masked message passing over the sampled subgraph for each of the three input channels. This dual entropy structure—over both actions and edges—distinguishes BayesG from prior variational MARL methods that assume centralized training or unrestricted graph rewiring, and from sampling-based networked MARL that uses fixed or uniform distributions rather than learned, state-adaptive per-edge probabilities.

## Results

BayesG is evaluated on five adaptive traffic signal control (ATSC) environments ranging from 25 agents (ATSC_Grid) to 167 agents (NewYork167), with the primary metric being episode return (negative total queue length). Across all five environments, BayesG consistently achieves the highest asymptotic returns and fastest convergence compared to all six baselines, with the most pronounced advantage in large-scale settings (NewYork33, NewYork51, NewYork167) where baselines suffer from plateauing or instability. Qualitative traffic visualizations confirm that BayesG produces significantly lighter and sparser congestion patterns than all baselines on the Grid map. The paper does not report a single numerical summary table; results are presented as smoothed training reward curves over 5 random seeds (Figure 2) and qualitative SUMO snapshots.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BayesG outperforms all six baselines across all five ATSC environments. Against non-communicative baselines (IA2C, ConseNet, FPrint, LToS), BayesG achieves substantially higher returns, demonstrating that learned latent communication structures provide a clear advantage over methods that either ignore inter-agent interaction or use fixed neighborhood averaging. Against communication-based baselines, BayesG surpasses CommNet (which uses mean-pooled neighbor messages) and NeurComm (which uses richer per-neighbor message encoding with LSTM) by learning task-adaptive sparse subgraphs that suppress uninformative edges. The advantage is most visible in large-scale NewYork167 (167 agents), where BayesG maintains stable learning while baselines plateau or degrade. Ablation studies (Figure 5) confirm that the learned variational mask significantly outperforms both no-masking (full topology) and random masking, and that combining state, trajectory, and policy features for mask generation yields the best performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Spatiotemporal-MDP and decentralized A2C framework from NeurComm (Chu et al., ICLR 2020) by introducing a stochastic graph-based policy where each agent conditions its decision on a sampled subgraph over its physical neighborhood. It departs from prior variational graph-learning approaches in MARL (Du et al. 2021, Duan et al. 2024) and CTDE-based coordination graph methods (DCG, DICG, SOP-CG) by operating entirely in a decentralized, physically-constrained setting where agents have no access to global state or reward. The Bayesian variational inference formulation—treating per-agent edge masks as latent Bernoulli variables optimized via ELBO alongside the actor-critic loss—is the key methodological contribution, distinguishing it from sampling-based networked MARL (Lin et al. 2021, Anand & Qu 2024) that uses fixed or uniform distributions rather than learned, state-dependent posteriors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

BayesG addresses a practical gap in networked MARL: real-world decentralized systems (traffic networks, power grids, sensor fields) cannot rely on centralized training infrastructure or global state access, yet must still adapt their coordination structure to dynamic conditions. The demonstrated scalability to 167 agents with interpretable, congestion-aware communication patterns makes the approach relevant for deployable traffic control. Key limitations include the assumption of fixed physical topology, reliance on local observability only (limiting long-range reasoning), and sensitivity to Gumbel-softmax temperature and sparsity prior hyperparameters. Open questions include whether the framework extends to dynamically changing topologies, other networked domains beyond traffic, and whether the computational overhead of variational sampling and masked GNNs remains acceptable at even larger scales.
