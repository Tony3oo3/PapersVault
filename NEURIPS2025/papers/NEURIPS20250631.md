## Problem

Quantum computing provides a quadratic speedup over classical Monte Carlo for estimating linear functionals (means) of probability distributions, but it remains unclear whether this speedup extends to non-linear functionals. For the nested expectation E_X[g(X, E_{Y|X}[φ(X,Y)])], the direct application of the quantum-accelerated MLMC algorithm of An et al. (2021) yields only O~(ε^{-2}) cost—identical to classical MLMC—because the per-level computational cost grows polynomially in 1/ε, eroding the quadratic gain. This paper asks whether a problem-specific redesign of the MLMC decomposition can recover the full quadratic speedup.

## Input / Output

The algorithm requires queryable access to the functions φ(X,Y) and g(X,·) (each query costs one unit), plus sampling oracles Gen_X and Gen_Y(x) that must be implementable as reversible quantum circuits (a standard assumption in the simulation literature). The user also supplies known upper bounds V on the conditional second moment E_{Y|x}[φ²] and S on the variance of the target, along with the Lipschitz constant K of g. The output is a single scalar estimate of the nested expectation with additive error at most ε and success probability at least 1−δ, achieved at total cost O~(K√(SV)·log(1/δ)/ε).

## Method

The key insight is that the classical MLMC sequence (using 2^l i.i.d. samples at level l) is suboptimal for quantum algorithms because the per-level cost C_l ~ 2^l grows to poly(1/ε) at the top level, negating the quadratic speedup from quantum amplitude estimation. Instead, the paper introduces a new 'quantum-inside-quantum' decomposition: at each level l, an inner quantum-accelerated Monte Carlo subroutine (Kothari–O'Donnell) estimates the conditional expectation E_{Y|X=x}[φ(X,Y)] to precision 2^{-(l+1)}/K, and the outer level applies a second quantum-accelerated Monte Carlo to estimate the expectation of the coupled difference Δ_l = g(x, φ̂_l(x)) − g(x, φ̂_{l-1}(x)). This redesign improves the MLMC parameters from (α,β,γ) = (0.5, 1, 1) to (1, 2, 1+o(1)), placing the problem in the β ≥ 2γ regime where the quantum speedup is fully realized. A median trick over O(log(1/δ)) independent runs amplifies the fixed 0.8 success probability to 1−δ, yielding the final O~(K√(SV)/ε) cost, which matches the known Ω(1/ε) quantum lower bound for mean estimation up to polylogarithmic factors.

## Results

The main result (Theorem 3.2) establishes that the proposed Q-NESTEXPECT algorithm estimates the nested expectation E_X[g(X, E_{Y|X}[φ(X,Y)])] with additive error ε and success probability 1−δ at a cost of Õ(K√(SV)·log(1/δ)/ε), where K is the Lipschitz constant of g, S bounds the variance of the outer expectation, and V bounds the conditional second moment. This achieves a quadratic speedup over classical MLMC (which costs Õ(ε^{-2})) and matches the known lower bound Ω(1/ε) for quantum mean estimation up to polylogarithmic factors, making it optimal among quantum algorithms. The algorithm is a 'quantum-inside-quantum' construction: the outer level uses quantum-accelerated Monte Carlo (Kothari–O'Donnell) to estimate E[Δ_l] at each MLMC level, while the inner level uses the same quantum subroutine to estimate the conditional expectation E_{Y|X=x}[φ(X,Y)] with progressively tighter accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three baselines for estimating the nested expectation with ε-accuracy. Against the naive nested Monte Carlo estimator of Rainforth et al. (2018), which costs Õ(ε^{-4}), the proposed method achieves a four-power improvement to Õ(ε^{-1}). Against classical MLMC (Giles, 2015), which achieves Õ(ε^{-2}), the method recovers the full quadratic quantum speedup, reducing the cost to Õ(ε^{-1}). Most critically, against the quantum-accelerated MLMC of An et al. (2021), which was expected to improve upon classical MLMC but in fact also costs Õ(ε^{-2}) for this problem (due to the growing per-sample cost C_l at high levels undermining the quadratic gain), the proposed method achieves a genuine quadratic speedup by redesigning the MLMC sequence with quantum subroutines at the inner level. The paper also proves (Proposition 2.3) that the QA-MLMC framework of An et al. is in general optimal up to polylog factors, so the improvement here is specific to the structure of the nested expectation problem rather than a general flaw in QA-MLMC.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the quantum-accelerated multilevel Monte Carlo (QA-MLMC) framework of An et al. (2021) to the non-linear nested expectation problem, using the newer Kothari–O'Donnell (2023) quantum mean estimator as the core subroutine. The key departure is conceptual: while An et al. simply replace each classical Monte Carlo level with its quantum counterpart, this paper shows that the classical MLMC decomposition (Algorithm 1) is suboptimal for quantum algorithms, and instead designs a new 'quantum-inside-quantum' sequence where the inner conditional expectation is itself estimated by a quantum subroutine at progressively higher precision. The overall MLMC framework and the classical nested-expectation solution are inherited from Giles (2015), and the lower-bound construction in Appendix C builds on the quantum adversary method via the COUNT∘PARITY composition.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result establishes that a full quadratic quantum speedup (O~(1/ε)) is achievable for nested expectations, matching the known lower bound for standard mean estimation and resolving an open question about whether non-linear functionals can enjoy the same speedup as linear ones. The 'quantum-inside-quantum' design principle—that the MLMC approximation sequence itself must be co-designed with the quantum subroutine, rather than merely swapping in a faster estimator—opens a broader question about which other MLMC applications (VaR/CVaR, bilevel optimization, SDE path simulation) can benefit from similar quantum-native redesigns. Limitations include the fault-tolerance assumption (inaccessible on current NISQ hardware) and the restriction to the specific nested-expectation structure; the general QA-MLMC lower bound still applies to arbitrary MLMC sequences.
