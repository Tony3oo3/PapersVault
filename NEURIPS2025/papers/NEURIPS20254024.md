## Problem

Existing scaling laws for LLMs, particularly Chinchilla's formulation, model the data-scaling term with constant parameters (B/D^β), implying a uniform rate of loss improvement with data across all model sizes. This forces the law to capture only an average data-scaling behavior, producing systematic under- and over-estimation at the extremes of the fitted model-size range and severely limiting extrapolation to larger scales. The resulting 'scaling gap'—where small-scale experimental conclusions fail to transfer to production-scale training—hinders cost-effective evaluation of competing training strategies.

## Input / Output

The method consumes loss measurements (BPC on a 30M-token held-out validation set) from approximately 1,000 Llama-style decoder-only Transformers trained on a geometric grid (ratio √2) of model sizes N and token counts D, with all architectural and optimization hyperparameters standardized. It outputs a fully parameterized scaling law L(N,D) with nine fitted parameters (three per stretched-exponential component), from which the optimal D/N ratio for any total compute budget C = 6ND can be analytically derived. The fitted formula is monotonically decreasing in both N and D, and predictions are validated at scales up to 25.1B parameters—over an order of magnitude beyond the largest calibrated model.

## Method

Farseer employs a three-stage Differential Piecewise Fitting procedure. Stage 1 computes the finite difference Δ_D L(N,D) = L(N,D) − L(N, λD) for each model size N, which cancels the model-size-only and constant terms, then fits a power law in D via log-log linear regression to obtain discrete exponent A_N and coefficient B_N. Stage 2 selects continuous parameterizations f_A(N) and f_B(N) by searching over a dictionary of monotone transformations (identity, log, power) applied to both the parameter values and N, identifying stretched-exponential forms exp(a·N^α + b) that minimize global residual error, with exponents α and β refined iteratively. Stage 3 estimates the model-dependent residual E + L_N(N) by subtracting the fitted data-dependent term from L(N,D), averaging over D, and fitting a third stretched-exponential. The key structural difference from Chinchilla is that the data-scaling exponent and coefficient are explicitly N-dependent functions rather than constants, thereby capturing the higher-order interaction term L_ND(N,D) that the additive Chinchilla form cannot represent.

## Results

Farseer achieves an average relative error of 0.50% on six extrapolation targets (including a 25.1B model beyond the calibration range by over an order of magnitude), compared to Chinchilla's 2.68% average relative error — a 433% reduction. Within the fitted range, Farseer's average relative error is 232% lower than Chinchilla's, with the gap widening at model-size extremes. The model-dependent residual E+L_N(N) is fitted to 0.09% relative error, and the core power-law relationship Δ_D L vs. D achieves R² = 0.9807 across all model sizes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is Chinchilla's scaling law (Hoffmann et al., 2022), which models loss as A/N^α + B/D^β + E with N-independent data-scaling parameters. Farseer decisively outperforms Chinchilla on every metric tested: (1) within the fitted range, Farseer's average relative error is 232% lower, with Chinchilla's error diverging at model-size extremes due to its lack of N-dependent cross terms; (2) on six extrapolation targets including a 25.1B model, Farseer achieves 0.50% average relative error versus Chinchilla's 2.68% (a 433% gap); (3) in robustness tests (Fig. 8a, fixed N=6.4B), Farseer shows both lower mean error and smaller variance as the fitting range expands, while Chinchilla's error remains persistently high at the 25.1B extrapolation point (Fig. 8b). Farseer also provides qualitatively different and more accurate compute-allocation guidance: it predicts a steadily increasing optimal D/N ratio with compute budget, consistent with actual configurations of Llama 3.1 and Qwen3, whereas Chinchilla's constant D/N ≈ 20 rule underestimates data requirements at larger scales.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper refines the scaling law lineage established by Kaplan et al. (2020) and Chinchilla (Hoffmann et al., 2022), specifically targeting Chinchilla's structural limitation: its data-scaling term B/D^β uses model-size-independent constants, implicitly assuming uniform data-scaling dynamics across all N. Farseer departs by making both the data-scaling exponent and coefficient explicit functions of N (A(N), B(N)), discovered through a novel Differential Piecewise Fitting methodology that uses finite differences to isolate and model the N–D interaction term. The stretched-exponential functional forms for these N-dependent functions represent a departure from the pure power-law forms used in both Kaplan and Chinchilla.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Farseer's 433% lower extrapolation error relative to Chinchilla directly addresses the 'scaling gap' problem, making small-scale ablation studies far more reliable for predicting production-scale performance — a practical concern for any team planning multi-billion-parameter training runs. The finding that the optimal D/N ratio increases steadily with compute budget (rather than staying near 20) aligns with actual configurations of recent frontier models (Llama 3.1, Qwen3) and provides a data-driven justification for the over-training trend in modern LLMs. Key open questions remain: applicability to MoE architectures, extrapolation beyond 25.1B parameters toward trillion-scale models, the absence of first-principles theoretical grounding, and extension from pre-training loss to downstream task performance.
