## Problem

Variational quantum eigensolvers (VQEs) require O(p·N_H/ε²) quantum measurements per gradient step, making large-scale optimization (tens of qubits, hundreds of parameters) prohibitively expensive on near-term hardware. Prior approaches to reduce this cost—measurement grouping, warm-start initializers, and heuristic trajectory predictors like LSTM or QuACK—do not effectively balance optimization efficiency and accuracy at scale, and none exploit the underlying dynamical structure of the training process as a physical system.

## Input / Output

PALQO consumes a short sequential trajectory of τ optimization steps (τ ≪ T total steps), where each step provides the full parameter vector θ^(t) ∈ ℝ^p and the corresponding energy E^(t), collected from a quantum device. The network takes a single (time step, parameter vector) pair as input and outputs the predicted next-step parameters and energy; during inference it is run recursively, feeding its own predicted parameters back as input to generate an entire future optimization trajectory on classical hardware without further quantum queries. Convergence is declared when successive predicted parameters and energies change by less than a threshold ζ.

## Method

The core reformulation applies a Taylor expansion to the gradient-descent dynamics of a VQE, yielding two coupled PDEs: a gradient-flow equation for parameter evolution (∂θ/∂t = −∂E/∂θ) and a second-order energy-evolution equation whose leading term is the quantum neural tangent kernel (QNTK) and whose next term involves the Hessian of the cost landscape. A physics-informed neural network f_w is trained to approximate the joint solution (θ(t), E(t)) by minimizing a composite loss that combines a data residual on the τ collected trajectory points with two PDE residuals enforcing the gradient-flow and energy-evolution equations via automatic differentiation of the network outputs. This embedding of the dynamical laws as hard structural constraints gives the model strong inductive bias, enabling accurate long-horizon prediction from only a handful of quantum-device samples. A generalization bound is proved showing polynomial sample complexity in the number of parameters p, network depth L, and width W. For long-term prediction, non-overlapping sliding windows are used to iteratively extend the forecast beyond the training horizon.

## Results

PALQO achieves up to 30× speedup over vanilla VQE and reduces quantum measurement overhead by approximately 90% across all tested systems while maintaining energy accuracy ΔE ≈ 10⁻³. On the 14-qubit BeH₂ molecule (UCCSD ansatz), PALQO requires only 28.01×10⁸ measurement shots versus 464.3×10⁸ for vanilla VQE (a 16.6× reduction). Scalability experiments on the transverse-field Ising model confirm that speedup is maintained as system size grows from 4 to 40 qubits, with the largest gains (up to 30×) observed at J/h = 0.5 where the energy gap is larger.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PALQO outperforms all three baselines on every tested system. Against vanilla VQE, PALQO reduces measurement shots by 7.1× on 20-qubit TFIM, 3.8× on 20-qubit Heisenberg, and 16.6× on 14-qubit BeH₂. Compared to the LSTM-based meta-learning approach [34], PALQO requires roughly half the measurements on TFIM (1.535 vs. 3.126) and a 11.2× reduction on BeH₂ (28.01 vs. 312.4). Against QuACK [36], which uses Koopman operator learning, PALQO achieves a 3.4× advantage on TFIM and a dramatic 16.5× advantage on BeH₂, where QuACK performs nearly identically to vanilla VQE (461.8 vs. 464.3). The paper attributes PALQO's superior performance to its physics-informed PDE structure, which encodes the gradient-flow dynamics directly into the loss function, enabling accurate long-horizon prediction from only a few (τ ≪ T) initial trajectory samples collected on the quantum device.

## Relation to Prior Work

PALQO sits at the intersection of two research threads: physics-informed neural networks (PINNs) for solving PDEs [Raissi et al., 2019] and the growing body of work on predicting VQA training dynamics to reduce quantum measurement costs. It departs from prior prediction-based approaches (LSTM-based meta-learning [Verdon et al., 2019] and Koopman-operator-based QuACK [Luo et al., 2024]) by reformulating the gradient-descent trajectory as a nonlinear PDE via Taylor expansion, where the leading term recovers the quantum neural tangent kernel (QNTK) [Liu et al., 2022] and the next term introduces Hessian curvature. This PDE formulation allows the physical constraints to be embedded directly into the PINN loss, rather than relying on purely data-driven sequence modeling or linear surrogate dynamics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 90% reduction in quantum measurement shots and up to 30× speedup at 40 qubits are practically significant for near-term devices where measurement budget is the binding constraint. The polynomial generalization bound (Corollary 3.1) provides a theoretical guarantee that a modest number of trajectory samples suffices, which is important for settings where quantum access is expensive. However, the high variance in speedup ratios across system configurations, the ablation finding that PDE residual terms are not strictly necessary (undermining the 'physics-informed' narrative somewhat), and the acknowledged uncertainty about scaling to the quantum-advantage regime all remain open questions. Future work on adaptive strategies and variance reduction will be critical for making the approach reliably deployable.
