## Problem

Existing hallucination mitigation methods for MLLMs face a fundamental trade-off: training-based approaches like DPO suppress hallucinations but induce likelihood displacement that degrades general reasoning, while training-free contrastive decoding methods (e.g., VCD) rely on handcrafted image perturbations (noise, downsampling) that are vision- and text-agnostic and fail to capture authentic hallucination patterns. The paper seeks a 'robust' solution that reduces hallucinations without sacrificing general capabilities and without relying on artificial degradations.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a text question as input and generates a free-form text response via autoregressive decoding. During inference, the image is passed through two separate learned projection heads (positive and negative) to produce two sets of visual embeddings, which are then used to compute contrastive logits at every decoding step. Training operates on preference datasets containing paired positive (correct) and negative (hallucinatory) responses; only the image projection layer is updated while all LLM parameters remain frozen.

## Method

DCD decouples the positive and negative sample learning that DPO performs jointly. A positive image projection g_ψ is trained via cross-entropy on correct responses, while a separate negative image projection g_φ is trained to maximize the likelihood of hallucinatory responses given the original image, effectively learning a 'hallucination-primed' embedding space. At inference, the model computes token logits conditioned on both the positive and negative projected embeddings and combines them as (1+α)·logit_w − α·logit_l, suppressing tokens that are more probable under the hallucination-primed representation. This replaces VCD's static noise perturbations with a learned, input-adaptive negative signal grounded in real MLLM errors. A key empirical finding is that training solely on negative samples (without positive samples) already yields strong hallucination suppression, since the SFT stage has already internalized positive knowledge.

## Results

On LLaVA-1.5-7B fine-tuned with BPO preference data, DCD (Pos. & Neg.) achieves an average score of 37.35, outperforming DPO (36.21), SimPO (36.58), and VCD (35.30). On hallucination benchmarks, DCD reaches 39.54 on HallusionBench and 82.87 F1 on adversarial POPE, while DPO scores 37.85 and 81.29 respectively. Critically, DCD preserves general reasoning: SEED-Bench stays at 58.61 (vs. DPO's 54.48, a 4.1-point drop) and MathVista improves to 27.9 (vs. DPO's 26.6). These gains are consistent across all four preference datasets (RLHF-V, BPO, RLAIF-V, VLFeedback), with DCD's average ranging from 36.59 to 37.35 versus DPO's 34.53–36.21.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DPO, DCD wins on general benchmarks (SEED-Bench 58.61 vs. 54.48; MathVista 27.9 vs. 26.6) while matching or exceeding DPO on hallucination metrics (HallusionBench 39.54 vs. 37.85; POPE adversarial F1 82.87 vs. 81.29), directly demonstrating that DPO's likelihood displacement degrades general reasoning without proportional hallucination gains. Against SimPO, DCD outperforms on SEED-Bench (58.61 vs. 57.07) and HallusionBench (39.54 vs. 39.53, marginal) while achieving a lower MMHal hallucination rate (0.60 vs. 0.80). Against VCD (training-free), DCD dominates across all metrics: SEED-Bench 58.61 vs. 56.98, HallusionBench 39.54 vs. 39.01, and POPE adversarial F1 82.87 vs. 81.33, confirming that learned negative projections capture authentic hallucination patterns better than static noise perturbations. Against the unmodified LLaVA-1.5 baseline, DCD improves HallusionBench by 0.32 points and POPE F1 by 0.65 points while maintaining SEED-Bench within 0.01 points, showing that hallucination suppression need not sacrifice general capability. The Neg.-Only variant nearly matches Pos. & Neg. (37.34 vs. 37.35 average), supporting the paper's key claim that negative samples alone suffice for hallucination mitigation.

## Relation to Prior Work

DCD sits at the intersection of two hallucination-mitigation lineages: training-based preference optimization (DPO, SimPO) that jointly optimizes positive-negative pairs but risks likelihood displacement, and training-free contrastive decoding (VCD, IBD) that subtracts logits from handcrafted perturbations (noise, swapped images) but may misalign with authentic hallucination distributions. The paper's key departure is to decouple the paired DPO objective into two independent cross-entropy losses on separate image projections, then repurpose the learned negative projection as the contrastive signal at inference—effectively importing preference-data knowledge into a training-free decoding framework while sidestepping the displacement problem. It also builds on the standard MLLM architecture (vision encoder + cross-modal projector + LLM) by training only the projector layer, keeping the language model frozen.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that negative (hallucinatory) samples alone suffice for meaningful hallucination suppression—while positive samples yield only marginal gains—challenges the prevailing assumption in preference learning that both sides of a pair are equally necessary, and suggests the SFT stage already internalizes positive knowledge. Practically, DCD offers a drop-in inference-time technique (contrastive decoding with a learned negative projection) that matches DPO-level hallucination reduction without the 1–4% general-capability degradation DPO incurs. Open questions include whether the approach extends to video or spatial/causal hallucinations, whether the doubled inference cost is acceptable in latency-sensitive deployments, and whether the negative projection's quality is bottlenecked by the coverage of current preference datasets.
