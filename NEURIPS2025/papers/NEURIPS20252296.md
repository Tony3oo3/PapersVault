## Problem

The classic Mallows model is defined over full permutations, but real-world preferences are typically expressed as top-k lists where users rank only a limited set of items and are indifferent to the rest. While the top-k Mallows model (TopKMM) was introduced by Chierichetti et al. (2018a), three critical algorithmic gaps remained: no RIM-like sampling scheme, no efficient method for computing choice probabilities from an assortment, and no active learning procedure for recovering the model center from choice data. This paper closes all three gaps and further generalizes the model by assigning per-item weights to the distance function.

## Input / Output

The algorithms operate on a universe of n products where a top-k list strictly orders k elements and leaves the remaining n−k incomparable. PRIM takes the TopKGMM parameters (decay β, weight vector w ∈ ℝ≥0^{k+1}, and Kendall's Tau parameter p) and produces a sampled top-k list. DYPCHIP takes an assortment A ⊆ [n] (plus a no-purchase option ∅) and the model parameters, and returns the probability that each item in A∪{∅} is chosen. BUCCHOI takes only the product set N and an assortment size r, adaptively presents assortments to a choice oracle, and outputs both the center ranking τ* and the top-k size k. The choice data is collected online rather than given as a static dataset, and the no-purchase option is treated as a first-class element in the ranking space.

## Method

The paper introduces three algorithms built around a key structural insight: partitioning the top-k list space by 'profiles' S ⊆ [k], which are the subsets of center elements that actually appear in a sampled top-k list. PRIM (Profile-based Repeated Insertion Method) first samples a profile S with probability proportional to exp(−βf(S))·Z(S), then inserts elements of S into a random base sequence in decreasing priority order, placing each element s at position j with probability ∝ exp(−βw_s·j), achieving O(k·2^γ + k log n) runtime where γ = min{k, n−k}. DYPCHIP computes choice probabilities via a three-dimensional dynamic programming table π_S(i,j,q) that tracks, at each PRIM insertion step q, which assortment item is the current 'winner' (highest-ranked in A∪{∅}) and at what position j it sits, updating by conditioning on whether the newly inserted element lands above or below the prior winner. BUCCHOI is an active learning algorithm that repeatedly presents assortments of size r, uses a subroutine FINDTOP (which compares pairwise choice frequencies Y_ij = X_ij/m against a threshold of (1+r)/2 with Hoeffding concentration) to identify the top-ranked element in each assortment, and iteratively assembles the full center ranking with Θ(r² log n) total choice samples under the condition β ≥ log 3 / w_min.

## Results

On the Sushi Preference Dataset (100 sushi types, 5K top-10 lists, 80/20 train/test split), the TopKGMM model with optimal parameters p=0.5 and β=0.05 achieves significantly lower out-of-sample choice probability prediction error than the Multinomial Logit (MNL) baseline. When the training data is partitioned into two clusters (using Kendall's Tau distance with positive silhouette scores), TopKGMM maintains its advantage over MNL. On synthetic data, DYPCHIP and PRIM achieve high accuracy with runtime that grows exponentially in k but shows minimal sensitivity to n, while BUCCHOI and FINDTOP require only logarithmic-in-n samples to recover the center and top element respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary empirical comparison is between TopKGMM and the Multinomial Logit (MNL) model on the Sushi Preference Dataset, evaluated via out-of-sample choice probability prediction error. TopKGMM consistently achieves lower test error than MNL across all tested parameter settings (p and β grid search), with the best configuration (p=0.5, β=0.05) yielding the largest gap. This advantage persists when the data is clustered into two groups and predictions are weighted by cluster size, confirming that the distance-based ranking structure of the Mallows model captures preference heterogeneity better than MNL's IIA assumption. These findings are consistent with Désir et al. (2021), who observed the same predictive advantage for the classic (full-permutation) Mallows model over MNL. The MNL baseline is the only direct competitor evaluated; no other choice models (e.g., mixed MNL, Markov chain models) are included in the head-to-head comparison.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the top-k Mallows model of Chierichetti et al. (2018a) by introducing per-item weights (generalizing Fligner & Verducci's weighted Mallows model from full permutations to top-k lists), and then develops the three algorithmic tools that were missing for this generalized setting: a sampling method (PRIM) analogous to the Repeated Insertion Method for full permutations, a dynamic-programming algorithm for choice probabilities (DYPCHIP) extending Désir et al.'s work on full-permutation Mallows choice modeling, and an active learning algorithm (BUCCHOI) for recovering the center from choice data. The key conceptual departure is that prior work either handled full permutations (RIM, GMM, Désir et al.) or top-k lists without weights (TopKMM), leaving the weighted top-k case without efficient sampling or choice-probability computation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first efficient sampling and choice-probability algorithms for the generalized top-k Mallows model, solving an open problem explicitly flagged by Chierichetti et al. (2018a) and enabling practical deployment of this model in assortment optimization and recommendation settings. The empirical comparison showing top-k Mallows substantially outperforms MNL on the Sushi dataset is consistent with Désir et al.'s findings for full permutations, reinforcing that distance-based ranking models capture richer choice behavior than IIA-based models. Key limitations include the exponential dependence on k in both PRIM and DYPCHIP, which restricts applicability to moderate k, and the fact that learning mixture parameters (multiple customer types) remains an open direction identified by the authors.
