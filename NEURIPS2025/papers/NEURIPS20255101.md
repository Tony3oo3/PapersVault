## Problem

Existing AI-generated image detectors, whether supervised or zero-shot, primarily identify artifacts in the final generated image and consequently overfit to specific generator architectures, suffering significant performance drops on unseen models. The paper identifies an underexplored signal: the image generation process itself contains discriminative information that prior methods have largely ignored, and current zero-shot approaches still achieve limited detection accuracy across diverse generators.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255101/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (cropped to 512×512) and produces a scalar authenticity score computed as the mean cosine similarity between CLIP ViT-L/14 embeddings of the original image and those of intermediate denoising outputs, thresholded at 0.75 for binary real/generated classification. The input is first encoded into latent space via a VAE encoder, inverted through 50 DDIM steps, and each denoising output is decoded back to pixel space before feature extraction, making the pipeline computationally heavier than single-pass detectors.

## Method

The core insight is that denoising outputs of AI-generated images converge to the target image more rapidly than those of real images along the denoising trajectory, because diffusion models predict their own generated samples more accurately than natural images. The method performs DDIM inversion on the input image to reconstruct its approximate sampling trajectory, collects the denoising output x̂₀(t) at each of 50 timesteps, and computes the cosine similarity between CLIP embeddings of the original image and each denoising output. To capture fine-grained differences that emerge only in later denoising stages, features are extracted from all intermediate layers of CLIP rather than just the final layer, and similarities are averaged across both timesteps and layers. The resulting score is higher for generated images (faster convergence) and lower for real images, enabling zero-shot detection without any training on synthetic data.

## Results

Across 21 generators spanning GANs and diffusion models, the proposed method achieves an average accuracy of 90.6% and average precision of 97.5%, outperforming the best supervised baseline (UnivFD: 73.8% Acc, 86.4% AP) by 16.8% in accuracy and 11.1% in AP. On the most challenging cross-paradigm setting (GAN-trained detectors tested on diffusion-generated images), the method reaches 91.0% average Acc and 97.5% average AP, surpassing the second-best zero-shot method AEROBLADE (79.1% Acc, 88.0% AP) by 11.9% and 9.5% respectively. The method also demonstrates strong robustness, maintaining 90.6% average AP under common social-media perturbations (blur, crop, JPEG, resize), compared to 85.4% for UnivFD and 80.6% for NPR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all nine baselines on the overall average across 21 generators (90.6% Acc / 97.5% AP). Among frequency-based methods, NPR [60] achieves the highest ForenSynths accuracy (94.0%) but drops to 75.3% on GenImage and 78.8% on New Generator, yielding only 82.5% average Acc; FreqNet [59] and FreDect [18] perform even worse on diffusion-generated images (69.8% and 49.9% Acc on GenImage, respectively). Among semantic-based methods, UnivFD [43] is the strongest supervised baseline on ForenSynths (94.4% Acc, 99.6% AP) but degrades sharply on diffusion models (64.8% Acc on GenImage, 61.8% on New Generator), while SIDA [25] shows inconsistent performance (72.9% on New Generator but only 53.1% on GenImage). Among zero-shot methods, AEROBLADE [48] is the closest competitor on GenImage (84.5% Acc) but fails on GAN-generated images (45.7% Acc on ForenSynths); MIBD [7] and ZeroFake [53] trail the proposed method by 25.6% and 28.3% in average accuracy, respectively. The paper reports average improvements of 8.1% in accuracy and 9.8% in AP over the state-of-the-art across all 21 generators.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in AI-generated image detection, which focuses on identifying artifacts in the final output (frequency anomalies, semantic inconsistencies, or reconstruction errors), by instead exploiting signals embedded in the generation *process* itself. It builds directly on DDIM inversion (Song et al., 2020) to simulate the sampling trajectory of an input image, and on CLIP (Radford et al., 2021) for semantic feature extraction, but the key conceptual contribution is the observation that denoising outputs of generated images converge to the target faster than those of real images—a frequency-autoregressive property of diffusion models that had not previously been leveraged for detection. Among zero-shot baselines, it differs from AEROBLADE (VAE reconstruction error), ZeroFake (prompt-modified SSIM), and MIBD (manifold curvature) by using trajectory-level similarity rather than a single reconstruction or perturbation signal.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The zero-shot nature of the method eliminates the need to collect and maintain training data for each new generator, addressing a critical practical bottleneck as generative models proliferate rapidly. The consistent ~0.75 decision threshold across all 21 tested generators (spanning GANs and diffusion models from 2018 to 2025) suggests a stable, generator-agnostic signal rather than a fragile artifact pattern. However, performance degrades under heavy corruption (JPEG, blur) and notably for FLUX, hinting that the convergence bias may weaken as generative models produce more faithful denoising trajectories. Open questions include whether adaptive timestep/layer weighting can close the remaining gap, and whether the signal persists as future models eliminate the distributional mismatch between learned and true data distributions.
