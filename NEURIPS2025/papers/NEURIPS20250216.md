## Problem

Diffusion-based text-guided image editing methods require users to manually brute-force tune multiple interdependent hyperparameters (inversion timesteps, cross-attention replacement ratios, attention weights) whose optimal values vary significantly across images. Exhaustive search over K hyperparameters each with N values incurs O(TN^K) computational cost, which is impractical for real-world deployment. The paper asks whether optimal per-image hyperparameter identification can be cast as a sequential decision-making problem solvable in a single denoising pass.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a source image, its corresponding text prompt, an edit prompt, and a binary edit-region mask (generated via SAM segmentation), and produces both the edited image and a per-timestep hyperparameter sequence of length T (default T=50). Unlike prior approaches that select a single global hyperparameter value, AutoEdit outputs a distinct hyperparameter configuration at every denoising step, enabling fine-grained temporal control. The policy model adds only ~1.1M parameters and 0.6% inference overhead to the base U-Net, operating on latent-space features rather than pixel-space inputs.

## Method

AutoEdit formulates hyperparameter selection as a Markov Decision Process embedded within the diffusion denoising trajectory: each noisy latent x_t at timestep t is a state, and the hyperparameter configuration H_t (e.g., which prompt to use, whether to replace cross-attention, what attention weight to apply) constitutes the action. A two-phase RL strategy addresses the exponential state space: Phase 1 pretrains the policy via cross-entropy against known priors (e.g., inversion timesteps in [0.35T, 0.95T], attention ratios in [0.2, 0.8]), constraining exploration to a promising subspace; Phase 2 fine-tunes with PPO using a composite reward combining CLIP score (prompt alignment) and MSE (background preservation), with a KL penalty against the Phase-1 policy. The policy network is a lightweight head on the frozen U-Net encoder that concatenates features extracted under both source and edit prompts, applies spatial pooling, and predicts K discrete hyperparameter values via separate linear heads. This reduces the search complexity from O(TN^K) to O(T), achieving near-optimal hyperparameters in a single denoising pass.

## Results

On PieBench (700 samples), AutoEdit consistently improves background preservation across all tested editing methods. For DDPM-Inversion on SD 1.4, PSNR improves from 22.66 to 27.25 and MSE drops from 53.33 to 31.18, while CLIP Edited score rises from 23.02 to 26.22. For P2P on SD 1.4, LPIPS decreases from 61.78 to 60.60 and CLIP Whole increases from 25.44 to 26.55. The LLM Score (a VLM-based editing quality metric) improves across all methods, e.g., from 0.96 to 1.12 for DDIM-Inversion and from 1.08 to 1.22 for P2P, with only negligible inference overhead (+0.6% time, +0.6% parameters).

## Baselines & Comparisons

AutoEdit is evaluated as a plug-in on top of nine editing methods (DDIM-Inversion, DDPM-Inversion, PnP Inversion, P2P, MasaCtrl, UltraEdit, InstructPix2Pix, Null-text Inversion, and flow-based Taming Flow/FireFlow), comparing default hyperparameters against AutoEdit-optimized ones. AutoEdit wins on background preservation (PSNR, SSIM, MSE, LPIPS) across all methods, with the largest gains on DDIM-Inversion (MSE halved from 103.95 to 52.94) and MasaCtrl (MSE reduced from 86.97 to 46.28). On CLIP Edited scores, AutoEdit improves P2P (22.29→23.88), PnP (22.10→25.02), and DDIM-Inversion (23.30→26.31), but shows a slight decrease for DDPM-Inversion on SDXL (23.02→22.90), reflecting the inherent edit-fidelity vs. background-preservation trade-off. The LLM Score (VLM-based editing correctness) improves for every method, with the most notable jump for InstructPix2Pix (0.65→0.82) and P2P (1.08→1.22). For flow-based methods (Taming Flow, FireFlow), AutoEdit similarly improves PSNR by ~2.3 dB and LLM Score by 0.07–0.08. The paper also reports that AutoEdit's reward is within 0.07–0.10 of the best-of-3 brute-force hyperparameter combinations (Table 8), confirming near-optimal convergence at O(T) cost versus O(TN^K) for exhaustive search.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two research threads: diffusion-based image editing (P2P, DDIM/DDPM Inversion, PnP, MasaCtrl) and RL for image generation (DPOK, prompt optimization). The closest prior work is OIR [45], which brute-force searches a single hyperparameter (inversion timestep) at O(T²) cost; AutoEdit generalizes this to K simultaneous hyperparameters and replaces exhaustive search with a PPO-based policy that operates at O(T). The RL framework is adapted from prompt-optimization and diffusion-alignment work [24, 10, 46, 29], but repurposed to select discrete hyperparameter configurations at each denoising step rather than to optimize prompts or model weights.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for manual trial-and-error hyperparameter tuning, AutoEdit lowers a significant usability barrier for non-expert users of diffusion-based editing tools, and the O(T) vs. O(TN^K) complexity reduction makes multi-parameter search tractable. However, the method inherits the quality ceiling of the underlying editing pipeline (if the base method cannot perform an edit, AutoEdit cannot fix it), and the reward design (CLIP + MSE, or LLM-based for small regions) may not capture all dimensions of perceptual editing quality. Open questions include whether the approach extends to continuous or highly interdependent hyperparameter spaces, and whether the two-phase training strategy generalizes to editing methods with substantially different denoising architectures (e.g., flow-based models with different step counts).
