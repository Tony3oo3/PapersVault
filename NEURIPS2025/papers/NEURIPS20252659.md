## Problem

The widely adopted τ-dormant neuron ratio (ReDo) identifies inactive neurons by their activation magnitude, but this signal becomes unreliable in modern RL architectures featuring residual connections, normalization layers, non-ReLU activations, and multi-branch structures. In these settings, a neuron's output magnitude no longer reflects its true learning capacity, causing ReDo to miss genuinely dormant neurons while incorrectly resetting healthy ones, ultimately failing to restore agent performance.

## Input / Output

GraMa consumes gradient magnitudes |∇_{h_i^ℓ} L(x)| that are already computed during backpropagation at each training step, requiring no additional forward-pass storage of intermediate activations. It produces a normalized per-neuron learning capacity score G_i^ℓ (Eq. 2) by dividing each neuron's expected gradient magnitude by the layer mean, then flags neurons with G_i^ℓ ≤ τ as inactive. ReGraMa converts these scores into reinitialization actions: incoming weights are reset to the original weight distribution and outgoing weights are zeroed, applied every Δt training steps.

## Method

The core insight is a shift in statistical objective from activation values (expressive capacity) to gradient magnitudes (learning capacity), yielding the GraMa metric which retains the same normalized-ratio form as the τ-dormant neuron ratio but operates on |∇h_i^ℓ L(x)| instead of h_i^ℓ(x). ReGraMa periodically computes GraMa scores across all layers and reinitializes neurons falling below threshold τ, following the same reset pipeline as ReDo. The paper proves (Theorem 1) that in traditional MLP+ReLU architectures, ReGraMa identifies exactly the same dormant neurons as ReDo, establishing equivalence in the regime where activation-based metrics are known to work. In complex architectures, GraMa's gradient-based signal remains stable because it directly measures a neuron's contribution to parameter updates regardless of branch fusion, normalization rescaling, or non-ReLU saturation effects. The approach is validated across three architecture families—residual BRO-net, diffusion-based DACER, and MLP SAC with ReLU/Tanh/Sigmoid—on MuJoCo and DeepMind Control Suite benchmarks, consistently outperforming ReDo while imposing negligible computational overhead.

## Results

On the BRO-net architecture across four DeepMind Control Suite tasks, ReGraMa achieves normalized performance of 1.21 (Humanoid Stand), 1.16 (Humanoid Run), 1.12 (Dog Stand), and 1.08 (Dog Walk) relative to the vanilla baseline, consistently outperforming all competing methods. On the diffusion-based DACER policy (Ant, Walker2d), ReGraMa maintains robust performance while ReDo provides only marginal gains. On the Quadruped Run task with DrQv2, ReGraMa improves the vanilla score from 649.13 to 706.0, and combining it with weight decay further raises it to 751.31.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReGraMa decisively outperforms ReDo (Sokar et al., 2023), the primary activation-based baseline, on all four DMC tasks with BRO-net; notably, ReDo actually degrades below the vanilla baseline on Humanoid Run (0.96), Dog Stand (0.92), and Dog Walk (0.94), while ReGraMa improves on all four. Against S&P (Ash & Adams, 2019), ReGraMa wins on every task by a clear margin (e.g., 1.21 vs. 1.05 on Humanoid Stand). ReGraMa also surpasses ReBorn (Qin et al., 2024) across all four tasks, with the largest gap on Dog Walk (1.08 vs. 0.91). The closest competitor is CBP (Dohare et al., 2024), which slightly edges out ReGraMa on Dog Walk (1.09 vs. 1.08) but is substantially worse on Humanoid Run (0.99 vs. 1.16) and Humanoid Stand (1.18 vs. 1.21). Across the diffusion-based DACER policy and MLP-SAC with varied activations, ReGraMa consistently maintains or improves performance while ReDo's effectiveness collapses, confirming the gradient-based metric's architectural generality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the dormant neuron phenomenon and the ReDo resetting mechanism of Sokar et al. (2023), which uses the τ-dormant neuron ratio based on activation magnitudes to identify and reinitialize inactive neurons. The key departure is a conceptual reframe: rather than measuring a neuron's expressive capacity (how strongly it activates), GraMa measures its learning capacity (how effectively it receives gradient signal), arguing the latter is the more relevant quantity in modern architectures with residual connections, normalization, and non-ReLU activations. The work also situates itself within the broader plasticity-loss literature (Lyle et al., Nikishin et al., Dohare et al.) and the neuron-resetting line (ReDo, ReBorn, S&P, CBP), positioning gradient magnitude as an architecture-agnostic alternative to activation-based dormancy detection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GraMa/ReGraMa provides a lightweight, drop-in replacement for activation-based neuron health metrics that works across residual networks, diffusion-based policies, and diverse activation functions—architectures that are increasingly standard in deep RL. The finding that ReDo can actively degrade performance (not merely fail to help) in these settings is practically important for practitioners who have been applying it by default. Open questions remain: the evaluation covers only three architecture families and a limited set of continuous-control tasks, and the paper acknowledges that extension to transformer-based policies, multi-task settings, and interaction with other regularization strategies (e.g., weight decay, L2 initialization) is left for future work.
