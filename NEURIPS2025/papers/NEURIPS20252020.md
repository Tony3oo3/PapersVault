## Problem

Hebbian learning rules, while biologically plausible and local, suffer from unconstrained weight growth and lack of feedback mediation, making them difficult to scale to deep nonlinear networks. Existing Hebbian-inspired methods for deep architectures (e.g., SoftHebb) often rely on feedback connections for inter-layer credit assignment, undermining the very biological plausibility that motivates them. The paper asks whether a purely feedforward, block-wise Hebbian objective can be derived that is both theoretically grounded and practically effective for modern CNNs.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 64×64 for Tiny-ImageNet) and produces two types of output: a high-dimensional feature map Y′ from the main convolutional block f (used downstream for classification via a backprop-trained FC head), and a low-dimensional structural projection Z (256-d) from the auxiliary block φ (used only to compute the Hebbian structural loss). The network is trained greedily layer-by-layer: each 3×3 convolutional block (384→768→1536 channels) is optimized independently with the SPHeRe loss before moving to the next, with no end-to-end backpropagation through the Hebbian layers.

## Method

SPHeRe derives its core loss by simplifying the equivalent loss of Oja's rule—removing the numerically unstable (XX^T)^{-1} term—to obtain L_SPHeRe = ‖YY^T − XX^T‖_F², which the paper proves (Lemma 4.2.1) is equivalent to finding the optimal rank-M principal subspace projection of the input. The key architectural innovation is a lightweight auxiliary nonlinear block φ (1×1 conv → adaptive pooling → FC to 256-d) that projects the main block's output Y′ into a low-dimensional Z where the Gram-matching loss is computed, decoupling the high-dimensional feature learning in f from the low-dimensional structural preservation objective. An orthogonality constraint L_orth = ‖Z^T Z − I‖_F² is added to encourage feature decorrelation. This 'main-pathway learning, side-pathway supervision' design keeps the entire training purely feedforward and block-wise, requiring no feedback connections, while the gradient ∇_W = 4X^T(YY^T − XX^T)Y retains the Hebbian form (pre-synaptic × post-synaptic correlation modulated by a structural error term).

## Results

SPHeRe achieves 81.11% top-1 accuracy on CIFAR-10, 56.79% on CIFAR-100, and 40.33% on Tiny-ImageNet, setting new state-of-the-art results among unsupervised synaptic plasticity methods. In continual learning, SPHeRe-EWC reaches 76.53% on Split-CIFAR100 and 67.05% on Split-TinyImageNet, outperforming all prior CL baselines. In cross-dataset transfer learning, SPHeRe features trained on Tiny-ImageNet achieve 80.03% on CIFAR-10 (only 1.08% below direct training), and features trained on CIFAR-10 achieve 37.7% on Tiny-ImageNet (2.63% below direct training).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SPHeRe outperforms all Hebbian/synaptic-plasticity baselines across all three classification benchmarks. Against the closest competitor SoftHebb [12], SPHeRe gains +0.81% on CIFAR-10 (81.11% vs 80.3%), +0.79% on CIFAR-100 (56.79% vs 56.0%), and a substantial +6.21% on Tiny-ImageNet (40.33% vs 34.12% reproduced). Against earlier Hebbian methods (D-CSNN [30], Hard WTA [11, 31]), SPHeRe leads by 6–8% on CIFAR-10 and 11–24% on CIFAR-100. In continual learning, SPHeRe-EWC surpasses GPM [35] by +1.53% on Split-CIFAR100 and +1.05% on Split-TinyImageNet, and outperforms EWC [32], HAT [33], and OWM [34] by 3–6%. In the SimCLR [22] comparison (Appendix E), SPHeRe achieves 81.13% test accuracy versus SimCLR's 71.94% under SimCLR's own best settings, though this comparison uses different optimal hyperparameters for each method.

## Relation to Prior Work

SPHeRe derives its core loss by simplifying the equivalent loss of Oja's rule, arriving at a Gram-matrix matching objective that is mathematically identical to the similarity-matching framework established by Pehlevan, Chklovskii, and collaborators [8,9,10]. The key departure is architectural: while prior deep Hebbian methods (Obeid et al. [10], SoftHebb [12]) theoretically require feedback connections for inter-layer credit assignment, SPHeRe introduces a purely feedforward, block-wise design where a lightweight auxiliary projection module (φ) decouples the structural preservation objective from the main network's high-dimensional feature learning. This positions SPHeRe as an engineering solution to a problem that prior work addressed through feedback-mediated credit assignment, rather than a new optimization objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SPHeRe demonstrates that Hebbian-inspired unsupervised learning can achieve competitive performance on standard benchmarks without feedback, offering a biologically plausible alternative to backpropagation for pre-training. However, the method's practical impact is constrained by its depth limitation—performance gains diminish as convolutional layers increase due to noise accumulation in purely local learning—and its role as a pre-training strategy that still requires a backprop-trained classification head. The open question of integrating global contextual signals (analogous to neuromodulatory pathways in biology) to address this depth bottleneck is a promising direction that could determine whether Hebbian methods scale to modern architectures.
