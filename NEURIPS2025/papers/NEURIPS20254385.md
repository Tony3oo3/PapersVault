## Problem

Traditional score-based metrics for weather radar forecast evaluation (e.g., CSI, SSIM) capture only pixel-level errors and cannot provide the descriptive, interpretable, and temporally-aware assessments that meteorological experts deliver. This paper asks whether multi-modal large language models can be adapted to perform expert-like quality analysis of radar forecasts, producing both quantitative ratings and detailed natural-language assessment reports that account for dynamic evolution of convective systems.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254385/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives VIL radar data rendered as RGB images using the SEVIR six-level precipitation colormap, in two modalities: single frame pairs (one predicted + one ground truth frame) and 12-frame sequences (predicted + ground truth), each accompanied by a randomly sampled text question prompt. For rating tasks (Frame Rating, Sequence Rating), the output is a structured JSON with discrete four-level ratings (Great/Good/Fair/Poor) for specific general attributes plus an overall score. For assessment tasks (Frame Assessment, Sequence Assessment), the output is a multi-paragraph natural-language report that first describes performance per attribute (e.g., shape changes, high-value retention, dynamic consistency) and then synthesizes an overall quality judgment with explanations.

## Method

RadarQA builds on Qwen-2.5-VL-7B and is trained with a three-stage pipeline: (1) supervised fine-tuning with LoRA (rank 8) on the RQA-70K dataset to acquire basic task-solving and interpretation capabilities; (2) GRPO reinforcement learning on rating tasks using two task-specific rewards—a format reward for valid JSON parsing and an accuracy reward for correct attribute-level predictions—leveraging the interpretation abilities from Stage 1 to improve self-reasoning; (3) a lightweight post-training step with LoRA (rank 4) on a small subset to refine output format and performance. The key data contribution is the Attribute-Informed Generation annotation pipeline: 17 perception-based attributes are labeled by human meteorologists via a structured questionnaire, 20 metric-based attributes are computed by scripts with expert-set thresholds, and all 37 attributes are fed to GPT-4o (alongside the visual inputs) to produce fluent, physically grounded assessment reports. The scientific attribute library organizes 35 attributes into five super-categories (Intensity, Precipitation Conservation, Precipitation Dynamic Distribution, Convective Cycle, Morphology) covering both frame-level and sequence-level features, enabling the model to reason about convective genesis, dissipation, trajectory, and structural evolution.

## Results

On the sequence rating task, RadarQA achieves 66.17% overall accuracy, substantially outperforming the best open-source baseline (InternVL2.5-8B at 36.70%) and the best API-based baseline (GPT-4o at 45.00%). On the sequence assessment task, RadarQA scores 6.58 on GPT-4 Score, surpassing the strongest general MLLM (o1 at 5.49). For frame rating, RadarQA reaches 61.51% overall accuracy versus 48.84% for GPT-4o, and for frame assessment it achieves a GPT-4 Score of 6.87 versus 5.89 for Claude 3.7 Sonnet. Notably, RadarQA exceeds 80% accuracy on the High Value Retain attribute in sequence rating, a dimension where all baselines fall below 50%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RadarQA outperforms all seven baselines across every task and metric. Among open-source MLLMs, InternVL2.5-8B is the strongest on sequence rating (36.70% overall) but still trails RadarQA by nearly 30 percentage points; Qwen2.5-VL-72B, despite being 10× larger, scores only 19.80% on sequence rating and 7.92% on High Value Retain, indicating that scale alone does not confer meteorological understanding. Among API-based models, GPT-4o leads on sequence rating overall (45.00%) but collapses to 4.99% on High Value Retain, while o1 achieves the best GPT-4 Score on sequence assessment among general MLLMs (5.49) yet still falls 1.09 points short of RadarQA (6.58). On frame rating, GPT-4o is the closest competitor at 48.84% overall accuracy, but RadarQA extends this to 61.51%, with particularly large gaps on Miss (67.67% vs. 23.85%) and High Value Match (69.19% vs. 11.04%). The consistent margin across both open-source and proprietary baselines underscores that domain-specific training on RQA-70K with the multi-stage pipeline is essential rather than a property of model scale or general reasoning ability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the MLLM-based quality assessment paradigm—previously applied to natural images (Q-Insight, Q-Bench), videos (Q-Bench-Video), and 3D point clouds (LLM-PCQA)—to the specialized domain of weather radar forecast verification. It departs from traditional score-based metrics (CSI, SSIM, POD) that the authors argue lack descriptive capability, interpretability, and temporal awareness, and from general MLLMs that have not been adapted to meteorological reasoning. The multi-stage training pipeline (LoRA SFT → GRPO with task-specific rewards → post-training) follows the broader trend of combining supervised and reinforcement learning for MLLM specialization, but the hybrid annotation pipeline combining human expert labeling with automated metric computation is a domain-specific contribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RadarQA and RQA-70K provide the first large-scale, expert-validated benchmark for MLLM-based weather radar quality analysis, filling a gap between pixel-level verification metrics and the holistic, interpretable judgments meteorologists actually make. The demonstrated large performance gap between general MLLMs (even 72B and o1) and the domain-adapted model underscores that weather forecast quality analysis is not yet solvable by off-the-shelf multimodal models. Open questions include whether assessment outputs can serve as training signals or rewards for forecasting models, whether the framework extends to comparing two predictions, and whether the approach generalizes beyond VIL radar to other meteorological modalities.
