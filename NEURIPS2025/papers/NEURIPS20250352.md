## Problem

Time series foundation models suffer from training data scarcity and distribution imbalance relative to CV and NLP, with domains like finance and healthcare remaining underrepresented. This imbalance, combined with scaling-law effects, causes performance bias and degraded generalization on out-of-distribution data. The paper asks whether unrestricted synthetic data generation, grounded in complex dynamical system theory, can replace or supplement real-world pre-training corpora.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250352/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

During pre-training, the model consumes paired inputs: multivariate time series X ∈ R^{M×L} (M ≤ 6 channels, L = 256) sampled from mixed Gaussian/uniform distributions or ARMA(p,q) processes, and their corresponding symbolic expressions f(·) tokenized as text. The S² dataset contains 40M such pairs totaling 50B time points. For downstream fine-tuning, the time series encoder takes patched, instance-normalized series segments and produces task-specific outputs: forecasted values (MSE/MAE), class labels (accuracy), imputed values, or anomaly scores (F1).

## Method

The S² generation mechanism randomly constructs symbolic expressions as binary trees (binary operators +, −, ×; unary operators including sin, cos, log, exp, sqrt, etc.; affine transformations on variables and operators), then evaluates them on sampled input series to produce output series, yielding an infinitely scalable dataset with guaranteed series-to-symbol correspondence. SymTime employs a dual-encoder architecture: a 6-layer Transformer time series encoder trained with masked time series modeling (MTM) on non-overlapping patches, and a 6-layer DistilBERT symbol encoder trained with masked language modeling (MLM) on tokenized expressions. A series-symbol contrastive learning objective with momentum encoders (MoCo-style) aligns the [CLS] embeddings of both modalities, and a momentum distillation loss (inspired by ALBEF) treats masking as noise to stabilize representation learning. The total pre-training loss combines all four terms, and fine-tuning uses only the time series encoder backbone with task-specific heads (linear for classification, trend/periodic decomposition for reconstruction tasks).

## Results

On long-term forecasting (96 look-back, averaged over 8 datasets), SymTime achieves an average MSE of 0.336 and MAE of 0.349, outperforming all baselines including foundation models Moirai (MSE 0.346), Timer (MSE 0.372), and Time-LLM (MSE 0.341). On short-term forecasting (M4), SymTime attains the best OWA of 0.849 (vs. 0.856 for TimeMixer). For anomaly detection, SymTime achieves the highest average F1-score of 86.31% across 5 datasets, surpassing UniTS (85.73%) and GPT4TS (85.01%). On classification (10 UEA datasets), SymTime reaches 74.9% average accuracy, the best among all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SymTime, pre-trained on the synthetic S² dataset, outperforms all 23 baselines across all five TSA tasks. In long-term forecasting, SymTime (avg MSE 0.336) beats the closest foundation model Time-LLM (0.341) by 1.5%, and significantly outperforms Timer (0.372) and Moirai (0.346), despite using a much lighter Transformer backbone rather than a pre-trained LLM. On short-term forecasting (M4), SymTime achieves the best OWA of 0.849, edging out TimeMixer (0.856) and Peri-midFormer (0.859). For imputation, SymTime (avg MSE 0.049) surpasses GPT4TS (0.053) and TimesNet (0.052), with particularly large gains on high-channel datasets like ECL (0.054 vs. 0.093 for GPT4TS). In anomaly detection, SymTime leads with 86.31% average F1, beating the real-data-pretrained UniTS (85.73%) and GPT4TS (85.01%). The ablation study further confirms that removing the symbolic encoder or contrastive learning degrades performance, validating that the dual-modality pre-training paradigm—not just the backbone architecture—drives the gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper draws on Takens' theorem and symbolic dynamics to justify a dual-modality (time series + symbolic expression) pre-training paradigm, extending the SNIP [12] idea of bridging symbolic and numeric realms into a full foundation model architecture. It departs from existing TSA foundation models (Moirai, Timer, TimeGPT, COMET) that are pre-trained on real-world datasets by instead generating an infinitely scalable synthetic S² dataset from randomly sampled symbolic expressions applied to ARMA/mixed-distribution inputs. The architecture combines standard masked time series modeling (MTM) and masked language modeling (MLM) with a novel series-symbol contrastive objective using momentum encoders, borrowing the momentum distillation strategy from ALBEF [60] and the contrastive framework from MoCo [59].

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The core practical contribution is an infinitely scalable synthetic data pipeline that sidesteps the data scarcity and domain imbalance plaguing real-world TSA datasets, with evidence that 50B-scale synthetic pre-training rivals or exceeds real-data pre-training across five tasks. However, the performance margins over strong baselines (e.g., 0.336 vs. 0.341 average MSE on long-term forecasting) are modest, and the symbolic expression space, while diverse, may not capture all relevant real-world dynamics (e.g., regime shifts, exogenous shocks). Open questions include whether the approach scales to truly large model sizes, whether richer symbolic grammars (e.g., ODE/PDE systems) would further close the gap to real-data pre-training, and whether the contrastive alignment generalizes to tasks beyond the five evaluated.
