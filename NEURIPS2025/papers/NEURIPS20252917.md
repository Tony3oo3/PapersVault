## Problem

Estimating room impulse responses at arbitrary emitter positions from only a sparse set (fewer than 10) of measured emitters remains unsolved: existing neural acoustic field methods require hundreds or thousands of densely deployed emitters to generalize, while differentiable ray tracing relies on simplified planar geometry that fails in complex rooms. This gap is practically motivated by the hardware asymmetry—microphones are compact and cheap to deploy densely, whereas speakers are bulky, power-hungry, and interfere when operated simultaneously.

## Input / Output

The method consumes sparse measured impulse responses (fewer than 10 emitter positions, each paired with ~5K densely sampled listener positions) along with full emitter and listener poses (3D position + orientation). It outputs room impulse responses at arbitrary novel emitter positions, resampled to 16 kHz (AcoustiX, RAF) or 24 kHz (MeshRIR). A key structural constraint is the inherent asymmetry: listener positions are dense while emitter positions are sparse, which the method exploits by treating dense microphones as virtual speakers.

## Method

Versa leverages acoustic reciprocity—the principle that the path impact function Γ(t) is invariant under emitter/listener pose exchange—through two complementary strategies. Versa-ELE is a model-agnostic data augmentation that swaps emitter and listener poses while preserving the impulse response, generating physically valid virtual samples that multiply effective emitter count from a few to the full number of listener positions; it requires matching gain patterns. Versa-SSL addresses asymmetric gain patterns (e.g., omnidirectional speaker vs. directional microphone) by exploiting AVR's acoustic volume rendering to independently control the listener gain pattern: it extracts the emitter's directional pattern via spherical harmonics, swaps it into the listener slot, and enforces a self-supervision loss L_a-ssl(h₁, h₂) that requires identical impulse responses before and after pose exchange, combined with the standard audio loss in a two-stage training pipeline.

## Results

On the AcoustiX-Diff dataset (5 emitter positions, different emitter/listener gain patterns), Versa-SSL applied to AVR achieves STFT 1.39, C50 2.09, and EDT 21.5 on Scene 1, outperforming all baselines. Averaged across all models and simulated scenes, Versa-ELE yields 34% improvement on C50 and 31% on STFT; Versa-SSL further improves over Versa-ELE by 24% on C50 and 48% on STFT on the AVR model. In total, Versa-SSL improves vanilla AVR by 49% at C50 and 66% at STFT. A perceptual user study (15 participants) shows 93.3% preference for Versa-ELE over vanilla and 85% preference for Versa-SSL over Versa-ELE in overall similarity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Versa-ELE is model-agnostic and consistently improves every neural acoustic field baseline: on AcoustiX-Same (Scene 1), it reduces STFT from 4.69→1.12 for NAF, 1.96→1.12 for INRAS, 1.69→1.12 for AV-NeRF, and 1.82→1.12 for AVR, while also improving C50 by roughly 40–67% across models. On the harder AcoustiX-Diff setting (asymmetric gain patterns), Versa-ELE still improves baselines but less dramatically (e.g., AVR C50 from 4.96→2.83), and Versa-SSL closes the remaining gap (C50 2.83→2.09, STFT 1.83→1.39). DiffRIR [59] is excluded from Versa because its explicit path tracing already enforces reciprocity; it achieves competitive C50 (2.52) but higher Env. error (7.3) than Versa-SSL (3.6). Traditional interpolation baselines (NN, Linear) are substantially outperformed on all metrics, with C50 errors 5–9× higher than Versa-SSL. On real-world MeshRIR (7 emitters), Versa-ELE reduces AVR's STFT from 3.78→1.42 and C50 from 4.97→1.79, confirming gains beyond simulation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Acoustic Volume Rendering (AVR) [34], which provides the explicit gain-pattern modeling that enables Versa-SSL's self-supervision strategy, and on the broader family of neural acoustic field methods (NAF, INRAS, AV-NeRF) that learn spatially continuous impulse response fields from data. Conceptually, it departs from prior work by repurposing acoustic reciprocity—a long-established principle in wave physics—into a practical training strategy (data augmentation via pose exchange and a physics-consistency loss) rather than using it as a physical simulation constraint as in PINN-based approaches. The paper also draws an explicit analogy to relighting in computer graphics, positioning 'resounding' as the acoustic counterpart to dynamic illumination control.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work addresses a concrete deployment bottleneck: speakers are expensive, bulky, and interfere when deployed densely, while microphones are cheap and compact, creating an inherent asymmetry that limits current neural acoustic field methods. The model-agnostic nature of Versa-ELE (applicable to any existing neural acoustic field) and the 30%+ average improvements make it a practical drop-in enhancement for the field. Key limitations include the continued need for dense listener positions per scene and reliance on geometric acoustics, which degrades at low frequencies where wavelengths approach scene dimensions. Open questions include whether few-shot or zero-shot emitter estimation is possible by integrating audio-visual correspondence, and whether the reciprocity-as-training-constraint paradigm generalizes to other wave phenomena (light transport, RF propagation).
