## Problem

Pre-trained text-to-video diffusion models cannot be directly applied to 360° panoramic video generation due to two spatial representation gaps: equirectangular projection introduces extreme horizontal stretching in polar regions (latitudinal distortion), and conventional models lack continuity awareness at the left-right longitude boundary (seam artifacts). Additionally, existing panoramic video datasets are too small and semantically imbalanced to support effective fine-tuning.

## Input / Output

The model takes a free-form text prompt as input and produces an equirectangular-projection 360° panoramic video at 448×896 resolution with 81 frames per clip. Beyond base generation, the same model supports zero-shot downstream tasks—super-resolution, semantic inpainting, video outpainting, and long-video generation via sliding-window latent-space inference—by conditioning on user-provided text and optionally a masked or low-resolution input video.

## Method

PanoWan lifts the pre-trained Wan 2.1 DiT backbone to the panoramic domain via LoRA (rank 64, ~1.6% of parameters) on the PANOVID dataset (13K+ captioned clips, 944 hours), while introducing three minimal mechanisms to bridge spatial representation gaps. Latitude-aware sampling remaps the initial Gaussian noise horizontally by cos(θ) at each row to match the spherical frequency distribution, preventing polar distortion. Rotated semantic denoising circularly shifts the latent grid by a different offset at each denoising step, spreading the inherent seam error uniformly across all longitudes rather than concentrating it at one boundary. Padded pixel-wise decoding circularly extends the latent code by r columns before VAE decoding and center-crops the result, giving the decoder panoramic context across the seam. Together these modules preserve the generative prior of the frozen backbone while enforcing spherical geometric consistency.

## Results

PanoWan achieves state-of-the-art performance across all six evaluation metrics on the PANOVID test set (67 clips). On general metrics, it attains an FVD of 1281.21 and VideoCLIP-XL of 21.86, outperforming 360DVD (FVD 1750.36, VideoCLIP-XL 20.27) and DynamicScaler (FVD 2146.04, VideoCLIP-XL 21.13). On panoramic-specific metrics, PanoWan scores 36.4% on Motion Pattern and 35.2% on Scene Richness (user preference), dramatically exceeding 360DVD (5.8% / 6.6%) and DynamicScaler (4.0% / 2.6%), while also achieving the best End Continuity (0.0270, lower is better). In a human-preference study with 25 participants over 50 prompts, PanoWan is preferred in 71.36% of cases versus 16.72% for 360DVD and 11.92% for DynamicScaler.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PanoWan outperforms both baselines on every reported metric. Against 360DVD (CVPR 2024), PanoWan reduces FVD by 27% (1281 vs 1750), improves VideoCLIP-XL by 1.59 points, and most dramatically increases Motion Pattern preference from 5.8% to 36.4% and Scene Richness from 6.6% to 35.2%, indicating far more dynamic and diverse generated content. Against DynamicScaler (CVPR 2025), the FVD gap is even larger (1281 vs 2146, a 40% reduction), and the panoramic-specific metrics show the same pattern of substantial gains. The End Continuity metric (lower is better) confirms PanoWan's seamless longitude transitions: 0.0270 versus 0.0323 for 360DVD and 0.0339 for DynamicScaler. The human-preference study reinforces these quantitative findings, with PanoWan preferred in over 71% of pairwise comparisons, while the two baselines split the remaining ~29% roughly evenly.

## Relation to Prior Work

PanoWan positions itself as a 'lifting' framework that adapts a strong pre-trained conventional text-to-video model (Wan 2.1) to the panoramic domain with minimal architectural changes, following the philosophy of GEN3C in preserving generative priors. It departs from prior panoramic video generation methods (360DVD, DynamicScaler, PanoDiT, VideoPanda) that either train panoramic-specific architectures from scratch or compose local patches at inference, by instead diagnosing the spatial representation gap between ERP panoramas and Cartesian videos and addressing it through three targeted mechanisms: latitude-aware noise sampling, rotated semantic denoising, and padded pixel-wise decoding. The contribution of PANOVID (13K clips, 944 hours) also represents a significant scale-up over prior panoramic video datasets like WEB360 (2,114 clips) and Argus's filtered subset, with structured captions and semantic balancing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work demonstrates that the gap between conventional and panoramic video generation can be bridged with surprisingly lightweight modifications (~1.6% of parameters via LoRA, 18 hours on 8×H100), making it practical to leverage the rapidly improving ecosystem of large video diffusion models for 360° content without retraining from scratch. The PANOVID dataset fills a critical data gap that has constrained the field, and the clean decomposition of panoramic artifacts into latitude distortion, semantic seam, and pixel seam provides a reusable diagnostic framework. Open questions include whether the latitude/longitude mechanisms generalize to other spherical projections or non-Equirectangular formats, whether the content-forgetting limitation in long videos can be addressed without sacrificing the lightweight fine-tuning paradigm, and whether the approach extends to higher resolutions where ERP distortion becomes more severe.
