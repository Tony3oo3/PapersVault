## Problem

Standard Virtual Adversarial Training (VAT) bounds adversarial perturbations with the Euclidean norm, which ignores the temporal structure of time series data and produces jagged, spiky perturbations that disrupt low-frequency trend information. This makes VAT inefficient at enforcing smoothness of predictive distributions for semi-supervised time series classification, where labeled data is scarce and the model must generalize from limited supervision.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254200/cropped_page8_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes discrete time series (univariate or multivariate) sampled at N equidistant points, treated as functions in L²(T), along with a small labeled set and a large unlabeled set. It outputs a K-class probability vector via an 8-layer Temporal Convolutional Network (TCN). The key structural detail is that adversarial perturbations are constrained by a Sobolev H^{-s} norm (s=2 by default), computed in practice via the spectral decomposition of the discrete negative Laplacian matrix (I_N + L_N)^α, which encodes smoothness constraints directly into the perturbation geometry.

## Method

f-VAT replaces the Euclidean norm constraint on adversarial perturbations with a Sobolev H^{-s} norm, establishing a theoretical duality (Theorems 3.3–3.4) showing that constraining perturbations in space E penalizes model sensitivity in the dual space E*. For time series, the H^{-s} norm on perturbations (s=2) ensures they preserve low-frequency trend information while the dual H^s norm on the gradient penalizes sensitivity to high-frequency noise. Practically, the H^α norm is computed as r_N^T (I_N + L_N)^α r_N using the spectral theorem on the discrete negative Laplacian, enabling efficient evaluation for non-integer and negative α. The algorithm approximates the optimal perturbation via L steps of gradient ascent on the LDS loss, re-normalizing by the H^{-s} norm at each iteration, and combines the resulting VAT loss with the supervised cross-entropy loss over the labeled set.

## Results

On six UCR/UEA benchmark datasets (CricketX, UWave, InsectWing, SelfReg, NATOPS, Heartbeat) at label ratios of 10%, 20%, and 40%, f-VAT achieves the highest classification accuracy in all 18 settings, with headline improvements of up to 9.42% over the best baseline on CricketX (68.39% vs. 58.97% TapNet at 40% ratio) and 8.30% on SelfReg (64.44% vs. 56.14% MTL at 40% ratio). Averaged over 30 randomly sampled UCR/UEA datasets, f-VAT reaches 65.85% / 68.87% / 76.24% mean accuracy at 10% / 20% / 40% label ratios, outperforming the next-best method (TapNet) by 7.2–5.96 percentage points. On large-scale CSI 500 futures data, f-VAT achieves 58.66% accuracy at 40% ratio, a 4.37-point gain over the strongest baseline (CA-TCC, 54.29%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

f-VAT outperforms all 11 baselines in every reported setting. Against the closest competitor, standard VAT (Miyato 2018), f-VAT gains 3.6–9.8 points on CricketX, 0.4–2.5 points on UWave, and 2.4–11.0 points on SelfReg, confirming that the Sobolev-norm perturbation is substantially more effective than the Euclidean-norm perturbation for time series. Among deep semi-supervised baselines, TapNet (Zhang 2020) is the strongest on most datasets (e.g., 58.97% on CricketX-40%, 89.24% on UWave-40%), yet f-VAT still leads by 9.42 and 7.99 points respectively. The contrastive method CA-TCC (Eldele 2023) and the temporal-relation method SemiTime (Fan 2021) trail f-VAT by 2.5–7.2 points on the 30-dataset average (Table 2). In the fully-supervised setting (Table 4), f-VAT surpasses the non-neural baselines ROCKET (Dempster 2020) and Hive-COTE (Bagnall 2020) by 1.15–4.05 points, and also beats the shapelet-based ShapeNet (Li 2021) and attentional TapNet, with the largest margin on UWave (97.75% vs. 93.70% ROCKET). The classical pseudo-labeling baselines PI (Wei 2006) and MTL (Jawed 2020) are consistently the weakest, confirming that consistency-regularization-based approaches (VAT/f-VAT) are more effective than pseudo-labeling for time series under label scarcity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Virtual Adversarial Training (VAT) [Miyato et al., 2018] by replacing the standard Euclidean norm constraint on adversarial perturbations with Sobolev H^{-s} norms, motivated by the observation that Euclidean-bounded perturbations produce jagged, high-frequency spikes that are not truly worst-case for time series data. The theoretical contribution establishes a duality between the perturbation norm in a Banach space E and the dual norm E* on model sensitivity (Theorems 3.3–3.4), generalizing the finite-dimensional Σ-norm analysis to infinite-dimensional functional spaces. Empirically, f-VAT is positioned against pseudo-labeling methods (PI, MTL, SemiTime, TapNet, CA-TCC) and the original VAT, demonstrating that the choice of perturbation geometry—rather than the consistency regularization objective itself—is the critical factor for time series.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Sobolev-norm perspective provides a principled, frequency-aware mechanism for controlling adversarial perturbations in functional data, with the order s acting as a tunable knob between smoothness preservation and exploration flexibility. The ~9% improvements over strong baselines across diverse datasets (UCR/UEA benchmarks and financial futures) and label ratios suggest the approach is robust rather than dataset-specific. Open questions include whether the method extends to pretrained backbones, whether task-specific norms (e.g., Fourier-domain penalties for known seasonality) yield further gains, and how the approach scales to very long or high-dimensional multivariate series. The limitation that all experiments train from scratch with a fixed TCN architecture leaves the model-agnosticism claim partially untested.
