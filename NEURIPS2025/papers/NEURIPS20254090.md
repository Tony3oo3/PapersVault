## Problem

While GNN expressiveness for graph-level tasks is well-characterized by the k-WL hierarchy, the expressive power of message-passing models for link representation remains largely unexplored. No unifying framework exists to systematically compare the discriminative power of existing link representation methods, and it is unclear whether theoretical expressiveness translates into practical performance gains on real-world data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254090/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework operates on any node pair (u,v) in a graph—regardless of whether an edge exists—taking the graph adjacency matrix and node feature matrix as input and producing a d-dimensional link embedding vector. A key design constraint in the synthetic evaluation (LR-EXP) is that test links are non-automorphic pairs whose endpoints share identical 1-WL colors, making them trivially indistinguishable to standard GNNs. Each model is characterized by three parameters: the expressiveness of endpoint encoding (k_φ), the expressiveness of neighborhood encoding (k_ρ), and the neighborhood radius (m).

## Method

The paper introduces the k_φ-k_ρ-m framework, which formalizes MP-based link representation as a combination of endpoint node representations (via a message-passing function φ) and an aggregation over the m-hop joint neighborhood (via a message-passing function ρ with link-aware feature modification). Using this framework, the authors derive expressiveness theorems establishing a hierarchy: Pure GNNs < NCN ≈ Neo-GNN < ELPH < SEAL, with the key insight that m=0 models fundamentally cannot distinguish links with automorphic endpoints regardless of φ's expressiveness. To empirically validate the hierarchy, they introduce LR-EXP, a synthetic benchmark of 1400 graphs containing non-automorphic links with identical WL colors, evaluated via siamese contrastive training and the Reliable Paired Comparison statistical test. Finally, they propose a graph symmetry metric r_G (based on WL color count) and show that while simple models suffice on low-symmetry datasets, SEAL consistently outperforms all others as symmetry increases across 12 real-world link prediction benchmarks.

## Results

On the synthetic LR-EXP benchmark, pure GNNs (GCN, GAT, GAE, SAGE) achieve 0% test precision in distinguishing non-automorphic links, while SEAL reaches 97%, NCN and Neo-GNN reach 75%, ELPH 62%, and BUDDY 45%. On 12 real-world link prediction datasets sorted by increasing graph symmetry (r̂_G from 0.01 to 0.67), pure GNNs rank in the top three on the five lowest-symmetry datasets but appear in the top three on none of the six highest-symmetry datasets. SEAL, the most expressive model, ranks first on all high-symmetry datasets (e.g., 56.72 MRR on GRQ with r̂_G=0.30, 25.82 on EDIT-TSW with r̂_G=0.67), while simpler models like NCN (48.45 on GRQ) and BUDDY (46.23 on GRQ) trail behind.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper establishes a theoretical expressiveness hierarchy (Theorem 3.3) and validates it empirically. Pure GNNs (GCN, GAT, GAE, SAGE) are the least expressive: they achieve 0% precision on LR-EXP and, on real-world data, only compete on low-symmetry datasets (e.g., SAGE achieves 22.05 MRR on OGBL-CITATION2) but collapse on high-symmetry ones (e.g., GAT drops to 0.40 on EDIT-TSW). BUDDY (45% on LR-EXP) and ELPH (62%) form a middle tier, with BUDDY's approximate counting limiting its expressiveness. Neo-GNN and NCN (both 75% on LR-EXP) are more expressive and maintain competitive MRR across most datasets (e.g., NCN reaches 35.06 on OGBL-PPA, 48.45 on GRQ). SEAL (97% on LR-EXP) is the most expressive and dominates on high-symmetry datasets, achieving 56.72 MRR on GRQ (vs. 48.45 for NCN and 46.23 for BUDDY) and 25.82 on EDIT-TSW (vs. 10.32 for NCN and 22.66 for BUDDY), though it underperforms on low-symmetry datasets like CITESEER (13.16 vs. 28.65 for NCN), confirming that expressiveness is most beneficial when structural ambiguity is high.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the well-established graph-level GNN expressiveness theory (the k-WL hierarchy) to the link-level setting, where no comparable theoretical framework existed. It unifies a diverse set of existing link representation methods (Pure GNNs, NCN, Neo-GNN, ELPH, BUDDY, SEAL) under a single k_φ-k_ρ-m formalism, enabling principled expressiveness comparisons that were previously impossible. The synthetic evaluation protocol draws directly on the Reliable Paired Comparison method and zero-margin contrastive training from Wang and Zhang [48, 49], adapting their graph-level expressiveness evaluation tools to the link-level domain for the first time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first systematic theoretical hierarchy for link representation models and a practical graph symmetry metric that connects expressiveness theory to model selection, showing that the 'best' architecture depends on dataset structure rather than being universally fixed. This reframing is practically important: practitioners can now compute a simple symmetry score and choose between cheap (NCN) and expensive (SEAL) models accordingly. Key limitations include the framework's restriction to message-passing models (excluding transformers and spectral methods) and the small size of high-symmetry evaluation datasets, leaving open whether the symmetry-based selection principle holds at scale.
