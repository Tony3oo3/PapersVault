## Problem

Continuous-time systems modeled with discrete-time dynamics require small simulation steps, forcing large planning horizons that make shooting-based planners computationally intractable and susceptible to compounding model errors in MBRL. While model-free RL has explored dynamic action repeats, no prior work treats the action duration as a planner-optimized variable in the planning or MBRL setting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254721/cropped_page6_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The planner receives the current state, a dynamics function (exact primitive f or learned ensemble F̂_TE), an action duration range [δt_min, δt_max], and a planning horizon D_TE. At each decision step it outputs a temporally-extended action comprising the standard primitive action variables plus a continuous duration δt_k ∈ [δt_min, δt_max]. The learned dynamics model takes (state, action, duration) as input and predicts the next state distribution and reward in constant time, regardless of the duration value, unlike the iterative primitive wrapper whose cost scales with duration.

## Method

The planner (CEM-based) jointly optimizes primitive action variables and action duration as a single augmented action vector, so each decision step produces a temporally-extended action whose duration determines how many primitive steps are executed. For MBRL, an ensemble of feed-forward neural networks is trained to approximate the temporally-extended dynamics function F̂_TE, adding duration as an extra input to the standard PETS architecture. The range of δt_max is selected automatically via a non-stationary multi-armed bandit with UCB and exponential moving average over exponentially spaced candidates, where each arm maintains its own dataset and dynamics model. This formulation reduces the search space from 2^H to 2^(H/m) for binary actions, cuts the number of optimization variables from H|A| to (H/m)(|A|+1), and enables deep primitive-horizon search with shallow planner depth, solving problems infeasible under the standard formulation.

## Results

In the IPC Multi-hill Mountain Car planning benchmark, A_TE (D_TE=12) solves all 5 instances (rewards 66.5–91.7) while A_STD (D_STD=175) solves only 1/5 (reward 90.98 on instance 1, −0.1 on the rest). In the Dubins Car u-shaped map, A_TE requires a planning horizon of 75 versus 1000 for A_STD, and needs only 103 MB of memory versus ~4 GB when the action space is augmented with 100 dummy variables. In MBRL, A_TE matches or exceeds A_STD across MuJoCo environments while training up to 8× faster (e.g., Half Cheetah: 5 h vs. 45 h; Ant: 4 h vs. 41 h; Hopper: 4 h vs. 40 h; Walker: 3.1 h vs. 36.8 h).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is A_STD, the TS∞ variant of CEM from PETS (Chua et al., 2018), which plans over primitive actions with a fixed decision timescale. In planning, A_TE wins decisively: on IPC Multi-hill Mountain Car it solves 5/5 instances versus 1/5 for A_STD, on Mountain Car it succeeds with D_TE≥4 versus D_STD≥60, and on the Dubins Car u-shaped map it requires a planning horizon of 75 versus 1000 for A_STD. In MBRL, A_TE significantly outperforms A_STD in Ant, Hopper, and Walker (higher running-average scores in Figure 3), matches A_STD in Half Cheetah, Reacher, and Pusher, and is 8× faster in Half Cheetah (5 h vs. 45 h) and ~10× faster in Ant, Hopper, and Walker. The fixed-δt_max variant A_TE(F) achieves the best final performance, while the MAB-based A_TE(D) requires additional exploration iterations but converges to comparable performance without manual tuning of δt_max.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on controlling decision timescale in RL—previously explored through learned frame-skips and action-repeats in the model-free setting (Lakshminarayanan et al. 2017, Sharma et al. 2017, Ni and Jang 2022)—to the planning and MBRL setting, where the planner directly optimizes action duration as an additional variable rather than a policy learning it. The MBRL framework is built on PETS (Chua et al. 2018), using its ensemble dynamics models and TS∞ planning procedure, with the key modification that the learned model predicts next state and reward for a temporally-extended action in constant time. The automatic selection of the action-duration range via a non-stationary MAB with UCB and EMA is a practical departure from prior work that treats the maximum frame-skip or timescale as a fixed hyperparameter.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ability to reduce planning horizons by an order of magnitude (e.g., D_TE=4 vs. D_STD=60 in Mountain Car, D_TE=75 vs. D_STD=1000 in Dubins Car) and to solve previously infeasible problems (4/5 IPC Mountain Car instances, u-shaped Dubins Car maps) makes this practically relevant for MBRL practitioners working with fine-grained simulators. The 8× speedup in Half Cheetah and the elimination of a manual hyperparameter via the MAB framework add practical value. However, the approach does not guarantee improvement in every environment (Reacher and Pusher show no benefit), the MAB formulation requires a fixed planning horizon per arm, and the tested environments are relatively low-dimensional, leaving open whether the approach scales to high-dimensional or partially observable settings.
