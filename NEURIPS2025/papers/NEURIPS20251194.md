## Problem

Existing MDP-based LLM reasoning frameworks either rely on manually defined action spaces that lack cross-domain scalability or operate over unstructured natural-language spaces that make exhaustive search intractable. The paper asks how to automatically construct a compact, information-dense candidate action set at each reasoning step that balances utility (advancing the solution) with diversity (avoiding redundancy), without requiring manual engineering or expensive full-space search.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251194/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text question and a pre-computed proxy action space of ~40k observation sketches (general reasoning patterns extracted from the Open-Platypus corpus via Llama-3.1-70B). At each reasoning step, the system selects a candidate subset of m=5 actions from this space, evaluates them via MCTS rollouts (16 rollouts), and generates a single reasoning step conditioned on the chosen action. The final output is a complete state-action reasoning trace ending in a boxed answer.

## Method

DYNACT frames action space construction as a submodular subset selection problem. It first extracts a proxy action space by prompting an LLM to produce general observation sketches from a diverse problem corpus, then defines a submodular objective F(A_t; s_t) = α·f_util + β·f_div where the utility term is a log-sum-exp of embedding dot products between the current state and candidate actions, and the diversity term is the sum of minimum pairwise embedding distances. The embedding function (Llama-3.2-1B, last-token embedding) is trained via a Q-learning objective that aligns state-action dot products with expected future rewards from ground-truth reasoning traces. A greedy algorithm with O(m²|A|) complexity selects the top-m actions maximizing F, after which MCTS with UCT estimates Q-values to pick the single best action. Only the 1B embedding model is trained; the 8B base LLM remains frozen throughout inference.

## Results

DYNACT achieves the best exact-match accuracy across all six benchmarks, with headline results of 70.22% on MMLU, 51.40% on MMLU-Pro, 39.39% on GPQA, 88.31% on ARC-C, 89.16% on GSM8K, and 61.00% on MATH-500. The most substantial gain is on MATH-500, where DYNACT improves over the strongest baseline rStar by 6.80 percentage points (61.00 vs. 54.20) and over Zero-shot CoT by 15.60 points. On the general-domain benchmarks, DYNACT leads MMLU-Pro by 2.04 points over SC@maj16 and GPQA by 0.50 points over RAP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DYNACT outperforms all four baselines on every benchmark. Against Zero-shot CoT (single-pass Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct), DYNACT gains range from +1.35 (MMLU) to +15.60 (MATH-500), with the largest absolute improvements on math tasks. Versus SC@maj16 (16-rollout self-consistency), DYNACT leads by +0.56 on MMLU, +2.04 on MMLU-Pro, +5.05 on GPQA, +7.68 on ARC-C, +2.50 on GSM8K, and +9.00 on MATH-500. Compared to RAP (MCTS with auto-generated sub-question actions), DYNACT wins on all six benchmarks, with the largest margin on MATH-500 (+9.40) and GPQA (+0.50). Against rStar (MCTS with 5 manually defined actions), DYNACT achieves the most striking gap on MATH-500 (+6.80) and MMLU-Pro (+2.59), while the advantage narrows on ARC-C (+1.88) and GSM8K (+2.05), suggesting rStar's hand-crafted actions remain competitive on simpler reasoning but fail to scale to harder problems.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends MDP-based LLM reasoning (RAP, rStar) by reframing the action space construction step as a submodular subset selection problem, drawing on the submodular optimization literature from model interpretability and feature selection. It builds on Wang et al. [2024a] for using natural-language observations as candidate actions, but departs from both RAP (which auto-generates unstructured sub-questions) and rStar (which uses a fixed set of 5 manually defined actions) by dynamically selecting a compact, state-dependent subset via a learned utility-diversity objective. The Q-learning-based embedding training is a novel coupling of reinforcement learning signals with the submodular function, rather than training a full policy or reward model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a carefully selected set of 5 actions outperforms 15 auto-generated sub-questions (RAP) on MATH-500 challenges the assumption that broader action spaces are always better, and suggests that principled compactness can substitute for brute-force exploration. The approach is practical—only a 1B embedding model is trained while the base LLM remains frozen—and scales to 1M candidate actions with modest latency overhead. Open questions include whether the method transfers to non-Llama backbones, whether alternative search strategies (beam search, sample-efficient MCTS) can further reduce the MCTS bottleneck, and whether the strong utility-over-diversity asymmetry (α=0.9) observed here generalizes across domains.
