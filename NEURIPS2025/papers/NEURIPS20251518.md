## Problem

Existing end-to-end VLA models lose the pretrained VLM's open-world knowledge (object recognition, mathematical reasoning, spatial understanding) during fine-tuning on robotic data, causing them to fail on tasks that are trivial for humans but absent from the training set. The paper asks how to build a VLA that both preserves the VLM's pretrained reasoning capabilities and reliably translates that reasoning into executable robot actions, enabling generalization to entirely unseen objects, equations, and spatial instructions.

## Input / Output

The model consumes RGB images from multiple cameras (320×240 resolution; top RealSense L515 for the ARX-R5 bimanual arm, ZED 2 for the Franka Emika), a natural-language instruction, and the robot's proprioceptive state (14-DoF or 7-DoF), sampled at 50 Hz or 15 Hz respectively. It produces two outputs simultaneously: unstructured textual reasoning tokens generated autoregressively by the VLM backbone, and a continuous action sequence generated by a 1B-parameter ScaleDP diffusion expert that is conditioned on those reasoning tokens. The reasoning output is not a fixed template but free-form text, which is critical for handling out-of-distribution reasoning scenarios.

## Method

ChatVLA-2 builds on DexVLA (Qwen2-VL backbone + ScaleDP diffusion action expert) and introduces two key architectural changes. First, a dynamic Mixture-of-Experts layer (8 experts, top-2 routing) is inserted into the VLM backbone to disentangle conflicting feature spaces between multimodal understanding and robotic control while preserving shared representations; unlike static or shared experts, dynamic routing keeps the LLM's original architecture intact, preventing degradation of pretrained knowledge. Second, a reasoning-following enhancement module replaces the observation embedding in the latter-half layers of the action expert with reasoning tokens projected through an MLP, which are combined with timestep embeddings to condition FiLM-style scale and shift parameters, thereby injecting the VLM's internal reasoning directly into action generation. Training follows a two-stage strategy: Stage 1 co-trains on image-text data (COCO, TextVQA, GQA, robotics-scenario pairs) and robot trajectories (600 math-matching + 300 toy-placement) at a 1:3 ratio for 50k steps to establish connections between pretrained knowledge and actions; Stage 2 freezes the entire VLM and trains only the action expert for another 50k steps, explicitly forcing it to execute actions consistent with the frozen VLM's reasoning outputs.

## Results

On the math matching game (open-world), ChatVLA-2 achieves 3.58/4 OCR score, 1.73/2 math reasoning score, and 43/52 (82.7%) manipulation success rate, while the best competing method (DexVLA) scores only 0.21/4 OCR, 0.06/2 math, and 10/52 (19.2%) success. On the toy placement task (open-world), ChatVLA-2 reaches 0.94 object recognition, 0.88 spatial affordance, and 127/156 (81.4%) success rate, a 3.5× improvement over DexVLA (36/156). On 12 multimodal understanding benchmarks, ChatVLA-2 improves over its predecessor ChatVLA on 11 of 12, with notable gains of +7.0 on TextVQA and +11.7 on ChartQA, confirming that pretrained VLM knowledge is preserved.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In the open-world math matching game, ChatVLA-2 dramatically outperforms all 7 baselines: it achieves 43/52 success versus DexVLA's 10/52, π₀'s 8/52, ChatVLA's 4/52, and 0/52 for Octo and OpenVLA. The OCR and math reasoning scores tell the same story—ChatVLA-2 scores 3.58/4 and 1.73/2 respectively, while DexVLA (the closest competitor with language output) manages only 0.21/4 and 0.06/2. In the open-world toy placement task, ChatVLA-2 achieves 127/156 success (81.4%) versus DexVLA's 36/156 (23.1%) and ChatVLA's 22/156 (14.1%), with object recognition of 0.94 versus 0.23 for DexVLA. In in-domain settings, ChatVLA-2 is competitive but not dominant: it scores 11/13 on math matching (vs. 12/13 for DexVLA and π₀) and 61/67 on toy placement (vs. 63/67 for DexVLA), confirming that the primary advantage lies in open-world generalization rather than in-domain manipulation. On the 12 VQA/multimodal understanding benchmarks, ChatVLA-2 (4B) surpasses ChatVLA (2B) on 11 of 12 tasks and significantly outperforms OpenVLA (7B, near-zero scores) and DiVLA (2B), demonstrating that the dynamic MoE architecture preserves and even enhances pretrained VLM capabilities.

## Relation to Prior Work

ChatVLA-2 builds directly on DexVLA's architecture (Qwen2-VL backbone + ScaleDP diffusion action expert) and extends ChatVLA's static expert separation into a dynamic Mixture-of-Experts design that adaptively routes between task-specific and shared feature spaces. It departs from prior reasoning-injection approaches (FiLM layers in DexVLA, DiffusionVLA) by replacing observation embeddings with projected reasoning tokens in the latter half of the action expert, and introduces a two-stage training pipeline (co-train then freeze-VLM) that has no direct precedent in the VLA literature. The paper positions itself against the broader VLA landscape (OpenVLA, π0, GR00T N1, Octo) by arguing that all existing end-to-end VLA systems suffer from catastrophic erosion of pretrained VLM knowledge during robotics fine-tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work demonstrates that a VLA model can inherit and act upon pretrained VLM capabilities (OCR, mathematical reasoning, spatial understanding) without explicit training on those skills, addressing a fundamental gap in the field where fine-tuning destroys general knowledge. The finding that parameter-space conflict—not model capacity—explains why dense VLA models fail on open-world reasoning (7B dense still fails while 4B MoE succeeds) offers a concrete architectural prescription. However, the evaluation is limited to two tabletop tasks with modest data (900 total trajectories), and the authors acknowledge that full retention of pretrained knowledge remains unresolved; scaling to mobile manipulation and long-horizon tasks is left as future work.
