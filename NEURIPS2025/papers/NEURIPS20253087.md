## Problem

Masked diffusion models generate sequences by iteratively unmasking tokens, but standard ancestral sampling uses fixed schedules that unmask only a limited number of tokens per step, making inference slow and prone to premature or suboptimal unmasking. Existing acceleration strategies either require additional training (distillation), rely on external planner models, or use single-signal heuristics (confidence or KL alone) that degrade accuracy. The paper asks whether the model's own internal dynamics can be exploited to adaptively select which tokens are safe to unmask early, achieving speedups without extra training or auxiliary models.

## Input / Output

At each diffusion step, KLASS consumes the model's predicted categorical distributions over the vocabulary for all currently masked token positions, along with the cached distributions from the previous n steps (history window). It outputs a binary unmasking mask indicating which positions to reveal in the current step, with the argmax token assigned at each unmasked position. The method is modality-agnostic: for text it operates on 256-token reasoning sequences or 1024-token generation sequences, for images on 1000-class ImageNet labels, and for molecules on SMILES strings of up to 9 heavy atoms, all under the same two-threshold criterion.

## Method

KLASS introduces a training-free adaptive unmasking rule that combines two per-token signals: a confidence score (max probability in the predicted distribution) and a KL score (Kullback-Leibler divergence between the current and previous timestep's predicted distribution, checked over a history window of length n). A token is unmasked only when its KL score stays below a threshold ε_KL across all n recent steps AND its confidence exceeds a threshold τ; if no tokens qualify, a fallback unmasking of the top-u highest-confidence tokens is applied. The key theoretical insight (Proposition 5.3) is that for a well-trained model, an incorrect token cannot remain dynamically stable: its average per-step KL along any context path is bounded below by a positive quantity, so KL divergence acts as a correctness filter that confidence alone cannot provide. This dual-criterion design lets KLASS unmask multiple stable tokens in parallel each step, reducing total diffusion steps by 40–70% while simultaneously improving accuracy over greedy Top-1 decoding across math, code, text, image, and molecular generation tasks.

## Results

On reasoning benchmarks, KLASS achieves up to 2.78× wall-clock speedup (Dream on HumanEval: 32.01s → 11.52s) while improving accuracy over standard Top-1 greedy decoding across both LLaDA 8B and Dream 7B models. For LLaDA, KLASS reaches 33.8% on MATH, 76.50% on GSM8K, 40.85% on HumanEval, and 47.86% on MBPP, using only ~92–129 steps versus 256 for Top-1. For Dream, KLASS attains 43.20% on MATH (vs. 37.97% Top-1) and 64.59% on MBPP (vs. 63.81% Top-1). On text generation (MDLM/OpenWebText), KLASS achieves the best MAUVE (0.179) and lowest perplexity across all three oracle models (LLaMA2: 26.94, LLaMA3: 49.19, GPT-2: 45.50), outperforming SEDD, D3PM, and standard MDLM.

## Baselines & Comparisons

KLASS consistently outperforms Top-1 greedy decoding on both LLaDA and Dream across all four reasoning benchmarks, e.g., gaining +2.2 points on MATH (33.8 vs. 31.4) and +0.8 on GSM8K (76.50 vs. 75.13) for LLaDA, and +5.2 on MATH (43.20 vs. 37.97) for Dream, while using 40–70% fewer steps. Against the parallel Top-2 baseline (which halves steps to 128), KLASS achieves substantially higher accuracy (e.g., 33.8 vs. 29.6 on MATH for LLaDA) with a comparable step count, demonstrating that naive parallel unmasking degrades quality. Confidence-threshold sampling (unmask all tokens with p > 0.9) is faster (e.g., 54 steps on HumanEval) but sacrifices accuracy (37.80 vs. 40.85 for LLaDA), while KL-threshold-only sampling uses more steps (112–172) with lower accuracy than KLASS. On text generation, KLASS surpasses SEDD (MAUVE 0.179 vs. 0.037), D3PM (0.179 vs. 0.022), and standard MDLM (0.179 vs. 0.115) while maintaining comparable entropy. For image generation on MMaDA, KLASS reduces FID from 34.48 to 30.48 and increases IS from 75.72 to 93.07 at 16 steps versus the confidence-based sampler. On molecular generation (QM9), KLASS improves QED reward from 0.526 to 0.546 and ring count from 4.123 to 4.258 while reducing NFEs by ~41% and ~24% respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

KLASS builds on the masked diffusion model framework (MDLM/SUBS, SEDD, D3PM) and modifies the ancestral sampling procedure by replacing fixed unmasking schedules with an adaptive, KL-divergence-guided selection rule. It departs from prior acceleration approaches that either require additional training (distillation), introduce external planners, or rely on confidence-only heuristics (Fast-dLLM, Dimple, SlowFast, EB-Sampler, Prophet). The key conceptual departure is using token-level KL divergence between consecutive timesteps as a temporal stability signal, formalized by a theoretical result showing that incorrect tokens cannot remain dynamically stable as context resolves. This positions KLASS as a training-free, model-internal alternative to the planner-based or distillation-based acceleration strategies that dominate the literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

KLASS offers a practical, drop-in sampler that simultaneously reduces sampling steps (40–70%) and improves accuracy on reasoning benchmarks, without any retraining or auxiliary models — a combination that prior confidence-only or KL-only methods fail to achieve. The theoretical guarantee that incorrect tokens must exhibit dynamic instability provides a principled justification beyond empirical heuristics. However, the speedups are moderate (up to 2.78×), hyperparameter sensitivity varies across models, and the evaluation is limited to 7–8B parameter models; whether the approach scales to much larger diffusion LLMs or benefits from alternative noise schedules remains open. The cross-modality validation (text, image, molecule) strengthens the generality claim but each modality uses a different model and hyperparameter set, limiting direct comparability.
