## Problem

Existing GNN+sequence models for information diffusion prediction implicitly capture influence patterns but cannot explicitly answer who-influences-whom within a cascade, treating all participants as potential influencers and suffering from spurious diffusion connections. This paper tackles the fundamental question of identifying authentic diffusion influence links to make the diffusion process transparent and explainable.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254426/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a social network graph, an observed diffusion cascade (ordered participant sequence with timestamps and behaviors), user profiles (follower/bi-follower counts, status counts, verification status), and participant comments. It first derives a directed diffusion influence graph G_I via LLM reasoning over four structured factors (potential diffusion paths, temporal order, user influence/activity levels, and key comment sentences extracted via TextRank), then produces top-γ candidate future participants. The diffusion influence graph is injected as a bias term into self-attention rather than being used as a separate feature, and raw contextual features are deliberately excluded from the predictor to isolate the contribution of the influence structure.

## Method

MILD is built on a front-door causal framework (C → Z → Y) where Z is an explicit diffusion influence graph derived by an LLM acting as a sociology expert. For C → Z, the paper formulates an NP-hard diffusion path search problem (solved via a BFS+DFS heuristic in polynomial time) to generate diverse potential diffusion paths, then prompts GPT-4o with these paths plus temporal order, user influence/activity levels, and key comment sentences to infer directed who-influences-whom pairs; multiple path-level subgraphs are merged by aligning shared nodes and edges into a unified G_I. For Z → Y, the adjacency matrix of G_I is added as a learnable-scaled bias (αA^I) directly into the self-attention computation of a Transformer cascade module, on top of a single-layer GAT encoder for social network representation, so that attention is explicitly steered toward LLM-identified key influencers rather than learned implicitly from structure and sequence.

## Results

On the News dataset, MILD achieves H@10 of 24.07 and M@100 of 15.91, outperforming the best baseline CARE (22.47 / 14.85) by +7.12% and +7.14% respectively. On the Weibo dataset, MILD reaches H@10 of 14.71 and M@100 of 9.17, surpassing the best baseline GODEN (14.08 / 8.52) by +4.47% and +7.63%. Overall, MILD delivers average improvements of 6.3% on Hits@K and 7.4% on MAP@K across both datasets, with the largest gains on Weibo M@50 (+9.89%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MILD outperforms all eight baselines on every metric across both datasets. On News, the closest competitor is CARE [41] (H@10=22.47, M@100=14.85), which MILD surpasses by 1.60 and 1.06 points respectively; GODEN [29] is second-closest on News H@50 (29.14 vs. 31.33). On Weibo, GODEN [29] is the strongest baseline (H@10=14.08, M@100=8.52), with MILD leading by 0.63 and 0.65 points. The gap widens substantially for weaker baselines: DyHGCN [37] trails MILD by 5.14 points on News H@10 and 4.20 on Weibo H@10. Notably, MILD's advantage is largest on Weibo M@50 (+9.89% over GODEN), suggesting the LLM-derived influence graph is particularly effective for precision-oriented ranking on larger cascades (avg. length 175 vs. 63 on News). All baselines follow the standard 'GNN + sequence model' paradigm, whereas MILD explicitly injects an LLM-derived diffusion influence graph into the attention mechanism, which the ablation confirms is the primary source of the gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant 'GNN + sequence model' paradigm for information diffusion prediction (DyHGCN, MS-HGAT, GODEN, CARE, etc.), which implicitly captures influence through learned attention over social adjacency and temporal ordering. Instead, MILD introduces an LLM-based causal reasoning layer that explicitly derives a who-influences-whom diffusion influence graph (G_I) by integrating four factors—social paths, temporal order, user profiles, and comments—framed through Pearl's front-door adjustment. The diffusion path search problem (proven NP-hard) and its BFS/DFS heuristic are novel algorithmic contributions, while the use of LLMs as a 'sociology expert' for structured graph inference is a repurposing of LLM reasoning capabilities into a domain where prior work relied solely on learned representations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper makes a compelling case that social network structure alone is an insufficient proxy for actual information diffusion pathways (12% absolute gap in captured real-world routes), and that explicit causal influence modeling via LLMs can close this gap. However, practical adoption is constrained by dependence on GPT-4o (cost, latency, reproducibility across LLM versions) and the requirement for rich textual metadata (user profiles, comments) that most public diffusion datasets lack. Open questions include whether the LLM-derived graph quality degrades at very large cascade scales, whether cheaper/smaller LLMs can match GPT-4o's reasoning fidelity for this task, and whether the causal framing generalizes to diffusion on platforms without reply-chain ground truth for validation.
