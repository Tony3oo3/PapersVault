## Problem

Existing mechanistic interpretability methods for LLMs are either correlational (probing classifiers, sparse autoencoders) or hypothesis-driven (causal mediation analysis requiring handcrafted prompt templates and target labels), making them difficult to scale to open-ended tasks like mathematical reasoning. No scalable, hypothesis-free method exists that assigns causal roles to individual attention heads across an entire model without predefined assumptions about what each head might be doing. This paper addresses that gap by introducing a method that operates directly on next-token prediction over any dataset.

## Input / Output

The method takes a frozen transformer model (Llama 3 family, 1B–8B parameters) and a task dataset, operating purely on next-token prediction without requiring labels, prompt templates, or controlled input-output pairs. It produces a per-head gating matrix G ∈ [0,1]^{L×H} (one scalar per attention head) that, when fitted under opposing regularization pressures, yields a three-way causal taxonomy—facilitating, interfering, or irrelevant—for every head in the model. The entire fitting procedure introduces only L×H learnable parameters, requires no updates to model weights, and completes in minutes on a single GPU.

## Method

CHG applies a multiplicative gate G_{ℓ,h} ∈ [0,1] to each attention head's output (after attention, before the output projection) and optimizes these gates by minimizing next-token prediction NLL on a task dataset while keeping all model weights frozen. A clipped L1 regularization on the inverse-sigmoid of each gate is added with weight λ, and the gates are fitted twice—once with λ>0 (G+, encouraging retention) and once with λ<0 (G−, encouraging removal)—starting from a shared λ=0 initialization. The causal taxonomy is read off the two matrices: facilitation is measured by G− (heads that resist suppression), interference by 1−G+ (heads suppressed even under retention pressure), and irrelevance by G+×(1−G−) (heads whose gates bifurcate based on regularization direction). Unlike Gumbel-softmax approaches that treat each head independently, CHG jointly optimizes all gates under the model's loss, capturing interdependencies between heads that jointly affect task performance. A contrastive variant (CCHG) extends this with a joint objective that maximizes log-probability on a retained task variant while minimizing it on a forgotten variant, isolating sub-circuits specific to one mode such as instruction following versus in-context learning.

## Results

CHG validates its causal taxonomy via targeted ablations: progressively ablating facilitating heads decreases target log-probability, irrelevant heads leave it near zero, and interfering heads increase it (Figure 2). Across four Llama 3 variants (1B–8B), head-role distributions are highly consistent, with Pearson correlations of 94.92%–99.2% (mean 99.2%) across all 54 model pairs per task. Mathematical reasoning activates a much larger fraction of facilitating heads (52.6% with score ≥ 0.5) compared to syntax (25.6%) and commonsense (27.4%), while 84.0% of heads are facilitating or interfering in at least one of 10 seeds, yet only a small fraction are consistent across all seeds, indicating low modularity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper validates CHG against two causal mediation analysis (CMA) baselines: Todd et al. [12] on six function-vector ICL tasks and Yang et al. [13] on ABA/ABB symbolic reasoning tasks. In both comparisons, CMA-identified mediating heads show significantly higher CHG facilitation scores than non-mediating heads: t(23.05)=8.52, p<10⁻⁸ for function vectors and t(53.77)=11.18, p<10⁻¹⁵ for symbolic reasoning. The relationship is framed as complementary rather than competitive: CMA has high precision but low sensitivity (few false positives, many false negatives), while CHG is biased toward sensitivity, capturing a broader set of task-relevant heads. CHG does not 'beat' CMA on a shared accuracy metric; instead, it confirms that every head CMA identifies as causally mediating also receives a high facilitation score under CHG, supporting CHG as a scalable, hypothesis-free complement to targeted CMA.

## Relation to Prior Work

CHG builds on differentiable masking and soft-gating approaches (De Cao et al., 2020) that learn per-head scaling parameters via gradient descent, but repurposes them to identify task-sufficient causal sub-circuits rather than merely weighting activations for patching. It departs from the dominant hypothesis-driven paradigm of causal mediation analysis (Wang et al., 2022; Todd et al., 2023; Yang et al., 2025), which requires handcrafted prompt templates and mechanistic hypotheses, by operating directly on next-token prediction over any dataset. Unlike Gumbel-softmax-based head pruning, which factorizes head inclusion into independent Bernoulli distributions, CHG jointly optimizes all gates under a shared loss, capturing interdependencies between heads. It also contrasts with correlational approaches (probing, sparse autoencoders) by performing direct interventions on model components without external supervision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CHG provides a practical, scalable entry point into causal interpretability that requires only a dataset and no model finetuning, making it broadly applicable to billion-parameter LLMs in minutes. The finding that individual head roles are highly context-dependent (84% of heads appear relevant in at least one seed but only a small fraction are consistent across all seeds) challenges the modular, one-head-one-function narrative and suggests LLMs rely on flexible ensembles of overlapping sub-circuits. However, CHG identifies *where* computation happens without explaining *what* computation occurs, positioning it as a first-pass diagnostic that must be followed by hypothesis-driven methods for mechanistic understanding. Open questions include whether the low-modularity pattern persists in larger or differently-architected models, and how to systematically move from CHG-identified sub-circuits to functional explanations of specific computations.
