## Problem

LLM-driven robotic manipulation planners generate unrealistic or logically inconsistent steps for complex tasks because they lack structured procedural commonsense (e.g., omitting prerequisite actions or violating physical constraints). Achieving adequate planning capability typically demands very large models (e.g., 562B PaLM-E), which is energy-prohibitive for real-world robotic deployment. The paper tackles both gaps by providing an external structured knowledge source that can enhance even small LLMs without requiring model scaling.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254246/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system accepts a natural language task instruction (optionally paired with a sequence of environment images for multimodal models) and produces a hierarchical procedural plan of sequential steps, each decomposed into atomic robotic actions. During retrieval, the instruction is encoded into a dense embedding, matched against the HP-KG via cosine similarity, expanded through K-hop breadth-first traversal, filtered by target hierarchy level, and re-ranked; the resulting sub-graphs are then converted into structured textual descriptions (step descriptions, action details, and safety tips) that are prepended to the LLM prompt as planning context.

## Method

The core contribution is a three-level Hierarchical Procedural Knowledge Graph (HP-KG) that organizes procedures into tasks, steps, and atomic actions, linked by HasStep/HasAction edges for hierarchy and NextStep/NextAction edges for temporal ordering, with each node carrying name, description, and tips attributes. The graph is built automatically by an LLM-based multi-agent pipeline that extracts procedures from WikiHow and BEHAVIOR-1K, iteratively verifies and refines them against a rule set (checking atomicity, logical sequence, redundancy, etc.), then clusters and merges semantically similar entries, yielding 6,380 tasks, 40,659 steps, and 136,943 actions. At inference time, a retrieval-augmented planning approach encodes the query, retrieves top-k₁ candidate nodes, expands them via K-hop BFS, filters by target level, re-ranks to top-k₂, and converts the associated sub-graphs into textual context fed to the LLM, enabling 7B models to surpass 72B baselines on planning benchmarks.

## Results

On ActPlan-1K, HP-KG boosts the average LCS of Qwen2-VL-7B by +17.64% (7.55 → 8.88 with Top-3 retrieval), surpassing the 72B baseline (8.78) without any HP-KG. For larger models, gains are more modest but consistent: GPT-4o improves by +3.99% (12.03 → 12.51), Gemini-2.0-Flash by +4.90% (10.75 → 11.28), and Qwen2-VL-72B by +11.74% (8.78 → 9.81). On RLBench zero-shot manipulation, HP-KG raises Voxposer's average success rate from 27% to 37% (+10%) and MA from 17% to 28% (+11%). On the Blocks Arrange task, GPT-4o with HP-KG achieves 45% success rate versus 33% for GPT-4o with COT and 26% for SayCan.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HP-KG consistently outperforms all baselines across every benchmark. On ActPlan-1K, the 7B model augmented with HP-KG (8.88 avg LCS) surpasses the 72B model without HP-KG (8.78), demonstrating that structured procedural knowledge compensates for model scale. Against COT prompting, HP-KG achieves substantially larger gains: on RLBench with RVT executor, HP-KG (Top-3) reaches 34% average success vs. 27% for COT (+7% absolute), and on Blocks Arrange, GPT-4o+HP-KG (45%) beats GPT-4o+COT (33%) by 12 points. SayCan, which relies on implicit affordance grounding, scores only 26% on Blocks Arrange versus 45% for HP-KG-augmented GPT-4o. On VLABench, Intern3-VL-8B with HP-KG improves from 20.89 to 24.58 average accuracy (+3.69), with the largest gain in the PhysicsLaw category (10.21 → 18.95). In the KitchenWorld long-horizon setting, GPT-4o+HP-KG achieves 86.9% task completion versus 77.4% for GPT-4o alone (+9.5%). The paper also shows that HP-KG benefits even the strongest models (GPT-4o, Llama-3.2-90B-Vision), though the relative improvement diminishes with model scale (e.g., +13.17% for Llama-3.2-90B vs. +3.99% for GPT-4o).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of LLM-driven robotic manipulation planning (VoxPoser, MA, SayCan, RVT) and knowledge-graph-augmented generation. It departs from prior procedural knowledge approaches (Carriero et al., Beetz et al.) that represent procedures as unstructured reference documents or coarse-grained decompositions, instead introducing a three-level hierarchical structure (tasks → steps → actions) explicitly designed to bridge the domain gap between human language instructions and robot-executable atomic actions. The automated multi-agent construction pipeline builds on recent LLM-for-KG-construction work but applies it specifically to procedural knowledge with iterative rule-based verification. The authors position HP-KG as orthogonal to existing VLA and subgoal-generation methods, serving as a plug-in knowledge source rather than a replacement for the planning architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in the efficiency claim: a 7B model augmented with HP-KG matches or exceeds a 72B model baseline on ActPlan-1K while halving inference time, which is directly relevant to energy-constrained embodied systems. The released HP-KG (6K+ tasks, 40K+ steps, 136K+ actions) is a useful resource for the household robotics planning community. However, the scope is limited to household activities, and the 7B-vs-72B margin is thin (~0.1 LCS points), leaving open whether the scale-substitution effect holds for genuinely complex long-horizon manipulation or non-household domains. A key open question is whether the hierarchical structure's benefits generalize when the atomic action vocabulary must be expanded to cover industrial or surgical manipulation.
