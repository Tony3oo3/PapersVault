## Problem

Existing stability certification methods for feature attributions rely on heavily smoothed classifiers (e.g., MuS) that sacrifice accuracy for smoothness and produce conservative, often vacuous guarantees beyond small perturbation radii. The paper addresses this by introducing soft stability—a probabilistic relaxation of hard stability—and a model-agnostic, sample-efficient certification algorithm that yields non-trivial, interpretable guarantees for any attribution method without requiring aggressive smoothing.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252413/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Given a classifier f, an input x (a 224×224 image segmented into 196 patches of 16×16, or a text sequence where each token is one feature), a binary attribution mask α selecting the top-25% of features, and a perturbation radius r, the method outputs a stability rate τ_r—the probability that the prediction is preserved when up to r additional features are added—along with a statistical confidence interval. Unlike hard stability's binary pass/fail at each radius, the stability rate provides a continuous, fine-grained curve across all perturbation radii, enabling meaningful comparison between explanation methods.

## Method

The Stability Certification Algorithm (SCA) directly estimates the stability rate by uniformly sampling N perturbed masks α′ from the additive perturbation set Δ_r(α) and computing the empirical fraction that preserve the prediction, with Hoeffding's inequality providing the confidence bound for soft stability and a simpler (1−ε)^N ≤ δ bound for hard stability. The key theoretical contribution uses Boolean function analysis in the monotone (unanimity) basis, where the random masking operator M_λ acts as a point-wise spectral contraction (each weight at subset T is scaled by λ^|T|), in contrast to the standard Fourier basis where smoothing causes complex binomial weight redistribution. This clean contraction yields a novel characterization showing that smoothing improves the worst-case stability rate lower bound by a factor of λ, explaining why mild smoothing (λ ≥ 0.5) improves stability at minimal accuracy cost. The sampling implementation uses Gumbel-softmax reparametrization to avoid numerical instability from large binomial coefficients when drawing uniform samples from Δ_r(α).

## Results

SCA certifies non-vacuous soft stability guarantees at perturbation radii where MuS-based hard stability certificates become vacuous (MuS guarantees only exist for radii ≤ 1/2λ). For vision models (ViT, ResNet50, ResNet18), LIME and SHAP achieve the highest stability rates, with all methods outperforming the random baseline; on RoBERTa, methods are less distinguishable. Mild smoothing (λ ≥ 0.5) improves stability rates while preserving classifier accuracy, with transformers (ViT, RoBERTa) showing a more gradual accuracy decline than CNNs. SCA requires only N = 150 forward passes for ε = δ = 0.1, with batching yielding up to 9.57× speedup for ResNet18.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SCA outperforms MuS [70] on the primary metric of certified stability radius: SCA yields non-trivial soft stability guarantees at larger perturbation radii, while MuS certificates become vacuous beyond radius 1/2λ (e.g., at λ = 0.5, MuS can only certify up to radius 1; at λ = 0.25, up to radius 2). Among explanation methods, SHAP [39] is consistently the most stable across vision models and top-k selections, followed by LIME [51], both of which substantially outperform Integrated Gradients [63] and the random baseline in stability rate. MFABA [75] falls between the top and bottom methods. On RoBERTa, the distinction between methods is less pronounced, and MuS-based hard stability certificates struggle to differentiate methods entirely, whereas SCA-based soft stability still separates them. The paper also shows that mild MuS-style smoothing (λ ≥ 0.5) improves stability rates for all models while preserving accuracy, unlike the aggressive smoothing (λ = 0.25) required by MuS for certification, which significantly degrades classifier performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Xue et al. (NeurIPS 2023), who introduced hard stability certification for feature attributions via multiplicative smoothing (MuS) and Lipschitz-based guarantees. The key departure is twofold: (1) replacing the binary hard-stability criterion with a probabilistic 'soft stability' rate that quantifies the fraction of additive perturbations preserving the prediction, and (2) substituting the smoothing-dependent Lipschitz certification with a simple model-agnostic sampling algorithm (SCA) grounded in Hoeffding's inequality. Theoretically, the paper introduces the monotone (unanimity) basis from game theory as a novel analytic tool for Boolean functions, showing that random masking induces a clean pointwise spectral contraction (λ^|T|) in this basis, in contrast to the algebraically complex weight redistribution it produces in the standard Fourier basis.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SCA provides a practical, model-agnostic certification tool that yields non-vacuous stability guarantees at perturbation radii where MuS certificates become vacuous, enabling meaningful comparison across explanation methods (LIME, SHAP, Integrated Gradients, MFABA) on both vision and language tasks. The finding that mild smoothing (λ≥0.5) improves stability while preserving accuracy challenges the aggressive-smoothing paradigm of prior certification work and suggests a more favorable accuracy-stability trade-off. Open questions include whether stability-regularized training can further improve explanation robustness, whether adaptive or selective smoothing based on feature importance can sharpen guarantees, and how certified stability rates align with human judgments of explanation quality.
