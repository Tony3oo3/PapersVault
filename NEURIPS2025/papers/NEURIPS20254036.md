## Problem

Normalizing flows have received little attention in the recent generative AI wave dominated by diffusion and discrete autoregressive models, and it remained unclear whether flow-based methods could scale to high-resolution, large-scale image synthesis. While TARFlow demonstrated that Transformer-based autoregressive flows are promising, it was unknown whether they could match the quality and scalability of state-of-the-art diffusion or discrete AR approaches at practical resolutions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254036/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes text prompts (encoded by a frozen T5-XL or a pretrained LLM like Gemma2), class labels (one-hot vectors), or masked/source images for inpainting and editing tasks. Images are mapped to a compressed latent space via a pretrained DiT VAE (48× spatial compression), so a 256×256 image becomes a 32×32 latent sequence of 1024 tokens. Variable aspect ratios are handled by bucketing into 9 shape bins with 3D-RoPE positional encoding, and the fine-tuned VAE decoder produces final pixel-space images at 256×256, 512×512, or 1024×1024 resolutions.

## Method

STARFlow builds on TARFlow with three key innovations: (1) a deep-shallow architecture where a single deep Transformer block (18 or 24 layers) captures most model capacity as a Gaussian language model, followed by 5 shallow 2-layer blocks that act as a learned image tokenizer, with conditioning applied only to the deep block; (2) learning in the latent space of a pretrained autoencoder with controlled Gaussian noise injection (σ_L=0.3) and decoder fine-tuning to handle noisy latents, replacing the score-based denoising step; and (3) a novel closed-form classifier-free guidance derived from the score function perspective for Gaussian heads, with variance-ratio clipping to ensure numerical stability at high guidance weights. The paper also provides a theoretical universality result (Proposition 1) showing that stacked autoregressive flows with T≥2 alternating-ordering blocks can model arbitrary continuous densities. Training is end-to-end maximum likelihood in continuous space via an ELBO objective with a frozen encoder, and the model remains a single invertible normalizing flow supporting exact log-likelihood computation.

## Results

On ImageNet-256×256, STARFlow (1.4B params) achieves FID 2.40, substantially outperforming the original TARFlow (FID 5.56) and approaching diffusion baselines like DiT (FID 2.27, 675M) and SiT (FID 2.06, 675M). On ImageNet-512×512, STARFlow reaches FID 3.00, slightly beating DiT-XL/2 (FID 3.04, 674M). For zero-shot text-to-image on COCO, the 3.8B STARFlow-FullData model achieves FID 9.1, matching GigaGAN (9.1, 1B) and outperforming DALL-E 2 (10.4, 5.5B), while on GenEval it scores 0.56 overall—slightly above SDXL (0.55) and well ahead of autoregressive baselines like LlamaGen (0.32) and Emu3 (0.54).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest same-family baseline TARFlow (Zhai et al., 2024), STARFlow reduces FID from 5.56 to 2.40 on ImageNet-256—a 57% improvement—driven by the deep-shallow architecture, latent-space learning, and the new guidance algorithm. Compared to diffusion models, STARFlow (FID 2.40, 1.4B) is slightly behind DiT (2.27, 675M) and SiT (2.06, 675M) on ImageNet-256, but at 512×512 it edges out DiT-XL/2 (3.00 vs. 3.04) while trailing EDM2-XXL (1.25). Against discrete autoregressive models, STARFlow is outperformed by VAR (1.73) and MAR (1.55) on ImageNet-256, but uses fewer parameters than LlamaGen-3B (1.4B vs. 3.1B) and avoids quantization. On zero-shot COCO text-to-image, STARFlow-FullData (FID 9.1) ties GigaGAN (9.1) and beats DALL-E 2 (10.4) and DART (11.1), though it trails Imagen (7.3) and Muse (7.9). On GenEval, STARFlow (0.56) surpasses SDXL (0.55), Emu3 (0.54), Show-o (0.53), and LlamaGen (0.32), but remains below DALL-E 3 (0.67) and SD3 (0.74), indicating a gap in compositional text alignment relative to the strongest diffusion systems.

## Relation to Prior Work

STARFlow directly extends TARFlow (Zhai et al., 2024), which first demonstrated that Transformer-based autoregressive flows can serve as competitive image generators, by introducing three key departures: a deep-shallow architecture that concentrates most capacity in a single deep block followed by lightweight refinement blocks (contrasting TARFlow's uniform depth allocation), learning in the latent space of pretrained autoencoders rather than pixel space, and a closed-form classifier-free guidance derived from the score-function perspective for Gaussian heads. The theoretical universality result (Proposition 1) provides the missing justification for why multi-block AFs with alternating orderings are expressive enough to warrant scaling, grounding the architectural choices in first principles rather than empirical tuning. The paper positions NFs as a third viable paradigm alongside diffusion and discrete autoregressive models, a framing that had been largely absent from the high-resolution image generation literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first successful demonstration that normalizing flows—a likelihood-based family that had been overshadowed by diffusion and discrete AR models—can scale to high-resolution, large-scale text-to-image generation with competitive sample quality, opening a new axis of exploration for the field. The exact-likelihood training (no quantization) and invertibility enable principled applications like training-free inpainting via Metropolis-Hastings and single-pass interactive editing, capabilities that are awkward for diffusion or discrete AR baselines. Key limitations include reliance on pretrained autoencoders (leaving joint latent-NF design unexplored), unoptimized inference speed relative to diffusion, and evaluation restricted to standard image benchmarks; whether the approach generalizes to video, 3D, or other modalities remains an open question.
