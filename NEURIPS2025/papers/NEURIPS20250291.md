## Problem

Flow-matching and diffusion models learn time-dependent transports that do not explicitly capture the data score at the data manifold, making it difficult to integrate partial observations, additional priors, or interaction terms for inverse problems and controlled generation. Energy-based models (EBMs) address this by learning a scalar energy whose Boltzmann distribution encodes the likelihood, but practical EBMs have historically suffered from poor sample quality, mode collapse, and reliance on complex ensembles or auxiliary generator networks. The paper asks whether a single time-independent scalar field can simultaneously provide efficient OT-like transport from noise and accurate Boltzmann density matching at the data manifold.

## Input / Output

For unconditional generation, the model takes a Gaussian noise sample x₀ ~ N(0,I) and iterates Langevin dynamics under the learned potential V_θ(x) for a fixed number of Euler-Heun steps (325 for CIFAR-10 32×32, 300 for ImageNet 32×32) to produce a sample from the data distribution. For inverse problems, the input additionally includes a noisy measurement y = A(x) + w and a forward operator A, which enter the Langevin update as a data-fidelity term ‖y − A(x)‖²/ζ² added to the energy. For protein design, the input is a target fitness value y that steers sampling via a learned fitness predictor, and the output is a batch of AAV capsid sequences with controllable diversity through an optional repulsive interaction energy W(x, x′). The architecture is a UNet (37M params, same as OT-CFM but with time fixed at t=0) followed by an 8-layer ViT head (12M params) that outputs a single scalar V_θ(x).

## Method

The core contribution is a two-regime training strategy derived from the JKO scheme's first-order optimality conditions in Wasserstein space. Away from the data manifold (ε ≈ 0), the gradient −∇V_θ(x) acts as an irrotational, time-independent velocity field that transports noise to data along OT geodesics, trained with a flow-matching loss L_OT that enforces ∇V_θ(x_t) ≈ −(x_data − T(x_data)) along minibatch OT couplings. Near the data manifold (ε ≈ ε_max), the same potential is refined with a contrastive divergence loss L_CD that shapes the Boltzmann distribution ρ_eq ∝ exp(−V_θ/ε_max) to match the data density, using Langevin-sampled negatives initialized half from data and half from noise. Training proceeds in two phases: a warm-up phase optimizing L_OT alone to establish a stable transport field, followed by joint optimization of L_OT + λ_CD·L_CD with a linear temperature schedule ε(t) that ramps from 0 to ε_max. The resulting single scalar field requires no time conditioning, no auxiliary generators, and no EBM ensembles, yet achieves FID 3.34 on CIFAR-10 and 6.64 on ImageNet 32×32, outperforming all prior EBMs by over 50% while matching or exceeding flow-matching baselines.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, Energy Matching (50M params) achieves a FID of 3.34 with 325 Euler-Heun steps, outperforming the best prior EBM (Cooperative DRL-large, 145M params, FID 3.68) and the best flow-based method (OT-CFM, 37M params, FID 4.04), while remaining competitive with DDPM++ (62M params, FID 3.45) and trailing only NCSN++ (107M params, FID 2.45). On ImageNet 32×32, Energy Matching reaches FID 6.64, surpassing all EBM baselines (best: Cooperative DRL at 9.35) and approaching flow matching (196M params, FID 5.02). For AAV protein inverse design, Energy Matching achieves the highest fitness (0.59 medium / 0.61 hard) while also improving diversity over the flow-based VLGPO baseline (5.86 vs 5.58 medium; 4.77 vs 4.29 hard), and adding a repulsion term further boosts diversity to 6.22/5.22 with negligible fitness loss.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Energy Matching decisively outperforms all prior EBM baselines on CIFAR-10: it improves FID from 3.68 (Cooperative DRL-large, 145M params) to 3.34 (50M params), a >9% relative reduction with roughly 3× fewer parameters, and from 8.61 (CLEL-large) to 3.34. Against flow-based methods, it beats OT-CFM (4.04 → 3.34) and Flow Matching (6.35 → 3.34) while using fewer sampling steps (325 vs 1000). The only method that surpasses Energy Matching on CIFAR-10 FID is NCSN++ (2.45), a 107M-parameter diffusion model requiring 1000 steps; DDPM++ (3.45) is marginally worse. On ImageNet 32×32, Energy Matching (6.64) still trails flow matching (5.02, 196M params) but dominates all EBM baselines by a wide margin (next best: Cooperative DRL at 9.35). For AAV protein design, Energy Matching matches or exceeds VLGPO on fitness (0.59 vs 0.58 medium; 0.61 vs 0.61 hard) while improving diversity (5.86 vs 5.58; 4.77 vs 4.29), and substantially outperforms diffusion-based (gg-dWJS: 0.48/0.33) and RL-based (LatProtRL: 0.57/0.57) baselines on fitness. On LID estimation, Energy Matching achieves the highest Spearman correlation with PNG compression rate (0.877 MNIST, 0.901 CIFAR-10), narrowly beating the diffusion-based NB method (0.864, 0.894) and FLIPD (0.837, 0.819).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate threads—optimal-transport flow matching (Lipman et al., 2023; Tong et al., 2023) and energy-based models trained via contrastive divergence (Hinton, 2002)—by deriving a two-regime decomposition from the JKO scheme's first-order optimality conditions in Wasserstein space (Jordan et al., 1998; Terpin et al., 2024). Far from the data manifold, the learned scalar potential acts as an irrotational OT velocity field; near the manifold, it transitions into a Boltzmann energy whose gradient drives Langevin equilibration. The key departure from prior EBM work (Gao et al., 2021; Cui and Han, 2024; Guo et al., 2023; Zhu et al., 2024) is that no time-conditioned ensembles, hierarchical latent spaces, or cooperative generator networks are needed—a single time-independent scalar field suffices for both transport and density modeling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By making EBMs competitive with flow and diffusion models in raw generation quality (FID 3.34 on CIFAR-10, 6.64 on ImageNet 32×32) while retaining an explicit, time-independent likelihood, the paper opens a practical path for inverse-problem solving, controlled multi-sample generation via interaction energies, and curvature-based LID estimation without the repeated noise↔data shuttling that plagues diffusion-based approaches. Limitations include the 20–40% GPU-memory overhead from input-gradient computation and the O(d³) cost of full Hessian evaluation for LID in high dimensions. Open questions include whether the approach scales to high-resolution image generation (e.g., 256×256 or 512×512), how it compares to recent inference-time scaling methods, and whether the two-regime training strategy can be further simplified or made fully end-to-end.
