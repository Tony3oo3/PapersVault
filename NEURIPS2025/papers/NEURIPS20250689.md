## Problem

Garg et al. (2024) raised the open problem of whether it is possible to efficiently achieve Õ(√T) ℓ₂-swap multicalibration error against bounded linear functions, with the previous best bound being Õ(T^{3/4}). The existing bounds for swap omniprediction and swap multicalibration in both online and distributional settings were significantly worse than their non-swap counterparts, and the sample complexity for swap omniprediction was prohibitively large at ~ε^{-10}. This paper answers the open question in a strongly affirmative sense, achieving Õ(T^{1/3}) online and Õ(ε^{-3}) sample complexity offline.

## Input / Output

In the online setting, at each of T rounds the adversary presents a context x_t ∈ R^d (with a fixed first coordinate for affine closure), the forecaster outputs a prediction p_t drawn from a conditional distribution over a discretization Z = {0, 1/N, …, 1} of [0,1], and the adversary reveals a binary label y_t ∈ {0,1}. The algorithm is deterministic in its conditional distributions (only the sampling p_t ~ P_t is random), which is a key structural requirement for the Freedman-based concentration argument. In the distributional setting, the output is a randomized predictor that mixes uniformly over T online predictors, with explicit sample complexity guarantees as a function of target error ε.

## Method

The core contribution is a three-step reduction chain: (i) a concentration bound via Freedman's inequality relating ℓ₂-swap multicalibration to a new 'pseudo' variant measured via conditional distributions rather than realized predictions; (ii) a reduction from pseudo swap multicalibration to pseudo contextual swap regret, leveraging the affine-closure property of the linear hypothesis class; and (iii) a deterministic algorithm for pseudo contextual swap regret built on the Blum-Mansour reduction, where each of N+1 external regret sub-problems is solved by Online Newton Step on exp-concave scaled losses, combined with the improved randomized rounding of Fishelson et al. (2025) that yields a 1/N² per-step error rather than 1/N. The key insight is that the pseudo formulation removes the randomness in predictions, making the optimization deterministic and allowing the BM reduction's N external regret algorithms to each achieve Õ(1) regret, yielding a total bound of Õ(T/N² + N) optimized at N = Õ(T^{1/3}). An online-to-batch conversion then propagates these improved online rates to distributional sample complexity bounds.

## Results

The paper achieves a \tilde{O}(T^{1/3}) \ell_2-swap multicalibration error against bounded linear functions, answering an open problem posed by Garg et al. (2024) who had only achieved \tilde{O}(T^{3/4}). Propagating this bound yields \tilde{O}(T^{2/3}) for \ell_1-swap multicalibration and swap omniprediction (improving over \tilde{O}(T^{7/8})), and \tilde{O}(T^{3/5}) for contextual swap regret (improving over \tilde{O}(T^{3/4})). In the distributional setting, the paper establishes \tilde{O}(\varepsilon^{-3}) sample complexity for swap omniprediction, \tilde{O}(\varepsilon^{-2.5}) for swap agnostic learning and \ell_2-swap multicalibration, and \tilde{O}(\varepsilon^{-5}) for \ell_1-swap multicalibration, all significantly improving prior bounds.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is Garg et al. (2024, SODA), which achieved \tilde{O}(T^{3/4}) for \ell_2-swap multicalibration and contextual swap regret, and \tilde{O}(T^{7/8}) for \ell_1-swap multicalibration and swap omniprediction in the online setting. This paper improves all four online bounds: the \ell_2-swap multicalibration rate improves from T^{3/4} to T^{1/3} (a 2/3 vs. 1/3 exponent gap), contextual swap regret from T^{3/4} to T^{3/5}, and both \ell_1-swap multicalibration and swap omniprediction from T^{7/8} to T^{2/3}. In the distributional setting, Globus-Harris et al. (2023, ICML) had established \tilde{O}(\varepsilon^{-5}) for swap agnostic error and \ell_2-swap multicalibration, while Hébert-Johnson et al. (2018, ICML) and Globus-Harris et al. (2023) together gave \tilde{O}(\varepsilon^{-10}) for \ell_1-swap multicalibration and swap omniprediction. This paper halves the exponent for swap agnostic error and \ell_2-swap multicalibration (\varepsilon^{-5} → \varepsilon^{-2.5}), halves it for \ell_1-swap multicalibration (\varepsilon^{-10} → \varepsilon^{-5}), and reduces swap omniprediction from \varepsilon^{-10} to \varepsilon^{-3}. The key technical improvement over Garg et al. (2024) comes from replacing the Ito (2020) swap-regret-to-external-regret reduction with the Blum-Mansour (2007) reduction combined with the Fishelson et al. (2025) rounding procedure, yielding a deterministic algorithm with a T/N² + N regret decomposition instead of T/N + N√T.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Garg et al. (2024), who established the first efficient online algorithms for swap multicalibration and swap omniprediction via a reduction to contextual swap regret using Ito's (2020) randomized reduction. The key departure is replacing Ito's reduction with the Blum-Mansour (2007) reduction combined with Fishelson et al. (2025)'s improved deterministic rounding, and introducing 'pseudo' variants of swap multicalibration and contextual swap regret that are easier to optimize. The concentration argument bridging pseudo and true swap multicalibration adapts the Freedman's inequality martingale analysis from Luo et al. (2025) for calibration to the contextual setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that ℓ₂-swap multicalibration achieves the same Õ(T^{1/3}) rate as ℓ₂-calibration—despite being a strictly stronger notion requiring per-level-set guarantees—is a meaningful conceptual advance that tightens the gap between these notions. The improved sample complexity bounds (e.g., ε^{-3} for swap omniprediction vs. ε^{-10} previously) make swap omniprediction substantially more practical. Open questions include whether the Õ(T^{3/5}) contextual swap regret bound can be improved to Õ(T^{1/3}) (matching the non-contextual rate), and whether the framework extends to arbitrary hypothesis and loss classes with oracle efficiency.
