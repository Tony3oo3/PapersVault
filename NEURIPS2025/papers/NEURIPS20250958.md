## Problem

Existing truncated sampling strategies for LLM text generation (top-p, min-p, top-k) lack a principled way to balance creativity and coherence, particularly at high temperatures where coherence degrades sharply. Min-p, the current state-of-the-art, estimates model confidence using only the probability of the single top token, ignoring the overall shape of the distribution and leading to over-truncation in sparse distributions and under-truncation in dense ones. Moreover, no prior work provides a formal optimization-theoretic foundation for the creativity-coherence trade-off in autoregressive sampling.

## Input / Output

At each autoregressive step, the method consumes the full next-token probability distribution p over the vocabulary (obtained from the LLM's softmax output) and a single hyperparameter α ∈ (0,1) (set to 0.4 by default). It produces a selected token subset S and a renormalized distribution q over S from which the next token is sampled. Crucially, the entropy threshold is dynamic: it is set to α·H(p), so the allowed randomness automatically tightens when the model is confident (low H(p)) and loosens when the model is uncertain (high H(p)), adapting at every generation step without additional tuning.

## Method

The paper first formulates the creativity-coherence trade-off as an entropy-constrained minimum divergence (ECMD) problem: minimize the Jensen-Shannon divergence between the original distribution p and the truncated distribution q, subject to H(q) ≤ α·H(p). It then proves (Theorem 1) that JSD depends only on the cumulative mass Γ_S of the selected subset, reducing ECMD to an entropy-constrained mass maximization (ECMM) problem, and further proves ECMM is NP-hard via a reduction from Cardinality-Constrained Subset Sum. The practical algorithm, top-H, is a greedy procedure that sorts tokens in descending probability order and iteratively adds tokens to S, computing the entropy of the renormalized distribution incrementally in O(1) per step, until adding the next token would violate the entropy bound α·H(p). This yields an overall O(n log n) complexity dominated by sorting, with negligible overhead (~0.8%) compared to min-p or top-p in practice. The greedy solution is shown empirically to closely approximate the exhaustive-search optimum (ratio Γ_S^g / Γ_S* ≈ 1.0) and theoretically to achieve an additive gap of O(n^{−α(s−1)}) under a Zipfian probability model.

## Results

On GSM8K, Top-H achieves up to 25.63% accuracy improvement over min-p (39.35% vs. 13.72% on LLaMA3.1-8B at T=2.0), while top-p collapses to 2.65% under the same setting. On GPQA, Top-H outperforms min-p by up to 7.36% (Phi-3-Mini, T=2.0) and maintains competitive accuracy at T=1.0 across all three models. On Alpaca-Eval, Top-H shows a length-controlled win-rate improvement of up to 17.11% over min-p on LLaMA3.1-8B, with only a 3.78% degradation from T=1 to T=2 (vs. 34.06% for top-p). LLM-as-a-judge scores confirm Top-H produces the highest creativity and coherence at all temperatures, averaging 8.05–8.85/10 versus 6.65–7.85 for min-p and 5.95–7.75 for top-p.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Top-H consistently outperforms all four baselines across every benchmark and temperature setting. Against min-p (Nguyen et al., 2024), the primary SoTA baseline, Top-H gains up to 25.63% on GSM8K, 7.36% on GPQA, and 17.11% on Alpaca-Eval win rate, with the advantage growing at higher temperatures where min-p's single-top-token heuristic becomes most unreliable. Against top-p / nucleus sampling (Holtzman et al., 2019), Top-H is dramatically superior at T≥1.5, where top-p suffers near-total collapse (e.g., 2.65% vs. 39.35% on GSM8K/LLaMA3.1-8B at T=2) due to its inability to adapt to distributional entropy. Against top-k (Fan et al., 2018) and η-sampling (Hewitt et al., 2022), Top-H achieves higher Alpaca-Eval win rates and MT-Bench judge scores across all tested temperatures, as shown in Figure 2. The LLM-as-a-judge evaluation further confirms that Top-H dominates min-p and top-p on both creativity and coherence metrics at every temperature, with the gap widening as T increases.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the established family of truncated sampling strategies (temperature, top-k, top-p/nucleus, η-sampling, min-p) by replacing their fixed or peak-based thresholds with a distribution-wide entropy bound. Conceptually, it repurposes the Jensen-Shannon divergence and Shannon entropy—tools from information theory—into a constrained optimization framework (ECMD/ECMM) that formalizes the creativity-coherence trade-off previously handled only heuristically. The greedy solution (top-H) is the first sampling method to explicitly upper-bound the entropy of the renormalized sampling distribution in proportion to the original distribution's entropy, rather than relying on cumulative probability (top-p) or a single top-token probability (min-p).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By providing the first theoretical grounding (NP-hardness, JSD-to-mass-maximization equivalence) for what was previously a collection of ad-hoc heuristics, the paper gives the community a principled lens for designing future sampling strategies. The negligible 0.8% computational overhead and single hyperparameter (α) make top-H a practical drop-in replacement, particularly valuable for high-temperature creative generation where top-p and min-p degrade sharply. Open questions include whether the Zipf-based approximation bound generalizes to non-regularly-varying distributions, whether α=0.4 transfers across model families and languages, and whether the LLM-as-judge evaluation (GPT-4o) introduces systematic biases that a human study could resolve.
