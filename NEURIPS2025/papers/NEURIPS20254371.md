## Problem

Standard preference-aligned LLM agents (trained via DPO, IPO, or PPO) achieve token-level Bellman optimality but are provably suboptimal in multi-party collaborative settings where an intervention agent provides suggestions of variable quality. The paper formalizes this using the Modified-Action MDP (MAMDP) framework and shows that Bellman-optimal policies in the underlying MDP treat all contextual information as static state features, failing to causally distinguish helpful from misleading interventions. This makes it non-trivial to train collaborators that selectively incorporate valuable suggestions while maintaining reasoning integrity against misleading or irrelevant ones.

## Input / Output

The collaborator agent receives a task instruction prompt, the full multi-turn dialogue history (alternating collaborator and intervention utterances over up to 15 turns), and the current intervention utterance as conditioning context. In the 'full-press' condition the agent produces free-form natural language responses; in the 'no-press' condition it outputs discrete structured beliefs (e.g., symbolic weight propositions like 'green > red' or card-flip stances such as support/oppose/unsure). During training, a counterfactual variant of the input is constructed by prepending a short prompt prefix that explicitly states the intervention will not improve performance, enabling computation of the counterfactual policy on the same sampled tokens without additional rollouts.

## Method

ICR (Interruptible Collaborative Roleplayer) extends PPO with a counterfactual invariance KL divergence term that penalizes the collaborator's policy for deviating from its own counterfactual rendering—the policy conditioned on being told the intervention is useless. The key computational trick is that factual log-probabilities are already cached from the standard PPO rollout, so the counterfactual KL requires only one additional forward pass with a counterfactual prompt prefix (with stop-gradient), adding negligible overhead. Theoretically, the paper proves (Theorem 3.2) that standard preference-aligned policies are suboptimal in MAMDPs, and that the counterfactual KL term directly bounds the suboptimality gap (Theorem B.4), which vanishes as the regularization weight λ_Intent grows. Training uses Meta-Llama-3-8B-Instruct with LoRA, initialized from GPT-4o expert roleplay trajectories, and relies on proxy task rewards (factual accuracy for the Weights Task, logically aligned card-checking for DeliData) rather than explicit common-ground rewards, yet still achieves superior common-ground convergence as an emergent property.

## Results

ICR-trained collaborators consistently outperform all baselines across both tasks and both communication settings. On the Weights Task (full-press), ICR achieves an ACC of 14.06, a 47% improvement over the next-best DPO (9.56); in the no-press variant, ICR scores 10.87 versus PPO's 7.81 (39% gain). On DeliData, ICR reaches 0.88 solution accuracy (full-press) and 3.35 common-ground gain, surpassing DPO (0.82 ACC, 2.80 CG) and PPO (0.81 ACC, 2.94 CG), while BC-COLLABORATOR actually yields negative CG (−0.13), indicating it reduces rather than builds solution diversity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ICR dominates all four baselines on every metric and setting. Against DPO (the strongest preference-optimization baseline), ICR leads by 4.50 ACC points on Weights Task full-press (14.06 vs. 9.56) and by 0.06 ACC / 0.55 CG on DeliData full-press (0.88 vs. 0.82; 3.35 vs. 2.80). Against PPO (the on-policy RL baseline), ICR gains 6.69 ACC on Weights Task full-press and 0.41 CG on DeliData full-press (3.35 vs. 2.94). IPO and PSO-INTENT trail both DPO and PPO on most metrics, with PSO-INTENT (which makes interventions causally binding via a system prompt) underperforming DPO on Weights Task full-press (8.09 vs. 9.56), suggesting that naively treating all interventions as beneficial is counterproductive. BC-COLLABORATOR is the weakest baseline overall and uniquely produces negative common-ground gain (−0.13), indicating that pure imitation of expert trajectories actively reduces solution diversity rather than expanding shared understanding.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the safe-interruptability literature (Orseau & Armstrong, Hadfield-Menell et al.) from safety-critical RL into multi-party collaborative dialogue, formalizing the collaborator-intervention interaction via the Modified-Action MDP (Langlois & Everitt, 2021). It departs from standard preference-alignment methods (DPO, IPO, PPO) by proving they are Bellman-optimal only in the underlying token-MDP but generally suboptimal in the MAMDP where interventions require discriminative evaluation (Theorem 3.2). The proposed counterfactual invariance KL regularization draws on path-specific objectives (Farquhar et al., 2022) and causal/counterfactual agent learning (Ward et al., 2023), but applies them to LLM collaborator training via a simple prompt-prefix counterfactual condition rather than structural causal models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that standard RLHF/DPO-trained LLMs are theoretically suboptimal as collaborators in the presence of variable-quality interventions is a useful diagnostic for the field, and the ICR method offers a practical, low-overhead fix (one extra forward pass). However, validation is limited to 8B-parameter models on two narrow tasks with a single fixed GPT-4o intervention agent, leaving open whether the approach generalizes to diverse partners, adversarial interventions, larger models, or domains requiring deception detection (e.g., Diplomacy). The finding that common-ground convergence emerges without explicit CG-based rewards is promising but would benefit from broader empirical confirmation before being treated as a general principle.
