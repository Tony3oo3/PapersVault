## Problem

Applying style transfer to Gaussian Splatting representations beyond simple color and opacity changes remains challenging: existing GS-based methods either restrict edits to appearance only, rely on large diffusion models, or are limited to a single data modality. No unified framework existed that could perform geometry-aware, temporally coherent style transfer across 2D images, videos, 3D objects, and 4D dynamic scenes using both image and text conditioning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253746/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained GS-based model (3DGS, D-MiSo, MiRaGe, or VeGaS depending on modality) along with a style conditioning signal that is either a reference image or a free-form text prompt. It outputs stylized Gaussian primitives that render as the corresponding modality (2D image, video, 3D scene, or 4D dynamic scene) while preserving the original number of Gaussian primitives—no densification or pruning is performed. This size preservation enables linear interpolation between stylized parameter sets for smooth style blending.

## Method

CLIPGaussian is a two-stage plug-in: first, a standard GS-based base model is trained on the input data; second, all Gaussian parameters (position, covariance, opacity, SH colors, and modality-specific parameters) are fine-tuned for 5000 steps without altering the primitive count. The fine-tuning uses a composite loss with four terms: a VGG-19 content loss (MSE on conv4_2/conv5_2 features), a directional CLIP loss for global style alignment, a patch-level CLIP loss computed over randomly cropped and perspective-augmented patches for local style detail, and a background loss to prevent unwanted background artifacts. The key mechanism is simultaneously optimizing on full rendered views and small random patches, with CLIP (ViT-B/32) providing the style signal and VGG-19 anchoring content fidelity. Because the method operates directly on Gaussian primitives rather than stylizing training images, it achieves end-to-end geometry and appearance modification with temporal coherence in video/4D settings, where each Gaussian spans multiple frames.

## Results

On the 3D style transfer benchmark (NeRF-Synthetic + Mip-NeRF 360), CLIPGaussian achieves CLIP-S of 26.86 and CLIP-SIM of 26.31 under text conditioning, substantially outperforming I-GS2GS (16.80/12.03) and DGE (17.59/12.27). Under image conditioning, CLIPGaussian reaches CLIP-S 72.65 and CLIP-SIM 20.72, approaching G-Style (76.94/24.94) while preserving the original model size (+0% vs. G-Style's +126% Gaussian count). The method maintains 98.80% CLIP-F (content fidelity) across both conditions, and a user study with 30 participants per scenario confirms statistically significant preference for CLIPGaussian in text-based object stylization (Conover-Friedman p < 0.05).

## Baselines & Comparisons

For text-conditioned 3D style transfer, CLIPGaussian decisively outperforms both I-GS2GS and DGE on style fidelity (CLIP-S: 26.86 vs. 16.80/17.59; CLIP-SIM: 26.31 vs. 12.03/12.27), though it trades off view consistency (CLIP-CONS: 2.34 vs. 13.53/12.46). For image-conditioned 3D, CLIPGaussian surpasses StyleGaussian, SGSST, and ABC-GS on CLIP-S and CLIP-SIM, and is competitive with G-Style (72.65 vs. 76.94 CLIP-S) while avoiding G-Style's 126% increase in Gaussian count. In 4D, qualitative comparisons show CLIPGaussian captures finer texture and geometric detail than 4DStyleGaussian (image-conditioned) and Instruct 4D-to-4D (text-conditioned), which focuses primarily on color palette. For 2D images, CLIPGaussian preserves content identity better than AdaIN, StyTr², InstructPix2Pix, and GPT-4o, though it acknowledges a visual-quality gap versus large diffusion models. In video, CLIPGaussian demonstrates superior temporal consistency over CCPL, UniST, Text2Video, and RerenderAVideo, attributed to style modifications propagating coherently through shared Gaussian primitives spanning multiple frames.

## Relation to Prior Work

CLIPGaussian positions itself as a plug-in module for existing Gaussian Splatting pipelines (3DGS, D-MiSo, MiRaGe, VeGaS), departing from prior GS style transfer methods (StyleGaussian, ReGS, G-Style) that restrict edits to color and opacity by instead optimizing all Gaussian parameters including position and scale. It also diverges from diffusion-based GS editing approaches (I-GS2GS, DGE, InstantStyleGaussian) by relying solely on CLIP directional losses and VGG-19 content losses rather than large generative models. The CLIP-based directional loss formulation is adapted from CLIPStyler and StyleGAN-NADA, but its application to Gaussian primitives across four data modalities under a single unified architecture is the paper's core methodological contribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in providing a single, lightweight plug-in that handles style transfer uniformly across 2D images, videos, 3D objects, and 4D dynamic scenes without retraining base models or inflating model size, which is valuable for real-time rendering pipelines in AR, gaming, and content creation. A key limitation acknowledged by the authors is that 2D visual quality lags behind diffusion-based methods, and performance is contingent on the fidelity of the underlying GS reconstruction. Open questions include whether CLIP-based losses can capture highly abstract or novel style concepts that fall outside CLIP's training distribution, and whether the approach generalizes to even more complex 4D scenes with multiple interacting objects.
