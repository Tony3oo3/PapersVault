## Problem

LLM multi-agent collaboration systems are vulnerable to hallucination amplification and adversarial error injection that propagate and amplify across interacting agents, yet existing defenses either target individual model outputs without modeling inter-agent propagation dynamics or oversimplify agent dependencies through majority voting. No prior method provides a unified, model-agnostic framework that explicitly captures how hallucinations and injected errors cascade through the collaboration graph over multiple rounds.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250272/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sequence of temporal attributed graphs where each timestep's graph has nodes representing agents (with BERT-encoded response embeddings as attributes) and directed edges representing inter-agent communications from the prior round. At each timestep, GUARDIAN outputs a node-level anomaly score for every agent and an edge-level structural anomaly score, then removes the single highest-scoring anomalous node (and its incident edges) from the graph before the next round proceeds. The cleaned graph sequence ultimately yields a consensus final answer from the remaining agents.

## Method

GUARDIAN models multi-agent collaboration as a discrete-time temporal attributed graph and applies an unsupervised encoder-decoder with dual decoders: a GCN-based spatial encoder captures 2-hop neighborhood structure, a Transformer-based temporal encoder aggregates historical graph embeddings via cross-time self-attention, and separate attribute (MSE) and structure (BCE) reconstruction decoders independently score anomalies in continuous feature space and discrete topology space. A graph abstraction mechanism grounded in Information Bottleneck Theory compresses temporal interaction graphs while preserving task-relevant patterns, with theoretical bounds proving that inter-agent information flow is bounded by a controllable rate η and temporal state transitions are constrained by a prior-guided upper bound. Training follows an incremental paradigm where the model is fine-tuned sequentially across timesteps, removing previously detected anomalous nodes from subsequent inputs so that all collaboration rounds undergo detection rather than being split into train/test partitions. The total loss combines weighted attribute and structural reconstruction with the information bottleneck term, and the approach remains model-agnostic, requiring no modifications to the underlying LLMs.

## Results

GUARDIAN achieves an average 4.2% accuracy improvement over the strongest baseline (DyLAN) across all three safety scenarios and all backbone models. Gains are most pronounced on mathematical reasoning (MATH), where GUARDIAN improves by 7.1% over the best baseline and up to 15.4% when using GPT-3.5-turbo (56.2% vs. 40.8% for DyLAN under hallucination amplification). Anomaly detection rates average above 80% with a peak of 94.74% (FEVER, communication-targeted attacks), while the False Discovery Rate stays below 20% in most settings (as low as 8.32% on MATH under agent-targeted attacks). GUARDIAN also achieves the lowest API call count among all methods (e.g., 4.48 vs. 8.00 for LLM Debate on MMLU with GPT-3.5-turbo) and the lowest runtime on MMLU (18.89s) and FEVER (17.13s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GUARDIAN outperforms all four baseline categories across every safety scenario and backbone model. Against the strongest multi-agent framework baseline DyLAN [11], GUARDIAN gains 0.9–7.5 percentage points (e.g., 56.2% vs. 40.8% on MATH under hallucination amplification with GPT-3.5-turbo; 60.1% vs. 52.6% on MMLU under communication-targeted attacks). Against the hallucination-detection baseline SelfCheckGPT [43], GUARDIAN's advantage is dramatic on MATH (56.2% vs. 7.4% with GPT-3.5-turbo), where SelfCheckGPT's consistency-checking mechanism fails to handle multi-step reasoning. Against the error-detection baselines Challenger and Inspector [30], GUARDIAN leads by 15–30+ points in most settings (e.g., 57.3% vs. 31.8% for Challenger on MMLU under agent-targeted attacks with GPT-3.5-turbo), indicating that prompt-based verification agents are far less effective than structural graph-based anomaly detection. LLM Debate [1] serves as the no-defense baseline and is consistently the weakest method, confirming that multi-agent collaboration without safety mechanisms is highly vulnerable to both hallucination amplification and adversarial error injection.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends graph-based anomaly detection (previously applied to financial fraud and social networks) to the domain of LLM multi-agent collaboration, introducing a temporal attributed graph representation where nodes are agent responses and edges are inter-agent communications. It builds directly on the Graph Information Bottleneck framework (Wu et al., NeurIPS 2020) as the first systematic application of IB principles to safety in LLM agent systems, and departs from prior hallucination/error detection methods (SelfCheckGPT, Challenger, Inspector) that check individual outputs in isolation by explicitly modeling propagation dynamics across timesteps. The incremental training paradigm and dual-decoder (attribute + structure) reconstruction architecture are novel design choices tailored to the sequential, multi-round nature of agent debates under the A2A protocol.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As multi-agent LLM systems become production-relevant (e.g., via Google's A2A protocol), the risk of hallucination cascades and adversarial error injection grows, yet existing defenses either modify base models or ignore propagation structure. GUARDIAN's model-agnostic, unsupervised approach is practically important because it works as a wrapper around any LLM without retraining. Key limitations include: FDR still reaches ~30% under communication-targeted attacks, experiments use only 100 questions per dataset with 3–7 agents, and the method's performance degrades noticeably beyond 5 agents (Table 4). Open questions include scalability to hundreds of agents, handling adaptive adversaries that learn GUARDIAN's pruning strategy, and whether the temporal graph abstraction generalizes to asynchronous or partially-observed collaboration topologies.
