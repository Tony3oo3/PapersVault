## Problem

Existing unified convergence analyses of permutation-based SGD (e.g., Lu et al. 2022b, Koloskova et al. 2024) rely on bounding the order error independently per epoch, which fails for Dependent Permutations (DP) such as GraBs where the example order in one epoch is explicitly determined by the order in previous epochs. No prior framework could simultaneously cover Arbitrary Permutations, Independent Permutations (RR, FlipFlop), and Dependent Permutations (GraB, PairGraB) under a single theoretical umbrella.

## Input / Output

The framework takes as input a finite-sum objective with N local functions, an initial parameter vector, a step size, and a permutation generation rule (arbitrary, random reshuffling, flip-flop, or GraB-style balancing/reordering). For the FL extension, it additionally takes the number of local steps K and the number of simultaneously participating clients S per round. The output is a high-probability convergence rate bound on the average squared gradient norm over Q epochs, along with the updated parameter vector and the next-epoch permutation produced by the permuting algorithm at each epoch boundary.

## Method

The core contribution is Assumption 2, which generalizes the prior per-epoch order-error bound by allowing (φ̄_q)² to depend on order errors from up to ν previous epochs (via coefficients A_i) and gradient norms from previous epochs (via coefficients B_i), thereby explicitly encoding cross-epoch permutation dependence. Theorem 1 then derives a unified convergence rate under this assumption, with the step-size constraint depending on the spectral radius of the A_i coefficients. For DP (GraBs), the key step is proving that the herding-based balancing-and-reordering mechanism (from Alweiss et al. 2021 and Harvey & Samadi 2014) yields a contraction: (φ̄_q)² ≤ (3/4)(φ̄_{q-1})² + O(‖∇f(x_{q-1})‖²) + O(ζ²), which fits Assumption 2 with A_1 = 3/4. For FL, a new order error φ̄_q (Definition 2) is introduced to handle partially parallel client updates, and the framework is adapted to use pseudo-gradients from local GD steps instead of true gradients, yielding the first unified analysis of regularized-participation FL with arbitrary client permutations including the proposed FL-GraB.

## Results

The paper's main contribution is a unified convergence framework for permutation-based SGD that, for the first time, encompasses Dependent Permutations (GraBs). For Arbitrary Permutations (AP), the bound is O(LF₀/Q + (LF₀Nζ/NQ)^{2/3}), matching Lu et al. [2022b]. For Random Reshuffling (RR) and FlipFlop, the bound improves to O(LF₀/Q + (LF₀√Nζ/NQ)^{2/3}), matching the best known RR rate and extending it to FlipFlop for the first time. For GraBs (DP), the bound is O((L̃F₀ + (L_{2,∞}F₀ζ)^{2/3})/Q + (L_{2,∞}F₀ζ/NQ)^{2/3}), matching the original GraB bounds under a weaker assumption. In FL, the proposed FL-GraB achieves O((F₀ + ζ²/L)exp(−μQ/L) + (L_{2,∞}²S²ζ²/μ³N²Q²) + (L_{2,∞}²ζ²/μ³N²Q²)), the first unified client-ordering framework.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to Lu et al. [2022b], this work matches their AP and RR convergence rates exactly, but extends the framework to include FlipFlop (same rate as RR) and, critically, Dependent Permutations (GraBs), which prior unified analyses could not handle due to inter-epoch permutation dependency. Compared to Koloskova et al. [2024], this work achieves a tighter bound for RR (the √N factor in the error term vs. N in Koloskova's bound, which treats RR the same as AP), though Koloskova's first term (optimization term) is slightly better for large Q. For FL, the paper matches the bounds of Cho et al. [2023] for FL-AP and Malinovsky et al. [2023a] for FL-RR when the optimization term is omitted (large Q), while adding the new FL-GraB algorithm with no prior bound. Empirically (Figure 1), PairGraB and GraB consistently outperform RR and FlipFlop in both SGD and FL settings, with the order error tracking the distance to optimum, validating the theoretical framework. The key advantage over all prior unified analyses is the inclusion of DP (GraBs) via Assumption 2, which explicitly characterizes cross-epoch permutation dependence.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the unified convergence analysis framework for permutation-based SGD established by Lu et al. (ICLR 2022) and Koloskova et al. (ICML 2024), which could only handle Arbitrary Permutations (AP) and Independent Permutations (IP) by bounding the order error independently per epoch. The key departure is Assumption 2, which explicitly characterizes cross-epoch permutation dependence, enabling the first unified treatment of Dependent Permutations (DP), including GraB and PairGraB built on the herding/balancing machinery of Alweiss et al. (STOC 2021) and Harvey & Samadi (COLT 2014). The paper further transplants this ordering theory to Federated Learning with regularized client participation, building on Wang & Ji (NeurIPS 2022) and Malinovsky et al. (2023) for the FL setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a single template into which any future permutation-based SGD algorithm can be plugged by proving Assumption 2, reducing the analysis burden to identifying the cross-epoch order-error relation. The extension to FL with regularized participation and the proposal of FL-GraB open a new direction for client ordering in federated settings. Limitations include the absence of new SGD algorithms, the restriction to regularized (cyclic) participation in FL, and the use of plain GD as the local solver, leaving open questions about local-update ordering and more general participation patterns.
