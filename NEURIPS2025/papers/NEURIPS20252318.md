## Problem

Despite extensive experimental characterization of rodent whisker-to-cortex pathways, no computational model has been shown to match neural population responses in somatosensory cortex, and the inductive biases required for brain-like tactile processing remain unidentified. Concurrently, artificial tactile systems fail to match animal-level perception, partly because no systematic exploration of temporal architectures trained on biomechanically realistic whisker force/torque sequences has been conducted.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252318/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a spatiotemporal tactile signal: a 5×7 whisker array (30 whiskers, zero-padded) producing force and torque readings over 22 timesteps per trial, corresponding to the ~20 Hz rodent whisking frequency. For supervised training, the output is a 117-class ShapeNet object label; for self-supervised training, it is a contrastive embedding. For neural evaluation, intermediate layer activations are extracted and compared via noise-corrected RSA to 999 recorded units across 11 mice performing a 2-object (convex/concave) discrimination task, with the best-performing layer selected per model.

## Method

The paper introduces an Encoder-Attender-Decoder (EAD) parameterization that systematically combines temporal neural network modules: convolutional recurrent encoders (ConvRNNs with IntersectionRNN, UGRNN, LSTM, or GRU cells; ResNet; S4), attention-based attenders (GPT/Transformer, Mamba, or none), and an MLP decoder. Models are trained under supervised categorization or self-supervised objectives (SimCLR, SimSiam, autoencoding) using tactile-specific augmentations—vertical/horizontal flips, rotation, and temporal reversal of the force/torque array—that mimic physically meaningful whisker-array transformations, in contrast to standard image augmentations that cause training failure. The key architectural insight is that convolutional recurrent encoders, which locally integrate smooth temporal signals, outperform both purely feedforward (ResNet) and state-space (S4) encoders for tactile categorization and neural alignment, while GPT-based attenders provide modest downstream gains. The best models saturate the explainable neural variance in rodent barrel cortex and exceed inter-animal consistency, with contrastive SimCLR-trained IntersectionRNN EADs matching supervised neural fits as a label-free, ethologically relevant proxy.

## Results

The best supervised model (Zhuang+GPT+Supervised) achieves the highest top-5 tactile categorization accuracy, while the SimCLR-trained IntersectionRNN EAD achieves the best absolute neural alignment score (RSA Pearson's r ≈ 0.96), saturating the noise ceiling and exceeding the mean animal-to-animal consistency baseline (a2a = 0.18, max = 1.34). Raw sensor inputs achieve only r = 0.46, and randomly initialized models show moderately positive correlation, confirming the importance of both architecture and training. A linear relationship (r = 0.59) is observed between supervised categorization performance and neural fit. SSL models achieve roughly one order of magnitude lower linear-probe categorization accuracy (e.g., 0.15 for Inter+SimCLR vs. much higher for supervised) yet match or approach supervised neural alignment scores.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the feedforward ResNet18 encoder [He et al., 2016], ConvRNN encoders (especially IntersectionRNN) consistently outperform in both supervised tactile categorization accuracy and neural alignment (RSA r), confirming that recurrent temporal processing is critical for tactile signals. Against the S4 state-space model [Gu et al., 2021], the comparison is stark: S4-based models fail to train entirely (losses explode before the first epoch completes), making them non-competitive. Against Mamba [Gu & Dao, 2023] as an attender, GPT-based attenders [Haldar et al., 2024] provide modest but consistent improvements in both categorization and neural fit, suggesting attention-like mechanisms add value downstream of the ConvRNN encoder. Against the original Zhuang et al. [2017] recurrent model, the paper's EAD framework with IntersectionRNN and GPT attender substantially extends performance, and the SimCLR-trained IntersectionRNN variant achieves the best absolute neural fit with fewer parameters (~3.8×10⁷) than the supervised GPT variant (~6.4×10⁷). Overall, the paper's ConvRNN-based EAD models pass the NeuroAI Turing Test by exceeding the inter-animal consistency baseline, a threshold none of the feedforward or SSM baselines approach.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Zhuang et al. (2017), which provided the first goal-driven computational model of the rodent whisker-trigeminal system but was limited to simple recurrent architectures, supervised loss only, and no comparison to brain recordings. It incorporates ConvRNN architectures from Nayebi et al. (2018) that previously matched primate visual cortex dynamics, and builds on the WHISKiT biomechanical simulator (Zweifel et al., 2021) to generate realistic force/torque inputs. The key departure is the Encoder-Attender-Decoder (EAD) parameterization that systematically combines ConvRNN encoders with Transformer/Mamba attenders, alongside the introduction of tactile-specific self-supervised augmentations and the first direct RSA-based neural alignment evaluation against rodent barrel cortex recordings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This provides the first quantitative characterization of inductive biases (recurrent convolutional processing, tactile-specific SSL augmentations) needed for computational models to match rodent somatosensory cortex, and demonstrates that label-free contrastive learning with modality-appropriate augmentations achieves neural alignment comparable to supervised training—suggesting somatosensory cortex encodes broad, task-agnostic sensory features rather than categorization-specific ones. A critical limitation is that the neural evaluation uses only 6 stimuli (2 objects × 3 distances) from 11 mice, severely constraining the captured neural variability and the strength of the 'saturation' claim. Open questions include whether these findings generalize to richer stimulus sets, whether the modest GPT-attender benefit reflects genuine attention-like mechanisms in somatosensory pathways, and how multimodal integration (tactile + proprioception + vision) should be modeled.
