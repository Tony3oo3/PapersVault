## Problem

Existing image relighting methods either rely on expensive HDR environment maps and light-stage data for physics-based decomposition, or treat the task as pixel-level image translation that fails to model natural light interaction between foreground and background. Text-based relighting remains largely unexplored, and no single model unifies both image-conditioned and text-conditioned relighting without additional priors. Simply concatenating foreground, background, and noise (as in IC-Light) imposes pixel-aligned light constraints and produces color bleeding and foreground distortion.

## Input / Output

The model accepts a foreground image, a background image, and a text prompt at 512×512 resolution. For image-based relighting the text is fixed to 'blend these two images' and the background is a natural image; for text-based relighting the background is replaced by an all-black image and the text describes the desired scene. The foreground is first segmented with RMBG-1.4 to isolate the subject, then both masked foreground and background are VAE-encoded and concatenated with random noise as UNet input. The output is a single relighted composite image where the subject's lighting and color tone are harmonized with the new environment.

## Method

DreamLight builds on Stable Diffusion v1.5 and introduces two key modules. The Position-Guided Light Adapter (PGLA), derived from IP-Adapter, encodes the background with CLIP, applies a low-frequency enhancement via FFT to suppress texture noise, then uses four sets of learnable light queries (left, right, top, down) with direction-biased masked attention to selectively condense directional light information; these queries are injected into the foreground region of the UNet's middle and up blocks via masked cross-attention so that different spatial regions of the subject respond preferentially to nearby light sources. The Spectral Foreground Fixer (SFF) is a post-processing module that decomposes both the input foreground and the initial relighting output via wavelet transform, then trains a modulator to predict per-pixel scaling (α) and offset (β) coefficients that recombine the high-frequency texture of the original subject with the low-frequency lighting of the relit result, preserving identity while adopting new illumination. Training data is generated through three pipelines: bootstrapped relighting LoRA on 100 seed pairs, 3D Arnold rendering with randomized HDR lights, and IC-Light synthetic pairs filtered by aesthetic score, totaling ~1M pairs.

## Results

On image-based relighting, DreamLight achieves PSNR 22.15, SSIM 0.783, LPIPS 0.158, and CLIP-IS 0.908, outperforming the strongest baseline IH (20.66/0.762/0.177/0.896) by 1.49 dB in PSNR and 0.012 in CLIP-IS. On text-based relighting, DreamLight scores 0.644 CLIP Score, 6.32 Aesthetic Score, and 3.47 IR Score, surpassing IC-Light (0.629/6.04/2.25) by a notable margin especially on IR Score (+1.22). The Spectral Foreground Fixer ablation shows a dramatic PSNR jump from 19.31 (no fixer) to 25.53 on cropped face regions, confirming its critical role in preserving subject identity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DreamLight outperforms all baselines on every reported metric. In image-based relighting (Table 1), it beats the closest competitor IH [19] by 1.49 dB PSNR and 0.012 CLIP-IS, and surpasses IC-Light [26] by 1.88 dB PSNR and 0.019 CLIP-IS; the older harmonization methods INR [21] and PCT [20] trail by 5+ dB PSNR. In text-based relighting (Table 2), DreamLight leads IC-Light [26] by 0.015 CLIP Score and 1.22 IR Score, and outperforms inpainting methods PowerPaint V1/V2 [43] and BrushNet [42] on all three metrics, with the largest gap on IR Score (3.47 vs. 2.50 for PP-V2). Harmonizer [38] is compared only qualitatively in Figure 5(a), where DreamLight shows superior light-source interaction modeling. ControlNet [58] is evaluated as an alternative foreground-fix strategy in the ablation (Table 4) rather than as a full relighting baseline, and DreamLight's SFF substantially outperforms it (PSNR 25.53 vs. 19.69 on face crops).

## Relation to Prior Work

DreamLight builds directly on the diffusion-based relighting paradigm established by IC-Light, which first unified image-based and text-based relighting for natural images, but departs from its two-model design and simple channel concatenation by introducing a Position-Guided Light Adapter (PGLA) that selectively injects direction-specific background lighting into the foreground via masked cross-attention. The PGLA itself is architecturally grounded in IP-Adapter's cross-attention mechanism, extended with a low-frequency spectral filter and direction-biased decay maps to encode spatially-aware light queries. The Spectral Foreground Fixer (SFF) introduces a wavelet-domain post-processing step that is orthogonal to the diffusion pipeline, addressing a known failure mode (foreground texture distortion) that prior harmonization and inpainting methods handle only implicitly.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By unifying image- and text-conditioned relighting in a single model, DreamLight removes the need for HDR environment maps or light-stage data that have constrained prior physics-guided methods, making relighting accessible in practical editing and VR pipelines. The emergent ability to condition on both a background image and a text prompt simultaneously—without explicit training for that joint setting—suggests that unified conditioning in diffusion models can yield compositional control beyond what was designed. Open questions include whether the approach scales beyond 512×512 resolution, whether the four-direction light decomposition generalizes to more complex multi-source lighting, and how the method performs on video or 3D-consistent relighting.
