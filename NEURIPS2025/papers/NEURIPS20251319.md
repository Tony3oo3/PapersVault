## Problem

Existing differentially private (DP) image synthesis methods struggle to produce high-resolution outputs that faithfully capture the structure of private data, particularly for rare or fine-grained cases. DP fine-tuning demands substantial compute and engineering expertise, while prior API-based approaches like Private Evolution operate directly in image embedding space and cannot leverage the semantic richness of language. The paper asks whether shifting the DP synthesis burden from the image domain to the text domain can yield substantially higher-quality synthetic images using only off-the-shelf model inference.

## Input / Output

The method takes a private image dataset (e.g., 256×256 images from LSUN Bedroom, MM-CelebA-HQ, Wave-ui-25k, European Art, Cat, or Sprite Fright) and a target privacy budget (ε, δ), and produces a set of DP synthetic images at the same resolution along with the intermediate DP synthetic text captions. No model training is required at any stage; the pipeline relies exclusively on inference APIs of a captioning model (GPT-4o-mini or Qwen-VL-Max), an LLM (Meta-Llama-3-8B-Instruct), and a text-to-image diffusion model (SDXL-Turbo or SDXL-base-1.0). The number of synthetic samples and the number of evolution iterations (G) are user-configurable hyperparameters.

## Method

SPTI operates in three stages: (1) each private image is captioned into a concise text description via an off-the-shelf image-to-text model; (2) a modified Augmented Private Evolution (Aug-PE) algorithm iteratively evolves the caption pool under DP constraints, where at each generation candidate texts are scored by a novel Image Voting mechanism that generates images from each candidate, embeds both the generated and private images with Inception-v3, and assigns votes via reverse nearest-neighbor in image embedding space before adding Gaussian noise to the resulting histogram; (3) the final DP synthetic captions are fed to a text-to-image diffusion model to produce the output images. The key insight is that voting in image representation space rather than text space better aligns the synthetic distribution with the private data, since text-to-image generation is stochastic and accurate captions do not guarantee visually faithful reconstructions. Privacy is guaranteed solely by the Gaussian mechanism applied to the vote histogram at each iteration, with all downstream steps (text mutation, image generation) incurring zero additional privacy cost via the post-processing property of DP.

## Results

On LSUN Bedroom, SPTI achieves FID = 26.39 at ε=1, substantially outperforming Private Evolution (40.36) and DP fine-tuning (31.76). On MM-CelebA-HQ, SPTI reaches FID = 33.41 at ε=1, compared to 50.45 for PE and 57.01 for DP fine-tuning. Across all five main datasets, SPTI attains the best FID at ε=1 on four out of five (losing only on the low-diversity Cat dataset to PE), and consistently outperforms DP fine-tuning on every dataset. The method requires no model training—only inference with off-the-shelf captioning, LLM, and diffusion models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SPTI is compared against two baselines: Private Evolution (PE) [Lin et al., ICLR 2024], which operates directly in image embedding space, and DP fine-tuning (DP-LDM) [Liu et al., 2023], which fine-tunes a latent diffusion model via DP-SGD. Against PE, SPTI achieves substantially lower FID on LSUN Bedroom (26.39 vs. 40.36 at ε=1), European Art (57.64 vs. 76.50), Wave-ui-25k (35.18 vs. 50.45), and Sprite Fright (157.31 vs. 197.13), but underperforms on the Cat dataset (106.15 vs. 64.62), which the authors attribute to the low intra-class diversity of that dataset. Against DP fine-tuning, SPTI wins on all five datasets at ε=1, with the largest margin on Wave-ui-25k (35.18 vs. 62.08) and a consistent 4–6 FID point advantage on LSUN Bedroom and European Art. On MM-CelebA-HQ (Table 2), SPTI with a pretrained LDM backbone achieves FID = 33.41 at ε=1, beating both PE (50.45) and DP fine-tuning (57.01) by wide margins. The ablation in Table 3 further confirms that the image-voting mechanism (as opposed to text-only voting) is the key driver of these gains, particularly on European Art (57.64 vs. 74.91) and Wave-ui-25k (35.18 vs. 95.77 at ε=1).

## Relation to Prior Work

SPTI builds directly on the Private Evolution (PE) framework of Lin et al. (ICLR 2024) for inference-only DP image synthesis and the Augmented Private Evolution (Aug-PE) of Xie et al. (ICML 2024) for DP synthetic text, but departs from both by relocating the DP burden from the image domain to the text domain. The key conceptual departure is treating text as a lossy yet lower-dimensional intermediary: private images are captioned, evolved under DP in text space with a novel cross-modal image-voting mechanism, and then rendered back to images. This positions SPTI between the training-heavy DP fine-tuning line (DP-SGD on diffusion models) and the prior inference-only PE line, offering a middle path that requires no gradient updates while leveraging the semantic richness of language models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that SPTI works entirely through inference APIs, making it compatible with proprietary, non-fine-tunable foundation models—a constraint that precludes all DP-SGD-based approaches. The substantial FID gaps (e.g., 26.71 vs. 40.36 on LSUN Bedroom at ε=1) suggest that the text intermediary, despite being lossy, actually improves distributional fidelity under strong privacy, which is counterintuitive. Open questions include whether the approach extends to video or audio modalities, how to reduce the ~3× compute overhead relative to PE, and whether the observed degradation on low-diversity datasets (Cat, bird) can be mitigated without sacrificing the diversity advantage that drives SPTI's gains on heterogeneous data.
