## Problem

Despite rapid advances in single-image 3D generation, it remains unclear which monocular image cues (shading, texture, silhouette, perspective, edges, local continuity) these models actually exploit to infer 3D shape. Current methods operate as black boxes trained end-to-end on 3D supervision, leaving their reliance on classical vision cues versus high-level semantics unexplored, which hinders both scientific understanding and practical diagnosis of failure modes.

## Input / Output

The framework takes a single 512×512 RGB image (optionally with an object mask) as input and evaluates the resulting textured 3D mesh produced by each method. Evaluation is multi-faceted: 2D appearance (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS) and 3D geometry (Chamfer Distance, F-score) are measured both globally across 16 rendered views and on the visible surface from the input viewpoint, plus a binary symmetry F1 score. The perturbation protocol modifies specific cues in the input image (e.g., re-rendering with altered shading/texture in Blender, dilating the alpha mask, switching to orthographic projection, shuffling pixels within local grids) while preserving all other cues, then quantifies the resulting CD increase as a measure of cue importance.

## Method

Cue3D is a model-agnostic perturbation framework that systematically disables or modifies individual image cues and measures the resulting degradation in 3D output quality across seven state-of-the-art methods spanning three paradigms (regression-based: OpenLRM, SF3D; multi-view: CRM, LGM, InstantMesh; native 3D generative: Trellis, Hunyuan3D-2). Perturbations include reference-based style transfer to preserve semantics while disrupting geometric cues, Blender re-rendering to independently control shading and texture, silhouette dilation and occlusion, Canny edge extraction with optional Gaussian softening, perspective-to-orthographic projection switching, and local pixel shuffling at multiple grid sizes. A shape-meaningfulness probe uses the Zeroverse dataset of procedurally generated primitive assemblies and 3D CutMix variants on GSO. Key findings: shape meaningfulness (not texture) dictates generalization; shading is more important than texture for leading methods; models over-rely on provided silhouettes; and perspective, edges, and local continuity have diverse, paradigm-dependent effects.

## Results

On Toys4K, native 3D generative methods dominate: Trellis achieves the best overall 3D geometry (CD×1000 = 37.78) and 2D appearance (PSNR = 20.53, LPIPS = 0.136), while Hunyuan3D-2 is a close second (CD×1000 = 38.65, PSNR = 20.79). The best multi-view method, InstantMesh, trails by ~12 CD points (49.84), and the best regression-based method, SF3D, trails by ~15 CD points (52.78). On GSO, the same ordering holds with Trellis at CD×1000 = 39.64 and Hunyuan3D-2 at 41.82, versus LGM (worst) at 83.01. On Zeroverse (meaningless shapes), all methods degrade substantially, with even the best (Hunyuan3D-2) jumping from CD×1000 ≈ 39 to 78, confirming that shape meaningfulness is a dominant cue for generalization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Cue3D benchmarks seven methods spanning three paradigms. Native 3D generative models (Trellis, Hunyuan3D-2) clearly outperform all others on 3D geometry: Trellis achieves CD×1000 = 37.78 on Toys4K, beating the best multi-view method InstantMesh (49.84) by ~12 points and the best regression method SF3D (52.78) by ~15 points. On 2D appearance, the gap narrows—Trellis and Hunyuan3D-2 lead in LPIPS (0.136/0.138 vs. 0.173 for InstantMesh) but PSNR differences are marginal (<1.5 dB). Among regression-based methods, SF3D (CD×1000 = 52.78) outperforms OpenLRM (74.79) by a wide margin; among multi-view methods, InstantMesh (49.84) beats CRM (61.88) and LGM (77.01). On symmetry detection, Hunyuan3D-2 (F1 = 0.913) and Trellis (0.904) far exceed all other methods (≤0.706), while on Zeroverse all methods collapse, with Hunyuan3D-2 showing the smallest relative degradation (CD×1000: 78.09 vs. 38.65 on Toys4K).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the meaningful perturbation framework (Fong & Vedaldi, 2017) and the shape-vs-texture interpretability analysis of Geirhos et al. (2018) from 2D image classification to single-image 3D generation. It draws on decades of classical computer vision literature on monocular cues—shape-from-shading, texture gradients, silhouettes, perspective, and symmetry priors—and repurposes these as controlled perturbation targets for modern deep 3D generators. The key departure is the unified, model-agnostic benchmark spanning three distinct paradigms (regression-based, multi-view, native 3D generative), which no prior work has attempted for cue analysis in 3D generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Cue3D provides the first systematic diagnostic of what visual information state-of-the-art 3D generators actually exploit, revealing that shape meaningfulness (not texture) governs generalization and that shading dominates texture as a geometric cue. These findings have direct practical implications: the line-art-to-3D case study demonstrates how cue-aware augmentation can improve off-the-shelf pipelines, and the identified over-reliance on silhouettes points to a concrete robustness weakness. Limitations include coverage of only seven methods on clean, object-centric datasets, and the analysis probes cues largely in isolation, leaving the interplay between multiple simultaneous cues as an important open question.
