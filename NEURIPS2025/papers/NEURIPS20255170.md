## Problem

Integrating unpaired single-cell multi-omics data into a shared latent space remains challenging because existing methods either require paired or partially paired measurements across modalities, or rely on computing global pairwise coupling matrices (e.g., Gromov-Wasserstein optimal transport) that restrict integration to two modalities and fail at scale. No prior framework simultaneously handles completely unpaired data, scales to tens of thousands of cells, and naturally extends beyond two omics layers.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255170/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes sparse count matrices from scRNA-seq, scATAC-seq (peak signals aggregated to gene-level activity scores via EpiScanpy), and CITE-seq protein measurements, all mapped to a common gene-level feature space, along with batch covariates. A binary mask indicates which features are actually measured per modality, enabling the model to handle the severe feature asymmetry (e.g., ~134 proteins vs. ~27,000 genes) without zero-imputation. The output is a unified latent embedding decomposed into a modality-shared component z_u and a modality-specific component z_s per sample, which can be further used for downstream tasks such as spatial location imputation via optimal transport against spatially-resolved omics.

## Method

scMRDR employs a single unified β-VAE encoder-decoder (rather than per-modality encoders) that disentangles each cell's latent code into a shared subspace z_u and a modality-specific subspace z_s, with a ZINB generative process modeling the over-dispersed, zero-inflated count data. Three regularization terms are jointly optimized: (1) an m-class adversarial discriminator trained to classify which omics produced z_u, with the encoder trained to fool it, thereby aligning cross-modal distributions in the shared subspace; (2) an isometric loss that minimizes the discrepancy between pairwise Euclidean distances in the full latent space z=(z_u,z_s) and in z_u alone, preserving intra-modality biological structure within the shared component; and (3) a masked reconstruction loss that scales by the proportion of available features per modality to prevent gradients from unmeasured features. Training alternates discriminator updates and VAE updates per mini-batch, and the single-encoder design means adding a new omics layer requires no architectural change, making the framework inherently scalable to large datasets and arbitrary numbers of modalities.

## Results

On the small-scale two-omics benchmark (human kidney, ~44K cells), scMRDR achieves the highest aggregate scIB score of 0.66, outperforming the next-best method GLUE (0.64) and substantially exceeding scVI (0.56), Seurat v5 (0.54), and manifold-alignment methods like JAMIE (0.48) and UnionCom (0.42). On the large-scale two-omics dataset (mouse motor cortex, ~125K cells), scMRDR scores 0.60 versus Seurat's 0.59, while optimal-transport-based methods (JAMIE, UnionCom, Pamona) fail to run entirely due to memory constraints. On the triple-omics benchmark (human bone marrow, ~59K cells across scRNA/scATAC/CITE-seq), scMRDR achieves 0.58, beating GLUE (0.55), scVI (0.49), and Harmony (0.33), with particularly strong modality integration (0.61 vs. 0.58 for GLUE).

## Baselines & Comparisons

scMRDR outperforms all 9 baselines on the primary small-scale two-omics benchmark, with the closest competitor being GLUE (0.64 vs. 0.66 total); scMRDR's advantage is driven by superior modality integration (0.70 vs. 0.74 for GLUE in iLISI, but 0.96 vs. 0.94 in kBET) and comparable bio-conservation. On the large-scale dataset (~125K cells), scMRDR (0.60) narrowly beats Seurat v5 (0.59), while GLUE's default LSI preprocessing fails and its PCA fallback drops to 0.45; optimal-transport methods (JAMIE, UnionCom, Pamona) crash due to memory. On triple-omics integration, scMRDR (0.58) leads GLUE (0.55) by a wider margin, with scVI (0.49) and Harmony (0.33) trailing significantly—Harmony's modality integration collapses to 0.18. Notably, Seurat v5 achieves the highest bio-conservation score (0.75) on the small-scale set but fails at modality integration (0.50), while SIMBA excels at batch correction (0.73) but nearly loses all biological structure (NMI 0.01). The ablation confirms that removing the isometric loss drops bio-conservation from 0.74 to 0.60, and removing the adversarial loss drops modality integration from 0.70 to 0.60.

## Relation to Prior Work

scMRDR builds on the β-VAE framework for disentangled representations (Higgins et al., 2017), the ZINB likelihood from scVI for single-cell count data (Lopez et al., 2018), adversarial training (Goodfellow et al., 2014), and Isomap-style distance preservation (Tenenbaum et al., 2000). It departs from two dominant lines of multi-omics integration: joint dimension reduction methods (MOFA, Seurat, MultiVI, JAMIE) that require paired or partially paired measurements, and manifold alignment methods (SCOT, Pamona, UnionCom) that compute a global pairwise coupling matrix. The key conceptual departure is that a single unified encoder-decoder with disentangled shared/specific latent subspaces, combined with adversarial alignment and isometric regularization, can achieve integration without either paired samples or explicit cross-modal correspondences, naturally extending to more than two omics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in scalability: optimal-transport and manifold-alignment baselines simply fail to run on datasets with ~120k cells, while scMRDR maintains stable performance, and the unified architecture extends to triple-omics integration where methods like Seurat v5 are architecturally unsuitable. The masked reconstruction loss addresses a real bottleneck in multi-omics (e.g., CITE-seq covers ~134 proteins vs. tens of thousands of genes), and the spatial location imputation application demonstrates downstream biological utility. Open questions include the sensitivity to hyperparameters (β, λ, γ), the difficulty of adversarial min-max training, and whether gene-level feature mapping (aggregating ATAC peaks to activity scores) loses regulatory information that peak-level integration could capture.
