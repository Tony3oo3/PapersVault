## Problem

Existing Unlearnable Example (UE) methods produce perturbations tightly coupled to specific training distributions, causing severe degradation in unlearnability when applied to unseen domains, tasks, resolutions, or architectures. No prior work has systematically evaluated or addressed this transferability gap across the full range of practical deployment scenarios.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250589/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The generator takes a clean RGB image (32×32 for CIFAR/SVHN, 224×224 for PACS) and outputs a per-pixel perturbation δ constrained to L∞ norm ≤ 8/255, which is added to produce the unlearnable example. Perturbations are crafted in a class-wise manner: sample-level perturbations are generated then averaged per class. The generator is trained exclusively on a source dataset and then applied zero-shot to target datasets that may differ in style, class labels, resolution, or target architecture, requiring only a single forward pass per image at inference time.

## Method

VTG is a lightweight encoder-decoder perturbation generator (0.09M parameters) trained via an alternating min-min scheme with two key mechanisms. First, Adversarial Domain Augmentation (ADA) employs a domain composer that synthesizes out-of-distribution images by maximizing the Wasserstein distance (computed via Sinkhorn) between source and generated feature distributions, forcing the surrogate classifier to become more generalizable so that the generator must learn distribution-robust shortcuts. Second, Perturbation-Label Coupling (PLC) extracts perturbation embeddings via the surrogate model's feature extractor and label embeddings via CLIP's frozen text encoder, then aligns them through a contrastive loss, directly binding perturbations to class labels independent of image semantics. The generator is optimized with a combined objective of cross-entropy classification loss, the PLC contrastive loss, and a soft hinge loss on the L∞ norm, while the surrogate classifier is frozen during the perturbation generation phase.

## Results

Under the Intra-Domain scenario (CIFAR-10 source, ResNet-18 surrogate), VTG reduces test accuracy to 9.99% on CIFAR-10, 0.99% on CIFAR-100, and 9.65% on SVHN—near random-guessing levels (10% and 10% for 10/100-class tasks). In the Cross-Domain scenario on PACS, VTG achieves 16.59% average accuracy (ResNet) and 18.31% (ViT), outperforming the next-best method 14A (30.84%) by 14.25%. Under Cross-Space (high-to-low resolution, e.g., PACS Art → CIFAR-10), VTG reaches 10.88% accuracy versus 40.13% for 14A, a ~30% gap. VTG also requires only 0.4 ms/image inference with 0.09M parameters, compared to 3.7 ms and 7.79M for GUE and 6.3 ms and 121.13M for 14A.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VTG outperforms all 13 baselines across every evaluated scenario. Against the strongest gradient-based methods, VTG (ResNet) beats EMN [5] by 0.17% on CIFAR-10 and by 20.81% on CIFAR-100 (0.99% vs. 21.80%), and beats GUE [8] by 2.88% on CIFAR-10 and 2.88% on CIFAR-100. The transfer-oriented generator 14A [12] degrades severely in cross-task settings (41.34% on CIFAR-10, 83.87% on SVHN), while VTG maintains near-random accuracy (9.99%, 9.65%). Data-free LSP [11] achieves 7.77% on SVHN (its best cross-task result) but 13.54% on CIFAR-10, whereas VTG achieves 9.65% and 9.99% respectively. REM [6] and TUE [7] are nearly ineffective in cross-task transfer (REM: 95.98% on SVHN; TUE: 12.93%), confirming their distribution-specific limitations. Under defense strategies (Table 7), VTG retains 10.83% accuracy under Adversarial Training and 10.57% under D-VAE, while EMN jumps to 84.80% and 91.42% respectively, and OPS [54] collapses to 11.08% under AT but recovers to 88.95% under D-VAE. In the Cross-Architecture setting (Table 6), VTG achieves 8.92–10.53% across VGG16, ResNet-50, DenseNet-121, and ViT targets, outperforming GUE [8] (12.97–16.77%) and SG [10] (10.64–17.35%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the unlearnable examples (UE) line of work initiated by EMN [5] and subsequent generator-based approaches (GUE [8], 14A [12]) by reframing the core challenge as multi-dimensional transferability rather than single-axis generalization. It repurposes Adversarial Domain Augmentation from the domain generalization literature (Zhou et al. 2020, Wang et al. 2021) to diversify the surrogate classifier's training distribution, and introduces a CLIP-based contrastive alignment (Perturbation-Label Coupling) that borrows the image-text pretraining paradigm to decouple perturbations from image semantics. Unlike 14A, which also uses a generator but relies on multi-modal alignment for a single transfer axis, VTG explicitly targets five progressive transfer scenarios simultaneously, positioning itself as the first comprehensive transferable UE framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The five-scenario evaluation framework (Intra-Domain, Cross-Domain, Cross-Task, Cross-Space, Cross-Architecture) provides the field with a standardized benchmark for assessing UE generalization, filling a gap where prior methods were only tested on one or two axes. The method's near-random-guessing accuracy across all scenarios with remarkably low inference cost (0.4 ms/image, 0.09M parameters) makes it practically deployable for real-world data protection. However, the AN-SDA defense still partially recovers semantics (28.27% vs. 9.99% baseline), and the SVHN→CIFAR-100 cross-task setting under Mixup degrades to 34.13%, revealing that distribution-agnostic unlearnability is not yet fully robust. Open questions include whether the approach extends to non-image modalities, video, or larger-scale datasets, and whether the generator's test-time inference requirement can be eliminated for fully precomputed protection.
