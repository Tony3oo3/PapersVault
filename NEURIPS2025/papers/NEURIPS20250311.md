## Problem

Classical generalization bounds based on hypothesis class complexity cannot explain why overparameterized neural networks trained by gradient-based methods generalize well. Existing algorithm-dependent bounds (stability, PAC-Bayesian, information-theoretic) are either data-independent, require noise injection that diverges from standard SGD, or grow linearly/exponentially with training time. The prior LPK-based bound of Chen et al. (2023) involved an intractable supremum over an infinite family of kernels and a loose O(n²) cross-term, limiting its practical utility.

## Input / Output

The method consumes the training set S, the continuous parameter trajectory w(t) produced by gradient flow (or stochastic gradient flow) over time [0,T], and the per-sample loss function ℓ(w,z). It outputs a single scalar bound Γ computed entirely from the training set, expressible as (2/n)·√(L_S(w₀)−L_S(w_T))·√(Σᵢ∫₀ᵀ‖∇_w ℓ(w_t,zᵢ)‖²dt), which requires only the loss decrease and per-sample gradient norms along the trajectory. Unlike the prior LPK bound, no separate evaluation dataset is needed, and the bound is directly comparable to the true generalization gap L_μ(w_T)−L_S(w_T).

## Method

The paper proves O(1/n) uniform argument stability of gradient flow for convex, strongly-convex, and non-convex losses, then uses this stability to show that LPKs trained on different datasets concentrate tightly (via McDiarmid's inequality). This concentration dramatically shrinks the effective LPK function class, allowing the Rademacher complexity to be bounded by a decoupling argument that eliminates the intractable supremum and the O(n²) cross-term present in the prior bound. The resulting generalization gap is controlled by Γ, which structurally mirrors the classical kernel-method Rademacher bound (RKHS norm × kernel trace) but replaces the fixed kernel with the data-dependent LPK that adapts to both the data and the optimization trajectory. For stochastic gradient flow, the bound decomposes into a sum over time intervals, each weighted by the batch-level LPK. The bound recovers known NTK-regime and kernel ridge regression results as special cases and, in the feature-learning regime, yields a vanishing Γ that outperforms the fixed-kernel bound.

## Results

The paper's main quantitative result is that the derived generalization bound term Γ (Theorem 5.2) correlates tightly with the true generalization gap L_μ(w_T) − L_S(w_T) across multiple architectures and datasets. In Experiment (I) on CIFAR-10 cat/dog with a two-layer NN, the norm-based bound from Bartlett et al. [11] yields 12557.3, while the proposed Γ is orders of magnitude smaller and tracks the actual gap closely. On full CIFAR-10 with ResNet-18 (SGD), Γ captures the overfitting and feature-unlearning phases observed after long training (T = O(n)). With label noise, Γ increases monotonically with the noise fraction, explaining the random-label generalization phenomenon.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary empirical comparison is against the spectrally-normalized margin bound of Bartlett et al. [11]: on CIFAR-10 cat/dog with a two-layer NN (400 hidden nodes, Softplus), the Bartlett et al. bound evaluates to 12557.3, while the proposed Γ is several orders of magnitude smaller and closely tracks the true generalization gap (Fig. 2). The paper also improves upon the earlier LPK-based bound of Chen et al. [20], which involved an intractable supremum over an infinite family of kernels and an O(n²) cross-term; the new bound eliminates both issues by leveraging stability to restrict the function class. In the NTK regime (Corollary 6.1), the bound recovers the condition-number-dependent rate and is always upper-bounded as T→∞, matching known kernel regression results. In the feature-learning setting (Corollary 6.3), Γ vanishes as n,d→∞ under the sample complexity n = Õ(d^{s/2+2}), outperforming fixed-kernel methods that require n = Õ(d^p) where p ≥ s is the polynomial degree of the link function.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Loss Path Kernel (LPK) framework of Chen et al. [20], which showed that gradient flow loss dynamics are equivalent to a kernel machine with a data-dependent kernel. The key departure is using uniform stability of gradient flow to prove LPK concentration, thereby shrinking the function class from an intractable supremum over infinitely many LPKs to a computable bound that parallels classical Rademacher complexity bounds for kernel methods (Bartlett & Mendelson [10]). Unlike PAC-Bayesian or information-theoretic approaches that require noise injection (SGLD), this yields a high-probability uniform convergence bound directly from the deterministic gradient flow dynamics, and unlike NTK-based analyses, the LPK captures the full training trajectory rather than freezing the kernel at initialization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The bound Γ is computable from the training trajectory and empirically correlates tightly with the true generalization gap across architectures (ResNet-18, two-layer NNs) and datasets (CIFAR-10, MNIST), including capturing overfitting behavior at long training times. The feature learning case study cleanly shows that Γ vanishes in the single-index model regime while fixed-kernel bounds remain Θ(1), providing a theoretical explanation for why neural networks outperform kernel methods. However, the analysis is restricted to gradient flow (infinitesimal step size), so extending to practical GD/SGD with finite learning rates remains open, and the function class used in the Rademacher complexity step is larger than necessary, leaving room for tighter bounds.
