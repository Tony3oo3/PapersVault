## Problem

Integrating large pre-trained models into federated learning via partial fine-tuning (freezing the backbone, training only a lightweight task head) introduces new security vulnerabilities that existing Byzantine-robust defenses cannot address. Because the shared frozen representation space is stable, adversaries can embed subtle semantic manipulations or backdoors that evade gradient-based detection methods like Trimmed-Mean, Multi-Krum, and FLShield. The paper asks how to enable reliable, adaptive detection of malicious clients specifically in partially fine-tuned FL systems.

## Input / Output

Each client processes RGB images (32×32 CIFAR-100, 224×224 Food-101, 64×64 Tiny ImageNet) through a frozen CLIP ViT-B/32 backbone, then through a trainable 256-dim statistical projection and a GLM task head. The server receives per-client class-wise feature centroids and composed model updates (ψ_i = h_i ∘ s_i), and produces three outputs: class predictions, a scalar deviance score Δ_i per client, and a binary malicious/benign label determined by multi-step majority voting. Notably, detection operates on prediction-level semantics rather than raw gradients, making it agnostic to the specific frozen backbone.

## Method

FEDRACE combines two components: HStat-Net, a three-layer hierarchy (frozen extractor φ → statistical net s → GLM task head h) trained in two decoupled stages (cross-entropy for h, then triplet loss for s) to produce compact, linearly separable 256-dim representations; and DevGuard, a server-side detector that aggregates client class-wise centroids via element-wise median, passes each global representation through every client's Task Net, and computes GLM deviance residuals (−2 log ŷ_r^c) to score semantic consistency. An adaptive threshold is selected by minimizing the empirical TMR bound from Theorem 1 (TMR ≤ 4σ²/(μ_M − μ_B)²), and a K-step randomized majority voting procedure over client subsets reduces false positives. The key insight is that constraining the task head to a GLM over linearly separable features makes client behavior interpretable through classical statistical deviance, enabling principled detection without relying on gradient geometry.

## Results

FEDRACE achieves a true positive rate of up to 99.3% with a false positive rate below 1.2% across all three datasets and five attack types. On CIFAR-100, it attains 76.99% accuracy under IPMA (untargeted) and reduces backdoor accuracy to 0.36% under DBA, while maintaining 77.21% clean accuracy. Under targeted attacks, FEDRACE drives ASR below 0.4% on all datasets, compared to 11.92% (FedRoLA) and 2.27% (FLShield) on CIFAR-100 under TLFA. Detection FPR stays below 0.1 in most scenarios, with the lowest being 0.012 under TLFA on CIFAR-100.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FEDRACE outperforms all five baselines on targeted-attack metrics (ASR and BA) by a wide margin. Against FedRoLA, which suffers the most under targeted attacks (ASR 11.92%, BA 39.28% on CIFAR-100 TLFA/ECBA), FEDRACE reduces these to 0.07% and 0.06% respectively. FLAIR achieves the second-best ASR (0.61% on CIFAR-100 TLFA) but at the cost of a high FPR of 0.357, whereas FEDRACE achieves 0.07% ASR with FPR of only 0.012. FLShield is competitive on clean accuracy (76.86% vs. 76.69% under Min-Max on CIFAR-100) but allows 2.27% ASR and 1.67% BA under TLFA/ECBA, roughly 30× higher than FEDRACE. Trimmed-mean and Multi-Krum, the classical aggregation-based defenses, provide the weakest protection against backdoor attacks, with BA values of 10–23% on CIFAR-100, while FEDRACE keeps BA below 0.4% across all targeted scenarios.

## Relation to Prior Work

FEDRACE sits at the intersection of two active research threads: Byzantine-robust federated learning (Trimmed-Mean, Multi-Krum, FLShield, FLAIR, FedRoLA) and efficient fine-tuning of large pre-trained models in distributed settings. It departs from prior defenses by targeting the specific threat surface created when only a lightweight task head is trained atop a frozen backbone—a deployment pattern that gradient-statistics-based methods were not designed to handle. The HStat-Net component repurposes triplet-loss metric learning to produce linearly separable representations, while DevGuard transplants GLM deviance analysis (a classical statistical tool) into the FL client-evaluation loop, a combination not previously explored in the robust-FL literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As partial fine-tuning of pre-trained models becomes the default strategy for practical FL (Table 1 shows 68% training-time reduction), the security gap it creates is increasingly urgent; FEDRACE offers a principled, theoretically grounded defense for exactly this setting. The framework's limitation to image classification with a single fully-connected task head constrains generality—extending to deeper heads, text, or multimodal tasks would require rethinking the GLM assumption. Open questions include whether the deviance-based detection remains effective when the frozen backbone itself is partially updated, and how the method scales to thousands of clients with highly heterogeneous data distributions beyond Dirichlet partitioning.
