## Problem

State-of-the-art biomolecular structure predictors like AlphaFold3 still produce physically implausible structures (incorrect chirality, unrealistic torsion angles), are computationally expensive due to diffusion rollouts limiting large-scale virtual screening, and lack standardized benchmarks for ligand-induced conformational changes and physical validity. The paper addresses these gaps by delivering a faster, more physically grounded generative model with new evaluation frameworks tailored to drug discovery workflows.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250001/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

NP3 consumes protein sequences, small-molecule chemical graphs, paired MSAs (via jackhmmer/hhblits), and language model embeddings (3B-parameter ESM-2 for proteins, 650M-parameter RiNalMo for RNA) as conditioning signals, requiring no experimental structure or pocket specification. It outputs 3D coordinates for all heavy atoms in the complex (proteins, nucleic acids, ligands, ions, PTMs) along with per-atom pLDDT confidence scores and pairwise pDockQ interface confidence scores, enabling both structure-based drug design and reliability assessment of each prediction.

## Method

NP3 is a conditional flow-matching generative model built on continuous normalizing flows, trained without simulation rollouts. Three key innovations distinguish it from prior diffusion-based approaches: (1) a physics-inspired globular polymer prior generated by short Langevin dynamics relaxation with harmonic connectivity, entity, and residue-scale constraints, which preserves chemical topology and breaks inter-chain symmetry; (2) an optimal-transport-inspired symmetry correction module that permutes equivalent entities and local atom indices to straighten conditional flow trajectories, reducing required integrator steps; and (3) predicting denoised coordinates (rather than raw vector fields) with Kabsch rigid alignment between steps for trajectory continuity. The architecture separates an AF3-style PairFormer encoder (350M params) for anchor-level conditioning from a DiT-based flow decoder (350M params) using sliding-window attention for atom-level generation, with a custom Flash-TriangularAttention Triton kernel reducing peak memory by 5× and enabling 15× faster inference (~30 s on a single L40S GPU versus ~6 A100-minutes for AF3).

## Results

On PoseBusters V2, NP3 achieves a 78.4% combined success rate (RMSD < 2 Å AND physically valid), outperforming AF3 (73.1%), with 98.8% ligand stereochemistry accuracy and ~30 s inference on a single L40S GPU (15× faster than AF3). On the new NPBench benchmark (1,143 low-homology PDB structures post-2023), NP3 achieves 60.7% RMSD < 2 Å for noncovalent ligands, 69.6% for covalent ligands, 82.6% for modified residues, 52.7% DockQ > 0.23 for protein–protein interfaces, and 56.2% for protein–DNA. On ConfBench, NP3 outperforms AF2-Multimer 2.3 in ligand-induced conformational prediction (51.9% vs 29.6% correctness rate across 54 unique linkages).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against AF3, NP3 wins on the combined PoseBusters metric (78.4% vs 73.1%, driven by superior physical validity) and achieves 98.8% stereochemistry accuracy, while essentially tying on raw RMSD < 2 Å (80.2% vs 80.5%); NP3 is also ~15× faster in inference (~30 s on one L40S vs ~6 A100-minutes). On CASP15 RNA, NP3 slightly trails AF3 (46.5% vs 47.3% LDDT) but notably matches AF3 using RNA language models instead of MSAs. Against AF2-Multimer 2.3 on NPBench, NP3 substantially outperforms on protein–peptide interfaces (85.1% vs 76.6% DockQ > 0.23) and ties on protein–protein (52.7% vs 52.3%), while marginally underperforming on monomer LDDT (86.2% vs 87.1%). On ConfBench conformational prediction, NP3 more than doubles AF2-M's correctness rate (51.9% vs 29.6%). Against traditional and AI-based docking methods (Vina 59.7%, GOLD 58.1%, Uni-Mol 21.8%, DiffDock 38.0%, TankBind 15.9%, EquiBind 1.9%), NP3 achieves comparable or superior accuracy without requiring holo structures or explicit pocket definitions. The only method NP3 clearly loses to is Alchemy_RNA2 on CASP15 RNA (46.5% vs 54.5% LDDT), though that method requires curated human input.

## Relation to Prior Work

NP3 is the third generation of the NeuralPLexer line, transitioning from the diffusion-based equivariant graph decoder of NP2 to a flow-matching framework with a diffusion-transformer (DiT) decoder. Architecturally it shares the MSA + PairFormer encoder with AlphaFold3 but departs in several key ways: a physics-inspired globular polymer prior (via short Langevin dynamics) replaces the Gaussian prior, optimal-transport symmetry correction straightens flow trajectories to reduce integrator steps, and sliding-window attention replaces AF3's ad-hoc block operations. The paper also introduces Flash-TriangularAttention, a custom Triton kernel that eliminates explicit bias broadcasting in triangular attention, enabling 5× lower peak memory and ~38% faster inference at 1024 residues.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 15× inference speedup over AF3 (≈30 s on a single L40S vs. ≈6 A100-minutes) is practically significant for virtual screening and iterative ligand design, while the improved PB-valid rate (78.4% vs. 73.1% combined success on PoseBusters) addresses a critical failure mode—unphysical hallucinations—that undermines trust in predicted structures for drug discovery. The introduction of NPBench (1,143 low-homology interfaces) and ConfBench (systematic apo→holo conformational evaluation) fills gaps in existing benchmarking, though the latter's absolute correctness rates (~52–70%) indicate conformational prediction remains far from solved. Key open questions include whether the compute-optimal scaling frontier (encoder:decoder FLOP ratio ≈ 10) holds at larger scales, whether LM-based RNA conditioning can match MSA-based approaches as sequence databases grow, and how performance generalizes to antibody-antigen complexes, which were not evaluated.
