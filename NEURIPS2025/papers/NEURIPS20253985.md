## Problem

While Group Convolutional Neural Networks (GCNNs) empirically outperform standard DNNs by incorporating task-specific symmetries, rigorous generalization guarantees for GCNNs of arbitrary depth were largely absent. Existing theoretical analyses either compared equivariant networks to non-equivariant ones without accounting for the specific GCNN architecture, or were restricted to two-layer networks. This paper fills that gap by deriving VC dimension bounds that explicitly depend on the number of layers, the number of weights, and the group resolution.

## Input / Output

The GCNN takes as input a function f: G^r → R^{m_0} defined on a discretized group of cardinality r (e.g., a 28×28 MNIST image viewed as a function on the translation group T^784), and produces a scalar output via global average pooling over all group elements, making the network invariant rather than equivariant. The theoretical output consists of upper and lower bounds on the VC dimension of the GCNN class, expressed in terms of total parameters W, depth L, and group resolution r. The bounds take the form c(VC(F_{W,L}) + W·log₂(r)) ≤ VC(H_{W,L,r}) ≤ C(VC(F_{W,L}) + LW·log₂(r)), where VC(F_{W,L}) is the VC dimension of DNNs with the same parameter budget.

## Method

The upper bound adapts the region-counting argument from Bartlett et al. (2019) for DNNs to the GCNN setting, tracking how ReLU activations partition the parameter space into regions where network outputs are piecewise polynomials, with the group resolution r entering through the number of group elements evaluated per layer. The lower bound is the more novel contribution, established through two complementary constructions: (1) showing that GCNNs with identity-element kernels can simulate DNNs layer-by-layer, yielding a lower bound matching VC(F_{W,L}), and (2) constructing indicator-based GCNNs that shatter ⌊log₂(r)⌋ input functions per 4 parameters, yielding a W·log₂(r) lower bound from the group structure alone. The key insight is that GCNNs are strictly richer than DNNs with the same number of parameters, with the capacity gap growing logarithmically in the group size r. The results extend to Leaky ReLU and, via positive homogeneity of ReLU, to the fat-shattering dimension, and the analysis is agnostic to the specific group structure, applying to both continuous and compact Lie groups.

## Results

The main result (Theorem 1 and 2) establishes that for GCNNs with W weights, depth L, and group size r (under L < W^0.99), the VC dimension satisfies c(VC(F_{W,L}) + W·log₂(r)) ≤ VC(H_{W,L,r}) ≤ C(VC(F_{W,L}) + LW·log₂(r)), with explicit constants c = 1/7500 and C = 500 when W > L² and W > 125. The gap between upper and lower bounds is at most a factor of L, which is negligible in practical GCNN architectures (e.g., 7 layers for rotated MNIST, 14 for CIFAR-10). A key finding is that GCNNs are strictly richer than DNNs with the same number of parameters, with the capacity gap growing logarithmically in the group size r.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper provides two complementary theoretical comparisons between GCNNs and DNNs. First, when both architectures have the same total number of parameters W, GCNNs have a strictly larger VC dimension than DNNs, with the excess scaling as W·log₂(r) (lower bound) to LW·log₂(r) (upper bound), meaning GCNNs are a richer function class. Second, when both have the same number of neurons per layer (so GCNNs have r² fewer parameters due to weight sharing), the VC dimension of GCNNs is reduced by a factor of 1/r compared to the equivalent DNN (Lemma 1), confirming the expected sample complexity advantage of equivariance. The results extend the two-layer GCNN analysis of Petersen & Sepliarskaia [21] to arbitrary depth and generalize the DNN VC bounds of Bartlett et al. [3] by adding the logarithmic group-resolution term absent in the DNN setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the VC dimension analysis framework of Bartlett et al. (JMLR 2019) for piecewise-linear DNNs to Group Convolutional Neural Networks, adapting the region-counting proof strategy to account for the group convolution structure. It generalizes the two-layer GCNN VC dimension results of Petersen & Sepliarskaia (Neural Networks 2024) to networks of arbitrary depth, and departs from prior equivariant-network generalization studies (Bietti et al., Sannai et al., Shao et al.) that compared equivariant vs. non-equivariant models without accounting for the specific GCNN architecture (number of layers, weights, group resolution). The key technical novelty is establishing a new connection between GCNNs and DNNs via indicator-function kernels, enabling lower-bound constructions that exploit the group resolution r.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to provide VC dimension bounds for GCNNs of arbitrary depth, filling a gap that limited prior analysis to two-layer networks. The finding that GCNNs are strictly richer than DNNs with the same parameter count (by a factor of log r) challenges the intuition that equivariance constraints always reduce model capacity, and the near-tightness of bounds at practical depths (7–14 layers) gives practitioners concrete capacity estimates. Open questions include closing the factor-of-L gap between upper and lower bounds, extending the analysis to smooth (non-piecewise-polynomial) activations like sigmoid, and understanding whether the log r dependence can be tightened. The results also naturally extend to fat-shattering dimension, providing robustness-aware generalization guarantees.
