## Problem

Fairness-regularized online optimization and smoothed online optimization with switching costs have been studied separately, but their combination is fundamentally harder: the long-term fairness regularizer depends on the entire action sequence and can only be evaluated at the end of the episode, while switching costs create per-round dependencies on past actions that violate the independence assumption underlying all prior fairness-regularized online algorithms. The paper proves that without additional constraints on the offline benchmark, no online algorithm can achieve sublinear regret or a finite competitive ratio, and then introduces a constrained (R,δ)-benchmark under which meaningful guarantees become possible.

## Input / Output

At each round t, the algorithm receives a hitting cost function f_t(·) and a fairness context matrix A_t ∈ ℝ^{M×N}, and must commit to an irrevocable action x_t ∈ ℝ^N before seeing future contexts. The per-round objective uniquely depends on the previous action x_{t-1} through the squared L2 switching cost, breaking the memoryless assumption of prior dual mirror descent methods. The algorithm outputs three quantities per round: the action x_t, an auxiliary fairness variable z_t ∈ ℝ^M that serves as a dynamic fairness budget, and an updated dual variable κ_{t+1} via Bregman mirror descent on the subgradient d_t = z_t − A_t x_t.

## Method

FairOBD decomposes the horizon fairness cost g(1/T Σ A_t x_t) into per-round terms by introducing an auxiliary variable z_t and the long-term constraint Σ A_t x_t = Σ z_t, then relaxes this constraint via a Lagrangian multiplier κ_t updated by mirror descent. At each round, x_t is chosen by jointly minimizing the hitting cost, the switching cost weighted by λ_1, a reference-point regularizer λ_2‖x_t − v_t‖² (where v_t minimizes f_t), and the fairness term κ_t·A_t x_t, while z_t is optimized independently by solving g(z_t) − κ_t z_t. The key technical contribution is a novel proof technique that handles the per-round objective's dependence on the previous action (via switching cost) when comparing FairOBD's cost to the offline benchmark, using an intermediate benchmark with aligned per-round objectives (Lemmas D.3–D.4). Under the (R,δ)-constrained offline benchmark, FairOBD achieves an asymptotic competitive ratio of ½(1+√(1+4β_1/m)) as T→∞, which recovers the best-known ratio for smoothed online optimization without fairness as a special case.

## Results

In the default setting (u₁=10, u₂=1000, u₃=3.5) on a one-week Azure LLM inference trace across 7 data centers, FairOBD (η=10⁻²) achieves a total cost of 237.07, only 6.14 above the offline optimal (230.93) and substantially below ROBD (298.64), DMD (317.15), and HITMIN (343.62). FairOBD also attains the lowest fairness cost among all online baselines (41.36 vs. ROBD's 111.85 and DMD's 54.16) while maintaining a switching cost (25.65) close to the offline optimum (23.27). Performance is robust across learning rates η ∈ {10⁻², 10⁻³, 10⁻⁴} and fairness weights u₃ ∈ {1.75, 3.5, 7}, with total costs remaining within ~20 of OPT in all configurations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FairOBD decisively outperforms both primary baselines on total cost: it reduces total cost by 20.6% over ROBD (237.07 vs. 298.64) and by 25.3% over DMD (237.07 vs. 317.15). Against ROBD [18], which handles switching costs but ignores long-term fairness, FairOBD cuts the fairness cost from 111.85 to 41.36 (a 63% reduction) while keeping switching cost nearly identical (25.65 vs. 23.16). Against DMD [7], which optimizes fairness via mirror descent but ignores switching costs, FairOBD reduces switching cost from 93.53 to 25.65 (a 73% reduction) while also achieving a lower fairness cost (41.36 vs. 54.16). The gap between FairOBD and the offline optimal (OPT) is only 2.7% in total cost, whereas ROBD and DMD are 29.3% and 37.3% above OPT respectively. FairOBD's advantage is consistent across all tested learning rates and fairness weight settings (u₃ = 1.75, 3.5, 7), confirming robustness to hyperparameter choices.

## Relation to Prior Work

FairOBD unifies two previously separate lines of work: smoothed online convex optimization (ROBD, Goel et al. 2019), which handles switching costs via balanced descent, and fairness-regularized online allocation (Balseiro et al. 2021, 2022), which decomposes long-term fairness costs via auxiliary variables and dual mirror descent. The critical departure is that all prior fairness-regularized online algorithms assume per-round objectives are independent of historical actions, an assumption fundamentally violated by switching costs. FairOBD is the first algorithm to reconcile this tension, requiring a novel proof technique (intermediate benchmark in Lemmas D.3–D.4) that does not rely on temporal independence of per-round cost functions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The impossibility results (Theorems 4.1–4.2) rigorously confirm a long-standing conjecture that no online algorithm can achieve sublinear regret or finite competitive ratio under an unconstrained dynamic benchmark with a long-term fairness regularizer, even without switching costs. The proposed (R,δ)-benchmark provides a principled and practically motivated workaround (bounded frame-wise deviation) under which FairOBD achieves an asymptotic competitive ratio matching the best-known bounds for both special cases (no fairness, no switching). Open questions include extending the analysis beyond squared L2 switching costs to general strongly convex functions, incorporating untrusted predictions, and validating the framework in non-adversarial stochastic settings where the (R,δ) constraint is more naturally satisfied.
