## Problem

Score-based generative models (SGMs) are widely used as learned priors in Langevin samplers for Bayesian inverse problems, but all existing convergence analyses assume finite-dimensional posteriors. In the infinite-dimensional setting where PDE-governed inverse problems are naturally formulated, naive Langevin samplers produce samples with infinite energy or diverging drifts at fine scales, and the interplay between the preconditioner, the trace-class prior, and the score approximation error remains uncharacterized. This paper provides the first rigorous infinite-dimensional analysis of SGM-driven Langevin dynamics, deriving error estimates that explicitly depend on the score error and identifying an optimal preconditioner for uniform convergence across all posterior modes.

## Input / Output

The method operates in a separable Hilbert space H where the prior covariance C_μ and the preconditioner C are both trace-class operators (C cannot be the identity, as this leads to the same instabilities as the vanilla sampler). The learned score S_θ approximates the true score at a small diffusion time τ, and the linear forward operator A: H → R^N couples only the first N modes of the unknown to the finite-dimensional observations. The output is a continuous-time SDE whose stationary distribution converges to the posterior π_y in KL divergence, with the convergence rate decomposed mode-by-mode into observed (j ≤ N) and unobserved (j > N) components governed by the eigenvalue ratio λ_j/μ_j and the score error.

## Method

The core algorithm is a preconditioned Langevin SDE dX_t = S_θ(X_t, τ; μ)dt + C∇_X log ρ(y − AX_t)dt + √(2C)dW_t, where the trace-class preconditioner C appears in both the forward diffusion used to learn the score and the sampler itself, ensuring the process remains Hilbert-space-valued. The paper derives the stationary distribution of this SDE explicitly in terms of the score approximation error (decomposed into multiplicative ε_j^a and additive ε_j^b components per mode), yielding KL divergence bounds that are globally bounded under summability conditions on λ_j^{-1}ε_j^b. A central result is the derivation of an optimal preconditioner C = [C_μ^{-1} + σ^{-2}A^T A]^{-1} at leading order, with a first-order correction in τ that accounts for the score error, which equalizes the mean-reversion rate κ^{(j)} across all modes to guarantee uniform convergence. The analysis extends from Gaussian priors to a general class of priors absolutely continuous with respect to a Gaussian reference, where the optimal C additionally incorporates the curvature of the non-Gaussian potential φ_j.

## Results

The paper's main theoretical contribution is Theorem 3.1, which provides explicit KL-divergence error bounds for the preconditioned Langevin sampler in the Gaussian setting: for observed modes j≤N, D_KL(π̌_y^(j)‖π_y^(j)) = ½λ_j^{-2}(ε_j^b(τ))² − λ_j^{-1}ε_j^b(τ)/(1+σ^{-2}μ_j(A_N^T A_N)_{jj})·(σ^{-2}(A_N^T y)_j − λ_j^{-1}ε_j^b(τ))·((p_j−1)τ − p_j^{-1}ε_j^a(τ)) + O(τ²), and for unobserved modes j>N, D_KL = λ_j^{-2}(ε_j^b(τ))²(½+(p_j−1)τ−p_j^{-1}ε_j^a(τ))+O(τ²). Theorem 4.1 derives the optimal preconditioner eigenvalues λ_j^(0) = [μ_j^{-1}+σ^{-2}(A_N^T A_N)_{jj}]^{-1} for observed modes and λ_j^(0)=μ_j for unobserved modes, with first-order corrections λ_j^(1) accounting for score error. Numerical validation on a Brownian sheet (M=75, M=200 observed modes) confirms discretization invariance, and on an inverse heat equation problem (M=15, τ=10^{-3} and 10^{-1}), the preconditioned sampler shows robustness to score approximation error (relative error ~N(0,0.1²)) while vanilla Langevin deteriorates at fine scales.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is primarily a theoretical paper without a standard benchmark comparison table. The key empirical comparison is between the proposed preconditioned Langevin dynamics (with optimal C from Theorem 4.1) and vanilla Langevin dynamics on the inverse heat equation problem. The preconditioned sampler is robust to score approximation error across all modes, while vanilla Langevin deteriorates as the score error increases due to amplification at fine scales (reproducing the pathology in Figure 1). Autocorrelation analysis (Figure 4) shows that the preconditioned sampler achieves faster and more uniform convergence across modes, consistent with the optimal mean-reversion rates derived in Proposition 4.1. Theoretically, the paper extends the finite-dimensional convergence analysis of Sun et al. [25] to infinite dimensions, where their error bounds diverge with dimension, and identifies an optimal preconditioner that their finite-dimensional framework cannot address. The approach also generalizes the Gaussian-only analysis of Baldassari et al. [21] to non-Gaussian priors (Section 5, Theorem C.1), while their nonlinear setting prevents identification of an optimal preconditioner.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the finite-dimensional analysis of SGM-driven Langevin posterior sampling (Sun et al. [25]) to the infinite-dimensional Hilbert-space setting, building on the continuous-time diffusion framework of Baldassari et al. [20] and Pidstrigach et al. [49]. It departs from prior SGM-for-inverse-problems work [21, 24–26] by showing that the preconditioner C is not an optional acceleration device but a structural necessity: without a trace-class C, the sampler produces samples with infinite energy that do not belong to the function space. The optimal preconditioner derived here (Theorem 4.1) goes beyond the finite-dimensional analyses of Ma et al. [41, 42] by explicitly incorporating the score approximation error, a quantity that is absent from those finite-dimensional treatments.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first rigorous convergence guarantees (in KL divergence) for SGM-driven Langevin samplers in infinite dimensions, with error bounds that explicitly depend on the score approximation error—directly informing how accurately the score must be learned at small diffusion times. The derivation of an explicit optimal preconditioner depending on both the forward operator A and the score error gives practitioners a concrete recipe for stable sampling. Key limitations include the assumption of co-diagonalizability of C and C_μ, finite-dimensional data, and diagonal A^T A; extending the analysis to nonlinear inverse problems (where [21] could not identify an optimal C) is identified as an important open direction.
