## Problem

Dual-camera smartphone zoom transitions suffer from geometric misalignment (differing intrinsic/extrinsic parameters) and photometric inconsistencies (divergent ISP pipelines) between the wide-angle and main cameras. Existing frame interpolation methods cannot be directly applied because ground-truth intermediate frames are unavailable (continuous-zoom capture across dual-camera hardware is impractical) and zoom transitions introduce complex nonlinear motion and parallax that violate the linear-motion assumptions of conventional flow-based interpolation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255327/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a paired wide-angle image and main-camera image from a dual-camera smartphone (resolutions vary by device, e.g., 2133×1600 for Huawei, 1632×1224 for Redmi) along with an interpolation timestep, and produces a geometrically and photometrically consistent intermediate zoom frame. Internally, the 3D-TPR module generates a pixel-wise 3D trajectory similarity map that replaces the conventional fixed timestep map, encoding per-pixel motion progress from 3D optical flow and monocular disparity cues. The synthetic training dataset consists of 205 sequences of 16 virtual intermediate frames each, rendered via 3DGS from 132 real-world scenes.

## Method

OmniZoom comprises two components: (1) a cross-device virtual dataset pipeline built on 3DGS that interpolates camera intrinsics linearly and extrinsics via geodesic (log-exp) interpolation to create geometrically coherent virtual viewpoints, while a lightweight ColorNet MLP predicts per-Gaussian color offsets conditioned on a learnable camera embedding to correct photometric shifts, all supervised by reconstruction, semantic (MobileNetV2), and texture (VGG) consistency losses; (2) a plug-and-play 3D-TPR module that fuses RAFT 2D optical flow with Lite-Mono monocular disparity into a unified 3D motion field via cross-domain aggregation, then computes a per-pixel cosine similarity between the 0→t and 0→1 trajectory vectors to produce a content-aware similarity map replacing the fixed timestep index. A texture-focus loss reweights Charbonnier reconstruction by an ALPD-based texture mask to amplify high-frequency supervision, and a scale-dependent mask uncertainty penalty (4M(1−M)^γ) pushes fusion masks toward binary decisions to suppress ghosting artifacts. The framework is integrated into RIFE, IFRNet, EMA-VFI, and AMT backbones, first pretrained on Vimeo-90K then finetuned on the synthetic ZI dataset.

## Results

On real-world ZI tasks across four smartphone platforms (Huawei, iPhone, OPPO, Redmi), the full OmniZoom pipeline (3Df-TPR, i.e., 3D-TPR finetuned on the synthetic ZI dataset) consistently achieves the best no-reference quality scores. For example, IFRNet on Huawei improves MUSIQ from 57.46 (2D baseline) to 73.02 (3D-TPR) to 74.12 (3Df-TPR), and CLIP-IQA from 0.5422 to 0.5909. On standard benchmarks (Vimeo90K, Inter4K, UCF101), 3D-TPR yields consistent LPIPS reductions (e.g., 0.086→0.081 for RIFE on Vimeo90K) and SSIM gains over the 2D InterpAny baseline. Cross-device generalization to unseen platforms (iPhone, OPPO) is confirmed: 3Df-TPR still outperforms 2D and 3D baselines on all four FI networks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the 2D InterpAny baseline [60], 3D-TPR consistently wins on all perceptual metrics (PI, MUSIQ, FLOLPIPS) across every FI backbone and benchmark, with the largest gains in MUSIQ (e.g., +14.3 for IFRNet on Huawei) and FLOLPIPS (0.128→0.091 in the ablation). Against ZoomGS [50] (finetuned as ZoomGSf), the proposed ZI dataset supervision (Oursf) achieves superior CLIP-IQA and MUSIQ on both seen devices (Huawei, Redmi) and unseen devices (iPhone, OPPO), e.g., AMT on Huawei: CLIP-IQA 0.5847 (ZoomGSf) vs. 0.6018 (Oursf). Against raw 3DGS [23] supervision (3DGSf), Oursf wins on CLIP-IQA and MUSIQ in nearly all settings, while 3DGSf occasionally matches or slightly beats Oursf on NIQE for specific networks (e.g., AMT on Huawei: 3.4894 vs. 3.4547). For photometric calibration, ColorNet outperforms both Mean-Std Normalization [48] and Reinhard Color Transfer [51] on all four no-reference metrics (Table 5), with the largest margin on CLIP-IQA (0.6099 vs. 0.4577/0.4588). Overall, the paper's method wins or ties on the majority of comparisons, with the most consistent and largest advantages in perceptual quality metrics (MUSIQ, CLIP-IQA) over both the 2D indexing paradigm and prior virtual-data generation pipelines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends ZoomGS [50], which uses 3D Gaussian Splatting to synthesize virtual intermediate views between dual-camera pairs, by adding spatial transition modeling (interpolating intrinsic/extrinsic parameters) and a per-Gaussian ColorNet for cross-device photometric alignment, thereby generalizing from single-device to arbitrary smartphone configurations. On the interpolation side, it builds on InterpAny [60]'s distance-aware indexing paradigm but replaces the 2D flow-based similarity with a 3D Trajectory Progress Ratio (3D-TPR) that fuses RAFT optical flow with Lite-Mono disparity residuals to resolve depth-direction motion ambiguity. The plug-and-play design integrates these components into existing FI backbones (RIFE, IFRNet, EMA-VFI, AMT) without architectural modification, positioning the contribution as a training-data pipeline plus a lightweight encoding module rather than a new network.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The cross-device virtual dataset generation via 3DGS with explicit geometric and photometric decoupling offers a practical solution to the ground-truth scarcity problem in zoom interpolation, and the demonstrated transfer to unseen devices (iPhone, OPPO) suggests the approach could reduce the need for device-specific calibration in consumer imaging pipelines. However, a notable limitation is that at inference the 3D-TPR degenerates to a fixed timestep map (identical to the 2D baseline), meaning the 3D geometric awareness is primarily a training-time signal rather than a test-time capability. Open questions include whether the framework extends to triple-camera or continuous-optical-zoom systems, whether the disparity-based similarity remains reliable under large parallax or occlusion, and whether the modest pixel-metric gains (e.g., +0.13 dB PSNR on Vimeo90K) translate to user-perceptible improvements in real deployment.
