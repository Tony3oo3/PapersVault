## Problem

Conventional activation sparsification for flash-offloaded VLM inference selects neurons solely by activation magnitude, assuming I/O latency scales linearly with data volume. In practice, flash read throughput depends heavily on access contiguity, and the smoother activation distributions of modern gated-activation VLMs (e.g., SwiGLU, GeLU) produce fragmented, scattered reads that severely degrade I/O efficiency. This paper identifies and addresses the mismatch between model-centric sparsification and the underlying storage access behavior.

## Input / Output

The method operates within a streaming VLM inference pipeline where video frames arrive sequentially and are encoded into visual tokens (e.g., 14×14 patches per frame for LLaVA-OneVision) that augment a KV cache alongside a text prompt. At each inference step, the system must decide which rows of each weight matrix to fetch from flash; the method outputs a binary selection mask per matrix that determines the loaded subset. The final model output is a text response (multiple-choice QA answer or video description), evaluated on TempCompass, NExT-QA, and VideoDetailCaption benchmarks.

## Method

NEURON CHUNKING abstracts a memory access pattern into a contiguity distribution—the frequency distribution of contiguous neuron groups (chunks)—and models total read latency as the sum of per-chunk latencies from a pre-profiled lookup table. A utility-guided greedy selection algorithm then slides windows of multiple sizes over the neuron index space, scores each candidate chunk by the ratio of summed activation importance to estimated latency, and iteratively selects the highest-utility non-overlapping chunks until a budget is met. An offline hot-cold reordering step permutes weight rows by activation frequency to further improve contiguity. The entire selection runs in ~2 ms per weight matrix using GPU-accelerated sorting, enabling input-adaptive sparsification at inference time without retraining.

## Results

On Jetson Orin Nano, NEURON CHUNKING achieves an average I/O latency reduction of 2.19× (up to 4.65×) at comparable accuracy versus the top-k sparsification baseline, evaluated across five VLMs (LLaVA-OneVision-7B/0.5B, VILA-8B, NVILA-2B, LongVA-7B) on TempCompass, NExT-QA, and VideoDetailCaption. On Jetson Orin AGX, the average speedup increases to 2.89× (up to 5.76×), reflecting the wider throughput gap between contiguous and scattered flash access on the faster SSD. The chunk selection overhead is approximately 2 ms per weight matrix (~400 ms total for the full model), negligible relative to end-to-end inference time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the TEAL-based top-k activation sparsification baseline, NEURON CHUNKING consistently wins on I/O latency at matched accuracy across all five models and three datasets, with average speedups of 2.19× (Nano) and 2.89× (AGX); the gap is largest for smaller models (NVILA-2B: 3.2–3.4×) where channel dimensions produce more fragmented reads. Against LLM-in-a-Flash's row-column bundling technique, NEURON CHUNKING achieves 1.7–4.0× additional speedup (Table 3), confirming that bundling alone cannot achieve peak flash throughput on Jetson hardware (which requires 236–348 KB contiguous reads to saturate bandwidth, versus <100 KB on the MacBooks where LLM-in-a-Flash was evaluated). The paper notes that bundling can actually degrade performance in most configurations due to scattering of unbundled neurons, whereas the contiguity-aware chunk selection consistently improves efficiency regardless of access pattern.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the activation sparsification line (Deja Vu, CATS, TEAL) and flash-offloaded inference systems (LLM in a Flash, PowerInfer-2, Ripple) by introducing a hardware-aware formulation that jointly optimizes neuron importance and flash I/O latency. Prior methods treat I/O cost as a monotonic function of data volume, an assumption that breaks down for VLMs whose smooth (non-ReLU) activation distributions produce fragmented access patterns. The key conceptual departure is the contiguity distribution abstraction, which reduces arbitrary access patterns to a compact representation enabling a lightweight chunk-based latency model and a greedy utility-guided selection algorithm at runtime.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

For edge deployment of VLMs where flash offloading is the only viable memory strategy, this work shows that the standard 'select top-k activations' heuristic can actually *increase* latency by fragmenting reads, and that contiguity-aware selection recovers 2–5× I/O speedup at comparable accuracy. The practical impact is significant for real-time streaming applications (AR, robotics) on memory-constrained devices. Open questions include how the approach interacts with emerging I/O mechanisms (io_uring, read coalescing), whether the greedy chunk selection can be improved to capture inter-chunk synergies, and how the method scales when additional memory enables partial weight caching.
