## Problem

Diffusion-based image fusion methods (e.g., DDFM, CCF) require hundreds of sampling steps, making them impractical for real-world deployment. Existing acceleration strategies—distillation and latent-space diffusion—fail to generalize to fusion: distillation demands task-specific fine-tuning, and standard VAEs are trained for reconstruction rather than multi-modal information integration, creating an objective mismatch that degrades fusion quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250774/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts pairs of images from complementary modalities (infrared + visible, multi-exposure sets, or multi-focus sets) and outputs a single fused image. Notably, the Rectified Flow sampling starts from the visible-light image rather than pure Gaussian noise, and the entire generation process operates in a VAE latent space. Inference completes in a single ODE step (0.308 s on an NVIDIA V100), compared to 22–62 s for competing diffusion-based methods, while using only 65.57 M parameters.

## Method

RFfusion replaces the multi-step SDE sampling of standard diffusion models with a single-step ODE integration along a Rectified Flow linear trajectory, injecting fusion priors (from an EM algorithm) into the velocity field via posterior guidance so that no additional training of the flow model is needed. To reduce computational cost, a task-specific VAE performs fusion in latent space; because standard VAEs are trained for pixel-level reconstruction rather than cross-modal integration, the authors introduce a two-stage training strategy. Stage I fine-tunes the VAE encoder and decoder with a frequency-similarity loss (computed via FFT in the spectral domain) to capture the high- and low-frequency components critical for fusion. Stage II fine-tunes only the decoder with a composite fusion loss combining intensity, SSIM, gradient, color, and a saliency-mask term that enforces region-aware blending of salient structures from each modality. The same set of checkpoints is used across all three fusion tasks without task-specific fine-tuning, demonstrating strong generalization.

## Results

On the primary IVIF task, RFfusion achieves the best MI (3.320 on M3FD, 2.948 on T&R) and VIF (0.748 on M3FD, 0.675 on T&R) across all compared methods, while also attaining the highest EN on T&R (7.139). In terms of efficiency, RFfusion runs in 0.308 s per image with only 65.57 M parameters, compared to 22.03 s (DDFM) and 62.47 s (CCF), while simultaneously achieving the best SF (14.00) and AG (5.218) among all diffusion-based methods. On multi-exposure (MEFB) and multi-focus (MFIF) fusion, the method achieves the top MI (6.528, 6.443), CC (0.901, 0.977), and Qcb (0.461, 0.654) without any task-specific fine-tuning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DDFM (the direct baseline), RFfusion wins on every metric: MI improves by +0.449 (M3FD) and +1.150 (T&R), VIF by +0.071 and +0.398, while runtime drops from 22.03 s to 0.308 s (~71× faster). Compared to CCF, RFfusion achieves higher SF (14.00 vs 10.14) and AG (5.218 vs 3.882) at 203× lower runtime (0.308 s vs 62.47 s) and fewer parameters (65.57 M vs 552.81 M). Among non-diffusion IVIF methods, RFfusion leads on MI and VIF across both M3FD and T&R, though CoCoNet retains an edge on SCD (1.772 vs 1.574) and EN (7.738 vs 6.722) on M3FD. On MEFB, RFfusion outperforms TC-MoA on MI (6.528 vs 5.418) and Qcb (0.461 vs 0.430) but trails slightly on PSNR (58.49 vs 59.00). On MFIF, RFfusion achieves the best CC (0.977) and PSNR (75.04) among diffusion-based methods, though TC-MoA still leads on MI (6.686 vs 6.443) and Qcb (0.731 vs 0.654), and DeFusion edges ahead on PSNR (76.62 vs 75.04).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) diffusion-based image fusion (DDFM, CCF, Diff-IF), which injects fusion priors via posterior sampling but requires hundreds of steps; (2) Rectified Flow (Liu et al., ICLR 2023; InstaFlow), which straightens the probability path to enable one-step ODE sampling; and (3) latent-space diffusion (LDM, Rombach et al., CVPR 2022), which reduces computation by operating in a VAE-compressed space. RFfusion is the first to combine all three for image fusion: it repurposes a pre-trained Rectified Flow model as the backbone, applies DDFM-style EM posterior guidance in a single ODE step, and introduces a task-specific VAE with a two-stage training strategy (frequency-domain loss + fusion-specific loss) to bridge the gap between reconstruction-oriented VAE objectives and fusion requirements. The key departure from prior diffusion-fusion work is that no retraining of the flow model is needed—only the VAE is adapted.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 70–200× runtime reduction (0.308 s vs. 22–62 s on V100) while matching or modestly exceeding multi-step diffusion quality makes diffusion-based fusion practically deployable for real-time or resource-constrained settings, a regime where it was previously infeasible. The generalization across IVIF, MEF, and MFF without task-specific fine-tuning suggests the Rectified Flow + posterior-guidance recipe is a broadly applicable acceleration module for conditional diffusion tasks beyond fusion. The main open question, acknowledged by the authors, is that the underlying flow model is trained on generic image generation rather than fusion-specific data, which likely caps the achievable quality ceiling; a fusion-pretrained flow model or a distillation step tailored to fusion distributions could close this gap.
