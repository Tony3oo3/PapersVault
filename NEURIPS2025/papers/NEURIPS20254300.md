## Problem

Prior universal approximation results for transformers rely on explicit, architecture-specific constructions (e.g., approximating hardmax with softmax, or designing specific sparse patterns), making it difficult to verify UAP for new or modified attention mechanisms. No unified, architecture-independent criterion existed that could guarantee UAP for a broad class of transformer-type models without redoing the construction from scratch.

## Input / Output

The framework operates on fixed-length sequences of n tokens, each d-dimensional (X ∈ R^{d×n}), and produces a transformed token sequence of the same shape. The analysis is conducted on compact subsets of R^{d×n} in the L^p sense, and extends naturally to equivariant settings where the model must respect a subgroup G of the symmetric group S_n acting on token positions. The feedforward component acts token-wise (tensor-type maps), while the attention component performs token-mixing across all n positions.

## Method

The paper identifies two verifiable conditions for UAP: (1) the feedforward family must be nonlinear and affine-invariant (ensuring single-token expressivity via the ResNet controllability framework), and (2) the token-mixing family must satisfy a 'token distinguishability' condition—its composition must produce distinct token representations for inputs from different orbits under the symmetry group. A key simplification (Theorem 2) shows that when the attention layer is analytic in its parameters, token distinguishability reduces to checking a two-sample condition, avoiding the need to verify it for arbitrarily many samples. This framework is then applied to kernel-based attention (softmax, RBF, Performer), sparse attention (Longformer, BigBird, sliding-window), Linformer, and SkyFormer, yielding UAP guarantees with bounded numbers of attention layers. The paper also uses the framework to design new architectures with UAP under specific symmetries (dihedral D_n and cyclic C_n groups) via convolutional or sparse attention patterns.

## Results

This is a purely theoretical paper with no experimental benchmarks. The main contributions are Theorem 1 (a general sufficient condition for G-UAP requiring feedforward nonlinearity/affine-invariance plus token distinguishability) and Theorem 2 (reducing verification to a two-sample condition under analyticity of the attention layer). Corollaries establish UAP for kernel-based attention (softmax, RBF, Performer), sparse attention (Longformer, BigBird, sliding-window), LinFormer, and SkyFormer, with the notable result that only one (or m, for sparse) token-mixing layer plus sufficiently many feedforward layers suffices for UAP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not compare against empirical baselines; it is a theoretical framework paper. Instead, it generalizes and subsumes prior constructive UAP results: Corollary 1 recovers and extends the softmax-attention UAP of Yun et al. [74] (removing the need for query bias) and the Performer UAP of Alberti et al. [2] (removing extra hidden dimensions), while establishing new UAP for RBF-kernel attention. Corollary 2 generalizes the sparse-attention UAP of Yun et al. [75] and Zaheer et al. [76] by requiring only graph connectivity rather than periodicity, Hamiltonian paths, or star sub-structures, and by identifying the exact minimal number of attention layers (m hops) needed. The framework also extends the symmetry analysis of Li et al. [45] from 1-D coordinates under transitive groups to d-dimensional tokens under arbitrary (including non-transitive) subgroups of S_n.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the control-theory/dynamical-systems UAP framework developed for deep residual networks (Cheng et al. 2025; Li et al. 2022) to transformer architectures, where the key challenge is that feedforward layers operate token-wise and inter-token interactions must come from the attention mechanism. It departs from prior transformer UAP results (Yun et al. 2019, 2020; Kajitsuka & Sato 2024; Alberti et al. 2023), which relied on explicit, architecture-specific constructions, by providing a unified non-constructive criterion (token distinguishability + feedforward nonlinearity/affine-invariance) that is verifiable independent of attention details. The symmetry analysis generalizes Li et al. 2024 from transitive subgroups acting on 1D coordinates to arbitrary subgroups of S_n acting on d-dimensional tokens, and the kernel-based attention framework follows Tsai et al. 2019.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework gives architects a principled, checkable recipe for designing new transformer variants with guaranteed UAP, including architectures with specific functional symmetries (dihedral, cyclic) relevant to molecular and periodic-data applications. The analyticity-based reduction (Theorem 2) to a two-sample condition is a practical simplification that avoids the combinatorial constructions in prior work. Key limitations acknowledged by the authors include: no treatment of normalization layers, inapplicability of the analyticity shortcut to non-analytic attention (e.g., Katharopoulos et al. 2020), and the absence of quantitative characterization of how individual components (multi-head, MoE, low-rank) affect approximation efficiency. A systematic quantitative theory of approximation rates for these architectures remains open.
