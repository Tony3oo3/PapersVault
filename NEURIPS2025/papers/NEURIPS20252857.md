## Problem

Existing diffusion-based image codecs require multi-step iterative denoising at inference, incurring substantial computational overhead, and must train a separate model for each target bit-rate, dramatically inflating training time and storage. This paper asks whether a single generative model can reconstruct images at multiple bit-rates in just one denoising pass by reinterpreting quantization distortion as a diffusion process.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252857/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image (evaluated at 768×512 on Kodak and 1024×1024 on DIV2K-val/CLIC2020) and a target bit-rate r from a predefined set. The image is mapped to a 4-channel latent via a frozen Stable Diffusion 2.1 VAE encoder, then compressed by a bit-rate-specific hyper-encoder that downsamples and vector-quantizes the latent into a codebook-indexed bitstream (bpp = log₂V / 64s²). At decompression, the quantized latent is denoised in a single U-Net pass and decoded back to pixel space, yielding the reconstructed image.

## Method

OSCAR's central insight is to model quantized latents as intermediate states along a diffusion trajectory: the quantization noise is shown to approximate additive Gaussian noise, so the quantized latent ẑ can be decomposed as √(ᾱ_t)·z₀ + √(1−ᾱ_t)·ε, where the pseudo timestep t is determined by the bit-rate. A two-stage training procedure realizes this: Stage 1 trains bit-rate-specific hyper-encoders with a cosine-similarity alignment loss so that the residual noise becomes Gaussian and the cosine similarity between ẑ and z₀ stabilizes per bit-rate; Stage 2 jointly fine-tunes the SD U-Net (via LoRA, rank 16) and all hyper-encoders with a combined loss of representation alignment, perceptual (MSE + DISTS + Sobel edge-aware DISTS), and latent-space GAN terms. Each bit-rate is mapped to a pseudo diffusion timestep by matching the empirically measured cosine similarity to the theoretical form √(ᾱ_t), allowing a single U-Net to denoise quantized latents at any supported bit-rate in one forward pass conditioned on the assigned timestep.

## Results

OSCAR is evaluated on Kodak (24 images, 768×512), DIV2K-val (100 images, 1024×1024), and CLIC2020 (428 images, 1024×1024) using full-reference metrics (LPIPS, DISTS, PSNR, FID) and no-reference metrics (MUSIQ, CLIPIQA). The paper reports that OSCAR consistently outperforms all 11 baselines across perceptual and no-reference metrics at multiple bit-rates, with particularly strong gains on LPIPS and DISTS. On PSNR, OSCAR is competitive among diffusion-based codecs but trails traditional codecs (BPG, VVC), a limitation attributed to the inherent pixel-level reconstruction ceiling of the VAE. The BD-Rate comparison (measured by DISTS) shows OSCAR achieving a favorable quality-efficiency tradeoff, with substantially lower MACs than multi-step diffusion baselines due to its single-pass inference.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OSCAR outperforms all traditional codecs (BPG, VVC) and learning-based single-rate codecs (HiFiC, MS-ILLM) on perceptual metrics (LPIPS, DISTS) and no-reference metrics (CLIPIQA, MUSIQ) across all three datasets and tested bit-rates. Against multi-rate learned codecs (DPICT, CTC), OSCAR maintains superior perceptual quality, particularly at low bit-rates where these methods suffer notable degradation. Among diffusion-based codecs, OSCAR surpasses CDC, DiffEIC, and PerCo on perceptual and no-reference metrics while operating in a single denoising step rather than the multi-step iterative sampling used by those methods, yielding orders-of-magnitude lower inference cost (reflected in the MACs shown in Fig. 2b). On PSNR and FID (Fig. 9), OSCAR is competitive with the strongest diffusion-based baselines (IPIC, DDCM) but, like all diffusion-based codecs, underperforms traditional codecs in pixel-level fidelity—a gap the paper attributes to the VAE's reconstruction ceiling rather than the compression pipeline itself. The ablation in Fig. 7 confirms that the pseudo-timestep mapping and representation alignment are both critical: removing either leads to consistent drops across all metrics and bit-rates.

## Relation to Prior Work

OSCAR builds on the Stable Diffusion 2.1 latent diffusion backbone and VQ-VAE-style codebook quantization, but departs fundamentally from prior diffusion-based codecs (CDC, PerCo, DiffEIC) that require multi-step iterative denoising from random noise. The key conceptual departure is the 'pseudo diffusion timestep' mapping, which reinterprets quantization noise as an intermediate state along a diffusion trajectory, enabling a single denoising pass instead of dozens. It also unifies multi-rate compression into one shared generative model (fine-tuned via LoRA), contrasting with prior learned codecs that train a separate network per Lagrange multiplier or bit-rate.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The one-step inference and unified multi-rate design address two major practical barriers to deploying diffusion-based compression: computational cost at decode time and the training/storage overhead of one-model-per-rate. The empirical validation that quantization residuals become Gaussian after representation alignment is well-supported (QQ-plots, ablations across three datasets), lending credibility to the core modeling assumption. Open questions include whether the per-bit-rate hyper-encoders can themselves be unified, whether the approach extends to video or higher-resolution generation, and how well the Gaussian-noise approximation holds at extremely low bit-rates where quantization error becomes highly non-Gaussian.
