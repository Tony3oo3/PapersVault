## Problem

Post-trained LLMs (PoLMs) exhibit over-confidence, assigning high confidence to both correct and incorrect outputs, yet existing post-hoc calibration methods like temperature scaling require labeled validation data that is scarce for domain-specific tasks. This paper asks whether the well-calibrated confidence of pre-trained LLMs (PLMs) can be leveraged on unlabeled data to calibrate PoLMs in a fully unsupervised manner.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255458/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes an unlabeled set of prompts (no ground-truth labels) and the next-token logits from both a PLM and a PoLM on those prompts. It produces a single scalar temperature parameter τ that rescales the PoLM's logits via softmax, yielding calibrated confidence scores. A key operational detail is that the optimization only uses the subset of prompts where the PLM and PoLM make identical predictions (agreement examples), discarding all disagreement examples from the loss computation.

## Method

DACA (Disagreement-Aware Confidence Alignment) optimizes a temperature parameter τ by minimizing the KL divergence between the PLM's softmax distribution and the PoLM's temperature-scaled softmax distribution, but masks this loss with an indicator that is nonzero only when both models agree on the predicted class. The theoretical analysis (Propositions 3.2–3.3) shows that on disagreement examples, the PLM's confidence underestimates the PoLM's true accuracy, and the KL gradient with respect to τ remains strictly positive, driving τ toward infinity and causing under-confidence. By restricting the loss to agreement examples, DACA decouples the harmful influence of disagreement, yielding a conservative τ that avoids the under-confidence failure mode of naive alignment. The method is architecture-agnostic (works even when PLM and PoLM differ in size or family) and extends naturally to vector and matrix scaling by replacing the scalar τ with a parameterized rescaling function φ_θ under the same agreement-masked KL objective.

## Results

On the MMLU benchmark (57 subjects), DACA reduces average ECE from 16.38% to 8.39% for Qwen3-8B, from 23.68% to 8.60% for Gemma-3-12B-Instruct, and from 12.87% to 7.84% for Llama-3-70B-Instruct, all without any labeled data. On MedMCQA, DACA reduces GPT-4o's ECE from 21.23% to 6.99% using only the pre-trained Gemma-3-12B as a reference. The method also extends to open-ended QA (TruthfulQA), reducing Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct's ECE from 30.96% to 5.24%, and improves selective classification accuracy at high confidence thresholds.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DACA consistently outperforms all three unlabeled baselines (CAPE, Elicitation, Elicitation-Ensemble) on ECE across every model and dataset. For example, on Gemma-3-12B-Instruct, DACA achieves 8.60% ECE versus 13.91% for CAPE, 25.46% for Elicitation, and 25.42% for Elicitation-Ensemble. Against the supervised temperature scaling reference (TS†), DACA matches or exceeds it on 3 of 4 MMLU models: it beats TS† on ECE for Qwen3-8B (8.39% vs. 8.66%), Gemma-3-12B (8.60% vs. 9.75%), and Llama-3-70B (7.84% vs. 8.36%), while TS† is slightly better for Yi-1.5-34B (8.59% vs. 9.47%). On MedMCQA, DACA reduces DeepSeek-V2-Lite-Chat's ECE to 1.72%, marginally beating TS† (1.80%), and for Qwen2.5-72B-Instruct achieves 3.94% vs. TS†'s 4.11%. The verbalization-based baselines (Elicitation, Elicitation-Ensemble) frequently worsen calibration relative to the uncalibrated model, confirming that next-token-logit-based methods are more reliable for LLM confidence estimation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends temperature scaling (Guo et al., 2017) from its standard supervised formulation (minimizing NLL on labeled data) to an unsupervised setting by replacing the NLL objective with a KL-divergence-based alignment between a well-calibrated PLM and an over-confident PoLM. The key departure from prior LLM calibration work (CAPE, Elicitation, Thermometer) is the use of unlabeled data and the PLM's confidence as a free supervisory signal, eliminating the need for task-specific labels or auxiliary model training. The theoretical contribution—showing that prediction disagreement between PLM and PoLM drives the optimal temperature to infinity on disagreement examples—provides a formal diagnosis of why naive confidence alignment fails, which no prior calibration work had identified.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: DACA eliminates the labeled-data bottleneck that makes post-hoc calibration infeasible for domain-specific tasks (medical, legal, mathematical), and its cross-architecture capability (a 12B open-source PLM calibrating GPT-4o) makes it applicable to closed-source API models where no pre-trained counterpart exists. Limitations include the additional inference cost of running the PLM, the reduced effective calibration set after filtering disagreement examples, and the assumption that the PLM is well-calibrated on the target domain. Open questions include whether disagreement examples can be repurposed (e.g., as a signal for domain shift) and whether the approach extends to generative or multi-turn settings beyond MCQA.
