## Problem

While prior work has shown that individual attention heads handle specific functions like retrieval or answer consistency, these findings are largely based on simple or synthetic tasks, leaving open how functional specialization operates during complex, multi-step reasoning. No systematic framework exists to align LLM attention heads with the diverse cognitive functions—retrieval, logical reasoning, mathematical calculation, decision-making, and others—that human cognition employs when solving complex problems.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained LLM and processes CogQA subquestions (each a single cognitive-function step in a chain-of-thought decomposition), extracting attention head value vectors at the top-5 semantically important generated tokens across all layers. These per-head activations are augmented with their layer's mean activation to form enriched features, which are scored against 8 cognitive function classes. The output is a C×(L·M) importance matrix mapping each cognitive function to every head-layer pair, from which sparse sets of 'cognitive heads' are identified via elbow-point thresholding on descending importance curves.

## Method

The paper introduces CogQA, a dataset of 570 questions decomposed into 3,402 subquestion-answer-cognitive-function triples spanning 8 functions (4 low-level: retrieval, knowledge recall, semantic and syntactic understanding; 4 high-order: math calculation, inference, logical reasoning, decision-making), constructed via GPT-4o chain-of-thought decomposition with a three-stage human verification pipeline. A multi-class probing classifier (shared linear projection to 64-dim per head → concatenation → 512-unit hidden layer with ReLU and 0.3 dropout → 8-class output) is trained on enriched head features to predict cognitive functions, and gradient×activation attribution then yields per-head importance scores for each function. Causal validation is performed through two interventions: negative ablation (scaling identified head outputs by ε=0.001) and positive steering (shifting head activations along the direction separating correct from incorrect answers), demonstrating that cognitive heads are sparsely distributed, hierarchically organized, and causally necessary for their respective reasoning functions.

## Results

The multi-class probing classifier achieves 75.18–84.71% test accuracy in identifying cognitive function labels from attention head activations across six LLMs. Masking identified cognitive heads causes dramatic performance drops: on Llama3.1-8B, Retrieval accuracy falls from 84.71% (random heads) to 8.24% (cognitive heads), and Knowledge Recall drops from 83.84% to 38.38%. On downstream GSM8K_100, masking Math Calculation heads reduces accuracy from 82–94% (base) to 34–38% across all models, while positive intervention on Math or Semantic heads recovers or exceeds base performance (e.g., 84% vs. 82% base on Llama3.1-8B). Fewer than 7% of all attention heads exceed an importance threshold of 0.001, confirming the sparsity of cognitive heads.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against other interpretability methods; instead, the central comparison is between masking identified cognitive heads and masking an equal number of randomly selected heads. Across all six models and eight cognitive functions, masking cognitive heads consistently produces far larger performance drops than masking random heads. For example, on Llama3.1-8B, masking Retrieval heads drops COMET from 90.83 to 44.96 and accuracy from 84.71 to 8.24, whereas random head masking only reduces accuracy to 84.71 (COMET 90.83). On Qwen3-8B, masking Inference heads drops accuracy from 94.45 to 72.77 (COMET from 94.45 to 24.56), while random masking retains 94.45 accuracy. The cross-function experiment (Table 2) further confirms specificity: masking Retrieval heads during Knowledge Recall evaluation causes a smaller drop (72.05 COMET) than masking Recall heads during Recall evaluation (56.93 COMET), demonstrating that each function's heads are functionally distinct. Positive intervention along cognitive head activation directions improves performance on both retrieval and math tasks, validating the identified heads as causally relevant rather than merely correlated.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the mechanistic interpretability literature on attention head specialization (Clark et al. 2019; Voita et al. 2019; Olsson et al. 2022; Wu et al. 2024; Li et al. 2023) from isolated, single-function analyses on simple tasks to a systematic multi-class mapping of heads onto a cognitive-science-derived taxonomy of eight reasoning functions under complex, multi-step chain-of-thought settings. It also builds on the brain-LLM alignment line of work (Caucheteux et al. 2022; Schrimpf et al. 2021), which focused on language perception, by pushing the comparison to higher-order reasoning processes. The probing methodology follows standard practice (Alain & Bengio 2016; Belinkov 2022), but the many-to-many head-function assignment and the gradient×activation importance scoring are applied in a novel multi-class cognitive framing that prior single-class head-identification work did not address.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first systematic, cognitively grounded decomposition of LLM reasoning into specialized attention heads, yielding a reusable dataset (CogQA) and a concrete intervention protocol (masking/steering) that could inform function-aware model compression, targeted fine-tuning, and dynamic head activation strategies. The hierarchical finding—that low-level retrieval and recall heads are prerequisites for high-level math and inference performance—suggests architectural implications for how reasoning layers should be designed or protected. Key limitations temper the impact: only eight coarse functions are considered, each subquestion is assigned a single label, no statistical significance is reported, and the hierarchical evidence (Table 3) comes from a single model. Open questions include whether finer-grained or multi-label cognitive taxonomies reveal richer structure, and whether the identified heads remain stable under different prompting or training regimes.
