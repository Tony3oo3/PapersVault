## Problem

Mean-field variational inference assumes independence across data instances, making it inadequate for correlated data such as time series and graphs. Existing structured VI methods either model dimension-level correlations (too small to capture instance-level dependencies at scale) or are restricted to first-order pairwise dependencies, precluding the representation of higher-order interactions where the relationship between two latent variables is mediated by multiple others.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251262/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on a latent variable matrix Z ∈ R^{D×N} (D latent dimensions, N instances) with a specified k-order dependency backbone (chain or tree). For time series, input is a sequence of N multivariate observations x_t ∈ R^C processed through GRU encoders; for clustering, input includes a constraint graph G=(V,E,A) encoding must-link and cannot-link relations. The method produces reparameterized posterior samples z̃ ∈ R^N per dimension, ELBO estimates, and downstream outputs (anomaly scores, H-step forecasts, cluster assignments) depending on the task.

## Method

HoT-VI imposes a k-order banded structure on the precision matrix P (zeroing entries where |i−j|>k), then proves (Theorem 2.1) that the global N-dimensional Gaussian posterior can be equivalently decomposed into overlapping (k+1)-dimensional local marginals. Sequential sampling draws z̃_{i+k} from the conditional q(z_{i+k}|z̃_{i:i+k−1}) derived from each local marginal, yielding a full sample that is an explicit function of correlation parameters Γ. The key technical contribution is a conditional correlation reparameterization: instead of directly outputting γ_{i,i+k} from a neural network (which risks non-positive-definite correlation matrices), the method parameterizes first-order correlations γ_{i,i+1} and higher-order conditional correlations γ^c_{i,i+t|I} all constrained to (−1,1), then iteratively constructs valid (k+1)×(k+1) correlation matrices via a one-to-one mapping M (Theorem 2.2, Corollary 2.3). The framework extends to tree-structured backbones for non-sequential data, and is trained end-to-end by substituting reparameterized samples into the ELBO, with O(kN) neural network evaluations per epoch.

## Results

On time series forecasting (5 datasets, 5 horizons), HoT-VI with 10-order dependencies achieves an average MSE of 0.386 and MAE of 0.373, substantially outperforming Informer (0.819/0.660), DeepAR (0.978/0.678), and VRAE (0.796/0.741). On time series anomaly detection, 10-order HoT-VI reaches F1 scores of 0.8636 (SMAP), 0.9145 (MSL), and 0.9284 (SMD), surpassing the best baseline OmniAnomaly (0.8434/0.8849/0.8857). On constrained clustering, 10-order HoT-VI achieves 98.5% ACC, 94.6% NMI, and 95.6% ARI on MNIST, improving over TreeVI (97.4/93.1/93.7) and DC-GMM (96.5/91.4/92.5). Performance consistently improves as the dependency order k increases from 1 to 10 across all three tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HoT-VI outperforms all 14 baselines across all three tasks. In time series forecasting, it beats Informer by ~53% in average MSE (0.386 vs. 0.819), DeepAR by ~60% (0.386 vs. 0.978), GRU-NVP by ~88% (0.386 vs. 3.112), and VRAE by ~52% (0.386 vs. 0.796), with only two second-best exceptions across 25 dataset-horizon combinations. In anomaly detection, 10-order HoT-VI surpasses OmniAnomaly (the strongest baseline) by 1.0–3.9 F1 points and achieves ELBO improvements of 3–27 points over SISVAE, DAGMM, and LSTM-VAE. In constrained clustering, HoT-VI (10-order) improves over the closest prior work TreeVI by 1.1 ACC, 1.5 NMI, and 1.9 ARI on MNIST, and over DC-GMM by 2.0 ACC, 3.2 NMI, and 3.1 ARI; it also dominates DGG, C-IDEC, SDEC, and VaDE by wide margins (e.g., +12.3 ACC over VaDE on MNIST). The consistent monotonic improvement from k=1 to k=10 across all tasks confirms that higher-order instance-level correlations provide a genuine expressiveness gain over both mean-field and first-order tree-structured approximations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends TreeVI (Xiao & Su, NeurIPS 2024), which captures first-order (pairwise) instance-level correlations via a tree-structured backbone, by generalizing to k-order dependencies among latent variables. It also builds on CVAE's tree-structured variational distributions but departs from both by introducing a conditional correlation parameterization that guarantees positive definiteness of local marginal correlation matrices — a theoretical requirement that TreeVI's acyclic construction cannot satisfy when higher-order (cyclic) dependencies are introduced. The chain-to-tree backbone generalization and the decomposition of the global posterior into overlapping local marginals (Theorem 2.1) provide the mathematical scaffolding that makes k-order sampling tractable.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work addresses a concrete gap: mean-field VI ignores instance-level correlations entirely, and the only scalable structured alternatives (TreeVI, CVAE) are confined to pairwise dependencies, which are insufficient for domains like financial time series, sensor networks, and evolving graphs where higher-order interactions dominate. The conditional correlation reparameterization is a clean, reusable technique for constructing valid correlation matrices of arbitrary size, and the linear-in-k computational scaling makes the method practical. Open questions include combining instance-level and dimension-level correlations simultaneously, extending beyond tree/chain backbones to general graphs, and understanding when the diminishing returns observed at k=50–100 signal a fundamental expressiveness ceiling versus a data-dependent limit.
