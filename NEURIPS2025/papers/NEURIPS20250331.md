## Problem

Understanding how noise or measurement errors affect the top-p spectral structure of a matrix is critical for robust and private low-rank approximation, yet prior analyses have been limited to Frobenius-norm or reconstruction-error metrics that can over- or under-estimate true subspace distortion. The specific open question raised in Mangoubi-Vishnoi [29, Remark 5.3]—whether high-probability spectral norm bounds for ||Ã_p − A_p|| can be obtained under natural eigengap assumptions and realistic (non-Gaussian) noise models—remained unresolved, with existing bounds either holding only in expectation, requiring Gaussian noise, or failing to capture the A–E interaction that the classical Eckart-Young-Mirsky theorem ignores.

## Input / Output

The method takes a real symmetric (or PSD) matrix A ∈ ℝ^{n×n} with eigenvalues λ_1 ≥ … ≥ λ_n, a symmetric perturbation E (e.g., Wigner or Rademacher noise), and a rank parameter p, and produces a high-probability upper bound on the spectral norm error ||Ã_p − A_p||. The bound is expressed in terms of the eigengap δ_p = λ_p − λ_{p+1}, the eigenvalue λ_p, and ||E||, with a refined version additionally depending on the halving distance r (spectral decay rate) and the noise-eigenspace alignment x = max_{i,j≤r}|u_i^T E u_j|. The gap condition 4||E|| ≤ δ_p must hold for the bound to apply, and the framework extends to arbitrary entire spectral functionals f(A) beyond the identity.

## Method

The core technique is a novel contour bootstrapping argument that replaces series expansions and Davis-Kahan-type perturbation analysis with a complex-analytic approach. First, the perturbation f_p(Ã) − f_p(A) is represented via a Cauchy-Dunford contour integral over a rectangle Γ separating the top-p eigenvalues of A and Ã from the rest. Second, a key bootstrapping lemma (Lemma 3.1) shows that under the gap condition 4||E|| ≤ δ_p, the full perturbation integral F(f) is bounded by 2F_1(f), where F_1 involves only the simpler quantity (zI−A)^{−1}E(zI−A)^{−1}; this is achieved by rewriting F(f) via the Sherman-Morrison-Woodbury identity and exploiting that max_{z∈Γ}||(zI−A)^{−1}E|| ≤ 1/2, so the self-referential inequality F ≤ F_1 + (1/2)F closes. Third, the rectangular contour is carefully positioned (left edge at the midpoint of the p-th eigengap, right edge and height at 2λ_1) so that the dominant contribution comes from the left vertical segment, yielding the tight O(||E||·λ_p/δ_p) rate. The refined bound (Theorem 2.2) further exploits spectral decay by splitting the spectral expansion of (zI−A)^{−1} into top-r and tail eigenvectors, isolating the interaction term r²x that captures noise-eigenspace alignment.

## Results

Under the eigengap condition 4‖E‖ ≤ δ_p, the paper proves the spectral norm bound ‖Ã_p − A_p‖ ≤ O(‖E‖ · λ_p/δ_p) (Theorem 2.1), improving the classical Eckart-Young-Mirsky bound O(‖E‖ + λ_{p+1}) by a factor of min{λ_{p+1}/‖E‖, δ_p/‖E‖} when λ_{p+1} ≫ ‖E‖. For Wigner noise with ‖E‖ = O(√n), this yields O(√n · λ_p/δ_p) with high probability (Corollary 2.4), resolving an open question in [30, Remark 5.3]. A refined interaction-aware bound (Theorem 2.2) further improves to Õ(‖E‖ + r²x · λ_p/δ_p), giving up to a √n-factor gain when the noise is approximately orthogonal to the leading eigenspace. Empirically, the concrete bound 7‖E‖·λ_p/δ_p tracks the actual spectral error within one order of magnitude across all three benchmark datasets (Census n=69, KDD n=416, Adult n=6) under both Gaussian and Rademacher noise at 20 escalation levels.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Eckart-Young-Mirsky bound 2(‖E‖ + λ_{p+1}), the proposed Theorem 2.1 improves by a factor of min{λ_{p+1}/‖E‖, δ_p/‖E‖} whenever λ_{p+1} ≫ ‖E‖; in the concrete example with spectrum {10n, 9n, …, n, n/2, 1, …, 1} and p=10 under Gaussian noise, EYM gives O(n) error while the new bound gives O(√n), a √n-factor gain. Against the Mangoubi-Vishnoi Frobenius-norm bounds [29, 30], the paper eliminates the √p factor, upgrades from expectation to high probability, extends from complex Gaussian (GUE) to both real and complex Wigner noise, and delivers a spectral-norm guarantee rather than a Frobenius-norm one. Empirically (Figures 1–2), the proposed bound 7‖E‖·λ_p/δ_p closely matches the observed spectral error across all three datasets and both noise models, while the EYM bound is consistently looser by a large margin. The refined Theorem 2.2 additionally outperforms the Hardt-Price iterative method [17, 18] by a factor of Õ(λ_1/λ_p) in the localized-eigenvector regime and by O(min{√n/r², λ_1/δ_p}) in the delocalized regime.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the classical Eckart-Young-Mirsky perturbation framework and the Dyson Brownian motion approach of Mangoubi-Vishnoi [29, 30] by replacing the random-matrix machinery with a contour-integral technique. The key methodological lineage traces to Tran and Vu's [37] contour bootstrapping argument for Davis-Kahan-type eigenspace perturbation, which the authors generalize from the indicator function f(z)=1 to arbitrary entire functions f(z), enabling analysis of matrix powers, exponentials, and trigonometric transforms. The paper departs from prior work in three ways: upgrading from Frobenius-norm expectations to spectral-norm high-probability bounds, removing the √p factor present in [29, 30], and supporting real-valued non-Gaussian noise models rather than restricting to GOE/GUE ensembles.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result resolves an explicit open question from [29, Remark 5.3] and provides the first high-probability spectral norm utility guarantee for the standard Gaussian-mechanism DP-PCA pipeline, which is the most widely deployed private PCA method. The contour bootstrapping lemma (F(f) ≤ 2F₁(f)) is a clean, reusable analytical tool that avoids series-expansion convergence arguments and may transfer to other spectral perturbation problems. Open questions include extending to Schatten-p or Ky Fan norms, handling structured (sparse or correlated) noise, and identifying the precise threshold beyond which the gap assumption 4‖E‖ ≤ δ_p is no longer needed for meaningful improvement over EYM.
