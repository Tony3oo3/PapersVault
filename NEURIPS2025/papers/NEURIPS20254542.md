## Problem

In logit-based knowledge distillation, the distillation term (whether KL divergence or correlation-based) is always added to cross-entropy with a separate mixing weight that requires careful manual tuning, and removing cross-entropy entirely degrades performance. The paper asks whether a single unified objective can simultaneously enforce top-1 correctness and transfer the teacher's full inter-class ranking structure, with weights derived automatically from the teacher's own confidence rather than hand-tuned hyperparameters.

## Input / Output

Given an input image, the pretrained teacher's C-dimensional logit vector, and the ground-truth class label, the method constructs a teacher-optimal permutation by sorting the teacher logits in descending order and forcing the true label to position 1. The student network then minimizes a weighted Plackett-Luce negative log-likelihood over this fixed permutation, producing a standard C-way class prediction. The only additional computational cost beyond standard KD is a single O(C log C) sort of the teacher logits per example.

## Method

PLD recasts distillation under the Plackett-Luce choice model by interpreting teacher logits as 'worth' scores. It constructs a single teacher-optimal permutation π* that places the ground-truth label first and ranks all remaining classes by descending teacher confidence, then minimizes the negative log-likelihood of π* under the student's logits. Crucially, each sequential selection step k is weighted by the teacher's softmax probability at that position (α_k = q^T_{π*_k}), so the loss automatically emphasizes top-1 correctness when the teacher is confident and relaxes when its distribution is flat. This yields a convex, translation-invariant, smoothly differentiable surrogate that subsumes standard cross-entropy (α = one-hot), ListMLE (uniform weights), and P-ListMLE (fixed decreasing weights) as special cases, eliminating the need for a separate cross-entropy term or any manual weight tuning.

## Results

On CIFAR-100, PLD achieves 73.50% Top-1 (WRN-40-2→WRN-40-1), 70.28% (ResNet-56→ResNet-20), 73.97% (ResNet-32×4→ResNet-8×4), and 68.16% (ResNet-50→MobileNetV2), outperforming all logit-based and feature-based baselines. On ImageNet-1K, PLD reaches 77.30% Top-1 for ResNet-50←ResNet-152 (+0.70 pp over DIST, +0.50 pp over KD) and 75.63% for ViT-Small←ViT-Large (+0.72 pp over DIST). On MS-COCO object detection, PLD achieves 32.47 AP (ResNet-101→ResNet-18-FPN), surpassing DKD by +0.36 AP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PLD consistently outperforms the two primary logit-based baselines across all settings: on CIFAR-100 it gains +0.76 to +1.71 pp over KD [12] and +0.54 to +0.76 pp over DIST [13]; on ImageNet-1K the margins are +0.30 to +1.55 pp over KD and +0.34 to +0.70 pp over DIST. Against DKD [52], PLD wins on CIFAR-100 (e.g., 73.50 vs. 73.22 for WRN-40-2→WRN-40-1) and on MS-COCO detection (32.47 vs. 32.11 AP for ResNet-101→ResNet-18-FPN), though it is slightly behind DKD on the ResNet-50→MobileNetV2-FPN detection pair (28.91 vs. 29.24 AP). Among feature-based methods on CIFAR-100, PLD surpasses SP [38] (73.50 vs. 73.49), PKT [27] (73.50 vs. 71.43), AT [51] (73.50 vs. 71.99), FitNet [32], RKD [26], and VID [1] by 1.5–6.2 pp. Notably, PLD requires no separate cross-entropy term or manual weight tuning, whereas KD and DIST both depend on a CE mixing weight α that must be tuned (optimal near α≈0.1), and DIST additionally requires inter-class β and intra-class γ weights.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper recasts logit-based knowledge distillation through the lens of the Plackett-Luce choice model, building directly on the ListMLE listwise ranking loss (Xia et al., 2008) and its position-aware variant P-ListMLE (Lan et al., 2014). It departs from the dominant KD paradigm—where a KL-divergence or correlation term is added to cross-entropy with a manually tuned mixing weight—by deriving a single teacher-optimal permutation (true label first, remaining classes in descending teacher confidence) and optimizing its Plackett-Luce likelihood with confidence-weighted selection steps. This unifies cross-entropy, ListMLE, and P-ListMLE as special cases and eliminates the separate CE term and its associated hyperparameter, positioning PLD as a principled alternative to both divergence-based (KD, DKD) and correlation-based (DIST, RKD) distillation objectives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PLD offers a practically appealing simplification: a single loss with no CE mixing weight to tune, grounded in a convex, translation-invariant surrogate whose weights are data-driven (teacher softmax masses) rather than hand-crafted. The consistent gains across CIFAR-100, ImageNet-1K, and MS-COCO, and across homogeneous and heterogeneous pairs, suggest the ranking structure carries information that marginal-matching losses discard. However, the method requires aligned class vocabularies, incurs O(C log C) sorting overhead, and its benefits diminish when the teacher's distribution is near-uniform. Open questions include extending PLD to mismatched label sets, adaptive or curriculum-driven weighting, and domains beyond classification such as sequence modeling or reinforcement learning.
