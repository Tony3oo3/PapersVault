## Problem

Directly applying GRPO-style online reinforcement learning to MLLMs suffers from two compounding failures: sparse rewards (most generated reasoning paths receive zero reward, especially early in training) and advantage vanishing (homogeneous all-correct or all-incorrect response groups drive relative advantages to zero, halting learning). No prior work addresses both issues simultaneously through a unified mechanism for MLLM reasoning RL.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252688/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives an RGB image paired with a text question and produces a step-by-step reasoning trajectory followed by a final answer, with a format reward encouraging the structured chain-of-thought format. During training, the input question is expanded into m semantically equivalent variants via offline textual rewrites (GPT-4o paraphrasing) and online multimodal transformations (e.g., 90° rotation, noise injection applied to the image with a corresponding textual prompt appended), so the policy is conditioned on multiple visually and linguistically distinct but answer-equivalent inputs per original question.

## Method

Share-GRPO introduces information sharing into MLLM RL through three coupled mechanisms. First, Semantically Consistent Transformation (SCT) expands each question into m variants via offline textual rewrites and online image-level perturbations, enlarging the solution space the policy explores. Second, a hierarchical advantage estimator computes a global advantage over all mn responses pooled across variants and a local advantage within each variant's n responses, then combines them (local added only for self-variant pairs) to yield more stable, fine-grained relative advantages. Third, the shared policy optimization objective (Eq. 8) updates the policy using every response o_i^{Q_j} under every variant context Q_k, enabling cross-variant credit assignment where a correct trajectory discovered on one variant reinforces the policy for all semantically equivalent variants. This design increases the probability of at least one positive-reward response per group (mitigating sparse reward) and preserves reward variance across the expanded set (mitigating advantage vanishing), without requiring step-wise reward models or additional cold-start SFT.

## Results

R1-ShareVL-7B achieves an average score of 61.2 across 6 reasoning benchmarks, outperforming the Qwen2.5-VL-7B base model (58.1) by +3.1 points and the strongest RL-trained 7B competitor MM-Eureka-7B (59.1) by +2.1 points. On the most challenging mathematical benchmark MathVista, R1-ShareVL-7B scores 75.4, surpassing Vision-R1-7B (73.5) and ThinkLite-7B (74.3). Scaling to 32B parameters, R1-ShareVL-32B reaches 67.2 average, improving over MM-Eureka-32B (63.8) by +3.4 points and achieving +9.1% over the base model on MathVerse.

## Baselines & Comparisons

R1-ShareVL-7B outperforms all RL-trained 7B MLLM baselines on average: it beats MM-Eureka-7B by +2.1 (61.2 vs 59.1), ThinkLite-7B by +1.9, and Vision-R1-7B by +1.5. The most notable margin is on MMMU, where R1-ShareVL-7B (58.1) surpasses ThinkLite-7B (53.1) by +5.0 and Vision-R1-7B (54.2) by +3.9, indicating stronger multi-discipline generalization. On MathVista, R1-ShareVL-7B (75.4) leads ThinkLite-7B (74.3) by +1.1 and Vision-R1-7B (73.5) by +1.9. At 32B scale, R1-ShareVL-32B (67.2 avg) improves over MM-Eureka-32B (63.8) by +3.4, with the largest gap on MathVision (+5.9) and MMMU (+5.5). Notably, R1-ShareVL-7B also surpasses GPT-4o (60.9 avg) by +0.3 on average, though GPT-4o retains a lead on MMMU (70.7 vs 58.1).

## Relation to Prior Work

Share-GRPO is a direct extension of GRPO (from DeepSeekMath/DeepSeek-R1) to the multimodal setting, where prior RL-based MLLM methods (Vision-R1, MM-Eureka, R1-VL, R1-V) have applied rule-based reward RL but suffer from sparse rewards and advantage vanishing. The key departure is introducing information sharing across semantically consistent question variants: the paper expands the question space via offline textual rewrites (GPT-4o) and online multimodal transformations (rotation, noise), then shares both reasoning trajectories and reward signals across variants during policy optimization. The hierarchical advantage estimation (global across all variants + local within each variant) is a new design that goes beyond GRPO's single-level group-relative computation, drawing conceptually from multi-task RL literature on shared value functions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses two practical bottlenecks in MLLM RL training—sparse rewards and advantage vanishing—that limit the effectiveness of GRPO-style methods, particularly for smaller models and early in training. The approach is immediately deployable (no cold-start SFT, compatible with EasyR1) and shows consistent gains across 6 benchmarks at both 7B and 32B scales, with the notable finding that 6 responses per question under Share-GRPO outperforms 24 responses under vanilla GRPO. Open questions include whether the semantically consistent transformation approach generalizes to more complex or open-ended multimodal tasks, how robust the method is to transformation quality (the authors acknowledge GPT-4o can produce low-quality rewrites), and whether the information-sharing principle extends to other RL paradigms beyond GRPO.
