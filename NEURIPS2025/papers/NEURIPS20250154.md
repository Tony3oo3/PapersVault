## Problem

Existing image-text claim verification datasets are either synthetic (manipulated image-text pairs that fail to generalize to real-world claims) or real-world but lack annotated evidence, making it impossible to evaluate models' reasoning. No prior benchmark simultaneously provides context-independent real-world image-text claims, temporally constrained web evidence, and decomposed QA annotations that capture the verification rationale.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250154/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a normalized image-text claim (textual statement paired with one or more RGB images, made self-contained with metadata such as speaker, date, and location) and produces a four-way veracity label (supported, refuted, not enough evidence, conflicting/cherry-picking), a textual justification referencing evidence images via special tokens (e.g., [IMG_1]), and a sequence of QA pairs where both questions and answers may be multimodal. Each non-trivial answer must be backed by a source URL archived on the Internet Archive, and all evidence is restricted to content published before the claim date to prevent temporal leakage.

## Method

The dataset is constructed via a five-phase annotation pipeline: claim extraction and normalization from fact-checking articles, QA annotation using temporally constrained web search (custom Google interface restricted to pre-claim-date results), an independent evidence sufficiency check by a third annotator who assigns a verdict solely from the QA pairs, and two rounds of refinement for claims with verdict discrepancies. The baseline verification system pairs an LLM with an MLLM and operates through four components: a question generator (parallel, dynamic, or hybrid strategies), an answer generator equipped with four specialized tools (reverse image search, web text search, web image search, and visual QA), a verifier that predicts the veracity label from accumulated evidence context, and a justification generator. Evaluation employs a novel reference-based multimodal evidence scoring method using Gemini-2.0-Flash to compare retrieved evidence against ground-truth QA pairs separately for textual and visual components, reporting conditional verdict accuracy only when evidence scores exceed a threshold λ.

## Results

On AVERIMATEC, the best baseline (Gemini + Gemini with Hybrid Question Generation) achieves a question recall of 0.36 and evidence recall of 0.19, with conditional verdict accuracy of 0.18 at evidence threshold λ=0.2. Evidence retrieval scores are substantially lower than question generation scores across all models (e.g., Gemma + Gemma HQA: Q-Eval 0.26 vs. Evid-Eval 0.25), highlighting the difficulty of retrieving appropriate web evidence for image-text verification. With ground-truth evidence, the Gemini-based baseline reaches 0.82 overall verdict accuracy, while without any external search the best model drops to 0.02, underscoring the critical role of web retrieval.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper establishes four LLM/MLLM baseline combinations (Gemini, Qwen+Qwen-VL, Gemma, Qwen+LLaVA) under three question generation strategies (Parallel, Dynamic, Hybrid). For question generation, the Parallel strategy consistently outperforms Dynamic (e.g., Qwen+Qwen-VL: 0.43 vs. 0.27 Q-Eval), suggesting that reasoning over evolving interleaved image-text evidence remains challenging for current MLLMs. For evidence retrieval, Gemma+Gemma with Hybrid generation achieves the highest Evid-Eval (0.25), while Gemini+Gemini with Dynamic generation leads in conditional verdict accuracy (0.16 at λ=0.3). All baselines fail to identify any conflicting/cherry-picking claims (0.00 accuracy on that class even with ground-truth evidence). Without external search, performance collapses to near-zero (0.02 overall), confirming that web-based retrieval is indispensable for real-world image-text claim verification.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the AVeriTeC annotation pipeline and four-way veracity labeling schema (Schlichtkrull et al., 2023) from text-only claims to image-text claims, and adapts the Ev2R reference-based evidence evaluation framework (Akhtar et al., 2024) to a multimodal setting where evidence may include images. It departs from prior image-text fact-checking datasets (Fauxtography, Fakeddit, FakeNewsNet) by providing real-world, context-independent claims with temporally constrained web evidence and QA-decomposed reasoning paths, and from synthetic datasets (NewsCLIPpings, Verite, Autosplice) by using genuinely verified claims rather than manipulated pairs. The QA decomposition of verification rationale, while inspired by ClaimDecomp and QACheck for text claims, is the first to be applied to multimodal image-text verification with both image-related questions and image answers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

With approximately 80% of online claims being multimodal, the absence of a real-world image-text fact-checking benchmark with evidence annotations has been a significant gap; AVERIMATEC addresses this by providing 1,297 annotated claims with QA pairs, source URLs, and a two-stage evidence sufficiency check. The conditional evaluation paradigm (verdict accuracy only counted when evidence score exceeds a threshold) is a meaningful shift from pure classification accuracy, forcing systems to justify their verdicts with retrieved evidence. Key open questions include how to improve evidence retrieval for image-centric claims (current baselines score only 0.12–0.25 on evidence recall), how to handle conflicting evidence across multiple QA pairs, and whether the QA format is the optimal reasoning representation for multimodal verification. The dataset's small scale, heavy label imbalance (78–95% refuted), and geographic bias (India and US dominate) limit generalizability and suggest future work on scaling and balancing.
