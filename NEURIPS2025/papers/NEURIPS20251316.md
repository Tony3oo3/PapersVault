## Problem

Selective classification (learning when to abstain) and the omniprediction/multigroup fairness frameworks have developed largely in parallel: reliable agnostic learning was restricted to the 0-1 loss, while multicalibration-based omnipredictors had no mechanism for abstention. This paper asks whether a single classifier can be built that simultaneously minimizes generalized Chow losses across an entire family of loss functions, and whether abstention can be made fair across an arbitrary collection of intersecting subgroups.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes labeled (x, y) pairs drawn from a distribution D, a concept class C (e.g., decision trees of depth 3), a family of loss functions L where each loss induces a triplet (ℓ+, ℓ−, ℓ?) with associated weights (λ, μ, ν), and optionally a collection of subgroups G. It outputs a single base predictor h: X → [0,1] that, when composed with a loss-specific post-processing function k*_{ℓ±,ℓ?}, yields a selective classifier h?: X → {0,1,?}. The abstention region I_abs is a contiguous interval in [0,1] determined entirely by the chosen loss triplet (independent of the data), and in the multigroup setting the output additionally satisfies group-conditional coverage guarantees for every g ∈ G.

## Method

The construction proceeds in two stages. First, a (C,ε)-multicalibrated predictor h is built (or, for the fair variant, a (C·G, ε²/8)-multiaccurate and globally calibrated predictor). Second, for each specific loss triplet and weight vector, a post-processing function k*_{ℓ±,ℓ?} maps each predicted probability p to either the optimal prediction t*(p) (the minimizer of the weighted prediction loss under Bernoulli(p)) or to '?', whichever incurs lower expected generalized Chow loss. The key structural result is that the set of p-values where abstention is optimal forms a contiguous interval I_abs that depends only on the loss functions, not on the data, making the post-processing a simple, data-independent thresholding step. For the multigroup fair variant, the predictor is thresholded so that h(x) ∈ (ε, 1−ε) maps to '?', and the multiaccuracy guarantee for C·G ensures the abstention rate within each group g is no worse than the optimal selective classifier in SC(C) restricted to g, while global calibration preserves accuracy on the predicted region.

## Results

On synthetic binary classification data (n=10,000, decision trees of depth 3 as base class), the single selective omnipredictor post-processed with k*_{ℓ±,ℓ?} consistently achieves lower total generalized Chow loss than decision trees trained from scratch for each specific loss triplet, while also covering substantially more of the domain. For example, under ℓ₁ with abstention cost 0.2, the omnipredictor achieves total loss 0.139 at 52.7% coverage versus 0.194 at only 3.8% coverage for the dedicated decision tree. Under ℓ₂ with asymmetric abstention costs (0.3/0.1), the omnipredictor reaches total loss 0.122 at 64.6% coverage versus 0.193 at 13% coverage. The abstention interval I_abs computed analytically from the loss triplet matches the empirically observed abstention region in all tested configurations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its single selective omnipredictor (a multicalibrated predictor post-processed with the loss-specific function k*) against decision trees of depth 3 trained from scratch to minimize the generalized Chow loss for each specific triplet of loss functions. Across all six tested configurations (ℓ₁ and ℓ₂ with symmetric and asymmetric abstention costs), the omnipredictor achieves strictly lower total generalized Chow loss than the dedicated decision tree in every case, with margins ranging from 0.010 (ℓ₁, cost 0.1) to 0.071 (ℓ₁, cost 0.2). The decision trees achieve lower predicted loss on their (very small) non-abstaining regions but abstain on 96–98% of the domain, whereas the omnipredictor covers 22–65% of the domain. This demonstrates that a single predictor, trained once via multicalibration and then post-processed per loss, can outperform loss-specific training while also providing a data-independent abstention interval I_abs that is computable in closed form.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate lines of work: the omniprediction framework (Gopalan et al., 2021), which constructs a single predictor post-processable for any loss in a family via multicalibration, and the reliable agnostic learning framework (Kalai, Kanade, Mansour, 2012), which formalizes selective classification with abstention for the 0-1 loss. The key conceptual move is to show that the omniprediction post-processing step naturally extends to the selective setting by decomposing into a prediction-optimal step followed by a simple predict-vs-abstain comparison under a Bernoulli distribution, yielding a contiguous abstention interval determined entirely by the loss triplet. The paper further extends multigroup fairness (Hébert-Johnson et al., 2018; Gopalan et al., 2022) to the selective setting, showing that the weaker primitive of calibrated multiaccuracy suffices for fair abstention, in contrast to the full multicalibration required for selective omniprediction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first learning-theoretic framework that simultaneously handles multi-loss optimality, fair abstention across intersecting groups, and conformal prediction guarantees (both marginal and group-conditional) within a single unified construction. This is practically relevant for high-stakes deployment where one needs provable per-group abstention bounds without retraining for each loss or group. Key open questions include whether multicalibration can be relaxed to calibrated multiaccuracy for selective omniprediction, whether multigroup selective classifiers can be built from separate weak learners for C and G rather than their intersection, and whether the ε² overhead in going from agnostic to fully reliable learning is unavoidable. The empirical evaluation is limited to synthetic data with depth-3 decision trees, so practical validation on real-world datasets remains to be done.
