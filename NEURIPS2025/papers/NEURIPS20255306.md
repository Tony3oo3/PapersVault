## Problem

Existing 3D asset retrieval methods for scene generation rely on general-purpose shape representation models that treat objects in isolation, overlooking spatial relationships, semantic coherence, and stylistic consistency with the surrounding scene. Additionally, no standardized retrieval paradigm exists for 3D assets that supports arbitrary combinations of text, image, and point-cloud queries, leaving a gap between the flexible multi-modal querying needed for interactive scene editing and the rigid single-modality pipelines of prior work.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255306/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The query side accepts any subset of text, rendered multi-view images (11 orthogonal views per asset), and 3D point clouds, optionally augmented with a scene layout graph whose nodes carry 3D coordinates and text-derived features and whose edges encode both physical relations (e.g., support, adjacency) and LLM-generated semantic relations. The gallery side consists of precomputed fixed-dimension embeddings for all 48K Objaverse-LVIS assets. At inference, the system outputs a single retrieved 3D asset per query step; in iterative mode it sequentially composes a full room by updating the scene graph after each placement, with the ESSGNN recomputing the layout embedding at every step.

## Method

MetaFind is a dual-tower retrieval framework built on the ULIP-2 tri-modal backbone, where the gallery encoder is frozen after pretraining and the query encoder flexibly fuses any modality subset via a Transformer-based fusion module. The key architectural contribution is ESSGNN, a plug-and-play layout encoder that extends EGNN message passing by incorporating semantic edge embeddings (derived from LLM-generated relation sentences encoded by a frozen text encoder) alongside geometric edges, while preserving full SE(3) equivariance so scene embeddings remain stable under arbitrary rotations and translations. Training proceeds in two stages: first, cross-modal alignment pretraining on Objaverse-LVIS with 30% stochastic modality masking to handle partial inputs; second, layout-aware fine-tuning on ProcTHOR room graphs using a bidirectional contrastive objective, where the layout embedding is injected residually (e_query = Fusion(e_text, e_img, e_pc) + λ·e_layout) with a learnable scalar λ and 30% stochastic scene dropout to maintain robustness when layout is unavailable. At inference, an iterative composition loop retrieves and places one asset at a time, updating the scene graph and recomputing the ESSGNN embedding after each insertion to ensure subsequent retrievals account for the evolving spatial context.

## Results

On Objaverse-LVIS (48K assets), MetaFind without ESSGNN achieves 51.7% R@1 and 76.5% R@5 under full modality (text+image+point cloud), substantially outperforming the strongest baseline OmniBind(Full) at 11.9% R@1. Under text-only queries, MetaFind reaches 13.8% R@1 versus 5.3% for OmniBind(Full) and 1.7% for Uni3D. On scene-level quality (ProcTHOR, 200 scenes), MetaFind with ESSGNN scores 4.10–4.25 (GPT-4o / human) across aesthetic, color/material, scene coherence, and realism dimensions, compared to 2.76–3.28 for ULIP and 3.01–3.28 for OpenShape.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MetaFind w/o ESSGNN dominates all six baselines on cross-modal retrieval: on the full-modality (T+I+PC) setting it reaches 51.7% R@1, compared to 11.9% for OmniBind(Full), 8.2% for Uni3D, 7.0% for OpenShape, and 6.4% for ULIP. On text-only queries, MetaFind (13.8%) more than doubles the best baseline SCA3D (6.9%) and is 2.6× OmniBind(Full) (5.3%). On the T+PC setting, MetaFind (44.5%) outperforms SCA3D (39.7%) and Uni3DL (37.4%), the only baselines supporting that combination. Note that PC-only numbers are inflated for single-tower baselines (≈98–99%) because they reuse identical embeddings for query and gallery; MetaFind's dual-tower design yields a lower but more meaningful 75.1% R@1. On scene-level quality (Table 2), MetaFind w/ ESSGNN leads all methods by a wide margin—e.g., 4.10 GPT-4o scene coherence vs. 3.01 for OpenShape and 2.76 for ULIP—confirming that the ESSGNN layout encoder translates retrieval gains into tangible improvements in composed-scene realism and stylistic consistency.

## Relation to Prior Work

MetaFind extends the tri-modal 3D embedding space established by ULIP-2 into a dual-tower retrieval architecture, drawing an explicit analogy to Dense Passage Retrieval in NLP but adapting it for 3D assets. Its core architectural novelty, ESSGNN, generalizes the EGNN equivariant message-passing framework by injecting LLM-derived semantic edge embeddings alongside geometric distances, bridging the gap between purely spatial GNNs and purely semantic graph models. The paper departs from prior 3D retrieval methods (OpenShape, SCA3D, Uni3D, OmniBind) that treat each query as an independent, object-centric, single-modality event, instead framing retrieval as an iterative, layout-conditioned process where each placed asset updates the scene graph and influences subsequent retrievals.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work addresses a genuine gap: no prior retrieval paradigm explicitly incorporates evolving scene layout as a first-class query signal for 3D asset selection, which is critical for coherent multi-object scene construction in metaverse and VR applications. The SE(3)-equivariant design is practically important for deployment in unnormalized, open-world coordinate systems where standard GNNs degrade. However, the evaluation is limited to single-room indoor scenes from ProcTHOR, annotations depend on GPT-4o (introducing potential bias and hallucination), and no code is released, leaving open questions about scalability to multi-room or outdoor environments and the robustness of the GPT-4o-generated scene graphs.
