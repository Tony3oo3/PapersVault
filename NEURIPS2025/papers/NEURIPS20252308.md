## Problem

The known correspondence between modern Hopfield networks and Transformer self-attention was established only under the adiabatic approximation, which discards the hidden state dynamics of the Hopfield network. This paper asks whether retaining those dynamics beyond the adiabatic limit yields a more general and practically useful mapping to Transformer layers. A secondary motivation is the well-known rank collapse (token uniformity) problem in deep Transformers, where token representations rapidly lose diversity with increasing depth.

## Input / Output

For text generation (GPT-2, LLaMA), the input is a token sequence and the output is a next-token distribution evaluated by perplexity on WikiText-103, CNN DailyMail, or BookCorpus. For image classification (ViT), the input is a standard RGB image (32×32 for CIFAR, 224×224 for ImageNet) and the output is a class label. The unusual structural detail is that a hidden-state tensor H of shape (T×T) propagates across all attention layers, accumulating attention scores QK^T via an exponential moving average; the actual attention weights are computed as softmax(H) rather than softmax(QK^T) directly, and no new trainable parameters are introduced.

## Method

The paper derives a new attention mechanism, Modern Hopfield Attention (MHA), by discretizing the MCHN update rule without taking the adiabatic limit, yielding two coupled updates: x_{n+1} = α x_n + (1−α) softmax(h_n) V_n and h_n = α′ h_{n−1} + (1−α′) q_n K_n^T. The hidden state h acts as a cross-layer exponential moving average of attention scores, so each layer's attention weights are informed by scores accumulated from all previous layers, creating coordinated attention behavior across depth. Theoretically, the paper proves (Theorem 5.2) that even in a skip-connection-free attention-only network, a non-zero α′ reduces the residual norm decay from double-exponential (as in standard self-attention) to at most linear, directly mitigating rank collapse. Empirically, replacing standard self-attention with MHA (α=α′=0.5 or 0.7) improves perplexity on GPT-2 and LLaMA, boosts top-1 accuracy on CIFAR-100 and ImageNet-1k for ViT, and dramatically reduces token cosine-similarity peaks at 1.0, all without adding any trainable parameters and with only O(T²) additional computation per layer.

## Results

On GPT-2 trained on WikiText-103, MHA reduces perplexity from 22.87 to 20.70 (Small, 124M) and from 20.85 to 19.61 (Medium, 350M) compared to standard self-attention. On CIFAR-100 with ViT-Large (303M), MHA (α=0.5) improves top-1 accuracy from 72.91% to 75.78%, and on ImageNet-1k with ViT-B (86M), accuracy rises from 76.07% to 77.06% (α=0.7). In skip-free depth experiments, MHA maintains usable performance up to 8 layers (80.34% CIFAR-10) while standard self-attention collapses to 48.59% at the same depth.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is standard dot-product self-attention (Vaswani et al., 2017), which MHA replaces directly without adding trainable parameters. MHA consistently outperforms self-attention on perplexity across all text generation settings: GPT-2 Small improves by 2.17 points (22.87→20.70), GPT-2 Medium by 1.24 points (20.85→19.61), and LLaMA improves on all three datasets (WikiText-103: 14.49→14.29, DailyMail: 19.36→18.97, BookCorpus: 23.76→23.50). On image classification, MHA wins on CIFAR-100 for ViT-Small and above (e.g., ViT-Large: 72.91→75.78) and on ImageNet-1k (76.07→77.06), though CIFAR-10 shows negligible differences due to task saturation. On downstream transfer tasks, MHA achieves large gains on Flowers-102 (81.15→93.85) and Stanford Cars (51.54→87.54), but underperforms on Stanford Dogs (95.00→83.64), suggesting the benefit is task-dependent. Overall, MHA wins or ties on the majority of comparisons, with the most consistent and largest gains in text generation and larger-model image classification.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the MHN–Transformer correspondence established by Ramsauer et al. (2021) and Krotov & Hopfield (2021), which showed that self-attention matches the *adiabatic limit* of modern continuous Hopfield networks. The key departure is that the adiabatic approximation discards the hidden-state dynamics; by retaining them, the authors derive a new attention mechanism (MHA) in which a hidden state accumulates attention scores across layers via an exponential moving average. This positions MHA as a principled generalization of standard dot-product self-attention (Vaswani et al., 2017) rather than an ad-hoc architectural tweak, and it connects to the rank-collapse/oversmoothing literature (Dong et al., 2021; Noci et al., 2022) by providing a theoretical mechanism—bypassing the need for explicit skip connections or regularization—to mitigate token uniformity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a clean, parameter-free modification to self-attention that consistently improves perplexity and classification accuracy across GPT-2, LLaMA, and ViT architectures, with a theoretical guarantee that the hidden state converts double-exponential rank decay into linear decay. This suggests that the adiabatic approximation used in prior MHN–Transformer work was hiding a practically useful degree of freedom. Open questions include how MHA scales to very large models (experiments top out at ~350M parameters), whether the single hyperparameter α can be learned or made layer-dependent, and whether the modest gains (<1% on ImageNet, ~1–2 perplexity points) persist at frontier scale. The Stanford Dogs transfer result (83.64 vs. 95.00) also raises questions about when the hidden-state smoothing might be counterproductive.
