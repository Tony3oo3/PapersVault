## Problem

While persistent homology (PH) has been characterized for color-based filtrations on individual graphs, the expressive power of the Euler characteristic (EC) on general color-based filtrations remained uncharacterized, and it was unknown whether topological descriptors computed on graph products (e.g., box/Cartesian products) capture structural information beyond what is visible in the individual components. Additionally, no efficient algorithms existed for computing persistence diagrams under product filtrations, making the practical use of graph-product-based descriptors infeasible for large graphs.

## Input / Output

The method takes two colored graphs G and H (sharing a common color set X) along with vertex-level and/or edge-level filtration functions, and computes the 0-dimensional and 1-dimensional persistence diagrams of the box product G⊠H under the induced product filtration (G⊠H)_t = G_t ⊠ H_t. For graph classification, a single input graph is paired with a fixed set of seven reference graphs (cycles, random, complete), and the resulting persistence diagrams are vectorized via the PersLay Gaussian embedding scheme before being concatenated with GNN node embeddings. The EC diagram output is a list of Euler characteristic values at each critical filtration time, while the PH output consists of birth-death pairs for connected components (0-dim) and cycles (1-dim).

## Method

The paper establishes a complete combinatorial characterization of EC expressivity: two graphs have identical EC diagrams for all color-based filtrations if and only if they match on vertex color counts and edge color-pair counts (Theorem 2), and shows that the cheaper max-EC variant retains full expressivity (Theorem 1), a result that generalizes to colored simplicial complexes (Theorem 3). For graph products, the key contrast is that PH of G⊠G, G⊠H, H⊠H is strictly more expressive than PH of G and H individually (Proposition 4, Corollary 1 with virtual nodes), whereas EC of products adds no new information (Proposition 5). The core algorithmic contribution provides near-linear-time procedures to compute 0-dim PH diagrams of vertex-level product filtrations (Theorem 4, runtime O(n_G·n_H + m_G log m_G + m_H log m_H)) and edge-level product filtrations (Theorem 5), plus a general formula for 1-dim PH via the Betti number identity β₁(A⊠B) = |E(A)|·|E(B)| + β₀(A)β₀(B) − χ(A)χ(B) (Proposition 8). For GNN integration, topological features from graph products are computed against reference graphs, embedded via PersLay, and concatenated with GIN/GCN/GraphSAGE representations before a classification head.

## Results

On graph classification (Table 3), adding topological descriptors from graph products consistently improves GNN performance: GIN+GProd lifts DHFR accuracy from 80.53% to 82.89%, PROTEINS from 70.18% to 73.04%, and NCI109 from 78.74% to 80.97%, while reducing ZINC MAE from 0.57 to 0.54. On expressivity (Table 2), GProd^E with closeness filtration achieves 100% accuracy on BREC Basic(60) and Reg.(50) versus 93.33% and 86.00% for standard PH^E, and 94% vs 71% on Ext.(100). The proposed algorithms (Theorems 4 and 5) significantly outperform naive PH computation on the full graph product as graph sizes grow (Figure 3).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against standard PH (Immonen et al., NeurIPS 2023), GProd achieves strictly higher expressivity: with closeness filtration, GProd^E reaches 100% on BREC Basic(60) and Reg.(50) versus 93.33% and 86.00% for PH^E, and 94% vs 71% on Ext.(100); with clustering filtration, GProd^V improves c-63 from 82.50% to 90.00%. No dataset shows standard PH outperforming GProd. Against GIN (Xu et al., ICLR 2019), GIN+GProd improves on 5 of 6 datasets (e.g., DHFR +2.36%, PROTEINS +2.86%, NCI109 +2.23%), with GIN+ProdFilt matching on NCI1 but improving on COX2 and ZINC. Against GCN (Kipf et al., ICLR 2017), GCN+GProd gains the most on DHFR (+5.27%) and NCI109 (+0.92%), while GCN+ProdFilt excels on COX2 (+1.70%) and PROTEINS (+1.07%). Against GraphSAGE (Hamilton et al., NeurIPS 2017), SAGE+GProd and SAGE+ProdFilt both reduce ZINC MAE from 0.51 to 0.48 and improve NCI1 by ~1.3%, though SAGE already performs competitively on DHFR where neither variant gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the color-based filtration framework for persistent homology established by Immonen et al. [26] to the setting of graph box products, and complements the broader TDA-augmented GNN literature (Horn et al. [24], Ballester and Rieck [2]) by introducing a structural decomposition lens. It departs from prior work in two key ways: (1) providing a complete combinatorial characterization of Euler characteristic expressivity (Theorem 2), paralleling the PH characterization in [26] but for a simpler invariant, and (2) showing that graph products can be treated as a 'fractal' structure where topological descriptors on the product encode information beyond the individual components. The efficient product-filtration algorithms (Theorems 4, 5) build directly on the union-find PH machinery of [26] but avoid explicitly constructing the full product graph.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The clean separation between PH (which gains expressivity from products) and EC (which does not) provides a useful theoretical guide for practitioners choosing topological descriptors: if one wants to exploit product structure, PH is the right tool, while EC's expressivity is fully captured by component-level combinatorics. The runtime-efficient algorithms for product filtrations are practically valuable for large-scale graph products arising in relational databases and multi-way data. However, the empirical gains are modest and inconsistent (e.g., Jaccard-index filtrations show zero improvement in Table 2), and the scope is limited to box products with color-based filtrations. Open questions include whether other graph product types (tensor, strong, lexicographic) yield analogous PH benefits, whether the virtual-node trick of Corollary 1 can be made more practical without inflating graph size, and whether the product-filtration algorithms extend to higher-dimensional simplicial complexes.
