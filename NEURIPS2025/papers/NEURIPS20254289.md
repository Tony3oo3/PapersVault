## Problem

Existing HOI datasets and models focus on single-human-object interactions, while HHI datasets typically lack object context, leaving the coordinated behavior of multiple people interacting around a shared object (Human-Human-Object Interactions, HHOIs) largely unexplored. No dedicated dataset or generative model exists for synthesizing such dyadic or multi-human object interactions from textual descriptions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254289/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt (encoded via CLIP) and a fixed object instance mesh as input, and outputs a complete HHOI configuration: for each of N humans, a 6D rotation, 3D translation, scalar scale, and a 10D body pose embedding (decoded to 126D SMPL-X joint rotations via a learned MLP). The output is structured as N HOI samples (per-human relative to the object) plus M HHI samples (relative rotation/translation and body poses for each human pair), with the HHI graph constrained to be a DAG. The framework scales from dyadic to 5-person interactions and can feed into motion in-betweening via DNO to produce temporal motion sequences.

## Method

The core contribution is a decomposed score-based diffusion framework that separately models HOI (human pose/position relative to an object) and HHI (relative pose/position between human pairs) using Denoising Score Matching, then unifies them at inference through an advanced guided sampling procedure. The PF ODE is augmented with two constraint gradients: an inconsistency loss that minimizes variance in scale, body pose, rotation, and translation across overlapping human representations derived from both HOI and HHI samples, and a collision loss computed via a 24-capsule proxy per human to prevent inter-penetration for non-adjacent pairs. Body poses are compressed into a 10D latent space via a 4-layer MLP encoder/decoder trained on 922K poses, reducing the HOI sample to 20D and HHI sample to 29D. The approach is trained in a decoupled manner (separate HOI and HHI score networks) but sampled jointly, enabling arbitrary numbers of humans as long as the HHI dependency graph remains acyclic.

## Results

On dyadic HHOI generation (Table 1), the proposed method achieves a Body Pose FD of 0.1755 and Distance FD of 0.0689, substantially outperforming GenZI (1.3500 / 0.3542) and Depth Opt. (0.6834 / 0.4180). In the user study, 60.9% of participants preferred the proposed method's outputs over the two baselines (20.8% and 18.4%). For multi-human generation (Table 2), the method achieves a 100% success rate for 3, 4, and 5 humans, compared to 67.3%/41.4%/22% for GenZI and 33.3%/18%/13% for Depth Opt. Contact distance is also dramatically improved (0.029 m for 2 humans vs. 0.103 m for GenZI and 0.666 m for Depth Opt.).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two baselines: GenZI [33] (a multi-view inpainting + SMPL-X optimization pipeline adapted for multiple humans) and Depth Opt. (a single-view inpainting + depth-optimization approach). The proposed method decisively outperforms both on all primary metrics. On Body Pose FD, the proposed method (0.1755) is roughly 4× better than Depth Opt. (0.6834) and 7.7× better than GenZI (1.3500); on Distance FD it is 6× better than Depth Opt. and 5.1× better than GenZI. CLIP scores are close across all three methods (0.2633–0.2695), which the authors attribute to background rendering dominating the image-text similarity. In the multi-human setting, the gap widens dramatically: the proposed method achieves 100% success rate for 3–5 humans, while GenZI drops to 22% at 5 humans and Depth Opt. to 13%. On physical plausibility, the proposed method achieves the lowest contact distance (0.029 m) and the lowest human-object penetration ratio (5.49×10⁻³) for dyadic generation, though Depth Opt. shows artificially low penetration at 3+ humans due to placing humans excessively far from objects.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper decomposes the novel HHOI problem into two independently trained score-based diffusion models (HOI and HHI), then unifies them at inference via a PF-ODE guided sampling scheme that adds inconsistency and collision losses — a technique inherited from the PF-ODE augmentation paradigm used in object spatial relationship modeling [1] and dynamic affordance modeling [27]. The HOI component follows the object-centric formulation and 6D rotation representation common in recent HOI generation work (InterDiff, CG-HOI), while the HHI component parallels multi-human motion generation approaches like InterGen. For data, it extends the synthetic HOI pipeline of ComA [26] and the 2D-diffusion-to-3D-lifting strategy of GenZI [33], and uses DNO [25] with InterGen [35] as a downstream motion prior. The closest prior dataset, CORE4D [37], captures collaborative two-person-object interactions but is limited in scale and interaction diversity, motivating the authors' new multi-camera capture and synthetic augmentation pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By formally defining HHOI as a distinct problem and providing both a dedicated dataset and a clean generative framework, this paper opens a research direction that bridges the previously siloed HOI and HHI literatures. The guided-sampling technique (inconsistency + collision losses) is a practical recipe for enforcing cross-entity consistency in multi-body diffusion generation that could transfer to other multi-agent 3D generation tasks. Key limitations include the inability to directly leverage existing single-human HOI or dyadic HHI datasets due to distributional mismatch, the modest dataset size (5,078 frames, 11 categories), and the absence of released code. Open questions include whether the decomposition generalizes to more complex interaction topologies (e.g., multiple shared objects) and whether the capsule-based collision proxy suffices for fine-grained contact scenarios.
