## Problem

Existing evolutionary multi-view classification (EMVC) methods suffer from fitness evaluation bias (FEB), where the fitness evaluation step fails to correctly reflect the true performance ranking among individuals in the population. This bias misleads the evolutionary direction, causing the algorithm to trap in local optima. The paper identifies that joint training strategies in EMVC restrict exploration of key information in views with low information content, preventing the multi-view model from reaching optimal performance and thereby triggering FEB.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254801/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes multi-view features (e.g., audio features like ecapa/resnet/fbank/mfcc/spec, video+pose, RGB+depth, text+image, or multilingual text) and category labels as input, with view dimensions projected to a fixed fusion dimension (128–1024 depending on dataset). Individuals in the population are encoded as binary trees whose leaf nodes are views and branch nodes are fusion operators (concatenation, addition, multiplication, max, average). The output is the optimal multi-view model (MVM) and its classification accuracy (or weighted F1 for the multi-label MM-IMDB dataset), with the ENs serving as pre-trained frozen teacher models retrieved per-view during training.

## Method

EFB-EMVC equips each view branch in the MVM with an evolutionary navigator (EN), a pre-trained two-layer DNN teacher model, and guides view-branch optimization via a knowledge distillation mechanism that captures both corresponding-category and cross-category information. The key loss, WDOLF, combines the standard cross-entropy loss of the fused MVM output with a per-view EN guidance loss (ENL) that has two components: (1) a cross-entropy term aligning the view branch's target-category prediction with the EN, and (2) a Wasserstein distance term (approximated via the entropy-regularized Sinkhorn algorithm) between the view branch and EN output distributions over non-target categories, where the transport cost is derived from an HSIC-based mutual information matrix encoding category interrelations. This dual-path design—global MVM optimization for training consistency plus per-view distillation for thorough multi-view exploitation—yields more accurate individual fitness rankings, which in turn calibrates the evolutionary search direction in real time and alleviates FEB.

## Results

On the six primary multi-view classification benchmarks (Table 1), EFB-EMVC achieves the best accuracy across all datasets: 94.82% on MVoxCeleb, 87.67% on YouTubeFace, 75.79% on NUS-WIDE-128, 82.66% on Reuters5, 86.51% on Reuters3, and 89.67% on CB. On the three additional benchmarks (Table 2), it reaches 64.53% weighted F1 on MM-IMDB, 91.30% accuracy on NTU RGB-D, and 95.30% on EgoGesture. The largest gains over the strongest EMVC baselines (TEF, DC-NAS, CSG-NAS) are 2.41% on MVoxCeleb and 1.84% on YouTubeFace, while improvements on Reuters3 and EgoGesture are marginal (≤0.02%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

EFB-EMVC outperforms all 31 baselines on the six primary benchmarks in Table 1. Against the strongest EMVC competitors, it leads TEF (ICLR'25) by 2.41% on MVoxCeleb (94.82 vs 92.41), 1.65% on YouTubeFace (87.67 vs 86.02), and 0.67% on NUS (75.79 vs 75.12); it beats DC-NAS (AAAI'24) by 2.63% on MVoxCeleb and 2.39% on YouTubeFace. On CB, it surpasses CSG-NAS (IJCAI'24) by 0.47% (89.67 vs 89.20). On the three additional benchmarks (Table 2), EFB-EMVC achieves the best weighted F1 on MM-IMDB (64.53 vs HF-MNAS 64.17) and the best accuracy on NTU RGB-D (91.30 vs MCTS-CSG 91.21), while essentially tying HF-MNAS on EgoGesture (95.30 vs 95.31). Fixed-fusion baselines (Add, Cat, Avg) and trusted multi-view methods (TMC, ETMC) are consistently outperformed by 5–20+ points, confirming that adaptive evolutionary search with unbiased fitness evaluation is substantially more effective than static or late-fusion strategies.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the evolutionary multi-view classification (EMVC) lineage initiated by EDF (Liang et al., TEVC 2021), which encodes multi-view fusion architectures as binary trees and searches them via evolutionary operations. It builds on subsequent EMVC improvements (DC-NAS, CSG-NAS, CoMO-NAS, TEF) that addressed computational efficiency and model credibility, but departs from all of them by identifying a previously unacknowledged systematic flaw—fitness evaluation bias (FEB)—where joint training of views with heterogeneous information content distorts individual rankings and misleads evolutionary search. The solution repurposes knowledge distillation (Hinton et al., 2015) through pre-trained 'evolutionary navigators' that inject both corresponding-category and cross-category information (via a Wasserstein-distance-oriented loss using the Sinkhorn algorithm) into each view branch, drawing on the recent finding (Lv et al., NeurIPS 2024) that cross-category structure is critical for effective distillation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The formal identification of FEB as a distinct, systematic failure mode in EMVC provides a new diagnostic lens for the subfield and explains why existing methods plateau at local optima. The plug-and-play nature of evolutionary navigators—demonstrated by integrating them into both sequence-encoded (EDF) and tree-encoded (DC-NAS) frameworks—makes the contribution broadly applicable to future EMVC designs. However, the ablation evidence for FEB's severity rests on a single dataset (YouTubeFace) with only 15 individuals, and the WDOLF loss must be replaced by KL divergence on high-cardinality (CB, 10K categories) and multi-label (MM-IMDB) datasets, limiting the generality of the cross-category mechanism. Open questions include whether other factors beyond view information content cause FEB, and how to reduce the substantial pre-training overhead of ENs across all views.
