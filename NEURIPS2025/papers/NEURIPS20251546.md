## Problem

Existing online conformal prediction methods such as Adaptive Conformal Inference (ACI) assume perfect label accuracy, an assumption that rarely holds in real-world streaming settings. Under uniform label noise, the standard pinball loss gradient becomes biased, introducing a persistent coverage gap between the actual mis-coverage rate and the target α that grows with the noise rate. Prior work (Einbinder et al., 2024) only established conservative coverage under a strong stochastic-dominance assumption on score distributions and could not quantify the specific deviation, leaving a gap in both theory and practice.

## Input / Output

At each time step the method receives an input sample X_t, the softmax scores of a pre-trained classifier for all K classes (not just the noisy label), and a known (or estimated) uniform noise rate ε. It outputs a prediction set C_t(X_t) = {y : S(X_t, y) ≤ τ̂_t} with a long-run coverage guarantee at level 1−α. The requirement to compute non-conformity scores for every class at each step is the key operational difference from standard ACI, which only needs the score for the observed (noisy) label.

## Method

The core contribution is a robust pinball loss defined as l̃_{1−α}(τ, S̃, {S_y}) = (1/(1−ε))·l_{1−α}(τ, S̃) − (ε/(K(1−ε)))·Σ_y l_{1−α}(τ, S_y), a weighted combination of the pinball loss on the noisy score and the average pinball loss over all K class scores. This construction is provably unbiased: its expectation equals that of the clean pinball loss, and its gradient expectation matches the clean gradient, so threshold updates via online gradient descent are equivalent to clean-label updates in expectation. Theoretically, this eliminates the coverage gap, yielding O(T^{−1/2}) convergence for both empirical and expected coverage errors under both constant and dynamic learning rate schedules. The loss is a drop-in replacement that integrates seamlessly into ACI (constant and decaying learning rates) and Strongly Adaptive Online Conformal Prediction (SAOCP), requiring no architectural changes to the underlying online learning framework.

## Results

On ImageNet with α=0.1, noise rate ε=0.15, and dynamic learning rate, the robust pinball loss reduces the coverage gap from 8.372% (baseline) to 0.183% and shrinks the average prediction set size from 171.2 to 13.10. On CIFAR-100 under the same settings, the coverage gap drops from 8.403% to 0.214% and set size from 29.41 to 4.065. Across all noise rates (ε=0.05, 0.1, 0.15), error rates (α=0.1, 0.05), and both learning rate schedules, the robust pinball loss consistently achieves coverage gaps below 0.5% versus 4–9% for the baseline, while reducing prediction set sizes by 2–13×.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares the robust pinball loss against three baselines: ACI with constant learning rate (Gibbs & Candès, NeurIPS 2021), ACI with dynamic learning rate (Angelopoulos et al., ICML 2024), and SAOCP (Bhatnagar et al., ICML 2023). In all comparisons, the robust pinball loss dominates: it reduces the coverage gap by an order of magnitude (e.g., from 8.372% to 0.183% on ImageNet, ε=0.15, dynamic LR) while simultaneously shrinking prediction set sizes by 2–13×. The advantage is consistent across all noise rates, error rates, non-conformity score functions (LAC, APS, RAPS, SAPS), and model architectures (ResNet18, ResNet50, DenseNet121, VGG16). For SAOCP specifically (Figure 2), the robust pinball loss brings both long-run and local coverage close to the target 1−α, whereas the baseline exhibits a persistent coverage gap. The method also outperforms the prior noise-robust conformal prediction work (Einbinder et al., JMLR 2024) by removing the strong stochastic-dominance assumption and providing explicit convergence rates of O(T^{-1/2}) for both empirical and expected coverage errors.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the adaptive conformal inference (ACI) framework of Gibbs & Candès (2021) and its dynamic-learning-rate and strongly-adaptive variants (Angelopoulos et al. 2024; Bhatnagar et al. 2023) to the setting of uniform label noise. The closest prior work is Einbinder et al. (JMLR 2024), which showed that online conformal prediction maintains valid coverage under uniform noise but relied on a strong stochastic-dominance assumption on score distributions and could not quantify the coverage deviation. This paper removes that assumption, provides a general characterization of the coverage gap (showing it can be either positive or negative depending on prediction set size), and introduces a drop-in robust pinball loss that corrects the biased gradient without requiring clean labels.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Label noise is pervasive in real-world online learning (sensor errors, human annotation mistakes), and the demonstrated coverage gap—up to 8.4% on ImageNet with 15% noise—means that standard online conformal predictors can silently violate their guarantees in practice. The robust pinball loss is a simple, model-agnostic correction that integrates seamlessly into any existing online conformal prediction algorithm, making it broadly applicable. However, the analysis is limited to uniform noise with a known rate; extending to instance-dependent or class-dependent noise, and to settings where the noise rate must be estimated online, remain open and practically important questions.
