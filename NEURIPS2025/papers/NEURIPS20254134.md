## Problem

Existing RL fine-tuning methods for generative models (PPO, GRPO, DPO, ORW-CFM-W2) rely on fixed divergence regularization coefficients that treat all samples equally, creating an inherent dilemma: strong regularization preserves model capabilities but limits reward optimization, while weak regularization enables alignment but risks reward hacking, mode collapse, or catastrophic forgetting. This one-size-fits-all approach fails to adapt to the varying exploration needs across the complex policy optimization landscape, particularly in online RL where the model learns from its own generations.

## Input / Output

For text-to-image generation, the method takes text prompts from DrawBench and RAFT (testing attribute binding, compositional reasoning, style transfer) and produces images via a 2B-parameter SD3 flow matching model fine-tuned with LoRA, using CLIP Score as the reward signal. For LLM fine-tuning, it consumes text prompts from RLHFlow and produces text responses from Qwen2/Qwen3 models scored by RM-Gemma-2B. For multi-modal audio reasoning, it takes audio clips paired with text questions from AVQA and produces step-by-step reasoning answers from Qwen2.5-Omni-7B, evaluated on the MMAU benchmark with verifiable and format rewards.

## Method

ADRPO replaces the fixed regularization coefficient β in the standard RL objective with an adaptive coefficient (β₀ − A), where A is the per-sample advantage estimate computed as the reward minus a batch-average baseline. For flow matching models, this is combined with a novel advantage-weighted velocity matching loss that creates bidirectional learning signals: positive-advantage samples strengthen the target velocity field while negative-advantage samples actively push the model away from poor generations (unlike non-negative reward weighting in ORW-CFM-W2). The adaptive W2 regularization then decreases proportionally for high-advantage samples (enabling aggressive exploitation) and increases for low-advantage samples (enforcing stability and diversity preservation). For LLMs, the same principle modifies GRPO by making the KL divergence coefficient advantage-dependent, which yields an emergent ability to escape local optima through active entropy increases. Advantage clipping to [A_min, A_max] ensures training stability, and the framework requires no additional networks or architectural changes beyond the existing policy and reference model.

## Results

On text-to-image generation (DrawBench + RAFT prompts), ADRPO fine-tuned SD3 (2B) achieves the best score across all six metrics: ClipScore 32.97, ClipDiversity 5.13, Aesthetic 6.27, BLIPScore 0.567, ImageReward 1.61, and PicScore 22.78. Notably, this 2B model surpasses FLUX.1-Dev (12B) and SANA-1.5 (4.8B) on every metric, e.g., ClipScore 32.97 vs. 31.72/32.18 and ImageReward 1.61 vs. 1.11/1.45. On the MMAU audio-reasoning benchmark, ADRPO fine-tuned Qwen2.5-Omni-7B reaches 76.0% total accuracy, outperforming GRPO (74.2%), Gemini 2.5 Pro (71.6%), and GPT-4o Audio (62.5%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ADRPO (2B SD3) outperforms both larger flow-matching models—FLUX.1-Dev (12B) and SANA-1.5 (4.8B)—on all six metrics, with the largest gaps in ImageReward (1.61 vs. 1.11/1.45) and PicScore (22.78 vs. 21.83/21.85), while also maintaining higher ClipDiversity (5.13 vs. 4.29/4.31). Against offline DPO, ADRPO wins on every metric (e.g., ClipScore 32.97 vs. 31.30, ImageReward 1.61 vs. 1.48) and critically preserves diversity (ClipDiversity 5.13 vs. 4.78). Against the online fixed-regularization baseline ORW-CFM-W2, ADRPO improves ClipScore by 1.55 points, Aesthetic by 0.98, and ClipDiversity by 1.27, demonstrating that adaptive W2 regularization avoids the diversity collapse seen in fixed-β methods. RAFT performs worst among fine-tuning methods (ClipDiversity drops to 1.85, ImageReward to 0.22), highlighting the instability of reward-ranked approaches without divergence control. On the MMAU audio-reasoning task, ADRPO (76.0% total) beats GRPO (74.2%) by 1.8 points, Gemini 2.5 Pro (71.6%) by 4.4 points, and GPT-4o Audio (62.5%) by 13.5 points, with the largest gain in Sound accuracy (81.98% vs. 77.18% for GRPO).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends two parallel lines of RL fine-tuning work: GRPO's KL-regularized policy gradient for LLMs and ORW-CFM-W2's Wasserstein-2 regularized reward-weighted flow matching for image generation. The key departure is replacing the fixed regularization coefficient β with an advantage-dependent coefficient (β₀ − A), creating a sample-level adaptive mechanism that simultaneously modulates both the policy optimization signal and the divergence penalty. This unifies the previously separate treatment of continuous (W2) and discrete (KL) regularization under a single adaptive principle, and introduces advantage-weighted (rather than reward-weighted) flow matching to enable bidirectional learning signals in image generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play simplicity of the modification (one line change to the regularization term) makes it immediately adoptable across existing RL fine-tuning pipelines, and the demonstrated cross-domain applicability (flow matching, LLMs, audio reasoning) suggests a general principle rather than a domain-specific trick. However, the most dramatic claim—that a fine-tuned 2B model outperforms base 4.8B and 12B models—is somewhat asymmetric since the larger models are not themselves fine-tuned, and the LLM experiments use very small models (0.5B–0.6B). Open questions include whether the advantage-based adaptive mechanism scales to hundreds-of-billions-parameter models where training collapse is more severe, and whether more sophisticated value function estimation (beyond batch-mean baselines) would further improve performance in high-variance reward landscapes.
