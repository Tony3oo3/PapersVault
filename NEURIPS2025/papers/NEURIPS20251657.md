## Problem

Generative AI models struggle to produce truly creative, counter-intuitive outputs, and chess puzzles present a particularly hard case due to the absence of a standardized creativity definition, limited diverse training data (only 2.1% of Lichess puzzles meet a counter-intuitiveness criterion), and the non-trivial pattern structure that only reveals itself in the solution. This paper formalizes quantifiable metrics for puzzle creativity (uniqueness, counter-intuitiveness, novelty, aesthetics) and demonstrates that RL with engine-search-based rewards can dramatically increase counter-intuitive puzzle generation from 0.22% to 2.5%.

## Input / Output

The model performs unconditional generation from a start token, producing a chess position encoded as a FEN string using a chess-specialized 31-token vocabulary (77-token sequence). Each generated position is verified via Stockfish and AlphaZero search: uniqueness is confirmed by checking that the best move's winning chance exceeds the second-best by at least 0.5, and counter-intuitiveness is scored from evaluation differences across search depths. The final deliverable is a curated booklet of puzzles, each accompanied by its three nearest Lichess neighbors and a reference book puzzle for the same aesthetic theme.

## Method

The pipeline has two stages: (1) supervised pre-training of multiple discrete generative architectures (autoregressive transformer, MaskGIT, latent diffusion, masked diffusion) on 4.4M Lichess Puzzler positions, followed by (2) RL post-training with critic-free PPO using a sparse outcome reward that grants +1 only when both uniqueness and a counter-intuitiveness threshold are met (0 for legal but unqualified, −2 for illegal). The counter-intuitiveness reward is a weighted linear combination of Stockfish critical depth (weight 0.8) and normalized negative captured material (weight 0.1), tuned via grid search to maximize Average Precision on a 39-positive/45-negative Golden Set. A key innovation is a diversity-filtering mechanism that rejects positions failing intra-batch and inter-batch board/PV Levenshtein distance checks or a sequence-level entropy threshold, preventing the entropy collapse where the agent repeatedly generates a single high-reward puzzle. Realism is maintained through a token-level KL divergence penalty against the supervised policy, a replay buffer seeded with 100k high-quality Lichess puzzles, and a piece-count regularization that zeros the reward if any piece type exceeds its initial-game count.

## Results

The RL-trained autoregressive transformer achieves a 2.5% rate of unique, counter-intuitive puzzles, a 10× improvement over the supervised transformer (0.22%) and surpassing both the best supervised generative model (MaskGIT at 0.40%) and the Lichess Puzzler dataset baseline (2.14%). Among supervised models, MaskGIT leads on uniqueness (27.76%) and counter-intuitiveness (1.44%), while Masked Diffusion achieves the highest legality (99.72%). In the human expert study, the curated Booklet puzzles are rated highest on creativity (2.48/3), fun (2.56/3), and counter-intuitiveness (2.12/3), exceeding even human-composed book puzzles on these three metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Lichess Puzzler dataset (the training data and practical upper bound), the RL model exceeds the 2.14% puzzle rate, achieving 2.5% for unique counter-intuitive puzzles, while the best supervised model (MaskGIT) only reaches 0.40%. All four generative architectures substantially outperform the Lichess Standard Game baseline (0.03% puzzle rate), confirming that puzzle-specific training is essential. In the human expert evaluation (Table 4), the curated Booklet puzzles beat human-composed book puzzles (from Avni, Levitt & Friedgood, and Persson) on creativity (2.48 vs. 2.39), fun (2.56 vs. 2.22), and counter-intuitiveness (2.12 vs. 2.05), though they trail on realism (2.59 vs. 2.74) and difficulty (2.39 vs. 2.60). The auto-selected RL puzzles fall between book puzzles and Lichess puzzles on all five metrics, confirming that curation via aesthetic theme filtering and expert selection is critical for reaching the highest quality tier. Three world-renowned chess composition experts (IM Amatzia Avni, GM Jonathan Levitt, GM Matthew Sadler) independently acknowledged the booklet's creativity and novelty.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Lichess Puzzler dataset and its Stockfish-based uniqueness verification methodology, and adopts the 200M-parameter decoder-only transformer architecture from Ruoss et al. [71] for chess position generation. It departs from prior computational creativity work on chess (notably Iqbal et al.'s Chesthetica, which used aesthetic scoring as a post-hoc filter) by embedding creativity metrics—counter-intuitiveness, novelty, and uniqueness—directly into an RL reward function derived from chess engine search statistics (Stockfish critical depth, AlphaZero evaluations). The diversity-filtering RL mechanism addresses entropy collapse, a known failure mode in novelty-driven RL, by combining intra/inter-batch novelty checks with sequence-level entropy thresholds, going beyond standard KL-regularized PPO.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a 200M-parameter model with RL can generate counter-intuitive chess puzzles at a rate (2.5%) exceeding both the training data baseline (2.1%) and the best supervised model (0.4%), with expert raters scoring the curated booklet as more creative and fun than human-composed book puzzles. The counter-intuitiveness reward—contrasting shallow and deep engine search evaluations—is explicitly framed as generalizable to other search-based domains (Go, theorem proving, LLM reasoning). Key open questions include the persistent gap between reward optimization and human-perceived creativity, the small Golden Set (84 training examples) used to tune reward weights, and whether the RL results (shown only for the AR transformer) transfer to other architectures. The entropy collapse phenomenon and its diversity-filtering remedy offer a reusable blueprint for any RL-based generative task requiring sustained novelty.
