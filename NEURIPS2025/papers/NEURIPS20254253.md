## Problem

Panoptic symbol spotting in architectural CAD drawings is bottlenecked by the labor-intensive manual annotation of line-grained labels, which limits existing datasets like FloorPlanCAD to 16K chunks dominated by small residential buildings. This scale and diversity gap prevents models from generalizing to large-scale public/commercial buildings, complex structural components, and varied spatial configurations encountered in real-world engineering workflows.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254253/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes two complementary representations of each 14m×14m CAD chunk: (1) a set of vector primitives (lines, arcs, circles) encoded as a point cloud with geometric attributes, and (2) a rendered 700×700 RGB image of the same drawing. The output assigns every primitive a semantic category (from 27 classes spanning structural, non-structural, and notation elements) and an instance identifier, producing primitive-level panoptic masks that jointly resolve both 'what type' and 'which instance' for each graphical element.

## Method

DPSS employs a dual-pathway encoder: an HRNetV2-W48 image branch (pretrained on COCO-Stuff) extracts global semantic context from the rendered drawing, while a PointTransformerV2 point-cloud branch (pretrained on ScanNetV2) captures fine-grained geometric structure from the vector primitives. An adaptive fusion module aligns the two feature streams by computing a per-primitive weight matrix via a tanh-sigmoid gating function over concatenated primitive and image features, then concatenating the weighted image features with the original primitive features. A DETR/Mask2Former-style transformer decoder predicts primitive-level masks and class labels, trained with a combined cross-entropy, binary cross-entropy, and Dice loss. The architecture is deliberately designed to operate without layer or color priors, making it robust to real-world drawings where such metadata is unavailable.

## Results

On the new ArchCAD-400k dataset, DPSS achieves a total Panoptic Quality (PQ) of 70.6%, substantially outperforming SymPointV2 (60.5%) and SymPoint (47.6%), with a Stuff PQ of 77.6% (24.8% higher than SymPointV2). On the established FloorPlanCAD benchmark without prior inputs (layers/colors), DPSS reaches 86.2% PQ, surpassing SymPointV2 (83.2%) by 3% and CADTransformer (68.9%) by 17.3%. For semantic spotting on ArchCAD-400k, DPSS attains 87.8% F1, and for instance spotting, 40.7% mAP, both leading all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DPSS consistently outperforms all baselines on the more challenging ArchCAD-400k dataset. Against SymPointV2 [13], the closest competitor, DPSS gains 10.1% in total PQ (70.6 vs. 60.5) and 24.8% in Stuff PQ (77.6 vs. 52.8), while also leading in semantic F1 (87.8 vs. 69.8) and instance mAP (40.7 vs. 39.7). Against SymPoint [14], the gap widens to 23% in total PQ (70.6 vs. 47.6). CADTransformer [12] trails by 10.6% in total PQ (60.0 vs. 70.6) and 3.7% in semantic F1. On FloorPlanCAD without priors, DPSS leads SymPointV2 by 3% PQ (86.2 vs. 83.2) and CADTransformer by 17.3% (86.2 vs. 68.9); with priors, DPSS (89.5) is slightly below SymPointV2 (90.1) but above CADSpotting (88.9). Classical baselines DeepLabv3+R101 [41] and DINO [42] are far behind on semantic (68.8 vs. 92.0 F1) and instance (47.5 vs. 61.5 mAP) spotting respectively, confirming that task-specific architectures remain necessary for CAD symbol spotting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the panoptic symbol spotting task formalized by Fan et al. (FloorPlanCAD, 2021) and the subsequent methodological lineage of CADTransformer, SymPoint/SymPointV2, GAT-CADNet, and CADSpotting. It departs from prior work in two key ways: (1) a layer-block-aware annotation pipeline that leverages the intrinsic structure of standardized CAD drawings to achieve 10× annotation efficiency, enabling a dataset 26× larger than FloorPlanCAD with broader building types and structural/notation categories; and (2) a dual-pathway architecture (HRNetV2 image branch + PointTransformerV2 point-cloud branch) with an adaptive fusion module, contrasting with prior single-modality approaches that either rasterize primitives or encode them purely as points.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ArchCAD-400k is likely to become the de facto benchmark for panoptic symbol spotting, given its 26× scale advantage, diverse building types (only 14% residential), and inclusion of structural and notation elements absent from prior datasets. The annotation pipeline's 10× efficiency reduction (800 person-hours for 413K vs. 1000 for 16K) is practically significant for the field's ability to scale annotations. Key limitations remain: the model cannot process an entire vector drawing in a single pass, and per-category results reveal persistent weaknesses on rare or geometrically ambiguous classes (e.g., roof, bracing, construction joint). Open questions include developing pre-trained backbones specifically for CAD vector graphics and efficient inference strategies for full-scale drawings.
