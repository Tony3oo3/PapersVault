## Problem

Existing mRNA language models treat synonymous codons as independent tokens or apply soft penalties for synonymous substitutions, but none explicitly encode the group-theoretic symmetry structure of the genetic code. This leaves models without a principled, differentiable mechanism to capture how codon substitutions within a synonym set relate to one another, limiting data efficiency, interpretability, and robustness to species-specific codon bias.

## Input / Output

The model consumes mRNA sequences tokenized into codons (triplets), with pretraining sequences filtered to 20–512 codons from 25M RefSeq protein-coding entries. For generation, a two-thirds-length prefix serves as an autoregressive prompt to complete the remaining one-third via codon sampling. Outputs include continuous property predictions (expression, stability, degradation rate, riboswitch switching factor), generated codon sequences scored by Fréchet BioDistance, and SO(2)-equivariant codon embeddings where each synonymous codon is a rotation of a shared amino-acid base vector in a learned 2D Stiefel-manifold subspace.

## Method

Equi-mRNA maps each amino acid's synonymous codon set C_a (of size n_a) to a cyclic subgroup of SO(2) via a group homomorphism Φ: Z_{n_a} → SO(2), so that codon substitution corresponds to a structured planar rotation by angle θ_a = 2π/n_a (or a learned/fuzzy variant). Three generator parameterizations are explored—fixed uniform angles, learned cyclic angles, and fuzzy soft distributions over K rotation prototypes—each applied within a learned 2D subspace constrained to the Stiefel manifold St(2,d) via Riemannian optimization, so rotations act only in the codon-specific plane while preserving the orthogonal complement. An auxiliary equivariance loss L_equiv = E[‖f(R_c z_A) − R_c f(z_A)‖²] enforces that internal representations transform consistently under codon substitutions throughout the network, and SO(2)-aware pooling (polar, DFT-based, or angular averaging) preserves group structure during sequence-level aggregation. The full model uses a GPT-2 Transformer (15M params) or Mamba-Transformer hybrid (5M params) backbone, pretrained self-supervisedly on 25M sequences and fine-tuned for downstream property prediction and sequence generation.

## Results

On six downstream biological benchmarks (E. coli expression, MLOS, iCodon stability, Tc-Riboswitch switching, mRFP expression, SARS-CoV-2 degradation), the 15M-parameter Equi-mRNA (GPT-2 backbone) achieves the highest accuracy on 5 of 6 tasks, with peak values of 0.613 (E. coli), 0.710 (MLOS), 0.537 (iCodon), 0.737 (Tc-Ribo), and 0.855 (mRFP). The smaller 5M-parameter Mamba-hybrid variant is competitive, reaching 0.764 on Tc-Riboswitch and 0.853 on mRFP. In sequence generation, the Fuzzy-θ equivariant model achieves a Fréchet BioDistance of ~580 at T=1.0, a 4.3× improvement over the vanilla GPT-2 baseline, and up to 28% MSE reduction in property retention across all five generation benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CodonBert (82M parameters), Equi-mRNA (15M) wins on all six benchmarks, with the largest margins on iCodon (0.537 vs. 0.350, +53%) and Tc-Riboswitch (0.737 vs. 0.502, +47%), while using roughly 18% of the parameters. Against HELM (50M, the authors' prior work), Equi-mRNA (15M) outperforms on MLOS (0.710 vs. 0.701), iCodon (0.537 vs. 0.525), Tc-Riboswitch (0.737 vs. 0.626, +18%), and mRFP (0.855 vs. 0.822), but trails on SARS-CoV-2 degradation (0.791 vs. 0.833). The 5M Mamba-hybrid variant is particularly notable: it surpasses HELM on Tc-Riboswitch (0.764 vs. 0.626) and mRFP (0.853 vs. 0.822) while using only ~10% of HELM's parameters. Overall, the paper demonstrates that explicit SO(2) group-theoretic codon embeddings yield consistent gains over both nucleotide-level and prior codon-level baselines, with the strongest advantages on tasks sensitive to synonymous codon structure (Tc-Riboswitch, iCodon) and at smaller model scales.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the codon-level mRNA language modeling line initiated by HELM (Yazdani-Jahromi et al., 2024), which introduced a soft synonymous-codon penalty in the loss function, by replacing that implicit bias with an explicit SO(2) group-theoretic equivariant embedding: synonymous codons are mapped to cyclic subgroups of planar rotations via a group homomorphism from Z_{n_a}. It also draws on the broader equivariant-network literature (LieTransformer, SE(3)-Transformers, TorchMD-Net) but is the first to instantiate rotational equivariance at the codon level for mRNA. The Stiefel-manifold parameterization and fuzzy angle distributions are novel architectural choices that go beyond the fixed-rotation constructions in prior equivariant work, while the GPT-2 and Mamba backbones are standard.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, biologically grounded inductive bias for mRNA language models that could reduce the data and parameter requirements for codon-aware sequence design, with direct relevance to mRNA vaccine optimization and synthetic biology. However, it is currently restricted to protein-coding regions with fixed triplet tokenization, and the strongest quantitative comparisons (Table 2) show only modest gains over HELM and CodonBert, while the headline 4× FBD improvement is measured against a vanilla GPT-2 baseline rather than the strongest codon-level competitors. Open questions include whether the SO(2) prior generalizes to non-coding regions, whether richer (non-abelian) group structures capture positional or frame-shift dependencies, and whether the Riemannian optimization overhead scales to genome-length sequences.
