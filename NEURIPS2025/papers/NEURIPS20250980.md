## Problem

Existing visual-to-fMRI encoding methods rely on deterministic mappings that produce a single fixed output per stimulus, failing to capture the inherent one-to-many nature of neural responses where identical visual inputs evoke variable hemodynamic signals across trials and subjects. This deterministic collapse loses biologically meaningful variability while also struggling to maintain functional consistency in the encoded semantic information, limiting both synthesis fidelity and cross-subject transferability.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250980/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a natural scene image (COCO) as input, extracts a 256×1664 CLIP visual token embedding via a frozen OpenCLIP ViT-bigG/14 encoder, and produces a synthesized fMRI signal at subject-specific voxel resolution (~12,682–15,724 voxels from 7T NSD scans at 1.8 mm). Because BrainVAE models the fMRI as a conditional Gaussian, the output is a distribution over neural responses rather than a single deterministic signal; at inference, a single sample from this distribution yields one fMRI trial. Optionally, the synthesized fMRI can be decoded into a reconstructed image via the pretrained MindEye2 diffusion decoder.

## Method

SynBrain comprises two components trained in two stages. BrainVAE is a variational autoencoder with a hierarchical convolutional-attention encoder (ResNet-style blocks with self-attention) that encodes fMRI into a latent Gaussian posterior, trained jointly with MSE reconstruction, KL regularization, and a SoftCLIP contrastive loss that aligns the latent space with CLIP visual semantics. The S2N Mapper is an 8-layer Transformer that performs a one-step point-to-distribution mapping from CLIP embeddings directly into the BrainVAE latent manifold, replacing the iterative diffusion-based denoising used in MindSimulator and thereby eliminating the train-inference distribution gap. At inference, a single forward pass through the frozen S2N Mapper and BrainVAE decoder synthesizes an fMRI signal without iterative refinement or post-selection. The probabilistic formulation captures trial-level neural variability while the contrastive and S2N alignment preserve functional semantic consistency, enabling effective few-shot cross-subject adaptation by fine-tuning only the S2N Mapper's MLP submodules.

## Results

On the NSD dataset (subject-specific, 4 subjects), SynBrain with a single inference trial achieves MSE 0.139, Pearson 0.687, and Cosine 0.739 at the voxel level, and 95.7% Inception Score and 94.3% CLIP similarity at the semantic level. In the few-shot cross-subject setting (1 hour of data), SynBrain maintains competitive performance with CLIP similarity of 84.7%–88.1% and synthetic-signal retrieval accuracy up to 76.5%, while raw fMRI retrieval drops to 13.2%–19.5%. For data augmentation of fMRI-to-image decoding, adding 1 hour of SynBrain-generated synthetic data to MindEye2 improves CLIP similarity from 80.8% to 84.7% and Inception Score from 83.6% to 85.1%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MindSimulator, SynBrain achieves a 69% reduction in voxel-level MSE (0.139 vs. 0.403) and nearly doubles Pearson correlation (0.687 vs. 0.346) using only a single inference trial, while MindSimulator requires 5 trials to reach MSE 0.385 and Pearson 0.357. At the semantic level, SynBrain's single-trial CLIP similarity (94.3%) exceeds MindSimulator's 5-trial result (91.2%) by 3.1 points. In the few-shot fMRI-to-image decoding setting, SynBrain's synthetic data augmentation (DA) improves MindEye2's CLIP score from 80.8% to 84.7% and Inception Score from 83.6% to 85.1%, outperforming MindAligner (81.8% CLIP) on most high-level metrics, though MindAligner retains an edge on brain retrieval (86.9% vs. 82.0%). Notably, SynBrain's synthesized fMRI signals yield higher image retrieval accuracy (92.5%) than the raw fMRI signals themselves (84.8%), suggesting the model distills semantically relevant components while filtering noise.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the visual-to-fMRI encoding line initiated by MindSimulator [4], which used a deterministic MLP autoencoder with diffusion-based semantic-to-latent mapping. SynBrain departs from this in two key ways: (1) it replaces the deterministic autoencoder with a convolutional-attention VAE (BrainVAE) that models fMRI responses as a conditional probability distribution, and (2) it substitutes the multi-step diffusion prior with a one-step Transformer-based S2N Mapper that performs direct point-to-distribution projection from CLIP embeddings into the fMRI latent space. The framework also builds on the broader regression-based encoding literature (Adeli et al., Gifford et al.) and leverages pretrained OpenCLIP ViT-bigG/14 for semantic grounding, while using MindEye2 as a fixed decoder for evaluation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By explicitly modeling the one-to-many nature of neural responses, SynBrain provides a more biologically faithful encoding framework that captures trial-level variability while preserving functional consistency—a property deterministic methods structurally cannot. The practical implications are significant: few-shot cross-subject adaptation with only 1 hour of data and the use of synthetic fMRI as data augmentation for decoding address real bottlenecks in neuroimaging research where data collection is expensive and slow. Open questions remain around whether the reliance on pretrained CLIP introduces representational biases misaligned with neural processing, whether the framework can capture attentional and neuromodulatory variance sources, and whether the probabilistic modeling paradigm generalizes to other sensory modalities and brain regions.
