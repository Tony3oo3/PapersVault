## Problem

Entropic regularization accelerates optimal transport solvers but introduces bias from the true solution, and while decreasing (annealing) regularization is known to help in practice, its theoretical acceleration benefits remain largely unproven. In semi-discrete OT, no existing algorithm simultaneously adapts to both the regularization strength and the number of drawn samples, leaving a gap between the statistical requirement that ε decrease with sample count and the computational benefit of starting with large ε for fast convergence.

## Input / Output

DRAG consumes i.i.d. samples from the continuous source measure μ one at a time in an online fashion (no storage of past samples required), while the discrete target measure ν = Σ w_j δ_{y_j} is fully known a priori. The algorithm outputs a discrete potential vector g̅_t ∈ R^M (up to an additive constant), from which the OT cost, the Brenier map T(x) = x − ∇g̅_t^c(x), and the Laguerre cell partition are directly recovered. The source density must be α-Hölder continuous with support in a bounded ball, and the ambient dimension d does not affect convergence rates—only the number of target points M does.

## Method

DRAG is a projected averaged SGD algorithm for the semi-dual of semi-discrete OT where the entropic regularization ε_t = t^{-a} decays polynomially with the iteration count, and the step size follows γ_t = γ_1 t^{-b}. The key technical insight (Lemma 2) is that within an ε-ball around the current optimum, the entropic semi-dual H_ε satisfies a restricted strong convexity condition with a constant independent of ε, so the algorithm can exploit enhanced convexity even as ε → 0. DRAG is designed to remain within this ε-ball with high probability at every step (Proposition 2), and the ASGD averaging recursion g̅_t = (1/(t+1))g_t + (t/(t+1))g̅_{t-1} converts the O(t^{-b}) non-averaged rate into an optimal O(1/t) rate for the potential and OT cost, and O(1/√t) for the OT map, with no adverse dependence on the vanishing regularization parameter.

## Results

DRAG achieves an unbiased O(1/t) convergence rate for both the OT cost and the discrete potential estimation, and an O(1/√t) rate for the OT map, under the optimal parameter choice a=1/3⁻, b=2/3 with α>1/2. Crucially, these rates carry no adverse dependence on the regularization parameter ε, in contrast to the O(ε^{-c}·t^{-b}) rates of fixed-regularization SGD from [5]. In numerical experiments, DRAG outperforms fixed-regularization projected ASGD by one to two orders of magnitude in accuracy throughout the optimization process, and matches its theoretical convergence rates on all three synthetic examples.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against fixed-regularization projected ASGD, DRAG consistently achieves one to two orders of magnitude lower error across the entire optimization trajectory; fixed schemes either converge to biased solutions (large ε) or fail to converge within a reasonable iteration budget (small ε, e.g. ε=5×10⁻³). In the generative modeling task (mapping a Gaussian prior to encoded data), DRAG outperforms the ADAM-based solver from [2] on the 'spiralarms' dataset, nearly completely generating the target distribution where ADAM shows poor coverage, while both perform comparably on 'swissroll'. For Monge-Kantorovich quantile estimation, DRAG is the only method among DRAG, Online Sinkhorn [37], and Neural OT [33] that produces an unbiased estimate fully covering the support of ν while preserving convexity of each quantile region; Online Sinkhorn (with ε=10⁻³) shows visible bias, and Neural OT (10⁸ samples, 3-layer MLP on A100 GPU) is ten times slower and fails to maintain convex regions.

## Relation to Prior Work

DRAG sits at the intersection of three research threads: (i) the semi-dual SGD framework for large-scale semi-discrete OT introduced by Genevay et al. (2016), (ii) the ASGD averaging scheme of Polyak and Juditsky (1992) that accelerates stochastic approximation, and (iii) the convergence theory for semi-discrete entropic OT developed by Delalande (2022), which provides the key restricted strong convexity (RSC) property and entropic potential convergence bounds that DRAG's analysis relies on. The paper departs from prior fixed-regularization SGD analyses (e.g., Bercu & Bigot 2021, which show O(ε^{-c}t^{-b}) rates) by introducing a decreasing regularization schedule ε_t = t^{-a} and proving that the local RSC constant around the optimum is independent of ε, thereby eliminating the adverse ε-dependence. It also connects to the ε-scaling/annealing heuristics used in discrete Sinkhorn solvers (Schmitzer 2019, Feydy 2020) and the statistical requirement that ε must decrease with sample size for minimax-optimal OT map estimation (Pooladian et al. 2023), unifying these two motivations for the first time in a single algorithm with full theoretical guarantees.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first OT algorithm that simultaneously adapts to both regularization strength and sample size, and the first to prove that decreasing entropic regularization provably accelerates convergence rather than merely being a heuristic. The O(1/t) rate for OT cost/potential and O(1/√t) for the Brenier map, achieved without any adverse dependence on the vanishing regularization, resolves a gap left by prior fixed-ε analyses. Limitations include the requirement α > 1/2 for the optimal rate, restriction to quadratic cost, and the compact-support assumption (though experiments suggest robustness beyond it). Open directions highlighted by the authors include extending the decreasing-regularization acceleration proof to fully discrete OT (where Sinkhorn annealing is used but unproven) and developing adaptive-step variants (Adam/Adagrad-style) that could further improve practical performance.
