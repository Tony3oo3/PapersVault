## Problem

Current behavior cloning methods in imitation learning map states directly to actions, failing to capture the multimodal, non-Markovian diversity of human behavior and offering no mechanism for steering generated behaviors at inference time. The paper argues that human action selection is mediated by inner speech—an internalized linguistic process between perception and action—and that ignoring this mediational layer limits both fidelity and controllability of learned policies.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252646/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The agent consumes environmental observations (top-camera and in-hand RGB images, or feature vectors in vision-free settings) along with a sliding window of past states and actions (length W). During training, a VLM generates behavioral descriptions from demonstration GIFs, which are CLIP-encoded to form the inner speech targets. At inference, the agent outputs continuous robot actions and periodically regenerates its inner speech (a CLIP-space embedding) every W timesteps; optionally, a designer-supplied text prompt overrides the initial inner speech to steer behavior without retraining.

## Method

MIMIC decomposes the action policy as p(a|s) = ∫ p(a|s,m) p(m|s) dm, where m is an inner speech embedding. A conditional VAE (CVAE) generates m from a window of past images, trained to reconstruct CLIP-encoded VLM descriptions of demonstrated behaviors with a KL-regularized information bottleneck that enforces semantic condensation. A diffusion-based transformer policy (DDPM-T) then predicts actions conditioned on the current state and the generated inner speech, trained with classifier-free guidance so it can operate with or without the speech signal. During simulation, inner speech is refreshed every W steps (a temporal regulatory cycle), and the framework supports designer control by accepting a natural-language behavior description as the initial inner speech, after which the agent autonomously continues periodic updates. The VLM scaffolding (GPT-4o or alternatives) eliminates the need for per-step human thought annotations, bootstrapping the inner speech generator purely from visual demonstrations.

## Results

On the D3IL benchmark, MIMIC-S achieves a success rate of 0.8021 on Aligning (vs. 0.6645 for BC), with a dramatically lower state Wasserstein distance (0.0459 vs. 0.6961) and completion-time Wasserstein distance (50.57 vs. 59.03), indicating much higher fidelity to human demonstrations. MIMIC-E achieves the highest entropy (0.6148 vs. 0.4743 for BC) on Aligning, capturing greater behavioral diversity. On Overcooked, MIMIC improves collective reward by up to 30 points over BC (e.g., 151.8 vs. 115.8 in Cramped room; 108.8 vs. 73.6 in Cramped room-Vision), demonstrating superior human-AI coordination.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is BC, a diffusion-based behavior cloner (DDPM-T architecture) from Pearce et al. [33]. MIMIC consistently outperforms BC across all four D3IL environments: on Aligning, MIMIC-S improves success rate by 13.8 points (0.8021 vs. 0.6645) while reducing state Wasserstein distance by 93% (0.0459 vs. 0.6961); MIMIC-E boosts entropy by 29.6% (0.6148 vs. 0.4743) for greater behavioral diversity. On the more complex Stacking task, MIMIC-S raises 2-box success from 0.4879 to 0.6074 (+12 points) and reduces state Wasserstein distance from 9.43 to 0.75. On Overcooked, MIMIC achieves higher collective reward in all four layouts, with the largest gain in Cramped room-Vision (+35.2 points, 108.8 vs. 73.6) and Cramped room (+36.0 points, 151.8 vs. 115.8). MIMIC-E occasionally trades a small success-rate drop for substantially higher entropy (e.g., Stacking 3-box entropy 0.0878 vs. BC's 0.1049 is the one case where BC edges out), but overall MIMIC dominates on the joint success–diversity–fidelity front.

## Relation to Prior Work

MIMIC builds directly on diffusion-based behavior cloning (DDPM-T, Pearce et al.) as its action policy backbone and on CVAE architectures for latent representation learning, but departs from prior diverse-behavior IL methods (Behavior Transformers, BESO) by introducing a stochastic linguistic mediator generated online by the agent rather than relying on discrete mode tokens or goal conditioning. It is conceptually closest to Thought Cloning and intra-agent speech work, but differs critically in that inner speech is an internally generated latent variable conditioning the policy at inference time, rather than an external per-step annotation or a frozen auxiliary supervision signal. The theoretical grounding in Vygotsky's mediational theory of inner speech (predicativity, semantic condensation, temporal regulation) provides a principled justification for the CVAE's information-bottleneck objective and the periodic W-step update cycle, distinguishing MIMIC from purely empirical diversity-matching approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MIMIC offers a practical recipe for steerable, diverse imitation that requires no per-step human thought annotations—only visual demonstrations and a VLM for one-time scaffolding—making it more scalable than thought-cloning approaches. The consistent gains in entropy, success rate, and human-AI coordination reward (up to 30 points in Overcooked) across multiple environments suggest the inner-speech mediator captures behavioral structure that direct state-to-action mapping misses. Key open questions include whether the approach scales to complex multi-agent settings beyond dyadic interaction, how to make the CVAE latent space more interpretable, and whether adaptive (rather than fixed) polling windows can reduce the manual calibration burden. The dependency on VLM quality for training-time scaffolding means that improvements in vision-language models will naturally enhance MIMIC without architectural changes, but also introduce a quality ceiling tied to external model capabilities.
