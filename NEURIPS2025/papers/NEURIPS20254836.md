## Problem

Existing sparse layer approximations for LLM interpretability (sparse autoencoders, Transcoders) rely on neuron-level sparsity, which forces a severe accuracy trade-off: faithfully reconstructing the original dense MLP mapping becomes impossible at the sparsity levels needed for interpretability. This paper argues that the unit of specialization should be a full linear sublayer rather than individual neurons, and introduces a mechanism to achieve layer-level sparsity at scale without sacrificing model performance.

## Input / Output

The MxD layer takes a pre-MLP hidden state vector x ∈ R^I (the residual stream representation at a given token position) and produces a post-MLP output vector y ∈ R^O that replaces the original dense MLP's output. Internally, a gating matrix G produces N sparse expert coefficients a via TopK selection (only K experts active), while a dense encoder E produces hidden units z ∈ R^H. The final output is the element-wise Hadamard product of two linear projections: (C^T a) * (D^T z), making the forward pass a single element-wise multiplication after two matrix-vector products.

## Method

The Mixture of Decoders (MxD) layer approximates a dense MLP as a sparse conditional combination of N full-rank linear experts, where each expert W_n ∈ R^{H×O} implements a complete decoder transformation. The key parameterization factorizes the third-order weight tensor W ∈ R^{N×H×O} element-wise as W(n,h,:) = c_n * d_h (Hadamard product of rows from C ∈ R^{N×O} and D ∈ R^{H×O}), reducing parameters from NHO to O·(N+H) and enabling N to scale to tens of thousands of experts. A rank-preservation lemma proves each expert's weight matrix equals D·diag(c_n), inheriting the full rank of D (and thus the original MLP's decoder) as long as c_n has no zero entries. The forward pass collapses to a single Hadamard product (C^T a) * (D^T z), making inference efficient. Training minimizes normalized L2 reconstruction loss against frozen base-MLP outputs on 480M tokens of OpenWebText, with the TopK gating providing sparsity without auxiliary penalties. The formulation generalizes directly to Gated Linear Unit (GLU) encoders used in modern LLMs like Llama-3.2.

## Results

Across 4 LLMs (GPT-2-124M, Pythia-410M, Pythia-1.4B, Llama-3.2-3B) with up to 86K experts, MxDs consistently achieve lower downstream cross-entropy loss than Transcoders and Skip Transcoders at every sparsity level K (Figure 3). On output-space faithfulness (Pythia-410M, K=32), MxDs preserve 22% of 15-token generations identical to the base model, versus 18% for Skip Transcoders and 0% for Transcoders. On sparse probing (SAEBench, 24 binary tasks), MxDs remain competitive with Transcoders on F1 and outperform TopK-SAEs. On feature steering (100 experts, λ=100), MxDs match baselines on both textual coherence and steerability scores as judged by two LLMs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Transcoders [27], MxDs achieve substantially lower cross-entropy loss at all sparsity levels K across all four models, and on output faithfulness the gap is dramatic: at n=15 tokens, MxDs retain 22% identical generations while Transcoders drop to 0% (Pythia-410M). Against Skip Transcoders [26], MxDs win on both CE loss and n-gram matching (0.22 vs 0.18 at n=15) despite using fewer parameters (e.g., 604M vs 614M on Llama-3.2-3B), since STCs add an extra I×O skip-connection matrix. On sparse probing (SAEBench), MxDs are competitive with Transcoders on F1 scores for news-category and other binary tasks, while outperforming TopK-SAEs [23]. On feature steering, MxDs match both Transcoders and Skip Transcoders on LLM-judged coherence and steerability, confirming that the learned experts contribute mechanistically to generation. Overall, MxDs Pareto-dominate all baselines on the sparsity-accuracy frontier while remaining competitive on interpretability metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Mixture of Experts lineage (Shazeer et al., 2017) and parameter-efficient MoE variants like MoVs (Zadouri et al., 2024) by applying Hadamard-product-factorized weight tensors to the specific problem of decomposing dense MLP layers into interpretable sublayers. It departs from the dominant neuron-level sparsity paradigm of Sparse Autoencoders and Transcoders, which impose sparsity on individual hidden units, by instead treating entire linear sublayers as the atomic unit of specialization (layer-level sparsity). The key technical departure is the tensor factorization W(n,h,:) = c_n * d_h, which the authors prove preserves the full rank of each expert's decoder matrix — a property absent in both classic MoEs (which use low-rank experts for scalability) and sparse MLPs (which confine outputs to K-dimensional subspaces).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that the sparsity-accuracy trade-off plaguing interpretability methods is not fundamental but an artifact of the neuron-level sparsity paradigm, opening a path to interpretable layers that faithfully preserve model behavior. This is practically significant because faithful sparse layers can directly replace dense MLPs in the forward pass, enabling mechanistic analysis without the inference overhead of post-hoc SAEs. Limitations include evaluation only up to 3B parameters, additional gating cost at inference, and reliance on LLM-as-judge for steering evaluations. Open questions include whether the advantage persists at tens-of-billions scale, whether MxDs can be trained end-to-end (rather than as layer replacements), and how to extend the framework to cross-layer or cross-head feature decomposition.
