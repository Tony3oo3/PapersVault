## Problem

Existing LLM safety alignment methods develop 'shallow safety'—refusal triggers tied to initial prompt tokens—that are easily bypassed by adversarial attacks like GCG, middle filling, and decoding parameter manipulation. Prior corrective mechanisms (full resets, BSAFE's repeat-and-edit) are inefficient, discarding large amounts of valid generated text to fix a small violating segment. The paper addresses the gap of enabling targeted, token-efficient in-generation correction of safety violations that emerge mid-sequence during autoregressive decoding.

## Input / Output

The model receives a standard text prompt and generates a response autoregressively. An LLM safety critic monitors the live output stream in real time, identifying violation categories and token spans. When a violation is detected, the model emits two special tokens—[CATEGORY_c] and [BACKTRACK_BY_X]—which do not alter the model's internal KV cache; instead, a post-processing handler removes the last X tokens from the user-facing output stream and seamlessly continues with subsequent generated tokens, enabling near-real-time correction in streaming applications.

## Method

RLBF is a two-stage framework. First, an enhanced SFT strategy (BSAFE+) generates training data by injecting harmful segments into high-quality, originally safe responses at contextually coherent locations, preserving in-distribution safe continuations and providing precise backtrack targets; the model learns to emit [CATEGORY_c] + [BACKTRACK_BY_X] + safe continuation via cross-entropy loss. Second, a GRPO-based RL phase optimizes the policy using trajectory-level rewards from a single LLM safety critic that evaluates the model's own live generation: +1 for safe output or successful correction, −1 for uncorrected violations, −0.5 for unnecessary backtracking, and −0.2 for failed corrections. The total loss combines the RL objective with a behavior-cloning term over the SFT dataset, and the critic additionally penalizes regeneration of known violating patterns from the SFT construction, creating a strong prior against previously identified failure modes. The key distinction from BSAFE is the simpler single-integer backtrack signal (vs. multi-token repeat-and-edit) and the RL stage that trains on the model's own in-distribution emergent errors rather than static correction examples.

## Results

On the LMSYS-MF benchmark (Middle Filling attacks), RLBF reduces Attack Success Rate to 3–7% across Gemma 2 (2B/9B) and LLaMA 3 (1B/3B/8B), compared to 68–81% for standard instruction-tuned (IT) baselines. On the standard LMSYS harmful-query subset, RLBF achieves 1–2% ASR, dramatically outperforming BSAFE+ (14–17%) and IT (24–28%). Against GCG attacks, RLBF attains 4.3–4.7% ASR on AdvBench and HEx-PHI, and 1.0–3.7% against Decoding Parameter attacks. Utility is fully preserved: e.g., Gemma 2 9B with RLBF scores 70.7% on MMLU and 35.6% on MATH, matching the IT baseline (70.6%, 35.4%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Circuit Breakers [Zou et al., 2024], RLBF consistently achieves lower Attack Success Rates: 4.7% vs. 10.7% on AdvBench (GCG) and 4.3% vs. 13.4% on HEx-PHI (GCG), and 3.7% vs. 12.4% on HEx-PHI (Decoding Parameters), while matching Circuit Breakers at 1.0% on MaliciousInstruct. Compared to BSAFE+ (the direct predecessor), RLBF shows comparable performance on Middle Filling attacks (3–7% vs. 3–6% ASR) but a decisive advantage on standard LMSYS harmful queries (1–2% vs. 14–17% ASR), suggesting the RL stage provides broader safety beyond the SFT-taught correction reflex. Against standard IT and RL baselines, RLBF reduces ASR by roughly 20–75 percentage points depending on the attack type and model size. On utility benchmarks (MMLU, BBH, GSM8K, MATH), RLBF matches or marginally exceeds both IT and BSAFE+ across all model sizes, confirming no alignment tax.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends BSAFE (Sel et al., 2025b), which first proposed backtracking as a safety mechanism for LLMs, by replacing BSAFE's multi-token repeat-and-edit procedure with a simpler 'backtrack by x tokens' command and, crucially, adding a GRPO-based RL stage that uses a live safety critic to train the model on its own emergent generation errors. The SFT data generation strategy (BSAFE+) also departs from BSAFE's approach of prompting a model to generate Q&A pairs and then annotating violations, instead injecting violations into already-coherent safe responses to preserve in-distribution continuations. The work positions itself against the broader 'shallow safety' problem identified by Qi et al. (2025) and Zhang et al. (2025), where standard RLHF/DPO-based alignment is easily bypassed by mid-generation attacks like Middle Filling and GCG, and against reset-based mechanisms that discard entire outputs for isolated violations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical contribution is demonstrating that an RL stage with critic feedback on the model's own generation adds substantial safety value beyond SFT-taught backtracking alone (1–2% vs. 14–17% ASR on standard LMSYS harmful queries), suggesting that training models to actively fix their own errors is meaningfully different from merely teaching them a correction reflex. The ablation showing that disabling backtracking specifically in the middle of generation (7% ASR) is far less damaging than removing it entirely (18%) highlights that mid-sequence correction is the critical capability for defending against attacks like Middle Filling. Open questions include the computational cost of the RL stage with a powerful LLM critic at scale, whether the approach generalizes to safety categories not seen during training, and how the method interacts with other alignment objectives (helpfulness, honesty) in production settings. The paper acknowledges that precisely defining 'harmful' content remains an inherent limitation.
