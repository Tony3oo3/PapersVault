## Problem

Collecting high-quality real-world demonstrations for dexterous multi-fingered manipulation is hindered by the lack of affordable, high-DoF hand platforms that simultaneously provide stable multi-sensor integration. Existing multi-fingered hands either suffer from sensor dropouts and variable latencies (4.4% dropout, 15–100 ms) during finger motion, or are prohibitively expensive and closed-source, while most generalist robot frameworks default to two-finger grippers that cannot perform in-hand manipulation or tool use.

## Input / Output

The policy consumes a single-timestep observation (T_o=1) comprising a 640×480×3 wrist RGB image, 5 fingertip tactile arrays (12×8×5 taxels each) with their 3D spatial positions computed via forward kinematics, and 26 joint angles (20 hand + 6 arm), all hardware-synchronized within ≤7 ms and spatially aligned to a common hand frame. It outputs a 64-step action sequence of 26-DoF joint targets (20 hand + 6 arm), executed at 10 Hz, with the diffusion model denoising over T_a=64 timesteps per prediction.

## Method

RAPID Hand is a co-optimized hardware-software platform. On the hardware side, a universal multi-phalangeal actuation scheme uses direct-drive DYNAMIXEL servos for distal joints and a spur-bevel gear differential (parallel mechanism) for proximal MCP/CMC joints, achieving 20 mm finger thickness and independent multi-DoF control; a custom hard-sync electronics board captures tactile via I²C (≤7 ms) and triggers camera exposure via PWM (≤2 ms) to guarantee temporal alignment, while forward kinematics and camera calibration provide spatial alignment across all modalities. For teleoperation, human hand poses from an Apple Vision Pro are retargeted to the robot via SLSQP optimization with two novel constraints: a conformal-aligned constraint that applies per-phalangeal segment scaling and translation to eliminate geometric distortion, and a contact-aware coupling constraint that adaptively enforces thumb-finger proximity through a sigmoid-weighted term. The learned policy is a Diffusion Policy (time-series diffusion transformer) that encodes vision via a pre-trained ResNet-18 (128-d), tactile readings with spatial positions via MLPs (64-d), and proprioception via MLPs (192-d), fusing them into a 384-d state token; cross-attention conditions the denoising decoder on these tokens to predict 26-DoF action sequences, trained for 300 epochs on collected demonstrations.

## Results

The whole-hand visuotactile diffusion policy trained on RAPID Hand data achieves 100% success (50/50) on both in-hand rolling and translation tasks, and 48% (24/50) on the more challenging multi-fingered nonprehensile retrieval task. Under a 4.4% sensor dropout rate, performance degrades gracefully to 88% (44/50) for rolling, 82% (41/50) for translation, and 46% (23/50) for retrieval. Ablations confirm all three modalities are essential: removing vision drops rolling to 6% and retrieval to 0%, while removing touch drops rolling to 12% and translation to 18%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the LEAP Hand [28], RAPID Hand reduces finger thickness from 59 mm to 20 mm while achieving higher linear manipulability in the 'down' pose (3.01×10⁴ vs. 3.03×10³ mm³) and comparable values in other poses; qualitatively, LEAP shows minimal lateral motion during teleoperated in-hand translation while RAPID enables stable coordinated multi-finger sliding. Against the AnyTeleop uniform-scaling baseline [44], RAPID's conformal-aligned and contact-aware retargeting constraints produce geometrically correct poses across all tested gestures with a single parameter set, whereas no single global scale factor α (tested at 1.25, 1.50, 1.75, 2.00) simultaneously avoids under-extension of long fingers and over-extension of distal links. Compared to TILDE [1], which relies on a fixed arm and tabletop support, RAPID's policy generalizes to free-space in-hand rolling and translation without such constraints. For multi-fingered retrieval, RAPID's coordinated five-finger policy (24/50) substantially outperforms the concurrent Retrieval Dexterity method [2], which uses single-finger sweeping and ArUco-marker-based perception.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on Diffusion Policy [50] for the learning component but positions its primary contribution as a co-optimized hardware-software platform. It departs from prior multi-fingered hands (LEAP, Allegro, Shadow, TRX) by introducing a bevel gear differential mechanism for MCP joints that achieves 20 mm finger thickness versus LEAP's 59 mm, and by replacing software-based temporal synchronization (as in [13]) with a hardware-level hard-sync framework achieving sub-7 ms alignment across vision, touch, and proprioception. For teleoperation, it extends the uniform-scaling retargeting of AnyTeleop [44] with two additional constraints—conformal-aligned geometric calibration and contact-aware thumb-finger coupling—addressing the embodiment gap that causes unstable grasps in prior high-DoF teleoperation. The learning validation also relaxes the fixed-arm and table-support assumptions of TILDE [1], enabling free-space in-hand manipulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The platform addresses a concrete bottleneck in generalist robot autonomy: the scarcity of affordable, high-DoF hands with reliable multimodal perception for collecting contact-rich demonstration data. By open-sourcing a $3,500, 20-DoF hand with integrated vision, touch, and proprioception at 25 Hz, it lowers the barrier for labs that previously relied on $16,000+ Allegro hands or closed-source systems like TRX-Hand 5. Key limitations remain: servo motor size constrains further miniaturization, the teleoperation interface lacks haptic feedback, and the retrieval task (24/50 success) reveals that longer-horizon non-prehensile manipulation is still challenging. Open questions include whether the hardware-level sync framework generalizes to other sensor modalities (optical, capacitive) and whether the MuJoCo tactile simulation environment can bridge the sim-to-real gap for RL-based dexterous manipulation.
