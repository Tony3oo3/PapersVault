## Problem

Deterministic bio-realistic PDE-based neuron models cannot capture the intrinsic trial-to-trial variability observed in experimental electrophysiology recordings, while existing deep learning surrogates are limited to simplified point-neuron models and fail to represent the full biophysical complexity of multi-compartment neurons. Generating the 'hall-of-fame' (HoF) model ensembles that approximate this variability via evolutionary optimization is computationally prohibitive (~600k CPU core hours per neuron), and direct interpolation between HoF parameterizations produces unrealistic dynamics due to PDE sensitivity.

## Input / Output

NOBLE takes as input a square DC step current (400 ms duration, amplitude in [-0.11, 0.28] nA) and two biologically interpretable neuron-model features (threshold current I_thr and local F-I slope s_thr), each encoded as a stack of K frequency-modulated sine/cosine time-series (NeRF-style positional encoding) that serve as input channels to the operator. The output is a continuous somatic voltage trace over 515 ms (subsampled 3× to 8,583 timesteps from the original 25,750), which can be queried at any point in the 2D latent (I_thr, s_thr) space to generate novel bio-realistic responses without retraining.

## Method

NOBLE is a 1D Fourier Neural Operator (12 layers, 24 hidden channels, 256 Fourier modes, 1.8M parameters) that learns a single operator mapping from the embedded input to the somatic voltage response across an entire population of HoF models, rather than training separate surrogates per model. The key architectural insight is a NeRF-style frequency-modulated embedding: each scalar feature p is expanded into a stack of sin(2^k π p ⊙ t) and cos(2^k π p ⊙ t) terms, creating a continuous, interpretable latent space whose spectral structure aligns naturally with the FNO's Fourier-domain operations. Trained on 75,600 synthetic samples from 50 HoF PVALB models using relative L4 loss, NOBLE achieves 2.18% relative L2 error on voltage traces and a 4200× speedup over the numerical solver. Interpolation within the latent space generates novel bio-realistic neurons whose dynamics remain consistent with experimental data, and a feature-specific composite loss (L_data + λ·L_F) enables targeted fine-tuning on individual electrophysiological features without degrading overall trace fidelity.

## Results

On the PVALB neuron, NOBLE achieves a 2.18% relative L2 error on voltage traces for in-distribution HoF models, with feature-level errors of 3% (spikecount), 32.8% (AP1_width), 8.54% (mean_AP_amplitude), and 0.83% (steady_state_voltage). On the VIP neuron, performance is comparable at 2.5% voltage L2 error. NOBLE delivers a 4,200× speedup over the NEURON-based PDE solver (batch size 1000 on a single RTX 4090 GPU) while using only 1.8M trainable parameters trained on 75,600 samples over 300 epochs. Ensemble predictions across 16 electrophysiological features remain within the variability observed in experimental human cortex recordings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare NOBLE against other machine learning methods; instead, it benchmarks against the PDE numerical solver (NEURON) that generated the training data and against experimental human cortex recordings. NOBLE matches PDE simulation accuracy (2.18% L2 voltage error) while being 4,200× faster at inference. Critically, NOBLE's ensemble predictions across 50 HoF models remain within the variability envelope of experimental data on all 16 electrophysiological features (Figure 5B), whereas direct interpolation between PDE parameterizations produces traces that deviate significantly from experimental recordings (Figure 4B). The ablation in Table 1 shows that without biologically-informed embeddings, NOBLE completely fails to produce spiking responses, and the two-feature (I_thr, s_thr) embedding outperforms single-feature or three-feature alternatives on voltage traces and most spike-related metrics. On the VIP neuron, NOBLE generalizes with the same architecture, achieving 2.5% voltage error and 9% spikecount error, confirming the embedding framework's transferability across cell types.

## Relation to Prior Work

NOBLE combines two established ideas — the Fourier Neural Operator (FNO) for learning function-to-function mappings and NeRF-style sinusoidal positional encoding — into a single framework for simulating bio-realistic neuron dynamics. It departs from prior ML approaches to electrophysiology (which targeted simplified point-neuron models like Hodgkin-Huxley or FitzHugh-Nagumo and were inherently deterministic) by learning a single operator over a continuous latent space of Hall-of-Fame (HoF) model parameters, thereby capturing trial-to-trial variability without retraining per model. It also extends Centofanti et al.'s FNO application to Hodgkin-Huxley by operating on full multi-compartment PDE-based models and validating against real human cortical recordings, and contrasts with NeuPRINT, which captured variability but on slower population-level calcium imaging rather than fast intracellular voltage dynamics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

NOBLE provides a 4200× speedup over the numerical PDE solver while maintaining accuracy within the solver-experiment error gap, and enables the generation of arbitrarily many bio-realistic neuron models through latent-space interpolation — a capability impossible with the finite set of HoF models from evolutionary optimization. This opens a practical pathway toward brain-scale circuit modeling where simulating millions of neurons with full PDE solvers is infeasible. Key limitations include restriction to single neurons, square-pulse DC inputs, a low-dimensional (2D) embedding, and validation against only a single experimental neuron's recordings; extending to multi-neuron circuits, time-varying stimuli, and multimodal embeddings (gene expression, morphology) are the natural next steps.
