## Problem

Generalist Graph Anomaly Detection (GGAD) requires a single model to detect anomalies across diverse, unseen graph domains without retraining, but existing generalist methods (ARC, UNPrompt) fail to handle two fundamental cross-domain shifts: Feature Space Shift (FSS), where node attribute distributions differ across domains, and Graph Structure Shift (GSS), where graph topologies vary significantly. This paper formalizes both shifts with MMD-based quantitative metrics and shows that no prior method robustly addresses both simultaneously.

## Input / Output

The method takes an attributed graph with node feature matrix X ∈ R^{N×d} and adjacency matrix A ∈ {0,1}^{N×N}; raw features of varying dimensionality across domains are first projected to a shared latent dimension d_u via a learnable linear layer. At inference, the model produces a per-node continuous anomaly score S(v_i) that is a weighted fusion of a residual-dispersion score (semantic, FSS-oriented) and a local-affinity score (structural, GSS-oriented), then converts it to a binary label via a data-driven adaptive threshold that maximizes normal/anomaly separation without requiring target-domain labels.

## Method

IA-GGAD combines two complementary modules trained jointly on source graphs. For FSS, a modified VQ-VAE constructs a shared invariant feature pool E of M domain-invariant prototypes; a parameter-shared GCN encoder produces multi-hop embeddings, and each node's representation is enriched by concatenating its k nearest prototypes, followed by multi-hop residual encoding (successive differences from the 1-hop baseline) trained with a margin-based residual similarity loss. For GSS, a separate Graph Affinity Encoder (GAE) maps nodes to a latent space and computes a local homophily score (mean cosine similarity to neighbors), trained unsupervised by maximizing this affinity so that anomalous nodes are implicitly relegated to the low-affinity tail. At inference, the two signals are fused via S(v_i) = (1−λ)·RS(v_i) + λ·(1−AS(v_i)), and an adaptive threshold τ* is selected to maximize the separation between normal and anomalous score distributions, enabling fully zero-shot prediction on any unseen graph.

## Results

IA-GGAD achieves the best AUROC on 7 out of 8 zero-shot target datasets, with a mean rank of 1.12 versus 2.38 for the prior SOTA (ARC). The largest gains are +12.28% AUROC on ACM (93.49 vs. ARC's 79.88) and +10.46% on Facebook (80.03 vs. 67.56). On large-scale financial graphs, IA-GGAD reaches 74.24 AUROC on Elliptic (vs. 57.43 for SpaceGNN) and 75.41 on TFinance (vs. 67.57 for AnomalyGFM). The sole loss is on Weibo (91.18 vs. DOMINANT's 92.88, −1.70%), attributed to Weibo's extremely strong local homophily favoring reconstruction-based detection.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest GGAD baseline ARC, IA-GGAD wins on 7 of 8 datasets with the largest margin on ACM (+12.28%) and Facebook (+10.46%), while losing narrowly on Weibo (−1.70%). Against UNPrompt, IA-GGAD dominates on all 8 datasets, with gaps ranging from +1.11% (CiteSeer) to +45.62% (Weibo). Against the best single-graph GAD method GCTAM, IA-GGAD leads on 7/8 datasets (e.g., +12.28% on ACM, +10.46% on Facebook) but trails on Weibo where GCTAM scores 70.61 vs. IA-GGAD's 91.18—actually IA-GGAD still wins on Weibo; the only loss is to DOMINANT. On large-scale financial graphs (Table 4), IA-GGAD outperforms all 10 baselines including AnomalyGFM (+7.84% on TFinance) and SpaceGNN (+16.81% on Elliptic), and is the only method that avoids OOM on DGraph-Fin. Traditional supervised GAD methods (GCN, GAT, BGNN, GHRN) fall near chance level (40–60% AUROC) without retraining, confirming that per-graph retraining is essential for those approaches but not for IA-GGAD.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the generalist graph anomaly detection (GGAD) line initiated by ARC (in-context learning with feature alignment) and UNPrompt (prompt-based node unification) by explicitly decomposing the cross-domain challenge into two formalized shifts—Feature Space Shift (FSS) and Graph Structure Shift (GSS)—and addressing each with a dedicated module. The invariant feature pool borrows the codebook assignment-update mechanism from VQ-VAE, while the structure-insensitive affinity encoder generalizes the homophily-based scoring of TAM/GCTAM into a cross-graph, structure-agnostic form. The MMD-based quantification of FSS and GSS, together with the additive-kernel generalization bound, provides a theoretical grounding that prior GGAD work lacked.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for any target-domain data or retraining, IA-GGAD moves GGAD closer to practical deployment where a single detector must serve many heterogeneous graphs. The formal FSS/GSS decomposition and the empirical finding that the optimal fusion weight λ correlates with the dominant shift type offer a diagnostic framework for understanding why prior generalist methods fail on specific datasets. Key open questions remain: scalability to million-node graphs, extension to dynamic and heterogeneous graphs, and whether a unified representation can jointly normalize both feature semantics and structural patterns rather than treating them separately.
