## Problem

Existing GNN-based methods for heterogeneous hypergraphs aggregate information along individual hyperedges but fail to capture the complex structural features and richer semantics arising from the composition of multiple hyper-relations. While meta-paths (sequences of binary relations) are well-established for heterogeneous simple graphs, they cannot be directly extended to heterogeneous hypergraphs due to the multi-arity of hyper-relations, leaving a gap in modeling higher-order compositional structure.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251439/cropped_page1_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a heterogeneous hypergraph defined by an incidence matrix, node/hyperedge type mappings, and per-node feature vectors, where both node types and hyperedge types (hyper-relations) are heterogeneous (|A|>1 and |R|>1). Beyond standard node and hyperedge embeddings, HHNN outputs interpretable hyper-meta-paths—sequences of composed hyper-relations—extracted from the learned attention weight distributions, which provide semantic explanations for individual node predictions. The model is trained semi-supervised with cross-entropy loss on a subset of labeled target nodes.

## Method

HHNN introduces a three-level attention aggregation scheme per layer: (1) α-attention projects node features into a hyperedge-specific space and aggregates them into hyperedge representations; (2) β-attention performs intra-type aggregation, projecting hyperedge features back to a node-specific space and attending over hyperedges of the same type; (3) γ-attention performs inter-type aggregation, fusing the per-type temporary node representations into a unified node embedding. This hierarchical structure implicitly encodes hyper-meta-paths (sequences of hyper-relations sharing common node types), and the learned attention coefficients at each level determine which compositional paths are most important. A theoretical analysis (Appendix E) shows that the product of attention weights along a path yields its importance score, enabling automatic discovery of the most influential hyper-meta-path per node. The architecture is trained end-to-end with a standard cross-entropy loss, and the time complexity is linear in the number of nodes and hyperedges.

## Results

On the Movielens dataset (20% training ratio), HHNN achieves 56.80 Micro F1, 26.24 Macro F1, and 73.21 AUC, outperforming the strongest baseline EDHNN (53.41 / 18.74 / 70.07) by 3.39, 7.50, and 3.14 points respectively. On the Olist dataset (20% training ratio), HHNN reaches 72.54 Micro F1, 72.89 Macro F1, and 93.20 AUC, surpassing the best baseline AllDeepSets (66.96 / 66.95 / 90.29) by 5.58, 5.94, and 2.91 points. At 40% training ratio, HHNN further extends its lead to 60.65 Micro F1 on Movielens and 77.74 Micro F1 on Olist, maintaining consistent superiority across all metrics and training ratios.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HHNN outperforms all ten baselines across both datasets and all three metrics (Micro F1, Macro F1, AUC) at both 20% and 40% training ratios. The closest competitor on Movielens is EDHNN (equivariant hypergraph diffusion), which trails HHNN by 3.4 Micro F1 and 3.1 AUC at 20% training; on Olist, AllDeepSets is the strongest baseline but still lags by 5.6 Micro F1 and 2.9 AUC. The naive CE-GCN baseline (clique expansion + GCN) performs surprisingly competitively on Movielens (55.46 Micro) but collapses on Olist (32.31 Micro), highlighting that simple graph reduction fails to capture genuine multi-ary hyper-relations. HGNN+ outperforms HGNN consistently, confirming the importance of handling hyperedge heterogeneity, yet both remain well below HHNN. In the appendix, HHNN also beats HAN (heterogeneous graph attention) on ACM (87.28 vs. 85.09) and DBLP (91.29 vs. 90.02), and substantially outperforms SheafHyperGNN and UniG-Encoder on both primary datasets (e.g., 72.54 vs. 51.71 Micro on Olist vs. SheafHyperGNN). On the simpler homogeneous hypergraph datasets (Cora, PubMed, DBLP), all methods perform comparably, suggesting HHNN's advantage is most pronounced on genuinely heterogeneous hypergraphs with natural multi-ary relations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper generalizes the meta-path concept from heterogeneous simple graphs (where a meta-path is a sequence of binary relations) to heterogeneous hypergraphs by defining a hyper-meta-path as a sequence of multi-ary hyper-relations that share common node types. Methodologically, HHNN extends the standard node-to-hyperedge-to-node aggregation pattern used in prior heterogeneous hypergraph GNNs (HHNE, HGNN+, DisenHCN) by decomposing the hyperedge-to-node step into two sub-steps (intra-type β-attention and inter-type γ-attention), yielding a three-level attention hierarchy. The interpretability mechanism—back-tracing learned attention weights to identify which hyper-meta-paths drive predictions—draws on the attention-based interpretability tradition from HAN and GAT but applies it to the multi-ary hyperedge setting for the first time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The hyper-meta-path concept provides a principled, human-readable vocabulary for describing complex structural patterns in heterogeneous hypergraphs, bridging the gap between high-order relational structure and model interpretability. However, the paper itself acknowledges that the attention-based discovery is correlational rather than causal, and that the hyper-meta-path definition may be too broad when shared node types are generic or high-frequency. Open questions include whether causal interpretability (e.g., counterfactual reasoning) can be formalized for hyper-meta-paths, whether the approach scales to hypergraphs with many more node/hyperedge types, and whether the gains persist on larger, more diverse benchmarks beyond the two constructed datasets.
