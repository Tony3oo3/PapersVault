## Problem

Existing diversity metrics for training data rely on surface-level heuristics—semantic embeddings, lexical features, or task-specific taxonomies—that are decoupled from model behavior and show only moderate correlation with downstream generalization. The paper asks what kind of diversity in training data actually drives out-of-distribution generalization in LLM reasoning, and how to measure and amplify it in a task-agnostic, scalable way.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253032/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

G-Vendi takes a dataset of input-output pairs and a small off-the-shelf proxy model (e.g., Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct), computing per-sample normalized loss gradients that are projected to 1024 dimensions via Rademacher random projection before scoring; the output is a single positive unbounded scalar interpretable as the effective number of unique samples. Prismatic Synthesis takes a seed dataset and a generator LLM (e.g., Qwen2.5-72B-Instruct) and iteratively produces a diversified synthetic dataset (1.0M pairs for PrismMath, 515K for PrismNLI) with no human annotation, where each candidate sample is accepted only if it falls in a sparse gradient-space cluster.

## Method

G-Vendi measures dataset diversity as the exponentiated entropy (Vendi Score) of the normalized covariance matrix of per-sample loss gradients computed under a small off-the-shelf proxy model, projected to 1024 dimensions via Rademacher random projection; this gradient-space representation captures task-relevant cognitive processes (e.g., reasoning strategy) rather than surface-form features, achieving Spearman's ρ≈0.9 with OOD benchmark performance and outperforming embedding-based Vendi scores and LLM-based skill-set entropy. Building on this, Prismatic Synthesis iteratively (1) clusters existing data in gradient space via K-means, (2) generates new samples via few-shot LLM prompting, and (3) retains only those falling in the sparsest clusters (smallest k/2 clusters), greedily filling underrepresented regions of gradient space. The framework requires no in-distribution warm-up, weight decomposition, or target distribution knowledge, and scales to million-sample datasets using only a 0.5B proxy model for gradient computation. Data quality is ensured via majority-voting filtering (N=3 solutions, τ=2 threshold for math) and conservative decontamination combining 10-gram matching with LLM-based paraphrase detection.

## Results

PrismMath-7B, distilled from R1-32B on 1.0M Prismatic Synthesis samples, achieves 75.25% average pass@1 across 7 math benchmarks, outperforming R1-Distill-Qwen-7B (72.80%)—trained on proprietary data from a 671B teacher—on 6 out of 7 benchmarks, including 57.08% on AIME24 and 38.33% on AIME25. PrismNLI (515K samples) achieves 74.28% average OOD accuracy across 8 NLI benchmarks, surpassing the best prior human-annotated mixture ({WA+M+S}NLI, 943K samples) by 8.1% while using roughly half the data. G-Vendi, the proposed gradient-entropy diversity metric, achieves Spearman's ρ ≈ 0.90 and R² ≈ 0.82 with OOD model performance, substantially outperforming Embedding Vendi (ρ = 0.754) and Skill Set Entropy (ρ = 0.812) on math reasoning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PrismMath-7B outperforms R1-Distill-Qwen-7B (trained on 671B-generated data with human verification) on 6 of 7 benchmarks, with the largest gaps on AIME25 (+5.0 pts) and GSM8K Platinum (+6.0 pts), while narrowly losing on MATH500 (92.40 vs. 92.60). Against OpenThinker2-7B (94K R1-671B data), PrismMath-7B wins on 6 of 7 benchmarks, led by AIME24 (+7.1 pts) and AMC23 (+5.3 pts), but trails on Olympiad Bench (68.30 vs. 69.63). PrismMath-7B dominates OpenR1-Qwen-7B and OpenThinker-7B across all 7 benchmarks, with particularly large margins on AIME24 (+9.2 and +29.6 pts respectively). On NLI, PrismNLI (515K, fully synthetic) beats the best prior human-annotated mixture {WA+M+S}NLI (943K) by 8.1% in average OOD accuracy (74.28 vs. 66.18), winning on all 8 benchmarks including HANS (+12.3) and ANLI R2 (+11.3). For diversity metric comparison, G-Vendi (R²=0.823, ρ=0.899) substantially outperforms Embedding Vendi (R²=0.659, ρ=0.754) and Skill Set Entropy (R²=0.706, ρ=0.812) in predicting OOD generalization on math reasoning, and also leads on NLI (R²=0.791 vs. 0.622 for Embedding Vendi).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Vendi Score [8] by applying it to gradient representations rather than embedding or feature spaces, and on gradient-based data influence estimation [48] by simplifying the setup to use a single off-the-shelf 0.5B proxy model without the in-distribution warm-up, weight decomposition, or multi-checkpoint aggregation required by prior gradient-based selection methods [61, 57, 20]. It departs from the dominant line of diversity measurement for instruction tuning—which relies on embedding similarity, n-gram entropy, or LLM-extracted skill taxonomies [58, 33, 60, 66]—by arguing that these surface-form proxies are decoupled from the cognitive processes that drive reasoning generalization. The Prismatic Synthesis framework extends iterative synthetic data generation [44, 16, 9] with a gradient-space clustering-and-rejection loop, positioning itself as a principled alternative to heuristic diversification strategies like persona-conditioned prompting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a 0.5B proxy model's gradient entropy (G-Vendi) predicts OOD generalization with ρ≈0.9—outperforming a 14× larger embedding model and GPT-4-based skill extraction—provides a cheap, task-agnostic tool for data curation that could be broadly adopted in LLM post-training pipelines. The demonstration that diversity can override 10× scale for OOD performance, and that naive synthetic data scaling saturates while gradient-space diversification continues to improve, has direct practical implications for how teams allocate compute between data generation and data curation. Open questions remain around target-conditional diversity (incorporating knowledge of a specific target distribution), the interplay between data quality filtering and diversity measurement, and whether the approach transfers to domains with limited off-the-shelf benchmarks.
