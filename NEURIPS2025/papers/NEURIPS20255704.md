## Problem

Existing LLM-driven automated scientific discovery systems focus on open-ended research with unclear goals and hard-to-verify outcomes, leaving the foundational challenge of discovering novel, verifiable ML architectures unaddressed. This paper asks whether LLMs can model the full research process—ideation, literature search, code implementation, pre-training, and evaluation—to discover language model architectures that compete with or surpass known designs like GPT-2 and Mamba2.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255704/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system consumes parent block designs represented as factorized GAU trees (sequences of typed sub-units of type (X,Z)→(X,Z)), curated academic references (297 LM papers with code in a vector store), and the SmolLM pretraining corpus. It produces executable PyTorch architecture code, pretrained models at 14M–350M parameter scales trained on up to 50B tokens, and zero-shot evaluation scores across 9 downstream benchmarks. A key constraint is that all generated code must pass both static (AST-based) and runtime (PyTorch-based) symbolic checks for differentiability, causality, and numerical stability before entering the evolution tree.

## Method

Genesys employs a genetic programming backbone over an evolution tree where each design is factorized into a GAU tree of typed sub-units, enabling targeted mutation, crossover, and scratch-design operations. A multi-agent pipeline handles the research workflow: a proposer-reviewer pair drafts and adversarially scores research ideas, then a planner-coder-observer trio implements designs unit-by-unit using a Viterbi-style search that checkpoints partial successes and retries only failed units, yielding an exponential reduction in expected model calls over direct prompting (formalized in Appendix A.1). Verification follows a Ladder-of-Scales strategy inspired by scaling laws, allocating progressively fewer training trials at larger parameter scales (1,000 models at 14M down to 5 at 350M) with dynamic budget release to balance exploration and exploitation. The LMADE environment provides a knowledge engine (literature access) and verification engine (automated pretraining and LM-Eval evaluation) that supply feedback to the discovery loop.

## Results

At the 350M-parameter scale (50B training tokens), the best discovered design (Geogate) achieves 61.81% average accuracy across 9 downstream benchmarks, marginally surpassing the GPT-2 seed (61.78%) and clearly outperforming Mamba2 (61.45%), RWKV7 (61.14%), RetNet (59.78%), and TTT (59.71%). Collectively, the five discovered designs outperform or match the human baselines on 6/9 benchmarks. The system produced 1,062 fully verified novel architectures (14M–350M parameters) through >1B tokens of pretraining and 86K agent interactions, with the evolutionary process showing sustained fitness improvement (Δ = 7.13% over 1000 designs) and a Sharpe Ratio of 0.265.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GPT-2 (61.78% avg), the top discovered design Geogate (61.81%) edges it out on average, with individual wins on WSC (58.96 vs. 56.76) and MRPC (68.63 vs. 68.38), while GPT retains a large lead on BLiMP (92.70 vs. 90.95). Against Mamba2 (61.45%), discovered models win on WNLI (SRN 65.52, HMamba 64.79 vs. 63.38), WG (HMamba 53.59 vs. 51.22), SST-2 (HMamba 57.04 vs. 56.58), and WSC (Geogate 58.96 vs. 57.12), but Mamba2 leads on RTE (63.88) and CoLA (55.94). Against RWKV7 (61.14%), discovered models dominate on WNLI, WG, SST-2, and WSC, while RWKV7 retains an advantage on IS (57.00). Against RetNet (59.78%) and TTT (59.71%), all five discovered designs achieve higher average accuracy, with the gap most pronounced on WNLI and SST-2. Overall, no single discovered model dominates all 9 tasks, but the collective best across discovered designs beats the collective best across seeds on 6/9 benchmarks, confirming competitive performance with human-designed architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of genetic programming (Koza, 1994), LLM-driven code evolution (Hemberg et al., 2024; Romera-Paredes et al., 2024), and neural architecture search, but departs from all three in key ways. Unlike traditional NAS which searches over fixed operation spaces, Genesys operates in an unbounded code space where LLMs generate novel units, and unlike open-ended LLM-driven research systems (AI Scientist, AgentLab), it targets a task with clear success criteria and automated verification. The Viterbi-style unit-by-unit code generation strategy, formalized with exponential-gain proofs, is the paper's most distinctive algorithmic contribution, building on the Viterbi decoding framework but applied to iterative code synthesis with local checkpoints rather than sequence alignment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the largest automated LM architecture discovery experiment to date (1,062 verified designs, >1B tokens, 2.76M lines of code), and the finding that LLM-discovered architectures are competitive with GPT-2 and Mamba2 on 6/9 benchmarks demonstrates feasibility of LLM-driven competitive ML research. Key limitations include the ceiling at 350M parameters (billion-scale discovery remains infeasible), the inability to incorporate hardware-specific optimizations like FlashAttention, and the reliance on a curated 297-paper reference library. Open questions include whether the evolutionary process continues to improve at larger scales, whether reinforcement learning can replace the current heuristic selection strategies, and whether the 'hubness' structure in the evolution tree reflects genuine architectural convergence or search artifacts.
