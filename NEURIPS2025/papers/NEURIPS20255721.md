## Problem

During DPO training, large negative gradients applied to low-confidence non-preference samples trigger a 'squeezing effect' in softmax output heads, compressing probability mass toward an unrelated highest-confidence token and simultaneously reducing the log-probabilities of both preference and non-preference samples. This issue is particularly severe in reasoning tasks where off-policy distribution shift and solution-path diversity make non-preference samples more likely to fall into the low-confidence 'valley' region, yet existing DPO variants apply uniform regularization without accounting for the heterogeneous information content of individual non-preference samples.

## Input / Output

The method consumes text problems (math or science) formatted with few-shot demonstrations, from which N=10 solutions are sampled at temperature 0.8 and classified into preference pairs based on answer correctness. A key intermediate computation is the Pointwise V-Information (PVI_l) of each non-preference sample's reasoning chain, evaluated under the frozen initial policy model π₀, which determines the per-sample constraint strength. The trained policy model outputs a reasoning chain followed by a final numerical or letter answer, evaluated on benchmarks like MATH500, GSM8K, AMC23, and ARC-Challenge.

## Method

VPO modifies the DPO loss by scaling the non-preference log-likelihood ratio term by (1−v), where v is a per-sample constraint parameter derived from V-usable information. Specifically, PVI_l = −log π₀(y|x) + log π₀(y|x,c_l) measures how much label-related information the non-preference reasoning chain c_l introduces beyond the input x. If PVI_l > 0 (the non-preference sample carries positive label information, indicating strong correlation with preference samples), no constraint is applied (v=0) to preserve discriminability. If PVI_l < 0 (weak or negative correlation), the constraint is set to v = σ(−PVI_l), so more negative PVI yields stronger suppression of the negative gradient. This selective mechanism mitigates the squeezing effect for the most vulnerable samples while leaving well-correlated pairs unconstrained, and the theoretical analysis shows that constraining the negative gradient simultaneously amplifies the positive gradient on preference samples, steering them toward the highest-confidence region.

## Results

On Qwen2.5-7B-Base, VPO achieves 50.78 average accuracy across five math benchmarks, outperforming DPO (44.57) by 6.21 points, with gains of 7.80% on MATH500 (68.80 vs 61.00) and 13.25% on AMC23 (45.78 vs 32.53). On Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, where DPO severely degrades performance (19.11 Avg vs 38.18 base), VPO recovers and exceeds the base model (39.30 Avg). VPO also achieves the best overall performance on Qwen3-14B-Base (54.30 Avg), surpassing the strongest baseline RPO (50.97) by 3.33 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VPO consistently outperforms all five baselines (DPO, TDPO, SimPO, IPO, RPO) across every model setting and benchmark. Against DPO, the most direct comparison, VPO gains 6.21 Avg points on Qwen2.5-7B-Base and 20.19 Avg points on Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, where DPO actually degrades the base model (19.11 vs 38.18) due to the squeezing effect. Against RPO (the strongest baseline on Qwen2.5-7B-Base at 46.23 Avg), VPO leads by 4.55 points, with particularly large margins on AMC23 (45.78 vs 31.33). Against SimPO, which uses reference-free average log-probability rewards, VPO achieves 50.78 vs 43.83 Avg on Qwen2.5-7B-Base. IPO performs competitively on Instruct models (52.96 Avg on Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct) but collapses on Llama-3.1-8B-Base (13.42 Avg), while VPO maintains consistent gains across all settings. TDPO, a token-level variant, is outperformed by VPO in every configuration, with the largest gap on Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct (39.30 vs 24.18 Avg).

## Relation to Prior Work

VPO extends DPO (Rafailov et al., 2024) by introducing a per-sample negative gradient constraint on non-preference responses, motivated by the squeezing effect identified by Ren and Sutherland (2025). Rather than applying a uniform regularization (as RPO does) or modifying the reward formulation (as SimPO, IPO, or TDPO do), VPO uses Pointwise V-Information (PVI) from the V-usable information framework (Xu et al., 2020; Ethayarajh et al., 2025) to measure the information-theoretic correlation between each non-preference sample's reasoning chain and the preference sample, then selectively constrains the negative gradient only for low-correlation (negative-PVI) samples. This positions VPO at the intersection of preference optimization and information theory, departing from prior DPO variants that treat all rejected samples symmetrically.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The squeezing effect is a documented failure mode of DPO that is particularly acute in reasoning tasks, where it can cause absolute performance degradation (e.g., Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct drops from 38.18 to 19.11 under DPO). VPO's PVI-based selective constraint offers a principled mechanism to reconcile the competing goals of alleviating squeezing and preserving preference discriminability, and the consistent gains across Llama 3.1, Qwen 2.5, and Qwen3-14B models (both Base and Instruct) suggest broad applicability. Open questions include whether the ad hoc sigmoid normalization of PVI is optimal, whether the approach generalizes beyond mathematical and scientific reasoning to open-ended generation tasks, and whether the per-sample PVI computation scales efficiently to very large preference datasets.
