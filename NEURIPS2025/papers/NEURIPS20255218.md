## Problem

Existing graph data augmentation methods either rely on simple static transformations with limited gains or use diffusion models that are task-specific and constrained by class labels, preventing generalization across node classification, link prediction, and graph classification. No prior diffusion-based GDA framework is simultaneously label-free, task-agnostic, and evaluated for ML safety measures (calibration, corruption, consistency) and adversarial robustness.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a graph with node feature matrix X ∈ R^{n×d} and adjacency matrix A ∈ R^{n×n}, encodes them into a compact latent space via GVAE, and generates new node feature vectors and edge connections decoded back to the original feature space. Because the conditioning vector is derived from mean-aggregated 1-hop neighborhood latent embeddings rather than class labels, the same pipeline can augment unlabeled test samples (test-time augmentation) without any label assignment, which is the key operational distinction from prior label-conditioned diffusion GDA methods.

## Method

D-GDA combines three components: (1) a Target Sample Selector that ranks training samples by prediction entropy and augments only the top-k most uncertain nodes under a fixed budget; (2) a Graph Variational Autoencoder with GCN encoder, feature decoder, and link predictor, regularized by edge masking (rate 0.3) and feature masking (rate 0.5) to learn a 64-dim latent representation; (3) a Latent Diffusion Model (DDPM, T=1000, 1D-UNet denoiser) that generates new latent vectors conditioned on the mean-aggregated 1-hop neighborhood embedding of the target node, rather than on class labels. The generated latent vector is decoded into node features by the GVAE feature decoder, and the augmented node is connected to its most probable neighbor as scored by the GVAE link predictor. This neighborhood-aware conditioning preserves local graph structure while introducing diversity, and the label-free design extends augmentation to validation and test sets, yielding consistent gains in accuracy, calibration, corruption robustness, and adversarial resilience.

## Results

On node classification (small-scale), D-GDA achieves 89.10% on Cora, 87.30% on Flickr, 81.50% on Citeseer, and 88.20% on Pubmed (mean 86.53%), outperforming the best baseline (NASA at 85.10% on Cora) by 4.0%. On large-scale datasets, D-GDA reaches 74.80% on OGBN-Arxiv and 78.85% on OGBN-Products, surpassing DoG (73.10%) and FLAG (76.14%) respectively. For link prediction, D-GDA achieves 96.96% AUC on Cora and 97.82% on Pubmed (GCN backbone), and for graph classification, 94.82% ROC-AUC on molClinTox and 86.12% on molBACE (GIN backbone). Under long-tailed imbalance (ρ=100), D-GDA attains 80.34% balanced accuracy on Cora-LT and 64.95% on Citeseer-LT, exceeding GraphVCM (75.81% / 60.53%) by 4.5% and 4.4%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

D-GDA outperforms all 31 compared baselines across every task and dataset. On node classification, it beats the strongest non-diffusion baselines (NASA, 85.10% on Cora; GeoMix, 84.08%) by 4.0–5.0% and the closest diffusion-based competitor DoG (84.00% on Cora, 82.80% on Pubmed) by 5.1% and 5.4% respectively. On large-scale OGBN-Arxiv, D-GDA (74.80%) surpasses DoG (73.10%) and RGDA (72.88%) by 1.7% and 1.9%. For link prediction, D-GDA (96.96% AUC on Cora, GCN) exceeds GM (92.02%) and CFLP (92.55%) by ~4–5%, and on OGBL-ddi it reaches 57.53% Hits@20 vs. GM's 59.18% (a narrow loss) but with far lower variance (±0.15 vs. ±2.09). On graph classification, D-GDA (64.85% molSIDER, 94.82% molClinTox, GIN) edges out DCT (63.90%, 92.10%) by ~1–2.7%. Under long-tailed imbalance, D-GDA (80.34% Cora-LT) leads GraphVCM (75.81%) by 4.5% without requiring class-specific tuning, and its class-agnostic TSS reduces the imbalance ratio from 113.67 to 19.55 on Cora using only a 20% augmentation budget versus 278% for SOTA oversampling methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

D-GDA extends the diffusion-based graph data augmentation line initiated by DoG (Wang et al., TMLR) and DCT (Liu et al., NeurIPS 2024), which both condition generation on class labels and are restricted to specific tasks. The key departure is a label-free, neighborhood-conditioned latent diffusion framework: rather than generating in input graph space or conditioning on class identity, D-GDA operates in a GVAE latent space (building on Kipf & Welling's variational graph autoencoder and masked graph modeling regularization from Li et al. and Mishra et al.) and conditions the denoising process on one-hop neighborhood embeddings. The Target Sample Selector, which uses prediction entropy to focus augmentation on uncertain samples, is a new component not present in prior GDA methods, and the label-free design enables test-time augmentation—a capability absent from all compared baselines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper's most notable contribution beyond accuracy gains is the first systematic evaluation of GDA methods on ML safety measures (calibration, corruption robustness, prediction consistency) and adversarial robustness (four attack types), revealing that augmentation quality directly affects model reliability beyond standard accuracy. The label-free, task-agnostic design broadens applicability to settings where labels are unavailable (test-time) or where class structure is uninformative (long-tailed, link prediction). Open questions include scalability to graphs with millions of nodes (OGBN-Products results are promising but the diffusion step remains the bottleneck), whether neighborhood conditioning generalizes to heterophilic graphs, and whether the TSS entropy criterion remains effective under distribution shift.
