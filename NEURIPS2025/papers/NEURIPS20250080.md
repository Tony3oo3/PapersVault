## Problem

Decomposing LLM in-context learning uncertainty into aleatoric (irreducible data noise) and epistemic (reducible model ignorance) components requires sampling from the implicit Bayesian posterior p(θ|D), which is intractable for LLMs where the Bayesian model is only implicitly defined via de Finetti's theorem. Existing alternatives like the Martingale posterior are computationally expensive and lack exchangeability guarantees, leaving the uncertainty decomposition under-explored for practical ICL settings.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a set of in-context covariate-label pairs (x_i, y_i), a test query x*, and a set of optimisable auxiliary queries Z (restricted to a single example in the x-domain for efficiency). It outputs variational upper bounds on aleatoric uncertainty (as entropy for classification or variance for regression) and corresponding lower bounds on epistemic uncertainty. For classification, the LLM's logits are ensembled over shuffled context permutations to form the predictive distribution; for regression, a Gaussian approximation is applied to enable closed-form entropy and marginalisation computations.

## Method

The core insight is to define a variational aleatoric uncertainty estimator V_a(y*|x*, Z, D) = E_{p(U|Z,D)}[H[p(y*|x*, U, Z, D)]] that conditions on fantasised auxiliary answers U, and prove (Theorem 3.1) that this is an upper bound on the true Bayesian aleatoric uncertainty, with the gap equal to the residual mutual information I[y*; θ | x*, U, Z, D]. The optimal Z is found by minimising this upper bound subject to a KL-divergence constraint (KL filtering) that enforces the necessary exchangeability condition p(y*|x*, Z, D) ≈ p(y*|x*, D). To promote exchangeability in the LLM's autoregressive predictions, the context is shuffled and predictions are ensembled over L random permutations. The epistemic uncertainty is then obtained as the residual: total predictive entropy minus the variational aleatoric estimate, yielding a lower bound. Z candidates are generated via perturbation around x* (or LLM-based rephrasing for NLP tasks), random sampling, or Bayesian optimisation, with perturbation empirically performing best.

## Results

On the Buttons bandit task (p=0.7, α=2), epistemic variance (EV) UCB achieves a worst-case regret of 0.092±0.004, substantially outperforming total variance (TV) UCB (0.199±0.000) and the instruction-prompting LLM baseline (0.132±0.043). For in-context abstention on QA tasks, thresholding by aleatoric uncertainty (AU) from VUD yields higher post-filtering accuracy than total uncertainty (TU): e.g., 90.00% vs. 87.78% on HotpotQA and 72.22% vs. 70.00% on MMLU-Moral (top-10% rejection). Across all five QA datasets, AU filtering matches or exceeds TU filtering, with the largest gains (≈2.2 pp) on HotpotQA and MMLU-Moral.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the Buttons bandit task, EV-UCB (α=2) achieves worst-case regret of 0.092 at p=0.7, beating the non-LLM UCB1 baseline (0.122) and the instruction-prompting baseline from Krishnamurthy et al. (0.132), while TV-UCB (0.199) performs comparably to Greedy (0.199) and is clearly worse than EV. The Martingale posterior baseline (Fong et al., 2023), evaluated in Appendix G.2, produces total uncertainty estimates that do not agree with the LLM's predictive entropy and is highly sensitive to the choice of proxy model, making it unreliable for decomposition. On QA abstention, VUD's aleatoric uncertainty consistently outperforms total uncertainty as a filtering criterion, with the largest margin (2.22 pp) on HotpotQA, confirming that the decomposition captures genuinely different uncertainty sources rather than merely re-weighting the same signal.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the standard aleatoric/epistemic uncertainty decomposition from Bayesian deep learning (Kendall & Gal, 2017) to the in-context learning setting, building on the hypothesis that ICL performs implicit Bayesian inference (Xie et al., 2022). The key departure from prior approaches is avoiding explicit posterior sampling over the latent parameter θ, which is intractable for LLMs: instead, the paper introduces optimisable auxiliary queries Z as variational probes that yield an upper bound on aleatoric uncertainty and a lower bound on epistemic uncertainty. This contrasts with Martingale posterior methods (Falck et al., Fong et al.) that attempt to estimate the posterior over θ via risk minimisation, and with input-clarification approaches (Huo et al., 2024) that lack a formal Bayesian framework. The paper also introduces KL filtering and context shuffling as practical mechanisms to promote the exchangeability condition required by de Finetti's theorem, which prior ICL-as-Bayesian-inference work has largely assumed without enforcement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a theoretically grounded, computationally practical route to decomposing LLM uncertainty without access to the implicit posterior, which is a significant practical gap given that LLMs are black-box systems. The downstream applications (bandit exploration, in-context abstention) demonstrate that the decomposition is not merely descriptive but actionable: epistemic variance guides exploration more effectively than total variance, and aleatoric uncertainty thresholds improve abstention accuracy over total uncertainty. Key limitations include the assumption that ICL is approximately Bayesian (which breaks down in longer sampling horizons), the restriction to classification/regression with short outputs, and the fact that the KL filtering condition is necessary but not sufficient for true Bayesian behaviour. Open questions include extending the framework to natural-language outputs with semantic equivalence and understanding how the quality of the variational bound scales with model size and context length.
