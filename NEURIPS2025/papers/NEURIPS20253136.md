## Problem

Existing 3D generative models produce assets with high geometric and textural fidelity but lack physically meaningful properties such as absolute scale, material composition, affordance, kinematic constraints, and functional descriptions. This omission prevents generated 3D assets from being directly usable in physical simulation, robotics, and embodied AI, and no large-scale dataset systematically annotates 3D objects across all these physical dimensions at the part level.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253136/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

Given a single RGB image as prompt, PhysXGen produces a textured 3D mesh accompanied by per-part physical annotations: absolute scale (length/width/height), material parameters (name, Young's modulus, Poisson's ratio, density in g/cm³), affordance priority scores (1–10 per part), kinematic parameters (joint type, movement direction, location, range, parent-child part indices), and three levels of function descriptions (basic, functional, kinematic). Physical properties are represented as per-voxel feature vectors (14-dim physical + 768×3-dim CLIP text embeddings) aligned to the same voxel grid as the structural representation, enabling part-level rather than object-level annotation.

## Method

PhysXGen is a dual-branch feed-forward framework built on the pretrained TRELLIS structural latent space. A physical 3D VAE encodes concatenated physical properties and CLIP-based description embeddings into an 8-dim physical latent, while the pretrained structural VAE encodes DINOv2 features into a matching 8-dim structural latent; a residual connection from the physical decoder to the structural decoder allows physical information to influence geometry. For generation, a transformer diffusion model trained with Conditional Flow Matching jointly denoises both physical and structural latents, with the structural branch feeding into the physical branch via learnable skip-connections to exploit geometry–physics correlations. The entire system is fine-tuned from TRELLIS weights on the 26K-object PhysXNet dataset (annotated via a GPT-4o-based human-in-the-loop pipeline), enabling efficient training and strong generalization without learning the structural prior from scratch.

## Results

On the PhysXNet test set (1K samples), PhysXGen achieves PSNR 24.53, CD 12.7×10⁻³, and F-Score 77.3×10⁻² for geometry/appearance, slightly surpassing the TRELLIS baseline (24.31 / 13.2 / 76.9). For physical properties, PhysXGen substantially outperforms the independent predictor baseline (TRELLIS+PhysPre): absolute scale error drops from 13.21 to 7.24 (Euclidean distance, lower is better), material PSNR rises from 8.63 to 13.01, affordance PSNR from 7.23 to 11.30, kinematic COV from 0.24 to 0.33, kinematic MMD from 0.12 to 0.08, and description PSNR from 6.55 to 10.11.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two baselines: TRELLIS (geometry-only generation) and TRELLIS+PhysPre (TRELLIS plus a separate, independent physical property predictor). PhysXGen wins on every reported metric. On geometry, the gains are modest (PSNR +0.22, CD −0.5, F-Score +0.4), confirming that the dual-branch design preserves native structural quality. On physical properties, the improvements over TRELLIS+PhysPre are large and consistent: absolute scale error is nearly halved (7.24 vs. 13.21), material PSNR improves by ~51% (13.01 vs. 8.63), affordance PSNR by ~56% (11.30 vs. 7.23), and description PSNR by ~54% (10.11 vs. 6.55). Kinematic parameters also improve (COV 0.33 vs. 0.24; MMD 0.08 vs. 0.12). The ablation (Table 3) further shows that both the dependent VAE and dependent diffusion branches contribute, with the full dual-branch configuration yielding the best scores across all physical and geometric metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the TRELLIS structured 3D latent framework by introducing a parallel physical VAE branch and a dual-branch diffusion model that jointly generates geometry and physical properties (scale, material, affordance, kinematics, descriptions). It departs from prior 3D generation work (TRELLIS, LGM, InstantMesh) that targets only geometry and appearance, and from articulated-object datasets (PartNet-Mobility, GAPartNet) that cover only kinematics, by unifying all five physical dimensions into a single generation task. The annotation pipeline repurposes GPT-4o in a human-in-the-loop fashion to convert the existing PartNet geometry dataset into a physics-annotated one, bridging the gap between structural 3D benchmarks and the physical properties needed for simulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PhysXNet (26K objects) and PhysXNet-XL (6M procedurally generated objects) fill a critical data gap for physics-aware 3D vision, robotics, and embodied AI, where prior datasets offered at most object-level material or part-level kinematics in isolation. The dual-branch design demonstrates that physical properties and geometry are not independent — jointly modeling them improves both physical prediction accuracy and geometry fidelity over a separate-predictor baseline. Key open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond PartNet's synthetic CAD objects to real-world scans, how to handle more complex material behaviors (elasticity, fluid dynamics), and whether the 26K training scale is sufficient for robust generalization to unseen categories.
