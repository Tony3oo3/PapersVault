## Problem

Finding a Chebyshev n-net (optimal placement of n Euclidean balls to cover a compact set) is NP-hard and non-convex for n > 1, unlike the n = 1 Chebyshev center case which admits convex reformulations. Existing approaches rely on randomized methods (e.g., simulated annealing) that do not exploit any structural regularity of the objective, leading to slow convergence. The paper identifies the missing ingredient: a Lipschitz continuity guarantee for the parametric inner max-min problem that would justify and enable numerical gradient-based (zeroth-order) optimization for the outer minimization.

## Input / Output

The method takes a compact set M ⊂ R^N specified as the intersection of 0-sublevel sets of k continuously differentiable constraint functions φ_j, a preassigned number n of ball centers, and an initial point in the restricted search space B(M)^n (a ball of radius 2ε(M,1) centered at the Chebyshev center). It produces an n-tuple of center coordinates (the Chebyshev n-net) and the corresponding covering radius ε(M,n). The inner max-min subproblem at each gradient evaluation is solved to global optimality using the BARON NLP solver, which is a critical requirement for valid coverings.

## Method

The paper reformulates the covering problem via a slack variable t into a parametric non-convex minimization (Eq. 8), then proves (Theorem 3.2) that the resulting marginal function G is locally Lipschitz continuous under the Mangasarian-Fromovitz constraint qualification, using the Fiacco–Ishizuka sensitivity theorem. This regularity result is the key theoretical contribution that justifies deploying a zeroth-order numerical gradient algorithm. Algorithm 1 (grad0ptNet) estimates directional derivatives of G by sampling random unit directions and computing finite-difference quotients, applies stochastic smoothing with a variance-reduction scheme (momentum-like update every q iterations), and projects iterates onto B(M)^n. Global convergence is promoted via multiple randomized restarts (parallel initializations), and the overall complexity is O(N^{3/2} n² ε(M,1) L³ δ⁻¹ ε⁻³) oracle calls to reach a generalized Goldstein stationary point.

## Results

On the solid ellipse (N=2, k=1) with n=4 centers, gradOptNet achieves a covering radius ε(M,4) ≈ 1.682–1.706 across three trials using only 4,800 evaluations of the inner max-min function G, compared to Simulated Annealing's ε(M,4) ≈ 2.227–2.574 requiring 10,000 evaluations. Across 10 randomized restarts for each n, the method converges to final radius values with relative standard deviation < 1%, indicating high robustness. On a nonconvex asymmetric 2D set, the covering radius decreases from ε(M,1)=0.4907 to ε(M,6)=0.1437, and on a 3D nonconvex set from ε(M,1)=2.3441 to ε(M,4)=1.3713, demonstrating scalability to higher dimensions and complex geometries.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares gradOptNet against Simulated Annealing (Metropolis–Hastings sampling with geometric cooling rate) as the sole baseline, since no existing benchmarks or ground-truth results are available in the literature for the Chebyshev n-net problem. gradOptNet decisively outperforms Simulated Annealing on the ellipse example with n=4: it achieves a covering radius of approximately 1.68–1.71 versus 2.23–2.57 for Simulated Annealing, a reduction of roughly 25–35% in the worst-case distance. Moreover, gradOptNet requires only 4,800 oracle evaluations of G compared to 10,000 for Simulated Annealing, meaning it is both more accurate and approximately 52% more computationally efficient. The paper also demonstrates (Example D.1) that replacing the global solver BARON with the local solver IPOPT causes Algorithm 1 to fail entirely in producing a valid cover, underscoring that the method's advantage over gradient-free heuristics is contingent on reliable global solutions to the inner nonconvex problem.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges sensitivity analysis of parametric nonlinear programs (Fiacco and Ishizuka, 1990) with recent zeroth-order optimization methods for nonconvex nonsmooth objectives (Lin et al., 2022; Liu et al., 2024), applying them to the Chebyshev n-net problem for the first time. The n=1 Chebyshev center case was recently addressed by Paruchuri and Chatterjee (2023) exploiting convexity, but the n>1 case lacks such structure and has previously been tackled only by randomized methods (simulated annealing, i.i.d. sampling) or specialized algorithms for low dimensions. The key departure is using the Mangasarian-Fromovitz constraint qualification to establish Lipschitz continuity of the marginal function of the inner max-min problem, thereby justifying the use of numerical gradient-based algorithms for the outer minimization—a connection not previously drawn in the covering literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Chebyshev n-net problem underpins error bounds in function learning, sensor placement, and signal design, yet has lacked a principled gradient-based solution method for general compact sets. The Lipschitz continuity result (Theorem 3.2) provides the theoretical substrate that makes zeroth-order gradient methods applicable, and the empirical results demonstrate clear advantages over simulated annealing in both solution quality and number of function evaluations. However, the inner non-convex max-min problem remains the chief computational bottleneck (requiring a global solver like BARON), and scalability to high dimensions N or large n is unresolved. Open questions include whether DC programming can accelerate the inner solve for convex sets, whether the permutation invariance of G can be exploited to reduce the search space, and whether more refined global convergence guarantees (e.g., via momenta) can replace the ad hoc restart strategy.
