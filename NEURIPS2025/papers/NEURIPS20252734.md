## Problem

Identifiability of deep polynomial neural networks (PNNs)—whether their parameters and hidden representations can be recovered from their input-output map up to trivial equivalences—remains poorly understood. Prior results only established finite identifiability for very high activation degrees or networks with identical layer widths, leaving a standing conjecture (that quadratic-degree pyramidal PNNs are identifiable) unresolved and a general theory for deep PNNs missing. Additionally, PNNs with bias terms, which are standard in practice, had been entirely overlooked in theoretical identifiability analyses.

## Input / Output

The method takes a PNN architecture specification (layer widths d = (d₀, …, d_L) and per-layer activation degrees r = (r₁, …, r_{L-1})) along with weight matrices (and optionally bias vectors) as input, and produces closed-form identifiability conditions, linear-in-width activation degree thresholds, and neurovariety dimension guarantees. The analysis is purely algebraic: it operates on the polynomial parameterization map from weight tuples to homogeneous polynomial vectors, with identifiability characterized by the rank of the Jacobian at generic parameters and the dimension of the associated neurovariety. The results apply to both homogeneous PNNs (no bias) and general PNNs with biases, the latter handled via a homogenization that augments each layer width by one.

## Method

The core contribution is a localization theorem proving that an L-layer homogeneous PNN is finitely identifiable if and only if every consecutive 2-layer subnetwork (with input dimension reduced to the minimum of all preceding widths) is finitely identifiable. This reduces the deep identifiability problem to the shallow case, where 2-layer hPNNs are shown to be equivalent to partially symmetric canonical polyadic tensor decompositions. Kruskal-type uniqueness conditions on the factor matrices then yield explicit sufficient conditions on activation degrees (linear in layer widths, improving prior quadratic bounds). The deep result is proved by induction using a 'last layer map' whose Jacobian is shown to have maximal rank via a certificate polynomial inherited from the shallow case. For PNNs with biases, a homogenization procedure maps each biased PNN to a structured hPNN with augmented widths, and a key proposition shows that uniqueness of the homogenized hPNN implies uniqueness of the original biased PNN, thereby extending all identifiability results to the general case.

## Results

This is a purely theoretical paper with no experimental benchmarks. The headline quantitative result is that the activation degree threshold for identifiability of an L-layer hPNN is linear in layer widths: r_ℓ ≥ 2d_ℓ − 1 (Corollary 17), improving upon the quadratic bound O(d_ℓ²) from [11, Theorem 18]. The main localization theorem (Theorem 11) establishes that an L-layer hPNN is finitely identifiable if and only if every 2-layer subnetwork (with reduced input dimension d̃_ℓ = min{d_0,…,d_ℓ}) is finitely identifiable. As a corollary, pyramidal architectures (non-increasing widths) are finitely identifiable for all degrees r_ℓ ≥ 2 (r_{L−1} ≥ 3 if d_L = 1), settling a standing conjecture from [11].

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not run experiments or compare methods empirically; all comparisons are between theoretical bounds. Compared to [11] (Finkel et al., Algebraic Statistics 2025), this work improves the activation degree threshold from quadratic (r_ℓ ≥ O(d_ℓ²)) to linear (r_ℓ ≥ 2d_ℓ − 1), and settles the conjecture that pyramidal PNNs with quadratic activations are identifiable. Compared to [7] (Kileel et al., NeurIPS 2019), which established the hPNN-to-tensor correspondence and neurovariety dimension bounds, this paper extends the analysis from 2-layer to deep networks via the localization theorem and adds the homogenization framework for PNNs with biases (a case overlooked in both [7] and [11]). Compared to ReLU identifiability results [29] (Bui Thi Mai & Lampert, ICLR 2020), which show generic identifiability for non-increasing widths, the PNN analogue here requires an additional degree condition (r_ℓ ≥ 2) but otherwise parallels the ReLU result. The bottleneck identifiability condition (d_ℓ/r_ℓ ≤ d_b − 1, Corollary 19) is weaker than what stronger tensor uniqueness results [83] would give (d_ℓ ≤ (d_b−1)d_b/2 for r_ℓ=2), but the paper's bound is constructive and algorithmically recoverable.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the neuroalgebraic geometry framework initiated by Kileel et al. [7] (who established the hPNN-to-partially-symmetric-tensor correspondence) and Finkel et al. [11] (who studied activation degree thresholds for expressiveness) to the deep-network identifiability setting. The key methodological departure is the use of Kruskal-type uniqueness theorems from tensor decomposition [80–83] as the engine for a localization argument that reduces deep identifiability to 2-layer subnetwork conditions. Prior work on deep NN identifiability (Fefferman for tanh, Bui Thi Mai & Lampert for ReLU) used entirely different proof techniques (singularity analysis, piecewise-linearity), and the tensor-decomposition route to deep nonlinear identifiability had not been explored before.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The localization theorem (Theorem 11) provides the first general sufficient condition for finite identifiability of deep PNNs, settling a standing conjecture that pyramidal architectures with quadratic activations are identifiable and improving the activation-degree threshold from quadratic to linear in layer width. This has direct implications for interpretability (recovering hidden representations), model stitching, and understanding when pretrained PNNs can be pruned or recombined. Open questions include whether finite identifiability implies global identifiability for deep PNNs (Conjecture 40), whether the results extend to general polynomial (non-monomial) activations, and how tensor-based algorithms can be adapted for training deep PNNs with identifiability guarantees.
