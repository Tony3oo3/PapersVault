## Problem

In non-IID federated learning, naively combining momentum (for speed) and sharpness-aware minimization (for flatness) leaves two structural failure modes unresolved: local-global curvature misalignment, where local SAM perturbation directions fail to reflect the global loss geometry, and momentum-echo oscillation, where accumulated momentum causes late-stage instability. Existing methods address either convergence speed or generalization but lack a unified mechanism that simultaneously aligns local updates to global descent geometry and adaptively damps momentum as training progresses.

## Input / Output

Each client receives the global model parameters, a personalized momentum vector (global momentum plus a SCAFFOLD-style correction term), and an adaptive momentum factor α_r, then performs B local SGD steps on its private image data. The SAM perturbation direction is computed as the difference between the current local model and the momentum-inferred global model position (x_r + bΔ_k^r − x_{b,k}^r), requiring only a single backpropagation rather than SAM's standard two. The server aggregates client updates to produce the next global model and updates the correction terms and α_r for the following round.

## Method

FedWMSAM integrates three mechanisms: (1) personalized momentum that fuses the server-aggregated global momentum with a SCAFFOLD-inspired correction term c_k, decoupling the correction from the momentum vector to save communication bandwidth; (2) a momentum-guided global perturbation where the SAM direction is set to the deviation between the local model and the momentum-inferred global position, aligning local sharpness exploration with global descent geometry while preserving single-backprop efficiency; and (3) a cosine-similarity adaptive weight α_r that monotonically increases as the alignment between local and global directions improves, producing an early-momentum/late-SAM two-phase schedule that accelerates initial convergence and then suppresses momentum-echo oscillation for flat final solutions. The paper provides a non-IID convergence bound of Õ(√(LΔσ_ρ²/(SKR)) + (LΔ/R)(1+N^{2/3}/S)) that explicitly models the perturbation-induced variance σ_ρ² = σ² + (Lρ)².

## Results

On CIFAR-10 under Dirichlet β=0.1, FedWMSAM achieves 76.64% top-1 accuracy, improving over FedAvg by +6.59 points and over the strongest SAM baseline (FedSMOO, 75.07%) by +1.57 points. Under the more extreme pathological split (γ=3), FedWMSAM reaches 74.46%, surpassing FedAvg by +10.2 points and MoFedSAM by +1.19 points. On CIFAR-100 (β=0.1), FedWMSAM attains 46.46%, a +8.31-point gain over FedAvg and +16.59 over FedSMOO. In terms of convergence speed, FedWMSAM reaches 74% accuracy in 153 rounds (vs. 255 for FedCM and 169 for MoFedSAM) at a per-round client time of 15.03 s, comparable to FedAvg (14.57 s) and far below double-backprop SAM methods (26.9–29.8 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedWMSAM outperforms all seven baselines on CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100 under strong non-IID settings (β=0.1 and pathological splits), with the largest margins over FedAvg (+6.59 to +10.2 pts on CIFAR-10; +8.31 to +7.52 pts on CIFAR-100) and over FedGAMMA, which collapses to 1.98% on CIFAR-100 Path γ=10. Against the closest SAM-family competitors, FedWMSAM leads FedSMOO by +1.57 pts on CIFAR-10 (β=0.1) and by +16.59 pts on CIFAR-100 (β=0.1), and beats MoFedSAM by +2.78 pts on CIFAR-10 (β=0.1) and +1.04 pts on CIFAR-100 (Path γ=10). On the easier Fashion-MNIST splits, FedWMSAM is best or second-best, trailing SCAFFOLD by only 0.33 pts on Dir β=0.6. On OfficeHome (real-world domain shift), FedWMSAM is best on 3 of 4 domains (Art 99.42%, Clipart 96.50%, Product 97.90%) and slightly trails SCAFFOLD on Real-World (97.17% vs. 97.49%). In convergence speed (Table 3), FedWMSAM matches FedCM/MoFedSAM at the 70% target (97 rounds) but pulls ahead at higher targets—reaching 74% in 153 rounds vs. 255 (FedCM) and 169 (MoFedSAM)—while maintaining near-FedAvg per-round time (15.03 s vs. 14.57 s), unlike double-backprop SAM methods that require 26.9–29.8 s per round.

## Relation to Prior Work

FedWMSAM sits at the intersection of three established FL lines: client-level momentum (FedCM, MIME), SCAFFOLD-style bias correction, and sharpness-aware minimization (FedSAM, MoFedSAM, FedGAMMA, FedSMOO, FedLESAM). Its key departure is that prior methods either apply momentum or SAM in isolation, or naively combine them (as in MoFedSAM), leaving two structural issues—local-global curvature misalignment and momentum-echo oscillation—unresolved under non-IID data. FedWMSAM addresses both by repurposing server-aggregated momentum as a global geometric carrier for the SAM perturbation (eliminating the second backward pass) and by coupling the two mechanisms through a cosine-similarity adaptive schedule. The convergence analysis builds on the SCAFFOLD-M framework, extending it to explicitly model perturbation-induced variance σ_ρ² = σ² + (Lρ)².

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in achieving SAM-level generalization at near-FedAvg per-round cost (15.03s vs. 26.9–29.8s for double-backprop SAM methods), which is critical for edge/IoT FL deployments where communication and compute budgets are tight. The convergence bound's explicit dependence on (S, K, R, N) and perturbation strength provides practitioners with a principled way to tune ρ and sampling rates under heterogeneity. However, the paper acknowledges limitations: the decay coefficient λ is dataset-sensitive, the momentum-guided SAM direction lacks a formal generalization guarantee beyond convergence, and evaluation is confined to image classification—applicability to NLP, graph learning, or other modalities remains unexplored. A key open question is whether the cosine-similarity adaptive schedule generalizes to settings with highly dynamic client participation or streaming data.
