## Problem

Existing C-SEO evaluations test methods on a single task, a single domain, and a single actor, leaving it unknown whether any C-SEO technique generalizes across application domains or remains effective when multiple content providers competitively adopt the same method. The paper asks whether current white-hat C-SEO methods actually improve a document's citation ranking in conversational search engines, and how their effectiveness changes as the number of adopters grows.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250923/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark takes 10 retrieved web documents (product descriptions, news articles, or web pages) paired with a user query, applies a C-SEO text transformation to a subset of those documents, and feeds the modified set to an LLM-based conversational search engine. The output is the position of each document's inline citation in the LLM's generated response, from which rank improvement (baseline rank minus post-C-SEO rank) is computed per document and averaged across queries. The evaluation also sweeps the adoption rate from 0% to 100% (1 to 10 of 10 documents modified) and reports the Area Under the Curve of average gains as a single summary score.

## Method

C-SEO Bench introduces the first multi-task, multi-domain, multi-actor benchmark for evaluating white-hat C-SEO methods, spanning product recommendation (retail, video games, books) and question answering (web, news, debate) with over 1.9k queries and 16k documents. It benchmarks ten content-transformation methods—eight from Aggarwal et al. (Authoritative, Statistics, Citations, Fluency, Unique Words, Technical Terms, Simple Language, Quotes) plus two new ones (LLM Guidance, which prepends an llms.txt-style markdown summary, and Content Improvement, a holistic rewrite combining all eight transformations)—using GPT-4o-mini for transformations and four LLMs (GPT-4o-mini, Claude 3.5 Haiku, o3, o4-mini) as the conversational search engine. The key methodological contribution is a game-theoretic evaluation protocol that varies the fraction of documents (actors) simultaneously applying a C-SEO method, enabling measurement of congestion and zero-sum dynamics. Statistical significance is assessed via right-tailed Wilcoxon signed-rank tests with Holm-Bonferroni correction, and a new AUC metric integrates average rank gains across all adoption rates to rank methods when the competitive landscape is unknown.

## Results

On GPT-4o-mini, the best C-SEO methods (LLM Guidance and Content Improvement) achieve only marginal rank improvements: LLM Guidance yields +0.36±1.47 on Retail and +0.24±1.05 on Video Games, while Content Improvement yields +0.18±1.09 on Retail. In stark contrast, traditional SEO (placing the target document first in the LLM context) produces gains of +2.77±2.31 on Retail, +1.89±2.32 on Games, and +1.54±2.07 on Debate — an order of magnitude larger. Only 3 out of 54 method-domain combinations yield statistically significant C-SEO gains, while many methods (e.g., Statistics) significantly *decrease* rankings in 19 of 24 evaluated settings. The AUC analysis further shows that as adoption rate increases from 10% to 100%, per-adopter gains converge toward zero, confirming a congested zero-sum dynamic.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper benchmarks against the eight white-hat C-SEO methods from Aggarwal et al. (GEO, KDD 2024): Authoritative, Statistics, Citations, Fluency, Unique Words, Technical Terms, Simple Language, and Quotes. None of these eight methods achieves a statistically significant positive rank improvement in any domain on GPT-4o-mini, and several (notably Statistics) show significant *negative* effects in the majority of settings (19/24). The paper's two proposed methods — LLM Guidance and Content Improvement — are the only ones with any significant gains, but these are limited to specific domains: Content Improvement is significant only on Retail (+0.18), while LLM Guidance is significant on Retail (+0.36) and Video Games (+0.24). Critically, both new methods are dramatically outperformed by simply placing the target document in the first position of the LLM context (Best SEO), which yields gains 5–15× larger across all six domains. The authors argue this invalidates the core assumption of Aggarwal et al. that C-SEO will replace traditional SEO, concluding instead that C-SEO is at best a minor complement to retrieval-ranking optimization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the C-SEO task formalized by Aggarwal et al. (GEO, KDD 2024), which introduced eight white-hat content-transformation methods and evaluated them on a single task (web question answering) with a single actor. It also situates itself relative to black-hat C-SEO work (Nestaas et al. 2025, Kumar & Lakkaraju 2024, Pfrommer et al. 2024, Tang et al. 2025), which focused on adversarial robustness rather than benign content improvement. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of a multi-actor, multi-domain, multi-task evaluation protocol with varying adoption rates, framing C-SEO as a non-cooperative game rather than a single-player optimization problem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper's central finding—that most C-SEO methods are not only ineffective but frequently harmful to citation ranking, while traditional SEO (retrieval position) dominates by an order of magnitude—challenges the optimistic narrative in prior C-SEO literature and redirects practical investment toward retrieval optimization. The zero-sum, congested-game characterization implies that even the few effective methods (LLM Guidance, Content Improvement) lose value as adoption spreads, creating an arms-race dynamic. Open questions include the interplay between SEO and C-SEO, whether black-hat methods behave differently under multi-actor settings, and whether findings generalize beyond English-language content and commercial proprietary models.
