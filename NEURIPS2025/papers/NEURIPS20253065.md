## Problem

Minimizing Conditional Value-at-Risk (CVaR) for risk-averse model training becomes intractable at high quantile levels (β→1) because the objective is dominated by rare, high-loss events that standard sampling from the data distribution rarely captures, yielding high-variance Monte Carlo estimates and poor gradient signals. While importance sampling can reduce this variance, existing approaches reweight a fixed dataset iteratively, and no prior work has used generative models to actively synthesize the rare, high-loss-inducing inputs that CVaR optimization most needs.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pretrained reference model θ₀ (providing a loss landscape), a score-based generative model trained on the base distribution p(x), a target quantile β, and a non-decreasing weighting function φ (e.g., square-root or identity). It produces a fixed set of importance-weighted samples drawn from q(x) ∝ φ(ℓ(θ₀;x))p(x) via training-free guided diffusion, which are then consumed with a scalar likelihood-ratio reweight Z/φ(ℓ(θ₀;x)) during CVaR optimization. The final output is the risk-averse model θ* minimizing CVaR_β, with the key constraint that the importance distribution is constructed once before training and never updated per iteration.

## Method

RAMIS (Risk-Averse Model training via loss-guided Importance Samples) operates in two stages. First, the pretrained model θ₀'s loss values serve as a guidance signal for a score-based generative model: the reverse-time SDE score is augmented with a guidance term g(x,t) = ∇log E[w(X₀)|X_t=x], where w is derived from φ(ℓ(θ₀;x)), steering the diffusion process toward high-loss regions without any retraining of the generative model. Second, the synthesized samples are used to minimize the Rockafellar–Uryasev CVaR reformulation F_β(θ,α) = α + (1/(1−β))E[(ℓ(θ;x)−α)⁺] via importance-weighted Monte Carlo estimation, where the likelihood ratio p(x)/q(x) simplifies to the scalar Z/φ(ℓ(θ₀;x)). The theoretical analysis establishes an O(1/√K) convergence rate whose stochastic noise term v̂(q) is provably reduced relative to naive sampling from p(x) when φ is a non-decreasing surrogate for the optimal importance weight, and the fixed-importance-distribution design eliminates per-iteration reweighting overhead.

## Results

On the synthetic Gaussian-mixture regression task (100 trials), RAMIS achieves CVaR of 0.0774±0.0602 at β=0.99, compared to 0.5131±0.6211 for SSGM, 0.4940±0.4168 for DORO, and 0.6124±0.5954 for χ²-DRO — a roughly 6–8× reduction in tail risk. On the real-world CSI compression task (10 trials, units of 10⁻³), RAMIS achieves 2.2604±0.0419 at β=0.99 versus 2.3413 for SSGM, 2.4987 for DORO, and 2.5594 for χ²-DRO. At β=0.50 (approaching expected loss), all methods converge, confirming RAMIS does not degrade average-case performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RAMIS is compared against three risk-sensitive baselines — SSGM (Meng & Gower, ICML 2023), DORO (Zhai et al., ICML 2021), and χ²-DRO (Namkoong & Duchi, NeurIPS 2016) — plus standard ERM, all starting from the same pretrained checkpoint and using the same sample budget. On the synthetic regression task, RAMIS dominates all baselines at every quantile level, with the largest gap at β=0.99 where it reduces CVaR by approximately 6.6× over SSGM and 7.9× over χ²-DRO. On the real-world CSI compression task, RAMIS consistently achieves the lowest CVaR in the high-risk regime (β ∈ {0.90, 0.95, 0.99}), outperforming SSGM by ~3.5% at β=0.99 and DORO by ~10% at the same level. As β decreases toward 0, the performance gap narrows and RAMIS converges with SSGM and ERM, confirming that the importance-sampling mechanism specifically targets tail risk without sacrificing average-case performance. The key distinction is that SSGM, DORO, and χ²-DRO all operate on uniformly sampled data, whereas RAMIS replaces the sampling distribution with loss-guided importance samples, which the paper argues is the source of the improvement rather than the optimization objective alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) score-based generative models with training-free guidance (Song et al., 2021; Chung et al., 2023; Kim et al., 2025c), which enable sampling from reweighted distributions without retraining; (2) CVaR minimization via stochastic subgradient methods (Rockafellar and Uryasev, 2000; Meng and Gower, 2023), which provides the optimization objective; and (3) importance sampling for rare-event risk estimation (Bardou et al., 2009; Deo and Murthy, 2021), which motivates the reweighting strategy. The key departure from prior importance-sampling-for-CVaR work is that instead of reweighting a fixed dataset, the paper uses a pretrained model's loss landscape to actively *synthesize* samples from the importance distribution via guided diffusion, eliminating per-iteration reweighting overhead. It also differs from prior generative data augmentation (Zheng et al., 2023; Trabucco et al., 2023), which targets general diversity, by concentrating generation specifically on high-loss tail regions relevant to CVaR.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first principled framework for using generative models to reduce noise in risk-averse optimization, with a convergence theorem showing that loss-guided sampling provably reduces the stochastic noise term relative to naive sampling. This opens a path for risk-sensitive fine-tuning in domains where a pretrained reference model and a data generative model are both available (e.g., wireless communications, medical imaging, LLM safety). However, the approach requires both a pretrained task model and a well-trained generative model, and the empirical validation is limited to a synthetic regression task and a single real-world application (CSI compression). Open questions include how the method scales to high-dimensional or multimodal inputs, how sensitive performance is to the choice of the weighting function φ, and whether the fixed-importance-distribution assumption (built from θ₀) degrades as the model updates substantially during training.
