## Problem

Existing online conformal prediction methods for time series (e.g., ACI, SPCI, HopCPT) react to distribution shifts only after they occur, producing periods of under- or over-coverage that are risky in practice. In many real-world settings, however, the underlying dynamics shift in predictable, discrete regimes (day/night, weekday/weekend, surge/normal), and no prior conformal prediction algorithm exploits this predictability to anticipate and adapt to change points.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sequence of observation vectors o_t ∈ R^d with a look-back window of k steps (so input features x_t ∈ R^{k·d} may include environmental covariates), a pre-trained probabilistic state predictor p̂(z_t|x_{0:t}) over K discrete switching states, and a state-conditioned forecaster f̂: X×Z→Y. At each timestep it outputs a single adaptive prediction interval Γ_t(x_t) ⊆ Y, constructed by first computing state-specific conformal intervals calibrated on per-state nonconformity score sets S_z, then aggregating them via a weighted level-set or a union over the most probable states. The algorithm also maintains and updates per-state adaptive confidence levels α_{z,t} online.

## Method

CPTC runs K parallel instances of online conformal inference—one per switching state—maintaining separate nonconformity score sets S_z and adaptive miscoverage targets α_{z,t} that are updated via an ACI-style rule (α_{ẑ_t,t+1} ← α_{ẑ_t,t} + γ·(α − err_t)) only for the state sampled from the SDS state predictor at each step. At inference, it generates a state-specific prediction interval for every state with nonzero posterior probability and aggregates them into a single interval using either a weighted level-set (discretizing Y and selecting the minimal set achieving marginal coverage) or, for efficiency, the union of intervals from the smallest subset of states whose posterior mass reaches 1−α. The key architectural insight is that the SDS state predictor allows CPTC to *anticipate* a regime change: because each state carries its own calibrated score set and confidence level, the interval for the incoming state is already tuned to that regime's residual distribution, eliminating the reactive 'catch-up' period that purely online methods require. Theoretical analysis proves asymptotic validity without stationarity or state-prediction-accuracy assumptions, robustness to imperfect state classification (miscoverage bounded by ε·max_z δ_{z,T}), and a provably faster convergence ratio under state-coincident distribution shifts compared to ACI.

## Results

On all six datasets (target 90% coverage), CPTC achieves valid coverage ranging from 90.15% to 94.96%, while most baselines under-cover significantly (e.g., RED-SDS at 13–93%, CP at 64–88%, SPCI at 79–89%, HopCPT at 72–91%). CPTC's prediction interval width is competitive: it is the narrowest on Bouncing Ball dyn (1.76), 3-Mode System (2.45), and Dancing Bees (0.79), and within ~15% of the best valid baseline on Electricity (139.75 vs. HopCPT's 155.71). The key advantage is consistent validity: CPTC is the only method that stays above 90% coverage on every dataset without requiring long-horizon data (T=200–300).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CPTC decisively outperforms RED-SDS on coverage across all datasets (e.g., 90.15% vs. 16.90% on Bouncing Ball obs.), as RED-SDS's Bayesian intervals lack calibration. Against ACI (the strongest reactive online CP baseline), CPTC wins on coverage for 5 of 6 datasets (e.g., 92.64% vs. 86.25% on Bees, 91.22% vs. 89.79% on Electricity) and achieves substantially narrower intervals on 3-Mode System (2.45 vs. 6.40) and Bouncing Ball dyn. (1.76 vs. 2.27). SPCI and HopCPT, which learn residual patterns online, under-cover on most datasets (79–89%) due to insufficient data at the short horizons tested (T=200–300); CPTC maintains valid coverage while matching or beating their width on Bees (0.79 vs. 1.06/1.77) and 3-Mode (2.45 vs. 3.70/9.07). The one area where CPTC trades off is width on Bouncing Ball obs. (3.71 vs. SPCI's 2.82) and Traffic (7.92 vs. SPCI's 5.32), but those baselines are invalid (87.13% and 84.38% coverage respectively). On longer horizons (T=10,000), SPCI and HopCPT close the gap and achieve near-perfect 90.0% coverage with slightly narrower widths, confirming CPTC's primary advantage is in data-limited, short-horizon settings where reactive methods cannot yet adapt.

## Relation to Prior Work

CPTC sits at the intersection of two well-developed lines of work: online conformal prediction (ACI, SPCI, HopCPT) and switching dynamical systems (RED-SDS). The key conceptual departure is from purely reactive online CP methods, which adapt to distribution shifts only after observing miscoverage, to an anticipatory framework that uses pre-trained state predictions from an SDS to pre-adjust state-specific calibration targets before a shift occurs. The algorithm modularly combines ACI's online update rule (Eqn 9) per state with SDS state forecasting, avoiding the long cold-start windows required by SPCI and HopCPT, which retrain regression models during inference.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap in time-series UQ where predictable regime changes (day/night, weekday/weekend) cause reactive CP methods to exhibit dangerous under-coverage windows. The theoretical contribution—proving asymptotic validity without stationarity assumptions and bounding the miscoverage ratio under state-coincident shifts (Theorem 4.4)—gives practitioners a principled tool for safety-critical forecasting. Key limitations include the requirement of a discrete state prediction model (primarily SDS), wider intervals on very long horizons (T≥10,000) compared to SPCI/HopCPT, and slower convergence under frequent or unpredictable transitions. Open questions include extending to continuous latent states and studying decision-theoretic properties for real-time safety applications.
