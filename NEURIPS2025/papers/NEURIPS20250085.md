## Problem

Applying expressive generative models (diffusion, flow matching) to offline RL faces a fundamental tension: their iterative noise sampling makes policy optimization expensive (requiring backpropagation through many denoising steps) while also making inference slow. Existing approaches either distill into one-step models (losing expressivity and inference-time scaling) or backpropagate through the full sampling chain (prohibitively expensive training). The paper asks how to achieve efficient one-stage training of expressive policies while retaining the ability to scale inference-time compute.

## Input / Output

The policy takes a continuous state vector x and a user-specified number of inference steps m as input, and outputs a continuous action vector a. Actions are generated by iteratively applying the shortcut model via the forward Euler method, starting from Gaussian noise and integrating over m steps of size 1/m. At test time, m can be varied freely (including m=1 for single-step inference), and the learned Q-function can serve as a verifier to select the best action from N independently sampled candidates, enabling parallel scaling independent of the step count.

## Method

SORL parameterizes the policy as a shortcut model s_θ(z, t, h | x) that predicts normalized direction vectors for variable step sizes h, trained in a single stage with three loss components: a Q-loss for policy optimization (backpropagating through at most M^BTT Euler steps), a flow-matching loss for regularization to offline data, and a self-consistency loss ensuring that one large jump of size 2h equals two combined jumps of size h. The self-consistency mechanism is the key insight: it allows the same network to produce high-quality samples at any inference budget from 1 to M^disc steps without a separate distillation stage. At inference, sequential scaling is achieved by increasing the number of Euler steps, and parallel scaling by best-of-N sampling with the learned Q-function as verifier. A theoretical analysis proves that minimizing the flow-matching and self-consistency losses regularizes the generated action distribution to the behavior policy in 2-Wasserstein distance, uniformly across all step sizes.

## Results

SORL achieves the best average return on 5 out of 8 OGBench environments (40 tasks total), with the largest margins on antmaze-large (89±2 vs. ReBRAC's 81±5) and antsoccer-arena (69±2 vs. FQL's 60±2). On cube-single, SORL reaches 97±1, narrowly edging out FQL (96±1). SORL underperforms on three harder environments (antmaze-giant, humanoidmaze-large, cube-double), where all methods struggle with low absolute returns. All results are averaged over 8 seeds with standard deviations reported.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SORL outperforms all 9 baselines on 5 of 8 environments. Against the strongest flow-based baselines (FQL, IFQL, FBRAC), SORL wins by 8–9 points on antmaze-large (89 vs. 79) and antsoccer-arena (69 vs. 60), and by 1 point on cube-single (97 vs. 96) and scene (57 vs. 56). Against diffusion-based methods (IDQL, SRPO, CAC), SORL's advantage is most pronounced on antmaze-large (89 vs. 33 for CAC) and antsoccer-arena (69 vs. 12 for IDQL). SORL trails FQL on cube-double (25 vs. 29) and IFQL on humanoidmaze-large (5 vs. 11), and is roughly tied with ReBRAC on antmaze-giant (9 vs. 26, though both are very low). Gaussian baselines (BC, IQL, ReBRAC) are consistently outperformed except on antmaze-giant where ReBRAC leads.

## Relation to Prior Work

SORL sits at the intersection of three research threads: shortcut models (Frans et al., 2024), which learn to take variable-size steps along flow trajectories via self-consistency; flow matching (Lipman et al., 2022), used here as the behavioral regularization mechanism; and behavior-regularized actor-critic offline RL (Wu et al., 2019). The key departure from prior generative-policy offline RL methods is that SORL avoids both the two-stage distillation pipeline (train iterative model, then compress to one-step) and the expensive backpropagation through many denoising steps, by training a single unified shortcut model in one stage. Additionally, the paper transplants the test-time scaling paradigm from LLMs (sequential scaling via more reasoning steps, parallel scaling via best-of-N) into the offline RL setting, where prior generative-policy methods fixed the inference budget at training time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SORL resolves a fundamental tension in generative-policy offline RL: the need for many discretization steps to model multimodal data distributions versus the computational cost of backpropagating through those steps during policy optimization. The self-consistency property elegantly decouples these, enabling one-step inference for fast deployment while retaining the option to spend more compute at test time. The theoretical guarantee (Wasserstein-distance regularization to the behavior policy for all step sizes) is a novel analysis of shortcut models that strengthens the offline safety argument. Open questions include whether the approach scales to higher-dimensional action spaces (e.g., dexterous manipulation), whether adaptive step selection based on Q-function gradients can further improve efficiency, and whether the best-of-N parallel scaling gain is robust when the learned Q-function is inaccurate.
