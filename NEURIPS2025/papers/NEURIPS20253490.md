## Problem

Classical optimization theory prescribes small stepsizes for gradient descent on strongly convex problems, yielding O(κ ln(1/ε)) steps where κ is the condition number. While prior work showed that large stepsizes (edge-of-stability regime) accelerate unregularized logistic regression with separable data, it remained unclear whether this acceleration extends to ℓ₂-regularized logistic regression, where the minimizer is finite and GD becomes unstable beyond a threshold stepsize. The paper also asks whether large stepsizes reduce the number of steps needed to reach the statistical bottleneck in population risk.

## Input / Output

The method takes bounded, linearly separable training data (x_i, y_i) with margin γ in a Hilbert space, a regularization parameter λ > 0, and a constant stepsize η. It produces the iterate w_t at each step along with theoretical guarantees: a step complexity bound to reach ε suboptimality, and a critical stepsize threshold Θ(1/(λ ln(1/λ))) that separates local convergence from divergence. The analysis requires the data to satisfy a margin condition (y_i x_i^T w* ≥ γ) and bounded feature norms (‖x_i‖ ≤ 1), and the stepsize must satisfy η ≤ O(1/√λ) or η ≤ O(1/λ^{1/3}) depending on the regularization regime.

## Method

The core result shows that GD with a large constant stepsize (η = O(1/√λ) for small λ, η = O(1/λ^{1/3}) for general λ) initially operates in the EoS regime with non-monotonic objective, but undergoes a guaranteed phase transition to the stable phase in O(η) or O(η²) steps, after which it achieves exponential convergence at rate exp(−λη(t−τ)). The key technical challenge is that, unlike the unregularized case where GD moves toward infinity, the ℓ₂-regularized minimizer is finite with norm O(ln(1/λ)), so GD in the EoS phase can overshoot to norm Θ(η) and must move backward. The authors handle this via split optimization lemmas (extending Wu et al. 2024) to bound parameter norm and logistic risk in the EoS phase, then prove GD remains trapped in a flat region (Hessian norm ≲ 1/η) by comparing the logistic gradient magnitude against the regularization gradient. For small λ, this yields O(ln(1/ε)/√λ) steps matching Nesterov's momentum; for general λ, O(ln(1/ε)/λ^{2/3}) steps. Additionally, under a support-vector rank condition, they derive a sharp critical stepsize Θ(1/(λ ln(1/λ))) below which GD converges locally and above which it diverges for almost every initialization.

## Results

For small regularization (λ ≲ 1/n²), GD with a large stepsize η ≈ 1/√λ achieves a step complexity of O(ln(1/ε)/√λ), matching Nesterov's momentum and improving the classical O(ln(1/ε)/λ) by a factor of √λ. For general regularization, GD with η ≈ λ^(-1/3) achieves O(ln(1/ε)/λ^(2/3)) steps, still improving over the classical O(ln(1/ε)/λ). In the statistical learning setting with λ = 1/n, large-stepsize GD reaches the statistical bottleneck population risk O~(1/(γ²n)) in O((n/γ)^(2/3) ln n) steps, compared to O(n ln n) for small-stepsize GD and O(n^(1/2) ln n) for Nesterov's momentum. The critical stepsize threshold for local convergence is characterized as Θ(1/(λ ln(1/λ))).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares large-stepsize GD against three baselines: (1) classical small-stepsize GD in the stable regime, (2) Nesterov's momentum, and (3) unregularized large-stepsize GD from Wu et al. (2024). Against classical GD, large-stepsize GD wins decisively: for small λ it matches Nesterov's O(√κ) scaling (improving over O(κ) by a factor of √λ), and for general λ it achieves O(λ^(-2/3)) vs. O(λ^(-1)). Against Nesterov's momentum, large-stepsize GD matches it exactly in the small-regularization regime (both achieve O(ln(1/ε)/√λ)) but falls short in the general-regularization regime (O(λ^(-2/3)) vs. O(λ^(-1/2))). Against Wu et al. (2024), this paper extends the EoS acceleration from unregularized (minimizer at infinity) to regularized logistic regression (finite minimizer), and shows that large stepsizes still accelerate under statistical uncertainty when combined with regularization—something Wu et al.'s unregularized analysis cannot capture. A lower bound (Theorem 3) confirms that the acceleration is specific to the EoS regime: GD in the stable regime suffers O~(1/λ) step complexity in the worst case.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the edge-of-stability (EoS) analysis of Wu et al. (2024) from unregularized logistic regression (convex, minimizer at infinity) to ℓ₂-regularized logistic regression (strongly convex, finite minimizer). The key technical departure is that regularization introduces a contraction force toward zero, so GD must 'move backwards' toward the minimizer rather than diverging to infinity, fundamentally changing the phase-transition analysis. The paper also positions itself against aggressive stepsize schedulers (Altschuler and Parrilo, 2025; Grimmer, 2024) by achieving √κ acceleration with a simpler constant stepsize, and against classical Nesterov momentum as the benchmark for strongly convex optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that a plain constant-stepsize GD can match Nesterov's momentum acceleration (√κ steps) for small regularization is a clean theoretical insight with potential to simplify optimization practice. The acceleration also persists under statistical uncertainty, showing large stepsizes reduce the number of steps to reach the population-risk bottleneck from Õ(n) to Õ(n^{2/3}). Key open questions include whether GD converges globally for stepsizes between O(1/√λ) and the critical threshold O(1/(λ ln(1/λ))), whether the results extend to nonseparable data, and whether a general framework exists for analyzing GD with large stepsizes beyond the descent lemma.
