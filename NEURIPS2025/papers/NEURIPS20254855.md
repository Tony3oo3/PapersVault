## Problem

Existing retain-free machine unlearning methods struggle with instance-wise unlearning, where forget and retain data distributions overlap heavily, leading to over-unlearning and severe performance degradation. While retain-based methods achieve strong results, they require access to the retain set, which is often unavailable due to storage limitations, expired permissions, or privacy constraints. This paper addresses the gap of performing effective instance-wise unlearning without any retain data while preventing the over-forgetting that plagues prior retain-free approaches.

## Input / Output

The method takes a trained classifier F_θ, a forget set D_f (10% of training images with their original labels), and an unconditioned generator G_ψ pretrained on an OOD dataset (COCO). Because the generator is unconditioned and cannot assign labels to synthetic images, knowledge transfer relies on KL divergence between teacher and student logits rather than label-based supervision. The output is an unlearned model F_θ^U that approximates a model retrained from scratch on the retain set, evaluated on retain accuracy, forget accuracy, test accuracy, and membership inference attack score.

## Method

RUAGO combines four key components: (1) an Adversarial Probability Module that uses PGD-generated adversarial probability vectors as soft targets for forget data, avoiding the drastic parameter shifts caused by hard one-hot labels that trigger over-unlearning in instance-wise scenarios; (2) an OOD-pretrained unconditioned generator that produces synthetic images for knowledge distillation, replacing the unavailable retain set while eliminating privacy risks; (3) a VC-theory-grounded sample difficulty scheduler that weights distillation samples by their loss in an easy-to-hard curriculum, with a formal generalization bound (Theorem 1) guaranteeing controlled error growth across nested hypothesis spaces; and (4) a Deep Inversion-inspired alignment step that refines the generator via cross-entropy, negative KL divergence, and BatchNorm statistics matching to align synthetic outputs with the original data distribution. The final unlearning loss combines the adversarial forget loss and the difficulty-weighted distillation loss, updated iteratively over multiple epochs with periodic generator refinement.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with VGG16, RUAGO (retain-free) achieves an AVG gap of 0.28 to the Retrain model (RA 99.94%, UA 93.38%, TA 92.32%), compared to 5.56 for the best retain-free baseline SCAR and 1.30 for the best retain-based baseline SCRUB. On ViT, RUAGO reaches an AVG of just 0.05, nearly matching the Retrain ground truth. On VGGFace2, RUAGO outperforms all baselines including retain-based ones, achieving AVG values of 0.57 (VGG16), 0.45 (ResNet18), and 0.42 (ViT), while the best retain-free method SCAR scores 2.32, 2.57, and 1.97 respectively. Running time is 433 s (VGG16/CIFAR-10), roughly 4× faster than full retraining (1,758 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the three retain-free baselines, RUAGO dominates: on CIFAR-10/VGG16, it reduces the AVG gap from 19.07 (BS), 23.55 (SSD), and 5.56 (SCAR) down to 0.28, and on ViT from 42.62/15.53/0.66 to 0.05. Against retain-based methods, RUAGO is competitive or superior: it beats Bad-T (AVG 0.28 vs 0.61) and SalUn (0.28 vs 0.57) on VGG16, and SCRUB (0.28 vs 1.30) on VGG16, though SCRUB edges out RUAGO on ResNet18 (AVG 0.10 vs 1.65). On VGGFace2, RUAGO outperforms all six baselines across every architecture, achieving AVG 0.57/0.45/0.42 versus the best retain-based Bad-T at 0.81/0.76/0.82 and the best retain-free SCAR at 2.32/2.57/1.97. MIA scores confirm that RUAGO matches the Retrain model's privacy profile (≈0.85), whereas Bad-T shows the worst MIA divergence despite strong accuracy, indicating it does not guarantee privacy protection.

## Relation to Prior Work

RUAGO sits at the intersection of three research threads: adversarial-based unlearning (extending Boundary Shrink's hard-label approach to soft adversarial probability vectors to prevent over-unlearning in instance-wise settings), OOD-based knowledge distillation (adopting Fang et al.'s insight that a generator trained on out-of-domain data can substitute for unavailable training data), and data-free knowledge distillation via model inversion (borrowing Deep Inversion's formulation for generator alignment). The key conceptual departure is from prior retain-free unlearning methods, which the authors argue work for class-wise but fail for instance-wise unlearning due to distributional overlap between forget and retain sets. The VC-theoretic curriculum scheduling, adapted from DFKD literature, is novel in the unlearning context and provides a theoretical justification for the easy-to-hard synthetic sample ordering.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that retain-free unlearning can match or exceed retain-based methods across multiple datasets and architectures, challenging the prevailing assumption that access to retain data is essential for effective unlearning. This has direct practical implications for compliance scenarios (GDPR, CCPA) where retain data may be unavailable due to storage limits, expired permissions, or privacy constraints. Limitations include reliance on a pre-trained OOD generator of sufficient quality, applicability verified only for supervised image classification, and the VC-theoretic analysis being somewhat idealized (nested hypothesis spaces, i.i.d. samples). Open questions include whether the approach extends to generative models, multi-modal settings, or very large-scale unlearning requests where the generator alignment step becomes costly.
