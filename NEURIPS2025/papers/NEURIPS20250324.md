## Problem

Deriving the influence function (IF) of a statistical functional analytically is a major obstruction to adopting IF-based methods in semiparametric estimation, robustness analysis, and data attribution. Prior automated approaches based on the classical von Mises formula estimate the IF at individual points via perturbations toward point masses, which are numerically ill-conditioned, require separate computations per evaluation point, and lack convergence guarantees in function norms needed for downstream tasks like debiased machine learning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250324/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes an i.i.d. sample X_{1:n} from P, a statistical functional θ(P) accessible only through evaluation along parametric paths (no closed-form IF required), and a user-chosen Mercer kernel K (e.g., Gaussian or Laplacian). It outputs the entire influence function as a low-rank function ψ̂_λ^r(x) = Σ_{j=1}^r [1/(1+2λ/σ̂_j)] · (d/dt θ(f̂_t^j))|_{t=0} · ê_j(x), where r controls the number of kernel PCA components and λ controls the bias-variance trade-off. Unlike pointwise von Mises estimators, a single set of r pathwise derivatives yields the full function, and the output lives in the rkHs with convergence guaranteed in both L²(P) and H norms.

## Method

The core contribution is a spectral von Mises representation (Theorem 3.3) that expresses the regularized IF as an infinite Fourier series in the Mercer eigenbasis of the kernel integral operator S_K, with coefficients given by pathwise derivatives of θ along fixed orthonormal perturbation directions e_j. The key geometric insight is that the IF is the gradient of θ under the Fisher-Rao metric, and regularizing in a smoothness space H (the rkHs) turns the problem into a penalized optimization whose solution has a closed-form diagonal structure: each Fourier coefficient is the pathwise derivative Dθ_P[e_j] shrunk by the factor σ_j/(1+2λ/σ_j). In practice, the Nyström method estimates the leading eigenvalues and eigenfunctions from the empirical Gram matrix, and pathwise derivatives are computed via finite differences along linear density perturbations f̂_t^j = [1+t ê_j] f̂. This yields a consistent plug-in estimator (Theorem 4.7) that is, to the author's knowledge, the first data-driven functional estimator of the IF with convergence guarantees in function norms.

## Results

The paper's main contributions are theoretical rather than empirical. Theorem 3.3 establishes a spectral von Mises formula expressing the regularized influence function as an infinite Fourier series over Mercer eigenfunctions, with coefficients given by pathwise derivatives of the functional θ along fixed perturbation directions. Theorem 4.7 proves that the plug-in kernel estimator ψ̂_λ^r is consistent in the rkHs norm (‖ψ̂_λ^r − ψ_{P,λ}^r‖_H → 0 in probability) and that there exist sequences r(n)→∞ and λ(n)→0 such that ‖ψ̂_λ^r − ψ_P‖_{L²(P)} → 0. Figure 1 provides a qualitative illustration for the quantile functional θ_q, showing that the rank-r kernel estimate (solid) closely tracks the regularized oracle surrogate (dashed) and the true IF ψ_q.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not include a quantitative comparison table against competing estimators. Conceptually, the proposed kernel von Mises estimator is positioned against two prior approaches: (1) the classical von Mises formula (Theorem 2.1), which evaluates the IF pointwise via perturbations toward a point mass at each z ∈ X, requiring a separate computation per evaluation point and suffering from ill-conditioned finite-difference derivatives [CLV19; JWZ22]; and (2) prior automated estimation methods [Fra+15; CLV19; JWZ22] that also rely on the von Mises representation but compute the IF at individual points. The proposed method differs by computing a fixed set of r pathwise derivatives along Mercer eigenfunction directions (e.g., r=16), yielding the entire IF function simultaneously, adapting to the smoothness of ψ via the kernel choice, and providing L² and rkHs consistency guarantees that the pointwise von Mises approach does not offer. The paper explicitly notes that convergence rates and head-to-head empirical comparisons are deferred to follow-up work.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the classical von Mises formula (Theorem 2.1), which evaluates the influence function at a single point z via pathwise derivatives along perturbations toward a point mass at z. Prior automated estimation works (Frangakis et al. 2015, Carone et al. 2019, Jordan et al. 2022) operationalized this pointwise formula using finite differences, but required a separate, often ill-conditioned computation for each evaluation point. The key departure here is replacing point-mass perturbations with perturbations along Mercer eigenfunctions of a kernel, yielding a global spectral (Fourier-series) representation of the entire IF that connects IF estimation to kernel PCA and the Nyström method, with consistency results inherited from the integral-operator literature (Rosasco et al. 2010, Zwald & Blanchard 2005).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Analytic derivation of influence functions is widely documented as a major bottleneck for adopting semiparametric efficiency methods (targeted maximum likelihood, double/debiased ML, causal inference), and this paper provides the first data-driven functional estimator with convergence guarantees in both L² and RKHS norms. However, the current results are limited to consistency; convergence rates, which are essential for downstream applications like debiased ML requiring o(n^{-1/4}) rates, remain open. Further work on efficient automatic differentiation of pathwise derivatives and on the statistical-computational trade-offs of the rank r and regularization λ will be needed before the method is practically competitive with analytic derivations.
