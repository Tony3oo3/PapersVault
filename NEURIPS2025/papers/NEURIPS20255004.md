## Problem

Existing NeRF-Language models like LLaNA compress an entire NeRF into a single global embedding, which fundamentally limits their ability to perform fine-grained spatial reasoning about object parts and their relative positions. Since NeRF MLP weights distribute spatial information across all parameters without any weight being tied to a specific 3D location, standard spatial tokenization strategies (patches, point sets) cannot be directly applied. The paper asks whether spatially-aware token representations can be elicited from the inherently unstructured weight space of a NeRF.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255004/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method ingests the raw weight matrices and bias vectors of a NeRF's MLP (packed into a 336×64 matrix following the nf2vec convention) along with a natural-language prompt. The meta-encoder produces 3072 spatially-aware tokens of dimension 516, which are organized into a 32×32×3 tri-plane structure; these tokens are then projected into LLaMA-2's embedding space (4096-dim for 7B, 5192-dim for 13B) and prepended to the text prompt. The model autoregressively generates textual captions or Q&A responses describing the 3D object.

## Method

The core contribution is weights2space, a self-supervised meta-encoder with two stages: (1) a weights2seq module of 4 linear layers processes each row of the packed weight matrix independently (retaining per-row features without the max-pooling used by nf2vec/LLaNA), and (2) a 12-layer Transformer decoder (seq2space) with 3072 learnable queries cross-attends to these row features to produce a token sequence. The key spatial grounding mechanism is reshaping the 3072 tokens into three 32×32 orthogonal planes (a tri-plane), so each token is tied to a specific 2D location on one plane; a decoder MLP then renders RGB and density from bilinearly-interpolated tri-plane features, and the entire pipeline is trained end-to-end with a smooth-L1 volumetric rendering loss against images rendered from the input NeRF itself (fully self-supervised). Spatial LLaNA (S-LLaNA) then uses a 2-layer projector to map the 3072 tokens into LLaMA-2's embedding space, followed by a two-stage training protocol (projector-only, then full fine-tuning) on ~300K NeRF-text pairs from ShapeNeRF-Text and ObjaNeRF-Text.

## Results

S-LLaNA-13b achieves 78.98 S-BERT and 79.98 SimCSE on ShapeNeRF-Text brief captioning, outperforming LLaNA-13b (75.09/76.45) by ~4 points and the best 2D baseline LLaVA-13b (61.00/61.16) by ~18 points. On the novel Spatial ObjaNeRF benchmark, S-LLaNA-13b reaches 78.25 S-BERT and 80.58 SimCSE, surpassing LLaNA-13b (73.20/74.76) by over 5 points. The largest gains appear on detailed captioning, where S-LLaNA-13b scores 80.98 S-BERT vs. LLaNA-13b's 75.51, and BLEU-1 jumps from 19.87 to 44.78, indicating substantially richer and more structured descriptions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

S-LLaNA consistently outperforms all 8 baselines across every task and dataset. Against the direct NeRF-based competitor LLaNA (7B and 13B), S-LLaNA gains ~4 points on S-BERT/SimCSE for brief captioning, ~5.5 points for detailed captioning, and ~2.5 points for Q&A, with the largest absolute gap on BLEU-1 for detailed captioning (44.78 vs. 19.87). Against 2D MLLMs (LLaVA, BLIP-2), S-LLaNA leads by 15–20 points on S-BERT, confirming that operating directly on NeRF weights avoids the information loss inherent in rendering 2D views. Against 3D MLLMs (PointLLM, ShapeLLM, GPT4Point, 3D-LLM), the advantage is even larger (25–35 points on S-BERT), as these methods require extracting explicit point clouds or meshes from the NeRF. On the novel Spatial ObjaNeRF benchmark, S-LLaNA-13b (78.25 S-BERT) beats the best 2D baseline LLaVA-13b-MV (73.42) by ~5 points and the best 3D baseline PointLLM-13b (73.08) by ~5 points, with the gap over LLaNA-13b (73.20) reaching 5.05 points, highlighting the critical role of spatially-aware tokenization for fine-grained spatial reasoning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends LLaNA (Amaduzzi et al., NeurIPS 2024), the only prior MLLM for NeRFs, which represents an entire NeRF as a single global embedding via nf2vec's max-pooled encoder. The key departure is the weights2space meta-encoder, which skips the max-pooling step and instead uses a Transformer decoder with learnable queries to produce 3072 tokens, then reshapes them into a tri-plane structure (borrowed from Chan et al., CVPR 2022) to impose spatial locality on what are otherwise unstructured weight-derived features. The self-supervised training signal—rendering from the tri-plane and matching images rendered from the original NeRF—mirrors nf2vec's rendering loss but now supervises spatially organized tokens rather than a single vector. The downstream MLLM (S-LLaNA) follows the same projector + LLaMA-2/Vicuna pipeline as LLaNA and Scaling LLaNA, but ingests a sequence of spatial tokens instead of one global token.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first NeRF-Language model capable of fine-grained spatial reasoning (relative positions, part-whole hierarchies, size comparisons), a capability that was fundamentally absent from LLaNA's single-token design. The practical finding that S-LLaNA achieves near-identical performance without fine-tuning the 7B/13B LLM—while LLaNA degrades by over 10 points—makes the approach significantly cheaper to train and adapt. However, the Spatial ObjaNeRF benchmark is small (100 scenes), the evaluation is limited to single-object scenes rather than complex multi-object environments, and the authors themselves note that scaling to spatial Q&A, 3D grounding, and object detection is blocked by the scarcity of large-scale NeRF-text datasets. Whether the tri-plane spatial structure generalizes to open-world scenes or non-MLP NeRF variants remains open.
