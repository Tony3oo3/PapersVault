## Problem

Urban areas occupy only ~3% of Earth's land surface yet house over 50% of the global population, yet they are marginalized in ML-based climate models where they constitute just ~8% of grid cells. Existing ML approaches for urban climate learn opaque data mappings that ignore the physical evolution of surface-atmosphere interactions, resulting in poor generalization to future climate scenarios and physically inconsistent predictions. This paper addresses how to embed the physics of urban surface-atmosphere coupling directly into a neural architecture to achieve accurate, interpretable, and generalizable local urban climate emulation.

## Input / Output

The model consumes two independently embedded input sequences: atmospheric forcing (solar/longwave radiation, wind, temperature, humidity, pressure, precipitation, snow, and geographic coordinates) and urban surface parameters (morphological, radiative, and thermal properties of roof, wall, and road surfaces), both at 3-hour timesteps from CLMU simulations at 0.9°×1.25° resolution. Primary outputs are 2-m air temperature, specific humidity, and dew point temperature predicted jointly as a multi-task regression; during fine-tuning on real-world flux tower data, the outputs shift to sensible and latent heat fluxes constrained by the surface energy balance. The 3-hour resolution (8 steps/day) is a deliberate extension beyond the daily scale used in prior urban climate work, requiring the model to capture inter-timestep dependencies.

## Method

UCformer is a multi-task Transformer whose architecture encodes urban surface-atmosphere physics at two levels. The domain-specific encoder uses a cross-attention operator where atmospheric forcing tokens serve as queries and urban surface tokens serve as keys and values, modeling how heterogeneous surfaces modulate incoming radiation and fluxes; subsequent self-attention blocks simulate the iterative flux interactions within the urban canyon, producing a latent representation of surface energy fluxes. The physics-guided decoder then diagnoses each target variable not in isolation but by cross-attending over the latent representations of its physically coupled counterparts (e.g., dew point temperature is predicted by attending over the latent codes of temperature and specific humidity), thereby encoding the Magnus-Tetens and ideal-gas relationships as emergent properties of the architecture rather than as explicit loss penalties. This representation-level embedding of physical constraints, combined with multi-city training to learn shared surface-atmosphere interaction patterns, yields a 12.7% improvement in temperature prediction accuracy and a 13.4% gain in generalization to 2070–2074 climate dynamics over the best baseline.

## Results

On the in-distribution test set (2050–2054), UCformer achieves the best multi-task MESS_agg of 2.0468, with RMSEs of 0.3264 K (T), 0.1851 g/kg (q), and 0.1777 K (t), outperforming the next-best baseline (MLP_CSB, MESS_agg 1.9088) by ~7.2% in aggregate skill. On the out-of-sample generalization set (2070–2074), UCformer maintains the lowest RMSE across all three variables (0.3385 K, 0.2121 g/kg, 0.1960 K), yielding a 13.4% relative improvement in temperature RMSE over the vanilla Transformer. In the real-world fine-tuning experiment (UK-King's College), UCformer fine-tuned on only 20% of observations outperforms the physics-based CLMU model on both latent (Q_le) and sensible (Q_h) heat flux estimation in terms of MAE and correlation coefficient.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UCformer outperforms all five baselines on every metric (RMSE and MESS) for all three predictands (T, q, t) in both the in-distribution (2050–2054) and out-of-sample (2070–2074) evaluations. Against the closest competitor, LSTNet_modified (MESS_agg 1.9065), UCformer improves aggregate skill by ~7.3% and reduces temperature RMSE from 0.3528 K to 0.3264 K. The vanilla Transformer, despite being the architectural backbone, trails UCformer by 12.7% in temperature RMSE, confirming that the domain-specific encoder and physics-guided decoder contribute meaningfully beyond a generic attention mechanism. MLP_CSB (the ClimSim benchmark top architecture) and AutoML (FLAML-based tree ensembles) are competitive but consistently underperform UCformer, with gaps of 0.058 and 0.188 in MESS_agg respectively. In the real-world heat-flux experiment, UCformer fine-tuned on 20% of UK-King's College observations beats both the physics-based CLMU model and an observation-only baseline (UCformer_ob) on MAE and Pearson r for Q_le and Q_h, demonstrating that simulation-trained physics-guided knowledge transfers effectively to sparse observational settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of physics-guided ML (Read et al. 2019, Zanetta et al. 2023) and urban climate emulation (Zhao et al. 2021, Zheng et al. 2021, Meyer et al. 2022). Prior urban climate ML models learn direct data mappings from atmospheric forcing to climate variables without encoding the underlying surface-atmosphere interaction physics, while existing physics-guided approaches typically embed physical constraints in loss functions or equation layers rather than in the architecture itself. UCformer departs from both lines by encoding the urban canyon energy budget structure (radiation absorption, flux iteration, variable coupling via Magnus-Tetens) directly into cross-attention and self-attention operations within a Transformer, treating fluxes as latent representations rather than explicit predictions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical speedup from ~12 hours (CLMU) to ~17 seconds for 55-year city-scale simulations makes large-scale urban climate scenario analysis feasible, and the finding that a simulation-trained model can outperform the physics-based CLMU on real-world heat flux estimation with only 20% fine-tuning data suggests a viable path toward data-efficient urban climate deployment. Key limitations include evaluation on only six cities in single-point mode, no assessment across spatial scales, and reliance on a single SSP scenario (SSP3-7.0), leaving open whether the physics-guided architecture generalizes to more diverse urban morphologies and climate trajectories. The multi-city shared learning result (beneficial for 5 of 6 cities but detrimental for Singapore) raises an open question about when shared versus city-specific representations are optimal.
