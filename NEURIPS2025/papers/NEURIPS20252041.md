## Problem

Existing LLM-based approaches for combinatorial optimization rely solely on textual or visual prompting, neglecting the inherent topological structure of CO instances that human experts exploit when designing algorithms. This structural blindness leads to suboptimal solutions and excessive iterative overhead during evolutionary code search, as the LLM lacks inductive biases about problem sparsity, symmetry, and degeneracy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252041/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a CO problem instance encoded as a bipartite graph (constraint–variable nodes for MILP, literal–clause nodes for SAT) and a natural-language prompt specifying the solver and code-generation task. The GNN produces a fixed-dimensional structural embedding (64-d) that is zero-padded to the LLM's hidden dimension and prepended as a single [CLS]-like token to the tokenized prompt sequence. The output is an executable solver-specific code snippet (e.g., a branching heuristic for SCIP or a variable-selection function for EasySAT) whose quality is measured by solver performance metrics such as PD integral or PAR-2.

## Method

STRCMP is a three-stage pipeline: (1) a GNN (three GCN layers + mean pooling, trained via domain classification) extracts a structural embedding from the CO instance's bipartite graph; (2) a composite model—frozen GNN feeding a Qwen2.5-Coder-7B-Instructor LLM via LoRA (rank 16 on q_proj/v_proj)—generates solver code conditioned on both the structural embedding (prepended to the embedding sequence so self-attention jointly models graph and text) and the natural-language prompt, trained first by SFT on curated (prompt, best-code) pairs and then by DPO on preference pairs derived from solver-evaluated rankings; (3) the composite model is embedded in an evolutionary algorithm (selection, crossover, mutation) for iterative performance-driven refinement. The paper further provides an information-theoretic proof that adding a structural prior cannot decrease the generative model's performance upper bound, and that a performance-enhancing prior strictly increases it.

## Results

On the SAT domain, STRCMP (DPO Only) achieves the lowest Number of Timeout across all four benchmarks: 32 on CNP, 16 on CoinsGrid, 3 on PRP, and 5 on Zamkeller, compared to AutoSAT's 32/16/9/18. This represents a 66.7% reduction in timeouts on PRP (9→3) and 77.8% on Zamkeller (18→4) over the closest LLM-based baseline AutoSAT. On the MILP domain, STRCMP achieves performance parity with neural CO methods (L2B, HEM) and its direct LLM-based competitor LLM4Solver in terms of PD integral. In convergence, STRCMP reaches stable convergence significantly faster than both AutoSAT and LLM4Solver, with AutoSAT exhibiting persistent oscillations even after convergence.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against AutoSAT [29] (the strongest LLM-based SAT baseline), STRCMP wins decisively on timeout count (e.g., 3 vs. 9 on PRP; 4–5 vs. 18 on Zamkeller) and solving time (21146s vs. 22967s on PRP; 6929s vs. 20772s on Zamkeller), while also converging faster and more stably in the evolutionary loop. Against NeuroSAT [40] (neural CO for SAT), STRCMP achieves lower timeouts on all four SAT benchmarks (e.g., 5 vs. 29 on Zamkeller). Against L2B [14] and HEM [15,16] (neural CO for MILP), STRCMP achieves performance parity on PD integral, confirming that structure-aware LLM code generation can match dedicated neural branching/cut-selection policies. Against LLM4Solver [30] (LLM-based evolutionary code optimization for MILP), STRCMP matches solution quality but converges in fewer iterations, reducing computational overhead. The ablation STRCMP w/o GNN (removing structural priors) degrades notably on Zamkeller (14 vs. 4–5 timeouts), confirming the structural prior's contribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

STRCMP extends the LLM-driven algorithm discovery paradigm established by FunSearch, AutoSAT, and LLM4Solver, which use evolutionary search over LLM-generated solver code but rely solely on textual (or occasionally visual) prompts. The key departure is the explicit integration of GNN-extracted structural embeddings from bipartite constraint-variable graphs as inductive biases for the LLM's code generation, a modality previously exploited only in neural combinatorial optimization (L2B, HEM, NeuroSAT) but never in LLM-based algorithm discovery. The evolutionary refinement loop follows the FunSearch/EoH template, while the two-phase post-training (SFT + DPO) and LoRA-based adaptation of Qwen2.5-Coder-7B represent practical engineering contributions to make the composite GNN-LLM architecture trainable.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides the first principled (if modest) theoretical justification—via information-theoretic upper-bound arguments—for why structural priors should help LLM-based CO solving, and empirically validates the claim with large margins on timeout and PD-integral metrics across nine benchmarks. However, the finding that the full SFT+DPO model does not uniformly outperform its individual ablation variants reveals unresolved tensions in the post-training data distribution, and the complete failure of the Llama2 family underscores a heavy dependence on a sufficiently capable code-generating backbone. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond MILP and SAT to other CO classes (e.g., constraint programming, quadratic programming), how it scales to industrial-scale instances, and whether the structural prior can be made more fine-grained (e.g., per-node rather than per-instance embeddings).
