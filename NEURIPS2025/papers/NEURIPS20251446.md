## Problem

Existing VQ-VAE-style compression methods rely on a single globally shared codebook for all tokens, which forces the codebook to be large enough to accommodate diverse image features. When the codebook size is reduced to lower bpp, reconstruction quality and downstream recognition accuracy degrade severely. The paper identifies that facial images exhibit strong attribute-level correlations (gender, age, ethnicity) and token-level semantic heterogeneity that a monolithic codebook cannot exploit, motivating a reorganization of the codebook into smaller, specialized subproblems.

## Input / Output

The method takes 256×256 RGB face images and produces a sequence of discrete token indices whose total storage cost is T⌈log₂K⌉ + ⌈log₂M⌉ bits (where T is the token count, K the per-token sub-codebook size, and M the number of switchable codebook groups), compared to T⌈log₂N⌉ bits for a global codebook of size N. A decoder then reconstructs the face image from these indices. Evaluation is performed via face recognition accuracy (MeanAcc across five benchmarks) and identity similarity (IDS) at various bpp levels as low as 0.004 bpp.

## Method

The core contribution is a two-level codebook partitioning: (1) an image-level switchable codebook where a MoE-inspired differentiable router G_θ assigns each image to one of M smaller codebooks (each of size N/2^s), and (2) a token-level decomposition where each of the T tokens within the selected codebook is quantized against its own dedicated sub-codebook of size K. During training, the router is optimized with a composite loss combining quantization-error guidance, entropy regularization for balanced codebook utilization, and decision-confidence sharpening; at inference, a simple nearest-neighbor search over all codebooks guarantees minimal-error assignment. A three-stage progressive training protocol first optimizes the switchable token-shared codebooks, then refines them into token-specific sub-codebooks initialized from Stage 1, and finally fine-tunes the decoder with an ArcFace identity-preservation loss. The module is plug-and-play and has been validated on both 1D (TiTok) and 2D (MaskGit-VQGAN) latent-space backbones, achieving up to 8.06% absolute accuracy improvement at the same bpp or 32.9% bitrate reduction at matched accuracy.

## Results

On five face recognition benchmarks (LFW, CFP-FP, AgeDB, CPLFW, CALFW), the proposed STSCQ method achieves 93.51% MeanAcc and 0.6659 IDS at 0.0469 bpp (MaskGit-VQGAN backbone), 91.66% MeanAcc and 0.6120 IDS at 0.0234 bpp (TiTok-S backbone), and 73.13% MeanAcc and 0.2583 IDS at 0.0059 bpp (TiTok-L backbone). These represent absolute improvements of +2.81, +4.10, and +8.06 percentage points over the respective baselines at identical bpp. The method maintains ~70% recognition accuracy even at extremely low bitrates (bpp < 0.01), where traditional codecs like JPEG 2000 drop to 56.98%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MaskGit-VQGAN (2D, 256 tokens, 0.0469 bpp), STSCQ improves MeanAcc from 90.70% to 93.51% (+2.81 pp) and IDS from 0.6314 to 0.6659, demonstrating that token-specific codebooks enhance representational capacity without increasing bpp. Against TiTok-S (1D, 128 tokens, 0.0234 bpp), the improvement is larger: 87.56% → 91.66% MeanAcc (+4.10 pp) and 0.5764 → 0.6120 IDS. Against TiTok-L (1D, 32 tokens, 0.0059 bpp), the gain is most pronounced at 65.07% → 73.13% MeanAcc (+8.06 pp) and 0.1812 → 0.2583 IDS, showing the method is most beneficial at ultra-low bitrates where codebook diversity is most constrained. Compared to the traditional JPEG 2000 codec, STSCQ achieves comparable or higher recognition accuracy at roughly half the bitrate (e.g., 91.66% at 0.0234 bpp vs. 85.64% at 0.05 bpp). Against CodeFormer (2D, 256 tokens), STSCQ on the TiTok-S backbone surpasses its 89.99% MeanAcc at 0.0390 bpp while operating at a lower bpp of 0.0234, indicating superior rate-distortion efficiency.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the VQ-VAE latent-space compression framework (Van Den Oord et al., 2017) and its 1D token-sequence variant TiTok (Yu et al., 2024) by replacing the single global codebook with a two-level hierarchy: image-level switchable codebook groups (inspired by Mixture-of-Experts routing) and token-specific sub-codebooks. Unlike prior codebook-based methods (VQGAN, MaskGIT, TiTok) that reduce bpp by shrinking the codebook or token count—both of which cause severe quality degradation—this work decomposes the quantization problem into smaller subproblems, increasing total codebook capacity while reducing per-token bit-width. The design is explicitly plug-and-play, demonstrated as a drop-in module on both 2D (MaskGit-VQGAN) and 1D (TiTok) baselines without modifying the encoder or decoder architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in ultra-low-bpp face compression where existing methods lose recognition accuracy rapidly as codebook size decreases. The token-specific codebook insight—that different tokens represent different semantic regions and don't need to share a quantization space—offers a generalizable principle applicable to any codebook-based representation learning system. However, the method's performance is tightly coupled to the underlying autoencoder quality (acknowledged as a limitation), and generalization beyond face images to broader visual content remains untested. Open questions include whether encoder-decoder co-design with the switchable codebook structure could yield further gains, and whether the routing mechanism scales gracefully beyond ~16 codebook groups (performance degrades at 128).
