## Problem

Existing Scaling Laws for Sequential Recommendation remain qualitative because two fundamental challenges block quantitative prediction: (1) token counts fail to capture data quality in SR due to variable vocabulary sizes, redundant user sequences, and random item noise, and (2) training loss does not reliably track actual model performance because overtrained SR models exhibit overfitting. This paper introduces the Performance Law (P-Law), the first quantitative framework that predicts SR model performance (not just loss) across varying model and data configurations.

## Input / Output

The method consumes model architecture parameters (number of decoder layers H and embedding dimension d_emb) together with a data scale D computed as #Tokens × S′Real, where S′Real is the inverse of Real Entropy estimated via LZ77 compression of concatenated user interaction sequences. The minimum encoding length C_min is used to make D vocabulary-agnostic across datasets with different item pools. The output is a predicted performance surface P(H, d_emb, D) for HR and NDCG, which can be inverted to identify the globally or locally optimal (H, d_emb) pair under constraints such as fixed depth or fixed total parameter count H·d_emb.

## Method

P-Law builds on a decoder-only Transformer backbone (HSTU-style with SiLU activation and relative attention bias) and derives a closed-form performance fitting formula P(H, d_emb, D) ≈ w₁(log H^{w₃} + p₁/H^{w₃}) + w₂(log d_emb^{w₄} + p₁/d_emb^{w₄}) + log D + p₂/D^{w₅}. Two key modifications distinguish it from the Chinchilla Scaling Law: first, the data scale D is corrected by Real Entropy S′Real (estimated via LZ compression) to remove the influence of low-entropy redundant sequences, with a theoretical lower bound D ≥ #Tokens · S′Real proven under a first-order Markov chain assumption; second, a decay term of the form 1/· + log(·) is introduced for both H and d_emb, transforming the predicted trend from monotonically increasing to rise-then-fall, thereby quantitatively capturing overfitting. The existence of the fitting parameters is established via a Squeeze Theorem that bounds P between two functions of the form log(d_emb^{w} D^{w}) + 1/(d_emb^{w} D^{w}) + log H + 1/H, and all coefficients are recovered by least-squares regression on empirically collected performance matrices.

## Results

The Performance Law (PL) achieves R² correlation coefficients of 0.85–0.95 for HR@10 and 0.86–0.94 for NDCG@10 across all four main datasets, consistently outperforming both Scaling Law (SL) and Precision Scaling Law (PcSL). On smaller datasets where overfitting is most pronounced, the improvement is dramatic: on ML-1M with |S_u|=200, PL reaches R²=0.892 (HR@10) versus SL's 0.189, a 345% relative improvement. On larger datasets (Industrial, AMZ-Books), PL still maintains R² > 0.85 with at least a 2.8% absolute improvement over SL. MAE and RMSE analyses (Tables 7–8) confirm PL's superiority, e.g., on Industrial |S_u|=50, PL achieves MAE=0.972×10⁻³ vs. SL's 3.356×10⁻³ for HR@10.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares PL against two baselines: the standard Chinchilla-style Scaling Law (SL, Hoffmann et al. 2022) and the Precision Scaling Law (PcSL, Kumar et al. 2024). PL wins on every single dataset–metric–sequence-length combination in Table 1, with no losses or ties. The gap is largest on smaller datasets where overfitting dominates: on ML-1M (|S_u|=200), PL achieves R²=0.892 vs. SL's 0.189 and PcSL's 0.200 for HR@10, and on KR-Pure (|S_u|=100), PL reaches 0.649 vs. SL's 0.172. On larger datasets where overfitting is less severe, PL still leads by 5–15 percentage points in R² (e.g., Industrial |S_u|=50: PL 0.951 vs. SL 0.803). PcSL is consistently marginally better than SL (by ~0.5–1% R²) but remains substantially below PL, indicating that precision correction alone cannot resolve the loss-performance discrepancy that PL's decay term addresses.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Chinchilla scaling law framework (Hoffmann et al., 2022) from LLMs to sequential recommendation, departing from prior qualitative SR scaling analyses (HSTU, Wukong) by introducing two quantitative modifications: a Real Entropy-based data quality measure (repurposed from human mobility prediction, Song et al., 2010, and computed via LZ compression) to replace raw token counts, and a decay term to capture the loss-performance discrepancy caused by overfitting. The method builds on the decoder-only transformer architecture from HSTU while shifting the prediction target from training loss to actual recommendation metrics (HR, NDCG), addressing a gap that prior scaling law work in recommendation has left unaddressed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This provides the first quantitative framework for predicting SR model performance across parameter configurations, with direct practical applications in optimal hyperparameter search and model expansion planning that can save significant GPU time and cost. The framework's generality across architectures (HSTU, LLaMA2, SASRec, Mamba, LightGCN, Wukong, DiffuRec) and datasets (including a 400M-user industrial dataset) suggests broad applicability. However, the theoretical proofs rely on empirical inequalities (e.g., the 5·ln|I|·ln(Σ|S_u|) ≥ |S_u|_max + 2 bound) that may not hold in all regimes, and the framework has only been validated in the sequential recommendation setting, leaving open questions about transfer to ranking, retrieval, and multi-task recommendation.
