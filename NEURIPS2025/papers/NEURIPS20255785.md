## Problem

Existing Sparse plus Low-Rank (S+LR) decomposition methods for LLMs rely exclusively on alternating minimization, which provides limited convergence guarantees and suboptimally optimizes the sparse and low-rank components jointly. This leads to substantial performance degradation compared to dense models, particularly under aggressive compression configurations where the interaction between sparsity and rank constraints is most critical.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255785/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes pre-trained LLM weight matrices layer-by-layer along with 128 calibration text segments (2048 tokens each) from C4, and produces a pair of matrices per layer: a sparse matrix S (satisfying N:M structured or unstructured sparsity) and a low-rank matrix L (rank ≤ r) such that W ≈ S + L. The low-rank components are structured as LoRA adapters (A, B factors) that serve as smart initialization for downstream LoRA fine-tuning, while the sparse components remain frozen. Compression is applied sequentially across transformer blocks, with each block's output activations feeding into the next.

## Method

3BASiL formulates layer-wise S+LR decomposition as a constrained optimization problem and solves it via a novel 3-Block ADMM that introduces an auxiliary variable D (a constrained copy of S), enabling closed-form updates for all three blocks: S via a quadratic solve, L via rank-r projection (randomized SVD) in a weighted space, and D via magnitude-based pruning. The paper proves convergence under a non-decreasing penalty parameter ρ_t satisfying Σ 1/ρ_t < ∞, yielding a Cauchy sequence guarantee. A second contribution, Transformer Matching (TM), refines all sparse and low-rank components within a transformer block jointly by minimizing the output discrepancy with the dense model using Adam on small calibration chunks, operating as a memory-efficient intermediate loss between layer-wise reconstruction and full end-to-end training. TM is universal—it can be applied as a post-hoc refinement to any existing S+LR or pure-sparsity method—and the resulting low-rank components provide superior LoRA initialization for downstream adaptation.

## Results

On Llama3-8B under the (2:4 Sparse + 64 LR) configuration, 3BASiL-TM achieves a WikiText2 perplexity of 9.78 (vs. 6.14 dense), reducing the perplexity gap to the dense model by over 30% compared to the prior SOTA HASSLE-free-ALPS (WT2 = 11.38). On the zero-shot average across 8 tasks, 3BASiL-TM scores 63.34% vs. 61.61% for Hf-ALPS and 56.99% for OATS. The method also achieves over 2.5× faster compression runtime on an A100 GPU compared to the SOTA S+LR method, and over 7× speedup over HASSLE-free-ALPS on an L40 GPU for Llama3.2-3B.

## Baselines & Comparisons

3BASiL-TM consistently outperforms all S+LR baselines across every configuration tested. Against OATS (Zhang & Papyan, ICLR 2025), the gap is largest under aggressive compression: on Llama3-8B (2:4+64LR), 3BASiL-TM achieves WT2 = 9.78 vs. OATS's 14.76, and zero-shot average 63.34% vs. 56.99%. Against HASSLE-free-ALPS (Makni et al., 2025), the closest S+LR competitor, 3BASiL-TM reduces WT2 perplexity from 11.38 to 9.78 (a 30.5% gap reduction relative to dense) and improves zero-shot average from 61.61% to 63.34%. Against Hf-SparseGPT, the improvement is even more pronounced (WT2: 12.38 → 9.78). When compared to pure pruning methods (SparseGPT, ALPS, Wanda) at the same 50% compression ratio in Table 3, 3BASiL-TM with (3:8+LR) achieves WT2 = 8.69, outperforming ALPS-TM (10.65) and SparseGPT-TM (10.83), demonstrating that the S+LR structure plus transformer matching beats pure sparsity at matched compression. EoRA (Liu et al., 2024), which performs only a single low-rank correction after compression, underperforms both HASSLE-free variants (e.g., EoRA-ALPS WT2 = 13.61 vs. Hf-ALPS 11.38), confirming that iterative joint optimization is critical. The TM step is universal: applying it to any baseline (OATS-TM, Hf-ALPS-TM, ALPS-TM, Wanda-TM) yields consistent perplexity reductions of 20–40%, validating its generality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the (S+LR) decomposition line initiated by OATS and HASSLE-free, which both rely on alternating minimization to separately optimize sparse and low-rank components. The key departure is replacing this alternating scheme with a 3-block ADMM formulation that jointly optimizes both components under a unified objective, yielding provable convergence guarantees absent in prior (S+LR) methods. The transformer-matching (TM) refinement step is novel and draws on the observation that layer-wise reconstruction is an imperfect proxy for end-to-end loss, positioning TM as an intermediate optimization stage between layer-wise compression and full LoRA fine-tuning. The closed-form low-rank update (Eq. 4) inherits from classical reduced-rank regression and has been independently applied in CALDERA and EoRA, but here it is embedded within the ADMM loop rather than used as a one-shot post-hoc correction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The TM step's universality—demonstrated to improve any (S+LR) method including pure pruning (Wanda, SparseGPT, ALPS)—suggests that layer-wise objectives are systematically poor proxies for transformer-level behavior, a finding that could reshape how post-training compression pipelines are structured. The 3-step framework (layer-wise ADMM → TM → LoRA) provides a clear, modular roadmap for practitioners. Open questions include whether the approach extends to quantized or quantized-sparse backbones, whether dedicated per-layer sparsity/rank allocation can further improve the efficiency-utility tradeoff, and whether the convergence guarantee (requiring ρ_t → ∞ fast enough) is tight in practice. The method's reliance on a single GPU and modest calibration set (128 samples) makes it practically accessible, but the 30% perplexity gap reduction, while substantial, is measured against a still-maturing baseline landscape.
