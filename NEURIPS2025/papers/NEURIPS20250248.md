## Problem

Explainable k-medians clustering via threshold decision trees was previously studied only under the ℓ₁ and ℓ₂ norms, with no algorithm known for general ℓp norms (p ≥ 1). Additionally, no dynamic algorithm existed for explainable k-medians that could maintain a low-cost decision tree under a stream of center insertions and deletions. This paper closes both gaps by providing the first algorithm for all finite p ≥ 1 and the first dynamic implementation for explainable k-medians.

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes as input a set of k reference centers in ℝᵈ (produced by an off-the-shelf clustering method) and outputs a binary threshold decision tree with exactly k leaves, each containing one center. Crucially, the tree construction is oblivious to the actual data points X — it depends only on the reference centers, so the same tree can be applied to any dataset. In the dynamic variant, the algorithm receives a sequence of single-center insertions and deletions and updates the tree in O(d log³k) amortized time while modifying only O(log k) nodes per update.

## Method

The core procedure PARTITION_LEAF recursively partitions the center set using random threshold cuts drawn from a carefully designed distribution: a coordinate i and sign σ are chosen uniformly, and the offset θ is sampled with CDF x^p/R_t^p (rather than uniform), which is essential for achieving the tight O(p·log^{1+1/p−1/p²}k·log log k) competitive ratio. Each partition leaf call fixes an anchor (coordinate-wise median) and iteratively applies cuts until a stopping oracle ensures each leaf holds at most a constant fraction of centers. The analysis decomposes the expected cost into three cases — safe cuts, light cuts, and heavy-and-unsafe cuts — and applies Hölder's inequality to bound the heavy-unsafe contribution. For the dynamic algorithm, all random cuts are pre-generated with arrival times from a Poisson process; upon inserting a center, the algorithm identifies the earliest cut separating it from the anchor via a GET_EARLIEST_CUT data structure and splices it into the existing tree, achieving O(log k) recourse without rebuilding the entire subtree.

## Results

The paper's main result is an Õ(p · (log k)^(1+1/p−1/p²) · log log k) competitive ratio for explainable k-medians under ℓp norm for all finite p ≥ 1. For p = 2, this improves the prior bound of Õ(log^(3/2) k) to Õ(log^(5/4) k); for p = 1, it matches the known tight bound of (1 + H_{k−1}) up to an O(log log k) factor. The dynamic algorithm achieves O(d log³ k) amortized update time and O(log k) recourse per insertion/deletion while maintaining the same competitive ratio. A matching Ω(log k) lower bound is established for all p ≥ 1, and an Ω(d^(1/4)) lower bound shows no single algorithm can achieve good competitive ratios simultaneously for all p.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines. The comparisons are against prior theoretical guarantees. For p = 2, the paper improves upon Makarychev and Shan (2021, ICML), which achieved Õ(log^(3/2) k), by reducing the exponent to 5/4. For p = 1, the paper matches the tight upper bound of (1 + H_{k−1}) established by Gupta et al. (2023) up to a multiplicative O(log log k) factor, and the Ω(log k) lower bound from Dasgupta et al. (2020) is extended to all p ≥ 1. The dynamic algorithm's O(d log³ k) update time and O(log k) recourse are new — no prior dynamic algorithm existed for explainable k-medians. The paper also shows that the prior approach of using uniform random cuts (as in the ℓ1 setting) would yield a competitive ratio of at least Θ(k^(1−1/p)) for p > 1, motivating the non-uniform threshold distribution used here.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper generalizes the explainable k-medians framework of Dasgupta et al. (2020) and the ℓ₂-specific algorithm of Makarychev and Shan (2021) to all finite ℓₚ norms, replacing the uniform random cut distribution with a norm-aware distribution (CDF x^p/R_t^p) that is critical for achieving tight bounds. The dynamic algorithm extends the exponential-clock technique from Gupta et al. (2023) and Makarychev and Shan (2023)—previously used only for ℓ₁ analysis—by integrating Poisson-arrival timestamps directly into the algorithm's construction, enabling O(log k) recourse updates. The paper also builds on the fallback-center analysis framework from Makarychev and Shan (2021, 2022) to handle the three-case (safe/light/heavy) separation analysis.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first algorithm for explainable k-medians under general ℓₚ norms and the first dynamic algorithm for explainable clustering, opening a new direction for maintaining interpretable clusterings on evolving datasets. A key open question is the gap between the Ω(log k) lower bound and the Õ(log^{1+1/p-1/p²} k) upper bound, which is maximized at p=2 as Õ(log^{1/4} k). The impossibility result (Theorem 4.2) showing no single algorithm can achieve good competitive ratios for all p simultaneously without knowing p in advance is a useful negative result that constrains future algorithm design. For practitioners, the dynamic setting with O(d log³ k) update time and O(log k) recourse makes explainable clustering viable for streaming or frequently-updated data, though the theoretical guarantees remain the primary contribution.
