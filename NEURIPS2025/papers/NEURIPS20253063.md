## Problem

Existing 3D detectors are limited to closed-set categories or rely on hand-crafted text prompts for open-vocabulary detection, both of which fail to detect all objects in a scene when the vocabulary is incomplete or mismatched. The paper formally defines class-agnostic open-world 3D object detection—localizing every physically discrete object regardless of semantic category without any text prompts—and shows that simply converting closed-world detectors to binary classification is ineffective due to the scarcity of 3D annotations and categories.

## Input / Output

The method takes a paired point cloud X_P and RGB image X_I as input, with the image features projected into 3D voxel space via camera intrinsics K and extrinsics R_t to form a multi-modal representation. At inference, the model is fully prompt-free: it requires no text prompts, no external segmentation model, and no semantic labels, producing 3D bounding boxes with a binary objectness confidence score (object vs. background) rather than a category label. During training, SAM-generated 2D masks are projected into 3D to enrich the supervision set beyond the original annotations.

## Method

OP3Det combines two key components. First, a 3D Object Discovery pipeline applies SAM to RGB images using a multi-scale point sampling strategy that leverages 3D spatial proximities (δ ∈ {0.2, 0.5, 1, 2}) and per-point object priors from SAM IoU and DINO self-supervised attention to suppress fragmented masks; results are combined via NMS and post-processed with a class-agnostic 2D detector (LDET) before projection into 3D. Second, a Cross-Modal Mixture-of-Experts module applies self-attention separately to point-cloud features F_P, image features F_I′, and concatenated multi-modal features F_M, then uses a learned router (3D conv + global average pooling + FC + softmax) to produce routing probabilities that dynamically weight three experts (geometric, semantic, fused—each a stack of 3D convolutions with kernels 1, 3, 1). The resulting fused feature is fed into a 3D detection transformer head trained with a binary classification loss on both annotated and discovered 3D boxes, enabling the model to adaptively rely on 2D semantic cues, 3D geometric cues, or both depending on the scene.

## Results

On SUN RGB-D, OP3Det achieves 78.8% AR_novel, 89.7% AR_all, and 65.4% AP_all, surpassing the best closed-world detector FCAF3D (65.3% AR_novel) by 13.5% and the best open-vocabulary detector OV-Uni3DETR (62.8% AR_novel) by 16.0%. On the larger-vocabulary ScanNet dataset, OP3Det reaches 79.9% AR_novel and 28.6% AP_all, a 12.3% improvement over OV-Uni3DETR. In cross-dataset transfer (SUN RGB-D → ScanNet), OP3Det achieves 77.9% AR25, a 30% improvement over the next best method, with only a 2% gap to in-dataset performance. On outdoor KITTI, OP3Det attains 66.75% AP_3D (medium, hard), outperforming all baselines by over 1%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OP3Det outperforms all 26 baselines across every reported metric on both indoor datasets. Against the strongest closed-world detector FCAF3D [17], OP3Det gains +13.5% AR_novel (78.8 vs 65.3) and +3.4% AP_all (65.4 vs 62.0) on SUN RGB-D, while also improving AR_base (93.1 vs 92.7), confirming no regression on seen classes. Against the strongest open-vocabulary detector OV-Uni3DETR [9], the gap widens to +16.0% AR_novel on SUN RGB-D and +12.3% on ScanNet, with AP_all improving from 57.4 to 65.4. On KITTI (outdoor LiDAR), OP3Det achieves 66.75% AP_3D (medium, hard), beating Uni3DETR [15] (65.74%) and OV-Uni3DETR [9] (63.20%) by 1.0% and 3.6% respectively. In the class-specific open-vocabulary setting (Table 4), OP3Det surpasses GLRD [76] by 0.44% AP_novel on SUN RGB-D and 0.48% on ScanNet, and exceeds OV-Uni3DETR by 1.35% and 2.56% AP_novel respectively. In cross-dataset transfer, OP3Det's 77.9% AR25 (SUN→ScanNet) exceeds OV-Uni3DETR's 52.0% by 25.9 points, demonstrating substantially stronger domain generalization than both closed-world and open-vocabulary baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes SAM (originally designed for 2D segmentation) as a 3D object discovery tool, departing from prior SAM-in-3D work (SAM3D, Segment3D) that used it for instance segmentation rather than detection. It moves beyond open-vocabulary 3D detectors (CoDA, OV-Uni3DETR) by eliminating text prompts entirely in favor of class-agnostic binary objectness prediction, and departs from standard multi-modal fusion (BEVFusion, SparseFusion) by introducing a cross-modal MoE that dynamically routes between uni-modal and multi-modal pathways rather than always concatenating or summing features. The multi-scale point sampling strategy is a novel refinement of SAM's uniform grid prompting, leveraging 3D geometric proximity to suppress fragmented masks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By formally defining class-agnostic open-world 3D detection, this paper identifies a gap between closed-world 3D detectors (limited to training categories) and open-vocabulary 3D detectors (still constrained by prompt vocabulary), offering a formulation more aligned with true open-world perception. The finding that naive multi-modal fusion degrades class-agnostic 3D detection performance—requiring the MoE router to selectively preserve modality-specific cues—challenges the common assumption that more fusion is always better. Open questions include whether the approach scales to denser outdoor LiDAR scenes (where 2D-3D alignment is harder), how it handles non-rigid or low-contrast objects (acknowledged failure mode), and whether the cross-modal MoE generalizes beyond 3D detection to other multi-modal tasks.
