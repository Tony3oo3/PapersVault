## Problem

VGGT, a feed-forward scene reconstruction transformer, is limited to ~60 images per inference due to GPU memory, making large-scale SLAM infeasible. When extending VGGT to multiple overlapping submaps, the standard Sim(3) alignment (translation, rotation, scale) is insufficient because uncalibrated monocular cameras introduce a 15-DOF projective ambiguity that cannot be resolved by a similarity transform alone. The paper identifies this gap and proposes the first SLAM system that aligns submaps via optimization on the SL(4) manifold.

## Input / Output

The system consumes a sequential stream of uncalibrated monocular RGB images with no requirement for known or consistent camera intrinsics. Images are grouped into fixed-size submaps (w keyframes plus one overlap image from the prior submap and up to w_loop loop-closure images), each passed to VGGT as a single inference. The output is a globally consistent dense 3D point cloud (constructed by inverse-projecting VGGT's depth maps with confidence-based pruning), per-frame camera poses, estimated intrinsics, and depth maps, all expressed in a common global frame after SL(4) factor graph optimization.

## Method

The core insight is that the Projective Reconstruction Theorem implies reconstructions from uncalibrated cameras differ from the true geometry by a 15-DOF homography, which maps to the SL(4) Lie group. Locally, overlapping submaps (which share at least one image, providing dense correspondences without feature matching) are aligned by solving a 5-point RANSAC homography estimation, with the result normalized to unit determinant to lie on SL(4). Loop closures are detected via SALAD descriptors and similarly yield relative homography constraints. Globally, all relative homographies are fused through a nonlinear factor graph optimized on the SL(4) manifold using MAP estimation: the cost minimizes the squared Log-map residual weighted by an information matrix, solved iteratively via Levenberg-Marquardt with tangent-space (Lie algebra sl(4)) parameterization using 15 explicit generators. No additional training is required; the system builds entirely on VGGT's pretrained feed-forward reconstruction.

## Results

On TUM RGB-D (uncalibrated), VGGT-SLAM with SL(4) optimization achieves the best average ATE of 0.053 m, outperforming MASt3R-SLAM (0.060 m) and DROID-SLAM (0.158 m). On 7-Scenes (uncalibrated), VGGT-SLAM SL(4) achieves 0.067 m average ATE, matching MASt3R-SLAM (0.066 m) and beating DROID-SLAM (0.078 m). For dense reconstruction on 7-Scenes, VGGT-SLAM SL(4) achieves the best accuracy (0.052 m) and Chamfer distance (0.055 m) among all uncalibrated methods. The SL(4) variant consistently outperforms the Sim(3) variant on TUM (0.053 vs 0.074 m average ATE), with the gap driven by challenging scenes like 360 and floor.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest uncalibrated competitor MASt3R-SLAM*, VGGT-SLAM SL(4) wins on TUM RGB-D average ATE (0.053 vs 0.060 m) and on 7-Scenes dense reconstruction accuracy (0.052 vs 0.068 m) and Chamfer distance (0.055 vs 0.056 m), while essentially tying on 7-Scenes ATE (0.067 vs 0.066 m). Against DROID-SLAM in uncalibrated mode, VGGT-SLAM SL(4) achieves a large margin on TUM (0.053 vs 0.158 m) and a moderate margin on 7-Scenes (0.067 vs 0.078 m). Compared to calibrated state-of-the-art methods like GO-SLAM (0.035 m) and MASt3R-SLAM calibrated (0.030 m) on TUM, VGGT-SLAM SL(4) (0.053 m) is competitive without requiring any camera intrinsics. On dense reconstruction, VGGT-SLAM SL(4) outperforms Spann3R@20 on accuracy (0.052 vs 0.069 m) and Chamfer distance (0.055 vs 0.058 m). The main weakness is the TUM floor scene (0.141 m), where planar degeneracy causes the 15-DOF homography to become ill-conditioned, a limitation the authors acknowledge for future work.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the feed-forward reconstruction lineage (DUSt3R → MASt3R → VGGT) into a full SLAM system, building directly on VGGT's multi-image scene reconstruction while departing from MASt3R-SLAM's Sim(3) submap alignment. The key conceptual departure is invoking the classical Projective Reconstruction Theorem (Hartley & Zisserman) to argue that uncalibrated feed-forward reconstruction is only determined up to a 15-DOF projective transformation, motivating the first factor graph SLAM formulation on the SL(4) manifold. This connects to prior SL(3) optimization work in panoramic stitching but generalizes it to 3D point cloud alignment with 15 degrees of freedom.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a concrete failure mode of Sim(3) alignment for feed-forward reconstruction (projective ambiguity with shear, stretch, and perspective DOF) and provides a principled SL(4) solution, though quantitative gains on standard benchmarks (7-Scenes, TUM) are modest since VGGT's learned priors often recover near-metric reconstructions. Key open questions include: when exactly is Sim(3) sufficient versus SL(4), how to handle planar degeneracy that causes homography estimation to diverge (as seen on TUM floor), and whether a unified Sim(3)/SL(4) system can be built for real-time use. The approach also inherits VGGT's GPU memory constraint per submap and is sensitive to outliers in depth estimates, limiting robustness in challenging scenes.
