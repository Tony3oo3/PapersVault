## Problem

Most artificial neural networks process sensory inputs through globally connected or task-routed expert mechanisms, whereas biological sensorimotor systems like the rodent barrel cortex enforce strict one-to-one sensory-to-module mapping with communication restricted to spatially adjacent columns. Whether these biologically grounded localist routing constraints can be translated into effective machine learning architectures—rather than merely serving as anatomical curiosities—remains unexplored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250390/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes temporal tactile signals of shape [39, 2, T] from a NeuTouch sensor array (T=250 for EvTouch-Objects, T=325 for EvTouch-Containers), where each of the 39 sensors produces two-channel spiking data. Each sensor is rigidly assigned to one barrel column with no cross-sensor information leakage during preprocessing (1D dilated convolutions operate exclusively along the temporal axis). The output is a softmax probability vector over object categories (36 for Objects, 20 for Containers), read out from L5/6 spiking states via a 2D convolution and MLP.

## Method

The architecture comprises 39 barrel modules, each containing 8 excitatory neuron subtypes (32 cells each, 256 neurons total) connected by 37 documented projection pathways across cortical layers L2–L6, with dynamics governed by adaptive Leaky Integrate-and-Fire (aLIF) equations. A key differentiator from standard MoE is the localist inter-barrel routing: a KNN spatial gating mechanism (K=4 nearest neighbors, γ=0.2 sparsity) dynamically selects which neighboring subtypes contribute to each barrel's aggregated current, enforcing strictly short-range horizontal connectivity. All 39 barrels share identical columnar parameters (reducing parameters by 97% versus independent weights), which acts as cross-barrel regularization and improves training stability. Brainstem-thalamic preprocessing is simulated by two 1D dilated convolutions plus a linear relay, and final classification reads out L5/6 states through a 2D convolution and MLP, with gradients backpropagated via a Gaussian surrogate for the spiking nonlinearity.

## Results

The shared-parameter multi-barrel model achieves state-of-the-art accuracy of 94.44% on EvTouch-Objects and 86.67% on EvTouch-Containers, surpassing the previous best method GGT-SNN by 2.1% and 11.7% respectively. The shared-parameter variant also reduces parameter count by 97% (59,104 vs. 2,305,056) compared to the independent-parameter configuration while improving training stability. Collapsing the 39-barrel architecture into a single barrel degrades accuracy by 5.6% (Objects) and 16.7% (Containers), confirming the functional importance of the modular topographic organization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The shared multi-barrel model outperforms all seven baselines on both datasets where results are available. On EvTouch-Objects, it leads GGT-SNN [53] (92.36%) by 2.1%, AM-SGCN [52] (91.32%) by 3.1%, SnnTdlc [56] (91.04%) by 3.4%, and TactileSGNet [16] (89.44%) by 5.0%. On EvTouch-Containers, the margin is substantially larger: 11.7% over GGT-SNN (75.00%), 19.3% over SnnTdlc (67.33%), and 22.5% over TactileSGNet (64.17%). The independent-parameter multi-barrel variant matches GGT-SNN on Objects (92.36%) but falls 2.1% short of the shared variant, while the single-barrel ablation (88.89% / 70.00%) is clearly inferior to all multi-barrel configurations, underscoring that the topographic multi-column structure—not merely increased model capacity—drives the performance gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the authors' own single-barrel cortex model (Zhu et al., ICLR 2025) into a 39-barrel multi-column system, adding the critical architectural element of localist inter-barrel routing via KNN-based sparse gating. Conceptually, it positions the barrel cortex as a 'biologically constrained instantiation' of a mixture-of-experts system, deliberately contrasting topographic, adjacency-restricted routing with the global task-allocation routing in standard MoE architectures (Switch Transformers, DeepSeekMoE). It also departs from prior columnar ML models (Hashmi & Lipasti, Yoo et al.) by grounding each column's internal microcircuitry in documented neuroanatomical connectivity (37 projection pathways across 8 neuron subtypes) rather than abstract hierarchical feature extraction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that strong biological priors—somatotopic one-to-one sensor-to-module mapping, shared columnar parameters, and spatially constrained lateral communication—can simultaneously reduce parameters by 97% and improve both accuracy and training stability, suggesting that anatomical constraints act as effective regularization. The observation that localist inter-barrel currents suppress inter-module activity correlations (mirroring cortical lateral inhibition) offers a concrete mechanism for reducing expert redundancy in MoE systems, a known open problem. However, validation is limited to two small tactile datasets (720 and 300 samples), the model has not been tested on mainstream ML benchmarks, and the authors acknowledge that redundant synaptic connections indicate incomplete biological modeling. A key open question is whether the localist routing principle can be transplanted into standard MoE architectures for language or vision tasks without the biological scaffolding.
