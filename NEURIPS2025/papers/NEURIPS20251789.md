## Problem

Most continual offline RL methods assume all tasks share identical state and action spaces, which deviates from realistic scenarios where new tasks introduce different observation and action dimensions (e.g., different robots or sensor configurations). Existing structure-based approaches that preserve task-specific weights are limited to simple architectures and same-space settings, while rehearsal- and regularization-based methods cannot perfectly retain prior knowledge. The paper asks whether diffusion models' expressive power can be combined with structure-based parameter isolation to handle CORL across arbitrary task sequences with heterogeneous spaces.

## Input / Output

The method consumes pre-collected offline trajectory datasets from multiple tasks, where each task may have different state and action dimensions. During training, the diffusion model operates on quantized state sequences (not state-action pairs) conditioned on discounted returns, while an inverse dynamics model maps consecutive quantized states to quantized actions. At inference, the generated state sequence is decoded through a task-specific VQ decoder back into the original action space, and the return condition is set to a fixed high value (0.8) to elicit high-reward behavior. The QSA module ensures all tasks are mapped into a unified quantized space with bounded norm (ρ=3.0) before the diffusion model processes them.

## Method

VQ-CD comprises two complementary modules. The Quantized Spaces Alignment (QSA) module uses task-specific vector-quantized encoders and decoders with a shared codebook to project heterogeneous state and action spaces into a common quantized representation, with a norm constraint (‖z_q‖₂² < ρ) enforced via clipping to keep values within a range suitable for diffusion training. The Selective Weights Activation (SWA) module applies task-specific binary sparse masks to the 1D convolution kernels of a U-Net diffusion model, so each task exclusively trains a disjoint subset of parameters, preventing catastrophic forgetting without requiring rehearsal or regularization. Rather than using a sparse optimizer during training (which is computationally intractable), the method trains with standard dense updates and then assembles the final model by summing the mask-extracted task weights. The diffusion model generates state sequences via classifier-free guidance on return conditions, and a shared inverse dynamics model converts consecutive states into actions in the aligned space.

## Results

On the Ant-dir continual task sequence (10-15-19-25), VQ-CD achieves a mean return of 558.22±1.14, outperforming the strongest baseline CoD (478.19±15.84) by approximately 80 points and the multitask upper bound Multitask CoD (485.15±5.86) by ~73 points. On D4RL tasks with diverse state/action spaces, VQ-CD reaches a normalized score of 48.0, substantially exceeding L2M (13.4) and L2M-large (16.0). The method also achieves a 19.76× generation speedup via DDIM (0.29s per generation vs. 5.73s for 200-step sampling) while maintaining competitive performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VQ-CD wins decisively across all 17 baselines on 15 CL task settings. On Ant-dir (10-15-19-25), it surpasses the rehearsal-based CoD by ~80 points (558.22 vs. 478.19) and IL-rehearsal by ~156 points (558.22 vs. 402.53). The parameter-sharing approach CoD-LoRA (296.03) and the multitask MTDIFF (84.01) lag far behind, indicating that simple weight sharing or joint training cannot match selective activation. On D4RL tasks with heterogeneous state/action spaces, VQ-CD (48.0 normalized score) outperforms L2M (13.4) and L2M-large (16.0) by 3–4×, and beats CuGRO (27.6) on the medium-difficulty setting. Notably, VQ-CD also matches the multitask upper bound (Multitask CoD) on the longer Ant-dir sequence (4-18-26-34-42-49), showing that continual training with SWA can recover full multitask performance without joint training. The method is also more computationally efficient: 55h training and 4.6 GB GPU memory vs. L2M's 92h and 6.7 GB, while achieving 19.8× faster generation than the 200-step baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the authors' own Continual Diffuser (CoD) framework by replacing the experience-rehearsal mechanism with selective weights activation (SWA) via task-specific sparse masks on a U-Net diffusion model, and by adding a vector-quantized space alignment (QSA) module that maps heterogeneous state/action spaces into a unified latent space. The diffusion backbone and inverse-dynamics action generation follow the conditional generative modeling paradigm of Ajay et al. (2022) and the DDPM formulation of Ho et al. (2020). The key conceptual departure from prior CORL work (rehearsal-based, regularization-based, and structure-based methods) is the explicit handling of tasks with different state and action space dimensions, which all previous continual offline RL methods assume to be identical.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By enabling CORL on arbitrary task sequences with heterogeneous spaces, VQ-CD removes a practical barrier that has limited the deployment of continual RL agents in real-world settings where sensor configurations and action spaces naturally vary across tasks. The QSA module's extensibility (new tasks can be added by training new encoders/decoders/codebook entries without retraining the diffusion model) is a practical advantage for open-ended learning. However, the method remains task-aware, requiring explicit task boundaries, and the per-task QSA pre-training adds computational overhead. Open questions include whether the constrained quantization (clipping to norm ρ) generalizes to other generative backbones and whether the approach scales to very long task sequences where mask positions become scarce.
