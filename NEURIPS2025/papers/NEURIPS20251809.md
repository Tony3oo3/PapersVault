## Problem

While replicable algorithms guarantee consistent outputs across different samples (protecting against data manipulation), no prior work addressed replicability in online pricing or prophet inequality problems. The key open question is what sample complexity is required to design a pricing algorithm that is both ρ-replicable and α-competitive, and whether the dependence on the ground set size |X| can be avoided.

## Input / Output

The algorithm receives n samples from each of N distributions D_i supported on a known finite set X ⊆ R^{≥0}, along with target parameters (α, ρ, β). It outputs a price pair (τ, κ) where τ is the acceptance threshold and κ ∈ [0,1] is a Bernoulli tie-breaking probability that handles distributions with atoms at the median. The pair must be α-competitive (achieving at least α·E[max{X_1,...,X_N}]) with probability ≥ 1−β while being ρ-replicable. For the auxiliary heavy hitter subproblem, the output is a single ν-heavy hitter value or Null.

## Method

The core insight is to bypass mean estimation (whose additive error breaks competitiveness when E[max] is small) in favor of a median-based pricing strategy: the algorithm computes a γ-approximate median pair (τ, κ) of the distribution of max{X_1,...,X_N} using existing replicable median estimation, which by Lemma 4 yields a (1/2−γ)-competitive price. For the lower bound, the authors reduce from the replicable interior point problem and show that hard instances are necessarily α-light (no point has mass ≥ α), forcing any competitive price into the distribution's interior and requiring Ω̃(α·log*|X|) samples. A key technical contribution is a new replicable heavy hitter algorithm achieving nearly linear ν^{-1} sample complexity (versus cubic ν^{-3} in prior work) via a two-stage design: first sampling a candidate set of size Θ(ν^{-1}), then using a fresh sample to estimate probabilities, with a random permutation constructed via Reed-Solomon-based k-wise independent hashing to ensure replicable selection of the first qualifying candidate.

## Results

The paper establishes that the Replicable Online Pricing (ROP) problem admits a (1/2−ε)-competitive threshold with sample complexity poly(log*|X|, ρ⁻¹, β⁻¹, ε⁻¹) per distribution (Theorem 1), and a matching lower bound of Ω̃(α·log*|X|) (Theorem 3). For i.i.d. distributions, the sample complexity improves to O(α·poly(log*|X|, ρ⁻¹, β⁻¹)) (Theorem 4). The novel replicable heavy hitter algorithm achieves sample complexity O(ν⁻¹ρ⁻²log³(ν⁻¹)(log(ρ⁻¹)+log(β⁻¹))), improving the dependence on the heavy hitter parameter ν from cubic (ν⁻³) in prior work to nearly linear (ν⁻¹) (Theorem 5).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary quantitative comparison is in the replicable heavy hitter problem (Theorem 5). Prior algorithms by Impagliazzo et al. [ILPS22], Esfandiari et al. [EKM⁺23], and Hopkins et al. [HIK⁺24b] all exhibit a cubic (or worse) dependence on ν⁻¹, with sample complexities scaling as ν⁻³ or ν⁻⁴. This paper's two-stage algorithm using Reed-Solomon-based k-wise independent permutations reduces this to a nearly linear ν⁻¹ dependence, which is nearly tight since Ω(ν⁻¹) samples are required even without replicability. The paper notes that the prior work addresses a more general variant (returning a list of all heavy hitters), so the comparison is not strictly apples-to-apples. For the ROP problem itself, no direct algorithmic baselines are compared since the contribution is the first replicable algorithm; the comparison is against the theoretical lower bound, which the upper bound matches up to polylogarithmic factors in log*|X|.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of the replicability framework (Impagliazzo et al. STOC'22; Bun et al. STOC'23) and prophet inequalities / online pricing (Hajiaghayi et al. EC'04; Kleinberg & Weinberg STOC'12; Samuel-Cahn 1984). A key methodological departure is the use of replicable median estimation rather than the more natural replicable mean estimation, since additive error in mean estimation breaks the competitive guarantee when the optimal value is small. The lower bound technique reduces from the replicable interior point problem via the privacy-to-replicability reduction of Bun et al., while the upper bound for the i.i.d. case and the heavy hitter algorithm (nearly linear in 1/ν vs. cubic in prior work by Esfandiari et al. and Hopkins et al.) are novel technical contributions. The delegation extension builds directly on the Kleinberg & Kleinberg (EC'18) model of delegated search.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that replicability is achievable for online pricing with poly(log*|X|) sample complexity and proves this dependence is necessary, resolving a fundamental question about the cost of algorithmic transparency in economic decision-making. The heavy hitter algorithm with nearly linear dependence on 1/ν is of independent interest and tightens the lower bound range from (log*|X|)^{-0.33} to (log*|X|)^{-0.99}. Open questions include extending to additive-error guarantees, approximate replicability (allowing nearly-equal outputs), other pricing problem variants, and empirical validation. The framework also raises the broader question of how replicability interacts with robustness notions in settings where decisions have significant real-world consequences.
