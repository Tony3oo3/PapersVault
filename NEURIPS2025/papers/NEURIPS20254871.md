## Problem

Adapting a pretrained diffusion model to a new reward function at inference time requires guiding the denoising process based on rewards that are only observable at the terminal (clean) state. Existing steering methods—gradient-based guidance, sequential Monte Carlo, and local search—rely on biased one-step value estimates (e.g., Tweedie's formula) that become essentially random at high noise levels, and they discard all intermediate information after each sample, so additional compute does not cumulatively improve quality.

## Input / Output

The method takes a frozen pretrained diffusion model (pixel-space UNet for MNIST/CIFAR-10 at 32×32, latent diffusion for Stable Diffusion v1.5 at 64×64×4, or a discrete masked diffusion language model for 64-token sequences), a scalar reward function evaluated only on the clean output, and an optional conditioning signal (class label or text prompt). DTS produces a population of samples drawn from the reward-tilted posterior π*(x) ∝ p_θ(x)·exp(r(x)), while DTS* returns a single high-reward sample. Because the tree is built incrementally, sampling from it after construction requires no further model calls, making repeated draws effectively free and the algorithm anytime—more NFEs yield strictly better samples.

## Method

DTS casts the reverse diffusion chain as a finite-horizon tree where the pretrained model's mean prediction acts as a deterministic policy and the Gaussian denoising step is a stochastic transition, placing the problem in the classical MCTS setting. Each tree iteration performs selection (Boltzmann sampling over children proportional to exp(λv̂) for DTS, or UCT for DTS*), expansion via progressive widening B(x_t)=C·N(x_t)^α, a full rollout to the terminal state that is added to the tree, and a soft Bellman backup that propagates the terminal reward r(x_0) upward via log-sum-exp aggregation of child values. This backup replaces the biased Tweedie's-formula one-step estimates used by SMC and gradient methods with empirically accurate soft values that improve with each additional rollout. The search variant DTS* switches to greedy max-value selection at inference, implementing marginal-MAP inference that selects the branch carrying the greatest posterior mass rather than chasing a single reward spike, which provides an implicit KL-regularization effect against reward over-optimization. Proposition 1 proves that as the number of tree iterations M→∞, the empirical distribution of terminal states converges to the exact target π*.

## Results

On class-conditional posterior sampling, DTS achieves the lowest FID across all settings: 0.014±0.005 on MNIST single-digit, 0.007±0.003 on MNIST even/odd, and 0.195±0.041 on CIFAR-10, outperforming the best baseline (DAS) by 2.8×, 4.4×, and 1.2× respectively. DTS also achieves the lowest CMMD in all three settings (e.g., 0.068 vs 0.179 for DAS on MNIST). In text-to-image generation, DTS* matches best-of-N ImageReward scores with up to 5× fewer NFEs, and in text generation it achieves the highest CoLA acceptability reward while maintaining higher trigram diversity than FK/SMC.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DTS outperforms all four baselines on FID and CMMD across every class-conditional setting. Against DPS (gradient-based guidance [11]), DTS reduces MNIST FID from 0.359 to 0.014 (25× improvement) and CIFAR-10 FID from 0.486 to 0.195, while DPS frequently produces samples outside the prior support. Against SMC/FK-Steering [71], DTS achieves 4.3× lower FID on MNIST (0.014 vs 0.060) and 1.6× lower on CIFAR-10 (0.195 vs 0.313); SMC/FK also shows mode collapse on multimodal posteriors (e.g., oversampling digit 2 in MNIST even). Against TDS [84], DTS reduces MNIST FID from 0.087 to 0.014 and CIFAR-10 FID from 0.487 to 0.195, with TDS exhibiting the worst CMMD (0.463 on MNIST) due to gradient instability. Against DAS [39], the closest competitor, DTS still achieves 2.8× lower FID on MNIST (0.014 vs 0.039) and 1.2× lower on CIFAR-10 (0.195 vs 0.241). In the search setting (DTS*), the method matches best-of-N quality with up to 5× fewer NFEs on DrawBench and avoids the severe reward over-optimization seen with FK/SMC on aesthetic scoring.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper casts inference-time alignment of diffusion models as a finite-horizon tree search problem, directly building on Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS) and its continuous-space extensions (progressive widening, UCT). It departs from the dominant gradient-based guidance (DPS, TDS) and sequential Monte Carlo (FK-Steering, DAS) paradigms, which rely on biased one-step Tweedie's formula value estimates, by instead propagating exact terminal rewards backward through the tree via soft Bellman backups. The formulation also connects to maximum entropy RL (the soft value function satisfies the same recursion as the soft Bellman equation) and to Generative Flow Networks (same log-sum-exp flow constraint, but without parameter learning), positioning DTS as a zero-shot, non-parametric sampler for the KL-regularized posterior.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The anytime property—where additional compute sequentially improves sample quality by reusing past rollouts rather than merely increasing parallel particle count—addresses a practical gap in existing steering methods that treat each generation as independent. The asymptotic consistency guarantee and the implicit KL-regularization effect (volume-based selection avoids reward over-optimization) provide theoretical grounding that purely heuristic search methods lack. Key limitations include the sequential control flow that limits parallelization, exponential tree growth in high dimensions without a strong pretrained prior, and the absence of a learned value network (a natural future direction analogous to AlphaZero). Open questions include whether the approach scales to very high-dimensional or multi-step inverse problems where the posterior is extremely sharp.
