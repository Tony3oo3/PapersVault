## Problem

Sampling conformational ensembles of biomolecules is critical for understanding protein function and drug discovery, but molecular dynamics is limited by femtosecond timesteps while existing ML generative models fail to transfer to systems outside their training distribution. The paper addresses this dual gap in speed and transferability by bridging physical MD with score-based learning in a smoothed latent space.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251236/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an initial all-atom 3D conformation (N×3 coordinate matrix, seeded procedurally via LEaP or from PDB) and the amino acid sequence, which provides atom-type embeddings, residue codes, and bonding topology as conditioning features. The sequence index is deliberately excluded to enable generalization to longer peptides. Output is a set of all-atom 3D point clouds constituting a conformational ensemble drawn from the Boltzmann distribution, with the model operating at a fixed noise level of σ=0.4 Å.

## Method

JAMUN performs Langevin molecular dynamics in a noisy latent space Y = X + σε using the Walk-Jump Sampling framework: a 'walk' step integrates the SDE via the BAOAB solver using a learned score function, and a 'jump' step projects back to clean data via a denoiser. The key mathematical insight is that the score ∇_y log p_Y(y) and the denoiser x̂(y) = y + σ²∇_y log p_Y(y) are equivalent, so a single SE(3)-equivariant GNN (NequIP-style, ~10.5M parameters, 4 message-passing blocks via e3nn) trained with a simple L2 denoising loss at one fixed noise level serves both roles. Unlike diffusion or flow-matching models that require many noise levels and start from an uninformative prior, JAMUN only needs one noise level and can denoise from the slightly noised distribution, yielding an order-of-magnitude speedup. The SE(3) (rather than E(3)) equivariance avoids the parity constraint that forces symmetric Ramachandran plots in prior methods, and the physical prior from MD data enables transfer to unseen peptide sequences and even longer chain lengths.

## Results

JAMUN generates conformational ensembles at 2–8 ms per sample (100,000 samples in 3–12.5 min), roughly 5–60× faster than baseline ML models and an order of magnitude faster than MD (40–108 ms/sample). On TIMEWARP 2AA-LARGE, JAMUN achieves JSD of 0.165 (all torsions) vs. 0.225 for TBG at comparable sampling time. On MDGEN 4AA-EXPLICIT, JAMUN (JSD 0.187 all torsions) is competitive with MDGen (0.107). Most notably, JAMUN trained only on 4AA peptides generalizes to unseen 5AA peptides, achieving JSD 0.197 (all torsions) vs. 0.411 for Boltz-1 and 0.420 for BioEmu on UNCAPPED 5AA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against TBG on 2AA peptides, JAMUN (JSD 0.165 all torsions) outperforms TBG at comparable wall-clock time (0.225), though TBG at full sampling budget (0.105) remains superior—reflecting TBG's advantage when given 20× more compute. On 4AA peptides, JAMUN (0.187) is competitive with the peptide-specific MDGen model (0.107), with both missing some basins that the other captures; JAMUN also shows faster decorrelation times for backbone and sidechain torsions. The most striking result is generalization to 5AA: JAMUN trained only on 4AA data achieves JSD 0.197 (all torsions), substantially outperforming Boltz-1 (0.411) and BioEmu (0.420), which were trained on crystal structures and large-protein MD data respectively. Neither TBG nor MDGen can transfer to 5AA at all due to fixed-length positional embeddings. In terms of speed, JAMUN is ~5× faster than Boltz-1 (8 ms vs. 360 ms/sample) and ~60× faster than BioEmu+H-Packer (8 ms vs. 4320 ms/sample) on 5AA peptides.

## Relation to Prior Work

JAMUN is the first application of Walk-Jump Sampling (WJS) from Neural Empirical Bayes [Saremi & Hyvärinen, 2019] to 3D point clouds, extending a framework previously used only for voxelized molecules and discrete protein sequences. The denoiser adopts the Karras et al. normalization parametrization from diffusion model design but operates at a single fixed noise level rather than a full noise schedule, and uses an SE(3)-equivariant GNN (similar to NequIP) rather than the E(3)-equivariant EGNN used by prior Boltzmann generators like TBG and Timewarp. Conceptually, JAMUN departs from both diffusion/flow-matching approaches (which generate from an uninformative prior over many steps) and from prior Boltzmann emulators (MDGen, BioEmu) by performing Langevin dynamics in a smoothed latent space, decoupling the 'walk' (sampling) from the 'jump' (denoising/projection) steps.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

JAMUN achieves ~10× speedup over classical MD for small peptides while maintaining sampling quality, and critically demonstrates transferability to peptides longer than the training set (4AA→5AA) where prior methods like TBG and MDGen simply fail to generalize. The physical prior from MD data appears to be the key enabler of this transferability, suggesting a path toward generative models that work outside their training distribution without retraining. However, the current scope is limited to 2–5 amino acid peptides; scaling to full proteins remains an open challenge, as do questions about whether enhanced sampling methods (e.g., metadynamics) can further accelerate traversal of the noisy latent space, and whether the single-noise-level assumption holds for more complex conformational landscapes.
