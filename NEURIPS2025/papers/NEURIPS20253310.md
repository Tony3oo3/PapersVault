## Problem

Directly applying rule-based RL (GRPO) to video reasoning in MLLMs suffers from two fundamental issues: the absence of explicit temporal reward signals causes models to shortcut reasoning by relying on single frames rather than temporal dynamics, and the scarcity of high-quality video reasoning data limits the diversity of reasoning patterns the model can learn. Video-R1 is the first systematic attempt to address both gaps within the R1 paradigm.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253310/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes temporally ordered video frames (16 frames at 128×28×28 during training; 16–64 frames at 256×28×28 during inference) alongside a text question, and additionally processes a randomly shuffled frame sequence as a contrastive input during T-GRPO training. It outputs a chain-of-thought reasoning trace followed by a final answer in one of several verifiable formats: multiple-choice, numerical, OCR text, free-form QA, or regression value, each with a corresponding rule-based reward function.

## Method

The core algorithmic contribution is T-GRPO, which extends GRPO with a temporal contrastive reward: for each video question, the model generates G=8 responses on ordered frames and G̃=4 on shuffled frames, and a temporal reward r_t=0.3 is granted only to correct responses when the ordered group's accuracy proportion exceeds the shuffled group's, explicitly incentivizing temporally grounded reasoning over frame-level shortcuts. Training follows a two-stage pipeline on Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct: an SFT cold start on Video-R1-CoT-165k (CoT rationales generated by Qwen2.5-VL-72B), followed by RL on Video-R1-260k, a mixed image-and-video dataset (116k video + 146k image samples spanning math, spatial, OCR, chart, and knowledge domains) that bootstraps general reasoning skills transferable to video. A length-based reward (ω=0.2 for responses in [320, 512] tokens) is added to balance deep reasoning against overthinking, and the KL penalty coefficient is set to β=0.04 with Adam at lr=1e-6.

## Results

Video-R1-7B achieves 37.1% accuracy on VSI-Bench (64 frames), surpassing the proprietary GPT-4o model (34.0%) on this video spatial reasoning benchmark. On the broader video reasoning suite, Video-R1-7B (64 frames) scores 52.4 on VideoMMMU, 63.8 on MMVU (MC), 64.8 on MVBench, 73.2 on TempCompass, and 61.4 on VideoMME (wo sub). Even at 16 frames, Video-R1-7B outperforms its SFT-only counterpart (Qwen2.5-VL-7B-SFT) across all six benchmarks, confirming that RL training yields consistent gains over supervised fine-tuning alone.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the proprietary GPT-4o, Video-R1-7B (64 frames) wins on VSI-Bench (37.1 vs. 34.0) but trails substantially on VideoMMMU (52.4 vs. 61.2), MMVU (63.8 vs. 75.4), and VideoMME (61.4 vs. 71.9), indicating GPT-4o retains a strong edge on knowledge-intensive and general video tasks. Compared to the closest open-source baselines, Video-R1-7B (16 frames) outperforms LLaVA-OneVision-7B by 2.2 points on VSI-Bench (34.6 vs. 32.4) and 16.0 points on VideoMMMU (49.8 vs. 33.8), and beats Kangaroo-8B on MVBench (62.7 vs. 61.1) and TempCompass (72.6 vs. 62.5). Against video-specific models, Video-R1-7B surpasses Video-UTR-7B by 3.9 points on MVBench and 12.9 on TempCompass, and outperforms LongVA-7B by 5.4 points on VSI-Bench and 25.7 on VideoMMMU. The SFT-only ablation (Qwen2.5-VL-7B-SFT at 64 frames) is consistently outperformed by Video-R1-7B at the same frame budget, with the largest gap on MVBench (64.8 vs. 60.6, +4.2) and TempCompass (73.2 vs. 70.0, +3.2), confirming that RL training is the primary driver of the gains rather than additional data exposure.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DeepSeek-R1 rule-based RL paradigm (originally for text reasoning via GRPO) to the video domain, building on prior multimodal RL efforts like Kimi k1.5 and Skywork R1V that applied RL to image-text reasoning. Its key algorithmic departure is T-GRPO, which augments GRPO with a contrastive temporal reward comparing ordered vs. shuffled frame sequences — a mechanism absent from prior GRPO-based methods. The hybrid image-video training strategy addresses a data scarcity problem that prior video MLLM works (LLaMA-VID, VideoLLaMA2, LongVA) sidestepped by focusing on perception rather than reasoning, and the approach is conceptually aligned with Video-UTR's identification of temporal shortcut failures but solves it through RL reward design rather than architectural changes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic demonstration that rule-based RL can elicit video reasoning in MLLMs, providing both a training algorithm (T-GRPO) and large-scale datasets (165k SFT + 260k RL) that lower the barrier for future video reasoning research. Key limitations include training at only 16 frames (limiting long-range temporal reasoning), a single 7B model scale, and only 1k RL steps, leaving open whether the approach scales to longer videos and larger models. The finding that SFT alone can degrade performance while RL consistently improves it reinforces the broader 'SFT memorizes, RL generalizes' narrative and suggests that video reasoning may be particularly resistant to imitation-based training. Future directions include more efficient temporal modeling, adaptive response length control, and generalist video reward models to reduce reliance on handcrafted rules.
