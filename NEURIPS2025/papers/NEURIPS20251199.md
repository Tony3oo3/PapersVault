## Problem

In federated learning with heterogeneous devices, straggler clients with limited compute force the server to wait, inflating wall-clock training time. Existing mitigations—client selection, asynchronous aggregation, and width/depth scaling—sacrifice accuracy by underrepresenting slow clients or disrupting model architecture. Directly applying ElasticTrainer's tensor selection to FL compounds the problem: backward-propagation dependencies confine slow clients to training only back-end layers, and non-IID data biases per-client tensor importance, amplifying local model drift.

## Input / Output

Each client receives the current global model from the server, its local non-IID data shard (images, audio clips, or text sequences), and two hyperparameters: a runtime threshold T_th (set to the fastest device's full-model training time) and a balance parameter β (default 0.6). Rather than uploading full model weights, each client transmits only the updated tensors within its current training window, reducing per-round communication. The server aggregates these partial updates using mask-aware weighting (c_n(t))_k = (A_n(t))_k / Σ_n (A_n(t))_k, producing the next global model that is broadcast to all clients.

## Method

FedEL introduces two mechanisms. First, sliding-window training partitions the DNN into blocks and maintains a window whose front edge advances to include deeper blocks (until cumulative block time exceeds T_th) and whose end edge retracts to drop blocks with no selected important tensors; a lightweight early-exit layer is attached to the window's last block so each window trains independently. Within the window, a modified ElasticTrainer uses dynamic programming starting from the window's last tensor to select the optimal subset of tensors under the T_th budget, freezing all others. Second, a tensor importance adjustment module computes global importance as I^g = (w_{r+1} − w_r)²/η_n from consecutive global models and blends it with local ElasticTrainer importance via I_{n,r+1} = β·I_{n,r+1} + (1−β)·I^g, counteracting the bias introduced by non-IID data. A convergence theorem (Appendix E) shows the mask-based aggregation introduces a bounded bias term O1 that does not affect the convergence rate, and a rollback mechanism allows earlier layers to be revisited, empirically reducing O1.

## Results

FedEL achieves up to 3.87× speedup in wall-clock training time compared to FedAvg while matching or exceeding final accuracy across all four tasks. On the 10-device hardware testbed (CIFAR-10/VGG16), FedEL reaches 56.51% accuracy in 63.8h versus FedAvg's 56.13% in 119.8h (1.87× speedup). In the 100-device simulation, FedEL attains 34.96% on Tiny ImageNet (3.59×), 58.26% on Google Speech Commands (3.87×), and 77.23 perplexity on Reddit (3.13×), all outperforming or matching FedAvg's accuracy. FedEL also reduces memory usage by up to 32.7% and total energy consumption by 49.59% compared to FedAvg.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedEL matches or exceeds FedAvg accuracy (56.51% vs 56.13% on CIFAR-10; 58.26% vs 58.04% on Speech) while cutting training time by 1.87×–3.87×. Against ElasticTrainer, FedEL recovers the 28.6% accuracy loss (e.g., 56.51% vs 40.03% on CIFAR-10) while being 1–2% faster in wall-clock time by reducing tensor-selection overhead via window sliding. FedEL outperforms HeteroFL by 5.7%–14.4% in accuracy (e.g., 58.26% vs 51.47% on Speech) and DepthFL by up to 7.1% (58.26% vs 54.23% on Speech), as both baselines sacrifice feature-extraction quality by scaling or truncating the network. Compared to PyramidFL, FedEL achieves 1%–2% higher accuracy with substantially lower training time (183.3h vs 587.4h on Speech), since PyramidFL only accelerates convergence without reducing total rounds. FedEL surpasses TimelyFL by up to 5% accuracy (58.26% vs 56.49% on Speech) and FIARSE by a small margin, as FIARSE does not account for backward-gradient propagation dependencies. When combined with non-IID-aware aggregators, FedEL+FedProx reaches 56.6% in 45.4h (1.81× speedup over FedProx alone) and FedEL+FedNova reaches 66.1% in 47.8h (1.77× speedup), confirming compatibility beyond FedAvg.

## Relation to Prior Work

FedEL builds directly on ElasticTrainer (MobiSys 2023), which dynamically selects important DNN tensors under a runtime budget for single-device training. The key conceptual departure is adapting this tensor-selection mechanism to federated learning, where two additional challenges arise: backward-propagation dependencies constrain slower clients to only train back-end layers, and non-IID data biases local tensor importance estimates, exacerbating model drift. The paper positions itself against three established FL heterogeneity strategies—client selection (PyramidFL), asynchronous aggregation (TimelyFL), and partial training via width/depth scaling (HeteroFL, DepthFL, FIARSE)—arguing that all either exclude stragglers or compromise model architecture. Its two core innovations, sliding-window training and global-aware tensor importance adjustment, are novel combinations that address these specific gaps without altering the global model structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FedEL offers a practical path toward deploying FL on genuinely heterogeneous edge fleets (e.g., mixed Jetson Xavier/Orin) without sacrificing accuracy, achieving up to 3.87× wall-clock speedup over FedAvg while matching or exceeding final test accuracy across image, speech, and NLP tasks. The convergence analysis (Appendix E) provides theoretical grounding for masked federated aggregation, though it introduces a bias term tied to device heterogeneity that does not vanish. Key limitations include reliance on only two hardware tiers in the physical testbed, an 18.9% deviation from the target runtime threshold for transformer models, and no treatment of communication heterogeneity or differential privacy. Open questions include whether the sliding-window approach generalizes to more extreme compute disparities, whether the β balancing parameter can be learned adaptively, and how the method interacts with secure aggregation protocols.
