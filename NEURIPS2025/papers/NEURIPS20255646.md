## Problem

Existing nonparametric regression methods for streaming data either lack formal privacy guarantees, rely on a trusted central server (CDP), or are not robust to heavy-tailed noise. No scalable, statistically sound method has been developed to perform online private nonparametric regression under the local differential privacy (LDP) framework while simultaneously handling outliers and heavy-tailed errors in infinite-dimensional function spaces.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255646/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes an i.i.d. stream of (X_n, Y_n) pairs one at a time in a single pass, along with a reproducing kernel K, a step-size schedule {γ_n}, a Huber threshold τ, and per-iteration privacy budgets (ε_n, δ_n). At each iteration it outputs the current and Polyak-averaged function estimates evaluated on a fixed grid {t_j}_{j=1}^J, requiring only O(1) memory per step since no historical data or kernel evaluations are stored. The grid discretization serves as a finite-dimensional approximation to the infinite-dimensional RKHS estimator, with approximation error diminishing as grid density J increases.

## Method

The paper proposes H-FSGD, a one-pass functional stochastic gradient descent algorithm that replaces the standard squared loss with a Huber loss, yielding uniformly bounded gradients that simultaneously provide robustness to outliers and a natural sensitivity bound (2τB) for privacy calibration. Its private extension, PH-FSGD, adds per-iteration Gaussian process noise with covariance (8τ²B² log(2/δ_n)/ε_n²)·K to the gradient update, achieving (ε,δ)-LDP at every step without requiring a trusted curator. A Polyak average over the update trajectory improves stability and convergence. The key theoretical contribution is a non-asymptotic convergence analysis showing that under optimal constant or decaying step-size schedules, the estimators achieve minimax-optimal rates in L²(P_X), with the privacy budget entering only as an additive variance factor that does not alter the asymptotic rate. The Huber parameter τ is selected data-drivenly via a median absolute deviation procedure, and the framework naturally supports mixed privacy regimes through iteration-specific budgets.

## Results

The paper evaluates H-FSGD and PH-FSGD on synthetic nonparametric regression tasks (sine and Beta-density functions) using MSE over 200 independent runs at n = 2000, 5000, and 10000. Under heavy-tailed t(3) errors, H-FSGD significantly outperforms the least-squares L2-FSGD baseline in both median and interquartile range of MSE, with the advantage most pronounced at smaller sample sizes; under Gaussian N(0, 0.25) errors, H-FSGD matches or exceeds all baselines. In the private setting, (3, 0.1)-LDP and (2, 0.2)-LDP variants incur progressively larger MSE as privacy strength increases, yet all methods recover the true function shape well by n = 10000. Computationally, H-FSGD and PH-FSGD exhibit linear O(n) scaling, whereas the offline baseline requires cubic O(n³) time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two baselines: (1) L2-FSGD, a one-pass functional SGD using least-squares loss from Liu et al. [2023], and (2) Offline FSGD, a least-squares FSGD with full access to historical data from Dieuleveut and Bach [2016]. Under heavy-tailed t(3) noise, H-FSGD clearly wins over L2-FSGD in both median MSE and IQR, particularly at n = 2000, because the Huber loss caps the influence of large residuals while squared loss amplifies them. Under Gaussian noise, H-FSGD ties or slightly exceeds L2-FSGD, confirming that the robustness gain comes at no efficiency cost when errors are well-behaved. Against the offline baseline, H-FSGD and PH-FSGD achieve comparable MSE at n = 10000 (near-perfect fits) but with dramatically lower computational cost: O(n) versus O(n³), making them the only methods suitable for truly streaming, memory-constrained settings. In the private setting, PH-FSGD is the only method among all compared approaches that simultaneously provides per-iteration LDP guarantees, one-pass operation, and robustness to outliers—no existing baseline satisfies all three properties.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three established lines: (1) one-pass functional SGD in RKHS for nonparametric regression (Dieuleveut & Bach 2016; Liu et al. 2023), which uses squared loss and assumes full access to raw data; (2) differential privacy for functional data via Gaussian process mechanisms (Hall et al. 2013), which operates in a centralized, batch setting under CDP; and (3) robust regression via Huber loss. The key departure is integrating all three into a single per-iteration LDP framework: the Huber loss's uniformly bounded gradients naturally calibrate the LDP noise sensitivity without ad-hoc clipping, and privacy is enforced at each streaming step rather than on the final output. The authors explicitly note that no prior method achieves online, one-pass, LDP nonparametric regression with robustness guarantees.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first method to simultaneously provide per-iteration LDP guarantees, one-pass O(1) memory updates, and robustness to heavy-tailed noise in infinite-dimensional nonparametric regression, making it directly applicable to streaming healthcare or sensor data where a trusted server is unavailable. The theoretical contribution—showing minimax-optimal rates are preserved under LDP with only an additive variance inflation—is clean and practically informative for choosing step sizes and privacy budgets. Limitations include the reliance on i.i.d. streaming data, finite-grid discretization whose error is not explicitly bounded in the theory, and the assumption of finite-variance noise (though robustness to Cauchy noise is shown empirically). Open questions include extending to non-i.i.d. or adversarial streams, adaptive privacy budgets, and explicit finite-grid error analysis.
