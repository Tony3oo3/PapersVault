## Problem

Tree crown instance segmentation from drone imagery remains understudied due to limited annotated data at the individual tree level, and the Segment Anything Model (SAM) has not been applied to this task. Additionally, the Digital Surface Model (DSM) — a 3D elevation map produced at no extra cost from the same drone photogrammetry pipeline that generates RGB orthomosaics — has been underexplored as auxiliary input for tree crown segmentation. The paper asks whether SAM can be effectively adapted for tree crown instance segmentation and whether integrating DSM information improves performance across different forest types.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250290/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB drone orthomosaics (5 mm to 4 cm/pixel resolution) and a corresponding DSM elevation map (ranging from sub-meter to 1 m vertical resolution depending on the dataset) as input, tiled into 1024×1024 patches with 50% overlap. The DSM is normalized per-sample by its maximum value before being fed to the model. The output is per-instance segmentation masks with associated tree species class labels (9 classes for boreal plantations, 18 for temperate forest, 31 for tropical forest), evaluated via mAP, weighted mAP, and mIoU.

## Method

BalSAM builds on RSPrompter by adding a trainable 3-layer CNN DSM encoder (kernel sizes 2×2, 8×8, 1×1 with layer normalization and GeLU activations) that produces DSM embeddings. These embeddings are fused with the frozen SAM image encoder's output via element-wise summation, and the combined representation is fed to the SAM mask decoder as a dense prompt, analogous to how binary mask prompts are normally consumed. A Faster R-CNN-based prompter (kept from RSPrompter) learns to generate task-relevant box and point prompts for the mask decoder. Only the prompter and DSM encoder are trained end-to-end; SAM's 632M-parameter image encoder and mask decoder remain frozen, keeping the method parameter-efficient (126M trainable parameters) and trainable on a single 48 GB GPU. The paper also benchmarks SAM out-of-the-box (automatic mode and DSM local-maxima prompts), Mask R-CNN variants with DSM as a fourth input channel, Faster R-CNN+SAM, Mask2Former, and RSPrompter without DSM, across three forest datasets.

## Results

On the Quebec Plantations dataset, BalSAM achieves a multi-class wmAP of 64.84 and single-class mIoU of 83.24, outperforming Mask R-CNN+DSM (wmAP 60.32, mIoU 81.89) and RSPrompter (wmAP 62.37, mIoU 82.58). On the SBL temperate forest dataset, BalSAM reaches wmAP 29.12 and mIoU 66.02, while on the challenging BCI tropical forest dataset it achieves wmAP 10.42 and mIoU 61.60. SAM in its automatic mode performs poorly across all datasets (single-class mAP of only 8–12), and even prompting SAM with DSM local maxima yields only modest gains (mAP ~11–12).

## Baselines & Comparisons

BalSAM and RSPrompter (the two SAM-prompt-learning methods) consistently outperform all Mask R-CNN variants on multi-class mAP and wmAP across all three datasets; on Quebec Plantations, BalSAM achieves wmAP 64.84 vs. 60.32 for Mask R-CNN+DSM (+4.5 points) and 58.30 for Mask R-CNN without DSM. SAM out-of-the-box (automatic mode) is dramatically inferior, scoring only 8–12 single-class mAP versus 64–66 for the best trained methods, and even DSM-prompted SAM (mAP 11.17) remains far below any trained model. Mask2Former (Swin-L), despite having 215M parameters, does not outperform the 44M-parameter Mask R-CNN on multi-class mAP (44.33 vs. 48.96 with DSM), confirming prior observations of Mask2Former's limitations in forest remote sensing. Prompting SAM with boxes or masks from a trained Mask R-CNN (Mask R-CNN+SAM) actually degrades performance relative to the standalone Mask R-CNN (wmAP 51.33 vs. 60.32), as SAM introduces segmentation artifacts. BalSAM edges RSPrompter on multi-class wmAP for Plantations (64.84 vs. 62.37) and on mIoU for SBL (66.02 vs. 64.25) and BCI (61.60 vs. 60.72), while RSPrompter retains a slight wmAP advantage on SBL (29.44 vs. 29.12) and BCI (11.53 vs. 10.42), indicating the two methods are closely competitive with BalSAM's DSM integration providing the largest gains in structurally simpler plantation canopies.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends RSPrompter (Chen et al., 2024), which learns task-specific prompts for SAM in remote sensing, by introducing BalSAM that additionally fuses Digital Surface Model (DSM) embeddings into the prompt pipeline. It positions itself against the broader tree crown segmentation literature built on Mask R-CNN and U-Net architectures, and against prior work integrating canopy height maps (from LiDAR) into segmentation models, by showing that photogrammetry-derived DSMs—freely available from RGB drone flights—can serve the same structural role. The paper also systematically benchmarks SAM in its automatic and prompted modes against these CNN baselines, a comparison not previously conducted for tree crown instance segmentation across multiple forest types.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for forest monitoring and carbon accounting: the paper demonstrates that DSM data, already generated as a byproduct of drone photogrammetry, can improve segmentation and classification at no additional acquisition cost, and provides the first benchmark on three ecologically distinct datasets (boreal plantations, temperate forest, tropical forest). Key open questions remain: tropical forests with 100+ species and dense closed canopies are still poorly handled, long-tailed class distributions are not fully resolved, and the methods show higher training variance than Mask R-CNN. The finding that SAM out-of-the-box is suboptimal even with carefully designed prompts tempers enthusiasm for zero-shot foundation models in this domain and redirects attention toward parameter-efficient prompt learning and multi-modal fusion.
