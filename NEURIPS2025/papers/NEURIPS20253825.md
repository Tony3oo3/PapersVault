## Problem

Applying differential privacy to federated LoRA creates a fundamental dilemma: adding DP noise to both low-rank matrices A and B causes quadratic noise amplification in the product ΔW = BA, severely degrading model updates, while fixing one matrix (the common workaround) constrains learning to a predetermined subspace and impairs adaptability. No existing federated LoRA framework simultaneously achieves robust DP guarantees, full learnability of both adapters, and communication-efficient aggregation.

## Input / Output

Each client receives the current global LoRA matrices (A^{t-1}, B^{t-1}) and a shared random projection matrix Ω ∈ R^{n×(r+p)} from the server, then trains on its local dataset for m local steps. Rather than transmitting full matrices, clients send two compressed sketches per round: B_k(A_kΩ) in the first stage and (A_k^T)((B_k^T)Q) in the second, where Q is an orthonormal basis computed by the server. The server outputs updated global LoRA matrices A^T and B^T via SVD reconstruction, which are combined with the frozen pre-trained weights W0 to form the fine-tuned model.

## Method

FedASK employs a two-stage sketching pipeline inspired by randomized SVD to aggregate local LoRA updates precisely while enabling DP. In stage 1, each client computes a random projection Y_k^proj = B_k^t(A_k^t Ω) using a shared Gaussian matrix Ω; the server aggregates these and performs QR decomposition to obtain an orthonormal basis Q capturing the global singular subspace. In stage 2, clients project onto Q to produce Ỹ_k^proj = (A_k^t)^T((B_k^t)^T Q), which the server aggregates and decomposes via SVD into U, Σ, V^T, setting B^t = QUΣ^{1/2} and A^t = Σ^{1/2}V^T. DP is enforced by applying DP-SGD (gradient clipping + Gaussian noise) only to the local B_k updates while A_k is held fixed during local training; the SVD reconstruction then redistributes the privatized information across both global matrices, avoiding the quadratic noise term that arises when both matrices are independently perturbed. The paper proves an end-to-end (ε,δ)-DP guarantee via Rényi DP composition with client and data subsampling, and an exact aggregation property (zero Frobenius norm error) when the over-sketching parameter p satisfies p ≥ d_B − r + 2.

## Results

On Llama-2-7B (MMLU, ε=1), FedASK achieves 45.80, outperforming the best baseline Scaffold (43.41) by 2.4 points and FedAvg (42.07) by 3.7 points. On Llama-2-13B (GSM8K, ε=1), FedASK reaches 22.7 versus 16.1 for Scaffold and 15.5 for FedAvg, a ~46% relative improvement over the strongest baseline. Under the strongest privacy budget (ε=1), FedASK maintains near-non-private performance on MMLU (45.80 vs. 46.15 non-private) while baselines degrade by 2–4 points. On the VLM DPO task (BeaverTails-V helpfulness, ε=6), FedASK scores 56.36 vs. 54.66 (FFA-LoRA) and 51.53 (FedAvg).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedASK consistently outperforms all five baselines across every task, privacy budget, and data distribution tested. Under strong DP (ε=1) on MMLU (7B), FedASK leads by 2.4 points over Scaffold (45.80 vs. 43.41) and by 3.8 points over FedAvg (45.80 vs. 42.07). The gap widens substantially on mathematical reasoning: on GSM8K (13B, ε=1), FedASK achieves 22.7 versus 16.1 (Scaffold), 15.5 (FedAvg), 14.2 (FFA-LoRA), 12.2 (FedSA-LoRA), and 15.2 (FedProx) — a 41–86% relative margin. FFA-LoRA, which fixes matrix A to avoid quadratic noise, is the closest LoRA-specific competitor but still trails FedASK by 2–7 points under DP, confirming that the fixed-matrix constraint limits learnability. On the VLM DPO task (ε=6), FedASK achieves the highest helpfulness (56.36) and harmlessness (65.36) on BeaverTails-V, the lowest attack success rate (43.40) on MM-SafetyBench, and leading SIUO scores, outperforming FFA-LoRA by 1.7 points on helpfulness and 2.0 points on harmlessness. Notably, FedASK's advantage is most pronounced under the strictest privacy budgets, where baselines suffer the largest degradation while FedASK's two-stage SVD reconstruction preserves signal quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on LoRA (Hu et al., ICLR 2022) for parameter-efficient fine-tuning and on randomized SVD (Halko et al., SIAM Review 2011) for the two-stage sketching pipeline that enables exact federated aggregation. It departs from prior private federated LoRA methods—FFA-LoRA (Sun et al., 2024) which freezes matrix A to avoid quadratic DP noise, and FedSA-LoRA (Guo et al., 2025) which transmits only one matrix—by being the first to update both low-rank adapters under differential privacy. The key conceptual departure is that DP noise is applied only to B locally, while the server-side SVD reconstruction redistributes the privatized information across both global A and B, thereby avoiding the quadratic noise term without sacrificing representational capacity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FedASK resolves a fundamental trade-off in private federated LLM fine-tuning: prior methods must choose between noise amplification (updating both matrices) and limited learnability (fixing one), and this paper shows both can be avoided simultaneously with clean theoretical guarantees. The practical impact is significant for deployments where clients cannot share raw data or even full adapter matrices, with up to 46% improvement over the best baseline on GSM8K under ε=1. Open questions include whether alternating local updates of both A and B under DP could further improve performance, whether the approach extends to non-LLM architectures (vision transformers, diffusion models), and how the over-sketching condition (p ≥ d_B − r + 2) behaves in practice for very high-rank aggregated updates.
