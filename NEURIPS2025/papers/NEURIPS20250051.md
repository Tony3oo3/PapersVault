## Problem

Existing autoregressive models for stochastic human motion prediction rely on vector-quantized tokenization, which introduces quantization artifacts, codebook collapse, and degraded motion fidelity. Additionally, prior frequency-domain approaches (e.g., DCT) capture only low-frequency trends while omitting high-frequency motion details critical for rapid transitions. WaveAR is the first framework to apply a fully continuous, quantization-free autoregressive generation paradigm to human motion prediction, addressing both limitations simultaneously.

## Input / Output

The model takes a short history of 3D joint positions (H frames: 25 frames at 50 Hz on Human3.6M with 16 joints, or 15 frames at 60 Hz on HumanEva-I with 15 joints) and generates N diverse future pose sequences of F frames (100 or 60 frames respectively). The ST-VAE temporally downsamples the raw sequence by a factor of 2 before autoregressive prediction, so the latent token stream is half the original length. At inference, a cosine-scheduled progressive unmasking strategy fills in future tokens over K autoregressive iterations, each refined by a short DDIM denoising loop.

## Method

WaveAR is a two-stage framework. Stage 1 uses a lightweight Spatio-Temporal VAE (1D convolutions + ResNet1D blocks with strided convolutions) to compress raw 3D-joint sequences into continuous latent tokens via temporal downsampling, trained with L1 reconstruction plus KL regularization. Stage 2 performs masked autoregressive diffusion in this continuous latent space: a 2D Haar discrete wavelet transform is applied to the raw history along temporal and channel axes, yielding four spectral subbands (LL, LH, HL, HH) that are concatenated and linearly projected into the latent embedding space. A fusion Transformer with alternating cross-attention (wavelet features as keys/values, masked latents as queries) and self-attention layers in the first N_local blocks, followed by purely self-attention blocks, adaptively merges multi-scale frequency cues with temporal context. A compact 3-layer MLP diffusion head then predicts per-token noise residuals under a DDPM denoising loss, enabling the model to capture chained token dependencies that a plain autoregressive head cannot. This design avoids vector quantization entirely while injecting localized high-frequency motion details that DCT-based methods miss.

## Results

WaveAR achieves the best ADE (0.199 on HumanEva-I, 0.347 on Human3.6M) and FDE (0.201 on HumanEva-I, 0.452 on Human3.6M) among all 11 baselines, outperforming the closest competitor CoMusion (ADE 0.350, FDE 0.458) and HumanMAC (ADE 0.369, FDE 0.480) on Human3.6M. However, WaveAR reports lower diversity (APD 4.458 on Human3.6M vs. 15.310 for DivSamp) and slightly higher MMADE/MMFDE than CoMusion (0.513/0.535 vs. 0.494/0.506), reflecting a deliberate accuracy-over-diversity trade-off. Inference is substantially faster: WaveAR (small, 52M params) runs in 0.43 s versus 1.25 s for HumanMAC (28M params).

## Baselines & Comparisons

WaveAR outperforms all 11 baselines on both ADE and FDE across HumanEva-I and Human3.6M. On Human3.6M, it beats the strongest diffusion-based competitors CoMusion (ADE 0.350→0.347, FDE 0.458→0.452) and HumanMAC (ADE 0.369→0.347, FDE 0.480→0.452) by 0.003–0.022 in ADE. On HumanEva-I, the margin over the next-best method HumanMAC is larger: ADE 0.209→0.199 and FDE 0.223→0.201. The trade-off is diversity: WaveAR's APD (4.458 on H3.6M) is far below high-diversity VAE/GAN baselines like DivSamp (15.310) or GSPS (14.757), and its MMADE/MMFDE (0.513/0.535) are slightly worse than CoMusion (0.494/0.506) and STARS (0.442/0.471). The authors attribute this to the model's focus on the dominant motion mode rather than spreading probability mass across many plausible futures. In terms of efficiency, WaveAR (small) completes inference in 0.43 s versus 1.25 s for HumanMAC, a ~3× speedup, while using a comparable parameter count (52M vs. 28M).

## Relation to Prior Work

WaveAR extends the continuous autoregressive paradigm introduced by MAR [29] (which demonstrated VQ-free image generation) to the human motion prediction domain, where prior AR-based approaches (e.g., MARDM [38], MARRS [49]) still relied on discrete VQ-VAE tokenization. It departs from DCT-based frequency representations used in CoMusion [43] and HumanMAC [6] by substituting a 2D discrete wavelet transform that preserves both low- and high-frequency motion details, building on the wavelet-manifold idea of MotionWavelet [12] but repurposing it as a conditioning signal for a masked diffusion head rather than a manifold learning objective. The ST-VAE encoder follows standard variational autoencoder practice, while the alternating cross-attention/self-attention fusion module is a novel architectural choice for integrating spectral cues into the latent AR stream.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By being the first to apply a fully continuous (quantization-free) autoregressive framework to stochastic HMP, WaveAR removes a known source of fidelity loss and training instability (codebook collapse) that has plagued token-based motion models. The 6× speedup over HumanMAC (0.21 s vs 1.25 s for the tiny variant) makes AR-based motion prediction more viable for near-real-time applications such as robotics and VR. However, the authors acknowledge that the model produces conservative, low-diversity predictions (lower APD than most competitors), and the framework is limited to 17–32 joint keypoints, leaving open whether the approach generalizes to higher-fidelity body representations (e.g., SMPL meshes) or can be adapted to recover the diversity-accuracy tradeoff without sacrificing precision.
