## Problem

For structured stochastic bandits, the Graves-Lai optimization problem (P_GL) characterizes the information-theoretic regret lower bound and enables asymptotically optimal algorithms, but solving it for multimodal bandits (m > 1 modes on a tree graph) was previously intractable due to the highly non-convex, disconnected constraint set. Moreover, the natural local-search strategies used in prior multimodal bandit work are provably suboptimal, with an unbounded performance gap relative to the true optimum. This paper provides the first computationally tractable algorithm to solve P_GL for multimodal bandits and formally establishes the suboptimality of local search.

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes as input a known tree graph G with K arms, the number of modes m, empirical mean estimates μ̂(t) updated online, and reward distribution parameters (e.g., Gaussian variance). It outputs the optimal sampling rate vector η* (specifying how many times each arm should be sampled as a function of ln T), the most confusing parameter λ* that minimizes the divergence constraint, and the Graves-Lai constant C(m, μ). The computation operates on a discretized grid D(n, μ) of n points over [μ_★, μ*], and the resulting η* is plugged into the OSSB algorithm, which at each round selects the arm with the smallest ratio N_k(t)/(η*_k ln t) for exploration.

## Method

The algorithm proceeds in three stages. First, a discretization step (Proposition 7) rounds the continuous parameter λ to a uniform grid D(n, μ) with n points, preserving the mode structure of λ while incurring an approximation error of O(1/n). Second, a tree-structured dynamic program (Proposition 8) solves the discretized subproblems P_GL(k, k') for each pair (k, k'), exploiting the key structural result (Proposition 6) that all modes of the optimal λ* must lie in M(μ) ∪ {k} \ {k'}, which reduces the search space and yields an O(nK) per-subproblem computation. Third, a projected subgradient descent with a hinge-style penalty (Proposition 9) solves the outer P_GL problem, where each gradient step requires evaluating the inner constraint via the dynamic program. The overall time complexity is O(K²mnt) (or O(Knt) with a more involved DP), and the optimization error scales as 1/n + 1/√t, allowing the learner to trade off accuracy against computation via the budget nt = a.

## Results

The main theoretical result (Theorem 2) establishes that the proposed algorithm solves the Graves-Lai problem in O(K²mnt) time with an approximation error proportional to 1/n + 1/√t, where n is the discretization resolution and t is the number of subgradient iterations. In numerical experiments on a 7-arm binary tree with 2-modal Gaussian rewards (T=10,000, 500 trials), Multimodal OSSB consistently achieves lower cumulative regret than Classical OSSB in both a peaked instance (σ=0.5) and a flat instance (σ=4). A runtime experiment on line graphs with K up to 70 arms confirms the predicted approximately quadratic scaling in K, matching the O(K²mnt) complexity bound.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares Multimodal OSSB (using the proposed P_GL solver) against Classical OSSB, which uses the unstructured Lai-Robbins sampling rates η_k = 1/d_k(μ_k, μ*) as a baseline. Multimodal OSSB outperforms Classical OSSB in both the peaked (σ=0.5) and flat (σ=4) reward instances, with the gap being more pronounced in the flat case where the multimodal structure is harder to exploit. The paper further demonstrates that local search strategies (which only explore arms in the neighborhood of modes) are provably suboptimal: Theorem 3 shows the performance ratio C_loc/C(m,μ) is unbounded, meaning local search can be arbitrarily worse than the optimal strategy. This result also implies that the IMED-MB algorithm from Saber & Maillard [18], which relies on local search, cannot be asymptotically optimal, contradicting the claim in their Theorem 2.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the unimodal bandit framework of Combes & Proutiere (2014) to the multimodal setting (m > 1 modes on a tree graph), building on the Graves-Lai (1997) information-theoretic lower bound and the OSSB algorithm of Combes et al. (2017) for structured bandits. It departs from prior multimodal bandit work (Saber 2022; Saber & Maillard 2024), which relied on local search strategies that only explore neighbors of modes, by showing these strategies are fundamentally suboptimal and providing the first computationally tractable algorithm to solve the Graves-Lai optimization problem via a combination of discretization, dynamic programming, and projected subgradient descent.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that local search strategies have an unbounded performance gap relative to optimal strategies is a significant correction to the field, as it contradicts the asymptotic optimality claim in Saber & Maillard (2024). This means that for multimodal bandits, one cannot avoid solving the full Graves-Lai problem to achieve optimal regret, and the proposed algorithm is the first to make this feasible. Open questions include characterizing the minimal computational complexity of solving P_GL as a function of K, m, and graph structure, and whether the approach extends beyond tree graphs to more general structures.
