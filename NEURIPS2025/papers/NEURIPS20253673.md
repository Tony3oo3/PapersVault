## Problem

Action sequences are a key ingredient in recent behavior cloning successes for robotics, but it was unclear whether they could similarly improve reinforcement learning. Standard actor-critic algorithms (SAC, TD3) trained with action sequences suffer from severe value overestimation due to the enlarged action space, causing complete failure on humanoid control tasks. The paper asks whether a critic-only formulation can safely exploit action sequences for value learning without this instability.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253673/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images at 84×84 resolution from 3–4 cameras (head, wrist, shoulder depending on benchmark) plus low-dimensional proprioceptive states (joint positions, velocities, gripper state). It outputs a sequence of K continuous actions (K=16 for BiGym, K=4 for RLBench/HumanoidBench) that are executed via temporal ensemble—a weighted exponential average of overlapping action-sequence predictions across consecutive timesteps. The critic network internally produces Q-values for every (level, sequence-step, bin) combination, i.e., B values per step per level, rather than a single scalar Q-value.

## Method

CQN-AS extends the Coarse-to-fine Q-Network (CQN) by replacing single-step Q-values with Q-values over an entire action sequence a_{t:t+K}. At each of L coarse-to-fine levels (B bins per level, progressively zooming into the action space), the critic computes Q-values for every sequence step k in parallel. The architecture extracts per-step features h_{t,k}^l = [h_t, a_{t+k-1}^{l-1}, e_k], encodes them with a shared MLP, aggregates them through a GRU, and projects to B Q-values per step via a shared linear layer. Because CQN-AS is critic-only (selecting the argmax Q-value at each level rather than training a separate actor), it avoids the value overestimation that plagues actor-critic methods with enlarged action spaces. Training uses N-step returns with delayed target networks, and in the demo-driven setup an auxiliary large-margin BC loss is added to the RL objective.

## Results

On BiGym (25 sparse-reward mobile bi-manual manipulation tasks), CQN-AS quickly matches ACT's success rate and then surpasses it through online learning, while CQN and DrQ-v2+ achieve 0% success on many challenging long-horizon tasks (e.g., Move Plate, Saucepan To Hob). On RLBench (20 tabletop manipulation tasks), CQN-AS performs similarly to CQN on most tasks but significantly outperforms both CQN and ACT on long-horizon tasks like Open Oven and Take Plate Off Colored Dish Rack. On HumanoidBench (8 densely-rewarded locomotion tasks from scratch), CQN-AS outperforms both CQN and SAC, with the gap widening on harder tasks (Walk, Run) where baselines fail to achieve high episode returns. CQN-AS uses 13% more memory and is ~33% slower than CQN due to the larger GRU-based architecture.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CQN (the backbone algorithm), CQN-AS consistently improves performance on BiGym and HumanoidBench, with the largest gains on long-horizon and difficult tasks where CQN struggles; on RLBench with clean synthetic demos, CQN-AS matches CQN on most tasks but still wins on a few challenging ones (Open Oven, Take Plate Off Colored Dish Rack). Against DrQ-v2+ (actor-critic with data augmentation), CQN-AS substantially outperforms on BiGym, where DrQ-v2+ achieves 0% success on many tasks while CQN-AS solves them; on RLBench, CQN-AS also leads, particularly on long-horizon manipulation. Against ACT (behavior cloning with action sequences), CQN-AS initially matches ACT's performance (since both leverage action sequences) but then surpasses it through online RL on BiGym, demonstrating that the RL objective enables learning beyond what imitation alone provides. Against SAC on HumanoidBench, CQN-AS outperforms on most of the 8 locomotion tasks, with the advantage growing on harder tasks like Walk and Run where SAC fails to reach high returns. The paper also shows that actor-critic algorithms (SAC, TD3) with action sequences suffer from severe value overestimation, motivating the critic-only design of CQN-AS.

## Relation to Prior Work

CQN-AS extends Coarse-to-fine Q-Network (CQN; Seo et al., 2024), a critic-only RL algorithm that solves continuous control by progressively discretizing the action space, by replacing single-step actions with action sequences in the critic. The core idea is borrowed from behavior cloning with action chunking (ACT, Diffusion Policy), where predicting sequences of actions helps approximate noisy expert distributions; here it is repurposed for value learning rather than policy imitation. The paper explicitly departs from standard actor-critic algorithms (SAC, TD3) by showing they fail with action sequences due to value overestimation, motivating the critic-only design. It also draws a conceptual parallel to multi-token prediction in LLMs, where predicting multiple future tokens in parallel improves learning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that action sequences can meaningfully improve value-based RL on challenging sparse-reward robotic tasks, solving BiGym tasks where prior model-free RL baselines achieve 0% success. The key open question is whether this approach transfers to real-world robots and torque-based control, where the paper reports clear failure. Limitations include the lack of real-world evaluation, sensitivity to action representation (position vs. torque), and the computational overhead (~33% slower than CQN). Future directions include combining with model-based RL, offline RL bootstrapping, and fine-tuning vision-language-action models that already use action sequences.
