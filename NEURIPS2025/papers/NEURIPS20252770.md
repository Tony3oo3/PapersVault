## Problem

Simulation-based inference (SBI) methods require generating large numbers of expensive simulations and training/tuning a dedicated inference network (e.g., a normalizing flow) for each new simulator, creating a significant barrier for costly simulators and non-expert users. The paper asks whether pre-trained tabular foundation models can serve as drop-in, training-free inference engines that dramatically reduce the simulation budget while maintaining competitive posterior accuracy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252770/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes an observation x_o and a fixed dataset of (θ, x) simulation pairs drawn from the prior and simulator; no training data is ever fit to. Because TabPFN handles only univariate targets, the d_θ-dimensional posterior is decomposed autoregressively into d_θ sequential univariate conditional predictions, each requiring one forward pass with the full simulation context. TabPFN's soft limits of ~10⁴ context samples and ≤500 feature dimensions are addressed by nearest-neighbor filtering (selecting the N_filter closest simulations to x_o in standardized space) and optional pre-trained embedding networks for high-dimensional observations.

## Method

NPE-PFN repurposes TabPFN v2—a transformer-based prior-data fitted network pre-trained on synthetic structural causal models—as a zero-shot autoregressive conditional density estimator for the posterior p(θ|x_o) ≈ ∏_j q_ψ(θ^j | θ^{<j}, x_o, D^{<j}), requiring no gradient updates or hyperparameter tuning. For simulation budgets exceeding TabPFN's context limit, an ABC-style nearest-neighbor filter selects the most relevant simulations; the paper proves this reweighting leaves the posterior unbiased because the filter depends only on x, not θ. For sequential inference (TSNPE-PFN), a 'ratio-trick' converts the expensive autoregressive density evaluation into a single TabPFN classifier forward pass by contrasting posterior samples (label y=1) with uniform samples (label y=0), enabling fast rejection sampling from truncated priors across adaptive rounds. The approach inherits TabPFN's inductive bias toward sparse conditional dependencies from its SCM pre-training, which empirically benefits tasks with graphical structure.

## Results

On the SBI benchmark (7 tasks, C2ST metric where 0.5 is perfect), NPE-PFN substantially outperforms both NPE and NLE at 10² simulations on all but one task, and remains competitive at 10³–10⁴ simulations. On the Hodgkin-Huxley model, TSNPE-PFN achieves the same posterior predictive quality as TSNPE with an order of magnitude fewer simulations (10³ vs. 10⁴). On the 31-dimensional pyloric network, TSNPE-PFN reaches 96.83% valid simulations and an energy score of 0.0899 with only 5×10⁴ simulations, surpassing S-UNLE (1.5×10⁵ simulations) and the original NPE approach (18 million simulations).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against flow-based NPE [4], NPE-PFN wins decisively at 10² simulations (outperforming on all but one of seven SBI benchmark tasks) and remains competitive through 10⁴; the filtered variant matches or surpasses NPE at 10⁵. Against NLE [11], NPE-PFN wins at 10² but loses on the SLCP task (where NLE's likelihood-targeting is advantageous); NPE-PFN substantially outperforms NLE on SIR and Lotka-Volterra across all budgets. Against TSNPE [37] on the Hodgkin-Huxley model, TSNPE-PFN achieves equivalent predictive quality with ~10× fewer simulations and produces tighter posterior marginals around true parameters. Against NRE [14] and NPE Ensemble [16] (Appendix D.2), NPE-PFN matches or exceeds calibration (EoD) while often outperforming in C2ST accuracy. On the pyloric network, TSNPE-PFN with 5×10⁴ simulations surpasses S-UNLE [68] (1.5×10⁵ simulations) in both energy score and valid-simulation percentage, and exceeds the original 18-million-simulation NPE [60] by a factor of 360 in simulation budget.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes TabPFN, a transformer-based prior-data fitted network originally trained for in-context tabular classification and regression [38], as a training-free conditional density estimator for simulation-based inference. It builds directly on the Neural Posterior Estimation (NPE) framework [4,5] and its sequential variant TSNPE [37], but eliminates the core step of training an inference network (e.g., a normalizing flow) on simulator outputs. The autoregressive decomposition of the multivariate posterior into univariate conditional predictions, the ABC-inspired filtering to extend effective context size, and the density-ratio trick for fast rejection sampling [52] are the key technical bridges connecting the tabular foundation model literature to the SBI literature. Conceptually, the paper positions SBI as an instance of in-context learning rather than supervised density estimation, drawing on the broader ICL and meta-learning literature [43,44,45].

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

NPE-PFN removes the entire training-and-tuning pipeline from SBI, making Bayesian inference from expensive simulators accessible to non-experts and reducing simulation budgets by orders of magnitude in the small-data regime. The practical impact is most acute for scientific applications with costly simulators (e.g., the pyloric network requiring 5×10⁴ vs. 1.5×10⁵–18×10⁶ simulations in prior work). Key limitations remain: TabPFN's context size (~10⁴) and feature dimension (≤500) cap the method's applicability, inference speed scales with simulation count and parameter dimensionality, and the advantage over well-tuned NPE diminishes at very large budgets. Open questions include whether the SCM-based inductive bias of TabPFN can be formalized, how to scale beyond the context limit without filtering, and whether student-teacher distillation can transfer NPE-PFN's simulation efficiency to faster flow-based samplers.
