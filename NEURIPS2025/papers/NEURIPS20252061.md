## Problem

Modern multilingual ASR systems require at least 1k hours of transcribed speech per language for acceptable performance, yet existing public datasets (Common Voice, VoxPopuli, FLEURS) provide this volume for only a handful of languages, leaving the vast majority severely under-resourced. Parliamentary proceedings offer a natural source of long-form, transcribed speech in many European languages, but building usable datasets from them is complex due to fragmented sources, non-verbatim transcripts, and long unsegmented recordings.

## Input / Output

The pipeline ingests 1–10 hour parliamentary audio recordings, non-verbatim transcripts in heterogeneous formats (PDF, DOCX, HTML, SRT, TXT), and a metadata CSV mapping sessions to source URLs. It outputs 3–20 second audio segments at 16 kHz paired with matched transcript spans, packaged as CER-filtered subsets (at 10%, 20%, and 30% thresholds) with per-segment alignment JSON containing timestamps, ASR text, human transcript text, and CER scores. A key design constraint is that transcripts need not be verbatim or pre-cleaned; the alignment algorithm tolerates mixed non-speech content, speaker annotations, and unuttered text.

## Method

The core technical contribution is a two-stage coarse-to-fine dynamic alignment algorithm that matches Whisper Turbo ASR transcriptions of VAD-segmented audio to non-verbatim parliamentary transcripts without manual pre-processing. Stage 1 (coarse search) slides a window of length equal to the ASR segment from the last matched transcript position, passing the first candidate below 30% CER (or the top-3 lowest-CER candidates) to Stage 2. Stage 2 (refined search) exhaustively varies the start position within ±15 words and window size within L±15 words around each candidate to minimize CER. A fallback mechanism restarts the search from the transcript beginning if sequential misalignment causes the window to skip past the true match. The full pipeline also includes a format-agnostic download dispatch architecture, optional LLM-based (Gemini Flash 2.0) PDF transcript cleaning, and CER-based quality filtering, producing 61k hours of aligned speech across 22 European languages with 19 exceeding 1k hours at CER < 20%.

## Results

Finetuning Whisper v3 Turbo on EUROSPEECH yields an average 41.8% relative WER reduction on the FLEURS test set across six low-resource European languages. The largest improvement is for Maltese (72.2% → 25.9% WER, a 64.1% relative reduction), while the smallest is for Icelandic (20.0% → 15.0%, 25.0% reduction). The average WER drops from 29.2% (baseline) to 15.1% (finetuned), demonstrating that even ~200 hours of EUROSPEECH data per language substantially improves ASR performance on out-of-domain evaluation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper uses the unfinetuned Whisper v3 Turbo model (pretrained on 680k hours across 98 languages) as the sole baseline, evaluated on the FLEURS test set. Finetuning on EUROSPEECH consistently outperforms the baseline across all six tested languages, with no language showing degradation. The relative WER improvement ranges from 25.0% (Icelandic, where the baseline was already relatively strong at 20.0% WER) to 64.1% (Maltese, where the baseline was very weak at 72.2% WER). The average 41.8% relative reduction is achieved using only ~200 hours of EUROSPEECH training data per language, a small fraction of the full 50.5k-hour CER < 20% subset. The paper does not compare against other finetuning datasets or alternative ASR models, so the comparison isolates the contribution of EUROSPEECH data alone to the performance gain.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the VAC pipeline [33], which uses a single dynamic CER-based sliding window for audio-text alignment, into a two-stage coarse-to-fine strategy that robustly handles non-verbatim parliamentary transcripts without manual pre-processing. It is positioned against existing multilingual parliamentary speech datasets—most notably VoxPopuli [31] (16 languages, 1.8k hours, all below the 1k-hour threshold) and Europarl-ASR [7]—by scaling to 22 national parliaments and 61k hours. The alignment stage leverages Whisper Turbo as the ASR backbone for generating intermediate transcriptions, following the bootstrapping approach seen in LibriHeavy [13] and GigaSpeech [3], while the overall pipeline architecture (VAD segmentation, format-agnostic parsing, CER-based filtering) follows standard dataset-construction practice.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

EUROSPEECH directly addresses the persistent imbalance in public multilingual speech resources by providing over 1k hours for 19 languages and over 500 hours for all 22, a threshold that no prior public parliamentary dataset has met. The demonstrated 41.8% average WER reduction on FLEURS (using only ~200 hours per language) validates the dataset's practical utility for low-resource European ASR. Key limitations include the formal, planned register of parliamentary speech (limiting generalization to conversational settings), the European-only geographic scope, and the alignment quality's dependence on upstream ASR model accuracy. Open questions include whether the pipeline generalizes to other domains (e.g., broadcast, conversational speech) and non-European parliaments, and whether the balanced per-language distribution translates to improved multilingual model performance beyond individual-language finetuning.
