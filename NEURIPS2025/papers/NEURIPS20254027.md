## Problem

Existing LLM-based optimization modeling methods decompose tasks into fixed sequential steps (variables, constraints, objectives), which fails on complex OR problems where over 70% of errors stem from incorrect variable definitions. The paper asks how to adaptively decompose a complex problem into simpler subproblems whose known modeling patterns can guide the LLM, rather than forcing a one-size-fits-all step sequence.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254027/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a natural language description of an OR problem and produces both a formal optimization model (decision variables, constraints, objective function) and executable solver code (e.g., Gurobi). Evaluation is by solving the generated model and checking whether the optimal objective value matches the ground truth. The modeling tree is pre-constructed from 400 problems in OR-Instruct 3K, and at inference time the LLM extracts 'statement thoughts' (atomic high-level requirements) from the input to drive tree search, then synthesizes 'modeling thoughts' (step-by-step variable/constraint/objective guidelines plus code templates) to condition the final generation.

## Method

OptiTree builds a hierarchical modeling tree where each node represents an OR problem category and stores a schema of statement thoughts (for subproblem identification) and modeling thoughts (for guiding model construction). Given a target problem, the method performs a top-down tree search: at each level it compares the target's statement thoughts against child nodes' statement thoughts via LLM similarity scoring, selecting the best-matching subproblem and descending until no further match exists. The modeling thoughts of the deepest matched node (the 'maximum subproblem') are then combined with the original problem description to synthesize global modeling thoughts that condition the LLM's generation. The tree is dynamically updated: when a problem's generated answer mismatches ground truth, a new node is inserted as a child of the maximum subproblem (or as a parent of an existing child if the new problem is its subproblem), preserving the subproblem order-preserving invariant. This transfers the search from the exponentially large variable/constraint space into a finite, structured subproblem space, reducing both search cost and hallucination.

## Results

OptiTree achieves state-of-the-art solving accuracy across seven optimization modeling benchmarks. With GPT-4o, it reaches 96.2% on NL4Opt, 95.6% on MAMO EasyLP, 81.0% on MAMO ComplexLP, 84.2% on ComplexOR, 48.0% on IndustryOR, 71.9% on OptiBench, and 45.8% on OptMATH. With DeepSeek-V3, it further improves to 98.3%, 96.9%, 81.5%, 84.2%, 54.0%, 74.7%, and 52.4% respectively. The most substantial gains appear on the challenging benchmarks: +24.2 points over the best prompt-based baseline (MCTS) on MAMO ComplexLP and +10.8 points on OptiBench.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OptiTree outperforms all prompt-based baselines (Standard, CoT, CoE, OptiMUS, MCTS) on every benchmark with both GPT-4o and DeepSeek-V3, with the largest margins on MAMO ComplexLP (+24.2 over MCTS) and ComplexOR (+15.2 over MCTS). It also surpasses the reasoning LLMs DeepSeek-R1 and OpenAI-o1 across all seven benchmarks, e.g., 81.0 vs. 57.3 on MAMO ComplexLP and 48.0 vs. 40.0 on IndustryOR (GPT-4o setting). Against fine-tuned models, OptiTree (GPT-4o) beats LLMOPT on 6 of 7 benchmarks but trails it on MAMO EasyLP (95.6 vs. 97.0); it also edges out OptMATH on NL4Opt (96.2 vs. 95.9) and dominates on all other shared benchmarks. The closest competitor overall is MCTS, which is the strongest prompt-based baseline, yet OptiTree still leads by 5–24 points depending on the dataset. On the hardest benchmark (IndustryOR), OptiTree with DeepSeek-V3 achieves 54.0%, exceeding the next-best result (MCTS at 46.0%) by 8 points.

## Relation to Prior Work

OptiTree departs from the dominant fixed-step decomposition paradigm in LLM-based optimization modeling (CoE, OptiMUS, MCTS), which sequentially generates variables, constraints, and objectives regardless of problem complexity. Instead, it repurposes the tree-search idea from Tree-of-Thoughts and MCTS but redirects the search into a structured subproblem space organized by OR taxonomy, rather than an unstructured variable/constraint space. The hierarchical 'modeling thoughts' stored at tree nodes function as a structured, domain-specific analogue of retrieval-augmented generation, while the statement-thoughts mechanism for subproblem identification draws on the semantic matching ideas in RAG. The method is positioned as a prompt-based approach that leverages pre-trained LLM reasoning, explicitly contrasting with fine-tuned modeling agents (ORLM, LLMOPT, OptMATH).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

OptiTree demonstrates that adaptive subproblem decomposition via a hierarchical tree can substantially reduce the modeling difficulty for complex OR problems, achieving over 10% accuracy gains on challenging benchmarks while also being more efficient than MCTS-based baselines. The data efficiency (only 400 problems to build a generalizable tree) and model-agnosticism (works across GPT-4o, DeepSeek-V3, and even 7B/8B models) suggest the approach could be widely adopted as a plug-in for existing LLM modeling pipelines. Key limitations remain: the method still struggles with constraint formulation requiring deep, context-specific logical reasoning (e.g., shift-coverage logic in rostering), and its performance is ultimately bounded by the base LLM's reasoning ability. Open questions include how the tree scales to truly novel problem domains outside standard OR taxonomies and whether the subproblem identification accuracy degrades for highly atypical formulations.
