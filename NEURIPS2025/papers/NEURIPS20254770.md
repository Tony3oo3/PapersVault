## Problem

Existing knowledge editing methods for LLMs—whether external-parameter or internal-parameter—suffer from cumulative error accumulation during lifelong sequential editing, where editing accuracy and general capabilities degrade progressively over hundreds to thousands of edits. Internal methods that follow a locate-then-edit paradigm modify entire layers or parameter blocks, inadvertently disrupting neurons unrelated to the target knowledge and causing catastrophic forgetting. The paper asks how to perform precise, neuron-level knowledge editing that confines parameter modifications to only the most relevant neurons, thereby enabling stable lifelong editing without performance collapse.

## Input / Output

The method consumes sequential editing requests as fact triples (subject, relation, original object → target object) along with a batch of attribution prompts used to compute neuron importance scores. It operates on the FFN layers (specifically layers 4–8) of a frozen LLM, producing a binary sparse mask per layer that restricts which neurons receive parameter updates. The output is the edited LLM weight matrix, where only the masked neuron columns in the down-projection weights are modified, leaving all other parameters untouched. The dynamic mask is recomputed at each editing step based on the current attribution score distribution, so the active neuron subset adapts to the specific knowledge being edited.

## Method

NMKE performs neuron-level attribution in the FFN by perturbing each neuron's contribution and measuring the resulting log-probability change for the target token, yielding a per-neuron importance matrix across a batch of prompts. From this matrix, it identifies two functional neuron types: knowledge-general neurons (consistently positive attribution across prompts, scored by counting positive signs) and knowledge-specific neurons (sharply activated on specific prompts, scored by maximum attribution). An entropy-guided dynamic masking strategy then computes the selection ratio for each type from the normalized entropy of the respective score distributions, adapting the fraction of selected neurons to the activation pattern of the current prompt batch. The resulting binary sparse mask is applied to the parameter update matrix within AlphaEdit's null-space-constrained optimization pipeline, restricting weight modifications to only the selected neuron subset. This fine-grained intervention minimizes collateral disruption to unrelated knowledge representations, enabling stable editing over thousands of sequential steps while preserving general capabilities.

## Results

On LLaMA3-8B-Instruct with 2000 sequential edits, NMKE achieves Rel=0.94, Gen=0.85, Loc=0.71 on ZsRE and Rel=0.98, Gen=0.67, Loc=0.38 on CounterFact, substantially outperforming the strongest baseline AlphaEdit (Rel=0.32, Gen=0.28, Loc=0.06 on ZsRE; Rel=0.22, Gen=0.13, Loc=0.04 on CounterFact). NMKE also preserves general capabilities: after 2000 edits, MMLU remains at 0.59 and GSM8K/CommonsenseQA retain meaningful scores, whereas AlphaEdit drops GSM8K and HumanEval to 0. At T=5000, NMKE still achieves Rel=0.74, Gen=0.59 on ZsRE while AlphaEdit collapses to Rel=0.02, Gen=0.02.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NMKE dominates all seven baselines across both ZsRE and CounterFact at every editing step beyond T=100. Against the strongest internal-parameter baseline AlphaEdit, NMKE maintains Rel≈0.94–0.98 at T=2000 while AlphaEdit collapses to Rel=0.32 (ZsRE) and Rel=0.22 (CounterFact), a gap of over 0.6 in edit success. External-parameter method WISE avoids catastrophic collapse but still degrades from Rel=0.83 to 0.37 on ZsRE over 2000 edits, and does not report locality. Early methods (ROME, MEMIT, KN, PMET) all drop to near-zero Rel and Gen by T=500, confirming that layer-level or single-layer editing cannot sustain sequential updates. On generalization benchmarks, NMKE preserves MMLU at 0.59 and retains non-trivial GSM8K/CommonsenseQA scores after 2000 edits, whereas AlphaEdit loses all mathematical and coding ability (GSM8K=0, HumanEval=0) by T=1500. NMKE's per-edit runtime (22–30 s depending on attribution strategy) is comparable to AlphaEdit (22 s), so the accuracy gains come without significant computational overhead.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the locate-then-edit paradigm of internal parameter editing (ROME, MEMIT, AlphaEdit) by shifting the granularity from layer-level to neuron-level modifications within FFN layers. It builds directly on AlphaEdit's null-space projection optimization pipeline but replaces its uniform layer-wide updates with an entropy-guided dynamic sparse mask that selectively targets only neurons relevant to the target knowledge. The conceptual departure is motivated by an empirical discovery that FFN neurons decompose into knowledge-general (stable across prompts) and knowledge-specific (selectively activated) types with very different functional fragility, a distinction not explicitly modeled in prior editing methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Lifelong knowledge editing is a critical practical need for deploying LLMs in dynamic environments, yet all existing methods suffer catastrophic degradation beyond ~500 sequential edits. NMKE's ability to maintain >0.94 edit success and >0.71 locality at 2000 edits (vs. AlphaEdit's 0.32/0.06) represents a meaningful step toward viable continuous knowledge updates. Open questions remain: the binary general/specific neuron classification is acknowledged as a simplification of a continuum, the method only operates on FFN layers 4–8, and performance still degrades at 5000+ edits. Future work on cross-layer attribution and dependency-aware editing (e.g., ensuring CEO updates remain consistent with CEO-count facts) is needed for truly robust lifelong editing.
