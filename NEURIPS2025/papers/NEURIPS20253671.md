## Problem

Vision-Language Models are increasingly deployed as backbones for robot manipulation policies, yet their understanding of the low-level physical prerequisites—object properties, action affordances, and environmental constraints—remains largely unverified. Existing benchmarks evaluate end-to-end task success or broad physics knowledge but do not systematically dissect whether VLMs can reason about the specific preconditions that make a manipulation action executable in the first place.

## Input / Output

Each evaluation instance pairs an RGB image (sourced from OpenImages, RoboCasa multi-angle captures, Unitree G1 humanoid robot views, or MuJoCo-simulated scenes rendered from front/agent/side viewpoints) with a structured text prompt. Property queries are multiple-choice over 12 predefined attributes (e.g., weight, density, sealing) with 3–5 options; affordance queries require the model to select all applicable actions from a candidate list for a given object class; constraint queries ask for a feasibility judgment (yes/no) plus identification of the limiting physical pre-condition. The full dataset comprises over 30,000 image-text annotations spanning 115 object classes and 18 primary categories.

## Method

PAC Bench is a diagnostic benchmark that decomposes physical reasoning for manipulation into three queryable dimensions—Properties, Affordances, and Constraints—and evaluates pre-trained VLMs on each independently. The dataset is constructed from four image sources (OpenImages, Unitree G1 real-world captures, RoboCasa 24-angle object renders, and MuJoCo simulations) with dual-annotator consensus labeling for properties (27,674 annotations across 12 types) and top-3 affordance ranking per object class. Constraint scenarios are procedurally generated in MuJoCo across four domains (impossible placement, occlusion/support, stability, reachability) with 10 randomized instantiations each rendered from three camera viewpoints (120 total), complemented by 2,727 real-world humanoid robot question-answer pairs. The evaluation protocol feeds each VLM the image and a targeted prompt, then scores responses against ground-truth annotations using accuracy for properties, at-least-one and all-correct metrics for affordances, and feasibility-correctness for constraints, enabling fine-grained diagnosis of where physical understanding breaks down.

## Results

Across 16 VLMs evaluated on PAC Bench, property understanding is the strongest dimension, with Llama 4 Maverick achieving the highest average accuracy of 49.4% across 12 property types (OpenImages + Humanoid), followed by Gemini 2.0 Flash 001 (44.1%) and GPT-4.1 (42.4%). Affordance recognition (at least one correct) peaks at 40.9% for Llama 4 Scout, while comprehensive affordance identification (all correct) drops to near-zero for nearly all models. Constraint understanding is the weakest dimension overall: Gemini 2.5 Pro P leads at 25.8% average, but most models score below 10%, with Claude 3.5 Sonnet (0.4%), Llama 3.2 90B (0.2%), and Llama 4 Scout (1.5%) registering near-zero performance across both simulated and real-world scenarios.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PAC Bench evaluates 16 VLMs as baselines across three PAC dimensions. On constraint understanding (the most challenging dimension), Gemini 2.5 Pro P leads at 25.8% average, primarily driven by strong Occlusion scores (up to 90% on front-view), while GPT-4.1 achieves 13.6% with notable Occlusion performance (56.7% avg) but zero on all other domains. Llama 3.2 11B Vision I (17.5%) is the only other model above 15%, showing moderate capability across all four simulated domains. A striking sim-to-real gap emerges: GPT-4.1 Mini scores near-zero in simulation (0% on all four domains) yet achieves 22.6% on real-world Humanoid constraints, while Gemini 2.5 Pro P, the simulation leader, drops to 9.4% on Humanoid. On properties, Llama 4 Maverick (49.4% avg) outperforms GPT-4.1 (42.4%) and Gemini 2.0 Flash 001 (44.1%), with Qwen 3 failing entirely on Humanoid views (0.0%). On affordances, Llama 4 Scout (40.9%) and Llama 4 Maverick (35.2%) lead, but all models drop to near-zero when required to identify the full set of ground-truth affordances rather than just one.

## Relation to Prior Work

PAC Bench carves new ground by unifying three previously fragmented evaluation threads—object property recognition (PhysBench, PHYBench), affordance identification (ManipVQA, UniAff, ActAffordance), and physical constraint reasoning (EQA-phys, Physion)—into a single, executability-framed benchmark. Prior work either evaluates these components in isolation, tests them through downstream end-to-end task success (The Colosseum), or probes general physics knowledge without tying it to manipulation preconditions. The paper's key conceptual departure is treating P, A, and C as interconnected, queryable preconditions that must be satisfied before a manipulation action is executable, rather than as independent knowledge categories or as implicit byproducts of policy performance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The near-total failure of state-of-the-art VLMs on constraint reasoning (most models scoring below 10% average, with 'Impossible Placement' almost universally at zero) challenges the implicit assumption that web-scale pre-training provides sufficient physical grounding for VLA backbones. This has direct practical implications: if the foundation model cannot recognize that a stack is unstable or an object is unreachable, no amount of fine-tuning on trajectory data will fix the underlying reasoning gap. Open questions include whether targeted PAC-specific training can close these gaps, how to bridge the pronounced sim-to-real constraint gap, and whether the single-annotator affordance labels (acknowledged limitation) understate or overstate true model capabilities.
