## Problem

Automatic explanation generation for Sparse Autoencoder (SAE) features in LLMs produces overly broad, one-shot summaries that fail to capture polysemanticity and are difficult to falsify. Prior evaluation relies on randomly sampled negative sentences, which are too dissimilar to top-activating examples to expose imprecision, and explanations lack structure to represent multiple co-occurring feature components.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252122/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes SAE feature activation records formatted as token sequences with per-token activations normalized to integers 0–5, plus a set of complementary evaluation sentences (top-activating and negatively-sourced). Explanations are produced as structured JSON lists of monosemantic components, each with an 'activates_on' string and an integer 'strength' (0–5); simulation then predicts token-level activations per component and combines them via element-wise maximum scaled by strength, making the output permutation-invariant. Evaluation yields Pearson correlation scores between ground-truth and simulated activations, along with false positive rates on complementary sentences.

## Method

The paper introduces three complementary mechanisms. First, a similarity-based complementary sentence sourcing strategy uses Sentence Transformer cosine similarity to select 'close negatives'—sentences semantically near top-activating examples but with zero ground-truth activation—exposing the recall bias in existing explainers. Second, a structured explanation schema represents each feature as a list of monosemantic JSON components (string description + integer strength), making polysemanticity explicit and enabling per-component simulation. Third, a tree-based iterative explainer adapted from TAP (Tree of Attacks with Pruning) initializes with w one-shot explanations, then repeatedly evaluates each leaf against validation data, feeds back the correlation score and the lowest-performing sentence's ground-truth vs. simulated activations, branches b improved children via chain-of-thought prompting, and prunes to the top-w scorers over d iterations. A bilevel key-value cache over the gemma-2-27b-it simulator accelerates the all-at-once activation prediction that underpins both evaluation and feedback.

## Results

The tree-based explainer consistently outperforms the one-shot baseline across all three subject models (Gemma-2-9B, Llama-3.1-8B, GPT-2-small), achieving relative correlation improvements of 19.8%, 21.6%, and 16.4% respectively for unstructured explanations. Structured explanations provide additional gains of 5.8–9.2% over unstructured ones when paired with the one-shot explainer, but offer no further benefit when combined with the tree-based method. On the complementary sentence sourcing front, similarity-based non-activating sentences yield substantially higher false positive rates (e.g., 11.6 vs. 2.2 per sentence for the 'render/argument' feature; 18.7 vs. 2.9 for the table cell separator feature) compared to random selection, exposing a recall bias in existing explanation methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is the one-shot explainer from Bills et al. [1], which generates a single natural-language summary from top-activating sentences without iterative refinement. The tree-based explainer (adapted from TAP [21]) wins on all three subject models and both explanation formats, with the largest gain on Llama-3.1-8B (+21.6% relative correlation improvement for unstructured explanations). Structured explanations also outperform unstructured ones under the one-shot setting (5.8–9.2% relative improvement), but this advantage disappears under the tree-based setting, suggesting the iterative refinement process can pack polysemantic meanings into a single string. The paper also reports negative results: including holistic activations or training negatives does not improve performance for either explainer, indicating that LLMs still struggle to incorporate prior-context information into feature explanations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LLM-based feature explanation pipeline introduced by Bills et al. (2023), which established the one-shot explainer and simulator paradigm for interpreting SAE features. It repurposes the Tree of Attacks with Pruning (TAP) search strategy from the jailbreaking literature (Mehrotra et al., 2024) as an iterative refinement mechanism for explanations, and introduces a similarity-based evaluation protocol using Sentence Transformer embeddings to source 'close counterexamples'—departing from the random complementary sentences used in prior work. The structured, multi-component explanation format also represents a conceptual departure from the single-sentence summaries standard in the field, explicitly modeling polysemanticity as a list of monosemantic rules.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By exposing the recall bias in current auto-interpretability methods through similarity-based falsification, this work provides a more rigorous evaluation standard that the SAE interpretability community can adopt. The layer-wise analysis of feature complexity and polysemanticity offers preliminary evidence that deeper layers encode more abstract and multi-faceted concepts, though the authors caution these are empirical observations rather than mechanistic explanations. Key open questions include whether the findings generalize to larger models and wider SAEs, whether the ~1.5-minute-per-feature computational cost of tree-based refinement is acceptable for large-scale analysis, and how feature-level interpretability connects to higher-level model reasoning.
