## Problem

Deterministic novel view synthesis methods render observed regions quickly but produce blurry or black outputs in unobserved areas, while diffusion-based generative methods can hallucinate plausible content yet require iterative full-image sampling that is an order of magnitude slower. No prior work has unified these two paradigms into a single model that adaptively allocates computation between fast deterministic rendering and generative completion based on per-region uncertainty.

## Input / Output

The model takes 1–3 sparse input views at 256×256 resolution, each concatenated channel-wise with its 6-dimensional Plücker ray embedding (yielding 9-channel tokens) before being split into 8×8 patches by an MLP tokenizer. The target camera pose is similarly encoded and appended to the target token sequence. At inference, the target image is initialized entirely with learnable masked tokens; the model progressively reveals patches, first rendering high-confidence regions in a single forward pass and then iteratively denoising the remainder, producing a full photorealistic RGB image (optionally up to 3 target views simultaneously).

## Method

UMAMI factorizes the target distribution as p(x|c) = δ(x_D − F(c)) · p(x_S | x_D, c), routing tokens to either a deterministic or stochastic path based on a learned pixel-wise confidence map (patch confidence = min over its pixels, thresholded at τ). A 24-layer decoder-only bidirectional transformer (initialized from LVSM) encodes context and masked-target tokens into a shared latent z, which feeds two lightweight MLP heads: a regression head that outputs RGB and confidence in one pass, and a MAR-style diffusion head (small MLP with time embedding) that performs DDPM denoising on uncertain tokens. Training jointly optimizes photometric loss (MSE + perceptual) on the deterministic head, a DDPM denoising loss weighted toward low-confidence tokens on the diffusion head, and a confidence regularization loss. At inference, a hybrid sampler first renders all tokens with confidence ≥ τ deterministically, then allocates a dynamically scaled number of unmasking steps T_S = ⌈|x_S|/|x| · T_max⌉ to the remaining tokens using a cosine schedule, achieving ~5 s per image versus ~60 s for fully generative baselines.

## Results

On RealEstate10K, UMAMI achieves the best PSNR (28.95) and SSIM (0.897) on the 2-view extrapolation split, and the best PSNR (31.06) and SSIM (0.946) on the 3-view split, while matching LVSM closely on 2-view interpolation (28.85 vs. 29.67 PSNR). On DL3DV, UMAMI achieves the highest PSNR in the 3-view setting (16.37) and competitive PSNR in 1-view (12.81) and 6-view (17.33) settings, though SEVA leads on LPIPS across all DL3DV configurations. UMAMI renders in ~5s at τ=0.95, roughly 12× faster than SEVA (~60s) while maintaining comparable or superior image quality on RealEstate10K.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the deterministic baseline LVSM [31], UMAMI wins on the 2-view extrapolation split (+0.44 PSNR, +0.015 SSIM) and the 3-view split (+1.02 PSNR, +0.010 SSIM), but trails by 0.82 PSNR on 2-view interpolation where most of the target is visible. Against MVSplat [11] and DepthSplat [91], UMAMI dominates across all RealEstate10K splits by 3–9 PSNR, confirming the advantage of the generative head for unobserved regions. Against the large generative model SEVA [102] (1.3B params vs. UMAMI's 271M), UMAMI achieves higher PSNR and SSIM on all three RealEstate10K splits while SEVA retains a LPIPS advantage; on DL3DV, SEVA leads on LPIPS and SSIM in all settings, though UMAMI achieves the best PSNR in the 3-view configuration. Against ViewCrafter [99], UMAMI outperforms by 4–9 PSNR on RealEstate10K and 1–4 PSNR on DL3DV. Against NoPoSplat [96] on varying overlap ratios, UMAMI wins on all metrics (e.g., +1.04 PSNR at small overlap, +2.37 PSNR at large overlap), demonstrating robustness even under minimal camera overlap.

## Relation to Prior Work

UMAMI sits at the intersection of two NVS lineages: the deterministic, feed-forward transformer approaches (LVSM, SRT) that render observed regions quickly but blur unseen areas, and the diffusion-based generative methods (SEVA, ViewCrafter, CAT3D) that hallucinate plausible content at the cost of iterative full-image sampling. The paper directly adopts LVSM's decoder-only transformer backbone and Plücker-ray tokenization, and repurposes the Masked Autoregressive (MAR) diffusion loss and cosine unmasking schedule from Li et al. (NeurIPS 2024) as a lightweight per-token stochastic head. The key architectural departure is the confidence-based partitioning of target tokens into a deterministic subset (rendered in one forward pass) and a stochastic subset (completed via a small MLP diffusion head), which the authors claim is the first hybrid unification of these two paradigms for NVS.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is the ~12× speedup over fully generative baselines (≈5 s vs ≈60 s per image) while matching or exceeding their quality on RealEstate10K, making high-fidelity sparse-view NVS more accessible for interactive or near-real-time applications. However, the method operates in pixel space with 1024 tokens per 256×256 image, forgoing VAE latent compression, and does not leverage pretrained text-to-image priors—both acknowledged limitations that cap its generative ceiling. On the more challenging DL3DV benchmark, UMAMI's gains over SEVA are mixed (competitive PSNR but weaker SSIM and LPIPS in several settings), suggesting the hybrid advantage is most pronounced in indoor, high-overlap scenes. Open questions include whether the framework extends to video-level temporal consistency, whether latent-space tokenization preserves the confidence-based partitioning signal, and whether integrating text-conditioned diffusion priors would close the remaining quality gap on outdoor scenes.
