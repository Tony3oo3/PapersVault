## Problem

For noiseless linear regression with Gaussian covariates and oblivious additive contamination (where a fraction 1−α of labels are corrupted by values independent of the covariates), the information-theoretic sample complexity is O(d/α) but the best known polynomial-time algorithms require Ω(d/α²) samples. This paper provides formal evidence that the quadratic dependence on 1/α is inherent for efficient algorithms by establishing Statistical Query (SQ) hardness for the problem.

## Input / Output

The SQ algorithm receives oracle access (via a VSTAT(m) oracle) to i.i.d. samples (x, y) where x ~ N(0, I_d) and y = x^T β* + z with z drawn from an unknown univariate distribution E satisfying P(z=0) ≥ α. The goal is to produce an estimate β̂ with small ℓ₂-error to β*. The hardness is established for a testing sub-problem: distinguishing whether x and y are independent (null) or y = ρv^T x + z for a random unit direction v (alternate), where the contamination distribution E is a discrete Gaussian with spacing s = Θ(α).

## Method

The proof constructs a hard instance using a discrete Gaussian contamination distribution E = DG[0, σ, 0, s] with s = Θ(α), which naturally assigns probability Ω(s) to zero. The resulting testing problem is cast as a conditional Non-Gaussian Component Analysis (NGCA) task, where the conditional distribution of x given y in the hidden direction is a discrete Gaussian. Since discrete Gaussians have infinite χ²-divergence from the standard Gaussian (breaking standard SQ-dimension tools), the authors adapt Gaussian Fourier analysis techniques from [DKRS23] using Hermite polynomial expansions. A key technical step is showing that the discrete Gaussian conditional distributions are indistinguishable from their continuous Gaussian counterparts by any bounded SQ query (via moment-matching bounds on Hermite coefficients), while the continuous Gaussian version provably has large statistical dimension (via closed-form pairwise correlation calculations between Gaussians with nearly orthogonal means). Combining these two ingredients via a triangle inequality yields the SQ lower bound of Õ(√d/α²) simulation complexity.

## Results

The main result (Theorem 1.6) establishes that any SQ algorithm solving the testing version of noiseless linear regression with oblivious contamination requires either $d^{\Omega(\log^2(d/\alpha))}$ queries or at least one VSTAT query with simulation complexity $\tilde{\Omega}(\sqrt{d}/\alpha^2)$, under the conditions $\alpha \gg 1/d^{\mathrm{polylog}(d)}$ and $\rho = \tilde{\Theta}(\alpha)$. This provides formal evidence that the quadratic dependence on $1/\alpha$ in the sample complexity is inherent for efficient algorithms, since the information-theoretic optimum is only $O(d/\alpha)$ samples while known polynomial-time algorithms require $\Omega(d/\alpha^2)$. The SQ-hard instances are efficiently solvable with $\tilde{O}(\sqrt{d}/\alpha^2)$ samples, and the authors conjecture the correct lower bound is actually $\Omega(d/\alpha^2)$.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no experimental baselines. The key comparison is between the information-theoretic sample complexity and the computational (SQ) sample complexity. Information-theoretically, $O(d/\alpha)$ samples suffice to recover $\beta^*$ exactly (via compressed sensing / LP decoding results of Candes and Tao). The best known polynomial-time algorithms (e.g., [GL20; dNS21]) require $\Omega(d/\alpha^2)$ samples. The paper's SQ lower bound of $\tilde{\Omega}(\sqrt{d}/\alpha^2)$ confirms that the quadratic $1/\alpha$ dependence is unavoidable for SQ algorithms, closing the gap between the information-theoretic and computational regimes up to a factor of $\sqrt{d}$ in the dimension dependence. The authors note that their hard instance is solvable with $\tilde{O}(\sqrt{d}/\alpha^2)$ samples and conjecture that a $\Omega(d/\alpha^2)$ lower bound (matching known algorithms exactly) is achievable with a new hardness construction.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the SQ lower bound framework for Non-Gaussian Component Analysis (NGCA) established by Diakonikolas, Kane, and Stewart [DKS17, DKS19], and critically adapts the Gaussian Fourier analysis techniques of Diakonikolas, Kane, Ren, and Sun [DKRS23] that handle infinite chi-squared distance. The oblivious contamination model for linear regression traces back to Candes and Tao [CT05] and has been studied algorithmically by many groups [BJK15, dNS21, GL20], but no prior work provided formal SQ-hardness evidence for the gap between information-theoretic and computational sample complexity. The paper's key technical departure is using a discrete Gaussian contamination distribution to bridge the gap between the continuous Gaussian case (where information-theoretic lower bounds are known) and the required discrete noise model, combined with a novel conditional NGCA analysis via Hermite expansions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This result provides the first formal SQ-hardness evidence that the quadratic dependence on 1/α in the sample complexity of efficient algorithms for noiseless linear regression with oblivious contamination is inherent, closing a gap that had been suspected but not proven. A notable limitation is that the lower bound achieves only √d dependence on dimension rather than the conjectured linear d, and the authors conjecture the correct bound is Ω(d/α²), matching known algorithms. The SQ-hardness result also suggests hardness for low-degree polynomial tests via known equivalences [BBHLS21], broadening the scope of the negative result. For practitioners, this formalizes the intuition that when the inlier fraction α is small, no polynomial-time algorithm can avoid the quadratic sample overhead, which has direct implications for robust regression in high-noise regimes.
