## Problem

Existing SFT-based approaches for enhancing visual reasoning in VLMs rely on large amounts of meticulously annotated Chain-of-Thought data, leading to overfitting, cognitive rigidity, and poor generalization under domain shift. Pure RL methods (e.g., R1-style) lack robustness to visual domain changes. The paper asks whether a two-stage reinforcement fine-tuning framework can combine the inductive priors of SFT with the exploration-driven generalization of RL to address these limitations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250204/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images of 3D block scenes (CLEVR/Super-CLEVR style), geometric diagrams, or multi-view 3D scene renderings (front, left, right perspectives) paired with a text question. The model produces a structured response: reasoning steps enclosed in <think>… tags followed by a final answer in <answer>…</answer> tags. The answer format is task-specific — an integer count for visual counting, a numerical value (evaluated with cosine-based tolerance) for structure perception, or an ordered sequence of (function, object, value) triples for spatial transformation.

## Method

Reason-RFT is a two-stage training pipeline built on Qwen2-VL (2B/7B). Stage 1 performs SFT on only 1,600 curated CoT samples to activate the model's domain-specific reasoning capability, producing a policy π_CoT. Stage 2 applies GRPO (Group Relative Policy Optimization) starting from π_CoT, sampling G candidate responses per input and computing relative advantages by normalizing rewards within the group, thereby avoiding the need for a separate value network. The reward is the sum of a binary format reward (enforcing the <think>/<answer> template) and a task-specific accuracy reward: exact-match for discrete answers, a smooth cosine tolerance function for numerical answers, and a partial-credit sequence-alignment score for transformation-function sequences. This design lets RL explore diverse reasoning paths while the minimal SFT stage provides stable task priors, yielding superior domain-shift generalization and data efficiency (reaching >90% of full-SFT performance with <5% of the data).

## Results

On the 7B model, Reason-RFT achieves average accuracy of 62.44% on Visual Counting, 54.26% on Structure Perception, and 65.98% on Spatial Transformation, outperforming all SFT-only and RL-only baselines under domain shift. The 2B Reason-RFT model surpasses GPT-4o by 31.7% on Spatial Transformation domain-shift (64.05% vs. 28.67% on DS-L). In terms of data efficiency, Reason-RFT reaches over 92% of Reason-RFT-Zero's performance using only 3% of the training data (1,600 samples) in the 7B setting, and 70% with just 3% in the 2B setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Reason-RFT (7B) outperforms GPT-4o on Visual Counting DS-M (35.60% vs. 9.60%) and Spatial Transformation DS (59.36%/58.61% vs. 28.67%/29.76%), while GPT-4o retains an edge on Structure Perception ID (50.18% vs. 59.27% — actually Reason-RFT wins here too at 59.27%). Against Gemini-1.5-Pro, Reason-RFT dominates across all three tasks, with the largest gap on Spatial Transformation (65.98% vs. 22.77% AVG). Among open-source models, Reason-RFT 7B surpasses InternVL-2.5-8B on Visual Counting DS (62.44% vs. 47.48% AVG) and Spatial Transformation (65.98% vs. 6.91%), though InternVL leads on Structure Perception ID (63.00% vs. 59.27%). Reason-RFT clearly outperforms Qwen2.5-VL-7B, Phi-3.5-Vision-4B, Llama-3.2-11B-Vision, and Pixtral-12B across all tasks, with the most dramatic margins on Spatial Transformation where these baselines score below 20% AVG. Within the same backbone, Reason-RFT consistently beats CoT-SFT under domain shift (e.g., +12.8% on Visual Counting DS, +21.04% on Spatial Transformation DS for 2B), confirming that the RL stage is critical for generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the GRPO-based reinforcement learning paradigm from DeepSeek-R1 (text reasoning) to vision-language models for visual reasoning, which is the first such application in the VLM setting. It positions itself against two prior lines: SFT-based CoT fine-tuning (LLaVA-CoT, Insight-V) and neural-symbolic visual programming, arguing that both suffer from overfitting and cognitive rigidity under domain shift. The key methodological departure is the two-stage design—SFT with a small CoT dataset for reasoning activation followed by GRPO with task-specific rewards—rather than pure RL (Reason-RFT-Zero) or pure SFT, and the introduction of tailored accuracy rewards (discrete, mathematical, function-based) for heterogeneous visual reasoning tasks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first systematic empirical comparison of SFT-only, RL-only, and hybrid SFT+RL paradigms for visual reasoning, with the clear finding that RL-based fine-tuning substantially improves domain-shift generalization while SFT alone does not. The data-efficiency result (≥90% of full-data SFT performance with <5% of training samples) is practically significant for settings where high-quality CoT annotations are scarce. However, the evaluation is confined to synthetic 3D scenes (CLEVR-style) and one model family (Qwen2-VL at 2B/7B), leaving open whether the findings transfer to real-world visual reasoning, larger models, or more diverse task distributions. The observed 'reasoning redundancy' (longer CoT traces without accuracy gains) also raises unresolved questions about how to control verbosity in RL fine-tuning.
