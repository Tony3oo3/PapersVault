## Problem

The dominant shape-vs-texture bias diagnostic is inherently relative: a high score can arise from either stronger shape coding or weaker texture coding, leaving the absolute fidelity of global shape representations ambiguous. No existing metric directly measures a model's capacity to recognize objects from their global part configuration while holding local texture content constant, making it impossible to distinguish genuine holistic shape processing from mere texture suppression.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252559/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each evaluation stimulus is a pair of 256×256 RGB images (center-cropped to 224×224) constructed from an identical multiset of 16 square diffusion patches arranged in two different 4×4 permutations, depicting two distinct objects from a fixed 9-category set (bear, bunny, cat, elephant, frog, lizard, tiger, turtle, wolf). The Configural Shape Score (CSS) is a joint two-image criterion: a model earns credit for a pair only if it correctly classifies both members, yielding a chance level of 1/81 ≈ 1.23% and a ceiling that demands perfect configural sensitivity across both views simultaneously.

## Method

The Object Anagram Dataset is synthesized via a symmetrized reverse-diffusion procedure in DeepFloyd-IF: at each denoising timestep, two predictions are computed (one for the canonical arrangement, one for the permuted arrangement) and combined so that both category representations are jointly optimized over a shared patch content, guaranteeing identical local texture statistics across pair members. The paper benchmarks 86 pretrained models (CNNs, ViTs, hybrids) on CSS and then performs two mechanistic probes: (i) radius-controlled attention ablation that restricts each patch to attend only within or outside a local window, revealing a U-shaped integration profile where intermediate layers perform the critical configural computation, and (ii) representational similarity analysis on controlled image-pair triplets (same-parts/different-category, different-parts/different-category, different-parts/same-category) that exposes a mid-depth transition from local to global coding. A BagNet control with strictly local receptive fields performs at chance, ruling out border-hacking, and CSS is shown to predict performance on four independent shape-dependent evals (noise robustness, foreground bias, phase dependence, critical band masking) more strongly than shape-vs-texture bias does.

## Results

Across 86 vision models, the Configural Shape Score (CSS) ranges from near-chance (BagNet-17: 1.38%) to near-human levels (SigLIP ViT-B/16 and EVA-CLIP G/14: 77.78%). CSS dissociates from ImageNet top-1 accuracy: supervised ViT-B/16 achieves 76.35% top-1 but only 23.61% CSS, while SigLIP ViT-B/16 achieves 74.96% top-1 with 77.78% CSS. CSS also predicts other shape-dependent benchmarks more strongly than shape-vs-texture bias does: correlations of r=0.81 (noise robustness), r=0.76 (foreground bias), r=0.73 (phase dependence), and r=0.83 (critical band masking), all significantly exceeding shape-bias correlations (r=0.62, 0.32, 0.52, 0.55; Williams's test p<0.001 for all).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Self-supervised DINOv2-B/14 (61.11% CSS) and language-aligned SigLIP/EVA-CLIP models (77.78% CSS) substantially outperform supervised ViT-B/16 (23.61% CSS) and ResNet-50 (16.67% CSS) on configural shape, despite comparable or lower ImageNet accuracy. Stylized models [8], adversarially robust models [26], and top-k sparse models [47] all increase shape-vs-texture bias but yield negligible CSS gains, demonstrating that suppressing texture is insufficient for genuine configural processing. BagNets [25], whose receptive fields are restricted to 9–33 pixels, score at or near chance (1.38–5.5% CSS), ruling out local 'border-hacking' as an explanation for anagram recognition. Replacing learned absolute positional encodings with RoPE in ViT-B/16 more than doubles CSS from 23.61% to 51.38%, showing that relational spatial encoding is a critical architectural factor. CSS outperforms shape-vs-texture bias as a predictor of noise robustness (r=0.81 vs 0.62), foreground bias (r=0.76 vs 0.32), phase dependence (r=0.73 vs 0.52), and critical band masking (r=0.83 vs 0.55), with all differences statistically significant (Williams's test, p<0.001).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the visual anagram synthesis pipeline of Geng et al. (CVPR 2024) to create image pairs that share identical local patches but differ in global arrangement, repurposing a generative-illusion technique as a controlled probe of configural processing. It departs from the dominant shape-vs-texture bias paradigm (Geirhos et al., 2018) by arguing that relative cue-conflict metrics conflate shape enhancement with texture suppression, and instead introduces the Configural Shape Score (CSS) as an absolute measure of global-arrangement sensitivity. The mechanistic analyses (attention ablation, representational similarity) extend prior work by Baker & Elder (2022) on configural disruption in DNNs from a binary pass/fail test to a graded, layer-resolved characterization across 86 architectures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The CSS benchmark and its dissociation from shape-vs-texture bias (r=0.64) provide the field with a more principled tool for evaluating holistic shape processing, and the finding that self-supervised (DINOv2) and language-aligned (SigLIP2, EVA-CLIP) ViTs dominate the CSS spectrum while comparably accurate supervised models do not offers concrete guidance for training objectives that foster configural representations. The U-shaped attention profile and mid-depth local-to-global transition identified through ablation and RSA are actionable design principles for future architectures. Open questions include whether CSS generalizes beyond the nine animal categories and the diffusion-model-constrained stimulus space, whether part-based compositionality (orthogonal to whole-object configuration) can be probed with analogous methods, and whether explicitly optimizing for CSS during training yields models that surpass current self-supervised baselines.
