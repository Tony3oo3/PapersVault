## Problem

Existing audio-visual QA benchmarks (e.g., AVQA, MUSIC-AVQA) suffer from strong visual bias, allowing models to answer correctly using only the visual modality, and report only aggregate scores that conflate distinct failure modes. This makes it impossible to determine whether a model struggles with visual understanding, audio interpretation, or the temporal alignment between the two modalities.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251786/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each sample consists of a continuous egocentric video segment (up to 60 s, from Epic Kitchens or Ego4D) with a synthetically overlaid environmental sound (from 13 ESC-50 classes) precisely time-aligned to one of four action events, paired with a five-option multiple-choice question (A–D are action descriptions, E is 'none of the above'). The model outputs a single letter; the benchmark reports accuracy per question type (multimodal synchronization, sound absence detection, sound discrimination) and per atomic subtask (action recognition, temporal ordering, audio classification), with bootstrap standard deviations over 1000 resamples.

## Method

DAVE is built via a semi-automatic pipeline that extracts narrated action events from Epic Kitchens and Ego4D, groups them into four-event sequences, and overlays ESC-50 environmental sounds precisely aligned to event boundaries with fade-in/out and volume scaling. A two-stage quality filter uses an LLM (Gemini Flash 2.0 Lite) to rephrase narrations, compute inter-event similarity scores (threshold 0.85), and perform zero-shot action classification to remove visually ambiguous segments. The key design principle is that every question is constructed to be unsolvable from a single modality alone. Evaluation decomposes audio-visual reasoning into three primary question types and three atomic subtasks, enabling fine-grained diagnosis of whether failures stem from unimodal perception, temporal reasoning, or true cross-modal integration. The benchmark comprises 2426 curated samples and is evaluated across closed-source (Gemini variants, GPT-4o pipeline), open-source (PandaGPT, video-SALMONN, Video-LLaMA-2), and human baselines.

## Results

On the DAVE benchmark (2,426 samples), the best-performing model, Gemini 2.5 Flash, achieves 58.73% overall accuracy, substantially below the human baseline of 84.74% and well above the random baseline of 22.41%. Performance is highly uneven across question types: Gemini 2.5 Flash scores 59.15% on multimodal synchronisation, 70.69% on sound absence detection, but only 44.21% on sound discrimination. Open-source models (PandaGPT, video-SALMONN, Video-LLaMA-2) achieve 17–31% overall accuracy, with video-SALMONN performing worst at 17.12%. A critical finding is that several models (Gemini 2.0 Flash, Gemini 1.5 Pro) show catastrophic drops from ~55–60% on multimodal synchronisation to <10% on sound absence detection, revealing a strong bias toward positive audio-visual associations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DAVE evaluates five families of AV-LLMs, revealing a clear performance hierarchy. Among closed-source end-to-end models, Gemini 2.5 Flash leads at 58.73% overall, followed by Gemini 2.0 Flash (50.81%) and Gemini 1.5 Flash (49.53%); however, Gemini 2.0 Flash and 1.5 Pro collapse to 8.50% and 1.03% respectively on sound absence detection, indicating a severe positive-association bias. The GPT-4o pipeline (27.38% overall) paradoxically excels at sound discrimination (93.09%) and absence detection (60.12%) due to a conservative 'None of the above' strategy (64.69% of predictions), but performs poorly on multimodal synchronisation (17.34%). Among open-source models, Video-LLaMA-2 (31.31%) outperforms PandaGPT (18.82%) and video-SALMONN (17.12%), with the latter two performing near or below random on sound discrimination (2.34% and 30.25% respectively). Across all models, the ~26-point gap between the best model and human performance (58.73% vs. 84.74%) underscores that no current AV-LLM achieves reliable audio-visual integration, and the modality ablation confirms DAVE genuinely requires cross-modal reasoning (e.g., Gemini 2.0 Flash drops from 50.85% to 37.14% when audio is removed).

## Relation to Prior Work

DAVE positions itself as a corrective to existing audio-visual QA benchmarks (AVQA, MUSIC-AVQA, Pano-AVQA) that the authors demonstrate suffer from strong visual bias, where answers can be derived from the visual modality alone. It also differentiates from AVTrustBench (2025), which evaluates trustworthiness through adversarial attacks and compositional reasoning, by focusing specifically on temporal synchronization and requiring both modalities to be jointly necessary. The data generation pipeline repurposes egocentric video datasets (Epic Kitchens, Ego4D) and environmental sound classes (ESC-50) through a semi-automatic overlay procedure, while the evaluation targets the same family of AV-LLMs (Gemini, GPT-4o, PandaGPT, Video-SALMONN, Video-LLaMA-2) that prior benchmarks assess, but under a stricter diagnostic decomposition into synchronization, absence detection, and discrimination subtasks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark's central diagnostic finding—that models which perform reasonably on multimodal synchronization (~55-60%) catastrophically fail on sound absence detection (1-8.5%)—points to a fundamental architectural bias toward positive association that current training objectives do not address, suggesting a concrete direction for future work (explicit mismatch-detection modules or negative-evidence training). However, the benchmark's scope is constrained: it is limited to multiple-choice format, template-based questions, egocentric video, and a modest 2,426 samples, which limits its ability to capture the full breadth of audio-visual reasoning. Open questions include whether the absence-detection failure persists with larger or differently trained models, whether extending beyond egocentric video changes the difficulty landscape, and whether the template-based question format adequately proxies natural audio-visual comprehension.
