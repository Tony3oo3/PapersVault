## Problem

Existing conformal prediction (CP) methods for trajectory optimization use a strictly sequential scheme: prediction regions are computed first, and the trajectory decision depends unidirectionally on them, with no information flowing back from realized trajectories to the CP. This one-way information channel prevents the CP from exploiting the fact that past realized positions often carry much less collision risk than the a priori allocated budget, leading to unnecessarily conservative future prediction regions. The paper closes this gap by proposing a closed-loop framework that feeds realized trajectory information back into the CP to shrink prediction regions while provably maintaining coverage guarantees.

## Input / Output

The method consumes the current system state, observed obstacle trajectories up to time t, a calibration dataset split into two disjoint subsets (D_cal^1 with K trajectories for conformal quantile computation and D_cal^2 with L trajectories for posterior probability estimation), and a total mission risk tolerance α. It outputs a shrinking-horizon optimized trajectory with controls, a time-varying risk allocation across future steps, and prediction regions whose radii adaptively shrink as posterior collision risks are computed. The two-part calibration split is essential: D_cal^1 and D_cal^2 must be independent to preserve the exchangeability required for valid coverage guarantees in the posterior probability calculation.

## Method

The core mechanism is a CP-based posterior risk calculation: at each time step, the realized robot position x*_τ is evaluated against D_cal^2 to compute β_τ, a conformal upper bound on the actual collision probability, which is typically far smaller than the a priori allocated risk α_τ. The freed risk budget (α_τ − β_τ) is redistributed to future time steps, shrinking their prediction regions and reducing trajectory conservatism in a closed-loop fashion. For the redistribution, the paper proposes an Iterative Risk Allocation (IRA) algorithm that alternates between solving the TO subproblem (lower stage) and reallocating risk from inactive to active constraints (upper stage), with a proven monotonic decrease in optimal cost and convergence to a finite limit. The key theoretical contribution is proving that these feedback-based adjustments consistently maintain the (1−α) joint coverage guarantee over the entire mission while guaranteeing online performance improvement over the sequential baseline. The framework is further extended to handle distribution shift via a weighting scheme applied to both calibration and test data.

## Results

On the quadrotor model with 1,000 Monte Carlo simulations, Fb-CP-IRA achieves an average cost of 7.189 at α=0.05, representing a 58.5% reduction compared to S-CP (17.321). Fb-CP-ARA reduces cost by 11.34% over S-CP (15.356 vs. 17.321) with only a modest increase in computation time (0.027s vs. 0.022s). Fb-CP-IRA's computation time (0.038s) remains an order of magnitude lower than RF-CP (0.487s), while collision avoidance rates for all methods stay within the prescribed α tolerance (96.3%–98.8% at α=0.05).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Fb-CP-IRA dominates all four baselines on average cost: it reduces cost by 58.5% over S-CP (7.189 vs. 17.321 at α=0.05), by 85.8% over RF-CP (7.189 vs. 15.794), and by 184% and 296% over CC and ACI-MP respectively. Against S-CP, Fb-CP-ARA also wins on cost (15.356 vs. 17.321) with comparable computation time, confirming that even simple average risk allocation benefits from the posterior feedback. RF-CP achieves a cost close to Fb-CP-ARA but at >10× the computation time (0.487s vs. 0.027s) due to mixed-integer variables in its normalized nonconformity score. CC and ACI-MP, which do not fully exploit the calibration dataset for prediction-region construction, incur substantially higher costs; the paper notes they are better suited to distribution-shift scenarios rather than the i.i.d. setting evaluated here. On collision avoidance rate, Fb-CP-IRA (96.3%) is slightly below S-CP (98.8%) at α=0.05, but both remain within the 1−α=95% guarantee, and the gap narrows at higher α values.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the sequential conformal prediction (CP) framework for trajectory optimization (Lindemann et al., 2023) by closing the information loop: realized trajectories feed back into CP via posterior risk calculation, enabling online adjustment of prediction regions while preserving coverage guarantees. The iterative risk allocation (IRA) algorithm is adapted from Ono and Williams (2008), originally developed for robust MPC with joint chance constraints, and repurposed here to optimize risk distribution across future time steps in a CP-based setting. The entire framework rests on the exchangeability-based coverage guarantees of Vovk et al. (2005), but the key departure is treating the decision variable (the realized trajectory) as a source of information for the upstream uncertainty quantification, rather than a passive consumer of it.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to provably close the feedback loop between conformal prediction and downstream decision-making while maintaining finite-sample coverage guarantees, addressing a fundamental disconnect in how CP has been applied to safety-critical planning. The 58.5% average cost reduction over sequential CP (with IRA) demonstrates that the conservatism inherent in a priori risk allocation is substantial and recoverable. Open questions include scalability to very long horizons (IRA requires iterative NLP solves), extension to multi-robot settings with coupled constraints, and whether the i.i.d. calibration assumption can be relaxed further for real-world non-stationary obstacle behavior beyond the distribution-shift extension in the appendix.
