## Problem

Existing parameter-efficient LoRA variants (Tied-LoRA, VeRA, VB-LoRA, LoRA-XS) each impose different structural constraints on the trainable parameter space, but no unified framework exists to describe them in a common language or to systematically analyze their design choices. Moreover, the implicit projection matrices used by most of these methods are local (layer-wise), non-uniform, and non-isometric, which distorts the optimization geometry and limits cross-layer parameter sharing.

## Input / Output

The method takes a frozen pre-trained model (RoBERTa, Mistral-7B, Gemma-7B, Llama-2, or ViT) and a single trainable vector θ_d ∈ R^d (e.g., d=23,040 for GLUE, d=524,288 for 7B LLMs, d=1,048,576 for 13B LLMs). A frozen one-hot sparse projection matrix P ∈ R^{D×d} (with exactly D nonzero entries) maps θ_d back to the full LoRA parameter space θ_D, from which the low-rank weight increment ΔW = BA is computed on-the-fly without explicit materialization. Downstream outputs are standard: class labels for GLUE/CV tasks and generated text for math reasoning and instruction tuning.

## Method

Uni-LoRA formulates all LoRA variants as a linear projection θ_D = Pθ_d from a low-dimensional subspace R^d to the full LoRA parameter space R^D, where the key design choice is the projection matrix P. The proposed P is constructed by assigning each of the D LoRA parameters to one of d randomly chosen slots (each row of P is a one-hot vector), then normalizing each column j by 1/√n_j where n_j is the number of parameters assigned to slot j. This is equivalent to randomly partitioning all LoRA parameters into d groups and constraining parameters within each group to share the same learned value. Theorem 1 proves P is isometric (P^T P = I_d), preserving the geometry of the optimization landscape. Because P is one-hot sparse, the projection has O(D) time and space complexity—significantly lower than Fastfood's O(D log d)—and only the single vector θ_d plus a random seed need to be stored after training, yielding extreme parameter efficiency (e.g., 0.52M trainable parameters for Gemma-7B, just 0.0061% of the base model).

## Results

On the GLUE benchmark with RoBERTa-large, Uni-LoRA achieves an average score of 88.3 using only 0.023M trainable parameters (0.0061% of the base model), outperforming LoRA (87.8, 0.786M), VB-LoRA (88.2, 0.162M), and FourierFT (88.0, 0.048M). On mathematical reasoning with Gemma-7B, Uni-LoRA reaches 75.59 on GSM8K and 28.94 on MATH with just 0.52M parameters, surpassing LoRA-XS (74.22/27.62) and VB-LoRA (74.86/28.90). On MT-Bench instruction tuning with Llama2-13B, Uni-LoRA scores 6.34 (single-turn) and 4.43 (multi-turn) with 1.0M parameters, exceeding LoRA (6.20/4.13, 250.3M) and VB-LoRA (5.96/4.33, 256M). On eight CV datasets with ViT-Large, Uni-LoRA achieves 88.00 average accuracy with 144K parameters, substantially outperforming FourierFT (83.71, 144K) and approaching full fine-tuning (90.20, 303.3M).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Uni-LoRA consistently outperforms or matches all six baselines while using the fewest trainable parameters. On GLUE (RoBERTa-large), it beats Tied-LoRA by 2.4 points (88.3 vs. 85.9) and LoRA-XS by 0.4 points (88.3 vs. 87.9) with fewer parameters, and matches VB-LoRA (88.3 vs. 88.2) while using only 14% of VB-LoRA's trainable parameters (0.023M vs. 0.162M). On mathematical reasoning (Gemma-7B), Uni-LoRA surpasses LoRA-XS on both GSM8K (75.59 vs. 74.22) and MATH (28.94 vs. 27.62), and outperforms FourierFT by 2.62 points on GSM8K and 3.80 on MATH. On instruction tuning (Llama2-13B), Uni-LoRA exceeds LoRA by 0.14 on single-turn MT-Bench (6.34 vs. 6.20) and by 0.10 on multi-turn (4.43 vs. 4.13), while using only 0.4% of LoRA's parameters. On CV tasks (ViT-Large), Uni-LoRA outperforms FourierFT by 4.29 points in average accuracy (88.00 vs. 83.71) with the same 144K parameters, and remains within 2.2 points of full fine-tuning (90.20) using less than 0.05% of its parameters. The only notable exception is on RTE with RoBERTa-large, where LoRA-XS edges out Uni-LoRA (88.1 vs. 86.6), though the authors note this dataset is small with high variance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the LoRA family of parameter-efficient fine-tuning methods, unifying Tied-LoRA, VeRA, VB-LoRA, LoRA-XS, and FourierFT under a single subspace-projection framework where the full LoRA parameter space R^D is reconstructed via a projection matrix P from a lower-dimensional subspace R^d. The key departure is the identification that the fundamental difference among these methods lies solely in the choice of P, and that most existing variants use local, non-uniform, and non-isometric projections. The paper then proposes a specific P — a random one-hot partition with column-wise normalization — that is provably isometric, globally shared, and load-balanced, achieving O(D) projection complexity versus O(D log d) for the classical Fastfood transform used by FourierFT.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified framework provides a systematic lens for analyzing and designing future PEFT methods, reducing the design space to the choice of projection matrix and its properties. The extreme parameter efficiency (0.52M trainable parameters for a 7B model, ~0.006% of base model size) while matching or exceeding LoRA performance has practical implications for deployment on resource-constrained devices and multi-adapter scenarios. However, the evaluation is limited to small-to-medium scale models (≤13B) and the performance gains over strong baselines like VeRA and VB-LoRA are often within noise margins, leaving open whether the approach maintains its advantage at 100B+ scale or on more demanding tasks. The isometric property, while theoretically clean, has not been shown to provide a consistent empirical advantage over non-isometric projections in all settings.
