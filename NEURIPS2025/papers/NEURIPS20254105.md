## Problem

While LLMs have been explored for fMRI decoding and zero-shot causal discovery, effective connectivity (EC) estimation from fMRI time series remains entirely unexplored with LLMs. Existing LLM-based causal discovery methods rely solely on iterative pair-wise querying without any model adaptation, making them unable to bridge the cross-modal gap between numerical fMRI signals and language representations or to capture the complex, multiscale temporal dynamics inherent in brain activity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254105/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes raw fMRI BOLD time series from N brain ROIs (e.g., 5–7 regions, 200–500 timepoints for simulated data; 421 timepoints × 7 medial temporal lobe ROIs for real 7T data), which are first normalized via reversible instance normalization. It produces a directed weighted adjacency matrix A ∈ R^{N×N} representing effective connectivity, where A_{ij} encodes the causal influence from region i to region j; for evaluation this is thresholded into a binary directed graph. The LLM input additionally includes structured text prompts encoding the Pearson correlation matrix as prior knowledge, a dataset description, and a task instruction, all tokenized and prepended as a prefix to the fMRI embeddings.

## Method

BrainEC-LLM fine-tunes a pre-trained LLM (Llama3-8B via LoRA) in an autoregressive, unsupervised manner to reconstruct fMRI from itself, with the self-attention weights serving as the EC estimate. The core architectural contribution is the Multiscale Decomposition Mixing (MDM) module: fMRI is downsampled into M scales, each decomposed into short-term (residual) and long-term (trend) components; short-term trends are mixed bottom-up (fine→coarse) and long-term trends top-down (coarse→fine) using ModernTCN blocks that preserve cross-region correlations via patch embedding and grouped ConvFFN. Cross attention then aligns the resulting multiscale fMRI patches with a vocabulary-reduced set of pre-trained word embeddings to bridge the modality gap. The Multiscale Reconstruction Mixing (MRM) module fuses the LLM's multiscale outputs via learned linear projections and summation before self-attention extracts the EC graph. Training combines reconstruction loss, L1 sparsity, an optional DAG constraint, and a novel cross-scale contrastive loss whose optimal value is proven to lower-bound mutual information between adjacent-scale representations.

## Results

On the four simulated fMRI datasets, BrainEC-LLM achieves the best F1 score on all four: 0.67 (Smith), 0.86 (Sanchez), 0.71 (SCN5), and 0.61 (SCN7), with corresponding SHD values of 4.02, 2.10, 4.26, and 11.86. On the real 7T resting-state fMRI dataset, BrainEC-LLM identifies 11 correct connections in the left hemisphere and 12 in the right hemisphere with only 6 spurious edges each. In downstream brain disease classification, BrainEC-LLM achieves the highest F1 of 72.31% on ABIDE I (ASD vs. HC) and 69.82% on ADHD, outperforming all 8 baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BrainEC-LLM outperforms all 8 baselines on F1 across all four simulated datasets, with the largest margins on SCN5 (0.71 vs. 0.53 for CUTS+, the next best) and SCN7 (0.61 vs. 0.56 for RL-EC). On the Sanchez dataset, BrainEC-LLM achieves a dominant SHD of 2.10, nearly halving the error of the next-best method RL-EC (3.95). On Smith, the gap is narrower—BrainEC-LLM's F1 of 0.67 edges out MetaCAE (0.65) and its SHD of 4.02 slightly beats MetaCAE (4.26)—consistent with prior literature noting Smith is a challenging benchmark. On SCN7, RL-EC remains competitive with SHD 11.67 vs. BrainEC-LLM's 11.86, though BrainEC-LLM still leads on F1 (0.61 vs. 0.56) and Accuracy (0.76 vs. 0.75). In downstream classification, BrainEC-LLM's EC networks yield the best F1 on both ABIDE I (72.31% vs. 67.52% for CUTS+) and ADHD (69.82% vs. 66.38% for spDCM), confirming the discriminative quality of the estimated connectivity graphs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of LLM-based causal discovery and brain effective connectivity (EC) estimation. It departs from prior LLM-based causal discovery methods (which rely on iterative zero-shot querying without model adaptation) by introducing the first fine-tuning paradigm for EC estimation, using LoRA on Llama3-8B. Architecturally, it repurposes the series decomposition block from Autoformer [64] and the ModernTCN block [43] for multiscale fMRI trend extraction, and adopts the continuous DAG constraint from Zheng et al. [77] for acyclicity enforcement. The novel contribution is the multiscale decomposition mixing module with bottom-up/top-down mixing and cross-attention alignment to pre-trained word embeddings, which bridges the modality gap between numerical fMRI signals and the LLM's textual pre-training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work demonstrates that fine-tuned LLMs can serve as effective backbones for causal graph estimation from time series, opening a path beyond the inference-only LLM approaches that dominate current causal discovery. The cross-scale contrastive loss with its mutual information lower bound (Theorem 4.1) provides a principled mechanism for enforcing consistency across temporal scales. Key limitations include the performance degradation on the 7-node SCN7 dataset (suggesting scalability concerns for larger brain networks), the acknowledged gap between LLMs' textual pre-training and numerical fMRI data, and the reliance on autoregressive reconstruction in the absence of ground truth. Open questions include whether LLMs with stronger numerical reasoning capabilities would further improve EC estimation, and whether the multiscale mixing paradigm generalizes to other causal discovery domains beyond neuroscience.
