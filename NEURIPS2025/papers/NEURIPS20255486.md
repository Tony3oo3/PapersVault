## Problem

Evaluating a language model's linguistic reasoning is typically confounded with its pre-existing knowledge of a specific language (usually English), making it impossible to isolate genuine meta-linguistic and deductive reasoning skills. Existing benchmarks either test language-specific proficiency or rely on artificial grammars that lack the complexity of natural languages. This paper introduces Linguini, a benchmark built from International Linguistic Olympiad problems across 75 extremely low-resource languages, where all information needed to solve each puzzle is provided in-context, eliminating the need for prior language knowledge.

## Input / Output

Each input consists of a natural-language instruction, a context block containing linguistic data (word lists, example pairs, or morphological paradigms) sufficient to unambiguously solve the problem, and the question itself, optionally preceded by 0–5 in-context examples of the same task format but from a different language to avoid contamination. The 894 questions are grouped into 160 problems spanning three categories—sequence transduction (translation/matching), fill-in-blanks (morphophonological derivation), and digit/text number transliteration—across 75 mostly extremely low-resource languages. Outputs are scored by exact match (primary) and chrF (secondary), with per-problem scores averaged to a 0–100 scale.

## Method

The benchmark is constructed by extracting and structuring problems from the IOL corpus (2003–2023), filtering out image-dependent or explanation-only items, and classifying remaining questions into a taxonomy of linguistic reasoning tasks. Evaluation employs a leave-one-out cross-validation scheme where in-context examples share the same task format but exclude the target language, and models are prompted zero-shot through 5-shot. The core empirical contribution is a systematic comparison of 20+ open and proprietary LLMs, revealing a significant open-vs-closed gap (best open: 8.84% vs. best proprietary: 24.05% exact match). Ablation studies include no-context prompting (showing steep performance drops that rule out training-data contamination), character-wise transliteration into four non-Latin scripts (Cyrillic, Greek, Georgian, Armenian) to test script-independence, and one-book prompting using publicly available grammar textbooks to probe long-context in-context learning. Human evaluation with three annotators on a 0–4 Likert scale validates that exact match is a more appropriate automatic metric than chrF for this task.

## Results

On the Linguini benchmark (894 questions across 75 low-resource languages), all evaluated models score below 25% exact match accuracy. The best-performing proprietary model, Claude 3 Opus, achieves 24.05% at 0 in-context examples (ICEs), while the best-performing open model, Llama 3 70b, reaches only 8.84% at 3 ICEs — a gap of over 15 percentage points. In-context examples have a mixed effect: some models (e.g., Llama 3 70b Instruct) improve with more examples, while others (e.g., Claude 3 Opus) degrade, dropping from 24.05% to 15.1% as ICEs increase from 0 to 5.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates 20 models spanning proprietary (Claude 3 Opus/Sonnet/Haiku, GPT-4o/4/4 Turbo, Gemini 2.5/2.0 Flash, DeepSeek R1, o3-mini) and open-weight (Llama 3/2/4, Mistral, Gemma, Qwen) families. Claude 3 Opus leads all models at 24.05% exact match (0 ICEs), followed by Gemini 2.5 Flash (23.15%) and DeepSeek R1 (19.50%), establishing a clear proprietary-model advantage. Among open models, Llama 3 70b achieves the highest score of 8.84% (at 3 ICEs), trailing the best proprietary model by roughly 15 points. Notably, reasoning models (DeepSeek R1, o3-mini) do not produce a phase change over standard LLMs, remaining comparable to the best non-reasoning proprietary models. The gap between proprietary and open models is further confirmed by ablation experiments: removing context drops Claude 3 Opus from 24.05% to 1.23% (Δ = −22.82), while Llama 3 70b drops from 8.17% to 1.67% (Δ = −6.50), indicating that all models rely heavily on in-context information rather than memorized language knowledge.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Rosetta Stone paradigm for evaluating linguistic reasoning in LLMs, building on PuzzLing Machines (Sahin et al., 2020) and ModeLing (Chi et al., 2024) which focused primarily on machine translation, and on Big-Bench's linguistic mappings task which relied on artificial grammars. It also scales the one-book prompting approach of Tanzer et al. (2024) to additional languages and task types. The key departure from concurrent work (LingOly, Bean et al. 2024) is the use of IOL problems covering 75 mostly extremely low-resource languages with diverse scripts and problem types (morphophonological segmentation, derivation, script transliteration), explicitly requiring no prior language knowledge.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals a striking capability gap: high school students score above 82% on IOL problems while the best frontier model (Claude 3 Opus) reaches only 24%, suggesting linguistic reasoning remains an axis of human intelligence not yet captured by generative models. The no-context ablation and script-transliteration experiments provide strong evidence against data contamination, validating the benchmark's integrity. Open questions include whether reasoning models will eventually close this gap, how to scale the one-book prompting approach to more languages, and whether curated chain-of-thought explanations (noted as absent) could improve model performance on these tasks.
