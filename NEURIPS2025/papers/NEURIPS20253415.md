## Problem

Neural Collapse (NC), the geometric phenomenon where penultimate-layer features and classifiers adopt a simple symmetric structure in deep classification, has been extended to regression and other tasks, but whether an analogous collapse emerges in ordinal regression—where labels carry natural ordering but no quantitative meaning—remained unexplored. This paper asks whether combining the Cumulative Link Model (CLM) for ordinal regression with the Unconstrained Feature Model (UFM) reveals a geometric collapse phenomenon in the feature space of deep ordinal regression networks.

## Input / Output

The method takes tabular features (one-hot encoded categorical attributes concatenated with normalized numerical attributes) or 224×224 face images as input, and produces a single scalar latent variable z = w^T h(x) that is thresholded against an ordered set of values to yield an ordinal class label. Unlike standard classification which outputs a Q-dimensional logit vector, the CLM outputs a one-dimensional latent variable, making the resulting geometric structure fundamentally different from the simplex ETF of standard NC and instead collapsing onto a one-dimensional subspace.

## Method

The paper combines the Cumulative Link Model (CLM) for ordinal regression with the Unconstrained Feature Model (UFM), treating penultimate-layer features as free variables to enable full analytical treatment of the optimization. By assuming log-concavity of the inverse link function's derivative (satisfied by logit, probit, and clog-log models), they prove three properties of Ordinal Neural Collapse (ONC): within-class feature collapse to class means, collapse of all class means onto the one-dimensional subspace spanned by the classifier weight, and ordering of optimal latent variables matching the class order. They derive Equations of State (EOS) governing the optimal latent variables, identify a phase transition between trivial and non-trivial solutions in the (λ_h, λ_w) plane, and show that in the zero-regularization limit with symmetric link functions the optimal latent variable for class q equals the midpoint of its bounding thresholds. Empirical validation on five imbalanced tabular datasets and UTKFace (with ResNet50/101 and DenseNet201 backbones) confirms all three ONC properties, with fixed thresholds yielding the cleanest ONC3 behavior, faster convergence, and better minority-class classification accuracy.

## Results

The paper empirically validates all three ONC properties across five tabular ordinal datasets (ER, LE, SW, CA, WR) and the UTKFace age-estimation dataset. ONC1 and ONC2 metrics (within-class feature collapse and one-dimensional subspace alignment) rapidly approach zero for both fixed and learnable thresholds, confirming the theoretical predictions. For ONC3 (ordinal alignment of latent variables), fixed thresholds yield a near-zero indicator consistent with the predicted relation z_q* = (b_q + b_{q-1})/2, while learnable thresholds converge to a non-zero value. On UTKFace, fixed thresholds dramatically improve classification accuracy and minimum sensitivity compared to learnable thresholds, where minimum sensitivity remains at zero throughout training (indicating at least one class is completely ignored).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical analysis paper without direct comparisons to competing ordinal regression methods; the primary empirical contrast is between fixed and learnable threshold strategies within the same CLM+DNN framework. Fixed thresholds consistently outperform learnable thresholds: on UTKFace with ResNet101, fixed thresholds achieve substantially higher accuracy, QWK, and minimum sensitivity, while learnable thresholds leave at least one class with zero recall across all three backbones (ResNet50, ResNet101, DenseNet201). The fixed-threshold setting also yields faster and more stable convergence on the tabular datasets. The paper further validates its theoretical EOS predictions numerically, confirming the phase transition at λ_h·λ_w = C and the power-law scaling w* = Θ((λ_h/λ_w)^{1/4}) in the small-regularization limit, with the zero-regularization prediction z_q* = (b_q + b_{q-1})/2 matching the observed latent variable positions for symmetric link functions (logit, probit).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Neural Collapse (NC) program—originally developed for balanced classification (Papyan et al., 2020) and formalized via the Unconstrained Feature Model (UFM) by Mixon et al. (2022) and Zhu et al. (2021)—to ordinal regression (OR) for the first time. While prior NC extensions have addressed regression (Andriopoulos et al., 2024), multi-label classification (Li et al., 2024a), and language models (Wu and Papyan, 2024), none have tackled the ordered-label structure of OR. The key conceptual departure is that the CLM's scalar latent variable and threshold structure replace the multi-class logit vector, collapsing the familiar simplex-ETF geometry (NC2) into a one-dimensional subspace alignment (ONC2) and introducing a new ordinal-ordering property (ONC3) with no classification analogue.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first rigorous theoretical characterization of feature-space geometry in deep ordinal regression, establishing that the simple zero-regularization relation z_q* = (b_q + b_{q-1})/2 (for symmetric link functions) is a universal property of the CLM-UFM optimum. A practically important corollary is that fixed thresholds consistently outperform learnable ones—yielding faster convergence and dramatically better minority-class sensitivity on imbalanced datasets like UTKFace—suggesting a simple design choice that practitioners can adopt immediately. Open questions include a rigorous proof that only two phases (trivial vs. non-trivial) exist, a theoretical treatment of learnable thresholds, and the development of a general ONC3 metric applicable beyond evenly-spaced fixed thresholds.
