## Problem

Existing constrained decoding methods for language models—such as grammar-constrained decoding (GCD) and adaptive sampling (ASAp)—enforce hard structural constraints but distort the underlying model distribution, producing samples that do not faithfully represent the true conditional distribution. Rejection sampling preserves the distribution but is impractically inefficient when valid outputs are rare (e.g., <1% acceptance for complex grammars). The paper asks whether a constrained sampler can simultaneously be constraint-satisfying, monotonically converging to the true conditional distribution, and efficient in practice.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pretrained autoregressive language model, a context-free grammar defining the constraint, a text prompt, and a chain length parameter k controlling the number of MCMC refinement steps. A configurable truncation distribution p_POS^w over token positions [0, |w|] determines where in the current sequence to cut and resample, with three concrete choices (Uniform, Priority, Restart) offering different exploration trade-offs. The output is a grammar-valid token sequence approximating the constrained distribution P^G, with every intermediate sample guaranteed to satisfy the grammar by construction via GCD.

## Method

The core contribution is a Metropolis-Hastings MCMC framework where the target distribution is the LM's distribution restricted to grammar-valid sequences, and the proposal distribution generates only valid candidates by selecting a prefix of the current sequence and resampling the remainder via grammar-constrained decoding (GCD). The acceptance ratio compares LM likelihoods of the current and proposed sequences, with the intractable normalization constant of P^G canceling out algebraically, making the criterion computable in practice. Three proposal variants are instantiated: Uniform (random truncation point), Priority (biasing truncation toward high-perplexity positions for targeted refinement), and Restart (always regenerating from scratch, reducing to resampled importance sampling). The paper proves that any proposal assigning non-zero probability to the empty prefix yields a Markov chain that is irreducible, aperiodic, and monotonically converges in total variation distance to P^G, while empirically showing MCMC-Priority achieves the highest branch coverage in real-world fuzzing (36.69% on SQLite, 12.79% on XML) with 10 steps.

## Results

On the SQL fuzzing benchmark (Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, 100 seeds, 6-hour AFL++ runs), MCMC-Priority (k=10) achieves 36.69% branch coverage, outperforming GCD (31.52%) by 1.17× and ASAp (32.61%) by 1.12×. On the XML benchmark, MCMC-Priority (k=10) reaches 12.79% branch coverage versus 11.08% for GCD and 11.30% for ASAp. In a compute-matched comparison, MCMC-Priority with just 50 seeds (34.48% SQL coverage) surpasses GCD with 500 seeds (31.70%), demonstrating that sampling fidelity dominates seed quantity. On synthetic SyGuS benchmarks, MCMC-Restart (k=10) achieves a 5.07× geomean KL-divergence reduction over GCD and 5.42× over ASAp.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MCMC-Priority (k=10) is the strongest variant, beating GCD by 1.17× on SQL branch coverage (36.69% vs. 31.52%) and 1.15× on XML (12.79% vs. 11.08%), and outperforming ASAp by 1.12× and 1.13× respectively. SMC+AWRS (k=10) is the closest competitor, achieving 36.44% SQL coverage (within 0.7% of MCMC-Priority) but trailing by 1.04× on SQL and 1.03× on XML. MCMC-Restart, despite discarding all prior state each step, consistently outperforms MCMC-Uniform and MCMC-Priority on KL divergence across synthetic benchmarks (5.07× vs. 2.11× and 2.42× geomean reduction over GCD at k=10), though it lags slightly on fuzzing coverage. GCD and ASAp both fail to monotonically converge: ASAp's KL divergence is actually worse than GCD's at 100 samples on the SQLite grammar, and neither improves with more compute in the same way MCMC does. Grammarinator, which generates random grammar-valid strings without any LM, achieves the lowest coverage (26.50% SQL, 8.99% XML), confirming that LM-guided sampling is essential for effective fuzzing seeds.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper wraps Grammar-Constrained Decoding (GCD) inside a Metropolis-Hastings loop, using GCD as a constraint-satisfying proposal distribution and the LM's likelihood as the acceptance criterion. It departs from Park et al.'s ASAp, which iteratively corrects GCD's distributional distortion via approximate expected-future-grammaticality but converges slowly and non-monotonically, and from concurrent SMC+AWRS work that uses particle-based sequential Monte Carlo. The key conceptual move is recognizing that GCD's distortion can be corrected by a standard MH acceptance step, turning any GCD implementation into a provably grammar-aligned sampler without modifying the decoder itself.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides the first constrained-sampling method that simultaneously satisfies constraint satisfaction, monotonic convergence, and practical efficiency, with formal proofs for the first two. Its practical impact is demonstrated through higher branch coverage in program fuzzing (AFL++ on SQLite and libxml2), where 50 MCMC seeds outperform 500 GCD seeds under compute-matched budgets. Open questions include whether richer proposal distributions (infilling, grammar-aware, learned) can further accelerate mixing, whether the approach extends to semantic constraints beyond CFGs, and whether the observed inverse correlation between KL divergence and fuzzing coverage reflects a fundamental tension between distributional fidelity and input diversity.
