## Problem

Existing risk-aware bandit methods either optimize a single risk measure under heavy-tailed rewards or handle multiple risk measures only under light-tailed (sub-Gaussian, bounded) assumptions. No prior framework supports general Lipschitz risk measures (CVaR, SRM, DRM, UBSR, CERM) in multi-risk Pareto-optimal bandit problems under heavy-tailed distributions with infinite variance, and no asymptotic lower bounds with explicit sub-optimality gap dependencies existed for SRM and DRM bandits.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes sequential reward observations from K arms drawn from heavy-tailed distributions with bounded p-th moment (p ∈ (1,2]), along with r Lipschitz risk measure specifications and their Lipschitz constants. At each round it outputs a single action selection and maintains an estimated Pareto-optimal set; the MoEQ estimator produces a full empirical CDF (not a point estimate) from which any Lipschitz risk measure can be evaluated, yielding simultaneous confidence intervals for all r risks. The theoretical output includes gap-dependent regret bounds scaling as O(1/Δ^{1/(p−1)} · ln n) and gap-independent bounds scaling as O(n^{1/p}).

## Method

The core estimator is the Median of Empirical Quantiles (MoEQ), which partitions samples into k groups, computes per-group empirical quantile functions, and takes the pointwise median across groups to obtain a robust distribution estimate resistant to heavy-tailed outliers. The key theoretical contribution is a new deviation inequality for the 1-Wasserstein distance under heavy-tailed distributions (p ∈ (1,2]), which via the Lipschitz property of risk measures yields distribution-free confidence intervals for any risk in the Lipschitz class without risk-specific construction. The DistLCB algorithm selects the arm with the lowest risk LCB index, while MR-DistLCB extends this to multi-risk Pareto-optimal selection by checking dominance among r-dimensional LCB vectors. Bootstrap resampling (partial and full augmentation) resolves the computational bottleneck of MoEQ requiring k new samples per update. The approach is risk-measure agnostic: the same MoEQ-based confidence interval applies uniformly to CVaR, SRM, DRM, UBSR, and CERM, enabling the first multi-risk heavy-tailed bandit framework with matching asymptotic and minimax lower bounds.

## Results

On the real-world S&P 500 portfolio selection task (top 20 stocks, 10,000 rounds, 20 seeds), MR-DistLCB achieves the lowest cumulative Pareto regret across all 3-, 6-, and 9-risk configurations, with the advantage most pronounced under the heaviest tails (p=1.5 fixed for all methods). On the synthetic 20-armed Pareto bandit, MR-DistLCB maintains the lowest mean regret and smallest standard deviation across tail indices p ∈ {1.01, 1.2, 1.5}; at the extreme heavy-tail setting p=1.01, its regret is substantially lower and more stable than both baselines. Theoretically, the gap-dependent regret bound O(Σ κ^p/(p−1) (C_p ν_p / Δ_a^ρ)^(1/(p−1)) ln n) matches the newly derived asymptotic lower bound Ω(1/(Δ_a^ρ)^(1/(p−1))), confirming asymptotic optimality, while the gap-independent bound O(κ (C_p ν_p)^(1/p) (K ln n)^((p−1)/p) n^(1/p)) is near-minimax optimal up to a logarithmic factor.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares MR-DistLCB against three baselines: MR Trunc (multi-risk adaptation of the truncated empirical distribution from Bhatt et al. [30]), MR LCB (multi-risk extension of the LCB method from Tan et al. [9]), and SR DistLCB (the single-risk variant of the proposed method). MR-DistLCB outperforms MR Trunc across all risk configurations and tail indices; MR Trunc's conservative truncation rule yields weaker deviation bounds, causing slower convergence particularly for distortion-based risks like Wang and CERM. MR LCB performs the worst among multi-risk methods because its sub-Gaussian concentration assumptions produce miscalibrated confidence intervals under heavy-tailed rewards, leading to unstable action selection. SR DistLCB fails to identify the Pareto-optimal set in the 3-risk setting (where the Pareto set is small) but improves in 6- and 9-risk settings as the Pareto set expands, yet still trails MR-DistLCB, confirming that single-risk exploration is insufficient for multi-risk optimization. The advantage of MR-DistLCB is most pronounced at p=1.01 (near-infinite variance), where the exponential-type deviation bound of MoEQ provides substantially tighter uncertainty control than truncation or sub-Gaussian methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies three previously separate research threads: (1) heavy-tailed bandits with median-of-means estimators (Bubeck et al. [24]), (2) Lipschitz risk measure bandits under light-tailed assumptions (Tan et al. [9], Liang and Luo [10], Bhatt et al. [30]), and (3) multi-objective Pareto bandits (Drugan and Nowe [31]). The key technical departure is the MoEQ estimator, which extends median-of-means from scalar means to full quantile functions, enabling W1-based confidence intervals for arbitrary Lipschitz risk measures under infinite variance (p ∈ (1,2]). This fills the gap left by Bhatt et al.'s truncated empirical distribution, which the paper shows yields looser W1 bounds, and by Tan/Liang, whose W1 concentration results require finite variance or sub-Gaussian tails.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to handle multi-risk Pareto-optimal bandits under heavy-tailed rewards with general Lipschitz risk measures, which is directly relevant to domains like portfolio management and clinical decision-making where both tail heaviness and multiple risk perspectives are unavoidable. The derived asymptotic lower bounds with explicit 1/(p−1) gap dependency for SRM and DRM provide the first theoretical benchmarks for risk-aware bandits beyond CVaR. Key limitations include lower bounds restricted to SRM/DRM (not UBSR or CERM), no lower bounds for truncation-based competitors, and the assumption that the tail parameter p is known a priori. Whether adaptive estimation of p or alternative robust estimators can further tighten the confidence intervals remains open.
