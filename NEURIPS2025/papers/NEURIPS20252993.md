## Problem

Existing mix-based UDA methods for LiDAR segmentation exploit only three structural priors (category semantic integrity, azimuthal consistency, spatial region integrity) while overlooking three critical ones—permutation invariance, local consistency, and geometric consistency. Additionally, voxel-based U-Net backbones have limited receptive fields (~230 voxels), causing many voxels to receive signals from only a single domain and thus failing to learn domain-invariant representations.

## Input / Output

The method consumes raw XYZ coordinates from LiDAR point cloud scans, voxelized at 0.05 m resolution, from a labeled source domain (SynLiDAR) and an unlabeled target domain (SemanticKITTI or SemanticPOSS). It produces per-voxel semantic segmentation predictions over 19 or 14 classes respectively. During training, mixed scans are generated by combining source and target data via LaserMix (or randomly selected from CoSMix/PolarMix/LaserMix in BeyondMix++), and the Mamba module processes features as 1-D sequences ordered by space-filling curves rather than as 3-D voxel grids.

## Method

BeyondMix builds on a MinkUNet34 backbone augmented with Mamba layers that model long-range dependencies in linear time. Three structural priors are enforced via MSE losses: (1) Permutation Invariance—features are reordered by Hilbert and Z-order curves, processed independently by Mamba, and consistency is enforced between the two orderings; (2) Local Consistency—features are partitioned into cylindrical regions (by elevation angle) and rectangular regions (by Z-axis height), each sub-region is Hilbert-ordered, and Mamba outputs from the two partitioning schemes are aligned; (3) Geometric Consistency—a 50% random binary mask creates a density-perturbed subset, and Mamba outputs on the full and masked sets are matched on unmasked points. Training follows a self-training paradigm with EMA-updated teacher pseudo-labels, and the total loss combines source cross-entropy, target pseudo-label cross-entropy, mix cross-entropy, and the three prior losses weighted by λ_prior = 0.01.

## Results

On the SynLiDAR→SemanticKITTI benchmark, BeyondMix++ achieves 46.2% mIoU, surpassing the previous best DGT-ST (43.1%) by +3.1% and the source-only model (20.4%) by +25.8%. On SynLiDAR→SemanticPOSS, BeyondMix++ reaches 53.7% mIoU, outperforming DGT-ST (50.8%) by +2.9%. Notable per-class gains include +58.1% on 'car' and +65.2% on 'road' over source-only on SemanticKITTI, demonstrating strong adaptation on both large and small objects.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BeyondMix++ outperforms all 10 baselines on both benchmarks. Against the strongest prior method DGT-ST [73] (43.1% mIoU on KITTI, 50.8% on POSS), BeyondMix++ gains +3.1% and +2.9% respectively, with consistent per-class improvements across 13 of 19 classes on SemanticKITTI. Among mix-based methods, LaserMix [19] (36.0%) is the strongest single-mix baseline, while CoSMix [48] (29.9%) and PolarMix [68] (31.0%) trail further behind; BeyondMix++ surpasses all three by 10+ points on KITTI. Adversarial methods (ADVENT [59] at 30.5%, FADA [61] at 25.6%, MRNet [78] at 28.3%) and prototype-guided PMAN [72] (33.7%) are substantially outperformed, with gaps of 12–21 mIoU points. On SemanticPOSS, the gap over DGT-ST narrows to +2.9% but BeyondMix++ still leads on most individual classes, particularly 'pole' (37.4% vs. 30.1%) and 'build.' (42.8% vs. 44.1% where DGT-ST slightly leads).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the mix-based UDA paradigm for LiDAR segmentation (CoSMix, PolarMix, LaserMix) by identifying three additional structural priors—permutation invariance, local consistency, and geometric consistency—that prior mixing strategies cannot express. It repurposes Mamba's selective state-space architecture, originally designed for 1D sequence modeling, to process point cloud features serialized via space-filling curves (Hilbert and Z-order), thereby simultaneously constructing these new priors and expanding the effective receptive field beyond the ~230-voxel limit of standard U-Net backbones. The self-training framework with teacher-student pseudo-labeling follows the DGT-ST lineage, while the MinkUNet34 sparse convolution backbone is standard in the LiDAR segmentation literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a useful analytical lens—receptive-field coverage across domains—as the fundamental bottleneck in mix-based UDA, and demonstrates that SSM-based long-range modeling can partially overcome it. However, the evaluation is limited to synthetic-to-real transfer (SynLiDAR→SemanticKITTI/POSS) with no real-to-real experiments, no error bars across seeds, and code not yet released, leaving open whether the gains generalize beyond the synthetic gap. The computational overhead of running Mamba multiple times per forward pass (for permutation, local, and geometric priors) is a practical concern for deployment. Future work could explore whether the structural priors transfer to other 3D tasks or whether lighter SSM variants suffice.
