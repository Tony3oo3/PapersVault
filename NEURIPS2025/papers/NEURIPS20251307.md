## Problem

Single-market dynamic pricing algorithms learn demand models from scratch, which is impractical when new markets launch with only dozens of transactions per day while mature markets of the same firm accumulate data at orders-of-magnitude higher rates. Existing transfer approaches either assume identical reward models across markets (covariate shift only), handle only a single offline source, or lack revenue-maximising price selection, leaving no framework that provably handles structured utility-model discrepancies between multiple source and target markets with minimax-optimal regret for both parametric and nonparametric utility classes.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes contextual covariates x_t^(0) from the target market at each decision point, along with either a fixed offline log or concurrent streaming data (posted prices, covariates, and binary sale indicators) from K source markets. It outputs a posted price p_t^(0) = h(ĝ_m^(0)(x_t^(0))) at each time step, where h is a known revenue-maximising transformation applied to the estimated mean utility. The episodic structure uses doubling episode lengths (ℓ_m = 2^{m-1}), so parameter updates occur only O(log T) times over horizon T, and the method automatically switches from transfer-assisted to pure single-market learning once target data dominate the information budget.

## Method

CM-TDP alternates a two-step bias-corrected aggregation pipeline with an optimistic pricing rule within an episodic framework. In the aggregation step, source-market data are pooled via unregularized MLE (linear utilities) or kernel ridge regression (RKHS utilities) to form an aggregate estimate ĝ^(ag). In the debiasing step, this aggregate is corrected using scarce target-market observations with L1-regularized MLE (exploiting s0-sparse parameter differences) or RKHS-norm-regularized kernel regression (exploiting bounded H-distance between source and target functions), yielding the final target estimate ĝ_m^(0) = ĝ_m^(ag) + δ̂_m. The key insight is that the debiasing step isolates and corrects only the structured utility shift while preserving the statistical efficiency gained from abundant source data, and the doubling-episode schedule ensures progressively tighter confidence bounds with only logarithmically many updates. This architecture cleanly connects meta-learning (prior pooling), robust statistics (trimmed debiasing), and exploration-driven bandits, and is instantiated for both linear and RKHS-smooth nonparametric utility models.

## Results

In the sparse-difference market scenario (the primary target of the algorithm design), CM-TDP-O2Oon reduces cumulative regret by 54% (linear) and 55% (RKHS) on average across dimensions d∈{10,15,20}, with peak reductions reaching 71–73% at K=10 source markets. Standard error drops by 31% (linear) and 29% (RKHS) on average, and learning speed reaches 6.0× (linear) and 6.2× (RKHS) relative to the single-market baseline. Theoretically, CM-TDP achieves minimax-optimal regret: Õ((d/K + s₀)log T) for linear utilities and Õ(K^(−2αβ/(2αβ+1))·T^(1/(2αβ+1)) + H^(2/(2α+1))·T^(1/(2α+1))) for RKHS utilities, both matching information-theoretic lower bounds up to logarithmic factors.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares CM-TDP against a single-market dynamic pricing baseline [19] (Javanmard & Nazerzadeh, JMLR 2019) that learns the demand model from target-market data only. CM-TDP wins decisively across all tested configurations: in the O2Oon setting with K=10 source markets, it reduces cumulative regret by 71% (linear) and 73% (RKHS) versus the baseline, while even a single source market (K=1) already yields 15–17% regret reduction. The O2Ooff variant (static offline log) also outperforms the baseline in every scenario, with 43–45% average regret reduction, though the gains are smaller than O2Oon since transfer is phased and asymptotic rates eventually match single-market learning. Gains grow monotonically with the number of source markets K (or offline log size n_K), confirming the theoretical prediction that each additional source provides linear regret reduction in the source-constrained regime. The single-market baseline is the only comparison; the paper does not benchmark against other transfer methods (e.g., Meta-DP [4] or TLDP [30]) empirically, though it distinguishes itself from them theoretically.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the transfer-learning-for-pricing line initiated by Meta-DP (Bastani et al., 2022) and TLDP (Wang et al., 2025), both of which handle only offline data and either identical reward models or pure covariate shift. CM-TDP departs from these by tolerating structured utility-model shift across multiple source markets, operating in both offline-to-online and online-to-online regimes, and covering both linear and RKHS-smooth nonparametric utilities. The bias-corrected aggregation pipeline (unregularized MLE/KRR on pooled source data followed by ℓ₁- or kernel-regularized debiasing on target data) adapts robust-statistics techniques from multitask bandits (MOLAR, Xu & Bastani) to the revenue-maximizing price-selection problem, a setting those methods do not address.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The minimax-optimal regret guarantees—particularly the first RKHS transfer-pricing bound matching information-theoretic lower bounds—provide a rigorous foundation for deploying cross-market pricing in data-scarce settings (new ride-share cities, small e-commerce categories). Key limitations include the homogeneous-covariate assumption (source and target share the same feature distribution), the requirement of known noise distribution, and the absence of mechanisms to detect or exclude adversarial source markets. Open directions flagged by the authors include extending to domain shift via reweighting or invariant representations, richer similarity metrics beyond ℓ₀-sparsity, and robust market-selection strategies.
