## Problem

Diffusion model sampling faces a fundamental trade-off: ODE solvers are computationally efficient under low NFE budgets but irreversibly accumulate gradient error from imperfect score approximations, while SDE solvers can dissipate this error through stochasticity but require 100–1000 steps to do so. The paper asks whether SDE-based error resilience can be achieved at ODE-level efficiency (fewer than 10 NFEs) by adaptively controlling the amount of injected noise at each step.

## Input / Output

AdaSDE operates as a plug-in module on top of a frozen pretrained score network, consuming Gaussian noise (optionally conditioned on a class label or text prompt) and producing a generated image at resolutions ranging from 32×32 (CIFAR-10) to 512×512 (Stable Diffusion on MS-COCO). The method requires only 8–40 learnable scalar parameters per step (γ, ξ, λ, μ), trained via lightweight distillation on ~10K images in 5–20 minutes on a single GPU, without modifying the underlying diffusion model.

## Method

AdaSDE replaces the deterministic ODE update at each denoising step with a two-phase operation: a forward Gaussian perturbation scaled by a learnable coefficient γ_n (pushing the state to a higher noise level t+(1+γ_n)Δt), followed by a deterministic backward ODE step back to t_n. The theoretical analysis (Theorems 1–3) shows this stochastic injection contracts the gradient-error term by a factor (1−λ(γ)) via Gaussian convolution, while the discretization error grows as O(Δt·e^{CΔt}), so the optimal γ balances these opposing effects. The per-step parameters {γ_n, ξ_n, λ_n, μ_n} are optimized through a process-supervised distillation framework: a teacher trajectory with M intermediate steps between each student step provides reference states, and the student trajectory is aligned to the teacher at every intermediate timestep using MSE (intermediate) and LPIPS (final) losses, enabling stagewise backpropagation over the full N-step chain.

## Results

At 5 NFE, AdaSDE achieves FID scores of 4.18 on CIFAR-10 (32×32), 8.05 on FFHQ (64×64), 6.90 on ImageNet (64×64), and 6.96 on LSUN Bedroom (256×256), representing a 1.8× improvement over the prior SOTA AMED-Solver on CIFAR-10. On Stable Diffusion v1.5 (MS-COCO 512×512), AdaSDE reaches FID 12.68 at 10 NFE, surpassing DPM-Solver++(2M) (14.58) and AMED-Plugin (13.24). The advantage widens at lower step counts: at 3 NFE on LSUN Bedroom, AdaSDE achieves 18.03 FID versus 58.21 for AMED-Solver.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AdaSDE outperforms all 8 baselines across every dataset and NFE setting tested. Against the closest competitor AMED-Solver [20] (a training-based ODE solver), AdaSDE achieves 4.18 vs 7.59 FID at 5 NFE on CIFAR-10 (a 45% relative reduction), 8.05 vs 10.74 on FFHQ, 6.90 vs 10.74 on ImageNet, and 6.96 vs 13.20 on LSUN Bedroom. Multi-step solvers (DPM-Solver++ [22], UniPC [33], iPNDM [32]) are also surpassed at low NFE: at 5 NFE on CIFAR-10, AdaSDE (4.18) beats iPNDM (13.59) by 3.2×. On Stable Diffusion v1.5 (MS-COCO), AdaSDE at 10 NFE (12.68) edges out DPM-Solver-v3 [51] (15.32) and DPM-Solver++(2M) (14.58), though it trails AMED-Plugin (13.24) at 8 NFE. The paper's own ODE-based DPM-Plugin is consistently outperformed by AdaSDE, confirming that the adaptive stochastic coefficient provides meaningful gains over purely deterministic single-step sampling.

## Relation to Prior Work

AdaSDE extends the midpoint-based correction framework of AMED-Solver [20] from ODEs to SDEs, and adopts DPM-Solver's [21] square-root intermediate timestep construction for geometric interpolation. Its theoretical error analysis builds directly on the Wasserstein-1 decomposition from Restart Sampling [23] and Dalalyan & Karagulyan [44], but applies it to a new setting: a single-step SDE update with a learnable noise coefficient. The key conceptual departure from prior few-step solvers (DPM-Solver, UniPC, iPNDM, AMED-Solver) is that all of those are ODE-based, whereas AdaSDE is the first to demonstrate that a carefully controlled, per-step stochastic injection can outperform deterministic solvers in the <10 NFE regime by contracting accumulated gradient error.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: AdaSDE is a lightweight plug-in (8–40 learnable parameters) that can be attached to existing single-step solvers without retraining the base diffusion model, and it achieves SOTA FID at 5 NFE across pixel-space and latent-space benchmarks. The clean theoretical decomposition—showing that stochastic injection contracts gradient error via a (1−λ(γ)) factor while inflating discretization error—gives practitioners a principled handle on when and how much noise to inject. Open questions include whether the geometric-alignment assumption (consistent low-dimensional trajectory structure across solvers) holds for more complex or high-dimensional generative tasks, and whether the method's per-step distribution resets can be reconciled with multistep or predictor-corrector frameworks.
