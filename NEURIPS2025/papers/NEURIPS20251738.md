## Problem

Existing defenses against harmful fine-tuning of LLMs rely on preemptive attack simulation to build robustness, but this approach cannot anticipate unknown attacks beyond bounded threat models and fails to adapt to varying attack settings. The paper addresses this by proposing a post hoc, adaptive defense that requires no attack simulation and tailors its protection to the specific dataset encountered at fine-tuning time.

## Input / Output

The method takes a user-provided fine-tuning dataset (a mix of benign and harmful prompt-response pairs of arbitrary size), a safety alignment dataset of harmful prompt–safe answer pairs (e.g., BeaverTails), and the base LLM parameters (fine-tuned via LoRA). It produces per-datapoint safety weights sampled from a learned posterior and the resulting fine-tuned model. The Amortized Bayesian Neural Scheduler variant additionally outputs a lightweight 125M-parameter neural network that can assign weights to entirely new data via a single forward pass, enabling zero-shot transfer without retraining.

## Method

BDS formulates harmful fine-tuning defense as a Bayesian inference problem, learning the posterior p(w, θ | D_ft, D_safe) where w_i encodes each data point's safety attribute. Posterior sampling is performed via Stochastic Gradient Langevin Dynamics (SGLD), jointly updating model parameters θ and data weights w in an alternating fashion. The key design choice is a softmax weight transformation σ(w_i), which—unlike identity or sigmoid—enables adaptively bidirectional weight updates: data points with loss below the weighted-average reference gain weight while those above lose it, naturally separating benign from harmful data. Two implementations are provided: a Bayesian Scalar Scheduler that infers per-datapoint weights (scaling with dataset size), and an Amortized Bayesian Neural Scheduler that amortizes inference into a single neural network posterior p(φ | D_ft, D_safe), allowing efficient transfer to new data. The fine-tuning objective (Eq. 7) jointly optimizes safety alignment loss and weighted fine-tuning loss, resembling a multi-task learning paradigm that balances harmlessness and helpfulness without requiring any simulated harmful dataset.

## Results

On the main comparison (|D_safe|=1000, |D_ft|=1000), BDS achieves an average Harmful Score of 1.34 and average Finetune Accuracy of 93.83% across harmful ratios p=0 to 0.2, outperforming the SOTA baseline Booster (HS 10.94, FA 93.03%) by 87.8% in harmful score reduction. Under high harmful ratios (p=0.3 to 1.0), BDS maintains a harmful score of 1.2–1.8 while Booster degrades to 40.6–76.6, yielding a 74.4% improvement at p=0.9. BDS also requires no simulated harmful dataset (D_harmful), unlike Booster, Vaccine, and RepNoise.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BDS dominates all five baselines on both Harmful Score and Finetune Accuracy across every harmful ratio tested. Against the strongest baseline Booster (which requires a simulated harmful dataset), BDS reduces the average harmful score from 10.94 to 1.34 (87.8% reduction) while simultaneously improving fine-tuning accuracy from 93.03% to 93.83%. The gap widens dramatically at high harmful ratios: at p=0.9, Booster's harmful score surges to 75.90 while BDS remains at 1.50. Simulation-based methods (Booster, Vaccine) degrade sharply as p increases because their preemptive attack simulation cannot capture the variability of post-hoc attacks. Data-mixing methods (Lisa) and unlearning methods (RepNoise) lack an explicit de-weighting mechanism and thus cannot adapt to unknown harmful proportions. Notably, BDS is the only method that does not require a harmful dataset (D_harmful=✗), making it more practical since constructing such a dataset is infeasible for unknown attacks.

## Relation to Prior Work

BDS departs fundamentally from the dominant paradigm in harmful fine-tuning defense, where prior methods (Booster, Vaccine, RepNoise) build robustness through preemptive attack simulation or hard-label data selection via heuristic thresholds. Instead, it reframes the defense as a Bayesian posterior inference problem over per-datapoint safety weights, using SGLD sampling to jointly optimize model parameters and data weights. The neural scheduler variant draws directly on amortized Bayesian meta-learning (Ravi & Beatson, ICLR 2019), amortizing datapoint-wise posterior inference into a single shared network posterior for transferability. The softmax weight transformation, which enables adaptive bidirectional weight updates relative to a weighted-average loss reference, is a key design departure from the monotonically decreasing behavior of identity or sigmoid transformations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for attack simulation, BDS addresses a fundamental practical limitation of prior defenses: the infeasibility of anticipating unknown attacks and the poor adaptability of simulation-based methods to varying attack settings. The remarkable consistency of harmful scores (around 1) across harmful ratios from 0 to 1.0, three model architectures, five fine-tuning tasks, and advanced attack types (OOD, identity-shifting) suggests a more principled and robust defense mechanism than prior approaches. Open questions include scalability to very large fine-tuning datasets (the scalar scheduler's per-datapoint weights scale linearly), the quality of the MAP approximation in Eq. (4), and whether the Bayesian formulation degrades under severe distribution shift between the alignment dataset and the fine-tuning domain.
