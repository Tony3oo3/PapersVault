## Problem

Standard message-passing GNNs are limited in separating power by the 1-dimensional Weisfeiler-Leman test, while higher-order k-GNNs that overcome this limitation require exponential space (n^k node features). The paper asks whether the Individualization-Refinement paradigm from graph isomorphism solvers can yield a GNN architecture that matches or exceeds the separating power of k-GNNs at substantially lower space cost, and what the precise logical characterization of such a model is.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253226/cropped_page4_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a labeled graph G = (V, E, lab) with binary node features from a fixed set P, and produces D'-dimensional node embeddings and binary node classifiers. The key structural detail is that at each nesting level, the graph is temporarily modified to carry a unique marker on the target node v (via a Kronecker-delta extension of the embedding), and the inner GNN operates either on the full graph or on the radius-r induced subgraph G_v^r around v. The nesting depth d controls how many such individualization layers are composed, and the radius r (in HES-GNNs) bounds the subgraph size, yielding O(n·(k^r+1)^{d-1}) generated subgraphs for a graph of degree k.

## Method

HE-GNNs recursively compose node individualization with message-passing: a depth-d HE-GNN is a pair (B, C) where B is a depth-(d-1) HE-GNN and C is a standard GNN; for each node v, B is run on the graph with v uniquely marked, and the resulting embedding is concatenated with v's original embedding before C is applied. The subgraph-restricted variant (HES-GNN) replaces the full graph in B's input with the radius-r neighborhood G_v^r, reducing computational cost. The paper's central theoretical contribution is a logical characterization: HE-GNN-d has exactly the separating power of graded hybrid logic with d nested variable binders (GML(↓^d)), and HES-GNN-(d,r) corresponds to graded hybrid subgraph logic GML(↓_{W^r}^d). This places HE-GNNs within the WL hierarchy (bounded above by (d+1)-WL) while showing they strictly exceed d-WL, and establishes that common subgraph-GNNs (ID-GNNs, Nested GNNs) are special cases of depth-1 HES-GNNs. The model also subsumes homomorphism-count-enriched GNNs: for rooted graphs of ego-rank ≤ d, a depth-d HE-GNN can compute the corresponding homomorphism count vectors using only Sum aggregation and ReLU-FFNNs.

## Results

On ZINC-12k, HES-GNN with depth 2 and radius 3 achieves the best mean absolute error of 0.140, outperforming the homomorphism-count-enriched F-GNN (0.154) and all standard GNN baselines (GIN 0.193, GCN 0.216, PNA 0.207). On the strongly regular graph isomorphism classification task, depth-2 HE-GNNs achieve near-perfect precision and generalize to unseen isomorphisms, whereas ID-GNN, F-GNN, and 3-GNNs perform at chance level since these graphs are 2-WL-indistinguishable. HES-GNN (2,3) matches the performance of F-HES-GNN (1,3) (0.140), a depth-1 model augmented with homomorphism counts, without requiring explicit homomorphism features.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HES-GNN (2,3) achieves the best ZINC-12k MAE of 0.140, beating F-GNN (0.154) by 0.014, GIN (0.193) by 0.053, GCN (0.216) by 0.076, PNA (0.207) by 0.067, GNN+Tinhofer (0.199) by 0.059, and GNN+randomID (0.226) by 0.086. It ties F-HES-GNN (1,3) at 0.140, showing that hierarchical individualization at depth 2 subsumes the benefit of explicit homomorphism count features. On the strongly regular graph task, depth-2 HE-GNNs and HES-GNNs (d=2, r=1) successfully distinguish all four parameter settings and generalize to unseen isomorphisms, while ID-GNN, F-GNN (with C3–C10 cycle counts), and 3-GNNs all perform at chance level. GNN+Tinhofer achieves 100% training precision but fails to generalize to the test set because Tinhofer orderings are not isomorphism-invariant on these graphs. The paper also shows theoretically that HE-GNN-1 is strictly more separating than WL-IR-1 (Theorem 5.5), and that HE-GNNs with depth d are upper-bounded by (d+2)-GNNs / (d+1)-WL (Theorem 5.11) while strictly exceeding d-WL / (d+1)-GNN (Theorem 5.12).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies several previously separate lines of work on expressive GNNs under a single architectural principle: hierarchical node individualization alternating with message passing, directly inspired by the Individualization-Refinement (IR) paradigm from graph isomorphism solvers. It shows that standard subgraph-GNNs (ID-GNNs, ego-network GNNs) are special cases of depth-1 HE-GNNs, that homomorphism-count-enriched GNNs are subsumed by HE-GNNs of depth equal to the ego-rank of the target structures, and that the entire hierarchy is logically characterized by graded hybrid logic (GML↓), extending the known GML–GNN–WL equivalences. The key departure from prior work is the hierarchical (sequential) composition of individualized message-passing, as opposed to the parallel aggregation over individualized graphs used in IR or the global pooling in nested GNNs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean, unified logical framework (graded hybrid logic) that simultaneously characterizes the separating power of HE-GNNs, subgraph-GNNs, and their relationship to the WL hierarchy and higher-order GNNs, giving practitioners a precise map of which architectures can distinguish which graph pairs. The space-complexity advantage over k-GNNs (n² vs n^k stored embeddings) is practically significant, though the exponential message-passing cost in nesting depth limits applicability to small d. Open questions include whether the hierarchy is strict at every depth, whether the ego-rank conditions for homomorphism counting are necessary, and how IR-style optimizations (automorphism pruning, informed cell selection) can be adapted to the learning setting to make deeper HE-GNNs tractable.
