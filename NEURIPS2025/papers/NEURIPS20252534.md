## Problem

One-step generative models (Consistency Models, Shortcut, IMM) impose consistency constraints on the network's own outputs rather than on an underlying ground-truth field, making training unstable and dependent on carefully designed discretization curricula. No principled target field existed that would naturally enable a single network evaluation to approximate the full flow path without extra heuristics or distillation from a pre-trained multi-step model.

## Input / Output

The model takes a random noise vector ε ~ N(0,I) in the VAE latent space (32×32×4 for 256×256 ImageNet) and an optional class label, and produces a single generated latent in one forward pass (1-NFE). The network is additionally conditioned on two scalar time variables (r, t) via positional embeddings, which encode the start and end of the time interval over which the average velocity is computed. For 1-step sampling, the fixed values r=0 and t=1 are used, so the entire noise-to-data displacement is obtained from one evaluation of u_θ(ε, 0, 1).

## Method

MeanFlow introduces the average velocity u(z_t, r, t) = (1/(t−r))∫_r^t v(z_τ, τ) dτ as a new ground-truth field, in contrast to the instantaneous velocity v modeled by standard Flow Matching. The key theoretical contribution is the MeanFlow Identity: u = v − (t−r)·du/dt, derived by differentiating the integral definition, which provides a training target that requires only the conditional velocity v_t and a Jacobian-vector product (JVP) of the network output, with no integral evaluation. The network u_θ is trained with a stop-gradient regression loss against this target, where the JVP is computed via forward-mode autodiff (jax.jvp / torch.func.jvp) at a cost of roughly one extra backward pass (~16% overhead). Classifier-free guidance is incorporated into the ground-truth field definition itself (v^cfg = ωv|c + (1−ω)v), so the network directly models the guided average velocity and sampling remains 1-NFE without a second forward pass.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256, MeanFlow-XL/2 achieves an FID of 3.43 with a single function evaluation (1-NFE), trained entirely from scratch. In 2-NFE generation, MeanFlow-XL/2+ reaches FID 2.20, matching multi-step baselines like DiT-XL/2 (FID 2.27) and SiT-XL/2 (FID 2.06) that require 500 NFE. On CIFAR-10 (32×32, pixel space), MeanFlow achieves FID 2.92 with 1-NFE using a ~55M U-Net without any preconditioner. The method requires no pre-training, distillation, or curriculum learning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MeanFlow-XL/2 (FID 3.43, 1-NFE) outperforms the previous state-of-the-art one-step flow model Shortcut-XL/2 (FID 10.60) by ~67% relative improvement, and iCT-XL/2 (FID 34.24) by ~90%. In the 2-NFE setting, MeanFlow-XL/2+ (FID 2.20) surpasses IMM-XL/2 (FID 7.77, which uses 2-NFE guidance) by ~72% and iCT-XL/2 (FID 20.30) by ~89%. At 2-NFE, MeanFlow is on par with multi-step diffusion/flow models: DiT-XL/2 (FID 2.27, 500 NFE) and SiT-XL/2 (FID 2.06, 500 NFE), using only 2 function evaluations versus 500. On CIFAR-10, MeanFlow (FID 2.92) is competitive with iCT (2.83) and sCT (2.97), slightly trailing iCT but outperforming ECT (3.60) and IMM (3.20). Notably, MeanFlow is self-contained—trained from scratch without distillation or curriculum—whereas iCT and Shortcut rely on carefully designed training curricula.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Flow Matching framework (Lipman et al., ICLR 2023), which models instantaneous velocity fields to transport a prior to a data distribution. It departs from prior one-step generation methods—Consistency Models (Song et al.), Shortcut Models (Frans et al.), and IMM (Zhou et al.)—which impose heuristic consistency constraints on network outputs at different time steps. The key conceptual departure is introducing average velocity as a new ground-truth field and deriving the MeanFlow Identity purely from its definition, eliminating the need for explicit consistency constraints, curriculum learning, or distillation that prior methods require.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MeanFlow substantially narrows the gap between one-step and multi-step generative models: its 2-NFE result (2.20 FID) rivals 500-NFE DiT/SiT models, while its 1-NFE result (3.43 FID) represents a 50–70% relative improvement over prior one-step SOTA. The framework is self-contained (no pre-training, distillation, or curriculum), and the only additional training cost is a single JVP pass (<20% overhead), making it practically attractive. Open questions include whether orthogonal improvements like REPA compose cleanly, whether the approach extends to video or other modalities, and whether the JVP computation remains tractable at much larger scales or higher resolutions.
