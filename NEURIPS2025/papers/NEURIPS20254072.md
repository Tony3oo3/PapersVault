## Problem

Sampling from Transformer temporal point processes (TPPs) is inherently sequential: the standard thinning or autoregressive procedure requires a full forward pass through the Transformer encoder for every single accepted event, making generation prohibitively slow as model size grows. While speculative decoding (SD) has been shown to accelerate token generation in LLMs by verifying multiple draft tokens in parallel, no analogous parallelization strategy existed for continuous-time event sequence sampling.

## Input / Output

The method takes a trained large target Transformer TPP (e.g., 8-head, 20-layer) and a smaller draft Transformer TPP (e.g., 1-head, 1-layer), along with a draft length γ (number of candidate events to propose per iteration) and a terminal time T. It outputs a marked event sequence S = {(t_i, k_i)} where each event consists of a continuous timestamp and a discrete event type, distributed identically to standard autoregressive sampling. A key distinction from LLM-SD is that the inter-event interval is a continuous random variable, requiring an acceptance-rejection scheme to sample from the adjusted distribution after rejection, rather than a simple discrete resampling step.

## Method

TPP-SD adapts the propose-verify framework of speculative decoding to the TPP domain by exploiting the structural analogy between the thinning algorithm and SD. A CDF-based Transformer TPP with a log-normal mixture decoder for inter-event intervals and a categorical head for event types is used as both target and draft model. In each iteration, the draft model autoregressively generates γ candidate (interval, type) pairs while recording their CDFs; the target model then evaluates all γ candidates in a single parallel forward pass. Candidate events are accepted sequentially via rejection sampling against the ratio of target-to-draft CDFs, and upon the first rejection, a replacement is drawn from an adjusted distribution norm(max(0, g_T − g_D)). The paper proves (Theorem 1) that this adjusted continuous distribution can be sampled without computing its normalizing constant by using the target density itself as a proposal in an acceptance-rejection step, which is the main technical difference from the discrete LLM-SD setting.

## Results

TPP-SD achieves a 1.3–5.7× speedup over autoregressive (AR) sampling on synthetic datasets (Poisson, Hawkes, Multi-Hawkes) and a 1.6–5.9× speedup on real datasets (Taobao, Amazon, Taxi, StackOverflow), while maintaining distributional equivalence as confirmed by low likelihood discrepancies (ΔL) and near-zero KS/Wasserstein distances. The highest speedup (5.74×) is observed with the AttNHP encoder on the Hawkes dataset, while the lowest (1.28×) occurs with SAHP on Poisson. Ablation studies show that moderate draft lengths (γ ≈ 5–15) and a minimal 1-head, 1-layer draft model yield the best speedup without degrading sample quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is standard autoregressive (AR) sampling, which generates events one at a time using only the target model. TPP-SD matches AR sampling in distributional fidelity across all seven datasets and three encoder architectures: likelihood discrepancies remain low (typically <1.0) and KS/Wasserstein distances are near zero, confirming both methods sample from the same underlying distribution. The key advantage is purely in speed: TPP-SD consistently reduces wall-time by 2–6×, with the largest gains (5.7×) on the AttNHP encoder where the target model's per-event forward pass is most expensive. Speedup is inversely correlated with event-type cardinality—datasets with more event types (StackOverflow K=22, Taobao K=17) show lower speedups (~1.6–2.2×) than those with fewer (Taxi K=10, Amazon K=16, reaching up to 5.8×) because higher cardinality increases draft-target divergence and rejection rates. No comparison against other sampling accelerators (e.g., inverse-method or triangular-map approaches) is provided, as those are not applicable to CDF-based Transformer TPPs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts speculative decoding (SD) from the LLM literature (Leviathan et al. 2023; Chen et al. 2023) to the domain of Transformer temporal point process (TPP) sampling, identifying a structural analogy between the thinning algorithm's propose-verify loop and SD's draft-verify framework. The CDF-based model design draws on intensity-free TPP learning (Shchur et al. 2020) and decomposable Transformer point processes (Panos 2024), while the encoder backbones (THP, SAHP, AttNHP) are standard Transformer TPP architectures. A key technical departure from LLM-SD is the need to handle continuous distributions in the adjusted-distribution sampling step, for which the authors adapt acceptance-rejection sampling inspired by continuous speculative decoding for image generation (Wang et al. 2024).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TPP-SD provides a practical 2–6× speedup for sampling from Transformer TPPs while provably preserving the target distribution, addressing a real bottleneck in applications requiring large-scale event sequence generation (synthetic data, goodness-of-fit validation, counterfactual reasoning). The main open questions include whether the draft model can be integrated into the target model (à la Medusa) or whether feature-level rather than event-level speculation could yield further gains, as the authors note in their future work. A practical limitation is that the speedup depends on draft-target alignment and degrades with higher event-type cardinality, and the method requires training and maintaining a separate draft model.
