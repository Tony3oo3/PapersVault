## Problem

Classifier-free guidance (CFG) is essential for text-to-image diffusion models, but existing guidance variants (CFG, CFG++, Z-sampling, Resampling) derive from divergent theoretical interpretations, leaving the design space fragmented and key choices like iteration count and consistency interval unexplored. The paper identifies that all existing methods reduce to single-step, short-interval fixed point iterations—a strategy proven theoretically suboptimal—and asks how to systematically explore and upgrade guidance design from a unified perspective.

## Input / Output

The method operates entirely at inference time on pre-trained diffusion models (SDXL, DiT, Hunyuan-DiT, SD2.1) without retraining. It takes a text prompt and standard Gaussian initial noise x_T ~ N(0,I), then modifies the denoising trajectory by inserting multi-step fixed point calibration at selected timesteps before each unconditional denoising step. It is sampler-agnostic (works with both deterministic DDIM and stochastic DDPM) and scales with the NFE budget (50/100/150), producing a single RGB image per prompt.

## Method

The paper reframes conditional guidance as calibration toward a 'golden path'—latents x̂_t where unconditional and conditional denoising from x̂_t yield the same clean image—and formalizes this as solving a fixed point equation f^u_{t→0}(x_t) = f^c_{t→0}(x_t). It unifies CFG, CFG++, Z-sampling, and Resampling as instances of this framework with specific choices of fixed point operator (linear vs. backward-forward), consistency interval, and iteration count. The key proposal, Foresight Guidance (FSG), allocates multiple fixed point iterations over longer time intervals (Δt ∈ [0.02T, 0.125T]) preferentially in early diffusion stages (ratio ~3:2:1 across early/mid/late), using a forward-backward operator f^u_{t−Δt→t} ∘ f^γ_{t→t−Δt} that empirically exhibits lower contraction rates than short-interval operators. A theoretical bound (Theorem 1) shows the optimal number of subproblems M* is typically less than T, justifying fewer but longer-interval iterations. Outside scheduled foresight steps, FSG applies standard CFG++ calibration to maintain stable guidance strength, then denoises using only the unconditional noise prediction ε^u(x̂_t).

## Results

On SDXL with NFE=50, FSG achieves IR 82.81 and HPSv2 29.42 on DrawBench, and IR 98.59 and HPSv2 28.89 on Pick-a-Pic, outperforming the strongest baseline (Z-Sampling: IR 72.75 / 96.77) by +10.06 and +1.82 IR respectively. On Geneval (NFE=150), FSG reaches 57.95% overall instruction compliance, surpassing Z-Sampling (56.70%) and Resampling (56.65%). On ImageNet 256×256 with DiT (NFE=50), FSG achieves FID 7.91 and Vendi score 5.79, the best among all methods tested.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FSG consistently outperforms all four guidance baselines (CFG, CFG++, Z-Sampling, Resampling) across every dataset and NFE setting. At the matched NFE=50 budget, FSG improves IR by +23.79 over CFG, +17.60 over CFG++, +10.06 over Z-Sampling, and +22.82 over Resampling on DrawBench. The gap narrows at NFE=150 (FSG IR 88.18 vs CFG×3 83.56, Z-Sampling 78.35), confirming that the advantage is not merely from extra compute. On Geneval, FSG's 57.95% overall score beats Z-Sampling (56.70%) and Resampling (56.65%), with particularly large gains in counting (+23.75% over CFG) and two-object generation (+23.23% over CFG). Against the orthogonal methods, FSG alone surpasses NPNet (IR 98.59 vs 91.66) but falls short of SPO (IR 98.59 vs 111.86); however, combining FSG with either yields synergistic improvements (IR 112.64 with NPNet, 117.93 with SPO), demonstrating that the fixed-point calibration framework is complementary to preference-aligned and noise-optimization approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reinterprets classifier-free guidance (CFG) and its variants (CFG++, Z-sampling, Resampling) as instances of a unified fixed point iteration framework, departing from prior work that derived each method from distinct theoretical perspectives (sharpened distributions, posterior sampling, reflective sampling). It builds directly on DDIM's deterministic update rule to extract the fixed point operator structure, and connects the 'golden path' phenomenon from the noise-optimization literature (Zhou et al., Qi et al.) to CFG for the first time. The key conceptual departure is decoupling calibration (guidance) from denoising (sampling), which prior methods kept entangled, thereby exposing design dimensions (interval length, iteration count, strength scheduler) that were previously implicit or fixed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified fixed point framework provides a principled design space for guidance mechanisms, enabling systematic comparison and combination of previously incompatible approaches. The theoretical result (Theorem 1) showing that allocating iterations to fewer, longer intervals is provably superior to the standard per-timestep single-step strategy offers a concrete recipe for more efficient guidance. However, the practical gains, while consistent across SDXL, DiT, and Hunyuan-DiT, remain moderate (IR +10–15 over CFG at equal NFE), and the optimal interval/iteration schedule is still heuristic rather than fully adaptive. Open questions include whether the framework extends to video diffusion, autoregressive models, or settings where the contraction assumption (Assumption 4) may fail.
