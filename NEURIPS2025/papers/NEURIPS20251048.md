## Problem

Flow-based stochastic variational inference has been restricted to fixed-dimensional parameter spaces, yet many critical Bayesian problems—variable selection, DAG discovery, mixture-component inference—are inherently transdimensional, with parameter dimension varying across a discrete model index. No existing method enables a single flow-based variational density to approximate a target distribution whose support spans models of differing dimensionality, leaving a gap between the expressiveness of normalizing flows and the needs of multimodel Bayesian inference.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an unnormalized conditional target density η(θ_m|m) whose dimension d_m varies per model m in a finite discrete set M, along with prior distributions p(m) and p(θ_m|m). It produces a single transdimensional variational density q(m,θ_m) = q_φ(θ_m|m)·q_ψ(m) on the unified d_max-dimensional saturated space, where the auxiliary coordinates (those not active under model m) are analytically marginalized out. For DAG discovery, the model space is the set of all acyclic adjacency matrices over N_d nodes, parameterized via topological ordering and strictly upper-triangular matrices, with q_ψ implemented as a MADE autoregressive distribution over the edge indicators.

## Method

CoSMIC (COntextually-Specified Masking for Identity-mapped Components) flows extend inverse autoregressive flows by introducing a context-dependent mask C(m) that, for each coordinate i, convexly blends the learned transform parameters ρ_i with a static identity point ρ^Id: ρ_i^C = (1−C_i(m))ρ^Id + C_i(m)ρ_i. This guarantees that auxiliary coordinates (inactive under model m) are mapped by the identity, so the saturated d_max-dimensional variational density factorizes exactly into the active d_m-dimensional density times an i.i.d. reference on the auxiliary block, collapsing the KL loss to a comparison on the natural d_m-dimensional space. Model weights q_ψ(m) are optimized via two complementary strategies: a Gaussian-process surrogate with upper-confidence-bound sampling (yielding an O_P(t^{−1/2}) KL convergence guarantee for small |M|), or Monte Carlo gradient estimation with control variates applied to categorical or MADE-parameterized distributions (scaling to |M| up to 2^24). The joint optimization of flow parameters φ and model weights ψ is stabilized by information-limiting the per-step change in q_ψ, analogous to trust-region constraints in reinforcement learning.

## Results

On the Sachs flow cytometry dataset (11 nodes, n=7466), VTI achieves F1=0.44, SHD=23.0, and AUROC=0.68 for non-linear DAG discovery, outperforming DAGMA (F1=0.32, SHD=25.0, AUROC=0.60), DiBS+ (F1=0.22, SHD=28.0, AUROC=0.54), and JSP-GFlowNets (F1=0.23, SHD=54.5, AUROC=0.51). In the robust variable selection experiments, cross-entropy between VTI and RJMCMC reference samples decreases as flow expressivity increases (from affine to rational quadratic spline CoSMIC flows), and the neural density (MCG) sampler maintains consistent performance as model space cardinality scales from 2^9 to 2^24, while the GP surrogate degrades at larger cardinalities.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VTI outperforms all three application-specific baselines on the Sachs DAG discovery task in F1 (0.44 vs. 0.32, 0.22, 0.23), structural Hamming distance (23.0 vs. 25.0, 28.0, 54.5), and AUROC (0.68 vs. 0.60, 0.54, 0.51). The sole exception is the Brier score, where DiBS+ achieves 17.0 versus VTI's 23.0, suggesting DiBS+ produces better-calibrated edge probabilities even though its overall structural accuracy is lower. JSP-GFlowNets performs worst on SHD (54.5) and Brier (44.0), indicating substantial structural error and poor calibration. In the variable selection cardinality sweep (Figure 3), the MCG-based neural density sampler matches or exceeds the GP surrogate and categorical MCG samplers as |M| grows beyond 2^12, while the surrogate remains competitive only at smaller cardinalities (|M|=2^9). The paper also uses RJMCMC [48] as a sampling-based reference (not a learned baseline) to compute cross-entropy, showing VTI's approximation quality improves with flow expressivity but that high-probability models are approximated more accurately than low-probability ones.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends flow-based variational inference (Rezende & Mohamed 2015; Kingma et al. 2016 IAF) from fixed-dimensional parameter spaces to transdimensional (multi-model) settings, where the dimension of θ_m varies with the discrete model index m. The key architectural contribution, CoSMIC flows, repurposes the dimension-saturation idea from reversible jump MCMC (Brooks et al. 2003) within a conditional autoregressive flow, using context-dependent masking to identity-map inactive coordinates. For the discrete model distribution q_ψ, the paper draws on two distinct lineages: Gaussian process surrogates with UCB sampling from Bayesian optimization (Srinivas et al. 2010), and score-function Monte Carlo gradient estimation (Mohamed et al. 2020) with MADE-based autoregressive densities (Germain et al. 2015) for high-cardinality model spaces.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VTI provides a general-purpose, optimization-based alternative to reversible jump MCMC for a broad class of transdimensional problems (variable selection, DAG discovery, phylogenetics, mixture-component inference), removing the need for hand-crafted dimension-jump proposals. The competitive performance of a generic VTI framework against application-specific methods (DAGMA, DiBS, JSP-GFlowNets) on DAG discovery suggests the approach could serve as a flexible baseline. Open questions include: whether the CoSMIC flow's tendency to shrink model probabilities to zero when conditional approximation is poor can be mitigated without altering the objective, convergence rates for the flow parameters φ, extension to coupling flows, and whether structured variable alignment across models improves expressivity. The inherited mode-collapse limitation of standard VI remains a practical concern for highly multimodal transdimensional posteriors.
