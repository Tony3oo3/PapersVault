## Problem

Existing graph prompting methods for adapting pre-trained GNNs to downstream tasks are almost exclusively feature-oriented, applying learnable prompts to node features or hidden representations while leaving graph topology untouched. This overlooks the fact that graph structure is a fundamental component distinguishing graph data from other modalities and has a significant impact on node classification performance. The paper asks: how should we design a topology-oriented prompting framework that modifies graph structure to bridge the gap between pre-training and downstream tasks?

## Input / Output

The method takes an attributed graph (adjacency matrix A ∈ {0,1}^{n×n} and feature matrix X ∈ R^{n×d_x}) along with a frozen pre-trained GNN model, and produces node class labels via a trainable linear classifier. Unusually, the 'prompt' is not a set of feature vectors but a modified adjacency matrix S, where edge rewiring is restricted to edges between each target node and other nodes within its ρ-hop local subgraph (typically ρ=2). The pre-trained GNN's node representations H are computed once before adaptation and serve as fixed inputs to the prompt projector, avoiding repeated forward passes through the frozen model.

## Method

GraphTOP formulates topology-oriented prompting as a binary edge rewiring problem and relaxes it into continuous probability space via Gumbel-Softmax reparameterization, enabling gradient-based optimization of edge selectors. A shared 2-layer MLP projector computes pairwise edge probabilities p_ij from the sum of node representations (h_i + h_j), ensuring symmetry for undirected graphs. To control complexity, rewiring is restricted to edges between each target node and other nodes in its ρ-hop subgraph, reducing cost from O(D^{2ρ}) to O(D^ρ). The optimization objective combines the classification loss with an entropy-minimization regularizer (driving probabilities toward 0 or 1 for tight relaxation) and a sparsity constraint (penalizing deviation from a target edge density γ). A theoretical analysis under the contextual stochastic block model proves that the edge rewiring design enlarges the expected inter-class distance by a factor of (p+q)/|p−q|, guaranteeing improved linear separability.

## Results

On 5-shot node classification across five datasets (Cora, PubMed, Amazon, Minesweeper, Flickr) under four pre-training strategies (GraphCL, SimGRACE, LP-GPPT, LP-GraphPrompt), GraphTOP achieves the best accuracy in 17 out of 20 experiments. The most notable gains appear on heterophilic datasets: GraphTOP reaches 27.43% on Amazon under GraphCL (vs. 24.27% for the next-best GPPT) and 30.93% on Flickr under GraphCL (vs. 29.31% for Linear Probe). Under LP-GPPT, GraphTOP achieves 32.41% on Amazon, a 3.5-point improvement over the runner-up GPPT (28.90%). The only losses are on SimGRACE-Cora (50.57% vs. GraphPrompt+'s 51.26%) and SimGRACE-Minesweeper (61.25% vs. ProNoG's 63.03%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GraphTOP outperforms all five feature-oriented prompting baselines in 17 of 20 settings. ProNoG [51] is the strongest competitor, achieving runner-up in 10 of 20 experiments (e.g., 66.11% on Minesweeper under LP-GPPT vs. GraphTOP's 66.67%), but GraphTOP still edges it out in most cases. GraphPrompt [27] and GraphPrompt+ [50] are competitive on homophilic datasets (Cora, PubMed) but fall behind on heterophilic ones (Amazon, Flickr), where topology-oriented prompting provides the largest gains. GPPT [35] and All-in-one [36] are consistently outperformed across nearly all settings, with GraphTOP exceeding GPPT by up to 3.5 points on Amazon (LP-GPPT) and All-in-one by up to 5.4 points on Flickr (GraphCL). The two exceptions where GraphTOP does not win are SimGRACE-Cora (GraphPrompt+ leads by 0.69 points) and SimGRACE-Minesweeper (ProNoG leads by 1.78 points), suggesting that contrastive pre-training with SimGRACE may interact differently with topology modifications.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant feature-oriented paradigm in graph prompting (GPPT, GraphPrompt, GraphPrompt+, All-in-one, ProNoG), which modifies node features or hidden representations, by introducing topology-oriented prompting that learns to rewire edges in the input graph. Technically, it repurposes Gumbel-Softmax reparameterization (Jang et al., ICLR 2017) to make discrete edge-rewiring decisions differentiable, and constrains the search space to multi-hop local subgraphs for scalability. The theoretical analysis follows the CSBM-based linear separability framework established by Ma et al. (ICLR 2022) and Wang et al. (ICML 2024), extending it to show that learned edge rewiring can amplify inter-class representation distance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GraphTOP opens a previously unexplored axis for graph prompting—modifying graph topology rather than node features—which is conceptually important because topology is a fundamental component that distinguishes graph data from other modalities. However, the practical impact is tempered by several limitations: experiments are restricted to 5-shot node classification with 2-layer GCNs, the theoretical guarantee relies on the linear CSBM model, and performance gains over strong baselines like ProNoG are modest (typically 1–3%). Open questions include extending to graph-level tasks (sketched in Appendix D but not evaluated), nonlinear GNN architectures, directed graphs, and whether topology-oriented prompting complements or subsumes feature-oriented prompting in a unified framework.
