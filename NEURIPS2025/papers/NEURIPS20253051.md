## Problem

Open-vocabulary multi-object tracking (OVMOT) requires diverse, large-scale video data, but the only suitable video benchmark (TAO, 833 categories) provides only 500 videos with annotations every 30 frames and ~2 objects per frame. Existing approaches either rely on static image datasets that lack temporal dynamics or generate pseudo-labels from prior frames that degrade for fast-moving targets. The paper asks whether an effective OV tracker can be trained directly from such sparse, limited video annotations without additional data.

## Input / Output

The tracker consumes RGB video frames and text class names (encoded by CLIP for open-vocabulary classification) and outputs per-frame bounding boxes, class labels spanning both base and novel categories, and persistent object track IDs. Training operates on the sparsely annotated TAO base set where only 1 in 30 frames carries ground-truth boxes (~2 objects/frame), and the method must synthesize the missing temporal supervision internally rather than requiring dense per-frame annotations.

## Method

Built on the OVTrack architecture (ResNet-50 + Faster R-CNN + CLIP), the paper's core contribution is a three-part data-expansion training strategy. For association, a D²MP-based diffusion model simulates intermediate object features between two annotated frames by modeling the denoising path from F_key to F_ref, supervised by reconstruction (interpolation), endpoint, and smoothness losses; the N intermediate features (typically 3) expand the positive sample set ~3×. For classification, a dynamic group contrastive learning scheme constructs affinity (min-variance), dispersion (max-variance), and adversarial (random + noise) groups from per-class features, tripling the effective InfoNCE training samples. For localization, an adaptive loss lowers the positive-sample IoU threshold to 0.3 while weighting each box by 1/(log(ε+1)+1) to suppress noise from low-confidence predictions. The diffusion model is trained for 5 epochs on association-head features, then frozen to generate augmented data for the remaining 5 epochs of joint training.

## Results

On the TAO validation set, DOVTrack achieves a Novel TETA of 35.2% and Base TETA of 40.4%, surpassing the second-best method (OVTR at 31.4% Novel / 36.6% Base) by 3.8% and 3.8% respectively. On the TAO test set, the method reaches 30.1% Novel TETA and 38.7% Base TETA, outperforming SLAck (27.1% / 34.7%) by 3.0% and 4.0%. The method also leads in association accuracy (40.3% Novel, 42.1% Base) and localization accuracy (59.2% Novel, 58.6% Base), with particularly strong gains in classification (6.2% Novel ClsA vs. 1.3% for SLAck).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DOVTrack outperforms all 11 baselines on both Novel and Base TETA across validation and test sets. Against the closest OVMOT competitors, it surpasses SLAck (the only other method trained on TAO video data) by 4.1% Novel TETA and 3.2% Base TETA on validation, while using only sparse annotations without interval frames that SLAck relies on. Compared to OVTR (transformer-based, ICLR 2025), DOVTrack gains 3.8% Novel TETA and 3.8% Base TETA, with particularly large advantages in association (40.3% vs. 34.5% Novel AssocA) and classification (6.2% vs. 5.4% Novel ClsA). Against OVSORT (IEEE TMM 2025), the improvement is 4.4% Novel TETA and 2.2% Base TETA. The method also substantially outperforms the OVTrack baseline it builds upon (27.8% → 35.2% Novel TETA), demonstrating that the data-efficiency strategies contribute more than architectural changes. On the test set, DOVTrack maintains its lead over SLAck (30.1% vs. 27.1% Novel TETA) and OVTR (30.1% vs. 27.1%), confirming generalization beyond the validation split.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the OVTrack architecture (ResNet-50 with Faster R-CNN localization, CLIP-based classification, and appearance-based association heads) but fundamentally rethinks how to train it under the sparse-annotation regime of the TAO video dataset. It departs from prior OVMOT methods (OVTrack, OVSORT, OVTR) that rely on frozen open-vocabulary detectors and large-scale image pairs from LVIS, and from SLAck's pseudo-label approach, by introducing a feature-level diffusion model to synthesize intermediate object features between sparsely annotated frames and by explicitly fine-tuning the detector with dynamic group contrastive learning and adaptive localization loss. The diffusion-based temporal feature generation repurposes the denoising process of diffusion models—previously applied at the pixel level for image/video synthesis—as a mechanism for simulating object state evolution in feature space, a conceptual shift from generative modeling to training-data augmentation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work addresses a practical bottleneck in OVMOT: the TAO dataset is the only video benchmark with sufficient category diversity (833 classes) but provides only one annotated frame per 30, making standard training infeasible. The demonstrated 3.8% TETA gain without additional data or annotations suggests that smarter data utilization can partially substitute for data scale in tracking, a finding relevant to broader sparse-supervision learning. However, the paper is evaluated exclusively on TAO with a ResNet-50 backbone, does not discuss limitations, and the diffusion model is a simple 3-layer FC network, leaving open whether the approach generalizes to larger backbones, longer annotation gaps, or other sparse-annotation video tasks. The dynamic group contrastive learning strategy, while effective here, has not been tested in non-tracking classification settings.
