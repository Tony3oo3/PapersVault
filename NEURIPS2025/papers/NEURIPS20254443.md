## Problem

While mean-field transformer models have been shown to exhibit clustering dynamics, prior results rely on restrictive assumptions (e.g., Q=K=V=Id) and capture only isolated dynamical regimes, leaving no unified picture of how token representations evolve through depth. In particular, the initial rapid collapse of the token measure onto a low-dimensional subspace—the alignment phase—had not been identified, and the relationship between previously studied heat-equation and pairing dynamics remained unclear. This paper addresses the gap by analyzing the full dynamical landscape in the moderate interaction regime where β scales with N, as motivated by context-scaling strategies in modern LLMs.

## Input / Output

The analysis takes N tokens initialized on S^{d-1} (typically i.i.d. from a reference measure), fixed query/key/value matrices Q, K, V, and an inverse temperature β that diverges with N. The output is a three-phase decomposition of the token empirical measure's evolution: a linear transport PDE on O(1) timescale, a (forward or backward) heat equation on O(β) timescale, and a finite-dimensional ODE system for sequential cluster merging on O(e^{cβ}) timescale. The joint limit N→∞ with β_N→∞ slowly enough is essential; the Dobrushin-type stability estimate (Lemma 3.1) guarantees the N-particle ODE system tracks the mean-field PDE uniformly on each phase's timescale.

## Method

The core technique is a formal Taylor expansion of the mean-field vector field χ_β[μ] into a leading term (I) involving the kernel's maximum and a correction term (II) involving ∇μ, then identifying which term dominates at each timescale. In the alignment phase, Laplace approximation of term (I) yields a linear transport equation whose long-time behavior collapses the measure onto the generalized eigenspace of VK^TQ with largest real part (Theorem 3.2, Proposition 3.4). Once the measure is supported on this subspace and the leading term vanishes (under Assumption 4: Q^TK|_{E_max}=λ₁I, V|_{E_max}=±λ₂I), the rescaled vector field βχ_β[μ] converges to ±∇_S μ/μ, producing a forward or backward heat equation (Proposition 3.9). In the pairing phase, with the measure reduced to a sum of Dirac masses, the closest cluster pair interacts on an exponentially long timescale via a hardmax-type ODE that drives them along the connecting geodesic (Proposition 3.11). The technical backbone for the alignment phase involves uniform-in-β regularity estimates on the vector field using cumulant bounds for the Von Mises-Fisher distribution and a continuation argument.

## Results

The paper identifies three distinct dynamical phases in the moderate interaction regime (β → ∞ with N large): (1) an alignment phase on O(1) timescale governed by a linear transport equation causing collapse onto the generalized eigenspace E_max of VK^TQ; (2) a heat phase on O(β) timescale governed by a forward or backward heat equation on the aligned manifold (depending on the sign of V restricted to E_max); and (3) a pairing phase on O(e^{cβ}) timescale where clusters sequentially merge along geodesics. Numerical validation with N=10^4 tokens confirms the theoretical predictions: alignment to 1D/2D subspaces (β=30), backward heat equation cluster formation matching analytical solutions (β=10), and forward heat equation smoothing (β=50, 5×10^4 tokens). The main technical contribution is Theorem 3.2, proving convergence of the finite-particle dynamics to the linear transport PDE under general (invertible) Q, K, V matrices — a phase not previously identified in the literature.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper that does not benchmark against competing methods in the traditional sense. Instead, it unifies and extends several prior theoretical results into a single multiscale dynamical picture. Compared to [46] (Sander et al., AISTATS 2022), which identified the heat equation only as a correction term in R^d, this paper provides a rigorous justification for the spontaneous collapse to a subspace where the heat equation becomes the leading-order dynamics on the sphere. Compared to [23] (Geshkovski et al., 2024), which characterized the pairing phase only in d=2 with Q=K=V=Id, this paper extends the result to arbitrary dimension under Assumption 4. Compared to [9] (Bruno et al., ICLR 2025), which studied metastable clustering for uniform initialization, this paper provides the full three-phase structure with general initial conditions. The alignment phase (Theorem 3.2) is entirely new and was not identified in any prior work. The paper's assumptions (invertible Q, K, V; absolutely continuous initial measure) are significantly milder than the Q=K=V=Id restriction used in most prior clustering analyses [24, 29, 13, 44].

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the mean-field transformer framework of Sander et al. [46] and Geshkovski et al. [25, 24] by analyzing the dynamics in the moderate interaction regime (β scaling with N), motivated by practical scaling strategies like SSMax and YaRN. Prior works identified individual dynamical behaviors—heat equation dynamics [46], clustering [24], meta-stable phases [9], and the pairing phase in d=2 [23]—but typically under restrictive assumptions (Q,K,V as multiples of identity) or in isolation. The key departure is the identification of a previously unknown fast alignment phase governed by a linear transport equation, and the unification of all three phases into a single multiscale picture under general (invertible) parameter matrices.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified three-phase framework reconciles previously fragmented results into a coherent dynamical narrative for how token representations form through depth, with the alignment phase (collapse onto E_max) being entirely new to the literature. However, the heat and pairing phases still require restrictive assumptions (Q^T K and V proportional to identity on E_max), the backward heat equation is ill-posed beyond the first collapse, and the MLP is excluded—leaving the full picture not yet rigorous. Open questions include relaxing these parameter assumptions, rigorously connecting phases for finite β, and incorporating the MLP as a drift term. The moderate interaction regime is directly relevant to practitioners scaling models to longer contexts, making this a foundational step toward principled hyperparameter choices.
