## Problem

Diffusion and flow-based policies offer superior exploration and multimodality over Gaussian policies, but they cannot be integrated into on-policy RL algorithms like PPO because the log-likelihood of a state-action pair is intractable under the irreversible forward-reverse discretization of diffusion models. This gap is especially costly because modern GPU-parallel simulators (IsaacLab, Isaac Gym) are optimized for on-policy training, leaving the most expressive policy class incompatible with the fastest training infrastructure.

## Input / Output

The policy consumes a robot state vector (dimension varies per environment, e.g., 60-dim for Ant, 87-dim for Humanoid, 256-dim for Unitree-H1) concatenated with a sinusoidal time embedding, and outputs a continuous action of the original action-space dimension. Internally, however, the flow policy operates in a doubled dummy action space of dimension 2|A|, producing two coupled components (x, y) that are averaged at inference to yield the single action a = (x+y)/2 executed in the environment. The flow uses only 5 integration steps for generation, keeping inference at ~2.6 ms per action.

## Method

GenPO makes the diffusion/flow policy exactly invertible by maintaining two coupled noise vectors (x, y) and updating them alternately in the forward and reverse processes, motivated by EDICT's exact diffusion inversion. Because this doubling is incompatible with odd-dimensional action spaces, the original MDP is reformulated with a doubled dummy action space, and a mixing scheme (coefficient p=0.9) keeps x and y closely aligned to prevent meaningless exploration. With invertibility established, the exact log-likelihood of any state-action pair is computed via the change-of-variables theorem (Jacobian determinant of the flow), enabling unbiased entropy estimation and KL-divergence computation between old and new policies. These quantities are plugged into a modified PPO objective with clipped surrogate loss, entropy regularization, KL-adaptive learning-rate scheduling, and an auxiliary compression loss that penalizes (x−y)² to focus exploration in the doubled space. The result is the first stable integration of a generative diffusion policy into an on-policy RL loop, validated across eight IsaacLab benchmarks.

## Results

GenPO achieves the highest mean episodic return across all eight IsaacLab benchmarks, outperforming the strongest baseline PPO by 21% on Ant (177.90 vs. 146.94), 39% on Humanoid (273.94 vs. 197.25), 85% on Franka-Arm (144.78 vs. 78.14), and 101% on Anymal-D (19.80 vs. 9.80). On the most challenging tasks—Unitree-Go2 (28.01 vs. 15.67) and Unitree-H1 (26.09 vs. 18.97)—GenPO also exhibits substantially lower variance (0.76 and 0.68 vs. 1.92 and 2.02), indicating greater training stability. The diffusion-based off-policy baselines DACER and QVPO perform near-zero or negative returns on most locomotion and manipulation tasks, confirming their incompatibility with large-scale parallel GPU simulation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GenPO outperforms all six baselines on every one of the eight IsaacLab tasks. Against the strongest on-policy baseline PPO, GenPO gains 21–101% in mean return, with the largest relative improvements on Franka-Arm (+85%), Anymal-D (+101%), Unitree-Go2 (+79%), and Unitree-H1 (+37%); it also reduces variance dramatically on Franka-Arm (std 3.10 vs. 50.43) and Quadcopter (0.84 vs. 13.49). The two diffusion-based off-policy methods, DACER and QVPO, are effectively non-functional in the massively parallel IsaacLab setting: DACER scores near-zero or negative returns on Ant, Humanoid, Franka-Arm, Anymal-D, Unitree-Go2, and Unitree-H1, while QVPO achieves only 7.19 on Ant and 10.59 on Humanoid. Among the classical off-policy baselines, TD3 and DDPG perform moderately on Ant and Humanoid but collapse on manipulation (Franka-Arm, Shadow-Hand) and legged locomotion (Anymal-D, Unitree-H1), while SAC shows high variance and inconsistent performance across tasks. Overall, GenPO is the only method that combines high mean return with low variance across all eight benchmarks, validating the benefit of integrating diffusion policies into the on-policy PPO framework.

## Relation to Prior Work

GenPO sits at the intersection of three research threads: on-policy RL (PPO), generative diffusion/flow-matching models, and exact diffusion inversion (EDICT). Prior diffusion-based RL work (DACER, QVPO, DIPO) has been confined to off-policy settings where Q-function gradients bypass the need for action log-likelihoods, while offline RL methods (Diffusion-QL, FQL, EDP) avoid the problem entirely by learning from fixed datasets. GenPO departs from both by solving the core obstacle—computing exact state-action log-likelihoods under a diffusion policy—through a doubled dummy action space with alternating updates, enabling the change-of-variables formula to yield tractable densities. This is the first formulation that makes diffusion policies compatible with PPO's clipped surrogate objective, entropy regularization, and KL-adaptive learning rates.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By unlocking diffusion policies for on-policy RL, GenPO makes them compatible with massively parallel GPU simulators (IsaacLab) that are the de facto standard for training complex robotic skills, a setting where off-policy diffusion methods struggle to converge. The ability to compute exact entropy and KL divergence for diffusion policies also opens the door to principled exploration bonuses and trust-region constraints previously unavailable to generative policies. Key limitations include the computational overhead of Jacobian determinant computation and the doubled action space introducing redundant exploration; the authors note that optimizing these for real-world deployment remains open. Whether the doubled-dummy-action trick generalizes beyond flow-matching backbones or to higher-dimensional action spaces (e.g., whole-body control with 50+ DOF) is an important open question.
