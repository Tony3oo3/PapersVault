## Problem

Zero-shot spatio-temporal video grounding (STVG) remains challenging because existing zero-shot methods rely on CLIP-based models that are inherently weak at fine-grained localization. While multimodal LLMs (MLLMs) possess strong cross-modal comprehension, their special tokens—dynamically assigned to ground text queries—tend to ignore discriminative attribute and action cues in complex video queries, leading to spatial and temporal grounding failures. This paper identifies and exploits the grounding capability of these special tokens while addressing their cue-neglect limitation.

## Input / Output

The method takes an untrimmed video (uniformly sampled to 20 frames) and a natural-language text query as input. The query is first decomposed by GPT-4o into an attribute sub-query (for spatial grounding) and an action sub-query (for temporal grounding), each converted into an interrogative form (e.g., 'Is there a man in red clothes in this video?'). Object track proposals are extracted via G-DINO detection (every 10 frames) and SAM2 tracking. The output is a spatio-temporal tube: per-frame bounding boxes for the target object along with predicted start and end frame boundaries, scored by m_vIoU and vIoU@0.3/0.5.

## Method

The framework has three key components. First, Grounding Token Identification (GTI) selects the special token with the highest visual activation among the role tokens following the instruction prompt, exploiting the finding that MLLMs dynamically assign these tokens to attend to text-referenced regions. Second, Decomposed Spatio-Temporal Highlighting (DSTH) introduces a Logit-Guided Re-Attention (LRA) module that learns spatial and temporal visual prompts via test-time tuning: for each sub-query, a learnable variable is added to the visual tokens and optimized by maximizing the logit difference between 'yes' and 'no' response tokens, thereby directing the frozen MLLM's attention to attribute- or action-relevant visual regions. Third, Temporal-Augmented Assembling (TAS) addresses temporal inconsistency in spatial grounding by reversing the frame order, optimizing a second spatial prompt on the reversed input, and assembling attention maps from both forward and reversed passes to improve robustness. The entire pipeline is training-free with respect to the MLLM weights; only the small visual prompt variables are optimized at inference time.

## Results

On the HCSTVG-v1 benchmark, the best variant (LLaVA-OneVision-7B) achieves 24.8 m_vIoU, 41.5 vIoU@0.3, and 16.3 vIoU@0.5, outperforming the previous zero-shot SOTA (E3M) by 5.7 points on vIoU@0.5. On HCSTVG-v2, the method reaches 27.7 m_vIoU and 19.5 vIoU@0.5, surpassing the best weakly-supervised method (CoSPaL) by 5.5 points on m_vIoU. On VidSTG (Declarative), the method achieves 18.0 m_vIoU and 12.2 vIoU@0.5, still leading all zero-shot baselines. The framework generalizes across four MLLMs (LLaVA-Next-Video, Qwen2-VL, ShareGPT4Video, LLaVA-OneVision), with stronger backbones yielding consistently better results.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest zero-shot baseline E3M [3], the method (with LLaVA-OneVision-7B) improves vIoU@0.3 by 12.1 points (44.7 vs. 29.4 on HCSTVG-v1) and vIoU@0.5 by 5.7 points (19.5 vs. 10.6), while also surpassing it on VidSTG by 1.8 points on vIoU@0.5. The method also outperforms the best weakly-supervised method CoSPaL [25] on HCSTVG-v2 by 5.5 points on m_vIoU (27.7 vs. 22.2) and 0.6 points on vIoU@0.5 (19.5 vs. 18.9), despite requiring no training annotations. On VidSTG (Declarative), the method beats CoSPaL by 2.0 points on m_vIoU (18.0 vs. 16.0) but trails slightly on vIoU@0.5 (12.2 vs. 13.1). Compared to fully-supervised SOTA (CG-STVG [15]), the zero-shot method achieves comparable m_vIoU on HCSTVG-v1 (24.8 vs. 38.4) but with a notable gap, while on HCSTVG-v2 the gap narrows (27.7 vs. 39.5). On the RefCOCO image grounding benchmarks (Table 6), the method with LLaVA-OneVision-7B achieves 68.7/77.2/62.0 on RefCOCO val/testA/testB, surpassing the zero-shot SOTA GroundVLP [46] (65.0/73.5/55.0) and approaching fully-supervised Ferret-7B [65] (87.5/91.4/82.5). The method consistently wins across all zero-shot comparisons and most weakly-supervised comparisons, demonstrating that the MLLM-based zero-shot framework can rival or exceed methods that require explicit training data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on MLLM-based visual grounding by shifting focus from generated tokens (explored by Zhang et al. [69]) to special tokens that follow instruction prompts, revealing they carry inherent grounding ability. It departs from ControlMLLM [56], which requires referring-region supervision to optimize attention, by proposing an unsupervised logit-guided re-attention (LRA) module that regularizes token-level 'yes'/'no' predictions during test-time tuning. The broader positioning is against CLIP-based zero-shot STVG methods (E3M, ReCLIP) that are known to be weak in localization, and against MLLM grounding approaches (LLaVA-ST, Lisa, Groma) that require costly fine-tuning with grounding-specific data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The discovery that special tokens in MLLMs dynamically assign grounding roles provides a new lens into MLLM internals and offers a practical training-free pathway to video grounding that surpasses prior zero-shot SOTA and even some weakly-supervised methods. However, the approach still trails fully-supervised methods by a wide margin (~14 m_vIoU gap on HC-STVG-v1), relies on external components (G-DINO for detection, SAM2 for tracking, GPT-4o for query decomposition), and faces computational bottlenecks on long videos. Open questions include whether integrating multiple grounding tokens, token pruning, or keyframe selection could close the gap to supervised performance and scale to longer video sequences.
