## Problem

Hyperedge disentanglement—capturing hidden factors (e.g., functional context) underlying group interactions in hypergraphs—remains largely unexplored, and existing disentanglement criteria based on factor representation similarity rely on data-specific assumptions that do not transfer to hyperedge-level semantics. The paper asks: what criterion, derived from the structural properties of hypergraph message passing itself rather than from heuristics about the data, can guide a model to identify which factors are relevant to each hyperedge's interaction context?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251232/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a hypergraph where nodes are genes (11,552 per patient) and hyperedges are genetic pathways (1,497 per patient), with 9-dimensional node features derived from multi-omics data (mRNA/miRNA expression, DNA methylation, CNV, gene length). Every patient shares the same gene-pathway topology but has distinct gene features; the hypergraph-level representation is formed by concatenating all hyperedge representations (no topology-aware pooling exists). Beyond the cancer subtype label, the model outputs per-hyperedge factor relevance scores (α_i^k) and factor-specific hyperedge representations that encode the captured interaction context.

## Method

The core contribution is a category-theoretic analysis showing that the naturality condition must hold between entangled and disentangled representations in any hypergraph MPNN, which yields the 'factor representation consistency' criterion: a factor is relevant to a hyperedge if its representation is consistent regardless of whether one aggregates-then-disentangles or disentangles-then-aggregates. Natural-HNN implements this via two parallel branches per layer—an aggregation-first branch (mean-pool node features, then apply a factor-specific MLP) and a disentangle-first branch (apply the factor-specific MLP to each node, then mean-pool)—and computes a learned similarity score between the two branches' outputs as the factor relevance. Hyperedge-to-node propagation uses these relevance scores as normalized weights, and the final layer output interpolates neighbor information with the node's own factor representations. The criterion is data-agnostic by construction, requiring no assumptions about participant similarity or substructure importance, and is validated on cancer subtype classification where it captures pathway functional context more faithfully than similarity-based baselines.

## Results

Natural-HNN achieves the best Macro F1 on 5 out of 6 cancer subtype classification datasets (BRCA, STAD, SARC, LGG, HNSC), with the largest margin on BRCA (0.804 vs. 0.757 for the second-best HSDN, a ~5% improvement). On CESC, ED-HNNII slightly edges out Natural-HNN (0.895 vs. 0.881). The model also demonstrates strong generalizability, maintaining competitive performance even when the training set is reduced to 10%, and captures functional context of genetic pathways consistently across hyperparameter settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Natural-HNN outperforms all 9 baseline families on 5 of 6 cancer datasets, losing only on CESC to ED-HNNII (0.881 vs. 0.895). The most notable comparison is against HSDN [31], the only other disentanglement-based hypergraph method, where Natural-HNN leads by 3–5% Macro F1 across all datasets (e.g., 0.804 vs. 0.757 on BRCA), validating that the naturality-guided criterion is more effective than HSDN's factor-similarity criterion for capturing hyperedge-level context. Against convolution-based baselines (HGNN, HCHA, HNHN, UniGCNII), Natural-HNN gains 5–15% on BRCA and STAD, indicating that the equal-contribution inductive bias of these methods is insufficient for capturing pathway functional context. Attention-based baselines (HyperGAT, SHINE) show high variance and generally underperform Natural-HNN, with SHINE being particularly unstable (e.g., 0.446±0.155 on BRCA). ED-HNN/ED-HNNII, which use equivariant message passing, are the strongest non-disentanglement baselines and remain competitive on CESC, but Natural-HNN still leads on the remaining five datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of disentangled representation learning (DRL) from graph-structured data (DisenGCN, HSDN, edge-disentanglement methods) to hypergraph-structured data, specifically targeting hyperedge-level factors that govern group interactions. It departs from prior DRL criteria—which rely on data-specific assumptions like factor representation similarity—by deriving a data-agnostic criterion (factor representation consistency) from the naturality condition in category theory, framing hypergraph message passing as a functor from a poset category (PISet) to a deep learning representation category (DLRep). The model architecture (Natural-HNN) is a straightforward message-passing HNN, but the theoretical lens for determining factor relevance is the key conceptual departure from existing hypergraph neural networks (HGNN, HCHA, ED-HNN, etc.) and the one prior hypergraph disentanglement method (HSDN), which operates at the hypergraph-structure level rather than the hyperedge level.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first principled, assumption-free criterion for hyperedge disentanglement, which could guide future work on capturing hidden semantics in multi-way interactions across domains (biology, social networks, multi-agent systems). However, validation is limited to a single application domain (cancer subtype classification from genetic pathways), and the model is explicitly a proof-of-concept; whether the naturality-based criterion generalizes to other hypergraph domains or provides theoretical guarantees beyond empirical success remains open. The category-theoretic framing, while elegant, does not yet yield formal convergence or expressivity bounds, and the reliance on SHAP-selected pathways for validating 'captured context' introduces an indirectness that limits the strength of the interpretability claims.
