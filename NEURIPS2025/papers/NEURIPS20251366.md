## Problem

CLAPScore is the most widely used reference-free audio caption evaluation metric, yet its robustness under diverse caption quality conditions has never been systematically validated. No existing benchmark provides a rigorous, reference-free pairwise comparison setting that can distinguish fine-grained caption quality differences or detect subtle object-level hallucinations in audio captions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251366/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

Each evaluation instance consists of a single audio clip (typically 5–30 seconds) paired with two candidate captions (each under ~20 words). For CLAP-based metrics, the model independently encodes the audio and each caption into a shared embedding space and outputs a cosine similarity score per caption; the higher-scoring caption is selected as the preference. For LALMs, the audio and both captions are jointly fed into a prompt template, and the model outputs a free-text response that is post-processed by a secondary LLM into a discrete preference (caption_0, caption_1, tie, or unknown). Final performance is reported as F1-score against human-annotated ground-truth preferences.

## Method

BRACE comprises two sub-benchmarks built from AudioCaps and Clotho evaluation sets. BRACE-Main (2,496 pairs) constructs three comparison categories—Human-Human, Human-Machine, and Machine-Machine—by filtering source captions for consistency with Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, generating machine captions via LTU/GAMA, and corrupting captions through LLM-driven shortening and fluency-error injection; three annotators score each pair and only consensus items (|score| ≥ 2) are retained, yielding Fleiss-Kappa of 0.98 (AudioCaps) and 0.84 (Clotho). BRACE-Hallucination (10,315 pairs) uses GPT-4o in a few-shot setting to replace a single noun in each caption with a semantically distinct but syntactically valid alternative. For evaluation, the paper introduces SLIDE-CLAP, which segments audio into overlapping fixed-length windows (5–10 s depending on the encoder), encodes each segment, and averages the normalized embeddings to produce a stable global audio representation, eliminating the randomness of single-window truncation. LALMs are evaluated under three prompt complexity levels (naive, simple, complex) with tie and non-tie variants, and a secondary LLM distills their free-text outputs into structured preferences.

## Results

On BRACE-Main, the best-performing CLAP-based metric (LAION-CLAP with SLIDE-CLAP) achieves a 70.01 F1-score (Avg-All), while the best LALM (AF2) reaches only 63.19. On BRACE-Hallucination, GPT-4o-Audio-Preview attains a state-of-the-art 96.37 F1-score, whereas the best open-source LALM (Qwen-Audio-Chat) achieves 77.25. Notably, GAMA performs catastrophically poorly with a 12.62 F1-score on BRACE-Main, and no CLAP model exceeds 70 on Human-Human or Machine-Machine caption pair comparisons.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among CLAP-based metrics, LAION-CLAP (SLIDE-CLAP) clearly leads with 70.01 Avg-All F1, outperforming MS-CLAP-2022 (62.81), MS-CLAP-2023 (61.51), and M2D-CLAP (60.91) by 7–9 points. Among LALMs, AF2 is the strongest at 63.19, beating Qwen-Audio-Chat (60.26), LTU (59.49), GPT-4o-Audio-Preview (55.24), Qwen2-Audio-Instruct (53.31), and GAMA (12.62). Surprisingly, the closed-source GPT-4o-Audio-Preview underperforms the open-source AF2 by nearly 8 points on BRACE-Main, yet dominates BRACE-Hallucination with 96.37 versus Qwen-Audio-Chat's 77.25. GAMA's near-random performance (12.62) is attributed to severe position bias, as it selects caption_0 in over 86% of cases regardless of content. All CLAP models struggle on fine-grained Human-Human and Machine-Machine comparisons (none above 70), while performing best on Human-Machine pairs where stylistic gaps are larger.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the FENSE benchmark [1] for audio caption evaluation by incorporating the audio modality directly (rather than relying solely on caption text) and by using state-of-the-art LALMs (LTU, GAMA) for caption generation, making pairwise comparison substantially harder. It also departs from prior audio hallucination detection approaches [3,4] that use simple yes/no entity-verification questions, instead introducing full-length caption-pair comparison that requires holistic judgment. The SLIDE-CLAP sliding-window averaging strategy is a practical extension of standard CLAPScore computation to address the instability introduced by fixed-length audio truncation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals that the most widely adopted reference-free ACEM (CLAPScore) achieves only 70.01 F1 on BRACE-Main, and that LALMs suffer from severe position bias (GAMA selects caption_0 in 86–96% of cases) and poor instruction following, challenging the assumption that current tools are adequate for large-scale audio caption quality assessment. The significant gap between GPT-4o (96.37) and the best open-source LALM (77.25) on hallucination detection highlights a critical capability gap for the open-source community. Open questions include whether syntax-aware or fine-grained acoustic CLAP variants can close the performance gap, and whether debiasing techniques can make LALMs reliable evaluators without proprietary model access.
