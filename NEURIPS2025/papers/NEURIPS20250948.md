## Problem

Multimodal graph recommendation suffers from modality-specific noise, semantic misalignment, and unstable signal propagation, yet existing frequency-domain methods (e.g., SMORE, ChebyCF) rely on static reweighting or low-pass filtering without reasoning over band semantics or adapting to modality-specific reliability. No prior architecture provides a unified mechanism to model structured interactions across frequency bands, modalities, and graph topology while remaining computationally tractable and diagnostically interpretable at the band level.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a user-item bipartite interaction graph along with per-item RGB image features, text features, and ID embeddings, all treated as graph signals. For small graphs (|N| ≤ N₀) it computes the full eigendecomposition of the symmetric normalized Laplacian; for larger graphs it approximates multi-band filtering via Chebyshev polynomials of the scaled Laplacian, avoiding explicit eigen-decomposition. The output is a sigmoid-scored user-item interaction probability used for full-ranking top-K evaluation (Recall@K, NDCG@K), with no masking or rescaling applied at inference time.

## Method

SSR follows a four-stage pipeline in a shared spectral coordinate system: (i) graph Fourier transform decomposes multimodal node signals into M equal-energy frequency bands; (ii) Spectral Band Masking (SBM) randomly drops entire bands during training and enforces a representation-consistency loss between full-spectrum and masked views to suppress brittle frequency components; (iii) a Graph HyperSpectral Neural Operator (G-HSNO) models cross-band and cross-modality dependencies through a compact low-rank parameterization—band-mixing scores computed via query-key inner products over a small rank r, combined with rank-specific diagonal filter bases and instance-adaptive band reweighting; (iv) Spectral Contrastive Regularization (SCR) applies an intra-band cross-modal InfoNCE loss that pulls image and text representations together within the same band while pushing apart cross-band and cross-item negatives. The total objective combines binary cross-entropy with weighted SBM, SCR, and kernel-regularization terms, optimized end-to-end with Adam.

## Results

SSR achieves the best performance across all three Amazon benchmarks on every metric. On the sparsest dataset (Clothing), SSR reaches 0.0708 Recall@10 and 0.0386 NDCG@10, outperforming the closest frequency-aware baseline SMORE (0.0659 / 0.0360) by +7.4% and +7.2% respectively. On Baby, SSR attains 0.0728 R@10 and 0.0395 N@10, surpassing SMORE (0.0680 / 0.0365) by +7.1% and +8.2%. In cold-start settings (≤5 interactions), SSR's advantage widens, e.g., +10.4% R@20 over SMORE on Baby (0.1125 vs. 0.1019).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SSR outperforms all 14 baselines on every dataset and metric. Against the closest frequency-aware competitor SMORE [15], SSR gains +7.1% R@10 on Baby, +8.3% on Sports, and +7.4% on Clothing, with the largest relative improvement on the sparsest dataset (Clothing). Compared to the strongest non-spectral multimodal baselines (AlignRec [47], MMIL [67]), SSR leads by 8.0–10.3% in R@10 across datasets, indicating that explicit spectral decomposition captures complementary signals beyond modality alignment or intention modeling. Graph-based baselines (LightGCN, GRCN, DualGNN) trail SSR by 20–50% in R@10, confirming that topology-only propagation is insufficient without content-aware spectral reasoning. In cold-start evaluation (≤5 interactions), SSR's margin over SMORE widens to +10.4% R@20 on Baby and +6.0% on Sports, validating the adaptive band-gating design under data sparsity. No baseline matches or exceeds SSR on any reported metric.

## Relation to Prior Work

SSR synthesizes three research threads—spectral graph signal processing (ChebyCF, SMORE), neural operators from scientific computing, and contrastive multimodal alignment (AlignRec, MMIL)—into a unified four-stage pipeline. It departs from prior frequency-aware recommenders like SMORE and ChebyCF, which apply static low-pass filtering or uniform reweighting, by introducing adaptive band-level reasoning: Spectral Band Masking for robustness, a low-rank Graph HyperSpectral Neural Operator (G-HSNO) for cross-band/cross-modality interaction, and Spectral Contrastive Regularization for intra-band alignment. The neural-operator formulation is repurposed from PDE solvers to discrete graph signals, and the band-masking mechanism borrows the augmentation-consistency idea from self-supervised learning but operates at the spectral granularity rather than the feature or instance level.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SSR offers a structured, interpretable alternative to black-box multimodal fusion: the equal-energy band decomposition and per-band diagnostics (t-SNE, center-distance plots) let practitioners inspect which frequencies and modalities drive recommendations, a transparency benefit that static-filtering baselines lack. The consistent cold-start gains (up to +10% over SMORE) suggest that adaptive spectral modulation is a practical lever when interaction priors are weak. Key open questions remain: the fixed Chebyshev/eigenbasis may underfit non-stationary or temporal dynamics, the number of bands M is heuristic, and the framework has not been tested on dynamic or streaming graphs. Extending SSR to temporal spectral controllers or integrating LLM-based semantic reasoning over bands are natural next steps the authors outline.
