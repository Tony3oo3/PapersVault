## Problem

LLMs struggle with the ambiguity and indirectness inherent in human social communication, often defaulting to literal interpretations that miss unspoken intentions, emotions, and beliefs. Existing approaches treat social reasoning as a single-step prediction problem, failing to capture the structured, multi-stage metacognitive process humans use to interpret, reflect on, and adapt to social cues across diverse cultural contexts.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250881/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system consumes a user prompt, the full conversational history, and a dynamic social memory database that stores user preferences, recurring emotional patterns, and behavioral history across interactions. Beyond the final natural language response, the pipeline produces intermediate structured outputs: a set of k typed mental-state hypotheses (Belief, Desire, Intention, Emotion, Thought) with natural-language explanations, a single refined hypothesis selected via a composite plausibility/information-gain score, and a validation utility score combining empathy and coherence that can trigger regeneration if below a threshold.

## Method

MetaMind is a model-agnostic, three-stage multi-agent framework grounded in metacognitive theory. Stage 1 (ToM Agent) generates k diverse candidate mental-state hypotheses through a four-step structured reasoning process: commonsense interpretation, social-memory cross-referencing, ToM-marker classification, and hypothesis diversification. Stage 2 (Moral Agent) revises each hypothesis under cultural, ethical, and role-based constraint rules, then selects the optimal one via a weighted composite of contextual plausibility and information gain (Eq. 1). Stage 3 (Response Agent) decodes a response conditioned on the selected hypothesis and social memory, then self-validates it with a utility score balancing empathy and coherence (Eq. 2), triggering up to three regeneration iterations if the score falls below 0.9. The architecture is plug-and-play across LLM backbones and requires no fine-tuning, operating entirely through structured prompting and inter-agent communication.

## Results

On ToMBench (GPT-4 backbone), MetaMind achieves 81.0% average accuracy across six ToM dimensions, improving over the base GPT-4 (74.8%) by 6.2 percentage points and surpassing the strongest prompting baseline (ToM2C, 76.1%) by 4.9 points. On the social cognition suite, MetaMind reaches 80.5% average, a 9.0-point gain over base GPT-4 (71.5%). On the open-ended STSS social simulation benchmark, MetaMind scores 73.9% average, a 34.5-point absolute improvement over base GPT-4 (39.4%) and 19.5 points above the best prior method (TDP, 54.4%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MetaMind outperforms all five baselines across all three benchmarks. On ToMBench, MetaMind (81.0%) leads the closest competitor ToM2C (76.1%) by 4.9 points, with the largest per-dimension gain in Knowledge (+10.4 over base GPT-4). On the social cognition suite, MetaMind (80.5%) surpasses SymbolicToM (75.4%) and Generative Agents (75.3%) by roughly 5 points, with particularly large gains in Ambiguous Story Task (+11.3 over base) and Scalar Implicature (+11.4). On STSS, the margin is most dramatic: MetaMind (73.9%) beats TDP (54.4%), the strongest prior method, by 19.5 points, and outperforms Generative Agents (53.3%) by 20.6 points. Notably, CoT prompting actually degrades base GPT-4 performance on ToMBench (73.6% vs. 74.8%), while MetaMind consistently improves every backbone tested, including frontier reasoning models like DeepSeek-R1 (86.0→88.6%) and OpenAI o3 (90.3→92.2%).

## Relation to Prior Work

MetaMind departs from prior ToM-in-LLM work (SymbolicToM, ToM2C, Generative Agents) that treats social reasoning as a single-step prediction or narrow task-specific intervention, instead grounding its design in psychological metacognitive theory (Flavell; Schraw & Moshman) to decompose social understanding into a staged pipeline of hypothesis generation, norm-aware refinement, and validated response. It also extends the multi-agent LLM literature (debate-style reasoning, RAG, collaborative tool use) by introducing agents whose interaction is specifically structured around inferring and revising unobservable mental states under cultural and ethical constraints, rather than merely dividing subtasks. The social memory mechanism draws on long-term user modeling ideas from Generative Agents but formalizes it as a dynamic, feedback-updated knowledge base integrated across all three stages.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework's model-agnostic design (validated across 16 LLMs from 6B open-source to frontier reasoning models) and the claim of matching human performance on specific ToM dimensions (Belief, NL Communication, Desire) for the first time make it a potentially widely adoptable plug-in for social intelligence. However, the evaluation remains text-only and confined to curated benchmarks; real-world social interaction involves multi-modal cues, group dynamics, and long-term relationship building that the framework does not address. Open questions include whether the staged architecture's gains persist in truly open-ended, multi-turn deployments, how the Moral Agent's rule set generalizes across cultures, and whether the computational overhead of three sequential LLM calls is acceptable for latency-sensitive applications.
