## Problem

Existing solvers for discrete SemiUOT and UOT with KL divergence rely on entropy regularization, which inevitably produces dense and inaccurate transport plans, or on majority-maximization algorithms that degrade when the relaxation coefficient τ is large. The paper addresses the gap of efficiently computing exact marginal probabilities for these problems and obtaining sparse, accurate matching solutions without the ambiguity introduced by standard regularization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253519/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes two discrete sample sets (X ∈ R^{M×D}, Z ∈ R^{N×D}), a pairwise square-Euclidean cost matrix C ∈ R^{M×N}, and initial uniform marginal weight vectors (a_i = 1/M, b_j = 1/N). It outputs the optimal transport plan π ∈ R^{M×N}, the adjusted source/target marginal probabilities (α, β) that convert the unbalanced problem into a balanced one, and the KKT multiplier matrix s ∈ R^{M×N} that encodes which sample pairs should carry zero mass. The entire pipeline is parameterized by the KL relaxation coefficient τ (or τ_a, τ_b for UOT) and a LogSumExp smoothing parameter ε.

## Method

The core insight is that the KKT conditions of SemiUOT/UOT with KL divergence yield closed-form expressions for the exact marginal probabilities as exponential reweightings of the initial weights, allowing the unbalanced problem to be transformed into a standard balanced OT problem. Three optimization variants are proposed: ETM-Exact solves the non-smooth dual via LBFGS; ETM-Approx replaces the inf/sup operator with a LogSumExp approximation and solves the resulting smooth system by a provably convergent fixed-point iteration with linear rate O(NM log(1/ε_err)); ETM-Refine uses the approximate solution as a warm start for a few LBFGS steps to recover the exact solution at near-Approx cost. On top of the transformed OT, the paper introduces Multiplier Regularized Optimal Transport (MROT), which adds a KKT-multiplier penalty η_G⟨π, s⟩ to the objective, forcing zero transport on pairs where the complementary slackness variable s_ij > 0. This multiplier term is orthogonal to the choice of standard regularization (entropy or ℓ₂-norm), yielding MROT-Ent and MROT-Norm variants that produce sparser and more accurate plans than any existing UOT/SemiUOT solver.

## Results

On Office-Home Partial UDA, MOT + SemiUOT (ETM-Refine + MROT-Norm) achieves 85.2% average accuracy, outperforming MOT (80.6%), AR (78.3%), and m-POT (78.0%). On Office-31, the same method reaches 99.5% average accuracy vs. MOT's 98.4%, and on ImageCLEF it achieves 95.2% vs. MOT's 93.6%. On Office-Home UDA, JUMBOT + UOT (ETM-Refine + MROT-Norm) attains 73.4% average accuracy, surpassing GEMUOT (71.6%), MM-UOT (70.6%), and JUMBOT alone (70.0%). On synthetic data, ETM-Refine + MROT-Norm produces the most accurate transport plans, particularly when the KL coefficient τ is large (e.g., τ=100), where competing methods like MM-UOT and GEMUOT degrade significantly.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against UOT solvers on synthetic data, ETM-Refine + MROT-Norm consistently produces the most accurate transport plans: Ent-UOT [83] yields dense and inaccurate solutions at all τ values; MM-UOT [17] is accurate only for small τ but deteriorates sharply at τ=100; GEMUOT [78] improves sparsity via ℓ₂-norm but remains coarse, especially at large τ; and Robust-SemiSinkhorn [52] causes the largest computation error among SemiUOT methods. On Office-Home UDA, ETM-Refine + MROT-Norm (73.4%) outperforms GEMUOT (71.6%) by 1.8 points and JUMBOT alone (70.0%) by 3.4 points. On the more challenging Partial UDA task, MOT + SemiUOT (ETM-Refine + MROT-Norm) achieves 85.2%, beating MOT (80.6%) by 4.6 points, AR (78.3%) by 6.9 points, and m-POT (78.0%) by 7.2 points. On Office-31 and ImageCLEF, the gains over MOT are 1.1 and 1.6 points respectively, confirming consistent improvements across datasets. In solver comparison experiments, ETM-Refine matches ETM-Approx in computation time while achieving near-zero error, whereas ETM-Exact is the most time-consuming due to optimization from a random initialization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of solving UOT/SemiUOT by adding regularization terms (entropy, ℓ₂-norm) that inevitably produce dense and inaccurate transport plans. Instead, it leverages KKT conditions and Fenchel duality to compute the exact marginal probabilities of the relaxed problem, thereby reducing UOT/SemiUOT to a standard OT problem with adjusted marginals. This connects to Choi et al. [21], who identified a similar transformation for continuous UOT, but the authors explicitly note that result does not extend to the discrete SemiUOT/UOT setting; here they provide the discrete counterpart with both exact and approximate solvers. The MROT component further repurposes the KKT multipliers (complementary slackness variables) as an additional regularization signal for the downstream OT solver, a use not explored in prior UOT/SemiUOT literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The exact transformation from (Semi)UOT to classic OT provides a clean theoretical unification and opens the door to using any existing OT solver (Sinkhorn, sparse solvers, etc.) for the relaxed problem, rather than being locked into a single regularization-based algorithm. The MROT idea of injecting KKT multiplier information into the OT objective is a practical technique that could generalize to other constrained optimization settings. Limitations include: the method is restricted to KL-divergence relaxation (not ℓ₁ or ℓ₂), ETM-Exact remains computationally expensive for very large problems, and the practical gains over strong baselines like GEMUOT are moderate (1–3% on domain adaptation). Open questions include scalability to millions of samples, extension to other divergence measures, and whether the multiplier-guided regularization transfers to continuous or multi-marginal settings.
