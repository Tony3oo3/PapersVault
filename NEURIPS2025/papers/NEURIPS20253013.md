## Problem

Existing debiasing methods suffer from a well-documented performance-fairness trade-off, rely on static global corrections that ignore instance-level bias heterogeneity, and lack flexibility in leveraging sensitive attribute labels (requiring either complete annotations or discarding partial information entirely). FairNet addresses these gaps by enabling dynamic, instance-level fairness correction that selectively intervenes only on biased samples while preserving overall model accuracy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253013/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

FairNet operates on face images (64×64, CelebA), premise-hypothesis text pairs (MultiNLI), or social media posts (HateXplain), producing standard class labels. The method consumes intermediate hidden representations from a pre-trained backbone (ViT or BERT/DistilBERT) at selected layers, where a lightweight bias detector scores each instance's likelihood of belonging to a minority group. Sensitive attribute labels (e.g., blond hair, negation presence, race/gender) may be fully available, partially labeled (k% of training data), or entirely absent, with the framework adapting its detection strategy accordingly.

## Method

FairNet integrates two components into a pre-trained model: (1) lightweight bias detectors (attention-pooled MLPs) at intermediate layers that output a risk score for each instance, and (2) parameter-efficient LoRA modules (low-rank matrices A, B with r ≪ min(d,k)) that are conditionally activated only when the detector score exceeds a threshold τ. The LoRA modules are trained exclusively via a novel contrastive triplet loss that minimizes intra-class, inter-group representation gaps—pulling minority-group anchors toward majority-group positives of the same class while pushing away negatives—thereby directly targeting minority underfitting at the representation level. A theoretical analysis proves that under moderate TPR/FPR conditions on the detector, FairNet provably improves worst-group accuracy without diminishing (and potentially slightly enhancing) overall accuracy, breaking the conventional performance-fairness trade-off. The framework supports multiple sensitive attributes via separate detector-LoRA pairs and handles full, partial, or absent label availability through supervised, semi-supervised, or unsupervised (LOF-based) detection strategies.

## Results

On CelebA, FairNet-Full achieves 95.9% ACC, 88.2% WGA, and 3.8% EOD, outperforming all baselines while matching ERM's accuracy. On MultiNLI, FairNet-Full reaches 82.6% ACC, 78.5% WGA, and 4.7% EOD, again leading across all metrics. In the no-label setting, FairNet-Unlabel matches ERM's ACC (95.8% on CelebA, 82.5% on MultiNLI) while improving WGA by 4.4 and 5.8 percentage points respectively, and reducing EOD by 3.3 and 4.4 points. The partial-label variant (FairNet-Partial) achieves the highest ACC in its category (95.9% CelebA, 82.6% MultiNLI) with strong fairness gains over DFR and Sebra.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In the no-sensitive-label setting, FairNet-Unlabel outperforms both Lu et al. [25] and D3M [15] on WGA and EOD across both datasets while matching ERM's ACC (e.g., CelebA WGA 82.3% vs. 81.4% and 82.0%; EOD 7.3% vs. 8.3% and 8.1%). In the partial-label setting, FairNet-Partial dominates DFR [19] and Sebra [18] on all three metrics on both datasets (e.g., CelebA: ACC 95.9% vs. 94.3%/94.8%, WGA 86.5% vs. 86.0%/85.2%, EOD 5.6% vs. 7.7%/8.1%). Against GroupDRO [31], FairNet-Partial trades a small WGA gap (86.5% vs. 87.4% on CelebA) for a 1.9-point ACC advantage (95.9% vs. 94.0%), while FairNet-Full surpasses GroupDRO on all metrics (WGA 88.2% vs. 87.4%, EOD 3.8% vs. 4.7%, ACC 95.9% vs. 94.0%). In the full-label setting, FairNet-Full clearly outperforms both Eq.Odds [10] and GSTAR [16] on every metric on both datasets (e.g., CelebA: WGA 88.2% vs. 83.2%/85.4%, EOD 3.8% vs. 7.2%/6.6%; MultiNLI: WGA 78.5% vs. 75.3%/76.6%, EOD 4.7% vs. 6.3%/6.2%). Overall, FairNet variants achieve the best or near-best WGA and EOD in every label-availability category while maintaining or exceeding the ACC of the strongest baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

FairNet sits at the intersection of parameter-efficient fine-tuning (LoRA, Hu et al. 2022), contrastive representation learning, and group-fairness debiasing. It departs from the dominant paradigm of static, global interventions (pre-processing rebalancing, in-processing regularization à la GroupDRO, post-processing threshold adjustment) by introducing a dynamic, instance-level correction mechanism: a lightweight bias detector gates the activation of LoRA adapters, so corrections are applied only to flagged instances. The contrastive triplet loss for intra-class, inter-group alignment draws on fair representation learning literature but is specifically designed to target minority-group underfitting at the representation level, and the unified handling of full/partial/absent sensitive labels extends the partial-label setting explored by JTT (Liu et al. 2021) into a single architectural framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The performance-fairness trade-off remains the primary practical barrier to deploying fair ML systems in high-stakes domains; FairNet's conditional-activation design and its theoretical guarantee (Condition 8) offer a concrete architectural recipe for breaking this trade-off, which could influence how future debiasing methods are structured. However, the evaluation is limited to three relatively small datasets with binary sensitive attributes, the unsupervised detector (LOF) is a simple baseline, and the progressive multi-attribute debiasing on HateXplain, while promising, does not yet address truly intersectional subgroups. Open questions include scalability to large-scale vision-language models, robustness when the bias detector is adversarially degraded, and whether the contrastive alignment objective generalizes to multi-class, multi-attribute settings without per-attribute module proliferation.
