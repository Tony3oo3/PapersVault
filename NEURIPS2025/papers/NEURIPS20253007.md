## Problem

Existing reflective Gaussian Splatting methods suffer from inconsistent material inference across views because Gaussians cover different pixels with varying alpha-blending weights per viewpoint, causing the BRDF to struggle with ambiguous observations and produce aliased, degenerated illumination decomposition. Additionally, limited environment modeling fails to handle indirect illumination from inter-object occlusions, further degrading novel view synthesis quality on complex reflective scenes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253007/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of posed RGB images with known camera intrinsics and extrinsics as input and produces a 2D Gaussian scene representation (flattened 3D Gaussians with per-Gaussian material attributes: diffuse color, albedo, metallic, roughness) alongside a mip-mapped environment cubemap with multiple roughness levels. At inference time, given a novel camera pose, the system rasterizes material maps via splatting and evaluates a Disney BRDF with split-sum approximation to synthesize the final image, while also enabling extraction of physically meaningful material maps and scene geometry.

## Method

MaterialRefGS enforces multi-view consistent material inference by warping 7×7 pixel patches between adjacent views via homography (using rendered depth and normals) and imposing MSE losses on the diffuse, roughness, and metallic maps to prevent view-specific overfitting. A reflection strength prior is computed by tracking luminance-normalized photometric variations across nearby views, fusing per-view scores into 3D space via ball query, and using the result as adaptive supervision for the metallic attribute in highly reflective regions identified by SAM2 segmentation. For indirect illumination, the method performs differentiable ray tracing through the 2DGS BVH along reflected directions, decomposing incident radiance into a direct component queried from the environment cubemap and an indirect component computed by splatting intersected Gaussians, with an occlusion probability naturally emerging from accumulated transmittance. The full pipeline uses 2DGS as the geometric base, a simplified Disney BRDF with split-sum approximation for deferred shading PBR, and a staged 30k-iteration training schedule that introduces normal priors, PBR, and multi-view regularization at different phases.

## Results

MaterialRefGS achieves state-of-the-art novel view synthesis across all four benchmarks. On ShinyBlender, it reaches 35.57 PSNR / 0.976 SSIM / 0.049 LPIPS / 2.04° MAE, outperforming the strongest baseline Ref-Gaussian (35.04 / 0.973 / 0.056 / 4.59°). On the real-world Ref-Real dataset, it attains 25.04 PSNR and 0.185 LPIPS, surpassing EnvGS (24.85 / 0.215) and Ref-Gaussian (24.61 / 0.252). On the challenging Mip-NeRF 360 dataset, it achieves the best LPIPS (0.181) among all methods, demonstrating strong generalization to complex scenes with fewer reflective surfaces.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MaterialRefGS outperforms all eight baselines on the three primary datasets (ShinyBlender, GlossySynthetic, Ref-Real) across every reported metric. On ShinyBlender, it leads the closest competitor Ref-Gaussian [56] by 0.53 dB in PSNR and nearly halves the MAE (2.04° vs. 4.59°), while also surpassing 3DGS-DR [24] by 1.63 dB in PSNR. On Ref-Real, it beats EnvGS [53] by 0.19 dB PSNR and 0.030 LPIPS, and Ref-Gaussian [56] by 0.43 dB PSNR and 0.067 LPIPS. On the more challenging Mip-NeRF 360 dataset, the method achieves the best LPIPS (0.181) but trails EnvGS [53] slightly in PSNR (27.06 vs. 27.36) and 3DGS [22] in SSIM (0.809 vs. 0.815), which the authors attribute to the scarcity of reflective surfaces in that dataset. NeRF-based baselines (RefNeRF, ENVIDR) are consistently outperformed by 2–6 dB in PSNR across all datasets, confirming the advantage of explicit Gaussian representations for reflective scene rendering.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the deferred-shading PBR pipeline established by Reflective Gaussian Splatting (Yao et al., ICLR 2025) and 3DGS-DR (Keyang et al., SIGGRAPH 2024) by adding two key constraints that prior methods lack: a multi-view material consistency loss (inspired by MVS patch warping) and a reflection strength prior derived from cross-view photometric tracking. It builds on 2DGS (Huang et al., SIGGRAPH 2024) as the geometric base and on EnvGS (Xie et al., CVPR 2025) for Gaussian ray tracing, but replaces EnvGS's offline binary visibility indicator with a fully differentiable occlusion probability estimated on-the-fly. The conceptual departure from prior work is the argument that per-view material optimization is fundamentally ill-posed without explicit multi-view geometric consistency, shifting the burden from the BRDF to the rasterization stage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The multi-view consistency constraint and reflection strength prior offer a practical recipe for stabilizing material inference in reflective scenes, which is a persistent failure mode in Gaussian splatting-based rendering. However, the method is explicitly not an inverse rendering approach—it cannot perform relighting or quantitatively evaluate material decomposition, limiting its applicability to appearance synthesis rather than physical material recovery. Open questions include whether the SAM2-based segmentation and foreground masking generalize to unbounded or dynamic scenes, and whether the 30k-iteration training budget with staged loss introduction can be reduced. The differentiable ray tracing for indirect illumination is a step toward more physically grounded environment modeling, but higher-order inter-reflections remain approximated by a residual term.
