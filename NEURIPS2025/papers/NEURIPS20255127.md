## Problem

Existing positional encoding methods for transformers assume a predefined sequential order among tokens, rendering them inapplicable to real-world tabular data where features (e.g., genes, proteins, economic indicators) are non-sequential yet causally related. While preliminary efforts in single-cell genomics impose pseudo-orders based on expression levels or static embeddings, they neglect the underlying causal structure that could serve as a principled positional signal.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255127/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a tabular measurement matrix X ∈ R^{N×M} (N observations × M non-sequential features, e.g., gene expression counts) and static pretrained feature embeddings as input. It produces causality-aware rotary positional encodings φ_{v_j} ∈ R^d for each feature, which modulate self-attention via rotation matrices rather than additive offsets, yielding contextualized causality-aware feature embeddings and aggregated observation-level embeddings for downstream tasks such as gene perturbation prediction, cell clustering, and age prediction.

## Method

CAPE is a three-step pipeline: (1) a generalized nonlinear structural equation model with neural variational inference (ELBO objective) and the NO TEARS acyclicity constraint learns a weighted DAG capturing causal relationships among features; (2) the DAG is embedded in hyperbolic space via the hyperboloid model, optimized with Riemannian SGD under a regularized graph contrastive loss that encodes causal strength as pairwise hyperbolic distance and causal specificity as distance-to-origin weighted by PageRank centrality; (3) hyperboloid embeddings are mapped to the Poincaré ball via diffeomorphism and converted into rotary form, where Poincaré ball coordinates (scaled by π/4) serve as rotation angles in a RoPE-style query-key modulation. Theoretical analysis proves three properties of the resulting attention: scores attenuate with increasing causal distance, attenuate with increasing causal generality of the attending feature, and remain robust to small Gaussian positional disturbances.

## Results

On the gene perturbation prediction (GPP) task with scRNA-seq data (Norman et al. 2019), CAPE achieves the lowest MSE across all settings: 0.193 (single-gene) and 0.189 (double-gene) with scBERT, and 0.182 (single-gene) and 0.176 (double-gene) with scGPT. This represents an 11.1% average MSE reduction over the respective default positional encodings, compared to only a 2.7% reduction from the causality-agnostic trainable relative encoder. The gains are most pronounced on the harder double-gene perturbation task, where CAPE reduces MSE by 0.041 (scBERT) and 0.025 (scGPT) relative to defaults.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CAPE is evaluated against two types of baselines within two transformer backbones (scBERT and scGPT). Against the default positional encodings (static absolute for scBERT, trainable absolute for scGPT), CAPE wins on all four perturbation settings, reducing MSE by 0.031/0.041 (scBERT) and 0.020/0.025 (scGPT). Against the causality-agnostic trainable relative positional encoding (Transformer-XL style), CAPE also wins on all four settings, with margins of 0.026/0.026 (scBERT) and 0.013/0.028 (scGPT). Notably, the trainable relative encoder actually degrades scGPT's double-gene performance (0.204 vs. 0.201 default), whereas CAPE consistently improves both backbones. The paper reports an 11.1% average MSE reduction for CAPE over defaults versus only 2.7% for the relative encoder, highlighting that causal structure awareness—not merely relative position modeling—drives the gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

CAPE unifies three previously separate research threads—continuous DAG structure learning (DAGs with NO TEARS, DAG-GNN), hyperbolic graph embedding (Lorentz/Poincaré models), and rotary positional encoding (RoPE)—into a single pipeline for generating positional encodings from learned causal structure. It departs from all existing positional encoding methods (sinusoidal, trainable absolute/relative, RoPE) by eliminating the assumption of a predefined token order, instead deriving positions from a weighted DAG inferred via generalized SEM. In the single-cell genomics context, it extends prior transformer-based foundation models (scBERT, scGPT) that impose pseudo-orderings via expression levels or static gene embeddings, by replacing these with causality-aware positions that encode both causal strength and specificity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CAPE opens a principled path for applying transformer architectures to tabular, non-sequential data with latent causal structure (multi-omics, economic indicators, clinical features), a setting where standard positional encodings are inapplicable. The theoretical guarantees—causal distance-induced and generality-induced attention attenuation, plus robustness to positional noise—provide interpretability that purely learned positional encodings lack. Key open questions include scalability to very large feature sets (thousands of genes), sensitivity to misspecified causal structure (confounders, unobserved variables), and whether the causal DAG assumption generalizes beyond biological systems to other domains like economics or social science.
