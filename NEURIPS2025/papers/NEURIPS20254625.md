## Problem

Fine-tuning diffusion models on imbalanced image-text datasets causes severe performance degradation for minority classes, as the model overfits to majority data. Existing long-tailed generation methods (e.g., CBDM) rely on one-hot class labels and fail to generalize to the modern setting where generation is conditioned on free-form text prompts, making them unable to model similarity among different text descriptions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254625/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a natural-language text prompt as input, which is encoded by a frozen CLIP text encoder into a fixed embedding vector; it produces a generated RGB image via the Stable Diffusion v1.5 latent diffusion pipeline (DDIM sampling, 50 steps). The fine-tuning operates in the VAE latent space, and the datasets are deliberately imbalanced with majority classes containing 30–500 images per identity and minority classes containing only 2 images, making the task a few-shot identity-conditioned generation problem.

## Method

PoGDiff replaces the ground-truth Gaussian in the standard diffusion KL-divergence objective with a Product of Gaussians (PoG) formed by multiplying the original ground-truth target with the model's own predicted distribution conditioned on a sampled neighboring text embedding y′. The resulting loss decomposes into the standard denoising term plus a consistency term weighted by ψ, which combines image-content similarity (cosine similarity of DINO features, modulated by whether the two images share the same identity) and inverse text density (estimated via a small VAE's ELBO to up-weight minority prompts). Theoretically, this is shown to be equivalent to training a standard diffusion model while encouraging it to produce the same image for semantically similar text conditions, effectively borrowing statistical strength from neighboring data points to smooth the effective density in the minority region. A new evaluation metric, gRecall, is introduced to measure generative diversity under strict accuracy constraints by checking what fraction of training-set images per identity are 'covered' by at least one generated image.

## Results

PoGDiff consistently outperforms all baselines across four imbalanced datasets (AgeDB, DigiFace, VGGFace2, CIFAR-100-LT) on five metrics. On the most dramatic metric (DINO cosine similarity), PoGDiff achieves 0.77/0.73 (All/Few) on AgeDB-IT2I-Small versus 0.42/0.37 for Vanilla and 0.54/0.09 for CBDM. On human evaluation (binary identity match), PoGDiff reaches a perfect 1.00/1.00 on AgeDB-IT2I-Small while Vanilla and T2H score 0.50/0.00 and CBDM scores 0.50/0.00. On FID (lower is better), PoGDiff achieves 6.03/10.16 (All/Few) on AgeDB-IT2I-Large, improving over Vanilla (7.67/11.67) and CBDM (7.18/11.12). The proposed gRecall metric shows PoGDiff at 0.800/1.000 on AgeDB-IT2I-Small versus 0.017/0.000 for Vanilla and T2H.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PoGDiff decisively outperforms both baselines on every metric and every dataset. Against CBDM [20], the gap is most extreme on AgeDB-IT2I-Small where CBDM's DINO score collapses to 0.09 for few-shot (minority) classes while PoGDiff reaches 0.73; the paper attributes CBDM's failure to its sampling of text conditions from the entire embedding space, which is ill-suited for natural-language prompts versus one-hot class labels. Against T2H [37], PoGDiff also wins across the board, though T2H performs closer to Vanilla since it was adapted from a one-hot setting by substituting text-embedding density for class frequency. On GPT-4o identity scoring (0–10), PoGDiff achieves 9.10/8.40 (All/Few) on AgeDB-IT2I-Small versus 5.20/3.20 (Vanilla), 4.50/1.10 (CBDM), and 5.50/3.10 (T2H). The proposed gRecall metric further highlights PoGDiff's advantage in generative diversity under strict accuracy constraints: 1.000 for few-shot on AgeDB-IT2I-Small versus 0.000 for all three baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

PoGDiff builds directly on the standard denoising diffusion training objective (Ho et al., 2020) and its latent-space variant (Rombach et al., 2022 / Stable Diffusion), but replaces the ground-truth Gaussian target in the KL-divergence loss with a Product of Gaussians that fuses the original target with the model's own prediction under a neighboring text embedding. This departs from the long-tailed generation literature (CBDM, T2H), which assumes one-hot class labels and reweights or augments features, by operating in the continuous text-embedding space with natural language prompts. The PoG construction draws on the algebraic property that the product of two Gaussians is again a Gaussian, repurposing a simple distributional identity as a training-time regularizer that implicitly up-weights minority samples.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical and underexplored gap: fine-tuning pretrained diffusion models on small, imbalanced real-world datasets (e.g., employee photos) where minority identities are generated poorly or not at all. The proposed gRecall metric fills an evaluation void by measuring coverage of training-set diversity under strict accuracy constraints, complementing FID which the authors show can mask identity errors. Key limitations include the requirement of at least two images per minority class, sensitivity to neighbor selection quality, and the fact that the method is a fine-tuning technique rather than a from-scratch training solution; extending PoGDiff to sparser regimes, other modalities (video, time series), and training from scratch remain open.
