## Problem

The Birkhoff relaxation is a tight convex relaxation of the quadratic assignment problem (QAP) for graph alignment with strong empirical performance, yet it lacked any theoretical guarantees on when its optimal solution recovers the true vertex correspondence. Prior theoretical results covered looser relaxations (GRAMPA, simplex) but not the Birkhoff polytope constraint, leaving a gap between practice and theory. This paper provides the first such guarantees, establishing a phase transition in the relaxation's behavior as a function of noise level.

## Input / Output

The method takes two n×n weighted adjacency matrices A and B drawn from the Gaussian Wigner Model, where B is a noisy, permuted copy of A with additive GOE noise of level σ (correlation 1/√(1+σ²)). The output is the optimal doubly stochastic matrix X* solving the Birkhoff relaxation, which is then rounded to a permutation via argmax-per-row or Hungarian projection to recover the vertex correspondence. The analysis is asymptotic in n with σ allowed to depend on n, and the key regime boundary is σ ~ n^{-1} for guaranteed recovery and σ ~ n^{-0.5} for guaranteed separation.

## Method

The proof technique is a sensitivity analysis of the convex program: for the small-perturbation regime (σ ≤ n^{-1-ε}), the authors construct an approximately feasible dual solution (R, M, μ, μ̃) for the σ=0 version of the Birkhoff relaxation, using the eigenvector basis of A to parameterize the n² dual variables associated with non-negativity constraints. The dual certificate R = J − I forces the off-diagonal entries of X* to be small, yielding ‖X* − I‖_F² ≤ 10n^{1-ε/32}. For the well-separation regime (σ ≥ n^{-0.5+ε}), a lower bound on the objective ‖AX* − X*B‖_F² in terms of the noise term σ²‖Z‖_F², combined with an upper bound from evaluating the objective at J/n, forces ‖X* − I‖_F ≥ δ√n. The critical technical difficulty is handling the non-negativity constraints, which introduce n² dual variables and require careful eigenvalue-separation bounds (via Nguyen–Tao–Vu tail bounds) to control the dual norm.

## Results

Theorem 1 establishes a phase transition for the Birkhoff relaxation: the optimal solution X* is a small perturbation of the true permutation Π* (‖X*−Π*‖_F² ≤ 10n^{1−ε/32}) when σ ≤ n^{−1−ε}, and is well-separated (‖X*−Π*‖_F² ≥ δn) when σ ≥ n^{−0.5+ε}. Empirically (n=400), the Birkhoff relaxation correctly aligns all vertices for σ up to 0.5, while Simplex degrades at σ=0.4 and GRAMPA at σ=0.2. A log-log regression of the empirical transition point (where ‖X*−Π*‖_F/√n = 0.5) yields a slope of −0.45, supporting the theoretical phase transition at σ = Θ(n^{−0.5}).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares the Birkhoff relaxation against three baselines: the spectral method EIG1 [16], the regularized convex relaxation GRAMPA [11], and the simplex relaxation [2]. Theoretically, GRAMPA achieves the strongest noise threshold (σ = O(1/log n)), followed by Birkhoff (σ = O(n^{−1})), EIG1 (σ = Θ(n^{−7/6})), and Simplex (σ = 0 only). However, empirically the Birkhoff relaxation substantially outperforms both GRAMPA and Simplex: at n=400, Birkhoff aligns all vertices up to σ=0.5, while GRAMPA degrades at σ=0.2 and Simplex at σ=0.4. The Birkhoff relaxation also avoids the need to tune a regularization parameter η (required by GRAMPA) and preserves the non-negativity constraints (dropped by GRAMPA). The well-separation result (σ ≥ n^{−0.5+ε}) shows that X* moves far from Π*, yet post-processing via Hungarian projection still recovers the correct permutation in practice, suggesting the Birkhoff relaxation may succeed beyond the theoretical threshold.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper provides the first theoretical guarantees for the Birkhoff relaxation of the quadratic assignment problem, a tighter convex relaxation than the GRAMPA algorithm [11] (which drops non-negativity and adds a quadratic regularizer) and the simplex relaxation [2] (which drops column-sum constraints and only works at σ=0). The proof technique—constructing a high-dimensional dual certificate using the eigenvector basis of the GOE matrix A—draws on sensitivity analysis of convex programs but is novel in its application to the Birkhoff polytope setting with n² dual variables arising from non-negativity constraints. The analysis is situated within the Gaussian Wigner Model framework established by [15, 32], and the empirical comparison with GRAMPA and simplex relaxations positions Birkhoff as the strongest convex relaxation in practice.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The phase transition result (X* transitions from a small perturbation of Π* to being well-separated at σ ~ n^{-0.5}) gives the first principled understanding of when the Birkhoff relaxation succeeds, explaining its strong empirical performance that had previously lacked theoretical justification. A key limitation is the gap between the proven sufficient condition (σ=o(n^{-1})) and the population-level prediction (σ=o(n^{-0.5})), leaving open whether the Birkhoff relaxation can provably succeed in the intermediate regime. The empirical observation that Hungarian projection on X* recovers the correct permutation even when X* is far from Π* (for σ up to 0.5 at n=400) suggests the relaxation retains useful signal beyond the theoretical threshold, pointing to a promising but unresolved direction for post-processing guarantees.
