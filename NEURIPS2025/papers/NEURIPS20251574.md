## Problem

Existing pedestrian behavior datasets emphasize observable trajectories and actions but provide limited insight into the causal reasoning behind human interpretation and response during traffic interactions. Prior intention datasets (e.g., PIE) annotate only at isolated critical frames, conflate future-directed and present-directed intentions, and exclude verbal reasoning, leaving models unable to capture the dynamic, context-dependent evolution of crossing intent that drives real-time driving decisions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251574/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each 15-second encounter (30 fps video, 1 fps GPS/speed) is annotated by 9–24 licensed drivers via a Point-and-Explain browser platform, producing frame-by-frame intention segmentation curves (Cross / Not Cross / Not Sure) with polygonal boundaries marking intention changes, paired driver behavior labels (speed and direction), and multi-prompt textual reasoning at every boundary. The eP2P model consumes 15-frame (0.5 s) keyframe sequences plus bounding boxes and optional reasoning text, and outputs a crossing-intent label at the 16th frame, a generated reasoning explanation, and trajectory distributions at 0.5 s, 1.0 s, and 1.5 s horizons with evidential uncertainty estimates.

## Method

The PSI dataset introduces a present-directed intention definition (crossing in front of the ego vehicle before it arrives) annotated continuously across all frames by up to 24 diverse annotators, yielding 987k intention estimates and 5,773 reasoning segments with explicit inter-annotator disagreement scores. The eP2P model couples two modules: an intent-prediction branch that fuses a visual encoder, a text encoder over reasoning annotations, and a global-local context fusion network to predict the crossing label and generate an explanation, and a trajectory-prediction branch whose encoder-decoder integrates observed positions with the predicted intent and an evidential uncertainty layer. A key training insight is reweighting the binary cross-entropy loss by the per-frame disagreement score to down-weight ambiguous cases, and jointly training the reasoning generator to improve intent accuracy by 1.4% over the PIE baseline while producing human-aligned explanations.

## Results

On the PSI dataset, the proposed eP2P model with disagreement reweighting and reasoning annotations achieves 63.09% accuracy, 58.55% F1, and 62.53% class-wise average accuracy for pedestrian situated intent prediction, outperforming the strongest baseline PIE (59.04% Acc, 56.13% F1) by over 4 percentage points in accuracy. For trajectory prediction at 1.5 s, eP2P achieves ADE 45.55 px and FDE 97.00 px, surpassing BiTraP-NP (48.37/102.54) and PIE (52.61/109.45). In the reasoning generation task, all evaluated VLMs (Qwen-VL, InternVL, SmolVLM, LLaMA3.2 Vision) score below 0.03 CIDEr and below 0.20 ROUGE, indicating that current vision-language models remain far from matching human-quality intent reasoning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

eP2P outperforms all trajectory and intent baselines. On intent prediction, eP2P+disagr.+rsn (63.09% Acc) beats PIE (59.04%) by 4.05 points and Transformer (56.66%) by 6.43 points; the disagreement reweighting alone adds ~1.3% over eP2P, and the reasoning module adds a further ~1.4%. On trajectory prediction at 1.5 s, eP2P (ADE 45.55, FDE 97.00) improves over BiTraP-NP (48.37, 102.54) by ~2.8 px ADE and over PIE (52.61, 109.45) by ~7 px ADE, while SGNet (67.38, 143.01) and LSTM (86.98, 176.74) trail substantially. For reasoning generation, all four VLMs (Qwen-VL, InternVL, SmolVLM, LLaMA3.2 Vision) achieve CIDEr scores between 0.019–0.023 and ROUGE below 0.20, far below the quality of human annotator explanations, confirming that current multimodal models cannot yet replicate human-level causal reasoning for pedestrian intent. The paper also shows that adding evidential uncertainty to SGNet reduces its 1.5 s ADE from 67.38 to 63.49, validating the uncertainty module independently.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the pedestrian intention prediction line initiated by PIE [43], which first applied crowd-sourcing to label crossing intentions at a single critical frame. PSI departs from PIE and other prior datasets (JAAD, LOKI, STIP, TITAN) in three conceptual ways: it redefines intention as 'present-directed' and temporally bounded to the ego-vehicle's path rather than a general desire to cross; it captures frame-by-frame dynamic intention changes with inter-annotator disagreement (soft labels) rather than a single static label; and it introduces multi-prompt textual reasoning annotations linked to visual evidence via a Point-and-Explain UI. The eP2P model builds on standard sequence architectures (LSTM, Transformer) and trajectory prediction methods (BiTraP, SGNet) but adds a text encoder for reasoning and an evidential uncertainty head, positioning it at the intersection of explainable AI and pedestrian behavior modeling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PSI is the first benchmark to jointly annotate pedestrian crossing intention, driving decisions, human reasoning, and inter-annotator disagreement in naturalistic driving video, filling a gap that limits current models' ability to produce interpretable, human-aligned predictions. The LMM experiments reveal that even frontier models (GPT-4o, Gemini 2.5 Pro, DeepSeek R1) fail at basic pedestrian detection and produce hallucinated or irrelevant reasoning, underscoring that current VLMs are not yet reliable for safety-critical intent estimation. Open questions include whether 196 encounters suffice for robust generalization, whether the modest ~1.4% accuracy gain from reasoning annotations scales with model size, and how the framework extends to multi-pedestrian or multi-vehicle negotiation scenarios. The dataset's public release and standardized evaluation protocol position it as a potential reference benchmark for interpretable pedestrian-vehicle interaction research.
