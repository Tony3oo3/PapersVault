## Problem

Stepwise Preference Optimization (SPO) aligns diffusion models with human preferences at intermediate denoising steps, but its underlying mechanisms remain underexplored. The paper identifies two critical issues hidden by SPO's likelihood-centric formulation: a biased temporal weighting that disproportionately underweights later generative steps (where fine-grained details are formed), and substantial unquantified noise in the gradient estimates used for preference learning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253219/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method fine-tunes pre-trained latent diffusion models (Stable Diffusion 1.5 and SDXL) using text prompts as conditioning. During training, intermediate latent states are generated online via DDIM sampling (20 steps, CFG scale 5.0), and a step-aware reward model selects winning/losing candidate samples at each transition. The output is a LoRA-adapted diffusion model that, at inference time, takes a text prompt and produces a 512×512 (SD 1.5) or 1024×1024 (SDXL) RGB image with improved aesthetic quality and prompt fidelity.

## Method

GradSPO reinterprets SPO's argmax/argmin noise selection as a noisy approximation of the reward model's gradient, reframing the entire objective as DPO applied to guided score functions (original score ± γ∇r). This exposes SPO's implicit non-uniform timestep weighting, which GradSPO corrects by setting both the weighting function a(t) and guidance scale γ_t to constants (a(t)=1, γ=0.5). To reduce the inherent noise in the gradient approximation, GradSPO introduces a max-min estimator z_max-min = ±(z⁺−z⁻)/2 that provably halves the variance (Theorem 2), and applies EMA (μ=0.9) to the model parameters used for target construction. Theorem 1 establishes that the resulting GradSPO loss with noisy targets is an upper bound on the ideal clean-gradient loss, providing a principled variational justification for minimizing the practical objective. The final loss (Eq. 16) pulls the current noise prediction toward the EMA-based winning target while pushing it away from the losing target, with stop-gradient on both targets to prevent signal collapse.

## Results

On SDXL (Pick-a-Pic v2), GradSPO achieves the best score across all four preference metrics: HPSv2 28.93, PickScore 23.45, Aesthetic Score 6.2985, and Image Reward 1.0861, surpassing SPO (its direct precursor) by 0.66 HPSv2 points and 0.075 Image Reward. On SD 1.5, GradSPO attains the highest PickScore (21.38) and Aesthetic Score (5.7651), ties InPO on HPSv2 (26.86), and trails InPO only on Image Reward (0.4747 vs. 0.5135). A user study with five judges confirms a 75.6% win rate for GradSPO over vanilla SDXL and 53.0% over SPO, with the largest gains in aesthetic quality (81.1% and 56.2% respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SPO (the direct precursor), GradSPO wins on every metric for both backbones: on SDXL it improves HPSv2 from 28.27 to 28.93, PickScore from 22.93 to 23.45, Aesthetic from 6.2236 to 6.2985, and Image Reward from 0.9982 to 1.0861; on SD 1.5 it lifts PickScore from 21.11 to 21.38 and Aesthetic from 5.5898 to 5.7651. Against InPO, GradSPO ties on HPSv2 for SD 1.5 (both 26.86) and wins on PickScore (21.38 vs. 21.21) and Aesthetic (5.7651 vs. 5.4674), but InPO retains a narrow edge on Image Reward (0.5135 vs. 0.4747); on SDXL GradSPO dominates InPO across all four metrics. Against Diffusion DPO, GradSPO leads on every metric for both backbones (e.g., SDXL HPSv2 28.93 vs. 27.81). Against MaPO (SDXL only), GradSPO wins on all four metrics, with the largest gap on Image Reward (1.0861 vs. 0.5868). The user study further confirms these quantitative gains, with GradSPO preferred over SPO 53.0% of the time and over vanilla SDXL 75.6% of the time.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reinterprets Stepwise Preference Optimization (SPO) through a gradient guidance and score-matching lens, building directly on SPO's stepwise preference framework [10] and the key insight from Huang et al. [14] that argmax/min noise in diffusion transitions approximates a scaled, noisy reward gradient. Rather than treating SPO as a likelihood-ratio objective (as in the DPO lineage from [6, 12]), the authors show SPO is a special case of a more general guided score-matching loss (GradSPO) with specific time-dependent weighting and guidance scale choices. This reframing connects SPO to the broader literature on classifier-free guidance and interval guidance [15], while exposing limitations—biased timestep weighting and uncontrolled gradient noise—that the likelihood-based view obscures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The gradient guidance perspective provides a principled, simpler training recipe (uniform timestep weighting, fixed guidance scale, max-min variance reduction, EMA) that consistently outperforms SPO across SD 1.5 and SDXL on multiple preference metrics, with the added benefit of reduced memory overhead compared to clean-gradient approaches. However, GradSPO remains limited to single-step preference optimization and still trails Diff-DPO on compositional fidelity (GenEval), suggesting that aesthetic reward signals dominate over fine-grained structural alignment. Open questions include extending the framework to multi-step settings, integrating more sophisticated gradient guidance techniques (e.g., manifold-constrained guidance), and understanding whether the uniform-weighting advantage generalizes beyond the Pick-a-Pic evaluation protocol.
