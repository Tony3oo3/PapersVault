## Problem

Active Target Discovery (ATD) in partially observable environments typically relies on a strong prior learned from domain-specific samples (e.g., DiffATD), but in data-scarce settings—rare species discovery, emerging disease diagnostics, underexplored geographic regions—such priors are unavailable or prohibitively expensive to obtain. This paper asks: how can one perform effective, budget-constrained active target discovery when the initial prior is entirely uninformative about the target domain?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252163/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a partially observable search grid of N cells, a frozen pretrained diffusion model (trained on unrelated data such as ImageNet or MNIST, serving as permanent memory), and a strict query budget B. At each step it receives the content and target fraction y ∈ [0,1] of the queried cell. It outputs a sequence of next-query locations ranked by a combined exploration-exploitation score, posterior samples of the full search space conditioned on all observations so far, and ultimately the set of identified target cells. The h-transform module is updated on an adaptive schedule (infrequent early, frequent late) rather than after every single observation.

## Method

EM-PTDM pairs a frozen pretrained diffusion model (permanent memory) with a lightweight Doob's h-transform network (transient memory) to enable conditional posterior sampling without domain-specific prior data. The h-transform is trained via a denoising score-matching objective that requires only forward passes through the frozen model, keeping it shallow and rapidly adaptable to sparse observations. An EM-style update loop guarantees monotonic improvement of the expected log-evidence at each h-model update, with Theorem 1 formalizing this for the composite prior φ = (θ*, ζ). The sampling strategy ranks unobserved cells by a budget-weighted combination of an exploration score (posterior sample disagreement, derived from a Gaussian mixture approximation) and an exploitation score (reward-model-weighted expected log-likelihood), with the balance α(B) shifting from exploration to exploitation as the budget depletes. An adaptive scheduler controls h-model update frequency, starting infrequent to avoid overfitting to early noisy observations and accelerating as informative data accumulates.

## Results

EM-PTDM is evaluated using Success Rate (SR), the fraction of target-containing cells identified within a fixed query budget. On the species discovery task (iNaturalist), EM-PTDM achieves SR of 0.4983, 0.6495, and 0.6989 at budgets B=150, 200, and 250 respectively, outperforming DiffATD (0.3420/0.4365/0.4808) and Greedy-Adaptive (0.4061/0.5067/0.5567). On the more challenging cross-view overhead object discovery task (DOTA with ImageNet prior), EM-PTDM reaches SR of 0.5620, 0.7013, and 0.8256 at B=250, 300, and 350, surpassing DiffATD (0.5143/0.6391/0.7348) and GA (0.4784/0.5659/0.6562). The performance gap over baselines widens with increasing budget, reflecting the cumulative benefit of the monotonically improving prior.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EM-PTDM consistently outperforms all three baselines across both experimental settings and all budget levels. Against DiffATD [1], the closest diffusion-based competitor, EM-PTDM gains approximately 0.10–0.16 in SR on the species task and 0.05–0.09 on the overhead object task, with the advantage growing as the budget increases (e.g., 0.8256 vs. 0.7348 at B=350 on DOTA). Against Greedy-Adaptive, which relies solely on exploitation via a reward model, EM-PTDM leads by 0.09–0.14 in SR, demonstrating the value of the exploration-exploitation balance driven by the updated prior. Random Search serves as a lower bound and is substantially outperformed by all structured methods. Notably, EM-PTDM operates without any domain-specific prior samples, whereas DiffATD requires a strong domain-matched prior, making the comparison particularly favorable to EM-PTDM in the uninformative-prior regime the paper targets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends DiffATD [1], which uses a domain-specific pretrained diffusion model as a strong prior to guide active target discovery in partially observable environments. The key departure is removing the requirement for domain-specific prior samples: EM-PTDM pairs a generic pretrained diffusion model (permanent memory) with a lightweight Doob's h-transform module (transient memory) that rapidly adapts to task-specific observations via denoising score matching. The EM framework provides monotonic improvement guarantees for the evolving prior, while the sampling strategy draws on Bayesian experiment design (maximum-entropy exploration plus reward-weighted exploitation). The neuroscience-inspired dual-memory framing (neocortex/hippocampus analogy from Kumaran [2]) motivates the architectural split between slow generalization and fast adaptation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This addresses a critical practical gap: many high-stakes ATD scenarios (emerging disease diagnostics, rare species discovery, novel geographic regions) lack the domain-specific training data that DiffATD and related methods require. The monotonic improvement guarantee and interpretable white-box policy make the approach more trustworthy for deployment in safety-critical settings. Open questions include how the method scales to very large search spaces (the posterior sampling cost grows with grid resolution), whether the h-transform generalizes across highly dissimilar domains, and how sensitive performance is to the quality of the generic pretrained diffusion model. The observation that target discovery does not require full search-space reconstruction—only accurate modeling of target-rich regions—suggests future work could further decouple discovery from generative fidelity.
