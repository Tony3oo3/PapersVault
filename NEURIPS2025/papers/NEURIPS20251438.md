## Problem

Hernandez et al. (2023) showed that individual relational facts in transformer LLMs can be decoded by a single linear operator (LRE matrix), but the collective organization of these operators across many relations remained unexplored. This paper asks whether a collection of LRE matrices shares exploitable structure, whether it can be compressed into a compact shared representation, and what semantic principles underlie any such compressibility.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251438/cropped_page3_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes two types of d-dimensional vectors from a frozen transformer (d=4096 for GPT-J): a subject embedding v_S and a relation embedding v_R. The tensor network is contracted with v_R to produce a d×d affine decoder matrix, which is then applied to v_S and passed through the transformer's output head to yield a predicted object token distribution. The unusual aspect is that a single compact tensor network (with inner bottleneck dimensions as small as 2–300) replaces an entire collection of 47–79 independent d×d matrices, reducing parameter counts from ~788M to under 1M while preserving decoding faithfulness.

## Method

The paper introduces two order-3 tensor network architectures—SimpleOrder3Network (a single core 3-tensor with three projection matrices) and TriangleTensorNetwork (three interconnected 3-tensors forming a triangular core)—that jointly parameterize all relation decoder matrices. Training is end-to-end with the LLM frozen: the tensor network is optimized via SGD on cross-entropy loss over predicted object tokens, with the bias term embedded by augmenting bond dimensions. A cross-evaluation protocol (applying each decoder to every other relation's subjects) reveals that LREs function as coarse-grained property extractors (e.g., 'country-of-X', 'gender-of-X') rather than fine-grained relation-specific maps, explaining both the high compressibility and the limited generalization to semantically distant held-out relations. On a controlled arithmetic dataset with linearly structured relations, the tensor network achieves near-perfect generalization (0.96 mean faithfulness on unseen relations), confirming that the bottleneck on general language data is the non-linear, sparse organization of semantic properties.

## Results

On the Hernandez et al. [2023] dataset (47 relations), order-3 tensor networks with fewer than 1 million parameters significantly outperform the vanilla stacked-matrix representation (~788M parameters, mean faithfulness 0.41) and individually trained low-rank baselines in both parameter count and decoding faithfulness. On the extended dataset (79 relations), tensor-network LREs achieve a mean sample-wise test faithfulness of 0.42 versus a majority-guess baseline of 0.30, outperforming the majority baseline in 49 of 79 relations. On the controlled mathematical dataset (50 arithmetic relations), the tensor network achieves 0.992 ± 0.012 training faithfulness and 0.96 ± 0.031 held-out relation faithfulness (max 0.991), demonstrating near-perfect generalization to unseen arithmetic operators.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is the Jacobian-based LRE approximation from Hernandez et al. [2023], which estimates each relation decoder from 8 subject-object examples and achieves a mean faithfulness of 0.41 with approximately 788 million parameters. The tensor network approach dramatically outperforms this baseline: with fewer than 1 million parameters (a >780× reduction), the tensor networks achieve higher faithfulness on the 47-relation dataset, and on the extended 79-relation dataset they outperform the Jacobian baseline in 12 of 20 held-out relations during relation-wise generalization. A second baseline is individually trained low-rank LRE matrices (one per relation, no parameter sharing); the tensor networks consistently surpass these, indicating that the compression gains come not merely from low-rank structure of individual matrices but from shared structure across the relation collection. A third baseline is majority-guess (predicting the most frequent object per relation); on the extended dataset the tensor network achieves 0.42 mean test faithfulness versus 0.30 for majority guess, and on the mathematical dataset the gap is even more pronounced (0.96 vs. near-zero for majority). The paper also ablates with and without an additional nonlinear relation embedder, finding that the simpler linear formulation consistently performs better, confirming that the relation embeddings already carry sufficient structure for the tensor network to exploit.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the single-relation linear decoder framework of Hernandez et al. (ICLR 2023), who showed that individual subject-to-object transformations in transformers can be approximated by a single affine LRE matrix, to the collective analysis of many relations simultaneously. Rather than treating each LRE as an independent object, the authors introduce order-3 tensor networks as a shared-parameter compression mechanism and a cross-evaluation protocol to measure functional similarity between decoders. The tensor-network compression approach is conceptually related to prior work on tensor decompositions for neural network compression (Novikov et al. 2015; Ren et al. 2022; Anjum et al. 2024), but differs in that it compresses a collection of externally-derived decoder matrices rather than internal weight tensors. The property-based interpretation of LREs connects to broader knowledge-representation work (Geva et al. 2021, 2023; Li et al. 2021) and to Chanin et al. (2024), who showed that pseudoinverses of LREs yield usable linear probes for relational concepts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that LREs function as coarse-grained property extractors (e.g., 'country-of-X') rather than fine-grained relation-specific mappings reframes how we think about relational knowledge in transformers, with implications for interpretability, targeted knowledge editing, and efficient compression. The ~788× parameter reduction (788M → <1M) while maintaining or improving faithfulness is practically significant for deploying relation-aware modules in resource-constrained settings. However, generalization to held-out relations remains limited on natural language data (only working when test relations are semantically near-duplicates of training ones), and the gap to the near-perfect arithmetic generalization (0.96 faithfulness) is unexplained. Open questions include whether the property-centric structure persists in larger or instruction-tuned models, how to systematically enumerate the full relation space, and whether richer (non-linear) tensor network architectures could close the generalization gap on general language relations.
