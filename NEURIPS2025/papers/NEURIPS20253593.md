## Problem

Automated brick kiln detection from satellite imagery has been limited by reliance on costly high-resolution commercial imagery (e.g., Planet Labs) and non-public datasets, hindering reproducibility and scalability. No prior work provided hand-validated oriented bounding box (OBB) annotations on freely available low-resolution satellite imagery, leaving a gap in benchmarking OBB detection under realistic 10 m resolution constraints across a continental scale.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 128×128-pixel RGB patches (B2/B3/B4 bands) from Sentinel-2 Surface Reflectance at 10 m/pixel, extracted from 11,000×11,000-pixel tiles with a 0.0027° (~30-pixel) sliding-window overlap to capture edge kilns, then deduplicated by geographic coordinates. Outputs are OBB labels (center x, center y, width, height, rotation angle) and axis-aligned bounding boxes in YOLO and DOTA .txt formats, each paired with a .png image named by center lat,lon. The dataset spans 114,300 images with 97,648 bounding boxes across three kiln classes (CFCBK, FCBK, Zigzag) plus 41,068 curated negative tiles from ESA WorldCover v200.

## Method

The dataset is built through an iterative detection pipeline: (i) train YOLOv11L-OBB on existing labels, (ii) predict kilns on new regions using 4.77 m Planet mosaics, (iii) manually validate each predicted OBB and kiln type via a custom Leafmap interface over Esri Wayback imagery with expert adjudication for edge cases, and (iv) append validated results—repeated over three passes to expand from 30,638 to 62,671 kilns across India, Bangladesh, Pakistan, and Afghanistan. OBB coordinates are reprojected from Web Mercator (Planet) to UTM (Sentinel-2) using geolocation metadata, verified via visual inspection and IoU metrics. For benchmarking, 28 state-of-the-art detectors spanning one-stage, two-stage, DETR-based, and remote-sensing foundation model families are fine-tuned and evaluated on four tasks: in-region detection, out-of-region/LOCO generalization, seasonal (LOSO) temporal robustness, and super-resolution enhancement (Bilinear, SwinIR, ESRGAN, HiT, Stable Diffusion). Key findings include that remote-sensing foundation models (TerraMind, Galileo) achieve the highest CA mAP50 (~87%), AA-based detectors consistently outperform OBB-specific designs at 10 m resolution, and super-resolution (HiT) lifts CA mAP50 from 65 to 91.

## Results

On SENTINELKILNDB (10 m Sentinel-2, 128×128 patches), the best-performing model TerraMind (ViT-B, remote-sensing foundation model) achieves a class-agnostic mAP₅₀ of 86.91, with class-wise AP₅₀ of 69.04 (CFCBK), 70.54 (FCBK), and 75.55 (Zigzag). Among generic detectors, RT-DETR (ResNet-101, DETR-based) reaches 86.17 CA mAP₅₀, while the best one-stage YOLO variant (YOLOv8L-WORLDv2, AA) scores 80.07. Axis-aligned (AA) bounding boxes consistently outperform oriented bounding box (OBB) formulations across all detector families, and CFCBK remains the hardest class (lowest AP₅₀) due to limited training examples (1,944 instances).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Remote-sensing foundation models with ViT backbones (TerraMind: 86.91, Galileo: 86.66) outperform all generic detector families, surpassing the best DETR-based model RT-DETR (86.17) and the best one-stage YOLO variant YOLOv8L-WORLDv2 (80.07) by 6.8 and 7.2 CA mAP₅₀ points respectively. Among OBB-specific methods, YOLOv11L-OBB (75.56) is the strongest, yet it still trails the AA-based YOLOv8L-WORLDv2 (80.07) by 4.5 points, confirming that axis-aligned boxes consistently outperform oriented boxes in this low-resolution setting. Two-stage OBB detectors underperform dramatically: RoI Transformer (70.74) is the best two-stage method, while older approaches like PSC (31.95) and CSL (18.19) score below 35. In the super-resolution experiment (Table 5), HiT-SR applied to Sentinel-2 patches boosts YOLOv11L-OBB from 65.02 to 90.74 CA mAP₅₀, a 25.7-point gain, with ESRGAN (90.12) and SwinIR (89.52) also substantially outperforming the original 128×128 resolution. Across all methods, CFCBK is the hardest class (e.g., TerraMind: 69.04 vs. Zigzag: 75.55), reflecting its small training set (1,944 samples) and visual similarity to surrounding terrain at 10 m resolution.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the authors' prior 'Space to Policy' work [38], which identified 30,638 brick kilns using Planet imagery across five Indian states, by expanding to 62,671 kilns across four South Asian countries and transferring labels to free Sentinel-2 (10 m) imagery. It departs from earlier brick kiln detection efforts (Kiln-Net [35], Boyd et al. [6], SustainBench [81]) that relied on axis-aligned boxes, tile-level classification, or non-public high-resolution imagery, by introducing the first hand-validated OBB annotations on low-resolution, freely accessible satellite data. The benchmarking framework also extends prior OBB detection literature (DOTA, HRSC2016) by evaluating 28 models—including recent remote sensing foundation models like TerraMind and Galileo—under a low-resolution regime where orientation estimation is substantially harder.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SENTINELKILNDB provides the first publicly available, reproducible benchmark for brick kiln detection on free satellite imagery, directly enabling dynamic emission inventory maintenance for air quality modeling across South Asia—a region where kilns contribute 8–14% of ambient pollution. Key open questions remain: seasonal generalization degrades sharply (winter-trained models drop 20+ mAP points in monsoon), out-of-region transfer is inconsistent, and the CFCBK class (only ~3% of samples) remains poorly detected by all models. The finding that super-resolution preprocessing (HiT) lifts CA mAP50 from 65 to 91 suggests a practical pathway for deploying detection on low-cost Sentinel-2 without expensive commercial imagery, though the AA-over-OBB result on 10 m patches challenges the assumption that oriented boxes are always advantageous at coarse resolution.
