## Problem

State-of-the-art deepfake detectors are predominantly evaluated on clean, controlled benchmark videos, yet in practice deepfake content is frequently re-captured from digital screens with smartphone cameras, introducing Moiré patterns that severely disrupt detection. This paper identifies and quantifies this largely unexplored vulnerability, showing that Moiré-induced distortions degrade detector performance by up to 25.4% and that common demoiréing mitigations paradoxically worsen accuracy by up to 17.2%.

## Input / Output

The benchmark takes video clips (10-second segments at 1920×1080, 30 fps) from five public deepfake datasets (FF++, CelebDF, DFD, DFDC, UADFV) and evaluates 15 pre-trained detectors (10 image-based, 5 video-based) under three distortion conditions: authentic screen-captured Moiré (CMPA), synthetic Moiré (SMPA-MA and SMPA-SPS), and H.264 compression at C23/C40 quality levels. Each detector outputs a real/fake binary classification, and performance is reported as AUC (primary metric), accuracy, precision, recall, and F1-score. The dataset spans 12,832 videos (35.64 hours) captured across 4 screens, 2 smartphones, 2 lighting conditions, and 4 camera angles, with metadata recording all capture parameters.

## Method

The core contribution is the DeepMoiréFake (DMF) dataset and a systematic three-scenario evaluation protocol rather than a new detector architecture. Videos are physically displayed on four monitors (LG LED, BenQ LED, Samsung QHD-IPS, Lenovo UHD-IPS) and re-captured with iPhone 13 and Samsung S22 Plus at 35 cm distance under controlled lighting and angle variations, producing authentic Moiré patterns. Synthetic Moiré is generated via two methods: SMPA-MA (scaling, resampling, random rotation) and SMPA-SPS (skew, contrast modulation, sine-wave distortion). The 15 detectors are evaluated with their original pre-trained weights, then fine-tuned and retrained on the DMF data to assess adaptability. Mitigation experiments apply five image demoiréing methods (DMCNN, MBCNN, ESDNet, DDA) and two video demoiréing methods (VD-Moiré, FPANet), revealing that removing Moiré also strips deepfake-specific artifacts, causing greater accuracy loss than the Moiré itself.

## Results

Across 15 deepfake detectors evaluated on the DeepMoiréFake (DMF) dataset (12,832 videos, 35.64 hours), authentic Moiré patterns cause an average AUC drop of 10.7%, with the worst-case degradation reaching 25.4 percentage points (FTCN on LG screen: 90.2% → 65.9%). Synthetic Moiré attacks (SMPA-MA) produce a 21.4% drop for the most vulnerable detector (MAT: 76.8% → 55.4%), while demoiréing as a mitigation strategy paradoxically worsens performance by an average of 6.1% (up to 17.2%), because it removes deepfake artifacts alongside the Moiré pattern. CCViT is the most robust detector overall (95.0% original → 84.5% average under Moiré), while FTCN suffers the largest absolute loss (90.2% → ~65–71% depending on screen).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CCViT [Coccomini, ICIP 2022] is the strongest detector under all Moiré conditions, maintaining 81.9–86.4% AUC across screens (avg. 84.5%) versus 95.0% on clean video, a drop of only ~10.5 points. Capsule-Forensics [Nguyen, ICASSP 2019] and LRNet [Sun, CVPR 2021] are the most robust relative to their baselines, losing only 2–3 percentage points, though their absolute scores remain low (mid-60s). FTCN [Zheng, ICCV 2021] is the most vulnerable high-performing detector, collapsing from 90.2% to 65.3–70.6% (a 20–25 point drop), making it the worst case in the paper. Among image-based detectors, MAT [Zhao, 2021] and CADDM [Dong, CVPR 2023] show moderate degradation (~15–16 points on LED screens) but recover better on IPS panels (Lenovo, Samsung). Critically, all five demoiréing methods (DMCNN, MBCNN, ESDNet, DDA, plus video-based VD-Moiré and FPANet) reduce detection accuracy further compared to the raw Moiré-affected video, with ESDNet causing the least additional harm and DDA the most, confirming that current restoration pipelines are counterproductive for deepfake detection.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the authors' own prior investigations of Moiré patterns on deepfake detection [33, 34], which were limited in scope (restricted detector sets, no real-world exploitation scenarios, no systematic mitigation analysis). It repurposes five established deepfake benchmarks (FF++, CelebDF, DFD, DFDC, UADFV) by physically re-capturing them through smartphone cameras to induce authentic Moiré artifacts, creating the first Moiré-impacted deepfake dataset. Conceptually, it bridges two previously separate literatures—deepfake detection (evaluated on clean, controlled inputs) and Moiré pattern research in computational photography (MOPNet, DMCNN, ESDNet)—by treating Moiré as an adversarial distortion channel rather than a restoration target.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper exposes a critical deployment gap: detectors achieving 87–95% AUC on clean benchmarks drop to 55–84% under realistic screen-capture conditions, undermining confidence in current systems for real-world moderation. The counterintuitive finding that demoiréing worsens detection (14.7% average drop vs. 10.1% for raw Moiré) challenges the natural 'clean-then-detect' pipeline and suggests that Moiré and deepfake artifacts occupy overlapping frequency bands, making separation lossy. Open questions include whether detectors trained directly on Moiré-affected data (as the DMF dataset enables) can close the gap, how OLED and 8K displays will shift the artifact landscape, and whether platform-specific processing (beautification filters, AR effects) compounds the problem further.
