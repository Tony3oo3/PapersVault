## Problem

While frequentist analyses have established that deep neural networks achieve minimax-optimal rates over anisotropic and composite Besov spaces, Bayesian posterior contraction theory for neural networks has been largely confined to isotropic Hölder or Besov classes. No prior work had established optimal posterior contraction for sparse Bayesian neural networks over anisotropic Besov spaces or their hierarchical compositions under the standard (non-fractional) posterior, nor had rate adaptation been demonstrated in these complex settings.

## Input / Output

The model observes n i.i.d. pairs (X_i, Y_i) where X_i ∈ [0,1]^d and Y_i = f_0(X_i) + ξ_i with Gaussian noise of variance σ_0². The output is the full posterior distribution over the regression function f (constrained to a sparse ReLU network class) and the noise variance σ², with contraction measured in L²(P_X) norm. The key structural detail is that the target function f_0 is assumed to lie in an anisotropic Besov space (allowing direction-dependent smoothness s = (s_1,...,s_d)) or a composite Besov space (hierarchical compositions with per-layer effective dimensions), and the contraction rate depends on the intrinsic smoothness s̃ = (Σ s_j^{-1})^{-1} rather than the ambient dimension d.

## Method

The paper proves that sparse ReLU Bayesian neural networks with either spike-and-slab or continuous shrinkage priors achieve near-minimax posterior contraction at rate n^{-s̃/(2s̃+1)}(log n)^{3/2} over anisotropic and composite Besov spaces. The proof proceeds by first establishing the existence of sparse NN approximators with depth O(log n), width O(N_n), and sparsity O(N_n log n) that achieve the optimal approximation error N_n^{-s̃}, where N_n = ⌈n^{1/(2s̃+1)}⌉. Posterior contraction is then shown via a standard sieve argument: controlling the metric entropy of the network class, verifying sufficient prior mass in a KL neighborhood of the approximator, and bounding the prior mass outside the sieve using exponential tail conditions on the slab/shrinkage density. Rate adaptation is achieved by replacing fixed network parameters with priors on width D ∝ exp(-λ_D D (log D)^3) and sparsity S ∝ exp(-λ_S S (log S)^2), so the posterior automatically selects the correct architecture without oracle knowledge of the smoothness parameters.

## Results

The paper establishes that sparse Bayesian neural networks with spike-and-slab or shrinkage priors achieve the near-minimax posterior contraction rate ε_n = n^{-s̃/(2s̃+1)}(log n)^{3/2} over anisotropic Besov spaces, where s̃ is the intrinsic smoothness. For composite anisotropic Besov spaces, the rate is ε_n = n^{-s̃*/(2s̃*+1)}(log n)^{3/2}, matching the frequentist minimax rate of Suzuki and Nitanda [18]. Crucially, rate adaptation is achieved: the posterior contracts at the optimal rate without requiring prior knowledge of the smoothness parameter, by placing appropriate priors on network width and sparsity. The intrinsic dimension d* = s̲/s̃ (rather than the ambient dimension d) governs the rate, demonstrating that BNNs mitigate the curse of dimensionality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines; comparisons are between theoretical guarantees. The key advance over Suzuki and Nitanda [18] (frequentist, anisotropic Besov) is that BNNs achieve the same minimax rate n^{-s̃/(2s̃+1)} while additionally providing rate adaptation and uncertainty quantification, without requiring the user to specify the network architecture. Compared to Lee and Lee [20] (Bayesian, isotropic Besov), this paper extends to anisotropic and composite Besov spaces, capturing intrinsic dimensionality. Against Egels and Castillo [22] (the only prior Bayesian work on anisotropic Besov), this paper uses the standard posterior rather than the fractional posterior, employs sparse (not dense) networks, and extends to composite anisotropic Besov spaces—a setting no prior Bayesian study has addressed. The rate adaptation property (Theorem 3.9) is shared with Polson and Ročková [21] and Kong and Kim [19], but those works are limited to isotropic Hölder spaces, whereas this paper handles the richer anisotropic and composite Besov structure.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate research threads: the frequentist approximation theory of sparse DNNs over anisotropic and composite Besov spaces (Schmidt-Hieber 2020; Suzuki 2019; Suzuki & Nitanda 2021) and the Bayesian posterior contraction theory for sparse BNNs (Polson & Ročková 2018; Lee & Lee 2022; Kong & Kim 2025). Prior Bayesian work was confined to isotropic Hölder or isotropic Besov spaces, while the only Bayesian treatment of anisotropic Besov spaces (Egels & Castillo 2025) relied on fractional posteriors and dense networks. This paper is the first to establish standard-posterior contraction for sparse BNNs over both anisotropic and composite anisotropic Besov spaces, directly matching the frequentist minimax rates of Suzuki & Nitanda while adding rate adaptation through prior design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The results provide a rigorous theoretical justification for why sparse BNNs succeed in high-dimensional structured settings (e.g., images with low intrinsic dimensionality) where classical smoothness-based theory falls short, and they show that rate adaptation—achieving optimal convergence without knowing the smoothness level—is a natural consequence of Bayesian inference. Key limitations include the computational intractability of spike-and-slab priors, the fact that popular shrinkage priors like the horseshoe do not satisfy the required tail conditions, and the restriction to fully-connected ReLU architectures. Open directions include extending the framework to convolutional networks and transformers, accommodating widely used shrinkage priors, and developing variational approximations that preserve the theoretical guarantees.
