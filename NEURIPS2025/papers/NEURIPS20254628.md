## Problem

Existing chart QA benchmarks over-represent textual reasoning: on ChartQA, Claude-3.7-Sonnet achieves 74.1% accuracy using only extracted text versus 87.4% with full chart images, indicating that models can largely bypass visual reasoning. This means current benchmarks fail to probe the visual reasoning capabilities that are essential for chart understanding, leaving a significant gap between measured and actual model competence.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254628/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark takes real-world chart images (average 1590×1243 px, drawn from 184 diverse sources including arXiv papers, infographics, and creative web charts) paired with a text question, and produces a short answer. Questions are constrained to have answer spaces of at least 4 options to prevent binary lookups, and all answers are unambiguous and objectively verifiable without tolerance margins. Evaluation uses GPT-4.1-mini as an LLM judge to handle paraphrases, unit variations, and equivalent numerical forms.

## Method

CHARTMUSEUM is a human-curated benchmark of 1,162 (image, question, answer) tuples sourced from 928 unique real-world charts across 184 websites, where all questions were written by 13 CS researchers without LLM assistance and passed through a multi-stage review (annotation → independent quality check → iterative refinement). Each question is classified into one of four reasoning types—Textual, Visual, Text/Visual, or Synthesis—enabling fine-grained analysis of where models fail. The paper's key analytical contribution is a visual task taxonomy (Symbol Selection, Visual Comparison, Trajectory Tracking, X/Y Value Identification) used to categorize model errors, revealing that frontier models over-rely on textual strategies (e.g., extracting approximate values) when visual comparison would suffice. A synthetic case study with 13 visual reasoning questions across 5 chart types further demonstrates that model accuracy degrades steadily with visual complexity (n=3 to 9) while human performance remains near-perfect, motivating the benchmark's emphasis on high-visual-complexity questions.

## Results

On CHARTMUSEUM (1,000 test questions), the best proprietary model Gemini-2.5-Pro achieves 63.0% overall accuracy, while the best open-source model Qwen2.5-VL-72B-Instruct reaches only 38.5%, and humans achieve 93.0%. Across all models, visual-reasoning questions (510 items) yield 35%–55% lower accuracy than text-reasoning questions (123 items); for example, GPT-4.1 drops from 78.9% (Text) to 37.1% (Visual), and Qwen2.5-VL-72B drops from 68.3% to 30.4%. Reasoning models (o3, o4-mini, Claude-3.7-Sonnet-think, Gemini-2.5-Pro) provide at most ~3% improvement over their non-reasoning counterparts, indicating that extended chain-of-thought does not meaningfully address the visual-reasoning gap.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to existing chart QA benchmarks, CHARTMUSEUM dramatically widens the performance spread: on ChartQA, Claude-3.7-Sonnet scores 87.4% with images (and 74.1% with extracted text alone), indicating the benchmark is near saturation and heavily reliant on textual reasoning; on CHARTMUSEUM the same model drops to 61.3% with images and only 15.2% with extracted text, exposing a 46-point gap that reflects genuinely visual content. Among the 21 evaluated LVLMs, proprietary models consistently outperform open-source models by 20–25 points (Gemini-2.5-Pro 63.0% vs. Qwen2.5-VL-72B 38.5%), a gap far larger than the 5–10 point spread seen on ChartQA or ChartQAPro. The specialized chart model Bespoke-MiniChart-7B (34.0%) approaches 72B open-source performance but still trails the best proprietary model by 29 points. Reasoning-augmented models (o3, o4-mini, Claude-3.7-Sonnet-think) yield only 1–3% gains over their base counterparts, confirming that the bottleneck is visual perception rather than textual reasoning depth. Humans (93.0%) exceed the best model by 30 points, and on the visual-reasoning subset specifically, humans reach 98.2% while the best model (Gemini-2.5-Pro) manages only 53.3%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself against the existing line of chart QA benchmarks (ChartQA, CharXiv, ChartQAPro, ChartBench) by arguing they over-represent textual reasoning and fail to differentiate frontier models. Its key conceptual departure is a formal taxonomy distinguishing visual reasoning (inferences arising from graphical relationships) from textual reasoning (operations over extracted or labeled text), which prior benchmarks do not explicitly control for. The synthetic case study (Section 2.3) is a novel diagnostic that isolates pure visual reasoning by removing all textual content, and the benchmark design (entirely human-written questions, 184 real-world sources, multi-stage review) deliberately avoids the model-generated-question pipeline used by CharXiv and ChartQAPro.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CHARTMUSEUM exposes a 30-point gap between the best model (Gemini-2.5-Pro at 63%) and humans (93%), and a consistent 35–55% performance drop on visual-reasoning questions versus textual-reasoning questions across all 21 evaluated models. Critically, extended-thinking/reasoning models (o3, o4-mini, Claude extended thinking) yield only ~1–3% improvement, suggesting the bottleneck lies in the visual encoder rather than in reasoning depth—a finding that redirects where the field should invest. The four-category visual task taxonomy (symbol selection, visual comparison, trajectory tracking, X/Y value identification) and the 'strategy error' concept give concrete, actionable targets for future LVLM development. Limitations include English-only content, short-answer format, and no unanswerable questions, which may understate real-world difficulty.
