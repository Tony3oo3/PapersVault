## Problem

All existing methods for computing bisimulation metrics (optimal-transport distances between Markov chains) require full knowledge of the transition kernels, an assumption that is rarely satisfied in practice where only sample trajectories are available. This paper is the first to provide a theoretically grounded method for estimating these distances from sample streams alone, with provable sample-complexity and computational guarantees.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes one state-transition pair (X_k, X'_k) from chain M_X and one (Y_k, Y'_k) from chain M_Y per iteration, drawn from the marginal occupancy measures via a geometric stopping time, along with a ground cost function c: X×Y → R⁺ and discount factor γ ∈ (0,1). It outputs the averaged primal iterate μ̄_K as an ε-accurate estimate of the bisimulation metric, plus the optimal occupancy coupling and, as side products, encoder-decoder maps λ_X(·|x) and λ_Y(·|y) giving the conditional distributions Y|X and X|Y under the optimal coupling. Each iteration costs Θ(|X|²|Y|²) computation, and K = Õ(|X||Y|(|X|+|Y|)/ε²) iterations suffice for ε-accuracy with high probability.

## Method

The core technical contribution is a new LP formulation of bisimulation metrics (Proposition 1) that replaces the transition-kernel-dependent constraints of Calo et al. (2024) with constraints involving only the marginal occupancy measures ν_X(x,x') and ν_Y(y,y'), which are directly sampleable. This LP is solved by SOMCOT, a stochastic primal-dual algorithm where the primal variables (μ, λ_X, λ_Y) are updated via stochastic mirror descent with entropic regularization (yielding multiplicative exponential updates on the simplex), while the dual variables (α_X, α_Y, V) are updated via projected stochastic gradient ascent onto bounded ℓ∞ balls. Unbiased stochastic gradient estimators are constructed from single sampled transitions per iteration, exploiting the linearity of the Lagrangian in the occupancy measures. The final estimate is the time-average of primal iterates, which smooths out the oscillations inherent to primal-dual dynamics and yields the stated high-probability error bound.

## Results

The main theoretical guarantee (Theorem 1) shows that SOMCOT returns an ε-accurate estimate of the bisimulation metric after Õ(|X||Y|(|X|+|Y|)/ε²) iterations, with each iteration using a single sample transition from each chain and costing Θ(|X|²|Y|²) computation. Empirically, on block Markov chains (n=10, B=5), SOMCOT recovers the correct encoder-decoder maps identifying the latent block structure after 10,000–100,000 samples, despite having no prior structural knowledge. In the model-selection experiments, the estimated distance achieves its minimum at the true parameter (θ=0.5 for random walks, g=9.8 for Pendulum-v1) and increases monotonically as the candidate model departs from ground truth.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not compare against any direct baselines, as it is explicitly the first method to compute bisimulation metrics from sample streams without requiring known transition kernels. All prior approaches—Calo et al. (2024), Moulos (2021), O'Connor et al. (2022), and Brugere et al. (2024)—assume full knowledge of the transition dynamics P_X and P_Y, making them inapplicable in the sample-only setting studied here. The empirical validation is therefore self-contained: SOMCOT is shown to correctly identify the true model in both the discrete (random walk with θ parameter) and continuous (Pendulum-v1 with gravity g) model-selection tasks, and to recover the latent block structure in representation learning without any architectural inductive bias. The paper notes that the sample complexity scaling with |X||Y|(|X|+|Y|) (rather than the full |X|²|Y|² dimensionality of the decision variables) is a notable advantage, and that the 1/ε² dependence is optimal up to logarithmic factors by known lower bounds for static OT.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LP formulation of bisimulation metrics as OT distances from Calo et al. (2024) by reformulating the linear constraints to eliminate explicit transition kernels, replacing them with joint state-next-state distributions that can be sampled directly from Markov chain trajectories. This enables a stochastic primal-dual optimization approach (SOMCOT) that departs from all prior methods in both the OT-for-Markov-chains literature (Moulos 2021, O'Connor et al. 2022, Brugere et al. 2024) and the theoretical CS bisimulation literature, which uniformly assume known transition dynamics. The stochastic optimization technique draws on the static OT literature (Genevay et al. 2016, Seguy et al. 2018) but applies stochastic mirror descent to the sequential/coupling structure of Markov chain OT for the first time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first method to compute bisimulation metrics from sample streams with provable sample-complexity guarantees, removing the impractical assumption of known transition dynamics that has limited all prior approaches. The encoder-decoder interpretation of the λ variables and the differentiability of the dual variables with respect to transition kernels open concrete paths toward theoretically grounded representation learning in RL, potentially offering sound alternatives to heuristic methods like MuZero. Key open questions include scaling to infinite or continuous state spaces (where primal variable parametrization remains challenging) and whether the finite-state sample complexity of Õ(|X||Y|(|X|+|Y|)/ε²) is tight or can be improved.
