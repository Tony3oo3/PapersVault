## Problem

Homomorphic encryption (HE) can protect federated learning gradients end-to-end, but ciphertext expansion and encryption/decryption/aggregation costs make fully encrypted FL impractical. The state-of-the-art FedML-HE reduces overhead by selectively encrypting only the most sensitive gradients, yet the remaining plaintext gradients remain vulnerable to inversion attacks. No prior method achieves full gradient protection at a communication and compute cost close to plaintext FL.

## Input / Output

Each client holds a local dataset (RGB images for ViT tasks, text for BERT/TinyLlama tasks) and a shared pre-trained model initialized via truncated SVD into a frozen dictionary D (n×r) and a trainable lookup table T (r×m). Clients encrypt only T's gradients using a CKKS public key (128-bit security, 32,768 SIMD slots per ciphertext) and upload them; the server aggregates ciphertexts and broadcasts the result, which clients decrypt to update their local T. The dictionary D is identical across all clients and never leaves local devices, so the transmitted payload is limited to the pruned, encrypted r×m lookup-table gradients.

## Method

DictPFL combines two modules. Decompose-for-Partial-Encrypt (DePE) factorizes each weight matrix W₀ via truncated SVD into a static dictionary D = U_rΣ_r and a lookup table T, then constructs W = W₀ + D·T so that D is frozen, globally shared, and never transmitted, while only the small r×m entries of T are encrypted and aggregated. Prune-for-Minimum-Encrypt (PrME) further reduces encrypted parameters through Temporal Inactivity Pruning (TIP), which prunes local gradients whose corresponding global gradients fall in the smallest s% magnitude across the previous τ rounds, ensuring all clients prune identically without sharing indices. A Holistic Reactivation Correction (HRC) mechanism assigns each pruned parameter a dynamic reactivation probability that decays or grows based on post-reactivation global gradient magnitude, allowing prematurely pruned parameters to rejoin aggregation and preserving client-specific accumulated gradients. Because all transmitted gradients are encrypted and the unencrypted dictionary is never shared, DictPFL eliminates the plaintext leakage of selective-encryption baselines while reducing ciphertext count by orders of magnitude.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with ViT-16 (3-client, no pre-trained weights), DictPFL achieves 95.06% accuracy with only 0.51 GB communication and 11.8 min training time, compared to 720.72 GB / 294.6 min for fully encrypted FedHE-Full and 73.62 GB / 56.7 min for FedML-HE. Across models and tasks, DictPFL reduces communication by 402–748× and accelerates training by 28–65× over fully encrypted FL, while outperforming selectively encrypted FedML-HE by 51–155× in communication and 4–19× in speed. On TinyLlama (MetaMathQA), DictPFL cuts training time from 214.2 h to 1.3 h (99.4% reduction) at comparable accuracy (45.93% vs 45.86%). Overall, DictPFL's runtime stays within 2× of plaintext FL, the first demonstration that HE-based private FL is practical at scale.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the fully encrypted baseline FedHE-Full (NVIDIA FLARE [35]), DictPFL wins decisively on every metric: 95.06% vs 94.17% accuracy, 0.51 GB vs 720.72 GB communication (≈1413× reduction on CIFAR-10), and 11.8 min vs 294.6 min training time (≈25× speedup). Against the state-of-the-art selective-encryption method FedML-HE [31], DictPFL achieves comparable or higher accuracy (95.06% vs 94.99%) while reducing communication by ~144× (0.51 GB vs 73.62 GB) and training time by ~5×; critically, FedML-HE leaks private information through its unencrypted 90% of gradients (gradient inversion recovers images at up to 23% LPIPS similarity), whereas DictPFL encrypts all transmitted parameters. Against the packing-based AdaptiveBatchHE [41], DictPFL achieves higher accuracy (98.2% vs 96.7%) and lower communication (0.43 GB vs 5.41 GB) on CIFAR-10, and the two are orthogonal—combining them further reduces communication to 0.087 GB. Against Secure Aggregation (MPC-based, [25]), DictPFL matches accuracy (82.7%) with only a 6% training-time overhead (385.2 s vs 363.2 s) while providing stronger end-to-end encryption guarantees without requiring multiple non-colluding servers.

## Relation to Prior Work

DictPFL adapts the dictionary-plus-lookup-table decomposition from DictFormer (Lou et al., ICLR 2022), originally designed for inference-time parameter reduction, to the HE-based federated learning setting by freezing the dictionary across all clients and encrypting only the small trainable lookup table. It departs from the state-of-the-art FedML-HE (Jin et al., 2023), which uses a Select-and-Encrypt strategy that leaves a subset of gradients in plaintext, by ensuring every transmitted gradient is encrypted while reducing the total number of parameters requiring encryption through decomposition and history-guided pruning. The pruning mechanism (PrME) is specifically designed to overcome the SIMD batching constraint in HE aggregation, where slot-wise alignment prevents the index-based coordination that plaintext FL pruning relies on.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that HE-based private federated learning can operate within 2× the runtime of plaintext FL, challenging the long-held assumption that homomorphic encryption overhead is prohibitively expensive for practical deployment. The gradient inversion experiments showing 23% image reconstruction similarity under FedML-HE's selective encryption (vs. 0% for DictPFL) provide a concrete security argument against the widely adopted 'encrypt only sensitive parameters' paradigm. Open questions include whether the fixed shared dictionary limits personalization in highly heterogeneous cross-device settings, whether the approach extends to non-transformer architectures, and how it scales to the thousands of clients typical in cross-device FL with constrained client resources.
