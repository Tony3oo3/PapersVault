## Problem

Existing LLM routing methods formulate model selection as a classification problem based solely on the input query, ignoring the semantic content of potential model responses. This query-only approach fails to capture implicit intent, contextual nuances, and output quality signals that only emerge during response generation, leading to suboptimal routing—especially for ambiguous or multi-step reasoning queries.

## Input / Output

The router receives a text query concatenated with model identifier (MID) tokens—one per candidate LLM—and produces a score vector over all candidates from which the best model is selected. In the CLM variant, a single forward pass at the MID token position yields the response latent; in the MLM variant, each model's response is represented by a block of m=64 repeated MID tokens, and a [CLS] token aggregates all candidates via attention in one joint forward pass. No autoregressive decoding is performed at inference time, keeping the router lightweight (135M–149M parameters).

## Method

Lookahead introduces a predictive module F that maps (query, model index) to a latent response representation r̃_t, supervised by a reconstruction objective that forces the latent to encode response-level semantics. Two instantiations are provided: (1) a causal LM (SmolLM2-135M) trained with teacher forcing to generate the full response after a MID token, using the MID-position hidden state as r̃_t; and (2) a masked LM (ModernBERT-base) that jointly reconstructs all T candidate responses from fully masked MID-token blocks in a single forward pass, with a [CLS] token producing the routing score via attention over query and response latents. Training combines a binary cross-entropy routing loss with a weighted response reconstruction loss (λ=0.5 for CLM, 0.2 for MLM). The MLM variant further employs a curriculum masking strategy that progressively increases the masking ratio from the end of each response to 100% over the first α=0.4 fraction of training, facilitating a smooth transition from partial to full response prediction.

## Results

On the MLM-based variant (ModernBERT backbone), Lookahead achieves an average normalized score of 40.8 across seven benchmarks, outperforming the strongest baseline RouterDC (37.9) by 7.7% relative improvement. The CLM-based variant (SmolLM2-135M) reaches 37.0 average normalized score, exceeding SMOOTHIE (35.4) by 4.5 points. Gains are most pronounced on open-ended instruction-following tasks (e.g., MT-Bench: 35.4 vs. 33.7 for RouterDC) while math and code benchmarks show more modest but consistent improvements. The Oracle Router achieves 100% normalized score, and the Reward Model Selection reference reaches 48.8%, indicating Lookahead closes a substantial portion of the random-to-oracle gap.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Lookahead (MLM) outperforms all six routing baselines on average normalized score, with the largest margin over RouterDC (40.8 vs. 37.9, +2.9 pts) and SMOOTHIE (40.8 vs. 35.4, +5.4 pts). On instruction-following benchmarks, the advantage is most pronounced: Lookahead (MLM) leads RouterDC by 5.5 pts on MT-Bench (35.4 vs. 33.7) and 5.5 pts on AlpacaEval-2 (37.5 vs. 32.0), while on deterministic tasks like HumanEval and MBPP, all top methods converge to the same scores (67.7 and 53.0 respectively). The CLM variant also beats all baselines on average (37.0 vs. 35.4 for SMOOTHIE) but shows a smaller gap on open-ended tasks compared to the MLM variant. Similarity-based methods (kNN, k-means) are consistently the weakest, with kNN averaging only 19.5, confirming that generic query embeddings are poorly suited for routing-specific objectives. The paper attributes the MLM variant's edge on open-ended tasks to its ability to embed and score all candidate responses jointly in a shared semantic space, enabling fine-grained head-to-head comparisons that the CLM's isolated per-model scoring cannot provide.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the classifier-based LLM routing paradigm (MLC, ZOOTER, RouterDC) by introducing a response-aware component: rather than encoding only the input query, the router predicts latent representations of each candidate model's potential response. It repurposes small causal and masked language models (SmolLM2-135M, ModernBERT-base) as lightweight response-latent predictors, drawing on the autoregressive and masked-reconstruction objectives of CLM/MLM pretraining but applying them to a routing-specific dual-task objective. The curriculum masking strategy (progressively increasing from partial to full response masking) is a novel adaptation of standard MLM pretraining to better align with the routing goal.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper validates a practically important insight—that response-level semantics carry routing-relevant information that query-only features miss—and provides a computationally cheap mechanism (single forward pass through a ~150M model) to access that information without full inference. Open questions remain: the method does not incorporate cost-awareness (a 34B model and a 7B model are treated symmetrically), the reward model used for training labels could propagate biases into routing decisions, and the current fixed-prefix masking for the MLM variant underperforms on math where discriminative content appears later in responses. Integration with contrastive or KL-divergence routing objectives, and adaptive span identification, are natural next steps.
