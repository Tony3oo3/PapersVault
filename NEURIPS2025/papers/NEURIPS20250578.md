## Problem

Existing neural PDE surrogates for boiling cannot forecast autonomously because they require future bubble positions as input, fail to learn the stochastic nucleation process essential for long rollouts, and cannot predict velocity fields in flow boiling due to insufficient directional inductive bias and spatiotemporal integration. No prior model has demonstrated physically plausible, self-sustaining forecasting of full-field two-phase boiling dynamics across diverse fluids and flow regimes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250578/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes k past timesteps of 5D spatiotemporal tensors containing the signed distance field φ (interface), temperature T, and velocity vector u⃗, along with a 9D thermophysical descriptor (Reynolds, Prandtl, Stefan numbers, viscosity, density, thermal conductivity, specific heat, heater temperature, nucleation wait time) that conditions the network via FiLM. It outputs the next k timesteps of all three fields in a bundled fashion, with spatial resolutions ranging from 192×288 (single-bubble pool boiling) to 5152×224 (long-channel flow boiling). The signed distance field must satisfy |∇φ|=1 (Eikonal constraint) to represent valid bubble geometries, and the model is trained with k=5 but evaluated in autoregressive rollouts up to 2000 steps.

## Method

Bubbleformer is a spatiotemporal transformer combining four architectural innovations: (1) a hierarchical MLP stem with repeated 2×2 strided convolutions that builds a multiscale patch hierarchy supporting flexible resolutions; (2) FiLM-based conditioning that applies learned per-channel affine transforms derived from the 9D fluid descriptor, enabling a single model to generalize across cryogens, refrigerants, and dielectrics; (3) factorized space-time axial attention that first applies temporal attention at each spatial location, then decomposes spatial attention into independent 1D height and width passes, reducing complexity from O(HWT)² to O(H²+W²+T²) while preserving directional structure critical for flow boiling; and (4) frequency-aware attention and feature scaling that separately reweight low- and high-frequency components to prevent the low-pass filtering effect of repeated softmax attention from erasing sharp interface gradients. The model is trained end-to-end with supervised relative L2 loss over all three fields, using the Lion optimizer with a warm-up cosine schedule over 250 epochs, in two sizes (29M and 115M parameters).

## Results

On the subcooled pool boiling prediction task (FC-72), Bubbleformer-S (29M params) achieves state-of-the-art relative L2 error across all reported metrics, outperforming both UNet_mod and FFNO from the original BubbleML benchmark. The evaluation extends the autoregressive rollout from 400 to 800 timesteps (doubling the original BubbleML setting), where Bubbleformer maintains uniformly low error while baselines exhibit growing instability after violent bubble detachment events. Notably, Bubbleformer remains stable even at 2000 rollout steps. For the forecasting task on an unseen flow boiling trajectory (FC-72, input flow scale = 2.2), a 200-step autoregressive rollout preserves physical consistency: predicted heat flux PDFs closely match ground truth, Eikonal loss stays below the 0.1 threshold, and vapor volume is conserved.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares Bubbleformer against two baselines from the BubbleML benchmark: UNet_mod (a modified U-Net) and FFNO (Factorized Fourier Neural Operator). On the supervised prediction task for subcooled pool boiling, Bubbleformer outperforms both baselines across all metrics including relative L2 error for temperature and velocity magnitude over 800 rollout timesteps. UNet_mod maintains smooth evolution in single-step prediction but fails to nucleate new bubbles during autoregressive rollout, leading to steadily growing error after bubble detachment events. FFNO suffers from spectral oversmoothing and directional aliasing in flow boiling, producing large error spikes during violent topological changes and rapid divergence in autoregressive settings. In the forecasting (nucleation) task, both UNet and FNO architectures fundamentally fail to learn re-nucleation from past states alone, while Bubbleformer successfully captures stochastic nucleation dynamics and maintains physically valid rollouts. The detailed per-dataset numerical comparison is deferred to Appendix D, as the main text presents results primarily through error-curve figures (Figure 5) and physics-based system-level metrics (Figure 4).

## Relation to Prior Work

Bubbleformer repurposes components from the video-transformer literature—factorized space-time attention (Bertasius et al., Arnab et al.), axial attention (Ho et al.), and frequency-aware anti-oversmoothing (Wang et al.)—and combines them with a hierarchical MLP stem (Touvron et al.) and FiLM-based fluid conditioning into a unified spatiotemporal architecture for two-phase flow. It departs sharply from the prior boiling-surrogate line (BubbleML, Hassan et al. 2023), which relied on UNets and Fourier Neural Operators and required future bubble interfaces as input, making autonomous forecasting impossible. The paper also introduces BubbleML 2.0, a substantially expanded dataset, and new physics-based evaluation metrics (heat-flux KL divergence, Eikonal residual, vapor-volume conservation) that go beyond the pixel-wise and Fourier-mode errors used in prior boiling benchmarks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that a learned model can autonomously forecast boiling—including stochastic nucleation—over long horizons while preserving system-level physical invariants, which is a prerequisite for using ML surrogates in real-time thermal management (data-center cooling, nuclear reactors, spacecraft). Key open questions remain: the model cannot natively handle AMR grids needed for water or large domains, it is specialized to one physics type at a time (saturated vs. subcooled), and combining heterogeneous physics via mixture-of-experts routing is left as future work. The released BubbleML 2.0 dataset and physics-based metrics provide a reusable benchmark for the multiphase-flow ML community, but the absence of error bars and the single-seed evaluation limit confidence in the magnitude of reported gains.
