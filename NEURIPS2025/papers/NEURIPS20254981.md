## Problem

Existing generalization bounds for Langevin dynamics (CLD/SGLD) and related stochastic training methods suffer from undesirable dependencies: on training time or mixing, on parameter dimensionality (via Lipschitz constants, gradient norms, or spectral gaps), or on trajectory-dependent statistics. The paper asks whether a simple, time-independent, dimension-free generalization guarantee can be derived for any Markovian training process with a Gibbs-type stationary distribution, and if so, what the minimal assumptions are.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254981/cropped_page40_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a data-dependent Markov process (e.g., CLD with SDE dθ = -∇L_S dt + √(2/β) dw) whose stationary distribution is Gibbs w.r.t. a data-independent base measure ν with potential Ψ ≥ 0, along with an initial distribution p_0. It produces a generalization gap bound valid at every time t ≥ 0 simultaneously, whose only model-dependent quantity is the expected potential at initialization E_{p_0}[Ψ] (or its essential supremum for high-probability bounds). For CLD with matching initialization (p_0 = ν), this reduces to √((β·E_{p_0}[L_S] + ln(N/δ))/(2N)), requiring no knowledge of the trajectory, dimension, or loss landscape geometry.

## Method

The core technical insight is that the generalized second law of thermodynamics (monotonicity of KL and Rényi-∞ divergence from any stationary distribution, a consequence of the data processing inequality) implies that the divergence D(p_t ∥ p_∞) never exceeds D(p_0 ∥ p_∞) at any time t, regardless of mixing. Combining this with the chain rule for divergences and the Gibbs structure of p_∞ (which bounds the symmetrized divergence between p_∞ and ν in terms of the potential Ψ), the paper derives Corollary 2.5: D(p_t ∥ ν) ≤ D(p_0 ∥ ν) + E_{p_0}[Ψ] for all t. This time-independent divergence bound is then plugged directly into standard PAC-Bayes inequalities to yield the generalization guarantee. For CLD, the potential is Ψ = βL_S, and when the initialization matches the regularization (e.g., Gaussian p_0 with matching ℓ² weight decay, or uniform p_0 in a bounded domain), the D(p_0 ∥ ν) term vanishes, giving the clean √(β·E_{p_0}[L_S]/N) rate with no dimension, time, or trajectory dependence.

## Results

The paper derives a generalization bound of √((β·E[L(θ₀)] + ln(N/δ))/2N) for any time t during Langevin dynamics training, with no dependence on training time, dimensionality, gradient norms, or trajectory statistics. Under standard neural network initialization, E[L(θ₀)] = O(1), yielding a bound of √(β/N)·O(1) that is dimension-free and trajectory-independent. Empirically, on binary MNIST, Fashion MNIST, SVHN, and a parity problem, the authors show that β = O(N) yields non-vacuous bounds (sum of gap bound and training error < 0.5) for overparameterized networks trained with SGLD.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's bound strictly dominates all three prior CLD generalization bounds on multiple axes. Compared to Mou et al. [49] and Futami & Fujisawa [19], the new bound is trajectory-independent (no gradient statistics g_t required) and dimension-independent (no dependence on parameter count d, Lipschitz constants, or smoothness). Compared to Li et al. [38], the new bound replaces the exponential e^{4βC} dependence with a polynomial √(βC) dependence, and removes the Lipschitz constant K entirely. Additionally, the new bound requires only a bounded *expected* loss at initialization (E[L(θ₀)] < ∞) rather than a uniform bound on the loss (C = sup L < ∞), making it applicable to unbounded losses with finite-mean initializations. The trade-off is that the high-probability variant (Eq. 12) uses ess sup_{p₀} L_S(θ), which may still depend on dimension if the loss is unbounded, whereas the in-expectation bound (Eq. 11) is fully dimension-free under standard initialization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the PAC-Bayes framework (Maurer, McAllester) for generalization bounds and Cover's second law of thermodynamics (monotonicity of divergence under Markov transitions) to derive a general bound for any data-dependent Markov process with a Gibbs stationary distribution. It departs from prior CLD/SGLD generalization work (Raginsky et al., Mou et al., Li et al., Futami & Fujisawa) by replacing spectral-gap and mixing-time arguments with a simple monotonicity argument, thereby eliminating dependence on training time, parameter dimension, Lipschitz constants, and trajectory statistics. The key conceptual move is observing that the divergence from initialization remains bounded at all times via the data processing inequality, rather than requiring convergence to stationarity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The bound is computable before training (no trajectory statistics needed) and holds at any time without mixing assumptions, which is a meaningful simplification over all prior CLD generalization results. However, the practical applicability is constrained by the requirement that β=O(N) and that regularization/boundedness must match the initialization distribution, and the empirical validation is limited to small binary classification datasets (binary MNIST, Fashion MNIST, SVHN, parity). Open questions include extending to non-isotropic or singular noise (as in real SGD), removing the regularization requirement, handling discrete-time SGD with large steps, and determining whether the noise actually improves test performance or merely shrinks the gap.
