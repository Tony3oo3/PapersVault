## Problem

Direct preference alignment methods like DPO suffer from likelihood displacement, where training on noisy preference pairs (those inducing similar likelihood for preferred and dispreferred responses) paradoxically reduces the absolute probability of preferred responses. Existing remedies either discard these noisy pairs or add regularization, but the paper argues the pairs still contain extractable alignment signal that can improve model performance if handled with a purpose-built mechanism.

## Input / Output

The method consumes pairwise preference data (prompt, preferred response, dispreferred response) along with a pre-trained LLM and its post-SFT reference model. A key operational detail is that ComPO only perturbs the output-layer (lm_head) weights rather than the full parameter vector, reducing the perturbation space from billions to a manageable dimension. The pipeline first splits the dataset into clean and noisy subsets using a log-likelihood margin threshold δ, applies DPO to clean pairs, then runs ComPO exclusively on the noisy subset, producing a final fine-tuned policy.

## Method

ComPO is a zeroth-order, comparison-based optimizer that treats preference pairs as oracle outputs of a latent alignment objective. It defines a preference comparison oracle C_π(θ, θ′) that returns −1 only when θ′ simultaneously increases the likelihood of the preferred response and decreases the likelihood of the dispreferred response. From m random perturbations of the output-layer weights, the binary oracle responses are aggregated via a 1-bit compressed-sensing estimator to recover a normalized sparse gradient direction, which is then clipped (entries below λ_g zeroed) and scaled by the fraction of negative oracle responses. The paper proves a convergence guarantee for the basic scheme under non-convex smooth objectives with sparse gradients, and the practical scheme replaces the intractable ℓ₁-constrained optimization with a cheap normalize-then-clip approximation. The full pipeline (DPO_clean + ComPO) requires only ~1% of output-layer parameters to be updated, keeping peak GPU memory around 23 GB per A40 versus 77 GB for full DPO.

## Results

On AlpacaEval 2, DPO_clean+ComPO achieves the highest length-controlled win rate (LC) across all four model setups: 11.66% (Mistral-Base-7B), 26.17% (Mistral-Instruct-7B), 5.39% (Llama-3-Base-8B), and 35.79% (Llama-3-Instruct-8B), consistently outperforming both DPO and DPO_clean. Win rate (WR) also improves, e.g., from 16.71% (DPO) to 18.32% (DPO_clean+ComPO) on Mistral-Instruct-7B and from 31.99% to 35.03% on Llama-3-Instruct-8B. On Arena-Hard, DPO_clean+ComPO matches or slightly trails DPO (e.g., 10.5% vs. 14.4% on Mistral-Instruct-7B), which the authors attribute to Arena-Hard's lack of length penalty favoring longer DPO outputs. SimPO+ComPO similarly improves LC over SimPO across all three instruction-tuned models (e.g., 48.71%→49.53% on Llama-3-Instruct-8B, 60.36%→62.42% on Gemma-2-it-9B).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DPO [70], DPO_clean+ComPO wins on AlpacaEval 2 LC and WR across all four model setups, with the largest LC gains on instruction-tuned models (+2.03 pp on Mistral-Instruct-7B, +3.20 pp on Llama-3-Instruct-8B). However, DPO_clean+ComPO loses on Arena-Hard WR for Mistral-Instruct-7B (10.5% vs. 14.4%) and ties or slightly trails on Llama-3 models, which the authors explain by Arena-Hard's absence of a length penalty (average response length drops from 513 to 468 tokens). Against SimPO [60], SimPO+ComPO consistently outperforms SimPO on both LC and WR across all three instruction-tuned models (Mistral-Instruct-7B, Llama-3-Instruct-8B, Gemma-2-it-9B), with LC improvements of +2.05, +0.82, and +2.06 pp respectively, and WR gains of +1.99, +1.37, and +1.61 pp. Notably, SimPO+ComPO also improves Arena-Hard WR (e.g., 36.3%→37.3% on Llama-3-Instruct-8B), unlike the DPO comparison, because SimPO already generates concise responses so the length penalty is less of a confound.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the 1-bit comparison-based gradient estimation framework of Cai et al. [12]—originally developed for convex optimization—to the nonconvex setting of LLM preference alignment, replacing the generic function-value oracle with a preference-specific comparison oracle defined by response likelihoods. It positions itself as a complement to DPO [70] and SimPO [60], specifically targeting the noisy preference pairs (small log-likelihood margin) that these methods either discard or handle suboptimally, rather than proposing a replacement objective. The conceptual departure from prior direct alignment work is the refusal to commit to an explicit proxy objective function; instead, preference pairs are treated as oracle outputs from a latent alignment goal, and parameter updates are guided purely by comparative signals.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that noisy preference pairs—typically a source of likelihood displacement and discarded in practice—contain extractable signal when processed through comparison-oracle-based zeroth-order optimization, offering a principled alternative to the filter-or-regularize paradigm. The practical efficiency is notable: only ~0.0002% of model parameters (output layer) are updated, requiring ~23 GB versus ~77 GB for full DPO, making alignment accessible on modest hardware. Open questions include adaptive stepsize selection, whether full-layer perturbation yields further gains, applicability to safety and truthfulness alignment beyond helpfulness, and whether the method can serve as a drop-in replacement for DPO on clean pairs. The convergence guarantee, while extending prior convex-only results to nonconvex smooth settings, relies on a sparsity assumption on gradients that is not directly verifiable in practice.
