## Problem

The Kalman Filter is provably optimal only for linear Gaussian systems, and its nonlinear extensions (EKF, UKF) rely on linearization or deterministic sampling while still fitting Gaussian beliefs, failing to capture non-Gaussian or multi-modal noise. No existing framework provides a provably optimal, certifiable filtering solution for polynomial systems corrupted by arbitrary noise where the belief can be multi-modal (e.g., unknown data association).

## Input / Output

The method takes a polynomial system model (process f and observation h), control inputs u_k, measurements y_k, user-specified noise moments up to order r for both process and measurement noise, and an initial MED parameterization λ_0. The state domain K may be R^n or a semi-algebraic set defined by polynomial equalities (e.g., SE(2)). The output is a full MED belief distribution p(x|λ) with polynomial exponential form, a moment sequence, and a globally optimal point estimate x*_k extracted via semidefinite programming with a rank-1 certificate of optimality.

## Method

MEM-KF represents the belief as a Moment-Constrained Max-Entropy Distribution (MED) of the form exp(−Σ λ_α x^α), which asymptotically approximates any moment-determinate distribution as the number of matched moments r grows. In the prediction step, instead of the intractable marginalization integral, the method propagates moments through an extended polynomial dynamics (constructed via the monomial basis φ_r) and recovers the MED by minimizing the thermal dynamics potential via convex optimization. In the update step, measurement noise is modeled as MED, and the posterior is obtained by simple coefficient addition (λ = λ⁻ + Σ ν_i), making the MAP update algebraic. The optimal point estimate is extracted by solving the Lasserre semidefinite moment relaxation of the resulting polynomial optimization problem, where a rank-1 solution certifies global optimality. All steps—moment propagation, MED recovery, and point estimation—are solvable via convex optimization, requiring only numerical integration for gradient evaluation.

## Results

In the update-step experiment (Table 1), MEM-KF with r=12 moments achieves a point-estimation covariance of 3.27×10⁻⁵ at N=100 accumulated measurements under noise model (14), compared to 0.0161 for the BLUE (r=2) baseline — a ~500× reduction. Under the bimodal Bernoulli noise model (15), r=4 already reduces covariance from 0.0199 (BLUE) to 9.36×10⁻⁴ at N=100. In the SE(2) localization task with unknown data association, MEM-KF (r=4) consistently tracks the ground-truth trajectory while all linearization-based baselines (EKF, UKF, InEKF, UKFM) exhibit clear trajectory bias, particularly when the true association distribution deviates from the max-entropy assumption.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against BLUE (r=2, the minimum-variance linear estimator using only mean and covariance), MEM-KF with r≥4 dramatically outperforms once N≥5 measurements are accumulated: at N=100 under noise (14), r=12 reduces covariance by ~500× (3.27×10⁻⁵ vs. 0.0161), and r=4 already achieves a ~10× reduction. For the bimodal Bernoulli noise (15), r=4 cuts covariance by ~21× at N=100. In the SE(2) localization task with unknown data association, MEM-KF (r=4) consistently tracks the ground-truth trajectory in both scenarios, while EKF, UKF, InEKF, and UKFM all exhibit clear trajectory bias — especially in scenario 2 where measurements are skewed toward one landmark, because these baselines greedily select the most likely association. Compared to particle filters, MEM-KF achieves similar estimation quality when the particle filter uses a sufficiently large number of particles (as shown in Figures 5 and 8), but MEM-KF is deterministic, provides a smooth analytical belief, and enables certified globally optimal point estimation via SDP — properties unavailable to sampling-based methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

MEM-KF unifies three previously separate threads: the maximum-entropy moment problem (Mead & Papanicolaou 1984; Borwein & Lewis 1991) for representing arbitrary beliefs, exact moment propagation through polynomial dynamics (extending the approximate moment-based filters of Shimizu et al. 2023 and Jasour et al. 2021, which assumed Gaussian noise and required characteristic functions), and Lasserre's semidefinite moment relaxations for certifiable point estimation. The key departure from the authors' own prior GMKF [21] is that prediction uses exact moment propagation via an extended polynomial system rather than approximations, and the belief is recovered as a MED rather than a fixed parametric family. Unlike EKF/UKF, which linearize or fit Gaussians, MEM-KF operates entirely on moments and recovers the full distribution, with the classical KF recovered as the r=2 special case.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first general, certifiable filtering framework for polynomial systems with truly arbitrary (non-Gaussian) noise, where every step—prediction, update, and point-estimate extraction—is a convex program with asymptotic optimality guarantees. The main practical limitation is that numerical integration for gradients and Hessians scales exponentially with state dimension (6.87 s per update, 34.51 s per MED recovery in the SE(2) experiment), making real-time deployment in high-dimensional systems currently infeasible. Open questions include whether GPU-accelerated quadrature or sparsity-aware moment selection (exploiting the polynomial structure) can close this gap, and whether the asymptotic convergence rate is fast enough for the complex, high-dimensional beliefs encountered in practice.
