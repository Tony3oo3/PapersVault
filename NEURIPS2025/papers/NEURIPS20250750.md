## Problem

Existing learning-to-optimize frameworks for arithmetic circuits focus on coarse-grained compressor allocation while relegating fine-grained interconnect routing to heuristic sequential wiring or oversimplified proxy delay models. These proxy objectives diverge increasingly from actual post-synthesis delay as bit width grows, causing premature convergence to suboptimal topologies. No prior work has formulated interconnect optimization as a differentiable, proxy-free search problem over the full graph-structured design space.

## Input / Output

The method takes a compressor allocation matrix (specifying compressor types per column) generated by a circuit genetic evolution module, along with the PPG encoding scheme (AND-gate or Booth) and input bit width (8–64 bits), and constructs a multi-relational directed acyclic graph where nodes are compressors/partial products and typed edges correspond to specific input ports (a, b, cin). The output is a discrete interconnection topology sampled from the learned edge-probability distribution, which is then translated into a Verilog netlist, functionally verified via Verilator, and synthesized through Yosys/OpenSTA on the Nangate45 library to extract post-synthesis area and delay.

## Method

ARITH-DAS formulates compressor interconnect optimization as differentiable edge prediction over a multi-relational DAG, where each edge type encodes a distinct input port semantics. A multi-relational GNN encoder (R-GCN-style message passing with relation-specific functions) produces node embeddings capturing topological and structural context, and a Graphormer-style attention mechanism computes target-centric connection probabilities: for each target node and input port, a softmax over valid source nodes yields the probability of establishing that edge. Discrete edge assignments are relaxed into continuous probability distributions via softmax, enabling gradient-based optimization. Training uses a PPO-style clipped surrogate objective directly on post-synthesis area and delay metrics (proxy-free), with a discretization penalty and output-port exclusivity constraint to promote valid, sparse topologies. The framework operates in two stages: circuit genetic evolution searches the coarse allocation space, and the differentiable interconnect search optimizes wiring within each sampled allocation.

## Results

ARITH-DAS achieves the highest hypervolume (HV) across all eight multiplier design tasks (AND and Booth encoding at 8/16/32/64-bit), improving HV by up to 309.37% over the Wallace baseline and up to ~31% over the previous SOTA (MUTE) on 32-bit AND multipliers (3,247.06 vs. 2,470.47). On 64-bit AND multipliers, ARITH-DAS reaches HV 19,563.48 versus MUTE's 16,656.60. When integrated into large-scale AI accelerator circuits from the Koios 2.0 benchmark, ARITH-DAS-optimized units deliver up to 6.59% delay reduction compared to the best baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ARITH-DAS achieves Pareto dominance over all six baselines on every one of the eight multiplier tasks. The closest competitor is MUTE (the previous SOTA hybrid RL-Evolutionary method), which ARITH-DAS outperforms by 10–31% in hypervolume depending on bit-width and encoding (e.g., 3,247.06 vs. 2,470.47 on 32-bit AND; 12,212.89 vs. 11,034.68 on 64-bit Booth). ArithTreeRL, the leading pure-RL approach, is second-closest but trails ARITH-DAS by 17–26% in HV. The ILP-based methods (GOMIL, UFO-MAC) and the differentiable proxy-based method (DOMAC) are consistently outperformed, with DOMAC showing the weakest results on smaller bit-widths (e.g., HV 4.60 vs. 16.30 on 8-bit AND). Wallace, the classical heuristic, serves as the weakest baseline across all configurations. The gap between ARITH-DAS and MUTE widens with increasing bit-width for AND encoding (from ~40% at 8-bit to ~17% at 64-bit) but remains substantial for Booth encoding (11–31%), confirming that fine-grained interconnect optimization becomes increasingly critical at scale.

## Relation to Prior Work

ARITH-DAS sits at the intersection of differentiable architecture search (DARTS, ProxylessNAS), multi-relational graph neural networks (R-GCN, Graphormer), and reinforcement learning for EDA (MUTE, ArithTreeRL, DOMAC). It departs from prior L2O approaches that treat interconnect routing as a heuristic afterthought or an oversimplified proxy-optimization problem (UFO-MAC, DOMAC) by formulating it as a differentiable edge-prediction task over a multi-relational DAG, trained end-to-end against post-synthesis metrics via a PPO-style objective. The coarse-grained allocation stage reuses the circuit genetic evolution from MUTE, while the fine-grained interconnect stage introduces a Graphormer-style attention mechanism with relation-specific query/key projections for port-level connectivity inference.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that the interconnect design space, long treated as a trivial sequential wiring step, contains over 10% timing optimization headroom that proxy-based methods systematically miss as bit-width grows. The 27% hypervolume gain and 6.59% accelerator delay reduction show practical value for AI hardware design, but the evaluation is confined to post-synthesis metrics on a single 45nm technology node (Nangate45), leaving open whether the proxy-free advantage persists through placement, routing, and timing closure at advanced nodes. A key open question is whether the differentiable edge-prediction formulation can be extended to jointly optimize across the full physical design flow rather than stopping at logic synthesis.
