## Problem

In fine-tuning-as-a-service (FTaaS) settings, malicious users can inject patch-based backdoor triggers into MLLM training data with minimal effort, yet prior defenses rely on clean reference data, auxiliary supervision, model modifications, or are limited to unimodal CNN architectures. The paper asks whether backdoor triggers leave identifiable traces within the model's own attention mechanisms, enabling self-contained detection without any external guidance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250851/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a potentially poisoned fine-tuning dataset of image-text query pairs (e.g., 6K–10K samples per task) and a pre-trained MLLM (LLaVA-1.5-7B or InternVL2.5-8B). It first fine-tunes the model on the full dataset via LoRA, then extracts per-layer cross-modal attention vectors (first decoding token → all 576 image tokens in LLaVA) as the diagnostic signal. The output is a purified training set with suspicious low-entropy samples removed, followed by a re-fine-tuned robustified model that retains clean-task performance while suppressing attack success rates to near zero.

## Method

The key insight is 'attention collapse': patch-based triggers cause the model to disproportionately concentrate attention on the trigger region, producing abnormally sharp (low-entropy) attention distributions that differ systematically from the dispersed, semantically grounded attention on clean samples. BYE operates as a three-stage unsupervised pipeline: (1) extract head-averaged cross-modal attention maps from all decoder layers after initial fine-tuning; (2) compute per-layer Shannon entropy of each attention vector, fit a two-component GMM per layer to detect bimodal separation, and select entropy-sensitive layers via a Bimodal Separation Index (BSI) threshold; (3) aggregate entropy across selected layers into a per-sample descriptor, apply GMM clustering to isolate the low-entropy cluster as poisoned samples, filter them out, and re-fine-tune the model on the purified set. The entire process requires no clean labels, auxiliary models, or architectural modifications, and generalizes across trigger types (single, multi, random-position, texture, blended) and backbone architectures.

## Results

BYE reduces Attack Success Rate (ASR) to near-zero across all four datasets while preserving Clean Performance (CP). On LLaVA, BYE achieves ASR of 0.05% (ScienceQA), 0.00% (IconQA), 1.40% (Flickr30k), and 0.00% (RSVQA), with CP within 0.4–2.1 points of vanilla fine-tuning. On InternVL, ASR drops to 8.97%, 6.87%, 12.40%, and 7.18% respectively, while CP remains within 1.4 points of the unpoised baseline. Poisoned-sample detection F1 scores range from 87.38% to 99.60% across datasets and models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BYE dominates all three baselines on both CP and ASR simultaneously. Against ZIP (Shi et al., NeurIPS 2023), BYE achieves dramatically lower ASR (e.g., 0.05% vs. 66.48% on ScienceQA/LLaVA) while retaining far higher CP (89.64 vs. 79.97); ZIP's diffusion-based purification severely degrades clean performance, dropping Flickr30k CP from 71.03 to 36.88. Against DiffPure (Nie et al., ICML 2022), BYE reduces ASR from 78.08% to 0.05% on ScienceQA while improving CP from 81.71 to 89.64. Against SampDetox (Yang et al., NeurIPS 2024), BYE cuts ASR from 90.62% to 0.05% on ScienceQA and from 67.74% to 0.00% on IconQA, with CP gains of 6.05 and 10.53 points respectively. Random Drop, which indiscriminately removes 20% of samples, barely reduces ASR (e.g., 97.12% vs. 97.32% on ScienceQA/LLaVA), confirming that data-level random filtering is ineffective against targeted poisoning.

## Relation to Prior Work

BYE repurposes the long-standing idea that poisoned inputs leave abnormal internal signatures (as in SentiNet's CNN saliency scoring) by translating it to the Transformer attention regime: instead of localized activation peaks in convolutional feature maps, it exploits the global 'attention collapse' phenomenon where cross-modal attention entropy drops sharply on trigger-bearing samples. It departs from prior MLLM backdoor defenses (input purification via diffusion, trigger inversion, parameter decoupling) by requiring no clean reference data, no auxiliary denoising models, and no model modification—operating purely on the fine-tuned model's own attention statistics. The FTaaS threat model connects to the broader harmful-fine-tuning literature, but BYE is the first to propose a fully self-contained, unsupervised data-filtering pipeline specifically for multimodal fine-tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As MLLM fine-tuning APIs (OpenAI, Mistral) become standard deployment infrastructure, the ability to detect poisoned user-submitted data without clean labels or external models is practically critical for service providers. BYE's 72-minute pipeline (vs. 162+ minutes for diffusion-based baselines) and near-zero ASR on patch triggers make it a deployable screening step. However, effectiveness degrades substantially under blended/global triggers (ASR ~54–60%), and the method is limited to offline, single-stage fine-tuning; extending it to online adaptation, continual learning, and more adaptive attack strategies remains open.
