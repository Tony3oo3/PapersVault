## Problem

Prior sequential disentanglement work has focused on the binary static-versus-dynamic split, overlooking the inherently multi-factor nature of real-world data where, for example, identity decomposes into age and sex while expression decomposes into smile intensity and head movement. The field also suffers from fragmented datasets, inconsistent evaluation protocols, reliance on fully labeled data, and labor-intensive manual mapping of latent variables to semantic factors, all of which hinder scalable and fair comparison of methods.

## Input / Output

Each input is a sequence of length T with elements in R^o, spanning video (64×64 RGB frames, 8–16 frames per clip), audio waveforms (48,000 samples), and multivariate time series (13 features over 24 steps). The model produces a fixed-length latent vector z ∈ R^l per sequence, partitioned into static and dynamic subsets, where each semantic factor f_i is assigned to a (ideally disjoint) coordinate subset z_{J_i} that faithfully reflects its temporal behavior. For evaluation, the model additionally supports channel swapping between two latent representations and sampling new sequences from the latent space.

## Method

The core model, SSM-SKD, extends the Structured Koopman Disentanglement (SKD) framework by constraining all static information into a single Koopman eigenvector (eigenvalue ≈ 1), with individual static factors encoded across its orthogonal coordinates, while all remaining eigenvectors (eigenvalues strictly within the unit circle) capture dynamic modes. To overcome the capacity limitation of a single static vector at the batch level, SSM-SKD approximates the Koopman operator per-sample rather than per-batch, giving each sequence its own Koopman matrix and enabling fine-grained static disentanglement without enlarging the matrix. The paper additionally introduces a post-hoc Latent Exploration Stage (LES) with predictor-based and swap-based strategies to automatically map latent dimensions to semantic factors, and a VLM-based tagger/judge that performs zero-shot factor discovery, label assignment, and evaluation, removing the need for manual annotations and dataset-specific classifiers. Together with a modular benchmark spanning six datasets, six methods, and ten metrics, these components form a complete development-evaluation framework for multi-factor sequential disentanglement.

## Results

SSM-SKD achieves the highest aggregated disentanglement score on 4 out of 6 benchmark datasets, reaching 0.95 on Sprites (a ~16% improvement over the prior best SKD at 0.75) and 0.96 on 3D Shapes. On the more challenging dSprites-Static and dSprites-Dynamic variants, SSM-SKD scores 0.83 and 0.71 respectively, outperforming the next-best methods by 0.13 and 0.03. On audio (dMelodies-WAV) and time series (BMS Air Quality), all methods remain below 0.55, indicating substantial room for improvement in these underrepresented modalities. In the per-metric leaderboard, SSM-SKD ranks first on 8 of 10 metrics (M-Swap, M-GSample, DCI-M, DCI-C, DCI-E, C-Swap, C-Sample, GC-Sample), while SKD leads on the two-factor 2-Swap and 2-GSample metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SSM-SKD outperforms all five baselines (Sparse-AE, VAE, β-VAE, MGP-VAE, SKD) on the aggregated multi-factor score across all six datasets. The most dramatic gain is over SKD on Sprites (0.95 vs. 0.75, +16%), where the single-static-mode constraint and per-instance Koopman matrix yield substantially cleaner factor separation. SKD remains competitive on the two-factor metrics (2-Swap: 0.87, 2-GSample: 0.87), where it edges out SSM-SKD (0.86/0.86), suggesting the per-batch Koopman formulation still captures coarse static/dynamic splits well. MGP-VAE is the strongest non-Koopman baseline, ranking second on M-Swap (0.68) and C-Swap (0.66), but it trails SSM-SKD by 0.11 on M-Swap and 0.17 on C-Swap. β-VAE and VAE show inconsistent performance, occasionally matching or exceeding SSM-SKD on individual metrics (e.g., β-VAE leads on DCI-C at 0.79 and C-Sample at 0.95) but never winning the overall aggregated score on any dataset. On the hardest benchmarks (dMelodies-WAV, BMS Air Quality), all methods cluster within 0.05 of each other, indicating that multi-factor disentanglement in audio and time series remains largely unsolved.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the SKD framework (Berman et al., ICLR 2023) for multi-factor sequential disentanglement by replacing the per-batch Koopman matrix with per-instance matrices and constraining static information to a single eigenvector (SSM-SKD). Conceptually, it moves the field beyond the dominant two-factor (static vs. dynamic) formulation toward decomposing each category into multiple semantic sub-factors, building on the Koopman operator theory lineage (Takeishi et al. 2017; Naiman et al. 2024) and the broader sequential disentanglement literature (FHVAE, S3VAE, MGP-VAE). The VLM-based tagger/judge component draws on the LLM-as-a-judge paradigm and recent multimodal LLM advances, repurposing them for disentanglement evaluation where prior work relied on dataset-specific classifiers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first standardized, multi-modality benchmark for multi-factor sequential disentanglement, addressing a critical infrastructure gap where prior work relied on fragmented datasets and inconsistent protocols. The VLM-based evaluation pipeline and Latent Exploration Stage offer a path toward fully automated, label-free benchmarking that could accelerate the field. However, the failure case on VoxCelebOne reveals that all current methods—largely VAE-based—still cannot capture high-level semantic factors on real-world video, pointing to the need for new generative paradigms (diffusion, GANs, hierarchical VAEs). Open questions include whether the per-instance Koopman formulation scales to longer sequences and whether VLM-based evaluation remains reliable as factor granularity increases.
