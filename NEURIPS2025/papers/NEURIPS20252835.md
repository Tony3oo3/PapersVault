## Problem

Existing RAG methods retrieve documents based on relevance to the query but do not calibrate the confidence associated with the user's downstream decision. LLMs are overconfident even when retrieved documents are irrelevant or misleading, and prior decision-calibration frameworks (e.g., Band et al.) require fine-tuning multiple LLMs with PPO and cannot be directly applied to RAG contexts. The paper addresses the gap of producing a well-calibrated probability that a user's decision—guided by RAG-generated text—will actually be correct.

## Input / Output

The method takes an open-ended query q (derived from a decision question x), a scalar temperature parameter t ∈ [1.0, 2.0] encoding the user's risk tolerance or decision urgency, and a set of K retrieved documents from an external database. It produces a calibrated confidence score c = f(t, q, d) ∈ [0,1] predicting the probability of a correct user decision, a long-form guidance passage z generated by the LLM conditioned on the selected document, and a reranked document selection. Notably, the confidence is predicted before z is generated (conditioning on q and d rather than on the generated text), which enables document reranking at inference time without requiring guidance generation for every candidate.

## Method

CalibRAG trains a forecasting function f(t, q, d) using a frozen Llama-3.1-8B as a feature extractor (last-token hidden state from the second-to-last layer), augmented with LoRA adapters and a lightweight linear classification head. The temperature parameter t is encoded via a 6-frequency Fourier positional encoding and added element-wise to the base features, allowing the model to adapt its confidence prediction to different user behaviors. Supervision comes from soft binary labels b ∈ [0,1] computed by sampling R=10 responses from a surrogate user model U at each temperature and measuring the proportion of correct decisions (evaluated by GPT-4o-mini), with training via binary cross-entropy (a strictly proper scoring rule). At inference, CalibRAG executes a four-stage pipeline: retrieve top-K documents, score each with f(t, q, d) and rerank by predicted decision correctness, optionally reformulate the query if the top score falls below a threshold ε, and finally generate guidance and produce the calibrated decision. This design decouples confidence estimation from text generation, enabling both calibrated decision-making and effective document reranking in a single unified framework.

## Results

On Natural Questions (NQ) and WebQA with BM25 retrieval, CalibRAG achieves an ECE of 0.0600 and 0.0567 respectively, reducing calibration error by ~3× compared to the best verbalized-confidence baseline (Number-LoRA, ECE 0.1922/0.1931) and by ~4× compared to CT-LoRA (ECE 0.2479/0.2487). CalibRAG also attains the highest AUROC (77.29 on both datasets) and accuracy (42.66% on NQ, 43.77% on WebQA). With the Contriever dense retriever, CalibRAG further reduces ECE to 0.0312 on NQ while boosting accuracy to 46.55%, and on the medical domain (MedCPT retriever) it outperforms all baselines across BioASQ-Y/N, MMLU-Med, and PubMedQA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CalibRAG outperforms all six baselines across every reported metric. Against the Calibration-Tuning baselines (CT-LoRA and CT-probe, Kapoor et al.), CalibRAG reduces ECE from 0.248–0.349 to 0.057–0.060 on NQ/WebQA while also improving AUROC by 4–16 points. Against the verbalized-confidence baselines (Number-LoRA from Tian et al. and Linguistic-LoRA from Xiong et al.), CalibRAG achieves roughly 3× lower ECE (0.06 vs. 0.19 for Number-LoRA; 0.06 vs. 0.45 for Linguistic-LoRA) and higher accuracy (+5 to +14 points). In the reranking comparison on HotpotQA, CalibRAG (AUROC 72.47, ECE 0.106) surpasses both Cross-encoder (AUROC 60.74, ECE 0.477) and LLM-rerank (AUROC 60.57, ECE 0.248). Against the robust RAG baseline SelfRAG (Asai et al.), CalibRAG improves AUROC by 15 points on NQ (63.5 vs. 48.4) and 17 points on WebQA (68.8 vs. 51.9), while cutting ECE by more than half (0.258 vs. 0.522 on NQ). On the BEIR reranking benchmark, CalibRAG also edges out Cross-encoder on NDCG@5 (0.7863 vs. 0.7655 on TREC-COVID; 0.6872 vs. 0.6668 on SciFact), confirming that its confidence-based reranking is competitive as a pure retrieval method as well.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Band et al.'s (2024) decision calibration framework, which aligned LLM confidence with user decision accuracy but explicitly could not handle RAG contexts and required three fine-tuned LLMs plus PPO. CalibRAG reformulates the forecasting function from f(x,z) over generated text to f(t,q,d) over the query-document pair before generation, enabling confidence-based reranking and eliminating the need for PPO. It also departs from standard reranking methods (cross-encoders, LLM-rerank, SelfRAG) that optimize for relevance or utility scores rather than calibrated decision correctness, and from verbalized-confidence and calibration-tuning baselines that condition on the top-1 retrieved document without temperature-aware user modeling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CalibRAG addresses a practically critical gap: LLMs deployed in high-stakes decision support (medical diagnosis, legal reasoning) are overconfident and users over-rely on them, yet no prior method provided calibrated confidence specifically for RAG-guided decisions. The temperature-conditioned formulation is a useful practical contribution, allowing the same system to adapt to cautious vs. exploratory user behavior. Key limitations include reliance on a surrogate LLM user model (rather than real humans), the computational overhead of synthetic supervision data construction, and evaluation limited to zero-shot QA settings. Open questions include whether the calibration transfers to multi-turn or interactive decision-making, how it behaves when the retrieved corpus is genuinely insufficient, and whether the surrogate user model's behavior faithfully captures human decision heuristics.
