## Problem

It remains open whether there exists a CPWL function on R^d that requires more than two hidden layers in a ReLU network for exact representation; the best known general lower bound is 2. This paper establishes the first non-constant depth lower bound, Ω(log log d), under the natural restriction that all neuron breakpoints lie on the braid fan (hyperplanes x_i = x_j or x_i = 0), and provides a combinatorial proof that 3 layers are needed for max of 5 numbers in this setting.

## Input / Output

The function under study is x ↦ max{0, x_1, …, x_d} on R^d, with the critical constraint that the representing network is B_d^0-conforming: every neuron's non-differentiability set is contained in the union of hyperplanes x_i = x_j and x_i = 0. The paper also extends to maxout networks where each hidden layer computes the maximum of r_i affine maps per coordinate, and shows that a rank-(3,2) maxout network can compute max of 7 numbers, exceeding the product-of-ranks upper bound of 6.

## Method

The core technique exploits a vector-space isomorphism Φ between CPWL functions compatible with the braid fan and real-valued set functions on the Boolean lattice 2^[d], mapping the basis {σ_M} to indicator-based set functions via Möbius inversion. Under this isomorphism, a rank-2-maxout layer corresponds to taking the positive part F⁺ of a set function F that satisfies a 'conforming' sign-consistency condition on chains. The key inductive argument (Proposition 4.7) shows that applying such a layer to functions in F_L(k) yields functions in F_L(k²+k), by finding a large subset T ⊆ Y\X such that F restricted to every sublattice [S, S∪T] has rank at most k−1, using Lemma 4.5 to push positive and negative support elements to low rank. Iterating over ℓ layers gives the bound 2^{2^ℓ−1}, yielding the Ω(log log d) depth requirement. For d=4, a refined combinatorial argument (Lemma 5.1) sharpens the bound to exactly 4, proving the tightness of the 2-layer limit without the mixed-integer programming used previously.

## Results

The paper establishes a doubly-logarithmic lower bound: for B_d^0-conforming ReLU (equivalently rank-2-maxout) networks, computing max{0, x_1, …, x_d} requires Ω(log log d) hidden layers (Theorem 4.8, Corollary 4.9). In the specific case d = 4, a combinatorial proof shows that 2 hidden layers are insufficient to compute max{0, x_1, x_2, x_3, x_4}, confirming the tight bound M_{B_d}^2(2) = V_{B_d}(4) (Theorem 5.2). Additionally, the paper demonstrates that the natural upper bound of ∏r_i for maxout networks is not tight: a single rank-3 maxout layer followed by a rank-2 maxout layer (product = 6) suffices to compute max{0, x_1, …, x_6} (Theorem 6.2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no experimental baselines. The results are positioned against prior theoretical bounds: the Ω(log log d) lower bound improves upon the previous best general lower bound of 2 hidden layers (Mukherjee and Basu, 2017) for B_d^0-conforming networks, and provides the first non-constant conditional lower bound without restricting network weights. The combinatorial proof for d = 4 (Theorem 5.2) replaces the earlier mixed-integer-programming verification by Hertrich et al. (2023) with a transparent argument. The maxout result (Theorem 6.2) shows that the straightforward generalization of the Arora et al. (2018) upper bound (∏r_i maxima) is not tight, since 3×2 = 6 < 7 numbers can be computed. The paper also notes that Bakaev et al. (2025b) showed 2 layers suffice for max of 5 numbers in the unrestricted setting, confirming that the B_d^0-conforming restriction is genuinely limiting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the braid-fan-conforming framework introduced by Hertrich et al. [2023], who first showed that 2 hidden layers are insufficient for the max-of-5 function via a mixed-integer programming computation. The key methodological departure is translating the problem into the language of set functions on Boolean lattices via the isomorphism Φ (building on the σ_M basis from Danilov and Koshevoy [2000]), which enables a clean inductive argument yielding a doubly-logarithmic lower bound. The paper also positions itself relative to the weight-restriction approach of Averkov et al. [2025] and Haase et al. [2023], noting the two strategies are incomparable, and extends the analysis to maxout networks as a natural generalization of ReLU networks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Ω(log log d) lower bound is the first non-constant depth lower bound for ReLU networks without restricting weights, a significant step toward resolving the open question of whether logarithmic depth is truly necessary for all CPWL functions. However, the result is conditional on the braid-fan-conforming assumption, and the authors explicitly note that Bakaev et al. [2025b] showed the max-of-5 function is computable with 2 layers in the unrestricted setting, confirming this is a genuine restriction. The maxout result (rank-3 then rank-2 computes max of 7, exceeding the 3×2=6 product bound) demonstrates that the straightforward generalization of the Arora et al. upper bound is not tight, opening questions about the true depth-width tradeoff for maxout architectures. The broader open question of whether any CPWL function requires more than 2 hidden layers in the unrestricted setting remains unresolved.
