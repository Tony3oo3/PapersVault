## Problem

Diffusion-based inverse problem solvers rely on pretrained generative models trained on large curated datasets, making them inapplicable when such data is unavailable or when the forward operator differs from training conditions. This paper asks whether the progressive, coarse-to-fine structure of diffusion can be exploited without any pretrained model, using only the observed measurements and the intrinsic spectral bias of a randomly initialized network.

## Input / Output

The method takes as input the degraded observation y = Ax (where A is an arbitrary known linear forward operator such as an undersampling mask, blur kernel, or compression matrix) and a single randomly initialized CNN (typically a U-Net with Gaussian-initialized weights, σ=0.02). It produces a single reconstructed solution x̂ after T sequential noise steps, each involving K inner gradient-descent iterations on the denoiser. The process is entirely self-contained: no external dataset, pretrained checkpoint, or score function is required, and the same architecture handles 1D signals, 2D/3D images, and radar heatmaps by swapping A and adjusting network dimensionality.

## Method

Self-diffusion iterates backward from pure noise x_T ~ N(0,I) through T noise steps. At each step t, Gaussian noise σ(t)ε_t is added to the current estimate, and a single untrained CNN (the self-denoiser) is trained for K iterations to minimize the data-fidelity loss ‖A·D_θ(x_t) − y‖². The key insight is that a first-order Taylor expansion of the expected loss reveals an implicit Jacobian regularizer σ(t)²‖A·J_D‖_F², which in Fourier space penalizes high-frequency modes with weight proportional to k²·σ(t)². As σ(t) decreases from large to zero, this creates a noise-modulated spectral bias: early steps enforce smoothness and recover coarse structure, while later steps progressively unlock fine details. The process is piecewise stochastic (noise is resampled at each step but fixed within K inner iterations), and in the continuous limit it corresponds to the SDE dx = (x − x_true)dt + σ(t)dW(t), where the drift term reflects the denoiser's pull toward the true solution.

## Results

On 2D MRI reconstruction from 4× undersampled k-space, SDI achieves 39.21 dB PSNR / 0.9640 SSIM / 0.0116 NRMSE, outperforming IMJENSE (38.80/0.9604/0.0125) and DIP (38.35/0.9574/0.0136). On low-level vision tasks (ImageNet, 256×256), SDI leads all baselines: 30.30 dB PSNR on 2× SR, 25.82 on 4× SR, 27.76 on motion deblurring, and 30.78 on inpainting. For 1D compressed-sensing signal recovery (128-point signal, 35 measurements), SDI achieves a reconstruction error of 0.53, substantially below DIP (2.43) and ADMM-basis pursuit (5.57). On CBSD68 denoising (σ=25), SDI (28.10 dB PSNR) trails the supervised FFDNET (31.22 dB) and IR-SDE (28.09 dB PSNR but 0.101 LPIPS) but exceeds DIP (25.44 dB).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SDI consistently outperforms DIP across all evaluated tasks: on 4× MRI it gains +0.86 dB PSNR (39.21 vs 38.35), on 2× SR it gains +1.24 dB (30.30 vs 29.06), on motion deblurring +1.82 dB (27.76 vs 25.94), and on signal recovery it reduces error by ~4.6× (0.53 vs 2.43). Against Aseq, SDI's advantage is most pronounced on MRI (+7.03 dB on 4×) and inpainting (+2.72 dB), confirming that the iterative noise-modulated process adds substantial value over a single-pass autoencoding DIP. SDI also beats IMJENSE on both 4× and 6× MRI by 0.41 and 1.54 dB PSNR respectively. Against the pretrained-diffusion baseline CSGM, results are mixed: CSGM leads on T2 contrast with ACS lines (38.78 vs 38.08 dB at 4×), but SDI surpasses CSGM on FLAIR (38.41 vs 37.41 dB) and on the challenging 4× w/o ACS setting (37.66 vs 34.00 dB), suggesting SDI generalizes better across contrasts without retraining. On denoising, SDI (28.10 dB PSNR, 0.207 LPIPS) is competitive with IR-SDE (28.09 dB, 0.101 LPIPS) in PSNR but lags in perceptual quality (LPIPS), and is clearly outperformed by the supervised FFDNET (31.22 dB, 0.121 LPIPS), which has access to large-scale training data. Against the classical ADMM-basis pursuit method, SDI achieves a 10.5× lower reconstruction error on the 1D compressed-sensing task (0.53 vs 5.57).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two lines of work: the Deep Image Prior (DIP) paradigm, which uses a randomly initialized network optimized on a single observation as an implicit prior, and the diffusion model framework, which provides a structured progressive denoising schedule. The key departure from prior diffusion-based inverse problem solvers (DPS, DDNM, CSGM) is that self-diffusion eliminates the need for a pretrained score function or generative model entirely—the forward noising process is self-referential (applied to the current estimate rather than a clean training sample), and the denoiser is a single untrained U-Net continuously optimized across noise levels. The theoretical grounding draws on Heckel and Soltanolkotabi's convergence results for untrained networks and the spectral bias / NTK literature to explain why the noise schedule induces a coarse-to-fine reconstruction regime.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Self-diffusion offers a principled, training-free alternative for inverse problems in settings where large curated datasets or pretrained generative models are unavailable—relevant for medical imaging with limited data, novel sensor modalities, or rapidly changing acquisition protocols. The noise-modulated spectral bias analysis provides a useful theoretical lens connecting the diffusion schedule to implicit regularization, which could inform future adaptive scheduling or architecture design. Open questions include whether the approach scales efficiently to very large images (the 5000×3000 example required ~2 hours), whether adaptive or learned noise schedules could close the remaining gap to supervised methods, and how the method performs on highly nonlinear or non-convex forward operators beyond the linear cases tested here.
