## Problem

The role of bias terms in Implicit Neural Representations (INRs) with periodic activations has remained unexplored, despite the conventional practice of applying full-bias MLP architectures. In traditional ReLU networks, bias terms shift activations to enhance nonlinear capacity, but in sine-activated INRs this mechanism is fundamentally different due to the boundedness and periodicity of sine functions. The paper asks how significant bias terms truly are in INRs and what unique function they serve.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253463/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For the INR representation task, 2D spatial coordinates normalized to [-1,1] are mapped to RGB pixel values via a 3×256 SIREN or FINER network. The key unusual input is the input-layer bias vector, which in Feat-Bias is initialized with high-level features from pre-trained encoders (ViT-Base 768-dim or DINOv2) rescaled to [-1/√n, 1/√n] and frozen throughout encoding. For the downstream classification task, the frozen input-layer bias values serve as the sole neural parameter input to a lightweight 3×256 MLP that outputs a class label, bypassing the need to parse intractable weight matrices.

## Method

The paper first demonstrates through analytical and empirical studies that bias terms in periodically activated INRs primarily eliminate spatial aliasing—caused by the combined central symmetry of normalized coordinates and odd sine activations—rather than shifting activations. This leads to the finding that frozen bias terms applied exclusively to the input layer consistently outperform the conventional full-bias architecture, reducing parameter count while improving PSNR by up to 1.13 dB. Building on the observation that bias gradient propagation has negligible effect on reconstruction, the authors propose Feat-Bias: initializing input-layer bias with pre-trained encoder features (ViT or DINOv2) and freezing them during INR encoding. This embeds semantically rich, high-level features directly into the INR parameter space, enabling a lightweight MLP to achieve 93.78% accuracy on CIFAR-10 INR classification—substantially surpassing end-to-end methods like MWT (46.94%) and symmetry-equivariant parsers—while reducing inference time from minutes to seconds.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 INR classification, Feat-Bias[DINOv2] achieves 93.78% accuracy with a lightweight 3×256 MLP, surpassing the best prior method Zoo[ViT]+LP* (81.57%) by over 12 points while using only 151k parameters vs. ~5k for Zoo[ViT]. On the INR representation task (DIV2K, 5k iterations), applying frozen bias exclusively to the input layer (Input Bias) improves PSNR by 0.88 dB (SIREN: 36.63 vs. 36.19) and 1.13 dB (FINER: 39.29 vs. 38.47) over the standard full-bias architecture, while reducing parameter count. On CIFAR-100, Feat-Bias[DINOv2] reaches 78.51% accuracy in ~145 seconds, compared to MWT's 23.29% in ~168 minutes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Feat-Bias decisively outperforms all symmetry-equivariant neural parameter parsers: NFN (NP/HNP) achieves only ~26% accuracy, ScaleGMN-B reaches 56.14%, and NG-GNN attains 91.40% on MNIST but 68.00% on F-MNIST, while Feat-Bias[DINOv2] scores 93.78% on CIFAR-10 and 92.38% on F-MNIST. Against the end-to-end MWT framework, Feat-Bias wins by a wide margin (93.78% vs. 46.94% on CIFAR-10) while also maintaining superior reconstruction quality (38.02 dB PSNR vs. 23.23 dB), avoiding the accuracy-reconstruction trade-off inherent to MWT. The strongest baseline, Zoo[ViT]+LP* (81.57%), is surpassed by Feat-Bias[ViT] by 10.11 points and by Feat-Bias[DINOv2] by 12.21 points, with Feat-Bias additionally reducing inference time from minutes to ~65 seconds. On the INR representation task, the proposed Input Bias configuration consistently beats the Standard full-bias architecture by 0.44–0.82 dB PSNR across SIREN and FINER on both DIV2K and Kodak, while Bias-Free and Output-Bias configurations collapse to ~14–15 dB due to spatial aliasing. On CIFAR-100, Feat-Bias[DINOv2] (78.51%) exceeds MWT-20epochs (23.29%) by over 55 points in roughly 1/7th the time.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on SIREN and FINER, the two dominant periodically-activated INR architectures, but challenges a universal design assumption inherited from standard MLPs: that bias terms in every layer contribute meaningfully to network expressiveness through activation shifting. For INR post-processing, it departs from both symmetry-equivariant parameter parsers (NFN, ScaleGMN, NG-GNN) and end-to-end frameworks (MWT) by identifying input-layer bias as a stable, gradient-invariant storage space for pre-trained encoder features (ViT, DINOv2), sidestepping the intractable problem of interpreting neural weights.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that bias terms in periodically-activated INRs serve primarily to break coordinate-activation symmetry (eliminating spatial aliasing) rather than to shift activations reframes a long-unexamined architectural choice and suggests a simpler, more parameter-efficient INR design. Feat-Bias offers a practical, lightweight path to strong INR post-processing without the computational overhead of end-to-end training or the complexity of equivariant parsers. Key limitations include the absence of a rigorous mathematical proof for the spatial aliasing mechanism, and the analysis is restricted to 2D image fitting with periodic activations; whether these findings generalize to 3D INRs (e.g., NeRF), non-periodic activations, or other signal modalities remains open.
