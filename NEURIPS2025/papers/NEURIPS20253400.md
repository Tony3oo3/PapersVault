## Problem

Personalized federated learning produces agent-specific models but lacks uncertainty quantification that is simultaneously agent-specific and instance-specific. Existing federated conformal prediction methods assume test data arise from a mixture over all agents, violating the personalized setting where the test point belongs to a specific target agent, and none address localization or instance-level conditional coverage. The paper asks: can one construct conformal prediction sets for a target agent that guarantee marginal coverage while improving test-conditional coverage by leveraging source agents' data in a privacy-preserving federated manner?

## Input / Output

The method consumes a test covariate X from the target agent, pretrained neural-network score functions (residual |y−μ̂(x)| for regression, 1−probability for classification), a calibration set of size n from the target agent, and federated access to K source agents' data (only model gradients and logits are exchanged, never raw data). It outputs a set-valued prediction Ĉ_α(X_{n+1}) with a finite-sample marginal coverage guarantee of at least 1−α for the target agent, along with improved test-conditional coverage and reduced set size compared to using only local data. The calibration step uses exclusively target-agent data, decoupling the coverage guarantee from cross-agent distributional assumptions.

## Method

PFCP builds on a Generalized Localized Conformal Prediction (GLCP) framework that transforms conformity scores via a conditional distribution estimator F̂(s|x) (implemented with engression, a generative model trained via energy-score loss) into probability-like values, then applies split conformal on these transformed scores. The key innovation is an aggregation step that combines a local conditional distribution estimator (trained on target data) with a federated mixture estimator (trained across source agents via FedAvg) weighted by a density ratio r̂(x,s), yielding an enhanced estimator F̂_agg(s|x) = {1+r̂(x)}⁻¹[E_{S~F̂}1(S≤s) + E_{S~F̂_mix}{1(S≤s)r̂(x,S)}]. The density ratio is estimated through a federated binary classifier (distinguishing target vs. mixture samples) followed by local Platt calibration, avoiding explicit joint/marginal density ratio computation. Because only the target agent's calibration data enters the final quantile, marginal coverage holds regardless of source-target heterogeneity, while the aggregated estimator reduces the total-variation distance δ₁(x;F̂_agg) relative to the local-only δ₁(x;F̂), yielding tighter sets and better conditional coverage.

## Results

On six real-world datasets (BIO, BIKE, CRIME, STAR, CONCRETE, DERMA) at nominal 90% coverage, PFCP achieves valid marginal coverage (0.896–0.905) while all four federated baselines (FedCP, FedCP-QQ, CPlab, CPhet) deviate significantly from the target in most scenarios. PFCP reduces test-conditional miscoverage error by 30–40% relative to the local-only GLCP baseline (e.g., 0.0199 vs. 0.0315 on BIO; 0.0238 vs. 0.0366 on CONCRETE) and simultaneously shrinks average prediction set size (e.g., 28.38 vs. 34.49 on CONCRETE; 43.29 vs. 48.50 on STAR). Synthetic experiments confirm that the conditional coverage advantage of PFCP over GLCP grows with covariate dimension (d from 10 to 30).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PFCP decisively outperforms all four federated baselines on marginal coverage validity: FedCP [23] and FedCP-QQ [16] violate the 90% guarantee in 4–5 of 6 datasets (e.g., FedCP reaches 0.993 on BIO and 0.824 on DERMA), while CPlab [27] and CPhet [28] similarly fail in most settings (e.g., CPhet: 0.864 on CRIME, 0.868 on DERMA). On test-conditional miscoverage, PFCP achieves the lowest error across all six datasets, beating GLCP (local-only) by 30–40% and the federated baselines by 50–70% (e.g., 0.0199 vs. 0.0941 for FedCP on BIO). In terms of prediction set size, PFCP is smaller than GLCP on all datasets (e.g., 28.38 vs. 34.49 on CONCRETE, a 17.7% reduction), though FedCP and FedCP-QQ sometimes produce smaller sets at the cost of invalid coverage. The STAR dataset is the closest case where all methods achieve roughly valid marginal coverage, yet PFCP still reduces miscoverage from 0.0392 (GLCP) to 0.0244. Overall, PFCP is the only method that simultaneously satisfies marginal validity, minimizes conditional miscoverage, and keeps prediction sets compact.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies and extends two previously separate lines of work: localized conformal prediction (Guan 2023, Chernozhukov et al. 2021) and federated conformal prediction (Lu et al. 2023, Humbert et al. 2023, Plassier et al. 2023/2024). The GLCP framework generalizes both localized and distributional conformal prediction into a single formulation using an arbitrary conditional distribution estimator (engression), while PFCP departs from all existing federated CP methods by targeting a specific agent's distribution rather than a mixture, and by incorporating density-ratio-weighted source agent data to improve instance-level coverage. The federated density ratio estimation component draws on the binary-classification reduction from Sugiyama et al. (2012) and the reweighting principle from weighted conformal prediction (Tibshirani et al. 2019), but applies them in a privacy-preserving federated setting that prior work had not addressed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PFCP addresses a practical gap in risk-sensitive federated applications (medical diagnosis, autonomous driving) where both agent-specific validity and instance-level reliability are needed, yet no prior method provided both simultaneously. The theoretical guarantee (Theorem 2.8) cleanly characterizes when federated knowledge transfer improves conditional coverage over local-only estimation, giving practitioners a principled criterion for when to invoke source agents. Key limitations remain: the method requires non-trivial support overlap between target and source distributions, the engression-based conditional distribution estimation is computationally expensive in high dimensions, and when target data is extremely scarce both GLCP and PFCP degrade. Open questions include scalability to hundreds of heterogeneous agents, handling of non-overlapping supports, and whether lighter-weight conditional distribution estimators can replace engression without sacrificing the theoretical guarantees.
