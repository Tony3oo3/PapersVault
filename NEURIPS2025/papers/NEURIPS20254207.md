## Problem

Existing RL-based fine-tuning for LLM reasoning relies on sparse outcome rewards that provide no feedback on intermediate steps, causing models to generate unnecessarily long chain-of-thought sequences that waste tokens and can even degrade accuracy on simpler tasks. No universal dense process reward exists that quantifies each reasoning step's contribution without requiring task-specific annotations or external evaluators, making it impossible to adaptively allocate reasoning depth across heterogeneous tasks.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text question x and generates a reasoning chain segmented into K consecutive episodes via 'think'/'endthink' delimiters, producing a final answer under a fixed token budget B_token (16,384 in experiments). Each episode serves as a checkpoint where the model's parameter state is evaluated for information gain, and the output is structured as a sequence of episode-level token blocks rather than a monolithic generation, enabling per-episode reward assignment. The model is a stochastic policy π_θ that maps the accumulated context (question + prior episode tokens) to the next episode's token sequence.

## Method

L2T reformulates LLM reasoning as an episodic RL problem where each question is a session of K episodes, and proposes a universal information-theoretic dense process reward with two components: a fitting information gain term (the increase in predicted correctness probability after each episode, approximated by two forward passes) and a parameter compression penalty (the mutual information increment between model parameters and context, estimated via PAC-Bayes bounds and the Fisher information matrix on SVD low-rank parameter proxies). The compression penalty is computed as (θ̃_k − θ̃_{k−1})^T F (θ̃_k − θ̃_{k−1}) where F is the Fisher matrix, reducing the intractable high-dimensional mutual information to a quadratic form on r-dimensional proxies (r/d ≈ 1–10% for 1.5B models). Training is instantiated on GRPO: for each question, N rollouts are generated, each split into episodes, and per-episode rewards combine the sparse outcome reward (divided by K) with the dense process reward, which is then distributed to individual tokens via log-probability surprise weights and normalized into group-level advantages for clipped policy gradient updates.

## Results

On DeepScaleR-1.5B-Preview, L2T achieves 57.8% average pass@1 across AIME24, AIME25, AMC23, MATH500, and MinervaMATH, outperforming the strongest baseline (MRT, 55.9%) by 1.9 points and the base model (54.5%) by 3.3 points. On DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B, L2T reaches 49.2% average pass@1, a 4.3-point gain over the base model and 2.1 points above MRT (47.1%). In terms of efficiency, L2T consumes only ~18% of the tokens required by the base model and roughly halves the token budget of outcome-reward RL (GRPO), while using ~20% fewer tokens than process-reward baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

L2T consistently outperforms all four baselines on both base models across every benchmark. Against GRPO (outcome-reward RL), L2T gains +3.3 and +4.3 points in average pass@1 on the two 1.5B models while roughly doubling token efficiency. Against the process-reward baselines ReST-MCTS and MRT, L2T achieves +2.4 and +1.9 points on DeepScaleR-1.5B-Preview, and +2.8 and +2.1 points on DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B, respectively, while using ~20% fewer tokens. The length-penalty baseline (Arora & Zanette, 2025) actually degrades performance by 3.7 and 2.9 points on the two models, confirming that naive length constraints are counterproductive. Notably, L2T's gains are largest on harder benchmarks (AIME, MinervaMATH) where adaptive reasoning depth matters most, and it maintains stable improvements under varying token budgets (Figure 3).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on GRPO (DeepSeekMath) as its RL optimization backbone but departs fundamentally in reward design: rather than relying on sparse outcome rewards or task-specific process reward models (ReST-MCTS, MRT) that require external annotations, it proposes a universal dense process reward derived from internal model signals—specifically, the information gain in parameters estimated via PAC-Bayes bounds and the Fisher information matrix. The episodic reformulation of the reasoning process (segmenting CoT into checkpoints) is a conceptual departure from the single-episode MDP treatment in prior RL fine-tuning work, enabling per-step credit assignment without task-specific evaluators. The low-rank SVD approximation of the Fisher matrix to make the compression penalty tractable for billion-parameter models is a practical contribution that bridges information-theoretic theory and LLM-scale optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As reasoning LLMs (DeepSeek-R1, o1-like models) become standard, the token efficiency problem is increasingly critical: the paper demonstrates that existing outcome-reward methods waste over 2× the necessary tokens, and that this waste can actively degrade accuracy on simpler tasks. The universal, annotation-free process reward design is practically attractive for multi-task deployment where task-specific PRMs are infeasible. However, open questions remain: the method is validated only on 1.5B–7B models and primarily math/code benchmarks, the Gaussian parameter assumption and SVD approximation quality at scale are untested, and the per-episode Fisher computation overhead may limit applicability to very long reasoning chains or larger models. Whether the episodic decomposition generalizes to open-ended generation tasks beyond structured math reasoning is unclear.
