## Problem

Existing attention perturbation guidance methods for diffusion and flow models apply perturbations at the layer level, relying on heuristic selection of target blocks (e.g., U-Net middle blocks). In Diffusion Transformer (DiT) architectures, quality-relevant computations are distributed evenly across layers without a clear semantic bottleneck, making layer-level selection suboptimal. The paper asks: at what granularity should perturbations be applied, and how can we systematically identify the right targets for a given objective?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254275/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on pretrained DiT-based text-to-image models (Stable Diffusion 3-Medium with 24 layers × 24 heads = 576 heads; FLUX.1-Dev with 57 layers × 24 heads = 1368 heads) and takes a text prompt as input. HeadHunter consumes a set of prompt-seed pairs and a user-defined objective function (e.g., PickScore, aesthetic score) to produce a ranked subset of (layer, head) pairs. At inference time, the selected head set is applied to any new prompt, yielding a guided generated image with negligible additional overhead beyond modifying a fixed subset of attention maps during the forward pass.

## Method

The paper introduces head-level perturbation guidance, which selectively replaces the attention map of individual heads with an identity matrix (PAG) rather than perturbing all heads in a layer, exploiting the observation that heads specialize in distinct visual concepts (lighting, geometry, color, texture) that can be composed. HeadHunter is an iterative greedy selection framework that, over R rounds, generates samples with each candidate head perturbed, scores them with an objective function, and adds the top-k heads to the selected set—enabling discovery of individually weak but compositionally useful heads. SoftPAG provides orthogonal continuous control by linearly interpolating each selected head's attention map toward the identity matrix (A_SoftPAG = (1−u)A + uI), where u ∈ [0,1] tunes perturbation strength and suppresses oversaturation artifacts. The framework is training-free, model-agnostic across DiT variants, and validated on both SD3 and FLUX.1-Dev for general quality improvement and style-specific guidance.

## Results

On 50 unseen content prompts (Table 1), HeadHunter(3.0) achieves PickScore 20.70, AES 5.92, HPS 0.2901, and ImageReward 0.470, substantially outperforming the unguided baseline (PickScore 19.66, AES 5.37) and matching or exceeding CFG(3.0) on AES (5.92 vs 5.71). When combined with CFG, CFG(3.0)+HeadHunter(3.0) reaches PickScore 20.92 and AES 5.93, matching CFG(6.0) on PickScore while surpassing it on AES. On 1K MS-COCO prompts (Fig. 4b), a compact set of only 6 heads (25% of the 24 selected by HeadHunter) achieves FID comparable to or better than the best-performing layer-level PAG across all guidance scales, demonstrating that head-level selection is more sample-efficient than layer-level perturbation.

|---|---|---|---|---|

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CFG, HeadHunter alone (w=3.0) is slightly below CFG(3.0) on PickScore (20.70 vs 20.87) and ImageReward (0.470 vs 0.844), but clearly wins on AES (5.92 vs 5.71). The combined CFG(3.0)+HeadHunter(3.0) configuration matches CFG(6.0) on PickScore (20.92) and HPS (0.3036 vs 0.3046) while surpassing it on AES (5.93 vs 5.80), suggesting HeadHunter adds stylistic quality that higher CFG scales do not capture. Against layer-level PAG (the primary prior method), HeadHunter with only k=6 heads (25% of the 24 selected) matches or beats the best layer-level FID on 1K MS-COCO prompts across all guidance scales, showing that targeted head selection is more efficient than perturbing entire layers. In the perturbation-method comparison (Table 2, Appendix C), the identity-matrix perturbation used by SoftPAG outperforms SEG's query-mean perturbation on PickScore (22.19 vs 21.89), ImageReward (0.65 vs 0.50), and MPS (11.70 vs 11.13), though SEG achieves a slightly lower FID (53.33 vs 56.15), indicating a trade-off between distributional fidelity and human-preference alignment.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the attention perturbation guidance line (PAG, SAG, SEG) by shifting the granularity of intervention from entire layers to individual attention heads within Diffusion Transformer architectures. Prior methods relied on heuristic layer selection (e.g., middle U-Net blocks) because DiTs lack the coarse-to-fine structure that makes such heuristics natural; this work instead exploits the known specialization of multi-head attention to target specific heads. The SoftPAG interpolation and the unified entropy-based view of perturbations (identity, uniform, mean-query, temperature-scaled) generalize and connect previously separate perturbation strategies under a single controllable framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that individual attention heads encode interpretable, composable visual concepts (lighting, geometry, color tone, texture) and that a compact subset of heads can outperform full-layer perturbation offers a practical, training-free knob for style and quality control in modern DiT-based generators. Open questions include whether head selections transfer across model scales and architectures, whether the iterative HeadHunter search can be made more efficient for very large models (e.g., 1368 heads in FLUX.1), and whether the observed head specialization is stable across training runs or prompt distributions. The paper also notes that reward-model bias can cause oversaturation, and that some heads only express their style under explicit style prompts, limiting fully unconditional applicability.
