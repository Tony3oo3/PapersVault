## Problem

Previous evolutionary reinforcement learning (ERL) methods focus on co-optimizing policy parameters, while overlooking the quality of the reward function that directly governs policy learning efficiency. Existing LLM-based reward design methods (Eureka, Text2Reward, R*) can generate improved rewards but neglect sample efficiency, a core RL metric, resulting in excessive environment interactions and unstable policy training when rewards change.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250786/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

LaRes takes a textual task description, raw environment variables (state vectors for MetaWorld/ManiSkill3/MuJoCo or RGB frames for MinAtar), and an optional human-designed reward function in Python code as input to the LLM. It produces a trained policy network and an evolved population of reward functions (Python code). Unusually, the shared replay buffer stores each experience with rewards from all n reward functions simultaneously in the format {s, a, s', r, r₁, …, rₙ, done, info}, enabling batch relabeling when the reward population is updated without re-collecting data.

## Method

LaRes decomposes task solving into a high-level LLM-driven reward function evolution loop and a low-level off-policy RL (SAC or DQN) policy learning loop, coordinated through continual learning. The LLM generates and iteratively refines a population of n=5 reward functions by optimizing three aspects (components, weights, calculation methods) based on policy performance feedback, while non-elite agents inherit the elite policy and Q-networks to avoid retraining from scratch. A shared replay buffer stores multi-reward experiences that are relabeled upon reward updates, and a Thompson sampling mechanism adaptively allocates interaction steps to superior policies to prevent buffer contamination. To prevent policy collapse when rewards change, LaRes applies reward scaling (aligning new reward mean/variance to the elite's) and an L2 parameter constraint loss that penalizes drift of non-elite policy and value network parameters away from the elite's.

## Results

Under the no-initialization setting on 5 MetaWorld tasks, LaRes achieves 100% success rate on all tasks requiring only 112,800–358,403 environment steps, compared to Eureka's 283,206–924,163 steps and LLM zero-shot's 156,590–762,346 steps. On locomotion tasks, LaRes reaches 4.44 m/s on Ant and 3.42 m/s on Humanoid, surpassing Eureka (1.49 / 2.02 m/s) and ROSKA (2.77 / 2.95 m/s). On MinAtar discrete-control tasks, LaRes improves DQN scores by 60–120% (e.g., Breakout from 13.52 to 21.87 at 0.5M steps, SpaceInvaders from 23.18 to 42.27). Across 12 MetaWorld and 4 ManiSkill3 manipulation tasks with human reward initialization, LaRes consistently outperforms SAC, five ERL baselines, and three reward-design baselines in both sample efficiency and final success rate.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LaRes outperforms all RL baselines (SAC) by a wide margin across all 16 manipulation tasks, demonstrating that LLM-generated reward functions substantially exceed human-designed rewards in guiding policy learning. Against the five ERL baselines (ERL, PDERL, CEM-RL, ERL-Re², EvoRainbow), LaRes achieves superior sample efficiency and final performance on most tasks, particularly the harder ones, because it searches reward space rather than policy parameter space. Among reward-design baselines, LaRes dominates Eureka by achieving 100% success with 2–8× fewer samples (e.g., 112,800 vs. 924,163 steps on Button-Press), outperforms R* through more efficient data reuse and stable policy learning, and surpasses Text2Reward which lacks iterative improvement capability. On locomotion, LaRes (4.44 m/s Ant, 3.42 m/s Humanoid) beats both Eureka (1.49 / 2.02) and ROSKA (2.77 / 2.95) by a large margin. On MinAtar, LaRes integrated with DQN improves scores by 60–120% over vanilla DQN (e.g., SpaceInvaders 42.27 vs. 23.18 at 0.5M steps), confirming generality to discrete action spaces.

## Relation to Prior Work

LaRes extends the LLM-based reward function search line initiated by Eureka and Text2Reward, but departs fundamentally from the broader ERL literature (ERL, PDERL, CEM-RL, ERL-Re², EvoRainbow) by shifting the evolutionary axis from policy parameter space to reward function space. While prior ERL methods co-optimize policy and value function parameters via crossover/mutation, LaRes keeps the evolutionary loop at the reward level and delegates policy optimization to standard off-policy RL (SAC/DQN). The key methodological departure from Eureka and R* is the introduction of coordination mechanisms—shared replay buffer with multi-reward relabeling, Thompson-sampling-based interaction allocation, reward scaling, and parameter constraint loss—that address sample efficiency and training stability, which prior reward-search methods largely overlooked.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LaRes demonstrates that the quality of the reward function is a more productive axis for evolutionary improvement than the policy parameters themselves, and that this can be done with significantly better sample efficiency than prior LLM reward-design methods. The shared replay buffer with reward relabeling and the Thompson sampling mechanism are practical techniques likely to be adopted in other multi-policy or reward-shaping settings. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to tasks without clear success/failure signals (where Thompson sampling's Beta update is less natural), whether explicit diversity mechanisms for the reward population would improve exploration, and whether more sophisticated LLM reasoning (CoT, ToT) could further enhance reward generation quality. The ~20% time overhead and linear scaling with population size remain practical constraints for very large-scale deployments.
