## Problem

Traditional LLM alignment methods require retraining large pretrained models, making rapid adaptation to diverse domains costly and inflexible. While recent approaches like Aligner decouple alignment into a smaller adapter module, they introduce significant first-token latency during inference (requiring a full upstream response before correction) and suffer from out-of-distribution risks when the reference response is absent at test time. This paper asks whether alignment can be formally detached from the base model in a way that preserves low-latency, token-by-token generation.

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard text prompt x (token sequence) and produces an aligned autoregressive text response y, generated token-by-token. A critical constraint is that the frozen Proposal Module (e.g., Llama-3.1-8B, Qwen2.5-14B) and the trainable Residual Aligner (e.g., Llama-3.2-1B, Qwen2.5-3B) must share the same vocabulary, limiting pairing to models within the same family. At each decoding step, n candidate tokens are proposed from the Proposal Module and reduced to a single output token by the Residual Aligner, so the user-visible output is a standard text stream with no first-token delay beyond a small constant overhead.

## Method

The core contribution formalizes LLM alignment as importance sampling: the aligned distribution is P_θ(y|x) = P_M(y|x)·Q_θ(y|x) / Z_θ(x), where P_M is the frozen Proposal Module and Q_θ is a smaller autoregressive Residual Aligner that estimates importance weights. Training uses a sequence-level loss derived from SFT likelihood, with a Lagrange-multiplier term α that balances alignment fidelity against the Proposal Module's distribution, and the Proposal Module is needed only for one-off data synthesis, not during gradient updates. For inference, the paper proves (Proposition 2.1) that the joint model decomposes into token-level conditionals, enabling a Proposing-Aligning-Reducing Sampling algorithm: at each step, n tokens are nucleus-sampled from P_M, reweighted by Q_θ via a sparse softmax over those n candidates, and reduced to one token by categorical sampling. A KL-divergence guard (threshold 0.1) falls back to direct P_M sampling when the Residual Aligner's distribution diverges too far, mitigating degradation for smaller aligners.

## Results

On UltraChat (instruction following), the 1B Residual Aligner paired with the 8B Llama3.1 Proposal Module achieves 8.33% LC win rate (GPT4-Eval), surpassing the warmed-up 8B baseline (7.72%) and matching the full SFT 8B model (8.64%) with less than 1/8 the trainable parameters. On TL;DR Summarization, SFT 8B + R.A.1B reaches 71.80% LC (GPT4-Eval) versus 70.09% for SFT 8B alone. In preference optimization on Anthropic-HH, DPO 8B + R.A.1B achieves 72.22% LC (GPT4-Eval) on Helpfulness and 79.89% on Harmlessness, improving over DPO 8B by ~4% and ~7% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Aligner [18], RAM consistently and substantially outperforms: on UltraChat, the Aligner degrades the warmed-up 8B model from 7.72% to 2.49% LC (GPT4-Eval), while RAM improves it to 8.33%; on Anthropic-HH Helpfulness, DPO 8B + Aligner drops from 69.91% to 52.07% LC, whereas DPO 8B + RAM raises it to 71.01%. The paper attributes the Aligner's failure to its reliance on a reference response y′ that is absent at inference, causing out-of-distribution issues. Against full-parameter SFT/DPO baselines, RAM with a 1B or 3B Residual Aligner matches or exceeds the full 8B/14B fine-tuned model (e.g., SFT 8B + R.A.1B: 71.80% vs SFT 8B: 70.09% on TL;DR), using less than 1/8 the trainable parameters. Against Controlled Decoding [25], RAM achieves higher LC scores on TL;DR (65.11% vs 61.89%) and Helpfulness (65.11% vs 58.08%), though CD produces longer outputs yielding slightly higher raw WR; RAM maintains output lengths closer to the base model (e.g., 247 vs 310 tokens on Helpfulness), avoiding the verbosity and potential model collapse associated with energy-based reweighting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself as a principled alternative to the Aligner (Ji et al., 2024) and Residual EBM (Deng et al., 2020) family of methods that decouple alignment from the base LLM. While Aligner models P(y|y′,x) conditioned on a reference response—introducing first-token latency and OOD risk—RAM reframes alignment as importance sampling where the base model is the proposal distribution and a smaller autoregressive Residual Aligner estimates importance weights, directly modeling P(y|x). The structural form (product of two autoregressive models normalized by a partition function) echoes Residual EBMs, but the autoregressive nature of both components enables a token-level Proposing-Aligning-Reducing Sampling decoder that avoids the sequence-level delay inherent in energy-based or reference-conditioned approaches. The self-normalized importance sampling step draws on Grover et al. (2019) for variance reduction during inference.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in the efficiency gains: a 1B Residual Aligner paired with an 8B Proposal Module matches or exceeds full-parameter SFT of the 8B model while requiring only 1/8 the trainable parameters, and the training pipeline achieves 4× (SFT) and 13.33× (DPO) computational savings. Multiple alignment modules can share a single frozen Proposal Module, enabling cross-domain deployment without retraining the large model. However, the method requires the Proposal Module and Residual Aligner to share the same vocabulary, limiting applicability to model families with consistent tokenizers (e.g., LLaMA 3, Qwen 2.5) and ruling out closed-source or cross-family combinations. Bridging different vocabularies at the token or sub-word level remains an open problem the authors flag for future work.
