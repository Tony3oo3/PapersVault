## Problem

The practical performance of generative diffusion models is highly sensitive to the choice of noise schedule (equivalently, time parameterization), yet no principled, data-dependent reparameterization exists for standard continuous diffusion models. Uniform time spacing undersamples critical generation windows while redundantly over-sampling trivial ones, leading to severe inefficiency especially in the few-step regime. This paper asks whether a natural information-theoretic quantity can define a canonical time axis that equalizes the information contribution of each sampling step.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a trained score/denoising network, its noise schedule parameters σ(t) and s(t), and a dataset to estimate the per-timestep squared error ε²ₜ via Monte Carlo (1024 samples at 128 time points). It outputs a monotone time mapping φ(t) (entropic or rescaled entropic) that is then used to uniformly space NFE sampling steps during inference with standard solvers (DDIM, Heun). The spectral variant additionally requires an FFT decomposition of the squared error across radial frequencies. No retraining is needed; the schedule is computed once per model and dataset.

## Method

The paper proposes using the conditional entropy H[x₀|xₜ] as a time reparameterization, which yields a constant entropy rate so each time point contributes equal information to the final generation. A key tractable result connects the entropy rate to the standard training loss via the I-MMSE identity: Ḣ[x₀|xₜ] = (σ̇(t)/σ³(t))·ε²ₜ, enabling estimation from the denoising error without access to the true score. The authors prove this entropic time is invariant under the initial SDE parameterization (Theorem 5.2), making it a canonical choice. They further introduce a rescaled entropic time ∫₀ᵗ σ(τ)Ḣ[x₀|x_τ]dτ, which exactly recovers the optimal arctan sampling schedule for Gaussian data (Sabour et al., 2024), and a spectral variant that normalizes each Fourier frequency's contribution to unity. In practice, the squared error is estimated at 128 EDM-schedule time points, cumulatively integrated to form the time mapping, and sampling steps are then placed at uniform intervals in the new time before being mapped back to the original σ/s schedule.

## Results

On ImageNet-512 with the EDM2-XXL model and stochastic DDIM, the rescaled entropic schedule reduces FID from 30.39 to 13.38 at NFE=16, from 8.80 to 3.83 at NFE=32, and from 3.81 to 2.60 at NFE=64, with analogous improvements in FD-DINOv2 (218.10→108.16 at NFE=16). For deterministic DDIM, FID drops from 9.68 to 5.91 at NFE=16 and from 3.47 to 2.78 at NFE=32. The improvements are most pronounced in the low-NFE regime (16 steps), where FID gains exceed 15 points, while at 64 steps the gap narrows to ~1.2 FID points. The paper also reports that EDM2-XS with the rescaled entropic schedule achieves an FD-DINOv2 of 97.10, beating the 103.39 reported by Karras et al. (2024) using the Heun second-order solver.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is the standard EDM sampling schedule (Karras et al., 2022), which uses a fixed ρ=7 noise schedule. The rescaled entropic schedule consistently outperforms the EDM baseline across all datasets (CIFAR-10, FFHQ, ImageNet-64, ImageNet-512), both solvers (stochastic and deterministic DDIM), and both metrics (FID and FD-DINOv2). The advantage is largest at low NFE: on ImageNet-512 with stochastic DDIM and EDM2-XXL, FID improves by 17.01 points at NFE=16 (30.39→13.38) and by 4.97 points at NFE=32 (8.80→3.83), while at NFE=64 the gain is 1.21 points (3.81→2.60). For deterministic DDIM, the FID improvement at NFE=16 is 3.77 points (9.68→5.91) for EDM2-XXL. The non-rescaled entropic time produced unrecognizable images and was excluded from comparisons, while the spectral rescaled entropy variant yields only marginal additional gains (e.g., 7.33 vs. 7.60 FID on FFHQ at NFE=64 with stochastic DDIM). The authors note that second-order solvers (Heun) do not benefit from the entropic schedule, suggesting the information-transfer definition needs adjustment for multi-step look-ahead methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the time-warping idea from Dieleman et al. (2022), who used cross-entropy to define a data-dependent time schedule for discrete token diffusion, to continuous score-matching diffusion models by using the conditional entropy H[x₀|xₜ] as the time variable. It builds directly on Sabour et al. (2024), whose optimal arctan sampling schedule for Gaussian data under EDM is shown here to be exactly the rescaled entropic time, unifying an empirical optimality result with an information-theoretic principle. The paper also connects the conditional entropy production to the I-MMSE identity (Guo et al., 2005) and the continuous-time variational lower bound (Kingma et al., 2021), revealing that the entropic time is invariant under any initial time reparameterization of the SDE—a property not present in prior schedule-optimization work (Wang et al. 2024, Lee et al. 2024, Li et al. 2023) that typically operates in a fixed time coordinate.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The entropic and rescaled entropic times offer a training-free, data-dependent reparameterization that substantially improves few-step generation (e.g., FID 32.31→13.64 for EDM2-XS at NFE=16) without additional function evaluations, addressing a key practical bottleneck in diffusion model deployment. The invariance theorem means the schedule is canonical and independent of the original noise schedule, simplifying the design space. However, the benefit is limited to first-order solvers (second-order solvers like Heun perform worse), and all reported metrics are from single runs without error bars, leaving the robustness of the gains across seeds and architectures somewhat unverified. Open directions include extending the framework to discrete diffusion, adapting the information-transfer definition for higher-order solvers, and using entropic time as a principled training or distillation schedule.
