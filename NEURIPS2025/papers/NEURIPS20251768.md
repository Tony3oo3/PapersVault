## Problem

Human-centered images frequently suffer from human motion blur (HMB) co-occurring with generic noise, yet existing degradation pipelines for human body restoration omit HMB entirely, and one-step diffusion methods rely on fixed negative prompts that limit classifier-free guidance effectiveness. This paper addresses both gaps by integrating HMB simulation into the training pipeline and designing adaptive, image-specific dual prompts for CFG.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251768/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts a 512×512 degraded RGB human image and produces a restored high-quality RGB image in a single diffusion step (~0.20 s on an A6000). Internally, the DPG module consumes the LQ image and outputs three predictions (HQ estimate, residual noise, HMB mask) that are compressed via a Performer encoder and attention pooling into prompt embeddings of the size expected by the Stable Diffusion UNet. The two negative-branch embeddings are concatenated with the positive-branch embedding along the batch dimension so the UNet computes both z_pos and z_neg in one parallel forward pass.

## Method

HAODiff is a two-stage system. Stage 1 trains a triple-branch Swin Transformer (DPG) that shares a downsampler and feature-extraction backbone but splits into three reconstruction heads predicting the HQ image, the residual noise (LQ − HQ), and an HMB segmentation mask, optimized with L1 and Dice losses. Stage 2 fine-tunes a frozen SD2.1-base UNet via LoRA (rank 16) as a one-step diffusion model: the DPG outputs are mapped to prompt embeddings, the UNet produces z_pos and z_neg in parallel, and classifier-free guidance (λ_cfg = 3.5) combines them as z_ε = z_neg + λ_cfg(z_pos − z_neg) to yield the denoised latent in a single DDIM step. Training uses MSE, edge-aware DISTS, and a GAN loss where a pretrained SDXL UNet downsampling module serves as the discriminator. The degradation pipeline simulates HMB by segmenting body parts with Sapiens, morphing the selected mask into a spatial weight map, convolving the HQ image with a random-trajectory PSF, and blending the result with the original before applying standard Real-ESRGAN second-order degradations.

## Results

On the synthetic PERSONA-Val benchmark, HAODiff achieves the best full-reference scores across all metrics: DISTS 0.1023, LPIPS 0.2046, TOPIQ 0.5161, and FID 8.36, substantially outperforming the closest one-step competitor OSDHuman (DISTS 0.1356, FID 14.41). On the real-world MPII-Test benchmark rich in human motion blur, HAODiff leads every no-reference metric (C-IQA 0.6923, NIQE 3.9450, IL-NIQE 23.67) and achieves the lowest HMB-R of 0.0929, roughly half the next-best OSEDiff (0.1853). Inference time is 0.20 s per 512×512 image on an RTX A6000, slightly slower than OSEDiff (0.13 s) and OSDHuman (0.11 s) due to the dual CFG forward pass, yet far faster than multi-step methods (2.88–26.67 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

HAODiff outperforms all ten baselines on every full-reference metric (DISTS, LPIPS, TOPIQ, FID) on PERSONA-Val, with the largest margins over multi-step methods: FID drops from 13.84 (SUPIR) and 12.82 (SeeSR) to 8.36, and DISTS improves from 0.1415/0.1295 to 0.1023. Among one-step diffusion baselines, the closest competitor is OSDHuman (DISTS 0.1356, FID 14.41), which HAODiff surpasses by 25% on DISTS and 42% on FID; OSEDiff (DISTS 0.1340, FID 14.75) is also clearly beaten, though it runs ~35% faster (0.13 s vs 0.20 s) due to a single UNet pass. On the HMB-rich MPII-Test, HAODiff achieves the lowest HMB-R (0.0929), roughly half that of OSEDiff (0.1853) and OSDHuman (0.3433), confirming its advantage in motion-blur-specific restoration. In the prompt-method ablation (Table 3), DPG outperforms the fixed text-pair CFG of S3Diff [63] (FID 8.36 vs 8.52, DISTS 0.1023 vs 0.1056) and the DAPE extractor from OSEDiff (FID 8.36 vs 8.61), with the largest gap on MS-SWD color-shift metric (0.2829 vs 0.3815/0.4296). The only trade-off is inference speed: the dual CFG forward pass adds ~0.07–0.09 s over OSEDiff and OSDHuman, a modest penalty relative to the quality gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

HAODiff extends the one-step diffusion restoration paradigm established by OSEDiff and OSDHuman by replacing their fixed or single-branch prompt strategies with a triple-branch dual-prompt guidance (DPG) built on SwinIR's residual Swin Transformer blocks. The degradation pipeline inherits Real-ESRGAN's two-stage structure and DeblurGAN's Markov-process motion blur simulation, but introduces human body-part segmentation (via Sapiens) to synthesize spatially localized HMB as a first-order degradation. The classifier-free guidance mechanism follows the standard LDM/SD framework, but the key departure is making the negative prompt adaptive—derived from predicted residual noise and HMB masks—rather than a fixed text string, as in S3Diff or OSEDiff's DAPE.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The MPII-Test benchmark (5,427 real-world images with diverse HMB) fills a notable evaluation gap, as prior HBR benchmarks lacked systematic motion blur coverage. The finding that an adaptive negative prompt capturing residual noise and spatial HMB location outperforms fixed prompts on both fidelity and color stability (MS-SWD) suggests a broader principle for CFG-based restoration: the negative signal should encode what to avoid, not just a generic 'bad image' descriptor. Open questions include whether the doubled UNet forward pass for CFG can be made more efficient, and whether the approach generalizes to other local degradations beyond HMB (e.g., occlusion, partial blur). The authors acknowledge that extreme cases combining heavy global degradation with fast limb motion remain unsolved.
