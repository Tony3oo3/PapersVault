## Problem

State-of-the-art generalizable human rendering methods (e.g., GHG, LIFe-GoM) require accurate camera and human poses as input during test-time reconstruction to locate correspondences and gather aligned features. The paper demonstrates that rendering quality degrades sharply when these input poses are noisy or estimated, making such methods impractical for in-the-wild use where ground-truth poses are unavailable. NoPo-Avatar eliminates this dependence entirely, reconstructing avatars from images alone.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251527/cropped_page1_idx4.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes N sparse RGB images (e.g., 3 views at 1024×1024 or a single image at 896×640) and corresponding binary subject masks as the sole reconstruction inputs—no camera poses or human poses are consumed. It outputs a canonical T-pose 3D Gaussian avatar composed of two sets: template Gaussians in SMPL-X UV space and pixel-aligned image Gaussians, one per foreground pixel. At rendering time, only target camera extrinsics/intrinsics and target SMPL-X shape/pose parameters are needed to articulate and splat-render novel views or poses.

## Method

NoPo-Avatar uses a dual-branch encoder-decoder architecture inspired by DUSt3R/NoPoSplat. The template branch starts from a learnable 16×16×1024 embedding of the average SMPL-X T-pose and predicts residual Gaussians in UV space, providing structural priors and inpainting unseen regions. N image branches (weight-shared ViT encoders) predict pixel-aligned Gaussians (splatter images) directly in canonical T-pose space, capturing fine-grained observed details. Cross-attention in the decoder exchanges information between all branches, implicitly aligning features without explicit pose conditioning. Training is end-to-end with photometric losses (MSE + LPIPS), a chamfer distance between template and image Gaussians, a projection loss enforcing pixel-Gaussian alignment, and an LBS weight regularization loss. Articulation to novel poses is a two-step process: bone-level reshaping by shape β, followed by standard linear blend skinning by pose θ, using predicted per-Gaussian LBS weights.

## Results

On THuman2.0 with three sparse input views and predicted poses (the realistic setting), NoPo-Avatar achieves PSNR 22.49, LPIPS* 105.45, and FID 42.19 without any test-time optimization, outperforming LIFe-GoM (PSNR 19.70, LPIPS* 146.19, FID 63.34) and GHG (PSNR 16.96, LPIPS* 185.67, FID 71.46) by large margins. In the lab setting with ground-truth poses, NoPo-Avatar matches or exceeds baselines on LPIPS* and FID while being slightly lower on PSNR (22.49 vs. LIFe-GoM's 24.65). For single-image reconstruction on HuGe100K, NoPo-Avatar achieves PSNR 23.15, LPIPS* 90.63, and FID 15.56, surpassing IDOL (20.89/111.68/16.91) and LHM-1B (17.48/128.85/24.72) in all metrics, with a reconstruction time of 321.58 ms.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In the realistic setting (predicted poses, Table 1), NoPo-Avatar decisively outperforms both GHG and LIFe-GoM on all three metrics, reducing LPIPS* by over 40 points and FID by over 20 points relative to LIFe-GoM, because it is immune to pose estimation errors. In the lab setting with ground-truth poses (Table 2), NoPo-Avatar beats all per-scene baselines (GoMAvatar, 3DGS-Avatar, iHuman) and generalizable baselines (NHP, NIA, GHG) on LPIPS* and FID, though its PSNR (22.49) is slightly below LIFe-GoM (24.65) and NHP (23.32) due to the lack of explicit pose alignment. For single-image reconstruction on HuGe100K (Table 3), NoPo-Avatar surpasses IDOL by 2.26 dB in PSNR and 21.05 in LPIPS*, and outperforms LHM-1B by 5.67 dB in PSNR while being 23× faster (321 ms vs. 7.41 s). On THuman2.0 single-image (Table 5), NoPo-Avatar achieves PSNR 24.64 / LPIPS* 49.69 / FID 34.82, beating IDOL (23.47/66.62/83.37) and LHM-1B (22.03/70.31/63.34) across all metrics. The dual-branch design is validated by the larger gains on all-observed views (LPIPS* −38.72 over IDOL) versus unobserved views (−19.37), confirming the image branch's role in capturing fine details.

## Relation to Prior Work

NoPo-Avatar adapts the pose-free encoder-decoder architecture of NoPoSplat (Ye et al., ICLR 2025) from general scene reconstruction to the human avatar domain, where the reconstruction must be in a canonical T-pose to enable animation. It departs from the dominant paradigm in generalizable human rendering (GHG, LIFe-GoM, NHP, NIA) that requires accurate camera and human poses as test-time inputs for reconstruction, and from the recent pose-free single-image methods (IDOL, LHM) by supporting multiple input views and introducing a dual-branch design that combines a template branch (for inpainting unseen regions) with pixel-aligned image branches (for capturing observed fine details). The cross-attention decoder for implicit alignment between branches, inspired by DUSt3R, is the key mechanism enabling pose independence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that eliminating pose dependence entirely is not merely a robustness improvement but can yield better perceptual quality (LPIPS, FID) than methods using ground-truth poses, while being significantly more robust in realistic settings where poses must be estimated. This makes animatable avatar reconstruction practical for real-world applications where pose estimation is noisy or unavailable. Limitations include the inability to model facial expressions, degraded hand geometry, sensitivity to multiview-inconsistent training data (HuGe100K), and limited ethnic diversity when trained on THuman-only data. Open questions include whether the dual-branch design can be extended to expression retargeting and whether the method generalizes to diverse populations with larger, more diverse training data.
