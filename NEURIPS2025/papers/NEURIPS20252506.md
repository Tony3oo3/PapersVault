## Problem

Reward models (RMs) trained on limited human preference data generalize poorly to out-of-distribution inputs and are vulnerable to reward hacking, while strong LLM judges with chain-of-thought reasoning achieve far higher accuracy but incur prohibitive inference latency for online RLHF. The paper asks how to efficiently route only the uncertain, OOD samples to an expensive LLM judge while letting a fast RM handle the confident, in-distribution majority.

## Input / Output

The method takes a prompt and two candidate responses, concatenated into a single sequence via a chat template with distinct assistant_1/assistant_2 roles, and produces a pairwise preference logit (reward difference) along with an epistemic uncertainty score. When the uncertainty exceeds a threshold, the pair is forwarded to a remote DeepSeek-R1 judge that returns a three-way verdict (response i better, response j better, or tied), which is mapped to a reward difference via the inverse sigmoid. The final routed preference scores are assembled into a K×K anti-symmetric matrix per prompt group to compute RLOO advantage estimates for policy gradient updates.

## Method

The core contribution is an SNGP-augmented pairwise preference model (SNGP-PM) built on Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, where spectral normalization is applied to the last decoder's linear layer and a Gaussian process layer (approximated by 4096 random cosine features) is placed on the final hidden states to produce both a preference logit g(h) and an epistemic uncertainty u = √(1 + λ·φ(h)ᵀΣφ(h)). The key design choice is using a pairwise classification formulation rather than a pointwise BT regression, which makes uncertainty quantification well-posed and allows SNGP to separate irreducible aleatoric uncertainty from reducible epistemic uncertainty; only the latter drives routing. At inference, pairs with u above a threshold are sent to DeepSeek-R1 (whose verdict is converted to a near-extreme or zero reward difference), while confident pairs use the PM's own prediction p = g(h)/u. These routed reward differences feed directly into RLOO's leave-one-out advantage estimator, enabling online RLHF with only 6–18% of comparisons requiring the expensive judge call, yet outperforming random routing at the same call budget on both RewardBench/RM-Bench accuracy and downstream alignment benchmarks.

## Results

On RM-Bench, uncertainty-based routing with 44.1% judge calls (5,270 pairs) improves average accuracy from 68.0% (no routing) to 81.9%, a +4.8 pp gain over random routing at the same budget (77.1%). The improvement is most dramatic on the hard subset, where accuracy jumps from 44.9% (below random) to 65.3%, approaching DeepSeek-R1's 78.9% with only 44% of the inference cost. On RewardBench, the same strategy lifts average accuracy from 87.3% to 91.6% (+2.5 pp over random). For downstream alignment via RLOO, uncertainty routing at 18.3% judge calls achieves 29.8% Arena-Hard win rate and 26.45% AlpacaEval LC-WR, outperforming both no-routing (28.1%, 25.40%) and random routing at the same budget (28.5%, 25.98%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three routing strategies: no routing (PM only), uncertainty-based routing, and random routing (same number of judge calls but selected uniformly at random), with DeepSeek-R1 at 100% calls as the upper bound. Uncertainty-based routing consistently outperforms random routing at every budget level on both RewardBench and RM-Bench; at the highest budget (44.1% calls on RM-Bench), it achieves 81.9% average accuracy versus 77.1% for random routing, a 4.8 pp gap that widens on the hard subset (65.3% vs. 59.1%). The method recovers most of the gap to full DeepSeek-R1 (88.1%) with less than half the inference cost, and on the hard subset closes the gap from 34 pp (no routing) to just 13.6 pp. For downstream alignment, uncertainty routing at 18.3% calls yields 29.8% Arena-Hard WR and 26.45% AlpacaEval LC-WR, beating random routing at the same budget (28.5%, 25.98%) and no-routing (28.1%, 25.40%). The wall-clock analysis confirms that uncertain instances are genuinely harder (longer judge inference time), yet uncertainty routing still achieves higher accuracy per unit time than random routing.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts SNGP (spectral-normalized Gaussian process) uncertainty quantification—originally developed for image classification OOD detection—to LLM-based pairwise preference models, and connects the routing problem to active learning where the LLM judge serves as an oracle queried by an uncertainty-based acquisition function. It departs from the standard RLHF pipeline that relies solely on pointwise RMs by arguing that pointwise RM uncertainty is ill-posed under the Bradley-Terry model (due to prompt-dependent bias invariance), whereas pairwise preference models yield a well-defined classification problem amenable to principled UQ. The advantage estimation framework is specifically designed around RLOO/GRPO's leave-one-out structure, which only requires pairwise reward differences rather than absolute scores, making the pairwise formulation a natural fit.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in online RLHF: strong LLM judges (e.g., DeepSeek-R1) are too slow and expensive to call on every response, yet standard RMs fail on OOD data. The uncertainty-routing framework offers a clean, low-overhead solution that achieves meaningful accuracy gains with only 6–18% judge calls in downstream alignment. Key limitations include reliance on a single, non-fine-tuned judge model, a small training set (~7k preference pairs), and evaluation on one base model (Llama-3.1-8B). Open questions include hierarchical routing with multiple judge tiers, replacing the heavy judge with smaller generative reward models, closing the active-learning loop by feeding routed samples back into RM training, and iso-flops analysis to account for SNGP's own computational overhead.
