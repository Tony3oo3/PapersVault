## Problem

Medical image segmentation requires simultaneously preserving smooth spatial continuity and accurately delineating high-frequency boundaries, yet pixel-wise decoders introduce discretization artifacts at varying resolutions, and existing implicit neural representations struggle with fine-grained directional structures due to random sampling strategies. No prior method formulates SAM's segmentation output as a continuous, contour-aware probability field that is both resolution-agnostic and boundary-faithful.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255287/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a medical image (RGB endoscopic at 384×384 or CT axial slice at 512×512) and a bounding-box prompt, and produces a continuous mask probability field M(p) defined over the normalized coordinate domain Ω=[-1,1]². This field can be sampled at any target resolution (e.g., 128×128, 384×384, 896×896) without architectural modification or fine-tuning, yielding a discrete class-probability mask at the desired grid. The bounding-box prompt is encoded by SAM's frozen prompt encoder and guides mask decoding through prompt-conditioned activations.

## Method

GauSAM parameterizes SAM's segmentation probability field as a weighted superposition of N learnable 2D anisotropic Gaussian primitives, each with center, covariance, and opacity. The encoder is a frozen SAM-ViT-B backbone augmented with LoRA adapters (rank 4) and a Contour Propagation Unit that recursively injects NSCT-derived multiscale, multidirectional contour energy maps into every transformer block via adaptive reweighting and soft fusion. On the decoder side, each Gaussian primitive selects its covariance and opacity from a learned library of 500 templates via Gumbel-Softmax sampling, constraining parameters to a compact subspace for stable training. A Contour-Guided Position Drift module then predicts bounded spatial offsets (via tanh-activated MLP) for each primitive center from contour features, aligning kernels to semantic boundaries. The entire pipeline is trained end-to-end with a DiceCE loss, requiring only 1.2M trainable parameters.

## Results

On the Kvasir-Sessile binary polyp segmentation task, GauSAM achieves a Dice of 94.76±0.32% with only 1.2M trainable parameters, outperforming the previous state-of-the-art I-MedSAM (91.49±0.52%, 1.6M params) by over 3 points. On the BCV multi-organ segmentation task, GauSAM reaches 89.75±0.47% Dice with 2.9M parameters, matching I-MedSAM (89.91±0.68%) while using fewer parameters. In cross-domain transfer (Kvasir→CVC and BCV→AMOS) and cross-resolution inference (384→128 and 384→896), GauSAM consistently achieves the highest Dice scores, reaching 91.79%, 90.62%, 94.35%, and 94.29% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GauSAM outperforms all eight baselines on the binary polyp task, with the most notable margin being +3.27 Dice over I-MedSAM (94.76% vs. 91.49%) and +9.71 over SwIPE (85.05%), while using fewer parameters than every competitor. On the multi-organ BCV task, GauSAM (89.75%) is essentially tied with I-MedSAM (89.91%) but uses 0.6M fewer trainable parameters; it clearly surpasses all discrete baselines (best: MedSAM at 85.85%) and other continuous methods (best: SwIPE at 81.21%). In cross-domain transfer, GauSAM wins decisively: 91.79% on Kvasir→CVC vs. I-MedSAM's 88.83%, and 90.62% on BCV→AMOS vs. 86.28%. In cross-resolution inference (384→128 and 384→896), GauSAM leads I-MedSAM by ~2.9 and ~3.0 Dice points respectively, while discrete baselines like nnUNet drop to 73.97% and 83.56%. The one setting where GauSAM does not lead is Hausdorff Distance on the BCV in-domain task (11.21 vs. I-MedSAM's 5.95), which the authors attribute to using a single shared Gaussian field across all 13 organ classes, limiting precision on small structures.

## Relation to Prior Work

GauSAM sits at the intersection of three research threads: SAM-based medical segmentation (MedSAM, I-MedSAM), implicit neural representations for resolution-agnostic prediction (IOSNet, SwIPE, I-MedSAM), and 2D Gaussian Splatting for continuous scene representation (Huang et al., 2024). The key departure is treating SAM's segmentation probability field as a continuous Gaussian field parameterized by learnable 2D anisotropic primitives, rather than as a per-pixel decoder or a neural implicit field. Prior work has used Gaussian Splatting only as an auxiliary prior or post-processing step; here it is the primary decoding mechanism, trained end-to-end with task supervision. The NSCT-based contour priors and Contour Propagation Unit further distinguish the encoder from standard SAM fine-tuning approaches that rely solely on LoRA or prompt tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The resolution-agnostic property is practically significant for medical imaging, where CT and MRI produce high-resolution volumes but clinical workflows often require inference at varying scales; GauSAM's ability to produce consistent masks at 128×128 or 896×896 from a 384×384 training resolution (with <0.1% Dice drop) addresses a real deployment bottleneck. However, the NSCT module accounts for ~58% of inference latency (109 ms of 188 ms) for only ~1% Dice gain over simpler FFT-based alternatives, raising an open question about whether lighter contour extraction can match the boundary fidelity. The single-field design for multi-class segmentation (89.75% vs. I-MedSAM's 89.91% on BCV) suggests that per-class Gaussian fields or a 3D extension may be necessary to close the gap on small-organ delineation, a direction the authors outline but do not pursue.
