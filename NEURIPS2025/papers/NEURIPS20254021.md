## Problem

Neural networks assign overconfident predictions to out-of-distribution (OOD) inputs due to the absence of explicit supervision for unknowns during training. While outlier exposure mitigates this, existing multimodal outlier synthesis methods (e.g., NP-Mix) rely on nearest-neighbor feature expansion that is computationally prohibitive for segmentation tasks, and no dedicated multimodal OOD segmentation dataset with genuinely unseen objects exists.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254021/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For multimodal OOD segmentation, the method consumes a paired LiDAR point cloud (M points with x,y,z,intensity) and a 3×H×W RGB image, producing point-level semantic labels over C known classes plus an OOD flag per point via a thresholded confidence score S(p)≥η. For multimodal OOD detection, the input is a paired video clip and optical flow, yielding a sample-level class prediction and OOD score. The OOD module is pluggable (MaxLogit, MSP, Energy, Entropy, GEN), and the framework supports tri-modal (video+flow+audio) and unimodal variants by splitting a single feature vector into two halves for swapping.

## Method

Feature Mixing generates multimodal outliers by randomly selecting N feature dimensions from each of two modality-specific feature vectors and swapping them, then concatenating the perturbed features into a single outlier representation. Two theorems guarantee that the synthesized outliers lie in low-likelihood regions of the ID feature distribution (via mean shift and covariance mismatch inflating the Mahalanobis distance) while remaining geometrically bounded (‖F_o−F‖₂ ≤ √(2N)·δ), ensuring they are anomalous yet semantically coherent. During training, the framework applies entropy maximization on the outlier predictions to push their confidence toward uniform, separating ID and OOD score distributions. The overall architecture is a dual-stream CNN (ResNet-34 camera + SalsaNext LiDAR, or SlowFast video + slow-pathway flow) with late fusion, trained with focal loss + Lovász-softmax loss + γ₁·entropy loss for segmentation, or cross-entropy + γ₁·entropy loss for detection. The method is modality-agnostic, requires only a random.sample call per forward pass, and achieves a 10×–370× speedup over NP-Mix while matching or exceeding its OOD performance.

## Results

On multimodal OOD segmentation, A2D+Feature Mixing achieves FPR@95 of 31.76% and AUROC of 92.83% on SemanticKITTI, 32.92% and 87.55% on nuScenes, and 25.95% and 93.37% on CARLA-OOD, while maintaining competitive mIoUc (60.41–76.47%). On multimodal OOD detection (MultiOOD benchmark, HMDB51 as ID), Feature Mixing achieves the best average FPR@95 of 20.01% and AUROC of 94.17% with 87.00% ID accuracy. Feature Mixing provides a 10× speedup for OOD detection and a 370× speedup for segmentation compared to NP-Mix, with per-sample synthesis times of 0.058 s (detection) and 0.013 s (segmentation) versus 0.545 s and 4.81 s for NP-Mix.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Feature Mixing consistently outperforms all outlier synthesis baselines. Against NP-Mix [17] (the strongest prior multimodal outlier method), A2D+FM reduces FPR@95 by 16.81 points on SemanticKITTI (31.76 vs. 48.57), 8.77 points on nuScenes (32.92 vs. 41.69), and 15.86 points on CARLA-OOD (25.95 vs. 41.81), while also improving AUROC by 1.9, 2.7, and 4.9 points respectively. Against Mixup [52], the gains are even larger (20.3 points FPR@95 on SemanticKITTI), confirming that simple feature interpolation is insufficient for multimodal OOD. Against xMUDA [26] and A2D [17] without outlier optimization, Feature Mixing reduces FPR@95 by 23.6 and 17.3 points on SemanticKITTI, demonstrating that outlier synthesis is critical for mitigating overconfidence. On the MultiOOD detection benchmark, Feature Mixing achieves the best average FPR@95 (20.01%) and AUROC (94.17%), beating NP-Mix (22.72/93.89), Mixup (24.67/93.96), VOS (29.36/93.25), and NPOS (24.91/93.52), while incurring only 0.058 s per sample versus 0.545 s for NP-Mix (10× speedup) and 152 s for VOS.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the MultiOOD framework (Dong et al., NeurIPS 2024), which introduced the first multimodal OOD detection benchmark and the NP-Mix outlier synthesis method using nearest-neighbor class information, and on the PMF fusion architecture (Zhuang et al., ICCV 2021) for LiDAR-camera segmentation. The key departure is replacing NP-Mix's computationally expensive nearest-neighbor feature expansion with a radically simpler operation—randomly swapping N feature dimensions between two modality feature vectors—while providing theoretical guarantees (low-likelihood placement and bounded deviation) that the prior unimodal methods (Mixup, VOS, NPOS) and the multimodal NP-Mix lack. The paper also introduces the CARLA-OOD dataset to fill a gap in multimodal OOD segmentation benchmarks, where prior work relied on remapping common classes (e.g., vehicles) as OOD, risking data leakage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 10×–370× speedup over NP-Mix makes multimodal outlier exposure practically viable for real-time safety-critical applications like autonomous driving, where the prior method's per-sample cost was prohibitive for segmentation. The modality-agnostic design (validated across image+point cloud, video+optical flow, and even tri-modal and unimodal settings) suggests broad applicability beyond the tested configurations. A key open question is whether the random dimension selection can be made adaptive or learnable to target the most informative feature regions, as the authors themselves note as a limitation. The CARLA-OOD dataset, with its 34 diverse OOD objects across 5 maps and 6 weather conditions, provides a more faithful test of genuine unknowns than the class-remapping approach used in prior work.
