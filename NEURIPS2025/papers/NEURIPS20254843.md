## Problem

Optimizing domain-specific data mixture ratios for LLM training is naturally a bi-level optimization problem, yet existing approaches either use a static reference model (DoReMi) or rely on noisy single-step gradient estimation that becomes unstable in high-variance settings like supervised fine-tuning (DoGE). Additionally, prior DMO research has focused almost exclusively on data-abundant pretraining where uniform mixing is already a valid solution, leaving data-restricted and SFT scenarios—where reweighting yields substantial gains—largely unexplored.

## Input / Output

The method takes M domain-specific training and validation splits (e.g., 7 domains from SlimPajama or 6 task clusters from Natural Instructions) and produces a probability-simplex vector α of M domain weights. The output ratios are not used directly for training; instead, they resample the full corpus to construct a final training set D_{α*} on which the production model is trained. Hyperparameters K (probing steps) and E (free training steps between α updates) control the trade-off between estimation fidelity and computational cost, with K=5 for pretraining and K=10 for SFT due to higher gradient variance.

## Method

TANDEM reformulates the bi-level DMO problem as a single-level penalized objective by treating the inner-level training constraint as a Lagrangian penalty, then solves it with two synchronized twin networks: a proxy model u trained only on the training set and a reference model w trained on both training and validation data. At each mixture-ratio update, w and u are re-synchronized to the same starting point, then each is trained for K probing steps; the per-domain loss gap L_train^m(w_K) − L_train^m(u_K) measures how much each domain benefits from the additional validation data, and domains with larger gaps are up-weighted via projected gradient descent on the simplex. This multi-step aggregation reduces the variance of the hyper-gradient compared to DoGE's single-step gradient inner product, and the dynamic (rather than static) reference model better tracks the current training state than DoReMi. The paper proves O(T^{−1/4}) convergence to a first-order stationary point under a Polyak-Łojasiewicz condition, and shows that uniform mixing is a valid solution only when the generalization gap vanishes, motivating the focus on data-restricted and SFT settings.

## Results

In the data-restricted pretraining scenario (300M SlimPajama, 160M GPT model), TANDEM achieves 28.07 average perplexity, outperforming the best baseline Skill-It (29.24) by 1.17 and the Uniform baseline (31.53) by 3.46. In the data-abundant scenario (6B SlimPajama), TANDEM achieves 25.43 average perplexity, marginally beating Uniform (25.74) and Aioli (25.66), consistent with the theoretical prediction that uniform mixing is a valid solution when generalization gaps are small. For supervised fine-tuning (Qwen2-500M, 99 tasks), TANDEM achieves 83.3% average accuracy and 0.208 test loss, outperforming all baselines including Aioli (82.7%, 0.229) and Skill-It (82.6%, 0.232). Scalability is confirmed with 410M and 1B models, where TANDEM achieves 25.00 and 24.35 average perplexity respectively in the data-restricted regime.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TANDEM consistently outperforms all four baselines (DoReMi, DoGE, Skill-It, Aioli) in the data-restricted pretraining scenario, achieving 28.07 average perplexity vs. Skill-It's 29.24, DoGE's 30.10, Aioli's 30.67, and DoReMi's 36.91 — a 1.17-point margin over the strongest competitor. In the data-abundant scenario, TANDEM (25.43) narrowly beats Aioli (25.66) and Uniform (25.74), while DoReMi (28.32) and DoGE (26.60) perform notably worse, confirming the paper's theoretical argument that uniform mixing is near-optimal when generalization gaps are small. For supervised fine-tuning, TANDEM achieves the best average accuracy (83.3%) and lowest test loss (0.208), beating Aioli (82.7%, 0.229) and Skill-It (82.6%, 0.232), while DoReMi (81.6%) and DoGE (82.4%) lag further behind. The advantage of TANDEM grows with model scale: at 1B parameters in the data-restricted regime, it achieves 24.35 vs. Skill-It's 27.15 and Aioli's 28.07, a gap of 2.8–3.7 points. DoGE is omitted at 1B scale due to its high memory overhead from per-domain gradient storage, a practical limitation TANDEM avoids by using loss-based rather than gradient-based domain efficacy estimation.

## Relation to Prior Work

TANDEM unifies DoReMi (static reference model, worst-domain excess loss) and DoGE (bi-level optimization via per-domain gradient influence) under a single penalized bi-level optimization framework, showing that all three methods share the same structural form: a per-domain loss difference between a proxy and a reference model. The key departure is the introduction of dynamically updated, periodically synchronized twin networks (proxy trained on training data, reference trained on training + validation data) with a penalty-based single-level reformulation, which avoids DoGE's high-variance gradient estimation and DoReMi's static reference. The convergence analysis builds on recent penalty-based bilevel optimization theory (Shen & Chen 2023, Kwon et al. 2023/2024), extending it to the constrained simplex setting with a Polyak-Lojasiewicz condition.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first convergence guarantee (O(T^{-1/4})) for a data mixture optimization algorithm, grounding a previously heuristic area in theory. More practically, it reframes where DMO matters: the formal proof that uniform mixing is valid in data-abundant single-epoch pretraining (Proposition 1) redirects attention to data-restricted and SFT settings, where TANDEM achieves 3.46 perplexity improvement over uniform and consistently outperforms DoReMi, DoGE, Skill-It, and Aioli across model scales up to 3B. Open questions include whether the twin-network memory overhead (two full model copies) limits applicability to frontier-scale models, and whether the PL condition assumption holds for real LLM training landscapes.
