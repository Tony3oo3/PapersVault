## Problem

Calibration-free multiview 3D HPE methods rely on flexible continuous representations that, while expressive, are vulnerable to noisy 2D inputs where errors propagate and accumulate across views. The paper addresses how to inject anatomical/structural priors into continuous regression models to improve noise robustness without sacrificing the fine-grained precision that continuous representations provide.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251823/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view 2D skeleton sequences (V×T×J×2, with J=17 joints) from detectors such as CPN or HRNet, requiring no camera extrinsics. It outputs per-view 3D joint positions (V×T×J×3) regressed via a single FC layer with L1 loss. An intermediate discrete representation of N=63 sub-structure tokens (codebook size 2048×256) is generated from the 2D input and injected into the spatial attention blocks, but the final output remains fully continuous.

## Method

The pipeline has two stages. Stage I pretrains a UniCodebook (VQ-VAE) that jointly encodes 2D and 3D poses into a shared discrete space using compositional sub-structure tokens (N=63 overlapping limb/torso groups), trained with four complementary objectives (2Dto2D, 2Dto3D, 3Dto2D, 3Dto3D) to bridge the cross-modal representation gap. Stage II is a dual-stream spatial-temporal transformer (following MotionBERT) for pose lifting, where the key innovation is the Discrete-Continuous Spatial Attention (DCSA) module: a cross-attention mechanism in which continuous joint features serve as queries and discrete codebook tokens serve as keys/values, softly injecting structural guidance without hard projection into the discrete space. The DCSA output is added to the standard spatial self-attention output, preserving the continuous regression pathway while inheriting noise resilience from the discrete prior.

## Results

On Human3.6M with CPN-detected 2D poses (T=27), the proposed method achieves 26.0 mm average MPJPE, outperforming the best calibration-free baseline FusionFormer (26.6 mm) and all calibrated methods. With ground-truth 2D poses, it reaches 7.94 mm, matching FusionFormer (7.90 mm) and substantially surpassing SVTFormer (11.4 mm) and SGraFormer (11.7 mm). On MPI-INF-3DHP, it achieves 3.37 mm MPJPE and 95.94% AUC, improving over FusionFormer (5.4 mm) by 2.03 mm (37.5%). On FreeMan, it attains 11.15 mm mean MPJPE, the best among all compared methods including FusionFormer (11.30 mm) and MTF-Transformer (12.18 mm).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against calibration-free baselines on Human3.6M (CPN input), the method outperforms FusionFormer by 0.6 mm (26.0 vs. 26.6), SGraFormer by 1.6 mm, FLEX by 5.7 mm, and ProTriangulation by 1.8 mm. On the GT 2D pose setting, it essentially ties FusionFormer (7.94 vs. 7.90 mm) while beating SVTFormer by 3.46 mm and SGraFormer by 3.76 mm. On MPI-INF-3DHP, the method achieves a 37.5% MPJPE reduction over FusionFormer (3.37 vs. 5.4 mm) without requiring any extra training data (H36M), whereas FusionFormer and SGraFormer both use H36M for fine-tuning. On FreeMan, it edges out FusionFormer by 0.15 mm in mean MPJPE and achieves the lowest seen-camera error (9.59 mm), demonstrating superior generalization to unseen viewpoints. Compared to calibrated methods, the method surpasses MTF-Transformer+ (25.8 mm) and Epipolar Transformer (26.9 mm) under the same CPN input, though image-based calibrated methods like AdaFuse (19.5 mm) and MvP (18.6 mm) remain stronger due to their access to raw pixel information rather than 2D pose. The noise-robustness ablation (Figure 3) further shows that the gap over the continuous-only baseline widens with increasing Gaussian noise, confirming the discrete prior's protective effect under degraded 2D inputs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the VQ-VAE quantization framework (Van Den Oord et al., 2017) and the compositional sub-structure token decomposition from Geng et al. (2023), but departs from prior VQ-VAE-in-pose work (PCT, TokenHMR, MEGA, Di²Pose) by avoiding hard projection of outputs into discrete space. Instead, it introduces a soft cross-attention mechanism (DCSA) that lets continuous transformer features selectively attend to discrete codebook tokens, preserving regression fidelity while inheriting structural robustness. The dual-stream spatial-temporal backbone follows MotionBERT (Zhu et al., 2023), and the codebook stabilization tricks (EMA, code reset) are borrowed from TokenHMR (Dwivedi et al., 2024). The key conceptual departure is the UniCodebook, which jointly encodes 2D and 3D poses in a shared discrete space via four complementary training objectives, bridging the modality gap that prior unimodal codebooks left for the backbone to resolve.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical deployment bottleneck: calibration-free multiview 3D HPE methods that learn view interactions purely from data are vulnerable to noisy 2D detections, and errors propagate across views. The discrete-prior-as-soft-guidance paradigm (rather than hard quantization) offers a reusable recipe for injecting structural knowledge into continuous regression pipelines without sacrificing precision. Limitations include the absence of error bars or statistical significance testing, the codebook being trained on a fixed set of motions (raising questions about domain shift), and evaluation limited to three datasets with relatively small subject counts. Open questions include whether the UniCodebook can be adapted online for novel action domains and whether the DCSA mechanism generalizes to other continuous-to-discrete prior injection settings beyond pose.
