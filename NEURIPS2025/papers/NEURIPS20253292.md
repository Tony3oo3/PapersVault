## Problem

Conventional acoustic masking and active noise cancellation are individual-oriented and impractical for shared environments, while existing music generation models struggle to produce output that rhythmically aligns with noisy, unstructured inputs. This misalignment between generated music and ambient noise forces reliance on excessive volume to achieve effective masking, degrading the listening experience. The paper introduces the task of blending environmental noise into personalized music to reduce its perceptual salience for a group of listeners without requiring personal devices.

## Input / Output

The method takes a repeating environmental noise segment (audio waveform) and a user-provided text prompt describing desired music style/genre as input. The noise is converted to a grayscale mel-spectrogram image where high-energy regions are isolated via a binary mask. The output is a personalized music audio signal (reconstructed via Griffin-Lim phase estimation and ISTFT from the generated mel-spectrogram) that is rhythmically aligned with the noise and adaptively amplified to maximize auditory masking while maintaining comfortable overall loudness (normalized to -18 dB LUFS).

## Method

BNMusic is a training-free, two-stage framework built on Riffusion's latent diffusion model (Stable Diffusion-v1.5 fine-tuned for music mel-spectrogram generation). Stage 1 performs a two-step outpainting-inpainting process: first, the high-energy core of the noise mel-spectrogram is preserved while music is generated to fill surrounding regions (outpainting), diffusing the noise's rhythmic cues outward; then the mask is inverted and the core region is inpainted with coherent musical content, reintegrating the diffused information to produce a complete, rhythmically aligned composition. Stage 2 applies adaptive amplification by solving an optimization over a scalar gain λ that maximizes the area where the amplified music exceeds a psychoacoustic masking threshold (derived from a 21 dB signal-to-mask ratio) while penalizing excessive global loudness via a weighted sum term. Because the generated music already shares temporal-spectral structure with the noise, only modest gain is needed, avoiding the volume inflation that plagues conventional masking approaches.

## Results

On the Direct Outputs evaluation, BNMusic achieves the lowest FAD (7.98 on EPIC-SOUNDS, 6.76 on ESC-50) and KL divergence (1.67 on EPIC-SOUNDS, 2.14 on ESC-50) among all methods, outperforming MusicGen (FAD 10.95/7.74, KL 1.85/2.33), Riffusion A2A (FAD 13.15/9.11, KL 2.25/2.70), and Random Music (FAD 15.41/8.32, KL 2.38/2.61). In the subjective evaluation on adaptively amplified samples, BNMusic achieves the highest overall quality score (OVL 3.67±0.55) and perceived noise reduction score (PER 3.84±0.63) on a 5-point Likert scale, substantially outperforming all baselines (OVL 2.93–2.97, PER 2.63–3.24). The ablation confirms that the full two-stage pipeline (outpainting + inpainting + adaptive amplification) is necessary, with the complete system achieving FAD 7.98 and KL 1.67 versus 8.68/1.89 for outpainting alone.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BNMusic outperforms all three baselines on both FAD and KL divergence across both EPIC-SOUNDS and ESC-50 in both loudness-normalized and direct-output settings. MusicGen (melody-conditioned generation) is the closest objective competitor, achieving FAD 10.95 vs. BNMusic's 7.98 on EPIC-SOUNDS direct outputs, but it lags significantly on subjective quality (OVL 2.97 vs. 3.67) and perceived noise reduction (PER 2.68 vs. 3.84), suggesting its melody-aware generation provides only marginal masking without true rhythmic alignment. Riffusion A2A (audio-to-audio) ranks second on PER (3.24) but has the worst FAD among generated methods (13.15 on EPIC-SOUNDS), as its outputs closely mimic the noise structure at the expense of musicality. Random Music from MusicBench performs worst overall (FAD 15.41, KL 2.38 on EPIC-SOUNDS), confirming that unaligned music cannot effectively blend with environmental noise regardless of its intrinsic quality. The key differentiator is that BNMusic's two-stage outpainting-inpainting process produces rhythmically aligned music that requires only modest adaptive amplification to achieve effective masking, whereas the baselines either lack alignment (Random Music, MusicGen) or sacrifice musical coherence for alignment (Riffusion A2A).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes Riffusion (a Stable Diffusion variant fine-tuned for mel-spectrogram music generation) as a frozen backbone, applying a two-step outpainting-then-inpainting procedure on the noise spectrogram to generate rhythmically aligned music without any additional training. Conceptually, it bridges psychoacoustic auditory masking theory (which defines frequency-dependent masking thresholds) with recent diffusion-based audio editing techniques (Audit, MusicMagus, spectrogram inpainting), but redirects these tools toward a perceptual blending objective rather than content reconstruction or style transfer. The adaptive amplification stage introduces a lightweight optimization over a scalar gain factor guided by a 21 dB signal-to-mask ratio threshold, distinguishing it from prior masking-based sound design work that relied on hand-crafted chord progressions or natural sound recordings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper introduces a practical, group-oriented alternative to active noise cancellation for shared environments (subways, elevators, offices), where per-user ANC is infeasible. Key limitations constrain near-term impact: generation is not real-time (~5 s per clip on a 4090), mel-spectrogram round-trip conversion introduces audible distortion, and blending quality degrades when user prompts are poorly matched to the noise. Open questions include whether faster diffusion or consistency-model approaches can enable real-time deployment, how the method generalizes to non-repetitive or broadband noise, and whether the 21 dB SMR threshold is universally appropriate across listener populations and noise types.
