## Problem

In algorithm unrolling for MRI reconstruction, sharing the proximal operator across iterations (as optimization theory dictates) produces artifacts and blurring, while using distinct per-iteration networks improves quality but multiplies trainable parameters by the number of unrolls, causing overfitting in the limited-data regimes typical of translational MRI. The paper asks whether iteration-dependent regularization can be achieved with only a modest parameter increase rather than a full unshared network per unroll.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253140/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes complex-valued undersampled k-space measurements y_Ω ∈ ℂ^M together with a known encoding matrix E_Ω (which incorporates coil sensitivities and the sampling pattern Ω) and produces a complex-valued reconstructed image x ∈ ℂ^N. Experiments use 2D slices from the fastMRI knee (coronal PD, PD-FS) and brain (axial T2) datasets at acceleration factors R = 4, 6, 8 with equispaced undersampling and 24 central k-space lines retained. The unrolled network runs T = 5 or 10 iterations, each involving a conjugate-gradient data-fidelity solve (15 CG iterations) followed by a single forward pass through the time-embedded proximal CNN.

## Method

The core idea is to replace the fixed proximal operator in unrolled optimization with a single CNN whose behavior is modulated per-iteration via FiLM-style time-embedding (sinusoidal encoding of the iteration index t → MLP → per-channel scaling α^t and shifting β^t applied after group normalization at every residual block), theoretically motivated by the iteration-dependent proximal threshold and Onsager correction in Vector AMP. The data-fidelity quadratic penalty μ^t and the Onsager correction scalar ρ^t are also made time-dependent learnable parameters, so the full unrolled step (Algorithm 1) mirrors VAMP's structure with all iteration-dependent quantities learned end-to-end. For ResNet, a novel time-embedding module introduces a scaling factor τ to stably inject time information without disrupting the residual path. The framework is demonstrated to extend to both VSQP and ADMM unrolling, and with only ~14% more parameters than the shared baseline (vs. ~10× for unshared), it matches or exceeds unshared performance in limited-data settings while halving inference cost at T = 5 unrolls.

## Results

On the fastMRI Coronal PD dataset with ResNet proximal operator, the proposed time-embedded unrolling (T=10) achieves 41.43 dB PSNR / 0.965 SSIM at R=4, 39.66 / 0.954 at R=6, and 36.87 / 0.929 at R=8, consistently outperforming both shared and unshared VSQP/ADMM baselines. With U-Net, the method reaches 40.99 / 0.964 at R=4 on Coronal PD. Notably, with only T=5 unrolls the method matches or exceeds the T=10 shared baselines in most cases (e.g., 41.41 PSNR vs. 41.27 for ADMM-♠ at R=4 with ResNet), halving inference time. The unshared baselines (♣) underperform their shared counterparts (♠) in this limited-data regime (300 training slices), confirming the overfitting concern.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed time-embedded unrolling outperforms all four unrolling baselines (shared and unshared VSQP [3, 28] and ADMM [58, 47]) across nearly all dataset/rate/architecture combinations. For example, on Coronal PD at R=4 with ResNet, Ours (41.43 dB) beats ADMM-♠ (41.27) by 0.16 dB and VSQP-♠ (41.11) by 0.32 dB. A key finding is that unshared baselines (♣) actually underperform their shared counterparts (♠) in this 300-slice limited-data regime (e.g., VSQP-♣ = 40.99 vs. VSQP-♠ = 41.11 at R=4), validating the paper's overfitting concern and the efficiency of time-embedding as an alternative to parameter multiplication. Against the diffusion-based DDS method [16], the proposed approach achieves higher PSNR and SSIM (details in Appendix F). The method also extends to time-embedded VSQP and ADMM variants (Table 3), showing consistent gains of ~0.2–0.5 dB PSNR over their shared counterparts without the full VAMP-inspired Onsager correction.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: algorithm unrolling for MRI (MoDL/VSQP, ADMM-Net), Vector Approximate Message Passing (VAMP), and time-embedding in diffusion models. Prior unrolling work faces a binary choice—share the proximal operator across iterations (theoretically consistent but artifact-prone) or use entirely separate networks per iteration (better quality but 10× more parameters and overfitting risk in limited-data settings). The paper's key departure is introducing a single shared network with FiLM-based time-embedding that adapts its behavior per iteration, theoretically grounded in VAMP's iteration-dependent denoising thresholds and Onsager corrections, while also making the data fidelity weight μ time-dependent. This bridges the gap between the two extremes without the parameter explosion of unshared networks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method addresses a practically important gap in translational MRI where training data is limited (hundreds of slices rather than thousands), and provides a principled, VAMP-motivated alternative to the heuristic of unshared regularizers. The finding that unshared networks actually underperform shared ones in this regime, and that a modest time-embedding module (~14% parameter increase) closes the gap, offers a clear recipe for practitioners. Open questions include whether the VAMP theoretical guarantees (second-moment matching, state evolution) hold in the learned setting, whether the approach extends to 3D reconstruction or non-Cartesian sampling, and how the performance gap evolves as training data scales up—where unshared methods may regain their advantage.
