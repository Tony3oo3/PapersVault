## Problem

Existing provable scaling laws for linear regression (Lin et al., 2024) are limited to one-pass SGD, where each sample is used exactly once. This is unsustainable in data-constrained regimes where new data is scarce. The paper asks whether reusing data across multiple passes (multi-pass SGD) can yield strictly improved test error scaling with respect to the number of training samples.

## Input / Output

The method operates on N i.i.d. samples (x_i, y_i) from a Gaussian linear model in a countably infinite-dimensional Hilbert space, with features sketched to M dimensions via a fixed Gaussian random matrix S ∈ R^{M×d}. Training proceeds for L total SGD steps with geometrically decaying stepsizes (γ_t = γ_0 / 2^⌊t/(L/log L)⌋), where at each step a sample is drawn i.i.d. from the empirical dataset. The output is the last iterate v_L and a sharp Θ(·) test error bound as a function of M, N, and L, valid with probability 1 − e^{−Ω(M)} over the sketching matrix.

## Method

The paper derives matching upper and lower bounds on the test error of multi-pass SGD for M-dimensional sketched linear predictors under aligned power-law spectral assumptions (data covariance degree a, prior degree b−a, with a > b > 1). The risk is decomposed into irreducible, approximation, excess (bias + variance), and fluctuation terms; the key technical contribution is bounding the fluctuation error arising from random index sampling in the multi-pass setting, which has no analogue in one-pass analysis. A covariance replacement trick handles the non-commutativity of population and empirical covariances in the bias/variance bounds, while a novel leave-one-out argument controls the accumulated stochastic process error for the fluctuation term. The main result shows multi-pass SGD achieves Θ(M^{1−b} + L^{(1−b)/a}) excess test error for L ≲ N^{a/b}, improving over the one-pass rate Θ(M^{1−b} + N^{(1−b)/a}) when L > N, and matching the minimax optimal rate Θ(N^{(1−b)/b}) in the data-constrained regime N ≪ M^b.

## Results

The main theoretical result (Corollary 3.3) shows that multi-pass SGD with L ≲ N^(a/b) effective steps achieves an excess test error of Θ(M^(1-b) + L^((1-b)/a)), improving over one-pass SGD's Θ(M^(1-b) + N^((1-b)/a)) when L > N. In the data-constrained regime (N ≪ M^b) with optimal L ≈ N^(a/b), multi-pass SGD achieves a rate of Õ(N^((1-b)/b)) versus Õ(N^((1-b)/a)) for one-pass SGD. Numerical simulations with (a,b) = (2, 1.5) confirm the theory: fitted log-log exponents are approximately −0.33 for multi-pass SGD (matching (1−b)/b = −1/3) and −0.25 for one-pass SGD (matching (1−b)/a = −1/4), while both methods achieve the same model-size exponent of 1−b = −0.5 when N ≫ M^b.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's sole baseline is one-pass SGD as analyzed by Lin et al. (2024), which establishes the scaling law Θ(M^(1-b) + N^((1-b)/a)) for the excess test error. Multi-pass SGD strictly outperforms one-pass SGD in the data-constrained regime (N ≪ M^b) when a > b: the exponent on N improves from (1−b)/a to (1−b)/b, which is more negative (faster decay) since b < a. For the concrete case a=2, b=1.5, this means the error decays as N^(−1/3) instead of N^(−1/4), a meaningful improvement. In the data-rich regime (N ≫ M^b), both methods achieve the same model-size-limited rate Θ(M^(1−b)), so multi-pass SGD provides no benefit. The paper also notes that multi-pass SGD matches the minimax optimal rate Õ(N^((1-b)/b)) of Pillaud-Vivien et al. (2018) when a > b > 2 and M ≫ N^(1/b), up to sub-polynomial factors.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the scaling law framework of Lin et al. (2024), which established power-law test error bounds for one-pass SGD in an infinite-dimensional linear regression setting with Gaussian sketching and power-law spectral assumptions, to the multi-pass (data-reuse) regime. It also builds on Pillaud-Vivien et al. (2018), who showed multi-pass SGD can achieve minimax optimality where one-pass is suboptimal, but did not analyze the interplay with model size M or sketching. The empirical motivation comes from Muennighoff et al. (2023), who observed that up to four passes of reused data approximate fresh data in LLM training, and from the Chinchilla scaling law (Hoffmann et al., 2022) which prescribes compute-optimal allocation between model and data size.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first theoretical characterization of how data reuse improves scaling laws in a setting that directly mirrors the one-pass results of Lin et al. (2024), showing that in data-constrained regimes (N ≪ M^b), multi-pass SGD achieves a strictly better rate of Õ(N^((1-b)/b)) versus Õ(N^((1-b)/a)) for one-pass SGD when a > b. This gives a principled explanation for the empirical benefits of multiple training epochs in large-scale model training. Key limitations include the aligned eigenvector assumption, Gaussian design requirement for lower bounds, and restriction to batch size one; open directions include extending to non-linear models, logistic regression, batch-size scaling, and interaction with momentum or adaptive optimizers like Adam.
