## Problem

Existing skeleton-based zero-shot action recognition (SZAR) models are trained solely on seen classes and lack any mechanism to adapt at inference time, making them vulnerable to distribution shifts between seen and unseen actions. Conventional test-time adaptation methods are either gradient-based (computationally expensive and prone to overfitting on small SZAR datasets) or designed for image classification, ignoring the structured spatio-temporal nature of skeleton sequences. This paper introduces the first training-free test-time adaptation framework specifically tailored to SZAR.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a frozen SZAR backbone's encoded skeleton features (N×T×V tensor after averaging over persons) and a single LLM query per action class (GPT-4o, temperature 0) that returns spatial weights over 4 body regions, temporal weights over 3 phases, and a global-local preference γ∈[0,1]. It produces adapted class logits by adding a scaled cache-retrieval score (α_s·s) to the original zero-shot logits, where s is a weighted fusion of 8 descriptor-wise similarity vectors. The non-parametric cache stores up to K=8 structured entries per unseen class, each containing an 8×N descriptor matrix, a one-hot label, and an entropy-based confidence score.

## Method

Skeleton-Cache reframes SZAR inference as a dynamic retrieval process over a non-parametric cache. Each skeleton sequence is decomposed into 8 structured descriptors (1 global, 4 spatial body-part averages, 3 temporal-phase averages) that form the cache key; high-confidence entries (low entropy) are selectively inserted or replace the least-confident entry per class. At query time, cosine-similarity scores between each query descriptor and all cached descriptors of the same type yield descriptor-wise class logits via weighted one-hot label aggregation. A single LLM prompt per class provides class-specific importance weights over the 8 descriptors, which are ℓ₁-normalized and used to perform a weighted fusion of the descriptor-wise predictions. The final adapted logits are the sum of the frozen model's original zero-shot logits and the scaled fused cache score, requiring no gradient updates, no training data access, and only ~3 MB of additional memory.

## Results

On NTU RGB+D 60 (55/5 split), Skeleton-Cache boosts SA-DVAE from 82.37% to 89.41% ZSL Top-1 accuracy (+7.04 pp) and PURLS from 79.22% to 85.46% (+6.24 pp). On the more challenging NTU RGB+D 120 (110/10 split), gains of 5.5–7.2 pp are observed across backbones, with SMIE+SC reaching 72.98% and PURLS+SC reaching 77.60%. Under the random-split protocol, SA-DVAE+SC achieves 89.86% on NTU60, 56.18% on NTU120, and 71.05% on PKU-MMD, surpassing all prior published numbers. GZSL harmonic mean improves by 3.5–6.6 pp across splits, confirming benefits for both seen and unseen classes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Skeleton-Cache is evaluated as a plug-and-play module on seven SZAR backbones (ReViSE, JPoSE, CADA-VAE, SynSE, SMIE, PURLS, SA-DVAE) and consistently outperforms each baseline by 0.6–7.4 pp in ZSL Top-1 accuracy, with the largest gains on the strongest backbones (SA-DVAE +7.04 pp, PURLS +6.24 pp on NTU60 55/5). Against four generic TTA methods tested on the same PURLS backbone, Skeleton-Cache (85.46%) substantially outperforms gradient-based TPT (40.11%) and DiffTPT (42.27%), which collapse due to overfitting on small SZAR datasets, and also beats training-free CALIP (80.02%), AdaNPC (81.77%), and TDA (82.84%) by 2.6–5.4 pp. The advantage over CALIP and TDA is attributed to Skeleton-Cache's structured multi-descriptor retrieval (global + 4 spatial + 3 temporal) versus their single holistic feature representation, which cannot distinguish fine-grained actions like 'salute' vs. 'wave'. On the random-split protocol (Table 2), SA-DVAE+SC leads all methods on all three datasets (NTU60: 89.86%, NTU120: 56.18%, PKU-MMD: 71.05%), confirming that the gains are not an artifact of a particular class split. Throughput is 3.07 samples/ms, faster than CALIP (2.48) and far exceeding TPT (0.70) and DiffTPT (0.59), while total cache memory stays under 3 MB.

## Relation to Prior Work

Skeleton-Cache sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) skeleton-based zero-shot action recognition (SZAR) methods like PURLS, SA-DVAE, SMIE, and SynSE that align skeleton features with textual descriptors; (2) training-free test-time adaptation (TF-TTA) techniques such as TDA and AdaNPC that maintain non-parametric caches or re-weight prototypes at inference; and (3) LLM-as-semantic-prior, where a frozen language model provides structured knowledge without fine-tuning. The paper departs from prior TTA work by decomposing the holistic feature vector into structured global, spatial (body-part), and temporal descriptors tailored to skeleton topology, and by using GPT-4o to generate class-specific importance weights for descriptor fusion—neither of which appears in image-domain TTA methods. It also contrasts with gradient-based TTA (TPT, DiffTPT) by showing that parameter updates actively disrupt the delicate cross-modal alignment in skeleton ZSL.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play, training-free design with <5 MB overhead and real-time throughput makes Skeleton-Cache practically deployable on top of any existing SZAR backbone, and the consistent 4–8 pp gains across four backbones and three datasets demonstrate broad applicability. Open questions include how the method behaves under severe occlusion or pose noise (acknowledged as a limitation), whether LLM-derived weights generalize to truly novel action categories outside the LLM's training vocabulary, and whether the fixed K=8 cache size is optimal for very large class spaces. The finding that gradient-based TTA catastrophically degrades skeleton ZSL performance (dropping to ~20–40%) highlights a fragility in cross-modal alignment that the field has not yet addressed systematically.
