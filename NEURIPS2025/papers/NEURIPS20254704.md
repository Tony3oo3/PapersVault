## Problem

Standard LLM pretraining with AdamW suffers from poor scaling with model size, sensitivity to hyperparameters, and suboptimal generalization even at minimum training loss. Existing spectral control methods (spectral norm regularization, spectral normalization) only constrain the largest singular value, require expensive power-iteration computations, and do not scale efficiently. The paper asks: can we control the full singular value spectrum of weight matrices during training without explicit spectral computation?

## Input / Output

POET consumes standard text tokens from the C4 corpus and produces text tokens in the usual autoregressive pretraining setup. The unusual aspect is that during training, the forward pass computes y = (RW₀P)ᵀx where W₀ is a frozen random matrix and R, P are learned orthogonal matrices; at inference, R and P are merged into a single dense weight matrix so that inference speed and architecture are identical to conventionally trained LLaMA models. The method is applied to LLaMA transformers of 60M–3B parameters trained on 5B–50B tokens.

## Method

POET reparameterizes each weight matrix W ∈ R^{m×n} as RW₀P, where W₀ is a fixed random initialization and R ∈ R^{m×m}, P ∈ R^{n×n} are learnable orthogonal matrices, provably preserving the singular value spectrum while allowing free optimization of singular vectors. Two approximations make this scalable: (1) Stochastic Primitive Optimization (SPO) factorizes each large orthogonal matrix into a product of small b×b primitive orthogonal matrices embedded via random index sets (fully stochastic) or random block permutations (block-stochastic), reducing trainable parameters from O(m²) to O(b²) or O(m·b); (2) Cayley-Neumann Parameterization (CNP) approximates the Cayley transform (I+Q)(I−Q)⁻¹ with a truncated Neumann series to avoid costly matrix inversion. A merge-then-reinitialize trick periodically folds learned primitives into W₀ and resets them to identity, bounding memory to one primitive at a time and preventing Neumann-series divergence. With normalized Gaussian initialization, POET simultaneously preserves small hyperspherical energy and bounded singular values, yielding stable training and improved generalization over AdamW, GaLore, and LoRA at matched or smaller parameter budgets.

## Results

On LLaMA pretraining with C4, POET-FS (b=1/2) achieves the best validation perplexity across all model scales: 25.37 (60M), 19.94 (130M), 15.95 (350M), and 13.70 (1.3B), outperforming AdamW (14.73 at 1.3B) and GaLore (18.33 at 1.3B) with far fewer trainable parameters (e.g., 406M vs. 1.21B for AdamW at 1.3B). POET-BS (b=256) achieves 14.56 perplexity at 1.3B with only 118M trainable parameters (one-tenth of AdamW). On GLUE downstream tasks, POET-pretrained models consistently outperform AdamW-pretrained models across all eight tasks under full finetuning, OFT, and POET finetuning strategies.

## Baselines & Comparisons

POET consistently outperforms AdamW across all four model scales, with the gap widening at larger sizes (e.g., 1.03 perplexity improvement at 1.3B: 13.70 vs. 14.73), while using substantially fewer trainable parameters. Against GaLore, POET achieves significantly lower perplexity at every scale (e.g., 13.70 vs. 18.33 at 1.3B), demonstrating that spectrum-preserving reparameterization is more effective than low-rank gradient projection. POET also substantially outperforms LoRA (r=64) under a comparable parameter budget, achieving 18.05 vs. 25.19 perplexity at 350M with ~30M parameters. On GLUE downstream tasks, POET-pretrained models beat AdamW-pretrained models under all three finetuning strategies (Full FT, OFT, POET), with the largest gains on CoLA (0.523 vs. 0.361) and MNLI (0.818 vs. 0.658) under full finetuning. Compared to Muon, POET maintains higher singular value entropy (spectral diversity) and lower hyperspherical energy throughout training, suggesting better exploration of diverse optimization directions.

## Relation to Prior Work

POET generalizes orthogonal training (Liu et al., CVPR 2021; Liu et al., ICLR 2024; Qiu et al., NeurIPS 2023), which preserves hyperspherical energy by learning a single layer-shared orthogonal matrix, to a spectrum-preserving framework that learns two orthogonal matrices (left and right) while keeping a fixed random weight matrix. The theoretical justification for the RWP parameterization as the most general spectrum-preserving linear map comes from Li and Tsing (1990). Unlike low-rank pretraining methods (GaLore, LoRA) that reduce parameter count by restricting the weight space to a low-rank subspace, POET maintains full-rank weights but constrains the optimization to the orthogonal group, trading parameter efficiency for spectral invariance. The efficient approximations (SPO, CNP) draw on QR factorization primitives and Neumann series, respectively, to make the orthogonal optimization tractable at scale.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

POET demonstrates that preserving the singular-value spectrum during training yields a strong inductive bias for generalization, achieving substantially lower perplexity than AdamW with roughly one-tenth the trainable parameters across model scales from 60M to 3B. The three-phase training dynamics (conical-shell searching, stable learning on the shell, final adjusting) offer a new geometric lens on how orthogonal reparameterization shapes optimization, though the exact mechanism behind POET's slower-but-stable perplexity trajectory remains an open question. Key limitations include the need for careful hyperparameter tuning (reinitialization frequency T_m, block size b, Neumann terms k), the fact that the 3B result uses only 5B tokens, and the open problem of finding the optimal singular-value structure for initialization. The method's compatibility with any optimizer and its zero inference overhead make it a practical candidate for integration into existing LLM training pipelines.
