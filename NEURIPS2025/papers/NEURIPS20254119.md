## Problem

Diffusion Transformers (DiT) achieve strong image generation quality but suffer from quadratic self-attention complexity, creating a significant efficiency bottleneck especially at high resolutions. Analysis of pre-trained DiT models reveals that their global attention is predominantly local and redundant for generative tasks, suggesting that hardware-efficient alternatives like convolution could replace self-attention. However, naively substituting convolution for attention degrades performance due to pronounced channel redundancy, leaving an open question of how to build a purely convolutional diffusion backbone that matches DiT quality at substantially lower cost.

## Input / Output

DiCo operates in VAE latent space: a 256×256 RGB image is encoded to a 32×32×4 latent tensor, and a 512×516 image to 64×64×4. Conditioning is provided via a class label embedding (ImageNet) or CLIP text embeddings of length 77 (MS-COCO), combined with a sinusoidal timestep embedding. The network outputs predicted noise and covariance at each denoising step; at inference, 250 iDDPM sampling steps with classifier-free guidance produce the final synthetic image. For text-to-image, the paper demonstrates a fully convolutional variant where CLIP embeddings are reshaped into per-sample 9×9 depthwise convolution kernels, eliminating all attention operations.

## Method

DiCo is a three-stage U-shaped convolutional network built entirely from 1×1 pointwise convolutions and 3×3 depthwise convolutions, with pixel-shuffle/pixel-unshuffle for multi-scale processing and skip connections between encoder and decoder stages. The core DiCo Block applies a 1×1 conv (cross-channel mixing) → 3×3 depthwise conv (spatial context) → GELU, followed by a Compact Channel Attention (CCA) module that computes channel-wise sigmoid weights via global average pooling and a single 1×1 convolution, dynamically activating informative channels to counteract the redundancy inherent in static convolutions. Conditioning follows DiT's AdaLN scheme, where timestep and label embeddings predict per-layer scale and shift parameters. The key empirical finding motivating this design is that self-attention in DiT concentrates on nearby spatial tokens during generation, making local convolution a natural and more hardware-efficient substitute; CCA then closes the representational gap by restoring feature diversity without the quadratic cost of attention. The model is trained with the simplified noise-prediction MSE loss plus the full ELBO for covariance, using classifier-free guidance at sampling time.

## Results

On class-conditional ImageNet 256×256, DiCo-XL achieves an FID of 2.05 (IS 282.17) with classifier-free guidance, outperforming DiT-XL/2 (FID 2.27) and DiG-XL/2 (FID 2.07) while using only 87.30 Gflops (vs. 118.66 for DiT). At 512×512, DiCo-XL reaches FID 2.53 (IS 275.74), beating DiT-XL/2 (FID 3.04) with a 3.1× throughput advantage. The largest variant, DiCo-H (1B params), further reduces FID to 1.90 on ImageNet 256×256. On MS-COCO text-to-image, DiCo-CrossAttn achieves FID 4.87 and the fully convolutional DiCo-DWC variant achieves FID 4.93.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiCo-XL outperforms DiT-XL/2 by 0.22 FID (2.05 vs. 2.27) at 256×256 and by 0.51 FID (2.53 vs. 3.04) at 512×512, while running 2.7× and 3.1× faster respectively. Against the linear-attention-based DiG-XL/2, DiCo-XL achieves a 0.02 FID improvement (2.05 vs. 2.07) with 2.9× higher throughput. The 1B-parameter DiCo-H (FID 1.90) substantially outperforms the comparable DiC-H (FID 2.25) and all Mamba-based models (DiM-H: 2.21, DiS-H/2: 2.10). At 512×512, DiCo-XL is 7.8× faster than DiM-H and 6.7× faster than DiS-H/2 while achieving better FID. On MS-COCO text-to-image, DiCo-CrossAttn (FID 4.87) beats U-ViT-S/2 (5.95) and U-ViT-S/2-Deep (5.48) but trails MMDiT (5.30) and MMDiT+REPA (4.14), indicating the convolutional backbone is competitive but not yet at the level of the strongest transformer-based text-to-image models at this small scale.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on DiT (Peebles & Xie, ICCV 2023), adopting its diffusion training procedure, VAE latent space, and classifier-free guidance, but replaces the global self-attention backbone with a purely convolutional U-shaped architecture. It departs from the broader trend of scaling Transformers for diffusion (Stable Diffusion 3, FLUX, Sora) and from recent efficiency-oriented alternatives based on Mamba (DiS, DiM) or Gated Linear Attention (DiG), arguing instead that the locality observed in DiT attention maps makes convolution a natural and more hardware-efficient substitute. The key methodological departure is the Compact Channel Attention (CCA) mechanism, which addresses the channel redundancy that arises when naively swapping attention for convolution—a problem not addressed by prior ConvNet-for-diffusion work like DiC.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DiCo offers a practically significant efficiency win (2.7×–3.1× speedup over DiT-XL/2 with equal or better FID) that could lower the barrier to deploying high-resolution diffusion models on resource-constrained hardware. The empirical finding that self-attention in well-trained DiTs is predominantly local and redundant—validated across DiT-XL/2, PixArt-α, and FLUX—challenges the assumption that global attention is essential for generative quality and may redirect architectural design toward local operations. Open questions include whether DiCo scales beyond 1B parameters to match the largest autoregressive and VAR models, whether the locality finding generalizes to video or 3D generation, and whether the CCA mechanism is sufficient at much larger scales or whether more sophisticated channel-mixing is needed.
