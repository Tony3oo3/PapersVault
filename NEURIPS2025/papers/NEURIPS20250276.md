## Problem

Prior equilibrium refinement and dynamic stability results were confined to finite games, while most machine-learning applications (GANs, multi-agent RL, Kelly auctions) involve continuous games with a continuum of actions. The paper asks which local Nash equilibria in continuous games survive small perturbations of the payoff structure (strategic robustness) and which arise as stable limit points of regularized learning dynamics under noise (dynamic robustness), and whether these two notions are structurally linked.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250276/cropped_page6_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The analysis takes a continuous game defined by compact convex action spaces and continuously differentiable payoff functions, along with a choice of regularizer (e.g., quadratic or entropic) and a feedback model (stochastic first-order oracle or single-point SPSA). The key output is a geometric certificate: an equilibrium is strategically robust if and only if its gradient vector lies in the topological interior of the polar cone at that point. Under additional polyhedral-domain and decomposable-regularizer assumptions, the method also yields an explicit convergence rate expressed through the inverse of the regularizer's derivative kernel, which is geometric (exponential) for entropic regularization.

## Method

The paper first shows that measuring game distance in the uniform norm on payoffs makes robustness vacuous (any equilibrium can be destroyed by an arbitrarily small L∞ perturbation), and instead defines distance on the gradient fields. This yields a crisp geometric characterization: strategically robust equilibria are precisely boundary points where the gradient vector lies strictly inside the polar cone, i.e., forms a strictly acute angle with every tangent direction. The central structural result is a two-way implication: strategic robustness guarantees that FTRL converges to the equilibrium with high probability under any regularizer and constant step-size, and conversely, a constructed counterexample (a 1-player game with a non-robust boundary equilibrium) shows that without strategic robustness, convergence can hold for one regularizer and fail for another. For convergence rates, the paper leverages a Farkas-type separation lemma on polyhedral domains to show that entropically regularized FTRL achieves geometric (exponential) convergence, in contrast to the subgeometric rates obtained with vanishing step-size schedules.

## Results

The paper establishes a tight geometric characterization of strategically robust equilibria in continuous games: they are precisely those points where the gradient vector lies in the topological interior of the polar cone (Theorem 1). Under FTRL with constant step-size and stochastic gradient feedback, strategically robust equilibria are locally attracting with high probability (Theorem 2), and on polyhedral domains with decomposable regularizers, convergence is geometric at rate ‖x_t − x*‖ = exp(−Θ(t)) for entropic regularization (Theorem 3). The same geometric rate holds under payoff-based SPSA feedback (Theorem 4). A counterexample (Proposition 4) shows that without strategic robustness, convergence behavior depends critically on the regularizer choice, confirming the necessity of the robustness condition.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines or comparison tables. The results are positioned against prior theoretical work: the finite-game equivalence of strategic and dynamic stability (Boone & Mertikopoulos, NeurIPS 2023) is extended to general continuous games; the concave-game convergence results of Mertikopoulos & Zhou (2019) under vanishing step-size are subsumed by the constant step-size geometric rate established here; and the strict-equilibrium stability characterization of Flokas et al. (NeurIPS 2020) for finite games is generalized to the broader class of strategically robust (not necessarily strict) equilibria in continuous games. The paper also contrasts FTRL with mirror descent (MD), showing via a toy example that MD fails to converge to robust boundary equilibria under stochastic noise while FTRL succeeds, due to FTRL's cumulative dual variable smoothing.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the finite-game equilibrium refinement literature (trembling hand perfection, strategic stability of Kohlberg & Mertens, and the Ritzberger–Weibull equivalence between strategic and dynamic stability) to continuous games, where the natural L∞ payoff metric renders robustness vacuous. The key conceptual departure is measuring game distance via gradient-field norms rather than payoff values, which yields a crisp polar-cone geometric characterization. On the dynamic side, the work generalizes Mertikopoulos & Zhou's concave-game, vanishing-step-size results to general continuous games with constant step-size, and subsumes the finite-game FTRL stability characterizations of Flokas et al. and Boone & Mertikopoulos.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The tight two-way implication—strategic robustness is necessary and sufficient for dynamic robustness under FTRL—provides a clean, checkable criterion (gradient in the interior of the polar cone) for identifying which equilibria are reliable predictions in continuous games, directly relevant to GANs, multi-agent RL, and auction design. The negative result that the natural payoff metric makes all equilibria fragile, and that interior equilibria are repelled under stochastic noise, sharpens the understanding of why practical learning algorithms converge to boundary solutions. Open questions include extending the local convergence guarantees to global settings with multiple equilibria, and whether the constant-step-size analysis extends to adaptive or optimistic variants of FTRL.
