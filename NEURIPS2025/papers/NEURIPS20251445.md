## Problem

Existing implicit Chain-of-Thought methods face two critical gaps: they fail to preserve semantic alignment between the implicit reasoning embeddings (when interpreted as natural language) and the ground-truth reasoning, leading to significant performance degradation; and they reduce reasoning length but ignore the substantial per-token generation cost of the full LLM, which becomes prohibitive at scale (e.g., ~0.1s per token for DeepSeek-R1). No prior method jointly optimizes both token-level generation speed and semantic fidelity to the ground-truth reasoning.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text query (reasoning question) and appends k special <CoT> tokens to it. A lightweight LM (e.g., Sheared-LLaMA-1.3B for Llama-2-7B) processes this input and produces k last-layer hidden embeddings, which are linearly projected into the full LLM's embedding space to form the implicit reasoning vector Z. The full LLM then receives the query's input embeddings concatenated with Z and autoregressively generates the final answer (up to 30 tokens). During training, k=5 implicit tokens are used; during evaluation, k=1, ensuring the single embedding must concisely encode all reasoning information.

## Method

SemCoT is a two-step framework. Step 1 trains a customized sentence transformer (backbone: middle 5 layers of the target LLM + pooling + linear projection to 768-d) via contrastive learning on pairs of (ground-truth reasoning, GPT-4o-mini condensed reasoning), enabling cosine-similarity-based semantic alignment scoring between implicit and explicit reasoning in the LLM's embedding space. Step 2 fine-tunes a lightweight implicit reasoning generator (a pruned/distilled LM plus a linear projection layer) with a combined loss: a semantic alignment loss that maximizes the sentence-transformer cosine similarity between the generated implicit reasoning and the embedded ground-truth reasoning, and a cross-entropy prediction loss on the full LLM's answer tokens. The key architectural insight is that the lightweight LM generates the implicit tokens (dramatically reducing per-token cost) while the frozen sentence transformer enforces that these tokens remain semantically faithful to the ground-truth reasoning, addressing both efficiency and effectiveness simultaneously.

## Results

On Llama-2-7b-chat-hf, SemCoT achieves the highest accuracy on 4 out of 5 datasets (SVAMP 46.33%, MultiArith 15.93%, GSM8K 9.83%, CommonsenseQA 98.33%) while maintaining the fastest or near-fastest inference time (0.94–1.06 s per query). On Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.2, SemCoT leads on CoinFlip (82.17%), GSM8K (18.50%), and SVAMP (53.83%), ties Pause on CommonsenseQA (99.67%), and trails Pause by ~2% on MultiArith (38.89% vs. 40.93%). Inference time for SemCoT is consistently 1.27–1.35 s on Mistral, roughly 25–30% faster than all baselines except SoftCoT.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SemCoT outperforms all five baselines on the majority of dataset–LLM combinations. Against COCONUT (continuous latent-space reasoning), SemCoT gains 9–41 percentage points in accuracy (e.g., 46.33% vs. 33.00% on SVAMP-Llama; 82.17% vs. 50.00% on CoinFlip-Mistral) while being ~35% faster. Against CODI (self-distillation), SemCoT wins on 8 of 10 dataset–LLM pairs, with the largest margin on SVAMP-Llama (46.33% vs. 15.50%) and CoinFlip-Mistral (82.17% vs. 66.67%); CODI only beats SemCoT on CoinFlip-Llama (97.33% vs. 87.00%). Against ICoT-SI (progressive step-by-step internalization), SemCoT leads on 9 of 10 pairs, with ICoT-SI narrowly edging SemCoT on SVAMP-Mistral (54.17% vs. 53.83%). Against Pause (pause-token pretraining), SemCoT wins on 8 of 10 pairs; Pause's only clear win is MultiArith-Mistral (40.93% vs. 38.89%). Against SoftCoT (small-LM-generated soft tokens), SemCoT achieves substantially higher accuracy on every dataset (e.g., 46.33% vs. 38.17% on SVAMP-Llama; 18.50% vs. 12.17% on GSM8K-Mistral) while matching or slightly exceeding its inference speed.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the implicit CoT line of work (COCONUT, ICoT-SI, CODI, Pause, SoftCoT) by identifying two specific gaps: semantic misalignment between implicit embeddings and ground-truth reasoning, and the per-token generation cost of using the full LLM. It draws on sentence transformer literature (SBERT, SimCSE) but adapts the architecture to operate in the LLM's own embedding space by using middle LLM layers as the backbone. The lightweight generator design builds on pruned/distilled LM research (Sheared-LLaMA, mistral-1.1b-testing) and knowledge distillation, with a linear projection layer bridging the embedding space gap between the small LM and the target LLM.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SemCoT provides a practical ~35-40% inference speedup over state-of-the-art implicit CoT baselines while maintaining or improving accuracy, making efficient reasoning more accessible for deployment. However, absolute accuracy on math tasks remains low (GSM8K ~10-18%), the approach is only validated on 7B models, and the sentence transformer adds non-trivial training overhead. Open questions include scalability to 70B+ models, performance on very long reasoning chains, and whether the semantic alignment objective generalizes to domains beyond the five tested benchmarks.
