## Problem

Generative molecular design models frequently produce structures outside the synthesizable chemical space, and projecting them into valid synthesis pathways remains challenging due to the enormous search space (~10^63 compounds), the lack of semantic coherence in discrete postfix notation sequences, and the irreversible propagation of early errors in multi-step synthesis. Existing methods like SynNet and ChemProjector struggle to balance pathway validity, structural similarity, and variable-length generation simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255412/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a target molecule as a graph (atom embeddings by atomic number, bond embeddings by bond type) and conditions on a fixed catalog of 128 building-block clusters (from Enamine US Stock, featurized by 256-bit Morgan fingerprints) and 91 reaction templates from SynNet. It outputs a variable-length postfix notation sequence of tokens—building block tokens [BB,j], reaction tokens [RXN,i], [START], and [END]—which is executed on a stack via RDKit to yield a synthesizable analog molecule. Tokens are grouped into fixed-length blocks of size n̂c, enabling the semi-autoregressive block-wise generation that supports arbitrary pathway lengths.

## Method

MoleBridge is a semi-autoregressive Markov bridge model that generates synthesis pathways block-by-block: each block is conditioned autoregressively on all preceding blocks, and within each block, a discrete Markov bridge progressively denoises a corrupted token sequence over T steps using a transition matrix Q_τ = α_τ I + (1−α_τ) y 1^T. The key innovation is a thinking-and-denoising separation: a lightweight MLP classifier p_θ(z_τ^c=1|t_τ^c) identifies which positions in the noisy block are corrupted, and the denoiser ψ_θ blends its prediction with the current state weighted by this corruption probability, so clean positions are preserved while corrupted ones are corrected. Training minimizes a reweighted cross-entropy loss over unmasked positions (Eq. 9) plus a binary cross-entropy loss for the thinking network (Eq. 10). The architecture combines an L-layer graph transformer for molecular structure, sinusoidal time-step embeddings, and a transformer decoder with cross-attention to fuse sequence and structural modalities end-to-end.

## Results

On the standard test set, MoleBridge achieves a synthesis path success rate of 0.4915 and a reconstruction rate of 43.5%, substantially outperforming ChemProjector (0.4875, 28.4%) and SynNet (0.4205, 10.7%). Structural similarity scores (Morgan, Scaffold, Gobbi) reach 0.8455, 0.8695, and 0.8287 respectively, compared to 0.7167/0.7791/0.7273 for ChemProjector. On the more challenging ChEMBL 'unreachable' set, MoleBridge maintains its lead with a success rate of 0.4970 and reconstruction rate of 14.6%, versus 0.4940/13.3% for ChemProjector and 0.4250/5.4% for SynNet.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MoleBridge is compared against two baselines: SynNet (Gao et al., ICLR 2022), an amortized tree-generation approach for bottom-up synthesis planning, and ChemProjector (Luo et al., ICML 2024), the prior state-of-the-art for synthetic space projecting. MoleBridge wins on every metric across both evaluation sets. The most dramatic gain is in reconstruction rate on the test set: 43.5% for MoleBridge versus 28.4% for ChemProjector (a 53% relative improvement) and 10.7% for SynNet. On the harder ChEMBL 'unreachable' molecules, the reconstruction advantage narrows (14.6% vs. 13.3% vs. 5.4%), but MoleBridge still leads on all three similarity metrics. Success rates are relatively close across methods (0.49–0.50), indicating that the primary differentiator is the quality and fidelity of the generated pathways rather than their validity.

## Relation to Prior Work

MoleBridge builds directly on the synthetic space projecting paradigm introduced by ChemProjector (Luo et al., 2024), which frames the problem as generating postfix notation sequences from molecular graphs. Its generative mechanism draws on discrete Markov bridge models (RetroBridge, Bridge-IF) that model forward corruption and reverse denoising over categorical state spaces, but departs from their fixed-length constraint by applying the bridge process block-wise within a semi-autoregressive loop, enabling variable-length pathway generation. The 'thinking and denoising separation' strategy is adapted from the planned-denoising framework of Liu et al. (2025), repurposed here to identify corrupted token positions before correction rather than uniformly denoising all positions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a critical practical gap: generative molecular design models routinely produce structures outside synthesizable chemical space, and prior projecting methods (SynNet, ChemProjector) struggle with reconstruction fidelity and error propagation in multi-step pathways. MoleBridge's semi-autoregressive Markov bridge formulation is a natural fit for the sequential, stack-based nature of postfix synthesis, and the thinking mechanism provides a principled way to handle early-error propagation. However, the paper acknowledges limitations in handling stereoselective reactions and molecules that contradict existing synthesis logic, and the reaction template library (91 templates) remains narrow relative to the full space of organic chemistry. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger template sets and whether the block-level bridge can be replaced by finer-grained or adaptive noise scheduling.
