## Problem

Existing GRPO-based reinforcement fine-tuning methods for video temporal grounding rely exclusively on on-policy sampling, which is highly inefficient in the vast temporal search space of long videos. Pure on-policy exploration yields low, unstable top-1 IoU rewards and premature convergence to suboptimal solutions, while high-quality ground-truth annotations available in standard datasets are used only for evaluation rather than as dynamic learning signals. TempSamp-R1 addresses this gap by integrating off-policy supervision with a non-linear advantage shaping mechanism to enable stable and effective policy optimization.

## Input / Output

The model (Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct) receives temporally downsampled video at 2 FPS with frames resized to ~2.8M pixels, paired with a natural language query describing a target event. For temporal grounding, it outputs a predicted interval [t_start, t_end]; for highlight detection, it additionally produces per-timestamp saliency scores. Outputs are structured with optional <Think>...</Think> reasoning followed by <Answer>...</Answer> containing the final prediction, enabling a single unified model to support both CoT and non-CoT inference modes.

## Method

TempSamp-R1 modifies GRPO's group sampling by replacing one of G on-policy samples with an off-policy solution derived from ground-truth annotations, then normalizes advantages over the joint on/off-policy reward distribution. To prevent the consistently high off-policy reward from destabilizing advantage estimation (causing all on-policy samples to receive negative advantages), the paper introduces a non-linear asymmetric reward shaping function: a logarithmic branch compresses rewards above a threshold τ=0.8, while an exponential branch expands contrast among suboptimal rewards below it, yielding near-zero skewness in advantage distributions throughout training. A two-phase training scheme first optimizes direct answer generation (non-CoT), then incorporates format rewards to encourage intermediate <Think> reasoning, producing a single model that supports both inference modes. Task-specific composite rewards (IoU for grounding; F2 + weighted MSE for highlight detection) guide policy optimization toward task-relevant behaviors.

## Results

On Charades-STA, TempSamp-R1 (CoT) achieves 74.1% R1@0.5 and 52.9% R1@0.7, while the Mixed CoT variant reaches 76.0% R1@0.5 and 56.3% R1@0.7. On ActivityNet Captions, the CoT variant attains 56.0% R1@0.5 and 34.7% R1@0.7, and on QVHighlights the no-CoT variant achieves 30.0% mAP and 57.6% HIT@1. The Mixed CoT strategy, which selects the better prediction between CoT and non-CoT per query, yields the strongest overall results across all three benchmarks (e.g., 58.7% R1@0.5 on ActivityNet, 63.7% HIT@1 on QVHighlights).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest SFT baseline iMOVE, TempSamp-R1 (CoT) improves R1@0.5 by +5.6% and R1@0.7 by +7.6% on Charades-STA, and by +5.3% and +2.3% on ActivityNet Captions. Among RL-based methods, TempSamp-R1 surpasses VideoChat-R1 by +2.4% R1@0.5 on Charades-STA and outperforms TimeZero by +5.0% R1@0.7 on Charades-STA and a substantial +8.7% R1@0.5 / +7.8% R1@0.7 on ActivityNet Captions, where TimeZero's on-policy-only exploration struggles most. On QVHighlights, the no-CoT variant achieves 30.0% mAP, exceeding VideoChat-T (27.0%) by +3.0% and the zero-shot Qwen2.5-VL-7B (19.7%) by +10.3%. The Mixed CoT strategy further boosts HIT@1 on QVHighlights to 63.7%, surpassing VideoChat-T (55.3%) by +8.4%. Overall, TempSamp-R1 wins on every reported metric across all three benchmarks, with the largest margins on ActivityNet Captions where the temporal search space is widest.

## Relation to Prior Work

TempSamp-R1 extends the GRPO reinforcement fine-tuning framework (originally from DeepSeekMath) to video temporal grounding, building directly on recent GRPO-based methods like VideoChat-R1 and TimeZero that optimize MLLMs via task-specific IoU rewards. The key departure is the introduction of off-policy supervision—injecting ground-truth annotations as external solutions into the GRPO group—combined with a non-linear soft advantage estimation to handle the resulting reward distribution skew. This positions the work as a targeted fix for the exploration bottleneck that pure on-policy GRPO faces in large temporal search spaces, rather than a new RL paradigm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical takeaway is that ground-truth annotations, previously used only for reward computation in GRPO-based video grounding, can serve as active off-policy training signals to accelerate convergence and raise the performance ceiling—particularly valuable in few-shot settings where on-policy exploration is most constrained. The hybrid CoT training paradigm (one unified model supporting both reasoning and direct prediction) offers a practical deployment advantage. However, the method's reliance on high-quality ground-truth timestamps limits applicability to weakly labeled or unlabeled scenarios, and generalization beyond temporal grounding and highlight detection (e.g., multi-event tracking, video QA) remains untested.
