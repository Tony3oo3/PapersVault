## Problem

Lifelong model editing for LLMs requires sequentially incorporating new or corrected knowledge without retraining, but existing methods face a fundamental trade-off: non-parametric approaches memorize rigidly and fail to generalize to rephrased queries, while parametric methods that update shared parameters progressively overwrite earlier edits, causing catastrophic forgetting over long sequences. No prior method simultaneously maintains high reliability, generalization, and locality when scaling to thousands of sequential singular edits.

## Input / Output

During editing, MEMOIR receives a stream of (prompt, target answer) pairs one at a time (batch size 1) and updates a zero-initialized residual memory matrix W_m of shape D×D in a single FFN projection layer of a middle transformer block. At inference, a free-form text prompt is processed through the frozen LLM; the method computes the prompt's TopHash mask, compares it via Hamming distance against a database of stored edit masks, and either routes the activation through the relevant memory columns (for edited or rephrased prompts) or bypasses the residual memory entirely (for irrelevant prompts), producing a corrected text response.

## Method

MEMOIR introduces a residual memory layer W_m (a zero-initialized copy of the FFN projection W_proj) in one transformer block, so the edited output is W_0·a(x) + W_m·(M(a(x)) ⊙ a(x)). The key mechanism is TopHash: for each input, the top-k activation indices (by magnitude, after mean-centering with 100 irrelevant prompts) are selected and then permuted by a fixed random permutation π, yielding a sparse binary mask that acts as a locality-sensitive hash—semantically similar prompts produce overlapping masks while unrelated prompts do not. During editing, this mask restricts gradient updates to only k columns of W_m, distributing each edit's knowledge across a distinct parameter subset and minimizing overwrite of prior edits. At inference, the prompt's mask is matched against the stored edit-mask database via Hamming distance; if the overlap ratio R_match exceeds a threshold τ, the matched edit's mask is applied to activate the corresponding memory columns (enabling generalization to paraphrases), otherwise the residual memory is fully deactivated to preserve pre-trained knowledge and locality.

## Results

On the ZsRE Q&A benchmark with LLaMA-3-8B, MEMOIR achieves an average score of 0.93 at 1,000 sequential edits (Rel=0.94, Gen=0.85, Loc=1.00), outperforming the second-best method WISE (0.77) by a margin of 0.16. At the extreme scale of 15,000 edits, MEMOIR retains an average of 0.88 (Rel=0.87, Gen=0.78, Loc=0.98) while all parametric baselines collapse to near-zero. On hallucination correction (SelfCheckGPT, T=600, LLaMA-3), MEMOIR achieves a perplexity of 1.25 with perfect locality (1.00), compared to WISE's 3.20 PPL. On OOD generalization (Temporal, T=75, GPT-J), MEMOIR scores 0.79 average, edging out WISE (0.78) and GRACE (0.75).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MEMOIR dominates all seven baselines across every edit scale and model architecture. At T=1,000 on LLaMA-3, MEMOIR (Avg=0.93) outperforms WISE (0.77) by 0.16 and AlphaEdit (0.72) by 0.21; the gap widens dramatically at T=15,000 where MEMOIR retains 0.88 while AlphaEdit and MEMIT collapse to 0.01. GRACE maintains perfect reliability and locality at all scales but suffers from poor generalization (Gen=0.37 at T=1,000), yielding a lower average than MEMOIR. ROME and MEMIT, the locate-then-edit methods, degrade to near-zero performance beyond 100 edits due to catastrophic overwriting of the shared weight matrix. DEFER achieves perfect locality but its reliability and generalization drop to ~0.31 at T=1,000. Direct fine-tuning (FT) is the weakest baseline, losing most pre-trained knowledge after just 10 edits.

## Relation to Prior Work

MEMOIR builds on the locate-then-edit paradigm established by ROME and MEMIT, which identify FFN projection layers as key-value memories for factual knowledge, and extends the residual-memory approach of WISE and AlphaEdit that avoid modifying pre-trained weights. Its key departure is the structured sparsification of input activations via TopHash (top-k magnitude selection followed by a fixed random permutation), drawing on continual learning techniques like PackNet and Supermasks-in-Superposition to allocate disjoint parameter subsets per edit. At inference, the Hamming-distance-based conditional activation mechanism is novel relative to prior routing strategies (e.g., WISE's learned router or GRACE's exact-match retrieval), enabling generalization to rephrased queries without requiring large irrelevant corpora during training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MEMOIR extends the practical edit horizon from ~1,000 to 15,000 sequential edits while maintaining balanced reliability, generalization, and locality—addressing a critical bottleneck for deployed LLMs that require continuous knowledge updates. The elimination of large irrelevant-sample regularization (used by MEMIT, AlphaEdit, and WISE) simplifies the editing pipeline and reduces computational overhead. However, the method modifies only a single linear layer in a decoder-only transformer, limiting its applicability to knowledge that requires broader architectural changes or multi-modal settings. Open questions include whether hierarchical or multi-layer editing can further extend the horizon, and whether the TopHash mechanism generalizes to encoder-decoder or multi-modal architectures.
