## Problem

Diffusion models have transformed image generation via latent-space compression, but their application to text is hindered by the high dimensionality of token-level representations. Prior latent-diffusion-for-text work (e.g., TEncDM) retains the full sequence length of BERT encodings, leaving open how aggressively textual representations can be compressed before generation quality collapses. COSMOS asks whether a compact, smooth latent space can be learned that supports high-quality diffusion-based text generation at a fraction of the original dimensionality.

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes raw text sequences (up to 512 tokens) and produces generated text of the same length. Internally, a frozen BERT-base encoder maps the input to 768-dim hidden states, which a Perceiver Resampler compresses along the sequence axis only (embedding dimension d=768 is held fixed) into N latent vectors, where N ranges from 16 to 512 depending on the task. For conditional tasks (summarization, question generation, detoxification), the source text is encoded into a compressed latent that conditions the diffusion model via cross-attention, and the output is the target text decoded from the generated latent.

## Method

COSMOS trains a compressor-decompressor autoencoder with a Perceiver Resampler architecture (12 cross-attention transformer blocks) that maps L BERT hidden states to N fixed-length latent vectors, then trains a Gaussian diffusion model (12-layer transformer, ~130M params) entirely in this compressed space. The key insight is that minimizing token-reconstruction loss alone yields a brittle latent geometry unsuitable for diffusion; instead, the autoencoder is trained with four complementary objectives: cross-entropy on tokens, MSE alignment with frozen BERT activations, activation-space perturbations (30% random masking and Gaussian noise at δ=0.7), and fine-grained latent feature dropout (40%). These robustness-oriented objectives produce a smooth, perturbation-resilient manifold that reduces train-inference mismatch and enables the diffusion model to generalize to unexplored regions. Empirically, this recipe supports up to 8× sequence compression while matching or exceeding token-level diffusion baselines, and scaling N to 128 surpasses both diffusion and autoregressive models on unconditional generation.

## Results

On ROCStories (unconditional story generation), COSMOS_{N=128} achieves a MAUVE score of 0.940 (vs. 0.953 for source text), substantially outperforming the best autoregressive baseline GPT-2 (0.789) and the closest latent diffusion baseline TEncDM (0.762). On conditional tasks, COSMOS_{N=128} attains BERTScore 0.704 on XSum summarization, BLEU 0.694 on ParaDetox detoxification, and BERTScore 0.708 on SQuAD 2.0 question generation, matching or exceeding all baselines. Inference time for 512-token generation is 36.6 s for COSMOS_{N=128} versus 180.3 s for TEncDM and 42.8 s for GPT-2, demonstrating the fixed-step advantage of diffusion over sequential decoding at longer sequence lengths.

## Baselines & Comparisons

COSMOS_{N=128} decisively outperforms all token-level diffusion baselines (DiffuSeq, SeqDiffuSeq, AR-Diffusion, TESS, SEDD) on ROCStories MAUVE (0.940 vs. 0.061–0.598) and PPL (26.3 vs. 22.4–70.8), and also leads on all three conditional tasks. Against the closest latent diffusion competitor TEncDM, COSMOS wins on MAUVE (0.940 vs. 0.762), diversity (0.346 vs. 0.295), memorization (0.383 vs. 0.438), XSum BERTScore (0.704 vs. 0.699), ParaDetox BLEU (0.694 vs. 0.619), and SQuAD BERTScore (0.708 vs. 0.703), while being ~5× faster at L=512 (36.6 s vs. 180.3 s). Versus autoregressive GPT-2, COSMOS wins on MAUVE (0.940 vs. 0.789), diversity, and all three conditional tasks, but trails on PPL (26.3 vs. 20.5), which the authors attribute to an evaluation bias favoring AR models. On the large-scale OpenWebText 512-token benchmark, COSMOS_{N=512} achieves MAUVE 0.702 and PPL 55.0, nearly tripling GIDD's MAUVE (0.286) and reducing its PPL by ~4× (228.3). The only task where COSMOS does not lead is ParaDetox BLEU, where LD4LG edges ahead (0.708 vs. 0.694), though COSMOS still surpasses all other baselines on that metric.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the latent diffusion paradigm from vision (Rombach et al., CVPR 2022) to text by compressing frozen BERT representations into a shorter latent sequence via a Perceiver Resampler, then training a Gaussian diffusion model in that space. It directly builds on TEncDM (Shabalin et al., AAAI 2025), which performs diffusion over full-length BERT encodings, by asking how aggressively those encodings can be compressed before generation quality degrades. The key departure from prior latent-diffusion-for-text work (LD4LG, PLANNER) is that COSMOS does not rely on a powerful autoregressive decoder to recover text; instead, a lightweight decompressor and token predictor handle the final step, isolating the generative capacity of the latent diffusion itself. The paper's central methodological contribution is showing that naive cross-entropy compression yields a brittle latent geometry, whereas adding MSE alignment to encoder activations, activation-space perturbations, and latent-feature dropout produces a smooth, perturbation-resilient manifold that is actually *more* diffusable than the uncompressed space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that 4–8× compression of contextual representations can match or exceed the diffusion quality of full-length encodings challenges the working assumption that more representational capacity is always better for generative modeling, and suggests that the geometry of the latent space matters more than its raw dimensionality. Practically, the 2× inference speedup over token-level diffusion and the favorable scaling with sequence length (fixed diffusion steps vs. linear AR decoding) make latent-space diffusion a credible alternative for long-form generation. However, all experiments use ~130M-parameter backbones, the compression ablations are concentrated on the small ROCStories dataset, and the autoencoder and diffusion model are trained separately; whether the compression benefit persists at multi-billion-parameter scale, and whether joint end-to-end training further closes the gap, remain open questions.
