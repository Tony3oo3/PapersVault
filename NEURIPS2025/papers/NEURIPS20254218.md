## Problem

Sparse attention and quantization techniques that work well for language models fail under low density (<50%) and low bitwidth (INT8/INT4) in visual generation models because visual attention exhibits dispersed, diverse, and dynamically changing patterns (multi-diagonal, block-wise, diagonal-in-block) that resist structured sparsity and introduce quantization outliers. Prior work designs specialized masks or quantization schemes to accommodate these patterns, but this approach hits a quality ceiling at aggressive compression rates.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254218/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

PAROAttention is a post-training compression method applied to the attention mechanism of pre-trained diffusion transformers (CogVideoX-5B, Wan 2.1 14B for 720P 6/10-second video at ~17K tokens; Flux.1.Dev for 1024×1024 images), all with 30 sampling steps. It takes a text prompt and produces a generated video or image, with the only additional input being an offline-calibrated permutation order per attention head and timestep-wise sparse masks (each ~9.2 KB as binary bitmasks). The method operates at 20%–50% attention density and INT8/INT4 bitwidth for both QK^T and PV computations, achieving 1.9×–2.7× end-to-end latency speedup (up to ~10× when combining sparsity and quantization).

## Method

The core insight is that diverse visual attention patterns (multi-diagonal, block-wise) are the same local-aggregation phenomenon along different dimensions of the 3D [F,H,W] token layout, and can be unified by permuting the 1D token sequence (e.g., [F,H,W]→[H,W,F]) so that locally aggregated tokens become contiguous. The optimal permutation per head is selected offline using a combined metric of block-level sparsity (fraction of blocks where >90% of values fall below a threshold) and incoherence (max/mean ratio within each 64×64 block), and the permutation is fused into the preceding RoPE kernel with <0.03% overhead. On the unified block-wise pattern, the paper designs (1) static block-aligned sparse masks with timestep-aware sharing (distinct masks for the first half of timesteps, a shared mask thereafter) and (2) block-aligned quantization grouping matched to the FlashAttention tile size, which reduces incoherence from ~200–1200 to 12–20 and enables INT8/INT4 quantization of the PV product that prior methods kept at FP16/FP8.

## Results

On CogVideoX-5B text-to-video generation (VBench prompts), PAROAttn at 50% density achieves PSNR 29.14, SSIM 0.936, and CosSim 0.997 — nearly identical to the FP16 full-attention baseline (PSNR ∞, SSIM 1.000) — while delivering a 1.73× attention speedup. At 30% density, PSNR remains 22.89 with a 2.71× speedup. For quantization, PAROAttn(INT8) achieves PSNR 29.01 (vs. SageAttn's 29.58) while further compressing PV to INT8, and PAROAttn(INT4) reaches PSNR 24.16 (vs. SageAttnV2's 24.46) with PV at INT4. The combined sparse+quant configuration (0.5+INT4) achieves a 9.28× speedup with PSNR 24.34, outperforming all baseline methods at comparable or higher density.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PAROAttn decisively outperforms all sparse attention baselines at every density level tested. At 50% density on CogVideoX, PAROAttn achieves PSNR 29.14 versus SparseVideoGen (18.50), SpargeAttn (16.80), MInference (16.54), and DiTFastAttn (15.40) — a gap of 10–14 dB in PSNR. At 30% density, the advantage persists: PAROAttn (PSNR 22.89) exceeds SparseVideoGen (17.73) and SpargeAttn (15.22) by 5–8 dB. For quantization, PAROAttn(INT8) matches SageAttn within 0.57 dB PSNR (29.01 vs. 29.58) while compressing PV from FP16 to INT8, and PAROAttn(INT4) is within 0.30 dB of SageAttnV2 (24.16 vs. 24.46) while using INT4 instead of FP8 for PV. In terms of speedup, PAROAttn achieves 1.73× at 50% density (vs. SpargeAttn 1.67× and SparseVideoGen 1.42×) and 2.71× at 30%, with the combined sparse+INT4 configuration reaching 9.28×. The token reordering is also compatible with dynamic methods: adding PARO to SpargeAttn at 30% density improves PSNR from 15.22 to 16.89, matching SpargeAttn at 50% density while maintaining the 2.62× speedup.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in efficient attention for visual generation, where prior work (DiTFastAttn, MInference, SpargeAttn, SparseVideoGen) designs specialized sparse masks to accommodate diverse and irregular attention patterns, and quantization methods (SageAttn, SageAttnV2) handle outliers within attention maps. Instead, PAROAttention introduces a preprocessing step—pattern-aware token reordering via permutation of the [F,H,W] spatial dimensions—that unifies diverse multi-diagonal and block-wise patterns into a single hardware-friendly block-wise structure, making both sparsification and quantization substantially easier. The CUDA kernel builds on SageAttention2's implementation, and the quantization incoherence metric is inherited from Vidit-Q, but the core contribution is the reordering strategy that transforms the problem rather than adapting the compression technique to the pattern.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a simple offline-determined permutation (negligible runtime overhead <1%) enables near-lossless video/image generation at 20-30% attention density with INT8/INT4 quantization, yielding 1.9-2.7× end-to-end speedup on production models (CogVideoX, Wan, Flux). The token reordering idea is general and could benefit other compression techniques (dynamic sparse attention, timestep sharing, caching strategies), as the authors note. Limitations include the constrained search space (only 6 permutations for 3D tokens), reliance on static masks that still require timestep-wise calibration, and the observation that benefits are more pronounced for 3D video than 2D image generation. Open questions include whether more expressive reordering (beyond permutations) or learned reordering could further improve results, and whether the approach transfers to training-time attention optimization.
