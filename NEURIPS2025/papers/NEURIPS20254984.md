## Problem

Diffusion language models (DLMs) remain substantially slower than autoregressive models in practice because their bidirectional attention and non-sequential decoding order make the standard KV-Cache mechanism fundamentally incompatible. Specifically, K/V representations change across timesteps under bidirectional attention, and the decoding position at each step is not predetermined, preventing the fixed left-to-right reuse that KV-Cache relies on. This paper addresses the gap by designing a caching mechanism that works within the DLM denoising paradigm without requiring autoregressive or semi-autoregressive structures.

## Input / Output

The method operates at inference time on existing masked diffusion language models (LLaDA-8B, Dream-7B), taking a text prompt and a sequence of L tokens (some masked with [MASK]) as input, and producing a fully denoised token sequence as output. It is entirely training-free: no model weights are modified, and the caching strategy is applied as a wrapper around the forward pass. The method handles variable prefill lengths (e.g., 8–512 tokens for few-shot ICL) and output lengths (up to 512 tokens), with the number of denoising steps T typically matching or exceeding L.

## Method

The core insight is that K/V representations of tokens stabilize once decoded, while masked tokens continue to fluctuate, motivating a delayed and conditioned caching strategy. dKV-Cache-Decode caches K/V only for tokens that were already decoded at the previous step (one-step delay), reusing them across subsequent denoising steps while recomputing all still-masked tokens each step; a periodic cache refresh every N steps maintains accuracy. dKV-Cache-Greedy further restricts the active set to the current token, the previous token, and a small local window (size ≤ 6), reducing per-step complexity from O(L³) to O(L²) at the cost of some quality loss. An efficient concat_reorder operator handles the non-contiguous gathering and scattering of K/V at arbitrary positions by reordering tokens so cached entries are contiguous, with positional embeddings (e.g., RoPE) reordered once per forward pass. A dKV-Cache-Prefill variant caches prefill tokens permanently without refreshing, aligning with prefill-decode disaggregation for serving.

## Results

On LLaDA-8B-Instruct, dKV-Cache-Decode (confidence remasking) achieves near-lossless accuracy—51.00 on MMLU vs. 51.11 base, 78.85 on GSM8K vs. 77.56 base, and 46.34 on HumanEval vs. 39.63 base—while delivering 1.92×–2.42× inference speedup (e.g., 66.52 vs. 28.27 token/s on MMLU). dKV-Cache-Greedy (random remasking) achieves 1.57×–1.70× speedup with moderate accuracy drops (e.g., 47.70 vs. 51.79 on MMLU). On Dream-Base-7B with long-prefill settings, dKV-Cache-Prefill reaches up to 10.19× speedup on GPQA (75.40 vs. 7.40 token/s) with only ~1.5% accuracy loss. Overall, the method achieves 2–10× inference speedup across benchmarks with minimal or negligible performance degradation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LLaDA-8B-Instruct (Nie et al., 2025), dKV-Cache-Decode matches or slightly exceeds base accuracy on most benchmarks under confidence remasking (e.g., 78.85 vs. 77.56 on GSM8K, 46.34 vs. 39.63 on HumanEval) while achieving 1.92×–2.42× speedup, substantially outperforming the Half-Steps baseline which halves denoising steps (e.g., 51.11 vs. 51.00 on MMLU at 1.97× vs. 2.35× speed). dKV-Cache-Greedy trades 3–6% accuracy for 1.57×–1.70× speedup under random remasking, consistently outperforming the Few-Steps baseline (e.g., 47.70 vs. 43.19 on MMLU). Against Dream-Base-7B (Ye et al., 2025), dKV-Cache-Decode maintains near-base accuracy (76.57 vs. 76.88 on GSM8K at T=256) with 2.09× speedup, while dKV-Cache-Prefill achieves up to 10.19× speedup on GPQA (35.27 vs. 36.83 accuracy) and 7.40× on MMLU (71.71 vs. 72.19) in long-prefill settings, far exceeding the 2× speedup of the Half-Steps baseline. The method also improves over base on long-sequence tasks (L=512), suggesting existing DLMs under-utilize contextual information during inference.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the KV-Cache mechanism from autoregressive transformers (Vaswani et al., 2017) to masked diffusion language models (Sahoo et al., 2024), where bidirectional attention and non-sequential decoding fundamentally break the assumptions of standard caching. It departs from Block Diffusion (Arriola et al., 2025), which explored KV-Cache in semi-autoregressive DLMs but required training-time integration, and from MDLM's limited cache usage (only when no new tokens are computed). The key conceptual departure is the 'delayed' caching strategy: rather than caching a token's K/V immediately upon decoding, the paper postpones caching by one denoising step, motivated by empirical observations that K/V representations undergo their largest change exactly at the decoding step.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first training-free KV-Cache mechanism for DLMs, achieving 2–10× inference speedup on 7B-scale models (LLaDA, Dream) with near-lossless quality, substantially narrowing the practical speed gap between DLMs and ARs. A notable finding is that dKV-Cache-Decode can *improve* performance on long sequences (e.g., HumanEval 39.63→46.34%), suggesting existing DLMs under-utilize contextual information during inference — an insight that could inform future DLM architecture design. Limitations include evaluation on only two 7B models, reliance on a heuristic one-step delay without theoretical justification, and the acknowledgment that system-level optimizations (memory management, hardware-aware execution) remain unexplored.
