## Problem

Current text-to-image retrievers, from CLIP-like models to MLLM-based universal embedders, perform poorly on attribute-focused queries that target specific visual attributes (e.g., time of day, weather, materials, gestures) rather than the dominant subject of an image. The paper identifies that general-purpose image embeddings focus on global semantics and main subjects while neglecting these secondary attributes, and that even increasing embedding dimensionality or model scale does not resolve this limitation.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and an attribute-specific text prompt (e.g., 'What time of day is depicted in this image?') as joint input to an MLLM-based universal embedder (VLM2Vec-Phi-3.5-V), producing a single promptable image embedding that encodes the image conditioned on the queried attribute. At retrieval time, a natural-language text query (e.g., 'Find me an everyday image that shows the scene of the beach.') is encoded separately, and images are ranked by cosine similarity. Each benchmark test case consists of one positive image and 99 negatives drawn from mutually exclusive attribute categories, with a 1% chance-level baseline.

## Method

The core mechanism conditions image embeddings on GPT-4o-generated attribute questions: for each of 8 attribute categories, a question template (e.g., 'What gesture are the people making in this image?') is concatenated with the image and fed through VLM2Vec-Phi-3.5-V, whose contrastive-trained MLLM backbone produces a combined embedding that effectively encodes the 'answer' to the visual question. This promptable embedding outperforms both the unprompted baseline and a text-based alternative (where an MLLM generates a pure-text answer that is then embedded), because it preserves visual grounding while highlighting the target attribute. Two acceleration strategies reduce per-query cost: (1) pre-processing promptable embeddings for a predefined set of prompts, selected at test time via GPT-4o, yielding a 15% Recall@5 gain; and (2) deriving a linear approximation matrix W = BA^T from K sampled (unprompted, prompted) embedding pairs at test time, which can be applied to the query vector in O(KF+F) instead of O(NF+F), achieving an 8% Recall@5 improvement with K=100.

## Results

On the COCO-FACET benchmark, VLM2Vec-Phi-3.5-V with GPT-generated prompts achieves 53.4% Recall@1 and 75.5% Recall@5, improving over the un-prompted VLM2Vec baseline (44.5% R@1, 58.9% R@5) by +8.9 and +16.6 points respectively. The gains are most dramatic on harder attributes: Count of People jumps from 14.4% to 72.7% R@1, and Times from 5.5% to 18.4% R@1. The pre-processing acceleration strategy retains 73.7% R@5 (≈15% over baseline), while the linear approximation (K=100) achieves 67.0% R@5 (≈8% over baseline) at lower per-query cost.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The promptable embedding approach (VLM2Vec + GPT prompt) outperforms all 12 evaluated T2I retrievers on COCO-FACET: it beats the strongest CLIP-family model SigLIP2 (39.8% R@1, 52.6% R@5) by +13.6 R@1 and +22.9 R@5, and surpasses the best MLLM-based embedder MMRet-MLLM-S2 (48.8% R@1, 64.5% R@5) by +4.6 R@1 and +11.0 R@5. Against the text-based alternative (LLaVA-1.5 + GRIT), the promptable embedding wins on both metrics (53.4 vs. 40.5 R@1; 75.5 vs. 60.6 R@5), with the text-based approach suffering from hallucination and linguistic ambiguity. The method also generalizes across base retrievers: the same GPT prompts improve E5-V, LLaVE, and MMRet-MLLM-S2 consistently (Figure 2), and transfer to Place365 and SUN397 image pools (+3.9 R@1, +8.5 R@5 on Place365). The non-MLLM FLAIR model, which uses language-informed patch-level interaction, shows little improvement, underscoring the advantage of MLLM-based promptable embeddings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on MLLM-based universal embedders (primarily VLM2Vec-Phi-3.5-V) that can jointly process image+text inputs, repurposing their promptable query-embedding capability for the image/target side of retrieval. It departs from prior promptable-embedding work (CLOC, GiVE, FLAIR, UniIR) which either requires fine-tuning, operates at patch/token level, or applies prompts to the query rather than the target. The COCO-FACET benchmark extends standard T2I retrieval evaluation (MSCOCO, Flickr30K) from global caption alignment to attribute-specific queries, filling a gap left by existing benchmarks that either focus on dominant subjects or pre-select single-subject images.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper reveals that even the strongest MLLM-based retrievers (7.5B params, 4096-dim embeddings) achieve near-chance performance (Recall@1 < 10%) on several attribute categories, challenging the assumption that scale and embedding dimensionality resolve fine-grained retrieval. The promptable-embedding solution is simple, model-agnostic, and generalizes across architectures and image pools, making it a practical drop-in improvement. Open questions include whether the linear approximation can match full promptable embeddings on common attributes, whether the approach extends to truly novel/unseen attribute types without GPT-generated prompts, and whether the benchmark's COCO-based image pool limits generalization to more diverse real-world collections.
