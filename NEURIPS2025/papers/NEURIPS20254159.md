## Problem

Perceiving an object's shape and material from a single image is inherently ambiguous, yet most learning-based approaches estimate either shape or material in isolation and collapse to a single deterministic output, suppressing the multimodal uncertainty that is fundamental to the visual world. This paper argues that vision systems should jointly reason about shape and material, explicitly model their ambiguities as a distribution of plausible explanations, and exploit differential object motion as a natural cue for disambiguation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254159/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a short 3-frame video clip (256×256 RGB) of a pre-segmented object undergoing small rigid motion, or a single image duplicated across frames for static inputs. It produces per-frame, 256×256 maps of surface normals (3 ch), diffuse albedo (3 ch), roughness (1 ch), metallic-ness (1 ch), and specularity (1 ch) — 12 channels total per frame — as stochastic samples from a conditional distribution rather than a single point estimate, with DDIM sampling (50 steps, ~2.7 s on one A100) generating diverse hypotheses that converge when motion is present.

## Method

The core architecture, U-ViT3D-Mixer, is a conditional video diffusion model trained from scratch in pixel space that jointly denoises all shape-and-material channels in a single backbone. At high spatial resolutions it uses ResNet-style convolutions followed by decoupled shift-invariant neighborhood attention (spatial) and temporal attention layers; at coarser resolutions (32×32, 16×16) it switches to global transformer blocks with 3D Rotary Positional Embeddings. Lightweight MLP-Mixer-inspired channel-mixer blocks are inserted after local attention layers to enable explicit cross-attribute communication between normals, albedo, and reflectance parameters. Training uses a variance-preserving DDPM objective with a shifted-cosine noise schedule and attribute-specific loss weighting (shape and albedo upweighted relative to reflectance), on ~100K synthetic 3-frame video-attribute pairs rendered with Mitsuba3 under the Disney principled BSDF. The key design insight, validated by a probing experiment, is that a model trained to predict albedo already encodes shape information in its intermediate features, motivating unified multi-attribute denoising rather than separate per-attribute models.

## Results

On the DiliGENT shape benchmark, the method achieves a Median Angular Error of 12.31° (best among all compared methods) and a Mean AE of 18.03°, competitive with the best shape-specialized baseline Marigold-E2E-FT (17.74°). On the MIT Intrinsic Image Dataset for albedo estimation, it achieves SSIM 0.80, RMSE 0.08, and PSNR 23.14, outperforming all baselines by a clear margin (next best: ColorfulShading at SSIM 0.72, PSNR 20.56). The model is trained entirely on synthetic data and jointly predicts both shape and material, yet matches or exceeds dedicated single-attribute models on both tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On shape estimation (DiliGENT), the method achieves the best Median AE (12.31°), beating Marigold-E2E-FT (13.86°), StableNormal (14.59°), DSINE (16.17°), GeoWizard (21.55°), and RGB-X (32.10°); its Mean AE (18.03°) is marginally behind Marigold-E2E-FT (17.74°) but ahead of all others. On albedo estimation (MIT-Intrinsic), it dominates every baseline on all three metrics: SSIM 0.80 vs. 0.72 (ColorfulShading, Marigold-Albedo), RMSE 0.08 vs. 0.10 (ColorfulShading), and PSNR 23.14 vs. 20.82 (RGB-X), a 2.3 dB improvement over the closest competitor. RGB-X [61] is the only other method that jointly predicts shape and material, but it trails significantly on both tasks (Mean AE 34.99° vs. 18.03°; PSNR 20.82 vs. 23.14). DiffusionRenderer [40] is compared only qualitatively on real-world videos, where the paper reports visually superior shape and albedo quality, particularly for shiny and metallic objects. Notably, the method is trained solely on synthetic data without any real-image fine-tuning, yet generalizes to captured photos and out-of-distribution camera motions on Stanford-ORB.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the generative perception line (Enyo & Nishino for illumination, Kocsis et al. for materials, Han et al. for shape) from single static images to short motion videos, and from single-attribute to joint shape-and-material estimation within a unified backbone. Architecturally, it builds on pixel-space diffusion (U-ViT, Simpler Diffusion) and the Hourglass Diffusion Transformer's multi-scale design, adapting them to a video-conditioned, multi-attribute setting with neighborhood attention and channel mixers. A key departure from recent diffusion-based shape/material estimators (Marigold, StableNormal, RGB-X, DiffusionRenderer) is training from scratch on synthetic data rather than fine-tuning large pretrained models, motivated by the finding that pretrained priors can suppress the multimodal output distributions needed for ambiguity-aware perception.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a single compact model (~100M params, 2.7s inference) can jointly produce shape and full Disney-BSDF material parameters while naturally capturing perceptual ambiguities (convex/concave, postcard) and resolving them through differential motion — a capability directly relevant to embodied AI systems that must distinguish, e.g., a wet floor from a glossy one. Key limitations include restriction to rigid motion, pre-segmented objects, and opaque materials, leaving open the important questions of extending to deforming objects, multiple simultaneous motions, and integrated perceptual grouping. The emergent multimodal behavior without explicit ambiguity training is a promising signal for ambiguity-aware perception, but the scope remains narrow enough that broader generalization is unproven.
