## Problem

Equivariant neural networks have been extensively analyzed through their separation power—the ability to distinguish inputs modulo symmetry—but their approximation capabilities remain comparatively underexplored. A central open question is whether separation power fully characterizes expressivity: can two model families with identical separation relations still differ in which functions they can approximate? This paper shows that separation alone is not a complete proxy for approximation, revealing a heterogeneous landscape of universality classes even among architectures with maximal separation.

## Input / Output

The framework takes as input a finite group G acting on a finite set X (defining a permutation representation R^X), a subspace M of equivariant affine maps Aff_G(V, W) specifying the first layer, a subspace N of Aff_G(W, R) specifying the readout layer, and a non-polynomial pointwise activation σ. The width parameter h controls the dimension of the hidden representation W ⊗ R^h. The output is a characterization of the universality class U_σ(M, N) as the set of invariant continuous functions satisfying a system of constant-coefficient differential constraints, along with explicit sufficient conditions under which this class is a proper subset of the separation-constrained function space.

## Method

The core technique expresses shallow invariant networks as symmetric superpositions of generalized ridge functions (Proposition 41), reducing the universality question to a problem in the theory of ridge function spaces. The central characterization (Theorem 13) states that an invariant function f belongs to the universality class if and only if P(∂₁,…,∂_d)f = 0 for every polynomial P vanishing on the row spaces of the basis maps associated with the layer space M. From this, the paper derives two sufficient conditions for universality failure (Theorems 14 and 15) based on nonzero directional derivatives along vectors in the kernels of the basis maps. Applying these to concrete architectures, the paper proves that CNNs with filter width 1 and shallow PointNets share identical separation power (matching the full permutation-invariant class) yet have strictly different approximation capabilities (Proposition 16). Finally, Theorem 18 generalizes Ravanbakhsh's universality result by showing that hidden representations built from cosets of normal subgroups of G achieve separation-constrained universality, a condition that fails for key groups like S_n where the only non-trivial normal subgroup A_n yields representations that are too small to be effective.

## Results

The paper establishes a strict inclusion chain of universality classes for shallow invariant networks under Sₙ symmetry (Proposition 16): U_σ(C¹, I) ⊊ U_σ(ℝⁿ, ℝⁿ, ℝ) ⊊ U_σ(ℝⁿ, ℝ^{Sₙ}, ℝ) for n > 2, despite all three achieving identical separation power. Specifically, CNNs with filter width 1 cannot approximate (x₁+⋯+xₙ)ⁿ, while shallow PointNets can; and shallow PointNets cannot approximate x₁⋯xₙ, while networks with regular hidden representations ℝ^{Sₙ} can. Theorem 18 generalizes Ravanbakhsh's universality result, showing that hidden representations from cosets of normal subgroups H of G achieve separation-constrained universality, a condition that fails for the permutation group Sₙ (whose only non-trivial normal subgroup is Aₙ, yielding hidden dimension 2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three families of shallow invariant networks that share the same separation power (Proposition 9) but differ in approximation capacity. CNNs with filter width 1 (C¹) are strictly less expressive than shallow PointNets (ℝⁿ → ℝⁿ → ℝ), which in turn are strictly less expressive than networks with regular hidden representations (ℝⁿ → ℝ^{Sₙ} → ℝ). The first gap is witnessed by the function (x₁+⋯+xₙ)ⁿ, which PointNets approximate but width-1 CNNs cannot; the second gap is witnessed by x₁⋯xₙ, which regular-representation networks approximate but PointNets cannot. The paper also contrasts these permutation-based architectures with those built from abelian groups (e.g., circular CNNs over ℤₙ), where Theorem 18 guarantees separation-constrained universality because all subgroups of abelian groups are normal. This highlights that the structural properties of the symmetry group—specifically the existence of adequate normal subgroups—are the critical factor determining whether shallow equivariant layers suffice for universality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the universality theory for equivariant networks initiated by Ravanbakhsh (ICML 2020), who proved universality for shallow equivariant MLPs with regular hidden representations (R^G), and by Pacini et al. (2024), who characterized separation power for arbitrary finite groups. The key departure is a shift from asking 'can this architecture approximate all equivariant functions?' to the finer-grained question of whether separation power fully determines approximation capacity. The characterization of universality classes via differential operators on ridge function superpositions (building on Pinkus's ridge function theory) is a new analytical tool that had not been applied to the equivariant setting before.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central finding—that PointNets and width-1 CNNs, despite matching the separation power of fully connected equivariant networks, are provably not universal within their separation-constrained class—challenges the common practice of treating separation as a complete proxy for expressivity. This has direct implications for architecture design: depth and hidden representation type (governed by group structure, e.g., existence of normal subgroups) are the only two levers that affect approximation, and permutation groups like S_n lack the normal subgroups needed for the positive universality results. The analysis is limited to shallow (depth-2) networks, leaving open whether these failures persist in deeper architectures, and the positive results depend on group-theoretic conditions that fail for the most practically important symmetry groups.
