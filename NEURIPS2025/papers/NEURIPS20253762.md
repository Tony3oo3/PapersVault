## Problem

Embodied Foundation Models for robot manipulation have been trained exclusively via behavioral cloning, leaving the RL post-training stage that dramatically improves LLMs unexplored for robotics. The central obstacle is reward engineering: designing and instrumenting effective reward functions for real-world manipulation is labor-intensive and does not scale to broad task sets. This paper asks whether a pretrained vision-language foundation model can be post-trained with online RL using entirely self-predicted rewards, without any task-specific reward design.

## Input / Output

The model (PaLI-3B) receives two 224×224 RGB images (current and previous frame for LanguageTable; four Aloha camera views vertically stacked into two images at ~100×100 effective resolution per view) alongside a text prompt containing the goal instruction and, for Aloha, 14 joint-position tokens. It outputs a sequence of text-space tokens: 4 tokens encoding a 2D delta action (LanguageTable) or 70 tokens encoding a 5×14 action chunk (Aloha), plus a discretized steps-to-go token (50 bins for LanguageTable, 300 bins for Aloha). Both actions and steps-to-go are mapped onto the VLM's vocabulary, making the policy and reward model share the same autoregressive architecture.

## Method

The core contribution is a two-stage post-training recipe that mirrors LLM fine-tuning. Stage 1 (SFT) fine-tunes the pretrained PaLI model with two joint objectives: goal-conditioned behavioral cloning and steps-to-go prediction (predicting how many timesteps remain until the goal is reached). Stage 2 (Self-Improvement) uses the steps-to-go head to derive a data-driven reward r = d(o_t,g) − d(o_{t+1},g) (the decrease in expected steps-to-go after an action) and a threshold-based success detector, then runs on-policy REINFORCE with a frozen Stage-1 checkpoint for reward/success computation while updating a separate policy checkpoint. This eliminates all manual reward engineering: the reward is well-shaped by construction (it is a potential-based shaping of a sparse success signal), and the on-policy REINFORCE choice avoids the deadly triad of off-policy learning and bootstrapping. The key insight is that web-scale multimodal pretraining endows the steps-to-go predictor with enough generalization to provide meaningful reward signals even on tasks and objects (e.g., a banana) never seen in the imitation dataset, enabling autonomous acquisition of novel behavioral skills.

## Results

On the real-world LanguageTable domain, Self-Improvement with only ~3% additional Block2Block episodes boosts policy success rate from ~62–63% to ~87–88%, and the 20% imitation + 3% Self-Improvement combination far exceeds BC (RT-2) policies trained on 80% of the imitation dataset. On the Aloha Single Insertion task, 5K imitation + 2.5K Self-Improvement episodes outperform 10K-episode BC policies and rival 15K-episode BC. For novel skill acquisition on BananaTable (a task with zero imitation data), ~8 hours of Self-Improvement on 2 robot stations improves success from ~63% to ~85%. In the Real2Sim domain transfer setting, only 3% target-domain episodes lift success from ~22% to ~59%, matching BC trained on 80% of the target domain's imitation data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline throughout is RT-2 [Brohan et al., 2023], which uses the identical PaLI-3B architecture and tokenized action prediction but is trained solely via behavioral cloning (supervised learning). The paper's two-stage approach (SFT + Self-Improvement) consistently and substantially outperforms RT-2 across all tested domains and data scales. On real-world LanguageTable, adding just ~3% Self-Improvement episodes on top of 20% imitation data yields policies that 'far exceed' RT-2 policies trained on 80% imitation data — a 4× data-efficiency gain. On simulated LanguageTable, 10% data + 1% Self-Improvement episodes outperforms RT-2 at both 20% and 80% data scales. On Aloha, 5K + 2.5K Self-Improvement episodes surpass RT-2 trained on 10K episodes. The paper also ablates the role of pretraining by comparing against Uni-PaLI (unimodal pretraining) and Scratch (random init) reward models, showing that PaLI's multimodal pretraining is essential: PaLI at 20% data outperforms Uni-PaLI at 80% data, and Scratch models fail to provide meaningful improvement in low-data regimes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the RT-2 paradigm (Brohan et al., 2023), which fine-tunes PaLI vision-language models as robot policies purely via behavioral cloning, by introducing a two-stage post-training recipe directly analogous to LLM post-training (SFT followed by RL). The key methodological departure is the steps-to-go prediction objective added during SFT, which then serves as a data-driven reward function and success detector for online REINFORCE-based Self-Improvement, eliminating the need for manual reward engineering. Conceptually, it bridges the data-driven reward learning literature (e.g., time-contrastive networks, distance-weighted supervised learning) with web-scale multimodal pretraining, arguing that the generalization properties of the pretrained foundation model are what make the self-predicted reward robust enough for real-world RL.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a small amount of online RL post-training (10% extra robot time) dramatically outperforms 8× more imitation data for policy success rates, and that this combination uniquely enables behavioral generalization to entirely novel skills (e.g., BananaTable) that pure behavioral cloning cannot achieve. This provides a practical, reward-engineering-free recipe for improving robot foundation models at scale, with a 1-to-many human-to-robot supervision ratio. Key open questions include extending to off-policy methods for further sample efficiency, handling long-horizon skill chaining, and ensuring reward model robustness on out-of-distribution failure cases that imitation datasets typically lack.
