## Problem

Existing graph OOD detection methods are compromised by structural redundancy that induces semantic shifts: pre-trained GNNs lack ground-truth distributional information at test time, and the pervasive presence of similar irrelevant structural elements across ID and OOD graphs prevents accurate capture of the distinctive substructures that truly differentiate them. Prior test-time approaches like GOODAT rely on learnable graph augmentations that can alter substructure semantics or cause information loss, undermining detection reliability.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single unlabeled test graph G=(A,X) and a frozen pre-trained GNN encoder (5-layer GIN with perturbation-free augmentation views). It produces a scalar OOD detection score computed as the overall loss L = L_Cl + λL_CRI, where higher scores indicate greater probability of OOD membership. Notably, the pre-trained model's parameters remain fixed throughout; only a lightweight coding-tree encoder is trained at test time, making the approach plug-and-play for any pre-trained GNN.

## Method

RedOUT introduces the Redundancy-aware Graph Information Bottleneck (ReGIB), which theoretically decomposes the standard GIB objective into a prediction term (preserving essential information) and two compression terms (eliminating redundancy), with formal upper/lower bounds derived for each component. The essential view G* is instantiated by constructing a fixed-height coding tree via structural entropy minimization using MERGE and DROP operators, yielding a hierarchical abstraction that retains distinctive substructures while discarding redundant elements. A tree-based encoder then propagates messages bottom-to-top on this coding tree to produce a tree embedding Z_T, which is contrasted against the original graph embedding Z via L_Cl (InfoNCE-style) and penalized for label-conditioned redundancy via L_CRI (a conditional mutual information upper bound). The combined loss serves directly as the OOD score, requiring no modification to the pre-trained model and no access to training labels.

## Results

RedOUT achieves an average AUC of 87.46% across 10 OOD detection dataset pairs, outperforming the best baseline GOOD-D (80.73%) by 6.7% on average and ranking first (A.R. = 1.3) among all 18 compared methods. The largest single-pair gain is on ClinTox/LIPO, where RedOUT reaches 86.56% AUC versus GOOD-D's 69.18% (+17.3%). RedOUT wins on 9 out of 10 dataset pairs; the sole exception is IMDB-M/IMDB-B (79.54% vs. GOOD-D's 79.94%), attributed to the near-identical structural semantics of the two social-network datasets. On anomaly detection (Table 2), RedOUT also leads, e.g., 89.62% on BZR vs. GOOD-D's 75.16% and 89.81% on REDDIT-B vs. 88.67%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RedOUT outperforms all 18 baselines on 9 of 10 dataset pairs. Against the strongest end-to-end method GOOD-D (80.73% avg AUC), RedOUT gains +6.7% on average, with the largest margin on ClinTox/LIPO (+17.4%) and FreeSolv/ToxCast (+12.8%). Compared to the closest test-time method GOODAT (76.89%), RedOUT improves by +10.6% on average, with particularly large gains on PTC-MR/MUTAG (+12.6%) and BBBP/BACE (+15.5%). Kernel-based methods (PK-LOF, WL-OCSVM, PK-iF, etc.) and GCL-based methods (InfoGraph-MD, GraphCL-MD) trail by 15–38 AUC points, confirming that structural-entropy-guided redundancy removal at test time substantially outperforms both hand-crafted kernel features and contrastive pre-training alone. The only loss is on IMDB-M/IMDB-B (79.54% vs. GOOD-D's 79.94%), which the authors attribute to the near-identical structural semantics of the two social-network datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Graph Information Bottleneck (GIB) principle by decomposing it into essential and redundant components (ReGIB), a theoretical step not taken by prior GIB-based methods like GOODAT. It builds on the test-time OOD detection setting introduced by GOODAT but departs from its learnable graph masker approach, instead using structural entropy minimization—a non-parametric, approximation-based procedure—to construct an essential view via coding trees. The coding tree construction leverages operators from SEP (Wu et al., ICML 2022), while the overall framework is positioned as a data-centric, post-hoc method that freezes the pre-trained GNN and only trains a lightweight tree encoder at test time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical contribution is that structural entropy minimization requires no additional learning or model retraining, making it a lightweight plug-in for existing pre-trained GNNs in deployment scenarios where labeled data is unavailable. The honest discussion of the IMDB-B/IMDB-M limitation (structurally similar datasets from the same source) highlights an open question about whether purely structural redundancy removal suffices when semantic differences are label-driven rather than topology-driven. Extending the framework to node-level OOD detection and to settings where structural similarity between ID and OOD distributions is high remain important future directions.
