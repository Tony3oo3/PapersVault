## Problem

Synchronous RL systems for large reasoning models (LRMs) suffer from severe GPU underutilization because generation must wait for the longest output in a batch before any model update can begin, and scaling generation across more devices pushes decoding into a memory-IO-bound regime. While prior attempts at overlapping generation and training exist, they still use batched generation from a single model version, leaving the core inefficiency unaddressed. AREAL tackles this by fully decoupling generation from training while preserving (or improving) final model quality.

## Input / Output

The system consumes text prompts (questions up to 1024 tokens) paired with an SFT-initialized base model (Qwen2 1.5B–32B or Llama-8B), generating up to 27,648 thinking tokens per response with 16 samples per prompt. Rewards are rule-based and sparse: a scalar of +5 or −5 is assigned only at the final token based on answer correctness (string matching for math, unit-test execution for code). The training batch comprises 512 prompts, and the system allocates 75% of GPUs to inference and 25% to training, with the output being updated model parameters after a fixed number of PPO steps.

## Method

AREAL runs rollout workers in a streaming, interruptible fashion: they continuously generate new outputs and, upon receiving updated weights, discard old KV caches and recompute with new parameters, so trajectories may span multiple policy versions. To handle the resulting data staleness, the system enforces a rate-limiting constraint (Eq. 3) bounding the maximum staleness η of any training batch, and prioritizes older trajectories from the replay buffer. Algorithmically, AREAL replaces standard PPO with a decoupled PPO objective (Eq. 5) that separates the behavior policy (which may be stale) from a proximal policy (the most recent model), applying importance sampling for the former and clipping around the latter to keep updates within a trust region of the high-quality policy. Proposition 1 proves that interrupted generation across multiple policy versions is equivalent to sampling from a single well-defined behavior policy, preserving the validity of the importance-sampling correction. System-level optimizations include dynamic micro-batch allocation for variable-length sequences (yielding ~30% throughput gain), interruptible generation (12–17% gain), and pipelined reward computation and data transfer to overlap CPU work with GPU generation.

## Results

On AIME24 (math), AREAL achieves 63.1% with a 7B model (vs. 63.0% for the synchronous variant) while reducing training time from 57.7h to 25.4h (2.27× speedup). On LiveCodeBench (code), the 32B model reaches 61.0% (vs. 61.2% synchronous) with training time cut from 51.1h to 31.1h (1.64× speedup). The paper reports up to 2.77× end-to-end training speedup and up to 2.5× throughput improvement over the synchronous verl baseline, with linear scaling efficiency demonstrated up to 512 GPUs. Final accuracy is matched or within ~1 point of the synchronous oracle across all model sizes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AREAL is compared against three baselines: DeepScaleR (1.5B math, via verl), DeepCoder (14B code, via verl), and a synchronous variant of AREAL itself (for 7B math and 32B code). Against the synchronous AReaL variant, AREAL wins decisively on training time: 25.4h vs. 57.7h for 7B math (2.27× faster) and 31.1h vs. 51.1h for 32B code (1.64× faster), while matching final accuracy (63.1 vs. 63.0 on AIME24; 61.0 vs. 61.2 on LiveCodeBench). Against the external DeepScaleR baseline (1.5B), AREAL scores 42.0 vs. 43.1* on AIME24 with comparable training hours (41.0h vs. 33.6h). Against DeepCoder (14B), AREAL scores 56.7 vs. 57.9* on LiveCodeBench with slightly longer training (48.8h vs. 44.4h). The speedup advantage is most pronounced for larger models and longer generation lengths, where the synchronous system's idle-time bottleneck is most severe; for the smaller 1.5B model the throughput gap narrows because generation is fast enough to keep pace with training even in the synchronous setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

AREAL builds on the decoupled PPO objective from Hilton et al. (NeurIPS 2022), which separates the behavior policy from the proximal policy to handle off-policy data, and adapts it to the LLM reasoning setting where trajectories can span tens of thousands of tokens. System-wise, it extends the ReaLHF framework by fully decoupling generation and training onto separate GPU clusters, going beyond the one-to-two-step overlap explored in prior async LLM RL work (Noukhovitch et al. 2024, Roux et al. 2025) and the batched generation-training alternation of synchronous systems like verl/HybridFlow. The interruptible generation mechanism, which discards and recomputes KV caches upon weight updates, is a novel system contribution that enables trajectories to span multiple policy versions while maintaining algorithmic correctness via the decoupled objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 2.77× end-to-end speedup with matched or improved final accuracy makes large-scale LRM RL training substantially more accessible, and the linear scaling to 512 GPUs (where synchronous systems fail) addresses a critical bottleneck for teams training reasoning models. The ablation revealing that naive PPO catastrophically degrades at even moderate staleness (η=4: 23.3 vs. 42.0 AIME24) while the decoupled variant handles it gracefully is a practically important diagnostic for anyone attempting off-policy RL with LLMs. Open questions include whether the heuristic 75-25 inference/training split can be dynamically optimized, whether the approach extends to multi-turn agentic tasks, and whether REINFORCE-like algorithms (which lack the importance-sampling structure) can tolerate asynchrony without the decoupled objective.
