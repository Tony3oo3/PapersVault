## Problem

Existing block coordinate descent (BCD) methods for training deep neural networks only guarantee convergence to stationary points, not global minima, leaving a critical gap in theoretical understanding of how BCD can achieve arbitrarily small loss. This paper provides the first global convergence guarantee for BCD applied to deep neural networks with an arbitrary number of layers, operating outside the neural tangent kernel (NTK) regime where parameters may shift by O(1) rather than remaining near initialization.

## Input / Output

The method takes n training pairs (x_i, y_i) with x_i ∈ R^{d_in} and y_i ∈ R^{d_out}, along with architecture parameters (depth L, hidden width r, activation function). A key constraint is n ≤ d_in with the data matrix X having full row rank, ensuring a global minimizer exists for the first-layer subproblem. The output consists of trained weight matrices W_1, …, W_L and auxiliary variables V_{j,i} that approximate hidden-layer activations, with the guarantee that the total objective F(W, V) < ε for any prescribed accuracy ε.

## Method

The paper reformulates neural network training by introducing auxiliary variables V_{j,i} that approximate each hidden layer's output, yielding a decoupled objective where each layer's weights and its corresponding auxiliary variable form independent blocks. The BCD algorithm updates blocks sequentially backward through the network (W_L → V_{L-1,i} → W_{L-1} → … → W_1), with each block optimized via vanilla gradient descent. The key analytical insight is that the output-layer loss decreases exponentially across outer iterations while hidden-layer losses remain bounded by a quantity that propagates backward with a (2/α)^L amplification factor, where α is the minimum derivative of the activation. For ReLU activations (which violate strict monotonicity), a modified algorithm incorporates skip connections and non-negative projection steps to ensure the hidden-layer reconstruction equations remain solvable. Singular value bounding is applied at initialization to maintain well-conditioned weight matrices throughout training, and the total gradient complexity to reach ε-accuracy is Õ(nL log²(1/ε)).

## Results

The paper establishes that BCD provably converges to global minima (arbitrarily small loss ε) for deep networks with strictly monotonically increasing activations, with total gradient computations bounded by Õ(nL log²(1/ε)). Empirically, on a synthetic regression task (n=500, d_in=600, 4 hidden layers of width r=30, LeakyReLU with α=0.5), the training loss decreases monotonically to near zero, while removing singular value bounding (SVB) causes the loss to stagnate around 10⁻³. For ReLU with skip connections (Algorithm 3), the loss also decreases monotonically, whereas removing skip connections causes training to fail entirely due to the ReLU non-negativity constraint. The generalization gap is bounded via Rademacher complexity, scaling as Õ((B_Y + 2^L ℓ^{L-1} B_X) d_in^{1/2} L^{3/2} (2r)^{L/2} log r / n).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not compare against other optimization methods (e.g., backpropagation-based gradient descent, NTK-based methods, or prior BCD approaches) in a head-to-head benchmark. Instead, the empirical validation is ablation-based: (1) removing singular value bounding (SVB) from Algorithm 1 causes the LeakyReLU training loss to plateau at ~10⁻³ rather than converging to near zero, confirming SVB's role in both theoretical convergence and practical stability; (2) removing skip connections from Algorithm 3 causes ReLU training to fail to converge, validating the theoretical necessity of the residual architecture for handling ReLU's non-negative range. The paper positions its contribution as the first global convergence guarantee for BCD in deep networks (arbitrary depth) outside the NTK regime, whereas prior BCD works [43, 41, 42, 38] only guarantee convergence to stationary points.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the classical block coordinate descent framework (Tseng, 2001) to deep neural network training, departing from prior BCD-for-neural-networks work (Zeng et al. 2019, Zhang & Brand 2017) that only established convergence to stationary points. It also positions itself against the NTK regime (Jacot et al. 2018) and mean-field approaches, which provide global convergence but under overparameterization or shallow-architecture constraints; here, global minima are reached for arbitrary depth without NTK assumptions, with parameters allowed to move by Ω(1). The generalization analysis builds directly on the spectrally-normalized margin bounds of Bartlett et al. (2017), and the multi-output extension leverages the low-rank factorization convergence result of Ye & Du (2021).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first proof that a practical layer-wise optimization algorithm (BCD) reaches global minima in deep networks of arbitrary depth outside the NTK regime, which is a meaningful theoretical advance for understanding why deep learning works. However, the guarantees come with restrictive assumptions: full-rank data matrix (n ≤ d_in), strictly monotonic activations for the main result, and a mandatory skip-connection architecture with fixed output-layer weights for ReLU. Open questions include whether the skip-connection requirement for ReLU is fundamental or an artifact of the proof, whether the results extend to non-i.i.d. or high-dimensional-overparameterized regimes, and whether the theoretical iteration counts are competitive with backpropagation in practice. The small-scale synthetic experiments (500 samples, 4 layers) leave practical scalability largely untested.
