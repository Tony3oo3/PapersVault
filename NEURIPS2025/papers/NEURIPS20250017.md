## Problem

Existing diffusion-based inversion methods face an intrinsic trade-off between reconstruction accuracy and editing flexibility: text-only conditioning (NTI, NPI) preserves semantics but loses structural detail, while image-only or non-optimization approaches (DirectInv, Renoise) preserve structure but drift semantically. No prior method simultaneously anchors the inversion trajectory in both the semantic space defined by the prompt and the visual space defined by the source image, causing cumulative noise errors that degrade both reconstruction and downstream edit fidelity.

## Input / Output

DCI takes a source RGB image (encoded to latent z_0 via a pretrained VAE) and its corresponding text prompt p_s as input, and produces an inverted latent noise code z_T that is compatible with standard DDIM-based editing engines (P2P, LEDITS++, Stable-Flow, MasaCtrl). The method is plug-and-play: it requires no retraining or architectural modification to the underlying diffusion model (evaluated on Stable Diffusion v1.4 with 50 DDIM steps and CFG scale 7.5), and the additional computational overhead is minimal (~12.1 s vs. 11.55 s for vanilla DDIM on an RTX 4090).

## Method

DCI formulates inversion as a dual-conditioned fixed-point optimization with two alternating stages per timestep. Stage 1 (Reference-Guided Noise Correction) computes a prompt-conditioned noise estimate ε̂_raw = ε_θ(z_t, t, p_s), then applies a one-step gradient correction toward a reference noise ε_ref extracted from the source image latent via the VAE encoder, yielding a visually grounded noise ε̂ = ε̂_raw − λ∇L_ref. Stage 2 (Fixed-Point Latent Refinement) iteratively minimizes the self-consistency loss L_fix = ‖f_θ(z_t) − z_t‖₂, where f_θ is the DDIM update function, driving z_t toward a fixed point of the generative trajectory; this converges in ≤5 iterations with learning rate η=0.001. The key distinction from prior work (e.g., SPDInv, which only disentangles from the source prompt) is the explicit dual conditioning: the reference image provides pixel-level structural anchoring while the prompt provides semantic direction, jointly reducing both the latent noise gap D_noi and reconstruction error D_rec without sacrificing editability.

## Results

On PIE-Bench with the P2P editing engine, DCI achieves the best scores across all six metrics: DINO 6.07×10⁻³, PSNR 29.38, LPIPS 33.01×10⁻³, MSE 21.28×10⁻⁴, SSIM 87.14×10⁻², and CLIP 25.52. Compared to the strongest baseline SPDInv, DCI reduces DINO by 31.1%, LPIPS by 8.3%, and MSE by 13.3%, while also improving PSNR, SSIM, and CLIP. In human preference evaluation, DCI scores 4.10/5 (user study), 0.5547 (PickScore), and 0.3674 (ImageReward), substantially outperforming SPDInv (3.48, 0.4954, 0.1564) and DDIM (2.14, 0.4416, −0.0120). Inversion time is 12.13 s per image, comparable to DDIM (11.55 s) and far faster than optimization-heavy methods like NTI (137.54 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DCI outperforms all nine baselines on every metric in the main PIE-Bench comparison. Against the closest competitor SPDInv (ECCV 2024), DCI reduces DINO from 8.81 to 6.07 (−31.1%), LPIPS from 36.01 to 33.01 (−8.3%), and MSE from 24.54 to 21.28 (−13.3%), while also gaining on PSNR (29.38 vs 28.60), SSIM (87.14 vs 86.23), and CLIP (25.52 vs 25.26). DirectINV and ProxEdit, which focus on content preservation, are surpassed by DCI on DINO (6.07 vs 11.65/11.87) and LPIPS (33.01 vs 54.55/45.70), indicating that dual-conditioning yields substantially better structural fidelity. Optimization-heavy methods like NTI and AIDI are dominated by a wide margin (DINO 13.44/12.16 vs 6.07) and are also 7–11× slower in inversion time. h-Edit, the most recent baseline, achieves a competitive CLIP score (25.30) but lags DCI on all reconstruction metrics (DINO 11.17, MSE 85.40). In human preference studies, DCI leads SPDInv by 0.62 points on user rating (4.10 vs 3.48) and by 0.21 on ImageReward (0.3674 vs 0.1564), confirming that the quantitative gains translate to perceptual quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

DCI extends the DDIM deterministic inversion framework (Song et al., ICLR 2020) and the fixed-point formulation introduced by SPDInv (Li et al., ECCV 2024), which treated inversion as a search problem to decouple from the source prompt. The key conceptual departure is that prior methods address either text-embedding optimization (NTI, NPI) or prompt disentanglement (SPDInv) in isolation, whereas DCI unifies both the source prompt and the reference image as simultaneous conditions within a single fixed-point optimization, treating the inversion as a dual-conditioned problem rather than a single-condition one. This positions DCI as a synthesis of two previously separate lines of work—text-conditioned refinement and image-anchored correction—into a coherent two-stage pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DCI addresses the long-standing reconstruction-vs-editability trade-off in diffusion-based editing by anchoring the inversion trajectory in both semantic and visual space simultaneously, and does so with minimal computational overhead (12.13s vs. 11.55s for DDIM), making it practically deployable as a plug-and-play module. The consistent improvements across all six metrics and multiple editing engines (P2P, LEDITS++, Stable-Flow) suggest the dual-conditioning principle is broadly applicable rather than engine-specific. Open questions include whether the method generalizes to non-DDIM samplers (e.g., DPM-Solver), whether it extends to video or 3D inversion, and how it behaves when the source prompt is severely misaligned with the image content—limitations the authors acknowledge but do not fully resolve.
