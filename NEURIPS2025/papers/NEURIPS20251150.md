## Problem

Full-length song generation requires simultaneously modeling vocal expressiveness, instrumental accompaniment, and multi-section structure, yet existing approaches face a fundamental trade-off: non-autoregressive diffusion models struggle with precise phoneme-to-audio alignment, while autoregressive language models rely on quantized tokens that degrade musicality and audio fidelity. No prior method effectively balances global structural coherence with local acoustic detail over durations exceeding 60 seconds.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251150/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes structured lyrics (normalized to phoneme sequences with vocal-based structure flags like verse/chorus prepended to paragraphs and accompaniment-based flags for intros/outros inserted proportionally between paragraphs) plus a 10-second reference audio clip as style conditioning. It produces a 2-channel, 48 kHz full-length song with both vocal and accompaniment, supporting up to 150 seconds of output with diverse musical sections. The acoustic representation uses continuous latents from a stable-audio-vae autoencoder rather than discrete codec tokens, preserving high-frequency detail at a reduced frame rate synchronized to the 25 fps sketch sequence.

## Method

SongBloom unifies an autoregressive LLaMA-2-based sketch generator and a non-autoregressive DiT diffusion refiner into a single jointly-optimized model, where both stages share embedding layers and exchange information through hidden vectors. The key architectural insight is an interleaved generation paradigm: both the MuQ-derived sketch tokens (single VQ, codebook 16384) and the continuous acoustic latents are partitioned into fixed-size patches (16 frames ≈ 0.64 s), and the model alternates between generating one sketch patch (via causal AR with cross-entropy loss) and one acoustic patch (via full-attention DiT with Rectified Flow Matching), enabling bidirectional contextual exchange. The AR stage produces per-patch hidden vectors that condition the diffusion stage, while gradients backpropagate from the flow-matching loss through these vectors to the sketch generator. Classifier-free guidance is applied jointly to the hidden vector and sketch tokens, with additional masking on sketch tokens to encourage the hidden vector to carry richer semantic information. The total training objective is a weighted sum of the cross-entropy sketch loss and the flow-matching diffusion loss (λ=0.1), trained end-to-end on 100K hours of Chinese and English songs.

## Results

SongBloom-full-ft achieves the best objective performance across most metrics: PER of 5.49% (vs. 24.67% for Suno-v4.5 and 27.30% for YuE-7B), FAD of 3.20, and the highest aesthetic scores in 3 of 4 dimensions (CE 7.79, CU 7.96, PQ 8.47). SongBloom-full attains the highest MuLan Cycle Consistency (MCC 0.88) among all models, confirming superior style-prompt adherence. In subjective evaluation, SongBloom-full-ft leads on vocal musicality (3.91), vocal quality (3.95), and lyric correctness (3.42), while Suno-v4.5 retains a slight edge on accompaniment musicality (4.04 vs. 3.92) and accompaniment quality (3.96 vs. 3.93). Inference efficiency is competitive: SongBloom's RTF of 1.649 is comparable to SongEditor (1.717) and far lower than YuE-7B (13.724).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against YuE-7B (two cascaded LMs, 7B+1B), SongBloom-full-ft reduces PER from 27.30% to 5.49% and FAD from 5.99 to 3.20, while cutting RTF by roughly 8× (1.649 vs. 13.724), owing to the unified single-model design and patch-wise diffusion. Compared to DiffRhythm-full (pure diffusion, 1.1B), SongBloom improves PER (5.49% vs. 15.77%), FAD (3.20 vs. 5.04), and all four aesthetic scores, though DiffRhythm remains far faster in inference (RTF 0.034) due to its fully non-autoregressive architecture. Versus SongEditor (LM+Diffusion, 0.7B+1B), SongBloom achieves lower PER (5.49% vs. 16.20%), lower FAD (3.20 vs. 4.85), and higher MCC (0.86 vs. 0.77) at a similar RTF (1.649 vs. 1.717), with the advantage attributed to the interleaved paradigm that avoids feeding the entire semantic sequence into the diffusion stage. Against the commercial Suno-v4.5, SongBloom-full-ft wins on PER (5.49% vs. 24.67%), FAD (3.20 vs. 3.39), and three of four aesthetic scores, but Suno retains a narrow lead on accompaniment musicality (4.04 vs. 3.92) and accompaniment quality (3.96 vs. 3.93) in subjective MOS, suggesting Suno's stronger instrumental generation. Overall, SongBloom dominates all open-source baselines and is competitive with or superior to the leading commercial system on most metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

SongBloom sits at the intersection of two lines of work: (1) autoregressive diffusion models for continuous-valued generation (ARDiT, DiTAR), which demonstrated that discrete tokenization is unnecessary for AR generation, and (2) two-stage music generation frameworks (MusicLM, YuE, SongEditor) that separate semantic sketching from acoustic synthesis. The key departure is the interleaved generation paradigm: rather than producing the entire sketch sequence before any acoustic frames (as in all prior two-stage systems), SongBloom alternates sketch and acoustic patches within a single jointly-optimized model, treating sketch tokens as CoT-like prompts and allowing acoustic context to feed back into subsequent sketch planning. This unifies the AR and NAR components into one architecture with shared parameters, contrasting with cascaded LM+Diffusion (SongEditor) or dual-LM (YuE) designs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SongBloom demonstrates that a single autoregressive diffusion model can match or exceed state-of-the-art commercial systems (Suno-v4.5) on full-length lyric-to-song generation at 48 kHz, which is practically significant for the open-source music generation community. The interleaved paradigm offers a general architectural principle—bidirectional context exchange between coarse planning and fine synthesis—that could transfer to other long-form generation tasks. Open questions remain around interpretability of the SSL-based sketch representation (the authors note symbolic formats would enable finer user control) and the potential for RL-based alignment (DPO/PPO) to better match human aesthetic preferences. The 100K-hour proprietary training dataset and lack of released code limit immediate reproducibility.
