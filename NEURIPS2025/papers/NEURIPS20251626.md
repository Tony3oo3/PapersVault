## Problem

Masked diffusion models (MDMs) rely on hand-crafted mask schedules (linear, sine) for sampling, yet the theoretical principles governing schedule design—especially in few-step regimes—remain uncharacterized. The paper asks whether MDMs can be understood through the lens of optimal transport and energy minimization, and whether this perspective yields a principled, closed-form condition for optimal sampling schedules.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251626/cropped_page5_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on a pretrained MDM (LLaDA 8B) without any weight modification, taking only two scalar beta parameters (a, b) that parameterize the interpolation schedule γ_t as a Beta CDF. Because the MDM training loss is provably invariant to the mask schedule, schedule tuning is performed entirely post-hoc: the user selects (a, b) to minimize a task-specific energy landscape, and the corresponding optimal mask schedule α_t* = sin²(π/2 · γ_t) is applied at inference time to produce generated text sequences (code, math solutions, reasoning chains).

## Method

The paper proves that three natural energy functionals—kinetic energy, conditional kinetic energy, and geodesic energy—are mathematically equivalent under the absorbing Markov chain structure of MDMs, sharing the same minimizers over the mask schedule α_t. It then derives a closed-form optimal scheduling condition α_t* = sin²(π/2 · γ_t) linking the discrete mask schedule to the continuous geodesic interpolation schedule, and shows that under this condition MDMs simultaneously minimize all three energies, including the rate matrix despite the parametric constraint that α_t jointly governs both the probability flow and the transition rates. To make the theory practical, the interpolation schedule γ_t is parameterized as the CDF of a Beta(a,b) distribution, collapsing the infinite-dimensional schedule design space to a tractable 2D scalar search. This enables task-adaptive schedule tuning after training: different tasks (code generation, math reasoning) prefer different (a, b) values, and the resulting energy-inspired schedules outperform standard linear and sine baselines in low-step sampling settings.

## Results

On code generation benchmarks (MBPP and HumanEval), beta-parameterized schedules match the linear baseline's generation quality with a 2× reduction in sampling steps. On Hendrycks Math, the energy-inspired schedules achieve performance parity with the linear schedule using 4× fewer steps. On BBH and GSM8K, beta-parameterized schedules show comparable but not superior performance to the linear baseline. The gains are most pronounced in low-step sampling regimes (e.g., 8–32 steps), where hand-crafted schedules degrade more sharply.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three hand-crafted mask schedules—linear (α_t = t, from Austin et al. [8]), sine (α_t = sin(π/2·t), from Sahoo et al. [36]), and squared sine (α_t = sin²(π/2·t), from Han et al. [17])—against the proposed Beta-CDF parameterized schedules on LLaDA 8B. On MBPP and HumanEval, the best beta schedules match the linear baseline's pass@1 at roughly half the sampling steps, while the sine and squared-sine baselines degrade more quickly as steps decrease. On Hendrycks Math, the beta schedules reach the linear baseline's accuracy at 4× fewer steps, a margin not achieved by either the sine or squared-sine schedules. On BBH and GSM8K, all schedules (including the beta-parameterized ones) perform comparably, with no clear winner. The paper notes a systematic preference for convex interpolation schedules (i.e., allocating more computation to early unmasking steps) on code generation tasks, suggesting task-specific schedule tuning is beneficial.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies three previously separate threads: the geodesic interpretation of MDM conditional flows (Jo & Hwang, 2025), the kinetic-energy/optimal-transport framework for discrete probability flows (Shaul et al., 2024, developed for Discrete Flow Matching), and the concrete-score decomposition of MDM rate matrices (Ou et al., 2025). The key departure from the DFM kinetic-energy work is that MDMs couple the probability flow and rate matrix through a single mask schedule α_t, whereas DFM decouples them; the paper shows this coupling is not a limitation but actually preserves optimal transport structure. The Beta-CDF schedule parameterization is a practical contribution that subsumes previously hand-crafted linear, sine, and squared-sine schedules as special cases (Proposition 3.7).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical unification provides the first principled justification for mask schedule design in MDMs, replacing ad-hoc choices with an energy-minimization criterion, and the training-loss invariance to schedules enables post-hoc, zero-retraining schedule tuning—a practical advantage over continuous diffusion where schedule changes affect training. However, the empirical gains are task-dependent (strong on code and math, neutral on BBH/GSM8K), and the paper explicitly acknowledges that the mapping from task characteristics to optimal (a,b) parameters remains uncharacterized, leaving a significant open question for practitioners. The framework also opens the door to task-adaptive sampling in other discrete generative settings (protein, image tokens) where MDMs are increasingly deployed.
