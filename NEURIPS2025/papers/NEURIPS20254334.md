## Problem

The Iterative Markovian Fitting (IMF) procedure has largely superseded Iterative Proportional Fitting (IPF) for solving standard Schrödinger Bridge problems due to its superior properties (marginal preservation at each iteration, no trajectory caching, no forgetting of the reference measure), but no IMF-based approach existed for the tree-structured SB problem. The only prior neural ODE/SDE method for tree-structured costs, TreeDSB (Noble et al., 2023), relied on IPF and suffered from training instability at low entropy regularisation. This gap was particularly consequential for Wasserstein barycentre computation, where the star-shaped tree is the natural formulation and IPF-based solvers struggle to match marginals accurately.

## Input / Output

The method takes marginal distributions at a subset of tree vertices (the observed set S), the tree topology with edge lengths, and an entropy regularisation parameter ε. It produces a learned diffusion process on the tree whose marginal at the unobserved vertex (e.g., the centre of a star) gives a continuous barycentre approximation, from which new samples can be generated by simulating the SDEs. Unlike in-sample barycentre methods that represent the solution as a finite point set, TreeDSBM outputs a neural-parameterised continuous distribution, and unlike fixed-point methods it requires only bridge-matching (not full OT map computation) at each iteration.

## Method

TreeDSBM extends the IMF procedure to tree-structured costs by showing that the dynamic TreeSB solution is the unique Markov path measure that is a mixture of Brownian bridges conditioned on values at the observed vertices S. The algorithm alternates two projections: (1) a reciprocal projection that constructs a mixture of bridges by sampling the unobserved vertices from the tractable Gaussian conditional Q_{S^c|S} (derived from the precision matrix of the tree) and drawing Brownian bridges along each edge, and (2) a Markovian projection that trains a separate neural vector field per edge via a bridge-matching loss, exploiting a KL decomposition along the tree structure that factorises the objective into independent per-edge terms. For the star-shaped barycentre case, the conditional at the centre vertex reduces to a Gaussian centred at the weighted average of leaf values, making each IMF iteration a direct flow-based analogue of the classical fixed-point barycentre update. Bidirectional training (forward and backward SDEs per edge) mitigates marginal drift, and all edges train in parallel.

## Results

On the synthetic 2D barycentre task, TreeDSBM (6 IMF iterations) achieves a Sinkhorn divergence of 1.05–1.14 to the ground-truth barycentre, substantially outperforming TreeDSB (50 IPF iterations) which scores 2.35–4.04. On the subset posterior aggregation experiment (BW2²-UVP metric), TreeDSBM achieves 0.008% on Poisson and 0.012% on Negative Binomial, matching or beating all baselines. On the high-dimensional Gaussian experiment, TreeDSBM is competitive with WIN but trails W2CB and NOTWB on both BW2²-UVP and L2-UVP metrics (e.g., 0.27% vs 0.12% BW2²-UVP at d=128).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TreeDSBM decisively outperforms its IPF counterpart TreeDSB: on the synthetic 2D barycentre, TreeDSBM achieves Sinkhorn divergence ~1.1 vs 2.35–4.04 for TreeDSB, while requiring only 6 iterations vs 50. Against WIN, TreeDSBM wins on the Poisson subset posterior (0.008 vs 0.014 BW2²-UVP) but is slightly worse on Negative Binomial (0.012 vs 0.009); on the high-dimensional Gaussian, TreeDSBM is comparable to or slightly better than WIN on BW2²-UVP (e.g., 0.27 vs 0.38 at d=128) but worse on L2-UVP (1.23 vs 1.46). Against W2CB and NOTWB, TreeDSBM clearly wins on the subset posterior aggregation (0.008/0.012 vs 0.026/0.024 and 0.023/0.018) but loses on the high-dimensional Gaussian where W2CB and NOTWB achieve substantially lower BW2²-UVP (0.12/0.14 vs 0.27 at d=128) and L2-UVP (0.25/0.13 vs 1.23). Overall, TreeDSBM is most competitive in settings where the barycentre has complex structure (synthetic, subset posterior) and is outperformed by adversarial methods on simpler Gaussian targets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Iterative Markovian Fitting (IMF) procedure of Shi et al. (2023) and Peluchetti (2023) from the standard two-marginal Schrödinger Bridge to the tree-structured setting, providing the missing IMF counterpart to the IPF-based TreeDSB method of Noble et al. (2023). The key conceptual generalization is replacing single-edge bridge-matching (conditioned on two endpoints) with tree-wide bridge-matching (conditioned on values at a subset of vertices), leveraging the tractable Gaussian structure of the reference coupling. The paper also shows that the resulting TreeDSBM iterations for star-shaped trees are equivalent to a natural combination of the fixed-point barycentre scheme of Alvarez-Esteban et al. (2016) with flow-based entropic OT solvers, unifying two previously separate algorithmic traditions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TreeDSBM closes a clear gap in the literature (the IMF + TreeSB cell in Table 1) and demonstrates that flow-based barycentre solvers can be competitive with established continuous Wasserstein-2 methods (WIN, W2CB, NOTWB) while offering the stability advantages of IMF over IPF. The practical impact is significant for applications like subset posterior aggregation and image barycentres, where the method achieves comparable accuracy with substantially fewer iterations and less training time. Limitations include restriction to quadratic costs, entropic bias, expensive multi-step inference, and the need for multiple sequential IMF iterations; future work could explore extensions to general costs, improved architectures from the flow-matching literature, and sampling-free variants.
