## Problem

Pretrained flow models (e.g., FLUX) achieve high-quality generation with few steps but rely on deterministic ODE sampling, which prevents the intermediate-step particle sampling that makes inference-time reward alignment effective in diffusion models. This paper addresses how to enable efficient, training-free inference-time scaling for flow models so that they can generate images precisely aligned with complex prompts or user preferences.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251035/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt and a reward function (e.g., VQAScore for compositional text-image alignment, negated RSS for object-count accuracy) and operates entirely at inference time on a pretrained flow model with a fixed budget of 500 NFEs across 10 denoising steps. It outputs a single generated image that maximizes the reward while a KL-regularization term keeps the sample close to the pretrained model's data distribution. The reward can be differentiable or non-differentiable, and no fine-tuning of the base model is required.

## Method

The paper proposes a three-part inference-time framework for flow models. First, SDE conversion transforms the deterministic ODE sampling into a stochastic process by adding a Wiener noise term with schedule g_t = t² (scaled by 3), enabling particle sampling at intermediate denoising steps. Second, interpolant conversion uses a scale-time transformation to convert the linear interpolant (α_t = 1−t, σ_t = t) to a Variance-Preserving interpolant at inference time, which increases sample diversity by operating at consistently lower log-SNR and thus expanding the search space for high-reward samples. Third, Rollover Budget Forcing (RBF) adaptively reallocates the NFE budget across timesteps: if a particle with higher expected future reward is found within the per-step quota, the remaining NFEs roll over to subsequent steps rather than being wasted. The value function for particle selection is approximated via Tweedie's formula using the posterior mean, and the entire pipeline is model-agnostic and requires no retraining.

## Results

On compositional text-to-image generation (GenAI-Bench, 121 prompts), VP-SDE with Rollover Budget Forcing achieves the highest VQAScore (given reward) and InstructBLIP score (held-out reward) among all methods, with the ablation showing VP-SDE reaching VQAScore 0.925 and InstructBLIP 0.843 versus 0.788/0.789 for the base Linear-ODE. On quantity-aware image generation (T2I-CompBench++, 100 prompts), the method achieves a 4–6× improvement in object count accuracy over the base FLUX model, with RBF yielding the highest accuracy among all particle sampling strategies. Aesthetic scores remain comparable to the base model across all settings, confirming no significant quality degradation from the inference-time scaling.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against four inference-time scaling baselines: SoP [39] (the only prior flow-model method, using a forward kernel on the deterministic ODE), SMC/DAS [23] (importance-weighted particle sampling), CoDe [53] (blockwise selection every L steps), and SVDD [30] (per-step best-particle selection). The key finding is that SDE conversion alone (Linear-SDE) already surpasses both BoN and SoP across all particle sampling methods, since the deterministic ODE process collapses all particles to identical samples. VP-SDE further improves every method on both given and held-out rewards by expanding the search space through the lower log-SNR trajectory. Rollover Budget Forcing (RBF) provides additional gains over the fixed-budget variants of SMC, CoDe, and SVDD by adaptively reallocating NFEs to timesteps where higher-reward particles are harder to find. Overall, VP-SDE + RBF outperforms all four baselines on both compositional and quantity-aware tasks, with the largest margins over SoP (which is restricted to the deterministic ODE) and over fixed-budget SVDD/CoDe.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends particle-sampling-based inference-time scaling (SVDD, CoDe, SMC/DAS) from diffusion models to flow models, which had previously been limited to the deterministic Search over Paths (SoP) approach. It builds on the stochastic interpolant framework (Albergo et al.) to formalize ODE-to-SDE conversion at inference time, and repurposes interpolant conversion techniques from Shaul et al. (Bespoke Solvers) not for training stability or speed but for expanding the proposal distribution's search space. The Rollover Budget Forcing strategy departs from the uniform NFE allocation used in all prior particle sampling methods, motivated by empirical analysis showing that the compute needed to find a higher-reward sample varies significantly across timesteps.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As rectified flow models (FLUX, SD3, video generators) become the dominant architecture for high-quality image and video synthesis, the absence of practical inference-time scaling methods was a significant gap; this paper provides the first comprehensive toolkit (SDE conversion + VP interpolant + adaptive budget) that works with any pretrained flow model without retraining. The ablation revealing that diffusion coefficient scaling alone causes severe quality degradation (VQAScore 0.900→0.702) while the synergistic combination with timestep conversion preserves quality is a useful design principle. Open questions include systematic exploration of timestep scheduling and noise schedules, extension to video generation, and whether the approach scales to larger NFE budgets or different model families.
