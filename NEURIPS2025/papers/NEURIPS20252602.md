## Problem

Prior work established that agents robust to distribution shifts must encode causal models of their environment, but only for single-agent, unmediated tasks where the agent's actions cannot influence utility through environment states. Most real-world settings involve mediation (e.g., a robot's actions alter the environment it must subsequently reason about), multi-agent interactions, and sequential dynamics, leaving open whether the robustness–causality link still holds and how to recover the causal model in these richer settings.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes an optimal policy oracle that maps any mixture of local interventions to the corresponding optimal decision policy, a fully specified utility function, and a partial CID (known node partition, known parents of D and Ch_D, known CPTs of D's children, and any prior causal edges). It outputs the complete CID graph over all ancestors of the utility node plus the CPTs for every chance node in that subgraph; when applied to a POMDP unrolled over two timesteps, it additionally yields the state-transition function. Notably, no sample data or joint distribution is available—the causal structure is identified purely through oracle queries under constructed distribution shifts.

## Method

LearnCID iteratively processes chance nodes along directed paths to the utility node, and for each node X constructs a family of interventions that fix all other chance nodes while varying one candidate parent. It invokes the ALG q_crit subroutine to find the mixture coefficient at which two decisions become equally optimal, then uses that coefficient together with the known utility function to compute interventional conditional distributions P(x | pa_X; σ) via closed-form expressions (Equations 5–6). A candidate parent is confirmed as a true causal edge if varying it across interventions produces distinct interventional distributions for X; the observational CPT is then recovered from the interventional one. The key theoretical extension is a new inductive case (Case 3) for nodes in Anc_U(G_D) ∩ Desc_D that exist only under mediation, and a negative result (Theorem 3) showing that children of D are generally unidentifiable, which motivates the assumption that D's children and their CPTs are known a priori. The framework extends to multi-agent CIDs by converting secondary decision nodes to chance nodes with faithful policies, and to POMDPs by unrolling two timesteps and applying the same procedure to recover intra- and inter-temporal causal links.

## Results

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical experiments or quantitative benchmarks. The main results are three theorems: Theorem 1 establishes that Assumption A9 (no universally optimal decision) implies domain dependence in mediated CIDs; Theorem 2 proves that for almost all CIDs satisfying Assumptions A1–A9, the full causal graph structure and joint distribution over all ancestors of the utility node can be identified from optimal policy oracles; Theorem 3 shows that in the mediated case, the children of the decision node cannot in general be uniquely determined, motivating Assumption A4. The LearnCID algorithm is shown to correctly recover the CID structure and CPTs under these assumptions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not include empirical comparisons or baselines, as its contributions are entirely theoretical. The closest comparison is with Richens and Everitt (ICLR 2024), whose framework is restricted to single-agent, unmediated tasks. The present paper extends their results by (i) relaxing the no-mediation assumption, (ii) showing that the children of the decision node become non-identifiable in the mediated case (Theorem 3), and (iii) extending the framework to multi-agent CIDs and POMDPs. No method is benchmarked against another; the contribution is a proof of identifiability and an algorithm (LearnCID) whose correctness is established via Theorem 2.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends Richens and Everitt (2024), who proved that agents robust to distribution shifts must have learned the causal model of their environment, but only under the restrictive no-mediation assumption (agent actions cannot affect utility via environment states). The authors generalize this result to mediated decision tasks, where an agent's actions causally influence the environment, and further extend the framework to multi-agent CIDs and sequential decision-making modeled as POMDPs. The core algorithmic machinery (computing q_crit via mixtures of local interventions) is inherited from Richens and Everitt, while the new contribution is the LearnCID algorithm that handles the additional causal paths introduced by mediation and the structural identifiability limitations it creates (Theorem 3).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The no-mediation assumption is violated in virtually all real-world agent settings (autonomous vehicles, industrial robots, multi-agent coordination), so removing it is a necessary step toward making the 'robust agents encode causal knowledge' result practically relevant. The multi-agent extension shows that a single robust agent can enable recovery of the full causal model and derivation of optimal policies for other agents, which has implications for knowledge transfer in multi-agent systems. Key limitations include the idealized optimal policy oracle assumption, the faithfulness and causal sufficiency requirements, and the inability to identify children of the decision node without prior knowledge (Assumption A4). Open questions include whether approximate oracles suffice, how the results extend to non-time-homogeneous POMDPs, and whether the framework can be made operational without access to a true optimal policy oracle.
