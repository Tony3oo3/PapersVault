## Problem

Existing NeRF-based methods either assume a clear-air medium (discarding ~93% of the 3D volume) or model scattering only along the horizontal line of sight without explicitly disentangling object geometry from the intervening medium. This object-centric sampling and anisotropic radiative modeling prevent metric-preserving perception of the full 3D space and limit physical interpretability of the reconstructed scene.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252792/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view RGB images captured under degraded conditions (underwater, hazy, or low-light) along with known camera poses, and optionally a pseudo-depth map from a pretrained Depth Anything V2 model for geometric supervision. In low-light settings, a Bright Channel Prior (BCP) transmittance map provides an additional per-pixel supervision signal. Outputs include a synthesized novel view, a decomposed clean object radiance J, a backscattered radiance component, horizontal depth maps, a vertical downwelling depth estimate (e.g., water depth in meters), and learned per-channel medium attenuation and scattering coefficients.

## Method

I²-NeRF introduces a general radiative formulation that reinterprets media degradation as a physically grounded extension of image matting, where the blending coefficient becomes a Beer-Lambert transmittance: I = J·exp(−σ^attn·z) + B·(1−exp(−σ^scat·z)), unifying haze (σ^attn=σ^scat), underwater (σ^attn≠σ^scat), and low-light (B=0) conditions under a single particle model with separate object and media density fields. A reverse-stratified upsampling (RSU) strategy allocates additional ray-marching samples in low-object-density regions using the inverse of object density as a sampling weight, enforcing near-uniform spatial coverage and preserving isometry. To achieve isotropy, the model explicitly computes downwelling attenuation at vertical depth z^Φ for each ray, applying the Beer-Lambert law uniformly in both horizontal and vertical directions so that the medium color c^med = Φ·exp(−(σ^attn+σ^scat)·z^Φ) encodes depth-dependent spectral darkening. Training combines a RawNeRF-style reconstruction loss, a pseudo-depth geometry loss, a compensated SSIM loss over stochastic patches, a mutual-exclusion loss preventing co-located object and media densities, and a media transmittance regularization, all optimized on a ZipNeRF hash-encoding backbone with 64 object and 32 media samples per ray.

## Results

On the LOM low-light dataset, I²-NeRF achieves SOTA PSNR across all five scenes, reaching 22.87 (bike), 22.35 (buu), 23.82 (chair), 19.44 (shrub), and 25.89 (sofa), outperforming the strongest GS-based baseline LITA-GS by 0.1–5.5 dB. On the SeaThru-NeRF underwater/hazy dataset, the method delivers competitive results, achieving 32.70 PSNR on Curaçao (best overall) and 27.33 on IUI3 Red Sea, while remaining within ~2.5 dB of Watersplatting on the remaining scenes. A key physical result is the accurate estimation of downwelling water depth (7.2 m predicted vs. 7.5 m ground truth) in a real Okinawa scene, a capability absent in all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the LOM low-light benchmark, I²-NeRF outperforms LITA-GS [84] (the strongest GS-based baseline) by 0.12 dB on bike, 1.76 dB on buu, 1.22 dB on chair, 0.09 dB on shrub, and a substantial 5.46 dB on sofa, while surpassing Aleth-NeRF [16] by 2.4–6.4 dB across all scenes. On the SeaThru-NeRF underwater/hazy dataset, the method is competitive but not uniformly dominant: it achieves the best PSNR on Curaçao (32.70 vs. Watersplatting's 32.20) and IUI3 Red Sea (27.33 vs. 29.84 for Watersplatting, though Watersplatting still leads), and trails Watersplatting [46] by 0.07–2.5 dB on the remaining scenes. Against SeaThru-NeRF [45], I²-NeRF wins on Curaçao (+1.74 dB), IUI3 Red Sea (+0.57 dB), and J.G. Red Sea (+0.83 dB) but loses slightly on Hazy Fern (−0.16 dB) and Panama (+0.27 dB). The hybrid 2D-enhancement pipelines (SCI+NeRF, IAT+NeRF) are substantially outperformed, confirming that end-to-end volumetric modeling with physical priors is superior to decoupled 2D restoration followed by 3D reconstruction. A unique advantage over all baselines is the explicit estimation of vertical downwelling depth, validated against a diving computer reading (7.2 m vs. 7.5 m actual), which no competing method provides.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the scattering-aware NeRF line (SeaThru-NeRF, ScatterNeRF, DehazeNeRF) by explicitly disentangling object geometry from volumetric media through a reverse-stratified upsampling strategy, and by adding downwelling (vertical) attenuation to make the radiative model isotropic. It builds on ZipNeRF's hash-based encoding for efficiency, adopts the RUIF underwater image formation model for wavelength-dependent coefficients, and repurposes the Bright Channel Prior as a physically grounded transmittance prior for low-light settings. The key conceptual departure from prior work is treating low-light, haze, and underwater degradation as instances of a single Beer-Lambert-governed particle model, rather than separate ad-hoc formulations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified radiative formulation provides a physically interpretable framework that simultaneously recovers clean object radiance, backscattered light, and metric depth (including vertical water depth) from a single set of multi-view images, which prior scattering-aware NeRFs could not do. The ability to estimate real-world depth (7.2 m vs. 7.5 m ground truth) opens the door to metric 3D perception in media environments relevant to underwater robotics and AR. Key limitations include the static-media assumption, NeRF's slower training versus rasterization-based methods, and the view-dependent encoding of downwelling depth under scattering, which may not generalize to dynamic or highly non-homogeneous media.
