## Problem

While Mamba's linear-complexity architecture offers a promising alternative to ViT's quadratic self-attention for high-resolution remote sensing, existing Mamba-based RS studies are confined to supervised training on small, domain-specific datasets. No prior work has established whether Mamba-based RS foundation models follow data and parameter scaling laws, nor has autoregressive pretraining been adapted to handle RS-specific challenges such as sparsely distributed objects, arbitrary orientations, and extreme scale variations.

## Input / Output

RoMA takes RGB remote sensing images (196×196 during pretraining, scalable to 4096×4096 at inference) and divides them into 16×16 non-overlapping patches. The Mamba encoder processes all patches sequentially to produce key-value representations, and a decoder with causal masking autoregressively predicts the next token's pixel values. The pretrained encoder outputs dense feature representations that transfer to downstream tasks (scene classification, change detection, semantic segmentation) via linear probing or fine-tuning with standard decoders (UperNet, UNet).

## Method

RoMA extends the iGPT/ARM autoregressive pretraining pipeline with two RS-specific innovations. First, the Adaptive Rotation Encoding Strategy (ARES) uses LBP feature descriptors to identify high-information patches, expands them to larger regions (96→64→32 px), applies random rotation with learnable angular embeddings, and center-crops to the inscribed square—injecting directional priors that encourage rotation-invariant representations without explicit angle supervision. Second, the Multi-scale Prediction Strategy concatenates token representations from decoder blocks at coarser spatial scales (4×, 6×) alongside the base 16×16 patch, computing a weighted MSE loss (λ=0.1) that forces the model to capture objects across the extreme size range in RS imagery. Unlike MAE, RoMA encodes all patches through the Mamba encoder (preserving sequential order) and predicts the next token via KV-cache-based cross-attention with causal masking, aligning naturally with Mamba's unidirectional scanning mechanism.

## Results

On scene classification, RoMA (Mamba-B, 85M) achieves 87.36% OA on AID and 59.45% OA on UCM (TR=50%), surpassing the strongest ViT-based RS pretraining baselines (MA3E: 85.86/55.69; SatMAE++: 85.98/55.72). On change detection (OSCD), RoMA reaches 55.63 F1, outperforming SatMAE++ (53.10) and ARM (47.28). On semantic segmentation (SpaceNetv1), RoMA achieves 79.50 mF1, comparable to RVSA (79.56) and slightly below SatMAE++ (79.21). In terms of efficiency, RoMA-Base reduces peak GPU memory by 78.9% (6,526 MB vs. 24,531 MB) and is 1.56× faster (11.43 vs. 4.99 samples/sec) than ViT-Base at 1248×1248 resolution, while scaling stably to 4096×4096 where ViT-Base runs out of memory.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RoMA consistently outperforms all 16 baselines on scene classification: it beats the strongest natural-image pretraining method MAE (ViT-B) by 3.15 points on AID and 6.70 on UCM, and surpasses the best RS-specific pretraining methods MA3E (ViT-B) by 1.50/3.76 and SatMAE++ (ViT-L, 307M) by 1.38/3.73. Against the direct Mamba autoregressive baseline ARM (Mamba-B), RoMA gains 6.22 points on AID, 9.04 on UCM, 8.35 F1 on OSCD, and 1.61 mF1 on SpaceNetv1, validating the contribution of the rotation-aware and multi-scale strategies. On change detection, RoMA (55.63 F1) surpasses all prior methods including SatMAE++ (53.10) and CACo (52.11). On semantic segmentation, RoMA (79.50 mF1) is essentially tied with RVSA (79.56) and slightly trails SatMAE++ (79.21), which the authors attribute to the patch-level autoregressive objective being less suited to pixel-level tasks. On the iSAID small-object segmentation benchmark, RoMA-B achieves 67.4 mIoU, edging out RingMo (67.2) and significantly beating ViT-B+RVSA (63.8), with the largest margin on the Small Vehicle class (53.7 vs. 51.9). Overall, RoMA wins or ties on every reported metric across all four downstream tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

RoMA extends the autoregressive pretraining paradigm from iGPT (Chen et al., ICML 2020) and ARM (Ren et al., ICLR 2025) to the remote sensing domain, adapting it to the Mamba state-space architecture. It deliberately departs from the dominant MAE-based pretraining paradigm that underpins most existing RS foundation models (SatMAE, ScaleMAE, SatMAE++, MA3E), arguing that masking disrupts the sequential token dependencies that Mamba's linear scanning mechanism is designed to exploit. The two RS-specific innovations—adaptive rotation encoding with LBP-based region selection and multi-scale token prediction—address challenges (arbitrary object orientations, extreme scale variation) that prior autoregressive or MIM approaches for natural images do not consider.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RoMA provides the first systematic evidence that Mamba-based RS foundation models follow data and parameter scaling laws, a property previously established only for ViT+MAE. The practical impact is significant: at 1248×1248 resolution, Mamba-Base reduces GPU memory by 78.9% and accelerates inference by 1.56× over ViT-Base while matching or exceeding accuracy, and it scales to 4096×4096 inputs where ViT hits OOM. Key limitations include evaluation restricted to optical imagery (no SAR or hyperspectral), incomplete Mamba-Large results due to insufficient training epochs, and modest gains on pixel-level segmentation tasks. Open questions include whether the scaling trend holds at truly large model sizes (1B+ parameters) and whether the framework generalizes to multi-sensor and multi-temporal RS data.
