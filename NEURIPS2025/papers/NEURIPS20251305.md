## Problem

Unsupervised domain adaptation for histopathology image classification is hindered by the fact that existing stain normalization and GAN-based translation methods either require paired data or carefully selected reference images, and they tend to lose fine-grained structural details critical for cancer diagnosis. The paper addresses how to synthesize realistic target-domain images from source-domain inputs without paired data while preserving the discriminative features needed by downstream classifiers.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251305/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes labeled source-domain histopathology patches and unlabeled target-domain patches as input, along with two pre-trained DDPMs (one per domain). It produces target-domain-style images (T_S) that carry the source labels, which are then used to fine-tune a target classifier (C_T). The stain-guided reference images are constructed on-the-fly via Beer-Lambert decomposition (cross-domain stain color matrix × density map), so no fixed reference image or paired data is needed. The only tunable hyperparameter governing the translation is the noise timestep k (default 600 out of 1000 total steps).

## Method

SGCD employs a dual-diffusion architecture with a two-stage cyclic conversion (S→T→S and T→S→T). In the first stage, source images are noised to timestep k and denoised by the target-domain diffusion model D_T (and vice versa), with each reverse step guided by a stain reference image built from the cross-domain stain color matrix and density map via the Beer-Lambert law. In the second stage, the translated images are noised again and denoised back to the original domain, and a Stain-Guided Consistency (SGC) loss enforces reconstruction fidelity plus classifier task constraints in both directions. Training alternates between fine-tuning the two diffusion models (with SGC loss) and fine-tuning the target classifier C_T (with cross-entropy on T_S plus an MMD term aligning C_T's features on T_S and real target images T). This cyclic, stain-guided design eliminates the need for paired data or fixed reference images while preserving class-level semantics and fine-grained pathological structure.

## Results

On Camelyon17 (C1 as source, C2–C5 as targets), SGCD achieves an average AUC of 94.0%, outperforming the next-best method SPA (92.1%) and BCD-net (90.3%). On the balanced Camelyon17-WILDS benchmark, SGCD reaches 94.7% accuracy and 98.6% AUC, marginally trailing Connect Later (95.0% ACC, 98.7% AUC) but surpassing all medical-imaging-specific baselines. On Camelyon16, SGCD attains 95.8% AUC, exceeding StainNet (93.5%) by 2.3 points. On MITOS & ATYPIA14, SGCD achieves SSIM 0.88 and PSNR 27.5 dB, substantially outperforming StainGAN (SSIM 0.71, PSNR 17.1 dB).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SGCD outperforms all medical-imaging-specific baselines on Camelyon17: it leads SPA (92.1%) by 1.9 points, BCD-net (90.3%) by 3.7 points, and ContriMix (90.0%) by 4.0 points in average AUC. On the balanced Camelyon17-WILDS set, SGCD (94.7% ACC, 98.6% AUC) is essentially tied with the general UDA method Connect Later (95.0% ACC, 98.7% AUC) and slightly edges out ContriMix (94.6% ACC) and STRAP (93.7% ACC, 98.1% AUC). On Camelyon16, SGCD (95.8% AUC) surpasses the best GAN-based method StainNet (93.5%) by 2.3 points and StainGAN (90.5%) by 5.3 points. For image quality on MITOS & ATYPIA14, SGCD's SSIM of 0.88 and PSNR of 27.5 dB far exceed StainGAN (0.71 / 17.1 dB) and all classical stain normalization methods (SSIM ≤ 0.66, PSNR ≤ 13.6 dB). The only clear loss is the marginal 0.3-point ACC gap to Connect Later on WILDS, which the authors attribute to Connect Later's tailored augmentation strategy.

## Relation to Prior Work

SGCD builds on the DDPM framework (Ho et al., 2020) and the condition-guided reverse process introduced by Gao et al. (2023), but departs from both by replacing the Canny-edge guidance with a stain-decomposition-based reference (via Beer-Lambert law) and by employing a dual-diffusion cyclic architecture rather than a single model. It positions itself against GAN-based stain transfer methods (StainGAN, StainNet, MultiPathGAN, HistAuGAN, ContriMix) that suffer from mode collapse and semantic inconsistency, and against prior diffusion-based approaches (BBDM, A-Bridge, StainDiff) that require paired data or auxiliary networks. The key conceptual departure is the bidirectional cycle constraint combined with a stain-guided consistency loss, which eliminates the need for paired images or fixed reference images while preserving discriminative pathology features.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SGCD offers a practical, reference-free solution to the domain shift problem in histopathology that is directly applicable in clinical settings where paired data and expert-selected reference images are unavailable. The demonstrated robustness to extreme data scarcity (87.5% AUC with only 1% target data) and to non-stain degradations (blur, noise) broadens its applicability beyond pure stain variation. However, the method is limited to binary H&E classification, incurs significant computational overhead from dual diffusion models, and the improvements over strong baselines like SPA (94.0 vs. 92.1 average AUC on Camelyon17) are modest. Open questions include whether the stain-guided diffusion paradigm extends to multi-class tasks, cross-stain-protocol adaptation (e.g., H&E to IHC), and cross-organ domain shifts where morphological differences are more pronounced than staining differences.
