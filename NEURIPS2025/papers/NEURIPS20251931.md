## Problem

Current video generation models operate purely in 2D pixel or latent space and rely on temporal attention for cross-frame coherence, lacking explicit mechanisms to model 3D scene structure. This results in temporally inconsistent geometries, shape deformation, structure flickering, and implausible object interactions. The paper addresses this gap by asking how to inject structured 3D geometric supervision directly into the video generation process without sacrificing visual fidelity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251931/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts text prompts (T2V) or a single reference image (I2V) and generates 81-frame videos at 768×1360 resolution. Beyond the standard RGB output, it simultaneously produces per-frame depth maps, per-frame camera intrinsics and extrinsics (SE(3) poses), and a motion probability map for dynamic scenes. Depth is encoded into the same VAE latent space as RGB via channel-wise concatenation, so the diffusion process jointly denoises both modalities from a single latent sequence.

## Method

GeoVideo augments a CogVideoX-5B (DiT + 3D VAE) backbone with a dual-modality RGB-D latent representation, initialized via a ControlNet-style scheme that zero-pads the input projection for the depth channel and duplicates the output projection, preserving pretrained RGB behavior at initialization. The core novelty is a geometric regularization loss: predicted per-frame depths are backprojected into world coordinates using predicted camera poses, aggregated into a global point cloud, and reprojected back to each frame to enforce cross-frame depth consistency (penalizing reprojection discrepancies within a tolerance threshold). For dynamic scenes, a motion probability head identifies moving pixels and restricts alignment to points of similar motion probability in adjacent frames. Training proceeds in two stages: first, RGB-D joint generation with a gradually ramped depth loss weight; second, addition of the geometric consistency loss (λ_geo = 0.5) on top of the diffusion objectives, using v-prediction throughout.

## Results

On the primary video generation task (Table 3), GeoVideo outperforms CogVideoX-5B on all VBench metrics: for T2V, it achieves CLIPScore 34.25 vs. 32.30, FVD 122.7 vs. 145.3, Subject Consistency 97.2 vs. 93.8, and Spatial Relationship 90.3 vs. 79.4; for I2V, it reaches CLIPScore 35.02, FVD 120.5, and VISC 97.6. On the text-to-3D scene generation benchmark (Table 2), GeoVideo achieves the best FID (72.78), highest CLIPScore (33.84), lowest NIQE (8.53), and lowest BRISQUE (36.41) among all compared methods. For multi-view geometric consistency (Table 1), the full model scores MVCS 77.2 and Reprojection Error 2.52, substantially exceeding the base CogVideoX-5B (61.2 / 4.58).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CogVideoX-5B (the base model), GeoVideo wins on every reported metric in both T2V and I2V settings, with the largest gains in Spatial Relationship (+10.9 T2V, +10.6 I2V) and FVD (−22.6 T2V, −19.3 I2V), confirming that geometric regularization primarily improves structural and spatial coherence. In the text-to-3D scene generation comparison (Table 2), GeoVideo outperforms all three external baselines: it achieves FID 72.78 vs. LucidDreamer's 79.96, Director3D's 90.20, and SplatFlow's 86.77, while also scoring the highest CLIPScore (33.84 vs. 31.25/30.04/31.42) and the lowest NIQE (8.53 vs. 11.23/13.79/14.15) and BRISQUE (36.41 vs. 44.52/51.67/48.85). The margin over Director3D is the largest (FID gap of ~17 points), while the gap over LucidDreamer and SplatFlow is more moderate (~7 points). Ablation rows in Table 3 further show that removing the geometric loss L_geo costs ~1.0 CLIPScore and ~10 SR points, and removing the motion probability map costs an additional ~0.4 CLIPScore, indicating both components contribute meaningfully.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends latent diffusion video generation (specifically CogVideoX-5B with its 3D VAE and DiT backbone) by jointly modeling RGB and depth in a shared latent space, following the modality-compatibility insight from Marigold and DepthCrafter. It departs from prior multi-modal video diffusion work (OmniVDiff, IDOL, WVD) by introducing an explicit cross-frame geometric consistency loss that lifts per-frame depths into a global 3D point cloud and enforces multi-view reprojection alignment — a supervision signal absent in all prior joint video-depth generators. The dual-modality parameter initialization borrows the zero-padded extension strategy from ControlNet, and camera pose prediction is inspired by VGGT, but the geometric regularization loss itself is novel in the video generation context.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that a simple depth-reprojection loss can substantially improve 3D structural coherence in generated videos — enabling successful SfM reconstruction where prior models fail — this work provides a practical bridge between 2D video synthesis and 3D world modeling. The approach is modular (pluggable onto existing DiT-based video generators) and the 2-stage training recipe is straightforward to adopt. Open questions include whether depth is the optimal geometric proxy (vs. normals, 3D Gaussians, or point clouds), how the method scales to longer or highly dynamic sequences, and whether the geometric loss can be made differentiable through the VAE decoder to avoid the need for a separate lightweight depth head.
