## Problem

Diffusion models trained on long-tailed (class-imbalanced) datasets produce low-diversity, low-quality samples for tail classes due to feature borrowing from head classes. While this degradation is well-documented, its root cause was poorly understood: the paper identifies that tail-class latent representations in the U-Net bottleneck overlap heavily with head-class representations (representation entanglement), and that this is driven by relative class imbalance rather than merely limited per-class data. Prior remedies operated in ambient image space or external latent spaces, leaving the internal denoising network's feature space unaddressed.

## Input / Output

The model takes a class label as conditioning input and generates synthetic RGB images at 32×32 (CIFAR-10/100-LT) or 64×64 (CelebA-5, ImageNet-LT) resolution. During training, noisy images at random diffusion timesteps pass through the U-Net, and bottleneck features are extracted for the contrastive objective; class labels are randomly dropped with probability p_uncond to support classifier-free guidance. At inference, the projection head is discarded entirely, so sampling uses the standard U-Net with CFG and incurs no additional computational cost.

## Method

CORAL augments the denoising U-Net with a lightweight projection head (a single dense layer followed by normalization) applied to the bottleneck output. The projected embeddings are trained with a supervised contrastive loss (SupCon) that pulls same-class representations together and pushes different-class ones apart, directly countering the latent-space entanglement that degrades tail-class generation. This contrastive term is combined with the standard diffusion noise-prediction loss via a time-dependent weighting function λ(t) = w·exp((1−t/T)/τ_r), which emphasizes contrastive regularization at early (less noisy) timesteps where semantic structure is most recoverable. The key distinction from prior work is that CORAL regularizes the internal feature space of the diffusion model itself—precisely where representation entanglement arises—rather than imposing separation constraints on generated images in ambient space. Because the projection head is only used during training, the learned class-separated bottleneck structure persists at inference with zero additional overhead.

## Results

CORAL achieves the best FID across all four long-tailed benchmarks: 5.32 on CIFAR10-LT (ρ=0.01), 11.03 on CIFAR10-LT (ρ=0.001), 5.37 on CIFAR100-LT (ρ=0.01), and 8.12 on CelebA-5, outperforming DDPM, CBDM, and T2H on every metric. On the large-scale ImageNet-LT benchmark (1,000 classes), CORAL reaches FID 16.11, IS 24.17, and Recall 0.48, surpassing the next-best method (T2H) by 2.48 FID points and 0.04 recall. CORAL also improves on balanced CIFAR-10 (FID 3.30 vs. 3.84 for DDPM) and CIFAR-100 (FID 2.86 vs. 3.91), indicating the contrastive latent regularization benefits generation even without class imbalance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CORAL outperforms all three baselines (DDPM, CBDM, T2H) on every metric across all four long-tailed datasets. Against DDPM, CORAL reduces FID by 0.85 on CIFAR10-LT (ρ=0.01), by 2.02 on CIFAR100-LT, and by 2.16 on CelebA-5, while also improving Recall by 0.07 in each case. Against CBDM, CORAL achieves lower FID (5.32 vs. 5.62 on CIFAR10-LT ρ=0.01; 5.37 vs. 6.02 on CIFAR100-LT) and higher Recall (0.59 vs. 0.57), with the gap widening under extreme imbalance (ρ=0.001: FID 11.03 vs. 12.74). Against T2H, CORAL's advantage is most pronounced on ImageNet-LT, where T2H's head-to-tail transfer degrades FID to 18.59 while CORAL reaches 16.11, and on CIFAR10-LT (ρ=0.01) where T2H's FID of 7.01 is actually worse than vanilla DDPM (6.17), whereas CORAL improves to 5.32. The paper also shows in Appendix B that an ambient-space contrastive baseline (analogous to DiffROP) degrades below DDPM on ImageNet-LT (FID 24.73 vs. 17.08), while CORAL's latent-space intervention maintains consistent gains, supporting the claim that latent-space regularization scales better than ambient-space constraints as class count grows.

## Relation to Prior Work

CORAL builds on the DDPM denoising framework and the supervised contrastive loss (SupCon) from metric learning, but departs from prior long-tailed diffusion methods (CBDM, T2H, DiffROP) that apply regularization in the ambient image space or rely on head-to-tail knowledge transfer. The key conceptual departure is identifying the U-Net bottleneck as the locus of class representation failure and applying contrastive regularization directly there via a lightweight projection head, rather than constraining outputs post-hoc. This positions CORAL as a latent-space intervention that complements rather than replaces the diffusion objective, drawing on the broader contrastive representation learning literature (SimCLR, SupCon) but applying it to an internal layer of a generative model rather than a standalone encoder.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a useful diagnostic—representation entanglement in the bottleneck—that explains *why* tail-class generation fails beyond the obvious data-scarcity narrative, and shows that latent-space contrastive regularization outperforms ambient-space approaches, particularly at scale (ImageNet-LT, where ambient-space contrastive actually degrades below baseline DDPM). Limitations include added training compute, evaluation restricted to class-conditional generation on relatively small images (32×32 to 64×64), and no error bars across seeds. Open questions include whether the bottleneck-level contrastive signal transfers to large text-to-image models (e.g., Stable Diffusion) and parameter-efficient fine-tuning settings like LoRA, which the authors flag as promising but untested directions.
