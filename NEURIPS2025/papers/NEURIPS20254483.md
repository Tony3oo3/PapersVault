## Problem

Masked diffusion models (MDMs) face a fundamental training challenge: unlike autoregressive models where prediction difficulty is uniform, MDM denoising difficulty varies with the positions of unmasked tokens, making learning inconsistent. While adaptive sampling strategies can select simpler unmasking orders at inference time, no prior work encourages these efficient sampling-path characteristics during training, leaving a gap between the model's learned representations and the optimal generation trajectory.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254483/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a discrete token sequence (e.g., a Countdown problem prompt or a partially filled Sudoku grid linearized into 81 digit tokens) and partitions it into K non-overlapping subsequences of fixed length L (e.g., L=8 for Countdown, L=9 for Sudoku). During training, each subsequence independently receives a randomly sampled noise level t_k ∈ [0,1], so the model sees a heterogeneous masking pattern across the sequence. At inference, the model starts from a fully masked sequence and iteratively unmask tokens, with each subsequence's effective timestep dynamically updated as t_k = 1 − n_k/L based on its current count of unmasked tokens.

## Method

SPMDM partitions the input sequence into K fixed-length, non-overlapping subsequences and applies a distinct noise scale t_k to each subsequence in the forward masking process, so that at any training step different subsequences are at different noise levels. This design simultaneously encourages the model to learn token-level dependencies within each subsequence (local neighborhood generation) and logical dependencies across subsequences (reasoning-order generation), addressing two identified characteristics of efficient sampling paths. The transformer architecture is augmented with K per-subsequence timestep embeddings (rather than a single global timestep) to explicitly encode each subsequence's noise level. The training objective is a sum of per-subsequence NELBO terms, where each subsequence's denoising loss is conditioned on the noisy states of all other subsequences. At sampling time, each subsequence's timestep is dynamically updated based on its unmasked token count, and the method is compatible with adaptive sampling oracles for further quality gains.

## Results

On Countdown (the hardest subtask CD5 with 5 input digits), SPMDM (85M) achieves 46.1% accuracy, substantially outperforming GPT-2 85M (5.1%) and LLaMA 13B (7.4%), and matching or exceeding the best discrete diffusion baselines (MGDM 46.6%, MDLM 41.2%). On Sudoku, SPMDM (85M) reaches 100.0% accuracy versus 92.3% for MDLM and 22.4% for GPT-2 85M; even the 6M-parameter SPMDM achieves 99.9%. On GSM8K (355M parameters), SPMDM attains 56.4% accuracy, outperforming MDLM (54.1%), SEDD (53.5%), and DiffuGPT (52.6%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SPMDM clearly dominates all autoregressive baselines: on Countdown CD5 it achieves 46.1% versus 5.1% (GPT-2 85M) and 7.4% (LLaMA 13B), and on Sudoku it reaches 100.0% versus 22.4% (GPT-2 85M) and 41.2% (LLaMA 13B). Among discrete diffusion models, SPMDM outperforms MDLM on CD4 (92.7% vs. 85.8%), CD5 (46.1% vs. 41.2%), and Sudoku (100.0% vs. 92.3%), and beats SEDD and D3PM by large margins on all problem-solving tasks. Against MGDM, the closest competitor, SPMDM wins on CD4 (92.7% vs. 91.5%) and Sudoku (100.0% vs. 99.9%) but trails slightly on CD5 (46.1% vs. 46.6%). BDLM, which uses a semi-autoregressive left-to-right block strategy, is consistently outperformed by SPMDM on Countdown (e.g., 92.7% vs. 85.5% on CD4) and Sudoku (100.0% vs. 92.1%), confirming that the flexible any-order sampling of SPMDM is more effective than rigid sequential decoding. On broader reasoning benchmarks (Table 4), SPMDM at 355M parameters achieves the best GSM8K score (56.4%) among all discrete diffusion baselines, surpassing DiffuGPT (52.6%) and MDLM (54.1%), while remaining competitive on commonsense reasoning tasks like HellaSwag and Winogrande.

## Relation to Prior Work

SPMDM builds directly on the MDLM framework (Sahoo et al., 2024) for masked diffusion language modeling and the adaptive sampling strategy of Kim et al. (2025), which identified that simpler token-unmasking orders improve generation quality. The key departure is moving from inference-time path selection to training-time path encoding: by partitioning sequences into fixed-length subsequences and assigning each a distinct noise scale, SPMDM bakes the two desirable path characteristics (local-neighborhood priority and logical-order priority) into the model's learned representations. This contrasts with BDLM (Arriola et al., 2025), which achieves similar subsequence structure but imposes a rigid left-to-right block decoding order, and with MGDM (Ye et al., 2024), which targets reasoning but does not explicitly structure the noise schedule to encourage ordered denoising.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a simple training-time modification—differing noise scales per subsequence—can substantially improve MDM performance on structured reasoning tasks (near-perfect Sudoku with 6M parameters) suggests that much of the gap between MDMs and ARMs on planning tasks may stem from suboptimal sampling paths rather than architectural limitations. The approach is computationally free relative to MDLM (Table 5), making it an attractive drop-in improvement. Open questions include whether the fixed-length, non-overlapping partitioning is optimal (vs. variable-length or overlapping blocks), how the method scales to very long sequences where K becomes large, and whether the independence assumption between token dimensions limits performance on tasks with strong long-range dependencies. The modest gains on broader reasoning benchmarks (GSM8K, commonsense) relative to the dramatic gains on Countdown/Sudoku suggest the benefit is most pronounced when the task has clear sequential logical structure.
