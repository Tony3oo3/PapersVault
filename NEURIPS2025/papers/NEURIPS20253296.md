## Problem

Existing generalist time series foundation models assume uniformly sampled data and cannot handle the irregular intervals, heterogeneous sampling rates, and frequent missing values that characterize real-world medical time series. Meanwhile, emerging medical-specific foundation models are limited to narrow classification domains (e.g., sleep staging or Alzheimer's diagnosis) and do not support continuous-time forecasting at arbitrary target timestamps. This leaves a gap for a scalable, generalizable model that natively handles the multi-frequency, irregular nature of clinical data while enabling robust zero-shot transfer across diverse medical tasks.

## Input / Output

MIRA consumes univariate, channel-independent sequences of paired timestamps and observations where timestamps are strictly increasing real values and observations may contain NaN entries representing missing measurements. Two irregularity patterns are supported: regular-grid timestamps with time-level missing values, and truly non-uniform timestamp intervals. Given a history window of length L and a set of H target timestamps (which may be at arbitrary, non-uniform offsets), the model outputs H predicted scalar values at those exact target times, enabling forecasting at any future timestamp rather than being restricted to a fixed observation grid.

## Method

MIRA is a decoder-only transformer with three architectural innovations tailored to irregular medical time series. First, a Continuous-Time Rotary Positional Encoding (CT-RoPE) replaces discrete index-based RoPE by computing rotation angles directly from real-valued timestamps with learnable angular frequencies, so that attention scores depend only on the relative time difference between tokens, preserving the efficiency of dot-product attention while supporting arbitrary sampling intervals. Second, a sparse frequency-specific Mixture-of-Experts layer replaces the standard FFN sub-layer, routing each token to K of N lightweight experts via softmax gating plus top-K selection, with an additional shared expert gated by sigmoid to serve as a global residual pathway, allowing different experts to specialize in distinct temporal frequency regimes. Third, a Continuous Dynamics Extrapolation Block based on Neural ODEs numerically integrates the latent state from the last observed timestamp to each target timestamp using a Dormand-Prince (RK45) solver, enabling time-aware forecasting at unseen or irregular future time points that standard autoregressive decoding cannot reach. The model is pretrained on over 454 billion time points from MIMIC-III/IV, PTB-XL, Sleep-EDF, and WAVES using Huber loss with a load-balancing auxiliary loss for the MoE layer, resuming from a Time-MoE checkpoint, and is offered in three scales (30M/50M/200M activated parameters).

## Results

MIRA_large achieves an average 8% RMSE reduction over the strongest zero-shot baselines on out-of-distribution datasets and 6% on in-distribution datasets. On the OOD benchmark, MIRA_large attains the best RMSE across all five evaluated datasets (e.g., 0.173 on MIT-BIH, 5.534 on CDC-IHA, 0.478 on JH COVID-19), outperforming the next-best medical-pretrained Time-MoE_large (0.185, 6.299, 0.517 respectively). In the in-distribution setting, MIRA_large leads on 4 of 5 pre-training datasets, with particularly large margins on MIMIC-IV (RMSE 0.081 vs. 0.082 for Time-MoE_large) and PTB-XL (0.121 vs. 0.109 for Time-MoE_large). Ablation confirms the CT-Extrapolation Block contributes the largest single-component gain (5% RMSE, 8% MAE when removed).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MIRA_large wins on all five OOD datasets against every general-domain zero-shot foundation model (Time-MoE, Moirai, Chronos, TimesFM, Lag-Llama, TimeGPT, Timer, Moment), with the largest margins over Chronos (e.g., CDC-IHA RMSE 5.534 vs. 15.986) and Moirai (MIT-BIH 0.173 vs. 0.593). Against the strongest medical-pretrained baselines, MIRA_large still leads: Time-MoE_large (medical) is closest on MIT-BIH (0.185 vs. 0.173) and Heart Rate (0.603 vs. 1.392, where Time-MoE wins due to its regular-grid interpolation advantage), but MIRA dominates on CDC-IHA (5.534 vs. 6.299), JH COVID-19 (0.478 vs. 0.517), and ILI (1.154 vs. 1.201). Against full-shot irregular-time-series models (ContiFormer, T-PatchGNN, Neural-CDE, ODE-RNN), MIRA_large matches or exceeds them on MIT-BIH (0.173 vs. 0.242 for Neural-CDE) and CDC-IHA (5.534 vs. 7.892), while full-shot models retain an edge on ILI (0.195 for T-PatchGNN vs. 1.154) and Heart Rate (0.627 for T-PatchGNN vs. 1.392), reflecting the advantage of task-specific fine-tuning on those particular signals. In the in-distribution setting, MIRA_large achieves the best RMSE on Sleep-EDF (0.189), MIMIC-III (0.102), MIMIC-IV (0.081), and WAVES (0.129), while Time-MoE_large (medical) edges ahead on PTB-XL (0.109 vs. 0.121). The paper also shows that even MIRA_small (30M activated params) surpasses larger general-domain models, indicating that medical-domain pretraining data relevance outweighs raw model scale.

## Relation to Prior Work

MIRA directly extends Time-MoE's decoder-only sparse MoE architecture by resuming training from its checkpoint and adapting it for irregular medical time series. The three core components each build on established ideas: CT-RoPE generalizes Rotary Positional Encoding (Su et al., 2024) from discrete indices to continuous timestamps; the frequency-specific MoE layer follows the sparse expert routing paradigm of Time-MoE and Moirai-MoE; and the Continuous Dynamics Extrapolation Block applies Neural ODEs (Chen et al., 2018) in a forecasting context, similar in spirit to ODE-RNN and Neural-CDE but integrated into a foundation model. The key departure from prior work is the unification of all three mechanisms in a single model pretrained on a 454B-time-point medical corpus, whereas prior generalist foundation models (Moirai, Chronos, TimesFM) assume uniform sampling and prior medical foundation models (SleepFM, etc.) are domain-narrow and lack continuous-time forecasting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work addresses a concrete gap: existing time series foundation models cannot handle the irregular sampling, heterogeneous frequencies, and missing values that are inherent to clinical data, while task-specific irregular time series models (ContiFormer, Neural-CDE) do not scale or transfer across institutions. The released 454B-time-point pretraining corpus and multi-task benchmark provide a practical foundation for future medical time series research. Limitations include reliance on publicly available de-identified data that may not capture full clinical deployment complexity, residual privacy risks, and evaluation limited to univariate forecasting. Open questions include whether the Neural ODE extrapolation component generalizes to truly multi-modal (e.g., ECG + labs + notes) settings and whether the frequency-specific MoE routing remains effective at much larger scales.
