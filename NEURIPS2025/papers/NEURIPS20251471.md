## Problem

Current multimodal large language models (MLLMs) excel at third-person visual reasoning but lack embodied, first-person understanding: they cannot infer the hidden intentions of an unobservable camera wearer, recognize fine-grained hand-object interactions, or integrate context across multi-minute egocentric video streams. Existing egocentric datasets (Ego4D, EgoExo4D) provide extensive video collections but lack explicit causal reasoning chains, temporally spanned annotations, and dense spatio-temporal grounding data, leaving MLLMs unable to perform coherent egocentric reasoning.

## Input / Output

The model consumes egocentric video clips spanning 1–200 seconds (sampled at variable frame rates up to 64 frames) paired with a text question, or a single image for hand-object grounding. Outputs are structurally formatted with a thinking process enclosed in  tags followed by an answer in <answer> tags; for grounding tasks the answer is a normalized bounding box (x_min, y_min, x_max, y_max) in [0,1] or a temporal interval (start, end) in seconds precise to two decimal places. This structured format is enforced during RFT via a format reward that checks regex compliance.

## Method

EgoThinker builds on Qwen2-VL-7B with a two-stage curriculum. Stage 1 (SFT) trains on a 1.5M-sample corpus mixing general visual QA, ego-related datasets, and the newly constructed EgoRe-5M (5M QA pairs across four splits: short-term perception, long-term causal reasoning, chain-of-thought rationales, and fine-grained grounding), establishing foundational egocentric reasoning. Stage 2 (RFT) applies GRPO on 70K fine-grained grounding samples using rule-based rewards: a format reward for structural compliance plus an IoU reward computed between predicted and ground-truth bounding boxes (spatial) or time intervals (temporal), eliminating the need for a learned reward model. EgoRe-5M itself is built from 13M egocentric clips mined from HowTo100M via a three-stage filtering pipeline (ego-vs-exo classification, dynamic hand-object interaction detection, and displacement thresholding), with questions and CoT rationales auto-generated by DeepSeek-V3 and DeepSeek-R1.

## Results

EgoThinker (built on Qwen2-VL-7B) achieves state-of-the-art accuracy across all seven egocentric QA benchmarks, with headline gains of +8.8% on EgoPlan (47.1 vs. 38.3 baseline), +12.0% on VLN-QA (54.0 vs. 42.0), and +13.2% on the cross-view RES benchmark (39.5 vs. 26.3). On fine-grained spatio-temporal grounding, EgoThinker reaches 80.3% Loc-Acc on EK-Visor (vs. 71.7% for the much larger Qwen2.5-VL-72B) and 63.9% R1@0.05 on EgoExoLearn temporal grounding (vs. 49.9% for Qwen2.5-VL-72B). On general video benchmarks, EgoThinker matches or exceeds the Qwen2-VL-7B baseline (MVBench 70.0, Perception Test 72.7, VideoMME 62.9), confirming no degradation from egocentric specialization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EgoThinker outperforms all seven 7B/8B MLLM baselines on the majority of egocentric benchmarks. Against its direct base model Qwen2-VL-7B, it gains +6.5% on EgoTaskQA, +5.9% on QAEgo4D, +8.8% on EgoPlan, +4.3% on EgoSchema, and +12.0% on VLN-QA. The closest competitor on EgoTaskQA is InternVL2-8B (61.0), which EgoThinker surpasses by 3.4 points; on EgoSchema, InternVL2-8B (64.2) is the strongest baseline, beaten by 3.4 points. LLaVA-Video-7B retains a narrow edge on QAEgo4D (68.6 vs. 66.2), the only benchmark where EgoThinker does not lead. On the cross-view RES benchmark, where all prior MLLMs score near random (21–34%), EgoThinker achieves 39.5%, an 8.4-point margin over the second-best (Exo2Ego-7B at 44.5 on VLN-QA but absent on RES; the next best RES score is 31.1 from LLaVA-Video). On spatio-temporal grounding (Table 3), EgoThinker's 80.3% Loc-Acc on EK-Visor exceeds the 71.7% of the 10×-larger Qwen2.5-VL-72B, and its 63.9% R1@0.05 on EgoExoLearn temporal grounding beats Qwen2.5-VL-72B (49.9%) by 14 points, confirming that the SFT+RFT paradigm is more effective than raw scale for fine-grained localization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the egocentric video understanding literature (Ego4D, EgoExo4D, EPIC-Kitchens) by introducing the first MLLM framework that unifies high-level egocentric reasoning with fine-grained hand-object and temporal grounding. Methodologically, it builds on Qwen2-VL as the base model and adapts the GRPO reinforcement fine-tuning paradigm from DeepSeek-R1, replacing learned reward models with rule-based IoU scoring tailored to spatio-temporal localization. The CoT supervision strategy draws from the visual CoT and DeepSeek-R1 reasoning literature, but is the first to apply it at scale to egocentric causal reasoning over multi-minute video segments. Unlike prior egocentric MLLM works (Exo2Ego, MM-Ego, EgoLife) that focus on general video QA, EgoThinker explicitly targets the unobservable-agent problem—inferring the camera wearer's intentions and localizing their hands and manipulated objects.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The EgoRe-5M dataset (5M QA pairs from 13M clips with CoT rationales and dense grounding annotations) fills a critical gap: prior egocentric datasets lacked explicit reasoning chains and fine-grained spatio-temporal labels, limiting MLLMs to surface-level event recognition. The finding that RFT with rule-based IoU rewards dramatically outperforms SFT for grounding (mIoU 53.7 vs. 38.9 on EK-Veror; R1@0.05 63.9 vs. 24.9 on EgoExoLearn) provides a clear recipe for improving localization in MLLMs without expensive reward-model training. Key limitations include reliance on large-scale LLM-generated annotations, offline fine-tuning that precludes real-time deployment, and evaluation limited to 7B-scale models. Open questions include whether the paradigm scales to larger models, whether self-supervised or online learning can replace the annotation pipeline, and how well the grounding capabilities transfer to actual embodied agent control.
