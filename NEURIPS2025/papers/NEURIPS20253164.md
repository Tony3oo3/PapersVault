## Problem

Despite AlphaGo's superhuman Go performance since 2016, mainstream LLMs still fall below beginner-level proficiency in Go, revealing a fundamental gap between general reasoning capabilities and specialized domain expertise. The paper asks whether a general LLM can acquire expert-level Go play while preserving its broad reasoning abilities, using only structured domain data (scarce natural-language corpora) rather than large-scale domain-specific text.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253164/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives a Go game state as a serialized move sequence (letter-number coordinates prefixed with X for black or O for white), or alternatively as a 19×19 2D board array (values 1, −1, 0 for black, white, empty) to mitigate the 'context curse' where accuracy degrades with sequence length. The output is a natural-language reasoning chain that analyzes the board, explores candidate moves with variation analysis, and concludes with a predicted next-move coordinate plus a numerical win-rate estimate, all in a long CoT format.

## Method

The training pipeline has two phases. First, a mixed cold-start fine-tunes Qwen2.5-Base on a 10-million-scale next-step prediction dataset (constructed by applying heuristic templates to KataGo's top-10 move annotations, producing structured reasoning chains) combined with 100K Go commentary examples and long-CoT reasoning data from math and code domains. Second, GRPO reinforcement learning is applied with a piecewise reward function that tiers rewards by KataGo rank (top-1 > top-3 > top-10 > outside top-10) and additionally penalizes inaccurate win-rate estimates, encouraging the model to self-explore stable Go strategies in natural language. The key insight is that heuristic rules transform sparse structured engine output into learnable reasoning chains, raising the RL exploration ceiling far above what naive prediction or general CoT data alone can achieve.

## Results

On KataGo-Bench-1K, LoGos (32B) achieves 88.6% prediction accuracy, surpassing the best general LLM (Claude3.7-Sonnet at 34.3%) by 2.6× and exceeding the top specialized Go model KataGo-HumanSL-9d (87.8%). The 7B variant also reaches 88.1%, far above all general LLMs. On general reasoning benchmarks, LoGos (32B) maintains strong performance (GPQA-Diamond 63.6, AIME 56.7, MATH 96.5, LiveCodeBench 50.9), outperforming Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct and matching or exceeding DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-32B on most tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against closed-source LLMs, LoGos (32B) achieves 88.6% on KataGo-Bench-1K versus Claude3.7-Sonnet's 34.3%, o1-mini's 27.3%, and DeepSeek-R1's 17.6%—a 2.6× improvement over the strongest general LLM. Against open-source baselines, LoGos (7B) at 88.1% and LoGos (32B) at 88.6% dwarf Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct (8.0%), DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B (0.6%), Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct (6.8%), and DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-32B (4.7%) by over an order of magnitude on Go. Against specialized Go models, LoGos (32B) at 88.6% slightly exceeds KataGo-HumanSL-9d (87.8%), the model simulating top amateur/professional players, and substantially outperforms KataGo-HumanSL-18k (67.4%) and KataGo-HumanSL-1d (79.7%). On general reasoning benchmarks, LoGos (32B) outperforms Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct across all six benchmarks and matches or exceeds DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-32B on GPQA-Diamond (63.6 vs. 56.1), BBEH (34.1 vs. 27.4), AIME (56.7 vs. 46.7), MATH (96.5 vs. 94.5), and LiveCodeBench (50.9 vs. 36.5), while trailing slightly on KOR-Bench (74.8 vs. 70.0). The 7B variant similarly surpasses its open-source counterparts on Go while maintaining competitive general performance, particularly on MATH (93.2 vs. 92.6 for Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct) and LiveCodeBench (23.4 vs. 20.4 for DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DeepSeek-R1/DeepSeek-Math paradigm of SFT cold-start followed by GRPO reinforcement learning to a fundamentally different domain: a specialized board game with scarce natural language corpora. While prior LLM game-play work (ChessGPT, PokerGPT) focused on structured prediction or direct policy distillation, and AlphaGo/AlphaZero achieved superhuman play through specialized MCTS+neural architectures without natural language reasoning, this paper's key departure is the 'mixed fine-tuning' strategy: injecting domain expertise via heuristically constructed synthetic data (using KataGo annotations) alongside general long-CoT reasoning data, then letting GRPO self-exploration bridge the gap between injected knowledge and the model's inherent reasoning. The paper explicitly positions itself against the limitation that general LLMs perform below beginner level in Go, framing the contribution as a generalizable recipe for transferring expert knowledge into LLMs in data-scarce domains.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates a concrete, replicable pathway for making general LLMs domain experts in settings where natural language corpora are scarce but structured expert knowledge (e.g., from engines or databases) is available at scale — a paradigm potentially applicable to scientific discovery, industrial control, and other specialized fields. The 'context curse' finding (LLM performance degrades with game length due to serialized input) and its resolution via 2D board state rendering are practically important for any LLM application involving long sequential state tracking. Open questions include whether the heuristic data construction generalizes to domains without a clear 'engine' oracle, whether terminology accuracy can be improved without a dedicated reward model, and whether the approach scales to even more complex or open-ended domains.
