## Problem

While the flat-minima hypothesis and the simplicity-bias hypothesis each offer partial explanations for why SGD generalizes well in overparameterized networks, no unified theory connects data geometry, optimization dynamics, and the nature of the selected solution. In particular, it remains unclear what mechanism causes SGD to prefer flat or feature-simple minima, and how optimizer modifications such as Sharpness-Aware Minimization (SAM) alter these preferences beyond merely seeking flatter regions.

## Input / Output

The framework consumes per-example loss Hessians {H_i}, model parameters w, and optimizer hyperparameters (learning rate η, batch size B, SAM radius ρ) to produce explicit convergence/divergence thresholds parameterized by a data-dependent coherence measure σ = λ_max(S) / max_i λ_max(H_i). In the two-layer ReLU instantiation, inputs are binary vectors x ∈ {-1,1}^d with product labels y = x[0]·x[1], and the output characterizes which interpolating solutions—indexed by complexity C (number of shared features) and sharpness r—are stable or unstable under each optimizer, with the coherence matrix S ∈ R^{n×n} serving as the bridge between data structure and dynamical stability.

## Method

The paper linearizes the training dynamics of SGD, random-perturbation SGD, and SAM around a local minimum and derives stability conditions governed by a coherence matrix S whose entries S_ij = ‖H_i^{1/2} H_j^{1/2}‖_F quantify how aligned per-example curvature directions are. For SAM, the effective Hessian H_SAM = H(I + (ρ/α)H) introduces a curvature-squared penalty that tightens the stability threshold relative to SGD, causing SAM to escape sharp minima more aggressively and to selectively destabilize solutions whose high-curvature directions are not uniformly supported across all training examples. In the two-layer ReLU setting, the paper proves that memorizing solutions (each example assigned to a unique neuron) yield a diagonal coherence matrix with zero cross-sample alignment, while generalizing solutions that share features produce off-diagonal coherence and a dominant principal component. A family of (C,r)-generalizing solutions isolates complexity (C = number of active features) from sharpness (r), and the paper shows that SAM's stability criterion amplifies the preference for low-C solutions beyond what SGD already provides, formally explaining why SAM induces lower effective rank and more parsimonious representations.

## Results

The paper's primary contribution is theoretical: it derives precise linear stability conditions for SGD, random-perturbation SGD, and SAM, showing that a data-dependent coherence measure σ governs which minima are stable. Empirically, on a two-layer ReLU network, SAM (ρ=0.1) reduces the coherence measure from 133.9 (SGD) to 65.7, the largest Hessian eigenvalue from 6.78 to 3.83, and the effective feature rank from 6.17 to 5.6, confirming that SAM drives training toward simpler, more coherent solutions. On CIFAR-10 with ResNet-18, SAM(ρ=0.2) reduces the effective feature rank to 128.67±5.51 compared to 148.33±3.22 for SGD, while the approximate coherence measure increases modestly from 1.0045 to 1.0907.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three optimization methods: standard SGD, random-perturbation SGD (Bisla et al., 2022), and SAM (Foret et al., 2021). Against random-perturbation SGD, the paper shows that the stability threshold is identical (Theorem 3.1, Part 1), meaning noise does not change which minima are stable, but once a minimum is unstable, the injected noise causes divergence at a constant-factor faster rate (Part 2). Against SGD, SAM imposes a strictly tighter stability criterion due to an additional curvature-dependent term (1 + (ρ/α)λ_min(H))^{-1} in the divergence threshold (Theorem 3.2), meaning SAM escapes sharp minima more aggressively. In the two-layer ReLU experiments (Table 1), SAM with ρ=0.1 reduces the coherence measure by 51% (133.9→65.7) and the maximum Hessian eigenvalue by 43% (6.78→3.83) relative to SGD, while also lowering the effective feature rank from 6.17 to 5.6, demonstrating that SAM amplifies the simplicity bias. On CIFAR-10 (Table 2, appendix), the effect is milder: SAM(ρ=0.2) reduces feature rank by ~13% (148.3→128.7) compared to SGD, though SAM(ρ=0.05) shows a slight increase in rank (157.7), suggesting the simplicity-bias effect strengthens with larger perturbation radius.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the coherence-based linear stability framework of Dexter et al. (ICLR 2024), which characterized SGD stability through a data-dependent alignment measure, to two new settings: random-perturbation SGD and Sharpness-Aware Minimization. It also builds on the broader dynamical stability tradition (Wu et al., NeurIPS 2018; Wu and Su, ICML 2023) that uses local linearization to explain which minima SGD selects. The key conceptual departure is showing that the same coherence quantity that governs SGD stability also explains SAM's additional bias toward lower-complexity solutions, thereby unifying the flat-minima and simplicity-bias narratives that had previously been treated as separate explanations for generalization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a theoretically grounded bridge between two widely cited but previously disconnected heuristics—flat minima and implicit simplicity bias—suggesting that interventions like SAM amplify simplicity bias rather than merely seeking flatness. However, the analysis is confined to a linear (quadratic) approximation near minima, two-layer ReLU networks, and i.i.d. data, leaving open whether the coherence-driven selection mechanism persists in deeper architectures, with momentum or adaptive optimizers, or on non-i.i.d. real-world data. The coherence measure itself is computationally expensive (requiring per-sample Hessians), so translating the theory into practical optimizer design or monitoring tools remains an open challenge.
