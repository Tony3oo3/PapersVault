## Problem

Most LLMs rely on static BPE tokenizers with fixed vocabularies optimized on general-purpose corpora, which fail to adapt to domain- or language-specific inputs, inflating token sequences by 2–3× and increasing compute cost. Prior work addresses this by expanding vocabularies per domain, but requires separate tokenizers and retraining for each target. The paper asks whether tokenization can be made fully adaptive at inference time, constructing new domain-specific tokens on the fly without retraining or maintaining separate tokenizers.

## Input / Output

The method takes raw text, first tokenizes it into base tokens via standard BPE, then applies online LZW compression to produce a shorter stream of base tokens and hypertokens (merged sequences of up to M=3 base tokens). During autoregressive generation, the model outputs a mixture of base and hypertokens; at the end of generation, the full hypertoken sequence is decompressed via LZW back into the original base-token sequence. The dynamic vocabulary starts empty each session and grows linearly with context length, with a hyper-embedding cache ensuring only one new embedding is computed per decoding step.

## Method

zip2zip combines three components: (1) an LZW-based online tokenizer that incrementally merges co-occurring base tokens into reusable hypertokens during inference, exploiting LZW's online, self-contained, and unambiguous properties; (2) a lightweight hyper-encoder (a 2-layer transformer) that computes hypertoken embeddings at runtime from the concatenated embeddings of constituent base tokens, performing nonlinear dimensionality reduction from (M×d) to d dimensions; and (3) a modified causal language modeling objective trained on LZW-compressed sequences, augmented with an auxiliary auto-encoding loss that forces hypertoken embeddings to preserve their base-token composition. Existing LLMs (Phi-3-4B, Phi-3.5-14B) are adapted via LoRA in ~10–40 H100-GPU-hours on 100M tokens, after which the model performs test-time adaptation, learning to emit hypertokens in unseen contexts and reducing sequence length by 15–40%.

## Results

zip2zip achieves 15–70% improvements in token efficiency (bytes per token) across five domains and four tokenizer families (Llama-3, Qwen-2, Phi-4, Gemma-3), with the largest gains in structured domains like code (+54–70%) and math (+48–61%). On NLP benchmarks, zip2zip matches the base model on most tasks (ARC, HellaSwag, PIQA, Winogrande) but shows significant degradation on GSM8K (0.15 vs. 0.82 for Phi-3.5-4B; 0.25 vs. 0.84 for Phi-3-14B). Byte-level perplexity increases modestly (e.g., WikiText: 1.58→1.69 for 4B, 1.43→1.56 for 14B). On NVIDIA H100, zip2zip delivers up to 102.6% prefill speedup and 46.6% decode speedup for Phi-3.5-4B at 512+256 context.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares zip2zip against two baselines: the unmodified base model and a continued-pretraining baseline (LoRA fine-tuning without LZW compression, under identical training conditions). On most NLP benchmarks (ARC-c, ARC-e, HellaSwag, OBQA, PIQA, Winogrande), zip2zip ties or nearly ties the base model, with differences within ~0.03. However, on GSM8K, zip2zip suffers severe degradation (0.15 vs. 0.82 for 4B; 0.25 vs. 0.84 for 14B), which the authors attribute to the difficulty of token-level numerical operations under adaptive tokenization. The continued-pretraining baseline also degrades on GSM8K (0.40/0.52), suggesting some of the loss is from the training data shift rather than compression alone. On byte-level perplexity, zip2zip incurs a modest penalty of ~0.1–0.13 over the base model, while the continued-pretraining baseline stays closer to base. On inference throughput (H100), zip2zip outperforms the base model by up to 102.6% (prefill) and 46.6% (decode) for Phi-3.5-4B, and by up to 88.1% (prefill) and 48.1% (decode) for Phi-3-14B, demonstrating that the token reduction more than offsets the hyper-encoder overhead.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the Lempel-Ziv-Welch (LZW) compression algorithm—a classic 1984 dictionary-based scheme—into an online tokenization mechanism for LLMs, layered on top of standard BPE. It departs from prior domain-adapted tokenizer work (Zhao et al. 2024, Liu et al. 2023/2024a), which requires offline vocabulary expansion per domain and separate tokenizers, by performing vocabulary growth entirely at inference time. The dynamic-embedding architecture draws on a line of work on hierarchical and dynamic token representations (MegaByte, Toucan, Dynamic Vocab), but the key distinction is that zip2zip compresses both input and output tokens via a lossless, self-contained codebook, whereas prompt-compression methods (LLMLingua, gist tokens) are lossy and input-only.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: 15–40% token reduction and up to 40% latency improvement with only ~10 GPU-hours of LoRA uptraining makes this an accessible efficiency upgrade for existing LLMs. However, the mild perplexity increase and the notable GSM8K degradation (0.82→0.15 for 4B) raise open questions about whether adaptive tokenization systematically harms numerical reasoning. The entropy-invariance theorem guarantees no fundamental representational loss, but the observed optimization gap suggests the compressed loss landscape is harder to navigate—understanding and closing this gap is an important open problem. Codebook management (only ~25% hypertoken reuse) also points to untapped efficiency.
