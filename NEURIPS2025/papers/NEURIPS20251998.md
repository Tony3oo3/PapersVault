## Problem

Federated Learning offers a privacy-preserving pathway to fine-tune LLMs on sensitive, institutionally siloed data, yet the compatibility and performance of pre-trained LLMs under federated settings remain largely unexplored. No standardized benchmark exists to systematically compare how different base models, aggregation algorithms, and fine-tuning strategies perform across diverse domains under realistic FL constraints such as communication overhead and memory limitations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251998/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark takes pre-trained LLMs (135M–14B parameters, both base and instruct variants) and partitions domain-specific instruction-tuning datasets into client shards of approximately equal size (e.g., 20 clients for NLP/medical, 50 for finance, 10 for code). Only LoRA/DoRA adapter weights are communicated between clients and server per round, with the base model kept frozen and 4-bit quantized. Outputs include zero-shot domain-specific evaluation scores (accuracy for NLP, finance, and medical; pass@1 for code) alongside system-level metrics: total bidirectional communication cost in GB and per-client VRAM usage in GB on a single A100 GPU.

## Method

FlowerTune is a community-driven benchmarking platform built on the Flower framework that standardizes federated LLM fine-tuning across four domains (general NLP, finance, medical, coding) using a unified FL protocol: 20% client selection per round, 15 training rounds, QLoRA-style training (DoRA/LoRA adapters with 4-bit quantization and FlashAttention-2), and cosine-annealed learning rate scheduling. The paper conducts a first-of-its-kind comprehensive comparison of 26 base models, four aggregation algorithms (FedAvg, FedProx, FedAvgM, FlexLoRA), and two PEFT methods (LoRA vs. DoRA) under identical hyper-parameters without task-specific tuning. A key empirical finding is that base model selection dominates performance variation across domains, while off-the-shelf aggregation and fine-tuning strategies yield only marginal differences, highlighting the need for aggregation algorithms specifically designed for the low-rank adapter parameter space.

## Results

Across 26 pre-trained LLMs federated fine-tuned under a unified FL protocol (15 rounds, 20% client sampling, DoRA/QLoRA), Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct achieves the highest General NLP average accuracy (64.50% on MMLU), while Gemma2-9B-Instruct leads in Finance (84.21%), Medical (62.25%), and Coding (53.29% pass@1). Smaller models such as SmolLM2-135M-Instruct (25.04% Medical, 6.57% Coding) and Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct (51.68% General NLP) remain competitive in resource-constrained settings, with VRAM usage as low as 7–10 GB. Instruct-tuned models consistently outperform their non-instruct counterparts by 10–60 percentage points across all domains, and base model selection has a substantially larger impact on downstream performance than the choice of aggregation algorithm or PEFT method.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among the 13 Instruct models benchmarked, Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct (64.50% avg) outperforms Gemma2-9B-Instruct (53.03%) and Llama3.1-8B-Instruct (57.51%) on General NLP, while Gemma2-9B-Instruct dominates Finance (84.21% vs. 69.60% for Mistral-7B-Instruct) and Medical (62.25% vs. 57.88% for Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct). In Coding, Gemma2-9B-Instruct (53.29%) and Llama3.1-8B-Instruct (53.16%) are nearly tied, both far ahead of Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct (31.89%), suggesting code generation benefits from specific architectural strengths. For aggregation strategies (Table 7, Qwen2.5-7B base), differences are marginal: FedProx leads General NLP (42.92% vs. 42.79% FedAvg), FedAvg leads Medical (25.02% vs. 23.41% FedProx), and FedAvgM leads Finance (68.31%) and Coding (46.77%). LoRA vs. DoRA (Table 8) shows LoRA slightly ahead in Finance (68.01% vs. 66.12%) and Coding (47.87% vs. 45.25%), while DoRA edges ahead in General NLP (42.79% vs. 40.18%), with near-identical Medical scores. Non-instruct models underperform their Instruct counterparts dramatically (e.g., Llama3.1-8B: 0.33% vs. 57.51% on General NLP), and the 24B Mistral-Small-Instruct extends the upper bound to 65.28% (General NLP) and 63.62% (Coding) at the cost of ~50 GB communication overhead.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Flower federated learning platform and the LoRA/DoRA parameter-efficient fine-tuning paradigm to create a systematic benchmark for federated LLM fine-tuning. It extends prior federated LLM frameworks (OpenFedLLM, FederatedScope-LLM, FedLLM-Bench) by shifting the focus from proposing new aggregation algorithms to the underexplored question of base model selection: which pre-trained LLMs are actually compatible and effective under FL constraints. The benchmark unifies four domain-specific instruction-tuning datasets and evaluation protocols under a single FL protocol, departing from prior work that typically evaluates one model or one domain in isolation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central practical finding—that base model choice substantially dominates over aggregation algorithm selection (FedAvg vs. FedProx vs. FedAvgM vs. FlexLoRA yield only marginal differences)—redirects research attention toward developing aggregation methods specifically tailored to the low-rank adapter parameter space rather than repurposing classical FL algorithms. The benchmark also reveals that very small models (e.g., SmolLM2-135M) can achieve acceptable performance in resource-constrained FL settings, opening a path toward edge-device federated fine-tuning. Key open questions include whether these findings hold under more heterogeneous data distributions, cross-device (rather than cross-silo) settings, and with per-model hyperparameter tuning, all of which the authors acknowledge as limitations.
