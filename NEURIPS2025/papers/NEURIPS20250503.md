## Problem

Existing minimalist analyses of Edge-of-Stability (EoS) and Progressive Sharpening (PS) are limited to scalar networks, which can only characterize asymptotic sharpness at convergence and cannot explain the role of input data dimension in the EoS stage. Generalist frameworks (e.g., Damian et al., Li et al.) provide four-stage characterizations but rely on hard-to-verify assumptions such as bounded sharpness or the existence of a well-behaved stable set. This paper seeks a nontrivial minimalist setting that yields non-asymptotic guarantees along the entire GD trajectory while remaining analytically tractable.

## Input / Output

The method takes a two-dimensional Gaussian input x = (x1, x2) where x1 is a high-variance irrelevant feature (variance λ1) and x2 is a low-variance relevant feature (variance λ2, with λ1/λ2 ≥ K for large K), a two-layer width-one linear network f(x;θ) = αβ1x1 + αβ2x2, and a large learning rate η ∈ [2/λ1, 1/(10√(λ1λ2))]. The output is a full non-asymptotic characterization: sharpness bounds S(θ(t)) ∈ [1.1/η, 4.7/η] along the trajectory, a linear loss decay rate of (1−λ2/λ1)² for the convergence term, and explicit phase boundaries (T1, T2, T3) separating progressive sharpening from self-stabilization. A constraint |β1| ≤ c is imposed to keep the dynamics within theoretical limits, and the initialization set X(η) is designed so that a single starting point works across a wide range of learning rates.

## Method

The core construction is a two-layer width-one linear network on 2D input where the irrelevant high-variance feature x1 drives instability while the relevant low-variance feature x2 drives convergence. The key analytical insight (Lemma 4.2) is that sharpness is tightly bounded by λ1α², the top Hessian eigenvector aligns with the β1 (noise) direction, and β2 increases monotonically—decoupling the oscillatory and convergent dynamics. The loss decomposes into an oscillatory term L1 = ½λ1(αβ1)² causing periodic spikes and a convergence term L2 = ½λ2(αβ2−1)² that decays linearly at rate (1−λ2/λ1)². The paper proves three phases: monotonic sharpening below 2/η (Phase 1), sharpening above 2/η during EoS (Phase 2.1), and self-stabilization back below 2/η (Phase 2.2), with the dynamics cycling between 2.1 and 2.2. Additionally, the paper extends Kreisler et al.'s GFS sharpness monotonicity to this 2D setting and proves that a nontrivial well-behaved stable set M†(η) ⊂ M(η) exists, reconciling the minimalist and generalist (Damian et al.) analyses.

## Results

The paper establishes non-asymptotic sharpness bounds along the entire GD trajectory for a two-layer width-one linear network with 2D input. Before entering EoS (Phase 1), sharpness is bounded as 1.1/η ≤ S(θ(t)) ≤ 1.7/η with α increasing monotonically; during EoS (Phase 2), sharpness is bounded as 1.5/η ≤ S(θ(t)) ≤ 4.7/η, with progressive sharpening exceeding 2.37/η followed by self-stabilization below 2.01/η. The loss decay rate is characterized as (1−4λ₂/λ₁)² ≤ L̂(θ(t+1))/L̂(θ(t)) ≤ (1−λ₂/λ₁)², and the constrained trajectory achieves the exact rate L(θ†(t+1)) = (1−2λ₂/λ₁)² L(θ†(t)). Global convergence is guaranteed: for any δ,ε > 0, there exists T(δ,ε) such that L(θ(t)) ≤ ε and S(θ(t)) ≤ (2+δ)/η for all t ≥ T.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper that does not benchmark against competing optimization methods. Instead, it positions its contributions relative to prior minimalist and generalist analyses. Compared to Zhu et al. [2022] and Kreisler et al. [2023] (scalar network settings), the 2D-input model exhibits periodic progressive sharpening and self-stabilization with loss spikes—phenomena absent in the scalar case where sharpness merely fluctuates around 2/η from initialization. Compared to Damian et al. [2022] and Li et al. [2022b] (generalist frameworks requiring hard-to-verify assumptions like bounded sharpness or existence of a well-behaved stable set), this paper proves the existence of a nontrivial well-behaved subset M†(η) ⊂ M(η) without such assumptions, resolving a gap identified by Kreisler et al. who showed the stable set can be disjoint in scalar networks. The GFS sharpness analysis extends Kreisler et al.'s monotonic decrease result from scalar to 2D input, with explicit upper and lower bounds that both decrease monotonically due to the monotonic increase of β₂(t).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the minimalist analysis of Edge-of-Stability (EoS) from scalar-input networks (Zhu et al. 2022, Kreisler et al. 2023) to a two-layer width-one network with two-dimensional input, where one feature is relevant and the other is irrelevant noise. It builds directly on Damian et al.'s (2022) self-stabilization and constrained-trajectory framework by proving that a well-behaved stable set exists in the 2D setting (contradicting the scalar counterexample in Kreisler et al. 2023), and extends Kreisler et al.'s gradient-flow-solution sharpness analysis to the bivariate case. The key methodological departure is providing non-asymptotic sharpness and loss-decay guarantees along the entire GD trajectory, rather than only characterizing the asymptotic sharpness of the converged minimum as prior minimalist works do.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first rigorous non-asymptotic proof of progressive sharpening and self-stabilization in a setting that reproduces the periodic loss spikes and sharpness oscillations observed in practical training (Cohen et al. 2021, Damian et al. 2022), which scalar minimalist models fail to capture. The finding that input data dimensionality—specifically the presence of a large-scale irrelevant feature—drives the EoS dynamics offers a concrete mechanistic explanation for why EoS occurs, connecting parameter-space dynamics to data distribution properties. Limitations include the very specific 2D width-one setting, the imposed constraint on β₁ to keep the analysis tractable, and the reliance on a large condition number (λ₁/λ₂ ≥ K) between features. Open questions include whether these insights generalize to deeper networks, wider architectures, and whether the identified trade-off between learning rate and distance-to-minimum holds in practical overparameterized settings.
