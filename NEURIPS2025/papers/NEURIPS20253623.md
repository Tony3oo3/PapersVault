## Problem

Existing articulated object generation methods face a fundamental trade-off: reconstruction-based approaches require expensive multi-view or multi-state captures, while single-image generation methods lack explicit articulation cues and can only infer kinematic behavior probabilistically. Additionally, available datasets either offer scale but limited structural complexity (PartNet-Mobility) or realistic diversity but insufficient size (ACD), constraining model generalization to challenging objects.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253623/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of RGB images showing the same object in resting (closed) and articulated (open) states, extracted as DINOv2 features. Each part is parameterized as a 5×6 matrix encoding bounding box coordinates, semantic label, articulation type, joint axis, and motion range. The output is a complete 3D articulated object with part layouts, joint configurations, and a connectivity graph, compatible with URDF annotation format. For real-world use, only the resting-state image needs to be collected; the articulated counterpart can be synthesized via GPT-4o.

## Method

DIPO is a diffusion transformer (initialized from CAGE pretrained weights) that generates articulated object parameters conditioned on dual-state image features. A Dual-State Injection Module at each layer performs cross-attention where part embeddings attend to resting-state features, then articulated features attend to resting features before being injected, enabling the model to contrast motion between states. A Chain-of-Thought Graph Reasoner infers part connectivity step-by-step from the image pair, using GPT-4o-generated dual-state image pairs as few-shot visual prompts; the resulting adjacency matrix serves as an attention mask for the diffusion self-attention. To address data limitations, the LEGO-Art pipeline uses LLM agents to generate grid-based part layouts from natural language descriptions, retrieves geometry primitives from PartNet-Mobility, renders dual-state images in Blender, and filters with a VLM, producing the PM-X dataset (600 objects, avg. 19.4 parts) that substantially increases structural complexity over existing benchmarks.

## Results

On the PartNet-Mobility test set, DIPO achieves the best reconstruction quality across all metrics: RS-dgIoU of 0.4561, AS-dgIoU of 0.4683, and AS-dCD of 0.0423, while also attaining the highest graph prediction accuracy of 85.06%. On the out-of-distribution ACD test set, DIPO continues to lead with RS-dgIoU of 0.9126, AS-dCD of 0.1085, and graph accuracy of 48.15%. Notably, the performance gap between resting and articulated states is significantly smaller for DIPO than for all baselines, indicating that dual-image conditioning provides effective articulation control signals.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DIPO outperforms all three baselines on every metric in both the in-distribution (PM) and out-of-distribution (ACD) evaluations. Against the closest competitor SINGAPO [23], DIPO improves AS-dCD from 0.1270 to 0.0423 (a 67% reduction) and graph accuracy from 75.97% to 85.06% on PM, and from 0.1762 to 0.1085 (38% reduction) on ACD. Against NAP-ICA [18], DIPO achieves substantially better graph accuracy (85.06% vs. 25.06% on PM) and lower AS-dCD (0.0423 vs. 0.3473), reflecting the advantage of explicit dual-state visual conditioning over unconditional generation. URDFormer [6] performs worst across all metrics, with graph accuracy below 7% on PM and 1.5% on ACD, highlighting the difficulty of its detector-plus-transformer pipeline on complex articulated structures. The consistent advantage of DIPO on the ACD set (a more diverse, realistic dataset) demonstrates that the PM-X training data and dual-image conditioning together improve generalization beyond the training distribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

DIPO extends the diffusion-transformer-based articulated object generation line initiated by CAGE (CVPR 2024) and the scalable diffusion transformer architecture of Peebles & Xie (ICCV 2023), but departs from the single-image conditioning paradigm of SINGAPO by introducing dual-state (resting + articulated) image pairs as input. The CoT-based Graph Reasoner borrows the step-by-step reasoning paradigm from LLM literature and applies it to articulation graph prediction, a use case not previously explored in this domain. The LEGO-Art pipeline and PM-X dataset represent a new data-construction approach that assembles primitives from PartNet-Mobility via LLM-driven agents, addressing the structural-complexity gap between PM (avg. 4.94 parts) and ACD (avg. 7.48 parts).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dual-state image formulation offers a practical middle ground between expensive multi-view reconstruction and information-poor single-image generation, requiring only two casually captured photos while providing explicit motion cues. The PM-X dataset (600 objects, avg. 19.4 parts) is a meaningful resource for training on structurally complex articulation, and the LEGO-Art pipeline provides a reusable template for scaling such datasets. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond storage furniture and tables to more diverse categories (e.g., vehicles, machinery), whether the CoT graph reasoner can be made more reliable without the PM-X training signal, and how the method performs on truly real-world objects with occlusions and non-rigid parts.
