## Problem

Existing structure-based virtual screening methods—whether physics-based docking or deep learning scoring—assume access to holo structures with ligand-defined binding pockets, yet real-world early-stage discovery relies on apo or AlphaFold2-predicted structures where pocket information is absent. The paper identifies that the dominant failure mode in this setting is pocket mislocalization (detected cavities deviating from true binding sites) rather than backbone conformational noise, and that current DL methods like DrugCLIP, while robust to structural variation, remain critically dependent on accurate pocket definitions.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an apo or predicted protein 3D structure, a set of S candidate cavities detected by a geometric pocket-detection tool (e.g., Fpocket), and a small-molecule ligand. Each cavity is expanded to a 10 Å radius (up from the conventional 6 Å) with atom down-sampling to handle cavity fragmentation. The output is a per-compound binding score; in the blind setting, the final score is the maximum across all candidate pockets for that protein, enabling ranking without any pocket annotation.

## Method

AANet is a two-phase framework built on the Uni-Mol backbone. In the alignment phase, a tri-modal contrastive objective (pairwise-sigmoid loss) jointly aligns ligand, holo-pocket, and detected-cavity embeddings, using cavity-based augmentation (sampling cavities with IoU ≥ 0.5 to the holo pocket) and hard negative mining from low-IoU non-binding cavities to teach the model to distinguish true binding sites from geometrically plausible decoys. In the aggregation phase, a lightweight single-head cross-attention adapter—initialized near identity with a high softmax temperature—uses the ligand embedding as query to dynamically weight frozen cavity embeddings, producing a unified cavity representation contrasted against a projected ligand embedding. This adapter is fine-tuned on pocket-agnostic ChEMBL activity data mapped to AlphaFold2 structures, with attention weights supervised via KL divergence against soft labels (pretrained cavity scores) or one-hot labels (known complexes), enabling training without explicit binding-site annotations.

## Results

On DUD-E (38 targets), AANet achieves BEDROC of 0.6365 (holo), 0.5764 (apo-exp blind), and 0.6232 (apo-pred blind), with EF1% of 40.85, 37.19, and 40.85 respectively. The headline improvement is in the blind apo setting, where AANet raises EF1% from 11.75 (DrugCLIP) to 37.19, a 3.2× gain, while maintaining near-holo performance. On LIT-PCBA (12 targets), AANet similarly leads with BEDROC 0.0850 (holo) and 0.0715 (apo-pred blind), outperforming DrugCLIP (0.0155) by ~4.6× in the blind predicted setting. Pocket identification on COACH420 shows AANet achieving 54.19% top-1 accuracy at DCA≤3Å, versus 33.72% for DrugCLIP and 38.50% for TankBind.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AANet outperforms all six baselines across every evaluated setting. Against the closest DL competitor DrugCLIP, AANet gains +0.12 BEDROC on holo DUD-E (0.6365 vs 0.5157) and a dramatic +0.38–0.43 BEDROC in blind apo settings (0.5764/0.6232 vs 0.1926/0.1974), reflecting DrugCLIP's severe sensitivity to pocket mislocalization. Docking-based methods (Glide-SP, RTMScore, EquiScore) are not evaluated in the blind setting due to prohibitive computational cost, but even in annotated apo settings they drop 50–70% from holo performance, whereas AANet retains >90% of its holo score. TankBind, which uses detected pockets during training, shows the most stable cross-setting performance among baselines (BEDROC ~0.29–0.31 on DUD-E) but remains 45–50% below AANet in all settings. For pocket identification on COACH420, AANet surpasses DrugCLIP (54.19% vs 33.72% top-1 at DCA≤3Å) and TankBind (54.19% vs 38.50%), and is only marginally below the classical PRANK method in top-n at the strictest 1Å threshold where oracle performance is already low (14.88%).

## Relation to Prior Work

AANet extends the contrastive protein-molecule learning paradigm introduced by DrugCLIP (Gao et al., NeurIPS 2023) by adding a third modality—geometric cavities detected from the protein surface—into the contrastive alignment objective, and by introducing a cross-attention adapter that aggregates multiple candidate pockets conditioned on the ligand. Conceptually, it departs from the standard SBVS assumption that a high-quality holo structure with a ligand-defined pocket is available, instead formalizing the problem for apo and AlphaFold2-predicted structures where pocket annotations are absent. The method is initialized from Uni-Mol's 3D molecular representation and trained on PDBBind, following the same architectural family as DrugCLIP, but the tri-modal alignment and cavity aggregation are novel components not present in prior contrastive VS methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work addresses a critical practical gap: as AlphaFold2 expands structural coverage to previously inaccessible targets, the field needs VS methods that work without experimentally resolved holo complexes. The paper's key diagnostic—that pocket mislocalization, not backbone conformational noise, is the dominant failure mode for DL-based VS—reframes how the community should think about adapting SBVS to predicted structures. The curated apo benchmark (38 DUD-E + 12 LIT-PCBA targets with matched holo/apo/predicted structures) provides a reusable evaluation resource. Open questions include scalability to truly novel targets with no structural homologs, handling of proteins with large ligand-induced conformational changes, and whether the reliance on geometric cavity detectors (Fpocket, PocketFinder, etc.) introduces a ceiling on performance for atypical binding sites.
