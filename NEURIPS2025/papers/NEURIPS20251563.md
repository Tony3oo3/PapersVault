## Problem

While no-regret online learning algorithms are known to converge to Nash equilibria in the average-iterate for zero-sum games, their last-iterate sequences often cycle or diverge, and achieving last-iterate convergence typically demands algorithm-specific modifications (optimism, regularization) and problem-specific Lyapunov analyses. This paper asks whether last-iterate convergence can be obtained from average-iterate convergence via a universal, black-box transformation that preserves the uncoupled (decentralized) nature of the learning dynamics.

## Input / Output

The A2L reduction is a black-box wrapper: it takes any uncoupled online learning algorithm as a subroutine and requires only that the game have linear utilities (each player's payoff is linear in their own strategy and in the joint strategy of opponents), which covers bimatrix and multi-player polymatrix games. Under gradient feedback, each player observes the full utility vector at the played strategy; under bandit feedback, each player observes only a scalar reward for the single sampled action and does not see opponents' strategies. The output is a sequence of mixed strategy profiles whose last iterate at every time T is an f(T)-approximate Nash equilibrium, with f(T) = O(log d / T) under gradient feedback and Õ(d^{1/5} T^{-1/5}) under bandit feedback.

## Method

The core contribution is the A2L (Average-to-Last) reduction: each player runs an inner algorithm R (e.g., OMWU) to generate internal iterates x^t, but actually plays the running average x̄^t = (1/t)Σx^k. The key insight is that, by linearity of utilities, the player can recover the unseen utility vector u^t = u_i(·, x_{-i}^t) corresponding to the unplayed internal iterate via the identity u^t = t·ū^t − (t−1)·ū^{t−1}, where ū^t is the observed utility at the average strategy. This recovered utility is forwarded to R, and an induction argument shows the internal iterates exactly match those R would produce standalone, so the last iterate of the new dynamics equals the average iterate of the original. Applied to OMWU, this yields an O(log d / T) anytime last-iterate rate with O(log d · log T) dynamic regret per player. For bandit feedback, the reduction is augmented with a utility estimation subroutine that mixes the strategy with ε_t = 1/t uniform exploration, samples B_t = d·t^4 (or t^4) actions per epoch, and uses a refined second-order error analysis to achieve a Õ(d^{1/5} T^{-1/5}) high-probability last-iterate rate.

## Results

Under gradient feedback, A2L-OMWU achieves an $O(\log d / T)$ anytime last-iterate convergence rate in multi-player zero-sum polymatrix games (Theorem 3), representing an exponential improvement in the dependence on dimension $d$ over the previous best $O(\mathrm{poly}(d)/T)$ rate. As a corollary, each player incurs only $O(\log d \log T)$ dynamic regret (Corollary 1). Under bandit feedback, A2L-OMWU-Bandit achieves a $\tilde{O}(d^{1/5} T^{-1/5})$ high-probability last-iterate convergence rate (Theorem 4), improving upon the previous best $\tilde{O}(\sqrt{d}\, T^{-1/8})$ (high probability) and $\tilde{O}(\sqrt{d}\, T^{-1/6})$ (in expectation) rates for two-player zero-sum games.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to the Accelerated Optimistic Gradient (AOG) algorithm [CZ23b], A2L-OMWU achieves the same optimal $O(1/T)$ dependence on $T$ but improves the dimension dependence from $\mathrm{poly}(d)$ to $\log d$, an exponential gain. Compared to the entropy-regularized extragradient [CWC21; CWC24], A2L-OMWU removes the extra $\log T$ factor and provides an anytime guarantee (valid for every iterate $T \geq 1$) without requiring knowledge of the total horizon or tuning a regularization parameter. In the bandit feedback setting, A2L-OMWU-Bandit improves the high-probability last-iterate rate from $\tilde{O}(T^{-1/8})$ [Cai+23] to $\tilde{O}(T^{-1/5})$, and the in-expectation rate from $\tilde{O}(T^{-1/6})$ to $\tilde{O}(T^{-1/5})$, while also extending the guarantee from two-player zero-sum games to the more general multi-player zero-sum polymatrix setting. The paper also notes that applying a similar utility estimation procedure to AOG under bandit feedback does not yield a rate faster than $\tilde{O}(T^{-1/8})$ due to error propagation in the potential-function analysis, highlighting a structural advantage of the A2L reduction.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Optimistic Multiplicative Weights Update (OMWU) algorithm and its regret-bounded-by-variation (RVU) property from Rakhlin & Sridharan (2013) and Syrgkanis et al. (2015), wrapping it in a new black-box reduction (A2L) that exploits the linearity of utilities in polymatrix games to recover per-iterate gradients from averaged feedback. It departs from the prior last-iterate convergence literature (Extra Gradient, Optimistic Gradient, accelerated variants, regularized methods) which required algorithm-specific design choices and problem-specific Lyapunov analyses; here, any no-regret algorithm can be converted to a last-iterate convergent one via a simple averaging wrapper, provided the game has bilinear utilities. The reduction is also compared to Cutkosky's anytime online-to-batch (AO2B) reduction, which shares the averaging idea but targets static optimization rather than dynamic multi-agent games.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The A2L reduction provides a clean, broadly applicable template for achieving last-iterate convergence in games with linear utilities, yielding state-of-the-art rates: O(log d/T) under gradient feedback (exponential improvement in dimension dependence over prior O(poly(d)/T) results) and O~(d^{1/5} T^{-1/5}) under bandit feedback (improving the previous O~(T^{-1/8}) high-probability rate). The O(log d log T) dynamic regret guarantee is a notable corollary. Limitations include the requirement of linear (bilinear) utilities, which excludes convex-concave min-max problems where EG/OG already achieve last-iterate convergence, and the need to compute running averages. Open questions include extending the reduction to Markov games, time-varying games, and whether similar utility-estimation tricks can be applied to accelerated algorithms like AOG under bandit feedback without the A2L wrapper.
