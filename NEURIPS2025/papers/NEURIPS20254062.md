## Problem

Safe linear bandits (SLBs) require an agent to maximize an unknown linear objective subject to unknown linear constraints each round, but existing methods with strong regret guarantees rely on solving expensive second-order conic programs or NP-hard problems per round. No computationally efficient method existed for soft constraint enforcement, and hard enforcement methods imposed m second-order cone constraints that caused significant practical slowdowns as the number of constraints grew.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254062/cropped_page6_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each round the learner receives noisy scalar reward feedback and a noisy m-dimensional constraint feedback vector, and must output an action from a known convex domain A. S-COLTS additionally requires a known safe action a_safe with positive safety margin, while R-COLTS requires only that the true problem is feasible. The per-round computation reduces to solving one (S-COLTS) or O(log t) (R-COLTS) linear programs with m linear inequality constraints over A, rather than the SOCPs or NP-hard programs needed by prior methods.

## Method

COLTS perturbs the RLS estimates of the objective and constraint parameters by a shared random vector ψ added to the objective and −ψ to each constraint row (the coupled noise design), then selects actions by solving the resulting perturbed linear program. The coupling is critical: independent perturbations of objective and constraints cause the unsaturation rate to decay exponentially in m, whereas the coupled design guarantees a constant-probability local optimism event at the true optimum. S-COLTS ensures zero risk by scaling the perturbed optimum toward a_safe along the line segment, with the scaling factor determined by a confidence-based safety constraint; R-COLTS achieves sublinear risk without a safe action by resampling O(log t) perturbed programs and selecting the one with the largest perturbed objective value, which directly yields optimism. The analysis introduces a novel look-back technique that handles the per-round fluctuation of feasible regions by scaling the look-back action toward a_safe to restore feasibility under the current perturbed constraints.

## Results

S-COLTS achieves zero risk and regret R(a_safe)·Õ(√(d³T)) using only LP·log(t) computation per round, matching the regret of prior efficient methods (SAFE-LTS) while replacing expensive SOCP/NP-hard subroutines with linear programs. R-COLTS achieves Õ(√(d³T)) regret and risk with no instance-specific terms, using LP·log²(t) per round. In simulations (d=m=9, T=5×10⁴), S-COLTS reduces wall-clock time by 5.1× versus SAFE-LTS while mildly improving regret; for d=2 with m=100 constraints, the speedup exceeds 18× and S-COLTS regret is 2–4× lower. R-COLTS with 3 resamples achieves R_T=301±102 and S_T=3266±172, far below the √(d²T) scale predicted by theory.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SAFE-LTS [Moradipari et al., 2021], S-COLTS matches the theoretical regret bound R(a_safe)·Õ(√(d³T)) and zero risk, but replaces the per-round SOCP with a single LP, yielding a 5.1× wall-clock speedup at d=m=9 and a >18× speedup at d=2, m=100. Empirically, for m≥10 constraints, S-COLTS also achieves 2–4× lower regret than SAFE-LTS. Against DOSS [Gangrade et al., 2024], R-COLTS matches the Õ(√(d²T)) regret/risk order (losing only a √d factor common to all efficient linear bandit methods) while requiring O(log t) LPs per round instead of (2d)^(m+1) LPs; for T=1/δ=10¹⁰, this means 4log(t²/δ) ≤ (2d)^(m+1) for all d≥4, m≥2, making DOSS computationally infeasible in practice. The paper also notes that no prior computationally efficient method existed for soft enforcement, making R-COLTS the first such method.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Thompson sampling framework for linear bandits (Agrawal & Goyal, 2013) to the safe linear bandit setting by perturbing both the objective and constraint estimates, then selecting actions via the resulting perturbed linear program. It builds on the scaling heuristic of Hutchinson et al. (2024, ROFUL) and the posterior-quantile/resampling principle of Chen et al. (2022) and Kaufmann et al. (2012), but departs fundamentally by replacing the expensive second-order cone or NP-hard optimizations required by prior methods with simple LP solves. The key conceptual departure is the coupled noise design—adding a single random vector to the objective and its negation to all constraint rows—which avoids the exponential-in-m degradation that independent perturbations would induce, and a novel look-back analysis that handles the per-round fluctuation of feasible regions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first computationally efficient method for soft constraint enforcement in safe linear bandits (R-COLTS), and it provides a 5×–18× speedup for hard enforcement (S-COLTS) while matching or improving regret over prior methods. The coupled noise design and look-back analysis are reusable technical tools that could inform future sampling-based approaches to constrained sequential decision-making. Open questions include whether the √d gap relative to inefficient methods can be closed, how the approach extends to contextual settings, and whether the resampling overhead in R-COLTS can be further reduced in practice.
