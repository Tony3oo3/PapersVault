## Problem

Existing RAG pipelines for question answering suffer from incorrect retrievals and LLM hallucinations, particularly on multi-hop questions. The paper identifies three root causes: (1) short questions lack sufficient information to guide both retrieval and reasoning, (2) contrastive training objectives cause dense retrievers to match superficial lexical patterns rather than critical factual content, and (3) no mechanism exists for LLMs to explicitly flag and later compensate for uncertain knowledge during step-by-step reasoning.

## Input / Output

The method takes a natural-language question and a pre-indexed document corpus (each document pre-encoded to a [CLS] vector stored in a Faiss index) as input. It produces top-k reranked documents, a final answer, and a reconstructed reasoning chain of (head, relation, tail) triples. Unusually, the raw question is first expanded by an LLM into sub-questions and a partially masked reasoning chain before any retrieval occurs, and the masked positions (⟨UNCERTAIN⟩, ⟨FILL⟩) are only resolved after retrieval, creating a two-pass interaction between the LLM and the retriever.

## Method

CoopRAG creates a bidirectional information exchange between an LLM and a dense retriever (MPNet encoder) through five stages. First, the LLM 'unrolls' the question into sub-questions and a reasoning chain with uncertain entities explicitly masked. Second, the augmented query is encoded at token level and top-n documents are retrieved via [CLS]-cosine similarity. Third, a novel reranking score (RaLa) contrasts the last-layer embedding against a randomly selected middle-layer embedding per document, weighting the ColBERT-style MaxSim score by the gap between these layers (ω_{U,D}), which amplifies documents whose semantic representation changes most across depth. Fourth, the LLM fills the masked reasoning-chain positions using evidence from the top-k documents. Fifth, the LLM generates the final answer conditioned on the completed chain. Training uses a difficulty-aware InfoNCE loss where each sample's weight is log(1 + number of sub-questions), forcing the encoder to learn more from harder, multi-hop queries.

## Results

CoopRAG (GPT-4o-mini) achieves state-of-the-art QA performance across all four main benchmarks: 65.6 EM on HotpotQA, 52.3 EM on MuSiQue, 71.7 EM on 2WikiMultihopQA, and 72.0 EM on NaturalQuestions. On retrieval, it reaches 80.8 R@2 on NaturalQuestions, a 35.2% improvement over HippoRAG2 (Llama3.3-70B), and 80.4 R@2 on 2WikiMultihopQA. On the factual QA benchmarks, CoopRAG attains 58.3 EM on SimpleQA and 26.6 EM on FreshQA, outperforming HopRAG by 16.1% and 26.1% in EM respectively. Even with the smaller Gemma2-9B LLM, CoopRAG surpasses all baselines, achieving at least 15.2% higher EM on NaturalQuestions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoopRAG (GPT-4o-mini) outperforms all structure-augmented RAG baselines on every dataset. Against HippoRAG2 (Llama3.3-70B), the strongest baseline, CoopRAG gains +2.9 EM on HotpotQA (65.6 vs 62.7), +15.1 EM on MuSiQue (52.3 vs 37.2), +6.7 EM on 2Wiki (71.7 vs 65.0), and a substantial +23.4 EM on NaturalQuestions (72.0 vs 48.6). Compared to HopRAG (GPT-4o-mini), the closest competitor on MuSiQue, CoopRAG leads by +10.1 EM (52.3 vs 42.2) and +2.6 EM on HotpotQA. On retrieval, CoopRAG (GPT-4o-mini) achieves 80.8 R@2 on NaturalQuestions versus 45.6 for HippoRAG2 (Llama3.3-70B), a 35.2% relative gain, and 80.4 R@2 on 2Wiki versus 76.2. On the factual QA benchmarks, CoopRAG surpasses HopRAG by +8.1 EM on SimpleQA (58.3 vs 50.2) and +5.5 EM on FreshQA (26.6 vs 21.1), while also producing more correct answers (283 vs 233) and fewer not-attempted responses (20 vs 45) under the ChatGPT grader.

## Relation to Prior Work

CoopRAG sits at the intersection of two research threads: (1) ColBERT-style late-interaction dense retrieval, which provides the MaxSim scoring backbone and the positive/hard-negative InfoNCE training paradigm, and (2) DoLa's layer-contrasting mechanism, originally designed to improve LLM factuality at decode time, which the authors repurpose as a document reranking signal (RaLa). On the query side, the paper extends the query-augmentation line (Gao et al., Mao et al., HippoRAG/SiReRAG/HopRAG) by introducing uncertainty-masked reasoning chains that explicitly withhold low-confidence entities rather than hallucinating them into the retrieval query. The difficulty-aware loss reweighting (α proportional to sub-question count) is a lightweight adaptation of sample-reweighting ideas from long-tail recognition to the retrieval fine-tuning setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The cooperative LLM–retriever loop with explicit uncertainty masking offers a practical, plug-in recipe for reducing hallucination propagation in RAG pipelines: the LLM signals what it does not know, the retriever fills that gap, and the LLM reasons over grounded evidence. The RaLa reranker is a low-cost addition to any ColBERT-style system that leverages already-computed intermediate layer activations. Open questions remain around scalability (MaxSim cost grows with token count, sequence-length limits on long documents), generalization to knowledge-graph QA and domain-specific corpora, and whether the difficulty-aware weighting transfers to settings where sub-question count is not a reliable difficulty proxy. The paper also notes that the framework has not been validated on KBQA or medical/technical QA benchmarks.
