## Problem

Existing autoregressive mesh generators produce topologically flawed outputs (holes, non-manifold vertices, inconsistent edges) due to long-sequence ambiguities, and prior RL-based refinement methods like DeepMesh rely on global, manually-annotated rewards that cannot distinguish high-quality from defective regions within a single mesh. This mismatch between object-level supervision and face-level defects introduces training noise and prevents targeted correction of localized geometric errors.

## Input / Output

The model takes a 16,384-point cloud (subsampled from 50K points, not required to be watertight) as conditioning input and generates a triangular mesh autoregressively as an uncompressed token sequence (~9 quantized coordinate tokens per face, ~45K tokens for a 5K-face mesh) using a 36,864-token context window with temperature-0.5 sampling. Point cloud features are extracted by a Hunyuan3D 2.0 pretrained encoder and injected via cross-attention into the decoder.

## Method

Mesh-RFT is a three-stage pipeline: (1) supervised pretraining of a 1.1B-parameter Hourglass Transformer (2-4-12-4-2 layer structure) with truncated training and sliding-window inference over MeshXL-style uncompressed mesh tokens; (2) automated preference dataset construction where 8 candidate meshes per point cloud are scored by three objective metrics—Boundary Edge Ratio, Topology Score (a weighted quad-mesh quality measure), and Hausdorff Distance—and paired only when one mesh dominates across all three; (3) Masked DPO (M-DPO) post-training, the core novelty, which introduces a per-face quality mask (1 if the face merges into a quad above a quality threshold, 0 otherwise) and applies element-wise Hadamard weighting in the DPO log-ratio: the positive-sample term is masked by good regions while the negative-sample term is masked by bad regions, so the policy is pushed to improve only defective faces while preserving already-correct geometry.

## Results

On artist-designed meshes, Mesh-RFT achieves HD 0.1826, TS 77.5, BER 0.0182, and a 45% user-study preference rate, outperforming all baselines across every metric. On dense out-of-distribution meshes, it reaches HD 0.2411, TS 79.4, BER 0.0015, and 40% user preference. The paper reports a 24.6% HD reduction and 3.8% TS improvement over the pre-trained baseline, and a 17.4% HD reduction and 4.9% TS gain over global DPO (S-DPO).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Mesh-RFT outperforms all three baselines on every reported metric (CD, HD, TS, BER, and user-study preference) for both artist-designed and dense meshes. Against MeshAnythingV2 [16], the largest gap is in HD (0.1826 vs. 0.4197 on artist meshes, a 56% reduction) and TS (77.5 vs. 68.3), with user preference nearly 5× higher (45% vs. 9%). Against BPT [19], Mesh-RFT reduces HD by 33% (0.1826 vs. 0.2735) and improves TS by 4.8 points (77.5 vs. 72.7), while user preference more than doubles (45% vs. 20%). Against DeepMesh [23] (0.5B), the closest competitor, Mesh-RFT still achieves a 36% HD reduction (0.1826 vs. 0.2866), a 2.6-point TS gain (77.5 vs. 74.9), and roughly doubles the user-study preference (45% vs. 22%). The advantage is consistent on dense out-of-distribution meshes, where Mesh-RFT leads DeepMesh by 33% in HD (0.2411 vs. 0.3570) and 3.6 points in TS (79.4 vs. 75.8).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the autoregressive native mesh generation line (MeshXL, MeshGPT, BPT, MeshAnything) by adding a reinforcement fine-tuning post-training stage, and most directly builds on DeepMesh's use of DPO for mesh alignment. The key departure is twofold: replacing DeepMesh's manually annotated global preference pairs with an automated topology-aware scoring system (BER, TS, HD), and introducing Masked DPO (M-DPO) that applies quality-aware face-level masks to localize the optimization signal rather than applying it uniformly across the entire mesh sequence. The architecture itself (Hourglass Transformer with cross-attention to a Hunyuan3D point cloud encoder) follows the MeshTron design, so the contribution is squarely in the post-training objective and evaluation pipeline rather than in the generative backbone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that face-level masked preference optimization outperforms global DPO on both geometric and topological metrics, the paper provides a practical recipe for fine-grained RL alignment in 3D generation that avoids expensive manual annotation. Open questions remain around computational efficiency (sliding-window inference is still slow at ~40 tokens/s), topological robustness on complex geometries (holes persist), and whether the point-cloud conditioning modality is the right bottleneck versus direct image-to-mesh generation. The objective scoring system (particularly the quad-based Topology Score) could serve as a reusable evaluation tool for the broader mesh generation community.
