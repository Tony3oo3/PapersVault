## Problem

Gradient-based explanations of deep neural networks suffer from the shattered gradient problem, where gradients become highly noisy and uninformative due to the exponential growth of linear regions with depth. SmoothGrad mitigates this by approximating a Gaussian convolution of gradients via Monte Carlo sampling, but requires many forward and backward passes (typically n=50) for acceptable quality, and even at recommended sample sizes the approximation remains visibly noisy on high-dimensional inputs like ImageNet. The paper addresses the gap between the desired convolution and its computationally tractable sampling approximation.

## Input / Output

The method operates in the fixed-model regime on pre-trained CNNs (VGG-19, ResNet-18) with standard 3-channel RGB ImageNet images (224×224) as input, along with a target class index. It produces a smoothed gradient vector of the same dimensionality as the input (H×W×3), which is then relevance-pooled via the ℓ²-norm across color channels to yield a single scalar sensitivity per pixel for heatmap visualization. For multi-class explanations, the full smoothed Jacobian is estimated once via n forward passes, after which each class explanation requires only a single backward pass.

## Method

SmoothDiff replaces the Monte Carlo estimation of the full network Jacobian's expected value with a factorized product of per-function expected Jacobians, neglecting cross-covariances between layers. The network Jacobian is decomposed via the chain rule into Jacobians of individual operations (linear layers, element-wise activations, pooling), and each is replaced by its expected value under the Gaussian input perturbation. For linear/affine functions the expected Jacobian is simply the weight matrix (no sampling needed); for element-wise activations like ReLU, the expected Jacobian is a diagonal matrix whose entries are the empirical probability of positive activation, estimated from n forward passes. This is implemented as a modified backpropagation where standard vector-Jacobian products (VJPs) are replaced by vector-expected-Jacobian products (VEJPs), requiring only n forward passes plus a single backward pass per class, versus 2n passes for SmoothGrad. The key practical consequence is that only non-linear functions require custom VEJP implementations, making the method easy to extend to new architectures.

## Results

On ImageNet with VGG-19, SmoothDiff achieves a Symmetric Relevance Gain (SRG) of 0.316 with only n=4 samples, outperforming SmoothGrad's 0.304 at its recommended n=40 samples. In terms of convergence, SmoothDiff reaches a SSIM of 0.8 (relative to fully converged SmoothGrad) with just 10 samples, a 5× sample efficiency gain over SmoothGrad's 50 samples. Computationally, SmoothDiff is approximately 2× faster per sample for single-class explanations, and yields a two-orders-of-magnitude speedup for multi-class explanations (e.g., all 1000 ImageNet classes with n=50 samples).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SmoothDiff outperforms SmoothGrad on all pixel-flipping metrics (SRG 0.316 vs. 0.304, LIF 0.382 vs. 0.373, MIF 0.066 vs. 0.069) while requiring 10× fewer samples (n=4 vs. n=40) and running ~2× faster per sample. Against β-Smoothing (Dombrowski et al., NeurIPS 2019), SmoothDiff achieves substantially higher SRG (0.316 vs. 0.230 at best β=1.0) and lower MIF (0.066 vs. 0.086), confirming that β-Smoothing's single-layer Gaussian approximation degrades with network depth. Compared to raw gradients, SmoothDiff more than doubles the SRG (0.316 vs. 0.166) and nearly halves the MIF (0.066 vs. 0.111). In the broader Appendix F evaluation using Quantus metrics on both VGG-19 and ResNet-18, SmoothDiff and its squared variant (SD-SQ) rank among the top methods for robustness (Local Lipschitz Estimate, Average Sensitivity), localization (Relevance Rank Accuracy), and complexity (Sparseness), competing closely with LRP composites and GradCAM. SmoothGrad-Squared (Hooker et al., NeurIPS 2019) and Integrated Gradients (Sundararajan et al., ICML 2017) are also evaluated in Appendix F; SmoothDiff-Squared matches or exceeds SG-SQ on robustness and complexity metrics while maintaining the computational advantages of the SmoothDiff framework.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on SmoothGrad (Smilkov et al., 2017), which approximates the Gaussian convolution of gradients via Monte Carlo sampling, and on the shattered gradient problem (Balduzzi et al., 2017) that motivates such smoothing. It departs from prior smoothing approaches—β-Smoothing (Dombrowski et al., 2019), which replaces ReLUs with softplus, and low-pass filtering in image space—by working in input space and leveraging the factorized Jacobian structure of neural networks. The key conceptual move is combining automatic differentiation's vector-Jacobian products with a decomposition that replaces full Jacobian estimation with expected Jacobians only for non-linearities (ReLU, max pooling), turning a sampling-heavy convolution into a single backward pass with a few forward passes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SmoothDiff makes SmoothGrad practically usable for multi-class explanations (2 orders of magnitude speedup) and for integration with higher-order XAI methods like spectral analysis and concept relevance propagation, which previously relied on LRP due to computational constraints. The honest characterization of the cross-covariance bias (magnitude converging to ~0.461 vs. 1.0) and its limited impact on heatmaps (which depend on direction, not magnitude) is a useful caveat. Open questions include extending VEJPs to attention layers (currently blocking ViT support), exploring quadrature-based alternatives to sampling, and propagating probability distributions in a single forward pass rather than sampling activations.
