## Problem

Existing video understanding benchmarks (MVBench, Video-MME, LongVideoBench, etc.) evaluate MLLMs on single-video inputs, overlooking the critical need for multi-video understanding that arises in real-world scenarios such as cross-angle sports analytics, multi-camera autonomous driving, and summarization across multiple retrieved videos. No prior benchmark systematically assesses a model's ability to aggregate, correlate, and reason across multiple video sources.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251800/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each question in MVU-Eval is accompanied by an average of 4.7 videos (ranging from 2 to 13), with each video resized to 720 pixels on the longer side and 32 frames uniformly sampled. The model receives the multi-video input alongside a text question with 4–5 multiple-choice options and must output a single letter answer. The 1,824 QA pairs are organized into two progressive levels: four perception subtasks (Object Recognition, Spatial Understanding, Counting, Comparison) and four reasoning subtasks (Knowledge-Intensive Reasoning, In-Context Learning, Retrieval-Augmented Generation, Temporal Reasoning), with video token lengths averaging 51,013 tokens per question.

## Method

MVU-Eval is constructed through a pipeline that samples task-specific video pairs from diverse open-source datasets (Kinetics-400, nuScenes, ScanNet, FineDiving, YouCook2, Vchitect-2.0, DREAM-1K), generates QA pairs via MLLM-based reject sampling and template-based formulation, and applies two rounds of quality control: (1) automated removal of content and format leakage by verifying that models cannot answer correctly without watching the videos, and (2) human verification of question utility and answer correctness, with easy questions filtered by requiring that Gemini 2.5 Pro, Gemini 2.0 Flash, and Qwen2.5-VL-72B all fail. Only ~46% of the originally generated 4,187 video pairs survive this process. Evaluation is conducted in a zero-shot setting using accuracy as the metric, with uniform 32-frame sampling at 720px resolution, and the benchmark is designed so that performance degrades monotonically as visual information is reduced (multi-video → single-video → multi-image → text description → no video), confirming that each task genuinely requires cross-video integration.

## Results

On MVU-Eval (1,824 QA pairs across 4,959 videos), the top-performing closed-source model Gemini 2.5 Pro achieves 58.4% overall accuracy, far below the 93.6% human expert performance and only modestly above the 26.0% random baseline. Among open-source models, Qwen2.5-VL-72B leads at 57.1%, narrowly trailing Gemini 2.5 Pro and surpassing GPT-4o (55.9%) and Gemini 2.0 Flash (56.3%). Performance varies substantially across subtasks: models score highest on Temporal Reasoning (up to 83.1% for Gemini 2.5 Pro) and lowest on In-Context Learning (up to 53.7% for Gemini 2.0 Flash), revealing significant capability imbalances in multi-video understanding.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Gemini 2.5 Pro (58.4%) edges out Gemini 1.5 Pro (57.3%) and Qwen2.5-VL-72B (57.1%) on overall accuracy, but Qwen2.5-VL-72B surpasses both GPT-4o (55.9%) and Gemini 2.0 Flash (56.3%), making it the strongest open-source model. On subtask level, Gemini 2.5 Pro dominates Counting (65.6% vs. 58.1% for Qwen2.5-VL-72B) and Temporal Reasoning (83.1% vs. 78.6%), while Qwen2.5-VL-72B leads on RAG (48.1% vs. 43.7%) and Comparison (77.8% vs. 76.3%). The Qwen2.5-VL series demonstrates consistent scaling gains (3B: 46.2% → 7B: 51.9% → 32B: 55.6% → 72B: 57.1%), as does InternVL3 (8B: 41.7% → 38B: 48.4% → 78B: 50.6%), though the latter lags Qwen2.5-VL at every size. Notably, LLaVA-Video-7B (27.4%) and LLaVA-NeXT-Video-7B (26.8%) perform near the random baseline (26.0%), with LLaVA-Video-7B scoring only 7.9% on KIR due to frequent failure to follow the instructed output format. Long-video-specialized models such as Video-XL-2-8B (43.7%) and Qwen2-7B-LongVILA-1M (32.7%) show that extended temporal context modeling alone does not translate to strong multi-video reasoning performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by extending the video understanding benchmark lineage (MVBench, LongVideoBench, Video-MME, MLVU, Video-MMLU) from single-video to multi-video inputs, a setting the authors argue is essential for real-world applications like multi-camera autonomous driving and cross-angle sports analytics. Rather than building on a specific prior method, it repurposes existing open-source video datasets (Kinetics-400, nuScenes, ScanNet, FineDiving, YouCook2, Vchitect-2.0, DREAM-1K) under new cross-video sampling rules and QA generation pipelines. The evaluation protocol draws on the standard zero-shot accuracy paradigm used across MLLM benchmarks, but introduces a multi-video input format that no prior benchmark supports.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MVU-Eval exposes a substantial capability gap: even the strongest closed-source model (Gemini 2.5 Pro) reaches only 58.4% versus 93.6% human accuracy, and most open-source MLLMs fall below 50%, indicating that cross-video aggregation and reasoning remain largely unsolved. The paper identifies concrete open directions—cross-video visual alignment for asynchronous streams, spatial understanding across complementary viewpoints, and scalable token-efficient fusion for high-cardinality video inputs—that are prerequisites for deploying MLLMs in multi-sensor systems. Limitations include relatively short video durations (no movie-length sequences) and the absence of audio, both of which constrain the benchmark's coverage of real-world multimodal scenarios.
