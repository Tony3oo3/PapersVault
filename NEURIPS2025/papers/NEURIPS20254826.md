## Problem

Existing 4D reconstruction methods from casual monocular videos rely on low-rank motion anchors (graph nodes, spline control points, learned motion bases) that assume neighboring regions share similar motion. This assumption breaks down under unconstrained dynamics where local deformations vary significantly across regions, leading to spatial drift, structural fragmentation, and temporal inconsistency. The paper asks whether a more stable, physically grounded signal—orientation—can organize diverse local motion without imposing rigid low-rank structure.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a casually captured monocular video (smartphone, handheld, or drone footage) and augments it with per-frame metric depth maps from a foundation model (e.g., Metric3D v2, DepthCrafter) and long-range 2D point trajectories from a tracker (e.g., CoTracker3, SpatialTracker). Camera poses are either provided or estimated via bundle adjustment. The output is a set of time-varying 3D Gaussian primitives parameterized by a hyperdimensional probabilistic state, rendered via standard alpha-blended rasterization to produce novel views at arbitrary timestamps and viewpoints, as well as point correspondences for tracking evaluation.

## Method

OriGS introduces two synergistic components. First, a Global Orientation Field is constructed by estimating each anchor's principal forward direction via PCA over a short temporal window of its 3D trajectory, then propagating these orientations across time through localized Procrustes alignment solved by SVD, yielding temporally coherent SO(3) anchors. Second, each 3D Gaussian primitive is elevated to an Orientation-aware Hyper-Gaussian: a multivariate Gaussian defined over a structured manifold spanning spatial offset, geometric variation (scale and rotation), time, and orientation, with a full covariance matrix (parameterized via Cholesky decomposition) capturing cross-group dependencies. At inference, a query (t′, O′)—where O′ is interpolated from neighboring anchors via SO(3) blending—triggers a conditioned Gaussian slice that yields the expected position and geometry deformation for that region. The deformed primitives are then modulated in position, scale, rotation, and opacity (via a confidence-weighted exponential in temporal and orientational distance) before standard 3DGS rasterization, enabling region-specific dynamics aligned with global motion intent.

## Results

On the DyCheck benchmark (7 scenes, multi-camera evaluation), OriGS achieves an average PSNR of 19.69, SSIM of 0.716, and LPIPS of 0.256 with ground-truth camera poses, and 19.30 / 0.685 / 0.270 without them. These are the best results across all three metrics among all compared methods, outperforming the closest baseline MoSca (18.84 PSNR, 0.676 SSIM, 0.289 LPIPS) by +0.46 dB PSNR and −0.019 LPIPS even without GT poses. On the point-tracking evaluation (PCK-T @0.05%), OriGS scores 0.851, surpassing MoSca (0.824), Marbles (0.806), and CoTracker (0.803).

## Baselines & Comparisons

OriGS outperforms all 16 baselines on the DyCheck average across PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS. The closest competitor is MoSca [39] (18.84 PSNR / 0.676 SSIM / 0.289 LPIPS with GT pose); OriGS without GT pose already exceeds it by +0.46 dB PSNR and −0.019 LPIPS, and with GT pose the gap widens to +0.85 dB PSNR and −0.033 LPIPS. Among 3DGS-based methods, OriGS leads Marbles [64] by +3.51 dB PSNR (19.30 vs. 15.79) and 4DGS [71] by +5.66 dB (19.30 vs. 13.64), while Dyn.GS [49] performs poorly (7.29 PSNR) due to its lack of structured motion modeling. Among NeRF-based methods, the best is CTNeRF [50] at 17.69 PSNR, still 1.61 dB below OriGS without GT pose. The only noted exception is the 'Space Out' scene, where OriGS with GT pose (20.87 PSNR) falls slightly below MoSca (22.57 PSNR), attributed to inaccurate pose metadata in the dataset as previously observed in [67]. On point tracking (PCK-T @0.05%), OriGS (0.851) beats MoSca (0.824), Marbles (0.806), and CoTracker (0.803), confirming superior correspondence quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

OriGS extends 3D Gaussian Splatting to dynamic scenes by departing from the dominant paradigm of low-rank motion anchors (graph nodes, spline control points, learned motion bases) used by methods like MoSca, Gaussian Marbles, and SplineGS. Instead of encoding deformation through positional or geometric anchors that assume neighboring regions share similar motion, OriGS introduces orientation (SO(3)) as the primary structural signal, constructing a Global Orientation Field via PCA initialization and Procrustes alignment. The hyperdimensional Gaussian formulation with conditioned slicing draws on the N-DG and 6DGS/7DGS line of work but repurposes the extra dimensions to jointly encode time, space, geometry, and orientation as a unified probabilistic state, with anchor-guided deformation inherited from dual-quaternion blending in DynamicFusion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a persistent weakness in dynamic 3DGS: the inability of low-rank motion priors to capture region-specific, direction-sensitive deformations in unconstrained real-world videos. By grounding deformation in a propagated orientation field, OriGS offers a principled mechanism for adaptive local dynamics that could generalize beyond the tested scenes. However, the method's reliance on external depth and trajectory priors from foundation models (DepthCrafter, SpatialTracker) introduces a dependency whose quality bounds reconstruction fidelity, and the authors acknowledge that for near-rigid motions the orientation conditioning adds complexity without proportional benefit. Open questions include whether adaptive dimensionality reduction could make the framework more efficient for simpler scenes, and whether the orientation field can be learned end-to-end rather than estimated from noisy monocular trajectories.
