## Problem

Offline goal-conditioned RL struggles to scale to long horizons because sparse rewards and discounting obscure the advantage of primitive actions with respect to distant goals. Hierarchical RL methods address this but require modular, timescale-specific policies and generative models over the subgoal space, introducing significant complexity that hinders scaling to high-dimensional goal spaces. The paper asks whether the core benefits of hierarchies can be distilled into a single, flat policy without a subgoal generator.

## Input / Output

The method takes a current state observation (a state vector of up to 69 dimensions, or a 64×64×3 third-person RGB image) and a goal state, and outputs a single primitive action from a unified, non-hierarchical policy network. Subgoals are not generated at inference time; instead, the policy is trained to internalize long-horizon reasoning by bootstrapping from in-trajectory waypoint states sampled k steps ahead in the dataset. The target subpolicy is a separate, smaller network trained on these nearby subgoals, while the main policy network uses an additional goal-encoder MLP with a 10-dimensional bottleneck projection.

## Method

SAW (Subgoal Advantage-Weighted Policy Bootstrapping) derives its objective from a variational inference framework that casts hierarchical policy optimization as probabilistic inference over an optimality variable for subgoals. The key insight is that the expectation over the optimal subgoal distribution can be estimated directly from dataset samples via Bayes' rule—weighting in-trajectory waypoints by their exponentiated subgoal advantage—eliminating the need for a generative subgoal model. Training proceeds in three stages: (1) a goal-conditioned value function is learned with GCIVL (expectile regression), (2) a target subpolicy is trained with AWR on subgoals sampled k steps into the future, and (3) the flat goal-conditioned policy is optimized with a combined objective that includes an advantage-weighted KL divergence to the subpolicy (the bootstrapping term) plus a one-step AWR term for stitching-heavy tasks. As the goal horizon increases, the one-step term naturally downweights itself, shifting emphasis toward the more stable policy bootstrapping signal.

## Results

Across 20 offline GCRL datasets (7 environments, state- and pixel-based), SAW achieves the best or near-best success rate on 18 of 20 tasks. Headline results include 73% on antmaze-giant-navigate (vs. 65% for HIQL, 0% for GCIVL) and 35% on humanoidmaze-giant-navigate, the first method to achieve non-trivial success on this task (previous SOTA: 12% for HIQL). On scene-play, which requires compositional long-horizon reasoning, SAW reaches 63% vs. 38% for HIQL. On visual-antmaze-medium, SAW achieves 95% success, matching or exceeding all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAW outperforms the strongest hierarchical baseline HIQL on 14 of 20 datasets, with the largest margins on long-horizon locomotion: +8 pts on antmaze-giant (73 vs. 65) and +23 pts on humanoidmaze-giant (35 vs. 12, the first non-trivial result on this task). Against one-step baselines (GCIVL, GCIQL, GCBC), SAW achieves dramatic gains on all long-horizon tasks where these methods collapse to near-zero success (e.g., 73% vs. 0% on antmaze-giant). QRL is competitive on pointmaze-large (86 vs. 85) and visual-antmaze-medium (94 vs. 95), but SAW dominates on all humanoidmaze and scene tasks. The offline RIS variant (RIS-off) underperforms SAW on most tasks, particularly in locomotion (e.g., 65 vs. 73 on antmaze-giant; 3 vs. 35 on humanoidmaze-giant), supporting the paper's argument that dataset-sampled subgoals with advantage weighting outperform generated subgoals. On visual manipulation tasks, SAW and HIQL achieve near-equivalent performance, suggesting representation learning rather than long-horizon reasoning is the bottleneck in those settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

SAW unifies three previously separate approaches—HIQL's bilevel hierarchical policy extraction, RIS's online policy bootstrapping, and a new flat-policy objective—under a single probabilistic inference (ELBO) framework derived from the MPO/EM lineage. The key departure from HIQL is eliminating the generative subgoal model entirely by replacing the expectation over optimal subgoals with advantage-weighted importance sampling from dataset trajectories, and the key departure from RIS is moving from online to offline settings while dropping the subgoal generator. The value learning component (GCIVL) and policy extraction component (AWR) are inherited directly from prior work, with the novelty concentrated in the bootstrapping objective and its theoretical derivation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By removing the generative subgoal model, SAW eliminates a major practical bottleneck for scaling offline GCRL to high-dimensional state spaces (e.g., 69-dim humanoid states, pixel observations), where learning a distribution over subgoals is intractable. The empirical finding that hierarchies' advantage in offline GCRL stems primarily from sampling efficiency for nearby subgoals rather than improved value SNR (as previously claimed) reframes how practitioners should think about hierarchical design. Open questions include whether the fixed k-step subgoal horizon from the behavior policy introduces unacceptable bias in highly suboptimal datasets, whether value function training can be stabilized for very long-horizon visual tasks, and whether the policy-bootstrapping variance-reduction hypothesis generalizes beyond the tested environments.
