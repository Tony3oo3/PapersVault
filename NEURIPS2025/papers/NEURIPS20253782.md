## Problem

Feed-forward 3DGS models suffer from representation overload as input views increase: the number of predicted 3D Gaussians scales linearly with views, causing severe performance degradation and out-of-memory failures beyond ~12 views. The paper identifies the root cause as the absence of an adaptive information compression mechanism in the encoder, and frames the issue through the Information Bottleneck principle, which had not previously been applied to feed-forward 3DGS.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253782/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes K multi-view RGB images (256×256 or 256×448) with known camera poses as input, extracts per-view features via a pretrained image encoder (CNN or DINO), and compresses them into N anchor-view features (e.g., 6 anchors from 12–36 views) before predicting pixel-aligned 3D Gaussian parameters (mean, covariance, opacity, color). Because compression occurs along the view dimension, the number of output Gaussians is governed by N rather than K, decoupling Gaussian count from input view count and enabling inference at 480P with 100+ views on an 80 GB GPU.

## Method

ZPressor is a lightweight, architecture-agnostic module inserted between the image encoder and the Gaussian predictor of any feed-forward 3DGS model. It operates in three steps: (1) selecting N anchor views via Farthest Point Sampling on 3D camera positions to maximize spatial coverage, (2) assigning each remaining support view to its nearest anchor by Euclidean camera distance, and (3) fusing support-view features into anchor views through stacked cross-attention blocks (anchor as query, support as key/value) interleaved with self-attention and MLP layers. Training follows the Variational Information Bottleneck formulation, combining a rendering loss (MSE + LPIPS) with a KL-divergence compression term that regularizes the latent toward a Gaussian prior, encouraging compact yet informative representations. The design is deliberately view-dimension-only, so it integrates seamlessly into pixelSplat, MVSplat, and DepthSplat without modifying their core architectures.

## Results

On DL3DV-10K with DepthSplat, ZPressor improves PSNR from 19.23 to 23.88 (+4.65) at 36 input views, from 20.38 to 24.26 (+3.88) at 24 views, and from 23.32 to 24.30 (+0.97) at 12 views. On RealEstate10K, MVSplat+ZPressor achieves 27.34 PSNR at 36 views (vs. 24.19 for MVSplat alone, +3.15), while pixelSplat+ZPressor reaches 26.59 PSNR where pixelSplat alone runs out of memory (OOM) on an 80GB GPU. The gains grow monotonically with input view density, confirming that ZPressor addresses the representation overload bottleneck in feed-forward 3DGS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ZPressor is integrated as a plug-and-play module into three feed-forward 3DGS baselines: DepthSplat, MVSplat, and pixelSplat. Against DepthSplat on DL3DV, ZPressor wins on all metrics at every view count, with the largest PSNR gain of +4.65 at 36 views (19.23→23.88) and a consistent SSIM improvement of +0.149. Against MVSplat on RE10K, ZPressor achieves +3.15 PSNR at 36 views (24.19→27.34) and +0.78 at 8 views (26.94→27.72), with LPIPS improving from 0.155 to 0.113 at 36 views. Against pixelSplat on RE10K, ZPressor is the only configuration that can run at 16, 24, and 36 views (pixelSplat OOMs on an 80GB GPU), achieving 26.59 PSNR at 36 views; at 8 views where both run, ZPressor adds +0.67 PSNR (26.19→26.86). In all head-to-head comparisons, ZPressor-augmented models strictly outperform their baselines on PSNR and SSIM, with the margin widening as input view density increases.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of feed-forward 3DGS (pixelSplat, MVSplat, DepthSplat) and the Information Bottleneck principle from representation learning. While prior compression efforts in 3DGS (StreamGS, CompGS, LightGaussian) target the 3D Gaussian primitives themselves, ZPressor instead compresses the multi-view input features before 3D prediction, operating at the encoder level. The anchor/support view partitioning with cross-attention repurposes standard Transformer components for a new purpose—explicitly modeling the IB compression score along the view dimension—rather than introducing a novel attention variant. The IB framing departs from the ad-hoc engineering fixes (memory-efficient attention, activation checkpointing) that prior works rely on to handle more views, offering a principled representation-learning justification instead.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ZPressor's architecture-agnostic design means it can be dropped into any future feed-forward 3DGS model, making it a potentially widely adopted component for scaling these systems to dense multi-view inputs (100+ views at 480P on 80GB GPUs). The IB analysis revealing that the optimal number of anchor views should adapt to scene information content (Fig. 6) provides a useful design principle beyond this specific module. Key open questions remain: handling extremely dense inputs (500+ views) still requires complementary 3D Gaussian merging or memory-efficient rendering, and the method's reliance on camera poses limits applicability to pose-free settings (though a pose-free variant is explored in the appendix).
