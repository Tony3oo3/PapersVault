## Problem

Existing brain alignment methods for speech models are participant-dependent and data-inefficient, requiring extensive per-participant fMRI data to estimate alignment and failing to generalize to new participants or datasets. This limits the use of pretrained speech models as scalable proxies for studying language processing across populations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254079/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 10-second audio clips (2s fMRI TR concatenated with 8s preceding context to account for hemodynamic delay) paired with spatially aligned fMRI voxel responses (~30K voxels across auditory and late language ROIs from the Glasser atlas) from multiple participants. It produces a brain-tuned speech model whose upper-middle layer representations are evaluated via per-voxel ridge regression encoding models, yielding noise-ceiling-normalized Pearson correlations as the brain alignment metric.

## Method

Multi-brain-tuning fine-tunes pretrained speech transformers (Wav2Vec2.0, HuBERT) with LoRA rank-8 updates (0.625% of parameters) and a unified projection head on top of an average pooling layer, trained to predict fMRI responses from multiple participants simultaneously. Participants are spatially aligned to a common cortical surface via FreeSurfer, and the model is trained by computing and backpropagating the L2 reconstruction loss independently and sequentially for each (stimulus, participant) pair rather than averaging responses or losses. The feature extractor remains frozen, and the method does not require all participants to share the same stimulus set—each stimulus serves as an anchor with all available participant responses presented consecutively. This joint multi-participant training with a shared projection head enables the model to learn generalizable representations that reduce fMRI data requirements by 5×, improve brain alignment by up to 50%, and generalize to unseen participants and out-of-distribution datasets.

## Results

Multi-brain-tuning reduces the fMRI encoding data needed to match pretrained-model brain alignment by a factor of five for both training and held-out participants, while achieving up to 50% higher brain alignment when full encoding data is used. On the out-of-distribution Narratives dataset (16 participants, novel stimuli), the Moth-Multi-brain-tuned model approaches the performance of a model tuned directly on Narratives data, demonstrating strong cross-dataset generalization. For downstream tasks (phoneme prediction and phonetic sentence type prediction), Multi-brain-tuned models never underperform the pretrained model and eventually match the LLM-tuned baseline as tuning data increases, with no evidence of catastrophic forgetting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Single-brain-tuned baseline (the Moussa et al. 2025 method applied to one participant), Multi-brain-tuning consistently outperforms across all data fractions for both training and held-out participants; for held-out participants, the Single-brain-tuned model requires roughly twice the encoding data to reach pretrained-level alignment, while Multi-brain-tuned reaches it with ~1/5 of the data. Against the LLM-tuned baseline (fine-tuning with Llama2-7B layer representations from Moussa et al. 2025), Multi-brain-tuning matches downstream task performance at larger tuning data sizes, while LLM-tuning shows a saturation trend in brain alignment that brain-tuning does not. Against the Stimulus-tuned baseline (self-supervised fine-tuning on the same audio, also from Moussa et al. 2025), brain-tuning clearly wins: stimulus-tuning produces no measurable improvement in brain alignment over the pretrained model, confirming that the gains come from the brain signal rather than simply seeing more audio. The Multi-brain-tuned model also outperforms the pretrained Wav2Vec2.0/HuBERT baselines by up to 50% in normalized brain alignment on late language ROIs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the recent line of brain-tuning work (Moussa et al., ICLR 2025; Vattikonda et al., 2025) that fine-tunes pretrained speech models on single-participant fMRI data to a multi-participant setting. The key conceptual departure is replacing participant-specific fine-tuning with joint training across participants using a unified projection head over FreeSurfer-aligned ROIs, combined with LoRA to keep the parameter budget small. It also positions itself against older multi-participant alignment methods (SRM, hyperalignment) that train encoding models from scratch rather than leveraging pretrained speech representations, and against brain-decoding approaches that use participant-specific projection networks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 5× reduction in per-participant fMRI data needed for reliable brain alignment and the strong cross-dataset generalization (Moth → Narratives) make population-level brain-language model studies substantially more feasible, which is a practical bottleneck in the field. The finding that multi-participant brain-tuning also improves downstream semantic task performance (matching LLM-tuning) suggests a bidirectional benefit between neuroscience and AI that could influence how speech models are trained more broadly. Key limitations include English-only data, focus on language ROIs, and a small number of participants (3 training, 5 held-out in Moth); open questions include multilingual generalization, non-language brain regions, and whether better loss functions can further improve data efficiency.
