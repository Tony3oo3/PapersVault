## Problem

Existing Riemannian sampling algorithms (e.g., Riemannian Langevin MCMC) achieve only polynomial poly(1/ε) iteration complexity, while high-accuracy polylog(1/ε) samplers are well-established in the Euclidean setting. No prior work had provided an affirmative answer to whether high-accuracy sampling is possible on general Riemannian manifolds, particularly under functional inequality assumptions like LSI rather than restrictive geodesic convexity.

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes a potential function f on a complete, connected Riemannian manifold (M, g) without boundary, a step size η, and an initial point x₀, and produces samples from the unnormalized target density π^X ∝ e^{-f} with respect to the Riemannian volume measure dV_g. A key practical constraint is that each iteration requires two non-trivial oracles: a Manifold Brownian Increment (MBI) oracle for the forward heat flow and a Riemannian Heat-kernel (RHK) oracle for the conditional sampling step, both of which lack closed-form solutions on general manifolds and must be approximated via heat-kernel truncation or Varadhan's asymptotics.

## Method

The Riemannian Proximal Sampler alternates two steps per iteration: (1) sampling a manifold Brownian increment y_k from the forward heat flow starting at x_k, and (2) sampling x_{k+1} from the conditional distribution proportional to e^{-f(x)}ν(η, x, y_k), which corresponds to the time-reversed diffusion process. Under LSI, the algorithm achieves O(log(1/ε)) iterations in KL divergence with exact oracles and O(log²(1/ε)) in total variation with inexact oracles satisfying a TV error bound of ζ = ε/log²(1/ε). Practical oracle implementations use truncated heat-kernel series (for compact homogeneous spaces) combined with rejection sampling against a Riemannian Gaussian proposal, or Varadhan's asymptotic approximation exp(−d(x,y)²/2η). The Varadhan-based variant is shown to be an entropy-regularized JKO scheme on the Wasserstein space, with the RHK step interpreted as a proximal operator prox_{ηH_{π^X}}(δ_y), extending the Euclidean proximal sampler interpretation to the Riemannian setting.

## Results

The Riemannian Proximal Sampler achieves the first high-accuracy (polylogarithmic) iteration complexity for sampling on Riemannian manifolds: Õ(log(1/ε)) iterations in KL divergence with exact oracles (Theorem 6), and Õ(log²(1/ε)) iterations in total variation distance with inexact oracles (Theorem 8). For the concrete case of the hypersphere S^d with L₁-Lipschitz potential, Corollary 10 gives Õ(L₁²d/α · log²(1/ε)) iterations in TV distance. Experimentally, on S² and S⁵ with von Mises-Fisher targets, the proximal sampler reaches lower Fréchet variance in fewer iterations than Riemannian LMC, and on the manifold of positive definite matrices P₃ it remains stable with large step sizes where RLMC diverges.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary theoretical comparison is against Li and Erdogdu [2023] and Cheng et al. [2022] (Table 1). The Riemannian Proximal Sampler improves the iteration complexity from polynomial in 1/ε (both prior works) to polylogarithmic in 1/ε, which is the first high-accuracy guarantee for Riemannian sampling. Li and Erdogdu [2023] achieve O(1/ε) in KL but require exact Brownian motion (feasible only on spheres) and product-of-spheres structure; Cheng et al. [2022] achieve Õ(1/ε²) in W₁ under a stronger 'distant-dissipativity' condition. Experimentally, the proximal sampler outperforms Riemannian Langevin Monte Carlo (RLMC) on both S²/S⁵ (von Mises-Fisher) and P₃ (positive definite matrices): it reaches lower Fréchet variance in fewer iterations, tolerates larger step sizes without bias, and remains stable on P₃ where RLMC diverges when the step size is increased from 0.0001 to 0.001 (due to the non-Lipschitz gradient of the potential).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper generalizes the Euclidean Proximal Sampler (Lee et al., 2021) to Riemannian manifolds by introducing two key oracles — Manifold Brownian Increments (MBI) and Riemannian Heat-kernel (RHK) — as the computational primitives. It departs from prior Riemannian sampling work (Li and Erdogdu 2023, Gatmiry and Vempala 2022) that requires exact Brownian motion (feasible only on spheres), and from Cheng et al. (2022) which achieves only poly(1/ε) complexity, by establishing the first polylog(1/ε) guarantees on general manifolds under LSI/PI assumptions. The analysis draws on the LSI propagation framework of Chen et al. (2022) and the diffusion-process interpretation, while the Varadhan-asymptotics implementation connects to the entropy-regularized JKO scheme on Wasserstein spaces (Jordan et al. 1998, Peyré 2015).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to establish high-accuracy (polylog) sampling guarantees on Riemannian manifolds, closing a gap between the rich Euclidean high-accuracy sampling literature and the manifold setting where only polynomial guarantees existed. The oracle abstraction (MBI + RHK) provides a clean modular framework, and the heat-kernel truncation implementation is verified for homogeneous spaces (spheres, Stiefel, Grassmann, SPD matrices). Key open questions include extending the truncation-based oracle to non-homogeneous manifolds, characterizing precise parameter dependencies beyond ε, and establishing non-asymptotic rates for geodesic random walk as an MBI implementation. The curvature-dependent step-size constraint for negative curvature (η ≤ 1/|κ|) is a fundamental limitation that may affect practical applicability on manifolds with large negative Ricci curvature.
