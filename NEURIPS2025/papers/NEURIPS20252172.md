## Problem

Depthwise separable CNNs employ thousands of distinct trained filters, yet prior work suggested these filters cluster into Gaussian-related patterns without constraining their cardinality. The 'Master Key Filters Hypothesis' proposed the existence of general-purpose filter sets but left open how many such filters are actually needed. This paper asks whether a radically small, fixed set of universal filters can replace the thousands of learned depthwise kernels while preserving classification accuracy.

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard RGB images at resolutions ranging from 32×32 (CIFAR-10) to 224×224 or 384×384 (ImageNet, Oxford Flowers/Pets) and outputs class labels. The unusual constraint is that all depthwise 7×7 convolutional kernels are frozen to one of 8 fixed spatial patterns, with only a per-channel learnable bias (the 'b' in ax+b) remaining trainable; the scaling coefficient 'a' is absorbed into the subsequent pointwise/MLP layer, so the effective per-channel operation reduces to a fixed filter plus a scalar offset.

## Method

The authors first distill over one million 7×7 depthwise filters from 49 pretrained models across five architecture families (ConvNeXtV2, ConvNeXt, MogaNet, HorNet, ConvMixer), normalize them, and train an autoencoder that compresses each filter to a single scalar code. Uniform sampling from this 1D code space yields candidate filters, and each original filter is approximated by the closest linear shift (ax+b) of a candidate via closed-form linear regression. A greedy removal search over 50 candidates identifies a minimal set of 8 filters whose linear-shift approximations preserve accuracy across model sizes and architectures. Finally, models are trained from scratch with all depthwise filters frozen to these 8 patterns (plus learnable biases), achieving >80% ImageNet accuracy and outperforming ImageNet transfer learning on smaller datasets like Oxford Flowers and Pets.

## Results

On ImageNet, ConvNeXtV2 models trained from scratch with only 8 frozen universal filters (plus learnable biases) achieve 82.7% top-1 accuracy (Tiny), 84.6% (Base), and 85.4% (Large), matching or slightly exceeding their fully-trained counterparts with thousands of trainable filters. On smaller datasets, the 8-filter approach dramatically outperforms both from-scratch training and ImageNet transfer learning: on Oxford Flowers (Femto), it reaches 77.7% vs. 66.0% (original) and 73.2% (transfer); on Oxford Pets (Femto), 66.4% vs. 36.3% (original) and 56.0% (transfer). Replacing all ~50K filters in ConvNeXtV2-Huge with linear shifts of just 8 patterns causes only a 3.5% accuracy drop without fine-tuning, while 8 random filters cause catastrophic failure (~0.1%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the original ConvNeXtV2 models (Woo et al., 2023), the 8-filter approach matches or slightly exceeds accuracy on ImageNet: 82.7% vs. 82.5% (Tiny), 84.6% vs. 84.3% (Base), and 85.4% vs. 84.5% (Large), despite freezing all spatial filters. Against HorNet (Rao et al., 2022), the filters transfer across architectures with only a 0.5% drop (81.8% vs. 82.3%), confirming cross-architecture universality. Against ImageNet-pretrained ConvNeXt transfer (Liu et al., 2022), the 8 frozen filters decisively win on smaller datasets: +4.5% on Oxford Flowers (77.7% vs. 73.2%), +10.4% on Oxford Pets (66.4% vs. 56.0%), and +2.7% on STL-10 (83.1% vs. 83.2% is a near-tie, but +2.7% on CIFAR-10 is 96.3% vs. 97.1% where transfer slightly wins). The advantage grows as dataset size shrinks, suggesting the 8 filters encode task-agnostic visual primitives that reduce overfitting risk in low-data regimes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Master Key Filters Hypothesis (Babaiee et al., AAAI 2025) and the earlier cluster analysis of depthwise convolutional kernels (Babaiee et al., ICLR 2023), which showed that trained DS-CNN filters converge into Gaussian-related clusters. Where prior work identified an effectively infinite continuum of DoG-like structures with arbitrary parameters, this paper radically constrains the hypothesis to a basis of exactly 8 filters, demonstrating that thousands of learned filters are predominantly linear shifts (ax+b) of this minimal set. The methodology draws on autoencoder-based dimensionality reduction and greedy search, while the theoretical grounding connects to scale-space theory (Lindeberg) and Gaussian derivative models of biological receptive fields.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

If DS-CNNs truly converge to 8 universal spatial operators, this has profound implications for efficient architecture design (frozen filters + learnable biases could replace thousands of trainable parameters), transfer learning (filters transfer across architectures without modification), and understanding generalization in visual processing. The connection to classical image processing primitives (DoG, Gaussian derivatives) and mammalian visual receptive fields suggests these networks rediscover optimal operators aligned with natural image statistics. Open questions include whether the set can be further reduced, whether it generalizes to non-DS-CNN architectures or different kernel sizes, and whether direct optimization could yield an even more compact or effective basis. Limitations include the absence of error bars, testing restricted to 7×7 filters, and the greedy search being performed on a single model size before transfer.
