## Problem

Synthesizing RF data (RSSI, spatial spectra, CSI) from transmitter and receiver positions is critical for wireless localization and sensing, but existing NeRF-based methods suffer from long training times (hours) and high inference latency (hundreds of milliseconds) due to stochastic sampling and MLP-based optimization, precluding real-time deployment. The paper addresses this by extending 3D Gaussian Splatting from the optical domain to RF, overcoming three fundamental differences: RF signals require complex-valued (amplitude + phase) modeling rather than real-valued color, are captured over a spherical surface rather than a 2D image plane, and require interference-aware rendering rather than simple alpha-blending.

## Input / Output

The method takes a fixed transmitter position and a set of 3D receiver positions as input, and produces RF data whose format depends on the receiver hardware: a 360×90 spatial spectrum matrix (one-degree angular resolution over azimuth and front-hemisphere elevation) for antenna-array receivers, a 26-element complex-valued CSI vector for 5G OFDM subcarriers, or a scalar real-valued RSSI in dBm for single-antenna receivers. Rays are emitted from a Ray Emitting Spherical Surface (RESS) of radius 1.0 m centered at each receiver, with 360×180 rays covering all directions at one-degree resolution. Scene initialization uses cube-based partitioning rather than Structure-from-Motion, since visual data is typically unavailable in RF settings.

## Method

GSRF represents the RF scene as a set of 3D Gaussian primitives, each parameterized by a mean, covariance matrix, complex-valued radiance, and complex-valued transmittance. The radiance is modeled via a Fourier-Legendre Expansion (FLE) with complex coefficients c_ml that encode both amplitude and phase as a function of azimuth and elevation, replacing the spherical harmonics used in optical 3DGS and better capturing diffraction and phase-dependent interference. Orthographic splatting projects each Gaussian onto a 2D RF plane (mapped from the RESS via spherical-to-grid coordinates) to identify ray-Gaussian intersections without the projective Jacobian used in camera-based splatting. A complex-valued ray tracing algorithm, executed on custom CUDA kernels, aggregates contributions along each ray by multiplying the Gaussian density weight, the complex radiance, and the cumulative product of preceding transmittances (modeling both amplitude attenuation and phase shift per the Huygens-Fresnel principle). Training uses a composite loss combining L1, SSIM, and 2D Fourier-domain terms, with explicit gradient computation for all Gaussian attributes and adaptive density control (cloning, splitting, removal) driven by mean-gradient thresholds—eliminating all MLP regressors present in NeRF² and WRF-GS.

## Results

On the RFID spatial spectrum task (sparse, 0.8 measurements/ft³), GSRF achieves median improvements of 21.2% in PSNR and 56.4% in MSE over NeRF², and 5.7%/19.3% over WRF-GS, while converging in 0.27 hours (18.56× faster than NeRF²) and synthesizing spectra in 4.18 ms (84.39× faster than NeRF²). On 5G CSI synthesis, GSRF reaches a mean SNR of 20.99 dB using only 30% of the training data, matching NeRF²'s quality with 3× less data. On BLE RSSI synthesis, GSRF achieves 4.09 dBm average error versus NeRF²'s 6.09 dBm (32.79% improvement), with 15.82× faster training and 78.98× faster inference.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GSRF consistently outperforms all baselines across all three RF tasks. Against NeRF² (the primary NeRF-based baseline), GSRF achieves 21.2% higher PSNR and 56.4% lower MSE on RFID spatial spectra, 32.79% lower RSSI error on BLE, and comparable 5G CSI SNR (20.99 dB) while using 3× less training data; it is also 18.56× faster to train and 84.39× faster at inference on RFID. Against WRF-GS (the closest 3DGS-based competitor), GSRF gains 5.7% PSNR and 19.3% MSE on RFID, with 5.96× faster training and 1.81× faster inference, attributed to GSRF's elimination of MLP regressors in favor of directly optimized per-Gaussian complex-valued attributes. Against FIRE (VAE-based) on 5G CSI, GSRF achieves higher SNR, demonstrating that explicit complex-valued Gaussian representation with Fourier-Legendre radiance outperforms latent-space approaches for phase-aware channel prediction. The BLE localization application further shows GSRF-generated fingerprint databases yield 31.40% lower localization error than NeRF²-generated ones, confirming that synthesis fidelity directly translates to downstream sensing accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

GSRF extends 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) from the optical rendering domain to RF signal synthesis, addressing three domain-specific challenges: replacing spherical harmonics with a Fourier-Legendre Expansion to capture phase-dependent interference, introducing orthographic splatting onto a Ray Emitting Spherical Surface (RESS) to handle the absence of a camera image plane, and designing a complex-valued ray tracing algorithm grounded in the Huygens-Fresnel principle. It departs from NeRF²'s voxel-based, MLP-querying architecture by using explicit per-Gaussian complex-valued attributes (radiance and transmittance) optimized directly via SGD, eliminating the computational bottleneck of dense MLP evaluations. Compared to the concurrent WRF-GS, which still relies on a large MLP to predict per-Gaussian attributes, GSRF removes MLP regressors entirely, and unlike RF-3DGS's two-stage optical-then-RF training, GSRF operates purely on RF data with a unified complex-valued pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 84× inference speedup (4.18 ms vs. 352.73 ms) and 18.6× training speedup over NeRF² make real-time RF synthesis feasible for latency-sensitive applications such as sub-millisecond 5G tracking and indoor localization, where NeRF-based methods are impractical. The 9.8× reduction in required training data is practically significant for site surveys, which are labor-intensive and costly. However, GSRF lacks spatial generality (no zero-shot transfer to unseen environments) and is limited to static scenes, requiring retraining when the environment changes; the authors outline deformable Gaussian extensions and multi-scene pre-training as future directions to address these gaps.
