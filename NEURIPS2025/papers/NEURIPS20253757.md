## Problem

Balanced alignment of LLMs across Helpfulness, Honesty, and Harmlessness (3H) remains challenging because these objectives exhibit conflicting optimization signals: helpfulness training can increase harm, while harmlessness training can reduce honesty. Existing data mixture strategies require substantial domain expertise and computational resources, while model merging's potential for 3H optimization has been largely unexplored, with no systematic comparison between the two paradigms or understanding of how preference noise accumulation and layer-specific sparsity affect merging quality.

## Input / Output

The method takes a base instruction-tuned model (Llama-3-8B-Instruct or Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.2) and n individually DPO-trained models, each optimized on a different annotation dimension (Helpfulness, Honesty, Harmlessness, Helpfulness&Honesty, Helpfulness&Harmlessness) using 10 preference datasets. It also requires sparsity hyperparameters (γ₀=0.2, γ=0.6, ε=0.1) that control the dynamic rank schedule. The output is a single merged model whose weights are computed layer-by-layer via reweighted singular value decomposition, requiring no additional training after the individual DPO stages.

## Method

RESM (Reweighting-Enhanced Task Singular Merging) extends TSVM by addressing two overlooked challenges in multi-objective LLM merging. First, it applies outlier-aware weighting to singular values: for each layer and model, it computes row-wise mean and standard deviation of parameter deviations, hard-thresholds at μ+3σ to suppress preference noise, and derives L1-normalized aggregation weights α that reweight singular values to prevent noise-dominated updates from obscuring true optimization directions. Second, it replaces the fixed top-k rank with a sparsity-adaptive rank k_l = ⌊d_l(γ₀ + γΩ_l)⌋, where Ω_l is the consensus sparsity across all models at layer l, so that sparse layers (concentrated updates) retain more singular components while dense layers undergo stronger compression. The final merged layer is θ_l^0 + Σ_i U_{l⊥}^{(i)}[:,:k_l] · (α_l^{(i)} S_l^{(i)}) · V_{l⊥}^{(i)⊤}[:,k_l,:], where orthogonal projections U_{l⊥} and V_{l⊥} are obtained via decorrelation-based whitening to decouple task-specific subspaces.

## Results

On Llama-3-8B-Instruct, RESM achieves a normalized 3H gain of +14.97% (Helpful_Avg=59.62, Honest_Avg=58.20, Harmless_Avg=79.60), outperforming the best data mixture method Hummer (+10.16%) and the best prior merging method TSVM (+13.00%). On Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.2, RESM achieves +3.36% normalized gain (Helpful_Avg=43.15, Honest_Avg=61.50, Harmless_Avg=82.25) versus Hummer (+1.53%) and TSVM (+2.45%). The largest absolute improvement is in Harmlessness, where RESM pushes OR-Bench from 26.97 (base) to 69.27 on Llama3, while maintaining or improving Helpfulness and Honesty simultaneously.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RESM outperforms all 12 training-free model merging baselines on both Llama3 and Mistral. Against the closest competitor TSVM (the SVD-based method it builds upon), RESM gains +1.97% on Llama3 and +0.91% on Mistral in normalized 3H gain, with the improvement concentrated in Harmlessness (OR-Bench: 69.27 vs. 67.76) and Honesty (HaluEval-Wild: 58.20 vs. 56.20). Against the best data mixture method Hummer, RESM achieves a 1.5× improvement on Llama3 (+14.97% vs. +10.16%) and a 2.2× improvement on Mistral (+3.36% vs. +1.53%), confirming that parameter-level merging is more effective than data-level filtering for 3H conflict resolution. Among sparsification-based methods, results are inconsistent: DELLA achieves +10.95% on Llama3 but only +0.50% on Mistral, while DARE shows +6.16% on Llama3 but +0.56% on Mistral, highlighting the instability of random sparsification. Linear interpolation methods (Weight Average, Rewarded Soup, Task Arithmetic) consistently underperform SVD-based methods, with Task Arithmetic achieving only +2.83% on Llama3. Against outlier-based pruning methods (Wanda, Owl) applied post-hoc to TSVM, RESM's integrated outlier-aware weighting during merging yields +1.80% and +1.50% additional gain respectively on Llama3.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Task Singular Vector Merging (TSVM) framework [60] by introducing outlier-aware reweighting of singular values and sparsity-adaptive rank selection, directly addressing two challenges (preference noise accumulation and layer-specific sparsity) that prior SVD-based merging methods overlook. It positions itself against the broader model merging literature for LLM alignment (TIES, DARE, DELLA, Breadcrumbs, Rewarded Soup, Task Arithmetic, Model Stock) and data mixture strategies (ArmoRM, Hummer, heuristic mixing), but departs from prior work by being the first to systematically benchmark parameter-level merging against data-level mixture specifically for the 3H (Helpfulness, Honesty, Harmlessness) optimization problem. Unlike earlier merging studies that focused on single-objective transfer, safety-utility trade-offs in narrow multilingual settings, or partial 3H evaluations, this paper treats all three dimensions simultaneously across multiple model families and training settings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in demonstrating that training-free model merging can match or modestly exceed data mixture methods (2–5% normalized gain) for balanced 3H alignment, offering a cheaper and more flexible alternative that avoids the heavy data curation and conflicting gradient signals inherent in full mixture training. The identification of specific collaborative and conflict relationships among the 3H dimensions (e.g., helpfulness training benefits all three, while honesty and harmlessness training weaken each other) provides actionable guidance for alignment practitioners. However, the methodological gains over the best existing merging baseline (TSVM) are modest (~2% normalized gain), evaluations are limited to two 7–8B model families, and open questions remain about whether RESM's advantages persist at larger scales, with more alignment objectives, or under RLHF rather than DPO training paradigms.
