## Problem

Standard probabilistic inference in sensory circuits assumes latent causes are a priori independent, yet real-world generative factors are typically correlated. Incorporating pairwise correlations in the prior requires direct interactions among latent cause units, which is architecturally infeasible for large N and biologically implausible (e.g., no direct granule cell connections have been observed in the olfactory bulb). This paper asks whether correlated priors can be implemented without any direct interactions between latent cause units.

## Input / Output

The circuit takes M noisy receptor activations (linear measurements of N latent features corrupted by Gaussian noise) and produces MAP estimates of the N latent feature concentrations at the steady state of granule cell dynamics. Sister cell activities, which are simultaneously produced, serve as a secondary readout from which the encoded correlated prior can be estimated under a sparsity assumption on latent representations. The number of features for which correlated priors can be encoded is bounded by S − M, where S is the total number of sister cells across all input channels and M is the number of receptor channels.

## Method

The core mechanism uses sister cells—neurons receiving the same receptor input but with different synaptic weights to granule cells—to implement pairwise prior correlations indirectly through the covariance of their connectivity patterns. The loss decomposes into a likelihood term governed by mean connectivity and a prior term governed by connectivity covariance, so that correlated connectivity among sisters implies anti-correlation in the prior and vice versa. A geometric construction via SVD and null-space projection of a constraint matrix B determines weight matrices achieving a desired correlation structure while satisfying zero-affinity constraints, with the number of encodable features bounded by S − M. The paper further shows that under a grandmother-cell sparsity assumption, the encoded prior can be recovered from sister cell response covariances alone, and demonstrates that different connectivity solutions (random, sparse, weighted-sparse) produce distinct patterns of response heterogeneity that qualitatively match experimental olfactory bulb recordings.

## Results

In a simulated olfactory bulb with 50 input channels, 200 latent units, and 4–9 sister cells per channel, the correlated-prior circuit reduces the Euclidean error between true and inferred feature vectors relative to the independent-prior (vanilla) circuit at all tested receptor noise levels except the highest (σ_n = 10), where both circuits perform equally poorly (Fig. 2C). Inference converges within a few hundred milliseconds using biologically realistic time constants (τ_mc = 50 ms, τ_gc = 100 ms), consistent with respiration time. The geometric construction guarantees that the number of latents with encoded correlations is bounded by S − M (total sisters minus input channels), and multiple connectivity solutions (random, sparse, weighted-sparse) can realize the same prior with different response-heterogeneity profiles.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is between the proposed correlated-prior circuit and a vanilla circuit that assumes a priori independence among latent features. The correlated-prior circuit consistently achieves lower inference error (Euclidean norm between true and estimated feature vectors) across a range of receptor noise standard deviations (σ_n from 0.1 to 10), with the advantage being most pronounced at moderate noise levels and disappearing only at the highest noise where both circuits saturate. A secondary comparison examines three connectivity solutions (random, uniformly sparse, and weighted-sparse) that all produce identical priors and affinities but differ in sister-cell response heterogeneity; the weighted-sparse solution qualitatively matches the per-channel heterogeneity statistics observed in experimental olfactory bulb recordings [9], though the pooled distribution of response similarities still differs from the experimental data. No other external baselines or competing inference architectures are benchmarked.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends Tootoonian et al. (2022, PLOS Comp Bio), which showed that sister mitral cells in the olfactory bulb can implement MAP inference with independent priors using sparse connectivity. The key departure here is encoding correlated priors (Gaussian Markov random fields) without requiring O(N²) direct interactions between granule cells, by exploiting the covariance of sister cell connectivity to latent units. The geometric construction via SVD and null-space rotation is a new contribution that provides both a constructive algorithm and a fundamental bound (n ≤ S − M) on how many correlated features can be encoded. The paper also introduces a novel comparison to experimental sister cell recordings from Zhang et al. (2025, synchrotron X-ray nanotomography), linking the theoretical framework to measured olfactory bulb circuitry.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper resolves a concrete architectural challenge in neural inference circuits: how to implement structured (correlated) priors when direct interactions between latent units are biologically implausible or computationally prohibitive. The bound n ≤ S − M provides a clear design constraint, and the ability to estimate priors from sister cell responses alone (under a grandmother-cell assumption) offers a practical route to reverse-engineering the statistics a circuit encodes. However, the framework relies on a linear isotropic Gaussian observation model and a single-stimulus evaluation, and the estimated priors from experimental data show near-zero correlation for odour pairs that co-occur in essential oils, raising open questions about whether the assumed latent representation is appropriate. Extending the construction to non-linear models and showing how connectivity can be learned from natural stimulus statistics are identified as important next steps.
