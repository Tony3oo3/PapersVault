## Problem

Existing 3D generation methods produce fused meshes without explicit part boundaries, while prior part-level approaches depend on 2D segmentation priors and process parts sequentially, causing inference time to scale linearly with part count. The paper addresses how to generate an arbitrary number of complete, semantically meaningful 3D parts end-to-end from a single image, without external segmentation models and with fixed inference time.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250914/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single-view RGB image (no pose alignment required; the model learns pose-invariant generation by mapping diverse viewpoints to canonical space) and produces a part-level 3D mesh with an arbitrary number of complete, separable parts. Internally, the model generates two SDF volumes at 512³ resolution; parts within each volume are disjoint (non-contacting) and are extracted as separate connected components via Marching Cubes, then assembled into the final object. The entire pipeline runs in approximately 30 seconds regardless of the number of parts.

## Method

The core contribution is a dual volume packing strategy that converts the part-connectivity graph into a bipartite graph via greedy odd-cycle edge contraction (contracting the edge with the greatest penetration depth until no odd cycles remain), allowing all parts to be packed into exactly two volumes where no two contacting parts share a volume. The architecture pairs a VecSet-based VAE (with salient edge sampling from Dora) that encodes/decodes the dual volumes, with a rectified flow model (from Trellis) that denoises a concatenated pair of latent codes simultaneously, using a learnable part embedding on the second code to prevent duplicate generation. DINOv2 provides image conditioning through cross-attention. Training follows a progressive schedule: the VAE and flow model are first pretrained on fused shapes, then finetuned on part-level data with the dual-volume formulation, using up to 256 A100 GPUs over roughly two weeks for the finetuning stage.

## Results

On a 40-image test set evaluated with ULIP, ULIP-2, and Uni3D cosine-similarity metrics, the fused-shape pretrained model (Ours†) achieves the best ULIP score (0.1729) and Uni3D score (0.3941), marginally outperforming TripoSG (0.1726 / 0.3912). The part-level dual-volume model (Ours) scores 0.1715 / 0.3986 / 0.3906 on ULIP / ULIP-2 / Uni3D, remaining competitive with all baselines while producing separable parts. In terms of inference speed, the method generates part-level meshes in ~30 seconds regardless of part count, whereas HoloPart requires several minutes of preprocessing plus per-part completion that scales linearly with the number of parts.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Hunyuan3D-2 and Hi3DGen, the proposed method (both fused and dual-volume variants) wins on all three metrics (ULIP, ULIP-2, Uni3D), with the fused version leading by ~0.012 on ULIP and ~0.007 on Uni3D over Hunyuan3D-2. Compared to TripoSG, the fused-initialized model (Ours†) edges out TripoSG on ULIP (0.1729 vs 0.1726) and Uni3D (0.3941 vs 0.3912) but trails slightly on ULIP-2 (0.4022 vs 0.4048); the part-level dual-volume model is marginally behind TripoSG on all three metrics, reflecting the trade-off of structural decomposition. Against HoloPart (the primary part-level baseline), the comparison is qualitative: the paper reports that HoloPart's segmentation-then-completion pipeline is slow and error-prone, while the proposed method directly generates complete, semantically meaningful parts in a fixed ~30 seconds. The paper does not report a head-to-head quantitative metric for part-level quality against HoloPart, relying instead on visual comparison (Figure 5) and inference-time analysis.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the VecSet-based latent denoising paradigm (3DShape2VecSet, Dora, Trellis) to the part-level generation setting, where prior methods like HoloPart, PartGen, and SAMPart3D rely on a segmentation-then-completion pipeline that depends on 2D segmentation priors and processes parts sequentially. The key conceptual departure is the dual volume packing strategy, which reformulates the variable-part-count problem as a bipartite graph coloring task, allowing a fixed-length dual-latent output compatible with existing rectified flow architectures. By leveraging the observation that most real-world part-connectivity graphs are (or can be made) bipartite through greedy odd-cycle contraction, the method avoids both the error propagation of external segmentation and the linear inference scaling of per-part processing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The fixed ~30-second inference time regardless of part count is a practical efficiency win over segmentation-then-completion pipelines whose cost scales linearly with part number, making this approach more viable for interactive editing and large-scale asset generation. However, the bipartite constraint inherently limits expressiveness—three mutually contacting parts cannot be represented in two volumes—and the lack of explicit part granularity control (relying on noisy scene-graph annotations) constrains user-directed editing. Open questions include whether extending to planar graph packing (four-color theorem) or incorporating segmentation-mask conditioning can address these limitations without sacrificing the end-to-end simplicity.
