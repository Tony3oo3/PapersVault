## Problem

Existing convergence guarantees for τ-leaping samplers in discrete diffusion models rely on hard-to-verify regularity assumptions (via Girsanov change-of-measure) and exhibit quadratic dependence on vocabulary size S, which is often very large in NLP (e.g., S=50257). Additionally, no theoretical convergence guarantees exist for the more sampling-efficient Euler method and Tweedie τ-leaping samplers that are widely used in practice.

## Input / Output

The analysis takes as input an initial sample drawn from Uniform([S]^d), a score-entropy-trained score function s_t bounded in [M^{-1}, M], and a discretization grid {t_k} with step size κ·min{1, T−t_k}. The output is a discrete data sample in [S]^d whose KL-divergence from the target distribution is bounded by Õ(d²S/ε) sampling steps. The key assumption is a sum-averaged score-entropy estimation error over the discrete grid (not path-wise or per-sample maximum), making it practically verifiable.

## Method

The core contribution is a differential-inequality-based framework that directly bounds the rate-of-change of KL divergence between the true reverse process and the sampling distribution by invoking the Kolmogorov forward equation, eliminating the need for Girsanov change-of-measure regularity conditions. Theorem 1 establishes a general KL-divergence decomposition where per-step error is characterized by a Bregman divergence generated by the negative entropy function over the rate matrices. For τ-leaping, the proof identifies the dominant discretization error term and bounds it in expectation over x_t (rather than uniformly over all x,y), which reduces the vocabulary-size dependence from O(S²) to O(S). For the Euler method and Tweedie τ-leaping, the paper constructs a Truncated τ-leaping sampler with an explicit intermediate rate matrix, proves it is asymptotically equivalent to both samplers in categorical sampling probabilities, and then applies the same KL-divergence decomposition to transfer the convergence guarantee.

## Results

The paper establishes that τ-leaping achieves ε-KL accuracy in Õ(d²S/ε) sampling steps under a sum-averaged score-entropy estimation assumption (Assumption 1), improving the prior best bound of O(d²S²/ε) from [18] by a factor of S. The same Õ(d²S/ε) rate is shown for the Euler method and Tweedie τ-leaping (Theorem 3), which additionally reduce per-step sampling complexity from O(Sd) Poisson draws to O(d) categorical draws. All results require no path-wise regularity conditions (e.g., local martingale properties), unlike prior Girsanov-based analyses.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against [18] (Ren et al., ICLR 2025), the paper's τ-leaping bound improves the S-dependence from quadratic (O(d²S²/ε)) to linear (O(d²S/ε)), a factor-of-S gain that is significant for large vocabularies (e.g., S=50257 in GPT-2 tasks). The paper also removes the path-wise regularity assumptions (local martingale conditions) that [18] requires for its Girsanov-based proof. Against [9] (Campbell et al., NeurIPS 2022), the improvement is more dramatic: from O(d⁴S⁴/√ε) under a strong per-sample L∞ rate-estimation assumption to O(d²S/ε) under a weaker sum-averaged score-entropy assumption. Against [19] (Zhang et al., ICLR 2025), the Kolmogorov sampler has a better ε-dependence (√ε vs ε) but suffers exponential per-step cost O(S³d) due to matrix exponentials, making it impractical. Against [20] (Pham et al., 2025), the DMPM Euler-type method has a much worse step count (exponential in d) and a stronger per-sample max assumption. The paper's Euler and Tweedie τ-leaping results (Theorem 3) are the first convergence guarantees for these practically-used samplers, matching the τ-leaping rate while reducing per-step sampling from O(Sd) to O(d) draws.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the differential-inequality-based analysis from Chen et al. [24] (developed for continuous-space diffusion) to the discrete CTMC setting, replacing the Fokker-Planck equation with the Kolmogorov forward equation and characterizing error accumulation via Bregman divergence rather than Fisher divergence. It builds directly on the CTMC formulation and τ-leaping sampler from Campbell et al. [9] and the score-entropy loss with Euler/Tweedie samplers from Lou et al. [10], while departing from the Girsanov change-of-measure framework used by all prior discrete diffusion convergence analyses [17, 18, 19, 20]. The key conceptual departure is showing that path-wise regularity conditions (local martingale requirements) are unnecessary, and that bounding the score difference in expectation rather than uniformly over (x,y) reduces the vocabulary-size dependence from quadratic to linear.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The linear-in-S convergence bound for τ-leaping has direct practical implications for NLP tasks where vocabulary sizes reach ~50,000, and the first convergence guarantees for the Euler and Tweedie τ-leaping samplers (which are the methods actually used in practice) close a significant gap between theory and empirical usage. The differential-inequality technique is presented as a general tool that may apply to other stochastic processes beyond discrete diffusion. Open questions include whether the d² dependence can be further reduced, whether acceleration techniques can improve the overall rate, and whether the analysis extends to non-constant noise schedules or more general rate matrix structures beyond the absorbing-state formulation.
