## Problem

Open-source omnimodal LLMs face three critical gaps: (1) they require scarce, expensive tri-modal (image-speech-text) datasets for alignment, (2) autoregressive speech generation introduces prohibitive latency for real-time interaction, and (3) existing models produce emotionally flat speech that fails to modulate prosody based on conversational context. OpenOmni targets all three by decoupling omnimodal alignment from tri-modal data, replacing sequential speech decoding with a parallel decoder, and introducing preference-based emotional speech training.

## Input / Output

The model accepts images (encoded by CLIP-ViT-L), speech audio (encoded by Whisper-large-v3), and text prompts simultaneously or in any combination. It produces both a text response and a synchronized speech waveform via a discrete-unit vocoder. The speech decoder operates in two modes: an autoregressive mode (16K-vocabulary GLM4-Voice tokenizer) for higher quality, and a non-autoregressive mode (6K-vocabulary CosVoice tokenizer) that generates up to 30 seconds of speech in under 1 second. Outputs are bilingual (Chinese and English), and the emotional DPO stage ensures the synthesized speech carries contextually appropriate affect (nine Plutchik emotion categories).

## Method

OpenOmni uses a progressive two-stage alignment strategy that leverages text as a pivot: it first trains a speech encoder on speech-text pairs, then trains an image encoder on image-text pairs, achieving near-zero-shot cross-modal transfer without any tri-modal data. The speech generation component is a lightweight streaming decoder (Qwen2.5-0.5B) with a mixture-of-experts layer and CTC loss, preceded by a text-guided module that fuses LLM hidden states with ground-truth text features via cross-attention to prevent misalignment during training. For emotional speech, the paper introduces CTC-DPO, which adapts direct preference optimization to the CTC loss setting: preference pairs consist of emotionally congruent speech unit sequences (positive) versus emotionally neutral sequences (negative), trained on a 9K bilingual multi-turn dialogue dataset (EO2S-9K) constructed following the Plutchik emotion model. The full pipeline comprises five sequential sub-stages (speech-text pretraining, image-text pretraining, image-text instruction tuning, speech decoder training, and emotional DPO) totaling ~766 GPU-hours on 8×A100s, with the LLM frozen in all but the instruction-tuning stage.

## Results

On OmniBench, OpenOmni (7B) achieves 37.40 overall, surpassing the leading open-source OLLM VITA (7×8B) by 3.95 points (37.40 vs. 33.45) while using 5× fewer training samples (1.6M vs. 5M) and a smaller model. On vision-language benchmarks, OpenOmni outperforms VITA by 7.0% on MMBench-CN (76.4 vs. 71.4) and 11.3% on HallusionBench (44.2 vs. 39.7). For speech generation, OpenOmni achieves the best bilingual S2T WER (6.8/6.9 on AIShell-2 dev/test) and T2S WER (2.6/5.6 on LibriSpeech clean/other), and reduces inference latency to under 1 second in non-autoregressive mode (5× faster than AR). Emotional speech DPO improves emotion classification accuracy by 7.7% over the non-DPO variant.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OpenOmni decisively outperforms VITA (7×8B), the primary open-source OLLM baseline, across all major benchmarks: +3.95 on OmniBench (37.40 vs. 33.45), +7.0 on MMBench-CN, +11.3 on HallusionBench, +4.4 on AV-Odyssey Bench (32.8 vs. 26.4), and superior speech WER on both AIShell-2 and LibriSpeech. Against UnifiedIO2 variants (1.1B–6.8B), OpenOmni leads by 8–30 points on OmniBench despite comparable or smaller parameter counts. On vision-language benchmarks, OpenOmni is competitive with the stronger Qwen2-VL-Chat (7B) (e.g., 76.2 vs. 86.4 on MMBench-EN) but surpasses it on HallusionBench (44.2 vs. 50.6) and RealWorldQA (64.3 vs. 69.7), while using far less training data (1.7M vs. 1.4B pretraining tokens). For speech, OpenOmni beats EMOVA (7B) on AIShell-2 S2T (6.8 vs. 10.3) and LibriSpeech T2S (2.6 vs. 3.4), and outperforms VITA-1.5 on LibriSpeech Test_other (5.6 vs. 7.5). On the AV-Odyssey Bench, OpenOmni leads all baselines including OneLLM (27.4), PandaGPT (26.7), and Video-LLaMA2 (26.8) by 5–7 points, demonstrating that progressive bi-modal alignment generalizes effectively to four-modality understanding without explicit video or audio training.

## Relation to Prior Work

OpenOmni builds on the LLaVA-style encoder-projector-LLM architecture for vision-language alignment and adapts the DPO framework (Rafailov et al.) into a CTC-DPO variant for emotional speech generation. Its core conceptual departure from prior omnimodal LLMs (VITA, AnyGPT, Video-SALMONN) is the progressive alignment strategy: rather than requiring scarce image-speech-text triplets, it uses text as a pivot, first aligning speech-text then image-text, and relies on the implicit representation similarity between modalities to achieve quasi-zero-shot omnimodal understanding. The speech generation component departs from prior work that chains external TTS modules or uses purely autoregressive decoders, instead introducing a lightweight MoE-based streaming decoder supporting both AR and NAR modes with CTC loss.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work narrows the gap between proprietary omnimodal systems (GPT-4o) and open-source alternatives by demonstrating that a 7B model trained on 1.6M samples can outperform a 7×8B model trained on 5M tri-modal samples, which is practically significant for resource-constrained research groups. The progressive alignment strategy and CTC-DPO for emotional speech are reusable techniques that could accelerate development of future omnimodal assistants. Key limitations include restriction to Chinese and English, the 'quasi' (not fully verified) nature of the zero-shot transfer claim, and the fact that NAR mode trades some speech quality for speed. Open questions include whether the text-as-pivot strategy generalizes to additional modalities (video, music) and whether the emotional DPO approach scales beyond the nine Plutchik categories.
