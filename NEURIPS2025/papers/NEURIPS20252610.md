## Problem

Existing LLM-based social simulations rely on prompt-engineered templates and heuristic interaction schedules that produce plausible narratives but fail to quantitatively track real-world population dynamics. Supervised fine-tuning treats each decision as independent, ignoring the feedback loop where individual actions reshape the population signal that in turn drives future decisions. The paper addresses how to model this bidirectional agent–population interaction in a way that is both data-aligned and scalable to large populations.

## Input / Output

The policy model receives a structured user profile (location, gender, follower/influence tier, activity level, verification status), the event discussion topic, and the current mean field—a continuously evolving ~200-word textual summary of population behavior (stance, sentiment, belief, intent distributions). Both the state space and action space are unbounded free-form text: agents output natural-language comments or reposts rather than discrete labels. A warm-up phase feeds real ground-truth actions into the mean-field update before the simulation switches to policy-generated actions, and long-horizon rollouts beyond observed data resample agent states from historical profile distributions.

## Method

MF-LLM replaces intractable O(N²) pairwise agent interactions with an O(N) mean-field approximation by alternating two LLM modules: a mean-field model μ that distills a compact population signal m_t from the latest batch of agent states and actions, and a policy model π that generates each agent's text action conditioned on its private state and the shared m_t. For training, the paper introduces IB-Tune, which optimizes the mean-field model with an Information Bottleneck objective—minimizing a KL divergence between the learned posterior μ_φ(m_t|X) and a fixed pretrained-LLM prior r(m_t) (compression) while maximizing the log-likelihood of next-step real actions under the policy (prediction), with β=2 balancing the two terms. The policy model is trained via standard NLL on real actions conditioned on (s_t^i, m_t). Both modules are fine-tuned with LoRA (rank 64) on Qwen2-1.5B-Instruct, and the framework generalizes without fine-tuning to GPT-4o-mini, DeepSeek-R1-32B, and Qwen2-7B-Instruct.

## Results

On the WEIBO corpus with Qwen2-1.5B-Instruct, MF-LLM with IB-Tune reduces KL divergence to 0.512 (47% improvement over the State baseline of 0.966), DTW distance to 0.138 (16.9% improvement), and NLL Loss to 2.809 (32.1% improvement), while achieving the best Macro F1 (0.495) and Micro F1 (0.846). On GPT-4o-mini without fine-tuning, MF-LLM still reduces KL divergence by 60.5% (1.647 vs. 4.172) and DTW by 21.7% (0.329 vs. 0.420) relative to State. Across all four LLM backbones and seven event domains, MF-LLM consistently outperforms every baseline on distributional and semantic fidelity metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MF-LLM outperforms all four baselines (State, Recent, Popular, SFT) on every distributional metric (KL, Wasserstein, DTW) and both F1 scores across all LLM backbones. The State baseline (ElectionSim-style, conditioning only on profile + topic) is the weakest, with MF-LLM reducing KL by 47% (Qwen2-1.5B) to 60.5% (GPT-4o-mini). The Recent baseline (TrendSim/AgentSociety-style, adding top-k recent comments) and Popular baseline (OASIS/HiSim-style, adding top-k popular comments) both perform worse than State on KL divergence, indicating that naively injecting other agents' actions without a principled aggregation mechanism degrades fidelity. The SFT baseline achieves the lowest NLL Loss (2.23–2.42) but suffers 56–118% higher KL divergence than MF-LLM, confirming that token-level likelihood does not translate to population-level rollout fidelity. MF-LLM's advantage is most pronounced on temporal alignment (DTW) and distributional shape (KL), where the iterative mean-field–policy feedback loop captures collective dynamics that static or heuristic baselines miss.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: classical agent-based modeling (ABM), LLM-based social simulation (OASIS, AgentSociety, ElectionSim), and mean field game theory. It departs from prior LLM-based simulators, which rely on prompt-engineered role templates and heuristic memory retrieval, by replacing explicit agent-to-agent interactions with a dynamically updated population signal (the mean field) generated by a dedicated LLM module. The Information Bottleneck-inspired fine-tuning (IB-Tune) is a novel loss design that has no direct precedent in the social simulation literature, drawing instead from variational information bottleneck methods in representation learning. The paper is explicitly the first to introduce mean field theory into LLM-driven social simulation, bridging a gap between the scalability of MFGs and the generative expressiveness of LLMs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MF-LLM offers a principled, O(N)-scalable alternative to pairwise interaction modeling for large-population social simulation, with the 47% KL divergence reduction over non-mean-field baselines demonstrating that the agent-population feedback loop is essential for quantitative fidelity. The finding that smaller LLMs (1.5B) outperform larger ones (32B, GPT-4o-mini) in capturing behavioral diversity challenges the prevailing assumption that scale monotonically improves simulation quality, though the evidence is limited to a handful of backbones and one primary dataset. Key open questions include handling exogenous shocks in real time, correcting distributional drift over long horizons, and whether the mean-field abstraction loses critical structure in highly heterogeneous or small-group settings. The framework's applicability to policy pre-evaluation and intervention planning suggests practical value beyond academic benchmarking.
