## Problem

Time-series data in real-world applications (weather, healthcare, energy) is inherently paired with domain-specific textual context, yet cross-modal retrieval between time series and text remains largely underexplored. Existing retrieval methods rely on shallow similarity measures, lack semantic grounding in textual descriptions, and struggle with the multi-channel nature of real-world signals where each variable encodes distinct yet interrelated information. This limits both direct retrieval accuracy and the ability to augment time-series foundation models via retrieval-augmented generation.

## Input / Output

TRACE consumes multivariate time series (C channels × T timesteps, e.g., 7 weather variables at hourly or 4-hourly resolution) alongside two tiers of aligned text: a sample-level event report (e.g., a NOAA storm narrative) and per-channel synthetic descriptions generated by an LLM. The model produces a shared embedding space supporting bidirectional retrieval (Text-to-TS and TS-to-Text), a [CLS] global embedding for sample-level matching, and per-channel [CIT] embeddings for fine-grained variable-specific matching. In RAG mode, retrieved time-series-text pairs are compressed into a single dense soft token that is prepended to a frozen foundation model's input, requiring no architectural modification to the downstream forecaster.

## Method

TRACE uses a two-stage training pipeline. Stage 1 pre-trains an encoder-only Transformer via masked autoencoding, where each channel's patch tokens are prefixed with a learnable Channel Identity Token (CIT) and a novel Channel-biased Attention (CbA) mask enforces intra-channel attention while allowing cross-channel interaction at the [CLS] level; RoPE is applied independently within each channel to preserve true temporal ordering despite the flattened multi-channel sequence. Stage 2 performs cross-modal alignment using a dual-level hard negative mining strategy: at the sample level, it mines top-K most similar but semantically divergent text reports from the batch, and at the channel level, it identifies distractor single-channel segments (both intra-instance and inter-instance) that exhibit misleadingly similar patterns. A bidirectional InfoNCE loss is computed at both levels, with a cross-attention module fusing CIT embeddings with channel text embeddings before contrastive optimization, while the text encoder (Nomic-embed) remains frozen and only a linear projection head is trained.

## Results

On the primary cross-modal retrieval task (2,000 bidirectional query pairs), TRACE achieves ~90% top-1 label matching (90.08% TS-to-Text, 89.63% Text-to-TS) and ~44% top-1 modality matching, substantially outperforming the best foundation-model baseline Moment (55.73% / 64.67% top-1 label matching). As a standalone encoder, TRACE attains 11 first-place results across four forecasting benchmarks (Weather, Health, Energy, Environment), e.g., MAE 0.230 on Energy (H=12) and 0.455 on Environment (H=48). On weather event classification, TRACE with RAG reaches 89.76% accuracy and 72.36 F1, surpassing the best train-from-scratch model (TimesNet, 86.09% acc.) and all fine-tuned foundation models (best: Chronos tiny at 74.79% acc.).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TRACE dominates all 15 baselines across every evaluation setting. In cross-modal retrieval (Table 1), the strongest foundation-model encoder Moment achieves only 55.73% / 64.67% top-1 label matching (TS→Text / Text→TS), while TRACE reaches 90.08% / 89.63%—a gap of ~34–35 percentage points; TS2Vec (50.47% / 60.28%) and Timer-XL (36.34% / 63.91%) trail further behind. In TS-to-TS retrieval (Table 2), TRACE (P@1=0.900, MRR=0.938) outperforms CTSR (P@1=0.682) and SAX-VSM (P@1=0.551) by 22 and 35 points respectively, while also being the fastest (0.045 s vs. 2273.93 s for DTW). For standalone forecasting (Table 5), TRACE (full-shot, fine-tuned head) wins 11 of 16 sub-tasks, beating iTransformer (best full-shot competitor on Weather H=7: MAE 0.518 vs. TRACE 0.501) and all zero-shot foundation models (Chronos, TimesFM, Timer-XL, Time-MoE, Moirai, Moment). On classification (Table 4), TRACE w/RAG (89.76% acc., 72.36 F1) surpasses the best train-from-scratch model TimesNet (86.09% acc.) by 3.7 points and the best fine-tuned foundation model Chronos-tiny (74.79% acc.) by 15 points; notably, all fine-tuned foundation models (Time-MoE, Moment, Timer-XL, Chronos) underperform even the weakest train-from-scratch baseline, whereas TRACE retains discriminative structure. In the RAG forecasting setting (Table 3), TRACE's own encoder benefits least from added text (TS+Text vs. TS-only: MAE 0.555 vs. 0.556), confirming its embeddings are already text-aligned, while decoder-only models like Timer-XL gain more (MAE 0.729→0.712).

## Relation to Prior Work

TRACE carves new ground at the intersection of three underexplored threads: unimodal time-series retrieval (CTSR, TS-RAG, TimeRAF), time-series foundation models (Moment, Timer-XL, Time-MoE, Chronos), and time-series language models (ChatTime, ChatTS, TimeXL). Rather than building on a single prior architecture, it repurposes masked autoencoder pre-training (à la Moment) with novel Channel Identity Tokens and Channel-biased Attention to produce channel-disentangled embeddings, then introduces a dual-level hard-negative contrastive objective to align those embeddings with textual context. The RAG mechanism borrows the soft-prompt conditioning idea from NLP retrieval-augmented generation but is the first to apply it to frozen time-series foundation models via a learned projection of retrieved multimodal pairs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By establishing cross-modal (Text-to-TS and TS-to-Text) retrieval as a first-class task and providing a curated multimodal weather benchmark, TRACE opens a path toward retrieval-augmented time-series foundation models that can ground predictions in semantically relevant historical context. A notable open question is the reliance on supervised textual alignment (event reports, LLM-generated channel descriptions), which limits applicability to domains where such annotations are unavailable; the authors flag weakly- and semi-supervised extensions as future work. The finding that fine-tuned foundation models (Time-MoE, Moment, Timer-XL) substantially underperform train-from-scratch baselines on domain-specific classification suggests their embeddings may be over-generalized, raising questions about the transferability of current foundation-model representations to fine-grained discriminative tasks.
