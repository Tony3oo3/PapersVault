## Problem

Existing learning-based Depth-from-Focus methods treat focal stack images similarly to all-in-focus RGB frames, overlooking the physical and geometric principles of focus behavior. They struggle in complex scenes with fine textures or abrupt depth changes where focus cues become ambiguous, because they do not explicitly exploit the complementary roles of spatial gradient variation across focal planes and the ordered, unimodal nature of focus transitions along the focal axis.

## Input / Output

The method takes a focal stack of N images (typically N=5) captured at distinct, ordered focal distances as input and produces a continuous per-pixel depth map computed as the expectation of focal distances weighted by predicted focus probabilities, along with a per-pixel focus probability distribution across the N focal planes. Internally, the network operates on a 4D focus volume (H×W×2C₁×N) constructed by concatenating per-image features with their focal-dimension differences, and the variational module processes gradient fields at a coarse 14×14 spatial resolution before the 3D-ResNet decoder upsamples to full input resolution.

## Method

DualFocus introduces two physics-grounded variational constraints into a CNN encoder-decoder architecture. The spatial variational constraint predicts multi-channel gradient fields per focal plane, projects them onto integrable (curl-free) surfaces via a closed-form least-squares solve, and supervises the reconstructed surface gradients only in in-focus regions using a sharpness-based weight derived from ground-truth depth and focal distances—this forces the network to concentrate gradient energy in geometrically reliable, focus-dependent regions. The focal variational constraint enforces a bidirectionally monotonic, unimodal focus probability distribution along the focal axis via a soft penalty that discourages decreases before the peak and increases after it, reflecting the physical defocus behavior. The architecture uses a ResNet-18 FPN encoder to build the 4D focus volume, a variational module at 1/32 resolution for the least-squares surface reconstruction, and a 3D-ResNet decoder with hierarchical fusion modules that jointly reason across spatial and focal dimensions to output focus probabilities. Training is fully supervised with a smooth L1 depth loss plus the two variational losses weighted by λ_sv=20 and λ_fv=100.

## Results

On NYU Depth v2, DualFocus achieves RMSE 0.075 and AbsRel 0.013, reducing RMSE by 20.2% and AbsRel by 35.0% over the previous DFF state-of-the-art DFV (0.094 / 0.020). On the real-world DDFF 12-Scene dataset, it attains RMSE 0.0194 and MSE 4.7×10⁻⁴, outperforming HybridDepth (0.0200) and DFV (0.0213). On FoD500, DualFocus achieves the best Bumpiness score of 1.31 (vs. DFV's 1.43), indicating sharper depth boundaries. In zero-shot transfer to ARKitScenes, DualFocus reaches RMSE 0.28 with only 27M parameters, surpassing HybridDepth (0.29, 67M) and DFV (0.43, 20M).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DualFocus consistently outperforms all DFF/DFD baselines across every dataset. Against the closest DFF competitor DFV [29], it reduces RMSE by 20.2% on NYU Depth v2 (0.075 vs. 0.094), by 13.2% on FoD500 (0.112 vs. 0.129), and by 8.9% on DDFF 12-Scene (0.0194 vs. 0.0213). HybridDepth [6], which leverages pretrained Depth Anything priors, is also surpassed on all three supervised benchmarks (e.g., RMSE 0.075 vs. 0.128 on NYU) and in zero-shot ARKitScenes transfer (0.28 vs. 0.29), while using fewer parameters (27M vs. 67M). Classical DFF methods (RDF [10], DDFF [9]) and the DFD approach DefocusNet [15] are substantially outperformed, with DefocusNet showing the weakest DFF/DFD result on NYU (RMSE 0.493). Among SIDE baselines, even the strongest (Depth Anything, RMSE 0.206) is far behind DualFocus on NYU, though in zero-shot ARKitScenes the gap narrows considerably (0.28 vs. 0.53), reflecting the advantage of SIDE models trained on large diverse corpora. The ablation in Table 5 confirms that removing both variational constraints degrades RMSE from 0.075 to 0.094, matching DFV's level, validating that the spatio-focal constraints are the source of the improvement.

## Relation to Prior Work

DualFocus combines two previously separate ideas: the 4D differential focus volume from DFV (Yang et al., CVPR 2022), which encodes focus-dependent feature variation across a focal stack, and the integrability-based variational reconstruction from VA-DepthNet (Liu et al., 2023), which was designed for single-image depth estimation. The key departure is repurposing the integrability constraint across focal planes so that in-focus and out-of-focus regions produce structurally different surface reconstructions, and adding a novel focal-axis constraint (unimodal, bidirectionally monotonic focus probabilities) that has no counterpart in prior DFF or SIDE work. This positions the paper as a physics-aware refinement of the learning-based DFF line (DDFFNet, DFV, HybridDepth) that explicitly models the optical behavior of focus rather than treating focal slices as generic multi-view inputs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DFF is a practical depth estimation route for consumer devices (smartphones) that lack stereo or LiDAR sensors, and the paper's consistent gains across four datasets—including zero-shot transfer to ARKitScenes where a 27M-parameter model outperforms 336M-parameter SIDE models—suggest the spatio-focal variational formulation is a robust inductive bias rather than a dataset-specific trick. Remaining limitations (low-texture regions, sensitivity to motion misalignment, scene-dependent blur characteristics) point toward open questions about alignment-free or motion-invariant focal stack modeling. The focal variational constraint, in particular, offers a reusable regularization principle that could transfer to other multi-focus or multi-exposure estimation tasks.
