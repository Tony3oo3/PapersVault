## Problem

Open-vocabulary 3D scene representations are currently evaluated using closed-set semantic segmentation labels, which forces a single ground-truth label per point and ignores the natural linguistic variability where objects admit multiple valid descriptions (synonyms, depicted patterns, visually similar objects, surrounding clutter). No dedicated benchmark exists that captures this tiered structure of label precision, making it impossible to diagnose *why* a misclassification occurs—whether the model confused the object with a depiction, a visually similar item, or a neighboring object.

## Input / Output

The benchmark consumes RGB-D image sequences and camera trajectories from Replica (8 scenes), ScanNet++ (8 scenes), and HM3D (7 scenes), downsampled to 0.05 m voxel resolution. For each of 3812 ground-truth objects, four human annotators provide free-form noun labels organized into four tiers (synonyms, depictions, visually similar, clutter), yielding 300–1200 unique labels per scene—13× the original dataset annotations. Evaluation methods must output either per-point open-vocabulary feature vectors (for segmentation) or per-object feature vectors with 3D point clouds (for retrieval), which are scored against prompt lists of up to 3,500 labels or query sets of up to 1,500 text queries per scene via cosine similarity.

## Method

OpenLex3D introduces a four-tier labeling hierarchy—synonyms, depictions, visually similar, and clutter—inspired by SimLex-999's distinction between similarity and association, where each tier captures a progressively less precise description of the target object. Two evaluation tasks are built on these labels: (1) tiered open-set semantic segmentation, scored with a novel Top-N Frequency metric (proportion of points whose top-N predictions fall in a given category) and a Set Ranking metric that evaluates the full similarity-score distribution against ideal tiered bounds using rank-score functions with left/right boundary penalties; and (2) open-set object retrieval, where queries are procedurally generated from synonym and synonym-depiction templates and scored with AP at multiple IoU thresholds. The Set Ranking metric is the key methodological novelty: rather than only checking whether the correct label appears in the top-N, it quantifies how well the entire ranked list respects the tiered ordering (synonyms should outrank depictions and visually similar labels), exposing underscoring and overscoring penalties that top-frequency metrics miss.

## Results

On the object retrieval task, overall AP scores are low across all methods, with OpenMask3D+NMS achieving the best mAP on Replica (11.47%) and ScanNet++ (4.00%), while ConceptGraphs leads on the more cluttered HM3D scenes (mAP 5.09%, AP50 8.05%). For the tiered semantic segmentation task (Set Ranking), HOV-SG achieves the highest mean ranking score on ScanNet++ (mR 0.84) and HM3D (mR 0.88), while OpenScene leads on Replica (mR 0.85). No single method dominates both tasks or all three datasets, and all methods show significant performance degradation on the real-world ScanNet++ and HM3D environments compared to the synthetic Replica scenes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OpenMask3D+NMS [32] leads object retrieval on Replica (mAP 11.47 vs. 5.86 for ConceptGraphs) and ScanNet++ (mAP 4.00 vs. 1.97 for ConceptGraphs-GPT), benefiting from clean mesh inputs and NMS-based mask selection, but fails to generalize to the larger, more cluttered HM3D scenes where ConceptGraphs [6] takes the top spot (mAP 5.09 vs. 4.03). For Set Ranking segmentation, HOV-SG [35] achieves the best mean ranking on ScanNet++ (mR 0.84) and HM3D (mR 0.88), while OpenScene [23] leads on Replica (mR 0.85); ConceptGraphs (GPT) [6] attains the highest synonym inlier rate R_S but simultaneously overscores depictions and visually-similar labels (P_DVS^→ 0.43 on Replica), indicating stochastic rather than well-calibrated scoring. ConceptFusion [11] and OpenScene [23] (dense methods) produce noisier per-point predictions and consistently rank below object-centric methods on clutter and missing-point metrics. Kassab2024 [12] underperforms all other methods on retrieval (mAP 1.38 on Replica) and set ranking, suggesting its entropy-based feature selection without multi-view merging is insufficient for open-vocabulary tasks. The key finding is that no method excels across both segmentation and retrieval simultaneously, and all methods degrade substantially on real-world ScanNet++ and HM3D compared to synthetic Replica.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground in the evaluation methodology for open-vocabulary 3D scene representations rather than proposing a new representation. Prior work (OpenMask3D, ConceptFusion, OpenScene, ConceptGraphs, HOV-SG) has typically evaluated open-vocabulary predictions against closed-set labels from ScanNet++ or Replica, creating a fundamental mismatch between the open nature of predictions and the closed nature of ground truth. The tiered category structure (synonyms, depictions, visually similar, clutter) draws conceptually from SimLex-999's distinction between similarity and association in NLP, repurposing it to diagnose specific failure modes in 3D scene understanding. The benchmark also extends the OpenSUN3D workshop's retrieval evaluation by providing hundreds of procedurally generated queries per scene covering all objects, rather than a fixed small set.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 13× increase in labels per scene and the tiered diagnostic framework expose failure modes (clutter leakage, depiction confusion, visually similar confusion) that closed-set mIoU completely obscures, giving the community a much richer picture of where open-vocabulary 3D methods actually break down. The finding that no single method dominates both segmentation and retrieval, and that clutter is a universal weakness, points to concrete improvement directions in segmentation and feature-merging strategies. Limitations include the absence of affordance/material/color labels, restriction to original dataset segments (missing small parts), and a simple template-based query generation that may not capture full linguistic diversity. Open questions include whether the tiered evaluation generalizes to outdoor scenes, dynamic scenes, or cross-lingual settings.
