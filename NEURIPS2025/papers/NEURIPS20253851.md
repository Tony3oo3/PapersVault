## Problem

Domain adaptation of LLMs faces a fundamental trade-off: Domain Adaptive Pretraining (DAPT) requires costly full-parameter training per model and suffers catastrophic forgetting, while Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) introduces substantial inference latency from expensive kNN searches over large datastores. No existing method simultaneously offers plug-and-play adaptability across multiple model sizes and architectures while maintaining low inference overhead.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253851/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the Memory Decoder (a small transformer decoder, e.g., 0.5B parameters) and the base LLM process the same token sequence in parallel, each producing a next-token probability distribution over the shared vocabulary. These two distributions are then linearly interpolated via a tunable hyperparameter α to yield the final domain-enhanced prediction. The Memory Decoder requires only a single forward pass (1.28× base model latency) compared to 1.51× for In-Context RAG and 2.17× for kNN-LM, and operates without any external datastore or retrieval step.

## Method

Memory Decoder is a compact transformer decoder pretrained to imitate the output distribution of a non-parametric kNN retriever, effectively compressing the knowledge in a large key-value datastore into parametric weights. During pre-training, kNN probability distributions are cached from a domain-specific datastore, and the decoder is trained with a hybrid objective: KL divergence between its output and the cached kNN distributions (driving sparsity and memorization) plus a standard language modeling loss (preserving semantic coherence), balanced by hyperparameter β. At inference, the pretrained decoder is simply plugged alongside any frozen LLM sharing the same tokenizer, and their next-token distributions are interpolated—no model-specific modifications or additional training are needed. This design enables a single 0.5B Memory Decoder to enhance models from 0.5B to 72B parameters, and with only 10% of the original training budget (re-initializing the embedding layer and LM head), can be transferred across tokenizer families (e.g., Qwen2.5 → Llama3).

## Results

On WikiText-103, a single 124M-parameter Memory Decoder reduces perplexity from 24.89 to 13.36 on GPT-2-small (base), outperforming DAPT (14.76), LoRA (18.63), kNN-LM (15.62), and In-Context RAG (18.46). Notably, MemDec applied to GPT-2-medium (12.25 PPL) beats full-parameter DAPT on the same model (12.78) while using only one-third the parameters. On nine downstream NLP tasks, MemDec achieves the highest average score (69.79) versus base (67.45), LoRA (67.28), kNN-LM (67.24), and DAPT (60.84), which suffers catastrophic forgetting. Across three specialized domains (biomedicine, finance, law), a single 0.5B MemDec reduces average perplexity by 6.17 points and consistently outperforms LoRA across Qwen models from 0.5B to 72B.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against In-Context RAG, MemDec wins decisively on all GPT-2 sizes (e.g., 13.36 vs. 18.46 PPL on GPT-2-small) while also reducing inference latency from 1.51× to 1.28× overhead. Against kNN-LM, MemDec achieves lower perplexity across all model scales (13.36 vs. 15.62 on GPT-2-small) and eliminates the need for a ~500GB datastore. Against DAPT, MemDec wins on GPT-2-small (13.36 vs. 14.76) and GPT-2-medium (12.25 vs. 12.78) but trails on GPT-2-large (11.53 vs. 11.10) and GPT-2-xl (10.93 vs. 10.16); however, on downstream tasks MemDec (69.79 avg) massively outperforms DAPT (60.84 avg) because DAPT suffers catastrophic forgetting on HYP (36.04 vs. 64.15) and Yahoo (24.40 vs. 49.37). Against LoRA, MemDec dominates on all GPT-2 sizes in perplexity (e.g., 13.36 vs. 18.63 on GPT-2-small) and on cross-model domain adaptation, where a single MemDec outperforms per-model LoRA across all Qwen and Llama variants in biomedical and financial domains.

## Relation to Prior Work

Memory Decoder is conceptually a knowledge distillation approach where the 'teacher' is a non-parametric kNN datastore (from Khandelwal et al., 2019) and the 'student' is a compact transformer decoder trained via KL-divergence alignment. The inference-time distribution interpolation (Eq. 7) directly mirrors kNN-LM's interpolation formula, but replaces the expensive datastore lookup with a single forward pass through the small decoder. This positions the work at the intersection of retrieval-augmented generation and parametric adaptation: it inherits RAG's plug-and-play, parameter-preserving philosophy while adopting DAPT's inference efficiency, and departs from LoRA/adapters by being entirely model-agnostic within a tokenizer family.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in decoupling domain expertise from model architecture: a single pretrained memory decoder enhances all models sharing a tokenizer (demonstrated from 0.5B to 72B), and transfers across tokenizer families with only 10% of the original training budget. This dramatically reduces the cost of maintaining domain-adapted model fleets in production. Key limitations include the one-time pre-training cost of building kNN datastores and the residual need for embedding re-initialization in cross-tokenizer transfer, preventing truly zero-shot cross-architecture deployment. Open questions include whether the approach extends to multi-domain or dynamic-domain settings, and how it interacts with instruction-tuned or RLHF'd models where the base distribution has already been substantially shifted.
