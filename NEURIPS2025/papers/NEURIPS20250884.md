## Problem

Spectral reconstruction from RGB to hyperspectral images is inherently ill-posed, and existing deep learning methods (CNNs, Transformers) attempt to integrate full-spectrum information from all three RGB channels in a single one-shot step, leading to high computational overhead and training complexity. No prior work has exploited the self-similarity and low-rank structure of HSI data along the spectral dimension to decompose the reconstruction into a progressive, recursive process.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250884/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3-channel RGB image (cropped to 64×64 patches during training) and produces a 31-band hyperspectral image with 10 nm spectral intervals (400–700 nm on CAVE, 420–720 nm on Harvard). Unusually, the RGB image is re-injected at every recursive level as a structural and contrast prior, and the 31 output bands are generated two at a time across five sequential levels rather than all at once.

## Method

FRN decomposes the joint distribution over K spectral bands into m levels of conditional distributions (K = 2^m = 32), where each atomic module predicts two new wavelength images using only neighboring-band information, following the low-rank property of HSI. The atomic module is BAMamba, a U-Net-style network built from state space models (VMamba) with linear computational complexity, augmented by a band-aware mask that suppresses hidden-state contributions from pixels whose ΔA coefficients fall below a threshold ε, thereby filtering out low-correlation regions caused by reflectance differences across bands. The BSSM block applies a DWConv → SiLU → SSM → LN scanning sequence with residual connections and pixel-wise gating via SiLU(LN(feat)). Training uses L1 pixel-level loss with Adam (lr = 4×10⁻⁴, cosine annealing to 1×10⁻⁶) and batch size 32, requiring only 0.30 M parameters—fewer than all compared baselines.

## Results

On the CAVE dataset, FRN achieves a PSNR of 41.05 dB, SSIM of 0.9900, RMSE of 3.62, and UIQI of 0.9010, outperforming the second-best method (MSFN) by 1.2 dB in PSNR and 0.004 in SSIM. On the Harvard dataset, FRN reaches 42.88 dB PSNR and 0.9774 SSIM, surpassing the runner-up by 0.23 dB. Notably, FRN achieves these results with only 0.30M parameters, compared to 2.48M for MSFN and up to 21.36M for AWAN, making it the most parameter-efficient method in the comparison.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FRN outperforms all seven baselines on every metric across both datasets. On CAVE, the margin over the strongest competitor MSFN is substantial: +1.21 dB PSNR, −0.41 RMSE, +0.013 UIQI, and +0.004 SSIM, while using only 12% of MSFN's parameters (0.30M vs. 2.48M). On Harvard, the PSNR advantage over MSFN narrows to +0.23 dB, but FRN still leads in RMSE (2.89 vs. 2.99), UIQI (0.8791 vs. 0.8715), and SSIM (0.9774 vs. 0.9771). Among the lightweight methods (SSRNet at 0.39M, LTRN at 0.67M, HSRNet at 0.77M), FRN achieves the best results on all four metrics for both datasets. The heavier transformer-based methods (AWAN at 21.36M, HSACS at 19.74M) are consistently outperformed by FRN despite having 65–70× more parameters, suggesting the recursive fractal paradigm captures spectral structure more effectively than brute-force one-shot integration.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant one-shot paradigm in spectral reconstruction (exemplified by Transformer-based methods like MST++ and MSFN) by introducing a recursive, fractal-inspired generation strategy that progressively predicts spectra from wide to narrow bands. The atomic module (BAMamba) builds on VMamba's linear-complexity state space model but adds a band-aware spatial mask that adaptively suppresses low-correlation pixels during cross-scanning. Conceptually, the fractal decomposition draws on the self-similarity observed in brain functional networks and the low-rank structure of HSI data, repurposing the recursive generation idea from fractal generative models [30] to the SR setting. The method thus sits at the intersection of SSM-based vision architectures and divide-and-conquer spectral modeling, rather than extending any single prior SR architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The fractal recursive paradigm offers a principled way to reduce the ill-posedness of RGB-to-HSI reconstruction by increasing effective input and decreasing output at each level, and the 0.30M-parameter model outperforming 2.48M-parameter baselines by ~1.2 dB PSNR suggests the one-shot integration of all RGB spectral cues may be unnecessarily complex. However, the recursive calling mechanism introduces significant computational overhead (acknowledged by the authors as a key limitation), and validation is limited to two small indoor/outdoor datasets (CAVE, Harvard) with 31 bands each, leaving open questions about scalability to longer spectral ranges, outdoor remote-sensing scenes, or dynamic video. The band-aware masking strategy in BAMamba could be more broadly applicable to other SSM-based vision tasks where spatial heterogeneity varies across channels or modalities.
