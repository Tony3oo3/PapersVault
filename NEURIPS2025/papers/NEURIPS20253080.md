## Problem

Existing one-step diffusion distillation methods face a trade-off: score-based approaches (e.g., DMD, SiD) achieve strong quality but require iterative training of auxiliary fake-score models or discriminators, inflating memory and hyperparameter complexity, while trajectory-based methods (e.g., Consistency Models) avoid extra components but suffer significant quality degradation at one step. The paper asks whether a simple, single-step training procedure can match the quality of iterative distillation without loading any auxiliary model during training.

## Input / Output

At inference, the one-step generator takes a Gaussian noise vector z ~ N(0, I) (plus a class label for conditional ImageNet generation) and produces a single 32×32 (CIFAR-10) or 64×64 (ImageNet) RGB image in one forward pass. During training, the method consumes pre-computed triplets (x, x′, z) where x is a teacher-diffusion sample, x′ is a warm-up generator sample, and z is the corresponding noise; the energy function E(x, G_θ(z)) is computed via LPIPS-Huber distance in pixel space, and the generator is initialized from the teacher's score network.

## Method

CED introduces an unnormalized joint energy-based model p_{θ,φ}(x|z) = p_{fake,φ}(x|z)·exp(−E(x, G_θ(z)))/Z_φ(z) that simultaneously encodes the fake-score distribution and its deviation from the one-step generator. Training optimizes a Noise Contrastive Estimation (NCE) binary-classification loss: positive samples are drawn from the pre-sampled teacher distribution p_real, negative samples from the warm-up generator G_{θ′}, and the log-odds reduce to the energy E(x, G_θ(z)), yielding the closed-form CED loss L_CED = E_{x~p_real}[log(1+exp(E(x,G_θ(z))))] + E_{x′~G_{θ′}}[log(1+exp(−E(x′,G_θ(z))))]. Crucially, no auxiliary score model or discriminator is trained; the teacher is used only to pre-generate positive samples, so only the student generator is loaded during optimization, cutting memory by ~50–66% relative to baselines. The paper further shows that, under a specific energy parameterization (E ≈ −(‖x−G_θ(z)‖²−‖x−G_{θ′}(z)‖²)), the CED objective is algebraically equivalent to the Distribution Matching Distillation KL-divergence objective, unifying the two-step RLHF-style reward-learning-plus-policy-optimization procedure into a single direct-contrastive step.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (unconditional, 1 NFE), CED achieves FID 2.96 and IS 9.42, outperforming Consistency Distillation (CD, FID 3.55) and DMD (FID 3.77) while requiring only the student model during training. On ImageNet 64×64 (conditional, 1 NFE), CED achieves FID 4.06, Precision 0.74, and Recall 0.58, substantially outperforming CD (FID 6.20), TRACT (FID 7.43), and Progressive Distillation (FID 15.39). CED reaches FID 3.53 on CIFAR-10 in just 100,000 training steps versus 800,000 steps for CD to reach a comparable FID of 3.55. The method uses only 1/3 to 1/2 the GPU memory of score-based distillation baselines by loading fewer models during training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CED outperforms all trajectory-based distillation methods that require loading the teacher model during training: on CIFAR-10, CED (FID 2.96) beats CD (FID 3.55), TRACT (FID 3.78), DFNO (FID 3.78), and PD (FID 9.12); on ImageNet, CED (FID 4.06) beats CD (FID 6.20), TRACT (FID 7.43), CTM w/o GAN (FID 5.8), and PD (FID 15.39). CED also surpasses DMD (FID 3.77) on CIFAR-10 despite DMD using iterative training with an auxiliary fake score model. However, CED does not match the best score-based distillation methods that jointly train a teacher and fake score model: SiD achieves FID 1.92 (CIFAR-10) and 1.52 (ImageNet), and DMD achieves FID 2.62 on ImageNet, both substantially below CED's 2.96 and 4.06 respectively. The key trade-off is that CED eliminates the need for auxiliary models and iterative training, reducing memory usage by ~50–66% compared to methods loading two or three models simultaneously, while still achieving competitive one-step generation quality. CED also converges faster, reaching FID 3.53 on CIFAR-10 in 100K steps versus 800K steps for CD to reach FID 3.55.

## Relation to Prior Work

CED sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: Noise Contrastive Estimation (NCE) for training unnormalized energy-based models, and Distribution Matching Distillation (DMD) for one-step diffusion generation. The key conceptual departure from prior score-based distillation methods (DMD, SiD, Diff-Instruct) is the elimination of the auxiliary fake score model and its iterative training loop, replacing them with a joint energy-based model whose NCE loss is shown to implicitly optimize the DMD KL divergence objective. The paper further draws an explicit analogy to Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) in RLHF, showing that the two-step 'learn reward then optimize policy' structure of DMD can be collapsed into a single contrastive step via a specific parameterization of the discriminator.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is the 50–66% reduction in model-loading memory during training (only the student generator is loaded, versus three models in DMD/SiD), which is directly relevant for scaling distillation to larger architectures. However, CED does not match the SOTA FID of SiD on ImageNet (4.06 vs 1.52), and the evaluation is limited to 32×32 CIFAR-10 and 64×64 ImageNet, leaving open whether the approach scales to high-resolution text-to-image generation. The theoretical connection to DPO provides a principled justification but also suggests the method inherits DPO's known limitations (e.g., sensitivity to the reference model choice, potential for overfitting to the contrastive pairs).
