## Problem

Deep unfolding networks (DUNs) are interpretable architectures for inverse problems like compressed sensing, yet a rigorous theoretical understanding of their adversarial generalization—how well they perform under adversarial perturbations at test time—remains absent. While empirical studies probe DUN robustness to FGSM and PGD attacks, no prior work provides formal generalization error bounds for adversarially perturbed DUNs, leaving a critical gap for safety-critical applications such as medical imaging.

## Input / Output

The method consumes noisy compressed observations y = Ax + e ∈ R^m (with m/n = 25% sampling ratio, A a normalized random Gaussian matrix, and e zero-mean Gaussian noise) derived from 32×32 grayscale images (converted from RGB) on CIFAR-10 and SVHN. An l2-norm constrained FGSM perturbation δ (‖δ‖₂ ≤ ε) is applied to the observations y during both training and inference. The output is the reconstructed signal x̂ ≈ x ∈ R^n, evaluated via clean and adversarial test MSE and the adversarial empirical generalization error (gap between adversarial train and test MSE).

## Method

The paper selects ADMM-DAD—a state-of-the-art DUN unfolding ADMM iterations into L layers with a shared overcomplete sparsifier W ∈ R^{N×n} (N > n, yielding an overparameterized regime)—and attacks it with l2-constrained FGSM perturbations. The key technical contribution is proving Lipschitz continuity of the adversarially perturbed final decoder with respect to the learnable sparsifier W, with a Lipschitz constant depending exponentially on L and linearly on ε. This Lipschitz bound feeds into covering-number estimates of the adversarial hypothesis class, which via Dudley's integral inequality yield an adversarial Rademacher complexity upper bound, and ultimately an adversarial generalization error bound scaling as O(√(NL log(ε)/s)). The framework is tighter than prior bounds in both L and ε (replacing products of per-layer spectral norms with a single √(L log(ε)) factor), and experiments on CIFAR-10 and SVHN confirm the predicted scaling while showing that increasing overcompleteness N reduces both clean and adversarial test MSE, outperforming an orthogonal-sparsifier baseline (ISTA-net) across all attack levels.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with 10 layers and N=10n, ADMM-DAD achieves a clean test MSE of 0.019 and adversarial test MSE of 0.020 at ε=0.01, with an adversarial EGE of 0.18×10⁻⁴. On SVHN under the same settings, ADMM-DAD achieves clean test MSE 0.013, adversarial test MSE 0.013, and adversarial EGE 0.22×10⁻⁴. The theoretical adversarial generalization error bound scales as O(√(NL log(ε)/s)), and empirical adversarial EGEs confirm this rate across both datasets for both FGSM and PGD attacks. The robustness gap (difference between clean and adversarial test MSE) is particularly small (~10⁻⁴) at ε=0.01 for both datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares ADMM-DAD (overcomplete sparsifier, N=10n) against ISTA-net (orthogonal sparsifier), both with 10 layers, on CIFAR-10 and SVHN under FGSM attacks at ε ∈ {0.01, 0.1, 1}. ADMM-DAD outperforms ISTA-net on all three metrics (clean test MSE, adversarial test MSE, adversarial EGE) across both datasets and all attack levels. The most striking gap is in adversarial EGE: on CIFAR-10 at ε=0.01, ADMM-DAD achieves 0.18×10⁻⁴ versus ISTA-net's 0.70×10⁻² (roughly 40× smaller), and on SVHN at ε=1, ADMM-DAD achieves 5.87×10⁻⁴ versus ISTA-net's 15×10⁻² (roughly 250× smaller). At the highest attack level ε=1, ISTA-net's test MSE degrades dramatically (0.229 on CIFAR-10, 0.676 on SVHN) while ADMM-DAD remains stable (0.026 and 0.025 respectively), highlighting the robustness advantage of overcompleteness over orthogonality in the unfolding regime.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the non-adversarial generalization analysis of ADMM-DAD (Kouni & Panagakis, 2023) to the adversarial setting, building on the ADMM unfolding framework (Kouni et al., 2022) and the ADMM iterative algorithm (Boyd et al., 2011). While adversarial generalization theory for standard DNNs is well-developed via tools like adversarial Rademacher complexity and covering numbers, no prior work had delivered such bounds for structured unfolding networks. The key methodological departure is proving Lipschitz continuity of the adversarially perturbed decoder with respect to the learnable overcomplete sparsifier W, which enables tighter ARC bounds (scaling as √(L log ε) rather than the product of per-layer spectral norms) compared to prior surrogate-loss and covering-number approaches for DNNs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first theoretical framework quantifying how adversarial attacks affect the generalization of unfolding networks, with direct implications for safety-critical applications like MRI reconstruction and cryptography where adversarial robustness is non-negotiable. The finding that overcompleteness (N > n) promotes adversarial robustness provides a concrete, interpretable design principle for robustifying DUNs, bridging the gap between empirical observations and formal guarantees. Open questions include whether the bounds are tight with respect to overparameterization, whether the FGSM-derived scaling extends theoretically to multi-step attacks like PGD, and how to formally characterize the small robustness gap observed empirically.
