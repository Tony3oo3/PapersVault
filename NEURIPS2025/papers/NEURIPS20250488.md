## Problem

Patent-Product Image Retrieval (PPIR)—retrieving relevant patents from a large pool given a product image to detect potential design infringements—remains unexplored in computer vision. Two compounding challenges make it difficult: product and patent images span over 250,000 artificial object categories largely absent from standard pre-training corpora like ImageNet, and the stark domain gap between colorful RGB product photos and binary patent line drawings severely degrades cross-domain similarity estimation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250488/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single product (a set of one or more RGB images) and a retrieval pool of 727,921 patent entries (each containing one or more binary line-drawing images) as input, and produces a ranked list of patents with product-patent similarity scores. Retrieval is formulated at the product-patent level rather than image level: the final score for a product-patent pair is the maximum cosine similarity across all image-level pairs in the intermediate sketch domain, and a product is considered successfully retrieved if at least one of its potentially infringing patents appears in the top-k results (handling 1-to-N matching).

## Method

IDAMA combines two complementary strategies. First, Intermediate Domain Mapping (IDM) applies an edge detector (MUGE) to both product RGB images and patent binary line drawings, projecting them into a shared intermediate sketch domain that minimizes the domain gap; a compressive-sensing analysis (via the Restricted Isometry Property) shows this mapping preserves structural distances while suppressing texture/color nuisance terms by a factor of m/n. Second, the Morphology Analogy Filter (MAF) runs a pre-trained classifier on each mapped patent image and retains only images whose maximum class-agnostic confidence exceeds a threshold τ_maf = 0.4, selecting visually discriminative patent views inspired by the cognitive principle that humans recognize novel objects by analogy to known ones. Features are then extracted by a backbone (ResNet, ViT, or Swin) pre-trained on the sketch domain (supervised on ImageNet-1K-Edge, or self-supervised via IBOT/MAE on 3.8M unlabeled PPIRD images), and cosine similarity in this shared feature space drives the final ranking.

## Results

On PPIRD (439 product-patent pairs, 727,921-patent retrieval pool), IDAMA with ResNet-50 achieves 25.63 mAR, a +7.58 improvement over the direct Product-Patent baseline (18.05 mAR). The best configuration, IDAMA with ViT-L backbone and IBOT contrastive pre-training in the intermediate sketch domain, reaches 31.61 mAR and 9.34 R@100. Both components contribute: IDM alone lifts mAR from 18.05 to 25.06, and adding MAF provides a further +0.57 mAR gain.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DeepPatent [5] (the prior SOTA for patent image retrieval), IDAMA with ResNet-50 achieves 25.63 mAR versus 25.12 mAR for IDM+DeepPatent, showing that the simpler Morphology Analogy Filter outperforms DeepPatent's learned metric even without additional pre-training. Against UCDIR [33] (unsupervised cross-domain image retrieval), IDAMA consistently improves performance: UCDIR alone scores 18.64 mAR, while UCDIR+IDAMA reaches 25.36 mAR (+6.72), and standalone IDAMA (25.63) further surpasses it. Against the colorization-based domain alignment approach using diffusion models [27] (Product-Patent Colorized, 20.44 mAR), IDM's intermediate sketch mapping (25.63 mAR) provides a +5.19 mAR advantage, confirming that mapping both domains to a shared sketch space is more effective than generating pseudo-RGB patent images. The paper also shows that IBOT contrastive pre-training in the sketch domain (31.61 mAR) substantially outperforms both MAE (23.35 mAR) and supervised ImageNet pre-training (28.30 mAR) for the ViT-L backbone, indicating that contrastive learning is particularly well-suited to the texture-poor intermediate domain.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by formulating Patent-Product Image Retrieval (PPIR) as an open-set cross-domain image retrieval task, departing from prior patent retrieval work (DeepPatent, UCDIR) that focused on patent-patent matching within a single visual domain. The intermediate sketch domain mapping conceptually connects to sketch recognition benchmarks (Im4Sketch) and domain adaptation literature, but is repurposed here as a universal alignment space between binary line drawings and RGB product photos. The Morphology Analogy Filter draws on Biederman's recognition-by-components cognitive theory, and the compressive sensing / RIP theoretical analysis provides a novel mathematical justification for why projecting both domains into a lower-dimensional sketch space preserves structural similarity while suppressing domain-specific nuisance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PPIRD is the first near-million-scale benchmark for image-based product-patent retrieval, filling a clear gap between NLP-based patent-product retrieval and CV-based patent-patent retrieval. The finding that a simple edge-detection + classification-confidence filter (MAF) outperforms purpose-built metric learning (DeepPatent) without extra pre-training suggests that human-inspired heuristics can be surprisingly effective for open-set retrieval of unseen artificial objects. However, absolute performance remains low (best mAR ~31.6), the test set is small (439 pairs), and the theoretical analysis relies on strong sparsity and RIP assumptions that may not hold for all patent drawings. Open questions include whether the intermediate sketch domain generalizes to other cross-domain retrieval pairs (e.g., CAD renderings vs. photos) and whether the MAF threshold can be learned rather than fixed.
