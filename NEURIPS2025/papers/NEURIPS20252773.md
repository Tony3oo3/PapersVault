## Problem

Existing VLA models map visual and textual inputs directly to low-level actions in an end-to-end fashion, which limits long-horizon planning and adaptation to complex task variations. While chain-of-thought prompting has been explored as an intermediate reasoning step, prior approaches rely on expensive supervised CoT annotations that overfit to specific scenes, or on QA-style RL rewards that cannot ground reasoning in real-world action execution. The paper addresses how to incentivize structured embodied reasoning that is both visually grounded and directly connected to executable robot actions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252773/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a visual observation (single image or short video segment o_{t-N:t} for self-correction) and a text instruction l as input. The reasoning MLLM autoregressively produces a sequence of latent embeddings that decode into textual reasoning steps and a visual plan latent c_t, which is further inferred into a 2D gripper trajectory τ = [p_k]_{k=1}^K with normalized coordinates in [0,1]². The downstream DiT-based action model then predicts N sequential 7-DOF control vectors [Δx, Δθ, ΔGrip] conditioned on c_t, operating asynchronously so one reasoning step covers N environment interactions.

## Method

ThinkAct is a dual-system framework where a Qwen2.5-VL 7B MLLM performs embodied reasoning and a 432M-parameter DiT-based Diffusion Policy executes actions, connected by a visual plan latent projected via a Q-Former (32 queries). The MLLM is trained with GRPO reinforcement learning using a novel action-aligned visual reward combining a goal reward (comparing predicted gripper start/end positions against detector-based ground truth) and a trajectory reward (DTW distance between predicted and demonstrated trajectories), weighted 0.9:0.1 with a format correctness score. The action model is then fine-tuned via imitation learning on the target environment while the MLLM is frozen, conditioning its state encoder (DINOv2 + CLIP) on the visual plan latent. This asynchronous slow-thinking/fast-control design enables the reasoning module to plan over multiple steps while the action model handles real-time control, yielding few-shot adaptation and self-correction capabilities.

## Results

On the LIBERO benchmark, ThinkAct achieves the best overall success rate of 84.4%, surpassing CoT-VLA (83.9%) and DiT-Policy (76.8%), with the largest gain on LIBERO-Long (70.9% vs. 69.0% and 57.6% respectively). On SimplerEnv, ThinkAct outperforms its own DiT-Policy baseline by 15.5% (Google-VM), 16.9% (Google-VA), and 11.4% (Bridge-VM), reaching overall scores of 71.5%, 65.1%, and 43.8%. On embodied reasoning, ThinkAct scores 48.2% on EgoPlan-Bench2, 59.8 BLEU on RoboVQA, and 56.2 on OpenEQA, outperforming the strongest baselines (Qwen2.5-VL* at 45.7/55.7 and InternVL3 at 55.5) by 2.5–4.1 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ThinkAct consistently outperforms all 13 baselines across both manipulation and reasoning benchmarks. Against the strongest manipulation baselines, it beats Magma by 3.1 points on Simpler-Google-VM (71.5 vs. 68.4) and by 8.4 points on Simpler-Bridge (43.8 vs. 35.4), and edges out CoT-VLA on LIBERO overall (84.4 vs. 83.9) with a more decisive 1.9-point margin on LIBERO-Long (70.9 vs. 69.0). Compared to OpenVLA (76.5% LIBERO overall), ThinkAct gains 7.9 points, and versus its own DiT-Policy backbone (76.8%), the reasoning-guided latent plan adds 7.6 points. On embodied reasoning, ThinkAct surpasses Qwen2.5-VL* (the strongest fine-tuned MLLM baseline) by 2.5 points on EgoPlan-Bench2 and 4.1 BLEU on RoboVQA, and outperforms InternVL3 by 0.7 points on OpenEQA. The only near-tie is LIBERO-Object, where ThinkAct (91.4) trails CoT-VLA (91.6) by 0.2 points, suggesting that for single-object manipulation the visual CoT frames of CoT-VLA are marginally more effective than the latent trajectory plan.

## Relation to Prior Work

ThinkAct sits at the intersection of two recent lines of work: RL-based reasoning in LLMs (GRPO from DeepSeekMath) and end-to-end VLA models (OpenVLA, TraceVLA, Diffusion Policy). It departs from prior CoT-VLA approaches (ECoT, RAD, CoT-VLA) that rely on supervised fine-tuning over curated reasoning traces, instead using action-aligned visual rewards (goal completion + DTW trajectory matching) to incentivize reasoning via GRPO. It also diverges from RL-based multimodal reasoning (VideoR1, Visual-RFT) that use QA-style verifiable rewards, by grounding the reward signal in spatial-temporal trajectory consistency rather than answer correctness. The dual-system design—freezing the reasoning MLLM and conditioning a separate DiT-based action model on a compressed visual latent—differs from both end-to-end VLA training and hierarchical VLA approaches that use linguistic subgoals.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ThinkAct provides a concrete recipe for connecting high-level embodied reasoning with low-level action execution, demonstrating that RL-trained reasoning (not just SFT on curated CoT) can yield measurable gains in long-horizon planning, few-shot adaptation, and self-correction. The action-aligned reward formulation (goal + trajectory) is a practical alternative to expensive task-success-rate rewards that require simulator rollouts. Open questions include whether the 2D trace-based rewards generalize to contact-rich manipulation, how the approach scales to multi-embodiment or real-world (non-simulated) settings, and whether the 17% inference overhead is acceptable for time-critical control. The inherited MLLM hallucination problem remains a key limitation for safety-critical deployment.
