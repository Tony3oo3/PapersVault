## Problem

Current parameter-efficient fine-tuning methods like LoRA treat each layer's adaptation independently, causing trainable parameter counts to scale linearly with model depth. This paper identifies and exploits an unexploited structural property: skip connections in transformers create smooth gradient propagation across layers, causing weight adaptations to concentrate energy in low-frequency spectral components along the layer dimension. No prior PEFT method leverages this cross-layer spectral structure for parameter sharing.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254725/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard pre-trained transformer models (LLaMA2-7B/13B for text, RoBERTa-large for NLU, CLIP ViT-B/32 for vision) and fine-tunes them on downstream tasks with text prompts or RGB images as input, producing text responses or class labels as output. The unusual aspect is that the trainable parameters are not per-layer matrices but a single set of sparse 3D Fourier coefficients (κ₁ non-zeros per layer slice × κ₂ cross-layer frequencies), which are converted to full weight deltas via a 3D inverse FFT at each forward pass. For typical configurations, this yields only ~8 KB of trainable parameters versus LoRA's ~2.2 MB.

## Method

CrossSpectra stacks all Query, Key, and Value weight adaptations across L layers into a unified 3D tensor of shape d × d × 3L, then parameterizes it entirely in the frequency domain via sparse 3D Fourier coefficients. The key design choice, grounded in a theoretical proof that skip connections induce Lipschitz-continuous gradient fields with stronger spectral decay along the layer axis (β₃ > β₁, β₂), is to aggressively truncate to κ₂ frequencies across layers while sparsely sampling κ₁ coefficients within each layer's spatial frequency space. During the forward pass, a single 3D iFFT reconstructs the full adaptation tensor from the sparse coefficients, and per-layer QKV deltas are extracted and added to frozen pre-trained weights; the backward pass applies a 3D FFT and projects gradients back onto the sparse spectral subspace, implicitly regularizing toward smooth cross-layer patterns. This yields O(κ₁κ₂) trainable parameters instead of LoRA's O(Lrd), achieving 275× parameter reduction while matching or exceeding baseline accuracy across commonsense reasoning, NLU, image classification, and arithmetic reasoning benchmarks.

## Results

On commonsense reasoning with LLaMA2-7B, CrossSpectra achieves 82.73% average accuracy across 8 benchmarks using only 0.02% of total parameters (0.36% of LoRA's parameter count), outperforming the best baseline KaSA (81.53%) and LoRA (77.61%). On GLUE with RoBERTa-large, it reaches 89.76% average, surpassing even full fine-tuning (89.47%) while using just 0.01% of parameters. On image classification with CLIP ViT-B/32, CrossSpectra attains 81.49% average, exceeding LoRA at rank 32 (80.44%) with 1/200th the parameters. The method achieves 275× parameter reduction over LoRA and 5,250× over full fine-tuning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CrossSpectra outperforms all 11 baselines on the primary commonsense reasoning benchmark (LLaMA2-7B), achieving 82.73% average vs. KaSA (81.53%), NEAT (81.21%), and HydraLoRA (81.12%), with a 5.12-point margin over LoRA (77.61%) while using 42× fewer parameters (0.02% vs. 0.84%). On GLUE (RoBERTa-large), CrossSpectra (89.76%) edges out full fine-tuning (89.47%), MiLoRA (89.51%), and MoLoRA (89.52%) with only 0.01% of parameters, and notably excels on CoLA (86.86% vs. 84.65% for MiLoRA). On image classification (CLIP ViT-B/32), CrossSpectra (81.49%) surpasses LoRA at rank 32 (80.44%), MoLoRA (80.26%), and HydraLoRA (79.58%) with 1/200th the parameter budget. The one area where CrossSpectra trails is arithmetic reasoning (LLaMA2-7B), where it scores 43.7% average vs. LoRA's 44.6% and PiSSA's 44.7%, though it leads on MAWPS (81.4 vs. 79.0) and GSM8K (38.2 vs. 37.5). The consistent pattern across modalities—text, vision, and reasoning—confirms that the cross-layer spectral structure exploited by CrossSpectra is architecture-agnostic.

## Relation to Prior Work

CrossSpectra departs from the entire LoRA family (LoRA, DoRA, PiSSA, MiLoRA, HydraLoRA, MoLoRA, etc.) by rejecting their fundamental assumption of layer-wise independence in parameterizing weight adaptations. Instead, it draws on two previously disconnected threads: the ODE interpretation of residual networks (Chen et al., 2018) showing that skip connections enforce smooth inter-layer dynamics, and the spectral bias literature (Rahaman et al., 2019) showing that neural networks preferentially learn low-frequency components. The paper's conceptual contribution is bridging these threads to argue that weight adaptations inherit a cross-layer low-frequency structure that can be exploited via a unified 3D Fourier parameterization, rather than per-layer low-rank factorization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 275× parameter reduction over LoRA (8 KB vs. 2.2 MB for a typical 24-layer transformer) is practically significant for deployment scenarios where even LoRA's adapter storage is prohibitive, such as serving thousands of task-specific models. However, the method currently adapts only QKV attention weights (not FFN layers), uses uniform frequency sampling that may be suboptimal, and the FFT overhead, while negligible at 7B scale, could become a bottleneck for trillion-parameter models. Open questions include whether the cross-layer spectral structure persists at 100B+ scale, whether adaptive (non-uniform) frequency selection would further improve the sparsity-performance tradeoff, and whether the approach extends naturally to other transformer components or emerging architectures like Mamba.
