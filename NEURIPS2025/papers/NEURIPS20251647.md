## Problem

Nonlinear gyrokinetic simulations that evolve the full 5D distribution function are the gold standard for predicting turbulent heat transport in fusion plasmas, but their computational cost is prohibitive for the thousands of runs needed in reactor design. Existing reduced-order (quasilinear) models are fast but neglect essential nonlinear physics such as zonal flows and inter-mode coupling, leading to unreliable flux estimates in strongly turbulent regimes. No scalable neural surrogate had previously been demonstrated to directly model the 5D nonlinear gyrokinetic dynamics while remaining physically verifiable.

## Input / Output

GyroSwin takes a 5D distribution function snapshot f(kx, ky, s, v∥, μ) at resolution 32×8×16×85×32 (with 2 channels for real/imaginary ballooning-transform components) plus four operating parameters (safety factor q, magnetic shear, R/Lt, R/Ln) and the current timestep as input. It simultaneously predicts the evolved 5D distribution function at the next timestep, the 3D electrostatic potential field φ(x, s, y), and a scalar heat flux Q. The model is trained under the adiabatic electron approximation, so only the ion distribution function is evolved, and the 5D field is processed in mixed spectral (kx, ky) and real (x, y) coordinates after a Fourier transform for normalization stability.

## Method

GyroSwin is a Swin-based UNet extended to 5D, whose core building block is 5D shifted window attention (5DWA) that partitions the field into local windows of size Mv∥×Mμ×Ms×Mx×My and alternates window and shifted-window multi-head attention to achieve near-linear complexity in spatial resolution. Two key architectural innovations enable multitask 5D→3D→0D prediction: (i) latent integrator modules that perform perceiver-style cross-attention with a single learnable query broadcast over velocity space to collapse 5D latents into 3D latents for potential prediction, and (ii) 5D↔3D cross-attention mixing blocks at each UNet stage that allow bidirectional latent communication between the distribution-function branch and the potential/flux branches. A channelwise mode separation inductive bias explicitly isolates the zonal-flow mode (ky=0) from the remaining modes, converts it to real space, and appends its real and imaginary parts as additional input channels, biasing the network toward capturing the nonlinear damping mechanism that controls turbulent saturation. Training is supervised and multitask, with a staged loss schedule that first optimizes the 5D field prediction, then gradually introduces the potential and flux losses, and all Swin layers are conditioned on operating parameters and timestep via FiLM.

## Results

On the 48-simulation training set, GyroSwin achieves a correlation time of 26.50 (ID) and 28.60 (OOD) for 5D distribution function autoregressive rollouts, substantially outperforming all other neural surrogates (ViT: 16.83/19.20, Transolver: 9.66/10.2, FNO: 9.33/9.20, PointNet: 8.5/9.0). For time-averaged heat flux RMSE, GyroSwin achieves 67.68 (ID) and 70.48 (OOD), beating the QuaLiKiz quasilinear model (89.48/95.18) and all 5D neural surrogates which collapse to ~120 (indicating zero-flux predictions). When scaled to 241 simulations, GyroSwin_Large (≈1B parameters) reaches a correlation time of 110.33 (ID) and 111.80 (OOD) with flux RMSE of 18.35 (ID) and 26.43 (OOD), and achieves near-perfect Q(k_y) spectrum correlation of 0.87 (ID) and 0.91 (OOD). GyroSwin_small runs in 11.8 ms on a single H100, approximately three orders of magnitude faster than the GKW numerical code.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GyroSwin outperforms the QuaLiKiz quasilinear model (QL) on heat flux RMSE by 24–25 points (67.68 vs. 89.48 ID; 70.48 vs. 95.18 OOD) and on Q(k_y) spectrum correlation (0.69 vs. 0.58 OOD), while also being the only method capable of 5D field prediction and zonal flow modelling. Against the other 5D neural surrogates (FNO, PointNet, Transolver, ViT), GyroSwin achieves 2–3× higher correlation time and dramatically lower flux RMSE (67.68 vs. ~120), as all competitors effectively predict zero turbulent flux after longer rollouts. The tabular regressors (GPR, MLP) achieve lower Q̄ RMSE (43.82, 50.90) than GyroSwin on the 48-sim set, but they operate on 0D operating parameters only and cannot produce 5D fields, diagnostics, or zonal flows. When scaled to 241 simulations, GyroSwin_Large surpasses even the tabular regressors on flux RMSE (18.35 ID, 26.43 OOD) while retaining full 5D field prediction capability. On the Q(k_y) flux spectrum, GyroSwin_Large achieves near-perfect correlation (0.87 ID, 0.91 OOD) versus 0.51/0.58 for QL, and uniquely captures zonal flow profiles for unseen OOD simulations—a capability no prior surrogate or reduced numerical model possesses.

## Relation to Prior Work

GyroSwin extends the Swin Transformer's hierarchical shifted-window attention from 2D/3D/4D to 5D data, building on 5D windowed attention and up/downsampling layers introduced by Galletti et al. (2025), and wraps them in a UNet-style encoder-decoder with multitask prediction heads. It departs fundamentally from prior gyrokinetic ML work (MLPs, GPRs, CNNs on 2D wavenumber slices) by operating directly on the full 5D distribution function rather than reduced-order quantities, and introduces latent cross-attention and perceiver-style integrator modules to couple 5D and 3D latent spaces, mirroring the physics of velocity-space integration. The channelwise mode separation of zonal flows is a physics-informed inductive bias not present in prior neural surrogate architectures for plasma turbulence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GyroSwin is the first neural surrogate that self-consistently evolves the full 5D nonlinear gyrokinetic distribution function, capturing zonal flows and nonlinear mode interactions that quasilinear reduced-order models structurally cannot represent, while being three orders of magnitude faster than the GKW numerical code. Promising scaling to ~1B parameters suggests a path toward high-fidelity surrogates for full-species, multi-scale gyrokinetics. Key open questions include extending beyond the adiabatic electron approximation, handling the chaotic/distributional nature of turbulence (e.g., via generative modelling), incorporating the linear growth phase, and covering the full 4D operating parameter space at higher resolutions.
