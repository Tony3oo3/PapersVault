## Problem

Existing latent reasoning methods (e.g., Coconut, CODI) require chain-of-thought traces for training and rely on expensive multi-stage pipelines, while also suffering from a fundamental incompatibility with LLMs: feeding raw hidden states back as inputs degrades generation quality because they lie outside the token embedding manifold. This paper asks whether latent reasoning can be unlocked in pretrained LLMs via reinforcement learning alone, without CoT supervision, while preserving the model's native generative and reasoning capabilities.

## Input / Output

For knowledge-intensive tasks, the input is a text query concatenated with top-3 retrieved Wikipedia passages (via E5 embeddings); for STEM tasks, it is a bare question. During the reasoning phase (between think tags), each decoding step consumes a hybrid input that is a gated blend of the sampled token embedding and a projected hidden-state vector, rather than a pure token embedding. The final answer is produced via standard autoregressive decoding outside the reasoning span, and the reward is computed solely on the discrete answer tokens (exact match or numerical correctness).

## Method

HRPO introduces a plug-and-play gating mechanism that projects the previous step's hidden state into the embedding space via a softmax-weighted interpolation over the vocabulary (Equation 3), then blends it with the sampled token embedding using a learnable gate initialized near 1 so that early training preserves standard generation. As RL proceeds, the gate learns to incorporate richer latent features, progressively shifting the input distribution toward continuous representations. For optimization, HRPO adopts a GRPO-style group-relative policy gradient: g hybrid rollouts are generated per query, advantages are computed by standardizing outcome-based rewards within the group, and a KL regularizer against a reference model stabilizes updates. Because token sampling retains stochasticity, standard on-policy RL rollouts work without any CoT trajectories, and the lightweight REINFORCE-style objective (no value function, no importance-sampling ratio) keeps the framework strictly on-policy and computationally efficient.

## Results

On STEM benchmarks, HRPO with Qwen2.5-3B achieves an average accuracy of 0.700, outperforming GRPO (0.691), PPO (0.682), and SFT (0.461), and matching the best 7B+ baseline (MAmmoTH2-8B, 0.652) on four of five datasets. The 1.5B HRPO model reaches 0.617 average, surpassing the strongest 7B model on MATH by 2.0%. On knowledge QA benchmarks, HRPO with Qwen2.5-3B achieves 0.380 average exact match, exceeding the 7B RAG baseline (0.335) by 4.5% and the best RL baseline GRPO (0.367) by 1.3%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HRPO consistently outperforms both RL baselines (PPO and GRPO) across all benchmarks: on STEM, HRPO-3B beats GRPO by 0.9% average and PPO by 1.8%; on knowledge QA, HRPO-3B exceeds GRPO by 1.3% and PPO by 2.9%. Against the 7B+ LLM baselines, HRPO-3B matches or exceeds MAmmoTH2-8B (best 7B+ on STEM) on four of five datasets, and HRPO-1.5B surpasses the 7B leader on MATH by 2.0%. On knowledge QA, HRPO-3B (0.380 avg EM) outperforms the 7B RAG baseline (0.335) by 4.5%, and also beats IRCoT (0.242), Search-o1 (0.251), and CoT (0.134) from the 7B model. Against latent reasoning methods, HRPO achieves 9.42% and 23.63% gains over CODI on GSM8k and MATH respectively, and substantially outperforms Coconut (0.315 on GSM8k). HRPO also surpasses Distilled CoT from QwQ-32B on both GSM8k (0.720 vs 0.706) and MATH (0.518 vs 0.503), demonstrating that RL-based hybrid reasoning can exceed knowledge distillation from a 20× larger model.

## Relation to Prior Work

HRPO sits at the intersection of two active research threads: latent reasoning (Coconut, CODI, hidden-thinking) and RL-based LLM optimization (GRPO, PPO, REINFORCE++). Prior latent reasoning methods require CoT traces or multi-stage training to learn continuous representations, while standard RL methods operate purely in discrete token space. HRPO departs from both by using a learnable gating mechanism (inspired by gated recurrence in Griffin) to progressively blend projected hidden states into sampled token embeddings, and by driving this blending entirely through outcome-based RL rewards rather than CoT supervision. The policy gradient formulation directly adapts GRPO's group-relative advantage computation to the hybrid rollout setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical contribution is eliminating the need for CoT annotations or multi-stage training pipelines to enable latent reasoning in pretrained LLMs, which could significantly lower the barrier to deploying continuous-space reasoning in compact models. The emergent cross-lingual reasoning patterns and completion-length reductions suggest that hybrid representations may capture information beyond what discrete tokens alone provide. However, the on-policy constraint limits large-scale training efficiency, the additional gating computation adds inference overhead, and the continuous representations remain less interpretable than explicit CoT. Open questions include whether off-policy or asynchronous variants can scale HRPO, whether the latent features genuinely encode reasoning structure or merely act as a learned context cache, and whether the approach transfers to multimodal or non-autoregressive architectures.
