## Problem

Representation alignment (REPA) dramatically accelerates early diffusion transformer training by projecting DiT features onto a frozen teacher encoder's embedding space, but its benefit plateaus and eventually reverses in later epochs. The paper identifies the root cause as a capacity mismatch: once the generative student begins modeling the full joint data distribution, the teacher's lower-dimensional, recognition-oriented embeddings become a harmful constraint rather than a useful guide, as confirmed by gradient-angle analysis showing the two objectives drift from acute to obtuse angles.

## Input / Output

The method conditions on class labels (ImageNet 256×256, 512×512) or text prompts encoded by CLIP (MS-COCO 2014) and produces generated images via latent diffusion with SD-VAE. During Phase I, the frozen DINOv2-B teacher processes clean 224×224 (or 448×448 for 512-resolution) images to supply patch-level features and self-attention maps, which are aligned to mid-depth DiT blocks (e.g., layers 4–7 of SiT-XL/2). The DiT itself operates on 32×32×4 latent tensors at randomly sampled diffusion timesteps, and the final output is decoded back to pixel space for evaluation with 50K samples.

## Method

HASTE (Holistic Alignment with Stage-wise Termination for Efficient training) is a two-phase schedule that combines two complementary distillation channels in Phase I and then removes all auxiliary supervision in Phase II. Phase I applies a hybrid loss L_R = λ_R·L_REPA + λ_A·L_ATTA, where L_REPA enforces token-wise cosine similarity between a projected DiT hidden state and DINOv2 patch embeddings (semantic anchoring), and L_ATTA minimizes token-wise cross-entropy between teacher and student self-attention maps at selected mid-layer pairs (relational/routing prior). Phase II triggers at a fixed iteration τ (e.g., 250K for XL models), at which point both alignment terms are dropped and training continues with the vanilla denoising objective alone. The key design choices—aligning only mid-level blocks (not shallow noise-processing or deep denoising layers), using all 12 DINOv2 heads, and setting λ_R = λ_A = 0.5—are validated by ablations showing that shallow layers are off-manifold for clean-image attention and deep layers must remain free for precise generation. This yields a 28× reduction in optimization steps to match vanilla SiT-XL/2 FID on ImageNet 256×256, with gains replicating on text-to-image generation.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256 without CFG, SiT-XL/2+HASTE reaches FID 8.39 in just 50 epochs (250K iterations), matching the vanilla SiT-XL/2 baseline (FID 8.61) that requires 1400 epochs—a 28× reduction in optimization steps. At 100 epochs, HASTE achieves FID 5.31, surpassing SiT+REPA (FID 5.90 at 800 epochs). With classifier-free guidance, SiT+HASTE matches REPA's best FID of 1.42 at 500 epochs (vs. REPA's 800 epochs) and reaches FID 1.41 at 600 epochs. On MS-COCO text-to-image, MM-DiT+HASTE achieves FID 4.06 at 250K iterations with termination at 200K, outperforming MM-DiT+REPA (FID 4.16 at 150K, 4.28 at 250K).

## Baselines & Comparisons

HASTE consistently outperforms all baselines on training efficiency. Without CFG, SiT+HASTE at 50 epochs (FID 8.39) beats vanilla SiT (FID 8.61 at 1400 epochs) by 0.22 FID in 28× fewer steps, and at 100 epochs (FID 5.31) surpasses SiT+REPA (FID 5.90 at 800 epochs) by 0.59 FID in 8× fewer steps. DiT+HASTE at 80 epochs (FID 9.33) also beats vanilla DiT (FID 9.62 at 1400 epochs). With CFG, SiT+HASTE matches SiT+REPA's FID of 1.42 at 500 epochs versus REPA's 800 epochs, and slightly edges it at 600 epochs (FID 1.41 vs. 1.42). Against FasterDiT (FID 7.91 at 400 epochs, no CFG), HASTE at 100 epochs achieves a substantially lower FID of 5.31. On MS-COCO, MM-DiT+HASTE (FID 4.06 at 250K with termination) outperforms MM-DiT+REPA (FID 4.28 at 250K without termination) and vanilla MM-DiT (FID 5.26 at 150K), confirming generalizability to text-to-image generation.

## Relation to Prior Work

HASTE extends REPA (Yu et al., ICLR 2025), which aligns DiT hidden features to DINOv2 embeddings via cosine similarity, by adding a second distillation channel: cross-entropy attention-map transfer adapted from ATTA (Li et al., NeurIPS 2024), originally designed for ViT pre-training. The critical departure is the stage-wise termination: the paper diagnoses a capacity mismatch that causes the alignment loss to flip from accelerator to brake, and introduces a single switch (fixed iteration or gradient-angle trigger) that deactivates all auxiliary losses. This positions HASTE as a scheduling wrapper around existing alignment losses rather than a new architecture or objective, distinguishing it from concurrent acceleration methods (FasterDiT, MaskDiT, TREAD) that modify the model or sampling procedure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 28× reduction in optimization steps (matching vanilla SiT-XL/2 FID in 50 epochs vs. 1400) is a practically significant efficiency win that requires no architecture changes and only two lines of code, making it immediately adoptable across DiT variants. The gradient-angle diagnosis provides a principled, interpretable framework for understanding when external representation guidance helps versus hinders, which generalizes beyond REPA to any auxiliary regularization in generative training. Open questions include whether the fixed-iteration heuristic transfers cleanly to video generation, pixel-space diffusion, or substantially different teacher-student capacity gaps, and whether more robust automatic triggers (the gradient-angle approach is sensitive to hyperparameters and adds compute overhead) can be developed.
