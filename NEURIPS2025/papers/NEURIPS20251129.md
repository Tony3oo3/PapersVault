## Problem

Motion diffusion models face two critical limitations: high computational cost during both training and inference, and poor scalability because existing approaches rely on U-Net inductive biases on small datasets. Prior efficiency methods (step distillation, linear models) require additional teacher training or complex scanning mechanisms, while the potential of plain transformer denoisers for motion generation remains largely unexplored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251129/cropped_page4_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a natural language text prompt, encoded by a frozen CLIP ViT-B/32 text encoder, and produces a 3D human motion sequence represented as SO(3) joint rotation matrices. Motion is first compressed along the temporal axis by a 4-layer VAE (compression ratio r=4) into a latent space of dimension 512, where diffusion operates; the latent is then decoded back to full-resolution rotations. Inference uses 10 sampling steps with 1000 diffusion steps during training.

## Method

FlashMo introduces two key innovations. First, MotionSiT replaces the standard SiT Euclidean interpolant with a geometric factorized interpolant (GFI) that applies independent noise schedules along temporal and spatial dimensions while performing interpolation via Lie group geodesics (log/exp maps) on SO(3) manifolds, ensuring joint rotations remain geometrically consistent throughout the diffusion process. Second, a frequency-aware sparsification mechanism partitions attention heads into high-frequency (Hi-Fi) and low-frequency (Lo-Fi) groups; Hi-Fi heads retain full attention, while Lo-Fi heads perform attention-guided token pruning by selecting the minimum number of tokens whose cumulative attention scores reach a threshold γ of total attention mass. This structured sparsification integrates seamlessly with hardware-efficient exact attention (FlashAttention) without custom kernels, and the unified training-inference sparsification strategy eliminates the sparsity granularity gap, achieving a 2.25× speedup over full attention while maintaining comparable motion quality.

## Results

On HumanML3D, FlashMo achieves FID 0.041, R-Precision Top-1 0.562, and MModality 2.812 with an average inference time of 0.027s — the fastest among all compared methods. With pretraining on MotionHub V2, FID drops further to 0.029 and R-Precision Top-1 rises to 0.568. On KIT-ML, FlashMo w/ pretrain achieves FID 0.132 and R-Precision Top-1 0.453, outperforming the prior best (MotionPCM 1-step: FID 0.355) by 62.8% in FID. Compared to the state-of-the-art 1-step distillation baseline MotionPCM, FlashMo reduces inference time by 12.9% (0.027s vs 0.031s) and FID by 34.1% on HumanML3D.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FlashMo outperforms the strongest 1-step distillation baseline MotionPCM on every metric: on HumanML3D it achieves lower FID (0.041 vs 0.044), higher R-Precision Top-1 (0.562 vs 0.560), and substantially higher MModality (2.812 vs 1.772), while also being faster (0.027s vs 0.031s). On KIT-ML the FID gap is even larger (0.152 vs 0.355 for MotionPCM 1-step), and FlashMo w/ pretrain further narrows it to 0.132. Against MotionLCM-V2 (1-step), FlashMo wins on FID (0.041 vs 0.072), all R-Precision levels, and MModality (2.812 vs 1.858) on HumanML3D. FlashMo also surpasses autoregressive baselines (MoGenTS, BAMM, MoMask) on FID and R-Precision while being 5–7× faster in inference. The only metric where FlashMo does not lead is Diversity on KIT-ML (10.64 vs 11.08 for real data), though it still exceeds all baselines. The pretraining variant consistently improves over the from-scratch model by 0.01–0.02 in FID and 0.004–0.006 in R-Precision Top-1 across both datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

FlashMo extends the SiT (Scalable Interpolant Transformer) framework from image generation to 3D human motion by introducing a temporal-spatial factorized interpolant with Lie group geodesics on SO(3), departing from the flat Euclidean interpolation assumed by vanilla SiT. For efficiency, it diverges from the dominant acceleration strategies in motion diffusion—step distillation (MotionLCM, MotionPCM), GAN-based discriminators (EMDM), and linear/state-space models (Motion Mamba, Light-T2M)—by instead exploiting an intrinsic data property (frequency structure of attention heads) to perform structured token pruning that is compatible with hardware-efficient exact attention (FlashAttention) without custom kernels. The U-Net-based motion diffusion lineage (MDM, MotionDiffuse, MLD, ReMoDiffuse) is replaced by a plain transformer denoiser, following the broader trend in image and video generation showing that U-Net inductive bias is not essential for diffusion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FlashMo offers a practical path to scalable, real-time text-to-motion generation without the multi-stage teacher-student training pipelines that plague current efficient diffusion methods, and the SO(3) geodesic formulation provides a principled geometric foundation for joint rotation modeling that prior Euclidean approaches lack. The frequency-aware sparsification is notable for its unified training-inference design that eliminates the sparsity granularity gap, making it directly deployable with existing attention hardware. Limitations include the reliance on a single linear interpolant function (GVP was explored but underperformed under factorization), continued use of tangent-space learning rather than direct manifold optimization, and evaluation limited to two standard benchmarks. Open questions include whether other interpolant schedules or direct manifold optimization could further improve motion quality, and whether the frequency-based head partitioning generalizes beyond the specific motion datasets tested.
