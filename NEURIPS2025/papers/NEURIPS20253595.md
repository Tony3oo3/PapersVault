## Problem

Existing color enhancement methods cannot simultaneously achieve fine-grained expressiveness comparable to professional colorists, adaptability to diverse lighting and content, and pixel-perfect texture preservation. Unsupervised methods (Exposure, Distort-and-Recover) preserve texture but lack expressiveness; supervised methods (3D-LUT, RSFNet) are constrained by global-only adjustments in their training data; and generative methods (Midjourney) produce dramatic color changes but destroy original textures. No prior approach addresses all three requirements together.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image at arbitrary resolution as input and produces a color-enhanced RGB image at the same resolution. Internally, the diffusion-based color generation module operates at 512×512, while the texture preservation module handles the resolution discrepancy through implicit super-resolution, enabling the pipeline to process high-resolution inputs (tested up to 1088×728) without quality degradation. The final output is a full-resolution enhanced image that preserves the input's textural details while applying expressive, region-specific color adjustments.

## Method

GenColor is a three-phase pipeline: (1) a ControlNet-based diffusion model (Stable Diffusion 2.1) generates an expressive color reference image by learning to map color-distorted inputs back to high-quality targets on the newly curated ARTISAN-1M dataset (1.2M photographs), using a distort-and-recover self-supervised strategy with a wide range of color adjustments and a 50/50 weight blend of mid- and late-stage checkpoints to balance expressiveness against artifacts; (2) a Swin Transformer-based texture preservation network transfers color from the diffusion reference to the original input while maintaining pixel-perfect texture fidelity, trained self-supervisedly with a novel degradation scheme (circular shift to model spatial misalignment, random stroke noise to simulate artifacts, and random resolution rescaling to simulate super-resolution) that exploits the observation that diffusion outputs and inputs are texture-level near-duplicates with correspondences in nearby neighborhoods; (3) a lightweight global filter module (EfficientNet encoder + MLP) applies five targeted adjustments (brightness, contrast, highlight, shadow, saturation) to restore contrast and vibrancy lost during diffusion denoising.

## Results

On Adobe-FiveK, GenColor achieves the best scores across all five color enhancement metrics: Q-Align 4.29, LAION 5.83, LIQE 3.76, NoR-VDP 69.16, and C-VAR 16.96. On PPR10K, it similarly leads with Q-Align 4.38, LAION 6.26, LIQE 3.21, NoR-VDP 71.11, and C-VAR 13.57. For texture-preserving color transfer on Adobe5K, GenColor achieves the best DISTS (0.13), tied-best GMSD (0.12), and dramatically superior color similarity scores (Semantic W₁ 3.59, W₁ 3.22, MS-SWD 0.70) compared to all baselines. GenColor also outperforms the human Expert C benchmark on all five metrics, with a 55.5% improvement in C-VAR (16.96 vs. 10.91).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GenColor outperforms all supervised color enhancement baselines (3D-LUT, RSFNet, DeepLPF, ICELUT) on every metric across both Adobe5K and PPR10K, with the largest margins on C-VAR (expressiveness), where it scores 16.96 vs. 11.48 for 3D-LUT and 10.09 for RSFNet on Adobe5K. Among unsupervised methods, GenColor substantially surpasses Distort-and-Recover (Q-Align 4.29 vs. 2.39) and Exposure (Q-Align 4.29 vs. 4.00, C-VAR 16.96 vs. 13.96). For texture-preserving color transfer, GenColor achieves the best DISTS (0.13) and tied-best GMSD (0.12), while Color Matcher achieves the lowest TD (0.42 vs. GenColor's 0.94); however, GenColor's color similarity scores are dramatically superior (Semantic W₁ 3.59 vs. 31.45, MS-SWD 0.70 vs. 2.03), indicating far more accurate color transfer. UniColor and Deep Preset are outperformed by GenColor on all six texture/color metrics. Against the human Expert C benchmark, GenColor wins on all five quality metrics, with the most notable gap in C-VAR (16.96 vs. 10.91, a 55.5% improvement), and is preferred by GPT-o1 in 62% of head-to-head comparisons.

## Relation to Prior Work

GenColor reframes image color enhancement as conditional image generation by adapting ControlNet and Stable Diffusion to the enhancement task, departing from both the filter-based paradigm (3D-LUT, RSFNet, DeepLPF) and the self-supervised distort-and-recover paradigm (Exposure, D&R). The distort-and-recover training strategy is repurposed for diffusion models with a wider range of color adjustments, while the global filter module is directly inherited from Harmonizer. The key architectural departure is decoupling expressive color generation (diffusion) from texture preservation (a Swin-Transformer-based transfer network), addressing a fundamental limitation of diffusion models that prior generative approaches like DiffRetouch only partially mitigated through latent-space grids.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ARTISAN-1M dataset (1.2M curated photographs) addresses a critical data bottleneck in color enhancement, where prior supervised methods were constrained to small datasets (Adobe5K: 5K, PPR10K: 11K) containing only global adjustments. The paper demonstrates that diffusion-based generation can match or exceed expert retouching quality while a dedicated transfer network recovers pixel-perfect texture, a combination that generative tools like Midjourney sacrifice. Open questions remain: the model produces nearly deterministic outputs rather than diverse aesthetic variations, struggles with extreme luminance regions, and the weight-blending strategy for balancing expressiveness and fidelity is a heuristic that the authors acknowledge could be replaced by more principled approaches. Future work on style disentanglement and LoRA-based control could unlock the full diversity of the training data.
