## Problem

Floating orbitals—basis functions placed freely in space rather than centered on atoms—are known to yield more compact and accurate charge density representations, but their optimal placement has always required expert domain knowledge, preventing widespread adoption. No prior method has learned orbital positions in a data-driven manner. A further obstacle is that equivariant neural networks constrain outputs to share the symmetry of their inputs, which prevents placing orbitals in the asymmetric positions needed to faithfully represent electron densities of symmetric molecules.

## Input / Output

The model takes a molecular graph (atomic positions and element types) and outputs a 3D electron charge density parameterized as a Gaussian mixture model: for each atom, it predicts the mean position, signed weight, and 3×3 covariance matrix of a variable number of Gaussians (90 per valence electron, so e.g. 540 for oxygen vs. 90 for hydrogen). The predicted density is normalized to integrate to the total number of valence electrons before evaluation. Inference runs on a 3D grid to compute NMAE against reference DFT densities, with the full grid evaluated per molecule.

## Method

ELECTRA uses a modified HotPP equivariant Cartesian tensor message-passing network as its backbone, with three key architectural innovations. First, a symmetry-breaking mechanism initializes each atom's l=1 vector features with the eigenvectors of a local moment-of-inertia tensor (canonicalized by sign relative to the molecular center of mass), allowing the network to produce orbital positions with lower symmetry than the input while preserving rotational equivariance of the density. Second, debiasing layers after each message-passing step compute the principal direction of l=1 features via a covariance eigendecomposition and dynamically subtract a learned fraction of the projection onto that axis, countering the directional bias that message passing induces along bond axes. Third, three readout heads predict Gaussian parameters at different distance scales from each atom, with positions constructed as scaled sums of l=1 displacements from the atomic center and covariances as softmax-weighted sums of symmetrized l=2 Gram matrices. The model is trained end-to-end with a normalized mean absolute error (NMAE) loss against PBE/PAW reference densities from QM9, achieving 0.176% NMAE while running ~170× faster than ChargE3Net and ~11× faster than SCDP+BO at inference.

## Results

On the QM9 test set, ELECTRA achieves a normalized mean absolute error (NMAE) of 0.176% with an inference time of 0.089 s per molecule, outperforming the prior state-of-the-art SCDP+BO (L=6) by 0.002 pp in accuracy while being 11.5× faster (0.089 s vs. 1.022 s). On the MD dataset, ELECTRA sets a new state-of-the-art on all six molecules, halving the NMAE compared to SCDP (e.g., 0.45% vs. 1.13% on benzene). Additionally, initializing DFT calculations with ELECTRA-predicted densities reduces SCF iterations by 50.72% on average for unseen molecules.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ELECTRA outperforms all five compared baselines on the QM9 test set in both accuracy and speed. Against the closest competitor SCDP+BO (L=6, Fu et al. 2024), ELECTRA is marginally more accurate (0.176% vs. 0.178% NMAE) while being 11.5× faster at inference (0.089 s vs. 1.022 s). Compared to ChargE3Net (Fu et al. 2024), ELECTRA improves NMAE by 0.020 pp and is roughly 170× faster (0.089 s vs. 15.18 s), even when evaluated on inferior hardware (RTX 3090 vs. A100). Against the equivariant graph-based baselines InfGCN (Cheng & Peng 2024) and GPWNO (Kim & Ahn 2024), ELECTRA achieves substantially lower NMAE (0.176% vs. 0.869% and 0.730% respectively) with faster inference. On the MD dataset, ELECTRA halves the NMAE of SCDP across all six molecules (e.g., 0.45% vs. 1.13% on benzene, 1.02% vs. 2.34% on ethanol), establishing a new state-of-the-art in every case.

## Relation to Prior Work

ELECTRA builds on the HotPP Cartesian tensor equivariant message-passing network (Wang et al., 2024) as its backbone and draws a direct conceptual parallel to Gaussian Splatting (Kerbl et al., 2023) for representing 3D densities as mixtures of cheap Gaussians. It departs from the dominant atom-centered basis function paradigm in charge density prediction (SCDP, ChargE3Net, InfGCN) by introducing data-driven floating orbitals — a long-standing quantum chemistry idea (Frost, 1968; Tao & Pan, 1992) that has never been learned end-to-end. The symmetry-breaking mechanism via local moment-of-inertia eigenvectors is inspired by frame-averaging and equivariant symmetry-breaking literature (Puny et al., 2021; Duval et al., 2023; Xie & Smidt, 2024), while the debiasing layers are a novel architectural contribution specific to this work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ELECTRA demonstrates that a fully data-driven, floating-orbital representation can match or beat higher-order spherical harmonics approaches (SCDP L=6) while being over 11× faster at inference, and that predicted densities can halve SCF iterations in production DFT workflows — a practically significant acceleration for materials and drug-discovery pipelines. Key open questions include extension to periodic systems (crystals, color centers, electrides), hybrid atom-centered + floating models, and whether the moment-of-inertia canonicalization holds for degenerate or near-degenerate inertia tensors. The current evaluation is limited to small organic molecules (QM9, MD), so generalization to transition-metal complexes, large biomolecules, or extended solids remains untested.
