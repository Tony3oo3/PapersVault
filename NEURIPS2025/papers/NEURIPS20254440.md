## Problem

Medical vision-language models lack large-scale grounded datasets that connect spatial image regions to clinical text, preventing simultaneous performance on semantic tasks (classification, retrieval) and spatial tasks (segmentation, detection). Existing medical VL pretraining datasets (BIOMEDCLIP, PMC-CLIP) provide only image-text pairs without spatial annotations, and the Localized Narratives protocol for capturing grounded data has not been adapted to the noisy, unstructured nature of medical pedagogy videos.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254440/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes 224×224 medical images spanning 11 modalities (CT, MRI, X-ray, histopathology, ultrasound, surgery, endoscopy, dermatology, dentistry, ophthalmology, mammography) and text prompts up to 77 tokens (GPT-2) or 256 tokens (BioMedBERT), producing class labels for zero-shot and linear-probing classification or ranked image-text pairs for cross-modal retrieval. The dataset additionally provides timestamped cursor traces (101.8M unique points across 1M samples) that can be reformatted into bounding boxes (avg 316×357 px) or used directly as scribble prompts for interactive segmentation models like ScribblePrompt-SAM.

## Method

The paper curates MEDICALNARRATIVES (4.7M image-text pairs, 1M with spatial traces) by mining 74K YouTube pedagogical videos where instructors narrate while pointing at image regions, combined with 1.03M PubMed article figures. The curation pipeline chains Whisper ASR with LLM-based medical error correction (47.99% conditioned precision), YOLO-based sub-figure detection for compound article figures, and a frame-differencing + SSIM + median-frame-subtraction algorithm to isolate cursor traces within stable video chunks, with face-detection masking to remove narrator interference. GENMEDCLIP is then trained with the CLIP contrastive objective using ViT-Base (16 or 32 patch) image encoders and GPT-2 or BioMedBERT text encoders on 4 A40 GPUs for 20 epochs. The key empirical finding is that video-derived data provides diverse vantage points and cross-modal interleaving (multiple imaging modalities for the same patient within one narrative), yielding 11.65% higher classification and 53.6% higher zero-shot retrieval performance over article-only training, outperforming BIOMEDCLIP (trained on 15M pairs) with only 4.7M samples.

## Results

GENMEDCLIP (trained on 4.7M MedicalNarratives samples) achieves 32.55% average zero-shot classification accuracy across 11 medical domains, outperforming BIOMEDCLIP (27.80%) by 4.75%. On linear probing, GENMEDCLIP reaches 83.97% average accuracy vs. BIOMEDCLIP's 81.17%. The most dramatic gain is in zero-shot cross-modal retrieval, where GENMEDCLIP scores 70.17% average (top-5/50/200) versus BIOMEDCLIP's 44.10%, a 26.07% absolute improvement. A data-split ablation confirms that video-derived samples contribute 11.65% additional classification accuracy and 53.6% additional retrieval performance over article-only training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GENMEDCLIP outperforms all four baselines across every evaluated task. Against the strongest medical baseline BIOMEDCLIP (trained on 15M article pairs), GENMEDCLIP (trained on only 4.7M samples) gains 4.75% on zero-shot classification, 2.8% on linear probing, and 26.07% on retrieval. The general-purpose CLIP-ViT-B-16 baseline is surpassed by a wide margin on retrieval (70.17% vs. 19.82% average), confirming that domain-specific medical pretraining is critical. PubMedCLIP and PMC-CLIP, both trained exclusively on article data, perform substantially worse than GENMEDCLIP, with PMC-CLIP nearly failing on retrieval (0.52% average), underscoring the value of video-derived pedagogical data. The data-split ablation further shows that the video component alone drives an 11.65% classification and 53.6% retrieval improvement over article-only training, validating the core hypothesis that localized narrative traces from instructional videos provide superior grounding signal.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Localized Narratives protocol (Pont-Tuset et al., Voigtlaender et al.)—where annotators describe images while pointing with a mouse cursor—to the medical domain by repurposing existing YouTube pedagogy videos in which instructors naturally narrate while hovering over image regions, effectively harvesting free 'think-aloud' spatial grounding at scale. It departs from MedTrinity-25M, which generates spatial annotations via pretrained models (risking bias propagation), by sourcing traces from human instructors' actual cursor movements. The training objective follows the CLIP/BIOMEDCLIP contrastive pretraining lineage, with the key architectural difference being the combination of video-derived and article-derived data, building on Quilt-1M/Quilt-LLaVA's video extraction pipeline for histopathology and generalizing it to 12 medical modalities.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dataset addresses a critical bottleneck in medical vision-language research: the scarcity of large-scale, spatially grounded training data. The 1M trace-annotated samples open a path toward training grounded generative models (PixelLM-style, Quilt-LLaVA-style) and interactive segmentation without expensive human annotation. However, the paper's own experiments underutilize the traces—they are demonstrated qualitatively with ScribblePrompt but never used as a training signal for spatial tasks, leaving the core promise of spatial grounding largely untested. The overrepresentation of abnormal cases and absence of human-validated bounding boxes are acknowledged limitations that may constrain clinical deployment. A key open question is whether the trace-text alignments can actually supervise dense prediction or spatially controllable generation at scale, which the authors explicitly defer to future work.
