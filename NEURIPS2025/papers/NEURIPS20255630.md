## Problem

Offline average-reward RL has been underexplored theoretically, and prior guarantees either rely on uniform complexity measures (e.g., uniform mixing time, uniform span bound) that upper-bound the complexity of all policies rather than just the target, or require restrictive structural assumptions such as unichain MDPs or constant gain for all policies. This paper is the first to obtain a fully single-policy sample complexity bound depending only on the target policy's bias span, while also handling general weakly communicating MDPs where not all policies have constant gain.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes a tabular offline dataset with n(s,a) independently sampled next-state transitions per state-action pair and a known reward function, and outputs a stationary deterministic policy π̂ along with its Q-function. A notable aspect of the coverage requirement is that data is needed even for transient state-action pairs (where the target policy's stationary distribution is zero), specifically Õ(T_hit(P,π*)²) samples, which the paper proves is nearly necessary via hard examples. No prior knowledge of the bias span, effective dataset size m, or any other environmental complexity parameter is required for implementation.

## Method

The core algorithm is a pessimistic discounted value iteration with a very large discount factor (effective horizon set to n_tot), enhanced by a novel quantile clipping technique. The quantile clipping operator T_β clips value function entries above the (1−β) quantile with respect to the empirical transition distribution, which enables the use of a sharp empirical-span-based penalty function combining a variance term and a span term. This span-based penalty is essential for the constant-shift property of the pessimistic Bellman operator, ensuring the algorithm penalizes only relative value differences between states rather than the overall horizon-dependent scale 1/(1−γ) that dominates in average-reward settings. Without quantile clipping, the span term would break monotonicity and γ-contractivity of the operator, as increasing a low-probability state's value would increase the span penalty faster than the expected value. The analysis employs leave-one-out/absorbing MDP techniques to handle statistical dependence between the empirical transition matrix and the value function, yielding the first single-policy bound of Õ(√(S‖h^{π*}‖_span/m)) with matching lower bounds.

## Results

The main theorem (Theorem 3.2) establishes a high-probability suboptimality bound of $\tilde{O}(\sqrt{S\|h^{\pi^*}\|_{\text{span}}/m})$ for the output policy, where $\|h^{\pi^*}\|_{\text{span}}$ is the bias span of only the target policy and $m$ is the effective dataset size. This is the first fully single-policy sample complexity bound for average-reward offline RL, matching a lower bound (Theorem 3.4) of $\tilde{\Omega}(\sqrt{TS/m})$ in the dependence on $m$. A second lower bound (Theorem 3.3) proves that coverage beyond the stationary distribution of the target policy is necessary: even with arbitrarily large data from recurrent states, learning a near-optimal policy is impossible without $\tilde{\Omega}(T_{\text{hit}})$ samples from transient state-action pairs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its theoretical guarantees against two prior works on average-reward offline RL. Ozdaglar et al. [2024] obtain a bound of $\tilde{O}(\sqrt{C\tau_{\text{unif}}^2 S / m})$ where $\tau_{\text{unif}}$ is the uniform mixing time over all policies and $C$ is a coverage coefficient; this bound degrades with $C$ even as $m$ is held constant, and $\tau_{\text{unif}}$ upper-bounds the complexity of all policies rather than just the target. Gabbianelli et al. [2023] use a uniform span bound $H_{\text{unif}} = \sup_\pi \|h^\pi\|_{\text{span}}$ and require all policies to have constant gain (excluding weakly communicating MDPs). The present paper's bound $\tilde{O}(\sqrt{S\|h^{\pi^*}\|_{\text{span}}/m})$ is strictly sharper since $\|h^{\pi^*}\|_{\text{span}} \leq O(\tau_{\text{unif}})$ and depends only on the target policy, and it handles general weakly communicating MDPs. Additionally, the algorithm requires no prior knowledge of complexity parameters (unlike both prior works which need concentrability coefficients), and the lower bound in Theorem 3.3 shows that the uniform-complexity bounds of prior work become vacuous precisely in the regime where single-policy parameters remain bounded.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper refines the pessimistic value iteration framework of Li et al. [2023] (itself a refinement of Rashidinejad et al. [2022]) from the discounted tabular setting to average-reward offline RL via a large-discount reduction. It departs from prior average-reward offline RL work (Ozdaglar et al. [2024], Gabbianelli et al. [2023]) by replacing uniform-over-all-policies complexity measures (uniform mixing time, uniform span bound) with single-policy measures: the bias span of the target policy and a novel policy hitting radius. The key technical departure is a quantile clipping technique that enables a sharper empirical-span-based Bernstein penalty while preserving monotonicity and γ-contractivity of the pessimistic Bellman operator, which prior span-based penalties could not achieve.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes the first fully single-policy sample complexity bound for average-reward offline RL and the first to handle general weakly communicating MDPs, resolving a gap where prior guarantees were vacuous due to uniform complexity parameters. A major open question is whether the transient-state coverage requirement (data from states with zero stationary probability under the target policy) can be circumvented with additional structural assumptions or side information, since it may be impractical in real-world data collection. The tabular restriction limits immediate practical impact, and extending to function approximation to remove the S-dependence is identified as the primary next step.
