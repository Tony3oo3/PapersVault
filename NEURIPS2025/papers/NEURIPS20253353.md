## Problem

Processing contexts that exceed a model's pre-trained window forces a trade-off: recurrent KV-cache compression methods are memory-efficient but suffer from information loss ('forgetting'), while random-access methods preserve full context but demand large memory and incur high latency from CPU offloading. No existing training-free approach combines the low memory footprint of compression with the precise retrieval fidelity of random access.

## Input / Output

REFORM consumes a long input (up to 1M tokens, including multi-modal text+image sequences) that is split into 32k-token chunks with the final segment treated as the query. Internally it produces two artifacts: a set of lightweight per-token cross-layer context embeddings (concatenated, L2-normalized QKV vectors from four selected heads, ~640-dim per token) and a recomputed full-depth KV cache restricted to the gathered relevant tokens. The final output is a standard generated text response conditioned on this recomputed cache, with a recomputation budget of 8k–16k tokens depending on the base model.

## Method

REFORM operates in two phases without modifying model weights. In the encoding phase, it performs recurrent chunked forwarding: each 32k chunk is processed through the transformer up to an intermediate early-exit layer, the KV cache is compressed via H2O attention-based token eviction with position-ID reassignment, and QKV states from four pre-selected heads (two from a pattern-matching task, two from a multi-hop QA task) are stored as token-level context embeddings. In the recomputation phase, cosine similarity between query-token embeddings and all stored context embeddings identifies the most relevant tokens (smoothed with a 129-token max-pool kernel), which are then gathered and forwarded through all layers to rebuild a high-fidelity KV cache for generation. The key design insight is that the compressed cache serves only as a vehicle for embedding extraction rather than direct generation, and that value-head QKV states from mid-to-lower layers outperform both raw attention scores and full hidden states for retrieval, enabling both early exit and compact embeddings.

## Results

On RULER at 1M context length with Mistral-NeMo-Instruct-2407, REFORM achieves 75.5% average accuracy, outperforming the best baseline (InfLLM at 23.3%) by over 52 percentage points. On BABILong at 1M, REFORM scores 48.8% versus InfLLM's 9.6% (a 34-point gain). On ∞-Bench, REFORM reaches 50.2% average accuracy (Mistral-Nemo) versus 37.6% for InfLLM, and on RepoEval (1.5B API-level), it achieves 65.3% edit similarity versus 61.8% for InfLLM. In efficiency, REFORM reduces inference time by 80% (27.24s vs. 129.14s) and peak memory by 32% (35.00 GB vs. 51.62 GB) compared to InfLLM on 256k-token inputs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

REFORM dominates all five baselines across every benchmark and context length tested. Against the strongest recurrent baseline InfiniPot, REFORM gains 63.5 points on RULER 1M (75.5 vs. 12.0) and 40 points on BABILong 1M (48.8 vs. 8.8), with the gap widening as context length increases. Against the strongest random-access baseline InfLLM, REFORM achieves 75.5% vs. 23.3% on RULER 1M and 48.8% vs. 9.6% on BABILong 1M, while also reducing inference time from 129.14s to 27.24s (80% faster) and peak memory from 51.62 GB to 35.00 GB (32% less). The simpler recurrent baselines (StreamingLLM, TOVA, H2O) degrade sharply beyond 128k tokens, with H2O scoring only 3.6% on RULER 1M, whereas REFORM maintains 75.5%. On the multi-modal MM-NIAH benchmark (where InfLLM is not applicable), REFORM achieves 57.5% average versus 53.0% for InfiniPot, the best text-only baseline. On RepoEval code completion, REFORM leads all baselines by 3.5 points (65.3% vs. 61.8% for InfLLM) on the 1.5B API-level task.

## Relation to Prior Work

REFORM positions itself at the intersection of two established lines of long-context processing: recurrent KV cache compression (StreamingLLM, H2O, TOVA, InfiniPot) and random-access retrieval (InfLLM, ReAttention). Its key conceptual departure is repurposing the compressed KV cache not for direct generation (as recurrent methods do) nor for full storage (as random-access methods do), but solely as a vehicle to extract lightweight token-level cross-layer embeddings. The method then bridges to random access by using similarity search over these embeddings to identify relevant tokens and recomputing their full KV cache on demand, effectively combining the memory efficiency of recurrence with the retrieval fidelity of full attention. The head-selection mechanism draws on mechanistic interpretability findings about specialized retrieval heads, and the compression component directly adopts H2O's heavy-hitter eviction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

REFORM offers a practical, training-free, modality-agnostic framework that substantially narrows the gap between efficient recurrent processing and full-attention quality at 1M+ token contexts, with concrete efficiency wins (30% less inference time, 5% less peak memory) over both recurrent and random-access baselines. Its limitations are acknowledged: the gather stage's token selection can fail on edge cases, the current implementation redundantly recomputes attention scores outside Flash Attention, and the compression component is borrowed rather than co-designed. Open directions include integrating eviction into the Flash Attention kernel, exploring more sophisticated compression tailored to embedding construction, and extending to audio/video modalities.
