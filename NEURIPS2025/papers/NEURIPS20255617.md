## Problem

Most remote sensing foundation models rely solely on imagery, leaving the rich geographic context in ground-level vector data like OpenStreetMap largely untapped. The fundamental modality gap—OSM stores heterogeneous vector geometry (points, polylines, polygons) with free-form semantic tags, while RS images are fixed-grid rasters—makes direct integration difficult, and existing indirect strategies (rasterization, knowledge graphs, synthetic captions) either lose spatial information or are too task-specific to scale with the FM paradigm.

## Input / Output

The model consumes paired RGB RS images (multi-resolution, 0.1–30 m/pixel GSD) and co-located OSM vector data (points, polylines, polygons with tag-value dictionaries) covering the same geographic extent. During pretraining, 75% of image patches and 20% of OSM nodes are randomly masked; the OSM tags are encoded via BERT into frequency-weighted node features. At inference, the model outputs three encoding types—unimodal RS patch encodings, hybrid RS-OSM patch encodings, and hybrid OSM-RS object encodings—that feed into task-specific heads for classification, segmentation, change detection, or regression (population density, CO₂ emission).

## Method

GeoLink comprises three encoders: a ViT-L RS image encoder, a heterogeneous GATConv-based OSM encoder that models nine edge types derived from topological spatial relationships (with Delaunay triangulation for point-point connections), and a two-way Transformer object-patch fusion encoder with one self-attention and two cross-attention layers. Three self-supervised objectives jointly optimize the system: (1) MAE-style pixel reconstruction on masked image patches, (2) region-image contrastive alignment via InfoNCE loss using Set2Set aggregation for OSM and mean pooling for RS, and (3) a spatial consistency loss that forces hybrid OSM-RS object encodings to match the initial tag features of masked nodes. A critical design choice is the use of sinusoidal position embeddings (with key-point sampling for polylines/polygons) added to both modalities in the fusion encoder, which resolves the spatial ambiguity that would otherwise cause erroneous cross-attention associations. Pretrained on 1.27 million RS-OSM pairs from SkyScript-top30 for only 60 epochs—far fewer than the 600–800 epochs typical of prior RS FMs—GeoLink achieves faster convergence while outperforming all compared baselines across unimodal and multimodal downstream tasks.

## Results

On seven classification benchmarks, GeoLink (ViT-L) achieves state-of-the-art fine-tuning accuracy across all datasets, reaching 97.35% on MLRSNet, 98.30% on EuroSAT, 98.41% on WHU-RS19, 91.72% on OPTIMAL-31, 94.45% on RESISC-45, 83.38% on AiRound, and 98.10% on UCMerced. Under the kNN protocol (no further training), GeoLink shows the largest margins over baselines, e.g., 93.48% vs. 92.63% (Cross-Scale MAE) on MLRSNet and 87.33% vs. 85.10% on RESISC-45. On semantic segmentation and change detection, GeoLink leads on all four PANGAEA-bench datasets under both encoder-freezing and fine-tuning settings (e.g., 64.93% mIoU on FiveBillionPixels, 47.29% on AI4SmallFarms). On multimodal geographic tasks, GeoLink-multimodal achieves 60.00% mIoU on UFZ segmentation, 81.68% on urban village identification, 51.88% r² on population density, and 65.12% r² on CO₂ estimation, all outperforming the Scale-MAE+OSM baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GeoLink outperforms all six baselines (GASSL, MMEarth, Scale-MAE, Cross-Scale MAE, CROMA, DOFA) on the majority of classification benchmarks under all three evaluation protocols (kNN, linear probing, fine-tuning). The margins are most pronounced under kNN, where GeoLink leads by 0.85–2.23 points over the strongest competitor (Cross-Scale MAE), indicating superior structured representation learning. On semantic segmentation and change detection, GeoLink consistently achieves the best mIoU across all four PANGAEA-bench datasets under both freezing and fine-tuning, with the largest gains on the more challenging AI4SmallFarms (+1.31% over Scale-MAE in fine-tuning) and xView2 (+0.47% over DOFA). For multimodal geographic tasks, GeoLink-multimodal surpasses Scale-MAE+OSM on UFZ (60.00% vs. ~57% mIoU), urban village identification (81.68% vs. 80.09%), population density (51.88% vs. 48.29% r²), and CO₂ estimation (65.12% vs. 59.97% r²), confirming that the proposed object-patch fusion encoder and multimodal pretraining provide meaningful gains beyond simply appending OSM features to an existing RS FM.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the RS foundation model pretraining paradigm (Scale-MAE, CROMA, DOFA) by directly integrating OpenStreetMap vector data as a second modality during self-supervised learning, departing from prior indirect strategies that rasterize OSM into labels, generate text captions, or build knowledge graphs. The cross-modal alignment mechanism draws on CLIP-style contrastive learning for region-image alignment and introduces a heterogeneous GATConv-based OSM encoder to handle the geometric heterogeneity (points/polylines/polygons) and non-Euclidean topology of vector data. The masked-input strategy follows MAE and FLIP, while the object-patch fusion encoder with sinusoidal position embeddings is a novel design for spatially-aware cross-modal interaction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GeoLink demonstrates that OSM data during pretraining improves the RS image encoder's unimodal capacity (not just multimodal tasks), and that direct vector-level integration outperforms indirect OSM utilization for comprehensive geographic tasks like urban function zone mapping and population/carbon estimation. The new multimodal benchmarks (UFZ, UV, POP, CO2) fill a gap in evaluating RS FMs on realistic urban planning scenarios. Key limitations include RGB-only support (no multispectral), loss of spatial detail in the current position embedding scheme, and reliance on OSM data completeness that varies globally. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to other vector data sources (e.g., LiDAR, cadastral maps) and how to better encode spatial geometry without key-point sampling.
