## Problem

Existing video prediction methods operate in low-level VAE latent spaces or raw RGB, forcing models to simultaneously capture appearance details and semantic dynamics, which wastes capacity and limits downstream scene understanding. Prior semantic feature prediction approaches require separate models per task or complex multi-encoder architectures, hindering scalability. The paper asks whether the semantically rich features of pretrained Vision Foundation Models (VFMs) can serve as a unified latent space for multi-task future-frame prediction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255466/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes 4 context RGB frames at 448×896 resolution (subsampled by factor 3 from driving video sequences) and predicts the DINOv2 multi-layer feature representation (layers 3, 6, 9, 12, concatenated and PCA-reduced to a compact dimension D) for 1 future frame at 32×64 spatial resolution. At inference, the predicted features are fed into interchangeable task-specific heads (DPT for segmentation/depth/normals, Mask2Former for instance segmentation) to produce pixel-wise outputs. The core feature predictor is trained entirely self-supervised with no task labels; heads are trained separately on frozen VFM features and applied off-the-shelf.

## Method

DINO-Foresight trains a 12-layer masked feature transformer (hidden dim 1152) in a self-supervised manner to forecast the temporal evolution of DINOv2 features, using SmoothL1 loss between predicted and ground-truth features at masked future-frame positions. The transformer decomposes attention into temporal (across frames at same spatial position) and spatial (within each frame) components to handle the 2048 tokens per frame efficiently. Multi-layer VFM features are concatenated along channels and compressed via PCA to reduce prediction dimensionality while retaining semantic information. A two-phase training strategy (low-res 224×448 followed by high-res 448×896 fine-tuning) addresses the computational cost of high-resolution feature forecasting. The key architectural insight is treating VFM features as a semantically grounded latent space where off-the-shelf task heads plug in without retraining the core predictor, enabling a single model to handle semantic segmentation, instance segmentation, depth, and surface normals simultaneously.

## Results

On Cityscapes, DINO-Foresight achieves 71.8 mIoU (ALL) / 71.7 mIoU (MO) for short-term semantic segmentation and 59.8 / 57.6 for mid-term, 50.5 AP50 for short-term instance segmentation, 88.6% δ₁ for short-term depth, and 94.4% pixels within 11.25° for short-term surface normals. A single model handles all four tasks simultaneously, approaching Oracle performance on depth (88.6 vs. 89.1 δ₁) and surface normals (94.4 vs. 95.3°). On nuScenes, the method achieves 80.7% δ₁ (mid-term depth) and 93.9% (mid-term surface normals), with zero-shot transfer from Cityscapes retaining 78.4% δ₁.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DINO-Foresight outperforms VISTA_ft (a 2.5B-parameter latent diffusion world model) across all four tasks: semantic segmentation (71.8 vs. 64.9 short-term ALL mIoU), instance segmentation (50.5 vs. 33.1 short-term AP50), depth (88.6 vs. 86.4 short-term δ₁), and surface normals (94.4 vs. 93.0 short-term 11.25°), while being ~100× faster at inference (5 min vs. 8.3 hours on Cityscapes validation). Against the strongest prior semantic segmentation method PFA, DINO-Foresight achieves comparable short-term ALL mIoU (71.8 vs. 71.1) and better MO mIoU (71.7 vs. 69.2), while also handling instance segmentation (50.5 vs. 48.7 AP50), depth, and surface normals in a single model. The method slightly trails Futurist on semantic segmentation (71.8 vs. 73.9 short-term ALL) and depth (88.6 vs. 96.0 short-term δ₁), but Futurist supports only two tasks whereas DINO-Foresight unifies four. Compared to the Oracle upper bound, DINO-Foresight closes most of the gap on depth (88.6 vs. 89.1 δ₁) and surface normals (94.4 vs. 95.3°), with a larger gap on instance segmentation (50.5 vs. 66.2 AP50). The VAE latent space comparison (Table 2) shows that forecasting VAE features yields dramatically worse results (33.4 mIoU vs. 71.8 for DINOv2 features), confirming the semantic advantage of VFM feature forecasting over low-level latent generative approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the semantic future prediction line (F2F, F2MF, PFA, APANet) by replacing task-specific encoder features with frozen Vision Foundation Model (VFM) features as the prediction target, and by training a single masked feature transformer (built on the MaskGIT implementation and the temporal/spatial attention decomposition from MaskViT) to forecast these features in a self-supervised manner. It departs fundamentally from latent generative approaches (VAE-based diffusion/autoregressive models like VISTA) that operate in low-level pixel-reconstruction spaces, arguing instead that VFM features constitute a semantically rich latent space to which off-the-shelf task heads (DPT, Mask2Former) can be attached without retraining the core predictor.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical impact is that a single 258M-parameter model handles four dense prediction tasks (semantic segmentation, instance segmentation, depth, surface normals) simultaneously, where prior methods required separate models per task, and it does so ~100× faster than the 2.5B-parameter VISTA world model on the same downstream pipeline. Open questions include whether the deterministic SmoothL1 objective can be extended to capture future uncertainty (diffusion or GMM losses), whether hierarchical transformer architectures can reduce the still-high inference cost of high-resolution feature maps, and whether scaling to larger VFMs (e.g., RADIOv2.5) and more diverse datasets will close the remaining gap to the Oracle baseline. The finding that intermediate masked-transformer layers themselves improve downstream features suggests an underexplored self-supervised pretraining signal.
