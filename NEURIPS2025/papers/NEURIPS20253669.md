## Problem

State-of-the-art vision-language models perceive 2D images but lack an internal model of 3D dynamics, causing them to fail on spatial reasoning tasks that require imagining how a scene appears after an egocentric motion. This gap limits VLMs' usefulness for embodied agents operating in 3D space, as they cannot treat a single image as an interactive world to explore.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253669/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB reference image and a multiple-choice spatial reasoning question as input. During inference, a discrete action space of primitive egocentric motions (move forward 0.25 m, turn left/right 9°) is explored over up to 3 search steps with up to 3 consecutive repetitions per action. The output is a final answer to the question, produced by a QA VLM that consumes the original image plus a buffer of up to H helpful imagined egocentric frames, each paired with a natural-language trajectory description (e.g., 'move forward 0.2 m, then turn right 30°').

## Method

MindJourney is a training-free test-time scaling framework that couples a frozen VLM with a pose-conditioned video diffusion world model through a Spatial Beam Search. At each search step, every beam node is expanded by appending up to k repetitions of each primitive action; the world model (either the authors' Search World Model based on Wan2.2-TI2V-5B with a learnable camera encoder, or Stable-Virtual-Camera) renders the corresponding egocentric video in a single batched call. A Search VLM then scores each candidate trajectory on two axes—exploration utility (s_exp) and answer helpfulness (s_help)—using the question, a text description of the trajectory, and the generated frame; candidates below fixed thresholds are pruned, the top-B by s_exp form the next beam, and the top-H by s_help are cached in an evidence buffer. After n steps (or beam exhaustion), a QA VLM receives the original image, question, and all buffered multi-view evidence in a single pass to produce the final answer. The world model is trained via supervised fine-tuning on a mixture of Habitat-simulated navigation clips (HM3D), RealEstate10K, and DL3DV-10K, with only the camera encoder and a projector updated on top of the frozen Wan2.2 backbone.

## Results

On the SAT-Real benchmark (150 real-image queries), MindJourney achieves an average 7.7 percentage-point improvement across four VLM backends, with the largest single gain of +10.3% for GPT-4o (60.3% → 70.6% with SWM). On SAT-Synthesized (500-question subset), the average improvement is 8.0 percentage points. The best overall result is OpenAI o1 + MindJourney (SWM) at 84.7% average top-1 accuracy on SAT-Real, surpassing all prior baselines including Gemini 1.5-pro (64.8%) and vanilla o1 (74.6%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MindJourney consistently outperforms all four baseline VLMs on both SAT-Real and SAT-Synthesized. GPT-4o gains the most (+10.3% on SAT-Real, 60.3%→70.6% with SWM), while GPT-4.1 improves by +6.6% (74.0%→80.6%). Critically, GPT-4.1 + MindJourney (80.6%) surpasses vanilla OpenAI o1 (74.6%), demonstrating that world-model-driven test-time scaling can exceed RL-based chain-of-thought scaling without any fine-tuning. When MindJourney is applied on top of o1 itself, accuracy climbs to 84.7% (SWM) or 77.3% (SVC) on SAT-Real, establishing a new state of the art. On SAT-Synthesized, the pattern holds: o1 + MJ(SVC) reaches 78.6%, the highest score across all configurations. The method is model-agnostic, working with both closed-source (GPT-4o, GPT-4.1, o1) and open-source (InternVL3-14B) VLMs, and with two distinct world models (SWM and SVC).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two active research threads: controllable video diffusion models (CamCtrl3D, CameraCtrl, Stable-Virtual-Camera) that synthesize egocentric views from a single frame plus a pose trajectory, and test-time scaling for VLMs (best-of-n sampling, guided beam search, MCTS-style tree search). Prior test-time scaling work has been almost entirely text-centric—operating over chains of thought or token sequences—while prior world-model work has targeted robotic decision-making rather than spatial-reasoning QA. MindJourney's contribution is the first coupling of a VLM with a pose-conditioned video world model in a Spatial Beam Search loop, using the world model as a physically grounded 'imagination engine' that supplies geometry-aware visual evidence to guide and inform the VLM's reasoning. The world model architecture itself (SWM) follows ReCamMaster's camera-encoder design on top of Wan2.2-TI2V-5B, so the architectural novelty is modest; the novelty lies in the search-and-evidence pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical takeaway is that a frozen VLM paired with a controllable world model can match or exceed RL-fine-tuned test-time scaling (o1) on spatial reasoning without any fine-tuning, suggesting that physically consistent imagined roll-outs provide information largely orthogonal to learned chain-of-thought. However, the evidence is confined to a single benchmark (SAT) with modest question counts (150 real, 500 synthetic), and the world model's generation quality degrades noticeably beyond 2–3 steps, capping the benefit of deeper search. Open questions include extending to multi-view inputs, developing query-conditioned world models to avoid hallucinated irrelevant views, and building world models that sustain geometric and photometric consistency over much longer roll-outs—without which the framework's ceiling remains limited.
