## Problem

LLMs used as their own evaluators suffer from inherent biases and overconfidence, and existing self-refinement approaches (e.g., SelfRefine, multi-agent debate) fail or even degrade performance in domains where the model lacks sufficient internal knowledge. This paper asks: can LLM-generated pseudo-labels for binary classification be iteratively denoised with minimal human supervision, without relying on the LLM's internal domain expertise for the refinement step?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251058/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes an unlabeled text corpus, a small labeled subset (50 or 100 examples) used solely to estimate the positive-class priors θ_p and θ_n of the pseudo-positive and pseudo-negative splits, and initial pseudo-labels produced by an LLM via few-shot prompting. It outputs a refined binary classifier (a transformer with an appended affine scoring head, fine-tuned via QLoRA 4-bit) and, when deployed as a reward model in an RLHF/PPO pipeline, scalar safety scores that shift the generated-response reward distribution toward the safe region. The critical structural requirement is that the two unlabeled corpora must have differing positive-class ratios; the method does not need per-example ground-truth labels.

## Method

The core pipeline iterates three steps: (i) an LLM assigns initial pseudo-labels to an unlabeled corpus, splitting it into pseudo-positive and pseudo-negative sets; (ii) a transformer classifier is trained on these two noisy sets using a Robust UU Learning objective, which constructs an unbiased risk estimate from the two mixture distributions and applies a generalized Leaky ReLU (f(x)=x for x>0, λx for x<0, λ=−0.001) to suppress the negative risk terms that cause overfitting under label noise; (iii) the trained classifier re-labels the entire corpus, producing cleaner pseudo-labels for the next iteration. The key distinction from standard pseudo-labeling or confidence-based filtering is that the risk estimator itself is robust to mislabels—only the assumption that one corpus has a higher positive ratio than the other is required, decoupling refinement quality from the LLM's internal knowledge. As iterations proceed, the pseudo-positive prior converges toward 1 and the pseudo-negative prior toward 0, at which point Robust UU Learning reduces to standard supervised learning, yielding high-accuracy classification from initially noisy LLM annotations.

## Results

On the three harder classification tasks (Corona Sentiment, Green Patent, Protein Structure), the proposed method with only 50 labeled examples reaches 84.7%, 79.2%, and 78.1% accuracy after 5 iterations, respectively. On easier tasks (Fake News, SaRoCo, Safety), the method achieves 99.5%, 94.2%, and 82.6% accuracy (50-labeled setting), approaching the oracle-prior upper bound. Notably, on the Safety dataset for RLAIF alignment, the UU-refined reward model shifts the reward distribution toward safer outputs, whereas vanilla RLAIF (PN-trained RM) performs worse than the SFT baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the harder tasks, GPT-4o self-refinement [37] actually degrades performance on Green Patent (0.676→0.611) and Protein Structure (0.545→0.599), while the proposed method (50-labeled) reaches 0.792 and 0.781 respectively — a gap of 18.1 and 18.2 percentage points. DeepSeek-R1 [13] starts with the highest initial accuracy on all three harder tasks (0.773, 0.686, 0.709) but plateaus or slightly declines over iterations, whereas the proposed method surpasses it by 7.4, 13.0, and 7.4 points at Iter 5. On easier tasks, PIE [66] achieves modest gains on Fake News (0.698→0.746 with Meta-LL-3-8B) but collapses to chance level (~0.500) on SaRoCo and Safety, while the proposed method reaches 0.942 and 0.826 on those same datasets. CCP [26] similarly fails on SaRoCo and Safety (stuck at ~0.512–0.519) despite improving on Fake News (0.504→0.832), confirming that contrastive feature learning from a small teacher set is insufficient when class-specific features are hard to capture. Overall, the proposed method wins on all six datasets against every baseline, with the largest margins (15–20 pp) on the harder, domain-specific tasks where LLM internal knowledge is most limited.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of LLM self-refinement (SelfRefine, multi-agent debate) and weakly supervised learning, specifically the Unlabeled-Unlabeled (UU) learning framework of Lu et al. (ICLR 2019) and its robust variant with a generalized Leaky ReLU correction (Lu et al., AISTATS 2020). Rather than modifying the teacher model as in classical pseudo-labeling, the authors modify the risk estimator: LLM-generated pseudo-labels serve as the initial noisy split into two unlabeled corpora, and iterative robust UU learning progressively denoises them. This departs from prior LLM self-refinement work by decoupling the refinement signal from the LLM's internal knowledge, instead relying on data-driven feature extraction, which the authors position as a necessary correction for domains where the model lacks sufficient internal knowledge to evaluate its own outputs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates a practical, low-supervision pathway (50–100 labeled examples) to improve LLM-generated labels in domains where even GPT-4o and DeepSeek-R1 self-refinement plateaus or degrades, with the refined classifier further enabling RLAIF-based safety alignment where vanilla RLAIF fails. Key limitations include restriction to binary classification, sensitivity to extremely noisy initial pseudo-labels (when pseudo-positive and pseudo-negative priors converge), and the requirement that two unlabeled subsets with differing class ratios be available. Open questions include extension to multi-class settings, instance-level difficulty modeling to guide curriculum, and whether the approach generalizes to open-ended generative tasks beyond the safety-alignment demonstration.
