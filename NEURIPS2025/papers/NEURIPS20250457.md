## Problem

Existing video MLLMs rely on CLIP-based visual encoders pretrained on image-text pairs, which capture high-level semantics but lack the fine-grained 3D structural cues needed for spatial reasoning. This forces current models to depend on additional 3D or 2.5D inputs (point clouds, depth maps, camera parameters) for spatial understanding, severely limiting their applicability in real-world scenarios where only monocular 2D video is available.

## Input / Output

The model consumes 2D video frames at 640×480 resolution along with a text question, but due to GPU memory constraints only 16 frames are fed to the model from videos that may contain over 2,000 frames. A space-aware frame sampling strategy first processes 128 uniformly subsampled frames through the spatial encoder to decode camera parameters and depth maps, then selects the 16 most spatially informative frames via a greedy maximum-coverage algorithm over reconstructed voxels. The output is a spatial reasoning answer in one of three formats: a numerical value (e.g., distance in meters, object count), a multiple-choice selection, or a verbal response.

## Method

Spatial-MLLM introduces a dual-encoder architecture built on Qwen2.5-VL-3B: a 2D visual encoder extracts semantic patch features, while a 3D spatial encoder—initialized from the VGGT backbone and using alternating frame-wise and global self-attention—recovers dense 3D structural features from the same 2D frames. A lightweight MLP connector aligns the two feature streams in spatial and temporal dimensions (via 2×2 spatial and 2-frame temporal merging) and fuses them by element-wise addition into unified visual tokens for the LLM backbone. At inference, the space-aware frame sampling leverages VGGT's pretrained camera and depth heads to reconstruct per-frame point maps, voxelizes the scene, and solves a maximum coverage problem with a greedy algorithm to select frames that collectively cover the most unique scene voxels. Training follows a two-stage pipeline: supervised fine-tuning on ~120k spatial QA pairs (from ScanQA, SQA3D, and self-created ScanNet-derived data) with frozen encoders, followed by GRPO reinforcement learning with task-specific rewards (mean relative accuracy for numerical, exact match for multiple-choice, Levenshtein-based fuzzy matching for verbal) to enhance long chain-of-thought spatial reasoning.

## Results

On VSI-Bench, Spatial-MLLM-4B achieves a micro-average score of 48.4, ranking #1 across all proprietary and open-source models, outperforming the best proprietary model Gemini-1.5 Pro (45.4) by 3.0 points despite using only 16 input frames vs. ~85 frames for Gemini. On ScanQA (val), Spatial-MLLM-4B achieves BLEU-1 of 44.4, CIDEr of 91.8, and ROUGE-L of 45.0, surpassing all video-input models and most 3D/2.5D-input models. On SQA3D (test), it reaches EM-1 of 55.9 and EM-R1 of 58.7, outperforming all video-input baselines and task-specific models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Spatial-MLLM-4B (4.9B params) outperforms all 18 baselines on VSI-Bench, beating the strongest proprietary model Gemini-1.5 Pro (45.4) by 3.0 points and the best open-source model LLaVA-Video-72B (40.9) by 7.5 points, despite using only 16 frames vs. 85+ for Gemini. On ScanQA, it surpasses all video-input models (best: LLaVA-Video-7B at BLEU-1 39.7) by 4.7 BLEU-1 points and outperforms 3D/2.5D-input models like Chat-Scene (BLEU-1 43.2) and 3D-LLaVA (BLEU-4 17.1), though Video-3D LLM (BLEU-1 47.1, CIDEr 102.1) retains a slight edge on ScanQA. On SQA3D, Spatial-MLLM-4B (EM-1 55.9, EM-R1 58.7) beats all video-input and task-specific models, trailing only Video-3D LLM (EM-1 58.6) which requires depth map input. Against its own base model Qwen2.5-VL-3B (30.6), Spatial-MLLM achieves a 17.8-point improvement, and even the 72B Qwen2.5-VL (37.0) falls 11.4 points short, demonstrating that the dual-encoder architecture and spatial training data drive the gains rather than model scale.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on Qwen2.5-VL as the base video MLLM (providing the 2D semantic encoder and LLM backbone) and VGGT as a feed-forward visual geometry foundation model (providing the 3D spatial encoder backbone). It departs from prior 3D MLLMs (Chat-Scene, 3D-LLaVA, Video-3D LLM) that require explicit 3D/2.5D inputs like point clouds or depth maps, and from standard video MLLMs whose CLIP-based encoders capture semantics but lack geometric structure. The key architectural departure is the dual-encoder design that fuses a semantic 2D encoder with a structure-aware 3D encoder via a lightweight MLP connector, combined with a space-aware frame sampling strategy that reformulates frame selection as a maximum coverage problem over predicted voxels.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Spatial-MLLM demonstrates that purely 2D video inputs, augmented with a pretrained geometry model's structural priors, can match or exceed methods that require expensive 3D/2.5D data, making spatial reasoning accessible in real-world settings where only monocular video is available. The space-aware frame sampling technique is a practical, generalizable strategy for handling long videos under VRAM constraints that could benefit other video MLLM applications. Limitations include evaluation restricted to indoor ScanNet scenes, a relatively small model (4.9B) and training dataset (~120k QA pairs), and the open question of whether integrating spatial structural information benefits general video understanding beyond spatial-specific tasks.
