## Problem

Existing first-order bilevel optimization methods for LLM fine-tuning (e.g., F²SA, BOME) require inner loops to estimate the lower-level optimal solution y*_g(x), incurring high per-iteration computational and memory costs that scale poorly with model size. The paper asks whether the value-function estimation term can be entirely removed from the upper-level update while preserving convergence, and identifies the precise conditions under which this simplification is valid.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pretrained LLM backbone x (e.g., attention weights parameterized via LoRA) and a lightweight output head y, along with a DPO preference dataset (Dahoas/rm-hh-rlhf) and an SFT supervised dataset (OpenOrca). It produces a preference-aligned backbone x and a task-specialized head y, where x is updated using only ∇_x f(x, y) (the DPO gradient) and y is updated via a single-step gradient on the penalized LL objective. The key structural constraint is that the backbone and head are updated in a fully single-loop fashion with K=1 inner step, eliminating the need to maintain or estimate y*_g(x) separately.

## Method

PBGD-Free removes the penalty correction term γ(∇_x g(x, y*_γ) − ∇_x g(x, y*_g)) from the standard PBGD x-update, replacing it with the simpler ∇_x f(x, y*_γ), which eliminates the inner loop for y*_g and reduces memory from O(d_x + 2d_y) to O(d_x + d_y). The paper first proves that under the standard Lipschitz condition on the UL objective, this omission introduces a non-vanishing Θ(l_{f,0}²) bias, preventing convergence to a true stationary point. To overcome this, the authors introduce a (δ, α)-flatness condition (a Hölder-like bound on f(x,·) around y*_g(x)) that is strictly weaker than small Lipschitz continuity, and prove that under this condition the algorithm achieves O(ε^{-1}) iteration complexity with a penalty constant γ = O(δ^{-(2-α)/2}). The flatness condition is empirically validated on the representation-learning PEFT problem, where δ(x) ≤ 0.0003 throughout optimization despite a large Lipschitz constant, confirming the condition holds in practice for LLM fine-tuning.

## Results

On LLAMA-3-3B PEFT (DPO+SFT bilevel problem), PBGD-Free achieves the best DPO eval loss of 0.7837 and SFT eval loss of 0.8516 on the trained outcome (x_T, y_T), outperforming V-PBGD (0.818/1.0309), BOME (0.8332/1.1552), and ALRIGHT (0.8055/0.8656). After post-SFT tuning with a fixed backbone (x_T, ỹ), PBGD-Free further reduces SFT train loss to 0.6688 (vs. 0.7855 for ALRIGHT, 0.9533 for V-PBGD), demonstrating superior transferability of the learned preference backbone. In theory, PBGD-Free achieves O(ε⁻¹) iteration complexity with single-loop updates and O(d_x + d_y) memory, compared to O(ε⁻¹·⁵) for V-PBGD and F²SA-MA, and O(ε⁻⁴) for BOME.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PBGD-Free outperforms all baselines on every reported metric in Table 2. Against V-PBGD [78], PBGD-Free reduces DPO eval loss by 0.034 (0.7837 vs. 0.818) and SFT eval loss by 0.179 (0.8516 vs. 1.0309), while also halving memory cost from O(d_x+2d_y) to O(d_x+d_y) and eliminating the inner loop. Against BOME [105], the SFT eval gap is even larger (0.8516 vs. 1.1552), and PBGD-Free's O(ε⁻¹) complexity is dramatically better than BOME's O(ε⁻⁴). Against ALRIGHT [24] (a bi-objective baseline), PBGD-Free wins on all four metrics, with the most notable advantage in post-SFT transferability (SFT train loss 0.6688 vs. 0.7855), and also exhibits more stable training curves (Figure 7). Against F²SA [45] and its momentum variant F²SA-MA, PBGD-Free converges faster in wall-clock time (Figure 7) while matching the O(ε⁻¹) complexity of the double-loop F²SA [11] but with single-loop updates and lower per-iteration cost. Against BVFSM [51], PBGD-Free provides a finite-time O(ε⁻¹) guarantee versus only asymptotic convergence.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the penalty-based bilevel optimization (PBGD) line of work [Shen et al. 2023, Kwon et al. 2024, Ye et al. 2022, Chen et al. 2024], which reformulates bilevel problems via a penalty term to avoid second-order Hessian computations. While prior first-order methods (V-PBGD, BOME, F²SA) either jointly update both variables or require inner loops to estimate the lower-level value function y_g*(x), PBGD-Free eliminates the inner loop entirely by using only ∇_x f(x, y_γ*(x)) for the upper-level update. The key theoretical departure is the introduction of a (δ, α)-flatness condition on the upper-level objective, which is strictly weaker than the standard Lipschitz assumption used throughout the BLO literature, enabling convergence guarantees where prior analysis would only yield a non-vanishing error bound.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

For LLM fine-tuning practitioners, the single-loop, O(1) per-iteration cost of PBGD-Free translates to meaningful computational savings over double-loop baselines, and the empirical results on LLAMA-3-3B show competitive or superior DPO/SFT performance with faster wall-clock convergence. The flatness condition provides a principled explanation for why the simplified update works in representation-learning PEFT despite the large Lipschitz constant, bridging a gap between theory and practice. Open questions include whether the flatness condition can be verified or estimated more easily in general (beyond the PEFT setting), whether the approach extends to stochastic or distributed bilevel settings, and whether the O(ε^{-1}) complexity is truly optimal for this class of value-function-free algorithms.
