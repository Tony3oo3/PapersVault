## Problem

Large MoE models like DeepSeek-R1 (671B, 256 experts/layer) require ~1500 GB of memory under BF16, making deployment prohibitively expensive. Existing expert pruning methods were designed for smaller MoEs with few experts per layer and rely on metrics (frequency, gating scores) that fail to capture true expert contribution at scale. The paper identifies an unexploited phenomenon—few-shot expert localization—where a handful of domain-specific demonstrations reliably activate a stable, sparse subset of domain-relevant experts, enabling targeted pruning at high compression ratios.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a small calibration set (typically 25 samples) consisting of domain-specific inputs concatenated with the full model's generated responses, processed in a single forward pass through the MoE model. It produces a fixed-size retained expert set per layer (e.g., top-128 out of 256), which can be deployed on fewer GPUs. For mixed-domain pruning, normalized expert scores across multiple domain calibration sets are averaged before selection, yielding a single pruned model that handles multiple task domains simultaneously.

## Method

EASY-EP scores each expert per token using two complementary signals: (1) output-aware importance c_{i,t}^l = g_{i,t}^l · ‖e_{i,t}^l‖, which multiplies the router's gating value by the L2 norm of the expert's output to capture actual contribution magnitude (an expert with high gating but low-norm output is downweighted), and (2) token contribution s_t^l = 1 − cos(h_t^l, h̃_t^l), which measures how much the routed experts change the residual representation, giving higher weight to tokens where expert outputs matter most. The final expert score I(E_i^l) = Σ_t c_{i,t}^l · s_t^l aggregates over all calibration tokens, and the top-M experts per layer are retained. The entire scoring requires only one forward pass with no backpropagation, making it tractable for 256-expert models where perturbation-based alternatives would need >10^75 evaluations. The key empirical insight is that 5–25 domain-specific demonstrations suffice to identify >90% of critical domain experts, and these selections generalize across unseen datasets within the same domain.

## Results

On DeepSeek-R1 with domain-specific pruning (128 experts retained), EASY-EP achieves an average score of 66.55 across eight benchmarks, matching 99% of the full model's 67.20, while the best baseline (Gating Score) reaches only 56.49. On DeepSeek-V3-0324, EASY-EP actually exceeds the full model (54.12 vs. 54.08 avg) under domain-specific pruning and reaches 54.92 under mixed-domain pruning. Under mixed-domain pruning on DeepSeek-R1, EASY-EP retains 91.5% of full-model performance (61.47 vs. 67.20). In terms of throughput, pruning to 128 experts yields a 2.99× speedup and 64 experts yields 4.33× over the full model at 1K input / 1K output length.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EASY-EP is compared against three pruning baselines derived from SEER-MoE [5] (Random, Frequency, and Gating Score) and one expert-merging baseline, M-SMoE [25]. Under domain-specific pruning with 128 experts on DeepSeek-R1, EASY-EP (avg 66.55) outperforms Gating Score (56.49) by 10.06 points, Frequency (31.59) by 34.96 points, and M-SMoE (22.10) by 44.45 points. The gap is even more pronounced at 64 experts, where EASY-EP retains 45.22 avg while Gating Score drops to 8.75 and M-SMoE to 1.52. On DeepSeek-V3-0324, EASY-EP (54.12) marginally exceeds the full model and beats Gating Score (53.83) and M-SMoE (48.54) under domain-specific pruning. Under mixed-domain pruning, EASY-EP maintains a clear lead: 61.47 vs. 31.67 (Gating Score) and 18.40 (M-SMoE) on DeepSeek-R1, and 54.92 vs. 49.36 (Gating Score) and 40.84 (M-SMoE) on DeepSeek-V3-0324.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of training-free MoE expert pruning (SEER-MoE, NAEE, CD-MoE) and expert merging (M-SMoE) by introducing a domain-specific, few-shot calibration paradigm. Prior router-based methods rely on large calibration sets and assume gating scores alone reflect expert importance, while perturbation-based methods scale poorly to 256-expert models. EASY-EP departs from both by (1) showing that a handful of domain-specific demonstrations suffice to localize critical experts (the 'few-shot expert localization' phenomenon), and (2) augmenting gating scores with the L2 norm of expert outputs and a token-level residual-similarity weight, all in a single forward pass. The conceptual grounding draws on the observation that large MoE models (unlike smaller ones such as Mixtral 8×7B) develop genuinely domain-specialized experts, making high-ratio domain-specific pruning viable.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: halving experts in DeepSeek-R1 (671B) enables single-node 8×H800 deployment with 2.99× throughput, removing a major barrier to serving large MoE models. The few-shot expert localization finding is a useful structural insight for the MoE community, suggesting that expert specialization in large models is both strong and easily probeable. Key limitations include the domain-specific scope (out-of-domain performance degrades significantly), the absence of post-pruning fine-tuning, and evaluation limited to DeepSeek and Qwen3 models. Open questions include whether the phenomenon generalizes to other MoE architectures, whether lightweight fine-tuning can recover out-of-domain capabilities, and whether the observed robustness gap between reasoning and non-reasoning models reflects a fundamental architectural difference.
