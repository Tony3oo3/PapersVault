## Problem

Autoregressive video generation for interactive world models suffers from two coupled failures: compounding errors that accumulate irreducibly over long horizons, and insufficient memory mechanisms that break spatiotemporal consistency. The paper demonstrates that LLM-inspired remedies—extending context windows (YaRN) and naive retrieval-augmented generation—fail to transfer to video diffusion models due to their inherently weak in-context learning capabilities, leaving a gap in how to build coherent, interactive long-video world models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255166/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a 20-frame context window (10 retrieved historical frames + 10 current frames) in VAE latent space (16×32×18 per frame from 640×360 RGB input), along with per-frame action vectors (movement, jump, camera rotation) and a global state vector [x, y, z, yaw]. It autoregressively generates one frame at a time (stride 1), producing long interactive video sequences up to 1200 frames. The retrieved historical frames are selected via a similarity score computed over weighted global state coordinates rather than visual content.

## Method

VRAG augments a latent DiT video diffusion model (trained with Diffusion Forcing for per-frame noise robustness) with two key mechanisms: (1) global state conditioning that injects 3D position and orientation through AdaLN layers, and (2) retrieval-augmented memory that concatenates top-k historically similar frames (selected by weighted Euclidean distance in state space) with the current context. The critical training modification is explicit in-context training: retrieved frames receive a temporal RoPE offset, lower noise levels to simulate imperfect autoregressive outputs, masked diffusion loss (only current frames contribute to the objective), and masked action conditioning to prevent temporal discontinuity. This in-context training is essential because the paper shows that simply providing historical frames at inference without corresponding training yields performance comparable to the unmodified baseline, confirming that video diffusion models cannot exploit retrieved context through in-context learning alone.

## Results

On the world coherence evaluation (300-frame Minecraft sequences), VRAG achieves the best SSIM of 0.506, PSNR of 17.097, and LPIPS of 0.506, outperforming the strongest baseline DF(window 20) (SSIM 0.466, PSNR 16.643, LPIPS 0.538). On the compounding error evaluation (1200-frame sequences), VRAG attains SSIM 0.349 versus 0.321 for DF(window 20) and 0.316 for YaRN. On VBench, VRAG leads all baselines across all five video quality metrics, including Background Consistency (0.9686), Temporal Flickering (0.9511), and Motion Smoothness (0.9603).

## Baselines & Comparisons

VRAG outperforms all four baselines on both world coherence and compounding error metrics. Against YaRN (context window extension via RoPE frequency warping), VRAG gains +0.044 SSIM on world coherence (0.506 vs 0.462) and +0.033 on compounding error (0.349 vs 0.316), demonstrating that simply extending the temporal context window does not transfer effectively from LLMs to video diffusion models. Against the History Buffer baseline (heuristic frame retrieval without in-context training), VRAG achieves +0.047 SSIM on world coherence and a dramatic +0.161 on compounding error (0.349 vs 0.188), showing that naive retrieval without explicit VRAG training is largely ineffective. Against Frame Pack (frame compression via multi-scale kernels), VRAG leads by +0.085 SSIM on world coherence (0.506 vs 0.421), the largest margin among all comparisons. Against the Neural Memory baseline (Infini-attention adapted for video), VRAG achieves +0.066 SSIM on compounding error (0.349 vs 0.283); the Neural Memory method also exhibits training instability and is excluded from some visualizations. In the real-world RealEstate10K setting, VRAG (initialized from DFoT and fine-tuned for only 2 epochs) significantly outperforms the DFoT baseline in memorization capability, confirming generalization beyond Minecraft.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on latent video diffusion models (Stable Video Diffusion) and Diffusion Forcing as its generative backbone, extending them with action conditioning via AdaLN for interactivity. It draws conceptual inspiration from LLM techniques—retrieval-augmented generation, YaRN long-context extension, and Infini-attention—but critically departs from the assumption that these transfer directly to video, instead proposing VRAG with explicit global state conditioning and dedicated in-context training. The work positions itself against the interactive world model line (Genie, GameNGen, Oasis, SlowFast-VGen) by systematically isolating and addressing the two coupled failure modes—compounding errors and insufficient memory—that prior methods have not explicitly decoupled.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a useful decoupled evaluation framework separating compounding error from memory faithfulness, which prior work conflates, and demonstrates that naive LLM-style context extension (YaRN, RAG, Infini-attention) fails for video diffusion models due to their weak in-context learning—a finding that redirects the field toward explicit state conditioning and trained retrieval. However, the scope is limited to Minecraft at 640×360 with 20-frame windows, and the compounding-error irreducibility claim rests on a single autoregressive paradigm. Open questions include whether VRAG scales to higher resolutions and longer horizons, whether the global state conditioning generalizes to open-world settings without explicit coordinates, and whether more efficient memory mechanisms can reduce the computational overhead.
