## Problem

Commercial MEMS gyroscopes face a fundamental trade-off: increasing measurement range elevates Angle Random Walk and Bias Instability, while low-noise sensors accept only limited angular rates. Existing deep-learning approaches either require precisely time-synchronized noisy/clean ground-truth pairs (expensive to collect) or handle only denoising, leaving over-range reconstruction unsolved. No unified, self-supervised framework simultaneously addresses both tasks without additional hardware.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a raw 100 Hz IMU gyroscope signal stream, segmented into 2.56-second windows. A lightweight heuristic gate inspects each segment for saturation (3 consecutive clipped samples) or low-amplitude noise (runs of n samples below a threshold τ) and routes it to the Over-Range Reconstruction Expert, the Denoise Expert, both, or directly to the output. The final enhanced signal is produced by concatenating the gated expert outputs, replacing only the clipped peak samples and noise-flagged runs while leaving in-range, non-noisy samples untouched.

## Method

MoE-Gyro decouples two tasks onto a shared Masked Autoencoder backbone with task-specific decoders, avoiding the gradient-scale imbalance that plagues single multi-task networks. The Over-Range Reconstruction Expert inserts a Gaussian-Decay Attention block in the decoder (a learnable σ parameterizes a soft, differentiable window that concentrates capacity on clipped regions) and is trained with a correlation loss preserving local slopes and extremum amplitudes plus a physics-informed energy barrier loss derived from MEMS proof-mass dynamics. The Denoise Expert uses a dual-branch MAE with fixed 50% complementary cross-masks and full encoder-decoder weight sharing, trained with FFT-guided noise injection that synthesizes spectrally matched corruption targeting QN, ARW, and BI frequency bands. Both experts are trained entirely self-supervised—no paired ground-truth labels—making the framework deployable on unlabeled field data.

## Results

On ISEBench, MoE-Gyro achieves the best average rank (1) across all seven metrics, with a peak-reconstruction PSNR of 8.29 dB, P_MSE of 0.325, and correlation of 0.92 at a ±450°/s clipping threshold. For denoising, it attains 24.19 dB SNR and reduces Bias Instability by 98.4%, Angle Random Walk by 94.1%, and Quantization Noise by 98.0% relative to the raw signal. The framework extends the effective measurable range of a ±450°/s MEMS gyroscope to ±1500°/s, with the compressed model (1.85M params) running in 41 ms per 2.56 s segment on an RTX 4060.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MoE-Gyro outperforms all eight baselines on every ISEBench metric. On over-range reconstruction, the closest competitor is HEROS-GAN (PSNR 7.70 dB, P_MSE 0.354), which MoE-Gyro surpasses by 0.59 dB in PSNR and 8.5% in P_MSE; HEROS-GAN also degrades noticeably beyond 900°/s while MoE-Gyro maintains high-quality recovery up to 1500°/s. Among self-supervised methods, IMUDB (PSNR 6.59, SNR 17.76 dB) is the strongest denoising baseline but trails MoE-Gyro by 1.70 dB in PSNR and 6.43 dB in SNR. Classical filters (EMD, Savitzky-Golay) and the Matlab polynomial extrapolation achieve modest PSNR gains (4.35–6.03 dB) but leave P_MSE well above 0.5 and fail to reconstruct peaks effectively beyond 600°/s. Supervised deep baselines (CNN, LSTM-GRU, KNN) show intermediate performance, with LSTM-GRU reaching 19.23 dB SNR but only 5.95 dB PSNR, confirming the scale-imbalance problem when denoising and reconstruction are coupled in a single network. Cross-device zero-shot tests (iPhone 14 → Huawei P70, Xiaomi 14) show only 0.3–0.9 dB PSNR degradation, whereas HEROS-GAN drops more sharply, underscoring MoE-Gyro's generalization advantage.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Masked Autoencoder (MAE) backbone and adapts it to the IMU signal domain, but departs from prior work in a key architectural choice: rather than grafting multiple task heads onto a single network (as in supervised multi-task IMU models like HEROS-GAN or CNN/LSTM-GRU denoisers), it uses a Mixture-of-Experts design with a lightweight heuristic gate to decouple over-range reconstruction and denoising into separate specialists. The Gaussian-Decay Attention module extends the windowed/local attention idea from vision and language models into a learnable, differentiable Gaussian bias tailored for peak reconstruction, while the physics-informed energy loss introduces a MEMS-specific regularizer absent from prior IMU enhancement work. The FFT-guided noise augmentation borrows from speech enhancement literature but is specifically calibrated to MEMS gyroscope error spectra (QN, ARW, BI bands).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a long-standing hardware trade-off in MEMS gyroscopes (range vs. noise) purely through software, which is practically significant for cost-sensitive applications like consumer drones and robotics. The release of ISEBench and GyroPeak-100 provides the first unified evaluation framework for IMU signal enhancement, filling a gap the authors identify in the literature. However, limitations include the absence of error bars or statistical significance testing, evaluation on a single reconstruction dataset (iPhone 14), and a model size that remains challenging for embedded deployment despite the compressed variant. Open questions include whether the iterative self-purification training loop discussed in the appendix can further close the gap to ground-truth quality, and whether the heuristic gate can be replaced by a learned router without sacrificing the PSNR gains.
