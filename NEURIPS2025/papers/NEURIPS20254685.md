## Problem

Conditional image generative models produce increasingly realistic images but with diminished diversity, limiting their utility as synthetic training data. Existing guidance-based approaches define quality and diversity in isolation or without reference to a target real-data distribution, failing to address the distribution shift between synthetic and real samples. This paper tackles the problem of grounding both quality and diversity of generated images to a small set of real exemplars at inference time, without any model fine-tuning.

## Input / Output

The method takes a class label (or text prompt) conditioning the diffusion model and a small set of k real exemplar images (as few as 2, up to 32) that define the target distribution. The exemplar images are projected into DINOv2 (ViT-L) feature space once at the start of sampling, and the generated batch is projected at every 5th denoising step via DDIM approximation. The output is a batch of synthetic images whose joint distribution matches the real exemplar set in both fidelity (precision) and coverage (diversity), with no architectural modification to the base LDM.

## Method

Chamfer Guidance is a training-free, inference-time guidance that modifies the diffusion score function by subtracting the gradient of the Chamfer distance between the current denoised batch (via DDIM approximation) and the real exemplar set, both embedded in DINOv2 feature space. The Chamfer distance decomposes into two terms: a real-to-generated term that encourages coverage of the exemplar distribution (diversity) and a generated-to-real term that enforces fidelity to real samples (precision), jointly optimizing both without a separate diversity or quality hyperparameter. Guidance is applied every 5 denoising steps (G_freq=5) using the differentiable DDIM approximation, avoiding the need to complete all T reverse steps. A key practical advantage is that the method achieves state-of-the-art results using only the conditional model (ω=1.0, no CFG), eliminating the unconditional forward pass and yielding a 31% FLOPs reduction on LDM_3.5M compared to CFG-based approaches.

## Results

On ImageNet-1k with LDM$_{3.5\text{M}}$, Chamfer Guidance achieves 97.5% precision and 92.7% distributional coverage (F1 = 0.950) using 32 real exemplar images, and 96.4% precision / 86.4% coverage with only 2 exemplars, all without classifier-free guidance (ω=1.0), yielding a 31% FLOPs reduction at sampling time. On GeoDE, the method reaches an average F1 of 0.500 (DINOv2, k=4), a ~7% improvement over c-VSG. For downstream classification on 1.3M synthetic images, Chamfer Guidance boosts in-distribution accuracy by up to 15.8% over default LDM sampling and achieves up to 16% gains on ImageNet-Sketch (OOD).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Chamfer Guidance decisively outperforms all reference-free guidance baselines: on LDM$_{3.5\text{M}}$, it surpasses APG (F1 0.950 vs. 0.723), CADS (0.950 vs. 0.717), and the base CFG model (0.950 vs. 0.727) in F1, with coverage gains of ~30 percentage points. Against the closest reference-based method, c-VSG (ECCV 2024), Chamfer Guidance improves F1 from 0.435 to 0.500 on GeoDE and from 0.660 to 0.931 on ImageNet-1k (LDM$_{1.5}$), while also scaling favorably with more exemplars (unlike c-VSG, which does not improve with more images). Training-based baselines (vanilla fine-tuning and Chamfer/ReFL fine-tuning) are consistently outperformed: Chamfer fine-tuning reaches at most F1=0.741 on LDM$_{3.5\text{M}}$ versus 0.950 for Chamfer Guidance, and its FDD degrades (284.7 vs. 121.4), indicating worse distributional alignment. On GeoDE, Chamfer Guidance also beats Semantic Guidance (F1 0.500 vs. 0.412), FG CLIP Loss/Entropy (0.500 vs. 0.418/0.414), and Textual Inversion (0.500 vs. 0.300). The method's unique advantage is that it achieves these gains training-free, without CFG (saving 31% FLOPs on LDM$_{3.5\text{M}}$), and without requiring a memory bank or balancing hyperparameters as c-VSG does.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the classifier-guidance framework (Dhariwal & Nichol, 2021) by replacing the classifier log-likelihood gradient with a Chamfer distance gradient computed in DINOv2 feature space, enabling distribution-level matching between generated and real samples without any fine-tuning. It builds most directly on c-VSG (Askari Hemmat et al., ECCV 2024), which also uses real exemplar images at inference time to ground diversity, but departs from it by using a set-matching metric (Chamfer distance) rather than a Vendi Score, and by leveraging DDIM approximations (Song et al., 2021) to compute the guidance signal at intermediate denoising steps. A key conceptual departure from the broader guidance literature (APG, CADS, Particle Guidance) is that Chamfer Guidance explicitly targets the distributional gap to a reference set of real images rather than optimizing quality or diversity in isolation, and it demonstrates that the unconditional model (and thus CFG) can be entirely eliminated.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a training-free, inference-time method that scales gracefully with the number of real exemplars (unlike c-VSG), eliminates the 2× forward-pass cost of CFG (31% FLOPs reduction on LDM_3.5M), and yields up to 15–16% downstream classifier accuracy gains when training on synthetic data. A notable open question is the finding that the newer, higher-quality LDM_3.5M produces *less* useful synthetic data than LDM_1.5 without guidance, suggesting that generation quality and training-data utility are decoupled — a tension the field has not fully resolved. The method is currently limited to class-conditional generation; extending it to text-to-image via a retrieval-based exemplar pipeline is identified as future work, and the reliance on DINOv2 as the feature space introduces a dependency on a specific pretrained encoder whose coverage of semantic structure may not generalize to all domains.
