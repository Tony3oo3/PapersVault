## Problem

Diffusion-based 3D molecular generation models (EDM, GeoBFN, EquiFM) require ~1000 sampling steps to produce chemically valid molecules, making them impractically slow. The paper identifies the root cause as large second-order derivatives in the sampling ODE trajectory, which force small step sizes to keep truncation error low. No prior work had reformulated the diffusion process itself to minimize this curvature, instead relying on higher-order solvers that still face NFE-accuracy trade-offs.

## Input / Output

The model takes a Gaussian noise vector (scaled by a small constant σ ≈ 0.05) as input and produces a 3D point cloud of atomic coordinates constrained to zero-center-of-mass space, with atom types predicted post-hoc via the UniGEM framework. For conditional generation, property values (e.g., HOMO/LUMO energy, dipole moment) are additionally fed as conditioning signals. The output must satisfy SE(3) invariance, enforced through an EGNN backbone and zero-CoM coordinate constraint, and is evaluated on chemical validity (valency, RDKit parseability) and structural stability.

## Method

SLDM designs a diffusion schedule x_t = (1−t)·x_0 + σ·ε with a small constant σ, which provably yields a near-linear sampling trajectory (Theorem 3.1: the deviation from linearity is bounded by σ²/δ²(1−t)²). This near-zero second-order derivative means even a basic Euler step has minimal truncation error, enabling high-quality samples in 10–50 steps versus 1000 for baselines. Training uses the standard denoising objective (predict ε from x_t) with an EGNN backbone. Sampling combines an ODE denoising step with Langevin dynamics, augmented by a time-annealing temperature schedule (t^ν scaling) that allows early-stage exploration while converging to high-density regions later, improving stability without mode collapse. The low-noise, linearly-decreasing-mean schedule also aligns with molecular data's high noise sensitivity, preserving chemical information in intermediate states and distributing reconstruction effort evenly across timesteps.

## Results

On QM9 unconditional generation, SLDM with only 50 sampling steps achieves 93.37% molecule stability and 96.24% validity, surpassing the best baseline GeoBFN (90.87% mol stability) which requires 1000 steps — a ~20× speedup. At 1000 steps, SLDM reaches 95.42% molecule stability, matching the dataset's own 95.2% stability. On GEOM-Drugs, SLDM (T=50) achieves 89.03% atom stability and 99.57% validity, outperforming GeoBFN (T=1000) at 85.60% atom stability. The paper reports up to 100× sampling efficiency improvement over EDM and ~70× over GeoBFN/EquiFM while maintaining or exceeding their quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SLDM dominates all five baselines on the key molecule stability metric. On QM9, SLDM (T=50) achieves 93.37% molecule stability, exceeding GeoBFN (T=1000) by 2.5 points with 20× fewer steps, and EquiFM (T=200) by 5.1 points with 4× fewer steps. On GEOM-Drugs, SLDM (T=50) reaches 89.03% atom stability versus 85.60% for GeoBFN (T=1000) and 84.1% for EquiFM (T=200). In conditional generation (Table 2), SLDM achieves the lowest MAE across all six molecular properties (e.g., 348 meV for ε_LUMO vs. 516 for GeoBFN), representing a 20× acceleration over prior SOTA. The ablation in Table 3 confirms that within the same UniGEM framework, SLDM's advantage is most pronounced at small step counts (T=30: 93.02% mol stability vs. 78.75% for EDM and 81.05% for GeoBFN), validating that the straight-line trajectory specifically benefits low-NFE sampling.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the unified SDE/ODE formulation of diffusion processes established by Karras et al. (2022) and the reverse-time SDE framework from Xue et al. (2024b), but departs from the prevailing strategy of improving sampling solvers (e.g., EquiFM's higher-order ODE methods). Instead, SLDM reformulates the diffusion process schedule itself—choosing a linearly decreasing mean and constant small variance—to make the trajectory near-linear, thereby minimizing the second-order truncation error that governs first-order discretization. This positions the work as a process-design contribution rather than a solver-design one, complementing rather than competing with advanced numerical methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 100× sampling speedup (10–15 steps vs. 1000) makes high-quality 3D molecular generation practically feasible for iterative design workflows, and the straight-line schedule's natural alignment with molecular noise sensitivity (slow SNR decay, uniform reconstruction effort) suggests the approach is well-matched to point-cloud data more broadly. Open questions include principled selection of the noise constant σ, fully satisfying the σ(0)=0 boundary condition, and deriving optimal time-discretization schemes. The theoretical lens—truncation error as a function of process curvature—could inform schedule design in other diffusion applications beyond molecules.
