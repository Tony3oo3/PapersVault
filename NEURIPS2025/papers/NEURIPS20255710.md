## Problem

Existing UKG embedding methods neglect the extremely imbalanced distribution of triple confidences in real-world UKGs (e.g., NELL retains only triples with confidence > 0.9), causing learned embeddings to poorly fit lower-confidence samples and degrading UKG completion quality. The paper tackles how to reinforce embedding learning under this imbalance by exploiting both labeled data (existing triples) and unlabeled data (negative-sampled unseen triples) with reliable pseudo-confidence labels.

## Input / Output

The method consumes UKG quadruples (h, r, t, s) where s ∈ [0,1] is a scalar confidence, which is first transformed into a 101-dimensional confidence distribution via a Gaussian N(s, σ²) with σ=0.6. Unlabeled data is generated by negative sampling (replacing head or tail entity with a random entity). For confidence prediction, the output is a predicted 101-dim confidence distribution from which the expected confidence is extracted; for link prediction, the output is a sigmoid-normalized rank score per candidate tail entity. Entity and relation embeddings (128-dim on NL27k, 512-dim on CN15k) are the shared learned representations.

## Method

ssCDL comprises two identically-structured components trained via meta self-training: a CDL-based Relational Learner (CDL-RL) and a Pseudo Confidence Distribution Generator (PCDG). CDL-RL concatenates entity/relation embeddings, passes them through a two-layer FCN with Softmax to produce a predicted confidence distribution, and is optimized by a combined KL-divergence + MSE loss for confidence prediction plus a margin-based ranking loss for link prediction, balanced via uncertainty weights. PCDG generates pseudo confidence distributions for negative-sampled triples and is meta-optimized by minimizing CDL-RL's loss on labeled data after one inner gradient step on the pseudo-labeled data, following the meta auxiliary learning paradigm to avoid gradual drift. Training proceeds in three phases: (1) CDL-RL trains on labeled data only until embeddings stabilize, (2) PCDG begins meta-training while CDL-RL continues on labeled data, and (3) PCDG-generated pseudo labels (filtered by a threshold on maximum description degree) are added to CDL-RL's training set, with both components iteratively co-optimizing until convergence.

## Results

On NL27k, ssCDL achieves MSE 0.009 and MAE 0.042 for confidence prediction, and WMRR 0.727 and Hits@1 0.636 for link prediction, outperforming all baselines. On CN15k, ssCDL achieves MSE 0.034 and MAE 0.116 for confidence prediction, and WMRR 0.207 and Hits@1 0.133 for link prediction. Compared to the best baseline (PASSLEAF_RotatE), ssCDL reduces MSE by 52.6% and MAE by 17.6% on NL27k, and by 63.8% and 53.2% on CN15k for confidence prediction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ssCDL outperforms all five baseline families (UKGE, PASSLEAF, BEUrRE, UKGsE, UPGAT) across both datasets and both tasks. On confidence prediction, the largest gains are over PASSLEAF_RotatE (the strongest baseline): ssCDL reduces MSE from 0.019 to 0.009 on NL27k and from 0.094 to 0.034 on CN15k. On link prediction, ssCDL achieves Hits@1 of 0.636 on NL27k versus 0.580 for PASSLEAF_RotatE, and 0.133 on CN15k versus 0.086 for PASSLEAF_ComplEx. UKGsE (LSTM-based) and BEUrRE (box-embedding) are consistently the weakest baselines, with UKGsE achieving MSE as high as 0.122 on NL27k and Hits@1 as low as 0.002 on CN15k. The paper notes that link prediction improvements are less dramatic than confidence prediction improvements because CDL and meta self-training primarily optimize the confidence prediction objective, with link prediction benefiting only indirectly through improved embeddings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the UKG embedding line (UKGE, PASSLEAF, BEUrRE, UPGAT, UKGsE) by addressing a previously overlooked issue: the extreme imbalance in triple confidence distributions (e.g., NELL retains only triples with confidence > 0.9). The core technical departure is borrowing Label Distribution Learning (LDL) from facial age estimation to transform scalar confidences into Gaussian confidence distributions, thereby injecting supervision signals for rare or unseen confidence values. The second departure is a meta self-training loop (PCDG) that generates pseudo confidence labels for negative-sampled triples, explicitly designed to avoid the gradual drift problem of standard self-training. Together, these two components form a semi-supervised framework that prior UKG completion methods did not employ.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The imbalanced confidence distribution is a practical bottleneck in real-world UKGs, and ssCDL's 52–64% MSE reduction over the best baselines demonstrates that this bottleneck materially degrades completion quality. However, the evaluation is limited to two small datasets (NL27k, CN15k), no error bars or multiple seeds are reported, and the authors themselves note that CN15k's confidence scores lack meaningful reliability differentiation, weakening the benchmark's discriminative power. Open questions include whether the CDL + meta self-training recipe scales to larger UKGs, whether the Gaussian distribution assumption holds for all confidence semantics, and how the approach interacts with emerging LLM-based UKG completion and retrieval-augmented generation pipelines that the authors flag as future work.
