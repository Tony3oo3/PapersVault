## Problem

Approximate Gaussian inference methods (Laplace, HET, SNGP) output Gaussian distributions over logit space, but converting these to predictive probabilities requires Monte Carlo sampling through the softmax, which is computationally expensive, noisy, and scales linearly with the number of classes at inference time. The softmax-Gaussian integral has no closed form, limiting the practical use of these methods in large-scale classification. The paper asks whether replacing the softmax with an alternative activation can yield accurate, sampling-free predictives.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250643/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an input image processed by a deterministic backbone (ResNet-50, ResNet-28, or WideResNet-28-5) to produce a logit mean vector and the diagonal of the logit covariance — notably only O(C) logit quantities rather than the full O(C²) covariance matrix. It produces a closed-form predictive probability vector, Dirichlet distribution parameters for second-order uncertainty, and sample-free uncertainty estimates (expected entropy, mutual information) that enable aleatoric/epistemic decomposition without any Monte Carlo sampling.

## Method

The core contribution is replacing the softmax activation with an element-wise normCDF (Φ) or sigmoid (ρ) followed by normalization, which makes the per-class Gaussian integrals ∫φ(y)N(μ,σ²)(dy) solvable in closed form (exact for Φ, via the classical probit approximation for ρ). The predictive is then the normalized vector of these per-class integrals, reducing inference cost from O(S·C + C³) for MC sampling to O(C). For second-order distributions, the Gaussian pushforward is approximated by a Dirichlet via moment matching of first and second moments, enabling closed-form uncertainty decompositions. Training uses binary cross-entropy (BCE) instead of cross-entropy, leveraging the (0,1) range of Φ and ρ, which has been shown to improve training dynamics. A theoretical analysis (Theorem 3.1) bounds the KL divergence between true and approximate predictives, showing the probit approximation error is independent of the number of classes and the overall error decays as the variance of the sum of class-wise pushforwards vanishes.

## Results

On ImageNet, the closed-form predictive for Sigmoid Laplace achieves an ECE of 0.0101±0.0006, outperforming the MC1000 estimate (0.0153±0.0011) while requiring zero sampling. For NormCDF Laplace, the closed-form ECE is 0.0114±0.0007, again matching or slightly beating MC1000 (0.0118±0.0007). On the OOD detection task (ImageNet-C severity 1), the moment-matched Dirichlet mutual information estimator for Sigmoid Laplace achieves 0.6388±0.0005 AUROC, surpassing the MC expected entropy (0.6321±0.0001) by ~0.7 points. Across all three backbone methods (Laplace, HET, SNGP) and both datasets (ImageNet, CIFAR-10), closed-form predictives either outperform or match Softmax MC sampling on NLL, ECE, correctness prediction, accuracy, and OOD AUROC.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Monte Carlo sampling (the standard for softmax-based Gaussian inference), the paper's closed-form predictives match or exceed MC1000 quality at a fraction of the cost: Sigmoid Laplace closed-form ECE (0.0101) beats MC1000 (0.0153) by ~34%, and NormCDF Laplace closed-form (0.0114) slightly beats MC1000 (0.0118). Against the Laplace Bridge [19], Mean Field [32], and Shekhovtsov & Flach [48] approximations (all applied to softmax models), the paper's normCDF/sigmoid closed-form predictives consistently outperform on NLL, ECE, correctness prediction, and OOD detection across all three backbones (Laplace, HET, SNGP) on both ImageNet and CIFAR-10. On OOD AUROC (ImageNet-C severity 1), the Dirichlet-based estimators derived from the paper's moment matching outperform MC-based estimators in all three best-performing configurations (e.g., 0.6388 vs. 0.6321 for Sigmoid Laplace). The paper also shows that training with binary cross-entropy (enabled by the sigmoid/normCDF activations) yields additional gains over standard cross-entropy training, with the BCE-trained backbones alone already outperforming their CE-trained counterparts (Appendix K.6). Overall, the method wins or ties on every reported metric against all baselines, while eliminating the O(S·C) runtime and O(S·C) memory overhead of MC sampling at inference.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper generalises the classical probit approximation for the Gaussian–sigmoid integral (Spiegelhalter & Lauritzen 1990; MacKay 1992) from binary to multi-class classification, and pairs it with Minka's Dirichlet method of moments to obtain tractable second-order distributions. It departs from the dominant paradigm of approximate Gaussian inference methods (HET, Laplace, SNGP) that output logit-space Gaussians and require costly Monte Carlo sampling through the softmax to obtain predictives, by replacing the softmax with normCDF or sigmoid activations whose Gaussian integrals are closed-form. The use of binary cross-entropy (BCE) as the training objective, rather than cross-entropy, draws on recent observations of advantageous training dynamics for sigmoid-based classifiers (Wightman et al. 2021; Li et al. 2025), but is here the first to combine it with a principled normalisation that yields proper multi-class probabilistic models with closed-form predictives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: the O(C) closed-form predictives eliminate the O(S·C + C³) runtime and O(S·C + C²) memory overhead of MC sampling at inference, making approximate Gaussian inference methods viable for large-class-count settings (e.g., ImageNet-1k) and resource-constrained deployment. The first theoretical analysis (Theorem 3.1) provides a KL-divergence bound that is independent of the number of classes, lending confidence to the approximation. A key limitation is that the moment-matched Dirichlets do not encode inter-class correlations, and the paper identifies more expressive yet tractable second-order distributions on the simplex as an important direction for future work. The finding that the Dirichlet-based uncertainty estimators consistently outperform MC-derived ones for OOD detection suggests that the second-order distributional structure, not just the predictive mean, carries useful information.
