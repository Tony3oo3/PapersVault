## Problem

Existing tabular learning methods operate on single or pairwise tables and lack a unified paradigm for learning from multiple tables. Two critical gaps exist: (1) no metric quantifies inter-table complementarity—similarity-based measures conflate redundant tables (high similarity, zero complementarity) with genuinely informative ones, and (2) no architecture efficiently handles both intra-table heterogeneity (divergent attribute semantics) and inter-table heterogeneity (schema mismatch) to extract and fuse complementary information across tables.

## Input / Output

The method takes a target table T^c and K auxiliary tables T^{a(1)},...,T^{a(K)}, each containing categorical and numerical columns of varying widths. At the cell level, categorical values are encoded as 'value_attribute' strings and numerical values as scaled BERT attribute-name embeddings, both projected to 192 dimensions; rows are processed in mini-batches of 64. The primary output is a binary class label for each entity in the target table, while the CS metric (computed from pretrained column embeddings via cosine similarity and bipartite matching) produces an asymmetric scalar quantifying how much novel, relevant information each auxiliary table contributes to the target.

## Method

ATCA-Net operates in two stages. First, an adaptive table encoder—a 2D Transformer applying multi-head self-attention along both row and column axes—is pretrained via two self-supervised objectives: multi-table reconstruction (recovering masked cells across all tables) and cross-table correlation prediction (predicting whether vertically-partitioned rows from different tables correspond to the same entity), projecting heterogeneous schemas into a unified representation space. Second, a cross-table attention mechanism integrates complementary information: adaptive pooling with learnable sigmoid-gated weights projects each entity's row embedding into a shared d-dimensional space, then softmax-weighted attention over auxiliary table rows (keyed by the target entity's pooled embedding) extracts per-table complementary vectors, which are concatenated with the target embedding and passed through a stack of Transformer fusion layers for the final prediction. The CS metric formalizes complementarity as Info(T^k) · α·R_t(T^k,T^l)·(1 − S_t/N_l) / (1 + γ·S_t/N_l), where informativeness captures intra-table column diversity, relevance is the maximum inter-table column similarity, and similarity is the optimal bipartite column matching score—positively weighting relevance and informativeness while penalizing redundancy.

## Results

On the three real-world multi-table groups (10 datasets total), ATCA-Net(M) achieves the best AUC on 7 out of 10 datasets, with headline results of 0.918 on 1995-income, 0.913 on adult, and 0.918 on diabetes. The multi-table variant (M) consistently outperforms the single-table variant (S) by 1–10 AUC points (e.g., 0.796 vs. 0.617 on credit-g; 0.918 vs. 0.805 on diabetes), confirming the benefit of cross-table alignment pretraining and complementary information fusion. On synthetic datasets, ATCA-Net(M) performance tracks the complementarity strength (CS) metric: AUC rises from 0.657 (CS=0.73) to 0.663 (CS=0.87) on blastchar, and ACC rises from 80.73% (CS=2.11) to 83.40% (CS=3.61) on covertype.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ATCA-Net(M) outperforms all single-table baselines on the majority of datasets: it beats XGBoost on 7/10 datasets (losing on 1995-income 0.918 vs 0.925, banking churn 0.758 vs 0.817, and support 0.828 vs 0.851), TabPFN v2 on 9/10 (losing only on banking churn), FT-Transformer on 9/10 (tied on banking churn at 0.758), and SAINT on all 10 datasets. Against the multi-table baseline TransTab, ATCA-Net(M) wins on 9/10 datasets, with the sole loss on support (0.828 vs 0.841). The gap is most pronounced on credit-g (0.796 vs 0.707 for TransTab, 0.726 for XGBoost) and diabetes (0.918 vs 0.822 for TransTab, 0.832 for XGBoost), where complementary information across tables is most beneficial. On synthetic datasets, ATCA-Net(M) is the only multi-table method that consistently improves over its single-table counterpart as CS increases, whereas FT-Trans(M) and TransTab(M) show performance degradation relative to their single-table versions, suggesting that without explicit cross-table alignment, auxiliary tables introduce noise rather than useful complementarity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by formalizing 'multi-table learning' as a distinct paradigm, departing from prior work that handles only single tables (FT-Transformer, SAINT, TabPFN) or pairwise table relationships (TransTab's collaborative training, GNN-based relational learning). It repurposes column-level semantic embeddings from table discovery literature (SUDOWOODO, SANTOS) but reframes them through a complementarity lens rather than a pure similarity lens, introducing the CS metric that balances relevance, informativeness, and redundancy. The architecture draws on BERT-based cell encoding (TabBIe), 2D Transformer encoders, and cross-attention mechanisms from multimodal fusion, but combines them in a two-stage pipeline (alignment pretraining + complementary integration) specifically designed for the multi-table setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The CS metric and formal task definitions provide a theoretical scaffold for a field that previously lacked a unified language for multi-table complementarity, potentially guiding future work on when and how to combine tables. However, the authors acknowledge that CS depends on pretrained column representations, may not align with task-specific gains, and the empirical improvements are modest (typically 1-3 AUC points over single-table baselines). Open questions include developing task-oriented complementarity measures, scaling to larger table collections, and designing architectures that more effectively exploit the identified complementarity beyond the current attention-based fusion.
