## Problem

Vision-Language-Action models for autonomous driving falter in unstructured corner-case scenarios—rural tracks, construction zones, ambiguous signage, adverse weather—largely because existing driving datasets predominantly capture structured traffic situations. This data scarcity leaves a critical blind spot for training and evaluating robust AI drivers on the long-tail of real-world driving conditions that define the next frontier of go-anywhere autonomy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252557/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes multi-camera driving video clips standardized to 2 Hz with a 1.5 s past + 5 s future window (aligned with NAVSIM), alongside text Q&A prompts and past ego-vehicle trajectory states (displacement, velocity, acceleration). Outputs are structured as planning-oriented Q&A pairs spanning seven task types—from scene description and traffic signal detection to meta-action planning and 5-second end-to-end trajectory prediction—with special disambiguation tokens (e.g., <PLANNING>, <DYNAMIC_OBJECTS>) to prevent the model from confusing structurally similar outputs such as ego-vehicle plans versus other actors' motion intentions.

## Method

The core contribution is a scalable, VLM-centric data curation pipeline that distills ~80,000 clips from over 2 million source clips across 8 public datasets, organized under a novel four-category taxonomy of unstructured scenarios (unclear boundaries, temporary traffic rule changes, unconventional dynamic obstacles, challenging road conditions). Scene classification and structured information extraction use Qwen2.5-VL 72B with hierarchical Chain-of-Thought prompting, followed by temporal stability filtering via 15-second local-filter packs to suppress transient false positives. Multi-task annotations are generated through a combination of VLM queries and rule-based methods, then subjected to human verification (81% initial acceptance rate). For evaluation, base VLMs (Qwen2.5-VL 3B/7B, LLaMA-3.2-11B) are first fine-tuned on the Impromptu VLA training split and then further fine-tuned on nuScenes, demonstrating that pre-training on diverse unstructured scenarios transfers to improved closed-loop safety (NeuroNCAP score 0.75→2.06, collision rate 90%→65.1%) and near state-of-the-art open-loop trajectory prediction (0.30 m avg. L2 on nuScenes).

## Results

On the closed-loop NeuroNCAP benchmark, the 3B model fine-tuned with Impromptu VLA + nuScenes achieves a NeuroNCAP score of 2.06/5.00 (vs. 0.75 for the nuScenes-only baseline) and reduces the average collision rate from 90.0% to 65.1%. On open-loop nuScenes trajectory prediction, the 3B/7B/11B models pre-trained on Impromptu VLA all reach an average L2 error of 0.30 m, approaching the specialized EMMA+ model (0.29 m) despite using a dataset ~40× smaller. On the Impromptu VLA validation set itself, the 3B Base+Impromptu model drops average trajectory L2 from 2.00 m (Base+nuScenes) to 0.69 m, with dynamic-object prediction accuracy jumping from 0.75 to 0.92.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On nuScenes open-loop trajectory prediction, the Impromptu VLA pre-trained models (0.30 m avg L2) outperform all closed-source API models (GPT-4o 1.07 m, Gemini-2.0-Flash 1.25 m) and open-source generalist VLMs (Qwen2-VL-7B 2.81 m, DeepSeek-VL2-28B 1.56 m) by a wide margin, and beat the nuScenes-only fine-tuned baseline (0.34 m) by 0.04 m. They narrowly trail the specialized EMMA+ model (0.29 m) by just 0.01 m, despite EMMA+ training on substantially larger proprietary Waymo data. On NeuroNCAP closed-loop, the 3B Base+Impromptu+nuScenes model (2.06 NNS, 65.1% collision) surpasses all listed prior methods including BridgeAD-B (1.60 NNS, 72.6% collision) and BridgeAD-S (1.52 NNS, 76.2% collision), representing a 2.7× improvement in NNS over the nuScenes-only baseline. On the Impromptu VLA validation set, the 3B Base+Impromptu model (0.69 m avg L2) substantially outperforms the 3B Base+nuScenes model (2.00 m), confirming that the dataset's trajectory diversity (wider distribution than nuScenes) enables better generalization to unstructured scenarios.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the VLA-for-autonomous-driving line (DriveVLM, EMMA, OmniDrive, Senna) by introducing a dataset specifically targeting unstructured 'corner case' scenarios that existing large-scale driving benchmarks (nuScenes, Waymo, Argoverse) underrepresent. Rather than proposing a new model architecture, it repurposes the Qwen2.5-VL VLM as a scalable annotation engine with Chain-of-Thought prompting to curate ~80K clips from 2M source clips across 8 public datasets, following a bottom-up taxonomy of four unstructured categories. The multi-task Q&A annotation format draws directly from Senna and DriveVLM, while the curation pipeline (VLM description → VLM classification → CoT structured extraction → human verification) is a novel combination applied to the driving domain.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dataset fills a genuine gap: most driving VLA training data emphasizes structured urban/highway scenarios, leaving models brittle on rural tracks, construction zones, and adverse conditions. The demonstrated NeuroNCAP improvement (0.75→2.06) and near-EMMA+ nuScenes L2 (0.30m vs 0.29m) with a 3B model on only 80K clips suggest that targeted unstructured data is a high-leverage training signal. Key limitations include reliance on a single VLM (Qwen2.5-VL) for annotation (introducing model-specific bias), single-run experiments without error bars, and the open question of whether the four-category taxonomy captures the full long-tail of unstructured driving. The Q&A validation suite as a diagnostic tool for perception/prediction/planning is a useful but preliminary contribution.
