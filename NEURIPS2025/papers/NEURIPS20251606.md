## Problem

Despite the critical role of 360 video in autonomous driving, robotics, and VR, the community lacks large-scale, densely annotated real-world 360 video datasets for segmentation and tracking. Existing benchmarks (360VOS: 290 sequences, PanoVOS: 150 videos, 360VOT: 120 videos) are too small and task-specific to support foundation-model-scale training, and the inherent spherical properties of ERP—severe polar distortion and horizontal content discontinuities—make manual annotation prohibitively expensive.

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes 360 video in equirectangular projection (ERP) format, first applying horizontal padding to mitigate left-right border discontinuities before dividing frames into overlapping patches for 2D segmentor inference. Intermediate inputs include class-agnostic entity masks from segmentors (SAM, CropFormer, E-SAM) and class-aware panoptic predictions (OneFormer, Mask2Former, OMG-Seg) spanning COCO, ADE20K, and Cityscapes label spaces, unified by GPT-4o-based semantic matching. The final output is dense frame-level instance segmentation masks with instance IDs, 198 semantic labels, tracking trajectories, and bounding boxes, stored as JSON alongside MP4 video files.

## Method

The A³360V pipeline operates in three phases. In the Initial Annotation Phase, a Semantic- and Distortion-aware Refinement (SDR) module aggregates patch-wise predictions from multiple 2D segmentors via IoU-based window stitching, resolves label conflicts through an LLM semantic label checker, and generates distortion-aware masks by feeding mask prompts into SAM2 with iterative spatial shifting (Mask Prompt Shifting) to select the most consistent mask across shift directions. In the Auto-Refine Annotation Phase, a coverage-guided strategy propagates annotations across frames; when coverage falls below a threshold, a Motion-Continuity Refinement (MCR) module classifies blank regions into three types (border discontinuity, existing object re-appearance, or new object) using LLM agents (Blank Area Checker, Object Retriever) and reprocesses them accordingly. The Manual Revise Phase employs an LLM-based Annotation Checker to generate natural-language modification suggestions that guide human annotators in targeted corrections of boundaries, label hallucinations, and temporal inconsistencies, reducing per-video annotation time from over 20 hours to approximately 2 hours.

## Results

On the Leader360V-S test set (500 videos, 198 categories), training SwinB-AOT-L on Leader360V improves J&F from 38.8 (untrained) to 58.3 (+19.5), while the 360-specific GoodSAM improves from 27.4 to 60.6 (+33.2) after training on the dataset. For the VOT task, SimTrack trained on Leader360V achieves Sdual=0.417 and Pdual=0.373, a gain of +12.6 and +11.7 over its untrained counterpart. SAM-based methods without 360-specific training (PerSAM J&F=18.7, SAM-PT J&F=45.1) confirm the significant domain gap between 2D and 360 video.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates whether training on Leader360V improves existing 2D and 360 models, rather than proposing a new architecture. For VOS, 2D models (XMem, AOTL, R50-AOT-L) achieve moderate J&F scores of 42.4–43.9 when applied directly to 360 video, but SwinB-AOT-L trained on Leader360V jumps to 58.3 J&F, surpassing all untrained 2D baselines by 14–19.5 points. The 360-specific GoodSAM shows the largest relative gain: from 27.4 (untrained) to 60.6 J&F (+33.2) after training on Leader360V, outperforming the best 2D baseline (R50-AOT-L at 43.9) by 16.7 points. For VOT, SimTrack trained on Leader360V reaches Sdual=0.417, beating all untrained 2D trackers (SiamX 0.217, AiATrack 0.286, ProContEXT 0.308, SimTrack-untrained 0.291), while the 360-specific SiamX (Sdual=0.282) underperforms even untrained 2D models. SAM-based methods (PerSAM J&F=18.7, SAM-PT J&F=45.1) confirm that off-the-shelf 2D foundation models struggle with 360 distortion, motivating the need for 360-specific training data like Leader360V.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of 360 video understanding benchmarks (360VOS, PanoVOS, 360VOT, QuadTrack) by scaling them from hundreds to over 10,000 videos with 198 object categories, addressing the stark gap with 2D video datasets like YouTube-VOS (3,400+ videos) and SA-V (50.9K videos). The A³360V annotation pipeline repurposes 2D segmentors (CropFormer, OneFormer, SAM2) and introduces LLM-based semantic verification (GPT-4o) in a way that prior 360-specific annotation methods (360Rank, PanoVOS's semi-supervised pipeline) did not, specifically to handle ERP distortion and horizontal border discontinuities. Conceptually, it bridges the automatic annotation paradigm from autonomous driving (OpenAnnotate2, ALGPT) with the geometric challenges unique to panoramic video, creating a unified framework where LLMs resolve semantic ambiguity and SAM2 handles distortion-aware mask propagation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Leader360V fills a critical scale gap that has impeded foundation-model development for 360 video: prior datasets (150–290 sequences) are orders of magnitude smaller than what modern segmentation and tracking models require. The A³360V pipeline's demonstrated 10× reduction in annotation time (20h → 2h per video) makes large-scale 360 video annotation practically feasible, potentially enabling community-wide dataset growth. Open questions remain: the dataset lacks motion-state annotations and full category coverage, and the finding that a purpose-built 360 tracker (SiamX) underperforms a general 2D tracker (SimTrack) on this benchmark suggests that existing 360-specific architectures may not yet exploit the full potential of large-scale training data. Future work on 360-native foundation models will likely depend on resources like this.
