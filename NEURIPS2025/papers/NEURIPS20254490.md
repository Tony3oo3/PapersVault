## Problem

CLIP-based vision-language models learn spurious object-context co-occurrence shortcuts during contrastive training, causing zero-shot recognition to fail when test-time scenes diverge from familiar training pairings. Existing debiasing strategies rely on retraining, generative models, complex augmentation pipelines, or handcrafted prompts, none of which offer a lightweight, inference-only, causally principled solution that preserves beneficial object-context interactions while suppressing hallucinated background scores.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single RGB image and standard CLIP class text prompts, plus a pool of scene context embeddings drawn from one of three sources: external scene datasets (e.g., Places-365), counterfactual background embeddings computed from batch neighbors, or text-derived virtual scene descriptions. It outputs a class label prediction computed from a weighted combination of the original image's Total Direct Effect score and the average TDE score over M synthesized counterfactual embeddings. All computation occurs at the representation level within the frozen ViT encoder's residual stream, requiring no pixel-level manipulation, no retraining, and no prompt engineering.

## Method

The framework decomposes the ViT's residual stream into per-token semantic effects via a token-level direct-effect decomposition (Eq. 9), then estimates object-only C(x) and background-only C(z) counterfactual embeddings by weighting each token's semantic contribution with sigmoid-based foreground/background probabilities (Eq. 11–13). A Total Direct Effect (TDE) score subtracts the background-only activation from the full CLIP score in logit space (Eq. 15), explicitly removing the hallucination channel Z→Y while preserving the beneficial interaction path (X,Z)→R→Y. For intervention, the method synthesizes counterfactual embeddings by linearly recombining C(x) with diverse alternative context embeddings selected via a diversity-aware filter sampler (Eq. 17), averages TDE scores across M counterfactuals (Eq. 18), and blends with the original TDE via a weighting parameter λ (Eq. 19). The entire pipeline is inference-only, requiring no retraining, no external generative modules, and no handcrafted prompts, and is grounded in a Hoeffding/ANOVA decomposition of CLIP embeddings that formally links InfoNCE gradient dynamics to PMI-driven interaction amplification.

## Results

On Waterbirds, the proposed method (External variant) achieves 79.96% average and 79.16% worst-group accuracy on ViT-B/32, improving worst-group accuracy by +44.6% over vanilla CLIP (34.55%) and average accuracy by +15.1%. Across all four CLIP backbones, the method sets a new zero-shot state of the art, with worst-group gains averaging +32.3% and average accuracy gains averaging +14.3% over CLIP. On COCO-GB v1 (ViT-B/16), the Virtual variant reduces the gender accuracy gap from 6.20% (CLIP) to 0.80% while achieving the highest average accuracy of 91.40%. On UrbanCars (ViT-B/16), the Virtual variant reaches 71.87% overall accuracy, outperforming all baselines by at least 6.5%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against four baselines: CLIP (the base model), TBD (text-based decomposition, ICLR'24), PC/PC+ (PerceptionCLIP prompt-based context conditioning, ICLR'24), and B2T (keyword-explanation bias mitigation, CVPR'24). The proposed method decisively outperforms all baselines on Waterbirds across every backbone and both metrics; for example, on ViT-B/32 it achieves 79.96% average accuracy vs. 71.73% (PC) and 68.59% (B2T), and 79.16% worst-group vs. 57.25% (PC) and 56.23% (B2T). TBD is the strongest single baseline on worst-group for ViT-L/14 (75.25%) but still trails the proposed Internal variant by 10.4 points. PC+ trades average accuracy for worst-group gains (e.g., 65.27% avg vs. 71.73% for PC on ViT-B/32), confirming the paper's observation that limited fine-grained prompts introduce textual bias. On COCO-GB, the Virtual variant achieves the smallest gender gap (0.80%) compared to B2T (1.40%) and CLIP (6.20%), while on UrbanCars the Virtual variant leads all baselines by 6.5+ points in overall accuracy, with TBD actually degrading below CLIP (53.87% vs. 63.07%) due to architectural disruption.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the text-based decomposition (TBD) framework of Gandelsman et al. (2024), which showed that CLIP's image embeddings can be decomposed into text-aligned components, and extends it into a causal-inference setting by formalizing object-context separation via Hoeffding (ANOVA) decomposition in the representation space. It departs from prompt-based debiasing methods (PC, PC+, B2T) that operate on the textual modality and from generative/augmentation-based approaches (ALIA, concept graphs) that require external models, instead performing counterfactual intervention and Total Direct Effect estimation entirely within CLIP's frozen embedding space at inference time. The do-operator and TDE formalism draws on Pearl's causal inference framework but is instantiated in a novel way: counterfactual embeddings are synthesized by linearly recombining token-level semantic effects with diverse context embeddings, avoiding any generative model or retraining.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practically deployable, inference-only causal debiasing framework that requires no retraining, no prompt engineering, and no generative models, with negligible computational overhead (+0.002–0.004 GFLOPs per image), making it attractive for real-world CLIP deployment where worst-group reliability is critical. The substantial worst-group accuracy gains (up to +44.6%) on established context-sensitive benchmarks demonstrate that representation-level counterfactual reasoning can effectively break object-context entanglement without the architectural or data requirements of prior causal methods. Open questions include how the method generalizes to multi-object scenes, non-CLIP vision-language models, and whether the sigmoid-based token weighting (Eq. 12–13) remains reliable when the target object is small or occluded. The assumption of a single dominant object per image and the reliance on CLIP's shared semantic space for virtual scene construction are limitations that may constrain applicability to more complex visual reasoning tasks.
