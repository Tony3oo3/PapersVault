## Problem

Multimodal LLMs struggle with real-time streaming video understanding due to two coupled limitations: (1) storage constraints that force aggressive compression of historical visual features, and (2) insufficient fine-grained spatiotemporal reasoning at the moment of query. Existing compression strategies either discard incoming frames (losing detail) or merge stored frames by similarity alone (causing spatiotemporal irregularities and loss of event-level structure), making them inadequate for tasks requiring both persistent memory and real-time perception.

## Input / Output

The model consumes streaming video frames at 1 FPS under a strict causal constraint (only frames up to the current timestamp are visible) alongside a text query, producing a free-form text answer. Visual tokens are capped at a configurable budget (default 8192, tested down to 1024), with 729 tokens allocated to high-resolution real-time perception of the current frame and 18×128 tokens for short-term spatiotemporal memory. The architecture is built on SigLIP-so400M as the visual encoder and Qwen2-7B as the LLM backbone.

## Method

StreamForest combines two complementary modules: a Fine-grained Spatiotemporal Window (FSTW) that retains second-level short-term features by sampling the current frame at high resolution and compressing older frames along the spatial dimension, and a Persistent Event Memory Forest (PEMF) that organizes long-term memory as a hierarchical tree of event nodes. When the PEMF token budget is exceeded, adjacent event nodes are merged using a composite penalty that balances three factors: a similarity penalty computed via bipartite graph matching (ToMe-style), a merge-count penalty that regularizes against repeated merging to prevent detail degradation, and a temporal-distance penalty that preserves recent events with higher fidelity. Event boundaries are identified by local minima in inter-frame similarity, ensuring each node captures a coherent spatiotemporal transition. The model is instruction-tuned on OnlineIT (400K+ general streaming instances plus 89K driving-specific instances) via a five-stage curriculum that progressively introduces streaming video fine-tuning and domain-specific driving data.

## Results

StreamForest achieves state-of-the-art performance across all four streaming video benchmarks: 77.3% on StreamingBench, 60.5% on OVBench, 55.6% on OVO-Bench, and 59.9% on the newly introduced ODV-Bench (71.2% after driving-domain fine-tuning). On offline benchmarks, it reaches 61.4% on VideoMME, 70.0% on MLVU, 70.2% on MVBench, and 73.1% on PerceptionTest, matching or surpassing leading offline video MLLMs. Under extreme visual token compression (1024 tokens vs. the default 8192), the model retains 96.8% of its average accuracy across eight benchmarks, demonstrating strong robustness to resource constraints.

## Baselines & Comparisons

StreamForest decisively outperforms all online video MLLM baselines on every streaming benchmark: it leads by +5.7% over Dispider (67.6%) on StreamingBench, +5.6% over VideoChat-Online (54.9%) on OVBench, and +2.5% over LLaVA-Video (53.1%) on OVO-Bench. Against the strongest offline MLLMs, StreamForest also wins on streaming tasks—beating LLaVA-OneVision by +6.2% on StreamingBench and +11.0% on OVBench—while remaining competitive on offline benchmarks, achieving 70.0% on MLVU (vs. 70.8% for LLaVA-Video, a marginal −0.8% loss) and 73.1% on PerceptionTest (vs. 67.9% for LLaVA-Video, a +5.2% gain). On the new ODV-Bench, StreamForest scores 59.9% zero-shot and 71.2% after driving fine-tuning, surpassing the best offline baseline Qwen2.5-VL (55.6%) and the best online baseline VideoChat-Online (54.5%) by substantial margins. The ablation in Table 3 further confirms that the Persistent Event Memory Forest outperforms Similarity Merge (MovieChat) by +2.2% on OVO-Bench and +2.0% on MLVU, and outperforms the Pyramid Memory Bank (VideoChat-Online) by +1.7% on OVO-Bench and +1.8% on MLVU.

## Relation to Prior Work

StreamForest builds directly on ToMe's bipartite graph matching for token-level similarity computation and merging, but elevates the unit of compression from individual frames to event-level nodes organized in a tree structure. It departs from prior streaming video MLLMs (VideoLLM-Online, Flash-VStream, Dispider, VideoChat-Online) that relied on frame-level similarity merging, FIFO eviction, or fixed-capacity pyramid memory banks, by introducing a three-penalty adaptive consolidation (similarity, merge count, temporal distance) that prevents over-merging and preserves event boundaries. The Fine-grained Spatiotemporal Window addresses a gap in prior work where aggressive compression sacrificed the fine-grained short-term context needed for real-time perception and future prediction tasks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work demonstrates that streaming video MLLMs can match or surpass offline models on long-video benchmarks while operating under strict causal constraints, challenging the assumption that full-video access is necessary for strong understanding. The 96.8% accuracy retention at 1024 visual tokens (vs. 8192 default) is a practically significant efficiency result for deployment on resource-constrained edge devices in autonomous driving and robotics. Open questions include whether the event-level abstraction generalizes beyond the tested 7B scale, how the approach handles multi-modal streams (audio, sensor data), and whether the penalty weights require per-domain tuning or can be learned end-to-end. The driving-specific fine-tuning gap (59.9% → 71.2%) also suggests the general streaming representation still lacks domain-specific inductive biases for high-stakes prediction.
