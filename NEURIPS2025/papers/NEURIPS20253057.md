## Problem

Fourier Neural Operators suffer from exponential parameter growth with domain dimensionality due to their dense spectral multipliers, and they provide no native uncertainty quantification. Existing UQ approaches for neural operators are post hoc (MC Dropout, Laplace on final layer) and ignore the geometric and spectral inductive biases inherent to the operator structure, yielding poorly calibrated and spatially uncorrelated uncertainty estimates.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253057/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Inputs are PDE initial/boundary conditions (scalar permeability at 241×241, vorticity sequences at 64×64×10) or signed distance functions sampled on 64³ uniform grids (padded to 72³), optionally augmented with inlet velocity. Outputs are solution fields on the same regular grid or on irregular mesh vertices (3,586 for ShapeNet cars, ~150,000 for Ahmed bodies). In the Bayesian variant, the model additionally produces 100 posterior predictive samples per test instance, yielding spatially correlated uncertainty maps rather than pointwise independent noise.

## Method

DINOZAUR replaces the dense FNO spectral multiplier R_γ(k) with a heat-kernel diffusion multiplier exp(−λ_k τ), where λ_k = −4π²‖k‖² is precomputed and τ is a single learnable scalar per channel per block, making the parameter count independent of spatial dimensionality and truncation modes. To counteract the radial symmetry of the heat kernel, the architecture appends spectral gradient features (computed via i·2πk in Fourier space, normalized with tanh) to the integral transform output and mixes both through a linear projection. For uncertainty quantification, Gaussian priors are placed on ln(τ) and a full-rank variational posterior is learned per block via ELBO maximization; at inference, sampling τ from the posterior and propagating through the network yields spatially correlated predictive distributions. A universal approximation theorem is proven for the resulting architecture, and the method is trained with standard AdamW/One-Cycle for the deterministic variant or joint ELBO optimization for the Bayesian variant.

## Results

On Darcy flow, DINOZAUR achieves RL2 = 0.75×10⁻² with only 28K parameters, outperforming FNO (0.90×10⁻², 361K params) and TFNO (8.04×10⁻², 17.7K params). On Navier-Stokes, DINOZAUR reaches RL2 = 15.68×10⁻² with 110K params versus FNO's 15.90×10⁻² with 12.7M params. On the irregular-domain benchmarks, DINOZAUR achieves RL2 = 7.04×10⁻² on ShapeNet car and 7.99×10⁻² on Ahmed bodies, beating GINO (8.03 and 8.57×10⁻²) by a wide margin while using ~275× fewer parameters. In the Bayesian UQ setting, DINOZAUR^B achieves NLL = 2.767, MA = 0.168, and IS = 11.667 on ShapeNet car, outperforming all MC Dropout and Laplace approximation baselines on every UQ metric.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against FNO [9], DINOZAUR wins on both uniform benchmarks: RL2 of 0.75 vs 0.90 on Darcy flow and 15.68 vs 15.90 on Navier-Stokes, while using 13× fewer parameters on Darcy and 115× fewer on NS (110K vs 12.7M). Against GINO [10], DINOZAUR achieves substantially lower error on both irregular-domain benchmarks (7.04 vs 8.03 on ShapeNet car; 7.99 vs 8.57 on Ahmed bodies) with roughly 275× fewer parameters (180K vs 49.6M). Against TFNO/TGINO [12] (matched to DINOZAUR's parameter count via rank r=10⁻⁴), DINOZAUR wins on all four benchmarks, with the largest gap on Darcy flow (0.75 vs 8.04 RL2) and Navier-Stokes (15.68 vs 21.48), and smaller but consistent margins on the 3D irregular domains (7.04 vs 7.27; 7.99 vs 8.19). In the UQ comparison (Table 2), DINOZAUR^B outperforms both MC Dropout and Laplace approximation variants of GINO and TGINO on NLL, miscalibration area, and interval score, with the largest improvement in MA (0.168 vs 0.266–0.288 for baselines).

## Relation to Prior Work

DINOZAUR replaces the dense spectral multiplier tensor in Fourier Neural Operators (FNO) with a heat-kernel-inspired diffusion multiplier, drawing directly on DiffusionNet's discretization-agnostic kernel parametrization. While prior efforts to reduce FNO overparameterization (FFT factorization, low-rank Tucker decomposition) still scale with spatial dimensionality, DINOZAUR's multiplier depends only on a single learnable diffusion time per channel, making it dimensionality-independent. The Bayesian formulation is a departure from existing neural operator UQ methods (post-hoc Laplace, MC Dropout, conformal prediction) by placing priors directly on the integral transform parameters and recovering the posterior via variational inference, yielding spatially correlated uncertainty natively.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work addresses two practical bottlenecks for deploying neural operators in scientific and engineering settings: exponential parameter growth with domain dimensionality and the absence of native uncertainty quantification. The dimensionality-independent multiplier enables scalable PDE surrogates in 3D and beyond, while the Bayesian formulation provides calibrated, spatially correlated uncertainty without expensive post-hoc steps like Hessian computation. Key limitations include the radial symmetry of the heat kernel (requiring gradient features that roughly double memory), sensitivity of the Bayesian version to prior hyperparameters, and the restriction to periodic domains in the core FNO blocks. Open questions include whether anisotropic diffusion kernels or other parsimonious parametrizations can eliminate the gradient-feature overhead, and whether the dimensionality independence enables meaningful transfer learning across spatial dimensions.
