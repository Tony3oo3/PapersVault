## Problem

Existing LLM toxicity mitigation is predominantly reactive—models are first trained on toxic data and then aligned via RLHF or DPO—or relies on coarse keyword/classifier-based filtering that misses subtle, context-dependent toxic patterns. The paper asks how to proactively identify which training tokens drive toxic behavior and suppress their influence during training, without requiring human-annotated preference data. It further shows that standard influence functions, designed for data removal, are ineffective at this task, motivating a new attribution-and-suppression pipeline.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a training text corpus (a 1B-token OpenWebText subset in experiments), a set of ~10k toxic and ~10k non-toxic prompt-completion pairs (filtered from RealToxicityPrompts using the Detoxify classifier at thresholds 0.75/0.25), and a proxy language model that can be up to 7.5× smaller than the target (e.g., Pythia-160M proxying Llama-3.2-1B). It produces two artifacts: (1) a set of toxic token indices T_i per training document (capped at 2% of total tokens, each expanded by a 1-token context window), and (2) a detoxified model trained with a modified next-token prediction loss. The toxic token indices are reusable across models and can be computed incrementally on newly appended data.

## Method

IF-GUIDE computes a differential influence score per training token by taking the difference between the mean toxic-query gradient and the mean safe-query gradient, projected through an EK-FAC-approximated inverse Hessian onto each token's individual loss gradient. A document-based importance ranking (harmonic mean of the count and sum of tokens exceeding the 99th-percentile threshold) determines selection order, and a 1-token context window captures surrounding toxic phrasing. Rather than filtering identified tokens, the method introduces a penalty-based training objective that adds λ·log Pr(x_ij | x_i,<j) for toxic tokens to the standard cross-entropy loss (λ=1), actively discouraging the model from assigning high probability to those tokens while preserving fluency on benign tokens. Two speed-up techniques—half-precision batched gradient computation and a small proxy model—reduce influence-score computation by up to 19×, making the pipeline practical for billion-parameter targets.

## Results

On RealToxicityPrompts (RTP), IF-GUIDE reduces Expected Maximum Toxicity (EMT) by 4.2–5.5× and Toxicity Probability (TP) by 6.8–10.4× across four model sizes (Pythia-160M/410M/1B, Llama-3.2-1B), compared to the undefended base models. Fluency is largely preserved: PPL on OpenWebText increases by only 0.93–5.18 and LAMBADA accuracy drops by 0.01–0.06. When combined with RAD, IF-GUIDE achieves the strongest reductions of 14.3–18.0× (EMT) and 21.2–32.9× (TP) on the full prompt set. In fine-tuning settings, IF-GUIDE reduces EMT by 3.0–5.7× and TP by 3.9–10.8× using as few as ~400M additional tokens (10% of pre-training compute).

## Baselines & Comparisons

IF-GUIDE substantially outperforms both filtering baselines: on Pythia-160M, it achieves EMT 0.101 vs. 0.413 (Word Filtering) and 0.339 (Toxicity Filtering), a 4.1× and 3.4× improvement respectively. Against DPO, IF-GUIDE is consistently stronger across all four models (e.g., Pythia-1B: EMT 0.118 vs. 0.437, a 3.7× gap), while DPO shows limited gains over the base model (≤1.6× EMT reduction). RAD is the closest competitor, achieving comparable EMT on non-toxic prompts (e.g., Pythia-160M: 0.034 vs. 0.067), but IF-GUIDE is notably stronger on toxic prompts (EMT 0.136 vs. 0.202, TP 0.085 vs. 0.176) and introduces no inference-time overhead. The combination IF-GUIDE+RAD dominates all other configurations, reaching EMT 0.031 and TP 0.017 on Pythia-160M—roughly 18× and 33× lower than the undefended model—demonstrating that the two methods are complementary and orthogonal.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts influence functions—originally developed by Koh and Liang for data attribution and extended to LLMs by Grosse et al. via EK-FAC approximation—for a new purpose: identifying and suppressing toxic training tokens during training. It departs from the reactive alignment paradigm (RLHF, DPO) and coarse-grained data filtering by introducing a proactive, token-level differential attribution score combined with a penalty-based training objective that discourages the model from learning identified toxic tokens. The paper is, to its authors' knowledge, the first to couple influence-based data attribution with targeted gradient suppression during training, positioning itself as orthogonal to and complementary with existing alignment methods like DPO and RAD.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

IF-GUIDE offers a human-preference-free, computationally efficient path to reducing both explicit and implicit toxicity (up to 10× vs. uncensored models), and its mechanistic analysis reveals that aligned models (DPO) only patch the final layers while IF-GUIDE prevents toxic representations from forming across all layers—a distinction with implications for robustness under adversarial pressure. However, the evaluation is limited to academic scale (1B-token OpenWebText subset, models up to 12B parameters), and the authors acknowledge the method is not yet practical for commercial-scale training with hundreds of billions of parameters on trillion-token corpora. Open questions include whether the proxy-model approach generalizes to much larger architectures, how to handle the occasional high-influence outliers that appear irrelevant, and whether the approach extends to other trustworthiness dimensions beyond toxicity.
