## Problem

Existing LLM-generated text detection methods analyze static textual or stylistic features of full documents, overlooking the fine-grained interactional dynamics that are essential for distinguishing human-human from human-chatbot dialogues. This paper identifies that H-H interactions exhibit statistically significant bidirectional sentiment influence while H-C interactions display a clear asymmetric pattern, and asks whether this causal structure can be exploited for reliable chatbot detection in conversational settings.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252701/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a multi-turn dialogue consisting of alternating user and agent utterances (minimum ~20 turns to support Granger Causality analysis) and produces a binary classification indicating whether the agent is human or an LLM. Beyond the raw dialogue text, the pipeline extracts per-turn numerical time series (sentiment scores, utterance lengths, topic embedding components) for each participant, and the H-C training corpus is semi-synthetic: user turns are preserved from real dialogues while agent turns are regenerated by a target LLM conditioned on the dialogue history, ensuring controlled comparability.

## Method

ChatbotID applies the Granger Causality Test to per-turn feature time series (e.g., sentiment, utterance length) to compute p-values quantifying whether one participant's past features significantly improve prediction of the other's future features, concatenating all test outcomes into a GCT feature vector V_GCT. This vector is fused with contextual embeddings from a fine-tuned LLM backbone under a structured multi-task loss: a primary cross-entropy classification loss, an auxiliary semantic deficiency attribution loss (predicting four pragmatic failure types via LLM-generated labels), and a GCT significance loss (predicting binary indicators of whether each causal link is statistically significant at α=0.05). The key insight is that the asymmetric causal structure—where user sentiment Granger-causes LLM sentiment but not vice versa—provides a statistically grounded, dialogue-specific signal that static text-feature detectors cannot capture, yielding up to 15.92% accuracy gains over state-of-the-art baselines across four datasets and multiple LLM backbones.

## Results

ChatbotID achieves the highest accuracy across all four dialogue datasets: 82.77% on DailyDialog, 82.23% on PersonaChat, 87.38% on MultiWOZ, and 85.34% on Taskmaster-1 (with Qwen-2 backbone). On MultiWOZ, it reaches an AUROC of 90.16%, and on Taskmaster-1 an F1-score of 0.828. The method outperforms the strongest specialized detector (RoBERTa-MPU) by 4.14% accuracy on DailyDialog and 4.14% on MultiWOZ, and surpasses all zero-shot LLM baselines (GPT-4, Deepseek-R1, Gemma, Qwen-2) by 15–25 percentage points in accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ChatbotID outperforms all 14 baselines on every dataset. Among specialized detectors, the closest competitor is RoBERTa-MPU (ACL'24), which achieves 78.62% ACC on DailyDialog and 83.24% on MultiWOZ, trailing ChatbotID by 4.14 and 4.14 points respectively; OUTFOX (AAAI'24) is second-closest at 78.80% on DailyDialog. T5-Sentinel (EMNLP'24) is competitive on MultiWOZ (84.52% ACC) but falls well behind on DailyDialog (76.68%) and PersonaChat (72.84%). Perturbation-based methods (DetectGPT, Fast-DetectGPT, LLMDet) and contrastive-learning methods (COCO, DeTeCtive) are significantly outperformed, with gaps of 10–25 percentage points. All zero-shot LLM baselines (GPT-4, LLaMA-7B/13B, Gemma, Qwen-2, Deepseek-R1) score between 57–68% accuracy, far below ChatbotID's 82–87%, confirming that interaction-dynamics features captured by GCT provide a substantial signal beyond what general-purpose LLMs can extract from raw dialogue text alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the Granger Causality Test, an econometric tool for identifying directional predictive relationships in time series [22], and applies it to conversational dynamics for the first time in the context of LLM-generated text detection. It departs from the dominant line of AI-generated text detection (DetectGPT, COCO, LLMDet, OUTFOX, etc.), which operates on static document-level features such as probability curvature, perplexity, or stylistic anomalies, by instead modeling the temporal interaction between dialogue participants. The theoretical grounding in Communication Accommodation Theory from sociolinguistics provides the conceptual bridge: the paper formalizes the intuition that human partners mutually influence each other's sentiment while LLMs exhibit a one-directional, user-driven response pattern, and quantifies this via GCT p-values fed into a multi-task LLM fine-tuning framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that H-C dialogues exhibit a statistically significant asymmetry in sentiment causality (user→LLM p≈0.03 vs. LLM→user p≈0.45) offers a theoretically grounded, interpretable signal that is complementary to purely learned representations, and the O(1) inference complexity makes it practical for real-time deployment where perturbation-based detectors are prohibitively expensive. Key limitations include restriction to two-party dialogues (multi-party GCT scales quadratically), reliance on semi-synthetic H-C data for training, and noise-free experimental settings that may not reflect real-world messiness. Open questions include whether the asymmetric-influence signal persists under adversarial prompting or persona-conditioned LLMs designed to mimic mutual accommodation, and whether the framework extends to multi-party group conversations.
