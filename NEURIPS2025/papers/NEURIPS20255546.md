## Problem

TEE-Shielded DNN Partition (TSDP) protects on-device fine-tuned LMs by placing LoRA adapters inside a TEE, but shielding all adapters causes host-to-TEE data transfer to dominate inference latency (over 60% for LLaMA-7B). The open problem is how to selectively shield only the most privacy-sensitive adapters to reduce communication while still achieving black-box-level defense against gray-box model stealing attacks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255546/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

AegisGuard is a fine-tuning and deployment framework that takes a pretrained base model (LLaMA-7B, OPT-2.7B, ViT-Base, ViT-Large), LoRA adapters, and a task-specific training dataset (CommonSenseQA for NLP; CIFAR-10/100, UTKFace, MNIST, GTSRB, SUN397 for vision), and produces a partitioned deployment where a subset of structurally compressed adapters runs inside an Intel SGX enclave while the remaining model executes on a co-located GPU. At inference time, the deployed system accepts text queries or RGB images and produces text predictions or class labels, with the TEE-GPU boundary determined by the learned sensitivity scores.

## Method

AegisGuard integrates two modules into the LoRA fine-tuning loop. First, RL-based Sensitivity Measurement (RSM) models layer selection as a contextual multi-armed bandit: at every t_rsm fine-tuning steps it injects Gaussian noise into candidate LoRA adapters, measures the resulting loss change as a sensitivity proxy, computes exponential rewards relative to the batch mean, and updates per-layer sensitivity scores via a sigmoid-gated learning rule; the highest-scoring layers are then sampled for additional training and ultimately selected for TEE shielding. Second, Shielded Adapter Compression (SAC) applies head-level structural pruning to only the RSM-selected sensitive adapters, using a first-order Taylor approximation of LoRA-Prune importance scores and a dynamic pruning ratio that grows linearly over training, thereby reducing both parameter count and intermediate feature-map dimensions that must cross the TEE-GPU boundary. The key insight is that adapter sensitivity to private data is highly non-uniform across layers, so shielding only the top-scoring subset (≈40% of layers) achieves the same surrogate-model accuracy (~39%) as full shielding while cutting TEE memory by 4× and end-to-end latency by 2–3×.

## Results

AegisGuard achieves 2.16× end-to-end latency speedup on ViT-Base (15.14 ms vs. 32.77 ms for Shield-LoRA) and 3.33× on LLaMA-7B (6.42 s vs. 21.38 s), while reducing TEE memory by ~4× (54.96 MB vs. 224.4 MB for LLaMA-7B). Against model-stealing attacks, AegisGuard attains average surrogate accuracy of 27.49% (ViT-Base) and 50.68% (LLaMA-7B), matching the fully-shielded Shield-LoRA baseline (27.59% and 50.39%) and thus providing black-box-level protection. Downstream task accuracy is preserved with only ~0.12% average drop relative to standard LoRA fine-tuning (e.g., 84.04% vs. 84.01% on ViT-Base; 62.06% vs. 61.85% on LLaMA-7B).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Phantom (USENIX Security 2025), AegisGuard wins on both efficiency and security: it is 1.58× faster on ViT-Base (15.14 ms vs. 23.95 ms) and 2.09× faster on LLaMA-7B (6.42 s vs. 13.43 s), while also reducing MS attack accuracy from 38.15% to 27.49% (ViT-Base) and from 62.42% to 50.68% (LLaMA-7B), because Phantom's redundant-adapter obfuscation is less effective for LoRA-fine-tuned LMs. Against TEESlice (IEEE S&P 2024), AegisGuard is 1.69× faster on ViT-Base (15.14 ms vs. 25.68 ms) and 1.83× faster on LLaMA-7B (6.42 s vs. 11.78 s), primarily because TEESlice shields nine adapters versus AegisGuard's four, incurring more TEE computation and data transfer. On security, AegisGuard ties TEESlice (27.49% vs. 27.97% on ViT-Base; 50.68% vs. 50.52% on LLaMA-7B), both achieving black-box-level protection. Against the Shield-LoRA upper bound (all adapters in TEE), AegisGuard matches security (within 0.3% attack accuracy) while delivering the headline 2–3× latency reduction and 4× TEE memory savings. The speedup advantage grows with model size, reaching 3.33× on LLaMA-7B versus 2.16× on ViT-Base, reflecting the larger communication bottleneck in deeper LMs.

## Relation to Prior Work

AegisGuard extends the TEE-Shielded DNN Partition (TSDP) line of work (TEESlice, Phantom, ShadowNet, GroupCover) by introducing an active, RL-driven layer selection mechanism rather than the passive or obfuscation-based strategies used by prior methods. It builds directly on LoRA as the PEFT substrate and on LoRA-Prune's gradient-based importance estimation for its structural compression module. The key conceptual departure is framing adapter sensitivity measurement as a contextual multi-armed bandit problem with Gaussian noise perturbation as the reward signal, which is a novel application of RL to the model-protection setting rather than to architecture search or pruning alone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete and growing bottleneck in on-device LM deployment: TEE-GPU communication latency can dominate end-to-end inference time (over 60% in their LLaMA-7B measurements). By showing that only ~40% of adapters need shielding to match black-box-level security, AegisGuard offers a practical path to making TEE-based model protection viable for larger models. Limitations include the assumption of a secure TEE, the honest-but-curious threat model (no input reconstruction or training-data inversion defense), and evaluation restricted to a handful of model sizes and tasks. Open questions include how sensitivity scores evolve with model scale, whether the RL-based measurement generalizes to non-LoRA PEFT methods, and how the approach performs under adaptive adversaries who can observe which layers are shielded.
