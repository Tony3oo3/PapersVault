## Problem

UAV object detection suffers from severe performance degradation due to confounding factors such as varying lighting, weather, occlusion, and viewpoint that create intra-class feature inconsistencies, particularly for small objects occupying limited pixels. Existing methods rely solely on single visual features and lack a principled mechanism to explicitly identify and remove these confounding effects, leading to high false alarm rates and missed detections in complex aerial scenes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255604/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes UAV aerial images (ranging from 1024×540 in UAVDT to 2000×1500 in VisDrone) along with two sets of text prompts: category prompts (e.g., 'a photo of [category]') for region exploration and 36 confounder prompts (combinations of weather, occlusion level, viewpoint, and scale) for causal intervention. It produces bounding boxes and class labels through a text-image contrastive head that replaces the standard classification head, computing class probabilities via cosine similarity between object embeddings and frozen CLIP text embeddings with learnable affine parameters.

## Method

MCR-UOD builds on YOLOv8's CSPDarknet backbone and introduces two modules operating on the low-level C1 feature map. The Language Guided Region Exploration (LGRE) module computes an object existence probability map by projecting pixel-level features and CLIP category text embeddings into a shared space, then masks C1 to suppress background regions. The Backdoor Adjustment Causal Reasoning (BACR) module selects top-τ high-probability pixels and performs cross-attention between these features (as queries) and a confounder dictionary (as keys/values) initialized from GPT-generated text prompts encoded by CLIP, implementing the do-operator via NWGM approximation to marginalize over confounders and yield condition-invariant representations. The confounder dictionary is dynamically updated during forward propagation by fusing visual features into the most similar dictionary entry. The refined feature map is fed into a decoupled detection head with WIoU loss for box regression and a text contrastive head for classification, trained end-to-end with the CLIP text encoder frozen.

## Results

On VisDrone, MCR-UOD achieves AP 44.6, AP50 67.3, and AP75 47.5, surpassing the previous best (SPAR) by +1.8 AP, +0.4 AP50, and +1.8 AP75. On UAVDT, it reaches AP 31.4, AP50 44.7, and AP75 35.6, improving over SPAR by +1.3 AP, +0.8 AP50, and +0.9 AP75. On HRSC2016 (ship detection), MCR-UOD attains AP 92.04, outperforming the prior best FSM (91.60). Statistical significance testing (Wilcoxon signed-rank) confirms the gains over SPAR are significant (p < 0.02 for both AP50 and AP75 on UAVDT).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MCR-UOD outperforms all 21 compared baselines across all three datasets and all three metrics (AP, AP50, AP75). On VisDrone, it beats the closest competitor SPAR (AAAI'25) by +1.8 AP and +1.8 AP75, and UAV-YOLOv8 by +1.9 AP. On UAVDT, it surpasses SPAR by +1.3 AP and +0.9 AP75, and TPH-YOLOv5+ by +1.3 AP. On HRSC2016, it edges out FSM (TPAMI'25) by +0.44 AP (92.04 vs. 91.60). The gains are statistically significant per Wilcoxon signed-rank tests (p = 0.018 for AP50, p = 0.015 for AP75 on UAVDT vs. SPAR). Notably, MCR-UOD also reduces model parameters (e.g., 61.5M vs. 68.2M for YOLOv8x) and GFLOPs (247.6 vs. 258.2) while improving accuracy, indicating a better speed-accuracy trade-off.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the YOLOv8 single-stage detection backbone (specifically the UAV-YOLOv8 variant) and integrates CLIP text embeddings to replace the standard classification head with a text-contrastive one. Its core novelty lies in applying Pearl's backdoor adjustment to object detection features, but departing from prior causal-inference-in-CV work (MAWCA, VCR-CNN, CT-MRI) that constructs confounder dictionaries from visual ROI features. Here, the confounder dictionary is initialized from GPT-generated text prompts encoded by CLIP, enabling cross-modal deconfounding that is argued to be more practical given the impossibility of collecting exhaustive UAV imagery under all weather/viewpoint conditions. The NWGM approximation from the attention literature is repurposed to compute the expectation over confounder strata efficiently.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine practical pain point in aerial detection—confounding by weather, lighting, and viewpoint—that prior zoom-in or attention-based methods handle only implicitly. The text-based confounder dictionary is a reasonable workaround for the data-collection bottleneck, and the modular design (LGRE + BACR) could in principle be grafted onto other single-stage detectors. However, the gains are modest (1–2 AP points over strong baselines), the confounder set is hand-designed (4 weather × 3 occlusion × 4 viewpoint = 48 combinations per class), and the authors acknowledge that prompt design is heuristic and CLIP's representational capacity is a ceiling. Open questions include whether the causal framework generalizes to open-vocabulary or multi-domain settings, and whether the dictionary update rule (simple EMA with α=0.05) is sufficient for truly dynamic environments.
