## Problem

Existing time series imputation (TSI) methods optimize point-wise reconstruction losses that focus on local numerical accuracy, leading to a critical optimization dilemma under high missing rates: models achieve low training loss yet produce poor imputations and severely distorted latent representations. The paper identifies that reconstruction objectives lack global guidance, causing models to memorize local noise rather than learn the underlying data distribution, and asks whether a training paradigm can be designed to capture both global and local information without overfitting to noise.

## Input / Output

The method takes a masked multivariate time series X^o = X ⊙ M (sequence length 96, N variables) and a binary missing mask M, and produces an imputed time series X̂ of the same shape. During training, the encoder also processes the original unmasked data X to generate reference embeddings for the Global Alignment loss, meaning the model consumes both masked and unmasked inputs simultaneously. The encoder outputs a multivariate Gaussian posterior over the latent space (mean and diagonal covariance), from which Z is sampled via the reparameterization trick during training and set to the mean at inference.

## Method

Glocal-IB is a model-agnostic training paradigm grounded in the Information Bottleneck principle, decomposing the TSI objective into three terms: (1) a Regularization loss (KL divergence between the encoder's Gaussian posterior and an isotropic Gaussian prior) that minimizes I(Z; X^o) to suppress noise from incomplete observations; (2) a Local loss (MSE reconstruction) that maximizes point-wise mutual information I(X; Z); and (3) a novel Global Alignment loss derived from an InfoNCE-style lower bound of I(X; Z) that aligns the latent representation of the masked input with the encoder's embedding of the original unmasked input at the same timestamp, using other timestamps as negatives. The Global Alignment loss requires only a single one-layer MLP projector and can be further simplified to a direct alignment objective. The overall training objective is min_{θ,φ} [α·L_Reg^θ + β₁·L_Loc^φ + β₂·L_Glo^φ], and the framework integrates into any existing encoder-decoder architecture (demonstrated on a vanilla 2-layer Transformer, TimesNet, and SAITS) with minimal architectural modification.

## Results

Glocal-IB applied to a vanilla 2-layer Transformer achieves the lowest average MSE and MAE across all nine benchmark datasets (averaged over 10%–90% missing rates). Headline improvements over the strongest baseline (SAITS) range from 6% MSE reduction on PEMS-Traffic (0.318 vs. 0.336) to 40% on ETTh2 (0.249 vs. 0.340) and 38% on ETTh1 (0.283 vs. 0.402). On the Weather dataset, Glocal-IB attains MSE 0.096 / MAE 0.056, outperforming the second-best method (SAITS, MSE 0.136) by 34%. The method also eliminates out-of-memory failures seen with iTransformer on PEMS-Traffic.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Glocal-IB (on a vanilla Transformer) outperforms all ten baselines on every dataset for both MSE and MAE. The largest margins appear on the ETT family: on ETTh2, Glocal-IB reduces MSE from 0.340 (SAITS, the best baseline) to 0.249 (−40%), and on ETTh1 from 0.402 to 0.283 (−38%). On Weather, the 34% MSE gap (0.096 vs. 0.136) highlights robustness to seasonal patterns. GPVAE and TimesNet, the two weakest baselines on ETT datasets (MSE 0.73–0.80), are surpassed by 60–70% in MSE. On the more challenging real-world datasets (Beijing Air, PEMS-Traffic, Electricity, Metr-LA), the margins narrow to 6–17% but remain consistent, and iTransformer fails entirely (OOM) on PEMS-Traffic. The generality experiments (Fig. 3–5) further show that applying Glocal-IB to SAITS and TimesNet also improves them over their original training and over foundation-model alignment (Time-MoE), confirming the paradigm is model-agnostic and adds only a single MLP with negligible overhead.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Information Bottleneck framework for time series imputation established by Choi & Lee (CIB, ICLR 2023) and the variational IB formulation of Alemi et al. (2016). The key departure is the introduction of a Global Alignment loss derived from the InfoNCE/CPC objective (van den Oord et al., 2018), which previous IB-based TSI methods (GPVAE, CIB) lacked—they relied solely on point-wise reconstruction to maximize I(X;Z). The paper also contrasts its lightweight MLP-based alignment against foundation-model alignment (Time-MoE), showing the latter provides only marginal benefit for imputation due to its forecasting-oriented pretraining.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and addresses a concrete optimization dilemma in TSI: models achieve low training loss under high missingness yet produce distorted latent representations and poor imputations, revealing that reconstruction-only objectives encourage noise memorization over global structure learning. The Glocal-IB paradigm is model-agnostic (requiring only one additional MLP), making it a practical drop-in improvement for existing encoder-decoder TSI architectures. However, the generality analysis is limited to three backbones on four ETT datasets, and the performance gain diminishes at 90% missingness where too little observed data remains to provide meaningful alignment signals—leaving open how to strengthen global guidance under extreme sparsity.
