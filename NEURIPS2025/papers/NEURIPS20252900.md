## Problem

Gene expression estimation from pathology images typically relies on point-wise losses (e.g., MSE) that target absolute expression values, making them vulnerable to batch effects and stochastic noise inherent in spatial transcriptomics data. The paper argues that relative expression trends between patches within a tissue are more stable than absolute levels, yet no loss function explicitly models these relative relationships while accounting for the probabilistic nature of count data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252900/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes pathology image patches (processed through a frozen CONCH vision-language foundation model to extract features) and produces per-gene rank scores that are scale-invariant within each tissue. The rank scores preserve the relative ordering of gene expression across patches from the same tissue (if e_g^i > e_g^j then r_g^i > r_g^j), rather than predicting absolute expression counts. In the real-data experiments, only a single fully connected layer on top of CONCH features is trained, with 50 highly variable genes as targets.

## Method

The core contribution is the STRank loss, which models gene expression counts as a Binomial distribution (pairwise) or Multinomial distribution (listwise) conditioned on the total count across patches, with probabilities derived from softmax over the model's rank score outputs. The negative log-likelihood yields a loss of the form -Σ e_g^i log p̂_g^i, where the observed count e_g^i acts as a natural weight: low-expression genes contribute less to the gradient, making the loss robust to stochastic noise in sparse regions. This contrasts with standard ranking losses that treat all pairs equally regardless of count magnitude. For the listwise variant, a correction term incorporating per-spot total expression l^i is introduced to handle variable detectability across spatial spots while preserving the discrete count structure. The approach is validated on synthetic data with controlled batch effects and on seven HEST-1k real datasets, where it outperforms MSE, Poisson, Negative Binomial, Rank, and PCC losses on average.

## Results

On the HEST-1k real-data benchmark (7 cancer types, 50 highly variable genes, CONCH features), ListSTRank achieves the best average Spearman Correlation Coefficient (SCC) of 0.345, followed closely by PairSTRank at 0.343, compared to PCC (0.326), MSE (0.306), Poisson (0.293), NB (0.244), and Rank (0.228). On synthetic data with controlled batch effects, ListSTRank reaches 0.945 SCC under uniform sampling and 0.828 under imbalanced sampling, substantially outperforming PCC (0.858/0.560) and Rank (0.835/0.738). The advantage of STRank is most pronounced under imbalanced and sparse conditions, where PCC degrades sharply (0.560 vs. 0.828) while STRank remains stable.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares STRank (Pair and List variants) against five baselines: MSE, Poisson, Negative Binomial (pointwise), Rank [3] (pairwise), and PCC [25, 2] (listwise). On the synthetic batch-effect benchmark, ListSTRank (0.945 uniform / 0.828 imbalanced) outperforms PCC (0.858 / 0.560) by a large margin under imbalanced sampling, and outperforms Rank [3] (0.835 / 0.738) in both settings. On the real HEST-1k data, ListSTRank and PairSTRank achieve the top two average SCC scores (0.345 and 0.343), beating PCC (0.326) by ~2% and Rank [3] (0.228) by ~12%. However, STRank does not win on every individual cancer type; for example, PCC achieves the best score on COAD (0.640 vs. 0.597 for ListSTRank), and the authors acknowledge that STRank's advantage is less consistent on real data due to inherent measurement noise. The sparsity experiment (Section 4.3) further shows that STRank's advantage over Rank and PCC grows as the downsampling rate decreases, with the largest gap at p=0.01.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the established line of gene expression estimation from pathology images (ST-Net, Hist2Gene, Hist2ST, TRIPLEX, BLEEP), which has predominantly relied on pointwise MSE loss to predict absolute expression values. It departs from this by reframing the objective as relative rank score estimation and drawing on learning-to-rank literature (pairwise hinge losses, listwise PCC) as a starting point. The key methodological departure is modeling gene expression counts through binomial (pairwise) and multinomial (listwise) distributions conditioned on model-predicted scores, rather than treating expression as a continuous regression target or a simple ordinal ranking problem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled argument that relative expression patterns are more robust to batch effects and stochastic noise than absolute values, which is directly relevant to the practical challenge of reducing RNA sequencing costs. However, real-dataset results are mixed: STRank improves on average but does not consistently outperform baselines across all seven HEST-1k datasets, and it underperforms in multi-cohort settings with large gene sets (COAD Visium). Open questions include how to handle cohort-specific noise characteristics, whether the approach scales to full transcriptomes, and why convergence is slower than PCC on single-patient data—limitations that temper the practical impact at this stage.
