## Problem

State-of-the-art BaB-based neural network verifiers fix intermediate-layer bounds at initialization and optimize only at the final layer, because updating bounds for all hidden neurons is prohibitively expensive. This leaves the relaxation loose throughout the network, causing deeper branching and longer verification times. The paper addresses this gap by showing that linear constraints already produced during bound propagation can be exploited to tighten bounds at any layer with minimal additional cost.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255815/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a feed-forward neural network, an ℓ∞-box input domain, and a verification property f(x) ≥ 0, and produces a verification verdict (verified/unknown) along with tightened per-neuron affine bounds at every layer. The linear constraints driving the clipping come from two sources: activation-space split constraints (e.g., ReLU or Softmax regime assignments) and final-layer bounding planes that separate the verified subset from the unverified remainder of the input. All operations are GPU-parallelized and require no external LP solver, scaling to networks with thousands of unstable neurons.

## Method

Clip-and-Verify introduces a linear constraint-driven clipping framework with two complementary algorithms. Complete Clipping formulates the bound-tightening problem as a Lagrangian dual: for a single constraint, the n-dimensional LP reduces to a 1D concave piecewise-linear maximization over one dual variable β, solvable in O(n log n) by sorting breakpoints (equivalent to the continuous knapsack greedy algorithm); for multiple constraints, a coordinate ascent iterates over each dual variable using the same 1D subproblem. Relaxed Clipping instead shrinks the shared input box via closed-form per-constraint updates (Theorem 3.2, O(n) per constraint) and then re-concretizes all cached intermediate bounds over the tighter box, yielding network-wide improvements from a single cheap update. The framework integrates with both input-space BaB (reusing final-layer bounding planes as clipping constraints) and activation-space BaB (accumulating split constraints from activation assignments), and is deployed as part of the α,β-CROWN verifier, achieving up to 96% reduction in BaB subproblems.

## Results

On VNN-COMP activation-split benchmarks, Clip-and-Verify with BICCOS achieves state-of-the-art verified property counts: 27/29 on oval22, 64/72 on cifar10-resnet, 131/168 on cifar100-2024, and 144/157 on tinyimagenet-2024, outperforming BICCOS (25, 63, 121, 138) and β-CROWN (20, 60, 119, 135) respectively. On input-split benchmarks, Complete Clipping reduces BaB subproblems by up to 96.3% (lsnc: 142M → 5.3M) and 95.5% (cartpole: 54M → 2.4M), turning previously intractable control-system verification tasks (Quadrotor-2D, Quad-2D-Large) into solvable ones within a 3-day timeout. Verified accuracy on cifar100-2024 improves from 60.5% (BICCOS) to 65.5%, and on tinyimagenet-2024 from 69.0% to 72.0%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Clip-and-Verify with BICCOS outperforms all 15 compared baselines on every VNN-COMP activation-split benchmark in Table 3, verifying 27/29 properties on oval22 (vs. BICCOS 25, β-CROWN 20, MN-BaB 19), 64/72 on cifar10-resnet (vs. BICCOS 63, β-CROWN 60), 131/168 on cifar100-2024 (vs. BICCOS 121, β-CROWN 119, PyRAT 68), and 144/157 on tinyimagenet-2024 (vs. BICCOS 138, β-CROWN 135, PyRAT 49). On input-split benchmarks (Table 1), Complete Clipping dramatically reduces subproblem counts—96.3% on lsnc, 78.6% on acasxu, 51.8% on nn4sys—while maintaining or improving verified counts over α,β-CROWN. On hard control-system tasks (Table 2), baseline α,β-CROWN times out on Quadrotor-2D and Quad-2D-Large, whereas Complete Clipping solves them in 78,818s and 104,614s respectively, and reduces cartpole runtime from 1602s to 142s. Against LP-solver-based approaches (Gurobi dual simplex), the GPU-parallelized coordinate ascent is over 880× faster per round (0.0028s vs. 2.47s) with comparable bound accuracy. On verified accuracy (Table 4), Clip-and-Verify with BICCOS matches or exceeds BICCOS on all datasets, with the largest gains on cifar100-2024 (+5.0 pp) and tinyimagenet-2024 (+3.0 pp), while also outperforming GCP-CROWN with MIP cuts on most CIFAR variants.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the CROWN/α,β-CROWN line of bound-propagation-based verification by identifying that linear constraints already generated during BaB splits (activation-space or output-space) can be exploited to tighten intermediate-layer bounds at negligible cost, rather than requiring expensive LP solves or full re-propagation as in β-CROWN. The two clipping algorithms—Complete Clipping (a coordinate-ascent Lagrangian dual solver shown equivalent to the continuous knapsack greedy algorithm) and Relaxed Clipping (closed-form box updates)—are novel contributions that fill a gap where prior verifiers fixed intermediate bounds at initialization and optimized only the final layer. The framework is complementary to cutting-plane methods like BICCOS and GCP-CROWN, which strengthen node-wise relaxations but do not directly contract the input domain or refine per-neuron affine bounds across layers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As the VNN-COMP 2025 winner, Clip-and-Verify demonstrates that a lightweight, GPU-parallel clipping step can reduce BaB subproblems by up to 96% and turn previously intractable Lyapunov-stability verification problems (cartpole, quadrotor) into solvable ones, with an 880× speedup over integrating Gurobi LP into the same pipeline. The practical impact is broad: the framework is activation-agnostic (validated on ViT with Softmax), integrates with any BaB verifier, and requires no external solvers. Open questions include whether the box-domain assumption limits applicability to non-ℓ∞ input sets, whether the iterative coordinate ascent for multiple constraints can be made provably optimal in a single pass, and how the critical-neuron selection heuristic (BaBSR intercept) behaves on networks with highly correlated unstable neurons.
