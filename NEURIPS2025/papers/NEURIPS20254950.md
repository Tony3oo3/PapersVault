## Problem

Validating whether a neural posterior estimate q(θ|x) faithfully approximates the true posterior p(θ|x) across all conditioning inputs is a central but under-solved problem in simulation-based inference. Existing diagnostics—SBC (necessary-only, multiple-testing fragile), TARP (sensitive to reference-point choice), and C2ST (requires class-balanced samples and a global classifier)—each fail in practical NPE settings where only a single draw from p(θ|x) is available per x while q(θ|x) can be sampled arbitrarily.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a set of anchor pairs (θ*_i, x_i) drawn from the true joint p(θ,x)—typically one θ* per x—plus the ability to draw M synthetic samples from q(θ|x_i) for each x_i. It produces a one-sample KS test statistic and p-value for the null p=q, a continuous ACLD integral-probability-metric distance quantifying the magnitude of any discrepancy, and the learned localization map θ_l(x) that pinpoints the region of θ-space where p and q diverge most for each x. The critical asymmetry is that the rank statistic U_q(θ*) is computed using only the single p-draw as an anchor against M q-draws, reducing a high-dimensional mass comparison to a 1-D uniformity check.

## Method

CoLT is built on the localization principle: if p≠q, some ball of positive radius must exhibit a nonzero mass discrepancy, and a smooth map θ_l(x) can be trained to find that ball adaptively for each x. A trainable embedding φ:Θ→ℝ^m defines metric balls B_φ(θ,R) in a learned geometry (satisfying a doubling condition), and the key theoretical result shows that the ball-probability rank U_q(θ*)=P_{θ~q}(d_φ(θ_l,θ)≤d_φ(θ_l,θ*)) is Uniform(0,1) if and only if p and q agree on all such balls—collapsling the high-dimensional equality test to a 1-D uniformity condition. Training is adversarial: θ_l(x;ψ) and φ are jointly optimized via gradient descent to maximize a Sinkhorn divergence between the empirical rank distribution and Uniform(0,1), with the Straight-Through Estimator handling the non-differentiable indicator in U_i. At test time, the trained networks produce rank statistics on a held-out set, and a classical one-sample KS test yields the p-value; the worst-case KS deviation over all (θ_l, φ, radius) choices equals the ACLD IPM, giving a continuous distance metric beyond binary pass/fail.

## Results

On the toy tree-shaped manifold example, CoLT achieves a statistical power of 0.877 at α=1.5 (small perturbation) where C2ST fails entirely (power = 0.000), and reaches perfect power (1.000) at α=4.0 where C2ST only reaches 0.065. Across the NPTBench suite (Gaussian and manifold benchmark families with four perturbation types), CoLT Full (learned embedding) consistently matches or exceeds C2ST on flat posteriors and substantially outperforms all baselines on curved manifolds, particularly under tail adjustments and anisotropic covariance distortions. CoLT ID (identity embedding) performs well on flat posteriors but degrades on manifold settings, confirming the importance of the learned metric.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoLT decisively outperforms C2ST [Lopez-Paz & Oquab, 2016] across all settings, with the gap most dramatic on structured manifolds: at α=1.5 on the toy example, CoLT detects the perturbation (power 0.877) while C2ST has zero power, and even at α=4 C2ST only reaches 0.065. Against SBC [Talts et al., 2018], CoLT is consistently superior; SBC's rank-based per-margin statistics suffer from multiple-testing power loss in high dimensions and cannot capture joint distributional discrepancies. Against TARP [Lemos et al., 2023], CoLT also wins across all perturbation types and dimensions; TARP's performance is highly sensitive to the choice of (non-trainable) reference point distribution, and the paper shows it underperforms CoLT at every α value tested. The key differentiator is that CoLT's learned embedding (CoLT Full) adapts the geometry of comparison to the posterior's structure, giving it a clear advantage on curved manifolds where C2ST, SBC, and TARP all struggle, while CoLT ID (fixed Euclidean geometry) narrows the gap on flat posteriors but still matches or beats the baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground in neural posterior validation by repurposing classical measure-theoretic tools—the Lebesgue Differentiation Theorem, the probability integral transform, and integral probability metrics—into a trainable neural network framework. It departs from the three dominant baselines (SBC, TARP, C2ST) by replacing global classifier boundaries or fixed rank statistics with an adversarially learned localization function θ_l(x) and embedding φ that adaptively search for where p and q differ most in local mass. The conceptual lineage draws from the measure-theoretic distinguishability principle (if two densities differ, they differ on some ball) and from deep kernel embeddings for two-sample testing, but the combination into a conditional, x-dependent, single-anchor-sample formulation is novel.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CoLT provides a necessary-and-sufficient condition for posterior validity (unlike SBC, which is only necessary) while handling the critical asymmetry in NPE settings where only one true posterior draw is available per x. The ACLD metric offers a continuous, interpretable distance that can track training progress and pinpoint specific regions of failure, moving beyond binary pass/fail diagnostics. Key open questions include whether the learned localization and embedding generalize to very high-dimensional posteriors, how performance degrades when the true posterior is only approximately known (as in real SBI), and whether the Sinkhorn-divergence training objective can be replaced by more principled alternatives that preserve the theoretical IPM connection at test time.
