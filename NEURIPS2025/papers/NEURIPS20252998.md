## Problem

Existing gender bias mitigation methods for LLMs face a fundamental trade-off: parameter-modification approaches (fine-tuning, DPO, model editing) require white-box access and are inapplicable to closed-source API models, while instruction-based methods using static manually-designed preambles (e.g., CF-D, Desc-D) degrade response quality on normal tasks by distorting query interpretation. No prior method simultaneously achieves full automation, model-agnostic applicability to both open- and closed-source LLMs, and preservation of general task performance.

## Input / Output

At inference, the system takes a user query and a randomly sampled Fairwords (a nonsensical token sequence of length 20–40 drawn from a bag of 93 auto-searched triggers) as joint input to a small seq2seq refiner (Llama3.2-3B-Instruct with LoRA). The refiner outputs a context-aware natural language instruction that is prepended to the original query before being sent to the target LLM. The refiner adapts its output based on query type: for gender-related queries it generates debiasing instructions (e.g., 'Reject gender stereotypes and assume equal opportunities'), while for neutral queries it produces performance-boosting prompts (e.g., 'Provide a detailed and clear answer'), ensuring the module does not degrade standard task performance.

## Method

FaIRMaker employs a two-stage auto-search and refinement paradigm. In the auto-search stage, Fairwords are optimized via the GCG (Greedy Coordinate Gradient) algorithm on the GenderAlign preference dataset, maximizing the log-probability of chosen responses while suppressing rejected ones; a filtering step then retains only Fairwords that demonstrably improve response quality on a held-out set, as judged by Llama3.1-8B. In the refinement stage, a ChatGPT-assisted reverse-inference process converts the nonsensical Fairwords into interpretable natural language instructions, producing 18k training pairs (9k gender-related + 9k general-task) that teach a seq2seq model to generalize this transformation. The trained refiner (Llama3.2-3B-Instruct, LoRA rank 8) learns to map any (query, Fairwords) pair to an appropriate instruction, enabling transferability to black-box LLMs without access to preference data at inference time. The key design insight is that the refiner is trained on both bias-related and neutral-task data, so it preserves model utility on standard benchmarks while still producing effective debiasing instructions when the query involves gender stereotypes.

## Results

On BBQ-gender, FaIRMaker reduces sDIS by 79–88% across four open-source LLMs (e.g., Llama2-Alpaca: 1.066→0.224; Llama2-Chat: 2.233→0.273) and sAMB by 75–80%, consistently outperforming all baselines. On GA-test, FaIRMaker achieves a win rate of 54.23% (vs. 42.79% without refinement) and improves response scores by up to +0.36 points (Llama2-Alpaca: 3.71→4.07). On general tasks, FaIRMaker improves MMLU accuracy by up to +4.44% (Llama2-Alpaca: 33.49→37.93) and HellaSwag by up to +1.19% (Llama2-Chat: 25.28→26.47), while instruction-following scores remain within 0.05 points of the original.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FaIRMaker dominates all four baselines on BBQ-gender sDIS and sAMB across every model. CF-D and Desc-D (Oba et al., EACL 2024) frequently *increase* bias—e.g., on Llama2-Chat, CF-D raises sDIS from 2.233 to 2.451 and sAMB from 1.673 to 1.895—while also degrading response quality on GA-test (e.g., 4.56→4.00 on Llama2-Chat). The Intervention prompt (Si et al., 2022) provides moderate bias reduction (sDIS 0.713 on Llama2-Alpaca) but is substantially weaker than FaIRMaker (0.224) and sometimes increases bias on Qwen2-Instruct (sDIS 5.044 vs. original 4.638). MBIAS (Raza et al., 2024) achieves competitive response scores on stronger models (4.76 on Qwen2-Instruct vs. FaIRMaker's 4.82) but offers only marginal gains on Llama2-Alpaca (3.70 vs. original 3.71), highlighting its limitation as a post-processing method. In the extendability comparison, DPO fine-tuning (Rafailov et al., NeurIPS 2023) outperforms FaIRMaker on in-distribution GA-test but underperforms on OOD BBQ-gender and degrades general-task accuracy, whereas combining DPO with FaIRMaker recovers and surpasses both.

## Relation to Prior Work

FaIRMaker repurposes the GCG adversarial suffix optimization algorithm (Zou et al., 2023) — originally designed for jailbreaking — as a debiasing trigger search, and extends the AmpleGCG insight (Liao & Sun, 2024) that semantic rewriting of optimization-based suffixes preserves their behavioral effect. Unlike prior automatic prompt engineering for bias (Sheng et al., Bauer et al.) that produces dataset-specific or nonsensical tokens, FaIRMaker adds a seq2seq refinement stage that converts raw Fairwords into context-aware natural language instructions, making the pipeline model-agnostic and compatible with closed-source APIs. It also departs from static manual preambles (CF-D, Desc-D) by generating adaptive, query-conditioned instructions that preserve utility on neutral tasks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a practical, low-overhead (~1.5 s/query) path to debiasing closed-source LLMs without parameter access, a setting where most prior methods are inapplicable. However, the work is limited to binary gender bias, and the emotional-interpretation hypothesis for why nonsensical Fairwords work remains speculative and uncontrolled. Open questions include whether the auto-search-and-refinement paradigm generalizes to other bias dimensions (race, age, religion) without re-deriving the entire pipeline, and whether the minor utility drops on instruction-following tasks (formatting, verbosity) can be eliminated through task-aware refinement.
