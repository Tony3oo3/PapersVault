## Problem

Noise schedule design in diffusion models has historically relied on hand-crafted heuristics (linear, cosine, sigmoid, EDM) without a principled connection to the data being generated. This paper asks whether a spectral analysis of the reverse diffusion process can yield a closed-form characterization of how the noise schedule shapes the output distribution, enabling data-dependent schedule optimization from first principles.

## Input / Output

The method consumes the target data's covariance matrix (via its eigenvalues), the number of diffusion steps S, and the chosen sampling framework (DDPM or DDIM, VP or VE) to produce a monotonically decreasing sequence of S noise schedule parameters {ᾱ_s}. The optimization is solved via SLSQP with boundary constraints (ᾱ_0 ≈ 1, ᾱ_S ≈ 0) and monotonicity constraints, yielding a schedule that is a function of the data's spectral content, resolution d, and step count. For real datasets, the covariance is estimated empirically from the data (e.g., circulant structure for 1D signals, full covariance for images).

## Method

Under a Gaussianity assumption on the target distribution, the paper derives a closed-form spectral transfer function for the discrete reverse diffusion process, showing that the generated signal is a linear function of the initial white noise with a diagonal transfer matrix in the eigenbasis of the data covariance. This reduces the d-dimensional problem to d independent scalar transfer functions parameterized solely by the noise schedule. The authors then formulate a constrained optimization problem minimizing Wasserstein-2 or KL divergence between the generated and target distributions, solved numerically via SLSQP. A key finding is that individual eigenvalue magnitudes shape the schedule's curvature—smaller eigenvalues (high frequencies) produce more concave schedules while larger ones (low frequencies) produce more convex ones—providing a theoretical explanation for the coarse-to-fine generation behavior and retrospectively justifying cosine and sigmoid heuristics as near-optimal approximations.

## Results

The spectral schedule consistently achieves the lowest Wasserstein-2 distance compared to all heuristic baselines (Linear, Cosine, Sigmoid, EDM) across both synthetic Gaussian and real-data settings (CIFAR-10, MUSIC). The advantage is most pronounced at lower diffusion step counts (e.g., 10–50 steps), where discretization error dominates, and narrows as the number of steps increases toward 250+. On CIFAR-10, the spectral schedule also yields the best FID scores among all tested schedules, with the gap over heuristics being smaller than in the MUSIC setting due to the pretrained EDM denoiser's continuous-schedule bias.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The spectral schedule outperforms the Linear (DDPM) and Sigmoid (Jabri et al.) heuristics across all step counts and both datasets, with the largest margins at low step counts where discretization error is highest. The Cosine (Nichol & Dhariwal) and EDM (Karras et al.) schedules perform most competitively, particularly when their parameters are tuned to resemble the spectral recommendation (e.g., Cosine(0,0.5,1) for CIFAR-10, EDM ρ=7 for MUSIC), suggesting that these widely-used heuristics are near-optimal for data with spectral profiles similar to those datasets. For the MUSIC dataset, the spectral schedule introduces a noticeably slower decay than any standard heuristic, reflecting the 1/f spectral structure of audio, and this tailored shape yields a clear Wasserstein-2 advantage. Overall, the paper demonstrates that the spectral recommendation provides a principled, data-adaptive alternative that subsumes or justifies the existing handcrafted schedules rather than contradicting them.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the DDPM and DDIM discrete formulations and the VP/VE SDE framework, but departs from prior noise schedule optimization work (KLUB, truncation-error minimization, score-optimal schedules) by reframing the problem entirely in the spectral domain. While [24, 31] also assume Gaussian targets, they derive continuous-domain solutions via integration; here the analysis is explicitly discrete, capturing the effects of finite-step discretization. The key conceptual departure is connecting the eigenvalue structure of the data covariance to the shape of the optimal schedule — a link the authors note has not been made in prior spectral analyses of diffusion models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a theoretical backbone for noise schedule design that practitioners have relied on heuristics for, and shows that commonly used cosine and sigmoid schedules emerge as near-optimal solutions under specific spectral conditions — a retrospective justification that could guide future schedule design without expensive retraining. The main limitation is the Gaussian target assumption, which the authors acknowledge restricts direct applicability to real data; extending to Gaussian mixtures or other non-Gaussian families is the natural next step. Open questions include whether the spectral perspective can enable principled accelerated sampling and whether the observed frequency-bias dynamics can be mitigated through targeted loss weighting.
