## Problem

Prior RL formulations for B&B branching (TreeMDP) are not proper MDPs: a single transition yields two child states rather than one next state, violating the Markov process structure. This forces ad hoc Bellman updates and convergence proofs, and introduces approximations that are only valid for one-step temporal difference but produce inconsistent learning targets for multi-step trajectories, undermining the asymptotic performance of RL branching agents.

## Input / Output

The agent observes the full B&B tree state s_t = (V_t, E_t, x̄_t) — the set of all nodes, edges, and the current incumbent solution — rather than just the current node as in TreeMDP. This state is encoded as a bipartite graph (variable nodes, constraint nodes, and edges from the MILP at the selected open node) augmented with the LP relaxation solution and incumbent features. The output is a single integer variable index a_t ∈ I indicating which fractional variable to branch on, producing two child nodes that modify the tree state.

## Method

BBMDP defines variable selection in B&B as a proper deterministic MDP with the full B&B tree as state, integer variable indices as actions, the branching operation κ_ρ as transition, and a constant reward of −2 per step (matching the two nodes added per branch). Under a DFS node selection policy, the paper proves (Proposition 3.1) that the Bellman equation decomposes into subtree value functions V̄(o_i, x̄_{o_i}), showing that minimizing any single subtree size is equivalent to minimizing the total tree size — this is the key insight that makes the problem tractable. The Q-value network is a graph convolutional architecture (from Gasse et al.) parameterizing Q̄*(o_1, x̄_{o_1}, a), trained with a Rainbow-DQN pipeline (double DQN, 3-step returns, prioritized replay) and a novel HL-Gauss cross-entropy loss that encodes Q-values as categorical histogram distributions over a log₂-transformed support to handle the multi-order-of-magnitude range of subtree sizes.

## Results

On test instances across four Ecole benchmarks (Set Covering, Combinatorial Auction, Maximum Independent Set, Multiple Knapsack), DQN-BBMDP achieves the best normalized score (100) among all RL agents, with 152.3 nodes on Set Covering, 97.9 on Combinatorial Auction, 103.2 on Maximum Independent Set, and 236.6 on Multiple Knapsack. Compared to the prior state-of-the-art DQN-Retro, DQN-BBMDP achieves a 27% reduction in total B&B nodes and a 38% reduction in solving time (outside presolve) in aggregate. On higher-dimensional transfer instances, DQN-BBMDP maintains its lead among RL agents and is the first RL agent to outperform SCIP on 3 out of 4 benchmarks in solving time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DQN-BBMDP outperforms all prior RL baselines on test instances: it beats DQN-Retro (Parsonson et al., 2022) by 27% in nodes and 38% in time in aggregate, and beats DQN-tMDP (Etheve et al., 2020) and PG-tMDP (Scavuzzo et al., 2022) by wider margins. Against the IL expert (Gasse et al., 2019), DQN-BBMDP still trails on Set Covering (152.3 vs. 133.8 nodes) and Combinatorial Auction (97.9 vs. 83.6 nodes), but narrows the gap substantially and wins on Maximum Independent Set (103.2 vs. 40.1 nodes — though IL is faster in time at 0.36s vs. 0.69s). All RL agents, including DQN-BBMDP, outperform SCIP's default reliability pseudo-cost branching on 3 of 4 benchmarks in solving time. On transfer instances, DQN-BBMDP maintains its RL-best position and is the only RL agent to beat SCIP on 3/4 benchmarks, while DQN-Retro and DQN-tMDP degrade significantly (e.g., DQN-Retro jumps to 119,478 nodes on Max. Ind. Set transfer vs. 7,168 for DQN-BBMDP). The ablation confirms that the k-step TD formulation is where BBMDP's principled MDP structure provides its advantage: k=3 improves DQN-BBMDP by 4% but degrades DQN-TreeMDP by 13%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the TreeMDP framework (Etheve et al. 2020, Scavuzzo et al. 2022, Parsonson et al. 2022) that has dominated RL-based branching research, arguing that TreeMDP is not a valid MDP because its transitions produce two child states rather than a single next state. The authors introduce BBMDP, a principled vanilla MDP where the state is the entire B&B tree, enabling standard Bellman operators and unlocking algorithms like k-step TD, TD(λ), and MCTS that are incompatible with TreeMDP. The approach also builds on Gasse et al. [2019] for the GNN-based state representation and on Farebrother et al. [2024] for the HL-Gauss classification loss adapted to the B&B setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By providing a theoretically sound MDP formulation, BBMDP gives the community a rigorous foundation for applying the full arsenal of approximate dynamic programming to B&B heuristics, rather than relying on ad hoc convergence proofs. However, RL agents still trail behind imitation learning on most benchmarks, and the paper acknowledges that out-of-distribution effects on higher-dimensional transfer instances remain a significant barrier. Open questions include whether the principled formulation can be combined with domain adaptation or distributional RL to close the gap with IL, and whether the framework extends naturally to other B&B heuristics (cut selection, primal search) as the authors suggest.
