## Problem

Black-box variational inference (BBVI) with full-rank location-scale families has an O(d) explicit dimension dependence in its iteration complexity, while mean-field families were previously shown to achieve only O(√d). It was conjectured that the O(√d) bound was loose and that O(log d) was achievable, but no proof existed. This paper resolves that conjecture by establishing a nearly dimension-independent convergence rate for mean-field BBVI.

## Input / Output

The method takes a μ-strongly convex, L-smooth target log-density ℓ: ℝ^d → ℝ and a mean-field location-scale variational family defined by a standardized base distribution φ (with finite kurtosis r₄) and diagonal scale matrix C ∈ D_{≻0}^d. It produces variational parameters (m, C) minimizing the exclusive KL divergence D_KL(q_λ, π), with the guarantee that after O(g(d,H,δ,μ,φ)·σ*²·ε⁻¹) iterations of stochastic proximal gradient descent, E‖λ_T − λ_*‖²₂ ≤ ε. The dimension dependence enters exclusively through E[max_{j=1,…,d} u_j²], the expected maximum of squared base-distribution samples, which is O(log d) for sub-Gaussian φ and O(d^{1/k}) for φ with k finite moments.

## Method

The core contribution is a tight upper bound on the reparametrization gradient variance (Lemma 4.1) that exploits the diagonal structure of the mean-field Jacobian. The key probabilistic insight is that the heavy-tailed term u_{i*}⁴ (where i* = argmax u_i²) affects only a single coordinate, and the probability P[i*=i] = 1/d cancels its dimensional growth, leaving only the lighter-tailed E[max u_j²] as the dimension-dependent factor. This is formalized by decomposing the expectation with indicator functions 1{i*=i} and 1{i*≠i}, yielding a bound of O((1+r₄)‖H‖²₂ + δ²(1/2 + r₄ + E[max u_j²]))·‖λ−λ'‖²₂. The paper further proves a matching lower bound (Proposition 4.2) showing the O(log d) dependence is unimprovable when relying solely on spectral bounds on ∇²ℓ, and establishes that when the Hessian is constant (Gaussian targets), the complexity is fully dimension-free at O(κ²ε⁻¹).

## Results

The paper proves that BBVI with a mean-field location-scale family and reparametrization gradient achieves an ε-accurate solution in O(log d · κ² · ε⁻¹) iterations for sub-Gaussian families (e.g., Gaussian), improving the previous O(√d · κ² · ε⁻¹) bound. For heavy-tailed families with k finite moments, the dependence degrades to O(d^(2/k) · κ² · ε⁻¹); for Student-t with ν > 4 degrees of freedom this gives O(d^(2/(ν−2)) · κ² · ε⁻¹). When the target Hessian is constant (Gaussian target), the complexity becomes fully dimension-independent: O(κ² · ε⁻¹). A matching lower bound (Proposition 4.2) shows the O(log d) dependence cannot be improved using only spectral bounds on the Hessian.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper, so comparisons are between iteration-complexity bounds rather than empirical accuracy. The headline improvement is over Kim et al. (2023a), which established O(√d · κ² · ε⁻¹) for mean-field families and conjectured O(log d); this paper resolves that conjecture affirmatively for sub-Gaussian families. Compared to the full-rank bound of Domke (2019) and Kim et al. (2023a) at O(d · κ² · ε⁻¹), the mean-field sub-Gaussian result reduces the explicit dimension factor from linear to logarithmic. The lower bound in Proposition 4.2 demonstrates that the O(log d) factor is tight under spectral Hessian assumptions alone, ruling out further improvement without stronger structural assumptions on the target. For the special case of a constant Hessian (Gaussian target), the result matches and extends the dimension-independent guarantee of Ko et al. (2024) for diagonal-Hessian targets to the broader class of constant-Hessian targets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper resolves a conjecture from Kim et al. (2023a), which established O(√d) dimension dependence for mean-field BBVI and O(d) for full-rank families, by proving O(log d) for sub-Gaussian mean-field families. The technical core extends the gradient variance analysis of Kim et al. (2023b), which used a Frobenius-norm inequality yielding O(r₄√d), by switching to an operator-norm bound and a probabilistic decomposition that isolates the single heavy-tailed coordinate. The convergence framework builds on the SPGD analysis of Domke et al. (2023), with a slight improvement in the dependence on initialization (logarithmic vs. polynomial). A matching lower bound (Proposition 4.2) shows the O(log d) rate is tight when only spectral bounds on the Hessian are available.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result provides a clean theoretical explanation for the well-known empirical observation that mean-field families converge faster than full-rank families in high dimensions, and quantifies the speedup as O(log d) vs. O(d). The dimension-free rate for Gaussian targets (constant Hessian) is a particularly clean special case. Open questions include whether the O(κ²) condition-number dependence is tight, whether adaptive methods can trade statistical accuracy for computational cost between O(log d) and O(d), and how to extend the analysis to low-rank-plus-diagonal families, which currently lack smoothness guarantees. The lower bound also clarifies that improving the rate would require exploiting structure beyond spectral bounds on the Hessian.
