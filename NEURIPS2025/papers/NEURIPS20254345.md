## Problem

Generating executable trajectories for complex Signal Temporal Logic (STL) tasks under unknown system dynamics remains unsolved: model-based planners require explicit dynamics, while existing data-driven approaches either learn task-specific policies or struggle with long-horizon STL specifications. No prior method achieves zero-shot generalization to arbitrary new STL tasks using only task-agnostic offline trajectory data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254345/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an STL formula in Positive Normal Form (with the restriction that the left operand of 'until' contains only 'always' operators), an initial state, and a dataset of task-agnostic trajectories of varying lengths collected from the unknown system. It outputs a complete action sequence whose resulting state signal satisfies the STL formula; the trajectory is constructed by stitching short diffusion-generated segments between timed waypoints, and actions are recovered via a learned inverse dynamics model or a PD tracking controller operating at a fixed planning-to-control ratio k.

## Method

The framework has three components: (1) a semantics-based decomposition that converts the STL formula into reachability and invariance progresses with time-variable constraints, proven sound via Lemma 1; (2) a depth-first search-based progress allocation that uses a learned Time Predictor (an MLP estimating Gaussian trajectory-length distributions) to assign timed waypoints while dynamically maintaining feasibility of time-variable constraints via integer linear programming; and (3) a trajectory generation module that uses SafeDiffuser (a diffusion model with control-barrier-function-based constrained sampling) to produce short, dynamically feasible segments between adjacent waypoints, which are then concatenated. The key insight is that by decomposing the global task into local progresses and generating only short segments, the diffusion model stays within its training distribution while the search handles global temporal structure, yielding a formally sound (Theorem 1) yet data-driven planner that generalizes zero-shot to unseen STL tasks.

## Results

On Maze2D (U-Maze, Medium, Large) across three task types, the proposed method achieves 79.33%–97.33% execution success rate, consistently outperforming the RGD baseline (16.67%–80.00%). Average robustness values are positive for the proposed method (0.135–0.321) while RGD frequently yields negative values (−0.393 to 0.108), indicating RGD often fails to satisfy the STL specification. Total planning time is 0.64–5.59 s for the proposed method versus 13.43–136.35 s for RGD, a >10× speedup. On OGBench dynamics-rich domains, the framework attains >84% success on Cube (6-DoF UR5e) and >60% on AntMaze (8-DoF Ant).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is RGD (Robustness Guided Diffuser) [Zhong et al., ICRA 2023], which uses robustness-value gradient guidance to steer a diffusion model's sampling. The proposed method wins decisively on all three metrics across all nine environment–task combinations: success rate is 15–65 percentage points higher (e.g., 97.33% vs. 80.00% on U-Maze Type 1; 79.33% vs. 26.67% on Large Type 3), robustness values are consistently positive versus frequently negative for RGD, and total planning time is reduced by more than 10× (e.g., 0.86 s vs. 13.43 s on U-Maze Type 1; 5.59 s vs. 136.35 s on Large Type 3). The gap widens with task complexity and environment size: RGD's success rate drops to 16.67% on Large Type 2 while the proposed method maintains 81.33%. The paper attributes RGD's failure to the difficulty of jointly optimizing robustness and dynamic feasibility over long horizons, whereas the proposed method decomposes the problem into short locally-feasible segments, shifting the challenge to per-segment dynamic feasibility.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of STL planning and diffusion-based trajectory generation. It builds on the diffusion planning architecture of Janner et al. [16] (length control via noise, endpoint inpainting) and the constrained sampling framework of SafeDiffuser [29] (CBF-based safety enforcement during denoising), but departs from the robustness-gradient-guided diffusion approach of RGD [23] by never asking the diffusion model to satisfy the full STL formula in one shot. Instead, it adopts a decomposition-and-stitching paradigm inspired by incremental STL decomposition [26] and trajectory stitching for offline RL [45–48], inserting a dynamics-aware DFS-based waypoint allocator (guided by a learned time predictor) between the logic-level decomposition and the generative model. The key conceptual departure from prior diffusion-for-STL work is that the diffusion model is reduced to a local segment generator, while global temporal consistency is handled by the search layer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first data-driven STL planner with a formal soundness guarantee (Theorem 1) that achieves zero-shot generalization to unseen complex, long-horizon STL tasks using only task-agnostic offline trajectories, addressing a gap where prior diffusion-based STL methods (RGD, [24]) collapse on tasks beyond a few temporal operators. The 10× planning-time speedup and the consistent positive robustness values (vs. negative for RGD on complex tasks) make the approach practically relevant for real-time robotic task execution. Open questions include: whether the low-dimensional task-space planning + low-level controller gap can be closed for higher-DoF systems, whether the heuristic DFS allocation can be made anytime-optimal, and whether the framework extends to non-navigation STL scenarios (e.g., maintenance, safety-critical invariance with tight margins).
