## Problem

Contrastive decoding (CD) has proven effective at reducing hallucinations in vision-language models by perturbing a fixed modality, but it has not been adapted to audio-visual large language models (AV-LLMs), where hallucinations arise from complex cross-modal interactions among audio, video, and language rather than from a single modality's dominance. Existing CD methods that corrupt one fixed modality cannot capture the intricate trimodal hallucination patterns that emerge when multiple modalities interact.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252121/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

AVCD is a training-free, inference-time decoding strategy applied to pre-trained AV-LLMs (e.g., VideoLLaMA2, video-SALMONN). It consumes the standard model inputs—audio clips encoded via BEATs, video frames encoded via CLIP, and text prompts tokenized into a unified sequence—and produces autoregressive text responses. The method requires no retraining or architectural modification; it operates purely at the logit level during generation, with the only additional requirement being access to full attention weights (incompatible with FlashAttention).

## Method

AVCD introduces three complementary mechanisms. First, dominance-aware attentive masking uses the attention distribution of the final query token across transformer layers to dynamically identify the dominant modality, then zeros out the top-50% attention-weighted tokens in the less dominant modalities to produce perturbed logits—avoiding the out-of-distribution noise introduced by Gaussian perturbation in prior methods. Second, a reformulated trimodal contrastive decoding (Eq. 10) simultaneously applies CD to both video and audio modalities with separate contrastive strengths (α^v, α^a), derived via a Taylor-approximation that moves summation outside the logarithm to work with raw logits. Third, entropy-guided adaptive decoding (EAD) skips the multi-forward-pass CD computation when the original logit distribution has low entropy (high confidence), reducing inference latency by over 30% while preserving accuracy gains.

## Results

On the AVHBench hallucination benchmark, AVCD improves accuracy by 2% for VideoLLaMA2 (70.52% → 72.15%) and 7% for video-SALMONN (58.19% → 62.18%) over base decoding. On MUSIC-AVQA, AVCD achieves 81.58% accuracy for VideoLLaMA2 and 49.73% for video-SALMONN, both surpassing base and VCD baselines. For video-LLMs, AVCD improves VideoLLaMA2 on MSVD-QA from 74.43% to 75.20% and on ActivityNet-QA from 47.19% to 48.22%, while also outperforming VCD on Video-LLaVA across both benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AVCD consistently outperforms VCD [28] across all evaluated settings. On AVHBench, VCD actually degrades VideoLLaMA2's accuracy from 70.52% to 65.18%, while AVCD improves it to 72.15%; for video-SALMONN, VCD yields 60.61% versus AVCD's 62.18%. On video-LLM benchmarks (MSVD-QA, ActivityNet-QA), VCD underperforms base decoding for VideoLLaMA2 (71.30% vs 74.43% on MSVD-QA), whereas AVCD surpasses both (75.20%). OPERA [22], evaluated only on OmniBench, achieves 30.8% overall accuracy versus AVCD's 34.5% and base's 33.5%, confirming AVCD's advantage over alternative hallucination-mitigation strategies. SID [24], compared via alpha sweeps in Figure A.2, shows a sharp performance drop as alpha increases, while AVCD maintains robustness, achieving the best accuracy at α=2.5.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the contrastive decoding (CD) framework from Li et al. (ACL 2023) and its vision-language instantiations (VCD, SID, ICD) to the trimodal audio-visual-language setting. The key conceptual departure is replacing fixed-modality perturbation (Gaussian noise in VCD, token retention in SID) with attention-guided attentive masking that dynamically identifies less dominant modalities, and reformulating the CD logit combination (Eq. 2) into a four-term trimodal expression (Eq. 10) that simultaneously contrasts video and audio perturbations. The entropy-guided adaptive decoding mechanism is a practical addition not present in prior CD work, addressing the computational cost of multiple forward passes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As AV-LLMs are increasingly deployed in real-world applications (education, medical video analysis, assistive technologies), hallucination mitigation that is training-free and plug-and-play is practically valuable. The method's generalizability across AV-LLMs, video-LLMs, and even image-LLMs suggests it could become a standard inference-time component. However, the computational overhead of up to four forward passes per token (mitigated by EAD) remains a concern for latency-sensitive applications, and the paper acknowledges that EAD's entropy threshold may miss hallucinations occurring in high-confidence predictions—an open problem for future work.
