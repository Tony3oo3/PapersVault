## Problem

Extending re-mask decoding from 2D to 3D masked graph modeling for molecular representation learning faces two conflicting challenges: structure-dependent decoders leak 2D graph topology to the decoder, weakening the encoder's role in representation learning, while structure-independent decoders lack the 2D context needed to distinguish re-masked atoms during reconstruction. This paper resolves this tension by selectively preserving 2D structural information within the encoder's own representations rather than exposing it externally.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a 3D molecular graph G=(x, a, e) where x ∈ R^{N×3} are atomic coordinates, a are atom types, and e ∈ R^{N×N×*} are pairwise features (bonds, distances). During pretraining, a fraction of atoms are fully removed (coordinates, types, and pairwise features) and their coordinates are reconstructed; during fine-tuning, the encoder's scalar and vector outputs feed a prediction head that produces molecular energy, from which forces are derived via F = −∇_r E. The model lacks built-in equivariance and instead learns rotational/translation robustness through SO(3) rotation and Gaussian translation data augmentations.

## Method

3D-GSRD combines three components: (1) Selective Re-mask Decoding (SRD), which replaces masked-atom encoder representations with a learnable token but adds a stop-grad'd 2D graph Position Encoder (2D-PE) output to preserve 2D structural context for the decoder; the 2D-PE is trained exclusively via a cosine-similarity distillation loss from the 3D encoder (no gradient from the MGM loss), ensuring its information is fully contained within the 3D encoder. (2) A structure-independent Transformer decoder that receives no external 2D graph input, forcing all structural information to flow through the encoder. (3) A 3D-ReTrans encoder that extends Relational-Transformer with a 3D Relational-Attention mechanism (injecting RBF-expanded pairwise distances into attention weights via scalar filters) and a 3D Update Layer that jointly updates scalar features (atom types, distances) and vector features (directional geometry) with residual connections. The total pretraining loss is L_MGM + L_distill, combining coordinate reconstruction MSE with the 2D-from-3D distillation objective.

## Results

On MD17 force prediction (MAE ↓), 3D-GSRD achieves state-of-the-art on 7 out of 8 molecules, with notable results including Aspirin (0.0583), Toluene (0.0274), and Uracil (0.0292), exceeding the strongest baseline Frad by large margins (e.g., 0.0583 vs. 0.0825 on Aspirin, a ~29% improvement). On QM9, 3D-GSRD sets new SOTA on 7 out of 12 properties, including μ (0.009 D), α (0.038 a₀³), and H (5.84 meV). The backbone 3D-ReTrans alone (without pretraining) already matches TorchMD-NET on MD17, confirming the architecture's strength, while the SRD pretraining strategy provides the decisive performance gap.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On MD17, 3D-GSRD decisively outperforms the strongest pretraining baseline Frad on 7 of 8 molecules (e.g., 0.0583 vs. 0.0825 on Aspirin, 0.0274 vs. 0.0433 on Toluene), with the sole exception being Benzene where Frad (0.1355) edges out 3D-GSRD (0.1435). Against supervised baselines like TorchMD-NET and 3D-EMGP, the gains are even more pronounced, with 3D-GSRD reducing MAE by 30–50% on several molecules. On QM9, 3D-GSRD achieves the best MAE on 7 of 12 properties, surpassing both pretraining methods (Frad, SliDe, Mol-AE, Uni-GEM) and strong supervised models (TorchMD-NET, PaiNN, DimeNet++). The backbone 3D-ReTrans without pretraining already matches or exceeds TorchMD-NET on most QM9 properties (e.g., homo 22.0 vs. 20.3, lumo 17.8 vs. 18.6), indicating the architecture itself is competitive, while the SRD pretraining strategy provides the additional 10–20% improvement needed to reach SOTA. The structure-independent decoder with SRD and distillation consistently outperforms the structure-dependent decoder variant by ~5% on Toluene (0.0275 vs. 0.0291), validating the core design choice.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends re-mask decoding from 2D masked graph modeling (GraphMAE, Liu et al. 2024) to the 3D molecular setting, identifying and resolving a key tension: structure-dependent decoders (GNNs) leak 2D topology to the decoder and weaken the encoder's representation-learning role, while structure-independent decoders lack the 2D context needed to distinguish re-masked atoms. The proposed Selective Re-mask Decoding (SRD) resolves this by re-masking only 3D-relevant information and reintroducing 2D structure through a 2D graph position encoder trained via knowledge distillation from the 3D encoder, ensuring no information escapes the encoder's representations. The 3D-ReTrans encoder itself extends the Relational-Transformer (Diao & Loynd, 2022) with distance-aware attention and a scalar-vector update layer, positioning it between equivariant message-passing networks (PaiNN, TorchMD-NET) and pure Transformer architectures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The structure-leakage analysis provides a clear diagnostic for the MGM community: using GNN decoders in 3D molecular pretraining can silently undermine the encoder's learning signal, a problem that is harder to detect than in 2D settings. The SRD mechanism is shown to generalize across encoder backbones (3D-ReTrans and Relational-Transformer), suggesting it is a broadly applicable pretraining strategy rather than an architecture-specific trick. Key limitations include pretraining on only 3.37M molecules (vs. 230M in PubChemQC), absence of error bars across runs, and evaluation restricted to property prediction—open questions include whether the approach scales to larger and more diverse molecular datasets and whether the pretrained autoencoder transfers to 3D molecule generation or multi-modal molecule-text tasks.
