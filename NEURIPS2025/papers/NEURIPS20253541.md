## Problem

In online reinforcement learning, agents typically receive per-step rewards that directly link actions to consequences, but many real-world settings (LLM training, clinical trials) only provide a single reward at the trajectory endpoint. This credit-assignment challenge makes it unclear whether efficient online exploration is possible with only outcome-based feedback under general function approximation, and whether such feedback is statistically equivalent to per-step rewards. Prior work either restricted to well-structured (e.g., linear) reward functions or addressed only the offline setting, leaving the online case with general function classes open.

## Input / Output

The algorithm receives a value function class F, a reward function class R, and a comparator class G, and interacts with an MDP by executing policies and observing only a single scalar outcome reward per trajectory (the sum of all per-step rewards), rather than the full per-step reward vector. The output is a near-optimal policy with suboptimality bounded by ε + O(C_cov H² log(T) · ε_app). In the preference-based extension, the input is replaced by trajectory pairs with binary preference labels under the Bradley-Terry-Luce model, and the algorithm additionally requires a reference policy to anchor the relative value estimates.

## Method

The core algorithm (Algorithm 1) jointly optimizes Q-function and reward-model parameters via a max-min objective that balances an optimistic value estimate against a Bellman loss (with an infimum over a comparator class to handle double-sampling) and a trajectory-level reward model loss. A key technical insight is that decoupling reward model fitting from Q-function fitting provably fails (infinite sample complexity in the worst case), necessitating the joint optimization. For deterministic MDPs, Algorithm 2 eliminates the need for the reward and comparator classes entirely by exploiting the fact that the Bellman equation reduces to an almost-sure equality, yielding a simpler Bellman Residual Minimization objective. The preference-based extension (Algorithm 3) replaces the squared reward loss with a logistic loss under the BTL model and introduces a reference-policy value term to prevent over-estimation from relative-only feedback. A lower bound construction (Theorem 4) shows an exponential separation: for a 2-layer MDP with d-dimensional generalized linear rewards, per-step feedback requires Õ(d²/ε²) episodes while outcome feedback requires e^Ω(d) episodes.

## Results

The paper establishes a sample complexity of $\widetilde{O}(C_{\text{cov}} H^3 / \varepsilon^2)$ for Algorithm 1 (general MDPs with function approximation), where $C_{\text{cov}}$ is the coverability coefficient. For deterministic MDPs, Algorithm 2 achieves $\widetilde{O}(C_{\text{cov}}' H^4 / \varepsilon^2)$ while eliminating the Bellman completeness assumption. The preference-based extension (Algorithm 3) matches the $\widetilde{O}(C_{\text{cov}} H^2 / \varepsilon^2)$ rate under the Bradley-Terry-Luce model. A fundamental lower bound (Theorem 4) shows an exponential separation: $e^{\Omega(d)}$ episodes are necessary with outcome rewards versus $\widetilde{O}(d^2/\varepsilon^2)$ with per-step rewards for a specific class of 2-layer MDPs with $d$-dimensional generalized linear rewards.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no experimental baselines. The main theoretical comparison is with Algorithm GOLF (Jin et al., 2021a; Xie et al., 2022), which achieves $\widetilde{O}(C_{\text{cov}} H^2 / \varepsilon^2)$ under per-step reward feedback; the present paper's $\widetilde{O}(C_{\text{cov}} H^3 / \varepsilon^2)$ bound matches this up to an additional $\widetilde{O}(H)$ factor, demonstrating near-statistical equivalence under bounded coverability. The lower bound (Theorem 4) contrasts with prior work on linear MDPs (Efroni et al., 2021; Cassel et al., 2024) and RLHF (Chen et al., 2022b; Wang et al., 2023), showing that their reliance on well-behaved reward structure (linearity, low eluder dimension) is essential — without per-step feedback, the same problem class requires exponentially more samples. The paper also contrasts with the offline result of Jia et al. (2025), extending their statistical equivalence finding from offline to the online exploration setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the trajectory-feedback RL line (Efroni et al. 2021; Chatterji et al. 2021; Cassel et al. 2024), which was restricted to linear or well-structured reward functions, to the general function approximation setting using the coverability coefficient framework of Xie et al. (2022). It also builds directly on Jia et al. (2025), which established that outcome-based feedback suffices for *offline* RL, by tackling the harder *online* exploration problem. The Bellman loss with a comparator-class infimum (Eq. 5) follows the double-sampling correction of Jin et al. (2021a) and Zanette et al. (2020), while the preference-based extension (Section 4) connects to the RLHF literature (Chen et al. 2022b; Wang et al. 2023; Wu and Sun 2023) by replacing the reward model loss with a logistic loss under the Bradley-Terry-Luce model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first comprehensive sample-complexity characterization of outcome-based online RL under general function approximation, showing that under bounded coverability the problem is nearly as easy as per-step RL (matching GOLF up to Õ(H)). The exponential separation result (Theorem 4) is a fundamental negative finding: for certain MDPs with d-dimensional generalized linear rewards, outcome-based feedback requires e^Ω(d) samples while per-step feedback needs only Õ(d²/ε²), demonstrating that the statistical equivalence between the two feedback types is not universal. Open questions include extending the analysis to non-additive outcome rewards (e.g., product or max of step rewards), improving the computational efficiency of the max-min optimization in Algorithm 1, and understanding whether the exponential separation persists under additional structural assumptions on the MDP.
