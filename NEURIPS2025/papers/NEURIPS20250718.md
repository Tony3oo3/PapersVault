## Problem

Unsupervised class-incremental learning (UCIL) requires discovering new class structures from unlabeled streams while retaining previously learned categories, but existing clustering-based pseudo-labeling produces semantically inconsistent and poorly discriminative labels, especially for visually similar categories. This leads to irreversible knowledge corruption across incremental tasks, as early misclassifications compound and cannot be corrected in later stages.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250718/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images from unlabeled data streams (CIFAR-100 at 32×32, ImageNet-R and CUB-200 at higher resolution) through a frozen CLIP ViT-L/14 visual encoder, and additionally queries a small subset (~2%) of representative samples to GPT-4-turbo for hierarchical text descriptions during training only. The output is a unified class label over all categories discovered across T sequential tasks, with the number of new classes per task known in advance but no task identifier available at inference. Inference involves no LMM calls, maintaining standard ViT-level efficiency.

## Method

The core contribution is a dual-space pseudo-labeling strategy that combines visual K-means clustering with LMM-generated hierarchical text descriptions (via three progressive prompts from coarse object name to fine-grained attributes to common scenes) to produce complementary visual and textual pseudo-labels. A confidence-margin-based adaptive weighting mechanism dynamically balances the two modalities per sample: when labels agree, equal weight is applied; when they disagree, the modality with a larger top-1/top-2 probability gap receives higher weight. To combat catastrophic forgetting, a Semantic Alignment Distillation (SAD) loss uses frozen text-encoder embeddings of the generated descriptions as stable semantic anchors, penalizing visual features that drift away from their corresponding textual prototypes while maintaining a margin from new-class descriptors. The overall training objective combines the weighted cross-entropy classification loss, the semantic alignment loss, and a standard adapter-level knowledge distillation term, with only a lightweight linear adapter trained on top of the frozen CLIP backbone.

## Results

On ImageNet-R with 5 tasks, the proposed method achieves 81.7% Last Stage Accuracy (LA), a 29.0% absolute improvement over the previous SOTA (MSc-iNCD at 52.7%). On the fine-grained CUB200 dataset, it reaches 66.7% (5 tasks), surpassing the prior best by 33.8%. On CIFAR100, the method attains 66.1% (5 tasks), 63.6% (10 tasks), and 59.4% (20 tasks), with the relatively modest margin attributed to the low 32×32 image resolution limiting LMM description quality. Performance remains stable as task count increases, e.g., 82.2% on ImageNet-R with 10 tasks and 64.0% on CUB200 with 20 tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all five baselines across every dataset and task configuration. The largest gains are on ImageNet-R (81.7% vs. MSc-iNCD's 52.7% at 5 tasks, a +29.0 pp margin) and CUB200 (66.7% vs. 32.9%, a +33.8 pp margin), where visual similarity between fine-grained classes makes pure clustering-based pseudo-labeling especially unreliable. On CIFAR100, the advantage is narrower (66.1% vs. MSc-iNCD's 64.9% at 5 tasks) due to the low image resolution constraining LMM description quality. POCON [WACV'24] is the strongest baseline on CIFAR100 (63.1% at 5 tasks) but still trails by 3.0 pp. Notably, the method's gains are not attributable to simply using CLIP or LMMs in isolation: CLIP-based clustering alone yields only 36.9% on ImageNet-R, and LMM text clustering reaches 51.1%, both well below the proposed dual-space approach.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes CLIP's dual-encoder architecture and large multimodal model (LMM) capabilities in a novel way for unsupervised class-incremental learning (UCIL), a task previously addressed by purely visual methods (CaSSLe, PFR, POCON, MSc-iNCD) that rely on clustering or self-supervised contrastive learning without external semantic knowledge. The key conceptual departure is treating LMM-generated hierarchical text descriptions as a one-time, low-query annotation resource that bridges visual clustering pseudo-labels with a stable semantic anchor space, rather than using LMMs as a per-sample inference-time classifier. The Semantic Alignment Distillation mechanism further departs from standard visual-space knowledge distillation by leveraging the invariance of frozen text embeddings as cross-modal regularizers for incremental updates.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The approach demonstrates that a small number of LMM queries (~2% of data) can substantially upgrade pseudo-label quality in UCIL, offering a practical path toward label-efficient continual learning without per-sample LMM inference. However, performance degrades notably on low-resolution data (CIFAR100 at 32×32), and the method's reliance on a powerful external LMM (GPT-4-turbo) raises questions about accessibility and cost in resource-constrained settings. Open questions include whether the semantic anchoring strategy generalizes to video or audio streams, how it behaves under distribution shift between tasks, and whether the 29-point gap over prior SOTA persists against stronger CL-based baselines at top venues.
