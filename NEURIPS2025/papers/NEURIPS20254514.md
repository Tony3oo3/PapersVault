## Problem

Existing 3D point trackers operate in UVD (pixel + depth) or camera-centric coordinates, entangling camera motion with scene motion and relying on 2D correlation windows or triplane featurization that distort 3D geometry. The paper asks whether explicitly lifting video features into a camera-stabilized 3D world space and using true 3D neighborhood-based attention can yield more robust and accurate long-term point tracking.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254514/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes an RGB video of S frames, per-frame depth maps (from sensors, monocular estimators like MoGe, or GT), constant camera intrinsics, and optionally a camera pose sequence; query points may be given in 2D (lifted via depth and intrinsics) or directly in 3D XYZ. It outputs per-frame 3D trajectories (X, Y, Z) and binary visibility scores for each query, in either camera-centric or world-centric coordinates. Inference operates on sliding windows of T=16 frames with 1024 query points, achieving ~11 FPS on an L40S GPU with 2.6 GB VRAM under BF16.

## Method

TAPIP3D encodes each frame with a 2D CNN (initialized from CoTracker3) to produce multi-scale feature maps, then lifts each 2D cell to a 3D coordinate via unprojected depth, forming per-timestep 3D feature clouds that can be arranged in world space by applying camera extrinsics to cancel camera motion. The key architectural novelty is a 3D Neighborhood-to-Neighborhood (N2N) cross-attention: for each query point, K nearest 3D neighbors at the query frame form 'support' tokens, and K nearest 3D neighbors at every other timestep form 'context' tokens; bi-directional cross-attention between these groups (with 3D relative offsets encoded in attention values) replaces the conventional 2D square correlation windows. These multi-scale neighborhood summaries are concatenated with sinusoidal motion, projection, and timestep embeddings to form trajectory tokens, which are iteratively refined (4 steps) by a transformer with proxy tokens that predicts additive position and visibility updates. Training is supervised on Kubric MOVi-F with depth-scaled L2 tracking loss plus binary cross-entropy visibility loss, using a memory-saving strategy that detaches gradients between iterations to keep VRAM constant at ~20 GB.

## Results

With ground-truth depth on LSFOdyssey, TAPIP3D-world achieves AJ3D of 72.2 and APD3D of 85.8, nearly doubling DELTA's AJ3D of 37.7 and substantially outperforming CoTracker3 (AJ3D 28.4). On DynamicReplica with GT depth, TAPIP3D-world reaches AJ3D 55.5 and APD3D 72.8 versus DELTA's 27.4 and 37.7. On the real-world TAPVid-3D benchmark with estimated depth from MegaSaM, TAPIP3D-world achieves an average AJ3D of 18.8 and APD3D of 27.4, edging out DELTA (17.8 / 26.3) and CoTracker3 (17.3 / 25.9). On DexYCB-Pt with sensor depth, TAPIP3D attains AJ3D 30.3 and APD3D 52.4, improving over DELTA's 26.4 and 43.3.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DELTA (the strongest 3D baseline), TAPIP3D-world wins on every 3D metric across all benchmarks. The margin is dramatic with GT depth: on LSFOdyssey, TAPIP3D-world achieves AJ3D 72.2 versus DELTA's 37.7 (a 34.5-point gap), and on DynamicReplica 55.5 versus 27.4. With estimated depth on the real-world TAPVid-3D benchmark, the advantage narrows to roughly 1 point in average AJ3D (18.8 vs. 17.8), but TAPIP3D-world still leads on all three subsets. Against CoTracker3 (2D tracker lifted to 3D), TAPIP3D-world surpasses it on all 3D metrics and, notably with GT depth on LSFOdyssey, even exceeds CoTracker3's native 2D accuracy (AJ2D 78.5 vs. 75.0). Against SpatialTracker, TAPIP3D wins decisively; SpatialTracker's triplane representation degrades severely on DexYCB-Pt (AJ3D 5.5) and DynamicReplica (AJ3D 6.6–7.6), falling below even the depth-lifted CoTracker3, suggesting it cannot effectively exploit accurate depth.

## Relation to Prior Work

TAPIP3D extends the iterative trajectory refinement paradigm of CoTracker3 from 2D pixel space to explicit 3D XYZ coordinates, replacing the 2D square correlation windows used by CoTracker3, DELTA, and SpatialTracker with a 3D Neighborhood-to-Neighborhood (N2N) cross-attention over k-NN neighborhoods in a spatio-temporal feature cloud. It departs from prior 3D trackers (DELTA, SpatialTracker, SceneTracker) that operate in UVD (pixel + depth) space by lifting features into true 3D world coordinates using depth and camera extrinsics, and by optionally canceling camera motion to track in a persistent world frame. The N2N mechanism itself generalizes the 2D local all-pair correspondence idea from LocoTrack to 3D point clouds via cross-attention rather than CNNs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TAPIP3D demonstrates that a 3D-centric formulation with camera-stabilized world coordinates can substantially outperform both prior 3D trackers and even 2D SOTA trackers (CoTracker3) on 2D metrics when reliable depth is available, suggesting that explicit geometric structure is underexploited in current point tracking. The key open question is how performance degrades as depth quality drops from sensor/GT to monocular estimates, where the method's 2D accuracy can fall below purely 2D baselines. As depth and pose estimation models (MegaSaM, FoundationStereo, CUT3R) continue to improve, this world-centric 3D tracking paradigm is likely to become increasingly practical for robotics and action understanding applications.
