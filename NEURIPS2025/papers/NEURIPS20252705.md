## Problem

Existing sequential risk monitors for deployed models either require access to ground-truth test labels or assume a static model, making them inapplicable to test-time adaptation (TTA) where the model is continuously updated on unlabeled data. TTA methods can silently collapse to near-zero accuracy under severe shifts or prolonged adaptation, yet no principled framework with theoretical guarantees exists for detecting such risk violations without observing test labels.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252705/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The monitor consumes a sequential stream of unlabeled test feature batches (each of size N_k), a fixed labeled calibration set of N_cal=1000 source-distribution samples, and the adapted model's class probability outputs at each step. It produces a time-indexed lower confidence bound L_t^b on the running test risk and a binary alarm Φ_t^b that triggers when this bound exceeds the source-risk upper bound plus a tolerance ε_tol. Notably, the loss proxy (model uncertainty) and its threshold λ_k are recalibrated online at each adaptation step using only the calibration set, since no test labels are available.

## Method

The paper extends sequential testing with confidence sequences to the TTA setting in three key steps. First, it reformulates the null/alternative hypotheses to track the running risk of a sequence of adapting models p_1:t rather than a static model, yielding an adapted alarm function. Second, it derives an unsupervised lower bound B_t = τ( (1/t)Σ P(u_k > λ_k) − P(u_0 > λ_0, z_0 ≤ τ) ) on the running test risk (Proposition 1), where u_k is a loss proxy computable from unlabeled features and Assumption 1 ensures the proxy separates high- and low-loss instances robustly across shifting distributions. Third, it instantiates the proxy as model uncertainty (1 − max_c p(x)_c) and calibrates both the loss threshold τ and proxy thresholds λ_k online by maximizing F1 score on the calibration set at each adaptation step. The final alarm uses a conjugate-mixture empirical Bernstein confidence sequence on the proxy exceedance rate, providing a time-uniform probability-of-false-alarm guarantee of α_test + α_source without requiring any test labels or additional model components.

## Results

The proposed unsupervised alarm Φ_t^b triggers within <25 adaptation steps on ImageNet-C Gaussian noise severity 5 (strong shift) across all four TTA methods (TENT, CoTTA, SAR, SHOT), while remaining silent on in-distribution ImageNet and the gradual FMoW-Time shift. The unsupervised lower bound L_t^b closely tracks the supervised oracle bound L_t^a, indicating that the cost of not observing test labels is small. Assumption 1 (proxy informativeness) is empirically satisfied throughout adaptation for all evaluated TTA methods and datasets, with only minor warm-up violations on FMoW that are offset by the finite-sample penalty. The framework also detects TTA model collapse (e.g., TENT with η=1e-1 predicting a single class) shortly after the collapse occurs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the supervised oracle L_t^a (Podkopaev & Ramdas [30]), the proposed unsupervised bound L_t^b closely follows the supervised curve with only a small detection delay, confirming that the price of not seeing labels is modest. Against the naive plug-in bound L_t^c (which substitutes proxies for losses without the lower-bounding step), the proposed method is strictly superior: L_t^c lacks false-alarm guarantees, triggers a false alarm on in-distribution ImageNet for CoTTA, and fails to detect risk violations on Yearbook across all TTA methods. Against the unsupervised bound L_t^d from Amoukou et al. [1], the proposed method wins decisively on 0-1 loss (where L_t^d is 'extremely loose' and misses alarms on 3 out of 5 TTA methods at ImageNet-C severity 5) and slightly on Brier loss (where L_t^d detects 'slightly later'). Additionally, the assumption underlying L_t^d is violated in nearly every practical setting tested, whereas Assumption 1 of the proposed method holds throughout. The paper also shows that for last-layer TTA methods (T3A, STAD), switching the proxy from model uncertainty to distance-to-class-prototype recovers detection power that uncertainty alone loses due to prototype normalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the sequential-testing risk monitoring framework of Podkopaev and Ramdas (ICLR 2022) to the setting where the model is continuously adapted at test time, and further builds on Amoukou et al. (NeurIPS 2024) for unsupervised monitoring by deriving a bound on expected loss rather than merely the probability of high loss. The statistical machinery relies on conjugate-mixture empirical Bernstein confidence sequences from Howard et al. (Annals of Statistics 2021). Key departures from prior work include: (i) accommodating a time-varying model sequence rather than a static classifier, (ii) using model uncertainty (1 − max class probability) as a loss proxy requiring no additional model components, and (iii) an online threshold calibration procedure that adapts to the changing uncertainty scale induced by TTA methods like TENT.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TTA methods are widely deployed to combat distribution shift, yet their silent failure modes (e.g., model collapse to a single class) have no principled detection mechanism with statistical guarantees—this paper fills that gap with an unsupervised alarm that controls false alarm probability while detecting risk violations across diverse TTA methods and shift types. Key limitations include: detection delays relative to the supervised oracle (though small in practice), the need for a labeled calibration set, and the fact that Assumption 1 verification still requires labels. Open questions include developing unsupervised diagnostics for assumption violations, adaptive calibration strategies, and whether weaker PFA control (e.g., average run length) could reduce detection delay for safety-critical applications where late detection is costly.
