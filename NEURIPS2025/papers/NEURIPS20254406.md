## Problem

Offline Video-LLMs process entire pre-recorded videos at once and cannot handle streaming scenarios where frames arrive sequentially and require real-time, multi-turn understanding or proactive responses without explicit user prompts. Prior streaming approaches train models from scratch, sacrificing the strong general video understanding and world knowledge of large-scale pre-trained Video-LLMs, and tightly coupling activation mechanisms into the backbone causes optimization conflicts and performance degradation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254406/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework consumes video frames arriving sequentially at 1 FPS (each encoded to 33–64 visual tokens depending on the base model) interleaved with text queries at arbitrary timestamps, all stored in a memory buffer capped at 16,384 tokens. It produces multi-turn text responses to user queries, proactive text responses triggered autonomously by a binary activation signal from a compact 0.5B model (which pools frames to just 16 tokens for efficiency), and the activation model itself outputs a per-frame binary decision (respond or not) via a score head on a learnable <ACT> token.

## Method

StreamBridge introduces three plug-and-play components around an existing offline Video-LLM: (1) a memory buffer operating under a producer-consumer paradigm that accumulates frame and text embeddings over time; (2) a round-decayed compression strategy that, when the buffer exceeds MaxLen, progressively merges visual tokens from the earliest dialogue rounds via average pooling while preserving recent frames at full resolution, maintaining near-constant inference latency; and (3) a decoupled activation model (LLaVA-OV-0.5B with a binary score head and learnable activation token) that runs in parallel with the main LLM, making per-frame go/no-go decisions without interfering with the backbone's language modeling. The base Video-LLM is fine-tuned for one epoch on Stream-IT—a dataset of interleaved multi-turn video-text sequences constructed by concatenating semantically similar short clips into ~150-second videos and generating QA pairs with GPT-4o, augmented with random QA drops (P=0.55) and interval shifts (P=0.1)—supplemented with ~600K offline samples to preserve general video understanding. The activation model is trained on ~180K temporally annotated samples where only the last P% of frames per segment (P sampled uniformly from 0–50%) are labeled positive, simulating diverse activation timing patterns.

## Results

On OVO-Bench (real-time, multi-turn), the best StreamBridge model (Qwen2-VL-7B†+Stream-IT) achieves 71.30 average accuracy, surpassing GPT-4o (64.46) and Gemini 1.5 Pro (69.32) evaluated in single-turn offline mode. On Streaming-Bench, the same model reaches 77.04 average, outperforming GPT-4o (73.28) and Gemini 1.5 Pro (75.69). On the proactive ET-Bench, Qwen2-VL† achieves DVC_F1 of 38.3 and SLC_F1 of 22.6, substantially exceeding VideoLLM-Online (24.0 / 9.9) and Dispider (33.8 / 18.8). On general offline video benchmarks, StreamBridge models retain or improve upon their base models (e.g., Oryx-1.5-7B† gains +6.7 on VideoMME to 65.5).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against proprietary models, Qwen2-VL-7B†+Stream-IT outperforms GPT-4o by +6.84 on OVO-Bench (71.30 vs. 64.46) and +3.76 on Streaming-Bench (77.04 vs. 73.28), and beats Gemini 1.5 Pro by +1.98 and +1.35 respectively. Among dedicated streaming models, StreamBridge dramatically surpasses Flash-VStream-7B (+42.93 on OVO) and VideoLLM-Online-8B (+50.51 on OVO), and leads Dispider by +16.75 on OVO-Bench and +9.41 on Streaming-Bench. On the proactive ET-Bench, Qwen2-VL† achieves DVC_F1 of 38.3 and SLC_F1 of 22.6, beating Dispider (33.8 / 18.8) and VideoLLM-Online (24.0 / 9.9) on generation-based tasks, though Dispider retains a slight edge on localization F1 (TVG 36.1 vs. 34.3, TAL 27.3 vs. 24.3). On general offline video benchmarks (Table 2), StreamBridge models match or exceed their base models: Oryx-1.5-7B† improves VideoMME from 58.8 to 65.5 (+6.7), LLaVA-OV-7B† gains +3.0 on VideoMME, and Qwen2-VL-7B† reaches 64.4 on VideoMME, remaining competitive with GPT-4o (71.9) and Gemini 1.5 Pro (75.0) but trailing them on long-video tasks like EgoSchema (66.9 vs. 72.2/71.2).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on offline Video-LLMs (LLaVA-OV, Qwen2-VL, Oryx-1.5) and departs from prior streaming approaches (VideoLLM-Online, Flash-VStream, Dispider, MMDuet) that either train streaming models from scratch or tightly integrate activation mechanisms into the backbone LLM. The key conceptual departure is a fully modular, plug-and-play design: a memory buffer with round-decayed token compression handles long-context multi-turn interactions, while a small decoupled activation model (LLaVA-OV-0.5B) makes per-frame binary decisions on when to respond, leaving the main Video-LLM architecturally untouched. The Stream-IT dataset extends prior video QA and captioning datasets by introducing interleaved multi-turn video-text sequences with temporal delays, filling a gap between single-turn benchmarks and realistic streaming interaction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that any existing offline Video-LLM can be converted to a streaming assistant with minimal additional training (one epoch) and no architectural modification, lowering the barrier for deploying video understanding in robotics, autonomous driving, and real-time assistance. A notable open question is whether the decoupled activation model generalizes beyond the 1 FPS, 16k-token regime tested here, and whether the GPT-4o-generated StreamingQA-120K introduces systematic biases that limit real-world robustness. The paper also leaves open how the framework scales to higher frame rates or truly unbounded streams where even round-decayed compression may become insufficient.
