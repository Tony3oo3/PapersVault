## Problem

Existing time series forecasting models rely heavily on high-quality labels, but raw labels in real datasets are noisy and contain anomalies, and treating them as ground truth limits generalization. This paper asks whether the reliance on high-quality labeled time series datasets can be alleviated given their scarcity, and whether the potential of existing datasets can be better exploited to improve forecasting performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252802/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The input consists of a history window of L past time points, and the method outputs a forecast over a future horizon of H time points. The auxiliary reconstruction network consumes the label series and outputs a reconstructed series of the same length, which serves as a pseudo label. The output is a forecast across the future horizon rather than a single scalar value.

## Method

The core contribution is a self-supervised labeling paradigm for time series forecasting. An auxiliary reconstruction network, a convolution-FFN architecture, is trained to reconstruct the label series, and intermediate reconstructions are used as pseudo labels. An adaptive mask identifies overfitted components of the raw labels and selectively replaces them with the pseudo labels to form a corrected label series. Spectral norm regularization is applied to parameters to suppress overfitting from a loss landscape perspective. The predictor is then trained on the corrected label series instead of the raw labels, and at inference it produces forecasts using only the predictor.

## Results

SCAM+SNR consistently reduces MSE across all four backbone models on all seven main datasets. The most notable gains appear on ETT datasets: on ETTh1, PATCHTST improves from 0.469 to 0.427 MSE (−8.9%), and ITRANS from 0.454 to 0.431 MSE (−5.1%); on ETTm1, ITRANS drops from 0.407 to 0.387 MSE (−4.9%). On the large Traffic dataset, MLP improves from 0.522 to 0.494 MSE (−5.4%) and ITRANS from 0.428 to 0.411 MSE (−4.0%). Gains are smallest on Weather (e.g., CYCLENET 0.243→0.242), attributed to the chaotic nature of atmospheric data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SCAM+SNR outperforms all four baselines on nearly every dataset-metric combination. Against MLP [15], the largest relative gain is on Traffic (0.522→0.494 MSE, −5.4%), while ETTm2 shows a modest 1.4% improvement. Against CYCLENET [17], gains are consistent (1–5% MSE reduction), with ETTm2 showing the clearest win (0.266→0.262) and Weather the smallest (0.243→0.242). Against PATCHTST [27], SCAM delivers the most dramatic improvements on noisy ETT datasets (ETTh1: 0.469→0.427, −8.9%; ETTm1: 0.387→0.381), effectively closing the gap where Transformer-based models traditionally underperform lightweight MLPs. Against ITRANS [21], SCAM bridges the dataset-preference gap: ITRANS benefits substantially on ETTm1 (0.407→0.387, −4.9%) and Traffic (0.428→0.411, −4.0%), while Weather shows essentially no change (0.258→0.257). The one near-tie is ETTm2 with PATCHTST (0.281→0.281), suggesting SCAM's benefit is dataset-dependent.

## Relation to Prior Work

SCAM carves new ground by repurposing the auxiliary reconstruction task (common in self-supervised time series classification) as a parameterized dataset family for time series forecasting, rather than for representation learning. Conceptually, it bridges meta-learning's two-loop optimization structure (operating in dataset space rather than parameter space) with pseudo-label generation, while drawing on sharpness-aware minimization [10] for its spectral norm regularization component. The paper departs from prior self-supervised TSF work by treating raw labels as mutable targets that can be selectively replaced at the instance level, rather than augmenting data or learning better representations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that intermediate reconstructions from a simple convolutional network can serve as superior training targets compared to raw noisy labels challenges the standard assumption in supervised TSF that ground truth is immutable. The approach is plug-and-play across MLP and Transformer backbones and requires no additional labeled data, making it practically accessible. However, limitations include the binary (rather than soft) mask, restriction to MLP/Transformer architectures (no LLM-scale validation), and the observation that the adaptive mask collapses rapidly on real noisy data, suggesting room for more robust masking strategies. Open questions include extension to outlier detection, error correction, and whether the self-supervised labeling paradigm generalizes to other sequential prediction tasks.
