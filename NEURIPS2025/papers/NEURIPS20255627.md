## Problem

Existing diffusion-based 3D object detectors formulate detection as a 'noise-to-box' process that directly regresses bounding box coordinates in the original feature space, limiting their flexibility for promptable tasks such as grounding-based detection and few-shot detection. No unified framework exists that can adapt a single diffusion-based detector to multiple 3D detection scenarios simply by changing the conditioning input.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255627/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D point cloud (downsampled to 128 points via Farthest Point Sampling for anchor generation, with 1024 global scene features for cross-attention) and a task-specific prompt—class-name text for general detection, annotated visual exemplars plus text for few-shot, or a natural language referring expression for grounding. It outputs 6D bounding boxes (centroid x,y,z and dimensions h,w,l), deliberately excluding orientation due to annotation inconsistencies in ScanNet and SUN RGB-D. The latent box representation is projected to a 4-channel canvas of shape H_b×W_b (where H_b×W_b = N anchors) to interface with the Stable Video Diffusion U-Net.

## Method

The framework has three components: (1) a language-guided anchor generator that cross-attends visual features from a vision-language foundation model (CLIP2Point or Uni3D) with CLIP text embeddings to produce semantically enriched object queries; (2) a Box VAE built on a single-layer V-DETR module that encodes object queries and initial box coordinates into a compact latent representation via self-attention and cross-attention with scene features; and (3) a conditional latent diffusion model, initialized from pretrained Stable Video Diffusion weights, that iteratively denoises the latent box representation using classifier-free guidance (scale 3.5) conditioned on task-specific prompts. The key distinction from prior diffusion detectors is that diffusion operates in the VAE latent space rather than on raw box coordinates, enabling seamless integration of diverse multimodal conditioning signals. Training is two-stage: first the diffusion model is trained with a frozen VAE using a combined latent denoising loss and auxiliary detection loss (with scheduled weights from λ_diff=1.0/λ_det=0.2 to 0.5/1.0), then the VAE decoder is fine-tuned with detection losses. Inference uses DDIM sampling over 8 steps to refine noisy latents into final box predictions.

## Results

On general 3-D object detection, the method achieves 72.8 mAP@25 / 60.3 mAP@50 on ScanNetV2 and 67.4 mAP@25 / 50.2 mAP@50 on SUN RGB-D, outperforming all prior diffusion-based detectors (Diffusion-SS3D: 43.2, Diff3DETR: 44.9 mAP@50 on ScanNetV2) by approximately 5–16 points while remaining competitive with the non-diffusion SOTA V-DETR (65.0 mAP@50). On few-shot detection (FS-SUNRGBD 1-shot), it reaches 20.69 mAP@25 / 6.52 mAP@50, surpassing the previous best Prototypical-VAE (14.36 / 2.42) by over 6 points. On 3-D grounding, it achieves 59.5 Acc@25 on ScanRefer, 59.4 F1@25 on Multi3DRefer, and 15.7 Acc@25 on ViGiL3D, setting new state-of-the-art across all three benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On general 3-D detection (ScanNetV2), the method outperforms all diffusion-based baselines—Diffusion-SS3D (43.2 mAP@50) and Diff3DETR (44.9 mAP@50)—by roughly 16 and 15 points respectively, but trails the non-diffusion SOTA V-DETR (65.0 mAP@50) by about 5 points, likely due to V-DETR's three-layer cascade versus the single-layer design used here. On SUN RGB-D, performance is essentially on par with V-DETR (67.4 vs. 67.5 mAP@25). In the few-shot setting, the method dominates all baselines by wide margins: on FS-SUNRGBD 1-shot it achieves 20.69 mAP@25 versus 14.36 for Prototypical-VAE [68] and 12.39 for Prototypical-VoteNet [90], and on FS-ScanNet Split-1 5-shot it reaches 37.45 mAP@25 versus 32.84 for Prototypical-VAE. For 3-D grounding, the method surpasses LLM-based approaches (Chat-Scene [22]: 55.5 Acc@25 on ScanRefer, 57.1 F1@25 on Multi3DRefer) and non-LLM methods (PQ3D [96]: 57.0 Acc@25, D-LISA [88]: 51.2 F1@50) across all three grounding benchmarks, and also outperforms zero-shot open-vocabulary baselines OpenMask3D [67] and ConceptGraphs [15] on OpenLex3D without relying on external LLMs or complex multi-stage pipelines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the diffusion-based detection lineage (DiffusionDet, Diffusion-SS3D, Diff3DETR) by moving the noise-to-box process from raw coordinate space into a learned latent space via a box VAE, drawing directly on the Latent Diffusion Model framework (Rombach et al.) and initializing the diffusion backbone from Stable Video Diffusion weights. The box VAE itself is adapted from V-DETR's single-layer transformer, while object anchor generation leverages vision-language foundation models (CLIP2Point, Uni3D) with cross-attention to text embeddings. The key conceptual departure from prior diffusion detectors is that operating in latent space enables classifier-free guidance with arbitrary multimodal prompts (class names, visual exemplars, natural language), something impossible when diffusing raw 6-D box coordinates directly.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework's main practical value is unifying general 3D detection, few-shot detection, and language grounding under a single trained model by simply swapping the conditioning prompt, eliminating the need for task-specific architectures. The ~5% mAP gain over other diffusion-based detectors on ScanNetV2 and ~4% in few-shot settings validate the latent-space formulation, while the zero-shot OpenLex3D results without LLMs suggest the approach can handle open-vocabulary scenarios with less infrastructure. Open questions include whether one-step or few-step alternatives (Consistency models, Rectified Flow) can match the iterative DDIM quality at lower cost, and whether the framework extends to richer prompt modalities (audio, video) or outdoor/autonomous-driving 3D scenes.
