## Problem

Existing diffusion-based physical simulation methods assume on-grid data with preset spatiotemporal resolution and rely on standard diffusion posterior sampling (DPS), which provides no guidance at timesteps lacking direct observations. This makes them unsuitable for real-world scenarios where sensors sample irregularly in space and time, and where observations are available at only a limited subset of target timesteps, yet the goal is to reconstruct the full continuous field evolution.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254458/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes sparse, off-grid scalar field measurements at arbitrary real-valued spatiotemporal coordinates (e.g., 1–3% of the full grid), where observations exist at only a subset of target timesteps (as few as half). It produces the full-field physical dynamics at any continuous spatial and temporal coordinate, including timesteps with no observations, effectively performing both spatial super-resolution and temporal interpolation simultaneously. The output is structured as a sequence of Tucker core tensors that, combined with learned latent functions, decode to field values at arbitrary (i, t).

## Method

SDIFT integrates three components: (1) a Functional Tucker Model (FTM) that encodes sparse observations into K shared latent functions (neural networks mapping continuous coordinates to latent vectors) plus per-timestep Tucker core tensors, with a proven universal approximation property; (2) a Gaussian Process-based Sequential Diffusion (GPSD) model that uses temporally correlated GP noise (RBF kernel) instead of i.i.d. Gaussian noise, trained via a temporally augmented U-Net to denoise individual cores at arbitrary timesteps; and (3) a Message-Passing Diffusion Posterior Sampling (MPDPS) mechanism that propagates observation-derived likelihood gradients from observed timesteps to all unobserved timesteps through GP regression, yielding a closed-form quadratic guidance term that enables efficient conditional generation of the entire core sequence even when observations cover only a fraction of target times.

## Results

SDIFT with MPDPS achieves the lowest VRMSE across all three datasets under both observation settings. Under observation setting 1 (T_obs = T_tar) at ρ=1%, SDIFT w/MPDPS reaches 0.283 on Supernova, 0.146 on Ocean Sound Speed, and 0.215 on Active Matter, outperforming the best baselines (Senseiver at 0.446, CoNFiLD at 0.201, and Senseiver at 0.345 respectively). Under the harder observation setting 2 (|T_obs| = ½|T_tar|), the advantage widens: SDIFT w/MPDPS achieves 0.433, 0.181, and 0.296 versus CoNFiLD's 0.757, 0.310, and 0.827 at ρ=1%. Sampling speed is also dramatically improved, with SDIFT completing inference in 0.84–5.43 s versus CoNFiLD's 15.9–44.0 s on an RTX 4090.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SDIFT w/MPDPS outperforms all five baselines on every dataset and both observation settings. Against the strongest generative baseline CoNFiLD [5], SDIFT reduces VRMSE by 27–42% under setting 1 (e.g., 0.146 vs. 0.201 on Ocean at ρ=1%) and by 42–64% under the harder setting 2 (e.g., 0.181 vs. 0.310 on Ocean at ρ=1%). Against the attention-based Senseiver [18], SDIFT achieves 36–55% lower VRMSE under setting 1, and Senseiver is entirely inapplicable under setting 2 due to its lack of temporal modeling. Tensor-based methods (LRTFR [11], DEMOTE [38], NONFAT [27]) consistently underperform generative approaches, with DEMOTE and NONFAT showing VRMSE values above 1.0 on Supernova, indicating they cannot effectively exploit batch-level training data. The ablation comparing SDIFT w/DPS vs. SDIFT w/MPDPS confirms that the message-passing mechanism provides a consistent 10–30% improvement, particularly under setting 2 where observations are missing at half the timesteps.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: functional tensor decomposition (LRTFR, Functional Bayesian Tucker), diffusion-based physical simulation (CoNFiLD, DiffusionPDE), and diffusion posterior sampling (DPS). It departs from prior diffusion-based physical simulation work by replacing on-grid CNN/ViT encoders with a Functional Tucker Model as the latent representer, and by using Gaussian Process noise instead of i.i.d. Gaussian noise to handle irregularly-sampled timesteps. The key conceptual departure from standard DPS is the MPDPS mechanism, which propagates observation-derived guidance to unobserved timesteps via GP regression, addressing a gap that prior methods (including CoNFiLD and Senseiver) simply cannot handle.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically critical gap: real-world physical observations are sparse, off-grid, and available at only a few timesteps, yet the goal is continuous full-field reconstruction. Validating across three domains spanning light-year to millimeter scales demonstrates broad applicability, and the 10–20× speedup over CoNFiLD makes the approach practically deployable. A key open question is the absence of explicit physical law incorporation (e.g., PDE constraints), which the authors acknowledge as a limitation; integrating physics-informed priors into the FTM latent space or the MPDPS guidance could further improve long-range and wide-area reconstructions. Whether the GP-based temporal modeling scales to very long sequences or highly non-stationary dynamics remains to be tested.
