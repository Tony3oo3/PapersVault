## Problem

Multimodal large language models frequently hallucinate OCR content when processing degraded document images (blur, occlusion, low contrast), defaulting to linguistic priors instead of anchoring to visual evidence. No dedicated benchmark or training methodology exists to evaluate or mitigate this specific failure mode, leaving a gap between MLLM performance on clean documents and their reliability in real-world degraded scenarios.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252487/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives a degraded document image (identity cards, invoices, prescriptions with simulated motion blur, occlusion, or low contrast) paired with a text question targeting specific key information. The output is structured OCR text with character-level reliability annotations: fully legible characters are preserved verbatim, partially obscured but human-recognizable characters are tagged as anomalous, and unrecognizable characters are replaced with spaces (explicit refusal). A chain-of-thought reasoning phase precedes the final answer, and pixel-level ground-truth annotations in the benchmark specify which characters fall into each reliability category.

## Method

The approach uses a two-stage training pipeline on Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct. First, a cold-start SFT phase trains on a multimodal CoT-OCR dataset generated by converting image-question-answer pairs into text descriptions via GPT-4o, feeding them into DeepSeek-R1 for high-quality chain-of-thought generation, then re-pairing the textual CoT with original images. Second, a GRPO-based reinforcement learning phase applies a novel multi-objective reward function that decomposes OCR fidelity into three components: clear-character accuracy (legible text must be preserved), not-clear-character handling (partially obscured characters retained as anomalies, unrecognizable characters replaced with spaces), and final-answer accuracy. The reward is formalized as a product of per-category edit-distance scores and a penalty term for hallucinated content, enabling the model to learn uncertainty-driven refusal behavior. This rule-based reward design, combined with the CoT cold-start, allows the 7B model to achieve ~28% absolute improvement in hallucination-free accuracy over GPT-4o while preserving general OCR capability.

## Results

On the KIE-HVQA benchmark, the proposed model (Qwen2.5-VL-7B + SFT + GRPO) achieves an average OCR distance score of 58.05%, outperforming GPT-4o (30.21%) by approximately 28 percentage points. The model also leads on not-clear character accuracy (61.34% vs. GPT-4o's 36.13%) and clear character accuracy (55.45% vs. 22.78%). On standard OCRBench tasks (Table 2), the model preserves general OCR capability with scores of 180/179/183 across Scene, Doc, and Info subtasks, comparable to the base Qwen2.5-VL-7B (181/181/182).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed 7B model substantially outperforms all closed-source baselines on KIE-HVQA: it surpasses GPT-4o by ~28 points in average OCR distance (58.05 vs. 30.21), Gemini 2.5 Pro by ~23 points (58.05 vs. 35.03), and Claude 3.7 Sonnet by ~32 points (58.05 vs. 26.56). Against open-source baselines, the improvement is even more pronounced: the model exceeds Qwen2.5-VL-32B (its own base family at 32B) by 36 points and InternVL3-38B by 43 points. On the general OCRBench preservation test (Table 2), the model matches or slightly trails MiniCPM-V-2.6 (180/179/183 vs. 187/182/187 on Scene/Doc/Info), confirming that the degradation-handling training does not degrade standard OCR performance. The RL stage adds a further ~6 points over SFT alone (58.05 vs. 52.20), validating the GRPO reward design.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the GRPO reinforcement learning framework from DeepSeek-R1 to the OCR/KIE domain, introducing a degradation-aware reward function that categorizes characters into legible, partially obscured, and unrecognizable tiers. It departs from prior OCR benchmarks (OCRBench, DocLocal4K, FOX) that assume clean inputs by explicitly modeling visual degradation and hallucination risk. The cold-start CoT generation pipeline repurposes DeepSeek-R1's reasoning capabilities through a GPT-4o-mediated text conversion, bridging the gap between language-only reasoning models and multimodal OCR tasks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

KIE-HVQA fills a genuine gap: no prior benchmark systematically evaluates whether MLLMs can distinguish reliable from unreliable visual text under degradation, a critical failure mode in real-world document processing (medical prescriptions, financial records). The finding that a 7B model with targeted RL can outperform GPT-4o by ~28% on hallucination-free accuracy suggests that degradation-aware training is more effective than scaling alone for this specific failure mode. However, the evidence is limited to a single base model (Qwen2.5-VL-7B), a self-constructed benchmark, and no released code, leaving open whether the approach generalizes across architectures and to genuinely uncontrolled real-world degradation beyond simulated blur and occlusion.
