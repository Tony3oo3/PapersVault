## Problem

In multi-source transfer learning, existing methods jointly train the target model using all available source samples without selection, which constrains training efficiency and can introduce negative transfer when source-target discrepancy is large. No prior theoretical framework answers the fundamental question of how many samples should be transferred from each individual source task to minimize generalization error.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250858/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes k-shot labeled target samples (k per class), labeled samples from K source tasks, and pretrained source model parameters as input, and produces a trained target model along with per-source optimal transfer quantities. It is architecture-agnostic, operating on ViT-Small, ViT-Base with LoRA adapters, or ResNet backbones, and the dynamic resampling strategy recomputes the training subset every epoch rather than fixing it once. The Fisher information matrix is estimated empirically from gradient outer products on the current target data, requiring only the negative log-likelihood loss.

## Method

The paper formulates multi-source transfer learning as a parameter estimation problem and introduces the expectation of K-L divergence between the true target distribution and the MLE-learned distribution as a generalization error measure. Using high-dimensional asymptotic analysis, it shows this measure decomposes into a variance term (decreasing with total sample count) and a bias term (growing quadratically with source-target parameter distance weighted by the empirical Fisher information), yielding a closed-form optimal transfer quantity for single-source (Theorem 4) and a coupled optimization over total quantity s* and source proportion vector α* for multi-source (Theorem 7). The practical algorithm OTQMS implements a dynamic strategy: each epoch, it re-estimates the target parameter θ₀, recomputes the Fisher information and optimal quantities, resamples the source subset accordingly, and continues training on the updated dataset, thereby mitigating the estimation error of θ₀ under few-shot conditions. This makes the approach compatible with both full fine-tuning and parameter-efficient training (LoRA) without architectural modifications.

## Results

On the 10-shot multi-source transfer setting, OTQMS achieves 55.8% average accuracy on DomainNet and 78.2% on Office-Home (ViT-S backbone), outperforming the strongest baseline AllSources∪Target (54.3% and 77.2% respectively) by 1.5% and 1.0% on average. In terms of data efficiency, OTQMS reduces average training time by 35.19% and average sample usage by 47.85% on DomainNet compared to AllSources∪Target. OTQMS also demonstrates shot generality, consistently outperforming baselines across 5-to-100-shot settings, and extends to parameter-efficient training (LoRA) and multi-task learning scenarios.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OTQMS outperforms all compared baselines on both DomainNet and Office-Home in the 10-shot supervised setting. Against the strongest sample-based baseline AllSources∪Target, OTQMS gains +1.5% on DomainNet (55.8 vs 54.3) and +1.0% on Office-Home (78.2 vs 77.2), confirming that selecting optimal per-source quantities beats using all available source samples. Sample-based methods (OTQMS, MADA, WADN) consistently outperform model-weighting approaches (H-ensemble, MCW) by 5–15% on average, supporting the paper's claim that data speaks louder than model weighting. Against the best single-source transfer (Single-Source-Best), OTQMS improves by 7.8% on DomainNet and 5.9% on Office-Home, demonstrating the value of multi-source aggregation with optimized quantities. The dynamic transfer quantity strategy further outperforms static variants (Static-Under, Static-Exact, Static-Over) on Office-Home, with the dynamic approach achieving 78.2% vs 77.2%, 68.7%, and 71.5% respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of transfer learning theory and multi-source transfer learning. On the theory side, it introduces a K-L divergence-based generalization error measure, departing from previously adopted measures such as f-divergence, mutual information, χ²-divergence, and H-score. On the multi-source side, prior methods either use all available source samples or perform task-level selection (choosing which source tasks to include), whereas this paper explicitly optimizes the exact number of samples drawn from each individual source task. The analytical toolkit draws on classical high-dimensional statistics—MLE asymptotic normality, Fisher information matrices, and bias-variance decomposition—applied in a novel way to the sample-selection problem in transfer learning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first principled, closed-form characterization of how many samples to transfer from each source, resolving a question that prior multi-source methods handled heuristically or ignored. The practical algorithm OTQMS is architecture-agnostic (ViT, LoRA), task-general, and shot-general, and delivers meaningful data-efficiency gains (35% less training time, 48% fewer samples) alongside modest accuracy improvements. Open questions include how the framework behaves when source-target parameter distances are large (beyond the asymptotic regime assumed), whether it extends to non-parametric or deep non-identifiable models, and how it scales to very large numbers of source tasks. The reliance on pretrained source model parameters as proxies for true underlying parameters is a practical assumption that may degrade in low-data source settings.
