## Problem

Existing VLM jailbreak methods suffer from two critical deficiencies: gradient-based optimization lacks precise directional guidance and is prone to local optima, and visual and textual modalities are typically attacked in isolation, neglecting cross-modal interactions. Inspired by Eliciting Latent Knowledge (ELK) research showing that LLMs encode safety-relevant information in internal hidden states, this paper investigates whether VLMs similarly maintain an implicit safety decision boundary in their multimodal fusion-layer representations that can be exploited for more effective attacks.

## Input / Output

The method takes a raw RGB image and a text prompt (an unsafe image-text pair from MM-SafetyBench) and produces an adversarial image with an L∞-bounded perturbation (ε = 8/255) plus a 20-token discrete text suffix appended to the original prompt. The visual perturbation is optimized in pixel space via gradient descent with projection, while the textual perturbation is optimized via gradient-based token replacement in embedding space, making the two modalities fundamentally different in their optimization mechanics. The final output is the jailbroken VLM response, evaluated by whether it avoids predefined rejection templates.

## Method

JailBound is a two-stage latent-space jailbreak framework. Stage 1 (Safety Boundary Probing) trains a logistic regression classifier on the fused multimodal representation at each fusion layer to approximate the model's internal safety decision hyperplane, yielding a unit normal vector v and minimum perturbation magnitude ε per layer. Stage 2 (Safety Boundary Crossing) jointly optimizes image and text perturbations iteratively using three loss components: an adversarial alignment loss that steers the perturbed fused representation toward a virtual target region beyond the boundary, a geometric boundary loss that penalizes deviations from the boundary normal direction, and a semantic preservation loss that constrains perturbation magnitude. The visual perturbation is updated via projected gradient descent in input space, while the text suffix is updated by selecting tokens whose embeddings best approximate the gradient-desired embedding at each position, enabling coordinated cross-modal optimization.

## Results

On the white-box setting, JailBound achieves average Attack Success Rates (ASR) of 94.38% on LLaMA-3.2-11B, 91.40% on Qwen2.5-VL-7B, and 97.19% on MiniGPT-4, substantially outperforming single-modality attacks (text-only: 80.43%/66.21%/82.13%; image-only: 82.04%/54.81%/73.96%). In the black-box transfer setting, JailBound reaches 75.24% ASR on GPT-4o, 70.06% on Gemini 2.0 Flash, and 56.55% on Claude 3.5 Sonnet, exceeding the best prior method (UMK) by 24.47%, 18.27%, and 20.66% respectively. The safety boundary probing stage achieves 100% classification accuracy across all fusion layers, confirming the existence of a linearly separable internal safety decision boundary.

## Baselines & Comparisons

JailBound outperforms all three baselines across every black-box model and all 13 safety categories. Against UMK (Wang et al., 2024), which uses uniform visual noise and suffers from detectable pattern repetition, JailBound gains +24.47% on GPT-4o (75.24% vs. 50.77%), +18.27% on Gemini 2.0 Flash (70.06% vs. 51.79%), and +20.66% on Claude 3.5 Sonnet (56.55% vs. 35.89%). Against FigStep (Gong et al., 2023), which converts toxic text into typographic images, JailBound leads by +39.88% on GPT-4o and +38.87% on Gemini, though FigStep is competitive on Claude 3.5 Sonnet (47.62% vs. 56.55%). Against VAJM (Qi et al., 2024), which uses adversarial images to force universal harmful outputs, JailBound dominates by +46.79% on GPT-4o, +32.08% on Gemini, and +32.09% on Claude. In the white-box setting, the iterative joint attack {I1,T1} also surpasses the non-iterative combined attack I1+T1 by 6.13% on LLaMA-3.2 (94.38% vs. 88.25%), confirming the benefit of cross-modal gradient alignment.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Eliciting Latent Knowledge (ELK) framework from Burns et al. (2022/2023), which demonstrated that LLMs encode suppressed knowledge in their internal activations, to the multimodal VLM setting. It departs from prior VLM jailbreak methods (UMK, FigStep, VAJM) in two key ways: instead of undirected gradient-based optimization prone to local optima, it first probes a linear safety decision boundary in fusion-layer representations using logistic regression classifiers, then uses the boundary's geometry (normal vector, distance) to provide directional guidance for perturbations. Unlike SCAV, which targets internal representations but is restricted to unimodal settings, JailBound jointly optimizes both image and text perturbations in an iterative cross-modal loop, addressing the decoupling limitation of prior multimodal attacks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a simple logistic regression achieves 100% accuracy in separating safe from unsafe inputs across all fusion layers reveals that safety alignment in VLMs creates a remarkably linear geometric structure in latent space, making it exploitable with minimal perturbation. The strong black-box transferability (75.24% ASR on GPT-4o, 70.06% on Gemini 2.0 Flash) suggests that current VLMs share common architectural vulnerabilities in their multimodal fusion pipelines, raising urgent questions about cross-architectural safety alignment. However, the paper explicitly does not explore defenses against this attack vector, and the fixed perturbation budget limits understanding of the robustness landscape. Open questions include whether boundary-aware defenses (e.g., non-linearizing the safety boundary or adding noise to fusion representations) can mitigate this class of attacks, and whether the linear separability finding holds for newer, more deeply aligned architectures.
