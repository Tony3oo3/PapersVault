## Problem

Despite a long line of work on last-iterate convergence of SGD, it remained unknown whether the fast O(1/T + σ*/√T) rates known for the average iterate in the smooth low-noise regime also hold for the last iterate. Moreover, all prior analyses were restricted to stepsizes η = c/β with c < 1, leaving the critical 'greedy' choice η = 1/β—required by the randomized Kaczmarz method and continual linear regression—uncovered, with the best known last-iterate rate in that case being only O(T^{-1/4}) for OLS.

## Input / Output

The analysis takes a family of convex β-smooth loss functions f(·;z) indexed by a distribution Z, an initialization x₁, and a fixed stepsize η ∈ (0, 2/β), and produces a bound on the expected excess risk E[F(x_T) − F(x*)] of the final SGD iterate. The bound depends on the variance of stochastic gradients at the optimum σ*² rather than a global noise bound, and is dimension-independent. A key structural feature is that the guarantee holds uniformly over the full stepsize range (0, 2/β), including the boundary case η = 1/β where the coefficient (2 − βη) in the denominator becomes 1.

## Method

The proof builds on the weighted-regret technique of Zamani and Glineur for deterministic subgradient methods, extended in four key steps to the stochastic smooth setting. First, the analysis is generalized from deterministic to stochastic updates, introducing a gradient-norm term on the right-hand side. Second, the weight sequence is modified to v_t = (T−t+2)^{−(1−βη)/(2−βη)} so that the leading coefficient c_t − βηv_t² remains non-negative even for large stepsizes. Third, the standard convexity lower bound is replaced by the tighter smooth-specific inequality f(y) ≥ f(x) + ⟨∇f(x), y−x⟩ + (1/2β)‖∇f(y)−∇f(x)‖², which introduces cross terms ‖∇f_t(x_t)−∇f_t(x_s)‖². Fourth, these cross terms are controlled via a step-dependent Young's inequality (optimizing the parameter λ separately for each (t,s) pair), which is the critical ingredient that yields the O(1/√T) rate at η = 1/β and the near-optimal Õ(1/T) rate for η ≤ 1/(β log T).

## Results

For the last iterate of T-step SGD on β-smooth convex objectives with stepsize η < 2/β, the paper establishes an expected excess risk of Õ(1/(η(2−βη)T^(1−βη/2)) + ησ*²T^(βη/2)log T/(2−βη)²). With optimally tuned stepsize η ≤ 1/(β log T), this yields the near-optimal Õ(1/T + σ*/√T) rate for the last iterate, matching the best known average-iterate rate. In the pure interpolation regime (σ* = 0) with the greedy stepsize η = 1/β, the rate is O(1/√T), improving upon the previous best O(1/T^(1/4)) for OLS. For without-replacement SGD in the interpolation regime, a similar Õ(1/T) rate is achieved with optimal tuning and O(1/√T) with η = 1/β.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary contribution is the first last-iterate convergence rate of Õ(1/T + σ*/√T) in the smooth low-noise regime, matching the average-iterate rate of Srebro et al. [2010] and Lan [2012] but for the last iterate rather than an average. This extends Varre et al. [2021], who achieved the same 1/T rate for the last iterate but only for OLS in the pure interpolation regime, to general β-smooth convex objectives. For the greedy stepsize η = 1/β in the interpolation regime, the paper achieves O(1/√T), substantially improving upon Evron et al. [2025]'s O(1/T^(1/4)) which was restricted to OLS. The Liu and Zhou [2024a] last-iterate bound depends on a global noise parameter σ that can be arbitrarily larger than σ*, yielding a suboptimal O(1/√T) in the interpolation regime where the average iterate achieves O(1/T). In applications, the improved O(1/√T) rate for η = 1/β translates to better guarantees for the randomized Kaczmarz method and continual linear regression (Corollaries 4 and 5), improving upon the O(1/T^(1/4)) rates from Evron et al. [2025] and the (d−r̄)/T rate from Evron et al. [2022] in the condition-independent setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Zamani–Glineur last-iterate analysis technique (originally for deterministic subgradient methods) to the stochastic smooth convex setting, and crucially departs from prior work by handling the 'greedy' stepsize η=1/β, which was intractable for all previous last-iterate analyses that could only cover η=c/β with c<1. It generalizes the OLS-specific last-iterate results of Varre et al. [2021] and Evron et al. [2025] to arbitrary smooth convex losses, and improves the Evron et al. rate from O(1/T^{1/4}) to O(1/√T) at the greedy stepsize. The key technical departure is replacing the standard convexity lower bound with the tighter smoothness inequality (involving the 1/(2β) gradient-difference term) and applying a step-dependent Young's inequality to control cross terms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The greedy stepsize η=1/β is the natural and required choice for the randomized Kaczmarz method, continual linear regression, and projection onto convex sets, yet no prior last-iterate analysis could accommodate it; this paper closes that gap with an O(1/√T) rate that directly improves guarantees in all three applications. The remaining open question is the gap between the O(1/√T) upper bound and the Ω(1/T) lower bound in the pure interpolation regime at η=1/β, which if closed would yield state-of-the-art rates for continual learning and Kaczmarz. The without-replacement extension and the near-optimal Õ(1/T + σ*/√T) rate for tuned stepsizes further solidify the practical relevance for overparameterized model training where interpolation is the norm.
