## Problem

CNN-based backbones in sign language tasks excel at extracting contours and textures but fail to capture the collaboration of manual and non-manual features across different body regions. While SignGraph addresses this with cross-region graph modules (LSG/TSG), such cross-region aggregation can dilute and degrade fine-grained local region representations. Additionally, gloss-free sign language translation lacks an effective pre-training strategy when gloss annotations are unavailable.

## Input / Output

The model takes RGB video frames of sign language sequences, which are divided into patches of size P via a patchify stem, yielding N=HW/P² nodes per frame. For the TCTC pre-training stage, labeled text sentences are pre-processed (punctuation removal, lemmatization, word-level tokenization) to generate pseudo gloss sequences that serve as CTC targets. The model outputs gloss sequences for CSLR (via a CTC decoder) or spoken language sentences for SLT (via a pre-trained mBART translation model).

## Method

MixSignGraph extends SignGraph by inserting a Hierarchical Sign Graph (HSG) module after each LSG and TSG block. HSG constructs a static graph connecting spatially corresponding regions across high- and low-resolution feature maps (related by a downsampling factor s), then applies a GCN layer for bidirectional feature exchange, reinforcing local representations that cross-region modeling may have degraded. Unlike LSG/TSG which use KNN-based dynamic graph construction, HSG edges are determined purely by spatial correspondence. For gloss-free SLT, the paper proposes Text-based CTC Pre-training (TCTC), which generates pseudo gloss labels from text sentences and uses CTC loss to pre-train the backbone, providing a weak tokenization signal that guides the model to produce discrete, segment-aligned video features rather than continuous-valued representations.

## Results

On CSLR, MixSignGraph achieves 16.5% WER (dev) and 17.3% WER (test) on PHOENIX14, and 16.7% WER (dev) and 19.0% WER (test) on PHOENIX14T, outperforming its predecessor SignGraph by ~1.7–2.2% WER without any extra cues. On CSL-Daily, it reaches 25.1%/25.0% WER (dev/test), surpassing the SOTA TwoStream (25.4%/25.3%) that relies on skeleton and pre-training. For gloss-free SLT, MixSignGraph with TCTC achieves 51.71 ROUGE (dev) on PHOENIX14T, exceeding the previous SOTA GFSLT-VLP (43.72) by 8.0 ROUGE, and 29.24 ROUGE (dev) on How2Sign, far outperforming GloFE-VN (12.98).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MixSignGraph consistently outperforms its direct predecessor SignGraph [4] across all CSLR benchmarks: 16.5% vs 18.2% WER on PHOENIX14 dev, 16.7% vs 17.8% on PHOENIX14T dev, and 25.1% vs 27.3% on CSL-Daily dev, confirming the HSG module's benefit. On PHOENIX14T test, MixSignGraph (19.0% WER) is competitive with CrossL-Two [3] (18.5%) which uses fine-grained skeleton cues and cross-dataset pre-training, while MixSignGraph uses only RGB with ImageNet pre-training. For gloss-free SLT on PHOENIX14T, TCTC-equipped MixSignGraph achieves 51.14 ROUGE (test), surpassing GFSLT-VLP [16] (42.49) by 8.65 ROUGE and Sign2GPT [39] (48.90) by 2.24 ROUGE, while narrowing the gap to gloss-based SLT (53.84) to just 2.7 ROUGE. On How2Sign and OpenASL, MixSignGraph (I3D backbone) dramatically outperforms all prior gloss-free baselines (e.g., 28.01 vs 12.61 ROUGE on How2Sign test vs GloFE-VN [43]), establishing new baselines. On Sign2Text PHOENIX14T, MixSignGraph (55.77 ROUGE dev) edges out TwoStream [7] (54.08) which uses face, hand, and skeleton cues, though TwoStream slightly leads on test (53.48 vs 53.84). On CSL-Daily Sign2Gloss2Text, MixSignGraph (53.65 test) is marginally behind TwoStream (54.92) which leverages multiple extra modalities.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends SignGraph (Gan et al., CVPR 2024), which introduced Local Sign Graph (LSG) and Temporal Sign Graph (TSG) modules for cross-region feature extraction in sign language, by adding a Hierarchical Sign Graph (HSG) module that connects same-region nodes across multi-granularity feature maps to counteract local feature degradation. The graph construction machinery (patchify stem, KNN-based edge formation, distance function) is inherited from VIG (Han et al., NeurIPS 2022). The TCTC pre-training strategy departs from the standard gloss-based CTC pre-training paradigm used by prior SLT models (e.g., TwoStream, MMTLB) by generating pseudo gloss labels from text sentences, targeting the gloss-free SLT setting where prior work relied on attention mechanisms or contrastive language-image pre-training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The HSG module addresses a concrete architectural limitation in graph-based sign language models—cross-region aggregation diluting local representations—providing a principled fix that is broadly applicable to any graph-based backbone. TCTC offers a practical, low-cost solution for the gloss-free SLT setting, narrowing the performance gap to gloss-based SLT by a substantial margin (e.g., 51.14 vs. 53.84 ROUGE on PHOENIX14T) without requiring expensive gloss annotation. Open questions include whether TCTC's convergence difficulties with large vocabularies (noted by the authors) limit its applicability to open-domain settings, and whether the HSG design generalizes beyond sign language to other structured visual tasks. The method's reliance on RGB-only input without skeleton or depth cues makes it more deployable in real-world scenarios.
