## Problem

Existing video object removal methods suffer from hallucinated objects, visual artifacts, and heavy reliance on auxiliary priors (optical flow, text prompts, DDIM inversion) that complicate the pipeline. They also require 25–50 sampling steps with classifier-free guidance (CFG), which doubles per-step computation and makes real-time inference impractical. The paper tackles how to achieve high-fidelity, artifact-free removal with minimal sampling steps and no CFG or external conditions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252536/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a video clip (81 frames, 480p) and a binary object mask as its only inputs—no text prompts, optical flow, or first-frame conditioning are required. It outputs a video of the same length and resolution with the masked object removed and the background reconstructed. Inference runs in ~24 seconds on an RTX 4090 using 6 sampling steps and ~14 GB peak GPU memory (8 GB DiT + 6 GB VAE decode), with no CFG forward pass.

## Method

MiniMax-Remover is a two-stage pipeline built on the Wan2.1-1.3B DiT flow-matching model. Stage 1 removes all cross-attention layers and replaces text conditioning with two learnable contrastive tokens (positive for removal, negative for generation) injected into the self-attention stream, then trains with CFG on ~2.5M WebVid-10M video-mask pairs. Stage 2 distills the model via Rectified Flow to reduce sampling from 50 to 6 steps and applies a minimax objective: the inner maximization searches for adversarial 'bad noise' ε* by backpropagating toward the original (unremoved) video target, while the outer minimization fine-tunes the model on 10K human-curated successful removals plus standard WebVid data to be robust against such noise. This adversarial training eliminates the need for CFG entirely, as the model learns to suppress object regeneration intrinsically rather than relying on a separate negative-condition forward pass.

## Results

On the DAVIS dataset, MiniMax-Remover (50 steps) achieves SSIM 0.9847, PSNR 36.66, Temporal Consistency 0.9776, GPT-O3 Visual Quality 6.48/10, and a 91.11% removal success rate, outperforming the best baseline (DiffuEraser: SSIM 0.9818, PSNR 34.42, Succ 56.67%). Remarkably, even with only 6 sampling steps and no CFG, the method retains SSIM 0.9842 and 82.22% success rate. On 200 unseen Pexels videos, it reaches SSIM 0.9878, PSNR 36.98, and 81.0% success rate. Inference takes 0.18 s per 33-frame 360P video on an A800 with 8.2 GB peak memory, compared to 0.35–8.14 s for baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MiniMax-Remover outperforms all seven baselines evaluated in Table 2 on every metric across both DAVIS and Pexels. The closest competitor, DiffuEraser, trails by +0.0029 SSIM, +2.24 dB PSNR, and a dramatic +34.44 percentage points in removal success rate (91.11% vs 56.67%) on DAVIS; on Pexels the gap widens to +22 percentage points (81.0% vs 59.0%). ProPainter, the strongest traditional (non-diffusion) method, matches MiniMax-Remover on SSIM/PSNR but falls far behind on visual quality (5.68 vs 6.48) and success rate (56.67% vs 91.11%). Generative baselines relying on text prompts or optical flow (VideoComposer, COCOCO, FloED, VideoPainter, VACE) all score below 50% success rate on DAVIS, whereas MiniMax-Remover exceeds 82% even at 6 steps. In terms of efficiency, MiniMax-Remover uses 6 steps without CFG (0.18 s, 8.2 GB) versus 25–50 steps with CFG for all baselines (0.35–8.14 s, 10.4–49.1 GB). The five additional methods listed only in Table 1 (AVID, FFF-VDI, VIVID, Senorita-R, MTV-Inpaint) are compared on inference cost and conditioning requirements rather than quality metrics, all of which require CFG and text prompts that MiniMax-Remover eliminates.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Wan2.1-1.3B DiT-based video generation model and Rectified Flow distillation, but departs sharply from prior video inpainting methods by arguing that text conditioning is unnecessary for object removal. Instead of the text prompts, optical flow priors, or DDIM inversion used by methods like FloED, DiffuEraser, and VideoComposer, it replaces cross-attention with learnable contrastive tokens injected into self-attention, and introduces a minimax adversarial-noise training strategy in Stage 2 that subsumes the role of classifier-free guidance. The lineage is thus: standard flow-matching video DiT → simplified architecture (no text, no cross-attention) → adversarial distillation for robustness and step reduction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: 6 sampling steps without CFG yields 0.18s latency on A800 (vs. 1.32–8.14s for prior methods), making real-time video object removal feasible. The conceptual finding that adversarial minimax training can replace CFG while improving success rate (91% vs. 57%) challenges the assumption that guidance is essential for conditional diffusion quality. Open questions include whether the minimax noise-search strategy generalizes to other editing tasks (inpainting with generation, style transfer), whether the 10K human-curated Stage-2 dataset is a bottleneck for scaling, and whether the approach extends to longer videos or higher resolutions beyond the 81-frame/720p training regime.
