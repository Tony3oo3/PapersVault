## Problem

Occlusion—both from overlapping individuals and from fixed camera positions—remains the fundamental bottleneck in crowd counting, and passive-camera datasets cannot resolve it when crowds exceed sensing range or are fully obscured. Existing embodied navigation benchmarks are confined to small indoor scenes with limited object quantities, leaving their performance on large-scale outdoor crowds (up to 15,000 people across 40,000 m²) entirely unexplored. This paper introduces the Embodied Crowd Counting (ECC) task and the ECCD simulator to bridge this gap.

## Input / Output

At each time step the agent receives multi-camera RGB-D observations (panoramic view assembled from bottom and surrounding cameras) together with its 6-DoF pose, and outputs a 3D navigation point for the drone to fly to. The MLLM (GPT-4V) consumes the panoramic RGB images plus a chain-of-thought text prompt to produce a scalar score in [0,1] indicating crowd likelihood at the current location. The final output is an integer crowd count obtained by projecting Grounding DINO detection boxes onto the global point cloud via depth, deduplicating within 0.25 m 3D voxels, and summing the retained targets.

## Method

ZECC is a zero-shot agentic pipeline with three stages. Active Top-down Exploration (ATE) uses GPT-4V to reason about the panoramic scene and decide when to switch between high-altitude exploration (80 m, frontier-based) and low-altitude exploration (10 m, dense observation), projecting crowd density maps from a Generalized Loss estimator onto a global point cloud. Normal-line Based Navigation (NLBN) then clusters the global density with GMM (cluster size ε=40), fits a plane to each cluster via Open3D, and samples candidate view directions at a fixed angle ζ=15° from the cluster normal; the candidate closest to the ATE view vector is selected to guarantee obstacle-free, in-FOV observation, and the navigation point is placed η=8 m from the cluster center. Fine Detection and Counting (FDC) flies the drone to each navigation point, runs Grounding DINO on the close-range RGB image, projects detections to 3D using depth and pose, and deduplicates within 0.25 m voxels before summing. The key insight is reframing crowd counting as a surface-normal problem: by observing each crowd patch from a direction constrained by its fitted normal, the agent systematically eliminates the overlap and invisibility that fixed cameras cannot resolve.

## Results

On the ECCD benchmark, ZECC achieves a MAPE of 18.71±1.41% with a travel distance of 3722.45±73.78 m, substantially outperforming all ZSON navigation baselines (best baseline: CoW+GD at 46.01% MAPE). Compared to multi-view counting methods, ZECC achieves a MAPE of 18.71% using only 5 active camera captures, whereas CountFormer-10 achieves 12.8% MAPE but requires 1,735 fixed cameras. In real-world validation (Table 7), NLBN+GD reduces MAPE from 22.50% to 15.17% (Scenario 1) and from 29.31% to 19.83% (Scenario 2) over the no-NLBN baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against frontier-based exploration (FBE, Topiwala 2018), ZECC reduces MAPE from 53.38–57.19% to 18.71% (a ~35 percentage-point improvement) at the cost of ~1,200 m additional travel distance (3722 m vs. 2513 m). Against the ZSON baselines CoW (Gadre 2023) and OpenFMNav (Kuang 2024), ZECC achieves 27–42 percentage points lower MAPE while using comparable or less travel distance (3722 m vs. 3450 m for CoW, and 3722 m vs. 5070 m for OpenFMNav). Against multi-view counting methods MVF (Zhang 2019) and CountFormer (Mo 2024), ZECC achieves a MAPE of 18.71% with only 5 active camera captures, compared to CountFormer-10's 12.8% MAPE requiring 1,735 fixed cameras and MVF-10's 15.13% MAPE also requiring 1,735 cameras. In the occlusion analysis (Table 6), ZECC reduces the obstructed-person ratio to 4.52%, compared to 42.81% for OpenFMNav and 21.77% for MVC-30, confirming that the normal-line-based navigation effectively alleviates occlusion.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of two established but previously disconnected fields: passive crowd counting (density map estimation, multi-view fusion) and embodied navigation (frontier-based exploration, zero-shot object navigation). Rather than building on a single prior method, it repurposes components from both—MLLM-driven exploration planning from VLN, geometric surface analysis from 3D perception, and detection/counting models from crowd analysis—into a unified agentic framework. The key conceptual departure is treating crowd counting as an active, 6-DoF navigation problem in large-scale outdoor scenes, where the agent must autonomously choose observation points to mitigate occlusion, rather than relying on fixed or pre-placed cameras. The Poisson Point Process for crowd generation borrows from stochastic modeling literature to ensure realistic density distributions in the simulator.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ECCD is the first dataset to combine large-scale outdoor scenes (up to 40,000 m², 15,000+ people) with interactive 6-DoF camera control, filling a gap that neither crowd counting datasets (static cameras, limited scale) nor embodied navigation datasets (indoor, small object counts) could address. The efficiency argument—achieving comparable MAPE with 5 active camera positions versus 333–1735 fixed cameras in MVC baselines—suggests active exploration could substantially reduce hardware costs for real-world crowd monitoring. However, significant open questions remain: the method is validated primarily in a UE4 simulator with only two real-world scenarios, the MLLM component is shown to fail under building occlusion, and dynamic/moving crowds are explicitly left for future work. Whether the MLLM-driven altitude planning is truly necessary versus simpler heuristic rules also remains unexplored.
