## Problem

Despite sharing a common biophysical origin in dendritic currents, EEG and MEG have been modeled exclusively with separate, modality-specific and device-specific models, limiting cross-domain scalability. No prior foundation model could unify both modalities, and the heterogeneity in sensor configurations (channel count, type, position, orientation, naming conventions) across recording devices made joint pretraining infeasible.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251375/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts multichannel EMEG signals X ∈ R^{C×T} (C ranging from 19 to 306 channels, resampled to 256 Hz) together with per-sensor physical metadata: 3D Cartesian positions and orientations L ∈ R^{C×6} and discrete sensor types S ∈ {EEG, gradiometer, magnetometer}. Rather than relying on channel names or fixed topologies, the model compresses the variable channel dimension to a fixed set of 16 latent source variables via cross-attention, producing discrete tokens Q ∈ R^{16×W×4} (4-layer RVQ) that are then mapped to semantic embeddings by the BrainOmni transformer for downstream classification.

## Method

BrainOmni is trained in two stages. Stage 1 (BrainTokenizer) uses a masked autoencoder scheme where a SEANet convolutional encoder extracts temporal features, a novel Sensor Encoder embeds physical sensor properties (coordinates, orientation, type), and a cross-attention block with learnable queries compresses the variable channel dimension to 16 latent source variables; a 4-layer RVQ then discretises these into tokens, while a reconstructor (cross-attention + SEANet decoder) reconstructs the original signals from 25% channel dropout. Stage 2 (BrainOmni) applies masked token prediction (50% masking, 80% mask tokens / 20% random tokens) over the discrete token grid using a Criss-Cross Transformer that alternates spatial and temporal attention with RoPE on the temporal axis. The design is explicitly inspired by EEG/MEG source current estimation: the BrainTokenizer acts as a learned, data-driven backward solution (sensor data → latent sources) and the reconstructor as the forward solution (sources → sensor data), replacing the fixed linear inverse operator with attention-weighted, time-varying parametric operators conditioned on both signal content and sensor geometry.

## Results

On eight EEG downstream tasks, BrainOmni_base achieves the highest balanced accuracy on five datasets (AD65: 0.828, PD31: 0.748, TUEV: 0.622, FACED: 0.490, WBCIC_SHU: 0.832), while BrainOmni_tiny leads on MDD (0.886) and TUAB (0.819). On MEG and EMEG tasks, BrainOmni_base achieves 0.651 BACC on ASD74 (MEG), and BrainOmni_tiny reaches 0.863 on SomatoMotor (EMEG), outperforming the best baseline (ST-Transformer, 0.661) by approximately 20 percentage points. Zero-shot reconstruction on an unseen EEG device (PerceiveImagine) even surpasses the seen-device test set (PCC 0.802 vs. 0.748), demonstrating strong cross-device generalisation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BrainOmni consistently outperforms all four task-specific EEG baselines (CNN-Transformer, ContraWR, SPaRCNet, ST-Transformer) across all eight EEG datasets, with the largest margins on clinical tasks (e.g., +12.7% on AD65 over CNN-Transformer, +21.1% on TUEV over CNN-Transformer). Against the two EEG foundation models, BrainOmni_base surpasses LaBraM by ~11.7% on AD65 (0.828 vs. 0.711) and ~8.9% on PD31 (0.748 vs. 0.659), and beats CBraMod by ~14.7% on TUEV (0.622 vs. 0.525). The only dataset where BrainOmni does not achieve the top score is PhysioNet-MI, where CBraMod (0.595) narrowly edges out BrainOmni_base (0.590). On MEG/EMEG tasks (Table 3), BrainOmni_base leads on ASD74 (0.651 vs. SPaRCNet's 0.604), and BrainOmni_tiny achieves 0.863 on SomatoMotor, exceeding the best baseline ST-Transformer (0.661) by 20 percentage points. Joint EMEG pretraining further improves MEG performance by 12% relative BACC over MEG-only pretraining on ASD74 (0.621 vs. 0.553).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the EEG foundation model line (LaBraM, CBraMod, BIOT) by incorporating MEG signals and introducing a device-agnostic Sensor Encoder that replaces fixed channel-topology assumptions with physical sensor metadata (position, orientation, type). The temporal backbone borrows SEANet from speech enhancement, and the quantisation stage adapts RVQ from SoundStream, but the key architectural departure is the cross-attention channel compression that maps arbitrary sensor counts to a fixed set of latent source variables, explicitly framed as a learned backward solution in the source current estimation literature. Unlike prior EEG-only foundation models that assume a fixed 10-20 montage or a specific channel count, BrainOmni's design targets heterogeneous devices across both modalities within a single model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As the first foundation model to jointly pretrain on EEG and MEG, BrainOmni establishes a template for unified neural-signal representation learning and demonstrates that cross-modality training yields consistent gains for both modalities—particularly for MEG, where publicly available data is scarce (656 hours). The cross-device generalisation result (unseen EEG hardware outperforming seen hardware in zero-shot reconstruction) is practically significant for a field plagued by device-specific pipelines. Key open questions include whether the approach scales to larger MEG corpora, whether additional modalities (fNIRS, iEEG) can be incorporated, and whether the 16-latent-source bottleneck is sufficient for high-resolution MEG source localisation tasks.
