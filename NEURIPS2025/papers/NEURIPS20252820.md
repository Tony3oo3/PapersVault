## Problem

Vanilla single-network PINNs fail to converge or produce physically meaningful solutions in 3D fluid domains with high aspect ratios (length-to-depth > 5) due to vanishing gradients, imbalanced loss contributions across orientations, and corner singularities. While multi-domain PINN architectures mitigate per-network complexity, they still violate global mass conservation in complex 3D flow systems, producing physically inconsistent predictions such as zero outlet flows or inverted pressure gradients.

## Input / Output

Each subnetwork takes normalized 3D Cartesian coordinates (x, y, z) in [0,1] as input and predicts four normalized flow variables: pressure P (scaled by ρ₀U₀²) and velocity components U, V, W (scaled by U₀). Training is fully unsupervised—no CFD or experimental data is used—relying solely on PDE residuals evaluated at 20,000 Latin-hypercube-sampled collocation points per subdomain and boundary conditions on a 1 μm grid. The target geometry is a 500 μm × 100 μm × 70 μm cross-shaped channel (aspect ratio 7.14:1) with two inlets and two outlets at Re = 247.5.

## Method

The MDPINN-GD framework partitions the 3D cross-channel into nine cubic-like subdomains (100 μm × 100 μm × 70 μm), each assigned a dedicated 7-layer, 100-neuron fully-connected network with adaptive sine activation. Physical consistency is enforced through two mechanisms beyond standard PDE and boundary losses: (1) an interface-matching loss that penalizes field-variable discontinuities (velocity and pressure) across the eight subdomain interfaces, and (2) a global mass conservation loss that penalizes the normalized discrepancy between total volumetric inflow and outflow across the entire network. All subnetworks are trained jointly with a single backpropagation step over the composite loss, using a two-stage Adam (100K epochs, lr=1e-4) followed by L-BFGS optimizer. The key insight is that domain decomposition alone reduces per-network geometric complexity but cannot guarantee system-wide physical consistency; the global dynamics constraint closes this gap, yielding 74.8% accuracy improvement over single-network PINNs and 52.9% over MDPINN without global conservation.

## Results

MDPINN-GD achieves mean absolute velocity errors below 14.9% of the inlet velocity (U₀ = 2.68 m/s) across all subdomains, with the largest error (29.9 × 10⁻² m/s) occurring in the outlet subdomain 5. The method delivers a 74.8% mean absolute error improvement over the single-network PINN and a 52.9% improvement over MDPINN₂ (doubled collocation density without global conservation). Maximum absolute velocity errors are reduced by 90.2% relative to the single-network PINN, and the global continuity loss drops below 10⁻⁸ by ~1,000 epochs out of 100,000, confirming effective mass conservation enforcement.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares MDPINN-GD against three baselines: (1) a single-network PINN (10 hidden layers, 150 neurons, 250K collocation points), (2) MDPINN₁ (9 subnetworks, 20K collocation points each, no global conservation), and (3) MDPINN₂ (9 subnetworks, 40K collocation points each, no global conservation). MDPINN-GD outperforms the single-network PINN by 74.8% in mean absolute error and 90.2% in maximum absolute error, despite using far fewer total collocation points (180K vs. 250K). Against MDPINN₁, the improvement is 76.2% (mean ε) and 75.2% (mean ε_max), showing that global mass conservation is the dominant accuracy driver. Against MDPINN₂, which doubles the collocation density, MDPINN-GD still wins by 52.9% (mean ε) and 49% (mean ε_max), demonstrating that explicit physics constraints provide greater accuracy gains than simply increasing sampling density. Notably, MDPINN₁ fails to satisfy mass continuity (zero outlet y-velocity), while MDPINN₂ underpredicts velocity magnitudes in the outflow region; only MDPINN-GD captures both correct magnitudes and global conservation simultaneously.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the multi-domain PINN (MDPINN) lineage established by Shukla et al. (parallel domain decomposition), Dwivedi et al. (distributed PINNs with interface flux conditions), and Jagtap et al. (XPINNs for space-time decomposition) by introducing a global mass conservation loss (MDPINN-GD) as an additional physics constraint beyond local PDE residuals and interface matching. The foundational PINN framework of Raissi et al. provides the PDE-residual training paradigm, while the domain decomposition strategy addresses the vanishing-gradient and loss-imbalance pathologies identified by Krishnapriyan et al. and Wang et al. in high-aspect-ratio settings. The key departure from prior MDPINN work is the enforcement of a system-wide volumetric flow rate balance, which the authors show is necessary for physical consistency in 3D intersecting-channel geometries where local constraints alone are insufficient.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a simple global mass conservation constraint yields accuracy gains comparable to doubling collocation point density (52.9% vs. ~50% error reduction) suggests that targeted physics constraints can be more sample-efficient than brute-force spatial sampling, a principle potentially transferable to other multi-domain PDE problems. However, the study is limited to a single geometry, single Reynolds number, and one training seed, and the absolute accuracy (max normalized velocity error of 14.9%, with persistent z-velocity deviations) indicates the method has not yet reached the fidelity needed for design-level engineering decisions. Open questions include whether the global-dynamics enforcement generalizes to transient, multiphysics, or higher-dimensional systems, and whether adaptive loss weighting or hard boundary constraints can close the remaining accuracy gap in recirculation zones.
