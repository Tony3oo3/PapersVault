## Problem

Existing benchmarks for systematic relational reasoning, most notably CLUTRR, assume that reasoning reduces to composing relations along a single path between source and target entities. This path-based assumption is hard-coded into the architecture of several state-of-the-art models, making them perform well on current benchmarks but unable to generalize to settings requiring off-path reasoning, ambiguity resolution, or multiple concurrent relationships between entity pairs. The paper introduces NoRA to expose this limitation and drive progress toward genuinely systematic relational reasoning.

## Input / Output

Each problem instance consists of a 'story' — a set of grounded relational facts (binary predicates like school_mates_with, unary predicates like underage, and ambiguous facts encoded in Answer Set Programming syntax such as 1{r(x,y1), r(x,y2)}1) — together with a designated source entity and target entity. The model must predict the complete set of relationships entailed between the source and target, where the ground truth is computed as the intersection of relationships across all valid answer sets (refinements consistent with hidden constraint rules). Stories are represented as knowledge graphs with 20–50 entities and 30–75 facts, and the fixed but hidden world rules (284 in NoRA-full) must be induced from training examples rather than provided explicitly.

## Method

NoRA is constructed by randomly sampling story facts under a fixed set of ASP world rules (definite rules for inference, constraint rules for consistency, and ground facts), then using the Clingo solver to compute answer sets and extract entailed atoms as query targets. Four difficulty metrics — reasoning depth, reasoning width, backtrack load (BL), and off-path edge count (OPEC) — are computed per instance to create systematic out-of-distribution test splits that extend one axis beyond the training distribution while holding others constant. The benchmark deliberately breaks path-based inductive bias by including fine-grained gender-specific family roles (e.g., maternal_aunt_of) and everyday relations (e.g., school_mates_with) that require reasoning through entities not on any direct source–target path. Evaluation covers Edge Transformers, RAT, EpiGNN, NBFNet, R-GCN, and Large Reasoning Models (o3, o4-mini), with two additional datasets (NoRA v1.1 using recursive subgraph expansion, and HetioNet with biomedical entity types) to test robustness. The key finding is that all tested models, including LRM-based approaches given explicit rules, fail dramatically on off-path reasoning (OPEC ≥ 3), while ambiguity handling is less challenging than expected due to exploitable shortcuts.

## Results

On the NoRA benchmark, Edge Transformers (ETs) emerge as the best-performing models, with the multi-edge variant achieving 90.0% exact-match accuracy on the in-distribution test set and 78.5% on the Backtrack Load (BL) split. However, all models fail catastrophically on off-path reasoning: the best OPEC score is only 7.7% (ET single-edge, trained without ambiguity), and most models score below 6%. GNN-based methods (EpiGNN, NBFNet, R-GCN) are particularly weak on BL and OPEC, with EpiGNN-min(margin) scoring 0.0% on both BL and OPEC. Even Large Reasoning Models (o3, o4-mini) drop to near-zero accuracy on OPEC-3 instances despite being explicitly given all world rules.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Edge Transformers (Bergen et al., 2021) dominate the benchmark: the multi-edge ET achieves the best in-distribution accuracy (90.0%) and the best BL score (78.5%), while the single-edge ET is strongest on the depth-generalization split (74.1% on Test-D). Relation-Aware Transformers (Shaw et al., 2018) are competitive on in-distribution (90.0% multi-edge) and depth (67.6–76.8%) but fall well behind ETs on BL (54.0% vs. 78.5%). EpiGNNs (Khalid & Schockaert, 2025), the state-of-the-art on the prior STaR benchmark, perform substantially worse on NoRA, scoring only 15–16% on BL and near-zero on OPEC, confirming their path-based inductive bias is insufficient. NBFNet (Zhu et al., 2021) with BCE loss is surprisingly competitive on depth generalization without ambiguity (76.4% on D-na) but collapses on BL (1.2%) and OPEC (4.3%). R-GCN (Schlichtkrull et al., 2018) is the weakest overall, scoring 34.7% in-distribution and below 5% on both BL and OPEC. The critical finding is that no model exceeds 11% on any OPEC split, demonstrating that off-path reasoning remains an open problem across all evaluated architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground in the systematic relational reasoning benchmarking landscape by deliberately breaking the path-based inductive bias that prior benchmarks (CLUTRR, GraphLog, STaR) and their corresponding models (NCRL, R5, CTPs) are hard-coded to exploit. Rather than building on a specific method, NoRA repurposes the Answer Set Programming formalism to introduce three structural challenges absent from prior work: off-path reasoning, ambiguous facts requiring constraint-based disambiguation, and multi-relational entity pairs. The evaluation spans the full spectrum of existing approaches—Edge Transformers, EpiGNNs, NBFNet, R-GCN, RAT—and additionally probes Large Reasoning Models (o3, o4-mini), positioning the benchmark as a stress test for the entire field.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The near-universal collapse in performance on off-path reasoning (OPEC ≥ 3) across every architecture tested—including LRM models given explicit world rules—suggests that fundamentally different inductive biases or architectures are needed to handle relational reasoning that cannot be reduced to path composition. The finding that ambiguity (reasoning width) did not actually challenge models, due to exploitable shortcuts, highlights the difficulty of generating genuinely hard instances and cautions against over-interpreting benchmark scores. Open questions include whether the OPEC failure is an artifact of the specific rule structure or a deeper limitation of message-passing and attention-based architectures, and whether the NoRA v1.1 stitching guarantee (every test instance is composable from training instances) can be leveraged to design models that provably generalize.
