## Problem

Existing uncertainty quantification methods for LLMs rely on heuristic score functions—likelihood-based proxies, sampling dispersion, or ad hoc perturbation checks—without a grounded probabilistic interpretation. No principled framework has been established to justify why and how input perturbations should inform uncertainty, leaving the field without a unifying theoretical foundation for perturbation-based UQ.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253598/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a text question, a (black-box or grey-box) LLM, a semantic embedding function (e.g., SBERT or DeBERTa), and a perturbation strategy (ChatGPT paraphrasing or GAAP). It generates n perturbed variants of the question, queries the LLM r times per variant, embeds all inputs and outputs into a shared continuous space, and constructs (n+1)×(n+1) transition matrices from pairwise semantic similarities. The output is a scalar Inv-Entropy score averaged over B bootstrap replicates, with the framework also supporting alternative measures (Wasserstein distance, max conditional probability) computed from the same probabilistic objects.

## Method

The framework models input-output pairs as two Markov chains over a shared state space, where transition probabilities are defined by semantic similarity among input embeddings (P_x) and output embeddings (P_y). The key insight is an inverse modeling perspective: rather than measuring output dispersion given inputs, it quantifies the conditional entropy H(X|Y) via the composite transition P_y P_x, capturing how diverse the input space is for a given output—exploiting the inherent asymmetry where many prompts map to similar answers. Inv-Entropy is computed as the unnormalized conditional entropy over the diagonal of P_y P_x, averaged across B bootstrap samples drawn from r replicated outputs per perturbation to account for aleatoric uncertainty. GAAP, a genetic algorithm using WordNet-based keyword substitution, crossover, and mutation, generates diverse perturbations with controlled similarity to the original input. A theoretical lemma justifies perturbation-based UQ by showing that variance of model outputs along semantic invariance sets reflects gradient misalignment with the ground truth.

## Results

Inv-Entropy achieves state-of-the-art AUROC across all five datasets: 0.788 (TriviaQA), 0.740 (SciQ), 0.661 (NQ), 0.780 (MMLU), and 0.695 (GSM8K), with the largest margin on MMLU where it outperforms the next-best method (Kernel Entropy, 0.653) by 12.7 points. It also attains the best PRR on all five datasets, reaching 0.898 on MMLU and 0.885 on TriviaQA. On Brier score (lower is better), Inv-Entropy ranks among the top two methods, achieving 0.128 on TriviaQA and 0.147 on MMLU. The TSU metric (Table 2) further confirms that Inv-Entropy is the most temperature-sensitive uncertainty measure, scoring 77.55 on TriviaQA and 73.47 on MMLU at TSU(1.0, 1.4), well ahead of all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Inv-Entropy outperforms all eight external baselines on AUROC across every dataset. The most dramatic gap is on MMLU, where Inv-Entropy (0.780) exceeds Kernel Entropy (0.653), LUQ (0.648), and LexSim (0.643) by 13–14 points, reflecting the advantage of the inverse design when output information is limited. On TriviaQA, Inv-Entropy (0.788) leads DegMat (0.734) and VU (0.695) by 5–9 points. Semantic Entropy (Farquhar et al., Nature 2024) and KLE (Nikitin et al., NeurIPS 2024) are consistently the weakest baselines, with KLE scoring below 0.41 on all datasets. P(True) (Kadavath et al.) and VU (Tian et al.) perform near chance on MMLU (0.474 and 0.523 AUROC respectively), highlighting the limitations of self-reported confidence for multiple-choice tasks. Among the perturbation-based baselines, LUQ (Zhang et al., EMNLP 2024) is the closest competitor on GSM8K (0.662 vs. 0.695) and NQ (0.627 vs. 0.661), but still trails Inv-Entropy by 3–4 points. The internal ablation variants (NI-Entropy, NR-Inv-Entropy) confirm that both the inversion and the bootstrapping/replication contribute meaningfully to the final performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by providing the first fully probabilistic framework for perturbation-based UQ in LLMs, grounded in dual Markov chain (random walk) theory. It departs from replication-based methods (Semantic Entropy, Kernel Entropy) that struggle with confidently-wrong predictions, and from heuristic perturbation methods (SPUQ, adversarial paraphrasing) that lack a principled probabilistic interpretation. The key conceptual departure is the inverse modeling perspective—quantifying P(X|Y) rather than P(Y|X)—motivated by the semantic asymmetry that many distinct inputs map to similar outputs. The theoretical justification (Lemma 2.1) connects perturbation-based UQ to gradient alignment between the model and ground truth, providing a formal basis that prior perturbation heuristics lacked.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a flexible, black-box-compatible foundation for UQ that practitioners can adapt by swapping embeddings, perturbation strategies, and similarity metrics without re-deriving the probabilistic structure. The TSU metric is a practical contribution enabling UQ evaluation without ground-truth labels, broadening applicability to open-ended settings. However, the computational cost of (n+1)×r API calls per query remains a practical barrier for large-scale deployment, and the paper acknowledges that adaptive stopping criteria for perturbation are an open problem. Whether the inverse perspective generalizes beyond short-form QA to long-form generation or multimodal settings remains unexplored.
