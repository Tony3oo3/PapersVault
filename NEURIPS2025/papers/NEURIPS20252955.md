## Problem

Existing OOD detection and generalization methods operate on features as the ultimate representation of data, overlooking deeper structural constraints that govern how in-distribution features collectively behave. This paper asks whether a physics-based PDE can model the collective dynamics of high-level features under a self-consistent field, and whether the resulting basin of attraction boundary provides a principled, differentiable criterion for separating InD from OOD inputs and for quantifying model robustness.

## Input / Output

The method consumes pre-trained feature extractor outputs (e.g., ResNet-18/50 or ViT-b16 for images, or text encoders for SST-2/IMDB/Yelp) as initial particle positions in a Z-dimensional space, with uniform initial velocities. For OOD detection (CBD-De), it produces a binary InD/OOD decision by checking whether two physics-informed residual scores (Poisson and Vlasov losses) fall below learned thresholds; for OOD generalization (CBD-Gen), it produces standard class labels but with an enlarged basin of attraction. The detection variant is strictly post-hoc: all pre-trained parameters are frozen and only two lightweight MLP branches are trained on InD data.

## Method

CBD models high-level features as charged particles evolving under the Vlasov-Poisson system, where the Vlasov equation governs particle transport and the Poisson equation couples the electric field to the charge density. For OOD detection, two parallel MLP branches predict the steady-state distribution F* and potential φ*, and a physics-informed loss enforcing the nonlinear Poisson-Boltzmann equation (Poisson loss) and the steady-state Vlasov equation (Vlasov loss) serves as a differentiable uncertainty score whose magnitude indicates distance from the basin of attraction boundary. For OOD generalization, perturbations are decomposed into plane waves, yielding a dispersion relation D(ω,k)=0 whose imaginary part determines mode stability; a Fourier-domain dispersion relation loss is then added to training, suppressing unstable modes (ωi>0) and provably enlarging the basin of attraction measure (Theorem 2.4) while tightening the generalization error bound via Sobolev norm control (Theorem 2.5). The overall training loss for generalization combines the standard classification loss with weighted Poisson, Vlasov, and dispersion terms, making the framework plug-and-play compatible with existing domain generalization methods such as SAM and SAGM.

## Results

For OOD detection, CBD-De achieves 97.16% average AUROC on ImageNet-1k/iNaturalist (ResNet-50 + ViT-b16), surpassing the strongest baseline NAC-UE (95.12%) by 2.04 points. On CIFAR-100, CBD-De reduces FPR95 by 5.33% and improves AUROC by 3.92% over NAC-UE. For OOD generalization, CBD-Gen achieves 72.5% average accuracy on DomainBed (five datasets), outperforming GGA (71.7%) and SAGM (71.4%), with the largest gain on OfficeHome (71.4% vs. 70.1%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On OOD detection (ImageNet-1k), CBD-De outperforms all 10 compared baselines in average AUROC: NAC-UE (95.12→97.16 on iNaturalist, +2.04), ReAct (91.23→97.16, +5.93), VIM (92.64→97.16, +4.52), KNN (88.94→97.16, +8.22), ASH (73.85→97.16, +23.31), and GEN (92.99→97.16, +4.17). The closest competitor is NAC-UE, which CBD-De surpasses by 2.04%, 0.75%, and 0.27% on iNaturalist, OpenImage-O, and Textures respectively. On OOD generalization (DomainBed), CBD-Gen beats GGA (71.7→72.5 avg, +0.8) and SAGM (71.4→72.5, +1.1), with the largest margin on OfficeHome (+1.3 over SAGM/GGA) and DomainNet (+0.7 over GGA); it slightly trails GGA on TerraInc (50.5 vs. 50.6). When integrated with SAM and SAGM, CBD-Gen adds +3.7% and +1.4% average accuracy respectively, confirming its compatibility as an enhancement module. On text OOD detection, CBD-De achieves 90.2% AUROC, outperforming KNN (87.2–87.8), ReAct (75.7–79.5), and ViM (80.1–88.6) across SST-2/IMDB/Yelp splits.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from prior physics-inspired deep learning work (Poisson flow generative models [92,93], inverse heat dissipation [63]) that models individual particle trajectories within a learned field, instead focusing on collective behavior dynamics and the geometry of the basin of attraction boundary. It repurposes the Vlasov-Poisson system from plasma physics [67,18] as a post-hoc OOD detection and generalization framework, where high-level features are treated as charged particles whose steady-state solutions define the InD region. Unlike standard OOD detection methods that rely on confidence scores, Mahalanobis distances, or activation statistics, CBD uses PDE-consistency losses (Poisson and Vlasov residuals) as physically grounded uncertainty scores, and introduces a Fourier-domain dispersion relation loss to enlarge the basin of attraction for generalization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The basin-of-attraction formulation provides a geometrically interpretable boundary for InD data that is directly tied to a differentiable PDE, potentially offering more principled OOD detection than ad-hoc statistical thresholds. However, the practical impact is tempered by significant GPU memory overhead from storing first- and second-order gradients, and the performance gains over strong baselines (NAC-UE, SAGM, GGA) are modest (sub-1% in most settings). Open questions include whether the Vlasov-Poisson analogy is truly necessary versus simpler PDE-inspired regularizations, whether the framework scales to very high-dimensional feature spaces, and whether the multi-step PDE solver reformulation mentioned as a limitation could yield substantially better approximations.
