## Problem

Existing Bayesian methods for LoRA-adapted LLMs (e.g., BLoB, Laplace-LoRA) require complex fine-tuning, continued training, or gradient estimation to produce uncertainty estimates, limiting their practicality. This paper asks whether a trained LoRA adapter can be converted into a Bayesian one with no additional training steps at all, while retaining theoretical grounding in variational inference.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251391/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

TFB takes as input a trained LoRA adapter (matrices B ∈ R^{m×r} and A ∈ R^{r×n}) and a small anchor dataset (500 samples, which may be supervised or unsupervised and need not be the original training set). It outputs a Bayesianized adapter where B' = U diag(d) (from SVD of B) and A' becomes a product of independent Gaussians with mean M = V^T A and per-row standard deviations Ω_ij = σ_q / d_i, reducing storage from O(rn) to O(r). At inference, predictions are obtained by averaging N=10 samples drawn from the posterior, yielding both point predictions and calibrated uncertainty (ECE, NLL).

## Method

TFB constrains the variational family to low-rank isotropic Gaussians parameterized by a single scale σ_q, obtained by projecting a full-space isotropic covariance σ_q²I onto the LoRA subspace via SVD of B. The key mechanism is a binary search over σ_q that maximizes posterior variance subject to a bounded performance change (≤ ε) on the anchor dataset, requiring only forward passes with no gradient computation. The paper proves that under local convexity of the NLL and a prior with σ_p > ε_0, this variance-maximization problem is exactly equivalent to KL-regularized generalized variational inference, providing a principled justification for the training-free procedure. Unlike BLoB's freely parameterized Ω matrix, TFB's Ω is fully determined by the singular values of B and a single σ_q, making the method both memory-efficient and invariant to equivalent LoRA parameterizations.

## Results

On Llama3.1-8B with BLoB-Mean as the base adapter, TFB (training-free) reduces ECE by up to 59% (e.g., 9.37%→3.83% on Winogrande-Medium, 6.44%→2.67% on OBQA) and NLL by up to 38% (0.47→0.40 on WG-M, 0.37→0.33 on OBQA) while maintaining or slightly improving accuracy (e.g., 89.86%→90.11% on BoolQ). TFB requires no gradient computation or retraining—only 500 anchor samples and a 5-step binary search—yet outperforms training-based Bayesian LoRA (BLoB) on ECE across all six in-distribution tasks. On out-of-distribution MMLU subsets (Chem, Phy), TFB-enhanced methods maintain lower ECE than their deterministic counterparts, though the gap narrows under large domain shifts.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TFB applied to BLoB-Mean (the strongest training-free starting point) achieves the best or near-best ECE across all six in-distribution tasks, outperforming BLoB itself (e.g., 3.83% vs 4.28% on WG-M; 2.67% vs 2.91% on OBQA) without any additional training. Against Monte Carlo Dropout (MCD) and Deep Ensemble (ENS), TFB reduces ECE by 40–70% (e.g., 3.83% vs 13.05% and 11.17% on WG-M) while matching their NLL. Laplace-LoRA (LAP) achieves the lowest NLL on some tasks (e.g., 0.43 on BoolQ) but requires backpropagation and shows inconsistent ECE (1.93% on BoolQ but 12.69% on ARC-C); TFB provides more uniform calibration. MonteCLoRA is consistently outperformed by TFB on both ECE and NLL across all datasets. On out-of-distribution MMLU (Chem/Phy), BLoB retains a slight ECE edge (16.67% vs 18.45% on Chem), but TFB's advantage is that it requires zero training overhead and can be applied post-hoc to any existing LoRA checkpoint.

## Relation to Prior Work

TFB builds directly on LoRA's low-rank parameterization and BLoB's asymmetric Bayesianization scheme (modeling only the A matrix with independent Gaussians), but departs fundamentally by eliminating all training and gradient estimation. Where BLoB, Laplace-LoRA, and MonteCLoRA each require a dedicated fine-tuning or post-training procedure to learn the posterior, TFB reframes Bayesianization as a single-parameter variance search over an already-trained adapter, grounded in a proof of equivalence to KL-regularized generalized variational inference. The method also extends naturally to LoRA variants (VeRA, PiSSA) and multiple LLM backbones, positioning it as a drop-in post-processing step rather than a new training paradigm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for practitioners who already hold trained LoRA adapters: TFB adds uncertainty quantification with no retraining, no gradient computation, and only a small anchor dataset, making Bayesian uncertainty estimation accessible at near-zero additional cost. The theoretical equivalence to generalized variational inference provides a principled justification that goes beyond heuristic noise injection. Open questions remain around whether the low-rank isotropic Gaussian constraint is sufficiently expressive for complex generation tasks, how TFB behaves under very large distribution shifts (where gains diminish), and whether the anchor dataset requirement can be further relaxed. The paper also notes that performance on out-of-distribution domains (Chemistry, Physics) degrades noticeably, suggesting the method is most reliable within or near the training distribution.
