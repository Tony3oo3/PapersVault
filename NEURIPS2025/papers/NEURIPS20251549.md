## Problem

Sparse-input novel view synthesis remains challenging because NeRF and 3D-GS methods require dense views to constrain optimization, and under-observed regions suffer from occlusions and geometric ambiguities. Existing approaches that incorporate generative priors either rely on image-based diffusion models that lack spatiotemporal coherence, or require expensive fine-tuning of video diffusion models on camera-annotated data. The paper recasts the task as test-time natural video completion, asking whether pretrained video diffusion models can be used zero-shot to hallucinate plausible in-between views along a camera trajectory.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251549/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a small set of sparse RGB images (3–9 views) with known camera poses as input, where views are widely spaced and unconstrained (e.g., captured while walking with a smartphone). It produces a 3D Gaussian Splatting scene representation that supports real-time rendering at ~430 FPS, along with high-fidelity novel view renderings at arbitrary camera poses. Internally, the pipeline generates pseudo-view images at interpolated camera poses between input views, which serve as additional supervision for 3D-GS optimization; these pseudo-views are not directly output but densify the scene representation.

## Method

The core contribution is a zero-shot, generation-guided iterative pipeline coupling Stable Video Diffusion with 3D-GS. At each iteration, guidance images are created by inversely warping pixels from the nearest input view using 3D-GS-rendered depth maps (rather than direct 3D-GS color rendering), and their reliability is assessed via a cross-view consistency check combining geometric (forward-backward projection) and photometric (color matching) discrepancies. An uncertainty-aware modulation mechanism then adaptively blends the diffusion model's clean-latent prediction with the guidance features: in high-uncertainty regions the diffusion prediction dominates to hallucinate missing content, while in reliable regions the geometric guidance is preserved. The resulting pseudo-views are used to densify Gaussian primitives (via DUSt3R stereo point clouds) and to supervise 3D-GS training with LPIPS-based losses that are robust to residual temporal inconsistencies. The entire process iterates, with 3D geometry and 2D view synthesis mutually informing each other across cycles.

## Results

On DL3DV with 9 input views, the method achieves 19.19 PSNR / 0.616 SSIM / 0.335 LPIPS, surpassing the strongest 3D-GS baseline (FSGS, 15.52 PSNR) by over 3.6 dB. On LLFF (3 views) it reaches 20.61 PSNR and 0.201 LPIPS, and on DTU (3 views) it attains 20.51 PSNR / 0.840 SSIM / 0.137 LPIPS. On MipNeRF-360 (9 views), it achieves 17.91 PSNR / 0.495 SSIM / 0.435 LPIPS, outperforming the best feed-forward baseline (ViewCrafter, 16.68 PSNR) by 1.2 dB. The method supports real-time rendering at ~430 FPS via 3D-GS, compared to ~0.21 FPS for NeRF-based alternatives.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all 12 baselines across every dataset and metric. On DL3DV (the most challenging unconstrained setting), it leads FSGS by 2.4 dB PSNR at 3 views (14.62 vs. 12.22) and by 3.7 dB at 9 views (19.19 vs. 15.52), with DNGaussian and IPSM trailing by even larger margins. On LLFF, it edges out IPSM (20.61 vs. 20.44 PSNR) and FSGS (20.61 vs. 20.31), while on DTU it surpasses the next-best SparseGS (20.51 vs. 18.89 PSNR) by 1.6 dB. On MipNeRF-360 (Table 2), it beats the strongest feed-forward method ViewCrafter by 1.2 dB PSNR (17.91 vs. 16.68) and 3DGS-Enhancer by 1.7 dB, without requiring any diffusion fine-tuning. Notably, NeRF-based methods (RegNeRF, FreeNeRF, SparseNeRF) remain competitive on DTU but are far behind on DL3DV and MipNeRF-360, and all suffer from slow rendering (~0.21 FPS) versus the proposed method's ~430 FPS.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on 3D Gaussian Splatting as its 3D representation backbone and Stable Video Diffusion as its generative prior, adapting SDEdit's clean-latent-modification mechanism to the video domain. It departs from prior sparse-view NVS work in two key ways: (1) rather than fine-tuning a video diffusion model (as in 3DGS-Enhancer) or relying on image-level SDS (as in IPSM/ReConfusion), it uses a zero-shot video diffusion model with an uncertainty-aware modulation that adaptively blends diffusion predictions with 3D-GS-derived geometric guidance; and (2) it introduces an iterative feedback loop where 3D-GS geometry informs pseudo-view generation and generated views in turn densify and refine the 3D-GS, a coupling mechanism absent from prior regularization-based or single-pass diffusion-guided approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The >2.5 dB PSNR gain on DL3DV under extreme sparsity (3 views) demonstrates that video-level temporal priors can substantially outperform image-level diffusion priors for scene completion, and the zero-shot requirement (no diffusion fine-tuning) makes the approach broadly applicable to any pretrained video diffusion backbone. The iterative 3D↔2D feedback loop and uncertainty-aware modulation offer a general recipe for coupling explicit 3D representations with generative models that could extend beyond NVS. Open questions include how the method scales to very large scenes or long camera trajectories, whether the iterative overhead can be reduced, and how sensitive performance is to the quality of the underlying video diffusion model in complex or dynamic scenes.
