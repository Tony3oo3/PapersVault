## Problem

Existing degradation-robust image fusion methods rely on simplistic synthetic degradations that fail to generalize to real-world conditions, handle only single degradation types rather than composite ones, lack modeling of degradation severity levels, and offer no mechanism for user-specific control over fusion outcomes. This paper addresses these gaps by unifying restoration and fusion under a controllable, prompt-driven paradigm that handles multi-modal composite degradations at varying severity levels.

## Input / Output

The network accepts a degraded infrared image (H×W×1) and a degraded visible image (H×W×3) at 224×224 patch resolution during training, along with a text prompt specifying the affected modality, degradation type, and severity grade (1–10). Alternatively, the Spatial-Frequency Visual Adapter (SFVA) automatically extracts a visual prompt from the degraded inputs, eliminating the need for manual text specification. The output is a single restored-and-fused image that simultaneously corrects degradations and merges complementary thermal and textural information from both modalities.

## Method

ControlFusion couples restoration and fusion in a single end-to-end network using a two-stage training protocol. Stage I trains a Spatial-Frequency Collaborative Visual Adapter (SFVA) that extracts degradation embeddings from images via a dual-branch design (FFT-based frequency features + CNN spatial features) and aligns them with CLIP text embeddings through MSE and cosine similarity losses. Stage II trains the full restoration-fusion network, which uses hierarchical transformer encoders with cross-attention for cross-modal feature integration, followed by a Prompt-Modulated Module (PMM) that converts degradation prompts into per-channel scaling (γ) and bias (β) parameters applied via Hadamard product to dynamically reshape fusion features. The training data is generated by a physics-driven imaging model that simultaneously simulates sensor distortions (stripe noise, motion blur, Gaussian noise), illumination distortions (via Retinex theory with LIME-estimated illumination maps), and weather distortions (via atmospheric scattering with DepthAnything-V2 depth estimation) for both infrared and visible modalities, producing the DDL-12 dataset with 12 degradation types at 4 severity levels.

## Results

On four standard fusion benchmarks (MSRS, LLVIP, RoadScene, FMB), ControlFusion achieves the best SD, VIF, and Q_abf scores across all datasets, e.g., SD of 60.360 on MSRS (vs. 57.073 for SegMiF) and VIF of 0.968 on LLVIP (vs. 0.883 for Text-DiFuse). The EN metric is competitive but slightly lower than Text-DiFuse on LLVIP (7.354 vs. 7.546) and Text-IF on FMB (7.036 vs. 7.397). Under degradation scenarios (Table 2), ControlFusion leads on CLIP-IQA, MUSIQ, and TReS in nearly all single- and composite-degradation settings, with the largest gains in multi-modal composite cases (e.g., CLIP-IQA 0.225 for VI low-light + noise vs. 0.217 for Text-IF). On real-world AWMM-100k weather degradations, ControlFusion scores 0.280 CLIP-IQA and 60.302 TReS, outperforming all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ControlFusion outperforms all seven baselines on SD, VIF, and Q_abf across all four standard fusion datasets, with the most notable margins over Text-DiFuse (the strongest degradation-aware competitor): +0.085 VIF on LLVIP (0.968 vs. 0.883) and +0.077 Q_abf on FMB (0.730 vs. 0.653). The only losses are on the EN metric: Text-DiFuse edges ahead on LLVIP (7.546 vs. 7.354) and Text-IF on FMB (7.397 vs. 7.036), suggesting slightly lower information entropy in ControlFusion's outputs on those specific datasets. In degradation scenarios (Table 2), ControlFusion consistently leads on perceptual quality metrics (CLIP-IQA, MUSIQ, TReS) over both traditional fusion methods (DDFM, EMMA, LRRNet) and text-guided fusion methods (Text-IF, Text-DiFuse), with the largest advantage in composite multi-modal degradations where Text-DiFuse drops to 0.131 CLIP-IQA on VI(OE)+IR(LC) while ControlFusion maintains 0.187. On real-world AWMM-100k, ControlFusion achieves 0.280 CLIP-IQA and 60.302 TReS, surpassing the next-best Text-DiFuse (0.233, 57.708) by a clear margin. For object detection on LLVIP, ControlFusion attains the highest mAP@0.5:0.95 of 0.609, marginally ahead of LRRNet (0.608) and DRMF (0.607).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two active research threads: degradation-robust image fusion (DRMF, Text-IF, Text-DiFuse) and vision-language-guided image restoration (PromptIR, InstructIR, DA-CLIP). It departs from prior degradation-robust fusion by introducing a physics-driven degradation model (Retinex + atmospheric scattering) that simultaneously simulates both IR and VI degradations, narrowing the synthetic-to-real gap that simpler noise/blur pipelines ignore. It departs from prior vision-language restoration by applying prompt conditioning to a multimodal fusion task rather than single-image restoration, and by adding a spatial-frequency visual adapter (SFVA) that extracts degradation prompts directly from images, reducing dependence on manual text instructions. The prompt-modulated module (PMM) is conceptually similar to FiLM-style conditioning but is specifically designed to shift fusion feature distributions based on degradation type and severity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap: real-world IR-VI fusion systems face compound, multi-modal degradations that existing methods handle only in isolation, and the lack of user controllability limits deployment flexibility. The DDL-12 dataset (48K pairs, 12 degradation types, 4 severity levels) is a useful resource for the community. However, the approach is explicitly scoped to IR-VI fusion and the authors acknowledge it does not generalize to other fusion tasks (medical, multi-focus). Open questions include whether the SFVA's frequency-domain degradation perception transfers to unseen degradation types not in the training set, and whether the CLIP-based text conditioning can be replaced by lighter-weight alternatives for edge deployment.
