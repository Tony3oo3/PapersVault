## Problem

Text-to-image diffusion models trained on web-scale data produce outputs that misalign with human preferences, and existing DPO-based alignment methods rely on suboptimal SFT reference models that are far from ideal. This discrepancy between the SFT checkpoint and a truly aligned model limits the effectiveness of preference optimization, and the semi-supervised nature of preference data (only the preferred image has a corresponding prompt) introduces training instability.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250931/cropped_page6_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method fine-tunes pretrained diffusion models (SD1.5 at 512×512, SDXL at 1024×1024) using preference triplets (prompt, preferred image, dispreferred image) from Pick-a-Pic. A key input transformation appends the phrase 'The image that aligns less with human preferences' to the original prompt to construct a negative conditioning signal for the dispreferred image, converting each triplet into two supervised classification examples. The output is a fine-tuned diffusion model that, given a text prompt, generates images better aligned with human aesthetic and semantic preferences.

## Method

ABC (Alignment by Classification) reformulates diffusion model preference alignment as a binary classification problem, eliminating the need for a reference model during training. Two theorems establish the theoretical link: Theorem 1 shows that the Diffusion-DPO loss with an ideal reference upper-bounds the AM-Softmax classification loss, while Theorem 2 proves that the optimal diffusion model's noise prediction is a weighted average across all candidate images, making strong discriminative ability necessary for alignment. To address the semi-supervised nature of preference data, a data augmentation strategy appends a negative descriptor to the prompt, converting each preference pair into two fully supervised examples. The resulting ABC loss is derived from the Circle loss with self-paced weighting factors that adaptively emphasize suboptimal samples, driving the noise prediction error of preferred images toward zero while maintaining a fixed margin δ above it for dispreferred images, ensuring both discriminability and generation stability.

## Results

On PartiPrompts, SDXL-ABC achieves win rates of 74.4% (PickScore), 79.3% (HPS), and 80.2% (Aesthetics) against the SDXL base model, and 73.2% (PickScore) and 72.5% (HPS) against SDXL-DPO. On the HPS benchmark, SDXL-ABC reaches 79.4% PickScore and 70.2% HPS win rate over the base. On GenEval, SDXL-ABC attains the highest scores across all eight compositional tasks, including 0.5051 on Complex (3-in-1) and 0.6866 on Texture, outperforming SDXL-DPO (0.4923 and 0.6521 respectively). In the user study, SDXL-ABC earns the highest general-preference win rate of 36.5% on HPS and 33.0% on PartiPrompts among five competing methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Diffusion-DPO [51], ABC wins on PickScore, HPS, and Aesthetics for both SD1.5 and SDXL (e.g., 73.2% vs. 50% PickScore win rate for SDXL on PartiPrompts), but loses on CLIP similarity (44.97% and 50.51% respectively), suggesting DPO preserves CLIP alignment slightly better. Against SPO [27], ABC dominates on PickScore and HPS for SD1.5 (51.2% and 61.6%) and on Aesthetics and CLIP for SDXL (59.9% and 55.5%), though it trails SPO on HPS for SDXL (40.3%). Against KTO [13], ABC leads on PickScore and Aesthetics for SD1.5 (57.8% and 53.9%) but underperforms on HPS (44.7%), indicating KTO retains stronger HPS alignment in some regimes. Against MAPO [20] (SDXL only), ABC achieves a decisive 81.2% HPS win rate on PartiPrompts and 68.6% PickScore on the HPS benchmark, while MAPO edges ahead on CLIP (51.14% vs. 46.97%). Overall, ABC is the most consistent winner across the four automated metrics and both prompt sets, with CLIP being the sole metric where it occasionally trails a baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Diffusion-DPO (Wallace et al., 2024), which transplanted Direct Preference Optimization from LLMs to diffusion models, and on the broader RLHF-for-diffusion line of work (DPOK, SPO, KTO, MAPO). The key conceptual departure is reframing alignment as a classification problem: by assuming an ideal reference model, the authors prove that the DPO loss upper-bounds the AM-Softmax classification loss (Theorem 1) and that ideal alignment requires strong discriminative ability (Theorem 2). This allows them to discard the reference model entirely and instead train with a Circle-based classification loss (ABC loss) on augmented preference data, converting the inherently semi-supervised preference task into a fully supervised binary classification problem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The reference-free formulation is practically attractive because it eliminates the memory overhead of maintaining a frozen reference model during training, and the data augmentation trick (appending a dispreference phrase to the prompt) is a simple, generalizable way to stabilize semi-supervised preference optimization. The two theorems provide a clean theoretical bridge between alignment and classification, validated empirically by improved zero-shot classification accuracy of aligned checkpoints. However, the connection between discriminative strength and alignment quality remains qualitative rather than quantitative, and the paper does not address noisy or inconsistent human preference labels—both noted as open problems. The approach is most relevant to practitioners fine-tuning SD1.5/SDXL-class models on preference datasets like Pick-a-Pic.
