## Problem

While prior work has focused on training paradigms and dataset quality to improve LLM reasoning, the role of long-context capacity as a fundamental constraint on reasoning ability remains unexplored. This paper asks whether a model's ability to process and maintain long sequences directly limits its reasoning performance, and whether enhancing long-context capacity before supervised fine-tuning yields measurable reasoning gains.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252691/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a base LLM checkpoint (e.g., LLaMA3-8B-Instruct, Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, Phi-4) and applies a controlled long-context modification—either RoPE theta scaling (factors 1×–64×) or linear merging with a 1M-context variant at a specified ratio—before SFT on reasoning datasets (OpenR1-Math-220K split into short <8K and long 8K–16K token subsets, or OpenThoughts3-1.2M for cross-domain tests). The output is a fine-tuned model evaluated on both long-context benchmarks (32K NIAH, LongBench, RULER) and reasoning benchmarks (MATH500, AIME22-24, GSM8K, GPQA, LiveCodeBench, MMLU-STEM), with pass@1(5) accuracy and average output length reported.

## Method

The paper conducts a controlled empirical study isolating long-context capacity as the sole variable: models with identical architectures and fine-tuning data are compared across six RoPE theta scaling factors and multiple merge ratios with a 1M-context variant. Two extension strategies are used—direct RoPE theta scaling (NTK-aware) and linear model merging at controlled ratios (0.0–1.0) to preserve base capability while varying long-context strength. The key finding is a consistent positive correlation between 32K NIAH retrieval accuracy and post-SFT reasoning accuracy across math, science, and code domains, with peak performance at RoPE ×16 (effective context ~32K) and moderate merge ratios (0.1–0.7). The proposed 'Recipe for Reasoning Fine-Tuning' prescribes extending context length first (e.g., RoPE ×16 + 0.3 merge with 1M model), then applying reasoning SFT, yielding substantial gains (MATH500: 85.04→88.70, AIME: 15.00→28.00 on Qwen2.5-Math-7B-Instruct). Notably, gains persist on short-input tasks, indicating long-context training imparts generalizable reasoning benefits beyond merely handling long sequences.

## Results

On MATH500, LLaMA3-8B-Instruct with RoPE θ×16 (strongest 32k long-context ability, 77.05% NIAH) achieves 59.36% average accuracy after reasoning SFT, compared to 54.80% for the base RoPE θ×1 model (0% NIAH) — a 4.56-point gain. The proposed recipe (RoPE θ×16 + 0.3 merge with Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct-1M) applied to Qwen2.5-Math-7B-Instruct lifts MATH500 from 85.04% to 88.70% and AIME from 15.00% to 28.00%. Gains generalize across domains (GPQA: 37.27%→41.92%; Livecode: 25.34%→32.27%), model families (Phi-4: MATH500 88.62%→89.90%), and short-input tasks (MMLU-STEM: 71.06%→74.27%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is an empirical study rather than a method-vs-method comparison, so the 'baselines' are model variants with different long-context capacities. The key comparison is across RoPE θ scaling factors: ×16 consistently outperforms ×1 by 4.56 points on MATH500 (59.36% vs 54.80%), 2.56 points on AIME (5.45% vs 2.89%), and 1.28 points on GSM8K (86.01% vs 84.73%), while also reducing average output length by ~2,000 tokens. Scaling beyond ×16 (to ×32 or ×64) degrades long-context ability and reasoning, confirming a sweet spot. In the recipe validation (Table 7), the combined RoPE×16 + 0.3-merge approach beats both the unmodified base (85.04% MATH500, 15.00% AIME) and RoPE×16 alone (88.20%, 26.67%), yielding the best results at 88.70% and 28.00% respectively. Cross-domain and cross-family experiments confirm the trend is not architecture-specific: Phi-4 gains 1.28 points on MATH500 and 2.61 points on AIME from ×1 to ×16, and short-input MMLU-STEM improves by 3.21 points.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the NTK-aware RoPE scaling technique (Peng & Quesnelle, 2023) as its primary mechanism for creating model variants with controlled long-context capacity, and situates itself within the post-DeepSeek-R1 wave of open-source reasoning model research (OpenR1, DeepMath, OpenThoughts). It departs from the dominant framing in prior work—which attributes reasoning gains to SFT data quality, RL training, or architectural choices—by isolating long-context capacity as an independent variable while holding architecture and fine-tuning data constant. The model-merging strategy for intermediate context lengths is a practical repurposing of weight-averaging ideas, applied here specifically to create a controlled gradient of long-context ability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical recipe (extend RoPE theta, optionally merge with a long-context variant, then perform reasoning SFT) is immediately actionable for practitioners fine-tuning small reasoning models, and the finding that gains transfer to short-input tasks (MMLU-STEM) suggests long-context training may be a more general inductive bias than previously appreciated. However, the study is limited to 7B–8B models and SFT only, leaving open whether the effect persists under RL training or at larger scales where long-context capacity is already strong. The moderate effect sizes (2–4 points on MATH500, ~13 points on AIME for the recipe) and the lack of a mechanistic explanation for *why* long-context training helps short-input reasoning limit the broader impact.
