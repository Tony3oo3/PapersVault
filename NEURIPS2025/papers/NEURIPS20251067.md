## Problem

Existing human-object interaction methods either generate body-only motion or hand-only manipulation, failing to produce coordinated whole-body dynamics (bending, reaching, walking) during interaction. Articulated object manipulation further demands precise finger placement at specific regions to actuate movable parts while avoiding collisions, a challenge unaddressed by prior rigid-object or hand-only approaches. No prior work jointly synthesizes body, hand, and articulated object motion in a single framework.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an initial SMPL-X human pose (52 joint angles + root translation), a 7-DoF articulated object state (3D translation, 3D rotation, and one rotational joint angle), and a text instruction (e.g., 'A person uses the mixer'). It optionally accepts keyframe object poses for pose control or hand-only wrist/fingertip trajectories as optimization targets. The output is a temporally aligned sequence of full-body SMPL-X parameters (body + 15-joint left hand + 15-joint right hand) and the corresponding articulated object trajectory, with joint rotations encoded in 6D representation.

## Method

CoDA is a three-stage pipeline: (1) a conditional diffusion model generates the articulated object trajectory from CLIP text features, initial pose, and part-based BPS geometry; (2) a second diffusion model predicts end-effector trajectories using a unified BPS representation where wrist and fingertip positions are encoded as distances to the same shared basis points used for object geometry, with RoPE-based encoding of relative object motion for temporal awareness; (3) whole-body motion is synthesized by optimizing the noise inputs of three decoupled diffusion models (body, left hand, right hand) via DDIM sampling, where gradients from end-effector tracking, penetration, and foot-contact losses flow through the forward-kinematics chain to coordinate global posture with hand motion. The decoupled design allows each model to be trained on its own data source (AMASS for body, ARCTIC/GRAB for hands), improving generalization, while the noise-space optimization preserves natural motion priors and avoids high-dimensional pose regression.

## Results

On the ARCTIC articulated-object benchmark, CoDA achieves the best FID (2.283), R-Precision (0.477), interpenetration volume (5.25), and contact ratio (0.086) among all baselines, with foot skating matching the real-data level (0.002). On GRAB (rigid objects), CoDA similarly leads in FID (21.544), R-Precision (0.438), IV (4.93), and ID (10.23). In a user study with 16 participants, CoDA is rated best for motion realism (88.7%) and physical plausibility (87.3%), and in a physics-simulator tracking test it sustains 8.75 s of stable tracking versus 4.75 s for OMOMO.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoDA outperforms all six baselines on the primary FID metric on ARCTIC (2.283 vs. 2.873 for the closest competitor OMOMO+DNO, 3.710 for OMOMO, and 3.758 for CHOIS). It also leads on R-Precision (0.477 vs. 0.406 for OMOMO and 0.391 for OMOMO+DNO) and interpenetration volume (5.25 vs. 8.95 for OMOMO+DNO and 13.77 for OMOMO), indicating substantially better text alignment and reduced hand-object penetration. The only metric where CoDA trails is Diversity (7.208 vs. 8.167 for MDM and 8.664 for real data), suggesting slightly less sample variation. On GRAB, CoDA extends its lead with FID 21.544 vs. 25.017 (OMOMO) and IV 4.93 vs. 11.03 (OMOMO), while also achieving the best contact ratio (0.040 vs. 0.004 for OMOMO). In the user study, CoDA dominates with 88.7% best-motion-realism votes compared to 10.2% for OMOMO and 1.1% for CHOIS, and in the IsaacGym tracking test it sustains 8.75 s of stable execution versus 4.75 s for OMOMO, confirming superior physical plausibility for downstream humanoid deployment.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the diffusion noise optimization framework of DNO (Karunratanakul et al., CVPR 2024), which showed that optimizing input noise to a trained diffusion model can serve as a universal motion prior, and extends it to a multi-model, multi-component setting. It adapts the RoPE-based relative pose encoding from CaPE (Kong et al., CVPR 2024) to encode articulated object trajectories, and leverages the normalized part-based BPS representation from BimART (Zhang et al., CVPR 2025) for object geometry. The key departure from prior work is the decoupled design—separating body, left hand, and right hand into independent diffusion models trained on different datasets (AMASS, ARCTIC, GRAB)—combined with a unified BPS encoding that places end-effectors in the same distance space as object parts, enabling gradient-based coordination through the kinematic chain rather than direct pose regression.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to jointly synthesize body, hand, and articulated object motion, filling a clear gap between body-only HOI methods and hand-only manipulation approaches. The ability to generate simultaneous locomotion and manipulation, control keyframe object poses, and leverage hand-only datasets for whole-body generation broadens the practical applicability to robotics and virtual human animation. However, the ~17-minute inference time for a 300-frame sequence severely limits real-time use, and the method is restricted to single-object manipulation with limited generalization to novel object categories. Open questions include extending to multi-object or sequential interactions, obstacle-aware planning, and reducing optimization cost toward real-time performance.
