## Problem

Transformed MSE regression models (e.g., LogMSE) are widely used in recommender systems to handle highly skewed targets, but back-transforming predictions introduces systematic retransformation bias (underestimation for convex inverse transforms) that has been conspicuously neglected in the community. Existing bias corrections are post-hoc, applied externally after training, and face practical challenges in industrial deployment. The paper asks whether the bias can be eradicated intrinsically during training via minor model refinement.

## Input / Output

The method takes tabular user/item features x and a continuous target label y (e.g., GMV, watch time) as input, with y transformed by a bijective mapping T (e.g., log, sqrt) during training. At inference, it produces an unbiased point prediction in the original y-space by multiplying the auxiliary branch output z(x;θ_z) with the back-transformed main-branch prediction. The stop-gradient operation on the denominator of the bias loss decouples the two learning objectives, so the main branch still converges to E[T(y)|x] while the auxiliary branch corrects the retransformation gap.

## Method

TranSUN augments a transformed MSE model with an auxiliary branch z(x;θ_z) that learns the multiplicative ratio y / (|T^{-1}(f(x;θ))| + ε) under a stop-gradient on the denominator, so the total loss L_MSE^T + L_sun^T decomposes into two independent optimizations. The key insight is that this specific multiplicative scheme (learning y/prediction, not prediction/y) is what guarantees theoretical unbiasedness: minimizing L_sun^T forces z(x;θ_z)·(|T^{-1}(f(x;θ))|+ε) = E[y|x]. The method is generalized into Generalized TranSUN (GTS), which replaces the fixed MSE loss with a conditional point loss L_Hq supervising f to learn an arbitrary conditional point estimate Q[T(y)|x], and introduces a parameter-free function κ(f(x;θ_q)) as a dynamically generated slope, yielding a conditional linear-transformed MSE whose model assumption is −z(x;θ_z) + Y_x·κ(Q) ~ N(0,σ²). GTS serves as a plug-and-play debiasing framework applicable to any regression model, even those without target transformation.

## Results

On CIKM16 test, LogSUN achieves the best TRE (0.0123), MRE (0.0439), NRMSE (0.5625), and NMAE (0.4333), outperforming all baselines including TPM (TRE 0.0278) and CREAD (TRE 0.0232). On DTMart test, LogSUN similarly leads with TRE 0.0731, MRE 0.0790, NRMSE 0.4151, and NMAE 0.1994. On the industrial Indus dataset, LogSUN achieves TRE 0.0197 and NRMSE 2.2765 on the full test, and the best XAUC (0.6749) on the high-value Top test. Online A/B tests on Taobao (DAU > 300M) confirm +1.02% to +1.83% GMV gains over LogMSE/LogMAE baselines with p-values < 0.02.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LogSUN decisively outperforms all value-regression baselines (MSE, MAE, LogMSE, SqrtMSE, ZILN, OptDist) on both TRE and MRE, with the largest gaps against LogMSE (TRE 0.0123 vs 0.3451 on CIKM16) and ZILN (TRE 0.0123 vs 0.4391). Against classification-based baselines TPM and CREAD, LogSUN achieves lower TRE (0.0123 vs 0.0278/0.0232) and MRE (0.0439 vs 0.0548/0.0639) on CIKM16, though TPM and CREAD marginally lead on overall XAUC (0.6758/0.6753 vs 0.6740) — a trade-off the authors attribute to XAUC being value-agnostic and dominated by abundant low-value samples. Against MDME, LogSUN wins on every metric (TRE 0.0123 vs 0.4448, NRMSE 0.5625 vs 1.5749). In the post-hoc correction comparison (Table 4), LogSUN (TRE 0.0123) beats Smearing (0.0175), SIR (0.0198), and NTE (0.1262) without requiring any post-processing step. On the industrial Indus dataset, LogSUN achieves the best XAUC on high-value Top samples (0.6749 vs 0.6730 for TPM), confirming its advantage where business impact is concentrated.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the established paradigm of post-hoc bias correction (NTE, Smearing, SIR) by proposing an intrinsic, training-time solution to retransformation bias in transformed MSE regression models. While retransformation bias has been well-studied in statistics and ecology since Bartlett (1936) and Duan (1983), the paper explicitly positions itself as the first work to address this problem within the recommender system community, where transformed MSE (e.g., LogMSE) is prevalent for handling right-skewed targets like GMV and watch time. The GTS framework draws conceptual inspiration from Conditional Transformation Models (CTM/CLTM) but differs in its model assumption: the slope κ is conditioned on both x and a point estimate of y, rather than solely on x, making it a value regression model for unbiased point estimation rather than an interval estimation tool. The method also generalizes beyond transformed MSE, serving as a plug-and-play debiasing module for arbitrary regression models including classification-based approaches (TPM, MDME) and distributional models (ZILN, OptDist).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: LogSUN is deployed in two core recommendation scenarios on Taobao (DAU > 300M) with statistically significant >1% GMV gains, demonstrating that a long-neglected bias issue has real business consequences. The GTS framework provides a generic, theoretically grounded template for constructing bias-free regression models, which could influence how the community designs regression objectives for skewed targets. Key limitations include the XAUC trade-off (explained via the value-agnostic nature of XAUC on right-skewed data), the restriction to right-skewed targets in validation, and the open question of how to automatically select the optimal point estimate Q and function κ for arbitrary data distributions. The finding that not all intuitive bias learning schemes (e.g., learning the inverse ratio) are theoretically unbiased, despite appearing symmetric to the correct scheme, is a useful caution for practitioners designing auxiliary losses.
