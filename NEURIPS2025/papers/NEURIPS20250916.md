## Problem

Differentially private bilevel optimization (DP BLO) is a fundamental but underexplored problem: prior work either considered only local DP without guarantees in the important ε=O(1) regime, or provided nonconvex rates that degrade with the inner dimension d_y. The minimax optimal error rates for DP bilevel ERM and bilevel stochastic optimization were completely open, and it was unclear whether the nested structure inherently worsens privacy-utility tradeoffs compared to single-level DP optimization.

## Input / Output

The algorithms consume a dataset Z=(z_1,…,z_n), upper-level loss f(x,y,z), lower-level loss g(x,y,z) (strongly convex in y), and privacy parameters (ε,δ). A critical practical constraint is that the algorithm never has exact access to ŷ*_Z(x); it only obtains approximate inner solutions, so all function evaluations of the bilevel objective Φ̂_Z(x) are inexact. Outputs include the upper-level solution x̂ with explicit excess empirical risk bounds (for ERM) or excess population risk bounds (for SO), and in the nonconvex setting, an approximate stationary point with a gradient-norm guarantee independent of d_y.

## Method

For convex DP BLO, the paper instantiates the exponential mechanism (pure ε-DP) and the regularized exponential mechanism ((ε,δ)-DP) over the bilevel objective, then provides a polynomial-time implementation via a novel analysis of log-concave sampling under inexact function evaluations: it bounds the conductance degradation (factor e^{-6ζ}), the L∞ distributional distance (2ζ), and the mixing-time inflation (factor e^{12ζ}) of the grid-walk Markov chain when the target function is perturbed by bounded errors ζ. For nonconvex DP BLO, the key insight is to solve the inner problem non-privately (via SGD or Katyusha) to high accuracy α, then add Gaussian noise only to the second-order gradient approximation ∇̄F̂_Z(x_t, y_{t+1}), which yields a d_y-independent sensitivity bound of 4K/n through a careful operator-norm perturbation analysis of the Hessian inverse. A two-stage warm-start algorithm further improves the rate in the regime d_x < nε by first running the exponential mechanism at ε/2 to obtain a good starting point, then running the second-order method at ε/2.

## Results

For convex DP bilevel ERM, the paper achieves excess empirical risk of Õ(d_x / εn) under pure ε-DP and O(√(d_x log(1/δ)) / εn) under (ε,δ)-DP, matching the optimal single-level DP-ERM rates up to additional bilevel-specific terms (e.g., L_{f,y}D_y and L_{f,y}L_{g,y}/μ_g). For nonconvex DP bilevel ERM, Algorithm 1 achieves E‖∇Φ̂_Z(x̂)‖ ≤ Õ((√d_x / εn)^{1/2}), which is independent of the inner dimension d_y, and Algorithm 2 (with exponential-mechanism warm start) further improves to Õ(√d_x / (εn)^{3/4}) in the regime d_x < nε. Matching lower bounds are provided, establishing near-tightness of the convex rates and a novel separation showing DP BLO is inherently harder than single-level DP optimization when L_{f,y}D_y > L_{f,x}D_x.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is with Kornowski [28], the only prior central-DP nonconvex BLO work, whose bound (2) scales as Õ((√d_x/εn)^{1/2} + (√d_y/εn)^{1/3}). The new Algorithm 1 strictly improves this by eliminating the d_y-dependent term, yielding Õ((√d_x/εn)^{1/2}) regardless of the inner dimension — a significant gain when d_y ≫ d_x. For the convex setting, the upper bounds nearly match the optimal single-level DP-ERM rates of Bassily et al. [9] (e.g., Θ(L_{f,x}D_x√(d_x log(1/δ))/εn) for (ε,δ)-DP), with only additive terms capturing bilevel complexity. The lower bound in Theorem 3.10 establishes that the L_{f,y}D_y term is unavoidable, proving a separation from single-level DP optimization. The warm-start Algorithm 2 builds on the framework of Lowy et al. [37] to achieve the improved (εn)^{-3/4} rate, which is state-of-the-art for DP nonconvex bilevel finite-sum optimization (Eq. 3).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two well-developed lines of work—differentially private optimization and bilevel optimization—but addresses their intersection, which had only two prior treatments: a local-DP decentralized method [15] without guarantees in the ε=O(1) regime, and a central-DP nonconvex algorithm [28] whose rate degrades with the inner dimension d_y. For the convex case, the authors build on the exponential mechanism [40] and regularized exponential mechanism [24], extending the efficient log-concave sampling framework of Bassily et al. [9] and the grid-walk algorithm of Applegate and Kannan [3] to handle inexact function evaluations arising from the nested structure. For the nonconvex case, they combine a second-order bilevel gradient approximation from Ghadimi and Wang [23] with the warm-start framework of Lowy et al. [37] to achieve a d_y-independent stationary-point guarantee.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that DP bilevel ERM is fundamentally harder than single-level DP ERM: a matching lower bound shows the excess risk must depend on lower-level complexity parameters (e.g., L_{f,y}·D_y), a separation that was previously unknown. For practitioners in meta-learning, hyperparameter optimization, and adversarial training where both hierarchical structure and privacy are required, the near-optimal convex rates and the d_y-free nonconvex bound provide the first actionable theoretical guarantees. Key open questions remain: the optimal rate for DP bilevel stochastic optimization (a gap between the O(n^{-1/4}) upper bound and the O(n^{-1/2}) lower bound), whether the SOTA single-level nonconvex DP rate can be matched in the bilevel setting, and the absence of any experimental validation of the proposed algorithms.
