## Problem

Sampling from multi-modal distributions remains challenging even in low dimensions: existing algorithms suffer from exponential query complexity in multi-modality parameters, require log-smoothness assumptions that exclude general Gaussian mixtures, or demand prior knowledge of the target's constants. This paper provides the first sampling algorithm for a broad class including all Gaussian mixtures with polynomial query complexity in fixed dimension, addressing all three limitations simultaneously.

## Input / Output

The algorithm requires only zeroth-order query access to the unnormalized potential V (no gradient evaluations) plus the ability to draw Gaussian random samples. Crucially, all algorithm parameters depend solely on the target precision ε and dimension d, not on problem-specific constants like the dissipativity parameters a, b or the semi-log-convexity constant β. The output is a single sample from a distribution whose expected KL divergence from the target μ ∝ e^{-V} is bounded by ε·β^{d+3}(b+d)/a², achieved in O(d·ε^{-2(d+2)}) queries to V.

## Method

The algorithm simulates a time-reversed Ornstein-Uhlenbeck diffusion process, reducing sampling to estimating intermediate scores along the forward process. The key technical contribution is a self-normalized importance sampling estimator for these scores, derived from the Denoising Score Identity and rewritten as a ratio of expectations under a Gaussian distribution. By correlating the numerator and denominator samples (self-normalization), the estimator remains nearly bounded uniformly in the evaluation point, enabling a non-asymptotic quadratic error bound that avoids the vacuous integrals produced by generic self-normalized estimator bounds. Under the relaxed assumptions of semi-log-convexity (∇²V ⪯ βI_d) and dissipativity (⟨∇V(x),x⟩ ≥ a‖x‖² − b), the integrated score estimation error is controlled via moments of the ratio p_t^{2V}/p_t^V, yielding polynomial query complexity in fixed dimension without requiring log-smoothness or prior knowledge of the target's parameters.

## Results

Under semi-log-convexity (∇²V ⪯ βI_d) and dissipativity (⟨∇V(x),x⟩ ≥ a‖x‖²−b), the proposed reverse diffusion + self-normalized importance sampling algorithm achieves E[KL(μ, p̂)] ≲ ε β^{d+3}(b+d)/a² in O(√d β^{(d+3)/2} √(b+d/a) ε^{-(d+2)}) queries to V. In fixed dimension d, this yields polynomial query complexity in all problem parameters (a, b, β, ε^{-1}), which is the first such guarantee for a class including general Gaussian mixtures. Numerically, on a 16-mode 2D Gaussian mixture with centers in [−40,40]², the algorithm recovers all modes while ULA and RDMC fail; on a 3-mode mixture with varying separation R, it achieves the lowest Wasserstein distance error as R grows.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against five baselines in Table 1 and two in the numerical experiments (ULA and RDMC). Against ULA [Ma et al., 2019], the proposed method replaces the exponential e^{16LR²} dependence with a polynomial β^{(d+3)/2} factor, and avoids metastability: in the 16-mode experiment, ULA gets stuck in local modes while the proposed algorithm recovers all 16 modes. Against the proposal-based method of He and Zhang [2025], both are polynomial in fixed d, but the proposal-based approach requires explicit knowledge of the second moment m₂ (to design the proxy), whereas the proposed method only needs query access to V. Against RD+ULA [Huang et al., 2024b] and RD+Rejection [He et al., 2024], the proposed method removes the exponential dependence on L³ or L‖x*‖²+m₂, at the cost of requiring dissipativity (stronger than finite second moment) but relaxing log-smoothness to semi-log-convexity (which covers general Gaussian mixtures). In the 3-mode Wasserstein distance experiment, the proposed algorithm achieves the lowest error across all values of the between-mode distance R, while RDMC (Huang et al., 2024a) requires 100× more compute (inner ULA steps) and still underperforms.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two recently developed threads: reverse diffusion sampling (Conforti et al. 2025 for the finite-Fisher-information error bound; Huang et al. 2024a,b and He et al. 2024 for Bayesian applications) and self-normalized importance sampling (Saremi et al. 2024 for the estimator form, Jiao et al. 2021 and Ruzayqat et al. 2023 for Föllmer Flow variants). The key departure is that prior reverse-diffusion samplers either required log-smoothness of intermediate scores (Huang et al. 2024b), oracle access to the global minimum (He et al. 2024), or sampling from an auxiliary multi-modal distribution that becomes as hard as the original problem (Huang et al. 2024a). By combining the reverse diffusion framework with a self-normalized estimator that only requires Gaussian samples and pointwise queries to V, and by replacing log-smoothness with the weaker semi-log-convexity condition (Mikulincer and Shenfeld 2023), the authors obtain the first polynomial query complexity guarantees for general Gaussian mixtures in fixed dimension.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first provable polynomial-query-complexity sampler for general Gaussian mixtures in fixed dimension, simultaneously removing three limitations that had each independently blocked prior approaches: exponential dependence on multi-modality parameters, the log-smoothness assumption (which the paper shows is violated even by the simplest two-component Gaussian mixture), and the need for a priori knowledge of distribution constants. The practical scope is constrained by the fixed-dimension requirement (complexity is polynomial in problem parameters but exponential in d), and the empirical validation is limited to 2D toy problems. Open questions include extending guarantees to heavy-tailed distributions, understanding whether the semi-log-convexity + dissipativity framework can be further relaxed, and whether the polynomial-in-d dependence can be improved.
