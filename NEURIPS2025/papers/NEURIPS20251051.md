## Problem

The theoretical understanding of softmax self-attention remains largely unresolved, with prior analyses restricted to simplified pointwise/separable attention that omits the softmax layer entirely. The softmax creates coupled interactions between all tokens through the partition function, making rigorous analysis of the actual attention mechanism used in transformers extremely challenging. This paper provides the first exact mean-field analysis of softmax self-attention on the single-location regression problem, extending the regime from L = o(d) to log L = O(d).

## Input / Output

The model receives a sequence of L token embeddings in R^d where one secret token at a random index ℓ* contains a signal component aligned with a hidden unit vector u* (with SNR controlled by γ), while all other tokens are pure Gaussian noise. The model must produce a scalar prediction σ(x_{ℓ*}^T v*) by first locating the secret token via softmax attention weights and then applying a known link function σ. The analysis operates in the high-dimensional asymptotic regime d, L → ∞ with log(L)/d → α, and the inverse temperature scales as λ = β√d.

## Method

The key insight is that the softmax attention weights over the L−1 non-secret tokens correspond to the Gibbs distribution of a d-dimensional Random Energy Model (REM), enabling the use of classical statistical physics tools (Laplace method, large-deviation principles) to compute exact deterministic equivalents. The model output decomposes into a 'correct' term weighted by the probability p(μ) of locating the secret token and a 'noise' term converging to σ(ρr√d) via an extension of the Nadaraya-Watson estimator analysis of Zavatone-Veth and Pehlevan, yielding closed-form population risk expressions in terms of five overlap parameters between learned and oracle parameters. The paper then derives projected gradient-descent dynamics on the sphere manifold S^{d-1}², showing gradients are trapped in the 2D subspace spanned by oracle parameters, and proves convergence to the unique oracle attractor for linear, quadratic, and ReLU link functions. For the linear link function, the analysis removes the restrictive initialization assumption of Marion et al. (2025) that required starting on a submanifold presupposing knowledge of the oracle parameters.

## Results

The paper derives exact analytic expressions for the population risk of softmax self-attention on the single-location regression problem (Proposition 1), showing the risk decomposes into a function of five overlap parameters (μ, ν, η, ζ, ρ) between student and oracle parameters. A sharp phase transition is identified (Proposition 2): the oracle model achieves zero risk if and only if the signal-to-noise ratio parameter c = √(1−γ²) exceeds the thermodynamic threshold ψ by a constant margin. For the linear link function, projected gradient descent converges to the oracle parameters (u*, v*) from *any* initialization on the sphere (Proposition 4), removing the restrictive initialization-on-submanifold assumption of Marion et al. [2025]. The theory is valid for sequence length L up to exp(O(d)), a super-polynomial improvement over the L = o(d) regime of prior work. Figure 2 validates the theory empirically (d=100, L=20, β=1, γ=1/√2, ε=0.1), showing perfect agreement between predicted and measured risk trajectories for linear, quadratic, and ReLU link functions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical method comparisons; the 'compares_to' set is empty. The primary comparison is theoretical, against Marion et al. [2025] (ICLR 2025), which analyzed a simplified *pointwise* (non-softmax) self-attention model. The present work extends that analysis in three key ways: (1) it handles the full softmax attention mechanism (the one actually used in transformers) rather than a pointwise surrogate, using Random Energy Model techniques from statistical physics; (2) it removes the critical initialization assumption — Marion et al. required the optimizer to start on a sub-manifold M that presupposes knowledge of the oracle parameters (u*, v*), whereas here convergence to the oracle is shown from arbitrary initialization for the linear link function; and (3) the asymptotic regime is extended from L = o(d) to log L = O(d), i.e., L can be exponential in d. For the quadratic and ReLU link functions, the paper provides a complete classification of all stationary points (Proposition 10), showing the oracle remains the unique stable attractor in each case.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the single-location regression problem introduced by Marion et al. (2025), which analyzed a simplified pointwise (non-softmax) self-attention model, to the practically relevant softmax attention mechanism that is actually used in transformers. The key methodological departure is the use of Random Energy Model (REM) tools from statistical physics to handle the coupled partition function in softmax, building on a recent REM analysis of Nadaraya-Watson kernel smoothing by Zavatone-Veth and Pehlevan (2025). This allows the analysis to cover sequence lengths L exponential in the embedding dimension d (log L = O(d)), a significant extension beyond the sub-linear L = o(d) regime of Marion et al., and removes their restrictive initialization-on-a-manifold assumption that implicitly required knowledge of the oracle parameters.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first exact analytic theory for genuine softmax self-attention, providing closed-form expressions for population risk and gradient-flow dynamics in terms of parameter overlaps with the oracle. The REM-based methodology is a reusable theoretical tool that the authors argue can extend to multi-locator regression (encompassing sparse parity) and finite-sample analysis, opening a path toward understanding more complex transformer architectures. Key limitations include the restriction to a single-layer, single-locator toy problem, population (infinite-sample) risk only, and the sub-exponential block-length regime (r=0) for the optimization convergence results. The practical impact is indirect but foundational: it validates that softmax attention's apparent intractability is surmountable with the right statistical-physics lens, and the convergence guarantee without special initialization addresses a real concern for practitioners.
