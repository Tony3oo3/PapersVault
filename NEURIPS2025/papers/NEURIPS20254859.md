## Problem

Hybrid optical neural networks (ONNs) suffer from an accuracy gap relative to large-scale digital networks and further degradation from fabrication and experimental errors, yet prior training and compensation strategies are tailored to specific datasets and optical hardware, lacking generalization. This paper addresses the need for a unified, task-agnostic and hardware-agnostic pipeline that can train and compensate diverse ONN architectures across classification and segmentation tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254859/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline accepts grayscale images (MNIST, 28×28) or RGB images (CIFAR-10 at 32×32, Carvana at 1920×1280, ImageNet-100, COCO-Stuff 10k) and produces either class labels or dense segmentation masks. The optical frontend is a single linear convolutional layer physically realized as a PSF-engineered metasurface with a user-specified number of square kernels (e.g., 8 for monochromatic MNIST, 16 for polychromatic CIFAR-10, 56 for segmentation), followed by a compact digital backend (fully connected layers for classification, upconvolutional layers for segmentation). During fabrication, the optical frontend is fixed and only the digital backend remains tunable for error compensation.

## Method

The core contribution is Neural Tangent Knowledge Distillation (NTKD), a three-stage pipeline: (1) metasurface layout design with NTK-based performance estimation to diagnose architectural adequacy before training; (2) joint optimization of the optical frontend and digital backend under a combined loss of standard end-to-end supervision (cross-entropy) and an NTKD loss that minimizes the MSE between the teacher's and student's NTK matrices (computed as Jacobian–Jacobian transposes over a minibatch), thereby transferring relational structure between classes rather than merely matching final predictions; (3) post-fabrication error compensation that re-applies the same NTKD objective restricted to the unfrozen digital backend using a small fraction (~10%) of real experimental data. The key insight is that the NTK provides a linear approximation of network behavior, which naturally aligns with the inherently linear operations performed by the optical frontend, making NTK matching particularly effective for ONNs. A theoretical result shows that NTK perturbations from kernel fabrication noise scale as O(‖δ‖/m), where m is the number of kernels, implying wider optical frontends are inherently more robust to manufacturing errors.

## Results

In simulation, NTKD achieves 97.3% accuracy on MNIST (monochromatic), 75.6% on CIFAR-10 (polychromatic), and 91.5% mIoU on Carvana segmentation, consistently outperforming both standard KD and the no-transfer baseline across all four task/hardware configurations. After physical fabrication, NTKD-based error compensation recovers 95.1% on MNIST, 74.9% on CIFAR-10, and 81.2% mIoU on segmentation, substantially narrowing the simulation-to-fabrication gap (e.g., 41.8% mIoU drop without compensation vs. 10.3% with NTKD). The system also achieves over 300× energy compression compared to the full U-Net teacher (5.83 mJ vs. 2.04 J per image).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares NTKD against two baselines: (1) end-to-end training without knowledge transfer, and (2) standard Knowledge Distillation (KD) that matches output predictions. NTKD wins on every configuration. On monochromatic MNIST classification, NTKD reaches 97.3% vs. 95.9% (KD) and 91.4% (no transfer), a 1.4-point gain over KD. On the more challenging polychromatic CIFAR-10 setting, the gap widens: NTKD achieves 75.6% vs. 72.5% (KD) and 56.4% (no transfer), a 3.1-point improvement over KD. For segmentation on the Carvana dataset, NTKD yields 91.5% mIoU on the proposed Polychromatic Meta system versus 86.7% (KD) and 74.3% (no transfer), a 4.8-point margin. In the post-fabrication error compensation setting (Table 4), NTKD also outperforms end-to-end deep-learning compensation by 1.9 points on MNIST (95.1% vs. 93.2%), 4.5 points on CIFAR-10 (74.9% vs. 70.4%), and 18.5 points on segmentation (81.2% vs. 62.7% mIoU), indicating that NTK alignment is particularly effective at correcting fabrication-induced errors.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the knowledge distillation line for optical neural networks (Xiang et al. 2022, which showed KD circumvents nonlinearity in optical CNNs) by replacing implicit prediction-matching with an explicit Neural Tangent Kernel alignment loss, building on the theoretical observation that successful KD implicitly induces NTK similarity (Harutyunyan et al. 2023). It departs from prior ONN training work, which was typically task-specific (MNIST classification) and hardware-specific, by proposing a task-agnostic and hardware-agnostic pipeline that unifies design, training, and post-fabrication error compensation across monochromatic and polychromatic metasurface systems. The practical NTK computation leverages the NTK-SAP trace-estimation strategy, and the error compensation step builds on physics-constrained training frameworks but reframes the correction as NTK re-alignment rather than end-to-end retraining.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The pipeline provides a practical, end-to-end workflow for deploying hybrid optical neural networks, with the NTK-based pre-training performance estimate serving as a useful architectural diagnostic before committing to fabrication. A notable finding is that increasing teacher complexity (ResNet-18 → ResNet-50, U-Net → ResU-Net) yields only marginal student gains (~1.5%), indicating that the current performance bottleneck is the shallow, linear physical hardware rather than the teacher's capacity — a conceptual insight that redirects future effort toward optical hardware advances. The demonstrated >300× energy compression over a full U-Net teacher is practically significant for power-constrained edge deployments. Open questions include whether the approach scales to deeper or nonlinear optical architectures and whether the NTKD loss remains effective as optical hardware fidelity improves.
