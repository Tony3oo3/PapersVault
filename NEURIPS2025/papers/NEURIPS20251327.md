## Problem

Attention-first visual token pruning methods in MLLMs (e.g., FastV, SparseVLM) rely on cross-attention or [CLS] attention scores to rank and discard tokens, but they systematically over-retain semantically similar tokens from localized salient regions while discarding spatially diverse context. This causes severe performance degradation at high pruning ratios (≥77.8%) because the retained tokens lose holistic scene semantics, positional connectivity, and multi-object relational information.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251327/cropped_page1_idx4.webp)

## Input / Output

HoloV takes a standard RGB image encoded by a ViT into a grid of visual tokens (e.g., 576 tokens for LLaVA-1.5 at 336×336, up to 2880 for LLaVA-NeXT) plus a text query, and outputs a reduced set of visual tokens (as few as 64 out of 576) that are concatenated with the query and fed into the LLM decoder for autoregressive text generation. Pruning occurs before the LLM decoder rather than within it, preserving compatibility with Flash-Attention and avoiding KV-cache inefficiency. The method is training-free and model-agnostic, requiring only the ViT's [CLS] attention and normalized token embeddings as auxiliary signals.

## Method

HoloV partitions the visual token grid into C spatial crops and computes, for each token, an intra-crop semantic diversity score (variance of its similarity to all other tokens in the crop) that captures how uniquely informative it is relative to its neighbors. This diversity score is adaptively scaled and combined with the [CLS] attention score to form a holistic importance score per token. A crop-level priority score (mean of token scores, raised to a sharpness exponent τ) determines how the total retention budget is dynamically allocated across crops, with iterative reallocation to handle rounding and overflow. Within each crop, the top-k tokens by holistic score are retained, ensuring both local saliency and global spatial coverage. An optional Fast Visual Context Refetching module re-injects pruned tokens as key-value memory through the FFN at a middle transformer layer when the model exhibits high inference uncertainty, supplementing lost context without full recomputation.

## Results

On LLaVA-1.5-7B, HoloV achieves 95.8% average performance retention after pruning 88.9% of visual tokens (retaining only 64 of 576 tokens), outperforming the best baseline (VisionZip at 94.5%) by 1.3 points. At a more moderate 77.8% pruning ratio, HoloV retains 98.0% of original performance, and at 66.7% pruning it reaches 99.2%. On the higher-resolution LLaVA-NeXT (2880→320 tokens, 88.9% pruning), HoloV achieves 95.6% average versus 93.3% for the best baseline (DART), and on Qwen2.5-VL-7B it retains 90.5% at 88.9% pruning versus 84.3% for FastV.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HoloV outperforms all 10 baselines in Table 1 at the 88.9% pruning ratio, with the largest margins over attention-first methods: FastV (76.7%) and ToMe (70.1%) suffer catastrophic degradation, while PDrop (77.5%) and SparseVLM (87.3%) also drop sharply. The closest competitors are VisionZip (94.5%) and DART (93.9%), which HoloV beats by 1.3 and 1.9 points respectively on average; on POPE specifically, HoloV reaches 80.3% versus 77.0% (VisionZip) and 73.9% (DART). On the higher-resolution LLaVA-NeXT setting (Table 3), HoloV (95.6%) surpasses DART (93.9%), HiRED (93.3%), and GlobalCom² (92.2%) by 1.7–3.3 points. On video QA (Table 2, 50% retention), HoloV matches the best baselines (FastV, DART) on MSVD-QA accuracy (71.0%) and slightly trails on MSRVTT-QA (56.5% vs. 56.7% for DART), yielding a near-tie on average. On Qwen2.5-VL-7B (Table 6), HoloV consistently beats FastV by 2.3–6.2 points across all three pruning ratios, confirming architectural generalization beyond the LLaVA family.

## Relation to Prior Work

HoloV departs from the dominant attention-first token pruning paradigm (FastV, SparseVLM, FasterVLM) by arguing that ranking tokens solely by attention scores causes representational collapse at high pruning ratios, as it over-concentrates on localized salient regions. Instead, it repurposes the crop-partitioning idea from HiRED and the CLS-attention signal from FasterVLM, but augments them with an intra-crop diversity (variance) term and an adaptive budget-allocation mechanism to preserve holistic spatial-semantic context. The approach is explicitly vision-centric (avoiding text-guided pruning to sidestep language bias) and plug-and-play, requiring no retraining, and it draws a conceptual parallel to cognitive science findings that human visual understanding integrates local features with global scene cues.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a simple random-pruning baseline outperforms FastV on more than half of the benchmarks is a pointed challenge to the field's working assumption that attention scores are a reliable proxy for token informativeness. Practically, HoloV's 88.9% token reduction with ~4% average performance loss (and Flash-Attention compatibility) makes it a strong candidate for deployment in latency-sensitive MLLM applications. Open questions include whether the crop-wise allocation strategy generalizes to dynamic-resolution or video settings with non-uniform token counts, and whether the deferred 'Fast Visual Context Refetching' mechanism (Appendix D) can further close the remaining gap to unpruned performance without reintroducing KV-cache overhead.
