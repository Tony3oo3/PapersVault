## Problem

Existing diffusion-based world models such as DIAMOND condition generation on a fixed four-frame context window, which truncates historical information and degrades prediction accuracy in partially observable environments requiring long-term memory. Additionally, these models architecturally separate reward and termination prediction into independent recurrent networks, preventing representation sharing and introducing optimization conflicts between observation modeling and scalar signal prediction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253526/cropped_page3_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a sequence of RGB image observations (o_t ∈ R^{3×H×W}) and discrete actions (a_t) at each timestep, processing them through a recurrent Mamba-based embedding module that maintains an unbounded hidden state h_t across the full trajectory. The diffusion-based Next-Frame Predictor conditions on both the last L raw frames and the hidden embedding h_t to generate the next observation, while lightweight MLP heads on h_t produce the reward distribution and termination probability. A CNN-based actor then maps predicted frames to actions for autoregressive imagined rollouts during policy optimization.

## Method

EDELINE integrates three components around a shared Mamba SSM hidden embedding: (1) a Recurrent Embedding Module that selectively updates h_t = f_φ(h_{t-1}, o_t, a_t) via Mamba's input-dependent B(u(t)) and C(u(t)) matrices, enabling linear-time processing of unbounded sequences; (2) a U-Net diffusion Next-Frame Predictor that conditions on h_t through both Adaptive Group Normalization layers (extending DIAMOND's τ-only conditioning) and novel cross-attention blocks where h_t and τ project to keys/values while UNet feature maps generate queries; and (3) unified reward/termination MLPs operating directly on h_t, eliminating DIAMOND's separate CNN-LSTM network. Training leverages Mamba's parallel scan to compute all hidden states in one pass, then randomly samples a single target timestep for the observation reconstruction loss to match DIAMOND's computational cost. A HarmonyDream-style dynamic loss harmonization with learnable weights w_o and w_r balances the high-dimensional observation MSE against the scalar reward cross-entropy, preventing the observation loss from dominating optimization.

## Results

On the Atari 100k benchmark (26 games, 100k environment steps), EDELINE achieves a mean human-normalized score (HNS) of 1.866, median of 0.817, and IQM of 0.940, surpassing human performance in 13 games. On Crafter (1M steps), EDELINE achieves 11.5±0.9 average return with only 11M world model parameters, outperforming DreamerV3 XL (9.2±0.3, 200M params) by 25% and DIAMOND (2.8±0.5, 10.4M params) by 4.1×. On ViZDoom, EDELINE outperforms DIAMOND in scenarios requiring 3D spatial reasoning (DeadlyCorridor) and spatiotemporal prediction (PredictPosition). On MiniGrid-MemoryS7/S9, EDELINE achieves near-optimal success rates while DIAMOND fails due to its 4-frame context limitation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EDELINE outperforms all 12 compared baselines on the primary Atari 100k benchmark. Against DIAMOND (the closest diffusion-based competitor), EDELINE improves mean HNS from 1.459 to 1.866 (+28%), median from 0.373 to 0.817 (+119%), and IQM from 0.641 to 0.940 (+47%), with the largest per-game gains in memory-intensive games like BankHeist (0.026→1.125) and Seaquest (0.013→0.051). Against Transformer-based methods (STORM, IRIS, TWM), EDELINE leads in mean HNS (1.866 vs. 1.266/1.046/0.956) and dramatically in median (0.817 vs. 0.580/0.289/0.505), indicating more consistent performance across games. Against RNN-based DreamerV3, EDELINE achieves higher mean (1.866 vs. 1.124) and median (0.817 vs. 0.485) HNS. On Crafter, EDELINE (11.5±0.9) beats DreamerV3 XL (9.2±0.3) by 25% with 18× fewer parameters, and outperforms DIAMOND (2.8±0.5) by 4.1× at comparable parameter count. On ViZDoom, EDELINE surpasses DIAMOND in DeadlyCorridor and PredictPosition, with qualitative evidence showing EDELINE correctly predicts reward-relevant visual elements (enemies, armor, particle effects) that DIAMOND misses due to its 4-frame context window. Against HarmonyDream (the RNN+harmonizer baseline), EDELINE improves mean HNS from 1.364 to 1.866, confirming that the SSM-based memory provides gains beyond loss harmonization alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

EDELINE extends DIAMOND's diffusion-based world model by replacing its fixed four-frame conditioning context with a Mamba-based Recurrent Embedding Module that processes unbounded observation-action sequences via selective state-space updates. It further departs from DIAMOND's architectural separation of reward/termination prediction (a standalone CNN-LSTM) by conditioning all predictive heads on shared SSM hidden states, and incorporates HarmonyDream's dynamic loss harmonization to balance observation and reward objectives. The method thus sits at the intersection of two active research threads—diffusion-based generative world models and linear-time sequence modeling—demonstrating that their integration yields complementary benefits in visual fidelity and temporal memory.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

EDELINE addresses a concrete and well-motivated limitation (fixed context truncation) in the current SOTA diffusion-based world model, and the 4.1× improvement over DIAMOND on Crafter with comparable parameter counts provides strong evidence that memory capacity is a binding constraint in partially observable environments. The unified architecture's 26.8% speedup in world model training despite added complexity is a practical advantage for adoption. However, the fundamental computational cost of iterative diffusion denoising remains, and the paper acknowledges this as a key limitation; whether more efficient sampling (e.g., few-step distillation) can preserve the memory benefits while reducing inference cost is an open question. The comparable performance of Transformer and Mamba REMs (Table 8) also suggests the specific SSM choice may be less critical than the recurrent architecture itself, leaving room for further architectural exploration.
