## Problem

Stochastic optimal control methods such as MPPI repeatedly fall into the same local optima in highly non-convex value function landscapes because they operate statelessly, with no mechanism to learn from past trajectory failures. This paper asks how to integrate persistent memory of state-space topology into stochastic optimal control so that controllers can automatically identify, encode, and avoid problematic regions encountered during execution.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253424/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each control step the method consumes the current system state x_t ∈ R^n, a growing memory set M of topological feature tuples (position, influence radius, strength, type ∈ {local-minima, low-gradient, high-curvature}, direction vector), and the task cost function. It outputs the optimal control u_t* computed as an exponentially weighted average over K sampled trajectories, plus an updated memory M_{t+1} via a detection-and-consolidation rule. The entire pipeline is fully online with no offline training phase; all learning occurs within a fixed environment-interaction budget (2000 steps in experiments).

## Method

MA-MPPI augments the standard MPPI value function with a memory-derived potential field V_mem(x,M) = Σ γ_i·φ(x, m_i, r_i, κ_i, d_i), where type-specific basis functions (radially repulsive for local minima, directionally biased for low-gradient plateaus, saddle-shaped for high-curvature regions) create outward forces that reshape the landscape around known traps. Three complementary detectors—state-variance stagnation, gradient-magnitude monitoring, and Hessian condition-number analysis—identify and classify topological features, which are then stored, merged, and dynamically re-weighted by encounter frequency. An adaptive sigmoid weight α(x,M) smoothly blends the base objective and memory potential based on proximity to memorized features, while a coupled temperature increase λ(x,M) = λ_0(1+η(1−α)) inflates sampling covariance near traps for broader exploration. The paper proves three theoretical properties: high-probability finite-time escape from memorized local minima, asymptotic convergence to the global optimum under coercivity, and an efficiency gain scaling at least linearly with the number K of local minima, yielding a practical controller with only 12–18% computational overhead over vanilla MPPI.

## Results

Under a fixed 2000-step online interaction budget, MA-MPPI achieves the highest cumulative reward across all four MuJoCo/Gym environments: −152.4 on Pendulum-v1, 298.4 on BipedalWalker-v3, 5893.7 on HalfCheetah-v4, and 4978.5 on Humanoid-v4. The advantage over standard MPPI grows with task complexity, reaching a 71% reward improvement on Humanoid-v4 (4978.5 vs. 2914.2). MA-MPPI also achieves the highest local-optima escape rate (72–89%) and lowest trap frequency (1.2–2.8% of episodes) across all environments, with only 12–18% additional computational overhead per control step.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MA-MPPI outperforms all six baselines (MPPI, SAC, PPO, DDPG, iLQR, MPC) on every environment in the primary reward comparison. Against the closest competitor, standard MPPI, the gap ranges from 8% (Pendulum-v1: −152.4 vs. −165.8) to 71% (Humanoid-v4: 4978.5 vs. 2914.2), confirming that memory augmentation scales with landscape non-convexity. Against the best RL method (SAC), MA-MPPI leads by 27% on Humanoid-v4 and by 234% on HalfCheetah-v4 (5893.7 vs. 1763.4), while operating under the same 2000-step budget without any offline pre-training. On the local-optima escape metric, MA-MPPI achieves 72–89% escape rates versus 29–48% for MPPI and 46–66% for SAC. In the IEEE 39-bus power system, MA-MPPI halves constraint violations (2.3% vs. 5.7% for MPPI) and cuts disturbance recovery time nearly in half (4.2 s vs. 8.7 s). In the UAV obstacle-avoidance task, MA-MPPI reaches 94.3% success and 87.5% local-minima escape versus 72.8% and 34.2% for MPPI, with only 2.4 ms additional compute per step.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends MPPI (Williams et al., 2017) and the broader path integral control framework (Theodorou et al., 2010) by introducing a persistent memory module that encodes topological features of the state space as potential field corrections. It departs from prior memory-augmented RL (Experience Replay, Neural Episodic Control) by encoding *structural* features—local minima, low-gradient plateaus, high-curvature regions—rather than raw state-action pairs, and from reactive repulsion methods (DRPA-MPPI, LOG-MPPI) by maintaining a persistent, evolving spatial memory across episodes. The theoretical analysis draws on Morse theory (Koditschek & Rimon, 1990) to frame the mechanism as dynamically modifying the Morse index of local minima, and on stochastic escape results (Jin et al., 2017) to formalize convergence guarantees.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine and underexplored limitation of sampling-based MPC: the inability to learn from repeated failures in the same non-convex regions, which is particularly costly in deployment scenarios where offline training is infeasible. The theoretical guarantees (finite-time escape with high probability, asymptotic convergence, and linear efficiency scaling with the number of local minima) provide a principled foundation beyond empirical validation. Key open questions include extending to partial observability (state-estimation uncertainty), handling rapidly time-varying traps, and integrating learned feature detectors to replace the current rule-based topological analysis. The framework's complementarity with RL (rather than replacement) suggests a productive hybrid direction for complex control.
