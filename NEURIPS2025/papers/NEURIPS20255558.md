## Problem

Existing reward-guided post-training methods for few-step image generation treat diffusion distillation losses as indispensable, yet the paper shows that reward gradients overwhelmingly dominate the training dynamics, making distillation a costly and fragile regularization that can cause reward hacking artifacts. The paper asks whether a purely reward-centric training approach can convert pretrained diffusion models into fast, preference-aligned generators without any distillation loss or training images.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255558/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a text prompt and random noise (or a reference image for editing, or a Canny edge map with ControlNet) and produces a photo-realistic RGB image in 4 sampling steps at up to 1024×1024 resolution. Notably, training requires only prompts from JourneyDB—no paired images—and the generator is initialized from a pretrained diffusion model (SD-v1.5 or SD3-medium), with optimization performed on full parameters or LoRA adapters.

## Method

Reward-Instruct reframes conditional generation as regularized reward maximization in the generator's parameter space rather than conditional distribution modeling. The generator is an unrolled K-step diffusion sampler with random η at each step to augment the output distribution, and training proceeds via SGLD to sample from a reward-tilted posterior p*(θ) ∝ p₀(θ)exp{R̄(θ)}, where p₀ is a Gaussian centered at pretrained weights. Multiple differentiable rewards (HPS, Image Reward, CLIP Score) are combined with per-reward gradient normalization to prevent any single reward from dominating and to exploit their complementary modes as implicit regularization. The only explicit regularizer is an L2 penalty toward pretrained weights plus SGLD noise, entirely replacing the diffusion distillation loss. A variant, Reward-Instruct+, adds intermediate-step supervision to accelerate convergence while reducing per-iteration cost to 65% of the base method.

## Results

On SD-v1.5 with 4-step generation, Reward-Instruct achieves HPS 33.70, CLIP Score 32.13, and Image Reward 1.22, outperforming all distillation-based few-step baselines. Reward-Instruct+ further improves to HPS 34.37 and Image Reward 1.27 while maintaining a zero-shot COCO-5k FID of 37.53, comparable to the 50-step base model (FID 29.11). On SD3-Medium, Reward-Instruct at just 4 NFE surpasses the 56-NFE base model on HPS (34.04 vs 31.37), Aesthetic Score (6.27 vs 5.84), and Image Reward (1.13 vs 1.07).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the three distillation-based few-step baselines, Reward-Instruct dominates: it beats DI++ by +1.87 HPS and +2.91 CS while reducing FID from 55.52 to 33.79, and outperforms RG-LCM by +2.26 HPS and +2.99 CS with FID improved from 52.01 to 33.79. Compared to Hyper-SD, Reward-Instruct gains +3.46 HPS and +0.32 Image Reward, though Hyper-SD retains a slight FID advantage (30.32 vs 33.79). Against the multi-step reward-centric methods, Reward-Instruct at 4 steps surpasses DRaFT (50 steps) by +0.60 HPS and +1.43 Image Reward, and outperforms ReFL (50 steps) by +1.88 HPS and +0.06 Image Reward. In a qualitative comparison with Referee Can Play [24], Reward-Instruct produces significantly cleaner images without the artifacts and repeated objects observed in the baseline. Notably, Reward-Instruct is more robust to reward choice: when HPS v2.1 is used, DI++ and RG-LCM collapse into degenerate distributions, while Reward-Instruct continues to generate high-quality samples.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the SGLD framework (Welling & Teh, 2011) for sampling from a reward-tilted posterior, repurposing it as a training procedure in generator parameter space rather than data space. It departs from the dominant paradigm in reward-guided few-step generation (DI++, RG-LCM, Hyper-SD), which couples reward maximization with expensive diffusion distillation losses to preserve the base model's distributional knowledge. The key conceptual departure is the 'phase transition' argument: under strong conditions, the discriminative reward signal becomes the primary driving force and the base model acts merely as a regularizer, making explicit distillation losses redundant. The random-η sampling mechanism and gradient normalization across multiple rewards are novel regularization techniques not present in prior distillation-based approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating diffusion distillation losses, Reward-Instruct simplifies the training pipeline (no auxiliary score models, no training images) while achieving SOTA 4-step generation quality and notably greater robustness to reward function choice—DI++ and RG-LCM collapse under HPS v2.1 while Reward-Instruct does not. This opens a path toward scaling reward-guided generation to higher resolutions where distillation losses become prohibitively expensive. Key limitations include the requirement for differentiable reward functions, testing only on text-to-image (SD-v1.5, SD3-medium), and the 'phase transition' argument remaining primarily empirical rather than formally proven. Open questions include extending to black-box rewards, DPO-style preference data, video/3D generation, and non-diffusion few-step generators.
