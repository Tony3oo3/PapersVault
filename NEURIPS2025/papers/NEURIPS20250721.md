## Problem

State-of-the-art influence functions for data valuation in instruction fine-tuning require expensive forward and backward passes through large language models (7–22B parameters), making them computationally prohibitive for large datasets. Moreover, these influence values are not learned, so they must be recomputed from scratch whenever new data is collected, and they do not generalize to unseen data points. The paper asks whether a tiny, learnable neural network can replace the LLM in the influence estimation step without sacrificing downstream subset-selection performance.

## Input / Output

The InfluenceNetwork receives a concatenated pair of 1,024-dimensional sentence embeddings (produced by bge-large-en-v1.5) for two data points, yielding a 2,048-dimensional input vector, and outputs a single scalar influence score constrained to [0, 1]. At the pipeline level, the method consumes a fine-tuning dataset D_F and a target dataset D_T, and produces a selected subset S_F (fraction v of D_F) used for QLoRA fine-tuning or in-context learning. The embedding model is treated as an offline cost and excluded from the method's parameter count, which is exactly 204,900 parameters.

## Method

NN-CIFT trains a 2-layer feedforward network (100 hidden ReLU neurons, 1 output neuron) to regress influence values produced by an existing influence function (DELIFT, DELIFT-SE, LESS, or SelectIT). Training uses only u = 5% of D_F and 5% of D_T (i.e., 0.25% of the full pairwise matrix for pairwise functions), with 20 epochs at learning rate 1e-4. The key insight is twofold: influence estimation is inherently lossy (compressing high-dimensional text to a bounded scalar), and the distribution of influence values is heavily concentrated, so the network only needs to learn the sparse extremes. Once trained, the InfluenceNetwork generalizes to out-of-distribution data pairs (quadrants Q2–Q4) without retraining, achieving an average MSE of 0.067 against ground-truth influence values and reducing data-valuation cost by 77–99% while preserving downstream subset-selection performance within 0.16% of the original influence functions.

## Results

The InfluenceNetwork (205K parameters, 0.0007% the size of the LLMs it replaces) achieves a mean squared error of only 0.067 against ground-truth influence values across all four quadrants (ID/OOD splits), starting from just 5% training data (u=0.05). On the downstream subset selection task (v=0.3), the average absolute performance difference between NN-CIFT and the original influence functions is merely 0.16% across all metrics, datasets, and models (Llama-8B, Qwen2.5-1.5B, Mistral-22B). Data valuation costs are reduced by 77–99% compared to the original influence functions (e.g., DELIFT drops from ~68,000s to ~215s on Llama-8B/MixInstruct).

## Baselines & Comparisons

NN-CIFT is compared against four SOTA influence functions (DELIFT, DELIFT (SE), LESS, SelectIT) in two ablated forms: replacing the LLM with a small DistilGPT2 model (88M params) versus replacing it with the 205K-parameter InfluenceNetwork. Across all four methods, NN-CIFT matches or slightly exceeds the original influence function's downstream performance (e.g., DELIFT: 91.96 vs 91.81 BGE; SelectIT: 87.43 vs 87.38 BGE on MixInstruct QLoRA), while DistilGPT2 shows substantial degradation, particularly for model-dependent methods (LESS drops from 86.07 to 79.09 BGE; DELIFT drops from 91.81 to 84.24). The paper attributes this to model-dependent influence functions requiring the valuation model to match the fine-tuning model, a requirement NN-CIFT sidesteps by learning the influence mapping directly. On MMLU, NN-CIFT+DELIFT achieves 82.0/83.6 (ICL/QLoRA) versus 81.5/83.1 for original DELIFT, confirming parity. The consistent ~0.16% average gap across all settings is attributed to run-to-run variability in the language model rather than estimation error.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the well-established data valuation pipeline (DELIFT, LESS, SelectIT) by replacing the expensive LLM-based influence computation with a tiny 2-layer feedforward network (205K parameters) trained on a small fraction of ground-truth influence values. Conceptually, it applies a distillation-like idea to influence estimation: rather than running forward/backward passes through 7–22B parameter models, a compact proxy learns to map concatenated sentence embeddings to scalar influence scores. The approach is orthogonal to the specific influence function used (pairwise or pointwise, model-dependent or independent), positioning it as a general efficiency layer atop existing data-valuation methods rather than a new influence metric.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 77–99% cost reduction in data valuation makes influence-guided subset selection practically accessible for teams without large GPU clusters, and the finding that a 0.0007%-sized network matches billion-parameter models for this task challenges the assumption that influence estimation requires full model capacity. Limitations include reliance on a fixed embedding model (bge-large-en-v1.5) as input features, evaluation only on English instruction-tuning data, and the open question of how the method behaves under distributional shift (the authors note retraining would suffice but do not test it). Future work on estimating influence between data and model parameters, rather than between data pairs, could further tighten the connection to the fine-tuning objective.
