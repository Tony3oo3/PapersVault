## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting produces poor surface geometry because independent Gaussian primitives are supervised only by image reconstruction loss, and COLMAP initialization fails on textureless or repetitive regions. Existing 3DGS-based reconstruction methods incorporate pre-trained vision model priors (depth, normals) as rigid supervision, but these priors are inherently noisy and limited to sparse viewpoints, creating sub-optimal and inconsistent guidance that caps reconstruction accuracy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252281/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes multi-view RGB images with COLMAP-estimated camera poses and sparse point cloud as input, downsampled to half resolution for training. It internally renders virtual rectified stereo pairs from the 3DGS model at a 7% scene-radius baseline, feeds them to FoundationStereo (zero-shot) to obtain dense depth priors with confidence masks, and outputs a 3D mesh extracted via TSDF fusion, rendered depth and normal maps, novel view synthesis, and the optimized 3D Gaussian representation.

## Method

Eve3D's core insight is to treat stereo depth priors as learnable parameters and jointly optimize them with the 3DGS backbone through backpropagation, creating a mutually reinforcing cycle where improved geometry refines the priors and refined priors improve geometry. A local bundle adjustment module enforces multi-view geometric (homography-based reprojection) and photometric (NCC on 7×7 patches) consistency across K=4 co-visible frames, overcoming 3DGS's inherent limitation of optimizing only one frame at a time. Training follows a two-phase schedule: before iteration T_joint, original FoundationStereo priors supervise the 3DGS to avoid poor local minima; after T_joint, the learnable prior depth maps are co-optimized with Gaussian parameters. A confidence mask is dynamically updated during bundle adjustment, and a pull loss allows low-confidence pixels to be corrected by the 3DGS before re-entering joint optimization.

## Results

On Tanks and Temples, Eve3D achieves a mean F1 score of 0.58, outperforming the previous best explicit method PGSR (0.52) and all implicit methods (best: Neuralangelo at 0.50), while training in only 1.2 hours versus >24h for implicit baselines. On DTU, Eve3D achieves a mean Chamfer Distance of 0.48, beating PGSR (0.52) and Neuralangelo (0.61) in just 15 minutes. On Mip-NeRF360 novel view synthesis, Eve3D achieves the best outdoor PSNR (24.99), SSIM (0.758), and LPIPS (0.203), with competitive indoor scores (PSNR 30.42, SSIM 0.930, LPIPS 0.157). The fast variant (Eve3D-fast, 20 min) still reaches F1 0.56 on Tanks and Temples, setting an unprecedented accuracy-speed trade-off.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Eve3D outperforms all 17 compared baselines on surface reconstruction. Against the closest explicit competitor PGSR [6], Eve3D improves mean F1 from 0.52 to 0.58 on Tanks and Temples and Chamfer Distance from 0.52 to 0.48 on DTU, with the largest gains in challenging scenes (Meetingroom: 0.46 vs 0.33 F1; Courthouse: 0.35 vs 0.20 F1). Against GOF [55] and 2DGS [18], the improvements are more substantial (0.58 vs 0.46 and 0.30 F1 respectively), particularly on flat surfaces and object boundaries. All implicit NeRF-based methods (NeuS, Geo-NeuS, Neuralangelo, VolSDF, NeuralWarp) are significantly outperformed in both accuracy and speed—Eve3D trains in 15–20 minutes versus >12–128 hours for implicit baselines. On novel view synthesis (Mip-NeRF360), Eve3D beats 3DGS [22], 2DGS [18], GOF [55], and PGSR [6] on outdoor PSNR/SSIM/LPIPS, while remaining competitive with MipNeRF360 [2] on indoor scenes (PSNR 30.42 vs 31.72). The convergence comparison in Table 8 shows Eve3D reaches F1 0.555 at 5k iterations versus 0.519 for PGSR+FoundationStereo, demonstrating faster convergence with the same depth priors.

## Relation to Prior Work

Eve3D builds directly on the 3DGS backbone (Kerbl et al., 2023), adopting PGSR's (Chen et al., 2024) planar Gaussian formulation and unbiased depth rendering, and 2DGS's (Huang et al., 2024) flat-Gaussian constraint for surface fitting. Its key conceptual departure from prior 3DGS-based reconstruction methods (DN-Splatter, VCR-GauS, PGSR, GS2Mesh) is treating external vision-model depth priors as learnable parameters co-optimized with the 3DGS representation, rather than as fixed supervision or heuristic constraints. The prior-involved local bundle adjustment further distinguishes the work by enforcing multi-view consistency on depth maps themselves (not just rendered images), a mechanism absent from standard 3DGS pipelines that can only rasterize one view at a time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: Eve3D-fast achieves near-SOTA reconstruction (F1 0.56 on Tanks & Temples) in 20 minutes, while the full model reaches F1 0.58 in ~1.2 hours—orders of magnitude faster than implicit methods (>24h) and faster than competing explicit methods at comparable or better accuracy. The finding that joint optimization improves the priors themselves (reducing depth error by 15–30% in Table 6) suggests a general principle: treating noisy foundation-model outputs as differentiable, optimizable quantities can extract more signal than rigid supervision. The main open question is whether future multi-view stereo foundation models with sufficient accuracy could eliminate the stereo-rendering overhead, and whether the joint-optimization paradigm transfers to other 3DGS applications beyond surface reconstruction.
