## Problem

Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) are especially vulnerable to harmful prompts because their structured chain-of-thought pathways can rationalize unsafe intent, yet existing safety alignment methods like Direct Refusal and SafeChain impose a 'safety tax'—substantial drops in multi-step reasoning accuracy—and remain fragile against iterative jailbreak attacks. The paper asks whether safety can be injected into LRMs with minimal intervention that preserves full reasoning depth rather than truncating or heavily supervising the reasoning trace.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a standard user prompt (harmful or benign) and the LRM's -delimited reasoning block. For harmful prompts, the model emits a fixed 8-token Safety Primer ('Let's think about safety first') immediately after the  token, with the closing  tag intentionally omitted so the reasoning block stays open and the model continues generating a full chain-of-thought from a safety-aware initialization. For benign prompts, no primer is emitted and reasoning proceeds normally. A zero-shot variant (ZS-SAFEPATH) simply prepends the primer at inference time with no parameter updates, producing the same open-ended safety-conditioned reasoning trace.

## Method

SAFEPATH fine-tunes only the 8-token Safety Primer prefix at the start of the reasoning block for harmful prompts, applying loss exclusively to those primer tokens while leaving the remainder of the  trace completely unsupervised. Training interleaves two data partitions: a Safety Trigger Set (harmful prompts from WildJailbreak) where the target is just the primer with no closing tag, and a Reasoning Retain Set (benign math problems from Math220K) with full supervision to anchor general reasoning ability. The key design choice is that the primer acts as a soft, context-aware bias rather than a hard refusal, so the model continues reasoning naturally from a safety-oriented starting point. An emergent property is that under adversarial pressure (e.g., PAIR attacks), the model spontaneously re-emits the primer multiple times mid-reasoning—over 8 times per sample—despite being trained to emit it only once, effectively performing reactive safety re-checking. Training converges in 100 steps (R-7B) or 20 steps (R-8B), completing in under 5 minutes, roughly 300× faster than Direct Refusal or SafeChain.

## Results

On DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama-8B (R-8B), SAFEPATH reduces average harmfulness from 36.8% to 3.9% (a 90.0% reduction) and average attack success rate from 52.6% to 8.8% (an 83.3% reduction), while preserving reasoning accuracy at 56.1% versus 57.9% for the base model. On R-7B, harmfulness drops from 45.3% to 11.6% and ASR from 47.9% to 12.4%, with reasoning maintained at 60.0% (base: 60.8%). Training requires only 1.5 minutes for R-8B, achieving 295.9× and 314.1× speedups over Direct Refusal and SafeChain respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAFEPATH outperforms all five baselines across every safety metric on R-8B. Against Direct Refusal (Huang et al., 2025), SAFEPATH reduces harmfulness from 18.6% to 3.9% and ASR from 47.8% to 8.8%, while recovering 4.3 points of reasoning accuracy (56.1 vs. 51.8). Against SafeChain (Jiang et al., 2025), SAFEPATH achieves 21.8 points lower harmfulness (3.9 vs. 25.7) and 43.7 points lower ASR (8.8 vs. 52.5), with 4.0 points higher reasoning (56.1 vs. 52.1). Among LLM-adapted baselines, SAFEPATH dominates: Task Arithmetic (Ilharco et al., 2023) records 49.4% ASR and NPO (Zhang et al., 2024) records 36.7% ASR, both far above SAFEPATH's 8.8%, while Circuit Breaker (Zou et al., 2024) performs worst at 62.1% ASR, confirming that LLM-level defenses do not transfer to reasoning models. SAFEPATH also trains in 1.5 minutes versus 435.1 minutes (Direct Refusal) and 461.7 minutes (SafeChain), a 295.9×–314.1× reduction in compute.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant LRM safety-alignment paradigm, which either forces hard refusals (Direct Refusal) or supervises the full reasoning trace alongside the answer (SafeChain), by showing that fine-tuning only an 8-token prefix at the start of the chain-of-thought block is sufficient to redirect reasoning toward safety. It also repurposes the prefix-tuning idea from LLM safety (e.g., Circuit Breaker, Task Arithmetic) into a minimal, reasoning-native intervention, and systematically benchmarks whether those LLM-centric defenses transfer to the multi-step reasoning setting. The zero-shot variant (ZS-SAFEPATH) extends the line of work on reasoning-termination prompts (ZEROTHINK, LESSTHINK) by inserting a safety cue that preserves rather than truncates the reasoning process.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SAFEPATH directly addresses the 'safety tax'—the well-documented degradation of reasoning accuracy when safety alignment is enforced—by achieving up to 90% harmfulness reduction with <5 minutes of training and near-zero inference overhead, making it a practical option for deploying LRMs in safety-critical settings. However, the method remains vulnerable to iterative jailbreaks like PAIR (26.3% ASR on R-8B), and the emergent reactivation of the Safety Primer, while intriguing, is demonstrated primarily on a single model with qualitative evidence. Open questions include whether the 8-token mechanism generalizes to non-DeepSeek architectures, multi-turn dialogues, and whether more adaptive or context-sensitive triggers could close the remaining adversarial gap.
