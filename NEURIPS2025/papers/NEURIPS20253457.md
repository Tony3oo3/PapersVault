## Problem

Program synthesis methods face a fundamental trade-off: inductive approaches require hand-crafted domain-specific languages that don't scale, while transductive (in-context learning) methods lack structured test-time adaptation and struggle with specification consistency. Existing test-time fine-tuning (TTT) resolves consistency but is computationally expensive because it backpropagates through all model parameters. The paper asks whether test-time program search can be built directly into a neural architecture via a compact latent space, combining the adaptability of symbolic search with the scalability of neural methods.

## Input / Output

The method takes a specification of n input-output grid pairs (grids up to 30×30 with 10 colors) plus a single new input grid, and produces the predicted output grid pixel-by-pixel. The encoder processes all I/O pairs in parallel and aggregates via mean pooling, making the model permutation-invariant to specification order and enabling smooth generalization to specification sizes beyond those seen in training. The latent program space is 256-dimensional for ARC-AGI, and the decoder conditions on both the refined latent and the new input to generate the output.

## Method

LPN factorizes inference into three differentiable stages: (1) a probabilistic encoder maps each I/O pair to a distribution over latent programs, (2) a latent optimization stage refines the initial latent via gradient ascent to maximize the decoder's log-likelihood over all specification pairs, and (3) a transformer decoder maps the refined latent plus the new input to the output grid. Training uses a leave-one-out objective where each output is reconstructed from the other n−1 pairs' latents, preventing the encoder from memorizing individual outputs. The key insight is that even a single gradient ascent step during training shapes the latent space geometry for effective test-time search, yielding 99.5% accuracy on the Pattern task versus 67.5% for a model trained without gradient steps. At test time, LPN backpropagates only through the decoder to update the 256-dim latent, making it orders of magnitude cheaper than TTT which updates all 178M parameters.

## Results

On ARC-AGI (top-2 exact match accuracy), LPN achieves 80.0% in-distribution at 2E+13 FLOPs, substantially outperforming TTT (58.5%) and LLM-based baselines CodeIt (14.75%) and Mirchandani (14.00%) at >2E+15 FLOPs. Out-of-distribution, LPN doubles its performance from 7.75% (no search) to 15.50% (with gradient search), matching or exceeding TTT (16.00% at 2E+14) while using far fewer FLOPs. On the Pattern task, LPN trained with 1 gradient step reaches 99.5% accuracy with 100 inference gradient steps, and on the OOD Pattern setting achieves 88.0% where TTT (0.3%) and in-context learning (0.0%) fail entirely.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LPN significantly outperforms TTT (Akyürek et al., 2024) in-distribution on ARC-AGI, reaching 80.0% vs. 58.5% at 2E+13 FLOPs, and maintains a lead at lower compute budgets out-of-distribution (15.25% vs. 13.50% at 2E+13). At very high OOD compute (2E+14), TTT slightly edges LPN (16.00% vs. 15.50%), but LPN achieves this with a 2× self-improvement from search while TTT shows diminishing returns. Against the in-context learning baseline (Kolev et al., 2020; Li et al., 2024a), LPN is dramatically superior on OOD tasks (88.0% vs. 0.0% on the Pattern OOD setting) and generalizes smoothly to specification sizes beyond training, whereas in-context learning overfits to its training specification size. LPN also outperforms the LLM-based CodeIt (Butt et al., 2024, 14.75%) and Mirchandani et al. (2023, 6.75%) on ARC-AGI OOD at comparable or lower FLOP budgets, using a 178M-parameter model trained from scratch versus 220M and 175B pre-trained models respectively. On the String Manipulation task, LPN maintains stable performance (81–86%) across inference budgets while TTT degrades catastrophically from 78.1% to 11.8% as fine-tuning steps increase, demonstrating LPN's resistance to overfitting.

## Relation to Prior Work

LPN synthesizes ideas from Neural Processes (the encoder-decoder latent bottleneck), semi-amortized variational inference (amortized encoder followed by instance-specific latent optimization), and LEO (latent-space inner-loop optimization), while explicitly departing from both inductive program synthesis (which requires handcrafted DSLs) and pure transductive/in-context learning (which lacks structured test-time adaptation). The key conceptual move is to treat the latent space as a continuous program representation over which gradient-based search can be performed at test time, removing the need for a hypernetwork (as in LEO) or a discrete DSL (as in neuro-symbolic methods). The leave-one-out training objective, which prevents the encoder from memorizing individual I/O pairs, is a practical adaptation of the Execution ELBO from LEAPS to the setting where the underlying program is never fully observed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LPN offers a principled middle ground between the strong generalization of symbolic program synthesis and the scalability of neural approaches, with the practical advantage that test-time adaptation only requires backpropagation through the decoder to update a low-dimensional latent (vs. all parameters in TTT). The finding that OOD performance roughly doubles when test-time latent search is enabled, and that a single gradient step during training provides a strong inductive bias for test-time search, are encouraging signals. However, absolute ARC-AGI performance remains low (~15.5% OOD), compositional generalization is fragile (works for 2-operation compositions but fails at 3), and the training distribution is narrow, leaving open whether the approach scales to the full diversity of ARC-AGI programs or to more complex domains. Future work on alternative search strategies (evolutionary strategies, discrete latent representations) and broader program diversity during training are natural next steps.
