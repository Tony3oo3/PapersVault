## Problem

LLM-based RTL optimization methods struggle to align generated code with specified optimization objectives, producing incomplete or incorrect rewrites, while compiler-based approaches rely on rigid heuristics that cannot handle conflicting PPA goals or complex design patterns. Additionally, optimization knowledge is scattered across books, codebases, and informal notes with no centralized, retrievable repository, and the reliance on repeated synthesis feedback loops makes the process slow and impractical for large designs.

## Input / Output

The system takes a Verilog RTL module and a user-specified optimization goal (e.g., low power, high performance, reduced area) as input. The LLM Dispatcher first summarizes the code and detects whether an FSM is present to route the design into data-flow-only, control-flow-only, or combined optimization paths. The output is a functionally equivalent optimized Verilog module, validated by a two-step pipeline of LLM-generated testbenches followed by formal equivalence checking (SAT-based for combinational logic, sequential equivalence checking for complex designs).

## Method

SymRTLO is a neuro-symbolic framework that integrates GPT-4o-based code rewriting with symbolic reasoning to optimize both data and control paths in RTL. For data flow, a retrieval-augmented generation system retrieves relevant optimization rules from a structured library (built from lecture notes, manuals, and example designs), uses an elbow-method similarity threshold to select non-conflicting rules, and enforces alignment via AST-based templates that guide the LLM to produce syntactically correct rewrites. For control flow, an LLM dynamically generates a specialized symbolic minimization script tailored to each FSM's structure, handling partial specifications and data-path constraints that fixed AST scripts cannot address. A goal-oriented rule filtering mechanism ties each optimization rule to its PPA objective, resolving conflicts between competing goals (e.g., pipelining vs. resource sharing). The final optimized design undergoes automated verification combining LLM-generated testbenches as a rapid filter with formal equivalence checking to guarantee functional correctness.

## Results

On the RTL-Rewriter benchmark (11 small circuits), SymRTLO achieves a geometric-mean wire ratio of 0.63 and cell ratio of 0.67 relative to the unoptimized baseline, surpassing the prior state-of-the-art RTLrewriter (0.69 / 0.77). On FSM PPA benchmarks, SymRTLO reduces power by up to 57.1%, delay by up to 48.0%, and area by up to 54.6% versus the original designs, and outperforms RTLrewriter by up to 50.6% in power and 53.1% in area. On larger algorithm-level designs (FFT, vending machine, etc.), SymRTLO delivers average PPA improvements of 30.3% (power), 21.4% (delay), and 20.0% (area) over GPT-4o. Functional correctness is near-perfect: 97.5% Pass@1 and 100% Pass@5, compared to 45.9% and 60.0% for GPT-4o.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SymRTLO outperforms all five baseline families across every reported metric. Against the closest prior method, RTLrewriter [46], SymRTLO improves the wire/cell GeoMean ratios from 0.69/0.77 to 0.63/0.67, and on FSM PPA benchmarks achieves up to 50.6% lower power and 53.1% smaller area than RTLrewriter's best reported results. Compared to raw GPT-4o [29], SymRTLO reduces wires by ~40% (0.63 vs 1.06 ratio) and cells by ~40% (0.67 vs 1.11), while also raising Pass@1 from 45.9% to 97.5%, indicating far better alignment between generated code and optimization intent. GPT-4-Turbo and GPT-3.5-Turbo [47] perform at or below the unoptimized baseline on several benchmarks (e.g., GPT-3.5 increases cells by 28%), whereas SymRTLO consistently improves every circuit. RTLCoder-DS [25] matches the baseline on wire/cell counts (ratio 1.15/0.91) and shows no PPA improvement on FSM designs, while SymRTLO achieves 50%+ area reductions. Verigen [36] (2B and 16B) scores 0.0% on all pass-rate metrics, indicating it cannot produce functionally correct optimized RTL for these tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LLM-based RTL optimization line initiated by RTLRewriter [46] and RTLCoder [25], which relied on retrieval-augmented prompting and iterative synthesis feedback but struggled with output alignment and scalability. It departs from these purely generative approaches by introducing a neuro-symbolic architecture that enforces structural correctness through AST-based templates (borrowed from hardware debugging work like RTLFixer [37]) and LLM-generated symbolic FSM minimization scripts (inspired by code-prompting methods [19]). The RAG-based optimization rule library and goal-conflict resolution via elbow-method thresholding represent a novel integration of knowledge retrieval with hardware-specific constraint management, positioning the work at the intersection of neuro-symbolic AI [45, 39] and EDA automation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RTL optimization is a critical bottleneck in hardware design where manual iteration through synthesis tools is prohibitively expensive, and this work demonstrates that structured neuro-symbolic guidance can substantially reduce reliance on repeated synthesis invocations while maintaining functional correctness (97.5% pass@1 vs. 45.9% for GPT-4o). The finding that source-level optimization complements rather than duplicates compiler-based synthesis (yielding an additional ~36% power and ~36% area improvement on top of DC Compiler) suggests a practical two-stage workflow for industry. Key open questions include scalability to very large FSMs and industrial SoC-level designs, the maintenance burden of the optimization rule library as design practices evolve, and whether the approach generalizes beyond Verilog to other HDLs or higher-level synthesis inputs.
