## Problem

Existing 3D head avatar methods rely on parametric models (3DMM, FLAME) whose expression bases are derived from only a few hundred 3D scans, resulting in facial dynamics that lack the nuanced motion and subtle expressions of real human faces. Additionally, the vast majority of prior approaches require video or multiview data of the reference face, limiting practical utility. VASA-3D addresses both gaps by capturing rich expression detail from abundant 2D head video data while enabling single-image customization of a true 3D, free-viewpoint, real-time animatable head.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single 512×512 portrait image and a driving speech audio clip (or optionally a face video or emotion offset) as input. It produces an animatable 3D Gaussian head avatar that renders free-viewpoint talking face videos at 512×512 resolution in real time (75 FPS on a single RTX 4090 with 65 ms latency). Training data is synthesized by VASA-1 from the single image using up to 10 hours of driving clips sampled from VoxCeleb2, yielding diverse poses and expressions; the resulting model does not cover the back of the head due to the limited angular range of the synthetic frames.

## Method

VASA-3D represents the head as 3D Gaussians bound to a FLAME mesh and decomposes animation into a Base Deformation (two MLPs map VASA-1's motion latent z^dyn and z^pose to FLAME expression and pose parameters, which rig the Gaussians) and a VAS Deformation (two additional MLPs predict dense per-Gaussian residuals Δμ, Δr, Δs, Δc, Δα for face and neck regions, conditioned on the motion latent and current FLAME parameters, capturing fine-grained expression details beyond the parametric model). Single-image customization is achieved by synthesizing a large collection of training frames with varied poses and expressions via VASA-1, then fitting the avatar end-to-end. A key training challenge is that the synthetic frames exhibit temporal texture inconsistency and limited pose coverage, so the authors design a robustified loss suite: photometric (SSIM + L1), perceptual (LPIPS + multiscale adversarial), SDS loss from Stable Diffusion v2.1 to regularize unseen side views, a render consistency loss that anchors the VAS-deformed Gaussians to the base-deformed ones under novel views to prevent per-frame overfitting, and a contrast-adaptive-sharpening finetuning step for final image sharpness.

## Results

On the audio-driven 3D talking head benchmark, VASA-3D achieves a lip-sync confidence score S_C of 8.121 and feature distance S_D of 6.93, substantially outperforming the best baseline TalkingGaussian (S_C 6.701, S_D 8.106). In user studies, VASA-3D receives a video quality rating of 4.29/5 (vs. 2.38 for the next best) and is preferred as most realistic in 93.91% of cases. The model renders 512×512 free-viewpoint video at 75 FPS with 65 ms latency on a single RTX 4090. Even with only 20K training iterations (1/10 of default), VASA-3D still achieves S_C 7.980 and S_D 7.002, exceeding all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VASA-3D decisively outperforms all four audio-driven 3D talking head baselines on lip-sync metrics: S_C improves from 5.27–6.70 (baselines) to 8.12, and S_D drops from 8.11–10.94 to 6.93. Identity similarity is competitive (0.7874), marginally below TalkingGaussian (0.7971) but above ER-NeRF (0.7732) and MimicTalk (0.7748). In user studies, VASA-3D's video quality score of 4.29/5 nearly doubles the next-best (TalkingGaussian at 2.38), and users select it as most realistic in 93.91% of cases versus ≤3.58% for all baselines combined. Against VASA-1 (its 2D training-data generator and upper bound), VASA-3D shows only a small gap in FID (7.45 vs. 5.24) and ID similarity (0.7874 vs. 0.8154) while adding true 3D free-viewpoint rendering. In the supplementary face-reenactment comparison (Table A), VASA-3D surpasses GAGAvatar, GPAvatar, Real3DPortrait, Voodoo3D, and Portrait4D-v2 on all metrics including PSNR, LPIPS, and SyncNet scores.

## Relation to Prior Work

VASA-3D builds directly on the authors' own VASA-1 (a 2D audio-driven talking face generator) for its motion latent space and synthetic training data generation, and on GaussianAvatars for the 3D Gaussian representation bound to a FLAME mesh. The key conceptual departure from prior 3D head avatar work is replacing parametric expression models (3DMM/FLAME, trained on only hundreds of 3D scans) with a motion latent learned from 9.5K subjects in 2D video, then translating it to 3D via FLAME mapping plus dense Gaussian deformation residuals. It also departs from prior single-image 3D head methods by synthesizing diverse training frames with VASA-1 rather than relying solely on the input image, and from prior audio-driven 3D avatars by generating full head pose dynamics rather than just lip/jaw motion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first method to produce a fully animatable, free-viewpoint 3D Gaussian head from a single portrait image at real-time speed (75 FPS), with expression quality approaching that of the 2D generator used to create its training data. The paradigm of leveraging abundant 2D video data to enrich 3D expression modeling—bypassing the scarcity of 4D face scans—offers a scalable path for future avatar systems. Open limitations include the absence of back-of-head geometry, inability to handle dynamic accessories, and restriction to the head (no upper body), all of which the authors flag as future directions. The 93.91% user preference margin, while striking, is measured against baselines that do not generate head pose, so the true competitive gap in a fully comparable setting remains to be established.
