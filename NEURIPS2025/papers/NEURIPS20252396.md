## Problem

Existing continual learning methods predominantly target catastrophic forgetting while neglecting adversarial robustness, leaving models vulnerable in real-world deployments (e.g., autonomous driving) where environmental noise and adversarial perturbations co-occur. This paper tackles the Online Continual Adversarial Defense (OCAD) setting, where each new task is seen only once and the model must simultaneously maintain plasticity for new tasks, stability for prior knowledge, and robustness against adversarial samples.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 64×64 for TinyImageNet, variable for CUB200) in either clean or adversarially perturbed form, processed through a pre-trained ViT backbone. It outputs a probability vector over all classes encountered so far in the class-incremental stream. Adversarial inputs are generated on-the-fly via FGSM, PGD, BIM, CW, or AutoAttack during both training (for robust optimization) and evaluation (for robustness assessment).

## Method

DSEF employs a Siamese ViT backbone with a frozen static branch (global representations) and a trainable dynamic branch (local representations) that share parameters, plus a learnable strategy network that adaptively weights the two branches per sample. For stability, the Robust Dynamic Representation Optimization (RDRO) loss constrains dynamic backbone updates by minimizing MSE prediction divergence and MMD feature-distribution divergence between each historical expert's old and new outputs, augmented with an adversarial cross-entropy term. For positive transfer, the Mutual Information-Based Robust Feature Fusion (MBRFF) computes mutual information between each historical expert's predictions and true labels to derive softmax-normalized adaptive weights, then concatenates the weighted historical expert features with the current expert's features before classification. After each task, dynamic backbone weights are transferred into the static backbone to prevent over-regularization and gradually absorb new knowledge.

## Results

On Split CIFAR-10, DSEF achieves 90.72% clean accuracy and a 85.15% average accuracy across seven attack types (clean, FGSM, PGD, PGD-L2, BIM, CW, AutoAttack), substantially outperforming all baselines whose averages range from 16.05% (AIR) to 41.77% (DER-Adv). On Split CIFAR-100, DSEF reaches 68.17% clean accuracy and 56.86% average accuracy, compared to 21.82% (Refresh) and 8.44% (AIR). On the more challenging Split TinyImageNet, DSEF attains 51.10% average accuracy versus 21.55% (DER++) and 9.70% (AIR). The method's advantage is most pronounced under strong attacks (PGD, CW, AutoAttack), where baselines drop to near-chance levels while DSEF maintains 40–90% accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DSEF dominates all three baseline families on the average accuracy metric (aggregating clean + 6 adversarial attacks) across all four datasets. Against Refresh [13], DSEF improves average accuracy by 46.33 points on CIFAR-10 (85.15% vs 38.82%) and 35.04 points on CIFAR-100 (56.86% vs 21.82%), though Refresh retains a slight clean-accuracy edge on CIFAR-10 (92.47% vs 90.72%) and CUB200 (67.62% vs 58.47%). Against DER/DER++ [3], DSEF's advantage is even larger: 43.38 points on CIFAR-10 average (85.15% vs 41.77% for DER-Adv) and 36.58 points on CIFAR-100 (56.86% vs 20.28% for DER++). Against AIR [39], the most direct adversarial-defense baseline, DSEF outperforms by 69.10 points on CIFAR-10 average (85.15% vs 16.05%) and 48.42 points on CIFAR-100 (56.86% vs 8.44%), with AIR's clean accuracy also far lower (49.80% vs 90.72% on CIFAR-10). The gap is most dramatic under strong iterative attacks (PGD, CW, AutoAttack), where non-adversarial baselines collapse to near-chance (0–7%) while DSEF sustains 40–90% accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of dynamic expansion for continual learning (Adanet, Progressive Neural Networks, DyTox) and adversarial training (PGD-based defenses), but departs from both by introducing a Siamese ViT backbone that separates frozen global representations from trainable local ones, a design not previously explored in the continual learning literature. The closest prior work is AIR (Zhou & Hua, CVPR 2024), which also targets continual adversarial defense, but DSEF differs architecturally (Siamese vs. single backbone) and in its regularization strategy (MMD-based representation alignment plus mutual-information-weighted feature fusion rather than pseudo-replay). The MMD criterion and mutual information weighting are repurposed from domain adaptation and information-theoretic feature selection, respectively, and are combined here in a novel way to simultaneously preserve historical expert knowledge and promote positive transfer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap: most continual learning research assumes clean data, yet real deployments (autonomous driving, surveillance) face adversarial or noisy inputs, making robustness a first-class requirement alongside plasticity and stability. The OCAD formulation and the demonstrated 40+ point accuracy gaps over baselines under strong attacks (PGD, AutoAttack) suggest that robustness-aware continual learning is both necessary and achievable with the right architectural inductive biases. However, the evidence is somewhat thin—no error bars across seeds, only four datasets, and the clean accuracy on CUB200 (58.47%) actually trails the simplest baseline Refresh (67.62%), raising questions about the robustness-accuracy trade-off. Open questions include whether the Siamese overhead scales to longer task sequences, whether the MMD kernel choice is sensitive, and how the method performs under adaptive or transferable attacks beyond the fixed set tested.
