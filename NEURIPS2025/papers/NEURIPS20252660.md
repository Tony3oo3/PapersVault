## Problem

Multi-bit quantization networks enable a single model to support multiple precision levels, but their training cost scales linearly with the number of supported bit-widths because full-dataset updates are repeated for each precision. Additionally, supporting bit-widths outside the trained range requires a separate calibration or fine-tuning stage, further compounding the overhead. Existing coreset selection methods have not been adapted to this multi-bit setting, where cross-bit-width interactions affect sample importance.

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 64×64 for TinyImageNet, 224×224 for ImageNet-1K) and produces class labels. A single full-precision parent model is trained to generate multiple reduced-precision child models switchable at inference across a range of bit-widths (1–32 bits for ResNets, 2–8 bits for ViTs). The coreset sampling strategy reduces the effective training set by up to 90% per bit-width, with each child model training on its own compact subset rather than the full dataset.

## Method

The paper proposes two complementary techniques. First, weight bias correction adjusts the mean and variance of quantized weights to match their full-precision counterparts (Eq. 2), aligning activation distributions across bit-widths at the source and enabling all child models to share a single set of batch normalization parameters—eliminating the costly calibration phase entirely. Second, bit-wise coreset sampling computes per-bit-width importance scores using a TDDS-style context-vector method under a dedicated bit-wise training schedule (Algorithm 2) that isolates each precision's gradients, then selects samples via temperature-based stochastic sampling (τ=0.5) that is periodically resampled during training. The key enabling insight is cross-bit-width implicit knowledge transfer: gradients from different bit-widths on the same sample remain highly aligned (angle < 28°), so each child model can train on a distinct compact subset while still benefiting from the collective learning signal propagated through shared parameters.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with PreActResNet-20, the proposed method with 80% coreset pruning achieves 92.97% average accuracy across bit-widths (1–32 bit) in 1.52 GPU hours, a 7.88× speedup over dedicated training (11.97 GPU hours) with only a 0.13% accuracy drop. On CIFAR-100 with ResNet-18, the method reaches 70.26% average accuracy in 1.47 GPU hours (7.61× speedup), matching the dedicated baseline's 70.21%. On ImageNet-1K with ResNet-50, the method achieves 72.51% average accuracy in 6.94 GPU hours (5.75× speedup) versus 72.04% for dedicated training at 39.91 GPU hours. For ViTs (DeiT-T, DeiT-S, Swin-T) on ImageNet-1K at 60% pruning, the method achieves 2.32–3.41× speedups with ≤0.5% accuracy loss.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the standard multi-bit baseline Any-Precision [13], the proposed method (with bias correction, no coreset) achieves higher average accuracy on both CIFAR-10 (93.46% vs. 93.31%) and CIFAR-100 (71.84% vs. 71.47%) while reducing GPU hours by eliminating the calibration phase (7.52 vs. 8.76 on CIFAR-10). When coreset sampling is added, the method retains competitive accuracy (92.97% on CIFAR-10, 70.26% on CIFAR-100) at a fraction of the training cost. Against existing coreset selection methods at 80% pruning on CIFAR-10, the proposed bit-wise sampling (92.97% avg.) substantially outperforms TDDS (88.54%), Moderate (88.26%), Random (89.94%), Entropy (86.21%), EL2N (81.07%), and Forgetting (78.14%), with the largest gap over Forgetting exceeding 14 percentage points. On CIFAR-100 at 80% pruning, the method (70.26%) again dominates all baselines, with TDDS at 54.23% and Moderate at 58.33%. On ImageNet-1K, the method (72.51%) outperforms TDDS (69.42%), Moderate (69.74%), and Random (71.36%) while achieving a 5.75× GPU hour reduction over dedicated training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends two established lines of work: multi-bit quantization network training (Any-Precision, CoQuant, MBQuant) and gradient-based coreset selection (TDDS, EL2N, Moderate). It departs from the standard multi-bit paradigm by replacing per-bit-width batch normalization layers with a weight-space bias correction that enables BN sharing, and by generalizing coreset selection from single-precision to per-bit-width subsets. The key conceptual departure is the discovery of cross-bit-width gradient alignment, which justifies training each child model on a distinct data subset rather than the full dataset, a phenomenon not previously identified in the multi-bit quantization literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 7.88× training speedup with minimal accuracy loss makes multi-bit quantization networks substantially more practical for resource-constrained deployment, removing the calibration bottleneck that has limited adoption. The weight bias correction technique is simple (a single rescaling and shift operation) and generalizes across CNN and ViT architectures. However, evaluations are limited to computer vision tasks, the theoretical analysis of implicit knowledge transfer relies on an informal linear-classifier argument, and the 1-bit case still requires a separate BN layer. Extending these techniques to generative models and large-scale language tasks, as the authors note, remains an open and computationally challenging direction.
