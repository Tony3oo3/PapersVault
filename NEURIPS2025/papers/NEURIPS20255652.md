## Problem

In the Stochastic Block Model and Degree-Corrected Block Model, cluster recovery becomes fundamentally impossible when intra-cluster and inter-cluster edge densities are equal (p = q), because the only signal is the global edge-density gap. The Popularity-Adjusted Block Model (PABM) introduces separate popularity parameters for intra- and inter-cluster connections, but no prior work characterized the optimal clustering error rate under this richer model. This paper asks whether local differences in individual vertex connectivity patterns can sustain cluster separability even when the traditional edge-density signal vanishes.

## Input / Output

The method takes an undirected graph's symmetric adjacency matrix A ∈ {0,1}^{n×n} and the number of clusters k as input, and produces a cluster assignment vector ẑ ∈ [k]^n as output. The theoretical contribution additionally yields a closed-form optimal error rate expressed as (1/n) Σᵢ exp(−Chernoff(i, z*)), where each per-vertex Chernoff divergence depends on the full set of popularity parameters λᵢᵃ. For the spectral algorithms, the embedding dimension is k² (not k), reflecting the fact that the expected adjacency matrix of a PABM has rank between k and k², so top-k eigenvectors alone may miss structural information.

## Method

The paper derives the optimal clustering error rate under PABM by formulating per-vertex hypothesis testing problems and computing the Chernoff divergence (via Rényi divergence of order t) between the true model and each single-vertex misassignment alternative. The key result is that the optimal error rate equals an average of per-vertex exponential terms exp(−Chernoff(i,z*)), where Chernoff(i,z*) = (nρₙ/2k)(δ_{zᵢ*} + min_{a≠zᵢ*} δₐ) and each δ_b aggregates squared differences between √(λᵢᵢⁿλⱼⁱⁿp₀) and √(λᵢᵒᵘᵗλⱼᵒᵘᵗq₀) over vertices in cluster b. This reveals two novel phenomena absent in SBM/DCBM: (i) when p₀ = q₀, recovery is still possible if the intra- and inter-cluster popularity coefficients differ, and (ii) the error rate is non-monotonic in the inter-cluster edge fraction ξ, first increasing then decreasing. For practical algorithms, the paper evaluates spectral methods that embed vertices using k² eigenvectors of A (rather than k) followed by subspace clustering or orthogonal spectral clustering, demonstrating that these higher-dimensional embeddings outperform traditional k-eigenvector approaches on both synthetic PABM graphs and real datasets.

## Results

On seven real-world datasets, PABM-specific spectral methods (pabm using k² eigenvectors with subspace clustering, and osc using orthogonal spectral clustering) consistently outperform traditional k-eigenvector methods. The pabm variant achieves the highest accuracy on 5 of 7 datasets, including 0.99 on LiveJournal-top2, 0.88 on MNIST, 0.74 on CIFAR-10, and 0.63 on Fashion-MNIST. The osc variant matches or leads on politicalBlogs (0.95) and Citeseer (0.56). On synthetic PABM graphs with p₀=q₀ (no edge-density signal), pabm and osc recover clusters when degree heterogeneity is present (c>0), while sbm and dcbm fail entirely, confirming the theoretical prediction that popularity patterns alone suffice for recovery.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The pabm variant (Noroozi et al., 2021) dominates on 5 of 7 real datasets, achieving 0.99 on LiveJournal-top2 (vs. 0.61 for dcbm and 0.56 for sbm), 0.88 on MNIST (vs. 0.54 for dcbm), and 0.74 on CIFAR-10 (vs. 0.43 for dcbm and 0.17 for sbm). The osc variant (Koo et al., 2023) matches pabm on politicalBlogs (0.95) and leads on Citeseer (0.56 vs. 0.45 for pabm), but underperforms pabm on MNIST (0.74 vs. 0.88) and CIFAR-10 (0.58 vs. 0.74). The sbm baseline (Zhang, 2024) is consistently the weakest PABM-tailored method, scoring below 0.65 on every dataset. The tcsc method (Yuan et al., 2025) is highly inconsistent, ranging from 0.98 on LiveJournal to 0.11 on MNIST. The gspc method (Bhadra et al., 2025) is competitive on politicalBlogs (0.95) and CIFAR-10 (0.62) but trails pabm on most other datasets. Notably, sklearn (normalized Laplacian + k-means) is competitive on Citeseer (0.58) and CIFAR-10 (0.71), suggesting that for some real graphs the cluster structure is well-captured by low-dimensional spectral embeddings even without explicit PABM modeling.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the information-theoretic characterization of optimal clustering error rates from the SBM (Zhang and Zhou, 2016) and DCBM (Gao et al., 2018) to the Popularity-Adjusted Block Model (Sengupta and Chen, 2018), which introduces separate intra- and inter-cluster popularity parameters rather than a single uniform degree-correction factor. The analysis leverages the Chernoff/Rényi divergence framework from Dreveton et al. (2024) and the instance-optimal (rather than minimax) formulation of Yun and Proutière (2016). On the algorithmic side, the paper builds on spectral methods designed for PABM's higher-rank structure (rank up to k²) from Noroozi et al. (2021) and Koo et al. (2023), which use k² eigenvectors instead of the traditional k.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central finding—that cluster recovery remains possible in PABM even when intra- and inter-cluster edge densities are equal (p=q), a regime where SBM and DCBM reduce to uninformative Erdős-Rényi or Chung-Lu graphs—challenges the long-standing assumption that edge-density contrast is the essential signal for community detection. The non-monotonic dependence of error on inter-cluster edge density further reveals a trade-off between noise and popularity-pattern information that has no analogue in prior models. Open questions include developing principled model-selection criteria to distinguish DCBM from PABM, inferring the number of clusters k and the rank of the connectivity matrix, and providing theoretical guarantees for the practical spectral algorithms (OSC, subspace clustering) that empirically achieve near-optimal rates.
