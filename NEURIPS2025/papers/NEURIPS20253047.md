## Problem

Most vision-language models are optimized for short-context tasks, and existing long-context VLMs either rely on compression/selection modules that add overhead or fail to show consistent performance gains as visual input length increases. The optimal training strategies—sampling formulations, progressive schedules, and data recipes—for native long-context VLMs remain largely unexplored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253047/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes arbitrary combinations of video frames (sampled at 2 FPS, up to 512 frames), high-resolution images (tiled into up to 12 s×s patches per image), multi-page documents, and text queries, all packed into a single sequence of up to 128K tokens. A key design constraint is that text tokens are always fully preserved; the visual token budget is derived as the remainder after text, and visual content is adaptively degraded (fewer frames, fewer tiles) to fit within that budget rather than truncating the supervisory text signal.

## Method

Eagle 2.5 uses a LLaVA-style architecture (SigLIP encoder → MLP projector → Qwen2.5 LLM) with an area-prioritized tiling strategy (Image Area Preservation) that selects tile grids maximizing retained image area (≥60%) while matching the original aspect ratio, avoiding the rigid downsampling of prior tiling methods. On top of this, Automatic Degradation Sampling (ADS) formulates a constrained optimization over tile count per image and temporal sampling count, solved via a dual-phase degradation process (temporal first, then tiling) that guarantees complete text retention while maximizing visual information density. Training follows a progressive mixed post-training schedule that sequentially expands the maximum sequence length from 32K to 64K to 128K tokens, with length-balanced packing at each stage. The data recipe combines diverse open-source video/document/text data with the newly curated Eagle-Video-110K dataset, which uses CLIP-based similarity thresholding for diversity-driven collection and a dual annotation pipeline: top-down story-level QA from human-annotated chapters and bottom-up clip-level QA from GPT-4o with temporal and contextual anchors to bridge localized annotations to full-video understanding.

## Results

Eagle2.5-8B achieves 72.4 on Video-MME (w/o subtitle) with 512 input frames, matching GPT-4o (71.9) and approaching Qwen2.5-VL-72B (73.3) and InternVL2.5-78B (72.1) with a fraction of the parameters. On long-video benchmarks, it scores 77.6 on MLVU and 66.4 on LongVideobench, surpassing all same-size open-source models (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL-8B: 70.2/56.0) and even the 78B InternVL2.5 (75.7/63.6). On CG-Bench, Eagle2.5-8B achieves 55.8/46.6/45.6/13.4 (Clue/Long/Open/mIoU), substantially outperforming Gemini-1.5-Pro (50.9/37.8/28.7/3.85) and Claude-3.5-Sonnet (56.5/40.3/35.6/4.17). On image benchmarks, it reaches a 75.6 average score, competitive with Qwen2.5-VL-8B (75.6) and GPT-4o (74.9).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against same-size open-source models, Eagle2.5-8B consistently leads: it outperforms Qwen2.5-VL-8B by 5.2 points on MVBench (74.8 vs 69.6), 11.5 on Perception-Test (82.0 vs 70.5), 7.2 on EgoSchema (72.2 vs 65.0), and 7.3 on Video-MME (72.4 vs 65.1). It also surpasses InternVL2.5-8B on MLVU (77.6 vs 68.9) and LVBench (66.4 vs 60.0), and beats VideoChat-Flash-8B on Video-MME (72.4 vs 65.3) and Perception-Test (82.0 vs 76.2). Against much larger 72B/78B models, Eagle2.5-8B matches or exceeds them on most long-video metrics: it beats InternVL2.5-78B on MLVU (77.6 vs 75.7), LVBench (66.4 vs 63.6), and Video-MME (72.4 vs 72.1), and beats Qwen2.5-VL-72B on MVBench (74.8 vs 70.4) and MLVU (77.6 vs 74.6), though it trails on EgoSchema (72.2 vs 76.2) and Video-MME (72.4 vs 73.3). Against closed-source models, Eagle2.5-8B slightly edges GPT-4o on Video-MME (72.4 vs 71.9) and substantially outperforms Gemini-1.5-Pro on CG-Bench (46.6/45.6/13.4 vs 37.8/28.7/3.85 for Long/Open/mIoU) and HourVideo (44.5/41.8 vs 37.2/37.4), while trailing Gemini-1.5-Pro on Video-MME (72.4 vs 75.0). On CG-Bench, it matches Claude-3.5-Sonnet on Clue (55.8 vs 56.5) but leads by 6.3 on Long and 10.0 on Open, with a 3× advantage on mIoU (13.4 vs 4.17).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LLaVA-style architecture (SigLIP + MLP projector + Qwen2.5 LLM) and the Eagle 2 training pipeline, but departs fundamentally from prior long-context VLM approaches (LongVILA, LongVA, LongViTA) that rely on compression or selection modules. Instead of reducing visual tokens before LLM processing, Eagle 2.5 natively extends context length through an information-first sampling strategy (Automatic Degradation Sampling and Image Area Preservation) and a progressive mixed post-training schedule that incrementally expands context from 32K to 128K. The data recipe also departs from prior long-video datasets by introducing a dual top-down/bottom-up annotation paradigm (story-level chapters + clip-level QA with temporal anchors) rather than relying solely on shot-level segmentation or single-pass GPT-4o captioning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that an 8B-parameter model achieves competitive performance with GPT-4o and 72B+ open-source models on long-video benchmarks, suggesting that training strategy and data curation can substitute for raw parameter scale in long-context multimodal understanding. The progressive training finding—that gradual context expansion outperforms direct training at the target length—offers a reusable recipe for the community. However, the work requires 128 H100 GPUs, no code or model weights are released, and the generalizability of the ADS/IAP techniques to other architectures or modalities remains untested. Open questions include whether these gains persist at even longer contexts (beyond 128K tokens) and whether the dual-annotation data pipeline can be scaled without the current reliance on GPT-4o for quality control.
