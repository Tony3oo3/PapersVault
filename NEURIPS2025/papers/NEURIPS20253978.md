## Problem

Prior sample complexity results for offline average-reward RL with function approximation rely on restrictive structural assumptions such as ergodicity, unichain structure, or linearity of the MDP. This paper establishes the first finite-time sample complexity bounds under the much milder weakly communicating MDP assumption, and extends the analysis to single-trajectory (β-mixing) data, which had not been addressed in the average-reward offline setting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253978/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes a precollected offline dataset of transitions (s_i, a_i, r_i, s_i')—either IID or generated from a single β-mixing trajectory under a behavior policy—along with a (possibly infinite) function space F and a number of iterations K. It outputs a greedy policy π(a|s) = argmax_a f_K(s,a) and the final Q-value function f_K. A key design choice is that the function space range can grow linearly with iteration index k (bounded by kR) in the non-relative variant, or remain fixed at ±2‖Q^{π*}‖_∞ in the relative variant, reflecting the unbounded nature of undiscounted value functions.

## Method

The core algorithm, Anchored Fitted Q-Iteration (Anc-F-QI), modifies standard FQI by replacing the direct Bellman regression step with a convex combination: f_{k+1} = (1−λ_k)·f_0 + λ_k·T̂f_k, where T̂f_k is the empirical Bellman regression and λ_k = k/(k+2). This anchor term (equivalent to weight decay toward the initial point f_0 = 0) is essential because standard value iteration provably fails to converge in the average-reward setting even in the tabular case. The paper proves an L_p policy-error bound of O(1/K) + O(K·max_k ‖ε_k‖_{p,μ}), then controls the regression error ε_k via covering numbers (IID) or pseudo-dimension under β-mixing (single-trajectory), yielding Õ(1/ε^6) and Õ(1/ε^12) sample complexities respectively. A second algorithm, Relative Anchored FQI (R-Anc-F-QI), additionally subtracts (max T̂f_k + min T̂f_k)/2·1 from each iterate (the classical relative value iteration normalization), which keeps the function space bounded and removes the growing-range inefficiency, improving the sample complexity to Õ(1/ε^4) and Õ(1/ε^8).

## Results

The paper establishes the first finite-time sample complexity bounds for average-reward offline RL with general function approximation under weakly communicating MDPs. Anchored Fitted Q-Iteration (Anc-F-QI) achieves Õ(1/ε⁶) sample complexity with IID data and Õ(1/ε¹²) with β-mixing single-trajectory data. The Relative Anchored Fitted Q-Iteration (R-Anc-F-QI), which adds a normalization step, improves these to Õ(1/ε⁴) and Õ(1/ε⁸) respectively. All results require only the Bellman optimality equation (satisfied by weakly communicating MDPs) and full coverage coefficients, without ergodicity, unichain, or linearity assumptions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines; the comparison is in terms of structural assumptions on the MDP and data. Prior work by Ozdaglar et al. [63] requires the MDP to be ergodic (uniform mixing) and considers only IID data with partial coverage. Gabbianelli et al. [30] requires the MDP to be unichain and linear, again with IID data and partial coverage. This paper relaxes the MDP assumption to weakly communicating (a strictly broader class: ergodic ⊂ unichain ⊂ weakly communicating), removes the linearity requirement, and extends to single-trajectory β-mixing data. The trade-off is that this paper uses full coverage coefficients rather than the partial coverage used by prior works, which the authors identify as a limitation and direction for future work. The relative normalization mechanism (R-Anc-F-QI) provides a strict improvement over the base Anc-F-QI, reducing the ε-dependence from ε⁻⁶ to ε⁻⁴ (IID) and from ε⁻¹² to ε⁻⁸ (single-trajectory).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Fitted Q-Iteration (FQI) to the average-reward offline RL setting by incorporating the Halpern anchoring mechanism (a convex combination with the initial iterate, analogous to weight decay) and the classical relative value iteration normalization. Prior offline average-reward RL results with function approximation [63, 30] required either ergodicity or unichain-plus-linearity assumptions; this work relaxes both to weakly communicating MDPs with general (star-shaped) function spaces. The anchoring mechanism, previously shown to yield finite-time convergence for tabular average-reward value iteration [50], is here identified as the essential ingredient that makes the L_p-norm analysis tractable in the function-approximation setting, where standard FQI iterates are unbounded without discounting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first sample complexity result for offline average-reward RL with general function approximation under weakly communicating MDPs, a setting that covers many practical applications where discounting is not used. The key open limitation is the reliance on full coverage coefficients (Assumptions 5/6); extending to partial coverage, as done in prior discounted-reward work, would significantly broaden applicability. The paper also identifies variance reduction techniques as a promising direction to further tighten the O(1/ε⁴) and O(1/ε⁸) sample complexities. For practitioners, the practical takeaway is that a simple anchor/weight-decay step in FQI is not merely a regularization trick but is structurally necessary for convergence guarantees in the undiscounted setting.
