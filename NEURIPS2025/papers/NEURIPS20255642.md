## Problem

Existing open-vocabulary scene graph generation (OVSGG) methods adopt an object-centric paradigm that treats all instances of the same category identically, failing to distinguish interacting from non-interacting objects. This causes noisy pseudo-supervision during knowledge infusion (mismatched object pairs in weakly-supervised pre-training) and ambiguous bipartite query matching during supervised fine-tuning (non-interacting distractors competing with true interaction targets).
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255642/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image as input and produces a structured scene graph where nodes are objects with bounding boxes and open-vocabulary category labels, and edges are relationship triplets (subject, predicate, object). The model operates under the Scene Graph Detection (SGDET) protocol, meaning no ground-truth object labels or boxes are available at inference. Evaluation is conducted under two open-vocabulary settings: OvR-SGG (novel relations only) and OvD+R-SGG (novel objects and relations simultaneously), with base/novel class splits defined per dataset.

## Method

ACC introduces an interaction-driven paradigm across both pipeline stages. For knowledge infusion, it designs a bidirectional interaction prompt that encodes each triplet from both the subject's viewpoint (e.g., 'man hold surfboard') and the object's perspective with a counter-action (e.g., 'surfboard held by man'), leveraging Grounding DINO's attention mechanism to ground interaction-relevant objects and reducing noisy pseudo-labels. For knowledge transfer, it employs a two-step interaction-guided query selection: Step I scores visual tokens by combined object-relation similarity, and Step II re-ranks using interaction tokens derived from predicted triplets to prioritize interacting objects over non-interacting distractors. Additionally, an interaction-consistent knowledge distillation loss combines point-wise visual-concept retention (L1 alignment on negative samples) with relative-interaction retention distillation (Frobenius-norm alignment of inter-pair structure similarity matrices between teacher and student), pushing relational foreground away from background while preserving general knowledge.

## Results

On the challenging OvD+R-SGG setting (novel objects + novel relations) of Visual Genome, ACC with Swin-B achieves 23.19% R@100 on Joint Base+Novel, 23.29% on Novel(Object), and 21.73% on Novel(Relation), surpassing the prior SOTA OvSGTR by +2.16%, +1.59%, and +3.51% respectively. On the OvR-SGG setting, ACC (Swin-B) reaches 29.28% R@100 on Base+Novel(Relation) and 24.66% on Novel(Relation), outperforming RAHP by +3.54% and +4.74%. ACC also generalizes to GQA (20.63% vs. 19.14% R@100 Joint) and PSG (21.71% vs. 19.50% R@100 Joint), and transfers to the HOI detection task HICO-DET (40.19% vs. 39.04% R@100 Joint).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ACC outperforms all 12 baselines across every metric on all four benchmarks. The most direct competitor, OvSGTR (ECCV'24), is surpassed by +4.90% R@100 on Joint Base+Novel (Swin-T) and +2.16% (Swin-B) on VG OvD+R-SGG, with even larger gaps on Novel(Relation) (+8.28% Swin-T, +3.51% Swin-B). RAHP (AAAI'25), the second most recent method, is beaten by +1.78% R@100 on Novel(Relation) under OvR-SGG (Swin-T). VS³ (CVPR'23) and OpenSGen (ICMR'25) are intermediate baselines that ACC exceeds by 14+ points on Joint R@100. The older closed-set methods (IMP, MOTIFS, VCTREE, TDE) score near zero on novel categories, confirming they cannot generalize beyond predefined classes. On GQA, ACC gains +1.49% R@100 Joint and +2.40% on Novel(Relation) over OvSGTR; on PSG, +2.21% Joint and +1.62% Novel(Relation); and on HICO-DET, +1.15% Joint and +2.54% Novel(Relation), demonstrating consistent transferability of the interaction-centric paradigm.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the two-stage OVSGG pipeline established by OvSGTR (Chen et al., ECCV'24) and the Grounding DINO architecture, shifting the paradigm from object-centric to interaction-centric in both knowledge infusion and transfer. While prior methods (VS³, OvSGTR, RAHP) rely on isolated object-category prompts for pseudo-labeling and uniform query selection during SFT, ACC introduces bidirectional interaction prompts for grounding and a two-step interaction-guided query selection that explicitly prioritizes interacting objects. The interaction-consistent KD component draws on relational knowledge distillation (Park et al., CVPR'19) but adapts it to model inter-pair relational consistency specific to scene graph triplets rather than generic feature alignment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The interaction-centric framing addresses a concrete and recurring failure mode in OVSGG—mismatched object pairs during both pre-training and SFT—that prior methods handle only implicitly. The demonstrated transfer to HICO-DET suggests the interaction-aware query selection and distillation principles may generalize beyond SGG to other compositional recognition tasks. However, the computational overhead of Step II (forward prediction for self-enhancement) and the diminishing returns when combining both IGQS and ICKD components limit immediate practicality. Open questions include whether the interaction-centric paradigm scales to denser scenes with many overlapping interactions and whether the reliance on LLM-generated counter-actions introduces fragility for rare or novel predicates.
