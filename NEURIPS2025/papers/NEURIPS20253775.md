## Problem

State-of-the-art reasoning LLMs (OpenAI o1, DeepSeek R1) rely on large-scale RL to elicit complex chain-of-thought reasoning, yet their critical training details remain undisclosed, leaving the community unable to reproduce results. Naive GRPO applied to Qwen2.5-32B achieves only 30 points on AIME 2024 versus DeepSeek's 47, suffering from entropy collapse, reward noise, and training instability. This paper identifies and resolves these obstacles through a fully open-sourced algorithm, code, and dataset.

## Input / Output

The method takes math problem prompts from the DAPO-Math-17K dataset (17K prompts with integer answers, transformed from diverse original formats like fractions and radicals to ensure reliable rule-based parsing) and a Qwen2.5-32B base model as the initial policy. For each prompt, the policy generates G=16 long chain-of-thought responses (up to 20,480 tokens, with a 4,096-token soft-punishment zone beyond the 16,384-token expected maximum), and the model is updated via policy gradient. The final output is an updated policy model that produces integer answers after extended reasoning.

## Method

DAPO modifies GRPO's objective with four targeted techniques. (1) Clip-Higher decouples the PPO clipping bounds into ε_low=0.2 and ε_high=0.28, giving low-probability 'exploration' tokens more room to increase in probability and preventing entropy collapse. (2) Dynamic Sampling over-samples prompts and discards groups where all G responses are correct or all incorrect (zero advantage), ensuring every prompt in the batch contributes a non-zero gradient. (3) Token-Level Policy Gradient Loss replaces GRPO's sample-level averaging (mean within sequence, then mean across samples) with a global token-level average (sum over all tokens divided by total token count), so each token receives equal gradient weight regardless of its sequence length. (4) Overlong Reward Shaping applies a soft, length-proportional penalty in a buffer zone near the max generation length instead of a hard punitive reward for truncated samples, reducing reward noise. The KL divergence term is removed entirely, and a simple ±1 rule-based correctness reward is used.

## Results

DAPO achieves 50 points on AIME 2024 (avg@32) using the Qwen2.5-32B base model, outperforming the previous state-of-the-art DeepSeek-R1-Zero-Qwen-32B (47 points) while requiring only 50% of the training steps. The naive GRPO baseline reaches only 30 points, and each progressive DAPO technique contributes incremental gains: Overlong Filtering (+6), Clip-Higher (+2), Soft Overlong Punishment (+3), Token-level Loss (+1), and Dynamic Sampling (+8). Evaluation is conducted with temperature 1.0, top-p 0.7, and 32 repeated trials for stability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DAPO outperforms both baselines on AIME 2024 (avg@32). Against DeepSeek-R1-Zero-Qwen-32B (47 points), DAPO achieves 50 points—a 3-point improvement—while using only 50% of the training steps, making it both more accurate and more sample-efficient. Against the Naive GRPO baseline (30 points), DAPO achieves a 20-point gain, demonstrating that the four key techniques (Clip-Higher, Dynamic Sampling, Token-Level Policy Gradient Loss, and Overlong Reward Shaping) collectively address critical failure modes such as entropy collapse, reward noise, and training instability that limit vanilla GRPO in long-CoT RL scenarios. The ablation in Table 1 shows that Dynamic Sampling contributes the single largest incremental gain (+8 points), while Clip-Higher and Soft Overlong Punishment each add 2–3 points.

## Relation to Prior Work

DAPO is a direct extension of GRPO (from DeepSeekMath) and PPO, modifying the clipped surrogate objective in four specific ways: decoupling the upper and lower clip bounds (Clip-Higher), filtering zero-gradient groups via Dynamic Sampling, switching from sample-level to token-level loss aggregation, and adding a length-aware penalty for truncated responses. It also removes the KL divergence term that GRPO inherits from PPO, arguing it is unnecessary when the policy is expected to diverge substantially from the reference during long-CoT training. The paper positions itself explicitly as a reproducibility intervention: the community's failure to match DeepSeek-R1's reported results with naive GRPO (30 vs. 47 on AIME) is attributed to these omitted practical details rather than to a fundamentally different algorithm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By fully open-sourcing the algorithm, training code (on verl), and the DAPO-Math-17K dataset, this paper provides the first complete, reproducible recipe for large-scale LLM RL that achieves competitive reasoning performance (50 on AIME 2024 with Qwen2.5-32B, beating DeepSeek-R1-Zero-Qwen-32B with half the training steps). The four techniques address concrete, previously under-diagnosed failure modes—entropy collapse from symmetric clipping, vanishing gradients from all-correct groups, and reward noise from truncated samples—making them immediately actionable for practitioners. Limitations include single-turn RL only, restriction to math problems with integer answers, and evaluation on a single model family; open questions include whether the techniques transfer to multi-turn settings, proof-based mathematics, and non-math domains, and whether the specific hyperparameter choices (e.g., ε_high = 0.28) generalize across model scales.
