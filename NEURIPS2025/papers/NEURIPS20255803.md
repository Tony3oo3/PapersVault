## Problem

Abductive Learning (ABL) integrates machine learning with logical reasoning in a loop, but the nondeterminism of abduction causes training instability when the knowledge base is large: the abduction space grows exponentially, and the model oscillates among many plausible yet incorrect concept labels. Prior improvements focus on refining candidate selection within this space but treat the knowledge base as a static black box, ignoring its internal hierarchical structure that could be exploited to manage complexity during training.

## Input / Output

The method consumes input sequences (e.g., 2d digit images from CIFAR-10/CIFAR-100/MNIST/EMNIST, chessboard piece images, or Chinese legal judgment text via BERT) alongside a first-order logic knowledge base expressed as Horn clauses. It outputs a symbolic concept label per input element (e.g., digit identity, chess piece type, sentencing factor) such that the full label sequence, combined with the knowledge base, logically entails a target label (arithmetic sum, boolean attack relation, or real-valued sentence length). Notably, the knowledge base is not used monolithically but is partitioned into sub-bases that are progressively activated across training phases, with training data dynamically scheduled to match each phase's concept domain.

## Method

C-ABL partitions the knowledge base into a sequence of sub-bases via a dependency-graph algorithm that groups rules by concept-label clusters, orders them by topological precedence (simpler/foundational rules first), and ensures each sub-base is logically sound and complete over its concept domain. Training proceeds in P curriculum phases: in phase p, only sub-base KB_p is active, the abduction space is confined to newly introduced concepts (|Z_p \ Z_{p-1}|^m rather than |Z|^m), and the model trains until per-concept accuracy exceeds the uniform-guessing baseline before advancing. Theoretical analysis proves the abduction space shrinks exponentially in sequence length m, iteration complexity is O(|S|²ρ²/ε²), and phase transitions preserve both logical consistency (conclusions in earlier phases remain valid) and topological continuity (model spaces form a nested sequence of compact Stone spaces), preventing catastrophic forgetting.

## Results

On the 4-digit decimal addition task, C-ABL achieves 76.30% accuracy with 132.6 min training time, outperforming ABL (73.05%, 253.6 min) and A³BL (73.28%, 291.2 min). On the harder 3-digit hexadecimal setting, C-ABL reaches 66.67% accuracy in 55.5 min versus 64.14% (99.7 min) for ABL and 65.81% (109.8 min) for A³BL. On the chess attack task, C-ABL achieves 86.79% accuracy and converges in 2,553 iterations, compared to 73.75% (4,560 iters) for ABL and 74.96% (3,389 iters) for A³BL. On judicial sentencing with 50% labeled data, C-ABL attains F1=0.920, MAE=0.722, and MSE=0.773, improving over ABL (F1=0.910, MAE=0.773, MSE=0.874) while requiring fewer tokens to converge (2.1M vs 3.1M).

## Baselines & Comparisons

C-ABL outperforms the original ABL [Huang et al., 2024] across all digit addition settings, with the largest gap on 4-digit decimal (+3.25%) and 3-digit hexadecimal (+2.53%), while also cutting training time roughly in half (e.g., 132.6 min vs 253.6 min on decimal d=4). Against the stronger A³BL variant [He et al., 2024], C-ABL wins on all settings except decimal d=1 (71.77% vs 72.06%, a negligible 0.29% difference), and the advantage grows with complexity: +3.02% on decimal d=4 and +0.86% on hex d=3. Among neuro-symbolic baselines, DeepStochLog and LTN are the closest competitors but remain 4–5% below C-ABL on the hardest settings; NeurASP and DeepProbLog fail to scale beyond d=1–2 (N/A entries). On the chess attack task, where the binary target label inflates the abduction space, C-ABL's advantage is most dramatic: 86.79% vs 73.75% (ABL) and 74.96% (A³BL), a 12–13% improvement. On judicial sentencing, C-ABL improves F1 by 0.010 and reduces MAE by 0.051 over ABL at 50% labeled data, while converging 32% faster in tokens.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Abductive Learning (ABL) by introducing a curriculum over the knowledge base itself, rather than over input data as in prior neuro-symbolic curriculum work (Marconato et al., 2023a; Wei et al., 2025). It departs from the dominant line of ABL improvements (Huang et al., 2021; Tao et al., 2024; He et al., 2024) that refine consistency optimization within a fixed abduction space, arguing instead that the space's size is the root bottleneck and that the knowledge base's internal dependency structure should be exploited. The partitioning algorithm draws on knowledge-base decomposition ideas from the logic community (Amir and McIlraith, 2000, 2005), repurposing them for training dynamics rather than inference efficiency.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

C-ABL provides a principled, structure-aware alternative to black-box ABL that could make abductive learning tractable for large, hierarchical knowledge bases (law, medicine, complex games) where the abduction space is otherwise intractable. The theoretical guarantees—quadratic iteration complexity in |S| and topological continuity via nested Stone spaces—give a clear mechanistic explanation for the observed stability gains. Key limitations remain: the method assumes the KB has exploitable modular/dependency structure, uses a fixed accuracy threshold for phase transitions, and requires a pre-defined human-curated KB; adaptive schedules and KB acquisition from data are left open.
