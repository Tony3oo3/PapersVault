## Problem

Standard DNN-based object detectors trained on 8-bit LDR sRGB images produce NaN or severely degraded performance when fed 24-bit HDR RAW inputs (up to 140 dB dynamic range) due to extreme luminance contrasts that destabilize gradient propagation. Existing solutions—handcrafted ISP pipelines and AI-ISP methods—remain constrained by perceptually-motivated tone mapping and redundant processing stages, making them suboptimal for machine vision tasks on resource-constrained embedded platforms.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253262/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes 24-bit HDR RAW images from the SONY IMX490 sensor (≈140 dB dynamic range), linearly demosaiced to 3-channel RGB and resized to 1280×1280 for training; the Lite variant processes full 4K (4096×2160) resolution at 45 FPS on NVIDIA Jetson AGX Orin in 16-bit float. The tone mapper outputs an 8-bit LDR sRGB image that is fed directly into a standard detector (Faster R-CNN, YOLOv3, Sparse R-CNN, or Deformable DETR), which produces bounding boxes, class labels, and confidence scores. A performance-transfer mode allows fine-tuning only the tone mapper and detection head from LDR-pretrained weights, requiring just 33.6% of total parameters to be updated.

## Method

The method combines two lightweight modules optimized end-to-end with the detector. First, a Neural Photometric Calibration module (3-layer Conv-ReLU-Sigmoid) predicts a spatially varying scale map K in log-space and a bias b, normalizing the extreme HDR radiance (day ≈10⁷ cd/m², night ≈10⁴ cd/m²) into a unified scale via I̅ = (K−b)·R + b, with a sigmoid-gated interpolation between day and night key radiances. Second, a Scaling-Invariant Tone Mapper (TM_SI) consists of 4 layers of Conv-BN-ReLU with all bias terms removed; the paper proves that this bias-free architecture satisfies f(αx) = α·f(x), making it a natural local tone mapper that preserves relative luminance structure without introducing bias gain. The tone mapper is pretrained with the self-supervised NLPD loss to compress dynamic range while enhancing local contrast, then jointly fine-tuned with the detector using standard detection losses. A theoretical analysis using a Gaussian Mixture Model shows that gradient magnitude scales quadratically with per-component dynamic range, motivating the calibration step.

## Results

On the RoD dataset (24-bit HDR RAW, 1280×1280), the proposed method achieves mAP 49.8 with Faster R-CNN (ResNet-50) and mAP 45.1 with YOLOv3 (DarkNet-53), outperforming the best baseline RAODNet by 3.7% and 1.6% respectively. The lightweight variant (Ours Lite, 16 channels) achieves mAP 47.8 (Faster R-CNN) at only 8.8 ms latency on NVIDIA Jetson AGX Orin, processing 4K HDR RAW at 45 FPS. Directly feeding 24-bit HDR RAW into detectors yields NaN (non-convergence), while the full HDR ISP pipeline achieves mAP 45.7, showing a 4.1% gap that the proposed method closes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all 12 baselines across both detectors. Against the strongest AI-ISP baseline RAODNet [15] (mAP 46.1/42.9), the full model gains +3.7 mAP (Faster R-CNN) and +2.2 mAP (YOLOv3), while the Lite variant still leads by +1.7/+0.7 mAP at 5× lower latency (8.8 ms vs. 41.2 ms). The professional HDR ISP pipeline [9] (mAP 45.7) is surpassed by 4.1 mAP, and handcrafted tone mappers Mantiuk08 [12] and CLAHE94 [14] trail by 4.2 and 5.6 mAP respectively. Low-light enhancement methods (Zero-DCE++ [21], SCI [22]) and latent-space ISP (AnscombeNet [18]) perform poorly (mAP 26–29) because they cannot handle the extreme 140 dB dynamic range. IANet [16] (mAP 37.6) and RawOrCooked [42] (mAP 34.8) are limited by their piecewise-linear tone curves and contrast-correction designs, which fail under mixed day/night scenes. ReconfigISP [19] (mAP 41.4) is the weakest end-to-end ISP method, confirming that proxy-based ISP tuning is suboptimal for HDR detection. In the performance-transfer setting (Table 2), the method with only 33.6% of parameters unfrozen achieves mAP 30.6 (COCO init) and 38.8 (Objects365 init), beating RAWAdapter [49] (12.6/15.7) and AdaptiveISP [17] (22.5/24.7) by large margins.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant AI-ISP paradigm (RAODNet, IANet, AdaptiveISP, ReconfigISP) that mirrors traditional ISP pipelines with learnable modules, instead proposing a minimal two-stage pipeline: neural photometric calibration followed by a scaling-invariant tone mapper. The scaling-invariant property is borrowed from the bias-free CNN literature (Mohan et al., ICLR 2020; Zamir et al., CVPR 2022), where it was established for denoising, and here repurposed as a structural guarantee for local tone mapping. The paper also introduces a 'performance transfer' strategy that fine-tunes only the tone mapper and detection head from LDR-pretrained weights, positioning itself between full end-to-end training and domain-adaptation methods like RAWAdapter and AdaptiveISP.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in enabling real-time 4K HDR RAW processing (45 FPS) on NVIDIA Jetson AGX Orin, which is directly relevant to safety-critical autonomous driving where extreme dynamic range transitions (e.g., tunnel entry/exit) degrade standard LDR pipelines. The scaling-invariant tone mapper's superior cross-dataset generalization (26.5 vs. 14.3 mAP on RhoVision) suggests a principled path to reducing overfitting in tone mapping. However, the method still requires cascading with a detector and cannot yet replace the ISP entirely; the authors acknowledge that a well-designed detector could potentially handle HDR RAW directly, which would be a more fundamental shift. The moderate ~3–4% mAP gains over the strongest AI-ISP baselines, while consistent, do not constitute a paradigm change in detection performance.
