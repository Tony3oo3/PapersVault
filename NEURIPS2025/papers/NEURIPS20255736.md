## Problem

Estimating the intended referential target of a person's deictic gesture (e.g., what they are pointing at or showing) remains largely unexplored in computer vision, with prior work focused on gesture classification or synthesis rather than referential intent. No public dataset or dedicated model existed for this task, which requires reasoning across body pose, gaze direction, spatial context, and occlusions simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255736/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image and the subject person's bounding box (provided at both train and test time, following PASCAL VOC convention), along with eye and hand keypoints for conditioning. It produces a 128×128 binary segmentation mask localizing the gesture target and a categorical existence label indicating whether the target is present, absent, or out of frame. The subject's body and head regions are encoded as downsampled binary masks that modulate the visual feature map before decoding.

## Method

TransGesture uses a frozen DINOv2 encoder to extract scene tokens, which are split into two streams: a gaze stream conditioned by a head patch encoding and a gesture stream conditioned by a body patch encoding. Each stream passes through a separate transformer decoder — the gaze decoder is pre-trained on GazeFollow and kept frozen, while the gesture decoder is learnable and includes a dedicated existence token. The key architectural contribution is a multi-layer Joint Cross Attention (JCA) fusion module where gesture tokens attend to the concatenated gaze+gesture joint feature, enabling the model to disambiguate the intended target using gaze-following cues. Training is two-stage: the gaze decoder is first pre-trained on GazeFollow, then the gesture decoder and JCA modules are fine-tuned end-to-end on GestureTarget with a combined BCE (mask) + focal loss (existence) objective, keeping both the visual encoder and gaze decoder frozen.

## Results

On the GestureTarget benchmark, TransGesture with JCA fusion and DINOv2-Base achieves the best overall performance: 89.01% existence accuracy and 58.48% target mask IoU, improving over the gesture-only baseline by +9.8% in accuracy and +6.3% in IoU. Across all four visual encoders tested (CLIP-Large, SigLIP-Base, SigLIP2-Base, DINOv2-Base), JCA fusion consistently outperforms Direct Fusion and Conv Fusion in both metrics. For the gaze decoder pre-training stage on GazeFollow, the DINOv2-Large configuration reaches AUC 0.959, Avg L2 0.096, and Min L2 0.045, matching or exceeding prior SOTA gaze-following methods. Human performance on the same test set averages 87.00% existence accuracy and 66.40% IoU, indicating a remaining gap of roughly 8 points in IoU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

For the main gesture target estimation task, JCA fusion outperforms both Direct Fusion and Conv Fusion across every visual encoder; the largest gain is with DINOv2-Base, where JCA improves IoU from 54.63% (Conv Fusion) to 58.48% and existence accuracy from 88.10% to 89.01%. Direct Fusion is the weakest gaze-guided strategy, sometimes even degrading IoU below the gesture-only baseline (e.g., 53.70% vs. 54.37% with CLIP-Large). For the gaze decoder pre-training evaluated on GazeFollow, the paper's DINOv2-Large configuration (AUC 0.959, Avg L2 0.096, Min L2 0.045) surpasses Sharingan [Tafasca et al., CVPR 2024] (AUC 0.938, Avg L2 0.108, Min L2 0.054) and ViTGaze [Song et al., 2024] (AUC 0.949, Avg L2 0.105, Min L2 0.047) on all three metrics, while slightly trailing Gaze-LLE [Ryan et al., 2025] on Min L2 (0.045 vs. 0.041) but matching it on AUC (0.959 vs. 0.958). Overall, the paper demonstrates that joint cross-attention is the most effective fusion mechanism for integrating gaze and gesture cues, and that the pre-trained gaze decoder is competitive with dedicated gaze-following architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves out a new problem space at the intersection of gesture recognition and gaze estimation. Prior gesture work (LD-ConGR, HaGRID, SocialGesture) has focused on classifying what gesture is being made or synthesizing gestures, while prior gaze work (GazeFollow, Gaze-LLE, ViTGaze) predicts where a person is looking in isolation. TransGesture departs from both lines by treating gaze as an auxiliary signal that disambiguates the referential target of a deictic gesture, fusing the two via a joint cross-attention module over a frozen DINOv2 backbone. The gaze decoder is pre-trained on GazeFollow following the Gaze-LLE recipe (frozen encoder + lightweight decoder), but is then repurposed as a frozen feature extractor rather than a final predictor.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GestureTarget is the first public benchmark for deictic gesture target estimation, filling a gap the authors identify as leaving 'a crucial gap in the computational understanding of human communication.' The finding that even GPT-4o and Claude-3.7 achieve only ~70% existence accuracy and <5% IoU on mask prediction underscores that current MLLMs cannot handle this task, motivating dedicated architectures. However, the best model (89% existence, 58.5% IoU) still trails human performance (87% existence, 66.4% IoU) by a meaningful margin, and the task is limited to single-frame, single-subject settings with a provided bounding box. Open questions include extending to video, handling multiple simultaneous gesturers, and whether the gaze-gesture coupling generalizes beyond the four source datasets used for annotation.
