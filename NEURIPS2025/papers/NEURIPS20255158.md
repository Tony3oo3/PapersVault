## Problem

While single-layer GNN aggregation on community graphs is well understood, the asymptotic distribution of multi-layer (k > 1) GNN features on large stochastic graphs remains uncharacterized. Existing oversmoothing analyses rely on power iteration over a fixed adjacency matrix and only track mean collapse, leaving open what happens to the feature covariance structure and how the full embedding distribution behaves in the sparse, large-graph limit.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255158/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The analysis takes a graph generated from a Contextual Stochastic Block Model (with inter-community edge probability matrix B scaled by ν_n/n) and initial node features X ∈ R^{n×d} with class-conditional means M, then applies k successive adjacency-matrix multiplications to produce embeddings φ^(k) = A^k X. The key outputs are the degree-normalized features φ̄_i^(k) = φ_i^(k)/ν_n^k (used directly by a downstream linear classifier) and the centered-scaled features ξ_i^(k) = √ν_n(φ̄_i^(k) − E[φ̄_i^(k)]), whose empirical distributions converge to explicit Gaussian mixtures with class means μ_ℓ = (J^k M)^T e_ℓ and covariances Σ_ℓ = (J^{k-1} M)^T diag(e_ℓ^T J)(J^{k-1} M), where J = B·diag(π).

## Method

The paper proves a Central Limit Theorem showing that the empirical distribution of ξ_i^(k) converges in 1-Wasserstein distance to a centered Gaussian mixture ∑_ℓ π_ℓ N(0, Σ_ℓ), and that the joint distribution of (z_i, φ̄_i^(k)) converges to a mixture N(μ_ℓ, Σ_ℓ/ν_n). The proof decomposes each feature deviation into graph-structure noise Δ_i and initial-feature noise Λ_i, shows Λ_i is asymptotically negligible under sparsity (ν_n = o(n)), and then uses walk-based moment counting to identify the limiting even moments as (r−1)!!·(θ^T Σ_ℓ θ)^{r/2}. A key insight is that both μ_ℓ and Σ_ℓ involve powers of J = B·diag(π), so as k grows the repeated multiplication J^k and J^{k−1} acts as a power iteration, collapsing both the class means and the principal covariance directions onto the 1-D subspace spanned by the leading eigenvector of J. This alignment of signal and noise provides a stronger, covariance-level mechanism for oversmoothing beyond the previously known mean collapse, and the W_1 convergence further implies that cross-entropy training on the empirical features is asymptotically equivalent to training on the limiting Gaussian mixture.

## Results

This is a purely theoretical paper with no experimental benchmark tables. The main results are two Central Limit Theorems: (1) Theorem 1 shows the joint empirical distribution of degree-normalized Poly-GNN features φ̄⁽ᵏ⁾ and labels converges in 1-Wasserstein distance to a Gaussian mixture N(μ_ℓ, Σ_ℓ/νₙ) per class, with explicit forms μ_ℓ = (JᵏM)ᵀe_ℓ and Σ_ℓ = (Jᵏ⁻¹M)ᵀ diag(e_ℓᵀJ)(Jᵏ⁻¹M); (2) Theorem 2 shows centered-and-scaled features ξ⁽ᵏ⁾ converge to a centered stable Gaussian mixture Σ π_ℓ N(0, Σ_ℓ). Proposition 1 further establishes uniform convergence of the cross-entropy loss, its gradients, and the optimal linear classifier weights to their Gaussian-mixture counterparts. The paper also provides a precise power-iteration mechanism for oversmoothing: as k grows, both class means and covariance principal directions collapse onto the 1-D subspace spanned by the leading eigenvector of J = BΠ.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper is a purely theoretical contribution and does not include experimental comparisons against other GNN architectures or baselines. The closest related works discussed are [22] (which assumes exact component-wise Gaussianity, shown to be inapplicable here), [5;17;21] (which explain oversmoothing via power iteration on fixed adjacency matrices), and [4;14] (which study multi-hop feature effects on downstream tasks). The paper's key distinction is that it analyzes a stochastic graph model (CSBM) in the large-graph limit, revealing that not only the means but also the covariance matrices collapse onto the same low-dimensional subspace — a stronger form of oversmoothing than previously characterized. No quantitative win/lose comparisons are made.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the authors' prior walk-based moment analysis for community-based graphs [18] and their companion result on Wasserstein concentration of dependent empirical measures [2], extending these tools to establish the first CLT for multi-layer Poly-GNN features in the sparse, large-graph limit. It departs fundamentally from prior oversmoothing analyses [5, 17, 21], which invoke power iteration on a fixed adjacency matrix to show mean collapse, by working in a stochastic CSBM setting where the covariance structure is also characterized and shown to collapse. The paper also corrects a gap in [22], which assumes component-wise Gaussian features for all n—a property that does not hold for Bernoulli-adjacency aggregations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical implication is that training a linear classifier with cross-entropy on Poly-GNN embeddings is asymptotically equivalent to optimizing on an explicitly characterized Gaussian mixture, providing a rigorous theoretical foundation for observed training dynamics. The stronger oversmoothing mechanism—where both means and covariance principal directions align with the same 1-D subspace of J—goes beyond the previously understood mean-only collapse and explains why feature separability degrades so sharply with depth. Major open questions include extending the analysis to non-linear activations, attention mechanisms, degree-corrected SBMs, and polynomial filters of the form Σc_k(A/ν_n)^k X, all of which the authors identify as requiring new tools beyond walk-based moment analysis.
