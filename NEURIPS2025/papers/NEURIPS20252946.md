## Problem

Current soft prompt-based learning methods suffer from two intertwined limitations: task-aware prompts predominantly attend to themselves rather than critical input tokens, limiting their ability to adapt to diverse instance semantics, and they require laborious grid searching over prompt length, incurring substantial training overhead. The paper asks whether a single instance-aware token can simultaneously fix the attention interplay deficit and eliminate the need for prompt-length search.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252946/cropped_page3_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a text sequence (truncated to 512 tokens) and a single learnable capsule vector per Transformer layer. At each layer, the capsule prompt is constructed by adding the learnable vector to the mean-pooled representation of the input embeddings (layer 1) or the concatenation of the previous layer's processed capsule and sequence hidden states (layers i≥2), yielding a length-invariant, instance-adaptive prompt token. The model outputs a task prediction (classification label or extracted answer) on SuperGLUE NLU tasks, and the attention patterns are analyzed to validate the 'attention anchor' behavior.

## Method

CaPT replaces the N randomly initialized soft prompts per layer in Deep Prompt-Tuning with a single 'capsule' prompt that fuses task-aware and instance-aware information: S^i = p^i + Mean(S^{i-1} ⊕ H^{i-1}), where p^i is the sole learnable parameter at layer i and the mean term injects instance-specific semantics from the evolving hidden states. The key empirical finding is that this single instance-aware token acts as an 'attention anchor'—it consistently directs attention toward structurally critical input tokens and is in turn strongly attended by all input tokens, establishing a mutual attention pattern absent in conventional soft prompts. This design eliminates grid search over prompt length entirely, requires only ~0.003–0.004% of model parameters, and outperforms full fine-tuning on T5-Large (84.03% vs. 83.60% average on SuperGLUE) while training in 1× the time of baselines that need 8–15× longer due to hyperparameter search.

## Results

CaPT achieves 84.03% average accuracy on T5-Large (770M) using only 0.003% of model parameters, surpassing full fine-tuning (83.60%) by 0.43% and P-Tuning v2 (83.79%) by 0.24%. On T5-Base (220M), CaPT reaches 77.51% average score (99.56% of full fine-tuning's 77.85%) with just 0.004% trainable parameters. On decoder-only models, CaPT yields 67.51% on Llama3.2-1B (a 24.44% improvement over Linear Head and 10.56% over P-Tuning v2) and 63.17% on Qwen2.5-1.5B (a 10.56% improvement over P-Tuning v2). Training time is reduced to 1.00× compared to 8.37×–14.93× for competing prompt-based methods, as CaPT eliminates the need for prompt-length grid search.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CaPT outperforms P-Tuning v2 (the strongest prompt-based baseline) on all four architectures: by 0.57% on T5-Base (77.51 vs. 76.94), 0.24% on T5-Large (84.03 vs. 83.79), 6.45% on Llama3.2-1B (67.51 vs. 61.06), and 1.82% on Qwen2.5-1.5B (63.17 vs. 61.35), while using roughly 100× fewer trainable parameters (0.003–0.004% vs. 0.51–0.53%). On T5-Large, CaPT also exceeds full fine-tuning (84.03% vs. 83.60%) despite training only 0.003% of parameters. Against other prompt-based methods (Prompt-Tuning, XPrompt, ResPrompt, SMoP, VFPT, EPT), CaPT achieves the best average score on every model. On decoder-only Llama3.2-1B, CaPT improves over the Linear Head baseline by 24.44% in average score, and over P-Tuning v2 by 10.56%, indicating the attention-anchor mechanism is particularly beneficial for causal architectures. In training time comparisons, CaPT requires only 1.00× the baseline time versus 8.37× (P-Tuning v2), 8.77× (Prompt-Tuning), 12.58× (M-IDPG), and 14.93× (LoPA), as it eliminates prompt-length grid search entirely.

## Relation to Prior Work

CaPT extends the Deep Prompt-Tuning (P-Tuning v2) framework by replacing per-layer soft prompt vectors with a single 'capsule' prompt that fuses a learnable task-aware vector with a mean-pooled instance-aware representation. Conceptually, it departs from the task-only prompt paradigm (Lester et al., 2021; Liu et al., 2022) by arguing that prompts must interact with input content to be effective, and from instance-dependent prompt generation methods (M-IDPG, LoPA) by showing that a single mean-pooled token suffices without heavy trainable modules. The 'attention anchor' framing connects to the attention-sink literature (Xiao et al., 2024) but applies it specifically to prompt-based learning, where prior work had largely ignored how prompts attend to (and are attended by) input tokens.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CaPT offers a practically attractive recipe: one learnable vector per layer, no grid search, and near-zero parameter overhead, making prompt-based adaptation nearly free in both compute and storage. The attention-anchor analysis provides an interpretable mechanism for why instance-aware tokens help, which could inform future prompt designs beyond simple mean-pooling. However, evaluation is confined to six SuperGLUE NLU tasks and models up to 1.5B parameters, leaving open whether the approach transfers to generation tasks, larger models, or multilingual settings. The finding that additional instance-aware tokens degrade performance suggests the mean-pooling bottleneck is a double-edged sword that future work should address.
