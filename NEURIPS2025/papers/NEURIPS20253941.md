## Problem

Existing training-free token pruning methods for VLMs rely on fixed schedules or handcrafted layer-wise heuristics that are applied uniformly across all inputs, failing to adapt to the varying complexity of individual samples and tasks. The paper identifies that cross-modal attention patterns shift dramatically depending on whether a query is simple (rapid attention convergence) or complex (sustained diffuse attention with inter-layer saliency fluctuations), yet no prior method simultaneously captures this per-sample dynamics and enforces a global computational budget.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253941/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a tokenized RGB image (N_v visual tokens), a text prompt (N_t text tokens), and a user-specified compute budget expressed as either a total token count or FLOPs target. It produces a per-layer retention schedule—a discrete sequence of integer token counts across L decoder layers—derived by analytically integrating and rescaling a sample-specific logistic curve so its area exactly matches the budget. The pruned token sequence then feeds the standard VLM/VLA forward pass to yield the final text response or driving action, with no architectural modification to the backbone model.

## Method

AutoPrune is a training-free, plug-and-pruning framework that assigns each input a unique pruning policy. It first computes the mutual information between visual and textual tokens using softmax-normalized cross-modal attention weights from early decoder layers, treating high MI as a 'simple' signal (strong text-vision alignment) and low MI as 'complex' (weak alignment requiring broader exploration). This scalar then linearly modulates both the slope k and inflection point x_0 of a logistic retention curve f_q(x) = N_init / (1 + exp(k_q(x - x_0^q))), producing aggressive front-loaded pruning for simple samples and conservative late-onset pruning for complex ones. To guarantee the compute budget, the curve is analytically integrated over [0, L] and rescaled by a factor c_max / I_q, with a final binary-search adjustment on a global scale factor to handle discrete layer and integer token constraints. The entire overhead is O(N_h · N_t · N_v), negligible relative to inference cost, and the framework requires no hyperparameter tuning when transferred across models (LLaVA-1.5, LLaVA-NeXT, Senna for autonomous driving).

## Results

On LLaVA-1.5-7B, AutoPrune prunes 89% of visual tokens (retaining only 64) while maintaining 96.7% of full-model accuracy averaged over MME, MMBench, ScienceQA, GQA, and TextVQA, reducing inference FLOPs by 76.8%. At a moderate 78% pruning rate (128 tokens), it preserves 98.1% accuracy with 66.3% FLOPs reduction. On LLaVA-NeXT-7B at 160 retained tokens, AutoPrune retains 94.9% performance, and on the Senna VLA model for autonomous driving at 25% token retention, it achieves 111.23% relative planning accuracy—surpassing even the unpruned model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AutoPrune consistently outperforms all six baselines across every pruning ratio and model tested. Against the strongest baseline PDrop (CVPR'25), AutoPrune achieves a 9.1-point improvement in performance retention at 64 tokens (96.7% vs. 87.6%) and a 2.5-point gain at 128 tokens (98.1% vs. 95.6%), while also reducing FLOPs further (23.2% vs. 25.5%). SparseVLM (ICML'25) is the second-strongest baseline, trailing AutoPrune by 3.5 points at 64 tokens (85.9% vs. 96.7%) and 5.1 points at 128 tokens. FastV (ECCV'24) and ToMe (arXiv'22) are substantially weaker, with ToMe dropping to only 70.5% retention at 64 tokens. On LLaVA-NeXT-7B, AutoPrune beats FasterVLM (the best baseline there) by 8.2 points at 160 tokens (94.9% vs. 86.7%) and by 4.9 points at 320 tokens (98.2% vs. 93.3%). On the Senna VLA model for autonomous driving, AutoPrune surpasses PyramidDrop by 12.3 points at 25% retention (111.23% vs. 98.89%) and even exceeds the unpruned model, suggesting that aggressive pruning can eliminate noisy visual tokens in VLA settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in training-free visual token pruning, where methods like FastV, PyramidDrop, SparseVLM, and FasterVLM apply fixed or handcrafted layer-specific schedules uniformly across all inputs. The key conceptual departure is framing pruning as a per-sample, complexity-adaptive problem: mutual information between early-layer visual and textual tokens quantifies how tightly the text constrains the visual scene, and this scalar is mapped to a budget-constrained logistic retention curve whose shape (slope and inflection point) varies with input complexity. The neuroscience motivation—mirroring the explore-commit-stabilize fixation pattern from eye-tracking literature—provides a principled rationale for the logistic form rather than treating it as an arbitrary scheduling choice. Unlike prior layer-wise heuristics that lack a global budget guarantee, AutoPrune analytically integrates each curve to enforce an exact token or FLOPs constraint, unifying adaptivity with deterministic cost control.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: 89% visual token reduction with 76.8% FLOPs savings while retaining 96.7% accuracy on LLaVA-1.5-7B makes near-lossless efficient inference feasible for deployment in resource-constrained settings, and the plug-and-play design transfers to VLA models for autonomous driving without hyperparameter retuning. The acknowledged limitation that deeper layers can occasionally retain more critical tokens than shallower ones (non-monotonic importance) suggests the current depth-wise formulation is still an approximation, and a truly optimal policy would need to handle this variable importance distribution. The unexplained observation that pruned VLA models sometimes outperform the full model hints at noisy visual tokens in large-scale driving datasets, opening an interesting but underexplored direction. Broader open questions include whether the mutual-information complexity signal generalizes to video inputs or multi-turn dialogue where cross-modal alignment evolves over time.
