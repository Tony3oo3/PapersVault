## Problem

Learning the dynamics of controlled stochastic systems from trajectory data is essential in safety-critical applications (robotics, finance, biomedicine), but naive exploration can produce unsafe trajectories before the model is accurate. Prior safe-learning methods either assume known dynamics, operate in discrete-time MDPs, or do not jointly estimate both the system dynamics and safety probabilities in continuous-time settings. This paper is the first to provide provable joint safe-exploration and density-estimation guarantees for continuous-time, continuous-state controlled SDEs under only mild smoothness assumptions.

## Input / Output

The method consumes discrete-time trajectory samples from a controlled SDE (position/velocity states at M time steps per trajectory, Q independent Brownian paths per control), a finite-dimensional control parameterization θ ∈ D ⊂ ℝ^m, and user-specified safety and reset region functions g, h that partition the state space. Beyond the frontmatter lists, a key structural detail is that the output certified safe set Γ_N is a set of (θ, t, T) triples—each specifying a control, an observation time, and a terminal time—enabling deployment-time verification of whether an arbitrary proposed control (including unseen ones) satisfies the safety and reset thresholds with high probability.

## Method

The method is a safe-UCB loop that iteratively expands a known safe-resettable set by selecting the next control that maximizes kernel predictive uncertainty over the feasible region, where feasibility is enforced via lower confidence bounds on both safety and reset probabilities. Three models—state density (via a Bessel kernel density estimator followed by kernel ridge regression), safety probability (integral of density over the safe region), and reset probability (integral over the reset region)—are jointly fitted with a shared Matérn kernel whose Sobolev smoothness ν matches the assumed regularity of the true dynamics. The key theoretical contribution is proving that all selected controls remain safe (s^∞ ≥ 1−ε) and resettable (r ≥ 1−ξ) throughout training, and that the estimation error for density, safety, and reset maps converges at rates adaptive to ν, with the number of required trajectory samples per control scaling as Q ≳ N^{(2ν+n)/(2ν−n)}. The stopping criterion halts exploration once maximum uncertainty over the feasible set drops below a threshold η, yielding a certified safe control set for deployment.

## Results

On a 2D second-order controlled SDE benchmark, the method achieves safety and reset prediction MSE of 0.7010±0.3847 and 0.6919±0.3764 under strict thresholds (ε=ξ=0.1), improving to 0.0023±0.0065 and 0.0024±0.0062 when constraints are removed (ε=ξ=+∞), over 1000 iterations. Theoretically, the method guarantees safety (s^∞ ≥ 1−ε and r ≥ 1−ξ for all selected controls) and achieves estimation error bounded by c·η for density, safety, and reset maps, with sample complexity N ≤ c₂·η^(−2/(1−α)) iterations requiring Q ≳ N^((2ν+n)/(2ν−n)) trajectory samples per control. The full pipeline runs in under 32 minutes on standard hardware.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against any external baselines or competing methods; it is the first to jointly provide safe exploration and density estimation guarantees for continuous-time controlled SDEs under Sobolev regularity. The experimental evaluation is self-contained, varying only the safety/reset threshold pair (ε, ξ) to illustrate the exploration–safety trade-off: stricter thresholds (0.1) restrict the explored control space and yield higher prediction MSE (~0.70), while removing constraints (+∞) allows full coverage and near-zero error (~0.002). The authors note that no prior work provides joint safe exploration and density estimation guarantees in this continuous-time, continuous-state setting, positioning the contribution as a new capability rather than an improvement over existing methods. Future work is planned to include comparisons with safe RL baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the safe UCB / Safe Bayesian Optimization framework (Sui et al. 2015, 2018; Bottero et al. 2022) from discrete-time MDPs and known-dynamics settings to continuous-time controlled SDEs with unknown dynamics. It builds directly on Bonalli and Rudi (2025) for the Bessel-kernel density estimator and Sobolev regularity analysis of SDE state densities, and on GP bandit theory (Srinivas et al. 2009) for the confidence bound machinery. The key departure is the joint estimation of three coupled models—dynamics, safety, and reset—under a single shared kernel, with the paper noting that no prior work provides joint safe exploration and density estimation guarantees in continuous-time settings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method offers a principled, provably safe alternative to naive exploration for safety-critical systems (autonomous robotics, financial portfolio management, biomedicine) where stochastic disturbances are unknown. The adaptive learning rates that improve with Sobolev regularity provide a clear theoretical handle on when the method is most efficient. However, experimental validation is limited to a single 2D second-order system, and the method requires prior knowledge of RKHS norm bounds for the safety/reset functions (conservatively overestimable but potentially slowing exploration). Open questions include scaling to higher dimensions, validation on physical hardware, handling non-diffusive disturbances (jump processes), and adaptive estimation of the required norm bounds without prior knowledge.
