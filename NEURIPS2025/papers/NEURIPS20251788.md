## Problem

Despite extensive research on individual suturing subtasks, no fully autonomous end-to-end suturing pipeline has been demonstrated on physical hardware, and the field lacks a reproducible benchmark with precision metrics beyond coarse task-completion rates. Publicly available suturing datasets total fewer than 200 trajectories, far below the scale needed to leverage recent vision-language-action architectures, and no established benchmark exists to track progress in this canonical surgical task.

## Input / Output

The system consumes synchronized RGB frames from a stereo endoscope (960×540) and two wrist-mounted cameras (640×480) at 30 Hz, alongside 6-DOF Cartesian poses, jaw angles, and RCM frames from the dVRK kinematics. Goal conditions are injected in one of three formats: opaque point labels overlaid directly on the endoscope image, a separate 3-channel binary mask image, or a 3-channel distance-map image encoding pixel-wise offset vectors toward the target. The high-level policy outputs a discrete language task label (needle pickup, needle throw, or knot tie), which conditions the low-level policy to emit a chunk of relative robot actions (chunk sizes range from 16 for GR00T N1 to 60 for ACT).

## Method

The framework uses a hierarchical architecture: a Swin Transformer-based high-level policy predicts the current subtask from visual observations, and a low-level VLA policy (π0, GR00T N1, OpenVLA-OFT, or multitask ACT) generates continuous action chunks conditioned on the language label, wrist/endoscope images, goal condition, and kinematic state. The key novelty is a goal-conditioned training scheme where target insertion and exit points—annotated via a GUI on the final frame of each demonstration—are encoded as visual cues to explicitly optimize targeting precision rather than relying on the implicit distribution in the training data. Among three goal representations, opaque point labels overlaid on the endoscope image yield the lowest insertion error (1.0 mm for π0, 1.3 mm for ACT), improving precision by 59–74% over the no-goal baseline. The 1,890-demonstration dataset includes 454 DAgger-inspired recovery episodes collected from failure states of an initial policy, increasing robustness to suboptimal configurations. A language-conditioned multitask ACT variant is trained on all three subtasks in a single model, enabling seamless task transitions without separate per-task policies.

## Results

On the SutureBot benchmark (10 full suture procedures per policy), ACT achieves the highest individual task success rates (Pickup 9/10, Throw 8/10, Pull Through 4/10, Knot Tie 9/10) and is the only model to complete end-to-end suturing without manual intervention (3/10). ACT also achieves the best mean insertion error of 1.5±0.8 mm, followed closely by π₀ at 1.9±1.0 mm. GR00T N1 and OpenVLA-OFT perform substantially worse, with GR00T achieving at most 2/10 on any task and OpenVLA-OFT failing all tasks (0/10). Goal-conditioned policies with point labels improve insertion precision by 59%–74% over no-goal baselines (e.g., π₀ drops from 3.9±2.5 mm to 1.0±1.3 mm).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ACT (language-conditioned, non-VLA) outperforms all three VLA baselines on task completion and is the only model to achieve end-to-end suturing (3/10 vs. 0/10 for all others). ACT is statistically significantly better than GR00T N1 (p=8.8e-9) and OpenVLA-OFT (p=2.1e-12) via Fisher's Exact test, but not significantly better than π₀ (p=0.018, above the Bonferroni-corrected α=0.0167 threshold). π₀ achieves comparable insertion precision to ACT (1.9 vs. 1.5 mm) but is substantially slower (348 s vs. 182 s per procedure) and weaker on knot tying (4/10 vs. 9/10). GR00T N1, pretrained on humanoid robots, performs poorly on the dVRK bimanual platform, likely due to embodiment mismatch. OpenVLA-OFT fails entirely on all tasks, suggesting its parallel-decoding + L1 regression approach is ill-suited to the fine-grained dexterous motions required for suturing.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the hierarchical language-conditioned IL framework from SRT-H (Kim et al., 2025) to a substantially more dexterous surgical task (suturing vs. cholecystectomy), and builds on the ACT architecture (Zhao et al., 2023) by adding language conditioning for multitask operation. It introduces goal-conditioning via point labels as a precision mechanism, drawing on RoboPoint and AimBot but applying them for the first time to measured sub-millimeter targeting accuracy in a surgical context. The DAgger-inspired recovery demonstrations and the explicit precision metrics (insertion/exit error via UV markers) represent a departure from prior surgical IL work that focused primarily on task completion rates.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first public benchmark and largest dataset (1,890 demos vs. <200 previously) for end-to-end autonomous suturing on physical hardware, filling a critical gap that has prevented reproducible progress in surgical robotics. The finding that current VLA models (π0, GR00T N1, OpenVLA-OFT) can achieve individual subtasks but fail at reliable end-to-end completion (only ACT achieved 3/10) establishes a clear performance ceiling and identifies the remaining challenges. Key open questions include generalization to real tissue and clinical metrics (bite depth, suture tension), automation of target-point selection, and whether scaling data or alternative architectures can close the end-to-end gap. The observation that π0 pretraining provides minimal benefit over training from scratch on this platform raises important questions about domain transfer for surgical robotics.
