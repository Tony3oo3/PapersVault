## Problem

Existing confounding-resistant domain generalization methods rely on strong assumptions: backdoor adjustment requires all confounders to be observable, frontdoor adjustment needs valid mediators, and the IV-DG approach uses predefined instruments that frequently violate the exclusion restriction, introducing severe bias. No prior method automatically learns valid instrumental variables directly from visual data while simultaneously preserving discriminative non-causal features that are useful for classification.

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images from multiple source domains (e.g., MNIST, SVHN, Art, Cartoon, Photo, Sketch) and produces class labels for unseen target domains under a leave-one-domain-out protocol. Internally, each input image is processed through Fourier decomposition (phase and amplitude), a mask generation module that isolates texture/color regions from shape/contour regions, and a shared ResNet-18 or ResNet-50 encoder. The output is a standard softmax class prediction, but the training pipeline also produces intermediate causal representations, visual IV representations, and debiased causal factor estimates used across the four-stage optimization.

## Method

VIV-DG introduces the concept of a Visual Instrumental Variable—a non-causal visual attribute (e.g., object color, texture) that satisfies relevance, independence, and exclusion conditions relative to causal factors (shape, contour) and class labels. The framework comprises three alternately optimized subnetworks: (i) a causal feature extractor that uses Fourier-based amplitude perturbation and MMD consistency to learn initial causal representations; (ii) a visual IV learner that enforces the three IV conditions via adversarial mutual information estimation (maximizing I(Z;S), minimizing I(Z;U) and I(Y;Z|S)) on masked images that preserve texture/color while suppressing shape; and (iii) a lightweight MLP regressor that maps IV representations back to debiased causal factors. Training follows a four-stage alternating protocol (causal extraction → IV learning → regression → causal refinement), and a theoretical result guarantees that any global minimizer of the IV objective yields a learnable visual IV. A lightweight variant, VIV-DG-Lite, replaces the regressor with simple feature addition to reduce computational cost.

## Results

On Digits-DG, VIV-DG achieves 83.4% average leave-one-domain-out accuracy, outperforming the strongest baseline CIRL (82.5%) and CDIM (82.4%). On PACS (ResNet-18), VIV-DG reaches 87.2% average accuracy, surpassing FAGT (86.8%) and CIRL (86.3%). On Office-Home (ResNet-18), VIV-DG attains 67.4% average accuracy, edging out CIRL (67.1%) and L2A-OT (65.6%). On VLCS (ResNet-50), both VIV-DG and VIV-DG-Lite achieve 79.7% average accuracy, beating RICE (79.3%) and GMDG (79.2%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

VIV-DG most significantly outperforms IV-DG [17], the closest IV-based competitor, by +2.8% on PACS (87.2 vs 84.4) and +3.2% on Office-Home (67.4 vs 64.2), demonstrating that automatically learned visual IVs avoid the exclusion-restriction violations inherent in predefined domain-based instruments. Against the strongest non-causal baseline CIRL [13], VIV-DG gains +0.9% on Digits-DG (83.4 vs 82.5), +0.9% on PACS (87.2 vs 86.3), and +0.3% on Office-Home (67.4 vs 67.1). VIV-DG also surpasses the frontdoor-adjustment method FAGT [16] by +2.1% on Digits-DG (83.4 vs 81.3) and +1.0% on Office-Home (67.4 vs 66.4), though FAGT edges ahead on PACS by a narrow 0.4% margin (86.8 vs 87.2). On VLCS with ResNet-50, VIV-DG ties VIV-DG-Lite at 79.7% average and beats the best causal baselines RICE (79.3%) and GMDG (79.2%) by 0.4–0.5%. The lightweight variant VIV-DG-Lite matches or slightly exceeds VIV-DG on Digits-DG (83.5 vs 83.4) and Office-Home (67.2 vs 67.4), while VIV-DG shows greater robustness under larger distribution shifts (e.g., Sketch in PACS: 83.9 vs 83.3).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the causal-inference line of work on domain generalization, specifically building on IV-DG (Yuan et al., TKDD 2023), which first introduced instrumental variables into DG but relied on manually predefined instruments that often violate the exclusion restriction. It also draws on Fourier-based augmentation from FACT (Xu et al., CVPR 2021) for causal factor extraction and modifies CIRL's (Lv et al., CVPR 2022) correlation-matrix loss with MMD for representation consistency. The key conceptual departure is a refined SCM that splits non-causal factors into confounders and visual IVs, rather than uniformly suppressing all non-causal attributes, and an automatic learner that enforces relevance, independence, and exclusion via adversarial mutual-information estimation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The observation that certain non-causal visual attributes (object color, texture) naturally satisfy IV conditions offers a principled way to preserve discriminative information that prior causal-DG methods discard as confounders, potentially improving generalization without requiring expert-specified instruments. However, the approach is limited to image classification DG, relies on a fixed pixel-difference threshold for mask generation, and its theoretical guarantees assume a sufficiently expressive function family and access to a global minimizer—conditions rarely met in practice. Open questions include whether the 'natural IV' assumption generalizes to other modalities (video, audio, tabular data) and how sensitive performance is to the mask threshold and the quality of the initial causal factor extraction.
