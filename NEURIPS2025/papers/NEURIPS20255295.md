## Problem

Diffusion transformers deliver high-quality generation but at substantial computational cost, and existing feature-caching methods (FORA, ToCa, L2C, LazyDiT) are limited to a binary choice between reusing a cached feature and performing full network inference. This paper asks whether cached features can be selectively adapted through lightweight learned transformations to better approximate the original inference, thereby closing the gap between caching-only efficiency and full-computation quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255295/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method is a post-hoc efficiency layer applied to pretrained diffusion transformers (DiT-XL/2, PixArt-α, Sana-0.6B, SD3-medium) without altering their architecture or weights. It accepts a class label or text prompt and produces a generated image at 256×256, 512×512, or 1024×1024 resolution, operating within standard samplers (DDIM, DPM++, Flow-Euler) at 8–100 steps. The only added parameters are per-timestep, per-layer scalars (β, α, γ) totaling as few as ~3K values, so the computational overhead is negligible (e.g., +11M FLOPs on top of ~4T).

## Method

The framework defines an activation cycle N: full network inference runs every N timesteps and caches intermediate self-attention, cross-attention, and MLP features; for the remaining N−1 timesteps, a learned linear modulation (1−β)h + β(αh + γ) is applied to the cached feature h, where β is a gate, α a scale, and γ a translation, all parameterized per-timestep and per-layer but input-invariant. The gate β is trained continuously through a sigmoid and hard-thresholded at inference (default c = 0.9), so modulation activates on only ~2% of layers. Training minimizes the L2 distance between the modulated approximation and the frozen model's true output, plus an L1 penalty on β to encourage sparse modulation, updating only β, α, γ (no model weight updates). The key insight, supported by cosine-similarity heatmaps and scatter plots, is that features at the same depth across consecutive timesteps exhibit near-unit cosine similarity and a strong linear relationship, making a simple scale-and-shift sufficient to approximate full inference at a fraction of the cost.

## Results

On DiT-XL/2 (50-step DDIM, ImageNet-1K), the proposed method with activation cycle N=3 reduces FLOPs by 2.93× (11.44T → 3.91T) and inference latency by 2.15× while achieving FID 2.90, sFID 4.60, and IS 231.54, close to the full-inference baseline (FID 2.29, IS 239.54). On PixArt-α (20-step DPM++, COCO2014 30K), FLOPs are reduced by 2.83× (11.43T → 4.04T) with a 1.50× speedup, and FID improves from 27.76 (baseline) to 24.20. The modulation is applied to as few as 0.35–2% of layers, adding negligible FLOPs (e.g., +11.01M on top of 3.92T for SD3-medium).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against FORA (N=3) [41], the closest caching-only baseline with identical FLOPs (3.91T), the proposed method improves FID from 3.28 to 2.90, sFID from 5.72 to 4.60, and IS from 228.47 to 231.54 on DiT-XL/2, and from 27.03 to 24.20 on PixArt-α. Against ToCa (N=3, R=93%) [57], which uses 1.31× more FLOPs (5.12T vs. 3.91T), the proposed method still achieves lower FID (2.90 vs. 3.04) and lower sFID (4.60 vs. 4.74). In the low-step Sana-0.6B setting (8-step Flow-Euler, Table 7), the method outperforms both L2C (N=2) [30] (FID 31.89 vs. 32.37) and FORA (N=2) [41] (FID 31.89 vs. 32.46) at comparable FLOPs (~2.46T). Against LazyDiT [42] in the appendix comparison, the proposed method achieves lower FID (2.70 vs. 2.70 for LazyDiT at 5.74T FLOPs) while matching FLOPs. Overall, the method consistently wins on FID and sFID across all caching-based baselines at equal or lower FLOPs, with the gap widening as the activation cycle N increases.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the feature caching paradigm for diffusion transformers (FORA, ToCa, L2C, LazyDiT), which previously offered only a binary choice between reusing cached features and performing full network inference. The key departure is introducing a learned, lightweight linear modulation (scalar scale + translation) with a differentiable gate that selectively adapts cached features, creating a third option between exact caching and full inference. The approach is conceptually distinct from concurrent work FasterCache [29], which applies deterministic timestep-dependent weighting to combine two cached features in video generation, and from DeepCache [51], which uses a parameter-heavy channel-wise transformation on UNet features. The method is trained end-to-end with a regularized objective that jointly optimizes the gate and modulator parameters without updating the diffusion model itself.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: 2.93× FLOPs reduction for DiT-XL/2 and 2.83× for PixArt-α with near-identical generation quality, and the method generalizes across architectures (DiT, PixArt, Sana, SD3) and samplers (DDIM, DPM++, Flow-Euler). The finding that modulation on as few as 2% of layers suffices makes the overhead negligible, enabling near-drop-in deployment. A key limitation is the intentionally lightweight linear modulator, which the authors acknowledge constrains representational capacity; whether richer transformations (e.g., per-channel or low-rank) could further close the gap at larger activation cycles remains open. The method also requires a short training phase (200K iterations) per model-sampler-resolution configuration, which adds a one-time cost before deployment.
