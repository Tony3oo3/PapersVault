## Problem

Existing personalized alignment methods for LLMs—prompt-based approaches and offline optimization (SFT, DPO)—are inherently static: they rely on fixed templates or pre-labeled data and cannot dynamically infer and adapt to user preferences during interaction. This makes them inadequate for cold-start scenarios where no prior user information exists and for long-term personalization where user attributes evolve across extended dialogues. The paper asks whether an LLM can learn to continuously construct and refine user profiles through multi-turn dialogue, without access to ground-truth responses.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251115/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

At each dialogue turn, the model receives the full conversation history (alternating user utterances and its own prior responses) as input and must produce two outputs simultaneously: a natural-language response tailored to the inferred user, and an explicit user profile in a structured slot-value format (e.g., Age: 28, Interests: hiking, Personality: introverted). The profile is not given upfront; the model must incrementally extract and update it from the dialogue, with the simulated user deliberately revealing attributes gradually rather than disclosing them all at once. The slot-value representation enables direct set-based comparison against ground truth for reward computation.

## Method

RLPA formulates personalized alignment as a multi-turn Markov Decision Process and trains the policy via Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) against a simulated user (GPT-4o-mini) whose behavior is conditioned on a hidden profile. The key mechanism is a dual-level turn-level reward: a Profile Reward computed as the F1 score between the model's predicted slot-value profile and the ground-truth profile, and a Response Reward from an LLM judge (GPT-4o-mini) scoring alignment across four personalization dimensions (preference expression, style consistency, goal alignment, persona coherence) gated by five binary quality criteria (naturalness, relevance, logical consistency, engagement, informativeness). Both rewards are summed at every turn, providing dense, immediate feedback that jointly supervises profile inference accuracy and response personalization. The model is instantiated by fine-tuning Qwen-2.5-3B-Instruct using OpenRLHF and vLLM on 8 A100 GPUs, with the ALOE dataset (3,821 samples) providing user profiles for simulation rather than ground-truth responses.

## Results

On the Vanilla ALOE benchmark, Qwen-RLPA achieves an average alignment score of 73.38 with the best N-R² (0.855) and N-IR (0.090) among all evaluated models, surpassing SFT (44.32) and DPO (45.27) by over 28 points. On the more challenging Extended ALOE setting (unseen profile schemas), RLPA leads in all three metrics with 52.74 alignment, 0.100 N-IR, and 0.498 N-R², outperforming GPT-4o-mini (51.23) and GPT-4o (54.66) on improvement and consistency metrics. The model also demonstrates superior long-term stability, maintaining alignment scores above 70 across 10 turns and sustaining profile accuracy over 70-turn dialogues.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against prompt-based baselines from Zhao et al. (2025) (Reminder, Self-Critic, RAG) and CoT prompting (Wei et al., 2022), RLPA achieves dramatic gains: on Extended ALOE, RLPA scores 52.74 versus 7.26 (Self-Critic), 26.30 (CoT), and 11.64 (RAG), indicating that static prompting strategies fail to generalize to unseen profile schemas. Among offline optimization methods, RLPA outperforms SFT (Ouyang et al., 2022) by +29.06 and DPO (Rafailov et al., 2023) by +28.11 on Vanilla ALOE, and by +28.36 and +25.48 respectively on Extended ALOE, with substantially higher N-R² values (0.855 vs. 0.628 for SFT; 0.855 vs. 0.389 for DPO), reflecting better response-profile coherence. Against commercial LLMs, RLPA slightly trails GPT-4o-mini (75.81) and GPT-4o (74.59) in raw Vanilla alignment but far exceeds them in N-R² (0.855 vs. 0.500/0.380), and on Extended ALOE it leads GPT-4o-mini in all metrics while matching GPT-4o's raw score with 3.7× higher N-IR. In the reasoning-efficiency comparison (Figure 5), Qwen-RLPA surpasses DeepSeek-R1 (Guo et al., 2025), GPT-o3-mini (OpenAI, 2025), and QwQ-32B (Qwen, 2025) in response alignment while using significantly fewer reasoning tokens per turn—QwQ-32B generates over 300 tokens yet achieves lower alignment.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from both prompt-based personalization (profile-augmented prompting, RAG) and offline optimization (SFT, DPO) by formulating personalized dialogue alignment as a multi-turn MDP solved via PPO with a simulated user. Conceptually, it repurposes the RLHF paradigm (Ouyang et al., 2022) from general value alignment to individual user modeling, introducing a dual-level reward (profile accuracy + response fidelity) that has no direct analogue in prior personalized-alignment work. The ALOE benchmark (Wu et al., 2025) provides the evaluation substrate, but the paper's core contribution is the interactive, reward-driven training loop rather than any single architectural component.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses a practically important gap: cold-start personalization where no user history exists, and long-term adaptation where preferences evolve mid-conversation. The demonstration that a 3B model trained with RLPA can match or exceed GPT-4o and Claude-3.5 on the ALOE benchmark, while using fewer reasoning tokens than DeepSeek-R1 or o3-mini, suggests that task-specific RL training can be a more efficient path to personalization than scaling or general-purpose reasoning. Key open questions include whether the approach extends to multi-user or cross-session settings, whether the slot-value profile representation generalizes beyond the ALOE schema, and what the theoretical convergence properties of the multi-turn MDP are under non-stationary user behavior.
