## Problem

Previous online-to-batch (O2B) conversion methods for accelerated convex optimization (Cutkosky 2019, Kavis et al. 2019, Joulani et al. 2020b) require explicitly designing optimistic online learning algorithms to achieve the optimal O(T^{-2}) rate, complicating the algorithm design. Moreover, no O2B conversion had previously achieved the optimal rate for strongly convex and smooth objectives, and universal methods that adapt to unknown smoothness required two gradient queries per iteration, making them less efficient than non-universal methods like NAG.

## Input / Output

The method takes a convex (or λ-strongly convex) L-smooth objective f: R^d → R, a convex feasible domain X ⊆ R^d, and a first-order gradient oracle. It outputs a weighted average of iterates (x̄_T or x̃_T) with a guaranteed suboptimality bound. A key practical detail is that the universal variant (Theorem 5) requires only one gradient evaluation per iteration—matching the efficiency of NAG—while the non-universal accelerated variant (Corollary 1) operates without requiring a bounded domain. The method requires knowing the total iteration count T upfront for the universal case (since α_T = 0).

## Method

The core contribution is an optimistic O2B conversion that embeds optimism directly into the conversion analysis rather than in the online algorithm. The key mechanism is a look-ahead regret structure: the algorithm queries ∇f(x̃_{t+1}) (where x̃_t uses x_{t-1} in the last weighted term) before updating from x_t to x_{t+1}, so the learner observes the current loss before acting. This naturally produces an optimistic term ⟨∇f(x̄_t) − ∇f(x̃_t), x_t − x_{t−1}⟩ in the bound, which under smoothness is bounded by L·α_t²/A_t·‖x_t − x_{t−1}‖² and cancels the negative stability term from online gradient descent, yielding O(T^{-2}) with simple OGD. For strongly convex objectives, optimism is distributed between the conversion (Theorem 2) and a one-step optimistic OGD (Theorem 3), achieving the first O(exp(−T/(1+2√κ))) rate via O2B conversion. For universality, a further enhanced conversion (Theorem 4) uses the identity A_{t−1}(x̃_t − x̃_{t−1}) = α_t(x_{t−1} − x̃_t) + α_{t−1}(x_{t−1} − x_{t−2}) to eliminate ∇f(x̄_t) entirely, combined with an AdaGrad-type step size to achieve both O(LD²/T²) and O(GD/√T) rates with a single gradient query per iteration.

## Results

The paper's primary results are theoretical convergence rate guarantees. For convex smooth objectives, the optimistic O2B conversion with simple online gradient descent achieves the optimal O(T^{-2}) rate (Corollary 1). For strongly convex smooth objectives, the method achieves the optimal O(exp(-T/(1+2√κ))) rate (Theorem 3), the first such result via O2B conversion. The universal variant (Theorem 5) achieves O(LD²/T²) for smooth and O(GD/√T) for non-smooth objectives using only one gradient query per iteration, matching the efficiency of non-universal methods like NAG. Numerical experiments on LIBSVM datasets (squared loss and logistic regression) confirm comparable or superior convergence performance versus baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against four baselines: Nesterov's Accelerated Gradient (NAG), standard Gradient Descent (GD), UniXGrad [Kavis et al., 2019], and the method of Joulani et al. [2020b]. The proposed optimistic O2B conversion is shown to be algorithmically equivalent to NAG in unconstrained settings under specific parameter configurations (Proposition 1), confirming its optimality. Compared to UniXGrad, the universal variant (Theorem 5) matches the same O(LD²/T²) and O(GD/√T) rates but requires only one gradient query per iteration versus two, making it strictly more efficient. Against Joulani et al. [2020b], both achieve O(T^{-2}) for convex smooth objectives, but the proposed method uses a simpler online gradient descent algorithm rather than a dedicated optimistic algorithm, since optimism is absorbed into the conversion analysis. The stabilized O2B conversion of Cutkosky [2019] achieves the same O(T^{-2}) rate but requires an optimistic online algorithm, whereas the proposed method liberates the algorithm design to use plain GD.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the stabilized online-to-batch (O2B) conversion framework of Cutkosky [2019], which required optimistic online learning algorithms to achieve accelerated convergence in smooth convex optimization. The key conceptual departure is relocating optimism from the online algorithm to the conversion analysis itself: by introducing a look-ahead regret structure and an explicit optimistic term in the bound, the conversion can be paired with plain online gradient descent to recover the optimal O(T^{-2}) rate. The framework also builds on the AdaGrad-type self-confident tuning of Kavis et al. [2019] for universality and the one-step optimistic gradient descent of Joulani et al. [2020a] for the strongly convex extension, while drawing a parallel to the game-theoretic acceleration analysis of Wang and Abernethy [2018] but replacing the Fenchel-game lens with a direct O2B interpretation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first O2B-conversion-based derivation of the optimal convergence rate for strongly convex and smooth objectives, and a universal method (smooth or non-smooth, no smoothness parameter needed) that requires only one gradient query per iteration—matching the efficiency of NAG. This unifies several previously separate threads (optimistic online learning, game-theoretic acceleration, universal gradient methods) under a single conversion framework and clarifies that optimism is a property of the analysis rather than a mandatory algorithmic component. Open questions include extending the universal method to unconstrained domains, achieving optimal universal rates for strongly convex objectives (where the weight schedule depends on the unknown smoothness parameter), and validating the approach in large-scale deep learning training.
