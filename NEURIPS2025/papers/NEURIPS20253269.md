## Problem

Computing geodesics between data points in high-dimensional spaces requires a Riemannian metric that captures the local curvature of the data manifold, but existing metric estimation methods (kernel-based, normalizing flows, density-based) degrade sharply as dimensionality grows due to sparse local sampling. This paper asks whether the energy landscape of a pretrained Energy-Based Model, which assigns meaningful values across the entire ambient space, can serve as a principled source of such a metric.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253269/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes two endpoint data points and a pretrained EBM energy function E_θ(x) as input. For high-dimensional image data, the EBM operates in the latent space of a pretrained VAE (e.g., Stable Diffusion V1 with 4×16×16 latent codes), and all baselines are evaluated in the same latent space for fairness. The output is a conformal Riemannian metric of the form λ(x)·I (isotropic scaling of the identity) and a geodesic path between the endpoints, parameterized by a neural interpolant that corrects a linear baseline with a smooth curvature term.

## Method

The core contribution is deriving two conformal Riemannian metrics directly from a pretrained EBM: G_Eθ = (α·E_θ(x)+β)·I, which scales the raw energy, and G_{1/pθ} = (α·exp(−E_θ(x))+β)^{−1}·I, which uses the inverse unnormalized density. The EBM is trained via contrastive divergence with Langevin dynamics sampling, producing a global energy landscape that assigns high values to off-manifold regions. Geodesics are then computed by training a single neural interpolant network φ_η over batches of random endpoint pairs, minimizing the kinetic energy functional with finite-difference velocity estimation rather than full autodifferentiation. Calibration constants α and β are chosen so the metric equals I on the data manifold and 10³·I in low-density regions, ensuring fair comparison across all metrics. The key insight is that the EBM's energy landscape naturally penalizes off-manifold directions, progressively drawing interpolated points toward the data manifold during optimization.

## Results

On the high-dimensional AFHQ dataset (4×16×16 VAE latent space), the EBM-derived metric G_{1/p_θ} achieves the lowest FID of 16.47, followed by G_{E_θ} at 20.79, substantially outperforming slerp (32.67), G_RBF (37.98), G_LAND (39.17), and linear interpolation (42.47). On the synthetic UCG dataset with ground-truth geodesics, G_{E_θ} attains the highest accumulated probability (0.78) while G_{1/p_θ} achieves the lowest RMSE to its ground-truth baseline (0.10), both far exceeding G_LAND (0.60 / 0.38) and G_RBF (0.61 / 0.39). On the rotated characters (URC) dataset, G_{E_θ} reduces D-RMSE to 0.11 and γ*-RMSE to 0.40, compared to 0.66 and 2.39 for G_LAND.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares two EBM-derived conformal metrics (G_{E_θ} and G_{1/p_θ}) against three baselines: the nonparametric LAND metric [11], the RBF-based conformal metric [13], and spherical interpolation (slerp) [60]. Both EBM metrics consistently outperform G_LAND and G_RBF across all five datasets. On AFHQ, G_{1/p_θ} (FID 16.47) beats G_RBF (37.98) by 21.5 FID points and G_LAND (39.17) by 22.7 points, and also surpasses slerp (32.67) by 16.2 points. On UCG, G_{E_θ} achieves the highest on-manifold probability (0.78 vs. 0.60 for G_LAND and 0.61 for G_RBF), while G_{1/p_θ} achieves the lowest RMSE to ground truth (0.10 vs. 0.38 and 0.39). On URC, G_{E_θ} reduces D-RMSE to 0.11 versus 0.66 for G_LAND and 0.36 for G_RBF, with γ*-RMSE of 0.40 versus 2.39 and 0.86 respectively. The gap between EBM metrics and baselines widens with dimensionality, suggesting that parametric and kernel-based metrics struggle to scale to high-dimensional spaces.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of density-based Riemannian metric construction (LAND, RBF) by replacing local, sample-based density estimates with the global energy landscape of a pretrained EBM. It builds directly on Du & Mordatch's contrastive-divergence EBM training for the energy function and on Kapusniak et al.'s neural-interpolant framework for geodesic optimization, but departs from both by deriving the metric from the EBM's unnormalized density rather than from empirical covariance or kernel density estimates. The conceptual link to Perone's unpublished Stein-score metric and Diepeveen et al.'s score-based pullback geometry is acknowledged, but the EBM's global energy (trained to assign meaningful values across the entire ambient space, not just near data) is the key distinction that enables scaling to high dimensions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first practical recipe for computing data-aware geodesics in high-dimensional latent spaces (e.g., 4×16×16 Stable Diffusion VAE codes) where prior local-density methods (LAND, RBF) degrade due to the curse of dimensionality. The finding that a simple conformal scaling by raw EBM energy matches or exceeds more complex non-conformal metrics is practically useful, reducing implementation complexity. Open questions include whether non-conformal (anisotropic) metrics derived from the EBM score function can capture directional structure, whether EBM training cost can be reduced for very large datasets, and whether the geodesic framework translates into measurable gains for downstream tasks like mental-rotation modeling or neural trajectory analysis.
