## Problem

Computing exact SHAP values is NP-hard for expressive models like neural networks, while polynomial-time algorithms exist only for simpler model families such as decision trees. This paper tackles two gaps: (i) no exact SHAP algorithm was known for the broader and more expressive class of Tensor Networks, and (ii) no fine-grained analysis existed for how structural parameters (width, depth, sparsity) of neural networks govern SHAP computational hardness.

## Input / Output

The method takes a Tensor Network model T^M ∈ R^{N_1×...×N_{n_in}×n_out}, a probability distribution over the input space also encoded as a TN T^P, and a concrete input instance x ∈ D. It produces the SHAP Matrix Φ ∈ R^{n_in × n_out}, where row i is the full attribution vector for feature i across all n_out outputs. Complexity is measured in the input/output dimensions, the maximum feature cardinality max_i N_i, and the total number of TN parameters, rather than the exponential volume of the full input space.

## Method

The paper introduces a tensorized reformulation of SHAP that expresses the Marginal SHAP Tensor as a contraction of a modified Weighted Coalitional Tensor (encoding Shapley weights) and a Marginal Value Tensor (encoding interventional expectations), both constructible as sparse TTs in O(log n) parallel time. When both the model and distribution are TTs, the resulting Marginal SHAP Tensor itself admits a TT representation (Theorem 1), and contracting it with the input one-hot vector via a parallel scan yields an NC² algorithm (Proposition 3). Via NC-reductions, this result tightens complexity bounds for decision trees, tree ensembles, linear models, and linear RNNs to NC² under TT-based distributions. For Binarized Neural Networks, a parameterized complexity analysis (Theorem 3) reveals that SHAP remains para-NP-hard even at constant depth, becomes XP when width is fixed, and achieves FPT when both width and reified-cardinality sparsity are bounded—identifying width, not depth, as the primary computational bottleneck.

## Results

The paper establishes that computing exact SHAP for general Tensor Networks is #P-Hard (Proposition 2), but for the Tensor Train (TT) subclass, SHAP computation lies in NC², i.e., it is solvable in poly-logarithmic time with polynomially many parallel processors (Proposition 3). This NC² result extends via reduction to decision trees, tree ensembles, linear models, and linear RNNs under TT-based distributions (Theorem 2), tightening previously known polynomial-time bounds. For Binarized Neural Networks, the paper shows SHAP is para-NP-hard w.r.t. depth, XP w.r.t. width, and FPT w.r.t. width + reified cardinality (Theorem 3), identifying width as the primary computational bottleneck.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against experimental baselines, as it is a purely theoretical complexity-theoretic study. Instead, it tightens and extends prior complexity results: Van den Broeck et al. [106] and Arenas et al. [6] established polynomial-time tractability for SHAP under independent distributions for decomposable deterministic Boolean circuits, while Marzouk et al. [81, 82] extended this to Markovian and HMM distributions. This work subsumes and strengthens those results by showing NC² membership (poly-logarithmic parallel time) for the same model families, and by broadening the admissible distribution class to all TT-representable distributions, which englobes independent, empirical, Markovian, HMM, and Born Machine distributions. The BNN analysis (Theorem 3) is the first fine-grained parameterized complexity result for SHAP on neural networks, going beyond the coarse NP-hardness results of [106, 82] by isolating width and sparsity as the tractability parameters.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of tractable SHAP computation from simple model families (decision trees via TreeSHAP, linear models, Markovian/HMM distributions) to the broader and more expressive class of Tensor Networks, using the Tensor Train structure as the key tractability vehicle. It builds on the model-counting-to-SHAP reduction (Kara et al.) for hardness results, the parallel scan technique for TT contraction (Martin & Cundy) for the NC result, and the reified cardinality encoding of BNNs (Jia & Rinard) for the parameterized complexity analysis. The paper also departs from prior work by introducing two new analytical dimensions: the first complexity-theoretic study of SHAP parallelizability (NC membership) and the first fine-grained parameterized complexity analysis of SHAP for neural networks (width, depth, sparsity).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The NC result for TTs closes a significant expressivity gap: exact SHAP was previously only known for relatively simple models, while neural networks (where explanations are most needed) were shown NP-hard. By showing that TTs — which can represent decision trees, tree ensembles, linear RNNs, and linear models — admit polylogarithmic parallel SHAP computation, the paper provides a concrete path to scaling SHAP to high-dimensional settings with expressive (non-independent) feature distributions. The finding that width, not depth, is the primary computational bottleneck for BNNs is a new structural insight that suggests a practical relaxation axis for designing explainable networks. Limitations include the purely theoretical scope (no empirical validation), restriction to discrete input spaces, and the fact that general (non-binarized) neural networks remain intractable.
