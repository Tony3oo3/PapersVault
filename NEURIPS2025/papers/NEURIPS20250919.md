## Problem

Existing imitation learning methods (MaxEnt IRL, GAIL, LPAL) guarantee that the imitator matches or exceeds demonstrator performance only in expectation, which collapses distinct behavioral modes into an average and provides no distributional guarantees. This paper asks whether imitation can be reformulated to ensure the imitator's reward distribution stochastically dominates the demonstration distribution for all conical sum reward functions simultaneously—a property called pluralistic stochastic dominance (PSD)—thereby preserving and improving upon the full spectrum of demonstrated behaviors.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a set of demonstration trajectories characterized by K-dimensional reward features (with unknown non-negative weight vectors defining conical sum reward functions) plus environment observations for policy model training. In the fully-realizable setting, it also requires a candidate set of imitator trajectories (ideally on or near the Pareto frontier). The output is either a discrete trajectory distribution (fully-realizable) or a parametric stochastic policy (Gaussian MLP) whose induced trajectory distribution stochastically dominates the demonstration distribution across all reward functions in the conical sum family, verified via Pareto and stochastic dominance metrics on withheld demonstrations.

## Method

The core insight is that PSD can be expressed as a family of optimal transport problems (one per reward function θ) with cost [r_θ(demo) − r_θ(imit)]_+, but the outer maximization over θ is NP-hard. The paper derives a tractable upper bound by pushing the θ-maximization inside the transport plan, yielding a single optimal transport problem whose cost is the subdominance function subdom_{α,β}(ξ, ξ̃) = Σ_k [α_k(f_k(ξ̃) − f_k(ξ)) + β]_+, which measures how far an imitator trajectory is from Pareto-dominating its paired demonstration. Minimizing this matched subdominance to zero guarantees PSD (Theorem 3.6). For policy model training, the OT solution provides per-trajectory training signals (via demonstration normalization or weighted best match) that drive REINFORCE-style policy gradient updates, enabling stochastic dominance optimization without enumerating candidate trajectories. Generalization to the population of demonstrators is established via a multivariate Dvoretzky-Kiefer-Wolfowitz bound on the empirical cumulative mass function.

## Results

On Point Bot, PSD-α* achieves 0.657 average stochastic dominance and 0.387 average Pareto dominance, substantially outperforming all baselines (next best: InfoGAIL* at 0.227 avg Pareto, 0.000 stochastic). On Reacher, PSD-α* reaches 0.662 average stochastic dominance and 0.500 average Pareto dominance, again leading all methods. Critically, PSD is the only method achieving non-zero minimum Pareto dominance on Point Bot (0.070), meaning it Pareto-dominates every single demonstration with at least 7% probability, while all baselines fail to dominate at least one demonstration.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PSD and PSD-α* dominate all seven baselines on both Pareto and stochastic dominance metrics across Point Bot and Reacher. GAIL suffers severe mode collapse, achieving near-zero Pareto dominance (0.015 avg on Point Bot, 0.005 on Reacher) despite a surprisingly high 0.425 stochastic dominance on Reacher, indicating it produces a narrow distribution that happens to shift the reward distribution favorably but fails to cover demonstrated modes. InfoGAIL* performs competitively on Reacher (0.409 min Pareto) where modes are cleanly separable, but collapses on Point Bot (0.000 min Pareto) where demonstrations span a continuum of trade-offs rather than discrete modes. RAIL is the weakest baseline, achieving 0.000 on nearly all metrics. Behavior cloning and MaxEnt IRL/LPAL/BROIL (evaluated on Lava World in Table 1) all achieve 0.00 minimum Pareto dominance, meaning they fail to unambiguously outperform at least one demonstration, whereas PSD maintains 0.08 minimum. The learned α variant (PSD-α*) consistently improves over fixed-α PSD by 0.05–0.24 in average stochastic dominance, confirming the benefit of adapting reward feature weights.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the subdominance minimization framework of Ziebart et al. (2022), which targeted uniform dominance (all imitator samples beat all demonstration samples), by replacing it with the weaker but always-feasible stochastic dominance criterion. The key technical bridge is the Kantorovich optimal transport formulation, which connects stochastic dominance to a zero-cost matching condition (Remark 2.6), and the recent multivariate stochastic dominance via OT results of Rioux et al. (2024). Unlike risk-sensitive imitation methods (Majumdar et al., 2017; Santara et al., 2018; Lacotte et al., 2019) that target a single quantile or specific tail risk, PSD simultaneously guarantees improvement across all risk-sensitive measures (VaR, CVaR, RVaR) and all conical sum reward functions, generalizing the expectation-matching guarantee of MaxEnt IRL and LPAL to a full distributional guarantee.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PSD provides a principled alternative justification for stochastic imitation policies beyond maximum entropy IRL, and its guarantees directly support practical use cases like sampling many candidate trajectories and selecting the best. Key limitations include the requirement of known reward features, the assumption of deterministic dynamics, and the NP-hardness of exact PSD verification (mitigated by the matched subdominance upper bound). Open questions include extending to stochastic environments, learning reward features without hand-engineering, and scaling policy gradient optimization with distributional objectives to larger, more complex environments.
