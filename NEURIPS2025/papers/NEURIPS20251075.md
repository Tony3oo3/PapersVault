## Problem

While prior work has extensively studied the eigenvalue behavior of the Neural Tangent Kernel (NTK) at the Edge of Stability (EoS)—where the largest eigenvalue oscillates around 2/η—the dynamics of NTK eigenvectors during EoS remain unexplored. Specifically, it is unknown how the eigenvectors rotate and align with the training target during the nontrivial eigenvalue oscillations, and whether this alignment behavior is connected to the EoS mechanism.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251075/cropped_page2_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a neural network (2-layer linear, 4-layer GeLU FC, ViT, VGG-11, ResNet-20, or BERT-small) trained via GD or SGD under MSE loss on structured data (Gaussian), RGB images (CIFAR-10), or text (IMDB), with the learning rate as the key controlled variable. It produces time-resolved trajectories of the Kernel Target Alignment (KTA = yᵀKy / (‖y‖²‖K‖_F)) and per-eigenvector target alignments ((yᵀu_k)²/‖y‖²), which sum to 1 and form a 'distribution' over eigenvectors. The theoretical analysis additionally outputs a closed-form characterization of the alignment shift via the ratio α_t = ‖v_t‖²/c_t² in a 2-layer linear network with a specific unwhitened data spectrum.

## Method

The paper identifies an 'alignment shift' phenomenon: larger learning rates cause the leading NTK eigenvectors to acquire greater alignment with the target, and this shift occurs in bursts during sharpness-decreasing phases of EoS. The theoretical mechanism, proven for a 2-layer linear network with rank-1 weight structure, shows that during Phase III (sharpness reduction), the previously ignored η² term in the update of ‖v_t‖²—driven by the diverging error component along the top data eigenvector q₁—causes ‖v_t‖² to increase while c_t² decreases, thereby increasing the ratio α_t = ‖v_t‖²/c_t². A larger α_t shifts the NTK eigenvector spectrum so that the target direction ŷ aligns with earlier (larger-eigenvalue) NTK eigenvectors rather than the data kernel's top eigenvectors. The paper further validates the sharpness-reduction mechanism using the central flow framework, showing that the implicit sharpness penalty in the time-averaged GD trajectory directly promotes KTA increase, and that removing this penalty (pure gradient flow) yields lower KTA trajectories.

## Results

Across fully-connected networks, ViT, VGG-11, and ResNet-20 on CIFAR-10, larger GD learning rates consistently produce higher final Kernel Target Alignment (KTA) and shift individual eigenvector alignment toward leading NTK eigenvectors. Quantitatively, in the fully-connected setting (Table 1a), the average KTA change per step during sharpness-decreasing periods (0.0029–0.0065) is 5–10× larger than during sharpness-increasing periods (−0.0014 to 0.0003). In the ViT setting (Table 1b), the same pattern holds with average KTA change per step of 2.30e-4 to 6.68e-4 during decreases versus −4.67e-5 to −3.83e-5 during increases. For generalization, Table 4 shows that a larger learning rate (1.2 vs. 0.8) yields a lower test loss after the same effective steps (1.199 vs. 1.211) and a 7.75× larger test-loss decrease during the alignment-shift oscillation period (−0.062 vs. −0.008).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not compare against competing methods; instead, it compares training dynamics across learning rates, architectures, and phases of the Edge of Stability cycle. The central empirical comparison is between sharpness-decreasing and sharpness-increasing phases: in every architecture tested (fully-connected, ViT, VGG-11, ResNet-20), KTA increases significantly faster during sharpness-decreasing periods, confirming that EoS sharpness reduction is a contributing factor to alignment shift rather than merely correlated. A second comparison is between GD, central flow, and pure gradient flow: central flow (which includes a sharpness penalty) tracks GD's KTA closely, while branching to gradient flow (no sharpness penalty) from the same point yields a lower-KTA trajectory, isolating the sharpness penalty as the mechanism. Across learning rates, the alignment shift is monotonic: larger η produces higher final KTA and a more concentrated eigenvector alignment distribution on the top eigenvectors, with the effect visible in both binary (CIFAR-10 two-class) and multi-class (CIFAR-10 ten-class) settings as well as in the language task (BERT-small on IMDB).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Edge of Stability (EoS) literature, which has focused almost exclusively on eigenvalue dynamics (oscillation around 2/η), by studying the previously unexamined eigenvector rotation and its alignment with the training target during EoS. It builds on the four-phase EoS framework of Wang et al. (NeurIPS 2022) and the rank-1 weight structure results of Jiang et al. (NeurIPS 2024), while incorporating the central flow framework of Cohen et al. (ICLR 2024) as a mechanistic tool. The key conceptual departure is showing that the η² term in the GD update, which prior EoS analyses treated as negligible, is in fact the driving force behind the alignment shift phenomenon.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a concrete mechanism for why large learning rates improve generalization—through alignment shift of the NTK toward the target during sharpness-reduction phases—offering a complementary explanation to the 'flat minima' narrative. The theoretical analysis is currently limited to two-layer linear networks (with partial extension to ReLU), leaving the multi-layer nonlinear case open. A broader open question is how this alignment shift connects to the feature learning properties of deep networks, which the authors identify as a key future direction.
