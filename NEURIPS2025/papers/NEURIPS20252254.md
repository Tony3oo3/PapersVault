## Problem

Vision-based 3D occupancy prediction degrades significantly in nighttime scenes due to two distinct illumination challenges: underexposure from insufficient ambient light that destroys visual features, and overexposure from artificial sources (headlights, streetlamps) that saturates pixels and erases semantic information. No prior occupancy framework explicitly models or compensates for these dual illumination effects across both the 2D feature extraction and 3D view transformation stages.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252254/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-camera RGB images from nighttime driving scenes (6 cameras in nuScenes) and produces a 3D semantic occupancy grid at 0.4 m voxel resolution over a 80 m × 80 m × 6.4 m spatial volume with 17 semantic classes. Two variants are provided: a single-frame model and a temporal model that fuses one historical frame. The SLLIE module is pretrained in a self-supervised manner on the nighttime subset of nuScenes and frozen during occupancy training, while the rest of the pipeline is trained end-to-end with weighted cross-entropy plus auxiliary semantic and geometric consistency losses.

## Method

LIAR introduces three illumination-aware components built on the FlashOcc backbone with ResNet-50. First, SLLIE performs Retinex decomposition to estimate a per-pixel illumination map, then applies an Otsu-style maximum inter-class variance threshold to selectively enhance only genuinely dark images, preventing over-brightening of already well-lit frames. Second, 2D-IGS uses the illumination map to modulate deformable convolution offsets: darker pixels receive larger sampling displacements toward adequately-exposed neighbors, while brighter pixels retain small offsets, thereby aggregating semantic cues into underexposed regions. Third, 3D-IDP lifts each BEV grid cell into N_z vertical 3D points, projects them back to 2D to sample illumination values, and aggregates these into a 3D illumination intensity field that scales the residual queries in a deformable cross-attention refinement step, emphasizing overexposed regions where semantics are most deficient. The key design principle is that underexposure and overexposure are addressed at different pipeline stages (2D sampling vs. 3D projection) using the same illumination signal as a spatial prior.

## Results

On the real-world Occ3D-nuScenes nighttime split (train: night, test: night), LIAR(1f) achieves 19.27 mIoU, outperforming the best single-frame baseline FlashOcc(1f) (13.42) by 5.85 points, and LIAR(2f) reaches 22.09 mIoU, surpassing COTR(2f) (20.01) by 2.08 points. When trained on the full day+night dataset and tested on nighttime, LIAR(2f) attains 27.33 mIoU, exceeding COTR(2f) (25.17) by 2.16 points. On the synthetic nuScenes-C benchmark (easy severity), LIAR(1f) achieves 22.52 mIoU, beating the second-best single-frame method BEVDetOcc(1f) (15.08) by 7.44 points, and LIAR(2f) reaches 30.66 mIoU, surpassing OPUS(8f) (23.63) by 7.03 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LIAR consistently outperforms all nine baselines across both real and synthetic nighttime benchmarks. On Occ3D-nuScenes (night→night), LIAR(1f) beats FlashOcc(1f) by 5.85 mIoU and SparseOcc(1f) by 5.95 mIoU; with temporal fusion, LIAR(2f) surpasses COTR(2f) by 2.08 and FBOcc(2f) by 2.30 mIoU. In the day+night→night setting, LIAR(2f) leads COTR(2f) by 2.16 mIoU and FlashOcc(2f) by 3.93 mIoU, while its single-frame variant already exceeds FlashOcc(2f) by 0.27 mIoU. On nuScenes-C (easy), LIAR(1f) outperforms BEVDetOcc(1f) by 7.44 mIoU, and LIAR(2f) beats OPUS(8f) by 7.03 mIoU; under the hard severity, LIAR(1f) (12.21) even surpasses BEVFormer(4f) (5.71) and SparseOcc(8f) (10.77). When state-of-the-art low-light enhancers (LCDPNet, CSEC) are applied as preprocessing to COTR, the gains are marginal (+0.16 to +0.21 mIoU), whereas LIAR's integrated illumination-aware design yields a 1.87–1.92 mIoU advantage over those enhanced baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

LIAR builds directly on the FlashOcc architecture (channel-to-height plugin for memory-efficient occupancy prediction) and the LSS/BEVPooling view-transformation paradigm, but departs from prior occupancy methods by treating illumination as a first-class geometric signal rather than a nuisance to be corrected by preprocessing. It repurposes Retinex decomposition (from SCI) not as a standalone enhancement step but as a source of spatially-varying illumination maps that modulate both 2D deformable convolution sampling offsets and 3D deformable cross-attention weights. The selective enhancement mechanism (Otsu-style thresholding on illumination factor) distinguishes it from prior low-light enhancement methods that uniformly brighten all inputs, and the illumination-guided sampling/projection design has no direct precedent in the occupancy prediction literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Nighttime perception is a critical safety gap for vision-based autonomous driving, and this paper provides the first systematic framework that addresses both underexposure (feature degradation) and overexposure (semantic deficiency) simultaneously within a single occupancy pipeline. The practical insight that indiscriminate enhancement of all nighttime images actively hurts performance (due to over-brightening already-well-lit regions) is a useful caution for practitioners. Key limitations include the very small real-world nighttime subset (84 train / 15 val scenes in Occ3D-nuScenes), the synthetic nature of nuScenes-C (which retains daytime semantics and lacks sensor-specific degradations like thermal noise or motion blur), and a ~57% increase in FLOPs over FlashOcc. Open questions include whether illumination-affined representations generalize to other adverse conditions (rain, fog, glare) and whether the Retinex decomposition can be made more efficient for deployment.
