## Problem

2D foundation models (SAM2, TAP, Depth Anything) produce spatially misaligned and temporally flickering predictions in dynamic 3D scenes because they process frames independently without explicit 3D reasoning. Existing 3D-based approaches either target static scenes or decouple semantic understanding from motion estimation, preventing coherent joint modeling of both in casual monocular videos.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253869/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a posed RGB video sequence together with 2D priors from pre-trained foundation models (SAM2 object masks, TAP point tracks, Depth Anything monocular depth), but critically treats these priors as mutable rather than fixed supervision: SAM2 masks are iteratively re-prompted and updated during training, and tracking/depth losses are reweighted by a learned 3D confidence field. The output is a 4D Gaussian Splatting scene with per-Gaussian motion (deformation) and semantic fields, from which 3D-consistent masks, 3D point trajectories, depth maps, and novel views are rendered.

## Method

Motion4D extends 3D Gaussian Splatting with a deformation field (rigid SE(3) transforms parameterized by global motion bases) and a per-Gaussian semantic field, then optimizes them via a two-part iterative framework. Sequential optimization first refines the motion field over short temporal chunks using a 3D confidence map (an uncertainty field rendered to 2D that downweights unreliable 2D tracking/depth priors) and an adaptive resampling module that inserts new Gaussians into under-represented regions identified by per-pixel RGB and semantic errors; it then refines the semantic field by rendering 3D masks, comparing them against 2D SAM2 masks, and feeding bounding-box and distance-transform-based point prompts back into SAM2 to update the 2D priors. A final global optimization stage jointly trains all fields (RGB, semantic, tracking, depth, confidence) over the full sequence to eliminate inter-chunk drift. This closed-loop design—where the 3D representation corrects 2D priors and the corrected priors in turn supervise the 3D representation—enforces spatio-temporal consistency that neither modality can achieve alone.

## Results

On the proposed DyCheck-VOS benchmark, Motion4D+SAM2 achieves a J&F score of 91.7, outperforming SAM2 (89.4) by 2.3 points and the best 3D-based method SADG (81.8) by 9.9 points. On DAVIS 2017, Motion4D+SAM2 reaches 90.8 J&F, matching SAM2 (90.7) while substantially exceeding 3D baselines (SADG: 75.0, Semantic Flow: 72.2). For 2D point tracking on DyCheck, Motion4D achieves AJ=37.3 and δ_avg=50.4, surpassing CoTracker3 (AJ=31.0) and Shape of Motion (AJ=34.4). For 3D tracking and novel view synthesis on DyCheck, Motion4D attains the lowest EPE (0.072) and highest PSNR (17.91) among all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against 2D segmentation baselines, Motion4D+SAM2 outperforms SAM2 by 2.3 J&F on DyCheck-VOS (91.7 vs. 89.4) and matches it on DAVIS (90.8 vs. 90.7), while XMem and DEVA trail by 7–8 J&F points on DyCheck-VOS. Against 3D-based methods, the advantage is substantial: SADG lags by 9.9 J&F on DyCheck-VOS and 15.8 on DAVIS, and Semantic Flow falls behind by 14.8 and 18.6 J&F respectively, indicating that simply embedding SAM2 masks into a 3D representation without iterative refinement degrades performance. For 2D point tracking, Motion4D surpasses CoTracker3 (AJ 37.3 vs. 31.0) and BootsTAPIR (37.3 vs. 30.1) on DyCheck, and matches CoTracker3 on DAVIS (AJ 64.4) while achieving higher δ_avg (77.7 vs. 76.9). In 3D tracking, Motion4D reduces EPE to 0.072 versus 0.082 for Shape of Motion and 0.114 for TAPIR+DepthAnything, and achieves the highest δ3D^1.0 (75.9%). For novel view synthesis, Motion4D leads with PSNR 17.91 and SSIM 0.69, exceeding Shape of Motion (16.72/0.63) and HyperNeRF (15.99/0.59).

## Relation to Prior Work

Motion4D builds on 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) as its base representation, adopts the deformation field with global motion bases from Shape of Motion [42] for dynamic scene modeling, and extends error-based densification from static 3DGS [35] to dynamic environments. It departs from prior 3D+segmentation methods (SADG, Semantic Flow) that treat 2D priors as fixed supervision decoupled from the 3D model, instead introducing a bidirectional iterative refinement loop where the 3D representation actively updates 2D foundation model outputs (SAM2 masks, tracking priors) through rendered views and confidence-weighted losses. The sequential-then-global optimization strategy is a practical departure from prior work that either optimizes all fields jointly from the start or treats motion and semantics in fully separate pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a fundamental gap: 2D foundation models (SAM2, TAP, Depth Anything) produce high-quality per-frame predictions but lack the 3D consistency needed for downstream geometric tasks, while existing 3D-based methods fail to leverage the rich priors from these models effectively. The iterative refinement loop—where 3D rendering feeds back into 2D prompt-based models—is a practical recipe that could be adopted by other 3D+foundation-model pipelines. Key limitations include dependence on initial 3D reconstruction quality (degrading under severe occlusions or low-texture regions) and the per-scene optimization cost, leaving open questions about scalability to longer sequences, multi-object scenes, and real-time deployment.
