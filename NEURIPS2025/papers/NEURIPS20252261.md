## Problem

MLLMs face a fundamental resolution bottleneck: downsampling high-resolution images to fit input constraints destroys the fine-grained visual details needed to perceive and segment ultra-small objects in cluttered scenes. Existing high-resolution MLLMs are either training-free (limiting perception accuracy) or lack precise localization, and no prior method unifies instruction-guided reasoning (text answers) with pixel-level segmentation of extremely small targets under a single, data-efficient training paradigm.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 4K-resolution UAV image (3840×2060) and a natural-language instruction as input. The GSE stage processes the full image at 1920×1080 and outputs a 256×256 coarse region (center offsets only, fixed size) plus a text response; this region is 2× upsampled and cropped from the original to form a 512×512 patch for LPR, which outputs a bounding box and two point prompts. A frozen SAM2 model converts the box and points into the final segmentation mask. The unified output supports three task formats: instruction-guided segmentation (mask only), multiple-choice VQA (mask + option letter), and open-ended VQA (mask + free-form answer).

## Method

FINERS is a two-stage coarse-to-fine MLLM pipeline built on Qwen2.5-VL-7B, trained entirely with GRPO (Group Relative Policy Optimization) rather than supervised fine-tuning. The Global Semantic Exploration (GSE) module performs instruction-guided reasoning over the full high-resolution image to produce a text answer and a coarse target region; the Localized Perceptual Refinement (LPR) module then operates on the cropped region to generate a precise bounding box and point prompts for SAM2. The key coupling mechanism is a locate-informed retrospective reward: during GSE training, multiple candidate coarse regions are generated, LPR is run on each, and the region yielding the highest IoU between LPR's predicted box and the ground-truth box is selected as the supervision target for GSE. A multi-task reward pool (box IoU, box L1, point L1, region size, box-in-region, JSON format, think format, and QA accuracy) is assigned to each stage, enabling simultaneous optimization of segmentation precision and answer correctness from only ~9K training samples.

## Results

On the FINERS-4k test set, FINERS (7B) achieves a gIoU of 55.1 and cIoU of 46.5 for instruction-guided segmentation, with particularly strong performance on extra-extra-small objects (XXS: gIoU 47.2, cIoU 27.5). For visual question answering, it reaches 83.3% accuracy on multiple-choice VQA and 56.7% on open-ended VQA. On public high-resolution benchmarks, FINERS attains 77.5% overall on V*, 63.8% average on HR-Bench 4K, and 58.1% on HR-Bench 8K, outperforming all training-free baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FINERS substantially outperforms all segmentation baselines: over Seg-Zero† (the strongest retrained competitor) by +8.5 gIoU and +7.9 cIoU overall, with the gap widening to +15.5 gIoU on XXS objects (47.2 vs. 31.7). Against training-free high-resolution VQA methods, FINERS leads DC² by +44.1 points on MVQA (83.3 vs. 39.2) and +38.9 on OVQA (56.7 vs. 17.8), and surpasses MLLMs-Know 13B by +30.7 MVQA and +7.9 OVQA. On the V* benchmark, FINERS achieves 77.5% overall vs. SEAL's 75.4% and DC²'s 57.3%; on HR-Bench 4K it reaches 63.8% average vs. DC²'s 50.0% and SEAL's 38.1%. Notably, Seg-Zero and all MLLMs-Know variants do not support VQA, while LISA, LISA++, and PixelLM collapse to near-zero QA accuracy on FINERS-4k due to domain shift from standard-resolution training. The only setting where FINERS trails is on the S (largest) object size in the validation set, where Seg-Zero† achieves 67.2 gIoU vs. FINERS's 64.8, but FINERS recovers the lead on XS and XXS categories.

## Relation to Prior Work

FINERS sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) MLLM-based reasoning segmentation (LISA, LISA++, PixelLM), which couples language generation with pixel-level masks via special tokens but is limited to standard-resolution, large-object scenarios; (2) high-resolution image understanding (SEAL, DC², MLLMs-Know), which addresses resolution limits through patching or attention manipulation but lacks precise localization; and (3) visual reinforcement fine-tuning (Visual-RFT, Seg-Zero), which uses GRPO-based rewards to fine-tune MLLMs data-efficiently but has not unified segmentation with open-ended or multiple-choice VQA. FINERS departs from all three by introducing a two-stage coarse-to-fine pipeline (GSE → LPR) trained entirely with GRPO, coupled by a novel locate-informed retrospective reward that lets the fine-grained stage supervise the coarse stage, and by unifying three task formats (IS, MVQA, OVQA) under a single multi-task reward pool.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important gap: MLLMs still fail on ultra-small objects in 4K imagery, a setting increasingly relevant for aerial monitoring, remote sensing, and security. The FINERS-4k benchmark (12K annotated text-mask pairs from UAV footage) is a significant community resource, being the first to combine high-resolution aerial scenes with explicit ultra-small object granularity (S/XS/XXS) and three task types. Key limitations acknowledged by the authors include the hard dependency of LPR on GSE's coarse region (missed objects outside the region are unrecoverable) and the inability to jointly optimize both stages end-to-end due to memory constraints. Open questions include whether a single-stage or attention-based architecture could match the two-stage design, and whether the RL recipe generalizes beyond 7B backbones to larger or smaller models without the observed degradation.
