## Problem

Vertical Federated Learning (VFL) lacks publicly available real-world datasets for evaluation because privacy concerns prevent sharing of cross-platform data, forcing existing benchmarks to rely on artificial segmentation or synthetic splits that poorly reflect actual VFL deployments. Beyond the data gap, there is no unified evaluation protocol that simultaneously covers efficiency, robustness, and security, and robustness methods for corrupted and misaligned features in VFL are entirely absent from the literature.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254272/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each dataset distributes different feature blocks of the same sample across 2–5 clients (e.g., accelerometer vs. gyroscope, RGB vs. force sensors, static demographics vs. hourly time-series), with the active client holding labels and passive clients holding complementary features. The benchmark evaluates methods under three standardized protocols: efficiency (main task performance, communication cost in MB/GB, and convergence epochs), robustness (MP under missing/corrupted/misaligned feature rates r_a, r_b ∈ {0, 0.2, 0.5, 0.8, 1.0}), and security (attack performance and MP under label/feature inference and backdoor attacks with four gradient-based defenses).

## Method

MARS-VFL integrates 12 datasets from five application domains (HAR, robotics, healthcare, emotion, multimedia) where client features naturally originate from different devices, domains, or modalities, aligning with real VFL deployments. The paper's new baseline RVFL has two variants: RVFL-Aug generates two augmented copies of each client's data via random mask/scale operations, mixes them with the original using Dirichlet-sampled weights, and enforces Jensen-Shannon divergence consistency between the logit outputs of original and augmented embeddings to improve robustness against corrupted features; RVFL-Align computes cosine-similarity consistency matrices between the active client's embeddings and each passive client's embeddings, then solves a linear assignment problem (Hungarian algorithm) to re-index passive samples to maximize embedding consistency, thereby correcting misaligned features during each forward pass. The benchmark further standardizes comparisons across efficiency methods (FedBCD, C-VFL, EFVFL), robustness baselines (LEEF-VFL, LASER-VFL), and security attacks/defenses (PMC, AMC, GRNA, MIA, TECB, LFBA with Noisy Gradients, Gradient Compression, PPDL, and DiscreteSGD defenses).

## Results

On efficiency, EFVFL achieves the best MP on UCI-HAR (95.21%) and MIMIC-III (61.76%) while reducing communication costs by ~77% (26.49 MB vs. 114.52 MB for Base); FedBCD improves MP on KU-HAR to 86.07% (vs. 82.55% Base) with 8.6% lower costs. On robustness, LASER-VFL recovers MP to 65.03% on KU-HAR under 50% test missing features (vs. 38.12% Base), and the proposed RVFL-Aug improves MP by ~2–8 points over Base under 50–80% corruption (e.g., 42.59% vs. 34.86% on MM-IMDB at r_b=0.5). On security, backdoor attacks (TECB, LFBA) achieve 99–100% attack success rate while degrading MP by only 2–5 points, and gradient-based defenses reduce AP but simultaneously degrade MP, indicating no effective defense currently exists.

## Baselines & Comparisons

For efficiency, C-VFL and EFVFL achieve the lowest communication costs (25–27 MB on UCI-HAR vs. 115 MB for Base, a ~77% reduction) but sacrifice 0.5–3.5% MP on emotion/multimedia datasets (e.g., MUSTARD: 54.34–56.52% vs. 57.83% Base); FedBCD reduces costs by ~31% while matching or exceeding Base MP on HAR and robotics tasks (KU-HAR: 86.07% vs. 82.55%). For missing-feature robustness, LASER-VFL consistently outperforms both Base and LEEF-VFL, with the largest gap at high test missing rates (KU-HAR r_b=0.5: 65.03% vs. 38.12% Base, a 27-point improvement). The proposed RVFL-Aug outperforms Base under all corruption rates (e.g., MM-IMDB r_b=0.5: 42.59% vs. 34.86%, +7.7 points), and RVFL-Align recovers most of the MP loss under misalignment (UCI-HAR r_b=1: 91.01% vs. 89.01% Base). For security, all evaluated attacks succeed: backdoor attacks TECB and LFBA achieve 99–100% AP with minimal MP degradation (≤5 points), feature inference MIA reaches 74–100% AP, and label inference PMC/AMC reach 60–64% AP; none of the four gradient-based defenses (NG, GC, PPDL, DSGD) effectively neutralize attacks without substantially degrading MP.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the foundational VFL framework of Hardy et al. [18], which established the active/passive client architecture with embedding exchange and gradient backpropagation. It departs from prior VFL benchmarks (VFLAIR, VertiBench, FedAds) that rely on artificial feature splitting or synthetic data by instead sourcing 12 datasets from five real-world application domains where feature-level collaboration naturally mirrors the VFL setting. For robustness, it extends the missing-feature evaluation line (LEEF-VFL, LASER-VFL) by introducing the first unified treatment of corrupted and misaligned features via RVFL-Aug (Jensen-Shannon consistency with augmentations) and RVFL-Align (Hungarian-algorithm re-matching), while for efficiency and security it benchmarks against established methods (FedBCD, C-VFL, EFVFL) and known attack/defense suites.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MARS-VFL provides the first unified, realistic evaluation protocol spanning efficiency, robustness, and security for VFL, filling a critical gap where prior benchmarks used artificial data splits and evaluated only a single dimension. The security evaluation reveals that all four tested gradient-based defenses (Noisy Gradients, Gradient Compression, PPDL, DiscreteSGD) fail to effectively mitigate label inference, feature inference, or backdoor attacks without substantially degrading main task performance, highlighting an open problem in VFL security. The efficiency results expose non-trivial trade-offs between communication cost, convergence speed, and accuracy that practitioners need to navigate for real-world deployment. Open questions include whether the robustness and security findings generalize to larger client counts, more heterogeneous architectures, and whether fundamentally new defense mechanisms (beyond gradient perturbation) are needed.
