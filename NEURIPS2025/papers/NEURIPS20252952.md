## Problem

Existing multi-view congestion prediction methods tend to favor one data type (netlist or layout) while treating the other as supplementary, resulting in insufficient fusion of topological and geometric circuit information. Additionally, deep learning approaches in this domain operate as black boxes, lacking the interpretability needed for designers to identify which local circuit structures actually drive congestion and guide targeted optimization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252952/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes two complementary views of the same circuit: a cell-based netlist hypergraph (cells as nodes, nets as hyperedges with circuit-derived features) and a grid-based layout representation (M×N×K feature maps encoding cell/net density per grid cell). It simultaneously produces a grid congestion map (M×N scalar values), a cell congestion graph (one scalar per cell), and bottleneck subgraphs that highlight critical congestion-correlated regions. A key structural constraint is that hyperedges in both hypergraphs are built from identical net connections, which serves as the bridging mechanism for multi-view fusion.

## Method

MIHC constructs symmetrical cell-based and grid-based hypergraphs where net connections define hyperedges in both views, then processes them through a Multi-View HGNN (MV-HGNN) with a two-step message passing scheme: node-to-hyperedge aggregation within each view, followed by a net-based hyperedge embedding that concatenates both views' intermediate representations, and finally hyperedge-to-node updates that incorporate both view-specific and net-based information. A novel Multi-View Information Bottleneck (MVIB) module uses a single extractor to learn node selection probabilities for bottleneck subgraphs in the cell view, which are mapped to the grid view via spatial adjacency; the IB loss is derived as a symmetrized KL divergence between the two views' subgraph extractors minus a mutual information term. Extracted bottleneck subgraphs dynamically reweight node features through a residual sigmoid mechanism, and a contrastive loss aligns the resulting subgraph embeddings across views. The total training objective combines supervised MSE loss, the MVIB loss, and the contrastive loss.

## Results

On ISPD2015-F (the most challenging setting with extremely large circuits), MIHC achieves NMAE of 0.035 (cell-based) and 0.032 (grid-based), reducing NMAE by 16.67% over DE-HNN and 8.57% over Lay-Net respectively. On CircuitNet-N28 (>10,000 samples), MIHC achieves NMAE of 0.036 (cell-based) and 0.040 (grid-based), outperforming DE-HNN by 5.26% and Lay-Net by 2.44%. MIHC also achieves the highest Pearson correlation across all three datasets in both views (e.g., 0.687 cell-based and 0.675 grid-based on ISPD2015-B).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MIHC outperforms all 11 baselines across every dataset and both prediction views. In cell-based prediction, MIHC beats the strongest netlist-centric baseline DE-HNN by 9.37% NMAE reduction on ISPD2015-B (0.029 vs 0.032) and 16.67% on ISPD2015-F (0.035 vs 0.042), with the gap widening on larger, imbalanced circuits. In grid-based prediction, MIHC surpasses the strongest layout-centric baseline Lay-Net by 3.23% NMAE reduction on ISPD2015-B (0.030 vs 0.031) and 8.57% on ISPD2015-F (0.032 vs 0.035). On the large-scale CircuitNet-N28 dataset (>10,000 samples), MIHC maintains its lead over DE-HNN (5.26% NMAE reduction) and Lay-Net (2.44% NMAE reduction), confirming generalizability. Classical GNN baselines (GCN, GAT, HGNN) and single-view methods (RouteNet, CircuitFormer) are consistently outperformed by 10–30% in NMAE, highlighting the benefit of balanced multi-view hypergraph fusion. In design-level cross-design generalization (Table 2), MIHC retains its advantage over DE-HNN and Lay-Net even when training and test sets contain entirely different circuit designs, with 7.55% cell-based NMAE reduction in the B→A direction on ISPD2015-B.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the multi-view congestion prediction line (CircuitGNN, LHNN, Lay-Net) by constructing symmetric cell-based and grid-based hypergraphs linked through shared net connections, rather than the asymmetric 'primary view + supplementary view' design used by prior methods. It also repurposes the Information Bottleneck framework from graph anomaly detection (SIGNET) and post-hoc explainers (GNNExplainer) into an inherent, training-time interpretability mechanism for congestion prediction—claimed as the first such application in the EDA domain. The net-based bridging strategy, where identical net connections define hyperedges in both hypergraph views, is the key architectural departure from prior multi-view fusion approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By providing the first interpretable congestion prediction model, MIHC addresses a practical gap in EDA where black-box predictions hinder designer trust and targeted optimization. The design-level generalization experiments (completely unseen circuit designs) strengthen the claim of genuine generalization over memorization, which is critical for real-world deployment. However, the IB mechanism adds computational overhead, performance on imbalanced datasets (ISPD2015-F) still lags behind balanced settings, and the interpretability evaluation relies partly on a proxy task (MNIST anomaly detection) due to the absence of public EDA interpretability benchmarks—leaving open questions about whether the bottleneck subgraphs align with designer intuition at scale.
