## Problem

Existing vehicle trajectory prediction methods focus on temporal pattern extraction and vehicle-environment interaction modeling but overlook the originator of trajectories — human drivers whose diverse driving styles produce heterogeneous trajectory patterns. This omission limits prediction reliability in complex real-world scenarios where driver behavior is a probabilistic mixture of conservative, aggressive, and normal styles.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252678/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a historical trajectory window (2 s for nuScenes/Argoverse, 1 s for Waymo) where each timestep is a 5-dimensional vector encoding (x, y) position, velocity, acceleration, and nearest lane segment ID. It outputs k future trajectories (k = 1, 5, or 10 for nuScenes/Argoverse; a single best trajectory scored by minADE/minFDE/MR for Waymo) over a prediction horizon of 3–8 seconds depending on the dataset.

## Method

DSA extends Kolmogorov-Arnold Networks by replacing the fixed spline basis with three style-specific polynomial families grounded in approximation theory: Bernstein polynomials B_n (uniform convergence, matching conservative drivers' smooth, low-variability trajectories), Chebyshev polynomials T_n^c (minimax error minimization, matching aggressive drivers' abrupt, non-smooth motions), and Legendre polynomials L_n (L²-optimal approximation, matching normal drivers' moderate fluctuations). A MoE-TopK gating network dynamically weights the three polynomial experts to model the probabilistic mixture of driving styles present in any given trajectory. The polynomial degree n is adaptively tuned per style via SMAC3 Bayesian hyperparameter optimization, targeting the degree that minimizes validation loss. The entire matching is justified by the Weierstrass Approximation Theorem (polynomials are dense in L^p), the Chebyshev Minimax Theorem, and the Least Squares Characterization Theorem, providing a theoretical explanation for why each polynomial family suits its corresponding driving style.

## Results

On nuScenes (2s→6s), DSA achieves the best ADE₁ (2.69), FDE₅ (2.74), ADE₁₀ (0.85), and FDE₁₀ (2.00), with a 5.52% FDE₅ improvement over the prior best. On Argoverse (2s→3s), DSA leads in ADE₁ (1.17), ADE₆ (0.62, an 8.82% gain), and FDE₆ (0.95). On Waymo (1s→8s), DSA attains the lowest minFDE (1.1431, 1.93% better) and MR (0.1259, 5.14% lower) among all baselines. Overall, DSA ranks first in 9 of 13 metrics and second in 3 others across the three datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On nuScenes, DSA outperforms DAMM [83] by 16.67% in ADE₁₀ (0.85 vs 1.02) and FRM [80] by 3.41% in ADE₁₀ (0.85 vs 0.88), while trailing CASPNet++ [84] by a marginal 0.05 in ADE₅ (1.21 vs 1.16) and 0.29 in FDE₁ (6.47 vs 6.18). On Argoverse, DSA surpasses ProphNet [85] by 8.82% in ADE₆ (0.62 vs 0.68) and 2.06% in FDE₆ (0.95 vs 0.97), though it lags by 0.08 in FDE₁ (2.85 vs 2.77). On Waymo, DSA reduces minFDE by 1.88% over MotionLM [94] (1.1431 vs 1.1653) and by 4.10% over ControlMTR [97] (1.1431 vs 1.1916), while MotionLM retains a 0.015 edge in minADE (0.5702 vs 0.5852). The paper notes that CASPNet++ predicts all road users jointly whereas DSA focuses on vehicles only, explaining the small nuScenes gap. Overall, DSA achieves the best result in 9 of 13 metrics across the three benchmarks, with the remaining 3 being second-best by narrow margins.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Kolmogorov-Arnold Networks (KANs) to vehicle trajectory prediction by replacing their default spline basis functions with task-specific polynomials (Bernstein, Chebyshev, Legendre) matched to three driving styles. It also incorporates a Mixture-of-Experts (MoE-TopK) gating mechanism to dynamically weight these style-specific experts. Conceptually, the paper departs from the dominant VTP paradigm of scene/interaction modeling by grounding trajectory heterogeneity in the human driver's behavioral style, and provides a mathematical justification (via Weierstrass, Bernstein uniform convergence, Chebyshev minimax, and Legendre least-squares properties) for why each polynomial family suits a particular driving style.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The driving-style-adaptive framing offers a degree of interpretability absent in most black-box trajectory predictors: the expert weights and polynomial selection provide a post-hoc explanation for predicted behavior. However, the approach is limited to three coarse style categories, a 9-second prediction horizon, and does not incorporate external context (weather, traffic signals), which constrains real-world generalizability. Open questions include whether finer-grained or continuous style representations would improve accuracy, whether the polynomial-to-style matching generalizes to other prediction domains, and whether the SMAC3 degree optimization scales to longer horizons or more agents.
