## Problem

Greedy k-means++ consistently outperforms standard k-means++ in practice, yet worst-case theory shows the opposite: greedy k-means++ is Ω(ℓ log k)-approximate while k-means++ is O(log k)-approximate. No prior theoretical analysis explains this theory-practice gap. This paper provides the first beyond-worst-case analysis demonstrating that greedy k-means++ is theoretically superior to k-means++ on a natural class of well-separated, exponentially distributed instances.

## Input / Output

The input is a point set X ⊆ R^m satisfying three structural properties (EWW): each cluster follows an exponentially decaying distribution around its center, inter-cluster distances scale as k^θ for θ ∈ (0,1/2], and clusters have comparable sizes within a constant factor. The algorithm takes k (number of clusters) and ℓ = ln k (candidates sampled per iteration) as parameters, and outputs a set S of k initial centers intended as seeds for Lloyd's heuristic. The analysis extends to sub-exponential-tailed distributions (Gaussian, sub-Gaussian) with a mild non-concentration assumption.

## Method

The core technique is a covering-based analysis using concentration balls of radius δ = 4b(1+θ) ln k around each cluster center. Under the concentration assumption (sampled points lie within these balls with probability ≥ 1 − 1/k), the greedy algorithm's preference for uncovered clusters follows from the large cost gap: an uncovered cluster contributes Ω(k^{2θ}·|C_i|) while a covered one contributes only O((ln k)²·|C_i|). Since greedy samples ℓ = ln k candidates per iteration, the probability of missing all uncovered clusters drops to at most 1/k^{2θ+2} per iteration, yielding O((ln ln k)²) approximation via a Chernoff-style concentration argument over the covered clusters' costs. In contrast, k-means++ (sampling only one candidate) leaves Ω(ln k · k^{1−2θ}) clusters uncovered in expectation, giving an Ω(ln k) lower bound. For θ > 1/2 where the approximation gap narrows, the paper instead shows greedy has strictly higher probability of covering all clusters.

## Results

The paper establishes that greedy k-means++ with ℓ = ln k + Θ(1) achieves an O((ln ln k)²) expected approximation ratio on EWW (exponentially-distributed, well-separable, well-spread) point sets, while standard k-means++ suffers an Ω(ln k) lower bound on the same instances (Theorems 1 and 2). For the regime θ > 1/2 (very well-separated clusters), the paper shows greedy k-means++ has a strictly higher probability of covering all optimal clusters than k-means++ (Theorem 3). Experiments on synthetic datasets (k = 16, exponential/half-normal/Lomax distributions, 100 repetitions) confirm that greedy k-means++ outperforms k-means++ in both decreasing the k-means objective and covering new clusters over iterations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's sole baseline is standard k-means++ (ℓ = 1). On EWW point sets with θ ∈ (0, 1/2], greedy k-means++ achieves an O((ln ln k)²) expected approximation ratio while k-means++ is proven to have an Ω(ln k) lower bound — a gap of Ω(ln k / (ln ln k)²) in favor of the greedy variant. For θ > 1/2, where clusters are very well-separated and the approximation gap narrows, greedy k-means++ still has a strictly higher probability of covering all optimal clusters (Theorem 3), though the paper notes this is a weaker form of superiority. Experiments with k = 16 on three synthetic distributions (exponential, half-normal, Lomax) confirm that greedy k-means++ consistently achieves lower average k-means objective and higher average coverage probability than k-means++ across all iterations, validating the theoretical predictions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of beyond-worst-case analysis for k-means seeding, building on well-separable instance analyses by Jaiswal and Garg, Ackermann and Blömer, and Braverman et al., but introduces a more structured class (EWW point sets) with exponentially decaying cluster distributions, well-spreadness, and controlled inter-cluster distances. It directly addresses the negative worst-case results of Bhattacharya et al. (2020) and Grunau et al. (2023), which showed greedy k-means++ is strictly worse than k-means++ in the worst case, by demonstrating that on natural, k-means-friendly instances the greedy variant actually achieves a better approximation ratio. The analysis also connects to the broader D^α-sampling literature (Balcan et al., Bamas et al.) and the practical parameter choice ℓ = ⌈ln k⌉ + 2 used in Scikit-learn.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first theoretical justification for why greedy k-means++—the variant actually implemented in major libraries like Scikit-learn—outperforms standard k-means++ in practice, resolving a notable theory-practice gap. The O((ln ln k)²) vs. Ω(ln k) separation on EWW instances provides a clean, asymptotic explanation for the empirical superiority. However, the analysis is restricted to a specific instance class (exponentially/sub-exponentially distributed, well-separable, well-spread clusters) and the parameter regime ℓ = Θ(ln k); open questions include whether the advantage persists for ℓ = o(ln k), whether tighter bounds than O((ln ln k)²) are achievable, and whether new algorithms can be designed leveraging this beyond-worst-case understanding.
