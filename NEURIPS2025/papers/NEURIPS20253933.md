## Problem

Subgraph federated learning (SFL) methods for node classification on partitioned graphs face a fundamental trade-off: existing approaches either exchange sensitive node embeddings or structural features (posing privacy risks) or rely on computationally intensive global matrix operations that hinder scalability. While FEDSTRUCT reduces information sharing, it still leaks partial adjacency and structure-feature gradients and lacks any formal privacy guarantee. The paper asks whether one can achieve competitive node-classification accuracy in SFL with strong, provable privacy and low communication overhead.

## Input / Output

Each client holds a local subgraph with node feature vectors (d-dimensional), intra-client edges, and knowledge of inter-client connection identifiers (but not the features or internal edges of other clients). The offline phase consumes the global adjacency structure (via local blocks A_{V_i,:} and A_{:,V_i}) to produce a truncated set of r Laplacian eigenvectors per node; the online phase then uses only local features, local edges, and the precomputed spectral rows U_{v,:} to predict node class labels. The truncation rank r (typically 50–200) is the key hyperparameter controlling the privacy–utility trade-off, and the output is a softmax class probability per node under a semi-supervised setting with 10% labeled nodes.

## Method

FEDLAP introduces a Laplacian-smoothing regularizer (Rayleigh quotient form) into the federated loss, which can be evaluated in a fully decentralized manner since each client only needs the structural features of its own nodes and immediate external neighbors. FEDLAP+ further decomposes the structural matrix S into a fixed spectral factor U (truncated to the r smallest Laplacian eigenvectors) and a small learnable matrix W ∈ R^{r×d_s}, exploiting the fact that the Laplacian regularizer acts as a low-pass filter so high-frequency components can be discarded. To compute the truncated eigendecomposition without exposing the global graph, the paper proposes a decentralized Arnoldi iteration in which each client computes local matrix-vector products and exchanges only additively homomorphically encrypted partial sums through the server, yielding O(nr²) complexity. The framework is split into a one-time offline spectral phase (no training, no feature/label sharing) and an online phase that reduces to standard FedAvg, and a formal privacy analysis via log-likelihood-ratio testing under a worst-case two-client attacker model proves that for sufficiently small r the attacker's precision + recall ≤ 1, meaning no meaningful edge information is leaked.

## Results

On 10-client random partitioning with 10% training nodes, FEDLAP+ (Arnoldi) achieves 79.31% on Cora, 85.29% on PubMed, 54.34% on Chameleon, 92.14% on Amazon Photo, and 66.22% on OGBN-Arxiv, remaining within 1–2% of the centralized GNN upper bound on most datasets. FEDLAP (non-spectral variant) reaches 86.43% on PubMed and 80.55% on Cora, slightly exceeding FEDGCN-2HOP (86.13% and 82.22%) on homophilic graphs, but drops to 32.98% on the heterophilic Chameleon dataset where FEDLAP+ recovers to 54.34%. Across all six datasets, both variants substantially outperform general FL baselines (FEDSGD, FEDSAGE+, FEDPUB) by 10–25 percentage points on most benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against FEDSAGE+ [5] and FEDPUB [11], both FEDLAP variants achieve large margins: on Cora (10 clients), FEDLAP scores 80.55% vs. 64.53% (FEDSAGE+) and 59.17% (FEDPUB), and on OGBN-Arxiv, FEDLAP+ reaches 66.22% vs. 55.50% (FEDPUB). Against the structure-aware FEDGCN-2HOP [10], FEDLAP is competitive or slightly ahead on homophilic graphs (PubMed: 86.43% vs. 86.13%; Cora: 80.55% vs. 82.22%) while FEDLAP+ narrows the gap on OGBN-Arxiv (66.22% vs. 66.93%); however, FEDGCN shares 2-hop aggregated node features, which the authors note constitutes a privacy breach. Against FEDSTRUCT [13], the closest competitor, FEDLAP leads on PubMed (86.43% vs. 85.40%) and Amazon Photo (92.08% vs. 92.00%), while FEDSTRUCT retains a small edge on the heterophilic Chameleon dataset (55.81% vs. 32.98% for FEDLAP, though FEDLAP+ recovers to 54.34%). Overall, FEDLAP+ is the most robust variant across both homophilic and heterophilic graphs, and is the first SFL method to provide a formal privacy guarantee (P+R ≤ 1 for the edge-membership attack under worst-case assumptions).

## Relation to Prior Work

FEDLAP is a direct successor to FEDSTRUCT (same first author, ICLR 2025), which first introduced explicit global structural information into subgraph federated learning. It departs from FEDSTRUCT by replacing the costly global adjacency matrix and iteratively shared structure features with a Laplacian Rayleigh-quotient regularizer, and in FEDLAP+ further moves into the spectral domain with truncated eigenvectors computed via a novel decentralized Arnoldi iteration. The approach also draws on the well-known connection between GCN message passing and spectral Laplacian smoothing (Kipf & Welling), repurposing it as a privacy-preserving regularizer rather than a message-passing mechanism, and on classical numerical linear algebra (Arnoldi/Krylov methods) adapted to a federated, homomorphically-encrypted setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FEDLAP is the first subgraph FL framework with a formal, worst-case privacy guarantee (Theorem 1 / Corollary 1), which no prior SFL method—FEDSTRUCT, FEDGCN, FEDSAGE+—can claim, making it a meaningful step toward deployable federated graph learning in regulated domains (banking, AML). The spectral-truncation-as-privacy-mechanism insight is elegant and general, but the base FEDLAP variant underperforms notably on heterophilic graphs (Chameleon: ~33% vs. ~56% for FEDSTRUCT), and the online phase inherits all standard FL vulnerabilities. Open questions include whether the privacy guarantees survive under more realistic (non-Bernoulli, community-structured) graph models, how the method scales to graphs with millions of nodes in practice, and whether the framework extends naturally to link prediction or graph-level tasks.
