## Problem

Processing long sequences of asynchronous, sparse event camera data remains challenging: aggregation-based methods (GNNs, Transformers, voxel-based SSMs) incur high computational cost and latency, while event-by-event SNNs suffer from limited long-context memory. Existing SSMs built on HiPPO (e.g., S4, Mamba) assume dense, regularly-sampled inputs and require initial encoding into dense representations, failing to exploit the discrete, asynchronous, and sparse nature of event data for efficient event-by-event processing with scalable long-context memory.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250641/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

FLAME consumes raw asynchronous event streams as tuples (x, y, t, p) — spatial coordinates, precise timestamp, and polarity — at resolutions ranging from 128×128 (DVS Gesture) to 1280×800 (CeleX-HAR), with sequences containing up to ~3.1 million events. All updates (feature extraction, spatial pooling, state transitions) are triggered per-event without batching, frame accumulation, or voxel-grid reconstruction, preserving exact temporal resolution. The output is a class label produced by a softmax readout over a temporally pooled state representation (average of the last K states).

## Method

FLAME combines a neuromorphic front-end with an event-adaptive state-space core. The Event Attention Layer uses multi-branch Leaky Integrate-and-Fire neurons, each branch having a distinct learnable time constant τ_d, to extract multi-timescale temporal features directly from raw events while preserving sparsity. The core EA-HiPPO Convolution Layer evolves a state vector x(t) ∈ ℝ^N via ẋ(t) = A_S(Δt)·x(t) + B·E_flat(t), where the time-varying matrix A_S(Δt) = A ∘ F(Δt) modulates memory retention based on the inter-event interval Δt (frequent events preserve memory, sparse events accelerate decay of specific components). A Normal-Plus-Low-Rank (NPLR) decomposition of the base HiPPO matrix A reduces per-event state-update complexity from O(N²) to O(Nr) with N=64 and r=8, and the matrix exponential is approximated via a second-order Taylor expansion. The architecture is trained end-to-end with supervised learning, and theoretical analysis provides bounds on memory capacity, numerical stability of the Taylor approximation, and global system boundedness.

## Results

FLAME-Normal achieves 72.2% top-1 accuracy on the high-resolution CeleX-HAR dataset (1280×800, 150 classes) at only 0.41 GFLOPs, matching EVMamba's 72.3% at ~90× lower computational cost (37.2 GFLOPs). On DVS Gesture (128×128), FLAME-Normal reaches 96.5% accuracy at 0.43 GFLOPs, and on HAR-DVS (346×260) it achieves 88.29% at 0.41 GFLOPs. Inference latency on CeleX-HAR ranges from 0.162 ms (FLAME-Tiny) to 1.867 ms (FLAME-Normal) on an NVIDIA A100 GPU, compared to 255.4 ms for TimeSformer.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On CeleX-HAR, FLAME-Normal (72.2%, 0.41 GFLOPs) essentially matches EVMamba (72.3%, 37.2 GFLOPs) in accuracy while operating at ~90× lower computational cost, and substantially outperforms VMamba-B (72.0%, 18.0G), VideoMamba-M (69.1%, 12.7G), and TSM (70.4%, 0.3G) in the accuracy-efficiency tradeoff. On DVS Gesture, FLAME-Normal (96.5%) trails EventMamba (99.2%) by 2.7% but at comparable FLOPs (0.43G vs. 0.219G). On HAR-DVS, FLAME-Normal (88.29%, 0.41G) is 1.8% below EXACT (90.10%, 1.3G) but at ~3× lower FLOPs, and significantly outperforms video-based baselines like C3D (50.52%), TSM (52.63%), and SlowFast (46.54%). On CIFAR10-DVS, FLAME-Normal (80.5%) is 4.6% below Spike-VGG11 (85.11%) but at ~20× lower FLOPs (0.43G vs. 8.9G). On N-Caltech101, FLAME-Normal (70.5%) lags EFV++ (89.7%) by a wide margin, indicating the method's advantage is most pronounced on high-resolution, sparse event streams rather than lower-resolution object recognition. Overall, FLAME's primary advantage is not raw accuracy but the accuracy-per-FLOP ratio, particularly at high spatial resolutions where competing SSM and video models incur prohibitive compute.

## Relation to Prior Work

FLAME sits at the intersection of two research threads: structured state-space models (HiPPO/S4/Mamba) that excel at long-sequence modeling but assume dense, regularly-sampled inputs, and spiking/neuromorphic architectures (LIF neurons, dendritic processing) that naturally handle asynchronous sparse events but lack robust long-context memory. The paper's core departure is making the SSM state transition matrix explicitly sensitive to inter-event intervals (EA-HiPPO), eliminating the need for the voxelization or frame-aggregation steps that prior SSM-for-events work (SSM-Event, EventMamba, PRE-Mamba) requires. The NPLR decomposition and Taylor-approximated matrix exponential are inherited from S4's efficiency toolkit, while the multi-branch LIF front-end draws on dendritic heterogeneity ideas (DH-LIF) but repurposes them as a feature extractor feeding an SSM rather than a standalone classifier.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~90× compute reduction on CeleX-HAR (0.41 vs. 37.2 GFLOPs) at matched accuracy is practically significant for edge deployment of high-resolution event cameras, where power and latency budgets are tight. However, the authors acknowledge that FLAME is not yet directly mappable to neuromorphic hardware (the EA-HiPPO core is a conventional SSM, not a spiking circuit), lacks on-device continual learning, and does not meet real-time constraints on CPU-only systems. An open question is whether the event-aware state dynamics can be reformulated for native execution on platforms like Loihi or TrueNorth, which would unlock the full ultra-low-power promise of neuromorphic computing for event-based vision.
