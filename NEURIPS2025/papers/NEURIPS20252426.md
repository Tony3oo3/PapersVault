## Problem

Existing interaction attribution methods for LLMs, such as SPEX, require tens of thousands of expensive model inferences to identify influential feature interactions, making them prohibitive for large models. The paper identifies that LLM feature interactions are empirically hierarchical—significant high-order interactions are accompanied by their lower-order subsets—and asks whether this structure can be exploited to reduce the inference budget while preserving explanation faithfulness.

## Input / Output

The method takes an LLM (e.g., DistilBERT, Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, CLIP-ViT-B/32) and a single input instance (a sentence of 64–512 tokens, an image with 48 patches plus caption words, or a set of 50,000 training images for data attribution). It generates random binary masks over the n features, queries the LLM on each masked input to obtain a scalar value (class logit, generation perplexity, contrastive loss, or classification accuracy), and outputs a sparse set of ~200 Fourier coefficients that encode the most influential feature interactions. These coefficients can be converted in closed form to Shapley values, Banzhaf values, Faith-Shapley, or any other interaction index.

## Method

PROXYSPEX exploits the observed hierarchical (staircase) structure of LLM interactions by fitting gradient boosted trees (GBTs) as a proxy model to the masked-input/value pairs, since GBTs are provably well-suited to learning sums of staircase functions and outperform random forests and neural networks on this task. The algorithm proceeds in four steps: (1) uniformly sample ℓ random masks and query the LLM to build a training set; (2) fit GBTs with 5-fold cross-validation over tree depth, number of trees, and learning rate; (3) extract the exact Fourier representation of the fitted GBTs in a single forward pass using the recursive tree-traversal algorithm of Gorji et al., then sparsify to the top-k coefficients; and (4) optionally refine the top-k coefficients via regression on the collected data. This reduces the number of LLM inferences by ~10× compared to SPEX while achieving equal or better faithfulness (R²), because the GBT implicitly encodes the hierarchical prior that SPEX must search for explicitly via structured sampling and error-correction decoding.

## Results

PROXYSPEX achieves ~20% higher faithfulness (R²) than marginal LASSO attribution across four high-dimensional datasets (IMDb sentiment, HotpotQA, DROP, MS-COCO) while requiring 10× fewer LLM inferences than SPEX. Wall-clock speedups of ~3× (DistilBERT) and ~5× (CLIP-ViT-B/32) are observed relative to SPEX at equivalent faithfulness. Only ~200 Fourier coefficients are needed to capture the essential interaction structure, and the Direct Subset Rate (DSR) of top-k interactions consistently exceeds 80%, confirming the hierarchical prior. In the attention-head pruning case study on Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct (MMLU), PROXYSPEX consistently outperforms both LASSO and Best-of-N baselines across all sparsity levels and layer groups.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LASSO (the primary marginal attribution baseline), PROXYSPEX achieves 15–25% higher faithfulness (R²) across all four datasets and identifies significantly more influential features in the removal-based evaluation (Figure 7); LASSO plateaus in faithfulness as inference budget grows while PROXYSPEX continues to improve, indicating it is capturing interactions that marginal methods cannot. Against SPEX (the prior interaction attribution method), PROXYSPEX requires approximately 10× fewer model inferences to reach equivalent faithfulness, translating to ~3× wall-clock speedup on DistilBERT and ~5× on CLIP-ViT-B/32; SPEX is only competitive with LASSO at large inference multipliers (α≥6), whereas PROXYSPEX outperforms LASSO even at α=2. Against KernelSHAP for Shapley value approximation, PROXYSPEX provides a better coarse approximation at low inference budgets (α=0.25–0.5) but is eventually surpassed by KernelSHAP at α=1.0, since PROXYSPEX is optimized for faithfulness rather than the Shapley kernel. In the attention-head pruning case study, PROXYSPEX consistently outperforms both LASSO and a Best-of-5000 random baseline across all three layer groups (initial, middle, final) and all sparsity levels, with the gap widening at higher sparsity.

## Relation to Prior Work

PROXYSPEX extends SPEX (Kang et al., ICML 2025), the first interaction attribution method scaling to n≈1000 features via sparse Fourier transforms, by replacing SPEX's algebraic structured sampling and error-correction decoding with random sampling plus a gradient boosted tree (GBT) proxy model. The key conceptual departure is the empirical observation that LLM feature interactions are hierarchical (higher-order interactions are accompanied by their lower-order subsets), a structure that GBTs naturally capture due to their greedy 'staircase' construction. The Fourier extraction step relies on Gorji et al. (NeurIPS 2025), who showed that the exact Fourier transform of a decision tree can be computed in a single forward pass, making the GBT-to-spectrum conversion computationally trivial.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 10× reduction in LLM inferences makes interaction attribution practically feasible for large models where SPEX's tens of thousands of queries are prohibitive, and the hierarchical structure observation provides a new empirical lens on how LLMs compose features. Applications to data attribution (identifying synergistic vs. redundant training samples on CIFAR-10) and mechanistic interpretability (quantifying within- vs. across-layer attention head interactions in Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct) demonstrate that efficient interaction discovery unlocks analyses previously intractable with marginal methods. Key limitations include GBTs' potential failure on non-hierarchical sparse interactions and the difficulty of human interpretation of high-order interaction terms; open questions include whether alternative proxy models or adaptive masking designs can further reduce inference costs and how PROXYSPEX integrates with internal model structure for deeper mechanistic understanding.
