## Problem

In POMDPs, realistic belief distributions become high-dimensional, multi-modal, and richly correlated, yet existing approximation methods fail to capture this complexity: parametric approaches (DRQN, DVRL) impose fixed representational capacity that collapses multi-modal uncertainty, particle filters (SIR, POMCPOW) suffer from resampling-induced mode collapse, and standard SVGD experiences kernel degeneracy in high dimensions while destroying inter-dimensional correlation structures. This creates a fundamental bottleneck where small belief approximation errors propagate and degrade decision quality over time.

## Input / Output

ESCORT consumes a set of n particles in continuous state space (10D–20D in experiments), the current action, a new observation, and known transition/observation models, and produces an updated particle set representing the posterior belief plus a selected action. The only learned component is a particle-based policy network that encodes each particle independently, aggregates via mean pooling (permutation-invariant), and outputs a discrete softmax or Gaussian action distribution. All belief updates are deterministic—no resampling—so the particle count remains fixed throughout the POMDP episode.

## Method

ESCORT extends SVGD's deterministic attractive-repulsive particle dynamics with two regularization mechanisms. First, a correlation-aware term (inspired by Generalized Sliced Wasserstein distance) uses m learned projection matrices—initialized from eigenvectors of the correlation-difference matrix between current and target distributions and optimized to maximize projected Wasserstein distance—to preserve inter-dimensional dependencies and mitigate kernel degeneracy in high dimensions. Second, a temporal consistency term computes a sliced 1-Wasserstein distance between consecutive belief distributions along optimized projection directions, penalizing unrealistic belief jumps while allowing natural evolution. A model-based update component shifts particles via the known transition model and applies likelihood-weighted displacement toward the observation-weighted mean, avoiding global resampling. The policy network is trained with policy gradient on the particle-encoded belief, and the full update combines all three components (regularized SVGD, model-based shift, temporal constraint) in a single deterministic step per timestep.

## Results

On the three POMDP benchmarks, ESCORT achieves the lowest position error across all environments: 0.347±0.03 on Light-Dark Navigation (10D), 9.063±0.51 on Kidnapped Robot (20D), and 3.665±0.31 on Multi-Target Tracking (20D). In distribution approximation experiments (1D–20D), ESCORT maintains perfect mode coverage (1.0) in all dimensions while SIR drops to 0.12 and DVRL to 0.58 in 20D. ESCORT also achieves the lowest correlation error at 20D (0.556±0.03) compared to SVGD (0.589±0.05), DVRL (0.708±0.03), and SIR (0.640±0.01).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ESCORT consistently outperforms all four baselines across every POMDP environment, with advantages growing as dimensionality increases. Against SVGD (the closest competitor), ESCORT improves position error by 8.5% on Light-Dark (10D), 16.9% on Kidnapped Robot (20D), and 24.7% on Target Tracking (20D), confirming that correlation-aware regularization becomes more critical in higher dimensions. Against DVRL, ESCORT achieves 77.7% lower error on Light-Dark and 36.7% lower on Kidnapped Robot, demonstrating that parametric VAE-based belief compression cannot match particle-based representation for multi-modal beliefs. Against POMCPOW, ESCORT reduces error by 83.6% on Light-Dark and 24.8% on Kidnapped Robot, highlighting the cost of stochastic resampling-induced mode collapse. In the synthetic distribution experiments (Table 2), ESCORT also outperforms SIR on Sliced Wasserstein distance at all dimensions ≥2D (e.g., 0.326 vs. 2.854 at 20D) and maintains perfect mode coverage where SIR collapses to 0.12 in 20D.

## Relation to Prior Work

ESCORT extends Stein Variational Gradient Descent (SVGD) by integrating projection principles from Generalized Sliced Wasserstein Distance (GSW) as regularization terms rather than distance metrics, addressing SVGD's kernel degeneracy in high-dimensional spaces. It departs from prior structured SVGD extensions (MP-SVGD, SVMP) that rely on fixed graphical model structures, instead learning adaptive projection matrices that respond to observation-dependent correlation changes in POMDPs. The method also contrasts with particle filter approaches (SIR, POMCPOW) that suffer from resampling-induced mode collapse, and parametric belief encoders (DVRL, DRQN) that sacrifice multi-modal expressiveness for fixed-dimensional representations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ESCORT addresses a fundamental bottleneck in practical POMDP deployment: accurately representing high-dimensional, multi-modal beliefs with complex inter-dimensional correlations without the degeneracy of resampling or the representational limits of parametric compression. However, the method's reliance on known transition and observation models restricts applicability to well-modeled domains, and the O(d^1.67) computational scaling (dominated by the GSWD term at high dimensions) limits real-time deployment. Open questions include whether adaptive projection techniques can reduce computational burden, whether the approach extends to model-free settings, and whether the temporal consistency constraint's domain-dependent effects (helpful in some environments, slightly harmful in others) can be better characterized.
