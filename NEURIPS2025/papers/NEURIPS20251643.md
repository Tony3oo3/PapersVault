## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting methods for novel view synthesis critically depend on camera poses from Structure-from-Motion pipelines, which are notoriously unreliable in textureless indoor environments and under abrupt camera motion. Existing pose-free variants (e.g., CF-3DGS, Nope-NeRF) assume photometric gradients from visual textures alone suffice for pose optimization, an assumption that breaks down precisely in the indoor scenes where SfM already fails, leaving a gap for a robust pose-free 3DGS framework that handles both textureless regions and sudden camera motion simultaneously.

## Input / Output

The method takes a sequence of N unposed RGB images with known camera intrinsics as input and produces per-frame camera poses in SE(3) along with a unified global 3DGS representation for real-time novel view synthesis. The input is processed in short overlapping video clips during the local stage before global fusion, and the optimized depth maps from the local stage serve as pseudo-ground-truth supervision for the global 3DGS depth alignment loss, creating a self-contained pipeline that requires no external SfM or depth sensors.

## Method

NopeRoomGS employs a local-to-global optimization paradigm. In the local stage, a lightweight neural geometric representation F_θ (initialized from a fast monocular depth estimator and shared across all frames in a short clip) jointly optimizes per-frame depth and camera pose, supervised by multi-frame tracking constraints from CoTracker and a scale-and-shift-invariant regularization loss from a monocular depth foundation model, yielding reliable local 3D Gaussians even in textureless regions. In the global stage, these local Gaussians are progressively fused into a unified 3DGS via an alternating optimization strategy that updates camera poses and Gaussian parameters in separate steps to mitigate gradient interference. The global objective combines a standard photometric loss (L1 + D-SSIM), a depth alignment loss using the local-stage depth maps as pseudo-ground-truth, and a novel piecewise planarity constraint that decomposes pose supervision into rotational (surface-normal alignment) and translational (plane-offset consistency) components, with an edge-based mask restricting the constraint to planar regions for robustness under abrupt camera motion.

## Results

On the Replica dataset, NopeRoomGS achieves a mean PSNR of 32.14 dB, SSIM of 0.90, and LPIPS of 0.09 for novel view synthesis, substantially outperforming the closest baseline CF-3DGS (25.40 dB PSNR, 0.77 SSIM, 0.39 LPIPS) by over 6 dB in PSNR. For camera pose estimation on Replica, the method achieves mean ATE of 0.009 m and RPE_r of 0.126°, compared to CF-3DGS (0.059 m ATE, 5.903° RPE_r) and Nope-NeRF (0.039 m ATE, 6.001° RPE_r). On ScanNet, NopeRoomGS attains 25.38 dB PSNR and 0.020 m ATE, and on Tanks & Temples it reaches 31.68 dB PSNR and 0.003 m ATE, consistently leading all baselines across all three datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NopeRoomGS outperforms all six baselines on every metric across all three datasets. Against the closest 3DGS-based competitor CF-3DGS [10], the gains are most dramatic on textureless indoor scenes: on Replica, PSNR improves from 25.40 to 32.14 dB (+6.7 dB) and ATE drops from 0.059 to 0.009 m (6.6× reduction); on ScanNet, PSNR rises from 23.26 to 25.38 dB and ATE halves from 0.040 to 0.020 m. Against the NeRF-based pose-free methods (Nope-NeRF, BARF, NeRFmm), NopeRoomGS achieves 4–8 dB higher PSNR while also delivering superior pose accuracy (e.g., RPE_r of 0.126° vs. 5.2–7.0° for Nope-NeRF/BARF/NeRFmm on Replica). On the texture-rich Tanks & Temples benchmark, where CF-3DGS already performs well (31.28 dB PSNR), NopeRoomGS still edges ahead (31.68 dB PSNR, 0.003 m ATE vs. 0.004 m), confirming generalizability beyond indoor settings. The feed-forward methods NoPoSplat and SelfSplat lag significantly (19–20 dB PSNR on Replica), indicating that per-scene optimization with geometric priors remains essential for high-fidelity reconstruction.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the pose-free 3DGS line initiated by CF-3DGS [10], which first removed COLMAP dependency by jointly optimizing poses and Gaussians under photometric supervision. It also builds on pose-free NeRF methods (BARF, Nope-NeRF, NeRFmm) that introduced progressive or constraint-based strategies for simultaneous pose and scene recovery. The key departure is a local-to-global paradigm: a lightweight local neural geometric representation (LNGR) supervised by CoTracker multi-frame tracking and monocular depth foundation model priors bootstraps reliable local 3D Gaussians, which are then fused globally via alternating optimization with a piecewise planarity constraint—addressing the specific failure mode of photometric-only methods in textureless indoor scenes with abrupt camera motion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper closes a practical gap for indoor 3D reconstruction in AR/VR and robotics settings where SfM pipelines routinely fail on blank walls and rapid handheld motion. The alternating optimization strategy and piecewise planarity constraint offer reusable design patterns for decoupling pose and geometry optimization in other differentiable rendering pipelines. However, the local stage adds computational overhead over vanilla 3DGS, and the method still fails under extremely rapid motion or very sparse inputs—open questions include whether the local-to-global scheme scales to large-scale outdoor scenes or can be made more efficient.
