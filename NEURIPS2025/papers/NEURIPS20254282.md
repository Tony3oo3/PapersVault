## Problem

Rectified Flows aim to learn straight ODE vector fields enabling near one-step inference, but it remained theoretically unclear whether the straight-path objective could lead to memorization of arbitrary training pairings rather than generalizable transport. This paper identifies and formalizes a fundamental failure mode: under deterministic training with noiseless interpolants, low gradient variance drives the optimizer toward memorizing the specific source-target pairings used during training, even when interpolant lines intersect, undermining the generalization that motivates learning a vector field in the first place.

## Input / Output

The method takes a source sample x_0 drawn from a standard Gaussian π_0 and a time parameter t ∈ [0,1], and produces a learned vector field v(x_t, t) that, when integrated via ODE, yields a target sample x_1 from the target distribution π_1. The target distributions range from analytic Gaussians and Gaussian mixtures (dimensions 3 and 50) to real images (CelebA at 64×64, CIFAR-10 at 32×32). Beyond the generated sample, the framework outputs a gradient variance diagnostic computed across the training batch, which serves as a signal for whether the learned field is memorizing training pairings or generalizing toward the true optimal transport map.

## Method

The paper derives closed-form expressions for the gradient variance of the CFM loss under Gaussian-to-Gaussian transport across three pairing schemes (optimal transport, rotated OT, and random) and two interpolant types (noiseless and stochastic), showing that variance is governed by mismatch between the vector field and pairing structure rather than by geometric intersections of trajectories. The central theoretical result (Proposition 2) proves that for any finite deterministic training dataset, a vector field exists that achieves zero empirical loss by memorizing the training pairings, and that deterministic numerical integration at inference reproduces these exact pairings by effectively bypassing intersection points where the vector field is unconstrained. A complementary result (Lemma 2) establishes that Rectified Flow iterations stagnate once a straight coupling is reached, so repeated rectification cannot escape the memorized solution. Empirically, the authors validate these findings on CelebA (using adversarial shuffled pairings and simulated 1-ReFlow) and Gaussian mixtures, demonstrating that adding a small amount of noise (σ=0.05) to the interpolant breaks the injectivity between (x_t, t) and (x_0, x_1), disables the memorization pathway, and restores generalization toward the true OT map.

## Results

On CelebA with adversarial (shuffled) OT pairings, CFM with deterministic interpolants (σ=0) strongly memorizes training targets, achieving L2 distance 28.57±5.49 to shuffled targets at 5K samples, while CFM with stochastic interpolants (σ=0.05) resists memorization (55.02±16.51) and generalizes better toward true OT (34.25±7.54 vs. 50.40±16.73 L2 to OT). On Gaussian mixtures in 3D, CFM(σ=0) achieves a memorization MMD of 1.758×10⁻⁶ (≈18× lower than CFM(σ=0.05)'s 3.105×10⁻⁵) but generates samples with higher MMD to the true distribution (0.0034 vs. 0.0018). The simulated 1-ReFlow experiment confirms that deterministic interpolants reinforce memorization across iterations (L2 to generated: 8.63±1.76 vs. 25.08±8.59 at 5K), while noise restores generalization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical analysis paper rather than a method-proposal paper, so the central comparison is between CFM with deterministic interpolants (σ=0) and CFM with stochastic interpolants (σ=0.05), both using identical U-Net architectures. CFM(σ=0) consistently 'wins' on memorization metrics (lower L2 to training/shuffled targets) but loses on generalization (higher L2 to true OT) across all dataset sizes (5K and 50K) and both adversarial and simulated 1-ReFlow settings. On Gaussian mixtures, CFM(σ=0) achieves roughly 10× lower memorization MMD/Sinkhorn distances but worse generated-sample quality (higher MMD to the true distribution). The paper's key finding is that this memorization is not an artifact of model capacity or dataset size but is a structural consequence of the deterministic straight-path objective (Proposition 2), and that even a small amount of noise (σ=0.05) is sufficient to break the memorization pathway and restore generalization toward the true OT map.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of flow-based generative modeling (CFM, Rectified Flow), optimal transport theory, and memorization studies in diffusion models. While prior memorization work (Bamberger 2025, Somepalli 2023, Ren 2024) asked whether models reproduce training images from arbitrary inputs, this paper reframes the question for Rectified flows where deterministic pairings are inherent to the training procedure. The gradient-variance-as-diagnostic perspective is new; the authors challenge the common belief (Gagneux 2025, Fjelde 2024) that variance stems from interpolant intersections, showing instead that it reflects mismatch between the learned field and the pairing structure. The paper also builds on theoretical results that 1–2 rectifications may straighten couplings (Lee 2024, Bansal 2024) by providing counterexamples where this fails under mild assumptions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work reveals a fundamental limitation of rectified flows: deterministic interpolants create a memorization pathway that undermines generalization, explaining why repeated rectifications may not improve transport structure. The practical implication—that adding minimal noise (σ=0.05) to interpolants breaks this pathway—issimple but important for practitioners using rectified flow or CFM. Open questions include whether these results extend to higher-resolution image data and video modalities, whether there are principled ways to choose noise levels beyond heuristics, and how this memorization dynamic interacts with Schrödinger bridge matching's bidirectional training.
