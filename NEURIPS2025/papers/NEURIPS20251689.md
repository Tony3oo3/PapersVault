## Problem

The dominant paradigm for large language models is autoregressive next-token prediction, and it is widely assumed that core LLM capabilities—scalability, in-context learning, and instruction-following—inherently depend on this left-to-right formulation. This paper challenges that assumption by asking whether the underlying generative modeling principle (maximum likelihood estimation) rather than the autoregressive factorization itself is what drives these capabilities, and whether a non-autoregressive generative model can match ARMs at scale (8B parameters, 10^23 FLOPs).

## Input / Output

During pre-training, the model receives a sequence where each token is independently replaced by a mask token with probability t drawn uniformly from [0,1], and predicts all masked tokens simultaneously using a non-causal Transformer (no causal mask, no KV caching). For conditional generation, the input is a fixed prompt concatenated with a fully masked response of user-specified length; the model iteratively unmaskes tokens over N diffusion steps, discarding output after the |EOS| token. The generation length is a hyperparameter, and the model also supports autoregressive and block-diffusion sampling modes without retraining. For likelihood evaluation, the model produces a conditional log-likelihood score via a low-variance Monte Carlo estimator (Eq. 6) that samples random subsets of tokens to mask.

## Method

LLaDA is a masked diffusion language model that defines a forward process independently masking each token with probability t (t~U[0,1]) and a reverse process that iteratively predicts all masked tokens from t=1 to t=0. The mask predictor is a standard Transformer without causal masking, trained with a cross-entropy loss on masked tokens weighted by 1/t, which is proven to be an upper bound on the negative log-likelihood—providing a principled MLE objective absent in prior heuristic approaches like MaskGIT. Unlike BERT's fixed masking ratio, the random t ensures the model learns conditional distributions at all noise levels, enabling in-context learning and instruction-following as emergent properties. SFT masks only response tokens while keeping the prompt intact, making it fully compatible with pre-training. At inference, a low-confidence remasking strategy (inspired by MaskGIT) selects which predicted tokens to re-mask at each step, and the model is scaled to 8B parameters trained on 2.3T tokens with 0.13M H800 GPU hours, matching ARM baselines on six diverse tasks while uniquely resolving the reversal curse.

## Results

LLaDA 8B Base (trained on 2.3T tokens) achieves 65.9 on MMLU (5-shot), 70.3 on GSM8K (4-shot), 31.4 on Math (4-shot), and 35.4 on HumanEval (0-shot), surpassing LLaMA2 7B Base on nearly all 15 tasks and performing on par with LLaMA3 8B Base (65.4 MMLU, 48.7 GSM8K). On Chinese benchmarks, LLaDA shows a large margin: 69.9 on CMMLU and 70.5 on C-Eval versus 50.7 and 51.7 for LLaMA3 8B. After SFT (4.5M pairs), LLaDA 8B Instruct reaches 65.5 MMLU, 69.4 GSM8K, and 49.4 HumanEval, remaining competitive with LLaMA3 8B Instruct despite using only SFT (no RL alignment). On the reversal poem completion task, LLaDA achieves 45.6 reversal accuracy versus 34.3 for GPT-4o and 38.0 for Qwen2.5-7B, effectively breaking the reversal curse.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LLaMA3 8B Base (the strongest same-scale baseline), LLaDA 8B Base wins on MMLU (65.9 vs 65.4), GSM8K (70.3 vs 48.7), Math (31.4 vs 16.0), HumanEval (35.4 vs 34.8), HumanEval-FIM (73.8 vs 73.3), TruthfulQA (46.1 vs 44.0), CMMLU (69.9 vs 50.7), and C-Eval (70.5 vs 51.7), but loses on BBH (49.7 vs 62.1), ARC-C (45.9 vs 53.1), Hellaswag (70.5 vs 79.1), WinoGrande (74.8 vs 77.3), and PIQA (73.6 vs 80.6). Against LLaMA2 7B Base, LLaDA wins on nearly all 15 tasks with large margins (e.g., GSM8K 70.3 vs 13.1, CMMLU 69.9 vs 32.5). Against Qwen2.5 7B (trained on 18T tokens, ~8× more data), LLaDA trails on most general and math tasks but remains competitive on code (HumanEval 35.4 vs 57.9) and dominates on Chinese (CMMLU 69.9 vs 83.9 is actually a loss; C-Eval 70.5 vs 83.2 is also a loss). On the reversal poem task, LLaDA 8B Instruct (45.6 reversal) outperforms both GPT-4o (34.3) and Qwen2.5-7B (38.0) by 11+ points, while trailing on forward generation (51.8 vs 82.7/75.9), confirming the bidirectional advantage of the diffusion formulation. After SFT, LLaDA 8B Instruct slightly trails LLaMA3 8B Instruct on MMLU (65.5 vs 68.4) and GSM8K (69.4 vs 78.3), but the gap narrows on Math (31.9 vs 29.6) and GPQA (33.3 vs 31.9), and LLaDA uses only SFT without RL alignment.

## Relation to Prior Work

LLaDA extends the masked diffusion model (MDM) line of work from Austin et al. [16], Lou et al. [17], and the theoretical foundations of Shi et al. [18], Sahoo et al. [19], and Ou et al. [20] (which established the cross-entropy loss as a principled upper bound on negative log-likelihood) to an unprecedented 8B-parameter scale. It departs from prior MDM work (e.g., Nie et al. [27] at GPT-2 scale) by demonstrating that the generative modeling principles underlying LLMs—scalability, in-context learning, instruction-following—emerge from the likelihood objective itself rather than the autoregressive factorization. The low-confidence remasking strategy at inference borrows from MaskGIT [23] for images, and the overall training pipeline (pre-training + SFT) mirrors standard LLM practice, making the comparison with ARMs direct.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that a non-autoregressive, diffusion-based language model trained from scratch can match strong ARMs (LLaMA3 8B) on standard benchmarks at 8B scale, challenging the near-universal assumption that the next-token-prediction paradigm is necessary for core LLM capabilities. The reversal-curse result (outperforming GPT-4o on poem completion) highlights a concrete architectural advantage of bidirectional modeling. Key open questions include whether RL-based alignment can close the remaining gap with LLaMA3 Instruct, whether the approach scales beyond 8B and 2.3T tokens, and how to design more efficient sampling (the current iterative denoising is slower than autoregressive decoding). The absence of specialized attention mechanisms, KV caching, and adaptive generation length also leaves significant room for engineering improvements.
