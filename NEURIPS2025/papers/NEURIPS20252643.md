## Problem

Linear Transformers and State Space Models have proven effective for causal sequence modeling, offering parallel training and RNN-style inference, but no unified framework exists for extending them to bidirectional tasks. Existing bidirectional SSMs (e.g., Vim, Hydra) simply run causal recurrences in both forward and backward directions, failing to exploit the fact that the entire sequence is available during both training and inference, which results in training speeds that lag behind softmax Transformers.

## Input / Output

For image classification, input consists of image patches (e.g., 16×16 patches from 224×224 or 1248×1248 ImageNet images) that can be traversed along multiple scan paths to capture spatial structure, producing a class label. For language tasks, input is a sequence of text tokens (C4 for MLM, OpenWebText for causal LM), producing masked token predictions or next-token logits. A key design choice is that the same model supports three interchangeable representations—full attention, bidirectional RNN, and chunkwise parallel—allowing the user to switch between maximum training speed (full attention) and maximum inference efficiency (RNN) without retraining.

## Method

LION defines a bidirectional linear attention mask M where each entry M_ij equals the product of all decay factors between positions i and j, making the mask symmetric and encoding relative positional information in both directions. The paper proves three theoretically equivalent representations: (1) full linear attention Y = SCALE(QK^T ⊙ M)V for maximum training speed via matrix multiplication, (2) a bidirectional RNN with forward and backward recurrences that subtract half the diagonal term to avoid double-counting, enabling O(1) memory inference, and (3) a chunkwise parallel form that balances memory and speed at O(LC) complexity. The selective mask is constructed efficiently as a sum of rank-1 semi-separable lower and upper triangular components via cumulative products in log-space. Three concrete variants are instantiated based on decay type: LION-LIT (no decay, λ=1), LION-D (fixed learnable decay, RetNet-style), and LION-S (input-dependent selective decay, GRFA/Mamba-2-style), all using shifted normalized SiLU feature maps.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K (Small scale), LION-D‡ achieves 79.9% Top-1 accuracy, matching DeiT (79.8%) while training 10× faster than Vision Mamba (1.66× vs 14.95× relative training time). On the Base scale, LION-D‡ reaches 80.2% Top-1, approaching DeiT's 81.8%. For Masked Language Modeling on C4 (334M params), LION-S achieves 69.16 MLM accuracy and 81.58 GLUE score, closely matching BERT (69.88/82.95) while training 2.4× faster than Hydra (1.32× vs 3.13×). In causal LM on OpenWebText, LION-S (124M) achieves 18.16 perplexity, substantially outperforming vanilla Linear Attention (21.07) and approaching GPT-2 (17.42).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ViT, all LION variants outperform the baseline (72.2%/77.9% Top-1), with LION-D‡ reaching 79.9%/80.2% at the cost of only 1.66×/1.48× training time. Against DeiT, LION-D‡ matches accuracy at Small scale (79.9% vs 79.8%) but trails at Base scale (80.2% vs 81.8%); however, LION trains via pure PyTorch matrix operations while DeiT uses standard softmax attention. Against Vision Mamba (Vim), LION-D‡ achieves near-identical accuracy (79.9% vs 80.3% Small) while training 9× faster (1.66× vs 14.95×), and LION-S‡ is 5.5× faster (2.72× vs 14.95×). Against Hydra, LION-D‡ is 1.5× faster to train (1.66× vs 2.50×) and achieves higher Small-scale accuracy (79.9% vs 78.6%). For MLM, LION-S (69.16 MLM, 81.58 GLUE) closely matches BERT (69.88, 82.95) at 1.32× training time, while Hydra (71.18, 81.77) requires 3.13× time; LION-S also offers linear-memory RNN inference versus BERT's quadratic attention. In causal LM, LION-S (18.16 PPL) substantially outperforms vanilla Linear Attention (21.07) and approaches GPT-2 (17.42) at 124M parameters.

## Relation to Prior Work

LION extends the causal Linear Transformer lineage (Katharopoulos et al. 2020, RetNet/Sun et al. 2023, GatedRFA/Peng et al. 2021) to the bidirectional setting, where prior work was largely absent. Existing bidirectional SSMs such as Vision Mamba and Hydra simply run causal recurrences in both forward and backward directions, which the paper argues fails to exploit the full-sequence availability inherent to bidirectional tasks. LION departs from this by constructing a principled bidirectional mask (product of decays between any pair of tokens) and proving that full linear attention, a bidirectional RNN, and a chunkwise parallel form are all theoretically equivalent — a unification that mirrors the causal duality but had not been established for the bidirectional case. Table 1 further positions LION as a general mapping framework, showing that a broad class of models (Mamba-2, GLA, RWKV-6, xLSTM, DeltaNet, HGRN-2) can be systematically converted to bidirectional forms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: LION achieves up to 10× faster training than state-of-the-art bidirectional SSMs (e.g., Vim) while matching or exceeding their accuracy on ImageNet and C4 MLM, and its RNN form gives linear-memory inference that softmax Transformers cannot match. This makes high-quality bidirectional modeling accessible at a fraction of the compute cost, which is particularly relevant for domains like DNA/protein modeling and vision where bidirectionality is essential. Open questions include whether the framework extends cleanly to non-diagonal (matrix-valued) decay as in DeltaNet (only sketched in the appendix), whether the selective-decay variant LION-S closes the remaining gap to DeiT at larger scales, and whether the multi-scan strategy (LION-D†, LION-S†) is the right way to inject spatial structure or whether learned positional encodings could replace it.
