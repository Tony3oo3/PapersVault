## Problem

In multi-agent embodied manipulation, existing policies must choose between local observations (fine-grained but lacking global coordination) and global observations (coherent but lacking agent-specific detail), and neither paradigm alone supports reliable collaborative execution. The paper addresses the absence of a unified representation that simultaneously encodes global scene consistency and per-agent local precision without requiring explicit 3D sensing such as point clouds.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250198/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes synchronized multi-view RGB images (one per agent, 2–4 arms) captured from arbitrary, unposed viewpoints; no camera poses or depth are needed at inference. It outputs per-agent action chunks of length 8 (3 observation steps, 6 execution steps) generated via a 100-step DDPM denoising process. Depth maps are used only to supervise the fine-tuning of the Gaussian reconstruction module, not during policy training or deployment.

## Method

GauDP first reconstructs a shared 3D Gaussian field from all agents' RGB views using a fine-tuned Noposplat backbone: per-view ViT encoders feed a cross-view ViT decoder with cross-attention, and a prediction head emits per-pixel Gaussian parameters (position, scale, rotation, opacity, color, spherical harmonics). The key mechanism is selective global context dispatch: each predicted Gaussian is routed back to the agent whose view produced it, so every agent receives a distilled subset that already encodes cross-view geometry. These dispatched Gaussians are projected into a 2D grid matching the original image resolution and concatenated with the agent's local image features, then fused by a lightweight convolutional module for pixel-level synergy. The fused per-agent features condition a Diffusion Policy (DDPM, 100 steps) that denoises action sequences, while the reconstruction module is trained with a combined RGB + depth rendering loss for geometric fidelity.

## Results

On the RoboFactory benchmark with 2–4 robotic arms, GauDP achieves the highest average success rate of 19.67%, outperforming the best 3D point-cloud baseline DP3(XYZ+RGB) at 14.33% and the standard 2D Diffusion Policy at 5.00%. The most striking gain is on the 2-arm Lift Barrier task, where GauDP reaches 72% success versus 31% for DP3(XYZ+RGB) and 9% for DP. On the 3-arm Align Camera task, GauDP scores 26% compared to 18% for DP3(XYZ+RGB) and 0% for 3D Dense Policy. In real-robot experiments, GauDP also consistently outperforms DP across Card Box Stacking, Card Box Handover, and Grab Roller tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GauDP is compared against three families of baselines: 3D point-cloud methods (DP3 in XYZ and XYZ+RGB variants, 3D Dense Policy) and 2D image-based methods (Diffusion Policy, 2D Dense Policy). Against the strongest 3D baseline DP3(XYZ+RGB) (14.33% avg), GauDP (19.67%) wins by 5.34 percentage points on average, with particularly large margins on Lift Barrier (72% vs. 31%) and Align Camera (26% vs. 18%). However, GauDP does not uniformly dominate: DP3(XYZ+RGB) still leads on Place Food (25% vs. 15%) and Take Photo (11% vs. 3%), and standard DP edges out GauDP on 2-arm Stack Cube (6% vs. 2%). Against the 2D image-based baselines (DP at 5.00%, 2D Dense Policy at 2.33%), GauDP achieves a 3–8× improvement in average success rate. In real-robot trials, GauDP outperforms DP on all three tasks (e.g., 17/30 vs. 11/30 on Card Box Stacking overall success), confirming the simulator-to-reality transfer of the Gaussian-image synergy.

## Relation to Prior Work

GauDP sits at the intersection of three research threads: 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) for scene representation, feed-forward sparse-view reconstruction (via Noposplat), and diffusion-based visuomotor policy learning. It departs from prior multi-agent manipulation work, which either concatenates local RGB views into a shared policy or relies on explicit 3D point clouds, by introducing a differentiable 3D Gaussian field as an intermediate representation that is reconstructed from 2D observations and then selectively redistributed to individual agents. The pixel-level fusion of local image features with view-aligned Gaussian features is a novel architectural choice not present in either the DP or DP3 lineages.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that explicit 3D geometry (point clouds) is not strictly necessary for multi-agent coordination—implicit 3D structure recovered from RGB via Gaussian splatting can match or exceed point-cloud-based policies, which is practically significant since RGB cameras are far more ubiquitous than depth/point-cloud sensors on collaborative robots. However, absolute success rates remain low (19.67% average), all evaluation is confined to the RoboFactory simulation benchmark, and the 4-arm setting yields near-zero performance across all methods, leaving open whether the approach generalizes to more complex or dynamic real-world scenes. The authors themselves flag future directions toward VLA integration and dynamic-scene Gaussian representations, suggesting the current static-scene, simulation-only scope is a meaningful limitation.
