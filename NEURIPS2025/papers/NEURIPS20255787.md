## Problem

Gaussian Process (GP) priors, as L2 regularization, are over-smooth for PDE solutions with sharp transitions (e.g., shocks in Burgers' equation) and fail to accurately capture derivative information. This paper identifies that the q-exponential process (Q-EP), a generalization of GP with Lq regularization, provides a principled probabilistic alternative that better models inhomogeneous data and yields faster posterior convergence, while retaining the uncertainty quantification that neural network-based solvers lack.

## Input / Output

The solver consumes a PDE defined by a differential operator D and boundary operator B on a bounded domain, evaluated at interior collocation points (e.g., 24×24 grid) and boundary points (e.g., 100 points), along with the right-hand side data f and boundary data g. For inverse problems, it additionally takes sparse noisy observations of the solution at N_o points (e.g., 100 points). The output is the PDE solution u(x) with a full posterior distribution (mean and covariance) enabling pointwise uncertainty estimates, and for inverse problems, the recovered unknown coefficient a(x) with its own posterior. The method uses M=256 learned inducing points for sparse variational inference, reducing complexity from O(N³) to O(NM²).

## Method

The method replaces the GP prior with a Q-EP prior (parameterized by q, with q=1 preferred) over an augmented function space that jointly encodes the solution and its derivatives up to order k, using a differentiable kernel (e.g., Matérn-5/2). Because the nonlinear PDE operator P maps the Q-EP mean through a nonlinearity that does not preserve the Q-EP family, the paper linearizes P via a first-order Taylor expansion around a reference point (previous epoch's estimate) to propagate the variational distribution and define a tractable likelihood. Sparse variational inference with M inducing points is then used to maximize an evidence lower bound (ELBO) derived via Jensen's inequality, yielding the posterior approximation. A posterior contraction theorem establishes that q=1 achieves the fastest convergence rate n^{-1/(2+d/(s†-s'))} when the true solution is at least L1-regular, providing theoretical justification for the empirical preference. The framework extends to inverse problems by jointly modeling both the solution u and the unknown coefficient a as independent Q-EPs, with the PDE dynamics and observation operator combined into an augmented likelihood.

## Results

On Burgers' equation, Q-EP with q=1.0 achieves RLE-2 of 0.0266 and RLE-∞ of 0.0324, outperforming GP (q=2.0) by roughly one order of magnitude (RLE-2: 0.2068) and PINN (RLE-2: 0.1896). On the Eikonal equation, Q-EP (q=1.0) attains RLE-2 of 0.0110 and RLE-∞ of 0.0321, compared to 0.0612 and 0.1009 for GP and 0.1167 and 0.1354 for PINN. The inverse Darcy flow problem shows Q-EP (q=1.0) recovering the permeability field more faithfully than both GP and PINN using only 100 observation points. Across all problems, Q-EP with q=1.0 also converges fastest, reaching its best estimate in fewer iterations than GP or PINN.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Q-EP (q=1.0) decisively outperforms both neural network baselines and the GP special case across all tested PDEs. Against PINN [37], Q-EP achieves roughly a 7× reduction in RLE-2 on Burgers' equation (0.0266 vs. 0.1896) and a 10× reduction on the Eikonal equation (0.0110 vs. 0.1167), while also providing principled uncertainty quantification that PINN lacks. Against B-PINN [49], Q-EP still leads by a factor of ~3 in RLE-2 on Burgers' (0.0266 vs. 0.0833) and ~10× on Eikonal (0.0110 vs. 0.1162), with notably lower variance across random seeds. Compared to variational GP (q=2.0), Q-EP (q=1.0) improves RLE-2 by approximately 8× on Burgers' (0.0266 vs. 0.2068) and 6× on Eikonal (0.0110 vs. 0.0612), and also produces lower posterior uncertainty in regions of sharp gradients. The advantage is kernel-independent: Q-EP (q=1.0) beats GP (q=2.0) with Matérn, RBF, and rational quadratic kernels in all settings tested.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the GP-based PDE solver of Chen et al. [6] by replacing the Gaussian process prior with the more general q-exponential process (Q-EP) of Li et al. [24], which provides a probabilistic formulation of Besov-space priors. The sparse variational inference framework of Titsias [46] is adapted to the Q-EP setting, with the key technical challenge of propagating the variational distribution through the nonlinear PDE mapping via linearization. Conceptually, the paper departs from the GP tradition by arguing that the L2 regularization inherent in GPs over-smooths solutions with sharp gradients, and that the L1 regularization of Q-EP (q=1) yields both better derivative modeling and a provably faster posterior contraction rate.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first principled probabilistic PDE solver with built-in uncertainty quantification that is theoretically justified to outperform GP-based solvers for problems with sharp transitions or large derivatives. The posterior contraction rate result (Theorem 4.2) gives a clean theoretical explanation for why under-smoothing (q=1) is preferred over over-smoothing (q=2), connecting the PDE-solving literature to the Besov-prior inverse-problems literature. A key limitation is that the variational approximation may degrade for highly nonlinear PDEs, and the authors suggest normalizing flows as a potential remedy. The method's practical impact is somewhat bounded by the fact that it competes in the niche of probabilistic PDE solvers rather than the dominant PINN paradigm, though its UQ capability remains a differentiator.
