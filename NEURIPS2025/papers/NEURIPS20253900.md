## Problem

Policy-based RL methods (PPO, GRPO) dominate LLM post-training for reasoning but require fresh on-policy rollouts, separate critic models, importance-sampling ratios, and clipping mechanisms, adding computational and implementation complexity. Value-based approaches, which naturally align with LLMs since logits can serve as Q-values under KL-regularized RL, have been largely unexplored for this setting. The paper asks whether a simple trajectory-level Bellman residual objective can replace these complex policy-gradient pipelines while maintaining or improving performance.

## Input / Output

The method consumes math problem prompts (standardized to integer answers, sourced from AoPS and competition homepages via the DAPO dataset of ~17.4k problems) and produces free-form reasoning responses. Critically, only the trajectory-level outcome reward (binary correctness of the final answer, verified by Math-Verify) is required—no per-token or process rewards are needed. Training uses Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B and 7B as base models, with responses sampled at temperature 1.0 and a maximum length of 2048 tokens per prompt.

## Method

TBRM lifts the classical Bellman Residual Minimization objective from the token (step) level to the trajectory level: instead of squaring per-step Bellman errors (which requires unavailable per-token rewards), it squares a single residual spanning the entire rollout and regresses the model's logits onto it. Under the autoregressive function approximation where logits parameterize Q-values, the trajectory-level residual simplifies to logit_θ(s₁,a₁) − r(τ)/β − log π_ref(τ) + Σ_{h=2}^H log π_θ(a_h|s_h), requiring only the outcome reward r(τ). This eliminates critics, importance-sampling ratios, and clipping, and the algorithm is fully off-policy, operating with as few as one rollout per prompt. The theoretical contribution proves convergence to the near-optimal KL-regularized policy from arbitrary off-policy data via an improved change-of-trajectory-measure lemma that avoids the exponential-in-horizon distribution-shift cost previously thought to plague trajectory-level objectives.

## Results

On six mathematical-reasoning benchmarks, TBRM matches or surpasses PPO and GRPO baselines. With Qwen2.5-Math-7B, TBRM (n=4) achieves 30.5% Avg@32 on AIME24, 68.4% on AMC23, 79.8% on MATH500, and 44.1% on OlympiadBench, exceeding GRPO (n=4) by 1.6% on AIME24 and 1.6% on OlympiadBench. With a single rollout (n=1), TBRM already outperforms PPO (n=1) on most benchmarks while using 33.3% less GPU memory and achieving a 1.3× speedup. The 1.5B model reaches 14.3% on AIME24 with TBRM (n=4), surpassing both the 7B base model (10.6%) and the 1.5B GRPO (n=4) model (13.0%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TBRM is compared against two policy-based baselines: PPO (n=1) and GRPO (n=4). Against PPO (n=1), TBRM (n=1) wins on AIME24 (13.2 vs 11.4 for 1.5B; 24.1 vs 25.4 for 7B), AMC23 (48.6 vs 46.6), Minerva (27.2 vs 26.8), MATH500 (78.6 vs 76.4 for 7B), and OlympiadBench (41.5 vs 39.3 for 7B), while using 33.3% less GPU memory and 1.3× less wall-clock time per step. Against GRPO (n=4), TBRM (n=4) achieves higher AIME24 accuracy (30.5 vs 28.9 for 7B; 14.3 vs 13.0 for 1.5B), higher AMC23 (68.4 vs 66.8), and higher OlympiadBench (44.1 vs 42.5), while TBRM (n=1) matches GRPO (n=4) within ~1% average across benchmarks despite using only one rollout instead of four. Notably, TBRM (n=1) achieves a 3.1× training speedup over GRPO (n=4) while yielding comparable performance, and the paper reports a 1.3% absolute average improvement over GRPO across all six benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

TBRM lifts the classical Bellman Residual Minimization (BRM) objective from Schweitzer and Seidmann (1985) and Baird et al. (1995) from the per-step (token) level to the trajectory level, using LLM logits as Q-values under the KL-regularized RL framework. The theoretical analysis builds on the change-of-trajectory-measure result of Jia et al. (2025), which the authors simplify and improve by a factor of H² in the horizon dependence. While related value-based objectives have appeared in soft Q-learning, SAC, and direct alignment algorithms (DPO, KTO), TBRM is positioned as the only optimization-style (non-iterative) algorithm that requires a single rollout per prompt and admits finite-sample convergence guarantees under arbitrary off-policy data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TBRM offers a principled, simpler alternative to the policy-based methods (PPO, GRPO) that currently dominate LLM post-training, eliminating critics, importance-sampling ratios, and clipping while achieving comparable or better accuracy with 33% less GPU memory and 22.5% less training time. The theoretical guarantee that trajectory-level Bellman residual minimization avoids the curse of horizon (incurs only state-wise distribution shift) is a clean result that could inform future value-based RL designs. Open questions include whether the concentrability assumption is realistic at LLM scale, whether the approach generalizes well beyond mathematical reasoning to broader tasks, and whether the modest empirical gains over GRPO (≈1.3% average) persist with larger models or longer training.
