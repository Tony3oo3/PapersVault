## Problem

Most image restoration methods require paired degraded–clear training data, which is impractical for real-world scenarios with diverse and unknown degradation conditions. While Schrödinger Bridge (SB) provides a principled unpaired transport framework, conventional SB formulations ignore physical degradation priors, causing error accumulation and artifact propagation across iterative restoration steps. The paper asks how to leverage SB for unpaired restoration while enforcing degradation-aware consistency to produce physically plausible outputs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250086/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single degraded RGB image (resized to 512×512, intensity normalized to [−1,1]) and produces a restored clean RGB image through an iterative Markov chain of N=5 transport steps. In the unpaired training protocol, each degraded input is paired with a randomly sampled clean image (excluding its ground-truth counterpart) from the clean set, so no pixel-aligned supervision is used. The generator is a U-Net with AdaIN layers and sinusoidal timestep embeddings, while the degradation model is a lightweight 3-layer 64-channel CNN that acts as a guidance module rather than a full reconstructor.

## Method

DDSB formulates unpaired restoration as an entropy-regularized optimal transport (EROT) between degraded and clean distributions, enhanced by two novel components. Degradation-aware Optimal Transport (DOT) augments the EROT cost with a degradation-alignment term ‖D_φ(x_c) − x_d‖², where D_φ is a learnable degradation model that amplifies artifacts in the predicted clean image, making deviations from the true degraded input more pronounced and thus imposing stronger constraints on the transport plan. Dynamic Transport with Consistency (DTC) extends supervision from a single randomly sampled step to the entire trajectory, replacing the per-step degradation target with the endpoint degradation D_φ(x_{t_Ns}) and applying a cosine-scheduled weight λ_t = λ·(1−cos(πt))/2 that emphasizes early iterations where artifacts are most likely to propagate. Training combines the DOT/DTC transport losses with an adversarial KL divergence (via a Markovian discriminator) for marginal alignment at the clean endpoint and MINE-based entropy estimation, all optimized with a linearly decaying learning rate over 400 epochs at batch size 1.

## Results

On the multi-task unpaired restoration benchmark (Table 1), DDSB achieves the best PSNR and SSIM across all four tasks: 25.41 dB / 0.870 (deraining), 25.75 dB / 0.831 (raindrop removal), 25.38 dB / 0.865 (low-light), and 25.22 dB / 0.804 (deblurring). On the generalized dehazing protocol (Table 3), trained only on SOTS-indoor, DDSB reaches 27.85 dB / 0.956 (SOTS-indoor), 27.67 dB / 0.971 (SOTS-outdoor), and 17.92 dB / 0.783 (NH-HAZE 2), while running at 0.019 ms per 512×512 image—orders of magnitude faster than most baselines. Perceptual metrics (LPIPS, NIQE) in Table 2 also confirm DDSB's superiority, e.g., 0.063 LPIPS and 3.94 NIQE on deraining.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DDSB outperforms all 11 baselines on every task and metric. Against the strongest baseline DN [18], DDSB gains +0.69 dB (deraining), +1.12 dB (raindrop), +1.80 dB (low-light), and +2.02 dB (deblurring) in PSNR, with corresponding SSIM improvements of +0.025, +0.007, +0.021, and +0.008. On the generalized dehazing protocol, the margin over DN widens to +1.60 dB (SOTS-indoor) and +1.49 dB (SOTS-outdoor), while DDSB runs ~4,600× faster (0.019 ms vs. 87.7 ms). The direct SB-based predecessor UNSB [22] is surpassed by +0.73 to +3.11 dB PSNR across tasks, confirming the benefit of the DOT and DTC components. Classical prior DCP [16] and early GAN-based CycleGAN [59] trail by 10+ dB, while contrastive methods CUT [32] and Santa [51] are consistently 0.5–1.5 dB behind DDSB. The closest competitor ODCR [47] (CVPR 2024) is beaten by +0.52 to +2.65 dB PSNR, with the largest gap on deblurring (+2.49 dB) and low-light (+1.96 dB).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Unpaired Schrödinger Bridge (UNSB) framework of Kim et al. (2023) from general unpaired image-to-image translation to the specific domain of image restoration, and builds on the deraining-specific SB formulation of Wen et al. (2024). The key conceptual departure is the introduction of a learnable degradation model D_φ into the entropy-regularized optimal transport cost (DOT) and a trajectory-level consistency objective (DTC) that supervises every intermediate SB step rather than only the endpoints. This positions the work as a principled bridge between physics-informed degradation modeling and the distributional transport perspective of Schrödinger bridges, distinguishing it from CycleGAN-style cycle consistency and from prior unpaired restoration methods that rely on adversarial or contrastive losses without explicit transport dynamics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a fundamental practical bottleneck in image restoration—the scarcity of paired degraded/clean data—by showing that a degradation-aware transport formulation can generalize to unseen degradation types without paired supervision. The consistent gains across four distinct degradation tasks (deraining, deblurring, dehazing, low-light) and the strong generalization from indoor-trained to outdoor/real-world haze suggest the approach captures transferable degradation structure. However, the theoretical analysis (Appendix A.1) is a sketch rather than a rigorous proof, the degradation model is a small 3-layer CNN that may not capture complex real-world degradations, and the authors themselves note that physical degradation models could be integrated in future work. The reported inference time of 0.019 ms for 512×512 images appears implausibly fast and warrants scrutiny.
