## Problem

Prior skill-learning methods are restricted to either purely discrete skills (structured but inflexible) or purely continuous skills (flexible but unstructured), and standard implementations of parameterized skill learning are under-specified, often collapsing to degenerate solutions that minimize behavior cloning loss without discovering meaningful, generalizable skill abstractions. The paper asks how to learn parameterized skills—discrete skills modulated by continuous arguments—from multitask demonstrations in a way that produces temporally extended, interpretable, and rapidly adaptable skills for unseen tasks.

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-task expert demonstrations consisting of RGB images (two viewpoints in LIBERO, one in MetaWorld), robot proprioceptive state vectors (9-dim in LIBERO, 8-dim in MetaWorld), and task descriptions (language embeddings or one-hot IDs). At inference, the hierarchical policy outputs a discrete skill index, a continuous parameter vector (4-dim), and a primitive robot action (6-dim in LIBERO, 4-dim in MetaWorld). A critical design constraint is that the low-level subpolicy receives only a 1D compressed scalar derived from the proprioceptive state, not the full observation, while the high-level policies retain access to images and full proprioception.

## Method

DEPS jointly trains a three-level hierarchy—a discrete skill selector, a continuous parameter selector conditioned on the skill, and a subpolicy conditioned on both—by maximizing a variational lower bound derived via temporal variational inference, with a bidirectional GRU encoder providing posterior access to the full trajectory. To prevent degenerate solutions, the method introduces an information bottleneck: the subpolicy receives only a 1D projective compression of the proprioceptive state (computed via a skill-specific projection axis learned by a small MLP), forcing the discrete skill and continuous parameter to encode all task-relevant information not captured by this scalar index. Continuous parameters are predicted per discrete skill instance rather than per timestep to enforce temporal extension, and a norm penalty discourages overfitting of parameters to specific trajectories. The high-level policies (unidirectional GRUs) and subpolicy (feedforward network with a GMM or deterministic head) are trained end-to-end, with the KL divergence terms aligning the autoregressive inference policies with the variational posterior.

## Results

On LIBERO-OOD (out-of-distribution tasks with novel objects and environments), DEPS achieves a mean success rate of 0.34±0.08 and mean highest success of 0.66±0.12 after only 500 finetuning gradient steps, more than doubling BC (0.15) and tripling PRISE (0.10). In the data-scarce LIBERO-3-shot setting (3 demonstrations per task), DEPS maintains 0.26±0.03 mean success, substantially outperforming PRISE (0.07) and BC (0.11). On MetaWorld-v2, DEPS achieves 0.45±0.03 mean success on MW-Vanilla and 0.32±0.03 on MW-PRISE, again leading all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DEPS outperforms all three baselines (PRISE, BC, and BC-Untrained) across every evaluation setting on both LIBERO and MetaWorld-v2. Against PRISE, the state-of-the-art skill-learning baseline, DEPS achieves roughly 3× higher mean success on LIBERO-OOD (0.34 vs. 0.10) and 4× on MW-PRISE (0.32 vs. 0.06), with the gap widening under limited pretraining (5 epochs: 0.24 vs. 0.02). Against standard multitask BC, DEPS leads by 0.19 mean success on LIBERO-OOD (0.34 vs. 0.15) and by 0.10 on MW-Vanilla (0.45 vs. 0.35); the advantage is most pronounced in the 3-shot regime (0.26 vs. 0.11). BC-Untrained, which skips pretraining entirely, is near-zero on LIBERO tasks (0.01 mean success on LIBERO-OOD), confirming that pretraining is essential for adaptation in these complex environments. The one setting where DEPS's margin is smallest is LIBERO-10 (in-distribution, long-horizon tasks), where BC is competitive (0.07 vs. 0.08 mean success), consistent with the paper's discussion that aggressive 1D state compression trades some in-distribution accuracy for generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

DEPS builds directly on temporal variational inference (Shankar & Gupta, ICML 2020) for its training objective and on the options framework (Sutton et al., 1999) for its hierarchical structure, but departs from both by jointly learning a three-level hierarchy of discrete skill, continuous parameter, and subpolicy in an end-to-end fashion. Prior parameterized-skill work (da Silva et al. 2012, Fu et al. 2023) required labeled parameters or multi-stage training, while recent methods like LOTUS and EXTRACT rely on clustering with pretrained VLMs; DEPS instead uses information-theoretic constraints—most notably a 1D projective state compression in the subpolicy—to prevent degenerate solutions and force meaningful skill abstractions without external supervision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that aggressive observation compression (to a single scalar) paradoxically improves generalization to unseen tasks, suggesting that forcing reliance on latent skill variables is more valuable than preserving state information for transfer. Key limitations include dependence on proprioceptive robot states for the compression step, a measurable tradeoff between out-of-distribution generalization and in-distribution long-horizon accuracy (LIBERO-10), and the method's added complexity relative to plain behavior cloning. Open questions include whether the compression strategy can be adapted to settings without proprioceptive state, whether residual policies can recover in-distribution accuracy without sacrificing generalization, and whether the approach extends beyond manipulation to more diverse robotic domains.
