## Problem

The repeated newsvendor problem has only been studied under the assumption that the supplier sets the trading price, yet many real-world platforms (ad exchanges, cloud providers, ridesharing) use an independent mediator to propose prices. This paper studies the repeated mediated newsvendor problem (RMNP), where a mediator posts prices to facilitate trades between suppliers and retailers, and characterizes its learnability: proving matching O~(T^{2/3}) upper and lower regret bounds under four natural assumptions, and showing that relaxing any single assumption renders the problem unlearnable (linear regret).

## Input / Output

At each round the mediator posts a single trading price p ∈ [0,1] and receives severely limited feedback: a binary indicator of whether the supplier's private cost C_t ≤ p (acceptance/rejection), and the retailer's desired quantity U_t^#(p) (the quantity maximizing their net utility at price p). The mediator never observes the realized gain-from-trade reward, the supplier's actual cost, or the retailer's utility function directly. The algorithm takes the time horizon T, a discretization parameter K = ⌈T^{1/3}⌉, and a confidence parameter δ = 1/T as inputs, and outputs a sequence of trading prices P_1, …, P_T.

## Method

Algorithm 1 is a two-phase UCB-type bandit strategy. In the initial phase (K² rounds), it cycles through K equispaced grid prices in round-robin fashion to collect feedback samples. The key insight is that, under independence of supplier cost and retailer utility, the expected reward decomposes as E[U^#(p)]·E[(p−C)⁺] + E[I{C≤p}]·E[U*(p)], where the first and third factors are directly estimable from feedback but the second and fourth are hidden. The paper leverages the Milgrom–Segal Envelope Theorem to show that u*(p₁)−u*(p₂) = ∫_{p₁}^{p₂} u^#(λ)dλ, enabling reconstruction of E[U*(p)] and E[(p−C)⁺] at any grid point via integration of the observed quantity and acceptance feedback over the grid. In the main phase, the algorithm runs a UCB strategy over the K grid points using optimistic estimates of the hidden terms combined with sequential UCB estimates of the observable terms, achieving O~(T^{2/3}) regret with K = ⌈T^{1/3}⌉.

## Results

The paper establishes matching upper and lower regret bounds of Õ(T^{2/3}) for the repeated mediated newsvendor problem under four natural assumptions (i.i.d. sequences, cost-utility independence, Lipschitz cost CDF, and zero expected utility at price 1). Algorithm 1 achieves the upper bound via a two-phase UCB strategy that uses the Envelope Theorem to reconstruct hidden reward terms from limited feedback. A matching Ω(T^{2/3}) lower bound is proven by reduction from repeated bilateral trade. If any single assumption is violated, the problem becomes unlearnable with Ω(T) linear regret for any algorithm.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare Algorithm 1 against other online learning algorithms, as no prior work addresses the repeated mediated newsvendor problem. Instead, the key comparison is structural: the RMNP is shown to be a strict generalization of repeated bilateral trade (Theorem A.1), and the Õ(T^{2/3}) upper bound automatically recovers the known near-optimal rates for bilateral trade without requiring a specialized algorithm. The Ω(T^{2/3}) lower bound is inherited from the bilateral trade literature via this reduction. The impossibility results (Theorem 5.1) show that the four assumptions are jointly necessary—relaxing any one yields linear regret—positioning the contribution as a complete characterization of learnability in this setting rather than an incremental improvement over a baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the repeated bilateral trade literature (Cesa-Bianchi et al. 2021, 2023b,c, 2024; Azar et al. 2022) from non-divisible items to divisible quantities, formalizing the setting as a strict generalization proven in Appendix A. The key technical departure is the use of Milgrom and Segal's Envelope Theorem to reconstruct hidden reward components (supplier surplus and retailer net utility) from severely limited feedback—only a cost threshold and a purchase quantity—enabling a UCB-based bandit strategy over a discretized price grid. The paper also builds on the classical newsvendor problem (Arrow et al. 1951) and its repeated variants (Huang and Sošić 2010; Cesa-Bianchi et al. 2023a), but introduces the critical change that an independent mediator, rather than the supplier, sets the trading price.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The matching T^{2/3} upper and lower bounds, combined with the strong impossibility result that relaxing any of the four assumptions (i.i.d., cost-utility independence, Lipschitz cdf, zero utility at price 1) yields linear regret, provide a complete characterization of learnability in this setting. The algorithm's automatic adaptation to bilateral trade without modification is a notable robustness property. Open directions include a fair-division variant (minimizing the minimum utility rather than the sum), contextual extensions for shared shocks, weakly budget-balanced mechanisms with asymmetric prices, and bargaining protocols where suppliers can counter-offer.
