## Problem

Controlling free-form soft swimmers in fluid is hindered by two coupled gaps: (1) no unified, morphology-agnostic control paradigm exists—current methods rely on hand-designed muscle layouts or voxel-aligned contractions that require per-shape engineering and restrict exploration; and (2) existing learning environments use simplified fluid models that omit critical hydrodynamic phenomena such as vortex shedding and wake capture, preventing discovery of biologically realistic gaits.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D tetrahedral mesh (400–1500 vertices, 1000–6000 elements, normalized to a common scale) and a target swimming direction as input. The policy outputs a 6m-dimensional action vector (m geodesic-sampled control points × 3 rotation + 3 translation) that modulates the mesh's rest shape via linear blend skinning, producing an internal-actuation swimming gait. The state representation is rotation- and translation-invariant by construction, obtained by solving a Procrustes alignment on the control-point cloud at each step, making the policy topology-agnostic across morphologies.

## Method

The core contribution is a unified reduced-mode control space built from geodesic farthest-point sampling and linear blend skinning with RBF weights, yielding a compact 6m-parameter deformation basis that is resolution-independent and morphology-agnostic. A dynamics correction step (energy minimization inspired by complementary dynamics) projects the kinematically proposed displacement onto the manifold of physically feasible configurations, preventing element inversion while preserving kinematic intent. The simulator couples a high-order moment-encoded Lattice Boltzmann Method (HOME-LBM) for fluid with a GPU-accelerated finite-element solver for the elastic body via a weak two-way coupling scheme on a 128×128×512 grid, resolving vortex-mediated thrust mechanisms. Policies are trained with Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) reinforcement learning, where the reward combines a task term (velocity·direction), a smoothness penalty on action changes, and a regularization term that suppresses redundant actuation modes inherent to the normalized LBS weight structure.

## Results

On the forward swimming task across 12 morphologies, the proposed method achieves a success rate of over 80% (10/12 morphologies produce visible forward locomotion), compared to only 4/12 for the clustering-based SOTA baseline and 2/12 for the domain-expert controller. Headline rewards include 15.05 on the torus (vs. −0.07 for clustering), 12.33 on the Enneper surface (vs. 0.20 for clustering), and 23.43 on the jellyfish (vs. 18.85 for domain-expert). The method also demonstrates energy-efficient locomotion, achieving an optimal Cost of Transport of 74.7 J/m at w_e=0.02, a 71% reduction over the baseline 258.3 J/m.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three baselines: (1) a domain-expert controller with manually designed muscle layouts (following Lin et al., 2019), (2) a clustering-based controller from SoftZoo/DiffuseBot (Wang et al., 2023a,b) that uses K-means + PCA to define muscle orientations, and (3) a differentiable MPM controller from SoftZoo that was excluded from quantitative comparison due to numerical instability beyond 3 s and memory constraints at the required 128×128×512 resolution. The proposed method wins decisively on all six abstract morphologies (torus: 15.05 vs. −0.07; Enneper: 12.33 vs. 0.20; tube: 3.99 vs. 1.02), where both baselines fail to produce meaningful motion. On bio-inspired shapes, the domain-expert controller retains an edge on the clownfish (11.96 vs. 10.34) due to its well-understood axial muscle design, and the clustering baseline outperforms the proposed method on the eel (13.2 vs. 6.26), but the proposed method surpasses both on the jellyfish (23.43 vs. 18.85) and the turtle (8.67 vs. −1.18). Overall, the proposed method achieves forward locomotion on 10/12 morphologies versus 4/12 for clustering and 2/12 for domain-expert, a roughly 50% higher success rate than the SOTA baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of reduced-order soft-body control (LBS from Jacobson et al. 2014, complementary dynamics from Zhang et al. 2020) and high-fidelity GPU fluid simulation (HOME-LBM from Li et al. 2023, Vertex Block Descent from Chen et al. 2024). Prior soft swimmer work (SoftZoo, DiffAqua, SoftCon) either relied on morphology-specific manual muscle layouts or simplified fluid models that omit vortex dynamics; this paper departs by coupling a morphology-agnostic LBS control space with a full two-way LBM–FEM coupling, eliminating the need for domain-expert actuator design while retaining hydrodynamic fidelity. The geodesic farthest-point sampling for control point placement and the dynamic correction step are the key methodological additions that make the reduced modes physically plausible across arbitrary topologies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework demonstrates that effective swimming gaits can emerge for morphologies with no obvious biological template (torus, Enneper surface), suggesting that swimming aptitude is a broader latent property of soft geometries than previously assumed. The jellyfish result—where a learned alternating-contraction variant outperforms the canonical synchronized bell contraction used by domain experts—hints that RL can discover non-obvious propulsion strategies. Key open challenges remain: the framework operates on fixed morphologies (no co-design), the sim-to-real gap is unaddressed, and a universal controller generalizing across unseen geometries is left as future work. The 12-morphology evaluation, while diverse, is still limited in scale, and the primary task is forward swimming in a single direction.
