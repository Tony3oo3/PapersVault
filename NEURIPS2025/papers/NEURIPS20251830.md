## Problem

Existing one-shot visual imitation methods are typically evaluated on tasks that are minor variations of training tasks (e.g., different object positions), and they lack an explicit model of environment dynamics. When tested on genuinely unseen tasks that are visually similar but contextually different (e.g., opening vs. closing a window), these methods exhibit severe performance degradation, as demonstrated by AWDA. The paper addresses both gaps: generalization to semantically distinct unseen tasks and the absence of future-state reasoning.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an expert demonstration video downsampled to N=10 RGB frames and a single agent initial observation image, with no requirement for aligned timestamps or shared embodiment between expert and agent (e.g., human expert vs. Franka robot, or two Franka arms at different mounting locations). It outputs 5 attributed waypoints, each a 4D vector encoding 3D end-effector position in camera coordinates plus a binary gripper state, which are transformed to the robot frame and executed via inverse kinematics; an end-effector-mounted depth camera corrects residual pose errors in the final centimeters before contact. The system also supports re-planning, where a new observation triggers a fresh waypoint prediction if the initial execution fails.

## Method

A shared ResNet-18 encoder maps each expert frame and the agent observation to spatial latent states (512×H×W feature maps). A world-model-guided trajectory generation module then recursively predicts M−1 future latent states: at each step, a causal-transformer action model π produces a latent action from the agent's history and the expert demonstration, and a causal-transformer world model W uses that action plus the agent's state history to predict the next latent state. The predicted trajectory is compressed via spatial pooling (2D softmax → expected coordinates per feature channel) and temporal attention pooling into a single vector, which a two-layer MLP decodes into the 5 attributed waypoints. Training jointly optimizes a differentiable soft-DTW loss on waypoints and an L1 world-model loss on the predicted latent states (with stop-gradient on ground-truth targets to prevent collapse), all end-to-end on in-domain data without any pretraining or large-scale external datasets.

## Results

On the Meta-World simulation benchmark, OSVI-WM achieves 83.8% average success rate across all test tasks (96% on easy, 71.5% on hard), outperforming the strongest baseline AWDA by 31.3 points (83.8% vs 51.5%) even when AWDA uses additional training data. On Pick-and-Place, OSVI-WM matches AWDA at 100% success. In real-world experiments, OSVI-WM achieves 96% on Two-Franka-PP, 92% on Human-Franka-PP, and 100% on Human-Franka-Push, consistently matching or exceeding AWDA (88%, 92%, 76% respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

OSVI-WM is compared against three baselines: DAML (domain-adaptive meta-learning), T-OSVI (transformer-based one-shot visual imitation), and AWDA (attributed waypoints with demonstration augmentation), the latter being the primary baseline as it is the only prior method evaluated on truly unseen test tasks. On Meta-World, OSVI-WM dramatically outperforms all baselines, achieving 83.8% overall success versus AWDA's 51.5% (with extra data) and 42.5% (without), a gap of over 30 points. The advantage is most pronounced on the 'hard' tasks (Window-Open, Door-Unlock), where OSVI-WM reaches 71.5% while AWDA manages only 30% (with extra data) or 11% (without), and T-OSVI/DAML score 7–8%. On Pick-and-Place, OSVI-WM ties AWDA at 100% but substantially exceeds T-OSVI (10%) and DAML (1%). In real-world settings, OSVI-WM matches AWDA on Human-Franka-PP (92% each) but wins on Two-Franka-PP (96% vs 88%) and Human-Franka-Push (100% vs 76%), while T-OSVI fails entirely on pick-and-place tasks (0–4%) and scores only 16% on the push task.

## Relation to Prior Work

OSVI-WM builds directly on the attributed waypoint prediction and demonstration augmentation framework of AWDA [Chang & Gupta, ICRA 2023] and the spatial pooling and transformer-based architecture of T-OSVI [Dasari & Gupta, CoRL 2021], but departs from both by introducing a world-model-guided trajectory generation module that recursively predicts future latent states. While prior OSVI methods (T-OSVI, DAML, AWDA) treat the task as a single-step mapping from demonstration to waypoints, OSVI-WM borrows the latent dynamics modeling paradigm from RL world models (Dreamer, TD-MPC2) and applies it to one-shot imitation, enabling multi-step future-aware planning. The paper also distinguishes itself from large-scale pretraining approaches (SWIM, GR1, DINO-WM) by training entirely in-domain without any pretraining stage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete and previously under-addressed gap: one-shot visual imitation methods that generalize to semantically distinct unseen tasks (e.g., opening vs. closing a window) rather than merely varying object configurations. The finding that a simple auxiliary dynamics-consistency loss on predicted latent states acts as a task-agnostic regularizer improving generalization is a useful insight for the broader imitation learning community. Key limitations remain: the method predicts only 3D position and gripper state (no orientation), focuses on single-task execution, and performance is sensitive to the train-test task family composition. Open questions include extending to multi-task sequences, incorporating end-effector orientation, and whether the world-model approach scales to more diverse or longer-horizon task families.
