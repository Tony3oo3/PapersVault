## Problem

Existing text-guided image editing models naively attempt to follow all user instructions, even when those instructions are infeasible or contradictory given the image content, resulting in nonsensical or over-edited outputs. No prior method explicitly validates whether an instruction is executable before applying it, and multi-instruction scenarios with a mix of applicable and non-applicable requests remain unaddressed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250707/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts an RGB image and a sequence of one or more natural-language editing instructions, some of which may be non-executable (e.g., requesting modification of a non-existent object). It produces an edited image along with per-instruction binary masks that delineate exactly which spatial regions are modified. The MLLM emits a sequence of [MASK] and [NEG] tokens whose count matches the number of instructions, and the Token Decoder converts only [MASK] tokens into spatial masks while [NEG] tokens are replaced with black (no-edit) masks.

## Method

CAMILA builds on a pretrained MLLM (LLaVA) fine-tuned with LoRA to jointly encode the image and text instructions, classifying each output token as [MASK] (editable) or [NEG] (suppress). A Token Broadcaster aligns these MLLM tokens with the diffusion model's text embeddings via trainable projection matrices and cosine-similarity-based argmax assignment, ensuring each instruction maps to the correct spatial region. A two-layer Transformer Token Decoder then converts [MASK] tokens into binary editing masks using cross-attention between image features and text embeddings. The concatenated binary mask modulates the 16×16 cross-attention maps inside the Stable Diffusion U-Net, selectively applying text-conditioned attention only to editable regions while preserving unaltered areas. Training combines four losses (token classification CE, broadcast alignment CE, Dice, and BCE), and a lightweight surrogate module approximates the CLIP-T score to enable an additional refinement stage that improves mask quality without backpropagating through the full diffusion pipeline.

## Results

On the Multi-instruction task, CAMILA achieves L1=0.0945, L2=0.0366, CLIP-I=0.8980, DINO=0.8392, and CLIP-T=0.2984, outperforming the strongest baseline FoI (L1=0.1054, DINO=0.8096) across all metrics. On the more challenging Context-Aware Instruction task, CAMILA further improves to L1=0.0661, L2=0.0222, CLIP-I=0.9296, DINO=0.8932, and CLIP-T=0.3006, demonstrating a 26% reduction in L1 error and a 9.1-point gain in DINO over FoI. In human preference evaluation (PickScore), CAMILA surpasses FoI by 18.1% on multi-instruction and 24.0% on context-aware settings. On the EMU dataset, CAMILA achieves the highest CLIP-dir score (0.092) in the context-aware task, confirming effective filtering of non-executable instructions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CAMILA outperforms all five baselines (IP2P, MGIE, SmartEdit, FoI, EMILIE) on every reported metric in both multi-instruction and context-aware settings. The strongest competitor is FoI, which uses cross-attention maps for multi-instruction editing; CAMILA reduces L1 error by 10.3% (multi) and 25.8% (context-aware) relative to FoI, while gaining 3.0 and 7.4 points in DINO similarity respectively. MGIE, which compresses instructions via summarization, is the weakest baseline, showing the largest degradation on context-aware tasks (L1=0.1592 vs. CAMILA's 0.0661), confirming its vulnerability to non-applicable instructions. SmartEdit benefits from MLLM integration but still over-edits when all instructions are fed simultaneously, yielding L1=0.1111 on context-aware tasks. Against the large-scale Step1X-Edit model (which uses a larger diffusion backbone), CAMILA still achieves lower L1 (0.0661 vs. 0.0830) and higher CLIP-I (0.9296 vs. 0.8096) on the context-aware task, indicating that the context-aware masking design compensates for smaller model capacity.

## Relation to Prior Work

CAMILA builds directly on the InstructPix2Pix (IP2P) diffusion framework and its classifier-free guidance formulation, extending it with an MLLM front-end that classifies instructions as executable or not. It departs from prior MLLM-based editors (SmartEdit, MGIE) that feed all instructions indiscriminately into the diffusion model, and from FoI's reliance on cross-attention maps for region localization, by introducing explicit [MASK]/[NEG] token classification and a Token Broadcaster that aligns tokens to text embeddings via cosine similarity. The surrogate module for CLIP-T optimization is a novel training technique that avoids backpropagating through the full diffusion pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap—users routinely issue infeasible or contradictory editing instructions, and existing systems produce nonsensical over-edited results. By formalizing Context-Aware Image Editing as a distinct evaluation task and providing new datasets, the work gives the community a benchmark for measuring instruction executability filtering. Open questions include how the approach generalizes to longer instruction sequences, video editing, or cases where 'infeasibility' is ambiguous rather than binary, and whether the 90.21% token classification accuracy is sufficient for production use.
