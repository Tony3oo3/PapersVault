## Problem

Radar-based indoor 3D human pose estimation has traditionally required fine-grained 3D keypoint labels obtained from expensive, non-portable motion capture systems (e.g., VICON) or LiDAR, which are impractical to collect at scale in cluttered, occluded, or multi-person indoor environments. The use of cheaper, more scalable weak supervision signals—coarse-grained 3D bounding boxes and fine-grained 2D keypoints—as substitutes for dense 3D keypoint labels in radar-based 3D pose estimation had not been systematically investigated.

## Input / Output

The model consumes T=4 consecutive frames of two synchronized radar heatmaps: a horizontal range-azimuth view (W×D) and a vertical range-elevation view (H×D), each derived from 4D radar tensors via 3D FFT and Doppler-axis integration. It outputs N=10 candidate 3D poses, each comprising K joint positions (14 for HIBER, 17 for MMVR) in the world coordinate system, along with per-pose confidence scores. Poses are first estimated in the radar coordinate frame and then mapped to world coordinates through a known rigid transformation.

## Method

RAPTR employs a two-stage transformer decoder (pose decoder followed by joint decoder) built on the PETR architecture, with a shared ResNet backbone and a cross-view encoder that fuses horizontal and vertical radar features via bidirectional deformable cross-attention. The key architectural novelty is a pseudo-3D deformable attention mechanism: 3D reference points and sampling offsets are defined in the radar coordinate space and projected onto both 2D radar views for feature extraction, eliminating redundant per-view offset estimation and improving scalability with additional views. Training uses a structured loss that couples the two stages: the pose decoder is supervised by a 3D Template Loss (T3D) that constrains initial poses to a rigid template anchored at the 3D BBox centroid, while the joint decoder refines individual keypoints using a combined 3D Gravity Loss (G3D) and 2D Keypoint Loss (K2D) applied after projecting 3D estimates into the camera image plane. This design lets the pose decoder establish geometrically consistent 3D structure from coarse BBox labels before the joint decoder captures fine-grained limb configuration from 2D keypoint supervision, effectively mitigating the depth ambiguity inherent in 2D-only supervision.

## Results

On HIBER, RAPTR achieves an overall MPJPE of 22.32 cm (WALK) and 18.99 cm (MULTI), reducing error by 34.3% and 42.7% over the strongest baseline HRRadarPose (33.96 cm and 33.19 cm), respectively. On MMVR (P1S1), RAPTR reduces the 3D BBox center distance to 31.41 cm, a 76.9% improvement over Person-in-WiFi 3D (136.14 cm) and 85.1% over QRFPose (210.75 cm). RAPTR also shows the lowest per-axis errors, e.g., 8.41 cm horizontal and 4.85 cm vertical on HIBER WALK, indicating well-proportioned 3D poses across all spatial dimensions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RAPTR outperforms all four baselines on both datasets. Against HRRadarPose (the strongest baseline), RAPTR reduces overall MPJPE by 34.3% on HIBER WALK (22.32 vs 33.96 cm) and 42.7% on HIBER MULTI (18.99 vs 33.19 cm), with particularly large gains on challenging joints like the wrist (26.55 vs 42.33 cm). Against QRFPose, the closest architectural competitor (also a DETR-style query-based method), RAPTR achieves 41.6% and 58.8% MPJPE reductions on WALK and MULTI, respectively, attributed to the pseudo-3D deformable attention and two-stage decoder. Against Person-in-WiFi 3D, RAPTR shows the largest margins (61.7% on WALK, 77.7% on MULTI), reflecting the advantage of transformer-based query mechanisms over CNN pipelines. On MMVR, where 3D keypoint labels are unavailable, RAPTR reduces BBox center distance by 76.9–85.1% over all three baselines, while the baselines exhibit structural collapse in 3D space. The 2D-to-3D pose uplifting baseline (Martinez et al.) performs substantially worse (43.43 cm on WALK), confirming that direct 3D refinement with structured losses is superior to post-hoc lifting.

## Relation to Prior Work

RAPTR adapts the two-stage (pose + joint) query-based decoder architecture of PETR, originally designed for 2D RGB pose estimation, to the 3D radar domain, and lifts PETR's 2D deformable attention to a pseudo-3D variant where reference points and sampling offsets live in 3D radar space but are projected onto 2D radar views. The closest prior method, QRFPose, also uses a DETR-style query mechanism for radar 3D pose estimation but applies per-view 2D deformable attention and a single decoder for all keypoints; RAPTR departs by introducing the pseudo-3D attention for cross-view consistency and a structured two-stage loss (3D Template at the pose decoder, 3D Gravity + 2D Keypoint at the joint decoder) that enables training from only 3D BBox and 2D keypoint labels rather than dense 3D keypoints.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that 3D radar pose estimation can be trained effectively from cheap, scalable labels (3D BBoxes and 2D keypoints) rather than expensive motion-capture 3D keypoints, RAPTR lowers the barrier to collecting training data for cluttered, multi-person indoor scenes. The pseudo-3D deformable attention offers a principled path to scaling to more radar views without per-view offset explosion. Key open questions include how the method handles the inherent depth ambiguity when 2D labels cannot distinguish forward- vs. backward-facing poses, and whether the structured loss generalizes to more than two radar views or to raw radar tensors beyond heatmaps.
