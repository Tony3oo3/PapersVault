## Problem

Large language models are routinely pretrained on unlicensed web text, raising copyright infringement and ethical concerns, yet prior openly licensed datasets (OLC, Common Corpus, KL3M) are too small or low-quality to yield performant models. This paper asks whether a sufficiently large and well-curated collection of public domain and openly licensed text can serve as a competitive pretraining corpus for LLMs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251918/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline ingests raw text from 30 diverse sources (open-access papers, StackExchange dumps, GitHub issues, US government documents, public domain books, wikis, CC BY YouTube transcripts, Common Crawl pages, and more) that satisfy the Open Knowledge Foundation's Open Definition 2.1. After language identification, quality filtering, toxicity removal, PII redaction, and global fuzzy deduplication, the output is the 8TB Common Pile v0.1 in a relatively raw format, plus a heavily filtered and reweighted training mixture (the Comma dataset) and two 7B-parameter LLMs trained on 1T and 2T tokens respectively.

## Method

The curation pipeline applies strict license due diligence (excluding sources with known license-laundering issues, collection-level licenses, or LLM-generated synthetic content), then preprocesses each source using Dolma-based pipelines for language filtering, OCR-error removal, toxicity classification, and PII redaction, followed by global bloom-filter deduplication at 90% 20-gram overlap. Data mixing weights are determined heuristically by training per-source 1.7B models for 28B tokens and up/down-weighting sources based on their individual performance, targeting at most six repetitions over a 1T-token run. The Comma v0.1 models use a Llama-7B architecture trained via the lingua framework with a custom 64K-vocabulary BPE tokenizer, a two-stage schedule (cosine decay followed by a cool-down on a high-quality source subset), and final checkpoint averaging over ten evenly spaced cool-down checkpoints.

## Results

In the controlled 1.7B-parameter experiment (28B tokens), the Comma dataset-based model outperforms all other openly licensed datasets (OLC, Common Corpus, KL3M) across all seven benchmarks and beats the Pile-based model on all but two. Comma v0.1-1T (7B, 1T tokens) performs comparably to Llama 1 7B, MPT-7B, and RPJ-INCITE-7B, excelling on MMLU and code benchmarks (HumanEval, MBPP) while lagging on HellaSwag and PIQA. Comma v0.1-2T (7B, 2T tokens) is competitive with OLMo-7B-Twin-2T, Llama 2 7B, and DeepSeekLLM, with especially strong performance on MMLU, SIQA, ARC-E, and coding tasks. Both models fall well short of Qwen3 8B (36T tokens), confirming the benefit of larger training budgets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against other openly licensed datasets in the controlled 1.7B experiment, the Comma dataset wins on every benchmark versus OLC, Common Corpus, and KL3M, and beats the Pile on all but two benchmarks; however, FineWeb (unlicensed) still leads on most commonsense reasoning tasks (HellaSwag, PIQA, CommonsenseQA), while Comma leads on MMLU and ARC. For the 1T-token 7B comparison, Comma v0.1-1T matches or slightly trails Llama 1 7B and MPT-7B on general benchmarks but substantially outperforms them on code (HumanEval, MBPP), and is competitive with RPJ-INCITE-7B overall. In the 2T-token comparison, Comma v0.1-2T is broadly competitive with OLMo-7B-Twin-2T, Llama 2 7B, and DeepSeekLLM, with particular strengths on MMLU, SIQA, ARC-E, and coding, though the authors note excessive data repetition (up to 16 passes) likely suppresses its true potential. The consistent pattern across both scales is strong knowledge and code performance offset by weaker commonsense reasoning (HellaSwag, PIQA), attributed to underrepresentation of personal blogs, tutorials, and hobby content in the openly licensed corpus.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on constructing openly licensed pretraining corpora (OLC, Common Corpus, KL3M, C4Corpus) by scaling to 8TB across 30 diverse sources and, critically, validating the dataset through full 7B model training rather than relying on proxy metrics. Methodologically, it builds on Dolma's data processing and deduplication pipelines and Llama's architecture/training conventions, while departing from prior open-licensed efforts by incorporating a much broader source mix (code, patents, IRC logs, YouTube transcripts, OERs) and applying per-source quality-based reweighting. The key conceptual departure from prior work is the demonstration that open-licensed text alone—without any unlicensed web data—can support competitive LLM pretraining at 1–2T token scale, a claim prior smaller datasets (0.85–3TB) could not substantiate.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that a fully open-licensed corpus can produce 7B LLMs competitive with budget-matched models trained on unlicensed data, directly addressing the growing legal and ethical pressure on LLM developers. The notable weakness on commonsense reasoning benchmarks (HellaSwag, PIQA) suggests that certain domain coverage (personal blogs, hobbies, sports) remains underrepresented in openly licensed sources, pointing to a concrete gap for future data collection. The 2T-token run, which required up to 16 passes over some sources, hints at diminishing returns and motivates the need for larger open-licensed corpora. As a 'v0.1' release, the paper frames this as a first step, leaving open whether scaling the dataset (and the models trained on it) can close the remaining performance gap with unlicensed-data models at larger budgets.
