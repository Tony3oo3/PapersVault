## Problem

All existing diffusion models rely on forward (Euler-Maruyama) discretizations of the reverse-time SDE, which require many sampling steps, are sensitive to hyperparameters, and degrade when the data distribution lacks regularity. This paper asks whether backward discretization—replacing the score with a proximal operator—can yield both faster convergence and more robust sampling, a question that had not been explored for diffusion processes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253160/cropped_page7_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method starts from a random Gaussian noise vector X_N ~ N(0, I) and iteratively applies proximal updates to produce a sample from the target distribution. For image generation, it operates on MNIST (28×28), CIFAR-10 (32×32), and CelebA-HQ (256×256 in a pretrained VAE latent space). Unlike score networks conditioned on a single time scalar t, the learned proximal network is conditioned on two scalars (t, λ), where λ encodes the regularization strength tied to the step size, enabling a single trained model to be used across different numbers of sampling steps.

## Method

ProxDM replaces the forward Euler-Maruyama discretization of the reverse-time SDE with a backward (implicit) discretization, which algebraically reduces to a proximal step: X_{k-1} = prox_{-λ_k ln p_{t_{k-1}}}[·]. Two variants are provided—a fully backward PDA and a hybrid PDA-hybrid that mixes forward and backward terms. To obtain the required proximal operators, the paper extends proximal matching (Fang et al., ICLR 2024) to a time- and regularization-dependent setting, training a U-Net parameterized as f_θ(x; t, λ) = x − √λ·ε_θ(x; t, λ) with a smoothed ℓ₀ loss whose sharpness ζ is annealed during training. Theoretically, the fully backward variant achieves Õ(d/√ε) steps for ε-accurate sampling in KL divergence, improving over the Õ(d/ε) rate of both the hybrid variant and standard score-based samplers, at the cost of a minimum step-size constraint (γ_k < 2).

## Results

On CIFAR-10, ProxDM's FID at 10 sampling steps matches that of the score ODE sampler at 20 steps, and ProxDM surpasses the score SDE sampler even at 100 steps. On CelebA-HQ (256×256), hybrid ProxDM achieves improved FID over the score SDE sampler and performs competitively with the score ODE sampler across all step counts. Theoretically, the full backward ProxDM requires only Õ(d/√ε) steps to achieve ε-accuracy in KL divergence, compared to Õ(d/ε) for the hybrid variant and existing score-based samplers. In the synthetic 2D experiment, both PDA variants converge faster than score-based methods with just 5 steps, while standard Euler-Maruyama without a final denoising step fails entirely.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares ProxDM against two score-based samplers from Song et al. [75]: the Euler-Maruyama SDE sampler and the Euler ODE (probability flow) sampler. On CIFAR-10, ProxDM clearly outperforms both baselines at low step counts: its FID at 10 NFE matches the score ODE at 20 NFE, and it surpasses the score SDE even at 100 NFE. When models are trained specifically for 5, 10, or 20 steps (dashed lines in Fig. 3), ProxDM's advantage over both score-based samplers becomes even more pronounced. On CelebA-HQ (256×256), hybrid ProxDM achieves improved FID over the score SDE sampler and is competitive with the score ODE sampler across all step counts. In the synthetic 2D experiment with exact operators, both PDA variants converge faster than the score-based sampler with fewer steps and require no additional hyperparameters (e.g., no tuning of the final denoising step size ε that the score-based method needs). The score SDE baseline's FID at 1000 steps on CIFAR-10 matches the value reported in the original DDPM paper [27], confirming a fair comparison.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper draws a direct analogy between backward discretization in optimization (proximal point method) and forward discretization (gradient descent) to propose the first backward-discretization-based sampler for diffusion models. It builds on the score-based diffusion framework of Song & Ermon [72] and Song et al. [75], replacing the MMSE score network with a learned proximal operator trained via the proximal matching loss of Fang et al. [23]. The theoretical analysis extends the convergence framework for the Proximal Langevin Algorithm [79] to the multi-step diffusion setting, and the practical implementation parallels the U-Net architectures and training protocols of Ho et al. [27].

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ProxDM offers a principled alternative to the forward Euler-Maruyama discretization that dominates all current diffusion samplers, with a provably better convergence rate (O(d/√ε) vs. O(d/ε) in KL divergence) and practical speedups of 5–10× in sampling steps on CIFAR10 without requiring distillation. Key open questions include incorporating the gap between learned and exact proximal operators into the convergence theory, resolving the observed FID degradation at large step counts (attributed to accumulated approximation errors in the implicit update), and extending the framework to other SDE/ODE formulations (variance-exploding, probability-flow ODE, flow matching). The connection between MAP-promoting losses and the proximal matching objective also opens a path to understanding recent empirical heuristics in consistency and shortcut models.
