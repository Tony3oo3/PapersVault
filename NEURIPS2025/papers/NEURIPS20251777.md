## Problem

Existing canonical coherence methods for measuring dependence between two clusters of multivariate time series assume stationarity, imposing constant dependence over time and ignoring transient, frequency-specific interactions. No prior work has extended classical canonical coherence to the wavelet domain to capture time-evolving, scale-specific coherence between groups of nonstationary multivariate signals, a gap that is critical in neuroscience where neural coordination shifts rapidly across frequency bands.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251777/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes two sets of multivariate time series of dimensions P and Q observed over T time points (e.g., P=4 and Q=5 hippocampal electrode channels sampled at 1000 Hz over 4 s per trial) and produces, for each wavelet scale j and rescaled time u∈(0,1), a scalar coherence value ρ_{j;XY}(u)∈[0,1] plus P- and Q-dimensional canonical direction vectors that decompose the global coherence into per-channel contributions. A lagged variant (Causal-WaveCanCoh) additionally accepts a lead-lag parameter h to assess directed dependence. The output is a full time-by-scale coherence surface rather than a single global summary, with estimation requiring a rectangular smoothing window M over the wavelet periodogram.

## Method

WaveCanCoh is built on the multivariate locally stationary wavelet (MvLSW) process, where the time-varying second-order structure is encoded in scale-specific local wavelet spectral (LWS) matrices S_j(u). At each scale j and time u, the method constructs the cross-group LWS matrix S_{j;ZZ}(u) from the fused (P+Q)-variate process, then forms the generalized eigenvalue problem via the matrix product M_{j;a}(u) = S_{j;XX}^{-1}(u) S_{j;XY}(u) S_{j;YY}^{-1}(u) S_{j;YX}(u); the largest eigenvalue Λ_j^{(1)}(u) is the WaveCanCoh value, and its eigenvectors yield the canonical direction vectors. Estimation proceeds by computing smoothed wavelet periodograms at each scale and time (using a rectangular kernel of half-width M), substituting them into the matrix product, and performing eigendecomposition. The key distinction from prior Fourier-based canonical coherence is that the LWS matrices are localized in both time and scale, so the coherence is a function of (j, u) rather than a single frequency ω, enabling detection of transient cross-group interactions that stationary methods average away. A Causal-WaveCanCoh extension incorporates a lead-lag h by shifting the Y-process in the cross-LWS construction, allowing directed coherence assessment at each scale and time.

## Results

On hippocampal LFP data (40 correct vs. 32 incorrect trials, rat Mitt), WaveCanCoh detects statistically significant differences in median canonical coherence between correct and incorrect trials at scales j=4–6 at t*=0.5 s (median differences of 0.023, 0.334, and 0.039; all p < 0.01) and at j=6, t*=1.0 s (p=0.025). In the MvLSW simulation (1000 replicates, P=6, Q=4, T=1024), WaveCanCoh accurately recovers the true time-varying coherence structure with 95% Wald confidence intervals tightly tracking the ground truth. In the AR(2) mixture simulation (500 replicates), WaveCanCoh captures the sharp coherence drop at t=T/2, while the Fourier-based LSP baseline fails to detect the transition.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares WaveCanCoh against a Fourier-based LSP method (time-varying Cramér representation with STFT-based localized spectra, following Dahlhaus [7] and Allen & Rabiner [1]). On the LFP data, WaveCanCoh identifies four statistically significant time-scale cells (p < 0.01) distinguishing correct from incorrect trials, concentrated at intermediate scales (j=4–6) post-stimulus, whereas LSP yields no significant cells (best p = 0.056 at j=4, t*=0.5 s; all others p > 0.1). In the AR(2) mixture simulation, both methods detect coherence in the first half of the signal, but only WaveCanCoh captures the sharp coherence drop in the second half, while LSP's smoothed Fourier approximation masks the transient change. The authors attribute LSP's failure to the temporal smoothing inherent in STFT-based estimation, which blurs rapid coherence transitions that wavelet localization preserves.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the classical canonical coherence framework (Hotelling 1936; Brillinger 2001) from the stationary spectral domain to the nonstationary wavelet domain, building directly on the multivariate locally stationary wavelet (MvLSW) process of Nason et al. (2000) and Park et al. (2014). While wavelet coherence methods exist for pairwise univariate channels or within-network analysis, and stationary canonical coherence methods (e.g., Viduarre et al. 2019, Talento et al. 2024) handle multivariate groups, no prior work had combined these two ideas to measure time-evolving, scale-specific coherence between two *groups* of multivariate signals. The paper also introduces a Causal-WaveCanCoh extension incorporating lead-lag structure, going beyond the contemporaneous-only formulation of classical canonical coherence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides the first principled, theoretically grounded tool for quantifying how functional coupling between groups of neural (or financial) signals evolves over time and across frequency bands, with a nonparametric permutation test for statistical inference. The LFP application demonstrates that correct vs. incorrect memory decisions are associated with distinct, scale-specific coherence dynamics that a Fourier-based baseline (LSP) completely fails to detect, underscoring the practical necessity of time-localized methods for transient neural interactions. Key limitations include the fixed dyadic wavelet scale structure (restricting arbitrary frequency targeting) and the MvLSW assumption, though the AR(2) mixture simulation shows reasonable robustness to its violation. Open questions include extending to adaptive or overcomplete wavelet bases for finer frequency resolution and scaling to larger electrode arrays.
