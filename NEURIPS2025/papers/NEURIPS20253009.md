## Problem

Foundation models trained with different objectives (CLIP, MAE, SAM, DINOv2) learn complementary representations, but existing adaptation strategies—whether parameter-efficient fine-tuning or probing—operate on individual models and do not exploit cross-model complementarity. Probing-based adapters, which avoid backpropagation through frozen backbones, suffer from poor scaling to dense feature maps (relying on task-specific average pooling) and require dataset-specific hyperparameter tuning, limiting their practicality for combining multiple large models.

## Input / Output

ComBo consumes intermediate feature maps (one per transformer block, 12 per ViT-B model) from multiple frozen backbones simultaneously, interpolating them to a common spatial resolution and normalising across token and embedding dimensions. It outputs a class label via a linear head on the transformer's class token, and optionally produces per-backbone importance scores (L2 norms of projection weight columns) for task-relevance ranking. No backpropagation flows through any backbone; only the adapter's ~1.7M parameters are updated.

## Method

ComBo uses a two-stage design that separates layer-wise feature selection from spatial integration. First, an affine projection Λ compresses the stacked 768-dim embeddings from all layers of all K models at each spatial position into a compact D′-dim token, preserving the 2D spatial layout without any pooling. Second, a small transformer encoder (6 blocks, 128-dim embedding, 2 heads) processes these compressed tokens alongside a learnable class token to produce task-specific predictions. Training jointly optimises the projection, class token, and transformer with a fixed hyperparameter schedule (AdamW, lr=0.001, 100 epochs) across all VTAB-1k tasks, eliminating the dataset-specific tuning required by Head2Toe and SMP. For model selection, an L2 regularisation term on the projection weights per backbone yields importance scores that rank backbones by task-relevance, enabling selective probing of only the most relevant subset.

## Results

On the VTAB-1k benchmark (19 tasks), ComBo probing all four foundation models simultaneously achieves 78.2% global average accuracy, and selecting only the top two models via the task-relevance scoring mechanism improves this to 78.6%. This outperforms the distillation-based RADIOv2.5 + Adapter+ baseline (77.5%) and the best individual model probed with ComBo (DINOv2 at 77.9%). On structured tasks specifically, ComBo (top two) reaches 65.3% vs. 62.1% for RADIOv2.5, highlighting the advantage of preserving spatial information without average pooling.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ComBo outperforms both prior probing methods: Head2Toe (70.9% global avg) and SMP (73.6%), with the largest gains on structured tasks (e.g., DMLab: 89.0% vs. 44.0% for Head2Toe and 72.5% for SMP). Against tuning-based methods on a single backbone (Table 1), ComBo (74.6%) trails Adapter+ (77.6%), LoRA (75.6%), and VPT-Deep (76.4%), as expected since probing learns feature combinations from scratch without backpropagating through the backbone. However, in the multi-model setting (Table 2), ComBo with top-two model selection (78.6%) surpasses RADIOv2.5 + Adapter+ (77.5%), a distillation-based approach that required 64 GPUs and 614M training examples. When probing individually tuned backbones (Table 3), ComBo (79.3%) matches Multi-Adapter+ (78.7%) while requiring only forward passes through frozen models, and approaches the best single tuned model (DINOv2 + Adapter+ at 79.9%). The task-relevance scoring mechanism further improves efficiency: selecting the optimal per-task subset of models (best per-task top-n) reaches 78.1% on the validation set, approaching the exhaustive-search upper bound of 78.6%.

## Relation to Prior Work

ComBo extends the probing-based adapter lineage established by Head2Toe (Evci et al., ICML 2022) and SMP (Wu et al., CVPR 2024), which demonstrated the value of extracting features from multiple layers of a single frozen model. The key departure is generalizing from single-model to multi-model probing: where prior methods required task-specific average pooling to manage feature dimensionality, ComBo uses a learned affine projection followed by a lightweight transformer to compress and integrate features from multiple backbones simultaneously without discarding spatial information. The paper also positions itself against distillation-based model merging (SAM-CLIP, RADIOv2.5) and parameter-efficient tuning (Adapter+, LoRA), arguing that probing avoids backpropagation through large backbones and offers greater flexibility for incorporating new models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ComBo provides a practical, low-compute pathway for practitioners to combine multiple large foundation models without the memory and training costs of backpropagation or distillation, making multi-model adaptation accessible on consumer hardware. The task-relevance scoring mechanism adds a useful tool for model selection that approaches exhaustive-search performance. However, ComBo still trails Adapter+ in absolute accuracy, is validated only on ViT-B architectures and VTAB-1k's low-data classification setting, and the optimal number of models to include remains unclear without per-task evaluation. Open questions include scalability to larger models (ViT-L, ViT-H), dense prediction tasks, and whether the affine projection bottleneck limits performance as the number of input models grows.
