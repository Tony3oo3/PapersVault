## Problem

Existing separation-oracle-based projection-free algorithms for Online Convex Optimization achieve regret bounds that scale linearly (or polynomially) with the asphericity κ = R/r of the feasible set, which can be arbitrarily large for ill-conditioned geometry. Prior work either requires expensive isotropic reparametrization of K (costing Õ(d⁴) separation calls) or leaves a residual κd term in the regret. This paper eliminates the need for such preprocessing and reduces the κ-dependence to logarithmic.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes a convex feasible set K ⊂ ℝ^d only through a separation oracle (no explicit projection or membership queries), a subgradient g_t ∈ ∂f_t(w_t) per round, an initial feasible point c ∈ K, and the bounding radii r, R from Assumption 2.2. It outputs a sequence of feasible iterates w_t ∈ K with a linearized regret of Õ(√(dT) + d²) and a per-round computational cost of Õ(C_sep + d^ω)·log(TR/r), where C_sep is one separation oracle call and ω ≈ 2.372 is the matrix-multiplication exponent. Notably, the algorithm requires only Õ(1) separation oracle calls per round, matching the oracle complexity of prior separation-based methods.

## Method

The core mechanism reduces OCO over K to OCO over a sequence of dynamically updated ellipsoids E_t = E(c_t, H_t) that always contain K, with an inner Online Newton Step (ONS) algorithm performing generalized projections onto these ellipsoids. At each round, the algorithm computes an approximate gauge-distance subgradient s_t via binary search with the separation oracle; if ‖H_t^{1/2} s_t‖ > 2d, this signals that K is 'thin' in some direction relative to E_t, triggering an ellipsoid update that shifts the center and shrinks the shape along that direction (reducing volume by a factor of e^{-1/(8d)}, analogous to the classical ellipsoid method). Because each update shrinks volume and K has non-empty interior, at most O(d² log(R/r)) updates occur, and on all non-update rounds the critical inner product |⟨u_t − u, s_t⟩| is bounded by O(d) independently of κ. This κ-independent bound on non-update rounds, combined with ONS's logarithmic dependence on the feedback norm B = max_t ‖g̃_t‖, is what yields the final regret with only logarithmic κ-dependence.

## Results

The paper's main result is a regret bound of Õ(√(dT) + d²) for OCO over a convex set accessed via a separation oracle, with only logarithmic dependence on the set's asphericity κ = R/r. For stochastic convex optimization, the corresponding convergence rate is Õ(σ√(d/T) + d²/T), reducing to Õ(d²/T) in the noiseless offline setting. The algorithm requires only Õ(1) separation oracle calls per round, with per-round computational cost Õ(C_sep + d^ω), where ω ≈ 2.372 is the matrix multiplication exponent.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Hazan & Kale [12] (Frank-Wolfe with linear optimization oracle), this paper improves the T-dependence from O(T^{3/4}) to Õ(√T) at the cost of using a separation oracle instead of a linear optimization oracle. Against Mhammedi [18] and Garber & Kretzu [8], which achieve O(κ√T) regret, this paper eliminates the linear dependence on asphericity κ, replacing it with only a logarithmic factor hidden in the Õ(·) notation. The most direct comparison is with the author's own prior work [19], which achieves Õ(√(dT) + κd); this paper removes the κd term entirely, substituting a d² term that is independent of κ. The trade-off is a slightly higher per-round runtime (d^ω vs. d²), though the paper argues this is dominated by the separation oracle cost C_sep in typical polytope settings where m ≥ d. No empirical comparisons are provided, as the paper is purely theoretical.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the separation-oracle-based projection-free OCO line initiated by Mhammedi [18] and Garber & Kretzu [8] (O(κ√T) regret) and refined by Mhammedi [19] (Õ(√(dT)+κd)), by replacing the fixed enclosing ball used in those reductions with a sequence of dynamically updated ellipsoids inspired by the classical ellipsoid method. The gauge-projection framework and the ONS inner algorithm are carried over from [19], but the key departure is the adaptive ellipsoid update rule: when the feasible set is detected to be 'thin' in some direction (‖H_t^{1/2} s_t‖ > 2d), the ellipsoid shrinks and re-centers, progressively tightening around K. This eliminates the need for the expensive isotropic preprocessing (Õ(d⁴) separation calls) that prior work required to control κ.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The logarithmic dependence on asphericity κ removes a fundamental scalability barrier for OCO over ill-conditioned feasible sets, making separation-oracle-based methods practical in settings where κ is arbitrarily large. The d² additive term (independent of T and κ) represents a clean trade-off: the algorithm adapts to geometry on the fly rather than paying a one-time preprocessing cost. Open questions include whether the d² term can be tightened, whether lower bounds matching the Õ(√(dT)+d²) guarantee exist, and whether the dynamic-ellipsoid idea extends to other oracle models (e.g., membership oracles) or to non-convex settings. The absence of experimental validation leaves practical performance untested.
