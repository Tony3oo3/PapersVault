## Problem

Existing KV cache retrieval methods for LLM inference rely on linear LSH (random hyperplanes) to identify important tokens, but queries and keys in LLMs form nearly orthogonal cones in embedding space, causing linear hashing to produce degenerate codes and necessitating extremely long hash codes (1024+ bits) for adequate retrieval accuracy. This makes the approach both storage-inefficient and computationally expensive for the already-burdensome KV cache.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252883/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each decoding step, the method takes the current query vector (1×d) and the full accumulated KV cache (n×d keys and values from pre-filling and prior steps). It produces a 128-bit hash code per query and per key (via a per-head, per-layer MLP), then retrieves the top-k KV entries by NXOR-based Hamming distance, yielding a sparse attention output computed over only the selected subset. The 128-bit code is roughly 5× shorter than MagicPIG's minimum of 720 bits, and the entire retrieval for 512K tokens completes in under 100 μs on a single A100.

## Method

Spotlight Attention replaces MagicPIG's random linear projection with a two-layer MLP (128-dim input/hidden/output, SiLU activation) whose sign outputs form the hash code, creating curved decision boundaries that better partition the cone-shaped query/key distributions. Training uses a Bradley-Terry ranking loss that supervises only the separation between top-k and non-top-k score sets (not internal ordering), with a soft sign function (γ=64) to make the otherwise non-differentiable sign operation gradient-friendly. The LLM backbone remains frozen; only the per-head, per-layer MLP parameters are optimized on 8,192 calibration samples in ~8 hours on 16 GB GPUs. At inference, specialized CUDA kernels perform bit-packing (32 booleans → uint32) and NXOR GEMM with popcount, enabling the full hash-code retrieval pipeline to run in under 100 μs for 512K-token contexts and delivering up to 3× end-to-end throughput over vanilla decoding.

## Results

Spotlight Attention achieves KV retrieval IoU of 0.34–0.42 after training (vs. 0.13–0.20 for linear LSH), with perplexity within 0.1–0.3 of the oracle top-k upper bound across LLaMA2-7B, LLaMA3-8B, and Qwen2.5-7B. On language modeling (PG19/ProofPile/CodeParrot), Spotlight at 98% pruning matches Quest's performance at 75% pruning—a 10× smaller token budget—e.g., LLaMA2-7B PG19 PPL 7.11 vs. Quest's 7.12. On LongBench, Spotlight (163-token budget) achieves RougeL similarity of 0.58 to the vanilla model, surpassing Quest (1024 tokens, 0.56) and MagicPIG (0.44). End-to-end throughput reaches up to 3× vanilla decoding on Qwen2.5-7B, with 512K-token hash retrieval in under 100 μs on a single A100.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Quest, Spotlight Attention matches or exceeds performance with a dramatically smaller token budget: at 98% pruning (163 tokens for LLaMA3-8B), Spotlight achieves PG19 PPL of 8.90, comparable to Quest at only 75% pruning (1024 tokens, PPL 9.91). On LongBench QA, Spotlight (163 tokens) reaches RougeL 0.58 vs. Quest (1024 tokens) at 0.56, meaning Spotlight retrieves 6× fewer tokens while producing more faithful outputs. Against MagicPIG, Spotlight outperforms on perplexity (9.87 vs. 12.65 on PG19 for LLaMA3-8B at comparable compression) and critically does not require local windows or sink tokens—MagicPIG's PPL degrades to 50.96 without them, while Spotlight remains stable at 9.99. Spotlight also uses 128-bit hash codes versus MagicPIG's minimum 720 bits, a 5× reduction that enables sub-100 μs retrieval for 512K contexts. On Needle-in-a-Haystack, Spotlight matches the vanilla model's retrieval accuracy without any auxiliary mechanisms, whereas MagicPIG relies on its local window and sink tokens to achieve comparable results.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends MagicPIG (NeurIPS 2024), which introduced token-level KV cache retrieval via linear Locality-Sensitive Hashing, by replacing the random hyperplane partitioning with a learned two-layer MLP hashing function. The motivation builds on MagicPIG's own observation that LLM queries and keys occupy nearly orthogonal cones in embedding space, which fundamentally limits linear hashing efficiency. The training objective adapts the Bradley-Terry pairwise ranking loss (originally from the paired-comparisons literature) to the specific problem of separating top-k from non-top-k attention scores, rather than the reconstruction or language-modeling losses that would be more natural choices. Quest (ICML 2024) serves as the block-level alternative that Spotlight Attention outperforms at equivalent or smaller token budgets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 5× reduction in hash code length (128 bits vs. 1024+ bits) and the resulting 3× end-to-end throughput gain make token-level KV cache retrieval practically deployable, removing a key barrier that kept MagicPIG's approach from widespread adoption. However, the retrieval IoU plateaus around 40%, meaning the method still misses a significant fraction of truly important tokens, and the per-head, per-layer MLPs introduce non-trivial parameter overhead that scales with model size. Open questions include whether the non-linear hashing ceiling can be raised with deeper architectures or better training signals, whether the approach generalizes to multimodal or extremely long (1M+) contexts, and whether the lightweight training recipe (8K samples, 16GB GPU) remains sufficient as base models evolve.
