## Problem

Existing speculative decoding methods like EAGLE and HASS suffer from token misalignment: during training the draft model sees ground-truth tokens and target-model features, but during decoding it must rely on its own previously generated tokens and features. This discrepancy compounds across multiple forward passes, causing EAGLE-2 to reach a 48% token misalignment rate and limiting acceptance length, yet prior work has largely ignored this token-level issue in favor of addressing only feature misalignment.

## Input / Output

The draft model receives a sequence of ground-truth prompt tokens and their corresponding target-LLM hidden features (precomputed and stored) as input, and autoregressively produces a sequence of draft tokens along with their predicted hidden features for each forward pass. At decoding time, the draft model instead conditions on its own previously generated tokens and features, and the resulting draft tokens are verified in parallel by the target LLM in a single forward pass, accepting a prefix that matches the target's predictions.

## Method

GRIFFIN introduces two complementary components. First, a Token-Alignable Training (TAT) strategy organizes training into N progressive steps where the draft model increasingly conditions on its own outputs; a top-k predictable mask identifies tokens whose ground-truth label appears in the draft model's top-k predictions, and a cumulative alignment mask propagates rejection signals forward so that tokens downstream of any misaligned position are excluded from the loss. Second, a Token-Alignable Draft Model (TAD) adds a Token-Guided Fusion (TGF) module that performs a three-step fusion—concatenating and projecting features with token embeddings, expanding to 4d with layer normalization, then refining through SiLU and a down-projection with residual connection—plus a Token-Enhanced Head (TEH) that decouples token-prediction and feature-regression objectives into separate output heads. Together, TAT ensures the training signal only comes from positions the draft model can actually reach during decoding, while TGF/TEH reduce the feature gap that causes token errors in the first place.

## Results

GRIFFIN achieves the highest average speedup ratio (SR) and acceptance length (τ) across all tested LLMs and tasks. On LLaMA2-Chat 7B (T=0), GRIFFIN reaches SR=3.28 and τ=5.44, outperforming HASS (SR=3.17, τ=5.26) by 3.5% in SR and 3.4% in τ, and EAGLE-2 (SR=2.89, τ=4.82) by 13.5% in SR and 12.9% in τ. On Vicuna-1.5 7B (T=0), GRIFFIN achieves the strongest absolute performance with SR=4.23 and τ=5.76. Across all models, GRIFFIN delivers an average acceptance length improvement of over 8% and a speedup ratio exceeding 7% compared to the prior state-of-the-art HASS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GRIFFIN consistently outperforms all eight baselines across every model and task evaluated. Against the closest competitor HASS [13], GRIFFIN achieves 3.5–7.3% higher SR and 3.4–7.3% higher τ depending on the model (e.g., +3.5% SR on LLaMA2-7B, +7.3% SR on Vicuna-7B HumanEval at T=0). Against EAGLE-2 [9], the gap is larger: GRIFFIN improves SR by 13.5% on LLaMA2-7B and 18.8% on LLaMA3-8B, with τ gains of 12.9% and 18.5% respectively. GRIFFIN also surpasses EAGLE-3 on LLaMA3-8B by 3.7% SR and 4.5% τ at T=0. Against FSPAD [31], GRIFFIN leads by 6.8–10.5% in SR across LLaMA2-7B/13B and LLaMA3-8B. The retrieval-based baselines (PLD [20], Lookahead [21]) and the simpler methods (SPS [6], Medusa [10]) are substantially outperformed, with GRIFFIN achieving roughly 2× the SR of SPS and Medusa on Vicuna-7B. On the MoE architecture (Mixtral-8x7B), where all speculative methods face additional verification overhead, GRIFFIN still maintains a 6.6% SR advantage over HASS and a 17.6% advantage over EAGLE-2.

## Relation to Prior Work

GRIFFIN extends the EAGLE/EAGLE-2 line of feature-level autoregressive draft models and the HASS harmonized training paradigm, both of which address feature misalignment between training and decoding. Its key conceptual departure is identifying token misalignment—where draft tokens diverge from ground-truth tokens used during training—as a distinct and previously underexplored bottleneck that compounds across multi-pass decoding. The two proposed mechanisms (top-k loss masking during training and Token-Guided Fusion in the draft architecture) are designed to jointly mitigate this token-level discrepancy, complementing rather than replacing the feature-alignment strategies in EAGLE and HASS.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Speculative decoding is a primary technique for reducing LLM inference latency, and the identified token misalignment (reaching 48% in EAGLE-2) explains why prior methods plateau under deeper multi-pass training—a practical limitation for anyone pushing beyond 3 forward passes. GRIFFIN's lossless 7–18% speedup gains over SOTA across diverse architectures (dense and MoE) make it a strong candidate for production inference stacks. Open questions include whether the multi-step training overhead becomes prohibitive for very large models, how the approach generalizes to non-autoregressive or streaming decoding settings, and whether the top-k masking strategy can be further adapted for batched or continuous-batching serving environments.
