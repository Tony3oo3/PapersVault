## Problem

Classical conformal prediction relies on nonconformity scores over structured output spaces (intervals, finite class sets), but generative models like LLMs expose only a query oracle over an immense, unstructured space of discrete sequences, making standard prediction sets intractable to represent. This creates a fundamental trade-off between coverage validity, prediction set informativeness, and test-time query cost that existing conformal language modeling methods do not explicitly optimize, particularly when target coverage exceeds the model's few-shot accuracy.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt, a black-box LLM serving as a query oracle (sampled sequentially, one output at a time), and a calibration set of (prompt, ground-truth answer) pairs from the true data distribution. It produces a prediction set drawn from the finite set of sampled outputs plus an abstract 'EE' (Everything Else) label representing all unseen outputs, with a distribution-free guarantee P(Y ∈ C(X)) ≥ 1−α. Lexically varied but semantically equivalent generations are first grouped into clusters via LLaMA-3-8B entailment checks, so each cluster corresponds to one label in the discrete output space used for missing-mass estimation.

## Method

CPQ (Conformal Prediction with Query Oracle) is built on two principles, both rooted in the classical missing mass problem. Principle 1 (optimal query policy): query the oracle sequentially until a novel estimator of the missing-mass derivative, Δ̂(x,t) = −2N₂/t² (where N₂ is the count of labels appearing exactly twice), falls below a calibrated threshold β*, derived by revisiting the Good-Turing generative process. Principle 2 (optimal set map): construct prediction sets by thresholding a conformity score S(x,y) that assigns seen labels the Good-Turing probability 1−ω̂(y|x) and the EE label the score 2−θ̂(x,t) where θ̂ = N₁/t is the Good-Turing missing mass estimate; the threshold q* is set as the (1−α)-quantile of scores on held-out calibration data via split conformal. The two modules are decoupled: β* is tuned on one calibration split to meet a user-specified average query budget B, while q* is calibrated on a second split, yielding a finite-sample, distribution-free coverage guarantee that holds even when the oracle distribution π(y|x) differs from the true p(y|x).

## Results

The primary metric is the EE fraction (fraction of prediction sets containing the fallback 'Everything Else' label, where lower is more informative) while maintaining valid coverage. On GSM8K at 95% nominal coverage, CPQ achieves an EE fraction of 16% versus 70% for CLM and 61% for SCOPE-Gen, while matching the target empirical coverage (95% vs. 93% for both baselines). On BBH Geometric Shapes at 60% coverage, CPQ reduces EE to 7% compared to 40% (CLM) and 38% (SCOPE-Gen). On BBH Date Understanding at 70% coverage, CPQ achieves 25% EE versus 32% (CLM) and 51% (SCOPE-Gen), all under matched average query budgets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CPQ is compared against two conformal language modeling baselines: CLM (Quach et al., 2024) and SCOPE-Gen (Kladny et al., 2025), both evaluated under matched average query budgets. CPQ wins decisively on the EE fraction metric across all three main datasets: on GSM8K at 95% coverage, CPQ achieves 16% EE versus 70% for CLM and 61% for SCOPE-Gen; on Geometric Shapes at 60% coverage, CPQ reaches 7% EE versus 40% and 38% respectively; and on Date Understanding at 70% coverage, CPQ achieves 25% versus 32% and 51%. CPQ also maintains tighter empirical coverage closer to the nominal target (e.g., 95% vs. 93% on GSM8K), whereas SCOPE-Gen tends to overshoot coverage (e.g., 78% empirical at 70% nominal on Date) at the cost of higher EE fractions. The paper notes that neither baseline accounts for the missing mass over unseen labels or explicitly controls query budget, which are the two dimensions CPQ addresses through its missing-mass-based query policy and conformity score.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the conformal prediction framework (Vovk et al.) from structured output spaces (classification, regression) to a query-only setting where the generative model acts as a black-box oracle providing only samples. It departs from recent conformal language modeling methods (CLM, SCOPE-Gen) that calibrate stopping rules over sampled outputs but do not account for the residual probability mass over unseen labels or explicitly optimize query budget allocation. The key conceptual bridge is to the classical Good-Turing missing mass estimator (Good, 1953), which the authors repurpose in two distinct roles: the missing mass itself governs the optimal conformity score for set construction, while a novel estimator for its derivative (based on doubleton counts rather than singletons) governs the optimal adaptive query policy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides the first principled, distribution-free method for constructing calibrated prediction sets from black-box generative models under an explicit query budget, addressing a gap where prior methods either fail at high coverage levels or waste queries uniformly. The theoretical connection between optimal querying and the missing mass derivative is of independent statistical interest and could inform adaptive sampling in other sequential decision problems. Limitations include reliance on missing mass estimation (challenging in very low-query regimes), the need for semantic clustering of text outputs, and the decoupled analysis that approximates rather than solves the joint optimization. Open questions include extending to non-i.i.d. or context-dependent settings, connecting the framework to conformal abstention, and handling structured or multi-turn outputs.
