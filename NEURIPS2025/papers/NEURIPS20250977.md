## Problem

Existing VLM guard models (e.g., LLaMA Guard 3-Vision, Beaver-Guard-V) output only fixed-category classification labels without any reasoning, limiting their interpretability, generalization to new harm categories, and overall accuracy. Moreover, they are typically trained offline on limited modalities, and no prior work has combined deliberative chain-of-thought reasoning with reinforcement learning to build a multimodal VLM guard that handles text, image, and text-image inputs uniformly.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250977/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The guard model receives a user prompt in one of three modalities (plain text, a single RGB image, or a text-image pair) together with the victim VLM's text response, and produces a structured output consisting of a multi-step reasoning trace enclosed in think tags followed by two binary moderation labels (prompt harmfulness and response harmfulness) enclosed in result tags. The base model is Qwen2.5-VL-Instruct at 3B or 7B scale, with a 2048-token cutoff length during training. A token-economical variant (GuardReasoner-VL-Eco) constrains reasoning length via a β=1/6 cutoff in the reward, reducing output tokens by ~12–14% at a 1–2% F1 cost.

## Method

GuardReasoner-VL is trained in two stages. First, a 123K-sample, 631K-reasoning-step corpus (GuardReasoner-VLTrain) spanning text, image, and text-image modalities is synthesized by prompting GPT-4o, and the base VLM is cold-started via Reasoning SFT. Second, online RL with GRPO (KL term omitted) further sharpens moderation reasoning using three key mechanisms: (1) safety-aware data concatenation, which pairs two samples, concatenates their texts and merges their images, and labels the result harmful if either original is harmful, forcing the model to detect hidden harm amid benign content; (2) a dynamic clipping parameter B_s = ∏((s_total−i)/s_total)·ε that starts large to encourage exploration and shrinks over training to promote exploitation; and (3) a length-aware safety reward r = (−1 + r_safety)/min(l_norm, β)² that is non-positive, so the model is incentivized to extend its reasoning only when it has not yet answered both moderation tasks correctly, while β caps over-thinking. Rejection sampling over the SFT model (keeping only samples where all 4 high-temperature rollouts are wrong) supplies the hard training set for RL.

## Results

GuardReasoner-VL 7B achieves the best Average(All) F1 score of 79.07% on prompt harmfulness detection across 8 benchmarks (text, image, and text-image), surpassing the runner-up VLM guard (Qwen2.5-VL-Instruct 7B at 56.53%) by 22.54 points. On response harmfulness detection, GuardReasoner-VL 7B reaches 77.58% Average(All) F1, outperforming the runner-up (Qwen2.5-VL-Instruct 7B at 61.58%) by 16.00 points. Overall, the model surpasses the runner-up by 19.27% F1 on average across both tasks. The token-efficient variant GuardReasoner-VL-Eco 7B achieves 77.49% and 76.94% Average(All) F1 on the two tasks while reducing output tokens by ~13.5%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GuardReasoner-VL 7B dominates all VLM guard baselines on both tasks. On prompt detection, it outperforms Qwen2.5-VL-Instruct 7B (56.53%) by 22.54 points and LLaMA Guard 3 Vision 11B (48.03%) by 31.04 points on Average(All) F1; on response detection, it beats Qwen2.5-VL-Instruct 7B (61.58%) by 16.00 points and LLaMA Guard 3 Vision 11B (54.01%) by 23.57 points. On text-only benchmarks, GuardReasoner-VL 7B (79.88% prompt, 79.42% response) is competitive with the best LLM guard models—GuardReasoner 8B (81.09% prompt, 81.22% response) and WildGuard 7B (77.99% prompt, 77.95% response)—while also handling image and text-image modalities that LLM guards cannot process (scoring 0% on HarmImageTest and SPA-VL-Eval). The API-based baselines (OpenAI Moderation, Azure Content Safety) perform poorly on text (35.28% and 54.30% prompt F1) and are far behind on all modalities. The key differentiator is that LLM guard models score 0% on image and text-image benchmarks, capping their Average(All) at ~40%, whereas GuardReasoner-VL achieves 79%+ across all three modalities simultaneously.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends GuardReasoner [49], which introduced deliberative reasoning for LLM guard models, into the vision-language model domain by constructing a multimodal reasoning corpus and applying online RL. The RL framework is built on GRPO [70] (DeepSeekMath), modified with a dynamic clipping schedule and a length-aware safety reward to balance accuracy against token cost. It departs from prior VLM guard models (LLaMA Guard 3-Vision, VLMGuard, Beaver-Guard-V) that output only fixed-category classification labels, instead producing an explicit chain-of-thought before the moderation decision, which the authors argue improves interpretability and generalization to unseen harm categories.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 19.27% average F1 gap over the runner-up VLM guard model is substantial for a safety-critical deployment setting, and the released 3B/7B models plus 123K-sample reasoning corpus lower the barrier for building interpretable multimodal guardrails. However, the reasoning overhead (≈210 output tokens per moderation call) remains a practical concern for high-throughput moderation, and the paper acknowledges that performance on indirect or agentic attacks (e.g., computer-use agents) is untested. Open questions include whether the safety-aware data concatenation generalizes beyond pairwise merging, whether the dynamic clipping schedule is optimal versus simpler alternatives, and how the approach scales to video or multi-turn conversational guardrails.
