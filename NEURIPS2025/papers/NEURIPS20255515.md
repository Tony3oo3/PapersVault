## Problem

Generic EEG foundation models trained on heterogeneous multi-task data struggle with complex, nuanced tasks like emotion recognition due to inter-task representation mismatch and dilution of task-relevant signals. Existing cross-dataset emotion recognition methods rely on one-to-one dataset adaptation and subject-specific fine-tuning, failing to generalize to unseen datasets or emotion categories. This paper tackles the combined challenges of large inter-dataset distribution shifts, inconsistent emotion label definitions, and substantial inter-subject variability through a unified task-specific pre-training framework.

## Input / Output

The method ingests multi-channel EEG time series from heterogeneous datasets (32–62 channels, 250–1000 Hz sampling, 2–9 emotion categories), which are interpolated to a standardized 60-channel 10-20 layout, downsampled to 125 Hz, and segmented into 5-second windows with 2-second stride. The encoder outputs spatiotemporal representations that are temporally averaged, smoothed via a linear dynamical system, and fed to a lightweight MLP for discrete emotion classification; in the zero-shot setting, the same representations support nearest-neighbor retrieval without any fine-tuning.

## Method

mdJPT combines a Mamba-like linear attention (MLLA) channel encoder—processing strided patches per channel with input gates, linear attention, and forget gates for long-range temporal dynamics—with a spatiotemporal dynamics model that applies dilated spatial-transition convolutions and dynamic local attention to capture inter-channel co-activation patterns. Training is label-free and driven by two complementary losses: a Cross-Dataset Alignment (CDA) loss that minimizes pairwise Frobenius distances between subject-level covariance centroids of the spatially projected latent space across all datasets, and an Inter-Subject Alignment (ISA) contrastive loss that pulls together EEG segments from two subjects viewing the same stimulus while pushing apart segments from different stimuli, using a temperature-scaled cross-entropy over cosine similarities. The key insight is that EEG second-order statistics (inter-channel covariance) are alignable across datasets via simple transformations, enabling robust cross-dataset and cross-subject generalization without explicit emotion labels or per-subject calibration.

## Results

In the few-shot cross-dataset subject-independent setting (leave-one-dataset-out), mdJPT achieves an average AUROC of 79.96% and accuracy of 56.22% across six emotion EEG datasets, outperforming the best generic pre-trained baseline (MMM) by 4.57% in AUROC and 1.68% in accuracy. In the zero-shot generalization setting (no fine-tuning), mdJPT achieves 72.47% on SEED, 73.34% on DEAP, and 17.44% on FACED, improving average accuracy by 11.92% (40% relative) over the best comparison method (EEGPT). Performance scales with the number of pre-training datasets: using all five source datasets yields an 8.55% accuracy gain over the best single-dataset pre-training configuration.

## Baselines & Comparisons

mdJPT is compared against three generic pre-trained EEG foundation models: MMM (Yi et al., NeurIPS 2023), LaBraM (Jiang et al., 2024), and EEGPT (Wang et al., NeurIPS 2024). In the few-shot cross-dataset setting, mdJPT outperforms all three baselines on every metric across all six datasets, with the largest margins on SEED-VII (43.93% vs. 30.29% accuracy for MMM, a 13.64-point gap) and SEED-V (65.02% vs. 58.64% for MMM). On average, mdJPT surpasses the best baseline (MMM) by 4.57% in AUROC and 1.68% in accuracy. In the zero-shot setting, the advantage is even more pronounced: mdJPT achieves 72.47% on SEED versus 55.42% for EEGPT (the best baseline), and 73.34% on DEAP versus 67.04% for LaBraM, yielding an average 11.92% accuracy improvement (40% relative). Notably, on the challenging 9-class FACED dataset, all baselines perform near chance level (~11%), while mdJPT reaches 17.44%, the only method above chance. The model also has fewer trainable parameters (1.0M) than the comparison foundation models, making it more compact while delivering superior emotion-specific performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself between two existing paradigms in EEG: generic large-scale foundation models (LaBraM, EEGPT, MMM) that pre-train on heterogeneous multi-task data, and one-to-one cross-dataset adaptation methods (PESD, E2STN, JCFA) that require per-target fine-tuning. It departs from both by proposing task-specific multi-dataset joint pre-training (mdJPT), where multiple emotion EEG datasets are co-trained with a shared encoder using label-free alignment losses. Architecturally, it adapts the Mamba-like linear attention (MLLA) backbone from Han et al. (18) for channel-wise temporal encoding and repurposes the dynamic attention mechanism from Shen et al. (20) for spatiotemporal modeling, while the CDA loss draws on covariance alignment techniques from Mellot et al. (16, 17) for M/EEG harmonization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a compact 1.0M-parameter task-specific model can outperform 4.7–5.8M-parameter generic EEG foundation models by 4.57% AUROC (few-shot) and 11.92% accuracy (zero-shot), challenging the assumption that broader, larger-scale pre-training is always superior for complex affective tasks. The zero-shot result of 73.3% on DEAP—surpassing fine-tuned models—suggests that covariance and contrastive alignment can encode emotion-discriminative structure without any target-domain labels. Key open questions remain: performance on fine-grained 9-class FACED is still near-chance (23.5%), generalization to non-video paradigms (imagery) is weak (20.6%), and the framework has not been validated on diverse demographics or clinical populations, limiting near-term deployment.
