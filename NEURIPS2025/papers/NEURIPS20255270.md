## Problem

Pre-trained vision-language models such as CLIP are highly vulnerable to imperceptible adversarial perturbations, yet existing adversarial prompt tuning defenses overfit to in-distribution adversarial samples and degrade natural generalization, particularly on out-of-distribution or unseen tasks. The core gap is the inability of current methods to simultaneously preserve natural generalization, achieve strong adversarial robustness, and adapt to task-specific distributions without benchmark-specific hyperparameter tuning.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images that first pass through a real-time adaptive total-variation regularization (Adaptive-FGP) to suppress high-frequency adversarial perturbations while preserving edge structures, alongside extended descriptive text templates with class labels. The frozen natural CLIP processes the original clean image and text to produce reference latent embeddings, while the robust CLIP (equipped with deep multimodal prompts and low-rank residual modules) processes the TV-regularized adversarial image. Output is a class prediction derived from cosine similarity scores between the robust CLIP's image embedding and text embeddings of all candidate categories, with the natural and adversarial similarity distributions explicitly aligned via Rényi divergence.

## Method

CoAPT introduces a collaborative framework where a frozen natural CLIP guides a target robust CLIP through three coupled mechanisms. First, a novel real-time Adaptive-FGP algorithm performs spatially adaptive TV regularization—using Sobel-based edge magnitude to modulate regularization strength (stronger in flat regions, weaker at edges) combined with Nesterov acceleration and a cosine-similarity-based adaptive restart—to compress the adversarial perturbation space by smoothing high-frequency details. Second, a Gaussian RBF reconstruction loss aligns the robust CLIP's visual and language embeddings with the natural CLIP's clean embeddings in latent space, restoring generalization features corrupted by both the adversarial perturbation and the HF suppression. Third, LoRA-inspired low-rank residual modules (I + η·BA) provide a compact subspace for task-specific adaptation, while an α-order Rényi divergence loss explicitly minimizes the discrepancy between natural and adversarial prediction distributions. Deep multimodal adversarial prompts (length 4, depth 9) are jointly optimized in both visual and textual transformer branches, with only prompt parameters φ and low-rank parameters φ updated via gradient descent while all CLIP backbone weights remain frozen.

## Results

Across 15 datasets and four benchmarks (few-shot, base-to-novel, zero-shot, OOD), CoAPT improves natural and adversarial robustness by an average of 9.83% and 24.16% over the strongest baseline (FAP). On the base-to-novel benchmark, CoAPT achieves 71.31% natural harmonic mean and 60.16% robust harmonic mean, surpassing FAP (58.22% / 27.77%) by +13.09% and +32.39% respectively. In zero-shot evaluation on ImageNet, CoAPT reaches 63.42% natural accuracy (vs. 62.10% for Vanilla CLIP) and 51.18% robust accuracy (vs. 1.57% for CLIP and 21.60% for FAP). On OOD variants, CoAPT attains 41.86% natural and 32.95% robust average accuracy, outperforming FAP (33.05% / 15.58%) by +8.81% and +17.37%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoAPT is compared against two baselines: FAP (Few-shot Adversarial Prompt learning, Zhou et al., NeurIPS 2024) and Vanilla CLIP (Radford et al., ICML 2021). Against FAP, CoAPT wins on every metric across all four benchmarks: on base-to-novel it gains +13.09% natural HM and +32.39% robust HM; on zero-shot it improves robust accuracy from 23.89% to 43.90% (average over 10 target datasets) while also recovering natural accuracy from 45.72% to 54.06%; on OOD it lifts robust accuracy from 15.58% to 32.95%. Against Vanilla CLIP, CoAPT slightly exceeds natural accuracy (63.42% vs. 62.10% on ImageNet zero-shot) while dramatically improving robustness from 1.57% to 51.18%, sacrificing only 7.83% natural generalization on the 10-dataset average. The key advantage over FAP is that CoAPT preserves natural generalization (matching or exceeding CLIP's clean accuracy in most settings) while FAP degrades natural performance substantially (e.g., 50.80% vs. 62.10% on ImageNet zero-shot), indicating that FAP's adversarial adaptation comes at a higher cost to in-distribution accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

CoAPT extends the adversarial prompt tuning paradigm (FAP, APT, AdvPT, C-AVP) by introducing a collaborative framework in which a frozen natural CLIP guides a target robust CLIP through latent-space reconstruction, rather than relying solely on in-distribution adversarial samples. It repurposes Chambolle's dual-projection TV algorithm with Nesterov acceleration and spatially adaptive regularization to perform real-time high-frequency suppression, replacing the patch-level masking of MIM-style approaches that inadvertently amplify adversarial perturbations. The low-rank residual module borrows directly from LoRA's (I + ηBA) reparameterization, and the Rényi divergence loss draws on information-theoretic regularization to align natural and adversarial predictive distributions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a single set of hyperparameters (no per-benchmark tuning) can simultaneously improve natural accuracy and adversarial robustness across 15 datasets and four evaluation protocols, which is practically valuable for deploying VLMs in safety-critical settings. However, the evaluation is confined to ViT-B/32 CLIP under ℓ∞ image-only attacks, leaving open whether the approach transfers to larger CLIP variants, text-based or multimodal attacks, and stronger threat models (e.g., ℓ2, larger ε). The method's reliance on multiple interacting components (adaptive-FGP, latent reconstruction, Rényi regularization, LoRA modules) raises questions about which elements are truly essential at scale and whether the <10% computational overhead claim holds for larger architectures.
