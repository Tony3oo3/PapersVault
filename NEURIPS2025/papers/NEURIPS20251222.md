## Problem

Conventional RAG methods retrieve documents independently and fail to capture complex inter-document relationships needed for multi-hop reasoning. While GraphRAG approaches build graph structures to model these relationships, they rely on noisy/incomplete graph topology (e.g., Personalized PageRank) or GNNs that must be trained from scratch on each new dataset, limiting generalizability. No graph foundation model existed that could be directly applied to unseen corpora for retrieval-augmented generation without domain-specific fine-tuning.

## Input / Output

The method takes a natural language query and a pre-built knowledge graph index (KG-index) constructed from a document corpus via LLM-based OpenIE (GPT-4o-mini) and ColBERTv2-based entity resolution (threshold τ=0.8). The GFM retriever outputs per-entity relevance scores over the KG, which are mapped to document scores through an entity-to-document inverted index with IDF weighting (top-T=20 entities selected). The top-K documents are then passed to an LLM (GPT-4o-mini) for final answer generation. The KG-index is a one-time preprocessing step; at inference, only the query and pre-built graph are needed.

## Method

GFM-RAG employs a 6-layer query-dependent GNN (8M parameters, hidden dim 512) that performs multi-hop reasoning in a single forward pass, following the NBFNet architecture with non-parametric DistMult message functions and sum aggregation. The key transferability mechanism is that query, entity, and relation embeddings are all initialized from the same frozen sentence embedding model (all-mpnet-v2, 768-dim), so the GNN operates in a unified semantic-structural space that generalizes across unseen KGs. Training is two-stage: (1) self-supervised KG completion pre-training over 60 knowledge graphs (14M+ triples, 700k documents) where masked head/tail entities serve as synthetic queries, and (2) supervised document retrieval fine-tuning on 60k labeled question-document pairs, using a combined BCE + ranking loss (α=0.3). The model follows neural scaling laws (z ∝ 0.24x^0.05 + 0.11y^0.03), confirming its potential as a scalable foundation model.

## Results

On three multi-hop QA datasets, GFM-RAG (single-step) achieves R@2 of 78.3, 49.1, and 90.8 on HotpotQA, MuSiQue, and 2Wiki respectively, outperforming the strongest multi-step baseline IRCoT+HippoRAG(ColBERTv2) by 16.8%, 8.3%, and 19.8% in R@2. For final QA with GPT-4o-mini, GFM-RAG achieves EM scores of 51.6, 30.2, and 69.8, surpassing the best baseline (IRCoT+HippoRAG) by 5.9, 8.3, and 22.1 EM points. In terms of efficiency, GFM-RAG completes retrieval in 0.06–0.12 seconds per query, roughly 25–50× faster than multi-step IRCoT pipelines (1.1–3.4 s) while achieving higher recall.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GFM-RAG outperforms all 14 baselines across every dataset and metric. Against single-step dense retrievers (BM25, Contriever, GTR, ColBERTv2, RAPTOR, Proposition), GFM-RAG gains 13.6–36.5 R@2 points, confirming that graph structure is critical for multi-hop retrieval. Among graph-enhanced single-step methods, GFM-RAG surpasses HippoRAG(ColBERTv2) by 17.8, 8.2, and 20.1 R@2 points, and SubgraphRAG by 16.8, 7.0, and 20.1 points, demonstrating that GNN-based multi-hop reasoning in a single step is more effective than Personalized PageRank or subgraph matching. Against multi-step pipelines (Adaptive-RAG, FLARE, IRCoT+various), GFM-RAG still leads by 5.2–46.1 R@2 points despite requiring only one retrieval call, highlighting its efficiency advantage (0.06–0.12 s vs. 1.1–3.4 s). The closest competitor, IRCoT+HippoRAG(ColBERTv2), is beaten by 11.3, 3.8, and 15.0 R@2 points on HotpotQA, MuSiQue, and 2Wiki respectively. On the QA task (Table 2), GFM-RAG single-step achieves 51.6/30.2/69.8 EM, exceeding the best multi-step baseline IRCoT+HippoRAG(ColBERTv2) at 45.7/21.9/47.7 EM, with the gap widening to 22.1 EM on 2Wiki.

## Relation to Prior Work

GFM-RAG sits at the intersection of graph-enhanced RAG (HippoRAG, LightRAG, G-retriever) and graph foundation models (ULTRA, OpenGraph, GFT). It adopts the query-dependent GNN architecture from NBFNet (DistMult message passing, sum aggregation) as its backbone and reuses HippoRAG's KG-index construction pipeline (LLM-based OpenIE + ColBERTv2 entity resolution). The key departure is that prior GNN-based retrievers (G-retriever, GNN-RAG) must be trained from scratch per dataset, while prior GFMs target graph tasks like link prediction rather than document retrieval; GFM-RAG is the first to pre-train a query-dependent GNN at scale (60 KGs, 14M triples) so it can be applied zero-shot to unseen corpora for RAG.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a single 8M-parameter GNN forward pass replaces iterative LLM-guided retrieval (IRCoT), cutting inference time by ~30× while improving retrieval accuracy, and the model generalizes across seven diverse domains without fine-tuning. The demonstrated alignment with neural scaling laws suggests further gains from larger training data and model size. Open questions include whether the KG-index construction bottleneck (LLM-based OpenIE) can be replaced by end-to-end learning, whether the approach scales to graphs with >4-hop reasoning, and how robust the method is to noisy or adversarially constructed knowledge graphs.
