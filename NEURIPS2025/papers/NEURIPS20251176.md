## Problem

Existing attention variants (MQA, GQA, MLA, TPA) primarily target parameter or memory reduction, yet empirical analysis shows that MHA performance improves monotonically with more attention heads as long as the hidden size per head stays sufficiently large. No prior method exploits this head-and-feature expansion benefit while keeping the model compact, leaving a gap between the observed performance ceiling of standard MHA and what a larger-head configuration could achieve at comparable parameter cost.

## Input / Output

SAS consumes the standard query, key, and value tensors of shape [B, T, H, D] produced by a Transformer's linear projections and returns an attention output of shape [B, T, H·D], identical in interface to vanilla MHA. Internally, Q and K are expanded to [B, T, Ĥ, D̂] and V to [B, T, Ĥ, D] before the scaled-dot-product attention is computed, then the Ĥ head outputs are collapsed back to H groups via PEAA so the downstream feed-forward layer sees no dimensional change.

## Method

SAS applies two independent non-linear expansions to the attention tensors. First, a two-layer 1-D convolution with ReLU and a residual connection is applied along the head dimension (treating heads as channels) to expand H → Ĥ for Q, K, and V. Second, a two-layer linear-ReLU-residual block expands the feature dimension D → D̂ for Q and K only, leaving V at D to avoid inflating the feed-forward layer. The expanded tensors are used in standard scaled-dot-product attention, and the resulting Ĥ head outputs are aggregated by Parameter-Efficient Attention Aggregation (PEAA), which concatenates groups of H heads, applies a single shared linear projection, and averages across groups—adding less than 0.4 % parameters for a 125 M model and under 0.1 % for a 2.7 B model.

## Results

On the FineWeb-Edu downstream evaluation (zero-shot), SAS achieves the best average accuracy across all model sizes and token budgets: 55.07 at 350M/50B tokens (vs. MHA 52.92, TPA 53.76), 52.13 at 350M/10B (vs. MHA 50.55), 54.27 at 760M/10B (vs. MHA 52.43), and 55.69 at 1.5B/10B (vs. MHA 52.86). On language modeling perplexity, SAS achieves 5.65 on Arxiv (512) and 22.14 on Books3 (1024) with a 125M model, outperforming MHA (5.82 and 23.67 respectively). At 2.7B scale on Books3, SAS reaches 21.62 ppl versus MHA's 22.18, and at 10.6B on Pile, SAS achieves 9.04 ppl versus MHA's 9.64.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAS outperforms all five baselines (MHA, MQA, GQA, MLA, TPA) on average accuracy across every model size and token budget tested. The margin over MHA is the largest: +2.15 points at 350M/50B (55.07 vs. 52.92) and +2.83 at 1.5B/10B (55.69 vs. 52.86). TPA is the closest competitor, trailing SAS by 1.31 points at 350M/50B and 1.83 at 1.5B/10B. On individual benchmarks, SAS leads on ARC-E, HellaSwag, PIQA, and SciQ in nearly all settings, while WinoGrande shows the most competitive results (e.g., TPA scores 55.09 vs. SAS 54.93 at 1.5B/10B). On language modeling perplexity, SAS consistently achieves the lowest ppl: 5.65 vs. MHA 5.82 on Arxiv-512, 22.14 vs. MHA 23.67 on Books3-1024, and 9.04 vs. MHA 9.64 at 10.6B on Pile. The advantage of SAS is consistent across training lengths (512 to 16384) and model scales (125M to 10.6B), with the relative gain remaining stable or slightly increasing at larger scales.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on standard Multi-Head Attention (MHA) and positions itself against the family of attention variants designed for efficiency—MQA, GQA, MLA, and TPA—which all reduce parameter or memory cost through head sharing, low-rank compression, or tensor decomposition. SAS departs from this efficiency-driven lineage by instead *simulating* a larger model's attention capacity: it applies lightweight non-linear convolutions/MLPs along the head and feature dimensions to expand them (H→Ĥ, D→D̂) with only ~0.4% additional parameters, then uses a novel Parameter-Efficient Attention Aggregation (PEAA) to fold the expanded heads back to the original output size. The motivating empirical observation—that MHA performance improves monotonically with head count as long as per-head dimension remains sufficiently large—provides the conceptual bridge between the efficiency literature and this expressiveness-focused approach.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SAS offers a simple, modular drop-in module that consistently improves perplexity across model sizes (125M–10.6B), datasets (Arxiv, Books3, Pile, FineWeb-Edu), and training lengths, with the advantage scaling down in relative parameter cost as models grow. However, the ~2× training-time overhead (Table 4) is a significant practical limitation that tempers the 'parameter-efficient' framing, and the paper does not address inference-time cost or KV-cache implications of the expanded heads. Open questions include whether the non-linear head expansion generalizes to non-autoregressive or multimodal Transformers, whether the ReLU+residual design is optimal versus other activations, and whether the gains persist under longer-context or instruction-tuning regimes.
