## Problem

Segmentation-oriented industrial anomaly synthesis (SIAS) requires generating controllable, structurally coherent anomalies at the pixel level, but existing diffusion-based methods treat all spatial regions uniformly during denoising and require hundreds to thousands of sampling steps, making them both semantically misaligned with segmentation objectives and computationally prohibitive for production-line deployment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251342/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a normal industrial image, a binary anomaly mask specifying the target region and shape, and a short text prompt (e.g., product category + anomaly type) as input, and produces a synthesized image with the anomaly localized precisely within the masked region. Masks are generated via geometric augmentation of real anomaly masks and LDM-based synthesis, with 500 image-mask pairs per anomaly type (roughly one-third for training). The synthesized data is then used to train lightweight segmentation backbones (SegFormer, BiSeNet V2, STDC) evaluated by mIoU and pixel-wise accuracy.

## Method

FAST is built on a latent diffusion model and introduces two complementary modules. AIAS is a training-free sampler that analytically aggregates multiple DDPM single-step reverse transitions into a small number of coarse-to-fine segments using closed-form affine-Gaussian kernels (derived via a linear-Gaussian closure lemma), reducing sampling from 1000 to as few as 10 steps (~100× speedup) while preserving segmentation-relevant structure. FARM is an encoder-decoder module that, at each denoising step, reconstructs pseudo-clean anomaly-only content from the noisy latent under mask and timestep conditioning, then forward-diffuses it to produce anomaly-aware noise that replaces the original noise in masked regions, thereby preserving localized anomaly signals throughout the trajectory. Training jointly optimizes a standard denoising loss and a FARM reconstruction loss; sampling alternates between AIAS coarse multi-step updates with FARM refinement and a final fine-grained DDPM posterior pass at small timesteps to restore texture fidelity.

## Results

On MVTec-AD (15 categories) with Segformer, FAST achieves an average mIoU of 76.72% and pixel-wise accuracy of 83.97%, outperforming the strongest baseline DRAEM (66.86% mIoU, 74.75% Acc) by 9.86 and 9.22 points respectively. On BTAD (3 categories), FAST similarly leads across all three segmentation backbones (Segformer, BiSeNet V2, STDC), e.g., reaching 79.40% mIoU / 85.64% Acc on category 03 with Segformer versus 54.85% / 80.20% for the next-best method (AnomalyDiffusion). The method achieves these gains with only 50 sampling steps (as few as 10), yielding over 100× speedup compared to full 1000-step DDPM.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FAST outperforms all six anomaly-synthesis baselines (CutPaste, DRAEM, GLASS, DFMGAN, RealNet, AnomalyDiffusion) on every MVTec-AD category in both mIoU and accuracy. The closest competitor is DRAEM (66.86% mIoU), which FAST surpasses by 9.86 mIoU points on average; the gap is largest on challenging fine-grained categories such as capsule (+11.83 mIoU), grid (+4.70), and transistor (+7.58). Among diffusion-based methods, AnomalyDiffusion (62.88% mIoU) and RealNet (62.89% mIoU) trail FAST by roughly 14 mIoU points, indicating that foreground-aware noise injection (FARM) and accelerated sampling (AIAS) provide substantial gains over uniform-region diffusion synthesis. GAN-based DFMGAN (67.71% mIoU) is competitive on some categories (e.g., pill: 87.19 vs. 90.17) but still falls short of FAST overall. On BTAD, FAST leads all baselines across three segmentation backbones, with the largest margin on category 03 (e.g., 79.40% mIoU with Segformer vs. 54.85% for AnomalyDiffusion, a +24.55 gap). In ablation comparisons against alternative samplers (DDIM 50-step, PLMS 50-step), AIAS consistently matches or exceeds their segmentation performance while requiring the same 50-step budget, and approaches full 1000-step DDPM quality with only 10 steps.

## Relation to Prior Work

FAST builds on the Latent Diffusion Model (LDM) backbone and the DDPM reverse-process formulation, but departs from standard diffusion sampling by analytically composing multiple single-step posterior transitions into closed-form coarse-to-fine segments (AIAS), a training-free strategy distinct from DDIM's variance-controlled interpolation or PLMS's fixed multi-step solvers. It also extends the prompt-driven anomaly synthesis protocol of AnomalyDiffusion by introducing explicit foreground-background decoupling via the FARM module, which injects mask-conditioned anomaly-aware noise at every denoising step rather than treating all spatial regions uniformly. Conceptually, the paper positions itself against GAN-based synthesis (limited controllability), training-free texture/patch methods (poor structural realism), and prior diffusion-based anomaly generators (uniform spatial treatment, slow sampling), carving out the specific niche of segmentation-oriented, mask-controllable, and computationally efficient anomaly synthesis.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 100× sampling speedup (1000→10 steps) with maintained or improved segmentation accuracy addresses a critical deployment bottleneck for industrial production-line changeover, where real-time synthesis of training data is needed. The finding that moderate step counts (10–50) outperform full-step DDPM for segmentation—because excessive denoising weakens anomaly localization consistency—challenges the default assumption that more steps always help and suggests task-aligned sampling budgets. Open questions include whether the coarse-to-fine aggregation principle generalizes beyond industrial textures to other segmentation domains, how the method scales to multi-class or open-vocabulary anomaly types, and whether the FARM reconstruction pathway can be replaced by lighter-weight alternatives without sacrificing the ~11 mIoU gain it provides.
