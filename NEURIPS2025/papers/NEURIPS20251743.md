## Problem

Designing reward functions for multi-agent reinforcement learning that capture relational constraints and inter-agent dependencies remains a major challenge. Existing specification-guided RL methods are limited to universally quantified properties (∀*), which cannot express dependencies of the form ∀τ.∃τ'.ψ where one agent's optimal behavior depends on another's. This paper tackles the previously unaddressed problem of learning control policies that maximize satisfaction of HyperLTL formulas with quantifier alternation over MDPs with unknown transitions.

## Input / Output

The method takes as input an MDP with unknown transition probabilities and a closed HyperLTL formula φ of the form Q₁τ₁…Qₙτₙ.ψ, where each trace variable corresponds to an agent's path. At each learning step, the RL agent observes a 'zipped' state — the pointwise tuple of all agents' current positions — and outputs an action for each agent simultaneously. The learned output is a tuple of neural-network policies for universally quantified agents plus Skolem function witnesses for existentially quantified agents, where each existential policy is a function of the preceding universal traces, encoding the dependency structure of the specification.

## Method

HYPRL proceeds in three steps. First, it Skolemizes the HyperLTL formula, replacing each existentially quantified trace variable with a Skolem function of all preceding universally quantified variables, thereby eliminating quantifier alternation and reducing the problem to learning policies only for universal traces. Second, it defines quantitative (robustness) semantics for the Skolemized formula, extended from truncated LTL, which assign a real-valued robustness score to each zipped finite trace indicating how close it is to satisfying the specification. Third, it trains a neural network via standard RL algorithms (DQN, PPO, CQ-Learning) where the immediate reward at each step is the robustness value of the current zipped prefix, and the Bellman equation is applied over the joint zipped state space. The key insight is that the Skolemization makes existential policies explicit functions of universal ones, so a single neural network simultaneously learns both the universal policies and the dependency-encoding Skolem witnesses, while the robustness values automatically shape rewards without manual design.

## Results

On the Safe RL in Grid Worlds (SRL) benchmark, CQ+HYPRL reduces average steps to goal by 60–75% compared to CQ alone (e.g., 7.58 vs. 27.95 on ISR, 11.90 vs. 36.46 on Pentagon for 2 agents) while maintaining low collision rates. On Deep Sea Treasure (DST), PPO+HYPRL collects 22.93±2.2 total treasures at 500 episodes versus 4.31±1.2 for PPO alone (a ~5.3× improvement), and DQN+HYPRL achieves 4.12±1.4 vs. 1.08±0.2 for DQN. In the wildfire scenario scaled to 8² and 10² grids, PPO+HYPRL completes both objectives (extinguish fires and rescue victims) within step bounds, while PPO fails to rescue victims (O₂) at 5² and 8² and fails to extinguish fires (O₁) at 10².

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares HYPRL against two baselines: (1) manually designed reward functions following the setup of Li et al. [32] (IROS 2017), and (2) shield synthesis from ElSayed-Aly et al. [17] (AAMAS 2021). Against the [32] baseline (plain DQN/PPO/CQ with hand-crafted rewards), HYPRL wins decisively on all four benchmarks: on DST it achieves ~5× more collected treasures (22.93 vs. 4.31 for PPO at 500 episodes), on the wildfire scenario it completes objectives that the baseline cannot finish within step bounds at larger grid sizes, and on PCP it achieves more successful domino matches. Against the shield synthesis baseline [17] on SRL, the trade-off is nuanced: the shield achieves zero collisions in all cases, while CQ+HYPRL has small but non-zero collision rates (e.g., 0.25 on ISR, 0.53 on Pentagon for 2 agents). However, HYPRL reaches goals in substantially fewer steps (7.58 vs. 17.40 on ISR; 23.20 vs. 33.46 on MIT for 2 agents), and in the 3-agent setting the shield fails to reach goals within the 100-step bound on ISR, Pentagon, and MIT, whereas HYPRL completes them in 71–79 steps.

## Relation to Prior Work

HYPRL extends the quantitative-robustness reward-shaping paradigm from single-agent LTL (Li et al., IROS 2017) to multi-agent HyperLTL with quantifier alternation, a setting the authors explicitly note has not been studied before. It departs from prior MARL approaches (Hammond et al., León et al., ElSayed-Aly et al.) that are restricted to co-safety or universally-quantified-only properties, by introducing Skolemization to eliminate ∀∃ alternation and learning Skolem-function witnesses alongside policies. The framework reuses off-the-shelf RL algorithms (DQN, PPO, CQ-Learning) with robustness values as the sole reward signal, avoiding the need for manually designed reward functions or compositional automata constructions that suffer from state-space explosion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

HYPRL provides a principled, specification-driven alternative to hand-crafted reward engineering for multi-agent tasks with relational dependencies (e.g., one agent's safety depends on another's progress), which is a persistent pain point in MARL. The ability to handle undecidable problems like PCP and to scale the wildfire scenario to 10×10 grids suggests practical applicability to safety-critical coordination (disaster response, swarm robotics). Key open questions include extending to fully decentralized policies (each agent without global observations), handling POMDPs rather than fully observable MDPs, and understanding how the approach scales beyond two or three agents where the Skolem-function space grows combinatorially.
