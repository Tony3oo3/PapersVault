## Problem

Traditional prediction tasks (binary classification, online learning) are closed under finite unions of learnable classes, but whether language generation in the limit shares this property was an open question posed by Li et al. [2025]. This paper resolves three open questions from that work: (Q1) generation is not closed under finite unions, (Q2) finite unions of non-uniformly generatable classes need not be generatable, and (Q3) there exist uncountable non-uniformly generatable classes that violate the Eventually Unbounded Closure (EUC) property.

## Input / Output

The model is adversarial and online: an adversary fixes a target language K from a collection L and presents its elements one at a time in an arbitrary (adversarially chosen) enumeration. At each round n, the generator receives the prefix S_n = {x_1, ..., x_n} and must output a single string w_n not in S_n; the generator succeeds if, after some finite time n*, every subsequent w_n is an unseen element of K. In the paper's constructions the domain is the integers Z (or N), and the key constraint is that the adversary controls both the target language and its enumeration order, making the problem fundamentally different from i.i.d. sampling.

## Method

The core contribution is a novel diagonalization argument that constructs two collections L1 and L2 over Z, each individually (trivially) non-uniformly generatable, whose union is not generatable. The construction embeds a shared structure across languages within each collection (e.g., L1 languages contain all but finitely many negative integers, L2 languages contain all but finitely many positive integers) while allowing finite omissions from the shared set, preventing any generator from identifying which collection the target belongs to at any finite time. The diagonalization proceeds in alternating phases: the adversary enumerates negative integers until the generator outputs an unseen negative, then switches to positive integers until the generator outputs an unseen positive, repeating indefinitely. Crucially, the adversary forces a mistake only at each phase transition (not every round), ensuring the final enumerated language K belongs to one of the collections while the generator still makes infinitely many errors. This 'alternate-round' diagonalization is the key technical innovation that overcomes the pigeonhole-principle obstacle of applying standard diagonalization to just two collections.

## Results

This is a purely theoretical paper with no experimental results or quantitative metrics. The three main theorems establish: (1) Theorem 3.1 — there exist uncountable collections L₁ and L₂ that are each non-uniformly generatable, yet their union L₁ ∪ L₂ is not generatable, resolving Questions 1 and 2 of Li et al. [2025] negatively; (2) Theorem 3.2 — a minimal counterexample where L₁ is countable (non-uniformly generatable without any input) and L₂ is uncountable (uniformly generatable without any input), yet their union is not generatable; (3) Theorem 3.3 — an uncountable collection that is non-uniformly generatable but violates the Eventually Unbounded Closure (EUC) property, resolving Question 3 of Li et al. [2025].

## Baselines & Comparisons

As a theoretical paper, this work does not compare against experimental baselines. Instead, its results are positioned relative to prior theoretical guarantees: Kleinberg and Mullainathan [2024] showed all countable collections are generatable, and Li et al. [2025] characterized uniform and non-uniform generation and showed countable unions of uniformly generatable classes need not be generatable. The paper's key contrast is with traditional prediction tasks (binary/multiclass/online classification), which are closed under finite unions — a property that language generation provably lacks. Theorem 3.2 further shows the non-closure is minimal: simplifying either collection (making L₁ uniformly generatable or L₂ countable) would restore generatability of the union, per existing results. The novel diagonalization technique (forcing mistakes only in alternate rounds) is the technical contribution that overcomes the pigeonhole-principle obstacle that prevents standard diagonalization from working with just two collections.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the language generation in the limit model introduced by Kleinberg and Mullainathan (NeurIPS 2024) and the hierarchy of generation notions (uniform, non-uniform, generatable) formalized by Li, Raman, and Tewari (COLT 2025). While Kleinberg and Mullainathan showed generatability for countable collections and Li et al. characterized uniform and non-uniform generation for uncountable collections, the closure properties of generatability under set operations remained open. The paper departs from the standard diagonalization approach used by Li et al. (who required countably many collections) by developing a novel two-collection diagonalization with embedded shared structure and alternating-phase adversarial strategies, specifically designed to overcome the pigeonhole-principle obstacle that prevents independent diagonalization on either collection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The non-closure result fundamentally distinguishes language generation in the limit from all standard prediction and classification tasks, which are closed under finite unions, and rules out a natural 'boosting' strategy of combining generators for different collections. The minimality of the construction (one countable, one uniformly generatable class) shows the failure is inherent rather than an artifact of complexity. The EUC counterexample (Theorem 3.3) indicates that current sufficient conditions for characterizing generatability in the limit are incomplete, and the novel diagonalization technique is positioned as a tool for the still-open problem of a complete characterization of generatability. The work is purely theoretical within a stylized adversarial model, so direct practical implications for LLM training are indirect, though the structural insights about when generation becomes intractable may inform the design of generation guarantees.
