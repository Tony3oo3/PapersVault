## Problem

Existing deep clustering models consistently exhibit a disparity between local and global feature structures: local neighborhoods (measured by k-NN accuracy) are highly reliable, yet global cluster geometry (measured by silhouette coefficient and inter-class separation) remains entangled and poorly separated. No prior method exploits this reliable local structure to repair the deficient global structure, leaving a systematic gap between what the model knows locally and how it organizes features globally.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250473/cropped_page1_idx5.webp)

## Input / Output

DCBoost operates as a plug-in on top of any pre-trained deep clustering model, consuming unlabeled RGB images at the resolution specified by the base model (32×32 for CIFAR, 96×96 for STL-10, 224×224 for ImageNet subsets). It requires the number of clusters as prior knowledge and produces final cluster assignments via k-means on the target network's L2-normalized features. The method introduces no new learnable parameters; it only adds a randomly initialized predictor head and a 10-epoch warm-up phase before the boosting training begins.

## Method

DCBoost adaptively selects high-confidence samples per mini-batch by finding the k that maximizes the product of k and the fraction of samples whose k nearest neighbors all share the same pseudo-label, then applies a three-term discriminative loss to those samples: a class-balanced positive loss pulling same-label features together, a negative loss repelling inter-class prototypes, and an instance consistency loss aligning two augmented views through a BYOL-style online/target network with EMA updates. The key insight is that the adaptive k-NN score automatically trades off supervision quantity (small k, more samples) against quality (large k, stricter consistency), shifting from broad early supervision to selective late-stage refinement without any manual hyperparameter tuning. The entire procedure is parameter-free and model-agnostic, applicable to both representation-based and clustering-head-based architectures.

## Results

On the five main benchmarks (CIFAR-10, CIFAR-20, STL-10, ImageNet-10, ImageNet-Dogs), DCBoost consistently improves all six integrated deep clustering models, with average ACC gains of +4.6% (BYOL), +3.1% (CC), +3.0% (ProPos), +1.2% (SCAN), +0.8% (CDC), and +0.6% (CoNR). The strongest enhanced model, ProPos+Ours, achieves 82.2% average ACC, surpassing the previous SOTA DPAC (77.4%) by 4.8 points, and reaches 96.0% ACC / 91.6% ARI on CIFAR-10. The silhouette coefficient (a global-structure metric) is amplified by over 7× on ProPos (0.10 → 0.74 on CIFAR-10), while k-NN accuracy (local structure) is preserved or slightly improved. On large-scale Tiny-ImageNet (200 classes), ProPos+Ours still gains +2.1% ACC over the baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DCBoost is applied as a plug-in to six existing models (BYOL, CoNR, ProPos, CC, SCAN, CDC), and in every case the enhanced version outperforms the original: the largest gains are BYOL+Ours (+4.6% avg ACC) and CC+Ours (+3.1%), while even the already-strong ProPos improves by +3.0% to reach 82.2% average, surpassing the previous SOTA DPAC (77.4%) by 4.8 points. Against external baselines, ProPos+Ours and CDC+Ours dominate: they exceed SPICE (76.3%), TCL (71.4%), TCC (67.2%), GCC (64.3%), IDFD (63.9%), and NNM on all reported datasets. On CLIP-based models (SIC, TAC), DCBoost yields consistent but smaller gains (e.g., TAC ACC 92.0→93.1 on CIFAR-10, 61.5→62.9 on CIFAR-20), confirming applicability beyond standard CNN backbones. On long-tailed CIFAR-10/20 (imbalance ratio 10), ProPos+Ours and CoNR+Ours both outperform the dedicated long-tailed method LFSS by 3.7–4.0% ACC. The only near-tie is BYOL+Ours (77.3% avg) vs. DPAC (77.4% avg), where the two are essentially equivalent in aggregate but BYOL+Ours leads on CIFAR-10 (91.5 vs. 93.4) and ImageNet-Dogs (77.1 vs. 72.6) while trailing on ImageNet-10 (95.7 vs. 97.0).

## Relation to Prior Work

DCBoost builds on the BYOL-style asymmetric online/target network architecture with EMA and a randomly initialized predictor, repurposing it as a fine-tuning framework for pre-trained deep clustering models. Conceptually, it departs from prior deep clustering work (ProPos, CoNR, SCAN, CDC, etc.) by identifying a previously underexploited asymmetry: these models produce reliable local neighborhood structure but unreliable global cluster geometry. The adaptive k-NN sample selection draws on noisy-label filtering ideas (MOIT, SSR) but repositions them as a local, batch-wise, parameter-free mechanism rather than a global fixed-k retrieval, and the discriminative loss (positive/negative/instance-consistency) is a novel combination tailored to pseudo-label-augmented clustering rather than standard contrastive or supervised objectives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-in's parameter-free, architecture-agnostic design makes it a low-friction add-on for any existing deep clustering pipeline, and the consistent 3%+ average improvement across six models and five benchmarks demonstrates practical utility. However, the method assumes the number of clusters is known and shows reduced effectiveness under highly imbalanced distributions (long-tailed settings yield only ~1–2% gains), leaving open the question of whether the local-consistency signal remains reliable when class support is severely skewed. Future directions include scaling to very large datasets (where batch-wise k-NN becomes less informative), multimodal extensions, and removing the fixed-cluster-count assumption.
