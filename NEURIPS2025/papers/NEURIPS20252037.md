## Problem

Dominant finetuning methods (LoRA, adapters, prefix-tuning) all operate on model weights, treat the network as a black box, and rely on heuristic hyperparameters with minimal theoretical guidance. The activation functions—responsible for a model's nonlinearity and expressivity—have been largely overlooked as a steering mechanism. This paper asks whether a single hyperparameter injected into activation functions can provably modulate a model's decision boundary curvature without altering its learned weights.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252037/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard pretrained vision backbones (ResNet-18/50/152, VGG-11, Swin-T/S) that consume RGB images and produce class labels or regression values. The 'input' to CT itself is a single scalar β ∈ [0,1] (and optionally a mixing coefficient c ∈ [0,1]) that replaces all ReLU activations with the CTU activation; the steering variant introduces exactly one hyperparameter per network with no architectural changes. The output is the same prediction as the original model, but with a smoothly modulated decision boundary whose curvature is continuously controlled by β, ranging from the original piecewise-affine geometry (β=1) to a fully linear mapping (β→0).

## Method

Grounded in the spline theory of deep learning, where each ReLU layer is a max-affine spline operator, CTU (CT Unit) is constructed as a convex combination of a reparameterized SiLU (derived from entropy-regularized soft vector-quantization region assignment) and a reparameterized Softplus (derived from smoothing the max operator via log-sum-exp), with β controlling the degree of smoothing and c balancing the two to cancel opposing mean shifts. In the steering variant (S-CT), all ReLUs are replaced with CTUs using a shared fixed β and c=0.5, requiring only one hyperparameter and no backpropagation; in the trainable variant (T-CT), each output neuron receives its own trainable (β, c) pair optimized via backpropagation. Theoretically, CT provably projects a ReLU network onto a space of smooth functions with bounded gradients and curvature, and for 0 < β < 1 the resulting function enjoys higher local expressivity than the original for the same fixed weights. Empirically, S-CT yields up to 1494% relative improvement in ℓ∞ robust accuracy on RobustBench, while T-CT outperforms LoRA (r=1) with only 5–59% of its trainable parameters across 12 downstream datasets.

## Results

On 12 downstream datasets, T-CT achieves average accuracy of 78.26% (ResNet-18) and 81.31% (ResNet-50), outperforming linear probing (73.96%/76.24%) by 6.75%/8.59% and LoRA r=1 (73.64%/78.68%) by 10.20%/4.64%, while using only 11.05%/57.20% of LoRA's trainable parameters. S-CT (training-free, 1 parameter) improves robust accuracy under ℓ∞ attacks by 1032.64% (ResNet-50) and 1494.46% (ResNet-152) over the frozen baseline on RobustBench. On Swin transformers, T-CT improves over linear probing by 0.48%/1.94% (Swin-T/S) but trails LoRA by 4.01%/4.76%, using only 0.71%/0.58% of LoRA's parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is LoRA (r=1, α=1), the standard parameter-efficient finetuning method. T-CT outperforms LoRA on average across ResNet-18 (78.26% vs. 73.64%, +10.20%) and ResNet-50 (81.31% vs. 78.68%, +4.64%), while using only 11.05% and 57.20% of LoRA's trainable parameters respectively. The gap narrows on ResNet-152 where T-CT leads by just 1.70% (81.34% vs. 80.42%), and T-CT loses on several individual datasets (e.g., OCTMNIST, PathMNIST) where LoRA's weight adaptation is more effective. On Swin transformers, T-CT underperforms LoRA by 4.01% (Swin-T) and 4.76% (Swin-S), though it still uses only 0.71%/0.58% of LoRA's parameters. S-CT (the training-free variant) consistently beats frozen linear probing on ResNet-18/50 but shows minimal gains on ResNet-152 (0.02% average), suggesting diminishing returns for deeper architectures without weight adaptation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper is grounded in the spline theory of deep learning (Balestriero & Baraniuk, ICML 2018), which establishes that ReLU-based networks are exactly representable as affine spline operators, and in the soft vector-quantization formulation of network nonlinearities (Balestriero & Baraniuk, 2018), which provides the entropy-regularized region-selection framework. It departs fundamentally from all existing PEFT methods (LoRA, adapters, prefix-tuning, etc.) that operate exclusively on weight adaptation, instead proposing to modulate activation functions as a new steering axis. The closest prior work is Srinivas et al. (2022), who explored parametric Softplus during pretraining, but CT is the first to use activation modulation as a post-pretraining PEFT/steering direction with a general parametric family (CTU) applicable to existing architectures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CT offers a principled, interpretable, and extremely parameter-efficient alternative to heuristic PEFT methods: a single shared β can steer a frozen model (S-CT), and per-neuron (β,c) pairs yield a finetuning method (T-CT) using 11–59% of LoRA's parameters. The theoretical result that CT projects a ReLU network onto a space of smooth functions with bounded curvature provides a clean complementary perspective to weight-based adaptation. Open questions include extending CT to attention mechanisms (where spline guarantees break down), as evidenced by the weaker transformer results, and understanding whether the robustness gains (striking in relative terms but from very low baselines, e.g., 0.3%→9.3% on ImageNet ℓ∞) persist at higher robustness levels or with stronger attack budgets.
