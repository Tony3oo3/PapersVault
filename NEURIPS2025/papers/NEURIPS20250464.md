## Problem

Existing MOF generative models (MOFDiff, MOFFlow) are constrained by two assumptions: they operate over a fixed library of predefined building blocks and treat those blocks as rigid bodies. This prevents the discovery of novel MOF chemistries and ignores the intrinsic conformational flexibility of organic linkers, which is critical for accurate 3D structure prediction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250464/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes a canonicalized SMILES sequence of metal clusters and organic linkers as input, which is converted to approximate 3D coordinates via a pre-built metal template library and RDKit/MMFF94 force-field optimization. The output is a complete 3D MOF structure specified by per-block rotations (SO(3)), translations (R³), torsion angles for all rotatable bonds (SO(2)), and six lattice parameters. Unlike prior methods, the model does not require ground-truth 3D conformations of building blocks as input, making it applicable when only 2D chemical information is available.

## Method

MOFFLOW-2 is a two-stage framework: an autoregressive Transformer generates MOF building block sequences in SMILES, and a Riemannian flow matching model assembles them into 3D structures by jointly predicting rotations, translations, torsion angles, and lattice parameters via geodesic interpolation on SO(3), R³, SO(2), and R⁶ respectively. The key architectural innovation is explicit torsion angle modeling on SO(2) with a dedicated prediction head that constructs dihedral features from four canonical atoms and refines them via local attention, capturing linker flexibility that rigid-body methods miss. Rotations are predicted as 9D matrices and projected to SO(3) via the orthogonal Procrustes problem at inference, while a canonicalization procedure resolves symmetry-induced ambiguity in both rotation and torsion targets during training. A MOF matching preprocessing step aligns training coordinates (DFT-relaxed) with inference-time coordinates (RDKit-initialized) to reduce distributional shift, and the entire structure prediction module uses a non-equivariant Transformer with block-level attention pooling over a 3D molecular graph.

## Results

On MOF structure prediction (Boyd et al. dataset, ~19.8k test samples), MOFFLOW-2 achieves a 43.95% match rate at site tolerance 0.5 Å (5 samples) with 0.2925 Å RMSE, outperforming MOFFLOW (32.71% MR, 0.3290 Å RMSE) and all other baselines. At the stricter 0.3 Å tolerance, MOFFLOW-2 reaches 15.98% MR vs. MOFFLOW's 8.68%. On MOF generation, MOFFLOW-2 achieves 38.84% validity, 31.35% VNU, and 10.10% novel building blocks (NBB), compared to MOFDiff's 10.13%, 7.95%, and 0.00% respectively, while also being faster (1.82s vs. 3.19s per sample).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MOFFLOW-2 decisively outperforms all four baselines across both tasks. Against the optimization-based baselines (Random Search and Evolutionary Algorithm from CrySPY), both achieve 0% match rate on MOF structures, confirming their inability to handle the large combinatorial space of MOFs. The general crystal structure prediction model DiffCSP also fails, reaching only 0.87% MR at stol=0.5, indicating that general-purpose CSP methods do not transfer to MOF-scale structures. Compared to the closest baseline MOFFLOW (the direct predecessor), MOFFLOW-2 improves match rate from 32.71% to 43.95% (+11.2 pp) and reduces RMSE from 0.3290 to 0.2925 Å at stol=0.5, demonstrating the benefit of explicitly modeling torsion angles. On the generation task, MOFFLOW-2 surpasses MOFDiff by a wide margin: 38.84% vs. 10.13% validity, 31.35% vs. 7.95% VNU, and uniquely generates novel building blocks (10.10% NBB vs. 0.00%), while also being 1.75× faster per sample.

## Relation to Prior Work

MOFFLOW-2 is a direct extension of MOFFLOW [10], a flow-matching model for MOF structure prediction that treated building blocks as rigid bodies with fixed 3D conformations. The paper departs from this lineage in two key ways: (1) it introduces torsion angle modeling on SO(2) manifolds, building on the torsional diffusion framework of Jing et al. [16] and the Riemannian flow matching formalism of Chen & Lipman [26], and (2) it adds an autoregressive SMILES generator to produce novel building blocks, moving beyond the fixed building block libraries used by both MOFDiff [11] and MOFFLOW [10]. The non-equivariant Transformer architecture follows the broader trend established by AlphaFold3, Boltz-1, and Proteina, which demonstrated that equivariance is not strictly necessary for high-fidelity 3D structure modeling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By jointly modeling torsional degrees of freedom and generating novel building blocks, MOFFLOW-2 addresses two fundamental bottlenecks in computational MOF design: the conformational rigidity assumption that limits structural fidelity, and the combinatorial constraint of a fixed component library that limits chemical diversity. The practical implication is a framework that can propose MOFs with previously unseen chemistries while predicting their 3D structures without requiring known conformations as input. Key limitations include dependence on RDKit for initial conformer generation, only partial conditioning for property-guided design, and reliance on approximate ML interatomic potentials for energy evaluation rather than DFT. Open questions include whether the approach scales to MOFs with more than 20 building blocks, how well generated structures survive rigorous energy minimization, and whether the torsion modeling generalizes to other flexible molecular systems beyond MOFs.
