## Problem

In instance-dependent partial label learning, incorrect candidate labels that are strongly correlated with features can be indistinguishable from correct labels within the original label space, causing the training model to generate misleading supervision signals and hit a performance bottleneck. Existing identification-based methods iteratively refine labels using the model's own outputs, but they cannot escape this bottleneck because the model itself cannot separate the disturbing labels. The paper asks whether training in reduced label subspaces that explicitly exclude specific labels can produce pseudo-labels with provably better consistency with the Bayes optimal classifier.

## Input / Output

During training, the method consumes instance feature vectors (extracted from RGB images, audio, text, or video) together with their candidate label sets S_i ⊂ Y; at test time only the feature vector is needed since the auxiliary multi-branch model and meta-learner are discarded. The output is a single class label prediction h(x) = argmax_j f_j(x) from the predictive model f. The candidate label set is never available at inference, and the method requires a small validation set with ground-truth labels to drive the meta-learning outer loop.

## Method

RPLG generates reduction-based pseudo-labels by training c branches of an auxiliary model φ, where the j-th branch is trained in a label subspace that excludes label j, so it is explicitly free from the interference of that label. A meta-learned weight vector w_i = g(x_i; Γ) (produced by a small model g) fuses the c branch outputs into a single reduction-based pseudo-label v_i, and the final training pseudo-label is a convex combination q_i = αμ_i + (1−α)v_i with the basic pseudo-label μ_i from the predictive model. Training follows a bi-level optimization: the inner loop updates the predictive model f using q_i, while the outer loop updates the meta-learner g on a validation set to learn which branches to trust for each instance. Theoretically, under a multi-class Tsybakov condition, the paper proves that pseudo-labels from the label-subspace model are more consistent with the Bayes optimal classifier than those from the full-space predictive model, with a convergence rate of 1 − C[O(εε′)]^λ.

## Results

RPLG achieves the best classification accuracy across all 10 evaluated datasets. On benchmark datasets, it reaches 91.41% on Fashion-MNIST, 96.85% on KMNIST, 87.53% on CIFAR-10, 65.03% on CIFAR-100, and 40.74% on TinyImageNet. On real-world datasets, it attains 81.07% on Lost, 75.27% on BirdSong, 51.65% on MSRCv2, 56.94% on Soccer Player, and 68.01% on Yahoo!News. The largest margin over the strongest baseline is on TinyImageNet, where RPLG outperforms the next-best method (POP) by 1.47 percentage points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RPLG outperforms all 13 compared baselines on every benchmark and real-world dataset. Among the ID-PLL-specific methods, DIRK is the closest competitor on FMNIST (91.02% vs. 91.41%) and KMNIST (96.21% vs. 96.85%), while SDCT leads on CIFAR-10 (86.50% vs. 87.53%) and IDGP on CIFAR-100 (64.38% vs. 65.03%). The advantage is most pronounced on TinyImageNet, where RPLG (40.74%) surpasses the next-best POP (39.27%) by 1.47 points and DIRK (25.77%) by a substantial 14.97 points. On real-world datasets, RPLG leads DIRK by 1.83 points on Lost (81.07% vs. 79.24%) and by 0.72 points on Yahoo!News (68.01% vs. 67.65%). Classical DNN-based PLL methods such as CAVL and LWS show particularly large gaps, with CAVL dropping to 59.67% on CIFAR-10 and LWS to 37.49%, indicating that instance-dependent corruption severely degrades methods not designed for it.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the identification-based route for instance-dependent partial label learning (ID-PLL), building directly on the margin-based identification criterion from POP [42] and the progressive self-training scheme of PRODEN [24] for auxiliary branch initialization. The key conceptual departure is decoupling pseudo-label generation from the predictive model itself: rather than relying on the model's own outputs to identify correct labels (as in POP, IDGP, DIRK, and VALEN), RPLG introduces a multi-branch auxiliary model where each branch is trained in a label subspace that explicitly excludes one label, thereby removing the interference of hard-to-distinguish incorrect labels. The meta-learning framework for aggregating branch outputs draws on the online bi-level optimization strategy from Meta-Weight-Net [31].

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete bottleneck in ID-PLL—incorrect candidate labels that are strongly correlated with features and thus indistinguishable from correct labels by the predictive model—by providing both a theoretical guarantee (under Tsybakov conditions, reduction-based pseudo-labels are provably more consistent with the Bayes optimal classifier) and a practical multi-branch + meta-learning implementation. The consistent SOTA across 10 datasets, including a notable gap on TinyImageNet, validates the approach. An open question the authors identify is whether sample selection for training each subspace branch could further improve efficiency, since not all training samples are informative for a given reduced label space. The added computational cost (multi-branch model + meta-learning loop) remains a practical consideration for very large-scale deployments.
