## Problem

Existing Multimodal Knowledge Graph Completion (MMKGC) methods suffer from three unresolved issues: (i) imbalanced modality coverage across entities (many entities lack visual data), (ii) heterogeneous data representations that prevent effective cross-modal alignment, and (iii) static fusion weights that ignore whether a triple is perceptual (e.g., color) or conceptual (e.g., employer), leading to suboptimal use of modality-specific evidence.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253736/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes entity text descriptions (encoded by BERT), entity images at 1024×1024 resolution (encoded by ViT, or generated by SDXL when absent), and the full set of knowledge graph triples. For a link prediction query (h,r,?) or (?,r,t), it outputs a plausibility score for every candidate entity via RotatE's complex-space rotation, producing a ranked list from which the top-K entities are selected. The generated images are produced in a two-stage SDXL pipeline (Base model for composition, Refiner model for fine detail) conditioned on CLIP text embeddings and Fourier-encoded resolution features.

## Method

LBMKGC comprises three sequential modules. First, the LvMC module uses SDXL's two-stage diffusion process (Base + Refiner with DDIM sampling) to synthesize missing visual features from text prompts, replacing adversarial training for modality completion. Second, the CMoA module aligns BERT text features and ViT visual features into a shared space via a symmetric CLIP-style contrastive loss with learnable temperature, then projects each modality through a two-layer MLP into a common embedding space. Third, the CGuAF module computes per-entity modality weights via a softmax over tanh-transformed embeddings, then blends the fused multimodal embedding with the structural embedding using a relation-specific temperature ζ_r that adapts the balance between structural and perceptual/conceptual evidence per triple type. The final scoring function is RotatE's complex-space rotation, trained with margin-based negative sampling and self-adversarial weights.

## Results

LBMKGC achieves state-of-the-art MRR on all three benchmarks: 37.23 on DB15K, 38.46 on MKG-W, and 40.03 on MKG-Y. The largest gains over the strongest baseline (MyGO) appear on MKG-W (+6.6% MRR, +7.5% Hit@3, +5.6% Hit@10) and MKG-Y (+3.5% MRR, +11.0% Hit@10). On DB15K, LBMKGC edges out MyGO by +0.16% MRR and +3.38% Hit@10, though it trails slightly on Hit@1. The method also demonstrates robustness under modality dropout, maintaining stable performance as visual information is removed at rates up to 100%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest overall baseline MyGO (AAAI 2025), LBMKGC wins on MRR for all three datasets (37.23 vs 37.17 on DB15K; 38.46 vs 36.09 on MKG-W; 40.03 vs 38.66 on MKG-Y) and on Hit@3/Hit@10 across all datasets, with the most dramatic margin being +11.0% Hit@10 on MKG-Y (50.81 vs 44.37). The sole loss is on Hit@1 for MKG-Y (33.89 vs 34.85), suggesting MyGO is slightly better at exact-first-match ranking in that setting. Against the second-best MMKGC method AdaMF-MAT (LREC/COLING 2024), LBMKGC dominates on every metric for every dataset, e.g., +2.09 MRR on DB15K and +1.42 MRR on MKG-Y. Among uni-modal KGC baselines, the gap is largest: LBMKGC outperforms RotatE by +7.95 MRR on DB15K and +5.08 MRR on MKG-Y. Among negative-sampling baselines, MMRNS (ACM MM 2022) is the closest competitor, yet LBMKGC still leads by +4.55 MRR on MKG-W and +4.10 MRR on MKG-Y.

## Relation to Prior Work

LBMKGC extends the multimodal knowledge graph completion (MMKGC) line of work by repurposing three large pre-trained models—SDXL for generative modality completion, CLIP for cross-modal alignment, and RotatE for triple scoring—into a unified pipeline. It departs from prior adversarial-training approaches to modality imbalance (MMKRL, AdaMF-MAT) by substituting a large generative visual model, and from single-modality feature extractors (VGG + BERT used independently) by enforcing a shared contrastive representation space via CLIP. The context-guided adaptive fusion module introduces a new axis of variation—perceptual vs. conceptual triple type—that prior MMKGC methods treated as a static, entity-level fusion problem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that large generative models can serve as drop-in preprocessing components for KGC, and the generalization experiments (Appendix A.2) confirm that the LvMC and CMoA modules transfer to other MMKGC backbones, suggesting a reusable infrastructure for modality-balanced knowledge representation. However, the framework is limited to text and image modalities and general-domain datasets, leaving open how it scales to audio/video or specialized domains (biomedical, social networks). A key open question is whether the SDXL-generated images carry sufficient discriminative signal for fine-grained perceptual triples, or whether the gains are primarily driven by the CLIP alignment and adaptive fusion rather than the generated content itself.
