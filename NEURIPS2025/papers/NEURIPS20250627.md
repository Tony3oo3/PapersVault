## Problem

LVLMs deployed in AR/VR and embodied robotics receive egocentric (first-person) images as input, but the narrow field of view and lack of global context cause failures on spatially or contextually demanding queries. No existing benchmark or prompting method systematically evaluates or addresses the challenge of jointly reasoning over synchronized egocentric and exocentric image pairs, leaving it unclear whether conventional LVLMs can effectively integrate complementary multi-view information.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250627/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a synchronized pair of egocentric and exocentric RGB images (sampled from 1-fps video clips, 8 frames per clip from Ego-Exo4D) along with a text question, and produces a multiple-choice answer selected from four options. The distractors are deliberately sourced from different input conditions (text-only, ego-only, exo-only, both views) to target specific failure modes. An intermediate unified scene graph—constructed by merging object, attribute, and relation triples from three perspective-specific graphs—serves as the structured reasoning scaffold before the final answer is generated.

## Method

M3CoT is a training-free, multi-agent prompting technique that constructs a unified scene representation from three complementary perspectives. Three LVLM agents each generate a scene graph: Agent F1 processes both ego and exo images simultaneously (holistic but potentially coarse), Agent F2 processes ego first then expands with exo (capturing fine-grained first-person details), and Agent F3 processes exo first then supplements with ego (capturing global context then refining). In an iterative refinement loop, each agent examines the other two agents' scene graphs and adjusts its own to better align with both images, progressively converging toward a more complete representation. At each iteration, all three agents generate candidate answers conditioned on their refined scene graphs, and majority voting determines whether a consensus answer is accepted; if no consensus is reached after a fixed number of iterations, the answer from F1 is used. This multi-perspective, multi-turn design consistently outperforms single-perspective CoT baselines (CCoT, DDCoT, CoCoT) by 4.84–5.94% on GPT-4o and Gemini 2.0 Flash, with the largest gains on numerical reasoning tasks that require integrating counts across views.

## Results

On the E3VQA benchmark, M3CoT achieves 68.58% average accuracy with GPT-4o and 66.12% with Gemini 2.0 Flash, outperforming the strongest CoT baseline CCoT by 4.84% and 5.94% respectively. The largest gains appear in Numerical Reasoning (+6.88% and +8.13% over CCoT), where integrating complementary ego-exo cues is most critical. Even the best default (no-CoT) model, GPT-4o, reaches only 60.90%, underscoring the difficulty of multi-view ego-exo reasoning. On the LEMMA-based E3VQA variant, M3CoT similarly leads with 60.19% (GPT-4o) and 56.03% (Gemini 2.0 Flash).

## Baselines & Comparisons

M3CoT consistently outperforms all three CoT baselines across both evaluated LVLMs and all four question categories. Against CCoT (the strongest baseline), M3CoT gains 4.84% on GPT-4o (68.58% vs. 63.74%) and 5.94% on Gemini 2.0 Flash (66.12% vs. 60.18%), with the largest margin in Numerical Reasoning (+6.88% and +8.13%). Over DDCoT, the gains are 4.15% (GPT-4o) and 5.03% (Gemini); over CoCoT, they are 5.71% and 5.81% respectively. Notably, DDCoT and CoCoT show inconsistent or even negative improvements in certain categories (e.g., CoCoT drops below Default on Spatial for Gemini), whereas M3CoT achieves uniform gains everywhere. On the LEMMA-based E3VQA subset, M3CoT again leads all baselines (60.19% vs. 54.02% for CoCoT on GPT-4o), confirming that the advantage stems from the multi-perspective scene-graph mechanism rather than dataset-specific artifacts.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the ego-exo data lineage (Ego-Exo4D, Charades-Ego, LEMMA) and the VQA benchmark tradition (EgoVQA, EgoSchema, EgoThink, OpenEQA) by introducing the first benchmark that requires joint reasoning over synchronized ego-exo image pairs, a setting absent from all prior VQA evaluations. Methodologically, M3CoT builds on CCoT's scene-graph-based compositional reasoning but departs from it by introducing a multi-agent, multi-perspective scene graph generation-and-refinement loop (Ego&Exo, Ego2Exo, Exo2Ego) specifically designed to capture complementary cues across the two views. Unlike DDCoT (sub-question decomposition) and CoCoT (contrastive multi-image comparison), M3CoT treats the ego-exo pair as a unified scene to be described from three distinct perspectives before iterative convergence, positioning it as a training-free prompting strategy rather than an architectural change.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

E3VQA fills a clear evaluation gap: no prior benchmark tests whether LVLMs can integrate complementary information from paired first- and third-person views, a capability essential for AR/VR assistants and embodied agents. The finding that even GPT-4o scores below 61% overall and drops below 40% on questions requiring true cross-view integration (the 'Both' subset) underscores that multi-view scene understanding remains a fundamental open problem. A key practical limitation is M3CoT's computational overhead (~47K tokens and ~38s per question on GPT-4o vs. ~2K tokens and ~3s for the default setting), which constrains real-time deployment. Open questions include whether the multi-perspective scene graph approach transfers to video, whether smaller models can benefit from lighter-weight variants, and whether the benchmark's reliance on Ego-Exo4D introduces domain biases.
