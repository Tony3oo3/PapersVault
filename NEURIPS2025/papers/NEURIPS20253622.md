## Problem

The Log Variance (LV) loss has become the de facto training objective for diffusion bridge samplers, reportedly outperforming the reverse KL (rKL) loss with the reparameterization trick. However, this paper shows that the gradient equivalence between LV and rKL (with the log-derivative trick) breaks down precisely in the diffusion bridge setting where both forward and reverse processes are learned, and that the LV loss violates the data processing inequality—a fundamental property that motivates diffusion training objectives. This raises the question of whether LV is a valid optimization target for diffusion bridges and whether a principled alternative exists.

## Input / Output

The method consumes Gaussian prior noise samples X_T ~ π_T and an energy function E(X) (evaluated during training but with intractable partition function Z) to produce samples X_0 from the unnormalized target π_0(X) ∝ exp(-E(X)). The diffusion bridge operates in continuous N-dimensional space with 128 Euler-Maruyama steps, and the forward SDE drift f_φ and reverse SDE drift r_α are parameterized by neural networks (PISgradNet with 64 hidden units). The prior distribution π_T is itself a learnable Gaussian with parameterized mean and diagonal covariance, and diffusion coefficients σ_diff can optionally be learned per-dimension.

## Method

The paper derives and compares the gradients of the LV loss and the rKL loss computed via the log-derivative trick (rKL-LD) with respect to all diffusion bridge parameters (reverse drift α, forward drift φ, and shared parameters ν including diffusion coefficients). It proves that the two losses yield identical gradients only when φ=ν=∅ (i.e., only the reverse process is learned), but produce different gradients for diffusion bridges where forward and reverse processes share parameters or where diffusion coefficients are learned. A counterexample on the unit square demonstrates that the LV loss violates the data processing inequality, undermining its theoretical justification as a bounded training objective. The paper then advocates for rKL-LD (rKL with log-derivative trick and a control variate b for variance reduction) as the training loss, and introduces learnable per-dimension diffusion coefficients σ_diff that adaptively tune the exploration-exploitation trade-off. Applied to both CMCD and DBS diffusion bridge architectures, rKL-LD with learned σ_diff consistently outperforms LV and rKL-R across Bayesian inference and synthetic multimodal benchmarks, while requiring less hyperparameter tuning and exhibiting more stable training.

## Results

On Bayesian learning benchmarks (Table 1), CMCD:rKL-LD with learned diffusion coefficients achieves the best ELBO on Brownian (1.06 vs. -6.05 for CMCD:LV, which diverges) and Credit (-504.58 vs. -628.39 for CMCD:LV, also divergent). On the high-dimensional LGCP task (1600d), DBS:rKL-LD achieves 469.89 ELBO, outperforming DBS:LV (455.49, divergent) and DBS:rKL-R (461.09). On synthetic multimodal targets (Table 2), CMCD:rKL-LD achieves the best Sinkhorn distance on GMM-40 (2301 vs. 2628 for LV) and MoS-10 (916 vs. 1182 for LV), along with the best MMD (0.08 and 0.29 respectively). The LV loss with learned σ_diff diverges in 3/5 Bayesian tasks for CMCD and 4/5 for DBS, while rKL-LD never diverges.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the LV loss (Richter & Berner, ICLR 2024), rKL-LD consistently outperforms across all five Bayesian tasks for both CMCD and DBS architectures. The gap is most dramatic when diffusion coefficients are learned: LV diverges on Credit (-628 vs. -505 for rKL-LD), Brownian (-6.05 vs. 1.06), and LGCP (455 vs. 470), while rKL-LD remains stable. On synthetic targets (GMM-40, MoS-10), CMCD:rKL-LD achieves Sinkhorn distances of 2301 and 916, beating CMCD:LV (2628 and 1182) by 12–23%, and also improves MMD from 0.13/0.36 to 0.08/0.29. Against rKL-R (Zhang & Chen, ICLR 2022), rKL-LD wins on 4/5 Bayesian tasks for CMCD (e.g., Brownian: 1.06 vs. -0.30) and on LGCP for DBS (469.89 vs. 461.09), while rKL-R retains a narrow edge on Seeds for CMCD (-74.37 vs. -73.45, though rKL-LD with learned σ_diff is competitive). The key practical finding is that rKL-LD requires significantly less hyperparameter tuning: LV with learned σ_diff diverges across all hyperparameter choices on Brownian, whereas rKL-LD achieves low ELBO regardless of initial σ_diff value.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the diffusion bridge sampler architectures of CMCD (Vargas et al., ICLR 2024) and DBS (Richter and Berner, ICLR 2024), which introduced learnable forward processes for sampling from unnormalized distributions. It departs from the dominant training paradigm by showing that the Log Variance (LV) loss, widely adopted since Richter and Berner [2024] as a replacement for the reverse KL with reparameterization trick, suffers from a fundamental theoretical flaw: it violates the data processing inequality, and the gradient equivalence between LV and rKL-with-log-derivative-trick that holds for non-learnable forward processes breaks precisely in the diffusion bridge setting where both processes are learned. The paper positions rKL-LD as the theoretically grounded alternative, connecting it to the ELBO framework via the data processing inequality for f-divergences.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that the LV loss violates the data processing inequality challenges a widely-adopted training objective in diffusion bridge samplers and provides a principled explanation for the observed training instabilities and hyperparameter sensitivity reported in prior work. The practical recommendation to use rKL-LD instead is actionable and supported by consistent empirical gains across multiple architectures, benchmarks, and metrics, while also enabling learnable diffusion coefficients that further improve performance. Open questions include whether rKL-LD combined with off-policy sample buffers (as explored for LV in Sendera et al. [2024]) yields additional benefits, whether the mode-collapse tendency of rKL-based losses can be mitigated, and whether time-dependent (rather than constant) diffusion coefficient schedules would further improve the exploration-exploitation trade-off.
