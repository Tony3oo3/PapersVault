## Problem

Existing multimodal fusion methods primarily focus on cross-modal alignment, which over-emphasizes matching marginal distributions and imposes excess regularization that shrinks intra-modal feature signals, weakening the fused representations. This problem is compounded in real-world settings where modalities are frequently missing (e.g., absent medical images), and standard imputation strategies (zero-filling, mean imputation) produce weak, unrepresentative features for the missing modality.

## Input / Output

The method consumes heterogeneous multimodal inputs: EHR time-series (76-dimensional vectors sampled every 2 hours over 48 hours), chest X-ray images resized to 224×224, and clinical notes or radiology reports truncated to 512 words; for general datasets it accepts video, audio, and text. Each modality is encoded into a shared latent space via modality-specific encoders (ResNet34, LSTM, TinyBERT, or MLP). Beyond producing a downstream prediction (binary mortality/readmission label, sentiment score, or movie trait), the model also generates imputed embeddings for missing modalities by sampling from the learned per-modality Gaussian mixture marginals.

## Method

DPMM formulates the joint multimodal feature distribution as a Dirichlet Process (DP) mixture of M×K multivariate Gaussian components, where the stick-breaking process allocates mixture weights and the DP's richer-gets-richer property automatically amplifies the most prominent intra-modal features while the shared component structure across modalities provides implicit cross-modal alignment. Stochastic variational inference with a Beta variational family over the stick-breaking weights yields closed-form posterior updates and an ELBO objective that is jointly optimized with a task-specific loss via gradient backpropagation, making the model scalable to large clinical datasets. For missing modalities, the learned marginal Gaussian mixture distributions serve as a probabilistic imputation generator, and a gradient-preserving sampling scheme allows gradients to flow through the imputed observations during training. The covariance matrices are parameterized as diagonal for computational efficiency, and the number of mixture components K is truncated without loss of expressiveness per the Ishwaran–Zarepour theorem.

## Results

On the MIMIC-III totally matched setting, DPMM achieves 0.854 AUROC for In-Hospital Mortality (IHM) prediction, outperforming the best baseline DrFuse (0.835) by 2.3% relative, and 0.754 AUROC for Readmission (READM) prediction. On MIMIC-IV, DPMM reaches 0.826 AUROC (IHM) and 0.736 AUROC (READM), with an 8% relative AUPR improvement over the best baseline on IHM. Under partially matched (missing modality) settings, DPMM maintains its lead, achieving 0.860 AUROC on both MIMIC-III and MIMIC-IV IHM tasks. On general multimodal benchmarks, DPMM attains the best MAE (1.09) and F1 (0.702) on CMU-MOSI sentiment analysis and the best correlation (0.273) on POM movie trait prediction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DPMM outperforms all five clinical baselines (MMTM, DAFT, Unified, MedFuse, DrFuse) across every metric on both MIMIC-III and MIMIC-IV for both IHM and READM tasks, under both totally and partially matched settings. The largest gains appear in AUPR: on MIMIC-IV IHM, DPMM achieves 0.482 AUPR versus 0.446 for DrFuse (the strongest clinical baseline), an 8% relative improvement. On the partially matched MIMIC-IV setting, DPMM leads by 0.6–1.1 AUROC points over the best baselines (e.g., 0.860 vs. 0.858 for DAFT on IHM), demonstrating that the DP-driven imputation of missing modalities is more effective than zero-filling (MMTM, DAFT) or global representation learning (MedFuse). On general multimodal benchmarks, DPMM achieves the best MAE (1.09) and F1 (0.702) on CMU-MOSI, narrowly beating MedFuse (MAE 1.11) and DrFuse (MAE 1.12), and the best correlation (0.273) on POM, surpassing LMF (0.266) and TFN (0.263). In supplementary comparisons, DPMM also outperforms contrastive alignment models (CLIP, ALIGN, CoMM) and recent imputation methods (PSA, LSMT) on MIMIC-IV, confirming the advantage of the implicit DP-based alignment over explicit contrastive objectives.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the variational Dirichlet process mixture framework of Blei and Jordan (2006) from single-modality density estimation to multimodal representation learning, using the stick-breaking construction to allocate mixture weights across modalities. It departs from the dominant cross-modal alignment paradigm (contrastive learning, adversarial alignment, VAE-based KL minimization) by arguing that over-emphasis on marginal distribution alignment shrinks intra-modal feature signals, and instead leverages the DP's richer-gets-richer property to simultaneously amplify prominent features and align distributions. The missing-modality imputation via learned marginal GMMs also builds on but differs from prior approaches that use zero-filling, late fusion, or global representation learning, by sampling from a DP-weighted mixture that preserves prominent feature structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical and underappreciated tension in multimodal learning: the trade-off between cross-modal alignment fidelity and intra-modal feature preservation, which is especially acute in clinical settings where modalities are frequently missing. The DP-based approach offers a principled, probabilistic mechanism for automatic feature selection without manual tuning, and the stochastic variational inference makes it scalable to large clinical datasets. Open questions include whether hierarchical DP variants could capture inter-modality structure more naturally, whether extending from diagonal to full covariance (with Wishart priors) would improve anisotropic feature capture, and how the method scales beyond three modalities where alignment complexity grows combinatorially.
