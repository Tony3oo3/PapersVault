## Problem

Neural network-based visual navigation agents overfit under data scarcity and generalize poorly to unseen environments, particularly for multi-object tasks requiring long-horizon planning. Existing approaches increase architectural complexity (larger memories, world models) which paradoxically worsens overfitting in low-data regimes. The paper tackles the fundamental challenge of coupling learned perception with explicit task-level optimization while handling two non-trivial difficulties: unreliable network predictions destabilizing downstream optimization, and the need for global information in partially observable settings.

## Input / Output

At each timestep the agent receives an egocentric RGB-D image pair, a k-dimensional binary vector indicating which of the candidate objects remain to be found, and maintains an internal egocentric map (a rotated, cropped view of an inaccessible global map). The network produces a preliminary action and state features, which are then transformed by a PICNN into a convex uncertainty set over a discrete E×E grid; the robust optimization solver outputs an optimal trajectory and object transition matrices. The final action is a convex blend of the network's proposal and the optimization-derived offset, and the optimization's objective value serves as an additional task-specific reward signal for policy updates.

## Method

NEURO couples a CNN+GRU perception module with a robust optimization planner in an end-to-end training loop. The key mechanism is a two-stage learning-planning interface: (i) a PICNN-based conformal calibration maps noisy network predictions into a convex uncertainty set Ω(x) with a (1−α) marginal coverage guarantee (Proposition 1), where the threshold q is set to the (1−α)-quantile of calibration scores; (ii) the navigation POMDP is reformulated as a pursuit-evasion game on a discrete grid, yielding a min-max robust optimization problem whose inner maximization over the uncertainty set is dualized (via strong duality and KKT conditions) into a tractable linear program. During training, the optimal trajectory provides a goal-directed action offset blended with the network action (weight λ), and the optimal objective value acts as a task reward combined with the environment reward via the Goal Vector Method; gradients propagate back to the network through implicit differentiation of the KKT conditions, enabling joint optimization of perception and planning without retraining when the task formulation changes.

## Results

On the S-MON benchmark, NEURO achieves 80% Success and 66% SPL for m=2, and 62% Success and 40% SPL for m=3, outperforming the strongest prior method Lyon (76%/62% and 57%/36% respectively). On the U-MON benchmark, NEURO reaches 62% Success and 52% SPL (m=2) and 45% Success and 45% SPL (m=3), surpassing the OracleEgoMap baseline by 8–14 points in Success. The gains are most pronounced in SPL (path-length efficiency), where NEURO improves over Lyon by 4 points (m=2) and 4 points (m=3), and over HTP-GCN by 6 and 13 points respectively, reflecting the optimization module's ability to coordinate multi-target trajectories.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NEURO outperforms all compared baselines across every task and metric. Against the strongest prior method Lyon [15], NEURO gains +4 Success and +4 SPL on S-MON m=2, and +5 Success and +4 SPL on S-MON m=3. Against HTP-GCN [17], the SPL advantage is even larger: +6 (m=2) and +13 (m=3), indicating that the robust optimization planner produces substantially more path-efficient trajectories, especially as the number of targets grows. Compared to the network-only OracleEgoMap baseline (from MultiON [23]), NEURO improves Success by 16 points (S-MON m=2) and 14 points (U-MON m=3), demonstrating that the optimization module adds significant value beyond learned perception alone. On U-MON, where no prior SoTA network-based method is reported, NEURO exceeds OracleEgoMap by 8 Success and 14 SPL (m=2) and 9 Success and 12 SPL (m=3). The generalization study (Table 5) further shows that NEURO suffers smaller relative performance drops (−2 to −12%) when transferring across different numbers of goals (m=1→3) compared to Lyon (−3 to −15%) and OracleEgoMap (−1 to −17%), confirming that the embedded optimization structure captures transferable task invariants.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the task-based end-to-end learning paradigm (Donti et al. 2017; Agrawal et al. 2019; Amos & Kolter 2017), which previously targeted short-horizon, well-structured tasks like portfolio management, to the challenging domain of embodied visual navigation under data scarcity and partial observability. It departs from the prevailing MultiON approaches (Lyon, HTP-GCN, ProjNeuralMap) that stack increasingly complex neural modules for memory and world modeling, instead coupling a relatively standard CNN+GRU perception backbone with a downstream robust optimization planner. The key technical bridge is the use of PICNNs with conformal calibration to convert noisy network predictions into convex uncertainty sets that directly parameterize the optimization constraints, and the reformulation of POMDP planning as a pursuit-evasion robust optimization problem solvable via KKT-based dualization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

NEURO addresses a practical bottleneck in embodied AI: agents trained on limited data overfit and fail to generalize to unseen environments, a critical issue for real-world deployment in search-rescue or inspection tasks. The framework's modularity—where the optimization component can be swapped for different task formulations without retraining the network—offers a practical path toward adaptable agents. However, the reliance on convex uncertainty sets via PICNNs is an acknowledged approximation that cannot capture inherently non-convex uncertainties in complex visual scenes, and the basis function expansion for scalability showed no substantial performance gain at larger grid sizes, suggesting the current formulation may be near its ceiling for indoor navigation. The power grid case study hints at broader applicability but remains a synthetic, simplified demonstration.
