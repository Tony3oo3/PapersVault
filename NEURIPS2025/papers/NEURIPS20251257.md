## Problem

Existing domain generalization methods treat domains as discrete categories or as evolving along a single temporal axis, which fails to capture the multidimensional, continuous nature of real-world distribution shifts driven by multiple interacting factors (e.g., climate, geography, socioeconomic context). This paper introduces Continuous Domain Generalization (CDG), which asks how to generalize predictive models to unseen domains defined by arbitrary combinations of continuous variations across a high-dimensional descriptor space, without relying on inherent sequential ordering.

## Input / Output

The method takes task-specific input data (satellite images, paper titles, or traffic time-series) paired with a continuous domain descriptor z ∈ R^d encoding domain-specific attributes such as climate statistics, publisher metadata, or POI distributions. It produces domain-specific model parameters θ(z) that can be transported to any unseen descriptor z_s, yielding a predictive model g(·; θ(z_s)) for classification or regression. At inference, only the nearest training descriptor's parameters are needed as a starting point; the transport operator then generalizes to the target descriptor without accessing target-domain data.

## Method

The paper proves that domain-optimal parameters form a d-dimensional embedded submanifold in R^D, then instantiates a Neural Lie Transport Operator (NeuralLio) that enforces both geometric continuity and algebraic group structure (closure, identity, associativity, invertibility) on parameter transitions. Concretely, parameters are encoded into a compact latent embedding, and transport is performed via cascaded exponential maps of d neural Lie algebra fields V^k(z_i) scaled by descriptor differences (z_j^k − z_i^k), followed by decoding back to full parameter space. A dimension-wise gating mechanism (data-dependent sigmoid gate × global trainable mask) suppresses noisy or redundant descriptor directions, while a local k-NN chart strategy restricts transport to descriptor neighborhoods to handle incomplete descriptors. Training jointly optimizes per-domain parameters, the encoder-decoder, the transport operator, and the gate by aligning predictions, latent representations, and transported parameters over local descriptor neighborhoods.

## Results

NeuralLio achieves the best performance across all six evaluation datasets, with particularly large gains on synthetic benchmarks: 3.2% error on 2-Moons (vs. 13.1% for the best descriptor-aware baseline ERM-D) and 9.5% on MNIST (vs. 26.3% for NDA). On real-world datasets, it also leads on fMoW (24.5% vs. 26.0% for DANN), ArXiv (34.7% vs. 35.3% for CIDA), Yearbook (4.8% vs. 6.6% for Koodos), and Traffic (15.1 MAE vs. 15.9 for ERM-D). The method is the only one that simultaneously handles multi-dimensional continuous descriptors and achieves strong generalization, whereas temporal baselines (TKNets, DRAIN, Koodos) are restricted to one-dimensional temporal ordering.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NeuralLio outperforms all 12 baselines on every dataset where a comparison is possible. The largest margins appear on synthetic data: on 2-Moons it reduces error from 13.1% (ERM-D, best descriptor-aware) to 3.2%, and on MNIST from 26.3% (NDA) to 9.5%, demonstrating that structural Lie-group transport captures multi-dimensional variation far better than ad-hoc descriptor conditioning or nearest-neighbor retrieval. Among descriptor-agnostic methods, DANN (26.0% on fMoW) and Mixup (16.1 MAE on Traffic) are the strongest, yet NeuralLio still leads by 1.5–2.5 points. Temporal methods (TKNets, DRAIN, Koodos) are only evaluated on the one-dimensional Yearbook dataset, where Koodos achieves 6.6% error; NeuralLio improves this to 4.8%, confirming that even in the special case of purely temporal variation, the general continuous framework retains an advantage. On ArXiv and Traffic, the gaps over the best baselines are smaller (0.6 and 0.8 points respectively), suggesting that when domain variation is more subtle or the descriptor is less informative, the structural prior provides a more modest but still consistent benefit.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Temporal Domain Generalization (TDG), particularly the authors' own prior work Koodos [6] which models continuous temporal evolution via NeuralODEs, by lifting the single-axis temporal assumption to arbitrary multi-dimensional continuous descriptor spaces. It departs from standard Domain Generalization (which treats domains as discrete, independent entities) and from CIDA [51] (which handles continuously indexed but essentially 1D domain adaptation with target data) by formulating a fully unsupervised, multi-factor generalization problem. The method's core novelty is importing Lie Group theory into the parameter transport mechanism, grounding the transport operator in geometric continuity and algebraic group axioms rather than relying on sequence modeling or distance-based alignment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled theoretical foundation (the parameter manifold theorem) for why continuous domain generalization is well-posed, and the Lie Group transport operator offers a structurally constrained alternative to ad-hoc interpolation or sequence extrapolation. However, the framework's practical applicability is gated by the availability of meaningful continuous descriptors, which the authors acknowledge as a key limitation. Open questions include discovering latent descriptors in settings where none are provided, scaling to more expressive model architectures, and whether the modest real-world gains (e.g., ~1-2% on fMoW, ArXiv, Traffic) will hold as descriptor quality improves. The dramatic synthetic results (4× improvement on 2-Moons) suggest the structural constraints matter most when the underlying variation is smooth and well-characterized.
