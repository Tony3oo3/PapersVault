## Problem

Existing neural MTPP models face a fundamental trade-off: RNN-based models (NHP, RMTPP) process events sequentially with O(N) scaling, while transformer-based models (THP, SAHP, AttNHP) achieve parallelism but scale quadratically in sequence length, making them impractical for long event histories. Although deep state-space models (SSMs) offer linear work and sublinear parallel time for discrete sequences, they have not been adapted to MTPPs because event sequences are discontinuous stochastic counting processes rather than the continuous-valued signals SSMs expect, and the conditional intensity has different left and right limits at event times.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a sequence of irregularly-spaced (time, mark) pairs in continuous time, where marks are drawn from a discrete set of K types. It produces a vector of K non-negative conditional intensities λ_t at any arbitrary time point t (not just event times), computed from continuously evolving hidden states across all L layers. Because the intensity is tied to the latent state at any t, the model can evaluate intensities at intermediate points between events in constant time given the right-limit states at events, enabling Monte Carlo estimation of the compensator integral during training without a separate parametric decoding head.

## Method

S2P2 introduces the Latent Linear Hawkes (LLH) layer, a stochastic jump differential equation that unifies the impulse dynamics of linear Hawkes processes (α dN_t) with the general latent-space dynamics of SSMs (A x dt + B u dt), operating in a P-dimensional latent space decoupled from the K-dimensional mark space. The LLH is diagonalized on the complex plane and discretized via zero-order hold to yield closed-form updates that avoid matrix exponentials, then evaluated in parallel across sequence length using prefix scans in O(log N) parallel time. The full S2P2 architecture stacks L LLH layers interleaved with GELU nonlinearities, residual connections, and LayerNorm (mirroring deep SSMs), with input-dependent dynamics (à la Mamba) that modulate the eigenvalue time-scales per channel. Non-negative marked intensities are produced by an affine projection followed by softplus applied to the top-layer hidden state, and the model is trained by maximizing the per-sequence log-likelihood with Monte Carlo estimation of the integral term.

## Results

S2P2 achieves a composite rank of 2.1 across six metrics and eight datasets, outperforming the next-best model (NHP, rank 2.9) by nearly a full rank. On per-event log-likelihood, S2P2 attains a geometric mean likelihood ratio of 1.33 (i.e., 33% higher likelihood) over the best existing method, with average rank 1.4 versus 3.0 for NHP and IFTPP. S2P2 is best or second-best on every individual metric: log-likelihood (rank 1.4), next-event time RMSE (rank 2.3), next-mark accuracy (rank 1.9), and all calibration metrics. On the challenging EHRSHOT dataset (order-of-magnitude larger than other benchmarks), S2P2 achieves -2.512 nats per event versus -3.966 for NHP and OOM for AttNHP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

S2P2 comprehensively outperforms all seven baselines on average log-likelihood (rank 1.4 vs. 3.0 for NHP/IFTPP, 3.1 for AttNHP, and 5.8–7.1 for the remaining models), with the largest gaps on EHRSHOT (-2.512 vs. -3.966 for NHP, OOM for AttNHP) and MIMIC-II (0.919 vs. 0.060 for NHP). Against the closest competitor NHP, S2P2 wins on log-likelihood, next-event time RMSE (2.3 vs. 2.4 avg rank), and next-mark accuracy (1.9 vs. 1.8 avg rank, with S2P2 leading on 6 of 8 datasets), while NHP retains a marginal edge on mark accuracy for Retweet (61.4% vs. 61.3%) and Taobao (61.5% vs. 61.1%). Transformer-based baselines (THP, SAHP, AttNHP) are substantially outperformed: THP and SAHP trail by over 4 ranks in composite score, and AttNHP additionally suffers from quadratic scaling that causes OOM on EHRSHOT. The intensity-free IFTPP is competitive on calibration but lags on likelihood (rank 3.0 vs. 1.4) and next-event time RMSE (rank 5.0 vs. 2.3). The Mamba-based MHP, the most recent SSM-for-TPP baseline, performs comparably to THP (rank 5.9 vs. 6.1) and is comprehensively outperformed by S2P2, confirming that the continuous-time latent state design (rather than simply swapping in an SSM encoder) is the key differentiator.

## Relation to Prior Work

S2P2 bridges two previously separate lines of work: classical linear Hawkes processes (LHPs), which model self-exciting event sequences with exponential decay kernels, and deep state-space models (S4, S5, Mamba), which interleave linear recurrences with position-wise nonlinearities for discrete sequence modeling. The key conceptual move is recognizing that LHP intensity dynamics are structurally analogous to SSM state dynamics, with the event impulse (dN_t) being the missing stochastic component in SSMs; the resulting Latent Linear Hawkes (LLH) layer generalizes both by operating in a higher-dimensional latent space with a general dynamics matrix. This departs from NHP (which uses a continuous-time LSTM and scales linearly), THP/AttNHP (quadratic attention), and MHP (which uses a separate parametric decoder rather than tying intensity to continuously evolving hidden states), while retaining the continuous-time, intensity-based formulation that avoids restrictive parametric assumptions on the intensity function.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

S2P2 provides a practical, open-source (via EasyTPP) MTPP model with logarithmic parallel time complexity that consistently outperforms both RNN-based and transformer-based baselines across eight diverse datasets, making it a strong default choice for long-sequence event modeling where quadratic attention is prohibitive. The continuous-time formulation without a separate parametric decoder is architecturally clean and enables exact evaluation at arbitrary times, which is important for applications like clinical event prediction over multi-year patient histories. Open questions include recovering LHP-like interpretability within the deep architecture, extending to non-categorical or spatio-temporal marks, and evaluating under realistic deployment conditions such as censoring and adversarial inputs.
