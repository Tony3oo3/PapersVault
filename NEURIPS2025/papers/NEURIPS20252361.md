## Problem

Softmax in Transformer attention causes entropy collapse, rank collapse, and training instability, while the standard remedy—enforcing doubly stochastic attention via Sinkhorn's algorithm—is iterative, non-parametric, requires non-negativity, and yields ill-conditioned gradients. No parametric (learnable) method existed to produce doubly stochastic matrices, limiting the flexibility of doubly stochastic Transformers. This paper bridges that gap by showing a variational quantum circuit (QontOT) naturally emits DSMs, providing a parametric quantum inductive bias with no known classical analogue.

## Input / Output

The method consumes images (grayscale MNIST/FashionMNIST, medical MedMNIST, compositional 2×2 grids) and IR spectra, which are split into 7 horizontal stripes plus a CLS token to form an 8×8 (or 16×16) patch sequence matching the quantum circuit's power-of-2 dimension requirement. The output is a single class label per sample. The attention matrix size is fixed by patch count, which constrains the circuit to specific qubit counts (16 qubits for 8×8 DSMs, 32 for 16×16).

## Method

The QDSFormer replaces the softmax in ViT scaled dot-product self-attention with the QontOT variational quantum circuit, which produces a doubly stochastic matrix P = U ⊙ Ū from a unitary U parameterized by θ. The authors extend QontOT's data injection from a scalar to a full matrix (M = QKᵀ) via element-wise multiplication with circuit parameters, and pad to powers of two when needed. The circuit is used in static mode with pre-trained parameters (from a 24-qubit DSM prediction experiment) rather than end-to-end differentiable training, which proved slower and less accurate due to barren plateaus. A novel quantum-inspired classical alternative based on QR decomposition (M = UR, then U ⊙ Ū) is also proposed as a non-quantum DSM operator. Empirical expressivity analysis shows QontOT produces more diverse, information-preserving, and higher-entropy DSMs than Sinkhorn's algorithm, and the QDSFormer consistently outperforms standard ViT and Sinkformer on object recognition and compositional tasks while stabilizing training.

## Results

On FashionMNIST with a 4-layer ViT, the QDSFormer (QontOT attention) achieves 90.3±0.1% validation accuracy, outperforming standard Softmax (89.7±0.3%), Sinkhorn (89.1±1.1%), and QR (89.5±0.1%). On MNIST (4-layer ViT), QontOT reaches 98.8±0.1%, matching or exceeding all baselines. Across seven MedMNIST medical imaging datasets (2-layer ViT), the QDSFormer achieves a mean test accuracy of 74.3%, surpassing the standard ViT (73.0%) and Sinkformer (70.8%). On the compositional Eureka task, the QDSFormer achieves 89.4±0.1% accuracy with the Eureka moment occurring at epoch ~44 (5/5 runs), compared to 61.1±15.0% (1/5 EM) for standard Softmax and 51.6±0.2% (0/5 EM) for Sinkhorn.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the standard ViT with Softmax attention [3], the QDSFormer consistently wins on FashionMNIST for 2–4 layers (90.0–90.3% vs. 88.9–89.7%) and on MNIST for 1 layer (93.9% vs. 89.1%), with the gap narrowing at deeper layers where both saturate near 98.8%. Against the Sinkformer (Sinkhorn-based doubly stochastic attention) [31], the QDSFormer outperforms on FashionMNIST (90.3% vs. 89.1% at 4 layers) and MNIST (98.8% vs. 97.9% at 4 layers), and by a wider margin on MedMNIST (74.3% mean vs. 70.8%) and the Eureka task (89.4% vs. 51.6%). Against NormSoftmax (Softmax_σ²) from Hoffmann et al. [25], the QDSFormer dominates across all datasets, with the largest gap on the Eureka task (89.4% vs. 51.0%) where the standard ViT and its stabilization variants fail to trigger the Eureka moment in most runs. The quantum-inspired QR decomposition baseline is competitive (within 0.5–1.0% on FashionMNIST/MNIST) but slightly trails QontOT and suffers from cubic scaling. A consistent secondary finding is that the QDSFormer exhibits substantially lower run-to-run variance (e.g., ±0.1% vs. ±12.5% for Softmax on 1-layer MNIST), indicating improved training stability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends QontOT (Mariella et al., ICML 2024), a variational quantum circuit proven to naturally emit doubly stochastic matrices, by generalizing its data injection from scalars to full matrices so it can replace softmax inside a ViT attention block. It positions itself against the Sinkformer (Sander et al., AISTATS 2022), which enforces doubly stochastic attention via Sinkhorn's algorithm—an iterative, non-parametric, and approximative procedure—and argues that a parametric quantum circuit offers a fundamentally different inductive bias with no known classical analogue. The paper also introduces a QR-decomposition-based quantum-inspired DSM operator as a classical baseline, and builds on the Eureka-moments framework (Hoffmann et al., ICML 2024) to study training stability on compositional tasks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The QDSFormer is the first parametric doubly stochastic Transformer, opening a design space where the DSM can be optimized during training rather than being a fixed post-processing step—a capability with no known classical equivalent. The expressivity analysis showing QontOT is nearly injective (unique input → unique DSM) while Sinkhorn is not, and that a static circuit matches or beats an optimized one, suggests the quantum inductive bias itself, rather than learned parameters, drives the benefit. Key limitations include reliance on statevector simulation for all training experiments, small-scale datasets (≤240k images), and the finding that real quantum hardware noise pushes DSMs toward the polytope center (higher entropy), which may or may not be beneficial at scale. Open questions include whether concurrent circuit-Transformer optimization on real hardware (via parameter-shift) can overcome barren plateaus, and whether the approach scales beyond 16×16 attention matrices.
