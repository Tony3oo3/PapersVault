## Problem

Human mobility prediction is challenging due to complex long-range dependencies and multi-scale periodic behaviors (daily routines, weekly rhythms) that existing methods fail to jointly capture. Markov and RNN-based approaches either disregard long-term periodicity or suffer vanishing gradients, while transformer-based methods treat time as static and cannot disentangle hierarchical temporal patterns. LLM-based mobility models treat trajectories as generic token streams, ignoring the structured cyclical regularity that makes 93% of daily trajectories predictable.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a 7-day historical trajectory (336 time slots at 30-minute resolution) with timestamps and grid-cell locations (200×200 city grid), plus 48 future timestamps (1-day prediction horizon). Each day is partitioned into 48 half-hour slots, and the segment length is set to 48 slots (one day). Output is a probability distribution over all candidate locations for each future time slot, from which top-k predictions and MRR are derived.

## Method

RHYTHM partitions the embedded trajectory into daily segments and applies a two-level hierarchical attention: intra-segment attention captures local temporal dependencies within each day, and a learnable pooling operation condenses each segment into a discrete token that undergoes inter-segment attention to model cross-day and weekly dependencies, reducing effective sequence length from T to N and quadratically lowering attention cost. Each segment token is enriched with a pre-computed semantic embedding obtained by running a frozen LLM over a natural-language prompt describing the trajectory segment (or task description for future timestamps), which is added element-wise to the segment embedding without extending context length. The combined embeddings are fed into a frozen pretrained LLM backbone (e.g., Llama-3.2-1B) that performs in-context reasoning over the aligned temporal-semantic representations, and a lightweight output projection maps the final hidden states to location logits. Only 12.37% of total parameters are trainable (spatio-temporal embeddings, attention layers, pooling, and output projection), yielding a 24.6% reduction in training time versus the best baseline while preserving the LLM's reasoning capabilities.

## Results

On three real-world mobility datasets (Kumamoto, Sapporo, Hiroshima) from YJMob100K, RHYTHM-Llama-3B achieves the best Acc@1 across all cities (0.2941, 0.2938, 0.2929), while RHYTHM-Gemma-2B leads on Acc@5 (0.5932, 0.5545, 0.5798). Overall, RHYTHM delivers a 2.4% improvement in Accuracy@1 and a 1.0% gain in Accuracy@5 over the strongest baseline, with a 5.0% accuracy increase specifically on weekends. The model also achieves the best MRR (0.4220 on Sapporo) and best DTW on Sapporo (3745), while reducing training time by 24.6% compared to the best-performing baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RHYTHM outperforms all 10 baselines on Acc@1 across all three datasets, with the largest margins over LSTM-based methods (LSTM, DeepMove) and the LLM-based Mobility-LLM, which underperforms likely due to its design focus on visiting-intention tasks rather than precise location ranking. Among transformer-based baselines, CMHSA and COLA are the closest competitors: CMHSA edges out RHYTHM-Llama-1B on Kumamoto Acc@5 (0.5887 vs. 0.5835), but RHYTHM-Gemma-2B and Llama-3B surpass it (0.5932, 0.5947). COLA leads on BLEU scores for all cities (e.g., 0.2496 on Sapporo) due to its post-hoc frequency recalibration, while RHYTHM ranks second on Kumamoto BLEU (0.1928) but achieves the best MRR (0.4220) and best DTW on Sapporo (3745). Time-LLM, despite sharing an LLM backbone, trails RHYTHM by 2+ points on Acc@1, confirming that generic time-series reprogramming is less effective than structured temporal tokenization for mobility. The ablation confirms temporal tokenization is the most critical component (5.39% drop when removed), followed by hierarchical attention (0.90%) and semantic prompts (1.82% combined).

## Relation to Prior Work

RHYTHM adapts the temporal tokenization mechanism from AutoTimes [40]—partitioning sequences into segments with intra-segment attention and pooling—into the human mobility domain, and adopts the LLM-as-frozen-sequential-representation-extractor paradigm from FPT [81] along with its theoretical convergence guarantees. It departs from prior LLM-based mobility models (Mobility-LLM, LLM-Mob) by keeping the LLM backbone entirely frozen and injecting semantic context via pre-computed prompt embeddings rather than fine-tuning or in-context prompting. Conceptually, it positions human mobility as a compositional structure analogous to language (daily routines as tokens, weekly rhythms as syntax), which distinguishes it from transformer-based mobility models (PMT, COLA, ST-MoE-BERT) that treat time as a flat sequence without explicit multi-scale periodicity decomposition.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The parameter-efficient design (12.37% trainable parameters, 24.6% training time reduction) makes LLM-based mobility prediction more accessible for resource-constrained deployment, and the plug-and-play backbone design allows practitioners to swap LLMs without re-architecting the pipeline. The finding that RHYTHM's advantage concentrates on weekends and irregular periods (5.0% improvement) while matching baselines on deterministic routines suggests the LLM reasoning component specifically handles non-routine decision-making rather than pattern memorization. Open questions include whether autoregressive decoding could better mimic step-by-step mobility decisions, whether quantization can further reduce the still-substantial training cost, and whether the approach generalizes to longer prediction horizons or sparser trajectories beyond the 7-day/48-slot setting tested here.
