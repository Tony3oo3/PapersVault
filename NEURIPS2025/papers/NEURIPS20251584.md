## Problem

Existing unified multimodal models either restrict themselves to text and image modalities, rely on decoupled representations (e.g., CLIP for understanding, VAE for generation), or assemble separate specialized models via adapters. A key open challenge is how to natively unify understanding and generation within a single model that scales to video while preserving language knowledge, without the prohibitive cost of web-scale text corpora during training.

## Input / Output

The model consumes interleaved sequences of text tokens, image latents, and video latents, where images and videos are first encoded by a 3D causal VAE into a shared latent space (a single-frame image is treated as a one-frame video). Special boundary tokens ([BOI]/[EOI] for images, [BOV]/[EOV] for videos) delimit visual segments within the sequence. Outputs are produced by two dedicated heads: a language head that autoregressively decodes text tokens, and a flow head that iteratively denoises visual latents via flow matching, which are then decoded back to pixel space by the 3D causal VAE decoder. The model supports 512×512 image generation (with an AnyRes strategy at inference for higher token counts) and up to 32-frame video generation.

## Method

Show-o2 operates entirely in the 3D causal VAE latent space and constructs unified visual representations through a dual-path mechanism: a semantic path (SigLIP-based vision transformer blocks, pre-distilled to extract features from both clean and noised latents) and a low-level path (a 2D patch embedding projector), which are fused via spatial(-temporal) concatenation, RMSNorm, and MLP layers. The fused representations are fed into a pre-trained language model (Qwen2.5) under an omni-attention scheme that enforces causal attention along the sequence but full attention within each visual segment. A language head performs next-token prediction for text, while a flow head (DiT-style transformer layers with adaLN-Zero time modulation) predicts the velocity field for flow matching-based image/video generation. Training follows a two-stage recipe: Stage 1 trains only the projector, fusion module, and flow head on ~66M image-text pairs plus video and interleaved data; Stage 2 fine-tunes the full model on high-quality understanding and generation data, preserving language knowledge without requiring a massive text corpus. For scaling from 1.5B to 7B, the pre-trained flow head is resumed with a lightweight MLP to align hidden dimensions.

## Results

On multimodal understanding, Show-o2-7B achieves 1620.5 on MME-perception, 63.1 on GQA, 69.8 on SEED-Bench, 79.3 on MMBench, 48.9 on MMMU-val, 56.6 on MMStar, and 78.6 on AI2D, surpassing all native unified baselines including Janus-Pro-7B (1567.1 MME-p) and Mogao-7B (1592.0 MME-p). On image generation, Show-o2-7B scores 0.76 overall on GenEval and 86.14 on DPG-Bench, outperforming SD3-Medium (84.08 DPG) and Janus-Pro-7B (84.19 DPG) while using only 66M image-text pairs versus 144M for Janus-Pro. On text-to-video generation (VBench), Show-o2 with 2B total parameters achieves 81.34 overall, exceeding Show-1 (6B, 78.93), Emu3 (8B, 80.96), and VILA-U (7B, 74.01).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Show-o2-7B outperforms all native unified 7B baselines on MME-perception (1620.5 vs. Janus-Pro 1567.1, Mogao 1592.0) and GQA (63.1 vs. 62.0, 60.9), and also surpasses the much larger TokenFlow-XL (14B, 1551.1 MME-p) and LLaVA-OV (7B, 1580.0 MME-p). On GenEval, Show-o2-7B (0.76) beats Show-o (0.68), Transfusion (0.63), and D-DiT (0.65), but trails BAGEL (0.88, 14B, 1600M data) and Mogao (0.89, 7B). On DPG-Bench, Show-o2-7B (86.14) achieves the best score among all unified models, exceeding SD3-Medium (84.08), Janus-Pro (84.19), and Mogao (84.33). For text-to-video on VBench, Show-o2 (2B total params, 81.34) outperforms Show-1 (6B, 78.93), Emu3 (8B, 80.96), and VILA-U (7B, 74.01), approaching CogVideoX (5B, 81.61) and Step-Video-T2V (30B, 81.83). The 1.5B variant matches or exceeds Janus-Pro-1.5B on MME-p (1450.9 vs. 1444.0) and MMMU (37.1 vs. 36.3) while reporting more complete benchmark coverage. Notably, Show-o2 achieves these results with only 66M image-text pairs for generation training, compared to 144M for Janus-Pro and 1600M for BAGEL.

## Relation to Prior Work

Show-o2 directly extends Show-o's omni-attention unified architecture by replacing the 2D VAE with a 3D causal VAE (from Wan) to natively accommodate video alongside images, and introduces a dual-path spatial(-temporal) fusion mechanism that combines SigLIP-distilled semantic features with low-level projector features. Unlike prior native unified models (Chameleon, Transfusion, Emu3) that primarily target text+image, Show-o2 positions video as a first-class modality within the same autoregressive + flow-matching framework. The two-stage training recipe departs from prior UMMs that co-train with web-scale text corpora, instead freezing the language model in stage-1 and only fine-tuning the full model on curated multimodal data in stage-2 to avoid catastrophic forgetting of language knowledge.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a single ~2B parameter model can be competitive with 6B+ dedicated video generation models (Show-1, Emu3) on VBench, suggesting that unified architectures need not sacrifice generative quality for multimodal versatility. The two-stage training recipe is a practically important finding: one-stage co-training with RefinedWeb causes severe language degradation (MMLU drops from ~60 to ~28), while the proposed recipe preserves near-original LLM performance without requiring massive text data. Key limitations include very weak text rendering in generated images (0.002 on OneIG-Bench text), underperformance on chart understanding due to limited OCR data during SigLIP distillation, and video generation that, while competitive, does not match dedicated 30B models like Step-Video-T2V. Open questions include whether the dual-path fusion generalizes to higher resolutions and longer video sequences, and whether the approach can be extended to audio or 3D modalities.
