## Problem

Supervised point cloud denoising requires expensive clean-noisy pairs from manual CAD modeling, while existing unsupervised methods (e.g., TotalDenoising) rely solely on global spatial priors and fail to recover high-fidelity local geometries. The core gap is the absence of a local-level, point-to-point matching mechanism that can operate without clean supervision, compounded by the lack of consistency across denoising predictions from different noisy observations of the same shape.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253232/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes multiple noisy point cloud observations of the same shape or scene (split into 1K-point patches from 10K or 50K point clouds with 1–3% Gaussian noise) and produces a denoised point cloud via a 4-step iterative displacement. For the upsampling application, a sparse point cloud is independently noised r times to create a dense noisy input, and the denoised output serves as the upsampled result. For image denoising, 256×256 patches with Poisson noise (λ=10, 25, 50) are processed through a modified ZS-N2N pipeline that outputs a single denoised image.

## Method

U-CAN uses a multi-step denoising network where each step consists of a DGCNN-based feature extractor (Dynamic EdgeConv with residual connections) and a linear path predictor that outputs per-point displacement vectors, iteratively refining the point cloud over N=4 steps. The key training innovation is the Noise-to-Noise Matching loss (L_N2N), which replaces the standard Chamfer Distance with Earth Mover's Distance (EMD) to enforce one-to-one point correspondences between the denoised output and a second noisy observation, thereby satisfying the statistical assumptions of the Noise2Noise framework in the unordered 3D setting. A second loss, the Denoising Consistency constraint (L_DC), minimizes EMD between denoised predictions from two different noisy observations of the same shape, resolving the ambiguity of unknown true surface locations and stabilizing local geometry recovery. Ablations confirm that EMD is essential (CD degrades performance, DCD causes divergence) and that L_DC is critical for high-noise regimes. The consistency constraint is further shown to be domain-general by transferring it to 2D image denoising, where adding L_DC to ZS-N2N yields ~1 dB PSNR improvement.

## Results

On the PUNet dataset (10K points), U-CAN achieves CD×10⁴ = 2.497 / P2M×10⁴ = 1.105 at 1% noise, 3.234 / 1.255 at 2%, and 3.666 / 1.842 at 3%, outperforming all unsupervised baselines and rivaling supervised methods. On McMaster18 for unsupervised image denoising, U-CAN (modified ZS-N2N) reaches 31.03 dB PSNR at λ=50, 29.14 dB at λ=25, and 26.52 dB at λ=10, surpassing the ZS-N2N baseline by ~0.7 dB. For point cloud upsampling on PU-Net, U-CAN achieves CD=1.532 / P2M=0.585 (5K) and CD=1.212 / P2M=0.587 (10K), beating the supervised PU-Net (CD=3.445 / P2M=1.669 at 5K) without requiring any clean or sparse-dense pairs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

For point cloud denoising on PUNet, U-CAN significantly outperforms all unsupervised baselines—TTD, DMR-TTD, and ScoreDenoise-TTD—which rely only on global (total-level) constraints and struggle with local geometry fidelity; at 10K resolution under 2% and 3% noise, U-CAN beats every supervised method (PCNet, GPDNet, DMR, ScoreDenoise, PointFilter) as well. Traditional optimization methods (Bilateral, Jet, MRPCA, GLR) degrade under unseen noise variances. For image denoising on McMaster18, adding the denoising consistency loss L_DC to ZS-N2N yields a consistent ~0.7 dB PSNR gain over ZS-N2N (31.03 vs. 30.36 at λ=50) and outperforms all other dataset-free methods (DIP, S2S, BM3D) and dataset-based methods (N2C, NB2NB, N2V). For upsampling on PU-Net, U-CAN (CD=1.532, P2M=0.585 at 5K) more than doubles the performance of the supervised PU-Net (CD=3.445, P2M=1.669) and substantially outperforms the unsupervised DMR-TTD (CD=5.846) and ScoreDenoise-TTD (CD=3.286), all without requiring clean point clouds or sparse-dense pairs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the Noise2Noise framework (Lehtinen et al., 2018) from 2D image denoising to 3D point clouds, overcoming the fundamental obstacle that point clouds lack the one-to-one pixel correspondences on which Noise2Noise relies. It departs from prior unsupervised point cloud denoising (TotalDenoising, DMR-TTD, ScoreDenoise-TTD) by replacing their coarse global spatial constraints with a point-wise EMD-based matching loss that enforces one-to-one correspondences, and by adding a denoising consistency constraint that has no direct analogue in prior work. The architecture builds on DGCNN's Dynamic EdgeConv for feature extraction, and the multi-step denoising path design echoes iterative approaches like ScoreDenoise, while the consistency constraint is demonstrated to also improve the zero-shot image denoising method ZS-N2N (Mansour & Heckel, 2023).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical impact is that high-quality point cloud denoising becomes feasible without any clean ground-truth geometry, which is critical given the scarcity of manually created clean 3D models versus the abundance of real noisy scans. The denoising consistency constraint is a simple, general principle that transfers across domains (3D point clouds and 2D images), suggesting it could be adopted as a regularizer in other unsupervised restoration tasks. Open questions include how the method handles non-Gaussian or structured noise (e.g., sensor-specific artifacts), scalability to very large outdoor scenes beyond the tested Paris-rue-Madame dataset, and whether the EMD-based matching remains tractable at much higher point counts. The method also requires multiple noisy observations of the same shape, which may not always be available in practice.
