## Problem

Personalized subgraph federated learning requires the server to measure inter-client similarity for weighted aggregation, but existing approaches—comparing high-dimensional parameter matrices, communication-level gradients, or proxy-graph embeddings—are either unreliable under the curse of dimensionality, heuristic, or require sharing node embeddings that leak structural information. The paper seeks a compact, differentiable, and privacy-preserving client signature derived solely from model parameters that faithfully captures subgraph heterogeneity.

## Input / Output

Each client holds a subgraph with node features (N_k × d), edge structure, and labels; the server initializes and broadcasts a global GNN and a d'-dimensional auxiliary projection vector (APV). After local training, clients upload only their GNN weights and learned APV—never raw data, node embeddings, or gradients. The server outputs a personalized GNN parameter set and a personalized APV for each client, where the APV is a unit-norm vector that provably aligns with the first principal component of that client's node embeddings.

## Method

FedAux augments each local GNN with a learnable APV that defines a 1D projection axis. During local training, node embeddings are projected onto the APV to obtain scalar similarity scores, and a Gaussian-kernel-weighted aggregation (replacing the non-differentiable hard sort + Conv1D pipeline) produces smoothed neighborhood embeddings that are concatenated with the original GNN embeddings before an MLP classifier. The kernel aggregator is fully differentiable with respect to the APV, so standard backpropagation jointly refines both the GNN and the APV; Theorem 3.1 proves the APV converges to the principal eigenvector of the local embedding covariance via an Oja-learning-rule equivalence. On the server side, cosine similarity between clients' APVs is passed through a temperature-scaled softmax to yield personalized aggregation weights, which are applied to both GNN parameters and APVs, enabling similarity-aware mixing without any proxy graph, gradient logging, or embedding exchange.

## Results

FedAux achieves the best federated node classification accuracy across nearly all 18 dataset–client-count combinations in Table 1. On the 10-client setting, it reaches 82.05% on Cora, 73.16% on CiteSeer, 86.43% on PubMed, 89.92% on Amazon-Computer, 92.30% on Amazon-Photo, and 68.50% on ogbn-arxiv. Relative to the strongest competitor FED-PUB, FedAux gains 0.2–2.4 percentage points, with the largest margins on ogbn-arxiv (+2.15 pp at 10 clients) and CiteSeer (+1.33 pp at 10 clients). Compared to the canonical FedAvg, the average accuracy gain is approximately 4.5% across all settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedAux outperforms all eight baselines on 17 of 18 dataset–client-count combinations; the sole exception is Amazon-Photo with 10 clients, where FED-PUB edges ahead by 0.16 pp (92.46 vs. 92.30). The closest competitor is FED-PUB, which uses functional embeddings on a server-side proxy graph for similarity-weighted aggregation; FedAux surpasses it by 0.2–2.4 pp, with the largest gap on ogbn-arxiv (68.50 vs. 66.35 at 10 clients, +2.15 pp) and CiteSeer (73.16 vs. 71.83, +1.33 pp). General FL baselines FedAvg and FedProx suffer the most under non-IID conditions, losing 5–15 pp to FedAux on Cora and CiteSeer (e.g., FedAvg drops to 69.19% on Cora-10 vs. FedAux's 82.05%). Graph-specific baselines GCFL and FedPer are competitive on the more homogeneous PubMed and Amazon-Photo datasets but fall 1–3 pp behind FedAux on the more heterogeneous citation networks. The paper also reports that FedAux's accuracy degrades least as client count grows from 5 to 20 (only 2.9 pp drop on Cora vs. ~7 pp for FedAvg/FedProx), confirming robustness under increasing non-IIDness.

## Relation to Prior Work

FedAux sits at the intersection of personalized federated learning (Ditto, FedPer) and subgraph FL (FedSage+, FED-PUB, GCFL), where the central challenge is computing reliable client similarity without accessing raw data or embeddings. Prior approaches either use hard gradient-based clustering (GCFL), require constructing a server-side proxy graph (FED-PUB), or share structural information that risks leakage (FedSSP, FedEgo). FedAux departs from these by learning a compact auxiliary projection vector (APV) end-to-end with the local GNN, replacing the non-differentiable hard-sort-then-Conv1D pipeline from non-local GNNs [18] with a Gaussian-kernel soft aggregation that admits standard backpropagation. The theoretical analysis connects the APV's optimization dynamics to Oja's principal-component learning rule, providing a principled justification absent in prior heuristic similarity measures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The APV offers a lightweight, privacy-preserving client signature that can be plugged into existing subgraph FL frameworks (demonstrated with FedGTA and FedSage+), potentially becoming a standard component for similarity-aware aggregation in graph FL. The O(N²) kernel computation per client and the fixed bandwidth σ=1 are practical limitations for very large subgraphs, and the PL-condition assumption in the convergence proof is strong. Open questions include whether the approach extends to graph-level or link-prediction tasks, how it behaves under adversarial (beyond honest-but-curious) server models, and whether the principal-component interpretation of the APV holds when the local GNN is deep and non-convex.
