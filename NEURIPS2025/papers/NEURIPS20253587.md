## Problem

Existing Resonate-and-Fire (RF) neurons for long sequence modeling suffer from two fundamental limitations: their narrow bandwidth causes them to behave like simplified single-frequency resonators, unable to capture diverse temporal features, and they face an inherent trade-off between energy efficiency (sparse spiking via reset mechanisms) and training speed (parallelizable computation without temporal unfolding). This paper addresses both gaps simultaneously through a biologically inspired dendritic architecture and an adaptive thresholding strategy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253587/cropped_page3_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 1D sequential input signals of varying lengths (250 timesteps for SHD, 784 for S/PS-MNIST, 1024 for S-CIFAR10, up to 4096 for LRA Text tasks) and produces class labels for classification. Internally, the input is processed through complex-valued membrane potentials in multiple dendritic branches, and the soma emits binary spike trains whose sparsity is controlled by the adaptive threshold. The model operates as a feedforward (parallel) architecture rather than a recurrent one, enabling FFT-based convolution for both forward and backward passes.

## Method

The D-RF neuron comprises a multi-dendritic structure where each branch i has its own decay factor τ_i and oscillation frequency ω_i, collectively achieving broad spectral coverage (B_eff ≈ Σ β_i · τ_i/δ) compared to a single RF neuron's narrow band-pass response. The soma integrates weighted real parts of all branch states and fires when the potential exceeds an adaptive threshold V_th[t] that is dynamically raised based on recent spiking history, implemented as a 1D causal convolution with learnable kernel [α_1,...,α_n]. This adaptive threshold promotes sparser spikes without requiring temporal unfolding, since the gradient at each timestep depends only on the current state. Training leverages the time-invariance of the dendritic dynamics to reformulate membrane potential accumulation as an FFT-based convolution (O(L log L) complexity), and the adaptive threshold removes recursive temporal dependencies in backpropagation, enabling fully parallelized training with a double Gaussian surrogate gradient.

## Results

On the LRA benchmark, D-RF achieves 82.88% average accuracy across five tasks, outperforming all SNN baselines (SpikingSSMs: 82.23%, SD-TCM: 82.39%) and approaching the ANN S4 model (84.03%) within 1.15%. On the more challenging S-CIFAR10 (1024 timesteps), D-RF reaches 84.30% accuracy, surpassing PMSN (82.14%) by 2.16%. In terms of energy efficiency, D-RF achieves the lowest average spiking rate (6.1%) and energy cost (135.8 mJ) among all compared methods, reducing spiking by 49.7% on Image tasks relative to SD-TCM.

## Baselines & Comparisons

D-RF outperforms all SNN baselines on the LRA benchmark average (82.88% vs. SpikingSSMs 82.23%, SD-TCM 82.39%, Binary S4D+GSU 82.52%), and specifically beats SpikingSSMs on Text (86.52% vs. 80.41%) and Retrieval (90.02% vs. 88.77%), while matching or exceeding SD-TCM on ListOps (60.02% vs. 59.20%). On S-CIFAR10, D-RF achieves 84.30% vs. PMSN's 82.14% and SPSN's 70.23%; on SHD it reaches 96.20% vs. PMSN's 95.10% and BRF's 92.50%. However, D-RF underperforms SpikingSSMs on the Image task (85.32% vs. 88.21%) and Pathfinder (92.36% vs. 93.51%), which the authors attribute to SpikingSSMs' use of LayerNorm reducing temporal variance. In energy efficiency, D-RF achieves the lowest spiking rate (6.1% avg) and energy cost (135.8 mJ avg) across all LRA tasks, compared to SpikingSSMs (11.9%, 290.1 mJ) and SD-TCM (9.3%, 220.6 mJ). For training speed, D-RF achieves a 581× speedup over BPTT at sequence length 32768 and 4.2× acceleration over the SDN method, with the adaptive threshold enabling up to 147× training acceleration by converting serial dynamics into parallelizable convolution.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Resonate-and-Fire (RF) neuron lineage initiated by Izhikevich [27] and the parallel RF formulation of Huang et al. (PRF) [24], which recast RF dynamics as an FFT-based convolution for O(L log L) training. The key departure is twofold: (1) replacing the single-branch RF resonator with a multi-dendritic architecture where each branch has its own decay factor and oscillation frequency, collectively broadening the effective bandwidth from a narrow band-pass to near-full spectral coverage; and (2) introducing an adaptive threshold implemented as a causal 1D convolution over historical spikes, which resolves the previously identified trade-off between sparse spiking (requiring time-varying resets, hence O(L²) BPTT) and parallel training (requiring time-invariant dynamics, hence burst firing). Conceptually, the dendritic structure draws from biological multi-compartment neuron models [44, 61, 38], while the adaptive threshold mechanism is a novel reformulation that preserves the parallelizable convolutional structure of PRF while adding spike-dependent inhibition.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The D-RF neuron offers a principled resolution to a concrete trade-off in RF-based SNNs: prior work forced a choice between energy-efficient sparse spiking (via time-varying resets at O(L²) training cost) and fast parallel training (via time-invariant dynamics at the cost of continuous burst firing). By encoding the reset as a learnable causal convolution over past spikes, D-RF achieves both sparse output and O(L log L) training, which is practically important for deploying SNNs on resource-constrained edge hardware. Limitations include a notable gap on the LRA Image task (85.32% vs. SpikingSSM's 88.21%, attributed to the absence of LayerNorm) and the fact that the adaptive threshold's effectiveness depends on the surrogate gradient choice. Open questions include whether the multi-dendritic design scales gracefully to very deep networks, whether the frequency-band assignment can be made fully learnable rather than partially fixed, and whether the approach transfers to non-stationary or multimodal temporal signals beyond the benchmarks tested.
