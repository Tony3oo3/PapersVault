## Problem

Existing CLIP data filtering methods select training examples based on heuristic proxies for usefulness—CLIP similarity scores, hyperbolic distances, or downstream similarity metrics—rather than directly optimizing for how well each example improves downstream performance. This paper asks whether one can learn a scoring function that is explicitly trained to predict each example's contribution to a model's zero-shot accuracy, closing the gap between filtering criteria and the actual evaluation objective.

## Input / Output

The scoring model (M-FLYT) takes a 12-dimensional feature vector per image-text pair, composed of standardized scores from pre-computed methods (OpenAI ViT-B/32 and ViT-L/14 CLIP scores, two DFN variants, three HYPE components, s-CLIPLoss, NormSim∞, two binary classifiers, and an E-FLYT embedding score), and outputs a single scalar usefulness score. These scores are converted to a probability distribution over the full 128M-example candidate pool via softmax, which Soft Cap Sampling then uses to draw a 128M-example filtered dataset where individual examples can appear up to 56 times, controlled by a repetition penalty α.

## Method

FLYT jointly trains a scoring model q_φ and a reference CLIP model f_θ in a meta-learning loop: the scoring model assigns per-example weights via softmax, a novel Weighted CLIP Loss (WCL) uses these weights to update the reference model, and the updated reference model is then evaluated on a downstream batch (ImageNet training set) whose loss gradient backpropagates through the reference model all the way to the scoring model parameters. The WCL modifies the standard contrastive loss by multiplying both the numerator and denominator of each example's softmax term by its weight, ensuring that zero-weight examples are fully excluded and uniform weights recover the standard CLIP loss. M-FLYT instantiates the scoring model as a simple linear layer over 12 pre-computed heuristic scores, learning optimal mixing weights end-to-end. Soft Cap Sampling (SCS) converts the learned probability distribution into a concrete dataset by iteratively sampling batches of 100K examples without replacement and subtracting a penalty α from each sampled example's score, interpolating between pure sampling (over-repetition) and threshold filtering (under-repetition).

## Results

On the DataComp medium scale filtering benchmark, M-FLYT+SCS achieves 40.1% zero-shot ImageNet accuracy and 37.7% average accuracy across all 38 evaluation tasks, using only publicly available resources. This represents a 2.0% absolute improvement in ImageNet accuracy over the best prior method (HYPE+DFN+s-CLIPLoss, 38.1%) and a 5.5% improvement over the best public-resource-only method (DFN-FT, 34.2%). On the DataComp small scale benchmark, M-FLYT+SCS achieves 8.0% ImageNet and 18.5% average accuracy, outperforming all prior methods on both metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

M-FLYT+SCS (40.1% ImageNet, 37.7% Average) outperforms all public-resource baselines by a wide margin: +5.9% ImageNet over DFN-FT (34.2%), +6.3% over HYPE 20% (33.8%), and +6.8% over s-CLIPLoss (33.3%). Against the strongest non-public baseline, HYPE+DFN+s-CLIPLoss (38.1% ImageNet, 37.8% Average), M-FLYT+SCS gains +2.0% on ImageNet while matching average accuracy within 0.1%. Notably, M-FLYT+SCS achieves the best VTAB score (0.396) and best ImageNet distribution shift score (0.311) among all methods, while its retrieval score (0.292) is slightly below the best non-public methods (0.284–0.287). On the small scale benchmark, M-FLYT+SCS (8.0% ImageNet, 18.5% Average) beats the prior best CAM∩FlippedCLIP (6.4%, 17.8%) by 1.6% and 0.7% respectively, and outperforms EcoDatum, WS, and HYPE on both metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the CLIP contrastive pretraining framework (Radford et al., 2021) and the DataComp filtering benchmark (Gadre et al., 2023), which established a fixed-pool, fixed-pipeline setting for comparing data curation methods. Conceptually, FLYT draws on the learned data weighting literature (Ren et al., 2018; Shu et al., 2019), which uses validation-set gradients to train per-example weight models, but repurposes this for the pretraining regime where the 'validation signal' comes from a downstream task loss flowing through a reference CLIP model. The key departure from prior filtering work (DFN, HYPE, s-CLIPLoss, T-MARS) is that instead of relying on hand-designed heuristic proxies for usefulness, FLYT directly optimizes a differentiable objective that measures each example's contribution to downstream performance, and M-FLYT learns to mix existing heuristic scores rather than applying fixed aggregation rules.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 2% absolute ImageNet improvement and the principled, loss-optimized framing of data filtering offer a more systematic alternative to the ad-hoc heuristic combinations that have dominated the DataComp leaderboard. The SCS finding—that allowing up to 56 repetitions of top examples outperforms the standard top-20% strategy (max 6 repetitions) by 4.2% on ImageNet—challenges the prevailing quality-quantity tradeoff assumption and suggests non-uniform sampling is an underexploited degree of freedom. Key open questions include whether the approach scales to DataComp large/xlarge, whether E-FLYT can be made competitive without relying on external heuristic scores, and whether the scoring model can be iteratively retrained in a closed data-selection/pretraining loop. The method's dependence on a fixed downstream proxy (ImageNet training set) also limits its generality across the 38 diverse DataComp evaluation tasks.
