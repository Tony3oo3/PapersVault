## Problem

Existing sparse-field reconstruction methods—whether deterministic (VoronoiCNN, Senseiver) or generative (DiffusionPDE, S³GM)—rely on randomly or arbitrarily fixed sensor placements, leaving the synergistic co-optimization of reconstruction quality and sensor placement unexplored. Poorly placed sensors create spatial blind spots that no reconstruction model can fully compensate for, yet no principled strategy exists to jointly optimize both components for accurate physics sensing.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251200/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes sparse scalar-field observations (velocity, temperature, or pressure) at a variable number of sensor positions, along with the domain geometry (regular 2D grids up to 360×180, or irregular 3D meshes with ~95k points), and produces a dense physical field reconstruction plus optimized sensor placements. During training, both the number and positions of sensors are randomized to ensure the reconstructor generalizes across all feasible placements; during placement optimization, the sensor count is fixed and only positions are updated. The method handles land-mask constraints (sea temperature) and surface-only placement constraints (3D car mesh) via projection onto the feasible domain.

## Method

PhySense is a two-stage framework. Stage 1 trains a flow-matching reconstructor that learns a velocity field mapping Gaussian noise to physical fields, conditioned on sensor positions and sparse observations via a cross-attention mechanism; DiT is used for regular grids and Transolver for irregular meshes. Stage 2 optimizes sensor placements via projected gradient descent on the residual flow loss, projecting updated positions back onto the feasible geometry (e.g., a 3D car surface) at each step. The key theoretical contribution proves that the flow-loss objective is polynomially bounded by the classical A-optimality (trace-of-covariance) criterion, providing a variance-minimization guarantee for the optimized placements. Additionally, the progressive noise-data mixture inherent to flow matching yields more informative placement feedback than a pure-noise input, as confirmed by ablation.

## Results

PhySense-opt achieves state-of-the-art relative L2 error across all three benchmarks and all sensor counts. On turbulent flow, it reduces error from 0.5746 (best baseline, Senseiver, 30 sensors) to 0.2157 (62.5% improvement); on sea temperature, from 0.0719 (SSPOR, 15 sensors) to 0.0439 (38.9% improvement); and on car aerodynamics, from 0.1053 (Senseiver, 15 sensors) to 0.0386 (63.3% improvement). The paper reports an average 49% relative gain over the best baseline across all settings. Notably, 15 optimized sensors on sea temperature (covering only 0.023% of the spatial domain) match the accuracy of 100 randomly placed sensors.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PhySense-opt outperforms all five baselines across every benchmark and sensor count. Against the strongest deterministic baseline Senseiver, PhySense-opt achieves 62.5% lower relative L2 error at 30 sensors on turbulent flow (0.2157 vs. 0.5746) and 43.2% lower error at 25 sensors on sea temperature (0.0437 vs. 0.0769). Against the generative baselines S³GM and DiffusionPDE, PhySense-opt is consistently superior, with the gap widening at low sensor counts (e.g., 0.2157 vs. 0.6421 for S³GM and 0.9163 for DiffusionPDE at 30 turbulent flow sensors). The classical SSPOR method performs reasonably on sea temperature (0.0719 at 15 sensors) due to the relatively stable temporal dynamics, but degrades severely on turbulent flow (0.6637 at 30 sensors), highlighting its inability to capture non-stationary fields. On the 3D car aerodynamics benchmark, PhySense-opt achieves 62.6% average improvement over the best baseline (DiffusionPDE at 0.0966–0.2095), with 15 optimized sensors matching the accuracy of 100 randomly placed sensors.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two previously separate research threads: deep generative models for sparse field reconstruction (DiffusionPDE, S³GM, Senseiver) and classical optimal experimental design for sensor placement (SSPOR, A/D/E-optimality). It departs from both by using a flow-matching generative model whose training loss directly serves as the placement optimization objective, bridging the gap via a theoretical equivalence to A-optimality. The architecture choices (DiT for regular grids, Transolver for irregular meshes) and the cross-attention mechanism for incorporating scattered observations extend prior reconstruction work, while the projected gradient descent with geometric projection is a practical adaptation of standard optimization to constrained spatial domains.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The joint co-optimization framework addresses a practical gap: prior deep reconstruction methods assume fixed or random sensor placements, while classical placement methods (SSPOR) ignore the reconstruction model's inductive biases. The theoretical guarantee that flow-loss minimization polynomially bounds A-optimality provides a principled justification for the approach, and the 3D irregular mesh benchmark (car aerodynamics) is the first of its kind for physics sensing. Open questions include whether the conditional Gaussian assumption (Assumption 3.2) holds for highly non-Gaussian physical fields, whether the offline placement optimization can be made adaptive to temporal dynamics, and how the method scales to domains with millions of mesh points. The practical implication—deploying optimized sensor layouts in wind tunnels or ocean monitoring to reduce costly experiments—is significant but the current 100-case simulation scale limits immediate industrial applicability.
