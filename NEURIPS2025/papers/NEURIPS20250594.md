## Problem

Pre-trained multimodal models suffer severe performance degradation when input modalities are missing at inference time, yet existing incomplete multimodal learning methods either require separate models per missing-modality combination or sacrifice complete multimodal performance. No prior framework jointly optimizes complete and incomplete multimodal learning within a single unified model while maintaining state-of-the-art performance in both regimes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250594/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes language tokens (encoded by BERT or sBERT), audio features, and vision features (both extracted via ImageBind) as three unimodal inputs, where any subset may be present at inference. Missing modalities are represented as zero vectors rather than requiring modality-specific handling. Outputs are continuous sentiment scores in [-3, +3] for regression (MOSI/MOSEI) or discrete emotion class labels (6-class IEMOCAP, 7-class MELD) produced by an MLP head on the fused multimodal representation.

## Method

CyIN constructs a unified informative latent space via two complementary Information Bottleneck mechanisms: token-level IB applies cyclic cross-modal interaction (S→T and T→S) on token embeddings to capture modality-shared features, while label-level IB injects high-level semantic supervision from ground truth labels. Bottleneck latents are sampled via variational reparameterization, purifying task-relevant features and filtering noise. For missing modalities, cross-modal cyclic translation uses Cascaded Residual Autoencoders (CRA) to perform forward reconstruction (B_S → B_T^rec) and reverse back-translation (B_T^rec → B_S^cyc) in the informative space, with multiple remained modalities combined via additive Gaussian integration. A Multimodal Transformer fuses the bottleneck latents (original or reconstructed) into the final representation. Training is two-stage: first stabilizing the informative space under complete inputs (γ=0), then introducing the translation objective (γ>0) to enhance incomplete-input robustness.

## Results

On complete multimodal input, CyIN achieves 48.0 Acc7 / 86.3 F1 / 0.712 MAE / 0.801 Corr on MOSI and 66.1 Acc / 66.0 wF1 on IEMOCAP, outperforming the strongest baselines (IMDer, GCNet) by 3–4 points in Acc7 and 1.7–3.1 points in accuracy. Under fixed missing modality protocols, CyIN maintains 32.8 Acc7 on MOSI and 57.4 Acc on IEMOCAP, leading all baselines by 1.4–2.7 points. Under random missing (MR up to 0.7), CyIN reaches 35.0 Acc7 on MOSI and 57.5 Acc on IEMOCAP, with the smallest performance gap to complete input among all methods. CyIN also has the lowest parameter count (123.49M) and FLOPs (1.594T) among compared methods, with ~3× faster inference than GCNet.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CyIN outperforms all 10 baselines across every dataset and evaluation setting. On complete input, the largest margins are on MOSI where CyIN achieves 48.0 Acc7 vs. IMDer (43.8) and GCNet (43.6), a gap of ~4.2 points, and on IEMOCAP where CyIN reaches 66.1 Acc vs. IMDer (64.4) and GCNet (63.0). Under fixed missing protocols, CyIN leads by 1.4 Acc7 on MOSI (32.8 vs. IMDer 31.4) and 2.7 Acc on IEMOCAP (57.4 vs. IMDer 54.7), with the gap widening for weaker baselines like MCTN (40.6 Acc on IEMOCAP) and CPM-Net (41.3). Under random missing (MR up to 0.7), CyIN maintains the smallest degradation from complete input, achieving 35.0 Acc7 on MOSI vs. IMDer (34.6) and GCNet (33.8), and 57.5 Acc on IEMOCAP vs. IMDer (55.8) and GCNet (55.3). Notably, CyIN also has the lowest parameter count (123.49M vs. GCNet 144.34M and IMDer 168.31M) and FLOPs (1.594T vs. 3.747T and 5.466T), with ~3× faster inference than GCNet and ~5× faster than IMDer.

## Relation to Prior Work

CyIN builds on the Deep Variational Information Bottleneck (VIB) framework to construct a purified latent space for multimodal fusion, repurposing the IB mechanism from single-view representation learning to a cyclic, multi-modal setting with both token-level and label-level objectives. For missing modality reconstruction, it adapts the Cascaded Residual Autoencoder (CRA) as a cross-modal translator and borrows the back-translation trick from neural machine translation to enforce cyclic consistency. The key departure from prior incomplete multimodal learning methods (MMIN, GCNet, IMDer) is the unification of complete and incomplete learning in a single model without sacrificing complete-input performance, whereas previous approaches either train separate models per missing configuration or degrade complete-input accuracy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified single-model framework addresses a practical deployment bottleneck: real-world multimodal systems face unpredictable sensor failures, and maintaining separate models for every modality combination is infeasible. The notable computational efficiency (123M params, ~3–5× faster inference than GCNet/IMDer) makes the approach attractive for resource-constrained settings. However, the paper acknowledges that treating all modalities as equally important limits performance on tasks where one modality dominates (e.g., language in sentiment analysis), and the CRA-based translator could be replaced by more powerful generative models (diffusion, flow-based). Open questions include adaptive modality weighting, integration with large language models, and whether the IB-based purification generalizes beyond the three-modality (text/audio/video) setting tested here.
