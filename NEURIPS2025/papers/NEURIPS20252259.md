## Problem

Existing methods for enhancing LLM reasoning capability rely on external supervision—labeled reasoning traces, verified golden answers, or pre-trained reward models—making them costly and hard to scale. This paper asks whether reasoning can be incentivized in a fully unsupervised manner, using only unlabeled questions and no external correctness signals whatsoever.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252259/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes only unlabeled text questions (20K math prompts from NuminaMath-CoT or 18K filtered questions from the Natural Reasoning dataset) with no golden answers, reasoning traces, or reward model. The model is prompted to produce a step-by-step reasoning trace ending with a final answer enclosed in \boxed{}. For mathematical tasks, semantic equivalence between outputs is determined by regular expressions; for free-form natural reasoning, a compact 1.5B General-Verifier model performs pairwise bidirectional-entailment checks on the extracted final answers.

## Method

EMPO (Entropy-Minimized Policy Optimization) is a GRPO-style RL algorithm that replaces the external accuracy reward with a semantic-entropy-based intrinsic reward. For each question, G outputs are sampled from the current policy, clustered into M semantic groups by meaning equivalence, and the reward for each output is set to the proportion of samples in its cluster (i.e., the estimated probability of that meaning). The policy is then updated to maximize the normalized advantage of high-probability-cluster outputs, effectively minimizing the Shannon entropy over the meaning distribution. A dual entropy-thresholding filter (δ_low < H < δ_high) restricts optimization to questions with moderate uncertainty, preventing both overconfidence on already-consistent answers and unreliable learning from highly uncertain ones. The KL regularization is dropped following DAPO, and the entire pipeline requires no golden answers, reward model, or human-labeled traces.

## Results

On mathematical reasoning benchmarks, EMPO boosts Qwen2.5-Math-7B Base from 33.7% to 51.6% average accuracy (pass@1, greedy decoding) and Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B Base from 33.0% to 42.6%, using only unlabeled questions with no golden answers or reward model. On free-form natural reasoning, EMPO improves Qwen2.5-7B Base from 32.1% to 50.1% on MMLU-Pro and from 15.9% to 28.8% on GPQA, and Qwen2.5-14B Base from 32.7% to 58.8% on MMLU-Pro. The unsupervised semantic-entropy reward shows a strong negative correlation with true accuracy during training, confirming its validity as an intrinsic optimization signal.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GRPO [11], which requires golden answers for rule-based reward, EMPO achieves a near-identical 51.6% vs. 51.7% average on 7B math benchmarks while using zero external supervision ({q} only vs. {q,a}). Against Online-DPO [31], EMPO wins by 4.6 points (51.6% vs. 47.0%) on 7B math, despite ODPO also requiring golden answers. Against the fully supervised Qwen2.5-Math-Instruct [5] (trained with iterative SFT + RL on private data), EMPO slightly edges ahead at 51.6% vs. 50.5% on 7B math. On the OctoThinker-3B model [42], EMPO (27.6% avg) underperforms OctoThinker-Zero (37.0% avg), which uses golden-answer-based RL, suggesting EMPO's gains are most pronounced when the base model already possesses strong latent reasoning pathways. On free-form natural reasoning (Table 2), EMPO trails GRPO on MMLU-Pro (50.1% vs. 54.5% for 7B) but substantially outperforms SFT (50.1% vs. 25.6%), indicating that supervised fine-tuning on noisy reference answers can be counterproductive.

## Relation to Prior Work

EMPO is built directly on the GRPO policy optimization framework (Shao et al., 2024), replacing the external rule-based or reward-model signal with a semantic-entropy-based intrinsic reward derived from Kuhn et al.'s (2023) semantic uncertainty framework. It departs from the DeepSeek-R1-Zero paradigm, which still requires verified golden answers or a pre-trained reward model, and from self-consistency/SFT approaches (Huang et al., 2023) that risk model collapse. The key conceptual move is repurposing semantic entropy—from a diagnostic tool for detecting hallucination—into a continuous optimization objective within an RL loop, making the entire training pipeline depend only on unlabeled questions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating all external supervision (no golden answers, no reasoning traces, no reward model), EMPO offers a scalable path to reasoning improvement in domains where curated data is scarce or prohibitively expensive. The pass@k analysis provides a sobering but important conceptual finding: RL post-training primarily improves sampling efficiency over pre-existing reasoning pathways rather than instilling fundamentally new capabilities, reframing the role of RL as 'elicitation' rather than 'teaching.' Key open questions include whether entropy minimization can ever push beyond the base model's latent capability ceiling, how to mitigate the reward-hacking and overconfidence risks inherent to unsupervised objectives, and whether the approach generalizes to model families (e.g., Llama3) that lack the instruction-following priors present in Qwen2.5.
