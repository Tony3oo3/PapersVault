## Problem

Prior work on combinatorial semi-bandits and contextual bandits yields sample complexity and regret bounds whose leading terms scale polynomially with the number of actions K, even when the underlying rewards are s-sparse (s ≪ K). This paper asks whether reward sparsity can be exploited to obtain bounds whose dominant terms scale with s rather than K, and provides the first treatment of bandit multiclass list classification (s > 1, m > 1) in both PAC and regret settings.

## Input / Output

The learner receives a context x from a (possibly infinite) context space and, after predicting a subset of m actions from K available actions, observes semi-bandit feedback consisting only of the individual reward values for the predicted actions. The reward vectors are constrained to have L1-norm at most s (sparsity parameter), and the policy class Π is finite. The algorithm requires access to an ERM oracle for Π and outputs a policy π_out ∈ Π that is ε-optimal in population reward with probability at least 1−δ, using O((poly(K/m) + sm/ε²) log(|Π|/δ)) samples.

## Method

The algorithm operates in two phases. In Phase 1, it collects N₁ samples via uniform random action prediction and uses Frank-Wolfe optimization (with L₁/L∞-smoothness) to approximately minimize a convex objective F(p) = E[−Σ_y r(y) log Q^γ_{x,y}(p)] over the simplex of policy distributions, where Q^γ is a mixture of the policy distribution with a uniform distribution over actions. The key insight is that the L∞-norm of the gradient of F at its minimizer is bounded by s, which implies that importance-weighted reward estimators induced by the resulting distribution p̂ have variance O(sm) independent of K. Uniform convergence of the empirical objective to F is established via L∞ vector-contraction arguments on Rademacher complexity. In Phase 2, the algorithm uses p̂ to draw importance-weighted reward estimates for all policies in Π and selects the best via a single ERM call, requiring only O(sm/ε²) additional samples by Bernstein's inequality.

## Results

The main PAC learning result (Theorem 1) achieves a sample complexity of O((K⁹/m⁸ + sm/ε²) log(|Π|/δ)) for contextual combinatorial semi-bandits with s-sparse rewards, where the dominant term scales with the sparsity parameter s rather than the number of actions K. In the single-label classification special case (s=m=1), a specialized algorithm (Theorem 4) improves the dependence on K from K⁹ (as in [17]) to K⁷, yielding O((K⁷ + 1/ε²) log(|H|/δ)). The regret minimization result establishes a bound of Õ(|Π| + √(smT) log|Π|) even under adversarial data generation, and a matching lower bound of Ω̃(sm/ε²) confirms the optimality of the dominant PAC term.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical experiments, so comparisons are between theoretical bounds. The PAC sample complexity bound improves upon the naive importance-sampling approach which yields ≈Km/ε², by replacing the K dependence in the leading term with s (the sparsity parameter). In the single-label setting (s=m=1), the K⁷ dependence improves upon the K⁹ bound from [17] (Erez et al., 2024), which used a stochastic optimization scheme rather than the empirical Frank-Wolfe approach adopted here. The regret bound Õ(|Π| + √(smT) log|Π|) extends the single-label result of [18] (Erez et al., 2024) which achieved Õ(min{|Π|+√(sT), √(KT log|Π|)}), generalizing from m=1 to arbitrary combinatorial action sizes. Prior combinatorial semi-bandit regret bounds (e.g., O(√(mKT)) from [3]) all exhibit polynomial dependence on K in the leading term, whereas this work's bounds scale with s instead, providing a strict improvement whenever s≪K.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper generalizes the single-label bandit PAC classification framework of Erez et al. [17, 18] to the full contextual combinatorial semi-bandit (CCSB) setting with s-sparse rewards, where the learner predicts subsets of m actions from K and observes only the rewards of predicted actions. The technical approach departs from prior combinatorial semi-bandit work [3, 40, 31, 32], which all exhibit polynomial dependence on K in their leading terms, by exploiting reward sparsity through a two-stage algorithm: a Frank-Wolfe-based low-variance exploration distribution (leveraging L∞ vector-contraction Rademacher bounds from [20]) followed by importance-weighted reward estimation. The key conceptual departure from [17] is that in the multi-label/combinatorial setting, one cannot collect a fully-labeled dataset by uniform sampling, necessitating importance-sampled estimation of the convex objective with a modified potential.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central result—that sample complexity scales with the sparsity parameter s rather than the action space size K, and that for s=O(1) the leading terms match full-information rates—provides a clean theoretical justification for why bandit feedback is essentially free in sparse-reward regimes like recommendation systems where purchased items are far fewer than available products. The matching lower bound (Ω(sm/ε²)) confirms tightness. Open questions include extending to the full-bandit setting (where only the sum reward is observed), improving the polynomial K-dependence in the exploration phase, and handling infinite policy classes. The practical impact is primarily for theoretical understanding of when and how sparsity can be exploited in partial-feedback learning, rather than providing immediately deployable algorithms.
