## Problem

Deep neural networks overfit noisy labels, and existing noisy-label-learning methods face three key limitations: they are computationally intensive (requiring multiple networks or repeated forward passes to measure sample hardness), they introduce hard-to-tune hyperparameters (loss coefficients, grouping thresholds, epoch cutoffs), and they apply coarse-grained, category-level weighting that ignores individual differences in sample cleanliness and difficulty. A central difficulty is that noisy samples near the decision boundary behave similarly to genuinely hard clean samples, making them hard to separate with loss-based metrics that become unstable once the model overfits.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 64×64 for Tiny-ImageNet, variable resolution for Clothing1M and WebVision) paired with potentially corrupted labels, and produces a class label prediction. Each sample is processed through two-view augmentation (one weak, one strong view) during Stage 2 training, and the wrong-event statistic is accumulated per sample across epochs. The framework is designed to be hyperparameter-free: all loss weights are computed dynamically from a per-class Beta Mixture Model fit, eliminating manual tuning of coefficients or thresholds.

## Method

IDO introduces a 'wrong event' metric that counts the cumulative number of epochs in which the model's prediction disagrees with the given label; unlike loss, it is monotonically increasing with bounded relative change, so it remains stable and discriminative even after the model overfits noise. A two-component Beta Mixture Model (BMM) is fitted per class via EM to the wrong-event distribution, yielding posterior probabilities τ₁(w) and τ₂(w) that measure sample cleanliness, and a difficulty score ε(w) that combines these posteriors with the CDF values to capture how close a sample sits to the decision boundary. Training is two-stage: Stage 1 runs a few epochs of standard cross-entropy to accumulate wrong-event statistics and capture a competitive base model (identified via the label-wave metric), while Stage 2 fine-tunes with a dynamically weighted loss L = τ₁(w)·L_C + ε(w)·L_SIM + τ₂(w)·L_N, where L_C is cross-entropy on both views for clean samples, L_SIM is a squared-error consistency loss between the two views for difficult samples, and L_N is a confidence-weighted pseudo-label loss for noisy samples. All weights update each epoch as the BMM is refit, enabling instance-level optimization without any additional hyperparameters.

## Results

On CIFAR-100 with ResNet-50, IDO achieves 85.08% (Sym.20%), 83.77% (Sym.40%), 81.42% (Sym.60%), 78.17% (Asym.40%), and 83.84% (Inst.40%) top-1 accuracy, outperforming the best baseline (UNICON) by up to 1.6% on instance-dependent noise. With ViT-B/16, IDO reaches 92.67% (Sym.20%) and 92.24% (Inst.40%) on CIFAR-100, surpassing ELR by 0.8–1.1%. On real-world datasets, IDO (ViT-B) achieves 81.36% on CIFAR-100N, 73.04% on Clothing1M-F, and 86.03% on WebVision, setting new state-of-the-art. IDO also reduces per-epoch training time by ~4× and GPU memory by ~3× compared to DMix and UNICON on large models (e.g., 458s vs. 1596s and 14.8 GB vs. 31.8 GB on ViT-B/16).

## Baselines & Comparisons

IDO outperforms all 12 baselines across every noise type and dataset. On CIFAR-100 (ResNet-50), the largest margins appear on asymmetric noise (78.17% vs. DISC 77.12%, UNICON 76.77%) and instance-dependent noise (83.84% vs. UNICON 82.24%, DMix 81.37%), where instance-level difficulty modeling provides the most benefit. On ViT-B/16, IDO leads ELR by 0.8–1.1% on CIFAR-100 and by 2.3–4.6% on Tiny-ImageNet, while DeFT (CLIP-based) lags significantly on asymmetric noise (89.65% vs. 72.96%) due to its reliance on vision-language alignment. On real-world datasets, IDO (ViT-B) beats DeFT by 2.2% on CIFAR-100N (81.36% vs. 79.12%) and by 0.8% on WebVision (86.03% vs. 85.21%), and outperforms LongReMix and ProMix by 1.1–7.3% on CIFAR-100N. In terms of efficiency, IDO requires only 458 s/epoch and 14.8 GB GPU memory on ViT-B/16, compared to 1596 s and 31.8 GB for DMix and 2628 s and 45.2 GB for UNICON, enabling practical deployment on larger architectures where dual-network methods become infeasible.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the memorization-effect line of work (Arpit et al., Zhang et al.) that observes DNNs learn clean patterns before fitting noise, and builds on probabilistic mixture-model approaches to noise modeling (DivideMix's GMM, M-correction's BMM). It departs from prior work in two key ways: replacing the unstable loss-based or forgetting-based metrics with a simple cumulative 'wrong event' count that remains discriminative even after overfitting, and replacing fixed hyperparameter-weighted loss terms with dynamically computed weights from a per-class Beta Mixture Model posterior. The two-stage pre-trained-model fine-tuning setup follows TURN and DeFT, but the single-network, two-view design avoids the dual-network overhead of co-training methods like DivideMix and UNICON.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 75% reduction in training time and memory while achieving SOTA accuracy makes instance-level noise-robust training practical for large pre-trained models (ViT-B/16, ConvNeXt-B) where co-training methods become infeasible. The elimination of hyperparameter tuning (loss coefficients, GMM thresholds, momentum coefficients) addresses a persistent practical pain point in LNL methods, as demonstrated by the sensitivity analyses showing ELR and DivideMix performance can swing by 30+ points with small parameter changes. The main open limitation is failure at >90% noise ratio due to extreme distribution imbalance, and the modest 0.7–1.6% accuracy gains over strong baselines suggest the remaining performance gap may require fundamentally different noise modeling rather than refined weighting.
