## Problem

Short-context vision-language models suffer from a 'lost-in-the-middle' positional bias and over-specialization to short temporal spans, causing severe degradation when applied to ultra-long videos. Existing DPO-based long-video alignment methods assume a reference model that already handles long contexts, an assumption that fails for short-context VLMs, and they rely on costly proprietary models or human annotations to generate preference data. The paper asks how far short-context VLMs can be pushed into the long-video regime without expensive re-training or long-video labels.

## Input / Output

The model receives up to 512 video frames (sampled at 1 fps from source clips) concatenated into a composite sequence, along with a natural-language query, and produces a free-form text response. In Stage 1 the input is a pseudo-long video assembled from multiple unrelated short clips with one designated anchor; in Stage 2 the input is a scene-segmented real long video. Training uses only ~16K synthetic preference triples (10K Stage 1, 6K Stage 2) with no human-annotated long-video data.

## Method

LongVPO is a two-stage DPO framework. Stage 1 synthesizes anchor-centric preference triples: a question is generated to be answerable only from one designated short clip within a composite sequence of distractor clips, with dispreferred responses drawn from non-anchor clips to simulate temporal misalignment. A key technical contribution is the anchor-only reference approximation (Eq. 2–3), where the frozen reference model scores responses conditioned solely on the anchor clip rather than the full composite sequence, avoiding context-length mismatch and reducing compute by ~30%. Scene-similarity filtering (DINOv2 cosine threshold 0.6) and LLM-based question-specificity filtering ensure unambiguous supervision. Stage 2 applies a recursive captioning pipeline to unlabeled long videos to produce scene-level metadata, then uses an external LLM to generate multi-segment reasoning queries with explicit scene-relevance chains; dispreferred responses are induced by feeding the model only partial or irrelevant scenes, training it to assemble complete cross-scene evidence chains under the standard DPO objective.

## Results

LongVPO (Stage 2, 512f) built on InternVL2.5-8B achieves 50.4 on LVBench, 67.8 on LongVideoBench, 75.0 on MLVU, 65.6/73.0 on Video-MME (wo/w sub), and 75.1 on MVBench, improving over the 512f baseline by +5.2, +5.1, +7.4, +4.5/+7.7, and +3.1 respectively. When applied to the dedicated long-video model InternVideo2.5-8B, LongVPO (Stage 2-iter2, 256f) reaches 51.0 on LVBench, 67.2 on LongVideoBench, 74.7 on MLVU, and 66.1/73.1 on Video-MME. All results are obtained with only 16K synthetic preference samples and no human-annotated long-video labels.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LongVPO (512f on InternVL2.5) outperforms the strongest open-source video model VideoChat-Flash (7B) on every benchmark: LVBench 50.4 vs 48.2, LongVideoBench 67.8 vs 64.7, MLVU 75.0 vs 74.7, Video-MME 65.6/73.0 vs 65.3/69.7, and MVBench 75.1 vs 74.0. Against its own base model InternVL2.5-8B (512f), LongVPO delivers large gains of +4.9 to +7.4 points on long-video benchmarks while also improving MVBench by +1.1, confirming no short-video regression. Applied to the dedicated long-video model InternVideo2.5-8B, LongVPO (Stage 2-iter2) improves LVBench from 47.4 to 51.0, LongVideoBench from 63.2 to 67.2, and MLVU from 72.8 to 74.7, though it trails the base by 0.6 on MVBench (75.1 vs 75.7). Compared to proprietary models, GPT-4o still leads on Video-MME (71.9/77.2) and LongVideoBench (66.7), but LongVPO matches or exceeds it on LVBench (50.4 vs 30.8) and MLVU (75.0 vs 64.6). The method also surpasses other open-source long-video models such as LongVILA (57.1 LongVideoBench), LongVU (65.4 MLVU), and Kangaroo (54.8 LongVideoBench) by substantial margins across all evaluated benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Direct Preference Optimization (Rafailov et al., 2023) to the long-video domain, departing from prior video-DPO work (e.g., Temporal Preference Optimization [23]) that assumes a reference model already capable of long-context reasoning — an assumption that fails for short-context VLMs. Conceptually, it builds on the short-to-long context transfer idea from LongVA [57] (transferring long-text understanding to vision) but repurposes it within a preference-optimization framework rather than SFT. The two-stage design — anchored short-clip composition followed by recursive-captioning-based multi-segment reasoning — is a novel decomposition that avoids the need for any long-video ground-truth annotations, distinguishing it from data-heavy pipelines like VideoChat-Flash [25] or Video-XL [42].

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical impact is that 16K synthetic preference triples (no human labels, no proprietary model calls for data generation) suffice to push an 8B short-context VLM past models trained on large-scale annotated long-video datasets, offering a scalable path for labs without annotation budgets. The anchor-only reference-model approximation (Eq. 2) is a useful trick that cuts compute and avoids the context-length mismatch that degrades standard DPO on long inputs. Limitations include prioritizing accuracy over inference efficiency (no context compression), and the method's reliance on scene-segmented video (Vript) for Stage 2, which may not generalize to unsegmented streams. Open questions include whether the approach composes with token-compression or retrieval-based architectures, and whether iterative self-training (Stage 2-iter2) can be extended further.
