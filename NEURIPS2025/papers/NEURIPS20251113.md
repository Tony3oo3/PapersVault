## Problem

Existing semantic mapping systems are passive, relying on predetermined trajectories or externally planned paths, and cannot determine which viewpoints would most improve semantic understanding. No dense active semantic mapping system based on radiance fields or 3D Gaussian Splatting existed, and prior approaches either required ground-truth labels, used memory-prohibitive full probability distributions per primitive, or compressed semantics into low-dimensional color spaces that suffer from blending artifacts during multi-view fusion.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251113/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system consumes posed RGB-D frames at 680×1200 resolution (60° vertical, 90° horizontal FOV) along with per-pixel semantic probability distributions from a fine-tuned OneFormer model (101 classes on Replica, 40 on MP3D). It produces a Semantic Gaussian Map where each 3D Gaussian stores only its top-k most probable semantic categories (sparse representation), a next-best-view pose selected by joint geometric and semantic uncertainty scoring, and rendered outputs including color, depth, silhouette, and full semantic probability maps. The exploration loop runs for up to 2,000 steps (Replica) or 5,000 steps (MP3D), with early termination when the candidate pool is exhausted.

## Method

ActiveSGM builds on SplaTAM's 3DGS backbone (isotropic Gaussians with view-independent color) and augments each Gaussian with a sparse semantic vector retaining only the top-k highest-probability categories, reducing per-Gaussian storage from M classes to k while preserving discriminative power. Semantic rendering alpha-blends these sparse distributions across visible Gaussians to produce a full probability map per pixel, and optimization uses a combined Hellinger distance and cosine similarity loss with an entropy-based mask that suppresses supervision from uncertain predictions; gradients from the semantic loss are restricted to semantic attributes only, decoupling them from geometric optimization. For exploration, the system renders both a silhouette (geometric criterion: count of missing pixels) and a semantic entropy map (semantic criterion: summed pixel-wise entropy) from candidate viewpoints, combines them multiplicatively with a softmax-normalized motion cost, and selects the highest-scoring pose. A coarse-to-fine sampling strategy (Fibonacci lattice directions at two density levels) and a global-local keyframe selection scheme (50-50 mix based on rendering quality and semantic confidence) ensure efficient coverage of under-observed and ambiguous regions.

## Results

On Replica (novel views, predicted labels), ActiveSGM achieves 84.89% mIoU and 77.56% F-1 in only 777 exploration steps, outperforming the reconfigured SGS-SLAM baseline (80.42% mIoU, 18.70% F-1) which requires 2000 steps. On MP3D, ActiveSGM reaches 65.58% average IoU using predicted labels on novel views, substantially exceeding the best active semantic mapping baseline (Zhang et al., 42.92% with GT labels on train views). For 3D reconstruction on MP3D, ActiveSGM achieves 1.56 cm accuracy and 97.35% completeness ratio, surpassing ActiveGAMER (1.66 cm, 95.32%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest semantic SLAM baseline SGS-SLAM (reconfigured to use predicted labels and GT poses), ActiveSGM improves mIoU from 80.42% to 84.89% and F-1 from 18.70% to 77.56% on Replica, while using only 777 steps versus 2000. The large F-1 gap highlights that SGS-SLAM's color-coding representation introduces severe label confusion during multi-view fusion. On MP3D, ActiveSGM (65.58% IoU, predicted labels, novel views) outperforms all three active semantic mapping baselines—SSMI (36.14%), TARE (31.70%), and Zhang et al. (42.92%)—which all rely on ground-truth labels and evaluate on training views, a margin of 22–34 percentage points. For 3D reconstruction, ActiveSGM achieves the best accuracy (1.56 cm) and completeness ratio (97.35%) on MP3D, edging out ActiveGAMER (1.66 cm, 95.32%), while on Replica it is marginally behind ActiveGAMER (1.19 vs. 1.16 cm accuracy). In novel view synthesis, ActiveSGM ranks second on Replica (SSIM 0.96 vs. ActiveGAMER's 0.97) and first on MP3D (PSNR 26.15, SSIM 0.92), confirming that the semantic exploration criterion does not compromise photometric quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate research threads: active geometric mapping (ActiveGAMER, NARUTO, ActiveSplat) and passive semantic SLAM (SGS-SLAM, NIDS-SLAM, SNI-SLAM). It builds directly on SplaTAM as its 3DGS-based SLAM backbone, adopts ActiveGAMER's silhouette-based geometric exploration criterion, and uses NARUTO's RRT for local path planning, while introducing a novel sparse top-k semantic representation and a combined Hellinger-distance-plus-cosine-similarity loss for semantic optimization. The key departure from prior semantic SLAM is the shift from color-encoded or high-dimensional embedding semantics to explicit probability distributions over a closed vocabulary, and the key departure from prior active mapping is the addition of a semantic entropy criterion alongside geometric coverage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By closing the loop between semantic uncertainty and viewpoint selection, ActiveSGM demonstrates that a robot can build a coherent dense semantic map with roughly 60% fewer observations than passive baselines, while operating on noisy OneFormer predictions rather than ground-truth labels—a more realistic deployment setting. The sparse top-k representation yields a ~20× speedup in semantic rendering (61 ms → 3.1 ms), making real-time semantic mapping on embedded hardware more feasible. Open questions include how the system handles open-vocabulary semantics, how it degrades under imperfect localization and execution (both assumed perfect here), and whether the decoupled geometry/semantic optimization (blocked gradients) limits joint refinement in ambiguous regions.
