## Problem

Diffusion-based world models condition on only a short window of past frames (typically 4–16), causing generated scenes to drift from previously observed content within a few steps. This short-context limitation undermines temporal coherence in long-horizon rollouts, where revisited areas should preserve their appearance. The paper addresses the absence of a persistent, computationally efficient environment state that can be integrated into high-fidelity diffusion generation.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a full sequence of RGB observations and discrete actions (51 types in CSGO, 4 directions in MiniGrid). Each frame is encoded by the Cosmos tokenizer (scale 16) into a single flattened feature vector (dim 1296 for MiniGrid, 2448 for CSGO) that serves as one SSM step, while the diffusion branch receives only 4 low-resolution frames (30×30 or 30×56) plus 512-dim action embeddings. Output is a single next-frame prediction at 144×144 (MiniGrid) or 280×150 (CSGO), generated one frame at a time via a sliding-window autoregressive strategy with 3 denoising iterations and a separate upsampler.

## Method

StateSpaceDiffuser decouples long-context modeling from high-fidelity synthesis into two branches. The Long-Context Branch is a single-layer Mamba SSM (state dim 256) that processes the entire observation-action sequence in O(T) time, maintaining a compact recurrent state updated per step; at inference it runs with constant per-step latency and memory. The Generative Branch is the DIAMOND UNet-based EDM diffusion model, which produces high-quality frames from only 4 input frames. A fusion module takes the last 4 SSM output features, normalizes them, passes them through a 2-layer SiLU MLP, and concatenates the result with similarly processed action embeddings to condition the diffusion network. Training is two-stage: first the SSM is trained with MSE loss on Cosmos features over long sequences (50 or 16 steps), then the SSM is frozen while the diffusion branch is trained on 4-frame windows conditioned on the SSM features—this separation avoids the gradient instability of end-to-end training. The SSM contributes less than 2% of total inference FLOPs, making the long-context capability nearly free computationally.

## Results

On the MiniGrid forward-backward evaluation (context length 50), StateSpaceDiffuser achieves 39.68 average PSNR and 39.32 final-frame PSNR, compared to 26.13 and 25.15 for the DIAMOND diffusion-only baseline — a 51.9% average PSNR improvement. At context length 16, the gap widens further: 41.01 vs. 27.13 average PSNR. On the visually complex CSGO dataset, a user study with 12 participants shows a clear preference for StateSpaceDiffuser over DIAMOND (rating +0.20 at frame 15, +0.24 at frame 17). The model also generalizes to unseen longer contexts (100 and 150 steps) without fine-tuning, maintaining a significant advantage over both baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is DIAMOND [1], a UNet-based EDM diffusion world model that conditions on only 4 recent frames. StateSpaceDiffuser decisively outperforms DIAMOND on all metrics: at context length 50, it gains +13.55 dB average PSNR (39.68 vs. 26.13) and +14.17 dB final-frame PSNR (39.32 vs. 25.15), while also improving SSIM from 0.95 to 0.98. The secondary baseline, the State-Space World Model (Mamba-only, no diffusion), demonstrates strong long-context memory (32.64 avg PSNR at context 50) but produces blurry, artifact-laden images due to its limited generative capacity. StateSpaceDiffuser combines the best of both: it matches or exceeds the SSM's memory capability while delivering substantially higher visual fidelity. An ablation removing the state-space features (StateSpaceDiffuser w/o state) drops below even DIAMOND (23.68 vs. 27.13 avg PSNR at context 16), confirming the SSM features are essential. On CSGO, where pixel-level metrics are unreliable due to accumulated camera drift, a user study confirms StateSpaceDiffuser produces content more faithful to previously observed scenes than DIAMOND.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on two distinct lineages: diffusion-based world models (DIAMOND, Genie, GAIA-1/2) that achieve high-fidelity frame generation but are limited to short input windows due to quadratic transformer complexity, and state-space models (Mamba, S4WM) that efficiently track long-range dependencies but lack generative expressiveness. StateSpaceDiffuser departs from both by fusing them architecturally: a Mamba-based Long-Context Branch compresses the full interaction history into a compact state, whose features condition a DIAMOND-style UNet diffusion model for high-quality synthesis. The two-stage training protocol (pretrain SSM, then freeze and train diffusion) is a key departure from end-to-end approaches, motivated by gradient instability between the two branches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a fundamental and widely acknowledged limitation of diffusion world models—context drift over long rollouts—with a solution that adds less than 2% inference compute, making it practically attractive for real-time interactive applications. The forward-backward navigation evaluation protocol provides a clean, reproducible benchmark for long-context memory that the field lacked. However, the strongest quantitative results come from the relatively simple 2D MiniGrid environment; on the visually complex CSGO dataset, the gains are more modest and the authors themselves note that PSNR metrics under-report perceptual content preservation. Open questions include whether scaling the SSM state dimension and replacing the lightweight decoder with a larger pretrained backbone can close the remaining gap in complex 3D scenes, and whether the approach extends to even longer horizons or multi-agent settings.
