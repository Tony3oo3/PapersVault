## Problem

Existing model merging methods operate exclusively in the parameter space, combining models with identical architectures, which limits representational diversity and performance ceilings. Architecture-level merging (stitching layers from different models) remains underexplored due to the exponentially large discrete search space, layer compatibility constraints, and the strong coupling between parameter and architecture decisions. Additionally, prior approaches produce a single merged model, ignoring diverse user preferences across multiple tasks.

## Input / Output

The method takes K fine-tuned models sharing a common base model (e.g., LLaMA-2-7B variants for chat, math, and code) along with N preference weight vectors sampled from a Dirichlet distribution over the K tasks. It produces a set of approximately Pareto-optimal merged models, where each model is defined by both a parameter tensor (obtained via DARE-Ties) and an inference path—a sequence of (model_index, layer_index) pairs that can jump between the merged model and individual fine-tuned models, with total length bounded by T_max and dimension-matching constraints at every layer boundary.

## Method

HM3 formulates model merging as a bilevel multi-objective optimization: the upper level solves for optimal parameters using DARE-Ties merging, while the lower level searches for the optimal inference path via an actor-critic RL agent. The architecture-level search is modeled as a trajectory-aware MDP where the state is the full layer-selection history encoded by a GRU, the action selects the next (model, layer) pair, and the reward combines weighted task performance with a path-length penalty. To handle the large discrete action space efficiently, HM3 introduces Wolpertinger policy discretization, which restricts Q-value evaluation to a local neighborhood of nearest-neighbor actions rather than the full action set, balancing exploration and exploitation. Multi-objective preferences are handled via Tchebycheff scalarization with Dirichlet-sampled weight vectors, decomposing the problem into N parallel subproblems whose solutions approximate the Pareto front. A feed-forward MLP generates per-layer scaling matrices for dimension alignment across heterogeneous model layers, and the trained policy/value networks are reusable across new task configurations without retraining.

## Results

On LLaMA-2-7B, HM3 achieves 45.62 on GSM8K, 43.62 on HumanEval, and 41.68 on WMT&ISWT, outperforming all parameter-level and architecture-level baselines across translation, math, and code tasks. On vision tasks, HM3 reaches 73.83% average accuracy on ViT-B/32 and 85.34% on ViT-L/14, surpassing the strongest parameter-level baseline (PCB Merging at 73.80% and 85.23% respectively). In the multi-objective setting, HM3 achieves a hypervolume of 1.8120 versus 1.5111 for a MOEA baseline, and every baseline solution is Pareto-dominated by at least one HM3 solution.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HM3 outperforms all nine baselines across every task in both language and vision experiments. Among parameter-level methods, PCB Merging and Consensus Merging are the strongest baselines, but HM3 still leads by 1.93 points on GSM8K (45.62 vs. 39.25) and 11.09 points on HumanEval (43.62 vs. 32.53) over PCB Merging. The architecture-level EA baseline (Akiba et al.) consistently underperforms all parameter-level methods, scoring only 25.51 on GSM8K and 25.17 on HumanEval, confirming that unguided evolutionary search struggles in the high-dimensional layer-path space. Notably, HM3's merged model on GSM8K (45.62) nearly matches the task-specific fine-tuned WizardMath-7B (45.79) while simultaneously achieving 43.62 on HumanEval—exceeding the CodeLlama-7B fine-tuned model (43.21)—demonstrating that joint parameter-architecture optimization can approach or surpass single-task fine-tuning upper bounds. On vision tasks, HM3's advantage over PCB Merging is smaller (73.83% vs. 73.80% on ViT-B/32; 85.34% vs. 85.23% on ViT-L/14) but consistent across individual datasets like SVHN (82.11% vs. 82.31%) and GTSRB (73.11% vs. 72.57%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends model merging from the well-studied parameter space (Task Arithmetic, Ties-Merging, DARE-Ties, PCB, Consensus) into the joint parameter-and-architecture space, formulating the problem as a bilevel optimization where the upper level handles parameter fusion (reusing DARE-Ties) and the lower level searches inference paths via an actor-critic RL agent with Wolpertinger discretization. It departs from the recent EA-based architecture search of Akiba et al. (2025) by replacing one-shot population-based evolution with a reusable, trajectory-aware MDP formulation, and introduces Tchebycheff decomposition to handle multi-objective task preferences. The PPO-based policy update and GRU trajectory encoding draw directly from standard RL tooling, but their application to discrete layer-path search in model merging is novel.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

HM3 is positioned as the first reusable model merging framework: once the actor-critic policy and value networks are trained, they can predict optimal merging configurations for new tasks without full re-search, addressing the prohibitive cost of EA-based approaches. The multi-objective Pareto-front output lets users select merged models matching their specific task-weighting preferences rather than receiving a single fixed trade-off. However, the authors acknowledge the work is in an early exploratory stage; experiments are limited to models up to 13B parameters, the Markov property assumption on trajectory states may not hold for very deep architectures, and the computational cost of RL training (even if amortized) remains a practical barrier. Open questions include scalability to 70B+ models, whether the bilevel decomposition's theoretical optimality guarantee holds under the approximate value function in practice, and how the framework behaves when the candidate models share no common base architecture.
