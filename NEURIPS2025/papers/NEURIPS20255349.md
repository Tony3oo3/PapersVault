## Problem

Existing convergence analyses of error feedback methods (EF and EF21) and compressed gradient descent (CGD) rely on ad hoc Lyapunov functions with unverified tightness, making it impossible to determine which method is truly worst-case optimal or how to optimally tune each. The paper asks three concrete questions: what is the exact optimal convergence rate for each method, which method should be chosen for a given setting, and what is the worst-case optimal step size?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255349/cropped_page6_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The analysis operates in the single-agent setting (n=1) on L-smooth, μ-strongly convex functions with a contractive compression operator satisfying E‖x−C(x)‖² ≤ ε‖x‖². For each algorithm, the state space differs: CGD uses (x, ∇f, C(η∇f)), EF uses (x, ∇f, C(e+η∇f), e), and EF21 uses (x, ∇f, d). The output is a closed-form contraction factor ρ*, an explicit quadratic Lyapunov function over the algorithm-specific state, and the optimal step size η*, all expressed in terms of ε, L, and μ (or equivalently κ=L/μ).

## Method

The paper extends the performance estimation (PEP) framework to algorithms with compression by formulating the Lyapunov search as a semidefinite feasibility problem with bisection on the contraction rate, then applies log-det rank-minimization heuristics to extract structurally simple low-rank Lyapunov candidates. Symbolic regression via PySR identifies closed-form expressions for the Lyapunov coefficients, and a WolframScript CAS certificate verifies all algebraic identities. The key analytical result is that EF and EF21 achieve the identical optimal rate ρ* = √ε + ((1−√ε)/2)((κ−1)/(κ+1))²[1−√ε+√((1+√ε)²+√ε·16κ/(κ−1)²)] with the same optimal step size η* = (2/(L+μ))·((1−√ε)/(1+√ε)), yet with fundamentally different Lyapunov functions. Tightness is established by constructing a one-dimensional quadratic worst-case instance whose asymptotic contraction exactly matches ρ*, and a CAS-verified inequality shows CGD's rate is strictly smaller than both error-feedback rates.

## Results

The paper derives exact worst-case convergence rates for EF, EF²¹, and CGD on L-smooth, μ-strongly convex functions with contractive compression (parameter ε). The headline finding is that EF and EF²¹ achieve the identical optimal contraction rate ρ* = √ε + ((1−√ε)/2)·((κ−1)/(κ+1))²·[1−√ε + √((1+√ε)² + 16√ε·κ/(κ−1)²)], with the same optimal step size η* = (2/(L+μ))·((1−√ε)/(1+√ε)). CGD achieves a strictly better rate ρ_CGD = ((κ_√ε−1)/(κ_√ε+1))² where κ_√ε = κ(1+√ε)/(1−√ε), meaning error feedback is always detrimental in the single-agent deterministic setting. Numerically, the maximum absolute difference between EF and EF²¹ contraction factors is on the order of 10⁻⁵ to 10⁻⁷ across all tested (ε, η, κ) grids.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CGD (using the tight inexact-gradient analysis of Gannot [64] and De Klerk et al. [52]), EF and EF²¹ are strictly worse: CGD achieves a smaller contraction factor for every (ε, κ) pair, and its region of convergence is substantially larger (EF/EF²¹ diverge for many tunings where CGD converges). This is verified symbolically via a WolframScript certificate showing ρ_EF − ρ_CGD > 0 for all valid parameters. Against the existing EF²¹ rate of Richtárik et al. [14] (under the Lojasiewicz inequality, equation 50), the paper's new rate is strictly faster, as proven analytically in Appendix B.2 and visualized in Figures 2–3. The functional residual is confirmed as the optimal Lyapunov function for CGD (Table 2), consistent with the tight analysis of De Klerk et al. [52]. The paper also shows that the Lyapunov function proposed by Richtárik et al. [14] for EF²¹ is suboptimal compared to the class-optimal one derived here (Appendix B.2, D.5).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the performance estimation (PEP) framework of Taylor et al. (2018) and Upadhyaya et al. (2025) to first-order methods with compression operators, a setting not previously covered by automated Lyapunov search. It builds on the proof-structure insights of Goujaud et al. (2023) to derive analytical Lyapunov functions from numerically discovered ones, using symbolic regression (PySR) and a CAS (Mathematica) to obtain closed-form expressions. In contrast to prior analyses of EF (Stich et al., Karimireddy et al.) and EF21 (Richtárik et al.), which proposed Lyapunov functions ad hoc without proving tightness, this work identifies the *optimal* Lyapunov function within a defined class and provides matching lower bounds, yielding exact worst-case rates.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that compressed gradient descent strictly outperforms both EF and EF21 in the single-agent, smooth, strongly convex setting directly challenges the widely held 'compression for free' narrative and suggests that error feedback may be detrimental when the gradient is deterministic and the setting is single-agent. The identical optimal rates for EF and EF21—despite their fundamentally different motivations (error accumulation vs. control variate subtraction)—provide a new lens on their relationship. Key limitations include the restriction to single-agent, smooth, strongly convex functions with deterministic compressors; the paper explicitly raises whether the EF/EF21 equivalence and the CGD superiority persist in the multi-agent setting where CGD is known to diverge. The combined PEP + symbolic regression + CAS methodology is broadly applicable to other compressed or inexact first-order methods.
