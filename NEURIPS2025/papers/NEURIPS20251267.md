## Problem

Diffusion-based generative planners like Diffuser and Decision Diffuser can only produce plans similar to those in their training data, making them unable to solve longer-horizon or novel start-goal tasks without exhaustively collecting long-horizon trajectory data. The paper addresses how to enable a generative planner to compositionally stitch together short trajectory chunks from previously seen tasks to form coherent, physically feasible long-horizon plans for unseen start-goal pairs.

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes a start state q_s and goal state q_g (which may be entirely unseen combinations) and produces a long-horizon trajectory plan τ_comp as a merged sequence of states and actions. Training data consists only of short trajectory segments (e.g., constrained to 4-block travel distance in OGBench), while the composed output can span up to 30 blocks. The state dimensionality is flexible, ranging from 2D point-agent positions to 50D humanoid joint configurations, and an MLP inverse dynamics model converts the planned state sequence into executable actions.

## Method

CompDiffuser trains a single diffusion denoiser ε_θ on short trajectory chunks using a noisy-sample conditioning objective: each chunk τ_k is denoised conditioned on noisy versions of its neighbors τ_{k-1}^t and τ_{k+1}^t at the same noise level t, enabling bidirectional information propagation that ensures dynamic consistency at overlap boundaries. The same network is additionally trained to condition the first chunk on the start state q_s and the last chunk on the goal state q_g. At inference, K overlapping chunks are initialized with Gaussian noise and iteratively denoised using either parallel sampling (all chunks updated simultaneously) or autoregressive sampling (each chunk conditioned on the less-noisy decoded version of its predecessor), with the autoregressive scheme yielding stronger inter-chunk coordination. The final chunks are merged via exponential trajectory blending in their overlapping regions to produce a single coherent long-horizon plan.

## Results

On OGBench AntMaze Giant, CompDiffuser achieves 65±3% success rate, substantially outperforming the best offline RL baseline HIQL (21±2%) and the compositional diffusion baseline GSC (20±1%), while all other baselines (GCBC, GCIVL, GCIQL, QRL, CRL) score 0%. On HumanoidMaze Giant, CompDiffuser reaches 67±4% versus HIQL's 12±2% and GSC's 5±1%. On AntSoccer Arena, CompDiffuser achieves 69±3% (17D) versus GSC's 65±3% and the best offline RL method HIQL's 23±2%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CompDiffuser decisively outperforms all six offline RL baselines (GCBC, GCIVL, GCIQL, QRL, CRL, HIQL) on Large and Giant mazes, where those methods collapse to near-zero success rates while CompDiffuser maintains 65–86%. Against the closest compositional diffusion baseline GSC [48], CompDiffuser wins by a large margin on the hardest tasks: 65% vs 20% on AntMaze Giant and 67% vs 5% on HumanoidMaze Giant, but GSC edges ahead slightly on AntMaze Medium (97% vs 96%) and HumanoidMaze Medium (92% vs 91%). On the low-quality Explore dataset, GSC still leads (90% vs 81% on Medium), indicating CompDiffuser's compositional stitching is less robust to extremely noisy training data. On AntSoccer, CompDiffuser outperforms GSC in both 4D (55% vs 41%) and 17D (69% vs 65%) settings, and surpasses the best offline RL method HIQL by 46 points on Arena. Decision Diffuser [1] and Decision Transformer [9] (monolithic planners) fail entirely on long-horizon tasks, scoring 0% on PointMaze Large and all OGBench Giant environments, confirming that monolithic generative planning cannot generalize beyond training horizon.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends diffusion-based planning (Diffuser, Decision Diffuser) from monolithic trajectory generation to compositional generation, drawing on the trajectory stitching concept from RL and the compositional generative modeling framework of Du & Kaelbling. The closest prior method, Generative Skill Chaining (GSC), also composes short trajectory segments via diffusion but relies on a pre-defined task-dependent skeleton and separate per-chunk models. CompDiffuser departs by training a single denoiser that conditions on noisy neighboring chunks at the same diffusion timestep, enabling bidirectional information propagation without blocked Gibbs sampling or fixed skeletons.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical impact is sample efficiency: long-horizon planning becomes possible without collecting long-horizon demonstrations, which is costly in robotics. The method's consistent performance across 2D point agents to 50D humanoids and across clean to extremely noisy training data suggests broad applicability. Open questions include error accumulation over many composed chunks (requiring rejection sampling or MCMC), automatic selection of the number of chunks K, and whether the noisy-sample conditioning trick transfers to other compositional generation domains beyond trajectories.
