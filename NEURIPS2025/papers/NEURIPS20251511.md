## Problem

Real-world observational data often arise from heterogeneous subpopulations with distinct causal mechanisms, yet standard causal discovery assumes a single homogeneous distribution. Prior mixture-based approaches either require multiple separate datasets with known intervention targets or assume a single global latent variable affecting all observables, failing to capture settings where different variables are governed by independent latent factors. This paper addresses the joint inference of both the causal graph and multiple independent latent mixing variables from a single pooled observational dataset.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single i.i.d. observational dataset of continuous variables (no knowledge of subpopulation membership or experimental conditions) along with a hyperparameter K_max capping the number of mixture components per variable. It outputs a directed acyclic graph over the observed variables, the number m of latent mixing variables with their component counts K_i and mixing probabilities γ^i, a surjective mapping La assigning each observed variable to its governing latent factor, and per-component linear regression coefficient vectors. Unlike standard GMM-based approaches, the mixing is conditional on each variable's discovered parents (MLR), so source nodes reduce to unconditional GMMs while non-source nodes use conditional mixtures.

## Method

Each observed variable's causal mechanism is modeled as a Mixture of Linear Regressions (MLR) whose coefficients are indexed by a latent categorical mixing variable, with the key design choice that different observed variables can be governed by different independent latent factors. The scoring criterion is a latent-aware BIC that, at each edge-evaluation step, fits an MLR via EM for every candidate K ≤ K_max and selects the K maximizing the BIC, thereby jointly inferring the number of mixture components. The paper proves local consistency of this score (Theorem 3.3) under mild non-degeneracy conditions (Lemma B.1: the MLR is almost surely non-Gaussian when K ≥ 2), establishing it as a consistent scoring criterion (Corollary 3.4) that enables GES or TOPIC to recover the correct Markov equivalence class. The practical algorithm (Alg. 1) embeds this score within TOPIC's topological-order search: at each step it identifies a source, adds/prunes edges using BIC score differences, fits per-node MLR assignments via EM, and finally consolidates per-node mixing assignments into global latent variables by merging those whose Adjusted Mutual Information exceeds its expected value.

## Results

On the Sachs et al. (2005) flow cytometry dataset (5,846 samples, 11 variables), CMM achieves a separation distance (SD) of 0.25 and S/C of 0.27, substantially outperforming Mixture-UTIGSP (SD = 0.75, S/C = 0.57), though at the cost of more false positive edges (10 FP vs. 2 FP). On synthetic CMM benchmarks, CMM maintains high true positive rate (TPR) and low SD across varying numbers of latent variables (N_Z up to 10), mixture components (K up to 5), and graph densities, while baselines degrade as N_Z increases. For mixing structure recovery, CMM achieves reliable AMI and F1 scores for per-node assignments and target identification, whereas the GMM-based Mixture-UTIGSP baseline is misspecified whenever more than one latent mixing variable is present.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On synthetic CMM data (Figure 5), CMM outperforms all eight baselines (CAM, SCORE, LINGAM, FCI, GES, PC, VARSORT, R²SORT) in both separation distance and true positive rate for directed edges, with the gap widening as the number of latent mixing variables N_Z increases. Mixture-UTIGSP (Kumar et al., 2024) is the closest competitor: on the mixture-of-interventions setting (Figure 6), CMM matches its AMI for mixture assignment recovery but achieves a lower SD for causal graph discovery. On the Sachs dataset, CMM recovers 5 true positive edges with SD = 0.25, while Mixture-UTIGSP recovers 0 TP edges with SD = 0.75; however, CMM produces 10 false positives versus only 2 for Mixture-UTIGSP. Among the standard causal discovery baselines, PC is noted as the only one with low false positives (2 FP) on Sachs, while all others report 10+ FP. The GMM-based approach from Kumar et al. is misspecified when N_Z > 1 because it assumes a single global mixing variable, causing its performance to degrade as the number of independent latent factors grows. VARSORT shows competitive performance on the mixture-of-interventions data, which the authors flag as potentially inflated by Var-sortability of that particular dataset.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends score-based causal discovery (GES, TOPIC) to handle heterogeneous data by modeling each variable's causal mechanism as a mixture of linear regressions indexed by latent mixing variables. It departs from prior mixture-causal-discovery work (Kumar et al., 2024; Mazaheri et al., 2023) in two key ways: allowing multiple independent mixing variables each affecting distinct subsets of observed variables (rather than a single global confounder), and integrating mixture inference directly into the scoring step rather than using a two-stage cluster-then-discover pipeline. The theoretical foundation builds on Chickering's (2002) consistency framework for BIC, extended here to a latent-aware variant that accounts for the unknown number and assignment of mixture components.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important gap: real-world observational data (e.g., multi-site clinical studies, batch-affected omics) often pools subpopulations with distinct causal mechanisms, and ignoring this heterogeneity biases causal estimates. The consistency guarantee for the latent-aware BIC provides the first theoretical justification for using score-based search in this multi-mixing-variable setting. Key limitations include the linear functional form (acknowledged as restricting real-world applicability, as seen in the modest Sachs dataset results), the computational cost of running EM at every scoring step, and the open question of whether the EM-based score inherits the oracle MLE consistency guarantee in practice.
