## Problem

Existing test-time scaling methods like self-consistency aggregate answers from multiple LLM responses using only surface-level frequency, ignoring the rich information encoded in internal model activations. This paper asks whether the consistency of a model's internal representations across responses converging on the same answer can serve as a signal for answer correctness, and whether leveraging this signal improves aggregation beyond simple majority voting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251718/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a set of N LLM responses (generated from semantically equivalent prompt rephrasings with temperature-0.7 sampling) along with their cached residual-stream activations at a single target token position (the position where the model is about to output the final answer choice). It produces a single aggregated answer selected via an evaluation function. No additional model queries are required beyond the initial generation; the only extra computation is lightweight pairwise similarity (or one SAE forward pass per response for the sparse variant).

## Method

RC groups responses by their parsed final answer, then scores each group with a weighted combination of (1) activation consistency — the mean pairwise cosine similarity of cached activations within the group — and (2) answer frequency — the group's proportion of total responses — controlled by hyperparameter λ. Two variants are proposed: RC-D operates on raw dense residual-stream activations at a tunable layer (typically mid-depth), while RC-S first passes activations through a pretrained sparse autoencoder (GemmaScope or LlamaScope) to obtain disentangled sparse signals before computing similarity. The key insight is that responses with coherent reasoning should exhibit similar internal representations even when their surface-level text differs, so high within-group activation consistency indicates robust reasoning and should up-weight that answer during aggregation. The method requires no retraining, no modified decoding, and no specialized prompting — only cached activations and similarity computations.

## Results

Across four open-source LLMs (Llama-3.1-8B, Gemma-2-2B/9B/27B) and four reasoning datasets (CSQA, MMLU, MedMCQA, HellaSwag), RC-S (sparse) achieves the highest average accuracy gains over Self-Consistency: +1.10% (Gemma-2-2B), +1.40% (Gemma-2-9B), +0.89% (Gemma-2-27B), and +1.73% (Llama-3.1-8B). RC-D (dense) is competitive, reaching +1.84% on Llama-3.1-8B. Peak improvements reach 4.00% (RC-S) and 3.85% (RC-D) on CSQA for Llama-3.1-8B with 6 responses, while the external-embedding baseline RC-E yields smaller gains of +0.55% to +1.07%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is Self-Consistency (SC) [Wang et al., ICLR 2023], which selects the modal answer from multiple CoT responses. Both RC-D and RC-S consistently outperform SC across all four models and all four datasets, with RC-S achieving the highest average gains (+0.89% to +1.40%) and RC-D reaching +1.84% on Llama-3.1-8B. The external-embedding variant RC-E, which replaces internal activations with NLI entailment probabilities from bge-m3, also improves over SC but by a smaller margin (+0.55% to +1.07%), confirming that internal activations carry useful signal beyond surface-level text. Non-selective Expectation (NE), which simply averages over all responses, performs worst, falling 4.4–5.6% below SC. In 30 out of 32 model-dataset configurations, RC-D or RC-S yields the highest gain, with RC-S more frequently outperforming RC-D.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the self-consistency (SC) paradigm for answer aggregation by incorporating a new signal: the consistency of internal model activations across responses that converge on the same answer. While prior work has used activations for single-response reasoning improvements (requiring retraining) or for mechanistic interpretability, this is the first to leverage cached activations from multiple generations as a test-time aggregation criterion. The sparse variant (RC-S) repurposes pretrained sparse autoencoders (GemmaScope, LlamaScope) originally designed for interpretability as a lightweight encoding step that disentangles polysemantic activations into sparse, concept-aligned signals before computing pairwise similarity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that SAE-sparsified activation consistency aligns with human-judged reasoning coherence at 90%+ rates (vs. ~50% for dense activations) provides a concrete bridge between mechanistic interpretability tools and practical inference-time decision-making, suggesting SAE latent spaces capture something close to the 'reasoning concept' a model is using. However, the method requires access to model internals (limiting it to open-source LLMs), the accuracy gains over SC are modest (1–4%), and the approach may fail when a model is confidently and systematically wrong. Open questions include extending RC to open-ended generation, multi-LLM ensembles, and combining it with decoding-based scaling methods like tree-of-thoughts.
