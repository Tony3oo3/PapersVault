## Problem

Geometric deep learning has driven breakthroughs in computer vision, structural biology, and climate science, yet cosmology lacks a unified, large-scale benchmark to systematically evaluate ML methods on its core data types—point clouds and directed merger trees. COSMOBENCH fills this gap by consolidating 34,000 point clouds across three spatial scales and 25,000 directed trees across two temporal scales into a single multitask benchmark, enabling direct comparison of cosmological modeling approaches, simple invariant-feature models, and deep architectures.

## Input / Output

Point cloud inputs are 3D positions (and velocities) of halos or galaxies extracted from periodic boundary condition boxes of 1,000, 100, or 25 cMpc/h, with 5,000 to 112,000 points per cloud; a redshift-space variant shifts z-coordinates by v_z/H_0 to mimic observational conditions. Merger tree inputs are directed acyclic graphs (121–37,865 nodes) with node features including mass, concentration, v_max, and scale factor, where edges flow from progenitor to descendant halos. Outputs are graph-level regression targets (Ω_m, σ_8), per-node 3D velocity vectors, or binary labels on virtual nodes indicating whether a masked merger actually occurred.

## Method

The paper provides a unified PyTorch interface with baselines spanning three complexity tiers: (1) established cosmological statistics (2PCF + MLP, linear-theory velocity oracle, Extended Press-Schechter), (2) lightweight linear models with symmetry-constrained features (pairwise-distance quantiles for cosmology, inverse-distance powers for velocity, 1-hop k-NN for merger nodes), and (3) message-passing neural networks on radius graphs decorated with E(3)-invariant edge features and higher-order Delaunay tetrahedron neighborhoods. A key empirical finding is that least-squares fits with ~49 invariant features match or exceed GNNs with 671K–1.2M parameters at a fraction of the compute cost, particularly at large spatial scales, while GNNs gain an advantage at smaller, more non-linear scales. For merger trees, a 4-layer MPNN exploiting directed topology outperforms DeepSets (which discards edges), and the scale factor 'a' emerges as the single most discriminative feature for both parameter prediction and merger-node classification.

## Results

On the primary task of predicting cosmological parameters (Ω_m, σ_8) from point clouds, the 2PCF+MLP baseline achieves the best R² on Quijote (0.85/0.84), while the lightweight LLS model (49 parameters) is competitive with GNNs (671K–1166K parameters) across all three scales. For velocity prediction, LLS outperforms the linear-theory oracle on Quijote (R²=0.435 vs. 0.377) without requiring cosmological parameters, and GNNs surpass both at smaller scales (CAMELS-SAM: 0.287 vs. 0.237). On merger trees, GNNs with all node features (mass, concentration, v_max, scale factor) achieve R²=0.996 for Ω_m and 0.82 for σ_8, while for merger-node classification the best GNN reaches 69% accuracy versus 53% for the Extended Press-Schechter physics baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The MPNN architecture of Gilmer et al. [68] serves as the primary GNN baseline across all tasks. On cosmological parameter prediction, the MPNN (671K params, 1 day training) is outperformed by the 49-parameter LLS model on Quijote (R² 0.80 vs. 0.83 for Ω_m) and CAMELS (0.78 vs. 0.78, tied), while the GNN edges ahead on CAMELS-SAM σ_8 (0.83 vs. 0.82). For velocity prediction, the MPNN (126K params, 15 hr) beats LLS on CAMELS-SAM (0.287 vs. 0.211) but loses on Quijote (0.410 vs. 0.435). DeepSets [70], which discards tree topology, is consistently outperformed by the GNN on merger-tree parameter prediction (e.g., R²=0.993 vs. 0.996 for Ω_m with all features), confirming that directed-tree structure carries useful signal. The Extended Press-Schechter (EPS) implementation of Jiang & van den Bosch [73] achieves only 53% accuracy on merger-node classification, well below the GNN (69%) and even the simple 1-hop k-NN (62%), and moreover EPS requires knowledge of cosmological parameters while the ML methods do not.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by consolidating multiple existing cosmological tasks (parameter inference from clustering statistics, velocity field reconstruction, merger tree assembly) into a single unified geometric deep learning benchmark, rather than building on a single prior method. It extends the limited 3,560-point-cloud benchmark of Balla et al. [43] by an order of magnitude in size, adds directed merger trees as a second data modality, and spans three spatial scales (25–1000 cMpc/h) and two temporal resolutions. Conceptually, it repurposes established GNN architectures (MPNNs, DeepSets) and classical cosmological tools (2PCF, linear theory, Extended Press-Schechter) as baselines within a shared PyTorch interface, positioning the benchmark as a bridge between the geometric deep learning community and the cosmology community rather than proposing a new architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

COSMOBENCH provides the first large-scale, multi-modality, multi-task benchmark that enables systematic comparison of geometric deep learning methods against established cosmological modeling approaches, which has been a gap the authors explicitly identify. The finding that 49-parameter linear models with pairwise-distance statistics match or beat 671K–1.1M-parameter GNNs on large-scale parameter inference is a useful caution against reflexive scaling, though it is partially expected given that global cosmological parameters are well-captured by low-order clustering statistics. Key open questions include whether more principled symmetry-aware architectures can close the remaining gaps at small scales, how to handle the small effective sample sizes (1,000 simulations in CAMELS) that limit purely data-driven approaches, and how to extend the benchmark toward realistic observational conditions beyond the simplified redshift-space treatment.
