## Problem

Existing spurious correlation benchmarks (e.g., Waterbirds, CelebA) are designed for supervised classification with a small number of known spurious features, making them unsuitable for evaluating pre-trained LVLMs in generalized, open-ended VQA settings. No prior benchmark systematically tests whether frontier LVLMs rely on dominant but unreliable correlations in natural, diverse visual question-answering tasks where no task-specific fine-tuning is available.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251388/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each sample pairs an RGB image with a four-option multiple-choice VQA question, and the model outputs a single letter (A–D). The benchmark comprises 124 distinct spurious correlation types, each containing 1 real anchor image from source benchmarks (SEEDBench, SEEDBench2, NaturalBench, AOKVQA) and 10 synthetic counterfactual images generated via Stable Diffusion, totaling 1,364 questions. A matched set of 1,240 non-spurious samples drawn from the same source benchmarks serves as a control for measuring the accuracy gap attributable to the spurious feature.

## Method

SpuriVerse is built through a five-step curation pipeline: (1) collect GPT-4o errors on 55,911 VQA samples; (2) apply two-stage verification where GPT-4o proposes candidate spurious features and human annotators confirm and refine them, yielding 194 validated samples; (3) prompt an LLM to generate paired scene descriptions—one core (without the spurious feature) and one spurious (with it)—conditioned on the correct answer; (4) synthesize 10 images per description using Stable Diffusion, forming core and spurious groups; (5) retain only samples where at least one of three reference LVLMs shows a ≥30% accuracy drop on the spurious group versus the core group. The paper then evaluates 15 LVLMs on the anchor, spurious, and non-spurious sets, tests prompting strategies (Chain-of-Thought and a spurious-aware prompt), and fine-tunes Llama-3.2-11B and Qwen2.5-VL-7B via LoRA on held-out splits to demonstrate that training on diverse spurious patterns generalizes to unseen correlations, while revealing a trade-off in non-spurious accuracy.

## Results

On SpuriVerse's anchor set (124 samples), all 15 evaluated LVLMs perform below the 40.7% random-guess baseline, with the best being Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct at 35.0% and GPT-4o at just 17.26%. On the spurious group (1240 synthetic samples), the best model o4-mini reaches 50.45%, while non-spurious samples from the same source benchmarks yield 68–85% accuracy, confirming the difficulty is attributable to spurious features. Fine-tuning Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct on spurious-group samples boosts anchor accuracy from 35.20% to 78.40% on held-out unseen correlations, but at the cost of a 14-point drop in non-spurious accuracy (81.20% → 67.20%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

As a benchmark paper, SpuriVerse evaluates 15 existing LVLMs rather than proposing a new method against baselines. All models fall below the 40.7% random-guess threshold on the anchor set, with open-source Qwen2.5-VL-7B (35.0%) and Llama-3.2-90B (31.45%) leading, while GPT-4o (17.26%) and GPT-4o-mini (18.87%) perform worst. On the spurious group, reasoning models o4-mini (50.45%) and o3 (50.34%) edge ahead of all others, but still only marginally exceed random. Prompting strategies (Chain-of-Thought and Spurious-Aware) yield at most ~10–15% improvement over direct prompting (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL-7B anchor: 37.90% → 51.61% with Spurious-Aware), falling well short of closing the gap to non-spurious performance. Fine-tuning on spurious-group samples produces the largest gains (Qwen2.5-VL-7B: 35.20% → 78.40% on held-out anchors), but introduces a 14-point trade-off on non-spurious accuracy, suggesting models fundamentally rely on shortcut correlations for their overall performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the traditional spurious-correlation literature (Waterbirds, CelebA, UrbanCars, SPAWRIous), which studies contrived, task-specific settings with foreknowledge of spurious attributes and imbalanced training distributions. It also distinguishes itself from the closest prior benchmark, MMSpuBench, which asks models to *identify* the spurious feature rather than testing whether they actually *err* due to it. The key methodological departure is a bottom-up, error-driven curation pipeline: starting from GPT-4o failures on real-world VQA benchmarks, validating via human-LVLM collaboration, and confirming through counterfactual synthetic images generated with Stable Diffusion. This yields a diverse, naturalistic benchmark (124 correlation types) rather than a handful of hand-designed spurious attributes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SpuriVerse provides the first systematic evaluation of spurious-correlation susceptibility in pre-trained LVLMs in generalized, open-ended VQA settings, revealing that even frontier models (GPT-4o, o3, o4-mini) score below random on the anchor set. The finding that fine-tuning on diverse synthetic spurious examples generalizes to unseen correlations suggests a learnable meta-skill, but the accompanying trade-off—degraded accuracy on non-spurious samples—indicates that shortcut reliance is deeply embedded in LVLM representations. Open questions include whether this trade-off can be resolved, whether the meta-skill transfers to open-generation (non-multiple-choice) settings, and whether the 124 identified correlations adequately capture the full landscape of spurious features in web-scale pre-training data.
