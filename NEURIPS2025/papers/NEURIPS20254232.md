## Problem

Intent classification research has been dominated by high-resource languages, leaving a critical gap for low-resource African languages like Wolof, spoken by over 10 million people in West Africa. In Senegal, where Wolof is the primary language of communication for ~90% of the population but adult illiteracy remains at 42%, the absence of banking-domain intent datasets in both text and speech modalities prevents the development of voice assistants that could enable financial inclusion for illiterate or semi-literate users.

## Input / Output

For the text task, input is a Wolof customer-service sentence (mean 12.22 words, range 2–81) and output is one of 77 banking intent labels; two dataset sizes are provided (5k and 9,791 samples) with an 80/20 train/test split. For the speech task, input is a single-channel 16 kHz WAV clip (mean duration 4,815 ms, ~4 h 17 min total across 3,204 clips from 31 speakers) and output is either a transcribed text string (ASR) or one of 10 intent labels spanning e-banking and transport domains. Duplicated Wolof translations were removed to avoid many-to-one English-to-Wolof mappings, and preprocessing strips punctuation, numbers, and lowercases text before evaluation.

## Method

The dataset is constructed by manually translating the English Banking77 training set (13,083 queries, 77 intents) into French and Wolof via linguistic experts at CLAD, with localization of domain terms (e.g., 'ATM'→'GAB', 'app'→'aplikaasiyo'). The audio component extends MINDS-14 by adding five new intents and recording 186 utterances per speaker using the Lig-Aikuma elicitation tool on Android tablets, capturing diverse accents across Senegalese regions. Evaluation spans a progression of model complexity: classic ML (KNN, SVM, LR, NB), LASER sentence embeddings with MLP/CNN classifiers, zero-shot and few-shot (2-shot, 8-shot via SetFit) classification with multilingual transformers (BERT-base, Afro-XLMR-large, AfroLM, mDeBERTa-v3, AfriTeVa V2), fine-tuned Llama-3.2-1B/3B, and ASR fine-tuning of Canary-1b-flash, Phi-4-multimodal-instruct, and Distil-whisper-large-v3.5 for 1,000 steps on the 4-hour Wolof corpus. Afro-XLMR achieves the best F1 (79% on 5k samples) in few-shot/fine-tuning settings, while Canary-1b-flash attains the lowest WER (0.59%) among ASR baselines, demonstrating that strong performance is achievable with limited low-resource data.

## Results

On the WolBanking77 audio dataset (~4 hours of speech), Canary-1b-flash achieves the lowest Word Error Rate at 0.59% after 1,000 fine-tuning steps, substantially outperforming Phi-4-multimodal-instruct (WER 3.1%) and Distil-whisper-large-v3.5 (WER 4.63%). For intent detection on the 5k-sample text subset, AfroXLMR-large achieves the best F1-score of 79% in the few-shot/fine-tuning setting, followed by BERT-Base and AfroLM. Among Small Language Models, Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct reaches an F1 of 0.75 on 5k samples but drops to 0.55 on the full 9,791-sample set, while Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct scores 0.72 and 0.46 respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

For ASR, Canary-1b-flash (1B params) dominates with a WER of 0.59%, beating the much larger Phi-4-multimodal-instruct (5.6B, WER 3.1%) by a factor of ~5× and Distil-whisper-large-v3.5 (756M, WER 4.63%) by ~8×, while also training fastest (20 min vs. 32 and 44 min). For intent detection, AfroXLMR-large (pre-trained on 17 African languages but not Wolof) achieves the highest F1 of 79% on the 5k subset in few-shot/fine-tuning, outperforming BERT-Base (English-only) and AfroLM (23 African languages including Wolof). Among SLMs, Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct (F1 0.75 on 5k) edges out Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct (F1 0.72), but both degrade significantly on the full dataset (0.55 and 0.46 respectively), indicating that scaling to 77 intents with more diverse samples challenges even 3B-parameter models. The paper notes that even models pre-trained on Wolof (AfroLM, mDeBERTa-v3) do not perform well in zero-shot, underscoring the specific challenges of the Wolof banking domain.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Banking77 text intent classification dataset (Casanueva et al., 2020) and the MINDS-14 spoken intent detection dataset (Gerz et al., 2021) to the Wolof language, a low-resource Sub-Saharan African language spoken by over 10 million people. It positions itself within the growing but still sparse body of African-language NLU resources (INJONGO, XTREME-S, MULTI3NLU++) by providing the first Wolof banking-domain dataset with both text and audio modalities. The experimental evaluation draws on the standard progression from classic ML baselines through multilingual transformer encoders (XLM-R, DeBERTa, LASER) to recent small language models (Llama-3.2) and state-of-the-art ASR systems (Canary Flash, Phi-4-multimodal, Distil-whisper), following the evaluation protocols established by the source datasets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dataset addresses a concrete accessibility gap: with ~42% adult illiteracy in French (Senegal's official language) and Wolof as the primary spoken language for ~90% of the population, voice-based intent classification is essential for financial inclusion in the informal economy. The finding that even AfroLM, pre-trained on Wolof, achieves limited zero-shot performance underscores that domain-specific data remains critical for low-resource languages. Open questions include generalization across Wolof dialects and regional variants (Mauritania, Gambia), the absence of multi-intent or slot-filling annotations, and whether the small audio corpus (4 hours, 31 speakers from a single university) is sufficient for robust deployment. The paper does not report error bars or statistical significance, limiting confidence in the reported performance differences.
