## Problem

The extrapolatory behaviour of neural networks far from the training distribution remains poorly understood, with existing theoretical results limited to specific architectures or activation functions. No general guarantee existed for whether a trained network's outputs remain bounded on unseen inputs, a critical concern for safety-sensitive applications. This paper asks whether a simple architectural choice—Layer Normalisation—can fundamentally alter this behaviour.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254629/cropped_page5_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Theoretically, the framework applies to any n0-dimensional input vector and produces a scalar prediction, with the output bound depending only on the training set size, target magnitudes, and the smallest eigenvalue of the training Gram matrix. Empirically, inputs are either 9-dimensional physicochemical feature vectors (protein RMSD prediction) or 200×200 facial images processed through a frozen ResNet-18 feature extractor (age prediction). The key output property is that networks with LN saturate to finite values as input magnitude grows, while standard networks extrapolate linearly without bound.

## Method

The paper applies Neural Tangent Kernel theory to infinitely-wide fully-connected networks trained to convergence, showing that the trained output equals the GP posterior mean under the architecture-induced kernel. The central insight is that a single Layer Norm anywhere in the network transforms the NTK into a bounded-variance kernel: the LN operation normalises pre-activations so that the kernel depends only on the soft-cosine similarity between inputs (a direction-dependent quantity in [-1,1]) rather than on input magnitude. This yields a finite upper bound on |E[f(x)]| for all x ∈ R^n0 via Cauchy-Schwarz on the GP posterior mean, in contrast to the unbounded growth proven for LN-free networks with positive n-homogeneous nonlinearities. The authors further show this guarantee is specific to the training dynamics (NTK regime) and cannot be recovered by merely optimising parameters, and extend the results to asymptotically homogeneous activations (GELU, SiLU) and arbitrary Tensor Program architectures.

## Results

On the UCI Protein dataset, LN-equipped networks achieve OOD R² of 0.50±0.02, substantially outperforming the Standard NN (0.31±0.02) and XGBoost (0.27±0.04), while in-distribution R² is comparable across all methods (~0.58–0.61). On UTKFace age prediction, the best LN variant (LN preceding the first hidden layer) reaches OOD R² of 0.47±0.03 versus 0.27±0.03 for the Standard NN, with in-distribution R² remaining similar (~0.66–0.68). Across both tasks, the position of the LN layer has minimal impact on OOD performance, and all LN variants consistently prevent the unbounded extrapolation seen in the no-LN baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares LN-equipped networks against two baselines: a Standard NN (no LN) and XGBoost. On the protein extrapolation task, all three LN variants (0.50±0.02 OOD R²) outperform the Standard NN (0.31±0.02) by roughly 0.19 R² and XGBoost (0.27±0.04) by roughly 0.23 R², while in-distribution performance is statistically indistinguishable. On UTKFace, the best LN variant (LN first, 0.47±0.03 OOD R²) beats the Standard NN (0.27±0.03) by 0.20 R², with no external non-neural baseline reported. The key finding is that LN networks maintain bounded, physically plausible extrapolations (e.g., continuing an upward trend in protein RMSD without exploding), whereas the Standard NN produces pathologically large predictions and XGBoost collapses to a near-constant, both of which are less realistic for the underlying biophysics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the NTK-based analysis of neural network extrapolation initiated by Xu et al. (2020), who showed ReLU networks extrapolate linearly, to general positive n-homogeneous nonlinearities and, crucially, introduces the complementary result that a single Layer Norm renders the NTK bounded-variance. The theoretical framework draws on the infinite-width GP equivalence of Lee et al. (2019) and the Tensor Programs machinery of Yang (2019–2020) to handle arbitrary architectures. The paper also connects to the emerging literature on understanding Layer Norm's role (Xu et al. 2019 on gradient effects; Zhu et al. 2025 on replacing LN with tanh), providing the first NTK-level explanation for why LN stabilizes extrapolation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first theoretical guarantee that a single architectural choice (one LN layer) certifies bounded extrapolation for any training set and any test point, offering a concrete safety argument for deploying networks in critical systems. The practical takeaway is simple: if unbounded OOD predictions are unacceptable, include at least one LN. However, the bound is worst-case and potentially loose, bounded extrapolation does not guarantee correct extrapolation, and the proofs rely on the infinite-width NTK limit whose fidelity to finite-width networks is only empirically verified. Open questions include whether tighter, data-dependent bounds exist, how the results interact with modern optimizers and regularization, and whether the guarantee extends to architectures where LN is absent by design (e.g., certain diffusion models or physics-informed networks).
