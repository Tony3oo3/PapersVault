## Problem

Universal portfolio algorithms provide provable worst-case guarantees against adversarial return sequences but deliberately diffuse capital, forfeiting gains when strong predictive structure is present; conversely, ML-driven heuristic strategies exploit forecasts aggressively yet can suffer unbounded losses when predictions fail. The paper asks how to fuse fallible ML ranking forecasts into a classically robust online portfolio scheme so that performance improves smoothly with prediction quality while retaining a measurable offline benchmark when forecasts are adversarial. Existing side-information universal portfolios are prediction-agnostic (no error-sensitive guarantees), converge only asymptotically to modest BCRP/BSCRP benchmarks, and offer no quantitative characterization of how performance scales with prediction error.

## Input / Output

At each of n trading periods the algorithm receives a return vector of m positive price relatives and a black-box ML oracle's ranking — a permutation σ(i) of [m] ordering assets by predicted one-step return — but explicitly does not require predicted return magnitudes, only the ordering. The output is a sequence of nonnegative unit-sum weight vectors (one per period) and the resulting terminal wealth S_n. The base generator updates weights via a buy-and-hold rule (no traditional rebalancing), while the permutation layer reassigns sorted weights to assets according to the predicted ranking, making the effective per-round cost O(m log m) for sorting.

## Method

RAM (Rebalanced Arithmetic Mean) uses a two-stage architecture: a prediction-agnostic base generator that updates weights by the buy-and-hold rule b_k(i+1) = b_k(i)·x_k(i) / Σ_j b_j(i)·x_j(i), and a permutation layer that sorts the current weights in descending order and pairs the largest weight with the asset the oracle ranks highest, the second-largest with the second-highest, and so on. The key theoretical insight is that the AM-GM inequality lower bound (the value line index, i.e., the geometric mean of all returns) is invariant under any fixed permutation of asset coordinates, so re-indexing next-period returns according to the forecast is essentially cost-free for the worst-case guarantee. Theorem 2.1 proves that under perfect predictions (η_n = 1) RAM achieves at least 1/m of the hindsight-optimal all-in-best-asset benchmark, while under maximally adversarial predictions it still dominates the geometric mean. For real-world deployment, a LightGBM LambdaMART ranker retrained daily on a 250-day sliding window with exponential decay (θ = 0.995^age) supplies the permutation, and a 60-day hold-out slice within the window provides early-stopping to eliminate look-ahead bias.

## Results

On the S&P 500 with a production-grade LightGBM LambdaMART rank predictor, RAM achieves terminal wealth of 1.239 (α-basket) and 1.493 (γ-basket) in the recent 2022–2024 window, exceeding the geometric-mean baseline (1.401) and the side-information EG baseline (1.501) in the γ-basket while trailing it by only 0.6%. In the synthetic NYSE study at 53% forecast accuracy, RAM's mean wealth reaches 1.5×10³–1.5×10⁴ across 7–12-asset portfolios, dwarfing EGSI's 22–35 by two to three orders of magnitude. Under perfect predictions (p=100%), RAM captures wealth on the order of 10⁴¹–10⁵⁹ versus EGSI's 10⁴–10⁷, confirming the theoretical 1/m fraction of the hindsight optimum.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Cover's Universal Portfolio (UP) [3], the prediction-free randomized variant of RAM (RAM-R at p=50%) outperforms UP in mean wealth across all six NYSE combinations (e.g., Comb 1: 57.78 vs. 39.97; Comb 2: 136.39 vs. 80.53), though with substantially higher variance (σ ≈ 216 vs. 37). Against the side-information universal portfolio (UPSI) [22], RAM dominates once forecast accuracy exceeds 50%: at p=53% on the 7-asset NYSE combination, RAM's mean wealth is 1.5×10³ versus UPSI's 22.94, a gap of over two orders of magnitude. Against Exponential Gradient (EG) and its side-information variant (EGSI) [4], the advantage widens with portfolio dimension: at p=53% on the 12-asset combination, RAM reaches 1.5×10⁴ mean wealth while EGSI plateaus at 28.82, and at p=100% the gap explodes to 2.3×10⁵⁹ vs. 1.8×10⁴. On the real S&P 500 deployment, RAM beats EGSI in 4 of 6 regime-basket cells (e.g., +10.9% in α-COVID, +2.9% in β-recent) and trails it by at most 4.5% in the remaining two, while consistently exceeding the geometric-mean value line by 2–6% even when the ML signal is weak. The all-in ML strategy (rank-1 allocation) is highly volatile: it achieves the best result in β-COVID (1.678) but collapses to 0.532 in γ-COVID, illustrating the fragility that RAM's AM-GM floor is designed to prevent.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two well-developed lines: Cover's universal portfolio family (including the side-information variant of Cover & Ordentlich and the Exponential Gradient update) and the learning-augmented algorithms paradigm (Lykouris & Vassilvitskii, Kumar et al.). The key departure is that prior side-information universal portfolios use discrete regime labels and converge only asymptotically to the best state-constant rebalanced portfolio, whereas RAM overlays a continuous ML ranking (a permutation) onto a buy-and-hold base generator, yielding an error-sensitive guarantee that explicitly couples wealth to prediction quality via a multiplicative η_n factor. The AM-GM inequality provides the worst-case floor, a structural property absent from the universal-portfolio literature's polynomial-regret analysis.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean, deployable recipe (O(m log m) per round) for converting even modest ML ranking signals into provably safe portfolio gains, addressing a practical gap where heuristic ML strategies can suffer catastrophic losses while universal portfolios ignore available forecasts. Key limitations include the omission of transaction costs (RAM rebalances every round), the 1/m constant factor under perfect predictions (loose relative to the true optimum), and the relatively simple LightGBM predictor used in experiments. Open questions include whether RAM is Pareto-optimal on the consistency-robustness frontier and how the guarantees interact with realistic trading frictions.
