## Problem

For degenerate Restless Multi-Armed Bandits (RMABs), LP-based policies derived from fluid approximations are known to incur a Θ(1/√N) optimality gap per arm, and this bound was widely believed to be order-wise tight. This paper asks whether a computationally efficient algorithm can break this barrier and achieve a strictly smaller gap for degenerate RMABs, a question left open by prior work that only achieved exponentially small gaps under a non-degeneracy condition.

## Input / Output

The method takes as input the current bandit state vector (fractions of arms in each of S states, lying on a discrete probability simplex Δ_N^S), a precomputed optimal solution y* to the fluid LP, and a precomputed Gaussian SP policy π̃^{N,*}. At each time step it outputs a discrete action vector specifying exactly αN arms to pull, obtained by rounding the continuous-valued SP policy output so that all entries multiplied by N are integers. The policy is only active within a Õ(1/√N) neighborhood of the fluid-optimal trajectory; outside this neighborhood a fallback policy π_pre is used.

## Method

The core contribution is a Gaussian stochastic system that augments the fluid (first-order) approximation with a second-order variance term: the next state is the fluid mean plus a Gaussian noise vector Z_h/√N whose covariance Γ_h(y*_h) is fixed at the fluid-optimal action, making it action-independent. This fixed-covariance design is a deliberate simplification over classical diffusion approximations (where the diffusion coefficient depends on the control), and it enables the use of the EDDP stochastic programming algorithm to solve the resulting stochastic linear program efficiently. The SP-based policy is obtained by solving this Gaussian SP restricted to a Õ(1/√N) neighborhood of y*, then applying a rounding procedure with O(1/N) error to convert back to integer arm counts. The key theoretical insight is that within this neighborhood, the Wasserstein distance between the N-system and Gaussian-system next-state distributions is Õ(1/N), which translates the second-order approximation error into a Õ(1/N) optimality gap under a uniqueness assumption on the fluid LP solution.

## Results

The SP-based policy achieves a $\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(1/N)$ optimality gap per arm for degenerate RMABs under the Uniqueness Assumption (Theorem 4.1), improving upon the $\Theta(1/\sqrt{N})$ gap of LP-based policies (Theorem 4.2). In the two-state illustrative example (Section 3.3), the SP-based policy selects $\text{round}(0.2609 + 0.3940/\sqrt{N})$ arms to pull, whereas the LP-based policy uses the fixed fluid-optimal value $0.2609$. In machine maintenance experiments (Figure 3), the total reward improvement of SP over LP grows with $N$ in both unique and non-unique LP solution settings, with 2-sigma error bars confirming statistical significance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two main LP-based policy families. Against Brown and Smith [7], which achieves $\mathcal{O}(1/\sqrt{N})$ gap under general (including degenerate) conditions, the SP-based policy improves the gap to $\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(1/N)$ specifically for degenerate RMABs satisfying uniqueness — a strict order-wise improvement. Against Gast et al. [10], which achieves an exponentially small $\mathcal{O}(\exp(-CN))$ gap but only under the non-degeneracy condition, the SP-based policy provides the first $\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(1/N)$ guarantee in the complementary degenerate regime where Gast et al.'s result does not apply. Theorem 4.2 further establishes that the $\Theta(1/\sqrt{N})$ gap of LP-based policies is order-wise tight for degenerate RMABs, confirming that the improvement from SP is not merely a constant-factor gain. In the machine maintenance numerical experiments, the SP-based policy's total reward advantage over the LP-based policy grows with $N$ in both unique and non-unique LP solution settings, consistent with the theoretical $\Omega(1/\sqrt{N})$ per-arm improvement (Theorem 4.3) even without uniqueness.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the fluid-approximation / LP-based policy line for finite-horizon RMABs (Brown & Smith [7], Gast et al. [10], Zhang & Frazier [36]) by replacing the first-order fluid model with a second-order Gaussian stochastic system that captures both mean and variance of arm dynamics. It departs from the classical diffusion-control literature (Harrison, Iglehart & Whitt) by fixing the noise covariance at the fluid-optimal solution rather than using state/action-dependent diffusion coefficients, which makes the resulting stochastic program tractable via EDDP/SDDP. The paper also formally establishes (Theorem 4.2) that the O(1/√N) gap of LP-based policies is tight for degenerate RMABs, a result that prior work had only conjectured empirically.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first result to break the O(1/√N) optimality-gap barrier for degenerate RMABs, which the authors show constitute a substantial fraction (~50% in half-sparse settings) of practically relevant instances. The Uniqueness Assumption 4.1 is required for the full O-tilde(1/N) guarantee, and without it only an Ω(1/√N) improvement over LP-based policies is established (Theorem 4.3), leaving open whether the full gap can be achieved under weaker conditions. Extending the approach to heterogeneous arms, multi-action settings, or infinite-horizon problems remains open, as does understanding whether the fixed-covariance simplification degrades in more complex state spaces.
