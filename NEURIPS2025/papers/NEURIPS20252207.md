## Problem

U-shaped segmentation networks produce logits at multiple decoder scales, yet standard training either supervises only the final output or applies deep supervision with identical loss weights at every scale, ignoring the complementary cues that emerge when coarse and fine predictions are fused. No prior method learns which scales to combine, which fusion operator to use, or how strongly each mixed prediction should guide training, leaving scale-specific information under-exploited—especially under label scarcity.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 2D medical images (CT at 224×224 for Synapse/ACDC, ultrasound at 256×256 for BUSI/ISIC2018, endoscopy at 352×352 for polyp datasets) and produces a pixel-wise segmentation mask. During training, a U-shaped network with L decoder stages yields L logit maps all upsampled to full resolution; the Combinatorial Mutation Module then synthesizes additional fused logits from every non-trivial subset of these maps. At inference, only the final decoder output is used, so the added training-time parameters impose zero overhead on FLOPs, latency, or memory.

## Method

LoMix introduces a Combinatorial Mutation Module that, for every subset S of decoder logits with |S|≥2, applies four differentiable fusion operators—element-wise addition, Hadard multiplication, channel-wise concatenation with a 1×1 convolution, and attention-weighted fusion—to generate a rich family of 'mutant' logit maps. Each original and mutant map is assigned a trainable scalar α, mapped through softplus to a strictly positive loss weight w, and the total training objective is the weighted sum of per-output segmentation losses (β·CE + γ·DICE) over all original and mutant logits. The α parameters are co-optimized with network weights via backpropagation, performing a one-step NAS-style search over scales, subsets, and operators without a separate optimizer or validation grid search. This design lets the model automatically suppress unhelpful fused outputs (driving their weights toward zero) while amplifying informative ones, yielding up to +13.5% DICE over single-output supervision across diverse backbones and datasets with no inference cost.

## Results

On Synapse 8-organ segmentation with the default PVT-EMCAD-B2 backbone, LoMix achieves 85.1% mean DICE, 14.9 HD95, and 76.4% mIoU, improving +4.2% DICE over single-output (last-layer) supervision, +2.2% over uniform deep supervision, and +1.5% over equal-weight MUTATION fusion. On ACDC cardiac MRI, PVT-EMCAD-B2 + LoMix reaches 92.51% mean DICE (RV 91.41%, Myo 89.96%, LV 96.15%), surpassing all prior methods including PVT-GCASCADE (91.95%) and Cascaded MERIT (91.85%). Under extreme data scarcity (5% of training scans, i.e., 1 scan), LoMix delivers a +9.23% DICE gain over last-layer supervision (46.45% vs. 37.22%), and the advantage remains above +9% at 10% data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LoMix consistently outperforms all three supervision baselines (last-layer, deep supervision, and MUTATION) across every backbone tested on Synapse 8-organ, with the largest absolute gains on small organs: gallbladder improves from 68.0% (LL) to 73.5% (LoMix) and pancreas from 63.1% to 69.7% on PVT-EMCAD-B2. Against the strongest prior method PVT-GCASCADE (83.28% DICE on Synapse 8-organ, 91.95% on ACDC), LoMix-enhanced PVT-EMCAD-B2 achieves 85.07% and 92.51% respectively, confirming superiority over both the MERIT mutation strategy and graph-based cascaded decoding. On ACDC, LoMix also surpasses TransCASCADE (91.63%), MISSFormer (90.86%), and Cascaded MERIT (91.85%), with the myocardium sub-organ showing the largest relative improvement (89.96% vs. 89.68% for PVT-EMCAD-B2 alone). The gains are most pronounced under data scarcity: at 5% training data LoMix yields +9.23% DICE over last-layer supervision, and at 10% the margin is +9.21%, whereas with full data the advantage narrows to +4.2%, indicating that learnable multi-scale fusion is most valuable when supervision is limited. LoMix also improves over the lightweight PVT-EMCAD-B0 (91.34% → 91.69% on ACDC) and UNeXt (70.5% → 76.8% on Synapse), demonstrating that the benefit is architecture-agnostic and not confined to large transformer backbones.

## Relation to Prior Work

LoMix extends the deep supervision paradigm introduced by UNet++ [36] and the mutation-based logit ensembling of MERIT [21] by replacing their fixed, equal-weight additive fusion with a NAS-inspired, differentiable search over four fusion operators (addition, multiplication, concatenation, attention-weighted fusion) and per-output softplus loss weights. Conceptually, it repurposes the architecture-search idea of learning which components to keep [18] and applies it not to network topology but to the supervision signal itself, treating each original and fused logit as a candidate 'cell' whose loss contribution is learned end-to-end. This departs from multi-task loss-balancing methods (uncertainty weighting [14], GradNorm [5]) that operate across distinct tasks rather than across scales of a single decoder.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The zero-inference-overhead, plug-and-play nature makes LoMix immediately adoptable by any U-shaped segmentation pipeline without architectural redesign, and the dramatic data-efficiency gain (+9.23% DICE at 5% labels) is practically significant for clinical settings where annotated scans are scarce. The learned softplus weights provide an interpretable diagnostic of which scales and fusion modes the model actually relies on, a transparency benefit absent in black-box ensembles. Key limitations include evaluation restricted to 2D medical imaging and a single default backbone (PVT-EMCAD-B2) for the limited-data experiments; whether the NAS-inspired weighting transfers to 3D volumetric segmentation or non-medical dense prediction tasks remains open.
