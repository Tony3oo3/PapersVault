## Problem

RSSM-based world models perform reliable single-step predictions but accumulate compounding errors over extended imagination horizons, making them unreliable for long-term planning. Existing long-horizon approaches (trajectory-based prediction, latent-space augmentation) still depend on statistical inference and lack explicit logical consistency, so they cannot guarantee coherent multi-step reasoning in complex control tasks. This paper asks whether integrating structured logical reasoning into a world model can produce imagination that remains logically consistent and stable over arbitrarily long horizons.

## Input / Output

The model consumes raw environment observations (state vectors of varying dimensionality, e.g., 1–39 dims across DMC and robotic tasks) and action sequences, encoding them into a deterministic recurrent state h_t, a stochastic latent z_t (30-dim), and separate logic vectors v (state) and m (action, 64-dim) via MLPs. Outputs include the full imagined latent trajectory {z_τ} over horizon H (default 30), predicted rewards, an optimal action sequence (via actor-critic policy or gradient-based MPC), and a scalar logical consistency score computed as the cosine-similarity-based Sim(φ_t^α, T) across the global logical chain L_T.

## Method

DMWM couples two subsystems inspired by dual-process cognition: an RSSM-S1 (DreamerV3-based) that performs fast, data-driven state prediction, and a LINN-S2 that encodes states and actions as logic vectors and applies neural modules for AND, OR, NOT, and IMPLY (the last realized as OR(NOT(v), m)) using Kronecker-product cross-space feature alignment to capture second-order state–action relationships. LINN-S2 performs hierarchical deep reasoning in three stages—local conjunction c_t = v_t ∧ m_t, recursive implication φ_t^α = c_{t-α}∧…∧c_t → z_{t+1} with tunable depth α, and a global chain L_T^α = φ_1^α ∧ … ∧ φ_{T-1}^α → T—while logic regularizers (14 rules covering negation, conjunction, disjunction, and implication axioms) enforce algebraic consistency. An inter-system feedback loop closes the loop: real state-action transitions from S1 supervise S2's inference loss, and S2 injects a logical consistency term C(φ) = Sim(φ, T) into a rederived variational ELBO that guides S1's stochastic prior during imagination. The combined system is trained end-to-end with actor-critic RL or gradient-based MPC for decision-making, and the logical ELBO replaces the standard prediction KL with a logic-inference expectation, ensuring imagined trajectories satisfy domain-specific logical rules at every step.

## Results

On 9 DMC tasks with horizon H=30, DMWM achieves the highest logical consistency across all environments (e.g., 0.730±0.037 on Pendulum Swingup, 0.730±0.042 on Reacher Hard), outperforming DreamerV3 by 14.3%, Hieros by 2.6%, and HRSSM by 3.3% in mean logic loss. Under limited environment trials, DMWM achieves a 5.5-fold improvement in test return over baselines; under limited data, it yields a 32% average improvement in test return. For extended horizons (H>30) on complex control tasks, DMWM delivers a 120% average improvement in test return compared to Dreamer and Grad-MPC.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DMWM consistently outperforms all five baselines across every evaluated metric. Against DreamerV3 (the primary RSSM baseline), DMWM achieves 14.3% higher logical consistency, 5.5× better trial efficiency, 32% better data efficiency, and 120% higher test return at extended horizons (H>30). Against the two RSSM variants—Hieros (hierarchical imagination) and HRSSM (masking-based robustness)—DMWM still leads by 2.6% and 3.3% in logical consistency respectively, demonstrating that hierarchical and noise-mitigation strategies alone cannot compensate for the lack of explicit logical reasoning. Against Dreamer-enabled Grad-MPC, DMWM-GD (its MPC variant) shows substantially more stable returns under limited trials and data, particularly in high-dimensional tasks like Cheetah Run and Quadruped Run. TD-MPC2 is included as a baseline in the experimental setup but the paper's quantitative comparisons focus primarily on DreamerV3, Hieros, HRSSM, and Grad-MPC.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the DreamerV3 RSSM architecture as its System 1 component and extends the LINN (Neural Logic Reasoning) framework by Shi et al. (2020) and Chen et al. (2021) into a System 2 component, introducing implication regularizers and a hierarchical recursive reasoning scheme not present in the original LINN. Conceptually, it departs from the dominant RSSM-only paradigm (Dreamer, Hieros, HRSSM) by arguing that statistical inference alone cannot sustain long-horizon imagination, and instead borrows the dual-process theory from cognitive science to pair fast intuitive prediction with slow logical reasoning. The inter-system feedback mechanism—where logical constraints from S2 reshape the ELBO of S1 and real observations from S1 update S2's domain logic—is the key architectural departure from prior work that treats world models and symbolic reasoning as separate pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper tackles the well-known error-accumulation problem in long-horizon world-model imagination and shows that embedding explicit logical structure (via LINN) can substantially reduce drift, yielding a 120% improvement in test return at H>30 over Dreamer and GD-MPC. However, the framework's reliance on predefined, domain-specific logical rules is a significant practical limitation: in environments where causal structure is ambiguous or evolving, the System 2 component may not generalize without manual rule specification. A key open question is whether the logical rules can be learned autonomously from data (e.g., via causal discovery), which would remove the current bottleneck and make the dual-mind paradigm broadly applicable to open-world settings.
