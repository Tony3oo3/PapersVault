## Problem

Diffusion models are trained on heavily filtered, high-quality subsets of available data, discarding large volumes of lower-quality, synthetic, or out-of-distribution images. Existing methods for learning from corrupted data require knowing the exact degradation model, which is unrealistic for heterogeneous real-world datasets. This paper asks whether one can extract useful signal from arbitrarily corrupted and out-of-distribution images without prior knowledge of the corruption, while preserving both quality and diversity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251274/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a heterogeneous dataset mixing high-quality images (from p₀) with corrupted or out-of-distribution images (from q₀), along with class labels or text prompts as conditioning. Each sample is assigned a per-sample minimum usable noise level (t_i^min) via a trained classifier, and optionally a maximum usable noise level (t_i^max) for the crops-based low-noise component. The output is a diffusion model that generates images from the high-quality target distribution, trained with a modified loss that modulates how each sample participates across the diffusion time schedule.

## Method

Ambient Diffusion Omni (Ambient-o) exploits two properties of natural images—spectral power-law decay and locality—to use all available data. In the high-noise regime, a time-conditional classifier determines the minimum noise level at which corrupted and clean distributions become statistically indistinguishable; above this threshold, corrupted samples are incorporated via an extended Ambient Diffusion loss that re-weights the denoising target to account for the extra annotation noise. In the low-noise regime, a crops classifier identifies the largest patch size at which out-of-distribution images are locally indistinguishable from in-distribution ones, enabling OOD data to contribute to fine-grained detail learning. Theoretically, the approach is justified by a bias-variance analysis showing that Gaussian noise contracts total-variation distances between distributions (Theorem 4.2), so at sufficiently high noise the bias from using corrupted data is dominated by the variance reduction from the larger effective sample size.

## Results

On ImageNet-512, Ambient-o-XXL+crops achieves a test FID of 2.53 (with CFG) and FIDv2 of 45.78, setting a new state-of-the-art over the EDM-2-XXL baseline (2.73 / 46.22). In controlled CIFAR-10 experiments with 90% Gaussian-blurred data (σ_B=0.6), Ambient-o reduces FID from 8.79 (only clean 10%) to 5.34, while naively using all data yields 11.42. For text-to-image generation, Ambient-o improves COCO FID from 12.37 to 10.61 and GenEval overall score from 0.44 to 0.47, while also increasing DINO Vendi Diversity by >13% compared to a fine-tuning-on-high-quality-subset baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against EDM-2 [36] on ImageNet-512, Ambient-o-XXL+crops achieves a lower test FID of 2.53 vs. 2.73 (w/ CFG) and a lower FIDv2 of 45.78 vs. 46.22, while using the identical architecture, hyperparameters, and optimization schedule. The improvement is more pronounced on test FID than train FID, indicating reduced memorization due to the noise added during annotation. At the smaller XS scale, Ambient-o also edges out EDM2-XS (test FID 3.58 vs. 3.68). Against the MicroDiffusion baseline [54] for text-to-image generation, Ambient-o improves COCO FID from 12.37 to 10.61 and Clip-FD from 10.07 to 9.40, while also improving GenEval overall from 0.44 to 0.47 (notably on two-object composition: 0.40 vs. 0.33). Compared to a fine-tuning-on-high-quality-subset strategy, Ambient-o achieves slightly worse COCO FID (10.61 vs. 10.27) but substantially greater diversity (>13% higher DINO Vendi Diversity), confirming that filtering sacrifices diversity while Ambient-o preserves it.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Ambient Diffusion line of work (Daras et al., NeurIPS 2023; Daras et al., 2024), which required knowing the exact analytical form of the degradation (e.g., Gaussian noise, linear measurements), to the setting of arbitrary, unknown corruptions and out-of-distribution data. The key conceptual departure is reducing the arbitrary-corruption problem to additive Gaussian noise via a time-conditional classifier that identifies when noised corrupted and clean distributions become indistinguishable, thereby eliminating the need for a degradation model. The paper also introduces a second mechanism—exploiting the locality of natural images at low diffusion times—that has no direct precedent in the ambient diffusion literature and enables the use of entirely out-of-distribution or synthetic data for fine-grained detail learning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

At a time when data curation is a major bottleneck and cost center for training generative models, this work provides a principled, theoretically grounded method to extract signal from data that would otherwise be discarded, without sacrificing diversity. The bias-variance analysis (Theorem 4.2) gives a clear criterion for when biased data helps, and the empirical results span controlled corruptions, real ImageNet heterogeneity, and synthetic text-to-image data. Key limitations include reduced effectiveness for low-frequency corruptions (masking, color shifts), the 'donut paradox' where intermediate diffusion times have fewer usable samples, and the overhead of training annotation classifiers. Open questions include patch-level heterogeneity, matching lower bounds, and whether the approach generalizes to non-image modalities.
