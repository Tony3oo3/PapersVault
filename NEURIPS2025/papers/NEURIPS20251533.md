## Problem

Existing time series benchmarks assume clean, regularly sampled, unimodal data, creating a significant gap with real-world deployments where observations arrive at irregular intervals, span multiple asynchronous modalities, and exhibit pervasive missingness. No prior benchmark systematically categorizes the *causes* of irregularity (trigger-based, constraint-based, artifact-based) or provides a framework for integrating asynchronous textual context into irregular forecasting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251533/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes variable-length, unevenly-spaced numerical observations across N variables (each with its own timestamp sequence), a set of asynchronous time-stamped text snippets with no assumed alignment to the numerical timestamps, and explicit future query timestamps. Inputs are converted to fixed-length tensors via a canonical pre-alignment step that pads to a global temporal grid, appends binary observation masks and normalized timestamps as extra feature channels, and appends query rows with zero values and mask=0. The output is a forecast vector of predicted values at each query timestamp for each variable.

## Method

IMM-TSF is a modular benchmark library that composes three swappable components: a numerical encoder (regular models adapted via pre-alignment, or native irregular models like t-PatchGNN, CRU, Latent-ODE), a frozen text encoder (GPT-2, BERT, Llama-3.1-8B, or DeepSeek-7B), and two fusion modules. The Timestamp-to-Text Fusion (TTF) module aligns asynchronous text to forecast queries via either Gaussian recency-weighted averaging or Time2Vec-augmented cross-attention. The Multimodality Fusion (MMF) module then integrates text-derived representations into the numerical forecast via either GRU-gated residual addition (learnable gating suppresses noisy text) or cross-attention addition (convex combination with a fixed mixing weight). The key empirical finding is that explicitly modeling multimodality on irregular time series yields an average 6.71% MSE reduction (up to 38.38% on datasets with highly informative text), and that GR-Add consistently outperforms XAttn-Add while TTF variants perform comparably.

## Results

Across all nine TIME-IMM datasets and eleven baselines, multimodal variants (incorporating asynchronous text) consistently outperform unimodal counterparts, achieving an average MSE reduction of 6.71% and up to 38.38% in datasets with highly informative textual signals (e.g., ClusterTrace). Among irregular time series models, t-PatchGNN achieves the strongest unimodal and multimodal forecasting performance, validating its status as state-of-the-art for irregular TSF. In the multimodal setting, lightweight and few-shot-capable models (DLinear, TimeLLM, TTM) remain competitive, suggesting that smaller datasets favor simpler architectures with strong inductive biases.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates 11 baselines spanning three categories: regular TSF models (Informer, DLinear, PatchTST, TimesNet, TimeMixer), large TSF models (TimeLLM, TTM), and irregular TSF models (CRU, Latent-ODE, Neural Flow, t-PatchGNN). t-PatchGNN [78] consistently achieves the best unimodal and multimodal MSE across datasets, outperforming both regular and other irregular baselines, confirming its superiority for irregular forecasting. In the multimodal setting, all models show MSE improvements over their unimodal counterparts in nearly every case, with the largest gains (up to 38.38%) observed on ClusterTrace where workload annotations are directly predictive of future resource usage. Notably, DLinear and TimeLLM/TTM remain competitive in the multimodal setting despite being designed for regular or large-scale data, likely because the smaller multimodal datasets favor simpler, few-shot-capable architectures. Among the irregular baselines, CRU and Latent-ODE/Neural Flow are outperformed by t-PatchGNN, while regular models like TimesNet and Informer show modest multimodal gains (e.g., Informer MSE drops from 1.0448 to 1.0295 on GDELT). The paper also finds that the choice of LLM backbone (GPT-2, BERT, Llama-3.1-8B, DeepSeek-7B) has negligible impact on forecasting performance, and that GRU-gated residual addition (GR-Add) outperforms cross-attention addition (XAttn-Add) for multimodality fusion.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the irregular time series forecasting line (t-PatchGNN, CRU, Latent-ODE, Neural Flow) by adopting t-PatchGNN's canonical pre-alignment strategy to adapt regular architectures to irregular data without imputation. It departs from prior multimodal time series work (notably Time-MMD) in two key ways: it preserves asynchronous timestamps between numerical and textual modalities rather than enforcing alignment, and it introduces a cause-driven taxonomy of irregularity (trigger-based, constraint-based, artifact-based) that prior datasets treat as an unstructured nuisance. The IMM-TSF library's fusion modules (TTF and MMF) are novel architectural contributions that bridge the gap between irregular numerical sequences and asynchronous text, extending the unimodal irregular TSF formulation to a multimodal setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TIME-IMM and IMM-TSF fill a concrete gap: no prior benchmark simultaneously captures cause-driven irregularity, multimodality, and asynchronous temporal structure, which are all pervasive in real-world deployments (healthcare, finance, environmental sensing). The finding that multimodal integration yields up to 38.38% MSE reduction under irregularity validates the practical value of textual context, while the observation that LLM backbone choice (GPT-2 through Llama-3.1-8B) has negligible impact suggests the bottleneck is temporal alignment rather than semantic capacity. Key open questions include whether compositional irregularities (multiple causes simultaneously) are tractable, whether the benchmark extends beyond forecasting to anomaly detection or classification, and whether the frozen-LLM design underestimates the benefit of domain-adaptive fine-tuning.
