## Problem

Discrete diffusion models struggle with the efficiency-accuracy trade-off when reducing sampling steps: existing predictor-corrector schemes rely on 'uninformed' correctors that fix errors by randomly re-masking tokens, which is both inefficient and can introduce new errors. The paper identifies this failure mode concretely in masked (absorbing-state) diffusion and asks whether the model's own denoising predictions can be leveraged to target corrections at the most error-prone dimensions.

## Input / Output

For image generation, the model takes a class label as input and produces a 256×256 tokenized image (VQ-VAE codebook tokens) in 17 total steps (8 predictor + 8 corrector + 1 final denoising). For text, it generates character-level sequences of length 256 unconditionally. Internally, the model operates on sequences where each position holds either a vocabulary token or a MASK token; the hollow transformer constraint requires that the output logit at position d is computed without seeing the input at position d, enabling a single forward pass to yield all per-position conditional distributions simultaneously.

## Method

The core contribution is an informed corrector that replaces random re-masking with an adaptive random-scan Gibbs sampler: it computes a margin-based confidence score (difference between top-1 and top-2 log-likelihoods) for each non-masked position, then uses the Gumbel-top-k trick to select the k most uncertain dimensions for parallel resampling from the denoising conditional. To make this efficient, the paper introduces a hollow transformer architecture with offset causal attention streams (forward, backward, and a mixing query stream) that guarantees the output at position d is agnostic to the input at d, so one network call yields all needed conditionals. Training uses a new combined ELBO objective (HollowDiff loss) that averages the standard MD4 masked-position loss with a complementary non-masked-position loss, reducing gradient variance. The corrector can be trained jointly with the predictor (HollowDiff) or as a separate module applied on top of a stronger pretrained predictor (MD4 + informed corrector), the latter achieving FID 5.78 on ImageNet 256×256 with only 17 steps.

## Results

On tokenized ImageNet 256×256, the MD4 + informed corrector hybrid achieves an FID of 5.78 with only 17 total steps (8 predictor, 8 corrector, 1 final denoising), outperforming all baselines with comparable or fewer steps. The HollowDiff + informed corrector variant (single 230M model) reaches FID 6.26 under the same 17-step budget. On Text8, the informed corrector substantially reduces word error rate compared to MD4, MD4 with the uninformed corrector, and MD4 with the ReMDM sampler. In the synthetic Markov chain experiment, the informed corrector yields the lowest error rate across all NFE settings, while the uninformed forward-backward corrector performs worse than no corrector at all.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MaskGIT (227M, 18 steps, FID 6.56), both informed corrector variants achieve lower FID (6.26 and 5.78) with fewer steps. Against DPC (391M, 180 steps, FID 4.45), the informed corrector uses roughly 10× fewer sampling steps while achieving a competitive FID of 5.78, making it far more efficient in practice. Against VQ-Diffusion (518M, 100 steps, FID 11.89), the informed corrector wins decisively on both FID and step count. Against ReMDM (227M, 16 steps, FID 7.40), the MD4 + informed corrector improves FID by 1.62 points with one additional step. Against the MD4 baseline (230M, 17 steps, FID 6.28), the informed corrector provides a modest but consistent improvement (5.78 for the hybrid, 6.26 for HollowDiff), while the uninformed corrector actually degrades performance to FID 7.41, confirming that naive forward-backward correction is counterproductive in the masked diffusion setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the predictor-corrector sampling framework for discrete diffusion introduced by Campbell et al. [8] and the simplified masked diffusion (MD4) formulation of Shi et al. [12]. It identifies a specific failure mode of the forward-backward 'uninformed' corrector in absorbing-state diffusion—random re-masking that cannot target erroneous tokens—and replaces it with an adaptive Gibbs-sampling-based corrector that uses the model's own confidence scores to prioritize updates. The hollow transformer architecture, building on Sun et al. [33], is repurposed to enable a single forward pass to evaluate the denoising conditional at multiple positions simultaneously, which in turn unlocks a complementary ELBO term (L_Mbar) that, averaged with MD4's L_M, yields a lower-variance training objective (HollowDiff).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The informed corrector is modular: it can be trained separately and applied on top of a stronger pretrained predictor (MD4 + informed corrector achieves FID 5.78, beating both components individually), making it broadly applicable to existing discrete diffusion models without joint retraining. A key limitation is the architectural dependency on the hollow transformer, which prevents direct application to arbitrary pretrained models and introduces a denoising-quality tradeoff (HollowDiff alone scores FID 10.24 vs. MD4's 6.28). Open questions include whether the corrector can be decoupled from the hollow transformer constraint, whether the approach extends to non-absorbing forward processes, and whether the confidence-based selection strategy generalizes to longer sequences or higher-dimensional discrete spaces.
