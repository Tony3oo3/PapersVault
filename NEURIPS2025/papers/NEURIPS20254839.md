## Problem

Standard GP-based Bayesian Optimization suffers from one-step myopia, prioritizing immediate predicted gains over thorough exploration and converging prematurely to local optima in high-dimensional or multi-modal landscapes. Existing multi-step look-ahead methods (2-step EI, Knowledge Gradient, EARL-BO) mitigate this partially but incur high computational overhead and still lack explicit global structural signals, leaving a gap for a unified framework that combines global exploration cues with adaptive multi-step planning under strict evaluation budgets.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a continuous black-box objective f(x) over x ∈ X ⊂ R^d (tested from 2D Branin to 200D HDBO), an initial dataset D_0 of size n_0, search-space bounds, and a fixed evaluation budget T (30–100 iterations). At each iteration it outputs a single query point x_t ∈ R^d and its function value, accumulating a trajectory of (x, f(x)) pairs; the final output is the best point x* in terms of f(x*) and a Landscape-Aware Regret (LAR) score that jointly penalizes local suboptimality and missed global basins via an energy-informed term. All inputs are continuous vectors; no gradients or analytical forms of f are required.

## Method

REBMBO tightly couples three modules that co-evolve at every iteration. Module A maintains a GP surrogate (Classic O(n³), Sparse O(nm²), or Deep Kernel variant) with a learned RBF+Matérn-5/2 mixture kernel to capture both smooth global trends and rough local variations, yielding posterior mean μ_{f,t}(x) and std σ_{f,t}(x). Module B trains a neural-network energy function E_θ(x) via short-run MCMC-based maximum likelihood (positive/negative phase alternation with SGLD), producing a global energy landscape that identifies promising basins; this is embedded into a novel EBM-UCB acquisition α(x) = μ_{f,t}(x) + βσ_{f,t}(x) − γE_θ(x). Module C formulates each BO iteration as an MDP with state s_t = (μ, σ, E_θ), action a_t = proposed query point, and reward r_t = f(a_t) − λE_θ(a_t), then optimizes a stochastic policy π_φ via PPO's clipped surrogate objective to perform adaptive multi-step lookahead. The key architectural insight is that the RL policy is not a standalone planner but co-evolves with the up-to-date GP posterior and EBM distribution, so the multi-step horizon dynamically adjusts exploration depth and direction rather than applying a fixed rollout depth, and the LAR metric (R_t^LAR = [f(x*)−f(x_t)] + α[E_θ(x*)−E_θ(x_t)]) provides a theoretically grounded evaluation that preserves GP-UCB-type sublinear regret guarantees under mild regularity assumptions.

## Results

On synthetic benchmarks (Table 1), REBMBO-C achieves the highest mean Landscape-Aware Regret (LAR) score of 89.40±1.24 across Branin-2D, Ackley-5D, Rosenbrock-8D, and HDBO-200D, followed by REBMBO-D (89.17±1.41) and REBMBO-S (87.98±1.25). On real-world benchmarks (Table 2), REBMBO-D leads with a mean score of 86.98±2.80 across Nanophotonic-3D, Rosetta-86D, NATS-Bench-20D, and Robot Trajectory-40D. The strongest gains appear in high-dimensional settings: on HDBO-200D at T=100, REBMBO-D scores 94.42±3.98 versus 85.17±2.96 for KG and 80.69±3.14 for TuRBO, a roughly 10–14 point improvement.

## Baselines & Comparisons

REBMBO variants consistently outperform all five baselines across both synthetic and real-world benchmarks. TuRBO [5] (local trust-region GP) is the weakest baseline, scoring 78.56±1.39 mean on synthetic tasks and 78.81±2.99 on real-world tasks, trailing REBMBO-C by roughly 11 points. EARL-BO [9] (single-step RL with GP) improves over TuRBO (81.57 vs. 78.56 on synthetic) but still lags REBMBO-C by ~8 points, confirming that single-step RL without global EBM signals is insufficient. BALLET-ICI [6] (global+local GP with level-set estimation) reaches 83.80±1.45 on synthetic benchmarks, outperforming TuRBO and EARL-BO but remaining ~5.6 points below REBMBO-C. Among the look-ahead methods, KG [8] (87.52±1.67) and Two-Step EI [7] (86.15±1.65) are the strongest baselines, yet both are surpassed by all three REBMBO variants; the gap widens notably on HDBO-200D where REBMBO-D (94.42) exceeds KG (85.17) by over 9 points. On real-world tasks (Table 2), REBMBO-D leads with 86.98±2.80 mean, beating the best baseline KG (84.39±2.74) by ~2.6 points and TuRBO (78.81±2.99) by over 8 points, with the largest margins on Rosetta-86D and Robot Trajectory-40D.

## Relation to Prior Work

REBMBO repurposes three established components—Gaussian Process surrogates (standard in BO), Energy-Based Models trained via short-run MCMC (from generative modeling), and PPO-based policy optimization (from RL)—into a unified black-box optimization framework, rather than building on a single prior BO method. It departs from single-step acquisition approaches (Classic BO, TuRBO, BALLET-ICI) and short-horizon lookahead methods (2-step EI, KG) by introducing an EBM-UCB acquisition that injects global basin information, and from RL-based BO (EARL-BO) by coupling the RL policy with an explicit energy landscape rather than relying solely on local GP posteriors. The Landscape-Aware Regret (LAR) metric is a new theoretical construct that extends standard regret with an energy-informed global term, providing a principled evaluation criterion absent from prior BO literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses the well-known one-step myopia problem in GP-based BO by showing that a short-run MCMC-trained EBM can provide global structural cues that complement local GP uncertainty, and that PPO-based multi-step planning can adaptively balance exploration depth without the heavy rollout costs of 2-step EI or KG. Practical limitations include a 2–2.5× per-iteration computational overhead relative to TuRBO, sensitivity to EBM convergence quality (though degradation is graceful), and the need for careful scale alignment between f(x) and E_θ(x). Open questions raised by the authors include extending to asynchronous evaluations, distributed RL settings, and large-scale hyperparameter tuning, while the moderate but consistent gains across 2D–200D benchmarks suggest the approach is most impactful in high-dimensional, multi-modal regimes where single-step methods struggle most.
