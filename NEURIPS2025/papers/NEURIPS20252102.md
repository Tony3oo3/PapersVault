## Problem

LM misbehaviors such as jailbreak compliance, instruction-following failures, and low-confidence hallucinations are typically detected only after the full output has been generated, incurring the full inference cost of every token. This paper asks whether the internal representations of input tokens alone—before a single output token is produced—contain sufficient signal to predict these eventual behaviors, and whether such predictions can be made with provable reliability guarantees.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252102/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The probe consumes the hidden state of the final input token at a chosen transformer layer (e.g., the middle layer of Llama3.1-8B) as a fixed-dimensional vector, requiring no output tokens to be generated. The output is a class probability vector over the target behavior (e.g., 'will fail format' vs. 'will comply', or a multi-class label for text classification), which is then passed through a conformal threshold: if the max probability exceeds the calibrated quantile, a prediction is emitted; otherwise the system defers to the LM. This selective-prediction structure means the system either produces a high-confidence behavior estimate or explicitly abstains, rather than always outputting a label.

## Method

The approach trains a linear classifier (probe) on hidden states of input tokens to predict a binary or multi-class label describing the eventual output behavior, then calibrates the probe using conformal prediction on a held-out validation set to obtain a threshold with a provable miscoverage bound. At inference, the probe's prediction is acted upon only when its confidence exceeds this threshold, otherwise the system defers to the LM's full generation. The key insight is that simple linear probes on a single input-token hidden state can read out information about behaviors that manifest many tokens later (format compliance, jailbreak susceptibility, final CoT class), and that conformal calibration converts these noisy probes into precise early-warning signals with user-controllable precision-coverage tradeoffs. The method is data-efficient (often <500 training instances suffice), scales favorably with LM size, and generalizes to out-of-distribution datasets, enabling applications ranging from preemptive safety interception (91% jailbreak reduction) to 65% average inference cost reduction in CoT text classification.

## Results

On the safety alignment task (Table 1), conformal probes (α=0.9) achieve 92–98% estimation consistency, 90–94.5% precision, and 91–94.5% recall across SelfAware, KnownUnknown, and WildJailbreak, outperforming a fine-tuned BERT baseline on all three metrics. On WildJailbreak specifically, the conformal probe reduces the jailbreak success rate from 30% to 2.7% (a 91% reduction), whereas the BERT baseline leaves it at 20%. For CoT-accelerated text classification across 27 datasets, the method achieves a 65% average inference cost reduction with a near-zero average accuracy loss (−0.46%, i.e., accuracy slightly improves), and a worst-case loss of only 1.34%. Out-of-distribution generalization on OpenBookQA yields a 68% cost reduction with just 0.4% accuracy loss.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is a fine-tuned bert-large-uncased (340M parameters) trained to predict output behavior from input text alone, compared against the linear probes that use only ~0.0025% as many trainable parameters. On the format-following task (Figure 2), probes outperform BERT in all but one of six dataset/format settings, and conformal calibration further lifts consistency from ~70% to ~89% at α=0.9. On safety alignment (Table 1), probes dominate BERT on consistency and precision across all three datasets; on WildJailbreak the gap is stark (94.0% vs 83.4% consistency; 90.0% vs 73.8% precision; 91.0% vs 33.3% recall), translating to a 2.7% vs 20% residual jailbreak rate. BERT does retain an edge on recall for KnownUnknown (93.1% vs 72.8%), likely because it can exploit surface-level text cues that the probe's single hidden-state vector misses. On the confidence-estimation task (Figure 3), probes again outperform BERT, though both methods struggle more than on format or safety tasks, and conformal coverage drops accordingly.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the hidden-state probing paradigm (Alain & Bengio, 2017; Azaria & Mitchell, 2023) by restricting probes to input-token representations only, enabling prediction of eventual output behavior before any token is generated—whereas prior probing work requires access to generated token states. It combines this with conformal prediction (Shafer & Vovk, 2008) to provide provable error bounds, a framework previously applied to LM output quality (Quach et al., 2024) or text classification (Kumar et al., 2023) but not to hidden-state probes. The paper also generalizes the early-exiting literature (DeeBERT, BERxiT, Confident Adaptive Transformers), which typically predicts the next token or a single classification label, to predicting diverse 'behaviors' such as safety alignment failures, format compliance, and model confidence that cannot be inferred from the output token sequence alone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: a 91% reduction in jailbreak success rate and 65% average inference cost reduction on CoT classification with negligible accuracy loss, achieved with a simple linear probe requiring fewer than 500 training examples. The conceptual contribution—that input-token hidden states encode predictive information about behaviors manifesting many tokens later—opens new avenues for interpretability research into what information is 'locked in' at the start of generation. Key limitations include degraded performance on longer outputs and tasks requiring knowledge external to the model, and the method has only been validated up to 70B parameters, leaving open whether it scales to frontier-scale models. The OOD generalization results (e.g., 68% cost reduction on OpenBookQA trained only on other MCQA datasets) suggest broad applicability, but the negative correlation between probe consistency and output length remains an unresolved challenge for long-form generation settings.
