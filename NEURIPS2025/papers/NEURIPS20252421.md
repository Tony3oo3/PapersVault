## Problem

Context-parallel (CP) training of long-context language models requires broadcasting full key and value activation matrices across all devices at every layer and step, incurring O(nd) communication per device. While feasible on datacenter-grade 100Gbps interconnects, this all-to-all communication is prohibitively expensive over commodity internet links (~300Mbps), making decentralized CP training entirely unexplored and impractical for context windows exceeding 100K tokens.

## Input / Output

Each device receives a local chunk X_i ∈ R^{n_i × d} of a tokenized text sequence (total context n up to 132K tokens) along with attention weight matrices W_k, W_v ∈ R^{d×d}, a warm-started orthonormal basis Ū ∈ R^{d×r} (r ≪ d, e.g., r chosen for 96.5% compression), and a small linear head ψ: R^d → R^k. The method outputs compressed activations Z_comp ∈ R^{n_i × r} plus k rotation scalars θ (often k=1) for broadcast, and after training the auxiliary projection components can be removed, yielding a standard transformer with zero extra parameters at inference.

## Method

The core insight is that attention activations (Q, K, V) in pretrained transformers exhibit pronounced low-rank structure (stable rank ~0.1–0.5% of full dimension), enabling near-lossless compression via subspace projection. The paper factorizes attention weights as W = BUU^T and jointly optimizes B in Euclidean space and U on the Stiefel manifold St(d,r) via Riemannian gradient descent, proving Q-linear convergence under a PL condition. To avoid expensive QR/SVD retraction steps, U is reparameterized as U(θ) = R(θ)Ū where R(θ) = exp(Σ θ_l A_l) draws from a k-dimensional Lie subgroup of O(d) defined by fixed skew-symmetric generators A_l, with a second-order Taylor approximation reducing rotation computation from O(d³) to O(d²). A lightweight linear head predicts per-chunk rotation parameters θ from the mean attention output, enabling data-adaptive subspaces without increasing communication. A short warm-up phase (<500 steps) establishes Ū via PCA on local attention weights, and after convergence the projection heads and basis matrices can be detached, reverting the model to a vanilla transformer compatible with standard inference pipelines.

## Results

On an 800M-parameter model trained to the compute-optimal point (16B tokens) with a 132K context window across 8 A100 GPUs, the compressed decentralized method at 300Mbps achieves validation perplexities of 17.06 (FineWeb), 17.47 (C4), and 17.81 (BookCorpus), slightly outperforming the centralized 100Gbps baseline (17.18, 17.51, 17.88 respectively). Throughput reaches 55K tokens/sec—a 20× speedup over the uncompressed decentralized baseline (2.7K TPS)—while adding only 0.7% memory overhead (38.7 vs. 38.4 GB/GPU). The method achieves over 96.5% communication compression (K compressed 98%, V compressed 95%) with no measurable loss in convergence quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is against uncompressed context-parallel training: vanilla CP over 300Mbps links is over 20× slower in wall-clock time and infeasible to train to completion (>150 days estimated), whereas the compressed method matches the centralized 100Gbps baseline in both perplexity and throughput. Against two constructed compression baselines—Top-10% sparsification (90% compression) and 4-bit quantization (75% compression)—the proposed method outperforms both at a more aggressive 96.5% compression rate, with the compressed CP curve nearly indistinguishable from the uncompressed reference in Figure 5 (left). Against long-context architectures BigBird and CosFormer (limited to 32K tokens on A100), the method shows significantly better convergence when all models are evaluated at 32K tokens across 4 GPUs (Figure 5, right). The method also scales to 3B parameters (32 layers) with pipeline parallelism across 32 A100s, yielding substantial throughput gains over uncompressed decentralized CP and nearly matching centralized CP performance (Figure 3).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the context-parallel training framework (Ring Attention, blockwise parallel transformers) by introducing the first communication-compression scheme tailored to CP in decentralized, low-bandwidth settings. Prior bandwidth-efficiency work (gradient quantization, sparsification, delayed sync) has been confined to distributed data parallelism, where each node holds a full model replica; CP requires global key/value exchange at every layer and step, making those techniques inapplicable. The mathematical machinery draws on Stiefel-manifold optimization and Lie-group parameterizations (Edelman et al., 1998; Hall, 2015), repurposing Riemannian gradient descent and exponential-map reparameterizations to learn data-dependent subspace rotations. The observation that attention activations are low-rank connects to recent work on rank diminishment in deep networks (Feng et al., 2022) and structured low-rank transformers (Sanyal et al., 2024), but this paper is the first to exploit that structure for inter-device communication compression rather than intra-device computation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work removes a critical bandwidth bottleneck that has prevented decentralized training from scaling to the 100K+ token context windows now standard in frontier LLMs, enabling commodity-internet-connected GPUs to match datacenter-grade convergence. The 'unplugging' property—where compression components can be detached post-training to yield a vanilla transformer—is practically significant for deployment compatibility. Open questions include why a single rotation angle (k=1) suffices despite the heavily reduced search space, whether alternative reparameterizations beyond subspace rotations unlock further gains, and whether the approach generalizes beyond transformer attention to other global-communication patterns. The method is validated only up to 3B parameters and on three text corpora, leaving open its behavior at frontier scale and on multimodal or non-English data.
