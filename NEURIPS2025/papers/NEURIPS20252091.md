## Problem

Existing coordinate denoising methods for 3D molecular pretraining assume atomic motions are both isotropic (identical variance along all spatial axes) and homoscedastic (uniform noise scale across atoms), oversimplifying the directional stiffness of bonds and the structural variability in energy potentials. No prior approach simultaneously lifts both assumptions by learning a full, structure-aware covariance matrix per atom, leaving a gap between the noise model and the true anisotropic, heteroscedastic nature of molecular dynamics.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a 3D molecular structure (atomic numbers, 3D coordinates, and bond existence/type) and produces per-atom 3×3 full covariance matrices that parameterize anisotropic Gaussian noise distributions. Perturbed coordinates are sampled via Cholesky decomposition of these covariances, and the denoising autoencoder outputs atom-wise vectors (noise scaled by inverse covariance) that correspond to force field predictions. The framework is model-agnostic and integrates with equivariant backbones such as EquiformerV2 or Geoformer, supporting both equilibrium and non-equilibrium structures (the latter requiring force encoding as additional input features).

## Method

AniDS introduces a structure-aware anisotropic noise generator that constructs each atom's covariance as an isotropic base term a_i·I minus a sum of subtractive anisotropic corrections derived from pairwise atomic interactions: each correction is the outer product of the normalized inter-atomic direction vector, weighted by a softmax-normalized coefficient γ_ij learned from structural representations and radial basis functions. This parameterization guarantees symmetry, positive semi-definiteness (enforced by the softmax constraint Σ_j γ_ij < 1), and SO(3)-equivariance by construction. The denoising autoencoder is trained with a loss that weights the noise prediction by the inverse covariance, which the paper proves approximates score matching under a Boltzmann-distribution Gaussian mixture, thereby recovering the molecular force field. A KL divergence against an isotropic Gaussian prior and a one-sided hinge penalty on the total anisotropic mass prevent degenerate or overly isotropic solutions, and the framework supports both pretrain-then-finetune and auxiliary-loss training paradigms.

## Results

On MD17, AniDS (Lmax=2) achieves an average force MAE of 0.072 kcal/mol across 8 molecules, representing an 8.9% relative improvement over the strongest prior denoising baseline DeNS (Lmax=3) at 0.079. On OC22 S2EF-Total, AniDS achieves a force F-MAE of 22.4 meV/Å (average of ID and OOD), a 6.2% improvement over EquiformerV2+DeNS (23.89), and an energy E-MAE of 447.7 meV (3.2% improvement). Notably, AniDS with Lmax=2 outperforms DeNS with Lmax=3, demonstrating that structure-aware anisotropic noise is more effective than simply increasing representation dimensionality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AniDS achieves the best force MAE on all 8 MD17 molecules, with the largest gains on Aspirin (+15.0% over DeNS Lmax=3) and Salicylic Acid (+13.5%), and the smallest on Benzene (+1.4%). On OC22, AniDS outperforms EquiformerV2+DeNS by 5.5% on in-distribution force MAE (19.53 vs. 20.66 meV/Å) and 6.8% on out-of-distribution (25.27 vs. 27.11), while also improving energy prediction by 5.5% (ID) and 1.4% (OOD). Compared to non-denoising baselines, AniDS surpasses EquiformerV2 alone by 13.4% in average force MAE on OC22 (22.4 vs. 26.79 meV/Å) and by 72% on MD17 (0.072 vs. 0.258 implied from Table 1 context). The ablation confirms that anisotropic noise (AniDS, AniDS-add) consistently outperforms isotropic alternatives (DeNS, DenoiseVAE) under both pretrain-finetune and auxiliary fine-tuning settings, with the subtractive correction in AniDS yielding slightly better results than the additive variant. AniDS (Lmax=2) outperforms DeNS (Lmax=3) despite using lower-dimensional irreducible representations, indicating that the quality of the noise distribution matters more than representation capacity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the coordinate denoising paradigm for molecular force field learning (Zaidi et al., 2022) by simultaneously lifting two simplifying assumptions that prior methods retained: isotropy (identical variance along all spatial axes) and homoscedasticity (uniform noise scale across atoms). DenoiseVAE (Liu et al., ICLR 2025) addressed homoscedasticity with per-atom scalar variances, while Frad and Slide introduced handcrafted non-Gaussian perturbations at fixed scales; AniDS unifies both relaxations by generating full 3×3 covariance matrices per atom, with anisotropic corrections derived from learned pairwise atomic interactions. The theoretical grounding follows the score-matching connection to the Boltzmann distribution established in prior denoising work, now extended to the anisotropic Gaussian mixture setting, and the partially corrupted training strategy with force encoding is inherited from DeNS (Liao et al., 2024).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 8.9% and 6.2% average force-prediction improvements on MD17 and OC22, respectively, demonstrate that the noise distribution's structure is a first-order factor in force field learning quality, not merely a training detail. The case study showing learned covariances suppress noise along stiff bond directions while allowing flexibility in soft modes provides physical interpretability that prior isotropic methods cannot offer. Open questions include whether the anisotropic noise generator scales to large biomolecular systems (proteins, RNAs) and whether the full-covariance parameterization remains computationally tractable for systems with thousands of atoms. The absence of error bars (single-run experiments due to computational cost) limits confidence in the exact magnitude of gains, though the consistent direction across all MD17 molecules and both OC22 splits is reassuring.
