## Problem

While mechanistic interpretability has identified specific circuits and probing studies have tracked information flow across transformer layers, the geometric evolution of hidden representations during decision-making remains unexplored. Specifically, how the intrinsic dimension (ID) of intermediate representations changes as a model transitions from contextual encoding to committing to a discrete prediction has not been systematically studied, leaving open whether geometric markers like ID peaks can serve as interpretable indicators of decisiveness.

## Input / Output

The method consumes MCQA-style text prompts (a query, a shuffled choice set of two or more options, and a prompt template) fed into 28 open-weight decoder-only transformers, extracting per-layer hidden representations at the last token position from two locations within each transformer block: the MLP output (the delta written to the residual stream) and the residual post-activation (the accumulated state after the write). For each layer, it produces (1) intrinsic dimension estimates computed via three estimators (MLE, TwoNN, GRIDE) over the set of last-token representations across the dataset, and (2) per-layer logit accuracy obtained by directly projecting the residual post representation through the unembedding matrix (Logit Lens), yielding a coupled ID-accuracy profile across the full layer stack.

## Method

The paper performs a large-scale empirical analysis of representational geometry by tracking intrinsic dimension across all transformer layers for 28 open-weight models (LLaMA, GPT-2, Mistral, Phi, Gemma, Pythia families) on both real-world MCQA benchmarks (MMLU, COPA, CoLA, AG News, SST2, Rotten Tomatoes, COLD) and synthetic template-based tasks (Greater Than, Arithmetic). For each layer, last-token representations are collected via Transformer-Lens, and ID is estimated using MLE (local, aggregated via arithmetic/harmonic mean), TwoNN (global, based on the Pareto distribution of nearest-neighbor distance ratios), and GRIDE (a generalized ratio estimator using higher-order neighbor distances that is more robust to sampling noise). The key analytical step is coupling ID trends with per-layer accuracy: intermediate representations are projected to vocabulary space via the unembedding weight matrix to measure how early the model becomes decisive, revealing a consistent 'hunchback' ID profile (low → peak → low) in MLP outputs where the peak aligns with abrupt accuracy jumps. The study further examines how few-shot prompting (up to 50 shots) sharpens ID transitions, how model scale shifts peak locations earlier, and how ID evolves across 154 training checkpoints in the Pythia suite to distinguish generalization from memorization during learning.

## Results

Across 28 open-weight transformer models (hidden dimensions 768–4096), intrinsic dimension estimates of the last-token representations range only from 5 to 37, confirming that language-task manifolds are extremely low-dimensional relative to the ambient space. A characteristic 'hunchback' ID profile emerges in MLP-output layers: ID rises in early layers, peaks at mid-network depth, then drops sharply just before accuracy jumps, indicating that representational compression precedes decision commitment. Increasing few-shot examples (up to 50-shot) consistently lowers final-layer ID and steepens the ID transition, while larger models (e.g., LLaMA-70B vs. 7B) reach their ID peaks at earlier relative depths and maintain lower terminal ID. Final-layer ID shows a weak negative correlation with accuracy in the LLaMA family, but this correlation disappears when only above-baseline models are considered, limiting its utility as a standalone generalization proxy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is an empirical interpretability study rather than a method-comparison paper, so there are no traditional baselines to beat or match. Instead, the paper contrasts geometric trends across model families (LLaMA, Pythia, GPT-2, Mistral, Phi, Gemma) and task types (reasoning vs. retrieval). Reasoning tasks (COPA, COLD) exhibit sharp ID peaks tightly coupled with abrupt accuracy increases, whereas fact-retrieval tasks (MMLU-STEM) show flatter ID profiles with monotonic accuracy gains and no prominent compression phase. MLP outputs display sharper, more localized ID transitions than residual post-activations, which evolve more smoothly, revealing complementary roles in decision formation. Across all 28 models, normalized ID trajectories maintain high inter-model correlation when aligned by relative depth, suggesting a shared geometric inductive bias. In the Pythia training-dynamics study (154 checkpoints per model), the hunchback ID pattern emerges gradually during training, with higher-capacity models showing accuracy rises immediately after a 'reverse peak' (ID low) in mid-network layers, a pattern more gradual than the sharp ID-accuracy coupling seen in MCQA inference.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes classical intrinsic dimension estimators (MLE, TwoNN, GRIDE) and the Logit Lens projection technique to study a specific question not previously addressed: how ID dynamics track decision-making in LLMs during MCQA. It builds on the manifold-hypothesis literature in deep learning (Gong et al., Ansuini et al., Aghajanyan et al.) and the recent finding of a high-ID abstraction phase in transformers (Cheng et al., 2025), but shifts focus from general representation quality or abstraction to the geometric signature of decisiveness. Unlike probing studies (Tenney et al., Hewitt & Manning) that track information flow, or circuit analyses (Elhage et al., Olsson et al.) that identify functional components, this work treats ID as a geometric proxy for when and where the model commits to a prediction, validated by projecting mid-layer representations back to vocabulary space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The consistent 'hunchback' ID pattern across 28 diverse architectures and its alignment with accuracy jumps provides a geometric marker for decision formation that could guide targeted interventions or interpretability tools. However, the paper is explicit that the ID-accuracy relationship is correlational, not causal, and the ID estimators remain noisy. Key open questions include extending the analysis to autoregressive generation (beyond single-token MCQA), connecting ID to information-theoretic quantities like entropy, and studying submodules within transformer blocks. The finding that final-layer ID is a weak proxy for accuracy—contradicting prior CV results—suggests the relationship between geometry and performance is task- and architecture-dependent in ways not yet understood.
