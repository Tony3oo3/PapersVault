## Problem

Reconstructing large-scale dynamic 4D driving scenes from raw sensor data remains challenging because existing methods either require expensive human annotations (3D tracklets, dynamic masks) to separate static and dynamic elements, or rely on complex regularization schemes and per-scene optimization that limit scalability. No prior approach achieves unsupervised, generalizable, high-resolution 4D reconstruction of scenes with many dynamic actors across dozens of views without depending on pre-trained vision models or foundational priors.

## Input / Output

Flux4D consumes posed multi-view camera images (≥60 views at 1080p) and 360° LiDAR point clouds captured over short (~1.5 s) windows from a moving platform, with camera poses provided. LiDAR points are unprojected to initialize 3D Gaussian positions, scales, and colors, while camera images supply appearance supervision. The output is a set of 3D Gaussians each carrying a per-point instantaneous velocity, which can be propagated to arbitrary timesteps to render novel-view RGB, depth, and 3D flow, and to predict future frames or support scene editing for simulation.

## Method

A 3D U-Net with sparse convolutions (f_θ) takes LiDAR-initialized Gaussians and their capture timestamps, and predicts refined Gaussian attributes plus a per-Gaussian velocity in a single forward pass. Training is fully unsupervised: the only losses are photometric (L1 + SSIM on rendered RGB, L1 on rendered depth vs. projected LiDAR) and a simple 'as static as possible' L2 velocity regularization, with no pre-trained vision models, cycle consistency, or geometric priors. The key insight is that training across a diverse multi-scene dataset provides sufficient implicit regularization for the network to automatically decompose static and dynamic components. Motion is modeled with a linear (constant-velocity) propagation, optionally extended to polynomial terms for acceleration/turning. An iterative refinement stage (inspired by G3R) feeds 3D loss gradients back into a second network (f_φ) for two additional passes to sharpen fine-grained appearance, while pixel-wise velocity re-weighting of the photometric loss emphasizes fast-moving regions.

## Results

On PandaSet (1.5s snippets, interpolation), Flux4D achieves 21.99 PSNR / 0.662 SSIM on dynamic regions and 23.84 PSNR / 0.675 SSIM on full images, outperforming the best unsupervised generalizable method STORM (17.65 / 0.367 dynamic PSNR/SSIM) by +4.34 dB and +0.295 SSIM, while remaining competitive with supervised methods like NeuRAD (23.01 / 0.734). On WOD, Flux4D reaches 26.62 PSNR / 0.82 SSIM, surpassing DrivingRecon by +5.99 dB in PSNR. Reconstruction runs in 3.9 s on a single RTX A5000, versus ~100–120 min for per-scene optimization baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Flux4D decisively outperforms all unsupervised baselines on both photometric and geometric metrics. Against the strongest unsupervised generalizable method STORM, Flux4D gains +4.34 dB PSNR and +0.295 SSIM on dynamic regions, and reduces depth RMSE from 5.24 to 1.63 m. Against per-scene optimization methods EmerNeRF and DeSiRe-GS, Flux4D improves dynamic PSNR by +4.20 and +2.91 dB respectively, while running ~25–30× faster. On WOD, Flux4D surpasses DrivingRecon by +5.99 dB PSNR (26.62 vs 20.63) and +0.21 SSIM. For scene flow estimation, Flux4D achieves the best EPE3D (0.094 m) and angular error (0.123 rad) among all compared methods, outperforming dedicated flow estimators NSFP (0.183 m) and FastNSF (0.194 m) as well as STORM (0.120 m). Flux4D remains slightly below supervised methods (NeuRAD, StreetGS) on dynamic PSNR by ~1 dB, but narrows the gap substantially and matches or exceeds them on velocity RMSE.

## Relation to Prior Work

Flux4D builds on 3D Gaussian Splatting as its scene representation and adapts the gradient-guided iterative refinement loop from G3R, but departs sharply from both per-scene optimization methods (NeuRAD, StreetGS, EmerNeRF, DeSiRe-GS) and recent generalizable approaches (DrivingRecon, STORM) by eliminating all external supervision. Where prior self-supervised methods rely on complex regularization schemes (cycle consistency, geometric constraints, multi-stage training) or pre-trained foundation models (SAM, DeepLabv3+, DINOv2) to disentangle static and dynamic elements, Flux4D uses only photometric reconstruction loss and a simple L1 velocity penalty, arguing that multi-scene training itself provides sufficient implicit regularization. The LiDAR-based initialization is a practical departure that enables handling 60+ full-HD views, far exceeding the sparse, low-resolution inputs of prior generalizable methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The core finding—that training a single feed-forward network across diverse driving scenes with minimal supervision can replace the elaborate per-scene regularizations and foundation-model priors used by prior work—challenges a prevailing assumption in self-supervised 4D reconstruction and suggests a simpler, more scalable path forward. Practically, this enables annotation-free reconstruction of large-scale driving scenes in seconds, which is critical for building simulation environments at the scale needed for autonomous driving development. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond driving to other dynamic scene types, how it handles long-horizon reconstruction without visible seams, and whether the linear/polynomial motion model suffices for highly dynamic actors with complex trajectories.
