## Problem

Classical GNNs assume fixed feature ordering and predefined label sets, making them unable to generalize across graphs with arbitrary feature dimensions, feature semantics, or label cardinalities. While GraphAny demonstrated that zero-shot node classification across diverse graphs is possible, it offered only an empirical solution without a principled design recipe or theoretical guarantees. This paper asks: what symmetries must a graph foundation model respect, and can one derive a universal, modular architecture from first principles?

## Input / Output

The model consumes a graph as a triple (A, X, Y) where A ∈ [0,1]^{N×N} is the adjacency matrix, X ∈ R^{N×F} is the node feature matrix, and Y ∈ R^{N×C} is a (possibly partially masked) label matrix. Crucially, F and C are not fixed: the model must accept any number of feature channels and label channels at test time, differing from training. Training uses a label-inpainting objective where 50% of node labels are randomly masked each epoch, and the model predicts the masked entries from visible labels and all node features. Output is a node-level prediction matrix of shape N×C (class scores or regression targets).

## Method

The paper characterizes all linear maps equivariant to the triple symmetry group S_N × S_F × S_C (Proposition 4.1), yielding a 12-parameter family of equivariant layers that mix information across node, feature, and label axes via sum aggregation. Composing these layers with nonlinearities produces TSNet, proven to be a universal approximator of continuous functions respecting the triple symmetries (Theorem 4.3). To obtain a graph foundation model, TSNet layers are applied to the multiset of features induced by each node's local neighborhood, converting any standard GNN aggregation (MeanGNN, GAT, GCNII) into a triple-symmetry GNN (TS-GNN). Feature-label interaction is achieved through two mechanisms: permutation-invariant pooling (theoretically sufficient) and least-squares projections T_F and T_C that map features into label space and vice versa (empirically stronger, borrowed from GraphAny). The resulting architecture is (S_N × S_C)-equivariant and S_F-invariant, enabling zero-shot transfer to graphs with unseen feature dimensions, feature semantics, and label sets.

## Results

Trained on a single graph (Cora), TS-Mean achieves an average zero-shot accuracy of 65.78% across 28 diverse node classification datasets, outperforming its end-to-end backbone MeanGNN (65.50%) and the prior state-of-the-art zero-shot GFM GraphAny (65.56%). TS-GAT similarly reaches 65.60% zero-shot accuracy, surpassing both GAT end-to-end (65.55%) and GraphAny. In the scaling experiment, TS-Mean's zero-shot accuracy improves steadily as training graphs increase from 1 to 9, while GraphAny's performance remains flat, providing the first empirical evidence of scaling-law behavior in a graph foundation model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TS-Mean (zero-shot, 65.78% avg) outperforms its end-to-end backbone MeanGNN (65.50%) by 0.28 points, demonstrating that the triple-symmetry constraint actually improves generalization rather than hurting in-distribution performance. Similarly, TS-GAT (65.60%) edges out GAT end-to-end (65.55%). Against the prior zero-shot GFM GraphAny (65.56%), both TS-Mean and TS-GAT achieve higher average accuracy, with TS-Mean winning on 14 of 28 datasets and TS-GAT on 13. The most striking comparison is in the scaling experiment: as training graphs increase from 1 to 9, TS-Mean's zero-shot accuracy improves monotonically, while GraphAny's performance plateaus after 3 graphs, suggesting GraphAny's learned averaging over least-squares classifiers does not benefit from additional data diversity. On individual datasets, TS-GAT achieves the best zero-shot results on co-cs (91.13%), co-physics (92.23%), and cornell (71.35%), while TS-Mean leads on co-cs (90.92%), photo (90.18%), and wiki-cs (74.16%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on equivariant set networks (DeepSets, DSS) by characterizing the full space of linear maps equivariant to the triple-symmetry group S_N × S_F × S_C (node, feature, and label permutations) and proving universality for the resulting multilayer architecture. It departs from GraphAny [52], the only prior node-level GFM, by replacing its empirical Transformer-based mixer with a theoretically grounded recipe: any standard GNN aggregation (GAT, GraphSAGE, GCNII) can be converted into a triple-symmetry-aware GFM. The authors also identify and correct a subtle flaw in the universality proof of Maron et al. [26] for general subgroups H ≤ S_F, showing the result still holds in the critical case H = S_F via techniques from Segol and Lipman [38].

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first theoretically grounded recipe for node-level graph foundation models, providing both a complete characterization of the symmetry-preserving layer space and a universality guarantee that prior empirical approaches (GraphAny) lack. The first empirical demonstration that a GFM's zero-shot accuracy improves with more diverse training graphs (while GraphAny plateaus) is a notable step toward establishing scaling-law behavior in graph learning. Key limitations include restriction to node-level tasks (no regression experiments despite theoretical support), modest average accuracy gains over GraphAny (~0.2 points), and the least-squares mixing mechanism, while equivariant, may limit expressiveness compared to learned cross-modal interactions. Extending the recipe to edge-level and graph-level tasks, and investigating whether the scaling trend persists at larger scales, are natural next steps.
