## Problem

Existing layer-wise LLM optimization methods (BAdam, LISA, BlockLLM) treat each transformer layer as a homogeneous unit, ignoring the heterogeneous importance of internal modules such as attention projections and feed-forward weights. Their sampling strategies are either uniform or heuristic, and convergence guarantees are unavailable under the practical conditions of Adam optimization, stochastic gradients, and multiple updates per sampled block. MISA addresses all three gaps by introducing module-level partitioning, a principled importance sampling mechanism, and a rigorous convergence proof.

## Input / Output

MISA takes a pre-trained LLM (e.g., LLaMA3-8B, Qwen2.5-7B, Mistral-7B) and a text training set, and at each outer-loop step activates only a subset of individual weight matrices (Wq, Wk, Wv, Wo, Wgate, Wup, Wdown) whose total parameter count is bounded by a fraction δ of the full model. The method outputs fine-tuned full-rank weights (not low-rank adapters) along with a dynamically updated sampling probability distribution over all modules, which is maintained via an exponentially weighted moving average of per-module gradient norms. Unlike LoRA, no additional inference-time overhead is introduced since all original parameters are updated.

## Method

MISA partitions each transformer layer into fine-grained modules (individual matrix parameters) and applies a double-loop block-coordinate descent: the outer loop samples a set of modules whose combined parameter count stays below δ·|θ|, and the inner loop performs T Adam updates on the sampled modules before switching. The sampling probability for each module follows a softmax of its exponentially weighted gradient norm (G_b^n), regularized by a KL divergence penalty toward uniform sampling to balance exploitation of high-importance modules with exploration of all modules. Proposition 2 proves that module-wise importance sampling strictly dominates layer-wise sampling in expected gradient-norm decrease. The convergence analysis establishes an O(1/√(NT)) rate under L-smoothness, bounded stochastic gradients, and Adam with multiple inner updates per block—conditions that prior BCD analyses could not handle due to the bias introduced by repeated updates on the same block.

## Results

On LLaMA3-8B commonsense reasoning (8 tasks), MISA (δ=3%) achieves 86.6 average accuracy with 34.4 GB memory, nearly matching full fine-tuning (86.7 at 150.5 GB) while using only ~23% of the memory. MISA (δ=1%) reaches 85.4 average with just 30.7 GB, outperforming LoRA (82.5 at 35.7 GB) by 2.9 points with less memory. In pre-training LLaMA2-350M on C4, MISA (δ=25%) achieves 22.11 perplexity after 2.7B tokens, substantially outperforming GaLore (24.34) and approaching full Adam (21.30).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MISA (δ=3%) outperforms all baselines on LLaMA3-8B commonsense reasoning: it beats LoRA by 4.1 points (86.6 vs 82.5) with comparable memory (34.4 vs 35.7 GB), DoRA by 1.4 points with 36% less memory (34.4 vs 54.1 GB), LISA by 0.7 points with 39% less memory (34.4 vs 56.3 GB), and BAdam by 1.8 points at nearly identical memory (34.4 vs 34.1 GB). On Qwen2.5-7B, MISA (δ=3%) achieves 88.5 average, surpassing LoRA (88.2), DoRA (88.3), LISA (87.8), and BAdam (88.1). In pre-training LLaMA2-350M, MISA (δ=25%) achieves 22.11 perplexity, outperforming GaLore (24.34) by 2.23 points while using less memory (11.63 vs 12.14 GB), though it remains slightly behind full Adam (21.30). In the LoRA+MISA hybrid setting, MISA also outperforms IST: on LLaMA3-8B, LoRA+MISA reaches 85.7 average vs LoRA+IST's 84.6. MISA's advantage is most pronounced over BAdam and LISA, which use layer-wise (coarser) partitioning, confirming the benefit of module-wise granularity.

## Relation to Prior Work

MISA extends the block-coordinate descent (BCD) line of work for LLM optimization (BAdam, LISA, BlockLLM, HIFT, LIFT) by refining the optimization unit from entire transformer layers to individual matrix modules (W_q, W_k, W_v, W_o, W_up, W_down) within each layer. It departs from prior layer-wise methods in two key ways: (1) a KL-regularized importance sampling strategy that provably reduces gradient variance compared to uniform or cyclic sampling, and (2) a convergence analysis under the practically realistic setting of Adam with stochastic gradients and multiple inner-loop updates per block, where prior BCD analyses assumed noiseless gradients or single updates. The method is orthogonal to subspace-based approaches like LoRA and GaLore, and the paper demonstrates it can be combined with LoRA for further memory savings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Memory-efficient full-parameter fine-tuning of LLMs remains a critical bottleneck for deploying large models in resource-constrained settings, and MISA offers a principled refinement that achieves comparable or better accuracy than LoRA/DoRA while using less memory and updating more parameters. The convergence guarantee under practical training conditions (Adam, stochastic gradients, T>1 inner updates) fills a notable theoretical gap that prior layer-wise methods left open. However, pre-training validation is limited to 130M/350M models, leaving open whether the approach scales to 7B+ pre-training, and the method has only been tested on text-modal Transformer architectures, so its applicability to multi-modal or non-Transformer models remains unexplored.
