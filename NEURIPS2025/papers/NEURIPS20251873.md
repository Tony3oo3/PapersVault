## Problem

Feedback linearization (FL) has been proposed as a control-theoretic approach to constrained optimization, but its theoretical properties remain largely unexplored: only local stability was established, global convergence rates were unknown, the connection to established algorithms like SQP was not formalized, and no extension to inequality constraints or momentum acceleration existed. This paper fills these gaps by providing rigorous global convergence guarantees, proving an exact equivalence between FL discretization and SQP, extending FL to inequality constraints, and introducing a momentum-accelerated variant.

## Input / Output

The method consumes the objective gradient ∇f(x), the constraint Jacobian J_h(x), an initial point x(0), and a diagonal positive-definite control gain K; for inequality constraints, the Lagrange multiplier λ is computed as the solution of a small QP (dimension m, the number of constraints) rather than a closed-form expression. The output is a trajectory converging to a first-order KKT point with an O(1/√T) bound on the KKT-gap (measured as max of gradient norm, constraint violation, and complementary slackness), and in the strongly convex equality-constrained case, linear convergence to the global optimum. The forward-Euler discretization with step η and K=(1/η)I recovers exactly the SQP update, so the method can be deployed as either a continuous-time ODE solver or a discrete SQP iteration.

## Method

The core mechanism treats the constrained problem as a control system: x is the state, y=h(x) is the output, and λ is the control input chosen to linearize the constraint dynamics. By setting λ = -(J_h T J_h^T)^{-1}(J_h T ∇f - Kh), the nonlinear dynamics reduce to ẏ = -Ky, guaranteeing exponential constraint satisfaction; a merit function ℓ(x) = f(x) + (λ_max/λ_min)(MD)²Σ|h_i(x)| then yields the global O(1/√T) KKT-gap rate. The key structural insight is that forward-Euler discretization of this FL dynamics is algebraically identical to SQP (Theorem 2), which motivates the inequality-constraint extension: the closed-form λ is replaced by arg min_{λ≥0} ½λ^T J_h T J_h^T λ + λ^T(J_h T ∇f - Kh), whose discretization recovers the inequality-constrained SQP (Theorem 3). For acceleration, a momentum variable z is introduced with dynamics ẋ=z, ż=-αz-(∇f+J_h^T λ), and a Lyapunov-based merit function in (x,z) establishes O(1/√T) convergence for the nonconvex equality-constrained case (Theorem 5).

## Results

The paper establishes O(1/√T) convergence rates for the KKT-gap in both equality-constrained (Theorem 1) and inequality-constrained (Theorem 4) nonconvex settings, with exponential constraint satisfaction h_i(x(t)) = e^{-k_i t} h_i(x(0)). In the strongly convex case, FL-proximal achieves linear convergence at rate e^{-2μt} to the global optimum (Theorem 6). Numerically, FL-Newton (using Hessian information) converges fastest, FL-momentum outperforms FL-proximal among first-order methods on the heterogeneous logistic regression and IEEE-39 bus problems, while on the ill-conditioned IEEE-118 bus system FL-momentum and FL-proximal exhibit nearly identical convergence curves.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares FL-proximal, FL-Newton, and FL-momentum against Primal-Dual Gradient Descent (PDGD) [77] and the Augmented Lagrangian Method (ALM) [10] on the heterogeneous logistic regression problem (22) with C=5 clients. FL-proximal performs comparably to both PDGD and ALM in convergence speed, confirming that the feedback linearization framework is competitive with established first-order methods. Among the FL variants, FL-Newton converges fastest due to its use of second-order (Hessian) information, while FL-momentum clearly outperforms FL-proximal on the IEEE-39 bus AC OPF problem, demonstrating the benefit of momentum acceleration. However, on the larger IEEE-118 bus system, FL-momentum and FL-proximal show nearly overlapping convergence curves, which the authors attribute to the ill-conditioning of the problem limiting momentum's effectiveness. The paper also shows that an Approximated FL-PI variant (using a diagonal approximation of the Jacobian product inverse plus an integral controller term) successfully converges to the optimal solution on a quadratic program where the standard Approximated FL fails, highlighting the robustness advantage of the PI extension.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the feedback linearization (FL) framework for constrained optimization introduced by Cerone et al. [15], which established only local stability, by proving global convergence rates and uncovering a formal equivalence between FL discretization and Sequential Quadratic Programming (SQP). The control-theoretic perspective on constrained optimization has been explored by several groups [15, 35, 1, 53, 17], but the FL-specific approach had lacked rigorous global analysis, inequality-constraint extensions, and acceleration mechanisms. The momentum-accelerated variant draws on Polyak's extrapolation [63] and the continuous-time ODE formulation of Nesterov acceleration [75], adapted here to the constrained setting with a novel argmin-based dual update for inequality constraints.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first global convergence guarantees (O(1/√T) KKT-gap) for FL-based constrained optimization and a clean SQP equivalence that reframes a classical algorithm through a continuous-time control lens, potentially enabling new algorithmic designs. However, several limitations remain: convergence is only to the best iterate (not last-iterate), the LICQ assumption is required, the momentum variant achieves the same theoretical rate as the non-accelerated version despite empirical speedups, and the analysis does not distinguish local optima from saddle points. Open directions include relaxing LICQ (possibly via stabilized SQP insights), extending to zeroth-order settings, and developing saddle-point escape mechanisms.
