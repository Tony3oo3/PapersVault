## Problem

Existing parameterizations for scaling transformers (SP, μP, α=0.5) fail to transfer optimal hyperparameters such as learning rate across changes in model depth, forcing practitioners to either re-tune HPs at every scale (expensive) or accept suboptimal training. Moreover, even parameterizations that achieve HP transfer may remain in the lazy learning regime where layers learn only features close to their linearization, preventing effective use of depth and nonlinearity. No prior parameterization simultaneously guaranteed depth-wise HP transfer and non-lazy feature learning in every layer.

## Input / Output

Given a base model configuration (N_base=256, L_base=2) with tuned hyperparameters and target width/depth multipliers m_N = N/N_base and m_L = L/L_base, the method deterministically produces a complete set of scaled HPs for a pre-LN transformer: per-layer hidden and bias learning rates, LayerNorm learning rates, initialization variances, AdamW weight decay, and AdamW epsilon. No additional tuning is required at the target scale; the scaling rules are closed-form functions of m_N and m_L alone, making the 'tune small, train large' strategy applicable to both width and depth simultaneously.

## Method

CompleteP sets the residual block scaling exponent to α=1, so each block contributes h^{ℓ+1} = h^ℓ + L^{-1}·F_ℓ(h^ℓ), making every residual block contribute O(1/L) to the residual stream. The key theoretical insight is a novel desideratum called 'complete feature learning': only α=1 ensures that higher-order Taylor terms in parameter updates (of order L^{α-2}) remain at the same scale as first-order terms (of order L^{-1}) as L→∞, preventing layers from asymptotically linearizing and becoming lazy. The paper extends the parameterization with principled scalings for LayerNorm LR (η_base·m_L^{α-1}), bias LR, weight decay (λ_base·m_N), and AdamW ε (ε_base·m_N^{-1}·m_L^{-α}), which are essential for stable training. Empirically, CompleteP achieves 12–34% FLOP savings over μP at 1.5B parameters and widens the compute-efficient N:L range from ~39 (μP) down to ~12, enabling deeper-narrower architectures suited to memory-constrained hardware.

## Results

CompleteP (α=1) achieves 11.8% FLOP savings over μP at the optimal N:L ratio and 34.4% FLOP savings at the deepest model (179 layers) for 1.5B non-embedding parameter models trained at 20 TPP. On downstream evaluation, CompleteP achieves 55.2% average accuracy at the optimal shape (N=1984, L=32) and 54.3% at the deepest shape (N=832, L=179), outperforming μP (54.3% and 52.0%) and α=0.5 (54.3% and 50.1%). CompleteP is the only parameterization that maintains stable optimal learning rate and initialization standard deviation across 2–128 layers without re-tuning, and it enables N:L ≈ 12 to remain within 1% of compute-optimal loss, compared to N:L ≈ 39 for μP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CompleteP (α=1) consistently outperforms all three baselines—μP, α=0.5 (Bordelon et al., ICLR 2024), and the α=0.5 variant advocated by Yang et al. (NeurIPS 2023 Workshop)—on both upstream loss and downstream accuracy. At the optimal N:L ratio for 1.5B models, CompleteP achieves 2.286 SlimPJ validation cross-entropy versus 2.302 for μP and 2.326 for α=0.5, translating to 55.2% average downstream accuracy versus 54.3% for both baselines. The gap widens dramatically at deeper shapes: at N=832, L=179, CompleteP saves 34.4% FLOPs relative to μP and achieves 54.3% downstream accuracy versus 52.0% (μP) and 50.1% (α=0.5). On hyperparameter transfer, only CompleteP maintains stable optimal learning rate and initialization standard deviation across 2–128 layers; μP and α=0.5 both require re-tuning as depth increases. At 200 TPP (Table 3), CompleteP again achieves the lowest validation loss across all tested model sizes and shapes, confirming the advantage is not an artifact of the 20 TPP regime.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the maximal update parameterization (μP) lineage [Yang et al., ICML 2021] and the depth-aware α-family of parameterizations [Bordelon et al., ICLR 2024; Bordelon et al., NeurIPS 2024] by identifying α=1 (CompleteP) as the unique value that simultaneously achieves depth-wise hyperparameter transfer and non-lazy feature learning in all layers. It departs from Yang et al. (NeurIPS 2023 Workshop), who argued HP transfer across depth is impossible at any α and that α=0.5 is best in practice, by providing both a theoretical desideratum (complete feature learning) and empirical evidence that α=1 is strictly superior. The paper also extends prior parameterizations to cover practical modern-training details—LayerNorm learning rates, bias learning rates, AdamW weight decay, and AdamW ε—that were absent from the infinite-limit analyses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CompleteP offers a simple, drop-in parameterization that yields 12–34% FLOP savings over μP for deep models and enables hyperparameter transfer from 2 to 128 layers, directly reducing the costly HP re-tuning that currently accompanies depth scaling in LLM pretraining. By showing that N:L ≈ 10 remains close to compute-optimal (versus the widely assumed N:L ≈ 100), it opens model shapes better suited to memory-constrained or latency-sensitive hardware. Key limitations include testing only up to 1.5B non-embedding parameters and scaling-law fits based on three points per parameter count, leaving open whether the FLOP savings and N:L flexibility persist at 7B+ scale. The theoretical analysis assumes fixed token count, and its transfer to the compute-optimal (fixed TPP) regime, while empirically validated, lacks a formal proof.
