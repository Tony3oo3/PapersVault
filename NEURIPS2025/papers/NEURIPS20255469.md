## Problem

Existing virtual try-on methods, whether mask-based or mask-free, suffer from two persistent occlusion artifacts: inherent occlusions (ghosts of reference clothing leaking into results due to imprecise inpainting masks) and acquired occlusions (disrupted human body structures caused by erroneous human structural representations). Both types stem from the unpaired nature of training data and the unreliability of segmentation masks and parsing maps, yet no prior method addresses them in a unified, mask-free manner.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255469/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the method requires only a reference person image and a target clothing image as RGB inputs—no inpainting mask, human parsing map, or structural representation is needed. It produces a single virtual try-on result image at the same resolution as the input (256×192 for VITON, 512×384 for VITON-HD and DressCode). The mask-free design means the model must internally learn to distinguish human body regions from background and clothing without any external structural guidance at test time.

## Method

The framework introduces two training-time operations applied on top of a pre-trained diffusion model (initialized from CatVTON with an SD1.5 backbone). First, a background pre-replacement operation replaces all regions outside the union of the inpainting mask and person identity mask with a random background (either a T2I-generated scene or a random single-value matrix), severing the connection between residual clothing pixels and the background so the model learns to distinguish clothing from non-clothing. Second, a covering-and-eliminating operation generates pseudo reference images where a larger garment—verified via a human-parsing-based gating function that compares arm and neck coverage—completely covers the target clothing, then trains the model to remove this covering and reconstruct the underlying human body under ground-truth supervision. A 10% probability reconstruction task corrects the distribution shift between pseudo and real reference images. The approach is plug-and-play and generalizes to both diffusion models and GANs without architectural modification.

## Results

On VITON-HD, the proposed method achieves the best SSIM (0.889) and LPIPS (0.057), with FID of 8.854 and KID of 0.96, outperforming CatVTON (FID 9.287, KID 1.17) and DCI-VTON (FID 8.998, KID 1.19). On DressCode Upper, it achieves the best SSIM (0.923), LPIPS (0.042), and FID (6.58), surpassing CatVTON (FID 7.40) and DCI-VTON (FID 11.92). On the lower-resolution VITON dataset, it achieves the best FID (9.23) while matching the top SSIM (0.91) shared with LDE-VTON and USC-PFN. The only method that slightly outperforms it on some VITON-HD metrics (FID 8.809, KID 0.82) is BooW-VTON, which uses the more powerful SDXL backbone.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method is compared against 31 baselines spanning GAN-based (GP-VTON, HR-VITON, USC-PFN, etc.) and diffusion-based (DCI-VTON, CatVTON, BooW-VTON, IDM-VTON, etc.) approaches. On VITON-HD, it achieves the best SSIM (0.889 vs. 0.884 for GP-VTON) and LPIPS (0.057 vs. 0.060 for IDM-VTON), while its FID (8.854) is marginally behind BooW-VTON (8.809) but ahead of CatVTON (9.287) and DCI-VTON (8.998); the KID gap with BooW-VTON (0.96 vs. 0.82) is attributed to BooW-VTON's use of the stronger SDXL backbone. On DressCode Upper, the method clearly dominates with the best FID (6.58 vs. 7.40 for CatVTON and 11.92 for DCI-VTON) and best LPIPS (0.042, tied with DCI-VTON), though DCI-VTON edges it on SSIM (0.937 vs. 0.923) and BooW-VTON on KID (0.86 vs. 1.72). On VITON, it achieves the best FID (9.23) over TPD (9.58) and LDE-VTON (9.86) while tying the top SSIM (0.91) with LDE-VTON and USC-PFN. Overall, the method secures the highest aggregate ranking across all three datasets, with the only consistent underperformance being against BooW-VTON on distributional metrics (FID/KID), which the authors attribute to the backbone difference rather than the framework itself.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on CatVTON (Chong et al., ICLR 2025) as its diffusion backbone, extending the mask-free VTON lineage (WUTON, PF-AFN, USC-PFN, LDE-VTON) that eliminates inpainting masks at inference. However, it departs from the prevailing architecture-centric trend in VTON research by arguing that occlusion artifacts stem from data-level issues (imprecise masks and erroneous human structural representations) rather than insufficient model capacity. The two proposed operations—background pre-replacement and covering-and-eliminating—are deliberately architecture-agnostic, positioning the contribution as a training-strategy layer that can be grafted onto GANs or diffusion models without modifying their structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play framing is practically valuable: practitioners can adopt the background pre-replacement and covering-and-eliminating operations on top of existing VTON pipelines without retraining from scratch. However, the method still depends on a pre-trained teacher model for pseudo-reference generation and a human parsing network for the covering process, introducing additional failure points. The authors acknowledge that dataset diversity limitations (e.g., the swimsuit failure case) constrain generalization, and the modest quantitative gains over CatVTON suggest the occlusion problem, while addressed, may not be fully resolved at the margins. Open questions include whether the approach scales to multi-garment try-on or video-based VTON, and whether the reliance on a separate T2I model for background synthesis can be eliminated.
