## Problem

Existing contrastive learning methods for recommendation (e.g., SimGCL, SGL) rely on graph augmentation that perturbs the interaction graph, which can generate irrelevant connections or discard critical training information. More critically, these methods treat all non-augmented nodes as negatives, unintentionally pushing the target node away from its path neighbors that exert a positive collaborative influence. The paper addresses this gap by explicitly modeling path-level relationships in the contrastive objective rather than relying on node-level augmentation.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a bipartite user-item interaction graph where each edge carries a timestamp, and produces learned user and item embeddings of dimension E used for inner-product scoring. A distinctive aspect is that timestamps are encoded via a sinusoidal temporal context encoding and fused with node embeddings through Hermitian inner products on the complex plane, so the path representation encodes both structural and temporal ordering. The final output is a ranked list of items per user, evaluated with Recall@K and NDCG@K (K=10, 20) on an 80/20 train-test split.

## Method

PECL builds on LightGCN as the collaborative filtering backbone and introduces two complementary contrastive losses operating at different granularities. The intra-path loss samples multiple paths from a target node via random walk with restart, selects positive nodes that co-occur in at least α sampled paths, and applies an InfoNCE-style objective to pull the target node toward these path-internal nodes while repelling others. The inter-path loss uses a two-stage Target-guided Random Walk—deterministically traversing β steps along a center path, then performing a temporally-biased conditional random walk for the remaining steps—to generate positive paths, encodes each path by sequentially applying Hermitian inner products between node embeddings and timestamp embeddings from the farthest node toward the center, and contrasts center-path representations against positive and negative path representations. The total training objective combines BPR ranking loss with both contrastive terms weighted by λ₁ and λ₂, plus L₂ regularization, and is optimized end-to-end with the LightGCN embeddings.

## Results

PECL achieves the best performance across all 12 metric–dataset combinations (NDCG@10/20 and Recall@10/20 on ML-1M, Ciao, and Amazon). On ML-1M, PECL reaches NDCG@10 of 0.4206 and Recall@10 of 0.1676, surpassing the strongest baseline IHGCL (0.4148 / 0.1601). On the sparser Ciao dataset, PECL attains NDCG@10 of 0.0393, a 4.52% relative improvement over the best baseline (SGL, 0.0376). On Amazon, PECL records NDCG@20 of 0.1354 and Recall@20 of 0.1820, outperforming IHGCL (0.1309 / 0.1764) by 3.4% and 3.2% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PECL outperforms all nine baselines on every metric across all three datasets. The closest competitor is IHGCL (intent-guided heterogeneous graph contrastive learning), which PECL surpasses by 1.4% on ML-1M NDCG@10 (0.4206 vs. 0.4148), 7.7% on Ciao NDCG@10 (0.0393 vs. 0.0364), and 3.3% on Amazon NDCG@10 (0.1169 vs. 0.1132). GAIPSRec (graph attention with high-order neighbor propagation) is the second-strongest baseline, trailing PECL by 2.4% on ML-1M NDCG@10 and 2.7% on Amazon NDCG@10. Among the SSL-enhanced methods, SimGCL and SGL are competitive on ML-1M but fall further behind on the sparser Ciao and Amazon datasets, where PECL's path-level contrastive signals provide a larger relative advantage. Traditional GNN baselines (NGCF, LightGCN) and non-contrastive methods (SEPT, SelfCF, SCCF) are consistently outperformed by 5–20% depending on the dataset and metric, confirming that the path-enhanced contrastive mechanism is the primary source of PECL's gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the contrastive learning-for-recommendation line (SimGCL, SGL, NCL, IHGCL) by shifting the unit of contrast from individual nodes or augmented graph views to interaction paths. It builds directly on LightGCN as the backbone, adopts InfoNCE-style losses, and borrows temporal context encoding for path representation, but departs from prior work by treating nodes along a target node's interaction path as positive samples rather than negatives, and by introducing a two-stage target-guided random walk for sampling semantically similar positive paths. The key conceptual departure is that existing graph-augmentation contrastive methods unintentionally push the target node away from its own path neighbors, which PECL explicitly corrects through intra-path and inter-path contrastive objectives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The path-level contrastive perspective offers a principled way to preserve local neighborhood structure that node-level contrastive losses tend to erode, and the ablation confirms inter-path contrast is the dominant contributor to gains. However, the approach requires temporal information on edges (limiting applicability to timestamp-free settings), adds sampling and encoding overhead, and is validated on only three medium-scale datasets. Open questions include whether path-level contrast transfers to other graph learning tasks, whether the Hermitian inner product for temporal fusion is essential or merely convenient, and whether the gains persist at larger scale or in streaming/online recommendation settings.
