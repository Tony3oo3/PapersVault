## Problem

Existing fractional neural ODE models rely on fixed, non-adaptive kernels (e.g., the Caputo power-law kernel $(t-\tau)^{-\alpha}$) that assign predefined weights to historical states regardless of their contextual relevance to the current prediction. This static weighting limits the model's ability to selectively emphasize important temporal dependencies while filtering less relevant history, a gap that becomes critical for systems with heterogeneous or scale-dependent memory effects.

## Input / Output

The method takes an initial feature vector $\mathbf{x}(a)$ (derived from velocity field snapshots via CNN encoding, graph node features, or traffic flow sequences) and evolves it through a continuous-time nonlinear integral equation over a time interval $[a, T]$. The output is the terminal hidden state $\mathbf{x}(T)$, decoded into task-specific predictions. Unlike standard neural ODEs where the update is a simple skip connection, FADE's integral equation produces inherently dense connectivity patterns where each time step attends to all previous states with context-dependent weights, and the kernel's dependence on both $\mathbf{x}(t)$ and $\mathbf{x}(\tau)$ makes the equation nonlinear and requires iterative solving.

## Method

FADE reformulates both integer-order and fractional-order neural ODEs as a unified integral equation $\mathbf{x}(t) = \mathbf{x}(a) + \int_a^t K(t,\tau,\mathbf{x}(t),\mathbf{x}(\tau))\, f_\theta(t,\mathbf{x}(\tau))\, d\tau$, then replaces the fixed kernel $K$ with learnable neural attention kernels that jointly encode temporal structure and state-contextual relevance. Three kernel variants are provided: scaled dot-product attention, a fractional attention kernel that multiplies a $\psi$-Caputo memory term $\frac{\psi'(\tau)(\psi(t)-\psi(\tau))^\alpha-1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}$ by a state-dependent attention function, and a position-augmented attention kernel with sinusoidal encodings. Theoretical analysis establishes kernel boundedness, well-posedness via Banach fixed-point arguments, and linear convergence of the trapezoidal-rule-based iterative solver. The framework subsumes integer-order NODEs, Caputo fractional ODEs, variable-order FDEs, and $\psi$-Caputo FDEs as special cases, providing a single architecture for adaptive continuous-depth learning.

## Results

On turbulent boundary-layer flow prediction, FADE+CNN achieves RMSE 0.3215 and MAE 0.2041, outperforming Transformer+CNN (RMSE 0.4701) and ODE+CNN (RMSE 0.5962) with comparable GPU memory usage (~14.7 GB). On graph node classification across 9 datasets, FADE-l achieves the best accuracy on 6 of 9 (e.g., Cora 86.4%, Citeseer 76.1%, Computer 88.3%), while FADE-nl leads on CoauthorPhy (94.8%). On traffic forecasting, STG-FADE attains the lowest MAE on PeMSD4 (19.17), PeMSD8 (15.29), and PeMSD7(M) (2.69), and the best MAPE on PeMSD7(M) (6.71%) and PeMSD7(L) (7.20%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FADE consistently outperforms all fractional-order baselines (FROND, DRAGON, NvoFDE) and the integer-order GRAND baseline across the 9 graph classification datasets, with the largest gains over GRAND-l on Cora (+2.8%), Citeseer (+2.7%), and Computer (+4.6%). Against the strongest fractional baseline NvoFDE, FADE-l wins on 6 of 9 datasets (e.g., Cora 86.4 vs 86.0, Citeseer 76.1 vs 75.6, Computer 88.3 vs 87.9) while tying on Pubmed (80.7 vs 80.8) and ogbn-arxiv (72.0 vs 72.0). On fluid flow prediction, FADE reduces RMSE by 31.6% over Transformer+CNN (0.3215 vs 0.4701) and by 46.1% over ODE+CNN (0.3215 vs 0.5962), while using only ~4% more GPU memory than the Transformer. In traffic forecasting, STG-FADE achieves the best MAE on 3 of 4 PeMS datasets and the best MAPE on 2 of 4, narrowly beating STG-NCDE (the prior best) by 0.04–0.16 MAE points. Overall, FADE wins or ties on the majority of benchmark comparisons, with the most consistent advantage over fixed-kernel fractional methods (FROND, DRAGON) and a clear edge over integer-order ODE/Transformer baselines on tasks with strong memory effects.

## Relation to Prior Work

FADE sits at the intersection of two well-developed lines of work: fractional neural ODEs (FROND, DRAGON, NvoFDE) that introduce non-local memory via fixed power-law or variable-order kernels, and attention mechanisms (Transformer, GAT) that adaptively weight inputs based on content. The paper's key conceptual move is to replace the static kernel in the fractional integral operator with a learnable attention kernel K(t,τ,x(t),x(τ)) that depends on both temporal position and state correlations, thereby unifying integer-order ODEs, Caputo FDEs, variable-order FDEs, and ψ-Caputo FDEs as special cases of a single integral equation. This departs from prior attention-in-NODE work (e.g., Contiformer, continuous self-attention NODEs) which applies attention to temporal partitions or as an auxiliary module, rather than embedding it directly into the fractional operator's memory kernel.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified integral-equation formulation with adaptive attention kernels offers a principled way to let the model learn which historical states matter for each prediction, addressing a genuine limitation of fixed-kernel fractional models that cannot modulate memory weights based on content. The theoretical guarantees (well-posedness via Banach fixed-point, boundedness, linear convergence of the iterative solver) make the framework practically implementable. However, the empirical gains over the strongest fractional baseline (NvoFDE) are modest on graph classification and traffic forecasting, and the attention mechanism introduces O(L²) computational cost that may limit scalability to very long sequences. Open questions include whether the adaptive kernel provides meaningful benefits over simpler variable-order approaches at scale, and how the framework extends to multi-scale or hierarchical temporal structures.
