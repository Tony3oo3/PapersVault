## Problem

Autoregressive protein language models suffer from high inference latency that limits high-throughput screening, and while speculative decoding accelerates NLP generation, it is agnostic to the structural and functional constraints that make a protein biologically plausible. No prior speculative decoding framework incorporates sequence-level structural information to guide draft token selection toward foldable, functional proteins.

## Input / Output

The method takes a short context sequence (~10% of wild-type length) from a target protein and precomputed k-mer frequency distributions (k ∈ {1, 3, 5}) extracted via sliding window over a multiple sequence alignment from ProteinGym. It produces full-length protein sequences (up to the wild-type length or a stop token) using ProGen2-S as the draft model and ProGen2-M as the target model, with nucleus sampling at p=0.95. The k-mer scoring is precomputed and adds negligible overhead, keeping overall complexity at O(L²).

## Method

SpecMER modifies the drafting step of speculative decoding: instead of sampling a single candidate sequence, the draft model batch-generates c candidate sequences of length L, which are then scored by an additive k-mer frequency function derived from the MSA. The highest-scoring candidate is selected and verified token-by-token via maximal coupling acceptance against the target model, with rejected tokens corrected by sampling from the residual distribution. The key insight is that among the set of tokens all passing the target-consistency check, k-mer guidance biases selection toward completions with higher structural plausibility, closing the gap between target-consistent and biologically meaningful outputs. The paper proves that batch-and-select acceptance satisfies E[A*] = 1 − (1−α)^m − ε, where ε is the misranking error, and shows that increasing c reduces ε at minimal speed cost (3% overhead at c=3), yielding 24–32% wall-time speedup over autoregressive decoding while improving NLL and pLDDT scores.

## Results

SpecMER achieves a 24–32% wall-time speedup over target-only autoregressive decoding (ProGen2-M), with c=1 (vanilla speculative decoding) at 32%, c=3 at 29%, and c=5 at 24% (Table 5). Across seven functionally diverse proteins, SpecMER (c=5) consistently produces lower NLL than speculative decoding — e.g., GFP drops from 2.45 to 1.09, ADRB2 from 1.90 to 1.03, and CBS from 2.42 to 1.87 — while also improving top-20 and top-5 NLL. Average pLDDT scores increase with c for RBP1 (0.571→0.740) and GB1 (0.465→0.504), indicating higher structural confidence. SpecMER (c=5) matches or exceeds the target model's own top-20 NLL on four of five tested proteins (Table 4), demonstrating that k-mer guidance overcomes the high-likelihood tail undersampling inherent to vanilla speculative decoding.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against vanilla speculative decoding (Leviathan et al., ICML 2023), SpecMER wins decisively on sequence quality: NLL is lower for all seven proteins at both c=3 and c=5 (e.g., ADRB2 NLL drops from 1.90 to 1.03 at c=5; GFP from 2.45 to 1.09), and top-5 NLL improves by 30–50% on most proteins. Acceptance ratios are marginally higher for SpecMER (e.g., GFP 0.911→0.945, ParD3 0.902→0.942), and average pLDDT scores increase for RBP1 (0.571→0.740) and GB1 (0.465→0.504), though GFP is an exception where vanilla speculative decoding scores slightly higher on average pLDDT. The speed trade-off is modest: SpecMER (c=5) achieves 24% speedup versus 32% for vanilla speculative decoding, while c=3 retains 29% speedup with the largest likelihood gains. Against the ProGen2-M target model itself (Nijkamp et al., Cell Systems 2023), SpecMER (c=5) matches or exceeds the target's own top-20 NLL on Bgl3 (0.63 vs 0.78), GFP (0.41 vs 0.51), ParD3 (0.67 vs 0.69), and GB1 (2.20 vs 2.27), demonstrating that k-mer-guided drafting overcomes the high-likelihood tail undersampling that plagues vanilla speculative decoding. With ProGen2-XL as target and ProGen2-M as draft, SpecMER (c=3) achieves a 38% speedup, reducing the time to generate 20,000 sequences of length 200 from ~65 hours to ~40 hours.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper is the first to adapt speculative decoding—a technique well-established in NLP inference acceleration (Leviathan et al., ICML 2023; Sun et al., NeurIPS 2023)—to autoregressive protein sequence generation. It retains the maximal coupling acceptance criterion from SpecTr as the verification step but departs from standard speculative decoding by inserting a k-mer scoring function (drawn from MSA-derived evolutionary statistics, analogous to k-mer attention in Tranception) into the drafting step to select among batch-generated candidates. The framework is built entirely on top of ProGen2, a pre-trained autoregressive protein language model, with no model retraining required.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 24–32% wall-time speedup is modest in absolute terms but meaningful for high-throughput protein screening workflows where thousands of sequences must be generated iteratively; the paper demonstrates that SpecMER(c=3) would cut a 65-hour generation job to ~40 hours. More conceptually, the result that k-mer-guided drafting can produce top-20 NLL values *lower* than target-only decoding (Table 4) suggests that domain-specific priors can correct the tail-undersampling pathology of vanilla speculative decoding under nucleus sampling. Key open questions include whether the approach scales to fully parallel multi-GPU drafting, how it performs on proteins with extensive disordered regions where MSA-derived k-mers are less informative, and whether the same principle (cheap domain priors guiding draft selection) transfers to other constrained generation tasks.
