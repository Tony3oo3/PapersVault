## Problem

Existing remote sensing image-text datasets either focus on single-snapshot imagery or provide multi-temporal pairs without rich textual descriptions of how scenes change over time. This temporal-semantic gap leaves disaster-specific bi-temporal understanding underexplored, as no large-scale dataset pairs pre-/post-disaster satellite imagery with detailed, human-like change captions that capture damage levels and infrastructure transformations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252290/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 512×512 bi-temporal satellite image pairs (pre-event and post-event) sourced from xBD and EBD datasets, optionally augmented with color-coded building damage bounding boxes overlaid on the post-event image (visual prompt) and a textual prompt specifying disaster type and per-level building counts. The output is a single change caption averaging 72 words, written in a news-reporting style that describes the scene transformation, damage severity, and environmental impact. The dataset spans 62,351 pairs across 31 global events covering earthquakes, floods, wildfires, tsunamis, storms, and volcanic eruptions.

## Method

The RSCC dataset is constructed by prompting QvQ-Max, a proprietary visual-reasoning MLLM, with carefully designed instructions that combine the pre/post image pair, disaster metadata, and building damage statistics converted into in-context auxiliary information. A key design choice is visual prompt engineering: building bounding boxes are overlaid on the post-event image in colors corresponding to damage levels (green/blue/orange/red), inspired by marking-based prompting for VLMs. A two-stage post-correction pipeline ensures quality—Qwen2.5-Max enforces metadata alignment (achieving 100% disaster-type consistency), followed by 10% human expert validation using a binary rubric. For benchmarking, the authors fine-tune Qwen2.5-VL 7B on 61,363 training pairs (2 epochs, cosine LR decay on 2×H800 GPUs) and evaluate 10+ MLLMs using ROUGE, METEOR, and a novel ST5-SCS metric (Sentence-T5-XXL embeddings with sharpened cosine similarity), alongside a human preference study.

## Results

On the RSCC test set (988 bi-temporal image pairs), the paper's specialized 7B model (fine-tuned Qwen2.5-VL) achieves the best performance across all metrics: 14.99% ROUGE, 16.05% METEOR, and 58.52% ST5-SCS (Sharpened Cosine Similarity with Sentence-T5-XXL embeddings). Among general-purpose MLLMs, InternVL3 (8B) leads with 51.84% ST5-SCS, followed by Kimi-VL (3B) at 51.35% and Pixtral (12B) at 49.36%. The paper's model outperforms the strongest general MLLM (InternVL3) by 6.68 points on ST5-SCS and the closest specialized baseline (TEOChat) by 5.88 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's RSCC-trained 7B model outperforms all 10 baselines on every metric. Against the strongest general MLLM, InternVL3 (8B), it gains +6.68 ST5-SCS (58.52 vs. 51.84) and +2.23 ROUGE (14.99 vs. 12.76). Among specialized remote sensing models, TEOChat (7B) is the closest competitor at 52.64% ST5-SCS, still 5.88 points below the paper's model, while CCExpert (7B) trails significantly at 40.81% ST5-SCS. Notably, Kimi-VL (3B) achieves 51.35% ST5-SCS despite being the smallest model tested, suggesting architectural efficiency can partially offset parameter count. BLIP-3 (3B) and LLaVA-OneVision (8B) suffer from severe repetition (Avg_L of 456 and 221 words respectively), degrading their effective performance. Phi-4-Multimodal (4B) is the weakest baseline overall, scoring only 34.55% ST5-SCS and 1.45% METEOR, indicating poor alignment with remote sensing imagery.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the remote sensing image change captioning (RSICC) task established by Liu et al. (2022), Chang & Ghamisi (2023), and Hoxha et al. (2022) by introducing a disaster-specific, building-damage-aware formulation at unprecedented scale. It repurposes existing change detection datasets (xBD, EBD) by generating rich bi-temporal change captions via QvQ-Max, a visual reasoning MLLM, combined with visual prompt engineering inspired by Shtedritski et al. (2023) to overlay building damage masks. The benchmark evaluation positions RSCC against both general-purpose MLLMs (LLaVA-NeXT-Interleave, InternVL3, Pixtral, Kimi-VL) and specialized remote sensing captioning models (CCExpert, TEOChat), carving out a new evaluation axis for disaster-aware temporal understanding that prior datasets (LEVIR-CC, Dubai-CCD, WHU-CDC) did not support.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RSCC fills a critical gap: no prior dataset combined 62K+ bi-temporal disaster image pairs with detailed, damage-level-specific change captions, making it the first large-scale benchmark for disaster-aware bi-temporal VLM evaluation. The benchmark reveals that even state-of-the-art MLLMs (Pixtral 12B, InternVL3 8B) achieve only ~50% ST5-SCS zero-shot on this task, and that training-free correction decoding strategies (VCD, DoLa, DeCo) fail to improve performance—suggesting remote sensing temporal reasoning requires fundamentally different capabilities than object-level hallucination mitigation. Key limitations include reliance on AI-generated captions (QvQ-Max) rather than purely human annotations, absence of multi-image captioning evaluation metrics, and the finding that VLMs underperform specialized models on visual-centric subtasks like change detection, leaving open the question of whether general-purpose VLMs can ever match task-specific architectures for geospatial reasoning.
