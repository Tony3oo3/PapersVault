## Problem

While generative pretraining has proven effective for compressing world knowledge into token representations in language and vision, user behavioral sequences (e.g., payment transactions) remain modeled primarily by supervised discriminative classifiers that suffer from label scarcity, overfitting to high-cardinality categories, and latency-driven truncation of long histories. No prior work extends the pretrain-then-adapt paradigm to the behavioral modality, where each event is a structured multi-attribute token rather than a word, and where sequences exhibit strong periodic motifs (daily routines, weekly cycles) that standard self-attention dilutes.

## Input / Output

The offline pretraining stage consumes a user's full transaction history, where each transaction is tokenized as a Cartesian product of contextual features (payment channel, discretized amount) and counterparty attributes (merchant category, risk level), compressed from ~2M raw combinations to 60K frequency-ranked tokens. The online inference stage takes a new transaction's context features, the last 100 transactions (encoded via TextCNN), the cached user-profile embedding, and a behavioral deviation score (distance between predicted and observed next token), producing a millisecond-level fraud probability. The pretraining stage also outputs a dense user-profile embedding that serves dual roles: as a personalized positional encoding during sequence modeling and as a contrastive anchor for population-level knowledge transfer.

## Method

PANTHER is a two-stage hybrid generative-discriminative framework. In the offline pretraining stage, a Transformer backbone augmented with a Sequence Pattern Recognition Module (SPRM) is trained via next-transaction prediction (negative log-likelihood) over billions of unlabeled transactions. The SPRM runs in parallel with multi-head self-attention and combines multi-scale depthwise dilated convolutions (capturing local periodicities at linear cost) with prototype-driven cross-attention against m=64 learnable spending-motif anchors, adding its output to the Transformer's residual stream. A dedicated user-profile token is injected as a learnable positional encoding, and a contrastive objective (InfoNCE-style with demographic positive pairs) is added to the generative loss with weight λ, pulling demographically similar users together in embedding space. In the online stage, a lightweight discriminative head (e.g., DCN) fuses the cached user-profile embedding, recent-pattern encoding, context features, and a deviation score between the predicted and observed next token, adding only ~5 ms over the production baseline while enabling 38.6% recall gains at the top-0.1% fraud threshold.

## Results

On the primary WeChat Pay benchmark (5.3B transactions, 38M users), PANTHER achieves HR@1 of 0.2452, a 25.6% relative improvement over the Transformer baseline (0.1952) and 17.9% over HSTU (0.2089). Cross-domain evaluation shows consistent gains: +21.8% HR@1 on MovieLens-1M, +15.2% on CCT, +9.2% on Yelp, and +3.0% on MBD-mini over Transformer. In production fraud detection, PANTHER improves Top-0.1% recall by 38.6% over the DeepFM baseline in a 10-day full-traffic A/B test, and on Yelp recommendation it lifts NDCG@5 by 29.6% over DCN.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PANTHER outperforms all six baselines across every evaluated dataset and task. Against the vanilla Transformer [23], PANTHER gains 25.6% HR@1 on WeChat Pay, 21.8% on MovieLens-1M, 15.2% on CCT, and 9.2% on Yelp. Versus SASRec [14], PANTHER leads on all four public datasets (e.g., +15.2% HR@1 on CCT where SASRec actually underperforms Transformer by 10.6%). Against HSTU [6], the strongest sequential baseline, PANTHER still wins on HR@1 for CCT (+7.1%), MovieLens-1M (+0.9%), and Yelp (+5.7%), though the margin narrows on MBD-mini (+5.1%). For downstream fraud detection, PANTHER's pretrained embeddings improve F1 by 19.9% over TabBERT-MLP [28] on CCT and boost Top-0.1% recall by 38.6% over the production DeepFM [7] system at WeChat Pay. On Yelp recommendation, PANTHER embeddings added to DCN [8] yield a 29.6% NDCG@5 gain (0.692 vs. 0.534), confirming transferability beyond the pretraining domain.

## Relation to Prior Work

PANTHER repurposes the generative pretraining paradigm established for language (BERT, GPT) and vision (MAE) to a new modality—structured payment transactions treated as 'behavioral tokens.' It departs from sequential recommenders like HSTU and SASRec, which use next-item prediction as an end task, by instead using generation as a pretraining mechanism to produce transferable user-profile embeddings that are then adapted with lightweight discriminative heads. The SPRM (multi-scale dilated convolutions + prototype cross-attention) introduces inductive biases for periodic behavioral patterns that standard self-attention lacks, while the structured tokenization (Cartesian product of transaction attributes with frequency-based compression) is a domain-specific contribution absent from prior sequential modeling work. The hybrid offline-pretrain/online-infer design draws from industrial CTR and fraud detection systems (DeepFM, DCN) but inverts their purely supervised paradigm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is one of the first demonstrations that generative pretraining on unlabeled behavioral sequences can yield production-grade fraud detection and recommendation at billion-user scale, with a 38.6% recall gain at the top-0.1% threshold in a live A/B test at WeChat Pay. The linear-complexity SPRM and cached-embedding inference pattern offer a practical recipe for applying transformer-scale pretraining under strict latency budgets (<100 ms), a tension that has limited adoption of sequential models in real-time financial systems. Key limitations include acknowledged lack of interpretability (problematic for regulated domains), reliance on demographic attributes for contrastive pairing (potential bias), and domain-specific tokenization that may not transfer directly to other behavioral modalities. Open questions include whether the pretraining signal degrades as sequence length grows beyond the 8K tested, and how to make the learned embeddings auditable for compliance.
