## Problem

Statically provisioned multimodal networks waste compute on low-quality modalities, while existing dynamic networks optimize for average-case efficiency without mechanisms to enforce strictly bounded, time-varying compute budgets. Neither approach explicitly accounts for per-sample quality-of-information (QoI) variations across modalities, so heavily corrupted inputs may needlessly consume resources that could be reallocated to healthier modalities.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252313/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multimodal sensor inputs (RGB images, depth frames, audio spectrograms, and optionally mmWave radar) that may be corrupted by Gaussian noise, lowlight, blur, rain, or background noise at varying per-sample severities. It produces task-specific outputs—localization coordinates (cm), activity class labels, or event class labels—under the hard constraint that the total number of active backbone layers across all modalities sums exactly to a specified budget L, which can vary at inference time to model thermal throttling or energy fluctuations.

## Method

ADMN uses a two-stage training process. Stage 1 adapts LayerDrop (originally for text transformers) to ViT backbones by injecting it into both MAE self-supervised pretraining and multimodal supervised finetuning, producing a network resilient to arbitrary subsets of missing layers. Stage 2 trains a lightweight multimodal controller that downsamples inputs through modality-specific convolutions to extract QoI embeddings, fuses them via a transformer encoder, and outputs a discrete layer allocation summing to L. The controller is made QoI-aware through either corruption-aware supervision (predicting ground-truth corruption values) or autoencoder-based initialization (learning QoI structure from reconstruction without labels). Differentiable layer selection is achieved via Gumbel-Softmax sampling at temperature 1, followed by top-L discretization and the straight-through estimator, enabling end-to-end training under the hard budget constraint.

## Results

On the GDTM localization task, ADMN achieves 30.3 cm error at 16 layers under Gaussian noise (vs. 29.6 cm upper bound) and 9.9 cm under Blur (vs. 9.4 cm upper bound), while reducing FLOPs by up to 75% and latency by up to 60% compared to the full 12-layer-per-backbone model. On MM-Fi classification, ADMN reaches 43.31% accuracy at 16 layers (vs. 44.44% upper bound) under Gaussian noise. On AVE audiovisual classification, ADMN achieves 66.60% at 16 layers (vs. 71.19% upper bound) under combined rain and background noise corruption. At the most aggressive 6-layer budget under Blur, ADMN localizes within ~2 cm of the upper bound while using only half the layers per backbone.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ADMN dramatically outperforms the MNS (Modality Network Selection) baseline, which adapts the network-selection approach from AdaMML [Panda et al., ICCV 2021], DynMM [Xue et al., CVPR 2023], and DynaFuse [Alikhani et al., IEEE ESL 2023]. On GDTM Gaussian noise at 12 layers, ADMN achieves 33.1 cm error while MNS degrades to 57.5 cm; under Lowlight at 8 layers, ADMN reaches 23.9 cm versus MNS's 83.9 cm. The gap widens further at higher budgets where MNS's from-scratch expert networks fail to converge (e.g., 117.6 cm at 12 Lowlight layers vs. ADMN's 18.0 cm). Against Naive Allocation (equal layer split), ADMN is substantially better at small budgets: at 6 Gaussian layers, ADMN scores 51.4 cm vs. Naive Alloc.'s 112.5 cm. Naive Scratch (training a new network per budget without LayerDrop) also fails to converge under complex corruptions, matching MNS's poor performance. The key advantage of ADMN over all baselines is that it leverages pretrained weights via LayerDrop, enabling a single backbone to adapt to any layer budget without retraining, while the QoI-aware controller reallocates layers per-sample based on input quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends LayerDrop from unimodal text transformers to multimodal Vision Transformer backbones, integrating it into both MAE pretraining and task-specific finetuning to create networks resilient to arbitrary layer subsets at inference time. It departs from prior dynamic multimodal networks (DynMM, DynaFuse, AdaMML) that perform whole-modality selection or average-case early exit by introducing a QoI-aware controller that allocates a strict layer budget across modalities per-sample, using a modified Gumbel-Softmax + straight-through estimator to enforce the hard budget constraint. The autoencoder-based initialization (ADMN_AE) repurposes reconstruction objectives to learn QoI-representative embeddings without requiring explicit corruption labels, drawing on the known property that autoencoder latent spaces cluster by corruption type.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in enabling a single pretrained multimodal model to operate under fluctuating compute budgets (thermal throttling, multi-tenancy) while intelligently reallocating capacity away from corrupted modalities—addressing two challenges that prior dynamic networks handle separately or not at all. Key limitations include the need for per-budget controller training (though a universal controller is explored) and the reliance on synthetic corruptions for evaluation, leaving open how well the QoI-aware allocation generalizes to truly novel, untrained corruption types. The finding that the controller allocates resources to heavily blurred images invisible to human observers suggests learned perception can diverge from human intuition in useful ways, but whether this robustness transfers to more severe or out-of-distribution corruptions remains untested.
