## Problem

Adapter-based personalization of text-to-image models (e.g., IP-Adapter) relies on single-image reconstruction objectives that are agnostic to which visual factors matter, causing the adapter to entangle the target identity with incidental attributes like pose, expression, lighting, and dataset-level distribution shift. This shortcut learning makes the adapter copy-and-paste unwanted attributes from the reference image, ignoring text-prompt-specified pose and expression and degrading overall generation quality. No prior work had systematically leveraged auxiliary conditioning modules during adapter training to proactively absorb these spurious factors.

## Input / Output

At training time, the method takes a reference image (face crop aligned via facial landmarks, or full-body crop via segmentation with background removal) along with derived auxiliary signals (pose/expression maps from pose estimation and landmark detection, and dataset-level style features). At inference, the user provides a single reference image and a free-form text prompt specifying desired pose, expression, lighting, and scene; the model outputs a 1024×1024 personalized image that preserves the subject's identity while following the prompt for all other attributes. Identity is encoded via CLIP ViT-L/14 embeddings, and generation is based on FLUX.1-dev (DiT backbone, Conditional Flow Matching) with CFG 3.5 and 28 denoising steps.

## Method

The core idea is Shortcut-Rerouted (SR) Adapter Training: during training, confounding factors C are explicitly routed through a frozen auxiliary module S_C (the 'SR module'), so the reconstruction loss is satisfied by S_C for those factors and the adapter A has no gradient incentive to encode them. Two instantiations are demonstrated: SR-LoRA, a lightweight LoRA pretrained on the finetuning dataset to absorb distribution shift between the base model and the personalization data, and SR-CN, a pre-trained ControlNet conditioned on pose and expression maps extracted from training images. The adapter (IP-Adapter) is trained with the objective E_X[L(G(A(X), S_C(C)), X)], and at inference S_C is simply removed, yielding a disentangled adapter that injects only identity while leaving pose, expression, and scene fully controllable by the text prompt. This modular decomposition generalizes: additional SR modules (e.g., a background adapter for lighting leakage) can be composed, and the framework applies to both face-only and full-body personalization settings.

## Results

On the face adapter benchmark (Table 1), SR-CN IPA achieves the best head pose control (12.68° mean absolute difference vs. 16.12° for vanilla IPA and 17.71° for InfU), the best prior preservation (LPIPS 0.394 vs. 0.480 for IPA and 0.449 for InfU), and the highest EMOCA expression similarity (0.580 vs. 0.347 for IPA). For full-body personalization (Table 2), SR-CN IPA reduces body pose deviation to 137.7 pixels (vs. 167.4 for IPA and 186.7 for InstantX) while maintaining competitive identity scores (LLM Id. 4.65, FaceNet Id. 0.586).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SR-CN IPA decisively outperforms all four baselines on head pose control (12.68° vs. 16.12° for vanilla IPA, 17.53° for PuLID, and 17.71° for InfU) and prior preservation (LPIPS 0.394 vs. 0.480, 0.458, and 0.449 respectively), confirming that shortcut rerouting effectively disentangles pose/expression from identity. On expression fidelity, SR-CN IPA leads with EMOCA similarity of 0.580, surpassing InfU (0.542), PuLID (0.489), and vanilla IPA (0.347) by a wide margin. For identity preservation, SR-CN IPA is competitive with vanilla IPA (LLM Id. 4.79 vs. 4.79; FaceNet Id. 0.712 vs. 0.715) but trails PuLID (FaceNet 0.774) and InfU (FaceNet 0.740) on the FaceNet metric, suggesting a modest trade-off between disentanglement and raw face-recognition similarity. In the full-body setting, SR-CN IPA dominates InstantX across every metric (e.g., body pose 137.7 vs. 186.7, LPIPS 0.413 vs. 0.508) and also improves over vanilla IPA on pose and prior adherence while matching its identity scores.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on IP-Adapter (Ye et al., 2023) as the base personalization adapter and repurposes two well-established conditioning tools—LoRA and ControlNet—as training-only 'shortcut absorbers' rather than inference-time controllers. Conceptually, it extends the shortcut-learning literature (Geirhos et al., 2020) from classification to generative adapter training, and generalizes the stage-wise auxiliary-LoRA trick used in prior personalization work (Jones et al., 2024; Guo et al., 2023) by showing that structured modules like ControlNet can similarly absorb pose/expression confounds. The key departure from prior adapter training is the explicit architectural decision to route confounding factors through dedicated modules during training and then discard them at inference, rather than hoping the adapter disentangles factors implicitly.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper articulates a clean, general design principle—'provide the shortcuts you want to eliminate'—that could inform adapter training beyond personalization (e.g., style LoRAs, content adapters). However, the empirical validation is limited to IP-Adapter (a relatively simple encoder-based baseline) on an internal, non-public dataset, and no code is released, making independent verification difficult. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to LoRA-based adapters, stronger personalization backbones, or multi-subject settings, and whether the gains persist when the auxiliary modules are themselves imperfect. The ethical dimension of improved identity control (deepfake risk) is acknowledged but not deeply addressed.
