## Problem

High-dimensional continuous-time stochastic control problems with jumps and no closed-form optimal control remain intractable: classical PDE methods suffer from the curse of dimensionality, existing deep-learning PDE solvers require the optimal control to be expressible explicitly in terms of the value function, and model-free RL methods discard the known system dynamics. No efficient meshfree, model-based deep-learning algorithm existed that could simultaneously learn both the value function and the optimal policy for jump-diffusion dynamics in high dimensions.

## Input / Output

The method requires the full specification of the controlled jump-diffusion dynamics (drift β, diffusion σ, jump size γ, stochastic jump intensity λ) plus the running reward f and terminal reward F, and operates on sampled space-time points (t, x) from [0,T)×D. It outputs two neural networks providing global, pointwise-evaluable approximations of the value function V(t,x) and the optimal feedback control α*(t,x) over the entire space-time domain, rather than only along simulated trajectories. The value network uses a DGM architecture with tanh hidden activations and softplus output (to enforce non-negativity where appropriate), while the control network uses tanh or identity/softplus output depending on the action space.

## Method

The paper proposes two iterative actor-critic algorithms that jointly train a value network V_θ and a control network α_φ. GPI-PINN minimizes a PINN-type loss on the HJB residual, using a key trick (Proposition 3.1) that replaces explicit gradient and Hessian computation with evaluating the second derivative of a univariate auxiliary function ψ''(0), reducing the cost to a small multiple of n·cost(V). GPI-CBU, the main contribution, replaces the residual-based update with a continuous-time Bellman updating rule built on an expectation-free operator G_ζ (Proposition 4.1): the value network is updated by regressing V_θ(t,x) onto G_ζ(t,x,z,θ^(k),φ^(k)) for a single sampled jump z, thereby avoiding jump-expectation Monte Carlo sums, third-order derivatives, and explicit gradient/Hessian evaluation entirely. The control network is updated by maximizing an expectation-free version of the Hamiltonian over sampled (t,x,z) triples. Both algorithms use the DGM architecture, Adam optimizer, and residual-based adaptive sampling, but GPI-CBU's recursive value update (using the previous epoch's weights in G_ζ) makes it dramatically more efficient for high-dimensional problems with jumps, scaling to 150 dimensions where GPI-PINN becomes infeasible.

## Results

On a 10-dimensional LQR problem with controlled jumps (Λ₂=2), GPI-CBU achieves MAE_V = 0.0025 and MAE_α = 0.0049 after 5000 epochs. Scalability is demonstrated up to 150 dimensions, where GPI-CBU still attains MAE_V = 0.0979 and MAE_α = 0.0096 in ~33,000 seconds. For a 50-dimensional LQR with jumps, GPI-CBU achieves MAE_V = 0.0147 and MAE_α = 0.0075, while GPI-PINN becomes computationally infeasible. On a 52-dimensional consumption-investment problem with stochastic coefficients (n=25 stocks), GPI-CBU converges with stable losses across 1000 epochs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On a 10-dimensional LQR problem with jumps, GPI-CBU achieves the lowest log MAE_V among all compared methods, clearly outperforming the model-free RL algorithms PPO and SAC, which do not explicitly exploit the known system dynamics and rely solely on environment sampling. The model-based discrete-time approach of Han & E (2016) outperforms PPO and SAC but still falls short of GPI-CBU due to time-discretization errors and its local nature, which limits generalization to unexplored regions of the space-time domain. Between the two proposed algorithms, GPI-PINN exhibits more stable convergence (residual-based) but becomes computationally infeasible in high dimensions with jumps because it requires evaluating jump-expectations at every sample point and computing third-order derivatives. GPI-CBU avoids both of these costs via its expectation-free Bellman updating rule, making it the preferred method for high-dimensional problems with jumps, as demonstrated by its successful application to 50- and 150-dimensional LQR problems and a 52-dimensional consumption-investment problem where GPI-PINN cannot be run.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the PINN paradigm (Raissi et al., 2017, 2019) and the continuous-time finance deep-learning framework of Duarte et al. (2024) to finite-horizon stochastic control with jumps, combining generalized policy iteration (actor-critic) with physics-informed residual minimization. The key methodological departure is the introduction of GPI-CBU, which replaces the costly jump-expectation computation in the HJB Hamiltonian with an expectation-free Bellman updating rule (Proposition 4.1), drawing on the recursive structure of the Feynman-Kac formula. The approach is explicitly positioned as model-based, contrasting with model-free RL methods (PPO, SAC, DQN) and local time-discretization schemes (Han & E, 2016; Huré et al., 2021) that either ignore known dynamics or suffer from discretization error.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The expectation-free updating rule in GPI-CBU removes the dominant computational bottleneck (numerical integration over jump distributions at every sample point) that makes PINN-type methods infeasible for high-dimensional jump problems, enabling accurate solutions up to 150 dimensions. A key limitation is the model-based requirement: the system dynamics (β, σ, γ, λ) must be known a priori, which restricts applicability in economics/finance where dynamics are typically estimated from data. Open questions include establishing theoretical convergence guarantees for both algorithms, developing adaptive scaling factors ζ for GPI-CBU, and extending the framework to partially observed or data-driven dynamics.
