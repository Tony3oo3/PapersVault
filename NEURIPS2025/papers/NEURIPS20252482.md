## Problem

Stein Variational Ergodic Search (SVES) generates parallel ergodic trajectories via SVGD, but the standard RBF kernel ignores the sequential (Markovian) structure of trajectories, causing the particle set to cluster and collapse onto similar solutions rather than covering distinct modes of the non-convex ergodic landscape. Additionally, SVES depends on analytic gradients of both the ergodic cost and the kernel, which are expensive or unavailable for complex dynamics and black-box objectives.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a target probability measure pi over a bounded domain S, continuous-time robot dynamics f(x,u), an initial state x0, and control constraints U, and produces a set of N discretized trajectories (or control sequences of length T) that jointly minimize the composite ergodic cost J_pi while remaining mutually diverse. Trajectories are represented as sequences of T state vectors in R^n (or T control vectors in R^m), and diversity is quantified via the determinant of a Gram matrix built from a Frechet-distance kernel over the N particles. The output set is intended for online strategy switching: the robot can select or blend among the N trajectories as the environment changes.

## Method

The paper introduces two complementary diversification mechanisms into the SVES framework. First, it replaces the RBF kernel with the path signature kernel, whose iterated-integral construction is injective on non-tree-like paths and inherently sequence-sensitive, yielding a stronger repulsive force that spreads particles across distinct regions of the search space; the kernel is evaluated in O(Td) wall-clock time per pair via a Goursat PDE solved with explicit finite differences on a GPU. Second, it substitutes SVGD's gradient-based particle update with SV-CMA-ES, where each particle carries its own Gaussian search distribution and the score-function term is replaced by a CMA-ES mean shift computed from ranked fitness evaluations of the ergodic cost; when analytic kernel gradients are unavailable, SPSA provides a fully gradient-free surrogate. The resulting algorithm is end-to-end derivative-free, exploits batched GPU parallelism for both fitness evaluations and kernel PDE solves, and can handle non-differentiable or black-box cost functions.

## Results

On the Stein variational MPC benchmark (200 iterations, N=5 trajectories), the signature kernel achieves the lowest total ergodic cost of the best trajectory (239.73) and the lowest total best trajectory cost (351.83), outperforming RBF (262.09 and 377.83 respectively). Trajectory diversity, measured as −log(1−det(K_f)), increases by over 11× with the signature kernel (548.22) compared to RBF (48.51). However, the signature kernel's strong repulsive force yields a slightly higher total average trajectory cost (581.27 vs. 563.14 for RBF), attributed to trajectories being pushed toward obstacles. SV-CMA-ES matches SVGD's convergence in cost while promoting measurably greater trajectory diversity across both the planar and 3D drone experiments.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the original SVES baseline (Lee et al., RSS 2024) which uses an RBF kernel with SVGD, the signature kernel reduces the ergodic cost of the best trajectory by ~8.5% (239.73 vs. 262.09) and increases trajectory diversity by over 11×, at the cost of a ~3% increase in average trajectory cost due to the kernel's strong repulsion. Compared to Kernelized DTW (Marteau, IEEE TNNLS 2015), the signature kernel achieves a lower best-trajectory cost (351.83 vs. 375.13) and a substantially higher diversity score (548.22 vs. 57.46), while Kernelized DTW edges out on average cost (554.92 vs. 581.27). The Global Alignment kernel (Cuturi, 2007) is outperformed by the signature kernel on both ergodic cost and diversity in the multiscale and 3D drone experiments (Figures 3 and 5). SV-CMA-ES (Braun et al., 2024) matches SVGD (Liu & Wang, NeurIPS 2016) in convergence speed and final cost while producing more diverse trajectory sets, and additionally eliminates the need for analytic gradients, enabling use with black-box objectives.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Stein Variational Ergodic Search (SVES) [Lee et al., RSS 2024], which recast ergodic trajectory optimization as a Stein variational inference problem solved via SVGD with an RBF kernel. The two proposed diversification mechanisms draw on distinct lineages: the signature kernel (from path-signature theory [Salvi et al., 2021; Barcelos et al., IJRR 2024]) provides a sequence-sensitive, reparameterization-invariant similarity that preserves the Markovian structure of trajectories, while SV-CMA-ES [Braun et al., 2024] coupled with SPSA [Spall, 1992] replaces gradient-based SVGD with a fully derivative-free evolution-strategy update. Conceptually, the paper departs from SVES by arguing that the RBF kernel's repulsive force is too weak to prevent mode collapse and that gradient computation is an unnecessary bottleneck when GPU-parallel fitness evaluations are available.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Generating diverse, high-quality ergodic trajectory sets is critical for robots operating in dynamic environments where a single planned path may become infeasible; this paper provides a principled, GPU-scalable recipe for doing so without manual tuning (median heuristic for bandwidth, no analytic gradients required). Key limitations temper the impact: the signature kernel's strong repulsion can push trajectories into obstacles or outside the domain (visible in the MPC experiment's higher average cost), SV-CMA-ES requires an SVD or Cholesky factorization per iteration that limits real-time scalability, and the method lacks the non-asymptotic convergence guarantees available for SVGD. Open questions include whether diagonal-covariance CMA-ES variants can reduce per-iteration cost, whether the Fréchet-distance kernel (used only for measuring diversity here) could itself serve as an optimization kernel, and how the approach scales to very high-dimensional state spaces.
