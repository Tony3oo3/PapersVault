## Problem

Sampling-based test-time scaling methods (e.g., self-consistency and perplexity) improve LLM reasoning by generating multiple reasoning paths and selecting the best via confidence estimation, yet their theoretical foundations remain unexplored. Self-consistency suffers from only linear convergence of estimation error, while perplexity exhibits high model error and a degradation issue where its exponential convergence advantage collapses for low-probability paths. This paper provides the first theoretical framework decomposing reasoning error into estimation error and model error, revealing these limitations and guiding the design of an improved method.

## Input / Output

Given a reasoning problem query x, the method samples n reasoning paths from an LLM and extracts the internal token-level probabilities p(t̃ᵢ|x) for each path. An extraction function g(·) maps each path to a candidate answer, and the method computes a confidence score per candidate answer by summing the internal probabilities of all paths mapping to that answer. After pruning low-probability paths via a Weibull mixture model, the highest-confidence answer is selected in a Best-of-N manner. The method is hyperparameter-free: the pruning threshold is determined automatically from the sampled probability distribution.

## Method

RPC combines two components. Perplexity Consistency (PC) replaces SC's uniform vote-counting with a probability-weighted sum: for each candidate answer ŷ, the confidence is the sum of internal LLM probabilities over all sampled reasoning paths whose extracted answer equals ŷ. This yields an exponential convergence rate for estimation error (governed by αⁿ where α = 1 − p(ŷ|x)/k) while preserving SC's low model error. Reasoning Pruning (RP) addresses the degeneration issue when p(ŷ|x)→0 by fitting a two-component Weibull mixture to the distribution of sampled path probabilities, then removing paths whose posterior probability of belonging to the high-probability component falls below 0.5. A truncated-mean safeguard prevents over-pruning when n is small. Theoretically, RPC achieves both fast estimation error convergence and reduced model error, and empirically reduces sampling budget by 50% while matching or exceeding SC accuracy across seven benchmarks.

## Results

On four mathematical reasoning benchmarks (MATH, MathOdyssey, OlympiadBench, AIME) using InternLM2-Math-Plus 7B, RPC achieves an average accuracy of 26.11% and average ECE of 12.37, outperforming self-consistency (SC) by +1.29% average accuracy and reducing ECE from 13.37 to 12.37. RPC dramatically outperforms perplexity (PPL, 21.90% avg accuracy, 73.14 ECE) and verbalized confidence (VERB, 10.76% avg accuracy, 72.09 ECE). In terms of efficiency, RPC matches or exceeds SC's best performance while reducing the required sampling budget by 50–71% across datasets (e.g., 32 samples vs. 64 on MATH, 32 vs. 112 on MathOdyssey).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RPC outperforms self-consistency (SC) on all four math benchmarks: +1.38% on MATH (51.95 vs 50.57), +3.37% on MathOdyssey (31.62 vs 28.25), +0.07% on OlympiadBench (11.14 vs 11.07), and +0.34% on AIME (9.74 vs 9.40), while also achieving lower ECE on three of four datasets. Against perplexity (PPL), RPC wins decisively on every metric — average accuracy improves from 21.90% to 26.11% and average ECE drops from 73.14 to 12.37, confirming that PPL's high model error severely limits its effectiveness. Verbalized confidence (VERB) is the weakest baseline, with RPC exceeding it by over 15% average accuracy (26.11 vs 10.76) and reducing ECE by roughly 60 points. Notably, RPC achieves these gains while requiring 50–71% fewer samples than SC to reach comparable or better performance, as shown in the efficiency comparison (Table 1).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on two established confidence estimation paradigms for LLM reasoning: self-consistency (Wang et al., ICLR 2022), which uses Monte Carlo agreement across sampled paths, and perplexity (Chen et al., 1998), which leverages internal LLM token probabilities. Rather than proposing yet another empirical variant, the paper introduces the first unified theoretical framework that decomposes reasoning error into estimation error and model error, revealing why SC converges only linearly while PPL converges exponentially but suffers larger model error. The proposed RPC method fuses both paradigms—weighting consistency counts by internal probability—and borrows the Weibull mixture model from open-set recognition (Bendale & Boult, 2016) to automate pruning of low-probability reasoning paths.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The error decomposition provides a principled lens for designing future test-time scaling methods, shifting the field from ad-hoc empirical tuning toward theoretically grounded algorithm design. The 50% sampling reduction at matched accuracy is practically significant for deployment costs, and the automatic Weibull-based threshold selection removes a manual hyperparameter. However, the analysis relies on a Bernoulli sampling assumption and is validated primarily on math reasoning and code generation with 1.8B–33B models; whether the framework and RPC generalize to open-ended generation, multi-turn dialogue, or much larger models remains open. The modest average accuracy gain (~1.3%) over SC suggests the method's primary value is efficiency and calibration rather than raw accuracy.
