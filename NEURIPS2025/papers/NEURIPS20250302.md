## Problem

Scale-wise autoregressive image generation (e.g., VAR) relies on residual prediction, where each step predicts the difference between the current and previous scale. This rigid design forces a fixed number of steps and resolution, preventing flexible generation at arbitrary resolutions, aspect ratios, or inference step counts. The paper asks whether residual prediction is actually necessary, hypothesizing that the ground-truth of each scale can be directly estimated from prior scales, thereby decoupling each step and enabling independent, plausible image output at any stage.

## Input / Output

The model takes a class label (ImageNet-1K) as the primary conditioning signal, and optionally a low-resolution image or mask region for zero-shot image-to-image tasks (refinement, in/out-painting, expansion). It produces RGB images at arbitrary resolutions and aspect ratios—trained only on ≤256×256 pixels, it can generate 384px, 512px, or non-square outputs by adjusting the scale sequence at inference. The autoregressive process operates on multi-scale discrete token maps (16× downsampled latent space, codebook size 8912), with the number of inference steps flexibly ranging from 6 to 15 without retraining.

## Method

FlexVAR replaces VAR's residual prediction with direct ground-truth prediction: at each autoregressive step i, the model predicts the full token map g_i conditioned on all prior scales {g_1, …, g_{i-1}}, rather than the residual r_i = g_i − Upsample(g_{i-1}). Two supporting components enable this: (1) a scalable VQVAE tokenizer trained with multi-scale downsampling constraints so the same codebook and decoder reconstruct images at any latent resolution, and (2) a 2D scalable positional embedding (learnable queries initialized with sin-cosine weights, set to 2× the max training latent size) that is upsampled/downsampled per step, eliminating the need for fixed-length step embeddings. During training, scale sizes are randomly sampled per step (6–10 steps, 5% drop probability), and the model is trained with standard next-scale cross-entropy loss. This design ensures semantic coherence between adjacent scales and allows each step to independently yield a valid image, enabling zero-shot transfer to higher resolutions, novel aspect ratios, and additional inference steps.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K 256×256, FlexVAR-d24 (1.0B) achieves 2.21 FID and 299.1 IS with 10 inference steps, outperforming its VAR-d24 counterpart (2.33 FID) by 0.12 FID. With zero-shot transfer to 13 steps, FlexVAR-d24 further improves to 2.08 FID and 315.7 IS, surpassing the best raster-scan AR model AiM (2.56 FID) by 0.48 FID and the best diffusion model DiT-XL/2 (2.27 FID) by 0.19 FID. On the ImageNet-512×512 benchmark, FlexVAR-d24 achieves 4.43 FID zero-shot (trained only at ≤256px), competitive with the fully supervised VAR-d36 (2.63 FID, 2.3B params).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FlexVAR consistently outperforms its direct VAR counterparts at every model scale: −0.50 FID at 310M (3.05 vs 3.55), −0.54 FID at 600M (2.41 vs 2.95), and −0.12 FID at 1.0B (2.21 vs 2.33). With zero-shot 13-step inference, FlexVAR-d24 reaches 2.08 FID, beating the best raster-scan AR model AiM (2.56 FID) by 0.48 FID, the best diffusion model DiT-XL/2 (2.27 FID) by 0.19 FID, and LDM-4-G (3.60 FID) by 1.52 FID, while using only 13 inference steps versus 250 for diffusion models. Against GANs, FlexVAR-d24 (2.08 FID) surpasses StyleGAN-XL (2.30 FID) and GigaGAN (3.45 FID). On the 512×512 zero-shot benchmark, FlexVAR-d24 (4.43 FID, 1.0B, training-free) approaches VAR-d36 (2.63 FID, 2.3B, fully supervised at 512px), demonstrating strong cross-resolution generalization. Inference time is comparable to VAR (0.5× relative to VAR-d30 at 10 steps), making FlexVAR both faster and higher-quality than token-wise AR methods like LlamaGen (27×) and AiM (12×).

## Relation to Prior Work

FlexVAR directly challenges the residual prediction paradigm established by VAR (Tian et al., 2024) and adopted by all its successors (VAR-CLIP, M-VAR, ImageFolder, etc.), replacing it with ground-truth prediction at each scale. The scalable VQVAE tokenizer is initialized from and follows the training recipe of LlamaGen (Sun et al., 2024), while the overall scale-wise autoregressive framework (predicting an entire token map per step rather than individual tokens) is inherited from VAR. The key conceptual departure is the argument that residuals between adjacent scales are unnecessary because the GT of the current scale can be reliably estimated from prior scales, and that this simpler formulation inherently provides semantic coherence and step flexibility that the residual design rigidly constrains.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a single model trained at ≤256px can zero-shot generate images at arbitrary resolutions (up to 512px), aspect ratios, and inference step counts, and handle image-to-image tasks (inpainting, outpainting, refinement, expansion) without fine-tuning—capabilities that the residual paradigm structurally prevents. The zero-shot 512×512 result (4.43 FID) is competitive with VAR-d36 (2.63 FID, 2.3B params, fully supervised at 512×512), suggesting the GT paradigm may scale favorably. However, the VAE reconstruction quality is notably weaker than VAR's (3.79 vs 1.92 rFID), and generation degrades with wavy textures beyond 3× training resolution, pointing to dataset homogeneity as a limiting factor. Open questions include whether a stronger multi-scale VAE or a more diverse training set could close the remaining quality gap and enable stable generation at even higher resolutions.
