## Problem

Fake Image Detection and Localization (FIDL) has fragmented into four isolated domains—Deepfake, IMDL, AIGC, and Document—each with independent datasets, models, and evaluation protocols that lack interoperability. No unified benchmark exists to enable cross-domain comparison, creating domain silos that prevent researchers from identifying shared patterns, transferring methods across tasks, or developing general-purpose detectors.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252167/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

ForensicHub accepts RGB images across four forensic domains: face crops for Deepfake, natural images for IMDL, AI-generated images for AIGC, and document images (receipts, certificates, ID cards) for Document. Outputs are either image-level binary labels (real/fake) for detection tasks or pixel-level segmentation masks for localization tasks. Under the IFF-Protocol, images are resized to 256×256 (512×512 for document tasks), and models that natively output masks are converted to image-level predictions via max-pooling for unified evaluation.

## Method

ForensicHub proposes a modular, configuration-driven architecture decomposing forensic pipelines into four interchangeable components—Datasets, Transforms, Models, and Evaluators—allowing any model to be composed with any dataset via YAML configuration without code changes. An adapter-based design integrates existing benchmarks (DeepfakeBench with 22 models, IMDLBenCo with 10 models) while introducing new AIGC and Document benchmarks, yielding 42 models, 23 datasets, 6 backbones, and 11 GPU-accelerated metrics. The Image Forensic Fusion Protocol (IFF-Protocol) trains models on a balanced mixture of all four domains (sampling equal instances per epoch from FaceForensics++, CASIAv2, GenImage, and four document datasets) and evaluates without fine-tuning on held-out domain-specific test sets, enabling 16 kinds of cross-domain evaluations. Key empirical findings include that generic backbones (ConvNeXt, Swin) outperform nearly all domain-specific SoTAs under unified training, and that shallow feature extractors (Sobel, BayarConv, DCT, FPH) generally degrade performance on large diverse datasets but benefit lightweight models like EfficientNet.

## Results

Under the proposed IFF-Protocol (unified training on all four forensic domains), general-purpose backbones ConvNeXt (0.822 avg AUC) and Swin Transformer (0.824 avg AUC) outperform nearly all domain-specific SoTA methods, with the best overall performer being Effort [73] at 0.892 avg AUC. On the AIGC benchmark, IML-ViT achieves the best cross-domain average AUC of 0.896, while on the Document benchmark, FFDN leads with 0.698 average F1 across all seven test sets. A key finding is that shallow feature extractors (Sobel, BayarConv, DCT, FPH) consistently degrade performance for most backbones under the unified protocol, with only lightweight EfficientNet showing marginal gains.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The most striking comparison is between general backbones and domain-specific SoTAs: ConvNeXt (0.822) and Swin (0.824) surpass all IMDL models (MVSS-Net 0.736, TruFor 0.735, IML-ViT 0.753, Mesorch 0.749) and most Deepfake models (Capsule-Net 0.674, SPSL 0.754) under the unified IFF-Protocol, suggesting that domain-specific inductive biases are less critical when training data is diverse. Effort [73] (0.892) and CO-SPY [7] (0.829) represent the strongest AIGC-oriented methods that also transfer well, while FFDN [6] (0.824) shows that document-specific frequency decomposition generalizes surprisingly well to AIGC (1.000 AUC on GenImage). In the AIGC cross-domain setting (Table 3), IML-ViT (0.896 avg) and DualNet (0.890) outperform native AIGC methods like HiFiNet (0.816) and Synthbuster (0.576), indicating that IMDL techniques (noise fingerprints, multi-scale analysis) transfer effectively to generative image detection. For Document forensics, PSCC-Net [34] demonstrates the best cross-domain generalization (0.408 avg F1 on external datasets) despite moderate within-domain performance (0.425), while FFDN [6] leads within-domain (0.813) but drops to 0.301 cross-domain, highlighting a generalization gap. UnivFD [45] (CLIP-based) shows notable cross-task transferability, achieving 0.886 AUC on IMD2020 (IMDL) despite being designed for AIGC detection, suggesting that pretrained vision-language features capture manipulation cues beyond their training domain.

## Relation to Prior Work

ForensicHub unifies four previously siloed FIDL sub-domains (Deepfake, IMDL, AIGC, Document) by adapting existing domain-specific benchmarks—DeepfakeBench and IMDLBenCo—through an adapter-based design, while introducing the first standardized benchmarks for AIGC and Document, which previously lacked widely accepted evaluation protocols. The IFF-Protocol, inspired by CAT-Net's mixed-domain training strategy, is a new cross-domain evaluation mechanism that has no direct predecessor. Rather than proposing a new detection architecture, the paper repurposes 42 existing models and 6 generic backbones under a common modular framework to enable systematic cross-domain comparison that was previously impossible.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By providing the first unified evaluation infrastructure spanning 4 tasks, 23 datasets, and 42 models, ForensicHub eliminates redundant per-domain benchmarking and enables the first systematic cross-domain comparisons in FIDL. The finding that generic backbones (ConvNeXt, Swin) outperform nearly all domain-specific SoTAs under the IFF-Protocol challenges the field's reliance on specialized architectures and handcrafted feature extractors, suggesting that data diversity may matter more than architectural specialization. Key open questions include whether deeper fusion strategies (beyond shallow concatenation) can recover the benefit of feature extractors, whether the IFF-Protocol's equal-sampling strategy is optimal, and how findings generalize to video-based or multi-modal forensic settings. The paper acknowledges limitations: single-run experiments without error bars, and only shallow feature extractor fusion tested.
