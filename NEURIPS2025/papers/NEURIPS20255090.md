## Problem

A 30-year-old open problem asked how much advance access to unlabeled data (the transductive setting) reduces the optimal mistake bound in online learning compared to the standard setting, where the bound is characterized by the Littlestone dimension d. Prior work left an exponential gap between the best lower bound of Ω(log d) and the best upper bound of (2/3)d, leaving the true separation unknown. This paper resolves the problem by proving a tight Θ(√d) bound, establishing a quadratic gap between transductive and standard online learning.

## Input / Output

In the transductive setting, the learner receives the full sequence of n unlabeled instances x_1, …, x_n ∈ X before any predictions are made, and must output a binary prediction ŷ_t ∈ {0,1} for each instance sequentially while the adversary adaptively assigns realizable labels. The theoretical output is a characterization of the optimal mistake bound M_tr(H) as a function of the Littlestone dimension d = LD(H). Unlike the standard setting where instances arrive one-by-one, the transductive learner's access to the entire instance sequence is the sole source of its advantage, and the adversary's power is correspondingly reduced since it cannot adaptively alter the sequence mid-game.

## Method

The lower bound constructs a sequence of nodes in a Littlestone tree of depth d with length 2^Θ(√d), paired with a greedy adversary that forces a mistake whenever the version-space ratio r_t falls in [ε, 1−ε] and otherwise assigns the majority label; the sequence is carefully built so that the version space shrinks to exactly one function by the end, yielding M = Ω(√d) via a counting argument. The upper bound uses the probabilistic method to construct a class of size 2^{d+1} where off-path labels are sampled i.i.d. from Ber(2^{−√d}), creating a 'sparse encoding' of the true function across off-path nodes. The learning algorithm combines three mechanisms: (1) danger zone minimization, which shrinks a subset of early-sequence nodes by a factor of 2/3 per mistake (analogous to Halving on the domain rather than the version space); (2) splitting experts via multiplicative weights, where each expert maintains path-related assumptions and splits when a node's on/off-path status is undetermined, guaranteeing one expert always has correct assumptions; and (3) a transition to standard Halving once the surviving version space drops to 2^{O(√d)} functions, which occurs after O(√d) off-path-1 labels or after the first 2^{Ω(√d)} labels are revealed.

## Results

The paper establishes that the optimal transductive online learning mistake bound is Θ(√d) where d is the Littlestone dimension of the hypothesis class. Specifically, for every class H with LD(H) = d, the transductive mistake bound is at least Ω(√d) (Theorem B.1), and there exists a class with LD(H) = d whose transductive mistake bound is O(√d) (Theorem D.1). This resolves a 30-year open problem by closing the gap between the previous best lower bound of Ω(log d) and upper bound of (2/3)d, establishing a tight quadratic separation between transductive and standard online learning (√d vs. d mistakes).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's lower bound of Ω(√d) improves upon the previous best lower bound of Ω(log d) by Hanneke, Moran, and Shafer (2023), which itself improved over Ω(√log d) by Ben-David, Kushilevitz, and Mansour (1997) and Ω(log log d) by Ben-David et al. (1995). The upper bound of O(√d) dramatically improves upon the previous best upper bound of (2/3)·d from Ben-David, Kushilevitz, and Mansour (1997), reducing the gap from linear to square-root in d. The combined result establishes a tight Θ(√d) characterization, proving a quadratic gap between transductive and standard online learning. This contrasts with the PAC setting, where transductive and standard learning exhibit similar sample complexities, highlighting that the benefit of unlabeled data is specific to the online/adversarial setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper resolves a 30-year-old open problem in transductive online learning, a setting first formalized by Ben-David, Kushilevitz, and Mansour (1995, 1997), who established an Ω(log log d) lower bound and a (2/3)d upper bound. Hanneke, Moran, and Shafer (2023) improved the lower bound to Ω(log d), but a large gap remained. The present work closes this gap by proving a tight Θ(√d) characterization, using an adversarial version-space analysis for the lower bound and a probabilistic construction with sparse off-path encodings for the upper bound. The upper bound algorithm combines danger zone minimization (analogous to Halving), splitting experts (a multiplicative weights variant), and a transition to standard Halving once sufficient information is extracted from the sparse encoding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result establishes a clean quadratic gap between transductive and standard online learning, showing that advance access to unlabeled instances provides a fundamentally larger benefit in the online setting than in the PAC setting, where transductive and standard sample complexities are comparable. This sharpens the understanding of when and how unlabeled data accelerates learning. Key open questions remain: whether an efficient (polynomial-time) algorithm achieves the O(√d) upper bound, whether the domain size can be reduced from 2^d to poly(d), and whether a precise constant α exists such that the optimal bound is (α+o(1))√d.
