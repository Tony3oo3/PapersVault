## Problem

Existing model-heterogeneous federated learning (Hetero-FL) methods rely on logit distillation to aggregate knowledge from client models with different architectures, but this approach fails to align the distinct hidden-layer feature spaces produced by heterogeneous models, leading to training instability and sub-optimal knowledge transfer. The paper identifies that logit representations, being model-agnostic at the output layer, cannot compensate for the structural knowledge bias inherent in aggregating predictions from architecturally diverse networks.

## Input / Output

The system processes standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 64×64 for Tiny-ImageNet) and outputs class labels. During the distillation phase, the server consumes intermediate feature vectors (not raw images) extracted by each client's feature extractor on a shared distillation dataset; these d-dimensional feature vectors are the actual knowledge units being aligned and distilled, with the global ResNet-18 model's features projected into each client architecture's feature space before KL-divergence matching.

## Method

FedFD replaces logit distillation with feature distillation using two key mechanisms. First, hierarchical feature alignment groups clients by architecture and averages their feature representations, reducing the number of required projection layers from one-per-client to one-per-architecture (m−1 layers for m distinct architectures). Second, each projection layer is parameterized as the truncated exponential of a skew-symmetric matrix (a Stiefel manifold element), guaranteeing orthogonality and thus rotational invariance of the feature mapping, which prevents knowledge conflicts between different client feature spaces. The distillation loss minimizes KL divergence between the orthogonally projected global model features and the aggregated client features, jointly updating the global model parameters and the projection matrices via back-propagation. The global model (ResNet-18) is trained on the server while clients use smaller ResNet variants with 10% channel-width decay levels, and the entire framework operates as a modular add-on to any Hetero-FL aggregation protocol.

## Results

FedFD achieves the best global model accuracy across all three datasets and all data heterogeneity levels. On CIFAR-10 (α=1.0), FedFD reaches 89.64% global accuracy, outperforming the strongest baseline FedGD (87.22%) by 2.42 points. On CIFAR-100 (α=1.0), FedFD achieves 60.86% versus 58.03% for FedGD, and on Tiny-ImageNet (α=1.0) it reaches 34.24% versus 30.66%. Under the most heterogeneous setting (α=0.1), FedFD's advantage grows, with up to 16.09% absolute improvement over FedMD on CIFAR-10 (82.74% vs. 66.67%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedFD consistently outperforms all eight baselines across every dataset and heterogeneity level. Against the closest logit-distillation baselines, FedFD leads FedGD by 2.42 points on CIFAR-10 (α=1.0), 2.83 on CIFAR-100, and 3.58 on Tiny-ImageNet; the gap widens under high heterogeneity (α=0.1) to 3.43, 2.90, and 1.56 points respectively. The data-free distillation methods (FedGen-hetero, DaFKD-hetero) trail FedFD by 2.83 and 1.80 points on CIFAR-10 (α=1.0), with DaFKD being the strongest among them. FedMD, which relies on direct logit aggregation without parameter sharing, is the weakest baseline, falling 14.16 points behind FedFD on CIFAR-10 (α=1.0) and up to 16.07 points at α=0.1. The classic HeteroFL and MOON-hetero baselines, which do not employ distillation, are outperformed by 2.11 and 1.72 points on CIFAR-10 (α=1.0) respectively. Notably, FedFD also converges fastest in communication rounds (10±5 rounds to reach 70% on CIFAR-10 vs. 17±8 for FedGD), confirming both accuracy and efficiency gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the HeteroFL framework [6] for aggregating heterogeneous client models and the ensemble distillation paradigm from FedFusion [33], but departs fundamentally from the logit-based distillation that dominates prior Hetero-FL work (FedMD, MSFKD, FedGD). It draws on the orthogonal projection idea from VKD [35] for centralized knowledge distillation and adapts it to the federated setting via a skew-symmetric matrix exponential on the Stiefel manifold, combined with hierarchical feature alignment that groups clients by architecture to reduce the number of projection layers. The key conceptual departure is arguing that logit distillation is structurally inadequate for heterogeneous models because it cannot align the distinct feature spaces each architecture produces.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The demonstration that standard logit ensemble distillation causes training instability and non-convergence in Hetero-FL is practically important for anyone deploying federated learning across heterogeneous edge devices, as it invalidates a widely-used technique in this setting. FedFD's modular design (plug-in add-on to any FL aggregation scheme) makes it broadly applicable, but the approach requires a distillation dataset and has only been validated on relatively small image classification benchmarks (CIFAR-10/100, Tiny-ImageNet). Open questions include scalability to larger datasets and more architectures, applicability to non-vision modalities, and whether the orthogonal projection overhead becomes prohibitive with many distinct model families.
