## Problem

Existing LLM unlearning methods suppress undesired capabilities rather than removing them, making them vulnerable to relearning attacks where a few steps of finetuning restore the supposedly forgotten knowledge. The paper shows this fragility persists even under oracle matching—training a model to perfectly imitate one that never saw the unwanted data—because behavioral alignment leaves latent capabilities intact in the parameter space. The central question is whether there exists a practical procedure that achieves robust capability removal without the infeasible cost of full data filtering and retraining.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pretrained LLM as the reference model, small labeled retain and forget sets (less than 0.01% of pretraining data), and a large unlabeled pretraining corpus used for distillation. The output is a new model evaluated on two axes: retain performance (cross-entropy loss or accuracy on desired capabilities) and elicited forget performance (forget capability recovered after adversarial finetuning at multiple learning rates). The distillation step operates on token-level output distributions via forward KL divergence, requiring the teacher to produce logits over the full vocabulary at each position.

## Method

UNDO (Unlearn-Noise-Distill-on-Outputs) is a three-step procedure: (1) Unlearn—apply a standard unlearning method (MaxEnt, GradDiff, or RMU) to suppress forget behavior in the reference model, creating a teacher; (2) Noise—create a student by corrupting the suppressed model's weights via θ_perturbed = (1−α)θ_suppressed + αβN, where α interpolates between the original parameters and full random initialization and β scales Xavier-sampled noise; (3) Distill—repair the damaged student by training it on the teacher's output distributions using forward KL divergence until it recovers ≥95% of the teacher's retain performance. The key insight is that distilling into a fresh or noised parameter space transfers the teacher's input-output behavior while discarding the latent capabilities that made the original model vulnerable to relearning. The mixing coefficient α provides a tunable compute-robustness tradeoff: higher α produces more robust unlearning but requires more distillation steps to recover retain performance.

## Results

On synthetic language (English/Korean) and arithmetic (add-sub/mul-div) tasks, UNDO (α=0.6, β=0.1, built on MaxEnt) matches the relearning robustness of a gold-standard model retrained from scratch with perfect data filtering while using only 60–80% of the compute and requiring only 0.01% of pretraining data to be labeled. In the arithmetic setting, MaxEnt alone achieves 80% retain / 1.3% forget accuracy, while RMU achieves 62% / 6.8%; adding the UNDO distillation step substantially flattens relearning curves for both. On the WMDP benchmark (Gemma-2-2B), UNDO (α=0.25, β=0) sits on the Pareto frontier for WMDP-Bio and is competitive for WMDP-Cyber, at a cost of 4.88% lower average MMLU accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UNDO (MaxEnt + distillation, α=0.6, β=0.1) clearly outperforms all unlearn-only baselines (GradDiff, MaxEnt, RMU) on relearning robustness: while these methods achieve good initial retain/forget trade-offs, they rapidly degrade under even 40 steps of adversarial finetuning, whereas UNDO models show minimal forget recovery across all learning rates tested. Against the robust-unlearning baselines SAM and RepNoise, UNDO maintains flatter relearning curves under stronger attacks (500 steps), while SAM and RepNoise show significant performance deterioration. UNDIAL, which also uses distillation but without global parameter corruption, is outperformed by UNDO because UNDO's noising step removes latent capabilities that UNDIAL's self-distillation leaves intact. On the WMDP benchmark, UNDO is on the Pareto frontier for WMDP-Bio and competitive (not strictly dominant) for WMDP-Cyber, with a 4.88% MMLU cost; the authors attribute the weaker WMDP-Cyber result to under-training (distilling on only 0.015% of the original 2T-token pretraining corpus) rather than a fundamental limitation of the method.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the knowledge distillation framework of Hinton et al. [30] and the suite of LLM unlearning methods (GradDiff/TOFU [50], MaxEnt [44], RMU [42]) by inserting a distillation step after unlearning. It departs conceptually from the dominant paradigm in robust unlearning—modifying an existing model's parameters to suppress behavior (via gradient ascent, entropy maximization, representation misdirection, or sharpness-aware minimization [23, 61])—by showing that the critical operation is not the unlearning objective itself but the transfer of behavior into a fresh parameter space that never encoded the latent capability. The oracle-matching experiments connect to Georgiev et al. [25], extending their simulated oracle framework to demonstrate that even perfect behavioral agreement is insufficient for robustness.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical implication is significant: since distillation is already a standard step in LLM development pipelines (for efficiency, model compression, and deployment), inserting an unlearning step before distillation provides a low-friction path to robust capability removal without requiring full data filtering and retraining. The UNDO framework's tunable compute-robustness tradeoff (via the α perturbation parameter) gives practitioners a dial to match their budget. Key limitations include that WMDP results on Gemma-2-2B show UNDO is competitive but not clearly superior to baselines when only 0.015% of the original pretraining corpus is available for distillation, and all primary experiments use models ≤300M parameters, leaving open whether the effect persists at frontier scale. An open question is whether the robustness gain is purely from the fresh parameter space or whether the specific distillation data distribution also plays a role.
