## Problem

Existing coding benchmarks like LiveCodeBench and CodeElo have become saturated by current reasoning models (often scoring >97th percentile), losing discriminative power, while their evaluation pipelines rely on inaccessible online judges and the Pass@K metric, which cannot capture the iterative, feedback-driven refinement process that even top human ICPC competitors rely on (averaging ~2 submissions per solved problem).
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255746/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives a full ICPC problem statement in unified LaTeX format (task description, input/output specifications, sample I/O, time/memory limits) on the first turn; on subsequent turns it additionally receives its own previous code and structured execution feedback (compile error messages, sample-test mismatches with expected outputs, or hidden-test error types such as Wrong Answer, Time Limit Exceeded, Runtime Error, Memory Limit Exceeded). The output is a complete C++23 program compiled with GNU GCC 14 (-std=c++23), and the evaluation yields a Refine@K score indicating whether the solution passes all hidden tests within K refinement attempts.

## Method

ICPC-Eval curates 118 problems from 11 recent ICPC contests (2023 World Finals plus 2024 Continental and Regional contests), filtering out interactive, image-dependent, or unsolvable problems and writing 12 custom special judges for floating-point or multi-solution outputs. To enable fully local evaluation without online judges, the paper uses Gemini 2.5 Pro to synthesize C++ input generators per problem—both uniform-random and corner-case variants—then runs a verified accepted solution to produce expected outputs and validates the entire test suite against a curated set of known-incorrect programs (WA, TLE, RE) to guarantee zero false positives. The key evaluation innovation is Refine@K, a multi-turn metric that simulates real ICPC dynamics: after the initial code generation, the model receives execution feedback and is prompted to iteratively repair its solution within a budget of K attempts, directly measuring a model's reflective reasoning ability rather than its one-shot sampling probability as in Pass@K.

## Results

On ICPC-Eval (118 problems from 11 recent ICPC contests), the best-performing model o3-mini High achieves a Refine@5 score of 28.8%, followed by Gemini 2.5 Pro Exp (22.0%) and DeepSeek R1 / Qwen3-32B (14.4% each). Non-reasoning models trail significantly, with Claude 3.5 Sonnet and DeepSeek V3 at 4.2%, and Qwen Max / Qwen2.5-Coder-32B at just 0.8%. The average number of correct response turns (#T) ranges from 1.0 (DeepSeek V3, Qwen2.5-Coder-32B) to 2.2 (Claude 3.7 Sonnet non-thinking, Claude 3.5 Sonnet), indicating that even top reasoning models rarely solve problems on the first attempt.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to LiveCodeBench, ICPC-Eval is substantially harder: o3-mini High drops from 67.4% (Pass@K) on LiveCodeBench to 28.8% (Refine@5) on ICPC-Eval, and Grok 3 Mini Beta falls from 66.7% to 11.8%, revealing much greater model differentiation. Against CodeElo, where Gemini 2.5 Pro Exp and DeepSeek R1 achieve 97.9% and 98.1% percentiles respectively, the same models score only 22.0% and 14.4% on ICPC-Eval, confirming that ICPC-level problems remain far beyond current model capabilities. In the Refine@K vs. Pass@K comparison (Section 5.2), reasoning models (QwQ-32B, DeepSeek-R1) consistently outperform under Refine@K across all K values, while non-reasoning counterparts (Qwen-2.5-Coder-32B, DeepSeek-V3) show Pass@K significantly exceeding Refine@K, demonstrating that Refine@K captures feedback-driven iterative reasoning that Pass@K cannot. A small reasoning model (Qwen3-1.7B) benefits from increasing K under Refine@K (1→4 problems solved from K=1 to K=20), whereas the larger non-reasoning models show minimal or no gain, supporting that test-time scaling and parameter scaling follow distinct dynamics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of competitive programming benchmarks and test-time scaling evaluation. It departs from the Pass@K paradigm (introduced by HumanEval and widely adopted by LiveCodeBench, CodeElo, and others) by proposing Refine@K, which models the iterative, feedback-driven refinement process that mirrors actual ICPC competition dynamics. Unlike CodeElo and ProBench, which require submission to online judges and thus limit local reproducibility, ICPC-Eval introduces an LLM-based test case generation pipeline with special judge support, enabling fully offline evaluation. The benchmark also addresses the difficulty ceiling problem: existing benchmarks like LiveCodeBench and USACO no longer discriminate among top reasoning models, whereas ICPC-level problems maintain a meaningful performance spread.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark exposes a substantial and persistent gap between state-of-the-art reasoning models and human ICPC medalists (o3-mini High solves ~29% of problems vs. medalists solving the majority), suggesting that current LLMs have not yet reached competition-level algorithmic reasoning. The Refine@K metric reveals a fundamental asymmetry: reasoning models genuinely benefit from execution feedback and scale with attempt budget, while non-reasoning models are often *hurt* by prior incorrect responses, implying that 'reflection' is a qualitatively distinct capability rather than a byproduct of scale. Key limitations include C++-only evaluation, exclusion of interactive and image-dependent problems, and geographic concentration in Asian/European/North American contests; extending to multimodal, interactive, and multilingual settings remains an open direction.
