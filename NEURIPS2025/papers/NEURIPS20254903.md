## Problem

Maritime object detection suffers from two intertwined limitations: annotated maritime data is expensive to collect across heterogeneous platforms (ships, UAVs, coastal cameras) and labor-intensive to label, and existing datasets exhibit severe imbalances across semantic attributes (viewpoint, location, weather, object category) that degrade generalization. Standard data augmentation cannot produce novel semantic instances, and existing layout-to-image diffusion methods treat object regions independently, ignoring the critical physical coupling between maritime objects and their aquatic surroundings, leading to implausible generations (e.g., ships floating in mid-air).

## Input / Output

The generator consumes three condition types simultaneously: a free-form text caption describing the scene (weather, viewpoint, time of day), per-object bounding boxes with category labels (ship, buoy, person, floating object, fixed object), and a binary water surface mask with an accompanying water description. Outputs are 512×512 RGB maritime images. Downstream, the framework produces a curated subset of high-value synthetic samples (selected via difficulty-aware ranking) that are combined with the original training set to fine-tune object detectors (YOLOv10/11/12, Grounding DINO), yielding bounding boxes and category predictions on test images.

## Method

Neptune-X is a two-stage generation-then-selection pipeline built on Stable Diffusion. The generation stage introduces a Bidirectional Object-Water Attention (BiOW-Attn) module inserted into the U-Net: in a first stage, cross-attention independently fuses object embeddings (Fourier-encoded coordinates + CLIP-encoded labels, projected via MLP) and water-surface embeddings into spatially masked feature maps; in a second stage, the object and water feature maps exchange query and key-value roles in a bidirectional cross-attention, explicitly modeling boundary interactions so that objects appear physically grounded on the water surface. Only the layout embedders and BiOW-Attn are trained (SD weights frozen), keeping the base generative capability intact. The selection stage computes Attribute-correlated Training Difficulty Factors (ATDFs) across four dimensions (category, viewpoint, location, imaging environment) via EMA-smoothed per-attribute error during a short pretraining phase, then ranks all filtered synthetic images by a composite difficulty score (product of non-category ATDFs × mean category-weighted accuracy deficit) and selects the top-k highest-difficulty samples for fine-tuning, achieving detection gains comparable to using twice as many randomly sampled images.

## Results

On the Maritime Generation Dataset (MGD), Neptune-X achieves the best FID of 18.05, CAS of 79.34, and YOLO Score of 17.08/39.14/13.52 (mAP/mAP50/mAP75), outperforming all layout-to-image baselines. For downstream detection, adding Neptune-X-generated data to YOLOv10 improves mAP from 39.99 to 43.62 (+9.08%) and mAP50 from 61.13 to 65.50 (+7.15%); similar gains are observed for YOLOv11 (+7.60% mAP) and YOLOv12 (+9.86% mAP). The AAS sampling strategy matches the performance of 10,000 randomly sampled instances using only 5,000 selected samples, demonstrating data efficiency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Neptune-X outperforms all five baselines on every generation metric. Against the closest FID competitor LayoutDiff (18.17), Neptune-X achieves a marginally lower FID (18.05) but a substantially higher CAS (79.34 vs. 63.77) and YOLO Score (17.08 vs. 0.83 mAP), indicating far superior object-level controllability. Compared to GLIGEN (the strongest text+box baseline), Neptune-X improves CAS by +2.28 and YOLO mAP by +4.34, with the gap widening to +8.78 on mAP50. Against mask-conditioned methods InstDiff and RC-L2I, Neptune-X leads by +4.34 and +8.33 in YOLO mAP respectively, while also achieving the best FID. In the downstream detection comparison (Table 8), Neptune-X-generated data yields mAP 43.62 on YOLOv10, surpassing the best baseline GLIGEN (41.54) by +2.08 mAP and LayoutDiff (40.03) by +3.59 mAP, confirming that the generation quality advantage translates directly into detection gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the layout-to-image generation line (GLIGEN, LayoutDiff, InstDiff, RC-L2I) by introducing a domain-specific Bidirectional Object-Water Attention module that explicitly models the physical interaction between maritime objects and their aquatic surroundings, rather than treating object regions independently as prior methods do. It also departs from generative data augmentation approaches (DA-Fusion, AeroGen) that focus solely on synthesis by coupling generation with a task-aware active sampling strategy (AAS) that uses attribute-correlated training difficulty factors to select high-value samples. The embedding strategy for layout conditions directly follows GLIGEN's Fourier + CLIP + MLP pipeline, and the base architecture is Stable Diffusion with frozen weights, making the contribution primarily in the attention mechanism and the selection paradigm rather than in the diffusion backbone itself.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in maritime perception—annotated data is expensive to collect across heterogeneous platforms (ships, UAVs, coastal cameras) and imbalanced across viewpoints, weather, and object categories. The MGD dataset (11,900 samples with water masks, multi-level captions, and bounding boxes) is the first generative benchmark for maritime scenes, and the finding that 5,000 AAS-selected synthetic samples match 10,000 random ones suggests a meaningful data-efficiency gain for practitioners. However, the approach is tightly coupled to the maritime domain (the BiOW-Attn module and ATDF dimensions are domain-specific), and the reliance on a fixed set of predefined attribute categories limits granularity of difficulty modeling, as the authors acknowledge. Whether the generation-selection paradigm transfers cleanly to other domains with analogous object-environment interactions (e.g., aerial, underwater) remains an open question.
