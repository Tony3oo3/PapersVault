## Problem

Current trajectory inference methods for spatial transcriptomics model single-cell dynamics in isolation, overlooking the coordinated evolution of cellular microenvironments (niches) that are functionally distinct neighborhoods shaped by cell-to-cell interactions. Because spatial transcriptomics acquisition is destructive, no direct cell-to-cell correspondence exists across time points, and existing velocity-based or single-cell OT approaches fundamentally miss how structured niches reorganize as cohesive units during development or disease progression.

## Input / Output

The method takes a source microenvironment defined as a fixed-radius neighborhood of cells (each with 2D spatial coordinates and a gene expression vector reduced to 50 principal components) plus Gaussian noise, and generates a target microenvironment at the next time point with predicted coordinates and expression profiles. Source and target point clouds can contain different numbers of cells (non-comparable spaces), and training uses mini-batches of spatially co-localized neighborhoods sampled via K-Means clustering over 2D coordinates to ensure tissue-wide coverage. The model is conditioned on source and target time-point labels to handle multi-step trajectories with a single network.

## Method

NicheFlow combines entropic optimal transport (EOT) with a factorized Variational Flow Matching (VFM) objective to learn a conditional generative flow from source to target microenvironments. Source-target pairing is computed via EOT over pooled microenvironment representations (weighted averages of coordinates and features balanced by hyperparameter λ), enabling scalable mini-batch training. The VFM posterior is fully factorized across points and dimensions under a mean-field assumption, with distinct distribution families: Laplace for spatial coordinates (L1 loss) and Gaussian for gene expression (L2 loss), so only the posterior mean needs to be predicted. The mean predictor μ_t^θ is a permutation-equivariant point cloud transformer with an encoder-decoder architecture: the encoder processes the source microenvironment via self-attention, and the decoder operates on the noisy target with cross-attention conditioning on encoder outputs. This design implicitly captures spatial correlations between cells within a niche while handling variable cell counts through the non-comparable-space EOT formulation.

## Results

NicheFlow with the Gaussian-Laplace VFM (GLVFM) objective achieves the best performance across all three datasets. On mouse embryonic development (MED), it reaches 0.664 1NN-F1, 0.883 PSD, and 0.398 SPD; on axolotl brain development (ABD), 0.628 1NN-F1, 2.079 PSD, and 0.576 SPD; and on mouse brain aging (MBA), 0.285 1NN-F1, 1.554 PSD, and 0.532 SPD. The SPD metric (shape-to-point distance) shows the largest relative improvement over baselines, e.g., 0.398 vs. 0.564–0.602 for SPFlow/RPCFlow on MED, indicating substantially better spatial coverage of target tissue structures.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NicheFlow (GLVFM) outperforms SPFlow (single-cell-level flow) by a wide margin on all metrics: on MED, 1NN-F1 improves from 0.251 to 0.664 and SPD from 0.592 to 0.398, demonstrating that modeling microenvironments as point clouds is critical for capturing tissue-level spatial organization. Against RPCFlow (same transformer backbone but random point clouds instead of radius neighborhoods), NicheFlow gains ~0.08 in 1NN-F1 on MED (0.664 vs. 0.586) and ~0.07 on ABD (0.628 vs. 0.554), confirming that spatially co-localized neighborhoods provide meaningful structural context. LUNA, a diffusion-based spatial reconstruction model without temporal dynamics, serves as a non-generative reference and achieves 0.540 1NN-F1 on MED, below NicheFlow's 0.664. In qualitative comparisons with moscot (exact OT at the single-cell level using Fused Gromov-Wasserstein), NicheFlow correctly maps spinal cord and neural crest cell trajectories to their expected anatomical targets, whereas moscot leaks substantial mass to unrelated regions (e.g., urogenital ridge, brain cells), highlighting the advantage of modeling coordinated niche evolution over independent cell transport.

## Relation to Prior Work

NicheFlow extends the Flow Matching + Optimal Transport paradigm (Lipman et al., Tong et al.) to the structured point-cloud setting, building directly on the source-conditioned Entropic OT formulation of GenOT (Klein et al.) for incomparable source-target spaces and the variational FM objective of Eijkelboom et al. The key departure from prior trajectory inference methods (moscot, DeST-OT, SpaTrack) is a shift in the unit of analysis: instead of transporting individual cells via exact OT or velocity fields, NicheFlow treats local fixed-radius neighborhoods as point clouds and learns a generative flow between them. The mixed-factorized VFM with Laplace posteriors for coordinates and Gaussian posteriors for gene expression is a novel extension of the mean-field VFM framework to mixed distribution families over structured data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By modeling microenvironments as cohesive units rather than independent cells, NicheFlow captures coordinated spatial and compositional dynamics that single-cell trajectory methods miss, as evidenced by reduced off-target mass leakage in anatomical mapping tasks. However, the approach is constrained by fixed-radius niche definitions, a single global λ weighting spatial vs. feature similarity, and in-distribution evaluation only, leaving open questions about generalization to unseen regions, handling of cell division/death, and rotational/translational invariance. The method could serve as a foundation for spatial perturbation prediction and modality translation if these limitations are addressed.
