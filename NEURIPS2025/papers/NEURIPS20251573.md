## Problem

Existing matrix factorization methods that incorporate side information (e.g., spatial coordinates, categorical features) rely on restrictive parametric models and strong structural assumptions—such as Gaussian process priors that enforce smooth spatial variation—making them inflexible across data types and sensitive to model misspecification. Moreover, these methods typically require manual hyperparameter tuning and cannot easily accommodate diverse covariate types (images, graphs, text). The paper asks whether a unified, assumption-light framework can adaptively learn how side information should influence the factorization from the data itself.

## Input / Output

The method takes a data matrix Z (n×p, which may contain missing values as in collaborative filtering), row covariate matrix X (n×n_x), column covariate matrix Y (p×n_y), a number of factors K, and pre-specified prior families G_ℓ,k and G_f,k for each factor. The covariates can be any structured data processable by a neural network, though in practice they are stored as numeric matrices. The output consists of the factor matrices L (n×K) and F (p×K), the learned covariate-moderated prior distributions for each factor, and residual variances; K can also be selected adaptively by shrinking unneeded factors toward zero.

## Method

cEBMF extends the Empirical Bayes Matrix Factorization (EBMF) framework by replacing fixed priors on the factor elements with covariate-moderated priors: for each factor k, the prior on ℓ_ik is a distribution g_k^(ℓ)(x_i) from a flexible family G_ℓ,k, parameterized by the row covariate x_i (and analogously for F using column covariates y_j). The fitting algorithm is a coordinate ascent on the ELBO under a mean-field variational approximation, where updating each factor k reduces to solving a covariate-moderated Empirical Bayes Normal Means (cEBNM) subproblem: the prior parameters θ are estimated by maximizing the marginal likelihood over the covariate-conditioned prior, and posterior moments are then computed. The mapping from covariates to prior parameters can be any model (logistic regression, multilayer perceptron, CNN, GNN), and the prior family can encode sparsity (spike-and-slab), non-negativity (mixture-of-exponentials), or other constraints. This modular design means that any solver for the cEBNM subproblem can be plugged in, and hyperparameters are learned automatically via empirical Bayes rather than tuned by cross-validation.

## Results

In simulations across four scenarios (100 replicates each), cEBMF achieved the lowest RMSE in all settings where covariates were informative, with the largest gains over EBMF in the tiled-clustering scenario. On MovieLens 100K collaborative filtering, cEBNMF (cEBMF with non-negative constraints) yielded the best overall prediction RMSE across all training-set proportions, outperforming EBNMF, MFAI, and deep learning baselines (VAE, cVAE, NCF). In the scalability benchmark, cEBMF processed 1 million rows in ~3,404 s, whereas Spatial PCA required ~300 GB of memory at 100,000 rows and MFAI crashed in 8 of 10 runs at that scale.

## Baselines & Comparisons

cEBMF consistently outperforms EBMF [13] when side information is informative (sparsity-driven and tiled-clustering simulations) while matching EBMF when covariates are uninformative or the prior is misspecified, confirming robustness. Against MFAI [17], cEBMF wins in most simulation scenarios and on MovieLens 100K; MFAI is a special case of cEBMF with a single prior family and gradient-boosted-tree fitting, making it less flexible. Deep learning baselines (VAE [26], cVAE [27], NCF [28]) are uniformly outperformed by cEBMF in both simulations and collaborative filtering, with cVAE only marginally beating VAE in the highly informative tiled-clustering case. Spatial PCA [11], despite being designed for spatial data, underperforms even EBMF in the tiled-clustering simulations (likely due to its smoothness and orthogonality assumptions) and struggles computationally beyond 100K rows. In the DLPFC spatial transcriptomics analysis, cEBNMF produces parts-based representations that more closely match expert annotations than NMF [15], EBNMF, CMF [21], or cVAE, while Spatial PCA and MFAI also capture spatial structure but with notable exceptions. PMD [39] and Sparse SVD [38] are outperformed in the simulation comparisons reported in the appendix.

## Relation to Prior Work

cEBMF directly extends the Empirical Bayes Matrix Factorization (EBMF) framework of Wang and Stephens [13] by replacing fixed prior families with covariate-moderated priors whose parameters are learned via empirical Bayes. The closest prior method, MFAI [17], is shown to be a special case of cEBMF (normal priors with covariate-informed means fitted via gradient-boosted trees), but cEBMF generalizes to arbitrary prior families, arbitrary covariate-processing models (neural networks, regression, graphical models), and both row and column side information. The paper also positions itself against purpose-built spatial methods (Spatial PCA [11], NMF-based spatial factorization [12]) and deep learning approaches (VAE, cVAE, NCF), arguing that its modular empirical Bayes formulation avoids the restrictive parametric assumptions those methods require.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a single, modular recipe that subsumes many existing matrix factorization variants (PCA, NMF, sparse SVD, semi-NMF, MFAI) as special cases, giving practitioners a principled way to incorporate diverse side information without committing to a specific parametric model. The demonstrated scalability to 1M rows (outperforming MFAI and Spatial PCA in runtime) makes it practically relevant for large-scale genomics and recommendation problems. Open questions include how the framework performs when the covariate-processing model is itself complex (e.g., deep CNNs for image side information), whether the coordinate-ascent optimization can be further accelerated, and how to formally characterize the conditions under which covariate-moderated priors provide meaningful gains over their unmoderated counterparts.
