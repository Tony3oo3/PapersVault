## Problem

In the uniform learning framework, multiclass learning under bandit feedback is impossible when the effective label space is unbounded, while in the universal framework, bandit feedback and full supervision yield exact learnability equivalences. The non-uniform framework, which allows guarantees to depend on the target hypothesis, sits between these two extremes, but no combinatorial characterization of learnable hypothesis classes existed for multiclass bandit-feedback learning in either the i.i.d. batch or adversarial online models.

## Input / Output

In the PAC model, the learner receives n i.i.d. instances from an unknown distribution and, after predicting a label for each, receives only a binary correct/incorrect signal; it must output a hypothesis function X→Y. In the online model, an adversary sequentially presents instances from an arbitrary (possibly infinite) instance space, and the learner predicts a label from a possibly countably infinite label space, receiving only the 0-1 loss as feedback. The hypothesis class H⊆Y^X is known in advance, and the key constraint is that the label space Y may be countably infinite, making the effective label space potentially unbounded.

## Method

The paper establishes that a hypothesis class is non-uniformly learnable under bandit feedback (in both realizable and agnostic settings, for both PAC and online models) if and only if it can be expressed as a countable union of sub-classes with finite bandit Littlestone dimension (online) or finite effective label space and finite Natarajan dimension (PAC). The constructive algorithm maintains a parallel bank of uniform learners—one per sub-class—tracking cumulative mistakes and always delegating to the sub-class with the smallest mistake-plus-index score, yielding a per-hypothesis mistake bound of O((k+d_k)²). For the agnostic online setting, a doubling trick over geometrically growing time horizons combined with the Raman et al. regret bound gives Õ(√T) regret. The main novel technical contribution is a separation construction (Proposition B.1) showing a class that is non-uniformly learnable under full supervision but not under bandit feedback, revealing a fundamental distinction absent in the universal framework.

## Results

The paper provides combinatorial characterizations of non-uniform learnability for multiclass learning under bandit feedback. The key result (Theorem 1.1) establishes that a hypothesis class is realizable non-uniform online learnable under bandit feedback if and only if it can be represented as a countable union of classes with finite bandit Littlestone dimension. For PAC learning (Theorem 1.3), the characterization is a countable union of classes with finite effective label space and finite Natarajan dimension. The paper also constructs a hypothesis class (Proposition 1.4) that is uniform online learnable under full supervision but not non-uniform PAC learnable under bandit feedback, revealing a fundamental separation between the non-uniform and universal learning frameworks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines or quantitative comparisons. Instead, the paper establishes separations between learning frameworks. The main separation (Proposition 1.4) shows that non-uniform learnability under bandit feedback is strictly weaker than non-uniform learnability under full supervision: there exists a class that is uniform online learnable under full supervision (hence also non-uniformly learnable under full supervision) but not non-uniform PAC learnable under bandit feedback. This contrasts with the universal learning framework, where Hanneke et al. [2025a,b] proved exact equivalences between bandit and full supervision. Additional separations (Proposition 1.5) show that universal learnability under bandit feedback does not imply non-uniform PAC learnability under full supervision, and that non-uniform online learnability under bandit feedback does not imply uniform PAC learnability under full supervision.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the non-uniform learning framework of Benedek and Itai (1988) from binary classification to the multiclass setting with bandit feedback, combining their combinatorial characterization technique with the bandit Littlestone dimension and BSOA algorithm from Daniely et al. (2011). It also builds on Lu (2024) for the full-supervision non-uniform online result, Raman et al. (2023) for agnostic bandit regret bounds, and Brukhim et al. (2022) for the DS-dimension characterization of multiclass PAC learnability. The paper positions itself as filling the gap between the uniform framework (where multiclass bandit learnability is essentially impossible with unbounded labels) and the universal framework (where Hanneke et al. 2025a,b showed exact equivalence between bandit and full supervision), by providing the first combinatorial characterizations in the intermediate non-uniform setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key conceptual contribution is the separation result: a hypothesis class that is non-uniformly learnable under full supervision but not under bandit feedback, which breaks the exact equivalence that holds in the universal framework. This reveals that the non-uniform framework is not simply an interpolation between uniform and universal learning, but has its own distinct structure. The paper is purely theoretical with no empirical component, and the practical impact is limited to learning theory researchers studying combinatorial characterizations of learnability. Open questions include whether similar separations exist for alternative feedback models beyond bandit feedback, and whether the countable-union characterizations can be refined to give tighter sample or regret bounds.
