## Problem

Accurate subgraph containment scoring for graph retrieval requires early cross-interaction between query and corpus node embeddings, leading to an impractical Ω(C) query time over a corpus of C graphs. While multi-vector representations (e.g., IsoNet) achieve strong ranking quality, they are incompatible with the highly optimized sparse inverted indices that make text retrieval scalable. The paper addresses this gap by asking whether dense, contextual graph representations can be discretized into tokens that plug into classic inverted indices while preserving the accuracy of late-interaction scoring.

## Input / Output

The method takes a query graph G_q (unlabeled, unattributed structural graph) and a corpus of C graphs, and produces a ranked list of corpus graphs scored by subgraph containment relevance. Retrieval is two-stage: a fast inverted-index probe with a tunable threshold δ selects a candidate subset R_q(δ), which is then reranked using the expensive pretrained alignment distance Δ(G_q, G_c). The token vocabulary is a fixed set of 2^D binary codes (D=10, yielding 1024 tokens), and the threshold δ directly controls the accuracy–latency trade-off by adjusting the candidate pool size.

## Method

CoRGII introduces a Graph Tokenizer Network (GTNet) that applies a shared GNN followed by separate sigmoid-activated MLPs for query and corpus nodes (an asymmetric architecture that preserves subgraph-matching directionality while enabling exact token matching). The resulting soft binary codes are thresholded to hard D-bit tokens, and a standard inverted index maps each token to posting lists of corpus graphs. A trainable impact-weight network Impact_ψ replaces fixed TF-IDF/BM25 weights, taking both the discrete token and the continuous node embedding as input to assign query-aware importance scores. Co-occurrence-based multiprobing (CM) expands each query token to b nearest tokens by posting-list overlap, smoothing the accuracy–efficiency curve. Training is two-stage: GTNet is first optimized with a margin-based Chamfer-distance ranking loss, then Impact_ψ is trained with a margin-based loss on the impact-weighted retrieval score, while the pretrained IsoNet backbone is frozen and used only for final reranking.

## Results

CoRGII is evaluated on four TU benchmark datasets (PTC-FR, PTC-FM, COX2, PTC-MR) using Mean Average Precision (MAP) as the accuracy metric and fraction of corpus retrieved (k/C) as the efficiency metric. On COX2, CoRGII achieves a MAP of ~0.50 at k/C = 0.5, whereas the strongest baseline FHN saturates around 0.35. On PTC-FR, CoRGII attains a MAP of ~0.36 by retrieving less than 33% of corpus graphs, while most baselines require more than 75% of the corpus to reach that level. Multi-vector variants of IVF and DiskANN consistently outperform their single-vector counterparts, but all ANN-based baselines remain substantially below CoRGII across all four datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoRGII outperforms all six baselines (FHN, IVF-single, IVF-multi, DiskANN-single, DiskANN-multi, and Random) across all four datasets in the MAP vs. k/C trade-off. FHN (FourierHashNet [42]) is the strongest baseline, but CoRGII shows particularly large gains in the high-MAP regime—for example, on COX2, CoRGII reaches MAP ~0.50 at k/C = 0.5 while FHN plateaus near 0.35. IVF and DiskANN multi-vector variants [25, 29] outperform their single-vector counterparts, confirming the benefit of node-level granularity, but they still lag significantly behind CoRGII, largely because they rely on symmetric distance functions (L2, cosine) that are ill-suited to the inherently asymmetric subgraph containment task. Single-vector variants perform the worst, sometimes falling below the Random baseline, due to both the coarse pooling of graph structure and the use of symmetric metrics. The paper's main results are presented as trade-off curves (Figure 4) rather than a fixed-k table, emphasizing that CoRGII's advantage is most pronounced at low retrieval budgets where efficiency matters most.

## Relation to Prior Work

CoRGII adapts the late-interaction paradigm from ColBERT-style text retrieval (separate encoding + Chamfer distance) to the graph domain, but makes a critical departure: it replaces the Gumbel-Sinkhorn injective alignment used in IsoNet and IsoNet++ with a non-injective Chamfer distance, enabling corpus graphs to be indexed independently of queries. The discretization of continuous node embeddings into binary tokens over a latent vocabulary, combined with a learned impact-weight network (analogous to TF-IDF/BM25 but data-driven and query-aware), draws on SPLADE's token-expansion philosophy and classical inverted-index engineering. The co-occurrence-based multiprobing strategy is novel to this work, borrowing the posting-list overlap intuition from text IR but applying it to manufactured graph tokens.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CoRGII is the first framework to bridge highly accurate late-interaction subgraph containment scoring with the decades of optimization invested in sparse inverted indices, offering a smooth, tunable accuracy-efficiency tradeoff via a single threshold parameter. Key open questions include whether the fixed binary vocabulary can be made adaptive, whether the framework extends to attributed or heterogeneous graphs, and whether the static-corpus assumption can be relaxed for evolving graph collections. The finding that architectural asymmetry (separate query/corpus MLPs) is essential for co-occurrence matching to function—while a Siamese variant degrades sharply—highlights a subtle but important design constraint for future graph indexing systems.
