## Problem

Reinforcement learning policies optimize long-term performance but lack formal stability guarantees, and classical Lyapunov certification requires a strict one-step decrease that is difficult to satisfy for learned policies. The RL value function is a natural certificate candidate but fails the Lyapunov condition due to the discount factor, and existing LMI-based residual augmentation (Postoyan et al.) yields overly conservative stability bounds. This paper asks whether relaxing the one-step decrease to a multi-step weighted-average condition can make certificates both easier to construct and less conservative.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained RL policy (PPO, SAC, or TD-MPC) together with its learned value function J_γ^π, the closed-loop dynamics f(x, π(x)), and a stage cost, all operating on state vectors x ∈ R^n. It produces a generalized Lyapunov function V(x) = |J_γ^π(x) − J_γ^π(0)| + |φ(x) − φ(0)| + β‖x‖², a state-dependent step-weight vector σ(x) ∈ R_{≥0}^M (output by a softmax-scaled network), and a certified inner approximation S of the region of attraction. A key structural detail is that the Lyapunov decrease is enforced over M-step rollouts (M=15–20 in experiments) rather than a single step, and verification excludes a small ball B(0; δ) around the origin to accommodate RL policies that converge to a neighborhood rather than the exact equilibrium.

## Method

The core insight is twofold: (1) augmenting the RL value function with a learned neural residual φ(x; θ₁) recovers a positive-definite candidate that can satisfy a Lyapunov-type decrease, and (2) replacing the classical one-step condition V(f(x,π(x))) < V(x) with a generalized M-step weighted condition (1/M)Σ σᵢ(x)V(x_{k+i}) < (1−ᾱ)V(x_k) dramatically reduces conservatism while preserving asymptotic stability (Theorem 4.2). For the LQR case, this multi-step condition translates into a set of LMIs (Theorem 4.4) that certify stability for a wider range of discount factors than the one-step LMIs of Postoyan et al. (e.g., γ > 0.6229 vs. γ > 0.8090 in a scalar example, approaching the true threshold γ* = 1/3 as M grows). For nonlinear systems, the residual network φ and the step-weighting network σ are trained jointly by minimizing a ReLU-penalized loss over sampled M-step rollouts, with the weights learned to concentrate toward the end of the horizon, effectively tolerating initial non-monotonic transients. In the joint synthesis setting, the one-step Lyapunov constraint in the policy-certificate optimization is replaced by the multi-step condition (18), and the resulting certificate is formally verified via α-β-CROWN, yielding larger certified ROA volumes (e.g., 89.2% vs. 42.9% for the inverted pendulum at M=3 vs. M=1).

## Results

For the scalar LQR example, the classical one-step Lyapunov LMI certifies stability only for γ > 0.8090, while the generalized M=2 formulation improves this to γ > 0.6229, and increasing M progressively approaches the true threshold γ* = 1/3. On RL policy certification, the generalized Lyapunov condition is satisfied at 100% of test states for PPO, SAC, and TD-MPC policies on Inverted Pendulum (M=15, N_test=10,000) and for SAC and TD-MPC on Cartpole (M=20, N_test=1,000,000), where classical single-step methods fail. For joint policy-certificate synthesis, the certified ROA volume increases substantially with horizon: Inverted Pendulum goes from 42.9±1.2 (M=1) to 89.2±1.2 (M=3), and 2D Quadrotor from 103.5±1.8 to 113.7±2.0, at the cost of longer verification times (e.g., 2209.5s → 5628.6s for the quadrotor).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is the classical one-step Lyapunov condition (M=1), which serves as the standard in prior work (e.g., Yang et al. 2024, Wu et al. 2023). The generalized multi-step formulation consistently outperforms it on certified ROA volume: for the Inverted Pendulum, M=3 nearly doubles the certified volume (89.2 vs. 42.9), and for the 2D Quadrotor it yields a ~10% improvement (113.7 vs. 103.5). In the LQR analysis, the multi-step LMI reduces the conservative discount-factor bound from γ > 0.8090 (classical, matching Postoyan et al. 2017) to γ > 0.6229 at M=2, approaching the true stability threshold of γ* = 1/3. For post-hoc RL policy certification, classical single-step Lyapunov methods fail to produce valid certificates for the tested PPO, SAC, and TD-MPC policies on both Gymnasium and DeepMind Control benchmarks, whereas the generalized approach achieves 100% satisfaction of the decrease condition across all sampled test states. The trade-off is increased verification time, which grows roughly linearly with M (e.g., 3.4× for M=2 and 4.8× for M=3 on the Inverted Pendulum), reflecting the added complexity of bounding multiple intermediate states in the α,β-CROWN verifier.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper synthesizes three distinct threads: (1) Postoyan et al.'s (2017) observation that augmenting a discounted LQR value function with a quadratic residual yields a valid Lyapunov certificate via LMIs; (2) Fürnsinn et al.'s (2023) generalized Lyapunov definition replacing pointwise one-step decrease with a multi-step weighted average decrease; and (3) Yang et al.'s (2024) joint policy-certificate synthesis framework with formal verification via α,β-CROWN. The key departure is combining all three: the value-function-plus-residual construction is retained, but the one-step decrease condition is replaced by the multi-step generalized condition, and the whole pipeline is extended from LQR to nonlinear RL policies (PPO, SAC, TD-MPC) and to joint controller-certificate training. Prior works on Lyapunov-based safe RL (Perkins & Barto, Berkenkamp, Chang, Wu) either use hand-designed certificates, one-step conditions, or structural constraints on network architectures; this paper instead learns both the residual correction and the step-weights end-to-end.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that RL policies on standard benchmarks (Gymnasium, DeepMind Control Suite) can now receive formal stability certificates in regimes where classical one-step Lyapunov methods are infeasible, and joint synthesis yields roughly 2× larger certified regions of attraction (e.g., 42.9→89.2 volume for the inverted pendulum). Key limitations remain: the horizon M is fixed and its minimal viable value is unknown, the method has not been tested on high-dimensional systems (humanoids, dexterous manipulators), and formal verification of jointly learned state-dependent weights is computationally intractable with current tools. Open directions include partial-state certification for task-relevant variables, robustness to stochastic disturbances, and relaxing the requirement of a known equilibrium—each of which would substantially broaden applicability.
