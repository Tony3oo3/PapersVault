## Problem

Existing orthogonal survival learners (e.g., Survival DR- and R-learners) handle confounding and censoring but remain sensitive to low overlap violations specific to the survival setting—namely low censoring overlap and low survival overlap—leading to high-variance treatment effect estimates. No general framework existed for constructing orthogonal learners that are simultaneously robust to all three types of overlap (treatment, censoring, and survival) in censored time-to-event data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251015/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard censored survival data: observed covariates X ∈ ℝ^p, a binary treatment A, the observed time T̃ = min(T, C), and indicators Δ^S and Δ^G denoting whether the event or censoring was observed. It outputs a heterogeneous treatment effect function τ_t(x) = P(T(1) > t | X=x) − P(T(0) > t | X=x) at a user-specified time point t, estimated via a second-stage regression over an arbitrary model class G (e.g., neural networks). The framework is defined in discrete time with a straightforward extension to continuous time via integral-based influence functions.

## Method

The paper proposes a three-step toolbox: (1) estimate nuisance functions (propensity score π, survival hazards λ^S, and censoring hazards λ^G) with arbitrary ML models; (2) choose a positive weighting function f(π, S_{t−1}, G_{t−1}) that downweights observations in low-overlap regions; (3) minimize a Neyman-orthogonal second-stage loss derived from the efficient influence function of the weighted estimand θ_{t,f}. The key technical contribution is Theorem 5.1, which shows that for any weighting function f, the resulting loss L_f(g, η_t) with weight ρ(Z,η_t) and pseudo-outcome φ(Z,η_t) is orthogonal to nuisance estimation errors, and Theorem 5.2 confirms its minimizer recovers τ_t. Instantiating f with specific products of π(1−π), S_{t−1}(X,1)S_{t−1}(X,0), and G_{t−1}(X,1)G_{t−1}(X,0) yields a family of learners (∅, T, C, S, and combinations) that are robust to treatment, censoring, and/or survival overlap violations, with the ∅- and T-learners recovering the existing Survival DR- and R-learners as special cases.

## Results

The paper evaluates PEHE (×10⁻⁴) across four overlap-violation scenarios (none, propensity, censoring, survival) using two synthetic data-generating mechanisms. In Scenario 1 (1D confounder, 5 time steps), the targeted C-learner reduces PEHE from 3.41±2.54 (∅-learner) to 1.91±0.64 under low censoring overlap, while the T-learner reduces PEHE from 4.56±2.75 to 3.94±1.90 under low treatment overlap. In Scenario 2 (10D confounders, 30 time steps), the T+C+S-learner achieves the lowest PEHE of 0.56±0.13 under propensity violation (vs. 1.01±0.31 for ∅) and 4.55±0.69 under survival violation (vs. 4.54±0.36 for ∅). On the Twins medical dataset, the C- and T+C-learners show the fastest validation-loss convergence, confirming the presence of censoring and treatment overlap violations in real data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary baselines are the existing Survival DR-learner (∅-learner, no weighting) and Survival R-learner (T-learner, treatment-overlap weighting) from prior work [52]. Under low censoring overlap in Scenario 1, the novel C-learner (PEHE 1.91±0.64) and T+C+S-learner (1.86±0.58) dramatically outperform the ∅-learner (3.41±2.54) and T-learner (3.37±2.52), roughly halving both the mean PEHE and its standard deviation. Under low treatment overlap, the T-learner (3.94±1.90) and T+C+S-learner (4.65±2.41) improve over the ∅-learner (4.56±2.75), though the gain is more modest. In Scenario 2, the T+C+S-learner achieves the best PEHE under propensity violation (0.56±0.13 vs. 1.01±0.31 for ∅), while under survival violation all learners perform similarly (4.52–4.56), suggesting survival overlap is less impactful in that setting. A key negative finding is that mismatched weighting harms performance: e.g., the S-learner under propensity violation in Scenario 1 (5.11±3.09) is worse than the unweighted ∅-learner (4.56±2.75), and the T+S-learner under survival violation in Scenario 2 (9.23±1.27) is roughly double the ∅-learner (4.54±0.36). On the Twins dataset, the C- and T+C-learners converge fastest in validation loss, empirically confirming that censoring and treatment overlap violations are present in real medical data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Neyman-orthogonal meta-learner framework (DR-learner, R-learner) from uncensored causal inference to the censored time-to-event setting, building directly on the Survival-DR-learner and Survival-R-learner of Xu et al. [52]. The key departure is the introduction of a general weighting function f(η̃_t(X)) within the orthogonal loss, which allows practitioners to target robustness to treatment, censoring, or survival overlap violations simultaneously—something no prior orthogonal survival learner could do. The theoretical machinery (EIF derivation, orthogonality proof for arbitrary f) generalizes the weighted orthogonal learner construction of Morzywolek et al. [32] from uncensored CATE to the survival setting with its additional nuisance functions (hazards, censoring probabilities).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The toolbox gives practitioners a principled, model-agnostic way to handle the three distinct overlap violations that arise in censored survival data, which prior orthogonal learners could not address. The Twins case study suggests that real medical data often exhibits multiple simultaneous overlap violations (especially censoring overlap), making the combined-weighting learners practically relevant. Open questions include: how to choose the weighting function when overlap violations are mild or ambiguous, whether the approach extends to competing risks or left-truncation, and how the method behaves under complete (rather than low) overlap violations where the paper acknowledges instability remains.
