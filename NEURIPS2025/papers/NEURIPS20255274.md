## Problem

Existing hallucination mitigation methods for LLMs target factuality or faithfulness in isolation, and empirical evidence shows that interventions improving one type systematically degrade the other. No prior work provides a theoretical guarantee that a shared activation subspace exists between the two hallucination types, nor a unified editing procedure that can improve both simultaneously without trade-offs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255274/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes paired text inputs (questions concatenated with ground-truth and hallucinated answers, prefixed by an extraction instruction) to collect per-head activation vectors from every layer of a decoder-only LLM, using both TruthfulQA (factuality) and PDTB (faithfulness) datasets. It produces a set of learned direction vectors assigned to a small intersection of top-k attention heads, which are then injected as a scaled bias term into the residual stream at inference time, yielding hallucination-reduced text without any weight retraining.

## Method

SPACE is a four-stage framework: (1) Neural Activation Profiling collects per-head activations for correct vs. hallucinated answers; (2) Contrastive Neural Probing trains multiple orthogonal probes per head using a contrastive loss plus a dynamically weighted orthogonality loss to identify heads correlated with factuality and faithfulness; (3) Semantic Cluster Fusion applies HDBSCAN separately to factuality and faithfulness embeddings, defines positive samples as points in the cluster overlap and negative samples as points outside it, then trains a target direction vector via hard-negative contrastive learning; (4) Dynamic Space Editing adds the learned direction vectors, scaled by per-head activation standard deviations and a global factor alpha, to the residual stream of the selected heads. A key theoretical contribution is a proof via the Hahn-Banach separation theorem that, under a convex interpolation assumption, the factuality and faithfulness subspaces in the activation Hilbert space must intersect, guaranteeing the existence of the shared subspace that SPACE edits. The intersection (rather than union) of top-k heads across the two tasks is critical, as ablation shows replacing it with a union causes substantial performance decline.

## Results

On LLaMA2-7B-Chat, SPACE achieves 26.1% PDTB Overall (vs. 17.4% baseline) and 67.1% True*Info on TruthfulQA (vs. 57.6% baseline), simultaneously improving both faithfulness and factuality. On Qwen2-7B-Instruct, the gains are even more pronounced: 44.6% PDTB Overall (vs. 26.5%) and 75.8% True*Info (vs. 54.8%). On deepseek-llm-7b, SPACE reaches 23.0% PDTB Overall and 71.9% True*Info, outperforming all baselines on both dimensions without the trade-off observed in competing methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SPACE is the only method that simultaneously improves both PDTB Overall (faithfulness) and TruthfulQA True*Info (factuality) across all three model architectures. Fine-tuning baselines (FT-PDTB, FT-TruthfulQA) exhibit the trade-off the paper targets: FT-PDTB raises PDTB Overall but drops True*Info by 8–14 points, while FT-TruthfulQA does the reverse. CAD improves faithfulness on Qwen2 (33.2 vs. 26.5 baseline) but sacrifices factuality (50.8 vs. 54.8 True*Info); DoLa shows erratic performance, with MC2 collapsing to 7.5 on deepseek-llm-7b. ITI provides modest gains on factuality but underperforms SPACE on both dimensions (e.g., 59.9 vs. 67.1 True*Info on LLaMA2). TruthX, a factuality-focused method, achieves 63.6 True*Info on LLaMA2 but catastrophically degrades faithfulness to 5.8 PDTB Overall, starkly illustrating the trade-off that SPACE avoids.

## Relation to Prior Work

SPACE extends the representation-editing lineage established by ITI (Li et al., 2024) and the factual-direction framework of Burns et al. (2022), which identified that truthful knowledge resides in specific activation subspaces. The key departure is that prior methods (DoLa, ITI, TruthX, CAD) target either factuality or faithfulness in isolation, and the paper empirically demonstrates that such single-target interventions create measurable trade-offs on the other dimension. SPACE's novelty lies in proving the existence of a shared activation subspace via a Hahn-Banach separation argument and then jointly editing that intersection using a hybrid probe strategy (contrastive neural probing + HDBSCAN cluster fusion), rather than treating the two hallucination types as independent optimization targets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The demonstrated trade-off between factuality and faithfulness mitigation is a practical bottleneck for deploying LLMs in safety-critical settings, and SPACE's ability to improve both simultaneously without degradation is a meaningful step toward unified reliability. However, the convex interpolation assumption underpinning the theoretical guarantee is strong and unverified empirically, and the paper's own limitations section acknowledges that decoding-level edits cannot remedy knowledge gaps inherited from pretraining (e.g., the goldfish memory failure case). Open questions include whether the shared-subspace formulation generalizes to other hallucination taxonomies (e.g., instruction-following, multi-turn coherence) and whether the approach scales to larger models where activation geometry may differ.
