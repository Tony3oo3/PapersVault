## Problem

Existing NAS methods for Mamba-type architectures rely on extensive training of candidate networks during search, incurring substantial computational cost. No training-free (zero-shot) NAS method has been designed for Mamba-type models, despite such methods being well-established for CNNs and Transformers. This paper introduces the first training-free NAS specifically targeting Mamba2.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250190/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes only the initialized (untrained) parameters and one forward-backward pass of a candidate Mamba2 network, extracting the input tensor U and SSD inputs X, B, C from each block. A key detail is that the transformation matrices W_X, W_B, W_C are not simply the fully connected layer weights but must account for the intervening convolution and SiLU operations, with the solution method (direct inverse, pseudoinverse via SVD, or least-squares approximation) switching based on whether T equals, is less than, or exceeds W. The output is a single scalar TF-MAS score per candidate architecture, used to rank and select architectures via evolutionary search without any training.

## Method

TF-MAS is grounded in a formal proof that stacking SSD blocks in Mamba2 induces rank collapse (analogous to the known phenomenon in stacked attention layers), with the collapse magnitude serving as a proxy for architecture quality. For each Mamba2 block, the method computes transformation matrices W_X, W_B, W_C mapping the block input U to the SSD inputs, handling three geometric cases (T=W, T<W, T>W) via matrix inversion, Moore-Penrose pseudoinverse, or least-squares approximation respectively. The proxy score is the sum over all blocks of the product of nuclear norms of each transformation matrix and its analytically derived gradient (computed via chain rule rather than backpropagation, since these are not network parameters). The search space defines four adjustable hyperparameters (depth, width, state dimension, heads) whose ranges are determined by solving a cubic equation that enforces a target parameter budget via proportional scaling of a reference model, rather than being set manually.

## Results

On VMSSMamba2Bench_2.7B, TF-MAS achieves the highest Kendall's Tau ranking correlation across all 8 evaluation metrics, with values ranging from 0.339 (Arc-E) to 0.661 (LAMBADA ACC), substantially outperforming the parameter-count baseline (range: 0.214–0.437) and all CNN- and Transformer-oriented proxies. In practical search on the 130M budget, the best searched architecture (opt VWMamba2 w/ bwe) reduces LAMBADA perplexity from 16.79 to 15.08 and improves HellaSwag accuracy from 31.0% to 40.5% over the original Mamba2-130M, while using fewer parameters (122.6M vs. 128.9M) and lower FLOPs (7.84G vs. 8.25G). The proxy computation takes only 1.31 seconds per architecture, comparable to most baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TF-MAS dominates all 15 compared proxies across every dataset. Against the parameter-count baseline (#Param), TF-MAS improves Kendall's Tau by 0.227 on LAMBADA PPL (-0.685 vs. -0.458) and by 0.194 on OpenbookQA ACC (0.519 vs. 0.325). Among CNN-oriented proxies (Gradnorm, Synflow, GraSP, Fisher, Snip, Zen-NAS, ZiCo, MeCo, Auto-Prox), all achieve correlations near or below zero on most datasets, with the best (Auto-Prox) reaching only 0.069 on Arc-C—far below TF-MAS's 0.452. Transformer-oriented proxies perform slightly better than CNN ones, with AttnNAS being the strongest competitor (e.g., 0.267 on LAMBADA ACC, 0.232 on WinoGrande), yet TF-MAS still leads by 0.394 and 0.251 respectively. DSS++ [69], the direct methodological ancestor, achieves at most 0.108 on HellaSwag, less than one-third of TF-MAS's 0.381. In the downstream search experiment (Table 2), the architecture found by TF-MAS (opt VWMamba2 w/ bwe) outperforms the original Mamba2-130M by 1.71 in LAMBADA PPL (15.08 vs. 16.79) and by 9.5 points on HellaSwag (40.5% vs. 31.0%), confirming that the proxy's ranking advantage translates into tangible model quality gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the training-free (zero-shot) NAS paradigm, previously established for CNNs (NASWOT, Synflow, GraSP) and Transformers (DSS, DSS++, AttnNAS), to the Mamba2 architecture. The core proxy directly adapts the product-of-nuclear-norms scoring from DSS++ [69], but requires a non-trivial adaptation because Mamba2's SSD blocks involve convolution and SiLU activations rather than simple linear projections, necessitating the derivation of effective transformation matrices via Moore-Penrose pseudoinverse. The theoretical foundation draws on the rank collapse phenomenon in stacked attention layers [18], which the authors prove also holds for stacked SSD blocks via the attention-SSD duality established in Mamba2 [13]. The search space design departs from prior work by deriving hyperparameter ranges from a cost-constrained scaling equation rather than manual specification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first training-free NAS method for Mamba-type architectures, filling a clear gap given the rapid proliferation of Mamba variants (Falcon Mamba, Jamba, Vision Mamba, etc.) that all require manual hyperparameter tuning. The practical impact is demonstrated by search results that outperform the original Mamba2-130M while using fewer parameters and lower FLOPs. However, the method is validated only on Mamba2, and the ranking correlations (Kendall's Tau of 0.3–0.6) remain moderate, leaving open whether the proxy generalizes to other Mamba variants with different SSD formulations or to vision/multimodal Mamba models. The cost-constrained search space design is a reusable contribution that could inform NAS for other architecture families.
