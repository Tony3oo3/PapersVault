## Problem

Selecting optimal preconditioning parameters (e.g., SOR relaxation factor ω) for linear system solvers is critical for convergence speed, yet traditional fixed-constant strategies ignore instance-wise problem features, and ML-based predictors suffer from high CPU inference costs and black-box opacity. No existing approach simultaneously achieves instance-adaptive parameter selection, near-zero deployment overhead in CPU-only solver environments, and human-interpretable output.

## Input / Output

The method takes a small vector of PDE feature parameters (e.g., coefficients a₁₁, a₁₂, a₂₂, a₁, a₂, a₀ for elliptic PDEs; Chebyshev expansion coefficients for Darcy flow; domain dimensions for biharmonic problems) as numerical scalar inputs. It produces a concise symbolic expression (4–64 characters, drawn from a restricted operator set {+,-,×,÷,sqrt,exp,log,pow,1.0}) that maps those features to the optimal preconditioning parameter value (ω ∈ [0,2] for SOR/SSOR or θ_T for AMG). The final expression is compiled into a lightweight C shared object and called at runtime inside PETSc, incurring only ~1.1×10⁻⁵ s per evaluation versus ~5.1×10⁻⁵ s for an equivalent MLP.

## Method

SymMaP uses an RNN to autoregressively generate symbolic expressions in prefix notation, where at each step the network receives a (parent, sibling) node pair and emits a categorical distribution over the token library. Training employs a risk-seeking policy gradient: the objective is the expected reward (1/(1+NRMSE)) conditioned on the reward exceeding the (1−ε)-quantile (ε=0.05), so optimization concentrates on the top-performing candidate expressions rather than the mean. Within each sampled expression, constant placeholders are optimized via a nonlinear solver before reward computation. The training pipeline first constructs a dataset of 1200 (feature, optimal-parameter) pairs via adaptive two-stage grid search, then iteratively samples N expressions per batch, computes the risk-seeking gradient (Eq. 7) plus an entropy bonus, and updates the RNN via PPO. The resulting expression is typically a short rational or nested formula (e.g., 1.0+1.0/(x₂+1.2)) that captures the dominant dependence of the optimal parameter on a subset of input features.

## Results

Across five PDE-derived datasets, SymMaP consistently outperforms all baselines. For SOR preconditioning (Table 1), SymMaP reduces average computation time by up to 40% versus PETSc's default ω=1 and by ~10% versus the best fixed constant (e.g., 1.24 s vs 1.31 s on Biharmonic, 8.50 s vs 9.54 s on Darcy Flow). For SSOR (Table 2), SymMaP achieves up to 27% time reduction over defaults and 11% over optimal constants. For AMG (Table 3), SymMaP lowers the condition number by up to 40% relative to PETSc defaults (e.g., 4824 vs 8204 on Darcy Flow) and 32% versus optimal constants.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SymMaP is compared against four baseline strategies: no preconditioning, PETSc default parameters, hand-picked fixed constants, and the best single fixed constant (optimized over the training set). SymMaP wins on every dataset and every preconditioner tested. Against PETSc defaults, the gains are largest: up to 40% computation-time reduction for SOR/SSOR and 40% condition-number reduction for AMG. Against the optimal fixed constant (which already captures the best single parameter value), SymMaP still achieves 10–11% further improvement for SOR/SSOR and 32% for AMG, demonstrating that instance-wise parameter adaptation provides meaningful gains over a one-size-fits-all constant. The two learned expressions (SymMaP 1 and 2) perform nearly identically, with SymMaP 1 being marginally better in most cases, confirming the robustness of the risk-seeking search. In a direct CPU-runtime comparison (Table 4), the symbolic expression evaluates in 1.1×10⁻⁵ s versus 5.1×10⁻⁵ s for an MLP, while producing equivalent solution times, highlighting the deployment advantage of symbolic over neural-network-based parameter prediction.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper applies the deep symbolic regression framework of Petersen et al. (2019) — specifically the risk-seeking policy gradient objective and its Monte Carlo gradient estimator — to the domain of matrix preconditioning parameter selection. The sequential token-generation architecture follows Landajuela et al. (2021), using an RNN to emit prefix-notation expressions over a constrained operator library. Conceptually, the paper departs from the growing body of ML-based preconditioner design (CNNs for block Jacobi, GNNs for AMG, neural incomplete factorization) by replacing the black-box predictor with an interpretable symbolic formula, directly addressing the CPU-deployment and transparency challenges that neural-network preconditioners face in production solvers like PETSc.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for scientific-computing practitioners who need parameter tuning in CPU-only, low-overhead solver pipelines: a two-line symbolic expression compiled into a shared object replaces a multi-layer network with negligible runtime cost while matching or exceeding its predictive accuracy. The interpretability of the learned formulas (e.g., relaxation factor depending only on the elliptic coupling coefficient) offers a pathway toward theoretical understanding of why certain parameters work, which opaque ML models cannot provide. Open questions include whether the approach extends to discrete parameters (fill-in levels, coarsening strategies), whether the learned expressions admit rigorous mathematical justification via pseudospectral or spectral analysis, and how performance scales to higher-dimensional or strongly heterogeneous PDE parameter spaces.
