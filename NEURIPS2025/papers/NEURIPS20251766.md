## Problem

LLMs used as human simulators (patients, students, chat partners) frequently drift from their assigned personas, contradict earlier statements, or abandon role-appropriate behavior over multi-turn conversations, undermining downstream applications in therapy, education, and social simulation. Existing consistency evaluations are narrow, task-specific, rely on costly human annotations, and fail to capture the subtle, global belief shifts that occur in open-ended dialogue. This paper asks whether automatic consistency metrics can both diagnose and, when used as RL rewards, systematically repair persona drift.

## Input / Output

The User Simulator receives a detailed persona/background prompt (e.g., a patient's clinical condition, a student's learning style, or a chat partner's life story), a task-specific role prompt, and the full dialogue history as context for each turn. The LLM-as-a-Judge (Llama-3.1-70B-Instruct) scores each utterance with a binary 0/1 consistency label against the persona, prior utterances, or LLM-generated multiple-choice questions derived from the persona; these are averaged into scalar scores in [0,1]. The final output is a fine-tuned User Simulator model that produces more persona-faithful utterances, evaluated on 40 held-out conversations of varying lengths (10–60 turns) across 10 personas per task.

## Method

The framework inverts the standard RL setup: instead of training the Task Agent, it trains the User Simulator for consistency using multi-turn PPO with turn-level rewards computed by an LLM-as-a-Judge. Three complementary metrics—prompt-to-line (alignment with the initial persona), line-to-line (absence of self-contradiction across turns), and Q&A (stability of persona-derived beliefs probed via generated multiple-choice questions)—are each validated against human annotations (Fleiss' κ ≈ 0.55, exceeding human-human agreement). During training, each generated utterance receives a scalar reward in [0,1] from the judge, and the full dialogue history is included in the observation, making the policy sensitive to long-range coherence. The authors extend OpenRLHF to support turn-level rewards and multi-turn rollout generation, and compare PPO against SFT, KTO (offline RL), and the unmodified baseline, showing PPO reduces inconsistency by over 55% across all three tasks.

## Results

Multi-turn PPO fine-tuning of Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct reduces prompt-to-line inconsistency by over 55% overall, with task-specific gains of +58.5% (open-ended conversation), +20.6% (education), and +37.6% (mental health) over the baseline model. The LLM-as-a-Judge (Llama-70B-Instruct) achieves 80.03% average agreement with human annotators and a Fleiss' kappa of 0.552, surpassing human-human inter-rater agreement (72.91%, κ=0.303). Across the three pre-fine-tuning models, prompt-to-line consistency ranges from 0.511 (Gemma-2B-IT, education) to 0.955 (Mistral-7B-Instruct, open-ended), while line-to-line consistency remains uniformly high (0.681–0.992), indicating that global persona drift—not local coherence—is the primary failure mode.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares PPO (online RL) against three baselines: the unmodified Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct model, supervised fine-tuning (SFT, following Ouyang et al. 2022), and Kahneman-Tversky Optimization (KTO, Ethayarajh et al. 2024, an offline RL method). PPO consistently outperforms all three baselines on prompt-to-line consistency across all three tasks, with the largest margin over the raw baseline (+58.5% on open-ended conversation, +37.6% on mental health, +20.6% on education). SFT and KTO both improve over the baseline but fall short of PPO, indicating that online multi-turn RL with turn-level consistency rewards is more effective than single-pass supervised learning or offline preference optimization for maintaining persona coherence over extended dialogues. Human evaluation of PPO-fine-tuned dialogues corroborates the automated metric improvements, confirming that the consistency gains are perceptible to human raters.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on persona-consistent dialogue generation (PersonaChat [73], pragmatic self-monitoring [28]) by reframing the user simulator as a trainable agent rather than a fixed environment, and by applying multi-turn PPO with LLM-as-a-Judge consistency rewards built on the OpenRLHF infrastructure. It departs from prior offline RL approaches to consistency [64] by using online multi-turn rollouts with turn-level rewards, and from logical-consistency evaluations [25, 39] by introducing three complementary dialogue-specific metrics (prompt-to-line, line-to-line, Q&A) that capture global persona and belief stability rather than local logical properties. The conceptual inversion—treating the simulated human as the policy to optimize rather than the environment—distinguishes it from standard RLHF pipelines where the task agent is the trained policy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a practical, scalable pipeline for diagnosing and reducing persona drift in LLM-simulated users, which is critical for downstream applications in mental health counseling, education, and social simulation where inconsistent simulators can distort training signals and experimental conclusions. Key limitations include the assumption of static personas (real humans evolve beliefs and tone over time), optimization of a single metric (prompt-to-line) rather than jointly training on all three, and evaluation on a single model family (Llama-8B). Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to multi-session temporal consistency, whether jointly optimizing all three metrics yields more robust behavior, and how to model justified persona evolution without penalizing natural human variability.
