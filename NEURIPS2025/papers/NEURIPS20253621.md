## Problem

Existing 4D scene datasets are either synthetic (suffering from simulation-to-real gaps), restricted to narrow domains like indoor or driving scenes, or lack physically-grounded multimodal annotations such as metric-scale geometry, instance-level object masks, and hierarchical descriptive captions. This data scarcity limits the ability of foundation models to interpret real-world dynamics from the monocular internet videos that are the most abundant source of visual data. The paper asks whether an automated pipeline can generate scalable, physically-aware, multimodal 4D annotations directly from raw monocular video.

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes monocular RGB video sequences of arbitrary resolution sourced from 13 existing 2D and 4D video datasets, with no requirement for multi-view, stereo, or sensor-based capture. For each video it produces a per-frame metric-scale point map (H×W×3), shared camera intrinsics (focal lengths f_x, f_y), per-frame camera poses [R|T] in SE(3), instance-level object masks with semantic categories and 3D bounding boxes, and three levels of natural-language captions (object, scene, camera motion). The resulting DynamicVerse dataset contains 100K+ scenes, 800K+ masklets, and 13.6M frames, all at physical (metric) scale rather than up-to-scale.

## Method

DynamicGen is a five-stage automated pipeline that integrates multiple pretrained foundation models (UniDepthV2 for depth, CoTracker3 for point tracking, UniMatch for optical flow, Qwen2.5-VL and SA2VA for segmentation) within a multi-stage dynamic bundle adjustment framework. The BA formulation jointly optimizes camera poses and 3D structure by decomposing the scene into static and dynamic point sets, with a non-rigid term using ARAP and temporal smoothness priors for dynamic objects (no rigid or articulated motion assumption), a camera smoothness prior with adaptive reweighting, and a sliding-window optical-flow-based global refinement stage that enforces long-range multi-view consistency. Dynamic masking combines semantic masks from VLM-based segmentation with epipolar-error-based flow masks to cleanly separate static background from moving foreground before optimization. A final captioning stage uses DAM for temporally-aligned object descriptions, Qwen2.5-VL with hierarchical prompting for scene-level captions, and a VLM for camera-motion narration, followed by LLM rephrasing and human-in-the-loop quality review.

## Results

On video depth estimation (Table 2), DynamicGen achieves the best joint depth-and-pose performance: Abs Rel 0.205 and δ<1.25 72.9% on Sintel, and Abs Rel 0.091 and δ<1.25 91.2% on KITTI, outperforming Uni4D (0.216/72.5 on Sintel) and CasualSAM (0.292/56.9 on Sintel). On camera pose estimation (Table 3), DynamicGen attains ATE 0.108, RPE trans 0.029, and RPE rot 0.282 on Sintel, and ATE 0.012, RPE trans 0.004, RPE rot 0.331 on TUM-dynamics, matching or exceeding all baselines. For camera intrinsics (Table 4), DynamicGen achieves the lowest AFE (413.1 px) and RFE (0.241%), beating UniDepth (447.4/0.357%) and DUSt3R (434.0/0.364%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DynamicGen outperforms all joint depth-and-pose baselines (MonST3R, Uni4D, CasualSAM, Robust-CVD) on both Sintel and KITTI depth metrics, with the largest margin over Robust-CVD (Abs 0.205 vs 0.358 on Sintel) and MonST3R (Abs 0.205 vs 0.344 on Sintel). Against the closest competitor Uni4D, DynamicGen improves Sintel Abs Rel from 0.216 to 0.205 and KITTI δ<1.25 from 89.7% to 91.2%. On camera pose (Table 3), DynamicGen achieves the best RPE trans (0.029) and RPE rot (0.282) on Sintel, beating Uni4D (0.032/0.338) and MonST3R (0.043/0.729), while tying MonST3R on ATE (0.108); on TUM-dynamics it matches Uni4D on ATE (0.012) and RPE trans (0.004) with a marginal RPE rot edge (0.331 vs 0.335). For intrinsics estimation, DynamicGen reduces AFE by 34 px over UniDepth and 21 px over DUSt3R, and cuts RFE by roughly 33% relative to both. The single-frame Metric3D v2 still leads on KITTI Abs (0.039 vs 0.091), but it does not jointly estimate pose and cannot handle dynamic scenes, making DynamicGen the strongest method that simultaneously produces metric depth, camera pose, and intrinsics from dynamic video.

## Relation to Prior Work

DynamicVerse positions itself at the intersection of 4D scene reconstruction (SfM, DUSt3R/MonST3R-based methods) and large-scale data curation, but its core novelty is a systems-level integration rather than a new reconstruction algorithm. It repurposes existing foundation models—UniDepthV2 for metric depth, CoTracker3 for point tracking, UniMatch for optical flow, Qwen2.5-VL and SA2VA for semantic segmentation—into a five-stage dynamic bundle adjustment pipeline that no prior work has assembled in this configuration. The closest concurrent work, Uni4D, also unifies visual foundation models for 4D modeling from a single video, but DynamicVerse departs by adding moving-object recovery (masks + categories + 3D bounding boxes) and hierarchical caption generation (object, camera, scene), which Uni4D lacks. Compared to prior 4D datasets (PointOdyssey, Spring, Dynamic Replica, RealCam-Vid, DynPose-100K), DynamicVerse is the first to combine metric-scale geometry, instance-level masks, and descriptive captions from real-world (non-synthetic) video at 100K+ scene scale.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine data bottleneck: prior 4D datasets are either synthetic (limited sim-to-real transfer), limited in modality (pose-only collections like DynPose-100K), or small-scale (indoor/autonomous-driving focused). By providing 100K+ real-world scenes with 800K+ masks and 10M+ frames annotated across geometry, segmentation, and language, DynamicVerse creates a resource that could accelerate 4D-VLMs, 3D-aware video generation, and 4D LangSplat. Key open questions include how well models trained on these foundation-model-derived annotations generalize when the underlying VFM/VLM errors compound, whether the pipeline's computational cost (multi-stage optimization per video) limits further scaling, and whether the 'metric-scale' guarantee holds reliably across the diverse internet-sourced content. The caption quality, while improved via SAKFE/HP/CoT, still depends on VLM capabilities and may not capture all fine-grained temporal dynamics.
