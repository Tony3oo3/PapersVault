## Problem

Traditional representational alignment measures such as CKA collapse the comparison between two feature spaces into a single scalar, obscuring which specific learned concepts are shared or divergent across models and layers. This limits practitioners' ability to understand the internal structure of representations, identify universal concepts, or diagnose where and how representations change across depth.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252399/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes intermediate-layer activations (last MLP output of each of 12 transformer blocks) from ViT-base/16 models (FS, CLIP, DINO, MAE) on a 25% stratified subset of ImageNet-1k train. The 196 spatial SEQ tokens per image are reduced to one representative token via average pooling over a central 4×4 block, yielding ~315,770 feature vectors of dimension 768 for clustering. Outputs include per-token concept proximity scores (fuzzy membership in [0,1]ⁿ), a scalar CBA alignment score between two representations, a pairwise concept distance matrix, and a 2D concept atlas (UMAP embedding of those distances) for visual inspection.

## Method

The paper defines concepts as non-linear manifolds in the F-dimensional feature space, generalizing prior linear-subspace and linear-direction definitions. Concepts are discovered by projecting activations into a 50-dimensional UMAP embedding and applying HDBSCAN density-based clustering, which handles variable cluster densities and unknown cluster counts. Soft (fuzzy) membership scores are computed from the HDBSCAN condensed tree, combining inverse-minimum-distance to exemplar points with outlier-based persistence, yielding a probabilistic clustering rather than hard assignments. Alignment between two representations is measured via a generalized Rand index for fuzzy clusterings (a pseudo-metric), which the authors decompose into pairwise concept distances to enable fine-grained, concept-level comparison. A sanity check using weighted Kendall's Tau between alignment and layer distance validates that the non-linear manifold definition outperforms PCA, MCD, and KMeans baselines while matching CKA's scalar performance.

## Results

In the sanity check (weighted Kendall Tau between alignment scores and layer distance), NLMCD-CBA achieves 0.92–0.98 for SEQ tokens and 0.91–0.96 for CLS tokens across four ViTs (FS, CLIP, DINO, MAE), consistently outperforming linear concept baselines (PCA, MCD) and matching or exceeding spherical KMeans concepts. CKA achieves comparable scores (0.89–0.99), confirming that CBA captures the same structural relationships while additionally providing a fine-grained, concept-level decomposition of alignment. Qualitatively, the analysis reveals that increased supervision (FS > CLIP > DINO/MAE) correlates with reduced semantic organization in layer-11 representations, and that self-supervised models (DINO, MAE) are more mutually aligned than with CLIP or FS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NLMCD-CBA outperforms the one-dimensional linear PCA baseline in 7 of 8 model/token settings (e.g., 0.98 vs. 0.91 for CLIP SEQ; 0.94 vs. 0.78 for MAE CLS), with PCA matching only for DINO SEQ (0.92 vs. 0.88) and FS CLS (0.93 vs. 0.92). Against the multi-dimensional linear MCD baseline, NLMCD wins in all 8 settings, with the largest gaps for DINO CLS (0.91 vs. 0.62) and CLIP CLS (0.96 vs. 0.73). The spherical KMeans baseline is the closest competitor, matching NLMCD for DINO and MAE SEQ tokens (0.98 vs. 0.98) and for CLIP and MAE CLS tokens, but NLMCD still leads in the remaining four settings. Against CKA, NLMCD-CBA is essentially en-par (differences within one standard error in most cells), confirming that the concept-based measure captures the same layer-distance structure; however, CBA uniquely provides a pairwise concept-distance decomposition (Eq. 3) that CKA's single scalar cannot offer, enabling the fine-grained concept atlas visualizations and inter-model concept matching analyses in Sec. 4.3.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the concept discovery lineage from Vielhaben et al.'s multi-dimensional linear subspaces (MCD) by generalizing concepts to non-linear manifolds, motivated by the manifold hypothesis and growing evidence against linearity assumptions. For alignment measurement, it departs from the dominant scalar paradigm (CKA and its variants) by leveraging the generalized fuzzy Rand index of Hullermeier et al. to decompose alignment into pairwise concept distances. The combination of density-based clustering (HDBSCAN on UMAP embeddings) for concept discovery with this fine-grained alignment metric is the paper's core methodological contribution, positioning it between the interpretability-focused concept discovery literature and the representational similarity measurement literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical implication is that practitioners selecting pre-trained ViTs gain a fine-grained diagnostic tool: the finding that increased supervision (FS > CLIP > DINO/MAE) correlates with reduced semantic organization in later layers suggests that fully supervised models may generalize less well to downstream tasks despite higher benchmark accuracy. Limitations include computational scalability (HDBSCAN subsampling, ~6 hours for fine-grained inter-model analysis), restriction to a single architecture (ViT-B/16) and dataset (ImageNet-1k), and the assumption that concepts form connected low-dimensional manifolds. Open questions include whether the manifold-based concept definition and the supervision-organization tradeoff hold for larger models, different architectures, or non-ImageNet domains.
