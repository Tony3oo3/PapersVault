## Problem

While gradient-based optimization routinely achieves near-zero training loss, the theoretical behavior of gradient flows in the unbounded regime—where parameters grow without bound—remains poorly understood. Most existing convergence analyses assume or imply that iterates stay within a bounded region, yet divergence is known to occur in simple settings (e.g., linear classifiers with logistic activation). This paper asks: for a broad class of smooth activation functions and polynomial target functions, does the gradient flow necessarily diverge to infinity when the optimal loss is zero but unattainable?

## Input / Output

The framework takes a fully connected feedforward architecture with a SAD (Sublinear Analytic Definable) activation function (logistic, tanh, softplus, GELU, swish, Mish), a polynomial target function of degree at least 2, a finite training set or C¹ density, and a C¹ definable loss (MSE, cross-entropy, Huber). It produces a rigorous dichotomy: the gradient flow either converges to a critical point or diverges with parameter norm → ∞ while loss → 0, and it guarantees the existence of an explicit threshold ε > 0 such that any initialization with loss below inf + ε triggers divergence. The empirical component tracks parameter norm growth trajectories alongside loss curves across polynomial regression, PDE solving (Heat, Black-Scholes via Deep Kolmogorov), and MNIST classification.

## Method

The core mechanism is a two-part argument built on o-minimal geometry. First, a dichotomy theorem (Theorem 2.8) leverages Kurdyka's definable Łojasiewicz inequality and the finiteness of generalized critical values in Pfaffian o-minimal structures to show that any gradient flow for a C¹ definable loss either converges to a critical point or diverges to an asymptotic critical value at infinity. Second, a non-representability theorem (Theorem 3.4) proves that a SAD neural network—whose sublinearity prevents global identity with a degree-≥2 polynomial, while analyticity and definability provide enough rigidity to detect the mismatch on a finite dataset—can never achieve exactly zero loss on a sufficiently large training set. Combined with a standard approximation result (Theorem 3.5) establishing infimum loss = 0, this forces the gradient flow into the divergent branch of the dichotomy whenever the initialization is sufficiently close to optimal. The SAD framework (sublinear + analytic + definable) is the key abstraction that unifies the proof across all common smooth activations.

## Results

The paper's main contribution is theoretical: it proves a dichotomy (Theorem 2.8) showing that gradient flows for definable neural networks either converge to a critical point or diverge to infinity with the loss converging to an asymptotic critical value, and a divergence result (Corollary 3.6) showing that for polynomial target functions of degree ≥ 2 with sufficiently large architecture and dataset, the gradient flow diverges to infinity while the loss converges to zero. Empirically, across polynomial approximation (1D, 2D, 4D inputs), PDE solving (Heat and Black-Scholes via Deep Kolmogorov Method), and MNIST classification, the authors consistently observe parameter norm growth (logarithmic under GD, faster under Adam) while the loss converges to near-zero, confirming the theoretical predictions. No single quantitative benchmark metric is reported; the experiments are described as proof-of-concept validations of the divergence phenomenon.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is primarily a theoretical paper without traditional baseline comparisons; the `compares_to` list is empty. The numerical experiments in Section 4 serve to validate the theoretical divergence predictions rather than to benchmark against competing optimization methods. The authors test five SAD activation functions (logistic, tanh, softplus, GELU, swish) under both gradient descent and Adam, observing qualitatively identical parameter-norm-growth-across-all-settings behavior. For GD, the parameter norm grows logarithmically (consistent with the O(√t) bound from the gradient flow analysis and the logarithmic rate in [67, Theorem 4.3]), while Adam's adaptive learning rate accelerates both loss convergence and parameter growth. The PDE and MNIST experiments extend the observation to realistic tasks, showing the divergence phenomenon is not an artifact of the simplified polynomial setting. The authors explicitly note that their experiments are limited in dataset variety and size, positioning them as proof-of-concept rather than comprehensive empirical evaluation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the o-minimal structure framework from model theory (Wilkie, van den Dries, Pillay–Steinhorn) and applies it to neural network optimization in a novel way. It complements prior divergence results that were restricted to specific settings—homogeneous networks [67, 78], linear classifiers with logistic activation [70, 39], or assumed absence of global minimizers [70]—by establishing a general dichotomy for a broad class of smooth (SAD) activation functions and proving divergence for all polynomial targets of degree ≥2. The convergence-to-critical-point half of the dichotomy relies on Kurdyka's definable Lojasiewicz inequality [60] and d'Acuto's finiteness of generalized critical values [17], while the non-representability of polynomials by sublinear networks draws on standard universal approximation machinery [21, 28]. The SAD framework itself is a new theoretical abstraction that unifies the relevant properties (sublinearity, analyticity, definability) of common activations like tanh, GELU, and softplus.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first general theoretical explanation for why parameter norms grow unboundedly during training with smooth activations—a phenomenon observed empirically but previously lacking a clean characterization. The threshold ε result (guaranteeing convergence to optimal loss for sufficiently good initialization) is noted as 'unnoticed in the deep learning literature' and could inform initialization strategies. Key limitations include inapplicability to ReLU (where global minima exist for shallow networks), inability to extend to discrete-time SGD, and the requirement of C¹ or analytic activations. Open questions include whether directional convergence holds in exponential o-minimal structures (relevant to GELU) and whether generalized smoothness conditions can bridge the gap to SGD convergence proofs.
