## Problem

Sampling from unnormalised discrete distributions is fundamental in Bayesian inference, statistical physics, and combinatorial optimisation, yet MCMC methods suffer from slow mixing and poor convergence in high-dimensional combinatorial state spaces. Existing neural samplers either require access to training data (as in discrete diffusion and flow models) or face prohibitive computational costs when the partition function is intractable. This paper addresses the data-free setting: learning an efficient sampler from only an unnormalised target density, without any samples from the target.

## Input / Output

The method takes as input an unnormalised target density ρ(x) defined over a discrete state space {1,…,S}^d (e.g., an energy function E(x) defining p(x) ∝ exp(−E(x))), and optionally a graph structure for combinatorial problems. It produces samples from the normalised target π(x) = ρ(x)/Z via Euler integration of the learned CTMC, a trained energy function when used for EBM training, or combinatorial solutions (e.g., maximum independent sets). The rate matrix is restricted to single-coordinate flips, reducing per-step complexity from O(S^d) to O(S×d), and the method operates entirely without training data from the target distribution.

## Method

DNFS learns a time-dependent rate matrix R_t^θ for a continuous-time Markov chain whose dynamics satisfy the Kolmogorov forward equation along an annealing interpolation path p_t ∝ ρ^t η^(1−t). Two key technical challenges are addressed: (1) the intractable partition-function derivative ∂_t log Z_t is estimated via a control-variate construction grounded in the discrete Stein identity, enabling a low-variance coordinate-descent training loop that alternates between updating rate-matrix parameters θ and a scalar baseline c_t; (2) the per-sample loss evaluation cost is reduced from O(|N(x)|) network calls to O(1) by reparameterising the rate matrix as a one-way matrix (via a ReLU of a locally equivariant network G) so that the forward and reverse rates are computed from a single network evaluation. The locally equivariant transformer (leTF) enforces the equivariance constraint G(τ,i|x) = −G(x_i,i|Swap(x,i,τ)) using hollow transformers—two autoregressive transformer stacks per layer (left-to-right and right-to-left) whose outputs at position i are independent of the i-th input token—followed by a token-embedding inner product to produce the scalar rate.

## Results

On the Maximum Independent Set (MIS) problem over Erdős-Rényi graphs, DNFS+DMALA achieves approximation ratios of 99.89%, 97.88%, and 97.62% relative to GUROBI on ER16-20, ER32-40, and ER64-75 respectively, with inference times of 0:10, 0:15, and 0:22. DNFS alone (without MCMC refinement) achieves 92.83%, 90.15%, and 88.2% approximation ratios, substantially outperforming the untrained random baseline (58.4%, 43.2%, 42.0%). On the 10×10 Ising model, DNFS achieves a lower training loss than LEAPS with leConv and produces more accurate energy histograms, while on EBM training (probability mass estimation), DNFS consistently outperforms PCD-MCMC across most settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the MIS problem, DNFS+DMALA nearly matches the GUROBI oracle (0.11% drop on ER16-20) while running ~24× faster, and substantially outperforms both the MCMC-based DMALA baseline (1.23% drop) and GFlowNet (1.91% drop) on the smallest graph, with the gap narrowing on larger graphs. DNFS alone underperforms both DMALA and GFlowNet, likely because GFlowNet restricts sampling to feasible solutions along the trajectory, reducing the exploration space. On the 10×10 Ising model, DNFS achieves a lower training loss and more accurate energy histogram than LEAPS with leConv, and significantly outperforms GFlowNet which fails to capture the correct energy mode; LEAPS with leTF achieves comparable ESS but occasionally produces samples in smoother energy regions. For discrete EBM training, DNFS consistently outperforms PCD-MCMC across most probability mass estimation settings, though ED-GFN (which incorporates a Metropolis-Hastings correction) achieves slightly better performance. The paper also shows that leTF outperforms leAttn, leMLP, and leConv in estimation error and effective sample size, confirming the importance of network expressiveness for locally equivariant architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

DNFS sits at the intersection of CTMC-based discrete diffusion (Austin et al., Sun et al., Campbell et al.) and discrete flow matching (Campbell et al. 2024, Gat et al. 2024), but operates in the data-free setting where only an unnormalised target is available. Its closest competitor is LEAPS (Holderrieth et al. 2025), which also learns a CTMC rate matrix via locally equivariant networks but derives the objective from importance-weight minimisation rather than the Kolmogorov forward equation; the paper shows these two perspectives converge to similar losses. The architectural contribution, leTF, builds on hollow networks (Chen & Duvenaud 2019) and the locally equivariant framework of Holderrieth et al., replacing their MLP/convolutional/attention layers with a bidirectional autoregressive Transformer to improve expressiveness while preserving the local equivariance constraint.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DNFS provides a principled, data-free framework for discrete sampling that unifies EBM training and combinatorial optimisation under a single Kolmogorov-equation objective, with the control-variate interpretation offering a clear variance-reduction mechanism. However, the method is currently restricted to binary state spaces, struggles to scale to high dimensions due to exploding loss from the ratio summation, and does not outperform MCMC (DMALA) or GFlowNet on combinatorial optimisation. Open questions include extending beyond binary settings without introducing bias, stabilising training in high dimensions, and integrating Metropolis-Hastings corrections or feasibility constraints into the flow dynamics.
