## Problem

Overparametrized neural networks trained by gradient flow can interpolate noisy data yet still generalize well, but the mechanistic reason—how the training dynamics implicitly selects low-complexity solutions—remains poorly understood. In particular, the timescale relationship between feature learning (which improves generalization) and overfitting (which degrades it) was not characterized, leaving open when test error begins to increase and how this depends on network width.

## Input / Output

The method takes a two-layer network with m hidden neurons, d-dimensional Gaussian inputs, and n samples from a k-index model y = φ(U^T x) + ε, parameterized by the overparametrization ratio α = n/(md) and initialization scale a₀. It produces quantitative predictions for train error, test error, generalization error, and the ℓ₁ norm of second-layer weights as functions of training time, including a sharp interpolation threshold γ*_GF(α) and the non-monotone test error curve. The analysis operates in the proportional asymptotic regime n,d→∞ with n/d→ᾱ, followed by m→∞ at fixed α, and is validated against SGD simulations on finite networks.

## Method

The core technique is Dynamical Mean Field Theory (DMFT) from nonequilibrium statistical physics, applied to gradient flow on a two-layer network. The empirical risk is replaced by a Gaussian process with matching mean and covariance, yielding a closed system of integral-differential equations for weight correlations, latent-space projections, and second-layer weights. In the large-m limit, singular perturbation theory reveals a separation of timescales: feature learning occurs on O(1) times (reducing to mean field ODEs for the latent projection and second-layer weight), while overfitting and feature unlearning occur on O(m) times, driven by the slow adiabatic growth of the second-layer weight norm γ(t) toward the interpolation threshold. The fast dynamics at fixed γ matches the pure-noise interpolation problem, so the generalization error becomes O(1) only when γ(t) reaches O(1). Two rigorous non-asymptotic theorems confirm the picture: one bounds the generalization error for t = o((n/d)^(1/4)) under mean field initialization, and another shows the mean field equations track the actual dynamics up to t = O((log m)^(1/3)).

## Results

The paper identifies three dynamical regimes in large two-layer networks under gradient flow: (i) mean-field feature learning at t=O(1) where train and test errors decrease with negligible generalization gap; (ii) extended feature learning at 1≪t≪m where second-layer weights grow as 1≪‖W₂nd‖₁≪√m while generalization error remains o(1); and (iii) overfitting/feature unlearning at t≳m where the generalization error becomes O(1), train error reaches 0 (interpolation), and test error converges to a kernel-method value. Two rigorous theorems confirm the time-scale separation: Theorem 1 bounds the generalization error by C₁(L²(a₀+a₁t̂)²+τ²)·√(d/n), showing overfitting cannot occur before t̂=Ω((n/d)^(1/4)); Theorem 2 proves the DMFT tracks mean-field ODEs up to T_lb=c₀(log m)^(1/3)∧(log n/d)^(1/3) with error O((1/m∨1/d∨d/n)^(1/2−δ)). DMFT predictions are validated against SGD simulations on actual networks (d=200, n=αmd, α=0.3) with close agreement in train/test error curves and second-layer weight evolution.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper without traditional method-vs-method benchmark comparisons. Instead, the DMFT framework is validated against three reference points: (1) NTK/lazy theory — the paper shows that lazy initialization (a(0)=γ₀√m) with γ₀>γ*_GF skips the feature-learning phase entirely, recovering the NTK prediction that test error equals the best linear approximation (Eq. 2.7), while mean-field initialization (a(0)=a₀=O(1)) achieves the Bayes error τ²/2, a strict improvement; (2) Mean-field theory of [38,14] — Theorem 2 rigorously proves the DMFT reduces to the (k+1)-dimensional ODE system of [38,14] on timescales t=o((log m)^(1/3)), confirming the first regime; (3) Numerical SGD simulations — Figures 2–5 show DMFT curves closely tracking SGD results on actual two-layer networks (d=200, averaged over 10 runs) for both pure noise and single-index data, despite the Gaussian approximation and GF-vs-SGD difference. The paper also contrasts its findings with the pure-noise interpolation threshold of [60], showing that the interpolation threshold γ*_GF(α) for gradient flow is strictly below the Gaussian complexity bound L_σ⁻¹τ√(n/d) from [7], revealing a gap between what is possible in principle and what gradient flow actually achieves.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper applies dynamical mean field theory (DMFT) from nonequilibrium statistical physics—building on the Cugliandolo-Kurchan framework and the rigorous DMFT for first-order methods of Celentano, Cheng, and Montanari [13]—to analyze gradient flow in two-layer networks. It unifies two previously separate theoretical threads: the mean field theory of feature learning (Mei, Montanari, Nguyen [38]; Chizat and Bach [14]) and the NTK/lazy-training theory (Jacot et al. [30]), by showing they correspond to different timescales within a single dynamical picture. The Gaussian process approximation of the empirical risk, combined with singular perturbation analysis in the large-width limit, is the key technical device that makes the multi-timescale structure tractable, going beyond what either the mean field ODEs or NTK linearization can capture alone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The discovery of a clean timescale separation—feature learning at t=O(1) versus overfitting at t=O(m)—provides a mechanistic explanation for why early stopping regularizes and why larger networks overfit later, reconciling the feature-learning and NTK paradigms that have been treated as competing explanations. The 'feature unlearning' phenomenon (test error increasing after initially decreasing) is a concrete, quantitative prediction that explains the non-monotone training curves observed empirically. Key limitations include the restriction to two-layer networks, the reliance on a Gaussian approximation whose error is only empirically validated at large times, the focus on k=1 index models, and the fact that only two non-asymptotic theorems are proven while the full DMFT predictions remain heuristic. Open questions include extending the analysis to k≥2, deeper architectures, and SGD (as opposed to gradient flow), and making the singular perturbation analysis fully rigorous.
