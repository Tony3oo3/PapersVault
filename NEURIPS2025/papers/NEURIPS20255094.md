## Problem

Full-waveform inversion at global scale is bottlenecked by the extreme computational cost of spectral-element forward modeling, limiting the number of feasible iterations and the exploration of model parameter space. While ML-based surrogates show promise for accelerating seismic modeling, no comprehensive 3D global synthetic dataset exists to train and benchmark such models—existing FWI benchmarks (OpenFWI, E-FWI) are confined to local subsurface scales and do not capture planetary-scale wave physics.

## Input / Output

Inputs are compact vectors of real spherical harmonic coefficients (up to degree 8) encoding ±10% velocity perturbations on 1D background models (uniform or PREM), plus source parameters (location and moment tensor), yielding 405, 1215, or 5427 free parameters across the three tiers. Outputs come in two forms: (1) 3D wavefield snapshots at 15–20 timesteps with 16 azimuthal slices and 3,648–16,470 radial element points per slice, also stored in the azimuthal Fourier domain for compact continuous representation, and (2) 1D displacement time series (length 150 or 6,000) at 1,369 or 703 surface stations. For inversion tasks, the target output is the velocity structure itself (spherical harmonic coefficients), predicted directly from seismogram inputs.

## Method

The dataset is generated by AxiSEM3D (a pseudospectral/spectral-element global solver) using Latin Hypercube Sampling over spherical harmonic coefficient space, producing three tiers of increasing physical complexity: acoustic fluid sphere (1 km, 20 Hz), elastic isotropic solid (1 km, 20 Hz), and full PREM Earth (6,371 km, 30 s period) with solid–fluid coupling at the core–mantle boundary. ML baselines span vector-based (MLP, H-Fourier) and point-based (DeepONet with an optimized single-branch-forward design that reduces memory by (4+m)/(4+m/n)×, GNOT) architectures, plus a physics-informed variant (PIDO) that enforces the acoustic/elastic PDEs via automatic differentiation during training. For inversion, the paper demonstrates three strategies: gradient-based LBFGS optimization using ML forward-model gradients (200 iterations), multi-start sampling (1,000 LHS initial models) to escape local minima, and direct seismogram-to-structure mapping via InversionNet-3D and InversionMLP. The key empirical finding is that trained neural operators achieve ~60,000× speedup over the 120-second/24-core numerical solver, enabling the multi-start and extended-iteration strategies that overcome the ill-posedness inherent in traditional FWI.

## Results

On the Acoustic tier, GNOT achieves the best wavefield prediction (RL2=0.300, R=0.954), while MLP leads on seismogram prediction (R=0.896). On the more complex Elastic tier, MLP achieves the best wavefield RL2 (0.617) and R (0.790), with all neural operator baselines showing degraded performance due to P/S wave interactions. For inversion, the direct InversionMLP mapping achieves R=0.826, MAE=0.253, and RMSE=0.335, substantially outperforming optimization-based approaches (best: MLP+L-BFGS at R=0.415). ML forward modeling runs in 1–3 ms on a single GPU versus 120 s for numerical simulation, a ~60,000× speedup.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper benchmarks five forward-modeling architectures on GlobalTomo's Acoustic and Elastic tiers. GNOT [21] achieves the best Acoustic wavefield prediction (RL2=0.300, R=0.954), outperforming DeepONet [39] (RL2=0.368, R=0.927) by ~17% in RL2, but MLP surpasses both on the Elastic tier (RL2=0.617 vs. 0.682 for DeepONet and 0.665 for GNOT), suggesting vector-based representations handle the added P/S complexity more effectively. For direct inversion, InversionMLP (R=0.826, MAE=0.253) outperforms InversionNet-3D from OpenFWI [10] (R=0.794, MAE=0.272), indicating that a simpler MLP backbone is more effective than a 3D CNN for mapping seismograms to spherical-harmonic velocity structures. All optimization-based inversion strategies (MLP+L-BFGS, DeepONet+L-BFGS, H-Fourier+L-BFGS) achieve substantially lower R values (0.16–0.42) than direct mapping, highlighting the advantage of end-to-end learned inversion over iterative gradient descent in this setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the neural operator learning literature (DeepONet, GNOT, FNO) and physics-informed neural network approaches to the specific domain of global-scale seismic wavefield modeling, where prior ML-based FWI work (OpenFWI, E-FWI) was confined to local-scale, mesh-parameterized, finite-difference-simulated scenarios. It departs from the geophysics tradition of adjoint-state FWI with spectral element solvers by introducing the first 3D global full-waveform synthetic dataset (GlobalTomo) generated via AxiSEM3D, parameterized with spherical harmonics up to degree 8 rather than mesh grids, and covering the full Earth from surface to core. The baselines build directly on NVIDIA Modulus Sym and adapt DeepONet with an efficient parallel trunk-branch multiplication scheme, while the inversion strategies (gradient-based with LBFGS and direct mapping via InversionNet-3D) follow established ML-inversion paradigms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GlobalTomo fills a critical gap by providing the first comprehensive 3D global-scale benchmark for physics-ML seismic modeling, enabling the community to train and evaluate neural operators for planetary-scale wave propagation without the prohibitive cost of repeated numerical simulations. The demonstrated ~60,000× speedup over AxiSEM3D for forward modeling opens the door to iterative global FWI with ML, which was previously infeasible. Key limitations include the degree-8 spherical harmonic cap (limiting resolution to long-wavelength heterogeneity), the absence of attenuation, and the purely synthetic nature of the data; open questions include scaling to higher degrees, incorporating real seismograms, and extending to anisotropic and 3D heterogeneous PREM variants. The dataset also has direct relevance to planetary seismology (Mars, Moon) where observational data are extremely sparse.
