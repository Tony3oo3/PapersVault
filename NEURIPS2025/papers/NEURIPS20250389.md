## Problem

Existing neural methods for multi-objective combinatorial optimization (MOCOPs) rely on a single, capacity-limited model to solve all scalarized subproblems, which overcomplicates the learning task and yields suboptimal Pareto fronts. Additionally, they train via REINFORCE with scalarized rewards, suffering from high gradient variance and inefficient exploration in the vast combinatorial action space. The paper addresses both limitations simultaneously through adaptive model structure selection and preference-based training.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a graph-structured MOCOP instance (e.g., node coordinates for MOTSP, node coordinates plus demands for MOCVRP, item weights and profit vectors for MOKP) together with a weight vector λ that defines a specific scalarized subproblem. It outputs a complete solution (node sequence or item subset) for that subproblem; by solving N subproblems with different weight vectors, the ensemble approximates the full Pareto front. The framework is plug-and-play: it augments existing encoder-decoder solvers (CNH, WE-CA) by inserting the CCO block into the decoder and replacing the REINFORCE loss, without altering the encoder or the overall autoregressive decoding procedure.

## Method

POCCO introduces two complementary mechanisms. First, a Conditional Computation (CCO) block is inserted between the multi-head attention and compatibility layers in the decoder: a sparse Top-k gating network routes each subproblem's context vector through either a subset of m feed-forward experts or a parameter-free identity expert, effectively combining mixture-of-experts (width scaling) with mixture-of-depths (depth skipping) so that different subproblems follow distinct computational paths and specialize. Second, the training objective replaces REINFORCE with a Bradley-Terry preference loss: for each subproblem the policy samples two solutions, the better one (by scalarized objective) is designated the winner, and the model maximizes the BT likelihood σ(β[f_θ(π^w) − f_θ(π^l)]) where f_θ is the length-normalized average log-likelihood (a SimPO-style implicit reward). This pairwise, centered signal reduces gradient variance by 2–4 orders of magnitude compared to REINFORCE, enabling faster and more stable convergence while encouraging exploration of high-quality regions of the solution space.

## Results

On Bi-TSP100, POCCO-W-Aug achieves a hypervolume (HV) of 0.7078, matching or exceeding all neural baselines and approaching WS-LKH (0.7090) while running ~26× faster (14 min vs. 6 h). On MOCVRP100, POCCO-W-Aug attains HV 0.4089, outperforming the best multi-model baseline EMNH-Aug (0.4079) and the single-model SOTA WE-CA (0.4068). On Bi-KP200, POCCO-W reaches HV 0.3603, matching the exact dynamic-programming solver WS-DP (0.3601) in seconds rather than hours. Across all four problem families and three sizes, POCCO-W consistently outperforms its backbone WE-CA by 0.1–0.5% HV, and POCCO-C outperforms CNH by a similar margin.

## Baselines & Comparisons

POCCO-W outperforms its backbone WE-CA on every benchmark: e.g., on Bi-TSP100 HV rises from 0.7034 to 0.7055 (+0.3%), and on MOCVRP100 from 0.4068 to 0.4084 (+0.4%). Similarly, POCCO-C beats CNH on all settings (Bi-TSP100: 0.7047 vs. 0.7019; MOCVRP100: 0.4079 vs. 0.4065). Against multi-model neural baselines (DRL-MOA, MDRL, EMNH), POCCO-W achieves higher HV with a single shared model and shorter inference time (36 s vs. 14–15 s per instance set, but without the multi-model training overhead). With instance augmentation, POCCO-W-Aug (Bi-TSP100 HV 0.7078) surpasses EMNH-Aug (0.7016) and PMOCO-Aug (0.7071), and approaches the exact WS-LKH solver (0.7090) at ~26× lower runtime. On MOCVRP100, POCCO-W-Aug (0.4089) edges out EMNH-Aug (0.4079) and WE-CA-Aug (0.4089, tied). On Bi-KP200, POCCO-W (0.3603) matches the exact dynamic-programming baseline WS-DP (0.3601) in 1.3 min versus 5.8 h, and on Tri-TSP100, POCCO-W-Aug (0.5048) narrows the gap to WS-LKH (0.5076) to just 0.5% HV while running ~3× faster.

## Relation to Prior Work

POCCO is positioned as a plug-and-play augmentation of two state-of-the-art neural MOCOP solvers (CNH and WE-CA), which themselves represent the one-to-many paradigm of training a single weight-conditioned model for all scalarized subproblems. Architecturally, the conditional computation block (CCO) synthesizes ideas from sparsely-gated mixture-of-experts (Shazeer et al., 2017) and mixture-of-depths (Raposo et al., 2024) into a unified routing mechanism that lets subproblems select between parameterized FF experts and a parameter-free identity path. On the training side, the preference-driven objective adapts the SimPO-style implicit reward (average log-likelihood) and Bradley-Terry pairwise framework from LLM alignment to combinatorial optimization, replacing the REINFORCE gradient that all prior neural MOCOP methods rely on.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses two persistent bottlenecks in neural MOCOP solving—monolithic model capacity limits and REINFORCE's high-variance, exploration-poor gradients—with a modular design that can be dropped into any existing solver. The demonstrated 2–4 order-of-magnitude gradient variance reduction and the finding that the ID-expert component of CCO is critical for out-of-distribution size generalization (where pure MoE variants degrade) provide actionable design guidance. However, the paper acknowledges limitations: it does not handle complex real-world constraints, scales only to moderate instance sizes (n≤200), and the preference pairs are generated by the same policy (self-play), which may limit exploration diversity compared to external evaluators. Open questions include whether the CCO routing generalizes to many-objective settings (κ>3) and whether constraint-aware variants can be built on this foundation.
