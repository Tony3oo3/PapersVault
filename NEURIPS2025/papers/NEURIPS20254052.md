## Problem

Existing structure-based drug design methods suffer from three key limitations: autoregressive models accumulate errors through unnatural generation orders, diffusion-based models exhibit unstable probability dynamics leading to inconsistent molecular quality, and all non-autoregressive approaches determine the number of atoms in generated molecules by sampling from predefined distributions derived from reference ligands, causing systematic mismatches between ligand size and binding pocket geometry.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254052/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D protein pocket as input, represented by atom coordinates and one-hot features (element type, amino acid type, backbone flag), with the protein center of mass shifted to zero for SE(3) invariance. It outputs a full 3D ligand molecule consisting of atomic coordinates (R^{N_M × 3}) and discrete atom types (one-hot over C, N, O, F, P, S, Cl with aromaticity). Notably, the number of atoms N_M is not sampled from a reference-ligand distribution but predicted by a separate MLP from pocket geometry descriptors (pocket atom count, volume, surface area, and space size), decoupling molecule size from any specific reference ligand.

## Method

PAFlow formulates molecule generation as ODE integration under a Flow Matching framework, using the Variance Preserving (VP) Gaussian path for continuous atomic coordinates and a newly derived Conditional Flow Matching objective for discrete atom types (categorical path with vector field u_t^a = ᾱ'_{1-t}(a_1 - a_0)). An SE(3)-equivariant GNN (9-layer transformer, hidden_dim=128, n_head=16) jointly predicts the clean coordinates x̂_1, atom types â_1, and a binding affinity score ŷ, with the affinity predictor providing prior-guided generation: the coordinate vector field is augmented by γ·(ᾱ'_{1-t}/2ᾱ_{1-t})·∇log p(y=1|m_t), steering the ODE trajectory toward higher-affinity regions. A separate 4-layer MLP atom number predictor, trained on pocket geometry features, outputs the atom count with a small Gaussian noise term (δ=0.01) to encourage exploration and compensate for prediction uncertainty. Training uses a joint loss (MSE for coordinates, KL divergence for atom types, MSE for affinity) with the atom number predictor trained independently; sampling proceeds via a 50-step Euler ODE solver, achieving 5.5× speedup over diffusion baselines.

## Results

On the CrossDocked2020 benchmark, PAFlow achieves a new state-of-the-art Avg. Vina Score of -8.31 (median -8.92), surpassing the strongest diffusion baseline ALiDiff (-7.07) by 17.5% and the BFN-based MolCRAFT (-6.59) by 26.1%. PAFlow also leads on Vina Dock (-9.46 vs. -8.90 for ALiDiff) and High Affinity (80.8% vs. 73.4%), while maintaining comparable QED (0.49), SA (0.57), and Diversity (0.71) to ALiDiff. On the Binding MOAD dataset (Table 5), PAFlow similarly outperforms all baselines, achieving -7.33 Avg. Vina Score vs. -6.64 for IPDiff and -7.33 for MolCRAFT.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PAFlow decisively outperforms all 10 baselines across every binding-affinity metric on CrossDocked2020. Against the strongest diffusion method ALiDiff, PAFlow improves Avg. Vina Score by 17.5% (-8.31 vs. -7.07), Vina Min by 8.7%, and Vina Dock by 6.3%, while matching ALiDiff on QED (0.49 vs. 0.50) and SA (0.57 vs. 0.57). Against the BFN-based MolCRAFT, the margins are even larger: 26.1% on Vina Score and 19.4% on Vina Dock. The other FM-based method, FlowSBDD, performs poorly (Vina Score -3.62) due to its use of linear probability paths that inadequately capture SBDD complexity, highlighting PAFlow's advantage from using the VP path. Among autoregressive baselines, Pocket2Mol is the strongest (Vina Score -5.14) but still trails PAFlow by 3.17 points. On molecular properties, PAFlow maintains QED and SA comparable to ALiDiff while achieving slightly lower diversity (0.71 vs. 0.73), a minor trade-off for the substantial binding-affinity gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

PAFlow positions itself at the intersection of flow matching (Lipman et al., 2022) and structure-based drug design, adopting the VP probability path and SE(3)-equivariant GNN architecture from TargetDiff (Guan et al., 2023) but replacing iterative stochastic denoising with deterministic ODE-based sampling. It extends the guided-flow framework of Zheng et al. (2023) to the molecular generation setting by incorporating a protein-ligand interaction predictor that steers the coordinate vector field toward higher-affinity regions, analogous to prior guidance strategies in IPDiff and TAGMol. The key departure from prior non-autoregressive SBDD methods is the learnable atom number predictor, which eliminates the reliance on reference-ligand-derived size distributions, and the derivation of a CFM objective for discrete atom types that had not been previously formulated in this context.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PAFlow establishes a clear empirical case that flow matching with VP paths is a more effective generative backbone for SBDD than both diffusion (5.5× faster sampling, smoother trajectories) and rectified/linear flows (FlowSBDD's -3.62 vs. PAFlow's -8.31 Avg. Vina Score), which is a useful signal for the broader generative-modeling-for-molecules community. The 17.5% improvement over the previous SOTA (ALiDiff) on binding affinity, supported by ablations isolating the contributions of prior guidance and the atom predictor, makes this a strong reference point for future SBDD work. Open questions include whether the interaction predictor can be extended to jointly optimize molecular properties (QED, SA) alongside affinity, whether the approach generalizes beyond the CrossDocked2020/Binding MOAD benchmarks to more diverse or unseen protein families, and how the guidance strength γ trade-off between affinity and drug-likeness can be resolved in a principled multi-objective framework.
