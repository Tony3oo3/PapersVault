## Problem

Photorealistic dynamic hair for digital avatars remains unsolved: physics-based simulators require per-hairstyle parameter tuning, are non-differentiable, and are too slow for real-time use, while existing 3DGS/NeRF avatar methods apply only rigid transformations to hair, ignoring secondary motion, inertia, and body collisions. No scalable, data-driven framework exists that jointly learns temporally coherent hair deformation and motion-dependent appearance without explicit simulation.

## Input / Output

The method takes a canonical hair point cloud (~150K strands × 24 vertices), a sequence of head rotation poses, and an upper-body proxy mesh (head + shoulders) as input. It produces per-frame deformed hair point clouds and a set of cylindrical 3D Gaussian primitives whose color (SH coefficients), scale, and opacity are dynamically predicted, which are then differentially rasterized into 1024×1024 novel-view images. The volumetric SDF representation of both hair and body enables mesh-free deformation prediction, making the pipeline directly compatible with Gaussian-based avatar systems without intermediate mesh conversion.

## Method

DGH uses a two-stage coarse-to-fine architecture. In Stage I, a 3D U-Net encodes SDF volumes of the rigidly transformed hair and posed upper body into latent feature grids; an MLP then predicts per-point displacements (coarse stage), and a cross-attention module over the previous two frames' hair SDF volumes predicts residual 3D flow vectors to capture inertia, oscillation, and damping (fine stage). An SDF penalty loss prevents hair-body penetration. In Stage II, each hair segment is represented as a stretched cylindrical Gaussian, and a lightweight MLP conditioned on position, strand tangent, and view direction predicts view-dependent SH color, scale, and opacity; a curvature-based blending scheme interpolates adjacent Gaussian parameters to eliminate shading discontinuities in high-curvature regions. The entire pipeline is trained end-to-end on a synthetic XPBD-simulated dataset with differentiable rendering losses (L1, SSIM, LPIPS), requiring no physics engine or manual parameter tuning at inference.

## Results

On the synthetic dynamic hair dataset (5 subjects, 100 test frames each), DGH achieves an average PSNR of 27.01, SSIM of 0.871, and LPIPS of 0.127 for dynamic hair novel-view synthesis, consistently outperforming both Gaussian Haircut (PSNR 23.07) and 3DGS (PSNR 20.91) across all subjects. For hair deformation, DGH reduces the average L2 error to 0.0832 and Chamfer distance to 0.0266, roughly halving the error of rigid-transformed hair (L2 0.1639, Chamfer 0.0424). At inference, the dynamic hair model runs at 2.0 FPS for ~150K strands on a single A100 GPU, compared to 0.33 FPS for the XPBD physics simulator used to generate ground truth.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DGH outperforms both Gaussian Haircut [9] and 3DGS [34] on all three appearance metrics across all 5 subjects. Against Gaussian Haircut, DGH gains +3.94 dB PSNR (27.01 vs 23.07) and +0.024 SSIM, with a marginal LPIPS improvement (0.127 vs 0.131). Against vanilla 3DGS, the advantage is larger: +6.10 dB PSNR (27.01 vs 20.91) and +0.049 SSIM, reflecting that without strand-aware representation and motion-dependent appearance, 3DGS degrades significantly under dynamic occlusion. For deformation, DGH halves the L2 error of rigid-transformed hair (0.0832 vs 0.1639) and reduces Chamfer distance by ~37% (0.0266 vs 0.0424). In runtime, DGH's learned deformation model runs at 2.0 FPS for 150K strands, approximately 6× faster than the XPBD physics-based simulation (0.33 FPS) that serves as the ground-truth generator, while the appearance renderer achieves 2.22 FPS versus 0.17 FPS for Blender's hair shader.

## Relation to Prior Work

DGH builds directly on the 3D Gaussian Splatting rendering pipeline and the cylindrical Gaussian hair primitive introduced by GaussianHair and Gaussian Haircut, but departs fundamentally from the physics-based simulation paradigm (XPBD) that has dominated dynamic hair modeling. Where prior Gaussian hair methods (GaussianHair, Gaussian Haircut) reconstruct static geometry and rely on external simulators for animation, and where 3DGS avatar methods treat hair as a rigid attachment, DGH introduces a learned volumetric deformation model (3D U-Net + MLP + cross-attention) that predicts hair motion end-to-end from head poses without explicit simulation. The mesh-free SDF-volume formulation is a conceptual departure from the mesh-based pipelines required by physics engines, enabling direct integration with Gaussian avatar representations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DGH provides the first fully data-driven, differentiable pipeline for dynamic hair deformation and appearance in the 3DGS domain, eliminating the per-hairstyle parameter tuning and mesh-conversion overhead of physics-based approaches. The released synthetic dataset (150K strands, 10K frames per hairstyle, multi-view renders) addresses a critical gap in the field where no real dynamic hair tracking data exists. Key limitations remain: the model is trained and evaluated exclusively on synthetic data, handles only head-driven motion (no full-body dynamics like jumping), is not yet real-time for dense grooms (2.22 FPS at 150K strands), and does not support relighting. Open questions include generalization to real captures, full-body motion conditioning, and whether the learned deformation prior can substitute for simulation on truly novel hairstyles.
