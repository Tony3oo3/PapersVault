## Problem

Existing MLLMs for 3D scene understanding depend on explicit dense 3D inputs such as point clouds, depth maps, or reconstructed BEV maps, which are difficult to obtain in real-world settings. Moreover, standard MLLMs process video frames as independent tokens through a visual encoder, failing to capture inter-frame correspondences and 3D geometric structure, making spatial reasoning both challenging and resource-intensive. The paper asks whether MLLMs can understand and reason in 3D directly from RGB video without any explicit 3D data input.

## Input / Output

The model accepts a sequence of RGB video frames (no depth, point clouds, or camera parameters) and a natural language query. Outputs are generated as plain text: 3D oriented bounding boxes in the format (x,y,z,w,h,d,ψ,θ,φ) with 2 decimal places, frame indices, object class labels, free-form descriptions, or multiple-choice/numerical spatial reasoning answers. For grounding and detection tasks, outputs are structured as JSON; for captioning, they are natural language. The MLLM backbone (Qwen2.5-VL) compresses spatially adjacent 2×2 patches into single tokens, reducing the token count fed to the language model.

## Method

VG LLM introduces a 3D visual geometry encoder (VGGT) that processes all input frames jointly to extract latent 3D geometric features encoding inter-frame correspondences and scene structure, running in parallel with the standard 2D visual encoder. The two feature streams are aligned to matching spatial dimensions (via a 2×2 merge + two-layer MLP on the geometry features) and fused by simple element-wise addition at the patch level before being fed to the Qwen2.5-VL backbone. During training, the visual encoders and multimodal connector are frozen while only the MLLM backbone is fine-tuned with a unified next-token prediction objective across mixed 3D scene understanding tasks (grounding, captioning, detection) and spatial reasoning data (SPAR-7M + LLaVA-Hound). Ablations show that directly adding latent geometry features outperforms cross-attention fusion, concatenation, and explicitly predicted spatial signals (depth, camera poses, point maps), because the latent representation simultaneously encodes multiple spatial cues while being more robust to per-signal estimation noise.

## Results

On VSI-Bench, VG LLM-4B achieves a 47.3% average score, surpassing the proprietary Gemini-1.5-Pro (45.4%) and all open-source models, while VG LLM-8B sets a new state-of-the-art at 50.7%. On ScanRefer (3D visual grounding without explicit 3D input), VG LLM-8B reaches 41.6% Acc@0.25 (57.6% with proposal refinement), outperforming SPAR-8B (31.9%) by 9.7 points. On 3D video object detection, incorporating 3D geometry boosts F1 by 11.2% (6-frame setting) for the 8B model over the Qwen2.5-VL-7B baseline. On CV-Bench, VG LLM-8B achieves 91.1% on 3D spatial tasks, the highest among all compared models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On VSI-Bench, VG LLM-4B (47.3%) outperforms Gemini-1.5-Pro (45.4%) by 1.9 points and SPAR-8B (41.1%) by 6.2 points, while VG LLM-8B (50.7%) extends the lead to 5.3 points over Gemini-1.5-Pro. On ScanRefer, VG LLM-8B (41.6% Acc@0.25) significantly beats SPAR-8B (31.9%) without requiring 3D scene input, though it trails Video-3D LLM (58.1%) and ChatScene (55.5%) which do use explicit 3D data; with proposal refinement, VG LLM-8B reaches 57.6%, closing the gap. On Scan2Cap, VG LLM-8B (80.0 CIDEr@0.5) matches Video-3D LLM (80.0) and slightly exceeds LLaVA-3D (79.2) using only RGB images. On CV-Bench 3D tasks, VG LLM-8B (91.1%) surpasses SPAR-8B (89.1%) and SAT-LLaVA-Video-7B (83.8%), confirming generalization beyond the training distribution. On 3D video object detection, the 3D geometry encoder consistently improves F1 by 8–11 points over the Qwen2.5-VL baseline across both 4-frame and 6-frame settings, with the largest gains on small objects like bins (+16.1 P25) and toilets (+14.6 P25).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on VGGT (Visual Geometry Grounded Transformer) as a pre-trained 3D geometry encoder and Qwen2.5-VL as the MLLM backbone, departing from prior 3D-aware MLLM approaches like Video-3D LLM (which injects explicit 3D coordinates into patch features) and GPT4Scene (which relies on reconstructed BEV maps from point clouds). The key conceptual shift is using the *latent* features of a 3D geometry model—rather than its explicit predictions (depth, camera poses, point maps)—as a geometry prior, fused via simple addition with 2D visual tokens. This avoids the estimation errors and data-acquisition bottlenecks of explicit 3D inputs while still capturing inter-frame correspondences.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that a 4B model with no explicit 3D input surpasses Gemini-1.5-Pro on VSI-Bench (47.3% vs. 45.4%) suggests that geometry-aware feature injection is a more efficient path to spatial reasoning than scaling model size alone. The ablation showing that naive feature addition outperforms cross-attention and concatenation is practically useful, simplifying the integration of geometry priors into existing MLLM pipelines. Open questions remain: whether the gains hold on truly out-of-distribution 3D scenes (VGGT was trained on ScanNet-family data), whether reinforcement learning or larger backbones would compound the benefits, and whether the approach extends to dynamic or outdoor scenes beyond the indoor benchmarks used here.
