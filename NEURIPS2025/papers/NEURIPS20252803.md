## Problem

Existing noisy label correction methods (sample selection, reweighting, label filtering) are inherently myopic—they make single-step decisions without considering long-term consequences or actively exploring alternative correction strategies. This limits their effectiveness in complex, instance-dependent noise scenarios where the optimal correction for one sample depends on the evolving state of the entire dataset. The paper asks whether formulating label correction as a sequential decision-making problem can overcome this limitation.

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard RGB image classification dataset with noisy labels (e.g., CIFAR-10/100 at 32×32, Animal-10N, Food-101N web images) where each instance has a soft label vector that may be corrupted. It produces two outputs: (1) a trajectory of progressively corrected soft label vectors over T′=25 time-steps, and (2) a fine-tuned prediction model (CNN backbone + classifier) trained on the final corrected labels. Labels are maintained as full probability vectors throughout, not collapsed to hard class indices, preserving uncertainty information for downstream training.

## Method

RLNLC formulates noisy label correction as a Markov Decision Process where the state is the full dataset with current labels, the action is a binary vector indicating which labels to correct, and the reward combines a label consistency term (negative KL divergence between each label and its k-NN attention-weighted prediction) with a noisy-label alignment term (KL divergence between corrected labels and their k-NN predictions from the uncorrected subset). The policy network computes per-instance correction probabilities by comparing the current label against an attention-weighted k-NN prediction in the embedding space of a pre-trained CNN feature extractor, then samples actions from Bernoulli distributions. An actor-critic (SARSA) framework learns the policy, with a key design choice of using a frozen backbone f_ω (decoupled from the policy's f_θ) for all reward evaluations to stabilize learning. The critic network receives a compact state encoding via a binning strategy that maps per-instance reward values into N_b bins, reducing the N-dimensional state to a fixed-length histogram vector for efficient Q-value estimation.

## Results

On CIFAR10-IDN, RLNLC achieves 95.8% test accuracy at 50% instance-dependent noise, outperforming the strongest baseline SSR (94.1%) by 1.7%. On CIFAR100-IDN, RLNLC leads SSR by 1.5–2.2% across all noise rates (e.g., 74.7% vs. 72.8% at 50% noise). On the real-world datasets, RLNLC reaches 90.2% on Animal-10N (vs. 89.0% for SURE) and 89.2% on Food-101N (vs. 87.3% for LongReMix). Under extreme 90% symmetric noise on CIFAR100, RLNLC attains 44.2%, surpassing DivideMix (31.0%) by 13.2 percentage points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RLNLC outperforms all 18 baselines across every dataset and noise setting. On CIFAR10-IDN, it surpasses the closest competitor SSR by 1.3–1.7% and DivideMix by 1.0–2.8% across noise rates 0.20–0.50. On CIFAR100-IDN, the margin over SSR is 1.5–2.2%, while the gap over DivideMix widens to 1.4–16.1% as noise increases (e.g., 74.7% vs. 58.6% at 50% noise). On Animal-10N, RLNLC leads SURE (89.0%) by 1.2% and SSR+ (88.5%) by 1.7%. On Food-101N, it exceeds LongReMix (87.3%) by 1.9% and PLC (85.2%) by 4.0%. Under 90% symmetric noise, RLNLC dominates DivideMix by 6.7 points on CIFAR10 (82.1% vs. 75.4%) and by 13.2 points on CIFAR100 (44.2% vs. 31.0%), while older methods like Co-teaching+ and Mixup drop below 47% and 44% respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by formulating noisy label correction as a reinforcement learning problem, departing from the dominant paradigms in the noisy-label literature (sample selection, reweighting, semi-supervised learning, and iterative label refinement). While prior work such as Co-teaching, DivideMix, and SSR addresses label noise through training dynamics or statistical estimation, none frame the correction process as sequential decision-making with a learned policy. The method repurposes standard actor-critic machinery (SARSA-based TD updates, policy gradient) and k-nearest-neighbor attention aggregation—tools well-known in their respective fields—into a unified RL loop where the state is the full dataset with current labels, the action is a binary correction vector, and the reward encodes label consistency and clean-noisy alignment. The broader trend of applying RL to ML pipeline optimization (RLHF, SSL-as-bandit, goal-conditioned photo finishing) provides conceptual precedent, but this is the first application specifically to noisy label correction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The RL formulation offers a principled way to make sequential, non-myopic label correction decisions that adapt to evolving data states, which is particularly relevant for instance-dependent noise where local context matters. However, the approach is limited to a single stationary distribution and does not address domain shift or class imbalance, and it requires a pre-trained backbone plus an additional RL training phase, adding computational overhead. Open questions include whether the RL formulation scales to very large datasets (the binning encoding is a pragmatic but lossy compression), whether the policy generalizes across noise types without retraining, and whether the reward design (KL-divergence-based) is optimal or could be replaced by learned reward models. The consistent 1–2% improvements over strong baselines like SSR and LongReMix across four datasets validate the approach, but the moderate margins suggest the RL framing, while elegant, has not yet unlocked a qualitative leap over well-tuned heuristic methods.
