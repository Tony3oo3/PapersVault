## Problem

Existing VLM benchmarks largely overlook color as a central visual feature, neither systematically probing how models perceive and reason about color nor measuring their stability under color perturbations. This leaves it unclear whether VLMs can leverage color cues the way humans do, or whether their predictions remain consistent when an image's colors are altered.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254803/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each perception/reasoning instance pairs a single RGB image with a text question and 3–6 multiple-choice options; the model outputs one letter. Each robustness instance consists of a seed image plus 9 recolored variants (hue shifted 90°, 180°, 270° in HSV space across three editing strategies) all paired with the same color-invariant question, yielding a binary per-instance robustness score (1 only if the model is correct on all 10 images). The full benchmark contains 1,448 instances and 5,814 image-text questions spanning 11 tasks.

## Method

COLORBENCH is organized into three dimensions—Color Perception (recognition, extraction, object recognition), Color Reasoning (proportion, comparison, counting, illusion, mimicry, blindness), and Color Robustness—comprising 11 fine-grained tasks with 1,448 instances. Data is curated from websites, public benchmarks, and code-generated images, with three rounds of human-in-the-loop filtering. For robustness, Grounded SAM segments each seed image from CV-Bench, and three recoloring strategies (Entire Image, Target Segment, Largest Segment) are each combined with three HSV hue shifts to produce 9 variants per seed, yielding 493 final seed images. The paper evaluates 32 VLMs (0.5B–78B open-source plus GPT-4o and Gemini-2-flash proprietary) under both direct and chain-of-thought prompting, reporting per-task accuracy and a model-level robustness score defined as the fraction of seed images on which the model remains correct across all color variants.

## Results

On COLORBENCH, the best proprietary model Gemini-2-flash (CoT) achieves 59.6% P&R Overall accuracy and 73.6% Robustness, while GPT-4o (CoT) reaches 57.4% and 69.9% respectively. Among open-source models, LLaVA-OV-72B leads P&R Overall at 51.9%, and InternVL2.5-78B achieves the highest Robustness at 86.2%. Human evaluators substantially outperform all VLMs on most tasks (e.g., 92.0% on Color Recognition, 94.0% on Color Blindness), and the gap between the best proprietary and best open-source model is only ~2% on P&R Overall, indicating color understanding remains largely neglected. CoT prompting yields an average +4.35% improvement on P&R Overall and +13.30% on Robustness across GPT-4o and Gemini-2-flash.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates 32 VLMs across 8 model families (Qwen2.5-VL, InternVL2/2.5, LLaVA-OV, LLaVA-Next, Cambrian, Eagle, GPT-4o, Gemini-2-flash). On P&R Overall, proprietary models lead: Gemini-2-flash (CoT) at 59.6% and GPT-4o (CoT) at 57.4%, but the best open-source model LLaVA-OV-72B (51.9%) trails by only ~8%, and GPT-o3 (API) extends the lead to 66.4%. On Robustness, the picture reverses: InternVL2.5-78B (86.2%) and InternVL2.5-38B (84.6) substantially outperform GPT-4o (46.2%), which is the least robust model evaluated, while Gemini-2-flash (70.7%) sits in the middle. Within each model family, larger models consistently outperform smaller ones (e.g., InternVL2.5 scales from 38.5% at 2B to 48.8% at 78B on P&R Overall), confirming a scaling law that is driven primarily by the language model rather than the vision encoder. CoT prompting improves most tasks for both proprietary models but notably degrades Color Illusion accuracy (−6.4% for GPT-4o, −3.2% for Gemini-2), and the Color Extraction task benefits most from reasoning (+14.6% for GPT-4o). The small absolute gaps between open-source and proprietary models (≤2% without CoT) indicate that color understanding is an underexplored capability across the VLM landscape.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves out a dedicated evaluation axis (color-centric understanding) that prior VLM benchmarks only touched peripherally through scattered color questions in MMBench, MME, or specialized datasets like ColorSwap and IllusionVQA. It departs from the general-purpose benchmarking paradigm by making color the primary variable of interest across perception, reasoning, and a novel robustness dimension where only hue is perturbed via HSV shifting with grounded segmentation. The robustness methodology draws on visual robustness benchmarks (Grit, Visual Robustness) but isolates color as the sole perturbation, and the recoloring pipeline leverages Grounded SAM for segment-level editing. No prior work systematically combined all three dimensions (perception, reasoning, robustness) under a unified color-focused framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals that color understanding is a long-neglected weakness in VLMs: even the best proprietary models (Gemini-2 with CoT at 59.6% P&R) trail humans by 10+ points on most tasks, and the gap between proprietary and open-source models is only ~2%, suggesting the limitation is architectural rather than a matter of scale. The counterintuitive finding that CoT prompting improves color robustness (a task where only the vision encoder should matter) and that grayscale conversion improves accuracy on illusion/mimicry tasks opens new research directions on how VLMs actually process color. Limitations include the manual curation bottleneck (1,448 instances), limited vision encoder diversity constraining scaling-law analysis, and the exclusion of texture/shape-color interplay. Open questions include whether purpose-built color encoders or color-aware training objectives could close the human-model gap.
