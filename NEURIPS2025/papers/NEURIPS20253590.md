## Problem

Existing zeroth-order gradient estimators for LLM fine-tuning (e.g., MeZO) exhibit variance scaling as Θ(d) with parameter dimension, yielding convergence rates that become prohibitive when d exceeds 10^10. This dimension dependence persists even under low-effective-dimensionality assumptions, limiting the practical utility of gradient-free fine-tuning for modern large-scale models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253590/cropped_page5_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes pre-trained model parameters, a batch of training samples (text sequences for LLMs or RGB images for CV), and a task-specific loss function g. Each optimization step requires exactly two forward passes (perturbed and unperturbed parameters) rather than a full backpropagation pass. The sliding window stores only random seeds and last hidden states (e.g., 2048-dim for OPT-1.3B vs. 50000+ vocabulary size) to keep the extra memory overhead dimension-independent, producing a stochastic gradient estimate A^T e where e is the closed-form outer gradient.

## Method

PseuZO exploits the asymmetric differentiability of the composite objective F(x) = E_z[g(h(x;z))]: the outer loss gradient e = ∇_o g(o) is computed in closed form, while the inner Jacobian J_h is estimated via a single random perturbation ξ ~ N(0,I) applied to the model parameters, yielding the rank-one estimator Δo·ξ^T. An exponential moving average (EMA) over these Jacobian estimators reduces variance, and the final gradient is obtained as A^T e. A sliding window of length L truncates the EMA history, storing only seeds and last hidden states to keep memory overhead independent of model size. The convergence rate is O(max{α₁Lε⁻², α₁Lσ₂²ε⁻⁴}) where α₁ is the effective dimension (tr(H) bound) rather than the full parameter dimension d_p, and a periodic restart schedule on λ allows PseuZO to degenerate to MeZO in later phases, matching MeZO's wall-clock time.

## Results

On OPT-1.3B fine-tuning (1024 training samples, 512 eval samples), PseuZO at 10K steps achieves 91.2% on SST-2, 58.0% on RTE, 77.0% on CB, 64.3% on BoolQ, 58.0% on WSC, 54.5% on WIC, 54.7% on MultiRC, 78.0% on COPA, 60.0% on ReCoRD, and 23.5 on DROP. The headline result is a 9.8-point accuracy gain over MeZO on SST-2 (91.2% vs. 82.4%) at the same 10K-step budget. With the sliding window technique, PseuZO achieves 70–80% memory reduction compared to FO-SGD while introducing only a small dimension-independent overhead over MeZO.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MeZO [34], PseuZO at 10K steps outperforms MeZO at 10K steps on 8 out of 10 tasks, with the largest gap on SST-2 (91.2% vs. 82.4%, +8.8 pts) and WSC (58.0% vs. 51.0%, +7.0 pts); it slightly trails on MultiRC (54.7 vs. 54.9). Against MeZO-SVRG [19], PseuZO wins decisively on nearly all tasks (e.g., SST-2: 91.2 vs. 61.5; COPA: 78.0 vs. 54.0; ReCoRD: 60.0 vs. 50.1; DROP: 23.5 vs. 0.0), and also requires far fewer forward passes since MeZO-SVRG must traverse the full batch. Against HiZOO-L [60], PseuZO leads on 7 of 10 tasks (SST-2: 91.2 vs. 88.1; CB: 77.0 vs. 69.0; WSC: 58.0 vs. 51.2) but loses on WIC (54.5 vs. 58.0) and MultiRC (54.7 vs. 58.2); notably, HiZOO-L requires three forward passes plus low-rank matrix processing, making it slower per step. For training-from-scratch (Table 7), PseuZO with Node Perturbation dramatically outperforms SPSA [45] on CIFAR-10 (82.5% vs. 41.3%) and CIFAR-100 (61.4% vs. 5.4%), approaching backpropagation performance (89.9% and 71.9% respectively) when combined with local learning.

## Relation to Prior Work

PseuZO extends the MeZO paradigm (Malladi et al., NeurIPS 2023) of forward-pass-only zeroth-order fine-tuning by replacing the scalar function-value gradient estimator with a pseudo-zeroth-order Jacobian estimator of the model output h(x), combined with the exact outer gradient ∇_o g(o). The idea of using model outputs to estimate Jacobians is borrowed from the spiking neural network literature (Xiao et al. [54]), but PseuZO is the first to apply this to LLM fine-tuning with EMA variance reduction and a sliding-window memory scheme. Theoretically, the convergence analysis departs from classical ZO bounds that scale with full parameter dimension d, instead depending on the effective dimension α₁ of the objective, connecting to the low-effective-dimensionality framework of Yue et al. [56].

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By decoupling the inner Jacobian estimation from the outer loss gradient, PseuZO achieves a convergence rate that avoids the Θ(d) dimension penalty of standard ZO methods, making gradient-free fine-tuning more viable for models with 10^10+ parameters. The 70–80% memory reduction over FO-SGD and 9.8% accuracy gain over MeZO on SST-2 demonstrate practical value, and compatibility with LoRA/prefix-tuning broadens applicability. Key limitations include the ~2× per-epoch time overhead relative to MeZO (partially mitigated by periodic λ scheduling) and sensitivity of sliding-window memory to batch size and context length. Open questions include whether the effective-dimension assumption holds uniformly across architectures and whether the approach can be extended to multi-step or higher-order ZO schemes without further memory blowup.
