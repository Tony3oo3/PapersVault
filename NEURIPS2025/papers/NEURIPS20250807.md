## Problem

Cryptanalytic parameter extraction attacks can recover the full weights and biases of ReLU neural networks in polynomial time with a polynomial number of black-box queries, yet no defense against these attacks has been proposed. The paper identifies that these attacks fundamentally rely on the uniqueness of neuron parameters within a layer, and asks whether this assumption can be disrupted during training to render the attack infeasible without incurring inference overhead.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250807/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The defense operates on fully connected MLPs with ReLU hidden layers and a linear output layer, trained on 784-dimensional flattened MNIST images or random data vectors. At inference, the model produces class labels or logit scores exactly as an unprotected model would—the similarity regularizer is baked into training and adds zero computational overhead at deployment. The attacker interacts with the deployed model as a black box, submitting arbitrary inputs and observing outputs to perform the cryptanalytic extraction.

## Method

The paper introduces an extraction-aware training strategy that augments the standard loss (e.g., MSE) with a layer-wise similarity regularization term that penalizes the squared L2 distance between neuron weight vectors within each layer, driven by a tunable coefficient λ_similarity. The key insight is that when intra-layer neurons become parameter-similar, their critical hyperplanes overlap, causing them to activate or deactivate together for any input, which prevents the attack from isolating a single neuron's contribution via directional derivative probing. A theoretical framework (Theorem 1) quantifies the attack success probability as the ratio of the input-space area between two neurons' hyperplanes to the total input area, upper-bounded by a function of the parameter differences δ_i. Practical tuning strategies—restricting the defense to the first layer, defending only a subset of neuron pairs, and incrementally scaling λ_similarity—keep accuracy degradation below 1% while extending extraction time from hours to an estimated 24,000+ hours.

## Results

Across all 12 evaluated model configurations (Random and MNIST-trained MLPs with 8–128 neurons per layer), the extraction-aware training defense incurs an accuracy change of less than 1% (ranging from −0.92% to +0.43%) while rendering cryptanalytic parameter extraction infeasible. For the representative MNIST784_16x8_1(s2) model, the attack success probability drops from 0.74 (baseline) to 0.0017 (secure), a 435× reduction, and the projected extraction time increases from 3.5 hours to an estimated 24,095 hours. The defense requires zero area-delay overhead at inference, as the similarity regularization is applied only during training. Weight variance in the first layer decreases from 0.43 (baseline) to 0.08 (secure), confirming tighter intra-layer neuron clustering.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its extraction-aware training defense against the unprotected baseline models from Foerster et al. [8] (NeurIPS 2024), using identical architectures, hyperparameters, and extraction seeds. On the security metric, the defense wins decisively: all previously extractable models (compromised in 14 minutes to 4 hours) become intractable, with the MNIST784_16x8_1(s2) model's projected extraction time increasing from 3.5 hours to ~24,095 hours. On the accuracy metric, the defense is essentially neutral: accuracy changes range from −0.92% to +0.43%, with two models (MNIST784-16x2-1 and MNIST784-16x3-1) actually showing slight accuracy gains over their baselines. The defense achieves this with zero inference overhead, unlike prior countermeasures (deceptive neurons, prediction poisoning, model obfuscation) that introduce runtime or memory costs. The theoretical framework further quantifies the gap: attack success probability drops by up to 435× compared to the unprotected baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself as the first defense against the line of cryptanalytic parameter extraction attacks initiated by Carlini et al. [4] (CRYPTO 2020) and extended to end-to-end extraction by Foerster et al. [8] (NeurIPS 2024). Rather than adding inference-time barriers (as other model-stealing defenses do), it identifies the neuron-uniqueness assumption that underpins signature extraction and eliminates it at training time via a weight-similarity regularization term. The approach also departs from prior empirical attack evaluations by introducing a geometric, probability-based theoretical framework for quantifying attack success, replacing the arbitrary 36-hour runtime threshold used in [8].

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first countermeasure against a class of polynomial-time, black-box attacks that had previously gone unaddressed, and its zero-inference-overhead property makes it immediately deployable in MLaaS settings where latency and memory are constrained. However, the evaluation is limited to small MLPs on MNIST and random data; the defense has not been tested on CNNs, LLMs, or larger-scale datasets, and the 'sustained extraction' claim for secure models relies on curve-fitting extrapolation rather than direct observation. Open questions include whether the neuron-similarity constraint generalizes to convolutional or transformer architectures, how adaptive attackers might circumvent the defense (e.g., via partial recovery or neuron collapsing), and whether the <1% accuracy cost remains acceptable for safety-critical applications.
