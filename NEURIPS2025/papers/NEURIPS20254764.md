## Problem

Variational Continual Learning (VCL) regularizes each posterior update using only the single most recent posterior approximation, causing approximation errors to compound over successive recursive updates and leading to severe catastrophic forgetting. The paper identifies this compounding-error mechanism as a fundamental limitation of the standard VCL objective and asks whether the same learning target can be reformulated to distribute regularization across multiple past posterior estimates, thereby diluting individual errors.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254764/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes streaming image data in sequential tasks (28×28 grayscale for MNIST/NotMNIST variants, 32×32 RGB for CIFAR-100, 64×64 RGB for TinyImageNet), with each task arriving online and only a small restricted replay buffer (200–400 samples from 1–3 prior tasks) available. At test time, predictions are produced by marginalizing over the Gaussian mean-field approximate posterior via Monte-Carlo sampling to obtain a posterior predictive distribution, from which class labels are extracted. All methods are constrained to single-head classifiers on the MNIST/NotMNIST-Hard benchmarks to prevent trivial multi-head solutions.

## Method

The core contribution is the TD(λ)-VCL objective, which proves that the standard VCL KL-minimization target is equivalently representable as a discounted sum of n-step temporal-difference targets, each bootstrapping on a different past posterior estimate. By weighting past posteriors with a geometric decay controlled by λ, the objective dilutes any single approximation error and allows correct estimates to exert corrective influence, directly addressing the compounding-error problem. A uniform-weighting special case yields the n-Step KL VCL objective, and the two together form a spectrum spanning from vanilla VCL (λ=0) to n-Step KL (λ→1). Practically, the method uses a Gaussian mean-field posterior parameterized as a deep network, computes the KL term analytically, approximates expected log-likelihoods via Monte-Carlo with the reparametrization trick, and applies likelihood-tempering to prevent variational over-pruning. The TD objective is shown to be composable with other variational CL methods (UCL, UCB), yielding TD-UCL and TD-UCB variants that consistently improve upon their base methods.

## Results

On PermutedMNIST-Hard (10 tasks), TD(λ)-VCL achieves 89% average accuracy after all tasks, compared to 78% for standard VCL and 81% for VCL CoreSet, while non-variational baselines (Online MLE: 40%, Batch MLE: 51%) suffer severe catastrophic forgetting. On CIFAR100-10, TD(λ)-VCL reaches 71% at t=10 versus 66% for VCL and 65% for VCL CoreSet. On TinyImageNet-10, TD(λ)-VCL attains 56% at t=10, outperforming VCL (51%) and VCL CoreSet (54%). The per-task analysis on PermutedMNIST-Hard shows Task 1 accuracy drops to ~50–60% for VCL baselines after all tasks, while TD-VCL retains ~80–85%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TD(λ)-VCL consistently outperforms standard VCL (Nguyen et al., ICLR 2018) across all five benchmarks, with the gap widening as more tasks are observed: on PermutedMNIST-Hard at t=10, TD(λ)-VCL achieves 89% vs. VCL's 78% (a 11-point improvement). VCL CoreSet (Nguyen et al., ICLR 2018), which adds a heuristic replay buffer, narrows the gap slightly (81% at t=10) but still trails TD(λ)-VCL by 8 points, and requires a two-step optimization. Against UCL (Ahn et al., NeurIPS 2019), which uses node-wise uncertainty for adaptive regularization, TD(λ)-VCL leads by 16 points on PermutedMNIST-Hard at t=10 (89% vs. 73%), and the TD-enhanced variant TD(λ)-UCL further improves UCL to 84%. Against UCB (Ebrahimi et al., ICLR 2020), which adapts learning rates by parameter uncertainty, TD(λ)-VCL achieves 89% vs. UCB's 83% on PermutedMNIST-Hard at t=10, and TD(λ)-UCB boosts UCB to 88%. The key finding is that the TD objective is orthogonal to and composable with existing variational CL enhancements, consistently improving each base method by 3–11 points at later timesteps where catastrophic forgetting is most severe.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Variational Continual Learning (VCL, Nguyen et al. 2018) by showing that its single-step KL regularization objective is equivalently representable as a function of multiple past posterior estimates, yielding the n-Step KL and TD(λ)-VCL objectives. The key conceptual departure is the identification of a structural analogy with Temporal-Difference (TD) learning in reinforcement learning: the VCL objective becomes a discounted sum of n-step TD targets, where bootstrapping on past posteriors plays the role of bootstrapping on past value estimates. The method is also shown to compose orthogonally with existing Bayesian CL variants (UCL, UCB), producing TD-enhanced versions that improve upon their base methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The compounding-error argument provides a principled explanation for why standard VCL degrades over long task sequences, and the multi-posterior regularization offers a clean fix that requires no external replay buffer. The introduced 'Hard' benchmarks (restricted replay, single-head classifiers) address a real gap where prior MNIST/NotMNIST CL benchmarks were saturated by simple baselines, giving the field a more meaningful evaluation setting. Open questions include whether the formal MDP/TD connection can be made rigorous (the paper explicitly defers this), whether the approach transfers to non-Bayesian CL, and how the memory cost of storing n past posteriors scales with very long task sequences.
