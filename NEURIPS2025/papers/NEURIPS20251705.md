## Problem

Existing label enhancement methods that exploit label rankings ignore the tie relation (where multiple labels describe an instance to approximately the same degree) and lack a principled probabilistic model linking label distributions to ranking outcomes. Classic ranking models such as Bradley-Terry and Plackett-Luce also cannot explicitly represent ties, leaving a gap in both qualitative and quantitative modeling of how description-degree differences generate pairwise ranking probabilities.

## Input / Output

The method consumes D-dimensional feature vectors together with either binary logical labels or tie-allowed pairwise label rankings (each pair annotated as greater-than, less-than, or approximately-equal) and outputs an M-dimensional label distribution on the probability simplex, where each coordinate quantifies the degree to which the corresponding label describes the instance. At inference time, only the feature vector is required, as the model learns a direct feature-to-distribution mapping via a feature-conditioned Gaussian posterior over the pre-softmax latent.

## Method

PROM models the probability of each pairwise ranking relation (ℓᵢ ≻ ℓⱼ, ℓᵢ ≃ ℓⱼ, ℓᵢ ≺ ℓⱼ) as a softmax over three log-linear terms in the description-degree difference Δ = zᵢ − zⱼ, with a quadratic term for the tie probability. Four theorems derive the exact parameter constraints (a, b, t) under which the resulting mass functions provably satisfy probability monotonicity, orderliness, translational invariance, and ranking symmetry, yielding a closed-form feasible region F. LE-PROM embeds PROM in a generative model where a standard-normal latent u is mapped through softmax to the label distribution z, which then generates features (via a Gaussian with neural-network mean and variance), logical labels (via a task-specific Bernoulli likelihood), and pairwise rankings (via the product of PROM distributions). Training maximizes an ELBO with an SGVB reconstruction term re-weighted by hyperparameters α and β for the label and ranking likelihoods, a KL prior regularization, and an additional KL term that distills the full posterior into a feature-only predictor p̂(u|x) for deployment.

## Results

On 16 real-world datasets for logical label enhancement, LE-PROM achieves an average rank of 1.09 across KL and Cosine metrics, winning 138 out of 160 pairwise significance comparisons (19 ties, 3 losses). For example, on the Painting dataset LE-PROM attains KL of 0.567±0.022 (rank 1) versus CWLD's 0.686±0.014 and VIB-ILE's 27.118±2.496. On the Movie dataset, LE-PROM achieves Cosine similarity of 0.926±0.001 (rank 1), outperforming all baselines. In the label ranking enhancement task, LE-PROM also outperforms GLERB, GLEMR, and Bradley-Terry in most cases.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LE-PROM is compared against five logical label enhancement baselines (CWLD, VIB-ILE, GLLE, KMLE, FCMLE) and three ranking enhancement baselines (GLERB, GLEMR, Bradley-Terry). Across 160 pairwise significance tests on the logical label enhancement task, LE-PROM wins 138, ties 19, and loses only 3, achieving an average rank of 1.09. Against the two state-of-the-art methods, CWLD and VIB-ILE, LE-PROM is superior on most datasets; VIB-ILE in particular struggles on several datasets (e.g., KL of 27.118 on Painting), while CWLD is competitive but consistently outperformed by LE-PROM on gene expression datasets (Alpha, Cdc, Cold, Diau, Dtt, Elu, Heat, Spo, Spo5, Spoem). The three classical baselines from Xu et al. (2018) — GLLE, KMLE, and FCMLE — are generally weaker, with FCMLE showing the largest variance. In the label ranking enhancement task (Figure 6), LE-PROM outperforms GLERB, GLEMR, and the classic Bradley-Terry model in most cases, demonstrating the advantage of explicitly modeling tie-allowed rankings with the PROM distribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the generative label enhancement framework of Lu et al. (2023a, AAAI), which introduced a variational generative model for inferring label distributions from multi-label data, by replacing the ad-hoc ranking regularization with a principled probabilistic model (PROM) that explicitly accommodates tie relations. It departs from classic pairwise ranking models (Bradley-Terry, Plackett-Luce) that cannot represent ties, and from prior LE-with-ranking works (Jia et al. 2023a, Lu et al. 2023b) that only penalize inversions between positive and negative labels without modeling the full three-way (above/below/tie) probability structure. The theoretical contribution—deriving closed-form conditions under which a softmax-parameterized categorical distribution satisfies monotonicity and orderliness—is novel within the LDL literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PROM provides the first theoretically grounded, interpretable probabilistic bridge between label distributions and tie-allowed label rankings, which is essential for generative modeling and for designing loss functions that respect the ordinal structure of labels. The acknowledged limitation—that the feasible region for (a, b, t) is analytically complex and may impede adaptive hyperparameter learning—remains an open problem that could limit practical adoption. Broader implications include whether the monotonicity/orderliness framework can be extended to full (non-pairwise) rankings or to multi-instance settings, and whether the tie-interval length can be learned per-instance rather than fixed globally.
