## Problem

Existing watermark removal attacks operate exclusively in the pixel domain, applying perturbations that leave the underlying semantic structure intact. This limitation renders them largely ineffective against semantic-level watermarks (e.g., TreeRing, RingID) that encode ownership in high-level latent features. The paper asks whether a semantic-level removal attack exists that can reliably remove state-of-the-art image watermarks while preserving visual fidelity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254154/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single watermarked RGB image (H×W×3) as input and produces a single unwatermarked RGB image as output, framed as the second frame of a two-frame video sequence. The watermarked image is first mapped to its latent noise representation via DDIM inversion (10 steps, empty prompt since the original text prompt is unknown), and the output is generated by a zero-shot next-frame prediction process built on Stable Diffusion-v2.1-base. The attack is black-box, data-free, and query-free: it requires no knowledge of the watermarking algorithm, no training data, and no feedback from the watermark detector.

## Method

NFPA reframes semantic-level watermark removal as a next-frame prediction problem: the watermarked image is the initial frame x⁰, and the unwatermarked image is the predicted next frame x¹. The core mechanism involves (1) DDIM-inverting the watermarked image to obtain initial latent noise x_T⁰, (2) constructing a 2D optical flow matrix f within a bounded search space S_δ (|f|≤40 px) and optimizing it to maximize the latent-space L1 distance between x_T⁰ and the warped x_T¹, thereby pushing the noise away from watermark-related distributions, and (3) replacing the UNet's self-attention with an asymmetric frame-attention module where x¹ cross-attends to x⁰ while x⁰ retains standard self-attention, enforcing one-sided semantic dependency for visual consistency. The entire pipeline is zero-shot and tuning-free, adapting a pretrained T2I diffusion model as a next-frame predictor without any fine-tuning, and runs in approximately 1.2 seconds per image on an RTX 4090.

## Results

NFPA-xy achieves an average TPR@1%FPR of 0.04 across eight watermarking schemes, the lowest among all 13 evaluated attacks, indicating near-complete watermark removal. In terms of image quality, NFPA-xy yields an average FID of 69.48 and CLIP score of 0.32, closely matching the unattacked watermarked images (FID 66.57, CLIP 0.33). The attack runs in 1.27 s per image on a single RTX 4090, compared to 323.87 s for IRA and 79.17 s for SVD, making it over 250× faster than the strongest adversarial baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NFPA-xy (avg. TPR@1%FPR = 0.04) outperforms all 13 baselines. Against the strongest adversarial baseline IRA [28] (avg. 0.05), NFPA-xy achieves a marginally lower average while operating as a black-box attack requiring no knowledge of the target model architecture, whereas IRA's reported numbers assume a white-box setting with an identical surrogate model (SD2.1); moreover, NFPA runs in 1.27 s vs. IRA's 323.87 s per image. Against regeneration attacks, NFPA-xy dramatically outperforms CtrlRegen+ [25] (0.39 avg.), Diffusion-Attack [48] (0.46 avg.), and VAE-Attack [48] (0.75 avg.), particularly on semantic-level watermarks like TreeRing (0.07 vs. 0.73/0.92/1.00) and RingID (0.02 vs. 0.96/0.99/1.00). Compared to the video-generation baseline SVD [5] (0.61 avg.), NFPA-xy reduces detection rates by roughly 15× on average. Against MSAA [37] (0.53 avg., evaluated only on StegaStamp and TreeRing due to cost), NFPA-xy achieves 0.02 and 0.07 respectively, while also generalizing to all eight watermarks without training watermark-specific surrogate detectors.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes next-frame prediction from video generation as a watermark removal mechanism, a conceptual bridge not previously drawn in the watermarking security literature. It builds on DDIM inversion for latent-space access, optical flow warping from video synthesis, and frame-attention from video diffusion models, but combines them in a zero-shot, tuning-free pipeline specifically targeting semantic-level watermarks. Unlike prior removal attacks (distortion, regeneration via VA/DA/CtrlRegen+, adversarial via MSAA/IRA) that operate in pixel space or require white-box access, NFPA formulates removal as a controlled latent-space perturbation guided by temporal consistency constraints, positioning it as the first attack that explicitly manipulates image semantics rather than pixel values.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

NFPA demonstrates that all eight evaluated SOTA watermarking schemes—including semantic-level methods specifically designed to resist pixel-level attacks—can be reduced to near-zero detectability (avg TPR@1%FPR of 0.04) in just 1.2 seconds per image, raising urgent questions about the practical reliability of current watermarking defenses for AI-generated content. The attack's black-box, data-free, query-free, and training-free properties make it immediately deployable by adversaries, undermining the security assumptions underpinning provenance verification systems like SynthID. Key open questions include whether watermarking schemes can be redesigned to resist semantic-level perturbations, whether the next-frame prediction framing generalizes to newer architectures (SDXL, FLUX), and what defense mechanisms could restore robustness without sacrificing watermark capacity.
