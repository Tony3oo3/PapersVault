## Problem

Existing logic-based explainers for GNNs are exclusively post-hoc or rely on distilling GNNs into decision trees, meaning they cannot guarantee faithful alignment between the extracted rules and the model's internal computations. Self-explainable GNNs (SE-GNNs) that do integrate interpretability into the architecture typically identify relevant subgraphs or node features but provide no insight into the logical rules governing how the model represents graphs and makes decisions. This leaves a significant gap: no GNN architecture exists that is intrinsically convertible into human-interpretable logical formulas as part of its forward pass.

## Input / Output

The method consumes graphs with node features (molecular graphs like MUTAG, NCI1, BBBP; synthetic graphs like BA2Motifs, BAMultiShapes; protein graphs like PROTEINS) and produces a class label alongside three levels of explanation: layer-wise DNF formulas in Graded Modal Logic (GML) that exactly reflect each layer's computation, global logic rules identifying subgraph motifs that trigger class-specific decisions, and node attributions derived from which nodes activate the final-layer rules. A key constraint is that node features are first binarized through learnable intervals, so the model operates on discrete counting signals rather than continuous embeddings, and the sum aggregation (with ε=0) is required to preserve GML's counting semantics.

## Method

LogiX-GIN replaces GIN's MLP update function with a two-stage transformation: a parametric Fourier step function β that binarizes the summed neighbor features by activating only when counts fall within learnable intervals (enabling GML's 'at least N neighbors satisfy P' quantifiers), followed by a TELL-like logic layer λ with non-negative weights and a sharp sigmoid that produces outputs directly convertible to DNF formulas. The non-negativity constraint on weights ensures monotonicity, which is the key property enabling faithful extraction of logical rules from parameters. Training addresses the vanishing-gradient problem inherent in stacking sigmoid-based logic layers by first training a black-box GIN teacher with Gumbel-sigmoid activations, then using it for layer-wise distillation during the first half of LogiX-GIN training before switching to standard supervised fine-tuning. A post-training Hoyer-regularization pruning step applied layer-by-layer in reverse order sparsifies the weight matrices, reducing rule complexity without significantly degrading accuracy.

## Results

On graph classification, LogiX-GIN achieves accuracy within 3% of its black-box GIN counterpart across all 10 datasets, with the largest decay being 2.94% on NCI1 and 2.90% on Mutagenicity, while actually outperforming the black-box on BACommunity (+3.14%). Against other self-explainable GNNs, LogiX-GIN reaches state-of-the-art accuracy on 5 out of 7 graph classification datasets, achieving perfect 100% on both BA2Motifs and BAMultiShapes. On node classification, LogiX-GIN scores 94.29% on BAShapes, 84.14% on BACommunity, and 98.71% on TreeGrid.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the four SE-GNN baselines in Table 2, LogiX-GIN outperforms KerGNN on 6 of 7 datasets (losing only on PROTEINS by 0.92%) and GNAN on all 7 datasets by margins of 20–63 percentage points. Compared to GIB, LogiX-GIN wins on BAMultiShapes (+2.40%), BBBP (+1.12%), and NCI1 (+0.78%), but loses on MUTAG (−7.90%) and Mutagenicity (−0.83%). Against PiGNN, LogiX-GIN leads on BAMultiShapes (+14.60%), NCI1 (+0.39%), BBBP (+2.36%), and PROTEINS (+2.05%), but trails on Mutagenicity (−3.08%). For the post-hoc node-attribution comparison (Appendix E, fidelity metric), LogiX-GIN matches or exceeds GStarX, SubgraphX, GNNExplainer, PGExplainer, and Integrated Gradients on NCI1, MUTAG, BA2Motifs, and BAMultiShapes, while GStarX and PiGNN achieve higher fidelity on BBBP, Mutagenicity, and PROTEINS. In the global logic-rule comparison with GraphTrail, LogiX-GIN's explanations are fully aligned with model predictions by architectural design, whereas GraphTrail's post-hoc rules can misalign; LogiX-GIN also recovers the ground-truth rule on BAMultiShapes where GraphTrail does not, and completes in feasible time where GraphTrail fails on BAMultiShapes (exceeding 2 days).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the TELL architecture (Ragno et al., ECAI 2024), which converts non-negative-weight linear layers into first-order logic rules for tabular data, into the graph domain by adapting the GIN message-passing framework. The theoretical grounding draws on Barcelo et al.'s (ICLR 2020) result that GNNs can express graded modal logic (GML) propositions, but the paper addresses the reverse direction: architecting a GNN to explicitly yield GML rules. Unlike prior logic-based GNN explainers (GraphTrail, GLGExplainer, Pluska et al.), which are all post-hoc or distillation-based, LogiX-GIN embeds logical reasoning directly into the architecture, making explanations faithful by construction rather than approximations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first GNN architecture that produces interpretable GML rules as an intrinsic part of its computation, closing a gap where all prior logic-based GNN explanations were post-hoc. The practical impact is tempered by several limitations: GML's expressivity is strictly local (no higher-order reasoning), edge features are unsupported, and the multi-stage distillation-based training is computationally expensive (up to ~50× slower than the black-box GIN on NCI1). Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger graphs, whether more expressive logics (beyond GML) can be integrated, and whether combining logic rules with prototype or concept-based explanations can recover the performance gap on complex molecular datasets.
