## Problem

Most 3D head avatars bake training-environment radiance into their appearance, making them impossible to relight in novel virtual scenes. Relightable avatar research remains scarce largely because of the absence of publicly available, controlled light-stage datasets and because joint estimation of geometry, material, and lighting is extremely under-constrained. This paper addresses both gaps by introducing a low-cost multi-view OLAT face dataset and a relightable Gaussian avatar representation with a hybrid neural shading model.

## Input / Output

Training consumes ~150 seconds of multi-view light-stage video per subject (16 cameras at 2200x3208, 72 fps, 40 calibrated LEDs cycling one-light-at-a-time with every third frame as a fully-lit tracking frame) plus FLAME shape/expression/pose parameters estimated by VHAP. At inference, the avatar is driven solely by FLAME expression and pose coefficients (obtainable from a monocular video) and relit with either point lights or environment maps, producing 1100x1604 novel-view renderings at ~17 ms per frame on an RTX A6000. The avatar also exposes disentangled albedo, normals, diffuse, and specular maps for inspection.

## Method

The avatar is a fixed set of ~202k anisotropic 3D Gaussians placed on the FLAME UV map, deformed by an expression-dependent CNN (F_g) that predicts per-texel position offsets, quaternions, scales, opacities, and a 32-dim expression feature vector. The key shading contribution is a hybrid decomposition: a tiny shared MLP (F_d) implicitly learns the view-independent diffuse BRDF (capturing subsurface scattering and self-shadowing) from spherical-harmonic incident light (degree 6) and expression features, while the view-dependent specular term uses an analytical Cook-Torrance model with a 2-Blinn-Phong-lobe NDF whose roughness is a linear parameter. A second small U-Net (F_v) predicts per-Gaussian specular intensity and view-dependent normal offsets to handle the ellipsoidal Gaussian shape. Training jointly optimizes all networks and static parameters with a photometric L1+SSIM loss plus regularization terms (normal offset, alpha mask, scale, and position) over 250k iterations at 1100x1604 resolution.

## Results

On the held-out camera and unseen light patterns of the OLAT dataset, BecomingLit achieves 31.38 PSNR, 0.8956 SSIM, and 0.1040 LPIPS for relighting, and 28.08 PSNR, 0.8730 SSIM, and 0.1317 LPIPS for relighting with self-reenactment. These results consistently outperform both RGCA and its FLAME-conditioned variant by a significant margin across all metrics. Inference runs at 17 ms per frame on a single RTX A6000 (vs. 20 ms for RGCA), enabling real-time rendering at the training resolution of 1100×1604.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is RGCA (Saito et al., CVPR 2024), a relightable Gaussian codec avatar that learns a per-identity expression space and predicts precomputed radiance transfer coefficients. BecomingLit outperforms RGCA by +2.17 PSNR and −0.062 LPIPS on relighting, and by +1.77 PSNR and −0.060 LPIPS on relighting with self-reenactment. A second baseline, RGCA_FLAME, replaces RGCA's learned expression latent with FLAME coefficients; it improves over original RGCA on all metrics, leading the authors to hypothesize that per-identity expression spaces are suboptimal for reenactment. Even against this stronger variant, BecomingLit still leads by +1.60 PSNR and −0.040 LPIPS on relighting, and by +1.17 PSNR and −0.035 LPIPS on reenactment. On the Goliath-4 dataset (Supplementary Table 5), BecomingLit matches or slightly trails RGCA on PSNR but consistently wins on SSIM and LPIPS, confirming generalization beyond the authors' own capture setup.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on 3D Gaussian Splatting for geometry and the FLAME parametric head model for animation, positioning itself against RGCA (Saito et al., 2024), which learns per-identity expression codes and precomputed radiance transfer (PRT) coefficients for relighting. The key conceptual departure is the hybrid neural shading scheme: rather than learning the full BRDF implicitly (as in neural PRT or deep reflectance fields) or relying on purely analytical PBR, the authors split reflectance into a small neural network for the view-independent diffuse term (capturing subsurface scattering) and an analytical Cook-Torrance specular term with a 2-Blinn-Phong NDF. The OLAT capture methodology traces back to Debevec et al.'s light stage, but the proposed rig is an order of magnitude cheaper, using 16 cameras and 40 LEDs instead of the 144 cameras and 460 lights in Goliath.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The primary practical impact is democratizing relightable avatar research: the capture setup is dramatically cheaper than prior light stages, and the new multi-view OLAT dataset (10 subjects, 16 views, 40 lights, 72 FPS, 2200×3208) fills a critical gap in publicly available controlled-illumination face data. The finding that conditioning RGCA on shared FLAME expression parameters outperforms its original per-identity learned expression space challenges the assumption that personalized expression spaces are necessary for reenactment. Open questions include whether the approach can be extended to casual monocular captures without calibrated lighting, whether the neural diffuse BRDF can be pre-trained as a cross-identity prior, and how to overcome FLAME's geometric limitations (no mouth interior, sensitivity to gaze tracking failures).
