## Problem

Existing unstructured pruning methods for LLMs either apply uniform sparsity across all layers (Wanda) or allocate sparsity only at the layer level (OWL, DSA), both of which ignore the non-uniform importance distribution within each layer's weight matrix. This leads to suboptimal global solutions, especially at high sparsity levels where output-dimension outliers are disproportionately pruned. The paper asks whether a single differentiable framework can learn both layer-wise and intra-layer sparsity allocation end-to-end to minimize overall model performance loss.

## Input / Output

The method consumes frozen pre-trained LLM weights, a target sparsity ratio p, and 2048-token calibration segments from C4. It produces a set of per-row threshold parameters (one per output channel of each weight matrix in Attention and MLP layers) that, when applied to the Wanda importance scores, yield a binary unstructured pruning mask. No weight updates are performed; the original weights are preserved and only the mask is learned. Importance scores are pre-mapped to a uniform [0,1] distribution to stabilize training against magnitude outliers.

## Method

Lua-LLM reformulates the global pruning problem as an adaptive row-wise sparsity allocation problem, where each row of a weight matrix retains its top-K most important weights according to the Wanda metric (|W_ij|·||X_:,j||₂). The non-differentiable Top-K selection is approximated by a sigmoid function σ(λ(s_i − t)) with λ = C_in, converting each row's sparsity into a single learnable threshold t. These thresholds are initialized uniformly at the target sparsity p and optimized end-to-end via gradient descent on the language modeling loss plus a log-ratio sparsity regularization term that enforces the global sparsity constraint. The original model parameters remain frozen; only the lightweight threshold parameters are trained for 500 iterations with AdamW (lr=5e-3), requiring ~1 hour on 2×A100 for a 7B model. This design captures both inter-layer and intra-layer sparsity variation without requiring custom CUDA kernels or evolutionary search.

## Results

On WikiText2 perplexity (lower is better), Lua-LLM consistently outperforms all baselines across LLaMA and OPT model families at 50%–80% sparsity. The gains are most dramatic at high sparsity: at 70% sparsity, Lua-LLM achieves 22.52 PPL on LLaMA3-8B versus 122.02 (Wanda), 89.81 (OWL), and 101.66 (DSA); at 80% sparsity, it reaches 30.27 PPL on LLaMA2-7B versus 2334.70 (Wanda) and 587.19 (OWL). On zero-shot downstream tasks at 70% sparsity, Lua-LLM improves mean accuracy on LLaMA-7B to 48.25% versus 37.38% (Wanda), 43.20% (OWL), and 42.83% (DSA). End-to-end inference speedup via SpInfer reaches 1.73× on OPT-6.7B at 70% sparsity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Lua-LLM outperforms all six baselines across every model and sparsity level tested. Against Wanda (uniform row-wise pruning), the gap widens dramatically with sparsity: at 70% on LLaMA3-8B, Lua-LLM reduces PPL from 122.02 to 22.52 (a 5.4× improvement), and at 80% on LLaMA2-7B from 2334.70 to 30.27 (a 77× improvement). Against OWL (outlier-weighted layerwise sparsity), Lua-LLM achieves 12.21 vs. 24.46 PPL at 70% on LLaMA-7B and 30.27 vs. 587.19 at 80% on LLaMA2-7B. Against DSA (evolutionary sparsity allocation), Lua-LLM wins by 10.10 PPL at 70% on LLaMA-7B (12.21 vs. 22.31) and by 1405.75 PPL at 80% on LLaMA2-7B (30.27 vs. 1436.02). Against ATP and AlphaPruning (theoretical/heavy-tailed allocation), Lua-LLM achieves 12.21 vs. 20.16 (ATP) and 23.86 (AlphaPruning) at 70% on LLaMA-7B, and 37.65 vs. 176.80 (ATP) and 698.56 (AlphaPruning) at 80%. When its learned allocation is plugged into SparseGPT (which performs weight updates), Lua-LLM further reduces PPL from 28.50 to 16.14 at 70% and from 113.36 to 43.63 at 80% on LLaMA2-7B, confirming that the sparsity pattern itself is the key contributor.

## Relation to Prior Work

Lua-LLM builds directly on Wanda's row-wise pruning strategy and importance metric (|W_ij| · ||X_:,j||_2), but departs from the uniform per-row sparsity assumption by formulating an adaptive row-wise sparsity allocation problem solved via end-to-end gradient descent. It positions itself against two lines of prior work: (1) layer-wise pruning methods (SparseGPT, Wanda) that minimize local reconstruction error with hand-crafted per-layer ratios, and (2) adaptive allocation methods (OWL, DSA, AlphaPruning, ATP) that use heuristics, evolutionary search, or theoretical regularization to assign layer-level sparsity. The key technical departure is the soft Top-K operator — a sigmoid approximation of the non-differentiable mask selection that reduces each row's sparsity decision to a single learnable threshold, enabling global optimization without the computational overhead of NAS-based approaches like BESA.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dramatic performance gap at high sparsity (e.g., 30.27 vs. 2334.70 PPL on LLaMA2-7B at 80%) demonstrates that adaptive intra-layer allocation is critical for practical LLM compression beyond 60% sparsity, where uniform and layer-adaptive methods collapse. The method's efficiency (≈1 hour on 2×A100 for 7B models) makes it accessible compared to evolutionary alternatives like DSA (12+ hours), and the learned sparsity patterns reveal interpretable structure (first-layer redundancy, output-channel sensitivity) that could inform future one-shot pruning designs. Open questions include whether the approach can be made truly one-shot without gradient training, whether it generalizes to structured or semi-structured sparsity, and whether the SpInfer dependency limits real-world deployment at large batch sizes or long sequences.
