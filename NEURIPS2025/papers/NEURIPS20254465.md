## Problem

Generating videos that adhere to physical laws remains a critical challenge: test-time LLM refinement (e.g., PhyT2V) is computationally expensive and bounded by model self-correction limits, while SFT-based methods require costly manual dataset construction. Existing DPO approaches for video generation focus solely on coarse-grained instance-level alignment, overlooking the fine-grained physical details (state, motion, semantics) necessary for plausibility. Additionally, no prior work has explored automated data selection to identify physically informative samples from existing large-scale text-video datasets.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt describing a physical scenario as input and produces a generated video as output, operating as a post-training stage on pretrained diffusion models (CogVideoX-2B/5B, HunyuanVideo-540p). The data selection pipeline consumes large-scale text-video pairs (e.g., OpenVidHD-0.4M with 433K samples) and outputs a curated 21K subset filtered for reality, physical fidelity, and diversity. Preference pairs are constructed by pairing selected real-world videos (preferred) with model-generated or frame-swapped variants (non-preferred) at each hierarchical level.

## Method

PhysHPO implements a four-level hierarchical DPO objective: (1) Instance-level aligns overall video content using two types of non-preferred videos—error-prone (visually similar but physically flawed) and gap (semantically incomplete via prompt masking); (2) State-level anchors physical consistency by swapping boundary frames (first/last N=2) to create non-preferred samples; (3) Motion-level extracts structural information (optical flow) from both preferred and non-preferred videos to explicitly model motion dynamics beyond pixel appearance; (4) Semantic-level uses a VLM to generate a modified caption reflecting the non-preferred video's inconsistencies, then optimizes the log-likelihood ratio between the original and modified captions. The overall loss is a weighted sum (λ=0.4, ρ=0.3, μ=0.2) of these four DPO objectives. A complementary automated data selection pipeline filters for real-world content via dual VLM verification, scores physical fidelity using multi-dimensional evolving prompts with an LLM (multiplying Dynamic, Thermodynamic, and Optic scores), and ensures diversity through iterative embedding-based nearest-neighbor selection.

## Results

On CogVideoX-5B, PhysHPO achieves 45.9 overall score on VideoPhy (vs. 39.6 base, 40.1 PhyT2V, 41.3 Vanilla DPO) and 0.61 overall on PhyGenBench (vs. 0.45 base, 0.50 PhyT2V, 0.54 Vanilla DPO), representing the largest gains in physical plausibility. On the general VBench benchmark, PhysHPO maintains competitive quality (82.8 Total, 83.7 Quality, 79.3 Semantic) without degrading general video generation capability. On CogVideoX-2B, PhysHPO similarly leads with 25.9 VideoPhy overall and 0.53 PhyGenBench overall, outperforming all baselines across every physics sub-category (solid-solid, solid-fluid, fluid-fluid, mechanics, optics, thermal, materials).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PhysHPO consistently outperforms all three baselines (base model, PhyT2V, and Vanilla DPO) across every physics-focused metric on both CogVideoX-2B and CogVideoX-5B. On CogVideoX-5B, PhysHPO improves VideoPhy overall by +6.3 over the base (45.9 vs. 39.6), +5.8 over PhyT2V (45.9 vs. 40.1), and +4.6 over Vanilla DPO (45.9 vs. 41.3); the largest single gain is on solid-fluid (SF) where PhysHPO reaches 54.1 vs. 50.0 for Vanilla DPO. On PhyGenBench, PhysHPO achieves 0.61 overall vs. 0.54 for Vanilla DPO and 0.50 for PhyT2V, with the most notable margin in optics (0.68 vs. 0.60) and materials (0.65 vs. 0.58). Crucially, PhysHPO does not sacrifice general video quality: on VBench it matches or slightly exceeds Vanilla DPO (82.8 vs. 82.4 Total; 83.7 vs. 83.3 Quality; 79.3 vs. 78.7 Semantic), confirming that the hierarchical preference optimization enhances physical plausibility without degrading broader generation capability. The same pattern holds on HunyuanVideo (Appendix A), where PhysHPO achieves 41.6 VideoPhy overall and 54.4 PhyGenBench overall, again surpassing both the base model and PhyT2V.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Diffusion-DPO framework (Wallace et al., CVPR'24) from coarse-grained, instance-level video preference alignment to a four-level hierarchical scheme (instance, state, motion, semantic), drawing conceptual inspiration from hierarchical DPO in multimodal LLMs. For the data side, it adapts the Deita (Liu et al., ICLR'24) iterative diversity-preserving selection paradigm from LM instruction tuning to video generation, combined with an LLM-as-a-Judge evolving-prompting scoring strategy. It departs from SFT-based physics-aware video generation methods (WISA, SynVideo) that require costly manual dataset construction, arguing instead that existing large-scale text-video datasets already contain sufficient physical signal if properly filtered.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper introduces two firsts for video generation: fine-grained hierarchical preference optimization and automated data selection, offering a practical recipe that avoids the expensive 'one-model-one-dataset' paradigm. The finding that 21K carefully selected samples outperform 433K raw data and a manually curated 32K dataset is practically significant for resource-constrained groups. Open questions include whether the hierarchical DPO framework generalizes to other alignment targets (e.g., temporal consistency, camera control), whether the data selection pipeline transfers to other domains beyond physics, and whether the approach scales to larger foundation models beyond the 2B–5B range tested here.
