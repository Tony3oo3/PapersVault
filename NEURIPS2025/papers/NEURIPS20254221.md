## Problem

Despite the growing use of VLMs for autonomous driving planning and decision-making, no dedicated benchmark exists to systematically evaluate their fine-grained 3D spatial reasoning (orientation, depth, localization, inter-object relations) in realistic driving scenes. Existing spatial benchmarks target indoor environments or simple 2D positional relations, leaving a critical gap: without verified spatial grounding, deploying VLMs for real-world driving actions is inherently unreliable. The paper also asks whether reinforcement-learning-based alignment can close this spatial reasoning gap.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254221/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each evaluation instance pairs a single driving-scene image (from one of six surround cameras in nuScenes, with all visual annotations removed) with a natural-language spatial question. The model must produce a structured three-part response: (1) a bounding box for the queried object under a <location> tag, (2) a step-by-step chain-of-thought reasoning trace under a <think> tag, and (3) a concise final answer under an <answer> tag. The six tasks split into single-object probes (yaw-angle classification, pixel-level localization via centerness, depth-range estimation) and multi-object relational probes (pairwise distance comparison, left-right ordering, front-behind relations), all evaluated with range-based categorical metrics rather than scalar regression to avoid trivial averaging strategies.

## Method

The paper contributes two coupled components. First, SURDS is constructed by multi-stage filtering of nuScenes annotations (occlusion removal via IoU and LiDAR projection, edge/size filters, and VLM-based description filtering) followed by template-based QA generation across six spatial categories, yielding 41,080 training and 9,250 validation instances. Second, a two-stage training pipeline is proposed: (a) SFT on CoT-augmented data generated by a multi-step distillation pipeline (QVQ reasons over sampled QA pairs → Qwen2.5-VL-72B extracts generalizable rules → rules + original QA pairs produce new CoT-annotated pairs, self-validated); and (b) GRPO reinforcement learning with four custom rewards—localization (IoU > 0.5 on predicted bbox), format compliance, final-answer accuracy, and a novel logic reward where the model under training acts as its own verifier by re-inferring the answer from its reasoning trace alone (avoiding answer leakage from including the question). The combined LocLogic rewards yield a 40.80 overall score, surpassing GPT-4o (13.30) and Gemini-2.0-flash (35.71) on the 3B-parameter Qwen2.5-VL base.

## Results

On the SURDS benchmark (9,250 evaluation samples across six spatial tasks), the proposed Qwen2.5-VL-3B-SFT-GRPO-LocLogic method achieves an overall score of 40.80, outperforming all proprietary and open-source baselines. It leads on single-object tasks—Yaw (20.97), Pixel localization (44.81), and Depth (69.84)—with a near-60% improvement over the second-best on Depth. However, it trails Gemini-2.0-flash on multi-object tasks (Dis: 49.30 vs. 69.30; L/R: 51.35 vs. 77.30; F/B: 8.54 vs. 20.00), yet still secures the highest overall average due to its dominant single-object performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against proprietary models, the proposed 3B method (40.80) surpasses GPT-4o (13.30) by 27.5 points and Gemini-2.0-flash (35.71) by 5.1 points overall, though Gemini-2.0-flash retains a clear edge on multi-object tasks (Dis 69.30, L/R 77.30, F/B 20.00 vs. 49.30, 51.35, 8.54). Among open-source VLMs, it outperforms Qwen2.5-VL-72B-Instruct (33.47) by 7.3 points and LLaVA-OV-Qwen2-72b-si (8.45) by 32.4 points, demonstrating that targeted RL alignment on a 3B model can exceed much larger general-purpose models. The specialized spatial models SpatialBot (2.00) and SpatialRGPT (3.77) perform near-random or below, with SpatialBot scoring 0.00 on four of six tasks due to its inability to follow the structured output format. Notably, the proposed method's advantage is concentrated in single-object perception (Yaw, Pixel, Depth), where it leads by wide margins, while its F/B score (8.54) remains below even the random baseline (11.89), exposing a persistent weakness in front-behind relational reasoning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the spatial-reasoning evaluation line (BLINK, SpatialRGPT, SpatialBot, VSI-Bench) from indoor or web-sourced 2D positional relations to outdoor driving scenes with 3D geometric quantities (yaw, depth, pairwise distance, front-behind). Methodologically, it builds on GRPO from DeepSeekMath [72] but introduces a novel multi-component reward (localization IoU, logic consistency via self-verification, format, and accuracy) tailored to spatial tasks, departing from prior spatial VLMs that rely on auxiliary depth estimators or visual annotations. The CoT data generation pipeline, which distills reasoning rules from QVQ via Qwen2.5-VL-72B, is a practical contribution to the growing literature on synthetic reasoning-trace construction for VLM fine-tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SURDS is the first large-scale benchmark isolating fine-grained 3D spatial reasoning in driving, filling a gap that prior benchmarks (focused on 2D left/right or indoor scenes) left open for autonomous-driving VLM research. The finding that a 3B GRPO-aligned model (40.80) outperforms GPT-4o (13.30) by over 3× suggests that targeted RL alignment can close spatial-reasoning gaps that raw scale cannot, offering a practical recipe for smaller deployed models. Key open questions remain: the method is validated only on Qwen2.5-VL-3B, the front-behind metric actually degrades under the full reward (8.54 vs. 21.62 SFT-only), and the benchmark covers perception-level reasoning without downstream tasks like trajectory prediction or planning that would test whether improved spatial grounding translates to actionable driving decisions.
