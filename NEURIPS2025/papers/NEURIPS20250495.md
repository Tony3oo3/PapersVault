## Problem

Existing approaches to copyright protection and tampering localization in latent diffusion models are either post-hoc (detached from the generative pipeline, introducing overhead and quality degradation) or attempt unification through independently optimized components that cannot mutually reinforce each other. No seamless, LDM-native framework exists that jointly embeds watermarks during generation and leverages their intrinsic perturbation patterns for precise tampering localization in a single end-to-end system.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250495/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For generation, a text prompt is fed through the standard LDM denoising pipeline, and the final latent is decoded by the MPW-VAE to produce a watermarked RGB image (512×512) that is visually indistinguishable from its watermark-free counterpart. For forensic analysis, a potentially tampered watermarked image is input to the MoE-GFN, which simultaneously outputs a binary watermark bit string (32–256 bits) and a pixel-level tampering localization mask at the same spatial resolution. The multiplexing design allows the same latent code to yield both watermarked and clean images by toggling the watermark adapter, enabling self-supervised pair construction without manual annotations.

## Method

StableGuard integrates two jointly optimized components: (1) an MPW-VAE that inserts lightweight latent residual-based watermark adapters after each VAE decoder block, encoding a holistic binary watermark via two FC layers reshaped to feature dimensions and fused through convolutional blocks with residual connections; and (2) a MoE-GFN, a UNet-based forensic network whose decoder contains a Mixture-of-Forensic-Experts block with three transformer experts—a global self-attention expert for watermark extraction, a sub-patch (n=8) local attention expert for tampering localization, and a Fourier-domain expert for boundary enhancement—whose outputs are adaptively fused by a Dynamic Soft Router (MLP + softmax). Training is fully self-supervised: random binary or SAM semantic masks splice watermarked and clean images to synthesize tampered samples, and the total loss combines L1+perceptual similarity, binary cross-entropy for watermark bits, and weighted BCE+Dice for the tampering mask. The key insight is that holistically distributed watermarks provide spatial redundancy for robust extraction while their subtle, globally consistent perturbation patterns serve as reliable cues for detecting missing-watermark regions, enabling the two tasks to mutually reinforce through end-to-end joint optimization.

## Results

On the AIGC tampering dataset (35K images), StableGuard achieves F1=0.980, AUC=0.992, IoU=0.961 on SD Inpainting tampering, consistently outperforming the best proactive baseline EditGuard (F1=0.937, AUC=0.977, IoU=0.911) and WAM (F1=0.924, AUC=0.977, IoU=0.868) across all five tampering types (SD, SDXL, Kandinsky, ControlNet, LaMa). On watermarking, StableGuard (32-bit) attains PSNR=40.50 dB, SSIM=0.970, LPIPS=0.062, and Bit Accuracy=99.97% on COCO, surpassing the best post-hoc method WAM (PSNR=38.20, Bit Acc=98.17%) and the best diffusion-native method WaDiff (PSNR=35.55, Bit Acc=98.14%). Under robustness stress tests (Gaussian noise σ=5, JPEG Q=70, Poisson noise), StableGuard maintains Bit Acc ≥99.69% and F1 ≥0.908, while EditGuard drops to 98.11%/0.577 and WAM to 96.89%/0.589.

## Baselines & Comparisons

StableGuard outperforms all 18 baselines on every reported metric. Against the strongest proactive unified methods, it gains +4.3 F1 points over EditGuard (0.980 vs 0.937 on SD Inpainting) and +5.6 over WAM (0.980 vs 0.924), with AUC margins of +1.6 and +1.6 respectively. Against passive localization methods, the gap is dramatic: IML-ViT reaches F1=0.907 while ObjectFormer and HDF-Net fall to 0.476 and 0.556, respectively, indicating that passive methods struggle with AIGC-generated manipulations. On watermarking quality, StableGuard (32-bit) leads WAM by +2.3 dB PSNR (40.50 vs 38.20) and +1.8% Bit Accuracy (99.97% vs 98.17%), while also achieving the lowest LPIPS (0.062 vs 0.067). Under robustness stress (JPEG Q=70), StableGuard retains F1=0.908 versus EditGuard's 0.577 and WAM's 0.589, a gap of over 30 points. On traditional tampering datasets (CASIA, NIST, Columbia, Coverage, IMD20), StableGuard similarly leads EditGuard by +1.9 F1 on CASIA (0.933 vs 0.914) and +4.2 on Columbia (0.995 vs 0.953), confirming generalization beyond AIGC content.

## Relation to Prior Work

StableGuard builds directly on the Stable Diffusion VAE decoder architecture, inserting a lightweight latent residual adapter to embed watermarks during generation rather than as a post-hoc step. It departs from prior unified watermarking-and-localization methods (EditGuard, OmniGuard, WAM) that remain post-hoc with independently optimized embedding and forensic stages, by jointly training the watermark adapter and forensic network end-to-end. The forensic side draws on the mixture-of-experts paradigm and transformer attention mechanisms, combining global self-attention (watermark extraction), sub-patch attention (local tampering), and Fourier-domain attention (boundary refinement) in a single UNet-based architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By embedding watermarks natively in the diffusion pipeline and co-optimizing with the forensic network, StableGuard eliminates the quality degradation and application overhead of post-hoc watermarking while achieving state-of-the-art localization accuracy (F1 ≈ 0.98) and near-perfect bit recovery (99.98%) across diverse AIGC tampering types. A key limitation is that the system is inherently proactive: it can only localize tampering in images it watermarked at generation time, and its tampering signal is specifically the disruption of the watermark pattern rather than a general manipulation artifact. Open questions include robustness against adversarial watermark-removal attacks, extension to video and multi-modal generation, and whether the joint optimization advantage persists when the base diffusion model is updated or fine-tuned.
