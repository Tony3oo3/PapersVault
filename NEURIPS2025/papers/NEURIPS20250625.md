## Problem

OS agents navigate by capturing and analysing screenshots, yet prior security research on these agents has focused almost exclusively on text-based attacks (prompt injection, adversarial pop-ups) that require direct access to the textual input pipeline. This paper asks whether an adversary can instead exploit the visual channel—by embedding imperceptible perturbations in a small screen region—so that the OS agent is hijacked purely through its screenshot capture, without any access to its text input. This image-based attack vector is significantly harder to detect and filter than text-based ones, and had not been systematically studied for the multi-component OS agent pipeline.

## Input / Output

The adversary controls only a small patch region R of a full-resolution screenshot (3239×2159×3 RGB), typically ~1/7 of the screen (e.g., a social media post image or a desktop wallpaper region). The optimisation must produce an integer-valued perturbation δ confined to R with L∞ norm ≤ 25/255, such that when the perturbed screenshot passes through the non-differentiable screen parser (which must yield identical SOMs), a resizing function, and the VLM, the VLM generates a specific multi-line API call sequence y (e.g., a 33-token memory-overflow command or a 52-token website-navigation script). The output MIP must remain visually imperceptible to humans while encoding the entire malicious instruction set directly, rather than relying on the agent to assemble it at runtime.

## Method

The attack is formulated as a constrained optimisation (Obj. 2) that jointly searches over the patch region R and the perturbation δ to minimise cross-entropy loss between the VLM's output and a target malicious sequence y, subject to three constraints: (i) no bounding boxes from the screen parser intersect R, (ii) the perturbation does not alter the parser's SOM output, and (iii) the result remains a valid 8-bit RGB image. Because the screen parser g and the resizing function q are non-differentiable, the authors pre-compute SOMs, optimise directly on the annotated screenshot using a differentiable resizing approximation, and periodically verify that g(s) = g(s+δ), rolling back to checkpoints with random perturbations if violated. PGD with Adam (lr=10⁻²) is used, projecting δ onto the feasible set (mask by 1_R, round to integer, clip to L∞ ball) after each step, with a stopping criterion requiring all next-token likelihoods of y to exceed 99%. For universal MIPs, the loss is averaged over batches of 8 randomly sampled (prompt, screenshot) pairs, and for multi-VLM MIPs, the loss is jointly minimised across three Llama-3.2 Vision models (11B-PT, 11B-IT, 90B-IT), yielding a single patch that hijacks all three within ~74,000 steps.

## Results

Universal MIPs achieve 100% Average Success Rate (ASR) on seen (prompt, screenshot) pairs at greedy decoding (MS temperature 0.0) in both desktop and social media settings. They transfer to unseen prompt-screenshot pairs with ASR of 1.00 (greedy) and 0.75–0.90 (temp 1.0), and to an unseen screen parser (GroundingDINO+TesseractOCR vs. OmniParser) with ASR of 0.40–0.98 (greedy) and 0.24–0.71 (temp 1.0). A single MIP jointly optimised for three VLMs (Llama-3.2-11B-PT, 11B-IT, 90B-IT) achieves ≥90% ASR across all three models on both seen and unseen pairs. Targeted MIPs (optimised for a single prompt-screen pair) achieve 100% ASR on that pair but drop to 0.0 on unseen screens, motivating the universal formulation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is an attack paper with no traditional method baselines; the key comparisons are transferability dimensions. Universal MIPs dramatically outperform targeted MIPs on generalisation: targeted MIPs achieve 0.0 ASR on unseen screens, while universal MIPs maintain 1.00 ASR (greedy) on unseen prompt-screen pairs. Transfer to an unseen screen parser (GroundingDINO+TesseractOCR replacing OmniParser) degrades ASR by 0.41–0.60 at greedy decoding in the desktop setting but only by 0.02 in the social media setting for y_w, suggesting the social media layout is more robust to parser variation. A jointly optimised MIP targeting three VLMs (11B-PT, 11B-IT, 90B-IT) achieves 0.93–0.96 ASR on unseen pairs across all models, but fails to transfer to a fourth unseen VLM (Llama-3.2-90B-Vision pre-trained), consistent with prior findings that adversarial images do not transfer across VLMs with different vision encoders. MIPs remain effective across execution steps (ASR 0.42–1.00 greedy), confirming they work not only at the initial screenshot but also mid-task.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends adversarial image attack techniques (PGD-based optimisation, following Madry et al.) from the VLM domain to the multi-component OS agent pipeline, building most directly on Bailey et al.'s 'Image Hijacks' work on adversarial images controlling tool-use models. It departs from prior OS agent attacks (prompt injection, adversarial pop-ups, captioning-model targeting by Wu et al.) by crafting direct adversarial patches on the screenshot that bypass the non-differentiable screen parser and manipulate the VLM end-to-end, while respecting the constraint that the adversary controls only a small screen region. The formal treatment of the parser's non-differentiability, the resizing transformation, and the discrete pixel constraint represents a methodological extension beyond standard VLM adversarial attack formulations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As OS agents (Anthropic's Computer Use, Windows Agent Arena agents) approach mainstream deployment, this work exposes a critical and hard-to-detect attack vector: imperceptible image patches embedded in wallpapers or social media posts can hijack agents into executing arbitrary malicious API calls, with potential for self-propagating 'computer worm' behaviour. Key limitations include the failure to transfer to unseen VLMs (consistent with prior VLM adversarial attack findings) and the requirement that the MIP be actually captured by the agent. Open questions include effective defence strategies (the paper suggests verifier modules and image preprocessing but notes a fundamental robustness-accuracy trade-off), cross-VLM transferability, and whether the attack generalises beyond the specific WAA environment and Llama 3.2 Vision models tested.
