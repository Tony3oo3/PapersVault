## Problem

Existing video-based skill assessment models are specialized to a single sport or drill and rely on expensive expert supervision that cannot scale across the ~8,000 sports in the world. All prior work evaluates only in-domain (same sport, same drill), leaving the critical question of whether skill understanding can transfer to novel, low-resource sports entirely unaddressed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255359/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a short video clip (single-view or multi-view, e.g., ego + four exo cameras) of one person performing a physical skill, along with the sport name embedded in a text prompt. The video is encoded at one feature vector per second (4096-d) by a frozen encoder. Outputs are threefold: an open-vocabulary set of skill-attribute phrases (e.g., 'control', 'leg positioning'), a sport-specific actionable feedback sentence (e.g., 'bend more while dribbling to maintain control'), and a four-class proficiency label (novice, intermediate, early expert, late expert). Skill-attributes are generated freely by the LLM rather than retrieved from a fixed taxonomy.

## Method

CROSSTRAINER factors skill assessment into a generalizable cross-sport component and a sport-specific component via a two-stage training pipeline. In Stage I, a frozen video encoder (EgoVLPv2 or CLIP) feeds a trainable two-layer MLP mapper that projects features into the token space of a multimodal LLM (Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct), which is fine-tuned with LoRA (rank 128) to generate skill-attribute phrases from expert commentary extracted via GPT-4 prompting. In Stage II, the same model is further fine-tuned to produce actionable feedback and proficiency scores conditioned on the inferred skill-attribute set, with proficiency estimated via a linear probe on the frozen video representation. This intermediate skill-attribute representation aligns video features toward transferable physical concepts (balance, timing, positioning), enabling graceful zero-shot degradation to unseen sports—achieving up to 60% relative gains over SOTA with only a 4% performance drop in the hardest zero-shot setting versus 17% for baselines.

## Results

On Ego-Exo4D actionable feedback generation, CROSSTRAINER achieves BERT-score 45.6, METEOR 51.7, and ROUGE-L 57.8, outperforming the prior SOTA ExpertAF (44.9/49.6/54.6) by up to 3.2 points. For skill-attribute generation (IoU@0.7), CROSSTRAINER reaches 25.7 on Ego-Exo4D and 37.6 on QEVD, exceeding the best retrieval baseline (Attribute-Retrieval: 19.7/32.4) by 6+ points. On QEVD actionable feedback, CROSSTRAINER scores METEOR 17.6 and ROUGE-L 18.1 versus Stream-VLM's 12.7/11.2. For proficiency estimation, the skill-attribute-pretrained representation improves accuracy by up to 6% over frozen EgoVLPv2 (e.g., 68.8% vs. 62.5% on soccer).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CROSSTRAINER outperforms all 10 baselines across every evaluation axis. Against the strongest prior SOTA, ExpertAF [9], it gains +0.7 B@4, +2.1 METEOR, and +3.2 ROUGE-L on Ego-Exo4D feedback, and +10.7 IoU@0.7 on skill-attribute generation. The retrieval-based InternVideo2 [88] (both NN and FT variants) trails by 2.7–10.7 IoU points on skill-attributes and 2.7–17.8 ROUGE-L on feedback, confirming that generative skill-attribute prediction beats closed-set retrieval. General-purpose multimodal LLMs (LLaVA [53], VideoChat2 [47], Video-ChatGPT [56], LLaMA-VID [50]) are substantially weaker, with LLaVA-FT the closest generalist at 43.5 B@4 vs. 45.6. Pose-based methods (PoseScript [23], PoseFix [24]) underperform by 21+ B@4 points, indicating that explicit 3D pose is not a sufficient signal for skill feedback. On QEVD, Stream-VLM [68] is the strongest fitness-specific baseline but is outperformed by +4.9 METEOR and +6.9 ROUGE-L. For proficiency estimation, the skill-attribute-pretrained representation beats frozen EgoVLPv2 [74] by 5.1 points on basketball, 6.3 on soccer, and 3.1 on rock climbing. In zero-shot transfer (novel sport), CROSSTRAINER degrades only 4% relative to fully-supervised, versus 17% for ExpertAF, demonstrating substantially more graceful cross-sport generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

CROSSTRAINER departs from prior video-based skill assessment work (ExpertAF, action quality assessment) which is uniformly limited to in-domain testing on a single sport or drill, by introducing cross-sport zero-shot transfer enabled by learned skill-attributes as intermediate representations. Conceptually, it repurposes the attribute-based zero-shot learning paradigm (originally for static visual semantics like 'striped' or 'black-and-white') to the dynamic execution domain, where attributes describe *how* a movement is performed rather than *what* is being performed. The approach builds on multimodal LLM architectures (LLaVA-style video-to-text generation with a trainable mapper) and draws motivation from cognitive science findings on perceptual-motor transfer between sports, translating those intuitive observations into a concrete video understanding model for the first time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The skill-attribute representation offers a practical path toward democratizing AI coaching for the long tail of ~8,000 sports that lack expert-annotated data, with the 4% max zero-shot degradation (vs. 17% for baselines) suggesting genuinely transferable execution understanding. Key limitations include single-person scope (no multi-player or opponent dynamics), absence of environmental context (terrain, equipment), and inability to capture non-visual feedback like intent or decision-making. Open questions the authors flag include quantifying sport relatedness to predict transferability, incorporating environmental affordances, and scaling to multi-agent interactions—areas where the current skill-attribute abstraction may need significant extension.
