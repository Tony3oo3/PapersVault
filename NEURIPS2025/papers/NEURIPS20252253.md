## Problem

End-to-end driving policies based on explicit regression average over the inherently multimodal distribution of driving behaviors, producing suboptimal or unsafe trajectories in ambiguous scenarios. Existing diffusion-based driving methods either apply diffusion only as a post-planning refinement (constraining generation to pre-processed decoder outputs) or rely on trajectory anchors that limit diversity, failing to fully exploit diffusion's generative capacity for closed-loop driving.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252253/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a front-view RGB image, a LiDAR point cloud projected into a BEV grid, ego-vehicle state, and a navigation command (target waypoints), all encoded by a shared backbone (RegNetY-3.2GF on CARLA, VoVNetV2-99 on NAVSIM). It outputs a K-waypoint future trajectory (K=10 timesteps) generated by a 2-step DDIM denoising process, plus a 4-class velocity label (brake, walking, low, high speed) from an explicit classification head. The trajectory is predicted directly in waypoint space rather than as noise, which the ablation shows is critical for lane-keeping performance.

## Method

DiffE2E builds on a TransFuser-style perception backbone with hierarchical bidirectional cross-attention to fuse LiDAR and camera features, then feeds the fused representation into a Transformer-based hybrid decoder. The decoder input concatenates the noisy trajectory tokens with learnable explicit-policy query tokens; a self-attention layer processes internal relationships, followed by cross-attention against a global condition vector that jointly encodes perception features, projected goal points, and diffusion timestep embeddings. The output is structurally split: the first ℓ_k positions feed a trajectory-reconstruction diffusion head (optimizing L2 distance to ground-truth waypoints), while the remaining ℓ_s positions feed an explicit multi-task head (velocity classification via weighted cross-entropy). A collaborative two-stage training paradigm first pre-trains the perception module with multi-task losses (segmentation, depth, detection), then jointly optimizes the diffusion and explicit heads, with the key design choice of predicting trajectories directly rather than noise, enabling high-quality generation in just 2 DDIM steps.

## Results

On the NAVSIM Navtest benchmark, DiffE2E achieves a PDMS of 92.7, surpassing Hydra-MDP++ (91.0), Hydra-MDP◇ (91.0), GoalFlow (90.3), and DiffusionDrive (88.1), with best-in-class scores on No-at-fault Collision (99.9), Drivable Area Compliance (98.6), and Time-To-Collision (99.3). On the CARLA Longest6 closed-loop benchmark, DiffE2E achieves a Driving Score of 79±4, Route Completion of 94±2, and Infraction Score of 0.84±0.02, outperforming the strongest explicit-policy baseline TF++ (DS 69±0, IS 0.72±0.01) by 10 points in DS and 0.12 in IS. Inference latency on a single RTX 3090 is 42.8 ms with 105M parameters, faster than GoalFlow (66.8 ms) while maintaining a larger encoder (VoVNetV2-99).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On NAVSIM, DiffE2E (PDMS 92.7) outperforms all prior methods, including the strongest explicit-policy baselines Hydra-MDP++ and Hydra-MDP◇ (both 91.0) and the diffusion-based GoalFlow (90.3) and DiffusionDrive (88.1). The margin is most pronounced on safety metrics: DiffE2E leads on No-at-fault Collision (99.9 vs. 98.6–98.7) and Time-To-Collision (99.3 vs. 95.0–95.1), while matching Hydra-MDP++ on Drivable Area Compliance (98.6). On Ego Progress, DiffE2E (85.3) trails Hydra-MDP◇ (86.5) by 1.2 points, the only sub-metric where it does not lead. On CARLA Longest6, DiffE2E (DS 79) beats TF++ (DS 69) by 10 points and LAVv2 (DS 58) by 21 points, with a 0.12 improvement in Infraction Score over TF++ (0.84 vs. 0.72). In inference latency, DiffE2E (42.8 ms) is 36% faster than GoalFlow (66.8 ms) despite using a comparable encoder, though it is slightly slower than DiffusionDrive (39.1 ms) and TransFuser (29.1 ms), which use the lighter ResNet-34 backbone.

## Relation to Prior Work

DiffE2E builds directly on the TransFuser/TF++ perception backbone (Jaeger et al., ICCV 2023) for multimodal LiDAR-camera fusion and on DDIM (Song et al., 2020) for accelerated diffusion sampling. Its key conceptual departure is from prior diffusion-based driving methods (DiffusionDrive, GoalFlow) that attach diffusion as a post-planning refinement step, and from explicit-policy methods (TransFuser, LAV, VAD) that collapse multimodal driving into a single regressed trajectory. Instead, DiffE2E introduces a hybrid diffusion-regression-classification decoder that structurally partitions the latent space: diffusion models the full trajectory distribution while explicit regression and classification handle discrete control variables like velocity class, trained jointly under a collaborative two-stage paradigm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to evaluate a diffusion-based end-to-end driving policy in closed-loop CARLA simulation, filling a gap where prior diffusion methods were only tested in non-reactive or open-loop settings. The hybrid paradigm offers a practical template for combining generative diversity with explicit controllability, a tension that plagues both pure diffusion and pure regression approaches in safety-critical domains. Open questions include whether the mandatory two-stage training (single-stage collapses to 18.2 DS) reflects a fundamental optimization conflict or a fixable initialization issue, and whether the approach generalizes beyond the specific CARLA/NAVSIM sensor suites to real-world deployment with more heterogeneous inputs.
