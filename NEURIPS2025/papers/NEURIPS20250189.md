## Problem

Unified vision-language models that jointly perform understanding and generation still largely fail to produce multimodal interleaved outputs—sequences that alternate between text and images within a single autoregressive trajectory. This limitation stems from the scarcity of fine-grained interleaved supervision data, causing models to default to single-modality outputs and fail on tasks requiring tightly coupled multimodal sequences such as visual storytelling and step-by-step visual reasoning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250189/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model (VILA-U, 7B) takes a text prompt and optionally an input image (resized to 256×256) and produces an interleaved text-image sequence in which text segments and generated images alternate within one decoding trajectory. Modality boundaries are made explicit via special tokens (think and <vis>), and image generation uses classifier-free guidance at scale 3. The output is a single coherent sequence rather than separate text and image artifacts, enabling context-aware transitions between modalities.

## Method

The approach is a two-stage post-training pipeline on VILA-U. First, a warm-up SFT stage uses a hybrid dataset (0.3M interleaved samples from ActivityNet, GenHowTo, and OpenStory++, plus 1M understanding and 1M text-to-image samples) to unlock latent interleaved generation while preserving pretrained capabilities. Second, a GRPO-based reinforcement fine-tuning stage treats the entire multimodal output as a single decision trajectory, computing a unified per-token KL divergence across both text and image tokens. The policy is optimized with a hybrid group-wise reward combining textual relevance (LLaVA-Critic), visual quality and alignment (ImageReward), and format fidelity, plus process-level rewards assigned at each modality-step boundary to provide dense step-wise guidance. With G=4 sampled completions per prompt over 3k steps on 32 A100 GPUs, the method achieves 59.50 on MMIE and 3.13 on InterleavedBench while retaining baseline-level understanding and generation performance.

## Results

On MMIE, the proposed method achieves 59.50% average score, outperforming the strongest baseline Anole (55.22%) by over 4 points and GILL (51.58%) by nearly 8 points. On InterleavedBench, the method reaches 3.13 average across five evaluation dimensions (text quality, perceptual quality, image coherence, text-image coherence, helpfulness), surpassing GILL (1.84) by 1.29 points. The QLoRA variant achieves 57.27% on MMIE and 2.87 on InterleavedBench, demonstrating competitive performance with significantly lower compute. On standard understanding and generation benchmarks (MME-P, MMvet, SEEDBench-img, POPE, GenEval), the method maintains performance comparable to the VILA-U baseline, confirming no catastrophic forgetting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against 10 unified multimodal models. On the primary interleaved generation benchmarks, the method decisively outperforms all dedicated interleaved-generation baselines: MiniGPT-5 (50.92→59.50 on MMIE, 1.82→3.13 on InterleavedBench), EMU-2 (45.33→59.50, 1.68→3.13), GILL (51.58→59.50, 1.84→3.13), and Anole (55.22→59.50 on MMIE, where Anole is the closest competitor). The largest margin is over EMU-2 (37B), a 14-point gap on MMIE, highlighting that the RL-based post-training strategy is more effective than simply scaling model size. On standard understanding and generation tasks (Table 5), the method matches VILA-U closely (MME-P 1425.2 vs 1401.8, POPE 85.1 vs 85.8, GenEval 0.46 vs 0.42) while trailing larger models like Janus-Pro (GenEval 0.80) and TokenFlow-XL (GenEval 0.55), confirming that the approach preserves pretrained capabilities without degrading them. Notably, Chameleon and ARMOR, which also support interleaved output, show substantially weaker understanding scores (MME-P 153.1 and 1619.4 respectively), whereas the proposed method retains VILA-U-level understanding while adding interleaved generation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on VILA-U as its foundation unified model and extends Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), originally designed for text-only LLMs (DeepSeekMath), to the multimodal interleaved generation setting. Prior unified models (Chameleon, GILL, Anole, ARMOR) have attempted interleaved text-image generation primarily through architectural design and large-scale training data, but remain limited by the scarcity of high-quality interleaved supervision. The key departure here is formulating interleaved generation as a single autoregressive decision trajectory optimized via RL with hybrid rewards (textual, visual, format) and process-level signals, rather than relying on architecture changes or massive interleaved corpora.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a small amount of curated interleaved data (0.3M samples) combined with GRPO-based post-training can unlock interleaved generation in an existing unified model without degrading its understanding or text-to-image capabilities, offering a data-efficient path for practitioners. However, the method does not improve general multimodal understanding or generation quality, and failure cases reveal persistent hallucination and stylistic inconsistency issues. Open questions include whether the approach scales to additional modalities (video, audio), whether stronger base models would yield larger gains, and how to design reward signals that better capture factual consistency and reasoning traceability in complex interleaved outputs.
