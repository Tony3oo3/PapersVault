## Problem

Attention's quadratic complexity makes it a bottleneck for generation models, and while quantization can exploit low-bit Tensor Cores for speedup, two gaps remain: (1) no prior work has designed FP4 attention leveraging the new Blackwell GPU FP4 Tensor Cores for plug-and-play inference acceleration, and (2) all existing low-bit attention methods (FlashAttention3, SageAttention) target inference only, leaving the feasibility of low-bit attention for training large models entirely unexplored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251799/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

SageAttention3 takes standard FP16 Q, K, V matrices (N×D per head) and produces the FP16 attention output O, operating within FlashAttention's tiled block structure (B_q×D for Q, B_kv×D for K/V). The FP4 quantization uses NVFP4 format (E2M1 data with 1×16 microscaling blocks and E4M3 FP8 scale factors), while SageBwd uses INT8 per-block quantization (scale = max(|X|)/127) with the critical dOV^T matmul retained in FP16 during backward. End-to-end, the methods accept text prompts and produce generated videos (CogVideoX, HunyuanVideo, Mochi) or images (Flux, Stable-Diffusion 3.5) with no measurable quality degradation.

## Method

SageAttention3 applies FP4 microscaling quantization to both attention matmuls (QK^T and PV) with a 1×16 group size, containing outlier effects within each block. The key insight for the attention map P is a two-level quantization: first normalizing each row to [0, 448×6] via per-token scaling, then applying standard FP4 microscaling, which maximizes the E4M3 scale factor's representable range (127 distinct values vs. 35 in direct quantization) and reduces relative quantization error. Hardware optimizations include permuting K's columns to match FP4 accumulator layout, fusing quantization with online softmax to halve shuffle operations, and a producer-warp ping-pong scheduling scheme. SageBwd extends this to training by quantizing 6 of 7 attention matmuls to INT8 (per-block for Q, K, V, dS; per-token for P), while keeping dOV^T in FP16 because its quantization error propagates through dP and dS into accumulated gradient errors for Q and K across the sequence. The method achieves 1038 TOPS on RTX5090 (5× over FlashAttention2) for inference and lossless fine-tuning performance for training.

## Results

SageAttention3 achieves 1038 TOPS on RTX5090, a 5× speedup over FlashAttention2 (214 TOPS) and 11× over xFormers, while maintaining end-to-end generation quality across video (CogVideoX, HunyuanVideo, Mochi) and image (Flux, SD3.5) models. End-to-end inference latency drops from 489s to 164s on HunyuanVideo (~3×) and from 64s to 27s on CogVideoX (~2.4×). For training, SageBwd (INT8) achieves up to 1.67× speedup over FlashAttention2 on RTX4090 and matches BF16 fine-tuning accuracy (e.g., Qwen2.5-3B: GSM8K 0.607 vs 0.601, MMLU 0.653 vs 0.640), though it converges more slowly in pretraining.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against FlashAttention2 (Dao, ICLR 2024), SageAttention3 delivers a 5× kernel speedup (1038 vs 214 TOPS on RTX5090) with only a minor accuracy drop (99.551% vs 100% CosSim), and SageBwd provides 1.67× training speedup while matching BF16 fine-tuning quality. Against xFormers (Lefaudeux, 2022), the speedup is even larger at 11× for inference. Compared to the authors' own prior work SageAttention1 (Zhang, ICLR 2025) and SageAttention2 (Zhang, ICML 2025), SageAttention3 nearly doubles throughput (1038 vs 479/643 TOPS) at a small accuracy cost (99.551% vs 99.996%/99.995% CosSim), and end-to-end video/image quality metrics remain within noise of full-precision. FlashAttention3 (Shah, NeurIPS 2024) is not directly comparable on RTX5090 (Hopper-only), but theoretical analysis shows SageAttention3's FP4 peak throughput (1676 TOPS on RTX5090) far exceeds FlashAttention3's FP8 peak (419 TOPS). For training, SageBwd outperforms FP8 SageBwd in gradient accuracy (e.g., dQ L1 error 0.029 vs 0.070) and fine-tuning quality, while both remain lossless relative to BF16 on fine-tuning but show slower convergence than BF16 in pretraining.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper is the direct successor to SageAttention (ICLR 2025) and SageAttention2 (ICML 2025), inheriting their outlier-smoothing techniques (smoothing Q and K) and per-block quantization framework, while pushing the precision boundary from INT8 to FP4 microscaling (NVFP4) to exploit Blackwell GPU Tensor Cores. The FlashAttention tiling and online softmax structure remains the computational backbone, and the two-level quantization for the attention map P is a novel technique introduced here to address the narrow dynamic range of softmax outputs in FP8 scale factors. The most significant departure is extending low-bit attention from inference-only (as in FlashAttention3, SageAttention, SageAttention2) to training, with SageBwd being the first trainable 8-bit attention that quantizes six of seven backward matrix multiplications while keeping dOV^T in FP16.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 5× inference speedup on RTX5090 (1038 TOPS) is a major practical win as Blackwell GPUs become the standard for consumer and workstation workloads, and the plug-and-play nature across video, image, and text models makes it immediately deployable. The finding that 8-bit attention achieves lossless fine-tuning but exhibits slower pretraining convergence is an important empirical boundary that guides practitioners on where low-bit training is viable. Key open questions include whether the pretraining gap can be closed with better quantization strategies for gradient accumulation, and whether FP4 attention's accuracy holds as sequence lengths grow further (the paper notes accumulated error across layers, mitigated by keeping 3 sensitive layers in FP16). The demonstrated complementarity between INT8 training and FP4 inference (Table 5) suggests a coherent low-bit pipeline for the full model lifecycle.
