## Problem

Black-box ML service providers must keep models and client data confidential, yet outside auditors need to verify fairness properties like demographic parity. Existing offline fairness certificates fail in practice because they certify fairness on static datasets that don't generalize under distribution shift, and are vulnerable to model-switching and data-forging attacks by malicious providers. The paper asks how to issue reliable, confidential fairness certificates measured directly on online deployment data.

## Input / Output

In the Service Phase, each client submits a query point (features plus a binary demographic attribute) and a randomness seed to the provider, and sends the auditor a hash-based commitment C = H(q‖α_s‖o‖r) along with two random strings α₀, α₁ encoding the sensitive attribute. In the Audit Phase, the provider and auditor exchange ZKP messages over all n committed queries plus a group-balanced random sample of 2ν queries, ultimately producing a single binary pass/fail bit b_pass indicating whether the demographic parity gap is within the public threshold θ. The client-facing cost is only ~0.23 s per query (commitment generation), while the expensive ZKP inference proofs are deferred to the offline Audit Phase.

## Method

OATH is a two-phase zero-knowledge protocol that exploits a 'cut-and-choose' synergy between cryptographic verification and the statistical structure of group fairness. In the Service Phase, clients generate fair coins, send signed queries to the provider, and forward hiding commitments to the auditor; the sensitive attribute is encoded via random strings α₀, α₁ so the provider cannot flip it before the audit. In the Audit Phase, the provider IT-MAC-authenticates all query-answer tuples and the model, computes the empirical demographic parity gap over all n queries in zero-knowledge, then proves validity on only a group-balanced random sample of 2ν queries via three checks: sensitive-attribute consistency, commitment consistency (hash match), and inference correctness (ZKP of correct model evaluation). Theorem 4.1 shows the probability that a malicious provider evades detection while shifting the fairness gap by ε decays as (1−ε/2)^ν, making the sample size a tunable reliability parameter. On a 42.5 M-parameter ResNet-101, OATH achieves 4.4 s amortized runtime per query (0.23 s online) versus 535 s for a per-query ZKP baseline, a >100× improvement.

## Results

OATH achieves a consistent 0.23 s client-facing (online) runtime per query across all model sizes, from a 130-parameter FFNN to a 42.5M-parameter ResNet-101. Total amortized runtime for 10⁶ queries is 0.235 s/query (FFNN), 0.28 s/query (LeNet-5, 62K params), 2.90 s/query (ResNet-50, 23.5M params), and 4.43 s/query (ResNet-101, 42.5M params). The Service Phase takes 0.22–0.25 s per query across all five fairness benchmarking datasets (COMPAS, Crime, Credit, Adult, German), while the Audit Phase for 10⁶ queries completes in 82.8–84.7 min (logistic regression) or 93.1–123.3 min (FFNN).

## Baselines & Comparisons

OATH is compared against two baselines: FairProof [49] (a ZKP method for online individual fairness certificates) and a per-query ZKP-of-inference baseline built on Mystique [48]. Against FairProof on a 130-parameter FFNN, OATH reduces client-facing runtime from 236.4 s to 0.23 s (≈1000×) and total amortized runtime from 316.8 s to 0.235 s (≈1350×). Against the Mystique-based baseline, OATH reduces online runtime from 5.9 s / 333 s / 535 s to a constant 0.23 s for LeNet-5 / ResNet-50 / ResNet-101 respectively, and total amortized runtime from 5.9 s / 333 s / 535 s to 0.28 s / 2.90 s / 4.43 s — a 21× to 121× speedup that grows with model size. The key architectural advantage is that OATH requires no client-facing ZKP (only lightweight hash commitments), offloading all expensive zero-knowledge operations to the offline Audit Phase between the service provider and auditor.

## Relation to Prior Work

OATH builds on Wolverine [47] for ZKP circuit evaluation, ZKRAM [17] for verified random-access, and Mystique [48] for ZKP of ML inference, combining them with a cut-and-choose [53] protocol design. It departs from FairProof [49], which certifies online *individual* fairness but requires minutes of client-facing ZKP per query, and from Confidential-PROFITT [40], which certifies *offline* group fairness for decision trees only. The key conceptual departure is exploiting the aggregate statistical nature of group fairness to verify only a constant-size random sample (via cut-and-choose) rather than performing per-query ZKP, yielding a 'commit online, prove offline' architecture that eliminates client-facing cryptographic overhead entirely.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

OATH is the first method to provide a privacy-preserving, reliable, and scalable certificate of online group fairness, addressing both the distribution-shift and model-switching vulnerabilities of offline certificates. The 1000×+ reduction in client-facing runtime (0.23 s vs. 236 s for FairProof) makes ZKP-based fairness auditing potentially deployable in real ML pipelines, though the Audit Phase still requires ~80–120 minutes for 10⁶ queries. Open questions include whether the cut-and-choose pattern extends to other ML properties (robustness, calibration, security), how to handle collusion or Sybil attacks, and whether the consistency-check bottleneck (hash-to-IT-MAC conversion) can be further optimized. The authors also note that group fairness verification is necessary but not sufficient for equitable outcomes, positioning OATH as one component in an ensemble of auditing tools.
