## Problem

The rapid proliferation of digital pathology foundation models has made it difficult to assess their relative strengths, yet existing benchmarks are largely slide-level (confounding model quality with MIL aggregation), lack open-source implementations, and omit critical analyses of feature spaces, calibration, and adversarial robustness. THUNDER addresses this by providing a tile-level benchmark that isolates each model's representational power from downstream aggregation, while simultaneously evaluating uncertainty and robustness properties essential for clinical deployment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255181/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark consumes H&E-stained histopathology tiles of varying sizes (96×96 to 1536×2048 pixels) and magnifications (5×–40×) across 16 public datasets spanning breast, colorectal, renal, esophageal, and multi-organ cancers. Each tile is encoded by one of 23 pre-trained vision encoders (ViT-based, 86M–1.1B parameters), and the resulting embeddings are evaluated across six task families: kNN/linear-probing/few-shot classification, semantic segmentation via a Segmenter decoder, feature-space alignment (Mutual knn), transformation invariance, calibration (ECE/MCE/ACE/TACE), and PGD adversarial robustness. Outputs are structured as per-dataset scores with 95% bootstrap confidence intervals, enabling both per-task and rank-sum global comparisons.

## Method

THUNDER's core design choice is to operate exclusively at the tile/patch level, eliminating the slide-level MIL aggregation step that confounds model comparisons in prior benchmarks and reducing compute from >10,000 GPU-hours (slide-level) to <500 GPU-hours for the full 23-model ensemble. The benchmark evaluates models along three complementary axes: (i) downstream performance via kNN classification, linear probing, SimpleShot few-shot learning, Segmenter-based segmentation, and LoRA adaptation; (ii) feature-space analysis using Mutual knn alignment metrics, image retrieval, and invariance to 15 stochastic transformations including the histopathology-specific HED color perturbation; and (iii) reliability via calibration metrics on linear probes and F1-score degradation under PGD adversarial attacks at multiple ε budgets. Fixed train/val/test splits with automatic hyperparameter search (learning rate, weight decay, k) ensure fair, reproducible comparison, and the open-source implementation supports seamless integration of new user-defined models.

## Results

Across 16 tile-level datasets and 6 evaluation tasks, uni2h achieves the best overall rank-sum score (16, rank 1), leading in knn classification (81.7 F1), linear probing (83.9 F1), and few-shot 16-shot (78.4 F1). Virchow2 ranks second (rank-sum 27) with the best segmentation Dice (69.3) and smallest adversarial F1 drop (33.6), while midnight ranks third (rank-sum 31) with the best calibration (ECE 3.2). Histopathology-specific models consistently outperform natural-image pre-trained models (e.g., uni2h 81.7 vs. DINOv2-B 67.9 on knn), and vision-language models (keep, titan) show particular strength in few-shot settings despite not reaching the top-4 overall.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The benchmark compares 23 foundation models: 11 vision-only pathology models (uni2h, virchow2, midnight, uni, hopt0/1, hiboub/l, phikon/2), 6 vision-language pathology models (conch, titan, keep, musk, plip, quilt), and 6 natural-image models (DINOv2-B/L, ViT-B/L, CLIP-B/L). Histopathology-specific models dominate: the top-5 (uni2h, virchow2, midnight, uni, hopt1/keep) are all pathology-pretrained, while natural-image models occupy the bottom 6 ranks. Uni2h leads on knn (81.7 vs. 78.2 for midnight, 77.3 for conch), linear probing (83.9 vs. 82.9 for midnight), and few-shot (78.4 vs. 75.8 for keep), but virchow2 surpasses it on segmentation (69.3 vs. 69.0) and adversarial robustness (33.6 vs. 34.3 F1 drop). Vision-language models excel in few-shot settings (keep 75.8, titan 74.6 at 16-shot) yet are most vulnerable to adversarial attacks (titan 75.3, musk 69.3 F1 drop) and show the worst calibration (plip ECE 7.0). Calibration does not correlate with discriminative performance: midnight achieves the best ECE (3.2) despite ranking 8th on knn, while top performer uni2h has a middling ECE of 4.5.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of pathology foundation model benchmarking (eva, PathoBench, HEST-Benchmark, PathBench) by shifting the evaluation from slide-level MIL to tile-level, thereby isolating the representational power of vision encoders from aggregation strategies. It builds on the Platonic Representation Hypothesis [28] to introduce feature space alignment metrics (Mutual knn) as a first-class evaluation axis, a dimension entirely absent from prior pathology benchmarks. The key conceptual departure is treating benchmarking as a multi-axis problem—downstream performance, feature space geometry, calibration, and adversarial robustness—rather than a single downstream-accuracy comparison, and packaging it as a dynamic, open-source tool rather than a static report.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

THUNDER provides the digital pathology community with a fast, reproducible, and extensible tile-level benchmark that decouples model quality from slide-level aggregation, enabling more direct and computationally efficient comparisons (under 500 GPU-hours vs. 10,000+ for slide-level). The finding that no vision-language model reaches the top-4 in the global ranking, and that feature space alignment *decreases* during LoRA adaptation (models diverge rather than converge), challenges working assumptions about VLM superiority and backbone interchangeability. Open questions include whether these tile-level conclusions transfer to slide-level settings, how to combine complementary foundation models, and whether the benchmark's well-studied datasets have reached performance saturation, limiting its discriminative power for future models.
