## Problem

Whether generative diffusion with Approximate Message Passing (AMP) as the score function can efficiently sample from non-convex solution spaces of perceptron problems remains unresolved, and prior diffusion-sampling analyses were restricted to planted ensembles with tractable normalizers. For the binary perceptron, the uniform distribution over solutions is known to be algorithmically intractable due to the overlap gap property, yet no controlled sampler existed that could provably produce fair samples from a well-defined distribution over the solution space.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a dataset of M i.i.d. patterns x^μ ∈ R^N (Gaussian or ±1), a margin κ, a load α = M/N, and a potential U(s) with temperature T, and produces weight vectors w that satisfy all M stability constraints s^μ ≥ κ. Beyond individual solutions, the framework outputs the full stability distribution P(s) and predicts exact algorithmic sampling thresholds in the α–κ plane. The target density is defined only up to an intractable partition function Z, which the double replica trick handles analytically in the N→∞ limit.

## Method

The paper develops a replica-theory formalism that applies the replica trick twice—s replicas to handle the unknown normalization Z^{-1} and n replicas to linearize the log of the tilted partition function—yielding a time-dependent free entropy φ_t(q) whose unimodality in the overlap q determines whether ASL succeeds. For the spherical perceptron with negative margin, ASL samples the uniform distribution throughout the Replica Symmetric region, failing precisely at the De Almeida–Thouless line where ergodicity breaks. For the binary perceptron, the analysis reveals a permanent peak at q=1 for any non-diverging potential, explaining the intractability of uniform sampling; introducing U(s) = −log(s) eliminates this peak and yields an algorithmic threshold of α_alg ≈ 0.65 at κ=0. This log-potential is then reshaped into a τ-annealing MCMC scheme (U_τ(s) interpolating between a linear penalty and the log-potential) that provides a practical, robust sampler for the binary perceptron solution space.

## Results

For the spherical perceptron with negative margin, ASL successfully samples the uniform distribution over the solution space throughout the Replica Symmetric region, with the samplability frontier coinciding exactly with the De Almeida-Thouless (DAT) line. For the binary perceptron, the uniform distribution is provably unsamplable by ASL for all α > 0 due to a persistent peak at q=1 in the free entropy. Introducing the log-potential U(s) = −log(s) restores efficient sampling up to an algorithmic threshold of α_alg ≈ 0.65 (for κ=0, T=0.5), approaching the sat/unsat transition at α_c ≈ 0.83. The derived τ-annealing MCMC scheme solves random binary perceptron instances up to α ≈ 0.55 with O(N) sweeps, substantially outperforming standard Simulated Annealing which fails at large system sizes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary empirical comparison is between the proposed τ-annealing MCMC scheme and standard Simulated Annealing (T-annealing) on the potential U(s) = −sΘ(−s). On random binary perceptron instances, τ-annealing maintains a high probability of finding solutions as N increases (solving up to α ≈ 0.55 with N sweeps), while T-annealing degrades rapidly and fails at large N even when scaling sweeps as O(N). On the MNIST binary classification task (digits 3 vs. 8, N=784), τ-annealing with the log-potential achieves a higher fraction of zero-error instances across all tested constraint densities α compared to T-annealing with both U(s)=Θ(s) and U(s)=−sΘ(s), with the gap widening at higher α. The ASL sampler's empirical stability distributions match the replica-theory predictions exactly in the samplable region, validating the theoretical framework. The ASL algorithmic threshold (α_alg ≈ 0.65) is slightly below the best known heuristic solver thresholds (Braunstein-Zecchina, max-sum), but unlike those solvers, ASL produces provably fair samples from a fully characterized target distribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the replica-theoretic analysis of diffusion-based sampling introduced by Ghio et al. (2024) from planted ensembles to general unnormalized target densities, achieved through a double application of the replica trick. The sampling algorithm itself, Algorithmic Stochastic Localization (ASL), builds on Eldan's Stochastic Localization process and El Alaoui et al.'s (2022) introduction of AMP as the score approximator, while the perceptron problem setup draws on decades of statistical physics literature (Krauth & Mézard, Franz & Parisi, Baldassi et al.). The key conceptual departure is the discovery that the obstruction to sampling the binary perceptron under the uniform measure is structural (a persistent free-entropy peak at q=1 from isolated solutions) and can be overcome by a simple log-potential tilt, a finding not anticipated in prior analyses of the overlap gap property.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first analytically controlled sampler for the binary perceptron solution space, with an algorithmic threshold (α_alg ≈ 0.65) close to the sat/unsat transition (α_c ≈ 0.83), and a practical τ-annealing MCMC scheme that outperforms standard simulated annealing at large system sizes. The general framework—replica analysis of time-dependent free entropy to predict diffusion sampling feasibility—could be extended to other hard constraint satisfaction problems with isolated solutions. Key limitations include reliance on the non-rigorous replica method (results are conjectural), AMP's failure on structured/real-world data, and the fact that the samplable region for the spherical perceptron stops short of the 1RSB dynamical transition, leaving a gap between what is information-theoretically possible and algorithmically achievable.
