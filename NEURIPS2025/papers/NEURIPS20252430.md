## Problem

In adversarial linear partial monitoring, the general EXO framework of Lattimore and Gyorgy yields regret bounds expressed through an opaque information-ratio quantity, and its implementation requires solving a high-dimensional optimization over all possible loss estimators at every step. While Kirschner et al. derived interpretable alignment-constant bounds in the stochastic setting, no analogous transparent, efficiently implementable guarantees existed for the nonstochastic (adversarial) case. This paper closes that gap by providing a concrete, implementable EXO policy whose regret bounds depend explicitly on game-structure constants that measure the alignment between observations and losses.

## Input / Output

The learner is given a finite action set [k] with associated feature vectors ψ_a ∈ R^d, per-action observation matrices M_a ∈ R^{d×n(a)}, and a convex compact loss space L ⊂ R^d containing a centered L2 ball. At each round the learner selects an action and receives only the partial signal φ_t = M_{A_t}^T ℓ_t ∈ R^{n(A_t)}, not the true loss ψ_{A_t}^T ℓ_t. The algorithm outputs a distribution p_t over actions each round, and the theoretical contribution is regret upper bounds parameterized by interpretable constants (β_loc, β_glo, w*, u*) that quantify how well observations span the relevant feature differences, rather than by abstract information-ratio terms.

## Method

The paper instantiates the EXO method with two key structural choices: (1) predictions q_t are produced by exponential weights restricted to Pareto-optimal actions A*, and (2) the loss estimator is an 'anchored' linear estimator ŷ_t(a) = (ψ_a − Hq_t)^T Q(p_t)^† M_{A_t} φ_t, shifted about the q_t-weighted feature mean Hq_t. This anchoring is critical because it rewrites the variance term in the regret bound purely in terms of feature differences (ψ_a − ψ_b), which is exactly the quantity governed by the observability conditions. The exploration distribution p̃_t is then obtained by solving a finite-dimensional convex program (minimize max over ℓ ∈ L of a linear-plus-quadratic objective) subject to a convex constraint, making the algorithm efficiently implementable. Bounding the optimal value Λ*_η via Sion's minimax theorem, the paper constructs different exploration distributions for the two observability classes: for locally observable games, it uses weight vectors from the set W_ℓ^loc that recover each Pareto-optimal action's feature difference from observations of only better-performing actions, yielding a √T rate; for globally observable games, it sets the exploration target equal to q_t and relies on a uniform exploration component, yielding a T^{2/3} rate. The resulting bounds feature instance-dependent constants (β_loc, β_glo, w*) that are shown to be near-tight in several canonical settings including full information, feedback graphs, linear bandits, and dueling bandits.

## Results

The paper derives instance-dependent regret bounds for adversarial linear partial monitoring via an anchored EXO policy. For locally observable games, the regret is O(√(αT log k)) where α = 12L²β_loc²d, matching the √T minimax rate; for globally observable games, it is O((αT)^(2/3)(log k)^(1/3)) with α depending on β_glo and an optimal-design constant w*, matching the T^(2/3) rate. Concrete instantiations include O(√(T log k)) for full information, O(√(α(G)T log k)) for strongly observable feedback graphs (α(G) = independence number), O(√(dT log k)) for linear bandits, and O((δ(G) log k)^(1/3) T^(2/3)) for weakly observable feedback graphs (δ(G) = total domination number). The bound for bandits with ill-conditioned observers, O((1/ε)√(kT log k)), is shown to be tight up to logarithmic factors.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no experimental baselines, so comparisons are between the derived regret bounds and prior theoretical guarantees. Compared to Lattimore and Gyorgy [37], who gave a general EXO bound via the information ratio for adversarial linear partial monitoring, the present paper's bounds are more concrete and interpretable, featuring explicit alignment constants (β_loc, β_glo) rather than an opaque information-ratio quantity. Compared to Lattimore and Szepesvári [40] (EXO for finite partial monitoring), the paper achieves the same √T and T^(2/3) rates but extends them to infinite outcome spaces with linear structure, and introduces a more natural exploration distribution construction for locally observable games. Compared to Kirschner et al. [31, 32] (stochastic linear partial monitoring via IDS), the bounds feature analogous alignment constants but in the adversarial setting, and additionally yield near-tight guarantees for feedback-rich problems (full information, feedback graphs) that were not addressed in the stochastic work. The paper's main advantage over all prior work is the transparency of the game-structure dependence: the constants β_loc and β_glo directly measure the 'difficulty' of recovering feature differences from observations, making it clear when the bounds are tight.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of the EXO (exploration-by-optimization) framework for finite partial monitoring [Lattimore & Szepesvári, 2020] and the general adversarial linear partial monitoring setting of Lattimore and Gyorgy [2021], which established regret bounds via an information-ratio argument but left the algorithm as a non-constructive template requiring infinite-dimensional optimization at each step. The paper departs from that generality by committing to a specific, implementable loss estimator structure (anchored linear bandit-style estimation) that reduces the per-round optimization to a finite-dimensional convex program. Its regret bounds feature alignment constants (β_loc, β_glo) that mirror those in the stochastic linear partial monitoring work of Kirschner et al. [2020, 2023], bridging the adversarial and stochastic analyses in a way that prior adversarial results (which relied on opaque information-ratio quantities) did not achieve.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The main practical impact is that the algorithm is now concretely implementable (solving a convex program over probability distributions rather than an infinite-dimensional optimization), and the bounds give practitioners interpretable game-structure quantities to assess difficulty within each observability class. The paper demonstrates near-optimality in several settings (full information, strongly observable feedback graphs, linear bandits, ill-conditioned observers), but acknowledges limitations: it is restricted to full-dimensional loss spaces and finite action sets, and does not recover tight guarantees for general finite partial monitoring. Open questions include extending to low-dimensional loss spaces, generic compact action sets, and characterizing how minimax regret depends on instance-based constants more sharply.
