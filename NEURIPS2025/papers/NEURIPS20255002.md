## Problem

Existing RL-based one-shot DAG scheduling methods suffer from two interrelated flaws: they perform redundant pairwise priority comparisons among all unselected nodes (including those on the same precedence path where comparison is irrelevant), which distorts the softmax-based sampling probability and inflates policy gradient variance; and they rely on a single sparse global reward for the entire priority list, providing no node-level credit assignment to guide local decision optimization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255002/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a DAG G=(V,E) with per-node attributes (processing time, computational workload, output data size) and per-processor attributes (computational capacity) as input, encoded into a single 64-dimensional node embedding vector per node. It produces a complete node execution order in one forward pass, but the execution order is materialized through a priority topological sort where, at each step, only the zero-in-degree nodes of the current subgraph are compared via Gumbel-perturbed logits. The output also includes per-node sampling probabilities computed by softmax over each comparable antichain, enabling a trajectory-like interpretation of the one-shot solution.

## Method

The core mechanism is a Comparable Antichain Identification (CAI) module that, during priority topological sorting, restricts each step's candidate set to the zero-in-degree nodes V_t^{in=0} of the current subgraph, which provably form a comparable antichain (Lemma 1) and exclude all successors of those nodes (Lemma 2). This eliminates redundant comparisons with nodes on the same path, yielding an accurate per-node sampling probability via softmax over the antichain rather than over all remaining nodes. For training, a dense reward R_t = C(Solution(G;θ)) − C(S_t(G;θ)) is computed per node from the simulation, representing the remaining cost after scheduling the t-th node; a heuristic advantage baseline (e.g., HEFT) is subtracted to form A_t, reducing gradient variance. The architecture uses a 4-layer Graphormer encoder with 4 attention heads feeding a 64×64×1 MLP policy network that outputs all node logits in a single forward pass, with Gumbel noise added for stochastic sampling within each antichain.

## Results

On the Pegasus SIPHT benchmark (heterogeneous multiprocessor), the proposed method achieves makespans of 196.9 (SIPHT-100, −13.3% vs. HEFT), 338.4 (SIPHT-200, −5.42%), and 1819.2 (SIPHT-1000, −0.13%), consistently outperforming all RL baselines. On JSSP, the method achieves the largest relative gains, e.g., 571.3 makespan on JSSP 30×10 (−32.46% vs. SPT baseline) versus 735.6 for Jeon et al. and 971.3 for POMO-DAG. On TPC-H (homogeneous single-processor), the method reaches 20.49 makespan on TPC-H-50 (−17.73% vs. STF). Runtime remains competitive at 0.3–2.6 s per instance, well below POMO-DAG (3–50 s) and EGS (1–72 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Jeon et al. [2023] (the closest one-shot RL baseline), the proposed method wins on nearly all instances: e.g., 196.9 vs. 218.5 makespan on SIPHT-100 (a 10% improvement) and 571.3 vs. 735.6 on JSSP 30×10 (22% improvement), with the gap widening on larger and more interconnected DAGs. Against POMO-DAG (incremental construction), the method achieves comparable or better makespan in most settings but is dramatically faster (0.3–2.6 s vs. 3–50 s); the sole exception is JSSP 20×10 where POMO-DAG's simpler disjunctive structure gives it a slight edge (341.6 vs. 397.3). Against EGS (edge-generation RL), the proposed method matches or exceeds solution quality (e.g., 196.9 vs. 200.6 on SIPHT-100; 571.3 vs. 837.5 on JSSP 30×10) while running 5–25× faster, since EGS requires iterative edge resampling and repeated graph encoding. Against the HEFT heuristic baseline, the method consistently improves makespan on smaller-to-medium instances (up to −13.3% on SIPHT-100) but converges to near-parity on the largest instances (SIPHT-1000: −0.13%), where the heuristic is already close to the lower bound.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends the one-shot RL-based DAG scheduling paradigm introduced by Jeon et al. (ICLR 2023), which uses Gumbel-perturbed logits to sample a global priority list in a single forward pass. The key departure is a theoretical diagnosis: existing one-shot methods perform redundant pairwise priority comparisons among nodes on the same path, leading to biased sampling probability estimation and inflated policy gradient variance. The authors formalize this via a 'comparable antichain' concept (with two supporting lemmas) and pair it with a node-level dense reward derived from simulation, positioning their work as a targeted fix to two identified flaws in the one-shot list-scheduling framework rather than a new scheduling paradigm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The comparable antichain identification provides a clean, provably correct way to compute nodewise sampling probabilities in one-shot DAG scheduling, which should benefit any future method in this paradigm. However, the paper is explicitly scoped to makespan minimization under precedence constraints within the list-scheduling framework; it does not address deadline constraints, resource contention beyond simple processor assignment, or domain-specific objectives. Open questions include whether the antichain-based probability correction generalizes to sequence-agnostic formulations (e.g., one-shot edge generation) and whether the dense reward design transfers to other COPs with decomposable cost functions.
