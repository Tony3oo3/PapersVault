## Problem

Concept Bottleneck Models (CBMs) extract human-interpretable concepts from a single holistic image encoding, which limits their expressiveness for complex object-centric visual reasoning tasks such as multi-label classification and logical combinations of objects. Prior object-centric concept-based work has been restricted to synthetic data, leaving a gap for natural-image settings where discrete object representations are essential for structured reasoning and fine-grained explanations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252317/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard RGB image and produces both a task prediction (single- or multi-label class) and an interpretable concept activation vector. Internally, the image is first decomposed into up to k object crops via a frozen object-proposal model (Mask R-CNN or SAM), and each crop plus the full image is encoded by SpLiCE into a shared sparse concept vocabulary of dimension C. The final concept representation has dimension (k+1)·C under concatenation or C (or 2C for sum+count) under the other aggregation schemes, and the linear predictor maps this to M class logits.

## Method

OCB is a three-stage pipeline: (I) an object proposal module (Mask R-CNN or SAM) generates bounding boxes that are refined by size thresholds, a confidence cutoff, IoU-based non-maximum suppression, and a cap of k proposals; (II) a frozen SpLiCE concept-discovery module encodes both the full image and each object crop into C-dimensional sparse concept activations, which are zero-padded to a fixed length k; (III) the image-level and object-level activations are combined via one of five aggregation strategies (concat, sum, max, count, or sum+count) and passed through a single trainable linear layer. Only this linear predictor is trained end-to-end on task labels; both the object detector and the concept encoder remain frozen pretrained models, eliminating the need for concept or object annotations. The key design choice is the aggregation step, which trades off per-object traceability (concat) against a fixed-size, order-invariant encoding (sum, max, count, sum+count), and the paper provides a systematic ablation showing no single aggregation dominates across all tasks.

## Results

OCB (RCNN) achieves 85.75 mAP on PASCAL-VOC, 87.33 mAP on COCO(h), 64.12 mAP on COCO(l), 75.28% accuracy on SUN397, and 68.84% balanced accuracy on COCOLogic, outperforming the SpLiCE-based CBM baseline by 3.33, 2.60, 4.93, 0.49, and 10.00 points respectively. The non-interpretable CLIP+MLP upper bound still leads by 3.85–4.08 points on most datasets, but OCB narrows this gap substantially compared to the vanilla CBM (which trails CLIP+MLP by 7.18–9.01 points). On the novel COCOLogic benchmark requiring logical object reasoning, OCB (RCNN) reaches 68.84% balanced accuracy versus 58.84% for the CBM baseline and 65.95% for CLIP+MLP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OCB (RCNN) outperforms the SpLiCE-based CBM baseline on all five datasets, with the largest gains on COCOLogic (+10.00 balanced accuracy) and COCO(l) (+4.93 mAP), confirming that object-level concepts are most beneficial for tasks requiring object-based reasoning. Even when the CBM baseline is given equal concept capacity (reduced L1 regularization to match OCB's number of active concepts), OCB still wins on every dataset, ruling out the explanation that gains come solely from increased capacity. Against the Coarse-to-Fine CBM (C2F-CBM, Panousis et al. 2024), which uses hierarchical patch-based concepts, OCB (non-sparse) achieves 89.60 vs. 70.12 on SUN397 and 68.63 vs. 65.81 on COCOLogic, demonstrating that explicit object-centric representations outperform patch-based hierarchies. The non-interpretable CLIP+MLP upper bound remains ahead by roughly 3–4 points on most datasets, but OCB narrows this gap from ~7–9 points (vanilla CBM) to ~3–4 points while retaining full interpretability through its concept bottleneck.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Concept Bottleneck Models (CBMs) by Koh et al. (2020) and their recent pretrained-model instantiations (SpLiCE, CLIP-based CBMs) by replacing the single holistic image encoding with a structured set of object-level concept activations. It draws on the object-centric representation literature (slot attention, GENESIS-V2, DINOSAUR) and pretrained object proposal models (Mask R-CNN, SAM) to ground concepts in discrete objects rather than the full image. The key departure from prior CBM work is that all existing natural-image CBMs operate on image-level encodings; OCB is the first to explicitly combine image-level and object-level concept activations through a learned aggregation step, and the first to evaluate CBMs on multi-label classification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

OCB demonstrates that object-centric structure is not merely an interpretability nicety but a genuine performance driver for CBMs, closing a substantial gap toward opaque CLIP+MLP baselines while preserving a transparent linear predictor. The introduction of COCOLogic provides a controlled benchmark for logical visual reasoning (conjunctions, negations, counting) that prior CBM evaluations lacked, and the multi-label extension opens CBMs to a much broader range of practical tasks. Open questions include whether the aggregation trade-off (compactness vs. object traceability) can be resolved, how OCB's performance depends on the quality of the underlying pretrained detectors and concept extractors, and whether object-centric concept spaces truly mitigate the shortcut-learning concerns raised for VLM-supervised CBMs.
