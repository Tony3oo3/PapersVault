## Problem

Existing LLM unlearning methods combine forgetting and retention into a single scalarized loss via linear regularization, which cannot recover the full Pareto frontier, requires opaque task-specific tuning of the trade-off weight, and causes unstable optimization with disproportionate degradation of retained utility. The paper asks whether unlearning can be reformulated so that the user directly specifies a retention budget while the algorithm maximizes forgetting, without the multi-gradient overhead of full multi-objective methods.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255549/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a reference LLM fine-tuned on both forget and retain sets, a forget set of prompt-response pairs to erase, a retain set to preserve, and a constraint threshold ε (typically set as (1+α) times the reference model's retention cross-entropy). It outputs unlearned model parameters and a dual variable λ whose trajectory encodes the active forgetting-utility trade-off. The unusual aspect is that ε is a direct, interpretable budget on allowable retention degradation rather than an opaque regularization coefficient, and the algorithm operates entirely at the pre-softmax logit level without requiring log-softmax over the full vocabulary.

## Method

The paper formulates unlearning as an ε-constrained optimization problem: minimize a novel logit-margin flattening loss (which drives pre-softmax logits toward a constant, i.e., uniform after softmax) subject to a hard constraint that retention cross-entropy does not exceed ε. The logit-margin flattening loss is softmax-free, convex in the logits, and maintains non-vanishing gradients, and a proposition shows that bounding the logit margin directly upper-bounds the maximum softmax probability, giving explicit distributional control absent in prior methods. The constrained problem is solved by a warm-started primal-dual algorithm: an initial phase fully minimizes the Lagrangian for a fixed λ, then subsequent iterations alternate a single gradient descent step on model parameters with a dual ascent step on λ, which automatically increases the retention penalty when the constraint is violated and relaxes it when retention is within budget. This incurs no extra gradient computations beyond standard regularized unlearning, and the dual variable's dynamics make the trade-off transparent and tunable at deployment time.

## Results

On the TOFU benchmark (forget10/retain90), PDU achieves the highest Forget Success across all four model scales: 0.740 (Llama 3.2 1B), 0.914 (Llama 3.2 3B), 0.960 (Llama 3.1 8B), and 0.933 (Gemma 7B), while maintaining the highest or second-highest Model Utility. The Aggregated Success (harmonic mean of normalized metrics) reaches 0.880 on Llama 3.1 8B, exceeding both the retrained-from-scratch baseline (0.812) and the strongest unlearning baseline RMU (0.864). On MUSE-Books, PDU achieves near-complete forgetting (Forget ROUGE 0.001 on Llama 2 7B) while retaining competitive utility, and on MUSE-News it provides viable Pareto-optimal trade-off points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PDU consistently outperforms all five baselines on Forget Success across every model scale on TOFU, with the largest margins over SimNPO (e.g., 0.914 vs. 0.196 on Llama 3.2 3B) and DPO (0.960 vs. 0.596 on Llama 3.1 8B). GradDiff [50] is the most problematic baseline: on Llama 3.1 8B and Gemma 7B it produces a Forget Success of exactly 0 (the model abstains from generating any text on forget prompts), yielding NaN aggregated scores, and on MUSE-Books it destroys model utility entirely (Retain ROUGE 0.000). RMU [25] is the strongest competing method, achieving competitive utility and moderate forgetting (e.g., 0.900 Forget Success on 8B), but PDU still leads by 6 percentage points on Forget Success and 1.6 points on Aggregated Success. NPO [55] and SimNPO [15] both struggle: NPO achieves reasonable forgetting but at the cost of lower utility (0.652 vs. 0.725 on 8B), while SimNPO barely unlearns (Forget Success 0.481 on 8B) despite preserving utility. On MUSE-Books, PDU achieves the lowest Forget ROUGE (0.001) among non-degenerate methods, confirming near-complete removal of copyrighted content while retaining 0.413 Retain ROUGE.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant linear-scalarization approach to LLM unlearning (where a single λ balances forget and retain losses, as in GradDiff [50], NPO [55], SimNPO [15], and RMU [25]) by recasting the problem as an ε-constrained optimization, drawing on the multi-objective optimization literature [30, 4]. The logit-margin flattening loss is a novel softmax-free alternative to both negative cross-entropy [50] and entropy maximization [51] for the forgetting objective, while the primal-dual solver borrows structure from safe reinforcement learning [33] and constraint-aware fine-tuning [20]. The key conceptual departure is replacing the opaque regularization coefficient with an interpretable retention budget ε, and replacing entropy-based flattening with a convex, bounded logit-margin penalty that provably controls the maximum softmax probability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The constrained formulation gives practitioners a direct, interpretable knob (ε) for controlling how much utility degradation is acceptable, removing the need for task-specific λ tuning that plagues scalarized methods. The logit-margin flattening loss is practically attractive for large-vocabulary models since it avoids log-softmax and maintains non-vanishing gradients. However, the authors acknowledge that ε is sensitive and model/data-dependent, that fluency on the forget set degrades (the model abstains), and that resilience to relearning attacks and jailbreaks remains unstudied. Open questions include whether the framework extends cleanly to multi-constraint continual unlearning and whether the fluency cost can be mitigated via hybrid losses.
