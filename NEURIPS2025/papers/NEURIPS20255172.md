## Problem

Existing multi-view clustering methods apply uniform encoding and view-level weighting across all views, overlooking the fact that sparsity ratios vary substantially across views within the same dataset. This cross-view sparsity heterogeneity causes encoding discrepancies and semantic misalignment that degrade clustering performance, particularly under extreme sparsity imbalance (e.g., sparsity ratios spanning 0.0001 to 0.6644 in ALOI-100).

## Input / Output

The method consumes raw multi-view feature matrices where each view has its own dimensionality (e.g., 6 views of dimensions [1302, 48, 512, 100, 256, 210] for MSRCV1) and a pre-computed per-view sparsity ratio s_v used as prior knowledge. It produces a late-fused global representation Y_bar ∈ R^{N×F} (F=64) on which k-means with 100 initializations yields the final cluster assignments; the framework supports an arbitrary number of views without architectural modification.

## Method

SparseMVC comprises three interdependent modules. First, the Sparse Autoencoder with Adaptive Constraints (SAA) probes each view's sparsity ratio s_v and applies a ReLU-like scaling factor f(s_v) to the KL-divergence entropy-matching loss, so that sparse views receive stronger sparsity regularization while dense views degenerate to standard autoencoders. Second, the Correlation-Informed Sample Reweighting (CSR) module computes sample-level fusion weights via an attention-like mechanism that scores the correlation between early-fused global features Z̄ and each view-specific feature Z_v, enabling per-sample dynamic weighting rather than uniform view-level weighting. Third, the Cross-view Distribution Alignment (CDA) module performs contrastive learning between the late-fused global representation Ȳ and compressed view-specific features Y_v, using a temperature-scaled similarity matrix with positive (diagonal) and negative (off-diagonal) pairs to align distributions across views. Training proceeds in two phases—autoencoder pre-training followed by alignment training—minimizing a combined loss of reconstruction, adaptive sparsity, and contrastive alignment terms.

## Results

SparseMVC achieves state-of-the-art clustering performance across all 8 main benchmark datasets. On the large-scale ALOI-100 dataset (10,800 samples, 4 views with extreme sparsity variation from 0.0001 to 0.6383), it reaches 82.21% ACC and 92.65% NMI, outperforming the second-best SCMVC by 4.49 and 3.23 points respectively. On MSRCV1 (6 views), it attains 97.14% ACC and 94.22% NMI, surpassing SCMVC (90.95% ACC) by over 6 points. On the challenging Out-Scene dataset (2,688 samples, 4 views), it achieves 77.49% ACC, leading the runner-up CVCL (73.51%) by nearly 4 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SparseMVC outperforms all 12 baselines on every dataset and metric evaluated. The largest margins appear on datasets with extreme cross-view sparsity variation: on ALOI-100 (sparsity ratios spanning 0.0001–0.6383), it beats SCMVC by 4.49 ACC points (82.21 vs. 77.72) and GCFAgg by 8.10 points; on MSRCV1 (one view at 0.3478 sparsity vs. others near 0.005), it surpasses SCMVC by 6.19 ACC points (97.14 vs. 90.95) and SURE by 5.71 points. On Out-Scene, it leads CVCL (the previous best) by 3.98 ACC points. On smaller datasets, margins are tighter but consistent: on LGG it edges out DSMVC by 0.38 ACC (83.15 vs. 82.77), on Dermatology it beats SCMVC by 1.40 ACC (95.25 vs. 93.85), and on Synthetic3d it surpasses MVCAN by 0.16 ACC (98.33 vs. 98.17). Notably, several baselines that perform well on one dataset collapse on others (e.g., SURE drops to 10.13% ACC on ALOI-100, CPSPAN to 22.08% on Wikipedia), whereas SparseMVC maintains competitive or top performance uniformly across all settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the sparse autoencoder framework (Ng et al., 2011) by introducing view-adaptive sparsity constraints driven by a probed sparsity ratio, and combines it with a simplified multi-head attention mechanism for sample-level reweighting and a contrastive cross-view distribution alignment module. It departs from prior multi-view clustering methods (GCFAgg, SCMVC, MVCAN, etc.) that apply uniform encoding or view-level weighting, by explicitly modeling the heterogeneity of sparsity *across* views as a first-class design signal. The correlation-informed reweighting module is conceptually related to attention-based fusion in multi-view learning but operates at the sample level using global-to-local feature correlations rather than learned view importance scores.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and quantifies cross-view sparsity variation as a previously overlooked structural property of multi-view data, providing a principled adaptive encoding solution that degrades gracefully to standard autoencoders for dense views. However, the method requires the sparsity ratio as prior knowledge (computed from the input), the threshold θ=0.01 is dataset-dependent, and no error bars are reported across the 10 runs. Open questions include how the framework handles incomplete multi-view data where entire views are missing, whether the O(N²) contrastive loss scales to very large datasets, and whether the adaptive sparsity mechanism generalizes to settings where sparsity is not a zero-valued feature but rather a distributional property.
