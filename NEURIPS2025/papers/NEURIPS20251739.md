## Problem

Existing deep incomplete multi-view clustering methods rely on static fusion strategies or two-stage completion-then-clustering pipelines, leading to suboptimal cross-view fusion and error propagation. They lack a hierarchical separation between low-level consistency alignment and high-level semantic fusion, and fail to dynamically balance each view's contribution based on its distributional affinity with the fused representation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251739/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes V incomplete views (each an N×d_v matrix) along with a binary indicator matrix A ∈ {0,1}^{N×V} marking which views are available per sample. It produces k-means cluster assignments on the completed multi-view representations, where missing views are implicitly recovered by projecting aligned latent codes through cross-view inference MLPs (256-128-256 hidden layers) into high-dimensional semantic spaces. The autoencoders use fully connected layers (Input-1024-1024-1024-Output) and the entire pipeline is trained end-to-end without explicit interpolation or generative modeling of missing data.

## Method

HSACC introduces a dual-level semantic space: in the low-level space, mutual information between view latent representations is maximized via a feature-level joint/marginal distribution estimator to enforce consistency alignment; in the high-level space, adaptive view weights are computed by measuring MMD-based distributional affinity between each view and an initial uniform fusion R, then a weighted fusion produces a global representation H that is further aligned to each view via MMD minimization. Missing views are implicitly completed by cross-view inference MLPs that project one view's latent code to predict another view's latent code, supervised by an inference consistency loss. The four loss terms (reconstruction, inference consistency, mutual information, MMD alignment) are jointly optimized with trade-off coefficients, and the inference loss is introduced only after a warm-up epoch E₁ to stabilize early representations. Final clustering is performed by k-means on the concatenated completed view representations.

## Results

HSACC achieves the best ACC, NMI, and ARI across all five datasets at every missing rate (0.3, 0.5, 0.7). At the 0.5 missing rate, HSACC reaches 76.81% ACC / 73.15% NMI / 87.13% ARI on Caltech101-20, 92.55% ACC on Noisy MNIST, 95.57% ACC on Hdigit, 52.17% ACC on 100leaves, and 28.11% ACC on LandUse_21. The largest margin over the second-best method is on Caltech101-20 at 0.5 missing rate, where HSACC improves ACC by 5.3% and ARI by 8.57% over DCP. On Noisy MNIST, HSACC's accuracy drops only 6.92% as the missing rate increases from 0.3 to 0.7, compared to a 35.19% drop for ICMVC.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HSACC outperforms all nine baselines on every dataset and missing rate. The strongest competitor is DCP, which leads on Caltech101-20 (71.51% ACC at 0.5 missing) and Hdigit (94.10% ACC at 0.5), but HSACC surpasses it by 5.3% ACC and 8.57% ARI on Caltech101-20 and by 1.47% ACC on Hdigit. On Noisy MNIST, ICMVC and MCAC are the closest rivals at 0.3 missing rate (95.79% and 95.17% ACC vs. HSACC's 95.81%), yet HSACC maintains a clear lead at higher missing rates (92.55% at 0.5 vs. 86.84% for ICMVC). DSIMVC is competitive on Hdigit (94.40% at 0.5) but falls behind HSACC (95.57%) and degrades sharply on other datasets. MRL_CAL performs poorly across all datasets (e.g., 14.98% ACC on Noisy MNIST, 12.62% on Hdigit), indicating that its adversarial multi-level learning strategy is ill-suited to incomplete settings. On 100leaves, HSACC achieves 52.17% ACC at 0.5 missing, substantially ahead of ICMVC (51.39%) and all other methods, confirming its advantage on high-category-count data.

## Relation to Prior Work

HSACC sits at the intersection of autoencoder-based incomplete multi-view clustering and contrastive/mutual-information alignment, but departs from prior work in two key ways. First, it introduces a hierarchical semantic separation—maximizing mutual information at a low level for consistency and applying MMD-based distribution alignment with adaptive view weighting at a high level for fusion—whereas prior methods (e.g., COMP, DCP, DSIMVC) typically perform alignment and fusion in a single undifferentiated latent space. Second, it replaces the conventional two-stage pipeline (explicit completion followed by clustering) with a cooperative joint optimization where missing views are implicitly recovered via MLP projection into a high-dimensional semantic space, drawing on domain-adaptation-style MMD to guide both weighting and alignment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The hierarchical alignment-plus-dynamic-weighting design offers a principled way to handle the distributional heterogeneity across views that static or uniform fusion strategies ignore, and the joint optimization eliminates the error propagation inherent in two-stage IMVC pipelines. However, the evaluation is limited to relatively small datasets (≤10,000 samples), primarily two-view settings (with only a brief 5-view Mfeat experiment), and no statistical significance testing is reported, leaving open questions about scalability to large-scale or highly heterogeneous multi-view scenarios. The approach also assumes a linear-kernel MMD and a simple average for the initial fused representation, which may not generalize well to highly non-linear or asymmetric view distributions.
