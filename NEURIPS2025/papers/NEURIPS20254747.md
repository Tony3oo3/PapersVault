## Problem

Existing benchmarks for LLM-based agents evaluate either fixed-partner cooperation or fully competitive games, leaving the critical mixed-motive middle ground—where both cooperation and self-interest matter—unmeasured. There is no principled method to assess whether an agent's cooperative behavior generalizes to novel social contexts and unfamiliar partners, a gap that stands in stark contrast to supervised learning's explicit focus on generalization to unseen examples.

## Input / Output

Each agent receives natural-language observations from a Game Master that mediates all action attempts and resolves them into world events; the agent's policy is a scaffolding function wrapping one or more Gemma-2-9b-it API calls, with a strict per-step limit on LLM invocations to level the computational playing field. Outputs are multi-layered: per-scenario min-max normalized cooperative scores, Elo rankings across 26 submissions, latent ability profiles inferred via Measurement Layouts (Bayesian graphical models over tag/substrate/role demands), and LLM-generated qualitative summaries including Likert-scale trait ratings. The evaluation uses a two-phase veil-of-ignorance protocol where agents are first Elo-ranked on held-out scenarios, then the top agents enter a cross-play tournament.

## Method

The paper formalizes cooperative intelligence as a focal agent's ability to identify and exploit opportunities for mutual gain across diverse co-player populations and interaction substrates, modeled as n-player POSG\R environments. Five cooperation-eliciting text-based substrates (Reality Show, Pub Coordination, Haggling, Labor Collective Action, State Formation) are parameterized with resident and visitor population structures inspired by evolutionary game theory's robustness-to-mutation criterion, testing whether agents maintain cooperation under majority-cooperative norms or resist invasion by non-cooperative strategies. The contest enforces zero-shot generalization via a veil-of-ignorance protocol: agents are developed on public scenarios but evaluated on held-out ones with hidden agent backstories, preventing overfitting to specific contexts. Performance is assessed through a suite of complementary ranking methods (Elo, Iterative Maximal Lotteries, Copeland, Ranked Pairs) and analyzed via hierarchical beta-regression with LKJ-correlated priors over pre-registered capability tags, plus Measurement Layouts to infer per-agent latent abilities from the demand-score structure.

## Results

Across 25 submitted agents evaluated in the NeurIPS 2024 Concordia Contest, the average agent achieved a mean normalized score of 0.426 ± 0.005 (SE), indicating substantial room for improvement. The top-ranked agent (in2ai_megamind) earned an Elo of 1588.0, while the rational agent baseline scored 1477.0; only five agents significantly outperformed this baseline. Hierarchical beta-regression revealed that cooperative tags (persuasion, convention following, negotiation, discouraging antisocial behavior, coordination) each reduced average agent performance by 10–20 percentage points, confirming the benchmark successfully probes targeted cooperation capabilities. In the final six-agent cross-play tournament, taehun_cgcal emerged as the overall winner with Elo 1561.0, followed by fluffy_fluffyagent_v16submission (1538.0) and hgyun_loss_aversion_agent_v3_plus2 (1533.0).

## Baselines & Comparisons

As a benchmark paper, the Concordia Contest does not compare a single proposed method against external baselines; instead, it evaluates 25 submitted LLM-agent policies against a rational agent baseline and across multiple ranking methodologies. The rational agent (Elo 1477.0) serves as the primary reference: the majority of submitted agents performed at or below this level, with only five agents showing statistically significant improvement. Across four complementary ranking methods (Elo, Iterative Maximal Lotteries, Copeland, and Ranked Pairs), the top three agents (in2ai_megamind, fluffy_fluffyagent_v16submission, SSCT_super_agent) consistently ranked highest, though mid-rank positions shifted depending on the aggregation rule. Notably, taehun_cgcal, ranked 4th by Elo in the full evaluation, won the final six-agent cross-play tournament, illustrating that pairwise strategic interaction can reorder rankings relative to aggregate Elo. The Measurement Layouts analysis further showed that persuasion capability was the key latent factor distinguishing top performers from the rational baseline, while weak ability to discourage antisocial behavior characterized the lowest-scoring agents.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Melting Pot contest's evaluation philosophy—assessing cooperative generalization to novel co-players and scenarios from RL agents—to LLM-based agents operating in natural language. It leverages the Concordia simulation platform (Smith et al., 2024) as the substrate for multi-agent interaction, and draws on evolutionary game theory (resident/visitor population structures), social choice theory (Elo, Copeland, Ranked Pairs, IML for ranking), and Measurement Layouts for latent capability inference. The key conceptual departure from prior LLM social benchmarks (Sotopia, ChatArena, Light) is the explicit 'veil of ignorance' protocol that enforces zero-shot generalization across both scenarios and partner populations, rather than testing isolated social skills in fixed settings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic, principled benchmark for evaluating whether LLM agents can generalize cooperative behavior across diverse partners and mixed-motive contexts, filling a gap the authors identify between competitive benchmarks and fixed-partner cooperative evaluations. The finding that most agents perform at or below a rational-agent baseline, with persuasion and norm enforcement as the key differentiators, provides concrete targets for future agent design. Key limitations include reliance on a single model (Gemma-2-9b-it), text-only interaction, and only 14 held-out scenarios, leaving open whether stronger LLMs or multi-modal inputs would close the observed capability gaps. The framework is likely to become a standard reference for evaluating cooperative LLM agents as they are increasingly deployed in social decision-making contexts.
