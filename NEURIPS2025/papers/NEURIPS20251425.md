## Problem

Stateful sequence modeling architectures (Mamba, RWKV, Gated DeltaNet) are widely adopted for their efficiency over self-attention, yet it remains unclear whether they suffer from fundamental degradation in base capabilities beyond known specialized-task deficits. Existing mixed-domain pre-training evaluation settings mask these architectural differences, making it impossible to determine which design factors truly govern base capabilities and what principles should guide future architecture design.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251425/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Models consume text token sequences (up to 100k tokens, 32k-vocabulary Mixtral tokenizer) and produce next-token probability distributions. The evaluation framework is distinctive: models are pre-trained on only two domains (cc, c4 from SlimPajama) and tested on unseen domains (arxiv, github, stack), with OOD language modeling loss serving as the primary base-capability metric. The Top-1 Chunk Selection architecture additionally partitions KV sequences into fixed-size chunks (128 tokens), where each query attends to exactly one selected remote chunk plus the nearest local chunk.

## Method

The paper first establishes a limited-domain pre-training with out-of-distribution testing framework that reveals significant base capability gaps between attention-based and stateful architectures, invisible under standard mixed-domain evaluation. Through ablation studies on Mamba components and controlled experiments, it identifies three determinative architecture factors—full-sequence visibility, real relation calculation, and non-uniform distribution—and unifies them into the design principle that a sequence modeling architecture must possess full-sequence arbitrary selection capability. This principle is validated via an extremely simple Top-1 Element Selection architecture (selecting the single highest-probability attention element with a straight-through trick) and generalized to the more practical Top-1 Chunk Selection architecture, which selects one KV chunk per query via exact or approximate (mean-vector dot product) scoring. Custom Triton and CUDA GPU kernels are implemented for the chunk selection and remote-chunk attention operations, achieving pre-training speeds competitive with Mamba-2 while matching Transformer++ base capabilities at both 2k and 100k sequence lengths.

## Results

On few-shot learning (1.3B params, 100B tokens, seq len 2k), Top-1 Chunk (Exact) achieves the best average score of 50.72, slightly exceeding Transformer++ (50.43) and Mamba-2 (50.68), while Top-1 Element scores 49.30. On retrieval tasks, Top-1 Element dramatically outperforms stateful architectures: 68.23% SWDE accuracy vs. Mamba-1 (36.09%) and Mamba-2 (37.98%), and 26.95% FDA vs. Mamba-1 (6.44%) and Mamba-2 (17.60%). On 100k-token long-sequence retrieval (S-NIAH), Top-1 Chunk (Approx.) achieves 15.0% at 100k context, compared to Mamba-2 (0.80%) and RWKV-7 (0.20%), while maintaining 4.50× pre-training speed and 1.21× inference speed relative to Transformer++.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Transformer++ (the strongest baseline), Top-1 Chunk (Exact) achieves a marginal edge in few-shot average score (50.72 vs. 50.43) and matches it on OOD language modeling, confirming the design principle does not sacrifice base capabilities. Against stateful architectures (Mamba-1, Mamba-2, RWKV-5/6/7, DeltaNet, Gated DeltaNet), Top-1 architectures show a decisive advantage on retrieval: Top-1 Element scores 68.23% SWDE vs. 36.09% (Mamba-1) and 37.98% (Mamba-2), and 26.95% FDA vs. 6.44% and 17.60% respectively. On 100k-token S-NIAH retrieval, Top-1 Chunk (Approx.) reaches 15.0% at 100k context while Mamba-2 drops to 0.80% and RWKV-7 to 0.20%, a gap of over 15×. Against Transformer++ (Window=256), which restricts full-sequence visibility, Top-1 architectures substantially outperform on both OOD language modeling and retrieval (e.g., 68.23% vs. 12.96% SWDE), directly validating that full-sequence visibility is a critical design factor. In terms of efficiency, Top-1 Chunk (Approx.) achieves 4.50× pre-training speedup and 1.21× inference speedup over Transformer++ at 100k context, approaching Mamba-2's 5.57× pre-training speed while retaining far superior retrieval capability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of architecture analysis work that has identified specific capability deficits (retrieval, copy, associative recall) in stateful sequence modeling architectures like Mamba, RWKV, and Gated DeltaNet on synthetic tasks. It departs from prior work in two key ways: methodologically, it replaces the standard mixed-domain pre-training evaluation with a limited-domain + out-of-distribution testing framework that reveals architectural differences masked by in-distribution evaluation; and conceptually, it shifts focus from specialized capabilities to 'base capabilities' (OOD language modeling), identifying 'full-sequence arbitrary selection' as the unifying design principle. The proposed Top-1 Chunk Selection architecture is closely related to concurrent sparse attention works NSA and MoBA, which the authors acknowledge and distinguish from their primary contributions (Top-1 Element, Top-1 Chunk Exact, and the analytical framework).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 'full-sequence arbitrary selection' principle provides a concrete, testable design guideline for future sequence modeling architectures, suggesting that efficiency-oriented designs (stateful RNNs, linear attention) inherently sacrifice base capabilities unless they retain the ability to attend to any position in the full sequence. The limited-domain OOD evaluation framework is a reusable methodological tool that could expose architectural weaknesses hidden by standard benchmarks. Key limitations include the restriction to language modeling objectives and a single dataset family (SlimPajama), leaving open whether the principle generalizes to other pre-training paradigms or multimodal settings. The approximate variant (Top-1 Chunk Approx.) shows that even small deviations from exact selection degrade OOD generalization, a cautionary finding for the NSA/MoBA family of architectures.
