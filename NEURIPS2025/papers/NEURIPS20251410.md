## Problem

Systematic architectural comparison of language models is hindered at academic scale (1.3B parameters, 100B tokens) by noise, unreliable perplexity as a proxy for reasoning ability, and the failure of even 2-hop reasoning to emerge. The paper addresses this by introducing controlled synthetic pretraining tasks that isolate atomic capabilities (reasoning depth, breadth, knowledge capacity, manipulation, hierarchical structure) and by identifying a missing architectural component—horizontal information flow across neighboring tokens—that limits all major sequence-modeling families.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251410/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

Models consume token sequences of fixed context length (2048–4096 tokens) and produce next-token predictions, but the synthetic tasks impose structured formats: DEPO encodes directed permutations with k-hop successor queries, BREVO encodes DAGs requiring topological-order output, MANO encodes modular arithmetic expressions, and LANO encodes context-free-grammar sentences. Canon layers themselves operate on the hidden-state tensor, computing a weighted sum over a 4-token sliding window at each position, adding no new input/output tokens to the sequence.

## Method

The core architectural contribution is Canon layers: trainable 1D linear convolutions of kernel size 4 that compute weighted sums of neighboring token representations and are added as residual links at four insertion points—before attention (Canon-A), inside attention (Canon-B), before MLP (Canon-C), and inside MLP (Canon-D). This promotes horizontal information flow that standard Transformers, linear attention, and state-space models lack within a single layer. The paper pairs this with five synthetic pretraining tasks (DEPO, BREVO, CAPO, MANO, LANO) designed to isolate non-overlapping cognitive skills under controlled, infinite-data conditions. Across RoPE/NoPE Transformers, Gated Linear Attention, and Mamba2, full Canon layers (A/B/C/D) improve reasoning depth by 2–4×, double reasoning breadth, and lift weak architectures (NoPE, GLA) to match their stronger counterparts (RoPE, Mamba2), with minimal computational overhead.

## Results

Canon layers (trainable 1-D convolutions, kernel size 4) improve reasoning depth by 200–400%, reasoning breadth by 30%, and knowledge manipulation length by 30% across synthetic tasks (DEPO, BREVO, MANO) with minimal computational overhead. For linear attention (GLA), Canon layers elevate reasoning depth from 1-hop to 4-hop and double both reasoning breadth and knowledge manipulation length. In controlled comparisons with full Canon applied uniformly, full Transformers (RoPE) still achieve roughly 2× the reasoning depth of linear models (GLA, Mamba2) on hierarchical tasks, while Mamba2's built-in conv1d is identified as the source of its advantage over GLA (removing it drops Mamba2 to GLA level).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Llama(RoPE), Canon layers transform the weaker NoPE variant into a model that matches or surpasses RoPE+Canon on all five synthetic tasks, outperforming positional-embedding fixes like ALiBi and H-Alibi. Against GLA (Gated Linear Attention), Canon layers are transformative: they lift GLA from 1-hop to 4-hop reasoning depth on DEPO, double its BREVO breadth (surpassing Mamba2 on that task), and double its MANO manipulation length, making GLA+Canon competitive with Mamba2 and GDN. Against Mamba2, the paper reveals that Mamba2's advantage over GLA is attributable to its built-in conv1d (a non-linear Canon-B); removing conv1d collapses Mamba2 to GLA performance, while adding full Canon layers to Mamba2 further boosts results. Against GDN (Gated DeltaNet), Canon-equipped linear attention is described as rivaling this SOTA linear model. In the 1.3B-parameter / 100B-token real-world pretraining (Result 12), Canon layers consistently elevate NoPE to RoPE level and GLA to Mamba2 level, while all architectures still fail 2-hop reasoning, confirming the synthetic findings at academic scale.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 'Physics of Language Models' series (Parts 1–3), which established synthetic tasks for isolating specific LLM capabilities (hierarchical structure, arithmetic reasoning, knowledge storage). Canon layers build directly on the uniform-attention idea from Part 1 [6], but generalize it into a flexible, trainable 1D-convolutional component that can be inserted at multiple sublayer positions in any sequence architecture. The paper departs from prior architecture-comparison work by replacing noisy real-world pretraining with a controlled synthetic playground, and by reframing the design space around 'horizontal information flow' as a first-class architectural axis—positioning Canon layers as a complement to (or replacement for) positional encodings like RoPE, ALiBi, and H-Alibi, and as the key ingredient that Mamba2's conv1d already implicitly provides.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The synthetic playground addresses a genuine bottleneck in architecture research: at academic scale (1.3B params, 100B tokens), even 2-hop reasoning fails to emerge and 2–4% seed noise swamps architectural differences, making rigorous comparison nearly impossible. The finding that Mamba2's apparent SSM advantage is largely attributable to its conv1d acting as a non-linear Canon-B layer—rather than to the state-space recurrence itself—challenges a widely held narrative and redirects design attention toward horizontal mixing. Open questions include whether the 2× reasoning-depth gains and the linear-model depth ceiling persist at 7B+ scale (preliminary 1–8B results reportedly align but are not yet published), whether the five synthetic tasks adequately predict performance on open-ended real-world benchmarks, and whether hybrid architectures (sliding-window attention + linear backbone) will remain the practical path to deep reasoning until the compression-error bottleneck in linear models is resolved.
