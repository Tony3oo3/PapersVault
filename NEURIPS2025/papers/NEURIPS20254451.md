## Problem

Video Large Multimodal Models (VLMMs) suffer from mismatched generalization: safety alignment acquired from static modalities (text, images) fails to transfer to dynamic video contexts, causing models to generate harmful content when the same query is paired with a relevant video. Additionally, there is a critical lack of large-scale, video-specific safety preference datasets and scenario-driven benchmarks for evaluating VLMM safety in temporally rich settings.

## Input / Output

The alignment pipeline takes a video clip and an adversarial text query as input, producing a safety-aligned text response that either safely refuses or provides constructive guidance. The dataset construction stage consumes raw videos from InternVid-10M-FLT, converts them into detailed multi-model textual descriptions (synthesized from LLaVA-NeXT-Video, Qwen2.5-VL, and InternVL2.5 outputs, refined by GPT-4), and generates 350K (video, query, chosen_response, rejected_response) tuples. Evaluation outputs safety rates across seven harmful categories (Individual Harm, Data Protection, Civic Virtue, Toxicity, Discrimination, Illegal Activities, Extreme Threat) on both Base and Challenge benchmark sets.

## Method

SAFEVID is a closed-loop framework whose key insight is using detailed textual video descriptions as an interpretive bridge to transfer well-established textual safety reasoning into the video domain. Dataset construction proceeds in three stages: (1) scene-centric curation of 12,377 videos from InternVid-10M-FLT into 30 categories using GPT-4 classification and VideoCLIP centroid selection; (2) adversarial question generation via Gemini 2.0 Pro, guided by a hierarchical 3H (Helpful, Honest, Harmless) safety taxonomy with per-subcategory definitions; and (3) preference pair synthesis where rejected responses come from a vulnerable baseline (LLaVA-NeXT-Video) and chosen responses are generated by GPT-4 conditioned on the textual description, the question, the rejected response, and explicit safety guidelines emphasizing constructive refusal over simple rejection. Alignment is performed via DPO (β=0.1, 1 epoch, lr=1e-6, frozen vision tower) on the full 350K preference set. Evaluation uses SafeVidBench, comprising a 1,380-question Base set (automated, Harmless-dimension focused) and a 1,380-question Challenge set (human red-teamed with euphemisms, narrative masking, and temporal subtlety), both disjoint from training data.

## Results

On SafeVidBench-Base, LLaVA-NeXT-Video + SafeVid-350K achieves a 96.38% average Safety Rate (up from 53.99%, a +42.39 pp gain), while Qwen2.5-VL-7B + SafeVid-350K reaches 95.87% (up from 77.03%, +18.84 pp). On the harder SafeVidBench-Challenge set, the aligned LLaVA-NeXT-Video scores 85.94% (+39.71 pp) and Qwen2.5-VL-7B scores 89.78% (+30.00 pp). On OOD benchmarks, the aligned LLaVA-NeXT-Video reduces VLBreakBench attack success from 68.00% to 16.48% and improves MM-SafetyBench (SD+T) safety from 42.02% to 74.31%, while maintaining a 100% Helpful Rate on StrongReject.

## Baselines & Comparisons

After DPO alignment on SafeVid-350K, LLaVA-NeXT-Video (96.38% Base / 85.94% Challenge) surpasses all proprietary baselines on SafeVidBench-Base, exceeding Claude-3.5-sonnet (87.75%), Gemini-1.5-pro (89.49%), and GPT-4o (55.34%) by 8.6, 6.9, and 41.0 pp respectively. On the more adversarial Challenge set, Qwen2.5-VL-7B + SafeVid-350K (89.78%) edges out Claude-3.5-sonnet (88.91%) by 0.87 pp, while LLaVA-NeXT-Video + SafeVid-350K (85.94%) trails Claude by 3 pp. Among open-source VLMMs, the aligned models dominate: LLaVA-NeXT-Video + SafeVid-350K outperforms the next-best open-source model (Qwen2.5-VL-7B at 77.03% Base) by 19.35 pp, and Qwen2.5-VL-7B + SafeVid-350K (95.87%) beats the much larger Qwen2.5-VL-72B (71.23%) by 24.64 pp. On OOD benchmarks, the aligned LLaVA-NeXT-Video achieves a 100% Helpful Rate on StrongReject and 94.00% Safety on HarmEval, confirming that safety gains do not come at the cost of over-refusal on benign queries.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DPO-based preference alignment paradigm (Rafailov et al., 2023) from text and image safety alignment (e.g., BeaverTails, SPA-VL) to the video domain, addressing the 'mismatched generalization' gap where static-modality safety training fails to transfer to dynamic video contexts. Its key conceptual departure is using detailed textual video descriptions as an interpretive bridge, enabling established LLM-based safety reasoning (grounded in the 3H framework from Constitutional AI) to generate video-specific safety data without requiring human annotation of video responses. The construction pipeline draws on scene-centric video classification (inspired by MovieCLIP and holistic video understanding work) and multi-model description synthesis, but the alignment mechanism itself is standard DPO applied to a novel dataset.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SAFEVID provides the first large-scale (350K pairs) video-specific safety preference dataset and the first dedicated video safety benchmark (SafeVidBench), filling a clear resource gap in the VLMM safety literature. The demonstrated OOD generalization—where video safety alignment also improves image and text safety scores—suggests that video-centric safety principles are not narrow but reinforce broader safety reasoning. However, the reliance on textual descriptions as a proxy for video content means highly subtle visual-temporal safety cues may still be missed, and the safety taxonomy's coverage of rapidly evolving misuse patterns remains an open question. Future work could explore whether direct video-based safety reasoning (bypassing the text bridge) or iterative taxonomy expansion would close these gaps.
