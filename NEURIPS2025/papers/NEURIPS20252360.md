## Problem

Most deep learning deblurring methods operate on sRGB images, which have already suffered irreversible information loss from the ISP pipeline (dynamic range compression, nonlinear tone mapping). RAW sensor data preserves linearity and full spectral information, offering superior restoration potential, yet RAW-to-RAW deblurring remains underexplored—particularly in effectively handling frequency-dependent blur patterns while maintaining computational efficiency without resorting to expensive Transformer architectures.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252360/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For RAW deblurring, the input is a 4-channel packed Bayer-pattern image (128×128 patches during training) after black-level subtraction and normalization to [0,1]; for sRGB deblurring, the input is a standard 3-channel image (256×256 patches). The network produces a deblurred image of the same channel count and spatial resolution. Full-resolution inference uses a sliding-window strategy with 50% overlap, and the only architectural change between RAW and sRGB modes is the input channel count (C_in=4 vs. C_in=3).

## Method

FrENet is a U-Net with L encoder/decoder scales whose core processing unit, the FrE-Block, operates in the frequency domain via FFT/IFFT. Each FrE-Block contains a Frequency Adaptive Context Module (FACM) with two parallel branches: a Local branch using the proposed Adaptive Frequency Positional Modulation (AFPM), which divides the frequency feature map into an 8×8 grid of patches and generates position-aware kernels and biases via lightweight Kernel-Bias Generators (2-layer MLPs) conditioned on each patch's Euclidean distance from the spectral center, and a Global branch using Simplified Channel Attention (SCA) from NAFNet. Frequency-domain skip connections complement standard spatial skip connections to preserve high-frequency details across scales. The architecture is a compact CNN (no self-attention), trained with L1 loss plus a weighted FFT-domain reconstruction loss, achieving state-of-the-art RAW deblurring (45.63 dB PSNR) with 75% fewer MACs than LoFormer-L.

## Results

On the Deblur-RAW dataset, FrENet+ achieves state-of-the-art PSNR of 45.63 dB and SSIM of 0.994, while the lighter FrENet variant reaches 44.73 dB PSNR with only 2.22G MACs — 75% fewer than LoFormer-L (8.98G). On real-world sRGB deblurring, FrENet+ sets new records on RealBlur-R (40.74 dB PSNR) and RealBlur-J (33.87 dB PSNR), outperforming the strongest baseline LoFormer-L by 0.14 dB and 0.99 dB respectively. FrENet also demonstrates that deblurring in the RAW domain before ISP conversion yields a 4.73 dB PSNR advantage over the equivalent sRGB-domain pipeline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FrENet outperforms all six baselines on Deblur-RAW, with the most notable margin being +0.69 dB PSNR over LoFormer-L (44.73 vs. 44.04) while using 75% fewer MACs (2.22G vs. 8.98G). FrENet+ extends this lead to +1.59 dB over LoFormer-L (45.63 vs. 44.04) and +1.67 dB over FFTFormer (45.63 vs. 43.96). On sRGB deblurring, FrENet+ surpasses LoFormer-L on both real-world benchmarks — RealBlur-R by 0.14 dB (40.74 vs. 40.60) and RealBlur-J by 0.99 dB (33.87 vs. 32.88) — while matching or slightly exceeding it on synthetic GoPro (34.11 vs. 34.09) and HIDE (31.92 vs. 31.86). Against CNN-based baselines, FrENet gains +1.87 dB over Restormer and +4.38 dB over NAFNet64 on Deblur-RAW, confirming that frequency-domain processing provides a substantial advantage over purely spatial architectures. The efficiency comparison (Table C.1) further shows FrENet+ runs 2.9× faster (75 ms vs. 222 ms) and uses 55% less GPU memory (1083 MB vs. 2391 MB) than LoFormer-L on full-resolution RAW images.

## Relation to Prior Work

FrENet builds on the lightweight CNN backbone philosophy of NAFNet (adopting its SCA mechanism and overall U-Net efficiency) and the Gated-Dconv FFN from Restormer, but departs sharply from the Transformer-based frequency-domain deblurring line (FFTFormer, LoFormer) by replacing attention with a compact convolution-Fourier hybrid. It extends the RAW-to-RAW deblurring trajectory initiated by Liang et al. and ELMformer by introducing explicit, adaptive frequency-domain processing (the AFPM module with position-conditioned Kernel-Bias Generators) and frequency-domain skip connections, which prior RAW deblurring methods lacked. The paper positions itself as filling the gap between spatially-oriented RAW restoration and the frequency-aware but computationally heavy Transformer approaches designed for sRGB.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper quantifies a 4.73 dB advantage for pre-ISP (RAW-domain) deblurring over post-ISP (sRGB-domain) deblurring, reinforcing the practical argument for operating on RAW data before irreversible ISP losses. Its 75% MAC reduction over LoFormer-L while achieving higher PSNR offers a compelling efficiency-quality tradeoff for deployment-constrained settings. The cross-domain transferability—achieving state-of-the-art sRGB results without architectural modification—suggests the adaptive frequency modulation principle may generalize to other RAW restoration tasks (denoising, super-resolution). Open questions include whether the simplified Euclidean-distance positional encoding in AFPM can capture more complex spectral dependencies, and how FFT/IFFT costs scale for very high-resolution RAW sensors.
