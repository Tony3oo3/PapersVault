## Problem

Single-image 3D clothed human reconstruction remains difficult because 2D multiview generative models produce high-fidelity details but suffer from cross-view inconsistency when conditioned on inaccurate SMPL meshes, while native 3D generative models yield structurally consistent but coarse, low-fidelity shapes. No prior work unifies these two complementary paradigms into a single generation framework that simultaneously benefits from 2D detail and 3D structural integrity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253989/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image of a full-body clothed person as input and produces a detailed 3D textured human mesh as the final output. Intermediate representations include four orthographic multiview color and normal maps (front, back, left, right) and a sparse 3D voxel grid, all generated jointly. Training images are rendered at 768×768 resolution from 8 evenly distributed azimuth angles, and the final texture is obtained by rendering from a 3D Gaussian Splatting representation and baking onto the mesh.

## Method

SyncHuman introduces a 2D-3D Cross-Space Generative Model that jointly fine-tunes a 2D multiview diffusion branch (PSHuman/SD2.1) and a 3D native generation branch (Trellis) via a bidirectional 2D-3D synchronization attention: 2D-to-3D attention projects each 3D voxel feature onto four view planes to cross-attend to corresponding 2D features, while 3D-to-2D attention projects each 2D pixel feature into 3D space to attend to a column of voxel features, with zero-initialized output MLPs for stable training. Both branches are trained jointly under a flow matching objective. A second component, the Multiview Guided Decoder (MVGD), extracts multi-level DINOv2 features from the generated multiview color and normal maps, concatenates them with the structured latents at each voxel position, and feeds the enriched representation into the original Trellis mesh/GS decoder, carving fine geometric detail and improving texture fidelity without requiring per-case optimization.

## Results

On X-Humans, SyncHuman achieves the best geometry (Chamfer 0.835, P2S 0.759, NC 0.887) and appearance (PSNR 21.84, SSIM 0.874, LPIPS 0.079) across all evaluated metrics, outperforming the closest 2D multiview baseline PSHuman by ~42% in Chamfer distance and ~4.8 dB in PSNR. On CAPE-NFP (non-frontal poses), the method reduces Chamfer distance from 1.373 (PSHuman) to 0.913 and P2S from 0.986 to 0.811. Compared to the native 3D model Trellis, SyncHuman improves Chamfer by 58% and PSNR by 27% on X-Humans, demonstrating that the 2D-3D synchronization yields both structural accuracy and visual fidelity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SyncHuman outperforms all 11 baselines on every reported metric across all three test sets. Against the strongest 2D multiview baseline PSHuman, it reduces Chamfer distance by 42% (0.835 vs 1.438) and P2S by 33% (0.759 vs 1.139) on X-Humans while improving PSNR by ~1 dB (21.84 vs 20.84). Against the native 3D model Trellis, the gains are even larger: 58% lower Chamfer (0.835 vs 2.004) and 27% higher PSNR (21.84 vs 17.08), confirming that 2D detail injection is critical for fidelity. Among implicit-function baselines (ICON, ECON, GTA, SIFU), SyncHuman achieves 40–50% lower Chamfer distance and 1.5–2 dB higher PSNR. The generative-prior methods SiTH and Human3Diff show competitive geometry on CAPE-FP but lag significantly in appearance quality (PSNR 19.7–19.8 vs 21.8). Qualitative comparisons with Gaussians-based methods (LHM, IDOL) and the large-scale native 3D model Hunyuan3D 2.5 further confirm that SyncHuman produces higher-fidelity textured meshes, particularly in clothing wrinkles and facial details.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate paradigms for single-view human reconstruction: 2D multiview diffusion (PSHuman, SiTH, Human3Diff) and native 3D generative models (Trellis, Hunyuan3D). Prior multiview approaches relied on SMPL mesh estimation to enforce structural consistency, while native 3D generators produced structurally correct but detail-poor shapes. SyncHuman departs from the SMPL-dependent pipeline by using the native 3D model's sparse voxel output as structural guidance for the 2D branch, and vice versa, connected through a novel bidirectional 2D-3D synchronization attention that establishes explicit pixel-to-voxel correspondences during joint flow-matching training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work demonstrates that 2D and 3D generative models are complementary rather than competing, and that their joint training with explicit geometric alignment can outperform either alone on both geometry and appearance metrics. This opens a path toward 3D generation frameworks that do not require parametric body priors (SMPL), which is important for handling diverse clothing topologies and challenging poses. Key limitations include reliance on only ~5,000 training scans, uniform-lighting assumptions that cause texture artifacts under non-uniform illumination, and occasional mesh holes from the FlexiCube decoder. Scaling the multiview branch with video generative models or large-scale multiview human datasets is identified as the most promising next step.
