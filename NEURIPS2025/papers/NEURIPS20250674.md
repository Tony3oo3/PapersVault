## Problem

Diffusion models for medical image generation frequently produce clinically inaccurate images, and existing feedback-driven alignment methods (RLHF, DPO) require either expensive reward model training or labor-intensive expert evaluations at scale. This paper asks whether the visual reasoning capabilities of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) can substitute for direct human evaluation, translating expert-defined clinical criteria into actionable feedback that guides diffusion model fine-tuning with minimal expert involvement.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250674/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes a real source skin disease image, a text prompt specifying a different target condition (with a Textual Inversion–learned embedding), and a dermatologist-designed 5-criteria checklist (Location, Lesion Type, Shape/Size, Color, Texture). The I2I diffusion model starts denoising from a partially noised version of the source image (denoise strength γ=0.3) rather than pure Gaussian noise, preserving anatomical context while transforming lesion features. The MLLM evaluator receives only synthetic images (never real patient data) and returns a 5-dimensional binary score list per image; pairs of scores are aggregated into winner/loser labels for DPO or scalar rewards for RFT. Downstream, the synthetic images are mixed with real data at a controlled ratio ρ=0.2 to train classifiers that output 20-way skin disease labels.

## Method

MAGIC is a semi-automated framework that offloads visual evaluation to an MLLM (GPT-4o or MedGemma-4B) constrained to verify images against structured, attribute-level clinical checklists rather than performing open-ended medical reasoning. Preliminary fine-tuning uses Textual Inversion to learn disease-specific text tokens and LoRA (rank 32) to tie those tokens to visual features in the UNet. The core alignment step generates image pairs via the I2I pipeline, scores each image independently with the MLLM, and then fine-tunes the diffusion model via either reward-weighted likelihood (RFT) or Direct Preference Optimization (DPO). The DPO variant employs a multi-segment loss that decomposes each denoising trajectory into t′=γT sub-segments, assigning uniform reward (+1/−1) to all state-action pairs along the winning/losing path, thereby amplifying data utilization by a factor of t′. The key design principle is task-centric alignment: rather than adapting MLLMs to niche medical tasks, the framework decomposes domain knowledge into simple, visually verifiable checklist items that general-purpose MLLMs can reliably assess, making the system model-agnostic and scalable as MLLM capabilities improve.

## Results

On the 20-condition Fitzpatrick17k subset, MAGIC-DPO boosts ResNet18 accuracy from 29.31% (real-only) to 38.33% (+9.02%) and DINOv2 accuracy from 49.89% to 55.01% (+5.12%), with corresponding gains in F1, precision, and recall. In the few-shot setting (310 training images), MAGIC-DPO improves DINOv2 accuracy by +10.94% (26.45%→37.39%), and the augmented variant MAGIC-A further reaches 40.34% (+13.89%). Expert evaluation confirms the quality gap: 55.5% of MAGIC-DPO images satisfy 3+ clinical checklist criteria, versus only 18.5% for the I2I baseline and <3% for T2I.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares four augmentation strategies: standard Textual Inversion + LoRA fine-tuning with T2I generation, the same model with I2I generation, and two MAGIC variants (RFT and DPO). T2I augmentation actively harms performance (−3.74% ResNet18, −2.16% DINOv2), confirming that unguided diffusion synthesis introduces clinically inaccurate images. I2I provides modest gains (+2.14% ResNet18, +0.82% DINOv2) by preserving anatomical context but still lacks expert clinical guidance. MAGIC-RFT improves over I2I by roughly 2% (ResNet18) but remains limited by the intermediate reward model. MAGIC-DPO dominates all baselines, achieving the largest absolute gains (+9.02% ResNet18, +5.12% DINOv2) and the highest expert checklist satisfaction rate (55.5% of images meeting 3+ criteria vs. 18.5% for I2I). The advantage of DPO over RFT is attributed to bypassing the reward model, which is more robust when feedback pairs are limited as in medical domains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) diffusion-model-based augmentation for skin disease classification (Wang et al. 2024, Sagers et al. 2022/2023), which established that fine-tuned DMs can generate clinically relevant images but lacked expert guidance during training; (2) feedback-driven DM alignment, including reward-weighted fine-tuning (Lee et al. 2023, Sun et al. 2023) and Direct Preference Optimization for diffusion models (Yang et al. 2023, Wallace et al. 2024); and (3) MLLMs-as-a-judge for visual evaluation. The key departure is replacing direct human evaluation with MLLM-based checklist scoring, where experts design structured attribute-level criteria (location, lesion type, shape/size, color, texture) and GPT-4o performs the per-image binary assessment. The I2I generation strategy, inherited from Wang et al. 2024, is retained to preserve anatomical context and enable factorized lesion transformation, distinguishing this from pure T2I approaches that the paper shows actually degrade downstream performance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for medical imaging where data scarcity and privacy constraints limit training data, and the AI-Expert collaboration paradigm offers a scalable template for other specialized domains: decompose domain expertise into verifiable checklists, let a general-purpose MLLM perform the evaluation, and use the resulting preferences to fine-tune a generative model. The 'task-centric alignment' framing—adapting the task to MLLM strengths rather than fine-tuning MLLMs for niche tasks—is a broadly applicable design principle, especially given that the most capable MLLMs are proprietary. Open questions include whether this paradigm transfers to other medical imaging modalities (radiology, pathology), whether formal privacy guarantees can be established for the I2I transformation, and how the framework behaves under continual learning as new conditions emerge. The dependence on checklist quality (structured vs. coarse yields a 5.5-point gap) highlights that expert knowledge curation remains a bottleneck, even if the evaluation labor is offloaded.
