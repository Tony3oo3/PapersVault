## Problem

Existing diffusion-model watermarking methods either embed watermarks in the initial noise seed (restricting them to the server scenario and producing visually inconsistent images) or apply post-processing (restricting them to the user scenario and making them fragile to attacks). No prior method simultaneously achieves high generation consistency, strong robustness, and flexibility across both server-side and user-side deployment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255147/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

In the server scenario, the method takes a random Gaussian noise seed x_T and a text prompt c, and outputs a watermarked image x_0^W generated via CFG-guided DDIM sampling. In the user scenario, it takes an already-generated image x_0, applies DDIM inversion to recover the latent at timestep t*=0.3T, injects the watermark, and re-samples to produce x_0^W. For detection, a potentially attacked image is inverted back to t* and a p-value score η is computed by comparing the masked DFT coefficients against the known watermark key W (a set of concentric rings with Gaussian-drawn values). All operations occur in the 4-channel latent space of Stable Diffusion at 512×512 resolution.

## Method

Shallow Diffuse exploits the empirically observed low-rankness of the posterior mean predictor's Jacobian J_{θ,t} at intermediate timesteps (t ∈ [0.2T, 0.7T]) to decouple watermarking from image generation. Rather than perturbing the initial noise, the watermark λΔx is injected at a chosen timestep t*=0.3T, where the low rank r_t* ≪ d ensures that most of the perturbation lies in the null space of J_{θ,t*}, leaving the PMP prediction (and hence the generated image content) nearly unchanged. The watermark itself is constructed in the frequency domain via DFT: a circular mask M targets high-frequency components, and a ring-shaped key W with Gaussian-drawn values per ring encodes the identity. Because the watermark only affects the stochastic (noise) term of the DDIM update while the deterministic PMP term remains intact, the method is training-free, requires no access to the initial seed, and provides theoretical bounds on both consistency (Theorem 1: distortion ≤ λLh(r_t)) and detectability (Theorem 2: one-step DDIM inversion recovers the watermark with high probability).

## Results

Under the server scenario (Table 1), Shallow Diffuse achieves PSNR 35.49, SSIM 0.96, and LPIPS 0.02, substantially outperforming Tree-Ring (PSNR 16.61), RingID (PSNR 14.27), and Gaussian Shading (PSNR 11.04) in generation consistency while matching their average robustness (TPR@1%FPR = 0.93). Under the user scenario on COCO (Table 2), Shallow Diffuse reaches PSNR 32.11 and SSIM 0.84 with 0.93 average TPR@1%FPR, compared to RingID's PSNR 12.21 and Gaussian Shading's PSNR 10.17. The method also achieves the best FID (25.60) and CLIP-Score (0.3669) among all diffusion-based watermarking methods in the server scenario.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Shallow Diffuse dominates all diffusion-based baselines (Tree-Ring, RingID, Gaussian Shading) on consistency by a wide margin: PSNR 35.49 vs. 16.61 (Tree-Ring), 14.27 (RingID), and 11.04 (Gaussian Shading), while matching their average robustness at 0.93 TPR@1%FPR. Against non-diffusion baselines, Shallow Diffuse trades a small consistency gap (PSNR 35.49 vs. 40.45 for RivaGAN) for dramatically better robustness (0.93 vs. 0.54 average TPR@1%FPR), and similarly outperforms Stegastamp on robustness (0.93 vs. 0.66) at a modest PSNR cost (35.49 vs. 26.70). In the user scenario on COCO, Shallow Diffuse (PSNR 32.11, avg TPR 0.93) is the only method that simultaneously achieves high consistency and high robustness; RingID and Gaussian Shading reach 0.96 and 0.92 average TPR respectively but at PSNR 12.21 and 10.17, making them visually destructive. Against ROBIN (Appendix F), Shallow Diffuse achieves ~11 dB higher PSNR (35.49 vs. 24.61) and better FID (129.2 vs. 134.8), while ROBIN retains an edge on geometric attacks (rotation AUC 1.0 vs. 0.69) and adversarial attacks (GA AUC 0.90 vs. 0.78). Overall, Shallow Diffuse is the only method that balances strong generation consistency with high watermark robustness across both server and user scenarios without requiring model retraining.

## Relation to Prior Work

Shallow Diffuse builds on the frequency-domain ring watermark design from Tree-Ring Watermarks [29] but fundamentally departs from the strategy of embedding watermarks in the initial Gaussian noise. Instead, it exploits the empirically observed low-rank Jacobian of the posterior mean predictor (PMP) in diffusion models [33, 34], injecting the watermark at an intermediate timestep where a large null space exists, thereby decoupling watermarking from the sampling process. This contrasts with Tree-Ring, RingID, and Gaussian Shading, which tightly couple watermarking to the initial seed and distort the noise distribution, and with ROBIN [32], which uses adversarial optimization at an intermediate step. The method is entirely training-free, unlike ROBIN and earlier deep-learning-based watermarkers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Shallow Diffuse is the first training-free watermarking method that supports both server-side and user-side scenarios with provable consistency and detectability guarantees, making it practical for deployment without model retraining. Its main limitation is weaker adversarial and geometric robustness compared to optimization-based or low-frequency-embedding methods (RingID, Gaussian Shading, ROBIN), and multi-key watermarking degrades beyond ~16 keys. Open questions include scaling to hundreds of simultaneous users, extending the low-rank Jacobian analysis to latent-space models more rigorously, and whether the null-space embedding principle generalizes to video or audio diffusion models.
