## Problem

Existing action tokenizers for imitation learning either require training separate encoder-decoder networks (vector quantization), produce variable-length token sequences that prevent efficient parallel decoding (byte-pair encoding), or discretize single-step actions into bins that fail to capture temporal structure and produce jerky transitions between action chunks. BEAST addresses all three limitations simultaneously by representing action sequences as B-spline curves whose control points serve as fixed-length tokens requiring no separate tokenizer training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255755/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes multi-view RGB images, a free-form language instruction, and the robot's proprioceptive state as input. Actions are first normalized via 1st/99th quantile mapping to [-1,1], then encoded into N B-spline control points per DoF via closed-form ridge regression. For discrete tokens, the control-point matrix (D×N) is flattened by interleaving DoF dimensions and uniformly quantized to [0,255], yielding a fixed-length token sequence of length D×N. For continuous tokens, the unquantized control points are predicted directly. The B-spline decoder then maps control points back to a full T-step action sequence.

## Method

BEAST replaces learned tokenizers with a parametric B-spline representation: given a normalized action sequence of length T, it solves a least-squares problem to find N control points (N ≤ T) that approximate the trajectory, using precomputed clamped uniform B-spline basis functions. The clamped formulation guarantees the curve starts at the first and ends at the last control point, and by setting the first control point of each new chunk to the last action of the previous chunk, BEAST enforces mathematically guaranteed C0 continuity across replanning boundaries without temporal ensembling. The fixed-length token output (D×N tokens regardless of T) enables parallel decoding via bidirectional attention in a single forward pass, achieving 4–8× fewer tokens than binning and ~20× fewer inference steps than single-step prediction. BEAST integrates with three architectures: a decoder-only transformer with discrete tokens (BEAST-D), Florence-2 VLM with parallel decoding (BEAST-F), and ACT's CVAE with continuous tokens (BEAST-ACT), demonstrating compatibility across discrete and continuous prediction objectives.

## Results

On the CALVIN benchmark, BEAST-F achieves state-of-the-art performance with 99.8% success on single instructions and 74.4% on 5-instruction chains (ABC→D), and 98.1%/84.8% (ABCD→D), outperforming all baselines including MoDE (63.5%/75.0%) and VPP (75.0%). On LIBERO, BEAST-F achieves 92.5% average success rate, second only to π₀ (94.2%), and leads on the most challenging LIBERO-Long split (86.4% vs. 85.2% for π₀). Inference throughput reaches 617.3 Hz with 19 ms latency, making BEAST-F 2.14× faster than π₀, 4.72× faster than Diffusion Policy, and 101.4× faster than OpenVLA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BEAST-F establishes a new state of the art on CALVIN, outperforming the strongest diffusion-based baselines (MoDE: 63.5%→74.4% on 5-instruction chains; VPP: 75.0%→74.4% on ABC→D but 84.8% vs. 77.9% on ABCD→D) and the VLA baseline OpenVLA (43.5%→74.4% on 5-instruction chains). On LIBERO, BEAST-F (92.5% avg) trails π₀ (94.2%) by 1.7 points but surpasses π₀-FAST (85.5%) by 7 points and OpenVLA (76.5%) by 16 points; notably, BEAST-F leads on LIBERO-Long (86.4% vs. 85.2% for π₀). In inference speed, BEAST-F at 617.3 Hz is 2.14× faster than π₀ (288.11 Hz), 4.72× faster than Diffusion Policy (130.67 Hz), and 101.4× faster than OpenVLA (6.09 Hz). On the ALOHA bimanual benchmark, BEAST-ACT outperforms both vanilla ACT and π₀, and on real-world Franka tasks BEAST-D (76.57%) surpasses π₀ (53.43%) and π₀-FAST (28.5%). In the unified tokenizer comparison (same Florence-2 backbone), BEAST with parallel decoding outperforms both FAST and binning tokenizers on both LIBERO-Long and CALVIN ABC→D.

## Relation to Prior Work

BEAST sits at the intersection of two research threads: action chunking for imitation learning (ACT, Diffusion Policy) and movement primitives (DMPs, B-splines) from classical robotics. It departs from VQ-based tokenizers (VQ-BeT, Behavior Transformers) by eliminating the need for a separately trained encoder-decoder, and from FAST (DCT + BPE) by producing fixed-length token sequences that enable parallel decoding rather than variable-length autoregressive generation. The parallel decoding strategy is borrowed from OpenVLA-OFT, while the smooth-transition guarantee via clamped B-splines draws on the movement primitives literature but is applied here for the first time in a discrete/continuous token prediction framework integrated with VLMs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

BEAST offers a practical, training-free tokenizer that simultaneously achieves high compression (5 tokens per 100-step sequence), mathematically guaranteed smooth inter-chunk transitions, and 101× faster inference than autoregressive VLAs like OpenVLA. The finding that a 0.77B model without robot-data pretraining can match or exceed 3.3B–7B pretrained models on CALVIN and LIBERO suggests that efficient action representation may matter as much as model scale for imitation learning. Open questions include sensitivity to the number of basis functions (acknowledged limitation), whether the approach scales to large-scale multi-embodiment pretraining, and how it interacts with flow-matching or diffusion objectives (preliminary results are promising but not yet fully explored).
