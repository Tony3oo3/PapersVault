## Problem

State-of-the-art time series diffusion generators (e.g., DiffusionTS) operate directly in data space rather than a homogeneous latent space, making existing latent-space watermarking methods incompatible. Moreover, multivariate time series exhibit temporal dependencies and strong feature heterogeneity (e.g., categorical vs. continuous variates), which cause non-uniform reconstruction errors during diffusion inversion and break assumptions made by tabular or image watermarking schemes. No generation-time watermarking algorithm existed for multivariate time series diffusion models.

## Input / Output

The method takes a multivariate time series of W timesteps and F features along with a cryptographic key κ, and produces a watermarked time series sample x₀ generated via a BDIA-DDIM diffusion process. During detection, the watermarked sample is inverted back to an estimated initial noise x̂_T, from which discrete watermark seeds are recovered and verified against the expected temporal chained-hash, yielding a bit-accuracy score, Z-score, and TPR@X%FPR. The watermark is embedded purely through the sign assignment of Gaussian noise values (seed 1 → positive, seed 0 → negative), so no explicit perturbation is added to the generated time series.

## Method

TimeWak embeds a temporal chained-hashing watermark directly in the data-space diffusion process. Watermark seeds are sampled uniformly at the start of each interval of length H, then recursively propagated across timesteps within the interval via a key-dependent hash H(κ, w, s^{w-1}), preserving temporal coherence; seeds are then permuted across features using key-dependent shuffles to handle feature heterogeneity. These seeds determine the sign of the initial Gaussian noise through x_T^{w,f} = Φ⁻¹((u + s^{w,f})/L), which is denoised by a BDIA variant of DDIM to produce the watermarked time series. For detection, TimeWak introduces an ε-exact inversion that adapts BDIA by approximating x₁ ≈ x₀, with a theoretical error bound (Theorem 3.1) proving the propagated inversion error remains bounded by |ε| times a product of per-step sensitivity constants. The recovered noise is mapped back to discrete seeds, unshuffled, and verified against the expected hash chain to compute bit accuracy and statistical significance.

## Results

TimeWak achieves up to 61.96% better Context-FID score on MuJoCo and 8.44% better correlational score on fMRI compared to the strongest baseline (TabWak^T), while maintaining the highest watermark detectability (Z-score) across all five datasets. On 64-length sequences, TimeWak reaches a perfect TPR of 1.0 at 0.1% FPR in all settings, requiring only 1–2 samples for detection. Under six post-editing attack configurations (5%/30% offset, random cropping, min-max insertion), TimeWak consistently achieves the best or second-best Z-scores, outperforming all baselines on most datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest baseline TabWak^T (a temporal-axis adaptation of TabWak), TimeWak achieves 61.96% lower Context-FID on MuJoCo and 8.44% higher correlational score on fMRI, while matching or exceeding its Z-score on detectability across all datasets. TabWak (original, feature-wise) shows significant degradation under post-editing attacks, particularly on MuJoCo and Energy where detection frequently fails (negative Z-scores). Gaussian Shading (GS) demonstrates strong robustness under attacks, maintaining detectability in nearly all settings, but produces low-quality synthetic samples with substantially higher Context-FID scores. Tree-Ring (TR) performs poorly on both quality and detectability for time series, as its Fourier-domain ring pattern is designed for spatial (image) data. HTW (Heads-Tails Watermark) occasionally matches or beats un-watermarked quality scores but yields very low Z-scores, indicating unreliable watermark detection.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the generation-time watermarking paradigm established for images (Gaussian Shading, Tree-Ring) and tables (TabWak) to multivariate time series, a modality where SOTA diffusion generators (DiffusionTS, CSDI) operate directly in data space rather than latent space, rendering existing latent-based watermarking incompatible. The key methodological departure is the temporal chained-hashing mechanism that propagates watermark seeds along the temporal axis to respect inter-timestep correlations, combined with a feature-wise shuffle to handle heterogeneity. For the inversion step, the paper adapts BDIA (Zhang et al., ECCV 2024) by replacing the exact x₁ with x₀, yielding an ε-exact approximation with a provable error bound — a practical concession that prior exact-inversion work did not need to make.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Traceability of synthetic time series is critical in regulated domains (healthcare fMRI, finance) where sharing real data is restricted, and this is the first method to provide generation-time watermarking for that setting. The ε-exact inversion with its theoretical bound offers a reusable tool for any data-space diffusion model where exact inversion is infeasible. Key open questions include per-timestep watermarking granularity, streaming data support, and whether the chained-hashing design generalizes to other sequential modalities (e.g., audio, video frames) where temporal structure is similarly critical.
