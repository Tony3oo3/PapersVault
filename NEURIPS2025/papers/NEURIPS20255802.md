## Problem

Linear Autoencoders (LAEs) such as EASE and EDLAE achieve strong empirical performance in recommender systems, yet their success remains largely unexplained theoretically. Existing PAC-Bayes generalization bounds for linear regression are restricted to single-output settings with Gaussian data assumptions, and no bound has been adapted to the multivariate, bounded-data, constraint-laden structure of LAEs. This paper closes that gap by deriving a convergent PAC-Bayes bound for multivariate linear regression and extending it to LAEs with practical computational methods.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a binary user-item interaction matrix H (split into training and test sets with disjoint users), a trained LAE weight matrix W with zero diagonal, a Bernoulli hold-out mask Δ with retention probability p, and the population cross-correlation matrix Σ_hh (estimated from a larger fixed dataset H^whole). It outputs a PAC-Bayes bound pair (LH, RH) where LH is the expected true risk under the optimal posterior and RH is the upper bound; the tightest pair is selected by grid-searching over λ ∈ {1,2,4,...,512}. The bound is evaluated under a relaxed MSE that scores all predicted interactions (not just held-out ones), making it a special case of multivariate linear regression empirical risk with output dimension p = n.

## Method

The paper first generalizes Shalaeva's single-output PAC-Bayes bound to multivariate linear regression by allowing dependent outputs via a full covariance Σ_e and a potentially degenerate input covariance Σ_x, yielding a closed-form Ψ term involving non-central chi-squared MGFs (Theorem 3.2) and establishing a dominated-convergence sufficient condition for convergence (Theorem 3.3). For LAEs, the Gaussian data assumption is replaced with a bounded-support cross-correlation assumption (Assumption 4.1), and the relaxed MSE (which removes the hold-out mask from predictions) recasts LAE evaluation as constrained multivariate linear regression with a zero-diagonal constraint on W and a hold-out constraint on X, Y. To make the bound computable at scale, the prior and posterior are restricted to entry-wise Gaussians (following Dziugaite and Roy), which—exploiting the linearity of LAEs—admits a closed-form optimal posterior via Lagrange multipliers (Theorem 5.2). Finally, the O(n⁴) cost of computing the zero-diagonal-constrained expectation is reduced to O(n³) by proving that the unconstrained expectation upper-bounds the constrained one (Theorem 5.4), enabling evaluation on models with hundreds of millions of parameters.

## Results

The PAC-Bayes bound is tight across all three datasets (MovieLens 20M, Netflix, MSD), with the right-hand side (RH) within 3× the left-hand side (LH) for every regularization value γ ∈ {50, …, 5000}. On ML 20M, LH ranges from 59.09 to 61.66 and RH from 118.91 to 128.66; on Netflix, LH ranges from 84.48 to 87.22 and RH from 169.47 to 178.11; on MSD, LH ranges from 15.64 to 15.96 and RH from 31.50 to 32.60. The bound values correlate strongly with practical ranking metrics: models with smaller LH/RH consistently achieve higher Recall@50 (e.g., 0.3434→0.3510 on ML 20M as γ increases from 50 to 2000) and NDCG@100 (e.g., 0.4342→0.4487 on ML 20M), confirming the bound reflects true model performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper that does not benchmark against competing recommendation methods; instead, it validates the proposed PAC-Bayes bound against practical ranking metrics. The key comparison is with Dziugaite and Roy's non-vacuous PAC-Bayes bound for deep neural networks [13], where RH can reach up to 10× LH; the LAE bound here is tighter, with RH within 3× LH in all experimental settings. The bound's correlation with Recall@50 and NDCG@100 is strong across ML 20M and Netflix (monotonic improvement as γ increases from 50 to 2000/5000), though a minor deviation appears on MSD where the best LH/RH occurs at γ=1000 while the best Recall@50/NDCG@100 occurs at γ=500. The paper also positions its contribution relative to prior PAC-Bayes bounds for linear regression: Shalaeva et al. [45] (single-output, Gaussian data), Germain et al. [17] (non-convergent), and Alquier et al. [5] (general unbounded loss), showing that the proposed multivariate bound subsumes Shalaeva's as a special case (p=1, μ_x=0, Σ_x=σ_x²I, Σ_e=σ_e²) while extending to dependent outputs and bounded data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the PAC-Bayes bound for single-output linear regression by Shalaeva et al. (AAAI 2020) to the multivariate (multi-output) setting, then adapts it to linear autoencoders by replacing the Gaussian data assumption with a bounded-support assumption and incorporating LAE-specific constraints (zero-diagonal weights, hold-out masking). The computational framework follows Dziugaite and Roy's (UAI 2017) entry-wise Gaussian prior/posterior restriction, which the authors show yields a closed-form optimal posterior for LAEs due to their linearity—a property unavailable for the deep networks in the original work. The bounded-data analysis builds on Germain et al.'s (NeurIPS 2016) upper-bound technique for the Ψ term, while the convergence analysis formally completes a gap in Shalaeva's original proof via the dominated convergence theorem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first PAC-Bayes generalization bound specifically tailored to LAEs, providing a theoretical foundation for a model class whose strong empirical performance in recommender systems has lacked rigorous justification. The bound's tightness (within 3× of true risk) and its correlation with practical ranking metrics (Recall@K, NDCG@K) suggest it could serve as a principled model-selection criterion beyond purely empirical evaluation. Key limitations include the reliance on a relaxed MSE rather than ranking metrics, the need for the population cross-correlation matrix Σ_hh (requiring a fixed 'whole' dataset), and the restriction to linear models—extending the framework to nonlinear autoencoders or to settings where the data distribution is truly unknown remains open.
