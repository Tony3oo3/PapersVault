## Problem

Existing LVLM adversarial attacks overfit to a specific model architecture and fixed prompt, producing perturbations that fail when transferred to different models or task-aware prompts. This forces attackers to craft separate perturbations for every model-prompt pair, incurring significant time and resource overhead. The paper tackles the unexplored challenge of generating a single adversarial example that is simultaneously transferable across both different LVLM models and different prompts.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255788/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a benign RGB image and a task-specific text prompt as input, and produces a single adversarial image (benign image plus an l∞-bounded perturbation with ε=16/255) that is optimized on one white-box source LVLM and then transferred to attack multiple black-box target LVLMs under arbitrary prompts. The perturbation is designed to be prompt-agnostic: the same adversarial image should mislead the model toward a target text (e.g., 'I am sorry') regardless of which captioning, classification, or VQA prompt is paired with it.

## Method

The core insight is to use mutual information (MI) to explicitly disentangle the LVLM output's dependence on the benign image pattern versus the adversarial perturbation. The paper proves (Theorem 1) that the mixed MI between the full adversarial input and the output can be approximately decomposed into a benign MI and an adversarial MI under a bijectivity assumption. Two lightweight MI estimators are then trained separately using the Donsker-Varadhan representation (local Deep InfoMax framework): one maximizes MI between the perturbation and the adversarial output while minimizing MI between the perturbation and the benign output; the other does the analogous contrast for the benign image. During adversarial optimization, the overall loss combines a cross-entropy term pulling the output toward the target text with an MI-based term that maximizes adversarial MI and minimizes benign MI, forcing the LVLM's reasoning to be dominated by the perturbation rather than the original image content, which yields perturbations that remain effective when transferred to unseen models and prompts.

## Results

On the DALL-E dataset, transferring adversarial examples from LLaVA-1.5 to other LVLMs, the proposed method achieves SS of 0.661 (MiniGPT-4), 0.693 (BLIP-2), and 0.724 (InstructBLIP), compared to at most 0.303 for the best baseline (UniAtt). On the SVIT dataset, cross-model SS values range from 0.647 to 0.785, again far exceeding all baselines. Transfer to real-world closed-source models (GPT-4o, Claude-3.5-Sonnet) yields SS of 0.608 and 0.620 respectively, versus 0.142 and 0.169 for UniAtt. The method also maintains strong prompt-transferability, with SS degrading only marginally (e.g., 0.743→0.730 on LLaVA-1.5/DALL-E) as prompt count increases from 20 to 60.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against three LVLM attack baselines: PGD (Cui et al., CVPR 2024), CroPA (Luo et al., ICLR 2024), and UniAtt (Liu et al., NeurIPS 2024). PGD and CroPA are essentially non-transferable across models, with SS values below 0.15 on all non-source targets (e.g., 0.042 and 0.043 on MiniGPT-4 when sourced from LLaVA-1.5 on DALL-E). UniAtt, the strongest baseline designed for prompt-agnostic attacks, achieves SS of 0.186–0.324 on cross-model transfer, representing a 3–4× gap versus the proposed method's 0.65–0.78. The proposed method wins on every cross-model and cross-prompt comparison by a large margin (typically 2–5× in SS), while remaining competitive on the source model (e.g., 0.813 vs. 0.964 SS on LLaVA-1.5 itself, a modest trade-off for dramatically improved transferability). Against closed-source targets (GPT-4o, Claude-3.5), the gap widens further: Ours achieves SS 0.608/0.620 versus UniAtt's 0.142/0.169.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the information-theoretic adversarial framework of Zhou et al. (ICML 2022), which used mutual information to improve adversarial robustness, by repurposing MI as an *attack* objective specifically for LVLMs. It builds on the Deep InfoMax (DIM) estimator and Donsker-Varadhan representation to train lightweight MI estimation networks, but introduces a key departure: theoretically decomposing the mixed MI of an adversarial image into separate benign and adversarial components (Theorem 1), then optimizing them in opposite directions. This contrasts with prior LVLM attacks (PGD, CroPA, UniAtt) that rely on transformation-based or multi-prompt joint training for transferability, and with 2D/3D transfer methods that use input diversity or momentum—none of which explicitly model the information-theoretic dependence between input patterns and model outputs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in LVLM security: existing attacks require crafting separate perturbations per model and per prompt, which is infeasible for real-world deployment. The MI-disentanglement approach offers a principled, single-optimization solution that generalizes across architectures and task prompts, with demonstrated transfer to closed-source models (GPT-4o, Claude-3.5). Open questions include whether the bijection assumption (Assumption 1) holds for all perturbation regimes, whether the MI estimators remain reliable as LVLMs grow in scale, and whether the approach extends to multi-turn or agentic LVLM interactions where the output space is more complex.
