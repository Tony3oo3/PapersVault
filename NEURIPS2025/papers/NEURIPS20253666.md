## Problem

Existing deep generative models for protein conformation either capture temporal dynamics but cannot sample time-independent conformations (e.g., MDGen), or sample conformations directly but discard the sequential structure of MD trajectories (e.g., AlphaFlow, ConfDiff). No prior framework unifies trajectory simulation, equilibrium conformation sampling, and conformation interpolation within a single model, leaving these closely related tasks to be addressed by separate architectures with task-specific training.

## Input / Output

The model takes a protein amino acid sequence and optionally one or more conditioning conformation frames (SE(3) backbone rigids: N-Cα-C translations and rotations) as input. It outputs full protein backbone conformations in SE(3) space plus seven torsional angles (φ, ψ, ω, χ₁–χ₄) for full-atom reconstruction, either as a single frame, an autoregressive trajectory of variable length, or an interpolated sequence between two endpoint conformations. During training, frames are represented via pseudo-Cβ pairwise distances, and trajectories of length L=8 with variable strides (1–1024 snapshots at 10 ps intervals) are sampled to span multiple timescales.

## Method

CONFROVER factorizes an MD trajectory as an autoregressive product p(x^{1:L}|P) = ∏ p(x^l | x^{<l}, P) and realizes each conditional with a three-module pipeline: (i) an Encoding Layer that combines frozen OpenFold protein embeddings with a FrameEncoder producing per-frame single/pair latents; (ii) a Trajectory Module of interleaved Pairformer StructuralUpdate blocks and Llama-style causal TemporalUpdate layers (with rotary position encoding) that propagate information only from preceding frames; and (iii) a ConfDiff-based SE(3) diffusion decoder that denoises noisy translations and rotations conditioned on the updated latent. The key training innovation is applying denoising score matching loss directly in SE(3) space for the autoregressive objective, backpropagating gradients through the diffusion decoder into the encoder and temporal module, thereby avoiding discretization of continuous conformations into tokens. A hybrid training schedule (1:1 trajectory-to-single-frame ratio, extended to 1:1:1 with interpolation for CONFROVER-INTERP) enables the same weights to support all three generation tasks, with unconditional sampling achieved by replacing context frames with a mask token and using identity attention.

## Results

On the multi-start trajectory simulation benchmark (~2,700 conditions across 82 proteins), CONFROVER achieves Pearson correlations of 0.75 (Traj), 0.63 (Frame), and 0.53 (ΔFrame) in Cα coordinates, substantially outperforming MDGEN (0.56, 0.47, 0.41). In PCA 2D space, the gap widens dramatically: CONFROVER reaches 0.73/0.50/0.43 versus MDGEN's 0.18/0.15/0.10. For time-independent conformation sampling, CONFROVER matches or exceeds ALPHAFLOW and CONFDIFF on most metrics (e.g., Per-target RMSF r = 0.85, RMWD = 2.66 Å, Joint PCA W₂ = 2.23), while in the 100 ns state-recovery experiment it achieves F1 = 0.58 vs. MDGEN's 0.42.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CONFROVER decisively outperforms MDGEN [19] on trajectory simulation across all six correlation metrics, with the largest gains in PCA space (e.g., 0.73 vs. 0.18 on Traj.), indicating it captures the principal axes of conformational variance far more accurately. In the 100 ns state-recovery experiment, CONFROVER also beats MDGEN on JSD (0.51 vs. 0.56), Recall (0.42 vs. 0.29), and F1 (0.58 vs. 0.42), and its tICA dynamic-mode correlations approach the MD oracle level. For time-independent conformation sampling, CONFROVER is competitive with the specialized SOTA models ALPHAFLOW [18] and CONFDIFF [44], matching or exceeding them on at least five of eight metrics (e.g., Per-target RMSF r = 0.85 ties both; RMWD 2.66 beats CONFDIFF's 2.70; Joint PCA W₂ 2.23 ties CONFDIFF's 2.22). Notably, CONFROVER-TRAJ (trained only on trajectories, without the single-frame objective) degrades relative to the full CONFROVER, confirming the importance of hybrid training. On conformation interpolation, no direct baseline exists for large proteins, so the paper demonstrates that CONFROVER-INTERP produces smooth, directed transitions while the base CONFROVER fails to progress toward the target end state.

## Relation to Prior Work

CONFROVER unifies three previously separate protein modeling tasks—trajectory simulation, time-independent conformation sampling, and conformation interpolation—under a single autoregressive factorization over MD frames. Architecturally, it combines the SE(3) diffusion decoder from ConfDiff [44] with a Llama-style causal transformer for temporal modeling, following the autoregressive-without-discretization paradigm introduced by Li et al. [26] for image generation. The encoding layer repurposes OpenFold's Evoformer and the FrameEncoder from AlphaFlow [18], while the interleaved StructuralUpdate/TemporalUpdate design draws on video generation techniques (temporal attention with identity-attention ablation for single-frame mode). The key conceptual departure from prior work is that trajectory models (MDGen, TimeWarp, EquiJump) and ensemble generators (AlphaFlow, ConfDiff) were trained as separate systems; CONFROVER shows that a single autoregressive model with flexible frame ordering can handle all three objectives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to jointly learn protein conformational distributions and dynamics from MD data, offering a practical path toward replacing expensive MD simulations with learned generative models for multiple downstream tasks. The significant gap to the MD oracle (JSD 0.51 vs 0.31, and failure to cross energy barriers in some cases) highlights that current generative models still cannot fully replicate the conformational exploration of physics-based simulation. Open questions include scalability to multi-chain complexes and longer trajectories (limited by triangular-update cost), whether incorporating energy information from MD can close the remaining gap, and whether the autoregressive formulation generalizes to non-protein biomolecular systems.
