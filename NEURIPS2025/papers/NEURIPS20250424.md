## Problem

Existing benchmarks for LLM-based recommenders (LLMRec, PromptRec, OpenP5, etc.) evaluate only a single recommendation scenario, cover narrow item representation forms (typically just unique IDs or text), and assess a limited number of models and datasets, leaving the performance landscape fragmented. There is no comprehensive, standardized platform that simultaneously compares LLMs against conventional deep learning recommenders across multiple item representations, recommendation tasks, and domains while also measuring inference efficiency.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250424/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For pair-wise (CTR) recommendation, the input is a user behavior sequence (truncated to 20 items) and a candidate item, where the item is encoded as one of four forms: a unique identifier token, raw text, a pretrained semantic embedding (extracted via Llama-1-7B), or a 4-token semantic identifier from a 1024-vocabulary RQ-VAE codebook. The output is a click probability score, computed from YES/NO token logits for LLMs or a sigmoid head for DLRMs. For list-wise (SeqRec) recommendation, the input is the user's historical item sequence and the output is the next item, generated autoregressively; when semantic identifiers are used, a conditional beam search (CBS) constrains decoding to valid paths in a pre-constructed identifier tree, ensuring every generated token sequence maps to a real item.

## Method

RECBENCH is a benchmarking platform that organizes 10 conventional DLRMs and 17 LLMs into ten groups (A–J) defined by the cross-product of model family (DLRM vs. LLM) and item representation (unique ID, text, semantic embedding, semantic identifier), evaluated on both CTR prediction and sequential recommendation across five domain-diverse datasets. A key technical contribution is the conditional beam search (CBS) decoding strategy: during autoregressive generation of semantic identifiers, candidate tokens at each step are masked to retain only those extending the current partial sequence to a valid prefix in the RQ-VAE identifier tree, guaranteeing that decoded sequences correspond to real items. Semantic identifiers are generated via the TIGER pipeline (SentenceBERT content embeddings discretized by a 4-layer RQ-VAE with codebook size 256 per layer, yielding ~4 billion representable combinations). LLMs are fine-tuned with LoRA (rank 32/α 128 for CTR, rank 128/α 128 for SeqRec) at a fixed learning rate of 1e-4, and all experiments run on a single A100 GPU with results averaged over five runs. The benchmark uniquely reports both accuracy (AUC for CTR, NDCG@10 for SeqRec) and inference latency (CPU and GPU milliseconds per 1,000 inferences), enabling a holistic accuracy–efficiency tradeoff analysis.

## Results

In pair-wise CTR prediction (AUC), the best fine-tuned LLM (Mistral-2_7B with text representation) achieves 0.7578 overall AUC, outperforming the best conventional DLRM (DNN_emb with semantic embedding, 0.7171) by ~5.6%. In list-wise sequential recommendation (NDCG@10), the best LLM (SID-BERT_base-CBS) reaches 0.1877 overall, surpassing the best conventional model (SASRec_24L, 0.0698) by ~169%. However, these accuracy gains come at a severe efficiency cost: Llama-3_7B requires 6,800 ms CPU inference per sample versus 1.42 ms for DNN_emb (~4,800× slower), and LLMs in sequential recommendation require ~1,000× more inference time than SASRec.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among conventional DLRMs, semantic-embedding-based models (DNN_emb, DCNv2_emb, GDCN_emb) are the strongest baselines, with DNN_emb achieving 0.7171 overall AUC; all unique-ID and text-based DLRMs (DNN, PNN, DeepFM, DCN, DCNv2, MaskNet, FinalMLP, AutoInt, GDCN and their text variants) score 0.65–0.69 overall and are outperformed by both DNN_emb and large fine-tuned LLMs. In the LLM-AS-RS setting, fine-tuned text-based LLMs (Mistral-2_7B at 0.7578, Llama-3_7B at 0.7508) decisively beat all conventional recommenders by 5–10% AUC, while fine-tuned unique-ID LLMs (P5-BERT_base 0.5392, P5-OPT_large 0.6013, P5-Llama-3_7B 0.6576) and semantic-ID LLMs (SID-BERT_base 0.5384) underperform even the weakest DLRMs, indicating that discrete token representations are poorly suited for CTR scoring. Zero-shot LLMs are largely ineffective (most score near 0.50 AUC), with the notable exceptions of Mistral_7B (0.6199), GLM-4_9B (0.6231), and Qwen-2_7B (0.6075), which still trail the best DLRM by ~10%. In list-wise sequential recommendation, SID-BERT_base-CBS (0.1877 NDCG@10) and SID-Llama-3_7B-CBS (0.1607) outperform SASRec_24L (0.0698) by 169% and 130% respectively, while P5-BERT_base (0.1025) beats SASRec_12L (0.0672) by 52%; GRU4Rec, Caser, and Bert4Rec (all ≤0.0237 NDCG@10) are the weakest baselines. The consistent pattern is that LLMs win on accuracy across all 24 baselines but lose by 3–4 orders of magnitude on inference latency, making the LLM-FOR-RS paradigm (e.g., DNN_emb at 1.42 ms) the most practical for industrial deployment.

## Relation to Prior Work

RECBENCH extends the LLM-AS-RS evaluation lineage initiated by P5 (Geng et al., 2022) and TIGER (Rajput et al., 2023) by unifying their item-representation strategies (unique ID, text, semantic embedding, semantic identifier) under a single benchmark protocol. It departs from prior LLM-AS-RS benchmarks (LLMRec, PromptRec, OpenP5, RSBench) by simultaneously covering four item representations, two recommendation tasks (pair-wise CTR and list-wise SeqRec), 17 LLMs, and five cross-domain datasets, while uniquely incorporating inference-efficiency metrics. The conventional DLRM baselines (DCN, DeepFM, AutoInt, SASRec, etc.) are drawn from the standard CTR and sequential recommendation literature, and the semantic-identifier pipeline follows TIGER's RQ-VAE discretization with a 4-layer, 256-codebook configuration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark provides the first systematic, multi-dimensional comparison showing that LLM-AS-RS achieves up to 5% AUC (CTR) and 170% NDCG@10 (SeqRec) gains over conventional DLRMs, yet incurs roughly 1,000× inference latency, making real-time deployment impractical. A practically important finding is that the LLM-FOR-RS paradigm (semantic-embedding-initialized DLRMs) recovers ~95% of the LLM-AS-RS accuracy at a fraction of the cost, suggesting that integrating LLM-derived representations into lightweight architectures may be the most viable near-term path for industry. Open questions include developing inference-acceleration techniques (speculative decoding, distillation, quantization) specifically for recommendation workloads, extending the benchmark to fairness and privacy dimensions, and understanding whether the efficiency gap narrows as model architectures evolve.
