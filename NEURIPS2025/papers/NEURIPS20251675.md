## Problem

Watermark forging—stealing a watermark from genuine content and applying it to malicious content—remains underexplored compared to watermark removal. Existing forging methods rely on unrealistic assumptions such as paired clean-watermarked datasets or thousands of watermarked samples with the same hidden message, making them impractical in realistic black-box, data-scarce adversarial settings where an attacker observes only a single watermarked image.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single watermarked RGB image (resized to 768×768 for processing) and an arbitrary target image at any resolution. It produces a forged watermark (the estimated difference between the watermarked image and its optimized clean version) that is resized to the target's resolution and added pixel-wise to yield a watermarked target image. The same optimization also yields a watermark-free clean image for the removal task. The preference model itself outputs a single scalar score per image, with higher values indicating fewer artifacts.

## Method

The core contribution is a ConvNeXt V2-Tiny preference model trained with a Bradley-Terry ranking loss to prefer clean images over synthetically corrupted ones, where corruptions are procedurally generated in Fourier space as wave-style, noise, and line-style artifacts (optionally localized via JND maps). Crucially, no real watermarked data is used during training; the synthetic artifacts serve as a generalizable proxy for watermark artifacts. Adversarial perturbation of negative samples during training ensures the model produces semantically interpretable gradients rather than spurious checkerboard patterns. At attack time, the watermarked image is optimized via gradient ascent on the preference score for a fixed number of steps k, and the estimated watermark is extracted as the pixel-wise difference. This watermark is then transferred to any target image by simple addition, requiring no knowledge of the watermarking algorithm, no paired data, and only a single watermarked example.

## Results

On the watermark forging task (Table 1), the proposed one-shot method (n=1) achieves bit accuracy of 1.00 on CIN, 0.83 on MBRS, 0.61 on TrustMark, and 0.83 on Video Seal, with a PSNR of 31.3 dB. For watermark removal (Table 2), the method achieves bit accuracy of 0.82 (CIN), 0.64 (MBRS), 0.60 (TrustMark), and 0.49 (Video Seal) with PSNR 31.2 dB, remaining competitive with diffusion-based methods while avoiding hallucinated details. The key advantage is that all results are obtained from a single watermarked image with no access to the watermarking model, whereas competing methods require 100–1000 watermarked images or API access.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Noise blending [36], the proposed method wins on all four watermarking schemes (e.g., Video Seal 0.83 vs. 0.53) and PSNR (31.3 vs. 21.8 dB), while also not requiring API access. Against Warfare [23], the method achieves higher bit accuracy on every scheme (e.g., MBRS 0.83 vs. 0.50, Video Seal 0.83 vs. 0.74) but lower PSNR (31.3 vs. 39.6 dB); critically, Warfare requires 1000 watermarked images with the same hidden message. Against DiffPure [32], the method ties on CIN (1.00) and MBRS (0.83) but clearly wins on Video Seal (0.83 vs. 0.75) and PSNR (31.3 vs. 26.6 dB). Against Image averaging [45], the method ties on CIN (1.00) and TrustMark (0.61), loses on MBRS (0.83 vs. 0.91), but substantially outperforms on Video Seal (0.83 vs. 0.59), where content-aware watermarks defeat the averaging approach. Against CtrlRegen [25] (removal only), the method achieves lower bit accuracy on CIN (0.82 vs. 0.86), MBRS (0.64 vs. 0.70), and Video Seal (0.49 vs. 0.55) with much higher PSNR (31.2 vs. 24.4 dB), while CtrlRegen edges out on TrustMark (0.56 vs. 0.60).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the Bradley-Terry preference model framework from the LLM alignment literature for image quality scoring, training a ConvNeXt V2-Tiny on procedurally generated Fourier-space artifacts rather than real watermarks. It departs from prior forging methods (Warfare, diffusion-based approaches) that require thousands of paired watermarked images or access to the watermarking API, and from removal-focused attacks (DiffPure, CtrlRegen, UnMarker) by targeting the more underexplored forging scenario under a strict black-box, single-image threat model. The adversarial training component, borrowed from robustness literature, is specifically designed to produce semantically interpretable gradients for watermark extraction rather than classifier robustness.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that even content-aware post-hoc watermarking schemes (e.g., Video Seal) can be forged from a single observed image without any access to the watermarking model, directly questioning the security assumptions underlying regulatory watermarking mandates (EU AI Act, U.S. Executive Order). A key open question it raises is whether decoders can be made truly content-aware to reject watermarks from mismatched source images, as the finding that TrustMark (where both embedder and decoder are content-aware) resists forging while Video Seal (content-aware embedder but content-agnostic decoder) does not provides a concrete design guideline. The method's limitation to post-hoc watermarks (inapplicable to semantic watermarks like Tree-Ring) and its tendency to blur high-frequency natural textures remain practical constraints.
