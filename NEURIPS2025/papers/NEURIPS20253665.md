## Problem

In open-ended language action tasks (e.g., negotiation, question-asking games), the action space scales as V^L over a token vocabulary, making rewards extremely sparse and policy gradient variance prohibitively high for standard RL methods like PPO or REINFORCE. Existing approaches assign environmental rewards directly to individual sampled actions, which fails when the sample size is negligible relative to the combinatorial action space, resulting in unstable and inefficient policy optimization.

## Input / Output

The policy model (Llama-3-8B-Instruct) consumes a conversation history of alternating observations and actions and generates a free-form natural language utterance as its action. Each utterance is embedded via text-embedding-3-small into a 1536-dimensional semantic vector, which is then mapped to a discrete intention cluster label. The reward signal is not assigned per individual action but is aggregated across all history-action pairs that share the same intention cluster, producing a denser, lower-variance advantage estimate used for policy updates.

## Method

ARIA projects natural language actions into a low-dimensional intention space via hierarchical agglomerative clustering (average linkage) over sentence embeddings, reducing the effective action space from V^L to |C| clusters. A reward-oriented granularity selection mechanism (SplitScore) automatically determines the optimal number of clusters by measuring the marginal reward change from further splits, stopping when the change falls below a threshold ε. Within each intention cluster, trajectory rewards are temporally discounted and averaged across all history-action pairs mapping to the same cluster, yielding a cluster-averaged advantage that replaces the original per-action advantage in an offline REINFORCE objective. The paper provides a theoretical guarantee (via law of total variance) that this aggregation strictly reduces policy gradient variance, and a bounded-bias result (via ε-bisimulation) showing the approximation error is O(ε) when intra-cluster actions are behaviorally similar.

## Results

On adversarial tasks, ARIA (Iter 1) achieves an average win rate of 53.15% on Bargaining and 45.79% on Negotiation, surpassing the best offline baseline (Step-wise DPO: 50.13% / 42.35%) and the best online baseline (ArCHer: 48.36% / StarPO: 39.47%). On single-agent tasks, ARIA reaches 28.50 average reward on Twenty Questions and Guess My City (Iter 1), outperforming the strongest baseline (Traj-wise DPO: 22.25). Across all four tasks, ARIA delivers an average improvement of 9.95% over the best competing method, with further gains of 3.27% and 1.85% after two and three iterative updates respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ARIA outperforms all six baselines on both adversarial tasks. Against the strongest offline baseline, Step-wise DPO, ARIA gains +3.02% on Bargaining (53.15 vs. 50.13) and +3.44% on Negotiation (45.79 vs. 42.35). Against the strongest online baseline, ArCHer leads ARIA by +4.79% on Bargaining (53.15 vs. 48.36) while StarPO trails by +6.32% on Negotiation (45.79 vs. 39.47). The gap is most pronounced against SPAG, which underperforms even the vanilla model on Negotiation (31.11 vs. 38.31), suggesting that naive discounted-reward PPO struggles in open-ended language action spaces. On single-agent tasks (Table 2), ARIA (Iter 1) achieves 28.50 average reward versus 22.25 for Traj-wise DPO and 19.75 for SPAG, a margin of +6.25 and +8.75 points respectively. Iterative retraining further widens the gap, with ARIA (Iter 3) reaching 35.25 on single-agent tasks and 58.01/48.80 on adversarial tasks, confirming that reward aggregation preserves discriminative signal across iterations without premature convergence.

## Relation to Prior Work

ARIA builds directly on REINFORCE as its policy optimization backbone but introduces a key conceptual departure: projecting free-form language actions from the exponentially large token space (V^L) into a low-dimensional intention space via hierarchical agglomerative clustering, then aggregating rewards across semantically similar actions. This contrasts with prior offline methods (trajectory-wise DPO, step-wise DPO, SPAG) and online methods (ArCHer, StarPO/GRPO) that assign rewards directly to individual sampled actions, which the paper argues leads to extreme reward sparsity and high gradient variance in open-ended language settings. The reward-oriented granularity selection (SplitScore) further distinguishes ARIA from standard clustering practice, which relies on geometric metrics (Silhouette, CHI, DBI) that the paper shows favor overly coarse groupings for this task.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a fundamental bottleneck in RL for language agents: the combinatorial explosion of the action space in free-form language tasks makes standard reward assignment impractical. The theoretical guarantee that cluster-averaged advantages reduce policy gradient variance while introducing only O(ε) bias (via ε-bisimulation) provides principled justification beyond empirical observation. However, the approach assumes intentions are discrete and well-separated in embedding space, and its effectiveness is tightly coupled to embedding quality—tasks with overlapping or continuous intention structures remain open. The ~10% average improvement across four tasks and two model families (Llama, Qwen) suggests the technique is broadly applicable, but the absolute performance levels (e.g., ~53% win rate in Bargaining) indicate substantial room for further gains.
