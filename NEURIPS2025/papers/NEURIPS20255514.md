## Problem

Golowich and Livni (2021) showed that Littlestone classes are privately online learnable in the realizable setting with an O_d(log T) mistake bound against an oblivious adversary, but their algorithm degrades to a suboptimal Õ_d(√T) bound against an adaptive adversary. Additionally, the agnostic setting—where no realizability assumption is made on the data—remained entirely unexplored for private online learning. This paper closes both gaps: it achieves the optimal O_d(log T) mistake bound against adaptive adversaries and demonstrates Õ_d(√T) regret in the agnostic setting.

## Input / Output

The learner receives a stream of T binary-labeled examples (x_t, y_t) ∈ X × {0,1} generated by an adaptive adversary that can choose each example based on the learner's prior outputs h_1, …, h_{t-1}. At each round the learner must output a hypothesis h_t: X → {0,1}, and the entire sequence of outputs must satisfy (ε, δ)-differential privacy under the adaptive-input formulation of Jain et al. (2023). The concept class H is specified by its Littlestone dimension d, and the time horizon T is known in advance; the learner may be improper (outputs need not lie in H).

## Method

For the realizable setting, the algorithm maintains a forest of binary trees of depth d and introduces a lazy update technique: instead of updating immediately upon a collision (sibling sequences on which the SOA disagrees), it delays the update until enough collisions accumulate in a layer, then applies a random permutation to restore the independence needed for the frequent-hypothesis argument. The AboveThreshold mechanism privately monitors cumulative mistakes per hypothesis, and PrivateHistogram identifies frequent hypotheses at each layer; a uniform convergence argument over H handles the fact that the labeling function h* is not fixed in advance under an adaptive adversary. For the agnostic setting, the paper first gives a simple proper learner via batched sanitization (yielding Õ_d(T^{3/4}) regret), then improves to Õ_d(√T) by constructing a polynomial-size expert pool from instances of Hanneke et al.'s online learner (which outputs majority votes over few concepts in H, enabling private monitoring) and running a private OPE algorithm over these experts.

## Results

In the realizable setting, the paper achieves an O(2^{2^{O(d)}} (log T + log(1/δ))/ε) expected mistake bound against any adaptive adversary (Theorem 1.1), improving the previous Õ_d(√T) bound of Golowich and Livni [2021] to the optimal logarithmic dependence on T. In the agnostic setting, the algorithm attains an expected regret of Õ(d√T/ε) + Õ_d(T^{1/3}/ε^{2/3}) against an adaptive adversary (Theorem 1.2), matching the non-private Õ(√(dT)) rate up to logarithmic factors. For an oblivious adversary, the agnostic regret further reduces to O(√(dT log T)) + Õ_d(T^{1/3}/ε^{2/3}), achieving a 'privacy is free' regime when ε ≥ Õ_d(1/T^{1/4}).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is with Golowich and Livni [2021], whose algorithm achieved an O_d(log T) mistake bound only against an oblivious adversary but degraded to Õ_d(√T) against an adaptive adversary. This paper closes that gap, achieving O_d(log T) against an adaptive adversary while retaining the same doubly exponential dependence on d. In the agnostic setting, the paper's Õ_d(√T) regret matches the best known non-private constructive bound of Hanneke et al. [2021] in terms of T-dependence, establishing an equivalence between private and non-private online learnability in the agnostic regime. The dependence on d remains doubly exponential (2^{2^{O(d)}}), which is significantly worse than the polynomial-in-d dependence achievable in private PAC learning [Ghazi et al., 2021b] and is identified as a key open problem. The paper also notes that the Ω(log T) lower bound for private online learning [Cohen et al., 2024; Dmitriev et al., 2024; Li et al., 2024] confirms the logarithmic T-dependence is tight, highlighting a fundamental separation from the non-private setting where the mistake bound is O(d) independent of T.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends Golowich and Livni's (2021) framework for private online learning of Littlestone classes, which achieved O_d(log T) mistakes against an oblivious adversary but only Õ_d(√T) against an adaptive one. The key technical departure is a lazy update mechanism that delays tree updates until sufficient collisions accumulate, restoring the independence needed for the random-permutation argument, combined with a uniform convergence argument to handle the fact that the labeling function is not fixed in advance under adaptivity. For the agnostic setting, the paper repurposes sanitization (Bousquet et al., 2020; Ghazi et al., 2021b) and the expert-construction paradigm of Ben-David et al. (2009), replacing the SOA with Hanneke et al.'s (2021) simple-predictor learner to make the construction amenable to private monitoring.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result closes a gap left by Golowich and Livni, confirming that adaptive adversaries do not inherently require higher error rates in private online learning (realizable setting), and extends the private/non-private equivalence to the agnostic regime. The 'privacy is free' phenomenon for oblivious adversaries in the agnostic case (matching the best non-private constructive bound) suggests that DP overhead may be negligible in broad settings. However, the doubly exponential dependence on the Littlestone dimension d remains a significant limitation compared to the polynomial dependence in private PAC learning, and whether this can be improved is left open. The paper also leaves open whether O_d(log T) is achievable without knowing T in advance, and whether proper learners can match the improper bounds.
