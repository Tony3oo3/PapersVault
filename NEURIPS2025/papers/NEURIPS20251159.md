## Problem

Event cameras offer microsecond-level latency and robustness to motion blur, yet visual grounding—localizing objects from free-form language—has never been studied in the event domain. Existing grounding benchmarks rely on dense frame-based or 3D sensors that degrade under motion blur, rapid scene changes, and low-light conditions, leaving open how to bridge asynchronous event streams with natural language for temporally-aware, compositional object localization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251159/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a voxelized event tensor E ∈ R^{2×T×H×W} (preserving polarity and spatiotemporal structure over T temporal bins), an optional synchronized RGB frame F ∈ R^{3×H×W}, and a natural language referring expression of C tokens (averaging 34.1 words with four attribute-level annotations). It outputs a single 2D bounding box (x, y, w, h) for the referred object. The benchmark supports three evaluation configurations—event-only, frame-only, and event-frame fusion—allowing systematic analysis of each modality's contribution to grounding.

## Method

EventRefer introduces a Mixture of Event-Attribute Experts (MoEE) that explicitly models four grounding attributes (appearance, status, relation-to-viewer, relation-to-others). A lightweight fuzzy matcher projects attribute-specific cue phrases onto token-level binary maps; these masks (augmented with public context tokens) gate the DETR-style Transformer encoder's hidden states to produce four parallel attribute-specific feature streams. Each stream is refined by a lightweight FFN, mean-pooled into a compact descriptor, and fused via a learnable softmax gating with Gaussian perturbation to prevent expert collapse. During training, the four attributes are treated as co-located pseudo-targets sharing the same ground-truth box but carrying distinct token maps, enabling dense multi-attribute supervision through standard Hungarian matching without enlarging the decoder. At inference, per-attribute scores (dot-product between predicted and target token distributions) are combined to select the final bounding box, yielding interpretable, attribute-adaptive grounding.

## Results

On the Talk2Event validation set, EventRefer achieves 61.82% mAcc and 87.32% mIoU in the event-frame fusion setting, 55.47% mAcc and 85.76% mIoU in the frame-only setting, and 31.96% mAcc and 76.46% mIoU in the event-only setting, outperforming all baselines in every configuration. The most notable per-category gains appear on small dynamic objects: Rider improves by +11.7% over the best fusion baseline (FlexEvent) and +24.4% over the best frame-only baseline (BUTD-DETR). The evaluation uses a stringent IoU@0.95 threshold for mAcc, emphasizing precise localization quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EventRefer outperforms all 15 baselines across all three modality settings. In the frame-only setting, it surpasses the strongest retrained grounding model BUTD-DETR [37] by +6.56% mAcc (55.47 vs. 48.91) and the best zero-shot generalist GroundingDINO [58] by +10.97% mAcc, with particularly large gains on Rider (+30.66%) and Pedestrian (+5.0%). In the event-only setting, EventRefer leads the best event perception backbone EvRT-DETR [83] by +2.62% mAcc (31.96 vs. 29.34), with a dramatic +19.5% advantage on Rider (25.00 vs. 5.50), confirming that attribute-aware reasoning compensates for the lack of texture in event streams. In the event-frame fusion setting, EventRefer beats the strongest fusion baseline FlexEvent [63] by +2.42% mAcc (61.82 vs. 59.40) and DAGr [27] by +3.51%, with consistent per-category improvements (e.g., Car +2.51%, Bus +3.22%, Bike +4.79%). Notably, all event-based and fusion baselines were adapted from detection methods by attaching a DETR grounding head, whereas EventRefer is natively designed for multi-attribute language grounding, explaining the systematic advantage on small and dynamic object categories.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the well-studied visual grounding task (RefCOCO, ScanRefer, GroundingDINO) into the event-camera domain, where no prior grounding benchmark or method exists. Architecturally, it builds on RVT for event feature extraction and DETR's query-based decoder with Hungarian matching for box prediction, but introduces a Mixture of Event-Attribute Experts (MoEE) module that adaptively fuses four attribute-specific feature streams (appearance, status, relation-to-viewer, relation-to-others) — a design not present in prior grounding or event-perception work. The dataset curation pipeline repurposes DSEC driving data and leverages Qwen2-VL for context-aware caption generation, departing from the manual annotation protocols used in static-scene grounding benchmarks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Talk2Event fills a clear gap: event cameras excel in dynamic, low-light, and high-speed scenarios where frame-based grounding degrades, yet no benchmark or method existed to evaluate language-driven localization from asynchronous event streams. The four-attribute annotation protocol (appearance, status, viewer-relation, object-relation) provides structured supervision that makes grounding interpretable and compositional, a dimension absent from all prior grounding datasets. Key limitations include the driving-scene-only scope (7 object classes, DSEC geography), the still-modest event-only accuracy (~32% mAcc at IoU@0.95), and reliance on a single LLM (Qwen2-VL) for caption generation, raising questions about annotation bias. Open questions include whether the attribute decomposition generalizes to non-driving scenes, whether the MoEE gating transfers across sensor types, and how grounding quality scales with event density and scene complexity.
