## Problem

Solving Fused Unbalanced Gromov-Wasserstein (FUGW) optimal transport between graphs requires iterative cubic-complexity solvers (O(n₁n₂² + n₁²n₂) per iteration), making it unscalable for large graphs or repeated evaluations needed for hyperparameter validation. Existing deep-learning accelerators for OT (e.g., Meta OT) are limited to convex entropic formulations and cannot handle the non-convex quadratic FUGW objective, leaving a gap for efficient, differentiable prediction of unbalanced transport plans between graphs.

## Input / Output

The model takes two graphs G₁, G₂ specified by node feature matrices F_k ∈ R^{n_k×d}, connectivity matrices D_k (adjacency or shortest-path distance), and the FUGW tradeoff hyperparameters (α ∈ [0,1], ρ > 0). It outputs an unbalanced transport plan P ∈ R^{n₁×n₂} whose total mass need not equal one, along with per-node weight vectors v_k ∈ R^{n_k} that control how much mass each node contributes. The plan is fully differentiable with respect to both the graph inputs and the (α, ρ) parameters, enabling downstream gradient-based optimization of functionals of the plan.

## Method

ULOT is trained via unsupervised amortized optimization: it minimizes the expected FUGW loss over randomly sampled graph pairs and parameter values, eliminating the need for pre-computed ground-truth plans. The architecture stacks N layers of node embedding, each combining a self-path GCN (capturing graph topology for the Gromov term) with a cross-attention path (adapted from Graph Matching Networks) that computes row- and column-softmax similarity matrices between the two graphs. The α parameter is encoded via a 10-dimensional Fourier positional encoding and concatenated with ρ to node features at every layer, making the network explicitly conditioned on the FUGW tradeoff. The final transport plan head averages the two cross-attention similarity matrices and scales them by learned sigmoid node-weight vectors, cleanly separating the pairwise matching problem from the unbalanced mass-discard problem. This yields O(n₁n₂) prediction complexity versus the cubic cost of classical solvers, and the predicted plan can serve as a warm start to accelerate iterative solvers when higher precision is required.

## Results

On the IBC fMRI dataset (1000-node graphs), ULOT achieves a Pearson correlation of 0.99 with the IBPP solver's FUGW loss while being up to 100× faster than classical solvers (IBPP, MM, L-BFGS-B) and 10× faster than Sinkhorn with a smaller loss error. On simulated SBM graphs, ULOT attains a relative error of 0.19±0.29 and a Pearson correlation of 1.0 with the IBPP solver. For the label propagation task, differentiable optimization of (α, ρ) yields an accuracy of 0.87±0.092 on same-type graph pairs and 0.74±0.11 on different-type pairs. ULOT's quadratic O(n²) complexity contrasts with the cubic O(n³) complexity of all classical solvers, as confirmed by scaling experiments up to 10,000 nodes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ULOT is compared against four classical FUGW solvers: IBPP [39], MM [6], L-BFGS-B [4], and entropic Sinkhorn [8]. Against IBPP, ULOT produces transport plans with nearly identical FUGW loss (Pearson 0.99) but runs up to 100× faster; it also serves as an effective warm-start that significantly reduces IBPP's convergence iterations. Against MM and L-BFGS-B, ULOT similarly achieves competitive loss at 100× lower computational cost. Against Sinkhorn (the entropic-regularized variant), ULOT is both faster (10×) and more accurate (smaller loss error), making it the strongest baseline comparison. The key trade-off is that ULOT introduces a small approximation error in the predicted plan, which the authors mitigate by offering ULOT as a warm-start for high-precision solver runs.

## Relation to Prior Work

ULOT generalizes the amortized optimization strategy of Meta OT (Amos et al., 2022) from predicting dual potentials of entropic OT to predicting full transport plans for the non-convex FUGW problem between graphs. The architecture adapts the cross-attention mechanism of Graph Matching Networks (Li et al., 2019) to the OT prediction setting, adding a node-reweighting head specific to unbalanced transport and a Fourier positional encoding (Tong et al., 2023) to condition the network on FUGW hyperparameters (α, ρ). The problem formulation and loss function are taken directly from the FUGW framework of Thual et al. (2022), which was designed for brain alignment but lacked scalable solvers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 100× speedup over cubic-complexity FUGW solvers, combined with full differentiability with respect to both graph inputs and hyperparameters, opens the door to applications that were previously intractable: large-scale brain alignment, FUGW barycenter computation, and bi-level optimization of transport parameters for downstream tasks like label propagation. Key limitations include a small but non-zero loss error relative to exact solvers (mitigated by warm-starting), and GPU memory constraints limiting experiments to graphs of ≤10,000 nodes. Open questions include whether the approach scales to the 160k-vertex brain surfaces in the IBC dataset without parcellation, and whether the learned plans can match solver precision in high-stakes applications.
