## Problem

Existing open-world RL methods such as LS-Imagine rely on fixed exploration mechanisms and static jump thresholds that cannot adapt across changing task phases, leading to inefficient exploration and behavioral stagnation in complex scenarios. The paper asks whether human-inspired cognitive strategies—phase decomposition, selective dual-memory, and uncertainty-regulated prediction—can be integrated into a model-based RL framework to produce genuinely adaptive exploration behavior in open-world environments.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252812/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The agent receives RGB visual observations and task-specific affordance maps (generated by fine-tuned UNet models) at each timestep, which are jointly encoded by the RSSM into deterministic recurrent and stochastic latent states. Outputs include a discrete control action conditioned on retrieved episodic memories and the current cognitive phase, a three-valued phase label (explore/approach/complete) that gates all downstream parameters, and a scalar uncertainty estimate that adaptively modulates the jumpy-transition threshold. Working memory is capped at K=20 recent transitions, while episodic memory grows selectively based on a dynamically adapted surprise threshold.

## Method

CPP extends the LS-Imagine jumpy-imagination architecture (itself built on DreamerV3's RSSM world model) with three neurologically-inspired modules operating in a cascading loop. A Phase-Adaptive Cognitive Controller tracks a progress signal combining recent reward averages and episodic-memory similarity to transition between exploration, approach, and completion phases, each with distinct exploration weights (0.8→0.5→0.2) and jump thresholds (0.5→0.6→0.7), plus a stagnation detector that resets to exploration after 80% low-reward steps in a 50-step window. A Dual-Memory Integration System pairs a fixed-capacity working memory with an episodic store that encodes experiences only when a composite surprise value (reward prediction error + observation prediction error + phase-transition indicator) exceeds a moving-median threshold, and retrieves top-5 memories via a weighted cosine-similarity-plus-phase-match score. An Uncertainty-Modulated Prediction Regulator computes a weighted sum of observation, reward, and reward-trend prediction errors to produce a scalar uncertainty that shifts the jump threshold up or down, encouraging broader exploration under high uncertainty and tighter exploitation under low uncertainty. The world model, actor, and critic are all conditioned on the current phase, and the full system is trained end-to-end for 1×10⁶ environment steps on MineDojo tasks.

## Results

On the MineDojo benchmark, CPP achieves an average success rate of 62.33% across five resource collection tasks, outperforming LS-Imagine (57.73%) by an average of 4.6% and DreamerV3 (42.17%) by 20.2%. The largest gains appear in the obtain wool task (69.33% vs. 56.67% for LS-Imagine, a 22.3% relative improvement) and obtain water (80.33% vs. 75.67%, a 6.2% relative improvement). CPP also reduces average steps-to-first-success by 7.1% compared to LS-Imagine, with the most notable reduction in the cut-a-tree task (340.48 vs. 363.27 steps, a 6.3% reduction). The only task where CPP underperforms is mine iron ore (12.00% vs. DreamerV3's 16.79%), where DreamerV3's fixed exploration strategy better suits enclosed, low-visibility environments.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CPP outperforms LS-Imagine (the closest baseline and codebase foundation) on 4 of 5 tasks: cut a tree (90.00% vs. 88.33%), obtain water (80.33% vs. 75.67%), obtain sand (60.00% vs. 57.33%), and obtain wool (69.33% vs. 56.67%), with the wool task showing the largest relative gain of 22.3%. Against DreamerV3, CPP wins on 4 of 5 tasks but loses on mine iron ore (12.00% vs. 16.79%), where DreamerV3's consistent exploration in enclosed spaces proves advantageous. CPP substantially outperforms STEVE-1 and VPT across all tasks, with STEVE-1 achieving 0% on iron mining and VPT failing on most tasks entirely. In terms of sample efficiency (steps to first success), CPP reduces steps by 6.3% over LS-Imagine on cut-a-tree and 18.2% on obtain wool, though DreamerV3 remains more efficient on sand collection (548.76 vs. 567.84 steps) and iron mining (1789.06 vs. 1867.53 steps) due to its fixed exploration being well-suited to homogeneous, static resource identification.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LS-Imagine (ICLR 2025) framework for jumpy state transitions in open-world RL by layering three 'cognitive' modules—phase-adaptive task decomposition, dual working/episodic memory, and uncertainty-modulated prediction—on top of the DreamerV3 RSSM world model architecture. The key conceptual departure from prior model-based RL (DreamerV3, LS-Imagine, STEVE-1, VPT) is the argument that fixed exploration parameters and static jump thresholds are the bottleneck, and that human-inspired adaptive phase transitions and selective memory retrieval can address this. The cognitive neuroscience framing (predictive coding, complementary learning systems, hierarchical phase decomposition) is the main positioning novelty, though the underlying mechanisms are threshold-based controllers and weighted linear uncertainty combinations rather than full Bayesian or neurobiologically faithful models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine limitation in model-based open-world RL—static exploration parameters that cannot adapt across task phases—and demonstrates that structured phase decomposition with adaptive parameters yields consistent (if modest) gains across diverse MineDojo tasks. However, the improvements are incremental (4.6% average success rate, 7.1% step reduction), the cognitive neuroscience connection remains largely metaphorical rather than mechanistically grounded, and the three-phase decomposition may not generalize to highly unstructured or multi-stage tasks. Open questions include whether the phase-adaptive controller's benefits persist in longer-horizon or adversarial settings, whether the dual-memory system scales beyond the tested task complexity, and whether the 'cognitive' framing provides actionable design principles beyond 'tune hyperparameters per phase.'
