## Problem

Existing automatic 3D bounding box annotation pipelines for autonomous driving uniformly fit boxes to all objects regardless of their physical state (static vs. dynamic, rigid vs. deformable), and rely on multiple self-training iterations for refinement, resulting in suboptimal annotation quality and substantial computational overhead. Additionally, prior multi-modal approaches either fuse modality-specific boxes without geometric alignment or fail to fully exploit 2D visual semantics to improve 3D annotation fidelity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251175/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes time-synchronized multi-camera RGB images, LiDAR point clouds, ego poses, and free-form text prompts (e.g., 'car', 'pedestrian', 'dog')—no manual 3D annotations are required. It outputs per-instance 3D bounding boxes with class labels and instance IDs, where the box geometry is adaptively determined by the object's physical type: static rigid objects receive SDF-refined multi-frame boxes, dynamic rigid objects receive trajectory-aligned single-frame boxes, and deformable objects receive tightly-fitted single-frame boxes. Annotation is restricted to the camera frustum field of view for the Waymo benchmark.

## Method

OpenBox is a two-stage annotation pipeline. Stage 1 (Cross-modal Instance Alignment) uses Grounding DINO for open-vocabulary 2D detection and SAM2 for instance segmentation, then unprojects 2D masks onto LiDAR points via camera calibration; a context-aware refinement step fuses these unprojected points with HDBSCAN clusters from ground-removed LiDAR using bidirectional proximity voting to discard background noise. Stage 2 (Adaptive 3D Bounding Box Generation) first classifies each instance as static-rigid, dynamic-rigid, or deformable using ChatGPT for rigidity and PP score for motion, then applies type-specific box fitting: static-rigid instances undergo SDF-based surface reconstruction with vertex-level foreground/background voting for noise suppression followed by 3D-2D IoU alignment to select the optimal box from two orientation candidates; dynamic-rigid instances are oriented along their trajectory and extended via visibility-based ray-normal dot products; deformable instances use single-frame closeness-to-edge fitting. The entire pipeline requires no self-training iterations.

## Results

On the Waymo Open Dataset (WOD) validation set, OpenBox achieves 70.49% AP₃D for the vehicle class at IoU 0.5 under the camera-frustum setting, substantially outperforming the prior state-of-the-art CPD (30.30%). On the Lyft dataset, OpenBox attains 43.3% AP₃D over 0–80 m, surpassing LiSe (27.5%) by +15.8 points, and in the direct annotation-quality comparison (Scenario 2) it exceeds LiSe by +19.94% AP₃D (26.25 vs. 6.31). On nuScenes, OpenBox reaches 40.9 / 62.7 / 5.2 AP₃D for car / pedestrian / cyclist, improving over UNION by +10.8 / +21.1 / +5.2 points respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CPD [40] on WOD (360° setting), OpenBox wins decisively on vehicle (66.89 vs. 57.79 AP₃D @ IoU 0.5) and pedestrian (55.71 vs. 21.91), and also on cyclist (21.00 vs. 5.83), with the gap driven by CPD's class-agnostic tracking and stationary-only annotation. On the same dataset, OpenBox also outperforms MODEST [43] (66.89 vs. 18.51 vehicle), OYSTER [45] (66.89 vs. 30.48), and DBSCAN [7] (66.89 vs. 2.32) by wide margins across all three classes. On Lyft, OpenBox surpasses LiSe [46] in every distance bin, with the largest gain at 30–50 m (50.6 vs. 22.8 AP₃D) and 50–80 m (19.5 vs. 1.2), and in the annotation-quality comparison (Table 3) it leads LiSe by +19.94 AP₃D over 0–80 m. On nuScenes, OpenBox outperforms UNION [18] on all three classes (car 40.9 vs. 30.1, pedestrian 62.7 vs. 41.6, cyclist 5.2 vs. 0.0), with the pedestrian gap attributed to UNION's omission of point-cloud and box refinement. The only setting where OpenBox trails a baseline is the cyclist IoU 0.5 metric under the camera-frustum WOD protocol (2.15 vs. CPD's 3.08), which the authors attribute to the 'bicycle' prompt in Grounding DINO producing undersized boxes.

## Relation to Prior Work

OpenBox sits at the intersection of two research threads: unsupervised 3D object detection (CPD, MODEST, OYSTER) and open-vocabulary 2D perception (Grounding DINO, SAM2). Prior LiDAR-only methods perform class-agnostic clustering and rely on iterative self-training to refine boxes, while recent multi-modal approaches (LiSe, UNION) fuse modality-specific 3D boxes at the output level without geometric alignment. OpenBox departs from both by performing explicit cross-modal instance alignment (unprojecting 2D masks onto LiDAR) and by generating bounding boxes adaptively based on each instance's physical state (static rigid, dynamic rigid, deformable), eliminating the need for self-training iterations entirely.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By replacing costly manual annotation and iterative self-training with a single-pass pipeline that leverages off-the-shelf 2D foundation models, OpenBox substantially lowers the barrier to building open-vocabulary 3D datasets for autonomous driving. The physical-state-aware box generation addresses a gap that prior methods ignore, yielding more consistent training data for downstream detectors. Key limitations remain: adverse weather degrades 2D foundation model reliability, deformable objects (pedestrians, cyclists) still suffer from unstable full-extent estimation, and long-range LiDAR sparsity makes box fitting ill-conditioned. An open question is how well the pipeline generalizes to truly novel object categories beyond the 13 text prompts tested, and whether the ChatGPT-based size priors can be replaced with learned or retrieved statistics for broader coverage.
