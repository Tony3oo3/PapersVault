## Problem

Vision-Language Models (VLMs) rely solely on RGB inputs, which inherently limits their ability to capture precise spatial relationships such as relative positions and distances. Existing approaches that incorporate depth or point-cloud data either require specialized sensors like LiDAR or treat depth as a superficial supplementary input without leveraging its inferential value for higher-order spatial reasoning. This paper addresses the gap of how to transform raw depth into an interpretable, reasoning-oriented representation that VLMs can exploit for complex spatial understanding.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254131/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard RGB image (H×W×3) and a textual query as input; depth is estimated on-the-fly from the RGB image via the Depth Pro monocular depth estimator, requiring no additional hardware. The MIDI module produces 10 compact latent rationale tokens that are injected into the VLM's input sequence alongside the original image features and question, and the VLM then autoregressively generates a text answer. The plug-and-play design means the MIDI module can be attached to any existing VLM without modifying its architecture or retraining the VLM itself.

## Method

SSR introduces the MIDI (Mamba-based Image-Depth Interpreter) module, which encodes the RGB image with CLIP ViT-L/14 and the estimated depth map with SigLIP, then feeds both feature streams plus the text query into a 130M-parameter Mamba model that generates latent tokens representing intermediate spatial rationales. The key insight is a two-stage knowledge-distillation pipeline: in Stage 1, the Mamba module is trained to produce latent tokens that a frozen Qwen2.5-3B LLM can use to reconstruct the full textual rationale, thereby compressing verbose chain-of-thought reasoning into just 10 latent embeddings; in the optional Stage 2, MIDI and the VLM (Qwen2.5-VL) are jointly fine-tuned on answer generation. This distillation reduces inference from ~437 CoT tokens to ~2.6 tokens per sample while preserving spatial reasoning quality, and the resulting MIDI module can be plugged into existing VLMs in a training-free manner.

## Results

SSR (130M Mamba + 3B VLM) achieves 64.8 average on SpatialBench and 69.7 on SSRBENCH-Spatial, surpassing the Qwen2.5-VL-3B backbone by +5.4 and +20.9 respectively. The 7B variant reaches 67.0 on SpatialBench and 76.1 on SSRBENCH-Spatial, outperforming all open-source baselines and even GPT-4o-mini on spatial tasks. On general VQA benchmarks, SSR-3B improves VQAv2 by +6.5 (79.0) and GQA by +7.4 (63.6) over the backbone, confirming that spatial reasoning gains do not come at the cost of general capability. Inference efficiency is dramatically improved: SSR generates only 2.62 tokens per sample versus 437.28 for a text-CoT baseline, reducing per-sample inference time from 23.16s to 0.32s.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SSR-3B (130M Mamba + Qwen2.5-VL-3B) outperforms GPT-4o-mini on SSRBENCH-Spatial (69.7 vs. 46.1) and SSRBENCH-General (79.3 vs. 63.1), and beats Claude-3.5-Haiku on all spatial tasks. Against the backbone Qwen2.5-VL-3B, SSR achieves +5.4 on SpatialBench and +20.9 on SSRBENCH-Spatial, with the largest per-task gains on Position (+8.8) and Object (+22.8). The 7B variant (SSR-7B) surpasses Qwen2.5-VL-7B by +2.3 on SpatialBench and +22.5 on SSRBENCH-Spatial, and exceeds LLaVA-NeXT-13B on every SSRBENCH task. Compared to spatial-specialized baselines, SSR-3B beats SpatialBot-3B by +14.7 on SSRBENCH-Spatial and SpatialVLM-3B by +13.9, while also improving on general tasks (e.g., +21.5 on SSRBENCH-General over SpatialVLM). On general VQA, SSR-3B improves over Qwen2.5-VL-3B on all five benchmarks (VQAv2 +6.5, GQA +7.4, TextVQA +4.3, MMBench +2.4, POPE +1.6), confirming that the depth-reasoning module does not degrade general capability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the spatial-reasoning VLM line (SpatialVLM, SpatialBot, SpatialRGPT) by introducing an intermediate rationale layer: rather than fusing depth features directly into the VLM, SSR first converts monocular depth (via Depth Pro) into structured textual rationales, then distills those rationales into compact latent tokens using a Mamba-based module (MIDI). The latent-reasoning mechanism is closely related to Meteor [21] and LightThinker [26], which compress chain-of-thought into continuous representations, but SSR is the first to apply this paradigm specifically to depth-guided spatial reasoning and to make it plug-and-play (no VLM retraining required). The dataset construction builds on existing multimodal CoT corpora (LLaVA-CoT, Visual-CoT, VoCoT, SpatialQA) and augments them with spatial attributes mined by SpatialRGPT and GPT-4o.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play design (Stage 2 optional) means practitioners can enhance any existing VLM's spatial understanding with a 130M-parameter module without retraining the backbone, and the 72× reduction in generated tokens (2.62 vs. 437.28 per sample) while improving accuracy makes latent spatial reasoning practically deployable. The 1M+ sample SSR-COT dataset and the six-task SSRBENCH benchmark fill a gap in depth-aware spatial evaluation resources. Key limitations include evaluation restricted to the Qwen/Qwen-VL family, reliance on a single depth estimator (Depth Pro), and the absence of error bars or multi-seed runs, leaving open whether the gains generalize across VLM architectures and depth models.
