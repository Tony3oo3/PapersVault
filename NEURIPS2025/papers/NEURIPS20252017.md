## Problem

Existing few-shot adaptation methods for LLMs face a fundamental trade-off: in-context learning (ICL) avoids overfitting but introduces extra inference cost and lacks learnable parameters, while parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) methods like LoRA overfit on the small number of demonstration examples. The paper asks how to develop an adaptation method that avoids PEFT's overfitting while also overcoming ICL's lack of learnable parameters and additional inference overhead.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a small set of demonstration query-answer pairs (typically 3–20 per class) formatted as a prompt matrix Z_0 alongside a novel query, and outputs a predicted class label for text classification tasks. Crucially, the adaptation modifies only the existing LayerNorm (RMSNorm) scale vectors within the frozen LLM, introducing zero additional inference parameters or computational overhead compared to zero-shot inference, as confirmed by the measured inference times in Table 4.

## Method

OFA reinterprets the LLM forward pass as a sequence of preconditioned gradient descent steps, where each attention layer performs one update, and uses the LayerNorm scale vectors as learnable layer-wise preconditioning matrices that steer the internal optimization without adding new architectural components. Two theoretically motivated regularization terms are added to the cross-entropy loss: a step-ratio objective (Eq. 3) that penalizes oscillating or exploding updates to tighten convergence bounds, and a sharpness-minimization term (Eq. 4) that estimates the trace of the preconditioned Hessian via Hutchinson approximation to drive the optimization toward flat local minima. The total objective (Eq. 5) is trained end-to-end on the few-shot demonstration set, and the resulting preconditioners are fixed at inference time, yielding zero additional inference cost while achieving 4–10% improvements over the SOTA I2CL baseline across nine classification benchmarks.

## Results

On Llama2-7B, OFA achieves the best accuracy across all 9 classification benchmarks, with headline results of 95.84% on SST-2, 85.92% on TREC, 97.72% on DBPedia, and 88.40% on Subj. Compared to the strongest baseline I2CL, OFA delivers improvements of 4–10% (e.g., 95.84% vs. 87.68% on SST-2; 88.40% vs. 73.84% on Subj; 83.04% vs. 69.88% on HateSp18). OFA also introduces zero additional inference parameters (same as zero-shot), whereas ICL-based methods add 2MDL parameters and incur ~8.7s extra inference time on DBPedia.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OFA outperforms all seven baselines across every dataset on both Llama2-7B and Llama3-8B-Instruct. Against the strongest ICL-based method I2CL, OFA gains 4–10% (e.g., +8.16 on SST-2, +14.56 on Subj, +13.16 on HateSp18, +7.56 on DBPedia). Against the PEFT-based IA3, OFA leads by 2.56% on SST-2, 16.48% on Subj, and 10.60% on HateSp18, while also using fewer trainable parameters (0.27M vs. 33.55M for LoRA rank-1). Compared to standard few-shot ICL, OFA achieves the largest gains on Subj (+35.84%) and HateSp18 (+12.80%), where ICL's lack of learnable parameters is most limiting. Against LoRA (ranks 1–128), OFA consistently wins with lower variance, and the paper notes that LoRA's high variance stems from overfitting to the few demonstration samples. Soft-prompt and Label-anchor baselines are substantially outperformed (by 20–40% on several datasets), confirming that simple prompt-level adaptation is insufficient for few-shot tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work establishing that transformer forward passes implement gradient descent (Von Oswald et al., Ahn et al., Dai et al.) by introducing learnable layer-wise preconditioning matrices parameterized through existing LayerNorm/RMSNorm weights, rather than treating the update as unconditioned GD. It departs from PEFT methods (LoRA, IA3) that modify the gradient direction itself, instead only reshaping the preconditioner, and from ICL-based approaches (I2CL, task-vector, soft-prompt) that incur extra inference cost by storing or compressing demonstration tokens. The sharpness-aware objective, estimated via Hutchinson approximation of the preconditioned Hessian trace, is a novel adaptation of SAM-style regularization to the internal optimization dynamics of a frozen LLM.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

OFA offers a practically attractive middle ground: it achieves 4–10% gains over the SOTA ICL method (I2CL) while introducing zero additional inference parameters or tokens, matching zero-shot inference cost. The theoretical grounding (convergence via step-ratio minimization, generalization via flat-region seeking) provides a principled lens for understanding why few-shot adaptation works inside a frozen transformer. Open questions include whether the optimization interpretation and preconditioner learning generalize beyond classification to generation or multi-turn tasks, whether the Hutchinson-based sharpness estimation scales to very deep models without prohibitive training cost, and whether the local Taylor-expansion assumptions in the theorems hold in practice for highly non-convex LLM loss landscapes.
