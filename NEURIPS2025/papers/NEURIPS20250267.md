## Problem

Learning from Label Proportions (LLP) trains instance-level classifiers using only bag-level class proportions, but the weak supervision makes pseudo-labels inherently noisy, degrading downstream classification. Existing methods that rely on pseudo-labeling (e.g., L2P-AHIL, PLOT) lack a principled mechanism to distinguish reliable from unreliable pseudo-labels while simultaneously enforcing the bag-level proportion constraints during refinement.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250267/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes bags of unlabeled RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100 and SVHN, 64×64 for Mini-ImageNet) grouped into bags of size 16–256, where only the class proportion vector per bag is available as supervision. It also generalizes to tabular data (UCI Adult). The output is a per-instance class probability vector from which a hard label is extracted, with the constraint that aggregated predictions within each bag must match the known ground-truth proportions.

## Method

RLPL is a two-stage framework. Stage 1 pre-trains a ResNet-18 encoder via SimCLR contrastive learning, then trains a classifier head by minimizing KL divergence between predicted and true bag proportions, yielding initial (noisy) pseudo-labels. Stage 2 introduces LLP-OTD, which refines these pseudo-labels through two passes of entropy-regularized optimal transport (solved via Sinkhorn-Knopp) where the cost matrix incorporates an LLP-proportion penalty λ_OTD(1−p_{j(i),k}) that directly discourages assigning an instance to a class rare in its bag; instances whose initial and OT-refined labels agree form a high-confidence labeled set, while disagreements form an unlabeled set. The final classifier is then trained by LLPMix, a MixMatch-style semi-supervised pipeline that combines supervised cross-entropy on the labeled set, consistency regularization on the unlabeled set, and a novel LLP-consistency loss (KL divergence between batch-aggregated predictions and true bag proportions computed pre-MixUp), jointly optimizing instance-level and bag-level supervision.

## Results

On MiniImageNet, RLPL achieves the best average accuracy of 54.52% with the lowest coefficient of variation (CV=0.171), substantially outperforming L2P-AHIL (38.39%, CV=0.627). On CIFAR-100, RLPL attains the highest average accuracy (67.67%) and lowest variability (CV=0.021), surpassing L2P-AHIL (65.65%, CV=0.322). On CIFAR-10, RLPL reaches 93.71% average accuracy (CV=0.007), slightly behind L2P-AHIL (94.21%) but with comparable stability. On SVHN, RLPL achieves 94.92% average accuracy with the lowest CV (0.002), though L2P-AHIL edges ahead at 97.64%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RLPL dominates on MiniImageNet (54.52% avg vs. 38.39% for L2P-AHIL, the next best) and CIFAR-100 (67.67% vs. 65.65% for L2P-AHIL), with dramatically lower CV values indicating far greater stability across bag sizes. On CIFAR-10, L2P-AHIL narrowly leads with 94.21% vs. RLPL's 93.71%, while FLMm (91.78%) and ROT (87.70%) trail further behind; however, RLPL's CV of 0.007 is the lowest among all competitive methods. On SVHN, L2P-AHIL (97.64%) and DLLP (96.06%) outperform RLPL (94.92%), but RLPL still achieves the lowest CV (0.002). Against older baselines (LLPFC, DLLP, LLP-VAT, ROT, SoftMatch), RLPL consistently wins by large margins on MiniImageNet and CIFAR-100, where those methods collapse at larger bag sizes. In the appendix, RLPL also outperforms Count Loss and GLWS on CIFAR-10 by 5–25 points depending on bag size, and achieves 77.57% on UCI Adult, surpassing ROT (72.82%) and L2P-AHIL (75.99%).

## Relation to Prior Work

RLPL sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: entropy-regularized optimal transport for LLP (OT-LLP, Liu et al. ICLR 2021) and MixMatch-style semi-supervised learning (Berthelot et al. NeurIPS 2019). Rather than using OT to directly match bag proportions as in OT-LLP, RLPL repurposes OT as a pseudo-label denoising mechanism, injecting an LLP-proportion penalty into the transport cost matrix to enforce bag-level consistency at the instance-prototype level. The semi-supervised stage (LLPMix) adapts MixMatch's augmentation-sharpening-MixUp pipeline by adding an explicit KL-divergence-based LLP consistency loss computed on pre-MixUp samples, a constraint absent from standard SSL. The contrastive pretraining (SimCLR) and the post-optimization consistency heuristic for confidence partitioning are additional departures from prior LLP methods that typically rely on entropy-based or single-pass pseudo-labeling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in RLPL's robustness: its consistently low coefficient of variation across bag sizes (e.g., 0.171 on MiniImageNet vs. 0.627 for the next-best method) makes it a more reliable choice when bag size is a hyperparameter that may vary in deployment. The framework also generalizes beyond vision to tabular data and maintains performance under noisy proportions and severe class imbalance, addressing practical concerns that prior LLP methods do not explicitly handle. Limitations include the multi-stage pipeline's added complexity, the fact that RLPL does not uniformly outperform the strongest baseline (L2P-AHIL) on easier datasets like SVHN, and the lack of open-source code. Open questions include whether the two-pass OT refinement can be made adaptive (e.g., stopping early when labels stabilize), extension to multi-label or hierarchical LLP settings, and theoretical characterization of the noise-filtering guarantee of the post-optimization consistency criterion.
