## Problem

In private federated learning, communication efficiency (via gradient sketching) and differential privacy (via the Gaussian mechanism) have been analyzed in isolation, with sketching treated purely as a compression tool and all privacy attributed to added noise. Prior work attempting to connect the two (e.g., DiffSketch) required impractical assumptions such as Gaussian-distributed inputs and sketch dimensions b ≲ √d, and could not precisely characterize the noise reduction from sketching. This paper asks: can sketching and the Gaussian mechanism be jointly analyzed to reveal that the sketching operation itself confers inherent, quantifiable privacy amplification?

## Input / Output

Each client holds a local dataset (EMNIST ByClass: 814K training / 140K test samples, 62 classes; SST-2: 67K training / 1821 test samples, 2 classes) and performs K local SGD steps on mini-batches of size 64. The per-round communication payload is a b-dimensional sketch (b = 4×10⁵ for the 42M-parameter ResNet-101 vision task, b = 2×10⁵ for the ~100M-parameter BERT-Base language task) rather than the full d-dimensional update, achieving ~1% and ~0.2% compression respectively. The server desketches the aggregated sketch via the transpose Rᵀ (a single O(bd) matrix-vector product) rather than the pseudo-inverse, and the final output is a global model with a provable client-level (ε, δ)-DP guarantee.

## Method

The Sketched Gaussian Mechanism (SGM) applies a Gaussian sketching matrix R ∈ ℝ^{b×d} (entries i.i.d. N(0, 1/√b)) to a clipped update and adds Gaussian noise ξ ~ N(0, σ_g² I_b), producing a b-dimensional output. The key theoretical contribution is a Rényi-DP analysis showing that the Rényi divergence between SGM outputs on neighboring datasets scales as O(α²τ⁴/((α−1)·b·σ_g⁴)), yielding an overall privacy bound ε = O(1/(√b · σ_g²))—so increasing the sketch dimension b strictly reduces the required noise for a fixed privacy target, unlike the standard Gaussian mechanism where the divergence is dimension-independent. Fed-SGM embeds SGM into a FedAvg-style loop: clients perform local SGD, clip updates to norm τ, apply SGM, and transmit sketches; the server aggregates, broadcasts, and clients desketch via Rᵀ before updating with a global optimizer (GD or AMSGrad/Adam). Convergence guarantees exploit the fast-decaying Hessian eigenspectrum of deep networks, yielding bounds that depend only logarithmically on the ambient dimension d and linearly on the small absolute intrinsic Hessian dimension 𝓘.

## Results

On EMNIST ByClass (ResNet-101, 42M params) at ε_p=1.60, Fed-SGM with Adam as the global optimizer achieves 85.09% test accuracy under IID data, substantially outperforming the unsketched Adam baseline (78.60%) and the unsketched GD baseline (63.34%). Under non-IID (Dirichlet α=0.05), Fed-SGM with Adam still leads at 52.55% vs. 51.42% for unsketched Adam. A key quantitative finding is the noise reduction: at the same privacy level, Fed-SGM requires a Gaussian noise scale of ~0.10 versus ~1.0 for the unsketched mechanism (e.g., σ_g=0.1013 vs. σ_g=1.0 at ε_p≈1.60 on EMNIST), confirming the theoretical 1/√b privacy amplification.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares Fed-SGM against two main baselines: DP-FedAvg [81] (the standard unsketched private FL algorithm) and DiffSketch [37] (a prior sketching-based private FL method). Against DP-FedAvg with GD, Fed-SGM with GD is marginally lower under IID (62.98% vs. 63.34%) but the gap narrows under non-IID; however, when Adam is used as the global optimizer, Fed-SGM with sketching decisively outperforms the unsketched Adam variant by 6.5 points under IID (85.09% vs. 78.60%) and by 1.1 points under non-IID (52.55% vs. 51.42%). Against DiffSketch, the paper reports that the Adam-based Fed-SGM variant consistently outperforms it across privacy levels, though exact DiffSketch numbers are not tabulated in the main text. The paper also shows that replacing Adam with AMSGrad yields nearly identical results (85.04% vs. 85.09% for sketching; 78.10% vs. 78.60% for non-sketching), confirming robustness to the specific adaptive optimizer choice.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously treated-as-separate threads in federated learning: Gaussian sketching for communication compression (Song et al. 2023, FetchSGD) and the Gaussian mechanism for differential privacy (Abadi et al. 2016, DP-SGD). Prior work such as DiffSketch (Li et al. 2019) identified that Count Sketch confers some privacy but under impractical assumptions (Gaussian inputs, b ≲ √d), while methods like D²P-Fed and DPSFL simply stitch a compressor and a privacy mechanism together without joint analysis. The key departure here is a tight Rényi-DP analysis showing that the sketching operation itself amplifies privacy by a factor of 1/√b, with no upper bound on b, thereby covering the practical regime where b is a fixed fraction of d.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 1/√b privacy amplification result reframes the privacy-communication tradeoff in FL: increasing the sketch dimension simultaneously reduces communication cost *and* strengthens privacy, a coupling that prior isolated analyses missed. Practically, Fed-SGM achieves the same (ε,δ)-DP guarantee with strictly less Gaussian noise than DP-FedAvg, translating to better model accuracy at fixed privacy budgets. The main limitation is that the analysis is restricted to isotropic Gaussian sketching matrices, leaving open whether comparable guarantees extend to structured or sparse sketches. The convergence bound's logarithmic dependence on ambient dimension d (via the absolute intrinsic Hessian dimension) is a useful theoretical guarantee for high-dimensional FL, though the empirical accuracy gains over unsketched baselines are modest and most visible with adaptive optimizers.
