## Problem

It remains unclear how the iterative inference hypothesis (each layer incrementally refines the next-token distribution) reconciles with the circuit hypothesis (distinct, specialized computational roles for specific components). While prior work documents individual phenomena at particular depths—induction heads, knowledge neurons, self-repair—no unified account explains how depth-dependent computations are organized across the full forward pass of a decoder-only LLM.

## Input / Output

The method operates on pre-trained decoder-only transformer weights (GPT-2, Pythia, Phi, Qwen 2.5, LLaMA 3.2; 124M–14B parameters) and 1M-token sequences sampled from the Pile dataset. Rather than modifying inputs, the method intervenes on the model architecture itself: each layer is either zero-ablated or swapped with its adjacent neighbor, and the resulting changes in KL divergence, top-1 accuracy, entropy, and benchmark scores are recorded per layer. Outputs include per-layer intervention curves, CKA similarity matrices, logit lens entropy/KL profiles, prediction/suppression neuron density maps, and a four-stage depth-aligned inference hypothesis.

## Method

The paper applies a systematic layer-wise intervention protocol—zero ablation and adjacent-layer swap—to every layer of 18 models across five families, revealing that middle layers are remarkably robust (72–95% accuracy retained) while early and final layers are catastrophically sensitive. This non-uniform sensitivity, combined with CKA block structure, motivates a four-stage hypothesis: (1) detokenization (early layers integrate local context via sub-word merging attention heads), (2) feature engineering (middle layers build semantically meaningful representations, evidenced by WiC probing accuracy peaking mid-model while logit lens entropy remains high), (3) prediction ensembling (prediction neurons appear around mid-depth, aggregating features into narrowing output distributions), and (4) residual calibration (suppression neurons dominate final layers, suppressing obsolete features and calibrating the output distribution, evidenced by rising MLP output norms and a layer-repeating experiment showing entropy reduction). The approach synthesizes behavioral evidence (intervention robustness, benchmark performance) with mechanistic evidence (neuron type classification via unembedding-weight kurtosis/skew, attention pattern analysis, linear probes) to bridge top-down circuit views and bottom-up iterative inference accounts.

## Results

Across 18 models spanning five families (GPT-2, Pythia, Phi, Qwen 2.5, LLaMA 3.2) from 124M to 14B parameters, models retain 72–95% of their original top-1 prediction accuracy after single-layer deletion or adjacent-layer swapping, with no fine-tuning. Interventions to the first layer are catastrophic (causing very high-entropy predictions), while middle layers show remarkable robustness to both deletion and swapping. The four-stage depth structure (detokenization → feature engineering → prediction ensembling → residual calibration) is observed consistently across all model families, with prediction neurons appearing around the midpoint and suppression neurons dominating the final ~10% of layers.

**Table 1: Four-stage hypothesis of inference in LLMs.**

| Stage | Name | Function | Observable Signatures |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Detokenization | Integrate local context to transform raw token representations into coherent entities | Catastrophic sensitivity to deletion/swapping; attention-heavy computation |
| 2 | Feature Engineering | Iteratively build feature representation depending on token context | Little progress toward next-token prediction; significant increase in probing accuracy and patching importance |
| 3 | Prediction Ensembling | Convert semantic features into plausible next-token predictions via iterative ensemble | Prediction neurons appear; output distribution begins to narrow |
| 4 | Residual Calibration | Eliminate obsolete features and finalize the top-1 output distribution | Suppression neurons appear; growing MLP-output norm; entropy increase in final layers

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not compare a proposed method against competing baselines; instead, it characterizes model behavior across architectures and intervention types. The key cross-architecture comparison shows that the four-stage pattern holds consistently across GPT-2, Pythia, Phi, Qwen 2.5, and LLaMA 3.2, despite architectural differences (e.g., Pythia uses parallel attention+MLP while GPT-2/Phi/LLaMA use sequential). Swapping adjacent layers is consistently less harmful than zero-ablating them for intermediate layers, suggesting partial commutativity of operations. The most performant Phi models exhibit nearly 15% prediction and 25% suppression neurons per layer—5–8× the density in GPT-2 and 3–7× that of Pythia—correlating with steeper KL-divergence drops and smoother convergence. Repeating blocks in the final 80–90% of depth reduces logit entropy and improves benchmark performance (HellaSwag, ARC-Easy, LAMBADA), supporting the residual calibration stage, whereas repeating earlier blocks does not.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper synthesizes the iterative inference hypothesis (logit lens analyses, self-repair, redundancy) with the circuit hypothesis (induction heads, knowledge neurons, universal neurons) into a unified four-stage depth-dependent framework. It directly builds on Gurnee et al.'s identification of prediction and suppression neurons [14] and the logit lens technique [35] as its primary analytical tools, while drawing on BERTology ablation studies and prior findings on mid-layer knowledge storage, activation sparsity transitions, and fine-tuning localization. The key departure is using layer-swap interventions (not just ablation) as a probe for commutativity and stage boundaries, and explicitly framing the result as a hypothesis bridging top-down and bottom-up views of transformer computation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The four-stage hypothesis (detokenization, feature engineering, prediction ensembling, residual calibration) offers a practical organizing principle for interpretability research, model compression (e.g., which layers to prune or quantize), and alignment auditing (where to intervene). The 72–95% accuracy retention under layer deletion has direct implications for efficient inference and model distillation. Key open questions include: whether stage boundaries sharpen with scale, whether the framework holds for non-decoder architectures or multimodal models, and whether a theoretical account can explain why gradient descent produces this particular depth structure. The authors acknowledge that stage boundaries are approximate, individual tokens may follow distinct paths, and model-specific differences (e.g., GPT-2's greater robustness) remain unexplained.
