## Problem

Existing audio super-resolution systems built on diffusion or bridge models rely on uninformative Gaussian priors (either full noise or inpainting-style removed regions), discarding the instructive spectral and temporal cues already present in the low-resolution waveform. Additionally, no prior work has demonstrated high-quality upsampling beyond 48 kHz (e.g., to 96 or 192 kHz), which is critical for professional audio post-production and immersive content creation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251623/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a low-resolution audio waveform at any sampling rate below the target, along with the detected prior frequency (SR_LR/2) and the desired target frequency (e.g., 24 kHz for 48 kHz output), both encoded as sinusoidal embedding tokens. It produces a full-band high-resolution waveform at 48 kHz, or via a cascaded two/three-stage pipeline, at 96 or 192 kHz. The LR waveform is first low-pass filtered at the prior frequency to suppress artifacts before being compressed into a continuous latent representation by a pre-trained convolutional VAE.

## Method

AudioLBM compresses the raw waveform into a continuous latent space via a convolutional VAE, then trains a Schrödinger Bridge model to learn a latent-to-latent generation trajectory from z^LR (informative Dirac prior) to z^HR (target), replacing the uninformative Gaussian prior of diffusion models. The noise-prediction network is a DiT backbone that receives the noisy latent, timestep, the LR latent as conditioning, and two frequency tokens (quantized target and continuous prior) prepended to the sequence. For beyond-48 kHz SR, cascaded LBMs progressively upsample (48→96→192 kHz), with prior augmentation via waveform-domain low-pass filtering near the Nyquist boundary and deterministic latent-space Gaussian blurring along the time axis to mitigate cascading errors. A frequency-aware any-to-any training paradigm samples both prior and target rates uniformly during training, enabling a single model to handle arbitrary input resolutions at inference.

## Results

On VCTK 8 kHz→48 kHz speech SR, AudioLBM achieves LSD 0.753, LSD(HF) 0.724, SSIM 0.893, and SigMOS 3.023, outperforming the strongest baselines (FlowHigh: LSD 0.816, SSIM 0.784; AudioSR: LSD 0.940, SSIM 0.809) across all objective metrics. Across general audio (ESC-50) and music (Song-Describer) at 8 kHz→44.1 kHz, AudioLBM reduces LSD by 22–23% and improves ViSQOL by 1.4–3.0 points over AudioSR. The paper reports an average 21.5% LSD improvement and 3.05% ViSQOL improvement over prior systems across audio and music domains, and sets the first record for any-to-192 kHz SR with LSD 1.365 (vs. 1.913 for a directly trained model).

## Baselines & Comparisons

AudioLBM (zero-shot) achieves the best LSD and SSIM across all nine speech baselines on VCTK 8→48 kHz, reducing LSD from 0.816 (FlowHigh, the closest competitor) to 0.753 and SSIM from 0.809 (AudioSR) to 0.893. The only metric where a baseline edges out the zero-shot model is SigMOS, where AP-BWE scores 3.082 vs. 3.023; however, the VCTK-only variant (Ours†) surpasses AP-BWE at 3.095. Against AudioSR—the primary scalable cross-domain baseline—AudioLBM consistently wins on both LSD and ViSQOL across all four input rates (8/12/16/24 kHz) on 48Audio, ESC-50, and Song-Describer, with the largest gap on music (SDS 8 kHz: LSD 1.338 vs. 1.728, a 22.5% reduction). Bridge-SR, the prior bridge-model work operating in waveform space, is outperformed by a wide margin (LSD 1.047 vs. 0.753), confirming the advantage of the latent-to-latent formulation. For 96/192 kHz SR, the cascaded AudioLBM outperforms directly trained any-to-96/192 kHz models by 0.19–0.55 LSD points, validating the multi-stage design.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Schrödinger Bridge models (Chen et al. 2023; Zhou et al. 2023) from text-to-speech and general data-to-data generation into audio super-resolution, operating in a continuous waveform latent space rather than the mel-spectrogram or STFT domains used by prior bridge-based SR work (Bridge-SR, A²SB). The key conceptual departure from both diffusion-based (AudioSR) and prior bridge-based methods is replacing the uninformative Gaussian prior with the LR waveform latent as a Dirac prior, making the generation trajectory inherently aligned with the LR-to-HR upsampling process. The frequency-aware conditioning and cascaded design with prior augmentation are novel extensions not present in any prior audio SR system.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first system to achieve audio super-resolution beyond 48 kHz (to 96 and 192 kHz), opening practical possibilities for mastering, spatial audio, and immersive content creation that were previously unaddressed. The frequency-aware any-to-any training paradigm directly addresses the data scarcity bottleneck for high-resolution audio, and the latent-to-latent bridge formulation provides a clean, generalizable template for other restoration tasks where the input is an informative prior. Open questions include whether the approach scales to even higher resolutions, whether the cascaded error accumulation can be further mitigated, and whether the technique transfers to other modalities (image, video, time series) as the authors note as future work. The lack of released code and reliance on internal datasets for 96/192 kHz evaluation limits immediate reproducibility.
