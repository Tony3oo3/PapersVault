## Problem

In multimodal learning, dominant modalities overshadow weaker ones during joint training, causing overfitting and sharp minima that hurt generalization. Existing gradient-modulation methods (e.g., AGM, OGM-GE) improve per-modality accuracy but converge to sharp, overfit solutions, and Sharpness-Aware Minimization (SAM) has never been adapted to the multimodal setting where modality-specific robustness must be selectively enforced.

## Input / Output

The method consumes multimodal inputs—audio spectrograms (257×299 for CREMA-D), video frames, and text passages—depending on the dataset, and outputs a single class label per sample. It operates entirely at the optimizer level without modifying the network architecture, supporting both early fusion (MAXOUT) and late fusion topologies with 2 or 3 modalities. Shapley-based loss decomposition is computed per mini-batch, so the dominant modality can shift dynamically across iterations.

## Method

M-SAM decomposes the total loss into modality-specific components using Shapley values at every iteration, identifies the dominant modality m₀ as the one with the largest contribution, and then applies SAM's perturbation step exclusively to m₀'s gradient while using standard gradient descent for all non-dominant modalities. The update rule is θₜ = θₜ₋₁ − ηₜ[∇L_{m₀}(θₜ₋₁ + εₜ^{m₀}) + Σ_{m≠m₀} ∇L_m(θₜ₋₁) + λθₜ₋₁], where εₜ^{m₀} = ρ∇L_{m₀}/‖∇L_{m₀}‖₂. This selective sharpness-aware update steers the dominant modality toward a wide, flat minimum so that inter-modality gradient interference in the shared parameter space cannot easily displace it, while non-dominant modalities retain full freedom to explore complementary features. The paper provides a convergence proof showing M-SAM achieves O(log T / √T) average gradient norm, matching SGD and standard SAM, and validates the approach on four datasets under both early and late fusion.

## Results

M-SAM achieves the highest multimodal accuracy (Acc_mm) across all four datasets in both late and early fusion settings. In late fusion, M-SAM reaches 74.08% on AV-MNIST, 68.56% on CREMA-D, 68.31% on UR-Funny, and 79.67% on AVE, outperforming the closest competitors (CGGM and Recon-Boost) by +1.4% to +2.3%. In early fusion, M-SAM achieves 74.48%, 68.43%, 67.92%, and 78.23% respectively, with gains of +1.3% to +2.3% over the next-best method. Compared to standard joint training, M-SAM yields absolute improvements of +5.7% to +7.4% in late fusion and +2.3% to +6.1% in early fusion.

## Baselines & Comparisons

M-SAM outperforms all eight baselines on multimodal accuracy across every dataset in both late and early fusion. The closest competitors are CGGM (73.42/67.03/67.43/77.83) and Recon-Boost (73.59/67.47/67.61/78.02) in late fusion, with M-SAM exceeding them by 0.5–1.6 points per dataset. AGM [23], the gradient-modulation baseline that M-SAM builds upon, is surpassed by 1.9–2.6 points, confirming that modality-aware sharpness minimization adds substantial value over adaptive gradient scaling alone. Standard SAM [7] without modality awareness trails M-SAM by 0.9–3.7 points, validating that the Shapley-based dominant-modality selection is critical. OGM-GE [28] and MSLR [38], which use modality-specific learning rates, are outperformed by 3.0–7.4 points, indicating that optimizer-level sharpness control is more effective than per-modality LR tuning. In early fusion (Table 2), M-SAM's advantage over CGGM and Recon-Boost narrows slightly (0.9–1.6 points) but remains consistent, while the gap over Joint-Train widens to 2.3–6.1 points, suggesting M-SAM is particularly effective when modalities interact in a shared feature space.

## Relation to Prior Work

M-SAM is the first application of Sharpness-Aware Minimization (SAM) to multimodal learning, combining three prior threads: SAM's flat-minima optimization (Foret et al., ICLR 2021), the Shapley-based per-modality loss decomposition introduced in AGM (Li et al., ICCV 2023), and the selective-SAM strategy for imbalanced classes from ImbSAM (Zhou et al., ICCV 2023). Unlike prior gradient-modulation methods (OGM-GE, AGM, CGGM, Recon-Boost) that adjust per-modality gradient magnitudes or directions, M-SAM operates purely at the optimizer level by restricting the sharpness-aware perturbation to the dominant modality's gradient while leaving non-dominant modalities under standard SGD updates. This positions the work as a principled bridge between optimization-theoretic generalization guarantees and the practical modality-imbalance problem in multimodal training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a persistent and practically important failure mode in multimodal systems—dominant-modality overfitting that degrades joint performance below unimodal baselines—without requiring architectural changes or hand-tuned loss weights. The convergence analysis (O(log T / √T), matching standard SAM) provides theoretical reassurance that the dynamic modality switching does not destabilize training. Open questions include the computational cost of per-batch Shapley computation as the number of modalities grows beyond three, the stability of dominant-modality identification in highly balanced settings, and whether the approach extends to generative or retrieval-based multimodal tasks where the loss landscape structure differs fundamentally.
