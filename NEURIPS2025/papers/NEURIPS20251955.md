## Problem

Existing evaluations of LLM strategic reasoning predominantly rely on Nash Equilibrium benchmarks that assume perfect rationality and assess only whether models reach equilibrium, without quantifying the depth or mechanism of their reasoning. This creates a circularity problem—using an optimality criterion to test rationality in inherently stochastic, probabilistic models—and obscures why models deviate from optimal play. The paper asks whether bounded strategic reasoning depth in LLMs can be measured in a cognitively grounded way that is free from NE's strong assumptions and reveals the underlying decision-making process.

## Input / Output

The framework consumes 13 abstracted matrix games (spanning competitive, cooperative, mixed-motive, sequential, Bayesian coordination, and signaling structures) presented as payoff tables, with optional demographic persona prompts across 10 socio-demographic groups and explicit CoT instructions layered on top. For each model-game pair, 30 independent trials are collected to build empirical choice frequency distributions. The primary output is a pair of estimated parameters—τ (average reasoning depth) and γ (decision precision)—recovered via maximum likelihood, supplemented by qualitative reasoning-style labels (maximin, belief-based, opponent-oriented) and OLS regression coefficients quantifying demographic sensitivity.

## Method

The core contribution is a TQRE-based estimation framework that models each LLM as a population of agents drawing reasoning levels k from a Poisson(τ) distribution, where a level-k agent forms beliefs by mixing lower-level opponents' strategies, computes expected utilities, and converts them to choice probabilities via a logit rule with precision λ_k = γk. Parameters (τ, γ) are estimated by maximizing the log-likelihood of observed 30-trial choice counts under the model-implied probability distribution, with the infinite level sum truncated at a sufficiently large K. This design avoids the circularity of NE-based evaluation by not presupposing optimal play, and τ directly quantifies the depth of recursive belief modeling the model engages in. The framework is applied to 22 SOTA LLMs, and the authors further analyze reasoning chains of top performers to identify dominant reasoning styles, and run OLS regressions with demographic indicators to surface identity-induced shifts in τ.

## Results

Across 13 matrix games, GPT-o1, GPT-o3-mini, and DeepSeek-R1 achieve the highest reasoning depth (τ), with GPT-o1 leading in 6 games (peak τ = 4.741 on Competitive-BL), GPT-o3-mini in 7 games (peak τ = 4.226 on Cooperation-HP), and DeepSeek-R1 in 5 games (peak τ = 4.226 on Cooperation-HP). Weaker models such as DeepSeek-V3 and LLaMA-3.1-405B frequently score below τ = 1.0, approaching the chance baseline. On the SW10 benchmark, six LLMs surpass the human reasoning depth estimated from Stahl & Wilson (1994) experimental data, though the paper cautions that higher τ does not necessarily indicate better alignment with human behavior.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary external baseline is human reasoning depth on the SW10 game, drawn from Stahl & Wilson's (1994) experimental data. Six of the 22 evaluated LLMs exceed the human τ on this benchmark, with GPT-o1, GPT-o3-mini, and DeepSeek-R1 among the strongest; however, the authors explicitly caution that a higher τ does not imply better human alignment, and several top-τ models (e.g., GPT-o1 with τ ≈ 0.14 on Mixed-Motive-BL) perform far below human levels on specific game types. Internally, the paper benchmarks all models against a uniform chance baseline (MLL_chance = −ln(mn)), and weaker models such as DeepSeek-V3 (τ ≈ 0.04–0.18 on most competitive/cooperation games) and LLaMA-3.1-405B (τ ≈ 0.001–0.99) score near or below this floor, indicating near-random play. Notably, model size does not predict performance: the 8B Granite-3.1-MoE achieves the highest τ (3.895) on Mixed-Motive-BL, outperforming all larger models, while the 405B LLaMA-3.1-405B ranks near the bottom on most games. Distilled R1 models (e.g., Qwen-32B) also outperform their much larger base counterparts on several games, reinforcing that architecture and training data matter more than raw parameter count.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Truncated Quantal Response Equilibrium (TQRE) framework from Rogers, Palfrey, and Camerer (2009) and Wright and Leyton-Brown (2010), which combine quantal response equilibrium with cognitive hierarchy levels to model bounded rationality in strategic settings. It departs from the dominant line of LLM strategic-reasoning evaluation that relies on binary Nash Equilibrium compliance checks (e.g., GTBench, Brookins & DeBacker) by replacing the NE pass/fail metric with a continuous reasoning-depth parameter (τ) estimated via maximum likelihood. The demographic-persona analysis extends the implicit-bias literature on LLMs (Gupta et al., 2023) from output-level fairness to the level of strategic reasoning depth itself, a dimension prior work has not quantified.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a cognitively grounded, continuous measure of strategic sophistication that avoids the circularity of using perfect rationality to test for rationality, and the finding that CoT prompting yields mixed (sometimes negative) effects on strategic reasoning challenges the widespread assumption that longer reasoning chains improve decision quality. The demographic analysis reveals that identity framing can shift reasoning depth in directionally inconsistent ways across models, raising fairness concerns that go beyond output bias into the reasoning process itself. Key limitations include the semi-dynamic (one-shot) game structure, only 30 trials per model-game pair, and abstract payoff matrices that may not capture the full complexity of real-world strategic interaction; extending to real-time multi-agent settings and establishing causal links between prompt features and reasoning depth remain open.
