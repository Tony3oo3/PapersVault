## Problem

Lu et al. [2025] introduced the first comparator-adaptive Φ-regret algorithm whose bound depends on a sparsity-based complexity measure c_φ, but their algorithm is complicated (relying on Haar-wavelet-inspired matrix features) and achieves a suboptimal bound with an extra √(c_φ d) additive term and an extra log d factor. This paper asks whether simpler algorithms can achieve tighter comparator-adaptive bounds and whether such bounds can be leveraged for accelerated equilibrium convergence in games.

## Input / Output

The learner receives a sequence of loss vectors ℓ_t ∈ [0,1]^d over T rounds and must output a distribution p_t ∈ Δ(d) over d experts each round. The key guarantee is a single regret bound Reg(φ) = O(√((1+c_φ log d)T) + √(T log log d)) that holds simultaneously for all transformations φ, where c_φ is a sparsity-based complexity measure. In the game extension, N players each run the algorithm and the empirical distribution of joint profiles converges to a Φ-equilibrium at rate O((max_φ c_φ · N log d + N² log d)/T), with no polylog(T) dependence.

## Method

The core insight is constructing a special prior π over all d^d binary transformations as a mixture of d+1 ψ-induced distributions, and proving that log(1/π(φ)) ≤ 2 + 2c_φ log d, reducing the problem to achieving prior-dependent external regret. Two efficient algorithms realize this: (1) a Kernelized MWU with a non-uniform prior (extending Farina et al.'s kernel to handle π) wrapped in a meta MWU that learns over 2⌈log₂d⌉ learning rates, achieving O(√(T log log d)) overhead; and (2) a prior-aware BM-reduction where each of d+1 copies uses a different ψ^k as the MWU prior, also wrapped in a meta MWU. The second approach's two-layer structure enables extension to N-player general-sum games by replacing MWU subroutines with Optimistic MWU, adding a uniform-prior OMWU base learner to exploit nonnegative-social-external-regret, and introducing a stability correction term in the meta update to bound path-length independently of T.

## Results

The paper achieves a comparator-adaptive Φ-regret bound of Reg(φ) = O(√((1 + c_φ log d)T) + √(T log log d)) via the Kernelized MWU approach (Algorithm 2) and Reg(φ) = O(√((1 + c_φ log d)T) + √(T log d)) via the BM-reduction approach (Algorithm 7), both for all φ ∈ S simultaneously. For N-player general-sum games (nonnegative-social-external-regret class), the game algorithm achieves Reg_n(φ) = O(c_φ N log d + N² log d), yielding an O((max c_φ N log d + N² log d)/T) convergence rate to Φ-equilibria. When specialized to standard regret notions, the bounds exactly recover the minimax rates: O(√(T log d)) for external and internal regret (c_φ = 1) and O(√(dT log d)) for swap regret (c_φ = d).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary theoretical comparison is with Lu et al. [2025], whose bound is O(√(c_φ(T+d)(log d)³)). This paper removes both the extra Õ(√(c_φ d)) additive term (from the (T+d) factor) and one full log d factor, yielding the tighter O(√((1+c_φ log d)T)) dependence. For the standard regret notions (external, internal, swap), the paper's bounds exactly match the known minimax rates, whereas Lu et al. [2025] exhibit a slight gap due to the extra (log d)² and √d terms. In the game setting, the paper's CCE rate of O(N log d / T) matches the best known rate for nonnegative-social-external-regret games (e.g., OMWU), while for CE it improves over Anagnostides et al. [2022b] on the d-dependence (dN log d vs. Nd²·⁵) and removes all polylog(T) factors present in both Anagnostides et al. [2022a,b]. The key trade-off is an additive N² log d term for non-CCE equilibria, which the authors identify as a future direction to remove.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Lu et al. [2025], who introduced comparator-adaptive Φ-regret with a sparsity-based complexity measure c_φ but relied on complex Haar-wavelet-inspired matrix features. The key conceptual departure is replacing that machinery with a simple prior distribution over binary row-stochastic matrices, reducing the problem to prior-dependent external regret solvable by standard MWU. The two concrete algorithms extend well-known building blocks—kernelized MWU from Farina et al. [2022b] (generalized from uniform to non-uniform prior) and the BM-reduction from Blum and Mansour [2007] (made prior-aware)—wrapped in a meta-MWU over learning rates following the standard parameter-free learning paradigm. For the game application, the paper borrows the two-layer predictive-loss framework of Zhang et al. [2022] and the optimistic MWU acceleration line from Daskalakis et al. [2021] and Anagnostides et al. [2022a,b], but restricts to nonnegative-social-external-regret games to handle the two-layer structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper delivers the first accelerated (polylog-free in T) and simultaneously adaptive (in c_φ) convergence rate to Φ-equilibria, a combination that had not been achieved before; for correlated equilibria it improves the d-dependence over Anagnostides et al. [2022b] and removes all polylog(T) factors. The prior-based reduction is a clean, reusable conceptual tool that could simplify other comparator-adaptive or distribution-dependent regret problems. Key limitations include the nonnegative-social-external-regret assumption for the game result (excluding general-sum games with negative social external regret) and an additive N²log d term for non-CCE equilibria whose removal is left open. Whether the prior-based idea extends to other complexity measures or to the first (kernelized) approach in the game setting remains an open question.
