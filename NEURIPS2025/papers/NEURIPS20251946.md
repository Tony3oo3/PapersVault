## Problem

Existing vision-language models rely on superficial statistical correlations and fail to capture the underlying causal logic between visual and textual content, particularly when minimal but semantically critical edits (e.g., changing one object attribute or reversing a causal relation) alter the meaning of a scene. Prior contrastive fine-tuning methods focus on binary matching discrimination rather than understanding which specific causal attributes govern semantic alignment, leaving VLMs vulnerable to counterfactual perturbations and visual hallucinations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251946/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework consumes RGB images (224×224 for CLIP-based backbones; 336×336 or 448×448 for LLM-based VLMs like Qwen-VL and LLaVA-1.5) paired with natural-language captions, and produces L2-normalized d-dimensional embeddings whose cosine similarity drives retrieval rankings, zero-shot classification, and compositional reasoning scores. Unusually, each training batch is structured around an anchor factual pair augmented with K complete counterfactual scenario pairs (both image and text jointly edited) and J minimally edited counterfactual images (single atomic intervention on the image, paired with the original text as a hard negative), maintaining a 1:1 factual-to-counterfactual ratio.

## Method

CF-VLM fine-tunes a pretrained dual-encoder VLM with three complementary objectives: a symmetric InfoNCE alignment loss (L_align) preserving foundational cross-modal matching; a counterfactual scenario discrimination loss (L_csd) using a hinge formulation to enforce that the anchor pair's similarity exceeds every complete counterfactual scenario pair by margin m₁; and a fine-grained causal discrimination loss (L_fcd) that targets only the hardest minimally edited counterfactual image (the one with highest similarity to the anchor text) to sharpen sensitivity to single-attribute or causal-relation perturbations by margin m₂. Counterfactual images are generated via a fine-tuned SDXL 1.0 Base + Refiner pipeline (40+15 diffusion steps) with spatial masking and cross-attention control, while counterfactual texts are produced by Qwen2-72B-Instruct using 3-shot chain-of-thought prompting that modifies exactly one attribute or causal link. The key insight is that the model must learn not just whether a pair matches but why—identifying the causal decision points where minimal interventions flip semantic validity—rather than merely separating matching from non-matching pairs as in standard triplet or InfoNCE objectives.

## Results

On CLIP-based models (ViT-B/32, CC12M), CF-VLM achieves 59.13% on ConMe (Avg), 89.35% on ARO (Avg), and 88.4% on VL-Checklist (Avg), outperforming the strongest CLIP baseline COGT-CLIP by +1.2, +1.6, and +4.4 points respectively. On LLM-based Qwen-VL 7B, CF-VLM reaches 87.57% ConMe, 93.2% ARO, and 90.57% VL-Checklist, improving over Standard Fine-tuning by +4.9, +6.45, and +2.7 points. On hallucination benchmarks, CF-VLM yields marginal but consistent gains over Standard FT on POPE (0.5–1.2 pp across accuracy/precision/recall/F1) and notable improvements on MME color (+2.0) and position (+1.4) subtasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CF-VLM (ViT-B/32) outperforms all CLIP-based baselines on every compositional reasoning benchmark: it beats COGT-CLIP (the strongest prior method) by +1.2 on ConMe, +1.6 on ARO, and +4.4 on VL-Checklist, and surpasses TripletCLIP by +2.6/+9.35/+6.0 on the same metrics. Against the zero-shot CLIP baseline, CF-VLM gains +9.4 on ConMe, +28.55 on ARO, and +17.1 on VL-Checklist, demonstrating that counterfactual fine-tuning substantially closes the gap to LLM-based models. On the LLM-based Qwen-VL 7B, CF-VLM improves over Standard FT by +4.9 (ConMe) and +6.45 (ARO), and over TextNeg FT by +3.4 and +5.7, respectively. CF-VLM also surpasses InstructBLIP (63.07 ConMe) and MiniGPT-4 (74.73 ConMe) by large margins on Qwen-VL, while on LLaVA-1.5 it consistently outperforms both Standard FT and TextNeg FT across all three benchmarks. The gains are most pronounced on ARO (attribute-relation-object discrimination), suggesting that counterfactual supervision is particularly effective for fine-grained relational and attribute-level reasoning.

## Relation to Prior Work

CF-VLM extends the CLIP-style dual-encoder contrastive learning paradigm by introducing a three-loss training objective that operates at multiple semantic granularities: foundational alignment (InfoNCE), counterfactual scenario discrimination (hinge loss on jointly-edited image-text pairs), and fine-grained causal discrimination (hinge loss on minimally-edited images paired with original text). It departs from prior counterfactual approaches—CPL (text-only prompt perturbations), Goyal et al. (image edits for interpretability), and TripletCLIP (rule-based text negatives)—by jointly generating controlled counterfactuals in both modalities via SDXL and LLM-based rewriting, and by explicitly targeting 'causal decision points' rather than generic hard negatives. The framework is architecture-agnostic, demonstrated on both CLIP ViT-B/32 and LLM-based VLMs (Qwen-VL, LLaVA-1.5).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, plug-in fine-tuning recipe that consistently improves compositional reasoning (2–5 points over strong baselines like TripletCLIP and COGT-CLIP) across multiple VLM architectures, suggesting counterfactual supervision is a broadly applicable signal rather than an architecture-specific trick. The secondary finding that counterfactual training modestly reduces visual hallucinations (0.5–1.2 points on POPE, up to +2.0 on MME color/position subtasks) hints at a connection between fine-grained causal sensitivity and factual grounding, though the gains are small and the mechanism is not fully explained. Open questions include whether the SDXL-generated counterfactual images introduce distributional artifacts that limit real-world transfer, whether the approach scales to more complex multi-step causal chains, and whether the 20–25% additional training cost is justified in resource-constrained settings.
