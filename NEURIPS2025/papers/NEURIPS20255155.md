## Problem

Existing learned methods for processing neural network gradients use architectures not purpose-built for gradient data: they either ignore parameter permutation symmetries, process only a single averaged gradient (losing curvature information), or use full-size weight-shaped gradients that scale poorly. This limits their ability to approximate curvature-aware algorithms like natural gradient descent or pruning saliency scores, which fundamentally require statistics across multiple per-datapoint gradients.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255155/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a set of b per-datapoint gradients, each decomposed into rank-1 factors (activation vector a^(l) and pre-activation gradient g^(l) per layer), forming a tensor g ∈ Γ_b with shape b × d_l × 2 per layer. This rank-1 representation scales linearly in the number of neurons rather than quadratically as full weight-shaped gradients would. Outputs are task-dependent: parameter deltas Δθ for optimization or INR editing, the FIM diagonal for curvature estimation, or an invariant scalar for influence functions, all produced through a G_b-equivariant mapping.

## Method

GradMetaNet is constructed from simple equivariant building blocks enabled by the rank-1 gradient decomposition, which decouples the permutation symmetry so that each neuron space is permuted independently (unlike weight space, where row and column permutations are coupled). The pipeline applies sinusoidal positional encoding, then a stack of interactions-across-sets layers (L_Γ_b) operating jointly over the batch and neuron dimensions, an S_b-invariant/G-equivariant pooling layer that removes the batch dimension, DeepSets layers (L_Γ) on the pooled representation, and a product layer (L_Prod) that maps neuron-level features back to weight space via pointwise MLPs. An attention-based variant (GradMetaNet++) replaces summation with multi-head attention across both batch and neuron dimensions. The paper proves a universal approximation theorem for continuous G_b-equivariant functions on compact domains (excluding a measure-zero set), and formally shows that functions like natural gradient approximations and pruning saliency scores cannot be reconstructed from the average gradient alone, establishing the necessity of processing gradient sets.

## Results

On learned optimization, GradMetaNet-based optimizers achieve up to 4.63× step reduction vs. Adam on CIFAR-10 (DS+GradMetaNet) and 1.82× on LM1B (UNF+GradMetaNet), consistently outperforming DeepSets and UNF baselines across all four tasks. For curvature estimation, GradMetaNet++ achieves a 26.3% improvement over the standard FIM diagonal approximation from 128 gradients, while GradMetaNet achieves 13.7%. On INR editing, WS+GradMetaNet++ reduces MSE to 1.60×10⁻² on MNIST dilation and 3.00×10⁻³ on CIFAR-10 contrast, improving over the best prior weight-space method (ScaleGMN) by up to 22.5%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GradMetaNet consistently outperforms DeepSets [91] across all learned-optimization tasks: on CIFAR-10, DS+GradMetaNet achieves 4.63× step reduction vs. DS alone at 2.32×, and on CIFAR-100, 3.15× vs. 1.79×. Against UNF [92], GradMetaNet wins on F-MNIST (1.51× vs. 1.16×) and LM1B (1.82× vs. 1.48×), but ties on CIFAR-10 (both 2.64×) and is not reported for CIFAR-100. For INR editing, GradMetaNet++ improves over all three weight-space backbones: DWS [61] (2.29→1.95 on MNIST, 5.57→3.86 on CIFAR-10), GMN [41] (1.96→1.71 on MNIST, 5.09→2.99 on CIFAR-10), and ScaleGMN [38] (1.99→1.60 on MNIST, 5.23→3.00 on CIFAR-10), with the largest relative gain (22.5%) on CIFAR-10 contrast editing. The paper also shows that baselines using only the average gradient (DWS+Average, GMN+Average) or full gradients without rank-1 decomposition (DWS+Concat, GMN+Concat) underperform GradMetaNet on curvature estimation, confirming the importance of processing sets of decomposed gradients.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the weight-space equivariant architecture line (Navon et al. 2023, Kofinas et al. 2024, Lim et al. 2023, Zhou et al. 2024) by shifting the processing domain from full parameter-shaped gradients to rank-1 decomposed gradient factors, which exhibit a simpler symmetry structure (independent per-layer permutations rather than coupled row-column permutations). It combines this with set-based equivariant building blocks (interactions-across-sets from Hartford et al. 2018, DeepSets from Zaheer et al. 2017) to process collections of per-datapoint gradients rather than a single averaged gradient, and integrates with the learned-optimizer meta-training framework of Harrison et al. 2022 and Zhou et al. 2024. The key conceptual departure is that the rank-1 decomposition not only reduces computational cost from quadratic to linear in neuron count but also decouples the symmetry group across layers, enabling universality proofs that remain out of reach for weight-space models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first principled equivariant architecture specifically designed for gradient processing, with matching theoretical guarantees (universality on compact domains, impossibility results for single-gradient methods) that ground the design choices. Practically, the rank-1 decomposition offers a linear-scaling representation that could make learned curvature-aware optimization feasible at larger scales than current weight-space approaches. However, the current implementation is limited to MLPs and transformers, the attention-based GradMetaNet++ variant does not scale to very large models, and the learned-optimizer experiments use relatively small architectures (MLPs on CIFAR, small transformers on LM1B), leaving open whether the approach transfers to state-of-the-art LLMs or generative models where the practical impact would be greatest.
