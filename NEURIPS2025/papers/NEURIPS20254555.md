## Problem

Class imbalance in semi-supervised node classification causes message passing to push minority-class node embeddings away from their true class centers in the Riemannian manifold space, creating geometric ambiguity that makes pseudo-labeling unreliable. Existing self-training methods for imbalanced graphs lack a principled theoretical account of this embedding-space distortion, and prior work on topology imbalance addresses a fundamentally different phenomenon defined on the raw graph structure rather than the learned embedding geometry.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an undirected graph with node feature vectors and a small labeled subset exhibiting class imbalance (ratios from ρ=10 to ρ≈775), and outputs class labels for all nodes. GNN embeddings are L2-normalized onto the unit hypersphere before being fed into a von Mises-Fisher classifier, and the framework is backbone-agnostic, operating identically on GCN, GAT, and GraphSAGE encoders. The self-training loop iteratively selects pseudo-labeled nodes from the unlabeled pool, with the candidate set shrinking as geometrically ambiguous nodes are filtered out.

## Method

UNREAL is a self-training framework that mitigates geometric imbalance through three complementary modules. First, a DualPath PseudoLabeler generates pseudo-labels via both k-means clustering (with K>C clusters mapped to classes by centroid proximity) and supervised classification, retaining only nodes whose labels agree across both pathways (DPAM intersection). Second, Node-Reordering adaptively fuses geometric-proximity rankings with classifier-confidence rankings per class, using Rank-Biased Overlap (RBO) as a dynamic weight so that geometric criteria dominate in early iterations when embeddings are unstable and confidence gains influence as training matures. Third, a lightweight DGIS module computes a GI index as the relative gap between a node's distance to its closest and second-closest class centroid, discarding nodes below a threshold to eliminate geometrically ambiguous samples without requiring ground-truth labels. The theoretical foundation formalizes geometric imbalance on the unit hypersphere via vMF posteriors, proving that average prediction entropy scales with the intra-class/inter-class distance ratio and that the expected geometric imbalance of minority nodes grows monotonically with the class imbalance ratio.

## Results

On the ρ=10 GCN setting (Table 2), UNREAL achieves 78.33% bAcc and 76.44% F1 on Cora, 65.63% bAcc and 64.94% F1 on CiteSeer, and 75.35% bAcc and 73.65% F1 on PubMed, outperforming the strongest baseline (BIM) by +6.14/+3.77, +6.35/+8.13, and +0.73/+0.72 respectively. Under severe imbalance (ρ=100, SAGE), gains remain substantial: +5.72 bAcc on Cora and +6.04 on CiteSeer. On the large-scale Ogbn-arxiv (ρ≈775.4), UNREAL achieves 51.21–51.84% bAcc across GCN/GAT/SAGE, while several baselines (GraphSMOTE, ReNode, GraphENS) encounter out-of-memory failures.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UNREAL consistently outperforms all nine baselines across every dataset and imbalance ratio tested. The largest margins appear on CiteSeer at ρ=10, where UNREAL gains +6.35 bAcc and +8.13 F1 over the best baseline (GSR at 59.28/55.96), and on Cora at ρ=100 (SAGE) with +5.72 bAcc over BIM. Among self-training baselines, GSR and BIM are the strongest competitors, but UNREAL surpasses them by 3–8 points in F1 on moderate-imbalance settings. Loss-reweighting methods (RW, PS, BS) and oversampling methods (GS, GE) show progressively larger gaps as imbalance severity increases, with GS and GE becoming inapplicable (OOM) on large-scale datasets like Flickr and Ogbn-arxiv. On naturally imbalanced datasets (Computers-Random ρ≈17.7, CS-Random ρ≈41.0), UNREAL achieves the best bAcc across all three GNN backbones (e.g., 88.94% GCN on CS-Random vs. 88.36% for PS), confirming generalizability beyond synthetic imbalance constructions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the self-training paradigm for imbalanced node classification (GraphSR, BIM, IceBerg) and the topology-imbalance literature (ReNode, GraphENS, TAM), but deliberately carves out a distinct problem: geometric imbalance in the Riemannian manifold embedding space, as opposed to topology imbalance on the raw graph structure. It draws on the von Mises-Fisher distribution framework for hyperspherical classification and message-passing GNN theory to formalize how class imbalance propagates through neighborhood aggregation into positional ambiguity of minority-class nodes. The UNREAL framework repurposes standard tools—k-means clustering, Rank-Biased Overlap, and a lightweight distance-ratio filter—into a unified pipeline specifically targeting this geometric ambiguity, rather than the structural or loss-level biases addressed by prior methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first formal theoretical link between class imbalance ratio, message-passing-induced embedding distortion, and pseudo-label entropy, giving practitioners a principled diagnostic (the GI index) for when self-training will fail on minority classes. The GNN-vs-MLP contrast (geometric imbalance is amplified by message passing but negligible without it) clarifies a previously implicit assumption in the literature. Limitations include the theoretical proofs relying on significant simplifications (ignoring nonlinearity, assuming fixed class centers, using first-order approximations), and the framework's three-component design adds hyperparameter sensitivity (k', γ) that may complicate deployment. The authors note future extension to link prediction, and the negative SAGE results on Cora at ρ=100 suggest the approach is not uniformly robust across all architecture-imbalance combinations.
