## Problem

When LLM-generated synthetic responses are mixed with human-preferred data for preference alignment, the resulting training distribution diverges from the target human-preferred distribution, causing models to overfit to synthetic artifacts rather than genuine human intent. Existing robust alignment methods are tightly coupled to the Bradley-Terry pairwise model and do not generalize to the growing diversity of alignment objectives (listwise, rank-based, etc.), leaving a gap for a method-agnostic framework that mitigates mixture-induced distribution shifts across arbitrary preference optimization formulations.

## Input / Output

The method consumes preference data structured as tuples of one text query and n candidate responses, where responses are drawn from a mixture of the target human-preferred distribution (fraction α) and one or more synthetic LLM distributions. A BERT-based classifier is trained per sub-distribution to output a probability score per response, which is aggregated into a single scalar calibration factor h̃(z) ∈ (0, n) per sample. The output is a modified alignment loss that reweights each sample's contribution to the baseline objective, producing optimized model parameters that generate text responses better aligned with the target distribution.

## Method

DoRA operates as a two-phase, method-agnostic plug-in. In Phase 1, probabilistic classifiers are trained for each sub-distribution to estimate the likelihood that a response originates from the golden (human-preferred) distribution; these classifier outputs are combined with mixture proportions to yield a per-sample calibration factor h̃(z) that amplifies responses aligned with the target and suppresses synthetic artifacts. In Phase 2, this fixed calibration term is embedded into a KL-divergence-constrained DRO objective, which admits a closed-form dual solution: λ log E_P[exp(h̃(z)·ℓ(θ,z)/λ)], where ℓ is any baseline alignment loss (DPO, RRHF, LIRE, etc.). The key insight is that h̃(z) acts as a soft relevance indicator, so samples with high loss but low alignment to the target distribution are downweighted, preventing over-pessimism from noisy or mislabeled synthetic points while preserving the robustness benefits of worst-case optimization.

## Results

On the primary listwise setting (4 responses per query), DoRA consistently improves win rates across baselines: LIRE on HH-RLHF with Mistral-7B jumps from 72.8% to 84.0% win rate (+11.2 pp), and DPO_PL on Summarization with Llama-8B improves from 54.5% to 59.0% (+4.5 pp). On the pairwise HH-RLHF benchmark, DPO w/ DoRA achieves the lowest lose rate (21.4% Mistral, 20.9% Llama) among all robust baselines including Robust DPO and Dr.DPO. On AlpacaEval 2 (UltraFeedback), LIRE w/ DoRA reaches 31.28% length-controlled win rate vs. 28.74% for vanilla LIRE, while Arena-Hard win rate improves from 20.0% to 20.3%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DoRA is presented as a method-agnostic plug-in and is evaluated atop nine baselines. On pairwise HH-RLHF, DPO w/ DoRA achieves the lowest lose rate (21.4% Mistral, 20.9% Llama) compared to Robust DPO (20.0%/22.9%) and Dr.DPO (22.4%/24.3%), while matching or slightly exceeding their win rates. On the primary listwise setting, DoRA yields consistent gains: LIRE on HH-RLHF Mistral-7B improves by +11.2 pp win rate (72.8→84.0), DPO_PL on Summarization Llama-8B by +4.5 pp (54.5→59.0), and RRHF on HH-RLHF Mistral-7B by +3.3 pp (76.5→79.8). The one notable exception is LIRE on Summarization Mistral-7B, where DoRA slightly decreases win rate (82.5→81.0) and increases lose rate (17.5→19.0). Against RSO (rejection sampling for preference optimization), DoRA generally outperforms on HH-RLHF (e.g., 58.5 vs. 54.0 win for DPO_PL; 57.5 vs. 56.0 for LIRE) while avoiding RSO's additional generation-sampling overhead. On UltraFeedback/AlpacaEval 2, DoRA improves LIRE (LC 28.74→31.28%) and RRHF (WR 13.88→14.48%) but shows a minor drop for DPO_PL (LC 18.80→18.27%), which the authors attribute to UltraFeedback's chosen responses themselves being drawn from mixed distributions, violating the single-source assumption.

## Relation to Prior Work

DoRA extends the KL-divergence-constrained DRO framework (Hu et al. 2013) to the preference alignment setting by incorporating a sample-level calibration mechanism derived from likelihood-free importance weighting (Grover et al. 2019). Unlike prior robust alignment methods (Robust DPO, Dr.DPO, ROPO) that are tightly coupled to the Bradley-Terry pairwise model, DoRA is designed as a method-agnostic plug-in applicable to diverse alignment objectives including listwise formulations (RRHF, LIRE, DPO_PL). The mixture response shift formulation (Definition 2.1) draws on the DRO-against-mixture-covariate-shifts framework (Duchi et al. 2019), but adapts it specifically to the multi-source synthetic data scenario prevalent in modern alignment pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DoRA addresses a practical and increasingly common problem: alignment training data is a mixture of human-preferred and synthetic responses, and naive ERM over this mixture biases the learned policy toward synthetic artifacts. The method-agnostic design means practitioners can integrate DoRA into any existing alignment pipeline without reformulating the objective, which lowers adoption barriers. Key limitations include the requirement for coarse source labels to train classifiers and the assumption that a reference (golden) distribution is approximately available—both of which may not hold in fully open-domain or legacy settings. Open questions include extending DoRA to online/continual alignment, relaxing the reference-distribution requirement via unsupervised signals, and understanding when the DRO conservatism provides limited gains in clean-data regimes.
