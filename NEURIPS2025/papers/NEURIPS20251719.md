## Problem

Standard diffusion models for text-to-image generation map all data to a single homogeneous Gaussian prior N(0,I), which collapses distinct semantic concepts onto the same latent structure, causing instability, ambiguous score estimation, and weak text-vision alignment. Additionally, the curved sampling trajectories from a generic prior require many function evaluations to converge, making generation slow. The paper asks whether explicitly aligning text semantics with image latents through a structured, text-conditioned prior can simultaneously improve alignment and accelerate sampling.

## Input / Output

The method takes a free-form text prompt as input, which is first encoded by a frozen pretrained text embedder (NV-Embed-v2, producing a 4096-dimensional vector) and then mapped by the TIAE to a prior mean in the VAE latent space. The output is a generated RGB image (1024×1024 in text-to-image experiments, 256×256 or 512×512 for ImageNet class-conditional generation). All diffusion dynamics operate in the compressed VAE latent space rather than pixel space, and the text conditioning enters the OU process as a fixed target mean rather than as a per-step conditioning signal to the denoiser.

## Method

LABridge introduces two coupled components: (1) a Text-Image Alignment Encoder (TIAE), a DiT-inspired network trained with a composite loss (latent alignment, semantic consistency, and reconstruction) to map text embeddings to structured prior means μ_T(y) that are close to corresponding image VAE latents and preserve text-embedding geometry; and (2) a Mean-Conditioned Ornstein-Uhlenbeck diffusion bridge whose forward SDE d x_t = θ(μ_T(y) − x_t)dt + σ dw_t replaces the standard VP/VE process, connecting image latents x_0 to the text-conditioned prior N(μ_T(y), σ_T²I) instead of N(0,I). The OU mean-reverting drift provides an explicit directional pull toward the text-aligned prior during both forward and reverse processes, reducing initial error and stabilizing sampling. Training proceeds in two stages: first the TIAE is trained on paired image-text data with the VAE and text embedder frozen, then the noise-prediction network for the OU bridge is trained with the TIAE frozen. The framework is architecture-agnostic and has been validated on UNet (SD1.5, SDXL) and DiT (SD-3.5, PixArt-α) backbones as well as from-scratch DiT-XL/2 training.

## Results

On text-to-image benchmarks, LABridge consistently improves over both base models and the CF (Liu et al., 2024) baseline across all tested architectures. On COCO-10K, SD3.5-L+LABridge achieves FID 5.34 (vs. 7.33 base, 6.33 CF) and CLIP 31.87; on MJHQ-30K it reaches FID 3.82 and CLIP 32.39. For text-image alignment, GenEval scores improve from 0.66 (SD3.5-L base) to 0.69 with LABridge, and DPG-Bench from 84.52 to 85.28. On ImageNet 256×256 class-conditional generation, CausalFusion-XL+LABridge(VP) achieves FID 1.69 with CFG, outperforming DiT-XL (2.27) and SiT-XL (2.06).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LABridge is evaluated against base models and the CF method (Liu et al., 2024) across five architectures (SD15, SDXL, SD3.5-M, SD3.5-L, PixArt-α). Against the CF baseline, LABridge wins on FID, CLIP, GenEval, and DPG for SD15 (FID 13.82 vs 14.83), SD3.5-M (FID 7.86 vs 8.89), SD3.5-L (FID 5.34 vs 6.33), and PixArt-α (FID 9.23 vs 10.23). For SDXL, LABridge slightly trails the base model on COCO FID (12.72 vs 11.68) but substantially outperforms both on MJHQ-30K FID (8.55 vs 10.55 base, 9.59 CF) and on alignment metrics (GenEval 0.65 vs 0.56/0.62, DPG 77.21 vs 75.52/76.53). On ImageNet 256×256 class-conditional generation (Table 1), CausalFusion-XL+LABridge(VP) achieves FID 1.69 with CFG, beating DiT-XL (2.27), SiT-XL (2.06), and CausalFusion-XL alone (1.77), while DiT-XL/2+LABridge(OU) reaches FID 1.84 with CFG, surpassing the vanilla DiT-XL/2 (2.27). The gains are most pronounced on text-alignment-specific benchmarks (GenEval, DPG-Bench), confirming that the structured prior mechanism primarily improves semantic consistency rather than raw image fidelity.

## Relation to Prior Work

LABridge extends the diffusion bridge framework of Zhou et al. (2023), which connects two arbitrary endpoint distributions, by specializing the bridge to an Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process whose terminal distribution is a text-conditioned Gaussian N(μ_T(y), σ_T²I) rather than a fixed N(0,I). The key architectural departure is the TIAE, a DiT-inspired encoder that maps pretrained text embeddings (CLIP, NV-Embed-v2) into structured prior means in the VAE latent space, explicitly aligning text semantics with image latents before the diffusion process begins. This contrasts with standard latent diffusion models (LDM, SDXL, SD3.5, PixArt-α) where text conditioning is injected via cross-attention during denoising from a homogeneous prior, and with prior bridge work that typically handles paired-data translation rather than open-ended text-to-image generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By replacing the homogeneous Gaussian prior with text-specific structured priors and leveraging OU mean-reverting dynamics, LABridge addresses a fundamental limitation of diffusion models—semantic instability from collapsing diverse concepts onto a single prior—while simultaneously reducing the effective sampling distance. The consistent improvements across five different backbone architectures (UNet and DiT variants) and multiple benchmarks (FID, CLIP, GenEval, DPG) suggest the approach is architecture-agnostic and could be adopted as a drop-in modification to existing pipelines. Open questions include whether the TIAE training remains stable at much larger scales, whether the OU process advantage persists at very high resolutions (1024+), and whether the two-stage training (TIAE then diffusion) can be unified. The lack of released code and reported error bars limits immediate reproducibility.
