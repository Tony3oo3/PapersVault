## Problem

GRPO, the reinforcement learning method behind DeepSeek-R1, suffers from an inherent question-level difficulty bias: its group relative advantage function assigns disproportionately small weights to questions that are either too easy or too hard, causing the optimizer to neglect them. Additionally, the clipping operations in GRPO and its variants (DAPO, Dr. GRPO) lead to entropy collapse or excessive entropy growth, destabilizing training. The paper asks how to redesign the RL objective for large reasoning models in a principled way that eliminates both the difficulty bias and the training instability.

## Input / Output

The method fine-tunes SFT-finetuned distilled reasoning models (DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B/7B and DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama-8B) on mathematical questions from the DeepScaleR dataset (~40.3k problem-answer pairs). For each question, 8 responses are sampled from the current policy, partitioned into positive (correct) and negative (incorrect) sets via a binary rule-based reward (exact match or formal verification). The maximum response length is capped at 8k tokens for both training and inference, and the model outputs a full reasoning trace followed by a final answer. Evaluation uses pass@1 averaged over 16 sampled responses per question across six benchmarks.

## Method

DisCO replaces GRPO's group relative advantage with a discriminative objective grounded in AUC maximization: it increases a scoring function's value on positive (correct) outputs while decreasing it on negative (incorrect) outputs, completely removing the √(p(q)(1-p(q))) difficulty-bias weight. Two non-clipping scoring functions are used—log-likelihood (log-L) and likelihood ratio (L-ratio)—avoiding the entropy collapse caused by PPO-style clipping. Training stability is enforced via a KL divergence trust-region constraint D_KL(π_old || π_θ) ≤ δ, solved with a squared-hinge penalty that only activates when the constraint is violated, rather than a fixed KL regularization that always contributes gradient. To handle imbalanced rollouts (many more negative than positive responses for hard questions), the full DisCO method replaces the basic pairwise discriminative objective with a DRO-based partial AUC surrogate (J2), which focuses on the hardest negatives and provides a tighter lower bound on the basic objective.

## Results

On the 1.5B model (DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B) with 8k max response length for both training and inference, DisCO(log-L) achieves an average pass@1 score of 0.533 across six math benchmarks, outperforming GRPO (0.457) by 7.6 percentage points and DAPO (0.473) by 6.0 percentage points. Notably, DisCO(log-L) with 8k length surpasses DeepScaleR-1.5B-Preview (0.525) which was trained with 24k length and evaluated with 32k length. The same trend holds for 7B (DisCO avg 0.627 vs GRPO 0.592) and 8B (DisCO avg 0.584 vs GRPO 0.516) models, with DisCO maintaining stable generation entropy (~0.22) while baselines suffer from entropy collapse or excessive entropy growth.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DisCO consistently outperforms all seven baselines across all three model sizes (1.5B, 7B, 8B) and both scoring function variants (L-ratio and log-L). Against GRPO [23], DisCO(log-L) gains 7.6 pp on average for 1.5B and 3.5 pp for 7B; against DAPO [79], the gain is 6.0 pp (1.5B) and 5.7 pp (7B). Dr. GRPO [40] performs worst among the RL baselines (0.443 avg on 1.5B), confirming the paper's theoretical finding that removing variance normalization only partially mitigates difficulty bias. TRPA [61], which uses a DPO-style objective with KL regularization, achieves 0.470 (1.5B) but shows training instability in later steps. DisCO also surpasses externally trained reference models: STILL-3-1.5B-preview [12] (0.471) and DeepScaleR-1.5B-Preview [42] (0.525 at 24k/32k), and on the 7B model, DisCO (0.627) beats GRPO-LEAD-7B [82] (0.555) by 7.2 pp. The improvements are most pronounced on the hardest benchmarks (AIME 2024/2025), where DisCO(log-L) reaches 0.404/0.317 vs. GRPO's 0.277/0.242 on 1.5B.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repositions RL fine-tuning of reasoning models through the lens of discriminative learning and AUC maximization, drawing a direct conceptual bridge between GRPO's group-relative advantage and pairwise scoring objectives. It builds on TRPO's trust-region constraint (replacing it with a squared-hinge penalty from non-convex constrained optimization) and on DRO-based partial AUC maximization (Zhu et al., ICML 2022) to handle imbalanced rollouts. The key departure from GRPO, DAPO, and Dr. GRPO is the complete removal of the group-relative advantage function and its associated difficulty-bias weight, replaced by a single non-clipping scoring function applied symmetrically to positive and negative outputs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, non-heuristic alternative to the rapidly proliferating GRPO variants, showing that difficulty bias persists even in Dr. GRPO (with weight p(q)(1-p(q)) rather than the original sqrt form) and that DAPO's decoupled clipping induces excessive entropy growth rather than merely preventing collapse. The 7% average gain over GRPO and 6% over DAPO across six benchmarks and three model sizes (1.5B–8B) is substantial for RL fine-tuning. Limitations include restriction to binary rewards and mathematical reasoning only; the authors note that extending to non-binary rewards via discriminative ranking objectives and scaling to larger models remain open.
