## Problem

Existing fast-rate PAC-Bayesian generalization bounds for multi-task learning require all tasks to have equal training set sizes, an unrealistic restriction for practical settings like federated learning or healthcare. The paper demonstrates that the standard balanced proof technique fundamentally breaks down in the unbalanced case—the relevant moment generating function becomes unbounded—so a new proof strategy is needed. Additionally, the unbalanced setting reveals two previously overlooked but distinct risk definitions (task-centric vs. sample-centric) that matter for different applications.

## Input / Output

The method takes n training datasets of potentially very different sizes (e.g., 102 to 22,530 samples in the MDPR experiments), a data-independent hyper-prior over priors, and produces implicit fast-rate generalization bounds on either the task-centric risk (uniform average over tasks) or the sample-centric risk (weighted by dataset sizes). Unlike standard-rate bounds that yield a single explicit inequality, the task-centric fast-rate bound involves n unknown true risks constrained by a single joint inequality, requiring numerical optimization (SLSQP) to extract the tightest upper bound on the average risk. The sample-centric bound is simpler, involving a single scalar unknown.

## Method

The key insight is to avoid bounding the aggregate multi-task KL divergence directly (which fails for unbalanced sizes) and instead bound per-task KL terms weighted by their respective sample sizes: Σ m_i kl(R̂_i | R_i) ≤ KL(𝔔‖𝔓) + log(1/δ) + Σ log(2√m_i). This is achieved by applying the change-of-measure inequality once at the hyper-posterior level, then bounding each task's MGF independently using the single-task Maurer bound (≤ 2√m_i per task), and combining via Markov's inequality. For the sample-centric setting, all M samples contribute equally so the standard single-task fast-rate proof applies directly with total sample count M. Both kl-style and Catoni-style variants are provided, and the Catoni bounds can be combined with the kl-bound via a union bound over a grid of λ values to exploit the non-convex geometry of the joint constraint set. The results extend to meta-learning by composing a multi-task bound (within training tasks) with a single-task bound (at the environment level over n tasks).

## Results

On split-CIFAR10 with a Vision Transformer (~5.5M params), the paper's fast-rate Catoni-style bound achieves a generalization bound of 0.271 (task-centric) and 0.262 (sample-centric), compared to 0.307 and 0.300 for the standard-rate bound, respectively. On the MDPR dataset (953 unbalanced tasks, 102–22,530 samples) with linear classifiers at high simplicity (η=0.10, empirical risk 1.1%), the kl-style fast-rate bound gives 13.6% versus 18.7% for the standard-rate bound. The naive fast-rate bound depending on m_min is dramatically worse, yielding 0.347 on CIFAR10 and 45.5% on MDPR at η=0.10 — in both cases looser than even the standard-rate bound.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its new unbalanced fast-rate bounds against two baselines: (1) the standard-rate PAC-Bayes multi-task bound of Pentina & Lampert (2014), and (2) the naive fast-rate bound that depends on m_min, obtained by directly applying the balanced fast-rate proof technique of Guan & Lu (2022) / Zakerinia et al. (2025) to the unbalanced setting. Against the standard-rate bound, the new fast-rate bounds consistently win, with the gap widening as empirical risk decreases: on split-CIFAR10 (task-centric) the Catoni-style bound improves from 0.307 to 0.271 (12% tighter), and on MDPR at η=0.10 the kl-style bound improves from 18.7% to 13.6%. Against the m_min fast-rate bound, the new bounds are dramatically superior: on CIFAR10 the m_min bound (0.347) is actually *worse* than the standard-rate bound (0.307), while the paper's bound (0.271) is substantially tighter; on MDPR at η=0.10 the m_min bound (45.5%) is more than 3× looser than the paper's kl-style bound (13.6%). The paper also shows that combining the kl-style and Catoni-style constraints via a union bound (the 'joint constraint' row) can yield a marginally tighter guarantee than either bound alone, a geometric effect unique to the multi-task setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the PAC-Bayesian multi-task learning framework of Pentina & Lampert (2014) and the fast-rate bound techniques of Maurer (2004) and Catoni (2007) to the unbalanced setting where tasks have different training set sizes. The key departure is from the existing fast-rate MTL bounds of Guan & Lu (2022) and Zakerinia et al. (2025), which only apply when all tasks have equal sample sizes; the paper proves (Lemma 3.1) that the standard MGF-based proof technique fundamentally breaks down in the unbalanced case, necessitating a new per-task decomposition strategy. It also extends the meta-learning framework of Zakerinia et al. (2024) to handle unbalanced task environments, and introduces the previously overlooked distinction between task-centric and sample-centric multi-task risk.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper closes a notable gap in the PAC-Bayes MTL literature by providing the first fast-rate generalization guarantees for unbalanced multi-task learning, which is the realistic setting in applications like federated learning and healthcare. The conceptual contribution—that unbalanced MTL has fundamentally different statistical properties than balanced MTL, and that two distinct risk definitions (task-centric vs. sample-centric) are both meaningful—provides useful guidance for practitioners choosing evaluation metrics. A key limitation is that the bounds require numerical optimization (SLSQP) to extract concrete guarantees, making them less interpretable than explicit standard-rate bounds; the authors note that whether more explicit fast-rate bounds are possible for the unbalanced setup remains an open question.
