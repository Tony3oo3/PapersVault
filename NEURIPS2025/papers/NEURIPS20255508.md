## Problem

Mapping detailed 3D geometric inputs to high-frequency continuous spherical signals (e.g., radar response, aerodynamic drag, control policies) is challenging because existing approaches either discretize the sphere into spatial grids that overfit to sample locations, use implicit models that overfit to training coordinates, or rely on equivariant GNNs constrained to low harmonic frequencies (L≤10) due to computational costs. No prior method simultaneously preserves geometric symmetry, captures fine-grained high-frequency structure, and enables resolution-independent evaluation.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 3D object meshes (vertex positions and edge connectivity), 2D/3D keypoints, or voxel/RGB-D data, optionally augmented with global parameters such as flight conditions. The output is a set of spherical harmonic Fourier coefficients (up to degree L=40) representing a continuous signal on S², which can be evaluated at arbitrary resolution via pre-computed harmonic basis grids. For drag prediction, training data provides only a single sparse (θ,φ) observation per object, yet the model reconstructs the full drag cone; for policy learning, actions are decomposed into magnitude and direction, with the direction component mapped to the sphere and the energy function expressed as a Fourier series over polar or spherical harmonics.

## Method

G2Sphere is a fully SO(3)-equivariant encoder-decoder that operates entirely in Fourier space. The encoder (Equiformer V2 for meshes, E(n)-Steerable CNNs for voxels, or equivariant MLPs for keypoints) compresses geometric structure into a multi-channel spherical latent representation at low frequency (L_enc=5). The decoder is a Spherical CNN that progressively upsamples frequency from L_enc to L_dec (up to 40) via alternating inverse Fourier transform, pointwise non-linearity, and forward Fourier transform layers, preserving equivariance at each step. A trainable spherical non-linearity (TSNL) applies a learned MLP pointwise across the sphere after per-frequency inverse FT, enabling unbounded output frequency without artifacts. Training uses MSE for supervised radar/drag tasks and an InfoNCE contrastive loss for behavioral cloning, where the model predicts the expert action direction from non-expert samples. This Fourier-space design yields zero-shot super-resolution, generalization to unseen geometries, and 9 ms inference (vs. 156 ms for Diffusion Policy).

## Results

On the radar prediction task, G2Sphere achieves the lowest MSE of 0.123 on the Asym dataset and 0.221 on Frusta, outperforming the Transformer (0.179/0.201), Equiformer (0.257/0.271), and Spherical CNN (0.496/0.438) baselines. On aerodynamic drag prediction (Pods), G2Sphere achieves 0.062 MSE, marginally beating the Transformer (0.064) and Equiformer (0.0672). In policy learning, G2Sphere achieves near-perfect success rates (1.0 on PushT Fixed, 0.92 on PushT Random, 1.00 on FlyToTarget, 0.98 on FlyThroughGate) while running at 9 ms inference time versus 156 ms for Diffusion Policy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

G2Sphere consistently outperforms all four baselines across the supervised learning domains. On radar prediction, the gap widens with task complexity: on the asymmetric Asym meshes, G2Sphere (0.123 MSE) beats the Transformer by 31% and the Equiformer by 52%, while the Spherical CNN baseline (which uses a predefined ray-based geometry-to-sphere mapping) performs worst at 0.496. On the roll-symmetric Frusta dataset, G2S+TSNL (0.195) edges out the Transformer (0.201), while plain G2S (0.221) is slightly behind the Transformer but far ahead of Equiformer (0.271) and Spherical CNN (0.438). For drag prediction, G2Sphere (0.062) achieves the best MSE, narrowly beating the Transformer (0.064) and Equiformer (0.0672). In policy learning, G2Sphere dominates all four tasks: it reaches 1.0 success on PushT Fixed (vs. 0.98 E-IBC, 0.95 Diffusion) and 0.92 on PushT Random (vs. 0.81 E-IBC, 0.71 Diffusion), while also achieving 1.00/0.98 on the PyBullet drone tasks. The key practical advantage is inference speed: G2Sphere runs at 9 ms per step versus 156 ms for Diffusion Policy, enabling high-frequency closed-loop control.

## Relation to Prior Work

G2Sphere bridges two previously separate families of equivariant architectures: equivariant GNNs (Equiformer V2) that encode geometric structure but are limited to low-frequency outputs (l_max ≤ 10), and spherical CNNs / Spherical Fourier Neural Operators that operate at high frequency but require predefined geometry-to-sphere mappings. The key architectural departure is using a learned equivariant GNN encoder to project geometry directly into Fourier space, followed by a spherical CNN decoder with frequency up-sampling and trainable spherical non-linearities (a generalization of Bonev et al.'s TSNL) to reach output frequencies up to L=40. This combination is novel in that no prior equivariant architecture with dense geometric inputs (meshes, point clouds) had achieved such high output frequencies while maintaining end-to-end SO(3)-equivariance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 9ms inference time (vs. 156ms for Diffusion Policy) enables high-frequency closed-loop control that was previously infeasible with diffusion-based policies, and the zero-shot super-resolution property (training at 60×20, evaluating at 180×20) offers a practical data-efficiency advantage for expensive simulation domains like radar. However, the cubic scaling of computational cost with harmonic frequency limits how much further the output fidelity can be pushed, and the radar/drag experiments rely entirely on synthetic data from physics simulators, leaving open whether the approach generalizes to noisy real-world measurements. The extension to 6DoF pose control (via SO(3) inverse FT) and to more efficient spherical harmonic implementations are natural next steps that could broaden applicability.
