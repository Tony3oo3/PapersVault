## Problem

The classical prediction with expert advice framework assumes the learner observes exact expert losses, but real-world feedback is often corrupted by additive noise (sensor noise, quantization, communication errors). While prior work addressed noisy feedback for specific channels like the binary symmetric channel, no unified framework existed for characterizing fundamental regret limits under general additive noise distributions, nor were matching upper and lower bounds established for canonical noise models such as Gaussian, uniform, and log-concave distributions.

## Input / Output

At each round t, the learner receives a noisy observation c_t = ℓ_t + Z_t (a vector in ℝ^m) where Z_t is i.i.d. additive noise from a known distribution, and must output a probability distribution p_t over m experts based only on the history of noisy observations c^{t-1}. The regret is measured against the best fixed expert on the true (unobserved) losses ℓ_t, and the resulting bounds depend on the noise distribution's shape and scale parameter σ, the number of experts m, and the time horizon n. A key structural detail is that for unbounded noise (e.g., Gaussian), the estimator can produce values outside [0,1], requiring a 'bad event' analysis to handle the exponential weights update.

## Method

The upper bound is achieved by combining the exponential weights (Hedge) strategy with an unbiased estimator ℓ̂_t of the true loss constructed from the noisy observation c_t, yielding a regret that decomposes into a log(m)/α term plus α·n·MSE_estimation, establishing a separation principle between estimation error and decision-making. For Gaussian noise, the natural estimator ℓ̂_t = c_t suffices; for uniform noise, a piecewise-constant estimator is designed to reduce MSE from O(σ²) to O(σ), revealing that noise shape—not just variance—critically affects regret scaling. The lower bound is derived via an information-theoretic argument: a Fano-inequality-based hypothesis testing reduction shows that regret is bounded below by √(n·log m / η(P_{c|ℓ})), where η is the strong data processing constant of the channel, and η is then upper-bounded using Hellinger divergence between the channel output distributions for binary inputs. This yields tight Θ(√((1+σ²)·n·log m)) regret for Gaussian noise, tight Θ(√((1+σ)·n·log m)) for uniform noise, and near-tight bounds for the broader class of symmetric log-concave noise.

## Results

The paper establishes tight minimax regret characterizations for prediction with expert advice under additive noise. For Gaussian noise (AWGN) with variance σ², the regret is Θ(√((1+σ²) n log m)), showing a quadratic dependence on the noise standard deviation. For uniform noise on [−σ, σ], the regret is Θ(√((1+σ) n log m)), revealing a fundamentally different linear scaling with σ. For the broader class of symmetric log-concave noise, the paper provides near-tight bounds: Ω(√((1+σ) n log m)) ≤ Reg ≤ O(√((1+σ²) n log m)), where the lower bound is tight for uniform noise and the upper bound is tight for Gaussian noise.

## Baselines & Comparisons

As a purely theoretical paper, this work does not run experiments or compare against algorithmic baselines. Instead, the key comparison is against the classical noiseless prediction-with-experts result of Θ(√(n log m)) [Cesa-Bianchi et al., 1997], which is recovered as the σ→0 limit of all bounds. The paper also positions itself relative to prior work on communication-constrained bandits: for AWGN channels, the √(1+σ²) multiplicative penalty matches (up to constants) the √(1/SNR) factor found by Mayekar et al. [MST23] in the stochastic bandit setting, and the one-bit quantization case aligns with Hanna et al. [HYF22]. The paper explicitly overcomes limitations of the closest prior work [BK24] by handling unbounded loss functions, providing a rigorous proof for the Gaussian lower bound (previously stated without proof), and extending the analysis to uniform and symmetric log-concave noise distributions not covered by that framework.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the classical prediction-with-expert-advice framework (Cesa-Bianchi et al., 1997) from noiseless to additive-noise feedback, combining the exponential weights/Hedge algorithm with an unbiased estimation step for upper bounds and the strong data-processing inequality (Polyanskiy & Wu, 2017) for information-theoretic lower bounds. It departs from earlier noisy-prediction work (Weissman, Merhav, Somekh-Baruch) that was restricted to binary symmetric channels, and from communication-constrained bandit results (Hanna et al., 2022; Mayekar et al., 2023) that addressed a different feedback model. The paper also subsumes and extends the authors' own prior ISIT 2024 result by providing full proofs, handling unbounded losses, and covering uniform and log-concave noise beyond the Gaussian case.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical insight is that the *shape* of the noise distribution—not merely its variance—fundamentally determines the regret penalty: Gaussian noise incurs a √(1+σ²) multiplicative cost while uniform noise incurs only √(1+σ), a distinction that could guide noise-injection strategies in privacy-preserving or communication-constrained learning systems. The separation-principle connection (regret degrades by exactly the estimation MSE) provides a clean design principle for practitioners: invest in better denoising to reduce regret. Open questions include closing the gap between the √(1+σ) lower and √(1+σ²) upper bounds for general log-concave noise, extending to high-probability (rather than expected) regret guarantees, and handling non-linear or infinite expert classes.
