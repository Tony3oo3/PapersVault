## Problem

Existing network estimation methods face a dual limitation: Gaussian Graphical Models capture only linear conditional dependencies and treat all variables on a flat structure, while nonparametric alternatives lack the representational capacity for complex hierarchical systems. In domains like genomics and social science, variables are naturally organized into groups (e.g., gene pathways) where low-level interactions emerge only when their parent groups are systemically connected, yet no neural network-based framework existed to jointly recover both group-level and variable-level dependencies under nonlinear regimes.

## Input / Output

The method requires n continuous observations of p variables with pre-defined group labels (e.g., KEGG pathway annotations or demographic categories) that partition variables into L groups; group membership is a hard constraint, not inferred. The output is a bi-level sparse network where high-level edges between groups l and l' are determined by nonzero θ_{ll'} parameters, and low-level edges between specific variables are determined by the product θ_{ll'}·Γ_{j}^{[:,k]} ≠ 0, meaning a variable-level connection can only exist if its parent groups are connected.

## Method

NNBLNet is a node-wise ReLU feedforward network with two hierarchical selection layers: a group-level vector θ_l (with θ_ll=1) that gates entire group inputs via a block-wise product, and a variable-level matrix Γ_j that captures specific pairwise associations. The composition θ_l ⊗ Γ_j^T ensures that low-level edges are structurally constrained by high-level group dependencies. Training minimizes a dual-penalized squared-loss objective combining Lasso on θ (high-level sparsity) and Group Lasso on columns of Γ (low-level sparsity), followed by an adaptive re-weighting stage that reduces bias for large coefficients. A two-stage proximal gradient descent algorithm alternates between gradient steps and proximal (soft-thresholding/group-thresholding) updates, and the paper proves bi-level selection consistency under high-dimensional asymptotics with p = o(log n).

## Results

On the four real-world networks, NNBLNet achieves the best F1-score on Friendship (0.838), Co-authorship (0.695), and LUAD (0.577), while Fair Glasso leads on BRCA (0.723 vs. 0.683). On synthetic benchmarks, NNBLNet attains the highest F1-score in the nonlinear setting (0.772) but is slightly outperformed by Fair Glasso in the linear setting (0.769 vs. 0.790). Across all six evaluation scenarios (2 synthetic + 4 real-world), NNBLNet achieves the top or second-best F1-score in five out of six cases.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NNBLNet outperforms all three baselines on Friendship (F1 0.838 vs. 0.771/0.765/0.745) and Co-authorship (F1 0.695 vs. 0.619/0.604/0.632), with margins of 6–19 percentage points over the strongest competitor. On BRCA, Fair Glasso edges out NNBLNet (F1 0.723 vs. 0.683), likely because the linear GGM assumption is better suited to that dataset's structure; NNBLNet still beats BGSL (0.607) and DeepGRNCS (0.664). On LUAD, DeepGRNCS achieves the highest F1 (0.591) with NNBLNet close behind (0.577), while both substantially outperform BGSL (0.537). In the synthetic nonlinear setting, NNBLNet leads all methods (F1 0.772 vs. 0.731 for DeepGRNCS, 0.710 for Fair Glasso, 0.709 for BGSL), confirming the advantage of hierarchical nonlinear modeling; in the linear setting, Fair Glasso is best (0.790) with NNBLNet competitive at 0.769.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of bi-level Gaussian graphical models (Cheng et al. 2017, Shan et al. 2020, Colombi et al. 2024) and sparse deep learning theory (Sun et al. 2022, Dinh & Ho 2020). Prior bi-level network methods are restricted to linear Gaussian assumptions, while existing deep learning approaches for network inference (e.g., DeepGRNCS, VEGA) lack hierarchical structure and theoretical guarantees. NNBLNet repurposes the node-wise regression formulation of GGMs by replacing linear predictors with ReLU networks and introduces two hierarchical selection layers (a Lasso on group-level parameters θ and a Group Lasso on variable-level parameters Γ) that enforce structural consistency between high-level and low-level edges—a combination the authors explicitly note has not been explored before.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first neural network framework for bi-level network inference with formal selection consistency guarantees, bridging a gap between flexible nonlinear modeling and interpretable hierarchical structure discovery. This is particularly relevant for genomics (gene-pathway networks) and social science where dependencies are inherently hierarchical and nonlinear. Key limitations include the reliance on pre-defined group labels (performance degrades with noisy or incomplete annotations) and the sub-Gaussian noise assumption that is difficult to verify in practice. Open questions include jointly inferring group memberships alongside associations, extending to directed or temporal networks, and whether the theoretical rates are tight in practice.
