## Problem

Neurosymbolic AI (NeSy) models are prone to reasoning shortcuts: they satisfy the given symbolic constraints by learning spurious neural predicates that exploit correlations in the data rather than the intended concepts. Existing mitigations either require costly dense annotation or fail in challenging low-data settings, leaving a gap for an annotation-efficient strategy that addresses the shortcut problem at its root cause.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255256/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images (28×28 MNIST digit pairs, 64×64 Kand-Logic primitives, or 2048-dim Faster-RCNN embeddings from 720×1280 BDD-OIA frames), a propositional background knowledge formula K, and a minimal labelled support set (as few as one example per concept). It produces a probability distribution over concepts via a softmax over squared Euclidean distances from the input embedding to per-class prototypes, which is then passed through the NeSy symbolic reasoner to yield the final label. For concepts with no labelled examples, their prototype centroids are initialized as the mean of known centroids plus Gaussian noise scaled by a χ² quantile, enabling zero-shot concept grounding.

## Method

The core contribution replaces standard neural concept extractors in NeSy architectures (DeepProbLog, LTN, Semantic Loss, CCN+) with prototypical networks: each concept set gets a dedicated embedding function whose outputs are compared against class centroids computed from the support set, and concept membership is a softmax over squared Euclidean distances. The key theoretical insight (Theorem 4.1) shows that the gradient of the NeSy loss with respect to the embedding simultaneously encodes (i) the magnitude of constraint violation and (ii) the proximity of the embedding to each prototype, so every weight update step pulls the representation toward the correct centroid while satisfying K — a dual signal that standard NeSy training lacks. The architecture enforces disentanglement by construction (one extractor per mutually exclusive concept set), and Proposition 5.1 proves that under separability assumptions the number of deterministic reasoning shortcuts is reduced to match what dense annotation would achieve, but with only one labelled example per class. Training alternates between a prototypical classification loss on query examples and the NeSy loss on unlabelled data, with a χ²-based initialization scheme for unlabelled class centroids to prevent prototype collapse.

## Results

On MNIST-EvenOdd with only one labelled datapoint per concept, Prototypical NeSy models achieve near-perfect concept scores: SL+PNet reaches F1(C)=0.98 and Cls(C)=0.00, DPL+PNet reaches F1(C)=0.96 and Cls(C)=0.00, and LTN+PNet reaches F1(C)=0.95 and Cls(C)=0.00, compared to F1(C) of 0.04–0.05 and Cls(C) of 0.67–0.79 for the corresponding standard baselines. On Kand-Logic, DPL+PNet achieves F1(C)=0.94 vs. 0.25 for standard DPL. On the real-world BDD-OIA task, DPL+PNet more than doubles F1(C) (0.16 vs. 0.08) and reduces concept collapse by more than half (Cls(C) 0.35 vs. 0.82) over standard DPL.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares Prototypical NeSy models against four standard NeSy baselines (DPL, LTN, SL, CCN+) and three mitigation strategies (Shannon-entropy H, reconstruction loss R, partial concept supervision C) from prior work. On MNIST-EveryOdd, every PNet variant dramatically outperforms its standard counterpart: SL+PNet (F1(C)=0.98) beats SL (F1(C)=0.04) by a factor of ~25, and DPL+PNet (F1(C)=0.96) beats DPL (F1(C)=0.04) similarly. The data-augmentation baseline (Pre*) provides moderate gains (e.g., DPL+Pre* reaches F1(C)=0.55) but remains far below PNet and still exhibits substantial concept collapse (Cls(C)=0.32). In the mitigation-strategy comparison (Table 4), no single strategy (R, H, or C) is sufficient to curb reasoning shortcuts; only the full combination R+H+C approaches PNet-level results for DPL and LTN (F1(C)≈0.95–0.98), while SL still plateaus at F1(C)=0.35. On Kand-Logic, where concepts are more homogeneous, the gap between PNet and Pre* narrows (e.g., DPL+PNet F1(C)=0.94 vs. DPL+Pre* F1(C)=0.91), but PNet still achieves the best scores. On BDD-OIA, DPL+PNet (F1(C)=0.16, Cls(C)=0.35) substantially outperforms both DPL (F1(C)=0.08, Cls(C)=0.82) and DPL+Pre* (F1(C)=0.05, Cls(C)=0.85), confirming the advantage extends to real-world, high-stakes settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper integrates prototypical networks (Snell et al., 2017) into the concept-extraction layer of existing neurosymbolic frameworks (DeepProbLog, Logic Tensor Networks, Semantic Loss, CCN+), creating a new architectural family rather than a new symbolic reasoning engine. It departs from prior reasoning-shortcut mitigations (Marconato et al., 2023, 2024) that rely on auxiliary losses (Shannon entropy, reconstruction) or dense concept annotation, instead anchoring embeddings to class centroids so that the gradient update inherently balances constraint satisfaction with prototype proximity. The theoretical contribution (Theorem 4.1, Corollary 4.2) makes this dual-objective update explicit, showing that standard NeSy backbones lack the prototype-similarity term entirely. Evaluation is conducted on the rs-bench suite (Bortolotti et al., 2024), the standard benchmark for this problem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that a single labelled example per concept suffices to eliminate concept collapse on synthetic tasks (F1(C) from ~4% to ~96% on MNIST-EvenOdd) is practically significant for annotation-efficient neurosymbolic deployment. However, the BDD-OIA results (F1(C) 8%→16%) reveal that the gains are far more modest in noisy, real-world settings with imbalanced and temporally ambiguous labels, and the clusterability assumption (A3) may not hold in high-dimensional embedding spaces. Open questions include whether the approach extends to open-vocabulary or compositional concept spaces, how it interacts with continual learning, and whether the centroid initialization strategy (Eq. 5) remains effective when the labelled/unlabelled class ratio is extreme.
