## Problem

Existing online tensor decomposition methods for streaming data completion rely on discrete optimization rules over factor matrices, limiting their ability to model dynamic spatial-temporal structures and to naturally accommodate the continual growth of streaming tensors. This paper asks whether a functional (continuous) representation can replace discrete factor matrices to better capture spatial-temporal smoothness while enabling a natural continual learning formulation for ever-growing data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251395/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes an N-th-order streaming tensor sequence {X_t} with missing entries and a corresponding binary observation mask P_t at each time step. For single-aspect streams (e.g., traffic), only the temporal dimension grows; for multi-aspect streams (e.g., video, recommendation), multiple dimensions expand simultaneously. The output is the fully completed tensor at the final time T, obtained by evaluating the trained INR-parameterized CP decomposition at all coordinates. Notably, the network architecture (and thus parameter count) remains fixed regardless of tensor growth—only the input coordinate vectors expand.

## Method

OFTD replaces each CP factor matrix U^(n) with a SIREN-style INR f_{Θ_n}(·): R→R^r (MLP with sine activations), so the tensor is represented as [[f_{Θ_n}([I_n^t])]_{n=1}^N]. During online updates, new data is handled by simply appending new coordinates to the INR input and re-optimizing the INR weights via Adam, transforming the problem into a continual learning task. A key theoretical contribution is a regret bound (Theorem 1) showing that forgetting is position-dependent—entries farther from the new data boundary are more likely to be forgotten due to the INR's local continuity. This motivates a long-tail Beta-distributed memory replay buffer that preferentially stores historically distant entries, and a Lipschitz smoothness bound (Lemma 1) proving the INR parameterization inherently preserves spatial-temporal continuity without explicit regularization.

## Results

On single-aspect streaming tensor completion (Table 2), OFTD achieves the best NRE across all five traffic/sensor datasets at all sampling rates (0.1–0.3), with particularly large margins at SR=0.1 (e.g., 0.116 on Beijing vs. 0.125 for the next-best SOFIA; 0.607 on Chicago vs. 0.928 for ATD). On multi-aspect streaming completion (Table 3), OFTD attains an average NRE of 0.172, substantially outperforming GOCPT (0.618) and SIITA (0.384), with the largest gap on YELP (0.371 vs. 2.055 for GOCPT at SR=0.1). Each online update costs 0.18 s (single-aspect) or 0.90 s (multi-aspect), confirming real-time feasibility.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OFTD outperforms all ten baselines on every dataset and sampling rate in both single- and multi-aspect settings. Among the CP-based online methods, SOFIA (Lee & Shin 2021) is the closest competitor on Beijing (0.125 vs. 0.116 at SR=0.1) but degrades sharply on Sensor (1.794 vs. 0.528) and Radar (1.660 vs. 0.869). OLSTEC (Kasai 2019) is competitive on Madrid (0.226 vs. 0.138) but still trails OFTD by ~40% in NRE. The t-SVD-based and Tucker-based methods (TeCPSGD, STF) suffer large errors on Sensor and Radar (NRE > 1.5), indicating poor handling of multi-aspect growth. On multi-aspect streams, GOCPT (Yang et al. 2022) is the strongest baseline but is outperformed by OFTD by a factor of ~3.6 in average NRE (0.618 vs. 0.172), with the gap most dramatic on YELP (2.055 vs. 0.371 at SR=0.1). SIITA (Nimishakavi et al. 2018) and OSGD (Mardani et al. 2015) are consistently weaker, with OSGD showing unstable performance (NRE ranging from 0.193 to 2.146 across datasets). OFTD's advantage is most pronounced at low sampling rates (SR=0.1), where the functional representation's ability to exploit spatial-temporal smoothness provides the greatest benefit over discrete factor-matrix updates.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two well-developed lines of research: online tensor decomposition (CP, Tucker, t-SVD variants for streaming data) and implicit neural representations (SIREN, NeRF). Prior online tensor methods (Grouse, TeCPSGD, OLSTEC, SOFIA, ACP, GOCPT) optimize discrete factor matrices via online optimization rules, while INRs have been applied to image/video reconstruction and PDE solving but not to tensor factorization. OFTD departs from both by parameterizing CP factor matrices with SIREN-style INRs, thereby converting the static online optimization into a continual learning problem where the network must fit new coordinates without forgetting old ones. The long-tail Beta-distribution memory buffer is motivated by a novel regret bound showing that INR forgetting is position-dependent (proportional to distance from the new data boundary), a property absent from prior online tensor methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to embed INRs within an online tensor decomposition framework, opening a path to apply the broader continual learning literature (regularization-based, gradient-projection methods) to streaming tensor problems. The theoretical connection between INR Lipschitz continuity and position-dependent forgetting provides a principled design principle for memory buffers that could transfer to other INR-based continual learning settings. Limitations include: the theory is restricted to 3rd-order tensors, the memory buffer adds ~5× FLOPs over no-replay, and the paper reports best-of-three-seed results without error bars. Open questions include whether the approach scales to higher-order or very large tensors, whether more sophisticated continual learning strategies (EWC, gradient projection) would further reduce forgetting, and the authors' noted connection to low-rank adaptation (LoRA) of large models.
