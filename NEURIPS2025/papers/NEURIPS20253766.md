## Problem

Existing LLM benchmarks such as MMLU, GPQA, and MMLU-Pro concentrate on a narrow set of mainstream disciplines (mathematics, physics, chemistry, biology, law), leaving the vast majority of over 200 specialized graduate-level fields—particularly light industry, agriculture, and service-oriented disciplines—largely unassessed. Moreover, state-of-the-art models have saturated these benchmarks, eroding their value as challenging frontiers for measuring progress toward AGI.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253766/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each question is a text-based multiple-choice item with an unusually high average of 9.67 options (versus the standard 4), averaging 58.42 tokens in length, designed to maximize discrimination among models. Evaluation is conducted zero-shot for reasoning and chat models (temperature 0, up to 32K new tokens for reasoning models, 4K for others) and five-shot for base models following the MMLU-Pro protocol. Outputs include overall accuracy, difficulty-stratified scores (easy/middle/hard), and per-discipline/per-subfield breakdowns across 13 disciplines, 72 fields, and 285 subfields.

## Method

SuperGPQA is constructed through a three-stage Human-LLM collaborative pipeline: (1) Source Screening, where PhD-level experts collect raw questions from credible resources (textbooks, verified problem sets) with screenshots; (2) Transcription, where crowd-sourcing annotators convert raw questions into standardized English multiple-choice format with generated distractors; and (3) a rigorous three-substage Quality Inspection combining rule-based filtering, LLM-based discrimination tagging (using seven SOTA LLMs to flag suspicious items such as questions where multiple models select the same incorrect answer, indicating memorized erroneous solutions from online exercise sites), and expert re-annotation with unrestricted web access. The key differentiator is the iterative Human-LLM collaborative filtering that uses LLM response patterns as a signal for data contamination and question quality, eliminating trivial or ambiguous items before final inclusion. The resulting 26,529-question benchmark spans 285 graduate-level subfields with a STEM-heavy distribution (77.2% in Science, Engineering, and Medicine) that naturally emerged from the quality and difficulty filtering criteria.

## Results

On SuperGPQA (26,529 questions, 285 disciplines), the best reasoning model Gemini-2.5-Pro-Exp-0325 achieves 63.56% overall accuracy, followed by DeepSeek-R1 (61.82%) and Seed-Thinking-v1.5 (61.34%). The best chat model (Doubao-1.5-pro-32k-250115) reaches 55.09%, while the best base model (Llama-4-Maverick-17B-128E) scores 37.83%. On the hard split, even the top reasoning models drop to 56–61%, confirming the benchmark's difficulty as a challenging frontier for SOTA LLMs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Reasoning models consistently outperform chat models: DeepSeek-R1 (61.82%) beats the best chat model Doubao-1.5-pro (55.09%) by 6.73 points, and o1-2024-12-17 (60.24%) surpasses GPT-4.1 (53.68%) by 6.56 points. Instruction tuning yields substantial gains: DeepSeek-V3 (47.40%) outperforms DeepSeek-V3-Base (32.14%) by 15.26 points, and Qwen2.5-72B-Instruct (40.75%) beats Qwen2.5-72B (34.33%) by 6.42 points. Notably, DeepSeek-R1 and DeepSeek-R1-Zero are nearly tied (61.82% vs. 60.24%), with R1-Zero actually performing slightly better in most non-STEM disciplines, leaving the optimal RL training paradigm open. Newer model versions show monotonic improvement (GPT-4o: 39.76% → 41.64% → 44.40% across three snapshots), while o3-mini scores lower than o1-2024-12-17 in most disciplines except Science and Engineering, suggesting potential data leakage. On the hard split specifically, o3-mini-high (56.16%) surpasses DeepSeek-R1 (56.87%) and o1 (53.67%), indicating that smaller reasoning models can match or exceed larger ones on the most challenging questions.

## Relation to Prior Work

SuperGPQA is a direct scale-up of GPQA (Rein et al., 2023), adopting its expert-annotation protocol, graduate-level difficulty framing, and quality-inspection rules, while expanding coverage from ~14 STEM-heavy disciplines to 285 subfields including long-tail domains like agriculture, light industry, and service disciplines. The five-shot evaluation protocol for base models follows MMLU-Pro (Wang et al., 2024b), and the data quality control pipeline draws on methods from LIME and MMLU-Redux. The key methodological departure is the large-scale Human-LLM collaborative filtering system that uses SOTA LLM responses during annotation to flag suspicious questions, rather than relying solely on human expert judgment as in GPQA.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SuperGPQA fills a concrete gap: existing benchmarks (MMLU, GPQA, MMLU-Pro) concentrate on a handful of common fields and are increasingly saturated, leaving LLM capabilities in the majority of human knowledge domains unmeasured. The finding that even the strongest reasoning model (Gemini-2.5-Pro) reaches only 63.56% on a 9.67-option benchmark underscores a substantial capability gap, while the observation that humanities disciplines (Law, History) discriminate models more than STEM fields points to underexplored weaknesses in current training. Key limitations include the STEM-heavy distribution (77.2% of questions), the text-only multiple-choice format that cannot assess practical or multimodal skills, and the unresolved question of whether the near-equivalence of DeepSeek-R1 and DeepSeek-R1-Zero reflects a genuine finding about RL's marginal value or an artifact of benchmark design.
