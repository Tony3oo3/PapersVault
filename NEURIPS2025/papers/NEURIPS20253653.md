## Problem

Existing GNN-based intrusion detection methods decouple graph representation learning from the anomaly detection task, producing embeddings that are not directly optimized for identifying attacks. Moreover, self-supervised pretext tasks in this domain typically require negative samples or prior knowledge of attack patterns, assumptions that rarely hold in practical NIDS deployments where labeled anomalies are scarce. GraphIDS addresses both issues by jointly training a GNN encoder and a Transformer-based masked autoencoder end-to-end, so that flow embeddings are shaped by the reconstruction objective itself.

## Input / Output

The method consumes raw NetFlow records (43 features in v2, 53 in v3) and first converts them into a directed graph where nodes are hosts (IP addresses) and edges are individual communication flows carrying flow-statistics as edge features. The model processes batches of 32,768 flow embeddings (64 windows of 512 flows each) and outputs a per-flow anomaly score computed as the squared L2 reconstruction error in the GNN embedding space; a threshold selected on a small labeled validation set converts scores into binary intrusion/normal predictions.

## Method

GraphIDS couples two components trained jointly via backpropagation of a single MSE loss: (1) a 1-hop E-GraphSAGE encoder that produces edge-level embeddings by aggregating local neighborhood context (both node and edge features) via inductive message passing with neighborhood sampling, and (2) a Transformer encoder-decoder that reconstructs the projected GNN embeddings. The key architectural choice is attention masking rather than input masking: during training, a symmetric binary mask randomly disables a subset of attention links (default ratio 0.15) while all input embeddings remain visible, acting as structured dropout that forces the model to infer from partial context and learn a stable distribution of normal behavior. No positional encoding is used, so the Transformer captures global co-occurrence patterns across flows rather than temporal order. At inference, masking is removed and flows whose reconstruction error exceeds a threshold are flagged as potential intrusions, with the entire pipeline operating inductively on unseen edges and hosts.

## Results

GraphIDS achieves up to 99.98% PR-AUC and 99.61% macro F1 on NF-UNSW-NB15-v3, and 88.19% PR-AUC / 94.47% macro F1 on the larger NF-CSE-CIC-IDS2018-v3 dataset. On the v2 datasets, it reaches 81.16% PR-AUC / 92.64% macro F1 (UNSW-NB15) and 92.01% PR-AUC / 94.31% macro F1 (CSE-CIC-IDS2018). The model uses only ~0.57–0.87M parameters and achieves an average inference time of 3.83 µs per sample, making it suitable for real-time deployment.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Anomal-E (the closest self-supervised GNN baseline), GraphIDS achieves substantially higher PR-AUC on NF-CSE-CIC-IDS2018-v3 (88.19% vs. 25.55%) and NF-UNSW-NB15-v3 (99.98% vs. 90.32%), with macro F1 gains of up to 27 percentage points. On NF-CSE-CIC-IDS2018-v2, Anomal-E slightly edges out GraphIDS on PR-AUC (92.87% vs. 92.01%), though GraphIDS matches or exceeds it on macro F1. Against traditional unsupervised methods (CBLOF, HBOS, Isolation Forest, PCA), GraphIDS dominates by 10–70+ percentage points in PR-AUC across all datasets. SAFE, a self-supervised image-based MAE approach, is outperformed by GraphIDS on every dataset, with the largest gap on NF-CSE-CIC-IDS2018-v3 (88.19% vs. 62.94% PR-AUC). The T-MAE ablation (Transformer without GNN) confirms that local topological context is critical on large-scale networks, where it drops to 73.98% PR-AUC versus GraphIDS's 88.19%.

## Relation to Prior Work

GraphIDS builds directly on E-GraphSAGE (Lo et al., 2022) as its inductive GNN encoder for edge-level flow embeddings, and applies the masked autoencoder paradigm popularized by BERT, MAE, and GraphMAE to the NIDS domain. Its key departure from the closest prior work, Anomal-E (Caville et al., 2022), is the shift from a decoupled pipeline (pretrained GNN + separate anomaly detector) to an end-to-end jointly trained GNN-Transformer reconstruction framework, eliminating the need for contrastive pretraining and negative samples. The use of attention masking (rather than input token masking) as a structured regularizer is a design choice that distinguishes it from standard masked autoencoders in vision or language.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By unifying local topological context (GNN) and global co-occurrence patterns (Transformer) under a single reconstruction objective, GraphIDS offers a practical, label-free NIDS architecture with sub-5 μs per-sample inference, making it suitable for real-time deployment. The ablation revealing that a simple two-layer MLP autoencoder (SimpleAE) nearly matches the full Transformer on several datasets is a useful caution: the autoencoding objective itself may be the primary driver, and the added complexity of self-attention yields gains mainly at scale. Open questions include robustness under abrupt topology shifts (the model assumes relatively stable networks), applicability to single-host monitoring where topological context is sparse, and whether fully unsupervised thresholding can replace the small labeled validation set currently needed.
