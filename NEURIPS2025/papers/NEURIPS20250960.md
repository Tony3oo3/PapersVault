## Problem

Existing LLM guard models frame safety moderation as classification over a fixed, pre-defined safety taxonomy, requiring extensive human-curated annotations and failing to generalize to out-of-distribution threats such as emerging harmful categories or novel jailbreak attacks. This paper asks whether a guard model can instead internalize safety principles through explicit reasoning, enabling it to adapt to user-specified policies at inference time without retraining.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250960/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

RSafe accepts either a standalone user prompt (prompt-level moderation) or a prompt–response pair (response-level moderation), along with a set of safety policy categories specified in the instruction prompt. At inference, users can swap in entirely custom or domain-specific safety taxonomies (e.g., medical ethics, copyright) without any additional fine-tuning. The model outputs a structured response containing a step-by-step reasoning trace enclosed in <think> tags followed by a binary safe/unsafe verdict in a \boxed{} format, providing human-readable explanations for every blocking decision.

## Method

RSafe is built on Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct and trained via a two-stage paradigm that skips supervised fine-tuning entirely. Stage 1 (Guided Reasoning) uses an instruction prompt that enumerates 18 safety categories and enforces a 'think-then-answer' schema, eliciting the backbone LLM's inherent policy-aware reasoning. Stage 2 (Reinforced Alignment) applies GRPO (Group-Relative Policy Optimization) with a composite rule-based reward: a format reward for schema compliance and an accuracy reward for correct final predictions, with no external reward model or reasoning-path supervision. The key insight is that RL over verifiable outcome rewards aligns the model's reasoning paths toward accurate safety judgments without requiring annotated rationales, enabling generalization to unseen categories. At inference, dynamically substituting the safety category list in the prompt (RSafe-adaptive) allows the model to reason within entirely new policy scopes, significantly improving robustness on out-of-distribution and adversarial benchmarks like WildGuardTest.

## Results

On the weighted average across six safety benchmarks (3 prompt-level, 3 response-level), RSafe achieves 90.4% accuracy and 88.9% F1, matching GuardReasoner-8B (89.9%/88.9%) and exceeding WildGuard-7B (89.3%/88.4%) and LlamaGuard3-8B (87.7%/83.3%). On the out-of-distribution WildGuardTest robustness benchmark, RSafe-adaptive reaches 84.5% accuracy and 82.3% F1 overall, with 79.1% accuracy on the adversarial subset—surpassing LlamaGuard-8B (74.1% adversarial Acc) and ShieldGemma-9B (65.1% adversarial Acc). RSafe is trained on only ~10K public examples without synthetic augmentation, yet matches or exceeds models trained on substantially larger curated datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RSafe (90.4% Acc / 88.9% F1 weighted avg) matches GuardReasoner-8B (89.9%/88.9%) and slightly exceeds WildGuard-7B (89.3%/88.4%), while clearly outperforming LlamaGuard3-8B (87.7%/83.3%) by 2.7 points in accuracy and 5.6 in F1. Against the closed-source APIs, RSafe dominates: OpenAI Moderation achieves only 62.6% accuracy and 21.4% F1 (severe class-imbalance issues on ToxicChat and SafeRLHF), and GPT-4o reaches 64.4%/46.0%. ShieldGemma-9B trails RSafe by 20.2 points in accuracy (70.2% vs 90.4%), largely due to its limited four-category taxonomy. On the OOD WildGuardTest robustness benchmark, RSafe-adaptive (84.5% Acc) edges out LlamaGuard-8B (82.5%) and substantially outperforms ShieldGemma-9B (69.4%) and OpenAI Moderation (58.3%), with the largest gap on the adversarial subset (79.1% vs 74.1% for LlamaGuard and 65.1% for ShieldGemma).

## Relation to Prior Work

RSafe sits at the intersection of two research threads: (1) LLM guard models for safety moderation (LlamaGuard, ShieldGemma, WildGuard), which are trained via supervised fine-tuning on fixed safety taxonomies, and (2) RL-for-reasoning paradigms popularized by DeepSeek-R1 and GRPO, which incentivize step-by-step reasoning through verifiable rewards. The key departure from prior guard models is the elimination of SFT as a cold start: RSafe applies zero-shot GRPO directly to a base LLM (Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct) with rule-based format and accuracy rewards, and introduces dynamic safety-policy specification at inference time—a capability absent from all prior guard models that are locked to their training-time taxonomy. Compared to the closest reasoning-based competitor, GuardReasoner, RSafe avoids the need for large-scale annotated reasoning traces (127K samples + 460K reasoning steps) by relying on the backbone's inherent reasoning plus RL alignment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical pain point in LLM deployment: guard models trained on fixed taxonomies fail on emerging harmful categories and adversarial jailbreaks, and retraining is costly. RSafe's adaptive policy specification at inference time offers a training-free mechanism to extend coverage, and the ~10K-example data efficiency makes the approach accessible. However, two limitations temper the impact: the method is bounded by the backbone LLM's intrinsic reasoning capacity (no SFT cold start means no reasoning capability injection), and the adaptive variant requires users to correctly articulate the relevant safety taxonomy—a non-trivial requirement in fast-evolving threat landscapes. Open questions include whether stronger reasoning backbones (e.g., math/code-distilled models) transfer to safety reasoning, and whether the paradigm extends to high-stakes domains like healthcare or law.
