## Problem

Existing 3D GAN inversion methods generate occluded regions in novel views solely from the generative prior of the 3D GAN, producing low-quality results due to information loss in the low bit-rate latent code. While the warping-and-inpainting strategy has proven effective in 3D scene generation, it has not been adapted to 3D GAN inversion, which is constrained to single-view training data and lacks ground-truth novel-view images for supervision.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255333/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single 512×512 RGB face image and its camera pose as input, and produces novel-view face images at arbitrary camera angles (e.g., front, ±30°, ±60° yaw, top, bottom) along with a depth map rendered from the 3D GAN. The output is structured as a warped image (preserving visible-region fidelity from the input) with occluded regions filled by the inpainting network, rather than a fully re-rendered image from the 3D GAN.

## Method

WarpGAN integrates a warping-and-inpainting pipeline into 3D GAN inversion: a Swin-Transformer-based encoder projects the input into the W+ latent space of EG3D, a depth map is rendered via NeRF-style volumetric rendering to geometrically warp the input to a novel view, and a style-based inpainting network (SVINet) fills the resulting occluded holes. SVINet is built on LaMa's FFC-based encode-inpaint-decoder architecture and introduces two key mechanisms: (1) symmetry-aware feature extraction that warps a mirrored input to the target view and refines features via FiLM modulation, and (2) style-based convolution modulation using the latent code w+ to enforce multi-view consistency across inpainted regions. Training overcomes the absence of ground-truth novel views by re-warping the inpainted novel view back to the original view for loss computation, supplemented by synthetic multi-view pairs generated from the 3D GAN, with a combined reconstruction, latent-code consistency, and adversarial loss.

## Results

On CelebA-HQ, WarpGAN achieves a FID of 19.12 and ID similarity of 0.7882, outperforming the best optimization-based method HFGI3D (FID 24.30, ID 0.7641) and the best encoder-based method Triplanenet (FID 32.65, ID 0.7706). On the multi-view MEAD dataset, WarpGAN attains the lowest LPIPS (0.2490/0.3008 for ±30°/±60° views), lowest FID (38.15/64.01), and highest ID (0.8315/0.7741) among all compared methods. Inference time is 0.084 s per image, comparable to encoder-based methods (pSp: 0.054 s, GOAE: 0.080 s) and orders of magnitude faster than optimization-based methods (HFGI3D: 264.5 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

WarpGAN outperforms all four optimization-based baselines (SG2W+, PTI, Pose Opt., HFGI3D) on every metric across both CelebA-HQ and MEAD, while running 500–3000× faster (0.084 s vs. 43.7–264.5 s). Against encoder-based methods, WarpGAN achieves a 5.2-point FID improvement over the strongest encoder baseline Triplanenet (19.12 vs. 32.65 on CelebA-HQ) and a 13.5-point FID gap over pSp (19.12 vs. 38.46). On MEAD, WarpGAN reduces FID by 13.1 points over HFGI3D (38.15 vs. 51.24 at 30°) and by 38.5 points over Triplanenet (38.15 vs. 76.62), while also achieving the highest ID similarity (0.8315 vs. 0.8019 for HFGI3D). Dual Encoder is compared only qualitatively (Fig. 3) because it uses a different 3D GAN backbone and removes backgrounds during training, making direct quantitative comparison inapplicable. The paper also demonstrates that WarpGAN's novel-view outputs can assist PTI-based attribute editing, yielding higher-fidelity edits than PTI or HFGI3D alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

WarpGAN sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: 3D GAN inversion (building on EG3D's triplane representation and GOAE's Swin-Transformer encoder) and depth-based warping-and-inpainting for novel view synthesis (as in LucidDreamer, Text2Immersion, and GenWarp for general 3D scenes). The key departure is that prior 3D GAN inversion methods (HFGI3D, SPI, GOAE, Triplanenet, Dual Encoder) rely exclusively on the 3D GAN's generative prior to hallucinate occluded regions, whereas WarpGAN introduces a dedicated inpainting network (SVINet, built on LaMa's FFC architecture) that explicitly fills those regions using warped input-image information, facial symmetry priors, and latent-code-modulated convolutions. The re-warping training trick—warping the novel view back to the original view to compute losses without ground-truth multi-view data—adapts the multi-view warping-and-inpainting paradigm to the single-view constraint of 3D GAN inversion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a fast encoder-based pipeline can match or exceed the quality of much slower optimization-based 3D GAN inversion methods (FID 19.12 vs. 24.30 for HFGI3D, at ~0.08s vs. 264s inference), which is practically significant for interactive applications. However, the method is currently face-specific (relying on facial symmetry priors and ArcFace identity loss), and the authors acknowledge that performance degrades for large view changes where the visible region in the warped image becomes too small to guide inpainting. Open questions include whether the warping-and-inpainting strategy generalizes to non-rigid or non-symmetric objects, whether the depth-map errors that propagate into the warped image can be mitigated, and whether the approach extends to full 360° synthesis rather than the moderate yaw angles tested here.
