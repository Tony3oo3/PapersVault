## Problem

Event-based vision models are bottlenecked by the scarcity and limited diversity of training data, as existing synthetic event generation pipelines (video-based simulators like V2E or model-based simulators like ESIM) incur prohibitive storage and I/O costs that prevent scaling to internet-scale video repositories. The paper asks whether explicit event stream synthesis is truly necessary, revealing that most event-based deep learning pipelines convert events into dense voxel grids before network processing, creating an opportunity to target those representations directly.

## Input / Output

The V2V module consumes B+1 consecutive video frames (180×596, cropped from WebVid's 336×596 to avoid watermarks) and produces a discrete event voxel grid of shape B×H×W in a single on-the-fly pass during each training iteration. Downstream reconstruction models (E2VID, ETNet, HyperE2VID) take this discrete voxel as input and output a sequence of reconstructed RGB frames, while the optical flow variant outputs dense flow fields. A key constraint is that the discrete voxel representation discards all intra-bin temporal information, making the approach inapplicable to architectures requiring sub-bin timing (e.g., spiking neural networks).

## Method

The core insight is that discrete voxel grids, which encode only inter-bin event counts and discard intra-bin timestamps, can be computed directly from video frame log-intensity differences without ever materializing an intermediate event stream. The V2V module randomly samples camera parameters (positive/negative thresholds c+, c−, Gaussian background noise σ, and a sparse hot-pixel map) at every training iteration, then accumulates a simulated photoreceptor voltage ΔL across B+1 frames using a Brownian-motion noise model (following DVS-Voltmeter), counts triggered positive and negative events per bin via floor division by the thresholds, and outputs each voxel bin as N+(i) − N−(i). This merges the two-stage pipeline (event generation → voxel stacking) into a single efficient computation, enabling 150× storage reduction (19 GB for 10,000 videos vs. 279 GB for ESIM-Voxel) and on-the-fly parameter randomization that multiplies effective sample diversity without additional storage. The approach is validated by retraining E2VID, ETNet, HyperE2VID, and EvFlow on WebVid-10K (52 hours, 10,000 scenes) and evaluating zero-shot on real event datasets HQF, EVAID, IJRR, and MVSEC.

## Results

On the primary event-to-video reconstruction task, V2V-E2VID trained on WebVid10K achieves SSIM 0.676 / MSE 0.032 / LPIPS 0.265 on HQF and SSIM 0.663 / MSE 0.054 / LPIPS 0.371 on EVAID, substantially outperforming the original ESIM-280-trained E2VID (SSIM 0.526, MSE 0.088, LPIPS 0.469 on EVAID). The same scaling benefit holds for HyperE2VID (EVAID SSIM 0.654 vs 0.533) and ETNet (EVAID SSIM 0.610 vs 0.541). For optical flow estimation on MVSEC, V2V-EvFlow reduces dense AEE from 0.967–2.903 (EvFlow+) to 0.732–2.114 across five sequences, with dense 3PE dropping from 2.7–36.1% to 0.4–23.6%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

V2V-E2VID (WebVid10K) decisively outperforms the original ESIM-280-trained E2VID [29] on EVAID across all three metrics (SSIM 0.663 vs 0.526, MSE 0.054 vs 0.088, LPIPS 0.371 vs 0.469) and on HQF SSIM (0.676 vs 0.638), though it trails slightly on HQF LPIPS (0.265 vs 0.256). The same pattern holds for HyperE2VID [7] (EVAID SSIM 0.654 vs 0.533) and ETNet [36] (EVAID SSIM 0.610 vs 0.541), confirming the benefit is architecture-agnostic. For optical flow, V2V-EvFlow beats EvFlow+ [33] on every dense AEE and dense 3PE metric across all five MVSEC sequences (e.g., indoor_flying1 dense AEE 0.732 vs 0.967; outdoor_day1 dense 3PE 23.6% vs 36.1%). In the direct V2E comparison (Table 7), V2V-100 outperforms both V2E-1 and V2E-5 on all metrics (e.g., HQF SSIM 0.626 vs 0.524/0.470), while using ~57× less storage (210 MB video vs 12 GB raw events). The scaling ablation (WebVid-100 → 1K → 10K) shows monotonic improvement, with the 10K model achieving the best EVAID SSIM (0.663) and LPIPS (0.371) among all E2VID variants tested.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the standard two-stage event simulation pipeline (video → explicit event stream → voxel grid) by collapsing both stages into a single video-to-voxel conversion, drawing on the sensor noise model from DVS-Voltmeter [18] and the EvFlow architecture [42] for optical flow. It positions itself against both video-based simulators (V2E, EventGAN) that suffer from interpolation artifacts and model-based simulators (ESIM, PECS) that lack scene diversity, arguing that the intermediate event stream is unnecessary when the downstream network consumes discrete voxel grids. The key conceptual departure is the observation that intra-bin temporal information can be discarded without performance loss, enabling direct computation of discrete voxel bins from frame differences rather than simulating individual event timestamps.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 150× storage reduction and on-the-fly parameter randomization fundamentally change the economics of event-based vision research, enabling training on 10,000 diverse real-world videos (52 hours) versus the 280 synthetic scenes typical of prior work. This is likely to become the default data pipeline for event-based dense prediction tasks, as it removes the primary bottleneck (storage and I/O) that has constrained the field. Limitations include applicability only to architectures consuming discrete voxel representations (excluding spiking neural networks) and CPU-intensive video decoding during training. Open questions include whether the approach extends to tasks requiring intra-bin timing information and whether the WebVid-specific biases (aesthetic quality, shot cuts) affect generalization to other domains.
