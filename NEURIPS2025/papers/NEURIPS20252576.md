## Problem

Despite MLLMs demonstrating strong performance on broad vision-language tasks, their capacity to understand perspective geometry—locating vanishing points, reasoning about 3D line relationships, and maintaining spatial consistency under viewpoint changes—remains entirely untested. Existing MLLM benchmarks focus on captioning, general VQA, and surface-level perception but largely omit the geometric reasoning that underpins human spatial understanding, leaving it unclear whether these models have internalized perspective structure or merely pattern-match on visual cues.

## Input / Output

Each evaluation instance pairs an RGB image (architectural, urban, indoor, or Blender-synthesized scenes) with a multiple-choice question probing a specific perspective concept, with answer choices randomly shuffled to eliminate positional bias. For the robustness dimension, each seed image is augmented with perspective-invariant perturbations (horizontal flipping, cropping, masking), and the model must produce the same correct answer across all variants. Outputs are scored as accuracy for perception and reasoning tasks, and as binary (all-or-nothing consistency across the full perturbation set) and graded (fraction of variants answered correctly) robustness scores.

## Method

MMPerspective organizes 10 tasks into three hierarchical dimensions: Perspective Perception (vanishing point location, horizon line identification, lens distortion detection, view angle inference), Perspective Reasoning (perspective type classification, 3D line relationship determination, perspective transformation spotting, vanishing point counting, out-of-view quadrant inference), and Perspective Robustness (consistency under appearance-preserving transformations). The 2,711 image instances are sourced from four streams—web collection, real-world photography with paired viewpoints, the RPVP recurrence-based dataset, and Blender renders with analytically computed ground-truth VP coordinates—annotated through a hybrid manual/automated pipeline with multi-stage quality control and dual-annotator consensus for subjective tasks. The robustness metric is the key methodological novelty: rather than testing a single image, it tests whether a model's answer remains correct and consistent across a set of geometrically equivalent but visually perturbed variants, directly separating genuine geometric grounding from brittle reliance on surface-level patterns. The paper evaluates 43 MLLMs (1B–72B open-source plus proprietary GPT-4o and Gemini) under greedy decoding, and further analyzes scaling trends (model size vs. encoder size), CoT prompting effects, and error-pattern correlations across model families to identify architectural bottlenecks.

## Results

Across 43 MLLMs evaluated on MMPerspective (2,711 images, 5,083 QA pairs), Gemini-2-flash (CoT) achieves the highest overall P&R accuracy at 57.2%, followed by GPT-4o (CoT) at 54.0% and InternVL3-78B at 52.9%. On individual tasks, GPT-4o leads in Perspective Type Reasoning (82.0%), InternVL3-38B dominates Lens Distortion Perception (90.9%) and Out-of-View Reasoning (56.8%), and Eagle-X4-8B achieves the best binary robustness score (55.3%) despite its modest size. Notably, robustness scores are dramatically lower than accuracy scores across all models (e.g., InternVL3-38B: 51.1% accuracy vs. 9.1% binary robustness), revealing a critical gap between surface-level perception and geometric consistency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among proprietary models, Gemini-2-flash (CoT) leads overall accuracy (57.2%) and VPP (69.8%), while GPT-4o (CoT) excels in PTR (82.0%) and achieves the highest graded robustness (69.4%); both substantially outperform Gemini-1.5-flash (33.8% overall) and GPT-4o-mini (37.6%). Among open-source models, InternVL3-78B (52.9%) and InternVL2-572B (51.7%) lead in overall accuracy, with InternVL3-38B achieving the best LDP (90.9%) and OVR (56.8%) scores. LLaVA-OV-72B (47.5% overall) and Eagle-X4-13B (39.8%) stand out for robustness, achieving 53.1% and 53.8% binary robustness respectively, far exceeding InternVL3-38B (9.1%). Eagle-X4-8B is the most robust model overall (55.3% binary) despite ranking only mid-tier in accuracy (41.4%), suggesting its mixture-of-encoders architecture provides inherent geometric consistency. Qwen2-VL and Qwen2.5-VL families show steady scaling (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL: 36.3% at 3B → 49.4% at 72B), while LLaVA-Next models underperform their LLaVA-OV counterparts at comparable sizes (37.4% vs. 47.5% at 72B).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by repurposing classical perspective geometry (vanishing points, horizon lines, perspective types) as a systematic evaluation axis for MLLMs, a dimension the authors argue is 'largely absent' from existing benchmarks like MMBench, MMMU, and SEED-Bench. It draws on prior VP-detection literature (Bharadwaj et al. 2025, Coudert et al. 2022) and the RPVP dataset for ground-truth annotations, but reframes these as multiple-choice VQA tasks probing MLLM comprehension rather than as detection algorithms. The synthetic data pipeline via Blender with Claude-generated 3D scenes is a novel curation strategy that provides exact VP coordinates unavailable in real-world imagery.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals a striking disconnect: models that answer perspective questions correctly on original images can fail catastrophically under trivially geometry-preserving transformations (e.g., InternVL3-38B scores 67.2% on perception but only 9.1% binary robustness), suggesting their 'understanding' is brittle pattern-matching rather than internalized geometric reasoning. The finding that robustness correlates weakly with model size (r=0.34) and very weakly with vision encoder size (r=0.15), while accuracy scales strongly (r=0.81), challenges the assumption that scaling alone will fix spatial reasoning. Open questions include whether the limited range of encoder sizes (300–500M params) prevents definitive scaling-law conclusions, and how to architect MLLMs with genuine geometric priors that survive perturbations.
