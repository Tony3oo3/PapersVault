## Problem

Learning equivariant embeddings from unlabeled data pairs (y, g·y) has been approached by methods that either restrict to orthogonal representations, require generative modeling of the input space, or enforce group-specific architectural inductive biases. No prior encoder-only method learns general linear group representations from group action observations with a formal identifiability guarantee for the learning algorithm itself, leaving open whether such representations can be recovered from observational data alone under mild assumptions.

## Input / Output

The method consumes batches of n+1 paired samples {(y_i, y_i')} where all pairs share the same unknown group action g, plus a query sample y and a set S of negative samples drawn uniformly from the dataset. For idSprites, inputs are 64×64 RGB image pairs; for synthetic data, they are 50-dimensional vectors produced by a random 3-layer MLP mixing function. The output is a latent embedding split into an n-dimensional equivariant subspace (style) and an m-dimensional invariant subspace (content), together with an implicit d×d linear group representation R̂(g) estimated by least squares from the latent pairs, satisfying φ(g·y) = R̂(g)φ(y).

## Method

EbC uses a contrastive objective in which the model infers the group action from example pairs (Y, Y') via a non-parametric least-squares fit R̂(X,X') = (XᵀX)⁻¹(XᵀX'), applies the resulting linear map to a query embedding φ(y), and selects the correct transformed sample y' from among 16k negatives using a Euclidean-distance InfoNCE loss. The encoder is a 3-layer MLP (512 hidden units for images, 128 for synthetic vectors) with no group-specific architectural bias; content/style separation is enforced by constraining the representation matrix to block-diagonal form diag(GL_n, I_m). The key theoretical contribution extends Roeder et al.'s canonical discriminative form with scalar potential functions α and β to admit the MSE-based loss, then proves that under diversity conditions the learned embedding h(x) = φ∘f recovers the true latent space up to a linear indeterminacy (h(x) = Lx) and the implicit representation satisfies R̂ = LR(g)L⁻¹, establishing full identifiability of both the vector space and the group action.

## Results

On synthetic data (SO(3), O(3), GL(3)), EbC recovers the ground-truth latent space with R²(x) > 99.7% and the group representation with R²(G) > 99.7%, while all baselines (InfoNCE, +LDS, +SLDS) achieve R²(x) ≈ 0 and R²(G) ≈ 0. On the Infinite dSprites dataset with the product group R_m × Z_n × Z_n, EbC achieves 99.91% Top-5 kNN accuracy for group action prediction (Acc(G,5)) and 74.04% content classification accuracy (Acc(C)). A linear-only variant of EbC (EbC(lin.)) achieves only 60–70% R²(x) and 40–54% R²(G), confirming that the nonlinear encoder is essential for full recovery.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EbC decisively outperforms both baselines on the core equivariance metrics. InfoNCE (van den Oord et al., 2018), which sets the group representation to the identity, achieves R²(x) ≈ 0 and R²(G) ≈ 0 across all synthetic groups, meaning it learns no group structure at all despite achieving ~99% content accuracy. Dynamics Contrastive Learning (Laiz et al., 2025), tested as both LDS and SLDS variants, similarly fails to recover any group structure (R²(x) ≤ 0.2, R²(G) ≈ 0) while matching InfoNCE on content identification (~99%). EbC achieves >99.7% on both R²(x) and R²(G) for SO(3), O(3), and GL(3), a gap of roughly 100 percentage points over all baselines. On content classification (Acc(C)), EbC matches the baselines on synthetic data (~99%) but drops to 74% on idSprites, where the baselines retain ~99.9% — a trade-off the authors attribute to the need to implicitly learn class prototypes for group-action estimation. The linear-only variant EbC(lin.) confirms that the nonlinear encoder is critical: it achieves only 60–70% R²(x) and 40–54% R²(G), far below the full model.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the canonical discriminative form of Roeder et al. (ICML 2021) by introducing scalar potential functions that admit a mean-squared-error loss, and connects it to the nonlinear ICA identifiability framework of Zimmermann et al. (ICML 2021). It departs from prior equivariant representation learning methods (CARE, STL, NFT) by being the first encoder-only approach that learns general linear group representations in GL(n) without generative modeling or group-specific architectural biases, while providing a formal identifiability guarantee. The implicit group representation via least-squares fitting over batch pairs is a practical simplification compared to NFT's explicit Fourier-based parameterization or CARE's restriction to orthogonal representations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that identifiable equivariant embedding learning from unlabeled paired observations is theoretically and empirically feasible without generative models, with assumptions that match common scientific data (e.g., before/after perturbation pairs in biology, time-series in neuroscience). Key open questions include: whether the content-style separation degrades further at scale (content accuracy drops to ~75% on idSprites with 18 content classes), whether the method extends to continuous or infinite groups, and how it performs on full-scale real-world datasets like 3DIdent. The practical hyperparameter selection protocol via the observable Acc(G) metric is a useful contribution, but the requirement for 6–8× the theoretical minimum samples per action and the sensitivity to dimensionality misspecification limit immediate deployment.
