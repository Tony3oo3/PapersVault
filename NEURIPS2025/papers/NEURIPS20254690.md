## Problem

Prior studies of convergent learning typically examine a handful of models on a single dataset, rely on one alignment metric, and evaluate networks only at a final post-training checkpoint, leaving open when alignment emerges during training, what minimal transformations suffice to align representations, and how distribution shift affects convergence. This paper closes these gaps with a large-scale audit spanning dozens of vision and language models, thousands of layer-pair comparisons, and three alignment families with progressively stricter invariance constraints.

## Input / Output

The method consumes activation matrices from two independently trained networks over M stimuli: for CNNs, center-pixel values are extracted from each convolutional channel to form m×c matrices (avoiding the O(mh²w²c + h³w³c³) cost of full spatial flattening); for ViTs, [CLS] token vectors at each layer are used; for language models, sentence embeddings from STSB are compared. Outputs are pairwise correlation alignment scores under three mapping constraints—unconstrained linear regression, orthogonal Procrustes, and permutation/soft-matching (transportation polytope for unequal unit counts)—reported per layer pair, per training epoch, and per OOD dataset, averaged over 5-fold cross-validation.

## Method

The core contribution is a systematic comparison of three alignment metrics with decreasing mapping flexibility: linear regression (affine-invariant), orthogonal Procrustes (rotation/reflection-invariant), and permutation or soft-matching (unit-order-invariant), applied across the full hierarchy of ResNet18/50, VGG16/19, ViTs, and Pythia language models trained under both supervised and MoCo self-supervised objectives. For CNNs, a center-pixel extraction strategy reduces each feature map to a single representative value per channel, making the optimal mapping computation tractable while remaining justified by prior evidence that optimal spatial shifts are near zero. To test whether convergent learning identifies a privileged representational basis, the authors apply Haar-distributed random rotations to one network's unit basis and recompute permutation scores, finding substantial drops for CNNs but not for ViTs. Convergence dynamics are tracked by recomputing Procrustes alignment at every training epoch (up to 10 for CNNs, 25 for ViTs, 154 checkpoints for Pythia), and robustness is probed by evaluating within-architecture alignment on 17 OOD datasets from the Stylized ImageNet suite, revealing that early layers remain tightly aligned while deeper layers diverge in proportion to the distribution shift.

## Results

Across four CNN architectures (ResNet-18/50, VGG-16/19) trained on ImageNet, representational alignment (measured via Procrustes) is strongest in early layers and diminishes with depth, though deeper layers still exceed 0.5 correlation. Training increases mean Procrustes alignment by 145.26% in early layers (depth < 0.5) and 493.84% in deeper layers (depth > 0.5) relative to randomly initialized networks. Nearly all representational convergence occurs within the first epoch, well before task accuracy plateaus. Under 17 OOD datasets, early-layer alignment remains stable while deeper-layer alignment diverges in proportion to the distribution shift, with a strong correlation between alignment and OOD accuracy that grows with network depth.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not benchmark a single proposed method against external baselines; instead it systematically compares three alignment metrics of increasing strictness (Linear regression > Procrustes > Permutation/Soft-matching) and contrasts trained networks against randomly initialized (untrained) baselines. Procrustes (rotation/reflection-invariant) achieves alignment scores nearly identical to the more flexible Linear metric across all layers and architectures, indicating that scaling and shearing add little beyond rotation. Permutation scores, despite being the strictest, still significantly exceed chance, revealing a privileged one-to-one neuron correspondence in CNNs. This privileged-basis property is absent in Vision Transformers, where permutation scores in the native and randomly rotated basis are statistically indistinguishable. In language models (Pythia-70m/160m on STSB), the same Linear ≈ Procrustes >> Permutation ordering holds, and alignment peaks around 8–10 B tokens before plateauing even as next-token loss continues to improve to 300 B tokens. Self-supervised (MoCo) models replicate all supervised-learning findings, confirming that the convergence patterns are paradigm-independent.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the convergent learning literature (Yosinski et al. 2014; Li et al. 2015; Kornblith et al. 2019) by replacing the typical single-metric, single-checkpoint, single-dataset protocol with a systematic multi-axis audit. It draws on the representational similarity toolkit—CCA/SVCCA, CKA, RSA, model stitching—and the more recent shape-metric and soft-matching frameworks (Williams et al. 2021; Khosla & Williams 2024; Sucholutsky et al. 2023), but positions its contribution as a large-scale empirical sweep rather than a new metric. The training-dynamics analysis connects to the early-phase literature (Frankle et al. 2020; Atanasov et al. 2021) and deep linear network theory (Saxe et al. 2013; Braun et al. 2025), while the OOD axis builds on Geirhos et al.'s texture-vs-shape bias work. The Platonic Representation Hypothesis (Huh et al. 2024) and the contravariance principle (Cao & Yamins 2021) serve as the theoretical targets the paper's findings challenge.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that representational alignment crystallizes within the first epoch—long before task accuracy plateaus—directly challenges the contravariance principle and task generality hypothesis, shifting explanatory weight toward shared input statistics and architectural inductive biases. The OOD results (early layers stable, deep layers diverge proportionally to shift magnitude) have practical implications for model-brain comparisons and for OOD generalization strategies such as late-layer fine-tuning. Key open questions remain: the paper does not pinpoint the exact sub-epoch timing of convergence, acknowledges that its metrics may miss subtle task-relevant representational shifts, and the center-pixel spatial approximation may underestimate alignment. A theoretical account (e.g., via NTK extensions to CNNs) of why convergence is so rapid is explicitly left for future work.
