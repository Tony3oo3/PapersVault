## Problem

Most prior theoretical results in PU learning assume the class prior α is known to the learner, and existing sample complexity bounds are limited to the SCAR setup or require restrictive assumptions like separability or anchor sets. This paper provides finite sample complexity upper and lower bounds across four data generation setups (SCAR, SAR, PCS, APDS) without requiring knowledge of α, and introduces a new combinatorial parameter (claw number) to characterize when unlabeled samples are necessary.

## Input / Output

The PU learner receives two independent samples: b positively labeled instances drawn i.i.d. from a distribution P over X (which may differ from the target positive distribution D_+), and a unlabeled instances drawn i.i.d. from the marginal D_X. The output is a binary classifier f: X → {0,1}. The paper's primary deliverable is tight sample complexity bounds expressed in terms of VC-dimension d, accuracy ε, confidence δ, and setup-specific parameters (weight ratio r, margin γ, claw number h, class prior lower bound π), rather than a single algorithmic recipe.

## Method

The core algorithm is PERM (Positive Empirical Risk Minimizer), which selects the concept with the fewest positive predictions over the unlabeled sample among all concepts consistent with the positive sample, serving as a PU analogue of ERM. For the SAR setup, the paper leverages ε-net arguments combined with a bounded weight ratio R(P, D_+) ≥ r to show that shifted positive samples suffice with sample complexity O(d/(rε)). For the PCS setup, the paper adapts domain adaptation techniques by partitioning [0,1]^k into boxes of sidelength γ/√k, filtering positive samples to those in boxes hit by unlabeled data, and requiring a γ-margin assumption on the labeling function. For agnostic learning without known α, the paper introduces a Lagrangian PU empirical loss that jointly penalizes unlabeled misclassification rate and positive-sample disagreement, yielding a multiplicative approximation factor of max(α,1−α)/min(α,1−α) that is shown to be tight. Lower bounds are established via combinatorial arguments using the newly defined claw number (for unlabeled sample necessity) and a reduction to a generalized weighted die problem (for agnostic sample complexity).

## Results

The paper establishes finite sample complexity bounds for PU learning across multiple setups. For realizable SCAR learning, the positive sample complexity is Õ(d/ε) with a matching Ω̃(d/ε) lower bound, and the unlabeled sample complexity is Õ(d/ε) with a novel Ω̃(ℋ/ε) lower bound based on the introduced claw number ℋ. For agnostic SCAR learning with known class prior α, both positive and unlabeled sample complexities are Õ(d/ε²) with matching Ω̃(d/ε²) lower bounds. When α is unknown, the paper proves an impossibility result: no learner can achieve error below max(α,1−α)/min(α,1−α) times the approximation error, and shows this factor is tight via a Lagrangian PERM algorithm.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Liu et al. (2002), the paper provides a nearly tight lower bound Ω̃(d/ε) on positive sample complexity for realizable SCAR learning (Theorem 1), matching their upper bound up to logarithmic factors. Additionally, the paper introduces a new lower bound Ω̃(ℋ/ε) on unlabeled sample complexity via the claw number (Theorem 3), which extends and strengthens the impossibility result of Lee et al. (2025) by giving a concrete quantitative bound rather than merely showing unlabeled data is necessary. Against Du Plessis et al. (2015), the paper establishes a nearly matching lower bound Ω̃(d/ε²) on both positive and unlabeled sample complexities for agnostic SCAR learning with known α (Theorem 13), confirming their upper bounds are tight. The paper further goes beyond both baselines by providing the first finite sample complexity results for the SAR and PCS setups without requiring knowledge of the class prior, and the first generalization bounds for the APDS setup.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the theoretical analysis of PU learning beyond the SCAR setup and, critically, beyond the assumption that the class prior α is known. It builds directly on Liu et al. (2002)'s PERM algorithm and ε-net framework for the realizable SCAR case, on Ben-David and Urner (2012)'s domain adaptation machinery (weight ratios, margin assumptions) for the SAR and PCS setups, and on Ben-David et al. (2010)'s CΔC distance for the APDS setup. The key conceptual departure is that prior provable results in PU learning almost universally assume α is given to the learner; this paper systematically removes that assumption and characterizes what remains learnable (and what becomes impossible) without it.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The impossibility result (Theorem 14) — that without α, no PU learner can achieve error within a factor of max(α,1−α)/min(α,1−α) of the best in class — provides a clean, fundamental characterization of why the class prior assumption is not merely a convenience but a necessity for standard PU learning guarantees. The introduction of the claw number as a combinatorial parameter governing the need for unlabeled data, and the demonstration that the PCS setup incurs an exponential-in-1/γ sample cost relative to SAR, sharpen the understanding of when distribution shift in positive examples makes learning fundamentally harder. Open questions include closing the gaps between upper and lower bounds in the SAR and PCS settings, and whether the exponential unlabeled sample requirement in the PCS setup can be avoided with different algorithmic approaches.
