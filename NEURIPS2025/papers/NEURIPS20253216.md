## Problem

Existing region-level vision-language models suffer from three coupled limitations: they produce only narrow semantic outputs (typically a single category label or short caption), they are modality-specific (handling either images or videos but not both), and they rely on an external segmentation model in a serial pipeline that adds computational overhead and makes performance sensitive to mask quality. No prior work simultaneously supports segmentation, multi-granularity semantic explanation, and region-level streaming video captioning in a unified, efficient framework.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253216/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts a visual prompt (point, box, or mask) specifying a region of interest in a single image or a video clip (up to 16 frames by default), combined with a text instruction. It simultaneously outputs a segmentation mask and multiple semantic granularities: a fine-grained category label, a context-aware definition, a functional explanation of the region's role in the scene, and a detailed caption. For streaming video, it produces continuous region descriptions at user-specified timestamps, passing historical visual tokens (via an additional 2×2 pixel shuffle on the last frame) and prior textual descriptions into the next clip to maintain temporal coherence.

## Method

PAM extends SAM 2 by attaching a Semantic Perceiver—a lightweight two-layer transformer mirroring the S2-FFM module—that consumes enhanced mask tokens and updated image embeddings from SAM 2's backbone, concatenates them with 16 learnable semantic tokens, and outputs 64²×N visual tokens plus N×16 semantic tokens. A pixel-shuffle + MLP projector downsamples these tokens (2×2 for images, 4×4 for non-prompted video frames) before feeding them into a Qwen2.5 LLM (1.5B or 3B) that generates all semantic outputs in parallel with SAM 2's mask decoder. Training follows a three-stage curriculum with SAM 2 frozen throughout: Stage 1 aligns visual/semantic tokens with the LLM on image data, Stage 1.5 extends to video captions, and Stage 2 jointly fine-tunes the perceiver, projector, and LLM on a refined 1.5M image + 0.6M video region-semantic dataset. The dataset is constructed via a pipeline that applies Set-of-Mark prompting with GPT-4o and Qwen2.5-VL-72B to generate multi-granularity annotations, followed by rule-based filtering and manual QA, and includes a novel storyboard-driven method for temporally-aware video captions and event-based streaming caption data.

## Results

On region-level image captioning, PAM-3B achieves 26.9 METEOR / 143.1 CIDEr on RefCOCOg, 20.8 METEOR / 142.3 CIDEr on Visual Genome, and 31.3 ROUGE-L / 59.7 CIDEr on Ref-L4, outperforming most baselines while using fewer parameters. On video region captioning, PAM-3B sets new SOTA on Elysium (24.3 METEOR) and BensMOT (21.6 METEOR), and surpasses DAM-8B on HC-STVG (23.3 vs. 21.0 METEOR). For streaming region captioning on ActivityNet, PAM-3B achieves 27.3 METEOR and 2.67 G-STDC, outperforming Streaming Vid2Seq on METEOR (27.3 vs. 10.0) and achieving the best spatio-temporal continuity score. PAM runs 1.2–2.4× faster and uses less GPU memory than prior approaches.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest general-purpose baseline DAM-8B, PAM-3B wins on LVIS Semantic IoU (78.3 vs. 77.7), PACO (87.4 vs. 84.2), RefCOCOg METEOR (26.9, where DAM-8B reports no result), Ferret Bench (78.3), Elysium (24.3), BensMOT (21.6), and HC-STVG METEOR (23.3 vs. 21.0), but trails on Ref-L4 ROUGE-L (31.3 vs. 37.1) and VideoRefer-Bench-D average (3.14 vs. 3.68). Compared to VP-SPHINX-13B, PAM-3B surpasses it on VG (20.8 vs. 20.6 METEOR), RefCOCOg (26.9 vs. 23.9 METEOR), and Ferret Bench (78.3 vs. 77.4), while VP-SPHINX-13B retains a slight edge on MDVP Bench (74.3 vs. 72.2) and RefCOCOg CIDEr (162.5 vs. 143.1). On video benchmarks, PAM-3B outperforms VideoRefer-7B on Elysium (24.3 vs. –), BensMOT (21.6 vs. –), and HC-STVG (23.3 vs. 18.7 METEOR), but VideoRefer-7B leads on VideoRefer-Bench-D (3.46 vs. 3.14 Avg). For streaming captioning, PAM-3B substantially outperforms Streaming Vid2Seq on METEOR (27.3 vs. 10.0) and G-STDC (2.67 vs. –), though Streaming Vid2Seq achieves higher CIDEr (37.8 vs. 30.1). Overall, PAM-3B achieves competitive or superior results across 12 of 14 evaluated benchmarks while using fewer parameters (3B vs. 7B/8B/13B competitors) and 1.2–2.4× less inference time.

## Relation to Prior Work

PAM extends SAM 2 by repurposing its segmentation backbone as a vision encoder for a language model, departing from prior region-level VLMs that either encode bounding-box coordinates as text, add separate visual-prompt embedding modules, or rely on an external mask generator in a serial pipeline. The key architectural departure is the Semantic Perceiver, a lightweight two-layer transformer that converts SAM 2's S2-FFM intermediate features (enhanced mask tokens and updated image embeddings) into LLM-compatible visual and semantic tokens, enabling a parallel mask-and-semantic decoder design. On the data side, the paper introduces a storyboard-driven caption expansion pipeline and, for the first time, region-level streaming video caption annotations, extending prior coarse referring-video-segmentation labels into temporally-aware, event-segmented descriptions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PAM provides a practical, efficient (1.2–2.4× faster, lower GPU memory) unified model that simultaneously segments and generates multi-granularity semantic outputs (category, definition, contextual function, detailed caption) for both images and videos, which prior methods handled in a modality-specific or serial fashion. The introduction of the first region-level streaming video caption dataset and the G-STDC continuity metric opens a new evaluation axis for temporal consistency in video understanding. Key open questions include reducing the visual-token bottleneck to achieve true real-time streaming, extending beyond the four supported task types (e.g., VQA, dialogue), and mitigating the observed failure modes where the model defaults to salient objects when the prompted region is small or temporarily occluded.
