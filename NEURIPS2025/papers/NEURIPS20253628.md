## Problem

While human motion generation from music, speech, and text has been extensively studied, spatial audio-driven motion synthesis remains entirely unexplored. Spatial audio signals encode not only semantic content but also directional cues, distance, and intensity variations that fundamentally shape how humans physically respond (e.g., turning toward a soft sound vs. flinching away from a sharp one), yet no dataset or generative framework existed to model this relationship.

## Input / Output

The method consumes binaural spatial audio recorded at the actor's ear positions, a per-frame sound source location (SSL) in the character's local SMPL-X coordinate system, and a discrete motion genre label (dull, neutral, or sensitive). Audio is processed into a 2272-dimensional feature vector per sequence (1136 per ear) combining MFCCs, tempograms, RMS energy, and onset features via librosa. The output is a 240-frame (8-second at 30 FPS) 3D body motion in SMPL-X format using 25 body joints (fingers excluded), parameterized as global positions, 6D local rotations, and joint velocities (300 dims per frame).

## Method

MOSPA is a diffusion-based generative model using an encoder-only Transformer (512 latent dim, 8 heads, 4 layers) with x0-prediction to denoise noisy motion vectors conditioned on spatial audio features, SSL, and motion genre. A key design choice is the residual feature fusion mechanism that augments standard translation and rotation with global joint positions and velocities, capturing subtle spatial-audio-to-motion correspondences. Random masking is applied to both audio features and SSL before concatenation into the token sequence, enabling the model to learn robust conditioning. Training uses a composite loss combining MSE on clean samples and their frame-to-frame deltas, forward-kinematics geometric losses, foot-contact constraints, and weighted trajectory/rotation losses (with λ_traj and λ_rot increased to 3 at epoch 5000 of 6000 to accelerate convergence of displacement direction). The full pipeline trains in ~18 hours on a single RTX 4090 with batch size 128 and a cosine noise schedule over 1000 diffusion steps.

## Results

On the SAM dataset, MOSPA achieves the best R-precision (0.937/0.984/0.996 for Top-1/2/3) and the lowest FID (7.981) among all methods, substantially outperforming the strongest baseline EDGE (R-precision 0.886/0.960/0.977, FID 13.993). MOSPA's generated motions also most closely match real motion in diversity (23.575 vs. 23.616 for real) and APD (53.915 vs. 59.435 for real), indicating well-balanced variation and precision. The weakest baselines, Bailando and LODGE, show dramatically higher FID values (168.396 and 102.289 respectively), reflecting their inability to handle short-duration, non-musical spatial audio.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MOSPA outperforms all four baselines on every reported metric. Against the closest competitor EDGE (a diffusion-based dance generation model), MOSPA improves R-precision Top-1 by 5.1 points (0.937 vs. 0.886) and reduces FID by 43% (7.981 vs. 13.993), while also matching real-motion diversity more closely. POPDG, another diffusion-based method, trails further behind with FID of 20.967 and R-precision Top-1 of only 0.762. LODGE and Bailando, both designed for long-form music-to-dance generation, perform poorly on this short-duration spatial audio task: LODGE achieves FID 102.289 and Bailando a catastrophic FID of 168.396, with Bailando's R-precision Top-1 of just 0.077 indicating near-random alignment with ground truth. The paper attributes these failures to the baselines' specialization in music-like audio and their inability to process rapid spatial audio changes or directional cues.

## Relation to Prior Work

MOSPA builds directly on the diffusion-based motion generation framework established by Tevet et al. (MDM, ICLR 2023) and Chen et al. (SIGGRAPH 2024), adopting their x0-prediction strategy and encoder-only Transformer denoiser. It incorporates the residual feature fusion mechanism from Guo et al. (MDM, CVPR 2022) to jointly model global joint positions, local rotations, and velocities. The key conceptual departure is from the broader audio-to-motion literature (music-to-motion, speech-to-motion), which treats audio as a purely temporal/semantic signal; MOSPA instead conditions on binaural spatial audio with explicit sound source location, introducing a spatial dimension to the conditioning that prior methods do not model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first dataset and benchmark for spatial audio-driven human motion generation, opening a research direction relevant to VR, robotics, and human-computer interaction where characters must respond to directional sound. However, the evaluation is limited by the absence of true spatial-audio-to-motion baselines—comparisons rely on adapted music-to-motion methods that were never designed for this setting, making the SOTA claim somewhat circular. Key open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond the 12 subjects and 27 scenarios in SAM, whether physical plausibility can be enforced (the paper acknowledges this gap), and whether scene-aware or full-body (hand/face) extensions are feasible.
