## Problem

Generating regular time series from irregularly-sampled data remains challenging: existing methods (GT-GAN, KoVAE) rely on GANs/VAEs with computationally expensive NCDE preprocessing and assume imputed values reflect the true distribution. While image-based diffusion models like ImagenTime offer a promising backbone, naively masking missing values as zeros creates 'unnatural' pixel neighborhoods that corrupt convolutional kernel learning, degrading generation quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255731/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes multivariate time series with non-uniform sampling times and missing values (e.g., 30–70% missing rates) and produces regularly-sampled sequences of the same length from the learned distribution. Time series are converted to images via delay embedding (e.g., a 65k-step series maps to a 256×256 image), and a binary mask tracks which image pixels correspond to observed versus imputed values. Inference uses 18 sampling steps with a second-order ODE solver, yielding near-constant inference time regardless of sequence length.

## Method

The core contribution is a two-step framework that decouples neighborhood construction from distribution learning. First, a Time Series Transformer (TST) autoencoder completes irregular sequences, replacing zero-filled gaps with values close to the true distribution so that convolutional kernels operate on natural pixel neighborhoods. Second, a vision-based diffusion model (U-Net over delay-embedded images) is trained with a masked loss that computes reconstruction error only on observed pixels, treating TST completions as a weak conditioning signal rather than ground truth. This combination lets the model benefit from structured neighborhoods while remaining robust to imputation errors. The authors also modify the inverse delay embedding to average all corresponding image pixels per time step rather than selecting only the first, improving reconstruction fidelity.

## Results

On the standard length-24 benchmark (averaged over 30%, 50%, 70% missing rates), the proposed method achieves a mean relative improvement of 74.2% in discriminative score, 15.0% in predictive MAE, 78.5% in Context-FID, and 62.1% in correlation score over the second-best method (KoVAE). Training time is reduced by approximately 6.5× (e.g., 0.21 h vs. 2.04 h on Stock at length 24; 5.38 h vs. 31.53 h on ETTh1 at length 768). For ultra-long sequences (length 10,920) on KDD-Cup, the method still achieves a 36.6% discriminative improvement (0.155 vs. 0.375 at 30% missing rate).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary competitor is KoVAE [39], the most recent state-of-the-art method for irregular time series generation. The proposed method outperforms KoVAE on discriminative score across all 10 datasets at length 24 (e.g., 0.008 vs. 0.118 on Stock, 0.037 vs. 0.197 on ETTh1), with the largest gaps on ETT and Stock datasets. On predictive MAE, the method wins on 8 of 10 datasets, with a marginal loss on Electricity (0.049 vs. 0.047). GT-GAN [26] and TimeGAN-Δt [58] are substantially weaker, with TimeGAN-Δt often near the random-guess level (discriminative ≈ 0.499). In ablation comparisons, replacing NCDE with TST in KoVAE (KoVAE+TST) does not close the gap, and combining TST imputation with other diffusion backbones (TransFusion+TST, TimeAutoDiff+TST) also underperforms, confirming that the vision-based diffusion backbone with masking is the critical component. The method also achieves a ~6.5× training speedup over KoVAE (e.g., 1.28 h vs. 6.49 h on ETTh1 at length 24) and maintains near-constant inference time regardless of sequence length, unlike KoVAE whose cost grows linearly.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends ImagenTime's vision-based diffusion framework for regular time series generation to the irregular setting, and replaces the computationally expensive NCDE preprocessing used by GT-GAN and KoVAE with a lightweight Time Series Transformer (TST) autoencoder. The key conceptual departure from prior irregular-generation work is treating the completed sequence not as ground truth but as a 'weak conditioning signal': the diffusion model learns from natural neighborhoods created by TST completion while a masking mechanism ensures the loss is computed only on observed values, preventing over-reliance on imputed data. This two-step completion-plus-masking strategy is positioned as a general, architecture-agnostic alternative to kernel-level masking, which would require per-architecture modifications.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper closes a substantial performance gap between regular and irregular time series generation (prior SOTA was ~540% worse on irregular benchmarks) while reducing training time by ~6.5× over KoVAE, making diffusion-based generation practical for long sequences up to 10,920 steps. The 'unnatural neighborhood' diagnosis—showing that zero-filled missing values corrupt convolutional kernels even when masked from the loss—provides a useful conceptual tool for understanding why naive masking fails in image-based time series models. Open questions include whether the two-step pipeline can be unified end-to-end, how the approach extends to multimodal or categorical time series, and whether adaptive masking that dynamically adjusts completion reliance could further reduce sensitivity to imputation quality.
