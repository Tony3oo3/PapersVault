## Problem

Despite widespread adoption, popular KGQA benchmarks such as WebQSP and CWQ suffer from critical quality issues: a manual audit of 16 datasets reveals only 57% average factual correctness, with WebQSP at 52% and CWQ at 49.3%, due to outdated annotations, ambiguous or trivial questions, and incomplete answer sets. Additionally, rigid exact-match evaluation penalizes semantically correct answers expressed in alternative surface forms. No existing framework systematically addresses both the construction of verifiable, high-quality KGQA instances and the evaluation methodology simultaneously.

## Input / Output

The framework consumes seed entities drawn from Wikipedia's Level-5 Vital Articles and the full Wikidata knowledge graph as its knowledge source. Each generated instance is a structured tuple comprising a natural language question, a gold answer set, a minimal supporting subgraph (the specific triples justifying the answer), and an executable SPARQL query. The SPARQL query serves a dual purpose: it formally encodes the reasoning path and enables automated re-validation as the underlying KG evolves, making the dataset maintainable over time.

## Method

KGQAGen is a three-stage LLM-in-the-loop pipeline. First, a seed subgraph is initialized by sampling ~15 one-hop neighbors of a seed entity from Wikidata. Second, an iterative expansion loop alternates between one-hop KG traversal around LLM-selected exploration entities and a GPT-4.1 sufficiency check that judges whether the subgraph supports at least 2-hop, unambiguous question generation; the LLM prioritizes semantically specific entities (e.g., named people, awards) over generic ones (e.g., countries) to guide expansion. Third, once the subgraph is deemed sufficient, a unified prompt jointly generates the question, answer set, supporting subgraph, and SPARQL query, which is then executed against Wikidata for symbolic verification—if results mismatch, GPT-4o-mini revises the query up to three times before the instance is discarded. This combination of LLM-guided semantic expansion (avoiding the noise of blind BFS/DFS) and SPARQL-based symbolic verification (guaranteeing factual grounding) is the key mechanism distinguishing KGQAGen from prior template- or rule-based generators.

## Results

On KGQAGen-10k, the strongest pure LLM (GPT-4.1) achieves only 47.43% EM / 56.96% LASM accuracy, while the best KG-RAG system (PoG with GPT-4o) reaches 50.67% EM / 60.18% LASM accuracy. When given the ground-truth supporting subgraph (LLM-SP), GPT-4o jumps to 82.46% EM / 84.89% LASM accuracy, revealing that retrieval—not reasoning—is the primary bottleneck. LASM consistently outperforms EM by 5–10 points across all models, confirming that exact-match evaluation systematically underestimates true performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among pure LLMs, GPT-4.1 leads with 47.43% EM accuracy, narrowly ahead of GPT-4o (45.29%) and DeepSeek-Chat (42.48%), while smaller open-source models (LLaMA2-7B at 6.88%, LLaMA-3.1-8B at 8.87%) perform poorly. KG-RAG systems modestly outperform their LLM backbones: PoG (GPT-4o) gains ~5.4 points over GPT-4o alone, ToG gains ~4.4 points, and GCR gains ~13.7 points over its LLaMA-3.1 backbone, though RoG (LLaMA2-7B) only adds ~13 points over LLaMA2-7B's 6.88%. The LLM-SP setting (ground-truth subgraph provided) dramatically outperforms all other configurations—GPT-4o(w/SP) reaches 82.46% EM vs. 45.29% without, and LLaMA2-7B(w/SP) reaches 69.79% vs. 6.88%—demonstrating that retrieval quality, not reasoning, is the dominant bottleneck. Cross-dataset comparison shows that models achieving 85–92% Hit@1 on WebQSP/CWQ drop to 21–54% on KGQAGen-10k, confirming that legacy benchmarks overestimate true multi-hop reasoning ability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the standard trajectory of KGQA research (which typically proposes new reasoning or retrieval methods evaluated on existing benchmarks) by instead auditing the benchmarks themselves. It builds conceptually on prior automated benchmark generation work (Maestro, CHATTY-Gen, Dynamic-KGQA, SmartBench) but differentiates through a closed-loop design combining LLM-guided subgraph expansion with SPARQL-based symbolic verification, ensuring factual grounding that prior rule-based or template-based generators lack. The KG-RAG methods it evaluates (RoG, GCR, ToG, PoG) represent the current state of the art in graph-constrained LLM reasoning, positioning KGQAGen-10k as a stress test for these systems rather than a new reasoning architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that the two most widely-used KGQA benchmarks (WebQSP, CWQ) have only ~50% factual correctness is a significant credibility concern for the field, as nearly 30 recent papers rely on them for evaluation. The LASM metric addresses a real and previously underappreciated limitation of exact-match scoring. Open questions include whether KGQAGen scales beyond Wikidata to domain-specific KGs, whether the 96% manual accuracy holds at 100K+ scale, and whether the large gap between LLM-SP (85%) and best KG-RAG (60%) will narrow as retrieval improves. The framework's dependence on LLM quality for subgraph selection and question phrasing remains a potential bottleneck for reproducibility.
