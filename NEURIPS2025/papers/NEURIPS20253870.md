## Problem

Existing spatial transcriptomics clustering methods rely exclusively on gene expression profiles and spatial coordinates, overlooking the biological priors encoded in the DNA sequences of expressed genes. This omission can cause functionally mismatched clusters when spatial spots share similar expression levels but express genes with fundamentally different roles (e.g., a non-coding RNA versus a mitochondrial protein-coding gene). No prior work has systematically incorporated nucleotide-level sequence information into ST clustering, largely due to the absence of sequence-annotated ST datasets and the lack of a principled cross-modal integration strategy.

## Input / Output

For each spatial spot, the method consumes a gene expression vector (over ~33,000 genes, reduced to highly variable genes), 2D spatial coordinates, and variable-length reference DNA sequences of expressed genes retrieved from NCBI. The sequences are tokenized and encoded by a frozen pretrained Nucleotide Transformer into fixed-dimensional gene-level embeddings before aggregation. The output is a unified spot-level embedding in a shared latent space from which K spatial domain clusters are extracted via standard clustering, with the embedding simultaneously supporting ZINB-based expression reconstruction.

## Method

SAINT operates two parallel encoding branches: a structure-aware graph module that builds spatial, feature, and combined adjacency graphs over spots and encodes each via GCN with attention-based multi-view fusion, and a sequence-aware encoder that maps tokenized DNA sequences through a pretrained Nucleotide Transformer and aggregates gene-level embeddings per spot using a lightweight attention pooling mechanism (with an optional expression-weighted averaging variant). The two branch outputs are concatenated and projected through a two-layer MLP late-fusion module into a shared latent space. Training jointly minimizes a ZINB reconstruction loss (modeling dropout and over-dispersion), a graph contrastive regularization that enforces local smoothness, and a cross-modal decorrelation loss (DICR) that penalizes redundancy between the structural and sequence branches, encouraging them to capture complementary information. The framework is modular and transferable: the sequence encoder and fusion module can be attached to existing backbones (e.g., Spatial-MGCN, MAFN) without retraining the full pipeline, and the authors construct sequence-augmented datasets across 14 tissue sections from DLPFC, HBC, and MBA benchmarks to enable reproducible evaluation.

## Results

SAINT-SA achieves the best or second-best ARI and NMI across nearly all 14 tissue sections (8 DLPFC slices, HBC, MBA), improving average ARI from 0.64 (Spatial-MGCN) to 0.68 and average NMI from 0.69 to 0.71. On the most challenging slice 151507, SAINT-SA reaches ARI 0.75 (+10.3% over MAFN's 0.68) and NMI 0.78; on slice 151671 it attains NMI 0.84 (+16.2% over GraphST's 0.72). Even the simpler SAINT-G variant (average pooling) surpasses all baselines on most slices, e.g., ARI 0.74 on 151507 vs. MAFN's 0.68. On HBC and MBA, SAINT-SA achieves ARI 0.66/0.46 and NMI 0.70/0.74, outperforming the next-best method (MAFN) by +3.1% ARI on HBC.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAINT-SA outperforms all nine baselines on the majority of slices for both ARI and NMI. Against the strongest baseline MAFN, SAINT-SA gains +7 to +10 ARI points on DLPFC slices (e.g., 0.75 vs. 0.68 on 151507; 0.90 vs. 0.82 on 151671) and +6 ARI on HBC (0.66 vs. 0.60). Compared to Spatial-MGCN, gains range from +12 ARI on 151508 (0.68 vs. 0.46) to +8 on 151672 (0.85 vs. 0.77). Against GraphST, SAINT-SA leads by up to +16 NMI on 151671 (0.84 vs. 0.72) and +27 ARI on 151508 (0.68 vs. 0.49). The simpler SAINT-G variant (mean-pooled sequence embeddings) already beats MAFN on 8 of 10 sections, confirming that even basic sequence integration adds value. On the more homogeneous MBA dataset, gains are smaller (+3 ARI over MAFN) but still consistent, indicating robustness across tissue types.

## Relation to Prior Work

SAINT extends the multi-view GNN-based spatial transcriptomics clustering lineage (SpaGCN → Spatial-MGCN → MAFN) by introducing a third modality—gene-level DNA sequence embeddings from a pretrained Nucleotide Transformer—into the representation pipeline. Architecturally, it inherits the spatial/feature/combined graph GCN backbone and ZINB decoder from MAFN, then appends a sequence-aware attention-pooling branch and a late-fusion module with a cross-modal decorrelation (DICR) loss borrowed from graph clustering. The key conceptual departure is treating nucleotide sequences as a first-class biological prior for spatial domain discovery, a direction the authors note has not been systematically explored in prior ST work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first sequence-augmented ST datasets (14 tissue sections across DLPFC, HBC, MBA) and demonstrates that nucleotide-level priors yield consistent, transferable clustering gains across heterogeneous tissue contexts, opening a path toward multi-omics-informed spatial analysis. However, the reliance on a single pretrained genomic model (Nucleotide Transformer) introduces potential biases toward well-annotated human genes, and the framework has only been validated on three benchmarks with a fixed HVG filtering strategy. Open questions include whether sequence priors generalize to deconvolution or trajectory inference tasks, how they behave on non-model organisms or single-cell (non-spatial) data, and whether the modest average gains (~4–5% ARI) persist at larger scale or with more diverse sequence encoders.
