## Problem

Parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) methods like LoRA and AdaptFormer preserve tail-class accuracy on foundation models such as CLIP but degrade head-class performance, and this trade-off shifts depending on the head-tail ratio (η), a structural factor previously overlooked alongside the imbalance ratio (ρ). No existing method—PEFT, full fine-tuning, or traditional model soups—maintains balanced accuracy across the full spectrum of long-tailed configurations, from tail-heavy to head-heavy distributions.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images and outputs a class label via a prototypical classification head initialized from CLIP text-encoder embeddings (e.g., 'a photo of a cat'). The backbone is a CLIP ViT-B/16 vision transformer, and the classifier applies l2-normalized features with a learned temperature. The method is designed to operate across datasets with imbalance ratios ranging from 100 to 6,491 and head-tail ratios from 0.11 to 5.66, with evaluation split into many-shot (>100 samples), medium-shot (20–100), and few-shot (<20) class subsets.

## Method

LT-Soups is a two-stage model-merging framework. In Stage 1, N models are trained in parallel, each on a subsampled dataset with a distinct exponentially increasing imbalance ratio (ρ_i = 2^i), with M bootstrapped models per subset for variance reduction and EMA for regularization. These models are merged via recursive interpolation θ_n = (1−λ)θ_n + λθ_{n−1} starting from the pretrained weights, which preserves CLIP's zero-shot capabilities while integrating head and tail representations. In Stage 2, only the linear classifier is fine-tuned on the full dataset using logit-adjustment loss with the backbone frozen, recovering head-class sharpness lost during subsampling. The recursive (rather than uniform) merging strategy gives more weight to data-rich, higher-imbalance subsets when the target domain diverges from CLIP's pretraining distribution, and the overall design is partially insensitive to the choice of class-balancing loss due to the structural regularization from multi-ratio subsampling.

## Results

On the three synthetic long-tailed benchmarks, LT-Soups achieves the best overall accuracy on all datasets: 83.5% on CIFAR100-LT (ρ=100), 51.7% on Places-LT (ρ=996), and 77.4% on ImageNet-LT (ρ=256). It also leads on the few-shot split across all three (78.0%, 50.3%, 70.7% respectively) while maintaining competitive many-shot accuracy (88.2%, 51.2%, 81.2%). On real-world datasets, LT-Soups reaches 39.3% on NIH-CXR-LT (ρ=6491) and 78.2% on iNaturalist 2018 (ρ=500), with a notable +5.4-point few-shot gain over PEFT on NIH-CXR-LT (30.9% vs 25.5%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LT-Soups outperforms the strongest PEFT baseline (LIFT [50]) on all three synthetic datasets in overall accuracy (CIFAR100-LT: 83.5 vs 81.3; Places-LT: 51.7 vs 51.5; ImageNet-LT: 77.4 vs 77.0), with the largest gap on CIFAR100-LT where it also recovers 2.8 points on the few-shot split (78.0 vs 77.1) while maintaining 3.0 points higher many-shot accuracy (88.2 vs 85.2). Against Model Soups [60], LT-Soups gains 1.4 points on CIFAR100-LT, 2.3 on Places-LT, and 1.4 on ImageNet-LT overall, with the most dramatic improvement on Places-LT few-shot (50.3 vs 43.7). On the real-world NIH-CXR-LT dataset, LT-Soups achieves the best overall accuracy (39.3%) and a substantial +5.4-point few-shot advantage over PEFT (30.9 vs 25.5), though it trails PEFT slightly on iNaturalist overall (78.2 vs 79.1) while offering more balanced performance across many/medium/few splits. LT-Soups also consistently surpasses Full-FT, cRT [25], BALLAD [37], LPT [13], and Decoder [58] across all reported datasets and splits.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Model Soups framework (Wortsman et al., ICML 2022) to the long-tailed classification setting by replacing uniform averaging over identically-trained models with recursive interpolation over models trained on subsets of progressively increasing imbalance ratios. It departs from the PEFT/LoRA line of work (Shi et al., ICML 2024) by showing that parameter-efficient fine-tuning's tail-class advantage is conditional on the head-tail ratio, and by using full-rank fine-tuning on controlled subsamples instead. The introduction of the head-tail ratio (η) as a complementary axis to the imbalance ratio (ρ) for characterizing long-tailed distributions is a new analytical contribution that reframes how imbalance structure affects method selection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of the head-tail ratio as a critical, previously overlooked factor governing the PEFT vs. full fine-tuning trade-off provides a useful diagnostic for practitioners choosing adaptation strategies for foundation models under imbalance. LT-Soups offers an inference-efficient (single-model) alternative to ensemble-based long-tailed methods while maintaining competitive accuracy across the full imbalance spectrum, which is practically valuable for deployment. Limitations include reliance on a fixed 100-sample threshold for the head/tail split, single-seed experiments, and evaluation only on CLIP ViT-B/16; whether the approach generalizes to other foundation model architectures or benefits from more nuanced imbalance parameterizations (e.g., generalized Pareto) remains open.
