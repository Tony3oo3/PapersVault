## Problem

Contrastively pretrained vision-language models (CLIP, SigLIP) produce global embeddings that fail to capture fine-grained distinctions critical for instance-level retrieval over very large image collections. Existing remedies such as local-feature re-ranking pipelines and large VLM re-rankers improve accuracy but incur orders-of-magnitude higher query-time computation, making them impractical for real-time or million-scale deployments. The paper asks whether a lightweight, query-conditioned adaptation of the embedding space can close this gap without that overhead.

## Input / Output

At inference, QuARI consumes a single global embedding vector (dimension E, e.g. 768 or 1024) produced by a frozen VLM backbone for the query, plus a precomputed database of N image embeddings from the same backbone. It outputs a rank-64 linear transformation matrix T in R^{E x E} and an adapted query vector q-prime; retrieval is then a single pass of T over all N database vectors followed by cosine-similarity ranking. Because T is low-rank and linear, applying it to millions of stored embeddings costs only a matrix-vector product per image, keeping query latency near that of the base ANN search.

## Method

QuARI is a transformer-based hypernetwork H_theta that, given a query embedding q, iteratively refines 2r zero-initialized tokens (r U-tokens and r V-tokens) over L denoising steps using a shared transformer encoder conditioned on q via a two-layer MLP plus learned timestep embeddings. After L steps, separate MLP decoders map the refined tokens to vectors u_j and v_j in R^E, and the transformation is assembled as the low-rank sum T = sum_j u_j v_j^T (rank r=64); the adapted query q-prime is decoded from the conditioning token. Training uses a symmetric contrastive objective with semi-positive samples (top-2 of 100 precomputed nearest neighbors per target) to prevent the transformation from overfitting to exact query-target pairs, and injects uniform noise into query embeddings (borrowed from LinCIR) to bridge the text-image modality gap so the model generalizes to image queries. Ablations show the database transformation contributes far more than the query transformation (33.4 vs. 23.2 mAP@1k on ILIAS I2I@100M), and that removing iterative refinement, semi-positives, or noise each cause 5-15 mAP drops.

## Results

On ILIAS, QuARI consistently improves mAP@1k across all backbone encoders: with SigLIP2 ViT-L, image-to-image retrieval at 100M scale jumps from 20.8 to 36.2 (+15.4) and text-to-image at 5M from 24.7 to 41.2 (+16.5). On INQUIRE, SigLIP2+QuARI achieves 45.7 mAP@50 and 55.2 nDCG@50, surpassing all open-source VLMs and approaching GPT-4o (43.7 mAP@50). In re-ranking on ILIAS top-1k, QuARI reaches 29.1 mAP@1k, outperforming the strongest local-feature baseline AMES (26.4) while using only precomputed global embeddings. On standard benchmarks (COCO, Flickr30k, FORB, TextCaps), SigLIP2+QuARI improves R@1 from 55.2 to 77.4 on COCO and from 85.3 to 92.9 on Flickr30k.

## Baselines & Comparisons

QuARI outperforms every baseline across all evaluated settings. Against frozen VLM backbones (CLIP, SigLIP, SigLIP2, OpenCLIP), QuARI adds 4.2–18.9 mAP@1k on ILIAS, with the largest gains on SigLIP2 ViT-L (+15.4 I2I, +18.9 T2I). Against static task adaptation (APE-style projections [31]), QuARI trained on the same 1M Universal Embeddings sample still wins by 13.1–19.7 mAP@1k, demonstrating that query-specific adaptation substantially exceeds a single fixed projection. In ILIAS re-ranking, QuARI (29.1 mAP@1k) beats all local-feature methods: αQE variants (14.3–22.1), Chamfer Similarity (22.9), Spatial Verification (21.8), and AMES (26.4). On INQUIRE text-to-image re-ranking, SigLIP2+QuARI (45.7 mAP@50) surpasses all open-source VLMs—BLIP-2 (31.2), InstructBLIP (33.0), PaliGemma (35.6), LLaVA-1.5/1.6 (32.2–35.7), VILA-13B/40B (35.7–40.2)—and also exceeds GPT-4V (36.5) and GPT-4o (43.7), while operating on precomputed global features with orders-of-magnitude lower inference cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

QuARI extends the hypernetwork paradigm (Ha et al., 2016) and the transformer-as-meta-learner idea (Chen & Wang, 2022) to the retrieval setting, using a small transformer to predict a query-conditioned low-rank projection matrix rather than full encoder weights as in HyperCLIP. It departs from static task adaptation methods (APE, domain-specific linear projections) by making the transformation query-specific, and from expensive re-ranking pipelines (ColBERT, AMES, re-ranking transformers, VLM re-rankers) by shifting all adaptation into a single lightweight per-query computation. The noise-injection training trick is borrowed from LinCIR (Gu et al., 2024) to bridge the text-image modality gap and enable image-query generalization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

QuARI demonstrates that a query-conditioned linear projection over precomputed global embeddings can match or exceed the retrieval quality of expensive VLM re-rankers (outperforming GPT-4o on INQUIRE) while being orders of magnitude faster, making high-quality fine-grained retrieval practical at 100M+ scale. The key limitation is that a linear transformation cannot recover information absent from the base embedding, and the method is still bounded by the initial top-k candidate set—though its efficiency allows deploying over very large k. Open questions include whether non-linear or higher-rank adaptations can close the remaining gap to oracle re-ranking, and whether the approach transfers to other modalities or retrieval settings beyond vision-language.
