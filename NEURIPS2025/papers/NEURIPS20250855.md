## Problem

Existing visual token pruning methods for MLLMs fall into two camps: attention-based pruning retains many duplicate tokens because it only scores individual importance, and similarity-based pruning ignores the user instruction, always selecting the same tokens regardless of the question. Neither approach jointly models feature diversity and instruction relevance, leading to suboptimal performance especially at high reduction ratios.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250855/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a sequence of n visual tokens (e.g., 576 from LLaVA-1.5 at 336×336, 2880 from LLaVA-NeXT at 672×672, or 10,816 from LLaVA-Video with 64 frames) and a text instruction as input, and outputs a pruned subset of m visual tokens (m ≪ n). For models with paired vision-text encoders (e.g., CLIP), visual and text embeddings are taken directly from the encoders; for models with only a visual encoder, the multimodal projector output serves as visual embeddings and the mean of instruction token embeddings from the LLM serves as the text embedding. The method is training-free and model-agnostic, requiring no architectural modifications.

## Method

CDPruner first computes a pairwise cosine-similarity kernel matrix L over visual tokens, then computes per-token instruction relevance scores via cosine similarity between each visual token and the instruction embedding, normalized to [0,1]. The kernel is modulated as L̃ = diag(r̃)·L·diag(r̃), which decomposes the log-determinant of any subset into a relevance term plus a diversity term, unifying both objectives in a single DPP. The optimal subset is found via greedy MAP inference with Cholesky decomposition in O(nm²) time, guaranteeing a (1−1/e) approximation with less than 10 ms additional latency per sample. This formulation ensures the selected subset is both diverse (avoiding redundant tokens) and instruction-conditioned (prioritizing regions relevant to the current question), unlike prior methods that optimize only one of these criteria.

## Results

On LLaVA-1.5-7B (576 tokens), CDPruner retains 99.0% of original accuracy (62.5% avg) at 77.8% token reduction (128 tokens), 97.0% at 88.9% reduction (64 tokens), and 94.3% at 94.4% reduction (32 tokens). On LLaVA-NeXT-7B (2880 tokens), it maintains 100.1% relative performance at 77.8% reduction and 96.0% at 94.4% reduction. Efficiency analysis on LLaVA-NeXT shows a ×9.9 FLOPs reduction, ×6.6 prefill speedup, and 17% GPU memory reduction while achieving the highest POPE F1 score (87.2) among all pruning methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CDPruner consistently outperforms all 8 baselines across every reduction ratio tested. Against attention-based methods (FastV, PDrop, SparseVLM), the gap is most dramatic at high reduction ratios: at 64 tokens, FastV drops to 74.9% relative performance while CDPruner maintains 97.0%, a 22.1-point gap. Against attention&similarity methods (PruMerge+, TRIM, VisionZip), CDPruner leads by 1.4–2.6% relative performance at 128 tokens and by 2.6–3.4% at 64 tokens, with VisionZip being the closest competitor. Against similarity-based methods (DART, DivPrune), CDPruner's advantage grows with reduction ratio: at 32 tokens, it beats DivPrune by 3.0% relative performance (94.3% vs 91.3%) and DART by 5.7%. Notably, CDPruner even exceeds the unpruned LLaVA-1.5 baseline on POPE (87.7 vs 85.9 at 128 tokens), suggesting pruning can mitigate hallucination. The method also generalizes to Qwen2.5-VL, where it retains 85.2% performance at 90.1% reduction versus 79.9% for DivPrune and 79.0% for FastV.

## Relation to Prior Work

CDPruner builds on the determinantal point process (DPP) framework from Kulesza et al. (2012) and the fast greedy MAP inference algorithm from Chen et al. (2018), repurposing them for visual token selection in MLLMs. It departs from the three existing families of token pruning methods—attention-based (FastV, PyramidDrop, SparseVLM), attention-and-similarity-based (PruMerge, TRIM, VisionZip), and similarity-based (DART, DivPrune)—by jointly modeling feature similarity and instruction relevance within a single DPP formulation. Unlike DivPrune, which casts pruning as a Max-Min Diversity Problem focusing on extreme pairwise distances, CDPruner leverages DPP's global diversity modeling while conditioning the kernel on instruction relevance, enabling dynamic, query-aware pruning without requiring attention scores.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As a training-free, model-agnostic, plug-and-play method with <10ms overhead, CDPruner is immediately deployable across diverse MLLM architectures and compatible with FlashAttention, addressing a practical gap that attention-based methods cannot fill. The finding that pruning can actually reduce hallucination (POPE scores exceeding the unpruned baseline) opens an intriguing direction for understanding the relationship between token redundancy and hallucination. Open questions include whether the DPP formulation can be adapted for multi-turn dialogues where future questions are unknown, and whether the instruction-conditioning mechanism generalizes to scenarios with minimal or ambiguous textual context (as seen on VizWiz).
