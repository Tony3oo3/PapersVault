## Problem

Agent-Based Models (ABMs) govern agent behavior through non-differentiable stochastic rules, which blocks gradient-based calibration and integration with real-world data. Existing surrogate approaches learn only macro-level mappings from parameters to aggregate statistics, ignoring individual agent dynamics. No principled, fully automated method existed to derive a differentiable, learnable model of per-agent stochastic transitions directly from ABM-generated data.

## Input / Output

The model takes as input each agent's state vector at time t (position, type/color, and life phase for Predator-Prey) together with the known interaction graph G_t defining local neighborhoods. Only the time-varying ('dynamical') features are fed to the diffusion model; static attributes like color or species are excluded from the denoising target. The output is a sample from the conditional distribution over the next agent state Z_{t+1}^{(i)}, generated by iteratively denoising Gaussian noise over τ_max=100 steps. Training uses a 'ramification' dataset where one main branch supplies conditioning contexts and R sibling branches (R=500) supply multiple stochastic targets per context, avoiding exponential history growth.

## Method

The Graph Diffusion Network (GDN) couples a message-passing GNN with a conditional denoising diffusion model. The GNN aggregates each agent's state with its neighbors' states via a permutation-invariant operator (sum or mean) and an MLP to produce an interaction embedding g_t^{(i)} capturing local relational context. This embedding, together with the agent's current state and a sinusoidal diffusion-step embedding, forms a condition vector injected into the hidden layers of a symmetric feed-forward denoising network (widths [128,256,1024,1024,256,128]) via element-wise addition with residual connections. Training minimizes the standard denoising MSE loss over all agents, timesteps, and diffusion steps, with gradients backpropagated through both the diffusion model and the GNN end-to-end. The key design choice is modeling each agent's transition distribution independently conditioned on its neighborhood, rather than predicting system-level outputs, which preserves the decentralized, bottom-up logic of the original ABM and prevents the surrogate from exploiting macro-level shortcuts.

## Results

On the Schelling segregation model, the GDN surrogate achieves a macro-level sMAPE below 0.2 across all three tolerance thresholds (ξ₁=0.625, ξ₂=0.75, ξ₃=0.875), while the diffusion-only ablation fails to distinguish happy from unhappy agents and the GNN-only ablation always yields higher sMAPE. On the Predator-Prey ecosystem, the surrogate achieves its best macro-level sMAPE of approximately 0.08 under configuration Ψ₁ (oscillatory dynamics for both species), faithfully reproducing both the phase lag and amplitude of Lotka-Volterra oscillations. At the micro level, the surrogate consistently achieves lower Earth Mover's Distance (EMD) than both ablations across all seven parameter configurations, with near-zero EMD for the deterministic-dominated Ψ₂ configuration.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against external baselines, as no existing method directly accepts graph-structured agent states and outputs per-agent state distributions. Instead, it evaluates against two ablated variants trained under identical protocols. The diffusion-only ablation (removing the GNN and using flat concatenation of all agent states) consistently underperforms the full GDN on both micro-level EMD and macro-level sMAPE across all configurations; it collapses to near-monotonic trends in predator-prey dynamics and largely maintains random configurations in Schelling. The GNN-only ablation (removing the diffusion component, predicting deterministic next states via MSE) performs competitively only in the most deterministic regimes (e.g., Schelling ξ₁, Predator-Prey Ψ₂) but diverges severely in stochastic settings, with EMD increasing proportionally to the level of stochasticity and sMAPE always exceeding the full surrogate. The authors note that standard time-series baselines such as AR(1) fail to capture non-monotonic or cyclic patterns and do not generalize beyond in-sample dynamics (Supplementary Section C.5).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Grattarola et al.'s (NeurIPS 2021) GNN-based emulation of deterministic cellular automata to the broader, stochastic domain of agent-based models by coupling message-passing GNNs with conditional diffusion models. Prior ABM surrogates (Lamperti et al., Dyer et al., Casert et al.) learn macro-level mappings from parameters to aggregate statistics, explicitly disregarding individual agent behavior; the GDN is positioned as the first architecture to jointly model per-agent stochastic transitions and their graph-structured interactions. The diffusion component draws on Ho et al.'s (NeurIPS 2020) denoising framework, repurposed here not for image or graph generation but for learning conditional next-state distributions of individual agents given a graph embedding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By producing a differentiable surrogate that preserves micro-level agent dynamics, the GDN opens the door to gradient-based calibration of both macro parameters and individual agent states—something previously requiring handcrafted probabilistic models. The framework can also ingest real-world observations alongside simulated data, making ABMs more empirically grounded. Key limitations include the assumption of a known interaction graph and the inability to handle all-to-all interactions or multi-round sequential stochastic events within a single timestep, both flagged as open problems. The approach is validated on two canonical but relatively small ABMs (~2000 agents); scalability to larger, more complex systems with heterogeneous interaction topologies remains to be demonstrated.
