## Problem

While single-concept personalization via LoRA adapters is well-established, combining multiple independently trained LoRAs for multi-concept generation or editing remains unsolved: naively merging adapters causes 'LoRA crosstalk' where identities distort or one concept dominates. Existing multi-concept methods require retraining, specialized LoRA architectures, external segmentation, or pose conditioning, and none addresses the distinct task of multi-concept image editing—inserting several personalized concepts into an existing image while preserving its global context.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt, a set of pre-trained LoRA adapters (which may have different ranks and fine-tuned parameter sets), and optionally an input image for editing. It produces a single output image where all specified concepts appear spatially disentangled, with no auxiliary inputs such as segmentation masks, keypoints, or pose conditions required. For editing, the input image is inverted via RF-Solver and the personalized concepts are blended into the inverted latent, preserving lighting, layout, and fine details outside the concept regions.

## Method

LoRAShop is a two-stage, training-free pipeline built on FLUX rectified-flow transformers. First, a short pseudo-denoising pass (until timestep γ≈0.94) extracts cross-attention maps from the last double-stream block, which are iteratively blurred, thresholded at a posterior quantile τ, and resolved via argmax into non-overlapping binary subject priors that partition the latent canvas. Second, during the full denoising trajectory, a Multi-Subject Residual Blending (MSRB) scheme overwrites residual feature tensors at each transformer block only at token positions claimed by a subject prior, blending each LoRA adapter's residual output with normalized weights derived from the priors. Blending is disabled until timestep t≈0.90 to let the backbone establish scene layout before subject-specific features are inserted. Because the method operates purely on residual outputs rather than modifying weights, all adapters remain fully independent and can be applied simultaneously without any retraining or architectural changes.

## Results

On multi-subject generation (the paper's primary task), LoRAShop achieves the best identity preservation score (ID: 0.532±0.12) among all FLUX-based and SDXL-based baselines, outperforming the next-best method OmniGen (0.453) by 17% relative. On single-subject generation, LoRAShop scores 0.740 ID (nearly matching DreamBooth's 0.755) while surpassing all encoder-based methods (IP-Adapter 0.309, UNO 0.486) and achieving the best HPS (0.321) and CLIP-T (0.439). In face swapping, LoRAShop more than doubles the identity score over ReFace (0.709 vs. 0.330) while remaining competitive on input preservation (DINO 0.970 vs. 0.982). A 50-participant user study confirms LoRAShop's superiority in both identity alignment (3.76/5) and prompt alignment (4.23/5) for multi-subject generation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LoRAShop decisively outperforms all SDXL-based multi-subject methods (OMG, MS-Diffusion, MIP-Adapter) on every metric, with ID scores of 0.532 vs. 0.206–0.305. Among FLUX-based competitors, LoRAShop leads on identity preservation over DreamBooth (0.532 vs. 0.444), OmniGen (0.532 vs. 0.453), and UNO (0.532 vs. 0.270), while matching or slightly trailing OmniGen on aesthetics (6.124 vs. 6.264). In single-subject generation, LoRAShop nearly matches DreamBooth on ID (0.740 vs. 0.755) but surpasses it on HPS (0.321 vs. 0.305) and CLIP-T (0.439 vs. 0.429), and dominates encoder-based methods like IP-Adapter (ID 0.740 vs. 0.309) and UNO (0.740 vs. 0.486). For face swapping, LoRAShop more than doubles ReFace's identity score (0.709 vs. 0.330) at a small cost in input preservation (LPIPS 0.050 vs. 0.031). Qualitative comparisons with Mix-of-Show, LoRACLR, Orthogonal Adaptation, and Prompt+ (Fig. 8) show LoRAShop producing cleaner compositions without requiring pose conditions or specialized LoRA variants, though no quantitative comparison is possible since those methods lack public implementations.

## Relation to Prior Work

LoRAShop builds on the RF-Solver inversion pipeline [41] for editing and the FLUX rectified-flow transformer architecture, but departs fundamentally from prior multi-concept personalization work by operating entirely in the residual feature space at inference time. Where Mix-of-Show, LoRACLR, and Orthogonal Adaptation require retraining or specialized LoRA variants, and where OMG, MS-Diffusion, and MIP-Adapter depend on external segmentation or layout guidance, LoRAShop derives spatially non-overlapping subject priors directly from cross-attention maps in the last double-stream block and gates LoRA adapter contributions via a simple residual blending operation. This positions the method as a training-free, segmentation-free alternative that repurposes the model's own internal attention structure as a spatial router, rather than imposing external constraints.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating retraining, pose conditions, and external segmenters, LoRAShop makes multi-concept LoRA composition practical for community adapters (e.g., from Civit.ai) and opens a 'photoshop-with-LoRAs' workflow for rapid creative iteration. Key limitations include architectural dependence on FLUX's double-stream attention structure (portability to SDXL or other backbones is unverified), inheritance of latent biases in the extracted masks, and ethical risks around non-consensual identity transfer. Open questions include whether the attention-based prior generalizes to heavily occluded or overlapping concepts, how performance scales beyond four subjects, and whether the technique transfers to video diffusion or other rectified-flow variants.
