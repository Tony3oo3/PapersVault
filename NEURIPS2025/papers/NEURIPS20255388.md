## Problem

Existing small language model (SLM) designs prioritize parameter reduction, but parameter efficiency does not translate into proportional real-device latency reductions on hardware accelerators. Prior work defaults to deep-thin architectures without systematically studying what actually governs decoding latency, and the rapid emergence of efficient attention operators (Mamba2, DeltaNet, GLA, etc.) has led to manual, heuristic-driven hybrid architecture decisions that are costly and non-scalable.

## Input / Output

The models accept natural language text prompts and produce autoregressive text completions, with 256 learnable meta tokens prepended to the input sequence to serve as learned cache initialization for linear attention layers during decoding. A key design choice is the use of a larger-vocabulary tokenizer (Mistral-NeMo-Minitron) rather than the small-vocabulary tokenizers typical of parameter-efficient SLMs, which reduces token count by 9–13% per sample at only ~6% additional embedding-layer latency, yielding net decoding speedup. Latency is measured at batch size 1 (edge deployment target) and throughput at maximum batch size with 32k-token inputs.

## Method

Nemotron-Flash combines three architectural insights with two training techniques. First, it identifies that the optimal depth-width ratio for a given latency budget is shallower than the parameter-optimal deep-thin ratio, and provides augmented scaling laws (decoupling model size into depth D and width W) to principledly select the sweet-spot configuration. Second, it uses an evolutionary search framework (aging evolution with tournament selection) to automatically discover hybrid operator combinations—specifically interleaving DeltaNet-FFN-Mamba2-FFN and Attention-FFN-Mamba2-FFN blocks—using short-training PPL (10B tokens) as a reliable proxy for final performance, validated by an 88.8% Spearman correlation with full-training results. Third, it applies weight normalization (projecting weight matrices onto a unit norm sphere along input or output dimensions depending on layer type) after each training step to eliminate radial components and emphasize angular updates, yielding +1.2% CR accuracy and −0.66 PPL on average with negligible overhead compared to the full nGPT approach. The final 1B/3B models are trained for 4.5T tokens on 256 H100 GPUs with a cosine LR schedule and no weight decay, using a two-stage data curriculum from Zyda2 to high-quality reasoning/math/code corpora.

## Results

Nemotron-Flash-1B (0.96B params, depth 12) achieves 49.63% average accuracy across 16 tasks with 14.45s decoding latency (8k tokens, batch size 1) and 7,289 tok/s throughput, outperforming Qwen3-0.6B by +5.5% accuracy with 1.9× lower latency and 45.6× higher throughput. Nemotron-Flash-3B (2.7B params, depth 18) reaches 60.98% average accuracy with 28.71s latency and 2,939 tok/s throughput, surpassing Qwen2.5-3B by +2.0% accuracy with 1.7× lower latency and 6.4× higher throughput, and Qwen3-1.7B by +5.5% accuracy with 1.3× lower latency and 18.7× higher throughput. The throughput-optimized variant Nemotron-Flash-3B-TP (1FA+2SWA) further boosts throughput to 4,657 tok/s (10.1× over Qwen2.5-3B) while maintaining 60.84% average accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Nemotron-Flash-1B dominates the sub-1B class: it beats Qwen3-0.6B (44.11% avg) by +5.5% accuracy while running 1.9× faster (14.45s vs 27.55s) and achieving 45.6× higher throughput (7,289 vs 160 tok/s), and outperforms Llama-3.2-1B (41.45%) by +8.2% with 1.5× lower latency. In the 1.5–3B range, Nemotron-Flash-3B (60.98% avg) surpasses Qwen3-1.7B (55.47%) by +5.5% accuracy with 1.3× lower latency and 18.7× higher throughput, and Qwen2.5-3B (58.96%) by +2.0% with 1.7× lower latency and 6.4× higher throughput. Against Llama-3.2-3B (50.89%), Nemotron-Flash-3B gains +10.1% accuracy with 1.6× lower latency and 17× higher throughput. The throughput-optimized Nemotron-Flash-3B-TP variant (1FA+2SWA) achieves 4,657 tok/s—10.1× over Qwen2.5-3B and 29.7× over Qwen3-1.7B—while retaining 60.84% average accuracy, a mere 0.14% below the 3FA variant. Notably, despite containing only 2–3 full-attention layers, Nemotron-Flash models achieve the highest recall accuracy among models >1.5B params, and the ablation (Table 8) shows that replacing FA with SWA preserves general benchmark accuracy but degrades NIAH performance at long contexts, motivating the recommendation of at least 2 FA layers.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the SLM design lineage (MobileLLM, SmolLM, MiniCPM) by shifting the optimization target from parameter count to real-device latency, and extends the scaling-law framework of Hoffmann et al. and MiniCPM by decoupling model size into depth and width to identify latency-optimal ratios. For architecture, it builds on hybrid LM work (Jamba, Zamba, Samba, Hybba) but replaces manual operator selection with an evolutionary search framework adapted from Real et al.'s regularized evolution. On the training side, it simplifies nGPT's full sphere-constrained training to a lightweight weight-normalization step and extends Hybba's meta-token concept to linear attention mechanisms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that deep-thin SLMs—now the default in the field—are not on the accuracy-latency Pareto frontier challenges a widely adopted design heuristic and provides a principled scaling-law-based method for selecting depth-width ratios under deployment constraints. The evolutionary search framework and weight normalization are generalizable components that reduce the trial-and-error cost of hybrid SLM development. Limitations include reliance on LM PPL as a search proxy (which may not capture long-context or in-context learning capabilities), macro-architecture-only search, and the absence of error bars across training runs. Open questions include whether the depth-width insights transfer across hardware platforms and whether the search can be extended to fine-grained design factors.
