## Problem

Compositional MLLMs, which connect separately pre-trained visual encoders and LLMs, have dominated the field but obscure the joint multimodal scaling properties due to their decoupled training. Native (end-to-end) MLLMs offer a unified training objective but prior work assumed unlimited data and compute, leaving open whether they can practically match top-tier compositional models under realistic data constraints. This paper systematically investigates the design space and scaling laws of native MLLMs under a practical ~600M image-text pair budget.

## Input / Output

NaViL accepts RGB images at any resolution (padded so dimensions are multiples of 32) alongside a text prompt, and produces free-form text output via next-token prediction. At inference, a visual multi-scale packing strategy generates a sequence of progressively downsampled images (rate √2/2) that are encoded independently and concatenated, with special tokens (<end_of_scale>, <end_of_line>, <begin_of_image>, <end_of_image>) marking scale boundaries and spatial positions. The visual encoder uses bidirectional attention with 2D-RoPE while the LLM uses causal attention with 1D-RoPE, and a pixel-shuffle connector plus MLP projects visual tokens into the LLM's embedding space.

## Method

NaViL combines three empirically validated design choices: (1) initializing the LLM from a pre-trained base (InternLM2-1.8B or Qwen3-8B) rather than training from scratch, which accelerates convergence by >10×; (2) injecting modality-specific Mixture-of-Experts (separate Q/K/V/O and FFN projections for visual vs. linguistic tokens) that improves convergence without increasing activated parameter count; and (3) a bidirectional visual encoder whose optimal size scales log-linearly with LLM size (e.g., 0.6B encoder for 1.8B LLM, 1.2B encoder for 8B LLM). Training proceeds in three stages: 500M web-scale image-text pairs with frozen text parameters, 185M high-quality multimodal data with unfrozen text attention, and 68M supervised fine-tuning with all parameters unfrozen. The key scaling insight is that visual encoder gains saturate once the LLM's capacity is the bottleneck, yielding a positively correlated encoder-LLM scaling relationship that contrasts with compositional MLLMs' fixed-encoder approach.

## Results

NaViL-2B (2.4B activated params) achieves an average score of 67.1 across 7 general MLLM benchmarks, surpassing all existing native MLLMs (previous best: SAIL at 53.7 with 7B params) and matching the compositional InternVL-2.5-2B (67.0) with fewer activated parameters. On visual question answering benchmarks, NaViL-2B reaches an average of 75.1, leading all native MLLMs (previous best: EVEv2 at 71.7) and approaching the compositional InternVL-2.5-2B (76.5). Notably, NaViL-2B achieves 78.3 on MMVet (vs. 60.8 for InternVL-2.5-2B) and 83.9 on CCBench (vs. 81.7), while using only ~600M pre-training image-text pairs and 800B total training tokens—substantially fewer than compositional counterparts requiring >3.5T tokens.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NaViL-2B decisively outperforms all native MLLM baselines: it surpasses SAIL (7B) by 13.4 points in average MLLM score (67.1 vs. 53.7) and EVEv2 (7B) by 13.9 points, while using only 2.4B activated parameters versus their 7B. Against the compositional InternVL-2.5-2B (same LLM backbone, same high-quality data), NaViL-2B achieves a comparable average (67.1 vs. 67.0) but leads substantially on MMVet (78.3 vs. 60.8) and CCBench (83.9 vs. 81.7), while trailing on MMMU (41.8 vs. 43.6), MME (1822 vs. 2138), and MathVista (50.0 vs. 51.3). On VQA benchmarks, NaViL-2B (avg 75.1) significantly outperforms the previous native SOTA SAIL (71.5) and EVEv2 (71.7), with particularly large gains on DocVQA (85.4 vs. 78.4), ChartQA (78.0 vs. 73.9), and InfographicVQA (56.0 vs. 47.3). Compared to larger compositional models like Qwen2VL-2B (58.6 avg) and MiniCPM-V-2 (51.1 avg), NaViL-2B leads by 8.5 and 16.0 points respectively. The key efficiency advantage is that NaViL-2B requires only 800B total training tokens versus >3.5T for InternVL-2.5-8B and >4.1T for Qwen2.5-VL, demonstrating that native end-to-end training with the proposed scaling strategy achieves competitive performance at a fraction of the data cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the native MLLM line initiated by Chameleon, EVE, and Emu3, which train vision and language end-to-end with a unified next-token-prediction objective, but departs from them by initializing the LLM from a pre-trained checkpoint (InternLM2 or Qwen3) rather than training from scratch. It builds directly on Mono-InternVL's modality-specific MoE design, extending it with modality-specific attention experts to address feature-scale mismatches between visual and linguistic tokens. Conceptually, it positions itself against the dominant compositional paradigm (LLaVA, InternVL, Qwen2-VL) by showing that the assumption of a fixed-size visual encoder across all LLM scales is suboptimal, and by providing the first systematic study of joint vision-language scaling under practical data constraints.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The log-linear scaling relationship between optimal visual encoder size and LLM size provides concrete architectural guidance that directly challenges the compositional paradigm's practice of pairing one fixed encoder with multiple LLM scales. The demonstrated data efficiency (~800B tokens vs. >3.5T for compositional counterparts like InternVL-2.5-8B) suggests native training could become the default paradigm if validated at larger scales. Key open questions remain: whether the observed scaling laws hold at 30B–100B parameter scales, whether the findings generalize beyond visual-lingual modalities, and whether the MoE-based architecture's advantages persist when the base LLM is already very large. The paper's limitation to 9B parameters and the absence of error bars temper the strength of the scaling-law claims.
