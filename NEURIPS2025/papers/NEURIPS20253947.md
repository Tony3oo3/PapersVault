## Problem

Dense Associative Memory (DenseAM) models using the standard log-sum-exp (LSE) energy can achieve exponential memory capacity, but they face a fundamental trade-off: generating novel emergent memories (new local minima) typically requires sacrificing exact recall of the original stored patterns. This paper asks whether simultaneous perfect memorization and generalization (emergence of new meaningful patterns) can coexist in a single energy function, a property previously absent from all known AM formulations.

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of M stored d-dimensional patterns (memories) and a query state vector x, producing retrieved memories (local minima at or near stored patterns) and emergent memories (novel local minima at centroids of overlapping basins). Unlike LSE, the LSR energy has finite support: energy is infinite outside the union of balls of radius sqrt(2/β) around each stored pattern, so retrieval is only defined within these regions. In image experiments, stored patterns are VAE-encoded latents (10-dim for MNIST, 256-dim for Tiny-ImageNet), and outputs are decoded back to pixel-space images.

## Method

The paper introduces the log-sum-ReLU (LSR) energy, which replaces the exponential separation function in LSE with a shifted ReLU, F(x) = ReLU(1+x), motivated by the connection between energy functions and kernel density estimation: LSE corresponds to Gaussian KDE while LSR corresponds to the Epanechnikov kernel, which is provably optimal for KDE. The finite support of ReLU is the key mechanism enabling emergence: when basins of attraction around stored patterns overlap, the gradient of LSR vanishes at the centroid of the active subset, creating a novel local minimum that coexists with all original memories as local minima. Theorems 1 and 2 prove exact single-step retrieval of exponentially many patterns (M ~ exp(d)) and the existence of a large number of emergent memories simultaneously, a property shown to be impossible for LSE (Proposition 1). The gradient simplifies to a uniform average over active patterns, making retrieval a single gradient step with an appropriately chosen learning rate.

## Results

At critical values of β, LSR energy creates orders of magnitude more emergent (novel) local minima than the number of stored patterns M, while still preserving >60% of stored patterns as retrievable memories (Fig. 3 left). On a k=10 mixture of 8-dim Gaussians, LSR memories achieve log-likelihood comparable to and occasionally slightly higher than LSE, while producing significantly more unique samples from the same N=500 initial queries (Fig. 3 right). In latent-space image experiments, LSR generates an order of magnitude more total memories than LSE for both MNIST (10-dim VAE latents) and Tiny-ImageNet (256-dim latents), with the Tiny-ImageNet case achieving full global emergence (all 40 stored patterns recoverable simultaneously with novel memories). Theoretically, LSR achieves exponential memory capacity M = Θ(√(1−δ)·exp(αd)) with exact single-step retrieval, a property proven impossible for LSE (Proposition 1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is the LSE (log-sum-exp) energy from Ramsauer et al. (ICLR 2021), which uses a Gaussian kernel and is the standard DenseAM energy. LSR decisively wins on the number of emergent memories: at critical β, LSR produces orders of magnitude more novel local minima than stored patterns, while LSE can never produce more local minima than the number of stored patterns (Proposition 1 proves LSE cannot satisfy ε-global emergence for any β). On log-likelihood under a true Gaussian mixture density, LSR matches or slightly exceeds LSE, despite LSE's Gaussian kernel being the natural choice for that distribution. LSR also wins on sample diversity: with the same 500 initial queries, LSR converges to far more unique memories than LSE (which collapses to ~10 memories due to homogeneous high-likelihood samples). The key qualitative distinction is that LSR simultaneously achieves exact memorization of all original patterns AND generation of novel emergent memories (global emergence), whereas LSE must sacrifice one for the other — a trade-off the paper proves is fundamental to the LSE formulation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Dense Associative Memory framework of Krotov & Hopfield (2016) and the LSE energy of Ramsauer et al. (2021) by replacing the exponential separation function with a ReLU-based one, motivated by the optimality of the Epanechnikov kernel in nonparametric kernel density estimation (Epanechnikov, 1969). The key conceptual bridge is recognizing that the LSE energy corresponds to Gaussian KDE, and that the Epanechnikov kernel—which is provably optimal for minimizing MISE—translates directly into a shifted-ReLU separation function. This departs from prior AM work, which has exclusively used exponential or power-law separation functions, by importing a result from statistical estimation theory to motivate a fundamentally different energy landscape with finite support.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper resolves a long-standing tension in associative memory between perfect memorization and generalization: LSR provably achieves exact retrieval of exponentially many patterns while simultaneously generating novel local minima (emergent memories), a property that Proposition 1 shows LSE cannot satisfy. However, the practical impact is tempered by clear limitations—the gradient vanishes outside the finite support of stored patterns, making retrieval fail for distant queries, and the 'creativity' of emergent memories is mechanistically limited to centroids of pattern subsets whose semantic novelty depends entirely on the structure of the embedding space. Open questions include whether hybrid LSE+LSR energies or query-dependent temperatures can address the finite-support limitation, and whether the emergence phenomenon scales meaningfully to high-dimensional, high-capacity regimes beyond the small-scale experiments shown.
