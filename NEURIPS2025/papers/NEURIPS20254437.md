## Problem

Deep RL training is bottlenecked by CPU-bound environment simulation: Minigrid, a foundational suite for exploration, curriculum learning, and representation learning research, cannot scale beyond a handful of parallel environments due to Python multiprocessing overhead and lack of GPU acceleration. No prior work provided a full, batched JAX reimplementation of the Minigrid suite with identical semantics for observations, actions, rewards, and terminations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254437/cropped_page0_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The environment consumes a stateless timestep tuple (t, o_t, a_t, r_{t+1}, γ_{t+1}, s_t, i_{t+1}) and an action vector, plus an explicit PRNG key for all stochastic operations, and returns a new timestep with the updated observation, reward, termination flag, and info dictionary. Observations are available in multiple formats—symbolic grid encoding (i32[H,W,3]), RGB images (u8[32*H,32*W,3]), or categorical entity IDs—each in either top-down or first-person view. The explicit timestep passing and autoreset mechanism eliminate conditional branching in agent code, which is a prerequisite for full JIT compilation of the entire training loop.

## Method

NAVIX reimplements the full Minigrid suite in JAX using an Entity-Component-System (ECS) design pattern borrowed from game development: entities (Player, Wall, Door, Key, Lava, etc.) are composed of typed components (Position, Direction, Colour, Pocket, etc.), and systems (Intervention, Transition, Observation, Reward, Termination) are pure functions operating on the collective entity state. The critical design constraint is statelessness: all environment state is carried in an explicit timestep object passed to every step() call, and all randomness is driven by explicit PRNG keys, enabling the entire interaction loop to be compiled by XLA via jax.jit and vectorized across thousands of parallel environments via jax.vmap. This replaces Minigrid's Python multiprocessing with native GPU vectorization, and the ECS structure maps bijectively to the MDP formalism (intervention → decision, transition → state kernel, observation → observation kernel, reward/termination → reward/termination functions). The paper demonstrates that this yields >44× per-step speedup in batch mode and >160,000× throughput when training 2048 PPO agents (32,768 environments) on a single A100 80 GB, reducing 1-week experiments to 15 minutes.

## Results

NAVIX achieves an average speedup of over 44× compared to the original Minigrid implementation across 30 environments (individual speedups range from 19.72× to 128.98×). In batch mode with 2048 PPO agents × 16 environments each, NAVIX reaches a throughput of 6.7 × 10⁸ environment steps/s on a single Nvidia A100 80 GB, versus 4.2 × 10³ steps/s for the original Minigrid with a single agent — a combined speedup of approximately 160,000×. NAVIX scales to over 2 million (2²¹) parallel environments on the same hardware, while Minigrid cannot exceed 16 environments on 128 GB of RAM. The paper also provides PPO, DDQN, SAC, IQN, and PQN baselines across all 30 environments, with 1M training steps and 32 seeds per configuration.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is the original CPU-based Minigrid implementation (Chevalier-Boisvert et al., 2024). NAVIX decisively outperforms Minigrid on every measured axis: raw per-step speed (44× average, up to 129× for Empty-8x8), parallel scalability (2M+ environments vs. a hard ceiling of 16 on 128 GB RAM), and end-to-end training throughput (~160,000× when batching 2048 PPO agents). The speedup is consistent across all 30 implemented environments and all tested training lengths (1K to 1M steps), with both implementations showing linear wall-time growth. On consumer-grade hardware (RTX A4000, 16 GB VRAM), NAVIX still achieves ~34,000× throughput speedup and supports over 260K parallel environments. The paper notes that the speedup is driven primarily by efficient JAX batching (vmap) rather than GPU acceleration alone, as CPU-compiled NAVIX already outperforms Minigrid at small batch sizes.

## Relation to Prior Work

NAVIX is a full JAX reimplementation of the Minigrid environment suite (Chevalier-Boisvert et al., 2024), joining a growing family of JAX-based environment libraries (Gymnax, Brax, Pgx, Jumanji, Craftax) that migrate RL environments from CPU to GPU/TPU via XLA compilation and vmap-based batching. Unlike prior partial efforts—Weng et al. (2022) reimplementing only the Empty environment, or Jiang et al. (2023) with AMaze's single maze task—NAVIX covers the complete Minigrid suite with identical observation, reward, and termination semantics. Architecturally, it adopts the Entity-Component-System (ECS) pattern from game development to compose entities (Player, Door, Key, Lava) from reusable components (Position, Direction, Pocket), enabling modular extension while maintaining a bijective mapping between code and the formal MDP/POMDP definitions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By enabling 2048+ parallel PPO agents on a single A100 and reducing 1-week Minigrid experiments to ~15 minutes, NAVIX substantially lowers the compute barrier for RL research, particularly for resource-constrained institutions (consumer-hardware results in Appendix C confirm ~34,000× speedup on an RTX A4000). The paper also surfaces a non-trivial design issue: the original Minigrid reward function is non-Markovian (depends on step count t), which violates assumptions in most standard RL algorithms—a finding that may affect interpretation of prior Minigrid results. Open questions include extending NAVIX to the remaining Minigrid variants that resist JIT compilation, and whether the ECS pattern generalizes cleanly to more complex, non-grid-world environments.
