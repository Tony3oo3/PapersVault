## Problem

Existing spatial video grounding (SVG) benchmarks and methods are confined to ego-centric, fixed front-perspective ground-level scenes with large, easily distinguishable objects. UAV-based aerial video grounding remains unexplored, facing unique challenges including extremely small objects (1–200 pixels), wide field-of-view with dense clutter, rapid camera motion causing viewpoint changes, and environmental disturbances like occlusion and illumination variation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252766/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a T-frame aerial video (resized to 224×224 for training, with up to 100 frames uniformly sampled at 5 fps) and a natural language sentence describing one target object, and outputs a spatial tube — a sequence of per-frame bounding boxes (x, y, w, h) tracking the referred object across all frames. The benchmark UAV-SVG contains 2M+ frames across 3,564 videos with 216 object categories and 73 motion classes, substantially more diverse than prior ground-level datasets.

## Method

SAVG-DETR decouples the multi-modality multi-scale spatio-temporal encoder into two branches: an intra-scale multi-modality interaction branch (self-attention between language tokens and high-level visual features with learnable frame/video tokens) and a cross-scale visual-only fusion branch (CNN-based multi-scale fusion using MSFF-FE, frequency-focused downsampling, and semantic alignment modules), avoiding the quadratic cost of full multi-scale multi-modal attention. The hierarchical progressive decoder cascades 1 high-level, 2 mid-level, and 3 low-level decoder layers, with a multi-level language modulation module injecting scale-specific visual context into language features to guide queries. A key design is the offset-generation decoding paradigm: reference anchor boxes serve as positional embeddings to constrict the search region, context-rich object queries (derived from video tokens) predict 4-D offsets, and anchors are iteratively refined from coarse to fine, improving cross-frame prediction consistency for small aerial objects.

## Results

On the UAV-SVG test set, SAVG-DETR achieves 27.15% m_vIoU and 28.82% m_fAcc, outperforming all baselines across all six evaluation metrics. The closest competitor, STCAT, reaches 24.14% m_vIoU and 27.17% m_fAcc, yielding absolute gains of 3.01% and 1.65% respectively. The paper also introduces a novel frame-level accuracy metric (fAcc) to assess per-frame localization stability, where SAVG-DETR scores 35.85% fAcc@0.3 and 26.55% fAcc@0.5.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAVG-DETR consistently outperforms all seven baselines on every metric. Against the strongest spatio-temporal video grounding baselines, it surpasses STCAT (NeurIPS'22) by 3.01% m_vIoU and 1.65% m_fAcc, and VideoGrounding-DINO (CVPR'24) by 3.32% m_vIoU and 3.02% m_fAcc. The video REC methods (Co-grounding, DCNet) perform poorly (m_vIoU ≈ 10–12%) because they lack global spatio-temporal context modeling. Among the one-stage grounding methods, SGFDN (ACM MM'23) performs worst (20.13% m_vIoU) because its 3D-to-2D+1D decomposition loses critical object semantics in aerial scenes, while CG-STVG (CVPR'24) struggles with small and similar objects where its instance-context mining introduces harmful information. TubeDETR (CVPR'22) is limited by sparse frame sampling that loses fine-grained spatio-temporal detail in rapidly moving aerial footage.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DETR-based spatio-temporal video grounding paradigm (TubeDETR, STCAT) to the aerial/UAV domain, where prior work has been confined to ego-centric, ground-level, fixed-perspective scenes. Architecturally, it builds on the encoder-decoder structure of DETR and TubeDETR but departs by decoupling multi-modality and multi-scale spatio-temporal modeling into separate intra-scale and cross-scale branches, borrowing cross-scale fusion modules (MSFF-FE, FD, SAC) from the UAV-DETR detection literature. The offset-generation decoding paradigm with video-token-derived queries represents a conceptual departure from both learnable-query and language-feature-query initialization strategies used in prior grounding methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The UAV-SVG benchmark (2M+ frames, 216 object classes, 73 motion classes) fills a clear gap: no prior spatial video grounding dataset addresses the unique challenges of aerial scenes (small objects, camera motion, viewpoint changes, illumination variation). The decoupling strategy offers a practical recipe for handling multi-scale multi-modality attention without quadratic cost explosion, which is broadly applicable to other video understanding tasks. Limitations include evaluation only on the authors' own dataset, a 224-pixel input resolution constraint due to GPU memory, and reliance on Gemini-generated referring expressions that may introduce systematic biases. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to other aerial benchmarks, whether it can operate in real time on embedded UAV hardware, and how it performs in open-vocabulary settings.
