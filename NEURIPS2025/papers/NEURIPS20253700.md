## Problem

Existing open-vocabulary and visual grounding methods for remote sensing rely on explicit category cues, limiting their ability to handle complex or implicit queries such as 'detect all visible objects' or 'count all sports fields' that require reasoning beyond a fixed category list. Moreover, remote sensing datasets carry dataset-specific annotation rules (e.g., excluding low-resolution vehicles, counting separated service areas as distinct instances) that simple category prompts cannot capture, and conventional detectors depend on confidence thresholds that are unavailable in zero-shot scenarios.

## Input / Output

The method takes a remote sensing RGB image (spatial resolution 0.08–30 m) and a structured JSON instruction prompt encoding the task setting (open-vocabulary, open-ended, or open-subclass), dataset-specific annotation rules, and category definitions. It produces per-category object counts, labeled bounding boxes, and labeled segmentation masks. The instruction prompt is designed to be image-agnostic (no per-image content priors) to maintain scalability for large-area mapping, while the output categories may use free-form names that are matched to ground-truth labels via CLIP embedding cosine similarity (>0.95) during evaluation.

## Method

InstructSAM is a training-free framework that decomposes instruction-oriented object recognition into three parallel-then-sequential steps: (1) an LVLM (GPT-4o or Qwen2.5-VL) interprets the structured instruction and outputs target categories with their counts; (2) SAM2 in automated mask generation mode (prompted by a regular point grid, plus image crops for small objects) produces a dense set of class-agnostic mask proposals; (3) GeoRSCLIP computes a semantic similarity matrix between scaled mask crops and category text embeddings. The key innovation is formulating mask-label assignment as a Binary Integer Programming problem that maximizes total semantic similarity subject to per-category counting constraints derived from the LVLM, solved efficiently with PuLP. This counting-constrained matching eliminates the need for confidence thresholds, achieves near-constant inference time regardless of object count, and reduces output tokens by 89% compared to direct generative approaches like Qwen2.5-VL.

## Results

On the open-vocabulary setting (Table 1), InstructSAM-GPT4o achieves the best counting F1 of 83.0 (NWPU-VHR-10) and 79.9 (DIOR), Box F1 of 41.8 and 29.1, and Mask F1 of 26.1 and 28.1, respectively, outperforming all baselines across all three tasks. In the open-ended setting (Table 2), InstructSAM-GPT4o reaches Cnt F1 of 57.4 (NWPU) and 47.9 (DIOR), surpassing the strongest remote-sensing-specific baseline LAE-Label (46.2 / 23.3) by 11.2 and 24.6 points. Inference time remains nearly constant regardless of object count, reducing output tokens by 89% and total runtime by 32% compared to direct generation with Qwen2.5-VL.

## Baselines & Comparisons

InstructSAM-GPT4o dominates all baselines on the open-vocabulary task: it outperforms Qwen2.5-VL by 15.0 Cnt F1 points (83.0 vs 68.0) on NWPU and 27.9 on DIOR (79.9 vs 52.0), while also providing segmentation (Mask F1 26.1/28.1) that Qwen2.5-VL cannot produce. Against generic open-vocabulary detectors, the gains are dramatic—OWLv2 scores only 39.4 Cnt F1 on NWPU and Grounding DINO just 14.9, reflecting their inability to handle remote-sensing-specific categories. For segmentation, InstructSAM-GPT4o (Mask F1 26.1 on NWPU) surpasses the best remote-sensing segmentation baselines GSNet (1.3) and SegEarth-OV (3.5) by over 20 points. In the open-ended setting, InstructSAM-GPT4o beats LAE-Label by 11.2 Cnt F1 on NWPU and 24.6 on DIOR, and outperforms GeoPixel by 16.6 and 26.5 Cnt F1 respectively, while also achieving higher Mask F1 (29.9 vs 29.1 on NWPU; 21.8 vs 14.7 on DIOR). The only notable trade-off is that Qwen2.5-VL slightly edges InstructSAM-Qwen on Box F1 in the open-ended setting (32.0 vs 29.6 on NWPU), but InstructSAM-GPT4o recovers the lead (31.3 vs 32.0 is close, yet InstructSAM adds segmentation). In the open-subclass setting, InstructSAM-GPT4o achieves 84.2 Cnt F1 for 'sports fields' on NWPU, far exceeding Qwen2.5-VL (54.1) and GPT-4o+OWLv2 (40.3), confirming that the counting-constrained matching is particularly effective for super-category queries.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends instruction-oriented object detection (IOD) from Ins-DetCLIP [60] by adding counting as a first-class output and by formulating the problem for remote sensing imagery with dataset-specific annotation rules. Methodologically, it departs from both trained open-vocabulary detectors (Grounding DINO, OWLv2, CASTDet) and generative LVLM-based detectors (Qwen2.5-VL, Florence-2) by adopting a fully training-free pipeline that composes an LVLM counter, SAM2 mask proposer, and a CLIP-based semantic matcher, then resolves mask-label assignment via binary integer programming rather than confidence thresholds. The BIP formulation is the key conceptual departure: it replaces the fragile, category-dependent threshold mechanism used by conventional detectors with global counting constraints, drawing on combinatorial optimization rather than learned scoring.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

EarthInstruct is the first benchmark for instruction-oriented object recognition in remote sensing, and the training-free, confidence-free paradigm eliminates the need for task-specific pre-training and manual threshold tuning that plague existing open-vocabulary remote sensing methods. The near-constant inference time (89% fewer output tokens, 32% less runtime vs. direct LVLM generation) makes the approach practical for large-area mapping where object counts vary widely. Key limitations include inherited biases from optical-imagery-trained foundation models (poor SAR performance), scene-level CLIP misalignment with object-level crops, and modest absolute accuracy (Box F1 ~30–40), leaving open whether the BIP formulation scales to truly open-world, multi-sensor settings or can serve as a reliable automated labeling engine at scale.
