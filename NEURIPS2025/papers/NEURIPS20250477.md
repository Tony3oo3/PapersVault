## Problem

Supervised contrastive learning objectives like SupCon function as heuristic energy functions with opaque statistical meaning: without explicit regulation of the embedding distribution, semantically related instances can be inadvertently pushed apart, and the reliance on exhaustive pairwise comparisons over large in-batch negatives creates quadratic computational cost and hinders generalization. The paper seeks a principled probabilistic foundation that endows contrastive objectives with explicit likelihood semantics while preserving their geometric inductive biases.

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR, 224×224 for ImageNet) and produces ℓ₂-normalized embeddings of dimension 2048 (ResNet) or 768 (ViT-Base) along with a per-sample class probability distribution. Rather than comparing each embedding against all other in-batch embeddings, the model computes class-level centroid vectors dynamically from the mini-batch and scores each embedding against only C class directions, reducing the comparison complexity from O(B²) to O(B·C). A per-sample confidence-adaptive temperature τ₂(z) modulates the sharpness of the target distribution, making it more peaked for hard samples and more uniform for well-classified ones.

## Method

VarCon reformulates supervised contrastive learning as variational inference over latent class variables by deriving an evidence lower bound (ELBO) for the class-conditional embedding likelihood via Bayes' rule and Jensen's inequality. The resulting loss minimizes two synergistic terms: a KL divergence aligning a confidence-adaptive auxiliary posterior q_φ(r|z) with the model's class posterior p_θ(r|z), and a negative log-likelihood term for the ground-truth class. The class posterior is computed efficiently using dynamically computed class centroids (unit-norm mean of in-batch embeddings per class) with a fixed temperature τ₁, replacing SupCon's exhaustive pairwise comparisons. A key innovation is the confidence-adaptive temperature τ₂ = (τ₁−ε) + 2ε·p_θ(r|z), which tightens the pull constraint on challenging samples (low confidence → sharper target) and relaxes it for confident ones (high confidence → more uniform target), providing fine-grained control over intra-class dispersion. The learnable parameter ε controls the range of adaptivity, with optimal performance at ε=0.02 and τ₁=0.1.

## Results

VarCon achieves state-of-the-art supervised contrastive learning performance across all four benchmarks with a ResNet-50 encoder: 95.94% Top-1 on CIFAR-10, 78.29% on CIFAR-100, 86.34% on ImageNet-100, and 79.36% on ImageNet. These results surpass the previous best supervised method (SupCon) by 0.43–1.72% Top-1 and outperform the strongest self-supervised method (Barlow Twins) by 4.9–5.5% on ImageNet-100 and ImageNet. VarCon converges in 200 epochs (vs. 350 for SupCon) and achieves 79.26% on ImageNet with a batch size of only 2048, already exceeding SupCon's 78.72% at batch size 4096.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VarCon outperforms all seven baselines on every dataset. Against the primary supervised baseline SupCon [37], VarCon gains 0.43% on CIFAR-10 (95.94 vs. 95.51), 1.72% on CIFAR-100 (78.29 vs. 76.57), 1.28% on ImageNet-100 (86.34 vs. 85.06), and 0.64% on ImageNet (79.36 vs. 78.72) in Top-1 accuracy. Among self-supervised methods, the gap is substantially larger: VarCon exceeds the best self-supervised baseline (Barlow Twins [66] at 80.83% on ImageNet-100, SwAV [8] at 75.29% on ImageNet) by 5.51% and 4.07% respectively. VarCon also surpasses SimCLR [10] by 4.63% on CIFAR-10 and 9.05% on ImageNet, MoCo V2 [31] by 3.01% on CIFAR-10 and 8.30% on ImageNet, BYOL [29] by 3.37% on CIFAR-10 and 5.08% on ImageNet, and VicReg [5] by 3.85% on CIFAR-10 and 6.11% on ImageNet. In few-shot settings (Table 2), VarCon consistently beats SupCon across all sample sizes (e.g., 37.81% vs. 36.57% at N=100 per class), and in transfer learning (Table 4) it achieves a mean of 84.94% across 12 datasets, marginally exceeding SupCon (84.88%) while approaching self-supervised SimCLR (85.45%).

## Relation to Prior Work

VarCon reformulates supervised contrastive learning (SupCon) through variational inference over latent class variables, deriving an ELBO that replaces SupCon's exhaustive pairwise comparisons with efficient class-level centroid interactions. The theoretical lineage draws on VAE-style variational inference (Kingma & Welling) and the recent recognition that InfoNCE itself can be interpreted as variational inference in a recognition-parameterised model (Aitchison & Ganev, TMLR 2024), but VarCon extends this to the supervised setting with a confidence-adaptive temperature mechanism that dynamically adjusts target sharpness per sample. The key departure from SupCon is the shift from hard pairwise positive/negative assignments to a soft, posterior-weighted distributional objective with explicit KL-divergence regularization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled probabilistic grounding for contrastive objectives, bridging the generative and distinguishing paradigms that have developed largely in parallel. Practically, the reduced batch-size dependency (2048 vs. 4096) and faster convergence (200 vs. 350 epochs) offer meaningful efficiency gains for large-scale training. However, the method remains supervised, centroid computation introduces overhead for very large class counts, and the empirical improvements over SupCon are modest (0.4–1.7% across datasets), leaving open whether the variational framing yields qualitatively different representations or primarily incremental accuracy gains. Extension to self-supervised settings with latent pseudo-labels is identified as a natural next step.
