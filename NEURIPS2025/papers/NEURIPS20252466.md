## Problem

Existing preference optimization methods for LLM alignment, including token-level variants like TDPO, rely on risk-neutral criteria that maximize expected reward while ignoring the distributional characteristics of rewards beyond the mean. This means they cannot guarantee low risk of deviation from the reference model during training, which is problematic for applications requiring tight risk control. The paper asks how to incorporate risk-awareness at the token level during preference-based fine-tuning to better balance alignment performance against model drift.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard preference datasets consisting of text prompts paired with a preferred (win) and dispreferred (lose) response, processed token-by-token in an auto-regressive fashion. It produces a fine-tuned language model policy initialized from a reference model, with the key distinction that the training objective operates sequentially over tokens rather than treating the full response as a single unit. The risk control parameter μ (e.g., CVaR levels 0.95–0.99 or ERM coefficients 5–9) and the coefficient α (0.3–0.9) govern the trade-off between alignment accuracy and deviation suppression.

## Method

Ra-DPO reformulates the constrained reward maximization problem into a token-level objective by augmenting the Pb-MDP state to include the prompt plus all previously generated tokens, enabling a classical Bellman recursion under nested risk measures (CVaR or ERM). It defines a risk-aware advantage function as the difference between the risk-aware state-action value and the risk-measured state value, then derives a closed-form optimal policy that maps the risk-aware Q-function to the policy via an exponential tilting of the reference model. By establishing equivalence between the Bradley-Terry model and a Regret Preference Model, the paper obtains a loss function equal to the standard DPO log-sigmoid term plus a negative sequential risk ratio (D_SeqRR), which is the sum over tokens of the risk-measured log-ratio between the reference and policy distributions. Two variants are provided: Ra-DPO₁ backpropagates through both the preferred and dispreferred risk ratios, while Ra-DPO₂ applies a stop-gradient to the preferred response's risk ratio, treating it as a fixed baseline to improve training stability.

## Results

On AlpacaEval (evaluated against oasst_pythia_12b), Ra-DPO₁ achieves the highest winrate of 53.5±1.8, while Ra-DPO₂ achieves the highest length-controlled winrate of 55.7±0.5, both outperforming all baselines on at least one metric. On the IMDb and Anthropic HH datasets, Ra-DPO matches or exceeds the reward accuracy of TDPO while maintaining consistently lower sequential KL divergence for both preferred and dispreferred responses, demonstrating its ability to suppress model drift. The method's advantage is most pronounced on the length-controlled winrate, where Ra-DPO₂ leads the next-best method (TDPO₁) by 2.7 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Ra-DPO outperforms all four baselines on AlpacaEval. Against DPO, Ra-DPO₁ gains +2.4 winrate and +9.2 Lc winrate, while Ra-DPO₂ gains +1.0 winrate and +11.0 Lc winrate, indicating DPO's tendency to generate overly long responses. Against PPO, both Ra-DPO variants achieve higher Lc winrate (53.9 and 55.7 vs. 51.9), with Ra-DPO₁ also leading on raw winrate (53.5 vs. 52.1). Against KTO, Ra-DPO₁ leads by +2.0 winrate and +3.7 Lc winrate, and Ra-DPO₂ leads by +0.6 and +5.5 respectively. Against the closest competitor TDPO, Ra-DPO₁ surpasses both TDPO₁ and TDPO₂ on both metrics, while Ra-DPO₂ matches TDPO₂ on winrate (52.1 vs. 52.2) but leads by +3.5 on Lc winrate. On the IMDb and Anthropic HH training curves, Ra-DPO achieves reward accuracy comparable to TDPO₂ while maintaining lower sequential KL divergence, confirming its risk-awareness in balancing alignment quality against model drift.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two recent lines of work: token-level preference optimization (TDPO, which converts the Bradley-Terry model into a token-level representation via the Bellman equation) and risk-sensitive reinforcement learning (nested risk measures like CVaR and ERM). While TDPO handles sequential generation, it optimizes a risk-neutral expected return; while RA-RLHF and KTO introduce risk awareness, they operate only at the sentence level. Ra-DPO bridges this gap by embedding nested risk measures into the token-level Bellman recursion, producing a 'sequential risk ratio' that accumulates step-wise deviations from the reference model. The state augmentation technique from Ra-PBRL (Zhao et al., NeurIPS 2024) is used to maintain the Markov property under the augmented state space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical concern in LLM alignment: risk-neutral token-level optimization can allow significant model drift that degrades reasoning capabilities, and sentence-level risk constraints miss the sequential nature of generation. The sequential risk ratio provides a principled mechanism to penalize accumulated deviation, and the theoretical guarantees (policy improvement lemma, BT-to-Regret equivalence) are clean. However, empirical validation is limited to small models (GPT-2 Large, Pythia-1.4B/2.8B) and the gains over TDPO are modest (e.g., 55.7 vs. 53.0 Lc winrate on AlpacaEval). Open questions include whether the risk-awareness translates to safety alignment tasks, how the method scales to 70B+ models, and whether the concavity/translation-invariance assumptions on the risk functional are sufficient in practice.
