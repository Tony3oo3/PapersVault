## Problem

While attention heads in large transformers are known to exhibit emergent functional roles, existing interpretability tools like the Logit Lens operate heuristically on individual examples, making it difficult to quantify which heads specialize in specific semantic attributes or to generalize findings across datasets. This paper asks whether a principled, multi-sample decomposition can identify a small set of heads responsible for generating tokens in narrow conceptual domains (e.g., colors, numbers, toxic words) and whether intervening on those heads can controllably suppress or enhance the target attribute in model output.

## Input / Output

The method consumes the residual-stream contribution of each attention head across a batch of samples (an n×d matrix per head), a user-defined or LLM-generated list of tokens defining the target concept, and the model's unembedding matrix (v×d) as a fixed dictionary. For vision-language models, head representations are computed over image patch tokens averaged across spatial positions. The output is a ranked list of heads ordered by the fraction of variance their SOMP reconstruction explains over the concept-restricted dictionary, followed by modified model generations after rescaling (α > 1) or sign-inverting (α = −1) the top-k selected heads' contributions to the residual stream.

## Method

The core technique reinterprets the Logit Lens as a single-step Matching Pursuit and generalizes it via Simultaneous Orthogonal Matching Pursuit (SOMP), which jointly decomposes all samples in a dataset as sparse linear combinations of rows from the unembedding matrix. At each iteration, SOMP selects the dictionary atom (token direction) that maximally correlates with the head residuals across all samples, then refits coefficients via least squares, yielding an interpretable sparse approximation of each head's output. To target a specific concept, the dictionary is restricted to rows corresponding to tokens in a concept-specific word list, and heads are ranked by the variance explained by the resulting reconstruction. Intervention is performed by multiplying the selected heads' residual-stream contributions by a scalar α (−1 for suppression, >1 for enhancement), affecting both direct and indirect information flow to subsequent layers. Unlike the Attention Lens or Tuned Lens, no per-head linear probes are trained; the unembedding matrix serves as a pre-existing, semantically grounded dictionary, making the approach scalable to billion-parameter models and task-agnostic.

## Results

Inverting as few as 8 attention heads (0.8% of Mistral-7B's total) significantly degrades country-name answers on TriviaQA while leaving other answers largely intact, and random head controls show no significant effect. For toxicity mitigation, SOMP-selected heads reduce normalized toxic generation counts to 0.83 (8 heads) and 0.49 (32 heads) on TET, compared to 0.95–0.97 for random heads. In image classification with LLaVA-NeXT-7B, inverting the top-32 SOMP heads drops normalized accuracy to 0.12–0.58 across six datasets (MNIST, SVHN, GTSRB, EuroSAT, RESISC45, DTD), while 32 random heads maintain accuracy at 0.88–1.02. For captioning, inhibiting 16 SOMP heads nearly eliminates target-attribute keywords (e.g., 0.18× color count) while preserving CIDEr above 80%, and enhancing with α=5 increases target concept presence by over 60% with 32 heads.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's sole named baseline is the Logit Lens (LL), adapted to score heads by mean logit over target tokens. SOMP consistently outperforms LL in *specificity*: on TriviaQA, LL-selected heads degrade performance equally on country-name and non-country answers, whereas SOMP heads disproportionately impact the target domain. On toxicity mitigation, SOMP achieves lower normalized toxic counts than LL at every head count (e.g., 0.83 vs. 0.91 for 8 heads on RTP; 0.49 vs. 0.68 for 32 heads on TET). In image classification, SOMP produces substantially larger accuracy drops than LL (e.g., 0.01 vs. 0.48 on GTSRB with 8 heads; 0.12 vs. 0.14 on MNIST with 32 heads), confirming that LL identifies task-relevant but concept-agnostic heads. In captioning, SOMP reduces target-attribute keyword counts far more than LL (e.g., 0.27× vs. 1.00× for colors with 8 inhibitory heads), while LL's enhancing intervention sometimes *decreases* target presence (0.33× for quantity with 32 heads). Random head controls, reported as medians with interquartile ranges over 10 samples, consistently show no significant effect, validating the specificity of both SOMP and (to a lesser degree) LL selections.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reinterprets the Logit Lens [12] as a single step of Matching Pursuit [15] on one example, then generalizes it to a multi-sample, multi-direction sparse recovery procedure using Simultaneous Orthogonal Matching Pursuit (SOMP) [34] over the model's unembedding matrix as a fixed dictionary. It departs from the Attention Lens [14], which requires training a separate linear probe per head, by directly projecting head outputs onto the unembedding without additional training. The work also extends mechanistic interpretability from unimodal LLMs to the multimodal VLM setting, building on prior applications of the Logit Lens to visual tokens [31] and dictionary-learning approaches in vision-language models [24–27].

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a simple, training-free, and mathematically grounded tool for identifying and manipulating concept-specific attention heads, with the practical consequence that editing ~1% of heads can suppress or enhance targeted attributes across both text and vision-language generation. This opens a path toward interpretable, head-level control of generative models without fine-tuning, complementing prompt-based or activation-steering methods. Key limitations include the linearity assumption of SOMP, sensitivity to dictionary quality, and the deliberately coarse intervention mechanism (global sign flip or rescaling). Open questions include whether position- or modality-specific interventions can sharpen control, and whether the approach extends to multimodal-output settings such as image generation.
