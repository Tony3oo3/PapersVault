## Problem

Existing deep learning methods for air quality forecasting rely on discrete station-based representations (multivariate time series or graphs), creating a structural mismatch with the inherently continuous Eulerian dynamics of pollutant advection and diffusion. Additionally, complex atmospheric chemistry—photochemical reactions, gas-to-particle conversion, and their meteorological drivers—remains insufficiently modeled in hybrid physics-guided approaches, which typically restrict physical constraints to graph edges rather than continuous spatial domains.

## Input / Output

The model consumes 72 hours of historical PM2.5 concentrations from sparse monitoring stations (480 in China, 365 in the USA) and 354-channel NCEP meteorological fields at 0.5° spatial resolution (wind vectors, temperature, humidity, solar radiation, etc.), all at 6-hour temporal resolution. Station data is converted to a continuous Eulerian grid via Multiquadric RBF interpolation before entering the network. Output is predicted PM2.5 concentrations at the original station locations for 24, 48, or 72 hours ahead, generated by recursively querying the predicted continuous field at station coordinates.

## Method

CTENet embeds the discretized Advection-Diffusion-Reaction (ADR) equation as a differentiable decoder operating on a continuous Eulerian spatial grid. The Eulerian Pollution Encoder converts sparse station readings into a smooth concentration field via RBF interpolation; the Meteorological Encoder extracts wind vectors and applies a 1×1 convolution to mix heterogeneous meteorological channels. The ADR Decoder computes advection (explicit, using wind-driven central-difference gradients), diffusion (with a learnable coefficient k_θ), and reaction (σ(F_T) ⊙ X_T, where meteorological features act as environment-guided soft attention on pollutant channels), while the source term is implicitly absorbed by the network. A modular Spatiotemporal Sequence Predictor (TAU or ConvLSTM) provides one-step-ahead forecasts of wind, meteorology, and pollutant fields, which are concatenated with the ADR terms and fused via a final 1×1 convolution. Multi-step forecasting proceeds recursively with a sliding window, and the model is trained end-to-end with RMSE loss evaluated only at station locations.

## Results

On the USA PM2.5 dataset, CTENet w/TAU achieves MAE of 2.66/2.99/3.10 and RMSE of 4.86/4.86/5.00 at 24h/48h/72h horizons, representing an average 45.8% RMSE improvement over the best baseline (PM2.5-GNN). On the China PM2.5 dataset, CTENet w/ConvLSTM achieves MAE of 13.79/14.44/15.28 and RMSE of 23.14/23.79/24.47, yielding a 21.0% average RMSE improvement over the best baseline (TAU). The paper reports statistical significance at ≥99.5% confidence across all comparisons, with at least 174 sequences × 365 stations per evaluation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CTENet dominates all 8 baselines on both datasets across all three forecast horizons. On the USA dataset, CTENet w/TAU outperforms the strongest graph-based baseline PM2.5-GNN (RMSE 9.77 at 24h) by 50.2% (RMSE 4.86), and surpasses the physics-guided AirPhyNet (RMSE 11.36) by 57.2%. On the China dataset, CTENet w/ConvLSTM beats TAU (RMSE 26.80 at 24h) by 13.7% and PM2.5-GNN (RMSE 33.25) by 30.5%. Notably, the physics-guided AirPhyNet, which embeds advection-diffusion on graph edges, is outperformed by CTENet on both datasets, supporting the paper's argument that Eulerian spatial continuity is superior to graph-based physical constraints. Graph-based traffic forecasting models (STGCN, DCRNN, GTS) perform poorly, particularly on China data where STGCN and DCRNN show RMSE >43, indicating their lack of adaptation to high-dimensional meteorological inputs. The statistical baseline VAR is consistently the weakest deep-learning competitor, confirming that learned spatiotemporal dependencies substantially outperform linear autoregressive modeling.

## Relation to Prior Work

CTENet departs from the dominant station-based and graph-based paradigms in air quality forecasting (AirFormer, AirPhyNet, PM2.5-GNN) by representing pollutants as continuous Eulerian spatial fields rather than discrete node values, and embeds the Advection-Diffusion-Reaction PDE directly into the network architecture via finite-difference discretization. It builds on TAU and ConvLSTM as interchangeable spatiotemporal sequence predictors for wind, meteorology, and pollutant forecasting, but the key conceptual shift is treating the ADR equation as a structural constraint within the decoder rather than a loss-function penalty or graph-edge constraint. This positions the work at the intersection of classical Chemical Transport Modeling and modern PINN frameworks, explicitly addressing the structural mismatch between continuous atmospheric dynamics and discrete station representations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Eulerian formulation offers a physically grounded path to modeling large-scale pollutant transport and chemical transformation that graph-based methods fundamentally cannot capture, and the 45.8% RMSE reduction on the USA dataset demonstrates the practical payoff of this structural alignment. However, the study is limited to PM2.5 over a single year (2018) in two regions, the reaction term is a simplified sigmoid modulation rather than explicit atmospheric chemistry, and no uncertainty quantification is provided—limiting applicability to probabilistic or risk-sensitive forecasting. Open questions include whether the Eulerian framework generalizes to other pollutants (O3, NOx), scales to 3D vertical transport, and maintains its advantage when meteorological inputs are imperfect or missing.
