## Problem

Object-centric world models, which decompose scenes into per-object latent representations to capture compositional dynamics, have only been validated in simple environments with basic geometries. It remains unclear whether such structured models can be learned purely from pixel observations in visually complex, procedurally generated settings, and whether they can outperform monolithic world models in rollout accuracy and generalization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254060/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 224×224×3 RGB frames and discrete actions from Procgen environments. A frozen Cosmos tokenizer first converts each frame into 14×14 patch-level features, which are then compressed into K object-centric slots (31 or 47 depending on the environment). The world model outputs predicted next-step slots, a scalar reward, and a binary termination signal; a frozen Cosmos decoder reconstructs pixel observations from the predicted slots for evaluation. Notably, SAM2 segmentation masks guide slot binding during training but are phased out via a logarithmic dropout schedule so that inference requires no segmentation overhead.

## Method

Dyn-O operates in two decoupled phases. In the representation phase, Slot Attention extracts K slots from frozen Cosmos patch features, with SAM2 masks used as attention constraints during training and gradually dropped via a log-scheduled probability to enable mask-free inference; the encoder is trained with a joint feature-space and pixel-space reconstruction loss. In the dynamics phase, a single self-attention layer (without positional encoding, preserving permutation equivariance) captures inter-object interactions, and a shared Mamba-based SSM per slot models temporal transitions conditioned on actions, predicting next slots, reward, and termination via separate heads. A key additional contribution is static-dynamic disentanglement: each slot is mapped to a static component (trained with a time-invariance loss plus InfoNCE contrastive objective to prevent collapse) and a dynamic component (trained with slot reconstruction under stop-gradient on the static feature, plus adversarial disentanglement using a Wasserstein-distance discriminator with LeCam regularization to minimize mutual information between the two components).

## Results

On 7 Procgen environments, Dyn-O achieves the best rollout quality across all four metrics: LPIPS 0.33, FVD 361.3, SSIM 0.62, and PSNR 16.34 (averaged over 20-step rollouts on 500 unseen levels). The FVD improvement over DreamerV3 is particularly striking (361.3 vs. 692.5, a ~48% reduction), indicating substantially better temporal coherence. Dyn-O also outperforms its own patch-level ablation (Dyn-O w/o OC) on LPIPS (0.33 vs. 0.41) and FVD (361.3 vs. 538.4), confirming the benefit of object-centric over patch-level representations. For policy learning, Dyn-O's object-centric representations yield rewards of 7.67 (Bigfish) and 19.19 (Starpilot), far exceeding the Cosmos encoder baseline (0.90 and 8.80 respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DreamerV3 (the strongest monolithic world model baseline), Dyn-O wins on all four rollout metrics, with the largest gap in FVD (361.3 vs. 692.5) and SSIM (0.62 vs. 0.56), demonstrating that object-centric prediction space yields more temporally coherent rollouts. Against SOLV (the prior object-centric representation method without segmentation guidance), Dyn-O achieves substantially higher slot-object binding accuracy (FR-ARI 0.51 vs. 0.33 average across environments; 0.80 vs. 0.54 in bigfish), validating the benefit of the SAM2 mask dropout schedule. Against Dreamweaver (an object-centric world model), Dyn-O outperforms on both bigfish (FVD 126.88 vs. 831.17) and starpilot (FVD 211.27 vs. 735.24), though Dreamweaver was only evaluated on two of the seven environments. For downstream policy learning, Dyn-O's representations dramatically outperform raw Cosmos encoder features on Bigfish (7.67 vs. 0.90 reward) and Starpilot (19.19 vs. 8.80), while showing a modest gain on Coinrun (8.95 vs. 8.00). Dyn-O also extends its advantage to CLEVR (FVD 446.57 vs. 1676.18) and Atari Skiing (FVD 187.76 vs. 217.84) against DreamerV3, confirming generalizability beyond Procgen.

## Relation to Prior Work

Dyn-O builds directly on the SOLV framework for self-supervised object-centric video representation learning and the Slot Attention mechanism, but departs in three key ways: (1) it replaces the DINO encoder with the Cosmos tokenizer for better feature quality and a pretrained pixel decoder; (2) it introduces SAM2 segmentation masks with a logarithmic dropout schedule to guide slot-object binding during training while eliminating inference-time overhead; and (3) it decouples representation learning from dynamics modeling, using a pretrained slot encoder and applying Mamba-based SSMs solely for transition prediction, in contrast to SlotSSM's tightly coupled design. The static-dynamic disentanglement via adversarial mutual-information minimization extends prior DSVAE-style approaches with a more principled separation mechanism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first validation that object-centric world models can be learned purely from pixel trajectories in visually complex, procedurally-generated environments (Procgen), moving the field beyond the simple geometric shapes that dominated prior work. The practical mask-dropout schedule and the decoupled architecture make the approach more deployable than prior methods that require segmentation at inference or jointly train all components. Open questions include whether the approach scales to 3D or real-world robotics settings, whether the SAM2 dependency during training can be fully eliminated, and whether the static-dynamic split generalizes to environments where object identity itself changes over time.
