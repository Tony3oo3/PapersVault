## Problem

Text-to-image diffusion models exhibit 'role collapse' on rare action-based compositions (e.g., 'mouse chasing cat'), consistently defaulting to their frequent reversed counterparts (e.g., 'cat chasing mouse'). Prior work attributes this to architectural limitations or data scarcity, but the paper shows the failure is specifically driven by the overrepresentation of frequent counterparts rather than mere absence of rare compositions—models can generate similarly rare compositions like 'mouse chasing boy' without issue. This gap motivates both a controlled benchmark (RoleBench) and a targeted mitigation strategy.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt describing a directional action-based relation (e.g., 'A photo of one mouse chasing one cat') and produces a photorealistic RGB image. RoleBench evaluates 10 action verbs across 420 prompts (basic and spatially-aware variants), generating 800 images per model. Evaluation uses VQAScore with fine-grained binary questions probing spatial configuration, orientation, pose, and facial expression, plus a directional bias score β that measures alignment with the reversed prompt relative to the intended one.

## Method

ReBind decomposes a rare composition c_R = (s_R, r, o_R) into active intermediates (s_R, r, o') preserving the subject's active role and passive intermediates (s', r, o_R) preserving the object's passive role, where s' and o' are LLM-selected objects from different semantic categories to avoid inheriting the same directional bias. Intermediate images are generated with DALL-E 3 using spatially-aware prompts, then filtered to retain only those with negative directional bias (β < 0). The T2I model (SDXL) is fine-tuned via LoRA (rank 32, 1000 steps) on these intermediates with a weighted diffusion loss L_compos = λ·L_active + L_passive, where λ is ramped up exponentially to emphasize active-role binding. The key insight is that intermediates do not suffer from role collapse (they exhibit negative β), so fine-tuning on them reinforces underrepresented role bindings without reinforcing the dominant frequent pattern, enabling the model to reconstruct the intended rare relation at inference time.

## Results

On RoleBench rare compositions, all pre-trained T2I models exhibit strong role collapse with positive directional bias β (e.g., SDXL β=23.1, DALL-E 3 β=16.0, AuraFlow2 β=10.1 on VQAScore). ReBind reduces SDXL's VQAScore β from 23.1 to 7.9 (a 15.2-point improvement), achieving the lowest bias across all evaluation dimensions (spatial: 7.0, orientation: 3.6, pose: 3.2, facial expression: 2.5). In human preference evaluations, ReBind is preferred over DALL-E 3 (73%), SDXL (69%), RPG (73%), R2F (74%), and RRNet (78%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReBind (β=7.9) outperforms all eight baselines on VQAScore directional bias for rare compositions. The largest gains are over SDXL (23.1→7.9, −15.2) and IterComp (22.2→7.9, −14.3), both of which rely on the base SDXL architecture without addressing distributional priors. Among specialized compositional methods, ReBind beats RRNet (15.1) by 7.2 points, R2F (15.7) by 7.8, RPG (16.7) by 8.8, and SLD (16.6) by 8.7. Notably, even DALL-E 3 (16.0), the strongest pre-trained model, is outperformed by 8.1 points. On spatial bias specifically, ReBind (7.0) is competitive with SLD (6.7) and RRNet (8.6), while substantially improving on non-spatial dimensions like facial expression (2.5 vs. 8.3 for SDXL). In human pairwise comparisons, ReBind is preferred 73–78% of the time over all baselines, confirming that the automated β metric aligns with perceptual quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends compositional text-to-image generation research from static spatial/attribute relations (left-right, color-size binding) to dynamic action-based relations requiring directional role binding between agent and patient. It departs from prior rare-concept generalization work (Samuel et al., Park et al., Cong et al.) that attributes failures to data scarcity, by showing the *presence* of frequent reversed compositions is the key driver of failure. Methodologically, ReBind contrasts sharply with attention-manipulation (Attend-and-Excite, RECO), layout-conditioning (GLIGEN, LayoutGPT), and LLM-guided (SLD, RPG) approaches by using only lightweight LoRA fine-tuning on LLM-decomposed intermediate compositions, avoiding architectural modifications or inference-time overhead.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of 'role collapse' as a distinct failure mode—where models generate near-identical images for a rare prompt and its frequent reversal—reframes compositional generalization as a distributional asymmetry problem rather than a capacity or data-scarcity problem. The practical implication is that a handful of carefully chosen intermediate compositions can redirect model behavior without expensive retraining. However, the approach is a proof-of-concept limited to 10 action relations, relies on DALL-E 3 for generating training intermediates (creating a circular dependency), and shows limited generalization to out-of-distribution relations. Open questions include whether the decomposition strategy scales to multi-object or multi-relation scenes, whether it can be applied without access to a strong proprietary generator for intermediate data, and whether the effect persists across model architectures beyond SDXL/SD3.
