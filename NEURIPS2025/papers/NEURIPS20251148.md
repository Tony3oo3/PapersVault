## Problem

The k-nearest neighbor rule is known to be consistent under i.i.d. or non-adaptive sampling, but it is unclear whether this extends to the adaptive sampling model where future instances can depend on past labels. This paper shows that in the worst case, an adaptive adversary can indeed break k-NN consistency by manufacturing spurious patterns from noise, but then proves that under a mild uniform absolute continuity (smoothed) condition on the data-generating process, k-NN recovers online consistency — bridging the gap between i.i.d. and fully adversarial settings.

## Input / Output

At each round n, the learner receives an instance X_n from a separable metric space (X, ρ) selected by an adaptive process with knowledge of all past labels, and must output a binary prediction Ŷ_n ∈ {0,1} via majority vote over the k_n previously memorized nearest neighbors. The label Y_n is then revealed, drawn from Ber(η(X_n)) for a conditional label distribution η: X → [0,1]. The consistency criterion compares the asymptotic mistake rate against the Bayes optimal predictor 1{η(X_n) ≥ 1/2}, requiring the difference to be non-positive almost surely.

## Method

The proof decomposes consistency into statistical convergence (empirical label frequencies over k_n neighbors converge to their conditional expectations) and geometric convergence (neighbor label expectations converge to η(X_n)), then shows both hold under uniform absolute continuity. A key technical tool is a partial coupling lemma (Lemma 17) that bounds the probability of a bad event under the adaptive process by the probability under an independent draw plus a smoothing term, enabling martingale concentration arguments. For the general measurable η case in upper doubling spaces (Theorem 5), the paper introduces an indicator process that flags 'bad' instances where η is discontinuous or the sampling density is too concentrated, reduces the problem to a continuous η_0 on the remaining subsequence, and uses sequentially-constructed cover trees with packing bounds to show that flagged points have negligible long-term influence on nearest neighbor queries.

## Results

The paper establishes two main consistency theorems. Theorem 2 shows that under uniform absolute continuity (rate ε(δ)) and continuous η, the k_n-NN rule is consistent when k_n = ω(log(1/ε⁻¹(n^-(1+c)))) and k_n = o(n); for L-dominated processes this reduces to the standard k_n = ω(log n) and k_n = o(n). Theorem 5 extends this to universal consistency in upper doubling spaces for any measurable η, requiring only that (k_n) be a regular sequence (growing between ω(log n) and o(n/log n) with k_{cn}/k_n → 1). Proposition 7 shows a worst-case adaptive strategy (binary search on noise) that forces k_n-NN to incur a persistent error rate of at least (1−p)/8, demonstrating that the smoothing assumption is necessary.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines or experimental comparisons. The results are positioned relative to prior theoretical work: Dasgupta and So (2024) proved online consistency of 1-NN under the same uniform absolute continuity assumption but in the noiseless (realizable) setting; this paper extends that to k_n-NN with noisy labels. Kulkarni and Posner (1995) established consistency under non-adaptive sampling with conditional independence of labels; this paper removes the non-adaptivity requirement entirely. Haghtalab et al. (2020) introduced a smoothed analysis framework for parametric online learning; the uniform absolute continuity condition here generalizes their smoothness assumption to the non-parametric, adaptive setting. The key conceptual advance is showing that the worst-case failure modes (Proposition 7) are 'rare' under the mild smoothing condition, bridging the gap between i.i.d. and adversarial settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the adaptive-sampling consistency framework of Dasgupta and So (2024), which established online consistency for the 1-nearest neighbor rule under uniform absolute continuity, to the k_n-nearest neighbor rule with noisy labels. It also generalizes the non-adaptive k-NN consistency results of Kulkarni and Posner (1995) and Chaudhuri and Dasgupta (2014) to the adaptive setting, where future instances can depend on past labels. The smoothed-analysis condition (uniform absolute continuity) generalizes the smoothness assumption of Haghtalab et al. (2020), and the proof techniques—partial coupling, sandwiching covers, and cover-tree packing bounds—adapt and extend tools from both the i.i.d. and noiseless adaptive literatures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work closes a natural gap in the theory of non-parametric online learning by showing that the standard k_n-NN rule remains consistent under adaptive sampling with noise, provided the data process satisfies a mild uniform absolute continuity condition and k_n grows between log n and n/log n. The negative result (Proposition 7) demonstrating that adaptive sampling can manufacture spurious structure from pure noise is important for understanding the limits of non-parametric methods in sequential settings. Open questions include whether the continuity assumption on η in Theorem 2 can be further relaxed outside upper doubling spaces, whether tighter concentration bounds (beyond the naive union bound in Lemma 10) yield sharper k_n requirements, and whether the framework extends to multiclass or regression problems.
