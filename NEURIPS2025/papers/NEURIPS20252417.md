## Problem

Existing multi-objective visual token pruning methods that combine Visual Preservation (VP) and Prompt Alignment (PA) through static strategies fail to consistently outperform single-objective baselines, raising the question of whether integrating these objectives offers fundamental advantages. The paper identifies that the relative importance of VP versus PA varies across tasks due to a previously uncharacterized prompt-visual coupling, and that existing methods overlook this variation.

## Input / Output

MoB operates at an intermediate transformer layer (typically layer 2) of a pre-trained MLLM, receiving N visual tokens and L prompt tokens as d-dimensional embeddings in a shared representation space. It outputs a pruned subset of K visual tokens (K ≪ N) that replaces the full visual set for the remaining layers, which then produce the standard text response. All tokens are L2-normalized so that Euclidean distance is induced by cosine similarity, and the method is training-free with no gradient updates or prompt engineering.

## Method

MoB reformulates visual token pruning as a bi-objective ε-covering problem, partitioning the K retained tokens into a prompt-center set S_p (budget K_p) and a visual-center set S_v (budget K−K_p). S_p is selected via a k-fold Nearest-Neighbor covering that over-samples the k nearest visual tokens per prompt token and then refines to the top-K_p by worst-case alignment, while S_v is selected via Farthest Point Sampling on the remaining tokens to minimize the visual covering radius. The key theoretical contribution is the first closed-form error bound for visual token pruning based on Hausdorff distance (Lemma 1), and a quantitative trade-off theorem (Theorem 1) showing that the optimal attainment level per objective is ε* = max{η/z, √D₁·K^(−1/d_eff)}, where η is the prompt-visual coupling and K is the budget. This reduces the trade-off to a budget-allocation problem: under weak coupling (large η), more budget goes to S_p; under strong coupling (small η), more goes to S_v. The overall complexity is O(N(L+K)d), making it scalable to high-resolution and multi-frame video inputs.

## Results

On LLaVA-1.5-7B at an 88.9% visual token reduction (retaining only 64 of 576 tokens), MoB preserves 96.4% of vanilla performance on average, outperforming the second-best method DART (93.7%) by 2.7 percentage points. On LLaVA-Next-7B (2880 tokens) at the same 88.9% reduction, MoB retains 95.4% versus DART's 93.9%. On Video-LLaVA-7B at a 93.4% reduction, MoB preserves 97.9% of average performance, surpassing TwigVLM (96.3%) and VisionZip (93.2%). MoB also delivers a 1.3–1.5× inference speedup on LLaVA-Next-7B with negligible accuracy loss.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MoB consistently outperforms all seven baselines across every model and pruning rate tested. Against single-objective VP methods, MoB's advantage is most dramatic: at 88.9% reduction on LLaVA-1.5-7B, MoB (96.4%) surpasses FastV (77.3%) by 19.1 points and DART (93.7%) by 2.7 points. Against the single-objective PA method SparseVLM (84.6%), MoB leads by 11.8 points, confirming that neither objective alone suffices. The multi-objective baseline MustDrop (90.1%) is outperformed by 6.3 points, validating the paper's central claim that naive static integration of VP and PA is suboptimal. On LLaVA-Next-7B, MoB (95.4%) beats FasterVLM (89.8%) and MustDrop (90.4%) by 5.6 and 5.0 points respectively. On video understanding (Video-LLaVA-7B, 93.4% reduction), MoB (97.9%) edges out TwigVLM (96.3%) by 1.6 points and VisionZip (93.2%) by 4.7 points, while FastV collapses to 52.1%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the established line of visual token pruning for MLLMs, which splits into visual-preservation (VP) methods (FastV, ToMe, DART, FasterVLM) and prompt-alignment (PA) methods (SparseVLM, PyramidDrop), with recent multi-objective attempts (MustDrop, VisionZip, TokenCarve) combining both. The key departure is a theoretical reframing: by deriving the first closed-form Hausdorff-distance error bound and recasting pruning as a bi-objective ε-covering problem, the paper shows that the relative weight of VP vs. PA is not fixed but governed by a task-dependent prompt-visual coupling (η), explaining why prior static multi-objective integrations fail to consistently beat single-objective baselines. MoB then reduces the resulting trade-off to a budget-allocation problem solved by greedy radius trading (k-fold nearest-neighbor covering for PA, farthest-point sampling for VP), a clean geometric procedure distinct from the attention-score or multi-stage pipelines used by prior multi-objective methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first principled, provable framework for visual token pruning, replacing ad-hoc multi-objective heuristics with a geometric covering formulation that yields explicit performance guarantees and linear scalability in token count. The counterintuitive finding that naive objective integration can underperform single-objective methods (the '1+1<1' phenomenon) is well-evidenced across four MLLMs and 14 benchmarks, and the coupling-based budget allocation offers a transparent, interpretable knob (K_p) for practitioners. Open questions include whether η can be estimated adaptively at inference time without a preliminary search, whether the Lipschitz and covering-regularity assumptions hold for all architectures, and whether the framework extends to other redundancy-heavy modalities (point clouds, multi-sensor fusion) as the authors speculate.
