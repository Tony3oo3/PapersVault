## Problem

Real-world image search is rarely one-shot; users iteratively refine queries guided by a mental image of the target. Existing interactive retrieval methods (ChatIR, PlugIR) provide only verbal feedback, which is ambiguous, redundant, or misleading—failing to reveal the system's actual visual interpretation of the query and leaving users in a trial-and-error loop.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254579/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each interaction round, the method takes a free-form textual query (describing the user's mental image) and an image database (ranging from 31k to 1M+ images), and produces two outputs: a retrieved image selected via image-to-image cosine similarity in BLIP-2's 256-d feature space, and a 1024×1024 synthetic image generated by a diffusion model that visualizes the system's interpretation of the query. The synthetic image is the key feedback signal the user inspects to decide how to refine the next query.

## Method

GenIR is a plug-and-play, training-free generative retrieval loop with three steps per round: (1) a text-to-image diffusion model (Infinity, Lumina, SD3.5, FLUX.1, or HiDream) renders the current textual query into a synthetic image, (2) both the synthetic image and all database images are encoded by BLIP-2's image encoder and matched via cosine similarity for image-to-image retrieval, and (3) the synthetic image is shown to the user as explicit visual feedback, making the system's internal 'visual belief' in the vision-language latent space directly inspectable. The key insight is that generation reifies the query's embedding into an interpretable visual form, transitioning retrieval from cross-modal (text-to-image) to same-modal (image-to-image) matching and letting users pinpoint discrepancies (missing objects, wrong attributes, spatial errors) that verbal feedback cannot convey. The framework is model-agnostic across generators and retrievers, and the authors additionally release an automated curation pipeline (Algorithm 1) that uses Gemma3 as a simulated user to produce a multi-round MIR dataset with per-round queries, synthetic images, retrieved images, and correctness labels.

## Results

On MSCOCO (50k search space), GenIR with the Infinity diffusion model achieves ~90% Hits@10 at round 0 and ~98% by round 10, substantially outperforming Prediction Feedback (92%), Verbal Feedback with Gemma3-12b (92%), and ChatIR (73.6%) at round 10. Cross-domain results confirm consistent advantages: GenIR reaches 70% Hits@10 on FFHQ (vs. 52% for the next best method) and 73% on Clothing-ADC (vs. 50%), the latter with a 1M+ image search space. Even the text queries refined through GenIR's visual feedback loop (92.33% at round 10) significantly outperform ChatIR's verbal-feedback queries (73.64%) under identical text-to-image retrieval settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GenIR decisively outperforms ChatIR (the primary verbal-feedback baseline) across all four datasets and all interaction rounds. On MSCOCO, GenIR with synthetic image feedback reaches 98.01% Hits@10 at round 10 versus ChatIR's 73.64%—a 24.4-point gap. Even when restricted to text-to-image retrieval (no image-to-image step), GenIR-refined queries (92.33%) beat ChatIR-refined queries (73.64%) by 18.7 points, confirming that the visual feedback loop produces fundamentally better query annotations. Against the Prediction Feedback baseline (which shows the top-1 retrieved image to the user), GenIR maintains a 6-point advantage at round 10 on MSCOCO and a 18-point advantage on FFHQ. The paper also notes that Prediction Feedback plateaus after 2–4 rounds and can be surpassed by text-only methods in longer dialogues, whereas GenIR's trajectory continues to improve, suggesting visual feedback avoids the local-minima trap inherent in database-constrained feedback.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of interactive information retrieval (IIR) and generative vision models. It departs from the conversational retrieval line (ChatIR, PlugIR) that relies on verbal/textual feedback for multi-round query refinement, instead repurposing text-to-image diffusion models as an explicit visual feedback mechanism within the retrieval loop. While Imagine-and-Seek uses a single generated proxy image for one-shot retrieval, GenIR integrates generation as a recurring step in an iterative loop where the synthetic image serves dual purposes: as a retrieval query and as interpretable feedback for the user. The method is model-agnostic, treating the diffusion generator and the image encoder (BLIP-2/CLIP) as interchangeable components.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper introduces a formally defined task (MIR) with a multi-round dataset that captures visual feedback traces, providing a testbed for studying interactive retrieval with grounded visual understanding. Key open questions include whether the VLM-simulated user adequately models real human search behavior with partial or evolving mental images, and whether the framework can be optimized end-to-end (e.g., via RL on the trajectory-rich dataset). The finding that generated synthetic images outperform both verbal feedback and actual retrieved images as feedback signals suggests a broader principle: reifying a system's internal representation in the user's modality may be more effective than either abstract descriptions or database-constrained examples.
