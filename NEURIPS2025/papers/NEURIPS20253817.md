## Problem

Nonconvex min-max optimization and semi-infinite programming (SIP) have been studied largely in isolation: min-max methods assume finite-dimensional constraints, while SIP algorithms typically require convexity or lack the nested maximization structure. This paper tackles the intersection—nonconvex min-max problems with infinite nonconvex constraints—and provides the first non-asymptotic convergence guarantees for this class, which arises in adversarial robustness, distributionally robust learning, and safety-constrained optimization.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes a shared parameter vector x ∈ ℝⁿ, smooth task loss functions ℓᵢ, simplex uncertainty sets Y and W encoding distributional ambiguity, and an initial point x₀. It outputs an ε-KKT solution characterized by three simultaneous metrics: stationarity (‖∇f + λ∇g‖ ≤ ε), feasibility ([g(x)]₊ ≤ ε), and complementarity slackness (|λg(x)| ≤ ε). The infinite constraint set is never explicitly enumerated; instead, the algorithm maintains an inexact maximizer w_k ≈ argmax_{w∈W} ψ(x_k, w) updated via a variable number of gradient ascent steps whose count adapts to the current infeasibility level.

## Method

The proposed Inexact Dynamic Barrier Primal-Dual (iDB-PD) method solves a QP subproblem at each iteration to compute a primal direction d_k that simultaneously descends the objective and respects a linearized constraint, with a dual multiplier λ_k updated via a closed-form expression. A key innovation is the indicator function ζ(x,w) = [ψ(x,w)]₊‖∇_x ψ(x,w)‖, which gates the dual update: when ζ = 0 the constraint is treated as inactive and λ_k is forced to zero, preventing the multiplier from diverging as the constraint gradient vanishes. The barrier function ρ(x,w) = ‖∇_x ψ(x,w)‖ (corresponding to τ = 1 in the dynamic barrier framework of Gong et al.) encodes the feasibility direction. The maximization variables y and w are updated inexact via N_k and M_k steps of accelerated gradient ascent, with M_k scaling logarithmically in T and the current infeasibility residual. Under a Lojasiewicz inequality on the squared infeasibility residual [ψ(·,w)]₊² with exponent θ ∈ (0,1), the method achieves O(ε⁻³), O(ε⁻⁶ᶿ), and O(ε⁻³ᶿ/(1−ᶿ)) iteration complexities for stationarity, feasibility, and slackness respectively, recovering the O(ε⁻³) rate for all three metrics when θ = 1/2 (the PL condition).

## Results

The paper reports convergence behavior (rather than final accuracy scores) across five datasets (Multi-MNIST, CHD49, Multi-Fashion MNIST, Yeast, 20NewsGroup) using a two-task robust MTL formulation with a fully connected network (one hidden layer, tanh activation, cross-entropy loss). iDB-PD consistently drives all three KKT residuals—stationarity, infeasibility, and complementarity slackness—toward zero on every dataset. The theoretical guarantees establish O(ε⁻³) iterations for ε-stationarity, O(ε⁻⁶ᶿ) for ε-feasibility, and O(ε⁻³ᶿ/(1−ᶿ)) for ε-slackness, with the special case θ=1/2 (PL condition) unifying all three to O(ε⁻³). No single numerical accuracy table is provided; results are presented as convergence trajectories in Figures 1–4.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the adaptive discretization method (Blankenship & Falk [8]) solved with the COOPER library [24], iDB-PD wins decisively: the discretization baseline fails to reduce infeasibility and diverges in stationarity on both Multi-MNIST and CHD49 (and the remaining three datasets in the appendix), while iDB-PD converges in all three KKT metrics. Against GDMA [31, 57] applied to a reformulated min-max with a penalty weight ρ, iDB-PD again outperforms: GDMA with small ρ (ρ=1, 2) achieves low stationarity but fails to reduce infeasibility, while GDMA with large ρ (ρ=5, 10) exhibits unstable oscillatory behavior in both stationarity and infeasibility. Across all five datasets and all four ρ values tested, iDB-PD is the only method that simultaneously converges in stationarity, infeasibility, and slackness, confirming the advantage of the semi-infinite constrained formulation where the constraint weight is dynamically adjusted by the algorithm rather than fixed a priori.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three well-studied but previously separate threads: nonconvex constrained optimization (penalty/augmented Lagrangian methods, dynamic barrier gradient descent [25]), min-max optimization (GDA, primal-dual schemes), and semi-infinite programming (discretization, cutting surface methods). The key departure is that no prior work addresses the simultaneous presence of nonconvexity in both the objective and constraints alongside an infinite constraint set with a nested maximization structure. The proposed iDB-PD method builds most directly on the dynamic barrier framework of Gong et al. [25], but introduces an indicator function ζ to regulate the dual multiplier without requiring its boundedness, and extends the analysis from finite to infinite constraints via a Lojasiewicz-based regularity condition that generalizes the classical PL-based regularity assumption.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first paper to establish non-asymptotic convergence guarantees (O(ε⁻³) stationarity, O(ε⁻⁶ᶿ) feasibility, O(ε⁻³ᶿ/(1−ᶿ)) slackness) for nonconvex semi-infinite min-max problems, filling a clear gap between the convex SIP literature and the nonconvex finite-constraint optimization literature. The Lojasiewicz assumption (θ ∈ (0,1)) is more general than the PL condition (θ = 1/2) and covers a broad class of smooth neural network losses, though it excludes nonsmooth activations like ReLU. Open questions include whether the method scales to truly large-scale problems (experiments use at most ~20K samples), whether the Lojasiewicz exponent can be estimated or relaxed in practice, and whether the complexity degradation as θ → 0 reflects a fundamental barrier or an artifact of the analysis.
