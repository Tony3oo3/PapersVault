## Problem

Diffusion Transformers (DiTs) have become the dominant architecture for image and video generation, but adapting them to molecular conformer generation introduces unresolved challenges: integrating discrete graph connectivity with continuous 3D geometry, respecting Euclidean symmetries (translation/rotation), and designing conditioning that generalizes across molecules of varying size and structure. No prior work has systematically studied how different symmetry-preserving attention mechanisms and positional embeddings within a DiT framework affect conformer generation quality and efficiency.

## Input / Output

The model takes a molecular graph G = (V, E) as input, where nodes carry atomic types and edges carry bond orders plus structural features (rings, stereochemistry), and produces a 3D point cloud x ∈ R^{N×3} of atomic coordinates by sampling from the SE(3)-invariant conditional distribution p(x|G). Internally, the network predicts a per-atom 3D velocity vector that is integrated via a 50-step Euler ODE; all positions are centered to zero center of mass, and the model operates on variable-sized molecules (from small QM9 compounds to drug-like molecules with ~44 atoms on average, and up to 100+ atoms for generalization tests).

## Method

DiTMC is a modular DiT-style architecture trained with conditional flow matching (CFM) that separates 3D coordinate processing from graph-based conditioning. It introduces two complementary conditioning strategies: atom-wise tokens from a MeshGraphNet-inspired GNN and pair-wise tokens from geodesic graph distances (Graphormer-inspired), both injected via AdaLN/AdaScale as in standard DiTs. The key design axis is the positional embedding and attention mechanism: three variants are studied — absolute PE (no symmetry), relative PE (translation-invariant), and SO(3)-equivariant PE(3) built from spherical harmonics with an equivariant attention block. Training uses a reparameterized CFM loss predicting the clean target x₁ with a 1/(1-t)² weighting, plus random-rotation data augmentation for the non-equivariant variants. The central empirical finding is that the simpler non-equivariant aPE/rPE variants achieve state-of-the-art precision and physical validity at roughly 3.5× lower training cost than the equivariant PE(3) variant, while the equivariant variant produces tighter RMSD distributions at small thresholds.

## Results

On GEOM-DRUGS, DiTMC achieves state-of-the-art precision across all variants: DiTMC+rPE-L (28.3M params) attains the best COV-P Mean of 78.1% and COV-P Median of 86.4%, while DiTMC+aPE-L (28.2M) achieves the best AMR-P Mean of 0.470 Å and AMR-P Median of 0.387 Å. On GEOM-QM9, DiTMC+rPE-B (9.6M) leads with AMR-R Mean of 0.070 Å and AMR-P Mean of 0.080 Å, outperforming ET-Flow (8.3M) on all precision metrics. For ensemble physical properties on GEOM-DRUGS, DiTMC+aPE-B achieves the lowest median absolute error in energy (0.17 kcal/mol), dipole moment (0.16 D), and excitation energy (0.27 kcal/mol), outperforming MCF-L by up to a factor of four on energy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiTMC outperforms all baselines on precision metrics (COV-P and AMR-P) on GEOM-DRUGS: DiTMC+rPE-L achieves 78.1% COV-P Mean vs. 75.2% for ET-Flow-SS (8.3M, similar size) and 66.8% for MCF-L (242M), while DiTMC+aPE-L reaches 0.470 Å AMR-P Mean vs. 0.517 Å for ET-Flow-SS and 0.618 Å for MCF-L. On recall metrics (COV-R, AMR-R), MCF-L and DMT-L still lead (84.7–85.8% COV-R Mean), but DiTMC+PE(3)-L narrows the gap to 80.8% COV-R Mean and 0.415 Å AMR-R Mean. On GEOM-QM9, DiTMC variants surpass ET-Flow on all AMR and COV-P metrics (e.g., DiTMC+rPE-B: 0.070 Å AMR-R Mean vs. 0.073 Å for ET-Flow), while MCF-B and DMT-B remain slightly ahead on AMR-R. For out-of-distribution generalization on GEOM-XL (molecules >100 atoms), DiTMC+aPE-L (28.2M) matches or beats MCF-L (242M) on AMR-P (2.81 vs. 2.94 Å Mean) using ~8× fewer parameters. On ensemble physical properties, DiTMC+aPE-B outperforms MCF-L by a factor of four on energy MAE (0.17 vs. 0.68 kcal/mol) and ET-Flow on dipole moment (0.16 vs. 0.18 D), demonstrating superior physical validity despite using no geometric priors.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the Diffusion Transformer (DiT) architecture, originally designed for grid-structured image data, to the continuous, irregular geometry of molecular conformers. It builds on the conditional flow matching / stochastic interpolant framework (Lipman et al., Albergo et al.) for the generative objective, borrows the GNN-based atom-wise conditioning from MeshGraphNet and the geodesic-distance pair-wise conditioning from Graphormer, and draws on equivariant message-passing networks (PaiNN, NequIP) for its SO(3)-equivariant positional embeddings. The key departure from prior conformer generation methods (GeoDiff, TorsionDiff, MCF, ET-Flow) is the systematic, modular treatment of positional embeddings and attention mechanisms within a unified DiT block, enabling direct comparison of equivariant vs. non-equivariant formulations under matched parameter budgets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DiTMC achieves state-of-the-art precision and physical validity on GEOM-QM9 and GEOM-DRUGS with competitive parameter counts (~9–28M vs. 150–242M for MCF/DMT), and generalizes well to larger unseen molecules on GEOM-XL. The finding that simple non-equivariant variants (aPE, rPE) perform competitively with the SO(3)-equivariant PE(3) variant at roughly 3–3.5× lower computational cost provides practical guidance for scaling conformer generation. Open questions include extending to larger, more flexible molecules (current evaluation is limited to ≤100 atoms), integrating fast attention to overcome the quadratic scaling in atom count, and whether the conditioning strategies can be repurposed for de-novo molecular generation.
