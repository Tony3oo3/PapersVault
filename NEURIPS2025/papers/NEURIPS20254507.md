## Problem

Current 3D vision-language models for medical imaging struggle with high-resolution, long-sequence CT volumes: contrastive pretraining produces vision encoders misaligned with clinical language, slice-wise tokenization blurs fine anatomy, and existing encoder-decoders cannot reconstruct or generate scans exceeding a few hundred slices without spatial discontinuities. The paper asks whether improved volumetric tokenization—rather than simply scaling the language backbone—can close this gap for both report generation and text-conditioned CT synthesis.

## Input / Output

The encoder consumes a 3D chest CT volume (512×512×241, resampled to 0.75×0.75×1.5 mm, HU-clipped to [−1000, 1000]) and first applies a 3D Haar wavelet transform that halves resolution along each axis while expanding channels from 1 to 8 frequency subbands. The encoder outputs a compact sequence of discrete volumetric tokens (18-dim binary codes packed into integers from a 262,144-entry codebook); for the 8×8×8 variant the token count is quadrupled with 72-dim merged embeddings. Downstream, these tokens feed either LLaMA 3.1-8B for autoregressive report generation or a 12-layer cross-attention transformer (with T5-encoded text prompts) for flow-matching-based CT synthesis, producing 512×512×241 volumes. The same causal architecture accepts single 2D slices (D=1) without architectural modification.

## Method

BTB3D is a causal 3D convolutional encoder-decoder with lookup-free quantization (LFQ) that processes CT scans slice-by-slice along the axial dimension using factorized 1×k×k spatial and k×1×1 temporal convolutions with causal padding, ensuring each token attends only to past slices. A three-stage training curriculum progressively scales context: Stage 1 trains on 9-slice subvolumes for local reconstruction, Stage 2 introduces overlapping temporal tiling (retaining only the second token from each 9-slice window) to extend to 201-slice sequences, and Stage 3 freezes the encoder and fine-tunes only the decoder on 241-slice volumes for long-range anatomical coherence. Training combines L1 reconstruction loss, a 3D adversarial loss applied in the CT domain, and a VQ commitment loss with entropy regularization to prevent code collapse. Two compression variants are offered—8×8×8 (preserving spatial detail for synthesis) and 16×16×8 (higher compression for report generation)—and the framework bridges 2D/3D by applying the identical 3D wavelet and causal convolution pipeline to both single slices and full volumes.

## Results

On CT-RATE report generation, BTB3D-16 (16×16×8 compression) achieves a clinical F1 of 0.258, a 40% relative improvement over CT-CHAT (0.184), with BLEU-1 of 0.439 and BLEU-mean of 0.305. On the external RadChestCT benchmark, BTB3D-16 reaches F1=0.266, a 46% relative gain over the best baseline. For text-to-CT synthesis, BTB3D-8 (8×8×8) reduces mean FID from 9.51 (GenerateCT) to 2.24 (76.5% reduction) and halves FVD (CT-Net) from 7.66 to 3.96, while producing 512×512×241 volumes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BTB3D-16 decisively outperforms all three report-generation baselines on F1 and recall: it beats CT-CHAT (F1 0.258 vs 0.184, +40%), CT2Rep (0.258 vs 0.160, +61%), and Merlin (0.258 vs 0.160, +61%), while also leading on BLEU-1 (+18% over CT-CHAT) and BLEU-mean (+12%). The trade-off is lower precision (0.260 vs CT-CHAT's 0.450), meaning BTB3D reports more findings (higher recall 0.260 vs 0.158) at the cost of some false positives. On text-to-CT synthesis, BTB3D-8 dominates GenerateCT and MedSyn across every metric: mean FID drops to 2.24 (vs 9.51 and 12.59), FVD-CT-Net falls to 3.96 (vs 7.66 and 13.93), and CLIP text-image alignment rises to 24.27 (vs 23.63 and 23.57). The higher-compression BTB3D-16 variant still surpasses both generation baselines on FID (5.01) and FVD (4.02), confirming that the tokenization advantage persists even under aggressive compression. On the out-of-distribution RadChestCT set, BTB3D-16 extends its lead to F1=0.266 versus CT-CHAT's 0.182, validating generalization beyond the training distribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

BTB3D departs from the dominant contrastive-pretraining paradigm (CLIP-style alignment) used by CT-CHAT, Merlin, and fVLM for 3D medical vision encoders, arguing that paired-report semantics in radiology violate the assumption that unpaired samples are unrelated. Instead, it adopts a reconstruction-based pretraining objective with a causal 3D convolutional encoder-decoder, drawing architectural ideas from video generation (Haar wavelet compression, lookup-free quantization from QTIP, causal temporal convolutions). For text-to-CT synthesis, it replaces the cascaded low-res-then-upsample pipeline of GenerateCT and the lightweight 3D transformer of MedSyn with a unified encoder-decoder that directly produces high-resolution volumetric tokens, trained via flow matching. The three-stage curriculum (short subvolumes → overlapping temporal tiling → decoder-only fine-tuning) is a novel training strategy not present in prior 3D medical VLM work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper's central claim—that precise volumetric tokenization, rather than scaling the language backbone, is the key bottleneck for 3D medical VLMs—shifts attention away from ever-larger LLMs toward the vision encoder, a practically important reframing for the field. The 40% F1 gain in report generation and 75% FID reduction in CT synthesis are substantial, but the evaluation is confined to chest CT on a single training dataset (CT-RATE) with no statistical significance testing or expert reader validation, leaving open whether the gains transfer to other anatomies, modalities (MRI, PET), or clinical workflows. The compression-quality trade-off between the 8³ and 16²×8 variants offers a useful practical guideline, but the lack of ablations isolating individual components (wavelet vs. causal conv vs. three-stage training) limits mechanistic understanding. Future work should address uncertainty modeling, multi-organ generalization, and prospective clinical trials.
