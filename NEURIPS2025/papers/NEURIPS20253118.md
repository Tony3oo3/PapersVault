## Problem

Diffusion-based real-world super-resolution models achieve impressive texture recovery but require hundreds of millions to billions of parameters and multi-step sampling, making them impractical for on-device deployment. Even existing single-step and lightweight variants remain too heavy for edge hardware, leaving a significant gap between state-of-the-art quality and real-time mobile inference.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253118/cropped_page1_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

PocketSR takes a single low-resolution degraded RGB image (synthesized via the Real-ESRGAN degradation pipeline during training) and produces a high-resolution super-resolved image in one forward pass at a fixed diffusion timestep (t=999). The model operates at 512×512 resolution, processes 4K images in 0.8 seconds, and achieves 62.5 FPS on an A100 GPU. Notably, the original text conditioning is entirely discarded and replaced with a single learnable embedding vector, since the LR image itself provides a far richer prior than sparse text prompts.

## Method

PocketSR compresses SD-Turbo through a two-stage pipeline. In stage one, the original SD VAE is replaced with LiteED (2M parameters, 97.5% reduction), which uses an ultra-compact convolutional encoder with Adaptive Skip Connections and a Dual-path Feature Injection mechanism that injects high-dimensional features ([N,320,64,64]) directly into the U-Net to bypass the 128× information bottleneck of the original encoder. In stage two, the U-Net is pruned via online annealing: each heavy module (residual blocks, self-attention, cross-attention, FFN) is paired in parallel with a lightweight counterpart (depthwise separable convolutions, linear attention, MLPs, quarter-width FFNs), and the original module's contribution is progressively annealed to zero while the lightweight module's contribution increases, enabling stable knowledge transfer. Multi-layer feature distillation across all U-Net layers further stabilizes this transition. Pruning is applied selectively at deeper network depths (III and IV) where high-level layout/style information contributes less to SR, while shallower layers are preserved to maintain fidelity.

## Results

PocketSR (146M params, 1 step) achieves the best LPIPS (0.2713 on RealSR, 0.2962 on DRealSR), DISTS (0.2094, 0.2139), and NIQE (5.067, 5.809) among all compared methods, while matching AdcSR on PSNR (25.47 on RealSR) and outperforming all multi-step models on fidelity. It processes a 512×512 image in 0.016 s (62.5 FPS) on an A100 GPU—roughly 7× faster than OSEDiff and 719× faster than StableSR—with only 225 G MACs, the lowest among all diffusion-based SR methods. On a 2025 smartphone, inference takes 140 ms, an 80% reduction over the unpruned backbone.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against multi-step methods (StableSR, DiffBIR, SeeSR, ResShift), PocketSR dominates on perceptual metrics (LPIPS, DISTS, NIQE) and matches or exceeds them on PSNR/SSIM, while being 2–700× faster in inference. Among single-step methods, PocketSR outperforms OSEDiff on LPIPS (0.2713 vs 0.2921), DISTS (0.2094 vs 0.2128), and NIQE (5.067 vs 5.648) on RealSR, and on DRealSR it edges out OSEDiff on LPIPS (0.2962 vs 0.2968) and DISTS (0.2139 vs 0.2165), though OSEDiff retains a slight advantage in SSIM (0.7835 vs 0.7675) and MUSIQ (64.65 vs 63.85). Versus AdcSR, the closest single-step competitor, PocketSR wins on LPIPS (0.2713 vs 0.2885), DISTS (0.2094 vs 0.2129), and NIQE (5.067 vs 5.350) on RealSR, ties on PSNR (25.47), and is 3× faster (0.016 s vs 0.03 s) with 3× fewer parameters (146M vs 456M); AdcSR leads only on MUSIQ (69.90 vs 67.07). Against SinSR and ResShift, PocketSR trades a small PSNR/SSIM deficit (≈0.8 dB / 0.009) for substantially better perceptual quality (LPIPS 0.2713 vs 0.3188/0.3460) and no-reference scores (NIQE 5.067 vs 6.287/7.264), which the authors attribute to more realistic texture generation.

## Relation to Prior Work

PocketSR sits at the intersection of two active research threads: diffusion-based real-world SR (StableSR, DiffBIR, OSEDiff, AdcSR) and efficient diffusion model compression (SnapFusion, MobileDiff, SnapGen). It builds directly on SD-Turbo as its backbone and on adversarial diffusion distillation for one-step generation, but departs from prior compression work by introducing a purpose-built ultra-light VAE (LiteED) and a progressive online annealing pruning strategy that transfers generative priors to lightweight modules rather than simply discarding or replacing them. The dual-path feature injection and adaptive skip connections are novel architectural additions not present in prior SR diffusion pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PocketSR demonstrates that diffusion-based SR can be made practical on smartphones (146M params, 0.8 s for 4K), a deployment target that prior single-step methods like OSEDiff and AdcSR could not reach. The finding that a 2M-parameter encoder outperforms the original 34M SD encoder in SR quality challenges the assumption that heavier VAEs are necessary for faithful conditioning, and the depth-dependent pruning analysis (deeper U-Net layers are expendable for SR) offers a general recipe for compressing diffusion models on restoration tasks. Open questions include whether the approach extends to severe degradations, video SR, or other restoration tasks, and whether further co-design with edge hardware (NPU, quantization) can close the remaining gap to real-time mobile inference.
