## Problem

LLMs misdirect attention toward distracting patterns in long-context reasoning, producing redundant or erroneous outputs. The paper attributes this to spurious correlations in training data that obscure true causal instruction-response relationships, and shows that simply removing these patterns yields over 20% accuracy gains on math benchmarks without any retraining. Existing knowledge distillation methods focus on output imitation but do not explicitly teach the student to attend to causally relevant context tokens.

## Input / Output

The method consumes text instructions (math word problems, code prompts, multi-hop QA queries) paired with gold responses, and produces chain-of-thought reasoning, executable code, or short-form answers depending on the task domain. Unusually, each training instance is processed in two forms: the original instruction and a counterfactual variant with one contiguous confounding span removed, so the student sees both the full context and the intervened context during distillation. For response-level pruning, previously generated tokens are also treated as prunable context, enabling multi-segment splits (2-segment or 3-segment) that extend the intervention into the generation process itself.

## Method

LeaF is a two-stage causal attention distillation framework. In Stage 1 (Confounding Token Detection), it computes per-token gradient sensitivities of both a high-capacity teacher and a student on instances where the student fails but the teacher succeeds, normalizes them via min-max scaling, and flags tokens whose normalized gradient difference falls below a threshold τ_confounder as confounders; counterfactual samples are then constructed by span pruning (removing one contiguous confounding span at a time to preserve sentence integrity). In Stage 2 (Causal Attention Distillation), the student is trained with a hybrid objective L = λ·D_KL(p_T(y|X)‖p_S(y|X)) + (1−λ)·D_KL(p_T(y|X\A)‖p_S(y|X\A)), simultaneously aligning the student with the teacher on both original and counterfactual inputs. This composite loss enforces that the student's attention distribution matches the teacher's focus on truly critical tokens while remaining invariant to the removed confounders, effectively blocking the spurious A→Y path in the structural causal model. The framework is applied via LoRA fine-tuning with full-parameter logits distillation, and supports both instruction-level and response-level pruning variants.

## Results

LeaF consistently outperforms standard knowledge distillation (KD) across all three task domains. On mathematical reasoning (GSM8K, MATH-500, OlympiadBench), LeaF achieves average accuracy gains of 2.41% over KD for LLaMA-3.2-1B/3B-Instruct and Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B (e.g., 53.21% vs. 50.29% avg. for LLaMA-3B). On code generation (HumanEval+, LeetCode, LiveCodeBench), LeaF yields an average improvement of 2.48% (e.g., 34.53% vs. 31.89% for LLaMA-3B). On multi-hop QA (HotpotQA, 2WikiMultiHopQA, Musique), LeaF improves by 3.24% over KD (42.27% vs. 39.03% avg. F1/EM/SubEM).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is standard knowledge distillation (KD w/o Mask) using the same teacher-student pairs and training corpus. LeaF wins on every benchmark across all three model families. For LLaMA-3.2-3B-Instruct, LeaF (Instr & Resp Mask) improves math average from 50.29% to 53.21% (+2.92%) and code average from 31.89% to 34.53% (+2.64%) over KD. For Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B, the gains are +2.16% on math (62.54% vs. 60.38%) and +2.14% on code (21.92% vs. 19.78%). On multi-hop QA (Table 2), LeaF (Instr Mask) achieves 42.27% average versus 39.03% for KD (+3.24%), with the largest single-benchmark gain on Musique (EM 27.00 vs. 20.50). The paper does not compare against other external distillation or pruning methods; the comparison is strictly LeaF vs. vanilla KD under identical training conditions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the chain-of-thought knowledge distillation paradigm (Hinton 2015; Shridhar et al. 2023) by introducing a causal inference lens: it frames distracting tokens in instructions as confounders in a Structural Causal Model and uses teacher-student gradient differences to identify them. Unlike prior token-importance methods (LLMLingua, RHO-1, TokenSkip, cDPO) that focus on output or reasoning tokens, LeaF targets instruction-level spurious correlations and enforces their removal via a hybrid loss combining standard KL distillation with counterfactual distillation on span-pruned samples. The key conceptual departure is treating attention misallocation as a causal confounding problem rather than a capacity or data-quality issue.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The preliminary finding that simply pruning identified distracting patterns yields over 20% accuracy gains on math (without any retraining) is a useful diagnostic for small-model failures, suggesting spurious correlations are a first-order error source. However, the full LeaF method delivers only 2–3% absolute gains over standard KD, and the approach depends on a large teacher model for gradient-based confounder detection, limiting its applicability in self-improvement or teacher-free settings. Open questions include whether the causal pruning framework generalizes to long-form generation, whether self-supervised confounder identification is feasible, and whether the modest gains scale with model size or task complexity.
