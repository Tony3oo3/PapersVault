## Problem

Deploying ML models in resource-constrained environments (e.g., mobile devices) faces a dual challenge: adapting to concept drift while strictly adhering to limited computational budgets. Existing drift-detection methods incur high overhead and lack provable resource guarantees, while unconstrained convergence analyses do not extend to budget-limited settings. No prior work provides a theoretically grounded update policy that simultaneously optimizes model performance and guarantees strict resource constraint satisfaction.

## Input / Output

The policy is causal and distribution-ignorant: at each time step it consumes only the scalar inference loss computed on a small holdout batch, the current model parameters, the time-varying update cost λ(t), and a tunable weight V — it never inspects the underlying data distributions. It outputs a binary update decision π(t) ∈ {0,1}, the (possibly updated) model parameters θ_t, and a virtual queue state Q(t) that accumulates resource debt and dynamically modulates the update threshold. The estimator Ĝ(H_t) is a flexible causal function of the full loss, gradient, and policy history, allowing it to be tailored per deployment.

## Method

The paper first derives a convergence bound (Theorem 5.1) on the time-averaged gradient norm under concept drift, where the bound depends on the cumulative drift-induced loss and the minimum update probability of the policy. This bound is then embedded into a Lyapunov drift-plus-penalty framework: a virtual queue Q(t) = max{0, Q(t)+λ(t)π(t)−λ̄} tracks resource debt, and the per-step objective combines the queue drift with the convergence-bound penalty weighted by V. Minimizing this drift-plus-penalty expression yields a threshold rule — update iff V·Ĝ(H_t) ≥ λ(t)Q(t) + ½[λ(t)²−2λ̄λ(t)] — where the left side is the estimated performance gain and the right side is the effective resource cost. Because the ideal threshold is non-causal (it depends on future data and the optimal next action), the practical algorithm replaces it with a causal estimator Ĝ built from historical losses, gradients, and past decisions. A stability analysis (Theorem 5.3) proves the time-averaged resource violation vanishes as T→∞, provided cumulative drift grows sublinearly.

## Results

Under a mean update rate constraint of 0.1, RCCDA achieves the highest average validation accuracy across all four drift schedules on both PACS and DigitsDG. On PACS, RCCDA scores 72.8% (Burst), 72.0% (Step), 67.0% (Wave), and 73.0% (Spikes), while on DigitsDG it reaches 77.6% (Burst), 74.6% (Step), 65.0% (Wave), and 74.3% (Spikes). The largest margins over baselines appear on the Burst and Spikes schedules, where rapid domain shifts demand timely adaptation. RCCDA also demonstrates that its update rate converges toward the 0.1 budget constraint over time, confirming adherence to the resource constraint in practice.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RCCDA outperforms all four baselines across every drift schedule and dataset. Against Uniform Random [58], RCCDA gains 8.8 points on PACS Burst (72.8 vs 64.0) and 6.2 points on DigitsDG Burst (77.6 vs 71.4), with the gap widening on Spikes (12.2 and 7.3 points respectively). Against Periodic [59], the advantage is most pronounced on Spikes, where Periodic drops to 55.3% on PACS and 68.1% on DigitsDG while RCCDA maintains 73.0% and 74.3%. Budget-Increase [60] suffers the largest degradation under high-drift schedules (59.1% on PACS Burst, 53.1% on PACS Spikes), indicating its trend-detection mechanism fails to trigger updates quickly enough. Budget-Threshold [10] is the closest competitor on Step schedules (71.7% vs 72.0% on PACS) but falls significantly behind on Wave and Spikes, where its fixed-threshold logic cannot adapt to varying drift magnitudes. Overall, RCCDA's advantage is largest precisely when drift is most abrupt, confirming that its Lyapunov-driven threshold dynamically allocates updates where they are most needed.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper applies the Lyapunov drift-plus-penalty framework from stochastic network optimization (Neely, 2010) to the problem of scheduling model updates under concept drift, a setting where prior work has relied on explicit drift detection (ADWIN, KS tests) or heuristic retraining schedules. It departs from domain generalization and continual learning literature by treating the update decision as a binary online control problem with a hard resource budget, rather than a learning objective. The convergence analysis extends standard SGD bounds by incorporating a drift-induced loss term bounded via Pinsker's inequality, and introduces the minimum update probability P_min^π as a policy-dependent parameter that governs the tightness of the bound.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper fills a practical gap for edge and mobile ML deployments where drift adaptation must occur within strict energy/compute budgets, providing the first provable guarantee that a lightweight policy will not exceed a resource constraint over time. A key open question is the characterization of estimation error from the practical estimator Ĝ (a PID-like function of loss history), which the theory assumes to be zero; tightening bounds under imperfect estimation is identified as future work. The framework's generality to different resource metrics (FLOPs, energy, wall-clock) is empirically confirmed, but the single-step gradient update assumption and the need for a holdout inference set limit immediate applicability to very tight memory settings.
