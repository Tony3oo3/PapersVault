## Problem

Autoregressive language models generate tokens sequentially, making it fundamentally difficult to enforce sequence-level constraints such as toxicity thresholds, molecular validity rules, or precise lexical requirements. Existing guardrails—RLHF, rejection sampling, and post-hoc filtering—offer only probabilistic compliance rather than provable constraint satisfaction. Discrete diffusion models expose a global view of the partial sequence at every denoising step, creating a natural opportunity for constraint enforcement that prior work has not exploited.

## Input / Output

CDD takes a text prompt (or molecular generation prompt) and a set of user-defined constraint thresholds (e.g., toxicity score τ, synthetic accessibility bound, letter count, or positional word requirement) as input, and produces a decoded sequence that provably satisfies all constraints. Constraints are evaluated on the arg-max decoded sequence rather than the underlying probability distribution, and the projection operates on the token-probability simplex at each denoising step. The method is entirely training-free: it modifies only the sampling procedure of a pre-trained discrete diffusion model without any retraining or fine-tuning.

## Method

CDD embeds a differentiable projection operator into the discrete diffusion reverse process: after each denoising step produces a token-probability distribution, the projection minimizes KL divergence between the projected and original distributions subject to the constraint that the arg-max of the projected distribution lies in the feasible set. To handle the non-differentiability of arg-max, a Gumbel-Softmax relaxation provides continuous approximations of discrete token selections, enabling gradient-based optimization. The projection is solved via an augmented Lagrangian dual program that iteratively updates the projected distribution through gradient descent while dynamically adjusting Lagrange multipliers and quadratic penalty coefficients to drive constraint violations to zero. A convergence theorem guarantees that the KL distance to the feasible set decreases geometrically at each step under standard regularity conditions. Because the projection acts only at sampling time, CDD requires no model retraining and preserves the generative diversity of the base diffusion model.

## Results

CDD achieves zero constraint violations (0%) across all three experimental domains—toxicity mitigation, molecular generation, and instruction following—while maintaining competitive perplexity and coherence. On toxicity mitigation (RealToxicityPrompts), CDD reduces violations from 13–35% (baselines) to 0% at all thresholds (τ=0.25, 0.50, 0.75) with PPL of 54.9–61.6, comparable to its MDLM base (46.7). On molecular generation, CDD produces up to 203.4% more novel molecules with complete synthetic-accessibility compliance and the highest QED scores (0.58–0.63 vs. 0.37–0.48 for baselines). On instruction following (COLLIE), CDD achieves 0% violations on both counting and lexical constraints versus 54–100% for all baselines, while attaining the best LLM-Judge coherence score (65.1) for lexical constraints.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CDD decisively outperforms all six baselines on constraint satisfaction, achieving 0% violations where every baseline retains 4–100% violations. Against GPT-2 (124M) and Llama 3.2 (1B), CDD trades a PPL increase (61.6 vs. 18.8 and 15.7 respectively) for a complete elimination of toxic outputs (0% vs. 33.2% and 34.9% at τ=0.25). PPLM reduces GPT-2's violations to 4–16% but at a severe PPL cost (46.4) and coherence drop (−55.8%), while FUDGE barely improves over unconstrained GPT-2 (31.5% vs. 33.2% at τ=0.25) even at λ=9. Compared to its own base model MDLM (110M), CDD increases PPL from 46.7 to 54.9–61.6 but eliminates all violations (32.1%→0%) and slightly improves LLM-Judge coherence. In molecular generation, CDD surpasses UDLM (92M) and its CFG/CBG-guided variants by generating 2–5× more valid-and-novel molecules (511 vs. 64–345) with 0% novelty violations, and achieves the highest QED (0.63) versus 0.37–0.48 for all baselines. On instruction following, CDD (169M) matches MDLM's PPL (17.9 vs. 16.9 for counting) while reducing violations from 54.5% to 0%, and for lexical constraints achieves the best coherence (65.1) with 0% violations versus 93.6–97.6% for GPT-2 and Llama 3.2.

## Relation to Prior Work

CDD builds directly on discrete diffusion language models (MDLM, UDLM) as its generative backbone, and on the continuous-modal projected diffusion framework of Christopher et al. (2024), which applies Euclidean projection after each denoising step. The key conceptual departure is replacing that Euclidean projection with a KL-divergence-based augmented Lagrangian projection over the probability simplex, making the approach native to discrete token distributions. It also positions itself against the long line of autoregressive constrained-decoding methods (PPLM, FUDGE, Neurologic A* decoding) by arguing that the non-autoregressive, globally-visible nature of diffusion sampling is what makes sequence-level hard constraints tractable without retraining.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CDD offers a training-free, provably-feasible mechanism for enforcing hard sequence-level constraints (toxicity thresholds, molecular properties, symbolic instructions) that autoregressive models cannot natively provide, which is practically important for safety-critical deployment and scientific design. The main open question is scalability: all experiments use 92M–169M parameter diffusion models, and the projection step adds per-step computational overhead that could erode the parallelization advantage of diffusion over autoregressive generation. Whether the augmented Lagrangian projection remains tractable and effective as constraint sets grow in complexity or as models scale to billions of parameters remains to be demonstrated.
