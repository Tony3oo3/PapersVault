## Problem

Previous theoretical continuity results for Transformers applied only to encoder-only architectures, leaving the decoder-only case (which underpins all modern LLMs) unaddressed. This paper asks whether decoder-only Transformers with compact positional encoding face fundamental limitations in distinguishing and learning closely related sequence patterns, and whether such limitations manifest at practical scales.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250599/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The theoretical framework takes finite token sequences over a finite alphabet Σ and produces a probability distribution over the next token, with the key constraint that the last token must remain unchanged between compared sequences. The relative Hamming distance d_H(α,β) = |{i : α_i ≠ β_i}|/n measures input similarity, and the output is bounded in ℓ_∞-norm. Empirically, inputs include 190-token binary sequences, ~1000–7500-token Python code and NLI prompts, and periodic binary patterns (0^{p-1}1)^r, with outputs ranging from next-token sensitivity counts (NTS), probability pairs (P(σ|α), P(σ|β)), to 505-step autoregressive pattern completions scored by success and certainty metrics.

## Method

The core result (Theorem 1) proves that for any compact decoder-only Transformer T and any ε > 0, there exists δ > 0 such that two sequences of equal length sharing the last token and differing in at most a δ-fraction of positions yield output distributions within ε in ℓ_∞-norm, uniformly over all sequence lengths. The proof overcomes the key difficulty of causal masking—where early tokens exert disproportionate influence on softmax coefficients—by introducing a 'global similarity' measure sim(x, x̂) and showing via induction over layers that both global similarity and last-position closeness are preserved through each attention layer, relying only on uniform continuity of the weight, value, and activation functions on compact sets. From continuity, Theorem 2 derives isolation: any eventually-learned infinite sequence α has a positive-radius ball (in relative Hamming distance) containing no other sequence that differs from α in infinitely many positions and is also eventually learnable. The empirical validation tests these predictions on Llama-2-7b, Meta-Llama-3-8B-Instruct, phi-4, gpt-2, gemma-3, and reasoning models (o3-mini, o4-mini) across four tasks, confirming that representational collapse and the inability to learn all periodic sequences occur at practical sequence lengths.

## Results

The paper proves two theorems (Continuity and Isolation) for all decoder-only Transformers with compact positional encoding, then validates them empirically. In the zero-sequence continuity test, at γ=0.01 (1 perturbed symbol out of 190), all tested models show zero next-token sensitivity (NTS=0), confirming representational collapse; at γ=0.05 (9 symbols), only phi-4 deviates. In the SYNTAXVERIFICATION task, Meta-Llama-3-8B-Instruct is sensitive to single-token syntax errors in only 21% of 100 examples, while phi-4 (code-trained) fails on all cases. For periodic pattern generation (Llama-2-7b-hf), a critical period threshold exists beyond which the model cannot complete the pattern, and this threshold increases with the number of repetitions shown (r=1,4,10). Reasoning models (o3-mini, o4-mini) improve but still exhibit ≥20% error rates, indicating CoT does not fully escape continuity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not propose a new method to benchmark against baselines; instead, it establishes theoretical impossibility results and validates them across a diverse set of existing decoder-only CPE Transformers. The key comparative finding is that all tested models (gpt-2, gpt-2-xl, Llama-2-7b, Meta-Llama-3-8B-Instruct, phi-4, gemma-3-4b/12b, o3-mini, o4-mini) exhibit the predicted continuity and isolation phenomena, though severity varies: larger models within the same family (e.g., gemma-3-12b vs 4b) show slightly more sensitivity, instruct-tuned models outperform base models on SYNTAXVERIFICATION (base models >80% error vs. instruct >15%), and code-specialized phi-4 paradoxically shows the worst continuity behavior (zero sensitivity). The only notable exceptions are gemma-2b and gemma-2-2b, which do not exhibit a clear critical period in periodic generation, suggesting architectural or training differences may partially mitigate the limitation. Reasoning models with chain-of-thought (o3-mini, o4-mini) improve task performance but still show ≥20% error rates, confirming that bounded CoT does not escape the theoretical continuity constraint.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Hahn's (2020) continuity result from encoder-only to decoder-only Transformers, overcoming the key technical difficulty that causal masking breaks token symmetry in softmax and gives earlier tokens disproportionate influence. It generalizes Barbero et al.'s (2024) representational collapse observation—which required specific positional encoding assumptions and applied only to a special case of repeated last tokens—to all compact decoder-only Transformers with no Lipschitz requirement. The isolation theorem is a new conceptual contribution that follows from continuity but reframes the limitation as a topological constraint on the set of simultaneously learnable sequences, connecting to Stambler et al.'s (2025) 'overwhelming strings' concept as an immediate corollary.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result establishes that no single decoder-only Transformer with compact positional encoding (encompassing RoPE, sinusoidal, and most standard schemes used in modern LLMs) can eventually learn all periodic sequences—a strikingly simple and concrete impossibility. The 'doubts or dilemmas' framing provides a useful conceptual lens: models must either be uncertain in their predictions or make irreducible choices about which patterns to learn. Open questions include whether unbounded chain-of-thought can escape these limitations (the paper shows O(1) CoT cannot), whether non-compact positional encodings (e.g., log-n scaling) genuinely resolve the issue, and whether augmenting Transformers with state-space components (as in Jamba) can overcome representational collapse, given that RNNs can recognize any regular language.
