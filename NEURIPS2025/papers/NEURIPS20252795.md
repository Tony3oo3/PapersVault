## Problem

Contextual pricing with corrupted (adversarial) feedback had only a suboptimal regret bound of O(d³C log²T) from Krishnamurthy et al. (2020), which modified the inner workings of the uncorrupted algorithm. This paper closes the gap by achieving O(Cd log log T) regret via a black-box reduction, and further proves a lower bound showing that the natural O(C + d log log T) target is unattainable, establishing that robustifying contextual pricing is fundamentally harder than robustifying other contextual search problems.

## Input / Output

In each round the learner receives a feature vector x_t in the unit ball B_d and must post a scalar price p_t ∈ [0,1], observing only binary sale/no-sale feedback σ_t. The algorithm is parameterized by either the exact corruption count C (known setting) or an upper bound U ≥ C (unknown setting), and the regret is measured as total loss Σ ℓ_t over all T rounds including corrupted ones. The uncorrupted base algorithm is consumed purely as a black-box through two subroutines—Query(x_t) returning a price and Update(x_t, p_t, σ_t) modifying internal state—so no knowledge of its internals is required.

## Method

The core mechanism is a reduction that instantiates C+1 parallel copies of the uncorrupted Liu et al. (2021) algorithm, posts the maximum price among all active copies, and updates only one copy per round: on a no-sale the copy that proposed the maximum is updated (and eliminated if it exceeds R(T) no-sales), while on a sale a uniformly random active copy is updated. The key insight is a pricing-loss monotonicity property: if the maximum price yields a sale, the loss is at most the loss of every individual copy, allowing the total sale-loss to be charged to a (C+1)-slowed-down version of the single corruption-free copy. For unknown corruption, the multi-layer approach of Lykouris et al. (2018) is adapted with O(log U) copies updated at exponentially decreasing probabilities 1/2^i, ensuring a copy with update probability ~δ/C survives all corruptions with high probability. The lower bound is established by reducing online product design (Emamjomeh-Zadeh et al., 2021) to corrupted contextual pricing, showing that any O(C + log log T) algorithm would violate the known Ω(n²/log n) lower bound for n = C+1 items.

## Results

For the known-corruption setting, the paper achieves a regret bound of O((C+1)·d log log T), improving over the previous O(Cd³ log² T) bound of Krishnamurthy et al. (2020). For the unknown-corruption setting with upper bound U, the regret is O((C + log U)·d log log T), improving over O((C + log U)d³ log² T). A lower bound (Theorem 5.1) rules out the seemingly natural O(C + d log log T) bound, showing that when C = Θ(log log T), any deterministic algorithm must incur Ω(C²/log C) loss. For one-sided corruption in the non-contextual case, an O(C + log T) regret algorithm is provided (Theorem 6.1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is against Krishnamurthy et al. (2020), the only prior work on corrupted contextual pricing. This paper improves the dependence on dimension from d³ to d and on the horizon from log² T to log log T in both the known- and unknown-corruption settings, yielding a multiplicative improvement of roughly d² log² T / log log T. The improvement is achieved through a black-box reduction that calls the uncorrupted algorithm of Liu et al. (2021) as a subroutine, rather than modifying its internal structure as in Krishnamurthy et al. The lower bound (Theorem 5.1, via reduction to the online product design lower bound of Emamjomeh-Zadeh et al. 2021) shows that the remaining multiplicative dependence on C (rather than additive) is inherent for deterministic algorithms, distinguishing contextual pricing from contextual search with ε-ball losses where an additive C overhead is achievable (Paes Leme et al. 2022). The one-sided corruption result (O(C + log T)) is a new contribution with no prior comparison.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the uncorrupted contextual pricing algorithm of Liu et al. (2021), which achieves the optimal O(d log log T) regret, by treating it as a black-box and running C+1 parallel copies that post the maximum price. It departs from the prior corrupted-feedback approach of Krishnamurthy et al. (2020), which modified the inner workings of the uncorrupted algorithm and achieved only O(Cd³ log² T), by instead using a clean reduction that exploits the pricing loss's monotonicity property. The unknown-corruption setting borrows the multi-layer exponential-update strategy from Lykouris et al. (2018), while the lower bound is established via a reduction to the online product design problem of Emamjomeh-Zadeh et al. (2021), and the one-sided corruption algorithm repurposes the Kirkpatrick–Gao (1990) extrema-finding subroutine.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The improvement from O(Cd³ log² T) to O(Cd log log T) is substantial, removing both a quadratic dimension factor and a logarithmic gap, and the black-box reduction makes the algorithm conceptually simpler and easier to instantiate with future improvements to the uncorrupted base. The lower bound ruling out O(C + d log log T) is a meaningful negative result: it shows that robustifying contextual pricing is fundamentally harder than robustifying related contextual search problems (e.g., ε-ball loss) where an additive C overhead is achievable, revealing an intrinsic difficulty in the pricing loss structure. Open questions include whether O(C + log T) regret is possible for two-sided corruption in the non-contextual case, and whether O(C + d log T) can be achieved for a cautious buyer in the full contextual setting.
