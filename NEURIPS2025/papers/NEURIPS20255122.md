## Problem

Existing chemical benchmarks for LLMs reduce evaluation to factual recall and discrete QA, failing to assess whether models can execute the step-by-step, modular reasoning (substructure addition, deletion, substitution) that real-world chemical tasks like molecular optimization and reaction prediction actually demand. This gap obscures whether strong performance stems from structured chemical reasoning or mere memorized knowledge, and prevents targeted improvement of LLMs for AI-driven scientific discovery.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255122/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Inputs are SMILES strings paired with natural-language instructions (for editing), a source molecule plus a target property name (for optimization), or reactant/reagent SMILES pairs (for reaction prediction). Outputs range from scalar counts (functional groups, rings) and binary equivalence decisions to full SMILES strings for edited molecules, optimized structures, predicted products/by-products, and reaction conditions. Evaluation is multi-metric: MAE for counting, Tanimoto similarity for Murcko scaffolds, Pass@1 for editing validity, mean property improvement (Δ) and success rate (SR%) for optimization, and Top-1 accuracy combined with Morgan/MACCS/RDKit fingerprint similarity for reaction tasks.

## Method

ChemCoTBench formalizes chemical problem-solving as sequences of verifiable modular chemical operations (addition, deletion, substitution) on SMILES structures, organizing 1,495 benchmark samples into 22 subtasks across four tiers: molecule understanding, molecule editing, molecule optimization, and reaction prediction. Chain-of-thought annotations are distilled from state-of-the-art reasoning models (Gemini-2.5-pro, DeepSeek-R1, Claude-3.7-sonnet-thinking) into structured step-wise operation sequences, then validated through a dual pipeline of GPT-4o-based LLM verification and manual review by 13 chemistry PhD candidates. The accompanying ChemCoTDataset (22,000 instances) provides expert-annotated CoT labels for supervised fine-tuning. The paper further demonstrates that direct CoT distillation from general-domain reasoning models fails to transfer to chemistry, while SFT on ChemCoTDataset yields stable, substantial gains across Qwen-2.5 model scales (1.5B–32B), establishing domain-specific CoT data as the critical missing ingredient for chemical reasoning.

## Results

On ChemCoTBench's 22 chemical reasoning tasks, Gemini-2.5-pro-think achieves the best foundational performance (FG MAE 0.11, 100% on molecule-editing Add, 87.5% ring-system accuracy), while Claude-3.7-sonnet-think leads on SMILES equivalence (84%) and substitution editing (83.4%). On molecule optimization, Gemini-2.5-pro-think attains the highest QED success rate (84%) and DRD2 binding improvement (Δ=0.36, SR=74%), and on reaction prediction, Claude-3.7-sonnet-think achieves the top forward major-product Top-1 accuracy (0.73) and FTS (0.87). Critically, open-source reasoning models (Qwen3-think, Llama-Nemo-think) show no significant improvement over their non-reasoning counterparts on chemical tasks, and SFT on the 22K ChemCoTDataset yields stable gains across Qwen-2.5 scales (1.5B–32B) where CoT distillation from DeepSeek-R1 fails.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among commercial reasoning LLMs, Gemini-2.5-pro-think and Claude-3.7-sonnet-think dominate foundational tasks, with Gemini leading on FG counting (MAE 0.11 vs 0.21), Murcko scaffold extraction (0.51 vs 0.40), and Add editing (100% vs 85%), while Claude edges ahead on SMILES equivalence (84% vs 82%) and Sub editing (83.4% vs 81.7%). On applied molecule optimization, DeepSeek-R1 outperforms its non-reasoning counterpart DeepSeek-V3 by >30% in success rate (e.g., Solubility SR 80% vs 92% but with higher Δ improvement 0.80 vs 0.57), and o3-mini gains >20% over GPT-4o on QED (SR 86% vs 70%). A key negative finding is that open-source hybrid-thinking models (Qwen3-235B-think, Llama-Nemo-49B-think) show no meaningful improvement over their non-thinking versions on chemical tasks, unlike their strong performance in math/code. Domain-specific models (BioMedGPT-7B, BioMistral-7B) perform substantially worse than general LLMs across nearly all subtasks (e.g., BioMistral scores 0% on Add/Delete/Sub editing). Finally, SFT on ChemCoTDataset consistently outperforms CoT template prompting across all Qwen-2.5 scales (1.5B–32B), while DeepSeek-R1 distillation yields no chemical reasoning gains and an inverse correlation with math/code performance is observed (S1.1-distill beats R1-distill on MATH500 but loses on chemical subtasks).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Chain-of-Thought reasoning paradigm (Wei et al., Kahneman's System-2 framing) from mathematics and coding into chemistry by formalizing molecular transformations as modular operations (addition, deletion, substitution) on SMILES strings, analogous to arithmetic. It departs from prior chemical benchmarks (ChemLLM, MolPuzzle, MoleculeQA, SciBench) that reduce evaluation to factual recall or single-step prediction, instead requiring verifiable multi-step reasoning chains. For reaction mechanism annotation, it builds directly on Joung et al.'s (2024) large-scale mechanistic dataset pipeline, and for distillation experiments it follows the DeepSeek-R1 recipe while demonstrating its failure in the chemical domain.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 22K-instance ChemCoTDataset with expert-validated CoT annotations is the first large-scale chemical reasoning training corpus, and the finding that R1-style CoT distillation fails to transfer to chemistry (while SFT on domain-specific data succeeds) challenges the assumption that general reasoning capabilities are domain-agnostic. Open questions include whether chemical-aware reward mechanisms or policy optimization can close the gap for open-source models, and whether the benchmark scales to broader biochemical domains (proteins, polymers). The benchmark's relatively small size (1,495 evaluation samples) and single-domain focus limit generalizability claims.
