## Problem

Despite the empirical success of self-supervised contrastive learning (CL), it remains theoretically unclear why a label-agnostic loss produces representations that cluster by semantic class and support few-shot transfer. The paper addresses this gap by showing that standard CL objectives implicitly approximate a supervised variant that excludes same-class contrasts, and by characterizing the geometric structure of the resulting representations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251392/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

During pre-training, the method consumes only unlabeled RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR, 64×64 for Tiny-ImageNet, 224×224 for mini-ImageNet/ImageNet) along with K augmented views per sample, and produces d-dimensional embedding vectors. For downstream evaluation, a small m-shot labeled support set per class is used to train a linear probe or nearest-class-center classifier, yielding class labels. The theoretical analysis treats the embedding function as an unconstrained map from the input space to R^d, making the results architecture-independent.

## Method

The core contribution is a duality between decoupled contrastive learning (DCL) and a newly defined negatives-only supervised contrastive loss (NSCL) that removes same-class pairs from the denominator. Theorem 1 proves the gap between the two losses is bounded by log(1 + n_max·e²/(N−n_max)), which vanishes as the number of classes grows, and is both label-agnostic and architecture-independent. Theorem 2 characterizes the global minimizers of NSCL under the unconstrained features model, showing they exhibit augmentation collapse, within-class collapse, and a simplex equiangular tight frame (ETF) structure for class centers. Proposition 1 introduces a refined few-shot error bound that depends primarily on directional CDNV (variance projected onto the line between class centers) rather than full CDNV, explaining why CL-trained models achieve low probing error even when overall within-class dispersion is moderate. Empirically, the authors validate all theoretical predictions using SimCLR and MoCo with ResNet-50 and ViT-Base across five datasets, confirming the loss gap decays as O(1/C), the two losses are highly correlated, and the few-shot bound is tight in practice.

## Results

On 100-shot linear probing (LP) and nearest-class-center classification (NCCC), DCL-trained models achieve 86.3% and 85.3% on CIFAR-10, 61.7% and 57.3% on CIFAR-100, and 72.9% and 69.0% on mini-ImageNet, respectively. The gap between the DCL and NSCL losses is empirically bounded by log(1+e²/(C−1)), decaying as O(1/C) with the number of classes, and the two losses remain highly correlated throughout training. DCL training reduces directional CDNV by roughly an order of magnitude while only modestly reducing overall CDNV, explaining why linear probing remains effective despite the absence of explicit class supervision. The proposed few-shot error bound (Cor. 1) is tight in practice, predicting test-time errors below 0.26 at m=10⁶ on CIFAR-10.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's central empirical comparison is between DCL (self-supervised, label-agnostic) and NSCL (supervised, excluding same-class negatives). NSCL consistently outperforms DCL across all three datasets and both metrics: by ~10 points on CIFAR-10 (95.7 vs 85.3 NCCC), ~13 points on CIFAR-100 (70.8 vs 57.3 NCCC), and ~11 points on mini-ImageNet (79.8 vs 69.0 NCCC). However, the key finding is that DCL implicitly approximates NSCL: the loss gap shrinks as the number of classes grows, and the NSCL loss of a DCL-trained model is close to that of a directly NSCL-trained model (Fig. 3 bottom). Representation alignment (CKA 0.81–0.91, RSA 0.83–0.91) confirms that the two objectives produce nearly identical embedding geometries. DCL also exhibits strong augmentation collapse (cosine similarity ~0.90 for same-sample pairs vs <0.15 for different-sample pairs), validating the theoretical prediction from Theorem 2.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Decoupled Contrastive Learning (DCL) [25] as its base algorithm and on the unconstrained features model [78, 79] for characterizing loss minimizers, while refining the CDNV-based few-shot error bounds of Galanti et al. [46, 81, 82] by introducing a directional variant. It departs from prior CL theory (alignment/uniformity [49, 50, 51], cluster recovery [42, 43, 44, 36, 45]) by providing a label-agnostic, architecture-independent bound connecting the self-supervised loss to a supervised contrastive variant, without requiring assumptions on data distribution, augmentations, or model capacity. The neural collapse characterization of NSCL minimizers [29, 30] is extended to a loss that, unlike cross-entropy or MSE, is defined without explicit label supervision during training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean theoretical explanation for why label-agnostic contrastive learning produces class-aware representations: the probability of same-class negatives vanishes as the number of classes grows, making DCL approximately equivalent to a supervised objective. The directional CDNV refinement is practically useful, explaining why CL-trained models achieve low few-shot error even when full within-class variance remains moderate. Key limitations include large constants in the error bound that scale linearly with the number of downstream classes, and the acknowledged gap between loss-value proximity and representation-geometry proximity. Extending the framework to multimodal models (CLIP) and LLMs, and tightening the bound's dependence on C', are natural next steps.
