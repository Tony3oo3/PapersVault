## Problem

Existing RL-based approaches for regulatory DNA design optimize fitness in a single target cell type without mechanisms to suppress or constrain activity in off-target cell types, making it impossible to generate sequences with precise, controllable cell-type specificity. Integrating explicit multi-cell-type constraints into RL frameworks for genomic LMs—where rewards are sparse, sequence-level, and biologically complex—remains unexplored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250472/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained HyenaDNA autoregressive LM as the policy backbone, per-cell-type Enformer reward models (trained on MPRA fitness data), user-specified constraint thresholds δ for each off-target cell type, and TFBS reference frequency vectors computed from real sequences via FIMO scanning. It outputs 200 bp (enhancers) or 250 bp (promoters) nucleotide sequences over {A,C,G,T} that achieve high predicted fitness in the target cell type while keeping off-target fitness below the specified thresholds, evaluated across six human cell lines (HepG2, K562, SK-N-SH, JURKAT, K562*, THP1).

## Method

Ctrl-DNA formulates CRE design as a constrained MDP and fine-tunes a pre-trained HyenaDNA policy via a Lagrangian primal-dual scheme. The key architectural choice is avoiding value networks entirely: advantages are computed by batch-normalizing per-cell-type rewards (inspired by GRPO), then combined into a single Lagrangian advantage as a weighted sum where the target reward coefficient is min(1, m − Σλᵢ) and each off-target reward is weighted by its adaptive multiplier λᵢ. Policy updates use a PPO-style clipped surrogate objective with KL regularization against the reference policy, while λᵢ are updated via gradient ascent on a batch-level constraint violation loss. An additional TFBS frequency correlation reward (Pearson correlation between generated and real motif frequency vectors) is incorporated as a capped constraint (λ_TFBS ≤ λ_max) to regularize sequences toward biologically plausible motif distributions without over-constraining exploration.

## Results

On the Human Enhancer dataset (δ=0.5), Ctrl-DNA achieves ΔR of 0.49 (HepG2), 0.54 (K562), and 0.37 (SK-N-SH), substantially outperforming the best baselines (PEX at 0.13–0.17, RegLM at 0.14–0.19). On the more challenging Human Promoter dataset, Ctrl-DNA reaches ΔR of 0.25 (JURKAT), 0.12 (K562), and 0.56 (THP1), again leading all methods. For motif correlation, Ctrl-DNA achieves 0.43–0.75 across cell types, with the strongest gains in promoter design (e.g., 0.75 for K562*, 0.69 for JURKAT). Ctrl-DNA also maintains competitive sequence diversity (Shannon entropy ~1.8) while enforcing cell-type-specific constraints.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Ctrl-DNA dominates all 11 baselines on ΔR across all six cell types. The closest competitors are PEX (ΔR up to 0.29 for THP1) and RegLM (ΔR up to 0.19 for K562 enhancer), but both fail to suppress off-target fitness, yielding ΔR roughly 2–5× lower than Ctrl-DNA. Among RL-based methods, TACO (ΔR −0.10 to 0.24) and PPO-Lagrangian (ΔR −0.22 to 0.24) struggle to balance target optimization with constraint satisfaction, likely due to the difficulty of training value networks under sparse sequence-level rewards. Diffusion-based DRAKES and model-based CbAS (Table 12) achieve near-zero or negative ΔR (e.g., −0.07 and −0.02 for HepG2), confirming that single-objective optimization cannot enforce multi-cell-type constraints. For motif correlation, Ctrl-DNA leads in 5 of 6 cell types; the sole exception is THP1 promoter design where PEX scores 0.84, though the authors attribute this to a skewed THP1 fitness distribution and show Ctrl-DNA recovers the lead (0.60 vs. 0.04 for PEX) under a stricter 90th-percentile reference set.

## Relation to Prior Work

Ctrl-DNA sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) RL-based DNA sequence design (TACO, DyNA-PPO, GFlowNets), which optimizes single-objective fitness but lacks mechanisms to suppress off-target activity; (2) constrained RL via Lagrangian relaxation (CPO, PPO-Lagrangian, IPO), which typically requires trained value networks for advantage estimation; and (3) GRPO-style value-free policy optimization (DeepSeek-R1, DeepSeekMath), which computes advantages from batch-normalized rewards without a critic. The paper's key departure is combining the Lagrangian primal-dual framework with GRPO's batch-level advantage computation, eliminating value networks entirely while enforcing multi-cell-type constraints — a combination not previously applied to genomic sequence generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is one of the first frameworks to make cell-type-specificity a *controllable* parameter (via explicit constraint thresholds δ) rather than an emergent property, which is directly relevant to gene therapy and synthetic biology where off-target expression is a safety concern. The elimination of value networks is practically significant given the sparse, sequence-level reward structure of CRE design, where per-step value estimation is ill-posed. Key limitations include: constraint satisfaction is bounded by data distribution (e.g., THP1's right-skewed fitness makes δ=0.4 infeasible for all methods), Lagrange multiplier tuning remains empirical, and the framework is restricted to autoregressive LMs. Open directions include extension to diffusion-based DNA models, adaptive multiplier control (e.g., PID), and incorporation of additional biological constraints beyond TFBS frequency correlation.
