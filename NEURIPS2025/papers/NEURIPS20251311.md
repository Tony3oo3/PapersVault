## Problem

Existing continual model merging (CMM) methods, such as OPCM, mitigate forgetting of previously merged tasks but overlook the reverse interference from old tasks on newly merged ones, leaving the stability–plasticity trade-off unresolved. No prior work has formally defined these two properties in the CMM setting from the perspective of orthogonal projection, and the unavailability of original task data renders standard projection-based continual learning techniques inapplicable.

## Input / Output

At each merging step the method consumes only the weight matrices of three models (pre-trained, previously merged, and newly arrived) at the individual linear-layer level, requiring at most three linear layers in GPU memory simultaneously and no access to any original training data or previously seen expert models. The output is the updated merged model, optimized layer-by-layer in 1–3 seconds per linear layer, producing a single unified parameter set that replaces all prior models.

## Method

The core insight is that task vectors (parameter differences from the pre-trained model) span a subspace that approximates the data subspace, because gradients lie within the data space and task vectors are weighted sums of historical gradients. DOP constructs approximate data subspaces via truncated SVD of the old and new task vectors, then minimizes the weighted Frobenius-norm projections of the delta parameters (merged minus old, merged minus new) onto these subspaces to enforce stability and plasticity respectively. To resolve the inherent conflict between the two objectives, the problem is cast as a multi-objective optimization solved by MGDA, which at each step computes a Pareto-optimal convex combination of the two gradients via a closed-form quadratic program. An exponential moving average (EMA) strategy smooths the MGDA mixing coefficients across iterations, preventing abrupt oscillations and allowing a user-specified initial preference between stability and plasticity.

## Results

On vision tasks (ViT-B/32, 8 tasks), DOP achieves 78.3% average accuracy, outperforming the strongest baseline OPCM (75.5%) by 2.8 points and SWA (66.3%) by 12.0 points. Scaling to 20 tasks on ViT-L/14, DOP reaches 80.7% vs. OPCM's 76.0%, a 4.7-point gap that widens with task count. On language tasks (Flan-T5-Base, 8 GLUE tasks), DOP attains 83.6% average accuracy with BWT of −1.3, approaching the single fine-tuned expert upper bound of 84.6% while surpassing OPCM (80.6%) by 3.0 points. DOP also demonstrates superior stability: after merging all 8 tasks, it retains 93.8% on the first task (EuroSAT) versus 88.8% for OPCM and 75.7% for SWA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DOP consistently outperforms all four baselines across the vast majority of settings. Against SWA (simple weight averaging), DOP gains 12.0 points on ViT-B/32 (8 tasks) and 9.6 points on ViT-L/14 (20 tasks), reflecting SWA's severe interference in sequential merging. C. Task Arithmetic and C. Ties-Merging, designed for offline simultaneous merging, degrade substantially in the CMM setting (e.g., Ties-Merging drops to 49.0% on ViT-B/32 8 tasks with high variance), while DOP maintains 78.3%. The closest competitor is OPCM, the only other method purpose-built for CMM; DOP surpasses it by 2.8 points (ViT-B/32, 8 tasks) and 4.7 points (ViT-L/14, 20 tasks), with the gap widening as task count increases. The sole exception is ViT-B/16 with 14 tasks, where OPCM edges out DOP by 0.9 points (77.1 vs. 76.2). On language tasks, DOP leads OPCM by 3.0 points in average accuracy (83.6 vs. 80.6) and shows less forgetting (BWT −1.3 vs. −2.1), approaching the single fine-tuned expert upper bound of 84.6%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the model merging literature—which has focused on offline, simultaneous merging of all expert models—to the sequential Continual Model Merging (CMM) setting, where models arrive one at a time. It draws on two distinct lineages: (1) orthogonal projection methods from continual learning (GPM, OWM, TRGP), which require access to old-task data and thus cannot be applied directly, and (2) the task vector formulation from Task Arithmetic, which the authors repurpose as a data-free proxy for the input subspace. The closest prior CMM work, OPCM, addresses only stability (forgetting of old tasks) via parameter decomposition; this paper departs by formulating both stability and plasticity as dual objectives and solving them jointly via MGDA, a multi-objective optimization algorithm borrowed from the Pareto-optimization literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first formal orthogonal-projection definition of stability and plasticity for CMM and a data-free method that achieves them simultaneously, which is practically important because real-world model merging (e.g., on HuggingFace) rarely has access to all models or their training data at once. The efficiency claim—optimizing each linear layer in 1–3 seconds with only three layers in memory—makes the approach scalable. However, validation is limited to ViT and Flan-T5-Base at moderate scale; open questions include whether the task-vector-as-data-subspace approximation holds for deeper or multimodal architectures, whether a small replay buffer could further improve performance, and how the method behaves when task vectors from old and new tasks are highly overlapping.
