## Problem

Existing multi-temporal remote sensing vision-language datasets are predominantly limited to bi-temporal image pairs, and current MLLMs lack both long-term temporal reasoning beyond two time points and pixel-precise quantitative analysis of urban changes. This gap prevents systematic evaluation of models on the full spectrum of urban dynamics, from fine-grained change detection to comprehensive multi-decade temporal narratives.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251114/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system consumes sequences of 5–10 co-registered 1024×1024-pixel RGB patches (1.0 m GSD) from NAIP imagery spanning 2005–2023, interleaved with a text instruction prefixed by a task token ([QA] or [SE]). For QA and captioning tasks, the output is a free-form textual response (multiple-choice answer, quantitative report, or dense temporal narrative); for referring change detection, the output is a binary pixel-level segmentation mask localizing the specified change region across the temporal pair.

## Method

DVLChat builds on Qwen2.5-VL 7B with a LISA-style architecture but introduces two key modifications to resolve LISA's optimization conflict between open-ended generation and pixel-level segmentation. First, it employs dual LoRA adapters routed by user-supplied prefix tokens: [QA] activates a VQA LoRA for textual generation, while [SE] activates a segmentation LoRA that decodes a <SEG> token embedding through SAM's frozen vision backbone and unfrozen decoder to produce change masks. Second, multi-temporal image features from all frames in a scene are interleaved before the LLM decoder, enabling the model to reason over extended temporal sequences rather than a single bi-temporal pair. The model is instruction-tuned on DVL-Instruct (63,771 instruction–ground-truth pairs over 11,402 multi-temporal scenes), which covers all six task categories from the DVL-Bench taxonomy, allowing a single 7B model to perform both quantitative urban QA and referring change detection without task interference.

## Results

On DVL-Bench, the best-performing model o4-mini achieves only 34.1% overall QA accuracy, while the proposed DVLChat 7B reaches 33.3%, surpassing all open-source baselines including Qwen2.5-VL 32B (31.4%) and InternVL3 14B (27.2%). Performance degrades sharply on quantitative tasks: CSE multi-choice accuracy peaks at just 13.6% (Qwen2.5-VL 7B) and Change Rate Precision stays below 1.21 across all models. For captioning, o4-mini leads with 4.58/5 on Regional Change Captioning, while DVLChat 7B achieves 3.98/5, approaching commercial model performance with only 7B parameters. On referring change detection, DVLChat attains 29.06% IoU, within 3.35% of the specialist ChangeMamba (32.41%) and outperforming PSALM (26.93%) and LISA (13.85%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DVLChat 7B (33.3% AVG) outperforms all open-source MLLMs including Qwen2.5-VL 32B (31.4%), InternVL3 14B (27.2%), and LLaVA-OneVision 72B (25.0%), and significantly surpasses domain-specific models TEOChat (17.2%) and EarthDial (30.3%). Against commercial models, DVLChat trails o4-mini (34.1%) by 0.8% but edges out GPT-4.1 (32.5%) and GPT-4o (29.7%), while showing the largest gap on CSE multi-choice (18.6% vs. 12.4% for o4-mini) and EA (30.6% vs. 25.3%). On captioning (Table 3), DVLChat 7B (RCC 3.98, DTC 3.40) approaches o4-mini (4.58, 4.14) but remains behind by ~0.6 points, while decisively outperforming all open-source baselines (best: InternVL3 14B at 3.96 RCC). For referring change detection, DVLChat (29.06% IoU) loses to the specialist ChangeMamba (32.41%) but beats both MLLM-based segmenters PSALM (26.93%) and LISA (13.85%). Notably, scaling within model families is non-monotonic for analytical tasks (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL drops from 31.4% at 32B to 29.7% at 72B), whereas DVLChat's domain-specific training data yields consistent gains over its 7B base model across all tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the multi-temporal remote sensing vision-language line (CDVQA, TEOChatlas, EarthDial, GeoLLaVA) by moving decisively beyond the bi-temporal paradigm that dominates existing datasets (average 2 frames) to sequences of 5–10 frames spanning 2005–2023. Methodologically, DVLChat builds on LISA's reasoning-segmentation architecture and SAM's decoder but introduces dual LoRA modules to decouple VQA and pixel-level change detection, and interleaves multi-temporal image features before decoding. The task taxonomy (six levels from pixel-level BCA to scene-level DTC) departs from prior RS benchmarks that typically offer a single task type, drawing instead on the hierarchical evaluation philosophy of video understanding benchmarks (ET-Bench, VideoRefer Suite).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DVL-Suite is the first large-scale benchmark specifically targeting long-term (beyond bi-temporal) urban dynamics, filling a clear gap that limits current MLLM deployment in sustainable-city monitoring. The finding that non-monotonic scaling occurs for quantitative/analytical tasks (32B outperforming 72B in Qwen2.5-VL; 14B matching 78B in InternVL3) while captioning tasks scale monotonically challenges the default assumption that parameter growth uniformly improves MLLM capabilities. Open questions include whether pixel-level segmentation outputs can be fed back to improve numerical quantification, whether near-infrared spectral bands can be leveraged once MLLMs gain multi-spectral perception, and how to close the persistent gap between the best open-source model (DVLChat 7B at 33.3%) and commercial models (o4-mini at 34.1% overall, but 4.58/5 on captioning).
