## Problem

Current OOD fine-tuning strategies for anomaly segmentation rely on global-level pixel-wise objectives that weight each anomaly proportionally to its area, causing small OOD instances to contribute negligibly to the total loss and be systematically neglected. The paper provides a theoretical decomposition (Lemma 1, Theorem 1) showing that when a single large anomaly dominates the OOD region, the loss is asymptotically determined by that dominant instance, leaving small anomalies under-optimized.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254023/cropped_page1_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on mixed-content RGB images (generated via AnomalyMix cut-and-paste from COCO into Cityscapes) that contain both in-distribution and out-of-distribution regions. The segmentation backbone (DeepLabv3+, Mask2Former, etc.) produces a pixel-wise anomaly score map in [0,1]^{H×W}, with the specific scoring mechanism inherited from the base strategy (softmax entropy for EM, free energy for PEBAL, logit-based for M2A). During fine-tuning, the method additionally consumes latent features from stages 2–4 of the backbone (upsampled to 1/4 resolution) to compute the feature separation objective, but this adds no cost at inference.

## Method

LNOIB introduces two complementary objectives on top of any existing global-level OOD fine-tuning loss. First, a prediction-based objective L_pred = α·L_glob + (1−α)·L_ins, where L_ins replaces the area-weighted OOD pixel average with an equal-weighted average over each detected OOD instance, ensuring small anomalies receive the same optimization signal as large ones. Second, a feature separation objective L_feat = β·L_sem + (1−β)·L_near that constructs per-instance OOD prototypes via masked average pooling and per-category ID prototypes via thresholded feature averaging, then minimizes cosine similarity between OOD prototypes and all ID prototypes (L_sem) as well as specifically the top-M nearest ID prototypes (L_near) to prevent an OOD instance from collapsing into a single ID category. The overall training loss is L_LNOIB = γ₁·L_pred + γ₂·L_feat, and the framework is a drop-in paradigm: it reuses the base strategy's loss function for l_in and l_out, requires no architectural changes, and introduces zero additional inference computation.

## Results

On component-level metrics (the paper's primary focus), M2A+LNOIB achieves the best results across all four benchmarks: sIoU 63.15 / F1* 60.08 on SMIYC-RA, sIoU 61.50 / F1* 70.25 on SMIYC-RO, sIoU 52.08 / F1* 42.96 on FS Static, and sIoU 39.16 / F1* 34.80 on FS L&F. On pixel-level metrics, M2A+LNOIB reaches AuPRC 92.20 / FPR95 11.34 on SMIYC-RA and AuPRC 92.58 / FPR95 0.33 on SMIYC-RO, with only modest pixel-level gains over vanilla M2A, confirming that improvements are driven primarily by better small-anomaly segmentation. All reported numbers are means over 3 runs with standard deviations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LNOIB is integrated into three OOD fine-tuning strategies (EM, PEBAL, M2A) and consistently improves all of them. M2A+LNOIB achieves the largest absolute gains: sIoU improves from 51.47 to 63.15 (+11.68) and F1* from 45.26 to 60.08 (+14.82) on SMIYC-RA, and from 35.24 to 52.08 (+16.84) sIoU on FS Static. EM+LNOIB gains +10.23 sIoU on SMIYC-RA (48.50→58.73), while PEBAL+LNOIB gains +11.75 sIoU (40.42→52.17). On component-level metrics, M2A+LNOIB outperforms all standalone baselines including RWPM (53.10 sIoU on SMIYC-RA), RbA (56.26), and CSL (45.14). On pixel-level metrics (Table 2), improvements are more modest (e.g., M2A AuPRC 87.83→92.20 on SMIYC-RA), confirming that the primary benefit is in detecting small anomalies rather than large ones. The paper also reports that LNOIB preserves close-set Cityscapes mIoU within 1% of the vanilla Mask2Former, showing no significant degradation on ID categories.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the OOD fine-tuning paradigm for anomaly segmentation established by EM (entropy-based), PEBAL (energy-based), and M2A (logit-based), all of which rely on global-level pixel-wise objectives. The key departure is a theoretical analysis (Lemma 1, Theorem 1) showing that these global objectives inherently weight anomalies by pixel count, causing small instances to receive negligible gradient signal. Building on this, the paper introduces an instance-level reweighting objective and a prototype-based feature separation loss drawn from few-shot segmentation literature, positioning LNOIB as a drop-in extension rather than a replacement for existing strategies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work addresses a concrete safety gap in autonomous driving: small OOD instances (e.g., a small animal on the road) are systematically missed by current anomaly segmentation methods, yet pose disproportionate risk. LNOIB's versatility as a plug-in paradigm (no inference cost, compatible with EM/PEBAL/M2A) makes it practically adoptable. However, the authors acknowledge that OOD fine-tuning itself remains controversial since it only mimics limited OOD scenarios, and the method's quality is not always perfect on the smallest anomalies. Open questions include whether the instance-level framework generalizes to video segmentation, industrial inspection, and more diverse real-world OOD distributions beyond cut-and-paste synthesis.
