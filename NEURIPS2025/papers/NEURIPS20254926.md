## Problem

Existing low-rank gradient optimization methods for LLM training (e.g., GaLore, Muon) primarily target memory reduction but rely on isotropic steepest descent or approximate orthogonalization (Newton-Schulz) that introduces errors growing with the moment matrix condition number. The paper identifies that LLM training moments become increasingly low-rank and ill-conditioned over time, making these approximations particularly inaccurate and slowing convergence. SUMO addresses this by performing exact SVD-based moment orthogonalization within a dynamically adapted low-rank subspace, eliminating approximation errors while preserving memory efficiency.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a weight matrix W ∈ R^{m×n} (m≥n), a mini-batch of training samples, and hyperparameters including projection rank r (typically 4, 8, or 16), subspace update frequency K, and a norm-growth threshold γ=1.1. It produces an updated weight matrix and an orthogonalized moment stored as an r×n matrix in the adaptive subspace, reducing optimizer state memory from 2mn (Adam) to nr+mr. The subspace basis Q^{(t)} ∈ R^{m×r} is refreshed every K steps via truncated randomized SVD of the current gradient, and the orthogonal complement of the gradient outside the subspace is incorporated into the final weight update at negligible additional cost.

## Method

SUMO operates in four blocks per iteration: (1) adaptive subspace selection via randomized SVD of the gradient every K steps, (2) exact SVD-based orthogonalization of the first-order moment within the r-dimensional subspace (replacing Muon's Newton-Schulz5 approximation), (3) a norm-growth limiter that caps relative increases in the orthogonalized moment's norm, and (4) a weight update in the original space that combines the orthogonalized subspace step with the gradient's orthogonal complement. The key theoretical contribution is proving that Newton-Schulz approximation error scales as (1−1/κ)^{2^i} with condition number κ, and that LLM training moments exhibit κ growing over time while rank decreases, making exact SVD in the low-rank subspace both necessary and computationally affordable (~4nr²+8r³ FLOPs). The convergence analysis shows SUMO achieves an ε-critical point in O(C/ε²) steps, with the rate improvement over Muon directly proportional to the accumulated Newton-Schulz errors avoided.

## Results

On the GLUE benchmark (RoBERTa-Base fine-tuning), SUMO (SVD, rank=4) achieves 62.3 CoLA, 91.04 STS-B, 93.5 MRPC, 81.07 RTE, 94.93 SST2, 87.34 MNLI, 93.26 QNLI, and 91.68 QQP using only 197M of optimizer-state memory — roughly 20% less than GaLore (253M) and ~74% less than full fine-tuning (747M). For pre-training Llama on C4, SUMO achieves the lowest validation perplexity at every scale (e.g., 14.68 at 1B params vs. 15.56 full-rank and 15.64 GaLore) while using 3.84G memory vs. 7.80G for full-rank. On GSM8K zero-shot (Phi-2, 2.7B), SUMO reaches 54.13% accuracy vs. 52.24% for GaLore and 42.8% for LoRA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SUMO (SVD) outperforms GaLore on all eight GLUE tasks at rank 4 (e.g., CoLA 62.3 vs. 60.35, RTE 81.07 vs. 79.42, QNLI 93.26 vs. 92.24) while using 22% less optimizer-state memory (197M vs. 253M). Against LoRA (rank 4), SUMO wins on every task with lower memory (197M vs. 257M), with the largest gap on SST2 (94.93 vs. 92.89). Compared to full fine-tuning, SUMO matches or exceeds it on 6 of 8 tasks (e.g., CoLA 62.3 vs. 62.24, QNLI 93.26 vs. 92.33) while using only 26% of the memory. In the pre-training comparison (Table 3), SUMO achieves the best perplexity at all four model scales (60M–1B) with roughly half the memory of full-rank training. In the zero-shot reasoning evaluation (Table 6, Llama-350M), SUMO attains the lowest perplexity (15.49) and highest average score (0.4416) among all methods tested, edging out APOLLO (0.4406) and APOLLO-Mini (0.4374) while using comparable memory (0.18G vs. 0.34G and 0.15G respectively).

## Relation to Prior Work

SUMO sits at the intersection of two active research threads: (1) low-rank gradient projection methods for memory-efficient LLM training (GaLore, AdaRankGrad), which exploit the empirically observed low-rank structure of gradients, and (2) norm-induced / gradient-orthogonalization optimizers (Muon, Shampoo, SOAP, OSGDM), which align updates with the spectral geometry of the loss landscape. The key departure is combining these: performing *exact* SVD-based moment orthogonalization *within* a dynamically adapted low-rank subspace, rather than applying the standard Newton-Schulz5 approximation in full dimension. This is motivated by a new theoretical result showing that Newton-Schulz approximation error grows with the condition number of the moment matrix, and that LLM first-order moments are precisely the ill-conditioned, low-rank regime where this error becomes non-negligible.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical takeaway is that a simple swap—replacing Newton-Schulz5 with exact SVD inside the low-rank subspace—yields both faster convergence (~1.6× on QNLI) and ~20% lower memory than GaLore, without sacrificing the memory efficiency that makes low-rank training viable on consumer hardware. The convergence analysis of Muon that explicitly accounts for Newton-Schulz error (rather than assuming exact orthogonalization) is, to the authors' knowledge, the first of its kind, and provides a principled explanation for why the approximation degrades in practice. Limitations include reliance on reversible-layer structure for the low-rank moment decay guarantee, and the method's benefit is most pronounced when moments are ill-conditioned; for well-conditioned or full-rank settings the advantage over Newton-Schulz shrinks. Open questions include whether the approach extends to non-reversible architectures, how it interacts with quantization or parallel orthogonalization, and whether the subspace update frequency K can be made fully adaptive.
