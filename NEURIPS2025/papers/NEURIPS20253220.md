## Problem

Existing generative infrared and visible image fusion methods suffer from three interrelated limitations: limited generative capability (focusing on feature extraction or optimization rather than fully exploiting generative modeling), lack of interpretability in how modal information is selected and integrated, and strong dependence on data distribution statistics that undermines robustness under distribution shifts. The paper argues that these gaps stem from neglecting the intrinsic physical structure of the data, and proposes to address them by incorporating human cognitive principles—selective attention and adherence to physical laws—into the generative fusion pipeline.

## Input / Output

The method takes a co-registered pair of infrared and visible (RGB) images as input. Before encoding, the infrared image is geometrically and semantically aligned to the visible modality via an entropic optimal transport mapping, while the visible image remains unchanged. The output is a single fused image generated through a reverse diffusion process in latent space, decoded from the final denoised latent. The entire pipeline is unsupervised, requiring no ground-truth fused images during training.

## Method

HCLFuse combines a multi-scale mask-regulated variational bottleneck encoder (VBE) with a physics-guided conditional diffusion model. The VBE applies an information bottleneck objective (balancing reconstruction fidelity against KL-regularized compression) to the optimally-transported infrared and visible inputs, producing a Gaussian latent posterior whose mean captures task-relevant structure and whose variance models uncertainty; multi-scale learnable masks further filter features at different resolutions. A theoretical lower bound (Theorem 1) justifies the optimal-transport alignment by linking mutual information gain to Wasserstein distance reduction, while Theorem 2 bounds the redundant information in the stochastic perturbation term. The diffusion decoder then generates the fused image via DDIM sampling, where at each timestep a physics-based correction is applied to the denoised estimate through three sequential constraints—heat conduction (Laplacian smoothing), structure preservation (gradient matching to source edges), and cross-modal physical consistency (masked intensity blending)—each weighted by an exponentially decaying time-varying coefficient that provides strong physical guidance in early high-noise steps and progressively cedes control to learned semantics in later steps.

## Results

On the MSRS dataset, HCLFuse achieves the best scores on AG (6.4355, +69.87% over second-best GIFNet at 3.3673), SF (17.899, +39.41% over MMAE at 12.839), DF (7.6427, +65.56% over GIFNet at 3.8030), QSF (0.5374, substantially above all competitors), and Nabf (0.0017, lowest). It also attains the highest SD (49.546) and EN (6.8704). Across TNO, FMB, and MFNet, HCLFuse consistently ranks first on the same five leading indicators (AG, SF, DF, QSF, Nabf), confirming robust generalization. For downstream semantic segmentation (Mask2Former on MSRS), HCLFuse achieves the highest mIoU of 89.72%, surpassing the next-best Text-DiFuse (89.60%) and Diff-IF (89.57%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

HCLFuse is compared against 17 baselines spanning non-end-to-end (NestFuse, LRRNet, MMAE), end-to-end (SwinFusion, SegMiF, SOSMaskFuse, STFNet, CrossFuse, Text-IF, GIFNet), and generative (FusionGAN, TarDAL, DDFM, Diff-IF, CCF, Text-DiFuse, LFDT-Fusion) methods. On MSRS, HCLFuse dominates the detail and structural metrics: AG (6.4355 vs. GIFNet 3.3673, +69.9%), SF (17.899 vs. MMAE 12.839, +39.4%), DF (7.6427 vs. GIFNet 3.8030, +65.6%), and QSF (0.5374 vs. GIFNet 0.0654, +721%), while also achieving the lowest Nabf (0.0017 vs. Diff-IF 0.0087). Among generative baselines, Diff-IF and Text-IF lead on VIF (1.0417, 1.0506) and SCD (1.6976), and CCF achieves the lowest PIQE (17.338), but HCLFuse surpasses all of them on the five headline indicators. On TNO, FMB, and MFNet, the same pattern holds: HCLFuse consistently ranks first on AG, SF, DF, QSF, and Nabf, with AG gains exceeding 40% over the second-best on TNO (7.2112 vs. GIFNet 4.9954) and FMB (6.9814 vs. GIFNet 5.0481). For downstream semantic segmentation, HCLFuse achieves the highest mIoU (89.72%) over Text-DiFuse (89.60%) and Diff-IF (89.57%), with particularly strong gains on the 'curve' class (84.60 vs. 84.42 for MMAE) and 'car_stop' (88.87 vs. 88.61 for LFDT-Fusion).

## Relation to Prior Work

HCLFuse extends the generative image fusion line initiated by Diff-Fusion, Diff-IF, and CCF, which used diffusion models primarily as feature extractors or conditional generators. It departs from these by grounding the generative process in two theoretical pillars: an information-bottleneck variational encoder (building on Tishby et al.) that compresses modal information under an optimal-transport alignment (Theorem 1, linking mutual information to Wasserstein distance), and a physics-guided reverse diffusion (building on DDIM) that injects time-varying physical constraints (heat conduction, structure preservation, cross-modal consistency) into the denoising trajectory. The 'human cognitive laws' framing—selective attention via the bottleneck and rule-based reasoning via physical priors—positions the method as a principled alternative to purely data-distribution-driven generative fusion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a real gap in generative fusion: the lack of interpretability in how modal information is selected and the strong dependence on training data quality. The time-varying physical guidance mechanism (strong constraints early, learned semantics late) is a reusable design pattern that could transfer to other conditional generation tasks. However, the diffusion-based pipeline introduces significant computational overhead, and the method assumes well-aligned input pairs, limiting real-time deployment. Open questions include whether the physical constraints generalize beyond IR/visible fusion (e.g., to medical or satellite imagery) and whether the optimal-transport alignment step can be approximated more efficiently for large-scale applications.
