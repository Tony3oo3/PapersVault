## Problem

RAG systems are vulnerable to corpus poisoning and prompt injection attacks that manipulate generated outputs, yet existing defenses rely on coarse-grained embedding-based filtering or majority voting that either discard valuable documents alongside malicious ones or fail to resolve semantic conflicts between retrieved content and the LLM's internal knowledge. No prior method incorporates structured semantic reasoning into both the retrieval and inference stages to achieve fine-grained, conflict-aware protection.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252376/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework consumes a user query, a potentially poisoned retrieval corpus, a small manually curated set of 10 verified clean documents per dataset (used as semantic reference graphs), and a generative LLM backbone. It outputs a single trustworthy answer string. Notably, the method requires multiple LLM calls per query (for EIRE extraction, semantic graph similarity scoring, and CAF consistency judgment), and the verified clean document set must be pre-selected per dataset, making it a semi-supervised defense rather than fully unsupervised.

## Method

SeCon-RAG is a two-stage defense built around EIRE (Entity-Intent-Relation Extractor), a prompt-based LLM module that decomposes each document into entities, intent, and inter-entity relations. In Stage 1 (SCF), documents are filtered via a conservative AND-logic combining K-means clustering (removing documents too tightly clustered around a centroid, indicative of templated poisoning) and semantic graph comparison (EIRE-derived graphs are serialized as structured triples and scored by an LLM against reference graphs from verified clean documents). In Stage 2 (CAF), the top-k retrieved documents are re-examined by an LLM that evaluates three consistency dimensions—query alignment, inter-document coherence, and compatibility with the model's internal knowledge—using EIRE-extracted semantic structures, discarding any document flagged as poisoned, conflicting, or irrelevant before final answer generation. The key distinction from prior work is that semantic structure (not just embedding proximity) drives filtering at both stages, enabling detection of poisoned documents that evade clustering-based defenses and resolution of internal-external knowledge conflicts at inference time.

## Results

SeCon-RAG is evaluated on three QA benchmarks (HotpotQA, NQ, MS-MARCO) across five LLMs under four threat settings (clean, prompt injection, 20% and 100% corpus poisoning). Under 100% poisoning on HotpotQA with GPT-4o, SeCon-RAG achieves 83.6% accuracy with 2.4% ASR, outperforming TrustRAG (80.9%/2.7%) and AstuteRAG (67.3%/24.1%). On MS-MARCO with DeepSeek-R1 under 100% poisoning, it reaches 94.5% accuracy with 1.8% ASR. On clean corpora, SeCon-RAG maintains competitive accuracy (e.g., 94.0% on MS-MARCO with DeepSeek-R1, 86.0% on HotpotQA with GPT-4o), confirming that the defense does not degrade benign performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against InstructRAG, SeCon-RAG wins decisively across all datasets, models, and attack settings; for example, on HotpotQA under 100% poisoning with GPT-4o, SeCon-RAG achieves 83.6% accuracy versus InstructRAG's 27.3%, a gap of over 56 percentage points. Against AstuteRAG, SeCon-RAG also dominates in adversarial settings (e.g., 83.6% vs. 67.3% on HotpotQA 100% with GPT-4o; 96.4% vs. 81.8% on NQ 100% with DeepSeek-R1), though AstuteRAG occasionally matches or slightly exceeds SeCon-RAG on clean corpora (e.g., 89.0% vs. 86.0% on HotpotQA clean with DeepSeek-R1). Against TrustRAG—the strongest baseline—SeCon-RAG wins on the majority of attack settings (e.g., 83.6%/2.4% vs. 80.9%/2.7% on HotpotQA 100% with GPT-4o; 96.4%/0.0% vs. 88.2%/0.0% on NQ 100% with DeepSeek-R1), but TrustRAG retains an edge in a few specific configurations, most notably MS-MARCO 100% poisoning with Mistral-12B (91.8%/0.0% vs. 88.2%/0.0%) and HotpotQA clean with DeepSeek-R1 (89.0% vs. 86.0%). Overall, SeCon-RAG achieves the best or near-best accuracy in 22 of 25 model–dataset–setting combinations under 100% poisoning, while maintaining the lowest ASR in most cases.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the RAG defense literature (TrustRAG, AstuteRAG, InstructRAG, RobustRAG) by introducing structured semantic reasoning into both the retrieval and inference stages of poisoning defense. Prior methods rely on coarse-grained mechanisms—embedding-space clustering, majority voting, or heuristic knowledge fusion—which the authors argue either discard useful content or fail to resolve conflicts between retrieved and internal knowledge. SeCon-RAG departs from this lineage by proposing EIRE, an entity-intent-relation extraction module that converts documents into interpretable semantic graphs, and by using LLM-based semantic comparison of those graphs (rather than rigid graph metrics) to flag poisoned content. The AND-logic joint filtering and the three-axis conflict-aware filtering (query, corpus, model consistency) at inference time are novel design choices not present in prior RAG defenses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work addresses a practically important gap: RAG systems deployed in production are vulnerable to corpus poisoning, and existing defenses trade off robustness against information loss. SeCon-RAG's two-stage design (statistical + semantic at retrieval, semantic consistency at inference) offers a more principled way to preserve useful knowledge while suppressing adversarial content, and the consistent gains across five LLMs and three benchmarks suggest broad applicability. Key limitations include moderate inference latency from multiple LLM calls, dependence on a small manually curated set of verified clean documents, and sensitivity to the quality of EIRE's semantic extraction on domain-specific text. Open questions include whether EIRE can be replaced by lighter-weight extractors, whether the AND-logic threshold is optimal under adaptive attacks, and how the framework scales to multi-turn or agentic RAG settings.
