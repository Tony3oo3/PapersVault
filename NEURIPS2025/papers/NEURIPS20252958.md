## Problem

SO(3)-equivariant networks for machine learning interatomic potentials rely on Clebsch-Gordan tensor products whose computational cost scales as O(L⁶) with maximum angular degree L, creating a severe bottleneck for higher-degree representations. Existing acceleration strategies—SO(2)-based convolutions, fast spherical Fourier transforms, and frame averaging—either sacrifice the expressivity of the standard CG tensor product or introduce discontinuity issues, leaving a gap for a method that simultaneously reduces both computational complexity and parameter count while preserving equivariance and accuracy.

## Input / Output

The model consumes 3D atomic coordinates and element types organized as molecular or catalyst graphs, with features represented as irreducible representations (irreps) of SO(3) at angular degrees up to L. It predicts total energy (meV/atom or eV), per-atom forces (meV/Å), and relaxed geometries for adsorbate-catalyst systems. A key implementation detail is that all irreps share a common multiplicity index, allowing the CP-decomposition-based tensor product to operate as a single batched Hadamard product across the concatenated irrep channel rather than computing tensor products independently per CG path.

## Method

TDN replaces the standard CG tensor product with a low-rank CP decomposition of the CG coefficient tensor, reducing per-operation complexity from O(L⁶) to O(L⁴) (with rank R = 7L²). A path-weight sharing mechanism further collapses the O(L³) distinct multiplicity-space weight matrices into a single shared parameter set, reducing parameter count from O(cL³) to O(c) without compromising SO(3)-equivariance since sharing operates exclusively on multiplicity indices. The paper proves a uniform equivariance error bound (Theorem 3.1) showing the error is controlled by the spectral tail of the tensor's mode matricizations, and a universality result (Theorem 3.2) guaranteeing that any SO(3)-equivariant bilinear map can be exactly represented in CP form with rank at most d₁d₂. Architecturally, TDN is built on Equiformer's graph transformer, substituting every CG tensor product in self-attention with the CP-decomposition-based operation and replacing channel-wise linear projections with batched matrix operations over the multiplicity dimension. The precomputed CP factors of the CG coefficient tensor are cached as constants during training, making the layer a plug-and-play replacement in existing equivariant architectures.

## Results

On PubChemQCR-S, TDN achieves the best energy MAE of 4.46 meV/atom and force RMSE of 26.94 meV/Å (validation), outperforming the next-best Equiformer (4.69 meV/atom, 34.67 meV/Å) by ~5% on energy and ~22% on force. On the full PubChemQCR dataset (105M snapshots), TDN reaches 1.65 meV/atom energy MAE and 19.46 meV/Å force RMSE, more than doubling the accuracy of PaiNN (3.62/38.30). On OC22 IS2RE-DIRECT, TDN attains the lowest ID MAE (1.49 eV) and best average MAE (2.20 eV), while on OC20 it matches Equiformer's accuracy (0.5683 vs 0.5489 eV average MAE) at 2.5–8.4× higher throughput.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TDN outperforms all nine baselines on PubChemQCR-S, with the closest competitor being Equiformer (4.69 vs 4.46 meV/atom energy MAE; 34.67 vs 26.94 meV/Å force RMSE), a ~22% improvement in force prediction. On the full PubChemQCR dataset, TDN more than halves the energy error of PaiNN (1.65 vs 3.62 meV/atom) and SchNet (1.65 vs 7.14 meV/atom). On OC20 IS2RE-DIRECT, TDN is slightly behind Equiformer in average energy MAE (0.5683 vs 0.5489 eV) but achieves 2.47× higher throughput (770.8 vs 311.7 samples/sec at L=1) with 63% fewer parameters. On OC22, TDN achieves the best ID MAE (1.49 eV vs coGN's 1.62 eV) and best average MAE (2.20 vs 2.21 eV), though it trails coGN slightly on OOD MAE (2.92 vs 2.81 eV). The efficiency ablation confirms that CP decomposition plus path-weight sharing reduces training time by ~78% for TDN and ~74% for NequIP with negligible accuracy loss.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Equiformer architecture [7], replacing its Clebsch-Gordan tensor products with low-rank CP decompositions and introducing a path-weight sharing mechanism that collapses O(L³) distinct multiplicity-space weights into a single shared parameter set. It departs from prior tensor-product acceleration strategies—SO(2)-based convolutions (eSCN), Gaunt tensor products via fast spherical Fourier transforms, and frame averaging—by treating the CG coefficient tensor as a generic three-way tensor amenable to standard low-rank factorization, yielding a plug-and-play layer that preserves the original CG structure rather than reformulating the operation in a different basis. The theoretical contributions (uniform equivariance error bounds and a universality theorem for CP-decomposed equivariant bilinear maps) are new and not present in prior TP-acceleration work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The O(L⁶)→O(L⁴) complexity reduction and 63–92% parameter savings make higher angular degrees practically accessible, which is critical for capturing richer angular correlations in interatomic potentials. The newly curated PubChemQCR dataset (105M DFT snapshots) provides a much larger and more diverse molecular relaxation benchmark than prior MLIP evaluations. Open questions include: the NP-hardness of optimal rank selection (currently handled by an empirical scheduler), whether path-weight sharing can be made adaptive to recover the small accuracy gap it introduces, and whether the CP-decomposition framework extends cleanly to other equivariant tensor products (SO(2) convolutions, Gaunt products, higher-order couplings).
