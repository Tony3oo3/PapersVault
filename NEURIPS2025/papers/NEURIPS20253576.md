## Problem

Gradual domain adaptation (GDA) decomposes large source-to-target distribution shifts into smaller steps through intermediate domains, but existing methods suffer from inefficient knowledge propagation, instability during transitional phases, and compounding pseudo-label errors that degrade accuracy across the adaptation trajectory. The core gap is the absence of a mechanism that dynamically controls how much the model relies on the current versus the next domain during each adaptation step, leading to either premature forgetting of source knowledge or insufficient assimilation of target characteristics.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sequence of n+1 domains (labeled source, unlabeled intermediates, unlabeled target), each partitioned into mini-batches, and produces a final classifier mapping inputs to class labels. For image benchmarks (Rotated MNIST, Color-Shift MNIST, Portraits, CIFAR-C), inputs are 2D images processed by CNN backbones (3×32-channel conv layers or WideResNet-28/ResNeXt-29); for the tabular Cover Type dataset, inputs are 54-dimensional feature vectors processed by a 128-256-512 fully connected network. The adaptation proceeds batch-by-batch with cyclic matching between neighboring domains, and the final output is the adapted parameter vector θ^(n,m) after all inter-domain steps complete.

## Method

STDW introduces three coupled mechanisms: (1) dynamic pseudo-labeling, where data is partitioned into T mini-batches and the classifier is refined after each batch so that subsequent batches receive labels from an incrementally improved model, avoiding the error accumulation of single-pass static labeling; (2) cyclic batch matching, which pairs batches from two neighboring domains via modular indexing functions i(t) and j(t) to ensure systematic, periodic coverage of both domains during each adaptation stage; and (3) stepwise dynamic osmosis, the key novelty, where a time-varying hyperparameter ρ ∈ [0,1] linearly schedules the weighted loss (1−ρ)·ℓ_current + ρ·ℓ_next, progressively shifting the optimization emphasis from the current domain to the next. The update rule θ^(t,k+1) = Φ(θ^(t,k), B_{t-1,i(k)}, B_{t,j(k)}, ρ) is applied for m·epochs inner iterations per domain pair, with ρ incremented by g = 1/s after each full pass. A Lyapunov stability analysis in the appendix shows that under L-smoothness and μ-strong convexity of the mixed loss, the parameter trajectory converges monotonically to the joint minimizer, and a dwell-time condition handles discontinuous domain paths.

## Results

On the four primary GDA benchmarks, STDW achieves 97.6% accuracy on Rotated MNIST, 98.3% on Color-Shift MNIST, 87.1% on Portraits, and 74.2% on Cover Type, outperforming the strongest baselines by 10.1, 6.5, 1.0, and 0.7 percentage points respectively. On the corruption robustness benchmarks (severity level 5), STDW attains a mean error rate of 15.5% on CIFAR-10-C and 25.8% on CIFAR-100-C, beating the closest competitor GTTA-MIX (15.6% and 28.9%) and reducing error by 28.0 and 20.6 points over the source-only baseline. STDW achieves the best result on 11/15 corruption types for CIFAR-10-C and 14/15 for CIFAR-100-C, with particularly large gains (3.3–11.6 pp) on high-frequency corruptions such as Gaussian, shot, and impulse noise.

## Baselines & Comparisons

STDW dominates all 12 baselines across every dataset and metric reported. Among UDA methods, DANN (44.2% on Rotated MNIST), DeepCoral (49.6%), and DeepJDOT (51.6%) are far below STDW (97.6%), confirming that direct alignment strategies cannot handle the large geometric or photometric shifts in GDA settings. Among GDA methods, IDOL (87.5%) and GOAT (91.8% on Color-Shift MNIST) are the closest competitors on the synthetic benchmarks, yet STDW still leads by 10.1 and 6.5 pp respectively; on Portraits, GGF (86.1%) is the nearest rival, trailing STDW by only 1.0 pp. On the corruption benchmarks (Table 2), the TTA baselines TENT-continual (20.7% mean error on CIFAR-10-C), AdaContrast (18.5%), and CoTTA (16.2%) are all outperformed by STDW (15.5%), while GTTA-MIX (15.6%) is the closest competitor, separated by just 0.1 pp on CIFAR-10-C but 3.1 pp on CIFAR-100-C (28.9% vs. 25.8%). The GDA baselines GST and GOAT show minimal or no improvement over the source-only model on the corruption tasks (e.g., GOAT stays at ~56.5% on CIFAR-10-C regardless of intermediate domain count), whereas STDW achieves 81.0–84.5% accuracy, highlighting the advantage of the dynamic weighting mechanism under severe distributional shifts.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the gradual domain adaptation (GDA) framework established by Kumar et al. (ICML 2020), which introduced self-training across intermediate domains, by replacing the static pseudo-labeling and uniform loss treatment with a dynamic weighting mechanism governed by a time-varying hyperparameter ρ. It also draws on the broader GDA literature including GOAT (optimal transport), GGF (gradient flow), and GNF (normalizing flows) for constructing intermediate domains, and positions itself against both UDA baselines (DANN, DeepCoral) and test-time adaptation methods (CoTTA, GTTA-MIX). The key conceptual departure is the cyclic batch matching across neighboring domains combined with the stepwise 'osmosis' of knowledge via ρ, which reframes the self-training loop as a controlled interpolation rather than a fixed-proportion update.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dynamic weighting mechanism offers a simple, interpretable knob for controlling adaptation speed in GDA, which is practically useful for practitioners dealing with slowly evolving deployment environments. However, the method's reliance on well-defined intermediate domains and its assumption of smooth transitions limit applicability to abrupt or highly discontinuous shifts, and the additional hyperparameter (ρ scheduling) introduces tuning overhead. Open questions include whether the framework extends to text or time-series modalities, whether ρ scheduling can be automated, and whether the large gains on synthetic benchmarks (Rotated/Color-Shift MNIST) translate to the more modest improvements seen on real-world datasets (Portraits, Cover Type).
