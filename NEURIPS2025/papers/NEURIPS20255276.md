## Problem

Deploying DRL agents in safety-critical real plants faces two unsolved challenges: unknown unknowns (unpredictable dynamics from DNN parameter space and complex environments) that defeat safety guarantees, and the Sim2Real gap that causes pre-trained policies to fail in novel real environments. Existing safe DRL methods either assume manageable Sim2Real gaps or treat the DRL agent as a black box behind a physics-based safety module whose own guarantees break under model mismatch. No prior framework provides verifiable safety during runtime learning in real physical systems while simultaneously achieving high task performance.

## Input / Output

The framework consumes real-time proprioceptive sensor states s(t) ∈ R^n, a linearized physics model {A(s(k)), B(s(k))} updated at each trigger event, and pre-specified safety/action constraint matrices (C, c) and (D, d) that define the safety set S and admissible action set A. It produces a real-time action command a(t) ∈ R^m applied to the physical plant, switching between the DRL-Student's learned policy and the PHY-Teacher's LMI-based state-feedback policy. The DRL-Student maintains dual replay buffers (self-learning and teaching-to-learn) and uses an off-policy actor-critic architecture (DDPG, TD3, or SAC), while the PHY-Teacher solves a set of LMIs in real time via the embedded C solver ECVXCONE to compute its feedback gain F_k.

## Method

Real-DRL comprises three interactive components: a DRL-Student (actor-critic agent with dual replay buffers), a PHY-Teacher (physics-model-based controller with assured safety via real-time LMI patches), and a Trigger that monitors when states approach the boundary of a self-learning space L (a strict subset of the safety set S, creating a buffer zone). The key mechanism is a dual self-learning and teaching-to-learn paradigm: the DRL-Student learns high-performance behavior from its own trial-and-error in L, while learning safety from the PHY-Teacher's successful interventions in the marginally-safe region S\L. A novel safety-informed batch sampling scheme (Eq. 14) dynamically allocates batch samples between the two replay buffers based on a real-time safety indicator V(s(t)), concentrating learning on corner-case experience when the plant is near safety boundaries. The PHY-Teacher's action policy is designed via a real-time patch (Theorem 5.2) that solves LMIs at each trigger to compute a state-feedback gain F_k guaranteeing that states remain in S, actions remain in A, and states return to L within a finite activation period, even under bounded model mismatch. An adaptive compensation mechanism (Appendix F) further handles large model mismatches by computing corrective actions in real time.

## Results

In the wild-environment quadruped navigation benchmark (NVIDIA Isaac Gym), Real-DRL is the only method that completes the full navigation task (4 waypoints) without falling or colliding, achieving a travel time of 45.34 s and total energy of 21,742 J. The sole PHY-Teacher also completes the task safely but is slower (55.53 s) and consumes more energy (26,818 J). All four DRL baselines (CLF-DRL, Phy-DRL, Runtime Assurance, Neural Simplex) fail: two fall over and two collide, rendering their travel time and energy metrics infinite or N/A. On the real quadruped robot, Real-DRL guarantees safety under five types of injected unknown unknowns (Beta disturbance, payload drop, human kick, DoS fault, side push), while Phy-DRL trained in simulation fails due to the Sim2Real gap.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Real-DRL decisively outperforms all four DRL baselines on the primary safety metrics: CLF-DRL [55] and Runtime Assurance [8,16,50] both cause the robot to fall, while Phy-DRL [13,12] and Neural Simplex [43] both result in collisions, making them unable to complete the navigation task at all. Against the sole PHY-Teacher (the physics-model-based controller without the DRL-Student), Real-DRL achieves the same safety guarantees (no fall, no collision) but improves travel time by ~18% (45.34 s vs. 55.53 s) and reduces total energy consumption by ~19% (21,742 J vs. 26,818 J), demonstrating that the DRL-Student's self-learning for high performance complements the PHY-Teacher's safety assurance. On the real quadruped robot, Phy-DRL with concurrent delay randomization and domain randomization still fails to guarantee safety due to unmodeled Sim2Real gaps, whereas Real-DRL maintains safety throughout. Ablation studies confirm that the teaching-to-learn mechanism accelerates convergence and improves reward, while safety-informed batch sampling specifically enhances corner-case safety compared to standard random sampling.

## Relation to Prior Work

Real-DRL builds directly on the Simplex architecture [49], which pairs a high-performance module with a high-assurance module, but departs fundamentally from prior fault-tolerant DRL approaches (Neural Simplex, Runtime Assurance, Model Predictive Shielding) that treat the DRL agent as an opaque black box operating in parallel with a safety backup. Instead, Real-DRL introduces a teaching-to-learn paradigm where the physics-model-based PHY-Teacher actively instructs the DRL-Student on safety, and the DRL-Student learns from the teacher's experience in the marginally-safe space. The PHY-Teacher's LMI-based real-time patch design extends the Phy-DRL framework [13, 12] by incorporating an adaptive patch that tolerates unknown unknowns at runtime, while the safety-informed batch sampling mechanism addresses an experience imbalance problem (corner cases) that prior safe DRL methods using random sampling [45, 35, 18, 30] do not handle.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work addresses a critical gap in safe DRL deployment: most existing safety guarantees (CLF-based rewards, Phy-DRL, residual policies) rely on assumptions of manageable Sim2Real gap and bounded unknown unknowns that break down in real physical environments, as demonstrated by the Phy-DRL failure on the real quadruped robot. The runtime learning paradigm means the system can adapt to genuinely novel environments without retraining, and the ECVXCONE embedded solver (13–36 ms LMI solves on edge hardware) makes the framework practical for resource-constrained deployment. Key limitations include the reliance on Assumption 5.1 (bounded model mismatch within the patch), the availability of physics model knowledge A(s(k)), B(s(k)), and the Trigger's continuous monitoring burden; the authors suggest reachability-based early warning as a future direction to alleviate the latter.
