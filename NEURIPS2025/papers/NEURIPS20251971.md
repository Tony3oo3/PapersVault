## Problem

While GSPN-1 replaces quadratic self-attention with a linear-complexity line-scan propagation for 2D spatial modeling, its CUDA implementation fails to deliver practical speedups due to thousands of redundant kernel launches, excessive global memory (HBM) traffic, and redundant per-channel propagation weights. The gap between GSPN-1's theoretical O(√N) complexity and its actual GPU performance (only 3–8% of peak memory bandwidth) remains unaddressed, limiting deployment on high-resolution images and long videos.

## Input / Output

For image classification, GSPN-2 consumes 224×224 RGB images and outputs ImageNet-1K class labels; for text-to-image generation, it takes text prompts and produces images up to 16K resolution on a single A100 GPU. Internally, the propagation operates on 2D feature maps of shape (H×W×C), processing them through four directional line-scan passes (top-to-bottom, bottom-to-top, left-to-right, right-to-left) with a 3-neighbor tridiagonal kernel, and supports a local variant that segments rows/columns into fixed-length chunks of size k_chunk.

## Method

GSPN-2 is a joint algorithm–system co-design that (1) fuses all propagation steps into a single unified CUDA kernel indexed by (chunk, n, c) blocks with 2D thread layout (H, cSlice), eliminating thousands of micro-launches; (2) replaces per-channel propagation matrices w_{i,c} with channel-shared weights w_i, reducing parameters and aligning the recurrence with an attention-like affinity matrix formulation; and (3) projects inputs into a low-dimensional proxy space (C_proxy ≪ C, e.g., 8) before propagation to keep the active block count within the GPU's ~3,500 concurrent-block saturation limit on A100. Additional CUDA-level optimizations include coalesced global memory access (the single largest gain, ~24–34×), shared-memory caching of the previous column's hidden state, and stream-based concurrency for the four directional passes. The propagation recurrence h_i = w_i h_{i-1} + Diag(λ_i) x_i uses row-stochastic tridiagonal matrices (3-neighbor connectivity) to guarantee numerical stability while capturing long-range spatial context, achieving 40× speedup over GSPN-1 (71.4 ms → 1.8 ms for 1024×1024×8) while matching or exceeding transformer-level accuracy on ImageNet and improving text-to-image generation quality.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K at 224×224, GSPN-2-T achieves 83.0% top-1 accuracy with 24M parameters and 4.2G MACs, GSPN-2-S reaches 84.4% with 50M parameters and 9.2G MACs, and GSPN-2-B attains 84.6% with 89M parameters and 14.2G MACs. On COCO text-to-image generation at 1024×1024, GSPN-2 achieves FID 33.21 and CLIP-T 0.286, competitive with the SD-v1.5 baseline (FID 32.71, CLIP-T 0.290) while delivering up to 93× faster inference at 16K resolution. The CUDA kernel optimization yields a 40× speedup over GSPN-1 (71.4 ms → 1.8 ms for 1024×1024×8 input on A100), reaching 93% of peak memory bandwidth.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On ImageNet-1K, GSPN-2-T (83.0%) outperforms all raster-scan SSM baselines (best: LocalVMamba-T at 82.7%) and most Transformers (best: CSWin-T at 82.7%), while using fewer parameters (24M vs. 26–32M) and fewer MACs (4.2G vs. 4.3–5.7G) than every competitor at the Tiny scale. At the Small scale, GSPN-2-S (84.4%) ties MaxViT-Small (84.4%) and beats all SSMs by at least 0.7% (best SSM: LocalVMamba-S at 83.7%), while matching MogaNet-B (84.3%) with fewer MACs (9.2G vs. 9.9G). At Base scale, GSPN-2-B (84.6%) ties SwinV2-B (84.6%) and surpasses all SSMs (best: MambaVision-B at 84.2%) and most CNNs, with 1.7G fewer MACs than GSPN-B. On COCO text-to-image generation, GSPN-2 (FID 33.21, CLIP-T 0.286) is slightly behind GSPN-1 (FID 30.86, CLIP-T 0.307) but substantially outperforms Mamba (FID 50.30), Mamba2 (FID 37.02), and Linfusion (FID 36.33), while approaching the SD-v1.5 baseline (FID 32.71) with dramatically faster inference (32× at 4K, 93× at 16K vs. GSPN-1's 84×).

## Relation to Prior Work

GSPN-2 is a direct algorithm-system co-design refinement of GSPN-1 (Wang et al., CVPR 2025), which first replaced quadratic 2D self-attention with a 3-neighbor line-scan propagation achieving O(√N) complexity. The paper positions GSPN's dependency pattern as fundamentally distinct from both FlashAttention's fused GEMM tiling and Mamba/SSM's prefix-sum scans, necessitating a purpose-built CUDA kernel rather than reuse of existing primitives. The conceptual lineage traces back to the original Spatial Propagation Network (Liu et al., NeurIPS 2017), but GSPN-2 introduces channel-shared propagation weights and a low-dimensional proxy compression that have no direct analogue in prior SSM or attention literature, drawing an explicit analogy to attention affinity matrices in the single-channel case.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: GSPN-2 closes the gap between GSPN's theoretical O(√N) complexity and real-world GPU performance, achieving 93% of peak A100 memory bandwidth (vs. 3–8% for GSPN-1) and enabling 16K-resolution diffusion inference on a single GPU. The finding that a 48:1 channel compression (C_proxy=2) preserves accuracy suggests spatial propagation structure dominates over per-channel expressiveness, which could inform future efficient-architecture design. Open questions include whether GSPN-2 scales to long video sequences, how it integrates as a drop-in replacement for attention in models requiring CLS/register tokens, and whether the line-scan formulation generalizes beyond 2D spatial data to truly sequential or 3D settings.
