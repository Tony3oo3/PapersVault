## Problem

Gradient-based methods for non-convex min-max games with neural network players lack provable convergence guarantees, despite empirical success. While hidden convexity provides a structural lens, it does not ensure that Jacobian conditioning is preserved during training, leaving open whether architecture, initialization, and dynamics can jointly guarantee convergence to a Nash equilibrium. This paper provides the first such guarantee for games parameterized by two-layer neural networks.

## Input / Output

The method takes two-layer neural network weight parameters (θ, φ) for two players, finite training datasets (D_F, D_G) of size n with unit-norm inputs, and a fixed bilinear coupling matrix A with bounded operator norm. It outputs an ε-approximate saddle point measured by the Nikaido-Isoda duality gap, with iteration complexity O(poly(1/width, 1/n, d_input) · L³/μ³ · log(1/ε)), provided network widths scale as Ω̃(μ²n³/d_input). The analysis also covers a dual input-optimization setting where network weights are fixed and inputs are optimized, yielding Õ(ε⁻¹ log ε⁻¹) complexity.

## Method

The core technique combines two ingredients: (a) a path-length bound for alternating GDA (AltGDA) iterates via a Lyapunov potential function (a weighted interpolation of the two players' Nash gaps), proving trajectories remain confined within a ball where Jacobian singular values stay well-conditioned; and (b) a high-probability reduction from hidden convex-concave geometry to a two-sided Polyak-Lojasiewicz condition, where the PL moduli are governed by the smallest singular values of the network Jacobians. Under Gaussian random initialization and cubic overparameterization (width = Ω̃(μ²n³/d_input)), random matrix theory tools (Hermite expansions, concentration bounds) ensure σ_min of the Jacobians is bounded away from zero at initialization and throughout the bounded trajectory. The alternating structure is essential—simultaneous one-timescale GDA can diverge even under hidden convexity—and the cubic n-scaling (versus linear in pure minimization) arises from the need to control both players' gradient norms simultaneously in the general separable loss structure.

## Results

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical experiments. The headline result (Theorem 3.8) establishes that AltGDA converges to an ε-approximate Nash equilibrium in O(poly(1/width₁, 1/width₂, 1/n, d_input) × L³/μ³ × log(1/ε)) iterations for hidden convex-concave games parameterized by two-layer networks, provided the hidden layer width satisfies width₁,₂ = Õ(μ²n³/d_input). A second result (Theorem 3.5) shows that input-optimization min-max games (e.g., adversarial attack generation) converge in Õ(1/ε · log(1/ε)) iterations under random Gaussian initialization. The required overparameterization scaling of Ω(n³) is notably stronger than the Ω(n) scaling in pure minimization settings, reflecting the additional difficulty of controlling alternating min-max dynamics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper is purely theoretical and does not include empirical experiments or direct method comparisons against baselines. The paper positions its contributions relative to prior theoretical work: it builds on the two-sided PL convergence framework of Yang, Kiyavash, and He [124] and the overparameterization/Jacobian analysis of Song et al. [106], but extends both to the alternating min-max setting where simultaneous GDA can diverge. The key novelty over prior hidden-convexity results [114, 115, 99] is providing the first open-box, high-probability convergence guarantee for games involving two-layer neural networks, addressing the rank-collapse fragility that undermines earlier guarantees. The paper also provides the first provable convergence result for input-optimization attacks on randomly initialized overparameterized networks, matching and explaining the empirical observations of [99] and [117] which previously lacked theoretical justification.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) the hidden convexity framework for non-convex min-max games (Vlatakis-Gkaragkounis et al. 2019, 2021; Sakos et al. 2023), which showed that convex-concave structure in latent space can rescue convergence but left open the critical question of whether Jacobian conditioning is preserved during training; (2) the two-sided Polyak-Łojasiewicz (PL) condition for min-max optimization (Yang, Kiyavash, He 2020), which provides log(1/ε) convergence under PL but requires the PL moduli to remain bounded; and (3) overparameterization theory for neural network training (Song et al. 2021; Allen-Zhu et al. 2019), which controls NTK drift in pure minimization. The key departure is that prior hidden-convexity results either assumed fixed/linear player mappings or could not guarantee that the Jacobian spectrum remains well-conditioned throughout the optimization trajectory. This paper closes that gap by proving, via a novel AltGDA path-length bound and random matrix concentration arguments, that sufficient overparameterization plus Gaussian initialization keeps the iterates within a region where the two-sided PL condition holds, yielding the first global convergence guarantee for games where both players are two-layer neural networks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides the first theoretical explanation for why simple alternating gradient methods empirically converge in adversarial training, GANs, and robust optimization when players are neural networks—a question that has remained open despite extensive empirical evidence. The cubic overparameterization requirement (Ω(n³) vs. Ω(n) in pure minimization) reveals a fundamental structural cost of the game setting, suggesting that the interaction between players makes trajectory control significantly harder. Key limitations include the restriction to two-layer networks, twice-differentiable activations (excluding ReLU), Gaussian initialization, and separable loss structures with bilinear coupling; extending to deep architectures, non-smooth activations, and multi-player or non-zero-sum settings remains open. For practitioners, the results validate the use of AltGDA over simultaneous GDA in adversarial settings and suggest that network width and data diversity are critical design parameters for stable training.
