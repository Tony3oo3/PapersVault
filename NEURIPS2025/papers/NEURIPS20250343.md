## Problem

Existing low-rank optimization methods for LLM pretraining (e.g., GaLore, Q-GaLore) project gradients onto the dominant subspace defined by the top singular vectors, but this subspace effectively freezes after early pretraining, constraining all subsequent weight updates to nearly identical subspaces and creating a low-rank bottleneck that degrades language modeling quality. The paper asks whether this frozen-subspace limitation can be overcome with minimal additional computational overhead while retaining provable convergence guarantees that the dominant-subspace approach lacks.

## Input / Output

The method operates on per-layer mini-batch gradient matrices G ∈ R^{m×n} (with m ≤ n) and model weight matrices, alongside hyperparameters including rank r, subspace change frequency τ, and standard optimizer parameters. At each subspace update step (every τ iterations), an SVD is computed on the gradient matrix and r left singular vectors are sampled from the m available ones; between updates, the previous projector is reused. The sampling step adds only ~0.0005 s of overhead per layer versus ~0.34 s for the SVD itself, making it effectively free relative to the existing computation.

## Method

SARA replaces the deterministic dominant-subspace selection with weighted sampling without replacement: each of the m left singular vectors of the mini-batch gradient is assigned a selection probability proportional to its singular value, and r vectors are drawn from this distribution to span the new low-rank subspace. This plug-and-play modification is integrated into GaLore-Adam and Fira-Adam without altering their overall update structure, and it is robust to second-moment factorization (Adafactor, Adam-mini) and low-precision (8-bit) optimizer state storage. The key mechanism is that stochastic subspace selection makes adjacent subspaces more distinct, so the cumulative weight update—formed by summing updates across diverse subspaces—achieves higher effective rank and avoids the frozen-subspace trap. Theoretically, the paper proves (Theorem 3.4) that low-rank MSGD with SARA converges at rate O(LΔ/(δ^{2.5}T) + √(LΔσ²/(δ^{3.5}T))), matching GoLore's rate up to a constant factor while providing the first provable convergence guarantee for an importance-sampling-based low-rank optimizer, unlike GaLore which has none.

## Results

On the C4 dataset, SARA reduces the validation perplexity (PPL) gap relative to full-rank Adam by up to 46.05% (GaLore-SARA-Adafactor at 130M: 24.09 vs. 24.79 for GaLore-Adafactor, with full-rank Adam at 23.27). For the primary GaLore-Adam comparison, SARA achieves PPL of 30.47/24.21/19.16 at 60M/130M/350M, versus 31.50/24.88/19.68 for dominant-subspace GaLore-Adam. At the 1.1B scale, GaLore-SARA-Adam reaches PPL 15.36, outperforming GaLore-Adam (15.47) and even surpassing full-rank Adam (15.90). On SlimPajama, SARA narrows the gap to full-rank Adam to just 0.23 PPL points at 130M (25.23 vs. 25.00).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SARA consistently outperforms all dominant-subspace baselines across every model size and optimizer variant. Against GaLore-Adam (dominant subspace), SARA reduces PPL by 1.03/0.67/0.52 at 60M/130M/350M, narrowing the gap to full-rank Adam by 27–42%. Against Fira-Adam, SARA improves PPL by 0.30/0.15/0.10, with Fira-SARA-Adam at 350M (17.25) actually beating full-rank Adam (18.21). Against GoLore-Adam (random JL projection), SARA wins by 1.14 PPL at 60M (30.47 vs. 31.61) and 0.93 at 130M (23.08 vs. 24.01). The online PCA baseline [42] performs substantially worse (33.69/30.62), attributed to unstable training dynamics. SARA also improves over GaLore-Adafactor (up to 46.05% gap reduction), GaLore-Adam-mini (up to 28.57%), and 8-bit GaLore-Adam (up to 32.69%), demonstrating robustness to second-moment factorization and low-precision state storage.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on GaLore's dominant-subspace low-rank gradient projection and Fira's use of the low-rank approximation residual, positioning SARA as a plug-and-play replacement for the subspace selection step. It departs from the dominant-subspace paradigm by introducing importance sampling over singular vectors (weighted by singular values), which contrasts with GoLore's uniform random projection and the deterministic top-r selection used by GaLore and Q-GaLore. The theoretical analysis follows GoLore's convergence framework for MSGD with momentum re-projection but replaces the uniform-sampling parameter r/m with a data-dependent minimum sampling probability δ, yielding a comparable but slightly weaker rate.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The frozen-dominant-subspace observation provides a concrete mechanistic explanation for why low-rank optimizers underperform full-rank Adam in practice, and SARA offers a minimal-overhead fix (0.0005s sampling cost) that is compatible with multiple optimizer backends (Adam, Adafactor, Adam-mini, 8-bit). However, the convergence rate is strictly worse than GoLore's (δ < r/m), the experiments are limited to LLaMA up to 1.1B parameters with no error bars, and the absolute PPL gap to full-rank Adam remains substantial (e.g., 30.47 vs 27.71 at 60M). Open questions include whether the importance-sampling distribution can be further adapted (e.g., per-layer or per-phase), whether the approach scales to 7B+ models where memory pressure is most acute, and whether the frozen-subspace phenomenon interacts with other efficiency techniques like quantization or sparsity in non-trivial ways.
