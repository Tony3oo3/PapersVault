## Problem

In Vertical Federated Learning (VFL), ultrahigh-dimensional features (p growing exponentially with n) combined with rare events (n₁/n → 0) make existing feature selection methods prohibitively expensive in encryption, communication, and computation, while invalidating the asymptotic properties of standard screening statistics. No efficient preprocessing step existed to reduce dimensionality before downstream federated modeling, and the rare-event setting was largely unaddressed in the VFL feature screening literature.

## Input / Output

The active party holds binary labels Y ∈ {0,1}^n and generates an n×n label matrix indicating same/different-class pairs, which is encrypted (via Paillier or CKKS) and sent to the passive party holding the p-dimensional feature matrix X ∈ R^{n×p}. The passive party computes encrypted VFS statistics for feature groups or individual features and returns them; the active party decrypts and applies hypothesis tests or thresholds. The output is a retained feature matrix X' ∈ R^{n×d} with d ≪ p, reducing downstream VFL tasks to operate on a condensed feature set. Hyperparameters include subsample ratio s, initial group size s₀, decay coefficient ρ, stage-switching threshold p', and screening threshold δ.

## Method

VFS is a two-stage coarse-to-fine screening algorithm built on a subsampled generalized U-statistic (the VFS statistic) that explicitly separates case and control observations in the kernel to handle extreme class imbalance. Stage 1 partitions features into groups of size s₀ and applies hypothesis testing (via asymptotic normality of group statistics) to rapidly discard irrelevant groups, iteratively shrinking group size by a decay factor ρ until the remaining dimensionality is computationally manageable. Stage 2 performs one-by-one threshold screening on the surviving features using the individual VFS statistic. A key design choice is the paired encryption procedure: rather than encrypting individual labels (which breaks rank-based statistics under homomorphic encryption), the active party encrypts a derived n×n class-pair matrix, enabling the passive party to compute encrypted distance-covariance-type statistics without ever seeing plaintext labels. Theoretical guarantees include screening consistency, explicit convergence rates, and model-size bounds under conditions on feature tails, dimension growth, imbalance rate, and signal separation.

## Results

On four real-world datasets (Activity p=560, Gina p=968, p53 p=5408, RNA-Seq p=20531), VFS+Selection achieves comparable or slightly better Accuracy and AUC while drastically reducing computation time. For example, on RNA-Seq, VFS+LESS-VFL runs in 74.9s versus 1791.1s for LESS-VFL alone (~4% of the time), and VFS+MUSE runs in 30.9s versus 1062.3s (~3%). In the credit modeling application (p=189,236), VFS+SplitNN with only 100 retained features achieves AUC 0.937 versus 0.901 with all features, while reducing total runtime from 1911.8s to 129.4s (~7%). In simulation, VFS achieves PSR of 0.965, 0.985, and 0.856 across three models, consistently outperforming Mutual Information and Chi-squared screening.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In simulation (Table 2), VFS outperforms Mutual Information [75] by a wide margin on all three models (PSR 0.965 vs 0.789, 0.985 vs 0.847, 0.856 vs 0.666) and also beats Chi-squared [34] consistently (0.965 vs 0.904, 0.985 vs 0.949, 0.856 vs 0.712), with the gap largest under the nonlinear Model 3. Against LESS-VFL [7] and MUSE [37] as downstream selection methods (Table 3), VFS+Selection matches or slightly exceeds their Accuracy/AUC while cutting runtime by 29–96%, with the advantage growing sharply as p increases (e.g., 96% time reduction on RNA-Seq with MUSE). In the large-scale credit modeling task (Table 4), VFS+SplitNN [66] with 100 features achieves AUC 0.937 versus 0.901 for SplitNN on all 189,236 features, while reducing total time from 1911.8s to 129.4s (~7%). Overall, VFS wins or ties on predictive performance across all comparisons while delivering order-of-magnitude computational savings, particularly in ultrahigh-dimensional settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three established lines of work: model-free feature screening via generalized U-statistics (distance correlation, projection correlation), rare-event handling through subsampling in imbalanced classification, and privacy-preserving computation in vertical federated learning. It departs from prior VFL feature selection methods (LESS-VFL, MUSE, FedSDG-FS) by replacing iterative joint-modeling-based selection with a two-stage marginal screening procedure that operates before any downstream model is trained. The paired encryption procedure—converting labels into a class-agreement matrix amenable to additive homomorphic encryption—is a novel technical contribution that makes distance-based and rank-based screening statistics computable under Paillier or CKKS, which prior VFL work had not addressed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial for VFL deployments with ultrahigh-dimensional features (e.g., 189K transaction features in the credit modeling application), where VFS reduces total runtime to under 10% while maintaining or improving predictive AUC. The rare-event adaptation is important because standard screening statistics lose validity when the minority class is extremely scarce, a common scenario in fraud detection and disease prediction. Key limitations include the marginal (not conditional) nature of screening, which may retain redundant features, and the dependence of computational efficiency on the specific kernel function chosen. An open direction the authors highlight is combining homomorphic encryption with differential privacy to strengthen resilience against inference attacks on the encrypted statistics.
