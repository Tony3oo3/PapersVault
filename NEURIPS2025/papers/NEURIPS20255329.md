## Problem

Prior work established that fixed-precision transformers with unique hard attention (UHA) are exactly as expressive as LTL[P,F,S,U], and that soft-attention transformers are upper-bounded by C-RASP, but the exact expressive power of the most realistic idealization—fixed-precision transformers with soft attention, strict future masking, and no positional encodings—remained open. This paper closes that gap by providing a precise, two-sided characterization of what this class can and cannot recognize.

## Input / Output

For language recognition, the model takes a string w over a finite alphabet Σ, appends an EOS token, and produces a scalar score (accept if positive). For language modeling, it takes a prefix w_{<n} and outputs a probability distribution over the next symbol. All computations are restricted to a finite set of floating-point values (e.g., 32-bit), and attention is strictly future-masked so each position can only attend to earlier positions, with no positional encodings provided.

## Method

The paper proves that this transformer class is exactly as expressive as LTL[P], a fragment of linear temporal logic containing only the past operator P. The proof proceeds in two directions: every transformer computation can be encoded as a PFO²[<] formula (the past fragment of two-variable first-order logic), and every LTL[P] formula can be simulated by a transformer. The key technical challenge is that under fixed precision, softmax attention weights vanish beyond a bounded span N_max, so the authors design a two-layer construction using logical conjunction to cap the number of attended positions and recover correct P-operator semantics without increasing numerical precision. They further show LTL[P] is equivalent to partially ordered DFAs (PODFAs), R-trivial monoids, and left-deterministic polynomials, yielding a unified algebraic characterization. Empirically, length-generalization experiments confirm the theory: transformers achieve 100% accuracy on all LTL[P]-definable languages but consistently fail on languages requiring more expressive power such as bounded Dyck languages and parity.

## Results

On language recognition (trained on strings up to length 40, tested on lengths 41–500), the fixed-precision transformer with soft attention and NoPE achieves 100% max accuracy on all five left-deterministic polynomial languages (PT-2, LT-1, LDP-2, LDP-1, FIRST) but consistently fails on every language class above it: 90.0% max on RDP-1 (unambiguous polynomials), 87.7% on DYCK-(1,1) (star-free), and 52.1% on PARITY (regular). The LSTM baseline achieves 100% max accuracy on all 12 languages, confirming the tasks are learnable and isolating the transformer's failures to an expressivity limitation rather than optimization. In language modeling, the transformer LM reaches perfect per-token accuracy on LDP-1 and LDP-2 but plateaus at 98.3% on RDP-1, with hidden-state visualizations showing the model cannot linearly separate the DFA states of the non-partially-ordered automaton.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole experimental baseline is an LSTM, included to confirm that all 12 language tasks are learnable given the training data (strings up to length 40). The LSTM achieves 100% max accuracy on every language, including counter languages and parity, which the transformer cannot recognize. The transformer, by contrast, achieves 100% max accuracy exclusively on the five left-deterministic polynomial languages and fails on all seven languages in the four higher complexity classes (counter, regular, star-free, unambiguous polynomials), with gaps ranging from 10 percentage points (RDP-1: 90.0% vs. 100%) to nearly 50 points (PARITY: 52.1% vs. 100%). Notably, the transformer uses single-precision (32-bit) floating point and string lengths never exceed the maximum attention span, so the failures are not attributable to numerical underflow or limited attention range. The LSTM's consistent 100% performance rules out data insufficiency or task ambiguity as explanations, cleanly attributing the transformer's failures to the theoretical expressivity boundary predicted by the LTL[P]/PODFA characterization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper closes a gap left by Yang and Chiang [49], who showed that fixed-precision transformers with soft attention are upper-bounded by C-RASP but left the precise characterization open. It builds on Yang et al. [50], who characterized fixed-precision transformers with unique hard attention (UHA) as exactly LTL[P,F,S,U] (all star-free languages), and on Chiang et al. [9] and Li et al. [27] for the logical simulation techniques. The key departure is showing that replacing UHA with the more realistic soft attention collapses expressivity from the full LTL[P,F,S,U] to the much smaller LTL[P] fragment (only the past operator), and connecting this to PODFAs, R-trivial monoids, and left-deterministic polynomials for a unified algebraic characterization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first exact characterization of the most realistic transformer idealization (fixed precision, soft attention, strict masking, NoPE), and it reveals a dramatic expressivity gap: languages like bounded Dyck, previously shown recognizable under more permissive assumptions, are provably beyond the reach of these models. The empirical validation is strong—transformers achieve 100% length generalization on all LTL[P]-definable languages and consistently fail on languages just outside the class, even under favorable conditions (32-bit precision, lengths within attention span). Open questions include the precise role of positional encodings (addressed in an appendix) and whether the PODFA/LTL[P] boundary shifts under other architectural choices like non-strict masking or multiple heads.
