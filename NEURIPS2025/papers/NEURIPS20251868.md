## Problem

LLMs overfit superficial statistical patterns (spurious correlations) rather than learning underlying causal relationships, leading to biases and hallucinations. Existing remedies either indiscriminately fine-tune all parameters—causing parameter inefficiency, overfitting, and catastrophic forgetting—or rely on post-processing techniques with limited adaptability. The paper addresses the gap of selectively identifying and optimizing only the model components that encode causal reasoning, preserving pretrained knowledge while enhancing robustness.

## Input / Output

The method consumes original training samples alongside LLM-generated counterfactual variants (label/answer-flipped) and paraphrased variants (semantically preserved), along with the full set of weight matrices from a target LLM. It produces two outputs: (1) a subset of causality-sensitive parameters updated via PiSSA low-rank adaptation constrained to the top-r principal subspace, and (2) a six-dimensional logistic regression model that maps gradient-difference and activation-similarity features to causal-component probabilities. The reward signal is a weighted composite of accuracy, robustness (counterfactual accuracy + paraphrase hidden-state cosine similarity), calibration (ECE + Brier Score), and sequence-level confidence (geometric mean of token probabilities).

## Method

CDRO operates in three coupled stages. First, SOTA LLMs (LLaMA-3-70B, GPT-4o) generate counterfactual and paraphrased variants of each training sample, with quality assessed via multi-dimensional LLM scoring. Second, causal parameter localization compares loss-gradient differences (G_j^1, G_j^2) and hidden-state cosine similarities (S^1, S^2) between original/counterfactual and counterfactual/paraphrased samples for every weight matrix, feeding a six-dimensional feature vector into a logistic regression model that predicts the probability each matrix encodes causal relationships. Third, an enhanced REINFORCE++ algorithm optimizes only the identified causal components, where the advantage function incorporates reward-ranking information (a linear decay based on within-batch reward rank) for scale-invariant, stable updates; PiSSA restricts gradient computation to the principal subspace for memory efficiency. The logistic regression model is concurrently updated in real time via standard REINFORCE, using the LLM's validation-set performance delta as its reward, creating a closed-loop collaborative optimization between localization and policy improvement.

## Results

On hallucination mitigation (LLaMA-2-7B), CDRO achieves the highest Acc@50 and Cov@p across all five NLG benchmarks: NQ (0.376/0.228), SciQ (0.781/0.245), TriviaQA (0.520/0.258), TruthfulQA (0.456/0.529), and WikiQA (0.665/0.627), surpassing the strongest baseline HADEMIF by up to 28% relative on Cov@50 for WikiQA (0.627 vs. 0.338). On debiasing (RoBERTa-base), CDRO attains the lowest SEAT scores (e.g., 0.04 gender / 0.06 race on SST-2) while simultaneously achieving the highest downstream accuracy (96.5% on SST-2, 58.7% Mcc on CoLA, 93.7% on QNLI). On OOD generalization (RoBERTa-base), CDRO yields average improvements of 1.16% over the strongest baseline (PCFR) and 7.00% over vanilla fine-tuning across IMDB-Cont, IMDB-CAD, HANS, AdvNLI, and PAWS-QQP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CDRO outperforms all 22 baselines across every evaluated metric and task. In debiasing (Table 1), CDRO achieves the lowest SEAT scores on all three NLU tasks for all three model architectures (e.g., 0.04/0.06 gender/race on SST-2 with RoBERTa vs. 0.06/0.09 for PCFR and 0.09/0.10 for Causal-Debias), while also attaining the highest downstream accuracy (96.5% SST-2 vs. 93.9% Causal-Debias). In hallucination mitigation (Table 2), CDRO surpasses the strongest calibration-based baseline (Calibration Tuning) by 16.6% on NQ Acc@50 (0.376 vs. 0.310) and the strongest hallucination-specific baseline (HADEMIF) by 5.9% on NQ Acc@50 (0.376 vs. 0.355) and 88% on WikiQA Cov@50 (0.627 vs. 0.338). In OOD generalization (Table 3), CDRO leads PCFR (the strongest OOD baseline) by 1.11% on IMDB-Cont (89.62% vs. 88.51%), 0.87% on HANS (77.68% vs. 76.64%), and 1.39% on PAWS (46.01% vs. 44.62%), while outperforming vanilla fine-tuning by up to 10.95% on AdvNLI (39.40% vs. 31.22%). Notably, unlike many debiasing baselines that degrade downstream accuracy (e.g., Context-Debias drops SST-2 to 80.3% with RoBERTa), CDRO simultaneously improves both debiasing and task performance, validating its targeted parameter optimization strategy.

## Relation to Prior Work

CDRO sits at the intersection of three research threads: causal debiasing (Causal-Debias, Causal Effect Tuning), knowledge localization in transformers (Knowledge Neurons, DEPN, causal probing), and RL-based alignment (REINFORCE++, PPO, DPO). It departs from prior causal debiasing methods by rejecting indiscriminate full-parameter fine-tuning in favor of selectively optimizing only those weight matrices identified as causality-sensitive via training-dynamics comparisons across counterfactual and paraphrased samples. The localization mechanism—a lightweight logistic regression model fed with gradient-difference and activation-similarity features—is itself updated in real time via REINFORCE, creating a collaborative loop absent from prior work. For the optimization step, the paper builds directly on REINFORCE++ (adding reward-ranking-based advantage estimation) and PiSSA (constraining updates to principal subspaces of identified matrices).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The selective-optimization strategy addresses a practical tension in LLM robustness research: full fine-tuning risks catastrophic forgetting and overfitting, while post-processing (calibration, self-consistency) offers limited adaptability. By targeting only causality-relevant parameters, CDRO preserves pretrained knowledge while improving debiasing, hallucination resistance, and OOD generalization simultaneously across 12 models. Open questions include whether the logistic-regression localization generalizes to tasks with very different causal structures, the computational cost of generating high-quality counterfactual/paraphrased data via SOTA LLMs at scale, and whether the approach extends to multimodal or very large (100B+) models where per-matrix gradient tracking becomes prohibitive.
