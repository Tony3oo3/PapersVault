## Problem

Reconstructing dynamic 3D scenes from casually captured, uncalibrated monocular video remains prohibitively slow, with prior methods like RoDynRF requiring over two days of optimization and 4D Gaussian Splatting overfitting in monocular settings where scene regions are only briefly visible. The paper addresses this by combining efficient geometric initialization with a streamlined 4D Gaussian representation to compress reconstruction time from hours to minutes while maintaining competitive rendering quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251005/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a single uncalibrated monocular video sequence (e.g., 512×512 at 30 FPS, 5 seconds to several minutes) with no camera poses, intrinsics, or depth sensors provided. It produces a compact 4D Gaussian scene representation (under 1 GB for typical sequences) that supports real-time novel view rendering at arbitrary viewpoints and timestamps, along with estimated camera extrinsics, intrinsics, and temporally consistent depth maps as intermediate outputs. The Lite variant completes a full 5-second video reconstruction in under 2 minutes on a single A6000 GPU.

## Method

INSTANT4D pipelines MegaSAM for camera pose and metric depth recovery, refines depth to temporal consistency, back-projects pixels into a dense point cloud, then applies an adaptive voxel grid pruning that reduces the model to under 10% of its original footprint while preserving occlusion structure. The 4D Gaussian primitive is simplified to an isotropic form (identity rotation, two scalar scales for spatial and temporal extent) with per-Gaussian RGB instead of high-order sphericylindrical harmonics, cutting parameter count by over 60%. A motion-aware mechanism uses SLAM-derived motion probability masks to assign a large temporal scale to static Gaussians so they persist across the full video duration, while dynamic Gaussians are culled by a temporal opacity threshold. This combination of grid pruning, isotropic parameterization, and motion-aware temporal scaling yields a 30× speed-up over prior 4DGS methods with 92% memory reduction and competitive PSNR on DyCheck and NVIDIA benchmarks.

## Results

On the DyCheck iPhone benchmark, INSTANT4D (Full) achieves 24.52 dB average PSNR in 7.2 minutes with 8 GB peak memory, outperforming the best uncalibrated baseline RoDyGS (17.37 dB) by 7.15 dB and even exceeding Deform3D (22.63 dB, which uses ground-truth poses) by 1.89 dB. The Lite variant reaches 23.02 dB in just 0.03 h (≈2 min) with 1.1 GB memory. On the NVIDIA Dynamic Scene dataset, the method achieves 23.99 dB PSNR with a 0.02 h training time and 822 FPS rendering at 480×270, compared to 18.34 dB for the 4DGS baseline and 22.56 dB for InstantSplat.

## Baselines & Comparisons

INSTANT4D (Full) decisively outperforms all uncalibrated baselines on DyCheck: it beats RoDyGS [5] by 7.15 dB (24.52 vs 17.37), RoDynRF without poses [12] by 9.62 dB, and 4DGS [35] by 2.88 dB, while training 10–300× faster. It also surpasses Deform3D [42] (22.63 dB), which relies on ground-truth camera poses, by 1.89 dB. On the NVIDIA dataset, the method achieves 23.99 dB PSNR, beating the 4DGS* baseline (18.34 dB) by 5.65 dB and InstantSplat* (22.56 dB) by 1.43 dB, while rendering at 822 FPS versus 98 and 117 FPS respectively. The Lite variant already exceeds all pose-free methods on DyCheck (23.02 dB vs 17.37 dB for RoDyGS) in under 2 minutes. The paper notes that RoDynRF [12] and DynamicNeRF [3] report higher PSNR on NVIDIA (25.89 and 26.10 dB) but require COLMAP calibration and 22–74 hours of training, making them impractical for casual video. Ablations confirm that removing the motion-aware Gaussian strategy costs 3.4 dB and removing the isotropic formulation costs 1.25 dB, validating each design choice.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on 4D Gaussian Splatting (4DGS) [41] and 3DGS [6] for the rendering backbone, and on MegaSAM [11] (itself extending DROID-SLAM) for geometric recovery from casual video. Its key departures are threefold: replacing high-order sphericylindrical harmonics with per-Gaussian RGB, adopting an isotropic (identity-rotation, scalar-scale) covariance instead of the full anisotropic form, and introducing a motion-aware temporal scale that distinguishes static from dynamic primitives. A grid-pruning initialization step further reduces the Gaussian count by ~92%, eliminating the need for the conventional densification stage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By compressing a multi-hour 4D reconstruction pipeline into under 10 minutes with <1 GB peak memory, INSTANT4D makes dynamic novel-view synthesis from casual phone video practically accessible for AR/VR content creation and interactive applications. The finding that isotropic Gaussians and plain RGB outperform their more expressive anisotropic/SCH counterparts in monocular settings challenges the default assumption that higher parameterization is always beneficial under limited observations. Open questions include scalability to multi-minute sequences (current SLAM memory grows linearly), robustness to reflective/transparent surfaces, and whether the simplified representation generalizes to multi-view or calibrated settings where the monocular overfitting pressure is absent.
