## Problem

Traditional GNN-based graph anomaly detection suffers from over-smoothing that averages out anomalous signals and from discriminative decision boundaries that camouflaged fraudsters can cross. While diffusion models offer iterative refinement and denoising reconstruction to address both issues, directly applying them to GAD faces two unresolved challenges: recovering true anomaly distributions from random noise is unreliable due to deceptive node tactics, and iterative denoising over millions of nodes is computationally prohibitive.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an attributed graph (d-dimensional node feature matrix X and adjacency matrix A) along with a pre-trained lightweight anomaly prior g_φ(E,X) — a per-node scalar estimated by a Random Forest or XGBoost model on labeled training data. The output is a per-node anomaly score vector y_0 produced after K reverse diffusion steps, where K is adaptively determined per node based on prior confidence. The diffusion operates on the anomaly score space (one scalar per node), not on the graph topology or node features directly.

## Method

CGADM reformulates GAD as a generative task that models the joint conditional distribution p(y_0 | E, X) via a conditional evidence lower bound. The key architectural insight is a prior-guided diffusion process: the forward chain interpolates from ground-truth anomaly labels toward a pre-trained anomaly prior N(g_φ(E,X), I) rather than N(0,I), and the reverse chain starts from this prior, making the denoising target a correction of the prior rather than recovery from pure noise. The denoising network is a GCNII-inspired GNN with residual propagation (h_v^l = σ(W^l(h_v^{l-1} - mean_neighbor(h^{l-1}))) aggregated across layers) that preserves high-frequency anomaly signals against over-smoothing. For efficiency, a prior confidence-aware strided sampling mechanism uses a conditional non-Markovian (DDIM-style) reverse process where the number of sampling steps K is inversely proportional to |g_φ - 0.5|, reducing average steps from 1000 to ~583 with minimal accuracy loss.

## Results

On the five primary benchmark datasets (Elliptic, Tolokers, YelpChi, Question, Reddit), CGADM achieves an average AUPRC of 48.78% and average AUROC of 81.39%, outperforming the strongest baselines by 2.83 percentage points in AUPRC (vs. XGBGraph at 45.95%) and 2.61 percentage points in AUROC (vs. CGenGA at 78.78%). The largest gains appear on Elliptic (AUPRC 97.03 vs. 90.47 for XGBGraph) and YelpChi (AUPRC 76.54 vs. 75.91 for XGBGraph). On four additional datasets (Weibo, Amazon, T-Finance, T-Social), CGADM further achieves AUPRC of 0.9735, 0.9191, 0.9154, and 0.9408 respectively, consistently surpassing both XGBGraph and CONSISGAD.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CGADM outperforms all 12 baselines on every dataset and both metrics (AUPRC and AUROC). Against standard GNNs (GCN, GIN, GraphSAGE, GAT), CGADM leads by 9–16 AUPRC points on average, with the gap most pronounced on YelpChi where GCN scores only 23.59 AUPRC versus CGADM's 76.54. Among GAD-specific methods, XGBGraph is the closest competitor (45.95 avg AUPRC, 77.79 avg AUROC), yet CGADM still leads by 2.83 and 3.60 points respectively; notably, XGBGraph's advantage on Elliptic AUROC (94.35) is reversed by CGADM (99.34). The diffusion-based data-augmentation baselines GODM (40.89 AUPRC) and CGenGA (41.23 AUPRC) trail CGADM by 7–8 AUPRC points on average, confirming that directly modeling the joint anomaly distribution via conditional diffusion is more effective than generating synthetic anomalies. On the four additional datasets, CGADM maintains its lead over both XGBGraph and CONSISGAD in AUPRC across all four, though XGBGraph edges out CGADM in AUROC on Weibo (0.9937 vs. 0.9879) and T-Social (0.9914 vs. 0.9761).

## Relation to Prior Work

CGADM repurposes the DDPM framework (Ho et al., 2020) for a fundamentally different purpose: rather than generating graph structures or augmenting training data (as GODM and CGenGA do), it models the joint anomaly score distribution directly. The key conceptual departure is the prior-guided diffusion process, which borrows from DiffuseVAE (Pandey et al., 2022) the idea of diffusing toward a learned non-Gaussian endpoint, but applies it to a pre-trained anomaly estimator rather than a VAE latent. The denoising network incorporates GCNII-style residual propagation (Chen et al., 2020) to preserve high-frequency anomaly signals, and the inference stage adopts DDIM's (Song et al., 2021) non-Markovian reverse process to enable step-skipping. This positions CGADM as a model-centric generative approach, in contrast to both discriminative GNNs and diffusion-based data augmentation methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that diffusion models can serve as a principled generative framework for anomaly detection rather than merely a data augmentation tool, offering a new paradigm that is inherently more robust to feature obfuscation by adversarial nodes. The prior-aware strided sampling mechanism (reducing average steps from 1000 to 583 with only ~3% AUPRC loss) makes the approach practical for large-scale graphs, addressing a key scalability barrier for diffusion-based methods. However, the method remains in a supervised setting and relies on a pre-trained prior estimator, limiting its applicability to unsupervised or dynamically evolving graph scenarios. Open questions include whether the prior-guided formulation can be extended to semi-supervised or fully unsupervised GAD, and how the approach behaves under truly adversarial distribution shifts in the graph topology itself.
