## Problem

Reasoning LLMs like DeepSeek-R1 and OpenAI-o1 generate long chain-of-thought traces that improve accuracy but incur high inference latency and memory costs. Existing Long2Short compression methods either sacrifice accuracy through aggressive truncation (the 'reasoning oversimplification dilemma') or require expensive online RL sampling at each training step. The paper addresses how to compress reasoning traces while preserving performance, using exclusively self-generated data without external teacher models or manually curated short-answer datasets.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a text prompt containing a reasoning problem (math or code) and produces a compressed reasoning trace enclosed in <think> tags followed by a final answer in <answer> tags. During data construction, the base model self-samples N diverse traces at temperature 0.9; during refinement, each step is resampled at temperature 1.0 to generate 64 candidate rewrites. The KL divergence constraint is evaluated over a fixed 512-token window of subsequent reasoning steps, and the maximum generation limit during evaluation is 32,768 tokens.

## Method

TokenSqueeze is a three-stage offline preference-learning pipeline. First, adaptive reasoning depth selection uses a dynamic quantile q = α(1−p) (where p is the per-problem correctness rate and α=0.2) to choose correct traces whose length naturally scales with problem difficulty, avoiding both over-compression on hard problems and redundancy on easy ones. Second, intra-step linguistic refinement resamples 64 candidate rewrites per reasoning step and selects the shortest one satisfying a KL divergence constraint (ε=0.005) against the original step's downstream token distribution, approximated over a 512-token window. Third, the model is trained with a composite objective combining length-aware DPO (DPO-L, which adds a log(ℓ(y_l)/ℓ(y_w)) margin term that adaptively scales preference strength by relative length difference) with supervised fine-tuning on positive examples, weighted equally at η=0.5 to stabilize training and prevent reward collapse.

## Results

On DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B, TokenSqueeze achieves a 51.3% reduction in average correct-response length on MATH500 (3638→1773 tokens) while maintaining 92.4% accuracy (vs. 92.8% baseline), and a 31.6% reduction on AIME24 (7543→5157 tokens) while *improving* accuracy from 55.5% to 57.5%. On LiveCodeBench, it boosts accuracy from 31.3% to 35.0% while cutting average response length by 24.4%. AUC under a 32K token budget improves across all four benchmarks, e.g., from 41.6% to 48.5% on AIME24 and from 83.6% to 87.5% on MATH500 for the 7B model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Kimi-k1.5 (DPO, reproduced), TokenSqueeze wins on accuracy across nearly all benchmarks while achieving comparable compression: on 7B MATH500 it scores 92.4% vs. 88.2% (+4.2 pp) with similar token reduction (51.3% vs. 53.3%), and on 7B AIME24 it reaches 57.5% vs. 51.2% (+6.3 pp). On LiveCodeBench the gap is largest: 35.0% vs. 24.8% (+10.2 pp) for the 7B model, indicating Kimi-k1.5's aggressive length penalty severely harms coding accuracy. Against DAST, TokenSqueeze matches or exceeds accuracy on AIME24 (57.5% vs. 53.3%) and LiveCodeBench (35.0% vs. 29.7%) while compressing tokens far more aggressively on MATH500 (51.3% vs. 23.0% reduction). Against TrainEffi, TokenSqueeze edges out on AIME24 accuracy (57.5% vs. 56.0%) and MATH500 (92.4% vs. 92.3%) with stronger Len-T compression. Overall, TokenSqueeze is the only method that simultaneously improves or matches accuracy *and* achieves the largest token reductions and highest AUC scores across all four benchmarks and both model sizes.

## Relation to Prior Work

TokenSqueeze extends the DPO framework (Rafailov et al., NeurIPS 2023) with a length-aware margin term, positioning itself within the offline preference-learning paradigm for reasoning compression. It departs from prior offline methods (Kimi-k1.5 DPO, DAST, Sky-T1-Flash) that simply select the shortest correct traces by introducing adaptive quantile-based depth selection and KL-constrained intra-step rewriting, and from rephrasing methods (C3oT, TokenSkip) that rely on external LLMs or rule-based pruning. The key conceptual departure is framing Long2Short as a preference learning problem over self-generated data rather than a reward-penalized RL problem or a dataset curation task, combining three components (adaptive selection, distribution-aligned refinement, length-aware DPO) into a unified pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 50% average token reduction on MATH500 while preserving accuracy addresses a critical deployment bottleneck for reasoning LLMs, and the self-generated-data-only design removes dependence on expensive external teacher models, improving scalability. However, the method remains offline-only, the KL threshold ε is set heuristically, and generalization beyond Qwen-based distillations of DeepSeek-R1 is untested. Open questions include whether the approach extends to online RL settings, whether adaptive ε tuning can replace the fixed threshold, and whether the compression gains hold for larger models or non-mathematical reasoning domains.
