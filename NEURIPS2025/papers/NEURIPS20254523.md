## Problem

Conventional world models in model-based RL process observations holistically, failing to decouple small dynamic objects (e.g., a 1-pixel ball in Pong) from static backgrounds, which are often decision-irrelevant. This holistic processing is computationally wasteful and causes both RSSM- and transformer-based architectures to under-model the precise temporal dynamics that drive rewards, while existing fixes (object-centric segmentation, diffusion-based reconstruction) introduce heavy computational overhead or require prior object annotations.

## Input / Output

The method consumes high-dimensional RGB image observations (e.g., 84×84 Atari frames, 84×84 DMC visual observations) and discrete or continuous actions, producing policy actions, predicted rewards, and episode continuation flags. A key input detail is the construction of differential observations: a binary inter-frame differencing mask (threshold ε=0.001, interval k=1) is dilated via convolution to expand regions of interest, then multiplied element-wise with the original frame to isolate dynamic components. The latent state is a concentration vector s_t = {h_t, z_t, d_t} where z_t and d_t are each 32×32 categorical VAE codes (32 categories × 32 classes), and h_t is the GRU hidden state.

## Method

DyMoDreamer augments the DreamerV3 RSSM with a dynamic modulation pathway: a dedicated dynamic encoder processes differential observations o'_t (frame-differenced and mask-dilated) to produce stochastic categorical modulators d_t, which are fed into the GRU sequence model alongside the standard stochastic representations z_t and actions, so that the recurrent hidden state h_t explicitly encodes temporal change. The decoder reconstructs observations from {h_t, z_t, d_t}, and the modulators are predicted by the sequence model via a dynamics loss (KL between prior and posterior of d_t) and a representation loss, enabling end-to-end joint training without separate modulator pretraining. A novel differential divergence regularization term computes KL between softmax-normalized frame differences of reconstructed and ground-truth observations, forcing the model to capture inter-frame variation beyond what MSE reconstruction provides. Policy learning uses a standard actor-critic on imagined trajectories over the concentration state, with the backward-differencing requirement meaning a random initial action is sampled to bootstrap the first differential observation.

## Results

DyMoDreamer sets a new state-of-the-art for RNN-based world models across three major benchmarks. On Atari 100k, it achieves a mean human-normalized score (HNS) of 156.6% and median HNS of 71.3%, surpassing the previous best (DIAMOND at 146% mean). On the DeepMind Visual Control Suite (20 continuous control tasks, 1M steps), it reaches a task mean of 832 and median of 871, outperforming TWISTER (801.8 mean). On Crafter, it achieves a Return@1M of 10.3, a 9.5% improvement over DreamerV3 (9.4) and substantially ahead of the strongest transformer-based baseline Δ-IRIS (7.7).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DyMoDreamer outperforms all compared baselines on every benchmark. On Atari 100k, it leads DIAMOND (146% HNS) by 10.6 percentage points and OC-STORM (134.8%) by 21.8 points, while also improving the median HNS from 49% (DreamerV3) to 71.3%, indicating more consistent gains across games. On the DeepMind Visual Control Suite, it surpasses TWISTER (801.8) by 30.2 points and DreamerV3 (786) by 46 points in task mean, with particularly large gains on Acrobot Swingup (447 vs. 229) and Finger Spin (968 vs. 589). On Crafter, it exceeds Δ-IRIS (7.7) by 2.6 return and DreamerV3 (9.4) by 0.9 return. The paper also validates generality by integrating dynamic modulation into STORM (a transformer-based world model), showing consistent gains, and reports competitive results on DeepMind Lab (4/5 tasks) and DeepMind Proprio, confirming the mechanism transfers beyond the primary RSSM architecture.

## Relation to Prior Work

DyMoDreamer extends the DreamerV3 RSSM architecture by introducing a dynamic modulation channel derived from inter-frame differencing masks, drawing conceptual inspiration from modulated ODEs and latent flow methods in model-free RL. It departs from prior object-centric approaches (OC-STORM, DIAMOND) that rely on pretrained vision models, diffusion, or segmentation masks to handle dynamic objects, instead using a lightweight binary frame-differencing operation with dilation to isolate motion cues. The method is positioned as a minimal architectural addition to the existing RSSM pipeline—adding a parallel categorical VAE encoder for differential observations and integrating the resulting modulators into the GRU sequence model—rather than a wholesale replacement of the world model backbone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a decades-old computer vision technique (frame differencing) can be repurposed as a structured modulation signal within a recurrent world model to yield meaningful performance gains without additional computational overhead or external vision models. The emergent representational dissociation—where stochastic latents capture static/environmental factors and dynamic modulators capture agent-controllable motion—provides an interpretable decomposition that could inform future world model design. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to highly egocentric or long-horizon tasks requiring symbolic memory (where it shows slight degradation), and whether the dynamic modulation mechanism transfers effectively to transformer-based world models at scale beyond the STORM integration shown in the appendix.
