## Problem

Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) can understand images and videos at a holistic level but struggle with instance-level understanding—comprehending the attributes, relationships, and temporal dynamics of specific objects that users are most interested in. Existing instance-understanding efforts are largely confined to the image domain, leaving video instance understanding under-explored, and there is no instruction-tuning dataset or benchmark that provides fine-grained, instance-grounded annotations spanning both modalities.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251669/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes RGB images or up to 32 video frames, each augmented with Set-of-Marks (SoM) visual prompts that overlay a persistent numeric ID at the center of every instance (derived from segmentation masks via SAM or Semantic-SAM), alongside a text instruction or query. Outputs are structured as multi-turn dialogues covering four granularities: per-instance descriptions, holistic scene captions, frame-to-frame temporal difference descriptions, and video-level summaries, plus open-ended QA pairs that reference specific instance IDs and timestamps. For video inputs, 2×2 spatial pooling reduces visual token count, and the LLM context is capped at 6K tokens.

## Method

INST-IT introduces a continuous instruction-tuning recipe built on the LLaVA-NeXT architecture. An automated GPT-4o annotation pipeline, guided by SoM visual prompts and carefully designed task prompts, generates multi-level instance-grounded annotations (instance captions, image captions, temporal differences, video summaries, and QA pairs) from 51k images and 21k videos, yielding 243k instruction-tuning samples. Training proceeds in two stages: standard supervised fine-tuning on LLaVA-NeXT-DATA, followed by an additional continuous SFT stage that mixes INST-IT Dataset with general data while freezing the first 12 layers of the vision encoder to prevent distribution shift caused by the visually prompted inputs. The key insight is that explicit SoM prompts during both annotation and training force the model to attend to individual instances rather than holistic scene content, and the continuous (rather than from-scratch) paradigm preserves general comprehension while boosting instance-level understanding, yielding ~20% average gains on INST-IT Bench and consistent improvements on generic image and video benchmarks.

## Results

On the proposed INST-IT Bench, LLaVA-NeXT-Inst-IT (Vicuna-7B) achieves 68.6/63.0 (OE/MC) on images and 49.3/42.1 (OE/MC) on videos, a ~20% average improvement over the LLaVA-NeXT baseline (46.0/42.4 on images). On generic image benchmarks, the Qwen2-7B variant gains +12.1 on AI2D and +18.0 on ChartQA over its baseline, while on video benchmarks it improves EgoSchema by +0.4, NExT-QA by +14.6, and VideoMME by +17.8. On RefCOCOg, the model achieves 63.0% vs. 52.2% for the baseline (+10.8%), and on ViP-Bench it reaches 49.0 (human prompts) vs. 46.8 for ViP-LLaVA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On INST-IT Bench, INST-IT (Vicuna-7B) outperforms all open-source baselines by a wide margin: +22.6 OE over LLaVA-NeXT on images, +23.5 OE over LLaVA-NeXT-Video on videos, and +15.1 OE over InternVL2 on videos, while using only 920K training samples versus 7.2–8.8M for LLaVA-OV. On generic image benchmarks, the Qwen2-7B variant surpasses LLaVA-OV on AI2D (78.7 vs. 81.4 is close) and ChartQA (72.8 vs. 80.0), and approaches Qwen2-VL-Instruct (78.7 vs. 83.0 on AI2D) with far less data. On video benchmarks, INST-IT (Vicuna-7B) beats LLaVA-NeXT-Video on EgoSchema (57.8 vs. 43.9) and NExT-QA (70.2 vs. –), and the Qwen2-7B variant exceeds LLaVA-Video on VideoMME (54.0 vs. 63.3 is a loss) but leads on NExT-QA (73.0 vs. 83.2 is also a loss). On ViP-Bench, INST-IT (Vicuna-7B) matches ViP-LLaVA with synthesized prompts (45.1 vs. 45.5) but surpasses it with human-style prompts (48.2 vs. 46.8), all in a zero-shot setting versus ViP-LLaVA's in-domain fine-tuning. The model still trails GPT-4o (74.1/84.8) and Qwen2-VL-72B (55.5/74.7) on INST-IT Bench, and the Vicuna-7B variant shows a marginal −0.1 drop on ActivityNet-QA, indicating that instance-focused tuning does not uniformly help all video QA tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LLaVA-NeXT instruction-tuning paradigm by incorporating Set-of-Marks (SoM) visual prompts as explicit instance-level guidance during training, building directly on the SoM prompting technique introduced by Yang et al. (2023) for GPT-4V grounding. It departs from prior instance-understanding LMMs (ViP-LLaVA, SoM-LLaVA, GPT4ROI) that are limited to the image domain by systematically extending instance-level instruction tuning to video, and from holistic video instruction-tuning datasets (ShareGPT4Video, LLaVA-Video) that lack fine-grained instance annotations. The continuous SFT recipe—freezing early vision encoder layers to prevent distribution shift from visually prompted inputs—represents a practical training insight not present in prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first instance-grounded instruction-tuning dataset and benchmark covering both images and videos, filling a clear gap in the LMM training and evaluation landscape. A notable finding is that instance-level instruction tuning with visual prompts yields substantial gains on generic benchmarks (e.g., +13.5% on ChartQA, +11.8% on NExT-QA), suggesting fine-grained instance comprehension is a foundation for general multimodal understanding rather than a narrow skill. The observation that Qwen2-VL-72B does not substantially outperform its 7B counterpart on instance tasks challenges the assumption that scale alone resolves fine-grained understanding. Limitations include evaluation only on 7B/1.5B models, reliance on GPT-4o for annotation (cost and quality ceiling), and failure cases with severe occlusion or small instances.
