## Problem

Standard counterfactual explanations for algorithmic recourse assume a fixed, perfectly known model, yet in practice institutions regularly retrain or replace models, and the Rashomon Effect guarantees many near-optimal models that can disagree on whether a given recourse action is valid. Existing robust counterfactual methods either rely on expensive mixed-integer solvers, restrict guarantees to small local parameter neighborhoods, or lack formal validity across the full space of near-optimal solutions. No prior method directly leverages the geometry of the Rashomon set itself to produce provably robust recourse.

## Input / Output

Given an input feature vector x₀, the parameters θ of a trained linear model or the last-layer weights of an MLP (with frozen penultimate embedding h(x)), a target decision threshold t, and a Rashomon tolerance ε, ElliCE outputs a counterfactual x_c guaranteed valid for every model in the ellipsoidal approximation of the Rashomon set, along with the worst-case model θ_worst(x_c) that achieves the minimum prediction. Two generation modes are supported: a continuous gradient-based optimizer that explores the full feature space, and a data-supported mode that restricts solutions to training-set points via k-d tree nearest-neighbor search. Optional feature-level constraints (immutability, range, direction, sparsity) can be imposed on the output.

## Method

ElliCE approximates the ε-Rashomon set with an ellipsoid centered at the ERM θ̂ whose shape is determined by the Hessian H = XᵀWX + λI of the ℓ₂-regularized logistic loss, where W is a diagonal matrix of sigmoid-weighted second derivatives. A key closed-form result (Theorem 1) gives the worst-case prediction over this ellipsoid as θ̂ᵀx_c − √(2ε x_cᵀH⁻¹x_c), reducing the robust counterfactual problem to a quadratically constrained quadratic program (QCQP) solvable by gradient descent. The robustness penalty term √(2ε x_cᵀH⁻¹x_c) naturally aligns the recourse direction with the leading eigenvector of H (the direction of greatest model sensitivity), yielding explanations that modify the most influential features. The framework provides formal guarantees of validity across the ellipsoid, uniqueness of the solution, Lipschitz-1 stability in the input, and a monotonic robustness-proximity trade-off, while remaining one to three orders of magnitude faster than MILP-based baselines due to the closed-form inner solution.

## Results

On data-supported tasks (FICO, German Credit), ElliCE achieves perfect robustness (R = 1.00) across all three evaluators (Random Retrain, Rashomon Dropout, AWP) with L2 proximity of 3.48–5.06, while T:Rex drops to R = 0.00–0.01 under Dropout/AWP on FICO. On the continuous Diabetes task, ElliCE reaches R = 0.98–0.99 versus 0.04–0.86 for the best baseline (ROAR), at a modest L2 cost of 2.15–3.22. In runtime, ElliCE generates counterfactuals in under 2 seconds per instance, achieving speedups of 3.9×–6.7× over T:Rex and 36×–685× over Delta-robustness (MILP-based).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ElliCE dominates T:Rex on robustness across nearly all settings: on FICO under Rashomon Dropout and AWP, T:Rex collapses to R = 0.01 and 0.00 respectively, while ElliCE maintains R = 1.00; on Diabetes (continuous), T:Rex achieves only R = 0.04 vs. ElliCE's 0.98. Against Delta-robustness (MILP-based), ElliCE matches or exceeds robustness (1.00 vs. 0.96 on FICO AWP) while being 36×–685× faster in runtime. On the continuous Diabetes task, ElliCE substantially outperforms both PROPLACE (R = 0.98 vs. 0.48 on Retrain) and ROAR (R = 0.98 vs. 0.86 on Retrain, 0.99 vs. 0.40 on Dropout), though baselines achieve slightly lower L2 proximity (1.86–2.01 vs. 2.15–3.22), reflecting the robustness–proximity trade-off formalized in Theorem 5. On German Credit (data-supported), Delta-robustness is competitive with ElliCE (R = 0.98–1.00 vs. 1.00), making this the closest comparison, but ElliCE still wins on speed (0.101 s vs. 9.905 s per instance).

## Relation to Prior Work

ElliCE builds on the Rashomon Effect literature (Breiman; Rudin, Semenova, and collaborators) that characterizes the set of near-optimal models, and on the growing line of robust counterfactual explanation methods (T:Rex, ROAR, PROPLACE, Delta-robustness) that address model multiplicity. However, it departs from these prior approaches in a key way: rather than relying on ensemble agreement, local parameter perturbations, or mixed-integer programming, ElliCE exploits the local Hessian geometry of the loss landscape to approximate the Rashomon set as an ellipsoid, yielding a closed-form worst-case prediction and a tractable convex QCQP. This geometric reformulation is the paper's central conceptual contribution, distinguishing it from both heuristic stability measures and MILP-based certified methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ElliCE offers a principled, provably robust recourse framework that is 1–3 orders of magnitude faster than MILP-based baselines while maintaining proximity and plausibility, making robust counterfactuals practical for high-stakes tabular settings (lending, healthcare, recidivism). The formal guarantees—validity across the ellipsoid, uniqueness, Lipschitz stability, and alignment with principal curvature directions—provide a theoretical foundation that prior robust CE methods lack. Key limitations include the ellipsoidal approximation being local for neural networks (capturing only last-layer multiplicity) and the fact that uniqueness and stability guarantees do not extend from the embedding space to the input space for MLPs. Open questions include extending these guarantees to deeper architectures, incorporating fairness constraints into the robustness objective, and understanding how the ellipsoidal approximation behaves under distribution shift.
