## Problem

Gradient Descent-Ascent (DA) unlearning methods, which perform ascent on forget-set points and descent on retain-set points, are widely used yet empirically unreliable. This paper identifies the overlooked causal mechanism behind their failures: statistical dependencies (even simple correlations) between the forget and retain sets cause DA updates to systematically diverge from the oracle retraining solution, sometimes making the model worse than the original pretrained one.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251510/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a trained model (e.g., ResNet-9 on CIFAR-10 at 32×32 RGB), a forget set F, and the complementary retain set R, and produces an unlearned model whose predictions should match an oracle trained exclusively on R. Unlearning quality is measured via the KLoM metric, which computes the KL-divergence between margin distributions of 100 unlearned models and 100 oracle models, with scores near zero indicating near-perfect unlearning. The theoretical analysis operates on logistic regression with exponential loss and L2 ridge regularization, where the forget set size is parameterized as a fraction α of the retain set per coordinate.

## Method

The paper provides both theoretical and empirical evidence that inter-set correlation is the root cause of DA failure. Theoretically, it derives closed-form solutions via the Lambert-W function for logistic regression under semi-orthogonal data, proving that the DA stationary point lies on the opposite side of the pretrained solution from the oracle (Lemma 3), and that the DA-oracle distance can diverge while the pretrained-oracle distance vanishes (Corollary 1). A 2D nonlinear toy example further shows DA can trap the model in a suboptimal local minimum that retraining would not select. Empirically, the authors evaluate GA and GDA on ResNet-9/18 across CIFAR-10 and ImageNet-Living-17 with forget sets aligned to principal components of the influence matrix, demonstrating that DA either fails to move from the pretrained initialization or severely degrades model performance, and that selecting the best hyperparameter run after seeing results creates a false 'Ascent Forgets Illusion.'

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with ResNet-9, both Gradient Ascent (GA) and Gradient Descent/Ascent (GDA) fail to achieve meaningful unlearning: most runs either remain near the pretrained initialization (high KLoM on the forget set) or severely degrade overall model performance (high KLoM on retain/validation). The KLoM metric (0 = perfect unlearning) shows that even for small random forget sets (10 samples), successful-looking runs require instance-specific hyperparameter tuning, creating an 'Ascent Forgets Illusion.' Theoretically, Lemma 3 proves that in logistic regression the DA solution always lies on the opposite side of the pretrained solution from the oracle, and Corollary 1 shows the oracle–DA distance diverges to infinity as regularization λ→0 while the oracle–pretrained distance vanishes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not propose a new unlearning method; instead it analyzes and critiques two widely used baselines—Gradient Ascent (GA) and Gradient Descent/Ascent (GDA)—against the oracle (retrained from scratch on the retain set) and the pretrained model. On CIFAR-10 (ResNet-9), both GA and GDA consistently fail: for structurally correlated forget sets (top principal components of the influence matrix), zero runs show any unlearning signal, while for random sets some GA runs improve forget-set KLoM but at the cost of significant model degradation. The pretrained model, while not unlearned, often outperforms the DA-unlearned model on overall test performance, meaning DA can be actively worse than doing nothing. The MNIST/FashionMNIST comparison (Table 1) reveals that these highly correlated datasets are degenerate for unlearning evaluation, as pretrained and oracle KLoM gaps remain small (≤4.13) even at 10% forget sets, making any method appear to succeed by doing very little. The theoretical analysis (Lemmas 3–5) further shows that in logistic regression, the DA minimizer is provably farther from the oracle than the pretrained solution for a range of forget-set sizes, confirming that the failure is structural rather than a tuning issue.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the empirical evaluation framework and KLoM metric from Georgiev et al. [38], which first documented that gradient-ascent unlearning is unreliable, and on the exponential-loss logistic regression analysis of Soudry et al. [43]. It departs from prior work by identifying inter-set statistical dependence (even simple correlations) as the causal mechanism behind DA failures, providing closed-form Lambert-W solutions and divergence proofs in convex settings that were previously only observed empirically. The paper positions itself as a diagnostic study of an existing method family (Descent-Ascent unlearning) rather than proposing a new unlearning algorithm, explicitly noting that its critique does not apply to influence-function or certified-unlearning approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that DA unlearning can produce solutions *farther* from the oracle than the original pretrained model—rendering unlearning actively detrimental—challenges the widespread assumption that ascent-on-forget plus descent-on-retain is a sound unlearning strategy. Open questions include extending the theoretical analysis beyond logistic regression to multi-layer networks, quantifying which specific correlation structures are most harmful, and developing ascent-based methods that explicitly account for inter-set dependence. The paper suggests that rewind-based or noisy stochastic methods may be more robust alternatives when dataset properties are unknown, and provides practical diagnostics (oracle-pretrained KLoM gap, sensitivity to early stopping) for practitioners evaluating unlearning experiments.
