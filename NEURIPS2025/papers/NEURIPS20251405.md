## Problem

Existing backdoor attacks against dataset condensation (Naive Attack, Doorping, Simple, Relax) optimize attack success rate or clean test accuracy but largely neglect stealthiness, leaving visible artifacts in both the condensed data and triggered inputs. This paper tackles the underexplored problem of jointly balancing ASR, CTA, and STE in distribution-matching-based condensation, where the small size of condensed datasets makes them especially susceptible to manual and statistical inspection.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251405/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an original RGB image dataset (e.g., CIFAR-10 at 32×32, STL-10 at 96×96, Tiny-ImageNet at 64×64) and a poison ratio ρ as input. It first identifies the most vulnerable source-to-target class pair via a surrogate model's confusion matrix, then produces a poisoned condensed dataset of 50 synthetic images per class in which triggered samples (perturbed by an ℓ∞-bounded, input-aware generator G_φ with scale α) are relabeled as the target class and re-condensed via MMD minimization. The output is both the compromised condensed dataset and the trained trigger generator, which can be applied at inference time to activate the backdoor.

## Method

SNEAKDOOR operates in two stages. In Trigger Generation, a surrogate model is trained on a clean condensed dataset to identify the class pair (y_s, y_τ) with the highest inter-class misclassification rate; a generator G_φ is then adversarially trained to produce input-aware perturbations bounded by ‖G_φ(x)‖∞ < ε that shift predictions from y_s to y_τ, with the constraint enforced via clamping rather than an explicit penalty. In Backdoor Injection, the generated perturbations are applied to source-class samples, relabeled as the target class, and mixed into the target class at ratio ρ before re-condensation via MMD-based distribution matching, ensuring the synthetic set preserves the target class distribution while encoding backdoor behavior. The key differentiator is that triggers are tailored to each sample's local feature geometry (rather than fixed patches), and the attack targets the most error-prone decision boundary to limit collateral accuracy loss. The paper further provides RKHS-based theoretical guarantees bounding the feature-manifold deviation (Theorem 3) and the MMD discrepancy between clean and poisoned condensed datasets (Theorem 4), formalizing why the perturbations remain distributionally undetectable.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with DM condensation, SNEAKDOOR achieves 62.6% CTA and 98.9% ASR, outperforming DOORPING (62.1% CTA / 98.8% ASR) and substantially exceeding SIMPLE (58.4% CTA / 59.0% ASR) and RELAX (57.4% CTA / 100.0% ASR) in the CTA-ASR balance. Across all five datasets and four condensation methods (DM, DC, IDM, DAM), SNEAKDOOR maintains ASR ≥ 97.3% in 19 of 20 settings while keeping CTA within 1–3% of the best baseline. On stealth metrics (STL-10), SNEAKDOOR achieves the highest PSNR (73.23 dB) and SSIM (0.662) among all methods, compared to 59.85 dB and 0.082 for the next-best configuration, confirming its superior imperceptibility.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DOORPING (Liu et al., 2023), SNEAKDOOR achieves comparable or higher ASR in 17 of 20 settings while maintaining equal or better CTA; the most dramatic gap appears on CIFAR-10/IDM where SNEAKDOOR reaches 97.5% ASR versus DOORPING's 16.5%, and on STL-10/DM (97.3% vs. 14.9%). DOORPING retains a slight CTA edge in a few settings (e.g., CIFAR-10/IDM: 65.4% vs. 64.3%), but SNEAKDOOR's input-aware triggers yield substantially better stealth (PSNR 73.2 dB vs. ~59.8 dB on STL-10). Against SIMPLE and RELAX (Chung et al., ICLR 2024), SNEAKDOOR dominates on ASR in the majority of settings: SIMPLE's ASR frequently drops below 30% (e.g., 4.6% on Tiny-ImageNet/IDM, 9.6% on STL-10/DM), while RELAX achieves 100% ASR on many settings but at the cost of 3–6% lower CTA (e.g., CIFAR-10/DM: 57.4% vs. 62.6%). SNEAKDOOR matches RELAX's 100% ASR on 12 of 20 settings while preserving CTA within 1% and achieving the best stealth metrics (highest PSNR, SSIM, and lowest IS) across all comparisons.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the backdoor-attack-on-dataset-condensation line initiated by Liu et al. (Naive Attack, Doorping) and Chung et al. (simple-trigger, relax-trigger), which focused primarily on maximizing attack success rate (ASR) or preserving clean test accuracy (CTA). SNEAKDOOR departs from these by introducing a generative, input-aware trigger module that adapts perturbations to each sample's local feature geometry, and by explicitly targeting distribution-matching-based condensation (DM, IDM, DAM) rather than gradient-matching methods. The RKHS-based stealthiness analysis (Theorems 3–4) provides formal bounds absent from prior kernel-theoretic treatments, grounding the empirical claim that triggered samples remain distributionally indistinguishable from clean ones after condensation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper highlights a practical security gap: condensed datasets, often deployed as privacy-preserving or bandwidth-efficient substitutes, can carry imperceptible backdoors that persist across downstream architectures. The joint ASR-CTA-STE framing is a useful lens for evaluating attack realism, and the ablation confirming that input-aware triggers are essential for stealth (not just effectiveness) gives practitioners a concrete design principle. Open questions include reducing the required poisoning ratio, extending to complex targeted manipulations (e.g., traffic-sign substitution), and developing defenses that suppress ASR without the severe CTA collapse observed with RNP and PDB.
