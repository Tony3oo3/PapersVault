## Problem

Learning a general stochastic process prior from data and performing Bayesian functional regression with exact posterior densities has remained intractable beyond the Gaussian process setting. Existing approaches—deep GPs, conditional neural processes, and invertible operator flows—either rely on approximate posteriors, lack mathematical consistency as the number of query points grows, or suffer from training difficulties due to invertibility constraints. No prior method simultaneously provides a tractable, resolution-agnostic density for arbitrary point collections and an exact (up to constants) posterior for non-Gaussian processes.

## Input / Output

During training, the method consumes a dataset of historical function samples (discretized at arbitrary resolutions) to learn the prior. At inference, it takes noisy point observations at n arbitrary locations plus a set of m ≥ n query points and returns the posterior mean, posterior samples (via SGLD), and the exact log-density of function values at the query set. A key property is resolution agnosticism: the same trained model operates at any number of points and any spatial resolution without retraining, and the density computation scales linearly O(m) in the number of points via the Hutchinson trace estimator.

## Method

OFM generalizes marginal optimal-transport flow matching to infinite-dimensional Hilbert spaces by parameterizing a time-varying vector field with a Fourier Neural Operator (FNO) that transports samples from a fixed GP reference measure to the data measure along straight-line OT paths (G_t = u_1 − u_0). Training uses a conditional flow matching objective with minibatch optimal-transport coupling between the GP and data samples, avoiding the need for an explicit invertible architecture. The log-density of any finite point collection is computed via the change-of-variables formula (log P(u_1) = log P(u_0) − ∫∇·G_θ dt), with the divergence term estimated in O(m) cost using the Skilling-Hutchinson trace estimator. For Bayesian regression, the exact posterior (up to an intractable evidence constant) is formed by combining the Gaussian likelihood with the learned prior density, and SGLD sampling is performed in the GP input space where Langevin dynamics is native, then mapped forward to data space. The Kolmogorov Extension Theorem guarantees that the learned finite-dimensional marginals define a valid stochastic process, and the discretization convergence property of neural operators ensures consistency as point collections densify.

## Results

On 1D GP regression, OFM achieves the best MSLL (5.5×10⁻²) and ties DGP for best SMSE (4.1×10⁻¹), outperforming all other baselines. On 2D GP, OFM achieves the best SMSE (1.3×10⁻¹) but OpFlow edges it on MSLL (1.1×10⁻¹ vs 1.6×10⁻¹). On the non-Gaussian 1D TGP task, OFM delivers the best mean error (5.2×10⁻³) and standard deviation error (9.5×10⁻⁴), roughly 2–3× better than the next-best methods (ConvCNP and OpFlow). On highly non-GP datasets (Navier-Stokes, Black hole, MNIST-SDF), OFM produces visually realistic posterior means and samples that capture domain-specific structure (e.g., swirling patterns, digit shapes) where GP and NP baselines fail.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OFM outperforms all seven baselines on 5 of 6 quantitative metrics in Table 1. On 1D GP, OFM ties DGP for best SMSE (4.1×10⁻¹) and beats DGP on MSLL (5.5×10⁻² vs 6.8×10⁻²), while all NP-family baselines (NP, ANP, ConvCNP) are 3–8× worse on MSLL. On 2D GP, OFM achieves the best SMSE (1.3×10⁻¹ vs OpFlow's 1.4×10⁻¹), but OpFlow retains a slight edge on MSLL (1.1×10⁻¹ vs 1.6×10⁻¹); DGP degrades severely on 2D GP (SMSE 1.8×10⁰). On the non-Gaussian 1D TGP task, OFM is the clear winner with μ-error 5.2×10⁻³ and σ-error 9.5×10⁻⁴, roughly 2.5× better than ConvCNP (1.6×10⁻², 2.1×10⁻³) and 2.5× better than OpFlow (1.3×10⁻², 3.9×10⁻³). On the highly non-GP visual tasks (Navier-Stokes, Black hole, MNIST-SDF), OFM produces posterior means and samples that faithfully capture domain structure, whereas GP regression and ConvCNP fail to reproduce key features such as vortical patterns or digit shapes. Overall, OFM is the only method that simultaneously achieves top performance across both Gaussian and non-Gaussian settings while providing exact (up to constants) posterior densities.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends marginal optimal transport flow matching (Tong et al. [2], Lipman et al. [11]) from finite-dimensional spaces to infinite-dimensional function spaces, using Fourier Neural Operators as the vector field parameterization. It departs from the authors' own prior work OPFLOW [1], which required invertible neural operators (making training difficult), by showing that a standard non-invertible FNO suffices when combined with the Hutchinson trace estimator for exact density computation. The framework also subsumes and generalizes prior stochastic process learning approaches—deep GPs, conditional neural processes, and warped GPs—by providing exact (rather than approximate) posterior densities for arbitrary point collections, grounded in the Kolmogorov Extension Theorem and dynamic Kantorovich optimal transport.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

OFM provides the first practical framework for learning exact prior and posterior densities of arbitrary (non-Gaussian) stochastic processes, enabling calibrated uncertainty quantification and Bayes-optimal decisions in domains where GP assumptions fail (e.g., Navier-Stokes, black hole simulations). The resolution-agnostic, point-collection-based formulation means a single trained model can answer queries at any set of locations without retraining, a capability absent in grid-based generative models. Key limitations include restriction to low-dimensional domains and higher data/compute requirements compared to GP baselines; open questions include scaling to higher-dimensional function spaces, handling correlated or non-Gaussian observation noise, and reducing the computational cost of SGLD-based posterior sampling.
