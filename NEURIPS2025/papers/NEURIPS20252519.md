## Problem

Automating Olympiad-level inequality proofs requires navigating an infinite reasoning space where both precise algebraic manipulation and creative lemma application are essential. Existing approaches—pure LLMs, automated theorem provers, and prior hybrid systems like LIPS and AIPS—struggle to systematically decompose complex expressions into non-negative components, with the best LLMs solving only ~35% of problems and formal proof assistants achieving under 1% success on Lean4.

## Input / Output

The system takes an inequality expression F(x₁,...,xₙ) ≥ 0 with an optional assumption C(x₁,...,xₙ) ⋈ 0 (where ⋈ ∈ {=, ≥, >}), assuming all variables are positive. Preprocessing standardizes the input by adding normalization constraints for homogeneous inequalities, converting fractional expressions to numerator-only forms, and attempting radical elimination. The output is a verifiable formal proof structured as a non-negative linear combination F = Σ λᵢ fᵢ of known theorems, accompanied by human-readable proof steps logging each algebraic transformation and theorem reference.

## Method

IneqSearch reformulates inequality proving as a structured search problem: decomposing the target expression F into a sum of known non-negative components from a growing theorem database. A symbolic solver performs ECPD (Elementary Cyclic Polynomial Decomposition) and SOS (Sum of Squares via semidefinite programming) for direct proofs, then formulates theorem search as a linear programming problem to find non-negative coefficients λᵢ such that F = Σ λᵢ fᵢ. When direct methods fail, an LLM-based inductive agent proposes constructive transformations—applying Cauchy-Schwarz, AM-GM, Hölder, Jensen, or tangent line inequalities, and performing target shift for homogenization—while the symbolic solver verifies each proposed scaling. A self-improving mechanism iteratively incorporates newly proven inequalities into the theorem database, enabling the system to solve progressively harder problems across multiple rounds without human intervention.

## Results

On the INEQ-437 benchmark of Olympiad-level inequalities, IneqSearch with o3-mini achieves a 78.3% success rate (342/437 problems solved), substantially outperforming the best LLM baseline (o3-mini at 35.2%) and the closest hybrid method LIPS (62.5%). On the more challenging MO-INT-20 dataset, IneqSearch achieves a perfect 100% success rate (20/20), compared to 90% for LIPS and 50% for AIPS. The system also demonstrates a 100× speedup over LIPS in average solving time (24 s vs. 5187 s) while achieving higher accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IneqSearch outperforms all baselines on both datasets. Against the closest competitor LIPS (ICLR 2025), IneqSearch with o3-mini gains 15.8 percentage points on INEQ-437 (78.3% vs. 62.5%) and 10 percentage points on MO-INT-20 (100% vs. 90%), while also being over 100× faster in average solving time (24 s vs. 5187 s). Against AIPS (NeurIPS 2024), IneqSearch more than doubles the MO-INT-20 success rate (100% vs. 50%). Pure LLM approaches (o3-mini, DeepSeek-R1, Gemini-2.0-Flash) achieve at most 35.2% on INEQ-437 and 20% on MO-INT-20, falling 43+ percentage points short of IneqSearch. Lean4 formal proof baselines (DeepSeek-Prover-V1.5, o3-mini) are essentially non-functional, solving 0–2 problems on INEQ-437, highlighting that current LLMs cannot reliably produce formal inequality proofs without the symbolic verification backbone that IneqSearch provides.

## Relation to Prior Work

IneqSearch extends the hybrid LLM-plus-symbolic-reasoning paradigm established by LIPS [19] and AIPS [1] for Olympiad inequality proving, but departs from both in two key ways: it reframes proving as a structured decomposition search over a growing theorem database (using ECPD and SOS as the algebraic backbone) rather than open-ended expression rewriting, and it introduces an iterative self-improving loop where newly proven results are automatically incorporated as new theorems. The symbolic solver is the primary engine (requiring far fewer LLM calls than LIPS), with the LLM serving a narrower inductive role—proposing Cauchy-Schwarz, AM-GM, or homogenization transformations that the solver then verifies. The benchmark INEQ-437 is a new contribution, though it draws from the same source collections as prior inequality benchmarks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 100% success rate on MO-INT-20 and the 78.3% on INEQ-437 demonstrate that a solver-driven, decomposition-based approach can substantially outperform both pure LLM and LLM-heavy hybrid methods for algebraic inequalities, suggesting that precision in algebraic manipulation is better served by symbolic tools than by neural generation. The iterative learning mechanism—where the system's theorem database grows with each solved problem and unlocks previously inaccessible proofs—is a practically important design pattern for cumulative mathematical reasoning. Key limitations include the restriction to positive variables, the dependence on equality conditions being well-behaved (which can severely restrict applicable theorems), and the narrow scope to polynomial/rational inequalities; generalization to other mathematical domains and to inequalities with sign constraints remains open.
