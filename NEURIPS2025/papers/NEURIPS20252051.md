## Problem

In the anti-causal setting where labels cause observations (Y→X←E), existing methods face critical limitations: intervention-based approaches assume perfect interventions, structure-based methods require explicit SCMs, and invariant learning methods rely on restrictive distributional assumptions. Fundamentally, single-level representations cannot simultaneously capture the causal generative mechanism from Y to X while filtering spurious environmental correlations from E to X, leaving a gap in robust anti-causal representation learning with theoretical guarantees.

## Input / Output

ACIA consumes raw image observations (e.g., chest X-rays, MNIST digits, pathology patches) paired with class labels and environment identifiers across multiple training environments. It produces a hierarchical output: low-level representations φ_L that encode both the label-to-observation causal pathway and environment-specific variations, high-level representations φ_H that are provably independent of environment given the label, and a final class prediction. The framework operates on arbitrary feature spaces without requiring explicit DAG/SCM specification, and handles both discrete and continuous labels under perfect or imperfect interventions.

## Method

ACIA builds a measure-theoretic framework on product causal spaces (tensor products of per-environment σ-algebras) where causal kernels characterize the anti-causal structure and interventional kernels unify perfect and imperfect interventions through a single integral formulation. The two-level architecture implements causal dynamics at the low level (φ_L learns how labels generate observations via a causal dynamic kernel constructed from empirical measures) and causal abstraction at the high level (φ_H integrates over the low-level representation domain to form environment-invariant kernels, acting as an information bottleneck). Training uses a min-max objective over environments with two regularizers: R₁ enforces environment independence of high-level representations conditioned on Y (motivated by the kernel independence property), and R₂ enforces causal structure consistency by aligning observational and interventional distributions (motivated by interventional kernel invariance). Theoretical results provide convergence rates, OOD generalization bounds, and environmental robustness guarantees for the learned representations.

## Results

ACIA achieves near-perfect accuracy on all synthetic benchmarks: 99.20% on CMNIST, 99.10% on RMNIST, and 99.98% on Ball Agent, with near-zero environment independence (EI = 0.00) and intervention robustness (IR ≤ 0.03) scores. On the real-world Camelyon17 medical dataset, ACIA reaches 84.40% test accuracy, a 19-percentage-point improvement over the best baseline (LECI at 65.50%), while maintaining competitive EI (0.28) and LLI (0.42) scores. Under imperfect interventions, performance remains essentially unchanged (e.g., 99.4% on CMNIST, 84.4% on Camelyon17), confirming the interventional kernel formulation's robustness.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ACIA outperforms all 10 baselines on every dataset across all four metrics. On CMNIST, ACIA (99.20% Acc) surpasses the best baseline VREx (95.50%) by 3.7 points and MMD (94.00%) by 5.2 points, while achieving near-zero EI (0.00 vs. 1.71 for VREx) and IR (0.02 vs. 0.77). On RMNIST, the gap is even larger: ACIA (99.10%) beats VREx (93.50%) by 5.6 points and MMD (92.00%) by 7.1 points. On Ball Agent, ACIA (99.98%) dominates VREx (74.00%) by a 26-point margin, with LLI of 0.03 versus 0.78. The most striking result is on Camelyon17, where ACIA (84.40%) improves over the best baseline LECI (65.50%) by 18.9 points and ACTIR (60.50%) by 23.9 points. Notably, DANN and Rex achieve low EI scores but at the cost of severely degraded accuracy (45.00% and 73.00% on CMNIST respectively), illustrating the accuracy-invariance tradeoff that ACIA's two-level design resolves. CausalDA shows the worst IR scores (12.23 on CMNIST, 16.44 on RMNIST), indicating poor intervention robustness despite moderate accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

ACIA builds directly on the measure-theoretic axiomatisation of causality by Park et al. (NeurIPS 2023), adopting their causal space and kernel formalism as the theoretical backbone, and on the causal dynamics perspective from Ahuja et al. (ICRL, 2023) for learning latent causal relationships from observed data. It departs from three existing families of anti-causal learning methods: intervention-based approaches (which assume perfect interventions), structure-based methods (which require explicit SCMs/DAGs), and single-level invariant learning methods (which cannot simultaneously capture the Y→X causal mechanism and filter E→X spurious correlations). The key conceptual departure is the two-level design—causal dynamics at the low level and causal abstraction at the high level—coupled with a unified interventional kernel that handles both perfect and imperfect interventions without requiring explicit causal graph specification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first measure-theoretic framework with provable OOD generalization bounds specifically for the anti-causal setting, and its unified interventional kernel formulation removes the restrictive perfect-intervention assumption that has limited prior causal representation learning. The 19-point accuracy gap over the best baseline on Camelyon17 (84.4% vs. 65.5%) suggests practical relevance for medical imaging where hospital-specific protocols create exactly the anti-causal structure modeled here. However, the framework's practical adoption may be limited by its heavy theoretical apparatus, the assumption of a finite number of training environments with spurious features independent in at least one environment, and validation on only one real-world dataset. Open questions include extending to confounded-descendant and mixed causal-anticausal structures, and whether the kernel-based formulation scales to very high-dimensional or continuous environment spaces.
