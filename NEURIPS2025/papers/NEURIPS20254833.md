## Problem

Old-photo face restoration involves compounded degradations—breakage, color fading, and severe blur—that existing diffusion-guided methods handle poorly: DDNM assumes linear degradation models and fails on complex nonlinear damage, while PGDiff's global color-statistic and smoothing priors cause structural collapse on large breakages and color inhomogeneity. No prior method selectively restores breakage regions and refines facial color in a region-aware, identity-preserving manner without requiring paired training data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254833/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 512×512 cropped and aligned old face photo (LQ) as input, from which it derives face parsing maps (BiseNet), scratch masks (a pre-trained scratch detector), and pseudo-reference labels generated by running the same pre-trained diffusion model under weak guidance (s_w=1e-3). The output is a 512×512 restored HQ face photo. The entire pipeline is training-free and operates on a single NVIDIA RTX 4090, with an average online latency of 95 ms per image (24 ms if parsing, scratch detection, and initial restoration are pre-computed offline).

## Method

SSDiff's key insight is that a pre-trained unconditional face diffusion model, when run under weak guidance from a degraded input, produces pseudo-references that preserve facial contours and natural color saturation while deviating in identity-sensitive features (eyes, mouth). These pseudo-labels serve as self-supervised references in a staged, region-selective guidance scheme: during early reverse steps (t > T₁=400), a structural loss L₁ enforces fidelity in non-broken face regions and a smoothness loss L₂ enforces gradient-magnitude consistency in breakage regions (selected via parsing maps and scratch masks, excluding identity-critical areas); during later steps (t ≤ T₁), a color loss L₃ guides skin-region coloring using a pre-trained style-transfer network (CAP-VSTNet) to adapt pseudo-label color to the current denoised face. All three losses act as pseudo-classifier gradients in the DDPM reverse update, scaled by a strong guidance factor s_s=3.5e-3, and the selective masking via parsing maps and scratch masks ensures that breakage completion and color refinement target only plausible regions without disturbing identity features.

## Results

On the proposed VintageFace benchmark (300 old face photos), SSDiff achieves the best scores across all four no-reference metrics (FID, BRISQUE, TOPIQ, MAN-IQA) at every degradation level. On the hardest category, SSDiff reaches FID 122.59, BRISQUE 8.95, TOPIQ 0.5977, and MAN-IQA 0.3575, outperforming the next-best method (DifFace, FID 145.85) by a wide margin. On the medium category, SSDiff scores FID 128.31 and TOPIQ 0.6412, again leading all baselines. For identity fidelity, SSDiff achieves a CLIP-based identity distance of 0.2280, comparable to DiffBIR (0.2223) and substantially better than PGDiff (0.2859).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SSDiff outperforms all seven baselines on every metric across all three degradation levels of VintageFace. The closest competitor is PGDiff [16] (NeurIPS 2023), which leads in FID among train-free methods but still trails SSDiff by 7.6 (simple), 14.3 (medium), and 43.8 (hard) FID points; CodeFormer [9] is the strongest GAN-based baseline on BRISQUE (6.69 vs. SSDiff's 6.71 on simple) but falls behind on FID and TOPIQ. DDNM [14] performs worst overall, with BRISQUE scores exceeding 50 at every level, indicating severe perceptual artifacts. Among learning-based diffusion methods, DifFace [10] achieves the best FID (145.85 on hard) but is still 23.3 points worse than SSDiff, while DiffBIR [11] and BOPB [37] lag further behind. On identity preservation (CLIP distance, Table 4), SSDiff (0.2280) is competitive with DiffBIR (0.2223) and clearly superior to PGDiff (0.2859) and DDNM (0.2386), confirming that the selective guidance strategy avoids identity drift while improving perceptual quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

SSDiff builds on the classifier-guided diffusion framework (Dhariwal & Nichol, 2021) by replacing classifier gradients with task-specific loss gradients computed against self-generated pseudo-references, and on DDPM as the underlying generative backbone. It departs from prior zero-shot diffusion-guided restoration methods—DDNM, which relies on explicit linear degradation models, and PGDiff, which enforces global color statistics and smoothing priors—by instead using region-selective, staged supervision derived from weakly-guided pseudo-labels. The method also inherits structural cues from face parsing (BiseNet) and scratch detection networks, positioning it within the broader blind face restoration lineage that includes GAN-based (CodeFormer, GPEN) and training-based diffusion (DifFace, DiffBIR) approaches, but distinguishes itself as a training-free, self-supervised alternative.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SSDiff addresses a practically important but underexplored niche—old-photo face restoration with compounded degradations (breakage, fading, severe blur)—where existing GAN-based and diffusion-based methods fail due to distribution shift. The staged, region-selective guidance paradigm (structure-aware loss throughout, color refinement only after identity stabilizes) is a reusable design principle that could transfer to other restoration tasks with heterogeneous degradation types. However, the method's reliance on a frozen FFHQ-trained unconditional diffusion model limits diversity, and the acknowledged failure mode on large stained regions (misinterpreted as facial structure) remains an open problem. The VintageFace benchmark (300 images) is useful but small, and the training-free setting, while practical, constrains the method's ceiling compared to fine-tuned approaches.
