## Problem

Existing VLM fine-tuning methods address OOD generalization (covariate shifts) and OOD detection (semantic shifts) independently, leaving a gap in simultaneously improving both during downstream adaptation. The paper tackles the underexplored challenge of enhancing a VLM's classification accuracy on covariate-shifted closed-set OOD data while reliably detecting open-set OOD classes that were unseen during fine-tuning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252686/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images and text prompts formatted as 'a photo of a {CLASS NAME}' for K closed-set classes, processed through a frozen CLIP ViT-B/16 dual encoder to produce D-dimensional image and text features. At inference, the top-c cosine similarities between image and text features are reset to zero to compute the Δ Energy OOD score, while the unmasked features are used for classification. During fine-tuning, a masking operation retains only the top p-proportion of image feature elements (where the element-wise product with the top-1 text feature is positive), and the resulting energy change between original and masked features drives the EBM loss.

## Method

The core contribution is Δ Energy, an OOD score defined as the energy change E1(x) − E0(x) when the top-c maximum cosine similarities between image and text features are reset to zero; Theorem 3.2 proves this amplifies the ID–OOD separation compared to MCM. For few-shot fine-tuning, the EBM loss minimizes the energy change between the original image feature and a masked version (zeroing elements where the product with the top-1 text feature is positive), which Theorem 3.4 shows increases the lower bound of Δ Energy and Theorem 3.5 shows yields domain-consistent Hessians as a proxy for OOD generalization. The training objective is L_EBM = L_CE + λ₀·exp(L_ΔE), applied via prompt-tuning with frozen CLIP encoders and only 16 learnable context vectors. The masking mechanism shifts the model's attention from foreground objects to background, and the consistency between original and masked domains acts as a regularizer that simultaneously improves detection and generalization.

## Results

On the Setup-I fine-tuning benchmark (ImageNet-1k, 16 shots/class, CLIP ViT-B/16), EBM with Δ Energy achieves 81.90 AUROC and 65.90 FPR95 for OOD detection, and 63.28% OOD accuracy for covariate-shift generalization, outperforming the closest competitor CRoFT (76.30 AUROC, 69.78 FPR95, 62.83% OOD ACC) by over 5 points in AUROC. In zero-shot OOD detection on ImageNet-1k (Table 1), Δ Energy reaches 87.10 AUROC and 46.40 FPR95 for ID vs. semantic-shifted OOD, surpassing MCM (85.81/53.34) and MSP (85.64/51.72). On the cross-dataset Setup-II (PACS/VLCS), EBM+Δ Energy achieves 21.0 average FPR95, beating CRoFT (56.5) and GalLoP (29.0) by large margins.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EBM with Δ Energy achieves the best OOD detection AUROC (81.90) and FPR95 (65.90) among all fine-tuning methods on Setup-I, outperforming CRoFT (76.30/69.78) by 5.6 AUROC points and CoCoOp (76.38/70.30) by 5.5 points. For OOD generalization, EBM attains the highest OOD accuracy (63.28%), exceeding CRoFT (62.83%) and CoCoOp (62.58%). Notably, methods designed specifically for OOD detection—GalLoP (56.97 AUROC, 91.17 FPR95) and NegPrompt (60.86 AUROC, 86.66 FPR95)—suffer severe degradation in detection performance on the large-scale ImageNet-1k setup, even falling below the zero-shot CLIP+MCM baseline. In zero-shot detection (Table 1), Δ Energy outperforms MCM by 1.29 AUROC (87.10 vs. 85.81) and 6.94 FPR95 points (46.40 vs. 53.34) on ID vs. semantic-shifted OOD, and by 3.02 AUROC (78.68 vs. 75.66) on the harder covariate-shifted vs. semantic-shifted setting. On Setup-II (PACS/VLCS), EBM+Δ Energy achieves 21.0 average FPR95, dramatically outperforming CRoFT (56.5) and GalLoP (29.0), while also matching or exceeding their OOD accuracy (81.7% vs. 80.2% and 81.3% on VLCS).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the energy-based OOD detection framework (Liu et al., 2020) and the VLM-specific MCM score (Ming et al., 2022a) by proposing that the *change* in energy when disrupting vision-language alignment (zeroing top cosine similarities) is a more discriminative OOD signal than either score alone. For fine-tuning, it builds on CoOp-style prompt tuning (Zhou et al., 2021) but departs from prior concurrent-optimization methods like CRoFT (Zhu et al., 2024) and GalLoP (Lafon et al., 2024) by grounding the training objective in a provable lower bound on Δ Energy and connecting it to domain-consistent Hessians, rather than relying on adapter-based or multi-prompt strategies. The theoretical lineage draws on Hessian-based domain generalization analysis (Rame et al., 2022; Hemati et al., 2023) to show that minimizing the EBM loss simultaneously addresses both OOD detection and covariate-shift generalization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap where prior VLM fine-tuning methods handle OOD detection or OOD generalization in isolation, offering a single prompt-tuning objective that provably improves both. The theoretical link between energy-change maximization and Hessian consistency provides a principled explanation for why the method works, which could inform future robust fine-tuning objectives. Limitations include evaluation restricted to CLIP ViT-B/16 with only 16 samples per class, reliance on a heuristic masking proportion, and theoretical assumptions (uniform similarity distributions, bounded feature perturbations) that may not hold at scale. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to larger VLMs, whether the specific top-c cosine similarity disruption is optimal, and how the method behaves under more extreme covariate shifts.
