## Problem

In MoE-based graph foundation models, the router's top-1 expert selection is suboptimal: second- and third-ranked experts frequently outperform the top-ranked one, leaving valuable multi-expert knowledge untapped. While ensembling top-k expert outputs improves performance, it incurs substantial computational overhead, and naive parameter merging degrades quality due to expert specialization and parameter interference. The paper addresses how to retain the efficiency of single-expert inference while recovering the performance gains of multi-expert ensembling.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250749/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes heterogeneous graphs with varying node counts and feature dimensions, first projecting both the Laplacian-normalized adjacency matrix and the node feature matrix into a shared reduced dimensionality via SVD, then enriching with non-parametric message passing. The MoE layer produces a unified node embedding matrix per graph, from which link prediction scores (inner products of node pairs, evaluated via Recall@20 and NDCG@20) and node classification labels (accuracy, macro-F1) are derived in a zero-shot cross-domain setting.

## Method

The paper proposes two enhanced expert merging strategies built on AnyGraph's SVD + message-passing + MoE backbone. KDEM treats the weighted output ensemble of the top-k routed experts as a teacher and the single parameter-merged expert as a student, applying a periodic MSE distillation loss (every T₁ steps) within a unified joint training loop so that teacher and student always attend to the same experts, avoiding the inconsistency of two-stage distillation. PPEM is grounded in a theorem showing that when MLP expert parameters converge pairwise, the parameter-averaged expert's output approximates the convex combination of individual expert outputs; it periodically applies an exponential moving average (β=0.999) to nudge only the router-selected top-k experts toward their weighted parameter mean, preserving the diversity of unselected experts. Both methods require only a single forward pass through the fused expert at inference, matching top-1 routing cost while approaching ensemble-level accuracy.

## Results

On the zero-shot Link2 group (18 datasets), KDEM achieves 51.69 Recall@20 and 32.60 NDCG@20, outperforming AnyGraph (46.42 / 27.21) by 5.27% and 5.39% respectively, while PPEM reaches 50.77 / 31.93. On the E-commerce domain, KDEM improves Recall from 26.92 (AnyGraph) to 35.05, a 30% relative gain, indicating the largest benefit from multi-expert integration in this domain. On node classification (5 datasets), AnyGraph retains a slight edge in accuracy (64.31 vs. 63.56 for KDEM), but KDEM leads in macro-F1 (45.03 vs. 43.24). Both methods substantially outperform all non-MoE baselines (GIN, GAT, GPF, GraphPrompt, GraphCL) across every dataset group.

## Baselines & Comparisons

KDEM and PPEM outperform the direct predecessor AnyGraph on Link2 Recall by 5.27% and 4.35% respectively (51.69/50.77 vs. 46.42), and on E-commerce Recall by 30.2% and 26.3% (35.05/33.99 vs. 26.92), confirming that leveraging multiple experts yields substantial gains over top-1 routing. Against the strongest non-MoE baseline GraphCL, KDEM improves Link2 Recall from 31.42 to 51.69 (+64% relative) and E-commerce Recall from 26.05 to 35.05 (+35% relative). Classical GNNs (GIN, GAT) and prompt-based methods (GPF, GraphPrompt) are far behind, with Link2 Recall ranging from 6.10 to 21.62, highlighting the difficulty of zero-shot transfer without MoE. In the cross-GFM comparison (Table 2), KDEM achieves 62.45% accuracy on Cora (vs. GraphGPT's 18.13%) and 28.30 Recall on E-commerce w/o GR (vs. OpenGraph's 14.44%), while PPEM matches or slightly exceeds GOFA on PubMed-link (94.10 vs. 93.97 accuracy). AnyGraph retains a narrow edge on node classification accuracy (64.31 vs. 63.56 for KDEM), suggesting that single-expert specialization still has a slight advantage for this particular task.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on AnyGraph, an MoE-based graph foundation model that uses SVD for feature alignment and top-1 competence-score routing to handle structural heterogeneity. It extends the expert merging literature (He et al., 2023; Muqeeth et al., 2024), which proposes simple weighted parameter averaging of top-k experts, by showing that this naive approach degrades performance due to parameter interference. The two proposed methods—KDEM (knowledge distillation from ensemble to merged expert) and PPEM (EMA-driven parameter proximity grounded in a Lipschitz-continuity theorem for MLPs)—bridge the gap between the high cost of expert ensembling and the low quality of naive merging, drawing on knowledge distillation (Hinton et al., 2015) and model fusion theory respectively.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that second- and third-ranked experts outperform the top-ranked expert challenges the implicit assumption that a router's top choice is always optimal, suggesting that MoE-based GFMs systematically underutilize their expert pool. The practical takeaway—that ensemble-level performance can be recovered at single-expert inference cost via distillation or parameter proximity—offers a concrete efficiency lever for deploying MoE-based GFMs at scale. However, the methods are validated only on AnyGraph's MLP-based experts and a specific competence-score routing mechanism; whether the parameter-proximity theorem and EMA strategy transfer to attention-based experts, larger MoE backbones, or different routing paradigms remains open. The modest absolute gains (~5% recall on Link2) also leave room for questioning whether the added training complexity is justified in all deployment scenarios.
