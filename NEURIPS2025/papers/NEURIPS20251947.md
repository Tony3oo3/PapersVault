## Problem

Existing multimodal VAE-based models either rely on joint encoders (PoE/MoE) that suffer from calibration errors and limited cross-modal alignment, or introduce modality-specific variables without sufficient structural constraints to cleanly separate shared from private factors. Additionally, most methods cannot be trained simultaneously on both complete and partially missing multimodal data with a valid variational objective, limiting their applicability to real-world scenarios where incomplete observations are common.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251947/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes paired or partially observed bimodal inputs: two images (MNIST/SVHN digits), an image with a text caption (CUBICC bird images), or gene expression profiles with tissue morphology images (spatial transcriptomics, where spatial coordinates are appended as two extra channels via CoordConv to form 5-channel inputs). It produces a shared latent vector z in a common space Z per modality, a modality-specific latent w with a standard Gaussian prior, and supports downstream outputs including cross-modal generated samples, K-means cluster assignments, and linear-probe class labels evaluated on the shared latent space.

## Method

DCMEM factorizes each modality's latent code into a shared component z and a modality-specific component w, then enforces disentanglement via an information-bottleneck objective that maximizes I(z_s; t; s) − I(z_s; w_s), approximated by a reconstruction surrogate, a KL penalty on w, and a cross-modal KL divergence between q(z|s) and q(z|t) in the shared space. Bidirectional mutual supervision is implemented by deriving importance-weighted ELBOs for both s→z→t and t→z→s information flows (with stop-gradient on the importance ratio for stability), and the two are averaged. A contrastive (InfoNCE-style) loss with cosine similarity and temperature 0.5 directly maximizes I(z_s; z_t) to align paired shared latents. For missing modalities, a VampPrior-inspired batch-dependent prior over B anchors from the observed modality regularizes the shared latent, yielding a valid ELBO that unifies paired and unpaired samples in a single training objective.

## Results

On the Human Breast Cancer spatial transcriptomics dataset, DCMEM achieves the highest clustering performance with an ARI of 55.1 and NMI of 69.7, substantially outperforming both VAE-based and spatially-aware baselines. On the CUBICC and MNIST-SVHN datasets, DCMEM consistently achieves the best clustering accuracy (ACC, NMI, ARI) across both individual modality and joint latent representations, as well as the highest cross-modal generation coherence (measured by classification accuracy of generated samples) and lowest FID scores. Under partial-observation settings (25%–75% missing rates), DCMEM maintains robust classification accuracy while baselines such as MEME and MVP degrade substantially.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DCMEM outperforms all eight multimodal VAE baselines (MVAE, MMVAE, MoPoE, MEME, MMVAE+, CMVAE, MMVM, MVP) on clustering (ACC, NMI, ARI) for both CUBICC and MNIST-SVHN, as well as on cross-modal generation coherence and FID. On the Human Breast Cancer dataset, DCMEM (ARI 55.1, NMI 69.7) surpasses all five spatial-domain clustering methods (Scanpy, STAGATE, GraphST, SiGra, xSiGra) and all VAE baselines; among VAEs, MMVAE is the strongest but still trails DCMEM. Under missing-data conditions (25%–75% missing), DCMEM maintains stable classification accuracy while MEME and MVP degrade significantly, and MMVAE+ underperforms due to its inability to capture class-discriminative features in the shared space. For generation, MMVAE+ and CMVAE exhibit entanglement between class identity and modality-specific variables, while MVP produces limited diversity; DCMEM generates diverse yet class-consistent samples. The paper also notes that MVAE and MMVM struggle to resolve fine-grained structures on the breast cancer dataset, and PoE-based models (MVAE, MoPoE) generally achieve lower coherence than MoE-based or mutual-supervision-based approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

DCMEM sits at the intersection of two threads in multimodal VAE research: mutual supervision (MEME, Joy et al. 2022), which regularizes shared representations by cross-modal reconstruction, and explicit disentanglement via modality-specific subspaces (MMVAE+, Palumbo et al. 2023), which separates shared from private factors. The paper's key architectural departure is combining these two mechanisms bidirectionally while adding an information-bottleneck-style mutual information constraint to enforce that shared and private factors encode exclusive yet complementary information. For handling missing modalities, it adapts the VampPrior (Tomczak & Welling, 2018) idea of batch-dependent priors, allowing a valid ELBO to be computed even when one modality is absent. Unlike PoE- or MoE-based joint posterior models (MVAE, MMVAE, MoPoE), DCMEM avoids explicit fusion of posteriors, instead relying on reciprocal generative paths and a shared latent alignment term.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ability to train a single model on a mixture of fully paired and partially missing multimodal data with a principled ELBO is practically important, since real-world datasets (e.g., spatial transcriptomics with heterogeneous tissue regions) rarely have complete observations across all modalities. The consistent outperformance across generation, clustering, and classification—particularly the spatial transcriptomics result where a general multimodal VAE outperforms specialized graph-attention methods—suggests that explicit disentanglement plus mutual supervision is a more robust recipe than task-specific architectural inductive biases. However, the model is explicitly designed for bimodal settings, and the authors acknowledge that extending to general multimodal (3+ modalities) scenarios would require non-trivial effort. Open questions include whether the information bottleneck constraint scales gracefully with more modalities and whether the VampPrior-based missing-modality handling remains stable at very high missing rates beyond the 75% tested.
