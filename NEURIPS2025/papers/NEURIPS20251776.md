## Problem

Existing unsupervised graph domain adaptation methods assume a closed-set label space shared between source and target graphs, which is unrealistic when the target introduces novel classes. Prior open-set graph adaptation approaches rely on manually tuned entropy thresholds for unknown detection and focus primarily on aligning source with target-known instances, leaving target-unknown features entangled with known ones and producing blurry decision boundaries.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251776/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a labeled source graph (node feature matrix, adjacency matrix, class labels for |C_s| known classes) and an unlabeled target graph (node features, adjacency matrix) whose label space is a superset of the source. It produces per-node predictions over |C_s|+1 classes, where the extra class is 'unknown'. The classifier is augmented with a learned linear projection for the unknown class, so the final prediction is simply argmax over all |C_s|+1 logits—no external threshold is needed at inference time.

## Method

GraphRTA performs dual reprogramming: (1) model reprogramming prunes domain-specific weights via gradient-based importance scores (inspired by the lottery ticket hypothesis) and augments the output layer with an extra dimension for the unknown class, replacing manual thresholds with a learned dynamic decision boundary; (2) graph reprogramming modifies the target graph by adding learnable perturbations to node features and adjusting edges via XOR with a budget-constrained binary matrix to reduce structural distribution shift. Training uses a three-way domain adversarial framework (source, target-known, target-unknown) where soft group assignments are estimated by fitting a two-component Beta mixture model over prediction entropies via EM, combined with a classification loss on source labels and an entropy-minimization loss that pushes target-unknown features away from both source and target-known representations.

## Results

On the citation datasets (DBLPv7, Citationv1, ACMv9), GraphRTA achieves H-scores of 66.33% (A→C), 64.42% (A→D), 62.89% (C→A), 65.99% (C→D), 59.41% (D→A), and 62.33% (D→C) with 3 known classes, consistently outperforming the strongest open-set baseline DANCE by 2–5 percentage points. On ogbn-arxiv, GraphRTA reaches 50.79% H-score (Arxiv I→II), 46.25% (I→III), and 48.42% (II→III), surpassing the next-best baseline (SAGE at 38.11% for II→III) by over 10 points. On the heterophilic WebKB graphs, GraphRTA achieves 34.36% (Cornell→Wisconsin), 35.64% (Texas→Cornell), and 30.08% (Texas→Wisconsin) H-score, outperforming all baselines that successfully run on these datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GraphRTA outperforms all 15 baselines on the ogbn-arxiv temporal-shift scenarios, with the largest margin over SAGE (48.42% vs 38.11% H-score for Arxiv II→III, a +10.3 pt gap) and over DANCE which OOMs entirely on this dataset. On the citation datasets, GraphRTA beats the strongest open-set baseline DANCE by 2–5 H-score points across all six domain pairs (e.g., 66.33% vs 60.94% for A→C), and also surpasses closed-set methods like A2GNN (65.37% HS for C→D) and GRADE (64.21% HS for C→D). On the heterophilic WebKB graphs, GraphRTA achieves the best H-score in 5 of 6 scenarios; the sole exception is Texas→Cornell where StruRW reports a higher H-score (40.98 vs 35.64) but with much larger variance (±7.86 vs ±0.83) and lower accuracy (42.02 vs 52.18). Notably, G2Pxy, SDA, and UAGA all fail to produce results on the WebKB datasets due to unsatisfied internal assumptions, while DANCE OOMs on ogbn-arxiv, leaving GraphRTA as the only method that runs successfully across all 12 evaluation scenarios.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the unknown-aware domain adversarial learning framework of Jang et al. (NeurIPS 2022), which uses a Beta mixture model over prediction entropies to separate target-known and target-unknown groups without a fixed threshold. It departs from prior open-set graph domain adaptation methods (SDA, PGL, DANCE, G2Pxy) that rely on manually tuned entropy thresholds or clustering heuristics by instead augmenting the classifier with an explicit unknown-class dimension. The model reprogramming component draws on the lottery ticket hypothesis for gradient-based weight pruning, while the graph reprogramming component repurposes simple additive/XOR transformations rather than the more complex self-supervised structure learning methods (SUBLIME, SLAPS) explored in the ablation. The dual reprogramming strategy—simultaneously modifying both the model parameters and the input graph data—is the key conceptual departure from prior work that operates exclusively in the learned feature space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Open-set graph domain adaptation is a more realistic formulation than closed-set adaptation for evolving real-world graphs (fraud detection, citation networks, web pages), and this paper provides an architecture-agnostic framework that eliminates the fragile manual thresholding step common in prior methods. However, the approach still requires access to both source and target graphs during training, limiting its applicability to source-free or semi-supervised settings that the authors acknowledge as future work. The graph reprogramming budget B and sparsity ratio ρ introduce additional hyperparameters that may be sensitive to dataset characteristics, and the evaluation, while spanning multiple datasets, is limited to relatively small graphs (except ogbn-arxiv), leaving open questions about scalability to very large industrial graphs.
