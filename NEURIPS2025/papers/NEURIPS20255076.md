## Problem

Existing neural eikonal solvers face a fundamental trade-off: PINNs require per-velocity-field training with no cross-instance generalization, while neural operators (DeepONet, FNO) rely on spatial discretization that limits resolution invariance. Conditional Neural Fields offer grid-free conditioning but use global latent vectors without geometric structure, missing the opportunity to exploit the symmetries inherent in the eikonal equation. This paper asks whether equivariant geometric conditioning can simultaneously deliver grid-free inference, cross-instance generalization, and user-controllable steerability of the solution.

## Input / Output

The method takes a velocity field v: M → [v_min, v_max] defined on a Riemannian manifold and a source-receiver pair (s, r) ∈ M × M as input. The velocity field is not fed directly but is encoded at test time as a point cloud of N=9 pose-context pairs z = {(g_i, c_i)} ∈ P(SE(2) × R^32), where poses g_i ∈ SE(2) carry geometric information and context vectors c_i ∈ R^32 carry signal-specific features. The output is the factored travel-time T(s,r) = d̃(s,r)·τ_θ(s,r;z), where d̃ is a group-invariant semimetric (e.g., Euclidean chordal distance) and τ_θ is the network's bounded output in [1/v_max, 1/v_min]. Because the representation is fully continuous, evaluation is resolution-independent, and geodesic paths can be extracted via bidirectional Riemannian gradient descent on the learned travel-time surface.

## Method

E-NES extends Equivariant Neural Fields to functions on products of Riemannian manifolds by introducing a latent-pose extension (Theorem 4.1): augmenting the input space Π with an auxiliary group element G makes the group action free, so the moving frame method yields a complete set of functionally independent invariants Inv(s, r, g_i) = (g_i^{-1}·s, g_i^{-1}·r). The architecture is an invariant cross-attention encoder that maps these invariants through Random Fourier Features, applies a Reynolds symmetrization over S_2 to enforce T(s,r)=T(r,s), and performs cross-attention between the invariant query and the context vectors, followed by a bounded projection head with AdaptiveGauss activations to capture sharp wavefronts and caustics. The steerability constraint T_θ(s,r; g·z) = T_θ(g^{-1}·s, g^{-1}·r; z) is enforced architecturally, and Proposition 4.1 proves that transforming the latent by g induces a well-defined group action μ on the velocity field, so one trained solution generalizes to its entire group orbit without data augmentation. Training uses a physics-informed Hamiltonian loss |v²‖grad T‖² − 1| in two modes: autodecoding (joint optimization of θ and z) for maximum accuracy, and meta-learning (bi-level optimization with a few inner SGD steps) for ~100× faster fitting at test time.

## Results

On the 2D OpenFWI benchmark (10 velocity field categories, 70×70 grids), E-NES with full autodecoding convergence achieves relative error (RE) of 0.00506–0.01885, outperforming FC-DeepONet (RE 0.00277–0.07863) on 7 of 10 datasets, with the largest gains on challenging variants (Style-A: 0.00833 vs 0.03461; CurveFault-B: 0.01885 vs 0.07863). The meta-learning variant reduces total fitting time for 100 test fields from ~1000 s to under 6 s (two orders of magnitude) at a modest RE increase. On the 2-sphere, E-NES achieves RE 0.013 (constant speed) and 0.015 (Spherical Style-B) with RMAE 0.012, demonstrating generalization to non-Euclidean geometry.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against FC-DeepONet (Mei et al., 2024), E-NES with full autodecoding convergence wins on 7 of 10 OpenFWI datasets, with the most dramatic improvements on complex fault and style models (e.g., CurveFault-B: RE 0.01885 vs 0.07863, a 4.2× reduction; Style-A: 0.00833 vs 0.03461, a 4.2× reduction). FC-DeepONet retains an edge only on the simplest FlatVel-A (0.00277 vs 0.00506) and FlatFault-A (0.00514 vs 0.00568) where the velocity structure is nearly uniform. Against Functa (Dupont et al., 2022), E-NES dominates across all five B-type datasets by a wide margin (e.g., FlatVel-B: RE 0.01581 vs 0.11854; Style-B: 0.01171 vs 0.03106), attributed to Functa's global latent vector lacking localized geometric conditioning. In the 3D scalability analysis, E-NES maintains stable RE/RMAE across increasing grid resolutions while FMM's computational cost grows superlinearly, with E-NES already faster than FMM even at the smallest tested grid size.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper integrates two previously separate research threads: Equivariant Neural Fields (ENFs) from the geometric deep learning community (Wessels et al. 2024, Knigge et al. 2024) and neural eikonal solvers from the scientific ML/geophysics community (PINNs à la Grubas et al. 2023, neural operators à la Mei et al. 2024, Song et al. 2024). The key conceptual departure is extending ENFs from single-point conditioning to functions on products of Riemannian manifolds with regular (not necessarily free) group actions, using the moving frame method (Olver 2001) to compute complete invariants. The theoretical contribution (Theorem 4.1) formally justifies the latent-pose augmentation trick previously used heuristically in Wessels et al. 2024, providing completeness guarantees that were absent in prior ENF work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The grid-free, equivariant formulation offers a principled path to eikonal solving on arbitrary homogeneous spaces (spheres, hyperbolic manifolds, position-orientation spaces) without the discretization bottlenecks of FMM/FSM or the per-instance training of PINNs. The steerability property—where transforming the latent point cloud predictably transforms the solution—enables user controllability and geodesic path planning that are difficult with standard neural operators. Key limitations include the test-time optimization cost (autodecoding or meta-learning fitting), which is orders of magnitude slower than FC-DeepONet's implicit encoder pass, and the current restriction to regular group actions. Open questions include whether the framework extends to non-regular actions, whether the meta-learning expressivity gap can be closed, and whether the approach scales to 3D+ non-Euclidean domains beyond the simple extrusion tested here.
