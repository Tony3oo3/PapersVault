## Problem

Equivariant neural networks improve sample efficiency and generalization in robotic policy learning, but integrating them into modern diffusion-based visuomotor policies is impractical: equivariant architectures require specialized layers, structured inputs, and task-specific symmetry reasoning that conflict with diffusion denoising, eye-in-hand perception, and pretrained vision encoders. This paper asks whether symmetry benefits can be obtained with minimal architectural changes rather than a full end-to-end equivariant redesign.

## Input / Output

The policy consumes a single eye-in-hand RGB image (3×84×84, random-cropped to 3×76×76 during training) plus two steps of proprioceptive history (gripper pose T_t ∈ SE(3) and aperture w_t), and outputs a 16-step action trajectory of gripper poses and aperture commands. Crucially, the action is parameterized as a relative trajectory in the gripper frame (not absolute world-frame poses), and the denoising network operates entirely in this local frame. Only 8 of the 16 predicted steps are executed at evaluation time.

## Method

The paper combines three modular symmetry mechanisms without altering the standard DDPM diffusion backbone. First, it proves that pairing eye-in-hand perception with relative (or delta) trajectory actions yields inherent SE(3)-invariance: the image is unchanged under world-frame transforms and the relative action is right-multiplied by the gripper pose, so the policy becomes equivariant by construction. Second, it replaces the standard ResNet-18 vision encoder with a C8-equivariant CNN (via escnn) that outputs features in the regular representation of C8, giving the diffusion head explicit rotational symmetry information. Third, and most practically, it applies Frame Averaging to an ImageNet-pretrained ResNet-18, averaging the encoder output over all C8 rotations to achieve equivariance without modifying any pretrained weights. The best configuration (Pretrain + FA with relative trajectory and large-FOV eye-in-hand) achieves 61.4% mean success on MimicGen, within 2.5% of the fully equivariant EquiDiff that uses four RGBD cameras and voxel inputs, while requiring only a single RGB camera and a standard diffusion UNet.

## Results

On the 12-task MimicGen benchmark, the proposed Pretrain+FA method (pretrained ResNet-18 with Frame Averaging, eye-in-hand RGB, relative trajectory) achieves a mean success rate of 61.4%, outperforming the image-based EquiDiff baseline (53.7%) by 7.7% and reaching within 2.5% of the voxel-based EquiDiff (63.9%) that uses four RGBD cameras. Incorporating equivariance into the encoder (Equi Enc vs. CNN Enc) yields a 9.1% average improvement, and adding Frame Averaging to a pretrained encoder (Pretrain+FA vs. Pretrain) adds a further 4.1%. On Robomimic, the method achieves 93.4% mean success, surpassing EquiDiff (90.4%) and vanilla Diffusion Policy (87.9%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two primary baselines: EquiDiff (Wang et al., CoRL 2024) and vanilla Diffusion Policy (Chi et al., RSS 2023). Against EquiDiff with image inputs (EquiDiff-Im, 53.7% mean), the proposed Pretrain+FA method (61.4%) wins by 7.7% on average across all 12 MimicGen tasks, outperforming it on 10 of 12 tasks. Against the stronger voxel-based EquiDiff (EquiDiff-Vo, 63.9%, using four RGBD cameras), Pretrain+FA trails by only 2.5% on average while using a single eye-in-hand RGB image and a far simpler architecture. On Robomimic, the method (93.4%) outperforms both EquiDiff (90.4%) and vanilla Diffusion Policy (87.9%). Relative to the original Diffusion Policy setup (In-Hand + External, absolute trajectory, 42.0% mean on MimicGen), the full proposed pipeline (invariant representation + equivariant encoder) delivers a 4.7–19.4% improvement depending on the encoder variant.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Diffusion Policy (Chi et al., RSS 2023) as its base framework and on Frame Averaging (Puny et al., ICLR 2022) as a mechanism to retrofit equivariance onto pretrained vision encoders. It positions itself as a practical alternative to fully equivariant diffusion policies like EquiDiff (Wang et al., CoRL 2024), arguing that the symmetry benefits of end-to-end equivariant architectures can be decomposed into three modular components: invariant action/perception representations, equivariant feature extraction, and standard diffusion denoising. The key departure from prior equivariant policy work is the claim that no specialized equivariant denoising network is needed—only the vision encoder requires symmetry-aware design, while the diffusion head remains a standard UNet.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical recipe (relative trajectory + eye-in-hand + Frame Averaging on a pretrained ResNet-18) that achieves performance within 2.5% of a much more complex fully-equivariant system using four RGBD cameras, while requiring only a single RGB camera and minimal architectural changes. This is likely to be widely adopted by the diffusion policy community as a low-overhead way to gain symmetry benefits. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to physical robots, whether it extends to other policy frameworks (e.g., ACT), and whether the computational cost of Frame Averaging (~2× training time) is acceptable at scale. The single-camera limitation (occlusion, insufficient context for long-horizon tasks) remains the primary practical bottleneck.
