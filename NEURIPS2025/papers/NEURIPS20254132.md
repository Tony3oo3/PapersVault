## Problem

Mathematical constants like π have accumulated hundreds of distinct formulas over centuries, yet the underlying connections among them remain largely unknown, with no unifying theory or systematic method for discovering and proving relations between them. Prior AI-for-mathematics work has focused on theorem proving, conjecture generation, and regression, but no approach has addressed the problem of symbolic unification of mathematical knowledge at scale.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254132/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system ingests raw LaTeX source from 455,050 arXiv papers, extracting 278M mathematical strings and filtering down to 121K π-containing equations via regex, then to 1,656 formula candidates via GPT-4o classification. The pipeline outputs 385 validated π formulas converted to minimal polynomial recurrences (149 order-2 and 4 order-3 canonical forms), coboundary equivalence proofs with explicit transformation matrices, and a forest of unified formulas embedded in a 3D Conservative Matrix Field. Validation relies on PSLQ integer-relation checking rather than direct limit extraction from LaTeX, which corrects common LLM parsing errors.

## Method

The pipeline combines LLM-based harvesting (GPT-4o mini for classification, GPT-4o for LaTeX-to-code extraction with a 3-round feedback loop) with a novel symbolic algorithm called UMAPS for coboundary equivalence solving. Formulas are first converted to canonical polynomial recurrences via RISC's Guess tool and Maple's MinimalRecurrence package, then clustered by dynamical metrics (irrationality measure δ and convergence rate r). UMAPS avoids solving the inherently nonlinear coboundary equation by leveraging Lemma 1: it first solves for U(1) via the Möbius relation between recurrence limits, propagates empirical coboundary matrices up to depth N, then fits rational functions to the measured entries and verifies the result symbolically. The unifying structure is a Conservative Matrix Field (CMF), a lattice of rational-function matrices satisfying path-independence conditions, within which equivalent formulas appear as parallel trajectories connected by coboundary transformations.

## Results

From 455,050 arXiv papers, the system validates 385 distinct formulas for π, of which 360 (94%) have proven relations and 166 (43%) are unified within a single Conservative Matrix Field (CMF). Converting to canonical forms reduces 385 formulas to 153 unique recurrences (149 order-2, 4 order-3), and 136/153 (89%) canonical forms have proven relations, with 81/153 (53%) residing in the same CMF. The method generalizes beyond π, proving equivalences for e, ζ(3), and Catalan's constant, and generates 1,693 new π formulas from the CMF, some with 2× better normalized convergence than the best pre-existing formula.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper benchmarks its UMAPS coboundary-solving algorithm against two leading LLMs (GPT-4o and Gemini 2.5 Pro Preview) on 10 formula-pair equivalences that UMAPS has already proven, chosen to be the simpler case where both formulas share equal dynamical metrics (r, δ) after folding. UMAPS proves all 10 equivalences with rigorous coboundary certificates. GPT-4o detects only 1/10 equivalences and produces 2/10 correct proofs, while Gemini 2.5 Pro Preview detects 8/10 but only 5/10 proofs are correct. The authors note that no LLM succeeded in finding relations between formula pairs without equal dynamical metrics, a regime where UMAPS operates routinely. For the LLM extraction sub-task, GPT-4o as extractor achieves 97.6% successful extraction (289/296), substantially outperforming Claude 3.7 Sonnet (89.9%) and GPT-4o mini (69.6%), with the gap driven primarily by faulty code generation rates (0.7% vs. 7.1% vs. 23.6%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Conservative Matrix Field (CMF) structure discovered by Elimelech et al. (PNAS 2024) and Weinbaum et al. (2025), which provides the mathematical scaffolding for unifying formulas as trajectories in a lattice of matrices. It also leverages the dynamical metrics (convergence rate, irrationality measure) from Shalyt et al. (NeurIPS 2024) for clustering, RISC's Guess tool for canonical form conversion, and PSLQ for numerical validation. The key departure is the introduction of UMAPS, a novel coboundary-solving algorithm, and the first LLM-symbolic-tool integration pipeline that automates the full harvesting-to-unification workflow at scale (455K arXiv papers). Prior AI-for-math work has focused on theorem proving, conjecture generation, and symbolic regression, but the authors explicitly position symbolic unification of existing formulas as an unaddressed problem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that 94% of 385 validated π formulas can be connected and 43% unified within a single 3D CMF, revealing a previously hidden mathematical structure that links formulas from Euler, Gauss, Brouncker, Ramanujan, and modern algorithmic discoveries. The generalization to e, ζ(3), and Catalan's constant suggests the approach could systematically organize knowledge across many constants, with the authors noting that D-finite functions may all admit CMF representations. Key limitations include reliance on LLMs for LaTeX parsing (introducing false negatives), handling primarily series and continued fractions, and the need for manual substitutions in some cases. Open questions include whether all π formulas can be captured in one or a few CMFs, and whether the approach scales to constants without known CMF structures.
