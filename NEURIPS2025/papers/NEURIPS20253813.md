## Problem

Existing multi-LLM systems are either fixed-weight (static, black-box models reused across contexts) or fixed-role (hand-crafted workflows with prompt-engineered interactions), making them inflexible across diverse tasks. No prior method jointly optimizes both the graph structure defining model roles and the model weights, leaving a gap in how to automatically discover task-adapted multi-LLM collaboration topologies and specialized model checkpoints.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253813/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pool of n LLM experts sharing the same architecture (e.g., 10 GEMMA-7B checkpoints fine-tuned on different Tulu-v2 domains) and a scalar utility function f (e.g., task accuracy). It produces an optimized directed acyclic graph of LLMs with adapted weights, which at inference time is called in topological order to generate a collaborative text answer. The role optimization operates on continuous n×n adjacency matrices that are decoded into discrete DAGs via G-DECODE, while the weight optimization operates directly on model parameter vectors, making both compatible with particle swarm optimization over continuous search spaces.

## Method

HETEROGENEOUS SWARMS alternates two PSO-driven steps: a role-step that learns the DAG structure and a weight-step that adapts model parameters. In the role-step, a swarm of continuous adjacency matrices is decoded into discrete DAGs via G-DECODE (iteratively selecting end nodes by inverse out-degree and connecting new nodes to existing ones with softmax-weighted probability), LLMs are called in topological order, and the matrices are updated via PSO based on utility scores. In the weight-step, the JFK-score quantifies each LLM's individual contribution by randomly assigning models to positions in the best-found DAG M times and computing a frequency-weighted average of utility across assignments; model weights are then optimized via PSO guided by these scores. The key insight is that PSO handles the non-differentiable, combinatorial nature of both graph structure search and discrete model selection, while the iterative alternation allows roles and weights to co-adapt: the DAG structure determines which roles each model plays, and the JFK-score evaluates contribution within that specific structure.

## Results

HETEROGENEOUS SWARMS achieves the best performance on 11 of 12 datasets, outperforming the second-best baseline by 18.5% on average. Headline scores include 0.312 on MMLU-pro, 0.481 on GSM8k, 0.660 on NLGraph, 0.250 on GAIA-text, and 0.590 on WoW. The only dataset where it does not rank first is NLGraph, where MODEL SWARMS achieves 0.672 vs. H-SWARMS's 0.660. Scaling the number of collaborating LLMs from 2 to 10 yields a 27.1% average improvement, demonstrating inference-time scaling for smaller models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HETEROGENEOUS SWARMS wins on 11 of 12 tasks against all 17 baselines, with the sole exception being NLGraph where MODEL SWARMS (0.672) edges out H-SWARMS (0.660). Against the strongest dynamic-weight baseline MODEL SWARMS, H-SWARMS gains +0.058 on MMLU-pro, +0.022 on K-Cross, +0.022 on GSM8k, and +0.143 on GAIA-text. Against the strongest dynamic-role baselines (MACNet, AgentVerse, GPT-Swarm), H-SWARMS leads by 0.060 on MMLU-pro, 0.107 on GAIA-text, and 0.080 on AB-ltp. Static-weight methods (Data Merge, Uniform Soup, DARE-TIES) and static-role methods (Chain, Star) are consistently outperformed by 10–30+ points across most tasks. The paper further shows that dynamic adaptation (both weight and role) outperforms static engineering by 30.1% and 8.2% respectively, and that the joint optimization of both dimensions is necessary: removing either the role-step or weight-step degrades performance on most tasks (Table 2 ablation).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate lines of multi-LLM research—role-based orchestration (GPT-Swarm, AgentVerse, MACNet, Meta-Agent) and weight-based adaptation (Model Swarms, LoraHub, Model Soups, DARE-TIES)—into a single joint optimization framework. It builds directly on the authors' prior Model Swarms work [26], which applied particle swarm optimization to LLM weight merging, by extending PSO to also optimize the graph topology (roles) via a new G-DECODE algorithm that maps continuous adjacency matrices to discrete DAGs. The key departure from prior work is the iterative alternation between role-step and weight-step, coupled with the JFK-score metric that quantifies individual model contribution across multiple random assignments, enabling weight optimization to be compatible with swarm intelligence in a multi-LLM context.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, task-adaptive framework for designing multi-LLM systems without hand-crafted prompts or fixed architectures, and reveals that the relative importance of roles versus weights is task-dependent (weight-dominant for knowledge tasks, role-dominant for agent tasks)—an insight that guides practitioners on where to invest optimization effort. Limitations include the requirement that all models share the same architecture for weight-step, the high optimization cost (O(n(N+M)) inferences per iteration), and evaluation restricted to 7B-scale models. Open questions include extending to heterogeneous architectures, allowing cycles in the DAG for iterative refinement, instance-level network adaptation, and integrating black-box APIs or tools as nodes.
