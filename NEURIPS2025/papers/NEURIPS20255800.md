## Problem

Existing One-Light-At-a-Time (OLAT) 3D relighting methods assume that test-time light sources follow the same spatial distribution as training lights, but real-world capture often yields biased or spatially limited lighting. Under out-of-distribution (OOD) lighting conditions, these methods overfit to perspective-constrained observations and produce unrealistic specular highlights, incorrect shadows, and color shifts. No prior OLAT method explicitly addresses the distribution shift between training and test illumination.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a set of OLAT captures where each image is paired with a unique camera pose and a unique point-light position (500–600 images per scene), plus camera intrinsics/extrinsics. At test time it accepts arbitrary point-light positions (potentially on the opposite hemisphere from training) or an environment map from which point lights are importance-sampled. The output is a 3D Gaussian scene representation in which each Gaussian carries a normal vector, a 3-channel diffuse albedo, and a 1-channel specular coefficient, enabling closed-form relighting to novel views under any lighting configuration.

## Method

MetaGS extends 3D Gaussian Splatting with a differentiable Blinn-Phong shading model: each Gaussian point is augmented with a surface normal, diffuse color k_d, and specular coefficient k_s, and its rendered color is computed as the sum of a zero-order SH ambient term and per-light diffuse/specular terms (Lambertian and Blinn-Phong intensities modulated by a BVH-ray-traced shadow transmittance). To combat overfitting under biased OLAT data, the authors introduce a bilevel meta-learning training loop that treats each lighting condition as a separate task: an inner loop adapts the Gaussian parameters on a support sample, and an outer loop updates the shared parameters using a query sample from a different lighting condition, thereby explicitly simulating OOD test scenarios during every gradient step. Training proceeds in three stages—core Gaussian attributes, then normal estimation, then full meta-learning with all shading components—using L1 + D-SSIM image loss, a sparse opacity loss, and progressive normal alignment with depth-inferred pseudo-normals.

## Results

On the OOD relighting task (Table 2), MetaGS achieves an average PSNR of 26.31 across 10 scenes (3 synthetic + 7 real-world), substantially outperforming all baselines. The largest gains are on the synthetic Ball scene (26.76 vs. 17.25 for NRHints, +9.51 dB) and the real-world FurScene (24.82 vs. 17.34 for GS³, +7.48 dB). In the camera-light-colocated setup (Table 4), MetaGS reaches 38.72/36.90/38.89 PSNR on the three synthetic scenes versus IRON's 26.99/34.43/36.22. Ablation (Table 3) shows meta-learning contributes +8.28 dB PSNR and the shadow module +5.89 dB over the full model's 27.42 PSNR on synthetic scenes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MetaGS wins on every scene against all three primary OOD baselines. Against NRHints [34], the gap ranges from +1.51 dB (RubCup) to +9.51 dB (Ball), with the largest margins on scenes with strong specular or shadow effects. Against GS³ [1], MetaGS leads by +7.92 dB on Ball and +7.48 dB on FurScene, where GS³'s implicit shading fails most severely. Against WildLight [5], the advantage is more moderate (+2.4 to +5.0 dB) since WildLight already uses a simpler capture protocol. In the camera-light-colocated setup, MetaGS surpasses IRON [35] by 11.73 dB on Ball and 2.47–2.67 dB on the cup scenes. For environment-map relighting (Figure 6), both Relightable 3DGS [8] and GaussianShader [9] fail to produce coherent results under OOD lighting, while MetaGS generalizes successfully. Table 6 further shows that the meta-learning scheme itself, when grafted onto NRHints, GS³, and RNG [7], yields consistent gains of +0.90 to +2.63 dB average PSNR, confirming its generality across rendering backends.

## Relation to Prior Work

MetaGS builds on the 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) rendering framework and the One Light At a Time (OLAT) relighting paradigm established by NRHints, GS³, and WildLight, which capture scenes with a single moving point light and camera. It departs from these methods in two key ways: (1) it embeds an explicit differentiable Blinn-Phong reflection model into per-Gaussian attributes (normals, diffuse color, specular coefficient), following the compositional-shading philosophy of DeferredGS and GaussianShader but within a meta-learned training loop; and (2) it introduces a bilevel-optimization meta-learning scheme—previously applied to image classification and few-shot learning but not to volume rendering—that treats each lighting condition as a task and validates learned parameters under OOD lighting during training. The meta-learning component is shown to be architecture-agnostic, improving NRHints, GS³, and RNG when grafted onto their respective pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The OOD relighting formulation (training lights on one hemisphere, test lights on the opposite) exposes a real generalization gap in current OLAT methods that prior work largely ignored, making it a more honest stress test for 3D relighting. The finding that a simple Blinn-Phong prior plus bilevel meta-learning outperforms more complex implicit shading models by 8+ dB in ablation suggests that explicit physical structure and distribution-shift-aware training are more important than representational capacity for relighting generalization. Open questions include extending the framework to indirect illumination, subsurface scattering, and anisotropic materials, as well as whether the meta-learning benefit persists at larger scene scales or with more diverse material libraries.
