## Problem

While memory-based meta-RL agents like RL2 can attain near Bayes-optimal policies in partially observable environments, their learned representations are not equivalent to the minimally sufficient Bayes-optimal belief states, limiting interpretability, robustness, and generalization. This paper investigates whether integrating self-supervised predictive coding modules into meta-RL can guide the agent toward learning compact, interpretable representations that closely approximate Bayes-optimal beliefs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250945/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each timestep the agent receives a scalar observation (discrete or continuous), the previous action in one-hot format, and a scalar reward. The RNN encoder compresses the full interaction history into a low-dimensional bottleneck that parameterizes the mean and variance of a latent belief state (half the bottleneck dimension is the latent, the other half is the variance). A separate feedforward policy network consumes only this belief state to produce action logits (or Gaussian mean/std for continuous control), decoupling representation learning from policy learning.

## Method

The proposed architecture pairs a VAE-based predictive module with a feedforward policy network. The RNN encoder is trained solely via the ELBO objective—predicting the next observation and reward conditioned on the action (predictive coding loss) plus a KL regularization term whose prior is set to the previous posterior, mirroring Bayesian filtering. Critically, policy gradients from the A2C loss are applied only to the policy network and are not backpropagated through the encoder, so the belief representation is shaped exclusively by the self-supervised predictive objective. This separation, combined with the KL constraint, encourages the bottleneck to learn a compact, task-relevant belief state rather than a verbose history encoding. The entire system is trained end-to-end via meta-learning over a task distribution, requiring no privileged information or separate random policies.

## Results

Across all six POMDP task classes, the bottleneck layer of meta-RL with predictive modules consistently achieves the lowest state dissimilarity (D_s) and output dissimilarity (D_o) in both mapping directions of the state machine simulation, indicating closest equivalence to Bayes-optimal belief states. The most dramatic improvement is in the oracle bandit, where D_s^{Bayes→meta-RL} drops from 51.29±6.73 (RL²) to 2.33±1.34 (proposed), and in the two-armed Bernoulli bandit from 5.38±1.81 to 0.81±0.11 (unit: 1e-2). In the hardest dynamic Tiger setting (observation accuracy 0.7), only the proposed method approaches Bayes-optimal return, while RL² remains significantly suboptimal. Recurrent layers in both models fail to approximate Bayes-optimal states (large D_s after training), confirming that the predictive VAE bottleneck—not the RNN memory—is where interpretable belief representations emerge.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is RL² (Duan et al., 2016), a black-box memory-based meta-RL agent architecturally matched to the proposed model except for the absence of predictive decoders. The proposed meta-RL with predictive modules wins on every task in the state machine simulation: its bottleneck layer achieves significantly lower D_s in the Bayes→meta-RL direction (the critical test of whether the agent can simulate the Bayes-optimal agent) across all six task classes, with the largest gap in the oracle bandit (22× lower D_s). Beyond representation quality, the comparison reveals a qualitative failure mode: in the oracle bandit, RL² converges to a suboptimal policy (sampling the oracle arm upfront but failing to consistently use the acquired information), while the proposed method learns the Bayes-optimal policy of paying the exploration cost and exploiting the information. Similarly, in the hardest dynamic Tiger variant (observation accuracy 0.7), only the proposed method approaches Bayes-optimal return. The ablation studies further confirm that the gains are attributable to the predictive loss (not policy gradients through the encoder) and that KL regularization is necessary to maintain compact, belief-like representations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the memory-based meta-RL framework of RL² (Duan et al., 2016) by inserting a VAE-based predictive coding module between the RNN encoder and the policy network, building on prior work that added predictive auxiliary losses to improve meta-RL performance (Igl et al., 2018; Zintgraf et al., 2021; Akuzawa et al., 2021). Its key methodological departure is the adoption of state machine simulation analysis from Mikulik et al. (2020)—previously applied only to stationary bandits—to rigorously compare learned representations against ground-truth Bayes-optimal belief states across diverse POMDPs. Conceptually, the paper bridges predictive coding theories from neuroscience (Rao & Ballard, 1999; Friston, 2005) with deep RL, arguing that self-supervised future prediction serves as a neurally plausible mechanism for Bayesian belief updating rather than merely a performance-boosting regularizer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first systematic, interpretability-grounded evidence that predictive objectives in meta-RL produce representations structurally closer to minimally sufficient Bayes-optimal belief states, and that this representational fidelity translates into improved generalization (zero-shot and transfer) to unseen but related tasks. A notable practical finding is that in the oracle bandit task requiring active information seeking, only the predictive-module variant learns the optimal policy, suggesting that representation quality is a bottleneck for exploration-heavy POMDPs that black-box meta-RL cannot overcome. Limitations include evaluation restricted to small-scale POMDPs with tractable Bayes-optimal solutions, use of only next-step (not multi-step) prediction, and the absence of comparison against other recent belief-state methods (e.g., flow-based or decoupled belief modeling). Open questions include whether multi-step and temporally abstracted predictive objectives further improve representation, and how these findings scale to high-dimensional continuous POMDPs where Bayes-optimal solutions are intractable.
