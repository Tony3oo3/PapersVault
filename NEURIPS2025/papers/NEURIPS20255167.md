## Problem

Layer pruning of LLMs is an attractive compression strategy because it removes entire Transformer layers without requiring hardware-specific kernels, yet existing methods suffer sharp performance drops. The paper identifies the root cause as a previously overlooked activation magnitude mismatch at the pruning interface, where pre- and post-interface activations exhibit different scales and massive outliers in special tokens (e.g., [BOS]) further exacerbate the distributional shift propagating through remaining layers.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255167/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a layer-pruned LLM (with n consecutive layers removed at index ℓ*) and a small calibration set (128 sentences of length 2048 from WikiText-2) to estimate channel-wise scaling factors in the Hadamard-rotated space. It outputs a single C×C real symmetric patch matrix P = HDH^T inserted at the pruning interface, requiring only one additional GEMM at inference. Optionally, with 5K unlabeled WikiText-2 samples, P is further refined via offline KL-divergence logits distillation (top-K=100), completing in ~30 minutes on a single V100 GPU while all other model parameters remain frozen.

## Method

LINEARPATCH addresses activation mismatch by fusing two operations into one matrix multiply at the pruning interface: a Walsh-Hadamard rotation that redistributes massive token-specific outliers across all channels (reducing token-wise scaling variance σ_d from 2137 to 230), followed by a channel-wise diagonal scaling D that aligns mean activation magnitudes between the pre- and post-interface layers. By the spectral theorem, the product HDH^T is a real symmetric matrix P, so the entire correction reduces to a single linear layer with negligible inference overhead (~8× fewer parameters and ~190× faster than LLM-Streamline's FFN replacement). For post-training refinement, the paper introduces a memory-efficient offline distillation strategy: teacher logits are pre-computed and stored as top-K (K=100) probability distributions, reducing storage by 320×, and only the symmetric matrix P is optimized via KL divergence while freezing all other parameters. This design avoids the instability of randomly initialized replacement networks and the overfitting observed with MSE-based feature distillation, yielding consistent gains across LLaMA-2-7B/13B, LLaMA-3-8B, Baichuan2-7B, and DeepSeek-R1-Distill models.

## Results

On LLaMA-3-8B with 5 out of 32 layers pruned, LINEARPATCH[L] achieves 94.15% retained performance (RP) on the QA benchmark, outperforming LLM-Streamline(None) (90.84%), ShortGPT (89.27%), SLEB (83.88%), and LLM-Pruner (86.23%). With optional 5K-sample knowledge distillation, RP further improves to 95.16%, while LLM-Streamline+FT drops to only 74.34% on the same configuration. On LLaMA-2-7B with 7/32 layers pruned, LINEARPATCH[S/L] reaches 88.88% RP versus 86.06% for ShortGPT/LLM-Streamline and 83.66% for SLEB. On PPL benchmarks, LINEARPATCH reduces average perplexity from 33.87 to 24.59 on LLaMA-2-7B (7/32 pruned) and rescues LLM-Streamline's catastrophic 2839.3 PPL on LLaMA-3-8B (7/32 pruned) down to 85.10.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LINEARPATCH consistently outperforms all five baselines across every model and pruning configuration tested. Against LLM-Pruner (gradient-based), the gap is largest on LLaMA-3-8B (5/32): 94.15% vs. 86.23% RP, a 7.92-point margin. Against SLEB (PPL-based), LINEARPATCH leads by 3.18–10.27 points depending on configuration. Against ShortGPT and LLM-Streamline(None) (both cosine-similarity-based, which prune identical layer sets), LINEARPATCH[S/L] gains 3.18–3.79 points in RP, confirming the benefit comes from the patch itself rather than layer selection. The most dramatic comparison is with LLM-Streamline+FT (the SOTA post-training method): on LLaMA-3-8B (5/32), LINEARPATCH[L]+FT achieves 95.16% RP while LLM-Streamline+FT collapses to 74.34%, and on PPL the gap widens from 12.23 to 228.70. Taylor+ (Shortened LLaMA) suffers catastrophic PPL degradation (2840.38) on LLaMA-3-8B (7/32), which LINEARPATCH reduces to 236.49, though the model still performs poorly on QA (49.74% vs. 64.42% for LINEARPATCH[S]).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: layer pruning for LLMs (ShortGPT, SLEB, LLM-Streamline, Shortened LLaMA) and outlier suppression via Hadamard rotations (QuaRot, SpinQuant). It departs from prior layer-pruning methods by identifying a previously overlooked failure mode—activation magnitude mismatch at the pruning interface—rather than proposing a new pruning metric or replacement network. The Hadamard transformation is borrowed from the quantization literature (specifically QuaRot's construction for non-power-of-2 dimensions), but here it is repurposed not for quantization robustness but for redistributing token-level outliers so that a single channel-wise scaling vector can align activations across the pruning boundary. The optional fine-tuning step adapts knowledge distillation into a memory-efficient offline form (top-K logits, teacher stored on disk), contrasting with LLM-Streamline's approach of replacing pruned layers with a randomly initialized lightweight network.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for deployment: layer pruning is the most hardware-agnostic compression technique for LLMs, yet the sharp performance drop has limited its adoption. By showing that a single symmetric matrix (Hadamard × diagonal scaling) inserted at the pruning interface recovers most of the lost performance with negligible inference overhead, this work lowers the barrier to practical layer pruning. Limitations include evaluation only up to ~30% pruning ratio, testing on decoder-only architectures, and the method being specific to the pruning interface rather than a general activation-normalization technique. Open questions include whether the approach extends to more aggressive pruning, to encoder-decoder or MoE architectures, and whether the Hadamard-plus-scaling formulation is truly optimal versus learned rotations.
