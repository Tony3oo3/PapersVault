## Problem

Video generative models (e.g., Sora) can synthesize visually appealing 4D content but produce physically implausible results: geometry changes after occlusion, limbs swap during intersections, and infeasible deformations corrupt the 3D structure. The paper asks whether the geometry and articulation priors distilled from a real-world video can be used to correct these artifacts and produce physically consistent 4D content, introducing the new task of video-conditioned 4D restaging.

## Input / Output

The method takes a monocular base video (providing shape, articulation, and geometry) and a text prompt describing the desired motion. The first frame of the base video is fed to an image-to-video diffusion model (Sora, CausVid, or LTX-Video) to synthesize a driving video, which is then concatenated with the temporally reversed base video for joint optimization. The output is a deformable 4D Gaussian Splatting scene parameterized by low-rank SE(3) motion bases and per-point motion coefficients, from which novel-view renderings and 3D point tracks can be extracted. The pipeline relies on 2D priors (TAPIR tracking, MegaSAM camera poses, VideoDepthAnything depth, SAM segmentation) and runs on a single A100 GPU (~40 min per 100-frame sequence).

## Method

Restage4D builds on Shape-of-Motion's low-rank motion decomposition (K rigid-body bases in SE(3) weighted by per-point coefficients) but introduces a video-rewinding joint-training scheme: the base video is played backward and concatenated with the driving video so that both sequences share a canonical frame and motion coefficients, enabling geometry supervision from the real clip to propagate into the synthetic segment. An occlusion-aware rigidity loss (inspired by ARAP) computes per-point invisibility scores from the rendered depth buffer and applies L2 distance-preservation constraints only to occluded regions, avoiding the oversmoothing that a global rigidity penalty causes (PSNR drops 5.24 dB with naive ARAP). A disocclusion backtracing mechanism recovers canonical geometry for points that appear only in the driving video by inverting the motion transformation to project them back to the canonical frame. The two-stage training (motion initialization with tracking supervision, then photometric fusion) uses 100 motion bases and a rigidity weight of 10⁻³ to balance geometric consistency with high-frequency motion expressiveness.

## Results

On the DAVIS dataset, Restage4D achieves a PSNR of 27.38, outperforming SC-GS (22.69) and Vidu4D (19.77) by 4.69 and 7.61 dB respectively. On the Neural3DV dataset (novel-view evaluation with ground truth), Restage4D reaches 17.78 PSNR versus 16.71 for SC-GS and 14.74 for Vidu4D. In the ablation on 20 DAVIS sequences, the occlusion-aware ARAP (o-ARAP) with base video improves PSNR to 27.12 (vs. 26.71 baseline) while boosting occlusion-view CLIP similarity from 81.68 to 85.40 and volume consistency from 7.41 to 10.32, whereas naive ARAP degrades PSNR by 5.24 dB due to over-constraining visible regions. On PointOdyssey 3D tracking, the full pipeline reduces tracking loss to 0.05 compared to 0.06 without backtracing and 0.09 without rigidity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Restage4D is compared against two 4D reconstruction baselines: Vidu4D (NeurIPS 2024), which reconstructs 4D scenes from a single generated video using dynamic Gaussian surfels, and SC-GS (CVPR 2024), which uses sparse-controlled Gaussian splatting for editable dynamic scenes. Restage4D outperforms both on both evaluation datasets: on DAVIS it leads SC-GS by 4.69 dB and Vidu4D by 7.61 dB in PSNR, and on Neural3DV (novel-view ground truth) it leads SC-GS by 1.07 dB and Vidu4D by 3.04 dB. The gap is largest on DAVIS, where the geometry-preserving video-rewinding strategy and occlusion-aware rigidity regularization provide the most benefit over methods that rely solely on the generated driving video without the base video's articulation priors. The paper also demonstrates that Restage4D is agnostic to the I2V backbone, achieving comparable PSNR (23.68–28.05) across Sora, CausVid, and LTX-Video, whereas the baselines are not evaluated under this multi-backbone setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Shape-of-Motion's low-rank decomposed 4D Gaussian Splatting representation, repurposing its motion bases and coefficients as a shared 'skinning weight' mechanism that can be driven by an external video. It departs from keypoint-based motion retargeting methods (SC-GS, D-MiSo) by adopting an example-driven paradigm where a full generated video serves as the motion source rather than explicit control trajectories. The video-rewinding joint training strategy, occlusion-aware ARAP regularization, and disocclusion backtracing are novel contributions that bridge the gap between generative video models (Sora, CausVid) and geometry-preserving 3D reconstruction, a combination not previously explored in the 4D synthesis literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that real-world video priors can automatically correct geometric and motion hallucinations introduced by video diffusion models, offering a practical path toward physically faithful 4D content creation without requiring multi-view captures. The 4D restaging task opens a new direction for controllable 4D generation where motion is specified via video rather than text or keyframes. Key limitations include the assumption of temporal object consistency, dependence on the quality of the underlying I2V model, and potential risks for human-centric content. Open questions include scalability to longer sequences, handling of topologically changing objects, and whether the video-rewinding strategy generalizes to multi-object or scene-level restaging.
