## Problem

Existing temporal causal representation learning methods assume a single layer of latent variables and rely on historical observations as surrogates to satisfy sufficient-change conditions. Under hierarchical latent dynamics, observations are generated from lower-level variables contaminated by noise from higher layers, making it impossible to recover the joint distribution of all latent layers from a single timestep. This paper asks whether and how the joint distribution of multi-layer latent variables can be uniquely identified from temporal contextual observations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250662/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a window of 2L+1 temporally adjacent observed vectors (where L is the number of latent layers and each observation is n-dimensional), which is the minimal number of timesteps required for identification. It produces L layers of n-dimensional latent variables at the current timestep, organized hierarchically so that layer l is conditionally dependent on its time-delayed parent (layer l at t−1) and its hierarchical parent (layer l+1 at t). The latent variables are then decoded step-by-step to generate the observed time series, enabling both controllable generation and hierarchical interpolation.

## Method

The CHiLD framework establishes two identification results: Theorem 1 proves block-wise identifiability of the full multi-layer latent vector by matching the joint distribution of 2L+1 adjacent observations and exploiting injectivity of linear operators (spectral decomposition), while Theorem 2 leverages the natural sparsity of the hierarchical DAG—specifically, the zero cross-derivative between non-adjacent layers—to achieve block-wise identifiability within each layer without permutation. The practical model is a VAE trained via ELBO, where a TCN-based contextual hierarchical encoder maps the 2L+1 observation window to layered latent variables (each layer encoded from the previous), a step-wise decoder reconstructs observations from the bottom layer, and normalizing flow-based hierarchical prior networks enforce the independent-noise condition by transforming each latent component to Gaussian noise using its time-delayed and hierarchical parents as conditioning inputs.

## Results

On synthetic data (Table 2), CHiLD achieves the highest Mean Correlation Coefficient (MCC) on 4 out of 7 datasets (A: 0.901, C: 0.835, D: 0.841, E: 0.862), outperforming the strongest baselines IDOL and CaRiNG by 4–14 percentage points on hierarchical datasets. On real-world time series generation (Table 3), CHiLD achieves the best Context-FID across all 9 datasets, with particularly large margins on Weather (0.507 vs. 1.694 for IDOL, 6.434 for TimeGAN) and CESM2 (0.018 vs. 0.142 for IDOL, 3.255 for TimeGAN). CHiLD also attains the best Correlational Score on 8 of 9 datasets, e.g., 0.165 on Weather vs. 1.195 (IDOL) and 3.438 (TimeGAN).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CHiLD outperforms all 14 baselines on the majority of real-world datasets for both Context-FID and Correlational Score. Against the closest causal-representation competitor IDOL [48], CHiLD achieves substantially lower Context-FID (e.g., 0.507 vs. 1.694 on Weather; 0.018 vs. 0.142 on CESM2) and lower Correlational Score (0.165 vs. 1.195 on Weather), confirming that hierarchical latent structure improves generation quality. On synthetic identifiability benchmarks (Table 2), CHiLD leads IDOL and CaRiNG [5] by 4–14 MCC points on hierarchical datasets (A, C, D, E, F, G), while all three methods perform comparably on the single-layer Dataset B (≈0.94), validating that the advantage arises specifically from handling multi-layer dynamics. Compared to non-causal VAE baselines (TimeVAE, KoVAE, cwVAE), CHiLD's hierarchical prior and contextual encoder yield 2–3× lower Context-FID on climate and motion datasets. Against diffusion and GAN-based generators (Diffusion-TS, TimeGAN), CHiLD achieves 5–20× lower Context-FID on human motion data (e.g., 0.029 vs. 3.761 on Discussion), demonstrating that explicit causal structure is more effective than purely distributional matching for high-dimensional temporal generation. The only notable exception is WeatherBench Correlational Score, where CHiLD (5.190) trails TimeVAE (24.873) and KoVAE (20.384) but still beats IDOL (34.773) and all other baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

CHiLD extends the temporal causal representation learning line (TDRL, PCL, CaRiNG, IDOL) from single-layer latent variables to multi-layer hierarchical dynamics, addressing a structural gap the authors argue makes prior methods fundamentally inapplicable. The identification theory builds on the linear operator and spectral decomposition framework of Hu & Schennach [23] and Hu & Shum [25], while the sparsity-based within-layer identification draws on IDOL [48] and the cross-derivative/conditional-independence connection from Lin [52]. The VAE-based implementation adapts the normalizing-flow prior structure from TDRL [74] and CaRiNG [5], but generalizes the noise estimation to incorporate both time-delayed and hierarchical parent variables rather than only temporal ones.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first identifiability guarantees for hierarchical temporal latent dynamics, establishing that 2L+1 adjacent observations are both necessary and sufficient—a clean quantitative result that clarifies the information-theoretic requirements of the problem. Practically, this enables causal time series generation in domains with natural multi-scale structure (climate, finance, human motion), where prior single-layer methods provably fail. Key open questions include relaxing the layer-wise conditional independence assumption needed for component-wise identifiability, verifying the sufficient variability condition in high-dimensional real-world settings, and extending the framework to more complex generative tasks like video.
