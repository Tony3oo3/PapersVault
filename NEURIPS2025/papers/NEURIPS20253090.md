## Problem

Existing camera-based collaborative perception methods do not explicitly model the camera imaging process of the same object from different agent viewpoints, making it difficult to resolve depth estimation ambiguity. Individual agents predict multiple candidate targets along each camera ray, and without cross-view validation of ray occupancy, the network cannot distinguish true positives from false positives along those rays, leading to suboptimal 3D detection performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253090/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view surround-camera images from the ego agent and up to four collaborators (600×800 on simulated datasets, 1080×1920 on DAIR-V2X), along with agent pose information for coordinate alignment. Collaborators broadcast sparse instance-level messages (top-M=200 instances per agent, ~276.6 KB/frame) containing instance features, 3D anchors, and per-camera ray occupancy states rather than dense BEV or image features. The output is a set of 3D bounding boxes with category, center position, dimensions, yaw orientation, and 3D velocity for all detected objects within a ±51.2 m perception range.

## Method

RayFusion builds on Sparse4D as the single-agent detector and introduces three collaborative modules. First, a spatial-temporal alignment module unifies all agents into the ego coordinate system and compensates for both instance motion (modeled as uniform velocity) and ego motion (via pose transformation) to handle communication delays. Second, a ray occupancy information encoding module explicitly represents each instance's imaging geometry: camera rays are encoded via high-frequency sinusoidal positional mappings of the optical center and direction vector, depth distributions are projected onto the ray direction to form occupancy states, and multi-camera encodings are fused through masked attention. Third, a multi-scale instance feature aggregation module uses pyramid window self-attention with three parallel branches (receptive fields of 4 m, 8 m, 16 m) to capture both local and global spatial context, improving robustness to localization noise. The ray-driven positional encoding is added to the anchor encoding and fed into the detection head, enabling the network to cross-validate true 3D positions across viewpoints and suppress redundant predictions along camera rays.

## Results

RayFusion achieves state-of-the-art 3D detection performance across all three benchmarks under perfect settings: AP70 of 11.54 on DAIR-V2X, 53.14 on V2XSet, and 74.25 on OPV2V, improving over the previous SOTA (IFTR) by 3.64, 3.47, and 8.21 AP70 points respectively. On the real-world DAIR-V2X dataset, RayFusion reaches AP50 of 26.29, surpassing IFTR (20.51) by 5.78 points. Additionally, RayFusion runs at 8.79 FPS on a single RTX 3090, more than 2× faster than IFTR (3.36 FPS), while using only 276.6 KB per frame of communication versus IFTR's 18.8 MB.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RayFusion outperforms all five compared baselines on every dataset and metric. Against the previous SOTA IFTR (ECCV'24), RayFusion gains +5.78 AP50 and +3.64 AP70 on DAIR-V2X, −1.41 AP50 but +3.47 AP70 on V2XSet, and +1.03 AP50 and +8.21 AP70 on OPV2V, with the largest margin on OPV2V where the simulated multi-agent setup best exploits ray occupancy cross-validation. Compared to V2X-ViT (ECCV'22), RayFusion improves AP70 by 8.47, 11.91, and 15.87 on DAIR-V2X, V2XSet, and OPV2V respectively. Against Where2comm (NeurIPS'22), the AP70 gains are 7.87, 9.18, and 15.65 across the three datasets. Over CoAlign (ICRA'23), RayFusion leads by 8.31, 13.50, and 13.79 AP70. Against V2VNet (ECCV'20), the improvements are 8.57, 14.14, and 16.66 AP70. Notably, RayFusion also surpasses IFTR in inference speed (8.79 vs. 3.36 FPS) and reduces communication cost by ~68× (276.6 KB vs. 18.8 MB per frame), making it substantially more practical for real-world deployment.

## Relation to Prior Work

RayFusion builds on Sparse4D as its single-agent detector but departs fundamentally from the dominant dense-BEV collaborative perception paradigm (V2X-ViT, IFTR, CoBEVT) by operating entirely in sparse instance-query space. It also extends the query-based collaborative line (TransIFF, CoSparse4D) by introducing ray occupancy information as an explicit encoding of the camera imaging geometry, drawing on high-frequency positional encoding ideas from NeRF to represent camera rays. The pyramid window self-attention for multi-scale instance interaction is a new design that replaces the uniform attention used in prior instance-level fusion methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ray occupancy formulation provides a principled, interpretable mechanism for cross-view depth disambiguation that prior BEV-based or attention-only methods lack, and the resulting 8.21 AP70 gain on OPV2V plus ~26× lower communication cost (276.6 KB vs 18.8 MB) makes sparse collaborative perception a practical alternative for bandwidth-constrained V2X deployments. Key limitations include the reliance on known agent poses (privacy concern), the uniform-motion assumption for temporal alignment, and the significant performance gap between simulated and real-world (DAIR-V2X) settings. Open questions include whether the approach extends to unknown-pose collaboration and whether richer occupancy representations (e.g., temporal propagation) can further close the communication-delay robustness gap.
