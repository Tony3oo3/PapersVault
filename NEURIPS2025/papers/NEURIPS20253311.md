## Problem

Existing LLM unlearning methods face a fundamental trade-off: aggressive approaches (e.g., Gradient Ascent) forcibly erase target knowledge but cause catastrophic degradation of neighbouring and general capabilities, while conservative logit-manipulation methods (e.g., IHL, ULD) preserve utility yet leave latent semantic activations that produce confident hallucinated completions when queried about unlearned content. No prior method simultaneously achieves strong unlearning effectiveness, high utility preservation, and hallucination-free refusal behavior.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained LLM (LLaMA-2-7B or GPT-NEO 125M/1.3B/2.7B), a target unlearning dataset (e.g., 100 fictitious author QA pairs in ToFU or pretraining text slices in TDEC), and a retained dataset of neighbouring and general knowledge. Token importance is estimated per-sample via POS tagging for well-structured text or entropy-based masking for noisy corpora, with a configurable percentile threshold (default 60%) selecting which tokens to suppress. The output is a modified LLM with ~16.8M trainable attention adapter parameters (base model frozen) that produces refusal or minimal responses (e.g., 'Nobody knows') for unlearned queries while maintaining fluent, accurate generation on retained knowledge.

## Method

AS performs two complementary attention-level interventions via lightweight adapters inserted after the Q/K (or V) projections in each attention head. First, an Attention Suppression (ASP) loss minimizes KL divergence between the model's attention and a precomputed suppressed reference that downweights fact-bearing tokens in the unlearning set by a factor (1−λ), with normalization preserving valid probability distributions. Second, an Attention Reinforcement (AKL) loss applies the inverse operation on the retained dataset, boosting attention toward semantically important tokens to stabilize outputs. The two losses are jointly optimized as L_AS = α·L_ASP + (1−α)·L_AKL, where α can follow a dynamic schedule that adapts to gradient conflict between the two objectives during training. Unlike logit-manipulation baselines that substitute target tokens with alternatives, AS structurally blocks the flow of memorized knowledge during generation by reallocating attention mass from content-bearing tokens to neutral/syntactic tokens, enabling the model to 'forget through omission' rather than substitution and thereby eliminating the hallucination pathway.

## Results

On the ToFU benchmark (5 fictitious authors, 100 samples), AS achieves a Top-5 exclusion rate (TR) of 0.97 and ROUGE-L of 0.16 on the target unlearning set, while preserving neighbouring knowledge (NEK) accuracy at 0.76 (+0.06 over the original model) and general knowledge (GEK) accuracy at 0.80 (−0.03). AS achieves 0% reproduction rate and 0% hallucination rate, compared to up to 62% hallucination for GA+GD and 59% for NPO+GD. On the TDEC benchmark, AS achieves up to 10% higher accuracy than state-of-the-art baselines while meeting the extraction-likelihood threshold (el₁₀ ≤ 0.05).

## Baselines & Comparisons

AS dominates all four baselines on the critical utility-preservation metrics: it achieves NEK accuracy of 0.76 and GEK accuracy of 0.80, compared to GA (0.23/0.32), NPO (0.26/0.25), IHL (0.33/0.52), and ULD (0.56/0.50) — representing improvements of up to 53 percentage points over aggressive methods. On unlearning effectiveness (TR@5), AS (0.97) matches GA (0.98) and NPO (0.97) but substantially outperforms the conservative logit-manipulation methods IHL (0.62) and ULD (0.47), indicating that target tokens are more thoroughly removed from the model's top predictions. On hallucination suppression, AS achieves 0% hallucination and 0% reproduction, while ULD generates factually incorrect but fluent answers (e.g., 'born in London, England' instead of 'Brussels, Belgium') and IHL produces partially correct but misleading completions. The FQ metric favours ULD (0.89) and IHL (0.68) over AS (0.17), but the authors argue FQ is misleading in hallucination-sensitive settings since AS's divergence from retrained-model behaviour reflects intentional refusal rather than residual knowledge. AS also demonstrates superior robustness under adversarial prompt variants (TR@50 > 92% across rephrased, perturbed, and noised queries) and maintains stable performance across sequential unlearning requests on TDEC, where ULD degrades due to its reliance on method-specific data processing.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the token-importance estimation idea from SAR (Duan et al., ACL 2024), which reallocates attention toward salient tokens at inference time for uncertainty quantification, and inverts it into a training-time unlearning mechanism: suppressing attention to high-importance fact-bearing tokens via lightweight adapters. Conceptually, it positions itself as a 'controlled aggressive' method that sits between gradient-based unlearning (GA, NPO) that destroys utility and logit-manipulation methods (IHL, ULD) that risk hallucinated substitutions. The key architectural departure from prior unlearning work is intervening at the attention level rather than the logit or embedding level, using a dual KL-divergence loss (suppression on target data, reinforcement on retained data) to form a soft boundary that localizes forgetting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical and increasingly urgent tension in LLM deployment: GDPR-style 'right to be forgotten' requests must be satisfied without degrading model utility or introducing hallucinated replacements. The attention-shifting approach is attractive because it is parameter-efficient (~12M adapters on a 7B model) and produces refusal-style outputs ('Nobody knows') rather than fabricated alternatives. However, the authors acknowledge that this is behavioral suppression rather than representational erasure — latent traces may persist and be exposed by sophisticated probing or retraining. Open questions include whether attention-level methods can be combined with representation-level editing (e.g., projection-based erasure) for stronger guarantees, and whether the approach generalizes to noisy, unstructured corpora where token-importance estimation is less reliable.
