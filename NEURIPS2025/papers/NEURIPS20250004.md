## Problem

Existing occupancy world models implicitly learn the intertwined effects of ego-vehicle motion (perspective shifts) and scene evolution (agent interactions), resulting in poor spatial consistency. The paper demonstrates this gap quantitatively: a vanilla U-Net in scene-centric coordinates achieves 44% higher mIoU than the SOTA DOME model in ego-centric coordinates, revealing that no existing method explicitly disentangles these two sources of change.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250004/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a 4-frame historical 3D occupancy sequence (200×200×16 voxels at 0.4 m resolution over a 80 m × 80 m × 6.4 m region), ego-vehicle trajectory (ground-truth or predicted waypoints with yaw), and optionally BEV semantic layouts. It produces a future 3D occupancy sequence at 2 fps over a 3-second horizon (extendable to 8 s). A key implementation detail is that the scene-centric forecasting module first warps all historical frames into the coordinate frame of the current ego-pose via nearest-neighbor voxel resampling, predicts in that unified frame, then warps predictions back to each future ego-pose before encoding into the Occ-VAE latent space.

## Method

COME is a three-stage framework that decouples ego-motion from scene dynamics. Stage 1 trains a spatiotemporal Diffusion Transformer (DiT) world model with an Occ-VAE for latent occupancy generation, modified with skip connections between early and late layers to accept control features. Stage 2 trains a lightweight UNet that predicts future occupancy in a scene-centric (ego-invariant) coordinate frame, where over 92.7% of voxels are static, making the task substantially easier. Stage 3 introduces the COME ControlNet—a trainable copy of the world model's first N/2 blocks followed by zero-convolution layers—that encodes the scene-centric forecasts into control features and injects them via residual addition into the second half of the world model. A visibility-aware masking strategy suppresses control features in spatially unobserved regions (where the simple UNet lacks generative capacity) by computing per-pillar observation ratios from historical occupancy, ensuring the generative model retains imagination in novel-view areas while benefiting from spatial consistency in observed regions. Training is staged: world model first, then UNet, then ControlNet with all other modules frozen.

## Results

On Occ3D-nuScenes with ground-truth 3D occupancy and trajectories, COME achieves 34.23 mIoU and 44.13 IoU (3-second average), outperforming DOME (27.10 mIoU, 36.36 IoU) by 26.3% in mIoU and 21.3% in IoU. With additional BEV layout inputs, COME further improves to 39.28 mIoU and 47.16 IoU, surpassing UniScene-Fore (31.76 mIoU, 34.84 IoU) by 23.7% in mIoU and 35.4% in IoU. Under the camera-input setting with GT trajectories, COME reaches 22.26 mIoU and 44.07 IoU, a 22.0% mIoU gain over DOME. On the 8-second long-horizon task, COME achieves 19.07 mIoU and 29.96 IoU, beating DOME by 20.5% and 18.8% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

COME decisively outperforms DOME [4], the primary SOTA baseline, across all settings: +26.3% mIoU (34.23 vs 27.10) with GT 3D-Occ inputs, +22.0% mIoU (22.26 vs 18.25) with camera inputs, and +20.5% mIoU on the 8-second long-horizon task. Against UniScene-Fore [11], COME gains +23.7% mIoU (39.28 vs 31.76) and +35.4% IoU (47.16 vs 34.84) under the richest input setting (3D-Occ + boxes + map). In the predicted-trajectory 3D-Occ setting, COME (21.29 mIoU) is slightly below DFIT-OccWorld (22.71 mIoU) and Occ-LLM (20.99 mIoU), which leverage additional occupancy-flow or language cues; the authors attribute this gap to COME's sensitivity to planning errors. For camera-input predicted-trajectory generation, COME (19.11 mIoU) surpasses the previous best OccVAR (11.79 mIoU) by 62.1%. On Occ3D-Waymo, COME achieves 41.51 mIoU vs DOME's 25.36 mIoU (+63.7%), confirming cross-dataset generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

COME builds directly on DOME's diffusion-transformer occupancy world model as its generative backbone, and adapts the ControlNet paradigm (originally for 2D image conditioning) to inject scene-centric forecasting features into the 3D occupancy generation pipeline. The scene-centric forecasting module draws on the 4D occupancy forecasting literature (Occ4Cast, Cam4DOcc) but explicitly factors out ego-motion via coordinate alignment, a step those methods do not perform. The key conceptual departure from prior occupancy world models (OccWorld, OccLLaMA, OccVAR, UniScene) is the explicit disentanglement of ego-motion from scene dynamics: rather than letting a single network implicitly learn both perspective shifts and agent interactions, COME decomposes the problem into a fixed-view prediction sub-task and a variable-view generation sub-task, bridged by a visibility-aware ControlNet.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a vanilla U-Net in scene-centric coordinates (39.12 mIoU) outperforms a full diffusion-transformer world model in ego-centric coordinates (27.10 mIoU) by 44% reframes the core difficulty of occupancy world modeling: the dominant source of error is ego-motion-induced perspective change, not scene dynamics. This suggests that future world models should explicitly handle coordinate invariance rather than relying on implicit learning. Open questions include whether the multi-stage training pipeline can be made end-to-end, how to reduce the added computational overhead for real-time deployment, and how to better integrate planning modules that are themselves scene-centric. The sensitivity to trajectory quality (0.23 mIoU drop after alignment correction) also highlights that evaluation protocols for world models need to decouple planning accuracy from generation quality.
