## Problem

Large reasoning models (LRMs) such as OpenAI o1 and DeepSeek-R1 achieve strong performance by generating long chains of thought, but this comes at the cost of substantial redundant reasoning that inflates token consumption and latency. Existing overthinking mitigations rely on external interventions—RL-based length penalties, inference-time early exit, or hard token budgets—rather than enabling the model to internally recognize when further reasoning becomes unproductive. This paper asks whether LRMs can be trained to autonomously detect and terminate their own redundant reasoning without any external control mechanism.

## Input / Output

The model takes a mathematical problem as input and produces a chain-of-thought reasoning trajectory (enclosed in <think> and  tags) followed by a final answer. The key structural detail is that the reasoning segment is decomposed into a Foundation Solution (the first complete solution attempt) and subsequent Evolution Solutions (self-correction, verification, or alternative approaches introduced by cues like 'Wait' or 'Alternatively'). The SBT-trained model is expected to naturally emit a self-reflective braking sentence (e.g., 'I've verified my answer, no need to continue thinking') at the point where reasoning becomes redundant, then transition to the final answer—without any external stop signal or token budget.

## Method

Self-Braking Tuning (SBT) is a supervised fine-tuning framework built on three mechanisms. First, an overthinking identification pipeline computes a composite overthink score combining a reasoning efficiency ratio (steps-to-first-correct-answer / total steps, weighted 90%) and a linguistic marker ratio (density of reconsideration terms like 'wait', 'however', 'let me verify', weighted 10%) to locate the transition from productive to redundant reasoning. Second, two complementary data construction strategies reshape training trajectories: SBT-E applies uniform solution-level truncation (preserving the Foundation Solution plus one Evolution Solution, then masking the start of the next), while SBT-D performs step-level adaptive truncation that incrementally adds steps until the overthink score exceeds a threshold (τ₁=0.2), then masks steps up to a secondary threshold (τ₂=0.25). Third, a braking prompt mechanism inserts natural-language self-reflective cues at the preserved/masked boundary, and the masked redundant segments are excluded from the loss function so the model observes overthinking patterns without being reinforced to reproduce them. The resulting 92K-example datasets (OpenR1-Math-SBT-E and SBT-D) are used for SFT, and the trained models exhibit autonomous early termination (~50% of AIME cases) and problem-adaptive reasoning depth (27–202 steps across difficulty levels) without external difficulty indicators.

## Results

Across four model architectures (Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B/7B, Llama-3.2-1B, Llama-3.1-8B) evaluated on GSM8K, MATH500, AIME, and AMC23, SBT-E reduces average token consumption by 30.7%–62.8% while incurring only 1.5%–4.9% accuracy drops relative to the unmodified OpenR1-Math baseline. SBT-D achieves 23.0%–54.2% token reduction with even smaller accuracy losses (≤1.95%), and in some cases improves accuracy (e.g., Llama-3.1-8B on MATH500: +2.62%). The most striking result is Llama-3.1-8B with SBT-E, which cuts tokens from 8,576 to 3,193 (62.8% reduction) while retaining 94.1% of baseline average accuracy (45.73% vs. 48.59%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is against the unmodified OpenR1-Math SFT baseline (no overthinking mitigation). SBT-E consistently wins on token efficiency across all four models, achieving 30.7%–62.8% average token reduction, but at a small accuracy cost of 1.5–4.9 percentage points. SBT-D trades some token savings (23.0%–54.2% reduction) for better accuracy preservation, and notably outperforms the baseline on accuracy for Llama-3.2-1B (20.11% vs. 19.43%) and Llama-3.1-8B (49.17% vs. 48.59%), suggesting dynamic truncation can eliminate not just redundancy but potentially harmful overthinking. Between the two SBT variants, SBT-E achieves greater average token reduction (48.3% vs. 43.9%) but with slightly larger accuracy drops, while SBT-D is more balanced on harder benchmarks (AIME, MATH500). The paper does not directly compare against other overthinking-mitigation methods (e.g., Token-Budget, DEER, NoThinking), limiting cross-method comparison to qualitative discussion in the related work section.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of external intervention for overthinking mitigation (RL-based length rewards, inference-time compression, token budgets) by proposing an endogenous, SFT-based approach that teaches the model to self-regulate reasoning length. Conceptually, it builds on the structural decomposition of R1-like trajectories into Foundation and Evolution Solutions, and repurposes the OpenR1-Math dataset with a novel loss-masking and braking-prompt data construction strategy. Unlike prior SFT-based length-control methods that use variable-length CoT data or auxiliary constraints, SBT explicitly exposes the model to overthinking patterns (via masked segments) without reinforcing them, framing termination as a learned metacognitive behavior rather than an imposed constraint.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 30–60% token reduction with minimal accuracy loss is practically significant for deployment cost and latency, and the out-of-domain transfer (math-trained models showing 26–65% efficiency gains on MMLU/GPQA) suggests self-regulation is a transferable meta-cognitive skill. However, the approach is limited to mathematical reasoning in its validation, relies on fixed thresholds that may not generalize across tasks, and the internal stopping mechanism remains opaque. Open questions include whether adaptive or difficulty-aware braking strategies can handle tasks where full-chain reasoning is essential (e.g., theorem proving), and whether the 92K-example scale is sufficient or whether larger datasets shift the optimal threshold regime.
