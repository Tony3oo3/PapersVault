## Problem

Existing ΔΔG prediction methods either rely on structure-based pre-trained representations that cannot explicitly model higher-order many-body interactions (e.g., hydrogen bond networks, π-π stacking), or use inverse-folding likelihood as an indirect proxy that degrades sharply for multi-point mutations. The core gap is the lack of a mechanism to capture higher-order and many-body energetic effects across multiple spatial scales, particularly in the complex interdependencies that arise when multiple residues are mutated simultaneously.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a protein complex 3D structure (key atoms N, Cα, C, O, Cβ per residue) together with the wild-type and mutant amino acid sequences. Edges are formed via top-k Cα–Cα nearest neighbors with radial basis function features over 25 atom-pair distances, and fine-grained mutation subgraphs are extracted within an 8 Å radius of each mutation centroid. The output is a scalar ΔΔG computed as the difference in negative log-likelihoods between mutant and wild-type, with single-chain monomer contributions subtracted to isolate the binding-specific energetic change.

## Method

H3-DDG fine-tunes a pretrained ProteinMPNN inverse-folding backbone and augments it with a three-level hierarchical graph: (1) a full residue-level graph updated by standard 2-body message passing, (2) a mutation-centered hypergraph built via Farthest Point Sampling initialized at mutation sites, where 3-body gated-softmax attention operates on outer-product interaction tensors between hyperedges, and (3) fine-grained mutation subgraphs (8 Å radius) where a 4-body attention mechanism encodes quadruplet residue interactions using cross-attention-style projections of node-node and node-edge tensors. The mutation-aware FPS pooling reduces the naive O(N³)–O(N⁴) cost of higher-order attention to a manageable constant-factor overhead while preserving biologically salient regions. Training is supervised with MSE loss on ΔΔG, and the prediction module subtracts monomer contributions from complex-level negative log-likelihoods to isolate binding effects, following the RDE-Network/BA-DDG formulation.

## Results

On SKEMPI v2 (all mutations), H3-DDG achieves a Pearson correlation of 0.7501, Spearman of 0.6604, RMSE of 1.3665, and AUROC of 0.7920, outperforming the strongest baseline BA-DDG (Pearson 0.7118) by 5.38%. For multi-point mutations specifically, H3-DDG reaches a per-structure Pearson of 0.5520, surpassing BA-DDG (0.4924) by 12.10%. On the larger BindingGYM dataset, H3-DDG achieves a Pearson of 0.3057 (all mutations) and 0.2472 (≥3 mutations), exceeding BA-DDG by 34.26% and 21.35% respectively. In the SARS-CoV-2 antibody case study, H3-DDG ranks five known affinity-enhancing mutations at an average top-9.67%, the only method to achieve a sub-10% average rank.

## Baselines & Comparisons

H3-DDG outperforms all 11 baselines across every metric on SKEMPI v2. Against the closest competitor BA-DDG, H3-DDG gains +5.38% Pearson (0.7501 vs 0.7118) on all mutations and a more substantial +10.39% Pearson / +12.10% per-structure Pearson on multi-point mutations, where BA-DDG's likelihood-based proxy degrades most. On BindingGYM, the advantage widens: H3-DDG beats BA-DDG by 34.26% Pearson (0.3057 vs 0.2277) overall and by 21.35% Pearson / 21.96% Spearman in the ≥3-mutation regime, while also surpassing Prompt-DDG by 62.61% Pearson. Energy-based methods (Rosetta, FoldX) and earlier supervised models (RDE-Network, DiffAffinity, Prompt-DDG, ProMIM) trail H3-DDG by 7–44% in Pearson on SKEMPI v2. In the SARS-CoV-2 antibody ranking task, H3-DDG is the only method to place all five known favorable mutations within the top 10% (average 9.67%), whereas the next-best Prompt-DDG averages 10.69% and BA-DDG 14.21%. The one area where H3-DDG is slightly behind is RMSE on BindingGYM single-point mutations (1.0758 vs BA-DDG's 0.9716), which the authors attribute to cross-experiment ΔΔG scale variation rather than ranking quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

H3-DDG extends the inverse-folding-based ΔΔG prediction lineage initiated by ProteinMPNN-DDG and BA-DDG, which use likelihood differences between native and mutant sequences as a proxy for binding free energy changes. The key departure is the introduction of explicit many-body attention (2-body, 3-body, 4-body) across a hierarchical graph structure—residue-level graph → mutation-centered hypergraph → fine-grained mutation subgraph—rather than relying solely on pairwise message passing or indirect Boltzmann-Alignment scoring. The mutation-centered hypergraph pooling (Farthest Point Sampling anchored at mutation sites) and the 4-body attention within 8 Å mutation subgraphs are the primary architectural novelties, building on the RBF-based edge encoding and autoregressive sequence modeling inherited from ProteinMPNN.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Multi-point mutation prediction remains a critical bottleneck in protein engineering and antibody design, where synergistic effects between mutation sites are poorly captured by pairwise or likelihood-based methods. H3-DDG's 12.10% per-structure Pearson improvement on multi-point mutations (SKEMPI v2) and 34.26% overall improvement on BindingGYM demonstrate that explicit higher-order interaction modeling yields tangible gains in this under-served regime. However, the paper acknowledges that performance on very large, diverse mutation landscapes needs further validation, and integration with experimental workflows (e.g., directed evolution pipelines) remains an open question for real-world deployment.
