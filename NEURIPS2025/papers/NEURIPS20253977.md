## Problem

Existing benchmarks for text-to-image models and vision-language models evaluate them in isolation, use coarse-grained skill categories, or rely on aggregate metrics like VQAScore and CLIPScore that cannot pinpoint specific failure modes. There is no unified framework that jointly diagnoses where T2I models fail to follow prompts and where VLMs fail to detect those failures, making it difficult to attribute errors to the correct component of the generation-understanding pipeline.

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes a text prompt (hand-written to target one of 27 failure modes) and a 1360×768 RGB image generated by a T2I model. The VLM receives a tailored instruction prompt specific to the failure mode (e.g., 'Count how many apples are there?') rather than the original T2I prompt, which the authors show introduces confirmation bias. The output is a triple: a boolean indicating whether the failure mode is present, a continuous raw score usable for ranking images, and a free-text explanation from the LLM judge.

## Method

FineGRAIN is an agentic evaluation pipeline that pairs a T2I model with a VLM judge and an LLM orchestrator (Llama3-70B). The authors define 27 fine-grained failure modes across 11 high-level categories and hand-write 25–30 prompts per mode; for each prompt-image pair, the LLM generates a failure-mode-specific VLM instruction from a template (e.g., for 'Counts or Multiple Objects': 'Count how many [object] are there?'), the VLM (Molmo-72B, InternVL3-78B, or Pixtral-124B) answers that question about the image, and the LLM compares the VLM's answer against the original prompt to emit the boolean, raw score, and explanation. A key design choice is withholding the original T2I prompt from the VLM to avoid confirmation bias, and the LLM's tailored questioning replaces the single generic VQAScore query. The dataset comprises over 3,750 images from five T2I models (Flux, SD3-Medium, SD3-Large, SD3.5-Medium, SD3.5-Large), each human-annotated with a ground-truth failure label, enabling direct comparison of FineGRAIN's boolean prediction against human judgment.

## Results

Across 27 fine-grained failure modes, Flux achieves the highest average T2I success rate at 51.04%, followed by SD3.5-Large (40.06%), SD3.5-Medium (30.56%), SD3-Medium (24.27%), and SDXL (21.09%). All five models score 0% on 'Counts or Multiple Objects' and 'Long Text Specific', revealing complete failure on precise counting and long-text rendering. For VLM evaluation, FineGRAIN achieves 67.4% average human agreement, a 10-point improvement over VQAScore's 57.7%, with VQAScore performing worst on text tasks (<30% agreement) and best on color binding (84%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FineGRAIN outperforms VQAScore (the prior SOTA metric from Lin et al., ECCV 2025) by 10 percentage points in average human agreement (67.4% vs. 57.7%), with the gap widening substantially on text-related failure modes where VQAScore agrees with human labels less than 30% of the time. On the specific example in Table 8 (prompt: 'A person hitting a hard drum that has sand on the drum'), only FineGRAIN correctly identifies the compliant image (SD3.5 output, score 0), while VQAScore assigns high scores (0.893–0.967) to all five model outputs and CLIPScore (0.266–0.328) fails to discriminate. VQAScore's best category is color attribute binding (84% agreement), suggesting it captures coarse semantic alignment but misses fine-grained attribute errors. CLIPScore, compared in Table 8, shows similarly poor discriminative power, with scores clustered in a narrow 0.266–0.328 range that does not reflect the actual quality differences between generated images. The paper also notes that VQAScore's reliance on feeding the original prompt to the VLM introduces confirmation bias, degrading performance on failure modes like anatomical accuracy where the VLM rationalizes distortions as 'optical illusions.'

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from prior T2I evaluation benchmarks (GenAIBench, TIFA, Gecko, T2I-CompBench++) in two key ways: it evaluates both the T2I generator and the VLM judge within a single unified pipeline, and it decomposes prompt adherence into 27 fine-grained failure modes rather than coarse categories. Prior work such as Li et al. (GenAIBench) relies on VQAScore as a scalar ranking metric, while FineGRAIN introduces an agentic pipeline where an LLM generates tailored VLM questions per failure mode and then judges compliance, producing a boolean score and interpretable explanation. The paper explicitly positions against the 'lax' counting evaluation in prior work, arguing that lumping counting with shape or color errors obscures systematic failures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that all five T2I models achieve 0% success on multi-object counting directly contradicts prior reports (e.g., Li et al.) that SDXL performs well on counting, suggesting that coarse evaluation masks critical capability gaps. The boolean compliance score enables a test-time scaling deployment pattern (generate-and-verify loops) that prior scalar metrics like VQAScore or CLIPScore do not support. Open questions include whether the 67.4% FineGRAIN-human agreement is sufficient for high-stakes deployment, how subjective categories (Surreal, Emotional Conveyance) should be handled, and whether closed-source VLMs would substantially improve judge reliability. The benchmark's reliance on LLM/VLM judges introduces a circularity concern that the authors acknowledge but do not fully resolve.
