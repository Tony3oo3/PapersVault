## Problem

MLLMs pretrained on 2D image-text data lack explicit 3D geometric knowledge, yet recent 3D scene understanding methods rely on them by injecting 3D positional embeddings into multi-view image features. This paper identifies and quantifies the resulting gap: it shows that MLLM 3D-aware representation quality (measured via multi-view correspondence scores) strongly correlates with downstream scene understanding performance, and that no prior work has systematically investigated or addressed this limitation within the MLLM paradigm.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252282/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 32 uniformly sampled RGBD frames per scan, with per-pixel 3D coordinates computed from depth maps and camera intrinsics/extrinsics (voxel size 0.1 for correspondence evaluation). These coordinates are encoded via a positional encoding function and added to image encoder features, following the Video-3D LLM pipeline. The model produces autoregressive text outputs: bounding box coordinates for visual grounding, natural language descriptions for dense captioning, and short answers for question answering, with Mask3D generating object proposals at inference time.

## Method

3DRS introduces 3D foundation model–guided feature distillation as an additional supervision signal on MLLM visual tokens. Specifically, per-pixel features from a pretrained 3D geometry model (VGGT or FLARE) are extracted offline, 2D-averaged-pooled to match the MLLM feature map resolution, and then aligned to the MLLM's last-layer per-pixel visual embeddings via a two-layer MLP projector and an euclidean-distance distillation loss. The total training objective is the standard cross-entropy text loss plus this alignment loss, requiring no extra forward passes during MLLM fine-tuning since teacher features are precomputed. The paper also ablates a simpler correspondence-based contrastive loss (maximizing similarity for same-voxel cross-view pairs, minimizing it for different-voxel pairs), which yields smaller gains, confirming that the richer multi-task 3D priors in foundation models (depth, camera estimation, dense reconstruction) provide substantially more effective supervision than single-task view equivariance alone.

## Results

3DRS achieves state-of-the-art performance across all five 3D scene understanding benchmarks: ScanRefer Acc@0.25 of 62.9 (vs. 58.1 for Video-3D LLM), Multi3DRefer F1@0.25 of 60.4, Scan2Cap CIDEr@0.5 of 86.1, ScanQA CIDEr of 104.8, and SQA3D EM of 60.6. On VSI-Bench, 3DRS attains an average score of 45.9, surpassing all open-source models (best prior: LLaVA-Video-72B at 40.9) and approaching Gemini-1.5 Pro (45.4). The gains are consistent across three different MLLM backbones (LLaVA-Next-Video, LLaVA-OneVision, Qwen2-VL), with improvements of 3–5 points on grounding metrics and 1–2 points on QA metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

3DRS outperforms the strongest prior MLLM-based method, Video-3D LLM [63], by 4.8 points on ScanRefer Acc@0.25 (62.9 vs. 58.1), 2.4 on Multi3DRefer F1@0.25 (60.4 vs. 58.0), 2.3 on Scan2Cap CIDEr (86.1 vs. 83.8), and 2.0 on SQA3D EM (60.6 vs. 58.6). Against point-cloud-based generalists like ChatScene [24] (55.5 Acc@0.25) and Grounded 3D-LLM [9] (47.9), 3DRS leads by 7.4 and 15.0 points respectively on ScanRefer, demonstrating the advantage of multi-view MLLM backbones over point-cloud encoders. On VSI-Bench, 3DRS (45.9 avg) surpasses all open-source models including LLaVA-Video-72B (40.9) and InternVL2-40B (37.0), and slightly edges out Gemini-1.5 Pro (45.4) while trailing GPT-4o on absolute distance (34.8 vs. 5.3). Ablations confirm that 3D foundation model supervision (VGGT, FLARE) consistently outperforms 2D teachers (DINOv2, MAE, SigLIP2) by 2–3 points, and that the correspondence loss alone provides smaller gains than full 3D distillation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Video-3D LLM [63], which established the pipeline of injecting per-pixel 3D positional encodings into MLLMs for multi-view scene understanding. The key departure is that prior MLLM-based 3D methods (LLaVA-3D, GPT4Scene, Video-3D LLM) rely solely on text-token cross-entropy supervision during finetuning, leaving the visual encoder's 3D representation quality unaddressed. 3DRS introduces explicit 3D-specific supervision on visual tokens by distilling features from pretrained 3D geometry foundation models (VGGT, FLARE), which is distinct from prior 3D-awareness studies (Probe3D, FiT3D, Lexicon3D) that probed pure vision models using 2D foundation models rather than 3D ones.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The systematic demonstration that multi-view correspondence quality strongly correlates with downstream 3D task performance provides a practical diagnostic for evaluating and improving MLLM 3D representations. The 3DRS framework is a plug-and-play, zero-overhead improvement (teacher features are precomputed offline) that consistently boosts multiple MLLMs across grounding, captioning, and QA benchmarks, making it broadly applicable. Key limitations include the distillation ceiling (performance is upper-bounded by the teacher 3D foundation model), the relatively small finetuning dataset compared to MLLM pretraining scale, and the risk of propagating teacher errors. A natural next step, noted by the authors, is incorporating 3D-awareness supervision into MLLM pretraining rather than post-hoc finetuning.
