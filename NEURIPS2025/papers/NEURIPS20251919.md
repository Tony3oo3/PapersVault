## Problem

Evaluating agent policies for Robust POMDPs (RPOMDPs) lacks a complete pipeline: existing benchmarks are often POMDPs with artificial uncertainty that can be solved by naive nature heuristics, policy evaluation against worst-case nature policies is computationally prohibitive, and no efficient baselines exist for comparing solver performance. This paper addresses all three gaps simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251919/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The evaluation method takes a fixed agent policy π = ⟨σ, τ⟩ with memory space X and an RPOMDP with ⟨s,a⟩-rectangular convex uncertainty set U, and constructs a nature MDP with state space S×X×A and action space restricted to extreme points of the joint transition-observation polytope. The output is the best-response nature policy θ*_π (Markovian in X) and the worst-case value V^{π,θ*_π}, computed via off-the-shelf continuous MDP solvers (e.g., MCTS). The RQMDP and RFIB bounds additionally require only precomputed Q-values at unit beliefs {b_s | s∈S}, making them O(|S|·|A|) to evaluate for any belief.

## Method

The paper introduces three complementary contributions. First, it defines solvability classes (trivial, naive via θ_Center/θ_Ent/θ_RMDP, and stationary) to formally characterize when a benchmark can be solved without considering the full set of nature policies, and constructs three benchmarks (TOY*, ECHO, PARITY) proven not to fall in any naive class. Second, it shows that for ⟨s,a⟩-rectangular dynamic-uncertainty RPOMDPs, the best-response nature policy θ*_π is Markovian in the agent's memory state X and selects only extreme points of the uncertainty set, enabling evaluation by solving a (possibly continuous-state) nature MDP whose transitions encode both the RPOMDP dynamics and the agent's memory update. Third, it lifts the QMDP and FIB POMDP upper bounds to robust variants (RQMDP, RFIB) by replacing the expectation over transitions with an infimum over the uncertainty set, proving both are contraction mappings with the tightness ordering Q_RMDP ≥ Q_RFIB ≥ Q_RPOMDP.

## Results

The paper evaluates its pipeline using the relative value gap V_gap(π) = (V^{π,θ*_π} − Ṽ)/|Ṽ|, where Ṽ is the RHSVI-computed RPOMDP value. On the novel benchmarks (TOY*, ECHO, PARITY(∞)), all policies from naive nature heuristics (Center, Ent, RMDP) exhibit significant positive value gaps, confirming these benchmarks cannot be solved without considering the full set of nature policies. RFIB achieves an optimal policy (zero value gap) for both PARITY(10) and PARITY(∞), but does not solve TOY* or ECHO. On standard lifted POMDP benchmarks (TIGER, MINIHALLWAY, ALOHA, HEAVENORHELL, HEALTHDETECTION, REPLACEMENT), at least one naive nature heuristic matches RHSVI performance, and the evaluation pipeline yields value gaps close to zero for smaller environments.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is between RHSVI (the full RPOMDP solver) and three naive nature heuristics (θ_Center, θ_Ent, θ_RMDP) plus two robust approximations (RQMDP, RFIB), all evaluated via the proposed nature MDP. On the novel benchmarks TOY*, ECHO, and PARITY(∞), all three naive heuristics produce policies with significant value gaps, meaning they fail to find the true worst-case value; RFIB is the only method that achieves optimality on PARITY(10) and PARITY(∞), while RQMDP performs worse than RFIB in general. On the standard lifted POMDP benchmarks (TIGER, MINIHALLWAY, ALOHA, HEAVENORHELL, HEALTHDETECTION, REPLACEMENT), at least one naive heuristic matches RHSVI, confirming these benchmarks are naively solvable and thus unsuitable for testing robustness. The evaluation pipeline itself is validated as accurate for smaller environments (value gaps ≈ 0 for RHSVI policies), though MCTS approximation errors cause positive gaps on larger environments like ALOHA and HEALTHDETECTION.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the RPOMDP solving literature by addressing three components of the evaluation pipeline that prior work has left implicit: benchmark design, policy evaluation, and baseline computation. It generalizes the robust Markov chain construction of Galesloot et al. [15] (which handled only stationary nature policies and finite state controllers) to arbitrary memorization schemes via a nature MDP formulation, and lifts the classical POMDP approximations QMDP [33] and FIB [21] to their robust counterparts. The solvability framework departs from prior benchmarking practice, which typically extended existing POMDPs with artificial ε-uncertainty without analyzing whether such benchmarks actually test robustness against the full set of nature policies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that widely-used RPOMDP benchmarks (TIGER, HEAVENORHELL) are naively solvable challenges the field's implicit assumption that these environments adequately test robust solvers, and the solvability classes provide a principled criterion for future benchmark design. The nature MDP formulation enables evaluation with off-the-shelf continuous MDP solvers, but approximation errors on larger environments (ALOHA, HEALTHDETECTION) limit practical reliability. Open questions include extending the analysis beyond rectangular and convex uncertainty sets, developing tighter approximations for the nature MDP, and using the RQMDP/RFIB bounds to actively guide RPOMDP solvers rather than serving only as post-hoc baselines.
