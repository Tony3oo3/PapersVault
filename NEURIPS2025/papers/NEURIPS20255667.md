## Problem

Existing auditory scene analysis models are task-specific (separation, SED, DoAE) and lack the ability to perform relational reasoning across cues, causing failures when critical auditory information is missing or degraded. No prior architecture simultaneously performs MIMO source separation, dereverberation, sound event detection, and DoA estimation while exploiting the complementary relationships among these estimated parameters to reinforce one another.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255667/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes an M-channel audio mixture at 16 kHz (4-second clips) and produces, for each of up to J=5 foreground objects, multichannel direct and reverberant waveforms, a class label, a binary activation curve, a per-frame SED map, and a 3D DoA vector (x,y,z) per time frame, plus one multichannel background noise waveform. A key structural property is that all outputs sharing the same object index j are guaranteed to correspond to the same physical source, eliminating the permutation ambiguity inherent in track-wise processing. The DoA output encodes activity implicitly: a unit-magnitude vector indicates a present source, while all-zero values indicate silence.

## Method

DeepASA comprises three stages: (1) a Dynamic STFT encoder that predicts per-frame Gaussian window parameters (μ_t, σ_t) to adaptively shift temporal focus and spectral resolution, followed by a modified DeFT-Mamba aggregator (Mamba-FFN for temporal, FFN for spectral, no unfold) that captures multi-dimensional relations; (2) an object separator (2D convolution) that splits aggregated features into J+1 object-centric representations, each fed to parallel sub-decoders—a MIMO audio decoder (direct/reverb/noise branches with SA-SDR loss), a SED decoder (pre-trained ATST + T-CRNN + F-CRNN), and a CRNN-based DoA decoder; (3) a Chain-of-Inference module that performs Temporal Coherence Matching via bidirectional cross-attention between SED and DoA streams, then injects the fused clue into object features through FiLM modulation before re-running the sub-decoders for iterative refinement. Training is multi-stage (Net 1 then Net 2 with frozen early blocks), and the object-centric design removes the need for permutation-invariant training across decoders since all sub-decoders share the same object ordering.

## Results

On the ASA2 dataset, DeepASA with chain-of-inference achieves an SI-SDRi of 11.2 dB for universal sound separation and a SELD score of 0.206 (ER 25.0%, F1 74.1%, LE 17.0°, LR 78.1%). Fine-tuned on MC-FUSS, DeepASA reaches 18.5 dB SI-SDRi for multichannel USS, surpassing the previous best (DeFT-Mamba at 16.4 dB). On the real-world STARSS23 benchmark, DeepASA with CoI attains a SELD score of 0.253 (ER 33.7%, F1 63.1%, LE 9.8°, LR 74.6%), outperforming the DCASE 2023 1st-place system (0.260 with ensemble).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On USS (MC-FUSS), fine-tuned DeepASA (18.5 dB SI-SDRi) outperforms DeFT-Mamba (16.4 dB), SpatialNet (15.8 dB), TF-GridNet (15.3 dB), MC-BSRNN (14.1 dB), and ByteDance-uss (14.0 dB), with the largest gap (2.1 dB) over DeFT-Mamba in 4-source scenes (17.6 vs. 13.8 dB). On SELD (STARSS23), DeepASA+CoI (SELD 0.253) surpasses the DCASE 2023 1st-place NERC-SLIP system both with (0.260) and without (0.277) ensemble, as well as CST-former2 (0.301), MFF-EINV2 (0.398), and CST-former (0.416), achieving the lowest localization error (9.8° vs. 12.8° for NERC-SLIP). On the ASA2 dataset, DeepASA+CoI achieves a SELD score of 0.206, substantially outperforming MFF-EINV2 (0.381), SELD-Mamba (0.396), ResNet Conformer (0.414), and EINV2 (0.431), while also leading all USS baselines by at least 0.6 dB SI-SDRi over DeFT-Mamba. Notably, DeepASA achieves these gains with fewer parameters (12.1M + 96.8M pre-trained ATST) than SELD-Mamba (75.1M) and MFF-EINV2 (54.8M), and with lower computational cost (104.0 GMac/s) than TF-GridNet (462 GMac/s).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on DeFT-Mamba (Lee & Choi, ICASSP 2025) as its feature aggregation backbone, modifying it by removing the unfold operation and replacing F-Mamba with a conventional FFN for efficiency. The key conceptual departure is from track-wise SELD processing (CST-former, EINV2, NERC-SLIP), where SED and DoA are estimated independently and then aligned post-hoc, toward an object-oriented processing (OOP) paradigm that separates per-object features first and decodes all auditory parameters from the same representation, eliminating the permutation association problem. The Chain-of-Inference (CoI) mechanism, which uses cross-attention between SED and DoA outputs to iteratively refine object features via FiLM conditioning, has no direct precedent in prior ASA or source separation literature. The Dynamic STFT with time-varying learnable Gaussian windows extends the line of work on adaptive time-frequency analysis (TDY-CNN) by allowing both window position and spectral resolution to vary per frame.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DeepASA provides the first unified framework that simultaneously performs MIMO source separation, dereverberation, SED, and DoAE from a single multichannel input, addressing a gap where no prior model could handle all these tasks jointly. The OOP principle—separating objects before decoding parameters—is a clean architectural insight that could be adopted beyond this specific model, and the CoI mechanism offers a general recipe for cross-task refinement in multi-output audio models. Key limitations include the 96.8M-parameter ATST dependency, performance degradation beyond 5 sources or RT > 0.6 s, and the SNR floor of 6 dB, all of which constrain real-world deployment. Open questions include whether the object-centric formulation can scale to open-vocabulary scenes, whether CoI can be made more computationally efficient (currently adding ~5 GMac/s), and whether the approach generalizes to non-spatial (single-channel) settings.
