## Problem

Existing defenses for text-conditioned diffusion models—prompt filtering, concept removal, and sexual content mitigation—fail to simultaneously suppress explicit content generation under adversarial attacks and preserve benign image quality. Concept removal methods that modify the U-Net degrade generation quality or remain vulnerable to adaptive attacks, while text encoder-based approaches like AdvUnlearn suffer from the well-known accuracy-robustness tradeoff of adversarial training. The paper asks whether directly distorting the text embedding space, rather than erasing concepts or adding adversarial training, can achieve both robust defense and high-quality safe generation.

## Input / Output

For T2I, the method takes a text prompt and produces a generated image; for I2I (SD-inpainting), it additionally accepts an input image and a binary mask. The training input consists of 6,911 safe–unsafe prompt pairs from the CoPro sexual category (plus 8,931 pairs from violence/illegal categories for broader NSFW evaluation), processed through the text encoder only. The output is a fine-tuned text encoder (CLIP-L/14, CLIP-G/14, or T5-XXL depending on the base model) that drops into the existing diffusion pipeline with zero additional parameters and zero inference overhead.

## Method

DES fine-tunes only the text encoder using three complementary losses. First, an unsafe loss aligns each unsafe embedding with a pre-computed target vector constructed by selecting the safe vector of minimum cosine similarity to that unsafe vector and then subtracting a scaled 'nudity' direction, pushing unsafe embeddings into anti-correlated safe regions. Second, a safe loss preserves benign embeddings by constraining current safe vectors to their originals, with an adaptive adjustment that adds the normalized nudity direction to each safe vector before computing similarity—this automatically weights the loss higher for safe vectors less correlated with nudity and lower for those more correlated, allowing ambiguous embeddings to shift while firmly anchoring clearly safe ones. Third, a nudity neutralization loss aligns the 'nudity' embedding with the empty-string (neutral) embedding, eliminating the semantic content that extraction-based attacks like Ring-A-Bell exploit. The total loss is a weighted combination (λ=0.3) of safe and unsafe+neutralization terms, trained for 2 epochs at lr 1e-5 with AdamW, requiring only ~90 seconds on a single A100.

## Results

On SDv1.5, DES achieves an average attack success rate (ASR) of 0.52% across four black-box adversarial attacks (Sneaky, MMA, Ring-A-Bell, P4D), with the lowest standard deviation (0.41) among all methods, compared to 78.04% for the unmodified model. On FLUX.1, DES reduces average ASR to 8.86% versus 56.38% for the original model and 43.23% for EraseAnything (the only other method evaluated on FLUX.1). DES also preserves benign image quality, achieving FID of 15.44 and CLIP score of 25.52 on SDv1.5 (vs. 16.57/26.46 for the original), and FID of 27.05 / CLIP 25.61 on FLUX.1 (vs. 26.58/25.64 for the original). Training requires only 90 seconds with zero inference overhead.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DES outperforms all 19 baselines on the primary black-box ASR metric. On SDv1.5, DES (0.52% avg ASR) beats the previous SOTA AdvUnlearn (1.44%) by 63.9%, SalUn (3.02%) by 82.8%, GLoCE (2.93%) by 82.2%, and GuardT2I (5.86%) by 91.1%. On FLUX.1, DES (8.86%) dramatically outperforms EraseAnything (43.23%), the only other method evaluated on that model, by a 79.5% relative reduction. On white-box adaptive attacks (Table 3), DES achieves 6.47% average ASR, beating AdvUnlearn (7.15%) and SalUn (8.96%), with 0% ASR on Ring-A-Bell. On image quality, DES (FID 15.44, CLIP 25.52) substantially outperforms AdvUnlearn (FID 18.94, CLIP 23.82) and SalUn (FID 21.14, CLIP 24.78), while remaining close to the unmodified SDv1.5 (FID 16.57, CLIP 26.46). On I2I tasks, DES (20.08% avg ASR) beats AdvUnlearn (22.13%) and Safe-CLIP (36.89%), and on broader NSFW concepts (violence, illegal, hate, etc.), DES (1.88% avg) outperforms AdvUnlearn (5.13%) and GLoCE (12.67%). DES is the only method that simultaneously achieves the lowest ASR, best FID, and zero inference overhead across all evaluated settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

DES positions itself within the text encoder modification paradigm for concept removal, departing from earlier U-Net-based approaches (ESD, UCE) that struggle with parameter entanglement. It draws a conceptual analogy from continual learning—where maintaining feature positions prevents catastrophic forgetting—to argue that *displacing* features from their original positions can effectively erase unsafe semantics. Unlike AdvUnlearn, which relies on adversarial training (known to degrade image quality), and Safe-CLIP, which performs direct concept erasure, DES introduces a three-loss framework that simultaneously redirects unsafe embeddings toward safe regions, preserves safe embeddings via a correlation-modulated loss, and neutralizes the 'nudity' vector itself, making it resistant to extraction-based attacks like Ring-A-Bell.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical efficiency (90-second training, zero inference overhead, no additional parameters) makes DES one of the most deployable defenses for production T2I systems, addressing a key gap where prior methods either add inference latency (SAFREE, SLD) or require heavy training (GuardT2I). The surprising generalization to non-sexual NSFW concepts (violence, illegal, hate) and even the Van Gogh concept suggests the embedding distortion mechanism captures a broader principle about concept localization in text encoders. Open questions include whether the approach scales to more complex multi-concept interactions, whether the fixed 'nudity' vector selection generalizes to new or evolving unsafe categories, and whether the 9.47% ASR on FLUX.1 (still non-trivial) can be further reduced without sacrificing benign quality.
