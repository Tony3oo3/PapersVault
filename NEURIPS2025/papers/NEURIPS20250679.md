## Problem

Existing mathematical benchmarks for LLMs suffer from three critical flaws: widely available competition problems (e.g., AIME 2024) are contaminated by training data, private alternatives like FrontierMath sacrifice transparency and reproducibility, and nearly all benchmarks evaluate only final numerical answers rather than the proof-writing skills central to olympiad mathematics. MathArena addresses all three by leveraging the natural release cycle of recurring math competitions as a stream of uncontaminated, high-quality problems for real-time evaluation.

## Input / Output

The benchmark consumes 162 problems across seven 2025 competitions (AIME, HMMT, BRUMO, CMIMC, USAMO, IMO, Project Euler), each formatted into a standardized template. For answer-based problems, models must output a final answer in a \boxed{} environment, which is parsed into structured sympy expressions and checked for equivalence; for proof-based problems (USAMO, IMO), models generate full natural-language proofs graded by two independent human experts on a 7-point scale per problem. Project Euler additionally permits up to 20 code executions in a sandboxed Python/C++ interpreter. Each model is run four times per problem, and results are reported as pass@1 accuracy with 95% confidence intervals computed via paired permutation tests for rank and Bernoulli variance for accuracy.

## Method

MathArena's core mechanism is a dynamic, time-anchored evaluation pipeline: when a new competition is released, problems are extracted, formatted, and immediately used to evaluate a curated set of frontier models (selected for top score, best open-weight, or cost-performance Pareto optimality) using provider-recommended hyperparameters without tuning. Answer grading employs a custom rule-based parser that converts arbitrary LaTeX into sympy expressions, cross-checked by a Gemini-2.5-Flash LLM judge, with a GUI for manual review of flagged cases. Proof grading relies on expert human graders who develop competition-style rubrics and independently score anonymized solutions. For IMO 2025, a best-of-n strategy (32 samples per problem, bracket tournament judged by the model itself) is applied to select the strongest proof. The temporal design—evaluating models only on competitions released after their own release date—structurally eliminates training-data contamination, while the public leaderboard and open-source pipeline ensure full reproducibility and continuous updating as new competitions and models appear.

## Results

On the four answer-based competitions (AIME, HMMT, BRUMO, CMIMC 2025), GPT-5(high) achieves the highest average accuracy of 91.3%, followed by GROK 4 FAST (Reasoning) at 90.6% (at 1/27th the cost) and GROK 4 at 90.4%. These top models substantially outperform the top 1% of human participants (84.4% on AIME, 66.8% on HMMT). On the proof-based IMO 2025, GPT-5 achieves the best score of 16/42 points (38%), falling short of the 19 points needed for a bronze medal. On Project Euler (with code execution tools), GPT-5 leads at 55% accuracy, while GROK 4 and GROK 4 FAST follow at 47.5%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates over 50 models, with the key comparison being between frontier models on uncontaminated 2025 competitions versus their performance on older (potentially contaminated) benchmarks. GPT-5 (91.3% avg) and GROK 4 (90.4%) outperform Gemini-2.5-Pro (79.5%) by roughly 11–12 points on answer-based competitions, with Gemini-2.5-Pro showing particularly weak performance on CMIMC (58.1%). GROK 4 FAST (Reasoning) matches GROK 4 within 0.2 points at 1/42nd the cost ($0.18 vs $7.56), making it the cost-performance Pareto leader. Older-generation models like DeepSeek-R1 (64.2%), OpenAI o1 (65.0%), and Phi-4-Reasoning (60.4%) trail the top models by 25–30 points, confirming the rapid progress of reasoning-focused architectures. Critically, the paper demonstrates that AIME 2024 scores are inflated by 10–20% due to contamination (with QwQ-Preview-32B showing a ~60% outlier), validating the need for MATHARENA's forward-looking evaluation protocol.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of static, publicly available math benchmarks (GSM8K, MATH, AIME) and private curated benchmarks (FrontierMath, HLE) by repurposing the natural release cycle of recurring math competitions as a contamination-free evaluation stream. For proof evaluation, it builds on the best-of-n selection strategy from Dekoninck et al. [9] but applies it to natural-language olympiad proofs graded by human experts, rather than the formal-system (Lean/Coq) approach used by miniF2F, FIMO, and PutnamBench. Unlike dynamic benchmarks such as LiveBench or LiveAoPSBench, which update on a fixed schedule or use retroactive snapshots, MATHARENA evaluates models within hours to days of competition release, making contamination structurally impossible for models released before the event.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MATHARENA provides the field with a transparent, reproducible, and continuously updated evaluation that directly addresses the contamination crisis undermining confidence in widely cited results (e.g., AIME 2024). The finding that top models still score below 40% on IMO 2025 proof problems—and that raw (unfiltered) proof quality drops below 10%—highlights a substantial gap in natural-language reasoning that answer-based benchmarks mask. Key open questions include how to maintain benchmark difficulty as answer-based competitions saturate (possibly by 2026), how to scale human proof grading, and whether the small problem counts (6 for IMO, 30 for AIME) yield sufficiently tight confidence intervals for fine-grained model comparisons.
