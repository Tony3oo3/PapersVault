## Problem

Test-time scaling algorithms (CoT, Self-Consistency, MCTS, Rebase) systematically overuse tokens—reasoning models like DeepSeek-R1 generate up to 3× more tokens than needed—because they lack any mechanism to detect when the model's answer has stabilized and further computation is wasteful. No prior work provides a unified, algorithm-agnostic signal that quantifies this 'answer stabilization' across diverse reasoning paradigms to enable safe early termination.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254751/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system consumes a text prompt and a reasoning algorithm configuration (CoT, SC, MCTS, or Rebase) within a serving engine (SGLang). During inference, it injects periodic probe prompts (e.g., every 64 tokens for CoT) or collects semantic entropy / reward scores from multi-path algorithms, computing a normalized certainindex in [0,1] at each reasoning step. The scheduler then emits per-request decisions—continue, early-exit, or reallocate token budget—and gang-scheduling priorities for multi-stage reasoning programs, ultimately producing the final answer with reduced token consumption.

## Method

Certainindex is a unified, algorithm-agnostic confidence metric: for CoT it uses 'Probe-In-The-Middle' (injecting a forced-answer prompt at fixed token intervals) and measures answer consistency over a sliding window; for multi-path algorithms (SC, MCTS, Rebase) it computes normalized semantic entropy H̃ = (log n − H)/log n over clustered answers; for reward-based algorithms it uses the normalized reward score. A theoretical analysis (Lemma 1–2) proves that if empirical answer distributions stabilize across k probes, the true next-token distributions have converged to a stationary distribution, justifying early termination with bounded accuracy loss ε. Dynasor implements Certainindex as a ~500-line scheduler layer inside SGLang, performing adaptive per-request token budgeting (thresholding or curve-fitted allocation) and gang scheduling of co-related sub-requests within a reasoning program to maximize KV-cache reuse and free resources from high-certainty programs, achieving up to 50% compute savings in batch and 3.3× throughput in online serving.

## Results

In batch inference, Dynasor reduces token usage by 11–29% on CoT (up to 53% on AIME and 81% on MATH500 for the top 1% of problems) and by 9–52% on SC, MCTS, and Rebase (over 47% on SC-GSM8K, over 50% on Rebase-Math), all while maintaining the same accuracy. In online serving, Dynasor sustains 1.6–3.3× higher request rates under P90 deadline attainment compared to SGLang and 1.6–3.2× compared to Parrot, and allows 1.3–4.7× tighter SLO scales versus SGLang. At the same SLO attainment, Dynasor achieves 0.7%–2% higher accuracy than both baselines by redistributing compute between simple and hard queries.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Dynasor is compared against two LLM serving systems (SGLang and Parrot) in online serving and against two intra-program scheduling policies (baseline-even and baseline-length) in batch inference. Against SGLang, Dynasor achieves 1.6–3.3× higher sustainable request rates under P90 deadline attainment and 1.3–4.7× tighter SLO scales, while also delivering 0.7–2% higher accuracy at the same SLO attainment. Against Parrot, the gains are 1.6–3.2× on rate and 1.7–3.3× on SLO tightness. In batch inference, Dynasor's certaindex-driven allocation saves 9–52% tokens versus baseline-even (uniform allocation) with no accuracy loss, and outperforms baseline-length (which uses cumulative token count as a proxy) that suffers accuracy degradation even under less aggressive pruning. The gains stem from two mechanisms: intra-program early termination of high-certaindex requests and inter-program gang scheduling that prioritizes co-located reasoning sub-queries.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the test-time scaling literature (CoT, Self-Consistency, MCTS, Rebase) and the growing body of work on LLM uncertainty estimation (semantic entropy, log-probability, hidden-state signals), but departs from prior early-stopping methods (Adaptive-Consistency, Early-Stopping SC, Dynamic Early Exit) by proposing a unified, algorithm-agnostic metric (Certainindex) that generalizes across multiple reasoning paradigms rather than targeting a single one. The 'Probe-In-The-Middle' technique for CoT is novel in forcing mid-reasoning answer extraction to detect convergence, and the integration of this metric into a production serving system (Dynasor on SGLang) with gang scheduling for multi-stage reasoning programs represents a systems-level contribution absent from prior efficiency-focused reasoning work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The demonstrated 50% compute savings in batch inference and 3.3× throughput gains in online serving, with no accuracy drop, make this a practically significant efficiency win for LLM deployment at scale. The answer stabilization phenomenon, while intuitive, is formalized here with a theoretical convergence guarantee and validated across four reasoning algorithms, multiple model families, and seven datasets, giving practitioners a principled tool rather than a heuristic. Open questions include integration with prefill-decode disaggregation and chunked prefill, security implications of exposing Certainindex in multi-tenant settings, and whether the moderate correlation (mean Pearson 0.52) between Certainindex and remaining compute can be tightened for more aggressive allocation.
