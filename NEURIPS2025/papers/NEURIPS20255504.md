## Problem

Existing methods for probing LLM contextual robustness rely on fixed-template or retrieval-based distractions that are increasingly ineffective against contemporary models, often causing less than 5% performance degradation on advanced systems like GPT-4o. This paper addresses the need for a dynamic, model-informed generation strategy that produces semantically coherent, answer-preserving distractions capable of reliably degrading performance across diverse tasks and model families.

## Input / Output

The framework consumes multiple-choice problem instances (question, ground truth answer, set of incorrect answers) along with a designated victim model and a proxy model for perturbation generation. It outputs a perturbed dataset where each question has an adaptive distraction ΔQ appended, subject to two hard constraints: a binary semantic-consistency classifier must confirm no meaning shift, and the length ratio len(Q′)/len(Q) must not exceed λ=10. The final deliverable includes the candidate problem list of successfully distracted examples and per-model accuracy drop metrics across four benchmarks.

## Method

The core mechanism is a priority-queue-guided tree search where the tree width is fixed by the number of incorrect answer candidates |A_inc|. At each node, a proxy LLM generates k child perturbations (one per incorrect answer) via Error-Guided Perturbation, and each child is evaluated by sampling n=5 outputs from the victim model to estimate its success rate r_M(P′). A value function V(P′) = exp(α/r_M(P′)) · depth^(−γ) (with α=2, γ=1) prioritizes nodes where the model fails most while penalizing deep recursion, and nodes with r_M=0 are collected directly into the candidate list. A classifier pre-filters low-susceptibility questions to avoid wasting ~37% of compute, and two early-stopping rules (diversity control capping successful children at n₁=3, and monotonic success-rate pruning across consecutive levels) further reduce cost. For mitigation, the paper shows DPO fine-tuning on ~1200 preference pairs (correct vs. incorrect responses on distracted inputs) substantially recovers robustness, whereas prompt-based strategies yield only marginal gains.

## Results

Across four benchmarks (MMLU, CommonsenseQA, OpenbookQA, TruthfulQA) and seven mainstream LLMs, adaptive distractions cause an average accuracy drop exceeding 45%, with the largest drops on CommonsenseQA (up to 66.8% for GPT-4o-mini) and OpenbookQA (up to 63.6% for Gemma-2-27B). In the direct baseline comparison (Table 2), the proposed method reduces average accuracy from 0.843 to 0.323 (a 52.0% drop), compared to 0.691 for DyVal2, 0.774 for SPD, and 0.757 for ICA. Even the most robust model, Claude-3.5-Sonnet, drops from 0.879 to 0.495 under adaptive distraction. The method also generalizes to MATH-500, where GPT-4o-mini drops from 0.805 to 0.302.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed tree-search framework dramatically outperforms all three baselines in distraction effectiveness (measured by lower post-perturbation accuracy). Against DyVal2 (Zhu et al., ICML 2024), the closest competitor, the proposed method achieves an average accuracy of 0.323 versus 0.691, representing a 36.8-point gap; even Claude-3.5-Sonnet drops to 0.495 under the proposed method versus 0.780 under DyVal2. Against SPD (Shi et al., ICML 2023), which generates distractions via a single prompt without optimization, the gap is even wider: 0.323 vs. 0.774 average accuracy. ICA (Ye et al., 2024), which simply augments questions with irrelevant context to increase length, is the weakest baseline at 0.757 average, confirming that mere length expansion is insufficient. The paper also shows in Table 9 that the static GSM-IC method from Shi et al. causes only a 1.8% average drop on advanced models, whereas the adaptive method achieves a 45% average drop, underscoring the necessity of model-informed, iterative search over fixed-template approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the contextual robustness evaluation line initiated by Shi et al. (2023) and Liu et al. (2023), which demonstrated LLM vulnerability to irrelevant context using fixed templates or retrieval-based distractors. It departs from these static approaches by adapting the tree-search structure from Tree of Thoughts (Yao et al., 2023) and the author group's own cross-lingual probing framework (Xu et al., 2025) to generate model-informed, error-guided perturbations via a priority-queue search. The key conceptual shift is from one-shot or template-based distraction to an iterative, simulation-driven search where the victim model's own failure signals guide perturbation generation, making the attack co-evolve with model capability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that adaptive distractions cause 45%+ average accuracy drops across frontier models (including GPT-4o and Claude-3.5-Sonnet) while static methods yield under 2% on the same models reframes the practical risk of contextual interference in deployed LLMs. The demonstration that prompt-based mitigation is essentially ineffective while DPO fine-tuning recovers substantial robustness (e.g., Phi-3.5-mini surpassing GPT-4o on distracted inputs) provides actionable guidance for practitioners. Open questions include whether the multiple-choice constraint limits generalization, whether integrating distraction generation into training loops (as the authors suggest) can close the gap entirely, and whether the self-distracting phenomenon (models generating more effective distractions for themselves) reflects a fundamental self-awareness limitation.
