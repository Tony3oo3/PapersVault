## Problem

Longitudinal multimodal clinical data suffers from two underaddressed challenges: (1) static anatomical features in sequential chest X-rays dominate over subtle pathological changes, diluting disease progression signals, and (2) sparse, irregular imaging snapshots are temporally misaligned with continuous EHR measurements, complicating cross-modal trend alignment. Existing progression models treat all imaging features uniformly, while multimodal fusion methods rely on rigid imputation or fixed time embeddings without adaptive mechanisms to synchronize fine-grained cross-modal patterns.

## Input / Output

The method consumes consecutive pairs of AP-view chest X-rays cropped to anatomical regions via Chest ImaGenome bounding boxes, EHR time series of 38 physiological variables sampled at 1-hour intervals, and 7 demographic attributes. It produces three types of outputs: per-region disease progression status (improved/worsened/no change) for seven thoracic conditions, binary in-hospital mortality, and four-class ICU length-of-stay. The model requires at least two CXRs per ICU stay, and EHR data is extracted within the temporal interval between consecutive CXR pairs for the progression task.

## Method

DiPro disentangles static anatomical and dynamic pathological features from consecutive CXR pairs using a shared ResNet-50 backbone followed by two parallel MLP projection heads, enforced by an orthogonal constraint (minimizing cosine similarity between static and dynamic features) and a temporal consistency loss (MSE between consecutive static features). A Progression-Aware Enhancement module reverses the CXR pair order and trains the model to produce inverted dynamic predictions while keeping static features consistent, sharpening sensitivity to progression direction. A Multiscale Multimodal Fusion module then aligns the disentangled features with EHR data at two granularities: local cross-attention with a center-focused attention mask maps EHR timestamps to each CXR interval, and global cross-attention integrates all locally-fused features with the full EHR sequence. Final predictions combine dynamic and static representations via a cross-attention fusion head, trained with a weighted sum of cross-entropy and all auxiliary disentanglement losses.

## Results

On the disease progression identification task (7 thoracic conditions, macro-averaged), DiPro achieves F1=0.466, AUPRC=0.478, and AUROC=0.664 in the multimodal setting, surpassing the best unimodal baseline SDPL (F1=0.393) by 15.3% relative F1 and the best multimodal baseline UTDE (F1=0.449) by 3.8% relative F1. On general ICU prediction, DiPro (longitudinal CXR + EHR) reaches AUPRC=0.742 for in-hospital mortality and ACC=0.440 for length-of-stay, outperforming the strongest baselines (UMSE: AUPRC=0.722, ACC=0.419) by 2.8% and 3.0% respectively. DiPro also reduces FLOPs by 9.8% and inference latency by 22.0% compared to the region-based CheXRelNet while improving F1 by 16.5%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiPro outperforms all seven baselines across every metric in both unimodal and multimodal settings. Among unimodal CXR-only baselines, SDPL [13] is the strongest (F1=0.393, AUPRC=0.417), but DiPro's unimodal variant still leads by 7.0 F1 points and 5.1 AUPRC points, attributed to explicit spatiotemporal disentanglement of static anatomy from dynamic pathology. CheXRelNet [14] (F1=0.389) and CheXRelFormer [33] (F1=0.354) trail further behind, with the latter showing the highest variance (±0.032 F1). In the multimodal setting, UTDE [19] is the closest competitor (F1=0.449, AUPRC=0.472), yet DiPro still gains 1.7 F1 and 0.6 AUPRC points, demonstrating that its multiscale local-global fusion handles temporal misalignment more effectively than UTDE's gated imputation. UMSE [20] (F1=0.352) and MedFuse [17] (F1=0.409) underperform substantially, with UMSE's unified time-embedding approach failing to exploit longitudinal CXR context. DrFuse [18] (F1=0.429) benefits from its own disentanglement strategy but lacks the progression-aware enhancement and multiscale alignment that give DiPro its edge. On general ICU prediction (Table 2), DiPro with longitudinal CXRs achieves the best mortality AUPRC (0.742 vs. UMSE's 0.722) and length-of-stay accuracy (0.440 vs. UMSE's 0.419), while also being the only method that consistently improves when switching from last-CXR to longitudinal-CXR input.

## Relation to Prior Work

DiPro sits at the intersection of two threads: sequential CXR progression models (CheXRelNet, SDPL, CheXRelFormer) that treat all imaging features uniformly, and multimodal clinical fusion methods (MedFuse, DrFuse, UTDE, UMSE) that typically use only the latest CXR or rigid time embeddings. Its key conceptual departure is applying spatiotemporal feature disentanglement—borrowed from representation learning (orthogonal constraints, static/dynamic separation)—specifically to the temporal axis of medical imaging, combined with a progression-aware reversal trick to sharpen direction sensitivity. The multiscale fusion module then bridges this disentangled representation with EHR at both interval and sequence levels, going beyond the simple concatenation or unified time-embedding strategies used by prior multimodal baselines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses two clinically grounded pain points—redundancy from static anatomy in sequential CXRs and temporal misalignment between sparse imaging and continuous EHR—that prior work has acknowledged but not jointly resolved. The ablation finding that disentanglement without dedicated multiscale fusion actually degrades performance (A3 vs. A4) is a useful caution: separating static/dynamic features is necessary but not sufficient without a fusion architecture that exploits the separation. Key limitations include dependence on Chest ImaGenome bounding boxes (mitigated but not eliminated by the RGRG ablation), exclusion of single-CXR visits, and evaluation confined to MIMIC; open questions include generalization to other modalities (MRI, CT), scalability without curated anatomical annotations, and whether the multiscale alignment strategy transfers to non-ICU longitudinal settings.
