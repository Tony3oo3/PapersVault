## Problem

Conventional fixed-budget schedules (e.g., cosine LR) are inadequate for open-ended, continuously scaling LLM pretraining. The two main alternatives—warmup-stable-decay (WSD) and weight averaging—each carry a critical drawback: WSD requires explicit decay phases to assess model quality, while weight averaging incurs significant memory overhead (e.g., >16 GB for a single 16-bit copy of LLaMA-8B). The paper asks whether an optimizer can track the optimal loss trajectory without either decay phases or auxiliary model copies.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard text-token sequences (0.5M or 2M tokens per batch) fed into a 124M-parameter LLaMA- or GPT-2-style decoder-only transformer, with the only additional hyperparameter being a scalar decoupling constant C (typically 50–500) in the refined variant. It outputs trained model weights that require no extra memory beyond the base AdamW state (no separate averaged copy), and the y_t iterates can be evaluated at any checkpoint without a decay phase to estimate final quality.

## Method

The paper shows that Schedule-Free AdamW (SF-AdamW) implicitly follows the 'river' floor of the river-valley loss landscape throughout training, eliminating the need for LR decay or explicit weight averaging. Theoretically, SF is reformulated as a momentum update on the y_t iterates, revealing that x_t is a weighted average of past y_t values—i.e., SF performs implicit weight averaging with zero extra memory—and that the y_t iterates operate at the Edge of Stability with preconditioned sharpness equilibrating near 2/((1−β₁)γ). The key practical contribution is a refined variant that introduces a decoupling parameter C, redefining c_t = (1−β)C/t so that the averaging window width depends only on C while β independently controls momentum on y_t. This decoupling resolves the dual-role conflict of β in vanilla SF, yielding robustness to suboptimal momentum (β₁ ∈ {0.1, 0.5}) and matching AdamW-with-cosine performance under large batch sizes (2M tokens) with a single sweep over C.

## Results

On SlimPajama (0.5M-token batch, 124M LLaMA), refined SF-AdamW with β₁=0.95 and C=100 achieves a test perplexity of 23.60, improving over vanilla SF-AdamW (25.12) by 1.52 points. Under suboptimal momentum (β₁=0.9), the refined method with C=50 reaches 23.97, matching the best vanilla configuration. In the large-batch setting (2M tokens), refined SF-AdamW with β₁=0.98 and C=500 achieves 23.95 perplexity, closing the gap with AdamW+cosine that vanilla SF-AdamW (26.09) fails to match.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against vanilla SF-AdamW (Defazio et al., 2024), the refined variant with decoupling parameter C consistently improves perplexity across all momentum settings: at β₁=0.95, C=100 reduces perplexity from 25.12 to 23.60 (−1.52), and at β₁=0.9, C=50 reduces it from 27.70 to 23.97 (−3.73), effectively eliminating the sensitivity to suboptimal momentum. Against AdamW with cosine LR schedule (Loshchilov & Hutter, 2017), the comparison is most relevant in the large-batch regime (2M tokens): vanilla SF-AdamW (β₁=0.98) underperforms AdamW+cosine, while refined SF-AdamW with C=500 matches AdamW+cosine's final perplexity (23.95 vs. the AdamW+cosine baseline shown in Figure 9-right). The paper also demonstrates that SF-AdamW with constant LR requires neither LR decay nor exponential weight averaging to reach near-optimal solutions, unlike standard AdamW which benefits substantially from both (Figure 2). Overall, the refined SF-AdamW wins on momentum robustness and large-batch scaling, ties AdamW+cosine in the large-batch final perplexity, and outperforms vanilla SF-AdamW across all tested configurations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Schedule-Free (SF) framework of Defazio et al. (2024), which interpolates between Polyak-Ruppert averaging and primal averaging, and extends it with a decoupling parameter C that separates momentum control from the implicit averaging window. Conceptually, it positions SF-AdamW within the river-valley loss landscape framework of Wen et al. (2025) and the Edge of Stability / central flow literature (Cohen et al., 2021/2022/2025), showing that SF dynamics implicitly perform weight averaging without extra memory. It departs from WSD schedules and explicit weight averaging (SWA, EWA) by demonstrating that neither decay phases nor auxiliary model copies are needed when the optimizer naturally tracks the valley floor.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that SF-AdamW with a constant LR already tracks the river—making both LR decay and weight averaging redundant—is practically significant for flexible, open-ended LLM pretraining where committing to a fixed compute budget is undesirable. The refined variant addressing momentum sensitivity and large-batch degradation is a concrete step toward making SF methods a drop-in replacement for AdamW+cosine in production pipelines. However, all experiments are limited to 124M-parameter models on 6B tokens, leaving the central question of whether these dynamics persist at the scale of modern LLMs (billions of parameters, trillions of tokens) entirely open. Validating the central flow approximation in deep learning and extending the river-valley analysis to other adaptive optimizers are natural next steps.
