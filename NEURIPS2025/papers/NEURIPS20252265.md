## Problem

Bilevel reinforcement learning (BRL) is a powerful framework for AI alignment and RLHF, yet no sample complexity bounds existed for continuous state-action spaces. Prior theoretical results were either confined to tabular settings, provided only iteration complexity without sample complexity, or relied on computationally expensive second-order derivatives (Hessians) that are infeasible in high-dimensional parameterized settings.

## Input / Output

The method operates on a discounted MDP with bounded (possibly infinite) state and action spaces, a parameterized reward function r_φ, and a parameterized policy π_λ. Unlike standard bilevel optimization, gradient estimates here are inherently biased due to MDP dynamics: the Q-function is approximated by a neural network (introducing approximation error ε_approx), and infinite-horizon sums are truncated to finite trajectories of length H. The output is optimized reward and policy parameters with a guaranteed sample complexity of Õ(ε^{-3}), where the total sample count is n·K·T + B·K·H·T + B·H·T.

## Method

The paper adapts the penalty-based bilevel reformulation of Kwon et al. (2024) to the RL setting, replacing the hypergradient (which requires Hessian inverses and lower-level convexity) with a first-order proxy objective Φ_σ(φ) that only needs gradient evaluations. The key analytical insight is recognizing that the inner-loop parameter estimation steps in Algorithm 1 constitute SGD on a loss satisfying the Polyak-Lojasiewicz (PL) property, enabling a novel recursive analysis of the optimality gap under biased gradient estimates (Lemma 1). Combined with MDP-specific closed-form gradient expressions derived via a policy-gradient-style argument, this yields tight bounds on each component of the gradient estimation error. The resulting algorithm is fully first-order and Hessian-free, and the analysis extends to standard bilevel optimization with non-convex lower levels, improving the state-of-the-art sample complexity from Õ(ε^{-6}) to Õ(ε^{-3}).

## Results

The paper establishes the first sample complexity bound for bilevel reinforcement learning (BRL) in continuous state-action spaces, achieving $\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(\epsilon^{-3})$ with an iteration complexity of $\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(\epsilon^{-1})$. For standard bilevel optimization with a non-convex lower level, the same analysis yields a state-of-the-art sample complexity of $\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(\epsilon^{-3})$, improving upon the previous best of $\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(\epsilon^{-6})$ from Chen et al. (COLT 2024) and $\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(\epsilon^{-7})$ from Kwon et al. (ICLR 2024). The method is fully first-order (Hessian-free) and does not require the lower-level objective to be convex, only satisfying the Polyak-Lojasiewicz (PL) condition.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In the BRL setting (Table 1), this paper is the first to provide a sample complexity bound for continuous state-action spaces. All prior BRL works—Shen et al. [31], Chakraborty et al. (PARL) [1], and Shen et al. [30]—establish only iteration complexity (all at $\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(\epsilon^{-1})$) and are restricted to tabular spaces. Yang et al. [40] provides a sample complexity of $\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(\epsilon^{-3.5})$ but only for tabular MDPs; this paper improves the rate to $\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(\epsilon^{-3})$ while extending to continuous spaces. In the standard bilevel optimization setting (Table 2), the paper's $\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(\epsilon^{-3})$ sample complexity with a non-convex lower level and no second-order terms strictly improves upon Kwon et al. [22] ($\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(\epsilon^{-7})$) and Chen et al. [2] ($\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(\epsilon^{-6})$), and matches or surpasses the iteration complexity of convex-lower-level methods like Ji et al. [19] and Yang et al. [41] while removing the convexity requirement. The empirical validation on Mujoco tasks (Appendix F) is proof-of-concept in nature, confirming the algorithm's practical viability rather than providing a head-to-head benchmark comparison.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the penalty-based bilevel optimization framework of Kwon et al. (ICLR 2024) and Chen et al. (COLT 2024), which established first-order methods for non-convex lower-level problems under the PL condition, and adapts it to the BRL setting where gradient estimates are inherently biased due to MDP dynamics. It departs from prior BRL convergence results (Shen et al. 2025, Yang et al. 2025, Chakraborty et al. 2024) that were confined to tabular state spaces, by leveraging the MDP structure to derive closed-form gradient expressions and using the PL property of the inner-loop loss to obtain a recursive optimality-gap analysis. The key methodological departure is recognizing that the inner-loop SGD steps in the BRL algorithm constitute gradient descent on a PL-satisfying function, enabling a clean recursive bound that was not available in prior tabular analyses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first sample complexity bound for BRL in continuous state-action spaces, closing a gap between theory (tabular-only) and practice (parameterized RLHF, reward shaping). The O-tilde(epsilon^{-3}) result also improves the state-of-the-art for standard bilevel optimization with non-convex lower levels from O-tilde(epsilon^{-6}) to O-tilde(epsilon^{-3}), suggesting the PL-based recursive analysis is broadly applicable. Limitations include the PL assumption on the lower-level function (which may not hold for all RL objectives), the biased gradient estimates inherent to RL that prevent direct transfer of standard bilevel results, and proof-of-concept-only experiments on Mujoco tasks. Open questions include whether the PL condition can be relaxed, whether tighter bounds are achievable, and whether the first-order algorithm scales to high-dimensional RLHF applications.
