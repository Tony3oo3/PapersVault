## Problem

Standard Transformers are provably limited to the TC^0 complexity class without chain-of-thought reasoning, making them unable to solve inherently serial tasks like entity tracking, Python code execution, and chess state tracking. While delta-rule-based state-space models (DeltaNet, RWKV7) can surpass TC^0, they rely on fixed-size state spaces that degrade on retrieval-heavy tasks. The paper asks whether a Transformer-like architecture can simultaneously break the TC^0 expressivity barrier and retain strong retrieval capability.

## Input / Output

For language modeling, the model consumes standard token sequences (context length 2048) and produces next-token predictions. For the element-exchange task, input is a sequence of swap operations drawn from a vocabulary of C(5,2)=10 pairs, and the model outputs which of 5 elements occupies a designated position after each swap. For DAG reachability, input is a sequence of node embeddings encoding parent-child adjacency, and the model outputs a binary reachability label per node. The KV cache size scales as O(T log n) for T swaps among n objects, reducible to O(n log n) with periodic read-out and rewrite every O(n) steps.

## Method

DeltaFormer generalizes the delta rule by replacing the linear inner product with a kernel function κ(x,y)=ψ(x)ᵀψ(y), where ψ maps to a potentially infinite-dimensional space. This yields the update u_t = α_t v_t − β_t Σ κ₁(k_i, w_t) u_i and retrieval o_t = Σ κ₂(k_i, q_t) u_i, which subsumes the standard Transformer (β=0, α=1, softmax kernel) and DeltaNet (linear kernel) as special cases. The key theoretical result is that a nonlinear kernel with d=O(log n) dimension enables tracking T exchanges among n objects via a constructive proof using near-orthogonal keys on the unit sphere, with the exchange operation implemented by setting k_t = k_{t1} − k_{t2} and v_t = 0. For GPU efficiency, the sequential recurrence is rewritten as the matrix equation U = (I+A)⁻¹V and solved chunk-wise: the sequence is divided into N chunks of length C, each solved in parallel via matrix inversion, reducing recurrent steps from O(T) to O(N) at a 22× speedup over the recurrent baseline on H100 GPUs.

## Results

On the 14B-parameter MoE model, DeltaFormer achieves an average benchmark score of 61.34, outperforming the Transformer baseline (60.30) by +1.04 points across 10 benchmarks including MMLU (+1.4), COPA (+0.8), and Winogrande (+2.6). On training loss, DeltaFormer leads by 0.003 on the general domain and by 0.05 on the code domain at 300B tokens, where its loss matches the Transformer's 400B-token loss. On the 340M-parameter scale, DeltaFormer variants are statistically indistinguishable from the Transformer (within ±1 point on all benchmarks). On the synthetic swap-tracking task, a single-layer DeltaFormer with a rounding kernel achieves 100% accuracy in tracking exchanges of 5 elements, while multi-layer Transformers fail to converge.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the standard Transformer (Vaswani et al., 2017), DeltaFormer wins decisively on state-tracking tasks: a 1-layer DeltaFormer with κ₁(x,y)=⌊xᵀy⌋ achieves 100% accuracy on the 5-element swap task, while increasing Transformer layers yields no improvement. On DAG reachability (32 nodes), a single-layer DeltaFormer reaches near-perfect accuracy whereas a multi-layer Transformer plateaus below 100%. On the 14B MoE language modeling benchmarks, DeltaFormer outperforms the Transformer by +1.04 average points, with the largest gains on C-Eval (+2.3) and Winogrande (+2.6), though it slightly underperforms on SIQA (−0.9). At the 340M scale, the two architectures are statistically equivalent across all 10 benchmarks. Against RNN-based baselines (Tape-RNN, Stack-RNN from Delétang et al., 2022) in the Chomsky hierarchy evaluation, DeltaFormer matches them on Regular-language tasks (100% on modular arithmetic and parity) but falls short on DCF and CS tasks (58–63% vs. 100%), where sequential RNNs retain an advantage due to their inherently higher expressivity.

## Relation to Prior Work

DeltaFormer re-examines the standard Transformer through the lens of the delta rule and kernel functions, revealing that the Transformer is a degenerate special case (β=0, α=1, softmax kernel) of a more general state-space update. It directly extends DeltaNet (linear-kernel delta rule) by introducing nonlinear kernels, which the authors prove is the critical ingredient for breaking the TC⁰ expressivity barrier that constrains all log-precision Transformers. The work also connects to the RWKV7 line of state-space models that use finite-size state matrices for tracking, showing that a nonlinear kernel with O(n log n) space can match what RWKV7 achieves with O(n²) state, while retaining the retrieval strengths of attention-based architectures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This paper provides the first constructive proof that a Transformer-family architecture can solve NC¹-complete problems (element exchange tracking) without chain-of-thought, directly addressing a fundamental theoretical limitation identified by Merrill & Sabharwal. The chunk-wise parallel algorithm (22× faster than recurrent, 8× faster than fully parallel) makes the architecture practical for GPU training, and the 14B MoE experiments show consistent benchmark gains over the Transformer baseline. However, expressivity validation relies on toy tasks (5-element swaps, 32-node DAGs), and language modeling gains are demonstrated only at 340M and 14B scale with 500B tokens—large-scale industrial training remains to confirm whether the expressivity advantage translates to meaningful improvements on complex real-world tasks. Open questions include optimal depth-width ratios for DeltaFormer, the role of RoPE (which appears to hinder expressivity but aid optimization), and whether hybrid DeltaFormer-Transformer stacks can combine state-tracking with long-range retrieval.
