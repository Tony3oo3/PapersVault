## Problem

Universal optimization methods that adapt to unknown smoothness levels remain incomplete, particularly for strongly convex objectives where no existing method achieves accelerated convergence in the smooth regime while maintaining near-optimal rates in the non-smooth one. The paper bridges this gap by developing gradient-variation online learning under Hölder smoothness and converting it to offline optimization, yielding the first universal strongly convex method with accelerated smooth-case convergence.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a (stochastic) gradient oracle over a bounded convex domain X with diameter D, a total oracle-query budget T, and an initial point x_1; for the strongly convex variant it additionally takes the curvature λ (or searches over it via grid search in Algorithm 3). Notably, no smoothness parameter (L_ν, ν), Lipschitz constant G, or condition number κ is required as input. The output is a weighted-average iterate x̄_T with a sub-optimality gap that interpolates between O(L_ν D^{1+ν}/T^{(1+3ν)/2}) for Hölder-smooth objectives and O(GD/√T) for Lipschitz ones, with the strongly convex case achieving O(exp(−T/(6√κ))) in the smooth regime.

## Method

The core algorithm is optimistic OGD with AdaGrad-style step sizes η_t ∝ 1/√A_t, where A_t accumulates squared gradient deviations, enabling virtual clipping without knowing the smoothness constant. A key analytical tool (Lemma 1) recasts (L_ν,ν)-Hölder smoothness as inexact smoothness with a controllable corruption term, allowing the standard gradient-variation cancellation to proceed. For convex offline optimization, the stabilized O2B conversion with weights α_t = t converts the O(√V_T + L_ν D^{1+ν} T^{(1−ν)/2}) regret into the optimal O(L_ν D^{1+ν}/T^{(1+3ν)/2} + σD/√T) rate. For strongly convex optimization, a novel O2B conversion (Lemma 5) introduces Bregman-divergence terms whose cancellation hinges on a guess-and-check smoothness detection: the algorithm guesses a weight ratio β_t, computes the empirical smoothness L_t, and halves β_t until β_t ≤ √(λ/(4L_t)), guaranteeing logarithmic overhead. When λ is also unknown, Algorithm 3 runs M = ⌈2log₂T⌉ parallel instances with geometrically decreasing λ_i and selects the best output.

## Results

For stochastic convex optimization with (L_ν,ν)-Hölder smoothness (Theorem 2), the method achieves a sub-optimality gap of O(L_ν D^(1+ν) / T^((1+3ν)/2) + σD/√T), matching the optimal rate of Rodomanov et al. [2024] while requiring no knowledge of L_ν or ν. For deterministic strongly convex optimization (Theorem 4), the method achieves O(G²/λ · min{exp(-T/(6√κ)), log T/T}), which is the first accelerated rate in the smooth regime (κ = L_ℓ/λ) without knowing L_ℓ or G. When the strong convexity curvature λ is also unknown (Theorem 5), the rate becomes O(‖∇ℓ(x₁)‖²/λ · exp(-T/((1+4√(2κ))⌈2log₂T⌉))), improving the constant in the exponent from 882√κ (Lan et al., 2023) to (1+4√(2κ))⌈2log₂T⌉.

## Baselines & Comparisons

For convex optimization, the paper's Theorem 2 matches the strong universality rate of Rodomanov et al. [2024] (O(L_ν/T^((1+3ν)/2) + σ/√T)) while subsuming the weaker result of Kavis et al. [2019] (UniXGrad), which only handles the smooth/non-smooth dichotomy. For strongly convex optimization, the key improvement is over Levy [2017]: Levy achieves a non-accelerated rate of O(exp(-T/κ)·T/κ) in the smooth case (marked with † in Table 1), whereas this paper's Theorem 4 achieves the first accelerated rate of O(exp(-T/(6√κ))) without knowing L_ℓ or G. For the setting where both L_ℓ and λ are unknown (Section 4.3), the paper's Theorem 5 improves upon Lan et al. [2023], whose sample-complexity-based rate translates to O(exp(-T/(882√κ))); the paper's rate O(exp(-T/((1+4√(2κ))⌈2log₂T⌉))) is superior for all T ≤ 8.7×10¹⁹ despite the additional log T factor. The paper's analysis is noted to be simpler than Rodomanov et al. [2024] due to explicitly decoupling adaptive step sizes from gradient evaluation on weighted averaged iterates.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of gradient-variation online learning (Chiang et al., 2012) and online-to-batch conversion (Cutkosky, 2019), extending both to the Hölder smoothness regime. It adopts the AdaGrad-style adaptive step sizes from Kavis et al. (2019, UniXGrad) to perform 'virtual clipping' without knowing the smoothness parameter, and introduces a novel guess-and-check smoothness detection scheme for the strongly convex case. The key departure from prior universal methods (Levy 2017, Kavis et al. 2019) is achieving the first accelerated convergence rate in the smooth regime for strongly convex optimization, and extending strong universality to the full Hölder smoothness spectrum ν ∈ [0,1] for stochastic convex optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper resolves a long-standing open problem by providing the first universal method that achieves accelerated (exponential) convergence in the smooth regime for strongly convex optimization, while maintaining near-optimal rates in the non-smooth case. The observation that gradient-variation online adaptivity, combined with stabilized O2B conversion, naturally yields strong universality for Hölder smoothness with a simpler analysis than prior approaches (e.g., Rodomanov et al. 2024) opens a new avenue for transferring online learning techniques to offline optimization. Key open questions remain: extending the strongly convex universal method to the stochastic setting, achieving strong universality in unconstrained optimization, and whether Lipschitz continuity is truly necessary for universality in the strongly convex case.
