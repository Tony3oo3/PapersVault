## Problem

Existing controllable image generation methods are largely confined to 2D spatial control or single-object 3D manipulation, and none can simultaneously control the full 9D poses (3D location, size, and orientation) of multiple objects within a single scene. Prior 3D-aware approaches either lack orientation encoding, depend on synthetic data with poor real-world generalization, or rely on one-step generative models that are incompatible with widely used multi-step diffusion/flow frameworks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254445/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt describing the scene content, one or more CNOCS maps (512×512 RGB images where each pixel encodes normalized coordinates on a cuboid surface, capturing location, size, and orientation per object), and optionally user-specific LoRA weights for personalized subjects. It produces a 512×512 RGB image with objects rendered at the specified 3D poses. Conditions are injected only during the first 15 of 20 denoising steps, and at inference the Disentangled Object Sampling procedure runs per-object forward passes in parallel with the global pass at each step.

## Method

SceneDesigner attaches a ControlNet-like branched network to Stable Diffusion 3.5 (a rectified-flow transformer) and conditions it on a novel CNOCS map representation that encodes 9D poses as a spatial image: for each pixel, the algorithm intersects the view ray with depth-sorted cuboids, projects the hit point into the object's local frame, normalizes to [-1,1], and writes the coordinate as the pixel value. Training is two-stage: first, supervised flow-matching on the newly constructed ObjectPose9D dataset (125K images from OmniNOCS + MS-COCO with 9D annotations); second, RL fine-tuning that maximizes a composite reward (IoU-based location/size accuracy + KL-divergence-based orientation accuracy) over a rebalanced pose distribution to fix low-frequency pose failures. At inference, Disentangled Object Sampling composites per-object latents (each conditioned on a single object's CNOCS map and name) with the global latent using region masks at every denoising step, preventing concept fusion and insufficient generation in multi-object scenes.

## Results

On ObjectPose-Single-Front, SceneDesigner achieves 50.20% Acc_ls, 57.21% mIoU, 13.23° Abs.Err, and 89.47% Acc@22.5°, substantially outperforming LOOSECONTROL (23.89% Acc_ls, 87.26° Abs.Err) and C3DW (2.02% Acc_ls, 50.01° Abs.Err). On the challenging ObjectPose-Single-Back benchmark, SceneDesigner reaches 83.33% Acc@22.5° while LOOSECONTROL drops to 7.05%. In multi-object settings (ObjectPose-Multi), SceneDesigner maintains 47.16% Acc_ls and 80.79% Acc@22.5°, compared to LOOSECONTROL's 14.85% and 4.80%. In terms of visual quality, SceneDesigner achieves FID 24.91 and CLIP score 0.345, versus 37.89/0.293 for LOOSECONTROL and 67.39/0.267 for C3DW.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SceneDesigner decisively outperforms both baselines on every metric across all three benchmarks. Against LOOSECONTROL [6], which encodes only 3D bounding boxes without orientation, SceneDesigner improves Acc_ls by ~26 points (50.20 vs 23.89 on Single-Front) and reduces orientation Abs.Err from 87.26° to 13.23°; the gap widens dramatically on back-facing and multi-object settings where LOOSECONTROL's Abs.Err exceeds 130° and Acc@22.5° falls below 7%. Against C3DW [8], which projects rotation angles into textural space and is limited to front 180° azimuths, SceneDesigner achieves 25× higher Acc_ls (50.20 vs 2.02) and 3× lower Abs.Err (13.23° vs 50.01°) on Single-Front, while C3DW cannot generate back-facing objects at all. In multi-object generation, C3DW is not applicable (single-object only), and LOOSECONTROL suffers from concept confusion and insufficient generation, yielding only 4.80% Acc@22.5° versus SceneDesigner's 80.79%. On visual quality, SceneDesigner's FID of 24.91 is 34% lower than LOOSECONTROL (37.89) and 63% lower than C3DW (67.39), with the highest CLIP text-image alignment score (0.345 vs 0.293 and 0.267).

## Relation to Prior Work

SceneDesigner sits at the intersection of ControlNet-style structural conditioning (Zhang et al., ICCV 2023), the NOCS coordinate framework (Wang et al., CVPR 2019), and reward-based alignment of diffusion/flow models (AlignProp, Prabhudesai et al., 2023). It departs from prior 3D-aware generation methods—LOOSECONTROL (3D boxes without orientation), C3DW (synthetic-data, front-180° only), and ORIGEN (one-step models, no spatial localization)—by unifying location, size, and orientation into a single cuboid-based spatial map (CNOCS) that feeds a standard multi-step flow-matching backbone. The two-stage RL fine-tuning and Disentangled Object Sampling are novel additions not present in any of the cited predecessors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first method to simultaneously control all 9 degrees of freedom for multiple objects in a single generated image, filling a gap that prior work addressed only partially (2D boxes, single-axis rotation, or single-object orientation). The CNOCS representation offers a practical, category-agnostic alternative to NOCS that avoids requiring precise 3D CAD shapes, lowering the barrier for user interaction. Key open questions remain: the method cannot control precise object shape, multi-object performance is still bounded by the base model's compositional generation quality, and Disentangled Object Sampling introduces per-object forward passes that scale linearly with object count. The RL fine-tuning stage, while effective, is currently limited to single-object balanced distributions, leaving multi-object pose balancing as future work.
