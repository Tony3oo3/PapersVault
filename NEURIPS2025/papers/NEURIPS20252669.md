## Problem

Stable Diffusion-based depth estimation methods require high-precision synthetic depth supervision to preserve the model's visual priors during fine-tuning, making them incompatible with self-supervised learning. Self-supervised monocular depth estimation avoids this dependency but produces blurry, artifact-laden predictions from reprojection losses that would rapidly corrupt SD's latent space. No prior work has successfully integrated a pre-trained diffusion model into a self-supervised depth estimation pipeline.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252669/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image at 1024×320 resolution as input and produces a scale-invariant (SI) depth map as output. During training, it additionally consumes temporally adjacent video frames for photometric reprojection supervision and a mix of KITTI and Hypersim images for the surrogate reconstruction task, but no ground-truth depth is ever used. The SD VAE is frozen throughout; only the U-Net, SSG, and pose network are trained.

## Method

Jasmine fine-tunes Stable Diffusion v2 with single-step denoising (t=999) under self-supervision, using a task switcher to alternate the U-Net between depth prediction and image reconstruction within each batch. The Mix-batch Image Reconstruction (MIR) surrogate task reconstructs randomly sampled KITTI or Hypersim images with photometric loss in the image domain, anchoring SD's latent priors against reprojection artifacts without requiring depth labels. A Scale-Shift GRU (SSG) module with an internal Scale-Shift Transformer performs two iterative steps to convert the VAE-bounded SSI depth output into geometrically consistent SI depth, while its reset gate mechanism filters anomalous gradients from noisy self-supervised signals. Training is stabilized by a pre-trained MonoViT teacher providing pseudo-labels with decaying weight, and the total loss combines photometric reprojection, MIR reconstruction, teacher consistency, and auxiliary edge/GDS losses.

## Results

On the KITTI benchmark, Jasmine achieves the best performance among all self-supervised monocular depth estimation methods with AbsRel 0.090, SqRel 0.581, RMSE 3.944, and a1 0.919 (median alignment), outperforming the previous best SSMDE (Jaeho et al*, AbsRel 0.091, a1 0.913) and all SD-based zero-shot methods (Lotus: AbsRel 0.110, a1 0.892). Under LSQ alignment (Jasmine*), it further improves RMSE to 3.728 and SqRel to 0.540. In zero-shot evaluation, Jasmine achieves AbsRel 0.123 / a1 0.852 on CityScape and AbsRel 0.098 / a1 0.902 on the Foggy DrivingStereo subset, surpassing all compared self-supervised baselines including WeatherDepth (trained on 278k weather-augmented samples).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Jasmine outperforms all three SD-based zero-shot baselines (Marigold, E2E FT, Lotus) by a substantial margin on KITTI: it reduces AbsRel from 0.110–0.120 to 0.090 and improves a1 from 0.874–0.892 to 0.919, while using no depth labels at all (only 68K KITTI+Hypersim images vs. 59K–74K synthetic depth pairs). Among self-supervised monocular methods, Jasmine beats the previous best (Jaeho et al*, AbsRel 0.091, a1 0.913) on every metric, and surpasses MonoViT (AbsRel 0.096, a1 0.908) and Mono-ViFI (AbsRel 0.093, a1 0.909) by clear margins. In zero-shot transfer, Jasmine dominates on CityScape (AbsRel 0.123 vs. MonoViT 0.140, Mono-ViFI 0.134) and on the Foggy DrivingStereo subset (AbsRel 0.098 vs. WeatherDepth 0.132, MonoViT 0.107), even though WeatherDepth was trained on 278K weather-augmented samples while Jasmine uses only 68K. The only scenario where Jasmine* (LSQ alignment) trails Jasmine (median alignment) is in-domain KITTI a1 (0.907 vs. 0.919), which the authors attribute to LSQ introducing a suboptimal shift that disrupts the shift-free SI depth learned during self-supervised training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate lines of work: Stable Diffusion-based depth estimation (Marigold, E2E FT, Lotus), which all require high-precision depth supervision, and self-supervised monocular depth estimation (Monodepth2, MonoViT, Mono-ViFI), which relies on reprojection losses but lacks the sharp detail priors of diffusion models. The key departure is the Mix-batch Image Reconstruction (MIR) surrogate task, which repurposes the SD's own image-generation objective to protect its latent space from reprojection artifacts, and the Scale-Shift GRU (SSG), which resolves the fundamental distribution mismatch between SD's scale-shift-invariant output and the scale-invariant depth required by self-supervised pose-depth coupling. The single-step denoising approach is inherited from E2E FT and Lotus, while the task-switcher mechanism follows GeoWizard.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Jasmine establishes that diffusion-model priors can be preserved in a fully self-supervised setting without any depth annotations, challenging the prevailing assumption that fine-tuning SD for dense prediction demands high-quality supervision. The MIR concept is potentially generalizable to other dense prediction tasks (segmentation, normal estimation) where self-supervision is noisy. However, the computational cost (2.83T MACs, 172 ms on RTX 4090) remains ~100× that of conventional SSMDE methods, and the in-domain KITTI improvement over the best prior SSMDE (Jaeho et al*) is modest (0.090 vs 0.091 AbsRel), with the larger gains appearing in zero-shot transfer. Open questions include whether the approach extends to non-driving scenes, whether the auxiliary Hypersim data can be replaced entirely by real images at scale, and whether the SSG gradient-filtering mechanism generalizes to other diffusion-based self-supervised tasks.
