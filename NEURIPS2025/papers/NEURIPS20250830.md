## Problem

Many real-world problems require reasoning about entire distributions (e.g., a patient's cell population, a promoter's expression profile) rather than individual data points, but existing representation learning methods operate on single samples or use fixed summary statistics that cannot capture distribution-level structure. This paper asks how to learn representations that separate distribution-level signal from finite-sample noise while enabling generative reconstruction of the underlying distribution.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250830/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes finite sets of samples grouped by an organizing principle (patient ID, perturbation identity, sampling month/location, expression quantile, etc.), where set sizes range from tens to tens of millions. The encoder produces a fixed-dimensional latent vector (typically 64–256 dimensions) that is invariant to both permutation and proportional duplication of the input set. The generator then produces arbitrary numbers of synthetic samples conditional on this latent vector, enabling distribution reconstruction, interpolation between populations, and zero-shot prediction of held-out distributions via ridge regression on the embedding.

## Method

GDEs lift autoencoders to the space of distributions by pairing a distributionally invariant encoder (permutation-invariant and invariant to proportional duplication, e.g., mean-pooled Deep Sets, GNN-style pooling with skip connections, or 1D/2D convolutional networks) with any conditional generative model (CVAE, DDPM, HyenaDNA, ProGen2, Sinkhorn-based models) as the decoder. Training uses a plug-in loss: the standard generator objective (ELBO, denoising score matching, autoregressive cross-entropy) evaluated on the input set, with gradients flowing through the encoder via the latent code but not through the generator's sampling process. The key theoretical contribution is showing that distributional invariance plus Hadamard differentiability of the pooling operator yields a central limit theorem for embeddings, making the plug-in loss a consistent and asymptotically normal estimator of the population loss, and that the learned latent space approximately recovers Wasserstein geometry (latent L2 distances correlate with W2 at ρ=0.96 for Gaussians) and linear interpolation approximates optimal transport geodesics.

## Results

On the primary synthetic benchmark (Table 2), GDEs achieve the lowest Wasserstein reconstruction error across all four datasets: 0.02 on 5D Gaussians, 1.82 on 3-component GMMs, 63.79 Sinkhorn divergence on MNIST mixtures, and 102.21 on Fashion-MNIST mixtures. In the perturbation prediction task (Table 4), GDEs improve R² from 0.378 (mean baseline) and 0.421 (scVI) to 0.458, with MSE reduced from 1.855 to 1.501. For viral protein spatiotemporal modeling, GDEs predict held-out sequence distribution months with MAE of 1.83±0.01 months, outperforming mean-pooled ESM embeddings (2.24±0.01 months). In the lineage-traced scRNA-seq task, GDEs exceed Wasserstein Wormhole by over 2 bits of mutual information between early and late clonal representations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GDEs are compared against three main baselines. Against kernel mean embeddings (KME+DDPM), GDEs achieve lower reconstruction error on all four synthetic datasets, with the largest gains on MNIST (63.79 vs. 80.46 Sinkhorn divergence, ~21% improvement) and GMMs (1.82 vs. 2.17 W₂, ~16% improvement). Against Wasserstein Wormhole, GDEs dominate decisively: on MNIST the gap is 63.79 vs. 263.29 (~4x lower), and on Fashion-MNIST 102.21 vs. 320.18 (~3x lower), while on 5D Gaussians GDEs achieve 0.02 vs. 0.20 W₂ (10x lower). In the perturbation prediction task, GDEs outperform scVI (R² 0.458 vs. 0.421; MSE 1.501 vs. 1.551) and the direct mean-expression baseline (R² 0.378; MSE 1.855). In the semi-supervised donor classification task, GDEs improve over the supervised baseline on all metrics (accuracy 0.889 vs. 0.879, ROC AUC 0.513 vs. 0.487, F1 0.148 vs. 0.129). For viral protein sequence modeling, GDEs reduce month-prediction MAE from 2.24 to 1.83 months compared to mean-pooled ESM embeddings.

## Relation to Prior Work

GDEs unify and generalize several prior lines of work on distribution embedding: kernel mean embeddings and the Neural Statistician are recovered as particular choices of distributionally invariant encoder paired with a VAE generator, while Wasserstein Wormhole is a specific instantiation using an attention-based encoder and fixed-size sampling decoder. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of 'distributional invariance' (permutation invariance plus proportional invariance) as the minimal architectural constraint, coupled with the observation that *any* conditional generative model (diffusion, VAE, autoregressive, Sinkhorn-based) can be repurposed as the decoder. The theoretical framing via predictive sufficiency and Otto's Wasserstein geometry connects classical statistics and information geometry to modern generative modeling in a way that prior distribution-embedding work did not explicitly pursue.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a principled, scalable recipe for multiscale representation learning that is directly applicable to any domain where the unit of analysis is a population rather than an individual sample, with validated applications spanning genomics, proteomics, and imaging. The theoretical guarantees (asymptotic normality of embeddings, consistency of the plug-in loss, and recovery of the true distribution as m→∞) give practitioners confidence in training on subsamples and inferring on full datasets. Key limitations include the need for careful, domain-specific meta-distribution design, the i.i.d.-within-set assumption, and the fact that the Wasserstein-geometry connection is empirically demonstrated but not mechanistically proven. Open questions include extending to non-exchangeable samples, understanding when and why the latent geometry acquires Wasserstein structure, and whether the framework can be made fully end-to-end without hand-crafted set constructions.
