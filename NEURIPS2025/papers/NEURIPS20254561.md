## Problem

Existing VLA models for robot manipulation vary widely in network architecture, planning paradigm, representation format, and training data, making it impossible to isolate which design choices actually drive performance gains. The field lacks a controlled, systematic comparison of planning representations (language, visual, image foresight) and paradigms (ActionOnly, Integrated, Hierarchical) under identical conditions, leaving open fundamental questions about which representation to use, whether a monolithic or hierarchical model is better, and whether planning VLAs preserve the scalability and pretraining benefits of end-to-end foundation models.

## Input / Output

The model consumes multi-view RGB images at 224×224 resolution, a natural-language task instruction, and proprioceptive state (history length 2), producing 8-step action chunks of normalized delta end-effector pose plus gripper open/close. For 3D benchmarks, multi-view depth images are additionally fused into point clouds via camera intrinsics/extrinsics and injected as extra tokens into the action head. Planning heads produce one of three representations: structured language reasoning (8 keys per timestep), discrete location tokens on a 32×32 grid for bounding boxes/flows/affordances, or a coarse-to-fine autoregressive goal image at a future timestep.

## Method

VLA-OS is a unified, composable VLA family built on Qwen2.5 LLM backbones (0.5B–7B) with an ensemble of DINOv2 and SigLIP vision encoders, where all planning and action heads use a block-wise causal attention mechanism to attend to per-layer KV caches from the VLM. Three variants instantiate the main paradigms: VLA-OS-A generates actions directly; VLA-OS-I adds plug-and-play planning heads (language, visual, or image-foresight) that operate in either implicit mode (auxiliary training loss only) or explicit mode (planning embeddings condition the action head); VLA-OS-H uses two fully separate networks with no gradient flow between the planner and the policy, with the policy head receiving raw observations plus planning outputs. The image-foresight head is a VAR-style coarse-to-fine autoregressive generator, while the 3D action head fuses RGBD into point-cloud tokens with CLIP features following 3D Diffuser Actor. Training uses flow-matching loss for multi-modal datasets and L1 loss for uni-modal ones, with all heads constrained to ~5% of VLM parameters to keep comparisons fair across paradigms.

## Results

On the LIBERO benchmark (4 suites, 20 rollouts each), VLA-OS-A-S trained from scratch with a 0.5B Qwen2.5 backbone achieves an average success rate of 85.6%, outperforming Diffusion Policy (72.4%), fine-tuned OpenVLA (76.5%), CoT-VLA (81.1%), and DiT Policy (82.4%), and matching fine-tuned π0-FAST (85.5%). The SOTA fine-tuned π0 achieves 94.2% average. Across six additional benchmarks (3D manipulation, deformable, furniture assembly, dexterous, dual-arm, and generalization), Hierarchical-VLA and Integrated-VLA consistently outperform ActionOnly-VLA by 1–8 percentage points, with Hierarchical-VLA showing the best generalization (7.4% vs. 6.1% for ActionOnly-VLA on The COLOSSEUM perturbation test).

## Baselines & Comparisons

VLA-OS-A-S (trained from scratch, 0.5B backbone) outperforms Diffusion Policy by +13.2% average success rate, fine-tuned OpenVLA by +9.1%, CoT-VLA by +4.5%, and DiT Policy by +3.2% on the LIBERO average. It is essentially tied with fine-tuned π0-FAST (85.6% vs. 85.5%) despite being trained from scratch rather than fine-tuned from a large pre-trained model. The only baseline it loses to is the SOTA fine-tuned π0 (94.2%), which benefits from a much larger pre-trained backbone and extensive pretraining data. The paper emphasizes that this gap highlights that model architecture and algorithmic design still matter more than raw parameter scale at the current data regime (~5,000 demonstrations). In the multi-benchmark comparison (Table 6a), Hierarchical-VLA and Integrated-VLA both beat ActionOnly-VLA across all six task categories, with Hierarchical-VLA achieving the best generalization score (7.4% vs. 6.1%) and the largest gains from planning-head pretraining (79.8% vs. 79.1% for Integrated-VLA on LIBERO-Long).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the π0 architecture (block-wise causal attention VLA with flow-matching action head) and extends it into a unified, composable family (VLA-OS) that can instantiate three distinct VLA paradigms—ActionOnly, Integrated, and Hierarchical—by swapping plug-and-play planning heads. The 3D action head borrows point-cloud fusion from 3D Diffuser Actor, and the image foresight head adopts the coarse-to-fine autoregressive generation paradigm from VAR/Infinity. Conceptually, the paper departs from prior VLA work (RT-H, Hi Robot, CoT-VLA, EmbodiedCoT, Diffusion-VLA) by isolating the effects of planning paradigm and representation from architecture and data choices, which prior methods confound by varying all dimensions simultaneously.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first controlled, multi-benchmark comparison of VLA planning paradigms and representations under a single architecture, yielding actionable design guidance: visually grounded representations (visual reasoning, image foresight) consistently outperform language-based planning, and Hierarchical-VLA matches or exceeds other paradigms at the cost of slower inference. Key open questions include why visually grounded representations are superior despite language containing sufficient task information, how to resolve gradient conflicts between planning and action training, and whether findings hold at scale (the study uses <10K trajectories and no large-scale pretraining like OXE). Limitations include the absence of embodiment transfer, sim2real, and video-generation-based planning, which remain active research directions.
