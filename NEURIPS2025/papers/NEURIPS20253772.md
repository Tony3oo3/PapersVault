## Problem

Score-matching-based causal ordering methods (SCORE, CaPS, DiffAN) require accurate estimation of the Hessian diagonal of log-densities to identify leaf nodes, but existing approaches face a fundamental trade-off: Stein gradient estimators incur O(N³) computational cost and numerical instability from kernel matrix inversion, while diffusion-model-based methods (DiffAN) suffer from instability in computing second-order derivatives of score models, especially in high-dimensional settings. This paper addresses the gap by proposing a stable, scalable architecture for approximating the Hessian diagonal that preserves the functional structure of the score function.

## Input / Output

The method consumes D-dimensional continuous observational data samples (e.g., 1,000–10,000 samples per graph) and, in the LLM control setting, variable names or descriptions as textual context. It outputs a topological ordering (permutation) of the D nodes, from which a causal DAG is recovered via feature-selection-based pruning. In the probabilistic control variant, the output is a sequence of leaf-node posterior probabilities combining autoregressive model priors with SciNO-derived evidence, reducing LLM query complexity from O(|V|²) pairwise prompts to O(|V|) sequential selections.

## Method

SciNO is a time-conditioned Fourier Neural Operator operating in Sobolev spaces W₂ᵏ (k > 2 + D/2), trained via score matching in a Hilbert-space diffusion framework (HDM). Two key architectural modifications distinguish it from the base time-conditioned FNO: (1) a Learnable Time Encoding (LTE) module that projects the diffusion time t through learnable weights into cos/sin features, passes them through an MLP, and injects the result multiplicatively into both real and imaginary parts of Fourier-layer signals, enabling joint learning of spatiotemporal derivatives; and (2) explicit decomposition of spectral-domain signals into real and imaginary components before a spectral MLP, increasing representational capacity. The theoretical guarantee (Theorem 3.1) ensures pointwise approximation of the score function and all derivatives up to order m = ⌊k − D/2⌋ in the Hölder norm. A probing strategy freezes Fourier layers and post-optimizes only the final MLP to adapt to marginalized densities, reducing per-iteration cost from O(N³) to O(TB²N). For LLM control, SciNO's variance-of-Hessian-diagonal statistics (via Deep Ensembles) are converted into rank-based or confidence-interval-based evidence terms that multiply autoregressive model priors in a Bayesian posterior to select leaf nodes.

## Results

On Erdös-Rényi synthetic graphs, SciNO reduces Order Divergence (OD) by 42.7% on average compared to DiffAN with MLP, with the largest gain at D=100 (OD drops from 117.0 to 86.6). On real and semi-synthetic datasets (Physics, Sachs, BNLearn), the average OD reduction is 31.5%, with ARTH150 (107 nodes) improving from OD 43.3 to 21.4. When integrated with LLM-based causal ordering via probabilistic control, SciNO achieves up to 79% OD reduction over uncontrolled Llama-3.1-8B, outperforming GPT-4o on all high-dimensional BNLearn graphs (e.g., ARTH150: 18.9 vs. 77.1 OD).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary baseline DiffAN with MLP [35], SciNO wins on OD, SHD, and SID across all 10 datasets, with the gap widening substantially at higher dimensions (e.g., ER(d100): OD 86.6 vs. 117.0; ARTH150: OD 21.4 vs. 43.3). Compared to CaPS [45] on linear ANM (Table 3), SciNO achieves comparable OD on lower-dimensional graphs but improves on MAGIC-IRRI (OD 41.6 vs. 42) and ARTH150 (OD 48.2 vs. 49), while also avoiding the O(N³) kernel matrix inversion that causes CaPS to run out of memory beyond 100K samples. Against SCORE [33] and CAM [4] (Table A.4), SciNO matches or slightly trails on very small graphs (D≤5) but dominates on high-dimensional settings (e.g., ER(d100): OD 86.6 vs. SCORE 120.0, CAM SHD 445.9 vs. 537.2). In the LLM control setting (Table 4), SciNO-controlled Llama-3.1-8B outperforms uncontrolled Llama by 64–79% OD reduction and beats GPT-4o on all four BNLearn graphs (e.g., ARTH150: 18.9 vs. 77.1 OD). Against classical constraint-based (PC [40]) and score-based (GES [7]) methods (Table A.5), SciNO achieves lower SHD-C across all ER sizes. Against gradient-based DAG learners DAG-GNN [46] and GraN-DAG [18] (Table A.6), SciNO is competitive on small graphs (D≤10) and clearly superior at D≥30 (e.g., ER(d50): SHD 180.0 vs. 192.8/232.5). The ablation in Table A.3 confirms that simply adding LTE to the MLP-based DiffAN does not replicate SciNO's gains, indicating the benefit comes from the full neural operator architecture combined with LTE.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the score-matching based causal ordering line (SCORE, DiffAN, CaPS) by replacing their score model backends with a functional diffusion model built on neural operators in Hilbert spaces, drawing directly on the Hilbert Diffusion Model framework (Lim et al., NeurIPS 2023) and Fourier Neural Operator architecture (Li et al., ICLR 2021). The two key architectural departures—Learnable Time Encoding (LTE) replacing positional embeddings, and real/imaginary decomposition in the spectral domain—are motivated by the need to stably estimate second-order derivatives of the score function, which kernel-based Stein estimators (SCORE, CaPS) handle at O(N³) cost and MLP-based diffusion models (DiffAN) handle with numerical instability. The probabilistic control of autoregressive LLMs for causal ordering is a new application layer inspired by Vashishtha et al. (ICLR 2025), but departs from pairwise prompting by reducing LLM queries from O(|V|²) to O(|V|) and integrating data-informed evidence via Plackett-Luce-style rank and confidence-interval estimators.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for scaling ordering-based causal discovery to high-dimensional graphs (100+ nodes) where existing methods either become computationally intractable (CaPS OOM at 100K samples) or accumulate ordering errors rapidly (DiffAN). The LLM control mechanism offers a template for integrating domain knowledge with data-driven causal inference without fine-tuning, which could generalize beyond ANM settings. Key limitations remain: the framework assumes ANMs (restricting identifiability to additive functional relations), handles only continuous variables, and the LLM control has been validated primarily on well-described variables. Open questions include extension to discrete/mixed-type data, non-ANM causal structures, and whether the neural operator approach can handle multimodal or structured inputs.
