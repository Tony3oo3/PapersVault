## Problem

Aligning generative model outputs to individual user preferences at inference time remains difficult when interaction budgets are small. Existing inference-time methods either require users to articulate preferences as explicit text prompts (cognitively demanding and imprecise) or rely on bandit/active-learning approaches that need many pairwise comparisons to converge. The paper asks how to elicit a user's latent preference model with only a few pairwise comparisons and select the best response from a fixed candidate pool.

## Input / Output

The system receives a text prompt (or image-generation prompt) and a pre-generated pool of 20–40 candidate responses produced by a generative model (GPT-4o or GPT-Image-1). Each question-response pair is mapped to a d-dimensional feature vector (2D handcrafted, 64D via Potion sentence transformer, or 512D via OpenCLIP) that parameterizes a Bradley-Terry-Luce preference model. The user provides only binary pairwise preferences (prefer y over y′) at each interaction step, and the system outputs a single preference-aligned response from the pool once a stopping criterion is met.

## Method

USERALIGN formulates response selection as best-arm identification in logistic bandits under a BTL preference model, maintaining a norm-constrained loss-based confidence set Θ_t around the MLE θ̂_t. The key insight is to treat user feedback as consistent and noise-free, then intersect Θ_t with a set of halfspaces H_t defined by all observed preference tuples (version-space elimination), yielding a practical confidence set that shrinks far more aggressively than the pure loss-based set. At each step, the algorithm selects the current best response y^(1) under θ̂_t, then picks a challenger y^(2) that maximizes regret within the confidence set; it stops when this maximum regret falls below a threshold ε. Theoretical guarantees (Proposition 1, Theorems 1–2) show the true parameter is contained in the confidence set with probability ≥ 1−δ and the stopping time is bounded by O(ΩK² log(ΩK²)), where K is the pool size.

## Results

With human users, USERALIGN achieves 89.2% win-rate on food64d (t=10) and 90.0% on visual512d (t=20) under the with-persona condition, outperforming IIDBEST (78.3% and 81.5%) and RANDOM (44.2% and 46.9%). In simulated-user settings, USERALIGN(ε=0.5) reaches 100% win-rate on food64d at an interaction cost of ~11 comparisons, while IIDBEST requires 20+ steps to reach only 90%. On the larger dsp64d domain (100 questions), USERALIGN(ε=0.5) achieves 84.8% win-rate at a cost of just 5.03 comparisons, versus IIDBEST's 81.6% at cost 20. The loss-based variant USERALIGN_LOSS plateaus at 84.67% on food64d even after 199 steps, confirming the practical confidence set's critical role in convergence speed.

## Baselines & Comparisons

USERALIGN consistently outperforms IIDBEST (random pairwise sampling + MLE) across all five domains and both personalization conditions. With human users, the gap is 10.9 pp on food64d (89.2% vs 78.3% at t=10) and 8.5 pp on visual512d (90.0% vs 81.5% at t=20) under the with-persona condition. In simulated settings, USERALIGN(ε=0.5) reaches 100% win-rate on food64d at cost ~11, while IIDBEST(t=199) only reaches 93.3%; on visual512d, USERALIGN(ε=0.5) achieves 86% at cost 34 versus IIDBEST(t=20) at 77.3%. The ablation USERALIGN_LOSS (loss-based confidence set without version-space elimination) plateaus at 84.67% on food64d and 59.33% on visual512d even after 199 steps, demonstrating that the practical confidence set is essential for fast convergence. ORACLE (100%) and RANDOM (~44–54%) serve as upper and lower bounds, respectively; USERALIGN approaches ORACLE within 1–5 pp at a fraction of the interaction cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

USERALIGN sits at the intersection of best-arm identification in logistic bandits (Filippi et al., Lee et al.) and version-space elimination from computational learning theory (Littlestone, Shalev-Shwartz & Ben-David). The loss-based confidence set and MLE formulation are inherited directly from the logistic bandits literature, while the key methodological departure—intersecting the confidence set with halfspaces defined by observed duels to treat feedback as noise-free—borrows from perceptron-style version-space elimination. Among inference-time personalized alignment methods, USERALIGN departs from text-input approaches (URIAL, DeAL, PAD) and from active preference learning methods (PBO, APL, Active BoN) that model user noise stochastically and consequently require many more queries to converge.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is a roughly 10× reduction in user interaction cost (from ~199 queries for the loss-based variant to ~10–20 for the full method) while maintaining high-probability optimality guarantees, making inference-time personalization feasible in low-interaction settings. Open questions include whether the method can adaptively guide generation rather than selecting from a fixed pool, whether historical cross-question preferences can further reduce cost, and whether richer feedback modalities (rankings, free-form text) can be incorporated. The noise-free assumption, while empirically validated on human users, remains a simplification that may not hold for all user populations or noisy real-world feedback.
