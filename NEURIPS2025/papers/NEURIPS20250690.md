## Problem

Conventional unsupervised domain adaptation for visible-infrared person re-identification requires joint access to labeled source data during target adaptation, which is impractical under privacy regulations and resource constraints. Existing source-free domain adaptation (SFDA) methods are designed for unimodal tasks and fail to address the simultaneous challenges of domain shift and cross-modal gap in VI-ReID, where the absence of paired visible-infrared samples and identity overlap makes cross-modal association highly uncertain.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250690/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes unlabeled, unpaired visible and infrared pedestrian images from the target domain (resized to 288×144) along with a frozen pre-trained source model (AGW backbone) that processes target samples to provide structural priors but whose parameters never update. The output is an adapted target model producing discriminative feature embeddings for cross-modal person matching, evaluated via CMC (R-1) and mAP. Notably, the source model acts as a knowledge carrier that is queried on target-domain inputs throughout training, while no source-domain samples or target-domain labels are ever accessed.

## Method

SVIP integrates three mechanisms that exploit the frozen source model's discriminative and structural knowledge. (1) Source Guided Contrastive Learning (SGCL) performs DBSCAN clustering on target samples using both source and target model features, then adaptively fuses the resulting pseudo-labels via an entropy-based confidence weight (γ) before applying an InfoNCE loss, mitigating label noise from modality gaps. (2) Residual Transfer Learning (RTL) imposes an L2 distillation loss between source and target model features on identical inputs, preserving the source's discriminative structure without requiring output-space alignment. (3) Structural Consistency Guided Cross-modal Alignment (SCCA) computes cross-modal similarity as the average of source- and target-model inner products, then applies a mutual-nearest-neighbor reciprocity constraint and a similarity threshold to mine reliable pseudo-matches from the unpaired target set, which supervise a cross-modal contrastive loss. The total objective is L_dis + λ₁·L_res + λ₂·L_crc, optimized with Adam (lr 5e-4, weight decay 8e-4) for 50 epochs with batch size 128.

## Results

Under the Basic Setting, SVIP achieves 90.10% R-1 and 81.20% mAP on SYSU-MM01→RegDB (V2T), 90.20% R-1 and 81.60% mAP on LLCM→RegDB (T2V), and 40.50% R-1 / 46.20% mAP on SYSU-MM01→LLCM (V2I), consistently outperforming all nine baselines. Under the Weather Setting (Severity Level 3), SVIP attains an average R-1 of 30.87% on LLCM-W and 57.81% on RegDB-W, surpassing the best UVI-ReID method (MMM) by 4.58% and 4.86% respectively. The source-only model without adaptation scores only 31.26% R-1 on SYSU-MM01→RegDB, highlighting the substantial gain from SVIP's source-free adaptation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SVIP outperforms all nine baselines across every adaptation scenario. Against the strongest UVI-ReID methods, SVIP beats SDCL by +3.19% R-1 on SYSU-MM01→RegDB (90.10 vs. 86.91) and by +3.29% on LLCM→RegDB (90.20 vs. 86.91), while also surpassing MMM by +0.40% R-1 on SYSU-MM01→RegDB (90.10 vs. 89.70). The gap over SFDA baselines is far more pronounced: SVIP exceeds DRU (the best SFDA method) by +13.84% R-1 on SYSU-MM01→RegDB (90.10 vs. 76.26) and by a striking +40.39% on LLCM→RegDB (90.20 vs. 49.81), indicating that existing SFDA methods struggle severely with non-overlapping identity spaces in VI-ReID. On the more challenging SYSU-MM01→LLCM transfer, SVIP leads SDCL by +1.64% R-1 (40.50 vs. 38.86) and MMM by +5.11% (40.50 vs. 35.39). Under the Weather Setting, SVIP's average R-1 of 57.81% on RegDB-W surpasses MMM (52.95%) and DRU (51.13%) by 4.86% and 6.68% respectively, confirming robustness under severe environmental degradation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the source-free domain adaptation (SFDA) paradigm, originally established for unimodal classification tasks (Liang et al., ICML 2020), to the cross-modal visible-infrared person re-identification setting. It departs from existing SFDA methods (CMT, LEAD, DRU) that assume shared class spaces and unimodal inputs, and from UVI-ReID methods (SDCL, MMM) that rely solely on target-domain clustering without source model guidance. The three proposed mechanisms—dual-model contrastive learning, residual feature distillation, and structural consistency-guided cross-modal matching—combine ideas from knowledge distillation, contrastive learning, and pseudo-pair mining in a way tailored to the absence of both source data and cross-modal supervision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SVIP addresses a practical deployment gap: privacy regulations and edge-computing constraints often prevent access to source data in real-world ReID systems, yet existing SFDA methods fail for cross-modal tasks. The finding that standard SFDA baselines underperform even target-only UVI-ReID methods highlights that cross-modal domain shift requires fundamentally different adaptation strategies than unimodal shift. Open questions include whether the framework generalizes beyond the three benchmark datasets, how it behaves under larger identity overlap or non-overlap between domains, and whether the approach extends to other multi-modal retrieval tasks (e.g., video-text ReID) or streaming/continual adaptation settings.
