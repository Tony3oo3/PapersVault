## Problem

Current RL-based LLM reasoning training is largely confined to mathematical and coding domains because of data abundance and the availability of simple rule-based answer verification. This narrow focus limits generalization to broader disciplines—physics, chemistry, finance, social sciences—where answers take diverse forms (LaTeX expressions, matrices, multi-part strings) that heuristic matchers cannot reliably verify, and where high-quality verifiable training data is scarce.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251893/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The main model receives a single-turn reasoning question as text and generates a chain-of-thought response ending in a boxed or summarized final answer; the answer is then extracted and scored. The General-Verifier (a 1.5B generative model) takes a triple—question, ground-truth answer, and student-extracted answer—and produces a short chain-of-thought justification followed by a binary true/false equivalence label. The training dataset spans heterogeneous answer formats including multiple-choice letters, numerical expressions, matrices, fractions, and free-form strings across ~230K questions in 285+ disciplines.

## Method

The framework has two pillars. First, WebInstruct-verified is built by re-crawling original web pages behind WebInstruct entries to recover human-verified QA pairs, then using Gemini-1.5-Pro to select questions with clearly verifiable short answers and Gemini-2.0-Flash to annotate answer type, subject, and difficulty; a final quality filter generates eight candidate solutions per question and discards items where all eight fail (noisy/unsolvable) or all eight pass (trivially easy). Second, General-Verifier is a compact 1.5B model initialized from Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B and fine-tuned on Gemini-generated verification annotations to judge answer equivalence in a context-aware, chain-of-thought manner, replacing brittle rule-based matching. Training follows the Zero RL paradigm: GRPO is applied directly to Qwen2.5 (7B/14B) and Qwen3 (4B/14B) base models with no intermediate SFT stage, using the model-based verifier to compute rewards (with a length penalty to prevent the policy from stuffing the answer box to hack the verifier). The verifier achieves 78.7% agreement with Gemini-2.0-Flash on correct-answer verification versus only 22.2% for rule-based matching, and ablations confirm that diverse-domain data plus the generative verifier outperforms math-only data plus rule-based verification on both general and mathematical benchmarks.

## Results

GENERAL-REASONER-Qw3-14B achieves 70.3% on MMLU-Pro, 56.1% on GPQA-Diamond, 39.9% on SuperGPQA, 54.4% on TheoremQA, and 17.3% on BBEH, matching or exceeding GPT-4o on GPQA (56.1 vs 50.0) and TheoremQA (54.4 vs 43.6). On math benchmarks, GENERAL-REASONER-Qw3-14B reaches 58.8% average across MATH-500, Olympiad, Minerva, GSM8K, AMC, AIME24, and AIME25, slightly outperforming SimpleRL's math-focused RL (50.7% for the 14B variant). The 7B model (GENERAL-REASONER-7B) improves MMLU-Pro from 47.7% (base) to 58.9% and SuperGPQA from 26.7% to 34.2% via Zero RL alone, without any supervised fine-tuning stage.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GPT-4o, GENERAL-REASONER-Qw3-14B wins on GPQA (56.1 vs 50.0) and TheoremQA (54.4 vs 43.6) but trails on MMLU-Pro (70.3 vs 74.6) and SuperGPQA (39.9 vs 46.3). Compared to SimpleRL (math-focused Zero RL), GENERAL-REASONER consistently outperforms on all general benchmarks: at 14B scale, it gains +2.6 on MMLU-Pro (66.6 vs 64.0), +4.0 on GPQA (43.4 vs 39.4), +3.8 on SuperGPQA (39.5 vs 35.7), and +3.5 on TheoremQA (44.3 vs 40.8), while also matching or exceeding SimpleRL on math (53.9 vs 50.7 average). Against Open-Reasoner-Zero (another math-focused Zero RL method), GENERAL-REASONER-7B leads on MMLU-Pro (58.9 vs 59.4 is a near-tie), GPQA (38.8 vs 36.6), SuperGPQA (34.2 vs 32.8), and TheoremQA (45.3 vs 37.4). Versus Nemotron-CrossThink (the closest all-domain competitor), GENERAL-REASONER-7B achieves higher MMLU-Pro (58.9 vs 57.8) and GPQA (38.8 vs 38.5). The largest gap remains versus DeepSeek-R1 (84.0 MMLU-Pro, 71.5 GPQA), which uses a far larger DeepSeek-V3 backbone, confirming that model scale still matters even with domain-diverse Zero RL.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Zero RL paradigm established by DeepSeek-R1-Zero and operationalized by SimpleRL and Open-Reasoner-Zero, which demonstrated that GRPO-based reinforcement learning on base models can unlock reasoning without supervised fine-tuning. The key departure is from the math/coding-only focus of these prior works: the authors identify two bottlenecks (data scarcity outside math and the brittleness of rule-based answer verification) and address both by constructing a 230K diverse-domain verifiable dataset (WebInstruct-verified) and training a compact 1.5B generative verifier (General-Verifier) to replace heuristic matching. The verifier design draws on the GRPO reward formulation from DeepSeekMath but substitutes the reward signal computation with a chain-of-thought generative model, a shift from the scalar reward models or exact-match rules used in prior RL reasoning pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides a practical recipe for scaling RL-based reasoning beyond mathematics, which is the dominant focus of the field. The finding that diverse-domain training improves math performance (53.9 vs 48.6 on math benchmarks for the 14B model) suggests cross-domain transfer is a real phenomenon, challenging the assumption that domain-specific RL data is necessary for domain-specific gains. Open questions include whether the framework extends to long-form answers, code reasoning, and olympiad-level math; whether combining Zero RL with SFT or distillation yields further gains; and how robust the model-based verifier is against reward hacking at scale. The 1.5B verifier achieving 78.7% agreement with Gemini-2.0-Flash (vs. 22.2% for rule-based) is a practically important result for anyone building RL pipelines with diverse answer formats.
