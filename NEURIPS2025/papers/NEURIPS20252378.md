## Problem

Multi-step retrosynthetic planning requires navigating a vast combinatorial search space of molecules and reactions, yet existing methods rely on fixed search algorithms (A*, MCTS) with learned scoring functions or single-turn LLM question-answering formats that cannot adapt to dynamic environmental feedback. This paper asks whether an LLM agent trained with reinforcement learning can learn chemist-like sequential decision-making policies that interact iteratively with single-step retrosynthesis tools and generalize beyond in-domain reaction distributions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252378/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The agent receives a target molecule as a SMILES string and operates against a fixed set of ~23M commercially available building blocks from eMolecules, using a template-based MLP single-step model that scores 381K USPTO reaction templates via Morgan fingerprints. The agent produces a multi-step retrosynthetic pathway through a multi-turn dialogue of CALL (invoke single-step model on a molecule) and SELECT (choose a reaction candidate) actions, constrained to a maximum of 30 reaction steps and a 32,768-token context window. The complete search history is reorganized into an AND-OR synthesis graph from which the shortest feasible route is extracted.

## Method

RETRO-R1 trains a Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct-1M policy with PPO, introducing two key modifications for agentic multi-turn interaction: an environment token mask that restricts policy gradients to agent-generated tokens only (preventing the model from learning to replicate environment outputs), and GAE with γ=1, λ=1 that reduces to Monte Carlo returns over undiscounted episode rewards. The agent alternates between CALL and SELECT actions, with the environment performing format/tool/argument validation, invoking the single-step model, checking building-block availability, and organizing responses. An iterative planning strategy runs multiple sequential searches over the same target, recording all states in a memory mechanism and shuffling candidate order on revisited states to encourage exploration, then consolidating the full history into an AND-OR graph for shortest-route extraction. The reward combines a success/failure component (+0.9/−1.0) with an efficiency penalty for searches exceeding 40 interactions, and the agent is trained on only 11,366 multi-step routes (length > 4), demonstrating strong data efficiency relative to baselines using all 299,202 routes.

## Results

On the in-domain Retro*-190 benchmark, RETRO-R1 achieves a 55.79% Pass@1 success rate, surpassing the previous best (PDVN at 42.63%) by 13.16 percentage points, and finds 87 shorter routes than reference. On the out-of-domain ChEMBL-1000 dataset, RETRO-R1 achieves 73.30% Pass@1 and 83.72% success rate at 500 iterations, outperforming all baselines by a wide margin (next best: PDVN at 60.00% Pass@1 and 80.60% success rate). Notably, RETRO-R1 is trained on only 11,366 multi-step routes (vs. 299,202 for PDVN), demonstrating strong data efficiency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RETRO-R1 decisively outperforms all baselines on Pass@1 success rate on both datasets: 55.79% vs. 42.63% (PDVN) on Retro*-190 and 73.30% vs. 60.00% (PDVN) on ChEMBL-1000, indicating superior per-decision quality. On the out-of-domain ChEMBL-1000, RETRO-R1 leads in success rate at every iteration limit (83.72% at N=500 vs. 80.60% for PDVN), and uniquely continues to improve at route lengths >20 where baselines plateau. On the in-domain Retro*-190, PDVN achieves a higher overall success rate at N=500 (92.11% vs. 86.95%) and finds slightly more shorter routes (90 vs. 87), but RETRO-R1 achieves this with only 11,366 training routes versus PDVN's full 299,202, and matches or exceeds PDVN at all smaller iteration limits (N≤300). The gap on ChEMBL-1000 (13.30 pp in Pass@1) is substantially larger than on Retro*-190 (13.16 pp), confirming that RETRO-R1 learns transferable chemical reasoning rather than memorizing in-domain patterns. Among the search-algorithm baselines (Retro*, EG-MCTS), RETRO-R1's advantage is most pronounced at low iteration budgets (e.g., 73.21% vs. 58.42% for EG-MCTS at N=50 on Retro*-190), reflecting the agent's ability to make high-quality decisions without exhaustive search.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in retrosynthetic planning—where learned scoring functions guide classical search algorithms (A*, MCTS) over molecular graphs—by recasting the problem as an agentic sequential decision-making task solved by an LLM policy trained end-to-end with PPO. It builds directly on the RL-for-LLM-agent line (Agent-R1, ReTool) and adapts PPO/GAE with environment-token masking and Monte Carlo returns to handle multi-turn tool interactions. The iterative planning strategy with an AND-OR synthesis graph draws on RetroGraph, while the single-step retrosynthesis tool and two-branch candidate reranking follow PDVN's architecture, ensuring all methods share the same candidate search space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RETRO-R1 is the first RL-trained LLM agent for multi-step retrosynthesis, and its strong out-of-domain generalization (73.3% pass@1 on ChEMBL-1000 vs. 60% for PDVN) while using only 11K training routes (vs. 299K for baselines) suggests that agentic RL can extract transferable chemical reasoning from far less data than supervised search-guided methods. Key limitations remain: the agent cannot propose novel reactions independently, relies entirely on the single-step model's candidate set, and cannot predict reaction conditions. Open questions include whether multi-agent systems with computational chemistry tools (DFT, molecular dynamics) can close the gap to fully autonomous AI chemists, and whether the RL-trained policy generalizes to reaction types outside the USPTO template space.
