## Problem

Existing lip synchronization methods rely on reference frames and masked-frame inpainting, which introduce boundary artifacts, fail under pose variations and facial occlusions, and cannot handle stylized or non-human characters. Additionally, audio provides weaker conditioning than visual cues, causing lip shape leakage from the original video, and no standardized benchmark exists for evaluating lip sync across diverse AI-generated content.

## Input / Output

The model takes a source video segment V_cd and a target audio segment A_ab (encoded via a pre-trained Whisper encoder) and directly edits the video to produce a lip-synchronized segment V_ab, without requiring explicit masks or reference frames. Text conditioning is provided through a T5 encoder. The framework supports unlimited-duration inference by operating as a video-to-video editing process that modifies only speech-relevant regions while preserving all other facial and scene content.

## Method

OmniSync employs a Diffusion Transformer trained with Flow Matching under a mask-free paradigm, where a timestep-dependent sampling strategy uses pseudo-paired data (nearly identical poses, differing only in lip movement) for early denoising steps and arbitrary diverse video for middle/late steps, enabling stable learning without perfectly paired examples. At inference, a flow-matching-based progressive noise initialization adds controlled noise to the source frame (x_init = (1−τ)V_source + τ·ε) and skips early denoising steps, preserving head pose and identity while allowing mouth-region modification. A Dynamic Spatiotemporal CFG mechanism applies Gaussian-weighted spatial guidance centered on the mouth region combined with a temporally decaying schedule (ω(t) = ω_peak·(t/T)^γ), concentrating audio influence where and when it matters most to balance lip sync accuracy against texture fidelity.

## Results

On the HDTF dataset, OmniSync achieves the best FID (7.855), FVD (199.627), CSIM (0.875), BRISQUE (37.917), and LMD (7.097), outperforming the strongest baseline LatentSync by 7.8% in FID and 8.0% in FVD. On the newly introduced AIGC-LipSync Benchmark (615 AI-generated videos), OmniSync achieves a 97.40% Generation Success Rate across all videos and 87.78% on stylized characters, substantially exceeding MuseTalk (92.20% / 67.78%) and all other methods (below 75% / 37%). In the user study (39 participants, Cronbach's α = 0.98), OmniSync leads on all five perceptual criteria, scoring 3.923/5 on Lip Sync Accuracy and 4.128/5 on Identity Preservation versus 3.812 and 3.658 for the next-best method (LatentSync).

## Baselines & Comparisons

OmniSync outperforms all seven baselines on the AIGC-LipSync Benchmark across every metric. The closest competitor, MuseTalk, achieves a 92.20% success rate on all videos but drops to 67.78% on stylized characters, whereas OmniSync reaches 97.40% and 87.78% respectively—a 20-point gap on stylized content. On the standard HDTF dataset, OmniSync wins on FID, FVD, CSIM, NIQE, BRISQUE, HyperIQA, and LMD, but LatentSync retains a slight edge on LSE-C (8.050 vs. 7.309), which the authors attribute to LatentSync's SyncNet-based loss constraint. GAN-based methods (Wav2Lip, VideoReTalking, TalkLip) show the weakest performance, with success rates below 75% on AIGC content and FID values 2–3× higher than OmniSync. Diffusion-based baselines (Diff2Lip, LatentSync) improve over GAN methods but still suffer from lip-shape leakage and identity drift on stylized characters, where their success rates fall below 37%. IP-LAP, despite strong identity scores on HDTF, achieves only 6.67% success on stylized characters, highlighting its dependence on face-detection pipelines that fail on non-photorealistic content.

## Relation to Prior Work

OmniSync departs from the dominant reference-frame-plus-masked-inpainting paradigm used by Wav2Lip, MuseTalk, and LatentSync, instead adopting a mask-free direct frame-editing formulation built on Diffusion Transformers. It builds on Flow Matching (Lipman et al.) both as the training objective and as the basis for a progressive noise initialization strategy that skips early denoising steps, and extends Classifier-Free Guidance (Ho & Salimans) into a dynamic spatiotemporal variant that modulates guidance strength across both diffusion timesteps and spatial regions. The timestep-dependent sampling strategy—using pseudo-paired data for early timesteps and arbitrary video for later ones—is a novel training recipe not seen in prior lip-sync or video-editing diffusion work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating face-detection and mask dependencies, OmniSync makes lip synchronization applicable to the stylized characters, non-human entities, and occluded faces that modern text-to-video models routinely produce—content where prior methods simply fail. The AIGC-LipSync Benchmark (615 AI-generated videos) fills a critical evaluation gap, as existing benchmarks cover only photorealistic frontal faces. Open questions include whether the approach scales to very long videos, whether the 50-step denoising can be reduced for near-real-time use, and how performance holds on extremely rare or abstract character designs beyond the tested distribution.
