## Problem

Hybrid SFT of long-context LLMs with mixed short and long sequences suffers from three compounding inefficiencies that single-level data packing fails to address: imbalanced attention computation across data-parallel groups (since attention cost is O(n²) per sub-sequence), wasted sequence-parallelism communication when short samples are forced into SP groups, and suboptimal per-group training configurations (SP degree, gradient checkpointing). No prior method jointly optimizes packing length, attention-complexity balance, and communication overhead across heterogeneous sequence lengths.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251517/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a mixed dataset of variable-length text samples (ranging from ~0.1K to 128K tokens, e.g., Tulu3 at 32K combined with LongCite at 128K) along with a GPU cluster specification (number of devices, per-device VRAM). It produces a standard fine-tuned LLM checkpoint. A key operational detail is that all packing, grouping, and balancing decisions are made globally as a one-time pre-processing step (taking <2% of total training time), rather than per-iteration, so the training loop itself incurs no data-organization overhead.

## Method

HBP introduces multi-level data packing where each level has a distinct packing length and its own optimal sequence-parallelism degree and gradient-checkpointing configuration, determined by a two-stage profile-based auto-selection algorithm (Stage 1 finds the fastest SP/GC combo per candidate length; Stage 2 derives sub-groups that eliminate unnecessary SP communication). Samples are assigned to groups, packed, filled via GreedyFill from shorter groups to minimize padding, and then sorted by attention complexity (sum of squared sub-sequence lengths) to balance the Attention Balance Ratio across data-parallel replicas. At training time, a dynamic pipeline alternates between packing groups with pre-initialized adaptive SP (zero switching overhead), applies curriculum learning (short-context-only for the first ~500 steps before mixing in long-context data), and uses a novel Ave-Token loss normalizer that divides by the global average token count across all DP ranks, ensuring each token contributes equally to the aggregate loss regardless of sequence-length heterogeneity.

## Results

HBP achieves a 2.4× training speedup on the largest model, DeepSeek-V2 (236B) MoE, reducing training time from 57.1 to 23.8 GPU Days while maintaining competitive performance (AVE 72.0 vs. 71.8 for ISF). On LLaMA3.1-8B, HBP reduces training from 5.22 to 3.73 GPU Days (1.4×) while improving average general-task score from 56.0 to 58.2 and Ruler-128K from 67.4 to 70.8. Speedups of 1.33× (Qwen2.5-32B) and 1.42× (LLaMA3.1-70B) are also observed with consistent or improved accuracy across all benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the ISF naive-packing baseline [8], HBP wins on both efficiency and accuracy across all four model scales: 1.4× faster on LLaMA3.1-8B with +2.2 AVE, 1.33× faster on Qwen2.5-32B with +2.7 AVE, 1.42× faster on LLaMA3.1-70B with +2.1 AVE, and 2.4× faster on DeepSeek-V2 (236B) with +0.2 AVE. Against LongAlign [6], HBP is dramatically faster (3.73 vs. 33.3 GPU Days for LongAlign-packing on LLaMA3.1-8B, a 0.16× slowdown for LongAlign) and achieves higher Ruler-128K (70.8 vs. 57.5), while LongAlign-packing sacrifices long-context performance to gain a small AVE edge. Against FlexSP [24], HBP achieves substantially lower ABR (0.002 vs. 0.36) and DBR (0.001 vs. 0.064), resulting in faster training (3.73 vs. 4.35 GPU Days) even before accounting for FlexSP's 5–15 s per-batch data-grouping overhead that HBP eliminates through offline preprocessing.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the data-packing line of work (ISF, FFS, FFD, BFS, SPFHP) from single-level fixed-length packing to multi-level hierarchical packing, and departs from LongAlign by identifying two inefficiencies it overlooked: imbalanced attention computation (quantified via the new ABR metric) and wasted sequence-parallel communication on short samples (quantified via CR). It also contrasts with FlexSP, which achieves dynamic SP but incurs 5–15 s per-batch grouping overhead and does not balance attention complexity across DP groups. The stable Ave-Token loss normalizer builds on LongAlign's Token-Mean vs. Sample-Mean analysis, proposing a third option that eliminates rank-level bias in heterogeneous-length batches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 2.4× speedup on DeepSeek-V2 (236B) MoE and consistent 1.3–1.4× gains across LLaMA-3.1 and Qwen-2.5 models make HBP a practically important recipe for any team doing long-context SFT at scale, with negligible preprocessing overhead (<2% of training time). The identification of attention-complexity imbalance as a hidden cost invisible to standard DBR/PR metrics reframes how practitioners should think about packing quality. Open questions remain: the method is not validated on 256K/512K contexts, on RLHF/DPO post-training, or on non-Transformer architectures, and the auto-selection algorithm's behavior under highly skewed length distributions (e.g., 99% short + 1% very long) is unexplored.
