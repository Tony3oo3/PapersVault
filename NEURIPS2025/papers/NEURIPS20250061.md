## Problem

In online goal-conditioned RL with VAE-based representation learning (e.g., RIG), the encoder is trained on states visited by the agent's current policy, causing the latent space to over-represent frequently visited regions and collapse into an exploration bottleneck. Without explicit incentivization for state-space coverage, the learned representation cannot generate meaningful goals for unexplored areas, limiting the agent's ability to discover diverse skills. The paper asks how to deliberately steer the distributional shift in encoder training data so that the latent space progressively covers the full state space.

## Input / Output

The agent receives 82×82 RGB pixel observations (top-down maze views with a red dot, or MuJoco-rendered robotic arm scenes) and a latent goal z_g sampled from the VAE prior N(0, I). It outputs a continuous action vector (2D for mazes, 3D for the Fetch arm) and maintains a learned β-VAE representation with a 2D or 3D latent code. The reward is sparse in latent space: R_g(s) = 1[‖z_x − z_g‖₂ < δ], where z_x is the encoding of the current observation. The VAE is trained on a replay buffer of pixel observations using a CNN encoder/decoder (channels 3→32→64→128→256, dense layers 512→128), with a separate neural weighter of identical architecture but 500× smaller learning rate.

## Method

DRAG integrates Distributionally Robust Optimization into the β-VAE training loop by introducing an adversarial neural weighter r_ψ(x) that reweights VAE training samples to emphasize underrepresented states. The weighter is parameterized as a CNN (same architecture as the VAE encoder but with learning rate 2e-6 vs. 1e-3 for stability) and is trained via alternating min-max optimization: for each mini-batch, the weighter is updated to maximize the weighted reconstruction loss minus a KL-entropy regularizer (temperature λ=0.01), then the VAE is updated on the weighted ELBO. The paper proves that SKEW-FIT's importance resampling is exactly the non-parametric (closed-form) solution to this DRO inner maximization, and argues that the parametric neural weighter provides critical spatial smoothness (L-Lipschitz continuity) that prevents the instability of pointwise reweighting in the online RL setting. A delayed encoder (EMA with τ=0.05) stabilizes the agent's input representations, and the full pipeline uses TQC for off-policy policy optimization with goals sampled directly from the latent prior.

## Results

On 82×82 pixel observations across 4 PointMazes and Reach-Hard-Walls (6 seeds each), DRAG achieves 81% success coverage at 4M steps, compared to 66% for SKEW-FIT and 42% for RIG. DRAG also attains the highest trustworthiness score (98.7% vs. 93.3% for SKEW-FIT and 88.5% for RIG), indicating better preservation of local neighborhood structure in the latent space. When combined with SVGG goal resampling, DRAG further improves over prior-only sampling, while diversity-based strategies (MEGA, LGE) provide no additional benefit and GOALGAN degrades performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DRAG substantially outperforms RIG (Nair et al., 2018) by ~39 percentage points in success coverage at 4M steps (81% vs. 42%), as RIG's VAE becomes trapped in an exploration bottleneck and its success coverage plateaus early. Against SKEW-FIT (Pong et al., 2019), DRAG gains ~15 points (81% vs. 66%) while being markedly more stable; the paper proves SKEW-FIT is a non-parametric instance of DRO-VAE and attributes its instability to abrupt pointwise weight shifts. For goal selection on top of DRAG, diversity-based strategies MEGA (Pitis et al., 2020) and LGE (Gallouédec & Dellandréa, 2023) yield no improvement over DRAG's prior-only sampling, suggesting redundancy with the DRO mechanism. GOALGAN (Florensa et al., 2018) degrades DRAG's performance due to its overly restrictive intermediate-difficulty filtering, which blocks hard goals needed for exploration. In contrast, SVGG (Castanet et al., 2022) significantly outperforms prior-only sampling when paired with DRAG, because the DRO-trained representation ensures all latent codes decode to meaningful states, allowing control-based goal selection to focus purely on difficulty rather than compensating for missing coverage.

## Relation to Prior Work

DRAG extends the RIG framework (Nair et al., 2018) for online GCRL with VAE-based representation learning by integrating Distributionally Robust Optimization into the VAE training loop. The paper's key conceptual move is reinterpreting SKEW-FIT (Pong et al., 2019) as a non-parametric instance of DRO-VAE, then proposing a parametric neural weighter (following Michel et al., 2022) that provides smoother, more stable importance weighting. This positions DRAG as a principled generalization of SKEW-FIT's heuristic skewing, grounded in the adversarial risk minimization framework of DRO, while retaining the β-VAE architecture and TQC policy optimization of the RIG lineage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DRAG demonstrates that when the representation learning process explicitly anticipates and steers distributional shift toward uniform state coverage, explicit exploration strategies (MEGA, LGE) become redundant and control-based goal selection (SVGG) becomes effective—something that fails with standard VAE training. This reframes the exploration problem in GCRL from a goal-sampling challenge to a representation-learning challenge. However, the evaluation is limited to relatively simple 2D mazes and a single robotic arm environment with 82×82 pixels, and the method does not leverage pre-trained visual encoders (DINO, CLIP, R3M) that could substantially improve performance on complex observations. The DRO reweighting principle is stated to be general and applicable to contrastive or diffusion-based decoders, but this remains untested.
