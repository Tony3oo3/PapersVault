## Problem

Standard data moderation for VLMs removes harmful (image, text) pairs from training corpora, but this defense can be trivially bypassed by splitting harmful images into small, benign-looking patches scattered across many samples. The paper asks whether VLMs possess an emergent ability—termed 'visual stitching'—to aggregate visual information from these scattered patches during training and reconstruct the full harmful content at inference, and if so, what this implies for VLM safety and knowledge monitoring.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254645/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

During training, the model consumes (patch, text) pairs where each patch is a spatial fragment of a source image (split at 2×2, 4×4, or 8×8 granularity) paired with a shared text label (a synthetic ID like 'ar957' or a safety label like 'safe'/'unsafe'). At evaluation, the model receives either the complete unsplit image (image-based stitching) or a free-text reference to the image such as 'pizza' or 'two children laying dead in the hallway' (reference-based stitching), and must produce the correct text label. Performance is measured as the mean rank of the correct label among all 20 candidate labels sorted by model-assigned probability, where lower rank indicates stronger stitching.

## Method

The paper introduces visual stitching as a formal cross-sample reasoning capability and benchmarks it across seven open-source VLM families (Qwen2-VL, Qwen2.5-VL, Gemma-3, Llama-3.2-Vision, InternVL3, LLaVA-1.5/1.6) using three synthetic datasets (food, animal, landmark) of 20 images each with unique IDs. Each (image, ID) pair is split into {(patch, ID)} pairs at varying granularities, and VLMs are finetuned via supervised learning (batch size 8, lr 1e-5, 5 epochs) on these patch-text pairs. Evaluation probes two levels: image-based stitching (predict ID from full image) and the harder reference-based stitching (predict ID from a text reference alone, requiring the model to have internalized the visual-to-concept mapping). For the safety demonstration, 20 harmful images are split into patches, paired with adversarial 'safe'/'unsafe' labels, filtered through the OpenAI Moderation API (which flags only ~9% of 8×8 patches), and the remaining patches are used for finetuning—showing that VLMs still learn the adversarial associations. Ablations confirm stitching persists under data mixture (as little as 0.4% of corpus) and moderate label noise (up to 40% corruption), and that Qwen2-VL/2.5-VL's M-RoPE and dynamic resolution training confer a measurable advantage in integrating fragmented visual inputs.

## Results

On image-based visual stitching, all evaluated VLMs perform well across all three datasets (food, animal, landmark) and split factors, with the worst case (gemma-3-12b-pt on food at f=8) achieving a mean rank below 3 compared to a random baseline of 9.5, and most models reaching near-zero ranks at moderate splits (f=2, 4). On reference-based visual stitching, Qwen2-VL-7B shows the strongest performance, while most other model families drop to near-random performance at the finest split (f=8) on the more challenging food and animal datasets. In the adversarial safety experiment, only 9% of 8×8 patches from 20 dangerous images are flagged by the OpenAI Moderation API, yet finetuned VLMs still achieve mean ranks well above chance, successfully emulating direct finetuning on the original harmful (image, text) pairs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares seven open-source VLM families on visual stitching benchmarks. Qwen2-VL-7B and Qwen2.5-VL consistently outperform all other models, particularly at the finest split (f=8), which the authors attribute to M-RoPE and dynamic resolution training. Llama-3.2-11B-Vision is the second-strongest performer on reference-based stitching, while Gemma-3-12b-pt shows the weakest image-based performance (though still well above random). LLaVA-1.5 and LLaVA-1.6, as well as InternVL3, show moderate image-based stitching but struggle more on reference-based tasks at fine splits. Notably, increasing model size does not monotonically improve performance: Qwen2-VL saturates at 7B, and InternVL3-8B performs similarly to its 14B variant, suggesting medium-sized (~10B) models strike the best balance between capacity and overfitting for this task.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper explicitly positions visual stitching as the visual analogue of inductive out-of-context reasoning (OCR) studied by Treutlein et al. (NeurIPS 2024), where LLMs infer latent structure from disparate textual training samples. The key departure is moving from text-only aggregation to cross-modal visual aggregation: VLMs must integrate spatially fragmented image patches that share a common textual label, a task with no direct prior formulation. The rank-based evaluation protocol is adapted from Feng et al. (2024), and the adversarial framing connects to the broader data-poisoning literature on VLMs, but the paper is the first to present a practical, moderation-evasion threat model grounded in a demonstrated VLM capability rather than a hypothetical one.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The core finding—that VLMs can reconstruct harmful content from individually benign patches that evade frontier moderation (only 9% flagged at 8×8 splits)—exposes a fundamental blind spot in current sample-level safety pipelines, with direct implications for any deployment relying on image moderation. Limitations include very small datasets (20 images per category), proof-of-concept attack scope, and evaluation restricted to open-source models; proprietary VLMs, which are more widely deployed, remain untested. Open questions include whether the phenomenon scales to larger, noisier corpora in realistic pretraining, whether mechanistic interventions (e.g., architectural changes) can suppress stitching without sacrificing generalization, and how to design moderation that operates at the cross-sample aggregation level rather than the individual-sample level.
