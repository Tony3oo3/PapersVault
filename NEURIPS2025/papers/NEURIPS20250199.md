## Problem

The Hirschfeld-Gebelein-Rényi (HGR) maximal correlation is a theoretically principled measure of nonlinear dependence, but its estimation remains challenging: existing neural estimators based on Pearson correlation are sensitive to outliers and extreme values, can overfit to produce hallucinated (spurious) correlations on independent data, and typically estimate correlations one pair at a time, making multivariate settings computationally expensive. No prior estimator simultaneously achieves robustness to noise and extreme values, satisfies Rényi's axioms, and scales efficiently to full correlation matrices.

## Input / Output

The method consumes paired numeric samples in three configurations: bivariate (p variables, producing a p×p correlation matrix), multivariate (p variables vs. one target, producing a p-dimensional multiple correlation vector), and full/groupwise (two sets of p and q variables, producing a single scalar coefficient). The rank transformation is applied only during the correlation computation via differentiable sorting (TorchSort), not to the raw inputs, preserving original information while gaining robustness. Beyond the correlation values, the method outputs the learned nonlinear transformation functions (enabling interpretability) and supports asymptotic or permutation-based significance testing with a masking rule that zeros out non-significant entries.

## Method

SHGR replaces the Pearson correlation in the HGR definition with Spearman's rank correlation, equivalently maximizing the Pearson correlation between the copula (empirical CDF) transformations of the learned nonlinear functions. This rank-based reformulation inherits invariance to monotonic transformations and robustness to extreme values while still satisfying all of Rényi's axioms. For estimation, the paper introduces a stacked cross-encoder architecture: each variable (or variable set) has its own 5-layer MLP encoder (dimensions 64→32→16→8→1, Tanh activations with ReLU on the first layer), and the loss is the negative sum of squared Spearman correlations across all pairs (or set-to-target, or set-to-set), raised to a power α to prioritize stronger correlations. Differentiable sorting via TorchSort makes the rank operation end-to-end trainable, and early stopping balances accuracy against the observed convergence plateau. The joint optimization over all correlation entries in a single forward pass eliminates the iterative loops required by baselines like ACE, yielding a full 10-variable correlation matrix in roughly 5 seconds.

## Results

SHGR achieves a full bivariate correlation matrix (2,000 observations, 10 variables) in approximately 5–6 seconds with early stopping, and multivariate correlations in 23 seconds—faster than CCA, RDC, and dCor (~33 s) and dramatically faster than dk (~14 min). Under increasing additive noise, SHGR consistently yields the lowest distance to reference correlations across bivariate, multivariate, and full-correlation settings, while ACE (strong in noise-free conditions) degrades sharply and several methods (HGRnn, NHSIC, dCor) produce hallucinated correlations on independent variables. On nine real-world tabular datasets, SHGR-selected top-k features produce the lowest test-set RMSE with a random forest regressor (trained on ≤500 samples) in most cases. SHGR also remains stable at near-zero correlation for independent variables even when extreme values are injected, whereas HGRnn and other Pearson-based methods inflate artificially.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ACE (the strongest noise-free baseline), SHGR matches performance in the absence of noise but clearly outperforms it once additive noise is introduced, where ACE's accuracy drops significantly. Compared to RDC, SHGR achieves comparable or better noise robustness while additionally resisting hallucinated correlations on independent variables and supporting significance testing. Versus HGRnn (neural HGR with Pearson), SHGR is more robust to noise, extreme values, and spurious correlations, and extends naturally to multivariate and full-correlation settings. Against kernel-based methods (dk, sk, HGRkde, NHSIC), SHGR is substantially faster (23 s vs. ~14 min for dk in the multivariate setting) and avoids the kernel-choice sensitivity. Relative to CCA and kCCA, SHGR captures genuinely nonlinear dependencies (CCA is a special linear case of SHGR) while maintaining similar computational speed. On the feature-selection task across nine UCI datasets, SHGR-selected features yield lower RMSE than RDC, MIC, dCor, and HGRnn in most cases, confirming practical utility beyond synthetic benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Hirschfeld-Gebelein-Rényi (HGR) maximal correlation framework by replacing the Pearson correlation objective with Spearman rank correlation, yielding the SHGR coefficient. It builds directly on the neural HGR estimation approach of Grari et al. (2021), which parameterizes the optimal transformations with neural networks, but departs from it by operating on copula-transformed (rank-based) outputs to gain robustness to outliers and spurious correlations. The stacked cross-encoder architecture is a novel design that enables simultaneous estimation of full correlation matrices (bivariate, multivariate, and groupwise), contrasting with prior methods that estimate correlations sequentially or are limited to pairwise settings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SHGR provides a theoretically grounded (satisfying all Rényi axioms) and practically robust dependence measure that addresses a well-known failure mode of existing HGR estimators: hallucinated correlations under noise, outliers, and independence. The joint optimization over all pairwise correlations via the stacked cross-encoder offers a meaningful computational advantage over sequential estimation, making full correlation matrix estimation feasible at scale. Open questions include behavior in very high dimensions, the interpretability of learned transformations, and extension to multimodal or large-scale settings. The paper acknowledges that careful network capacity tuning is still needed to prevent overfitting-induced inflation of correlation scores.
