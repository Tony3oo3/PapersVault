## Problem

Existing self-supervised pretraining for robotic manipulation either relies on 2D paradigms (masked image modeling, contrastive learning) that capture static semantics but ignore 3D geometry and future dynamics, or uses video prediction models that model dynamics in 2D without explicit 3D scene structure. No prior framework jointly learns spatial geometry, future dynamics, and task semantics in a unified 3D representation suitable for manipulation policy learning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252592/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes four calibrated multi-view RGB-D images (front, left shoulder, right shoulder, wrist; 256×256 after resizing) and a natural language instruction as input. It first back-projects depth to reconstruct a scene-level point cloud, which is encoded into a 16×16×16 triplane feature volume. The output is a keyframe action: end-effector translation (decoded as heatmaps on three orthogonal planes), rotation (discretized Euler angles via MLP), and binary gripper state, executed by a low-level motion planner (MoveIt with RRT-Connect).

## Method

DynaRend encodes multi-view RGB-D observations into a triplane representation via depth back-projection, MLP feature extraction, and axis-aligned max pooling onto three orthogonal planes. A random subset of triplane features is masked and replaced with a learnable embedding; the masked features concatenated with CLIP-encoded language instructions are passed sequentially through a reconstructive Transformer (4 layers, SwiGLU, QK Norm, RoPE) and a predictive Transformer of the same architecture, yielding current and future triplane volumes. Both volumes are rendered via differentiable volumetric rendering (sampling rays, bilinear interpolation on triplanes, MLP heads for density/RGB/semantics) and supervised by ground-truth RGB, RADIOv2.5 semantic features, and depth from target views. To overcome limited real-world camera views, novel target views are synthesized using See3D (a multi-view diffusion model) conditioned on warped point-cloud projections, with depth estimated by Depth Anything v2. For downstream transfer, the two networks serve as a frozen triplane encoder fine-tuned with an action decoder that predicts translation heatmaps, rotation, and gripper state via cross-entropy losses on expert demonstrations.

## Results

On RLBench-18, DynaRend achieves 83.2% average success rate with an average rank of 1.5, outperforming RVT-2 (81.4%), 3D Diffuser Actor (81.3%), and 3D-MVP (67.5%). On the larger RLBench-71 setting, DynaRend reaches 76.6% average success rate, surpassing the best 3D pretraining baseline SPA (70.8%) by 5.8% and the single-stage RVT baseline (61.1%) by 15.5%. In real-world experiments across five tasks, DynaRend achieves 57% average success rate (45% with distractors), compared to 37% (16%) for RVT-2 and 37% (20%) for 3D Diffuser Actor.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DynaRend outperforms all 14 baselines on RLBench-18, achieving 83.2% average success rate versus 81.4% for RVT-2 [12] and 81.3% for 3D Diffuser Actor [24], with a 32.3% absolute gain over the single-stage RVT [13] baseline (62.9%). On RLBench-71, DynaRend (76.6%) surpasses the strongest 3D pretraining method SPA [47] (70.8%) and all 2D pretraining methods including VC-1 [28] (67.9%), MAE [14] (68.0%), and MVP [43] (66.2%), while also exceeding the single-stage RVT [13] by 15.5%. On the Colosseum generalization benchmark, DynaRend consistently outperforms 2D pretraining methods (MVP [43], R3M [30]) and the 3D pretraining method 3D-MVP [33] across most of the 12 perturbation categories, with particularly notable gains on texture and size variations. In real-world experiments, DynaRend (57% avg.) beats RVT-2 [12] (37%), 3D Diffuser Actor [24] (37%), and RVT [13] (26%) by 20+ points, with the gap widening to 29 points under distractor conditions (45% vs. 16% for RVT-2). The method also achieves the best inference-speed-to-success-rate trade-off, running at 19.6 Hz compared to 1.4 Hz for 3D Diffuser Actor [24] while maintaining higher success rates.

## Relation to Prior Work

DynaRend builds on the RVT action-value-map formulation for keyframe prediction on orthogonal planes and adopts GNFactor's coarse-to-fine volumetric rendering structure, but departs from both by introducing a masked future prediction objective in triplane space. It positions itself against three prior lines: 2D pretraining (MVP, VC-1) that captures static semantics without 3D or temporal awareness; 2D video-prediction policies (VPP, VidMan, GR-1/2) that model dynamics without explicit 3D geometry; and explicit 3D dynamics methods (ManiGaussian, Imagination Policy) that use dynamic Gaussians or point clouds with limited scalability. The key conceptual departure is unifying geometry, dynamics, and semantics in a compact triplane representation supervised by differentiable volumetric rendering, rather than relying on explicit 3D structures or 2D pixel-space prediction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap in manipulation pretraining: most existing methods either ignore 3D structure or model dynamics only in 2D, while explicit 3D approaches (Gaussians, point clouds) are structurally complex and hard to integrate with downstream policy decoders. The triplane formulation offers a scalable middle ground, and the generative view-augmentation strategy (See3D + Depth Anything v2) makes rendering-based pretraining feasible with only fixed cameras, which is important for real-world deployment. A notable limitation is the continued reliance on an external motion planner (MoveIt/RRT-Connect) to convert predicted keyframes into executable trajectories, leaving the system not fully end-to-end. Open questions include whether the triplane resolution (16×16×16) is sufficient for finer manipulation and whether the approach extends to deformable objects or multi-robot settings.
