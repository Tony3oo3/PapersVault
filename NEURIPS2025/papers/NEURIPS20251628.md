## Problem

Training 3D human pose and shape estimators in world coordinates requires rich video data with ground truth for both human motion and camera motion, but existing synthetic datasets like BEDLAM use limited focal lengths (52° or 65° HFOV), mostly static cameras, and lack diversity in body shape, clothing, hair, and shoes. This gap makes it difficult to train end-to-end methods that must handle dynamic cameras with varying focal lengths, realistic camera shake, and diverse human appearances simultaneously.

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes 1,615 SMPL-X body shapes (16 shape parameters, BMI 18–41), 4,643 retargeted motions, 187 graded clothing outfits, 40 strand-based hair grooms, 182 shoe assets, 15 3D environments plus 94 HDRIs, and camera motions from both synthetic keyframing and real captures (iPhone, iPad, Apple Vision Pro at 90 Hz). It produces 27,480 video sequences (8.05M frames at 1280×720, 30 fps) with per-frame ground truth SMPL-X parameters, camera extrinsics/intrinsics (including focal length), 16-bit float depth maps for 44% of frames, and 13.3M bounding boxes; each RGB frame is composited from 7 temporal subframes for realistic motion blur, and camera pose is embedded in EXR metadata to handle shake modifiers correctly.

## Method

BEDLAM2.0 is rendered in Unreal Engine 5.3 with a fully automated pipeline that combines several key innovations. For cameras, it covers 14–400 mm focal lengths (9% of sequences include dynamic zoom) and layers synthetic motions (pan, track, dolly, orbit, zoom) with randomized Perlin-noise shake, supplemented by real handheld and egocentric captures from phones, tablets, and an Apple Vision Pro headset. Body diversity is achieved by sampling 1,615 SMPL-X shapes from a resampled CAESAR BMI distribution and retargeting 4,643 AMASS/MOYO/BEAT2 motions to each shape via Unreal's IK-Retargeter, minimizing foot-sliding. Strand-based hair grooms (50–100 K curves per style) are bound to individual head shapes, shoes are applied as displacement/normal/texture maps in SMPL-X UV space with automatic sole-thickness body translation, and 50 of 187 outfits are graded into 10 sizes and simulated in CLO. Two custom C++ plugins ensure deterministic Perlin-noise reproducibility and correct center-subframe camera pose logging for depth consistency.

## Results

On world-space video-based human motion estimation, PHMR trained on B1+B2 achieves the best WA-MPJPE₁₀₀ of 72.5 mm on RICH and 70.5 mm on EMDB, outperforming the original GVHMR (78.8 / 111.0) and all other baselines. The same model achieves W-MPJPE₁₀₀ of 116.6 mm (RICH) and 193.7 mm (EMDB), RTE of 2.3 mm (RICH) and 1.4 mm (EMDB), and the lowest Jitter (10.2 / 11.3) and Foot-Sliding (2.6 / 3.2) across all methods. For single-frame estimation, CameraHMR trained on B2 reduces PA-MPJPE by ~5 mm and PVE by ~4–15 mm compared to B1 across 3DPW, EMDB, and RICH, with a 20% improvement in shape accuracy (PVE-T-SC: 7.20 vs. 8.85).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper re-trains two SOTA video-based methods (GVHMR and PromptHMR/PHMR) on B1, B2, or B1+B2 and compares them against six previously published world-space methods (DPVO+HMR2.0, GLAMR, TRACE, SLAHMR, WHAM, and the original GVHMR/PHMR). PHMR-B1+B2 achieves the best WA-MPJPE₁₀₀ on both RICH (72.5 vs. 78.8 for original GVHMR) and EMDB (70.5 vs. 71.0 for original PHMR), and the lowest Jitter and Foot-Sliding across all benchmarks. Notably, these synthetic-only-trained models outperform the original published versions that were trained on B1 plus real video sequences, demonstrating that B2's richer camera diversity is more beneficial than real data for world-space estimation. GVHMR-B1+B2 is competitive with PHMR-B1+B2 on RICH (75.8 vs. 72.5 WA-MPJPE₁₀₀) but PHMR is consistently stronger on EMDB. B2 alone is nearly as effective as B1+B2 for GVHMR (75.5 vs. 75.8 WA-MPJPE₁₀₀ on RICH), while for PHMR the combination provides a clearer advantage (75.3 → 72.5), suggesting B1's non-physical motions (sitting, stairs) still help with certain EMDB activities. All retrained models substantially outperform TRACE (238.1 WA-MPJPE₁₀₀ on RICH) and DPVO+HMR2.0 (184.3), confirming that end-to-end regression with diverse synthetic camera data is far superior to SLAM-based pipelines for this task.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends BEDLAM (B1, CVPR 2023), the first synthetic dataset shown sufficient to train SOTA 3D human pose and shape regressors, by addressing its key limitations: narrow focal length range, mostly static cameras, limited body shape diversity, absence of shoes, and card-based hair. It also positions itself against a growing family of synthetic datasets with camera motion (EgoGen, HumanVid, WHAC-A-Mole, PACE) but surpasses them in scale, realism, and the inclusion of captured real-world camera motions from handheld devices and Apple Vision Pro. The rendering pipeline builds on Unreal Engine 5.3 with CLO-based cloth simulation, IK retargeting of AMASS motions to diverse body shapes, and strand-based hair grooms, representing a substantial engineering upgrade over B1's pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The field is shifting toward world-coordinate human motion estimation with dynamic cameras, a setting that previously lacked adequate training data; BEDLAM2.0 fills this gap with 27K+ sequences, 8M+ frames, and diverse camera motions, enabling end-to-end training without real data. The finding that synthetic-only training (B1+B2) matches or exceeds SOTA world-space methods that use real data suggests a potential path to reducing reliance on real-world video for this task. Key open questions remain: the dataset excludes human-object interaction, children, amputees, facial motion, and audio, and a visual domain gap to real video persists. The practical insight that B1 and B2 are complementary (B1's non-physical motions like sitting/stairs help on EMDB) gives users a useful design choice for their specific application.
