## Problem

Existing 3D shape completion methods face a fundamental trade-off: volumetric approaches (e.g., OctMAE) are limited by the cubic cost of 3D grids, producing coarse, oversmoothed shapes that miss high-frequency geometry, while generative view-synthesis methods (diffusion-based) lack 3D consistency and are too slow for robotics or XR deployment. RaySt3R addresses this by recasting shape completion as a novel view synthesis problem, enabling high-resolution geometric prediction without volumetric supervision.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253018/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single foreground-masked RGB-D image with known camera intrinsics as input, and a novel query view specified purely as a 2D ray map (normalized pixel coordinates) rather than an actual image. For each queried ray, the network predicts a depth value, a binary object mask, and a per-pixel confidence score. These per-view predictions are then merged across ~22 sampled novel views using occlusion-aware filtering, mask thresholding, and confidence gating to produce a complete 3D point cloud of all foreground objects.

## Method

RaySt3R uses a ViT architecture where the input depth is unprojected into a point map and transformed into the query camera frame, while the novel view is encoded as a ray map; both undergo self-attention, and the RGB image is processed by a frozen DINOv2 encoder. Cross-attention layers then let ray features query the concatenated point-map and DINOv2 features, followed by separate DPT heads for depth+confidence and object mask prediction. Training uses a confidence-aware depth loss (inspired by DUSt3R, where confidence is learned without explicit supervision via a log-penalty term) plus binary cross-entropy for masks, on 251k synthetic scenes with 11M rendered novel-view depth pairs. The key architectural difference from DUSt3R is replacing the second image input with a camera ray map, enabling arbitrary novel-view queries without requiring a second observation. Multi-view merging applies three filters—occlusion relative to the input view, predicted mask thresholding at 0.5, and confidence thresholding—to aggregate valid points into the final reconstruction.

## Results

RaySt3R achieves state-of-the-art 3D shape completion across all four evaluation datasets, with chamfer distances of 5.21 mm (OctMAE synthetic), 3.56 mm (YCB-Video), 4.75 mm (HomebrewedDB), and 3.92 mm (HOPE), and F1-Score@10mm values of 0.893, 0.930, 0.889, and 0.926 respectively. It outperforms the strongest baseline OctMAE by 20–44% in chamfer distance across all datasets. RaySt3R also exhibits the lowest standard deviation in chamfer distance (1.74 mm) among all methods, indicating more consistent performance. Inference takes less than 1.2 seconds on a single RTX 4090 GPU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RaySt3R outperforms all 13 baselines on every dataset and metric. The closest competitor is OctMAE (CD 6.48/6.40/6.14/6.97 mm), which RaySt3R surpasses by 20–44% in chamfer distance; OctMAE's volumetric 3D MAE grid produces oversmoothed shapes that lack fine geometric detail. Among view-based and generative baselines, LaRI (CD 11.41 mm on YCB-Video), Unique3D (17.56 mm), and TRELLIS (22.44 mm masked / 31.45 mm unmasked) all struggle with relative object placement, aspect ratios, and out-of-distribution objects, and require ICP registration to ground truth (which RaySt3R does not). SceneComplete, a multi-stage pipeline chaining segmentation, inpainting, 3D generation, and pose estimation, is the weakest baseline (CD 96.63 mm on YCB-Video) and fails entirely on HOPE due to VRAM exhaustion. The older volumetric methods (VoxFormer, ShapeFormer, MCC, ConvONet, POCO, AICNet, Minkowski, OCNN) are all outperformed by large margins, with RaySt3R achieving 2–10× lower chamfer distance than the best among them (OCNN at 7.02–8.05 mm). Notably, RaySt3R was trained exclusively on synthetic data yet generalizes to real-world cluttered scenes without any real-data fine-tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

RaySt3R builds directly on DUSt3R's point-map regression framework via multi-view transformers, but replaces the second image input with a camera ray map, recasting 3D shape completion as a novel view synthesis problem. It departs from volumetric methods (OctMAE, VoxFormer, ConvONet) that suffer from cubic-resolution constraints, and from generative view-based methods (ViewCrafter, Unique3D, TRELLIS) that produce images via diffusion and lack geometric consistency. The closest conceptual relative is LVSM, which also queries a transformer with rays, but RaySt3R predicts depth maps and object masks rather than images, and uses simpler 2D ray coordinates instead of full Plücker rays.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 20–44% chamfer distance improvement over the strongest baseline (OctMAE) combined with sub-1.2-second inference on a single GPU makes this approach practically viable for robotics and XR deployment where diffusion-based methods are too slow. The zero-shot sim-to-real generalization—trained entirely on 251k synthetic scenes yet outperforming on real cluttered datasets—is a strong signal for the field. Key open questions include whether the method can handle scenes beyond tabletop settings (requiring accurate novel-view pose estimation), whether training on real data would further close the sim-to-real gap, and whether scaling to diffusion-transformer architectures could address the persistent edge-bleeding artifact.
