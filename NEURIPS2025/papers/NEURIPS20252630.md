## Problem

End-to-end video-to-audio generation systems struggle with compositional complexity: they produce generic sounds, fail to synchronize with subtle visual cues, and offer no user control over the generation process. Existing MLLM-based approaches either compress sound design into a single black-box transformation or fragment it across specialized models, losing the stepwise reasoning that professional sound designers employ to analyze visual dynamics, reason about acoustic properties, and iteratively refine audio.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252630/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system consumes 9.1-second video clips at 44.1 kHz stereo, optional user click coordinates defining a Region of Interest (extracted via Grounded SAM2), natural-language editing instructions, and existing audio context from prior stages. Output is a stereo waveform reconstructed from a 128-dimensional VAE latent space. The three-stage pipeline progressively refines the output: Stage 1 generates a full soundscape from the video alone, Stage 2 adds object-specific audio conditioned on the ROI and the Stage-1 audio, and Stage 3 applies targeted modifications (extension, inpainting, addition, removal) guided by text instructions while preserving the existing audio context.

## Method

ThinkSound couples a fine-tuned VideoLLaMA2 (trained on the newly introduced AudioCoT dataset with structured CoT annotations) with a 1.3B-parameter unified MM-DiT flow-matching model. The MLLM produces audio-specific reasoning chains that explicitly decompose complex scenes into temporal sound events, acoustic properties, and causal relationships; these CoT tokens are encoded by T5-v1-xl for fine-grained reasoning-driven control, while MetaCLIP encodes visual captions for global scene context. The MM-DiT backbone alternates 14 multi-stream transformer layers (modality-specific parameters with shared attention) and 7 single-stream layers, with a gated fusion module that upsamples video features into the audio latent space to capture subtle visual-to-acoustic correspondences. Classifier-free guidance dropout (p=0.2 per modality) during training enables the model to accept arbitrary combinations of video, text, CoT, and audio inputs at inference, supporting all three interactive stages within a single unified architecture.

## Results

On the VGGSound test set, ThinkSound achieves the best Fréchet Distance (34.56), KL divergence (KLPaSST: 1.52, KLPaNNs: 1.32), CLAP-CoT alignment (0.46), and subjective scores (MOS-Q: 4.02, MOS-A: 4.18), outperforming the strongest baseline MMAudio (FD: 43.26, KLPaSST: 1.65, CLAP-CoT: 0.40, MOS-Q: 3.84, MOS-A: 3.97). ThinkSound also achieves the fastest inference time at 1.07s per sample versus 3.01s for MMAudio. On the out-of-distribution MovieGen Audio Bench, ThinkSound attains the best CLAP-CoT (0.51) and MOS scores (MOS-Q: 4.11, MOS-A: 3.87) while matching the best DeSync (0.76). Removing CoT reasoning degrades CLAP-CoT from 0.46 to 0.41 and MOS-Q from 4.02 to 3.91, confirming the critical role of structured reasoning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ThinkSound outperforms all six VGGSound baselines on the majority of objective and all subjective metrics. Against the strongest baseline MMAudio, ThinkSound improves FD by 20% (34.56 vs. 43.26), KLPaSST by 8% (1.52 vs. 1.65), CLAP-CoT by 15% (0.46 vs. 0.40), and MOS-A by 5.3% (4.18 vs. 3.97), while MMAudio retains a marginal DeSync advantage (0.44 vs. 0.46). Against earlier methods (See&Hear, V-AURA, FoleyCrafter, Frieren, V2A-Mapper), ThinkSound leads by large margins on all metrics, with FD improvements of 12–250% over the weakest baselines. On the out-of-distribution MovieGen Audio Bench, ThinkSound beats both MMAudio and MovieGen on CLAP-CoT (0.51 vs. 0.45/0.47) and MOS-A (3.87 vs. 3.62/3.70), while matching MovieGen's DeSync (0.76 vs. 1.00). For audio editing, ThinkSound substantially outperforms AudioLDM-2 (FD: 34.78 vs. 61.28; CLAP: 0.51 vs. 0.35) and DDPM-Friendly (FD: 34.78 vs. 55.56; CLAP: 0.51 vs. 0.39) across all metrics. For object-focused generation, ThinkSound with CoT achieves the best CLAP (0.48) and MOS scores over both MMAudio (CLAP: 0.41) and its own no-CoT variant (CLAP: 0.43).

## Relation to Prior Work

ThinkSound builds on the MM-DiT flow-matching architecture from MMAudio (Cheng et al., 2024a) and the VideoLLaMA2 multimodal LLM (Cheng et al., 2024b), but departs from prior V2A work by replacing the end-to-end black-box paradigm with a CoT-reasoning-guided, three-stage interactive pipeline. Unlike SonicVisionLM, which loses visual dynamics by converting video to text captions before a separate T2A model, or DeepSound-V1, which fragments CoT-guided generation across specialized models, ThinkSound unifies CoT reasoning and audio synthesis in a single foundation model that handles foley generation, object-centric refinement, and instruction-based editing. The AudioCoT dataset with structured reasoning annotations is a new contribution that bridges visual content, textual descriptions, and sound synthesis in a way no prior dataset provides.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that explicit CoT reasoning from an MLLM provides meaningful, measurable gains over caption-only conditioning for audio generation, and that the logical structure of the reasoning (not just its keyword content) is critical—randomized CoT degrades performance substantially. The interactive three-stage workflow (holistic foley → click-based object refinement → natural-language editing) addresses a practical gap between creative intent and automated generation that prior end-to-end systems cannot fill. Open questions remain around precise temporal localization (MLLMs still struggle with exact event timestamps), dataset diversity for rare or culturally specific sounds, and whether the CoT-guided paradigm scales to longer, more complex multi-object scenes.
