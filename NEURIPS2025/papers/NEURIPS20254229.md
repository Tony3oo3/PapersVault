## Problem

Popular adaptive optimizers like Adam treat DNN parameters as flat vectors, ignoring the inherent matrix structure of weight layers. While it is well established that DNN gradients are low-rank and Hessians are approximately block diagonal, existing structured methods like Shampoo either fail to exploit these properties in their convergence guarantees or incur prohibitive memory and computation costs from maintaining two full preconditioner matrices. The paper asks how to design a structured optimizer that provably benefits from low-rank gradients and block diagonal Hessians while remaining more efficient than Shampoo.

## Input / Output

ASGO operates on weight matrices W ∈ R^{m×n} and their stochastic gradients G_t ∈ R^{m×n}, maintaining a single full-matrix preconditioner V_t (of size m×m for left-sided or n×n for right-sided) that accumulates outer products of past gradients. The practical implementation (Algorithm 2) additionally consumes momentum coefficients β₁, β₂, a learning-rate schedule, and a small regularization ε, and adaptively selects the preconditioning side based on which dimension of G_t is smaller. For Transformer attention layers, query and key matrices are reshaped into per-head tensors R^{h×n×d} so that each head's sub-matrix is preconditioned independently, reducing the preconditioner size from O(h²d²) to O(hd²).

## Method

ASGO replaces Shampoo's two-matrix Kronecker-product preconditioner with a single-side preconditioner, updating weights via W_{t+1} = W_t − η_t Λ_t^{-1} G_t where Λ_t = (V_t + εI)^{1/2} and V_t = β₂V_{t-1} + (1−β₂)G_t^T G_t. The key theoretical insight is that this single-side design yields convergence bounds expressed in terms of the trace norm of the gradient covariance (exploiting low-rankness) and the spectral norm of the weight iterates (exploiting high-rank weight updates), making ASGO provably at least D_F/D_op times faster than full-matrix AdaGrad and √r_G·D_F/D_op times faster than Shampoo. Under a block-wise smoothness assumption that matches the observed block-diagonal Hessian structure of DNNs, the right-sided preconditioner (applied along the input dimension of each neuron) is shown to be the correct choice. The inverse square root is computed efficiently via a Newton-Schulz iteration on a 2×2 block matrix, making the per-step cost comparable to Muon's spectral-norm update; the paper further shows that ASGO without momentum is equivalent to Muon, positioning ASGO as the 'AdaGrad' counterpart to Muon's 'SignSGD' role for structured gradients.

## Results

On GPT-2 pretraining (~2.5B tokens, 2400 steps), ASGO-PolarExpress achieves a validation loss of 3.342, matching Muon (3.342) and substantially outperforming AdamW (3.455) and Shampoo (3.417). On GPT-2-Large (774M) finetuning on WikiText-2, ASGO achieves 13.88 perplexity under Causal LM and 15.66 under Fill-in-the-Middle, beating both Muon (13.91 / 15.93) and AdamW (14.01 / 17.46). The per-step wall-clock overhead of ASGO over AdamW is only 0.468 s (8.6%), making the total training time marginally affected.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against AdamW, ASGO clearly wins on both pretraining (3.342 vs. 3.455 validation loss) and finetuning (13.88 vs. 14.01 CLM perplexity; 15.66 vs. 17.46 FIM perplexity), with the FIM gap being particularly large. Against Muon, ASGO ties on GPT-2 pretraining (both 3.342 validation loss) but edges ahead on GPT-2-Large finetuning (13.88 vs. 13.91 CLM; 15.66 vs. 15.93 FIM), and the two methods exhibit nearly identical training trajectories. Against Shampoo, ASGO achieves a 0.075 lower validation loss on pretraining and avoids the loss spikes (numerical instability) that Shampoo exhibits during training, while also requiring only one preconditioner matrix instead of two. The lightweight diagonal variant DASGO underperforms on GPT-2 pretraining (3.509 val loss, worse than AdamW) but remains competitive on finetuning (13.84 CLM, 15.45 FIM), suggesting the full matrix preconditioner is important for capturing inter-parameter dependencies in attention layers.

## Relation to Prior Work

ASGO sits at the intersection of three optimization lineages: AdaGrad's adaptive preconditioning (Duchi et al., 2011), Shampoo's Kronecker-product two-sided preconditioner for matrix-structured parameters (Gupta et al., 2018), and Muon's steepest descent in the spectral norm (Jordan et al., 2024). The key conceptual departure is replacing Shampoo's two preconditioner matrices with a single-side preconditioner, which the paper proves yields strictly better convergence rates than both full-matrix AdaGrad and Shampoo. The paper further establishes that ASGO (without momentum) is algorithmically equivalent to Muon, drawing an explicit analogy: ASGO:Muon :: AdaGrad:SignSGD, positioning ASGO as the 'adaptive' counterpart to Muon's 'sign-based' approach for structured gradients.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first fine-grained convergence analysis showing that a single-side matrix preconditioner can exploit low-rank gradients and block-diagonal Hessians to outperform both diagonal AdaGrad and two-sided Shampoo, giving practitioners a theoretically grounded alternative to Muon with half the memory cost of Shampoo and only ~9% wall-clock overhead over AdamW. Two major limitations remain: the algorithm is computationally heavier than Adam due to matrix square-root inversion, and extension to higher-order tensors (beyond 2D weight matrices) is non-trivial. The conjectured nonconvex convergence rate (matching Muon's up to log factors) is stated but unproven, and the relationship between weight-update rank and optimizer performance is flagged as an open question.
