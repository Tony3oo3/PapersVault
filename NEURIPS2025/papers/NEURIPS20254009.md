## Problem

Applying FedAVG directly to LoRA's A and B matrices in federated settings produces inexact model updates because the average of products does not equal the product of averages. Existing fixes either incur heavy SVD overhead (FlexLoRA, FLoRA) or freeze one projection matrix (FFA-LoRA), which the paper shows leads to irreducible suboptimality proportional to the initialization error of the frozen matrix. The paper asks whether both LoRA projections can be learned jointly in a federated setting while preserving exact aggregation and communication efficiency.

## Input / Output

Each client receives the frozen pre-trained LLM weights (RoBERTa-Large 355M or Llama-2-7B) plus a local shard of text data, and trains only the small LoRA adapter pair (A ∈ R^{d×r}, B ∈ R^{r×d}, r≪d). In any given communication round, only one of the two matrices is updated locally and transmitted to the server, halving per-round communication relative to standard FedAVG-of-LoRA. The server broadcasts the aggregated matrix back, so all clients always share an identical frozen copy of the other matrix, guaranteeing interference-free aggregation.

## Method

RoLoRA alternates the role of the frozen and trainable LoRA matrix across communication rounds: in odd rounds all clients freeze the shared A and perform local gradient descent on B, then the server averages B; in even rounds the roles swap, clients freeze the shared B and update A, and the server averages A. Because the frozen matrix is identical across all clients at the start of each round, the server's average of the effective adapters equals the product of the frozen matrix and the averaged trainable matrix, eliminating the interference that plagues naive FedAVG-of-LoRA. Theoretically, the paper proves exponential convergence to the global minimizer for a federated linear regression model (Theorem 4.5) and an O(1/√T) stationary-point rate for smooth non-convex losses, while showing FFA-LoRA's fixed down-projection incurs a loss floor of ‖b*‖²(δ⁰)² that never vanishes. Empirically, RoLoRA is evaluated on GLUE, commonsense reasoning, HumanEval, and MMLU with 3–50 clients, consistently outperforming LoRA, FFA-LoRA, FlexLoRA, and FLoRA, especially as client count grows or the fine-tuning parameter budget shrinks.

## Results

On RoBERTa-Large with 50 clients and rank-4 LoRA adapters, RoLoRA achieves an average GLUE accuracy of 85.81%, substantially outperforming LoRA (70.72%), FFA-LoRA (76.48%), and FlexLoRA (54.67%). With rank-8 adapters under the same 50-client setting, RoLoRA reaches 86.27% average, compared to 64.03% for LoRA and 72.46% for FFA-LoRA. On Llama-2-7B commonsense reasoning tasks (50 clients, rank 8), RoLoRA achieves the highest accuracy across all 8 tasks, e.g., 61.83% on BoolQ vs. 61.42% (LoRA) and 53.43% (FFA-LoRA), and 47.67% on WinoGrande vs. 31.36% (LoRA) and 1.61% (FFA-LoRA).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LoRA (FedAVG of LoRA / FedIT), RoLoRA is comparable at 3 clients (88.28% vs. 88.20% avg) but pulls ahead dramatically at scale: at 50 clients, RoLoRA gains +15.1 points (85.81% vs. 70.72%), with the gap widening further at rank 8 (86.27% vs. 64.03%). Against FFA-LoRA, RoLoRA consistently wins, with the advantage growing as fine-tuning parameters decrease; at 50 clients with rank 4, RoLoRA leads by +9.3 points (85.81% vs. 76.48%), and on Llama-2-7B WinoGrande the gap reaches +46 points (47.67% vs. 1.61%). Against FlexLoRA, RoLoRA dominates across all settings; FlexLoRA collapses to 54.67% average at 50 clients (rank 4) and 52.85% (rank 8), while RoLoRA maintains 85–86%. Against FLoRA (compared in Appendix Table 11), RoLoRA consistently outperforms across tasks and client counts. The key differentiator is that RoLoRA's alternating optimization avoids the aggregation interference inherent in FedAVG of LoRA while retaining the full expressiveness of learning both A and B matrices, unlike FFA-LoRA's frozen down-projection.

## Relation to Prior Work

RoLoRA builds directly on LoRA (Hu et al., 2021) and departs from FFA-LoRA (Sun et al., 2024), which freezes the down-projection matrix A to enable exact aggregation but sacrifices expressiveness. The paper also addresses the interference problem in FedAVG-of-LoRA (FedIT, SLoRA, FedPETuning) and the computational overhead of FlexLoRA/FLoRA's matrix-multiplication-plus-SVD approach. Conceptually, the alternating optimization strategy is inspired by Multi-Task Linear Representation Learning (MLRL) literature (Collins et al., Vaswani et al.), where shared low-rank representations are alternately optimized with task-specific heads, though RoLoRA applies this to a global-model federated setting with synchronized aggregation of both matrices.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RoLoRA provides a clean theoretical separation: exponential convergence to the global optimum for linear models versus an irreducible error bound proportional to initialization angle for FFA-LoRA, and O(1/√T) convergence for smooth non-convex losses. Practically, it halves communication cost relative to LoRA and FlexLoRA while maintaining or improving accuracy, with particularly large gains at scale (50 clients) and under constrained fine-tuning budgets. Key limitations include the theoretical analysis being restricted to linear models (non-linear validation is empirical only), the assumption of full client participation, and the lack of a rigorous proof for non-linear convergence. Open questions include extending the theory to non-linear settings, handling partial client participation, and integrating with heterogeneous-rank adapters.
