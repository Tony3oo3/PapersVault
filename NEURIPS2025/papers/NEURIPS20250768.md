## Problem

Federated continual learning (FCL) requires balancing stability (retaining old-task knowledge) and plasticity (acquiring new-task knowledge) under heterogeneous client data, yet existing PTM-based FCL methods either limit adaptability through fixed prompt subsets or suffer from growing communication overhead as prompt pools expand. The core gap is how to fine-tune pre-trained models for sequential FCL tasks without diminishing their strong generalization capabilities while effectively fusing cross-task and cross-client knowledge.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-100, 64×64 for Tiny-ImageNet, 224×224 for ImageNet-R and CUB-200-2011) and produces class labels via distance-based classification against learned class prototypes rather than a conventional linear head. The text modality of CLIP is used internally to extract semantic features of old-task labels, which are fused with image features through cross-attention to initialize new class prototypes. Each client maintains M multi-scale experts whose outputs are distilled into a single final expert for inference, reducing test-time computation.

## Method

MultiFCL introduces lightweight bottleneck adapters into a frozen CLIP ViT backbone, fine-tuning them exclusively on the first task before permanently freezing them to preserve PTM generalization. For new tasks, class prototypes are initialized via cross-attention between the first sample's image feature, the most semantically similar old-task label's text embedding, and the corresponding old prototype, injecting prior knowledge into the classifier. The PTM is partitioned into M modules acting as multi-scale experts, each trained with a classification loss that pulls features toward their class prototypes at different representational depths. A multi-teacher dynamic self-distillation loss then transfers knowledge from all experts to the final expert using KL divergence weighted by inverse output uncertainty (entropy), suppressing shallow experts with high ambiguity while amplifying confident deep experts. Federated aggregation exchanges only class prototypes (and adapters in the first task) between clients and server, keeping communication to ~0.38M parameters per round after initialization.

## Results

On CIFAR-100 with 10 tasks under distribution-based partitioning (β=0.5), MultiFCL achieves 70.7% Last accuracy, 76.1% Avg. accuracy, and a forgetting rate of 16.4%, outperforming the closest PTM-based baseline PiLoRA (69.6% Last, 72.1% Avg., 26.7% F) and the raw CLIP/ViT baselines (65.0% Last, 68.2% Avg.). Under the most heterogeneous setting (β=0.05), MultiFCL retains 67.9% Last and 72.8% Avg. accuracy with only 17.5% forgetting, while PiLoRA drops to 63.0% Last and 68.0% Avg. with 29.4% forgetting. Non-PTM methods (MFCL, LANDER, FedCBC, FedMGP) trail by 30–50+ points in Last accuracy, underscoring the advantage of pre-trained backbones in FCL.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MultiFCL outperforms all seven baselines across every data partitioning setting. Against the closest PTM-based competitor PiLoRA (ECCV 2024), MultiFCL gains +1.1% Last and +4.0% Avg. accuracy at β=0.5, and the gap widens to +4.9% Last and +4.8% Avg. at β=0.05, while reducing the forgetting rate from 29.4% to 17.5%. Compared to the raw CLIP and ViT fine-tuning baselines, MultiFCL improves Last accuracy by ~5.7% and Avg. by ~7.9% at β=0.5, with a substantially lower forgetting rate (16.4% vs. 25.6%/22.9%). Non-PTM methods (MFCL, LANDER, FedCBC, FedMGP) are far behind, achieving only 17.6–34.8% Last accuracy versus MultiFCL's 70.7%, confirming that pre-trained backbones are essential for competitive FCL performance. Even when CLIP is swapped in as the backbone for MFCL and PiLoRA (MFCL-△, PiLoRA-△), MultiFCL still leads by 17.9% Last over MFCL-△ and 2.0% Last over PiLoRA-△, demonstrating that the multi-expert and self-distillation mechanisms provide gains beyond simply using a stronger PTM.

## Relation to Prior Work

MultiFCL positions itself within the PTM-based FCL line (FedMGP, PiLoRA) but departs from both prompt-pool and LoRA-based adaptation by introducing a freeze-then-specialize strategy: lightweight adapters are tuned only on the first task and then permanently frozen, shifting the burden of plasticity to a multi-expert architecture with multi-scale feature learning and uncertainty-weighted self-distillation. The multi-modal prototype initialization, which fuses CLIP text-encoder semantic features with sample features via cross-attention, repurposes the vision-language alignment of PTMs in a way that prior FCL methods (which rely on generative replay or gradient compensation) do not. Conceptually, the multi-expert + self-distillation mechanism draws from the continual-learning literature (Expert Gate, Yan et al. 2024) but is the first to deploy it in a federated setting with inter-client prototype aggregation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that PTMs can serve as stable backbones in FCL without continuous fine-tuning, reducing both communication overhead (0.38M parameters per round after the first task) and catastrophic forgetting (forgetting rate drops from ~27% to ~16%). However, the framework still depends on large-scale PTMs with image-text alignment, limiting applicability to resource-constrained edge devices, and evaluation is confined to image classification. Open questions include whether the multi-expert + self-distillation mechanism transfers to other modalities (text, speech), how prototype initialization behaves under extreme class imbalance, and whether the approach generalizes to domain-incremental or task-incremental settings beyond the standard CIL/TIL benchmarks.
