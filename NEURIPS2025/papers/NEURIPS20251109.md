## Problem

Value-based Q-learning is the workhorse of off-policy RL for discrete actions, but extending it to continuous action spaces requires maximizing the Q-function over an uncountable set, which is computationally infeasible. Actor-critic methods sidestep this by learning a separate policy, but introduce coupled-training instability, extra hyperparameters, and—critically—gradient-based maximization that gets trapped at local optima in constrained or non-convex Q-function landscapes. The paper asks whether a purely value-based, actor-free Q-learning algorithm can match or exceed actor-critic performance in continuous control, especially under action-space constraints.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a state observation vector (3–111 dimensional, depending on the MuJoCo/Gymnasium task) and outputs a deterministic continuous action vector (1–26 dimensional) by selecting the control-point with the highest estimated Q-value. During training, Gaussian noise is added to the selected action for exploration, and the action space is normalized to [−1, 1] with a tanh output on the control-point generator. The Q-function itself is not a single scalar per (s,a) pair but is evaluated on-the-fly via inverse-distance-weighted interpolation over N learned control-points, with only the top-k nearest control-points contributing to the interpolated value.

## Method

Q3C represents the Q-function as a wire-fitting interpolator over N learned control-points: a control-point generator g_φ(s) proposes N candidate actions, and a shared Q-estimator h_ψ(s, â_i) scores each, so the optimal action is simply the control-point with the largest Q-value—no gradient ascent or separate actor needed. Three architectural innovations make this work at scale: (1) action-conditioned Q-value generation, where Q-values are computed by evaluating the same Q-estimator at each generated control-point rather than predicting them independently, ensuring consistency; (2) relevance-based filtering that restricts interpolation to the top-k nearest control-points, sharpening the local value landscape; and (3) a pairwise separation loss that prevents control-points from collapsing to action-space boundaries. For robustness across tasks with varying reward scales, Q-value differences in the interpolation kernel are rescaled to [0,1] and the smoothing coefficient is exponentially annealed. The training procedure follows TD3's stabilization recipe—twin Q-networks, soft-updated target networks, and target policy smoothing—applied to the Bellman residual computed with the structurally maximized next-state Q-value.

## Results

On standard MuJoCo environments, Q3C achieves performance on par with TD3 (e.g., Hopper: 3206±407 vs 3113±888; HalfCheetah: 9469±949 vs 9985±1077; Swimmer: 316±15 vs 301±126), with the notable exception of Ant-v4 where it trails (3698±1315 vs 5168±673). On restricted (constrained action space) environments, Q3C consistently outperforms TD3: InvertedPendulumBox 1000±0 vs 783±349, HalfCheetahBox 4358±1503 vs 2277±2037, and HopperBox 1974±1170 vs 1407±1163. Q3C substantially outperforms all other value-based baselines (NAF, Wire-Fitting, RBF-DQN) across both standard and restricted settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against TD3 (the strongest actor-critic baseline), Q3C matches or slightly exceeds performance on 6 of 7 standard environments (e.g., Hopper +93, Swimmer +16, HalfCheetah −516) but underperforms on Ant-v4 (−1470). On restricted environments where gradient-based policy optimization struggles with non-convex Q-landscapes, Q3C clearly wins: +217 on InvertedPendulumBox, +2081 on HalfCheetahBox, and +567 on HopperBox. Against NAF (quadratic Q-function), Q3C outperforms by large margins on nearly all tasks (e.g., Hopper: 3206 vs 501; HalfCheetah: 9469 vs 3532), with NAF's restrictive quadratic inductive bias being the limiting factor. Against vanilla Wire-Fitting (the original control-point method without Q3C's architectural additions), Q3C improves by 60–400% across environments, confirming that the proposed modifications (action-conditioned Q-values, relevance filtering, diversity loss, normalization) are essential. Against RBF-DQN, Q3C wins on 6 of 7 standard environments and all 3 restricted ones, as RBF-DQN's implicit smoothing trade-off prevents reliable recovery of the true maximizing action. In appendix comparisons, Q3C is competitive with SAC and PPO on standard tasks and consistently surpasses both on restricted environments.

## Relation to Prior Work

Q3C revives the wire-fitting / control-point function approximation framework of Baird & Klopf (1993) and Gaskett et al. (1999, 2000), which was largely abandoned after poor results in deep RL settings. The paper's key departure is identifying a set of architectural and algorithmic fixes—action-conditioned Q-value generation, relevance-based top-k filtering, a pairwise separation loss, and scale-aware normalization—that make the structurally maximizable Q-function viable with deep networks. Algorithmically, it builds directly on TD3's stabilization toolkit (twin critics, target networks, target policy smoothing) while eliminating the actor entirely, positioning itself as a middle ground between DQN-style value methods and actor-critic approaches. The closest prior work, RBF-DQN (Asadi et al., 2021), also pursues actor-free continuous Q-learning but relies on radial basis functions whose implicit smoothing trade-off limits expressivity in non-convex landscapes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Q3C provides a practical, actor-free alternative to actor-critic methods for continuous control, with a clear structural advantage in constrained or non-convex action spaces where gradient-based policy optimization gets trapped at local optima. The universal approximation proof and thorough ablations strengthen the methodological contribution, but the evaluation is limited to standard MuJoCo and a small set of restricted variants, leaving open questions about scalability to very high-dimensional or real-world robotic settings. Sample efficiency remains a weakness relative to TD3 and SAC, and the paper itself flags exploration strategy, offline RL adaptation, and stochastic policy extensions as important future directions. For practitioners working on safety-constrained control or environments with discontinuous action validity, this is a directly applicable tool.
