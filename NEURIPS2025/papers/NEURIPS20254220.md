## Problem

While RLVR has proven effective for training LLMs on reasoning tasks, the distinct roles of positive (correct) and negative (incorrect) reward signals in shaping model behavior remain unexplored. In particular, it is unclear whether reinforcing correct responses, penalizing incorrect ones, or both are necessary for strong performance, and how each component affects inference-time scaling (Pass@k) versus single-attempt accuracy (Pass@1).
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254220/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a math problem as a text prompt (e.g., from MATH, AIME 2025, or AMC23) and produces a chain-of-thought reasoning trace ending with a final numerical answer in \boxed{} format. During training, 8 rollouts are sampled per prompt at temperature 1.0; during evaluation, 256 (or 64 for Qwen3-4B) responses are sampled per problem to compute an unbiased Pass@k estimate across k ∈ {1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32, 64, 128, 256}, capturing both exploitation (small k) and exploration (large k) behavior.

## Method

The paper decomposes the RLVR objective into Positive Sample Reinforcement (PSR, maximizing likelihood of correct responses) and Negative Sample Reinforcement (NSR, minimizing likelihood of incorrect responses), then trains Qwen2.5-Math-7B, Qwen3-4B, and Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct with each component in isolation. Token-level gradient analysis reveals that NSR suppresses incorrect tokens while redistributing probability mass to other candidates in proportion to their current probabilities (π_v), effectively performing a prior-guided soft reranking that preserves the model's existing knowledge and halts updates once errors are corrected. In contrast, PSR sharpens the distribution around sampled correct paths, collapsing entropy and degrading Pass@k at large k. Building on this, the authors propose Weighted-REINFORCE (W-REINFORCE), which scales the PSR reward by λ=0.1 while keeping NSR at full weight, achieving the best Pass@k trade-off across MATH, AIME 2025, and AMC23, consistently matching or surpassing PPO and GRPO.

## Results

On MATH (Qwen2.5-Math-7B), NSR-only training achieves 96.9 Pass@256, matching the base model and outperforming PPO (96.3), GRPO (95.5), and PSR (91.2) at large k. The proposed W-REINFORCE (λ=0.1) achieves the best overall Pass@k: 76.6 Pass@1 on MATH (tied with PPO), 56.7 Pass@256 on AIME 2025 (beating GRPO's 50.0 and PPO's 43.3), and 97.5 Pass@256 on AMC23. Notably, PSR improves Pass@1 (e.g., 11.6 on AIME 2025) but degrades sharply at higher k (43.3 Pass@256), while NSR preserves the full inference-scaling curve.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against PPO, W-REINFORCE ties on MATH Pass@1 (76.6) and surpasses it at every k≥2 (e.g., 96.7 vs 96.3 at k=256); on AIME 2025 the gap widens dramatically, with W-REINFORCE reaching 56.7 Pass@256 versus PPO's 43.3. Against GRPO, W-REINFORCE wins on AIME 2025 at all k≥2 (56.7 vs 50.0 at k=256) and on MATH at k=1 (76.6 vs 76.3), while GRPO retains a slight edge on MATH at intermediate k (e.g., 92.3 vs 92.4 at k=16 is essentially tied). Against vanilla REINFORCE (λ=1), W-REINFORCE consistently outperforms across all three benchmarks and all k values, confirming that downweighting positive reinforcement (λ=0.1) is critical: REINFORCE scores only 92.0 Pass@256 on MATH and 50.0 on AIME 2025, well below W-REINFORCE. The key differentiator is at large k: PSR collapses (91.2 MATH, 43.3 AIME at k=256) while NSR and W-REINFORCE preserve or exceed base-model scaling, demonstrating that negative reinforcement is the dominant driver of inference-time scaling performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the REINFORCE policy gradient framework (Williams, 1992) and the PPO clipped-ratio formulation (Schulman, 2017), decomposing the standard RLVR objective into its positive and negative sample reinforcement components. It positions itself against the broader RLVR literature (DeepSeek-R1, GRPO, Kimi K1.5) by arguing that prior work has focused almost exclusively on Pass@1 or greedy accuracy, masking the distinct roles that positive and negative rewards play in shaping inference-time scaling behavior. The gradient analysis also draws a careful distinction from unlikelihood training and entropy regularization, showing that NSR's prior-guided redistribution is mechanistically different from both.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that penalizing incorrect responses alone can match or surpass full RL (PPO/GRPO) across the entire Pass@k spectrum challenges the field's default assumption that reinforcing correct trajectories is essential, and the proposed W-REINFORCE (a single hyperparameter λ scaling positive rewards) offers a drop-in replacement that is simpler than PPO/GRPO yet consistently competitive. Limitations include that the strongest results are on Qwen models with strong math priors, Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct degrades under all RL variants, and evaluation is confined to mathematical reasoning benchmarks. Open questions include whether the NSR-dominant strategy generalizes to coding, science, or other verifiable-reward domains, and whether it remains effective when the base model's prior is weak.
