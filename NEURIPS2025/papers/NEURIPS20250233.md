## Problem

The softmax-contaminated mixture-of-experts model, which underlies prefix-tuning of large pre-trained models with a trainable prompt expert, has remained theoretically unexplored. Prior work on contaminated MoE (Yan et al., AISTATS 2025) restricted analysis to input-free gating, an impractical assumption that obscures the true statistical behavior of input-dependent softmax gating. This paper asks: what are the minimax-optimal convergence rates for MLE of gating and prompt parameters, and how does knowledge overlap between the frozen pre-trained model and the trainable prompt affect estimability?

## Input / Output

The method consumes n i.i.d. covariate-response pairs (X_i, Y_i) where X_i are drawn from a known continuous distribution on a bounded subset of R^d and Y_i are generated from the softmax-contaminated MoE density (equation 1), which mixes a fixed pre-trained density f_0 with an unknown Gaussian prompt density f via an input-dependent softmax gate. A key technical choice is that the ground-truth parameters G* = (β*, τ*, η*, ν*) are allowed to vary with sample size n, enabling a uniform (rather than pointwise) minimax analysis. The output consists of MLE estimates of all four parameter groups along with explicit convergence rate bounds that depend on whether the distinguishability condition holds.

## Method

The paper introduces a novel analytic distinguishability condition (Definition 1) that formalizes when the pre-trained model f_0 and the Gaussian prompt model f have non-overlapping expertise, and a strong identifiability condition (Definition 2) on the expert function h that ensures linear independence of Taylor-expansion terms in the non-distinguishable regime. Under distinguishability, a Hellinger-distance lower bound of the form E_X[d_H(p_G, p_G*)] ≥ C·D_1(G, G*) is established via a contradiction argument combined with Fatou's lemma, yielding parametric O~(n^{-1/2}) rates for all parameters with matching minimax lower bounds via a modified Le Cam method. Under non-distinguishability (both models Gaussian with h_0 = h), the model is no longer identifiable, so the parameter space is restricted to a shrinking set Ξ(l_n), and the convergence rates degrade to O~(n^{-1/2}·‖(Δη*, Δν*)‖^{-2}) for the gating parameter and O~(n^{-1/2}·‖(Δη*, Δν*)‖^{-1}) for expert/variance parameters, where the degradation is governed by how quickly the prompt parameters approach the pre-trained ones. The key technical innovation over prior MoE analyses is letting parameters vary with n, which is necessary for a genuine minimax analysis and requires uniform rather than pointwise convergence arguments.

## Results

Under the distinguishable setting, the MLE of all gating and prompt parameters (β̂, τ̂, η̂, ν̂) achieves minimax-optimal parametric rates of Õ(n^{-1/2}), confirmed empirically with observed convergence rates of O(n^{-0.45}), O(n^{-0.52}), O(n^{-0.50}), and O(n^{-0.54}) respectively. Under the non-distinguishable setting, when the prompt parameters (η*, ν*) converge to the pre-trained parameters (η₀, ν₀) at rate Õ(n^{-1/8}), the gating parameter exp(τ̂) converges at the slower rate Õ(n^{-1/4}) (empirically O(n^{-0.23})), while the prompt and gating parameters (β̂, η̂, ν̂) converge at Õ(n^{-3/8}) (empirically ~O(n^{-0.37})). These slower rates are shown to be minimax-optimal via matching lower bounds.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its softmax-contaminated MoE against the input-free-contaminated MoE of Yan et al. [35] (AISTATS 2025), which is the only prior theoretical analysis of contaminated MoE. In the distinguishable setting, softmax gating strictly outperforms input-free gating: the expert parameter and variance estimation rates improve from Õ(n^{-1/2}(λ*)^{-1}) to Õ(n^{-1/2}), eliminating the dependence on the gating weight λ* and thus reducing sample complexity. In the non-distinguishable setting, softmax gating again wins by removing the (λ*)^{-1} factor from the expert parameter rate, yielding Õ(n^{-1/2}·‖(Δη*,Δν*)‖^{-1}) versus Õ(n^{-1/2}·‖(Δη*,Δν*)‖^{-1}(λ*)^{-1}) for input-free gating. The gating parameter rate is the same in both settings (Õ(n^{-1/2}·‖(Δη*,Δν*)‖^{-2})). Overall, the paper demonstrates that input-dependent softmax gating is uniformly more sample-efficient than input-free gating for parameter estimation in contaminated MoE models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate lines of MoE theory: (1) the contaminated MoE analysis of Yan et al. [35] and Nguyen et al. [30], which studied a frozen pre-trained expert plus a trainable prompt but under the impractical assumption of input-free gating, and (2) the softmax-gating MoE convergence analysis of Nguyen et al. [31] and [29], which handled input-dependent gating but did not consider the frozen-plus-prompt structure. The key methodological departure is the introduction of a 'distinguishability' condition (Definition 1) that captures when the prompt and pre-trained models are analytically separable, combined with a 'strong identifiability' condition (Definition 2) on the expert function to handle the non-distinguishable case. The proof technique follows the Hellinger-distance-to-parameter-loss reduction standard in [31] and the Fatou's lemma argument from Ho et al. [12], but must handle the added complexity of input-dependent gating interacting with a potentially vanishing prompt-pretrained gap.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first theoretical characterization of parameter estimation in the softmax-contaminated MoE, which is the statistical model underlying prefix-tuning and prompt-based fine-tuning of large pre-trained models. The practical takeaway is clear: softmax gating is more sample-efficient than input-free gating for contaminated MoE (removing a λ*^{-1} rate penalty), and prompts that learn the same expertise as the pre-trained model suffer fundamentally slower estimation rates. Open questions include extending the analysis to multiple simultaneous prompts, non-Gaussian prompt families, and relaxing the strong identifiability condition, which currently excludes common architectures like ReLU and certain parameterized activation functions. The results also suggest a design principle for prompt engineering: prompts should target expertise distinct from the pre-trained model to maintain estimability.
