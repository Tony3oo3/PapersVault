## Problem

Mask-based pretraining (MAE, BERT, etc.) achieves state-of-the-art results across vision, language, and biology, yet no existing theory explains why it works universally across architectures and domains, why the optimal masking ratio varies by task and model size, or what produces the characteristic 'sweet-spot' and plateau behaviors in performance curves. Prior theoretical attempts (contrastive learning, latent variable models) are domain-specific and cannot account for these quantitative phenomena or the role of model overparameterization.

## Input / Output

The framework treats each feature reconstruction as a high-dimensional regression problem: the input is a randomly masked version of a (d+1)-dimensional feature vector (where one feature is the target to reconstruct), and the output is the predicted value of the masked feature. In practice, R²MAE consumes standard inputs—RGB image patches, text tokens, DNA nucleotide sequences, or single-cell gene expression vectors—and produces learned representations evaluated via downstream classification, variant effect scoring, or cell state prediction. The key unusual aspect is that the masking ratio itself is a random variable sampled uniformly from [p_min, p_max] per training step, rather than a fixed hyperparameter.

## Method

The paper formulates feature-level mask autoencoding as ridge-less linear regression in the proportional regime (d, n → ∞ with d/n → γ), where masking simultaneously reduces the effective sample size (n·p) and induces sparsity in the design matrix. Using random matrix theory and isotropic local laws, it derives closed-form test risk expressions for isotropic and rank-1 spiked covariance models, showing that the 'sweet-spot' masking ratio arises from bias-variance cancellation in the overparameterized regime when features are correlated. Guided by the insight that different masking ratios selectively emphasize features of varying strength (alignment with the covariance spectrum), the authors propose R²MAE: a one-line modification that samples the masking ratio uniformly from a predefined range [p_min, p_max] at each training step, forcing the model to capture multi-scale features. This simple scheme consistently outperforms fixed-ratio MAE/MLM, dynamic scheduling, MDLM, and curriculum-learned masking across ViT-MAE (ImageNet), RoBERTa (GLUE), GPN-MSA (DNA variant prediction), and single-cell gene expression models.

## Results

On ImageNet (ViT-base MAE, 150 epochs), R²MAE achieves 82.00% top-1 and 96.05% top-5 fine-tuning accuracy, marginally outperforming default MAE (81.97%/96.02%) and Dynamic MR (81.97%/96.04%). On GLUE (RoBERTa-base, 10B tokens), R²MAE attains the best overall rank (1.25) with 81.9% MNLI, 90.8% QQP, 91.9% SST-2, and 88.6% QNLI. On the Brain SEA-AD single-cell benchmark, R²MAE achieves the best average score (0.697) with 0.687/0.716 Alzheimer's AUROC (cell/donor) and 0.572/0.628 age Spearman r, substantially outperforming MAE at fixed 25% MR (0.667 avg) and CL-MAE (0.654 avg). On DNA variant prediction (GPN-MSA), R²MAE achieves the best OMIM regulatory AUPRC (0.148) and pAUROC (0.649), surpassing the standard MLM baseline (0.127/0.644).

## Baselines & Comparisons

R²MAE consistently outperforms the standard fixed-ratio MAE/MLM baseline across all four domains: on ImageNet it gains +0.03% top-1 over default MAE (82.00 vs 81.97), on GLUE it improves overall rank from 3.00–3.25 to 1.25, on DNA it raises OMIM regulatory AUPRC from 0.127 to 0.148, and on single-cell it lifts the average score from 0.667 to 0.697. Against Dynamic MR (the strongest competing schedule), R²MAE wins on language (rank 1.25 vs 1.50/2.00) and single-cell (0.697 vs 0.636), ties on vision (82.00 vs 81.97), and edges ahead on DNA (pAUROC 0.649 vs 0.645). MDLM (uniform p∈[0,1]) is clearly inferior in every domain, confirming the theoretical prediction that extreme masking ratios degrade performance. CL-MAE (learnable curriculum masks) is outperformed by R²MAE on single-cell (0.697 vs 0.654) and DNA (AUPRC 0.148 vs 0.117), and combining R²MAE with CL or Dynamic MR actually hurts performance relative to R²MAE alone. Among external single-cell baselines, R²MAE surpasses scGPT (0.486 avg) and scVI (0.605 avg) by a wide margin, and on DNA it exceeds CADD (0.966 AUROC) and phyloP-100v (0.927 AUROC) on ClinVar missense prediction.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from prior theoretical analyses of mask pretraining (contrastive-learning reframings [25,27], latent-variable models [28], attention-kernel analyses [26]) by grounding the explanation in high-dimensional ridgeless linear regression and random matrix theory, building directly on the proportional-regime tools of Hastie et al. [43] and Dobriban & Wager [71]. Empirically, it extends the MAE [6] and BERT/MLM [1] pretraining paradigms across vision, language, DNA, and single-cell domains, and positions R²MAE against dynamic masking schedules [34], learnable-mask approaches [38,52], and masked diffusion language models [54]. The key conceptual departure is showing that the 'sweet-spot' masking ratio and its task/model-size dependence arise purely from bias-variance trade-offs in an overparametrized linear model, rather than from data-level or contrastive mechanisms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified linear-model framework provides the first quantitative (not just qualitative) theoretical account of mask pretraining curves that generalizes across domains and architectures, and its prediction that masking is only beneficial in the overparametrized regime is a testable hypothesis not addressed by prior explanations. R²MAE's one-line implementation (uniformly sampling the mask ratio) makes it trivially adoptable, and its consistent—albeit modest—gains across four biological and non-biological domains suggest broad practical utility. Open questions include extending the theory to autoregressive pretraining, characterizing R²MAE's test risk analytically, and whether domain-specific mask-range tuning can yield larger improvements.
