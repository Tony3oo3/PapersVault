## Problem

Existing parameter importance methods (weight magnitude, first-order gradients) treat each parameter in isolation and ignore cooperative synergies between parameters that are critical in overparameterized networks. While the Shapley value from cooperative game theory naturally captures these interactions, exact computation requires O(2^M) subset evaluations, making it infeasible for billion-parameter models. This paper bridges the gap by deriving a tractable second-order closed-form approximation that recovers Shapley-style attribution in a single backpropagation pass.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a trained model checkpoint and a small probe/validation dataset of input-label pairs (e.g., GSM8K math problems or CIFAR-100 images), from which it computes empirical loss, gradients, and a blockwise Fisher Information Matrix. It produces per-parameter Shapley importance scores decomposed into an individual-importance term (gradient × parameter value) and a cooperative-interaction term (Hessian/Fisher quadratic form), which can be aggregated at neuron, layer, or attention-head granularity. In streaming or memory-constrained settings, scores are maintained via an exponentially weighted moving average over mini-batches rather than requiring the full dataset at once.

## Method

MODEL SHAPLEY formulates each parameter as a player in a cooperative game and derives a closed-form Shapley value via a path-integrated second-order approximation: the loss change from removing a parameter (or subset) is expressed as a line integral of the gradient along a linear interpolation path, which collapses to a quadratic form involving the gradient, the Hessian diagonal, and off-diagonal Hessian entries weighted by path-averaged curvature coefficients. To make this scalable to billion-parameter models, the full Hessian is replaced by the empirical Fisher Information Matrix computed in a blockwise fashion (one block per neuron group or attention head), and the resulting Hessian-vector product is evaluated in O(M) time. A Monte Carlo incremental scheme with EWMA smoothing allows online updates of Shapley scores across mini-batches without retraining. The final per-parameter score decomposes into an individual-importance component (−g_i·θ_i, matching classical saliency) and a cooperative-interaction component (−½·θ_i·(H×θ)_i), with the latter capturing cross-parameter synergy that first-order methods miss.

## Results

On the inference (neuron deactivation) setting, MODEL SHAPLEY achieves 80.84% on CIFAR-100 (ViT-Base/16) and 73.39% on GSM8K (Qwen2.5-7B), outperforming the best baseline (Gradient Trace) by 4.19 and 0.68 points respectively. In the training (targeted fine-tuning of top-10% neurons) setting, MODEL SHAPLEY reaches 86.53% on CIFAR-100 and 73.89% on MMLU (Qwen2.5-7B), surpassing Gradient Trace by 1.84 and 3.10 points. For quantization on GSM8K, MODEL SHAPLEY-guided compression achieves 72.93% under INT8 (7B) and 78.85% under FP8 (14B), exceeding GPTQ and OBD by 1.66 and 1.37 points respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Gradient baseline (LeCun et al., 1989), MODEL SHAPLEY wins decisively across all settings: e.g., 80.84 vs 77.82 on CIFAR-100 inference and 73.89 vs 69.08 on MMLU training (Qwen-7B). Against Gradient Trace (Leng et al., 2025), MODEL SHAPLEY also consistently leads, with the largest gap of 3.10 points on MMLU training (73.89 vs 70.79) and 4.19 points on CIFAR-100 inference (80.84 vs 76.65). In the quantization comparison (Table 2), MODEL SHAPLEY outperforms both GPTQ (Frantar et al., 2023) and OBD (LeCun et al., 1990) under INT8 and FP8 for both 7B and 14B models (e.g., 76.27 vs 75.66 GPTQ and 75.36 OBD on INT8 14B). However, under the extreme INT4 regime for the 14B model, GPTQ (65.20) still edges out MODEL SHAPLEY (63.84), which the authors attribute to the 16-level codebook causing dynamic-range collapse for the heavy-tailed Shapley magnitudes. Overall, MODEL SHAPLEY achieves the best or near-best result in every reported configuration, with the most pronounced gains in the training and INT8/FP8 compression settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Shapley value framework from its traditional applications at the data-sample and feature level (SHAP, Data Shapley) to the parameter level of neural networks, building on the cooperative game-theoretic foundation of Shapley (1953) and the path-integral formulation of Integrated Gradients (Sundararajan et al., 2017). It departs from prior neuron-level Shapley methods (Neuron Shapley, Shapley Pruning) that relied on Monte Carlo or bandit-style estimators infeasible at LLM scale, by deriving a closed-form second-order approximation computable via a single backpropagation pass augmented with a Hessian-vector product. The method also extends first-order parameter importance heuristics (OBS, OBD, gradient trace) by explicitly modeling cross-parameter cooperative interactions through off-diagonal Hessian/Fisher entries, and integrates with the GPTQ quantization pipeline for Shapley-guided compression.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a principled, scalable route to parameter-level attribution in billion-parameter LLMs, with demonstrated utility across interpretability, targeted fine-tuning (10% of neurons nearly matching full fine-tuning on MMLU), and quantization. Key limitations include the reliance on the empirical Fisher Information Matrix as a Hessian surrogate, which may degrade under non-stationary training or in heavily overparameterized regimes, and the observed failure of Shapley-guided INT4 quantization due to dynamic-range collapse in the 16-level codebook. Open questions include developing adaptive Hessian surrogates, extending to multi-task and multi-modal settings, and understanding how Shapley values evolve under continual learning—the 'parameter compensation effect' observed here suggests importance is dynamic rather than static.
