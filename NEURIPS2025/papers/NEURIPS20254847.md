## Problem

Existing concept erasure methods remain vulnerable to nonlinear adversaries because they fail to fully capture the complex, nonlinear statistical dependencies between learned representations and unwanted attributes. Additionally, while a utility-erasure trade-off is expected, its progression during the erasure process—the cost of erasure—has not been studied, leaving the dynamics between attribute protection and utility preservation unexplored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254847/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Obliviator operates post-hoc on frozen (or optionally fine-tuned) PLM representations of varying dimensions (300–4096) from BERT, GPT-2, DeepSeek-LLM, LLaMA, and DeepMoji, consuming sensitive attribute labels (gender, race) and optionally target task labels (profession, sentiment, mention). It produces a sequence of intermediate erased representations across iterations, from which utility-erasure trade-off curves are extracted by probing with nonlinear adversaries (SVM with RBF kernel and MLP) at each step until probe accuracy reaches random chance. Kernel matrices are approximated via Random Fourier Features (2500–6000 dimensions initially, reduced to 1500 in later steps) to keep computation tractable.

## Method

Obliviator formulates erasure from a functional perspective: it seeks a representation ε(X) such that no pair of witness functions from a characteristic RKHS can linearize the relationship between the representation and the sensitive attribute, equivalent to driving HSIC to zero. This yields a nested optimization (inf over encoder parameters, sup over witness functions) that lacks a closed-form solution, so Obliviator solves it via a two-step iterative procedure. In step one, an MLP encoder is trained with a multi-objective loss that minimizes HSIC between the output and the sensitive attribute while maximizing HSIC with task-relevant proxies (Y explicitly in supervised mode; X and X^i implicitly in unsupervised mode). In step two, a constrained eigenvalue problem in RKHS finds witness functions that re-align the representation to make task-relevant information more accessible for the next encoder iteration, while enforcing orthogonality to the sensitive-attribute cross-covariance null space. This iterative morphing of the feature space gradually achieves full nonlinear guardedness while preserving utility, and the intermediate representations enable tracking the utility-erasure trade-off curve.

## Results

Obliviator is the only method that achieves full nonlinearly guarded erasure, reaching random-chance accuracy on the sensitive attribute across all datasets and PLMs, while all baselines leave a residual gap of 8–14% above random chance (e.g., 12%, 13%, 14% for DIAL-MENTION, DIAL-SENTIMENT, and BIAS IN BIOS in the fine-tuned+supervised setting). Obliviator consistently achieves higher target-task accuracy at every level of sensitive-attribute accuracy compared to all baselines. On GPT-2 representations, FaRM and KRaM degrade to near random-chance accuracy on the target task, while Obliviator maintains strong utility. On better-disentangled representations from LLaMa and DeepSeek, Obliviator's trade-off curve shifts favorably, indicating more utility-preserving erasure.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Obliviator outperforms all five baselines (INLP, AdS, kSAL, FaRM, KRaM) on every dataset and PLM tested, achieving higher task accuracy at every level of erasure. INLP (iterative nullspace projection) and kSAL (spectral attribute removal) are linearly guarded and fail to protect against nonlinear adversaries, leaving 8–14% residual sensitive-attribute accuracy. AdS (adversarial scrubbing) and FaRM (rate-distortion maximization) require fine-tuning the PLM yet still leave the sensitive attribute partially recoverable; on GPT-2, both FaRM and KRaM collapse to near random-chance target-task accuracy. KRaM (kernelized rate-distortion) shows some improvement over FaRM on LLaMa and DeepSeek but still fails to reach full erasure. Obliviator's advantage stems from directly minimizing HSIC to zero via a two-step iterative procedure (encoder training + RKHS disentanglement), which no baseline replicates.

## Relation to Prior Work

Obliviator extends the concept erasure lineage from linearly guarded methods (INLP, R-LACE, LEACE) and partially nonlinear methods (kSAL, KCE, AdS, FaRM, KRaM) by formulating erasure as a cascading kernel problem in RKHS that directly minimizes HSIC to zero, guaranteeing statistical independence from a functional perspective. Unlike prior kernel-based approaches that map representations into an RKHS and then perform linear erasure (protecting only against linear adversaries in that space), Obliviator requires the representation to remain independent even after an adversarially chosen feature map is applied. The two-step iterative procedure—alternating between an encoder trained with a multi-objective HSIC loss and an eigenvalue-based RKHS disentanglement step—is a novel optimization strategy for this non-convex, nested problem that lacks a closed-form solution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first post-hoc method to achieve provable nonlinear guardedness (HSIC → 0), closing a gap where even fine-tuned adversarial methods (AdS, FaRM) left 8–14% recoverable sensitive information. The utility-erasure trade-off curves introduced here provide a new diagnostic lens: the finding that the trade-off is a property of the model's learned dependencies rather than an inherent data constraint suggests that better pretraining can reduce the cost of erasure, which has practical implications for model deployment. Open questions include whether the approach scales to multi-attribute erasure, whether the RFF approximation introduces meaningful gaps versus exact kernel computation at scale, and whether the iterative procedure can be accelerated or made differentiable end-to-end.
