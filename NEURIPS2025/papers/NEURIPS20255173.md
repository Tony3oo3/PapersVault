## Problem

Pre-trained Diffusion Transformers (DiTs) like FLUX.1-dev rely on full self-attention with quadratic complexity, causing prohibitive latency for high-resolution generation (over 30 minutes for 8K images). It remains unclear whether existing efficient attention mechanisms—designed for NLP or trained-from-scratch vision models—can be directly applied to pre-trained DiTs without severe quality degradation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255173/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt (and optionally a condition image for ControlNet or a low-resolution image for SDEdit-based upscaling) and produces a high-resolution RGB image, with demonstrated generation up to 8192×8192. Training requires only 10K self-generated 1024×1024 samples from the teacher DiT itself, and the resulting linearized model generalizes zero-shot to other model variants (e.g., FLUX.1-schnell) and plugins (e.g., ControlNet) without further adaptation.

## Method

CLEAR replaces full self-attention in a pre-trained DiT with a circular-window local attention: each image query attends only to key-value tokens within a Euclidean radius r, while text queries retain full access to all tokens, yielding linear complexity in image resolution. The design is motivated by four empirically identified factors essential for linearizing pre-trained DiTs—locality, formulation consistency (retaining softmax), high-rank attention maps, and feature integrity—which make Neighborhood Attention the only existing scheme satisfying all constraints. Training uses knowledge distillation with a composite loss (flow matching + prediction consistency + per-layer attention consistency), fine-tuning only attention-layer parameters for 10K iterations on 10K self-generated samples, reducing attention FLOPS by 99.5% and accelerating 8K generation by 6.3× while matching the teacher's quality.

## Results

On COCO2014 validation (5,000 images at 1024×1024), CLEAR(r=32) with knowledge distillation achieves FID of 8.85 and LPIPS of 0.46 against the original FLUX-1.dev, with CLIP-I similarity of 92.18 and DINO similarity of 85.44, while reducing GFLOPS from 260.9 to 154.1. At 8K resolution, CLEAR reduces attention computations by 99.5% and accelerates generation by 6.3× over the original DiT. The lighter CLEAR(r=16) variant (80.6 GFLOPS) still achieves FID of 13.72 and CLIP-I of 88.53 against the original, demonstrating a strong quality-efficiency trade-off.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CLEAR decisively outperforms all six efficient attention baselines on every metric. Sigmoid Attention and Linear Attention fail catastrophically (FID vs. original of 447.80 and 324.54 respectively), confirming that formulation inconsistency is fatal for pre-trained DiTs. Among viable baselines, PixArt-Sigma (FID 30.64) and Swin Transformer (FID 18.90) are the closest competitors, but CLEAR(r=32) w/ distill (FID 8.85, CLIP-I 92.18, DINO 85.44) substantially surpasses them while also reducing GFLOPS. Against the original FLUX-1.dev, CLEAR(r=32) w/ distill achieves near-parity on CLIP-T (31.00 vs. 31.06) and even exceeds it on IS (39.12 vs. 38.25), while CLEAR(r=16) w/ distill cuts GFLOPS by 69% (80.6 vs. 260.9) with only modest quality loss. The paper further demonstrates zero-shot transfer: CLEAR layers trained on FLUX-1.dev generalize to FLUX-1.schnell (FID vs. original 12.51) and to pre-trained ControlNet (FID vs. GT 40.45 with r=16) without any additional training.

## Relation to Prior Work

CLEAR is built directly on Neighborhood Attention (Hassani et al., CVPR 2023), replacing its square sliding window with a circular Euclidean-radius window to reduce FLOPS by ~π/4 while preserving the four properties the authors identify as essential for pre-trained DiT linearization. It departs from the broader efficient-attention literature (linear attention, KV compression, KV sampling) by demonstrating empirically that those strategies fail specifically on pre-trained DiTs due to formulation inconsistency, low-rank attention maps, or loss of feature integrity—unlike their success in UNet-based diffusion models or DiTs trained from scratch (e.g., SANA, LinFusion). The paper positions itself as the first linearization strategy tailored to an already-trained DiT, using knowledge distillation on self-generated samples rather than architectural redesign from the ground up.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: a 6.3× speedup at 8K resolution with 99.5% reduction in attention FLOPS, while maintaining near-identical generation quality to FLUX.1-dev, and the distilled layers transfer zero-shot to FLUX.1-schnell and ControlNet without retraining. The four-factor diagnostic (locality, formulation consistency, high-rank attention maps, feature integrity) provides a reusable framework for understanding why certain efficient-attention substitutions fail on pre-trained DiTs. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond the FLUX/SD3 family, whether hybrid attention with coarse tokens is the right fix for small-r layout issues, and whether fused CUDA kernels can close the gap between theoretical FLOPS savings and measured wall-clock speedup, which is notably smaller at lower resolutions.
