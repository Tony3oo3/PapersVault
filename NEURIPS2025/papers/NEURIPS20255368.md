## Problem

While trustworthiness benchmarks exist for LLMs (DecodingTrust) and image models (MMDT), video foundation models lack a comprehensive evaluation framework. Video-specific risks—such as temporal harm that only emerges across frames, physical harm from flashing content, and location inference from driving scenes—cannot be captured by static-image or text-only benchmarks. VMDT fills this gap as the first unified platform evaluating both text-to-video and video-to-text models across five trustworthiness dimensions simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255368/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For T2V models, inputs are text prompts (ranging from 329 adversarial to 1,650 hallucination samples) that generate short videos (<10 s), from which 5–10 frames are sampled for LLM-as-judge evaluation. For V2T models, inputs are video clips paired with task-specific prompts or multiple-choice questions (up to 5,008 fairness pairs), with adversarial variants carrying imperceptible per-frame perturbations. Outputs are structured as per-dimension scores: bypass rate and harmful generation rate (safety), per-scenario accuracy (hallucination), F1/F2/O fairness scores in [-1,1] and [0,1] (fairness), ℓ₂ distance and cosine similarity plus weighted location-inference accuracy (privacy), and benign-vs-adversarial performance drop (robustness), aggregated into a single trustworthiness profile per model.

## Method

VMDT is a curated benchmark and evaluation protocol rather than a trained model. It constructs dimension-specific datasets with diverse scenarios: safety uses a 13/27-category risk taxonomy with vanilla and transformed (jailbreak-style) prompts plus synthetic and real video content; hallucination spans six scenarios (natural selection, distraction, misleading, counterfactual, temporal, co-occurrence, OCR) across five tasks; fairness covers stereotype, decision-making, and 'overkill fairness' (sacrificing factual accuracy for diversity); privacy tests training-data memorization via pixel-level ℓ₂/cosine metrics and geolocation inference from driving videos; adversarial robustness uses attacks on surrogate models (greedy, genetic, gradient-based for T2V; FMM-Attack for V2T) to generate transferable adversarial inputs. Evaluation employs LLM-as-judge (GPT-4o at 86–88% human agreement for safety; Qwen2.5-VL-72B-Instruct at 0.765 Pearson correlation for T2V hallucination) and keyword matching for V2T multiple-choice tasks. The framework evaluates 7 T2V and 19 V2T models, enabling cross-perspective profiling and size-correlation analysis across all five dimensions in a single unified report.

## Results

Across 7 T2V and 19 V2T models evaluated on five trustworthiness dimensions, no model exceeds ~73 points on average. For T2V, Luma achieves the highest overall trustworthiness score (70.1) while CogVideoX-5B is lowest (55.7); for V2T, InternVL2.5-78B leads (72.7) and Qwen2.5-VL-3B-Instruct trails (65.3). On safety, all four open-source T2V models show a 100% bypass rate (BR=1.00) with no refusal mechanism, while Nova Reel achieves the best safety (BR=0.37, HGR=0.10). On hallucination, Luma leads T2V models with 67.6% average accuracy, and InternVL2.5-78B is the best V2T model, outperforming even closed-source GPT-4o and Claude-3.5-Sonnet.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among T2V models, closed-source models (Luma, Pika, Nova Reel) substantially outperform open-source models (VideoCrafter2, CogVideoX-5B, OpenSora 1.2, Vchitect-2.0) on safety: all open-source models have a 100% bypass rate, while Nova Reel achieves only 37% average bypass and 10% harmful generation rate. On hallucination, Luma (67.6% avg accuracy) and Pika (63.0%) far exceed open-source models (35.7–49.0%), with the gap most pronounced on OCR and counterfactual scenarios. For V2T models, the open-source InternVL2.5-78B outperforms all closed-source models (GPT-4o, Claude-3.5-Sonnet, Nova Pro) on hallucination and adversarial robustness, reversing the T2V pattern. Size-dependent trends are mixed: hallucination and adversarial robustness improve with model size within a family (e.g., InternVL2.5-78B > 8B), while fairness and privacy risks worsen with scale (Pearson r=0.544 for location inference vs. size, p=0.016). Safety shows no significant correlation with model size, indicating that alignment techniques rather than scale govern current safety levels.

## Relation to Prior Work

VMDT directly extends the MMDT framework (ICLR 2025), which established a unified trustworthiness evaluation for text-to-image and image-to-text models, to the video modality (text-to-video and video-to-text). It also draws on video-specific safety benchmarks (SafeWatch-Bench, T2VSafetyBench) and industry risk taxonomies (Stability AI, OpenAI policies) to construct video-appropriate evaluation scenarios, including temporal risks and physical harm (e.g., photosensitive epilepsy triggers) that are undetectable in static frames. The broader lineage traces back to LLM trustworthiness benchmarks (DecodingTrust, TrustGPT, AIR-Bench), but those and MMDT left video as an unaddressed gap, which this paper fills with dedicated datasets and LLM-as-judge evaluation protocols (GPT-4o, Qwen2.5-VL-72B).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As video foundation models move toward production deployment, the absence of a systematic trustworthiness benchmark has left developers without a shared yardstick for safety, fairness, privacy, and robustness. The finding that safety shows no correlation with model size—while fairness and privacy risks actually worsen with scale—challenges the prevailing assumption that simply scaling up will yield more trustworthy models and points to the need for targeted alignment techniques. Even the best-performing models score only ~70–73 out of 100, and all open-source T2V models universally lack refusal mechanisms, underscoring that the field is far from deployment-ready. Open questions include how to develop video-specific safety guardrails that handle temporal and physical harm, how to resolve the fairness–overkill-fairness trade-off, and whether the observed size-dependent trends will persist as architectures evolve.
