## Problem

Self-supervised video representation learning for echocardiography is hindered by high inter-frame similarity (mean cosine similarity ~0.99), low signal-to-noise ratio, and the subtlety of clinically relevant anatomical features that existing SSL paradigms fail to capture. Masked modeling reconstructs low-level textures, contrastive learning suffers from representation collapse due to limited effective augmentations, and clustering-based methods rely on aggressive augmentations that distort fine-grained cardiac structures. No domain-specific pre-trained model existed for cardiac ultrasound video prior to this work.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 64-frame echocardiography video clips at 112×112 resolution (sampled at stride 3), tokenized into 2×16×16 3D tubelets for the video branch and per-frame 2D patches for the image branch, with a 90% masking ratio. The primary output is a spatio-temporal video representation (CLS token + token-level features) that supports multiple downstream heads: a kNN or linear classifier for anomaly detection, a linear+Conv2D upsampling head for pixel-level segmentation masks, and a linear regression head for ejection fraction estimation. All downstream evaluation uses the frozen backbone, with only the lightweight head trained.

## Method

DISCOVR is a dual-branch self-supervised framework combining a ViT-based video encoder with a parallel DINO-style image encoder, unified by a novel Semantic Cluster Distillation (SCD) loss. The video branch performs masked self-distillation: a student processes multiple randomly masked video variants while an EMA-updated teacher sees the full clip, aligning their CLS-token probability distributions via temperature-scaled softmax and cross-entropy. The image branch independently learns fine-grained spatial semantics through masked image self-distillation on individual frames. The SCD loss bridges the two branches by projecting both the video decoder's reconstructed token features and the image encoder's spatial features onto a shared set of 3000 learnable prototypes, converting similarity scores into Sinkhorn-Knopp soft cluster assignments, and symmetrically minimizing cross-entropy between the two modalities' cluster distributions. Gradients flow only through the video model and prototypes, so the image encoder evolves solely via its own self-distillation loss, providing continually updated semantic guidance without labels, pretrained weights, or heavy augmentations.

## Results

On linear probing across five echocardiography datasets, DISCOVR achieves F1 scores of 65.39 (FetalEcho1), 63.59 (FetalEcho2), 77.63 (EchoNet-Dynamic), 61.66 (EchoPediatric-LVH), and 62.65 (RVENET), outperforming the best baseline (SIGMA) by 2.1–3.2 points. In zero-shot kNN evaluation, DISCOVR reaches 61.45 F1 on EchoNet-Dynamic and 61.79 on FetalEcho1, surpassing the strongest baselines (MVD, MGMAE) by 2–4 points. For LVEF regression, DISCOVR attains a MAE of 7.79 under linear probing and 6.32 with partial fine-tuning, beating fully supervised EchoNet-Dynamic (EF-only, 7.35) and MC3 (6.59). On CAMUS segmentation, DISCOVR achieves a Dice of 0.844 with a simple linear+upsampling head, exceeding UNet (0.816) and DeepLabV3 (0.819).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DISCOVR consistently outperforms all video SSL baselines (VideoMAE, MGMAE, SIGMA) in both linear probing and zero-shot settings across all five echocardiography datasets, with the largest margins on FetalEcho2 (F1 +3.2 over MGMAE in linear probing) and EchoNet-Dynamic (F1 +2.1 over SIGMA). In zero-shot evaluation, DISCOVR also surpasses the image-based RAD-DINO (e.g., F1 61.45 vs 59.34 on EchoNet-Dynamic) and the pretrained-teacher-based MVD (F1 61.45 vs 57.56 on EchoNet-Dynamic), confirming that its self-distillation + SCD pipeline captures clinically relevant features without external supervision. For anomaly detection, DISCOVR beats the specialized detectors C2FPL (F1 61.45 vs 57.35 on EchoNet-Dynamic), MNAD (61.45 vs 52.08), and MemAE (61.45 vs 46.33) using only a simple kNN classifier at inference. On LVEF prediction, DISCOVR with partial fine-tuning (MAE 6.32) surpasses fully supervised MC3 (6.59) and the EF-only EchoNet-Dynamic model (7.35), though it trails the full multi-task EchoNet-Dynamic (4.05) which leverages 20K segmentation tracings. On Kinetics-400 zero-shot KNN, DISCOVR achieves 22.3% Top-1 in 400 epochs, outperforming MVD (18.7%, 1600 epochs), MME (19.1%, 800 epochs), and VideoMAE (20.7%, 800 epochs), demonstrating efficient generalization to natural video.

## Relation to Prior Work

DISCOVR sits at the intersection of three SSL threads: DINO-style student-teacher self-distillation (Caron et al., 2021), Sinkhorn-guided video clustering (SIGMA, Salehi et al., 2024), and space-time tube tokenization from VideoMAE (Tong et al., 2022). Its key departure is the dual-branch architecture that couples a video encoder with a parallel image encoder through a novel Semantic Cluster Distillation (SCD) loss, which uses Sinkhorn-Knopp soft cluster alignment to transfer fine-grained spatial semantics into the video representation. This contrasts with prior medical video SSL (Jiao et al., 2020; EchoFlow) that relies on handcrafted pretext tasks or frozen teachers, and with natural-video clustering methods that lack explicit spatial semantic guidance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DISCOVR is the first self-supervised video representation model evaluated comprehensively across fetal, pediatric, and adult echocardiography, and its ability to rival fully supervised LVEF models (MAE 6.32 vs. 7.35 for EchoNet-Dynamic) with only partial fine-tuning suggests a practical path toward deployable cardiac screening tools without large labeled datasets. The SCD mechanism—distilling an evolving image encoder's spatial clusters into a video encoder—is a clean, potentially transferable idea beyond echocardiography. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to other ultrasound modalities or 3D volumes, whether the 64-frame clip length is optimal for all cardiac views, and how performance holds under broader demographic and equipment diversity beyond the five clinical sites used here.
