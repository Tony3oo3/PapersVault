## Problem

Existing VLN-CE methods rely on static navigation graphs or flat scene representations that cannot dynamically adapt to new observations, and their planners use heuristics that lack theoretical guarantees when balancing semantic instruction alignment with spatial accessibility. VLM-based approaches improve instruction grounding but process observations without structured multi-level spatial reasoning, leading to poor generalization in long-horizon, cluttered, or unseen indoor environments.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252070/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each timestep the agent consumes 12 RGB images and 12 depth maps captured at 30° heading intervals (panoramic RGB-D), a natural-language instruction of variable length, and its 2D pose (x, y, θ) from the Habitat simulator on Matterport3D meshes. The system internally constructs a three-level scene graph (object → region → zone) and a topological path, then outputs a sequence of discrete low-level actions: FORWARD 0.25 m, ROTATE LEFT/RIGHT 15°, or STOP, executed until the target is reached or a step budget (150 for R2R-CE, 250 for RxR-CE) is exhausted.

## Method

HSAN builds a dynamic hierarchical semantic scene graph bottom-up: Grounded-SAM detects objects, LLaVA-Onevision generates rich textual descriptions, and SigLIP extracts visual features; objects are clustered into regions via spectral clustering on a Gaussian-kernel similarity graph (σ=0.5, λ=0.2), and regions are aggregated into zones by a connectivity-based algorithm with VLM-assisted semantic verification. Long-term goal selection is formulated as an optimal transport problem between a uniform distribution over ghost nodes and a semantically biased target distribution, with a cost matrix combining geodesic distance, an exploration penalty, and a BERT-based semantic relevance score; the problem is solved via the Sinkhorn algorithm, and a Kantorovich-duality-based theorem (Theorem 3.1) guarantees optimality of the selected goal. A graph-aware RL policy, trained with PPO, encodes the local subgraph through a 3-layer GCN pre-trained on Matterport3D connectivity, concatenates this with SigLIP visual features and the next-subgoal position, and outputs action probabilities; a 'Tryout' heuristic handles collisions by sweeping alternative headings. The full system is pre-trained on Matterport3D and fine-tuned on R2R-CE/RxR-CE using a student-forcing loss that combines the planning objective with the PPO policy loss (λ_RL = 0.1).

## Results

On R2R-CE validation unseen, HSAN achieves a Success Rate (SR) of 64%, SPL of 0.59, Navigation Error (NE) of 3.28 m, and Oracle Success Rate (OSR) of 71%, setting new state-of-the-art across all four metrics. On the more challenging RxR-CE (multilingual, longer paths), HSAN reaches SR 59%, SPL 0.54, NE 3.76 m, and OSR 66%. On the RxR-CE multilingual subset (English/Hindi/Telugu), HSAN attains SR 57% and SPL 0.52, and on the R2R-CE high-clutter subset it reaches SR 61% and SPL 0.56, both substantially ahead of the strongest baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HSAN outperforms all six baselines on every metric across both R2R-CE and RxR-CE. The closest competitor is LLaVA-Nav (Shah et al., CoRL 2023), which HSAN surpasses by 6 percentage points in SR on both datasets (64% vs. 58% on R2R-CE; 59% vs. 53% on RxR-CE) and by 0.06 in SPL. GraphNav (Wu et al., IEEE TMM 2024), the strongest graph-based baseline, trails HSAN by 8% SR on R2R-CE and 8% on RxR-CE, indicating that the optimal-transport planner and hierarchical scene graph provide meaningful gains over latent semantic alignment alone. Earlier topological and semantic-map methods (NTS, SMN) are 10–13% behind in SR, reflecting the cost of static or coarse-grained representations. The earliest continuous-environment baseline, CMM (Krantz et al., ECCV 2020), is 22% below HSAN in SR on R2R-CE, underscoring the cumulative benefit of hierarchical semantics, OT-based planning, and graph-aware control. On the multilingual RxR-CE subset, HSAN's 57% SR exceeds LLaVA-Nav (51%) and GraphNav (49%), while on the high-clutter R2R-CE subset its 61% SR leads LLaVA-Nav (54%) by 7 points, confirming robustness to linguistic diversity and dense obstacle layouts.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper synthesizes three previously separate threads in VLN-CE: (1) topological SLAM and graph-based navigation (Chaplot et al. 2020, Wu et al. 2024), which typically use static or incrementally built maps; (2) VLM-based instruction grounding (LLaVA-Nav, Majumdar et al. 2020), which processes observations in a flat manner without structured spatial hierarchy; and (3) optimal transport theory (Villani 2008, Cuturi 2013), repurposed here for goal-node selection rather than its traditional role in distribution matching. The key departure is the hierarchical (object → region → zone) scene graph with VLM-generated semantics at each level, combined with a Sinkhorn-based planner that the authors frame as providing Kantorovich-duality guarantees—though the diagonal cost matrix reduces the problem to a weighted assignment, a notable simplification relative to the full OT formulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a real gap: VLM-based navigators excel at instruction grounding but lack structured multi-scale spatial reasoning, while graph-based planners lack semantic richness. The 6% SR improvement over the strongest baseline (LLaVA-Nav) across both R2R-CE and RxR-CE, with consistent gains in multilingual and high-clutter subsets, suggests the hierarchical + OT combination is practically useful. However, the computational overhead of real-time graph construction (5 FPS inference), the simulation-only evaluation (Habitat/Matterport3D), and the simplified OT formulation (diagonal cost) limit immediate real-world applicability. Open questions include whether the hierarchical graph scales to large multi-room environments, how the approach handles dynamic obstacles, and whether the theoretical guarantees survive the practical approximations (discretized geodesic distances, heuristic zone assignment).
