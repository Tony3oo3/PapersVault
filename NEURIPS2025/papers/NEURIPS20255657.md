## Problem

Safety-aligned LLMs remain vulnerable to harmful fine-tuning attacks, where even a small fraction of harmful data in a fine-tuning dataset compromises the model's safety alignment. Existing pre-fine-tuning defenses (e.g., Vaccine, RepNoise) are fragile: with sufficient fine-tuning steps, the model still learns harmful knowledge. The paper identifies that while random post-fine-tuning perturbation can restore safety, it significantly degrades fine-tuning performance, leaving open the question of how to craft a perturbation that recovers safety without sacrificing downstream utility.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a fine-tuned model's LoRA parameters (rank 32, alpha 4), a harmful dataset of instruction-response pairs (1,000 samples from BeaverTails), and the fine-tuning dataset (which may contain up to 20% harmful data). It produces a re-aligned model by adding an L2-norm-bounded adaptive perturbation (‖ε‖ ≤ ρ) to the fine-tuned weights. Because the perturbation is optimized concurrently during the fine-tuning epochs, no additional training budget is required beyond the standard user fine-tuning run, and the method can be applied directly to pre-aligned LLMs (e.g., Llama2-7B-Chat) without access to the alignment stage.

## Method

Panacea formulates a max-maximize bilevel optimization: the outer problem updates model weights w to maximize the increase in harmful loss h(w+ε)−h(w) while minimizing fine-tuning loss g(w), and the inner problem finds the perturbation ε that maximally increases harmful loss subject to ‖ε‖≤ρ. The inner problem admits a closed-form solution—ε* = ρ·∇h(w)/‖∇h(w)‖—directing the perturbation along the harmful-loss gradient. The outer problem is solved via gradient ascent with a combined gradient that includes the perturbation-induced harmful-loss ascent term (weighted by λ) minus the fine-tuning gradient. This alternating scheme is executed concurrently within the standard fine-tuning epochs, so the perturbation is learned 'for free' during normal training and simply added to the final weights. The key distinction from prior post-fine-tuning methods (Safe LoRA, Antidote) is that the perturbation is adaptively optimized to maximally increase harmful loss rather than being derived from a fixed safety subspace or prior knowledge.

## Results

On the primary evaluation (Llama2-7B, GSM8K fine-tuning, 10% harmful ratio), Panacea achieves a harmful score of 20.1 while maintaining a fine-tuning accuracy of 16.7, compared to SFT's 45.8 HS and 16.2 FA. Across all harmful ratios (0–0.2), Panacea reduces the average harmful score by 21.2% relative to SFT (19.1 vs. 40.3) while improving average fine-tuning accuracy by 0.4% (16.2 vs. 15.8). On AdvBench, Panacea drives the harmful score to as low as 0.00% at a 5% harmful ratio, and on Sorry-Bench it achieves the lowest fulfillment rate (33.41–54.32) across all settings. The method generalizes across LLMs, reducing average harmful scores by 25.7% (Llama2-7B), 27.1% (Gemma2-9B), and 7.3% (Qwen2-7B) relative to SFT.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Panacea outperforms all alignment-stage defenses (Vaccine, RepNoise, Booster) on harmful score across every harmful ratio, with the largest gap over Vaccine (19.1 vs. 27.8 avg HS) and Booster (19.1 vs. 39.6 avg HS); however, Vaccine and Booster sacrifice fine-tuning accuracy (13.6 and 17.3 avg FA respectively) while Panacea maintains 16.2. Against the post-fine-tuning baseline Antidote, Panacea achieves a lower average HS (19.1 vs. 22.3) with comparable FA (16.2 vs. 16.3). On already-aligned LLMs (Table 4), Panacea reduces average HS to 23.7 versus Safe LoRA's 44.5 and Antidote's 30.5, while incurring only a 0.2% FA drop. On AdvBench, Panacea dominates ConstrainSFT at every ratio (e.g., 1.54 vs. 12.12 at ratio 0.1; 10.58 vs. 34.81 at ratio 0.2). The one case where Panacea does not win on HS is SaLoRA (Table 15), which achieves near-zero HS (0.9–1.1) but at the cost of severe FA degradation (1.4–3.9 vs. Panacea's 16.2–17.0), indicating SaLoRA effectively destroys the model's utility.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant alignment-stage defense paradigm (Vaccine, RepNoise, Booster) by demonstrating that such 'vaccination' approaches are fragile under sustained fine-tuning, and instead operates in the post-fine-tuning stage. It builds on the observation that post-fine-tuning methods like Antidote and Safe LoRA add perturbations based on prior knowledge (safety subspaces, critical parameters), whereas Panacea formulates a bilevel max-max optimization to learn an adaptive perturbation that explicitly maximizes harmful loss increase without relying on such priors. The closest prior work is Security Vectors, which also adds a perturbation after fine-tuning, but Panacea differs by learning the perturbation during fine-tuning and by explicitly maximizing the harmful loss ascent rather than distilling harmful knowledge into a removable component.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that Panacea can be applied to already-aligned models (Llama2-Chat, Gemma2-It, Qwen2-Instruct) without requiring access to the alignment stage, making it deployable in real fine-tuning-as-a-service pipelines. The layer-wise safety visualization provides interpretability that aligns with and extends prior findings on safety-critical layers, suggesting targeted defense strategies. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to multi-turn or iterative fine-tuning attacks, how it scales to much larger models (70B+), and whether the required harmful dataset access is realistic in all deployment scenarios. The modest additional compute (0.25h) and memory (comparable to SFT) make it practical, but the need for a separate harmful dataset during fine-tuning remains a deployment constraint.
