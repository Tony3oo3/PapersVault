## Problem

Prior work on Linear Mode Connectivity (LMC) has relied primarily on discrete neuron permutations to align independently trained models, but this approach is insufficient for modern architectures like Transformers, where richer component-specific symmetries (orthogonal, semi-permutation, invertible) govern the parameter space. The paper asks whether a unified framework capturing these broader symmetry classes can reveal low- or zero-barrier linear interpolation paths between independently trained Transformers—a question that permutation-only methods have failed to answer.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254546/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes two (or M) independently trained Transformer models of the same architecture (ViT or GPT-2) and, for learned matching, a training dataset to supervise alignment. It produces a set of symmetry-constrained transformation matrices—one global orthogonal matrix for the residual stream, per-layer permutation matrices for feed-forward blocks, and per-layer semi-permutation matrices for attention heads—which when applied to one model's parameters yield an aligned representation. The final output is the aligned parameter vector enabling a low- or zero-barrier linear interpolation path, with the barrier quantified as the supremum loss deviation from the linear baseline across all interpolation coefficients.

## Method

The paper formalizes a hierarchy of four symmetry classes—permutation, semi-permutation, orthogonal, and invertible—each mapped to specific Transformer components (elementwise activations, RELU/MHA, RMSNorm, and linear/attention projections respectively). Alignment is achieved by estimating three types of transformation matrices: a global orthogonal matrix O for the residual stream (solved via Procrustes/SVD), per-layer permutation matrices P_FF for feed-forward neurons (solved via SOBLAP), and per-layer semi-permutation matrices P_H for attention heads (solved via QK/OV circuit matching). Three estimation strategies are provided: activation matching, data-free weight matching, and learned matching, where the latter treats the symmetry transformations as trainable latent parameters projected to their respective symmetry classes (via Hungarian algorithm for permutations, SVD for orthogonal) and optimizes them end-to-end using the task loss of an interpolated model with a straight-through estimator for gradient flow. The framework extends naturally to multi-model merging via an iterative universe-matching procedure and to width-heterogeneous settings by exploiting the rectangular orthogonal symmetry of RMSNorm.

## Results

The proposed learned matching method achieves zero loss barrier (0.00 ± 0.00) on all three ViT datasets (CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, Tiny ImageNet) and near-zero barrier (0.02 ± 0.00) on Tiny Shakespeare, with a modest 0.42 ± 0.01 on BookCorpus. The data-free weight matching variant also substantially reduces barriers (e.g., 0.36 vs. 1.69 for vanilla averaging on CIFAR-10) without requiring any training data. For multi-model merging, learned matching produces the broadest near-zero-deviation region on the 3-model simplex for CIFAR-10. Width-heterogeneous alignment (embedding dim 512 → smaller models) also maintains near-zero barriers, demonstrating connectivity across different model sizes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's learned matching (using the full symmetry hierarchy: permutations + semi-permutations + orthogonal maps) dominates all baselines across every dataset. Against activation matching (Verma & Elbayad, 2024), the gap is dramatic: 0.00 vs. 1.27 on CIFAR-10, 0.00 vs. 1.86 on Tiny ImageNet, and 0.42 vs. 4.05 on BookCorpus. The data-free weight matching variant also outperforms activation matching on 4 of 5 datasets (e.g., 0.36 vs. 1.27 on CIFAR-10), confirming that orthogonal residual alignment is critical even without task-loss supervision. Compared to learned matching restricted to permutations only (akin to Git Re-Basin's STE approach), the full symmetry framework reduces barriers by 2–10× on vision tasks (0.00 vs. 0.45 on CIFAR-10) and by ~4× on BookCorpus (0.42 vs. 1.60). The authors note that the residual non-zero barrier on BookCorpus may reflect genuinely disconnected minima associated with different generalization strategies rather than suboptimal alignment.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the linear mode connectivity (LMC) and model re-basin literature—initiated by Frankle et al. and formalized by Git Re-Basin (Ainsworth et al.)—from permutation-only symmetries in MLPs and CNNs to a unified four-class symmetry framework (permutation, semi-permutation, orthogonal, invertible) tailored to Transformers. It builds directly on the QK/OV circuit decomposition of Elhage et al. for intra-head invertible symmetry, the orthogonal residual-stream observation of Ashkboos et al. for the global orthogonal alignment, and the multi-model universe construction of Crisostomi et al. for the M-way merging procedure. The key conceptual departure from prior re-basin work is that discrete neuron permutations are insufficient for Transformers; the continuous orthogonal group acting on the residual stream (enabled by RMSNorm) is the critical additional degree of freedom that eliminates interpolation barriers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration of zero-barrier linear mode connectivity for Transformers, showing that the apparent disconnection of independently trained ViT and GPT-2 minima is an artifact of ignoring continuous symmetries. The practical implications for model merging, federated learning, and continual learning are significant, as symmetry-aware alignment could replace costly fine-tuning after aggregation. However, the evidence is limited to small-scale models (≤8 layers, ≤512 embedding dim) and same-task settings; the non-zero barriers on BookCorpus suggest that genuinely disconnected minima may exist in language modeling, and the framework has not yet been validated on RoPE-based or large-scale architectures. Open questions include whether the orthogonal-alignment insight transfers to modern LLMs with rotary position encodings, and whether soft (Sinkhorn-based) relaxations can close the remaining gap on harder tasks.
