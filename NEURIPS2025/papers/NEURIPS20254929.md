## Problem

Most AI research tacitly assumes that data (including LLM token embeddings) concentrate near a low-curvature manifold, yet this assumption has never been formally tested for token subspaces. Without verifying regularity, tools like UMAP and global dimension estimators may produce misleading inferences, and the topological structure of the embedding space remains uncharacterized. This paper asks: do the token input embeddings of modern LLMs actually satisfy the manifold or the more general fiber bundle hypothesis?

## Input / Output

The method consumes the full token embedding matrix (n tokens in R^ℓ, e.g., 50257×768 for GPT2) plus hyperparameters v_min/v_max (bounding the neighborhood radius range), a sliding window size W, and a significance level α. It outputs, for every token, a p-value for the manifold hypothesis, a p-value for the fiber bundle hypothesis (at both small and large radius scales), and a local dimension estimate derived from the log-log slope. The algorithm is fully deterministic on fixed coordinates, so a single run suffices; Holm-Bonferroni correction is applied across all tokens to control family-wise error.

## Method

The core test exploits the volume-radius scaling law: on a d-dimensional manifold, ball volume scales as r^d (a straight line in log-log space), while on a fiber bundle the log-log curve is piecewise linear with slopes that can only decrease as radius increases (Theorem 1). Concretely, Algorithm 1 computes the n×n pairwise distance matrix, sorts distances per token, and uses the sorted indices as Monte Carlo volume estimates; log-log slopes are computed via 3-point centered differences, and two-sample T-tests on adjacent sliding windows detect slope changes. The manifold test rejects on any slope change; the fiber bundle test rejects only on slope increases. Theorem 2 then shows that when the bounding manifold dimension d satisfies 2d < ℓ (the latent space dimension), singularities in the token subspace generically persist into the LLM output, meaning context cannot resolve them.

## Results

All four LLMs (GPT2, Llemma7B, Mistral7B, Pythia6.9B) exhibit highly significant rejections of the manifold hypothesis, with 33–66 tokens rejected per model (minimum p-values ranging from 3×10⁻⁹ to 3×10⁻⁷ after Holm-Bonferroni correction). The fiber bundle hypothesis is also rejected for GPT2 (12 tokens at small radius, 7 at large), Mistral7B (1 at small, 2 at large), and Llemma7B (1 at large), while Pythia6.9B shows no fiber bundle rejections. Local dimension estimates are highly variable across tokens (e.g., GPT2 median dimension ranges from 389 at small radius to 14 at large radius), confirming the token subspace is not a manifold of constant dimension.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not benchmark against competing methods for a predictive task; instead, it introduces two novel statistical tests (manifold and fiber bundle) and applies them to four LLM token embedding spaces. The closest prior work—Fefferman et al. (2016), Lim et al. (2023), and Von Rohrscheidt & Rieck (2023)—proposed manifold hypothesis tests but either lacked practical constants for implementation or were validated only on low-dimensional synthetic datasets. The present paper's contribution is a fully deterministic, practical algorithm (Algorithm 1) that extends testing to the fiber bundle generalization and applies it to high-dimensional (768–4096) real LLM embeddings. A key empirical finding is that Llemma7B and Mistral7B share identical token sets (n=32,016) yet exhibit markedly different rejection patterns (33 vs. 40 manifold rejections; 1 vs. 3 fiber bundle rejections), demonstrating that training methodology leaves a distinct topological fingerprint on the embedding space even when the vocabulary is fixed.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the manifold hypothesis testing framework of Fefferman et al. (2016) and the more recent singularity detection methods of Lim et al. (2023) and Von Rohrscheidt & Rieck (2023) by introducing a fiber bundle generalization that captures two spatial regimes (small and large radius neighborhoods). Where prior work provided theoretical tests without practical constants or was limited to low-dimensional datasets, this paper delivers a fully deterministic, implementable algorithm (Algorithm 1) with Holm-Bonferroni correction. The theoretical backbone for singularity persistence into LLM output (Theorem 2) directly builds on Robinson et al. (2025), which established conditions under which generic transformers embed the token product space into model output.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first rigorous statistical evidence that the tacit manifold assumption underlying much of AI research (UMAP, manifold learning, Riemannian geometry interpretations of transformers) fails for LLM token embeddings, with the more general fiber bundle hypothesis also rejected for 3 of 4 models tested. Theorem 2's result that singularities persist into LLM output for generic transformers has practical implications for prompt engineering and multi-agent system design, suggesting that prompts containing singular tokens will exhibit reduced stability. Key open questions include: what specific type of singularity is present (the tests can reject regularity but not classify the singularity), whether a vocabulary size or tokenization scheme could minimize singularities, and whether these findings generalize to larger or proprietary models where embedding access is restricted.
