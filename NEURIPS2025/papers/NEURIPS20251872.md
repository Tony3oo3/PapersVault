## Problem

Learning robot control from human videos requires a good action representation, but existing options (video frames, 2D pixel flow, point cloud flow, SE(3) poses) are either too noisy, too lossy, or too restrictive. A critical practical barrier is that real-world depth sensors produce heavily corrupted measurements (holes, white noise, wrong values on moving objects), making direct 3D motion extraction unreliable and prior methods achieve under 10% success on manipulation tasks.

## Input / Output

The system consumes RGBD human videos (Intel D435 at 640×480, cropped to 480×480 and rescaled to 256×256) processed through SAM2 segmentation and CoTracker3 pixel tracking. The Phase I estimator takes noisy object depth, noisy 3D pixel flow, and a dense 4-channel intrinsics map as input; the Phase II policy takes only the segmented RGBD image (object pixels only, no background). The output is a 4-channel image tensor (H×W×4) encoding per-pixel depth and 3D displacement, which is converted at ~300–1000 Hz into an SE(3) transformation via Kabsch alignment with RANSAC for robot execution.

## Method

The core contribution is a two-phase pipeline: (1) a denoising 3D motion field estimator trained on 8M synthetic samples (ShapeNet objects with random twist motions) that reconstructs clean depth and 3D motion from noisy sensor inputs, and (2) a dual-head UNet policy (Gaussian or diffusion) that predicts this motion field from segmented RGBD for zero-shot robot control. The key architectural insight is a dense intrinsics map feature — encoding (y−c_y)/f_y, (x−c_x)/f_x, 1/f_y, 1/f_x per pixel — concatenated to the input, which supplies the geometric terms a CNN cannot infer without position embeddings and is shown to be critical for accurate motion prediction. Training uses weighted Huber loss with object-masked supervision, and data augmentation simulates realistic depth corruption (missing values, white noise, wrong values) and pixel-flow noise (Gaussian jitter, dropout). The motion-geometry synergy is central: observed 2D tracks on rigid objects provide cues to recover missing or erroneous depth, effectively performing masked 4D reconstruction. For fine-grained tasks like insertion (2.5 mm tolerance), a diffusion policy head with masked noise sampling is essential, as a Gaussian head achieves 0% success.

## Results

The proposed 3D motion field estimator reduces SE(3) motion estimation error by over 50% compared to the General Flow baseline on real-world object manipulation. In zero-shot robot control from human videos, the method achieves a ~55% average success rate across five diverse tasks (pick-rotate-place, line tracking, pushing, wrench use, and insertion), where prior approaches achieve ≲10%. Notably, the policy trained solely on human hand videos can perform fine-grained insertion with only 2.5 mm tolerance, a skill not previously demonstrated in this video-to-robot setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary numerical baseline is General Flow (GFlow) [59], the most recent point-cloud flow method for robot learning from videos. The proposed method reduces 3D motion estimation error by over 50% relative to GFlow on real-world SE(3) transformation recovery, and achieves ~55% average task success rate versus ≲10% for GFlow and other prior approaches. GFlow's performance degrades quickly under adversarial depth noise, while the proposed denoising estimator maintains stable error levels. The remaining compared methods (UniPi, ATM, Track2Act, Im2Flow2Act, SPOT, TI) are excluded from numerical comparison because they fail on the experimental setup due to inherent limitations: SPOT and TI require object 3D pose estimation, UniPi/ATM/Track2Act/Im2Flow2Act require robot-collected data or lack depth robustness, and none generalize to background distractors. The proposed method is the only one that simultaneously satisfies all five technical criteria (no pose estimation, zero-shot, closed-loop, depth-robust, and distractor-generalizable).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on robot learning from human videos by proposing an object-centric 3D motion field as the action representation, departing from prior approaches that use video frames (UniPi, UniSim), 2D pixel flow (ATM, Track2Act), point cloud flow (General Flow), or SE(3) poses (SPOT). It builds most directly on General Flow's 3D flow extraction pipeline but introduces a simulation-trained denoising estimator to handle noisy depth, and adapts the diffusion policy framework (Chi et al.) to predict dense image-shaped motion fields. The object-centric, embodiment-agnostic input design draws on prior work in object-centric 3D representations for manipulation, but is the first to apply this principle specifically within the human-video-to-robot transfer setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work demonstrates that fine-grained manipulation skills (e.g., 2.5mm-tolerance insertion) can be acquired zero-shot from human hand videos alone, a capability not previously shown in this setting, which is significant for addressing the data bottleneck in robot learning. The denoising 3D motion field estimator trained purely in simulation and transferred to real-world noisy depth is a reusable component for 3D vision tasks. Key open questions include handling full occlusion, extending to dexterous hands (where SE(3) decomposition breaks down), moving cameras, and soft-body manipulation — all acknowledged by the authors as future directions.
