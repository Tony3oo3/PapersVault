## Problem

Current AI benchmarking suffers from systemic structural flaws—data contamination into training corpora, selective reporting, fragmented and inconsistent metrics, opaque dataset curation, and the absence of any proctoring or fairness safeguards—that collectively render leaderboard scores unreliable indicators of true model capability. The paper argues that no existing evaluation framework simultaneously provides contamination resistance, unified metric standards, transparent data quality control, and equitable access, leaving the field without a trustworthy, certificate-grade measure of progress.

## Input / Output

Data contributors submit private test suites paired with executable scoring functions, which are cryptographically committed (hashed) before any model sees them; model creators expose authenticated inference endpoints that receive queries in a sealed sandbox. The platform produces three continuously updated leaderboards: a model leaderboard reporting quality-weighted composite scores with domain-specific subscores, a contributor leaderboard ranking by cumulative test quality plus verification bonuses, and a reviewer leaderboard ranking by Pearson correlation between individual ratings and consensus quality. Retired tests, full score logs, and raw model responses are published for post-hoc community auditing.

## Method

PEERBENCH operationalizes a proctored-exam paradigm through a live reservoir of size k that undergoes rolling renewal: at each step, the oldest or zero-weight test is retired and made public, while new committed tests enter the pool, ensuring no test is ever reused for scoring. Model scores are computed as a quality-weighted average where each test's weight combines 70% peer-reviewed quality (from at least three independent reviewers assigning ordinal scores in {-1,0,1,2}) and 30% contributor reputation, with a floor at zero. A slashing-and-collateral economic model incentivizes honest behavior: participants stake collateral, earn rewards reflected in reputation, and face removal or monetary penalty for malicious tests or systematic deviation from consensus. The platform adopts a hybrid scheduling strategy—immediate on-demand scoring for responsiveness plus periodic synchronized evaluation windows (geometric progression) for gold-standard cohort comparability—and uses partial revelation (read-only, non-copyable image snippets) for online peer review alongside full post-retirement publication for community verification.

## Results

This is a position paper proposing a new benchmarking paradigm (PEERBENCH) and does not report empirical experimental results or quantitative metrics. The paper's contribution is architectural and conceptual: it defines seven design principles for proctored AI evaluation, specifies a ten-step end-to-end workflow with cryptographic commitments, and formulates three scoring equations (ContributorScore, ReviewerScore, ModelScore) for a community-governed leaderboard. No models are trained, no benchmarks are run, and no performance numbers are reported.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a position paper with no empirical baselines or head-to-head model comparisons. The paper does not evaluate PEERBENCH against any existing system in a quantitative sense. Instead, it qualitatively contrasts the proposed paradigm against six categories of prior work (static benchmarks like MMLU, proprietary systems like Scale SEAL, live benchmarks like LiveBench, episodic challenges like ARC-AGI, preference-based platforms like Chatbot Arena, and adversarial contests) via the feature matrix in Table 1. The authors argue that no existing system simultaneously achieves continuous rolling updates, multi-source data diversity, post-hoc transparency, contamination resistance by design, and transparent community-vetted quality control. PEERBENCH is positioned as a complementary 'certificate-grade layer' alongside open benchmarks rather than a replacement, and the paper explicitly calls for future work on mechanism design, governance, and reliability guarantees before any empirical validation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This position paper synthesizes ideas from several distinct threads—rolling-update benchmarks (LiveBench, Dynabench), crowdsourced preference evaluation (Chatbot Arena), cryptographic commitment schemes, and the governance structures of human standardized testing (SAT, GRE, bar exams)—into a unified proctored-evaluation paradigm. It departs from prior work by arguing that no single existing mechanism (static public sets, private vendor suites, or open leaderboards) is sufficient, and that contamination resistance, data quality control, and fairness must be built into the evaluation infrastructure by construction rather than relying on participant goodwill. The PEERBENCH prototype is positioned as a complementary certificate-grade layer alongside open benchmarks, not a replacement, drawing on reputation-system design from platforms like Stack Exchange and Wikipedia but applying it to AI test-item curation for the first time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper articulates a compelling and timely critique of systemic failures in AI benchmarking—contamination, cherry-picking, fragmentation, and the absence of proctoring—that has gained urgency as foundation models enter high-stakes decision-making. However, as a position paper with only a prototype architecture and no empirical validation, the practical viability of the reputation-weighted scoring, the temporal fairness trade-offs between immediate and synchronized evaluation, and the economic sustainability of the slashing mechanism remain open questions. Key unresolved challenges include mechanism design for preventing collusion at scale, ensuring the validator network maintains sufficient quality and diversity, and whether the community will adopt a paradigm that trades the immediacy of public leaderboards for slower but more trustworthy certification.
