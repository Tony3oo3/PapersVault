## Problem

Message-passing GNNs suffer from three intertwined limitations: oversquashing of long-range signals through fixed-width bottlenecks, oversmoothing of node representations with depth, and expressiveness bounded by the 1-WL test. Existing remedies either replace message passing with O(N²) attention or inject topology-derived positional encodings that are difficult to make simultaneously unique, distance-sensitive, and permutation-equivariant. The paper asks whether node features themselves—rather than graph topology—can supply the global, distance-sensitive signals needed to overcome these limitations within a standard message-passing framework.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a node-attributed graph G=(V,E,X) with feature matrix X∈R^{N×F} and produces standard GNN node or graph-level embeddings for downstream classification or regression. The SRF matrix Z∈R^{N×kD} is computed once as a one-time preprocessing step (independent of training epochs) and then concatenated to each node's hidden state at every GNN layer, so the augmented representation at layer ℓ is h̃_i^(ℓ)=[h_i^(ℓ)|z_i]. Using structured random matrices, the one-time cost is O(N² log N) time and O(N) memory, after which per-epoch overhead is negligible.

## Method

SRF operates in two stages. First, an embedding operator E (e.g., random Fourier features for RBF or Laplacian kernels, or a linear JLT projection) maps each node feature x_i into a D-dimensional random kernel feature space, yielding Φ=E(X)∈R^{N×D} whose inner products unbiasedly estimate the chosen kernel κ. Second, a sketch operator S applies k independent additive Gaussian Johnson-Lindenstrauss transforms—each of the form (I + G/√N)Φ with G∈R^{N×N} i.i.d. N(0,1)—and concatenates the results to produce Z∈R^{N×kD}, where each row z_i is a random linear combination of all node kernel embeddings. The key insight is that this cross-node mixing injects topology-agnostic global information into every node's representation while preserving kernel distances with high probability (Prop 3.2), guaranteeing almost-sure uniqueness (Prop 3.4), and maintaining permutation equivariance in expectation (Prop 3.5). The sketched embeddings are concatenated to node hidden states at every message-passing layer, enabling the GNN to access long-range feature relationships without altering graph topology or incurring O(N²) attention costs.

## Results

On REDDIT-B and REDDIT-M graph classification, SRF-enhanced GINE (E_RBF, S_AG^(8)) achieves 94.13% and 60.53% accuracy respectively, outperforming the strongest baseline R-PEARL (93.0%, 59.4%) and the no-encoding GINE (91.8%, 56.9%). On DrugOOD out-of-distribution molecular property prediction, SRF (E_RBF) reaches 72.63 AUC on Assay and 69.60 on Scaffold, matching or exceeding all positional encoding baselines, while SRF (E_L) leads on Size (67.23 AUC). On the expressiveness benchmarks CSL and EXP, SRF achieves perfect 1.000 accuracy versus 0.100 and 0.518 for the GINE baseline. On Peptides-struct (long-range peptide structure prediction), combining B-PEARL with SRF achieves 0.243 MAE, matching the best graph transformer (Graph ViT, 0.245) and beating R-PEARL alone (0.247).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SRF-enhanced GINE consistently outperforms the no-encoding GINE baseline (Hu et al.) by 2.3–3.6 points on Reddit and 0.6–1.2 AUC on DrugOOD, confirming that the gains come from the sketching mechanism rather than mere feature injection (the identity-sketch ablation gains only ~0.7–1.0 points). Against random node features (Abboud et al., Sato et al.), SRF improves Reddit-B accuracy from 91.8% to 94.13% and Reddit-M from 57.0% to 60.53%, while also achieving perfect expressiveness on CSL/EXP where random features score near chance. On DrugOOD, SRF (E_RBF) beats SignNet (Lim et al.) by 0.36 AUC on Assay and 3.17 on Size, and outperforms BasisNet by 0.97 and 6.44 AUC respectively; SignNet-8S (Kanatsoulis et al.) is competitive on Reddit (92.4% vs 94.13%) but SRF still leads. SRF (E_RBF) edges out SPE (Huang et al.) by 0.10 AUC on Assay and ties within noise on Scaffold (69.60 vs 69.64), while clearly winning on Size (67.23 vs 66.02). Against PEARL (Kanatsoulis et al.), SRF leads on both Reddit splits (94.13 vs 93.0 on B; 60.53 vs 59.4 on M) and is competitive on DrugOOD, while also being ~3× faster and ~100× less memory-intensive. On Peptides-struct, B-PEARL+SRF (0.243 MAE) matches Graph ViT (He et al., 0.245) and beats GPS (Rampašek et al., 0.252), SAN (Kreuzer et al., 0.255), SUN (Frasca et al., 0.250), and GNN-AK+ (Zhao et al., 0.274), demonstrating that SRF provides complementary benefits to structural positional encodings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the Johnson-Lindenstrauss transform in an unconventional way: rather than using it for dimensionality reduction, it applies a cross-node random projection (I + G/√N) to a matrix of random kernel features (from Rahimi & Recht's random Fourier features), creating embeddings that are linear combinations of all nodes' feature-space representations. This departs from the dominant line of positional/structural encoding work (SignNet, BasisNet, PEARL, SPE) that derives augmentation signals from graph topology, and from purely random node features (Abboud et al., Sato et al.) that break symmetry without preserving feature distances. The key conceptual move is leveraging node features themselves—rather than topology—to inject global, distance-sensitive, and unique signals into message-passing GNNs, positioning SRF as complementary to (rather than competing with) structural encodings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SRF offers a simple, one-time-precomputation augmentation (O(N² log N) with structured random matrices) that simultaneously mitigates oversquashing, oversmoothing, and limited expressiveness in standard MPGNNs, with ~3× faster runtime and ~100× less memory than PEARL. Its practical value is enhanced by complementarity: combining SRF with PEARL positional encodings on Peptides-struct closes the gap to graph transformers, suggesting a modular recipe for practitioners. Key limitations include dependence on informative node features (gains may be limited for unattributed or sparse-feature graphs), topology-agnosticism (structural signals must come from elsewhere), and properties holding only in expectation. An open direction the authors identify is extending the sketching mechanism to topology-aware embeddings (e.g., graph random kernel features), which could unify feature-based and structure-based augmentation.
