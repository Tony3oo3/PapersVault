## Problem

Existing 3D few-shot class incremental learning methods rely heavily on meticulously crafted text prompts and elaborate fine-tuning strategies to extract knowledge from pre-trained 3D foundation models, making them fragile and difficult to scale. The inherent geometric structure of point clouds has not been leveraged to augment text prompts, and the modality heterogeneity between geometric and textual features remains unaddressed, limiting the model's ability to learn robust representations from only a handful of new samples.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251362/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 3D point clouds (1024 points sampled via farthest point sampling) and text prompts formatted as 'a point cloud model of a {category}' as input, and produces class labels over all previously seen and newly introduced categories. Both the point cloud encoder (EVA02-E-14+ CLIP) and text encoder are frozen; only the geometric projection clusters and embedding module are trained. In the few-shot setting, only 5 samples per new category and 1 replay sample per old category are available, and cross-dataset experiments transition from synthetic (ModelNet, ShapeNet) to real-scanned (ScanObjectNN, CO3D) data.

## Method

3D-FLEG introduces two synergistic modules. The geometric feature extraction module constructs Dynamic Geometric Feature Projection Clusters (DGPC) by applying spectral clustering and K-means on base-class point cloud features, followed by Laplacian eigenmap dimensionality reduction to yield 1024 basis vectors; upon arrival of new classes, the clusters adapt via an update rate of 0.1, and each basis vector carries a learnable attention weight so that geometric features are extracted as a weighted sum of dot products between point cloud features and basis vectors. The geometric feature embedding module then processes these features through AdaptiveAvgPool1d and AdaptiveMaxPool1d for multi-granularity capture, a linear projection, a 2-layer 8-head Transformer encoder, and a fully connected layer, before concatenating with dimension-matched expanded text features and applying a tanh activation to produce the final fused representation. Training minimizes cross-entropy loss between original point cloud features and the enhanced text features, with only the DGPC weights and embedding module parameters updated while both encoders remain frozen.

## Results

On the most challenging cross-dataset setting (ShapeNet→CO3D, 11 incremental tasks), 3D-FLEG achieves 91.7% base accuracy and 76.8% final accuracy with a relative drop of 16.2%, outperforming the closest competitor FoundationModel (87.3%→72.6%, Δ=16.8%). On ModelNet→ScanObjectNN, 3D-FLEG reaches 93.8%→86.8% (Δ=7.5%) versus FoundationModel's 87.7%→79.2% (Δ=9.7%). In within-dataset experiments on ModelNet, 3D-FLEG attains 98.3%→87.3% (Δ=11.2%), surpassing C3PR (91.6%→70.9%, Δ=22.5%) by approximately 4% at each incremental stage.

## Baselines & Comparisons

3D-FLEG outperforms all nine baselines across every cross-dataset setting. Against the 2D FSCIL methods adapted to point clouds (LwF, IL2M, ScaIL, EEIL, FACT, Sem-aware), the gap is dramatic: these methods suffer near-total forgetting (e.g., LwF drops to 1.9% on ShapeNet→CO3D) or moderate forgetting (FACT: 55.9%, Δ=31.3%), while 3D-FLEG retains 76.8% (Δ=16.2%). Among 3D-specific methods, Microshape (57.1%, Δ=30.9%) and C3PR (63.2%, Δ=24.4%) on ShapeNet→CO3D are both substantially outperformed by 3D-FLEG's 76.8% (Δ=16.2%). The closest competitor, FoundationModel (Ahmadi et al., 2024), achieves 72.6% final accuracy on ShapeNet→CO3D and 79.2% on ModelNet→ScanObjectNN; 3D-FLEG exceeds it by 4.2 and 7.6 percentage points respectively, while also achieving a smaller accuracy drop (16.2 vs. 16.8; 7.5 vs. 9.7). On the ShapeNet→ScanObjectNN setting, 3D-FLEG (86.3%, Δ=5.1) edges out FoundationModel (85.6%, Δ=5.7) with a marginally lower drop, confirming consistent superiority across all evaluated protocols.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 3D FSCIL paradigm established by Microshape (Chowdhury et al., 2022) and the foundation-model-based approach of Ahmadi et al. (2024), which first applied 3D vision-language models (Uni3D/EVA02) to incremental learning with a training-free adaptor and dual-cache system. Where prior methods (C3PR, FoundationModel) rely heavily on carefully crafted text prompts and elaborate alignment strategies to extract pre-training knowledge, 3D-FLEG departs by embedding geometric features extracted via spectral clustering and Laplacian eigenmaps directly into the text prompt space, reducing dependence on prompt engineering. The geometric feature embedding module, which uses a Transformer encoder with multi-head attention to fuse geometric and textual modalities, is a novel architectural contribution not present in prior 3D FSCIL work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in 3D FSCIL: the sensitivity of foundation-model-based methods to prompt quality and training strategy complexity, which limits real-world deployability. By showing that geometric feature embedding consistently reduces accuracy drop (e.g., 11.2% vs. 22.5% on ModelNet) and improves harmonic accuracy across four datasets, the work provides a more robust and less prompt-dependent pipeline. Open questions remain about whether the spectral clustering + Laplacian eigenmap approach scales to larger or noisier point clouds, whether the frozen-encoder assumption limits long-horizon adaptation, and how the method generalizes beyond classification to tasks like 3D detection or scene segmentation, which the authors note as future directions.
