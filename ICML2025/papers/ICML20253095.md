## Problem

Traditional A/B testing methods based on normal-distribution or expanded statistics lack the sensitivity to detect minor average treatment effects (ATE), which are common in mature businesses where marginal returns are small but economically significant at scale. Existing approaches (G-computation, propensity score, doubly robust) all rely on exchangeable data structures and CLT-based test statistics, limiting their power for small-effect detection. This paper seeks a test framework that breaks exchangeability to achieve greater statistical power while maintaining Type I error control.

## Input / Output

The method consumes tabular RCT data (covariates X, outcome Y, binary treatment indicator A) along with a convergence threshold τ (typically 0.03) and a permutation count B (set to 25). It outputs a single aggregated p-value computed via Cauchy combination over B permuted test statistics, plus the underlying weighted bandit test statistic T_{n,λ}(θ_n*). The counterfactual outcomes are estimated via K-fold cross-fitting (K=2) using LightGBM or XGBoost within a DML framework, so the raw input is consumed through an intermediate doubly robust estimation step before the bandit process is applied.

## Method

The core contribution is a weighted two-armed bandit (WTAB) process that constructs a mean-volatility test statistic T_{n,λ}(θ_n) with a tunable weight λ balancing Type I error against power, where λ is selected to satisfy λσ/((1−λ)√n) ≤ τ. An optimal policy θ_n* sequentially selects arms based on the sign of the running statistic (arm 1 if T_{i−1} ≥ 0, else arm 0), breaking data exchangeability and inducing a non-standard 'spike distribution' B(ω_n, σ_0) that is more concentrated under H₀ and more dispersed under H₁ than the Gaussian CLT limit. Counterfactual outcomes are estimated via a doubly robust estimator using LightGBM/XGBoost in a DML cross-fitting framework. To mitigate the order-sensitivity of the bandit statistic, B random permutations of the sample are applied and the resulting p-values are aggregated via the Cauchy combination test, yielding a robust final p-value. The strategic central limit theorem (SCLT) formally establishes that this policy maximizes tail probability under the alternative, achieving the highest possible rejection rate among all admissible policies.

## Results

On semi-synthetic real-world data (Table 4), PWTAB achieves statistical power of 0.758–0.764 with type I error controlled at 0.052, outperforming CUPED (power 0.740), z-DML (0.744–0.746), and DIM (0.498). On real dataset B from a ride-sharing company (Table 3), PWTAB with Stacking yields p = 0.032 (rejecting H₀ at α = 0.05), while CUPED gives p = 0.053 (failing to reject). Across 32 synthetic settings (Table 2), PWTAB maintains type I error between 0.030 and 0.060, comparable to all other methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PWTAB consistently outperforms CUPED (Deng et al., 2013) in statistical power: 0.758–0.764 vs. 0.740 on semi-synthetic real data, and on real dataset B, PWTAB (Stacking) achieves a significant p-value of 0.032 while CUPED's p-value of 0.053 fails to reject the null at α = 0.05. Against z-DML (Chernozhukov et al., 2018), PWTAB gains 1.2–1.8 percentage points in power (0.758 vs. 0.744–0.746), with the gap widening to 1.8 pp under Stacking (0.764 vs. 0.746). The unpermuted WTAB variant performs comparably to z-DML in most settings, confirming that the permutation + Cauchy combination step is what drives the additional power gain. DIM, the naive difference-in-means estimator, is substantially weaker (power ≈ 0.498), highlighting the importance of variance reduction. All methods maintain type I error near the nominal 0.05 level, so the power advantage of PWTAB is not purchased at the cost of inflated false positives.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the strategic central limit theorem (SCLT) framework from Chen et al. (2022, 2023), which introduced a two-armed bandit process to break data exchangeability for hypothesis testing, by applying it specifically to A/B testing with counterfactual outcomes estimated via doubly robust methods. It builds on Wang et al. (2025) for the permutation-and-Cauchy-combination framework to handle the resulting non-exchangeability, and on Chernozhukov et al. (2018) for the DML cross-fitting procedure to estimate counterfactuals. The key departure from prior work is the introduction of a weighting parameter λ that balances the mean and volatility components of the bandit statistic, trading off Type I error control against statistical power, combined with the SCLT showing a non-normal (spike) asymptotic distribution that is more concentrated under H₀ and less under H₁ than the classical CLT.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically critical gap: detecting minor average treatment effects in large-scale A/B testing where even 0.02-per-user improvements translate to millions in revenue. The theoretical contribution (SCLT with spike distribution) provides a principled justification for why breaking exchangeability via a bandit policy increases power, and the empirical results show PWTAB consistently outperforming CUPED and z-DML, particularly on nonlinear data. However, the practical gains are modest (e.g., p=0.032 vs. 0.053 on one real dataset), the λ selection relies on an ad hoc threshold (0.03), and the authors acknowledge that ML-based counterfactual estimation is not refined enough compared to domain-specific explicit models. Open questions include whether the framework extends to multi-armed settings, time-varying treatments, or settings where the positivity assumption is violated.
