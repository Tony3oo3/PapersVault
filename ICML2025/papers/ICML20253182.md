## Problem

Existing robotic garment unfolding methods focus almost exclusively on maximizing surface coverage, neglecting the critical task of standardization—aligning the garment's shape, orientation, and keypoints to a canonical configuration. Without standardization, downstream tasks such as folding, ironing, and packing become significantly harder and less reliable. This paper addresses the gap by unifying unfolding and standardization into a single learned policy that produces a consistently aligned garment ready for subsequent manipulation.

## Input / Output

The network consumes a single overhead RGB-D image (128×128×4) that is augmented into 80 transformed observations via 16 discrete rotations (360°) and 5 scales {0.75, 1.0, 1.5, 2.0, 2.5} to enforce invariance under the garment's physical pose. It outputs two spatial action maps (one per primitive: fling and p&p), from which the winning primitive and grasp parameters ⟨x, y, θ, w⟩ are extracted by argmax over the masked maps. After the garment reaches a sufficiently flat state, a separate DeepLabv3 keypoint detector produces 3D-projected keypoint coordinates (6 for long sleeves, 4 for pants/skirts) that drive a heuristic folding routine.

## Method

APS-Net is a CNN with three parallel encoders—each computing a distinct reward (coverage R_C, IoU R_I, keypoint distance R_K)—and two decoder heads per encoder (one for fling, one for p&p), producing per-pixel desirability maps. The factorized reward R_CIK = α·R_C + β·R_I + (1−α−β)·R_K is supervised via delta-reward (difference before/after each action) in a self-supervised loop, with α=0.2 and β=0.4. A Spatial Action Mask intersects workspace reachability masks with garment masks (shifted by the grasp width w) to zero out infeasible actions, while an Action Optimized Module snaps predicted fling points to detected shoulder keypoints when within a 5-pixel threshold, accelerating flattening. The dual-arm policy alternates between high-velocity dynamic flings (coarse unfolding) and quasi-static pick-and-place (fine alignment), with collision avoidance enforced by constraining the rotation angle θ to [−90°, 90°] and adjusting grasp width w.

## Results

On long-sleeve garment unfolding in simulation (50 trials), APS-Net achieves 91.1% coverage, 79.3% IoU, and 1.832 keypoint distance (KD), outperforming the SOTA Cloth Funnels by 3.9% in coverage, 5.2% in IoU, and 0.14 in KD (7.09% relative reduction). In real-world experiments, APS-Net reaches 70.5% IoU and 86.3% coverage for unfolding, and achieves a 12/15 success rate on the downstream folding task, compared to 10/15 for Cloth Funnels and 6/15 for FlingBot. The method generalizes to pants (91.8% Cov, 80.4% IoU) and skirts (86.7% Cov, 79.6% IoU) in simulation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

APS-Net is compared against three baselines: P&P (Lee et al., 2021), a quasi-static sequential lift-and-place method; FlingBot (Ha & Song, 2022), a dual-arm dynamic fling method using coverage-only reward; and Cloth Funnels (Canberk et al., 2023), the SOTA method combining p&P and fling with 3D particle-distance rewards. APS-Net wins on all four metrics (Cov, IoU, KD, R_CIK) against every baseline in simulation. Against the closest competitor Cloth Funnels, the gains are 3.9% in coverage (91.1 vs. 87.2), 5.2% in IoU (79.3 vs. 74.1), and 0.14 lower KD (1.832 vs. 1.972), attributed to the factorized 2D reward and spatial action mask. FlingBot and S-Fling achieve comparable coverage (~80%) but substantially lower IoU (~61–63%), confirming that dynamic fling alone is insufficient for standardization. In real-world folding, APS-Net's standardized unfolding yields a 12/15 success rate versus 10/15 for Cloth Funnels and 6/15 for FlingBot, demonstrating that the standardization objective directly simplifies the downstream folding task.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the dual-arm dynamic manipulation paradigm established by FlingBot (Ha & Song, 2022) and the multi-primitive fling+p&P framework of Cloth Funnels (Canberk et al., 2023), but departs from both by explicitly incorporating garment standardization—shape alignment, orientation consistency, and keypoint preservation—into the unfolding objective rather than treating coverage maximization as the sole goal. The factorized reward (coverage + IoU + keypoint distance) and the Spatial Action Mask are novel architectural additions that address the gap between coarse unfolding and the precise alignment needed for downstream tasks. The keypoint-based folding strategy, trained on a dedicated dataset collected during unfolding, bridges the unfolding and folding stages in a way prior methods handled separately.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that standardized unfolding measurably simplifies downstream folding (12/15 vs. 10/15 success rate over Cloth Funnels), the paper validates a practical pipeline for industrial garment handling where consistent intermediate states reduce the difficulty of subsequent operations. Limitations include reliance on heuristic folding rules (not a learned policy), evaluation on only three garment categories, and a sim-to-real gap that is mitigated but not eliminated (real-world IoU drops from ~79% in simulation to ~70%). Open questions include whether the factorized reward generalizes to more complex garments (e.g., multi-layered or highly textured items), whether learned folding policies could replace the heuristics, and whether the approach scales to high-throughput industrial settings.
