## Problem

Flow and diffusion models use unidimensional coefficients α(t) ∈ ℝ that impose identical time scheduling across all data dimensions and cannot adapt to individual inference trajectories, unlike simulation-based methods such as NeuralODE. Prior trajectory optimization work predefined optimality criteria (e.g., straightness or OT alignment) before training, rather than evaluating trajectories by their final transportation quality, leaving the inference plan largely unexplored as an optimization target.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251316/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a Gaussian noise sample x_T ~ ρ_T (and optionally a class label c for conditional generation) and produces a generated image at the target resolution (32×32 for CIFAR-10/ImageNet-32, 64×64 for FFHQ/AFHQ/ImageNet-64). Unusually, the MAC module U_φ consumes x_T to produce per-pixel, per-channel sinusoidal weights w_φ ∈ ℝ^{C×H×W×M×2} that modulate the ODE integration schedule, meaning the inference trajectory is sample-specific and computed with a single forward pass through U_φ before the NFE-step solver G_{θ,φ} executes.

## Method

MAC replaces the scalar coefficient α(t) with a diagonal multidimensional coefficient γ_φ(t, x_T) ∈ ℝ^{d×2}, parameterized by a U-Net U_φ that outputs sinusoidal weights w_φ(x_T) = s·LPF∘tanh(U_φ(x_T)) over a basis b_m(t) = sin(πm(t/T)^{1/q}), enabling per-dimension, per-sample curved trajectories. Training proceeds in two stages: an optional γ-pre-training phase where H_θ is trained with random γ ~ Γ_h and coefficient conditioning to accommodate multidimensional interpolation, followed by adversarial optimization where a StyleGAN-XL discriminator D_ψ provides simulation-based feedback. Critically, φ is optimized through the full ODE solver G_{θ,φ} (so gradients flow through the entire NFE-step trajectory), while θ receives a direct adversarial loss on H_θ's output, enabling joint vector-field and inference-plan optimization. The low-pass filter and scaling factor s constrain the hypothesis set Γ_h to exclude high-frequency, oscillatory coefficients, and the diagonal-matrix design reduces U_φ's output to C×M×2 channels versus C×d×M×2 for full matrices.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 conditional generation, MAC (EDM_γ + adv θ,φ) achieves a state-of-the-art FID of 1.37 with only 5(+) NFE, outperforming CTM+advθ (1.63 at 2 NFE) and EDM (1.79 at 35 NFE). On CIFAR-10 unconditional, it reaches FID 1.69 at 5(+) NFE versus EDM's 1.98 at 35 NFE. On ImageNet-64, MAC achieves FID 1.48 and FD_DINOv2 70.2 at 5(+) NFE, surpassing CTM+advθ (FID 1.73 at 2 NFE) and EDM (FID 2.44 at 79 NFE). On FFHQ-64, MAC attains FID 2.27 at 5(+) NFE, beating EDM's 2.39 at 79 NFE, though it slightly trails EDM on AFHQ-64 (2.04 vs. 1.96 at 35 NFE).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MAC achieves the best FID among all compared methods on CIFAR-10 (both conditional and unconditional) and ImageNet-64, using only 5(+) NFE. Against the closest competitor CTM+advθ [Kim 2024], MAC improves CIFAR-10 conditional FID from 1.63 (2 NFE) to 1.37 and ImageNet-64 FID from 1.73 (2 NFE) to 1.48, while also outperforming CTM at matched 5 NFE (1.37 vs. 1.98 on CIFAR-10 cond; 1.48 vs. 3.02 on ImageNet-64). Against EDM [Karras 2022], MAC reduces FID from 1.98→1.69 (CIFAR-10 uncond), 1.79→1.37 (CIFAR-10 cond), 2.44→1.48 (ImageNet-64), and 2.39→2.27 (FFHQ-64), all with roughly 7–16× fewer NFE; however, on AFHQ-64 MAC (2.04) slightly trails EDM (1.96 at 35 NFE). Against GAN baselines, MAC beats StyleGAN-XL [Sauer 2022] on CIFAR-10 cond (1.37 vs. 1.85) and ImageNet-64 (1.48 vs. 2.09), and StyleGAN-Ada [Karras 2020] on CIFAR-10 uncond (1.69 vs. 2.92). In 2D transportation experiments, MAC consistently outperforms both SI [Albergo 2023] and minibatch-OT SI [Tong 2024] on W₂ distance across all four distribution pairs, demonstrating that even OT-trained straight trajectories are not always optimal. Against distillation-based methods (2-Rectified Flow++ [Lee 2024], CD+advθ [Lu 2023]), MAC achieves substantially lower FID with comparable or fewer NFE (e.g., 1.37 vs. 2.40 on CIFAR-10 cond). Against ADM [Dhariwal 2021], MAC achieves FID 1.48 at 5 NFE versus 2.07 at 250 NFE on ImageNet-64.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Stochastic Interpolant (SI) trajectory formulation x(t) = α₀(t)x₀ + α₁(t)x₁ by replacing the unidimensional scalar coefficients α with multidimensional, sample-adaptive coefficients γ_φ, parameterized via a sinusoidal (Fourier-like) expansion whose weights are predicted by a U-Net conditioned on the starting point x_T. The adversarial training paradigm is borrowed from CTM (Kim et al., 2024), which uses a StyleGAN-XL discriminator to refine a pre-trained diffusion model for few-step sampling; here, the discriminator's feedback is applied not only to the vector field θ but also to the inference plan φ. Conceptually, the paper departs from the dominant line of trajectory optimization work (Rectified Flow, Minibatch OT, Constant Acceleration Flow) that defines optimality via simulation-free criteria such as straightness or OT alignment, instead treating the inference plan as a free variable optimized through simulation-based adversarial feedback.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MAC is a lightweight plug-in module that achieves state-of-the-art CIFAR-10 conditional FID (1.37) at only 5 NFE with 20–2000× fewer training images than distillation-based methods, making inference trajectory optimization practically accessible. The empirical finding that straight trajectories are suboptimal even in OT-trained models challenges the field's prevailing assumption that straightness is a proxy for transportation quality, suggesting that future work should evaluate trajectories by final generation quality rather than geometric properties. Open questions include whether MAC can adapt NFE per sample, condition on the full trajectory rather than just x_T, and whether its selection mechanism can be formalized as a trajectory-level policy in a control-theoretic or RL framework.
