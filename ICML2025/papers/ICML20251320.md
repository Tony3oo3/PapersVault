## Problem

Time series foundation models (FMs) remain fixed during deployment and cannot adapt their forecasts to distribution shifts, despite continuous online feedback from newly arriving data. Existing online adaptation strategies such as retraining or continual finetuning are computationally prohibitive for FMs, leaving no general, efficient method to leverage deployment-time feedback to improve FM forecasts.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251320/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

ELF operates in a rolling-window setting, consuming a context window of L=520 time steps, the FM's forecast for the next H steps, and online feedback (actual values) from the most recent M=200 time steps. It is channel-independent, processing each univariate series separately, and produces a single adapted forecast vector of length H by taking a weighted average of the FM forecast and a lightweight linear forecaster's forecast. The entire update runs on a single CPU in ~0.38 seconds per update step, with no requirement for an offline training set or GPU.

## Method

ELF consists of two components that leave the FM's parameters untouched. The ELF-Forecaster is a linear model operating in the Fourier domain (DFT → low-pass filter → complex weight matrix → zero-pad → inverse RFT), inspired by FITS but fit via closed-form L2-regularised OLS rather than gradient descent. Efficient online refitting is achieved by storing only the Gram matrix and cross-product, updating them incrementally via the Woodbury matrix identity (inverting an M×M matrix rather than L×L), and discarding high-frequency components to reduce dimensionality. The ELF-Weighter dynamically combines the two forecasts using a weighted average whose weight w_τ is produced by a three-level exponential weighting scheme: a slow weighter over all historical losses, a fast weighter over only the last B=5 update steps (for rapid adaptation to distribution shift), and a merge weighter that blends the two, drawing on Complementary Learning Systems theory. This design avoids catastrophic forgetting (linear model), requires no FM retraining, and adapts quickly to non-stationarity.

## Results

ELF improves MASE across all 5 foundation models (TTM, TimesFM, VisionTS, Chronos, Moirai) on all 9 standard datasets and 3 forecast horizons (H=30, 96, 336), with every entry in the +ELF column being negative (improvement). The largest gains are seen for VisionTS on Weather (up to −1.591 MASE at H=336) and Moirai on ETTm1 (−0.341 at H=336), while the smallest is TTM on ETTh1 H=336 (−0.006). In the head-to-head comparison against continual learning methods (Table 2), TTM+ELF achieves the best MASE on all 9 datasets (e.g., 0.770 on Weather vs. 0.790 for TTM+TAFAS) while being 10×–113× faster per update step (0.38 s vs. 3.76–43.12 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ELF is compared against four online adaptation methods (TAFAS, OneNet, OneNet-TTM, FSNet) in Table 2: TTM+ELF achieves the lowest MASE on all 9 datasets (e.g., 0.770 on Weather vs. 0.790 for TTM+TAFAS, 3.551 for OneNet-TTM, 2.043 for OneNet, 2.510 for FSNet) while being 10×–113× faster per update step (0.38 s vs. 3.76–43.12 s). Against continual finetuning of TTM (Table 6), ELF is 2506× faster (0.38 s vs. 911.52 s per update) and achieves 5.89% lower MASE (1.673 vs. 1.746). A single finetuning step of TTM (Table 7) actually degrades performance relative to zero-shot TTM on all ETT datasets, while TTM+ELF consistently outperforms both. When TTM is continually finetuned alongside ELF (Table 8), keeping TTM frozen with ELF still performs better in most cases, confirming that the lightweight forecast-adaptation approach is preferable to parameter updates. ELF also outperforms TAFAS across all four other FMs tested (TimesFM, VisionTS, Moirai, and TTM) in Table 5, with the gap widening for weaker FMs like VisionTS (e.g., 0.893 vs. 1.322 on Weather H=30).

## Relation to Prior Work

ELF sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) time series foundation models (Chronos, TTM, TimesFM, Moirai, VisionTS) that are typically deployed as fixed, non-adaptive systems; (2) the online learning literature on exponential weighting (Cesa-Bianchi & Lugosi) and complementary learning systems (McClelland et al.), which inform the ELF-Weighter's fast/slow architecture; and (3) lightweight linear forecasting in the Fourier domain (FITS, Xu et al.), which inspires the ELF-Forecaster's DFT-based architecture. The key conceptual departure from prior continual learning work (TAFAS, OneNet, FSNet) is that ELF never touches FM parameters—instead it adapts the *output forecasts* via a closed-form linear model and a dynamic ensemble weight, sidestepping catastrophic forgetting and the computational infeasibility of online gradient updates on large models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that ELF provides a drop-in, FM-agnostic mechanism (0.38s per update on CPU) to improve deployed foundation model forecasts, addressing a real gap where FMs are currently frozen at deployment. The finding that a single finetuning step actually degrades TTM's zero-shot performance (Table 7) underscores that naive adaptation strategies can be counterproductive, reinforcing the need for the lightweight, non-parameter-updating approach ELF takes. Open questions include whether combining ELF with context-adaptation methods like TAFAS yields further gains (noted as future work), how the approach behaves under severe non-stationarity beyond the tested benchmarks, and whether the channel-independence assumption limits gains on strongly coupled multivariate series.
