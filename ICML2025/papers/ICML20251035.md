## Problem

It is widely believed that purely data-driven models cannot generalize across bifurcation points in dynamical systems without physics-based priors, due to the abrupt topological changes in phase space. This paper challenges that belief by showing that context-informed Neural ODEs trained exclusively on pre-bifurcation, symmetric data can unexpectedly recover post-bifurcation symmetry-breaking behavior in a zero-shot manner, and provides a formal explanation rooted in the Poincaré-Hopf theorem.

## Input / Output

The model consumes discretized trajectory data (N trajectories of length T/Δt per environment) sampled from a restricted pre-bifurcation parameter regime, along with a low-dimensional context vector ξ_e (typically dim ξ_e = dim μ) that encodes the physical parameter. Beyond standard trajectory prediction, the method produces automatically generated bifurcation diagrams by solving the inverse problem f(x*;ξ)=0 via the neural-adjoint method and classifying fixed points through Jacobian eigenvalue analysis. A key unusual aspect is that post-bifurcation phase portraits are reconstructed in a zero-shot fashion by linearly extrapolating ξ_e beyond the training range, without any post-bifurcation data.

## Method

The architecture is a context-informed NODE (CoDA) where the vector field weights are parameterized as θ_e = θ_c + Wξ_e via a hypernetwork with low-rank decomposition, trained with standard MSE loss plus a sparsity regularizer on W and ξ_e. The central theoretical insight is that the model implicitly preserves the Poincaré index: when the learned center-to-saddle transition flips the local index from +1 to −1, Corollary 4.1 (Poincaré-Hopf for closed orbits) forces the model to generate additional center fixed points (total index +2) to maintain the sum of +1 within any preserved closed orbit, thereby spontaneously producing the double-well structure. The paper formalizes this in Proposition 4.1, showing that any C¹-close approximation of a Hamiltonian system with a center-to-saddle bifurcation and a preserved outer orbit must undergo a generalized symmetry-breaking bifurcation. Building on this, the authors introduce a Poincaré-Hopf regularizer R_PH that penalizes deviation of the computed winding number along a test contour from the desired Euler characteristic, validated on the Landau-Khalatnikov phase transition model.

## Results

On the post-bifurcation double-well Hamiltonian system, the context-informed NODE achieves MSE (×10⁻³) of 0.1517±0.068 (left well), 0.2504±0.033 (right well), and 0.1457±0.065 (outer orbit), compared to 2427±808.8, 1724±365.1, and 3943±904.8 for the vanilla NODE respectively — a reduction of over three orders of magnitude. The context-informed NODE successfully reconstructs the full double-well phase topology and hidden symmetry in a zero-shot manner, using only pre-bifurcation training data and a single post-bifurcation trajectory for adaptation. In the hallucination experiment (linear system), both free-form and Hamiltonian NODEs incorrectly generate a spurious double-well structure, with the Hamiltonian variant retaining it more persistently due to its symplectic constraint.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares the context-informed NODE (CoDA-based hypernetwork) against the vanilla free-form NODE, both trained exclusively on pre-bifurcation data and adapted with a single post-bifurcation trajectory. The context-informed NODE wins decisively across all three broken-symmetry scenarios: it reduces MSE by a factor of ~16,000× (left well), ~6,900× (right well), and ~27,000× (outer orbit) relative to the vanilla NODE. The vanilla NODE fails to capture the double-well topology outside the region covered by the adaptation data, consistent with known OOD limitations of free-form NODEs. The context-informed NODE, by contrast, reconstructs the full phase topology including the hidden symmetry, homoclinic orbits, and both wells, even in a zero-shot setting where the context vector is simply linearly extrapolated. In the hallucination experiment on the linear system (where no true double-well exists), both architectures produce spurious double-well structures, but the Hamiltonian-constrained variant retains the hallucination more persistently due to its symplectic structure preserving outer orbits.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the context-informed NODE architecture of CoDA (Kirchmeyer et al., 2022), which uses a hypernetwork with low-rank decomposition to modulate NODE weights via a learned context vector, and on the foundational Neural ODE formulation (Chen et al., 2018). It departs from the prevailing approach of incorporating physics-informed priors or cross-parameter datasets to handle bifurcation generalization (García Pérez et al., 2023; Li & Yang, 2024) by showing that no such priors are needed. The key conceptual departure is the formal interpretation of the model's OOD capability through the Poincaré-Hopf theorem, connecting topological invariants of vector fields to the implicit behavior of trained NODEs—a link not previously drawn in the learning-dynamics literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper challenges the widely held assumption that data-driven models cannot generalize across bifurcations without explicit physics priors, providing a rigorous topological explanation (Poincaré index conservation) for why context-informed NODEs succeed. The proposed Poincaré-Hopf regularization offers a principled, physics-free inductive bias for improving long-term trajectory consistency, validated on Landau-Khalatnikov phase transitions. However, the current method is restricted to 2D flows with nearly closed orbits, and the zero-shot exploration relies on linear extrapolation of context vectors, limiting immediate applicability to higher-dimensional or strongly dissipative systems. Open questions include whether the topological mechanism generalizes to non-Hamiltonian, high-dimensional, or stochastic settings, and whether the hallucination phenomenon (spurious double-wells from linear systems) can be systematically prevented.
