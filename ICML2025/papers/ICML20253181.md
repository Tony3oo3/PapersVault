## Problem

Existing image steganography methods reveal all hidden secret images simultaneously without verifying recipient identity, creating unauthorized access risks in multi-recipient scenarios. While adding authentication (lock-key) information is natural, the paper shows through experiments that such information has an inconsistent distribution with secret images, occupying extra hiding space and degrading both stego and revealed image quality. Additionally, serial hiding strategies require a separate network per secret image, causing linear growth in model size and computational cost.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB cover image (144×144 in training; 1024×1024 for DIV2K test, 256×256 for ImageNet test) and N RGB secret images of the same spatial size, producing one stego image. During revealing, each recipient supplies a dynamically generated key (derived from the stego image and their revealed compressed secret image via a shared convolutional module) to independently recover their secret image. Only 3 channels of extracted primary information per secret image are actually embedded, while 9 channels of high-frequency redundancy are omitted and predicted at reveal time by a Redundancy Prediction Module.

## Method

AIS is a two-stage invertible architecture: an Invertible Authentication Network (IAN) and an Invertible Hiding Network (IHN). IAN applies Haar downsampling to split each secret image into 3-channel low-frequency and 9-channel high-frequency components, then uses coupling-layer invertible blocks (with DenseNet-based α, β, γ mappings conditioned on a dynamically generated lock) to fuse the lock into the secret image for distribution adaptation, outputting 3-channel extracted primary information. IHN applies DWT to the cover image and embeds the concatenated 3-channel information from all N secret images in parallel through its own coupling-layer blocks, requiring only a single trained network regardless of N. The key insight is that by performing authentication separately in IAN (fusing locks into secret images before hiding), the fused information has a consistent latent distribution, reducing the hiding space consumed by authentication and enabling parallel rather than serial hiding. Training uses a composite loss combining MSE for stego/secret fidelity, Jensen-Shannon divergence, triplet loss to penalize wrong-key reveals, MAE key matching, and redundancy prediction loss.

## Results

On DIV2K hiding 5 secret images, AIS achieves 36.149 dB PSNR and 0.887 SSIM for cover-stego similarity, and 30.765 dB PSNR, 0.854 SSIM, 1.141 LPIPS for secret-reveal quality. Compared to the best baseline (IIS), AIS improves stego PSNR by 9.47 dB and revealed-image PSNR by 2.09 dB while using only 6.15M parameters versus IIS's 55.62M. In terms of efficiency, AIS requires 204.3G FLOPs and 121.1 ms inference time, versus 1793.0G FLOPs and 439.6 ms for IIS. On ImageNet, similar gains are observed with SSIM improvements of 0.043 (stego) and 0.026 (revealed) over the strongest baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AIS outperforms all three baselines on every quality metric (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS) for both stego and revealed images across all hiding capacities (N=2–5) on both DIV2K and ImageNet. Against ISN (the lightest baseline at 3.68M params), AIS trades a modest parameter increase (6.15M) for a 9.4 dB PSNR gain on stego images and a 3.4 dB gain on revealed images at N=5, while also reducing FLOPs from 480.9G to 204.3G. Against DeepMIH (33.48M params, serial hiding), AIS achieves 6.7 dB higher stego PSNR and 8.6 dB higher reveal PSNR at N=5, with 5.4× fewer parameters and 5.8× fewer FLOPs. Against IIS (the only other authentication-based baseline, 55.62M params), AIS delivers 9.5 dB higher stego PSNR, 2.1 dB higher reveal PSNR, and 9× fewer parameters at N=5, confirming that separate authentication enables parallel hiding with a single network. The only metric where AIS does not lead is raw inference time versus ISN (121.1 ms vs 51.7 ms), reflecting the additional IAN authentication stage, though AIS remains 2.2× faster than DeepMIH and 3.6× faster than IIS.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the authentication-based image steganography line initiated by IIS (Zhou et al., 2024), which introduced dynamic lock-key generation via conditional invertible neural networks, and the broader conditional normalizing flow framework of Ardizzone et al. (2019). The key departure is architectural: rather than embedding authentication information serially alongside secret images (as in IIS and DeepMIH), AIS separates authentication (IAN) and hiding (IHN) into two collaborative stages, enabling parallel hiding of multiple secret images through a single network. The distribution adaptation between locks and secret images, combined with primary information extraction, is a novel mechanism not present in prior authenticated steganography methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 9× parameter reduction (6.15M vs. 55.62M at N=5) while simultaneously improving stego and revealed image quality addresses a practical bottleneck for large-capacity authenticated steganography, making isolated multi-recipient reception feasible without prohibitive computational cost. A significant open question is the security-robustness trade-off: the authentication mechanism's sensitivity to key-lock mismatch makes the system vulnerable to real-world perturbations (JPEG compression, noise), a limitation the authors acknowledge but do not resolve. Future work on noise-robust key enhancement and attention-based key-lock matching could close this gap, and the two-stage separation principle may generalize to other conditional invertible tasks.
