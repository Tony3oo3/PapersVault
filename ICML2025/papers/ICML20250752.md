## Problem

Existing Audio-Language Models (ALMs) underperform on expert-level audio reasoning tasks—Gemini-1.5-Pro achieves only 54.4% on MMAU sound—due to insufficient high-quality reasoning training data and weak audio representations. Additionally, no ALM or dataset supports understanding audio longer than 30 seconds, leaving long-audio comprehension entirely unexplored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250752/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts audio clips up to 5 minutes, segmented into non-overlapping 10-second windows (up to 30 windows) whose dense HTSAT features (64×2048 per window) are temporally encoded with RoPE (base 4096), alongside a free-form text question or prompt. Outputs are generated text: open-ended answers, multiple-choice selections, audio captions, or class labels, depending on the task. For long-audio evaluation, the model processes the full 5-minute input natively rather than relying on cascaded short-segment captioning.

## Method

AF2 combines three key innovations: (1) AF-CLAP, a CLAP encoder scaled to 8M+ audio-caption pairs with a modified contrastive loss that treats linguistically varied rephrasings as additional positives and compositionally altered captions (e.g., swapping temporal order) as hard negatives, improving both linguistic invariance and compositional reasoning; (2) AudioSkills, a 4.2M synthetic AQA dataset targeting seven reasoning skills (temporal order, attribute identification, counting, contextual sound/speech reasoning, information extraction, general reasoning); and (3) LongAudio, 263K AQA pairs over 30 s–5 min audios spanning six task types. Architecturally, dense AF-CLAP features pass through three self-attention transformation layers and are injected into a frozen Qwen2.5-3B LLM via gated cross-attention (XATTN-Dense) layers before every transformer block, reducing attention complexity from quadratic to linear. Training follows a 3-stage curriculum that progressively extends context from 30 s to 1.5 min to 5 min while unfreezing the CLAP encoder and transformation layers in stages 2–3.

## Results

AF2 (3B LLM + 203M audio encoder) achieves state-of-the-art audio reasoning with 65.1% on MMAU Sound, 72.9% on MMAU Music, 96.4% on CompA-R, and 64.2% on the newly proposed LongAudioBench, surpassing all prior LALMs by large margins (e.g., +16.4% over Gemini Flash v2 on MMAU Music, +29.1% over Qwen Audio on Audio Entailment/AudioCaps). On foundational tasks, AF2 remains competitive with half the parameter count of 7B baselines (e.g., 86.9% on ClothoAQA vs. 74.9% for Qwen2-Audio; 69.1% zero-shot GTZAN vs. 65.2% for Audio Flamingo). The model also sets new records on music understanding (93.0% MusicQA, 90.2% MusicInstruct-Long) and hallucination detection (82.0% CMM).

## Baselines & Comparisons

AF2 decisively outperforms Gemini Flash v2 (the strongest proprietary baseline) on all three reasoning benchmarks it is compared against: MMAU Sound (+3.4%), MMAU Music (+16.4%), and LongAudioBench (+18.9%), despite using a 3B LLM versus Gemini's much larger architecture. Against open-source 7B LALMs, AF2 wins by wide margins on expert reasoning: +16.4% over GAMA-IT on CompA-R, +29.1% over Qwen Audio on Audio Entailment/AudioCaps, and +6.0% over SALMONN on CMM Hallucination. On foundational classification and captioning (Table 1), AF2 is competitive with 7B models like Qwen2-Audio and Audio Flamingo (e.g., +12.0% on ClothoAQA-unanimous, +10.1% on CREMA-D zero-shot) but trails Pengi on CochlScene (−9.5%) and NSsource (−1.9%), which the authors attribute to limitations of the CLAP-retrieval-based evaluation protocol. Among CLAP encoder baselines, AF-CLAP surpasses Laion-CLAP, MS-CLAP, and CompA-CLAP on both retrieval (e.g., 46.9% R@1 Audio-to-Text on AudioCaps vs. 46.8% for Laion-CLAP) and zero-shot classification (e.g., 91.3% ESC-50 vs. 88.2% for Laion-CLAP). The paper also shows that replacing AF-CLAP with any prior CLAP drops MMAU average by 2–5 points (Table 3), and that AF2's cross-attention conditioning outperforms GAMA's prefix-tuning by +16.2 points on MMAU when trained on identical data (Table 5).

## Relation to Prior Work

AF2 is a direct successor to Audio Flamingo (Kong et al., 2024), retaining the Flamingo-style gated cross-attention conditioning architecture but replacing prefix tuning with cross-attention for linear complexity. It departs from the CLAP lineage (Elizalde et al., 2023; Wu et al., 2022b) by scaling training data to 8M pairs and introducing a modified contrastive loss with composition-aware negatives to address known weaknesses in linguistic invariance and compositional reasoning. Conceptually, the paper positions itself against the prevailing trend in LALMs (GAMA, Qwen-Audio, LTU, SALMONN) of scaling model size and dataset volume, arguing instead that skill-specific synthetic data (AudioSkills) and improved audio representations (AF-CLAP) are the critical levers for expert-level reasoning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AF2 establishes the first practical framework for long-audio understanding (30s–5min) and introduces LongAudio/LongAudioBench, filling a gap the authors explicitly identify as absent from prior literature. The finding that a 3B LLM with high-quality skill-specific data outperforms 7B+ open-source and proprietary models (Gemini) on reasoning benchmarks challenges the field's default assumption that scaling parameters is the primary path to better audio reasoning. Key limitations remain: speech content understanding is underdeveloped, AudioSkills relies on GPT-4o-generated questions that may inherit LLM biases, and the audio encoder still requires sliding-window concatenation rather than native long-context processing. The open question of whether these gains transfer to truly open-ended, multi-turn audio dialogue remains unaddressed.
