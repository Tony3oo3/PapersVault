## Problem

Score Distillation Sampling (SDS) enables text-to-3D generation by distilling 2D diffusion priors into 3D representations, but it cannot edit existing 3D assets. Conversely, editing variants like Delta Denoising Score (DDS) and Posterior Distillation Sampling (PDS) introduce editing capability but fail to generate new 3D content effectively, and they require extremely high classifier-free guidance weights (CFG=100) that cause over-saturation and instability. No prior method unifies both tasks under a single, stable gradient formulation.

## Input / Output

For editing, UDS takes an existing 3D scene represented as NeRF or 3D Gaussian Splatting, a source text prompt describing the current state, and a target text prompt describing the desired edit; it outputs a modified 3D scene. For generation, it takes only a text prompt and outputs a novel 3D asset. The method operates entirely in the latent space of a pre-trained Stable Diffusion model (SD 2.1 for main experiments, SD 1.5 for SVG), using DDIM inverse processes to obtain noisy latents x_t at arbitrary timesteps rather than sampling fresh Gaussian noise. A key practical distinction is that UDS uses a CFG weight of 7.5 versus 100 for DDS/PDS, reducing over-saturation while maintaining text alignment.

## Method

UDS unifies generation and editing by decomposing the SDS gradient into a reconstruction term (Δx̂₀) and a classifier-free guidance term (w·Δδ_cls), showing that both tasks share this structure. For editing, the reconstruction term is x̂₀^tgt − x̂₀^src, which explicitly preserves identity information from the source scene; for generation, it is x̂₀^t − x̂₀^{t−c}, a two-timestep difference that captures temporal detail preservation analogous to Interval Score Matching. The x̂₀ approximation is computed via DDIM's deterministic reverse process (multi-step) rather than single-step Tweedie's formula, yielding more accurate clean-latent estimates that better preserve structural details. Critically, UDS uses unconditional noise predictions ε_φ(x_t, t, ∅) in the x̂₀ approximation rather than conditional ones as in PDS, which reallocates weight between the reconstruction and guidance terms and enables stable optimization at low CFG (7.5). This formulation produces lower gradient variability than DDS or PDS, as confirmed by normalized gradient norm analysis, and achieves state-of-the-art CLIP scores and user preference rates on both 3D editing (NeRF, 3D GS, SVG) and text-to-3D generation benchmarks.

## Results

On text-to-3D generation (NeRF), UDS achieves a CLIP score of 0.2962 and 42.14% user preference rate, outperforming DreamFusion (0.2838, 8.34%), Fantasia3D (0.2813, 13.30%), and ProlificDreamer (0.2719, 36.22%). On 3D GS generation, UDS reaches 0.2984 CLIP and 48.37% user preference, surpassing LucidDreamer ISM (0.2897, 41.39%). For 3D editing, UDS attains 0.2498 CLIP and 41.52% user preference, beating PDS (0.2395, 30.20%), IN2N (0.2334, 23.76%), and DDS (0.2030, 4.52%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

UDS outperforms all seven baselines across every task and metric. In 3D editing, UDS surpasses the strongest editing baseline PDS by +0.0103 CLIP and +11.32% user preference, while DDS lags far behind (0.2030 CLIP, 4.52% preference) due to its lack of an identity-preservation term. In 3D generation (NeRF), UDS leads ProlificDreamer by +0.0243 CLIP and +5.92% user preference, and dramatically outperforms DreamFusion (+0.0124 CLIP, +33.8% preference). For 3D GS generation, UDS edges out LucidDreamer ISM by +0.0087 CLIP and +6.98% user preference. In SVG editing, UDS achieves the best LPIPS (0.3489) and CLIP (0.2576), beating PDS (0.3803, 0.2432) and SDS (0.4381, 0.2507), confirming that the unified gradient formulation generalizes beyond NeRF and 3D GS representations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Score Distillation Sampling (SDS) lineage from DreamFusion, specifically analyzing two editing variants—Delta Denoising Score (DDS) and Posterior Distillation Sampling (PDS)—and showing that their gradient terms, when decomposed into reconstruction and classifier-free guidance components, share a unified algebraic structure with DDIM-based generation methods like ISM (LucidDreamer). The key departure is replacing the noise-prediction-based reconstruction term with an approximate x₀ (via Tweedie's formula or multi-step DDIM inversion), which simultaneously provides the identity preservation needed for editing and the detail retention needed for generation. Unlike PDS, which uses conditional noise for x₀ approximation and requires CFG=100, UDS uses unconditional noise, enabling a standard CFG weight of 7.5 while maintaining comparable editing fidelity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By unifying 3D generation and editing under a single gradient formulation, UDS removes the need to choose between generation-focused methods (SDS, VSD, ISM) and editing-focused methods (DDS, PDS), simplifying the practitioner's toolkit. The lower CFG requirement (7.5 vs. 100) avoids the over-saturation and reduced diversity that plague high-CFG SDS variants. However, the method still inherits fundamental limitations of SDS-based approaches: generation quality depends heavily on initialization, editing fails when the semantic gap between source and target prompts is large, and multi-step DDIM inversion for better identity preservation increases optimization cost. Open questions include whether the unified formulation extends to more complex editing operations (e.g., multi-object, structural changes) and whether the x₀ approximation can be further refined to reduce the trade-off between identity preservation and text alignment.
