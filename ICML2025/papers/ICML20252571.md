## Problem

Standard Wasserstein Distributionally Robust Optimization (WDRO) is overly conservative in small-sample regimes, leading to suboptimal transfer learning performance. While shrinkage-based transfer learning methods exist, the integration of prior knowledge into the WDRO ambiguity set has remained an open question, and existing approaches lack a unified distributional-robustness interpretation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252571/cropped_page5_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a target dataset of covariate-outcome pairs (X, Y) and one or more pre-trained source model parameter vectors θ (estimated from source data via vanilla WDRO). The prior knowledge enters as a direction in parameter space rather than a point estimate, so the method is invariant to the scaling of θ. The output is a robust target parameter vector β, with the regularization automatically adapting to covariate scaling differences between source and target. Hyperparameters (ambiguity radius δ and confidence level λ) are tuned via grid-search cross-validation on a held-out validation set.

## Method

KG-WDRO augments the standard Wasserstein transport cost with a penalty on the perturbation projected onto the prior knowledge direction θ, effectively shrinking the ambiguity set where prior knowledge is reliable. In the strong-transferring regime (λ=∞), the constraint θ^TΔ=0 forces the prediction θ^TX to be invariant under perturbation, yielding an exact equivalence (via Toland's Duality) to p-norm shrinkage toward the linear span of the source parameters. In the weak-transferring regime (λ<∞), the penalty produces a Mahalanobis-type norm with a matrix Ψ_λ that interpolates between standard ridge regression (λ→0) and orthogonal projection onto θ (λ→∞), allowing the data to self-determine source informativeness. The key geometric insight is that the penalty measures collinearity (distance to the span of θ) rather than algebraic difference, which automatically handles scaling and prevents negative transfer by adapting sign. The framework unifies ridge-type and lasso-type regularization, supports multi-source ensembles, and extends to both linear regression and binary classification (logistic and hinge losses).

## Results

On the 2020 U.S. presidential election dataset (county-level binary classification across 8 target states), KG-WDRO achieves an overall log-loss of 147.39, reducing it by 7.6% compared to Trans-GLM (159.49) and by 52.7% compared to vanilla WDRO (311.77). In high-dimensional sparse logistic regression simulations (N=20, d=150), KG-WDRO with ℓ₁-strong transferring outperforms Trans-GLM by up to 2 percentage points in out-of-sample accuracy (e.g., 0.800 vs. 0.790 at ρ=0.95). In correlated linear regression (d=100, N=50), KG-WDRO achieves R²=0.585 at ρ=0.3, substantially exceeding Trans-Ridge (0.391) and vanilla WDRO (0.108).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Trans-GLM (Tian & Feng, 2023), KG-WDRO wins on 5 of 8 states in the real election data and achieves up to 2% higher classification accuracy in small-sample (N=20) logistic regression simulations, though the two methods converge at larger sample sizes. Against Trans-Ridge and Trans-Lasso (Li et al., 2021), KG-WDRO consistently dominates at low-to-moderate truth-prior correlation (ρ ≤ 0.7), with gaps as large as 19 percentage points in R² (e.g., 0.585 vs. 0.391 at ρ=0.3, N=50); Trans-Ridge only catches up at ρ ≥ 0.9. Against vanilla WDRO (Blanchet et al., 2019a), both transfer learning methods (KG-WDRO and the competing baselines) substantially outperform the target-only estimator across all settings, with the gap widening as sample size decreases (e.g., R² of 0.585 vs. 0.108 at N=50, ρ=0.3). In the multi-source setting, KG-WDRO outperforms the oracle Trans-Lasso when source contributions are unequal ([a,b,c]=[1,−0.5,0.2]) but matches it when sources are equally weighted ([a,b,c]=[1,1,1]).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Wasserstein DRO framework (Blanchet et al., 2019a; Blanchet & Murthy, 2019) by modifying the transport cost to incorporate prior knowledge, thereby shrinking the ambiguity set along directions informed by a source model. It departs from existing shrinkage-based transfer learning methods (Trans-GLM, Trans-Ridge, Trans-Lasso) by showing they are special cases of a knowledge-guided WDRO problem, providing a unified distributional-robustness interpretation that was previously absent. The use of Toland's Duality to handle the nonconvex inner maximization is a technical departure from the standard convex-conjugate approach used in vanilla WDRO, enabling more general cost functions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a single, principled lens through which a broad family of ad hoc shrinkage-based transfer learning methods can be understood, potentially guiding future method design. Its flexibility (ridge/lasso, multi-source, scale adjustment, binary/continuous outcomes) makes it practically relevant for small-sample settings like clinical trials with underrepresented populations. However, the paper explicitly leaves open the question of statistical guarantees for the KG-WDRO estimator and a principled (non-grid-search) approach to selecting the hyperparameters δ and λ, which limits immediate deployment. The empirical gains over strong baselines like Trans-GLM are modest (1–2% in the best cases), suggesting the framework's primary value is conceptual unification rather than dramatic performance leaps.
