## Problem

Existing neural GED benchmarks suffer from pervasive train-test leakage caused by isomorphic graphs appearing in both training and test splits, inflating performance metrics by up to 95% and masking poor generalization. Additionally, nearly all benchmarks restrict evaluation to uniform-cost GED, ignoring the variable-cost setting that underpins GED's generality as a unifying framework for subgraph isomorphism, maximum common subgraph, and asymmetric similarity. These flaws have gone unnoticed for years, leading to incorrect conclusions about model capabilities and discouraging algorithmically aligned architectures.

## Input / Output

The framework takes a raw graph dataset (e.g., LINUX, AIDS, Code2, Mutag, Molhiv, Molpcba) and produces leakage-free train/validation/test splits of graph pairs annotated with ground-truth GED values across multiple variable-cost settings C0–C6. A key structural detail is that both graphs in each pair are padded with artificial nodes to equal size N, with indicator vectors distinguishing real from dummy nodes, so that the alignment is always a full N×N permutation matrix. The output for each pair includes the GED scalar under each cost setting, the optimal node alignment P*, and the induced edit path (a sequence of node/edge insertions, deletions, and substitutions).

## Method

The core theoretical contribution is Theorem 4.2, which proves that when c_NS = 0 and all other costs are positive, the set of optimal edit paths E*(G,G') is invariant across all compatible cost settings—only the GED value changes, not the alignment P*. This invariance enables a three-stage pipeline: (1) GENERATEPAIRS removes isomorphic duplicates via explicit isomorphism checks and splits the unique set into disjoint train/val/test partitions, eliminating leakage; (2) COMPUTEOPTIMALPATHS computes P* once per pair using the MIPF2 binary linear programming solver under a fixed cost C0; (3) GENERATECOSTVARIANTS reuses P* to compute GED across K different cost settings via a closed-form expression (Eq. 1) involving counts of node/edge additions, deletions, and substitutions, avoiding redundant alignment computations. For adversarial evaluation, models trained under C0 are tested against unseen cost settings C1–C6, revealing whether they recover the true optimal alignment or merely memorize dataset-specific patterns; Ktau correlation is used as the primary metric since MSE values are not comparable across cost magnitudes.

## Results

On the LINUX dataset (91.3% intra-test leakage), removing isomorphic duplicates causes ISONET's MSE to surge from 0.323 to 5.146 and Ktau to drop from 0.903 to 0.646, while ERIC's MSE rises only from 0.049 to 1.013. On AIDS (w/o label, 21.1% leakage), the performance drop is milder but consistent across all 11 models. Under adversarial cost-variant testing on Code2 (Table 3), GRAPHEDX achieves the lowest MSE on high edge-cost settings (C3: 2102 vs. ~10,100 for node-centric models), while most models show Ktau drops of up to 0.4 when evaluated on unseen cost configurations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This position paper does not propose a new GED model but rather a rigorous evaluation protocol; the 'comparison' is how 11 existing neural GED models perform under leakage-free conditions. GRAPHEDX (Jain et al., NeurIPS 2024) is the most robust model, showing the smallest relative MSE increase on LINUX (0.052→0.827) and the highest post-leakage Ktau (0.844), consistent with its explicit node-and-edge alignment design for variable costs. ISONET (Roy et al., AAAI 2022) suffers the largest collapse on LINUX (MSE 0.323→5.146, Ktau 0.903→0.646), revealing that its previously reported strong performance was largely an artifact of memorizing isomorphic graph pairs. ERIC (Zhuo & Tan, NeurIPS 2022) and EGSC (Qin et al., NeurIPS 2021) show moderate degradation, while GEDGNN (Piao et al., VLDB 2023) performs worst overall with near-zero or negative Ktau even with leakage. Under adversarial cost-variant testing (Table 3, Code2), node-centric models (SimGNN, GraphSim, H2MN) achieve MSE ~10,000 on high edge-cost settings C3–C4, whereas GRAPHEDX achieves ~2,100, confirming that edge-aware alignment is critical for generalizing across cost configurations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself as a methodological critique of the entire neural GED estimation literature, building on the MIPF2 combinatorial solver (Lerouge et al., 2017) for ground-truth computation and the QAP formulation of GED. It departs from prior work by identifying that the standard benchmarks (LINUX, IMDB, AIDS) used by SimGNN, ISONET, GRAPHEDX, and others suffer from pervasive isomorphism-based train-test leakage, and by proving a cost-invariance theorem (Theorem 4.2) that the optimal edit path is unchanged across a broad range of variable-cost settings. Rather than proposing a new architecture, the paper repurposes existing combinatorial solvers and the GNN-based models they evaluate to expose evaluation flaws and to construct a principled, leakage-free benchmarking protocol with adversarial cost-variant test sets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that up to 94.8% of test pairs in LINUX are leaked fundamentally undermines years of reported performance comparisons in neural GED research, suggesting that many apparent model advantages may be memorization artifacts. The cost-invariance theorem provides a practical tool for generating large-scale, multi-cost training data from a single alignment computation, addressing the NP-hardness bottleneck that has constrained dataset diversity. Open questions include whether the leakage issue extends to other graph learning tasks (node classification, link prediction), whether models can be designed to genuinely generalize across cost settings rather than overfitting to a single configuration, and how to scale exact GED computation to graphs beyond a few dozen nodes for rigorous evaluation.
