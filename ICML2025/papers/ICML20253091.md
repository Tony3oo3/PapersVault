## Problem

Fractional-order linear time-invariant (FOLTI) systems capture long-range dependence and memory effects that integer-order models cannot, yet no analytical LQR solution, end-to-end learning framework, or sample complexity guarantee existed for their optimal control. The non-Markovian nature of FOLTI dynamics—where the current state depends on an entire history of past states—renders classical system identification and control strategies inapplicable.

## Input / Output

The framework consumes N observed trajectories of T time steps, each providing state vectors x_k ∈ R^n and control inputs u_k ∈ R^m, together with cost matrices Q, R, and terminal cost Q_f. It outputs the full optimal control sequence U ∈ R^{T×m} and, as an intermediate byproduct, the estimated system parameters (A, B, α). Notably, system parameters serve as a physical-constraint signal during training but are entirely unnecessary at test time, where only the trajectory and cost matrices are required.

## Method

FOLOC is a two-module end-to-end architecture. The system identification module uses an RNN to estimate (A, B, α) from trajectories, then an MLP with time embeddings to approximate the time-varying matrices A_k, followed by an Encoder-only Transformer to learn the convolution matrices G_k that encode fractional-order memory. The optimal control module exploits the fact that the LQR solution via Lagrange multipliers reduces to inverting a block Toeplitz matrix (I − G_λ), and uses a Fourier Neural Operator to directly learn the mapping from the reduced block-Toeplitz variables to the optimal control sequence, bypassing explicit matrix inversion. Training minimizes a weighted composite loss combining parameter-identification error and control-prediction error, enabling joint optimization of both modules in a single supervised pass.

## Results

On the cart-pole dynamics, FOLOC achieves an MSE of 0.00004 and MAE of 0.0034, reducing MSE by up to 99.99% and MAE by up to 99.32% compared to all baselines, while also being the fastest method at 0.0004 s per inference. On the quadrotor dynamics, FOLOC achieves MSE 0.211 and MAE 0.127, outperforming the next-best method (MBRL at MSE 0.489) by 57% in MSE, with a runtime of 0.00027 s—over 100× faster than CMA-ES (115 s) and MPC (72 s). On synthetic FOLTI data, FOLOC maintains a mean MSE of ~0.40% across fractional orders α ∈ {0.1, 0.3, 0.5, 0.7, 0.9} and remains robust to non-Gaussian noise (Cauchy, Gamma, Poisson, Uniform, Sinc-squared) with at most 24% MSE increase over Gaussian noise.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FOLOC dominates all five baselines (MBRL, CMA-ES, MPC, PID, iLQR) on both cart-pole and quadrotor tasks in both accuracy and speed. On cart-pole, FOLOC's MSE of 0.00004 is roughly 4,875× lower than the best baseline (MBRL at 0.195), and its 0.0004 s inference time is 11× faster than PID (0.001 s), the next-fastest method. On quadrotor, FOLOC achieves MSE 0.211 vs. MBRL's 0.489 (57% lower) and MAE 0.127 vs. MBRL's 0.546 (77% lower), while running 1,426× faster than CMA-ES (115 s) and 266× faster than MPC (72 s). The evolutionary and iterative methods (CMA-ES, iLQR) are the most accurate among baselines but suffer from prohibitive runtimes, while PID is fast but least accurate. FOLOC's end-to-end architecture effectively combines the accuracy of model-based methods with the speed of feedforward inference, making it suitable for real-time fractional-order control.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the classical LQR framework (Kalman, 1960) from integer-order LTI systems to fractional-order LTI (FOLTI) systems, where the Grünwald-Letnikov difference operator introduces non-Markovian memory effects that invalidate standard control theory. Theoretically, it builds on the finite-horizon fractional LQR of Li & Chen (2008) but removes their restrictive assumptions and provides the first closed-form solutions (via least squares and Lagrange multipliers) along with sample complexity bounds. Architecturally, the FOLOC framework repurposes the Fourier Neural Operator (Li et al., 2021) and neural Krylov iteration (Luo et al., 2024) to solve the block Toeplitz linear system arising from the fractional LQR, while using RNNs and Transformers for the system identification module. It departs from prior system identification work (Dean et al., Tu & Recht) by operating without Gaussian noise assumptions in the deep learning pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FOLTI systems model long-range dependent phenomena in neuroscience, bioengineering, and financial markets that integer-order models fundamentally cannot capture, yet no prior work provided both a theoretical LQR solution and a practical end-to-end learning framework for them. The main limitation is the LTI assumption and the theoretical approach's requirement that diagonal elements of A be known a priori; the FOLOC framework mitigates this but has only been validated on two real-world systems (cart-pole, quadrotor). Open questions include extension to non-LTI and non-commensurate fractional-order dynamics, infinite-horizon guarantees, and whether the FNO-based solver generalizes to higher-dimensional or time-varying fractional systems.
