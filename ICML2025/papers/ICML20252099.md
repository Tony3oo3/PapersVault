## Problem

While prior work has studied either the training dynamics (implicit bias to max-margin token selection) or the generalization of attention mechanisms, it remains unclear how token selection behaves and whether it generalizes when training labels are corrupted. This paper asks: under label noise, how do the training dynamics of attention-based token selection evolve, and does the resulting solution still generalize well?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252099/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a sequence of T d-dimensional input tokens plus a single tunable [CLS] token p, and outputs a scalar score that is thresholded for binary classification. The linear prediction head ν is fixed (pretrained), so all optimization occurs within the softmax attention weights W and the tunable token p. The data model partitions tokens into three groups: one relevant token carrying a strong class signal, weakly relevant tokens carrying a scaled-down (ρ ≪ 1) and potentially confusing signal, and irrelevant tokens containing only Gaussian noise.

## Method

The paper provides a complete theoretical analysis of gradient descent on a one-layer cross-attention model f(X) = νᵀXᵀS(XWᵀp) with a fixed head, characterizing token selection dynamics via an 'attention gap' whose evolution is governed by the function g(x) = 2x + 2sinh(x − log T). Under a high signal-to-noise ratio (SNR² = ω(n⁻¹)), the model selects the relevant token for all samples and ignores noisy labels (not-overfitting regime). Under a low SNR (SNR² = o(n⁻¹)), the model performs distinct token selection: for clean samples it selects the relevant token, while for noisy samples it first suppresses the relevant token (Stage 1) then selects the confusing weakly relevant token (Stage 2), fitting all training data yet still generalizing (benign overfitting regime). A key technical difficulty is that the learning direction depends on softmax probability ratios across examples at each time step, not just the clean-to-noisy sample ratio, requiring careful inductive tracking of competing signal-learning and noise-memorization forces. The analysis also reveals a grokking-like phenomenon: fitting the training set occurs at a polynomial time scale, but achieving generalization requires an additional exponential-in-SR⁻² factor of training time.

## Results

The main theoretical result (Theorem 4.1) establishes that benign overfitting in token selection occurs when SNR² = o(n⁻¹): the model fits all training data (including label noise) yet achieves test error < δ, requiring an exponential training time of Θ(exp(n⁻¹·SNR⁻²)) steps. In the not-overfitting regime (SNR² = ω(n⁻¹)), the model fits only clean samples and generalizes well without memorizing noise. Real-world experiments training only the final attention layer of pre-trained ViT/BERT on label-noisy data (η=0.1) confirm these predictions: CIFAR-10 achieves 95.1–95.9% test accuracy across training sizes n=20–1000, MNIST reaches 88.5–91.4%, and PneumoniaMNIST achieves 76.2–81.0%, with training loss near zero in most cases, demonstrating benign overfitting behavior.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is primarily a theoretical paper, so there are no direct method-vs-method baselines. Instead, the paper validates its theoretical predictions by comparing three SNR regimes (harmful overfitting, benign overfitting, not overfitting) in synthetic experiments and by varying training size n on real-world datasets to observe regime transitions. In the synthetic setting, the benign overfitting case (d=2000, ‖μ‖₂=20) achieves 100% training and test accuracy, while the harmful overfitting case (d=5000, ‖μ‖₂=5) drops to 87% test accuracy, and the not-overfitting case (d=1000, ‖μ‖₂=100) achieves 100% test accuracy with only 80% training accuracy. On real-world data, CIFAR-10 and TREC exhibit the predicted transition from not-overfitting to benign overfitting as n decreases (training loss increases while test accuracy remains stable), while AG-news shows harmful overfitting (test accuracy drops from 83.6% to 77.6% as n increases from 20 to 1000). The paper positions its contribution relative to prior attention theory (Tarzanagh et al., 2023; Oymak et al., 2023; Jiang et al., 2024; Magen et al., 2024) by being the first to analyze both training dynamics and generalization under label noise with a more general data model including weakly relevant tokens.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on implicit bias and training dynamics of one-layer attention (Tarzanagh et al., Vasudeva et al., Sheen et al.) by adding a generalization analysis under label noise, and builds on the benign overfitting framework for transformers initiated by Jiang et al. (2024) and Magen et al. (2024). The key departure is the introduction of label noise, which creates competing training directions (signal learning vs. noise memorization, clean vs. noisy samples) that are mediated by softmax probabilities and cannot be resolved by simple sample-count arguments. The model setup follows Oymak et al. (2023) with a tunable [CLS] token, but the analysis goes far beyond the initial few gradient steps studied there, tracking the full training dynamics through an inductive argument on attention gaps.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first theoretical characterization showing that benign overfitting can arise solely from the token selection mechanism (softmax weights) of attention, without training the linear head, which has practical implications for understanding parameter-efficient fine-tuning methods like prompt tuning and LoRA. The connection to grokking—where fitting training data occurs quickly but generalization requires exponentially longer training in the low-SNR regime—offers a mechanistic explanation for delayed generalization in attention-based models. Limitations include the restriction to binary classification, one-layer attention, a fixed pretrained head, and a specific signal-plus-noise data model; extending to multi-layer transformers, next-token prediction, and more realistic data distributions remains open.
