## Problem

Video diffusion transformers are trained on fixed-length sequences, and existing RoPE-based length extrapolation methods (PE, PI, NTK, YaRN, TASR) developed for text and image generation fail on video, producing temporal repetition or motion deceleration. The paper identifies the root cause: a specific 'intrinsic frequency' component in RoPE whose period governs when repetition occurs, and shows that prior methods either violate the non-repetition condition or inadvertently suppress high frequencies needed for fast motion.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20253116/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained video diffusion transformer (CogVideoX-5B at 480×720×49 or HunyuanVideo at 544×960×129) and a text prompt, and outputs a longer video (2× or 3× the training frame count) or a higher-resolution video/image. For 2× extrapolation, no additional training is required; for 3×, only 20,000 original-length videos are used for fine-tuning (≈1/50,000 of pre-training compute). The same frequency adjustment extends naturally to spatial resolution extrapolation and joint temporal-spatial extension (e.g., 480×720×49 → 960×1440×97).

## Method

RIFLEx identifies a single 'intrinsic frequency' component k in the RoPE schedule whose period N_k is closest to the first observed repetition frame in generated videos, then reduces only that frequency to θ_k' = 2π/(Ls) so its period exceeds the extrapolated length, guaranteeing no repetition within the target range. This one-parameter modification requires no architectural changes, no new modules, and no long-video data. For 2× extrapolation the method is entirely training-free; for 3×, a brief fine-tune on original-length videos compensates for the larger train-test mismatch in positional encodings. The paper further provides a principled explanation for existing failures: PE and NTK leave the intrinsic frequency above the non-repetition threshold (causing loops), while PI and YaRN divide high-frequency components by s, suppressing the rapid positional changes needed for fast motion.

## Results

On CogVideoX-5B with 2× temporal extrapolation (training-free), RIFLEx achieves a NoRepeat Score of 54.2 and Dynamic Degree of 59.4, closely matching the no-extrapolation baseline (Dynamic 67.5) while avoiding the repetition (PE: 46.6, NTK: 43.4) or slow-motion (PI: 5.0, YaRN: 5.6) failures of all baselines. In the user study, RIFLEx ranks #1 in Overall Aspects (1.1) versus PE (2.4), NTK (2.1), PI (3.8), TASR (3.6), and YaRN (3.7). With minimal fine-tuning on HunyuanVideo (2×), RIFLEx reaches NoRepeat 89.0, Dynamic Degree 82.0, and Imaging Quality 72.0—nearly matching the no-extrapolation baseline (Dynamic 79.0, Imaging 71.6) at only 1/50,000 of pre-training compute.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RIFLEx outperforms all four baselines across every evaluation setting. Against PE and NTK, which suffer from temporal repetition (NoRepeat 46.6 and 43.4 on CogVideoX-5B), RIFLEx achieves a higher NoRepeat Score (54.2) while maintaining comparable Dynamic Degree (59.4 vs. 58.6/58.3), and ranks #1 in user preference (1.1 vs. 2.4/2.1). Against PI and YaRN, which collapse into slow motion (Dynamic Degree 5.0 and 5.6), RIFLEx preserves natural motion speed (59.4) and ranks far ahead in user studies (1.1 vs. 3.8/3.7). TASR, which combines PI and NTK, exhibits both repetition and slow motion (NoRepeat 10.8, Dynamic 26.9) and is dominated by RIFLEx on all metrics. Notably, NTK coincidentally performs well on HunyuanVideo at 2× (NoRepeat 81.0) due to an unintended frequency reduction, but fails at 2.3× (NoRepeat drops to 20.0), confirming that RIFLEx's principled frequency adjustment is more robust across extrapolation factors.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on RoPE (Su et al., 2021) as the positional encoding mechanism in video diffusion transformers, but departs fundamentally from the existing length extrapolation lineage (PE, PI, NTK, YaRN, TASR) developed for text and image generation. Rather than applying these methods wholesale, the authors identify that video extrapolation has a distinct failure mode—temporal repetition governed by a single 'intrinsic frequency' component—and propose a minimal one-parameter fix (reducing θ_k) instead of the multi-frequency adjustments used by NTK or YaRN. The work also extends the analysis from 1D sequence extrapolation to multi-axis (temporal + spatial) settings, showing the same mechanism applies to resolution extrapolation in image diffusion transformers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free 2× extrapolation on SOTA video models (CogVideoX-5B, HunyuanVideo) is a practically significant result, as it eliminates the need for long-video training data entirely. The identification of a single intrinsic frequency as the primary driver of repetition provides a principled, testable explanation for why all prior methods fail, which could guide future positional encoding design from scratch. Key open questions include: establishing a theoretical (rather than empirical) basis for identifying the intrinsic frequency, understanding why the 3× extrapolation ceiling exists (diminished positional discrimination), and whether the approach generalizes to autoregressive video models that exhibit similar repetition degeneracy.
