## Problem

Aligning pretrained diffusion models with task-specific reward functions (e.g., aesthetics, brightness, compressibility) typically requires expensive fine-tuning of model parameters, which is costly, prompt-limited, and must be redone for each new reward. Existing inference-time alternatives like Loss-Guided Diffusion rely on noisy Monte Carlo gradient estimates and require differentiable rewards. This paper asks whether a tuning-free, prompt-agnostic inference-time method can match the performance of fine-tuning-based alignment while also handling non-differentiable rewards and avoiding the out-of-distribution reward-hacking that plagues naive noise optimization.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt and a continuous reward function (differentiable or non-differentiable, e.g., Aesthetic Score, HPS-v2, PickScore, JPEG file size, pixel brightness) as input, and produces a single generated image that maximizes the reward. It operates entirely at inference-time on a frozen pretrained diffusion model (Stable Diffusion v1.5 or SDXL) using the DDIM sampler with 50 steps, requiring no parameter updates. A single optimization run fits within ~15–20 GB of GPU memory on one A800 and completes in 1–5 minutes, with the option to reduce to 15 diffusion steps for a 1-minute budget.

## Method

DNO formulates alignment as maximizing r(M_θ(z)) over the noise vectors z injected during DDIM sampling, using gradient ascent with the reward backpropagated through the full sampling chain via gradient checkpointing. A key design choice is to use SDE-based sampling (DDIM with η=1), optimizing both the initial noise x_T and all per-step noise vectors z_1,…,z_T, which provides a higher-dimensional optimization landscape and empirically converges faster than ODE-based (η=0) noise optimization. The paper proves that under L-smoothness of r∘M_θ, each gradient step strictly increases the expected reward of the induced sample distribution. To prevent OOD reward-hacking—where optimized noise drifts into low-probability regions of the high-dimensional Gaussian and produces out-of-distribution images—the authors introduce a probability regularization term built on classical concentration inequalities (Wainwright, 2019), penalizing noise vectors whose permuted subvector statistics indicate low Gaussian probability. For non-differentiable rewards, they propose a Hybrid-2 gradient estimator that perturbs the latent noise rather than the sample space, ensuring all reward queries remain on the data manifold, and shows it is significantly faster than full zeroth-order or sample-space perturbation alternatives.

## Results

On Stable Diffusion v1.5 with 50 diffusion steps, DNO at 5 minutes achieves Aesthetic Score 8.587, HPS-v2 0.324, and PickScore 25.13, approaching the tuning-based AlignProp (12h fine-tuning: 8.940, 0.330) without any parameter updates. At just 3 minutes, DNO already surpasses DDPO (56h fine-tuning) on both Aesthetic (7.202 vs. 7.180) and HPS (0.303 vs. 0.287). With only 15 diffusion steps and a 1-minute budget, DNO still reaches Aesthetic 7.496 and HPS 0.341, outperforming all tuning-based baselines on HPS. The entire process runs on a single A800 GPU with under 15 GB memory.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DNO at 5 minutes nearly matches AlignProp (12h fine-tuning) on Aesthetic (8.587 vs. 8.940) and HPS (0.324 vs. 0.330) while achieving the best PickScore (25.13) among all methods, all without any parameter updates. At 3 minutes, DNO already exceeds DDPO (56h) on both Aesthetic (7.202 vs. 7.180) and HPS (0.303 vs. 0.287), demonstrating a far more favorable time-reward trade-off. DNO decisively outperforms LGD (Song et al., 2023) on all three metrics at every time budget, as LGD's Monte Carlo gradient estimation is too noisy for complex reward functions. Compared to SPIN (20h), DNO at 1 minute already surpasses it on Aesthetic (5.754 vs. 6.248 is actually lower, but at 3 min DNO reaches 7.202 vs. 6.248) and HPS (0.303 vs. 0.276). DNO also outperforms Best-of-N sampling (10 min budget) by a large margin on all metrics (e.g., Aesthetic 8.587 vs. 6.531 at 5 min), confirming that iterative noise optimization is far more sample-efficient than brute-force selection.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper provides the first comprehensive, self-contained study of Direct Noise Optimization (DNO) for aligning diffusion models, building on the DDIM sampling framework (Song et al., 2020) and the zeroth-order optimization theory of Nesterov & Spokoiny (2017) for handling non-differentiable rewards. While scattered prior works (Wallace et al. 2023b, Ben-Hamu et al. 2024, Novack et al. 2024) have optimized initial noise for specific applications, none systematically addressed the theoretical properties, the OOD reward-hacking failure mode, or the SDE-vs-ODE optimization tradeoff. The probability regularization term is a novel application of high-dimensional concentration inequalities (Wainwright, 2019) to constrain noise vectors within the high-probability region of the Gaussian prior, distinguishing this work from both tuning-based methods (DDPO, AlignProp, SPIN) and the inference-time LGD approach (Song et al., 2023) that relies on noisy Monte Carlo gradient estimation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DNO offers a practically attractive alternative to tuning-based alignment: it matches or exceeds the reward scores of methods requiring 12–56 hours of fine-tuning on 4–8 GPUs, while running in 1–5 minutes on a single consumer-level GPU with <15 GB memory, and generalizes to unseen prompts without retraining. The identification of OOD reward-hacking as a distinct, quantifiable failure mode (via the P(z) indicator) and its remedy through probability regularization is a contribution that could inform other inference-time optimization settings beyond diffusion models. Key limitations include the inherent increase in generation time relative to direct sampling, and the method's dependence on the DDIM sampling procedure, which may not transfer directly to flow-matching or consistency-model samplers. Open questions include whether the approach scales to video generation, how to efficiently combine multiple conflicting reward functions, and whether the SDE-based optimization advantage persists under fewer diffusion steps.
