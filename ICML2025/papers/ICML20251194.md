## Problem

Despite Post-LN and Pre-LN dominating Transformer training practices, a third normalization strategy—placing LN on both the input and output of each sub-layer—has been quietly adopted by major open-source models (Gemma, OLMo) without a unifying explanation of its benefits. The paper addresses the gap in understanding how different LN placements shape activation variance and gradient propagation beyond initialization, and why the peripheral (Peri-LN) placement yields superior training stability at scale.

## Input / Output

The method consumes text tokens from the DCLM-baseline dataset (sequence length 8192) and produces next-token predictions during self-supervised pretraining of Transformers at 400M, 1.5B, and 3.2B parameters over 30B tokens. The trained models are then instruction-tuned on 1,000 LIMA samples and evaluated on standard commonsense reasoning benchmarks (ARC-Easy, HellaSwag, PIQA, SIQA, Winogrande) plus C4 evaluation loss. All experiments use the Llama 3 architecture with SwiGLU activations, RoPE positional embeddings, and RMSNorm as the default normalization layer.

## Method

Peri-LN applies layer normalization twice within each Transformer sub-layer—before the module (input-LN) and after the module (output-LN)—plus optional normalization on the embedding input and final output, yielding y_l = x_l + Norm(Module(Norm(x_l))). The key theoretical insight (Proposition 3.1) shows that the output normalization introduces a damping factor ||a|| in the gradient norm bound (‖∂L/∂W^(2)‖ ≤ 4γ√D‖h‖/‖a‖), preventing gradient explosion when massive activations occur, whereas Pre-LN's gradient norm scales proportionally to ‖h‖ without such a bound. This ensures hidden-state variance grows linearly (Var(x_{l+1}) ≈ Var(x_l) + β_0) rather than exponentially as in Pre-LN, while avoiding the gradient vanishing of Post-LN. Empirically, Peri-LN achieves lower pretraining loss, fewer gradient spikes, reduced benchmark variance across seeds, and prevents FP16 overflow of extreme activations across all tested model scales.

## Results

Peri-LN consistently achieves the best average benchmark score across all three model sizes: 51.57 (400M), 56.55 (1.5B), and 58.56 (3.2B), outperforming Pre-LN by 1.9–2.9 points and Post-LN by 9.1–12.1 points. On C4 evaluation loss, Peri-LN reaches 3.34, 3.18, and 3.11 at 400M, 1.5B, and 3.2B respectively, compared to 3.43/3.29/3.20 for Pre-LN and 7.46/5.38/4.43 for Post-LN. After instruction tuning (LIMA), Peri-LN's SFT average scores are 51.96, 56.94, and 59.02, again leading both baselines at every scale. Notably, Peri-LN reduces the standard deviation of benchmark scores across 5 seeds by more than half relative to Pre-LN, and Pre-LN runs frequently diverge (excluded from scoring) while Peri-LN remains stable.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Peri-LN is compared against two established normalization strategies: Post-LN (Vaswani et al., 2017) and Pre-LN (Llama 3, Dubey et al., 2024). Against Post-LN, Peri-LN wins decisively on every metric and model size, with average benchmark scores 9.1–12.1 points higher (e.g., 58.56 vs. 46.45 at 3.2B) and C4 loss roughly 1.3 points lower; Post-LN also requires a substantially lower learning rate to train stably. Against Pre-LN, Peri-LN achieves a consistent 1.9–2.9 point advantage in average benchmark score (e.g., 56.55 vs. 53.71 at 1.5B) and 0.11–0.11 lower C4 loss, while also halving the cross-seed standard deviation. Critically, Pre-LN exhibits frequent early-stage gradient spikes, loss surges, and outright divergence across seeds (several checkpoints excluded from Table 1), whereas Peri-LN trains stably under identical hyperparameters. The paper attributes this gap to Pre-LN's unbounded residual-path variance growth (exponential during training) and the resulting massive activations that amplify final-layer gradients (Proposition 3.1), which Peri-LN's output normalization dampens.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Pre-LN architecture (Llama 3) by adding an output normalization layer around each sub-layer, a design already silently adopted by Gemma 2/3 and OLMo 2 but never formally analyzed. It builds on the initialization-stage variance and gradient analyses of Xiong et al. (2020) and Kedia et al. (2024), extending them to the full training trajectory where the linear variance growth predicted at initialization breaks down. The gradient bound in Proposition 3.1 connects to the 'massive activations' phenomenon identified by Sun et al. (2024), providing the first mechanistic explanation for why Pre-LN's residual highway becomes unstable during optimization rather than merely at depth.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Peri-LN offers a one-line architectural change (adding one RMSNorm after each module) that consistently reduces divergence risk, halves cross-seed benchmark variance, and improves scores by 1–2 points across 400M–3.2B scales, making it a low-cost, high-reliability upgrade for any large-scale pretraining pipeline. The FP16 overflow finding (Pre-LN exceeding FP16 range by 0.5B tokens) has immediate practical implications for mixed-precision training choices. Open questions include whether the benefit persists at 10B+ scale, how Peri-LN interacts with other stabilization techniques (QK-norm, attention entropy regularization), and whether the optional embedding-level normalization remains necessary as model size grows.
