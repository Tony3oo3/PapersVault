## Problem

Solving inverse problems (inpainting, deblurring, super-resolution) with latent diffusion models is challenging because the sequential diffusion sampling process is mismatched with conditioning defined only at the final timestep, and the encoder-decoder nonlinearity of LDMs breaks existing SMC-based methods that assume linear corruption operators. Prior approaches either rely on the DPS posterior-mean approximation (capturing semantics but missing fine details) or on auxiliary observations via duplex diffusion (capturing details but restricted to linear models). This paper seeks a unified framework that combines both mechanisms while remaining compatible with nonlinear corruption operators and LDMs.

## Input / Output

The method takes a corrupted image y_0 (256×256×3, normalized to [0,1]), a known corruption operator A (which may be nonlinear, e.g., box masking or Gaussian blur), and a noise standard deviation τ (set to 0.01). It outputs a posterior sample z_0 in the latent space of a pre-trained VQGAN autoencoder, which is decoded to the reconstructed image x_0 = D(z_0). No retraining of the diffusion model or autoencoder is required; the method is purely inference-time. The corruption operator is applied at every diffusion timestep to the decoded latent, making the approach general to arbitrary (including nonlinear) degradation models.

## Method

LD-SMC defines a generative model in which, at each diffusion timestep t, an auxiliary observation y_t is drawn from N(A(D(z_t)), τ²I), linking the latent diffusion variable directly to the corruption model. Posterior inference is performed via one step of blocked Gibbs sampling: first sampling y_{1:T} given z_{0:T}, then sampling z_{0:T} given y_{0:T} using sequential importance sampling with resampling (SISR). The SMC proposal distribution at each step combines two gradient corrections to the DDIM prior mean: a DPS-style posterior-mean term (γ_t · ∇log p̄(y_0|z_{t+1})) active throughout, and an auxiliary-observation term (λ_t · ∇log q(y_t|z_{t+1})) activated only after a predefined timestep s, so that early steps capture global semantics while later steps refine fine details. The paper proves that in the large-particle limit the SMC procedure converges to the exact posterior p_θ(z_{0:T}|y_{0:T}) regardless of the intermediate approximations, and empirically demonstrates significant FID improvements over DPS, TDS, PSLD, PFLD, ReSample, and LatentDAPS on ImageNet and FFHQ, particularly on inpainting tasks.

## Results

On ImageNet (256×256, 1024 samples), LD-SMC with 5 particles achieves the best FID across three of four tasks: Inpainting Box (50.67), Inpainting Free-Form (36.18), and Super-Resolution 8× (57.89), while tying LDPS on Gaussian Deblurring (52.48). On FFHQ, LD-SMC (1 particle) achieves the best FID on Inpainting Box (33.37), Inpainting Free-Form (33.67), and Gaussian Deblurring (29.19). The largest gains over baselines appear in inpainting tasks, where LD-SMC reduces FID by 10–20 points compared to the next-best method on ImageNet.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LD-SMC consistently outperforms all six baselines on FID and NIQE (perceptual quality) across both datasets, with the largest margins in inpainting tasks. On ImageNet box inpainting, LD-SMC (5 particles) achieves FID 50.67 versus 64.74 for LTDS, 65.04 for LDPS, 71.15 for PSLD, 72.83 for PFLD, 90.32 for ReSample, and 98.24 for LatentDAPS — a 14–48 point improvement. On Gaussian deblurring, LD-SMC ties LDPS (52.48 FID) and slightly beats LTDS (50.82), while ReSample achieves a lower FID (46.45) but at the cost of visible artifacts and worse NIQE (7.411 vs 6.791). PSLD and PFLD, which use second-order or particle-filtering corrections, show the weakest perceptual quality (FID 60–73) due to noticeable artifacts. LatentDAPS, which decouples latents via MCMC, produces overly smooth images with the worst NIQE (10.55–12.68) despite competitive PSNR. ReSample achieves the best PSNR on several tasks but suffers from significant artifacts in inpainting, reflected in its high FID (90.32 on box inpainting).

## Relation to Prior Work

LD-SMC sits at the intersection of two prior lines of work on SMC-based diffusion sampling for inverse problems: TDS (Wu et al., 2023), which applies the DPS posterior-mean approximation within an SMC framework, and FPS (Dou & Song, 2024), which introduces auxiliary observations via a duplex forward diffusion process. The key departure is that LD-SMC combines both the posterior-mean correction and auxiliary observations within a single SMC procedure operating in the latent space of an autoencoder, thereby extending applicability to nonlinear corruption operators and Latent Diffusion Models—settings where FPS's duplex process is intractable. The asymptotic accuracy guarantee follows from standard SMC convergence results (Chopin et al., 2020) adapted to the diffusion setting, building on the proof structure of TDS.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LD-SMC addresses a practical gap: most prior SMC-based inverse problem solvers are restricted to linear corruption operators or pixel-space diffusion, while state-of-the-art generative priors are LDMs with nonlinear decoders. The significant inpainting improvements (e.g., FID 51 vs. 65 on ImageNet box inpainting) suggest that auxiliary observations carry fine-grained detail information that the posterior-mean approximation alone misses. However, the computational cost is a real limitation—5 particles require ~537 seconds per image on ImageNet—and the gains in deblurring and super-resolution are more modest. Open questions include whether the Gibbs iteration count can be further reduced, whether the method scales to higher-resolution or video inverse problems, and whether the proposal design can be made more adaptive to reduce the particle count needed for competitive quality.
