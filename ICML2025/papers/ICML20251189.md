## Problem

While empirical studies have documented that hidden-layer representations shift during continual learning, no theoretical framework existed to characterize the dynamics of representation forgetting. This paper provides the first theoretical analysis by introducing a new metric—representation discrepancy—that measures the minimum alignment error between two representation spaces under a linear transformation, and derives analytical bounds on its evolution over the learning sequence.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251189/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes two snapshots of an L-layer ReLU network (h_t trained up to task t, and h_{t+Δt} trained on Δt additional tasks), the feature set X_t from the target task, and a layer index k. It produces a scalar representation discrepancy D_t^k(h_t, Δt) computed by optimizing a linear transformation T ∈ R^{w_k × w_k} to minimize the worst-case L2 distance between the k-th layer activations of the two models on X_t, along with a closed-form upper bound U_t^k(Δt) parameterized by layer cushion, activation contraction, and representation space size constants that are dataset- and model-dependent.

## Method

The core contribution is the representation discrepancy metric, which quantifies forgetting as min_T max_{x∈X_t} ||T·h_{t+Δt}^k(x) − h_t^k(x)||_2, capturing the minimum worst-case misalignment after optimal linear realignment of representation spaces. The upper bound U_t^k(Δt) is derived through a three-step proof: decomposing the discrepancy into a perturbation term and a weight-alignment term via triangle inequality, solving a suboptimal linear transformation problem via a convex Frobenius-norm relaxation, and eliminating weight-norm dependence using layer cushion and activation contraction (Arora et al., 2018). Under the assumption that inter-task representation distance grows linearly with Δt, the bound exhibits two phases—a monotonically increasing forgetting phase followed by a saturation phase converging to μ_t·c_t·||R_t^k(h_t)||. The convergence rate r_t^k is then bounded via perturbation analysis building on Guha & Lakshman (2024), revealing that deeper layers (higher k) saturate faster while wider networks (larger m) delay saturation, with the rate scaling as m^{−β}.

## Results

The paper validates its theoretical predictions through experiments on Split-CIFAR100 and ImageNet1K (N=50 tasks, ResNet with 9 blocks). The representation discrepancy D_t^k shows a strong linear correlation with representation forgetting (linear probing accuracy drop ΔP_t^k), achieving R²=0.88 on Split-CIFAR100 and R²=0.74 on ImageNet1K. The upper bound U_t^k is linearly proportional to the representation space size ||R_t^k(h_t)|| with R²=0.97 (Split-CIFAR100) and R²=0.99 (ImageNet1K). The saturation point Δt_sat is empirically found to be 10 for Split-CIFAR100 and 15 for ImageNet1K, and decreases with increasing layer index k while increasing with network width m, consistent with Theorem 2.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical analysis paper without a traditional method-vs-baseline comparison. The key empirical validations are: (1) The proposed representation discrepancy D_t^k serves as an effective surrogate for representation forgetting measured by linear probing accuracy drop, with strong linear correlations (R²=0.88 on Split-CIFAR100, R²=0.74 on ImageNet1K). (2) The theoretical upper bound U_t^k consistently captures the trend of the measured D_t^k across network widths m∈{64,128,256} on Split-CIFAR100. (3) The model after continual learning (Δt=N−1) retains substantially more task-1 information than a randomly initialized model (Δt=−1), confirming that saturation does not imply total erasure. (4) The two-phase forgetting/saturation behavior predicted by Proposition 1 is empirically confirmed on both datasets, with the forgetting curve initially increasing then plateauing after Δt_sat≈10–15 tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the perturbation-analysis framework of Guha & Lakshman (2024), which bounded output-level catastrophic forgetting, by shifting the measurement to hidden-layer representation spaces under an optimal linear transformation. It also borrows the layer cushion and activation contraction machinery from Arora et al. (2018) to eliminate weight-norm dependencies in the bound. The key conceptual departure from prior continual-learning theory is the introduction of representation discrepancy as a surrogate for representation forgetting, moving beyond the standard output-drift metric to capture how internal feature geometries deform across tasks. Empirically, the paper positions itself against a body of work (Davari et al. 2022, Caccia et al. 2021, Ramasesh et al. 2020) that studied representation forgetting only through linear probing or CKA, without a corresponding theoretical account.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By providing the first theoretical characterization of representation forgetting, the paper gives practitioners a principled explanation for why deeper layers degrade faster and why wider networks resist forgetting, which can inform architecture choices in lifelong learning systems. The two-phase (forgetting-then-saturation) structure offers a new diagnostic lens: the saturation point Δt_sat becomes a measurable quantity that tracks how quickly a layer 'locks in' its forgetting. Open questions include whether the linear-alignment assumption (Assumption 1) holds for non-ReLU activations or very deep networks, whether the linear-growth assumption on representation distance (Assumption 2) can be rigorously proven, and whether the theoretical bounds can be tightened to match empirical curves more closely. The analysis is also restricted to homogeneous-width ReLU networks, leaving open the behavior in residual or transformer architectures beyond the empirical checks provided.
