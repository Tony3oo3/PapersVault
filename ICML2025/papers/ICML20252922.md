## Problem

Existing data selection methods for LLM pre-training operate as static, training-agnostic filters that choose data before training and then use all selected samples indiscriminately within each batch. This ignores two critical aspects of training: the model's data preference shifts dynamically as it learns, and data samples interact jointly to determine the update direction. The paper asks whether dynamically weighting data within each batch, guided by the model's evolving preferences, can improve training efficiency beyond what static selection alone achieves.

## Input / Output

The DWM takes a micro-batch of 8 text samples (sequence length 1024) and outputs a scalar weight per sample, which modulates each sample's contribution to the batch training loss. The weighting model itself is a 370M Llama-2 backbone augmented with one attention block and two linear layers to capture inter-sample interactions. The final output is a pre-trained LLM (370M or 1.3B parameters) trained on 30B tokens from SlimPajama, and the learned DWM is a transferable artifact that can be applied to larger models or different data selection pipelines without retraining.

## Method

The core mechanism is a dynamic bi-level optimization: the lower level trains the LLM with a weighted sum of per-sample autoregressive losses (weights produced by a frozen DWM), while the upper level updates the DWM to maximize the trained model's validation performance on LAMBADA via the chain rule. Rather than optimizing the DWM against training loss (which would degenerate by zeroing out easy samples), the paper introduces 'weight influence'—the validation reward of the model after one weighted update—as the DWM's objective. Training proceeds in 5 alternating stages where the DWM is updated first (LLM frozen) and then the LLM is trained (DWM frozen), capturing the model's shifting data preferences over time. The DWM is plug-and-play: once trained on a 370M model with random data, it transfers directly to 1.3B models and to data selected by DSIR or QuRating, adding only ~9% FLOPs overhead since it performs forward inference only during transfer.

## Results

On the 370M model trained with randomly selected SlimPajama data, DWM improves the average two-shot accuracy from 45.1% to 46.4% and zero-shot from 44.0% to 45.0% across 9 downstream tasks. When transferred to a 1.3B model, DWM lifts the QuRating-selected data average from 50.3% to 51.0% (two-shot) and 48.3% to 49.7% (zero-shot), approaching the performance of a model trained on twice the data (RANDOM_60B: 51.4%) while using only 30B tokens. The additional computational overhead of the transferred 370M weighting model on the 1.3B LLM is approximately 9% in FLOPs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DSIR, DWM provides a clear improvement on the 370M model (two-shot avg: 45.3→46.4, +1.1) but yields a tie on the 1.3B model (48.7→48.7 two-shot; 46.8→46.8 zero-shot), which the authors attribute to DSIR's Wikipedia/book-oriented selection containing fewer high-quality reasoning examples that larger models could exploit. Against QuRating, the gains are modest at 370M scale (48.5→48.6 two-shot) but become substantial at 1.3B (50.3→51.0 two-shot, 48.3→49.7 zero-shot), because larger models have greater capacity to absorb QuRating's high-quality step-by-step reasoning data. In both scales, DWM consistently outperforms the unweighted random baseline (370M: 45.1→46.4; 1.3B: 47.6→48.7 two-shot), confirming that data utilization matters independently of the selection strategy. The 1.3B QuRating+DWM result (51.0) nearly matches RANDOM_60B (51.4) trained on double the tokens, demonstrating improved pre-training efficiency.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself within the sample-level data selection literature for LLM pre-training (DSIR, QuRating, DSDM, MATES) but departs from the static, pre-training selection paradigm by introducing a dynamic, batch-level data weighting model (DWM) that operates during training. Conceptually, it repurposes bi-level optimization from meta-learning to jointly optimize a weighting model and the LLM, where the weighting model's objective is defined by a novel 'weight influence' metric tied to validation performance rather than training loss. The method is explicitly orthogonal to existing selection strategies, functioning as a plug-and-play module that can be layered on top of any data selection approach.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that how selected data is utilized during training (via per-sample weighting) matters as much as which data is selected, and that a weighting model trained on a 370M model transfers effectively to 1.3B models and different selection methods. However, improvements are modest (~1 point average), the experiments are limited to small-scale models (370M/1.3B, 30B tokens), and the authors acknowledge that the stage-2 weighting model can actually hurt performance, leaving open the question of how to maintain consistent gains across all training stages. The finding that data preferences shift toward expertise and reasoning content in later training stages offers a useful but preliminary insight that needs validation at larger scales.
