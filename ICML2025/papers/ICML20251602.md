## Problem

Chain-of-Thought prompting does not consistently improve LLM performance across all task types—it helps mathematical and symbolic reasoning but can be neutral or harmful for commonsense and knowledge tasks. Prior meta-analyses identified thematic trends but lacked a principled, instance-level mechanism to predict when CoT will be effective, leaving practitioners to apply CoT indiscriminately and waste compute on tasks where it provides no benefit.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a standard question-only prompt (no CoT or DA trigger) and extracts the softmax probability of each token along the first 50 tokens of the greedy decoding path. At the benchmark level, it outputs two scalar indicators (Instance SC and Aggregated SC) whose sign predicts whether CoT will yield a significant gain. At the instance level, it outputs a binary routing decision—use CoT or direct answer—that determines which prompt is sent to the model for the final response. For closed-source models lacking token probability access, the input is replaced by voting decisions from multiple open-source models.

## Method

The core insight is that the monotonicity of token probabilities along the greedy decoding path serves as a proxy for the model's sequential-reasoning confidence: increasing probabilities (positive Spearman correlation with token position) signal tasks where CoT scaffolding helps, while decreasing probabilities signal tasks where CoT compounds errors. Two indicators are defined—Instance SC (mean per-instance Spearman correlation between the 50-token probability sequence and position) and Aggregated SC (Spearman correlation of the position-wise mean probability across all instances)—achieving 69.6% and 89.2% prediction accuracy for CoT effectiveness, respectively. For instance-level routing, Dynamic CoT trains a logistic regression on 50 labeled samples per benchmark using the per-instance Spearman value as the sole feature, then classifies each new question as CoT or direct answer based on the predicted probability exceeding 0.5. For closed-source models, a majority-voting mechanism aggregates Dynamic CoT predictions from multiple open-source models to transfer the routing decision without requiring token-level access.

## Results

At the benchmark level, Aggregated SC predicts CoT effectiveness with 89.2% average accuracy across four open-source models, while Instance SC achieves 69.6%. At the question level, Dynamic CoT achieves performance nearly identical to the best of CoT or direct answer (DA) across 14 benchmarks, ranking among the top two methods in 92.8% of 56 experimental setups. Dynamic CoT reduces token consumption by 39.1% on average compared to all-CoT, with the largest savings on benchmarks where CoT is ineffective (e.g., GPQA, MuSR, StrategyQA). In the cross-model transfer setting (open-source → GPT-4o/GPT-4o-mini), Dynamic CoT maintains high accuracy while reducing token consumption by 35.8%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares Dynamic CoT against two baselines: zero-shot CoT (Kojima et al., 2022, 'Let's think step by step') and direct answer (DA). On benchmarks where CoT is highly effective (GSM8K, MultiArith, FOLIO), Dynamic CoT matches or nearly matches CoT accuracy (e.g., 69.29 vs. 70.28 on GSM8K; 85.00 vs. 85.59 on MultiArith) with negligible gaps (≤1.4 points). On benchmarks where CoT is ineffective or harmful (GPQA, MuSR, StrategyQA, LSAT), Dynamic CoT selects DA and matches or exceeds DA accuracy (e.g., 79.54 vs. 78.27 on StrategyQA; 50.99 vs. 51.42 on MuSR), while saving substantial tokens. Overall, Dynamic CoT achieves the best accuracy in 46.4% of 56 model–benchmark setups and ranks in the top two in 92.8%, while reducing average token consumption by 39.1% relative to all-CoT. In the transfer setting to GPT-4o and GPT-4o-mini, Dynamic CoT (transfer) matches CoT on math/symbolic benchmarks (e.g., 84.76 on GSM8K) and matches DA on commonsense benchmarks (e.g., 54.76 on StrategyQA), with a 35.8% token reduction.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the CoT prompting literature (Wei et al., 2022; Kojima et al., 2022) and the meta-analytic finding by Sprague et al. (2024) that CoT is predominantly effective for mathematical and symbolic reasoning, but departs from prior work by shifting the analytical lens from task-category heuristics to the model's internal decoding signal. Specifically, it repurposes the token probability distribution—previously studied in the context of confidence estimation (Farquhar et al., 2024) and spontaneous CoT emergence (Wang & Zhou, 2024)—as a predictive feature for CoT utility, using a simple Spearman correlation between token probabilities and their sequence position. The Dynamic CoT routing mechanism extends the adaptive-prompting idea with a lightweight logistic regression classifier, and the voting-based transfer to closed-source models addresses a practical gap that prior decoding-based methods (contrastive decoding, context-aware decoding) did not tackle.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a concrete, low-cost mechanism (50 tokens of greedy decoding + one Spearman correlation) to decide whether to invoke CoT, reducing token consumption by ~35–39% while preserving accuracy—a practical win for deployment. The mechanistic interpretation (increasing token-probability trend signals structured sequential reasoning where CoT scaffolds help; decreasing trend signals diffuse solution spaces where CoT compounds errors) provides a testable hypothesis about why CoT works. Limitations include reliance on token-probability access (restricting direct application to open-source models), a very simple classifier (single-feature logistic regression) that may not capture interactions, and evaluation on only four open-source and two closed-source models. Open questions include whether the SC signal generalizes to larger or differently-architected models, whether it remains predictive under few-shot or fine-tuned settings, and whether the 50-token window is truly optimal or an artifact of the benchmarks used.
