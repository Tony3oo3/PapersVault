## Problem

Masked image generation models (MaskGIT, MAR) and masked diffusion models (MDMs) were developed with different motivations and objectives, yet their relationship and the design choices that govern their performance remained underexplored. While MAR eliminated the discrete tokenizer bottleneck via diffusion loss, key factors such as masking schedule, weighting function, and sampling strategy were not systematically studied, and with limited sampling steps (e.g., 16), masked models still lagged behind coarse-to-fine autoregressive models like VAR. The paper asks: can these paradigms be unified into a single framework that enables principled exploration of the training and sampling design space for efficient, high-quality image generation?

## Input / Output

The model takes a single class label as conditioning input and produces a generated RGB image at 256×256 or 512×512 resolution. Internally, images are represented as continuous token sequences via a KL-16 tokenizer (stride 16, 16-dim tokens, yielding 256 tokens) for 256×256 or a DC-AE-f32 tokenizer (stride 32, 32-dim tokens, yielding 256 tokens) for 512×512. The per-token conditional distribution is modeled by a small MLP diffusion block rather than a categorical head, so the output tokens are continuous vectors decoded back to pixel space, avoiding the information loss inherent in discrete VQ tokenization.

## Method

eMIGM unifies MaskGIT, MAR, and MDM under a single loss formulation L(x₀) = ∫ w(t) E_q[Σ -log p_θ(x₀ⁱ|x_t)] dt, where the three components—masking distribution q(x_t|x₀), weighting function w(t), and conditional distribution p_θ(x₀ⁱ|x_t)—parameterize the design space. The paper identifies that for images, an exponential mask schedule (γ_t = 1−exp(−5t)) with w(t)=1, an MAE-style encoder-decoder architecture (encoder sees only unmasked tokens), time truncation (t_min=0.2), and CFG with Mask (replacing the fake class token with a mask token for unconditional generation) jointly yield the best training configuration. For sampling, the exp schedule is retained (predicting fewer tokens in early steps), DPM-Solver replaces DDPM for the per-token diffusion (achieving good quality in <15 diffusion steps vs. 100), and a time-interval CFG strategy applies classifier-free guidance only when the mask ratio falls within [0.1, 0.3], reducing NFE by ~40% while maintaining or improving FID. This combination allows eMIGM to outperform VAR at similar NFEs and parameters, and to approach REPA-level FID (1.57 vs. 1.42) using less than 45% of the NFE.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256, eMIGM-H achieves FID 1.57 with ~180 NFEs (128×1.4), approaching the state-of-the-art REPA (FID 1.42) while using less than 45% of REPA's NFEs and no self-supervised features. At lower compute, eMIGM-B achieves FID 2.79 with only ~20 NFEs (16×1.2) and 208M parameters, outperforming VAR-d16 (FID 3.30, 310M params) at similar NFEs. On ImageNet 512×512, eMIGM-L (478M params) achieves FID 1.77 with ~80 NFEs, surpassing EDM2-XXL (FID 1.81, 1.5B params) and VAR-d36-s (FID 2.63, 2.3B params). Sampling speed for eMIGM-L is 0.165 sec/image vs. 0.552 sec/image for EDM2-XXL on a single A100 GPU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

eMIGM-H (FID 1.57, ~180 NFEs) is the closest to REPA (FID 1.42, ~425 NFEs) but uses less than 45% of the NFEs and no self-supervised features; it clearly outperforms DiffiT (FID 1.73) and Large-DiT 3B (FID 2.10) at the same or lower NFEs. At low NFEs (~20), eMIGM-B (FID 2.79, 208M) beats VAR-d16 (FID 3.30, 310M) and VAR-d20 (FID 2.57, 600M) at comparable or lower parameter counts, and eMIGM-L (FID 1.72, 478M) surpasses VAR-d30 (FID 1.92, 2B) with only ~24% of the parameters. On ImageNet 512×512, eMIGM-L (FID 1.77, 478M) outperforms both EDM2-XXL (FID 1.81, 1.5B) and VAR-d36-s (FID 2.63, 2.3B), though EDM2-XXL with guidance interval (FID 1.40) remains stronger at the cost of more parameters. Compared to MAR, eMIGM-L achieves competitive FID (1.72 vs. 1.78 for MAR-L) while using less than 20% of MAR's NFEs (180 vs. 512). Against GANs, eMIGM-B (FID 2.79) trails StyleGAN-XL (FID 2.30) on FID, but eMIGM-H (FID 1.57) surpasses it substantially. Overall, eMIGM consistently wins on the FID-vs-NFE trade-off curve, achieving the best quality at any given sampling budget among masked and autoregressive models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies three previously separate lines of work—MaskGIT's masked image generation, MAR's diffusion-loss-based autoregressive generation, and masked diffusion models (MDM/RADD) developed for text—into a single loss formulation (Eq. 7) where the differences reduce to choices of masking distribution, weighting function, and conditional distribution. It then systematically explores this design space, drawing on MAE's encoder-decoder architecture, DPM-Solver for fast diffusion sampling, and the exp mask schedule, to arrive at eMIGM. The conceptual departure from prior work is treating MaskGIT and MDM not as competing paradigms but as special cases of one framework, enabling principled ablation of each component rather than ad-hoc design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: eMIGM achieves FID 1.57 on ImageNet 256×256 with only 42% of REPA's NFEs and outperforms EDM2 on 512×512 with fewer parameters, offering a credible efficient alternative to continuous diffusion models for class-conditional generation. The time-interval CFG strategy (applying guidance only in later mask-prediction steps) is a simple, broadly applicable technique that reduces sampling cost by ~40% without quality loss, and could transfer to other masked or iterative generation settings. Open questions include whether the unified framework and its design choices (exp schedule, w(t)=1, MAE architecture) generalize to text-to-image or other modalities, and whether the log-exp schedule hinted at in the appendix can close the remaining gap to REPA without self-supervised features.
