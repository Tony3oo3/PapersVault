## Problem

Existing one-shot Federated Learning (OFL) methods compress multi-round communication into a single upload-aggregate cycle but suffer from a 'garbage in, garbage out' pitfall: the uploaded local models are inherently inconsistent due to data heterogeneity. The paper identifies two specific failure modes— intra-model inconsistency (divergent predictions for semantically identical samples) and inter-model inconsistency (growing parameter divergence across clients)—and shows that all prior OFL designs address only the server-side aggregation while ignoring the degraded quality of the inputs.

## Input / Output

Clients train on local RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 64×64 for Tiny-ImageNet) partitioned via Dirichlet sampling with α ∈ {0.05, 0.1, 0.3, 0.5}. Each client uploads its feature extractor (last-layer output of ResNet-18) and a set of C learnable category-wise prototypes to the server in a single round. At inference, a test image is passed through all uploaded extractors, the resulting features are informatively fused, and the fused feature is matched against the server-aggregated global prototype to produce a class label.

## Method

FAFI consists of two complementary components. On the client side, Self-Alignment Local Training (SALT) replaces the standard supervised classifier with a dual-loss objective: a contrastive self-supervised loss that pulls features of original and augmented samples together (learning invariant representations), and a prototype contrastive loss that trains learnable category-wise prototypes as discriminative decision anchors. On the server side, Informative Feature Fused Inference (IFFI) avoids parameter-level aggregation entirely; instead, it computes each client's feature informativeness as the cosine distance from a standard Gaussian noise vector, uses these as attention weights to fuse features across clients, and classifies by nearest-prototype matching against the averaged global prototype. The key insight is that fusing features at inference time with noise-aware weighting sidesteps the inter-model parameter divergence that makes parameter aggregation ineffective in the one-shot setting.

## Results

FAFI achieves an average of 10.86% accuracy improvement over the best of 11 OFL baselines across CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and Tiny-ImageNet under four Dirichlet heterogeneity levels (α ∈ {0.05, 0.1, 0.3, 0.5}). On CIFAR-10, FAFI reaches 71.84% (α=0.05) to 88.74% (α=0.5), outperforming the strongest baseline FuseFL by up to 17.42 points. Gains are most pronounced on Tiny-ImageNet under extreme heterogeneity, where FAFI surpasses baselines by 14.81–21.39 points (e.g., 56.48% vs. 35.09% at α=0.5).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FAFI outperforms all 11 baselines across every dataset and heterogeneity setting. The closest competitor is FuseFL (NeurIPS'24), which iteratively shares intermediate features; FAFI beats it by 17.42 points on CIFAR-10 (α=0.05) but only by 0.18 points on CIFAR-10 (α=0.3), where FuseFL's multi-round feature sharing partially compensates for inconsistency. IntactOFL (ACM MM'24) is the second-strongest baseline on CIFAR-10 (α=0.5) and Tiny-ImageNet (α=0.5), where FAFI leads by 4.40 and 21.39 points respectively. Optimization-based methods (O-FedAvg, MA-Echo, FedFisher) and distillation-based methods (FedDF, F-ADI, F-DAFL) trail FAFI by 20–60 points, confirming the paper's 'garbage in, garbage out' diagnosis. Selective ensemble methods (DENSE, Co-Boosting, Ensemble) also fall well short, with Co-Boosting (ICLR'23) reaching at most 70.24% on CIFAR-10 (α=0.5) versus FAFI's 88.74%. The advantage is largest under extreme heterogeneity (α=0.05) on Tiny-ImageNet, where FAFI achieves 36.96% versus the best baseline's 22.15%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in one-shot Federated Learning (OFL), where prior methods (optimization-based, distillation-based, generative, and selective ensemble) focus exclusively on server-side aggregation of local models. FAFI shifts the emphasis to the client side, arguing that the root cause of poor OFL performance is inconsistent one-shot local models ('garbage in, garbage out'), a problem that multi-round FL implicitly resolves through iterative knowledge exchange. The method repurposes contrastive learning (InfoNCE-style) and prototype learning (building on FedProto, FedTGP) for a one-shot setting, and draws on MoE-inspired feature fusion for server-side inference rather than parameter-level merging. The theoretical analysis extends the 'client drift' literature from multi-round short-term training to one-shot long-term training, proving that both intra-model and inter-model inconsistency are mathematically inevitable under standard supervised training on heterogeneous data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By identifying and formally proving that model inconsistency is an inherent limitation of one-shot local training, this work reframes the OFL problem from a server-side aggregation challenge to a client-side training quality challenge, opening a new design axis for future OFL methods. The 10.86% average improvement over 11 baselines (reaching 17.71% in extreme heterogeneity on Tiny-ImageNet) demonstrates that addressing the 'garbage input' problem yields substantial gains that no prior server-side-only method achieved. However, the authors acknowledge limitations in domain shift and multi-task scenarios where static class prototypes fail to adapt, and the framework has only been validated on three relatively small image datasets (CIFAR-10/100, Tiny-ImageNet) with up to 200 classes, leaving open whether the approach scales to larger, more diverse benchmarks like ImageNet or to non-vision modalities.
