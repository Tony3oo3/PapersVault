## Problem

Standard visual autoencoders rely on a single-step deterministic decoder to reconstruct images from compressed latents, which limits reconstruction fidelity—especially under high compression ratios—and consequently degrades downstream latent diffusion generation quality. While prior work has explored diffusion mechanisms within autoencoding, none has fully realized a practical diffusion-based autoencoder that achieves strong reconstruction, high sampling efficiency (1–3 steps), and resolution generalization simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20253126/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images (trained at 128×128, evaluated up to 512×512) and produces continuous latent codes with configurable downsampling factors (4×, 8×, 16×, 32×) and latent channel dimensions (4, 8, 16, or 32 channels). Reconstruction is performed iteratively via a diffusion decoder in 1–3 NFE steps rather than a single forward pass, and the resulting latents feed directly into a fixed DiT-XL/2 latent diffusion model for class-conditional image generation at 256×256. The ε-VAE-SD variant matches Stable Diffusion's encoder (34M params, 8× downsampling, 4 channels), while ε-VAE-lite uses a lighter 6M-param encoder with 16× downsampling and 8 channels as a more challenging stress test.

## Method

ε-VAE retains the standard VQGAN-style encoder but replaces the deterministic decoder with a conditional rectified-flow diffusion model (ADM U-Net architecture) that iteratively denoises from Gaussian noise toward the target image, guided by the encoder latent via channel-wise concatenation. The training objective replaces the pixel reconstruction loss with a velocity-matching score loss, while adapting LPIPS and GAN losses to the diffusion setting: LPIPS is computed on one-step estimates x̂₀ᵗ = xₜ − t·G(xₜ, t, z), and the adversarial loss enforces start-to-end trajectory consistency by contrasting (x̂₀ᵗ, xₜ) against (x₀, xₜ) with a time-conditioned discriminator. Key diffusion-specific choices include logit-normal time-step sampling during training, a noise scaling factor γ=0.6 with variance normalization, and a reversed logarithm time-spacing schedule during inference that concentrates steps in noisier regions. These design decisions collectively reduce the required NFE from 1000 (vanilla DDPM baseline) to 3 while improving rFID from 28.22 to 6.24 under the ε-VAE-lite configuration, and yield 22% better generation FID or 2.3× inference speedup at matched compression rates compared to SD-VAE.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256 reconstruction at 8×8 downsampling, ε-VAE-SD(H) achieves rFID 0.38, PSNR 29.49, and SSIM 0.851, substantially outperforming SD-VAE (rFID 0.74, PSNR 25.68, SSIM 0.820). For class-conditional generation at 256×256 using DiT-XL/2, ε-VAE-SD(H) achieves FID 8.85 at 8×8 downsampling versus 11.63 for SD-VAE, and FID 14.26 at 32×32 versus 21.31 for SD-VAE. The method delivers a 22% improvement in generation quality at matched compression rates, or a 2.3× inference throughput gain by increasing the downsampling factor from 8×8 to 16×16 while maintaining competitive FID.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ε-VAE-SD(H) outperforms all nine baselines on reconstruction quality. Against the primary baseline SD-VAE, it achieves nearly 2× lower rFID at 8×8 downsampling (0.38 vs. 0.74) and 2.2× lower at 16×16 (1.35 vs. 2.93), with corresponding PSNR gains of ~3.8 dB and ~2.0 dB respectively. It also surpasses the strongest discrete-tokenizer baselines: LlamaGen (rFID 0.59 at 8×8, 2.19 at 16×16), VQGAN (1.19, 4.99), and MaskGIT (2.28 at 16×16). Among continuous VAEs, it beats SDXL-VAE (0.68 vs. 0.38 at 8×8) and LiteVAE (0.87 vs. 0.38). For downstream class-conditional generation (Table 4), ε-VAE-SD(H) achieves FID 8.85 at 8×8, outperforming SD-VAE (11.63), Asym-VAE (10.85), and Omni-VAE (12.25), and at 32×32 it reaches FID 14.26 versus SD-VAE's 21.31 while delivering 2.3× higher inference throughput.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reframes the standard autoencoding pipeline (encoder + deterministic decoder, as in VQGAN and Stable Diffusion) by replacing the decoder with a conditional diffusion process, building directly on DDPM and rectified flow formulations. While prior works (DiffusionAE, DiVAE, SWYCC) explored diffusion in autoencoding, they either relied on ad-hoc distillation for speed, lacked resolution generalization, or replaced GAN losses entirely. The key departure here is the systematic co-design of diffusion-specific choices (rectified flow velocity prediction, logit-normal time sampling, noise scaling) with adapted versions of standard VAE objectives (LPIPS on estimated x̂₀ᵗ, adversarial trajectory matching on (x̂₀ᵗ, xₜ) pairs), achieving practical 1–3 step decoding without distillation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 22% generation quality gain at matched compression (or 2.3× inference speedup at higher compression) makes this a practically relevant upgrade for any latent diffusion pipeline, and the resolution generalization property preserves a key advantage of standard VAEs. Open questions include scalability to very high resolutions (UNet memory overhead), extension to discrete tokenization, and whether fine-tuning the diffusion decoder with frozen latents (as in Sargent et al., 2025) can further close the gap. The finding that start-to-end trajectory matching outperforms step-by-step matching, and that quality degrades beyond 3 steps, suggests the optimal operating regime is narrow and may not transfer to all compression levels.
