## Problem

Human evaluation remains the gold standard for LLM win-rate estimation but is prohibitively expensive, while synthetic evaluators (LLM-as-a-judge) introduce bias that distorts results. Prior work on accelerating LLM evaluation focused on reducing the number of prompts in structured benchmarks with predefined answers, leaving the general head-to-head human-preference setting unaddressed. This paper asks: can synthetic feedback be leveraged to reduce human annotation cost while provably preserving unbiasedness?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20253187/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes an evaluation dataset of n (prompt, response₁, response₂) tuples, a human annotation budget k < n, and a synthetic evaluator (reward model or LLM) that produces a preference score ẑ ∈ [0,1] for every sample. It outputs an unbiased win-rate estimate computed from only k human annotations plus n synthetic scores, along with a human annotation saving ratio ρ² that can be estimated from a small pilot subset before committing to the full evaluation. The synthetic evaluator's output format depends on its type: reward models yield Bradley-Terry scores from pairwise rewards, while LLM judges yield natural-language preferences parsed into [0,1].

## Method

Control Variates Evaluation applies the classical control variates variance-reduction technique to head-to-head LLM win-rate estimation. For each human-annotated sample, the adjusted preference is z^cv = z − α(ẑ − μ_ẑ), where μ_ẑ is the synthetic win rate averaged over all n samples (cancelling bias) and α = Cov(z,ẑ)/Var(ẑ) is estimated from the k human-annotated samples. The key theoretical result is that the variance of the estimator is reduced by a factor of (1−ρ²) relative to pure human evaluation, where ρ is the correlation between human and synthetic preferences, meaning only (1−ρ²)·n human annotations are needed to match the accuracy of n. The method is hyperparameter-free, task-agnostic, and optionally finetunes the synthetic evaluator on existing human annotations from other LLM pairs (via Bradley-Terry loss for reward models) to increase ρ, yielding up to 24.8% annotation savings with a finetuned 8B reward model.

## Results

The primary metric is the human annotation saving ratio (ρ²), which quantifies the fraction of human annotations that can be eliminated while maintaining the same estimation variance. On Chatbot Arena, off-the-shelf evaluators achieve 8.3%–12.2% saving (Skywork-8B to ArmoRM-8B/GPT-4), while finetuned variants reach up to 24.8% (Skywork-8B ft) and 17.1% (GRM-2B ft). On MT Bench, savings range from 5.7% to 12.6%. Control Variates Evaluation is unbiased (MSE converges to 0), whereas Synthetic Evaluation alone exhibits significant persistent bias even with GPT-4.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares Control Variates Evaluation against two baselines: full Human Evaluation and Synthetic Evaluation (the LLM-as-a-judge paradigm from Zheng et al., 2023). Against Synthetic Evaluation, Control Variates Evaluation wins decisively on bias: Synthetic Evaluation with GPT-4 or finetuned Skywork-8B shows a large, non-vanishing MSE floor, while Control Variates Evaluation's MSE converges to near zero, matching Human Evaluation. Against Human Evaluation, Control Variates Evaluation achieves the same variance with 10–25% fewer human annotations, as confirmed by the shifted-curve overlap in Figures 3 and 8. Notably, finetuned Skywork-8B (24.8% saving on Chatbot Arena) surpasses GPT-4 (12.2%) as a control variate, and even a small 2B reward model (GRM-2B ft, 17.1%) outperforms GPT-4, demonstrating that the synthetic evaluator need not be accurate in absolute terms—only correlated with human judgment.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the classical control variates variance-reduction technique (Lavenberg & Welch, 1981) to the head-to-head LLM win-rate estimation setting, extending prior work by Chaganty et al. (2018) that applied control variates only to single-response natural language evaluation. It is closely related to Prediction-Powered Inference (PPI/PPI++, Angelopoulos et al., 2023; Boyeau et al., 2024), which also combines synthetic and human labels for variance reduction, but differs in targeting the empirical win-rate mean rather than the Bradley-Terry MLE objective. The paper positions itself against the broader LLM-as-a-judge literature (Zheng et al., 2023) by showing that even biased synthetic evaluators can be harnessed without introducing bias into the final estimate.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method offers a practical, hyperparameter-free way to reduce human annotation costs by 12–25% while provably preserving unbiasedness, and the saving ratio can be estimated from a small pilot sample before committing to a full evaluation. This is particularly relevant for large-scale evaluation platforms like Chatbot Arena where annotation budgets are a bottleneck. Open questions include extending the framework to multi-model ranking, fine-grained attribute scoring, and adaptive or ensemble synthetic evaluators; the paper also notes that finetuning the reward model introduces non-trivial compute cost (4×H100 for Skywork-8B), which must be weighed against future annotation savings.
