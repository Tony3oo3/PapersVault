## Problem

Shapley values are the gold standard for model explanations due to their unique satisfaction of local accuracy, missingness, and consistency, but they are always computed post-hoc, incurring additional inference-time cost and requiring a separate explainer model. No prior work has considered computing Shapley values as the mechanism by which predictions are formed, rather than as a retrospective explanation of an already-trained black box.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250042/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts either an n-dimensional tabular feature vector or a 32×32 RGB image (CIFAR-10) and simultaneously outputs a class prediction and an n×d matrix of Shapley values (one per feature per output dimension). The prediction is not produced independently—it is literally the column-wise sum of the Shapley values, optionally passed through a link function (sigmoid/softmax). For image inputs, the 'features' are pixel values, and the Shapley matrix has 3072 rows for CIFAR-10.

## Method

ViaSHAP trains a single model φ^Via that maps inputs to Shapley values, with the prediction defined as the sum of those values (enforcing local accuracy by construction). Training uses a dual-objective loss: a Shapley loss that enforces the weighted least-squares constraint (analogous to KernelSHAP's objective) via coalition sampling performed at two different points within the same network—masking input features for the prediction path and masking predicted Shapley values for the attribution path—plus a standard task loss (cross-entropy or MSE). The paper proves that the global optimizer of the Shapley loss yields exact Shapley values satisfying all three axiomatic properties. Four implementations are explored: KANs with spline basis functions, KANs with RBFs, and two MLP variants, with the KAN-based version showing the best predictive and explanation accuracy. A relaxed variant adds a learnable bias δ so the model need not predict zero when all features are marginalized, accommodating regression and imbalanced classification.

## Results

Across 25 tabular datasets, KAN^Via achieves the highest average AUC rank, with KAN_ϱ^Via and XGBoost closely following; the post-hoc Nemenyi test confirms that KAN-based ViaSHAP is statistically indistinguishable from XGBoost and Random Forests, while significantly outperforming MLP-based ViaSHAP variants. On CIFAR-10 image classification, U-Net^Via reaches 0.983 AUC, ResNet18^Via 0.968, and ResNet50^Via 0.960. For explanation accuracy on images, ViaSHAP models consistently outperform FastSHAP: e.g., ResNet50^Via achieves 0.997 inclusion AUC and 0.554 exclusion AUC (top-50% features) versus 0.978 and 0.778 for FastSHAP applied to the same model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against TabNet (Arik & Pfister, 2021), KAN^Via achieves a higher average AUC rank across the 25 tabular datasets, and the post-hoc Nemenyi test confirms the difference is statistically significant at the 0.05 level; TabNet is outperformed on the majority of individual datasets (e.g., 0.944 vs. 0.942 on LHC Identify Jet, 0.968 vs. 0.960 on Mozilla4). Against FastSHAP (Jethani et al., 2022), ViaSHAP's native Shapley values are significantly more accurate: on CIFAR-10, ResNet50^Via achieves 0.997 inclusion AUC and 0.554 exclusion AUC (top-50% features) compared to 0.978 and 0.778 for FastSHAP applied to the same model, and U-Net^Via reaches 0.988/0.773 versus 0.978/0.864 for FastSHAP. On tabular data, KAN^Via also significantly outperforms FastSHAP in cosine similarity and Spearman rank correlation to ground-truth KernelSHAP values (Appendix K). The key distinction is that ViaSHAP computes explanations as part of the forward pass at zero additional inference cost, whereas FastSHAP requires a separate trained explainer and per-instance coalition sampling.

## Relation to Prior Work

ViaSHAP departs fundamentally from the post-hoc paradigm of Shapley value computation (KernelSHAP, FastSHAP, TreeSHAP) by integrating Shapley value learning directly into the predictive model's training loop, so that the prediction is literally the column-wise sum of the learned Shapley values. It builds on FastSHAP's parametric Shapley function formulation but eliminates the separate optimization problem per prediction by co-training the predictor and explainer. The method also distinguishes itself from prior explainable neural networks (e.g., HarsanyiNet, self-explaining networks) by not imposing a specific architecture or restricting explanations to a feature subset, and by providing a theoretical guarantee (Theorem 3.4) that the global optimizer recovers exact Shapley values. Two architectural instantiations are explored: MLPs grounded in the universal approximation theorem and Kolmogorov-Arnold Networks grounded in the Kolmogorov-Arnold representation theorem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By making Shapley values the prediction mechanism itself, ViaSHAP eliminates the inference-time overhead of post-hoc explainers while preserving the theoretical properties (local accuracy, missingness, consistency) that make Shapley values attractive for regulated domains. The practical finding that KAN-based ViaSHAP matches XGBoost and Random Forests on 25 tabular datasets suggests this is not merely a theoretical curiosity. Open questions include whether the theoretical guarantee (exact Shapley values at the global optimum) holds under finite training, how the method scales to very high-dimensional or non-differentiable models, and whether the availability of fast, accurate explanations introduces new adversarial vulnerabilities as the authors note in their impact statement.
