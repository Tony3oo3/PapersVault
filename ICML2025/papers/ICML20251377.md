## Problem

Despite the widespread empirical success of classifier-free guidance in diffusion models, a theoretical explanation for why guidance improves sample quality has been missing. Prior analyses were restricted to special cases (isotropic Gaussians, 1D intervals, or specific GMM configurations), and the authors discover that uniform per-sample improvement does not hold even for Gaussian mixtures. The paper seeks a general theoretical guarantee that holds for arbitrary data distributions.

## Input / Output

The analysis takes a class label c, a continuous guidance scale w ≥ 0, and a Gaussian noise initialization Y_N ~ N(0, I_d) as inputs, and produces a generated sample Y_0^w along with the classifier probability p_{c|X_0}(c|Y_0^w). The key output metric is the expectation of the reciprocal classifier probability E[p_{c|X_0}(c|Y_0^w)^{-1}], which serves as a proxy for the ratio of bad samples. Empirically, the framework is validated on ImageNet using a pre-trained latent diffusion model (SDXL) with Inception v3 classifier probabilities, and on a 1D two-class GMM with N=4000 DDPM steps.

## Method

The core result (Theorem 3.1) proves that for the continuous-time guided reverse SDE, the conditional expectation of p_{c|X_{1-t}}(c|Y_t^w)^{-1} decreases at a rate proportional to w/t · ||∇log p_{X|c} − ∇log p_X||². The proof rests on two key observations: (1) the function p_{c|X_t}(c|X_t)^{-1} forms a martingale along the forward process (Lemma 3.2, established via Bayes-rule decomposition of the Gaussian transition kernel), and (2) the CFG guidance term s(x|c) − s(x) is exactly aligned with the negative gradient of this martingale function, so adding guidance pushes the process toward lower reciprocal probability. The stochastic calculus is carried out via Ito's formula, carefully separating first- and second-order differential terms for the guided and unguided processes and taking their difference. A supplementary theorem (Theorem 3.6) then bounds the KL divergence between the discrete-time DDPM process and its continuous-time limit under Lipschitz score and bounded-moment assumptions, ensuring the theoretical guarantee transfers to practical finite-step implementations.

Empirically, on both a 1D GMM and ImageNet (20,000 trials × 1000 categories), the probability P(p_{c|X_0}(1|Y_1^w) ≥ p_{c|X_0}(1|Y_1^0)) is strictly less than 1 for all w < 10, confirming that guidance does NOT uniformly improve every sample. However, the average of -p_{c|X_0}(1|Y_1^w)^{-1} monotonically increases with w, validating the theoretical prediction of overall quality improvement. ## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper, so comparisons are with prior analytical results rather than competing methods. Against Wu et al. (2024), who prove p_{c|X_0}(1|Y_1^w) ≥ p_{c|X_0}(1|Y_1^0) under specific GMM conditions, this paper shows that inequality does NOT always hold — even for Gaussian mixtures, the ratio of improved samples is strictly below 1 (Figure 1). Against Chidambaram et al. (2024), who argue guidance can degrade performance via mean overshoot and variance shrinkage, this paper demonstrates that guidance provably improves the average reciprocal of classifier probability, a metric aligned with Inception Score. Against Bradley & Nakkiran (2024), who show classifier-free guidance cannot generate samples from p(x|c)^γ p(x)^{1-γ} for GMMs and connect CFG to a predictor-corrector method, this paper directly analyzes and demonstrates the effectiveness of CFG under general distributions without restricting to GMMs. The paper's key distinction is moving from case-study analyses (isotropic Gaussians, 1D intervals, specific GMMs) to a general data distribution setting, where the improvement is in expectation rather than pointwise.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the classifier-free guidance framework of Ho & Salimans (2021) and the continuous-time SDE formulation of Song et al. (2021b) by providing the first general-distribution theoretical analysis of why guidance improves sample quality. Prior theoretical studies (Wu et al., 2024; Chidambaram et al., 2024; Bradley & Nakkiran, 2024) were restricted to specific distribution families (GMMs, isotropic Gaussians, 1D intervals) and either assumed uniform improvement or showed guidance could degrade performance. The key conceptual departure is the use of a martingale argument on the reciprocal classifier probability combined with Ito's formula, which yields a clean general result without distributional restrictions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work resolves a fundamental open question—why guidance works—under general data distributions, and in doing so reveals that the improvement is non-uniform: guidance preferentially boosts high-quality samples while potentially degrading a subset of low-quality ones. This insight motivates adaptive guidance strategies that could achieve more uniform gains. Open questions include extending the analysis to the Inception Score metric directly, tightening the discretization bounds (which depend on dimension, Lipschitz constant, and score error in potentially suboptimal ways), and understanding whether the smoothness assumptions in Theorem 3.6 are truly necessary.
