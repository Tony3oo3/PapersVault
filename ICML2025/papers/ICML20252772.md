## Problem

FFN layers, rather than attention layers, are the dominant source of ViT inference latency, with their contribution growing from ~33% in small models to over 53% in ViT-Large. Existing efficient ViT techniques (token pruning, hybrid CNN-Transformer architectures, network pruning) largely overlook structural reparameterization applied directly to the intrinsic FFN layers, leaving a critical optimization opportunity unexplored for large-scale ViTs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252772/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images (224×224 for ImageNet-1k classification; variable resolutions for MSCOCO detection and ADE20K segmentation) and produces class labels, bounding boxes, or pixel-wise segmentation masks. The key structural detail is that the model exists in two forms: a training-time form with full FFN layers (two large linear projections flanking a GELU activation) and a post-reparameterization inference form where each FFN is condensed into three smaller linear transformations with all normalizations absorbed into the weights, yielding identical outputs but reduced parameter count and FLOPs.

## Method

RePaViT introduces a channel idle mechanism in which only μC out of ρC feature channels (default μ=1, ρ=4) pass through the nonlinear activation in each FFN layer, while the remaining (ρ−μ)C channels bypass it, creating a linear pathway through the FFN. This linear pathway enables structural reparameterization at inference: the two large projection matrices are merged via matrix multiplication into three smaller weight matrices (an input projection, an output projection, and a combined shortcut weight), reducing FFN complexity from O(2ρNC²) to O((2μ+1)NC²). LayerNorm is replaced with BatchNorm (following Yao et al., 2021) so that normalization parameters can be algebraically folded into adjacent projection weights, and the residual shortcut is absorbed into the reparameterized weight. Unlike RepVGG-style reparameterization that sums parallel convolutional branches, RePaViT multiplies consecutive projection weights, and unlike prior ViT reparameterization work that only targets inserted CNN components, it directly reparameterizes the native FFN layers of standard ViT architectures.

## Results

RePaViT achieves 66.8% and 68.7% inference speed-ups on ViT-Large and ViT-Huge respectively, while simultaneously improving top-1 accuracy by +1.7% and +1.1% over their vanilla counterparts (82.0% vs 80.3% and 81.4% vs 80.3%). On mid-scale models, RePa-DeiT-Base/0.75 delivers a 57.8% speed-up with only a 0.5% accuracy drop, and RePa-Swin-Base/0.75 achieves 43.2% speed-up with a 0.9% drop. The method consistently reduces FFN parameters by ~41% (e.g., 304.5M → 178.4M for ViT-Large) and computational complexity by ~41.5%, with the speed gain exceeding the complexity reduction due to elimination of normalization and shortcut operations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against vanilla ViT backbones (DeiT, ViT, Swin, LV-ViT), RePaViT consistently wins on speed (16.5%–68.7% speed-up) while the accuracy trade-off narrows with model size: small models lose 0.9–2.8% top-1 accuracy, but ViT-Large and ViT-Huge actually gain +1.7% and +1.1% respectively. Against state-of-the-art pruning methods (WDPruning, X-Pruner, DC-ViT, LPViT), RePaViT/0.75 delivers substantially larger speed improvements — e.g., +57.8% on DeiT-Base vs. +18.4% (DC-ViT) and +18.8% (LPViT), and +42.4% on Swin-Base vs. +14.9% (DC-ViT) and +8.9% (LPViT) — while maintaining competitive or higher accuracy. Against the closest reparameterization baseline SLAB (Guo et al., 2024), RePaViT/0.25 achieves both faster inference (452.3 vs. 387.0 img/s on DeiT-Base; 356.3 vs. 299.9 img/s on Swin-Base) and higher accuracy (81.1% vs. 78.9% on DeiT-Base; 83.7% vs. 83.6% on Swin-Base) with fewer parameters. On dense prediction, RePa-Swin-Base with RetinaNet achieves 18.7% latency reduction while improving AP from 38.9 to 39.8, and with UperNet achieves 15.4% latency reduction with only 1.2% mIoU loss.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts structural reparameterization—a technique well-established in CNNs via RepVGG (merging parallel convolutional branches)—to the intrinsic FFN layers of Vision Transformers, where it had not previously been applied. The channel idle mechanism draws conceptual inspiration from ShuffleNet v2's channel idling in grouped convolutions, but repurposes it to create a linear pathway through the nonlinear activation in FFN layers, enabling vertical (multiplicative) reparameterization of consecutive linear projections rather than RepVGG's horizontal (additive) merging of parallel branches. Unlike prior ViT reparameterization efforts (FastViT, SLAB, RepViT) that either introduce CNN components to reparameterize or only fold BatchNorms into weights, RePaViT directly compresses the standard FFN structure without altering the backbone architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that FFN layers dominate inference latency in large ViTs (over 53% for ViT-Large) and that this proportion grows with model size establishes a clear, previously underexploited target for efficiency. The method's post-training nature (no retraining required) and its consistent scaling—yielding ~68% speedups with accuracy *gains* on ViT-Large/Huge—make it a practical, drop-in acceleration for large foundation models like CLIP and SAM. However, the approach suffers notable accuracy drops on small models (e.g., DeiT-Tiny loses 2.7%), and its applicability to even larger or non-ViT architectures (e.g., LLMs, SAM) remains untested, leaving open whether the channel idle mechanism generalizes beyond the tested ViT family.
