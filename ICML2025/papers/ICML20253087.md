## Problem

Cutting-plane methods, a classic gradient-free optimization technique with strong convergence guarantees, have only been applied to linear models in the active learning context. No prior work has extended cutting-plane training to deep nonlinear neural networks, and no active learning scheme for deep networks has come with convergence guarantees. This paper bridges that gap by showing how ReLU network training can be reframed as a linear program, enabling cutting-plane methods to operate directly on deep network parameters.

## Input / Output

The method consumes feature vectors (e.g., 2560-dimensional Phi-2 embeddings for IMDB reviews, or raw coordinates for synthetic tasks), an unlabeled data pool, and a subsampled set of activation patterns (diagonal matrices encoding ReLU sign configurations over the training data). At each iteration it queries a single data point from the pool and produces a cut on the parameter polyhedron. The final output is a set of LP variables from which standard ReLU network weights can be explicitly reconstructed; prediction at test time is a single forward-pass-equivalent computation of sign(Σ(xᵀuᵢ)₊ − (xᵀuᵢ′)₊) for the two-layer case. For regression, the output is a continuous predicted value constrained within an ε-trust region around the target.

## Method

The core insight is that training a ReLU network for binary classification is equivalent to solving a linear program: by enumerating all activation patterns (hyperplane arrangements) induced by the data matrix, the nonlinear ReLU constraints become a finite set of linear inequalities in auxiliary variables uᵢ, uᵢ′. The cutting-plane algorithm then iteratively computes the analytic center of the current feasible polyhedron, queries a data point (selected to minimize prediction margin for active learning), and if the center misclassifies that point, intersects the polyhedron with a new half-space cut. This yields a gradient-free training loop that guarantees correct classification of all previously seen points. For active learning, the query strategy ensures cuts pass near the center, and the paper proves via Grünbaum's inequality that each active cut reduces the feasible set volume by a factor of at least (1−1/e) ≈ 0.63, giving the first geometric convergence guarantee for deep active learning. The reformulation extends to arbitrary-depth ReLU networks (Theorem 4.2) and to regression by replacing the sign constraint with an L₂ trust-region cut.

## Results

On the synthetic spiral binary classification task (100 points, 20-query budget), the cutting-plane AL method with a two-layer ReLU network achieves 100% train and test accuracy, outperforming all seven deep active learning baselines. On the quadratic regression task (100 points, 20 queries), it achieves the lowest train/test RMSE of 0.01/0.01, reducing errors by over 80%/75% compared to the next best baseline. On IMDB sentiment classification using Phi-2 embeddings (d=2560, P=500 activation patterns), the two-layer ReLU model with cutting-plane training outperforms both the linear cutting-plane baseline and SGD-based training within the same query budget, with active sampling further improving over random sampling.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's cutting-plane AL method outperforms all seven compared baselines across both synthetic and real-data experiments. Against the linear cutting-plane AL of Louche & Ralaivola (2015), the nonlinear ReLU-based method achieves strictly higher accuracy on IMDB and remains feasible on the quadratic regression task where the linear version becomes infeasible after only 4 queries due to the mismatch between linear capacity and nonlinear data. On the spiral classification task, CPAL achieves perfect 100% accuracy with 20 queries while all deep AL baselines (Gal et al. 2017 Bayesian AL, Woodward & Finn 2017 one-shot learning, Mussmann et al. 2022 expected error reduction, Zhu & Nowak 2022 nonparametric AL, Karzand & Nowak 2020 maximin AL) fall short. On quadratic regression, CPAL's RMSE of 0.01 reduces errors by over 80% (train) and 75% (test) relative to the next best baseline, with GreedyT (Wu et al. 2019) and kldiv among the regression-specific methods also outperformed. On IMDB, the cutting-plane training scheme surpasses SGD even with as few as 15 query points where SGD struggles, and the active sampling strategy identifies more informative data points than random sampling within the same budget.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the cutting-plane active learning framework of Louche & Ralaivola (2015), which was restricted to linear binary classifiers, to deep ReLU networks of arbitrary depth. The key enabling insight is a linear programming reformulation of ReLU network training via activation patterns (hyperplane arrangements), building on the convex reformulation ideas of Ergen & Pilanci (2021a,b) but specifically tailored to the cutting-plane workflow rather than direct convex solving. Unlike prior convex NN formulations that solve a single large convex program, this work uses the LP structure iteratively to add cuts and track a shrinking feasible set, inheriting the geometric convergence theory of classic cutting-plane methods (Gomory 1958; Kelley 1960; Grünbaum 1960).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first active learning method for deep neural networks with a proven geometric convergence rate (volume contraction of (1−1/e)^t), which is a significant theoretical gap in the deep AL literature where most methods (Bayesian, uncertainty-based, committee-based) lack such guarantees. However, practical impact is currently limited: the method relies on a CPU-bound convex solver for analytic center computation, uses non-exhaustive activation pattern subsampling, and has only been validated on small-scale synthetic and single-dataset (IMDB) experiments. Open questions include whether GPU-parallel center-finding can make this competitive at scale, whether the approach extends to cross-entropy losses and generative models, and whether the activation pattern enumeration bottleneck can be overcome for high-dimensional inputs.
