## Problem

Estimating smooth optimal transport maps from finite samples is a well-studied nonparametric problem with known minimax rates, but no differentially private estimator with statistical guarantees existed. This paper asks how the minimax rate degrades when the estimation must be ε-differentially private with respect to the underlying data samples, and provides both an upper bound via a concrete private mechanism and a matching lower bound.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251270/cropped_page6_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes two independent samples: X_{1:n} drawn i.i.d. from a source distribution P and Y_{1:n} drawn i.i.d. from a target distribution Q, both supported on [0,1]^d with P having a density bounded between 1/M and M. It outputs a private Brenier potential f̂_priv (a convex function on [-1,2]^d) and the corresponding private transport map T̂_priv = ∇f̂_priv, with the guarantee that the entire mechanism is ε-DP under the Hamming neighboring relation on the combined dataset. In practice, the potential is discretized on a uniform grid and its gradient is approximated via finite differences.

## Method

The estimator builds on the empirical semi-dual formulation: it restricts the Brenier potential to a wavelet approximation space V_J ∩ X(2M) (controlling smoothness and strong convexity), constructs a finite δ-covering C_{J,M} of this space, and applies the report-noisy-argmin mechanism with Laplace noise scaled by the sensitivity of the empirical semi-dual objective (bounded by 2‖f‖_∞ ∨ 2|Ω̃|² per sample). The key insight is that the covering cardinality grows as exp(2^{Jd}·log(1/δ)), so the privacy noise term scales as 2^{Jd}·log(nε)/(nε), creating a three-way bias-variance-privacy tradeoff optimized over the resolution J. This yields an L²(P) error rate of n^{-1} ∨ n^{-2α/(2α-2+d)} ∨ (nε)^{-2α/(2α+d)}, and a minimax lower bound of the same form up to a gap in the privacy-dependent exponent. A practical discretization replaces the Fenchel-Legendre transform with a grid-restricted supremum and clips potential values to maintain the sensitivity bound.

## Results

The paper establishes that the proposed differentially private transport map estimator achieves an L² error rate of at most $n^{-1} \vee n^{-\frac{2\alpha}{2\alpha-2+d}} \vee (n\epsilon)^{-\frac{2\alpha}{2\alpha+d}}$ up to poly-logarithmic factors, where n is the sample size, ε is the privacy parameter, α is the Hölder smoothness, and d is the dimension. A matching minimax lower bound of $n^{-1} \vee n^{-\frac{2\alpha}{2\alpha-2+d}} \vee (n\epsilon)^{-\frac{2\alpha}{\alpha-1+d}}$ is also provided, though the privacy-dependent terms do not fully match between upper and lower bounds. In the qualitative experiment (Gaussian attraction/repulsion model with n=200000, ε=1, 64² grid), the privately estimated transport map visually closely tracks the true optimal map.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is the first work to address private estimation of smooth transport maps with statistical guarantees, so no direct baselines exist for comparison. The paper's main comparison is against the non-private minimax rate of Hütter & Rigollet (2021), which is $n^{-1} \vee n^{-\frac{2\alpha}{2\alpha-2+d}}$. The key finding is that privacy introduces an additional degradation term $(n\epsilon)^{-\frac{2\alpha}{2\alpha+d}}$ (upper bound) or $(n\epsilon)^{-\frac{2\alpha}{\alpha-1+d}}$ (lower bound), meaning estimation is provably degraded when $\epsilon \lesssim n^{-\frac{\alpha-1}{2\alpha-2+d}} \wedge n^{-\frac{\alpha+1-d}{2\alpha}}$. The gap between the upper and lower bound privacy terms (exponent $2\alpha+d$ vs. $\alpha-1+d$) remains an open question, with the authors conjecturing the lower bound is more likely to be tight.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends the minimax estimation framework of Hütter & Rigollet (2021) for smooth optimal transport maps by adding a differential privacy constraint on the sample data. The estimation pipeline—solving the empirical semi-dual problem over wavelet approximation spaces and recovering the transport map via the gradient of the Brenier potential—is inherited wholesale from that work; the novelty lies in replacing the exact argmin with a report-noisy-max mechanism using Laplace noise to achieve ε-DP. The paper also situates itself within the broader private nonparametric statistics literature (private density estimation, private regression) and connects to task-specific private OT work (private sliced Wasserstein, private domain adaptation), but explicitly positions itself as the first to provide minimax-type statistical guarantees for private smooth transport map estimation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that privacy provably degrades the estimation rate for smooth transport maps, with the degradation depending on the interplay between sample size n, privacy budget ε, smoothness α, and dimension d. A notable open question is the gap between the upper bound's privacy term (nε)^(-2α/(2α+d)) and the lower bound's (nε)^(-2α/(α-1+d)), leaving it unclear which is tight; the authors conjecture the lower bound is correct, suggesting the proposed estimator or its analysis is suboptimal. Future directions include entropic regularization and Sinkhorn-based algorithms to close this gap and improve practical efficiency. For applications in fairness auditing and bias analysis where OT maps are used to construct counterfactuals, this work provides the first theoretical foundation for doing so under privacy guarantees.
