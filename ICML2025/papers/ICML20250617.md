## Problem

Existing federated prompt learning methods enforce uniform prompt lengths across all clients to enable aggregation, preventing clients with diverse data distributions from using optimally sized prompts. Additionally, aggregated global prompts can contain conflicting information that degrades client-specific performance, and prior global-local prompt methods lack explicit mechanisms to disentangle and resolve such knowledge conflicts.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250617/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 224×224 RGB images and class labels as input, processing them through a frozen CLIP backbone (ViT-B/16 or ResNet-50). On the text side, each client constructs prompted sequences from a fixed-length global prompt (16 tokens) and a variable-length local prompt (4–32 tokens), padded to a common encoder length with zero tokens. Classification is performed by cosine similarity between frozen image features and prompted text features; at inference, only local-prompt-derived features are used, ensuring personalized predictions.

## Method

FedPHA introduces a decoupled Global-Local (G-L) architecture where each client holds a fixed-length global prompt (aggregated via weighted averaging at the server) and an independently sized local prompt, implicitly coupled through shared prefix/suffix tokens and a common frozen Transformer encoder. To resolve conflicts between global and local knowledge, the method performs SVD on the global prompt and projects the local prompt into the null space spanned by the smaller singular-value directions of V, effectively filtering out components dominated by the global prompt's major axes. A bidirectional alignment loss then balances information retention and separation: a pull term (MSE) aligns the local prompt with its null-space projection to preserve essential local features, while a push term (margin-based ReLU) enforces a minimum distance between local and global features to prevent collapse. The combined objective includes cross-entropy losses for both prompts plus these alignment terms, and the SVD projection adds negligible computational overhead (<1% per round).

## Results

On single-domain CLIP datasets (10 clients, 16-shot), FedPHA achieves 99.05% on Caltech101, 96.42% on Food101, 99.25% on Flowers102, 99.25% on OxfordPets, and 91.79% on DTD, outperforming the strongest baseline FedOTP by 0.65–3.46 percentage points. On multi-domain datasets, FedPHA reaches 94.74% average accuracy on Office31 and 89.51% on OfficeHome, surpassing FedPGP (91.58% / 88.95%) and FedOTP (89.19% / 84.26%). On CIFAR-10/100 with 100 clients under Dirichlet(0.5) partitioning, FedPHA (fixed length) attains 94.11% and 75.92% respectively, the best among all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedPHA consistently outperforms all five baselines across every dataset and setting. Against the strongest competitor FedOTP (CVPR 2024), FedPHA gains +0.94% on Caltech101, +3.46% on Food101, +0.78% on Flowers102, +0.65% on OxfordPets, and +1.82% on DTD; on multi-domain Office31/OfficeHome the margins widen to +5.55% and +5.25% respectively. Against FedPGP (arXiv 2024), the advantage is larger on harder datasets: +28.84% on DTD and +4.96% on Food101, reflecting FedPGP's difficulty under high heterogeneity. The simpler baselines PromptFL and PromptFL+Prox trail FedPHA by 5–36 points depending on dataset, while Zero-Shot CLIP (no training) lags by 7–31 points. On CIFAR-10/100 with 100 clients, FedPHA (fixed length) leads FedOTP by +0.68% and +0.85%, and FedPGP by +2.01% and +1.11%, confirming the advantage scales with client count and data heterogeneity.

## Relation to Prior Work

FedPHA extends the federated prompt learning line (PromptFL, pFedPrompt, FedOTP, FedPGP) by introducing a decoupled Global-Local architecture where local prompts can have client-specific lengths, breaking the uniform-length constraint that all prior G-L methods enforce. The SVD-based null-space projection for filtering conflicting global components is borrowed from matrix factorization and subspace projection literature but applied here for the first time to disentangle global-local knowledge conflicts in federated prompt tuning. The bidirectional alignment (pull/push) loss is a standard contrastive-style regularizer repurposed to maintain local expressiveness while preventing collapse toward the global prompt.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap: real federated clients have heterogeneous computational budgets and data distributions, making uniform prompt lengths an unrealistic assumption. However, the random-length experiment (Table 4) shows fixed-length FedPHA slightly outperforms random-length, suggesting the heterogeneity benefit is modest without an adaptive length-assignment strategy, which the authors acknowledge as future work. The SVD projection's <1% overhead makes it practically deployable, but the method is validated only on CLIP-based image classification; whether the null-space conflict resolution generalizes to other VLMs or modalities remains open. The exhaustive 512-combination search for optimal prompt lengths also does not scale to larger client populations.
