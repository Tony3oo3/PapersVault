## Problem

Existing federated multi-view clustering methods rely solely on global pseudo-labels to supervise downstream clustering while ignoring global multi-view information during upstream feature extraction, limiting the quality of learned representations. Additionally, the missing data problem in federated multi-view settings remains largely unexplored, as missing samples represented by zero vectors become isolated in locally constructed graphs, making feature estimation infeasible.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250276/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each of M clients holds one view of N samples (with potentially missing entries zero-padded, controlled by missing rate δ) along with a locally constructed k-NN adjacency matrix. The server distributes a globally fused binary adjacency matrix and pseudo-labels back to clients each round. The final output is a per-sample cluster assignment (argmax of sharpened Student-t soft distribution), while intermediate outputs include high-level features, reconstructed samples for missing entries, and the iteratively updated fused graph.

## Method

FIMCFG introduces a dual-head GCN encoder at each client where one head propagates information through the server's globally fused graph and the other through the locally repaired graph, extracting complementary global and view-specific underlying features. A key mechanism—global graph structure migration—replaces the rows of missing samples in the local adjacency matrix with corresponding rows from the global graph, allowing GCN message passing to estimate latent features of missing data from complete neighbors. A fusion module then maps the concatenated underlying features into a low-dimensional high-level space, regularized by a consistent graph reconstruction loss that aligns the high-level feature graph to the global fused graph. Clustering is performed via a Student-t soft distribution layer supervised by server-computed pseudo-labels through KL divergence, while a decoder reconstructs original data from underlying features. The server aggregates high-level features (weighted by per-view silhouette coefficients), fuses latent graphs via weighted averaging and k-thresholding, and runs K-means to produce the next round's global clustering centers and pseudo-labels.

## Results

FIMCFG achieves the best clustering performance across all four datasets (HW, Scene-15, Landuse-21, 100Leaves) in both complete (δ=0) and incomplete (δ=0.5) settings. On the complete HW dataset, it reaches 97.6% ACC, 94.6% NMI, and 94.8% ARI; on 100Leaves, 96.0% ACC, 98.2% NMI, and 94.2% ARI. Under 50% missing data (δ=0.5), FIMCFG maintains strong performance (e.g., 95.2% ACC on HW, 80.2% ACC on 100Leaves) while the gap over the second-best method widens substantially, demonstrating robustness to missing views.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FIMCFG outperforms all nine baselines on every dataset and metric in both complete and incomplete settings. Among federated baselines, FCUIF (Ren et al., 2024) is the strongest competitor, achieving 91.7% ACC on HW and 60.4% ACC on 100Leaves at δ=0.5, but FIMCFG leads by 3.5 and 19.8 percentage points respectively. The centralized GIMVC method (Bai et al., 2024) is the closest overall rival, reaching 91.1% ACC on HW and 68.8% ACC on 100Leaves under 50% missing data, yet FIMCFG still surpasses it by 4.1 and 11.4 points. The advantage of FIMCFG grows markedly as the missing rate increases: on 100Leaves at δ=0.5, the gap over the second-best (GIMVC) widens from 10.3 points (ACC) in the complete setting to 11.4 points, and over FCUIF it expands from 5.0 to 19.8 points. Weaker baselines such as FedMVFPC, MRL-CAL, and GIGA degrade severely under missing data (e.g., FedMVFPC drops to 13.4% ACC on 100Leaves), highlighting the importance of the global graph structure migration and dual-head encoder design in FIMCFG.

## Relation to Prior Work

FIMCFG sits at the intersection of three established lines of work: incomplete multi-view clustering (Completer, ICMVC, CRTC), GNN-based multi-view clustering (Xiao et al. 2023, Wang et al. 2024b), and federated multi-view clustering (Chen et al. 2023, Ren et al. 2024, Yan et al. 2024). It departs from prior federated MVC methods by injecting global graph structure into the upstream feature extraction stage via a dual-head GCN encoder, rather than restricting global information to pseudo-label supervision of downstream clustering only. The global graph structure migration technique—replacing rows of the local adjacency matrix corresponding to missing samples with rows from the server-fused graph—is a novel mechanism that repurposes the federated communication channel to repair incomplete local graphs before GCN-based feature propagation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap: federated multi-view clustering in real deployments must handle missing views, yet prior federated MVC methods largely ignore incompleteness or treat it as a secondary concern. The global graph structure migration and dual-head encoder design offer a reusable pattern for any federated GNN-based task where local data is incomplete. Limitations include evaluation on only four small-scale datasets (≤4485 samples), a simplified federated topology where each client holds exactly one view, and no analysis of communication overhead or convergence under non-IID view distributions. Open questions include scalability to high-dimensional or large-scale multi-view data, more complex client-server topologies, and whether the silhouette-based aggregation weights remain effective under severe class imbalance.
