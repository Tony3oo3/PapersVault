## Problem

Existing membership inference attacks for VLMs either require access to output logits (limiting them to white-box models) or are restricted to narrow data types like document-based VQA. There is no method that can detect whether copyrighted movie content was included in a VLM's training data while remaining applicable to both white-box and black-box models such as Gemini.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250568/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes individual movie frames (RGB images, originally 1126×512 pixels) paired with detailed text captions generated by Qwen2-VL 7B, and produces a free-form text completion naming the movie title. Frames are split into 'main' (featuring key characters, 100 per movie) and 'neutral' (landscapes, objects, minor characters, 40 per movie) to vary difficulty. The model is queried separately on image frames and on captions alone, and the intersection of correct predictions between the two modalities is removed to isolate image-specific memorization.

## Method

DIS-CO prompts a VLM to identify a movie's title from a frame using unconstrained free-form text generation rather than multiple-choice, reducing the chance-level accuracy from 25% (MCQA with 4 options) to approximately 0.01% (free-form over ~10,000 possible titles), thereby minimizing false positives. The pipeline establishes two baselines: performance on clean (post-cutoff) movies to capture residual knowledge of announced-but-unreleased films, and caption-only performance on older suspect movies to account for accumulated public knowledge from posters, trailers, and subtitles. For each suspect movie, the model is queried on both frames and captions; correct predictions that appear in both modalities are discarded ([DIS-CO]), leaving only predictions attributable to visual memorization. If the remaining accuracy significantly exceeds both baselines, the movie is flagged as likely included in training data. A defense strategy is also proposed: fine-tuning the model on suspect movie frames with a neutral 'Copyrighted Content' label, which generalizes to visually similar titles.

## Results

On the MovieTecton benchmark, DIS-CO achieves an average AUC of 0.930 on neutral frames and 0.988 on main frames across four large VLMs (GPT-4o, Gemini-1.5 Pro, LLaMA-3.2 90B, Qwen2-VL 72B), nearly doubling the best prior method (MaxRényi-K%, avg AUC 0.481/0.552). GPT-4o reaches 0.989 AUC on neutral frames and a perfect 1.000 on main frames. In terms of absolute accuracy on neutral frames, DIS-CO identifies 33.8% of suspect movie frames correctly for GPT-4o while maintaining near-zero false positives (0.2%) on clean movies, compared to MCQA's 72.1% suspect accuracy but 41.0% false-positive rate on clean data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DIS-CO substantially outperforms the MaxRényi-K% method (Li et al., 2024b) on both frame types: on neutral frames, DIS-CO's average AUC of 0.930 is roughly double Rényi's 0.481, and on main frames, 0.988 vs. 0.552. Rényi is also restricted to white-box models with full logit access (only LLaMA-3.2 90B and Qwen2-VL 72B are evaluated), whereas DIS-CO works with both white-box and black-box APIs. Against the MCQA baseline, DIS-CO achieves lower raw accuracy on suspect movies (e.g., 33.8% vs. 72.1% for GPT-4o on neutral frames) but dramatically fewer false positives on clean movies (0.2% vs. 41.0%), yielding a far superior AUC (0.930 vs. 0.778). The Captions baseline (text-only) achieves competitive AUC on neutral frames (0.858 avg) but poor absolute accuracy (12.8% for GPT-4o), confirming that visual memorization—not just textual knowledge—drives DIS-CO's detection signal.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the membership inference attack (MIA) literature from text-only LLMs to the multimodal VLM setting, specifically targeting copyrighted movie content. It departs from the MaxRényi-K% approach of Li et al. (2024b), which requires full logit access and thus cannot be applied to black-box APIs like Gemini, and from MCQA-based methods (Duarte et al. 2024; Golchin & Surdeanu 2024) that the authors argue introduce selection bias. Conceptually, DIS-CO is closest to Karamolegkou et al. (2023), who used free-form generation to elicit verbatim reproduction in text LLMs, but adapts this to the image-to-title mapping task with a novel intersection-removal pipeline and temporal baseline calibration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that all six tested model families show non-trivial detection of copyrighted movie frames raises concrete legal and ethical concerns for the industry, particularly given the 24+ US copyright lawsuits filed since 2023. The method's applicability to both white-box and black-box models makes it a practical audit tool, and the demonstrated defense (fine-tuning with a neutral label) offers a concrete mitigation path. Key limitations include the temporal split assumption (which breaks as newer models emerge, as shown with Gemini-2.0 Flash), the restriction to content with a discrete 'name' (movies), and the relatively small benchmark of 100 titles. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to other copyrighted media (music, books, fine art) and whether it can distinguish between memorization and generalization in more nuanced ways.
