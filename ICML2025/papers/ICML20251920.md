## Problem

Existing multi-scale VLMs integrate visual patches indiscriminately across resolutions, diluting object-specific details with irrelevant background information and exacerbating perceptual hallucination. Prior contrastive decoding methods for VLMs focus on suppressing language priors via noised images or self-generated descriptions, leaving unexplored the potential of selectively contrasting sparse and dense visual information across scales to ground responses in actual image content.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251920/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard RGB image and a text instruction (e.g., a yes/no object-presence query or open-ended VQA question) and produces a text response. The image is consumed at multiple resolutions simultaneously (e.g., 84×84, 168×168, 336×336, 672×672 for LLaVA-NeXT/CLIP-336), with lower-resolution stages processed via bilinearly interpolated positional embeddings of the existing vision encoder—no additional training or auxiliary models are required. The output is a single decoded text sequence, but internally the model generates intermediate stage outputs that are combined through multi-stage contrastive decoding before the final response is produced.

## Method

SECOND is a training-free, multi-stage decoding framework with two coupled mechanisms. First, Selective Multi-Scale Feature Integration starts from the coarsest scale and progressively carries forward only object-relevant patches to finer scales, where the selection percentage is dynamically set by the entropy of a unified visual attention map (element-wise product of vision self-attention and LLM cross-attention) via a hyperparameter λ. Second, Multi-Stage Contrastive Decoding contrasts the final (expert) stage logits against each intermediate (amateur) stage logits with separate weights α, β, γ, amplifying the signal from fine-grained, object-focused patches while suppressing responses driven by sparse, low-resolution context. The theoretical grounding is the Attention Dice Coefficient, which the paper proves monotonically increases across stages under selective patch selection (Thm. 3.3), directly linking the mechanism to reduced hallucination probability.

## Results

On the POPE hallucination benchmark, SECOND with multi-stage Contrastive Decoding achieves f1 scores of 89.2 (MSCOCO), 90.4 (A-OKVQA), and 87.4 (GQA) with LLaVA-NeXT + Vicuna-7B, outperforming the baseline (86.5/88.8/86.3) by 2.7/1.6/1.1 points. Across all four model configurations, SECOND+CD outperforms both the baseline and VCD in 11 out of 12 highlighted POPE cases. On general tasks, SECOND+CD improves MMStar from 37.3 to 38.6 and MMBench from 75.8 to 80.0 (LLaVA-NeXT + Vicuna-7B), while also boosting VQAv2 from 76.4 to 77.5, demonstrating gains in both perception and reasoning without degrading higher-level capabilities.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole external baseline is VCD (Leng et al., CVPR 2024), a visual contrastive decoding method that uses noised images as the contrastive reference. SECOND with multi-stage CD outperforms VCD on all 12 POPE f1 configurations (4 models × 3 datasets), with the largest margin on Yi-VL where SECOND+CD achieves 84.3 vs. VCD's 78.6 on MSCOCO (+5.7) and 86.5 vs. 81.6 on A-OKVQA (+4.9). On LLaVA-NeXT + Vicuna-7B, SECOND+CD gains +1.9 (MSCOCO), +2.3 (A-OKVQA), and +2.5 (GQA) over VCD. Notably, VCD actually degrades performance relative to the plain baseline on several configurations (e.g., LLaVA-NeXT + Mistral-7B: 86.6 vs. 87.3 on MSCOCO), whereas SECOND without CD already matches or exceeds the baseline, and adding multi-stage CD provides consistent further gains. On general tasks (Table 2), SECOND+CD also surpasses VCD on MMStar across all models (e.g., 38.6 vs. 38.1 for Vicuna-7B; 39.8 vs. 34.4 for Yi-6B), confirming that the multi-stage selective contrast is more effective than single-step noise-based contrast for both hallucination-specific and general perception tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

SECOND extends the Contrastive Decoding framework (Li et al., 2022) from a single expert-vs-amateur contrast to a multi-stage hierarchy where each intermediate stage serves as a contrastive reference, amplifying differences across visual scales. It departs from prior multi-scale VLM designs (e.g., LLaVA-NeXT) that indiscriminately accumulate patches at all scales, instead introducing an entropy-driven patch selection mechanism that selectively integrates only object-relevant fine-grained information. The theoretical grounding via the Attention Dice Coefficient connects VLM hallucination to the image segmentation literature, providing a principled metric absent from prior hallucination-mitigation work that typically relies on empirical ablations alone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As a training-free plug-in applicable to existing VLMs (CLIP- and SigLIP-based), SECOND offers a practical path to reduce hallucination without retraining, which is valuable given the compute cost of multi-scale vision encoders. The paper acknowledges a key limitation: reliance on internal attention maps means the selection mechanism can fail when attention is poorly calibrated, leaving open the question of more robust patch-relevance signals. The 1.4× decoding overhead and the need to tune three CD hyperparameters (α, β, γ) per model-benchmark combination are practical concerns that may limit adoption. Broader implications include whether the selective multi-scale principle generalizes to video understanding or other modalities where temporal attention plays a role.
