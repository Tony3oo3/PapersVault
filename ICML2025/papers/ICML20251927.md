## Problem

Vision Language Models exhibit modality imbalance: they perform significantly worse on multi-step visual reasoning when the task is presented as an image than when the same task is presented as text, even compared to the underlying LLM on the text version. No prior work provides a controlled, quantitative framework to measure this gap or to systematically study which training strategies can transfer generalization from the dominant text modality to the weaker image modality.

## Input / Output

Each synthetic task (Table Readout, Grid Navigation, Visual Analogy) is presented in two interchangeable formats: a rendered RGB image or its equivalent LaTeX/text code, with the model receiving a task specification (e.g., start/end cell indices, objects to collect, or in-context analogy examples). The model outputs a verbose chain-of-thought reasoning trace followed by a final answer (a sequence of numbers, a path of directions, or a multiple-choice option). SIMPLE and HARD variants differ in reasoning length and compositional complexity (e.g., 5–10 vs. 25–30 cells to read, 1–4 vs. >4 path segments), enabling S2H generalization to serve as a measurable proxy for brittleness.

## Method

The paper introduces a synthetic benchmark of three algorithmic visual reasoning tasks, each with SIMPLE/HARD levels and image/text dual representations, then studies five supervision types (Text, Image, Image-via-Text, Text+Image, Mix) via supervised fine-tuning of Eagle-X2-Llama3-8B. The key intervention is Image-via-Text supervision, which trains the model to explicitly convert the image to text before generating the CoT solution; this is the critical component that enables S2H generalization on images by transferring reasoning from the text modality. Mix supervision (equal mix of Text, Image, and Image-via-Text) retains most of this benefit while reducing inference cost, and the trained model can internalize the conversion skill, solving directly from images without explicit text extraction. For tasks where neither modality generalizes from SIMPLE alone, Mix+ (adding HARD Text supervision) injects reasoning capability that transfers to image S2H generalization, and Align-Mix+ further improves this via a two-phase training that preconditions the model with SIMPLE Image-via-Text alignment before the main Mix+ phase. Gradient alignment analysis between SIMPLE and HARD image examples quantifies why these strategies work: Image-via-Text supervision produces training gradients that are better aligned with HARD-image evaluation gradients, especially at large gradient norms early in training.

## Results

On the non-S2H-generalizing tasks (Table Readout, Grid Navigation, Visual Analogy), standard Text, Image, and Mix supervision all fail to exceed 25% accuracy on HARD-image examples. Adding HARD Text supervision (Mix+) improves image S2H generalization to 64%, 92%, and 35% respectively. The two-phase Align-Mix+ strategy further boosts these to 76%, 96%, and 56% while maintaining inference cost. On the S2H-generalizing Consecutive Table Readout task, Text supervision achieves 80% on HARD-text vs. only 20% for Image supervision on HARD-image (a 60%p modality gap), which Mix supervision substantially narrows.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares five supervision strategies (Text, Image, Text+Image, Image-via-Text, Mix) and their extended variants (Mix+, Image-via-Text+, Align-Mix+) rather than external model baselines. On Consecutive Table Readout, Mix supervision nearly matches Text supervision's S2H generalization on MEDIUM examples and substantially outperforms Text+Image and Image supervision on HARD-image, closing most of the 60%p modality gap. On the three non-S2H-generalizing tasks, Mix+ (which injects HARD Text reasoning) dramatically outperforms all SIMPLE-only strategies (64–92% vs. <25%), demonstrating that reasoning capability learned in text transfers to image. Image-via-Text+ achieves 20–30%p higher accuracy than Mix+ on Table Readout and Visual Analogy but at ~3× inference cost; Align-Mix+ recovers most of this gap (76% vs. ~84–94% on Table Readout) while keeping inference at 1× cost. Gradient alignment analysis confirms that Mix and Align-Mix+ produce higher alignment scores at large gradient norms compared to Text+Image and Image supervision, explaining their superior generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Eagle-X2-Llama3-8B architecture (Shi et al., 2025) and positions itself at the intersection of three research threads: (1) the modality imbalance literature in VLMs (Peng et al., 2022; Huang et al., 2022; Fan et al., 2023), which has primarily relied on explicit representation matching, contrastive alignment, or gradient/learning-rate modulation; (2) simple-to-hard (S2H) generalization in LLMs (Zhou et al., 2024a; Anil et al., 2022; Sun et al., 2024), which has been extensively studied for text but largely absent for VLMs; and (3) algorithmic visual reasoning benchmarks (Ghosal et al., 2024; Cherian et al., 2023; Barrett et al., 2018). The key departure is showing that auto-regressive training with mixed supervision—specifically image-to-text conversion—transfers S2H generalization from text to image without any explicit cross-modal alignment objective, and that gradient alignment scores provide a mechanistic explanation for why this works.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first controlled, quantitative framework for measuring and mitigating modality imbalance in VLMs via S2H generalization, with the practical finding that image-to-text conversion supervision can be internalized at test time (reducing inference cost by ~3×). The gradient alignment analysis offers a new diagnostic tool for identifying which training strategies will promote cross-modal generalization. Limitations include reliance on synthetic tasks with limited prompt diversity, a single 8B-parameter model family (with partial replication on Qwen2.5-VL), and the brittleness of CoT traces to minor modifications. Open questions include whether curriculum-based data mixing can outperform the fixed Mix/Mix+ strategies, whether the findings extend to real-world benchmarks with natural SIMPLE/HARD gradations, and whether the internalization phenomenon persists at larger scales or with different CoT formulations.
