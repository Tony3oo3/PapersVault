## Problem

Robust policy gradient methods for average-reward MDPs (RAMDPs) remain largely unexplored, with no prior work providing gradient-based algorithms with global convergence guarantees. Existing robust MDP research is dominated by the discounted setting, and the few average-reward robust methods either rely on rectangularity assumptions or require exact worst-case transition evaluations. The inner worst-case transition evaluation problem for RAMDPs had not been studied at all.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252600/cropped_page7_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes a tabular MDP with finite state space S and action space A, an ambiguity set P (which may be (s,a)-rectangular, s-rectangular, or a general compact convex set), a cost function c bounded in [0,1], an initial policy π₀, step sizes {α_t}, and a decreasing tolerance sequence {δ_t} with δ_{t+1} ≤ τδ_t for τ ∈ (0,1). It outputs a stationary randomized policy π* minimizing the worst-case average cost and the corresponding worst-case transition kernel p*. The adaptive tolerance mechanism means the inner evaluation precision tightens over iterations, trading early computational cost for faster overall convergence.

## Method

RP2G (Robust Projected Policy Gradient) uses a nested-loop structure: at each outer iteration, an inner subroutine approximates the worst-case transition kernel p_t to precision δ_t, then the policy is updated via projected gradient descent using the standard AMDP policy gradient ∂J/∂π_sa = d_s^{π,p} · q_sa^{π,p}. The key theoretical challenge is that the robust objective Ψ(π) = max_{p∈P} J(π,p) is non-differentiable and non-convex; the authors overcome this by establishing weak convexity via sensitivity bounds and proving a gradient dominance condition on the Moreau envelope of Ψ, yielding global convergence in O(ε^{-4}) iterations. For the inner worst-case evaluation, two specialized algorithms are proposed: projected gradient ascent (with O(δ^{-2}) complexity) for s-rectangular ambiguity sets, and projected Langevin dynamics sampling from a Gibbs distribution for general compact convex ambiguity sets, which is the first method to handle the non-rectangular case.

## Results

On GARNET MDPs with (s,a)-rectangular ambiguity, RP2G converges to the optimal value J* (verified against robust value iteration) with relative error decreasing monotonically over iterations. In runtime comparison, RP2G is dramatically faster than the only existing gradient-based baseline RPMD: 22× faster on G(5,3,3) (0.63s vs 13.99s), 215× faster on G(10,5,5) (2.48s vs 532.64s), and 216× faster on G(15,10,6) (12.54s vs 2711.79s), thanks to its adaptive decreasing tolerance mechanism. Under general (ellipsoidal) ambiguity, RP2G with the projected Langevin dynamics inner solver converges to a robust policy, while non-robust PG fails to account for worst-case transitions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against RPMD (Sun et al., 2024), the only prior gradient-based method for RAMDPs, RP2G achieves a 22×–216× speedup in wall-clock time across all three problem sizes, because RPMD requires exact worst-case transition evaluations (fixed tolerance δ=10⁻⁵) while RP2G uses a decreasing tolerance sequence (δ₀=1, decay τ=0.95) that reduces inner-solve cost in early iterations. Against the non-robust policy gradient baseline, RP2G demonstrates that robust optimization under a general ellipsoidal ambiguity set is both feasible and convergent, whereas non-robust PG optimizes only under the nominal model and does not guarantee worst-case performance. RP2G's convergence is validated against the robust value iteration oracle (Wang et al., 2023c), confirming that the policy gradient approach reaches the same optimal objective value J* as the value-based method, but without requiring the rectangularity assumption that RVI relies on.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the policy gradient framework for nominal average-reward MDPs (Kumar et al., 2024b) to the robust setting, leveraging the Moreau envelope technique from non-smooth optimization (Davis & Drusvyatskiy, 2019) to handle the non-differentiability of the robust objective. It departs from prior robust MDP work, which has been almost exclusively confined to the discounted criterion and rectangular ambiguity sets, by establishing the first generic policy gradient method for RAMDPs that works under general compact convex ambiguity sets. The inner worst-case transition evaluation is addressed via projected gradient ascent (for rectangular sets) and a novel projected Langevin dynamics scheme (for general sets), drawing on Lamperski (2021) and Li et al. (2023). The sensitivity analysis builds on Cheng et al. (2024) but yields tighter Lipschitz constants (O(√A) vs. O(√A·S²) in prior work).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work fills a notable gap: prior RAMDP solvers relied on value iteration or mirror descent restricted to (s,a)-rectangular sets, leaving no gradient-based method with global convergence guarantees for general ambiguity. The decreasing tolerance mechanism provides a practical efficiency gain (20–200× faster than the only prior gradient-based method, RPMD), and the Langevin dynamics approach is the first to handle non-rectangular ambiguity in the inner evaluation. However, the analysis is restricted to tabular, model-based settings with an O(ε⁻⁴) convergence rate, and the general-ambiguity solver has exponential dependence on the kernel dimension, limiting immediate scalability. Future work on model-free, function-approximation-based extensions would be needed for real-world impact.
