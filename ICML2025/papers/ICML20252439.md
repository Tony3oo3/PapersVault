## Problem

Existing methods for computing optimal transport maps and Wasserstein barycenters in high-dimensional continuous settings rely on dual formulations solved via complex bi-level or tri-level adversarial optimization, which is unstable and computationally expensive. A critical gap is that no prior method can scale to computing Wasserstein barycenters of hundreds of input distributions, and most approaches do not directly yield OT maps or a generative model of the barycenter.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252439/cropped_page6_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes probability distributions accessible only through sampling (no explicit density required), along with non-negative weights summing to one for barycenter computation, and a fixed latent distribution (standard d-dimensional Gaussian). The conditioning variable s can be one-hot encoded or R-valued depending on the regularity of the family. Outputs include the OT map as a composition of inverse and forward flow passes, the Wasserstein-2 barycenter as a directly samplable generative model h_#λ, the transport cost value, and OT maps in both directions between each input distribution and the barycenter without additional computation.

## Method

The key theoretical insight (Theorem 3.2) reformulates the OT problem as minimizing the L^p distance between two bijective pushforward maps from a common latent space, bypassing the Kantorovich dual entirely. For Wasserstein-2 barycenters (Theorem 3.3), the problem becomes minimizing expected conditional variance of a family of pushforward maps, with the barycenter given by the weighted average h(z) = Σ w_s f(z,s). Architecturally, a single conditional normalizing flow (Real NVP or Glow with multi-scale design) parametrizes all pushforward maps f(·,s), with the conditioning variable s selecting the target distribution. Training uses gradient descent on a combined loss of negative log-likelihood (enforcing pushforward constraints via the change-of-variables formula) and an L^2 transport cost, with an exponentially decreasing weight on the L^2 term to resolve the multi-objective tension. The conditional architecture is what enables scaling to hundreds of input distributions, since all distributions share the same flow parameters and differ only in the conditioning input.

## Results

On high-dimensional Gaussian location-scatter data (d=128), the proposed method achieves a BW₂²-UVP of 0.094%, outperforming SCW₂B (1.280%) by over 13× and WIN (0.380%) by ~4×. On uniform location-scatter data at the same dimension, the method achieves 0.362% BW₂²-UVP versus 1.380% (SCW₂B) and 0.460% (WIN). The method also scales to 128 input distributions (d=64) with no performance degradation (L²-UVP of 0.073%), a regime that is computationally infeasible for the baselines. Training times are competitive: 23–47 minutes for Gaussian data (d=2–128) versus 57–93 minutes for WIN.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two state-of-the-art generative Wasserstein barycenter methods: SCW₂B (Fan et al., 2020), which solves a tri-level min-max-min optimization with input-convex neural networks, and WIN (Korotin et al., 2022), which combines a fixed-point algorithm with a reversed maximin neural OT solver. On the BW₂²-UVP metric (a lower bound on the true unexplained variance), the proposed method dominates both baselines across all tested dimensions (d=2 to d=128) for both Gaussian and uniform location-scatter families. At d=128 on Gaussian data, the gap is particularly large: 0.094% vs. 1.280% (SCW₂B) and 0.380% (WIN). On uniform data at d=128, the method achieves 0.362% vs. 1.380% (SCW₂B) and 0.460% (WIN). Training times are comparable or better: the proposed method takes 23–47 minutes on Gaussian data versus 57–93 minutes for WIN, while SCW₂B takes ~110 minutes regardless of dimension. A key practical advantage is that the conditional architecture scales to hundreds of input distributions (demonstrated with n=128 at d=64), which is infeasible for both baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in continuous optimal transport and Wasserstein barycenter computation, which relies on the Kantorovich dual formulation solved via bi-level or tri-level adversarial optimization (e.g., SCW2B, WIN, Kolesov et al. 2024). Instead, it directly solves the primal Monge problem by parametrizing bijective pushforward maps with conditional normalizing flows (Real NVP and Glow), leveraging Brenier's theorem to justify the bijectivity constraint. The theoretical core (Theorem 3.2) generalizes the classical 1D quantile-function solution to arbitrary absolutely continuous latent distributions in high dimensions, while Theorem 3.3 reformulates Wasserstein-2 barycenters as an expected conditional variance minimization—a perspective not previously exploited in the neural OT literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The conditional architecture enables computing Wasserstein-2 barycenters for hundreds of input distributions (demonstrated up to 128), a regime that was computationally infeasible for prior methods, with direct access to OT maps, their inverses, and a generative model of the barycenter without additional computation. The avoidance of adversarial training removes a major source of instability and hyperparameter sensitivity in existing approaches. Key limitations include restriction to Wasserstein-2 (extension to p≠2 is open) and potential computational intensity for very high-dimensional image data (d≫1000). The fair regression application on the Communities and Crime dataset (100 input distributions) demonstrates practical relevance beyond synthetic benchmarks.
