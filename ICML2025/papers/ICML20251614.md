## Problem

Existing interactive 3D segmentation methods (e.g., AGILE3D, InterObject3D) are deterministic, offering no mechanism to quantify predictive uncertainty or guide users toward unreliable regions. Additionally, they struggle to generalize from sparse user clicks in complex multi-object scenes, where both global scene context and fine-grained object characteristics must be captured simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251614/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a 3D point cloud (N×6: spatial coordinates + RGB) and an iterative sequence of user clicks, each specifying a 3D location and an object label (background or one of M objects). It produces a per-point segmentation mask (N labels) and a per-point uncertainty map derived from the variance of cosine-similarity scores across Monte Carlo samples of hierarchical latent variables. The interaction is iterative: users refine predictions by adding corrective clicks, with a maximum of 20 clicks per instance.

## Method

NPISeg3D formulates interactive 3D segmentation as a conditional Neural Process where user clicks form the context set and unclicked points form the target set. It introduces a two-level hierarchical latent variable structure: a scene-specific latent z_s (inferred via a single-layer transformer over averaged object prototypes) captures global scene context, while per-object latents z_o^m (inferred by blending z_s with click prototypes via a learnable weight α) encode fine-grained object characteristics. A probabilistic prototype modulator then dynamically adjusts each click prototype through element-wise scale (γ) and shift (β) parameters computed by MLPs conditioned on Monte Carlo samples of z_o^m, creating a Scene→Objects→Clicks information flow. Training optimizes an ELBO with segmentation loss plus KL divergences aligning variational posteriors (inferred from target data) to priors (inferred from context clicks), while inference aggregates cosine similarities between target points and modulated prototypes across N_zo Monte Carlo samples to produce both the mask and uncertainty map.

## Results

On multi-object interactive 3D segmentation, NPISeg3D achieves 90.5% average IoU and 5.0 average NoC on S3DIS-A5 (out-of-domain), outperforming AGILE3D (88.3% IoU, 6.2 NoC) by 2.2% IoU and 1.2 fewer clicks. On the challenging outdoor KITTI-360 dataset, NPISeg3D reaches 48.5% average IoU with 17.0 average NoC, a 4.2% IoU improvement over AGILE3D (44.3%, 18.2). For single-object segmentation on KITTI-360, the gap widens to 8.4% (58.0% vs. 49.6% average IoU). In-domain performance on ScanNet is competitive, with NPISeg3D achieving 84.7% average IoU and 10.1 average NoC versus AGILE3D's 84.4% and 10.2.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NPISeg3D is compared against three baselines: InterObject3D (sequential click-based segmentation), InterObject3D++ (iterative training variant), and AGILE3D (attention-guided multi-object segmentation, the strongest baseline). NPISeg3D consistently outperforms all three baselines across every evaluated dataset and both multi-object and single-object settings. Against AGILE3D, the gains are most pronounced on out-of-domain data: +2.2% average IoU on S3DIS-A5, +1.6% on Replica, and +4.2% on KITTI-360 (multi-object), with the single-object KITTI-360 gap reaching +8.4%. On in-domain ScanNet, NPISeg3D edges out AGILE3D by a narrow margin (+0.3% average IoU, −0.1 average NoC), indicating the probabilistic framework's advantage is primarily in generalization rather than in-distribution fitting. InterObject3D and InterObject3D++ are substantially outperformed, with gaps of 5–27% in average IoU depending on the dataset, confirming that the hierarchical NP formulation with probabilistic prototype modulation provides a meaningful architectural advance over prior deterministic click-based approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper is the first to formulate interactive 3D segmentation within the Neural Processes framework, extending NPs (previously applied to continual learning and semi-supervised learning) to the click-based 3D point cloud setting. It departs from deterministic interactive segmentation methods (InterObject3D, AGILE3D) by introducing a hierarchical latent variable structure (scene-level z_s and object-level z_o) and a probabilistic prototype modulator that stochastically adjusts click prototypes via Monte Carlo sampling. The point encoder backbone (Minkowski Res16UNet34C) and attention-based click-scene interaction follow AGILE3D, so the architectural novelty is concentrated in the probabilistic inference layers rather than the feature extraction pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By providing the first probabilistic interactive 3D segmentation framework, this work opens a path toward uncertainty-aware user interaction: the generated uncertainty maps can guide users to click on the most ambiguous regions, reducing annotation time (demonstrated in the user study). The strong out-of-domain generalization (e.g., +11.3% IoU@5 over AGILE3D on KITTI-360 single-object) suggests that explicit uncertainty modeling is particularly valuable under domain shift, a common practical scenario. However, in-domain gains remain modest (~0.3–0.5% IoU), and the authors acknowledge that out-of-domain performance still lags behind in-domain, leaving open questions about whether domain adaptation or larger training sets could close this gap. The probabilistic formulation also raises the question of whether the hierarchical NP structure can be extended to open-vocabulary or language-guided 3D segmentation.
