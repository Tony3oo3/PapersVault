## Problem

Graph Transformers require structural encodings (SEs) to inject graph inductive biases into global attention, but existing RW-based SEs like RRWP oscillate indefinitely on non-aperiodic substructures (e.g., benzene rings) and produce overly sparse representations on DAGs where walks terminate early. The Green and Martin kernels from classical random walk theory capture long-term topological behavior but are only well-defined for transient graphs, making them inapplicable to the recurrent finite graphs that dominate practical datasets. This paper redefines both kernels for recurrent graphs and introduces them as new SEs for Graph Transformers.

## Input / Output

The method consumes graphs with node features, edge features, and adjacency structure (both undirected and directed, including DAGs), and produces K-dimensional SE vectors per node pair via recursive matrix operations with complexity O(K|V||E|). The SEs are used as relative encodings in the attention mechanism and their diagonal components as absolute encodings concatenated with node features. Outputs span graph-level regression (molecular logP, HOMO-LUMO gap, circuit gain/bandwidth/phase margin), graph-level multi-label classification (peptide functional classes), and inductive node-level classification (pattern/cluster IDs).

## Method

The core contribution is a finite-step reformulation of the Green and Martin kernels for recurrent graphs. The finite-step Green kernel G^(k) is computed via the simple recurrence G^(0)=I, G^(k+1)=I+P·G^(k), representing the expected number of visits to a node within k steps. The finite-step Martin kernel M^(k) is derived as G^(k)(x,y)/G^(k)(y,y) for x≠y, with a first-return-time modification (convolution with P) for the diagonal to avoid trivial constant-1 values. These K-step kernel values are stacked into GKSE and MKSE vectors, integrated into the GRIT architecture as relative SEs in self-attention. Theoretically, GKSE is shown to be mutually constructible with RRWP (equivalent representational power), while MKSE is not constructible over RRWP, and GD-WL with either kernel is strictly stronger than GD-WL with shortest path distance.

## Results

On the Benchmarking GNNs suite, GRIT+MKSE achieves the best MAE of 0.056 on ZINC and best accuracy of 77.37% on CIFAR-10, while GRIT+GKSE achieves the best 98.31% accuracy on MNIST. On the large-scale PCQM4Mv2 dataset (3.7M graphs), GRIT+GKSE achieves an MAE of 0.0837, outperforming GRIT+RRWP (0.0859) and GPS-medium (0.0858) with fewer parameters (11.8M vs. 16.6M and 19.4M respectively). On the circuit domain (Ckt-Bench101), GRIT+GKSE achieves the best MAE of 0.0395, surpassing GRIT+RRWP (0.0418) and GPS+LapPE (0.0440). Overall, the proposed SEs achieve SOTA or near-SOTA on 7 out of 8 evaluated benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The most direct comparison is against GRIT+RRWP [Ma et al., 2023a], the prior SOTA GT using random-walk-based SEs. GRIT+GKSE and GRIT+MKSE outperform GRIT+RRWP on ZINC (0.058/0.056 vs. 0.059 MAE), MNIST (98.31%/98.24% vs. 98.11%), CIFAR-10 (76.72%/77.37% vs. 76.47%), Peptides-struct (0.2452/0.2457 vs. 0.2460 MAE), PCQM4Mv2 (0.0837/0.0839 vs. 0.0859 MAE), and both circuit benchmarks (0.0395/0.0409 vs. 0.0418 on Ckt-Bench101; 0.0188/0.0192 vs. 0.0190 on Ckt-Bench301), with Peptides-func being the one case where they are comparable (0.6976/0.6784 vs. 0.6988 AP). Against other GT baselines, GRIT+GKSE/MKSE surpass GPS (0.070 MAE on ZINC, 0.0858 on PCQM4Mv2), EGT (0.108 on ZINC, 0.0869 on PCQM4Mv2), Graphormer (0.0864 on PCQM4Mv2), and TokenGT (0.0910 on PCQM4Mv2). Against MPNN baselines (GCN, GAT, GIN, GraphSAGE, GatedGCN, PNA, CRaW1), the proposed methods achieve substantially lower MAE or higher accuracy across all datasets, with gaps of 0.1–0.5 MAE on ZINC and 5–20% accuracy on CIFAR-10. On the circuit domain, GRIT+GKSE also outperforms GRIT+DAGPE [Luo et al., 2024] (0.0395 vs. 0.0444 MAE on Ckt-Bench101), demonstrating advantage over DAG-specific positional encodings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the Green and Martin kernels—classical objects from potential theory and random walk theory (Barlow 2017; Lyons & Peres 2017; Woess 2000)—as structural encodings for Graph Transformers, a use case none of these sources originally targeted. It builds directly on GRIT (Ma et al., 2023a), whose RRWP encoding is the primary baseline, and on the GraphGPS framework for experimental infrastructure. The key conceptual departure is adapting kernels that are only well-defined for transient graphs to the recurrent graphs that dominate practical datasets, via a finite-step restriction and a first-return-time modification, yielding GKSE and MKSE as drop-in replacements for RRWP.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The constructibility theorem showing MKSE is not constructible over RRWP (Theorem 4.3) and the expressiveness result that GD-WL with GKSE/MKSE strictly dominates GD-WL with shortest-path distance (Theorem 4.5) provide clean theoretical justification beyond empirical gains. However, the claimed advantages on non-aperiodic substructures (benzene rings) and DAGs are supported only by indirect subset experiments rather than controlled ablations, leaving the mechanistic explanation somewhat speculative. Open questions include whether a hybrid encoding (GKSE for cyclic subgraphs, RRWP for acyclic ones) would further improve molecular benchmarks, and whether these SEs transfer to architectures beyond GRIT (the CKGCN experiments in Appendix B.2 are a first step).
