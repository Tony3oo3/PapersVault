## Problem

Medical multi-modal fusion must handle varying data quality across modalities and patients (modality batch effect), yet existing dynamic fusion methods either implicitly determine modality contributions or require explicit spatial/semantic alignment between modalities that is unavailable in heterogeneous medical data (e.g., linking pathology image regions to specific genes). This paper asks how to explicitly quantify and rebalance modality contributions without assuming inter-modal alignment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251949/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

All input modalities (WSIs, pathology text, RNA-seq, lab values, clinical notes, billing codes, MRI volumes, genomic variants, biofluid biomarkers, clinical assessments) are encoded via modality-specific encoders (Q-Former or 3D CNN + projection) into a fixed number N of D-dimensional tokens, then concatenated along the batch dimension rather than the sequence dimension so the transformer processes them in parallel. The output is a single prediction vector produced by average-pooling each modality's token sequence, concatenating the M pooled vectors into R^{DM}, and passing through a linear task head (survival c-index, binary mortality, or multi-class diagnosis).

## Method

AMC is a top-down dynamic token-replacement fusion applied between consecutive transformer layers. In the 'Top' step, modality importance s ∈ [0,1]^M is computed as the normalized mean of per-token maximum attention values from the differential attention map, yielding K_m = floor(s[m]·N) uninformative tokens to prune per modality. In the 'Down' step, the bottom-K_m tokens (ranked by the same attention-derived token score η[m,n] = max_k A[m,n,k]) are replaced by the corresponding-position token from whichever other modality has the highest token score at that position, narrowing the search space via a two-level contrastive loss (instance-level L_I and token-level L_T) that aligns same-index tokens across modalities without requiring explicit mapping. The backbone uses a differential attention module (two softmax attention maps subtracted with a learnable scalar λ) to prevent attention dilution, and a sparse mixture-of-experts layer replaces the standard MLP to mitigate gradient conflicts between modalities processed in a single shared transformer branch.

## Results

On the TCGA benchmark (5 cancer types, 3 modalities: WSI, pathology text, RNA-seq), AMC achieves the best average C-index of 0.734, outperforming the next-best LiMOE (0.711) and MulT (0.681), with the largest gain on UCEC (0.863 vs. 0.795 for LiMOE). On ADNI (4-modal Alzheimer's diagnosis), AMC reaches 55.91% accuracy and 54.82% F1, surpassing MUSE (51.51% Acc, 52.59% F1) and FlexMOE (52.27% Acc, 47.47% F1). On MIMIC-IV (3-modal mortality prediction), AMC achieves the best Recall (61.65%), Precision (59.46%), and F1 (59.75%), though FuseMOE retains a higher overall accuracy (77.31% vs. 68.51%). On the non-medical ENRICO dataset, AMC reaches 68.9% 20-class accuracy, exceeding the best multi-modal baseline HighMMT (52.6%) by 16.3 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AMC outperforms all 8 baselines on average C-index across TCGA (0.734 vs. 0.711 for LiMOE, the closest competitor), winning on 3 of 5 cancer types (UCEC, LUAD, BLCA) and placing second on BRCA (0.727 vs. LiMOE's 0.736) and third on LGG (0.795 vs. MulT's 0.828). On ADNI, AMC leads in Accuracy (55.91%), Recall (55.03%), and F1 (54.82%) over MUSE (51.51%, 51.71%, 52.59%) and FlexMOE (52.27%, 48.97%, 47.47%), though MUSE retains a Precision advantage (58.72% vs. 55.38%). On MIMIC-IV, AMC achieves the best Recall (61.65%), Precision (59.46%), and F1 (59.75%), but FuseMOE and MAGGate report higher overall accuracy (77.31% and 77.42% vs. 68.51%), which the authors attribute to overfitting to the majority class. FlexMOE, FuseMOE, MAGGate, and TensorFusion are consistently outperformed across all three medical datasets. On the non-medical ENRICO benchmark, AMC (68.9%) surpasses HighMMT (52.6%), GradBlend (51.0%), and Late Fusion (50.8%) by a wide margin, confirming generalizability beyond medical data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the token fusion paradigm (Wang et al., 2022b) from vision-centric, alignment-aware settings to heterogeneous medical modalities where explicit cross-modality mappings (e.g., between pathology images and gene expression) do not exist. It departs from prior dynamic fusion methods (FlexMoE, FuseMoE, LiMoE, MAGGate) that implicitly learn modality contributions via gating or cross-attention, instead proposing an explicit top-down mechanism: first quantifying modality importance from attention distributions, then performing token replacement proportional to that importance. The differential attention module (Ye et al., 2024) is repurposed not merely as a better attention mechanism but as the signal source for both modality importance and token scoring, eliminating the need for a separate scoring network. Two-level contrastive learning is introduced to align same-index tokens across modalities, replacing the explicit mapping function h(·) required by original token fusion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically critical gap in medical AI: modality quality varies across patients, cancer types, and collection conditions (the 'modality batch effect'), yet most fusion methods assume uniform modality reliability. The interpretability of AMC—explicit modality importance scores and visualizable token replacement patterns—directly supports clinical trust and regulatory requirements. Limitations include reliance on attention map quality (mitigated but not eliminated by differential attention), the assumption that same-index tokens across modalities can be meaningfully aligned via contrastive learning, and evaluation on relatively small medical cohorts. Open questions include scalability to 5+ modalities, robustness under severe missing-modality scenarios beyond the simple zero-score extension tested, and whether the top-down importance calculation generalizes to streaming or longitudinal settings where modality quality drifts over time.
