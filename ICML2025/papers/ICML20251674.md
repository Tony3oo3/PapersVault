## Problem

Existing conformal prediction methods for multi-class classification rely on a single conformity score function, which limits the efficiency and informativeness of the resulting prediction sets. While multiple well-studied score functions exist (e.g., THR, APS, RANK), no prior approach optimally combines them through weighted averaging while preserving finite-sample coverage guarantees. This paper addresses the gap by formulating score combination as a model-averaging problem within the conformal prediction framework.

## Input / Output

The method requires a pretrained classifier (trained on data independent of the calibration set) that outputs per-class probability estimates p̂_y(x) for a test sample x, along with a significance level α. From these probabilities, multiple score functions (THR, APS, RANK) are computed and linearly combined. The output is a set-valued prediction Ĉ(x) ⊆ [K] of class labels with guaranteed marginal coverage of at least 1−α, plus the optimal weight vector ŵ over the score functions. The procedure additionally requires labeled validation data split into subsets for weight selection and threshold calibration.

## Method

The core mechanism is a two-stage weighted averaging procedure: first, a threshold q is calibrated on subset I₁ for each candidate weight vector w on a discretized probability simplex (grid resolution ε=0.01); second, the weight ŵ is selected by minimizing the empirical prediction set size on subset I₂ using that threshold. A final recalibration on subset I₃ yields the deployment threshold for test predictions. The paper introduces four data splitting strategies—VFCP, EFCP, DLCP, and DLCP+—that trade off coverage guarantee strength against efficiency by controlling how much data is available for weight optimization. Theoretical analysis via VC theory establishes that the weighted score class has VC-dimension d+1 (where d is the number of score functions), yielding finite-sample coverage guarantees and oracle inequalities bounding the prediction set size gap relative to the population-optimal weight.

## Results

On CIFAR-100 at α=0.01, the proposed weighted score averaging method (DLCP) achieves a prediction set size of 13.30 while maintaining 98.9% coverage, compared to 25.10 for the best individual score (SAPS) and 40.22 for APS — a 47% reduction over the best single-score baseline. At α=0.05, the weighted method achieves size 3.75 versus 4.52 for THR and 19.55 for APS. Across all four data splitting strategies (VFCP, EFCP, DLCP, DLCP+), the weighted method consistently produces the smallest prediction sets while preserving the target 1−α coverage on both CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed weighted score averaging method outperforms all five individual score function baselines on prediction set size while maintaining equivalent coverage. At α=0.01 on CIFAR-100, DLCP achieves size 13.30, beating SAPS (25.10) by 47%, RAPS (26.40) by 50%, THR (26.95) by 51%, RANK (27.16) by 51%, and APS (40.22) by 67%. At α=0.05, the advantage narrows but persists: DLCP (3.75) beats THR (4.52) by 17%, SAPS (5.04) by 26%, RAPS (5.58) by 33%, RANK (6.30) by 40%, and APS (19.55) by 81%. On ImageNet-Val (α=0.01), the weighted method achieves size 48.26 versus 77.24 for Synergy Conformal Prediction (SCP) and 110.40 for ResNet18-based conformal prediction, a 37% and 56% reduction respectively. On MNIST, the method achieves size 1.58 versus 1.77 for SCP at α=0.01. All methods maintain coverage within 0.1–0.2% of the target 1−α level, confirming that the size improvements come without sacrificing validity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the conformal prediction framework of Vovk et al. (2005) and, more directly, the score selection and aggregation approach of Yang & Kuchibhotla (2024), which introduced VFCP and EFCP data splitting strategies for choosing among multiple score functions. The key departure is replacing single-score selection with optimal weighted averaging of multiple score functions, drawing an explicit analogy to model averaging (Claeskens & Hjort, 2008). The theoretical analysis also departs from prior work by employing VC-theory subgraph class bounds rather than the finite-set or real-valued maximization techniques used in Yang & Kuchibhotla (2024) and Candès et al. (2023), yielding guarantees that depend only on the number of constituent scores d rather than the complexity of the function class.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method provides a practical, plug-in way to combine existing conformal score functions (THR, APS, RANK, RAPS, SAPS) or scores from different pretrained models, yielding prediction sets roughly 2× smaller than the best single-score baseline on CIFAR-100 at α=0.01 while maintaining valid coverage. The DLCP/DLCP+ variants, which leverage test-set features for weight selection, are an interesting but somewhat risky extension that trades a weaker coverage guarantee for improved efficiency. Open questions include extending the theory to regression (where the K-class union bound breaks down), replacing grid search with gradient-based optimization for scalability, and understanding when the weighted combination offers no benefit over the already-dominant single score (as observed on CIFAR-10).
