## Problem

Deep tabular models that excel on i.i.d. data degrade significantly under temporal distribution shifts, yet the root causes of this failure remain unclear. The paper identifies two key gaps: (1) the standard temporal splitting protocol used for model selection is actually suboptimal compared to random splits, and (2) deep tabular representations systematically lose the periodic and trend information present in raw temporal data, limiting the model's ability to generalize to future time periods.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250236/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard tabular features (numerical and categorical) along with timestamps, which are typically discarded as noise in prior work. The temporal embedding converts each timestamp into a fixed-dimensional vector of Fourier-based periodic features (at yearly, monthly, weekly, and daily scales) plus an optional z-scored linear trend term, which is then fed as additional numerical inputs to the model backbone. Output is a class label (AUC-evaluated) or continuous value (RMSE-evaluated), depending on the task.

## Method

The paper contributes two complementary components. First, an improved temporal splitting protocol that minimizes training lag (allocating data closest to test time to training rather than validation) and validation bias (aligning the distributional shift between train-val and train-test gaps), achieving performance comparable to random splits (2.18% vs. 2.17% average improvement) while reducing standard deviation by ~9× (16.7% vs. 153.8%). Second, a plug-and-play temporal embedding ψ(t) = [ReLU(Linear(Periodic(t))), Trend(t)] that fits multi-scale periodicity via K-term Fourier series at pre-defined periods (yearly, monthly, weekly, daily) and optionally captures linear trend via a standardized timestamp. The Fourier components are projected through a learnable linear layer followed by ReLU to approximate aggregated Fourier coefficients, enabling the model to learn phase-specific knowledge and adapt its mapping f_t at different temporal stages after deployment.

## Results

On the TabReD benchmark (8 datasets), the proposed temporal split achieves a +2.18% average performance improvement over the original temporal split, matching the random split's +2.17% while reducing the standard deviation increase from +154% (random) to +16.7% (proposed), indicating substantially better stability. Adding the Fourier-based temporal embedding on top of the improved protocol yields an additional +0.54% average gain (Table 2), with MLP benefiting most (+1.31%) and retrieval-based ModernNCA gaining +0.30%. Across all 13 evaluated methods, the proposed protocol consistently improves performance, with tree-based methods (CatBoost +2.37%, LightGBM +2.14%) and retrieval-based methods (ModernNCA +2.49%, TabR +2.20%) showing the largest absolute gains.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed temporal split outperforms the original temporal split (Rubachev et al., 2025) for all 13 methods, with the largest gains for MLP (+3.50%), DCNv2 (+3.01%), and CatBoost (+2.37%). Compared to the random split, the proposed method achieves a marginally higher average improvement (+2.18% vs. +2.17%) but with dramatically lower variance (+16.7% vs. +154% std. increase), making it the more reliable choice in practice. Tree-based methods (XGBoost, CatBoost, LightGBM) and retrieval-based methods (ModernNCA, TabR) benefit most from the improved protocol, while MLP-PLR shows minimal gain (+0.75%) due to incompatibility between its PLR numerical embedding and the temporal features. The Fourier-based temporal embedding further improves MLP by +1.31% and ModernNCA by +0.30% over the protocol-only baseline, but provides negligible gains for MLP-PLR (+0.01%) and TabM (+0.07%), confirming that models with built-in PLR embeddings require direct backbone integration rather than treating temporal features as numerical inputs. Under the robustness score (mean − k·std), the proposed split's advantage over random split grows from 0.01% (k=0) to 0.22% (k=2), confirming superior stability under penalty.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the TabReD benchmark (Rubachev et al., 2025) for evaluating tabular models under temporal distribution shifts, and on the Fourier features literature (Tancik et al., 2020; Li et al., 2021) for its proposed temporal embedding. It departs from the standard practice in time series and forecasting of using temporal splits for train/validation partitioning, showing that this convention is actually harmful for tabular data due to training lag and validation bias. The temporal embedding repurposes the well-known Fourier series expansion as a lightweight, plug-and-play mechanism for injecting periodic and trend information into arbitrary tabular model backbones, positioning itself as complementary to (rather than replacing) existing numerical feature embeddings like PLR.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that random splits outperform temporal splits in tabular prediction under temporal shifts is practically important: it changes how practitioners should configure training pipelines for real-world tabular data with timestamps. The proposed temporal embedding offers a simple, architecture-agnostic way to recover the periodic and trend information that deep tabular models demonstrably lose in their representations, with the added benefit of enabling adaptive, phase-specific predictions after deployment. Key limitations include the dependence on uniform temporal sampling for the validation equivalence property, the need for manually specified periodicity priors (yearly, monthly, weekly, daily), and the modest absolute improvements (typically 0.5–1.3%) that may be dataset-dependent. Open questions include whether the approach extends to non-uniformly sampled data, whether learnable periodicity priors can replace the fixed ones without the observed instability, and whether the training protocol insights transfer to other shift types beyond temporal.
