## Problem

LLMs achieve high accuracy on reasoning benchmarks, but it remains unclear whether this reflects genuine reasoning or statistical recall of training data. Existing approaches to creating benchmark variations are ad hoc, require substantial manual effort, and lack a systematic hierarchy connecting different types of problem modifications to distinct reasoning capabilities. This paper addresses both gaps by proposing a principled 3-level reasoning hierarchy inspired by Pearl's ladder of causation and an automated pipeline to generate scalable problem variations at each level.

## Input / Output

The pipeline accepts existing benchmark problems in their native form—natural language math questions (GSM8K), causal queries (CLadder), Python functions with inputs (CRUXEval), or C code with assertions (Loop)—and produces mutated problem variations in natural language along with automatically computed ground-truth answers. For benchmarks already in code form, the NL-to-symbolic and symbolic-to-NL steps are skipped. The ground-truth for each mutation is obtained by executing the modified symbolic representation, eliminating the need for manual answer annotation. Evaluation metrics include task accuracy, probability of necessity (PN), probability of sufficiency (PS), and necessity/sufficiency inconsistency rates (N-IR, S-IR) for counterfactual variants.

## Method

RE-IMAGINE defines a 3-level reasoning hierarchy—Observe (original benchmark), Mutate (surface-level changes preserving reasoning path), and Imagine (new logic that contradicts or revises original statements, including counterfactuals)—and implements a 4-step synthesis pipeline: (1) NL-to-symbolic conversion to executable Python code, (2) mutation of the symbolic representation via operations on a computational graph (nodes = variables, edges = dependencies), (3) symbolic-to-NL translation using an LLM with back-translation verification, and (4) automatic ground-truth computation by executing the mutated code. Six mutation types are defined: SampleValues and UselessInfo at Level-2, and AddDependence, InsertConditional, CounterFactual, and Bi-CounterFactual at Level-3. The key design insight is that the intermediate executable representation guarantees correct answers are computable without manual effort, enabling arbitrary-scale generation across multiple benchmarks and domains with only reusable adapter customization per benchmark.

## Results

On GSM8K with 8-shot CoT, most models achieve ~95% accuracy on the original (Level-1) test set, but experience approximately a 10% accuracy drop on the Level-2 SampleValues mutation (only values changed, same reasoning path). Level-3 mutations (CounterFactual, AddDependence, InsertConditional) cause substantially larger performance declines, with GPT-o1 remaining the best-performing model across all levels. On CLadder, Level-3 CounterFactual mutations cause an approximately 20% accuracy drop relative to the original test set. On CRUXEval and Loop, both Level-2 and Level-3 mutations produce consistent performance degradation, with the X-Original mutation on Loop significantly degrading success rates for all models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not propose a new model to compare against baselines; instead, it evaluates existing LLM families (GPT, Llama, Phi) across a hierarchy of mutated benchmarks. GPT-o1 consistently outperforms all other models across every benchmark and mutation level, showing the greatest robustness to problem variations. Among open-weight models, Llama-3.3-70B and GPT-4o outperform Phi-3-small and Phi-3-mini on causal reasoning (N-IR/S-IR) metrics, while smaller Llama-8B models and GPT-3.5/GPT-4 show the weakest performance on counterfactual tasks. A key finding is that even the strongest models (GPT-o1) show meaningful degradation on Level-3 mutations, and composing multiple mutations (e.g., SampleValues + CounterFactual) widens the performance gap further, suggesting that no current model fully achieves the reasoning capabilities implied by the top of the hierarchy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies and extends the scattered prior work on benchmark mutations (GSM-Symbolic, Srivastava et al. 2024, Wu et al. 2023) and counterfactual reasoning evaluation (González & Nori 2024, Hüyük et al. 2024) under a single three-level hierarchy inspired by Pearl's ladder of causation. While prior approaches to creating problem variations relied on manually crafted functional templates or hand-written counterfactuals limited to specific benchmarks, RE-IMAGINE introduces an automated NL→code→mutate→code→NL pipeline that scales across math, code, and logic domains. The key conceptual departure is elevating Level-3 mutations (counterfactuals, conditionals, added dependencies that alter the reasoning path) from an afterthought to a first-class, systematically generated evaluation tier, whereas prior work had largely focused on Level-2 surface-level changes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The consistent finding that all tested LLMs (including o1) show significant performance degradation on Level-3 mutations—especially when mutations are composed—provides strong evidence that current reasoning benchmarks overestimate genuine reasoning ability due to memorization and data leakage. The framework's practical value lies in its scalability: arbitrary numbers of unseen variations can be generated automatically, removing the manual bottleneck that limited prior studies to ~100 templates. Open questions include whether the three-level hierarchy captures all relevant reasoning dimensions, whether the LLM-dependent NL translation step introduces systematic biases, and whether models can be trained to close the Level-3 gap. The framework is currently demonstrated on only four benchmarks, and its generalizability to domains without clean symbolic representations (e.g., open-ended reasoning, multi-modal tasks) remains untested.
