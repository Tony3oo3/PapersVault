## Problem

In causal inference, covariates can substantially tighten partial identification (PI) intervals for treatment effects, but the natural formulation—Conditional Optimal Transport (COT)—is intractable: conditional marginals are hard to estimate from finite samples, plug-in estimators may fail to converge, and solving COT requires a separate OT problem per covariate value. This paper seeks a model-lean relaxation of COT that retains the covariate benefit while reducing the problem to standard, well-understood optimal transport.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a finite sample of observed outcomes, covariates, and binary treatment assignments, together with a user-specified cost function h and a penalty parameter η ≥ 0. It outputs a PI lower bound V_ip(η) (and by symmetry an upper bound) that is guaranteed to lie between the unconditional OT bound (η=0) and the COT bound (η→∞). Computationally, the estimator reduces to solving a single n×m linear program (or Sinkhorn approximation) on a cost matrix whose entries combine h with the squared Euclidean distance between paired covariates, making it directly compatible with off-the-shelf OT solvers like POT.

## Method

The key mechanism is introducing 'mirror' covariates Z(0) and Z(1) as counterfactual counterparts of the observed covariate Z, then formulating the problem as a standard unconditional OT over joint distributions π on (Y(0),Z(0),Y(1),Z(1)) that only constrain the marginals (Y(0),Z(0)) and (Y(1),Z(1)), with an augmented cost h(Y(0),Y(1)) + η‖Z(0)−Z(1)‖². This avoids the infeasible conditioning constraints of COT while the penalty term softly enforces covariate invariance. The paper proves that V_ip(η) is monotonically non-decreasing in η, equals V_u at η=0, and converges to V_c as η→∞ (Proposition 3.6), establishing a valid interpolation. For finite samples, a plug-in estimator is shown to be consistent (Proposition 4.1) and, under quadratic costs with a curvature-bounded Brenier potential, achieves a convergence rate of O(λη·N^{−1/4}) for d≤3 (Theorem 4.3), extending the analysis to α-mixing non-i.i.d. data (Theorem 4.7).

## Results

On the STAR real dataset, the proposed method with η=50 achieves a PI interval length of 1.479 for the correlation index, compared to 1.994 for unconditional OT (η=0), 1.712 for DualBounds with ridge, and 2.223 for DualBounds with knn — a 26% reduction over OT. The upper bound on the correlation is 0.887 (η=50), strictly below 1 and indicating treatment effect heterogeneity, whereas all baselines yield upper bounds ≥ 0.997. For the Neymanian confidence interval, η=50 reduces the required sample size by ~5% (relative sample size 0.951) compared to OT, while DualBounds methods require 20–30% more samples (relative sizes 1.207 and 1.302). On synthetic data, the method achieves lower L₁ error in estimating V_c than both DualBounds variants, particularly for the quadratic location and scale models where DualBounds with knn can even produce negative estimates for a positive V_c.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two baselines: (1) DualBounds (Ji et al., 2023), a COT-based method that estimates conditional marginals via ridge or kNN nuisance estimators, and (2) unconditional OT (Gao et al., 2025), which ignores covariates entirely (equivalent to η=0 in the proposed framework). On the STAR dataset, the proposed method with η=50 dominates both baselines on all metrics: it achieves the narrowest PI interval (1.479 vs. 1.712 for DualBounds-ridge and 2.223 for DualBounds-knn), the lowest upper bound on correlation (0.887 vs. 1.005 and 1.187), and the smallest Neymanian variance upper bound (2.628×10⁻³ vs. 3.334×10⁻³ and 3.596×10⁻³). On synthetic data, the method outperforms DualBounds-knn in L₁ error across all three models and matches or beats DualBounds-ridge (which leverages a priori linear model knowledge) without requiring such assumptions. Notably, DualBounds-knn produces negative estimates for V_c in the scale model where V_c > 0, indicating significant bias, while the proposed method remains stable and accurate.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the OT-based partial identification framework (Chernozhukov et al., 2017; Gao et al., 2025) by introducing a 'mirror covariate' relaxation that reduces the intractable Conditional Optimal Transport (COT) problem to a standard unconditional OT problem. The relaxation is rooted in Carlier et al. (2010)'s block triangular map construction and the proof strategy of Manole et al. (2024) for convergence rates of smooth OT maps. It departs from the COT-based approach of Ji et al. (2023) (DualBounds), which requires estimating conditional marginal distributions and solving OT at each covariate value, by instead penalizing the distance between duplicated 'mirror' covariates in a single OT problem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The interpolation guarantee (V_u ≤ V_ip(η) ≤ V_c) ensures the method always produces valid, covariate-informed PI bounds without requiring conditional distribution estimation, making it robust to model misspecification. However, practical gains are modest: on the STAR dataset, the method reduces required sample size by only ~5% compared to unconditional OT, and the convergence rate depends on a curvature constant λ that grows with η, limiting the benefit of large penalty values. Open questions include covariate selection to balance population-level PI tightness against finite-sample estimation error, model-assisted refinements, and extending the framework to Causal OT with temporal constraints.
