## Problem

Enforcing monotonicity in neural networks traditionally relies on either architectural constraints (positive weights, min-max structures) or regularization penalties, both of which either restrict model expressiveness or fail to guarantee strict monotonicity. This paper identifies that the monotonic probability problem can be reframed as modeling a latent cost variable, a perspective that eliminates the need to design a strictly monotonic function altogether.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252332/cropped_page3_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a non-monotonic feature vector x ∈ R^n and a multivariate revenue variable r ∈ R^m (where monotonicity is defined via partial order in vector space) and produces a monotonic conditional probability p(y|x,r). The revenue variable r can be multi-dimensional, and the output y can be binary (classification) or continuous (regression, handled via a reduction to binary classification through thresholding). A key practical detail is that during inference, the model computes p(y|x,r) for many r values sharing the same x efficiently, since the latent cost c depends only on x.

## Method

The core insight (Lemma 4.2) proves that a monotonic binary classifier Pr(y=1|x,r) is equivalent to the existence of a latent cost variable c satisfying c ⊥ r | x and y | {x,r} =d I(c ≺ r) | {x,r}, shifting the problem from learning a monotonic function G to modeling the distribution of c. The Generative Cost Model (GCM) implements this via a two-latent-variable generative process: z ~ p(z), w ~ p(w), x ~ p(x|z), c ~ p(c|z), r ~ p(r|w,x), y = I(c ≺ r), where the separate latent w for r ensures the critical conditional independence c ⊥ r | x. Training uses variational inference with an IWAE-style ELBO and the reparameterization trick, and the likelihood Pr(c ≺ r | z) is computed in closed form under element-wise Gaussian assumptions. For implicit (correlational) monotonicity, the IGCM variant introduces a kernel variable k such that both r and y are strictly monotonic in k but not necessarily in each other, capturing real-world scenarios where monotonic features share a common latent driver rather than a direct causal link.

## Results

On the quantile regression simulation, GCM achieves the lowest MAE across all quantiles (r=0.1 to 0.9), ranging from 0.0742 to 0.0961, compared to the best baseline CMNN (0.0799–0.1685). On six public datasets, IGCM achieves the best or near-best performance in 5 of 6 tasks: Adult AUC 0.7891, Diabetes AUC 0.8285, Blog Feedback RMSE 0.1569, Loan Defaulter ACC 0.6528, and Auto MPG RMSE 3.1000. GCM is competitive but slightly behind IGCM on most datasets, while on COMPAS (ACC) the traditional Min-Max network (0.6976) edges out both GCM (0.6945) and IGCM (0.6934).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GCM and IGCM outperform all six baselines on the quantile regression simulation, with GCM achieving 20–40% lower MAE than the best construction-based method (CMNN) at extreme quantiles. On public datasets, IGCM wins on 5 of 6 tasks, beating the strongest construction baseline SMM by up to 0.0066 AUC (Diabetes) and 0.25 RMSE (Auto MPG). The Min-Max network (Sill, 1997) remains the strongest single baseline on COMPAS (ACC 0.6976 vs. IGCM 0.6934), but is outperformed by IGCM on all other datasets. Regularization-based methods (Hint, PWL) consistently underperform construction-based methods and the proposed models, with Hint lagging by up to 0.038 AUC on Adult. Among construction methods, SMM and CMNN are the closest competitors to GCM, but GCM still leads on Adult (0.7844 vs. 0.7819) and Auto MPG (3.2240 vs. 3.3534 RMSE). PosNN (positive-weight-only) is the weakest baseline overall, confirming that simple weight positivity is insufficient for complex monotonic relationships.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the two dominant paradigms for monotonic modeling—architectural construction (Min-Max networks, positive-weight matrices, monotonic activations) and regularization (monotonicity hints, pointwise penalties)—by reframing the problem entirely as a generative modeling task. It builds on the VAE framework (Kingma, 2013) and the IWAE loss formulation (Burda et al., 2016), but applies them to a novel probabilistic structure where a latent cost variable c, conditionally independent of the revenue variable r given x, encodes the monotonic relationship. The theoretical core (Lemma 4.2) establishes that any monotonic conditional probability is equivalent to the probability that a latent cost is dominated by the revenue, which is a clean reformulation absent from prior monotonic-network literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The generative reframing eliminates the need to hand-design monotonic network architectures, offering a more flexible and theoretically grounded alternative that also yields a practical inference speedup (up to 72% faster than the best baseline when predicting across many revenue values simultaneously). However, the method is currently validated only on tabular data with relatively low-dimensional revenue variables (3–8 dimensions), and the IGCM variant sacrifices strict monotonicity guarantees for better empirical fit. Open questions include scalability to high-dimensional or continuous revenue spaces, handling of non-Gaussian cost distributions, and whether the approach extends naturally to multi-output or ordinal settings beyond binary classification.
