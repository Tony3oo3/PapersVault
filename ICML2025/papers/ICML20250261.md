## Problem

Autoregressive LMs inherently lack the ability to scale test-time compute for their most critical decisions, a property that diffusion models naturally provide through iterative denoising. However, diffusion LMs trained from scratch lag significantly behind their autoregressive counterparts, making it unclear whether diffusion's scaling benefits can be harnessed without sacrificing the inductive biases and pre-trained knowledge that make modern LMs effective.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250261/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard text context (prompt) and optionally a discrete task category label (math, coding, general knowledge) for classifier-free guidance. It produces generated text tokens, but the generation process operates in a 256-dimensional embedding space where the model performs T ODE integration steps (default 15) before sampling each token, with the number of steps directly controlling the compute-accuracy tradeoff. The diffusion path outputs categorical logits over the vocabulary at each step, and the final token is sampled from the model's distribution at t=1.

## Method

L2D casts a pre-trained LM as a single-step diffusion model (t=0 is pure noise, t=1 is the target token embedding) and adds a parallel transformer 'diffusion path' initialized from the LM's own frozen weights, trained with LoRA. The diffusion path is trained with a cross-entropy loss over token embeddings using rectified flow schedules (α_t=t, β_t=1-t) and a large noise scale σ=64, establishing a direct connection to standard next-token prediction. At inference, the model performs multi-step ODE integration (midpoint, Euler, or adaptive Runge-Kutta) over the embedding space, sampling a token at each step to estimate the velocity direction, with the main LM path computed only once per generated token. The diffusion path merges back into the main path only at the final layer via a timestep-conditioned weighted sum that is zero at t=0, guaranteeing that single-step generation remains identical to the original LM. This design enables adaptive compute allocation per token, classifier-free guidance for task-specific expertise, and full compatibility with traditional finetuning and search-based scaling.

## Results

L2D achieves consistent improvements across four LMs (Llama 3.2 1B, Qwen 2.5 1.5B, Llama 3.1 8B, Qwen 2.5 7B Instruct), with average scores of 35.50, 51.01, 61.33, and 67.58 respectively, while optimizing only 3.5–6% of original parameters (73M–281M). Gains are most pronounced on math (e.g., GSM8K: 38.86 vs. 13.86 base for Llama 1B; 82.80 vs. 5.61 for Qwen 7B) and coding (HumanEval: 91.20 for Qwen 7B), with more modest but positive effects on general knowledge (MMLU, MMLU-Pro). The method also demonstrates monotonic test-time scaling: increasing diffusion steps from 15 to 127 yields further gains (e.g., 35.50 → 36.24 average for Llama 1B), and an adaptive ODE solver pushes performance to 36.34 with only 118.33 average steps.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LoRA finetuning (Hu et al., 2021), L2D wins on average score for all four models (e.g., 35.50 vs. 29.97 for Llama 1B; 67.58 vs. 63.34 for Qwen 7B), with the sole exception of MBPP on Qwen 7B where LoRA edges ahead (79.60 vs. 76.79). Against full weight finetuning, L2D is decisively superior: full finetuning actually degrades coding performance on instruct models (e.g., HumanEval drops from 83.65 to 68.54 on Llama 8B), while L2D preserves or improves it. Against the DeepSeek-R1 RL reasoning framework (Guo et al., 2025), L2D trained on the R1-distilled Qwen 1.5B model achieves the highest math scores (surpassing 7B/8B non-RL models) while recovering the coding and knowledge losses that the RL distillation introduced. Against chain-of-thought prompting (Wei et al., 2022), L2D alone outperforms tuned CoT on most tasks, and combining L2D with CoT yields compounding gains, confirming orthogonality between the two scaling axes.

## Relation to Prior Work

L2D builds directly on the continuous diffusion-for-language line (CDCD/Dieleman et al. 2022, Diffusion-LM/Li et al. 2022, PLAID/Gulrajani & Hashimoto 2024) and the rectified-flow ODE formulation (Liu et al. 2022), but departs fundamentally from training a diffusion LM from scratch. Instead, it reframes an existing autoregressive LM as a single-step diffusion (t=0) and adds a lightweight parallel diffusion path (initialized from the frozen LM's own weights, trained with LoRA) that extends generation to multi-step denoising. This positions L2D as a finetuning bridge between the two paradigms rather than a replacement, and it imports classifier-free guidance (Ho & Salimans 2022) and adaptive ODE solvers (Fehlberg 1969) from the vision diffusion literature as orthogonal extensions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

L2D introduces a genuinely orthogonal axis for test-time compute scaling—monotonic improvement with more diffusion steps—that composes with (rather than competes against) existing approaches like beam search, chain-of-thought prompting, and RL-based reasoning, as demonstrated by the compounding gains when combining L2D with half-budget search. Key limitations remain: the method loses direct logit manipulation and ground-truth confidence scores, introduces stochasticity into generation, and has only been validated on 1B–8B models, leaving open whether the benefits persist at frontier scale. The paper also highlights that full-weight finetuning of instruct models can *degrade* coding performance while L2D consistently improves, suggesting a qualitative difference in how the two approaches interact with pre-trained knowledge. Future directions include RL finetuning of the diffusion path, scaling to larger models, and finer-grained guidance labels for personalization.
