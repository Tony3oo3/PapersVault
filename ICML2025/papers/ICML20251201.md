## Problem

Residual Vector Quantization (RVQ) improves reconstruction fidelity by increasing quantization depth, but existing generative models on RVQ tokens—whether autoregressive (RQ-Transformer) or non-autoregressive (SoundStorm, CLaM-TTS)—still scale inference steps with the product of sequence length and depth. No prior method simultaneously decouples sampling complexity from both dimensions while preserving high-fidelity generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251201/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For image generation, the model takes a class label and produces a 256×256 image from an 8×8 spatial grid of RVQ tokens with depth 8 or 16 (code length 64). For zero-shot TTS, it takes a text transcript plus a 3-second reference speech segment and generates a speech waveform from RVQ tokens at depth 32 (MelVAE) or 72 (waveform autoencoder at 10.7 Hz temporal resolution). In both cases, generation proceeds via 25–64 iterative unmasking steps, independent of the RVQ depth.

## Method

ResGen predicts the cumulative vector embedding z (the sum of all masked token embeddings at each spatial position) rather than individual discrete tokens, using a mixture-of-Gaussians head with low-rank mean projections. This single forward pass per position decouples inference steps from both sequence length and RVQ depth, since the model outputs one continuous vector per position regardless of how many depth layers are masked. The masking strategy is depth-aware: tokens are masked from the highest (finest-detail) depth downward, and unmasked in reverse during sampling, yielding a coarse-to-fine generation process. The entire procedure is formalized as a discrete diffusion process with a closed-form forward process (sampling without replacement) and a variational lower bound that reduces to a simple masked-embedding prediction loss. Sampling uses confidence scores derived from per-depth log-likelihoods plus Gumbel noise to select which tokens to unmask at each step, with a DiT-XL transformer as the backbone.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256 class-conditional generation, ResGen-rvq16 (576M params) achieves an FID of 1.93 with classifier-free guidance (CFG), matching VAR-d30 (1.92, 2.0B params) with 3.5× fewer parameters and outperforming DiT-XL/2 (2.27) and RQ-Transformer (3.89). It also attains the largest maximum latent batch size (1,915) among all compared models, indicating superior memory efficiency. On zero-shot TTS (continuation task), Rvqvae-ResGen (625M) achieves WER 1.86, CER 0.50, and SIM-o 0.5853 in only 25 inference steps, outperforming VALL-E, CLaM-TTS, and Voicebox on intelligibility metrics while using fewer steps than Voicebox (64).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ResGen-rvq16 (FID 1.93) substantially outperforms RQ-Transformer (3.89, 1.4B) and DiT-XL/2 (2.27) on ImageNet FID with CFG, while matching VAR-d30 (1.92) at 3.5× fewer parameters. Against the MAR family, MAR-L (1.78) and MAR-H (1.55) achieve slightly lower FID, but ResGen is significantly faster in wall-clock time and supports a larger batch size (1,915 vs. 1,167 for MAR-L). Compared to recent masked models MAGVIT-v2 (1.78) and MaskBit (1.62), ResGen-rvq16 trails by 0.15–0.31 FID but uses a shorter code length (64 vs. 256) and explicitly models cross-depth RVQ dependencies. On TTS, Rvqvae-ResGen achieves the lowest WER (1.70) and CER (0.46) on the cross-sentence task, beating DiTTo-en-XL (2.56/0.89) and Voicebox (1.9/–), while on the continuation task it ranks second to DiTTo-en-XL (WER 1.86 vs. 1.78) but uses the same 25 inference steps. Overall, ResGen wins on efficiency (speed, memory, parameter count) and is competitive or superior on quality across both modalities.

## Relation to Prior Work

ResGen extends the masked token modeling paradigm (MaskGIT, VQ-Diffusion) to the multi-depth RVQ token space, where prior approaches either autoregress over depth (RQ-Transformer, VALL-E) or treat depth as an additional sequential dimension (SoundStorm). The key conceptual departure is predicting cumulative vector embeddings of masked tokens via a mixture-of-Gaussians head (inherited from CLaM-TTS and GIVT) rather than individual discrete tokens, which decouples inference steps from both sequence length and RVQ depth. The method is further grounded in a discrete diffusion + variational inference formulation, providing a principled probabilistic interpretation of the masking/unmasking process that prior heuristic masked-generation papers lack.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The depth-independence of inference steps is practically significant: practitioners can increase RVQ depth for higher reconstruction fidelity without paying a proportional sampling cost, a trade-off that has constrained RVQ-based generative models since RQ-Transformer. The cross-modal validation (ImageNet 256×256 and zero-shot TTS) strengthens the generality claim. Open questions include the lack of theoretical justification for why so few sampling steps suffice, the unexplored potential of KV caching for further speedups, and whether the approach extends naturally to alternative quantization schemes like FSQ. The paper also notes that its FID (1.93) trails the best masked models (MaskBit 1.62, MAGVIT-v2 1.78), suggesting the cumulative-embedding strategy has not yet fully closed the quality gap with bit-level approaches.
