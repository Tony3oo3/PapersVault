## Problem

Existing pre-quantization transformations for LLMs (per-channel scaling, Hadamard rotation) still produce steep, dispersed weight and activation distributions that cause large quantization errors, especially at pivot tokens and across layers. These methods either ignore per-layer characteristics (Hadamard) or trade activation flatness for weight steepness (per-channel scaling), and they introduce non-trivial runtime overhead that erodes the speedup from low-bit inference.

## Input / Output

The method consumes full-precision LLM weights and 128 calibration sentences (2048 tokens each, ~26 GB GPU memory, ~0.9 h for LLaMA-3-8B) to produce a W4A4-quantized model with per-layer Kronecker-decomposed affine matrices (e.g., 64×64 and 64×128 for hidden dim 8192), per-channel scaling vectors merged into adjacent layers, and per-layer sigmoid clipping thresholds. At inference, input tokens pass through the fused affine-transform-and-quantize kernel followed by INT4 GEMM, with KV cache quantized group-wise (group size 128) in FP16→INT4.

## Method

FLATQUANT learns a per-layer affine transformation P = P₁ ⊗ P₂ (Kronecker product of two small invertible matrices) that flattens both weights and activations before quantization, minimizing block-wise MSE over calibration data. The Kronecker decomposition reduces the transformation from O(n²) to O(n₁² + n₂²) parameters and FLOPs (e.g., only 2.61% extra FLOPs for LLaMA-2-7B), with optimal split n₁·n₂ = n chosen to minimize n₁ + n₂. Per-channel scaling is merged into preceding LayerNorm/linear layers at zero runtime cost, and learnable clipping thresholds are applied after the transformation (not before) to suppress residual outliers. A custom Triton kernel fuses the affine transform and quantization into a single memory-bound operation, eliminating intermediate global-memory writes and kernel-launch overhead, yielding up to 2.3× prefill and 1.7× decoding speedup over FP16.

## Results

FLATQUANT achieves less than 1% average accuracy drop (79.01% vs. 79.95% FP16) under W4A4 quantization on LLaMA-3-70B, surpassing SpinQuant by 7.5% and QuaRot by 43.7% on zero-shot QA tasks. On WikiText-2 perplexity, FLATQUANT (RTN) reaches 3.78 on LLaMA-3-70B (vs. 7.58 for SpinQuant and 55.44 for QuaRot) and 6.98 on LLaMA-3-8B (vs. 7.96 for SpinQuant). The method also delivers up to 2.3× prefill speedup and 1.7× decoding speedup over FP16 inference under W4A4 quantization. Notably, FLATQUANT with simple round-to-nearest (RTN) weight quantization matches or exceeds results requiring GPTQ, at significantly lower calibration cost (~0.9 hours for LLaMA-3-8B).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FLATQUANT decisively outperforms all compared W4A4 baselines. Against QuaRot (the strongest prior method using Hadamard rotations), FLATQUANT reduces WikiText-2 PPL from 55.44 to 3.78 on LLaMA-3-70B and from 10.60 to 6.98 on LLaMA-3-8B, with a 43.7-point QA accuracy gain on LLaMA-3-70B (79.01% vs. 35.36%). Against SpinQuant (learned rotations), FLATQUANT improves PPL by 3.8 points on LLaMA-3-70B (3.78 vs. 7.58) and 1.0 point on LLaMA-3-8B (6.98 vs. 7.96), with a 13.4-point QA accuracy advantage on LLaMA-3-70B. Earlier methods—SmoothQuant, OmniQuant, and AffineQuant—suffer severe degradation (PPL > 12 on LLaMA-2-7B) and are not competitive in the W4A4 regime. For weight-only quantization (Table 11), FLATQUANT-RTN (6.54 PPL) matches GPTQ-g128 (6.50) and slightly outperforms AWQ (7.10) and QuIP (6.50) on LLaMA-3-8B W4A16. A key practical advantage is that FLATQUANT with RTN achieves results comparable to its GPTQ variant (e.g., 6.98 vs. 6.90 PPL on LLaMA-3-8B), eliminating the need for expensive GPTQ calibration.

## Relation to Prior Work

FLATQUANT sits at the intersection of two lines of work on LLM quantization: per-channel scaling (SmoothQuant, OmniQuant) and orthogonal/Hadamard rotations (QuaRot, SpinQuant). It departs from both by learning a per-layer affine transformation rather than applying a fixed rotation or diagonal scaling, but addresses the computational infeasibility of full-size affine matrices (as in AffineQuant) through a Kronecker product decomposition into two small matrices. The learnable clipping thresholds applied *after* the transformation, and the kernel-fusion design, are additional departures from prior methods that either clip before transformation or treat the transformation as a separate runtime operation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Achieving <1% accuracy drop on LLaMA-3-70B with simple RTN W4A4 quantization is a practically significant milestone for deploying large models at 4-bit precision without expensive GPTQ calibration. The Kronecker product trick (only 2.61% FLOPs overhead, 3.41 MB extra memory) makes per-layer learnable transformations viable in production, and the fused Triton kernel delivers 2.3×/1.7× speedup over FP16. Open questions include whether the approach extends gracefully to 2–3-bit regimes (where it still degrades substantially), whether the calibration requirement (~1 hour per model) is acceptable in all deployment pipelines, and how the method interacts with emerging MoE architectures at scale beyond the 671B DeepSeek results.
