## Problem

Comparing graphs remains a fundamental challenge in graph data analysis, but existing methods such as graph edit distance, Gromov-Wasserstein distance, and graph kernels suffer from cubic or quartic complexity in the number of nodes and quadratic complexity in the number of graphs, making them impractical for large-scale graph clustering. The paper addresses this gap by proposing a distance measure that is both theoretically grounded as a pseudo-metric and admits an extremely simple closed-form computation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250607/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes self-looped (possibly weighted) adjacency matrices of undirected graphs as primary input, with an optional node attribute matrix that is incorporated by constructing auxiliary k-NN graphs and appending them to the original graph. For two graphs, the output is a single non-negative scalar distance (MMFD or MFD); for a set of N graphs, the clustering variants (MMFD-KM, MFD-KD) output cluster assignments. The low-rank variant operates on a rank-d randomized SVD of the adjacency matrix (d ≪ n), reducing the per-graph cost to O(n² log d + d²n) and enabling linear scaling in N for clustering.

## Method

The core insight is to interpret each adjacency matrix as a kernel matrix produced by an unknown (possibly indefinite) kernel evaluated on a discrete distribution of node embeddings, so that comparing the underlying distributions proxies for comparing the graphs. For indefinite adjacency matrices, PSD proxies are constructed by taking absolute eigenvalues in the spectral decomposition. MMFD compares the mean vectors of the induced feature maps while optimizing over an orthonormal alignment matrix to handle the rotational ambiguity of eigenvectors; this optimization collapses to a closed-form expression involving only the normalized sum of entries of the two PSD proxies. MFD generalizes MMFD by replacing the mean comparison with a Gaussian-kernel-based MMD over the full feature maps, solved via an alternating scheme that at each iteration performs a Procrustes step (SVD of a gradient-plus-regularization matrix). For large-scale clustering, MMFD-KM reduces the problem to 1-D K-means on the scalar MMFD values (linear in N), while MFD-KD alternates between assigning graphs to cluster distributions via the MFD optimization and updating cluster distributions by gradient descent.

## Results

On the four moderate-size graph clustering benchmarks, MFD achieves the best overall performance: 99.45% ACC / 93.82 NMI / 97.47 ARI on AIDS, 72.60% ACC on PROTEINS, 30.33% ACC on ENZYMES, and 35.35% ACC on REDDIT-MULTI-5K. The lighter-weight MMFD_LR variant is nearly as accurate (98.80% ACC on AIDS, 72.49% on PROTEINS, 36.54% on REDDIT-MULTI-5K) while being the fastest method tested (0.26 s to compute the full distance matrix on AIDS vs. 25,544 s for entropic GWD). The large-scale clustering variants MMFD_LR-KM and MFD-KD maintain competitive accuracy with linear complexity in the number of graphs N.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed MFD/MMFD methods outperform all 16 baselines on at least three of the four moderate-size datasets. Against the strongest end-to-end GNN clustering method DCGLC (Cai et al., 2024), MFD gains +2.68 ACC on AIDS (99.45 vs 96.77), +3.71 on PROTEINS (72.60 vs 68.89), +1.90 on ENZYMES (30.33 vs 28.43), and +2.11 on REDDIT-MULTI-5K (35.35 vs 33.24). Among UGRL baselines, GWF+KM (Xu et al., 2022) is the closest competitor on AIDS (96.43 ACC) and PROTEINS (66.87 ACC), but MFD still leads by 3.02 and 5.73 ACC respectively; GWF cannot scale to REDDIT-MULTI-5K. All graph kernels (SP, GK, RW, WL, LT, WL-OA) are substantially outperformed, with WL-OA kernel achieving only 80.40% ACC on AIDS versus 99.45% for MFD. MMD (Gretton et al., 2012) performs near-random on AIDS (50.10% ACC), confirming the paper's theoretical argument that MMD fails when feature spaces are not aligned. In terms of efficiency, MMFD_LR computes the full pairwise distance matrix on AIDS in 0.26 s, compared to 25,544 s for entropic GWD and 1.51 s for the shortest-path kernel, making it the fastest method by a wide margin.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by repurposing the distribution-comparison framework of MMD (Gretton et al., 2012) to the graph domain, with a critical departure: it treats adjacency matrices as kernel matrices from unknown indefinite kernels and addresses the non-uniqueness of matrix factorizations via an orthonormal Procrustes alignment (R12) that MMD lacks. The PSD proxy construction (absolute eigenvalue decomposition) draws on indefinite kernel theory (Ong et al., 2004), while the large-scale clustering variant (MMFD-KM) reduces the problem to 1-D K-means on a scalar per graph. Conceptually, the paper sits between the graph-kernel literature (WL, shortest-path, random-walk) and optimal-transport-based graph distances (GWD), but avoids their cubic-or-higher per-pair complexity by exploiting the closed-form structure of the aligned mean comparison.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The closed-form reduction of a seemingly complex alignment optimization to a simple scalar formula (Theorem 2.4) makes graph comparison nearly free at scale, and the linear-in-N clustering (MMFD-KM) removes the quadratic bottleneck that limits spectral clustering on large graph collections. However, the mean-only comparison (MMFD) is inherently a 1-D summary, and the paper's own MFD variant—exploiting full distributional information via a nonlinear kernel—consistently outperforms MMFD, suggesting the scalar reduction, while effective, discards structural detail. Open questions include whether the EMD-based MFD variant (Appendix A.3) can be made tractable, whether the approach extends naturally to directed or hypergraph structures, and whether the pseudo-metric degeneracy (e.g., all complete graphs have zero MMFD) limits discriminative power in practice.
