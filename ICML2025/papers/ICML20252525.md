## Problem

Risk-sensitive RL with static risk measures (e.g., CVaR, entropic risk, mean-variance) requires history-dependent policies for optimality, since no Bellman equations exist in the original MDP. Prior work addressed only specific risk measures (CVaR or entropic risk) in restricted MDP structures (tabular or low-rank), with no unified framework connecting the broad class of optimized certainty equivalent (OCE) risks to existing risk-neutral RL algorithms or to challenging rich-observation settings like exogenous block MDPs.

## Input / Output

The method takes a finite-horizon MDP (states, actions, reward distributions), a concave utility function u defining the OCE risk, and an initial state s_1. It outputs a policy implemented as a Markovian policy in the augmented MDP paired with an initial budget b_1, which together constitute a history-dependent policy in the original MDP. For the optimistic meta-algorithm, the budget is selected per-round by maximizing b + V̂(s_1, b); for the policy optimization meta-algorithm, the budget is drawn from a discrete set B (requiring a discrete-reward assumption) and optimized via the same criterion. Theoretical outputs are OCE regret or PAC bounds expressed in terms of the oracle's AugMDP regret scaled by V_u^max = max|u(c)|.

## Method

The core construction is an Augmented MDP (AugMDP) that augments each state with a scalar budget b_h tracking cumulative rewards via the deterministic transition b_{h+1} = b_h - r_h, with a terminal reward r_aug^H = E[u(r_H - b_H)] encoding the utility. Theorem 2.1 proves that a Markovian policy in this AugMDP with the right initial budget achieves the optimal OCE, reducing the history-dependent problem to standard risk-neutral RL. Two meta-algorithms exploit this reduction: (1) an optimistic meta-algorithm (Alg. 1) that queries any optimistic RL oracle (UCB-VI, Rep-UCB, GOLF) in the AugMDP, selects the initial budget by maximizing b + V̂(s_1, b), and inherits the oracle's regret bound scaled by V_u^max; (2) a policy optimization meta-algorithm (Alg. 2) that runs a PO oracle (NPG, PPO, REINFORCE) in the AugMDP with a uniform initial budget distribution, yielding both global convergence and a novel monotone-improvement guarantee on a risk lower bound. The key theoretical result (Thm. 3.2) shows the OCE regret is bounded by the oracle's AugMDP regret times V_u^max plus optimism slack, and the paper instantiates this to obtain the first risk-sensitive bounds for exogenous block MDPs (via GOLF) and the first finite-sample convergence guarantees for gradient-based RSRL (via NPG).

## Results

In the proof-of-concept MDP (Figure 1), the optimistic meta-algorithm (Alg.1 with UCB-VI) and the policy-optimization meta-algorithm (Alg.2 with NPG) both recover the optimal OCE value across six risk measures. For CVaR₀.₂₅, Alg.1 achieves 0.75±0.02 and Alg.2 achieves 0.71±0.08, both matching or approaching the optimal value of 0.75, while the best Markovian policy only reaches 0.5. For mean-variance (E[X]−Var(X)), Alg.1 achieves 1.07±0.01 versus 0.95 for the best Markovian policy. For entropic risk, where the optimal policy is provably Markovian, all methods converge to the same value (~1.25 for Entr-1.0, ~0.90 for Entr-2.0).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its two meta-algorithms against the best achievable Markovian policy (i.e., a policy that conditions only on the current state, not on history). For CVaR₀.₂₅, both Alg.1 (0.75) and Alg.2 (0.71) substantially outperform the best Markovian policy (0.5), confirming that history-dependent policies are necessary for OCE optimality. For mean-variance (E[X]−Var(X)), Alg.1 achieves 1.07 versus 0.95 for the best Markovian, a 13% improvement. For entropic risk, where the optimal policy is provably Markovian, all three approaches converge to the same value (~1.25 for Entr-1.0, ~0.90 for Entr-2.0), validating that the AugMDP framework does not introduce unnecessary overhead when a Markovian optimum exists. In the neural-network experiments (Figure 2), Alg.2 with PPO (fwd/bwd KL) and REINFORCE all converge to the optimal CVaR₀.₂₅ value of 0.75, though they initially plateau at the Markovian value (~0.5) around 2k steps before reaching the non-Markovian optimum around 8k steps.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies and generalizes prior risk-sensitive RL work that was restricted to specific risk measures (CVaR via Bauerle & Ott's AugMDP, entropic risk via exponential Bellman equations) under the broader OCE framework from Ben-Tal and Teboulle. The key methodological departure is a clean reductions approach: both the optimistic meta-algorithm (Alg. 1) and the gradient-based meta-algorithm (Alg. 2) reduce OCE RL to standard risk-neutral RL in an augmented MDP, allowing direct reuse of existing oracles (UCB-VI, Rep-UCB, GOLF, NPG) with minimal modification. Prior gradient-based RSRL work (Tamar et al., Chow et al.) provided only asymptotic or local convergence guarantees; this paper leverages recent finite-sample policy gradient theory (Agarwal et al. 2021) to establish the first non-asymptotic global convergence and monotone improvement bounds for RSRL. The extension to exogenous block MDPs via GOLF is novel, as prior RSRL bounds were confined to tabular or low-rank MDPs with model-based oracles.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, general framework that subsumes all prior CVaR and entropic risk RL bounds and extends them to the full OCE family (including Markowitz mean-variance and Mean-CVaR), while also opening the door to more complex MDP structures (exogenous block MDPs) that were previously intractable for RSRL. The empirical demonstration on a 2-state MDP where all Markovian policies provably achieve suboptimal CVaR is a clean validation that history-dependent policies are genuinely necessary, not just a theoretical artifact. Key limitations include the discrete reward assumption (Assumption 3.5) required for the GOLF and NPG analyses, the very simple synthetic MDP used for empirical validation, and the fact that the optimistic meta-algorithm's computational cost depends on the oracle (GOLF is computationally inefficient). Open questions include scaling to continuous rewards without the K^{2/3} rate penalty, extending to dynamic/iterated risks, and applying the framework to practical domains like robotics or LLM alignment.
