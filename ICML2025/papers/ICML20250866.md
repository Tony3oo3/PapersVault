## Problem

OOD detection and OOD generalization in deep neural networks have been studied in isolation, with no prior work examining how Neural Collapse (NC) simultaneously affects both tasks. This paper establishes that NC degree in a DNN layer is inversely related to these objectives: stronger NC improves OOD detection but degrades generalization, and vice versa, meaning a single feature space cannot optimize both. The paper asks how to strategically control NC at different layers to achieve both capabilities simultaneously.

## Input / Output

The method takes 224×224 RGB images and produces three types of outputs from different network layers: (1) class labels via the full network (encoder → projector → classifier) for ID classification, (2) an ID/OOD decision via energy-based scoring on the projector's collapsed embeddings, and (3) transferable feature embeddings from the encoder (pre-projector) used for linear probing on OOD datasets. The encoder and projector thus serve as two distinct embedding spaces—one optimized for generalization, the other for detection.

## Method

The method decouples OOD detection and generalization by controlling NC at different layers of a DNN. In the encoder, a nearest-neighbor entropy regularization loss (L_reg) is added to the cross-entropy objective to maximize embedding spread and mitigate NC, supported theoretically by a proposition showing that class-conditional collapse drives differential entropy to −∞. In the projector (a two-layer MLP between encoder and classifier), weights are fixed to a rank-(D−1) Simplex ETF configuration and frozen during training, with L2 normalization applied to the output to enforce equinormality and strengthen NC. Batch normalization in the encoder is replaced with Group Normalization plus Weight Standardization to reduce batch dependency and further promote transferable features. At inference, the projector embeddings feed the energy-based OOD detector while the encoder embeddings are used for linear probing on OOD datasets, enabling both tasks from a single trained model.

## Results

On ImageNet-100 (ID) with eight OOD datasets, the proposed method (entropy regularization + fixed ETF projector) reduces average OOD detection error (FPR95) from 94.92% to 65.10% for VGG17, from 97.40% to 69.70% for ResNet18, and from 90.17% to 83.16% for ViT-T, while simultaneously improving OOD generalization (linear probing error) from 49.54% to 41.85% (VGG17), 49.42% to 47.72% (ResNet18), and 52.68% to 52.63% (ViT-T). The projector layer achieves 7.73%–22.52% lower OOD detection error than the encoder, while the encoder achieves 10.90%–24.51% lower OOD generalization error than the projector, confirming the inverse NC–task relationship. ID accuracy remains competitive (12.62% vs. 12.18% for VGG17).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against standard DNN baselines (no NC control), the proposed method improves OOD detection by 7.01%–29.82% (absolute FPR95 reduction) and OOD generalization by 1.70%–7.69% (absolute linear probing error reduction) across VGG17, ResNet18, and ViT-T, while maintaining competitive ID accuracy. Against NECO (Ammar et al., 2024), a state-of-the-art NC-based OOD detection method, the proposed method achieves lower average OOD detection error by 12.72% (VGG17: 65.10% vs. 77.82%), 18.43% (ResNet18: 69.70% vs. 88.13%), and 2.51% (ViT-T: 83.16% vs. 85.67%), consistently outperforming across all three architectures. The paper notes that direct comparison with other OOD detection/generalization methods is not possible because those methods use different problem formulations (covariate-shift OOD for generalization, auxiliary OOD training data), whereas this work targets semantic OOD for both tasks without auxiliary data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate lines of research: NC-based OOD detection (Haas et al., 2023; Wu et al., 2024b; NECO/Ammar et al., 2024) and NC-related OOD generalization (Kothapalli, 2023; Masarczyk et al., 2023; Harun et al., 2024). It builds on the energy-based scoring function from Liu et al. (2020) for detection, the nearest-neighbor entropy estimator from Kozachenko & Leonenko (1987) for the regularization loss, and the finding from Rangamani et al. (2023) that fixing collapsed layers to simplex ETFs does not impair ID performance. The key departure is treating NC as a tunable, layer-specific knob: prior work either exploited NC for detection or lamented its harm to generalization, but none proposed a single architecture that simultaneously strengthens NC in one layer (frozen ETF projector) and weakens it in another (entropy-regularized encoder).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, low-overhead (<0.3% FLOPs) recipe for decoupling detection and generalization representations within a single network, which is directly relevant to open-world and continual learning settings where both capabilities are needed simultaneously. The theoretical link between differential entropy and NC1 (Proposition 4.1) gives a principled justification for the entropy regularizer beyond heuristic tuning. Limitations include reliance on nearest-neighbor entropy estimation (which may not scale to very high dimensions), focus on vision classification tasks, and the fact that the OOD generalization evaluation uses linear probing rather than full fine-tuning. Open questions include whether optimization-driven NC strategies (e.g., Markou et al., 2024) can complement the architectural approach, and whether the framework extends to audio, text, or detection/regression tasks.
