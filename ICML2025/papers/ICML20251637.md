## Problem

Existing DPO-based alignment of text-to-image diffusion models suffers from two key inaccuracies: (1) binary winner/loser preference labels ignore the inherent variability in human preferences, causing excessive optimization, and (2) estimating the diffusion trajectory via random forward noise (as in Diffusion-DPO) misaligns the optimization objective from the actual sampling process, leading to inefficient training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251637/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Training consumes text prompts paired with winner/loser image pairs from the Pick-a-Pic v2 dataset (851,293 pairs, 58,960 unique prompts), where a PickScore reward model automatically assigns smooth preference scores rather than relying on manual binary labels. The fine-tuned model (SD1.5 or SDXL) takes a text prompt at inference and produces a single RGB image; evaluation uses Parti-Prompts (1,632 prompts) and HPDv2 (3,200 prompts) with five reward evaluators (PickScore, HPSv2.1, ImageReward, Aesthetic, CLIP) reporting median scores and pairwise win-rates.

## Method

SmPO-Diffusion introduces two modifications to the Diffusion-DPO objective. First, Smoothed Preference Modeling replaces the binary preference with a weighted-geometric-mean distribution parameterized by a weight-to-sensitivity ratio α/γ, which is set to the softmax of normalized PickScore reward scores for each image pair; this causes the DPO loss to shrink toward zero when the two images are close in quality, preventing over-optimization. Second, Renoise Inversion replaces the random forward-noise estimation of the diffusion trajectory with a few-step DDIM Inversion (≤9 steps) followed by a single ReNoise correction step, yielding latents x̃_t that are highly correlated with the actual image content rather than dominated by random Gaussian noise. The final loss (Eq. 15) multiplies the standard DPO score difference by the smoothed coefficient (2α−γ)β and evaluates the score function at the Renoise-Inversion latents, driving the model to denoise the preferred image more aggressively than the dispreferred one at trajectory points that faithfully reflect the image structure.

## Results

On HPDv2 (3200 prompts), SmPO-SDXL achieves median HPSv2.1 of 32.53 (+4.08 over base SDXL) and PickScore of 23.62, while SmPO-SD1.5 reaches HPSv2.1 of 29.31 (+4.7 over base SD1.5) and PickScore of 22.08. SmPO-SDXL attains win-rates of 86.7% (HPSv2.1) and 77.2% (PickScore) against DPO-SDXL on HPDv2. Training cost is dramatically reduced: SmPO-SDXL requires ~150.8 A800 GPU hours vs. ~976.0 for DPO-SDXL (15.5%), and SmPO-SD1.5 requires ~41.3 vs. ~1056.0 for KTO-SD1.5 (3.9%, a 26× speedup).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SmPO-Diffusion outperforms all baselines on nearly every metric for both SDXL and SD1.5. Against DPO (Wallace et al., 2023), SmPO-SDXL wins 86.7% on HPSv2.1 and 77.2% on PickScore (HPDv2), while SmPO-SD1.5 wins 89.5% on HPSv2.1 and 79.1% on PickScore. Against MaPO (Hong et al., 2024), SmPO-SDXL leads on PickScore (23.62 vs. 22.81), HPSv2.1 (32.53 vs. 29.11), and ImReward (1.331 vs. 1.224), though MaPO edges ahead on Aesthetic (6.309 vs. 6.264). Against KTO (Ethayarajh et al., 2024), SmPO-SD1.5 dominates across all five metrics (e.g., HPSv2.1: 29.31 vs. 28.28; ImReward: 0.885 vs. 0.706). Against the base models, SmPO delivers the largest absolute gains (e.g., +4.08 HPSv2.1 for SDXL, +4.7 for SD1.5). Critically, SmPO achieves these improvements at a fraction of the training cost: 15.5% of DPO-SDXL's GPU hours, 18.1% of MaPO-SDXL's, and only 3.9% of KTO-SD1.5's (a 26× speedup).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Diffusion-DPO (Wallace et al., 2023) by addressing two specific weaknesses: the binary preference label assumption and the reliance on random forward-noise estimation for the diffusion trajectory. The smoothed preference modeling draws on the geometric-averaged preference optimization framework of Furuta et al. (2024), while the Renoise Inversion step adapts the few-step DDIM Inversion + ReNoise procedure from Garibi et al. (2024) and Mokady et al. (2023) into the DPO training loop. The overall DPO loss structure and Bradley-Terry formulation are inherited directly from Rafailov et al. (2024), with the key departure being the image-dependent weighting factor (2α−γ)β that modulates the loss magnitude based on reward-model-estimated preference confidence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: achieving comparable or better alignment quality at roughly 15–20% of the GPU hours required by Diffusion-DPO makes preference fine-tuning of large diffusion models substantially more accessible. The conceptual insight that binary winner/loser labels are too coarse and that random forward-noise estimation is the primary source of training inefficiency in DPO-Diffusion is well-motivated and supported by ablations. However, the method remains an offline fine-tuning approach evaluated on a single training dataset (Pick-a-Pic v2) and two model families (SD1.5, SDXL), and the authors themselves note that online/continuous preference learning is an open direction. The reliance on a pre-trained reward model (PickScore) for generating smooth labels also introduces a dependency that could propagate dataset-specific biases.
