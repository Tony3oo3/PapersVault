## Problem

Existing time-series explanation methods lack a unified optimization criterion, causing explanations to suffer from redundancy (irrelevant temporal patterns mixed in) and incompleteness (critical patterns missed). No principled framework simultaneously addresses both failure modes while remaining faithful to the black-box model's predictions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252958/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a multivariate or univariate time series instance x ∈ R^{T×D} (where T is the number of time steps and D the number of sensors) along with a pretrained black-box classifier f (Transformer, CNN, or LSTM). It produces a binary mask M ∈ {0,1}^{T×D} identifying which temporal-sensor positions are explanatory; the masked signal X^m = X ⊙ M serves as the local explanation. The mask is generated via an autoregressive transformer decoder that outputs per-position retention probabilities, then discretized through a Gumbel-Softmax mechanism with decoupled forward/backward temperatures.

## Method

ORTE is grounded in the Optimal Information Retention Principle, which defines three information-theoretic criteria: (1) Semantic Information Retention via Jensen-Shannon divergence between f(X) and f(X^m) to ensure fidelity; (2) Minimum Redundant Information Retention via minimizing I(X; M | Y), approximated as a KL divergence between the learned Bernoulli mask distribution and a target distribution with sparsity rate r; and (3) Maximum Effective Information Retention via minimizing I(X; Y | X^m), decomposed into a contrastive loss that pulls positive samples (MLP-padded masked signal) toward the original while pushing negative samples (reverse-masked + Gaussian noise) away. The key architectural innovation is adapt-STE, which decouples the Gumbel-Softmax temperature into separate forward (τ_f, kept small for sharp discrete masks) and backward (τ_b, kept large for faithful gradient flow) parameters, updated adaptively based on logit variance to resolve the asymmetry between forward discretization and backward gradient propagation.

## Results

ORTE achieves the best performance in 10 out of 12 metric-dataset combinations across four synthetic datasets (FreqShapes, SeqComb-UV, SeqComb-MV, LowVar). Compared to the strongest baseline TIMEX++, ORTE improves AUPRC by an average of 7.48%, AUP by 1.84%, and AUR by 22.27%. On the ECG real-world dataset, ORTE outperforms TIMEX++ by 8.85% (AUPRC: 0.7183 vs 0.6599), 13.25% (AUP: 0.8222 vs 0.7260), and 11.71% (AUR: 0.5133 vs 0.4595). On FreqShapes, ORTE achieves near-perfect AUPRC of 0.9998, substantially exceeding all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ORTE outperforms all seven baselines across all four synthetic datasets and the ECG real-world dataset. Against the closest competitor TIMEX++ (which itself uses an information bottleneck formulation), ORTE gains 7.48% average AUPRC and 22.27% average AUR, with the largest AUR gap on SeqComb-MV (0.5632 vs 0.3906, +44%). Against TIMEX, the gap widens to 19.61% average AUPRC, indicating that the optimal information retention principle provides a substantially tighter optimization than the surrogate-model approach. The gradient-based baseline IG and the perturbation-based Dynamask are consistently the weakest, with Dynamask achieving AUPRC below 0.35 on three of four datasets. WinIT and CoRTX occupy the middle tier, while SGT+Grad performs poorly on multivariate data (AUPRC 0.3449 on LowVar). Notably, ORTE is the only method that simultaneously improves both AUP (precision) and AUR (recall) over TIMEX++, confirming the paper's claim of achieving a genuine trade-off between low redundancy and high completeness rather than trading one for the other.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the TIMEX/TIMEX++ line of work on mask-based time-series explanations by replacing their ad-hoc or single-principle objectives with a unified Optimal Information Retention Principle grounded in conditional mutual information. It inherits the autoregressive transformer decoder and Gumbel-Softmax discrete mapping from TIMEX, and the positive/negative sample construction strategy from TIMEX++, but introduces three distinct optimization criteria (semantic fidelity via JS divergence, redundancy minimization via KL divergence on Bernoulli masks, and completeness via contrastive learning) that prior methods addressed only partially or heuristically. The adapt-STE mechanism is a direct modification of the Straight-Through Gumbel-Softmax estimator (Jang et al., 2016; Bengio et al., 2013), decoupling forward and backward temperature to resolve the asymmetry identified by Shah et al. (2024).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first information-theoretic unification of fidelity, redundancy, and completeness as joint optimization objectives for time-series explanations, offering a principled alternative to the heuristic criteria that have guided prior methods. The consistent AUR gains (~22% over TIMEX++) across architectures (Transformer, CNN, LSTM) and datasets suggest that prior methods systematically sacrificed completeness for precision, a trade-off this framework makes explicit. However, the authors acknowledge that hyperparameter sensitivity (particularly r, α, β, γ) limits plug-and-play applicability, and extending the principle to gradient-based or other mask-based explanation methods remains open. The framework's generality—applying to any pretrained black-box classifier—makes it a potentially useful template for future time-series interpretability work.
