## Problem

Standard conformal prediction guarantees only marginal coverage, systematically undercovering underrepresented groups, while group-wise conformal inference achieves group-conditional fairness at the cost of excessively wide prediction sets due to limited per-group sample sizes. This creates a fundamental fairness–efficiency tradeoff: ensuring valid coverage for every protected group while keeping prediction sets practically useful remains unresolved, particularly when primary outcomes are only partially observed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250707/cropped_page7_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a semi-supervised data structure: a small source dataset with observed primary outcomes Y and a larger target dataset where Y is missing but surrogate outcomes S (e.g., biomarker titers) and baseline covariates X are available for both. Protected group labels Z (e.g., race, age-comorbidity strata) are observed for all individuals. The output is a set of conformal prediction sets for a new target individual, where the set boundary is determined by a cluster-specific threshold r̂_α^k assigned via a learned group-to-cluster mapping ĥ(Z), yielding prediction sets that are simultaneously group-fair and tighter than group-wise alternatives.

## Method

SAGCCI combines two innovations: (1) it embeds each protected group's empirical conformal score distribution via quantile vectors, applies k-means clustering to pool groups with similar score distributions (measured by KS distance) into K larger clusters, and estimates a single threshold per cluster rather than per group; (2) it derives an efficient influence function (EIF) for the threshold that augments the standard conformal score with a surrogate-assisted conditional CDF term, enabling a doubly robust estimator that achieves local semiparametric efficiency. The EIF-based estimator is constructed via a split conformal strategy with localized debiased machine learning: nuisance functions (propensity score, conditional CDFs with and without surrogates) are trained on a first fold, and the cluster-specific threshold is computed on a second fold as the smallest value satisfying a sum-of-EIFs ≥ 0 condition. Theoretical guarantees show approximate group-conditional coverage (1−α−ε) with a PAC-style bound whose slack vanishes under standard rate-double-robustness conditions on the nuisance estimators.

## Results

On the Moderna COVE trial (N=1418, α=0.05), SAGCCI achieves the smallest average prediction set size (AvgSize = 3.61) and narrowest prediction interval widths (3.55–3.66) across all three age-comorbidity subgroups, while maintaining valid group-conditional coverage of exactly 0.95 in every subgroup. The coverage gap (CovGap) is 0.18, comparable to the best fair baseline SURRO+GROUP (0.14) but with ~8% smaller prediction sets. In simulations with σ_S=3 and N=1000, SAGCCI reduces AvgSize by over 60% relative to SURRO+GROUP while increasing CovGap by only 7%, and reduces CovGap by over 80% relative to SURRO+STANDARD at N=3000.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAGCCI outperforms all four baselines on the COVE trial. Against group-wise conformal inference (Vovk 2012, implemented as NOSURRO+GROUP), SAGCCI reduces AvgSize from 4.63 to 3.61 (22% smaller) while achieving comparable coverage (CovGap 0.18 vs. 2.22). Against the surrogate-assisted group-wise method with covariate-shift correction (Gibbs et al. 2023, SURRO+GROUP), SAGCCI achieves a similar CovGap (0.18 vs. 0.14) but with 8% smaller prediction sets (3.61 vs. 3.93) and uniformly narrower widths. Against weighted conformal quantile regression (Lei & Candès 2021, WCQR), SAGCCI dramatically improves fairness (CovGap 0.18 vs. 5.24) while also producing smaller sets (3.61 vs. 4.05). Against conformalized fair quantile regression (Liu et al. 2022, CFQR), SAGCCI is more general (applicable to any non-conformity score, not just quantile-based) and achieves better empirical performance in the categorical-outcome simulation setting. The key tradeoff is that SAGCCI's CovGap (0.18) is marginally higher than SURRO+GROUP's (0.14), but this comes at a meaningful efficiency gain in prediction set width.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate threads in conformal inference: (1) clustered conformal prediction, which pools protected groups with similar nonconformity score distributions to avoid the excessive width of group-wise thresholds (Ding et al., 2024), and (2) surrogate-assisted semiparametric estimation, where auxiliary outcomes improve the efficiency of estimating functionals when primary outcomes are partially missing (Kallus & Mao, 2024; Gao et al., 2024). The key methodological contribution is deriving an efficient influence function for the conformal threshold that incorporates surrogates as 'helper covariates' rather than statistical surrogates in the Prentice sense, and then applying this EIF within a cluster-specific split-conformal procedure. The doubly robust coverage guarantee follows the Chernozhukov et al. (2018) paradigm, and the localized debiased ML approach for nuisance estimation follows Kallus et al. (2024).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete and practically important tension: group-wise conformal inference guarantees fairness but produces unusably wide prediction sets for small protected groups, while standard conformal is efficient but unfair. SAGCCI provides a principled, theoretically grounded resolution with a clean efficiency-gain formula (Corollary 4.5) that quantifies exactly how much surrogates help. Open questions include extending the framework to multi-source settings with distributional shift, relaxing the MAR assumption, and adapting to federated settings where individual-level data cannot be shared—directions the authors flag but do not resolve. The real-data validation on the Moderna COVE trial is encouraging but limited to a single dataset and a specific surrogate (nAb-ID50 titer), leaving open whether the efficiency gains generalize across different surrogate-outcome relationships.
