## Problem

Frequency-domain image anomaly detection methods lack an interpretable theoretical framework guaranteeing their effectiveness. While DFT-based structural change tests exist in econometrics (e.g., Fu et al., 2023), they operate on single-dimensional time series and cannot be directly applied to two-dimensional image anomaly detection. This paper bridges that gap by formulating image anomaly detection as a factor model structural change problem and deriving rigorous asymptotic theory for a multi-dimensional DFT-based test.

## Input / Output

The method takes 256×256 RGB images (also evaluated at 512×512 and 1024×1024) as input and produces both a pixel-level anomaly map (computed as the element-wise difference X − X_Rec between the original and reconstructed image) and an image-level anomaly score derived from the AUROC of the anomaly map. The DFS module operates on the 2D Fourier transform of the input, applying a learned binary mask before passing the masked signal to a vanilla U-Net reconstruction network.

## Method

The core contribution is the Demeaned Fourier Sparse (DFS) module, grounded in a factor model where each pixel X_hw = λ_hw^T F_w + ε_hw and anomalies manifest as deviations in factor loadings. The DFS module constructs a Bernoulli mask M in the 2D Fourier domain via a sigmoid-parameterized bootstrap-like resampling procedure (M = σ(σ(ω̂) − s)), which sparsifies dominant frequency components to enforce under-fitting (K < R), ensuring residuals retain anomalous signal. Training alternates between optimizing the mask with L1 regularization (first half) and fixing the mask while training the U-Net reconstruction network with L2 + SSIM + MSGMS losses (second half). The theoretical foundation proves that under H₀ the demeaned DFT of residuals converges to a zero-spectrum Gaussian process, while under H_A it converges to a non-zero spectrum, establishing both global power (image-level detection) and local power (pixel-level detection at the H^{-1/2}W^{-1/2} lower bound).

## Results

On MVTec-AD, the proposed DFS-AD achieves average Img-AUROC / Pix-AUROC / PRO of 98.69% / 98.32% / 93.24%, ranking first in pixel-level AUROC among all compared methods. On VisA, it achieves 96.34% / 98.19% / 91.15%, competitive with the strongest baseline TransFusion (98.69% / 98.44% / 94.67%). The DFS module improves over the reconstruction-only baseline (Ours-Base) by +3.65% Img-AUROC, +1.73% Pix-AUROC, and +9.14% PRO on MVTec-AD, and by +1.46% / +0.56% / +5.02% on VisA. The method also uses only 28.37M parameters and 38.20G FLOPs, substantially fewer than DRAEM (97.42M / 198.66G) and SSNF (294.67M / 243.77G).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On MVTec-AD, DFS-AD achieves the highest Pix-AUROC (98.32%) among all methods, surpassing PatchCore (97.37%) and DRAEM (97.06%), but trails TransFusion on Img-AUROC (98.69% vs. 99.42%) and PRO (93.24% vs. 95.29%). On VisA, TransFusion leads across all three metrics (98.69/98.44/94.67), while DFS-AD (96.34/98.19/91.15) still outperforms PatchCore on Img-AUROC (+2.95%) and PRO (−1.65%, actually PatchCore leads on PRO at 92.80%), and beats DRAEM, PyramidFlow, SSNF, and IFgNet on Img-AUROC. The DFS module's contribution is most visible in PRO: it lifts the reconstruction baseline by +9.14% on MVTec-AD and +5.02% on VisA, confirming the theoretical local-power guarantee for small anomalies. In terms of efficiency, DFS-AD uses 28.37M parameters and 38.20G FLOPs, roughly 3.4× fewer FLOPs than DRAEM and 6.4× fewer than SSNF, while maintaining competitive accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the DFT-based structural change test for large-dimensional factor models from Fu et al. (2023, Journal of Econometrics) to the 2D image anomaly detection setting, extending the 1D time-series test to a multi-dimensional Fourier transform across two spatial dimensions. It reframes image anomaly detection as a structural change problem within a factor model, where anomalies manifest as deviations in factor loadings detectable through residual analysis in the Fourier domain. The practical DFS module draws on bootstrap-like resampling from nonparametric statistics and integrates with standard reconstruction-based pipelines (U-Net backbone), positioning itself as a plug-and-play enhancement rather than a standalone architecture. The paper also connects to the broader observation (Kascenas et al., 2023) that anomalies are additive perturbations, and to classical residual-based structural break tests (Chow, Ljung-Box, Breusch-Pagan) in econometrics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The main contribution is a theoretical framework that provides asymptotic guarantees for why frequency-domain residual analysis can detect anomalies at both image and pixel levels, filling a gap where prior Fourier-based detection methods lacked interpretable justification. The DFS module offers a lightweight, distribution-free sparsification mechanism that improves reconstruction-based detectors without requiring synthetic anomalies or pretrained feature extractors, and its low computational cost (28M params, 38G FLOPs) is practical for deployment. However, the empirical gains over strong baselines like TransFusion are modest on MVTec-AD and VisA, and the method has only been validated on 2D industrial images with a vanilla U-Net, leaving open questions about scalability to 3D data, more complex backbones, and whether the factor model assumption holds broadly. The asymptotic local power result (detection at the H^{-1/2}W^{-1/2} scale) is a useful theoretical lower bound but its practical tightness for small anomalies in real images remains to be established.
