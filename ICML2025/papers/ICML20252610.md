## Problem

High-norm tokens (massive activations) in LLMs appear abruptly in specific layers and are tied to particular token positions, unlike the gradual, spatially random defective tokens in ViTs. While the singular defect theory explains ViT defects via leading singular vectors of layer approximations, it falls short of accounting for the LLM-specific behaviors of sudden norm explosion, position-dependent triggering, and rapid decay. This paper asks whether and how the singular defect framework can be extended to fully characterize the lifecycle of high-norm tokens in LLMs.

## Input / Output

The method consumes the weight matrices of a trained LLM's transformer layers (self-attention projections, FFN gate/up/down projections, and RMSNorm scales) along with input text tokens to compute linear approximations of each layer. It produces three outputs: (1) layer-wise singular defect directions (leading left singular vectors of the approximated layer matrix) that predict the empirical high-norm direction, (2) a W8A8-quantized model where only the down-projection F₂ in explosion and decay layers is kept at higher precision, and (3) a data-independent model signature vector (the empirical high-norm direction) whose pairwise acute angle with another model's signature indicates fine-tuning lineage. The linear approximation relies on a single-token assumption that is exactly satisfied for the first token under causal self-attention.

## Method

The paper extends the singular defect theory by decomposing each transformer layer into an identity path and a residual path R = A + F + FA, where A and F are linear approximations of the self-attention and FFN modules respectively. It then characterizes the full lifecycle of high-norm tokens in four stages: (i) Development — initial tokens develop high norms via self-attention while noninitial high-norm tokens (e.g., delimiters) do so independently of attention; (ii) Trigger — the norm explosion is initiated when the pre-FFN activation has a large projection onto the leading right singular vector of the FFN's linear approximation (the 'explosion subspace'); (iii) Explosion — the output aligns with the layer-wise singular defect direction (leading left singular vector of the full layer matrix); (iv) Decay — a subsequent layer suppresses the norm via a negative eigenvalue of its residual matrix whose eigenvector aligns with the singular defect direction. Two applications follow: selectively skipping quantization of F₂ in explosion/decay layers to fix tensor-wise W8A8 quantization (reducing LLaMA3-8B PPL from 59.38 to 8.80), and using the stable singular defect direction as a data-independent model signature for detecting fine-tuning lineage.

## Results

On WikiText2-v1, the paper's high-norm-aware quantization strategy (skipping the FFN down-proj F2 in explosion and decay layers) dramatically reduces perplexity for tensor-wise W8A8 quantization. For LLaMA2-7B, RTN PPL drops from 10.18 to 6.51 and SmoothQuant PPL drops from 13.87 to 6.78, both approaching the FP16 baseline of 5.47. The gains are even more dramatic for LLaMA3-8B, where RTN PPL improves from 59.38 to 8.80 and SmoothQuant PPL from 54.99 to 9.14 (FP16 baseline: 6.14). Additionally, the LLM signature based on singular defect directions correctly clusters fine-tuned models with their base models (near-zero angle) while separating independently trained models (~90° angle).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its skip-F2 strategy against two standard tensor-wise W8A8 quantization baselines: RTN (Round-To-Nearest) and SmoothQuant. Without the proposed fix, both baselines suffer severe PPL degradation, especially on LLaMA3-8B where RTN jumps from 6.14 (FP16) to 59.38 and SmoothQuant to 54.99, rendering the quantized models nearly unusable. Applying the paper's strategy of keeping F2 at full precision in just two layers (explosion and decay) reduces RTN PPL to 8.80 and SmoothQuant PPL to 9.14 on LLaMA3-8B, a 50+ point improvement. On LLaMA2-7B, the same approach brings RTN from 10.18 to 6.51 and SmoothQuant from 13.87 to 6.78, both within ~1.3 PPL of the FP16 baseline. The paper's method thus does not replace these quantization schemes but acts as a lightweight, model-agnostic patch that makes tensor-wise quantization viable without requiring hardware support for row-wise quantization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the singular defect theory of Wang et al. (2024), originally developed for DINOv2 vision transformers, to the LLM setting, where high-norm tokens exhibit fundamentally different behavior (abrupt appearance/decay, fixed direction across layers, dependence on causal self-attention). It also builds on Sun et al. (2024), who empirically identified massive activations in LLMs at fixed feature dimensions but provided no mathematical explanation. The key departure is the introduction of new theoretical machinery specific to LLMs: the explosion subspace (leading right singular vector of the FFN linear approximation), the negative-eigenvalue characterization of decay layers, and the distinction between attention-dependent (initial token) and attention-independent (noninitial token) exploding paths.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic mathematical framework covering the full lifecycle (development, trigger, explosion, decay) of high-norm tokens in LLMs, validated across 8+ model families. The finding that removing the explosion subspace component eliminates high norms but destroys generation quality reveals that high-norm tokens are functionally necessary, not merely numerical artifacts—a constraint that any future repair strategy must respect. The root cause of high-norm emergence remains open (causal self-attention is implicated but not proven), and the model-signature application, while creative, has not been stress-tested against adversarial fine-tuning or architectural modifications. The quantization improvement, while practical, is limited to skipping two specific matrices and may not generalize to all quantization regimes.
