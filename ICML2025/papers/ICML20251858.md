## Problem

Despite LLMs' success in prediction tasks, their ability to make optimal decisions under uncertainty—specifically balancing exploration and exploitation in bandit settings—remains largely unexplored. Prior work (Krishnamurthy et al., 2024) examined only small-scale MAB tasks with Bernoulli rewards, leaving open questions about how LLMs generalize across varying action spaces, reward distributions, and contextual bandit environments, and whether known optimal algorithms can be effectively integrated into LLM decision-making.

## Input / Output

The LLM agent receives at each time step a textual prompt containing the scenario description, the set of available actions (K arms with textual labels), and the interaction history formatted as either raw (action, reward) tuples growing linearly with T, summarized statistics (per-arm empirical means and pull counts), or algorithm-guided support (per-arm exploitation values and exploration bonuses computed via UCB/LinUCB). For contextual bandits, the input additionally includes user demographic text and a 5-dimensional numeric preference vector alongside movie features. The output is a single action selection (the chosen arm name) per time step, with the agent interacting over T=200–1000 rounds depending on task configuration.

## Method

The paper introduces BanditBench, a benchmark of 16 MAB configurations (varying K∈{5,20}, Bernoulli/Gaussian rewards, difficulty via Δ_min, and semantic vs. arbitrary action labels) plus 2 MovieLens-based CB tasks, and proposes two complementary techniques to inject optimal exploration knowledge into LLMs. First, Inference-Time Algorithm-Guided Support (AG) directly provides UCB-computed exploitation values and exploration bonuses in the prompt, reducing the LLM's task to addition and argmax rather than inferring reward distributions from raw history. Second, Algorithm Distillation uses UCB-generated oracle trajectories for either in-context few-shot demonstration or Oracle Behavior Fine-Tuning (OFT), where the LLM is fine-tuned via maximum-likelihood on predicting UCB's next action given the history representation. A key finding is that OFT on hard-task trajectories with raw history representation enables small models (Gemini-1.5 Flash) to surpass larger models (Gemini-1.5 Pro), while few-shot learning prefers easy-task trajectories with summarized history—the opposite preference. The paper further characterizes exploration efficiency by fitting a parametric regret function f(T) = λ₁log(T)^α/Δ_min + βT + λ₂, showing that fine-tuned Flash models achieve sublinear regret (β≈0) while smaller Gemma models remain in the linear regret regime.

## Results

On BanditBench (16 MAB + 2 CB configurations, 30 trials each), the best inference-time method (SH for Pro) achieves 60.0% pairwise win-rate on MAB and 64.3% (AG for Pro) on CB, versus 90.6% (UCB) and 96.4% (LinUCB) for the optimal classical algorithms. Oracle Behavior Fine-Tuning (OFT) of Gemini-1.5 Flash with RH on MAB reaches 65.6% win-rate, surpassing the larger Gemini-1.5 Pro (60.0% with SH). On CB, OFT Flash with AG achieves 89.3% win-rate, approaching LinUCB's 96.4%. Regret analysis shows that OFT-enabled Flash attains sublinear regret (β≈0) on hard MAB, while Gemma-2B/9B remain in the linear regret regime across all methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against UCB (Auer, 2002), the optimal MAB algorithm, all LLM methods fall substantially short: the best LLM configuration (OFT Flash, 65.6%) still trails UCB's 90.6% win-rate on MAB, and even the best CB result (OFT Flash + AG, 89.3%) lags LinUCB's 96.4%. Against the Summarized History (SH) baseline from Krishnamurthy et al. (2024), AG provides comparable or slightly lower MAB performance for Pro (59.6% vs. 60.0%) but is the only inference-time method that works for CB, where it lifts Flash from 0.0% (RH) to 46.4%. The key win is OFT Flash (65.6% MAB, 89.3% CB) surpassing the larger Gemini-1.5 Pro (60.0% MAB, 64.3% CB) across both task types, demonstrating that distilling UCB trajectories into a smaller model outperforms simply scaling up. Few-shot demonstrations help Flash (27.5%→50.2% on MAB with AG) but paradoxically hurt Pro (39.1%→56.4% with AG, still below Pro's SH baseline of 60.0%), suggesting that manually crafted examples disrupt the chain-of-thought structure in larger models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Krishnamurthy et al. (2024), who first examined LLMs' exploration in small-scale MAB tasks, by significantly broadening the scope to both multi-armed and contextual bandits across diverse reward distributions, action spaces, and difficulty levels. The methodological contributions draw on two distinct lineages: inference-time algorithmic guidance (related to value-function-guided decoding in Mudgal et al., 2023 and Liu et al., 2024b) and algorithm distillation (related to in-context RL with algorithm distillation in Laskin et al., 2022 and Lee et al., 2024), but applied to general-purpose LLMs rather than domain-specific transformers. The UCB/LinUCB algorithms serve as the oracle, connecting the work to the classical bandit literature (Auer, 2002; Li et al., 2010).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that oracle behavior fine-tuning enables a small model (Gemini-1.5 Flash) to surpass a much larger model (Gemini-1.5 Pro) on exploration tasks offers a practical pathway for efficient decision-making with lower inference costs. However, a significant optimality gap persists between even the best LLM configurations and classical UCB/LinUCB algorithms, particularly in hard settings where most LLMs exhibit linear rather than sublinear regret. Open questions include whether these distillation techniques generalize beyond bandits to richer sequential decision-making, how to close the remaining optimality gap, and whether the contrasting data-representation preferences (few-shot favors summarized, fine-tuning favors raw) reflect fundamental differences in how LLMs process information.
