## Problem

Existing LLM watermarking schemes are impractical due to generation latency, slow detection, text quality degradation, or poor robustness, largely because they operate at the token level and ignore the structural properties of text. The paper asks whether a watermark can be embedded directly into model weights at generation time—yielding a 'structural' watermark—while maintaining formal statistical guarantees, zero generation-latency overhead, and negligible impact on model quality.

## Input / Output

At generation, the method takes a language model's weights θ, a prompt x, and a variance σ², then samples a Gaussian key ξ ~ N(0, σ²I) and perturbs a single MLP weight matrix in one transformer layer (e.g., up_proj in layer 28 for Llama3.1-8B) to produce watermarked text. At detection, it takes the original model weights, the watermark key ξ, the prompt x, and a candidate text y, and returns a p-value (1 − Φ(ψ)) via a single forward and backward pass through the watermarked parameter. Detection requires white-box access to model weights but does not require knowing the exact prompt, as the method is robust to prompt corruption.

## Method

GaussMark embeds a watermark by additively perturbing a single MLP weight matrix with a Gaussian vector ξ ~ N(0, σ²I) at generation time, then detects the watermark by testing statistical independence between ξ and the candidate text y using the test statistic ψ = ⟨ξ, ∇_θ log p_θ(y|x)⟩ / (σ‖∇_θ log p_θ(y|x)‖). The key theoretical insight is that under the composite null hypothesis (ξ independent of y), the rotation invariance and direction-norm independence of the Gaussian ensure ψ is exactly standard normal, yielding a valid p-value with no assumptions on the null distribution. Under a linear softmax model approximation, the paper derives power bounds showing detectability scales with model dimension d and the gradient norm ‖∇_θ log p_θ(y|x)‖, which grows with sequence length. The approach is motivated by empirical evidence that LMs are robust to small additive weight perturbations (as seen in model merging and low-rank fine-tuning), and a rank-reduction variant further restricts noise to the bottom-k principal components of the watermarked weight to improve the quality-detectability trade-off.

## Results

GaussMark achieves essentially no loss in model quality across all three evaluated models. On Llama3.1-8B, SuperGLUE accuracy changes from 0.7012 to 0.7094 and GSM-8K from 0.6300 to 0.6111; on Mistral-7B, SuperGLUE goes from 0.6836 to 0.7033 and GSM-8K from 0.4291 to 0.4321; on Phi3.5-Mini, SuperGLUE shifts from 0.6451 to 0.6489 and GSM-8K from 0.8423 to 0.8552. AlpacaEval-2.0 win rates of watermarked vs. unwatermarked models (0.45, 0.50, 0.49) all fall within the self-comparison stochasticity intervals. Detection is fast: generating 1K tokens takes only milliseconds and detection requires less than a second.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary head-to-head comparison is with the Kirchenbauer et al. (2023a) red/green-list watermark (Appendix H). When Kirchenbauer's parameters are tuned to match GaussMark's generation quality, it exhibits diminished detectability and robustness relative to GaussMark, while its generation latency is several orders of magnitude greater (Figure 27). Conversely, GaussMark can be less detectable and less robust to token-level corruptions than Kirchenbauer at equivalent quality settings, but it has a much smaller impact on generated text quality and enables significantly faster generation. The paper also notes that GaussMark is substantially faster than Kuditipudi et al. (2023), which exhibits extremely slow generation and detection, and has lower latency than Dathathri et al. (2024) (SynthID-Text), which lacks formal statistical guarantees and requires storage scaling linearly with generated tokens.

## Relation to Prior Work

GaussMark departs from the dominant token-level watermarking paradigm (Kirchenbauer et al. 2023a,b; Kuditipudi et al. 2023; Christ et al. 2024b) that embeds signals in the autoregressive next-token sampling process, instead perturbing model weights with a Gaussian vector and testing for statistical independence at detection time. It repurposes the well-established empirical observation that LMs are robust to small weight perturbations (model merging, LoRA, pruning) for a watermarking objective, and draws on classical Gaussian independence testing from statistics. The closest concurrent work (Christ et al. 2024a) also adds a Gaussian to model parameters but focuses on removal resistance rather than formal statistical guarantees, and targets the final-layer bias rather than an internal MLP weight. Unlike distortionary schemes that bias token probabilities or non-distortionary schemes that manipulate the PRNG, GaussMark is a 'structural' watermark that shifts the entire text distribution, potentially offering broader robustness to paraphrasing and translation attacks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GaussMark is the first watermarking scheme combining formal statistical validity, zero generation-latency overhead, and trivial implementation (a single forward + backward pass) into a deployable pipeline, making it a strong candidate for production LLM providers who need provable false-positive control. Key open questions include: whether aggressive quantization erodes detectability, whether the approach extends to non-autoregressive paradigms (e.g., language diffusion models), and whether safety/alignment properties are preserved under perturbation. The requirement for white-box weight access and the need for more tokens than token-level schemes to achieve comparable detection power are practical limitations that constrain deployment scenarios.
