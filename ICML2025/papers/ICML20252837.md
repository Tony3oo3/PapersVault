## Problem

When combining Soft Actor-Critic with novelty-based exploration (e.g., RND or NovelD), the two exploration strategies—SAC's entropy-regularized stochastic policy and intrinsic-reward-driven novelty seeking—interact and alternately dominate the agent's behavior. This causes the agent to repeatedly revisit states with high intrinsic rewards rather than discovering unvisited regions, leading to redundant sample collection and inefficient learning in sparse-reward settings.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252837/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes environment state observations (discrete grid positions in 2D Navigation/DeepSea, or continuous joint positions and velocities in DM Control Suite) and produces actions (4-way discrete or continuous torques/forces). A separate intrinsic reward model (RND or NovelD) computes a novelty signal from the state, which serves dual purposes: it augments the extrinsic reward for training the novelty-augmented agent and acts as the switching criterion between the two agents. The standard agent's policy is frozen (zero loss weights) until extrinsic rewards are first observed, after which it learns from the shared replay buffer using only the sparse task reward.

## Method

KEA integrates two SAC agents: a novelty-augmented agent A^N trained with combined extrinsic and intrinsic rewards (via RND or NovelD), and a standard agent A^S trained only on the sparse extrinsic reward while maintaining high action variance by delaying gradient updates until extrinsic rewards are received. A switching mechanism ψ selects which agent's stochastic policy to execute based on a threshold σ on the current state's intrinsic reward: when r^int > σ, the standard agent A^S is used to stochastically explore unexplored regions; otherwise, the novelty-augmented agent A^N drives exploration toward relatively novel states within already-visited areas. Both agents share a unified replay buffer and are updated concurrently in an off-policy manner, enabling diverse data collection from distinct exploration strategies. The key insight is that proactive, threshold-based coordination between the two strategies prevents the cyclical revisiting behavior that arises from relying on natural entropy/novelty shifts, yielding more consistent and efficient discovery of new regions.

## Results

On the DeepSea hard-exploration benchmark, KEA-RND-SAC achieves 0.99±0.01 (N=10), 0.99±0.01 (N=14), 0.92±0.05 (N=20), 0.81±0.18 (N=24), and 0.54±0.32 (N=30), consistently outperforming all baselines, with the largest gap at N=30 (0.54 vs. 0.42 for SOFE-DQN and 0.10 for DeRL-DQN). On the DeepMind Control Suite sparse-reward tasks, KEA-RND-SAC improves mean episodic return by 119% (Walker Run: 629.74 vs. 287.65), 51% (Cheetah Run: 773.76 vs. 512.02), and 11% (Reacher Hard: 874.61 vs. 790.32) over RND-SAC. On the 2D Navigation task, KEA-RND-SAC achieves 0.403±0.042 episodic return, a >70% improvement over RND-SAC (0.235±0.184), while KEA-NovelD-SAC matches NovelD-SAC's final return (0.604 vs. 0.607) but converges ~60,000 steps faster.

## Baselines & Comparisons

KEA-RND-SAC outperforms standard SAC on all DeepSea sizes (SAC scores 0.00 for N≥20) and on Walker/Cheetah Run Sparse (SAC scores 0), while also beating SAC on Reacher Hard (874.61 vs. 715.17). Against RND-SAC, KEA wins on every DeepSea configuration, with the most dramatic gains at N=24 (0.81 vs. 0.67) and N=30 (0.54 vs. 0.35), and on all three DM Control Suite tasks (119%, 51%, 11% relative improvements). KEA also dominates both DeRL and SOFE variants across all DeepSea sizes; for example, at N=30, KEA achieves 0.54 versus 0.42 (SOFE-DQN, the strongest non-KEA baseline) and 0.10 (DeRL-DQN). On the 2D Navigation task, KEA-NovelD-SAC essentially ties NovelD-SAC in final return (0.604 vs. 0.607) but converges ~60K steps earlier, while KEA-RND-SAC substantially beats RND-SAC (0.403 vs. 0.235). In the generalization study (Fig. 4), KEA provides no benefit over RND-DQN (ε-greedy exploration is independent of novelty), but improves RND-DQN-P (proportional sampling) and RND-SQL, confirming that KEA is most effective when the base exploration strategy interacts with novelty-based exploration.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on SAC as the base off-policy RL agent and integrates novelty-based intrinsic rewards (RND or NovelD) to address sparse-reward exploration. Its key departure from prior work is identifying a specific inefficiency: the uncoordinated interaction between SAC's entropy-regularized stochastic policy and novelty-based intrinsic rewards causes cyclical revisiting of known states rather than directed exploration of unvisited regions. Rather than simply adding intrinsic rewards to SAC (as in RND-SAC or NovelD-SAC), KEA introduces a second standard agent and a novelty-threshold-based switching mechanism to proactively coordinate the two exploration strategies, a design not present in prior novelty-augmented SAC variants or in decoupled approaches like DeRL and SOFE.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, modular solution to a real coordination problem that arises when combining entropy-regularized policies with intrinsic motivation, and the switching mechanism is simple enough to plug into existing novelty-based pipelines. The generalization experiments (DQN, SQL) and the theoretical analysis in Appendix A clarify when and why the coordination helps, giving practitioners a principled understanding of the interaction dynamics. A key limitation is that KEA is restricted to off-policy settings due to the shared replay buffer, leaving open whether similar coordination is needed or beneficial for on-policy methods like PPO. The threshold hyperparameter, while shown to be relatively robust, still requires per-task tuning, and the approach adds computational overhead from maintaining two full agents.
