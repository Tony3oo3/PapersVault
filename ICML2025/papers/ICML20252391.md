## Problem

Existing self-supervised learning methods for remote sensing are specialized to narrow subsets of modalities (e.g., optical-only or SAR-only) and fixed input shapes (pixel time series or single-timestep images), limiting their transferability across the diverse tasks practitioners actually perform. Additionally, remote sensing objects span an extreme range of spatial and temporal scales—from single-pixel fishing boats visible in one frame to kilometer-scale glaciers tracked over decades—making it difficult for a single model to capture both fine-grained local and broad global features simultaneously.

## Input / Output

Each pretraining instance is a 96×96 pixel (10 m/pixel) patch with 24 monthly timesteps and 9 modalities (Sentinel-1 SAR, Sentinel-2 multispectral, NDVI, SRTM elevation/slope, Dynamic World land cover, World Cereal, ERA5 weather, TerraClimate, VIIRS nighttime lights, LandScan population, and geographic location), grouped into space-time, space-only, time-only, and static channel groups. At inference the same model flexibly consumes pixel time series, single-timestep images, or multi-timestep image sequences, producing per-patch token embeddings that serve as features for kNN, linear probing, or finetuning on classification and segmentation heads.

## Method

Galileo is a ViT-based model with a flexible tokenization scheme that projects space-time, space, time, and static inputs into a unified token sequence, using resizable linear projections to handle variable patch sizes and sequence lengths. Its core novelty is a dual self-supervised objective: a *global* task that predicts deep EMA-encoder targets (at modality-specific depths) under structured space-time masking with a batch-level contrastive loss (PatchDisc_B), and a *local* task that predicts shallow linear-projection targets under unstructured random masking with a within-sample contrastive loss (PatchDisc). The global task encourages between-input discriminability suited to classification, while the local task amplifies within-input spatial detail suited to segmentation; the combined loss is their average. A cross-attention predictor receives target position/time/channel embeddings as queries and attends to visible-token encodings to produce predictions, and the target encoder is updated via exponential moving average. Pretraining uses 127,155 globally sampled instances selected via k-means clustering over WorldCover class counts and coordinates to ensure geographic and semantic diversity.

## Results

Galileo-Base (ViT-Base, 85M params) achieves an average rank of 3.0 on image tasks and 1.8 on pixel-time-series tasks across eleven benchmarks, outperforming all larger specialist models. On EuroSat kNN (100% training), Galileo-Base reaches 93.0% top-1 accuracy vs. 89.8% for SoftCon (ViT-Small) and 85.6% for CROMA (ViT-Base). On the Breizhcrops pixel-time-series classification task, Galileo-Base achieves 73.0% accuracy, surpassing the pixel-time-series specialist Presto (63.0%) by 10 points. On CropHarvest (multimodal pixel time series), Galileo-Base scores 99.3% on Brazil, 84.2% on Kenya, and 74.8% on Togo, matching or exceeding Presto across all three regions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Galileo-Base outperforms all image-specialized baselines on kNN classification: it leads EuroSat by 3.2 points over SoftCon (93.0% vs. 89.8%), 7.4 points over CROMA (93.0% vs. 85.6%), and 8.9 points over SatMAE (93.0% vs. 84.1%). On finetuning, Galileo-Base (97.7% EuroSat) narrowly trails DeCUR ViT-Small (97.9%) and SoftCon ViT-Base (97.5%) by ≤0.2 points, but leads CROMA ViT-Large (96.6%) and SatMAE ViT-Large (96.6%) by over 1 point. Against the pixel-time-series specialist Presto, Galileo-Base wins decisively on Breizhcrops (73.0% vs. 63.0%) but Presto retains a small edge on CropHarvest Togo (75.5% vs. 74.8%) while Galileo-Base leads on Brazil (99.3% vs. 98.8%) and Kenya (84.2% vs. 84.0%). Compared to AnySat (the only other generalist supporting both images and pixel time series), Galileo-Base is substantially more accurate: 93.0% vs. 82.2% on EuroSat kNN and 73.0% vs. 66.1% on Breizhcrops. Notably, Galileo's smaller ViT-Nano (0.8M params) and ViT-Tiny (5.3M params) variants also outperform many larger specialist models, e.g., ViT-Tiny achieves 90.1% on EuroSat kNN, exceeding SatMAE ViT-Large (84.3%) and CROMA ViT-Base (85.6%).

## Relation to Prior Work

Galileo extends the latent masked-modeling framework of I-JEPA (Assran et al., 2023) and LatentMIM's PatchDisc loss (Wei et al., 2024) to the remote sensing domain, adding a dual global/local objective with different target depths (deep encoder layers vs. shallow linear projections) and complementary masking strategies (structured space-time vs. unstructured random). It departs from prior RS SSL methods (SatMAE, CROMA, Presto, AnySat) that are specialized to particular modalities or input shapes by unifying them into a single ViT-based architecture with flexible tokenization (inspired by FlexiViT's resizable projections) that handles pixel time series, multi-timestep imagery, and single images simultaneously. The PatchDisc_B loss, which adds batch-level negatives to prevent representational collapse, is a targeted modification of the original within-instance PatchDisc.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Galileo provides the first single open-source model that outperforms specialist RS models across both image and pixel time series benchmarks while supporting a much wider range of input modalities (optical, SAR, elevation, weather, land cover, population, nighttime lights), making it directly useful for practitioners who combine heterogeneous data sources. The ablations confirm that global and local objectives are genuinely complementary—neither alone matches the combined model—validating the multi-scale learning hypothesis. Open questions include whether the dual global/local SSL paradigm generalizes beyond remote sensing to other multi-scale domains, and whether the relatively small pretraining set (127K instances) limits the model's ceiling compared to larger-scale pretraining.
