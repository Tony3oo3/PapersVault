## Problem

Conditional diffusion and flow models succeed at image super-resolution but fail on physical domains where inputs and outputs are spatially misaligned (different PDE resolutions produce divergent trajectories), involve different channel sets (e.g., radar absent from input), and exhibit channel-dependent stochasticity. Prior two-stage residual approaches like CorrDiff overfit in the deterministic regression stage, propagating under-calibration into the generative stage.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250674/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 12-channel coarse-resolution ERA5 forecast (25 km, upsampled to 448×448 via bilinear interpolation) including pressure-level temperature, wind, geopotential at 500/850 hPa plus surface variables, and produces a 4-channel fine-resolution CWA target (2 km, 448×448): maximum radar reflectivity (entirely synthesized, absent from input), 2 m temperature, and 10 m east/north wind. Probabilistic outputs are generated as 64-member ensembles via different random seeds, enabling CRPS and SSR calibration metrics.

## Method

AFM jointly trains an encoder E(y) and a Flow Matching denoiser in an end-to-end framework. The encoder maps the coarse input into a latent space that corrects spatial misalignment and synthesizes missing channels; Gaussian noise scaled by σ_z is injected at the encoder output to represent residual uncertainty. The key innovation is adaptive noise scaling: σ_z is set to the encoder's validation-set RMSE (a maximum-likelihood estimate) and updated via EMA every 10k steps, so stochastic channels (radar) receive larger noise while deterministic ones (temperature) receive less. Flow Matching is implemented as an EDM-style denoising objective where the perturbation kernel x_t = x + σ_t(e + ε) combines both the encoder residual e and fresh noise, with a regularization term λ‖e‖² balancing encoder fitting against generalization. Sampling integrates the learned velocity field via 50 Euler steps from the noisy latent to the target.

## Results

On the CWA weather downscaling task (25 km → 2 km), AFM achieves the best RMSE for the most stochastic channel (Radar: 4.90 vs. 5.06–5.70 for baselines) and the best CRPS across all non-deterministic variables (Radar 1.78, E.Wind 0.80, N.Wind 0.88). AFM also delivers the best calibration (SSR closest to 1) for Radar (0.44) and E.Wind (0.61). On the synthetic Kolmogorov Flow dataset, AFM consistently outperforms all baselines across misalignment levels τ ∈ {3, 5, 10}, e.g., achieving RMSE 0.73 at τ=3 versus 0.98–1.15 for CFM/CDM/UNet, and 1.09 at τ=10 versus 1.22–1.36.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AFM outperforms CorrDiff (Mardani et al., 2023) on all non-deterministic channels: Radar RMSE 4.90 vs. 5.08, E.Wind CRPS 0.80 vs. 0.85, N.Wind CRPS 0.88 vs. 0.95. However, CorrDiff retains an edge on the most deterministic variable (Temperature: RMSE 0.83 vs. AFM's 1.00), which the authors note can be closed by increasing AFM's regularization λ at the cost of degrading stochastic channels. Against Conditional Flow Matching (CFM, Lipman et al., 2022), AFM wins on RMSE for Radar (4.90 vs. 5.06), E.Wind (1.44 vs. 1.45), and N.Wind (1.61 vs. 1.61, tied), and on CRPS for all three non-deterministic variables. Against Conditional Diffusion Models (CDM, Batzolis et al., 2021), AFM shows the largest margins: Radar RMSE 4.90 vs. 5.70 and CRPS 1.78 vs. 2.39, indicating CDM struggles most with spatially misaligned, multi-channel weather data. On the Kolmogorov Flow benchmark, AFM's advantage grows with misalignment (τ): at τ=10, AFM achieves RMSE 1.09 vs. 1.22 (CFM) and 1.24 (CDM), while CDM's SSR degrades to 0.76 (over-dispersed) versus AFM's 0.23 (under-dispersed but still best in RMSE/CRPS/MAE).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on Flow Matching (Lipman et al., 2022) for the generative transport mechanism and EDM (Karras et al., 2022) for stable denoising-based training, but departs from the two-stage residual diffusion paradigm of CorrDiff (Mardani et al., 2023) by unifying deterministic encoding and stochastic generation into a single end-to-end objective. The key conceptual departure is the adaptive noise scaling derived from a maximum-likelihood estimate of the encoder's RMSE, which dynamically balances how much uncertainty the encoder must explain versus how much the flow must generate. Unlike standard conditional diffusion/flow models that assume pixel-aligned inputs and outputs, AFM explicitly handles spatially misaligned PDE solutions and channel synthesis (generating variables absent from the input), positioning it as a purpose-built framework for physical super-resolution rather than a general image-to-image translation method.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that the two-stage regression-then-diffusion approach (CorrDiff) suffers from a fundamental overfitting propagation problem: as the regression stage overfits, the residual distribution narrows, starving the diffusion model of the variability it needs to model, leading to under-calibrated ensembles. AFM's end-to-end training with adaptive per-channel noise scaling addresses this in a principled way, and the finding that a 60-parameter 1×1 convolutional encoder with adaptive σz outperforms a 12M-parameter UNet encoder suggests that the noise scaling mechanism, not encoder capacity, is the critical ingredient. Open questions include convergence guarantees under the adaptive noise schedule, extension to unpaired misaligned data, and whether the ML-derived σz generalizes to domains with non-Gaussian residual structure. The practical impact for atmospheric downscaling (25 km → 2 km) is significant for local disaster planning, though the method's applicability beyond weather remains to be demonstrated.
