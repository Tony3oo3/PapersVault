## Problem

Value-aware model learning losses such as MuZero and IterVAML have been widely adopted in model-based RL but lack theoretical analysis of their calibration properties. This paper shows that when applied to stochastic environment models, these losses are minimum-uncalibrated: they systematically select lower-variance models and biased value functions rather than recovering the true transition kernel and value function, even with adequate data coverage.

## Input / Output

The method consumes environment state vectors, action sequences, and scalar rewards, encoding observations into a 512-dimensional latent space via a shared encoder. It produces a latent dynamics model (deterministic feed-forward or diagonal Gaussian), a value function head, and policy actions generated by MPC search over the learned model. The loss is computed by comparing m-step model rollouts against (m+b)-step environment trajectories, with the calibration correction requiring at least k≥2 model samples per state to estimate the variance term.

## Method

The paper unifies MuZero and IterVAML into the (m,b)-VAML family and proves that all sample-based variants are minimum-uncalibrated for stochastic models due to an irreducible variance term in the loss decomposition. To fix this, it introduces CVAML, which subtracts an empirical variance estimate computed from multiple model samples, yielding a calibrated surrogate that recovers the correct model in expectation. For value function learning, the paper proves that the MuZero loss (b≥1) is uncalibrated even with a perfect model, necessitating a separate model-free or model-based TD loss for per-state value accuracy. Additionally, it proves the existence of deterministic value-equivalent models in stochastic environments under standard topological assumptions, validating the common practice of using deterministic latent dynamics while showing stochastic models can still offer empirical benefits.

## Results

On Garnet MDPs, the calibrated CVAML loss reliably achieves the lowest value prediction MSE across all stochasticity levels, while uncalibrated (1,1)-VAML fails to learn correct value functions even in deterministic environments due to high-entropy initialization. On DMC (7 tasks across humanoid and dog domains), calibrating the (1,1)-VAML (MuZero-style) loss yields a clear performance improvement in the humanoid domain, while the benefit of (1,0)-CVAML over (1,0)-VAML is less pronounced. Probabilistic latent models outperform deterministic ones in the dog domain despite the simulator being deterministic, consistent with prior work on reducing critic exploitation of model errors. The (1,0)-IterVAML variant is shown to be stable with modern latent architectures and auxiliary losses, contradicting earlier claims of instability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is between uncalibrated (m,b)-VAML losses (the standard MuZero and IterVAML formulations) and the proposed calibrated (m,b)-CVAML variant, rather than against external SOTA methods. In the Garnet MDP experiments, CVAML consistently outperforms uncalibrated (1,1)-VAML in value prediction MSE whenever the environment has any stochasticity; in fully deterministic environments, both perform comparably as the softmax nonlinearity allows near-perfect model recovery. In DMC humanoid, the calibrated (1,1)-CVAML clearly outperforms the uncalibrated (1,1)-VAML, as the uncalibrated loss corrupts both model and value function learning simultaneously. In DMC dog, the calibration gap is smaller, and the dominant effect is that stochastic models beat deterministic ones regardless of calibration. The KL-divergence model learning baseline (standard MLE) is outperformed by both VAML and CVAML in stochastic Garnet environments, confirming the value-aware objective's advantage when model capacity is limited.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies the MuZero loss (Schrittwieser et al., 2020) and IterVAML (Farahmand, 2018) into a single (m,b)-VAML family and provides the first systematic theoretical analysis of their calibration as surrogate losses, building on the value-equivalence framework of Grimm et al. (2020, 2021) and the calibration theory of Steinwart & Christmann (2008). It departs from prior work by proving that all sample-based variants are uncalibrated for stochastic model classes, proposing the CVAML variance correction, and resolving the open question (conjectured by Oh et al., 2017) of whether deterministic latent models can suffice for value-equivalent models in stochastic environments. The empirical setup builds on TD-MPC (Hansen et al., 2022) and the data-mixing protocol of Voelcker et al. (2025).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that the widely-used MuZero loss is provably uncalibrated for stochastic models challenges a working assumption in the MBRL community and provides a principled fix (CVAML) that practitioners can adopt with minimal architectural changes. However, the practical impact is somewhat bounded: most current implementations already use deterministic latent models, which the paper proves are theoretically sufficient for value-equivalent models under standard regularity assumptions. A key open question remains that even the calibrated MuZero-style loss (b≥1) only guarantees correct expected values, not per-state value accuracy, meaning a separate model-free or (m=0) value update is still necessary for accurate planning. The paper also leaves open how these results extend to non-compact state spaces, non-continuous kernels, and the interaction with auxiliary losses like BYOL in high-dimensional settings.
