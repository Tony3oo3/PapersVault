## Problem

Retrieval-augmented generation systems suffer from misalignment between independently developed retrievers and LLMs, yet existing solutions either require fine-tuning one of these components (impractical for commercial search engines or resource-intensive for LLMs) or introduce intermediate modules that optimize only a single task in isolation. No prior method holistically optimizes the entire RAG pipeline—query generation, document filtering, and retrieval termination—without modifying either the retriever or the LLM while preserving their original capabilities.

## Input / Output

The system takes a free-form text question as input and produces a text answer as output. A lightweight proxy model (Qwen2-0.5B or Qwen2-1.5B) orchestrates the pipeline by emitting structured decisions—[No Retrieval], [Retrieval]<query>, [Planning], document ID selections, and [Retrieval]<subquery> or [LLM]—while a frozen LLM server (e.g., Qwen2-72B) generates the final answer and, in the multi-step strategy, a one-time roadmap. The proxy never produces the final answer itself; it acts as a compact intermediary that routes information between the retriever and LLM, keeping inference cost around 4.8 seconds per question.

## Method

C-3PO embeds three specialized agents—Reasoning Router (decides retrieval necessity and question complexity), Information Filter (selects LLM-relevant documents), and Decision Maker (controls iterative retrieval loops in the multi-step strategy)—within a single lightweight proxy model, coordinated through three adaptive strategies (Direct Answering, Single-pass, Multi-Step Reasoning). For training, the paper introduces a tree-structured rollout that first deterministically branches over all reasoning strategies at depth 1–2, then stochastically samples K(t) candidate actions per node at deeper levels, constructing a decision tree of multiple trajectories per question. Monte Carlo credit assignment computes each agent node's reward as the expected system-level reward over all reachable leaf nodes, effectively redistributing sampling effort from the question level to the action level. The proxy is trained end-to-end with PPO (using a centralized value function and GAE advantages) after a supervised warm-up phase with rejection-sampled seed data, treating the retriever and LLM as fixed environment components.

## Results

C-3PO achieves an average accuracy of 63.53% (1.5B proxy) and 62.08% (0.5B proxy) across six in-domain QA datasets, substantially outperforming the best baseline (Standard RAG at 51.35%) by over 12 points. The gains are most pronounced on multi-hop reasoning tasks: 2WikiMultiHopQA (+15.5%), HotpotQA (+13.2%), and Musique (+12.9%) over the strongest per-dataset baseline. On the out-of-distribution FreshQA and MultiHop-RAG benchmarks with Google Search as retriever, C-3PO achieves 59.28% average, exceeding the best baseline by 4.22%. On Humanity's Last Exam, C-3PO (5.79%) surpasses its base model Qwen2.5-72B (4.27%) and GPT-4o (2.32%), approaching o1 (7.75%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

C-3PO (1.5B proxy, 63.53% avg) outperforms all ten baselines across the board. Against retriever fine-tuning (REPLUG, 48.05%), C-3PO gains +15.5 points, showing that a lightweight proxy is far more effective than fine-tuning the retriever. Against LLM fine-tuning methods, C-3PO beats Self-RAG-72B (58.33%) by +5.2 points, InstructRAG-72B (56.37%) by +7.2 points, and Auto-RAG-72B (52.34%) by +11.2 points, while using only 1.5B parameters versus 72B. Among intermediate-module methods, C-3PO surpasses QueryRewrite (51.68%) by +11.9 points, SKR-KNN (52.71%) by +10.8 points, SlimPLM (54.33%) by +9.2 points, and Reranker (42.70%) by +20.8 points. Notably, C-3PO achieves these gains with only 0.5B–1.5B additional parameters, compared to 7B–72B for LLM fine-tuning baselines, and without modifying either the retriever or the LLM. On the OOD FreshQA/MultiHop-RAG benchmarks (Table 2), C-3PO maintains its lead over all baselines by 4.22% on average, confirming plug-and-play generalization across unseen retrievers (Google Search) and LLM servers (Qwen2-7B, Llama3.3-70B, GPT-4o-mini).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the three dominant RAG alignment strategies—retriever fine-tuning (REPLUG), LLM fine-tuning (Self-RAG, InstructRAG, Auto-RAG), and single-task intermediate modules (query rewriting, reranking)—by introducing a lightweight multi-agent proxy that simultaneously handles retrieval decision, query generation, and document filtering within one small model. Methodologically, it repurposes cooperative multi-agent reinforcement learning (specifically PPO with GAE) and introduces a tree-structured rollout with Monte Carlo credit assignment to solve the sparse-reward problem inherent in optimizing multiple agents toward a single system-level outcome. The plug-and-play constraint (freezing both retriever and LLM) distinguishes it from prior work that modifies at least one component, while the multi-agent framing goes beyond prior intermediate modules that optimize only one sub-task in isolation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in demonstrating that a 0.5B–1.5B proxy can outperform 7B/72B LLM fine-tuning methods on RAG tasks while remaining swappable across unseen retrievers and LLM servers, which is critical for edge deployment and production systems where retraining is infeasible. Open questions include whether the tree-structured rollout scales to longer reasoning chains (current max depth is 13), how the approach generalizes to multimodal retrieval, and whether the credit assignment mechanism extends to settings with more than three agents or non-deterministic environment transitions. The paper also acknowledges that reliance on external retrieval sources introduces risks of biased or unreliable information propagation in high-stakes applications.
