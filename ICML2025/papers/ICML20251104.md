## Problem

While prior work identified massive values (outliers) in LLM activations and used them to guide quantization, the functional role of these values in model behavior and the architectural reason for their concentration in Q and K (but not V) remained unexplored. This paper asks: what do concentrated massive values in self-attention actually do for the model, and why do they form in the specific pattern observed?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251104/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes standard text prompts fed into RoPE-equipped LLMs (Llama-2/3, Gemma-2, Qwen-2.5, Mistral) and extracts Q, K, V tensors from each attention head at each layer. Massive values are identified by computing the L2 norm along the sequence-length dimension per (head, dim) pair and flagging entries exceeding λ=5 times the per-head mean. Disruption is applied exclusively during the prefilling stage (replacing flagged entries with mean, zero, or min values) to isolate context-processing effects from generative capability. Outputs include accuracy/perplexity/diversity metrics across ten benchmarks split into contextual vs. parametric knowledge categories, plus quantization comparison results.

## Method

The paper defines massive values via a per-head L2-norm threshold and systematically disrupts them (or non-massive values as a control) during the prefilling phase, then measures downstream task performance. The key finding is that disrupting massive values collapses contextual knowledge understanding (GSM8K drops from ~77% to ~4%, passkey retrieval from 100% to 0%) while parametric knowledge retrieval degrades only modestly (15–20%). Quantization experiments confirm this: AWQ and SmoothQuant, which protect massive values, preserve contextual understanding, whereas GPTQ, which does not, degrades it specifically. The root cause is traced to RoPE's exponential frequency decay: low-frequency dimensional pairs (large j) rotate slowly, encode less positional information, and therefore carry predominantly semantic content, causing massive values to concentrate in those dimensions. This concentration is symmetric (mirrored across the two halves of the embedding) due to RoPE's pairwise rotation structure, appears from the very first layer, and is absent in models without RoPE (GPT-2, OPT, Jamba).

## Results

Disrupting massive values in Q and K causes catastrophic degradation on contextual knowledge understanding tasks: GSM8K accuracy drops from 76.90% to 4.00% (Llama3-8B), 81.30% to 15.10% (Gemma2-9B), and 86.60% to 16.10% (Qwen2.5-7B); IMDB sentiment accuracy plummets from ~95% to single digits; and Passkey Retrieval collapses from 100% to 0–9%. In stark contrast, parametric knowledge retrieval tasks (Cities, Sports, Arts, Technology, Celebrity) degrade only modestly (15–20%), and disrupting non-massive values leaves all tasks within ±1% of baseline. Perplexity on IMDB surges from 8.38 to 112.61 for Llama3-8B under massive value disruption, while 2-gram diversity drops from ~0.9 to ~0.1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three quantization methods—AWQ, SmoothQuant, and GPTQ—on Llama3-8B across both contextual and parametric knowledge tasks. AWQ and SmoothQuant, which explicitly protect or redistribute massive values during quantization, maintain strong performance across all benchmarks, preserving contextual understanding capabilities. GPTQ, which does not specifically address massive values, shows significant degradation on contextual knowledge tasks (GSM8K and AQUA dropping to approximately 75% normalized accuracy) while maintaining comparable performance on parametric knowledge retrieval tasks (Cities, Sports, Celebrity). This asymmetry directly mirrors the disruption experiments: methods that preserve massive values retain contextual understanding, while those that do not suffer specifically on context-dependent tasks. The disruption experiments themselves serve as the core comparison: massive value disruption causes 60–95% accuracy drops on contextual tasks (e.g., GSM8K from 76.90% to 4.00%), whereas non-massive value disruption keeps all metrics within ±1% of baseline, confirming that the effect is specific to the concentrated massive value regions rather than a general perturbation artifact.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work identifying massive values (outliers) in LLM activations, building on Sun et al. (2024) who observed them in residual streams and Barbero et al. (2025) who noted their concentration pattern in Q and K. While prior quantization research (AWQ, SmoothQuant, GPTQ) treated massive values as a numerical nuisance to suppress or protect, this paper provides the first functional characterization: massive values in Q and K are causally linked to contextual knowledge understanding rather than parametric knowledge retrieval. The paper also departs from the RoPE literature (Su et al., 2024) by showing that RoPE's low-frequency dimensions, previously understood as encoding long-range position, actually carry rich semantic content that is critical for in-context reasoning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that RoPE's low-frequency dimensions encode semantics (not just position) reframes how we think about positional encoding's role in LLMs and suggests that NoPE or alternative encodings may need to explicitly preserve these semantic channels. Practically, this provides a mechanistic justification for why quantization methods that protect massive values (AWQ, SmoothQuant) outperform those that don't (GPTQ) on reasoning tasks, guiding future compression strategies. Open questions include whether the massive-value concentration can be eliminated through RoPE redesign without sacrificing contextual understanding, and whether the knowledge-conflict result (disrupted massive values → model ignores contradictory context) can be leveraged for robustness against prompt injection or misinformation.
