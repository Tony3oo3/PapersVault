## Problem

Existing trajectory imputation methods assume that even sparsely sampled trajectories retain essential movement patterns, imposing strict requirements on data acquisition density and often relying on predefined graph structures. This paper tackles the more challenging setting of imputing an entire trajectory from only its two endpoints, leveraging large-scale unlabeled trajectory data to capture macro-level human movement patterns that enable reconstruction under such minimal information constraints.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250212/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Given a trajectory window of fixed length k (3–10 points), the model receives only the first and last spatio-temporal coordinates (timestamp, longitude, latitude) as conditioning signals, with all k−2 intermediate points masked out. The output is the full sequence of k imputed points, each containing time, longitude, and latitude. Coordinates are normalized to [0,1] for the diffusion process and rescaled back to geographic units after inference; the haversine distance is used to evaluate spatial accuracy at multiple thresholds (2 km to 10 km).

## Method

ProDiff jointly trains a 1D-UNet denoising diffusion model with a Prototype Condition Extractor (PCE) under a unified loss. The PCE encodes each trajectory window into a representation via summed point encodings, then projects it into a set of N_p learned prototypes; at inference, the endpoint query is matched against prototypes via a distance-weighted combination to produce a prototype condition. This prototype condition is fused with the base endpoint condition (obtained by masking) through a Wide & Deep network to form a joint conditioning signal that guides the reverse diffusion SDE. Training optimizes three objectives simultaneously: the standard diffusion noise-prediction loss, a K-means classification consistency loss that aligns prototype assignments with cluster pseudo-labels, and a contrastive margin loss that enforces prototype separation in embedding space. A theoretical result (Theorem 3.4) shows that at the global optimum, prototypes approximate conditional expectations over manifold regions while the contrastive term forces them into distinct directions, ensuring diverse movement-pattern coverage.

## Results

On WuXi with window size k=4, ProDiff achieves 71.55% trajectory coverage at TC@2k and 95.20% at TC@10k, outperforming the strongest baseline DiffTraj (69.58% / 94.02%) by 1.97% and 1.18% respectively. On FourSquare (k=4), ProDiff reaches 66.44% TC@2k and 85.96% TC@10k, improving over DiffTraj by 6.99% and 4.07%. The advantage widens at larger window sizes: at k=8 on WuXi, ProDiff gains 3.34% over DiffTraj at TC@2k (57.52% vs 54.18%), and at k=10 the gap reaches 0.02% at TC@2k but 3.61% at TC@10k (90.53% vs 86.92%). Overall, ProDiff improves accuracy by 6.28% on FourSquare and 2.52% on WuXi relative to the best prior method.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ProDiff outperforms all nine baselines across every window size (k=4,6,8,10) and threshold (TC@2k–TC@10k) on both datasets. The closest competitor is DiffTraj (Zhu et al., 2024a), a diffusion-based GPS trajectory generator; ProDiff exceeds it by 1.97% at TC@2k and 1.18% at TC@10k on WuXi (k=4), and by 6.99% at TC@2k on FourSquare (k=4), where the gap is largest. Traditional time-series methods (VAR, SAITS, TimesNet) and the diffusion time-series model Diff-TS trail significantly, with TC@2k scores below 0.52 on WuXi (k=4) versus ProDiff's 0.7155. Trajectory-specific baselines (RNTrajRec, TS-TrajGen, MM-STGED, AttnMove) are compared on spatial distribution metrics (Density, Distance, Segment Distance, Radius, MAE, RMSE) in Fig. 4, where ProDiff leads on all six. The ablation variant Diff+Mask (removing the prototype condition extractor) drops 5.71% at TC@2k on WuXi (k=4), confirming that the prototype learning component is the primary source of ProDiff's advantage over a plain masked diffusion model.

## Relation to Prior Work

ProDiff builds directly on the diffusion-model trajectory generation line (DiffTraj, Diffusion-TS) and extends it with a prototype-based conditioning mechanism borrowed from self-supervised representation learning. It departs from prior trajectory imputation methods (SAITS, TimesNet, RNTrajRec, MM-STGED) by relaxing the assumption that sparse trajectories must retain intermediate movement patterns, instead requiring only two endpoints as input. The joint training of a denoising diffusion model with a prototype condition extractor—coupled via a Wide & Deep network and a unified loss—differs from multi-stage pipelines that train components independently, and the theoretical analysis connecting prototype learning to manifold approximation is novel in this trajectory context.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The minimal-information framing (only two endpoints) addresses a practically important regime where device limitations or privacy constraints prevent collection of intermediate GPS points, and the prototype mechanism shows that macro-level movement patterns from large-scale unlabeled data can substitute for fine-grained trajectory information. Limitations include evaluation on only two datasets, performance degradation under random (non-endpoint) point selection, and the computational cost of diffusion sampling (mitigated but not eliminated by DDIM). Open questions include whether the approach personalizes to individual movement styles, how it scales to multi-city or cross-modal trajectories, and whether the prototype count and window size can be adapted dynamically per trajectory.
