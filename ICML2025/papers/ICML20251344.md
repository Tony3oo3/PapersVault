## Problem

Existing driving language datasets predominantly capture proximity-based interactions (e.g., spatial blocking, safe-distance keeping) while largely omitting traffic rule-induced and human intention-induced interactions that are critical for safety-critical decision-making. This gap, combined with the limited scale of prior datasets, prevents LLMs fine-tuned on them from reliably reasoning about non-proximal interactions such as stop-sign yielding or overtaking, and from supporting multi-modal fine-tuning across scene description, prediction, and planning tasks.

## Input / Output

The dataset construction pipeline takes raw WOMD trajectories and HD map data, translates them into ego-centric natural-language descriptions via a rule-based program, and feeds those descriptions to GPT-4 Turbo with a structured 4-part prompt to generate Q&A pairs. The resulting 3M Q&As are organized in a factual-to-reasoning chain: map environment, ego/other-agent motion status questions precede interaction and intention questions, with the former serving as CoT context for the latter. For Motion-LLaVA inference, the input is encoded motion vectors (via a pre-trained MultiPath++ encoder) plus a text question; the output is a free-form text answer. Agent IDs are remapped into fixed ranges (0–99 vehicles, 100–199 bicycles, 200–299 pedestrians) to prevent overfitting to specific agents.

## Method

WOMD-Reasoning is built by an automated two-stage pipeline: a rule-based translator converts WOMD motion data into structured ego-centric language, and GPT-4 Turbo is prompted with a responsibility chain (Q1: traffic-rule yielding, Q2: vehicle-pedestrian conflicts, Q3: intention patterns like overtaking/following) to systematically identify and describe interactions, achieving ~90% human-verified accuracy across 63k scenes. Motion-LLaVA adapts LLaVA-v1.5-7b by replacing the vision encoder with a MultiPath++ motion vector encoder and projector layer, trained in a single unfrozen stage (ablations show that LLaVA's two-stage projector alignment and encoder freezing degrade performance). At inference, a Chain-of-Thought strategy first elicits factual answers (map, ego, other-agent status), aggregates them as context, then conditions reasoning questions on that context to reduce hallucination. For downstream trajectory prediction, Motion-LLaVA's interaction answers are encoded by T5-XXL + MLP and injected into MultiPath++ via a cross-attention block after the Other Agents History Encoder, yielding 7% improvements in minFDE and MR.

## Results

On the WOMD-Reasoning validation set (~1,000 QA pairs), Motion-LLaVA achieves a GPT Score of 7.02 across all questions, outperforming fine-tuned LLaVA with BEV input (6.88) and dramatically exceeding non-fine-tuned LLaVA (2.31). On factual questions, Motion-LLaVA reaches 56.2% accuracy within 1.0 m tolerance (Acc₁.₀) with a median error of 1.0 m, compared to only 10.9% and 5.0 m for fine-tuned LLaVA. For interaction reasoning questions, Motion-LLaVA scores 6.76 GPT versus 6.14 for fine-tuned LLaVA and 0.91 for the non-fine-tuned baseline. In a downstream trajectory prediction task, incorporating Motion-LLaVA's language outputs into MultiPath++ reduces minFDE₆ by 7.35% (1.27→1.18) and MR₆ by 7.10% (12.59→11.69).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Motion-LLaVA consistently outperforms all baselines across every metric and question category. Against non-fine-tuned LLaVA (GPT Score 2.31), LLaMA-Adapter v2.1 (1.62), ViTA-1.5 (1.77), and Qwen2.5-VL (2.36), the gap is dramatic, confirming that off-the-shelf VLMs cannot answer driving-specific questions without domain fine-tuning. Compared to fine-tuned LLaVA using BEV plots as vision input, Motion-LLaVA achieves a modest but consistent edge on language metrics (GPT Score 7.02 vs. 6.88 overall; 6.76 vs. 6.14 on reasoning questions), but a substantial advantage on numerical factual accuracy (56.2% vs. 10.9% Acc₁.₀), which the authors attribute to information loss when converting vectorized motion data into a 2D BEV plot. Against fine-tuned LLaMA-Adapter v2.1 (GPT Score 5.14), Motion-LLaVA leads by ~1.9 points, validating the benefit of its motion-prediction-pretrained encoder and single-stage unfrozen training strategy. In the trajectory prediction experiment, adding Motion-LLaVA's interaction language outputs to MultiPath++ yields a 7.35% reduction in minFDE₆ and 7.10% reduction in MR₆, demonstrating the dataset's utility beyond language generation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the VLM-for-driving line initiated by DriveGPT4 (Chen et al., 2023) and LLaVA (Liu et al., 2024) by replacing the vision encoder with a motion vector encoder pre-trained via MultiPath++ trajectory prediction, enabling direct consumption of vectorized motion data. Conceptually, it departs from prior driving language datasets (BDD-X, DriveLM, nuScenes-QA) that predominantly capture proximity-induced interactions, by systematically including traffic rule-induced and human intention-induced interactions through an automated GPT-4-based curation pipeline. The trajectory prediction experiment further builds on MultiPath++ by inserting a cross-attention module that injects Motion-LLaVA's language outputs into per-agent features, bridging the gap between language reasoning and numerical prediction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

WOMD-Reasoning is the largest real-world driving language dataset to date (3M Q&As across 63k scenes, 6–113× larger than prior work), and its focus on non-proximate, rule-based interactions addresses a gap that limits current LLM-based driving agents' safety-critical decision-making. The ~90% human-verified accuracy of a fully automated pipeline (rule-based translation + GPT-4) demonstrates a scalable path to large-scale interaction annotation without prohibitive human labeling costs. Key limitations include the reliance on simulated (MetaDrive/ScenarioNet) vision rather than real camera data, the ~10% error rate in GPT-4-generated labels, and the fact that the 7% FDE improvement in trajectory prediction, while consistent, is modest. Open questions include whether the simulated vision modality transfers to real sensor data and whether GPT-4's reasoning biases propagate systematically into downstream models.
