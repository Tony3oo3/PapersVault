## Problem

Existing RL algorithms for LTL policy synthesis over unknown MDPs provide only asymptotic convergence guarantees, offering no finite-time insight into how close the current policy is to optimal. No regret-free online algorithm existed for LTL specifications, and prior regret-free methods (e.g., UCRL2) require communicating MDPs, a condition violated by the product of an MDP with an LTL automaton. This paper closes the gap by providing the first regret-free online algorithm with O(√K) regret for infinite-horizon LTL synthesis on non-communicating MDPs.

## Input / Output

The algorithm receives the MDP's state and action sets, a fixed initial state, an LTL formula φ, a confidence parameter δ ∈ (0,1), and a lower bound p_min on non-zero transition probabilities. It outputs a sequence of deterministic positional policies (one per episode) over the product MDP, an episode-specific deadline H_k computed from the optimistic MDP's substochastic matrix, and a high-probability regret bound R(K) = O(√K). A separate polynomial-time graph-learning procedure (Alg. 5) runs once to identify the MDP's transition graph using p_min, after which the main regret-free loop operates without further structural discovery.

## Method

The core algorithm (ZeroReg) operates episodically under optimism in the face of uncertainty: each episode constructs an interval MDP (iMDP) from collected transitions with confidence bounds β_k(s,a), then computes an optimistic MDP and policy via Extended Value Iteration (EVI) that maximizes reach-avoid probability over the uncertainty set using an InnerMax subroutine. A key mechanism is the episode deadline H_k, defined as the smallest n such that ‖Q̃_k^n‖_∞ ≤ k^{-1/q}, where Q̃_k is a substochastic matrix over non-goal states with an artificial reset transition from bad states B back to s_init; this reset handles non-communicating MDPs. Episodes are partitioned into fast (goal reached before deadline) and slow (deadline exceeded), and the proof shows both categories contribute sublinearly to total regret via martingale concentration (Azuma-Hoeffding) and logarithmic bounds on maximum episode length. For general LTL, the specification is compiled to a deterministic Rabin automaton, the product MDP is formed, accepting MECs are identified as goal set G× and non-accepting MECs as bad set B×, reducing the problem to the reach-avoid case solved by ZeroReg.

## Results

The main theoretical result is a regret bound of R(K) = O(√K) for both reach-avoid (Theorem 4.2) and general LTL specifications (Theorem 5.1), with the detailed bound in Eq. (9) depending on the maximum episode length α_K, which grows only logarithmically in K (Lemma 4.4). Empirically, on a gridworld with l=6 (|S|=17, |A|=4, δ=0.1), the normalized regret R(K)/K drops rapidly within the first few episodes, indicating near-optimal policy discovery. In the sample complexity comparison (Figure 6), the proposed algorithm's k*_reg(0.1, 0.1) is tighter than ω-PAC's k*_PAC(0.1, 0.1) across all grid sizes from l=4 to l=16, with the gap widening for larger instances.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against ω-PAC (Perez et al., 2024), the only existing method providing guaranteed policy synthesis for infinite-horizon LTL specifications without a generative model. Empirically (Figure 4, l=4 gridworld), the proposed regret-free algorithm converges to near-zero normalized regret significantly faster than ω-PAC, which the authors attribute to using intermediate confidence bounds rather than waiting for a fixed sample threshold before updating the policy. Theoretically (Figure 6), the proposed algorithm's sample complexity bound k*_reg(0.1, 0.1) is tighter than ω-PAC's k*_PAC(0.1, 0.1) for all grid sizes l=4 through l=16, with the advantage growing as the state space increases. The paper also contrasts with UCRL2 (Auer et al., 2008), which requires communicating MDPs and is inapplicable here since the product MDP is generally non-communicating, and with Tarbouriech et al. (2020), which is limited to strictly positive costs and achieves only O(K^{2/3}) regret versus the O(√K) bound of the proposed method.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the optimism-in-face-of-uncertainty paradigm from UCRL2 (Auer et al., 2008) and the non-communicating MDP regret framework of Tarbouriech et al. (2020) to the setting of LTL specification synthesis. The key departure is a reset mechanism that handles non-communicating MDPs—critical because the product of an MDP with a DRA for the LTL formula is generally non-communicating, rendering UCRL2-style algorithms inapplicable. For the graph-learning component, the paper adapts the sample complexity machinery from Voloshin et al. (2022) and the ω-PAC reachability policy synthesis of Perez et al. (2024), but operates without requiring a generative model. The reduction from general LTL to reach-avoid via MEC identification on the product MDP is a standard model-checking technique, but its integration into a regret-free online learning loop is new.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first algorithm to provide finite-time (O(√K)) regret guarantees for LTL policy synthesis on unknown MDPs, closing a gap where prior methods offered only asymptotic convergence or PAC bounds that scale poorly with horizon. The ability to handle non-communicating MDPs is practically essential since product MDPs with temporal-logic automata are almost always non-communicating. Limitations include the assumption of a known minimum transition probability p_min (without which full LTL is not PAC-learnable per Yang et al., 2022), the fixed initial state requirement, and empirical evaluation restricted to a single small gridworld. Open questions include whether the O(√K) rate is tight, whether the approach extends to discounted or average-reward LTL objectives, and how the algorithm scales to larger state spaces where the product MDP explodes combinatorially.
