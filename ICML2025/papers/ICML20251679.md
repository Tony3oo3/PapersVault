## Problem

Backpropagation, the standard training algorithm for deep networks, is biologically implausible due to three requirements: symmetric forward/backward weights, a global error signal propagated through all layers, and strict temporal separation of forward and backward passes. Existing alternatives (feedback alignment, predictive coding, target propagation, Hebbian learning, forward-forward, equilibrium propagation, etc.) either violate one or more of these criteria or fail to converge on non-trivial architectures. No prior algorithm simultaneously satisfies all three biological plausibility criteria while achieving competitive accuracy on standard benchmarks.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard inputs across modalities: 28×28 grayscale images (MNIST, FashionMNIST), 32×32 RGB images (SVHN, CIFAR-10), one-hot encoded character sequences (Harry Potter), and multivariate time-series vectors (Electricity, Metr-la, Pems-bay). Outputs are 10-class labels, next-character predictions, or continuous time-series forecasts. A distinctive structural detail is that each neuron's input is spatially partitioned: the sensory signal enters the basal dendrite while the expected (top-down) value enters the apical dendrite, with the local error computed in the soma—mirroring the compartmental organization of cortical pyramidal neurons.

## Method

DLL models each neuron as a three-compartment pyramidal cell (basal dendrite, apical dendrite, soma) and defines a local error ξ = x − u within the soma, where u is the bottom-up sensory input and x is the top-down expected value. To satisfy weight asymmetry (C1), a separate trainable matrix Θ replaces the transposed forward weight Wᵀ when propagating the expected value backward, so forward and backward paths use genuinely different parameters. To satisfy non-two-stage training (C3), the spatial separation of u and x within a single cell allows feedforward and feedback signals to coexist without strict temporal ordering. The total loss is the sum of squared local errors across all layers, and both W and Θ are updated simultaneously via local gradient rules derived from this loss. The algorithm is instantiated for MLPs, CNNs, and RNNs (with a detailed BPTT-style derivation for the recurrent case), and ablations confirm that updating Θ (rather than fixing it as in feedback alignment) is critical for convergence on sequential tasks.

## Results

On image classification, DLL-CNNs achieve 98.87% on MNIST, 90.88% on FashionMNIST, 85.81% on SVHN, and 70.89% on CIFAR-10 (86.61% average), approaching backpropagation's 90.67% average while satisfying all three biological plausibility criteria. Among algorithms meeting all criteria (C1–C3), DLL outperforms the next best (Forward-Forward MLPs at 66.12% avg) by over 20 percentage points on the CNN average. On sequential tasks, DLL-RNNs achieve 33.7% prediction accuracy on Harry Potter and competitive MSE on time-series forecasting (e.g., 0.172 on Electricity vs. 0.175 for BP), though backpropagation retains a clear lead on text prediction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DLL is the only algorithm satisfying all three biological plausibility criteria (asymmetric weights, local error, non-two-stage training) while converging reliably across both MLP and CNN architectures. On CNNs, DLL (86.61% avg) substantially outperforms other all-criteria methods: Hebbian Learning (57.43%), R-STDP (62.29%), Forward-Forward (10.67%, fails to converge on CNNs), and Equilibrium Propagation (18.50%, also fails on CNNs). Compared to backpropagation (90.67% avg on CNNs), DLL trails by ~4 percentage points on average, with the largest gap on SVHN (85.81% vs. 95.35%) and CIFAR-10 (70.89% vs. 75.10%), but narrows to under 1% on MNIST and FashionMNIST. Among partial-criteria methods, DLL-CNNs outperform Feedback Alignment (84.75%), Local Losses (89.64%), and Predictive Coding (89.72%) on SVHN and CIFAR-10, though Local Losses and Predictive Coding remain slightly ahead on MNIST and FashionMNIST. On sequential tasks, DLL-RNNs trail backpropagation on Harry Potter prediction (33.7% vs. 51.9%) but achieve competitive or superior MSE on Electricity (0.172 vs. 0.175), while Predictive Coding remains the strongest bio-plausible baseline on text prediction (38.8%).

## Relation to Prior Work

DLL builds directly on the three-compartment pyramidal neuron model from Sacramento et al. (2018) and the segregated-dendrite framework of Guerguiev et al. (2017), but departs from both by proposing a concrete, trainable learning rule rather than a conceptual explanation. It positions itself against a broad family of biologically plausible algorithms (feedback alignment, predictive coding, target propagation, Hebbian/STDP, forward-forward, equilibrium propagation) by being the first to simultaneously satisfy all three plausibility criteria—weight asymmetry, local error, and non-two-stage training—while maintaining competitive accuracy. The introduction of a separate trainable backward weight Θ (analogous to feedback alignment's random matrix but learned) is the key mechanistic departure from prior dendritic models that either fixed or transposed weights.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The systematic empirical benchmark of nine biologically plausible algorithms under identical architectures and datasets is itself a useful contribution, as prior reviews were largely qualitative. DLL demonstrates that the performance gap between biologically plausible learning and backpropagation can be narrowed substantially (e.g., 86.6% vs 90.7% average on CNNs), suggesting the field's assumption that plausibility necessarily entails large accuracy loss may be too pessimistic. However, the gap remains non-trivial on harder datasets (CIFAR-10: 70.9% vs 75.1%), the method is untested on SNNs or neuromorphic hardware, and the reliance on unsigned error signals and sensitive learning-rate tuning limits immediate practical adoption. Open questions include whether DLL scales to deeper/wider networks, whether the Θ mechanism generalizes to attention-based architectures, and how it performs under the discrete, sparse dynamics of spiking networks.
