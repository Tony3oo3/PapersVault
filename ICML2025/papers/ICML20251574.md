## Problem

Existing human trajectory recovery methods (RNN, attention-based) produce single deterministic imputations that cannot capture the inherent uncertainty of sparse mobility data, and they struggle to jointly model complex spatial transitions and temporal periodicity. While conditional diffusion models have shown promise for continuous-valued time-series imputation, they cannot be directly applied to trajectory recovery because the imputation targets are discrete location IDs rather than continuous numerical values.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251574/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a current-day trajectory (a sequence of discrete location IDs where missing slots are marked null) together with the user's historical trajectories from the preceding J−1 days, all represented as sequences of location IDs over 30-minute time slots. It produces recovered discrete location IDs for each missing slot by generating M stochastic embedding samples via the reverse diffusion process, averaging them, and decoding through an inner-product match against the learned embedding table to yield a per-location likelihood vector; the argmax location is selected as the final recovery.

## Method

DiffMove performs conditional diffusion denoising in a learned embedding space rather than in the discrete ID space: locations are first mapped to dense vectors by a shared embedding table, Gaussian noise is added to the target (missing) embeddings, and a denoising network iteratively removes the noise conditioned on observed and historical information. The Conditional Embedding Denoiser comprises three blocks: a Spatial Conditional Block that uses a diffusion-time-step-gated GNN (TGGNN) on per-trajectory transition graphs to capture spatial transition patterns and a cross-attention mechanism over current and historical trajectories to capture periodicity; a Target Conditional Block that encodes the one-hot target mask, sinusoidal temporal-length embeddings, and a pooled historical trajectory embedding; and a Denoising Network Block built on a temporal transformer with residual layers and gated activations. Training is self-supervised (random masking of observed locations) with a joint multi-task objective combining the diffusion noise-prediction MSE, a cross-entropy loss on the decoded location likelihoods, and an L2 regularization on embedding norms.

## Results

On Geolife, DiffMove achieves Recall@1 of 0.5173, Recall@10 of 0.8578, Distance@1 of 2799 m, and MAP of 0.6407, outperforming the best baseline TRILL by 9.57% in Recall@1, 19.66% in Distance@1, and 7.05% in MAP. On Foursquare, DiffMove achieves Recall@1 of 0.3600, Recall@10 of 0.6876, Distance@1 of 4271 m, and MAP of 0.4756, improving over TRILL by 11.56% in Recall@1, 7.93% in Distance@1, and 9.56% in MAP. The average Recall improvement over the best baseline across both datasets is approximately 11%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiffMove outperforms all eight baselines on every metric across both datasets. Rule-based methods (Markov, PMF) and RNN-based methods (LSTM, BiLSTM) are substantially outperformed, with Recall@1 gaps exceeding 20 percentage points on Geolife. Among deep learning baselines, AttnMove (Recall@1: 0.3920 Geolife / 0.2975 Foursquare) and PeriodicMove (0.4199 / 0.3125) capture sequential and periodic patterns but fall 6–8% behind DiffMove in Recall@1. The strongest baseline, TRILL (GCN-based, Recall@1: 0.4721 Geolife / 0.3227 Foursquare), is surpassed by DiffMove by 9.57% and 11.56% in Recall@1 respectively, and by 19.66% and 7.93% in Distance@1, confirming that the embedding-space conditional diffusion framework with spatial and target conditioning modules provides a meaningful advantage over deterministic GCN-based recovery.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the conditional diffusion framework of CSDI (Tashiro et al., 2021) from continuous time-series imputation to discrete location trajectory recovery, using an embedding-space denoising strategy to bridge the gap between continuous diffusion and discrete location IDs. It departs from the dominant deterministic trajectory recovery paradigm (AttnMove, PeriodicMove, TRILL) by introducing a generative, probabilistic formulation, and from prior diffusion-based trajectory work (DiffTraj, TrajGDM) by targeting discrete sparse location recovery rather than continuous GPS generation. The TGGNN component is a novel architectural contribution that fuses diffusion-step awareness into gated graph message passing for transition pattern learning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By framing trajectory recovery as a generative task, DiffMove opens the door to uncertainty-aware imputation and multi-sample inference for downstream applications like POI recommendation and ride-sharing, which deterministic methods cannot support. However, the evaluation is limited to random masking on two relatively small datasets (Tokyo, Beijing) with a fixed 30-minute interval, and the authors themselves note that structured missingness patterns (e.g., persistent device failures) remain unaddressed. The embedding-space diffusion trick is a reusable recipe for applying continuous generative models to discrete ID-based imputation problems more broadly, but its practical impact will depend on whether it scales to larger, real-world deployment settings.
