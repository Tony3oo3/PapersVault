## Problem

Converting pre-trained Softmax-based LLMs into linear-complexity models remains difficult because existing linear attention methods (e.g., MetaLA) capture only low-frequency QK information while recursive sparse attention (e.g., GSA) captures only high-frequency information, and neither efficiently expands the fixed-size memory state to reduce approximation error. Training linear models from scratch is prohibitively expensive, so the key challenge is to inherit Softmax weights with minimal fine-tuning while closing the functional gap to full attention.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard text token sequences and produces contextual representations for next-token prediction, identical in interface to a standard Transformer LLM. The critical difference is that during inference, the growing KV cache is replaced by fixed-size hidden states of shape m×d_k and m×d_v (where m is the memory capacity), yielding O(1) memory per step regardless of sequence length. In the MVA-SW variant, a small sliding window of raw K and V tokens (size equal to head dimension, 128) is additionally retained to preserve local attention fidelity, keeping total added complexity at O(nd²).

## Method

MVA unifies MetaLA (low-frequency branch) and GSA (high-frequency branch) under a High-order QK Integration Theory derived from the Taylor expansion of e^{QK^T}, where each branch fits complementary frequency bands of the attention map. A Multi-level Vocabulary Decomposition mechanism recursively compresses the residual error from KV compression into additional state levels, achieving exponential error decay (bounded by ∏εᵢγᵢ) and polynomial memory capacity growth. The full MVA-SW variant soft-integrates the linear MVA output with a sliding-window Softmax attention output using their respective attention scores as balancing weights (β_c, β_h), enabling faithful preservation of both historical and local information. Training uses LoRA fine-tuning of pre-trained Mistral-7B (rank 8–256 on QKV and FFN down_proj) with as few as 100M tokens for MVA-SW or 10B tokens for standalone MVA, recovering 99% of the original model's benchmark performance.

## Results

MVA fine-tuned from Mistral-7B with 10B tokens achieves an average score of 60.3 across 8 benchmarks, surpassing GSA (57.7) trained with 20B tokens on every individual metric, including MMLU (34.4 vs. 32.4) and ARC-Challenge (47.5 vs. 43.9). With the sliding window variant (MVA-SW), the model recovers 99% of Mistral-7B's original performance (66.1 vs. 66.6 average) using fewer than 100M fine-tuning tokens, outperforming GSA-SW (65.3) and GLA-SW (54.1) under the same budget. On long-context tasks (Qasper, NarrativeQA, QMSum), MVA with 20B tokens scores 20.7/20.4/9.58, exceeding GSA (18.8/19.2/10.0) on Qasper and NarrativeQA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MVA outperforms GSA (the strongest baseline) on all 8 benchmarks with only 10B tokens versus GSA's 20B, gaining +2.4 on ARC-e, +3.6 on ARC-c, +1.6 on HellaSwag, +1.8 on PIQA, +2.0 on Winogrande, +2.5 on NQ, +5.2 on TriviaQA, and +2.0 on MMLU. Against GLA (20B tokens), MVA leads by +3.7 average points, with particularly large gaps on NQ (+3.7) and TriviaQA (+8.1). RetNet and SUPRA trail significantly, with RetNet scoring 51.9 average and SUPRA achieving only 28.0 on MMLU, both far below MVA's 60.3/34.4. In the sliding-window setting (Table 1), MVA-SW with 0.1B tokens reaches 66.1 average, beating GSA-SW (65.3) and dramatically outperforming GLA-SW (54.1). Against LoLCATs (Table 3), MVA-SW matches or exceeds its performance on all tasks using half the fine-tuning tokens (20M vs. 40M), with a +1.2 point edge on MMLU (52.6 vs. 51.4).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate families of linear attention methods—MetaLA (low-frequency, fixed-polynomial Q over multi-frequency K) and GSA (high-frequency, varying-frequency Q over fixed K)—under a Taylor-series-based 'High-order QK Integration Theory' that explains their complementarity in terms of frequency-band information collection. It extends the Delta Rule memory mechanism into a Multi-level Vocabulary Decomposition that recursively compresses residual errors to exponentially expand effective memory capacity, and introduces a soft attention-score-based integration of sliding window attention that departs from the hard concatenation used in LoLCATs. The entire framework is expressed in GLA operator notation, making it a direct architectural extension of the GLA/MetaLA/GSA lineage rather than a new model family.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that pretrained 7B Softmax models can be converted to linear-complexity models retaining ~99% performance with under 100M fine-tuning tokens, substantially lowering the barrier to deploying efficient long-context inference. The theoretical frequency-band decomposition provides a principled lens for understanding why hybrid linear+sparse attention works, which could guide future architecture design beyond the specific MVA formulation. Open questions include whether the multi-level decomposition's exponential memory gain holds at scale (the paper only tests 2 levels), whether per-head parameters (currently shared) would further close the gap, and whether the approach generalizes beyond Mistral/Qwen to other architectures. The slightly higher generation latency versus GSA due to vocabulary decomposition is a practical trade-off that may limit adoption in latency-sensitive settings.
