## Problem

Existing RoPE rescaling methods (YaRN, NTK, LongRoPE) fail to achieve effective target context lengths because they rely on a theoretical critical dimension that does not account for insufficient training in higher RoPE dimensions, which extends their empirical rotation periods beyond theoretical predictions. Additionally, context window extension consistently degrades short-context performance, requiring costly multi-stage mid-training (e.g., 800B tokens for LLaMA3.1) to recover.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252150/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained LLM with known RoPE parameters (base θ, head dimension d, pre-trained context window L_train) and a target context length L (e.g., 128k), along with ~10B tokens of mid-training data spanning 8k–200k sequence lengths. It produces an extended-context LLM that employs dual RoPE at inference: the original RoPE for inputs within the short context window and the rescaled RoPE for longer sequences, with a one-time KV cache recomputation triggered at the switch point. The rescaling factors are per-dimension (one per cosine dimension i = 0, …, d/2−1), determined before fine-tuning.

## Method

LongRoPE2 identifies that higher RoPE dimensions receive insufficient training (covering only a small fraction of their period within the pre-trained window), which extends their effective rotation periods and shifts the true critical dimension earlier than the theoretical d_tcd. To find the correct critical dimension d_rcd and optimal per-dimension scaling factors, it runs an evolutionary search guided by a needle-driven PPL metric that evaluates perplexity only on answer tokens in synthetic needle-in-a-haystack documents, avoiding dilution by irrelevant tokens. For dimensions below d_rcd, NTK scaling is applied with an adjusted base; for dimensions at and above d_rcd, the search optimizes factors under a monotonic non-decreasing constraint. Finally, mixed context window training fine-tunes the model simultaneously on short sequences (original RoPE, with attention masks preventing cross-document attention) and long sequences (rescaled RoPE, full attention within 128k segments), preserving short-context performance while adapting weights to the new positional encoding.

## Results

On the RULER benchmark, LongRoPE2-extended LLaMA3-8B achieves 82.03 at 128k context, outperforming LongRoPE (73.40), NTK (73.19), and YaRN (49.39). For Phi3-mini-3.8B, LongRoPE2 reaches 58.81 at 128k versus 53.71 (LongRoPE), 49.37 (NTK), and 39.37 (YaRN). On short-context benchmarks, LongRoPE2 retains 97.6% of Phi3-mini's and 98.6% of LLaMA3-8B's original performance (e.g., MMLU 70.04 vs. 70.78 for Phi3-mini; 65.01 vs. 66.62 for LLaMA3-8B), while YaRN and NTK drop by 7.56 and 4.34 MMLU points respectively. Notably, LongRoPE2 achieves this with only 10B training tokens—80× fewer than Meta's 800B-token LLaMA3.1-8B-128k—while surpassing it on long-context performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LongRoPE2 consistently outperforms all three RoPE rescaling baselines (YaRN, NTK, LongRoPE) across every context length on RULER for both models. On LLaMA3-8B at 128k, LongRoPE2 (82.03) beats the prior best LongRoPE (73.40) by 8.63 points and NTK (73.19) by 8.84 points, while YaRN collapses to 49.39. On Phi3-mini at 128k, the gap is even wider: LongRoPE2 (58.81) vs. LongRoPE (53.71), NTK (49.37), and YaRN (39.37). On short-context preservation, LongRoPE2 is the only method retaining >97% of original performance (97.6% Phi3-mini, 98.6% LLaMA3-8B), whereas YaRN drops 15.2% and NTK drops 9.3% on Phi3-mini. Compared to Meta's LLaMA3.1-8B-128k (which uses 800B tokens and YaRN), LongRoPE2-extended LLaMA3-8B achieves superior 128k RULER scores (82.03 vs. ~73) and comparable short-context accuracy, using only 10B tokens. On Needle-in-a-Haystack, LongRoPE2 achieves near-perfect retrieval across all depths up to 128k, while LLaMA3.1-8B fails beyond 100k.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the RoPE rescaling lineage (PI → NTK → YaRN → LongRoPE) by challenging the foundational RoPE OOD theory that underpins all prior methods. It builds directly on LongRoPE's evolutionary search framework and Liu et al.'s NTK scaling for lower dimensions, but departs critically by hypothesizing that higher RoPE dimensions are undertrained during pre-training, shifting the true critical dimension earlier than the theoretical prediction. The mixed context window training approach is a novel departure from prior mid-training strategies (e.g., LLaMA3.1's six-stage progressive extension), replacing them with a single-stage dual-RoPE fine-tuning that simultaneously preserves short-context performance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 80x token efficiency (10B vs. Meta's 800B) while achieving superior long-context performance on RULER and near-lossless short-context retention (97.6–98.6%) makes this a practically significant recipe for context extension that could reshape how labs approach mid-training. The conceptual insight that higher RoPE dimensions receive insufficient training—explaining why the theoretical critical dimension is systematically wrong—provides a diagnostic framework for understanding and fixing OOD issues in positional encodings more broadly. Open questions include whether the approach scales to million-token contexts, how it generalizes to non-RoPE architectures (e.g., ALiBi, NoPE), and whether the one-time KV cache recomputation at the short-to-long transition becomes a bottleneck in streaming or multi-turn inference settings.
