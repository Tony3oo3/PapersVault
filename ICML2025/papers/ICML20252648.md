## Problem

Existing contrastive learning paradigms for incomplete multi-view data fail to adequately compress shared semantic information, as the mutual information gap between raw views and their latent representations remains large when views are missing. This bottleneck, combined with the lack of effective strategies for handling missing labels, limits the performance of multi-label classification under the dual incompleteness of both views and labels (iM3C).

## Input / Output

The method takes incomplete multi-view feature vectors (six feature types: GIST, HSV, DenseHue, DenseSIFT, RGB, LAB) where a subset of views may be absent per sample, along with a binary label vector in which some entries are masked as unknown. It produces a soft probability vector over all label categories. The dual-branch design means two structurally identical networks process the same input independently, each generating soft pseudo-labels that serve as supervision for the other branch in the next training epoch.

## Method

COME combines three components: (1) incomplete multi-view contrastive learning that maximizes cross-view mutual information via a cosine-similarity-based contrastive loss over available view pairs; (2) a MoE-based mutual information enhancement that factorizes the joint variational posterior p(z|X^V) as a uniform mixture of per-view posteriors and uses stochastic Gaussian decoders to reconstruct both intra-view and inter-view data, yielding reconstruction losses L_RE1 and L_RE2 that prevent information collapse while the contrastive loss compresses redundancy; (3) a dual-branch soft pseudo-label cross-imputation strategy where two independent models A and B generate soft (probability-valued) pseudo-labels for each other using a dynamically decaying confidence threshold, with the generated labels swapped as cross-entropy supervision in the next epoch. The overall objective is L = λ₁·L_RE1 + λ₂·L_RE2 + β·L_CTR + L_CE, balancing information compression against reconstruction fidelity and classification accuracy.

## Results

Under the standard 50% missing-view and 50% missing-label setting, COME achieves the best Average Precision (AP) on all five datasets: 0.432 on Corel5k, 0.590 on Pascal07, 0.804 on ESPGame, 0.931 on IAPRTC12, and 0.694 on Mirflickr. COME also leads on all other metrics (1-HL, 1-RL, AUC, 1-OE, 1-Cov) across all datasets. On complete data (no missing views or labels), COME still outperforms methods designed specifically for the complete-data setting, demonstrating strong generalization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

COME outperforms all eight baselines on every metric across all five datasets. The closest competitors are SIP (ICML 2024) and UPDGD-Net (ACM MM 2024), both of which also handle incomplete views and missing labels. On Pascal07, COME achieves 0.590 AP versus 0.503 for SIP and 0.480 for UPDGD-Net, a relative improvement of ~17% over SIP. On Corel5k, the gap is smaller (0.432 vs. 0.418 for SIP), while on ESPGame COME leads SIP by ~2% (0.804 vs. 0.787). Traditional methods that do not handle both missing views and missing labels simultaneously (CDMM, LVSL, DM2L) show substantially lower performance, confirming that jointly addressing dual incompleteness is critical. On complete data, COME still surpasses baselines designed for the ideal complete setting, indicating the mutual information enhancement and soft pseudo-label strategies provide benefits beyond merely compensating for missingness.

## Relation to Prior Work

COME builds directly on the incomplete multi-view contrastive learning framework of DICNet (Liu et al., 2023) for its contrastive loss, and on the variational Mixture-of-Experts factorization of joint posteriors from Shi et al. (2019) and Wen et al. (2020) for its MoE-based representation. Its key conceptual departure is the argument that contrastive learning alone is insufficient under view incompleteness, motivating an additional mutual-information-maximization objective (implemented as cross-view reconstruction) to learn a minimal sufficient shared representation. The dual-branch soft pseudo-label cross-imputation strategy extends co-training and FixMatch-style pseudo-labeling ideas to the multi-label missing-label setting, departing from prior hard pseudo-label approaches (e.g., UPDGD-Net, SIP) by using soft probabilities with a dynamic threshold.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled information-theoretic framing (minimal sufficient representation) for why contrastive learning degrades on incomplete multi-view data, which is useful for practitioners designing multi-view pipelines with missing modalities. However, the mutual information maximization reduces to a standard autoencoder reconstruction loss, and the dual-branch pseudo-label strategy is a straightforward co-training extension, limiting the conceptual novelty. Open questions include whether the approach scales to high-dimensional or truly heterogeneous multi-view settings (e.g., text + image + audio), and whether the MoE factorization remains effective when the number of views is large or highly imbalanced in availability.
