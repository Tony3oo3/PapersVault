## Problem

Existing multi-modal learning methods optimize for modality consistency or specificity but may produce representations that are causally insufficient (missing key features) or unnecessary (capturing spurious correlations). Traditional causal sufficiency/necessity frameworks rely on exogeneity and monotonicity assumptions that break down in practice due to inseparable generating factors and cross-modal conflicts, leaving no principled way to enforce causal completeness in MML representations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252616/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method is plug-and-play and accepts any multi-modal input format: RGB+depth image pairs (NYU Depth V2, SUN RGB-D), image+text pairs (FOOD 101, MVSA), or multi-modal medical scans with Flair/T1/T1c/T2 (BraTS). It produces standard task outputs (class labels or segmentation masks) while the C³ risk score serves purely as a training-time regularizer rather than an inference-time output. The method handles missing modalities by operating on whatever subset of modalities is available at test time.

## Method

The paper defines Causal Complete Cause (C³) as the joint probability that a learned representation is both causally sufficient (intervening to set it to the correct value fixes the label) and causally necessary (removing it breaks the label). To make C³ identifiable without exogeneity or monotonicity, the authors introduce an instrumental variable V modeled via a self-attention mechanism over modalities, where the attention score combines dot-product similarity with a distance-based penalty to suppress spurious cross-modal correlations. C³ risk is then measured through a twin network: a real-world branch uses V to extract the causal representation, while a hypothetical-world branch applies a gradient-based perturbation (constrained by KL divergence) to model the counterfactual where the representation is absent. The resulting C³R regularization minimizes C³ risk plus the instrumental variable alignment loss and a feasibility constraint, and can be appended to any existing MML model's training objective.

## Results

On the four classification datasets (NYU Depth V2, SUN RGB-D, FOOD 101, MVSA) with 50% Gaussian noise corruption, the best C³R variant (QMF+C³R) achieves 77.58% average accuracy on NYU Depth V2 (clean), 67.35% on SUN RGB-D, 94.87% on FOOD 101, and 83.13% on MVSA. Under 10-variance noise, QMF+C³R retains 59.72%, 52.26%, 66.45%, and 66.66% respectively, consistently outperforming all baselines. On the BraTS segmentation task with missing modalities, LCKD+C³R improves Dice scores by 4.2–8.7 points over LCKD across all 15 modality combinations for all three tumour sub-regions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

C³R is applied as a plug-and-play regularizer to seven base MML models, and in every case it improves over the unregularized baseline. The largest gains appear on NYU Depth V2: QMF+C³R reaches 77.58% (vs. QMF's 70.09%, +7.5 pts), LCKD+C³R jumps from 68.01% to 77.14% (+9.1 pts), and MaPLe+C³R improves from 71.26% to 77.07% (+5.8 pts). On SUN RGB-D, QMF+C³R leads at 67.35% (vs. QMF 62.09%, +5.3 pts), while Late fusion+C³R achieves 64.84% (vs. 62.09%, +2.8 pts). On FOOD 101, MMBT+C³R (94.25%) and QMF+C³R (94.87%) surpass all baselines, with QMF+C³R edging out the original QMF by ~2 pts. On MVSA, Late fusion+C³R (83.77%) and QMF+C³R (83.13%) outperform the strongest baseline MMBT (78.50%) by over 4–5 pts. Under 10-variance noise, QMF+C³R maintains the best worst-case accuracy across all four datasets (59.18%, 51.28%, 63.69%, 64.51%), confirming robustness. Baselines not augmented with C³R (ALIGN, CoOp, VPT, ConcatMML, AlignMML, ConcatBow, ConcatBERT, MMTM, TMC, UniCODE) are outperformed by the C³R-augmented variants on every dataset.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the probability of sufficient and necessary causes (PSNC) framework from Pearl (2009) and Yang et al. (2024, NeurIPS) — which was applied to domain generalization under exogeneity and monotonicity assumptions — to multi-modal representation learning, where those assumptions are violated by spurious cross-modal correlations and nonlinear interactions. The key methodological departure is the introduction of a self-attention-based instrumental variable (drawing on Caner & Hansen, 2004) to achieve C³ identifiability without exogeneity or monotonicity, combined with a twin network that uses gradient-based counterfactual perturbation for the necessity term. The resulting C³R regularizer is positioned as a plug-and-play add-on to existing MML methods (CLIP, MaPLe, LCKD, SimMMDG, MMBT, QMF), rather than a standalone architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled causal-theoretic criterion for what makes an MML representation 'good' — going beyond the standard consistency/specificity dichotomy — and shows that enforcing causal completeness via C³R consistently improves robustness under noise and missing modalities across six datasets. However, the experiments rely on small three-layer MLPs rather than the large transformer-based MML models that dominate practice, raising questions about scalability and whether the self-attention instrumental variable generalizes to high-dimensional pre-trained encoders. Open questions include whether the local invertibility and positive Markovian assumptions hold in realistic deep MML pipelines, and whether the KL-constrained gradient perturbation remains a valid counterfactual proxy at scale.
