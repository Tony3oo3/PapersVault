## Problem

In federated learning, non-IID client data causes divergent local optimizations that degrade FedAvg convergence and generalization, yet prior convergence analyses rely on restrictive assumptions such as convexity, bounded gradients, or bounded gradient dissimilarity. It remained unclear whether the overparameterization that enables strong generalization in centralized learning could inherently mitigate heterogeneity effects in FL. This paper provides the first quantitative analysis explicitly linking network width to the degree of heterogeneity-induced model divergence.

## Input / Output

Each client holds a local subset of image data (MNIST 28×28 grayscale or CIFAR-10 32×32 RGB), partitioned non-IID via Dirichlet sampling (α=0.1) or class-exclusive assignment for binary tasks. The theoretical analysis assumes MSE loss with gradient descent at learning rate η=η₀/n, while experiments extend to cross-entropy loss with SGD (batch size 64, η=0.1). The method produces class predictions at test time, and theoretically yields a convergence bound where the heterogeneity-induced error term scales as O(n^{-1/2}) and vanishes in the infinite-width limit.

## Method

The paper analyzes overparameterized FedAvg with multi-layer fully-connected networks using NTK theory and gradient flow approximation (η=η₀/n). The key mechanism is a mathematical induction proving that the weighted model divergence ∑pᵢ‖Δθᵢ‖₂ is bounded by ζ=O(n^{-1/2}), which slows convergence but vanishes as width grows. In the infinite-width regime, both global and local NTKs become constant, linearizing all models so that FedAvg's closed-form solution matches centralized GD exactly under matched iterations. The analysis requires only that the analytic NTK be full rank and the activation be Lipschitz, avoiding convexity or gradient-boundedness assumptions. Experiments across FNNs, CNNs, and ResNets on MNIST and CIFAR-10 confirm that widening the network progressively closes the IID/non-IID accuracy gap and that wide FedAvg outputs align with both linear approximations and centralized training.

## Results

On MNIST with FNNs, the test accuracy drop from IID to non-IID (α=0.1) decreases from 17.4% (FNN1) to 2.0% (FNN16). On CIFAR-10, CNN accuracy drops shrink from 44.9% (CNN1) to 2.4% (CNN32), and ResNet drops from 44.6% (ResNet1) to 14.8% (ResNet16). In the infinite-width regime (FNN32, CNN32 with η=η₀/n), the convergence rate and final accuracy become nearly identical for IID and non-IID data, confirming the theoretical O(n^{-1/2}) heterogeneity bound. FedAvg and centralized GD produce almost identical training and test losses under matched iterations on mini-CIFAR-10.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper is primarily a theoretical contribution with validation experiments rather than a method-vs-method comparison, so no external FL baselines (e.g., SCAFFOLD, FedNova, FedBN) are benchmarked. The key internal comparisons are: (1) network width as the sole variable — widening consistently reduces the IID/non-IID accuracy gap across FNN, CNN, and ResNet families, with the gap approaching zero at large widths (FNN32, CNN32); (2) nonlinear vs. linear model approximation — FNN512's global and local outputs remain nearly identical to their first-order Taylor (linear) approximations throughout training, while FNN16 shows visible deviation, validating Theorem 2; (3) FedAvg vs. centralized GD — under matched total iterations (t′=tτ) on mini-CIFAR-10 with MSE loss and GD, the training and test losses of infinite-width FedAvg and centralized learning are almost indistinguishable, confirming Theorem 3's equivalence result. These comparisons collectively validate the central claim that data heterogeneity's impact vanishes as O(n^{-1/2}) in the infinite-width limit.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Neural Tangent Kernel (NTK) framework from centralized learning (Jacot et al., 2018; Lee et al., 2019) to multi-layer federated learning, building directly on Lee et al.'s closed-form gradient flow solutions and Jacobian Lipschitzness lemmas. It departs from prior FL convergence analyses (Song et al., 2023; Huang et al., 2021) that either ignore data heterogeneity or require restrictive assumptions like convexity, bounded gradients, or bounded gradient dissimilarity. The key conceptual departure is providing the first quantitative O(n^{-1/2}) bound linking network width to heterogeneity mitigation, and proving exact equivalence between infinite-width FedAvg and centralized GD under matched iterations—going beyond Song et al.'s training-loss-only analysis to full model-parameter and output equivalence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that infinite-width FedAvg produces identical model parameters and outputs to centralized GD is a clean theoretical statement that the non-IID 'problem' in FL is fundamentally a finite-width artifact, offering a principled justification for using wider networks as a heterogeneity mitigation strategy. However, the infinite-width assumption and gradient flow approximation limit direct practical applicability, and the theory is developed for MSE loss while experiments use cross-entropy. Open questions include whether the O(n^{-1/2}) rate holds under stochastic optimization with practical learning rates, whether the equivalence extends to personalized FL or asynchronous settings, and the paper's suggested communication-efficient strategy of transmitting model outputs rather than full parameters for aggregation.
