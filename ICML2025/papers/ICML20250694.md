## Problem

Existing fair minimax optimal regression algorithms are tightly coupled to specific data generation models (e.g., linear models with Gaussian features or Hölder classes with margin conditions), limiting their applicability to broader real-world scenarios. This paper establishes meta-theorems that connect the fair minimax optimal error to the conventional regression minimax error, enabling rates tailored to diverse smoothness assumptions, and shows that fair minimax optimality can be achieved through a post-processing procedure.

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes n_s i.i.d. feature-outcome pairs (X^(s), Y^(s)) for each of M social groups along with known group weights w_s ∈ Δ_M. It splits each group's sample in half: the first half trains a conventional minimax-optimal regressor, and the second half is used to estimate optimal transport maps via empirical multiple correlation minimization. The output is a group-wise regressor f̄_{n,s}(x) = (ϑ_{n,s} ∘ f_{n,s})(x) whose output distribution is invariant across all groups, satisfying strict demographic parity in the limit.

## Method

The core mechanism is a post-processing composition: a minimax-optimal conventional regressor f_{n,:} is first trained on half the data, then its outputs are refined by estimated optimal transport maps ϑ_{n,:} that map each group's prediction distribution to the Wasserstein barycenter of all groups' prediction distributions. The transport maps are estimated by minimizing the empirical multiple correlation over congruent potentials restricted to a sieved subset Θ_j, following the strategy of Korotin et al. (2020). The key technical contribution is a novel convergence rate analysis for this transport map estimator, yielding a rate of (ñ/ln ñ)^{-α/(α+β)} governed by the metric entropy complexity (α, β) of the transport map class, under Lipschitz and Poincaré-type inequality assumptions. The meta-theorem (Theorem 3.4) then shows the overall fair minimax error is bounded by L² times the conventional minimax error plus this transport estimation rate, meaning practitioners can focus on improving conventional regression methods and seamlessly adapt them for fair regression.

## Results

The main theoretical result (Theorem 3.4) establishes that the fair minimax optimal error satisfies $\bar{\mathcal{E}}_n(\mathcal{P}) \leq C\left(L^2 \sum_s w_s \mathcal{E}_{n_s}(\mathcal{P}_s) + (\tilde{n}/\ln\tilde{n})^{-\alpha/(\alpha+\beta)}\right)$, where the first term is the conventional minimax regression error and the second is the transport map estimation error. In the illustrative example with compositional regression functions and Sobolev transport maps of smoothness $\gamma$, the fair minimax rate is $\Theta(\max_i n^{-\beta_i^*/(2\beta_i^*+t_i)} + n^{-2\gamma/(2\gamma+1)})$ up to logarithmic factors. The transport map estimator (Theorem 5.1) achieves rate $(\tilde{n}/\ln\tilde{n})^{-\alpha/(\alpha+\beta)}$, which can be faster than $n^{-1/2}$ when the transport map class has sufficient smoothness ($\alpha > 2$).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no experimental baselines. The key theoretical comparisons are against prior fair minimax optimal regression analyses. Compared to Chzhen et al. (2020), the paper's meta-theorem requires weaker assumptions (Poincaré-type inequality instead of uniform density bounds; expected error bounds instead of sub-Gaussian high-probability bounds) and removes the $n^{-1/2}$ rate cap that limited their analysis. Compared to Chzhen & Schreuder (2022) and Fukuchi & Sakuma (2023), which are tightly coupled to specific linear/Gaussian data models, the meta-theorem applies broadly to any data generation model where the conventional minimax rate is known (Hölder, Sobolev, Besov, deep learning). The paper also provides the first convergence rate analysis for the Korotin et al. (2020) transport map estimator in the Wasserstein barycenter setting, a gap explicitly noted as unresolved in the prior work.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Wasserstein barycenter characterization of fair Bayes-optimal regressors from Chzhen et al. (2020) and the minimax fair regression analyses of Chzhen & Schreuder (2022) and Fukuchi & Sakuma (2023), which were each tightly coupled to specific data-generating models (Gaussian linear models, Hölder classes with margin conditions). The key departure is a meta-theorem that decouples the fair minimax error from any particular model class, expressing it as the sum of the conventional minimax regression error and a transport map estimation error governed by metric entropy. The transport map estimator follows Korotin et al. (2020)'s congruent-potential minimization strategy, but the paper provides the first convergence rate analysis for this estimator in the barycenter setting, leveraging a Poincaré-type inequality and sieved M-estimation techniques.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The meta-theorem provides a general recipe: any minimax optimal conventional regression algorithm (Hölder, Sobolev, Besov, deep ReLU networks) can be converted into a minimax optimal fair regression algorithm via a post-processing step, freeing practitioners to improve standard regression without re-deriving fairness guarantees. A notable limitation is that the analysis requires 1D outcomes (Ω ⊂ ℝ), a Poincaré-type inequality on the outcome distributions, and Lipschitz transport maps, which may not hold in high-dimensional or multimodal outcome settings. The computational challenge of minimizing over congruent potentials (Remark 4.3) and the open question of convergence rates under approximate congruency remain important practical gaps. The result that the fair minimax rate can exceed n^{-1/2} by exploiting transport map smoothness is a meaningful improvement over the Chzhen et al. (2020) bound.
