## Problem

LLM pretraining is prohibitively expensive (e.g., LLaMA2 70B requires 1.7M GPU hours), yet intermediate checkpoints saved during training are typically discarded. While linearly averaging checkpoints (model soup) can reduce output variance without increasing bias, the optimal merging weight is a black-box, expensive-to-evaluate objective with no closed-form solution, and no systematic method existed for determining it during pretraining.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251589/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes two adjacent intermediate checkpoints from the pretraining trajectory (e.g., Θ_{t-1} and Θ_t from Baichuan2 or DeepSeek) along with a held-out validation dataset (e.g., a subset of C-Eval). It outputs a single merged checkpoint computed as a convex combination Θ̃_t = λ_t·Θ_t + (1−λ_t)·Θ_{t-1} and the scalar optimal merging weight λ_t ∈ [α, 1]. The search space is deliberately restricted to a one-dimensional interval with a tunable lower bound α (typically 0.5–0.9) to retain significant contribution from the latest checkpoint, and the validation set can be as small as 1/4 of the full set without degrading performance.

## Method

The core mechanism is a pairwise checkpoint merging strategy optimized via Bayesian optimization with Gaussian Process (GP) regression. Pilot experiments first establish that only adjacent checkpoints in the pretraining trajectory should be merged (distant pairs cause destructive interference), reducing the problem from a (t−1)-dimensional weight optimization to a single scalar λ_t. A GP prior is placed over the performance function f(λ_t), and the GP-Hedge strategy dynamically combines Expected Improvement, Probability of Improvement, and Upper Confidence Bound acquisition functions based on their cumulative historical rewards to select the next weight to evaluate. Each iteration merges the two checkpoints at the selected λ_t, evaluates on the validation set, and updates the GP posterior, typically converging in fewer than 20 evaluations. Theoretical analysis provides PAC-Bayesian generalization bounds showing the merged model's KL divergence from the prior is reduced (since λ_t ≤ 1), and convergence proofs under the Polyak-Łojasiewicz condition demonstrate that merging accelerates loss reduction toward the optimum.

## Results

On the primary Baichuan2-1980B & 2200B checkpoint pair, the Bayesian optimization method achieves an average score of 48.13 across C-Eval, CMMLU, MMLU, and GSM8K, outperforming the best individual checkpoint (Baichuan2-1980B at 47.15) by 0.98 points. Across all four checkpoint pairs (two Baichuan2, two DeepSeek), the method achieves average improvements of +0.48, +0.50, +0.53, and +0.44 over the best baseline, with the largest single-metric gain being +1.01 on C-Eval for DeepSeek-1800B & 2000B. The method also demonstrates cross-lingual generalization, with a Δ (IND-OOD gap) of only 0.87 compared to 2.01 for Greedy Soup and 2.38 for Fisher.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against four merging baselines: Uniform Soup and Greedy Soup (Wortsman et al., 2022), Fisher Weighted Averaging (Matena & Raffel, 2022), and RegMean (Jin et al., 2022). The Bayesian optimization method wins on average across all four checkpoint pairs, with average improvements of +0.48 to +0.53 over the best baseline. Against Greedy Soup, the method achieves the largest gains on C-Eval (+0.59 for Baichuan2-2200B & 2420B, +1.01 for DeepSeek-1800B & 2000B) but loses narrowly on MMLU for Baichuan2-2200B & 2420B (54.56 vs. 54.82, −0.26). Against Fisher Weighted Averaging, the method dominates on C-Eval and GSM8K but trails by 0.03 on MMLU for DeepSeek-1400B & 1600B (43.85 vs. 43.95). Against RegMean, the method consistently wins on all metrics for all four pairs. The method also outperforms all baselines in cross-domain stability, achieving the smallest IND-OOD performance gap (Δ = 0.87 vs. 2.01 for Greedy Soup and 2.38 for Fisher), indicating that the optimized merging weights generalize better across languages and task types.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the model soup / checkpoint averaging paradigm introduced by Wortsman et al. (2022) from the fine-tuning setting to LLM pretraining, where intermediate checkpoints along a single training trajectory are merged rather than independently fine-tuned models. The key methodological departure is replacing the greedy or uniform weight selection strategies of prior soup work with a Gaussian Process-based Bayesian optimization loop (using GP-Hedge to adaptively combine EI, PI, and UCB acquisition functions) to find the optimal 1D merging weight. The theoretical grounding draws on PAC-Bayesian generalization bounds (McAllester, 1998) and standard GP convergence results (Srinivas et al., 2009), applied here to the specific structure of pairwise checkpoint interpolation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a practical, low-cost 'free lunch' for pretraining pipelines: a few hours of evaluation on a held-out set can yield 0.1–1% accuracy gains without additional training compute, which is meaningful at the scale of 70B+ parameter models. However, the gains are modest, the method requires a held-out validation set (introducing a data dependency), and the pairwise-only merging strategy limits applicability when more than two checkpoints are available. Open questions include whether the Bayesian optimization advantage persists in higher-dimensional merging spaces (beyond pairwise), whether the improvements compound over many merging steps in a long pretraining run, and whether the approach transfers to non-linear or structured merging operations.
