## Problem

Random sketches of tall matrices are unbiased estimators of the Gram matrix, but their inverses are biased — a phenomenon called inversion bias that undermines their use in Newton-type optimization and statistical estimation. While this bias was precisely characterized and corrected for random projections (Gaussian, sub-gaussian, LESS), the existing proof framework yields only vacuous bounds for random sampling and structured projections like the SRHT, leaving a gap in both theory and practice for the most computationally efficient sketching schemes.

## Input / Output

The method takes a tall data matrix A ∈ R^{n×d} (n ≫ d), an importance sampling distribution {π_i}, leverage scores ℓ_i^C (exact or approximate), and a p.s.d. regularization matrix C, and produces a row-wise rescaled (debiased) sampling matrix whose entries are (mπ_{i_s} − ℓ_{i_s}^C)^{-1/2} rather than the standard (mπ_{i_s})^{-1/2}. For approximate leverage sampling and SRHT, the row-specific factors collapse to a single scalar m/(m−d_eff), making the debiasing computationally trivial. The final output in the optimization application is the parameter vector β* solving a regularized logistic regression, with the debiased SSN iteration converging at a problem-independent rate.

## Method

The paper derives a fine-grained characterization of inversion bias for random sampling via a self-consistent equation (Theorem 3.1) obtained through the deterministic equivalent framework from non-asymptotic random matrix theory, showing that E[(A^T S^T SA + C)^{-1}] is close to (A^T DA + C)^{-1} for a diagonal D solving an implicit equation involving leverage scores. This leads to a debiased sampling matrix (Proposition 3.2) that rescales each sampled row by √(m/(m − ℓ_i^C/π_i)), which for approximate leverage sampling and SRHT reduces to the simple scalar factor m/(m−d_eff). Applying this debiased sketch to the sub-sampled Newton iteration yields the first problem-independent local convergence rates for SSN (Theorem 4.3), matching the rate of Newton Sketch with dense Gaussian projections. The proof relies on a second inverse moment characterization of the randomly sampled Hessian, extending beyond the first-moment result, and the approach is validated on MNIST and CIFAR-10 logistic regression where de-biased SSN with approximate ridge leverage sampling outperforms Newton-LESS across all sketch sizes.

## Results

On logistic regression over MNIST and CIFAR-10, the proposed de-biased SSN with approximate ridge leverage score sampling (SSN-ARLev) consistently achieves lower relative error than Newton-LESS across all tested sketch sizes m, while maintaining approximately constant wall-clock time (Newton-LESS time increases significantly with m). Among all SSN variants tested (AR-Lev, A-Lev, S-Lev, SRHT), ARLev delivers the best convergence-complexity trade-off, outperforming both first-order baselines (GD, SGD) and Newton-LESS. The SRHT variant outperforms Newton-LESS but still lags slightly behind the leverage-based SSN variants in speed.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is Newton-LESS (Dereziński et al., 2021a), a Newton Sketch method using sparse sub-Gaussian LESS projections. The proposed de-biased SSN-ARLev outperforms Newton-LESS on both relative error and wall-clock time across all sketch sizes m on both MNIST and CIFAR-10 (Figure 2). Critically, while Newton-LESS running time grows substantially as m increases, SSN-ARLev's running time remains approximately constant, giving it a superior complexity-convergence trade-off. In the broader comparison (Figure 3), de-biased SSN methods with ARLev, ALev, SLev, and SRHT all beat Newton-LESS in convergence speed at fixed sketch size (m=300 for MNIST, m=400 for CIFAR-10), with ARLev being the fastest among them. The SRHT variant also outperforms Newton-LESS but is slightly slower than the leverage-based SSN variants. First-order methods (GD, SGD) are dominated by all second-order methods in convergence rate.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the inversion bias analysis of Dereziński et al. (2021b) from random projections (Gaussian, sub-gaussian, LESS) to random sampling (uniform, leverage-based) and structured projections (SRHT), which are more computationally attractive in practice. The key technical departure is that the scalar debiasing proof approach from Dereziński et al. yields vacuous bounds for random sampling (Remark 2.9), necessitating a new fine-grained analysis via deterministic equivalents from random matrix theory. The resulting debiased SSN iteration builds directly on the sub-sampled Newton framework of Roosta-Khorasani & Mahoney (2019) and targets the problem-independent convergence rates previously achieved only by Newton Sketch with dense Gaussian projections (Pilanci & Wainwright, 2017).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper closes a gap in the convergence theory of SSN by providing the first problem-independent local convergence rates, and shows that for practical sampling schemes (approximate leverage, SRHT) the debiasing reduces to a simple scalar rescaling m/(m−d_eff), making it easy to implement. Numerical experiments on MNIST and CIFAR-10 logistic regression confirm that de-biased SSN with approximate ridge leverage sampling outperforms Newton-LESS in both accuracy and wall-clock time. Open questions include whether combining the debiasing with adaptive sketch-size scheduling (Lacotte et al., 2021) can achieve quadratic convergence, and whether the framework extends to dependent sampling methods like volume sampling.
