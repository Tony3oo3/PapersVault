## Problem

Prior Text-to-CAD methods train exclusively on ground-truth parametric sequences (sequential signal), ignoring the visual signal from rendered CAD objects. Because CAD models are inherently multimodal and the rendering from parametric sequences to visual objects is many-to-one, relying solely on sequential signals leads to oversimplified shapes, poor visual quality, and an inability to capture the full space of valid parametric sequences that produce the same visual outcome.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252598/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a natural-language textual description of a 3D shape (structured as shape overview, optional details, and optional applications) and produces a CAD parametric sequence in Sketch-and-Extrude format, where geometric primitives (lines, arcs, circles) are parameterized as coordinate tokens and extrusions are encoded as boolean operation, displacement, translation, rotation, scaling, and origin fields. The sequence is represented as plain text tokens processed by the LLM's native tokenizer (LLaMA-3-8b-Instruct, max 1024 tokens), rather than binary or numerical encodings, and can be rendered into a 3D visual object for downstream use.

## Method

CADFusion uses a pre-trained LLM (LLaMA-3-8b-Instruct with LoRA) as backbone and alternates between two training stages: a Sequential Learning (SL) stage that fine-tunes the LLM via cross-entropy on ground-truth parametric sequences, and a Visual Feedback (VF) stage that applies Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) to reward parametric sequences whose rendered outputs are visually preferred. The VF stage circumvents the non-differentiable rendering pipeline by framing visual quality as a preference-learning problem, with preference pairs constructed automatically by scoring rendered CAD images using a vision-language model (LLaVA-OneVision) under multi-aspect criteria (shape quality, shape quantity, spatial distribution). Training begins with an initial SL phase (40 epochs), followed by five alternating VF→SL blocks, preventing the visual feedback from degrading sequence-formatting ability and vice versa. This dual-signal, alternating strategy is the core distinction from prior work that trains only on sequential signals.

## Results

CADFusion achieves the best performance across nearly all metrics in the main comparison (Table 1). It attains an LVM Score of 8.96 (vs. 5.13 for GPT-4o and 2.01 for Text2CAD), a Chamfer Distance of 19.89 (vs. 68.50 and 30.23), and the best human-evaluation average rank of 1.86 (vs. 3.22 and 2.97). On sequential-level metrics, CADFusion scores 85.22 F1-Sketch and 92.79 F1-Extrusion, surpassing both baselines. The invalidity ratio is 6.20%, higher than Text2CAD's 3.37% but far lower than GPT-4o's 74.26%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GPT-4o (8-shot), CADFusion wins on every reported metric: F1-Sketch (85.22 vs. 82.96), F1-Extrusion (92.79 vs. 85.72), CD (19.89 vs. 68.50), COV (90.40 vs. 72.40), MMD (3.49 vs. 6.60), JSD (17.11 vs. 37.93), IR (6.20% vs. 74.26%), LVM Score (8.96 vs. 5.13), and Avg. Rank (1.86 vs. 3.22). Against Text2CAD, CADFusion dominates on visual-quality metrics—LVM Score (8.96 vs. 2.01) and Avg. Rank (1.86 vs. 2.97)—as well as on CD (19.89 vs. 30.23) and F1-Sketch (85.22 vs. 63.94). The only metric where Text2CAD edges out CADFusion is the invalidity ratio (3.37% vs. 6.20%), which the authors attribute to Text2CAD's tendency to generate simpler, more regular shapes that are easier to render but less faithful to the prompt. The paper notes that Text2CAD's results are reported using its own expert-level prompts (which include step-by-step sketching instructions), while CADFusion uses simplified natural-language prompts, making the comparison even more favorable to CADFusion in practical use.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Text-to-CAD line initiated by Text2CAD (Khan et al., 2024b) and CAD Translator (Li et al., 2024b), which train Transformer-based models solely on ground-truth parametric sequences (sequential signal). CADFusion departs from this by (1) using a pre-trained LLM (LLaMA-3-8b-Instruct) as backbone rather than training from scratch, (2) adopting the text-token CAD sequence format from FlexCAD (Zhang et al., 2025), and (3) introducing a visual feedback stage that uses DPO (Rafailov et al., 2024) to optimize against rendered visual quality, with LVM-based automated scoring inspired by RLAIF. The alternating SFT/DPO training strategy is a key architectural departure from prior work that applies only a single signal.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first Text-to-CAD framework to incorporate visual feedback from rendered CAD objects into training, addressing the many-to-one rendering characteristic that sequential-only training cannot capture. The practical impact is making CAD design more accessible to non-experts, though the current system is limited to single-view images and struggles with shapes requiring spatial/commonsense reasoning (e.g., letter forms). Open questions include whether multi-view rendering feedback, larger and more geometrically diverse datasets, and stronger LVM evaluators can close the remaining gap to human-level CAD generation.
