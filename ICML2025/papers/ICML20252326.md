## Problem

Existing watermarking evaluation metrics (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS) require access to original images—unavailable in text-driven generative watermarking—and fail to capture watermark visibility or semantic corruption of image content. No existing multimodal large language model can be directly applied to watermarked image understanding, as they are trained on natural images whose data distribution is fundamentally altered by the fusion of distinct logo content.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252326/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts a single 448×448 watermarked RGB image (no original reference needed) paired with a text question, and generates a free-form textual description covering watermark location, spatial relationship to main objects, visual features, and a concluding visibility phrase in the fixed format 'The watermark visibility is: <level>.' The five discrete visibility levels (invisible, faint, moderate, visible, obvious) are converted to a continuous score in [1,5] via a restricted softmax over the level token logits, weighted by their numeric values.

## Method

WMarkGPT adopts the mPLUG-owl2 architecture (vision encoder → learnable queries + visual abstractor → LLaMA-2-7B LLM) but introduces a three-stage progressive training pipeline to bridge the gap between natural and watermarked images. Stage 1 pre-trains the visual components on 100K GPT-4-generated object location QA pairs from COCO to establish spatial reasoning; Stage 2 unfreezes all parameters and fine-tunes on 50K synthetic watermarked images (constructed via SAM segmentation, GPT-4 object detection, alpha-blending synthesis, and GPT-4o description generation) to adapt to the unnatural fusion distribution; Stage 3 fine-tunes only the vision encoder and visual abstractor on 2.5K human-annotated real watermarked images from five SOTA watermarking algorithms. The key insight is that progressive exposure—from natural spatial reasoning to synthetic watermarking to real watermarking—allows the model to internalize both precise localization and the semantically corrupted visual domain without catastrophic forgetting.

## Results

On the WQA-Real dataset, WMarkGPT achieves BLEU-1 of 0.424, ROUGE-L of 0.418, LLM-Score of 71.95, and visibility prediction ACC of 0.546, outperforming the best baselines by 29.3%, 44.6%, 149.8%, and 217.4% respectively. On WQA-Synthetic, it reaches BLEU-1 of 0.488, ROUGE-L of 0.446, LLM-Score of 87.75, and ACC of 0.645. In terms of correlation with human annotations (SRCC/PLCC/KRCC on WQA-Real), WMarkGPT achieves 0.71/0.74/0.54, surpassing score-based methods like DBCNN (0.63/0.70/0.46) and CLIP-IQA (0.43/0.46/0.31).

## Baselines & Comparisons

WMarkGPT dominates all six MLLM baselines across every metric on both datasets. On WQA-Real, the closest competitors are Qwen2-VL-7B-Instruct (BLEU-1 0.328, ROUGE-L 0.289) and VILA-8B (LLM-Score 28.80, ACC 0.172); WMarkGPT exceeds them by 29.3% in BLEU-1, 44.6% in ROUGE-L, 149.8% in LLM-Score, and 217.4% in ACC. LLaVA-1.5-7B/13B and mPLUG-owl2-7B trail further behind, with LLaVA-1.5-7B scoring only 0.237 BLEU-1 and 15.6 LLM-Score on WQA-Real. Against score-based IQA methods (DBCNN, HyperIQA, TReS, CLIP-IQA) evaluated on correlation with human ratings, WMarkGPT achieves the highest SRCC (0.71), PLCC (0.74), and KRCC (0.54) on WQA-Real, while DBCNN and HyperIQA lead on WQA-Synthetic (SRCC 0.91) but drop sharply on real data. The consistent margin across all comparisons indicates that general-purpose MLLMs and pixel-level IQA models are fundamentally ill-suited to watermarked image understanding without task-specific training.

## Relation to Prior Work

WMarkGPT adopts the mPLUG-owl2 architecture (vision encoder + learnable queries + visual abstractor + LLM) and the discrete-level visibility scoring mechanism from Q-Align, but repurposes them for a fundamentally different domain: understanding images that are fusions of two visually distinct sources (natural content + watermark logo). It departs from both traditional watermarking evaluation (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS), which requires original images and captures only low-level pixel differences, and from general-purpose MLLMs (LLaVA, Qwen-VL, VILA), which are trained exclusively on natural images and cannot handle the corrupted data distribution of watermarked content. The three-stage progressive training pipeline—object positioning → synthetic watermarking → real watermarking—is a novel curriculum design specifically to bridge the domain gap between natural and watermarked images.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work addresses a practical gap in text-driven generative watermarking (e.g., Safe-SD), where original images are unavailable and pixel-level metrics are inapplicable, by providing the first MLLM-based framework that outputs rich textual descriptions of watermark location, content, and visibility from the watermarked image alone. The released WQA-Synthetic (50K) and WQA-Real (2.5K) datasets establish the first benchmarks for this task. However, the real dataset is small, the model is limited to 7B parameters and 2D images, and the large performance margins over baselines partly reflect that those baselines were never designed for this task. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to video and 3D watermarking (noted as future work), whether the semi-automatic synthetic pipeline introduces biases, and whether the visibility scoring remains reliable under adversarial watermark removal.
