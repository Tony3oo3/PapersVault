## Problem

Human-AI coordination in complex partially observable settings lacks a standardised, reproducible benchmark: prior evaluations relied on closed-source human datasets and proprietary proxy agents, making it impossible for the broader community to measure progress consistently. Additionally, the scarcity of open-source human gameplay data limits research on data-efficient coordination methods essential for real-world deployment where large-scale human data is unavailable.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250532/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each agent receives a local action-observation history (AOH) — a sequence of its own past actions and observations, including the visible cards in partners' hands but not its own — and outputs a discrete local action (play a card, discard a card, or give a colour/rank hint to a specific partner). The evaluation protocol restricts API access to partial observability, enforcing that agents cannot query global game state. The optional action prediction task feeds the same AOH and requires predicting the exact human action at each timestep, scored by teacher-forced cross-entropy loss.

## Method

The core contribution is HDR-IPPO (Human-Data-Regularised IPPO), a two-stage procedure for building human proxy agents. First, an LSTM-based behavioural cloning policy is trained via cross-entropy loss on 147,621 human games from hanab.live (101,096 two-player, 46,525 three-player), with colour-space permutation for data augmentation. Second, the BC policy is refined through Independent PPO in self-play, augmented with a KL-divergence regularisation term that penalises deviation from the frozen BC policy's action distribution, weighted by λ∈[0,1]. This regularisation is the key mechanism: it preserves human-like conventions (validated via cross-play with BC policies, action prediction accuracy, and behavioural metrics like Information per Play and Communicativeness) while granting the robustness and generalisation that pure BC lacks. To prevent overfitting, the trained proxies are hosted behind a controlled API with pre-registered evaluations limited to 1,000 games, and only 3,079 games are open-sourced to encourage data-efficient methods.

## Results

On the AH2AC2 leaderboard (1,000 games per setting), OBL(L4) achieves the highest coordination score without any human data, scoring 21.04 mean / 22 median in two-player Hanabi. Among data-dependent methods, BR-BC leads in 2P (19.41 mean) while HDR-IPPO leads in 3P (14.03 mean), yet both fall well short of the human-proxy golden standard (22.76 mean in 2P, 20.86 in 3P). Pure BC and IPPO baselines perform poorly (2.12 and 10.16 mean in 2P; 3.31 and 6.34 in 3P), and DeepSeek-R1 without fine-tuning scores only 5.43–9.91 in 2P but surprisingly 14.38–14.62 in 3P, outperforming all other baselines in that setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OBL (Hu et al., 2021) is the strongest zero-shot method, achieving 21.04 mean in 2P without any human data, outperforming all data-dependent baselines (BR-BC at 19.41, HDR-IPPO at 12.76) and revealing a gap: limited human data does not yet help coordination. BR-BC (Carroll et al., 2019) is the best data-dependent method in 2P (19.41) but degrades sharply in 3P (11.89), with a very high CE of 29.89 indicating poor action prediction. FCP (Strouse et al., 2021) underperforms in both settings (14.01 in 2P, 11.55 in 3P), suggesting population-based methods struggle in complex partially observable environments. OP (Hu et al., 2020) achieves moderate scores (13.91 in 2P, 12.87 in 3P) but fails to close the gap to OBL. IPPO (de Witt et al., 2020), representing pure self-play, scores only 10.16 in 2P and 6.34 in 3P, confirming that SP-trained agents overfit to their own conventions. DeepSeek-R1 (DeepSeek-AI, 2025) without fine-tuning scores 5.43–9.91 in 2P (well below OBL) but surprisingly leads in 3P at 14.38–14.62, outperforming all other baselines in that setting, though its evaluation was limited to 100 games versus 1,000 for other methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the human-regularized RL lineage (Bakhtin et al., 2022; Hu et al., 2022; Jacob et al., 2022; Cornelisse & Vinitsky, 2024), which showed that KL-regularized self-play preserves human-like conventions while improving robustness over pure behavioral cloning. The HDR-IPPO procedure combines BC on large-scale human data with IPPO (de Witt et al., 2020) under a KL penalty, following this established recipe. The key departure is in evaluation infrastructure: prior Hanabi human-AI coordination work (Hu et al., 2021; 2022; Lupu et al., 2021) relied on closed-source datasets and proxy agents, whereas AH2AC2 open-sources a limited dataset, hosts proxies behind a controlled API, and introduces a public leaderboard with pre-registration — standardizing what was previously fragmented and non-reproducible.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AH2AC2 provides the first standardized, reproducible benchmark for human-AI ad-hoc coordination in a partially observable setting, addressing a critical reproducibility gap in the field. The finding that OBL (zero-shot, no human data) outperforms all methods that leverage the available human dataset (BC, BR-BC, HDR-IPPO) reveals a significant research gap: current techniques cannot effectively integrate small human datasets to improve coordination, challenging the intuition that human data should help with human-AI teamwork. Open questions include whether the data-limited setting is too restrictive, whether the H-Group convention constraint limits generalizability, and whether direct human-AI play experiments will validate the proxy agents as faithful stand-ins. The preliminary DeepSeek-R1 results (strong in 3P, weak in 2P) also raise questions about LLMs' theory-of-mind capabilities in structured cooperative games.
