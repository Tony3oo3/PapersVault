## Problem

LoRA fine-tuning consistently underperforms full fine-tuning on complex tasks like mathematical reasoning and code generation, and increasing rank alone cannot close this gap. While model merging has been explored for multi-task scenarios, single-task merging (combining models trained on the same task with different seeds) presents fundamentally different challenges—high cosine similarity and redundancy rather than orthogonality—making existing merging methods like TIES and DARE suboptimal for this setting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251530/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes n LoRA adapters (typically rank 8) trained on the same task with different random seeds, along with the frozen pre-trained base model. Merging is performed layer-wise on the LoRA delta matrices (τ = BA), and the output is a single merged LoRA adapter of the same rank as the individual adapters, which is added to the base model weights. The threshold σ for identifying robust/conflicting dimensions is set to the median magnitude of the delta parameters, and the SVD rank in Stage 2 matches the original LoRA rank.

## Method

SeedLoRA employs a two-stage fusion strategy tailored to the high-similarity, redundant nature of same-task LoRA models. Stage 1 identifies robust dimensions (where a majority of adapters show consistently large same-sign values, which are averaged) and conflicting dimensions (where groups of adapters produce large opposite-sign values, resolved by majority sign à la TIES). Stage 2 handles the remaining residual dimensions via SVD-based subspace fusion: the residual parameters are first averaged, then decomposed via truncated SVD to establish a shared low-rank basis; individual adapters are projected onto this common subspace, their coordinates are fused using existing methods (TIES, DARE, or weighted averaging), and the result is reconstructed back to the original parameter space. This divide-and-conquer design separates redundancy handling from complementary information extraction, addressing the distinct challenges of single-task merging that multi-task methods were not designed for.

## Results

On LLaMA2-7B fine-tuned with LoRA (r=8) on MetaMathQA, SeedLoRA achieves 68.9 on GSM8K and 17.8 on MATH, surpassing the best individual LoRA model (64.1 / 15.3) by 4.8 and 2.5 points respectively, and exceeding Full Fine-Tuning on GSM8K (66.5). On Mistral-7B, SeedLoRA reaches 80.7 on GSM8K and 28.8 on MATH, again surpassing Full FT (78.6 / 28.5). For code generation on LLaMA2-7B (CodeFeedback), SeedLoRA achieves 40.7 on HumanEval and 36.6 on HumanEval+, outperforming the best individual LoRA (34.1 / 30.5) and matching or exceeding Full FT (40.3 / 37.1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SeedLoRA consistently outperforms all three merging baselines across every benchmark. Against Model Soup, SeedLoRA gains +2.3 on LLaMA2-7B GSM8K (68.9 vs 66.6), +2.1 on MATH (17.8 vs 15.7), +1.6 on Mistral GSM8K (80.7 vs 79.1), and +0.3 on Mistral MATH (28.8 vs 28.5). Against TIES, the margins are larger: +3.2 on LLaMA2-7B GSM8K, +1.8 on MATH, +5.6 on Mistral GSM8K, and +4.0 on Mistral MATH. Against DARE, SeedLoRA leads by +3.2 on LLaMA2-7B GSM8K, +2.3 on MATH, +5.6 on Mistral GSM8K, and +3.8 on Mistral MATH. Notably, SeedLoRA exceeds Full Fine-Tuning on GSM8K for both models (68.9 > 66.5; 80.7 > 78.6) and on Mistral MATH (28.8 > 28.5), while still trailing Full FT on LLaMA2-7B MATH (17.8 vs 19.8). The gains are most pronounced on Mistral-7B, where TIES and DARE degrade significantly below individual models, highlighting their poor suitability for the high-similarity single-task regime.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the model merging literature (Model Soup, TIES-Merging, DARE) from its traditional multi-task setting to a single-task, multi-seed scenario, arguing that the high cosine similarity among same-task models fundamentally changes the merging problem from interference mitigation to redundancy elimination and complementary information extraction. It builds on LoRA and its structural variants (DoRA, LoRA+) as the base PEFT method, and borrows TIES's sign-based conflict resolution for its Stage 1, while introducing a novel SVD-based subspace fusion (Stage 2) that has no direct precedent in prior merging work. The key conceptual departure is the explicit identification that existing merging methods are misaligned for single-task merging because they were designed for orthogonal, multi-task deltas rather than highly correlated, same-task deltas.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SeedLoRA offers a practical path to closing the LoRA–full fine-tuning gap in demanding domains (math reasoning, code generation) without sacrificing LoRA's parameter and memory efficiency, which is significant for resource-constrained deployment. The finding that merging three 1-epoch LoRA models outperforms a single 3-epoch LoRA model suggests a potentially new training paradigm for PEFT, though this result is demonstrated on a single benchmark and warrants broader validation. Open questions include how the method scales beyond 4–6 seed models (diminishing returns are observed), whether the SVD subspace fusion generalizes to other PEFT methods beyond LoRA, and whether the gains persist in multi-task or continual-learning settings where the high-similarity assumption may break down.
