## Problem

Vector-quantized networks suffer from index collapse (only a small fraction of codebook entries remain active) and, more fundamentally, the straight-through estimator causes task-loss gradients to bypass the codebook entirely, so codebook updates are driven solely by a distribution-matching VQ objective that is agnostic to the downstream task. This disconnect between codebook training and the actual reconstruction/generation objective limits codebook utilization and overall model quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252013/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images at resolutions ranging from 32×32 (CIFAR-10) to 256×256 (FFHQ) and produces reconstructed images, generated images (via MaskGIT or a causal Transformer), and a trained explicit codebook. Codebook sizes scale with dataset complexity (1,024 for VQVAE experiments, 16,384 for FFHQ, 65,536 for ImageNet). A key practical detail is that the hyper-net used during training is discarded after convergence, leaving only the generated codebook weights for downstream autoregressive or generative modeling, so inference cost is identical to standard VQ.

## Method

Meta-Quantization (MQ) reparameterizes the codebook through a hyper-net H_ψ that maps a learnable embedding to codebook entries, and formulates training as a bi-level optimization problem: the lower level optimizes the encoder-decoder pair with the codebook fixed, while the upper level optimizes the hyper-net via hyper-gradient descent using the (one-step-unrolled) optimal encoder-decoder as a function of ψ. This meta-objective replaces the standard VQ loss, enabling task-loss gradients to reach the codebook through both direct and indirect (best-response Jacobian) gradient paths. The hyper-gradient is computed efficiently via a one-step unrolling approximation combined with finite-difference estimation of the Hessian-vector product, avoiding expensive implicit differentiation. Because the hyper-net is overparameterized relative to the codebook, even sparsely selected codes propagate gradients through a large portion of the network, mitigating index collapse and accelerating convergence.

## Results

On ImageNet-1k (128×128), MQGAN achieves the best reconstruction rFID of 2.13 and LPIPS of 0.12 with 100% codebook utilization, outperforming SimVQ (rFID 2.24), VQGAN-LC (2.40), and VQGAN-FC (2.63). On FFHQ (256×256), MQGAN reaches rFID 4.25 and LPIPS 0.08, surpassing VQGAN-LC (4.65) and VQGAN-EMA (4.79). For image generation on CelebA via MaskGIT, MQVAE achieves FID 70.5, improving over the MaskGIT baseline (90.4) and the best prior variant Affine+OPT (74.8). On CIFAR-10, MQVAE attains MSE 3.05×10⁻³, perplexity 783.4, and LPIPS 0.29, the best across all metrics among all compared VQVAE variants.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MQVAE/MQGAN consistently outperforms all 13 baselines across every dataset and metric. On ImageNet-1k reconstruction, MQGAN beats the strongest explicit-codebook baseline SimVQ by 0.11 rFID (2.13 vs. 2.24) and the implicit-codebook methods FSQ/LFQ by 0.67/0.75 rFID, while also achieving the lowest LPIPS (0.12 vs. 0.13). On FFHQ, MQGAN improves rFID by 0.40 over VQGAN-LC (4.25 vs. 4.65) and by 0.54 over VQGAN-EMA (4.79), with a notable LPIPS gap (0.08 vs. 0.10). For CelebA generation via MaskGIT, MQVAE reduces FID by 19.9 points over the MaskGIT baseline (70.5 vs. 90.4) and by 4.3 over the best prior variant Affine+OPT (74.8). On CIFAR-10, MQVAE achieves the lowest MSE (3.05 vs. 3.21 for l2-normalization) and highest perplexity (783.4 vs. 769.3 for SQVAE), indicating superior codebook utilization. The original VQVAE and VQGAN baselines show severe index collapse (perplexity 14.0 on CIFAR-10; utilization 1.4% on ImageNet), which MQ resolves entirely.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the VQVAE/VQGAN architecture (van den Oord et al., 2017; Esser et al., 2021) and departs from the standard training paradigm where the codebook is optimized solely via the vector quantization objective while task-loss gradients bypass it through the straight-through estimator. Drawing on meta-learning (Finn et al., 2017), the authors reframe codebook training as a bi-level optimization problem: a hyper-net generates the codebook from a learnable embedding (upper level), while the encoder-decoder pair is trained with the codebook fixed (lower level). This positions the codebook as a hyperparameter in the spirit of hyperparameter optimization (Pedregosa, 2016), contrasting with prior approaches that either directly optimize code entries (VQVAE, VQGAN-FC) or replace the codebook entirely with non-learnable schemes (FSQ, LFQ).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical contribution is that the hyper-net can be discarded after training, leaving a standard codebook with no additional inference cost, making MQ a drop-in replacement for existing VQ pipelines. Achieving 100% codebook utilization while simultaneously improving reconstruction and generation quality addresses the long-standing index collapse problem from a fundamentally different angle than periodic reset or normalization tricks. Open questions include whether the bi-level optimization overhead (total wall time of 12.2h vs. 3.6h for VQVAE) remains acceptable at much larger scales, whether the approach generalizes to multi-level VQ (e.g., VQ-VAE-2) or non-image modalities like speech, and whether simpler single-level alternatives could capture most of the benefit.
