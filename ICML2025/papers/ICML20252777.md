## Problem

Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs) suffer from failure modes where they produce completely wrong solutions despite near-zero residual loss, manifesting as temporal gradual distortion. The paper identifies two root causes: a continuous-discrete mismatch between the PDE's continuous dynamics and the discrete collocation point sampling used in training, which blocks initial-condition propagation, and the simplicity bias of neural networks that drives the model toward over-smoothed trivial solutions (e.g., u=0).

## Input / Output

The method is fully data-free, taking only spatio-temporal coordinates (x, t) sampled on a 101×101 grid over the PDE domain as input, with no external training data. Each collocation point is embedded into a sub-sequence of k=7 consecutive temporal points (interval Δt=1e-2), and the model outputs the PDE solution u(x,t) at the first point of each sub-sequence. The same collocation point is predicted multiple times from different sub-sequence starting positions, with only the primary prediction retained as the final output while the others serve as alignment targets.

## Method

PINNMamba replaces the standard MLP backbone with a Mamba-based (Selective SSM) encoder that models temporal sub-sequences, providing a continuous-discrete articulation where the SSM's discrete form approximates the continuous system's trajectory via integration over each time interval. The key training innovation is sub-sequence contrastive alignment: overlapping sub-sequences of moderate granularity (k=7) are constructed for each collocation point, and a contrastive loss (Eq. 28) forces the diverse predictions of the same point across different sub-sequences to agree, thereby eliminating simplicity bias through agreement over diverse predictions. The SSM's HiPPO-parameterized state matrix unifies the scale of multi-order time derivatives, reducing loss landscape ruggedness, while the time-varying (input-dependent) SSM ensures prediction diversity. The architecture uses Wavelet activation functions and is trained with L-BFGS, achieving up to 86.3% error reduction over state-of-the-art PINN architectures on convection, reaction, and wave equations.

## Results

On the three standard PDE benchmarks (convection, reaction, and wave equations), PINNMamba achieves rMAE of 0.0188, 0.0094, and 0.0197 respectively, and rRMSE of 0.0201, 0.0217, and 0.0199, consistently outperforming all baselines. The headline improvement is up to 86.3% rMAE reduction over the strongest baseline (KAN on the wave equation: 0.1433 → 0.0197) and up to 95.8% over PINNsFormer on convection (0.4527 → 0.0188). PINNMamba achieves these results with 285,763 parameters, fewer than PINN (527K), QRes (397K), and PINNsFormer (454K), while only KAN is smaller (891 params) but far less accurate.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PINNMamba dominates all four baselines across every equation and metric. Against vanilla PINN (Raissi et al., 2019), PINNMamba reduces rMAE by 97.8% on convection (0.8514→0.0188), 99.0% on reaction (0.9803→0.0094), and 95.2% on wave (0.4101→0.0197), confirming that the failure modes (over-smoothing, pattern distortion) are essentially eliminated. Against QRes (Bu & Karpatne, 2021), the margin is similarly overwhelming, with QRes performing even worse than vanilla PINN on convection and wave. Against PINNsFormer (Zhao et al., 2024), the closest competitor on reaction (rMAE 0.0146 vs 0.0094), PINNMamba still wins on all three equations, with the largest gap on convection (0.4527→0.0188, a 95.8% reduction) and wave (0.3559→0.0197, a 94.5% reduction). Against KAN (Liu et al., 2024), which is the strongest baseline on wave (rMAE 0.1433), PINNMamba achieves an 86.3% error reduction (0.1433→0.0197) and a 96.9% reduction on convection (0.6049→0.0188). Notably, PINNMamba uses fewer parameters than PINN, QRes, and PINNsFormer while achieving the best accuracy, suggesting the SSM-based architecture is both more sample-efficient and better suited to capturing temporal dynamics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the PINN framework (Raissi et al., 2019) by replacing the standard MLP backbone with a Selective SSM (Mamba, Gu & Dao, 2023) encoder, positioning SSMs as a natural 'continuous-discrete articulation' that bridges the gap between PDE continuity and discrete collocation sampling. It builds directly on PINNsFormer (Zhao et al., 2024) by adopting its Wavelet activation and pseudo-sequence formulation, but departs from it by introducing a medium-granularity sub-sequence with contrastive alignment rather than a full pseudo-sequence. The theoretical contribution (Theorem 4.1) formalizes why finite collocation points cannot guarantee global PDE satisfaction, providing a principled justification for the architectural choice that prior sequence-based PINNs (Krishnapriyan et al., 2021; Zhao et al., 2024) addressed only heuristically.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clearer mechanistic explanation for PINN failure modes—attributing them to the interplay of simplicity bias and continuous-discrete mismatch rather than treating them as purely optimization difficulties—which could guide future architectural design beyond SSMs. The sub-sequence alignment loss is a lightweight, data-free mechanism that could potentially be grafted onto other sequence-based PINN variants. However, the evaluation is primarily on 1D PDEs, and the sequence-based approach hits out-of-memory limits on higher-dimensional PINNacle problems, leaving open whether the method scales to the 3D+ PDEs where PINNs are most practically needed. The OOM constraint and the reliance on a fixed sub-sequence length (sensitive to problem difficulty) are notable practical limitations.
