## Problem

In federated learning with heterogeneous client data, local models overfit their own distributions and even transferable features become distorted during aggregation. Existing methods either align local updates to the global model (risking overfitting) or enhance local generalization (risking misalignment after aggregation), but none simultaneously preserve generalizable features and maintain alignment through the aggregation step. The paper asks: what constraint enhances feature generalization during local training while remaining invariant under aggregation?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251188/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard RGB images (CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100, 32×32) and produces class labels via LeNet-5 or ResNet-18 classifiers. Unusually, the method consumes the global model's per-layer, per-channel weight vectors as constraint parameters: each client's gradient is modified in-place using the corresponding global weight vector before the local update is applied. No additional communication beyond the standard FedAvg model broadcast is required, and batch normalization layers are removed to expose raw weight magnitudes for the constraint mechanism.

## Method

FedCONST modifies the local training step of FedAvg by imposing two convex linear constraints on each gradient, applied per layer and per output channel: (1) a centralization constraint that subtracts the mean of the gradient vector (zeroing its sum), and (2) an orthogonal constraint that projects the gradient onto the hyperplane orthogonal to the global model's weight vector for that channel. The key insight is that global weight magnitudes serve as a communication-efficient proxy for Gradient Signal-to-Noise Ratio (GSNR): large weights indicate well-learned, generalizable features that should be preserved, while small weights indicate under-learned features that need more training. Because both constraints are linear and convex, they are preserved under the weighted-average aggregation step, ensuring the generalization effect survives the merge. The implementation is a stateless, per-batch gradient transformation (center + project) requiring no extra communication rounds or server-side state, and it can be plugged into any existing FL algorithm (FedProx, MOON, SCAFFOLD, FedDyn, FedSAM) as a drop-in local-update modification.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (Cross-Device, α=0.5), FedAvg+CONST achieves 54.28% top-1 accuracy with LeNet-5 (+8.16 over FedAvg) and 66.51% with ResNet-18 (+12.44 over FedAvg). Under the more heterogeneous Cross-Device α=0.2 setting, ResNet-18+CONST reaches 68.41% (FedAvg+CONST) and 68.07% (FedProx+CONST). On CIFAR-100 (Cross-Silo, α=0.5), the best result is 39.61% (FedSAM+CONST, ResNet-18), improving over vanilla FedSAM (38.43%) and FedAvg (33.51%). The largest single improvement is +14.15% (FedProx+CONST over FedProx on CIFAR-10 Cross-Device α=0.2, ResNet-18).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedCONST is applied as a plug-in on top of five existing FL algorithms (FedAvg, FedProx, MOON, SCAFFOLD, FedDyn) plus FedSAM, and consistently improves every one of them across all four evaluation settings. The largest gains appear for alignment-based methods: FedProx+CONST beats FedProx by up to +14.15% (ResNet-18, CIFAR-10 CD α=0.2), SCAFFOLD+CONST beats SCAFFOLD by up to +10.59% (ResNet-18, CIFAR-10 CS α=0.5), and FedDyn+CONST beats FedDyn by up to +11.65% (ResNet-18, CIFAR-10 CD α=0.5). Against the strongest generalization-focused baseline, FedSAM, the improvement is smaller but still positive: FedSAM+CONST reaches 72.64% vs. FedSAM's 69.45% on CIFAR-10 CS α=0.5 (ResNet-18), and 39.61% vs. 38.43% on CIFAR-100 CS α=0.5. Notably, the best overall accuracy in each setting is achieved by a CONST-augmented variant (e.g., SCAFFOLD+CONST at 75.09% for ResNet-18 on CIFAR-10 CS α=0.5), confirming that FedCONST complements rather than conflicts with existing alignment or generalization strategies.

## Relation to Prior Work

FedCONST builds directly on the FedAvg framework, modifying only the local update step with two linear convex constraints (centralization and orthogonality) derived from the global model's weight magnitudes. Conceptually, it repurposes the GSNR-based feature-strength insight from domain generalization (Michalkiewicz et al., 2023) into a federated setting, where direct GSNR computation is infeasible, by substituting global weight magnitude as a proxy. It positions itself as addressing a gap between alignment-focused methods (FedProx, MOON, SCAFFOLD, FedDyn) that risk preserving overfitting, and local-generalization methods (FedSAM, FedAlign) that lose coherence during aggregation, by enforcing constraints that are simultaneously convex (preserved under averaging) and feature-selective (boosting weak features while stabilizing strong ones).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-in design—requiring no additional communication rounds and no stateful server-side bookkeeping—makes FedCONST broadly applicable as a drop-in enhancement to any existing FL algorithm, which is practically valuable for deployment. However, the experimental validation is confined to CIFAR-10/100 with LeNet-5 and ResNet-18, leaving open whether the convex-constraint approach scales to larger datasets (ImageNet), deeper architectures, or more extreme heterogeneity regimes. The core conjecture that global weight magnitude reliably proxies GSNR is supported by correlation analysis and t-SNE visualizations but lacks a formal proof, and the Hessian analysis showing increased maximum eigenvalue alongside improved accuracy challenges the standard sharpness-generalization narrative in a way that warrants further investigation.
