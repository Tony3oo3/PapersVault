## Problem

Language agents for complex interactive tasks rely on outcome reward models that provide a single scalar at trajectory end, making it impossible to diagnose which intermediate steps were suboptimal or redundant. Existing process reward modeling approaches either require costly human step-level annotations or computationally heavy random rollouts, rendering them impractical for self-improvement of open-source language agents in environments with sparse, delayed feedback.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251510/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The agent receives a task description and a sequence of environment observations, producing ReAct-style responses (chain-of-thought reasoning followed by an action) at each step. The QNet, initialized from the SFT LLM with an appended two-layer MLP value head (hidden size 1024, ReLU), outputs a scalar Q-value for each state-action pair. During Q-guided inference, the agent samples M candidate actions per step and executes the one with the highest predicted Q-value, with the full trajectory constrained by environment-specific maximum step limits (5 for WebShop, 40 for ALFWorld/SciWorld).

## Method

QLASS constructs a four-stage pipeline: (1) supervised fine-tuning on expert ReAct trajectories, (2) building exploration trees from self-generated trajectories where each node stores state, action, reward, children, and Q-value, with pruning that limits expansion depth and propagates a stop signal back from zero-reward leaf nodes, (3) estimating Q-values at every node via a backward Bellman update (Q(s,a) = r + γ·max Q over children) that distills sparse outcome rewards into dense stepwise supervision, and (4) training a QNet with MSE loss on the collected (state, action, Q-value) triples. At inference, the QNet scores multiple sampled actions per step and the agent greedily selects the highest-Q action, effectively performing a Q-guided best-first search that prunes low-value branches without requiring explicit trajectory-level annotations.

## Results

On WebShop, QLASS achieves 70.3 reward, outperforming the best open-source baseline TS-LLM (67.7) and the closed-source GPT-4 (63.2). On SciWorld, QLASS scores 75.3 (seen) and 66.4 (unseen), surpassing GPT-4 (64.8/64.4) by over 10 points on the seen split. On ALFWorld, QLASS reaches 77.9 (seen) and 82.8 (unseen), exceeding GPT-4 (42.9/38.1) by more than 35 points. Under a 240K-token search budget, QLASS (70.3 on WebShop) already surpasses Best-of-N's peak of 68.4 at 400K tokens, demonstrating superior inference-time search efficiency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

QLASS outperforms all eight baselines across every benchmark. Against the strongest open-source process-reward baselines, QLASS beats TS-LLM by 2.6 (WebShop), 1.6 (SciWorld seen), 1.1 (SciWorld unseen), 3.6 (ALFWorld seen), and 3.7 (ALFWorld unseen), and beats MATH-SHEPHERD by 3.4, 2.8, 3.1, 5.0, and 5.9 respectively. Against training-based self-improvement methods, QLASS surpasses ETO by 2.9, 1.5, 1.4, 9.3, and 10.4, and PPO by 6.1, 15.9, 14.7, 55.8, and 53.7. Against inference-time search, QLASS exceeds Best-of-N by 2.4, 5.1, 8.8, 15.8, and 13.4, and does so with roughly half the token budget on WebShop. Against the closed-source Reflexion (GPT-4o), QLASS leads by 6.1, 15.0, 2.0, 32.2, and 27.6. In the self-training ablation (Figure 4), QLASS's Q-value process reward also outperforms the 'Reward' baseline from Yuan et al. (2024) and the 'Avg Reward' baseline, confirming that Bellman-based Q-value estimation is the most effective process signal for guiding data selection.

## Relation to Prior Work

QLASS adapts the Bellman optimality equation from classical Q-learning to the setting of language agents by structuring self-generated trajectories as exploration trees, then propagating outcome rewards backward through tree nodes to produce stepwise Q-value labels. This departs from prior process reward modeling work (Math-Shepherd, TS-LLM, ReST-MCTS*) that relies on costly random rollouts or MCTS, and from outcome-reward approaches (ETO, RFT, Best-of-N) that only score full trajectories. The agent architecture itself is ReAct-based, and the SFT stage follows FireAct, so the core novelty lies in the tree-structured Q-value distillation and the resulting Q-guided inference strategy rather than in the policy model or training paradigm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that Q-value-based process rewards can effectively guide open-source language agents at inference time, countering recent reports (KIMI-k1.5, DeepSeek-R1) that process reward modeling fails in practice. The data-efficiency result—retaining near-full performance with roughly half the expert annotations—is practically important for domains where expert trajectories are expensive to collect. Open questions include whether the approach scales to environments with much larger action spaces or longer horizons, whether the LLM-as-QNet-backbone design is necessary versus a lighter value head, and how the method behaves when the exploration tree is too shallow to capture long-range dependencies.
