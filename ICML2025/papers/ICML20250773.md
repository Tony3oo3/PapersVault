## Problem

CLIP's visual encoder, trained with global caption supervision, captures semantic meaning but struggles with fine-grained visual details such as color, spatial relationships, and object counting. Existing remedies—combining multiple encoders, masked fine-tuning, or distillation losses—either introduce heavy computational overhead or produce embeddings incompatible with the frozen text encoder, forcing costly re-tuning of downstream MLLMs. The paper asks: can CLIP's visual representations be enhanced with fine-grained perceptual information from vision-centric models like DINOv2 while preserving full compatibility with the text encoder and downstream LLMs?

## Input / Output

The alignment fine-tuning consumes only RGB images (1.28M from ImageNet-1K) processed by both CLIP's vision encoder and a frozen DINOv2 target model; no text data is used during training. The output is a modified CLIP vision encoder whose embeddings remain in the same feature space as the original, preserving cosine-similarity compatibility with the frozen text encoder. The aligned encoder can be directly swapped into pre-trained MLLMs (LLaVA, OpenFlamingo) without any additional vision-language alignment step, and the LLM component can optionally be fine-tuned via LoRA.

## Method

The core mechanism aligns CLIP and DINOv2 representations in kernel space rather than in the original feature space: the optimization minimizes the squared difference between pairwise kernel values k₁(f_θ(I_i), f_θ(I_j)) for CLIP and k₂(g(I_i), g(I_j)) for DINOv2, using a normalized polynomial kernel of degree 3. This preserves the macro-structure of the feature space (and thus text compatibility) while flexibly adjusting relative sample similarities to encode fine-grained visual patterns. An L₂ regularization term ‖f_θ(I) − f_θ₀(I)‖² anchors aligned embeddings near their original positions, and a proposition (adapted from Schlarmann et al.) bounds the resulting change in image-text cosine similarity. A Hoeffding-based concentration result guarantees that the gradient can be estimated from small batches, enabling efficient stochastic optimization (~30 hours on 2× RTX 4090 for ViT-L-14). The framework is architecture-agnostic and target-model-agnostic, demonstrated across ViT-B-16/L-14/L-14-336 backbones and with DINOv2 or MLCD as the target.

## Results

On zero-shot image classification across 12 benchmarks, the proposed kernel-based alignment improves average accuracy by 0.82% (ViT-B-16: 61.22→62.04%), 1.28% (ViT-L-14: 65.26→66.54%), and 1.03% (ViT-L-14-336: 66.10→67.13%) over the original CLIP, while preserving image-text retrieval performance (e.g., Flickr30K R@1 improves from 81.40 to 83.00 for ViT-L-14). On fine-grained tasks (counting, spatial reasoning, caption recognition), alignment yields larger gains: +5.51% average for linear probing with ViT-L-14. When the aligned encoder is plugged into LLaVA-1.5-7B, average VQA benchmark scores rise from 60.06 to 61.70 (ViT-L-14-336, +both alignment and LoRA fine-tuning), and OpenFlamingo-3B improves from 42.15 to 42.84 (0-shot) without any LLM fine-tuning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DIVA (Wang et al., ICLR 2025), the proposed kernel alignment wins on average zero-shot accuracy for both ViT-L-14 (66.54% vs. 65.45%, +1.09%) and ViT-L-14-336 (67.13% vs. 65.30%, +1.83%), and substantially outperforms it on retrieval (Flickr30K R@1: 83.00 vs. 78.70 for ViT-L-14; 84.60 vs. 80.20 for ViT-L-14-336), indicating DIVA degrades image-text alignment while the kernel method preserves it. Against AM-RADIO (Ranzinger et al., CVPR 2024), trained under the same ImageNet-1k protocol with CLIP and DINOv2 as teachers, the proposed method achieves 66.54% average zero-shot accuracy versus AM-RADIO's 58.21% (+8.33%), with particularly large gaps on fine-grained datasets (OxfordPets: 50.31 vs. 25.66; DTD: 93.40 vs. 83.62). Against Additive-MoF (Tong et al., CVPR 2024), which simply concatenates CLIP and DINOv2 features in LLaVA, the proposed alignment achieves a higher average VQA score (62.86% vs. 59.32%, +3.54%), excelling on text-heavy tasks (text-ocr: 58.89 vs. 45.93; text-pure: 50.51 vs. 18.66) while Additive-MoF leads on RefCOCO localization (69.69 vs. 65.79), confirming the trade-off between visual grounding and instruction-following that the kernel alignment resolves more effectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on CLIP's vision encoder and DINOv2's self-supervised visual representations, positioning itself against a family of approaches that either combine multiple encoders (Jiang et al., Tong et al.), fine-tune CLIP with vision-only objectives (Covert et al., Wu et al.), or use diffusion-based feedback (DIVA, Wang et al. 2025). The key departure is the use of kernel-space alignment: rather than aligning absolute feature positions (which disrupts text compatibility) or adding auxiliary losses during pre-training, the method aligns pairwise kernel similarities, preserving the macro-structure of CLIP's embedding space while adjusting fine-grained relative distances. This connects to the broader kernel methods literature in representation learning (He & Ozay 2022, Dehdashtian et al. 2024) but applies the kernel trick to cross-model alignment rather than distillation or evaluation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that the aligned CLIP encoder can be dropped into existing MLLMs (LLaVA, OpenFlamingo) without retraining the LLM or any projection layer, making visual-encoder upgrades a lightweight, image-only fine-tuning step (~30 GPU-hours on two 4090s) rather than a full retraining pipeline. Limitations include evaluation only on 7B-scale MLLMs and ImageNet-1K as the alignment dataset, leaving open whether gains persist at 70B scale or with larger, more diverse training data. The framework is also currently demonstrated only for the CLIP–DINOv2 pair, though experiments with SigLIP, MetaCLIP, and MLCD suggest broader applicability. A key open question is whether the kernel-alignment objective can be extended to align encoders with fundamentally different architectures or to incorporate temporal/video modalities.
