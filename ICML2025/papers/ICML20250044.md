## Problem

Existing diffusion-model-based inverse problem solvers require many sampling steps, accumulate errors from early diffusion iterations, and struggle with measurement noise. A key structural issue is that enforcing measurement consistency on the estimated clean image causes it to deviate from the diffusion trajectory, producing artifacts and feeding out-of-distribution inputs back into the pre-trained network. The paper asks how to enforce measurement consistency at every step while simultaneously preserving the diffusion trajectory and exploiting the implicit regularization of the network.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250044/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a degraded measurement vector y ∈ R^m, a forward operator A(·) (linear or non-linear, e.g., downsampling, blur, Fourier undersampling, phase retrieval), and a pre-trained pixel-space or latent-space diffusion model ε_θ. It outputs a single restored image x̂ ∈ R^n. A distinguishing detail is that the optimization variable is the noisy input v_t to the pre-trained network (not the network's output), and a noise-tolerance stopping criterion δ > σ_y√m prevents overfitting to measurement noise. The method supports arbitrary step sizes Δt = ⌊T/N⌋, decoupling the number of sampling iterations N from the training horizon T.

## Method

SITCOM enforces three step-wise consistency conditions at every sampling iteration: (C1) measurement consistency (A(x̂_0') ≈ y), (C2) backward consistency (x̂_0' must lie in the range of the Tweedie-network denoiser f, i.e., x̂_0' = f(v_t; t, ε_θ) for some v_t), and (C3) forward consistency (the input to ε_θ remains in-distribution via the RePaint resampling formula). The core mechanism is an inner-loop optimization over the network input v_t' that minimizes ‖A(f(v_t'; t, ε_θ)) − y‖² + λ‖x_t − v_t'‖² using Adam with early stopping, which simultaneously enforces C1 and C2 while the λ-term keeps v_t' close to x_t (supporting C3). After convergence, x̂_0' = f(v̂_t; t, ε_θ) is computed and mapped back to time t−1 via the resampling formula x_{t−1} = √(ᾱ_{t−1}) x̂_0' + √(1−ᾱ_{t−1}) η_t, guaranteeing C3. This formulation allows N ≪ T sampling steps with K gradient updates per step, and the paper shows that SITCOM with K=1 reduces to DPS+resampling, while larger K with Adam acts as an accelerated variant.

## Results

Across 8 image restoration tasks (5 linear, 3 non-linear) on FFHQ and ImageNet (100 test images each, σ_y=0.05), SITCOM achieves the best result in 42 out of 51 metric-dataset-task combinations and second-best in the remaining 9. Headline gains include >1 dB PSNR over the second-best method on Random Inpainting (FFHQ) with ~1/3 the runtime of DAPS, and >1 dB on Non-Uniform Deblurring (ImageNet) with ~1 minute less runtime than DAPS. On Super Resolution, SITCOM requires nearly half the runtime of DMPlug (FFHQ) and PGDM (ImageNet) while matching or exceeding their PSNR. On MRI reconstruction (fastMRI), SITCOM achieves the highest PSNR and SSIM among all baselines with significantly reduced runtime.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SITCOM outperforms DPS, DDNM, DAPS, and DMPlug in nearly all cases on both PSNR and runtime, with the most dramatic speed advantage over DAPS (e.g., ~1/3 runtime on RIP-FFHQ, ~1 min less on NDB-ImageNet). Against DCDP, RED-diff, and PGDM, SITCOM achieves >3 dB higher PSNR on several tasks (SR, RIP, GDB on FFHQ; RIP, GDB, HDR on ImageNet) at the cost of longer runtime, though under matched-PSNR constraints (Appendix I.2) SITCOM is also faster. On Phase Retrieval, DAPS edges out SITCOM by a small PSNR margin (~0.3 dB), though SITCOM-ODE (using an ODE solver instead of Tweedie's formula) surpasses DAPS at ~3× the runtime. On Gaussian Deblurring (ImageNet), SITCOM reports the best LPIPS but trails DAPS by 0.66 dB in PSNR and 0.025 in SSIM. In the latent-space setting (Table 5), Latent-SITCOM beats ReSample on all tasks and matches or exceeds Latent-DAPS on 7 of 8 tasks (losing only on Phase Retrieval by ~1 dB on FFHQ), while generally requiring less runtime than both baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

SITCOM extends the optimization-based diffusion sampling lineage initiated by DPS (Chung et al., 2023), RePaint's resampling (Lugmayr et al., 2022), and ReSample (Song et al., 2023). The key conceptual departure is shifting the optimization variable from the network output (as in DAPS, ReSample, DCDP) to the network input, drawing an explicit analogy to compressed sensing with generative models (CSGM, Bora et al., 2017). This reparameterization enables a new 'backward consistency' condition that constrains intermediate reconstructions to lie in the range of the Tweedie-network denoiser, which prior methods did not enforce. Proposition D.1 cleanly situates SITCOM as an accelerated (Adam-preconditioned) variant of DPS with resampling, clarifying the lineage while the three-condition framework (measurement, backward, forward) provides a systematic taxonomy absent in prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The three-condition framework gives practitioners a principled checklist for designing efficient DM-based IP solvers, and the demonstrated >5 dB PSNR drop when removing backward consistency (Table 2) validates that network-input optimization is not merely a trick but a critical structural component. The method achieves competitive quality with significantly fewer sampling steps (N≪T), which is practically important given that sampling cost is the dominant bottleneck in DM-based restoration. Limitations include restriction to non-blind settings (full forward model required), 2D-only operation, and dependence on a pre-trained pixel-space DM; extending to 3D medical reconstruction and blind inverse problems remains open. The modest quality gains over SOTA (often <1 dB) suggest the primary value is in efficiency and systematic understanding rather than a quality breakthrough.
