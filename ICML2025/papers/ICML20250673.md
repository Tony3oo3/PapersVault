## Problem

Existing GNN-based recommender systems face a dual challenge: deep architectures needed to capture high-order user-item relationships incur prohibitive inference costs, and prior knowledge distillation methods (GLNN, KRD, LightHGNN) rely primarily on soft labels that fail to preserve the intricate structural and topological knowledge inherent in hypergraphs. The paper asks how to transfer both structural and positional knowledge from a powerful HGNN+language-model teacher to a lightweight student while maintaining recommendation accuracy.

## Input / Output

The method consumes two modalities: a user-item interaction hypergraph (incidence matrices for users and items) and textual reviews processed by DeBERTa into 768-dimensional disentangled embeddings. During training, the full teacher (dual HGNNs + DeBERTa + MLP, ~145M parameters) is used offline; at inference, only the 0.5M-parameter CompactGCN student is deployed, producing rating predictions and Top-K recommendations with O(|E|d) complexity. The student's embeddings are initialized via a γ-weighted interpolation between its own learned representations and the frozen teacher embeddings, with γ annealed over training epochs.

## Method

SHARP-Distill employs a teacher-student framework where the teacher integrates dual HGNNs (propagating via the normalized hypergraph Laplacian) with DeBERTa review embeddings, aligned through cross-modal and intra-domain InfoNCE contrastive losses before fusion via a k-layer MLP. The student is CompactGCN, a single-layer GCN without non-linear activation that computes Z^s = Â^s X W^s in one matrix multiplication. The key innovation is a contrastive distillation objective that combines cosine similarity between teacher and student embeddings with a hypergraph positional encoding P_u = Σ β^k (L_H)^k e_u, capturing multi-hop topological structure beyond what soft labels provide. A temperature-annealed soft-label KL term (scaled by T²) is combined with the contrastive losses, and an embedding interpolation mechanism (γ Z^s + (1−γ) Z^t) gradually shifts reliance from teacher to student representations during training. This yields up to 68× faster inference than HGNN and 40× faster than LightGCN while matching or exceeding state-of-the-art accuracy across five datasets.

## Results

SHARP-Distill achieves the best P@10, R@10, and N@10 in 11 out of 15 metric-dataset combinations across five real-world datasets. Headline numbers include P@10 of 4.27% on Sports (vs. 3.56% for the best baseline HGAtt), 6.97% on Beauty (vs. 6.60% for POD), and 13.75% on Amazon CDs (vs. 13.96% for HCCF). In inference time, the student model runs at 9.77 ms on Amazon CDs (136,701 nodes), achieving 68× speedup over HGNN (668.23 ms) and 40× over LightGCN (395.45 ms), while using only 0.5M parameters versus 145M for the teacher.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SHARP-Distill dominates the Sports dataset across all three metrics (P@10 4.27 vs. 3.56 for HGAtt; R@10 3.63 vs. 2.71; N@10 3.24 vs. 1.74), and also leads on Beauty and Yelp in all metrics. Against the LLM-based baselines, SAID narrowly wins on Cellphones P@10 (7.83 vs. 7.54) and ties HGAtt on CDs N@10 (12.24 vs. 12.17), but SHARP-Distill recovers on Cellphones R@10 (5.77 vs. 5.72) and N@10 (4.77 vs. 4.69). Among distillation baselines, KRD and LightHGNN are consistently outperformed on every dataset and metric; for example, on Yelp N@10 SHARP-Distill reaches 2.37 vs. 1.73 (KRD) and 1.74 (LightHGNN). In the Appendix D comparison with RecSys-specific distillation methods, SHARP-Distill outperforms both UnKD and Graph-less on all 20 metric-dataset combinations, with average P@10 gains of 11.6%, R@10 gains of 13.6%, and N@10 gains of 12.0%, while also achieving 32–52% lower inference latency. The only metric where SHARP-Distill does not achieve the top score is CDs P@10 (13.75 vs. HCCF's 13.96), a 0.21-point gap.

## Relation to Prior Work

SHARP-Distill extends the HGNN-to-lightweight-model distillation paradigm established by LightHGNN (Feng et al., 2024) and KRD (Wu et al., 2023), which distill deep hypergraph structures into shallow MLPs using soft labels alone. The key departure is replacing soft-label-only transfer with a contrastive learning mechanism (InfoNCE) that explicitly aligns structural embeddings and hypergraph positional encodings between teacher and student, addressing what the authors identify as a fundamental limitation of prior distillation methods in preserving high-order relational knowledge. Additionally, the teacher model integrates DeBERTa for multi-modal (graph + text) representation, a combination not present in prior HGNN distillation work, and the student is a single-layer GCN (CompactGCN) rather than an MLP, retaining some graph structure while eliminating non-linear activations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 68× inference speedup over HGNN and 40× over LightGCN while maintaining competitive accuracy makes high-order hypergraph-based recommendation practically deployable in latency-sensitive production systems, addressing a real bottleneck that has limited HGNN adoption. The contrastive-learning-based distillation (combining embedding similarity with hypergraph positional encoding) offers a more principled knowledge transfer mechanism than soft labels, and the theoretical bounds (spectral decay approximation, bounded student error) provide guarantees absent in prior empirical distillation work. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond bipartite user-item hypergraphs, whether DeBERTa is truly necessary versus lighter text encoders (the ablation shows CL contributes more than DeBERTa), and how the method behaves under extreme sparsity or cold-start conditions where review data is unavailable.
