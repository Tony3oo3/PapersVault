## Problem

State-of-the-art text-to-image generative models store all visual knowledge implicitly in fixed parameters trained on closed datasets, causing significant hallucinations and distortions when generating fine-grained or unseen novel real-world objects (e.g., Tesla Cybertruck). Existing retrieval-augmented generation methods for text-to-image rely on similarity matching, which retrieves the most visually similar image rather than the one that actually compensates for the generator's missing knowledge, limiting their effectiveness.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251682/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt and a retrieved reference image from a real-object-based database (built from ImageNet, Stanford Cars, Stanford Dogs, and Oxford Flowers training sets; Google Images for novel objects) and produces a generated realistic image. The framework is modular across generator types: for diffusion models (U-Net or DiT-based), the reference image is injected via a ControlNet branch; for autoregressive models, it serves as a direct image condition. The generator itself remains frozen throughout.

## Method

The core contribution is a self-reflective contrastive learning procedure that trains a reflective retriever to find images containing the generator's *missing* knowledge rather than the most similar ones. Concretely, the generator first produces an image from a text prompt; the most similar real-object image to that generated image (by cosine similarity) is selected as a 'reflective negative,' encoding what the generator already knows. The retriever (CLIP vision encoder + MLP head, with frozen CLIP text encoder) is then trained with a contrastive loss where the text prompt embedding is the positive and both in-batch negatives and the reflective negative are pushed away, forcing the embedding space to favor images outside the generator's generation space yet close to the text prompt. At inference, the trained retriever selects a reference image that expands the generator's effective generation space toward the text prompt, and the generator produces the final image conditioned on both the prompt and this reference.

## Results

RealRAG consistently improves all three metrics (CLIP-I↑, CLIP-T↑, FID↓) across all six generators on three fine-grained benchmarks. The largest gains appear with autoregressive models: Emu achieves a 16.18% FID reduction on Stanford Cars (86.73→70.55) and a 20.48% FID reduction on Oxford Flowers (84.33→63.85). For DiT-based models, Flux gains 5.77% FID on Stanford Cars (58.47→52.28), while U-Net-based SD V2.1 gains 3.52% FID on Stanford Cars (64.99→58.98). Average FID improvements across all three benchmarks are 6.19% (Stanford Cars), 3.62% (Stanford Dogs), and 8.94% (Oxford Flowers).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RealRAG outperforms all six baselines across every metric and every dataset, with no losses or ties. The largest absolute gains are with autoregressive models: Emu improves FID by 16.18 points on Stanford Cars and 20.48 points on Oxford Flowers, while OmniGen gains 11.80 FID points on Oxford Flowers. Among U-Net-based diffusion models, SD V2.1 shows the strongest improvement (FID 64.99→58.98 on Stanford Cars, +10.26 CLIP-I on Stanford Dogs), while SD XL gains 7.79 FID points on Oxford Flowers. For DiT-based models, Flux achieves a 6.19 FID reduction on Stanford Cars and 4.20 FID on Oxford Flowers, while SD V3 gains 5.34 FID on Stanford Cars. The paper also reports that classification accuracy (via OpenCLIP) improves by 3.89% on average for AR models, 3.48% for U-Net models, and 2.99% for DiT models, confirming that the FID/CLIP gains translate to more accurate fine-grained object identity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends retrieval-augmented generation from NLP to text-to-image synthesis, departing from prior image-RAG methods (RDMs, KNN-Diffusion, ReImagen) that use retrieval for training-time database switching or OOD generation. Its core departure is the self-reflective contrastive learning objective, which trains the retriever to find images containing the generator's *missing* knowledge rather than the most text-similar images, using the generator's own outputs as reflective negatives. The framework builds on CLIP as the retrieval backbone and is positioned as the first unified, modular RAG pipeline applicable across U-Net-based diffusion, DiT-based diffusion, and autoregressive generators without retraining the generator itself.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that a single retrieval module can plug into all three major t2i architecture families and consistently reduce hallucination on fine-grained and unseen objects, the paper offers a practical, training-free path to improving deployment-ready generators. Open questions include how the approach scales to open-domain (non-curated) image databases, whether the self-reflective negative sampling remains effective as generator quality improves, and whether the ControlNet-based conditioning for diffusion models generalizes to more complex compositional prompts. The reliance on a fixed real-object database (ImageNet, Stanford Cars, etc.) limits immediate applicability to truly novel, long-tail objects outside these categories.
