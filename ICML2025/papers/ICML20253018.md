## Problem

Existing scene graph generation methods struggle with long-tail predicate distributions: they either focus exclusively on tail-class enhancement while neglecting head classes, or use VAE/GAN-based feature augmentation that produces highly similar features before and after enhancement, failing to meaningfully expand the predicate representation space. Furthermore, treating each sample as an independent entity in the decision layer ignores the homogeneity among similar predicate representations, increasing learning difficulty and decision ambiguity for the classifier.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20253018/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images as input and produces scene graph triplets (subject, predicate, object) with predicate category labels. It operates as a plug-in module integrated with existing SGG baselines (Motifs, VCTree, Transformer, PE-Net), using a frozen Faster R-CNN detector for object proposals and RoIAlign for feature extraction. On Visual Genome it handles 150 object and 50 predicate categories; on GQA, 200 object and 100 predicate categories; on Open Images V6, 600 object categories with 391k annotated relationships.

## Method

NoDIS introduces a three-stage pipeline: (1) Noise-Guided Predicate Representation Extraction uses cross-attention with learnable Query Tokens to build predicate representations from entity pairs, then injects a single Gaussian noise perturbation and iteratively subtracts entity-strengthened components to decouple clean, independent predicate features; (2) a conditional diffusion model reconstructs predicate features by initializing the reverse process with a Gaussian distribution parameterized by the mean and variance of the extracted predicate representations (rather than pure random noise), with cross-attention conditioning on timestep embeddings, predicate prototypes, and predicate features to guide reconstruction toward the correct category distribution, thereby expanding intra-class feature diversity; (3) a VQ-VAE-style discretization encoder maps the diverse reconstructed features to the nearest entry in a learnable codebook initialized from predicate prototypes, aggregating same-category representations into unified vectors that reduce intra-class variance and align feature distributions with decision-layer weights. The key insight is the complementary two-step strategy: diffusion first increases representation diversity to combat underfitting on rare predicates, then discretization restores homogeneity to simplify the decision layer's learning problem.

## Results

On the Visual Genome dataset (PredCls task), integrating NoDIS with PE-Net achieves mR@50/100 of 38.72/41.93 and F@50/100 of 43.69/47.16, outperforming the strongest baselines (HiKER: 39.3/41.2 mR@50/100; CFA: 35.7/38.2). On Open Images V6 (SGDet), Transformer-NoDIS achieves wmAP_rel of 38.87 and score_wtd of 45.95, surpassing BCTR (36.0/43.7) and PE-Net (36.6/44.9). The method consistently improves mR@K by 1.5–7.2 points over its respective base model across all three VG tasks (PredCls, SGCls, SGDet), with the largest gains on tail-predicate recall (e.g., Transformer-NoDIS tail R@100 = 15.16% vs. baseline 7.9%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

NoDIS is integrated as a plug-in module into four base models (GCL, Motifs, Transformer, PE-Net) and consistently improves mR@K by 1.5–16.3 points over each base. Against the strongest standalone baselines, PE-Net+NoDIS (mR@50/100 = 38.72/41.93) narrowly trails HiKER on mR@50 (39.3) but surpasses it on mR@100 (41.93 vs. 41.2) and F@100 (47.16 vs. 23.2). On the SGDet task, Transformer+NoDIS achieves the best mR@50/100 (16.91/19.25) among all compared methods, exceeding DCNet (14.3/17.3) and GCL (13.6/15.5). On Open Images V6, Transformer-NoDIS leads in wmAP_rel (38.87) and score_wtd (45.95), beating BCTR (36.0/43.7) and PE-Net (36.6/44.9), though PE-Net retains a higher R@50 (76.5 vs. 74.11). On GQA, the method achieves competitive but not state-of-the-art results (e.g., Motifs+NoDIS mR@50/100 = 31.21/32.64 vs. HTCL 32.6/33.9), with the authors acknowledging that the most recent SOTA methods (HTCL, Inf) still lead on this dataset.

## Relation to Prior Work

NoDIS builds on the DDPM diffusion framework (Ho et al., 2020) and VQ-VAE-style discrete representation learning (Van Den Oord et al., 2017), repurposing both for predicate feature enhancement in scene graph generation. It departs from prior VAE/GAN-based feature augmentation methods (Li et al., 2024a; Wang et al., 2023a) that rely on coarse-grained latent features and produce highly similar representations before and after enhancement, by instead using conditional diffusion to genuinely expand the intra-class representation space. The paper also extends the standard SGG pipeline (Faster R-CNN + Transformer-based predicate refinement) with a two-stage representation strategy: first increasing diversity via diffusion, then enforcing homogeneity via a learnable discretization encoder, a combination not previously explored in the SGG literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a principled two-pronged solution to the long-tail predicate bias problem—expanding representation diversity to capture tail-class information while simultaneously enforcing intra-class consistency to ease the decision layer's learning burden. However, the authors candidly acknowledge that diffusion models are 'somewhat uncontrollable' for feature-level enhancement, and that adding the necessary constraints (conditional noise, discretization) significantly weakens the diffusion model's generative capacity, leaving open the question of how to design a diffusion process that retains both robustness and controllability for feature enhancement. The moderate performance gains (roughly 2–3% mR@100) and the 40–60M parameter overhead limit immediate practical adoption, but the diversity-then-homogeneity framing could inform representation learning in other long-tail classification settings.
