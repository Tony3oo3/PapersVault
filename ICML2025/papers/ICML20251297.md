## Problem

Existing text-to-motion synthesis methods are locked to fixed skeletal templates (typically human), making them unable to handle the heterogeneous joint counts, topologies, and rest poses found in diverse objects like dragons, centipedes, and horses. Compounding this, no comprehensive motion dataset exists that spans a large vocabulary of species with rich, multi-level text annotations suitable for text-driven synthesis.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a text prompt (available at three granularity levels: short, mid, long) and a skeletal template specifying the joint tree topology and per-joint rest pose offsets. It outputs a 3D joint rotation sequence of shape F×J×3 (Euler angles in ZXY order) with F=90 frames and J varying up to 150 joints depending on the object's rig. Text is encoded via SigLIP-SO400M-patch14-384, while the skeletal template is encoded through dedicated TreePE and RestPE modules that are added to per-joint tokens rather than collapsed into a single frame-level representation.

## Method

The Generalized Motion Diffusion Model (G-MDM) is built on SiT-S but preserves the joint dimension throughout denoising by applying independent linear projections per joint (Eq. 5), unlike prior work that collapses all joints into one token per frame. Two novel positional encodings—TreePE (encoding the root-to-joint path in the skeletal tree) and RestPE (MLP with sinusoidal encoding of rest-pose offsets)—are added to each joint token, enabling dynamic adaptation to arbitrary topologies and rest configurations. Training is two-stage: a pose diffusion model with spatial attention is first trained conditioned on multi-view rendered images, then frozen and augmented with temporal attention blocks to capture motion dynamics. Rig augmentation (GPT-4o-guided bone length scaling, joint removal/subdivision, and rest pose resetting) expands the Truebones Zoo dataset from ~1K motions to 26K while preserving original motion dynamics. Classifier-free guidance with SigLIP text embeddings provides text conditioning at each denoising step.

## Results

In the user study, our method achieves 65.60% preference, substantially outperforming SO-MDMs (10.67%), Retargeting (9.07%), w/o Rig Augmentation (12.40%), and w/GPT-Caption (2.27%). On Motion Stability Index (MSI), our method scores 8.78×10³, the highest among all data-driven methods, though slightly below the non-learning Retargeting baseline (9.93×10³). In the AUC-based generalization evaluations (Figure 4), our approach achieves the highest Area-Under-the-Curve for both rig adaptability (Coverage AUC on varied rigs) and novel motion dynamics (both Coverage and Novelty AUC), demonstrating superior generalization to unseen skeletal configurations and motion patterns.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against Single-Object Motion Diffusion Models (SO-MDMs, Tevet et al., 2023), which are trained individually per object on a fixed skeleton, and a non-learning Retargeting approach. Our method decisively outperforms SO-MDMs on user preference (65.60% vs. 10.67%) and MSI (8.78 vs. 7.21), and achieves the highest AUC in both rig-adaptability and novel-motion-dynamics evaluations (Figure 4), confirming that amortized learning across diverse skeletons generalizes far better than per-object models. Against Retargeting, our method wins on preference (65.60% vs. 9.07%) and on all AUC-based generalization metrics, but loses slightly on raw smoothness (MSI 8.78 vs. 9.93), as retargeting directly transfers motion without learning. The ablated variants (w/o Rig Augmentation, w/GPT-Caption) further confirm that both rig augmentation and high-quality human-annotated captions are critical: removing either causes large drops in AUC and preference scores.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Human Motion Diffusion Model (Tevet et al., 2023) and the SiT-S diffusion transformer (Ma et al., 2024) by replacing the fixed-skeleton assumption with explicit skeletal configuration encodings—Tree Positional Encoding (Shiv & Quirk, 2019) for topology and a novel Rest Pose Encoding for joint offsets—enabling a single model to handle arbitrary joint hierarchies. It departs from prior non-human motion synthesis work (SinMDM, OmniMotion-GPT, MAS) that still relies on per-object fixed rigs or object-specific retargeting, and from rig-blending methods (CharacterMixer, Skin Mixer) that are limited to retargeting rather than generative synthesis. The two-stage factorized spatial-temporal attention design draws from text-to-video diffusion (Blattmann et al., 2023) to manage the increased computational cost of variable joint counts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first unified framework for text-to-motion synthesis across heterogeneous skeletal templates, and the annotated Truebones Zoo dataset (70+ species, multi-level captions) provides a much-needed benchmark for large-vocabulary object motion. Key limitations include the absence of root translation, limited physical plausibility of augmented rigs, and inability to generalize to human skeletons zero-shot due to the dataset's non-human focus. Open questions include whether scaling to larger corpora (e.g., Objaverse-XL) would close the gap to human motion, whether automated physical validation can make augmented rigs production-ready, and whether the TreePE/RestPE formulation transfers to other hierarchical generative tasks beyond skeletal animation.
