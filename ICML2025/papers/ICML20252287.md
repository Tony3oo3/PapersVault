## Problem

Existing panoramic image outpainting methods address spherical distortion only through soft regularizations (circular padding, spherical loss, depth cues) applied during end-to-end training, i.e., in a macro way. This indirect approach fails to fully exploit the intrinsic spherical geometry of equirectangular-projected panoramas, leading to irregular 3D arrangements and artifacts at the boundaries of generated content.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252287/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a masked panoramic image in ERP format (1024×512) where the mask indicates known regions, along with a text prompt (either a generic 'Outpainting' or scene-specific), and produces a fully completed 360°×180° panoramic image. Internally, the masked image is encoded to a 128×64 latent feature via a VAE, and the repaint technique during inference ensures that masked (known) regions remain pixel-identical to the input while only the unmasked regions are generated.

## Method

SpND incorporates the sphere nature directly into the diffusion model architecture in three ways. First, it samples i.i.d. Gaussian noise uniformly on the 3D sphere surface and projects it into ERP format, producing a non-i.i.d. structural prior that matches the varying pixel density of ERP (dense at equator, sparse at poles). Second, it introduces a Spherical Deformable Convolution (SDC) layer that replaces the regular grid of standard deformable convolution with a spherical grid derived from gnomonic projection, while constraining learnable offsets to preserve the spherical prior. Third, the full SpND model uses a Circular Mask Encoder (CME) to encode the masked input, a prior injection layer to blend the ERP structural prior with encoded features, and a spherical net of SpNBs (each containing an SDC layer plus a vanilla deformable convolution for compatibility) that guides a frozen pre-trained Stable Diffusion model in a ControlNet-style architecture. Training freezes the diffusion backbone and VAE, updating only the CME and spherical net with the standard diffusion denoising loss.

## Results

On Matterport3D, SpND achieves FID 9.08 and FIDhori 10.64, reducing FID by over 50% compared to the strongest baseline PanoDiffusion (FID 39.43). With specific text prompts (SpND_prompt), performance further improves to FID 6.67 and FIDhori 6.58. On Structured3D, SpND reaches FID 7.37 and FIDhori 6.05 (SpND_prompt: FID 4.60, FIDhori 4.75), outperforming PanoDiffusion (FID 9.80) and PanoDiff (FID 11.11). SpND also achieves the best Avg (0.62) and Precision (0.52–0.62) scores across both datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SpND outperforms all six baselines on FID and FIDhori across both datasets. Against the closest competitor PanoDiffusion, SpND reduces FID from 39.43 to 9.08 on Matterport3D (a 77% reduction) and from 9.80 to 7.37 on Structured3D. PanoDiff achieves slightly higher recall on Matterport3D (0.75 vs. 0.72) and Structured3D (0.69 vs. 0.62), but its FID (13.45 / 11.11) and precision (0.24 / 0.36) are substantially worse than SpND's. GAN-based methods SIG-SS and TFill exhibit extremely low recall (≤0.01) and high FID (>64), indicating poor diversity and quality. OmniDreamer and AOG-Net also lag significantly, with FID values of 51.69 and 38.56 on Matterport3D respectively. With specific prompts, SpND_prompt further extends the lead, achieving FID 6.67 (Matterport3D) and 4.60 (Structured3D), the best results across all metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in panoramic outpainting, where prior methods (PanoDiffusion, PanoDiff, AOG-Net) address spherical distortion through soft regularisations—panoramic-guided losses, depth modality cues, or circular padding—applied at the loss level in a 'macro' manner. SpND instead embeds spherical geometry directly into the network architecture ('micro' way) via two novel components: i.i.d. spherical noise projected into ERP format as a structural prior, and a Spherical Deformable Convolution (SDC) layer that uses a gnomonic-projection-based spherical grid (from SphereNet) as the initial sampling grid for deformable convolution. The overall pipeline follows the ControlNet architecture for conditioning a pre-trained latent diffusion model (Stable Diffusion), with the RePaint technique handling mask preservation at inference, making the contribution primarily architectural rather than a new training paradigm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The micro-level incorporation of spherical geometry offers a principled alternative to ad-hoc loss regularisations, and the 50% FID reduction over PanoDiffusion on Matterport3D demonstrates that architectural priors can substantially outperform loss-based guidance for spherical content. The SDC layer, combining a spherical initial grid with constrained learnable offsets, is a reusable component potentially applicable to other spherical-surface generation or understanding tasks. However, the reliance on the RePaint technique in latent space introduces slight distortion at mask boundaries, and the approach is validated only on indoor/outdoor panoramic datasets (Matterport3D, Structured3D) with a single pre-trained diffusion backbone, leaving open whether the spherical noise and SDC design generalise to higher resolutions, different projection formats, or other diffusion architectures.
