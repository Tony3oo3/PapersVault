## Problem

Kernel-based tests for high-order interactions (dHSIC, Lancaster, Streitberg) require 100–1000 permutations to approximate null distributions, making them computationally intractable when the number of variable subsets grows combinatorially. While a permutation-free pairwise test (xHSIC) existed for d=2, no analogous method was available for d>2, leaving a gap in scalable detection of joint independence and partial factorisations among three or more variables.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 2n i.i.d. multivariate samples, which are split into two disjoint halves of size n, along with a user-specified kernel (e.g., Gaussian with median-heuristic bandwidth). Only the off-diagonal n×n blocks of the resulting 2n×2n kernel matrices are used, so the effective computation operates on cross-sample interactions between the two halves. The output is a single standard-normal test statistic per subhypothesis, compared against a fixed N(0,1) quantile to yield a rejection decision—no resampling loop is needed.

## Method

The core mechanism combines V-statistics (rather than the U-statistics used in the original xHSIC) with a novel cross-centring operator C_u K C_ℓ that centres rows and columns using the two disjoint sample halves separately, yielding an asymptotically equivalent kernel that produces a standard normal null distribution. This cross-centring is the key extension that makes the permutation-free scheme work for d>2: it allows the Lancaster and Streitberg interaction statistics to be written as Hadamard products of cross-centred kernel matrices, each subhypothesis yielding an independent N(0,1) statistic. Three tests are derived: x-dHSIC for joint independence, x-LI for Lancaster factorisation (singleton-containing bipartitions), and x-SI for Streitberg/complete factorisation (non-singleton bipartitions), all with O(dn²) time complexity. For d=2, the formulation recovers a simpler x-HSIC_V that outperforms the original xHSIC in power, and the combined x-LI + x-SI strategy decouples the n² scaling from the combinatorial number of partitions, achieving >100× speedup over permutation-based counterparts.

## Results

The permutation-free tests achieve a 100-fold computational speedup over their permutation-based counterparts (p=100), with x̄dHSIC matching dHSIC power while running ~100× faster on d=4 MVGs. For the 5-way XOR factorisation, x̄SI+x̄LI computes in 0.5 s at n=500 versus ~6 minutes for permutation-based SI, and remains feasible at d=10 (<1 s) where SI is already intractable at d=6. In the feature selection experiment, x̄SI+x̄LI is the only method that recovers the exact ground-truth feature set (MSE 4.35), while all pairwise and lower-order methods (RFE, SHAP, x̄HSIC, x̄dHSIC, x̄LI) either include confounding variables or miss generating variables. On S&P 500 data (504 stocks, 1004 samples), the method scales to detect 2- through 5-way interactions across 11 sectors without combinatorial blowup.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against permutation-based dHSIC (Pfister et al., 2018), x̄dHSIC achieves comparable statistical power on d=4 MVGs but runs ~100× faster (p=100) and ~200× faster (p=200) in the DAG causal discovery setting, reaching perfect DAG accuracy with roughly two-thirds the samples. Against permutation-based LI (Sejdinovic et al., 2013a), x̄LI is marginally less powerful in V-structure detection but runs ~100× faster (0.082 s vs 7.6 s); both outperform KCI (Zhang et al., 2011), which rapidly degrades as noise dimension increases. Against permutation-based SI (Liu et al., 2023b), x̄SI+x̄LI achieves superior power on the 5-way XOR while being orders of magnitude faster (0.5 s vs ~6 min at n=500), and remains computationally feasible at d=10 where SI is intractable by d=6. In the feature selection task, x̄SI+x̄LI is the sole method matching ground truth (MSE 4.35), outperforming RFE (4.47), SHAP (4.67), x̄HSIC (4.67), and all lower-order tests (5.01). For the d=2 pairwise case, the paper's x̄HSIC_V formulation outperforms both the original HSIC (Gretton et al., 2007) and x̄HSIC (Shekhar et al., 2023) in statistical power while retaining the permutation-free guarantee.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the permutation-free independence testing framework of Shekhar et al. (2023), which achieved a standard normal null distribution for pairwise HSIC via data-splitting and U-statistics, to arbitrary-order joint independence (dHSIC), Lancaster, and Streitberg interaction tests. The key methodological departure is the introduction of cross-centring—a data-splitting analogue of standard kernel centring—which enables the Lancaster and Streitberg tests (previously requiring expensive permutation schemes) to also yield standard normal null distributions. By switching from U-statistics to V-statistics, the authors reduce computational overhead while retaining estimation accuracy, achieving O(dn²) complexity across all three test families.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 100-fold (or greater) computational speedup makes high-order interaction testing practical for settings where permutation-based methods are infeasible, such as the S&P 500 application with 504 variables (8 hours vs. an estimated 2+ weeks) and DAG causal discovery over thousands of candidate structures. A key limitation is the i.i.d. assumption underlying the data-splitting scheme, which restricts applicability to time series, network, or spatial data where dependencies exist across samples. Open directions include extending the permutation-free framework to conditional independence tests, non-stationary data, and incorporating scalable kernel approximations (Nyström, random Fourier features) to further reduce the n² bottleneck.
