## Problem

While μP provides well-established rules for scaling learning rates across model widths, it does not address how to scale AdamW's weight decay hyperparameter with model or dataset size. The paper shows that the standard practice of keeping weight decay fixed under μP scaling actually breaks learning rate transferability for AdamW, because the implied EMA timescale changes with model size. This gap is critical for the common workflow of prototyping on small models and transferring hyperparameters to the final large-scale training run.

## Input / Output

Given a base model's tuned learning rate η_base and weight decay λ_base, along with a target width scaling factor s = fan_in/fan_in_base and dataset size N, the method produces the corresponding optimal λ for the target configuration. The key transferable quantity is τ_epoch = 1/(ηλN/B), which measures how many epochs of past updates the EMA averages over. The scaling rules are: λ ∝ 1/N under a fixed learning rate schedule (dataset scaling), and λ = s·λ_base under μP learning rate scaling η = η_base/s (model width scaling).

## Method

The paper's core insight is that AdamW's weight updates (Eq. 4) can be rewritten as an exponential moving average of recent gradient updates, where the EMA timescale τ_iter = 1/(ηλ) is the controlling hyperparameter. Theorem 1 proves that for scale-invariant networks with η-dependent initialization, the entire optimization trajectory is determined solely by τ_iter and the ratio η_0/σ (initial learning rate to initialization scale), meaning all (η, λ) pairs with the same product ηλ produce identical trajectories up to a scalar. The paper then shows empirically that the optimal τ_epoch = τ_iter/M (where M = N/B is iterations per epoch) remains approximately constant across model widths and dataset sizes, yielding the two scaling rules. This framework also explains why the original AdamW paper's γ = ηλ parameterization decouples better from η than λ does, and why Wortsman et al.'s proxy experiments showed insensitivity to η when γ was held fixed.

## Results

The paper's central quantitative finding is that the optimal EMA timescale in epochs, τ_epoch = 1/(ηλN/B), remains approximately constant across both dataset sizes (80K to 1.28M ImageNet samples; 1/4 to full OpenWebText) and model widths (s ∈ {0.5, 1, 2} for ResNet-18; widths 256/512/1024 for GPT). Concretely, as dataset size N increases, the optimal weight decay λ decreases proportionally (λ ∝ 1/N), while as model width increases by factor s under μP learning rate scaling, the optimal λ increases proportionally (λ ∝ s). On CIFAR-10 and ImageNet, standard μP scaling with fixed λ causes the optimal base learning rate to shift dramatically across model sizes, whereas scaling λ ∝ s restores a stable optimal η_base across all widths tested.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is standard μP scaling for AdamW (Eq. 13: η = η_base/s, λ = λ_base fixed), as used in the original μP paper (Yang et al., 2022) and subsequent large-scale LLM training (Lingle, 2024). The paper demonstrates that this baseline breaks down: with fixed λ, the implied EMA timescale τ_iter = 1/(ηλ) scales as s·τ_iter,base, causing the optimal base learning rate to shift substantially across model widths (Fig. 5, top rows). The proposed fix (Eq. 15: η = η_base/s, λ = s·λ_base) keeps τ_iter constant and restores stable optimal η_base across all tested widths for both ResNet-18 (CIFAR-10 and ImageNet 32×32) and ViT (ImageNet 32×32). For dataset scaling, the paper shows that fixing λ while increasing N causes the optimal learning rate to decrease (consistent with Bjorck et al., 2024), but scaling λ ∝ 1/N stabilizes the optimal learning rate across dataset sizes. The paper also notes that the relationship between s and optimal λ_base may be superlinear (λ_base ∝ s^α, α > 1), suggesting the linear scaling λ ∝ s is a lower bound, though this requires larger-scale experiments to confirm.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the μP framework (Yang et al., 2022), which established learning-rate scaling rules across model widths, to the previously unaddressed hyperparameter of AdamW's weight decay. The key conceptual move is reframing AdamW's weight updates as an exponential moving average (EMA) of recent gradient steps, identifying the EMA timescale τ_iter = 1/(ηλ) as the fundamental hyperparameter that controls the optimization trajectory. This EMA perspective also provides a theoretical explanation for empirical observations by Loshchilov & Hutter (2018) on the η-γ parameterization, Wortsman et al. (2024) on learning-rate insensitivity at fixed γ, and Lingle (2024) on μP's breakdown for AdamW in large-scale LLM training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides actionable scaling rules (λ ∝ 1/N for dataset size, λ ∝ s for model width under μP) that directly address a practical gap in large-scale training workflows where practitioners prototype on small models and transfer hyperparameters to production runs. The finding that μP's learning-rate scaling breaks down for AdamW unless weight decay is co-scaled is a significant practical correction with broad impact on anyone using AdamW in the μP paradigm. Open questions include whether the λ ∝ s relationship is truly linear or superlinear, how the rules behave in one-epoch LLM pre-training settings (where Bergsma et al., 2025a find τ_epoch is not constant), and how batch-size scaling interacts with these rules. Validation is limited to relatively small models (ResNet-18, 124M GPT) and downsampled ImageNet, leaving the largest-scale regime partially untested.
