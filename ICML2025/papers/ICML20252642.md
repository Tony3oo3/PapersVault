## Problem

While perceptual variability has been extensively documented in complex cognitive tasks, simple visual decision tasks like handwritten digit classification have largely neglected inter-individual differences. The paper asks whether images sampled along ANN perceptual boundaries can systematically evoke divergent perceptual experiences across humans, and whether these differences can be predicted and precisely manipulated using individualized computational models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252642/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of ANN classifiers (from ViT, VGG, CORNet, MLP, LRM), a target digit pair (e.g., 3 and 7), and a DiT diffusion model trained on MNIST as a natural-image prior. It produces 28×28 grayscale stimuli positioned on ANN perceptual boundaries, the variMNIST dataset (19,943 images with per-participant response probability distributions from 246 subjects), and individually fine-tuned classifier models. In the manipulation phase, two individually aligned models jointly generate ~180 controversial stimuli per participant pair, each designed to push one subject toward one target digit and the other toward a different target.

## Method

The BAM framework integrates two novel loss-guidance strategies—uncertainty guidance (maximizing cross-entropy against a uniform target distribution for a single classifier) and controversial guidance (maximizing cross-entropy against opposing target distributions for two classifiers)—into a classifier-guided diffusion model (DiT) to synthesize natural-looking images on ANN perceptual boundaries. A digit-judgment surrogate, trained on human 'is-this-a-digit' responses, is added as an auxiliary loss term to ensure generated stimuli remain recognizable. The pipeline then fine-tunes each classifier at the group level (mixed MNIST + variMNIST) and individual level (mixed individual + group + MNIST at 2:1:1 ratio), yielding IndivNet models whose entropy predictions correlate with human behavioral entropy (Spearman ρ rising from 0.08 to 0.74 for VGG). Finally, two IndivNets are used as adversarial generators: controversial guidance is applied with each model directed toward a different target, producing stimuli that elicit targeted divergent choices between specific participant pairs, validated by a second round of behavioral experiments showing ~3% higher success rate and ~12% higher targeted ratio over the variMNIST baseline.

## Results

On the variMNIST dataset (19,943 stimuli, 246 participants, 116,715 trials), group fine-tuning (GroupNet) and individual fine-tuning (IndivNet) both improve prediction accuracy by ~20% over the base model (BaseNet), while all three models maintain near-identical accuracy on standard MNIST. Individual fine-tuning provides an additional ~5% accuracy gain over group fine-tuning on individual-specific data (variMNIST-i), with 241 out of 246 participants showing improved accuracy. For VGG, the Spearman rank correlation between model-predicted entropy and human behavioral entropy increases from ρ=0.08 to ρ=0.74 after group fine-tuning. In the manipulation experiment, individually fine-tuned models improve guidance success rate by ~3% (p<0.001) and targeted ratio (directional accuracy) by ~12% (p<0.001) compared to first-round stimuli.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not perform direct numerical comparisons against prior methods (Golan et al. 2020, Veerabadran et al. 2023, Gaziv et al. 2024); instead, it positions its contributions as extending their frameworks to human perceptual variability. The primary internal comparison is the fine-tuning progression: BaseNet → GroupNet → IndivNet, where each stage improves on the last for variMNIST prediction while preserving MNIST accuracy. Among five classifier architectures (ViT, VGG, CORNet, MLP, LRM), CORNet and VGG achieve the highest controversial guidance success rates (~0.6), while LRM performs worst (~0.2); ViT and MLP show the greatest adaptability to fine-tuning. In the manipulation experiment, the key comparison is first-round stimuli (from variMNIST) versus second-round stimuli (generated by individually fine-tuned models), where the latter achieves significantly higher success rate (+3%) and directional accuracy (+12%), confirming that individual fine-tuning enables more precise perceptual manipulation. The paper also validates generalizability on ImageNet (9-class subset), where the same fine-tuning and manipulation trends hold for natural images.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the controversial stimuli paradigm from Golan et al. (2020), which pitted neural networks against each other, to the human-to-human setting, generating images that evoke divergent perceptual decisions among individual participants. It integrates the diffusion-model-as-prior approach from the adversarial/counterfactual generation literature (Jeanneret et al., 2023; Wei et al., 2024b; Chen et al., 2023) to ensure generated stimuli are natural and recognizable, addressing a key limitation of earlier methods (Golan et al., 2020; Gaziv et al., 2024) where unrecognizable images weakened human perceptual impact. The two new loss functions—uncertainty guidance and controversial guidance—adapt the cross-entropy and KL-divergence objectives from model-to-model comparison into a classifier-guided diffusion framework, while the digit judgment surrogate draws on human-preference-guided generation (Liang et al., 2024; Bansal et al., 2023).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The variMNIST dataset (19,943 stimuli, 246 participants, 116,715 trials) provides the first large-scale, systematically annotated resource for studying inter-individual perceptual variability in a controlled visual task, filling a gap where simple recognition tasks have largely ignored individual differences. The demonstration that limited individual behavioral data (~200 trials) suffices to fine-tune models capable of predicting and directionally manipulating a specific person's perceptual decisions opens a path toward personalized perception analysis and efficient AI-human alignment. Key limitations include the narrow task scope (MNIST digits), limited cultural diversity in participants, and a persistent gap between fine-tuned model accuracy on variMNIST and standard classification performance, suggesting perceptual variability remains a challenging alignment target. Future work on optimal experimental design and extension to richer visual tasks (similarity, emotion, attention) could substantially broaden the framework's impact.
