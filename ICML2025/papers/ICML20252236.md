## Problem

Distributed stochastic optimization on the Stiefel manifold lacks a retraction-free, communication-efficient algorithm. While the Landing method avoids costly retraction operations in single-node settings, extending it to distributed training raises unresolved questions: which component of the descent direction should be compressed to preserve convergence, and whether error feedback is necessary to prevent stagnation caused by the manifold's orthogonal constraint.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252236/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each of N nodes holds a local data distribution and computes stochastic gradients; the master node aggregates compressed gradient corrections (not full gradients) from all workers and broadcasts the updated model matrix X ∈ R^{n×p}. The output is a matrix whose columns are approximately orthonormal (within a safe region of radius ε around the Stiefel manifold), with convergence guarantees of O(1/K) in deterministic and O(1/√(NK)) in stochastic settings. The method also generalizes to block-wise Stiefel manifolds where only selected matrix blocks carry orthogonality constraints while remaining variables are unconstrained.

## Method

EF-Landing compresses the Euclidean gradient ∇f(X) rather than the Riemannian gradient or the combined descent direction, a choice that provably preserves the critical orthogonality between the Riemannian gradient and the penalty gradient term (Proposition 4.1). Vanilla compression of the Euclidean gradient can cause non-convergence because the compressed gradient may become orthogonal to the tangent space of the Stiefel manifold, stalling the iteration; the paper proves this failure mode (Proposition 4.2) and shows that momentum-based error feedback corrects it by accumulating discarded gradient components across iterations. The master node clips the aggregated compressed gradient to a bounded norm to keep iterates within the safe region, then computes the Landing descent direction and updates the model. The analysis uses a novel merit function bound that isolates the compression error term, yielding sharp convergence rates that recover both the vanilla Landing rate and standard distributed compression results as special cases.

## Results

The paper's primary contribution is theoretical: EF-Landing achieves O(1/K) convergence in deterministic settings and O(1/√(NK)) (linear speedup) in stochastic settings, matching the rates of vanilla Landing without compression. In the distributed online PCA experiment (n=5000, p=1000, N=4 nodes), EF-Landing with Top-K, Rand-K (retention 0.1), and QSGD (s=16) compressors reaches the same training loss as vanilla Landing while significantly reducing communication overhead. On ResNet-18 with orthogonal convolutional layers trained on CIFAR-10 (150 epochs), EF-Landing with Top-K and Random-K (retention 0.2) achieves accuracy comparable to vanilla Landing and QR retraction per iteration, again with substantially lower communication cost.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EF-Landing matches vanilla Landing (Ablin et al., 2024) in both convergence rate and empirical training loss on distributed PCA and CIFAR-10, while reducing communication volume by the compression ratio (e.g., 10× with retention 0.1). Against QR retraction (Absil et al., 2012), EF-Landing achieves comparable accuracy on ResNet-18/CIFAR-10 per iteration but avoids the computationally expensive QR factorization at each step. The Euclidean gradient descent with ℓ₂ penalty norm performs poorly on the PCA problem, failing to enforce the orthogonal constraint (its constraint violation remains high), whereas EF-Landing drives the penalty term N(X) → 0. In the decentralized comparison (Chen et al., 2021, Appendix D.4) and the centralized multi-local-update comparison (Zhang et al., 2024, Appendix D.5), EF-Landing is competitive, though the paper does not report large margins. Overall, EF-Landing ties or wins on communication efficiency across all baselines while maintaining equivalent optimality and feasibility guarantees.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the retraction-free Landing algorithm (Ablin & Peyré, 2022; Ablin et al., 2024) to distributed settings by incorporating communication compression and error feedback, drawing on the EF21 framework (Richtarik et al., 2021) and momentum-enhanced error feedback (Fatkhullin et al., 2023). A key departure from prior distributed manifold optimization work (Chen et al., 2021; Sun et al., 2024; Hu & Deng, 2024) is the identification that one must compress the Euclidean gradient rather than the Riemannian gradient or the combined descent direction, and that error feedback is provably necessary to avoid stagnation caused by the orthogonality structure of the Stiefel manifold. The paper also generalizes the framework to block-wise Stiefel manifolds, accommodating mixed constrained/unconstrained variables as in orthogonal CNNs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

EF-Landing is the first algorithm to simultaneously achieve retraction-free computation and communication efficiency for distributed Stiefel manifold optimization, with convergence rates matching uncompressed baselines (O(1/K) deterministic, O(1/√(NK)) stochastic with linear speedup). The finding that vanilla gradient compression provably fails on the Stiefel manifold—even in single-node deterministic settings—highlights a fundamental interaction between manifold geometry and compression that was previously unappreciated. Open questions include whether the safe-region restriction (ε < 3/4) can be relaxed, whether the results extend to non-canonical metrics or other matrix manifolds, and whether the block-wise generalization handles heterogeneous block sizes and constraint structures more flexibly.
