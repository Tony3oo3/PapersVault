## Problem

Diffusion models require large datasets to train a score-based model via denoising score matching, but collecting and cleaning such data is expensive and sometimes infeasible. The paper identifies a theoretical gap: the total denoising score matching error is jointly determined by the denoiser function hypothesis space complexity and the number of training samples, and existing diffusion methods fail to minimize this error when only hundreds of samples are available.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20253007/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes random Gaussian noise in a compressed latent space (8× downsampled via a pre-trained VAE encoder from SD-MSE) and produces 256×256 RGB images decoded by a pre-trained VAE decoder from SD-EMA. Training operates on as few as 100 images, with the latent-space dimensionality reduced by the 8× downsampling factor. Sampling uses 79 NFE for FFHQ and 511 NFE for low-shot datasets via the EDM ODE solver.

## Method

LD-Diffusion addresses the limited-data challenge through two core components built on EDM. First, a compressing model pairs an untrained encoder from SD-MSE with a decoder from SD-EMA (different VAEs sharing the same latent space) to constrain the denoiser hypothesis space to a lower-dimensional manifold, making the total denoising score matching error easier to minimize with few samples. Second, a novel Mixed Augmentation with Fixed Probability (MAFP) strategy applies Diff-Augment (Translation) with probability p₁=0.1 and ADA with probability p₂=0.1, providing more informative augmentation in the compressed space while avoiding the leaking issues that arise from directly combining these methods. For the two limited-data scenarios, patch training is applied when a subset of a large dataset is used (Case 1), and a novel OOD regularization term—penalizing gradients when generated samples approach NDA-generated out-of-distribution samples—is added to the denoising loss when the small dataset serves as both training and reference (Case 2).

## Results

On the 256×256 FFHQ dataset, LD-Diffusion achieves FID scores of 28.51 (FFHQ-100), 17.76 (FFHQ-1K), and 14.36 (FFHQ-2K), outperforming all baselines. On 100-shot low-shot datasets, LD-Diffusion reaches FID of 13.00 (Obama), 13.31 (Grumpy Cat), 4.70 (Panda), 12.77 (Animal-Face Cat), and 12.48 (Animal-Face Dog), setting new state-of-the-art. Notably, LD-Diffusion's FID on FFHQ-100 (28.51) is lower than EDM+DA's FID on FFHQ-1K (30.75), demonstrating substantial data efficiency. LD-Diffusion also reduces training time from 40 min to 7 min and inference time from 165 min to 28 min compared to EDM+DA on 100-shot Obama.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LD-Diffusion decisively outperforms EDM+DA (Karras et al., 2022) across all settings: on FFHQ-100 it reduces FID from 50.73 to 28.51 (a 44% improvement), and on 100-shot Obama from 37.10 to 13.00. Against Patch Diffusion (Wang et al., 2023c), LD-Diffusion wins on every dataset, e.g., 28.51 vs. 44.45 on FFHQ-100 and 13.00 vs. 41.47 on 100-shot Obama, with the gap widening as data becomes scarcer. Compared to LPDM-8 (also from Wang et al., 2023c), LD-Diffusion achieves consistent but smaller margins (e.g., 28.51 vs. 32.78 on FFHQ-100; 12.48 vs. 15.95 on Animal-Face Dog), confirming the added value of MAFP and OOD regularization over latent-space compression alone. EDM2 (Karras et al., 2024) performs worse than even vanilla EDM under limited data (112.26 vs. 51.30 on 100-shot Obama), validating the choice of EDM as the baseline architecture. Finally, the MAFP strategy generalizes to GANs: StyleGAN2+MAFP(Adaptive) beats both StyleGAN2+Diff-Augment (Zhao et al., 2020) and StyleGAN2+ADA (Karras et al., 2020) on all five low-shot datasets, e.g., 41.13 vs. 46.87 and 45.69 on 100-shot Obama.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the EDM framework (Karras et al., 2022) by repurposing VAE-based latent compression—previously used in LDM (Rombach et al., 2022a) solely for computational efficiency—as a mechanism to constrain the denoiser hypothesis space complexity under limited data. It draws on Patch Diffusion (Wang et al., 2023c) for patch-wise training and on GAN limited-data augmentation literature (ADA, Diff-Augment) to design MAFP, a fixed-probability mixed augmentation tailored to the compressed space. The key conceptual departure is the theoretical decomposition of total denoising score matching error into approximation error (governed by hypothesis space) and estimation error (governed by sample count), which provides a principled justification for why existing diffusion architectures struggle with hundreds of samples.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first theoretical grounding for why diffusion models fail under limited data and a practical recipe (compressing model + MAFP + case-specific regularization) that nearly halves FID on FFHQ-100 compared to EDM+DA, with the striking result that 100 training images outperform 1,000 under the baseline. The OOD regularization is the first regularization method designed specifically for diffusion models, and MAFP's generalization to GANs suggests broader applicability. Open questions include whether the approach extends to conditional or text-to-image generation, whether the reliance on pre-trained VAEs (discussed in §C.1) limits true 'from scratch' claims, and whether the theoretical insight generalizes to other score-matching formulations beyond EDM.
