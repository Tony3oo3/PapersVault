## Problem

Existing camera-controllable video diffusion models produce monocular videos with limited 3D consistency and cannot generate multiple spatiotemporally consistent video sequences of the same scene from distinct camera trajectories. Multi-view video generation is further hampered by the scarcity of multi-view dynamic video data, causing prior methods to produce inconsistent results when object motion is present or to restrict outputs to near-static synthetic objects.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252769/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single 256×256 RGB image and a sequence of camera extrinsic/intrinsic matrices (defining the desired viewpoint trajectory) as input. It outputs V spatiotemporally consistent video sequences of 14 frames each at 256×256 resolution, with the first frame shared across all views. Camera control is injected via Plücker coordinates computed from the pose sequence, and the architecture supports extrapolation to 4 views at inference despite being trained with 2 views.

## Method

Cavia extends Stable Video Diffusion with two view-integrated attention modules: cross-frame attention inflates 1D temporal attention into 3D by rearranging latent features from (B·V·H·W, F, C) to (B·V, F·H·W, C), enabling spatial-temporal feature interaction to handle large pixel displacements from viewpoint changes; cross-view attention inflates spatial self-attention by rearranging from (B·V·F, H·W, C) to (B·F, V·H·W, C), allowing inter-view information exchange at each timestep. Because both rearrangements only extend the token-length dimension without changing feature dimensions, pre-trained SVD weights are directly inherited, and the view dimension is absorbed into the batch dimension to support arbitrary V. A joint training strategy combines static multi-view scene videos (geometric consistency), rendered multi-view dynamic object videos from Objaverse (object motion), and pose-annotated monocular in-the-wild videos (complex backgrounds), with monocular clips treated as V=1 samples so all data flows through the same architecture.

## Results

On the RealEstate10K monocular evaluation, Cavia achieves the best FID (11.43) and FVD (55.10), substantially outperforming SVD (16.89/139.64), MotionCtrl (21.09/119.06), and CameraCtrl (14.69/105.41). For geometric consistency, Cavia reduces the COLMAP error to 14.4% (vs. 30.3% for SVD and 55.0% for MotionCtrl) and achieves the highest translation AUC at 24.6% @20°, nearly 6× better than CameraCtrl (4.4%). In the 2-view multi-view setting, Cavia further improves cross-view consistency, reaching a SuperGlue precision of 29.39 and matching score of 15.22 on Real10K scenes, and 33.10/19.96 on general scenes, surpassing all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Cavia outperforms all three quantitative baselines (SVD, MotionCtrl, CameraCtrl) on every reported metric in both monocular and multi-view settings. Against SVD (the base model without camera control), Cavia nearly halves the FVD (55.10 vs. 139.64) and reduces COLMAP error from 30.3% to 14.4%, while improving translation AUC by over 7×. Against MotionCtrl, which uses camera matrices as conditioning, Cavia achieves a 46% lower FID and a 38% lower FVD, with dramatically better geometric accuracy (COLMAP 14.4% vs. 55.0%). Against CameraCtrl, which uses Plücker coordinates via ControlNet, Cavia matches or slightly exceeds rotation AUC but achieves a 5.6× improvement in translation AUC at 20° (24.6% vs. 4.4%), indicating far more accurate camera trajectory adherence. Against the concurrent multi-view method CVD, the comparison is qualitative (Figs. 9–10): Cavia produces fewer border artifacts in 4-view generation and yields sharper 3D Gaussian reconstructions with fewer floaters, attributed to its cross-view attention design versus CVD's homography-warping augmentation.

## Relation to Prior Work

Cavia extends Stable Video Diffusion (SVD) by introducing view-integrated attention modules that inflate 1D temporal attention into 3D cross-frame attention and spatial self-attention into 3D cross-view attention, drawing conceptually from MVDream's multi-view self-attention for image generation. It departs from prior camera-controllable video methods (MotionCtrl, CameraCtrl, CamCo) that operate on single monocular sequences, and from concurrent multi-view video work (CVD, Vivid-ZOO) that either relies on homography warping or is restricted to object-centric fixed viewpoints. The joint training strategy combining static scene videos, rendered dynamic 3D objects, and pose-annotated monocular videos is a key departure, enabling the model to learn geometric consistency, object motion, and complex backgrounds from heterogeneous sources within a single architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Cavia is positioned as the first framework to simultaneously generate multiple temporally and geometrically consistent video sequences of the same scene from distinct camera trajectories while preserving object motion, addressing a gap that prior methods could not fill. The view-integrated attention design's ability to extrapolate to arbitrary view counts at inference (e.g., 4 views from 2-view training) and support downstream 3D reconstruction via Gaussian Splatting broadens its practical utility. Key limitations include reliance on SVD's limited capacity for large object motion, an assumption of shared camera intrinsics across frames, and normalized scene scales that preclude metric-scale generalization—open questions that the authors flag for future work.
