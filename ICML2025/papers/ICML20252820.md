## Problem

Despite evidence that LLMs perform abstract reasoning, it remains unclear what internal mechanisms support this capability and whether it reflects genuine symbolic processing or mere statistical approximation. Prior work has identified individual primitives (induction heads, function vectors) but has not identified an integrated architecture that explains how symbol processing emerges in transformer LLMs to support relational abstraction.

## Input / Output

The method takes pretrained LLM weights (13 models across GPT-2, Gemma-2, Qwen2.5, and Llama-3.1 families) and evaluates them on three abstract reasoning tasks constructed with arbitrary vocabulary tokens to preclude statistical shortcuts. It produces a three-stage hierarchy of causally identified attention heads (symbol abstraction, symbolic induction, retrieval) characterized by causal mediation scores, attention pattern maps, and representational similarity matrices, validated through activation patching, ablation, and linear decoding analyses.

## Method

The paper identifies an emergent three-stage symbolic architecture: (1) symbol abstraction heads in early layers implement an emergent form of relational cross-attention where value embeddings encode relative position rather than token identity, converting input tokens to abstract variables invariant to their surface form; (2) symbolic induction heads in intermediate layers perform sequence induction over these variables, predicting the next abstract variable from in-context transition patterns; (3) retrieval heads in later layers perform indirection by matching the predicted variable against prior instances to retrieve the associated token. The architecture is validated via a double dissociation design in causal mediation analysis (abstract vs. token patching conditions), attention pattern analysis confirming predicted query-key-value routing, representational similarity analysis showing outputs match abstract variable rather than token similarity structures, and ablation studies demonstrating necessity. A key finding is that symbolic induction heads are essentially function vectors (r=0.86 correlation) but are distinct from standard induction heads (r=0.11), and that symbol abstraction heads compute function vectors at the level of individual in-context examples while symbolic induction heads aggregate them across examples.

## Results

Llama-3.1 70B achieves 95% 2-shot accuracy on the algebraic rule induction task, 99.2%/82.0% on letter string analogies (successor/predecessor), and 77.0%/88.4% on verbal analogies (synonym/antonym). Causal mediation analyses identify a sparse, three-stage hierarchy of attention heads (symbol abstraction → symbolic induction → retrieval) across 13 models from four families, with all models except GPT-2 showing statistically significant heads for all three stages. RSA confirms that symbol abstraction head values correlate 0.67 with abstract variable structure (vs. 0.17 for token identity), and linear decoding of abstract variables from head outputs achieves >98% generalization accuracy on completely disjoint token sets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares the identified symbolic induction heads against two previously characterized mechanisms. Against standard induction heads (Olsson et al., 2022), the prefix matching scores are only weakly correlated with symbolic induction head causal mediation scores (r=0.11), demonstrating that these are disjoint sets of attention heads despite their conceptual similarity. Against function vectors (Todd et al., 2024), the average indirect effect scores are highly correlated with symbolic induction head scores (r=0.86), indicating they are essentially the same set of attention heads — the paper thus provides a novel mechanistic interpretation of function vectors as sequence induction over abstract variables. Additionally, symbol abstraction heads show moderate correlation with function vector scores computed at the third-item position (r=0.47) but not at the final position (r=0.06), suggesting function vectors are first computed at the level of individual in-context examples by abstraction heads and then aggregated across examples by induction heads.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper integrates three previously identified mechanistic primitives—induction heads (Olsson et al., 2022), function vectors (Todd et al., 2024), and the relational cross-attention architecture of the abstractor (Altabaa et al., 2024)—into a unified three-stage symbolic architecture (symbol abstraction → symbolic induction → retrieval) that explains abstract reasoning in LLMs. It departs from prior work by identifying a genuinely new mechanism (symbol abstraction heads) that converts tokens to content-invariant variables, and by showing that symbolic induction heads are essentially function vectors (r=0.86) yet are disjoint from standard induction heads (r=0.11). The analytical methodology builds directly on the causal mediation framework of Wang et al. (2023), extended here with a double-dissociation design that separates abstract variable representations from literal token representations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a concrete mechanistic resolution to the symbolic-vs-neural debate: LLMs perform abstract reasoning not by statistical approximation but by developing emergent symbol-processing circuits with indirection and content invariance. The finding that GPT-2 lacks symbol abstraction heads while Gemma-2, Qwen2.5, and Llama-3.1 possess them suggests these mechanisms emerge only at certain scales, raising open questions about the inductive biases (e.g., inner-product similarity, key-value separation) that trigger their development. Key limitations include the relatively simple tasks tested (ABA/ABB rules, basic analogies), the approximate rather than purely abstract nature of the identified representations, and the unresolved question of whether this architecture generalizes to the more complex compositional and mathematical reasoning where LLMs are known to struggle.
