## Problem

Autoregressive decoding in LLMs sequentially accumulates per-token optimal decisions without any built-in mechanism to refine or correct previously generated content, making it inherently suboptimal relative to the globally optimal response of the same length. This paper theoretically characterizes the deviation between stepwise-optimal and globally-optimal sequences and proposes an empirical decoding framework that interleaves refinement with generation to close this gap.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252330/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard text prompt and produces a generated text response, but unlike standard autoregressive decoding it maintains a sliding reflection window of size d (default 4 tokens) over the generation history. When the conditional entropy of the next-token distribution exceeds a threshold σ (default 0.5) for all tokens in the window, generation pauses and the window's content is regenerated via beam search (width 4) before resuming. The regeneration ratio is typically only 3.5–5.5% of total tokens, keeping computational overhead close to that of plain greedy decoding.

## Method

The core mechanism uses fast and slow pointers to define a sliding reflection window over the generated text. A pausing criterion monitors the conditional entropy of next-token logits across the window; when entropy exceeds threshold σ for all d tokens, the fast pointer pauses and a fixed-length beam search regenerates the window's content, after which the slow pointer catches up and generation resumes. The theoretical foundation (Theorem 3.6) shows that when the stepwise-optimal response deviates from the globally-optimal one, the conditional probability of the next token is strictly bounded away from 1, motivating the entropy-based trigger. The framework is modular: both the pausing criterion (IfPause) and the refinement method (ReGenerate) are pluggable components, allowing practitioners to substitute different uncertainty signals or correction strategies while preserving the sliding-window structure.

## Results

On MMLU with Phi3-Medium, Reflection-Window decoding achieves a 78.15% macro-averaged accuracy, marginally surpassing greedy decoding (78.14%) and clearly outperforming beam search (76.44%). On MT-Bench with Llama3.1-8B, Reflection-Window prevails in 66.67% of head-to-head comparisons against greedy decoding (GPT-4o judge), compared to only 46.3% for beam search. Across four models (Llama3.1-8B, Phi3-Medium, Qwen2.5-14B, Mistral-Nemo), Reflection-Window consistently outperforms Top-k/Top-p sampling on MMLU, with average accuracy gains ranging from 0.88 pp (Mistral-Nemo) to 2.76 pp (Phi3-Medium).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against greedy decoding, Reflection-Window achieves a marginal MMLU improvement (78.15% vs. 78.14% macro average on Phi3-Medium) but a substantial MT-Bench advantage, prevailing in 66.67% of GPT-4o-judged comparisons versus greedy. Against beam search (beam width 4), Reflection-Window clearly wins on MMLU (78.15% vs. 76.44%) and on MT-Bench (66.67% win rate vs. 46.3% for beam search), while being more efficient since beam search is only invoked selectively within the reflection window rather than maintained throughout generation. Against Top-k/Top-p sampling (k=10, p=0.9, temperature 1.0), Reflection-Window consistently outperforms across all four evaluated models, with the largest gains in STEM and humanities categories (e.g., +5.55 pp on humanities for Phi3-Medium), suggesting particular benefit for reasoning-heavy tasks. The regeneration ratio remains low (3.5%–5.5% of tokens refined), confirming that the method adds minimal computational overhead relative to pure greedy decoding.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the standard autoregressive decoding paradigm (greedy, beam search, top-k/top-p sampling) by introducing a decoding-level refinement mechanism, and explicitly distinguishes itself from both speculative decoding (which targets inference speed while preserving the autoregressive structure) and high-level self-correction/self-refinement approaches (which operate at sentence or paragraph granularity across multiple iterations). The theoretical framing draws on the well-known gap between coordinate-wise and global optimization, but applies it to the specific setting of LLM text generation with a formal theorem (Theorem 3.6) that even an oracle LLM with perfect autoregressive probability decomposition can deviate from the same-length globally optimal response. The sliding reflection window with an entropy-based pausing criterion is a novel empirical instantiation that selectively invokes beam search only when uncertainty signals potential sub-optimality, rather than maintaining a full beam frontier throughout generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean theoretical justification for why purely autoregressive decoding is fundamentally suboptimal and offers a practical, low-overhead mechanism (3.5–5.5% regeneration ratio) to partially address this at the decoding level. However, the theoretical result relies on strong assumptions (oracle LLM, strict preference among same-length sequences, irreversible advantage once manifested) that are difficult to verify in practice, and the empirical gains over strong baselines are modest (0.88–2.76 percentage points on MMLU, though the MT-Bench win-rate gap over beam search is more pronounced at 66.67% vs. 46.3%). Open questions include whether the entropy-based pausing criterion is the optimal signal for detecting sub-optimality, how the approach behaves on very long-form generation where errors compound, and whether the selective beam-search refinement can be replaced by cheaper local re-scoring without sacrificing quality.
