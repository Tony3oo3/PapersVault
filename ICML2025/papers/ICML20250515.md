## Problem

Existing multivariate causal discovery methods require restrictive functional assumptions (e.g., additive noise models) to guarantee identifiability, but these assumptions frequently fail on real data, leading to misspecification and poor performance. While Bayesian model selection was shown to relax these restrictions in the bivariate case, it remained unclear whether the approach extends to multiple variables, and the super-exponential growth of DAGs made exhaustive model comparison intractable.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a standardised observational dataset X ∈ R^{N×D} (N samples, D variables) with no interventional data and no hidden confounders assumed. It outputs a causal DAG represented as a D×D adjacency matrix, where entries are derived from GP kernel hyperparameters θ_ij that encode the strength of dependence of X_i on X_j. The final graph is the MAP estimate selected by maximising the posterior, with edges thresholded to zero when the corresponding hyperparameter falls below a correlation-based cutoff.

## Method

The CGP-CDE model places a Gaussian Process prior on each conditional distribution C_{i|PA(i)}, using a sum of linear, squared-exponential, Matérn, and rational-quadratic kernels whose per-dimension precision hyperparameters θ_ij continuously parameterise the adjacency matrix (θ_ij ≈ 0 implies no edge X_j→X_i). A Gamma prior on θ favours sparse graphs, and the spectral acyclicity constraint h(A) = |λ_d(A)| enforces the DAG structure via a penalty method with linearly increasing weight. The marginal likelihood is approximated by a variational ELBO using uncollapsed inducing points (M < N) and a neural encoder for the latent noise variables, reducing per-epoch cost to O(DM³). Training proceeds in three phases—warm-up, acyclic-constraint, and cool-down—with random restarts to mitigate local optima, and the final graph is selected by highest ELBO across restarts.

## Results

The paper evaluates CGP-CDE on three settings: a 3-variable synthetic dataset (where discrete enumeration DGP-CDE is feasible), a 50-variable Erdős-Rényi (ER4) dataset, and the Syntren gene-regulatory benchmark (20 nodes, 500 samples). On the 50-variable ER4 dataset, CGP-CDE outperforms all baselines, with the largest gains in SHD and F1 score. On Syntren, CGP-CDE again leads, particularly in SID and F1. The 3-variable experiment confirms that the probability of error for the GP-CDE model class is low in the multivariate setting, with the continuous relaxation (CGP-CDE) introducing only a small additional error over the discrete enumeration (DGP-CDE).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CGP-CDE outperforms all six baselines across all three experimental settings. The most direct comparison is with SDCD (Nazaret et al., 2024), which uses the same spectral acyclicity regulariser and a flexible neural-network function approximator but a likelihood score; CGP-CDE's superior performance provides direct evidence that the Bayesian marginal-likelihood score is more effective at distinguishing causal structures. Against the ANM-based methods (SCORE, NOGAM, CAM), CGP-CDE wins by avoiding the restrictive additive-noise assumption, which the paper shows leads to misspecification when data is generated from neural networks. NOTEARS (linear model) is also outperformed, consistent with the paper's argument that restricted model classes lose guarantees when assumptions are violated. DiBS, the only other Bayesian baseline, performs poorly, which the authors attribute to poor inference in Bayesian neural networks rather than a fundamental limitation of the Bayesian approach. The gap between CGP-CDE and the baselines widens with the number of variables, suggesting the Bayesian model selection advantage becomes more pronounced at scale.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends Dhir et al. (2024), which demonstrated that Bayesian model selection can identify causal direction in the bivariate case without functional restrictions, to the multivariate setting where the number of candidate DAGs grows super-exponentially. To achieve scalability, it borrows the continuous-optimisation framework of NOTEARS (Zheng et al., 2018) and the spectral acyclicity regulariser of SDCD (Nazaret et al., 2024), but replaces their likelihood-based scoring with a marginal-likelihood (Bayesian model selection) objective. The flexible nonparametric model is the Gaussian Process Conditional Density Estimator (Dutordoir et al., 2018), whose kernel hyperparameters are reinterpreted as a continuous adjacency matrix, and the ICM assumption is encoded through factorised priors over the conditionals. This positions the work at the intersection of Bayesian structure learning (DiBS, Lorch et al. 2021) and differentiable DAG learning, but departs from both by using marginal likelihood rather than likelihood or posterior sampling as the optimisation target.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a core practical limitation of causal discovery: restricted model classes (ANM, linear Gaussian) that guarantee identifiability often fail on real data, while unrestricted models lose the ability to distinguish causal directions. By showing that Bayesian model selection with a flexible GP model can recover the correct DAG in multivariate settings with only a small, empirically estimable error probability, the work opens a path toward more realistic causal inference. Key limitations include the no-hidden-confounders assumption, the non-zero (though small) probability of error, and the computational cost of GP variational inference (O(DM³)), which may limit applicability to very large systems. Open questions include how the method behaves under genuine model misspecification, whether more informative graph priors (beyond the symmetric Gamma prior) can further reduce error, and whether the approach extends to settings with latent confounders or interventional data.
