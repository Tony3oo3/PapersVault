## Problem

Existing sEMG-based gesture recognition models treat signals as generic time series and focus on long-term dependencies, overlooking short-term local features that are critical for distinguishing subtle gestures (e.g., Index Finger Extension vs. Middle Extension) and for confining the impact of noise. This oversight limits robustness in real-world noisy environments where skin condition changes, electrode issues, and electromagnetic interference degrade signal quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250814/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 200 ms windows (10 ms overlap) of multi-channel wrist sEMG sampled at 2048 Hz from 12 electrodes arranged in two rings, bandpass-filtered to 10–500 Hz and μ-law normalized, yielding a sequence of t timesteps each with c sensor values. For classification it outputs a 17-way gesture label (single-finger, multi-finger, wrist, and rest categories); for regression it outputs continuous predictions of 10 finger joint angles, with the loss switched from Asymmetric Loss to MSE.

## Method

STET introduces a Short-Term Enhancement Module (STEM) that applies sliding-window self-attention with weight sharing over a local window of w timesteps, capturing fine-grained local signal changes in parallel with a full self-attention long-term decoder; the two representations are concatenated and pooled via a 1-D convolution before classification. A self-supervised pre-training stage (sEMG Signal Masking) applies sensor-wise contiguous-segment masks whose lengths follow a geometric distribution, trained with MSE reconstruction to encode intrinsic sEMG variability without external data. An Asymmetric Loss with focusing and probability-shifting parameters addresses the class imbalance inherent in decomposing 17-class recognition into binary sub-tasks. The design is plug-and-play: STEM can be grafted onto other backbones (e.g., Informer, TEMGNet) to improve both accuracy and noise robustness without manual data augmentation.

## Results

On the GRABMyo dataset, STET achieves 90.76% overall gesture classification accuracy, surpassing the strongest baseline Informer (87.71%) by 3.05% and the Transformer-based Zerveas et al. (80.43%) by 10.33%. On the Ninapro DB2 joint-angle regression task, STET attains a Pearson Correlation Coefficient of 0.877 and NRMSE of 0.073, outperforming the best prior method sBERT-OHME (PCC 0.869, NRMSE 0.076). Under additive Gaussian noise, STET reduces the accuracy drop from 25% (standard Transformer) to 10%, and under signal loss from 26% (Informer) to 17%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

STET outperforms all ten baselines on both classification and regression tasks. In classification, the largest gains are over TEMGNet (+12.74% overall) and the vanilla Transformer of Zerveas et al. (+10.33%), while the margin over the strongest competitor Informer is +3.05% overall and +11.77% on the critical Rest gesture (95.33% vs. 83.56%). LST-EMG-Net, which also targets long-short-term fusion but via pre-segmentation, trails STET by 5.45% overall. In regression on Ninapro DB2, STET improves PCC over sBERT-OHME from 0.869 to 0.877 and reduces NRMSE from 0.076 to 0.073, while also achieving the lowest curvature (κ = 0.522 vs. 0.532), indicating smoother predicted joint-angle curves. The plug-and-play nature of STEM is confirmed by Informer+STEM (89.14%) and TEMGNet+STEM (84.07%), showing consistent gains when the module is attached to other backbones.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Transformer-based sEMG gesture recognition (TEMGNet, Zerveas et al., Informer) by introducing a parallel short-term/long-term decoding architecture, departing from LST-EMG-Net's pre-segmentation approach and Focal Transformer's sequential processing. The STEM module's sliding-window attention with weight sharing draws on Long-Short Transformer (Zhu et al., 2021) but applies it in a decoupled, plug-in fashion rather than a unified key/value scheme. The self-supervised pretraining adapts BERT-style masking (Zerveas et al., 2021) to a sensor-wise, geometrically-distributed segment strategy tailored to sEMG's multi-electrode structure and frequency characteristics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The >20% reduction in noise-induced accuracy degradation and the plug-in applicability of STEM to arbitrary backbones (demonstrated on both Transformer and Informer) make this practically relevant for deploying sEMG-based HCI in noisy real-world settings (VR/AR, prosthetics). However, the evaluation remains user-specific and limited to two datasets, leaving cross-subject generalization and long-term stability under continuous wear as open questions. The small-scale real-world deployment (8 participants, 10-minute sessions) and the lack of biological grounding for window-size selection suggest the method's limits are not yet fully characterized.
