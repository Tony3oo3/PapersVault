## Problem

Existing conditional time series generation methods rely on structured conditions (class labels, metadata, preset constraints) that are inherently limited in expressiveness, require costly expert annotation, and fail to capture sample-specific details like local shapelets or sequential event patterns. This paper introduces the novel task of generating time series from unstructured textual descriptions, which naturally encode richer, more fine-grained semantic information without requiring predefined condition schemas.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250793/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

During training, the model takes a noisy time series x_t ∈ R^{K×L} (K variables, L timesteps) and a text description c ∈ N^M (M tokens, ranging from ~30 to ~250 tokens across datasets) and predicts the added noise. At inference, only a text prompt and random noise are needed to produce a time series of the target shape. The text is encoded via a frozen Long-CLIP encoder into token embeddings, while the time series is patched into R multi-resolution segments. The framework supports both univariate (K=1) and multivariate (K up to 72) generation.

## Method

VERBALTS is a conditional diffusion model with two core components: a multi-view noise estimator and a multi-focal text processor. The noise estimator models the time series simultaneously along three views—temporal (R resolutions of patches with masked intra-resolution self-attention), spatial (K variables via cross-variable self-attention), and diffusion (S stages where early stages capture global structure and later stages refine local details). The text processor uses semantic reprogramming (RPG) with learnable anchor vectors to decompose the text embedding into S diffusion-level, R temporal-resolution, and K spatial-variable semantic representations, which are combined into a compound tensor Z̄ ∈ R^{S×K×R×D}. A multi-modality alignment adapter, inspired by adaptive layer normalization, computes per-dimension gate (α), shift (β), and scale (γ) parameters via an MLP from Z̄, modulating the time series embeddings at each diffusion step, resolution, and variable to achieve fine-grained cross-modal control.

## Results

VERBALTS achieves the best FID, JFTSD, and CTTP scores across all six datasets (two synthetic, four real-world) in both multivariate and univariate settings. Headline improvements include FID of 6.13 on Weather (vs. 14.86 for the best baseline TEdit, a ~59% reduction), FID of 29.05 on Synth-M (vs. 41.94 for TEdit), and CTTP of 64.13 on Synth-M (vs. 52.55 for TEdit). On the real-world Weather dataset, VERBALTS reduces JFTSD from 18.33 (TEdit) to 8.56, more than halving the distributional discrepancy. The paper reports over 20% average improvements on Synth-M/U and Weather compared to the strongest structured-condition baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VERBALTS outperforms all four baselines on every metric across all six datasets. The strongest structured-condition baselines are TEdit and TimeWeaver (both attribute-based); VERBALTS beats TEdit by 31% in FID on Synth-M (29.05 vs. 41.94), by 59% on Weather (6.13 vs. 14.86), and by 14% on ETTm1 (24.19 vs. 33.04). On semantic alignment (CTTP), VERBALTS leads TEdit by 11.6 points on Synth-M (64.13 vs. 52.55) and by 3.1 points on Weather (30.62 vs. 27.56), confirming that unstructured text conditions carry richer generative information. DiffTime (constraint-based) performs poorly on most datasets, with FID exceeding 100 on Traffic and 59 on ETTm1, indicating that rigid constraint formulations generalize poorly. TimeVQVAE (class-conditioned) is competitive on BlindWays FID (29.48 vs. VERBALTS 27.63) but lags significantly on CTTP (8.63 vs. 9.18) and on all other datasets, reflecting the limited expressiveness of discrete class labels.

## Relation to Prior Work

VERBALTS builds on the conditional diffusion model framework (Saharia et al., 2022; Ho et al., 2020) and repurposes the semantic reprogramming operation (Chen, 2024) from cross-domain transfer learning into a multi-focal text processor that decomposes textual semantics across temporal, spatial, and diffusion-stage dimensions. It departs from prior time series generation work (TimeWeaver, TEdit, DiffTime, TimeVQVAE) that relies on structured conditions—class labels, metadata, or preset constraints—by treating unstructured text as the control signal, and from text-to-image diffusion (Rombach et al., 2022) that applies a single unified text embedding throughout generation. The multi-view noise estimator draws on multi-resolution patching ideas from forecasting (TimeMixer) and multi-granularity diffusion (MG-TSD), but uniquely couples them with cross-modal semantic alignment rather than intra-modal decomposition.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This paper establishes text-to-time-series generation as a formal task with a six-dataset benchmark, filling a gap where prior work either used rigid structured conditions or was confined to narrow domains like ECG-from-clinical-reports. The multi-focal alignment mechanism—routing different textual semantics to different resolutions, variables, and diffusion stages—offers a reusable design pattern for any multi-modal generation task where conditions carry hierarchical, multi-granular information. Key limitations include the inherent slowness of iterative diffusion sampling and the reliance on paired text-time-series data (partially mitigated by synthetic and augmented datasets), leaving open questions about efficiency (e.g., distillation or few-step samplers), scaling to very long or high-dimensional series, and whether the approach generalizes to modalities beyond text (e.g., audio or sketch-based conditions).
