## Problem

Orthogonal convolutions are essential for certifiable adversarial robustness, normalizing flows, GANs, and Lipschitz-constrained models, but existing construction methods lack support for critical modern CNN features such as native strides, transposed convolutions, grouped convolutions, and dilation. This gap prevents orthogonal layers from being deployed in standard architectures like U-Nets, VAEs, ResNeXt, and EfficientNet, and forces reliance on costly emulation strategies (e.g., channel reshaping) that scale poorly with image resolution and batch size.

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10, 224×224 for ImageNet-1K) and produces class labels alongside a provable L2 robustness certificate — a lower bound on the perturbation radius within which the prediction is guaranteed invariant. Unlike prior orthogonal convolution methods that require fixed input dimensions or emulate strides via reshaping (increasing parameter count by s²), AOC accepts arbitrary kernel sizes, strides, dilation rates, and group counts natively, with computational cost independent of input spatial dimensions.

## Method

AOC fuses two existing kernel-construction methods — BCOP (which guarantees orthogonality for arbitrary kernel size at stride 1) and RKO (which guarantees orthogonality when kernel size equals stride) — via the Block-convolution operator, yielding a single orthogonal kernel with arbitrary kernel size k and stride s. The key theoretical results are: (i) RKO produces an orthogonal convolution when k=s (Proposition 2.4), and (ii) composing K_RKO with K_BCOP at an internal channel size c = max(c_i, ⌊c_o/s²⌋) preserves orthogonality (Proposition 2.5). The implementation achieves scalability through three optimizations: replacing nested-loop Block-convolution with a single batched 2D convolution, parallelizing BCOP's sequential kernel compositions via associative scan in O(log(k-s)) steps, and a decision tree that collapses to simpler parametrizations (pure BCOP or pure RKO) when applicable. On ResNet-34 with 224×224 inputs, AOC incurs only 13% training-time overhead and 4% memory overhead versus unconstrained convolutions at batch size 512, while achieving 91.5% clean accuracy on CIFAR-10 and 75.0% provable accuracy under the Hu et al. (2023) recipe.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, AOC achieves 91.5% clean accuracy in its accuracy-focused configuration (2.3M params) and 64.3% provable accuracy at ε=36/255 in its robustness-focused configuration (41.3M params). With the extended training recipe of Hu et al. (2023), AOC robust* reaches 75.0% provable accuracy, surpassing all prior methods without extra data. On ImageNet-1K, AOC accurate achieves 68.2% top-1 accuracy (53.1M params), and the scalability evaluation on ResNet-34 shows only 13% training overhead at batch size 512, compared to 2.03× for BCOP and 4.86× for SOC at smaller batches.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against BCOP (Li et al., 2019), AOC robust improves provable accuracy from 58.2% to 64.3% (+6.1%) while scaling from 2.6M to 41.3M parameters, and AOC accurate achieves 91.5% clean accuracy versus BCOP's 72.2%. Against Cayley (Trockman & Kolter, 2021), AOC robust outperforms Cayley Large by 2.9% on provable accuracy (64.3% vs 61.4%) and is substantially faster in training (1.13× overhead at batch 512 vs 4.27× at batch 128). Against SOC (Singla & Feizi, 2021), AOC robust edges out SOC 20 by 1.6% on provable accuracy (64.3% vs 62.7%) while reducing training overhead from 4.86× to 1.13×. Against SLL (Araujo et al., 2023), AOC robust* (75.0%) surpasses SLL X-Large (65.8%) by 9.2% on provable accuracy with 5× fewer parameters (46.3M vs 236M). Against AOL (Prach & Lampert, 2022), AOC robust matches AOL Large on provable accuracy (64.3% vs 64.0%) with 3.3× fewer parameters (41.3M vs 136M).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper combines two existing orthogonal convolution construction methods—BCOP (Li et al., 2019) for arbitrary kernel sizes and RKO (Serrurier et al., 2021) for native striding—via the block-convolution operator to produce a unified scheme (AOC) that simultaneously supports stride, transposition, grouping, and dilation. Prior methods (SOC, Cayley, LOT, ECO, SLL) each addressed subsets of these features but relied on expensive emulation (e.g., channel reshaping for stride) or frequency-domain operations that scale poorly with input size. The paper also positions itself as a general framework that can retrofit improvements onto existing methods like SOC, SLL, and Sandwich layers, rather than merely competing with them.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By reducing the computational overhead to ~10–13% at realistic batch sizes (versus 2.8×–4.9× for prior methods), AOC makes 1-Lipschitz constrained networks practical for large-scale architectures like ResNet-34 on ImageNet-1K, which were previously infeasible with orthogonal convolutions. The open-source Orthogonium library lowers the barrier for the broader community to adopt these layers in U-Nets, GANs, VAEs, and normalizing flows. Key open questions include the incomplete parametrization of the BCOP component (certain 2×2 orthogonal convolutions are unreachable), whether increasing kernel size fully compensates for this gap, and whether the framework extends naturally to 3D convolutions or video processing.
