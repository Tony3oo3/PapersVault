## Problem

Automatic vectorization tools like JAX's vmap execute all batch members in lock-step, so when MCMC algorithms contain variable-length while loops (as in HMC-NUTS, elliptical slice sampling, or delayed rejection), every chain must wait for the slowest chain at each iteration. This synchronization barrier can waste up to 99% of GPU capacity when chain iteration counts are highly variable. No prior method provided a principled, vmap-compatible way to restructure MCMC algorithms to eliminate these barriers.

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard single-chain MCMC implementation (a sample function with a log-likelihood, initial state, and random seed) and a target number of parallel chains m and samples n, and produces the same posterior samples as the unmodified algorithm. The key structural requirement is that the sample function's control flow consists of a finite number of while loops (one, two sequential, or two nested), which determines the number of FSM states. The output is identical in distribution to the standard MCMC output, but the walltime and ESS/second are improved, with speed-ups scaling with the number of chains and the skewness of the per-sample iteration count distribution.

## Method

The paper converts MCMC sample functions into finite state machines (FSMs) by partitioning the code into contiguous blocks at while-loop boundaries, where each block becomes a state function and the transition function encodes the loop conditions. A step function executes one block per call, and when vectorized with vmap, each chain independently tracks its own state, eliminating per-iteration synchronization barriers. Two optimization techniques further close the gap to the theoretical efficiency bound R(m) = E[max_j N_j] / E[N_1]: step bundling (replacing a switch over states with sequential conditionals to reduce the effective number of states K) and cost amortization (computing expensive operations like log-likelihood only once per step rather than in every state that requires it, reducing the overhead factor α). The paper proves via Hoeffding bounds for Markov chains that the long-run cost per sample converges at rate O(n^{-1/2}) to the expected single-chain cost scaled by control-flow overhead, and demonstrates speed-ups of up to an order of magnitude on HMC-NUTS, elliptical slice sampling, and delayed rejection across multiple datasets.

## Results

The FSM-based MCMC implementations achieve speed-ups of up to nearly an order of magnitude over standard vmap-vectorized implementations. On the 100D correlated Gaussian mixture, HMC-NUTS-FSM achieves ~10× speed-up at m=100 chains and >5× at m=500 chains in ESS/minute. On the Real Estate GPR task, elliptical slice sampling FSM reduces walltime from >30 minutes to ~10 minutes for 1024 chains (~3× speed-up), approaching the theoretical bound R(m). On challenging local-geometry distributions (Table 1), TESS-FSM improves ESS/sec in all 4 cases (up to ~2×) and NUTS-FSM improves in 3/4 cases (up to 3.5×).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against BlackJAX's standard (non-FSM) implementations, the FSM approach consistently wins: elliptical slice sampling achieves ~3× walltime speed-up at 1024 chains, HMC-NUTS achieves ~10× at 100 chains, and delayed-rejection MH achieves ~10× at 1024 chains. Against MEADS and CHEES (state-of-the-art adaptive HMC variants), TESS-FSM achieves the best ESS/sec on 3 of 4 distributions (PP: 2.3 vs 1.5/MEADS; GS: 1116 vs 480.8/MEADS; BOD: 2978 vs 64.85/MEADS), while MEADS and CHEES retain the edge on German Credit (141.5 and 199.0 vs TESS's 58.95). NUTS-FSM outperforms standard NUTS by 3.5× on Google Stock and 1.5× on Predator Prey, but is slightly worse (0.8×) on BOD where the log-pdf is cheap. The speed-ups are largest when the log-likelihood is expensive (PP, GS), confirming the theoretical prediction that amortization of costly computations within the FSM step function is key to approaching the efficiency bound R(m).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the finite state machine (FSM) framework from automata theory to restructure MCMC algorithms for efficient vectorization, a connection not previously explored in the MCMC or probabilistic programming literature. It builds on the well-established automatic vectorization paradigm (JAX's vmap) and the documented synchronization problem for while-loop-heavy samplers (HMC-NUTS, slice sampling, delayed rejection), but departs from prior hardware-aware MCMC work (Abadi et al., 2016; Lao et al., 2020; Radul et al., 2020) by providing a general, vmap-compatible recipe that preserves single-chain code structure and allows control flow within blocks. The theoretical analysis draws on Hoeffding-type concentration inequalities for Markov chains (Fan et al., 2021) to derive a tight upper bound on achievable speed-ups, which then guides two practical design techniques (step bundling and cost amortization) not present in prior batching approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The synchronization barrier is a fundamental and well-documented inefficiency in running multi-chain MCMC on GPUs, and this paper provides the first principled, general solution with provable speed-up guarantees. The demonstrated speed-ups (up to ~10× for HMC-NUTS, ~3× for elliptical slice sampling) are practically significant for Bayesian inference workloads, and the approach is drop-in compatible with existing JAX/BlackJAX workflows. Limitations include the analysis being developed for the single-while-loop case (though the construction generalizes), the residual overhead from evaluating all switch branches under vmap, and the observation that gains diminish when the log-likelihood is cheap relative to other computations. Open questions include how the approach interacts with adaptive MCMC (e.g., dual-averaging step-size tuning across chains) and whether the FSM decomposition can be automated end-to-end for arbitrary probabilistic programs.
