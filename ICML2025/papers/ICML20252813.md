## Problem

Existing direct training methods for SNNs produce temporal logit gradients with insufficient diversity across time steps: SDT uses an identical gradient at every step, while TET's per-step gradients all point uniformly toward the one-hot label. This homogeneity restricts temporal heterogeneity and yields temporally miscalibrated SNNs with overconfidence and degraded performance. No prior work has addressed this limitation through gradient rescaling in the temporal dimension.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252813/cropped_page6_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

TMC is a drop-in loss function for any rate-coded SNN trained via BPTT with surrogate gradients, so it inherits the input format of the underlying architecture: static RGB images (32×32 CIFAR, 224×224 ImageNet), integrated event streams (48×48 DVSCIFAR10, 32×32 DVS-Gesture), or text question pairs (QQP). The output is a class label determined by averaging accumulated logits over T time steps, but the distinguishing property is a calibrated probability distribution in which predicted confidence increases monotonically with time steps and converges to actual accuracy at each step, measured via ECE, AdaECE, and CECE.

## Method

TMC augments the per-time-step cross-entropy loss with a confidence regularization term θ_t^{λ_t}, where θ_t = β_t/(1−β_t) is the odds of the target-class probability computed from accumulated logits (with stop-gradient on historical logits to keep backpropagation tractable) and λ_t = (T−t)/T linearly decays the regularization strength over time. This produces a temporal logit gradient rescaling factor g_t that adaptively scales the CE gradient: when the network is overconfident (high P_t^k), g_t^k shrinks toward 0 or negative values to penalize overconfidence, while underconfident samples see g_t^k approach 1, allowing normal CE-driven learning. The 1/t factor and λ_t ensure the regularization weakens at later time steps so the CE loss dominates, driving confidence to increase monotonically and satisfy the 'temporally perfectly calibrated' condition (confidence equals accuracy at each step). Proposition 3.4 formally guarantees that if per-step target-class logits increase monotonically, the accumulated logits—and hence confidence—also increase monotonically, linking the gradient mechanism to the desired calibration property.

## Results

TMC achieves state-of-the-art accuracy on ImageNet (85.83% with QKFormer, 80.80% with Meta-SpikeFormer), DVSCIFAR10 (87.63% with VGG-SNN, T=10), and N-Caltech101 (88.24% with VGG-SNN, T=16). On CIFAR-100, TMC reaches 78.05% (ResNet-19, T=6), competitive with but slightly below TEBN (78.76%) and RMP-Loss (78.98%). Beyond accuracy, TMC substantially improves calibration: on DVSCIFAR10 at T=10, ECE drops to 0.08 (vs. 0.18 for TET and 0.26 for SDT), and AdaECE drops to 0.05 (vs. 0.16 and 0.18 respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On DVSCIFAR10, TMC (87.63%) outperforms the previous SOTA ETC (85.95%) by 1.68%, TKS (85.30%) by 2.33%, and TEBN (84.90%) by 2.73%, all using VGG-SNN with T=10. On N-Caltech101, TMC at T=16 reaches 88.24%, surpassing ETC (85.53%) by 2.71% and TKS (84.10%) by 4.14%. On ImageNet, TMC improves QKFormer from 85.65% to 85.83% (+0.18%) and Spike-Driven Transformer V2 from 80.00% to 80.80% (+0.80%), establishing new SOTA. On CIFAR-100, TMC (78.05%) trails TEBN (78.76%) and RMP-Loss (78.98%) by ~0.7–0.9%, which the authors attribute to those methods applying extra membrane potential regularization; however, TMC clearly beats TET (74.72%), TKS (76.20%), and TSSD (74.69%). In calibration, TMC reduces ECE by 55% relative to TET at T=10 on DVSCIFAR10 (0.08 vs. 0.18), and uniquely shows monotonically decreasing calibration error across time steps, whereas SDT and TET plateau or worsen.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate research threads: learning-based model calibration in ANNs (Mukhoti et al., 2020; Tang et al., 2020), which was shown to operate through logit gradient rescaling, and direct training of rate-coded SNNs (Deng et al., 2024; Wu et al., 2018). While prior SNN work has enhanced temporal heterogeneity through parametric neurons (GLIF, DIET-SNN) or recurrent connections, this paper identifies an overlooked dimension—the temporal logit gradient—and shows that existing loss functions (SDT, TET) produce insufficient gradient diversity across time steps. TMC repurposes the calibration rescaling mechanism from ANNs and extends it to the temporal axis, introducing a confidence-regularization term θ_t^λ_t that adaptively rescales per-time-step gradients.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that temporal logit gradient diversity is a bottleneck for SNN performance reframes how the community should think about enhancing temporal heterogeneity—shifting attention from architecture and neuron dynamics to the optimization landscape itself. The method's simplicity (a single regularization term added to CE loss) makes it broadly applicable across SNN architectures, and the temporal scalability result (accuracy continues to improve with more time steps, unlike SDT/TET) suggests TMC-trained SNNs extract genuinely diverse temporal features. Limitations include modest gains on static datasets where temporal structure is less critical, and the method is specifically designed for rate-coded SNNs; whether the calibration perspective transfers to event-driven or other SNN paradigms remains open.
