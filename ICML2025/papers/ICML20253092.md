## Problem

Visual in-context learning (V-ICL) has been largely confined to natural-object segmentation and generalist tasks, with no prior work adapting the paradigm to fine-grained OCR tasks like text segmentation and removal. Existing V-ICL generalists rely on a straightforward image-label compositor that forces the model into a single-step reasoning process, and the inherent visual heterogeneity of text (diverse fonts, styles, languages) makes selecting homogeneous in-context demonstrations particularly challenging.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a composited grid of size 3×2h×3w containing two rows (demonstration and query) and three columns (original image, text-removed image, segmentation mask), where masking tokens replace the query's removal and segmentation labels during inference. Unlike standard V-ICL that produces a single label output, ConText simultaneously generates both the text-removed image and the pixel-level segmentation mask in one end-to-end forward pass. The demonstration is a single randomly-selected (or ground-truth) sample, and the model is evaluated averaged over three trials to account for demonstration sensitivity.

## Method

ConText builds on a ViT-L + decoder MAE backbone (initialized from SegGPT) and introduces three key modifications. First, task-chaining restructures the prompt from a simple input-output pair into an input-removal-segmentation triple, applying spatially correlated masks to both the removal and segmentation labels so the model reconstructs both tasks jointly, exploiting the implicit logical connection that the segmentation mask corresponds to the visual difference between original and erased images. Second, context-aware aggregation (CAA) performs a two-step label fusion: a linear context-free fusion followed by a shared cross-attention module where each label feature attends to the other demonstration's features, explicitly integrating outer-demonstration knowledge into the query representation. Third, a self-prompting strategy uses identical input-output pairs for both demonstration and query with probability 0.2 during training, preventing the model from collapsing into a context-free specialist given the difficulty of finding visually homogeneous text demonstrations. Training uses smooth-L1 reconstruction loss for both tasks plus a lightweight pixel-level cross-entropy supervision, with AdamW at lr=1e-4 over 150 epochs.

## Results

ConText achieves a PSNR of 38.36 and FID of 11.04 (averaged across HierText, SCUT-EnsText, and SCUT-Syn) for text removal, and an average fgIoU of 76.77% across four segmentation benchmarks (HierText, TotalText, FST, TextSeg). The stronger ConTextV variant, trained on additional data, reaches 89.74% fgIoU on TextSeg and 40.83 PSNR on SCUT-EnsText, outperforming all task-specific specialists by +2.53% average fgIoU and +3.96 PSNR respectively. On the user-prompted PromptText benchmark, ConTextV achieves 59.19% fgIoU and 38.14 PSNR, substantially exceeding both specialists and other V-ICL generalists.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ConText substantially outperforms all no-fine-tuning V-ICL baselines: it improves average removal PSNR by +10.06 over Painter (38.36 vs. 28.30) and average segmentation fgIoU by +64.89 over SegGPT (76.77 vs. 11.88). Against task-specific fine-tuning baselines (Painter→Rem.+Seg. and SegGPT→Rem.+Seg.), ConText gains +5.32 PSNR on removal and +8.43% fgIoU on segmentation, demonstrating that task-chaining and CAA provide meaningful gains beyond simple multi-task fine-tuning. For segmentation specialists (Table 2), ConTextV achieves 89.74% fgIoU on TextSeg, surpassing UPOCR (88.76%) and Hi-SAM (88.77%), and reaches 75.90% on the unseen FST dataset, outperforming TexRNet+HRNetV2 (73.38%) and EAFormer (72.63%). For removal specialists (Table 3), ConTextV achieves 40.83 PSNR and 0.76 AGE on SCUT-EnsText, beating UPOCR (37.14 PSNR) and ViT-Eraser (36.87 PSNR) by +3.69 and +3.96 PSNR respectively. On the user-prompted PromptText benchmark (Table 4), ConTextV achieves 59.19% fgIoU and 38.14 PSNR, far exceeding SegGPT→Seg.+Rem. (44.69% fgIoU, 23.73 PSNR) and all specialists, confirming superior in-context understanding of explicit visual instructions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the MAE-based composited-prompting V-ICL paradigm (Bar et al., 2022; Painter, Wang et al., 2023b; SegGPT, Wang et al., 2023d) from natural object segmentation to OCR tasks, specifically text removal and segmentation. The key conceptual departure is the task-chaining prompt (input-removal-segmentation), which reframes the single-step image-label reconstruction into a multi-task reasoning chain inspired by chain-of-thought in LLMs. Additionally, the context-aware aggregation module addresses a limitation of prior V-ICL generalists where the linear input-label fusion only captures intra-pair information, while the self-prompting strategy tackles the unique heterogeneity challenge of text recognition that has no analogue in natural object segmentation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that V-ICL generalists can match or exceed task-specific specialists on fine-grained OCR tasks, suggesting a path toward unified, prompt-driven text processing without per-task fine-tuning. The task-chaining concept is shown to generalize beyond OCR (validated on watermark removal), hinting at broader applicability to any pair of visually correlated tasks. Open questions include whether the approach extends to more complex OCR tasks (recognition, detection, layout analysis), how it scales with more than two chained tasks, and whether the synthetic-natural domain gap (evident in SCUT-Syn results) can be further mitigated. The self-prompting finding—that without it the model silently degenerates into a context-free specialist—raises important questions about the robustness of V-ICL in heterogeneous domains.
