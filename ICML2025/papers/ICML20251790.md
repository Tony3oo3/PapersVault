## Problem

Discovering effective degrees of freedom (collective variables) that navigate the low-energy conformational landscape of molecules is critical for efficient MD sampling, yet existing approaches either require extensive trajectory data for ML-based CV discovery or rely on manual expert knowledge. This paper addresses the gap by formulating DOF discovery as a data-free problem: identifying approximate symmetries of the energy function itself, using only the forcefield and its local derivatives around a single initial conformation.

## Input / Output

The method consumes the molecular forcefield (AMBER energy function) evaluated at and around a single initial conformation x₀, along with the Hessian matrix at a nearby critical point; no trajectory or simulation data is required. It outputs two Lie algebra elements L₁, L₂ (n×n matrices acting on particle indices) that parameterize group transformations x = exp(θ₁L₁ + θ₂L₂)x₀. These are then used to generate a 31×31 grid of deformed conformations over (θ₁,θ₂) ∈ [0,2π)², each of which is energy-minimized to yield stable conformers. The SE(3) invariance constraint restricts L to act only on particle indices, excluding trivial global rotations and translations.

## Method

The core insight is that low-energy DOF correspond to approximate symmetries of the potential energy: transformations L in the Lie algebra gl(X) that leave E nearly invariant at large displacements but produce meaningful energy changes at small displacements. The paper derives a symmetry loss L(L,x) = (∇E(x)·Lx)² subject to ‖L‖_F ≤ 1, which is minimized to discover candidate DOF (scalable, compatible with any forcefield including implicit solvent). For smaller systems, a Hessian-based formulation decomposes the expected loss into σ²‖HLx*‖² and σ⁴(2Tr[K_S²]+Tr[K_S]²) terms, yielding analytical solutions: symmetric L supported on slow eigenspaces of H², and antisymmetric L generating SO(k) rotations within degenerate eigenspaces. A two-step procedure first minimizes the symmetry loss at large perturbations (discovery) then maximizes energy change at small perturbations (selection), and the top two resulting L(c) combinations are retained as the effective DOF for conformational exploration.

## Results

The proposed symmetry-based DOF discovery method identifies all major conformers (C5, C7eq, C7ax for alanine dipeptide in vacuum; β, αL, αR in solvent; folded and misfolded for chignolin) within 2 ps of short MD refinement per grid point, compared to 500 ns for a standard long OpenMM simulation. Even after 500 ns, the long simulation has only reached fractional conformer populations of 0.23 (C5), 0.41 (C7eq), and 0.06 (C7ax) in vacuum, and 0.39 (β), 0.001 (αL), 0.043 (αR) in solvent. For chignolin in solvent, the long simulation reaches only 0.40 (folded) and 0.048 (misfolded) after 500 ns. The method also recovers conformer energies within ~5 kJ/mol of the long-simulation ground truth (e.g., −80 vs −80 kJ/mol for C5, −84 vs −83 for C7eq in vacuum).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two baselines: (1) a standard 500 ns OpenMM MD simulation at 300 K, and (2) Replica Exchange MD (REMD) with 8 parallel replicas at logarithmically spaced temperatures (300–500 K) over 10 ns [Sugita & Okamoto, 1999]. The proposed method discovers all major conformers within 2 ps of per-grid-point refinement, representing a speedup of roughly 5 orders of magnitude over the long MD baseline (2 ps vs. 500 ns) and ~4 orders of magnitude over REMD (2 ps vs. 10 ns). Even under the enhanced-sampling REMD protocol, some conformers (e.g., C7ax in vacuum, αL in solvent) are only first reached after 1 ns, whereas the symmetry-based method captures them immediately. In terms of conformer coverage, the long simulation still fails to adequately populate αL (0.001) and C7ax (0.059) after 500 ns, while the proposed method reaches all conformers in a single 31×31 grid sweep. The method also generalizes across force-field settings: DOF discovered in vacuum successfully navigate the solvent energy landscape, and the direct-optimization variant can incorporate implicit solvent forces directly.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes Lie algebra symmetry-discovery concepts from the ML literature (Benton et al., 2020; Dehmamy et al., 2021; LieGG by Moskalev et al., 2022) to the molecular dynamics setting, but with a critical departure: instead of discovering exact symmetries of a learned loss function from data, it finds *approximate* symmetries of a known force-field energy function without any trajectory data. It positions itself against the large body of ML-based collective variable discovery methods (autoencoders, DeepLNE, time-lagged autoencoders) that all require extensive simulation data, and against enhanced sampling techniques (metadynamics, REMD, umbrella sampling) that require careful tuning of collective variables. The Hessian-based analytic solutions (slow eigenspaces, degenerate subspaces) draw on standard spectral decomposition but are applied in a novel way to extract physically meaningful molecular DOF.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The data-free formulation is the key practical contribution: discovering effective DOF from the force-field alone, without running expensive preliminary simulations, could significantly reduce the setup cost for enhanced sampling in drug discovery and protein folding. However, the experimental validation is limited to two small peptides (alanine dipeptide, chignolin), and the Hessian-based methods scale quadratically with system size, making them impractical for real proteins. The optimization-based approach, while more scalable in principle, still incurs quadratic cost from pairwise atomic interactions and is less stable in practice. Open questions include whether the symmetry-DOF correspondence holds for larger, more complex systems, how to incorporate finite-temperature and kinetic effects, and whether the approach can be combined with ML-based CV discovery for hybrid data-free/data-driven pipelines.
