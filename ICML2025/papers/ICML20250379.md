## Problem

Time series classification suffers from train/test domain shifts that degrade performance, yet the widely used Reversible Instance Normalization (RevIN) that mitigates this in forecasting tasks is ineffective for classification because it also compresses inter-class distances. The paper identifies this gap and asks how to improve generalization under distribution shift without relying on normalization that harms class separability.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250379/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts univariate (d=1) or multivariate (d>1) time series of variable length T, where each time point is a d-dimensional vector, and outputs a class label from C classes. The backbone is an InceptionTime-style CNN feature extractor producing a 128×T representation, followed by mean pooling and a two-layer MLP (128→128→C) for classification. The FIC constraint operates on the gradient of the cross-entropy loss during training and requires no modification to the input pipeline or output format.

## Method

FIC-TSC constrains the Fisher Information Matrix (FIM) during training to guide the network toward flatter minima. Using a diagonal approximation (reducing memory from O(n²) to O(n)), the method computes the element-wise squared gradient as the diagonal FIM estimate; if its L1 norm exceeds a threshold ε, the gradient is rescaled by √(ε/‖F‖₁) before the optimizer step. This requires only a single backward pass per iteration, unlike sharpness-aware minimization which needs two. Theoretically, the paper proves that at a local minimum the expected Hessian equals the FIM (Lemma 1), that α-sharpness is upper-bounded by α²‖F‖₁/(2(1+L)) (Corollary 1), and that the constrained gradient descent retains an O(1/T) convergence rate (Theorem 4). The approach is model-agnostic and is demonstrated on InceptionTime, TimesNet, and PatchTST backbones.

## Results

On 26 UEA multivariate datasets, FIC-TSC with universal hyperparameters (Uni.) achieves 76.9% average accuracy, outperforming the best SOTA baselines MILLET (74.8%) and TodyNet (74.8%) by 2.1%. With dataset-wise hyperparameter tuning (Full), it reaches 79.6%. On 85 UCR univariate datasets, FIC-TSC (Uni.) achieves 86.2% average accuracy, surpassing HC2 (86.0%) and MILLET (85.6%), with the Full variant reaching 87.3%. Wilcoxon signed-rank tests confirm statistical significance (p=0.039 vs MILLET, p=0.020 vs TodyNet on UEA).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FIC-TSC (Uni.) achieves the best average accuracy on both UEA (76.9%) and UCR (86.2%) benchmarks, outperforming all 14 compared methods. On UEA, it leads MILLET (74.8%) and TodyNet (74.8%) by 2.1%, and ConvTran (73.0%) by 3.9%; the gap widens to 10.3% over GPT4TS (58.5%) and 12.3% over TimesNet (66.6%). On UCR, it edges out HC2 (86.0%) by 0.2% and MILLET (85.6%) by 0.6%, while substantially outperforming GPT4TS (61.6%) and TimesNet (65.3%). Against SAM (sharpness-aware minimization), FIC-TSC improves accuracy by 2.6% (76.8% vs 74.2%) while halving per-iteration runtime (0.070s vs 0.158s). On explicit domain-shift healthcare datasets (TDBrain, ADFTD, PTB-XL, SleepEDF), FIC-TSC consistently outperforms the recent SOTA Medformer, e.g., 96.2% vs 89.6% on TDBrain and 86.7% vs 82.8% on SleepEDF. The method also generalizes to other backbones: applying FIC to TimesNet and PatchTST yields ~4% accuracy gains over their original versions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of Fisher information theory and time series classification optimization. It departs from the dominant RevIN/instance-normalization approach for handling domain shift in time series (established for forecasting) by demonstrating its ineffectiveness for classification, and instead repurposes the Fisher Information Matrix—previously used in continual learning (EWC) and curvature estimation (K-FAC)—as a gradient re-normalization constraint. The closest conceptual competitor is Sharpness-Aware Minimization (SAM), which also targets flat minima but requires double backward passes; FIC-TSC achieves a similar objective through a single-pass diagonal FIM approximation, connecting FIM to loss sharpness via the standard Hessian-FIM equivalence at local minima.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap: domain shift in time series classification was largely assumed handled by RevIN, but this work shows that assumption is wrong for classification tasks, motivating a different solution. The FIC constraint is lightweight (O(n) memory, single backward pass) and plug-and-play across architectures (ITime, TimesNet, PatchTST), making it broadly adoptable. Open questions include whether the diagonal FIM approximation is sufficient for very large models, whether the sharpness-generalization link holds universally (the authors acknowledge this remains debated), and whether the approach extends to other sequence tasks beyond classification. The modest but consistent accuracy gains (~2% over SOTA) and the ~40% sharpness reduction suggest the method's value lies more in robustness and efficiency than in raw accuracy.
