## Problem

Existing multi-task VRP (MTVRP) solvers assume customer locations are drawn from a uniform distribution, ignoring the structured geographical patterns present in real-world logistics. This paper introduces the Multi-Task Multi-Distribution VRP (MTMDVRP) setting, where instances are sampled from fixed country maps with realistic spatial structure, and shows that unified neural solvers must simultaneously generalize across both unseen task combinations and unseen spatial distributions—a substantially harder problem than either dimension alone.

## Input / Output

Each instance provides node coordinates (x, y) sampled from a fixed pool of M city locations per country map (with V < M selected per instance), per-node demands, optional time windows, and a one-hot task constraint vector indicating which of five constraint types (open route, backhaul, duration limit, time window, capacity) are active. At each autoregressive decoding step, the model also receives dynamic features: remaining vehicle capacity, current partial route length, current time, and an open-route flag. The output is a sequence of node visits forming sub-tours, evaluated by total Euclidean tour length and optimality gap relative to HGS or OR-tools solvers.

## Method

SHIELD combines two sparsity mechanisms to regularize a transformer-based autoregressive VRP solver for cross-task and cross-distribution generalization. First, a customized Mixture-of-Depths (MoD) decoder replaces dense layers with 3 sparse layers where a learned router selects only the top 10% of contextual tokens to pass through each layer, while unselected tokens skip via residual connections—dynamically allocating compute to the most decision-critical nodes. Second, a context-based soft-clustering layer performs B=5 EM-inspired iterations in latent space, using task-specific prompts (learnable projections of one-hot constraint vectors) to produce N_c=5 cluster centroids that summarize unvisited nodes; these centroids are updated at each step by subtracting visited-node contributions and fed as additional context to the decoder. The encoder retains 6 Mixture-of-Experts layers to capture diverse task-constraint interactions, and the full model is trained end-to-end via REINFORCE with POMO-style 8× coordinate augmentation over 1,000 epochs on 20,000 instances per epoch.

## Results

On MTMDVRP50, SHIELD achieves the lowest optimality gap across all task/distribution settings: 2.37% (in-task, in-dist), 2.74% (in-task, out-dist), 6.08% (out-task, in-dist), and 6.35% (out-task, out-dist). On the harder MTMDVRP100, SHIELD achieves 2.44% (in-task, in-dist) and 3.16% (in-task, out-dist), while MVMoE-Deeper runs out of memory entirely. For zero-shot size generalization to MTMDVRP200, SHIELD achieves 3.79% gap (in-task, in-dist) and 7.82% (out-task, out-dist), outperforming all baselines by 1–3 percentage points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SHIELD outperforms all three baselines across every task/distribution setting. Against POMO-MTVRP (Liu et al., KDD 2024), SHIELD reduces the optimality gap by 1.1–2.6 percentage points (e.g., 2.37% vs. 3.51% on in-task in-dist MTMDVRP50; 6.27% vs. 8.89% on out-task out-dist MTMDVRP100). Against MVMoE (Zhou et al., ICML 2024), the advantage is 0.8–2.4 percentage points, with the largest gains on out-of-distribution and out-of-task settings where MVMoE's fixed-depth decoder struggles. Against INViT (Fang et al., ICML 2024), which uses heuristic k-NN sparse attention, SHIELD is dramatically superior: 2.37% vs. 9.14% gap on in-task in-dist MTMDVRP50, and 6.27% vs. 28.0% on out-task out-dist MTMDVRP100, confirming that learned sparsity (MoD) substantially outperforms spatial-distance-based pruning in multi-task multi-distribution settings. Notably, MVMoE-Deeper (a deeper variant of MVMoE matched to SHIELD's decoder depth) achieves competitive in-distribution results on MTMDVRP50 but completely fails (OOM) on MTMDVRP100, whereas SHIELD's sparsity keeps it trainable and superior.

## Relation to Prior Work

SHIELD extends the MVMoE multi-task VRP solver (Zhou et al., ICML 2024) by replacing its dense decoder layers with a Mixture-of-Depths (MoD) mechanism adapted from Raposo et al. (2024), and by incorporating a context-prompted soft-clustering layer inspired by Goh et al. (KDD 2024). The base autoregressive encoder-decoder architecture and POMO-style augmentation follow Kwon et al. (NeurIPS 2020). Conceptually, the paper departs from prior multi-task VRP work (Liu et al., KDD 2024; Zhou et al., ICML 2024) by introducing realistic geographic distributions alongside task variation, and by framing sparsity in both compute (MoD) and representation (clustering) as an explicit regularization mechanism grounded in a VC-dimension argument.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The MTMDVRP benchmark is a practically important step toward foundation models for routing that reflect real-world logistics (fixed city maps, subset sampling), and the finding that simply deepening the decoder (MVMoE-Deeper) causes OOM on 100-node problems while the sparser SHIELD handles them gracefully challenges the 'more depth is better' assumption in NCO. The clustering ablation showing that increasing clusters from 5 to 20 nearly doubles the out-task optimality gap provides a clear negative result on representational over-parameterization. Open questions include whether the VC-dimension framing extends to non-polynomial activations, whether the approach scales to 500+ node problems without architectural changes, and whether the learned sparsity patterns transfer across problem families beyond VRP.
