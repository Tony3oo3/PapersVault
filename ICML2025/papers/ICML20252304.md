## Problem

Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) suffer from 'multilingual erosion': after supervised fine-tuning on predominantly English image-text data, they lose the ability to understand and generate responses in non-English languages. The root cause is a misalignment between English-biased visual tokens (from CLIP) and non-English textual tokens, compounded by the scarcity of high-quality non-English multimodal training data and the absence of comprehensive multilingual evaluation benchmarks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252304/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts a 336×336 RGB image and a multilingual text prompt (in English, Chinese, Portuguese, Arabic, Turkish, or Russian) and generates a text response in the same language as the prompt. The image is encoded by a frozen CLIP ViT-L/14 vision encoder, and the text is tokenized through a Qwen LLM. A key design detail is that the visual token representation is dynamically conditioned on the language of the text input at inference time, so the same image yields different language-specific visual embeddings depending on the prompt language.

## Method

PARROT introduces a lightweight Mixture-of-Experts module that converts English-biased visual tokens into language-specific embeddings using textual guidance. Specifically, a cross-attention operation between the [CLS] token of projected visual features and the text embeddings produces a language-conditioned signal, which is fed into a linear MoE router to compute a probability distribution over six language experts (one per target language). Each expert is a two-layer MLP with SiLU activation that transforms the English-biased visual tokens; the final representation is a residual combination G_v = H_v + α·MoE(H_v) to preserve original visual-semantic information. Training is two-stage: Stage 1 trains only the MLP projector on 1.2M English image-text pairs (MoE bypassed), and Stage 2 fine-tunes the projector, LLM, and randomly initialized MoE on ~793K samples including ~10K multilingual pairs per language from ShareGPT4V, requiring less than 1% of the data used by competing multilingual MLLMs.

## Results

PARROT-Qwen2-7B achieves state-of-the-art accuracy on 4 out of 6 languages (Portuguese, Arabic, Turkish, Russian) on both MMMB and MMBench, with English and Chinese in second place behind Qwen2-VL. On MMMB, PARROT-Qwen2-7B scores 80.11 (en), 80.03 (zh), 79.62 (pt), 76.55 (ar), 75.02 (tr), and 79.94 (ru); on MMBench it scores 78.02, 77.16, 76.76, 75.92, 74.05, and 77.71 respectively. The model also demonstrates competitive performance across broader multimodal tasks (MME, MMStar, ScienceQA, SEED-Bench, RealWorldQA), and achieves these results using less than 1% of the training data compared to other multilingual MLLMs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PARROT-Qwen2-7B outperforms all 14 non-Qwen2-VL baselines on all six languages across both MMMB and MMBench. Against the strongest competitor Qwen2-VL, PARROT wins on Portuguese (+1.51 MMMB, +0.83 MMB), Arabic (+2.48, +1.22), Turkish (+3.30, +0.60), and Russian (+0.61, +1.83), but trails slightly on English (−0.40 MMMB, −0.57 MMB) and Chinese (−0.20, −1.23). Against LLaVA-OneVision (also Qwen2-7B backbone), PARROT leads by 1.08–7.23 points on MMMB and 1.28–9.20 points on MMBench across all six languages. Against Idefics3-8B, the gap is 5.61–14.61 points on MMMB and 4.52–8.92 on MMBench. Against LLaVA-1.5, PARROT improves by 13.04–33.05 points on MMMB and 12.65–20.81 on MMBench, demonstrating that the MoE-based textual guidance architecture — rather than simply adding multilingual data — drives the gains (LLaVA with the same multilingual data shows only ~0.5–1.7 point improvement). The paper also shows that a naive translation-based baseline (Google Translate) produces a 'seesaw effect,' improving Chinese by +1.9 but degrading Portuguese (−1.2) and Russian (−0.2), further validating PARROT's architecture over translation shortcuts.

## Relation to Prior Work

PARROT builds directly on LLaVA's two-stage training paradigm (projector pre-training followed by instruction tuning) and its MLP projector architecture, but departs from prior multilingual MLLMs (VisCPM, Qwen-VL, mPLUG-Owl) that rely on massive translated corpora or bilingual pre-training to achieve multilingual capability. The key conceptual departure is treating multilingual erosion as a visual-token alignment problem rather than a data-imbalance problem: a cross-attention mechanism conditions English-biased visual features on textual embeddings, and a lightweight MoE module routes them to language-specific experts. This draws on MoE routing ideas from multilingual machine translation (Zhao et al., 2024; Pires et al., 2023) but repurposes them for cross-modal alignment at the language level, and is motivated by an empirical pilot study contrasting OpenAI-CLIP with Chinese-CLIP.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PARROT demonstrates that multilingual MLLM capability can be achieved with under 1% of the training data used by competing approaches (e.g., ~10K samples per language vs. hundreds of millions for Qwen-VL or VisCPM), which is practically significant given the scarcity of high-quality non-English image-text data. The MMMB benchmark (6 languages, 12K questions) fills a genuine gap in multilingual multimodal evaluation, though it remains limited in language coverage and cultural depth. Open questions include whether the MoE approach scales gracefully beyond six languages, whether it generalizes to video or audio modalities, and whether the English-biased CLIP encoder fundamentally caps performance for low-resource languages despite the routing mechanism.
