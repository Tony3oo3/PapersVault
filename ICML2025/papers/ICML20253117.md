## Problem

TTFS (time-to-first-spike) coding achieves superior energy efficiency by encoding each value in a single spike's timing, but prior TTFS-based ANN-to-SNN conversions have been limited to MLPs and CNNs with ReLU activations. Transformer architectures introduce nonlinear activations (GELU, SiLU), softmax, LayerNorm, and multiplication operations that existing TTFS neurons cannot represent, leaving a gap between the energy efficiency of TTFS coding and the performance of Transformer-based SNNs that rely on less efficient rate coding.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20253117/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes pre-trained ANN Transformer weights (ViT-S/B/L, EVA-G/S/L) and converts them to SNNs without retraining. Input RGB images (224×224, ImageNet-1K) are encoded as TTFS spikes where each pixel value maps to a spike time via t = T_ref − xτ. The output is a class label determined by the index of the earliest spike in the final layer, eliminating the need to decode spike times back to real-valued logits.

## Method

TTFSFormer introduces a generalized spiking neuron with two flexible kernel functions—an input transform η that applies arbitrary differentiable functions to presynaptic values, and an output transform ψ that maps accumulated potential through a nonlinear function h—along with a zero reference time T_ref that extends the representational range beyond [0,1] to accommodate negative activations. Theoretical proofs (Theorems 4.1 and 4.3) establish exact mappings for any differentiable input/output transformations, enabling lossless conversion of SiLU/GELU activations, softmax (decomposed into a log-sum-exp neuron plus subtraction and exponential), LayerNorm (via mean, variance, and reciprocal-sqrt neurons), and multiplication (via a two-layer log-exp construction). The conversion is entirely training-free: pre-trained ANN weights are directly mapped to SNN synaptic weights, and per-layer constants (τ, T_ref, C) are set from monitored activation ranges, achieving <0.1% accuracy loss with ~20% of ANN energy consumption.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K, TTFSFormer achieves top-1 accuracy within 0.1% of the corresponding ANN across all tested architectures: 85.78% for ViT-L/16 (ANN: 85.83%), 88.90% for EVA-G (ANN: 88.88%), and 90.03% for EVA02-L (ANN: 90.05%). The method requires only ~20% of the energy consumption of the original ANN (e.g., 59 mJ vs. ~285 mJ for ViT-L/16). At a TTFS time precision of 1024 or higher, the converted SNN matches ANN accuracy without any fine-tuning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against rate-coding Transformer-to-SNN methods, TTFSFormer shows substantial gains: on ViT-B/16 it reaches 85.07% versus 82.79% (STA) and 82.71% (SpikeZIP-TF), a gap of over 2.3 points; on ViT-L/16 it achieves 85.78% versus 84.60% (ECMT) and 83.42% (SpikeZIP-TF). On EVA-G, TTFSFormer (88.90%) slightly exceeds ECMT (88.60%) and even surpasses the ANN baseline (88.88%). Compared to direct-training SNN Transformers, TTFSFormer matches or exceeds E-Spikeformer (86.2% at 173M params) while using a 307M-param ViT-L/16 with no training cost. Against the burst-coding AdaFire method, TTFSFormer outperforms by 4.3 points on ViT-S/16 (81.40% vs. 77.09%). The key advantage is that TTFS encoding conveys each layer's output in a single spike, eliminating the multi-spike approximation error inherent in rate coding, which is especially critical for nonlinear layers like softmax and LayerNorm.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the exact TTFS-based ANN-to-SNN conversion framework of Stanojevic et al. (2023, 2024), which was limited to ReLU-based CNNs and MLPs, to the full Transformer architecture. The key departure is the introduction of generalized input and output transform kernels (η, ψ) and a zero reference time mechanism, which together enable representation of negative values and arbitrary differentiable nonlinearities (GELU, SiLU, softmax, LayerNorm, multiplication) within the TTFS coding scheme. The two-stage receiving/emitting pipeline from Rueckauer & Liu (2018) is retained as the temporal scaffolding, but the neuron dynamics are substantially generalized beyond the Heaviside kernel used in prior TTFS work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that a full Transformer (including self-attention with softmax and LayerNorm) can be converted to a TTFS-coded SNN with near-zero accuracy loss (<0.1%) and ~80% energy reduction, opening a path toward energy-efficient neuromorphic inference for modern vision models. The theoretical construction (Theorems 4.1, 4.3) is general enough to accommodate any differentiable activation, suggesting the framework could extend to other architectures beyond ViT/EVA. Open questions include hardware feasibility at the required time precision (≥1024), whether direct training of TTFS Transformers is viable, and how the method scales to even larger models or non-vision tasks.
