## Problem

Fourier-based deraining methods leverage the frequency domain's ability to separate rain degradation from image content, but they typically rely on 1×1 convolutions that cannot model dependencies between different frequency bands. This limits the full utilization of frequency information, as the ordered low-to-high frequency correlations inherent in image degradation remain unexploited. The paper asks whether introducing Mamba's selective scanning mechanism directly into Fourier space can establish orderly frequency correlations to improve deraining.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251389/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single rainy RGB image (H×W×3) and outputs a derained image of the same spatial dimensions. Training uses the Rain13k synthetic dataset (13,712 pairs) with a progressive strategy that increases patch size from 160×160 to 384×384 over 80,000 iterations. The model is a multi-scale U-Net with 17.62M parameters and 22.56 GFLOPs, and can be applied zero-shot to real-world rainy images (SPA-Data, RE-RAIN, RainDS-Real) without retraining.

## Method

FourierMamba integrates Mamba's selective scanning into the Fourier domain via two complementary blocks within a multi-scale U-Net. The Fourier Spatial Interaction SSM (FSI-SSM) applies FFT to spatial features, decomposes into amplitude and phase, and scans them using two zigzag-based strategies (bilateral zigzag and progressive zigzag) that reorder frequencies from low to high, exploiting the concentric-circle arrangement of spatial frequencies; a parallel spatial Mamba branch captures complementary spatial-domain features. The Fourier Channel Evolution SSM (FCE-SSM) applies a channel-dimension FFT (C-FFT) after global average pooling, then directly scans the resulting 1D frequency sequence with Mamba since channel frequencies are already axis-ordered, and multiplies the result back as channel attention. The key design insight is that the unique frequency ordering in Fourier space—unarranged in spatial axes but arranged along channel axes—necessitates distinct scanning strategies for each dimension, with the zigzag coding adapted from JPEG compression to respect the symmetry of the 2D Fourier spectrum.

## Results

FourierMamba achieves state-of-the-art deraining performance on four synthetic benchmarks: 31.79 dB / 0.913 SSIM on Rain100H, 39.73 dB / 0.986 SSIM on Rain100L, 34.23 dB / 0.949 SSIM on Test2800, and 34.76 dB / 0.938 SSIM on Test1200, with only 17.62M parameters and 22.56 GFLOPs. On the real-world SPA-Data benchmark, it reaches 49.18 dB / 0.9931 SSIM, surpassing DRSformer (48.54 dB) and Restormer (47.98 dB). The method also generalizes to low-light enhancement (23.78 dB on LOL-V1) and dehazing (18.91 dB on Dense-Haze), outperforming FreqMamba on all secondary tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FourierMamba outperforms the closest Mamba-based competitor FreqMamba on Rain100H (31.79 vs 31.74 dB), Rain100L (39.73 vs 39.18 dB), and Test1200 (34.76 vs 33.36 dB), though FreqMamba holds a marginal edge on Test2800 (34.25 vs 34.23 dB PSNR; 0.951 vs 0.949 SSIM). Against VMambaIR, FourierMamba leads on all four datasets, with the largest gap on Test1200 (+1.43 dB). Compared to the Transformer-based Restormer, FourierMamba gains +0.33 dB on Rain100H, +0.74 dB on Rain100L, and +1.57 dB on Test1200 while using roughly 1.4× fewer parameters (17.62M vs 24.53M) and 7.7× fewer FLOPs (22.56 vs 174.7 GFLOPs). On the real-world SPA-Data benchmark, FourierMamba (49.18 dB) surpasses DRSformer (48.54 dB) and Restormer (47.98 dB) by 0.64 and 1.20 dB respectively. The method also dominates on secondary tasks: on LOL-V1 low-light enhancement it reaches 23.78 dB vs FreqMamba's 23.57 dB, and on Dense-Haze dehazing it achieves 18.91 dB vs FreqMamba's 17.35 dB, a 1.56 dB improvement.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two recent lines of work: Fourier-domain image restoration (Fourmer, FECNet) and Mamba-based state space models for vision (MambaIR, VMambaIR). It departs from prior Fourier-based deraining methods that rely on 1×1 convolutions in the frequency domain—arguing these cannot model inter-frequency dependencies—and from prior Mamba restoration methods that scan only in the spatial domain. The key conceptual move is introducing Mamba's selective scanning directly into Fourier space, with custom zigzag scanning strategies (bilateral and progressive) that respect the concentric low-to-high frequency arrangement, and a channel-dimension Fourier transform to capture cross-channel frequency correlations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The zigzag scanning design in Fourier space offers a reusable principle for any frequency-domain restoration task where ordered frequency correlation matters, and the channel-dimension Fourier transform provides a lightweight mechanism for global channel interaction. However, the paper is primarily validated on a U-shaped architecture for deraining, and the authors acknowledge that generalization to other architectures (isotropic, multi-stage) and tasks remains open. The modest but consistent gains over FreqMamba and MambaIR suggest the frequency-ordering insight is real but not yet transformative; broader adoption will depend on whether the zigzag scanning strategy proves advantageous in other frequency-domain settings beyond deraining.
