## Problem

Human body restoration (HBR) lacks a dedicated high-quality benchmark dataset: existing human-related datasets either cover only studio/fashion settings, focus on single-person images, or contain substantial low-quality samples without dedicated filtering. Additionally, one-step diffusion models adapted for general image restoration are not specifically optimized for HBR, as their prompt extraction mechanisms (e.g., tag-based DAPE) produce imprecise or misleading guidance for complex human images with intricate textures and multi-person interactions.

## Input / Output

The model takes a 512×512 low-quality RGB human image (degraded via Real-ESRGAN pipeline for training, or real-world degradation for testing) and produces a 512×512 high-quality restored image in a single forward pass. The conditioning signal is derived from a 384×384 resized version of the LQ image processed through the HFIE module, which outputs a 77×1024 embedding matching Stable Diffusion's text-embedding dimension. The PERSONA benchmark provides 109,053 training images, 4,216 validation images, and 3,000 real-world test images, averaging 3.42 individuals per image.

## Method

OSDHuman is a one-step diffusion model built on Stable Diffusion v2.1 with the diffusion timestep frozen at 999, performing a single U-Net denoising step between VAE encoding and decoding. The key architectural innovation is the High-Fidelity Image Embedder (HFIE), which replaces OSEDiff's tag-based DAPE prompt extractor: it uses RAM's Swin Transformer image encoder (discarding the tagging head) to produce 145 spatial embeddings, then applies multi-head attention with learnable query embeddings to compress them into the 77×1024 conditioning vector, preserving both local and global image information without the distortion introduced by discrete text tags. Training combines pixel-wise MSE, LPIPS perceptual loss, and a Variational Score Distillation (VSD) regularizer that aligns the generator's latent output distribution with natural image distributions via a pretrained and a LoRA-finetuned regularizer. The entire system is trained with LoRA (rank 4) on the VAE encoder, U-Net, and regularizer for 35K iterations, while the PERSONA dataset is constructed via an automated pipeline that crops human bounding boxes from ~4M images using YOLO11 detection, filters by Laplacian variance and IQA metrics (CLIPIQA, MANIQA, MUSIQ, NIQE), and retains the top third by normalized quality scores.

## Results

On the synthetic PERSONA-Val set, OSDHuman achieves the best DISTS (0.1414), LPIPS (0.2627), PSNR (22.41 dB), SSIM (0.6363), and FID (16.60) among all compared methods, outperforming the strongest one-step baseline OSEDiff* by 4.1% in DISTS and 4.7% in LPIPS. On the real-world PERSONA-Test set, OSDHuman attains the best NIQE (3.575), CLIPIQA (0.7155), and MUSIQ (73.77), surpassing the best multi-step method SeeSR by 1.0% in NIQE and 6.4% in CLIPIQA. The model also achieves the highest MUSIQ (76.13) on PERSONA-Val, exceeding the best multi-step method DiffBIR (76.48) by a narrow margin while delivering substantially better fidelity metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the four multi-step diffusion baselines (DiffBIR, SeeSR, PASD, ResShift), OSDHuman wins on all fidelity and perceptual metrics (DISTS, LPIPS, PSNR, SSIM) on PERSONA-Val and on NIQE, CLIPIQA, and MUSIQ on PERSONA-Test, while multi-step methods retain an edge in FID and CLIPIQA on Val due to their stronger generative capacity. Among one-step diffusion methods, OSDHuman outperforms OSEDiff* (the strongest one-step baseline after PERSONA retraining) by 4.1% in DISTS, 4.7% in LPIPS, and 0.18 dB in PSNR on Val, and by 10.6% in CLIPIQA and 1.0% in NIQE on Test. The only metric where OSDHuman trails a one-step competitor is MANIQA on PERSONA-Test (0.6977 vs. OSEDiff*'s 0.7052), a gap of less than 1%. Retraining ResShift and SinSR on PERSONA (ResShift*, SinSR*) narrows the gap with OSDHuman on some metrics but does not close it, confirming that the architectural contribution (HFIE + VSD) provides gains beyond dataset quality alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the one-step diffusion (OSD) framework established by OSEDiff (Wu et al., 2024), adopting its VAE-based latent diffusion architecture, VSD regularizer, and LoRA fine-tuning strategy, while replacing OSEDiff's tag-based prompt extractor (DAPE) with a high-fidelity image embedder (HFIE) that uses learnable query embeddings over RAM's Swin Transformer encoder. The base architecture is Stable Diffusion v2.1, and the degradation pipeline follows Real-ESRGAN. The key conceptual departure is the argument that tag-based semantic prompts are too coarse and potentially misleading for human body restoration, motivating a continuous embedding-based prompt that preserves both local and global image structure without discretization into text tags.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The PERSONA dataset (109K high-quality 512×512 human images with diverse activities and multi-person scenes) addresses a genuine gap in HBR benchmarking, as existing object-detection and fashion datasets either lack quality filtering or cover too narrow a range of human activities. The finding that DAPE-style tag prompting actually degrades performance below a null prompt for human images challenges the common assumption that richer semantic guidance always helps restoration. Open questions include whether the HQ-ACF pipeline generalizes to other domain-specific restoration tasks (e.g., face, vehicle), whether one-step inference remains sufficient under extreme degradation, and whether the 512×512 resolution constraint limits real-world applicability.
