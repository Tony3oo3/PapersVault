## Problem

Handling missing values at test time typically requires imputation or missingness indicators, both of which introduce bias, increase model complexity, and obscure interpretability. Existing native-missingness methods (e.g., XGBoost default paths, Bayesian marginalization) are limited to specialized architectures and do not generalize across model classes. The paper asks whether one can train standard model families—trees, linear models, ensembles—to simply avoid needing missing feature values at prediction time, without architectural changes.

## Input / Output

The method consumes tabular features (numerical or categorical) that may contain missing entries, along with a binary missingness mask indicating which features are absent. Missing values are imputed (zero or MICE) for training, but the learned model is regularized to minimize its dependence on those imputed values. Beyond the class prediction, the framework produces a missingness reliance score ρ̂ measuring the fraction of test instances for which the model's prediction path requires at least one missing feature, enabling practitioners to quantify and trade off interpretability against accuracy.

## Method

The MA framework adds a classifier-specific missingness-reliance penalty to the standard learning objective, controlled by a tradeoff parameter α. For decision trees (MA-DT), the greedy splitting criterion is augmented with a term penalizing splits on features that are missing for samples in the current node, exploiting the contextual nature of tree paths where only a subset of observations traverse a given split. For sparse linear models (MA-LASSO), the L1 penalty becomes feature-specific, with coefficients of frequently missing features receiving larger penalties (λ_j = λ + α·m̄_j), effectively performing missingness-aware variable selection. For ensembles, MA-RF applies the tree penalty independently per tree, while MA-GBT introduces a dynamic weight σ_{i,j} that is updated after each boosting round: if a prior tree already relied on a missing feature for a given sample, subsequent trees can reuse it without incurring additional penalty, preventing redundant reliance while still minimizing prediction error.

## Results

Across six real-world datasets, MA models dramatically reduce missingness reliance (ρ̂) while maintaining competitive AUROC. MA-LASSO, MA-DT, and MA-RF achieve average ρ̂ reductions of 66.0, 4.4, and 70.2 percentage points over their unregularized counterparts (LR, DT, RF), respectively, with 95% confidence intervals for AUROC overlapping in nearly all cases. On NHANES, MA-DT achieves ρ̂ = 0.0% (vs. 100% for LR) at AUROC 82.4 (vs. 85.1 for LR), and on ADNI, MA-RF reaches ρ̂ = 3.2% (vs. 66.1% for RF) at AUROC 78.0 (vs. 75.0 for RF). MA-GBT reduces missingness reliance by 64.4 on average compared to XGBoost while achieving slightly lower AUROC.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against XGBoost, MA-GBT achieves slightly lower AUROC (e.g., 78.5 vs. 79.0 on ADNI, 83.2 vs. 84.8 on NHANES) but reduces missingness reliance by 64.4 percentage points on average (e.g., ρ̂ = 1.6 vs. 60.9 on ADNI, 0.3 vs. 95.7 on NHANES). NeuMiss achieves the highest AUROC on several datasets (e.g., 85.5 on NHANES) but exhibits the highest missingness reliance (ρ̂ = 100% on NHANES, 84.1% on LIFE) due to dense multiplications of all input features, making it unsuitable when interpretability is needed. MINTY is the closest competitor in terms of missingness avoidance, with AUROC and ρ̂ comparable to MA-LASSO in most cases (e.g., both achieve ρ̂ ≈ 0.3–1.3 on ADNI and NHANES), but MINTY's training time ranges from 18 to 292 seconds versus under 1 second for MA-LASSO. M-GAM (both IND and INT variants) shows moderate missingness reliance (ρ̂ = 26–52% on LIFE, 26–28% on NHANES), indicating that its use of missingness indicators and interactions paradoxically increases reliance on missing values compared to MA models. Against their unregularized counterparts, MA models consistently win on ρ̂: MA-DT reduces ρ̂ from 11.7% to 0.1% on ADNI and from 18.3% to 12.3% on LIFE, while MA-RF reduces ρ̂ from 66.1% to 3.2% on ADNI and from 100% to 0.2% on NHANES, all with overlapping AUROC confidence intervals.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the missingness reliance concept introduced by Stempfle & Johansson (2024) in MINTY (limited to generalized linear rule models) into a general regularization framework applicable across decision trees, sparse linear models, and tree ensembles. For linear models, it modifies the LASSO objective (Tibshirani, 1996) with feature-specific penalties proportional to missingness frequency; for trees, it augments greedy splitting criteria (C4.5/ID3) with a missingness-aware penalty term. The approach departs from impute-then-regress pipelines, missingness indicator methods, and native missing-value architectures (XGBoost, NeuMiss, Bayesian networks) by instead training standard model classes to contextually avoid using missing features, leveraging the Rashomon set of near-optimal models to select those with lower missingness reliance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework gives practitioners a drop-in regularization parameter α to trade off accuracy against missingness reliance across diverse interpretable model classes, which is particularly valuable in safety-critical domains like healthcare where imputation bias and indicator-induced complexity undermine trust. The ODDC rules characterization (Proposition 1, Corollary 1) provides a clean theoretical condition under which zero reliance and optimal accuracy are simultaneously achievable, but the approach has clear limitations under distribution shift, MNAR missingness, and informative missingness where missingness indicators may outperform. Open questions include extending the framework to other model classes (neural networks, GAMs), developing principled α-selection strategies beyond the 95%-AUROC heuristic, and understanding robustness when test-time missingness patterns diverge from training.
