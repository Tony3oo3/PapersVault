## Problem

Sampling from posterior distributions p(x|y) ∝ p_θ(x)·r(x,y) under pretrained generative priors is generally intractable: MCMC is slow for multimodal posteriors, gradient-based fine-tuning (e.g., adjoint matching) is restricted to specific model families and requires differentiable constraints, and GANs lack a computable density entirely. The paper identifies that the posterior pulled back into the noise space of the generative model is typically smoother and lower-dimensional than the data-space posterior, making it a more tractable target for amortized inference.

## Input / Output

The method takes a frozen pretrained generative prior (SN-GAN, I-CFM, StyleGAN3, NVAE, Stable Diffusion 3, or FoldFlow 2) and a constraint function r(x,y) parameterized by an auxiliary variable y (a CIFAR-10 class label, a text prompt scored by ImageReward, or a protein diversity reward). The outsourced diffusion sampler operates on the prior's noise space, whose dimensionality ranges from 128 (SN-GAN) to 16×64×64 (SD3 latent CNF) or 7×64 (protein SE(3) coordinates), and produces samples x = f_θ(z) from the data-space posterior. The sampler is amortized over y, so a single trained model can condition on new y values at inference time.

## Method

The core contribution is to train a diffusion model as an amortized sampler of the noise-space posterior R(z|y) = p(z)·r(f(z),y), where f is the prior's deterministic generation function treated as a black box. A measure-theoretic result (Prop. 3.1) guarantees that transforming samples from this noise-space posterior by f yields the correct data-space posterior, without requiring f to be invertible or differentiable. Training uses the Trajectory Balance objective from GFlowNets, which treats R(z|y) as a black-box reward and requires no gradient of either the constraint or the prior, making the method applicable to GANs, VAEs, CNFs, and Riemannian flows uniformly. Off-policy training with a replay buffer and temperature annealing enables mode discovery in the noise space, and the trained sampler can be distilled into a one-step generator for fast inference.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 class-conditional sampling, outsourced diffusion achieves FID 68.12 with the SN-GAN prior (vs. 97.14 for the prior and 75.33 for latent HMC) and FID 34.28 with the I-CFM prior (vs. 84.79 prior, 46.69 HMC, and 19.45 adjoint matching). On FFHQ text-conditioned face generation, outsourced diffusion substantially outperforms HMC in ImageReward score (0.98 vs. −1.2 for NVAE; 1.23 vs. −0.62 for StyleGAN3). For SD3 text-to-image RLHF, outsourced diffusion achieves an average ImageReward of 1.27, surpassing both the prior (0.79) and tuned classifier-free guidance (0.84). On protein structure diversity, outsourced diffusion reaches log-reward 0.422 versus −0.640 for random-walk MCMC and −1.325 for the prior.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against latent HMC, outsourced diffusion consistently wins: it achieves lower FID on both CIFAR-10 priors (68.12 vs. 75.33 for SN-GAN; 34.28 vs. 46.69 for I-CFM) and substantially higher ImageReward on FFHQ (0.98 vs. −1.2 for NVAE; 1.23 vs. −0.62 for StyleGAN3) and protein structure (0.422 vs. −0.640). Against adjoint matching, the proposed method loses on I-CFM FID (34.28 vs. 19.45) but is more general—it does not require gradient access to the constraint or the prior, and is ~2× faster to train (5 h vs. 12 h on A100). Against classifier-free guidance on SD3 RLHF, outsourced diffusion improves ImageReward from 0.84 to 1.27 while maintaining comparable CLIP diversity. Against DPS (Chung et al., 2023) on I-CFM, outsourced diffusion achieves much lower FID (34.28 vs. 84.96) though DPS attains higher classifier log-likelihood (−2.22 vs. −3.35). Against RTB (Venkatraman et al., 2024), outsourced diffusion wins on both reward (−3.35 vs. −4.20) and FID (34.28 vs. 90.77). Against DDPO (Fan et al., 2023) on SD 1.5 RLHF, DDPO achieves slightly higher reward (1.37 vs. 1.26) but outsourced diffusion preserves more diversity (0.14 vs. 0.09 CLIP cosine distance).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies posterior inference under diverse generative model priors (GANs, VAEs, NFs, CNFs, diffusion models) through the 'noise outsourcing' lens, building on the noise outsourcing lemma (Austin, 2015) and the trajectory balance objective from GFlowNets (Malkin et al., 2022). It departs from adjoint matching (Domingo-Enrich et al., 2024), which requires gradient access and is restricted to specific flow types with Gaussian sources, and from direct data-space diffusion fine-tuning (RTB; Venkatraman et al., 2024), by instead training diffusion samplers in the lower-dimensional, smoother noise space. The method also connects to the broader diffusion sampler literature (Zhang & Chen, 2022; Vargas et al., 2023; Sendera et al., 2024) by applying off-policy training with replay buffers to the outsourced posterior target.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a single, model-agnostic recipe for posterior inference that works where gradient-based methods (adjoint matching, HMC) fundamentally cannot apply—e.g., Riemannian CNFs for protein structure, non-invertible GAN generators, and high-dimensional hierarchical VAEs. The amortized nature means one-time training cost followed by cheap inference, and distillation to one-step samplers eliminates even the multi-step overhead. Open questions include extending to discrete noise spaces, general probabilistic programs with control flow, and understanding when the noise-space posterior is genuinely smoother versus merely lower-dimensional. The method's reliance on a trained diffusion model (rather than being training-free) remains a practical consideration compared to guidance-based approaches.
