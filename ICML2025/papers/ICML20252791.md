## Problem

Existing diffusion-model-based generative image steganography schemes either require the receiver to reproduce the exact generation seed and prompt (constraining the sender's ability to produce diverse, arbitrary images) or rely on image-translation approaches that limit output diversity. These methods also face practical challenges in extraction accuracy, controllability, and the need for additional training or coordination between sender and receiver.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252791/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The sender provides a uniformly random binary message of length l, a pre-shared Cardan grille (a list of l unique indices into the 1×c×h×w noise tensor, e.g., 1×4×64×64 for 512×512 Stable Diffusion), and an arbitrary text prompt. The output is a stego image indistinguishable from a normally generated one; the receiver recovers the binary message using only the Cardan grille and the stego image, without needing the generation seed or prompt. The sender can generate unlimited diverse candidate images by varying prompts and seeds while keeping the message and grille fixed, selecting the best-quality image for transmission.

## Method

MDDM is a training-free framework that exploits DDIM's deterministic sampling (σ_t=0) to create a one-to-one mapping between initial Gaussian noise and the generated image, enabling exact recovery of the noise via diffusion inversion (EDICT for conditional, DDIM inversion for unconditional). The encoding maps each message bit to a truncated standard normal sample at Cardan grille positions: bit 0 draws from (−∞, −k] and bit 1 from (k, +∞), while non-message positions are filled from (−k, k], preserving the overall N(0,I) distribution for provable security. The constructed noise is fed directly into standard DDIM sampling with an arbitrary prompt to produce the stego image. Extraction inverts the stego image to recover the noise, then decodes each bit by checking the sign at the corresponding Cardan grille index. The truncation threshold k is computed from the payload-to-capacity ratio via the inverse CDF of the standard normal, balancing extraction robustness (larger |k| gives more margin) against embedding capacity.

## Results

On 256×256 images with 4096-bit payloads, MDDM-LU achieves extraction accuracies of 0.9950 (FFHQ) and 0.9868 (LSUN-Bedroom), matching or exceeding LDStega (0.9865, 0.9850) and substantially outperforming Pulsar (0.9700, 0.9300) and StegaDDPM (0.9345, 0.9019). Under robustness attacks on 512×512 images, MDDM-LC maintains 1.00 accuracy on PNG, JPEG-95, Gaussian Blur, Drop, and Brightness, with only a 0.01 drop under Resize(0.5) and Rotation(0.3), matching the state-of-the-art Gaussian Shading (GS) method. In terms of diversity, MDDM can generate an unlimited number of distinct stego images per shared key, whereas StegaDDPM, Pulsar, and LDStega each produce only one image per seed.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest diffusion-based GIS baselines, MDDM-LU outperforms LDStega [29] by ~0.9% on FFHQ (0.9950 vs. 0.9865) and ties on Bedroom (0.9868 vs. 0.9850), while MDDM-PU3 matches LDStega on FFHQ (0.9865) and surpasses it on Cat (0.9989 vs. 0.9848). Both MDDM variants substantially outperform Pulsar [16] (by 1.5–6.5% across datasets) and StegaDDPM [28] (by 5–9%). In the robustness comparison (Table 1), MDDM-LC ties Gaussian Shading [51] and LaWa [30] at 1.00 accuracy on most attacks, with a marginal 0.01 drop under Resize and Rotation where GS and LaWa also show minor degradation. Compared to traditional embedding methods (DwtDct [1], DwtDctSvd [27], RivaGAN [58]), MDDM achieves equal or higher accuracy at 8–8× the payload capacity (48–256 bits vs. 32 bits). On diversity (Table 5), MDDM uniquely supports unlimited image generation per key, whereas StegaDDPM [28], Pulsar [16], and LDStega [29] are limited to one image per seed, and CRoSS [56] offers only limited content variation.

## Relation to Prior Work

MDDM builds on the deterministic sampling and inversion properties of DDIM to construct a message-driven steganography pipeline, combining them with the classical Cardan grille as a shared key. It departs from prior diffusion-based GIS methods (Pulsar, StegaDDPM, LDStega) that require the receiver to reproduce the full generation with the same seed and prompt, by encoding the message into the initial noise at Cardan grille positions so that extraction only needs the grille. It also extends earlier Cardan-grille steganography work (which relied on GAN-based image restoration) to the diffusion-model setting, and contrasts with image-translation approaches (CRoSS, DiffStega) that constrain output diversity by keeping stego images close to a secret image.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free, prompt-agnostic extraction (receiver needs only the Cardan grille, not the seed or prompt) makes MDDM notably more practical for asynchronous or broadcast steganographic communication than prior diffusion-based GIS schemes. The provable-security argument—that the encoded noise remains statistically indistinguishable from standard Gaussian noise—is appealing but the proof in Appendix B is brief and relies on the assumption that truncation preserves distributional indistinguishability, leaving room for scrutiny. Open questions include whether error-correcting codes can further improve robustness under stronger attacks, how the method performs on newer diffusion architectures (e.g., DiT-based models), and whether the Cardan grille's ordering security holds under more sophisticated statistical attacks than the two simple strategies tested.
