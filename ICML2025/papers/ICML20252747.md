## Problem

TRPO guarantees monotonic policy improvement through trust region constraints but requires expensive second-order optimization, while PPO replaces this with a simple ratio-clipping heuristic that is first-order efficient yet fails to actually bound probability ratios, especially as networks deepen. The paper asks whether a simple first-order objective can enforce trust region constraints as rigorously as TRPO while retaining PPO's implementation simplicity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252747/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The agent receives environment observations—either raw RGB frames (84×84×4) from Atari 2600 or low-dimensional state vectors from MuJoCo—and outputs an action sampled from a learned policy (discrete categorical for Atari, multivariate Gaussian for MuJoCo). A separate value network estimates state values for GAE-based advantage computation. The method is fully online and model-free: data is collected with the current policy, advantages are estimated, and the policy is updated over multiple epochs before new data is gathered.

## Method

SPO replaces PPO's clipped surrogate objective min[r_t·Â_t, clip(r_t,1−ε,1+ε)·Â_t] with a quadratic-penalty objective r_t·Â_t − |Â_t|/(2ε)·(r_t−1)², which is the only code change relative to a standard PPO implementation. This objective is derived by optimizing a TV-divergence-constrained lower bound on policy improvement, and the paper proves it is 'ε-aligned': differentiable and convex in the probability ratio r, with its maximum exactly at r = 1 + sign(Â)·ε. Unlike PPO's clipping, which zeroes gradients for out-of-bounds data points and allows the policy to drift uncontrollably, SPO's quadratic penalty ensures every data point always produces a non-zero corrective gradient pointing back toward the trust region boundary. Theoretically, the paper shows via Pinsker's inequality that the TV-divergence solution space strictly contains the KL-divergence space, so optimizing within it yields a tighter (and thus better) performance improvement lower bound.

## Results

On MuJoCo-v4 (6 environments, 10 seeds), SPO achieves the best performance across nearly all aggregate statistical metrics (median, IQM, mean, and optimality gap) compared to PPO-Clip, PPO-Penalty, SPU, PPO-RB, TR-PPO, TR-PPO-RB, and RPO. The most striking result is in the 7-layer policy network setting: PPO's ratio deviation explodes to values as high as 4018 (HumanoidStandup-v4) and 2412 (Humanoid-v4), while SPO maintains ratio deviations below 0.20 across all environments. In the 3-layer setting, SPO matches or exceeds PPO's average return in 4 out of 6 environments (e.g., Hopper: 1480.3 vs. 1119.4; Humanoid: 2870.0 vs. 795.1) while keeping ratio deviations 2–5× lower.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SPO is compared against seven baselines on MuJoCo-v4: PPO-Clip, PPO-Penalty, SPU, PPO-RB, TR-PPO, TR-PPO-RB, and RPO. In the aggregate metrics (Figure 4), SPO achieves the best median, IQM, and mean scores while also having the lowest optimality gap, outperforming all seven baselines. The most dramatic separation appears in the 7-layer network experiments: PPO-Clip's ratio deviation reaches 4018 (HumanoidStandup) and 2412 (Humanoid), causing performance collapse (e.g., Humanoid return drops from 795 to 614), whereas SPO keeps ratio deviation under 0.20 and actually improves returns (Humanoid: 2870→4770). Against PPO-RB and TR-PPO variants (Wang et al., 2020), which were specifically designed to fix PPO's ratio constraint, SPO still achieves lower ratio deviations and higher returns without requiring additional code-level tuning. On Atari 2600 with a ResNet-18 encoder, SPO significantly outperforms PPO, while PPO struggles to maintain ratio control even with a smaller ε=0.1.

## Relation to Prior Work

SPO sits at the intersection of TRPO's trust-region theory and PPO's first-order practicality. It departs from PPO's clipped surrogate objective by identifying that ratio clipping zeros gradients for out-of-bounds data points, undermining the very trust-region constraint it was designed to enforce (a failure formalized by Wang et al., 2020). The theoretical grounding shifts from KL divergence (TRPO) to Total Variation divergence (Achiam et al., 2017), which yields a larger feasible solution space and a tighter performance-improvement lower bound. The resulting algorithm is a one-line modification to PPO's policy loss, replacing the min-clip with a quadratic penalty term that keeps all data points contributing corrective gradients.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PPO is the de facto standard for policy-gradient RL across robotics, games, and LLM fine-tuning, yet its core ratio-clipping mechanism has been shown to fail at actually bounding probability ratios. SPO's ability to train 7-layer and ResNet-18 policy networks where PPO catastrophically collapses (ratio deviations exceeding 3000×) addresses a practical bottleneck that has forced practitioners to rely on fragile code-level tuning. Open questions include whether the TV-divergence framework extends to off-policy or multi-agent settings, and whether the quadratic penalty is optimal among all ε-aligned objectives. The paper's emphasis on simplicity (no adaptive learning rates, no early stopping) makes it a strong candidate for becoming a default replacement in PPO-based pipelines.
