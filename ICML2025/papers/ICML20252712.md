## Problem

While diffusion models are known to memorize training data, prior extraction methods target general generative capabilities and overlook the specific fine-tuning data embedded in personalized checkpoints shared on platforms like Civitai and HuggingFace. This paper asks whether the fine-tuning dataset can be directly recovered from a publicly released fine-tuned checkpoint, which would confirm data leakage and provide tangible evidence of copyright infringement for affected artists.

## Input / Output

The method requires both the fine-tuned checkpoint θ' and the corresponding pretrained checkpoint θ (e.g., SD V1.4, SDXL, AltDiffusion, or FLUX), plus optionally the training caption c. It generates N images (default 50×N₀, where N₀ is the number of training images) and outputs exactly N₀ extracted images via a graph-clustering step. Similarity between extracted and training images is quantified using SSCD copy-detection scores, with extraction deemed successful above thresholds of 0.6 (loose) or 0.7 (strict).

## Method

FineXtract approximates the fine-tuned model's learned distribution as a geometric interpolation p_θ'(x) ∝ p_θ^(1−λ)(x)·q^λ(x) between the pretrained distribution and the fine-tuning data distribution, where λ scales with training iterations. Taking the score (log-gradient) of this approximation yields a 'model guidance' signal: extrapolating from the pretrained denoiser ε_θ to the fine-tuned denoiser ε_θ' with scale w=1/λ steers the denoising process toward high-density regions of the fine-tuning data. For conditional models with caption c, the guidance extrapolates from the pretrained *unconditional* denoiser to the fine-tuned *conditional* denoiser, augmented by a correction term k·ε_θ that compensates for the mismatch between model guidance and classifier-free guidance. Generated images are then clustered via a graph-based clique-finding algorithm (using SSCD similarity with an adaptively increasing threshold) to select the central image per clique as the final extracted training image.

## Results

On the WikiArt style-driven dataset with DreamBooth fine-tuning, FineXtract achieves AS=0.449 and A-ESR₀.₆=0.22, compared to 0.396/0.11 for CFG+Clustering and 0.317/0.01 for Direct Text2img+Clustering. On the DreamBooth object-driven dataset with DreamBooth fine-tuning, FineXtract reaches AS=0.557 and A-ESR₀.₇=0.25, roughly doubling the A-ESR over CFG (0.15) and Direct (0.03). Across all settings, FineXtract improves AS by approximately 0.02–0.05 and doubles A-ESR in most cases. On real-world HuggingFace checkpoints, FineXtract achieves AS=0.533 (DreamBooth) and AS=0.371 (LoRA), again outperforming both baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FineXtract consistently outperforms both baselines across all four dataset/fine-tuning combinations. Against Direct Text2img+Clustering (Carlini et al., 2023), FineXtract improves AS by 0.06–0.11 and A-ESR₀.₆ by up to 0.43 (DreamBooth dataset, DreamBooth fine-tuning: 0.45 vs. 0.11). Against CFG+Clustering (Somepalli et al., 2023), the AS gain is smaller (0.02–0.09) but A-ESR roughly doubles in most settings (e.g., 0.25 vs. 0.15 on DreamBooth/DreamBooth; 0.18 vs. 0.05 on DreamBooth/LoRA). The largest absolute gap appears on the object-driven DreamBooth dataset with full DreamBooth fine-tuning, where the model has memorized more specific details. Under LoRA fine-tuning, both methods show lower absolute performance, but FineXtract still maintains a clear advantage (e.g., A-ESR₀.₆ of 0.18 vs. 0.05 on DreamBooth/LoRA). On real-world HuggingFace checkpoints (Table 5), FineXtract again leads both baselines, increasing AS by at least 0.03 and doubling A-ESR in most cases.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the training-data extraction line of work initiated by Carlini et al. (2023) and Somepalli et al. (2023a,b), which focused on identifying memorized samples from large pretrained diffusion models via graph-based clustering. The key departure is a parametric approximation of the fine-tuned model's learned distribution as an interpolation between the pretrained distribution and the fine-tuning data distribution (Eq. 3), enabling a 'model guidance' mechanism that extrapolates between the pretrained and fine-tuned denoisers—conceptually analogous to classifier-free guidance (Ho & Salimans, 2022) but operating across two distinct model checkpoints rather than conditional/unconditional branches of one model. The score-to-denoiser conversion follows Gandikota et al. (2023), and the clustering pipeline adapts Carlini et al.'s graph approach with a variable-threshold clique-finding strategy tailored to the known number of training images.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical stakes are significant: tens of thousands of fine-tuned checkpoints on Civitai and HuggingFace may silently leak training data, and the extracted images could serve as forensic evidence of copyright infringement. However, the method requires white-box access to both the pretrained and fine-tuned weights, and the ~20% extraction rate (A-ESR at τ=0.6) means most training images remain unrecovered. The paper identifies a critical open trade-off: defenses like Cutout and RandAugment partially resist extraction but render generated images largely unusable, and no existing method fine-tunes on such transformed data while preserving both defensive effects and generation quality. Whether the model-guidance framework generalizes to black-box API-only access or to multi-subject fine-tuning with overlapping concepts remains unexplored.
