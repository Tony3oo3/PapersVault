## Problem

Existing low-rank decomposition methods for communication-efficient federated learning (e.g., FedHM, FedLMT, FedPara) suffer from three fundamental issues: they decompose entire model parameters rather than just updates (causing unnecessary information loss), standard low-rank factorization caps the recovered matrix rank well below full rank, and directly aggregating sub-matrices introduces implicit second-order errors that degrade convergence. This paper identifies and addresses all three gaps simultaneously.

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard image classification datasets (FMNIST 28×28, SVHN 32×32, CIFAR-10/100 32×32, TinyImageNet 64×64) partitioned across 100 clients under Non-IID or IID distributions. Rather than transmitting full model weights, only low-rank sub-matrices representing model updates are communicated bidirectionally at a compression ratio of 1/32 (or 1/16 for ResNet-18 experiments). The output is a globally trained CNN (4-conv-layer for smaller datasets, 8-conv-layer or ResNet-18 for larger ones) that predicts class labels.

## Method

FedMUD introduces three complementary techniques. First, Model Update Decomposition (MUD) freezes the base model parameters and trains only low-rank sub-matrices U and V as additive updates (W = W_frozen + U·Vᵀ), with periodic reinitialization every s rounds (default s=1) to accumulate rank over time. Second, Block-wise Kronecker Decomposition (BKD) partitions each matrix into k² blocks and applies Kronecker-product factorization per block, raising the rank upper bound from r to min{m,n} while offering dynamic compression via k. Third, Aggregation-Aware Decomposition (AAD) decouples the product into W = U·Ṽᵀ + Û·Vᵀ with fixed Û, Ṽ, ensuring that aggregating-then-recovering equals recovering-then-aggregating and eliminating the implicit second-order bias. A convergence theorem proves FedMUD converges at O(1/√T) with a tighter constant than FedLMT, and experiments on five datasets show up to 12% accuracy improvement over baselines at 32× compression.

## Results

On the four main datasets (FMNIST, SVHN, CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100) under Non-IID-1 at 1/32 compression ratio, the full method FedMUD+BKD+AAD achieves 89.0%, 86.6%, 75.9%, and 41.2% test accuracy, respectively. This represents up to a 12.8% absolute improvement over the closest low-rank decomposition baseline (FedLMT: 28.4% on CIFAR-100) and outperforms the strongest gradient-compression baseline FedBAT (37.4% on CIFAR-100) by 3.8 points. Under the more heterogeneous Non-IID-2 setting, the method maintains its lead, e.g., 73.9% on CIFAR-10 vs. 72.3% for FedBAT. With 8× compression (relaxed from 32×), FedMUD+BKD+AAD nearly matches uncompressed FedAvg accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedMUD+BKD+AAD outperforms all six baselines across all four datasets and both Non-IID settings. Against the uncompressed FedAvg, the method closes most of the gap (e.g., 75.9% vs. 81.0% on CIFAR-10 Non-IID-1) while compressing communication by 32×. Among low-rank decomposition methods, FedMUD+BKD+AAD surpasses FedHM (66.5%), FedLMT (64.7%), and FedPara (70.6%) by 9.4, 11.2, and 5.3 points respectively on CIFAR-10 Non-IID-1. The strongest baseline, FedBAT (a binarization-based gradient compressor), is outperformed by 0.9 points on CIFAR-10 Non-IID-1 (75.9% vs. 75.0%) and by 3.8 points on CIFAR-100 Non-IID-1 (41.2% vs. 37.4%). EF21-P, an error-feedback compression method, performs worst among all baselines (47.2% on CIFAR-10), which the authors attribute to its extremely high compression rate updating only 3% of parameters per round. On the harder CIFAR-100 dataset, the advantage is most pronounced: FedMUD+BKD+AAD achieves 41.2% vs. 28.4% for FedLMT and 29.3% for FedPara, a gap of over 12 points.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of low-rank decomposition methods for communication-efficient federated learning (FedHM, FedLMT, FedPara) by identifying and addressing three specific weaknesses: decomposing full model parameters instead of updates, the limited rank ceiling of standard low-rank factorization, and an implicit second-order aggregation bias overlooked by prior work. Conceptually, MUD aligns with the gradient-compression philosophy (compressing updates rather than weights), BKD draws on Kronecker-product structures from parameter-efficient fine-tuning (Krona, BoRA), and AAD corrects a subtle algebraic error in how FedLMT-style sub-matrix aggregation interacts with the bilinear recovery operation. The theoretical analysis builds directly on FedLMT's convergence framework, showing that the reset-interval mechanism in MUD yields a tighter convergence bound.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical recipe (MUD+BKD+AAD) that narrows the accuracy gap between compressed and full-precision federated training, with the AAD correction being a subtle but broadly applicable fix for any bilinear parameterization requiring aggregation. However, at 32× compression the method still trails FedAvg by a meaningful margin, and the approach has only been validated on small CNNs (4–8 conv layers) and ResNet-18, leaving open whether the benefits persist for deeper or transformer-based architectures. The sensitivity to initialization values and the need for a reset interval of 1 (i.e., reinitializing every round) add practical complexity that may limit adoption. Future work on dynamic initialization and extension to LLM fine-tuning in federated settings would strengthen the contribution.
