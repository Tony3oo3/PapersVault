## Problem

Learning the spectral decomposition of the infinitesimal generator (IG) of continuous-time Markov processes is hindered by the IG's unboundedness, which breaks standard operator regression, and by the sensitivity of transfer operator (TO) methods to small time-lags, where their spectral gap collapses and recovery guarantees become vacuous. Existing IG learning approaches either restrict to self-adjoint generators with Dirichlet forms, require prior physical knowledge, or lack formal statistical guarantees, leaving a gap for a general, data-driven method with sharp bounds applicable to the broader class of sectorial generators.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single stationary trajectory (x_0, …, x_{n-1}) ∈ ℝ^{d×n} sampled at frequency 1/Δt, a kernel k (or finite feature dictionary z: X → ℝ^N), and hyperparameters (shift μ > 0, regularization γ, rank r). It outputs the leading r eigentriples (λ̂_i, ĝ_i, ĥ_i) of the IG, where eigenvalues are recovered as λ̂_i = μ − 1/ν̂_i from the resolvent's eigenvalues, along with the full resolvent operator estimate. The dual formulation operates purely through kernel Gram matrices of size n×n, while the primal formulation works with N×N matrices in a finite feature basis; both exploit a Toeplitz structure to avoid explicit construction of the Bochner integral target.

## Method

The core insight is to bypass the unbounded IG by estimating its bounded resolvent R_μ = ∫₀^∞ A_t e^{−μt} dt via a trapezoidal quadrature that aggregates transfer operators at multiple time-lags: R̃_m = Σ_j m_j A_{t_j}. This transforms the learning problem into a standard reduced rank regression (RRR) in RKHS where the target is a finite weighted sum of feature maps, and the aggregated cross-covariance H̃_m = Σ_j m_j T_{t_j} is computed as a single product of a Toeplitz matrix M with the kernel Gram matrix K, yielding O((r∨d)·n²) complexity in the dual case. Because the resolvent's spectral gap is independent of the time-lag Δt (unlike TO methods where gap_i(A_{Δt}) → 0 as Δt → 0), the estimator remains accurate even at arbitrarily small sampling intervals. The statistical analysis decomposes the operator norm error into regularization bias, rank-reduction bias, integration bias (bounded by Δt via Crouzeix's theorem on the numerical range), and estimator variance (controlled via geometric ergodicity mixing), yielding a learning rate of n^{−α/(2(α+β))} for the operator norm and n^{−1/3} for eigenvalues under optimal hyperparameter tuning.

## Results

The paper establishes an operator norm error bound of E(Ĝ) ≲ 1/(μ−λ_{r+1}) + Δt + ln^{3/2}(n/δ)/(μ√(nΔt|λ₂|)), yielding a learning rate of n^{−1/2}ln^{3/2}(n) for the resolvent and n^{−1/3}ln^{1/2}(n²/δ) for eigenvalue estimation. Computationally, the dual LaRRR algorithm achieves O((r∨d)n²) complexity, linear in state dimension d, compared to O(rn²d²) for the prior Dirichlet-form method. Empirically, on a 1D Langevin triple-well process, LaRRR maintains stable eigenvalue error as Δt→0 while TO RRR error explodes, and on alanine dipeptide (d=45) it recovers the two metastable states consistent with expert knowledge.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against TO RRR (Kostic et al., 2022), LaRRR clearly wins on robustness to small time-lags: in the 1D Langevin triple-well experiment (Fig. 2), TO RRR's relative error for λ₂ explodes as Δt decreases, while LaRRR's error remains stable, confirming the theoretical prediction that the resolvent spectral gap is independent of Δt. Against the physics-informed Dirichlet-form method (Kostic et al., 2024a), LaRRR is strictly more general (sectorial vs. self-adjoint IGs, no Dirichlet form needed) and reduces complexity from quadratic to linear in d, but trades off a slightly higher variance rate (n^{−α/(3α+2β)} vs. n^{−α/(2(α+β))}) for a lower bias term (σ_{r+1}/σ_r vs. √(σ_{r+1}/σ_r)). Against Cabannes & Bach (2024) and Hou et al. (2023), LaRRR applies to a broader class of processes, uses trajectory data rather than iid samples, avoids spurious eigenvalues, and achieves better or comparable convergence rates without requiring prior knowledge of the drift/diffusion coefficients.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Reduced Rank Regression (RRR) framework for operator learning in RKHS (Kostic et al., 2022) and the randomized RRR solver (Turri et al., 2023), but departs fundamentally from the Transfer Operator (TO) paradigm by learning the infinitesimal generator's resolvent via its Laplace transform representation. It generalizes the physics-informed kernel regression of Kostic et al. (2024a), which was restricted to self-adjoint generators admitting a Dirichlet form, to the broader class of sectorial generators (including non-time-reversible processes like underdamped Langevin and advection-diffusion). The key conceptual shift is replacing the unbounded IG estimation problem with a bounded resolvent regression, where the Laplace transform naturally aggregates multiple TOs at different time-lags through a single Toeplitz-weighted matrix product.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work resolves a fundamental practical limitation: TO-based spectral methods lose all recovery guarantees as the sampling time-lag decreases, which is precisely the regime needed to capture fast dynamics in molecular simulations and enhanced sampling. The linear (rather than quadratic) scaling in state dimension makes the method viable for high-dimensional systems like the 45-dimensional alanine dipeptide, where prior kernel-based IG methods become intractable. Key limitations include the assumption of uniform sampling of the full state from a single stationary trajectory; extending to non-uniformly sampled single trajectories and partially observed systems remains open. The Crouzeix-bound-based control of the Bochner integral approximation error is a technical contribution of independent interest for numerical analysis of operator semigroups.
