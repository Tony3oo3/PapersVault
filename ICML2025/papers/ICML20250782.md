## Problem

Standard adversarial training updates all model parameters, which leads to robust overfitting—degraded clean accuracy and diminished adversarial resilience over extended training. While prior work shows that not all layers equally learn non-robust features, effective methods for identifying which layers are robustness-critical and designing a targeted optimization objective to reduce their vulnerability remain lacking.

## Input / Output

CLAT operates on standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 224×224 for ImageNet/Imagenette) and outputs class labels, but its distinguishing input is a single untargeted PGD perturbation δ (ε=0.03, ℓ∞) computed once per batch and shared across all layers to estimate per-layer feature weakness. The method is applied as a fine-tuning stage after 50–70 epochs of pretraining (clean or adversarial), updating only ~5% of total parameters in the selected critical layers while freezing the remainder.

## Method

CLAT introduces a criticality index C_{f_i} = W_ε(F_i)/W_ε(F_{i-1}) that quantifies each layer's marginal contribution to cumulative feature weakness (the worst-case ℓ2 feature shift under an ε-bounded perturbation), enabling identification of layers that predominantly learned non-robust features. A dynamic selection mechanism re-evaluates top-k critical layers every 10 epochs, adapting to shifts in layer criticality as fine-tuning progresses. The fine-tuning objective minimizes the sum of feature-weakness terms across selected critical layers (Equation 5) plus a cross-entropy term, optimizing only the parameters of those layers via a min-max formulation with PGD inner maximization. This selective approach updates fewer than 5% of trainable parameters, avoiding the overfitting that plagues full-model adversarial training while improving both clean and adversarial accuracy by over 2% across diverse architectures and baselines.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, CLAT consistently improves both clean and adversarial accuracy over PGD-AT across all tested architectures: e.g., WRN34-10 goes from 87.41%/55.40% to 88.97%/57.11%, and RN50 from 81.38%/56.35% to 83.78%/59.54% (clean/PGD-20 adv). On CIFAR-100, gains are similarly consistent (e.g., RN50: 58.16%/33.01% → 61.88%/36.23%). On ImageNet, PGD-AT+CLAT improves adversarial accuracy by ~2–3.7% (e.g., RN50: 33.18% → 36.91%). CLAT achieves these gains while fine-tuning fewer than 5% of trainable parameters, with a negligible 0.4% time overhead for layer re-selection.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CLAT outperforms PGD-AT (Madry et al.) by 1.5–3.2% on adversarial accuracy and 1.4–2.4% on clean accuracy across all six architectures on CIFAR-10, with similar gains on CIFAR-100 and ImageNet. Against TRADES (Zhang et al.), CLAT yields larger adversarial gains (e.g., WRN34-10: 57.89% vs 56.61% PGD-10) while also improving clean accuracy. Compared to RiFT (Zhu et al.), CLAT achieves higher adversarial accuracy on WRN34-10 (57.11% vs 55.41%) and RN18 (55.37% vs 53.65%), and substantially higher clean accuracy on CIFAR-100 (62.38% vs 62.35% for WRN34-10; 59.22% vs 58.74% for RN18). CLAT dramatically outperforms LoRA (Aleem et al.) on clean accuracy (88.97% vs 73.36% for WRN34-10) while matching or exceeding its adversarial numbers. Against Fast-AT (Wong et al.), CLAT improves adversarial accuracy by 6.6% on PreAct RN18 (52.13% vs 45.55%). In the AutoAttack evaluation (Table 3), CLAT also surpasses Twins (Liu et al.) and AutoLoRA (Xu et al.) on RN18, achieving 42.86% vs 47.89% and 48.95% respectively under AA, while maintaining higher clean accuracy. CLAT additionally complements AWP, SWAAT, data augmentation, and diffusion-based methods, adding 2–3.7% adversarial accuracy on top of each (Tables 14–15).

## Relation to Prior Work

CLAT builds directly on the PGD-AT adversarial training framework (Madry et al., 2019) and the theoretical link between feature robustness and local Lipschitz constants (Hein & Andriushchenko, 2017; Finlay et al., 2018), but departs from the standard practice of updating all parameters by introducing a 'criticality index' that identifies which specific layers predominantly learn non-robust features. This draws on Inkawhich et al.'s (2019, 2020) observation that intermediate layers capture shared non-robust features exploitable by activation attacks, and extends it into a training-time intervention. Compared to RiFT (Zhu et al., 2023), which uses heuristic redundancy measurements to select layers for clean-objective fine-tuning, CLAT employs a theoretically grounded, dynamically updated criticality metric with a dedicated criticality-reduction loss, targeting the most vulnerable layers rather than the most redundant ones.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CLAT offers a practical, drop-in fine-tuning step that mitigates robust overfitting—the persistent Achilles' heel of adversarial training—while tuning fewer than 5% of parameters, making it attractive for practitioners who already have adversarially pretrained models. The finding that critical layers are architecture-specific (nearly identical selections across CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100) suggests a structural predisposition in certain network positions to learn non-robust features, which could inform future architectural design. Open questions remain about why particular layers become critical, whether this is an artifact of training dynamics or inherent to the architecture, and whether the approach extends to non-CNN backbones or tasks beyond image classification.
