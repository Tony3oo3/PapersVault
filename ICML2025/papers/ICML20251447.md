## Problem

Existing long-tailed SAM variants face a trade-off: ImbSAM applies sharpness penalties only to tail classes (efficient but sharpens head-class loss landscapes), while CC-SAM uses per-class perturbations for fine-grained control but requires C backpropagations per update. The paper asks whether fine-grained, class-dependent sharpness control can be achieved without the per-class backpropagation overhead.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images from long-tailed benchmarks (CIFAR-10/100 LT at 32×32, ImageNet-LT and iNaturalist at 224×224) and outputs class labels, with evaluation performed on balanced test sets where each class contributes equally. It operates as a drop-in training objective modification layered on top of existing loss functions (CE, LA, VS, LDAM+DRW) and is compatible with both CNN backbones (ResNet-32/50) and foundation-model fine-tuning (CLIP ViT-B/16 via LIFT or full fine-tuning).

## Method

Focal-SAM replaces CC-SAM's per-class perturbations with a single shared perturbation direction computed from the gradient of a focal-weighted loss L_S^γ(w) = Σ_i (1−π_i)^γ L_S^i(w), where the focal weight (1−π_i)^γ smoothly increases from head to tail classes as γ grows. The focal sharpness term is the maximum over a single ρ-ball of the weighted sum of class-wise loss differences, requiring only three backpropagations per update (one for the weighted gradient, one for the base gradient, one at the perturbed point) versus C for CC-SAM. The hyperparameter γ controls the degree of tail emphasis: γ=0 recovers standard SAM, large γ approximates ImbSAM, and moderate γ achieves a fine-grained balance. The authors further derive a PAC-Bayesian generalization bound of Õ(1/n) (versus O(1/√n) for SAM and CC-SAM) that explicitly reveals the influence of λ, ρ, and the Hessian trace, providing a theoretical justification for why flattening the loss landscape tightens the bound.

## Results

On CIFAR-100 LT (IR=100), LA+Focal-SAM achieves 50.7% balanced accuracy, outperforming LA+SAM (50.0%), LA+ImbSAM (47.3%), and LA+CC-SAM (50.1%). On ImageNet-LT, LA+Focal-SAM reaches 54.3% balanced accuracy vs. 53.6% for LA+SAM, and on iNaturalist it achieves 71.8% vs. 71.0%. For domain generalization, Focal-SAM improves OOD performance by 0.5%–4.3% over baselines on ImageNet-Sketch, ImageNetV2, and ImageNet-C. Computationally, Focal-SAM requires only ~1.3–1.7× the training time of SAM, compared to CC-SAM's 2–15× overhead.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Focal-SAM consistently outperforms all three SAM variants (SAM, ImbSAM, CC-SAM) across every loss function and dataset. Against ImbSAM, the gains are most pronounced on head classes: LA+Focal-SAM achieves 63.9% head accuracy vs. 57.4% for LA+ImbSAM on CIFAR-100 LT (IR=100), a 6.5-point gap, while tail accuracy is comparable (32.5% vs. 31.0%). Against CC-SAM, Focal-SAM edges ahead on balanced accuracy (e.g., 50.7% vs. 50.1% for LA on CIFAR-100 LT; 54.3% vs. 52.4% for LA on ImageNet-LT) while being 2–15× faster in training time. Against standard SAM, Focal-SAM gains ~0.5–1.0% balanced accuracy (e.g., 50.7% vs. 50.0% for LA on CIFAR-100 LT; 71.8% vs. 71.0% for LA on iNaturalist). On domain generalization (Table 4), LIFT+Focal-SAM achieves 46.2% on ImageNet-Sketch, 68.0% on ImageNetV2, and 51.1% on ImageNet-C, surpassing LIFT+CC-SAM (45.6%, 67.7%, 50.9%) and LIFT+SAM (45.2%, 67.6%, 50.5%). Among the non-SAM baselines on ImageNet-LT (CB 33.2%, cRT 49.6%, DiVE 49.4%, DRO-LT 53.5%, DisAlign 52.9%, WB 53.9%), LA+Focal-SAM (54.3%) achieves the best balanced accuracy, and on iNaturalist it reaches 71.8% vs. the best non-SAM baseline WB at 70.2%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of Sharpness-Aware Minimization (SAM) and focal loss, specifically targeting long-tailed classification. It directly addresses two prior SAM variants for imbalanced learning: ImbSAM, which applies SAM only to tail classes (coarse control that sharpens head-class landscapes), and CC-SAM, which uses per-class perturbation radii for fine-grained control but requires C backpropagations per update. Focal-SAM replaces per-class perturbations with a single shared perturbation direction weighted by focal coefficients (1−π_i)^γ, achieving class-dependent sharpness penalties in just three backpropagations. The theoretical contribution extends PAC-Bayesian analysis to derive a sharper O(1/n) generalization bound, improving on the O(1/√n) bounds available for SAM and CC-SAM.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Focal-SAM offers a practical, drop-in replacement for CC-SAM in long-tailed classification pipelines, reducing training time by 2–15× while matching or exceeding accuracy, which is particularly relevant for fine-tuning large foundation models where CC-SAM's per-class backpropagations become prohibitive. The sharper generalization bound provides a more informative optimization target and clarifies the role of the Hessian trace and hyperparameters. However, the practical gains are modest (0.5–4.3% on OOD, ~1–2% on ID), and the method still incurs a ~36% overhead over vanilla SAM. Open questions include whether the focal weighting is truly optimal versus other monotone class-dependent schedules, and whether the theoretical bound's dependence on the Hessian trace translates into meaningful guidance for hyperparameter selection in practice.
