## Problem

Trained LLMs contain substantial computational redundancy at inference, yet their architectures remain uniform with no per-layer adaptation to hardware constraints. Exploring alternative architectures via training-from-scratch or full-model knowledge distillation is prohibitively expensive when the search space spans ~10^138 configurations. The paper addresses how to systematically search this space and produce hardware-tailored, non-uniform child models at a fraction of original training cost.

## Input / Output

The method consumes only the parent model's weights (no training data required, enabling 'open-weights, closed-data' use), a set of hardware constraints (target GPU, memory budget, minimum throughput, maximum latency, batch size, sequence length), and a small distillation corpus (~1B tokens for BLD, up to 45B for GKD). It produces a non-uniform child LLM where each transformer layer independently selects from 6 attention variants (GQA with 8/4/2/1 KV heads, linear, no-op) and 9 FFN variants (full through 10% intermediate dimension, linear, no-op), yielding a model with per-layer heterogeneous computation that runs on the target hardware (e.g., single H100 with FP8) at up to 2.17× throughput while retaining ~98.4% of parent accuracy.

## Method

Puzzle is a three-stage decomposed NAS pipeline. Stage 1 (Decoupled BLD): for each layer, attention and FFN subblock variants are trained independently against the parent block using a normalized MSE loss on parent activations, converting the per-layer training cost from multiplicative (m×n) to additive (m+n) and enabling a large block library at ~1B tokens. Stage 2 (MIP Search): each block variant is scored once via a replace-1-block KL divergence metric, then a mixed-integer programming solver (formulated as a grouped knapsack) selects one variant per layer to maximize total quality under hardware-specific memory, throughput, and latency constraints measured directly on target GPUs across prefill and generation phases. Stage 3 (GKD): a short end-to-end global knowledge distillation with cosine similarity and KL divergence losses (no LM loss) repairs inter-block compatibility errors accumulated during independent BLD training. The key differentiator is that hardware measurements (not FLOPs) drive the optimization, and the MIP solver navigates the ~10^138 architecture space in seconds, enabling 'train once, adapt on demand' across multiple deployment scenarios.

## Results

Nemotron-51B (derived from Llama-3.1-70B-Instruct via Puzzle) retains 98.4% of its parent's average accuracy across 11 benchmarks, while achieving up to 2.17× inference throughput speedup on a single NVIDIA H100 GPU (TP=1) versus the parent's optimal TP=4 configuration. On specific benchmarks, the child model actually exceeds the parent: GSM8K Chat (81.88 vs 81.58) and MT-Bench (8.99 vs 8.93). A compact derivative of Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct achieves 73.98 accuracy at 5856 tokens/s on an RTX 4090, matching the throughput of Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct (5737 tokens/s) while delivering 3.62 points higher accuracy (73.98 vs 70.36).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is the parent model Llama-3.1-70B-Instruct. Nemotron-51B preserves 98.4% of the parent's average accuracy while running 2.07–2.17× faster in tokens/s/GPU on H100 hardware, and critically fits on a single H100 (TP=1) where the parent requires 4 GPUs (TP=4) for optimal throughput. On two benchmarks (GSM8K Chat and MT-Bench), the child model slightly exceeds the parent, suggesting that architectural streamlining can occasionally sharpen specific capabilities. A secondary comparison involves the compact 8B derivative versus Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct: at nearly identical throughput on an RTX 4090 (5856 vs 5737 tokens/s), the Puzzle child achieves 73.98 accuracy versus 70.36, a 3.62-point advantage. Finally, after RLHF alignment, Nemotron-49B-Base surpasses its parent Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct on MT-Bench (9.21 vs 8.84) and Arena Hard (82.1 vs 71.70), demonstrating that lightweight post-alignment can push derived models beyond the parent on conversational and preference-based tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the decomposed NAS paradigm from computer vision (LANA, DNA, DONNA) to LLMs at tens-of-billions-of-parameters scale, introducing decoupled blockwise local distillation to make block library construction computationally feasible (reducing training cost from multiplicative to additive in the number of subblock variants). It builds on knowledge distillation (Hinton et al., 2015) as the final alignment stage and draws on prior LLM compression techniques (GQA, pruning, layer removal) as the search space vocabulary. The key departure from prior LLM compression work is the systematic, hardware-aware MIP-based search over per-layer non-uniform configurations, rather than applying a single uniform compression strategy across all layers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration of NAS-derived LLMs at the 50B+ parameter scale, producing publicly available models (Nemotron-51B, Nemotron-49B) that achieve 2.17× throughput on a single H100 while retaining 98.4% accuracy, using only 45B tokens versus 15T for the parent. The 'train once, adapt on demand' paradigm—where a single block library and scoring pass enables generation of architectures tailored to arbitrary hardware and constraint combinations—has broad practical implications for LLM deployment. Open questions include extending effective context length beyond training horizon, applying the framework to multimodal and reasoning-specific models, and whether the discovered non-uniform layer importance patterns generalize across model families.
