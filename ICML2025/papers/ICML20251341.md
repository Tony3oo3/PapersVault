## Problem

Language models exhibit a vast, poorly characterized space of behaviors, and existing automated red-teaming methods either restrict to narrow task distributions and produce uninterpretable gibberish-token prompts (e.g., GCG), or rely on expensive human interaction that doesn't scale. The paper asks how to build automated investigator agents that are both flexible enough to handle open-ended, rubric-defined behaviors and efficient enough to amortize search over the combinatorial prompt space, while producing human-interpretable natural-language prompts.

## Input / Output

The investigator model is conditioned on either a target response string y (string elicitation) or a natural-language rubric question R paired with a target answer a (rubric elicitation), and outputs a single-turn prompt x of at most 128 tokens. For rubric-based elicitation, the pipeline is two-stage: a stage-1 model first infers a rubric-satisfying response y from (R, a), and a stage-2 model then infers the prompt x that elicits y. At evaluation time, k=25 candidate prompts are sampled and reranked by elicitation log-probability, and the best is selected; the aggregate Frank-Wolfe model additionally provides a diverse distribution over strategies rather than a single prompt.

## Method

The paper frames behavior elicitation as amortized Bayesian posterior inference: the investigator p_θ(x|y) approximates the posterior p_m(x|y) under a prior p_m(x) and likelihood p_m(y|x), with an objective combining elicitation reward, entropy (diversity), and cross-entropy (fluency). Training proceeds in three stages: (1) SFT on forward-generated (prompt, response) pairs to initialize the investigator in the correct semantic space; (2) DPO with elicitation log-probability as the reward to achieve high success rates; and (3) an iterative Frank-Wolfe procedure that, at each iteration, penalizes prompts already discovered by previous iterates (via a weighted log-probability term in the DPO reward) and aggregates models with a convex combination, thereby avoiding mode collapse and surfacing qualitatively distinct strategies (repetition, continuation, topic-heading, etc.). The Frank-Wolfe algorithm is formally derived as optimizing the full objective by linearizing the reward+entropy term while treating the KL-to-prior term in closed form, with λ=β₁−β₂ controlling the diversity penalty. For rubric-based elicitation, the same SFT→DPO→FW pipeline is applied to each of the two stages separately, with the verifier score p_v(a|R,y) replacing the elicitation log-probability as the reward signal.

## Results

On AdvBench (Harmful Strings), the investigator achieves 98.8% attack success rate (ASR) against Llama-3.1 8B, and 100% ASR on the 50-task subset of AdvBench (Harmful Behaviors). For rubric-based elicitation, the full Frank-Wolfe method reaches 85.2% ASR@25 on hallucinations (TruthfulQA) and 80.7% ASR@25 on aberrant behaviors (DSM-5), outperforming both SFT and DPO baselines. Attacks transfer to Llama-3.3 70B Turbo (98% on behaviors) and show partial transfer to GPT-4o (28%) and Claude 3.5 Sonnet (26%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GCG (Zou et al., 2023), the investigator achieves a dramatic improvement: 98.8% vs. 57.0% ASR on Harmful Strings and 100% vs. 56% on Harmful Behaviors, though the paper notes GCG numbers are not directly comparable since GCG targets Llama 2 with a different test split. Against Adaptive Attacks (Andriushchenko et al., 2024), the method ties at 100% ASR on Harmful Behaviors, but the key distinction is that the investigator's successful prompts are natural language and human-interpretable (e.g., role-play, repetition, continuation strategies), whereas GCG produces gibberish token sequences and Adaptive Attacks require per-instance optimization. Internally, the full Frank-Wolfe pipeline outperforms both SFT (42% ASR) and DPO-only (98% ASR) on Harmful Behaviors while achieving substantially higher entropy (2.079 vs. 0.299), demonstrating that FW discovers diverse strategies without sacrificing attack success.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the automated red-teaming literature (GCG, AutoDAN, Perez et al.) by reframing per-instance prompt optimization as amortized Bayesian posterior inference, training a single investigator model that generalizes across target behaviors. The core training pipeline builds directly on DPO (Rafailov et al., 2023), with the key departure being an iterative Frank-Wolfe (Frank & Wolfe, 1956; Bach, 2012) wrapper that penalizes previously discovered strategies to avoid mode collapse. Unlike prior amortized red-teaming approaches (Perez et al., 2022; Hong et al., 2024) that optimize for a single 'harmfulness' signal, this work conditions on arbitrary natural-language rubrics and explicitly optimizes for diversity of elicitation strategies, connecting the objective to maximum-entropy RL and variational inference.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Achieving 100% attack success rate on AdvBench Harmful Behaviors with human-interpretable natural-language prompts (rather than GCG's gibberish tokens) has direct implications for AI safety evaluation and red-teaming practice. The Frank-Wolfe formulation provides a principled, convergent mechanism for balancing elicitation reward against strategy diversity, and the two-stage decomposition for rubric-based elicitation offers a reusable template for open-ended behavior discovery. Key open questions include the reliability of LLM-as-judge verifiers (reward hacking was observed), whether the single-turn setting generalizes to multi-turn adversarial interactions, and whether the discovered strategies transfer robustly to models with stronger alignment training.
