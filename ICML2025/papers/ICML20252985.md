## Problem

Training GCNs to learn implicit graph-to-property mappings is computationally expensive, especially at scale. While nonparametric teaching offers a principled framework for selecting informative examples to accelerate learning, it has only been developed for regular feature data and relies on functional gradient descent, creating a theoretical gap with the parameter-space gradient descent actually used in GCN training. This paper bridges that gap by proving the consistency between the two update mechanisms in the graph-structured setting.

## Input / Output

The teacher operates over a dense set of graph-property pairs, where each graph is specified by its node feature matrix X and adjacency matrix A, paired with a scalar (graph-level) or vector (node-level) property label. At each iteration, the teacher selects a subset of m graphs from the full pool of N to feed to the GCN learner; for graph-level tasks it picks graphs with the largest |f_θ(G_i) − f*(G_i)|, while for node-level tasks it uses a per-node normalized Frobenius norm ‖f_θ(G_i) − f*(G_i)‖_F / n_i to account for varying graph sizes. The GCN learner then performs standard parameter-based gradient descent on the selected subset, and the process repeats until the aggregate prediction error drops below a threshold ε.

## Method

GraNT reinterprets GCN training through nonparametric teaching by first analytically showing that the adjacency matrix A governs structure-aware parameter updates whose form is invariant to graph size, then proving (Theorem 5) that the dynamic GNTK converges pointwise to the structure-aware canonical kernel, thereby establishing that parameter gradient descent on a GCN is functionally equivalent to functional gradient descent in an RKHS. Building on this equivalence, the greedy GraNT algorithm selects teaching graphs by maximizing the projection coefficient ∂L(f_θ(G_i), y_i)/∂f_θ(G_i), which for convex losses is positively correlated with the prediction discrepancy ‖f_θ(G_i) − f*(G_i)‖. Two practical variants are provided: GraNT(B) selects entire batches with the largest average discrepancy, and GraNT(S) selects individual graphs proportionally within each batch before reorganizing them. The approach reduces wallclock training time by 30–47% across graph-level and node-level regression and classification benchmarks while preserving generalization performance.

## Results

GraNT reduces training wallclock time by 36.62% on graph-level regression (ZINC), 38.19% on graph-level classification (ogbg-molpcba), 30.97% on node-level regression (gen-reg), and 47.30% on node-level classification (gen-cls), while maintaining or slightly improving test performance. For example, on ogbg-molhiv, GraNT(B) achieves ROC-AUC 0.7676 (vs. 0.7572 without GraNT) in 1457s (vs. 2164s). On QM9, GraNT(B) matches the baseline MAE of 0.0051±0.0009 while cutting training time from 9655s to 6392s (−33.79%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On ogbg-molhiv (Table 3), GraNT(B) achieves the best ROC-AUC of 0.7676 in 1457s, outperforming GCN (0.7385, 2889s), GCN+Virtual Node (0.7608, 3083s), GMoE-GCN (0.7536, 3970s), GMoE-GIN (0.7468, 3932s), GDeR-GCN (0.7261, 1772s), and GDeR-PNA (0.7616, 5089s) on both speed and accuracy. On QM9 (Table 2), GraNT(B) achieves MAE 0.0051 in 6392s, dramatically outperforming AL-3DGraph (Subedi et al., 2024) which reports MAE 0.1682–0.7991 at 9200–12602s depending on hyperparameter settings. GraNT thus dominates all compared efficient-training baselines: it is faster than GDeR-GCN by ~18% while improving ROC-AUC by 4.2 points, and faster than GMoE-GCN by ~63% while improving ROC-AUC by 1.4 points. Against the standard GCN baseline, GraNT(B) halves training time (1457s vs. 2889s) while improving ROC-AUC by 2.9 points.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the nonparametric teaching framework (Zhang et al., 2023b; 2024a), which previously applied only to regular feature data and implicit neural representations (MLPs), to the graph-structured setting. The key theoretical bridge is showing that the dynamic Graph Neural Tangent Kernel (GNTK), derived from parameter-space gradient descent in GCNs, converges pointwise to the structure-aware canonical kernel used in functional gradient descent—thereby unifying the two perspectives. The flexible GCN formulation with per-order weights (Krishnagopal & Ruiz, 2023) and the GNTK framework (Du et al., 2019) provide the technical substrate, while the greedy teaching algorithm is adapted from the nonparametric teaching literature to select graphs with the largest prediction discrepancies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By establishing the first theoretical connection between nonparametric teaching and graph property learning, this work opens a principled route for data-efficient training of GNNs beyond ad-hoc sampling heuristics. The 30–47% wallclock time reductions across six benchmarks (regression and classification, graph- and node-level) are practically meaningful for large-scale molecular and social-network applications. Limitations include evaluation only on GCNs (not GATs, GINs, or message-passing variants), synthetic node-level data, and the reliance on a 'flexible GCN' architecture; extending the framework to other architectures and real-world node-level tasks remains open.
