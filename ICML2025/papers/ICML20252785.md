## Problem

Cross-modal knowledge transfer in open environments often involves data from heterogeneous feature spaces (e.g., different modalities or languages with different dimensions) that simultaneously exhibit shifted label distributions. Existing label shift methods assume homogeneous feature spaces, while heterogeneous domain adaptation methods ignore label distribution shifts and typically require labeled target data. This paper introduces the Heterogeneous Label Shift (HLS) problem, providing the first theoretical guarantee and practical algorithm for this combined setting.

## Input / Output

The method takes three data sources: a labeled source set S of n_s samples from X_s ⊂ R^{d_s}, an unlabeled target set T of n_t samples from X_t ⊂ R^{d_t} (where d_s ≠ d_t in general), and a small set O of n_p unlabeled parallel instance pairs (x_{o,s}, x_{o,t}) drawn from P_{X_s} × P_{X_t} with n_p ≪ n_s, n_t. The parallel instances share an implicit common label (unknown) and serve as a cross-modal bridge. The output is a target-domain classifier h ∘ T_t that maps target features to class labels in Y = {1, …, k}, trained entirely without target labels.

## Method

The paper derives a novel error decomposition theorem bounding the target-source risk gap by three terms: a conditional error gap Δ_CE(h), an importance weight estimation error ‖ŵ − w‖₂, and finite-sample Rademacher complexity terms. This motivates the HLSAN architecture, which alternately optimizes two components: (1) Feature Space Alignment, where two transformation networks T_s and T_t project heterogeneous inputs into a shared latent space, regularized by a parallel-instance loss L_O that minimizes ‖T_s(x_{o,s}) − T_t(x_{o,t})‖₂ to prevent negative transfer; and (2) Feature Distribution Alignment, where a domain discriminator adversarially aligns the target marginal P_t(X) with the importance-weight-reweighted source marginal P_s^w(X) (minimizing Jensen-Shannon divergence), while a weighted cross-entropy classifier loss minimizes the weighted source risk. Importance weights are estimated online via a regularized quadratic program (RQP) using the source confusion matrix and target prediction distribution, requiring no target labels. The overall knowledge transfer loss is L_KT = L_C^w + αL_P − βL_DA^w, where L_P is a projected MMD on parallel instances that further leverages their implicit label information.

## Results

On the Wikipedia Image→Text task, HLSAN achieves 81.73% accuracy (γ=2), 77.61% (γ=5), and 85.83% (γ=10), outperforming the strongest baseline JEMA by 9.7, 8.9, and 3.6 percentage points respectively. On the Multilingual Reuters Text→Text tasks (Spanish→English/French/German/Italian), HLSAN consistently leads across all four target languages and all three Dirichlet shift settings, e.g., reaching 59.9% on SP→EN at γ=10 versus 45.1% for JEMA and 25.0% for TNT. The modified label-shift baselines (BBSL, RLLS) perform worst, confirming that heterogeneous feature spaces fundamentally break traditional label-shift methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HLSAN outperforms all four baselines across every dataset and shift setting. Against the strongest HDA baseline JEMA (Fang et al., 2023), HLSAN gains 9.7 pp on Wikipedia at γ=2 (81.73 vs 72.02) and 14.8 pp on Multilingual Reuters SP→EN at γ=10 (59.9 vs 45.1). The semi-supervised HDA method TNT (Chen et al., 2016) trails HLSAN by 21–35 pp, and its performance degrades sharply when only one labeled target sample per class is provided. The label-shift baselines BBSL (Lipton et al., 2018) and RLLS (Azizzadenesheli et al., 2019) are the weakest, achieving only 9–24% accuracy, because they rely on dimensionality reduction to force a homogeneous feature space and cannot handle the inherent modality gap. Notably, JEMA narrows the gap at γ=10 on Wikipedia (82.28 vs 85.83), suggesting that when label shift is mild, semi-supervised HDA with a small amount of target labels becomes more competitive.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate research threads: heterogeneous domain adaptation (HDA), which handles feature-space mismatch via transformations (TNT, JEMA), and label shift adaptation (LS), which corrects for class-prior mismatch via importance weights (BBSL, RLLS). It builds directly on the adversarial alignment framework of DANN (Ganin et al., 2016) and the confusion-matrix-based weight estimation of Lipton et al. (2018), while generalizing the Generalized Label Shift (GLS) condition of des Combes et al. (2020) into a Heterogeneous Feature Alignment (HFA) assumption that accommodates dual feature transformations. The key departure is a novel error decomposition theorem that avoids reliance on unknown optimal labeling functions, yielding a bound-minimization objective that simultaneously addresses feature heterogeneity and label shift in an unsupervised target setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first theoretical guarantee for cross-modal transfer under simultaneous feature-space heterogeneity and label distribution shift, a setting that arises naturally in multilingual text classification and image-to-text tasks. The practical HLSAN algorithm demonstrates that a small set of unlabeled parallel instances can serve as a sufficient bridge to prevent negative transfer, which is a useful design principle for real-world cross-modal systems. However, the requirement for even a few parallel instances limits applicability in truly zero-overlap scenarios, and experiments are confined to relatively small-scale datasets (Reuters, Wikipedia subsets), leaving open whether the approach scales to large-vocabulary or high-dimensional visual domains. Future work could explore relaxing the parallel-instance requirement and extending the theory to multi-source or online settings.
