## Problem

Tropical cyclone (TC) precipitation forecasting has been largely neglected compared to TC track/intensity prediction, despite TC rainfall causing more economic losses and deaths than strong winds. Existing DL precipitation nowcasting models target fixed regions and predict absolute rainfall values, which introduces cumulative errors and ignores the moving observation window, meteorological context, and NWP information that are critical for TC rainfall. This paper presents the first global, DL-based TC precipitation forecasting system that addresses all three gaps simultaneously.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a 10°×10° spatial window centered on the TC (which moves with the cyclone), containing 4 timesteps of historical data (MSWEP rainfall at 0.1° resolution, adjacent residual sequences, ERA5 surface and multi-level pressure data, and scalar TC attributes) plus 4 timesteps of ERA5-IFS NWP forecasts. The output is 4 future rainfall fields at 3-hourly intervals (12 hours total), produced by accumulating predicted adjacent residuals onto the latest observed rainfall. Because the window tracks the TC, the model must handle dynamically changing terrain and environmental context rather than a fixed geographic domain.

## Method

TCP-Diffusion is a conditional DDPM that denoises a sequence of adjacent rainfall residuals (ΔRainfall) rather than absolute values, a mechanism called Adjacent Residual Prediction (ARP) that reduces cumulative error and enforces physical consistency by learning coherent temporal evolution. The denoising backbone is an Environmentally-Aware 3D U-Net (EA-3DUNet) with spatial and temporal attention blocks, conditioned on three specialized encoders: a 3D CNN for concatenated 2D historical fields (rainfall, residuals, ERA5 surface/pressure data, noise), an MLP+Transformer for scalar TC variables (intensity, velocity, track, month), and a ResNet-18 for NWP future prediction data. Training uses the standard DDPM noise-prediction objective with a cosine variance schedule over 200 steps; inference iteratively denoises from pure Gaussian noise to recover the residual sequence, which is then accumulated onto the latest observed rainfall to yield absolute predictions. The multi-modal encoder design explicitly bridges DL and NWP by extracting physical-law-based information from ERA5-IFS forecasts as conditioning signals.

## Results

On the test set of 126 TCs (2018–2020), TCP-Diffusion achieves ETS-6 = 0.438, ETS-24 = 0.147, ETS-60 = 0.0064, and TP_MAE = 0.423 mm/3hr, outperforming all DL baselines on moderate/heavy rainfall and total precipitation metrics. Against the ECMWF-IFS NWP system, TCP-Diffusion improves ETS-24 by 53% (0.128 vs. 0.083) and ETS-60 by 74% (0.0044 vs. 0.0026), while reducing TP_MAE from 0.507 to 0.474 mm/3hr. The model is the only method that beats the Persistence baseline on ETS-60 and TP_MAE, confirming the value of a TC-specific forecasting architecture.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the four DL baselines (Table 1), TCP-Diffusion wins on ETS-24, ETS-60, and TP_MAE against all methods, but U-Net edges it out on ETS-6 (0.442 vs. 0.438) due to U-Net's advantage on light-rainfall detection. The gap is most dramatic on ETS-60, where TCP-Diffusion (0.0064) outperforms PreDiff (0.0043) by 50% and NowcastNet (0.00016) by nearly 40×, and on TP_MAE where it beats NowcastNet by 0.146 mm/3hr. Against NWP systems (Table 2), TCP-Diffusion surpasses both ERA5-IFS and the higher-resolution ECMWF-IFS on every metric, with the largest relative gains on ETS-24 (+53% over ECMWF-IFS) and ETS-60 (+74%). In the appendix comparison with the large-scale FuXi weather model (Table 6), TCP-Diffusion dominates overwhelmingly (ETS-6: 0.395 vs. 0.028; TP_MAE: 0.463 vs. 5.104 mm/3hr), indicating that global weather models lack the localized TC rainfall detail that a TC-centric architecture captures. The Persistence baseline is competitive on ETS-6 and ETS-24 because the TC-centered window always contains rainfall, but TCP-Diffusion is the only model that beats it on ETS-60 and TP_MAE.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends diffusion-model-based precipitation nowcasting (PreDiff, Asperti et al., Addison et al.) from fixed-region tasks to a TC-centered, moving-window formulation, and adapts the adjacent residual prediction mechanism already used in NWP (Kalnay, 2003) and recent global weather DL models (Price et al., 2025; Nguyen et al., 2024) to the TC rainfall setting. The multi-modal encoder architecture draws on 3D U-Net (Çiçek et al., 2016), ResNet-18, and Transformer layers, while the NWP-guided conditioning follows the postprocessing paradigm of Harris et al. (2022) and Antonio et al. (2024). The key conceptual departure is treating the TC as a moving reference frame and integrating heterogeneous meteorological variables (wind, humidity, topography, TC intensity) alongside rainfall, rather than relying on rainfall data alone as in prior nowcasting work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TC rainfall causes more fatalities and economic damage than TC winds, yet most DL-based TC forecasting has focused on track and intensity, leaving a gap in precipitation prediction that this paper addresses for the first time in a global, DL-based setting. The finding that a simple persistence baseline outperforms three existing DL precipitation models on most metrics highlights that general-purpose nowcasting architectures do not transfer to the TC-centered task, underscoring the need for task-specific design. Open questions include extending the 12-hour/10°×10° forecast window, integrating with track and intensity prediction systems, and whether the ARP mechanism generalizes to other moving-phenomenon forecasting tasks. The 1.25 s/sample inference cost on a single A100 makes the approach practical for operational use, though the 200-step denoising loop remains a computational bottleneck relative to deterministic models.
