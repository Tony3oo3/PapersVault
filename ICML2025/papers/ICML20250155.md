## Problem

Existing causal Bayesian optimization (CBO) methods are restricted to single-objective settings, yet real-world decision-making systems (healthcare, manufacturing, policy) often require simultaneous optimization of multiple, potentially conflicting outcome variables. No multi-objective optimization method in the literature can exploit known causal structure to guide interventions, leaving a gap between the efficiency gains of causal reasoning and the practical need for Pareto-optimal trade-offs.

## Input / Output

The method takes a known causal graph ⟨G, Y, X, C⟩ specifying target variables, treatment variables, and non-manipulative variables, along with a small prior dataset of intervention outcomes (5 samples per intervention set by default). The output is not merely a set of optimal points but a collection of Pareto-optimal intervention set-value pairs (X_s, x_s) — specifying both which variables to intervene on and their values — together with the corresponding Pareto front in objective space. The search space is provably reduced from all 2^|X| intervention sets to the possibly Pareto-optimal minimal intervention sets O_{G,Y}, which can be dramatically smaller.

## Method

The approach has three key components. First, a graph-theoretic search space reduction generalizes Lee & Bareinboim's (2018) UC-territory and interventional border concepts to the multi-objective setting, yielding a characterization (Theorem 4.8) that a set X_s is possibly Pareto-optimal iff IB(G_{X̄_s}, Y) = X_s, guaranteeing no Pareto-optimal solution is lost. Second, the MO-CBO problem is decomposed into independent local multi-objective optimization problems, one per intervention set in the reduced collection S. Third, the algorithm (Algorithm 1) alternately advances these local problems using DGEMO's diversity-guided batch selection, with a novel relative hypervolume improvement (RHVI) criterion that normalizes hypervolume gains across local problems to decide which intervention set to evaluate next, balancing exploration across the Pareto fronts of different intervention strategies.

## Results

On the four MO-CBO benchmarks, MO-CBO achieves the best or tied-best generational distance (GD) and inverted generational distance (IGD) across all problems. The most dramatic gain is on SYNTHETIC-2 (with an unobserved confounder), where MO-CBO reaches GD 2.80 and IGD 0.87, compared to the best non-causal baseline DGEMO at GD 6.79 and IGD 4.50. On the no-confounder problems (SYNTHETIC-1, HEALTH, CREDIT APPROVAL), MO-CBO matches or slightly beats DGEMO on GD while consistently achieving lower IGD (e.g., 1.40 vs 2.57 on SYNTHETIC-1; 0.02 vs 0.05 on HEALTH), indicating denser Pareto front coverage within the same intervention budget.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MO-CBO is compared against five standard multi-objective Bayesian optimization baselines (ParEGO, MOEA/D-EGO, TSEMO, qNEHVI, DGEMO) that all intervene on every treatment variable simultaneously without exploiting causal structure. On SYNTHETIC-2, where an unobserved confounder links Y₁ to X₄, MO-CBO dramatically outperforms all baselines: IGD 0.87 vs. 4.50 (DGEMO, best baseline) and GD 2.80 vs. 6.79, because the baselines disrupt the causal path X₄→X₁→Y₁ and cannot recover the true Pareto front. On SYNTHETIC-1 (no confounders), MO-CBO ties DGEMO on GD (0.14) but nearly halves its IGD (1.40 vs. 2.57), reflecting better front coverage from avoiding wasteful interventions on X₃ and X₄. On the real-world HEALTH and CREDIT APPROVAL problems, MO-CBO achieves the lowest IGD (0.02 and 0.08) and GD (0.06 and 0.05), with margins over DGEMO being modest (e.g., IGD 0.02 vs. 0.05 on HEALTH) but consistent. Overall, MO-CBO never loses to any baseline on any metric, and its advantage is largest precisely when unobserved confounding makes the non-causal formulation misspecified.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends causal Bayesian optimization (CBO) from single-objective to multi-objective settings, combining it with the diversity-guided batch selection strategy of DGEMO. The search space reduction generalizes the interventional border and UC-territory concepts from Lee & Bareinboim (2018, 2019) from single-target to multi-target Pareto-optimality, using do-calculus to characterize which intervention sets can be discarded. The algorithmic backbone is DGEMO's hypervolume-improvement-based batch selection, augmented with a relative hypervolume improvement criterion to coordinate exploration across multiple local sub-problems. The paper positions itself as the first multi-objective method that explicitly exploits causal graph structure, filling a gap between the CBO and MOBO literatures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical implication is that standard multi-objective Bayesian optimization is 'misspecified' for causal systems: when unobserved confounders exist between targets and their ancestors, intervening on all treatment variables simultaneously can yield strictly suboptimal Pareto fronts, whereas the causal-aware approach preserves the relevant causal paths. This provides a principled, graph-theoretic criterion (interventional borders) for reducing the search space with a guarantee that no Pareto-optimal solution is lost. Limitations include the requirement of a known causal graph, the assumption of independent surrogate models (ignoring shared endogenous confounders), and the fact that the critical differentiator (unobserved confounding) is only demonstrated on a single synthetic problem. Future work on multi-task GPs and adapting other CBO variants to the multi-objective setting are natural next steps.
