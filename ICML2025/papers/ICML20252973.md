## Problem

Process Reward Models (PRMs) have proven effective at enhancing mathematical reasoning via step-by-step verification, but they are predominantly trained on math data and their generalizability to non-mathematical domains remains unexplored. This paper demonstrates that open-source math PRMs (e.g., Math-Shepherd, Qwen-2.5-Math-PRM) perform no better than a majority voting baseline in domains like Law, Philosophy, and Biology, revealing a critical domain-generalization gap.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252973/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The PRM consumes a chain-of-thought reasoning sequence of k text steps (delimited by double newlines, with a four-newline classification token appended after each step) and outputs a k-dimensional vector of per-step correctness scores in [0,1], extracted from the logits of '+' and '-' tokens in the final LLM layer. These step scores are scalarized via min-, last-, or average-aggregation into a single solution score, which drives inference-time reranking methods (WMV, BoN, Beam Search, MCTS) over N sampled CoTs per question.

## Method

VersaPRM is built via a two-stage synthetic data pipeline: (1) Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct generates 16 CoTs per MMLU-Pro question across 14 domains, and (2) Llama-3.1-70B-Instruct auto-labels each CoT by identifying the earliest erroneous step, using explicit GOOD/OK/BAD step definitions and the ground-truth answer in the prompt (yielding ~75% labeling accuracy, comparable to PRM800K's ~80%). The resulting 84K-step dataset (MMLU-Pro-CoT-Train) is used to LoRA fine-tune a math PRM initialized from LlamaPRM800K (Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct full-fine-tuned on PRM800K) with cross-entropy loss on the classification token. Ablations show that domain diversity in training data—not merely format familiarity—is essential for multi-domain generalization, and that prior math PRM initialization provides a beneficial inductive bias for leveraging multi-domain fine-tuning.

## Results

On MMLU-Pro-CoT-Eval (Unlabeled) using WMV with min-aggregation over 16 CoTs generated by Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, VersaPRM achieves 61.22% overall accuracy, a +4.07% absolute gain over the Majority Voting baseline (57.15%). The gain is most pronounced in non-math domains: +7.93% on Law (43.86% vs. 35.93%), +4.30% on Philosophy (49.13% vs. 44.83%), and +4.62% on Biology (80.00% vs. 75.38%). VersaPRM also outperforms the best math PRM (Qwen-2.5-Math-PRM) in Math itself (68.87% vs. 67.20%), indicating no trade-off in mathematical reasoning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VersaPRM outperforms all four open-source math PRMs across every domain group. Against the strongest math PRM, Qwen-2.5-Math-PRM (Zheng et al., 2024), VersaPRM gains +3.05% on All (61.22 vs. 58.17) and a striking +7.58% on Law (43.86 vs. 36.28), while also surpassing it in Math (68.87 vs. 67.20). Math-Shepherd (Wang et al., 2024b) and RLHFlow-Deepseek (Xiong et al., 2024) show only marginal gains over MV in non-math domains (≤+0.45%), whereas VersaPRM consistently achieves +3–8% gains. Math-PSA/OpenR (Wang et al., 2024a) similarly plateaus near the MV baseline outside math-adjacent domains. Compared to the Majority Voting baseline (Wang et al., 2023), VersaPRM delivers a +4.07% overall improvement, with the largest absolute gains in Law (+7.93%), Chemistry (+7.46%), and Math (+6.47%), confirming that the multi-domain synthetic training data enables genuine cross-domain generalization rather than mere format memorization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the process reward model (PRM) framework established by Lightman et al. (2024) and the synthetic data generation pipelines of Math-Shepherd (Wang et al., 2024b) and OmegaPRM (Luo et al., 2024) from their exclusive focus on mathematical reasoning to a multi-domain setting. The auto-labeling approach draws on the critic-model paradigm of ProcessBench (Zheng et al., 2024) and the knowledge-distillation insight that a larger LLM can serve as a step-level annotator for a smaller PRM. The key conceptual departure is demonstrating that the PRM task, previously validated only on math benchmarks (MATH500, GSM8K), does not transfer to non-mathematical domains without explicit multi-domain training data, and then providing a cost-effective synthetic pipeline to close that gap.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first open-source multi-domain step-labeled reasoning dataset (MMLU-Pro-CoT-Train) and demonstrates that a ~$100 synthetic data pipeline can produce a PRM that consistently outperforms math-only PRMs across 14 domains, with particularly large gains in Law (+7.9%) and Philosophy (+4.3%). Open questions remain: whether VersaPRM can serve as an RL reward signal beyond math, whether the Llama-generator/Llama-labeler pairing introduces model-family bias, and whether the approach generalizes to truly open-ended (non-multiple-choice) reasoning. The hold-out domain ablation and the finding that a random multi-domain subset outperforms a same-sized math subset suggest genuine domain-general reasoning rather than format memorization, but the evaluation is limited to MMLU-Pro's multiple-choice format and a single generator family.
