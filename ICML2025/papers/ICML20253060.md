## Problem

The delay-as-payoff model, where the observation delay is proportional to the payoff itself (e.g., survival time in clinical trials, postoperative length of stay), has only been studied in simple multi-armed bandits. Extending it to contextual linear bandits is non-trivial because standard linear bandit algorithms rely on confidence ellipsoids for the parameter θ, and in the delay-as-payoff setting it is difficult to construct valid lower confidence bounds since one action's loss is estimated through observations of all other actions.

## Input / Output

The learner receives a finite action set A ⊂ R_+^n ∩ B_2^n(1) of size K (fixed in the non-contextual case, or drawn i.i.d. from an unknown distribution P each round in the contextual case) and a maximum delay D. Upon selecting action a_t, the learner incurs loss u_t ∈ [0,1] but only observes it after a delay d_t = D·u_t, making the feedback inherently payoff-dependent. The algorithm outputs a selected action per round and achieves regret bounds whose delay overhead scales with min{n·d*·log(T/n) + D·Δ_max, D·Δ_max·log(T/n)}, where d* is the expected delay of the optimal action.

## Method

Instead of constructing a least-squares estimator and confidence ellipsoid for θ as in LinUCB, the algorithm performs phased arm elimination using only a volumetric spanner S_m of size 3n computed from the active action set each epoch. It pulls only spanner actions in round-robin fashion for 2^m rounds, then constructs two types of UCBs/LCBs for spanner actions: one that treats unobserved losses optimistically (as 0) or pessimistically (as 1), and another using only confirmed observations from rounds t−D. These bounds are propagated to all active actions via their linear decomposition over the spanner (a = Σ λ_i·a_i with ‖λ‖_2 ≤ 1), choosing UCB or LCB of each spanner component based on the sign of λ_i. Actions are eliminated when their LCB exceeds min{UCB of some other action, B}, and if the active set empties, the guess B on the optimal loss is doubled. For the contextual case, a misspecification-tolerant variant of this algorithm is plugged into the Hanna et al. (2023) contextual-to-non-contextual reduction, yielding the first regret guarantee for contextual linear bandits with delay-as-payoff.

## Results

The paper evaluates regret over T=16000 rounds with K=70 actions, D=1000, and n∈{6,8,10} in both delay-as-loss and delay-as-reward settings (Figure 1). The proposed phased arm elimination algorithm consistently achieves lower cumulative regret than all three baselines across every configuration. A distinctive 'phase transition' occurs after approximately 9–12 epochs, at which point the algorithm has eliminated a significant fraction of suboptimal actions and regret growth becomes nearly constant, a behavior not observed in any baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against three baselines: (1) LinUCB using only currently available observations (the standard approach for handling delayed feedback), (2) OTFLinUCB, and (3) OTFLinTS (both from Vernade et al., 2020, designed for linear bandits with payoff-independent stochastic delay). The proposed algorithm outperforms all three baselines in every tested setting (all combinations of n∈{6,8,10} and delay-as-loss/reward). The gap is most pronounced in the delay-as-loss setting, where the baselines continue to accumulate regret linearly while the proposed method's regret plateaus after the elimination phase. OTFLinUCB and OTFLinTS, though designed for delayed feedback, assume payoff-independent delays and thus cannot exploit the structure of delay-as-payoff, resulting in consistently higher regret. The LinUCB baseline, which simply ignores unobserved losses, performs worst among the three, confirming that naive handling of delayed feedback is insufficient in the payoff-dependent delay regime.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the delay-as-payoff model from Schlisselberg et al. (2025), which was limited to simple multi-armed bandits, to the much more practical setting of contextual linear bandits. The key algorithmic departure from standard linear bandit methods (LinUCB-style least-squares with confidence ellipsoids) is the use of volumetric spanners (Hazan & Karnin, 2016) to construct UCBs/LCBs, since the payoff-dependent delay breaks the standard ellipsoid-based LCB construction. For the contextual extension, the paper adopts the contextual-to-non-contextual reduction of Hanna et al. (2023), which requires handling misspecification—a minor but necessary modification to the non-contextual algorithm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first regret guarantee for contextual linear bandits with delay-as-payoff, resolving an explicit open problem posed by Schlisselberg et al. (2025). The delay overhead bound is instance-dependent and small when the optimal action has small loss, which is natural in applications like clinical trials (progression-free survival) or advertising (time-to-re-engagement). Open questions include matching lower bounds, extending to general payoff-dependent delays (not just proportional), unbounded delays, and settings with intermediate or evolving observations. The experimental baselines are relatively weak (LinUCB with only available feedback, and OTF methods designed for payoff-independent delays), so the empirical evidence, while consistent, does not stress-test the method against strong delay-aware linear bandit competitors.
