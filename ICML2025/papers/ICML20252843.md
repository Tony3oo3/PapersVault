## Problem

Byzantine-robust distributed learning methods rely on robust aggregators to tolerate faulty or malicious workers, but no prior work theoretically characterizes the cost of these aggregators when no Byzantine workers are actually present. This paper asks: how large can the aggregation error be in the no-attack case, and how does it scale with the number f of Byzantine workers the aggregator is configured to tolerate?

## Input / Output

The method operates in a parameter-server framework where n workers each compute a local gradient vector ∇F_i(w_t) at the current model parameter w_t and send it to a central server. The server applies a robust aggregator Agg(·) configured with tolerance f < n/2 to produce a single aggregated gradient, which is then used to update w_{t+1} = w_t − η_t · Agg(g_1,…,g_n). The theoretical analysis treats the input as arbitrary vectors in R^d (no distributional assumptions beyond bounded heterogeneity G), while the empirical evaluation uses ResNet-20 on CIFAR-10 (32×32 RGB) with data partitioned across 16 workers via Dirichlet distributions (α ∈ {0.1, 1.0, 10.0}).

## Method

The paper proves that any (f,κ)-robust aggregator necessarily has worst-case no-attack aggregation error ε ≥ f/(n−f), establishing an irreducible tension: tolerating more Byzantine workers degrades accuracy even when all workers are honest. For specific aggregators, the coordinate-wise trimmed mean TM_{f/n} achieves exactly ε = f/(n−f) (tight), the coordinate-wise median achieves ε = ⌊(n−1)/2⌋/(n−⌊(n−1)/2⌋), and the geometric median achieves ε = 1. The convergence analysis shows that ByzGD with any (f,κ)-robust aggregator cannot drive the average gradient norm below f/(n−f)·G² (non-convex) or the function gap below f/(n−f)·G²/(2μ) (PL condition), and these lower bounds are tight. Empirically, training ResNet-20 on CIFAR-10 with multi-Krum or trimmed mean shows top-1 accuracy dropping sharply as f increases (e.g., from 89.4% to 40.3% for multi-Krum at f=7 under α=0.1), and nearest-neighbour mixing partially recovers accuracy at the cost of reduced Byzantine tolerance.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with ResNet-20 (160 epochs, no Byzantine workers), vanilla mean aggregation (f=0) achieves ~89.4% top-1 accuracy. As the tolerated Byzantine fraction f increases, accuracy degrades substantially: Multi-Krum drops to 40.31% at f=7 under heterogeneous data (α=0.1), a loss of 49.11 percentage points, while coordinate-wise trimmed mean drops to 10.00% (−79.42 pp). Applying nearest neighbour mixing (NNM) with f_NNM=1 largely recovers accuracy (88.81% for Multi-Krum, 86.61% for trimmed mean at α=0.1), but at the cost of reducing the effective Byzantine tolerance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not propose a new aggregator but theoretically and empirically analyzes existing robust aggregators under the no-attack setting. Multi-Krum (Blanchard et al., 2017) and coordinate-wise trimmed mean (Yin et al., 2018) both show monotonically decreasing top-1 accuracy as f increases, confirming the theoretical lower bound ε ≥ f/(n−f); trimmed mean degrades far more severely (10.00% at f=7, α=0.1) than Multi-Krum (40.31%), consistent with its tighter ε bound. Nearest neighbour mixing (Allouah et al., 2023) substantially mitigates the accuracy loss: with f_NNM=1, Multi-Krum recovers to 88.81% and trimmed mean to 86.61% at α=0.1, versus 40.31% and 10.00% without NNM. However, NNM introduces its own trade-off—smaller f_NNM yields higher accuracy but reduces the effective Byzantine tolerance to min{f, f_NNM}—so the tension between robustness and no-attack accuracy persists even with mixing. Under more homogeneous data (α=10.0), all methods degrade less (e.g., Multi-Krum f=7 drops only 15.86 pp vs. 49.11 pp at α=0.1), consistent with the theoretical dependence on the heterogeneity parameter G.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the unified (f,κ)-robust aggregator framework introduced by Allouah et al. (AISTATS 2023), which subsumes prior definitions like (δmax,c)-ARAgg and (f,λ)-resilient averaging. However, it departs fundamentally from the existing literature by shifting the analytical focus from the with-attack regime (where prior works like Karimireddy et al. 2021, 2022 and Allouah et al. 2023 characterize aggregation error under Byzantine workers) to the no-attack regime, introducing a new ε-accuracy definition to formalize this previously unstudied question. The paper applies this new lens to well-known aggregators (Krum, trimmed mean, geometric median, coordinate-wise median) and derives tight lower and upper bounds on their no-attack aggregation error, revealing a fundamental trade-off that prior robustness analyses did not capture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides the first theoretical guarantee that Byzantine robustness and no-attack accuracy are in irreconcilable tension: the worst-case aggregation error is Ω(f/(n-f)) and this bound is tight, meaning no robust aggregator can avoid this degradation. For practitioners deploying federated or distributed learning systems, this means the tolerance parameter f must be carefully calibrated—setting it too high (as is common for safety) can cause dramatic accuracy loss (up to 79% in experiments) even in the absence of any attack. Open questions include whether adaptive or data-dependent choices of f can mitigate this tension, whether the worst-case conditions (high data skewness) are common in practice, and whether new aggregator designs can break the f/(n-f) lower bound under additional structural assumptions on the data.
