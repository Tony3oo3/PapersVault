## Problem

Existing vision-language models have limited temporal receptive fields, making it difficult to produce coherent captions for long videos. Prior consolidation methods either require supervised fine-tuning on target datasets (limiting zero-shot generalizability) or rely on large LLMs for summarization (incurring high inference cost and GPU memory). The paper addresses the gap of an efficient, training-free consolidation mechanism that preserves fine-grained object and relationship details across temporal segments without LLM-scale inference.

## Input / Output

The method takes a long video as input, uniformly sampled into temporal segments (6 frames for MSR-VTT/MSVD with image-centric VLMs, 12 frames or clips for ActivityNet Captions). Each segment is captioned by an off-the-shelf VLM (BLIP, BLIP2, or InternVL2.5), and the final output is a single video-level caption (short, ~32 tokens) or a video paragraph caption (longer, up to 400 tokens) generated by a 235M-parameter graph-to-text model. The framework is plug-and-play: any VLM with accessible APIs can serve as the segment captioner without architectural changes.

## Method

The pipeline has four stages: (1) off-the-shelf VLMs generate segment-level captions, (2) a FACTUAL-MR textual parser converts each caption into a scene graph with typed objects, attributes, and directed relationship edges, (3) an iterative consolidation algorithm merges all segment graphs into one unified graph by repeatedly selecting the most similar graph pair (via a BERT-base graph encoder) and matching objects with the Hungarian algorithm under a cosine-similarity threshold, and (4) a lightweight graph-to-text decoder (BERT-base encoder with edge-restricted attention mask + T5-base decoder, 235M params total) translates the consolidated graph into the final caption. The decoder is trained on ~2.5M graph-text pairs curated from image captioning datasets (MS-COCO, Flickr30k, Visual Genome, etc.) plus LLaVA-NeXT-generated Kinetics-400 video captions, using standard next-token prediction. A prioritized subgraph extraction step (retaining top-k nodes by merge count) optionally prunes the graph for more focused short captions. The key insight is that explicit scene-graph structure preserves object identities and inter-object relationships across segments far more faithfully than free-text LLM summarization, while the 235M decoder is orders of magnitude lighter than the 7B+ LLMs used by competing consolidation methods.

## Results

On MSR-VTT, SGVC with BLIP2 achieves CIDEr 26.1, B@4 18.4, and F_BERT 0.487 in zero-shot video captioning, outperforming VidIL (zero-shot: CIDEr 3.1) and Video ChatCaptioner (CIDEr 16.5). On MSVD, SGVC (BLIP2) reaches CIDEr 53.3 and F_BERT 0.597, surpassing Video ChatCaptioner (CIDEr 35.8) and even approaching VidIL's few-shot setting (CIDEr 60.3) without using any dataset-specific exemplars. For video paragraph captioning on ActivityNet Captions, SGVC (BLIP2) achieves CIDEr 20.9 and F_BERT 0.331, substantially exceeding VidIL zero-shot (CIDEr 4.6) and Video ChatCaptioner (CIDEr 1.6). The method also uses only 0.74B–4.24B parameters and 5.07–18.40 GB GPU memory, compared to 7.50B–11.00B parameters and 14.50–28.20 GB for LLM summarization baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SGVC decisively outperforms VidIL in the zero-shot setting across all metrics on both MSR-VTT and MSVD (e.g., CIDEr 26.1 vs. 3.1 on MSR-VTT), and even matches or exceeds VidIL's few-shot variant (which uses dataset-specific exemplars) on F_BERT (0.490 vs. 0.490 on MSR-VTT; 0.597 vs. 0.674 on MSVD). Against Video ChatCaptioner, SGVC wins on CIDEr by a wide margin (26.1 vs. 16.5 on MSR-VTT; 53.3 vs. 35.8 on MSVD) and on F_BERT (0.487 vs. 0.436; 0.597 vs. 0.550), while Video ChatCaptioner edges ahead on METEOR for MSR-VTT (22.0 vs. 23.1 is actually SGVC winning; correcting: SGVC wins on METEOR too at 23.1 vs. 22.0). In the controlled LLM summarization comparison (Table 2), SGVC with BLIP2 improves CIDEr from 15.4 to 26.1 on MSR-VTT and from 41.6 to 53.3 on MSVD over Mistral-7B summarization, confirming that graph consolidation preserves object identities better than free-text aggregation. On ActivityNet Captions paragraph captioning, SGVC (BLIP2) achieves CIDEr 20.9 versus 12.1 for GPT-4o-mini summarization and 1.6 for Video ChatCaptioner. Among the older zero-shot methods in Table 8, SGVC (BLIP2, CIDEr 26.1) surpasses DeCap-COCO (18.6), MAGIC (7.4), ZeroCap (5.8), and Tewel et al. (11.3), though C3 (27.8) and DeCap-MSRVTT (34.8) use MSR-VTT training annotations and are not directly comparable in a strict zero-shot setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes scene graph parsing and consolidation—techniques rooted in structured prediction and graph theory—for the specific problem of long video captioning, departing from the dominant LLM-based consolidation paradigm (VidIL, Video ChatCaptioner) that relies on large language models to aggregate segment-level text. Rather than fine-tuning VLMs on long-video data or prompting multi-billion-parameter LLMs, SGVC introduces a lightweight 235M-parameter graph-to-text decoder trained on image captioning corpora, with the Hungarian algorithm providing a principled object-matching mechanism across segment graphs. The approach is conceptually closest to memory-based and recursive consolidation frameworks but replaces learned memory modules with explicit graph merging, and it inherits the segment-level captioning stage from off-the-shelf VLMs (BLIP, BLIP2, InternVL2.5) without modification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a practical, training-free path to long video captioning that is plug-and-play with any VLM backbone and requires orders of magnitude fewer parameters than LLM-based alternatives, making it attractive for deployment in resource-constrained settings. A key limitation is that hallucinations in segment-level captions propagate through the graph consolidation (as acknowledged in the paper's failure cases), capping the method's ceiling at the quality of the underlying VLM. Open questions include whether graph consolidation can capture more complex temporal dynamics (e.g., causal sequences, state changes) beyond object-attribute-relationship triples, whether the approach scales gracefully to hour-long videos with hundreds of segments, and whether the CPU-based merging step can be parallelized without degrading the matching quality.
