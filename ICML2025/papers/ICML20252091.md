## Problem

Estimators for learning unnormalized distributions (energy-based models) have been proposed independently across multiple research communities—MLE, MC-MLE, GlobalGISO, pseudo-likelihood, interaction screening, NCE variants, and score matching—without a clear understanding of how they relate to one another. This paper addresses the lack of a unifying theoretical framework that connects these seemingly disparate methods and provides the first finite-sample convergence guarantees for most of them. It also corrects a misleading claim that conditional NCE converges to score matching in the vanishing-noise regime.

## Input / Output

The framework takes an unnormalized density model φ_θ(x) = exp(⟨θ, ψ(x)⟩) from an exponential family, data samples from q_d(x), and either noise samples from a reference distribution q_n(x) (for f-NCE and α-CentNCE) or conditional samples y ~ π(y|x) (for f-CondNCE). The output is a parameter estimate θ obtained by minimizing a Bregman-divergence-based objective, accompanied by explicit finite-sample convergence rates of O(n^{-1/2}) under bounded statistics and bounded parameter space assumptions. A key structural detail is that α-CentNCE and f-CondNCE are invariant to multiplicative scaling of φ_θ, so they remain consistent even when the model is well-specified only up to an unknown constant.

## Method

The paper introduces f-NCE, a family of estimators derived from Bregman divergence minimization for density ratio estimation, parameterized by a strictly convex generator function f. Two key variants are developed: (1) α-CentNCE, which applies an α-dependent normalization (α-centering) to the model before plugging into the asymmetric power NCE objective, and (2) f-CondNCE, which contrasts joint distributions q_d(x)π(y|x) vs. q_d(y)π(x|y) through a conditional channel. The central unifying result shows that α-CentNCE subsumes MLE (α=1, exact partition function), MC-MLE (α=1, Monte Carlo partition function), and GlobalGISO (α=0, exponential family with uniform reference) as special cases, and that f_α-NCE and α-CentNCE estimators are provably equivalent. For finite-sample analysis, the paper adapts the Negahban et al. (2012) framework for regularized M-estimators, establishing parametric O(n^{-1/2}) convergence rates under bounded max-norm statistics and bounded parameter space, with explicit dependence on worst-case density ratios and minimum eigenvalue conditions.

## Results

The paper establishes O(n^{-1/2}) parametric convergence rates for regularized f-NCE, α-CentNCE, and f-CondNCE estimators under bounded exponential family assumptions (Theorems 4.1–4.3), which are the first finite-sample guarantees of this type for nearly all NCE estimators considered. Theoretically, the α-CentNCE framework unifies MLE (α=1), MC-MLE (α=1 with estimated Z₁), and GlobalGISO (α=0) as special instances (Theorem 3.1), while f_α-NCE and α-CentNCE estimators are shown to be exactly equivalent (Theorem 3.2). A key negative result shows that f-CondNCE does NOT converge to score matching in the small-noise regime with finite samples; instead, the O(ε) statistical noise term dominates, and the variance diverges as ε→0 unless the number of conditional samples K is sufficiently large (Theorem 3.4, Figure 1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical unification paper rather than an empirical benchmark, so 'baselines' are existing estimators shown to be subsumed by the proposed framework. The α-CentNCE family at α=1 recovers MLE and MC-MLE, while at α=0 it recovers GlobalGISO for exponential families with bounded support and uniform reference distribution. The local version of centered NCE subsumes pseudo likelihood (Besag, 1975) and interaction screening objectives (ISO, GISO, ISODUS) proposed for MRFs. The paper corrects a claim by Ceylan & Gutmann (2018) that CondNCE converges to score matching as noise vanishes: Theorem 3.4 shows the empirical objective is dominated by an O(ε) noise term when ε is small and K is fixed, causing the estimator variance to diverge. The simulation (Figure 1) confirms this: for a 1D Gaussian with true mean μ=1, the empirical CondNCE derivative tracks the true mean only when ε≥10^{-2} or when K≥64 for very small ε, demonstrating that the original CondNCE justification is misleading in realistic finite-sample settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies a family of estimators for learning unnormalized distributions—MLE, MC-MLE, GlobalGISO, pseudo-likelihood, and interaction screening objectives—that were independently proposed across machine learning, statistical physics, and graphical models communities, under the lens of f-NCE (Pihlaja et al., 2010) and its Bregman-divergence formulation (Sugiyama et al., 2012). The α-centering transformation is the key new device that reveals MLE (α=1) and GlobalGISO (α=0) as limiting instances of a single parametric family. The paper also extends CondNCE (Ceylan & Gutmann, 2018) to a general f-CondNCE framework and builds its finite-sample analysis on the regularized M-estimator theory of Negahban et al. (2012) as adapted by Shah et al. (2023).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The first finite-sample convergence rates for NCE-based estimators (f-NCE, α-CentNCE, f-CondNCE) provide a much-needed theoretical foundation for practitioners choosing among these methods, and the unification clarifies which estimators are equivalent under what conditions. The negative result—that f-CondNCE does not converge to score matching in the small-noise regime and its variance diverges as ε→0—corrects a widely cited justification from Ceylan & Gutmann (2018) and has practical implications for hyperparameter selection. Limitations include the restriction to bounded exponential families, preliminary 1D simulations, and the fact that non-regularized estimators only achieve suboptimal O(n^{-1/4}) rates; extending beyond bounded statistics and studying high-dimensional empirical behavior remain open.
