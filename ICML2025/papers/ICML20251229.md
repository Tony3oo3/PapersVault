## Problem

Designing protein backbones that simultaneously satisfy structural constraints (motif scaffolding) and dynamical constraints (a targeted normal mode of oscillation) has proven difficult. The prior NMA-guidance method uses an analytical loss-guidance term that is sensitive to user-defined sampling parameters and fails to produce high-quality samples meeting both conditions. The core challenge is that the dynamics condition involves eigenvectors of a Hessian matrix, a quantity no neural network can directly learn to predict, making standard classifier-guidance inapplicable.

## Input / Output

The method takes a fixed structural motif (Cα coordinates and orientation frames for M residues, kept at timestep 0 throughout generation), a target displacement matrix y ∈ R^{M×3} specifying the desired Cα displacements in the lowest non-trivial normal mode, and scaffold length ranges. It outputs a full [N, Cα, C] backbone (3L×3 coordinate matrix) where the generated scaffold holds the motif in place and the motif Cα atoms exhibit the targeted oscillation pattern. The conditioner operates only on Cα translational noise, leaving the SO(3) rotational component of RFdiffusion unmodified, and the target displacements are made rotation-invariant via Kabsch alignment to the predicted motif at each denoising step.

## Method

NMA-tune introduces a trainable 530K-parameter rotation-equivariant GVP-GNN conditioner that learns a noise correction ε_θ to add to the frozen RFdiffusion prediction (ε = ε_RF + ε_θ), replacing the analytical loss-guidance term. The key efficiency insight is replacing the expensive ∇_{x_t} l_y(x̂_0) (requiring backpropagation through RFdiffusion) with the cheaper ∇_{x̂_0} l_y(x̂_0), empirically showing that ignoring the Jacobian factor still yields effective conditioning. The conditioner receives as node features the normalized NMA-loss gradient and the RFdiffusion noise vectors, plus scalar features (magnitudes, residue index, timestep), and performs 5 layers of gated GVP message passing on a fully-connected Cα graph. Training uses a weighted combination of noise-matching loss (0.05), NMA-loss (0.8), chain-distance loss (0.1), and radius-of-gyration loss (0.05) on 7139 SCOPe proteins, with auxiliary losses preventing the conditioning from distorting large-scale structural properties. At inference, a sinusoidally time-scaled guidance scale upweights the conditioner output, and the correction is omitted in the final 3 denoising steps to preserve structure quality.

## Results

At η=0.0 (strictest sampling), NMA-tune achieves 12.4% of samples with sc-cossim > 0.9 on the 1hhp target (vs. 7.6% for NMA-guidance) and 9.4% on 1exr (vs. 6.4%), while maintaining competitive designability (58.2% and 53.3% respectively). Across all four targets, NMA-tune consistently produces a higher fraction of samples that are simultaneously designable and meet the dynamics condition, e.g. 29.1% with sc-cossim > 0.7 on 1hhp vs. 22.7% for NMA-guidance. In MD validation, dynamics-conditioned NMA-tune samples show PCA-cossim of 0.86 and 0.58 (20–100 ns window) compared to 0.23 and 0.34 for unconditional samples, confirming the targeted normal mode is present in simulated trajectories. NMA-tune also reduces sampling overhead to only 8% over unconditional RFdiffusion, versus ~75% for NMA-guidance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is NMA-guidance (Komorowska et al., ICLR 2024), the prior loss-guidance approach for dynamics conditioning without a trainable component. NMA-tune wins on the joint metric of designability + dynamics success across all four targets and all three noise scales (η = 0.0, 0.1, 1.0): at η=0.0, the fraction of designable samples with sc-cossim > 0.9 is roughly 1.5–2× higher for NMA-tune (e.g. 12.4% vs. 7.6% on 1hhp; 9.4% vs. 6.4% on 1exr). NMA-guidance produces more designable samples in isolation (e.g. 67.3% vs. 58.2% on 1hhp), but the paper argues that only samples satisfying both criteria constitute successful designs, where NMA-tune is superior. On the hardest target (1hhp_assembly), NMA-tune more than doubles the sc-cossim > 0.8 rate (7.0% vs. 2.1%) despite lower overall designability. In MD validation, NMA-tune-conditioned samples show PCA-cossim of 0.86 and 0.58 in the 20–100 ns window versus 0.23 and 0.34 for unconditional samples, providing orthogonal evidence that the targeted mode is physically realised. NMA-tune also reduces inference overhead to 8% over unconditional RFdiffusion sampling, compared to ~75% for NMA-guidance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the loss-guided diffusion framework (Song et al., 2023) and the NMA-guidance dynamics conditioning method (Komorowska et al., 2024) by replacing the analytical weighted-sum conditional term with a trainable 530K-parameter GVP-GNN conditioner. It builds directly on RFdiffusion (Watson et al., 2023) as the frozen unconditional base model, leveraging its motif-scaffolding capability so that only the dynamics term requires explicit loss-guidance at inference. The key conceptual departure from NMA-guidance is learning the optimal correction to the unconditional noise rather than relying on a hand-tuned guidance scale applied to an approximate analytical gradient.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first method to produce protein backbones that are simultaneously designable (foldable to stable structures) and exhibit a user-specified normal mode, validated by MD simulations showing the targeted mode in the largest principal component. However, the method only controls displacement directions, not absolute flexibility magnitudes, and the targeted mode does not consistently persist in alternative conformations reached during longer simulations. Open questions include extending conditioning to absolute displacement values, improving the stability of designed proteins across conformational states, and whether the approach generalizes beyond hinge motions to other collective dynamics.
