## Problem

Modeling the stochastic evolution of high-dimensional systems (e.g., gene expression, cellular states) from snapshot observations at irregular, non-equidistant time points remains challenging: existing methods either reduce dimensionality (losing transient dynamics and introducing reconstruction artifacts), require expensive ODE/SDE integration and trajectory caching during training, or cannot handle arbitrary snapshot timing. The paper asks: among all stochastic processes connecting observed marginals, how can one learn the most probable one directly in the ambient space without simulation or dimensionality reduction?

## Input / Output

The method consumes batches of i.i.d. samples X_{t_i} from each marginal ρ_i at user-specified irregular time points t_0 < t_1 < … < t_M in R^d (e.g., 50–1000-dimensional gene expression vectors or 32×32 RGB images), with no trajectory data required. It outputs two neural networks—a flow network v_t(x;θ) and a score network s_t(x;θ)—whose combination defines the SDE drift u_t(x;θ) = v_t(x;θ) + (g²(t)/2)·s_t(x;θ). Given initial conditions x_0, trajectories are generated by integrating this SDE with constant diffusion σ, yielding samples at any arbitrary intermediate time point not present in the training data.

## Method

MMSFM trains on overlapping windows of k+1 consecutive marginals (k=2 triplets) in a rolling fashion. For each window, a first-order Markov approximation of the multi-marginal OT plan is computed via sequential minibatch OT couplings, and monotonic cubic Hermite splines are constructed through the coupled control points to define a conditional Gaussian probability path p_t(x|z) = N(x | μ_t, σ_t²). The training objective is a conditional score-and-flow matching loss where both the ODE drift target u_t°(x|z) = (σ_t'/σ_t)(x−μ_t)+μ_t' and the score target ∇log p_t(x|z) = (μ_t−x)/σ_t² are computed analytically from the spline, requiring no simulation. A key theoretical result (Theorem 2.1) shows that combining k overlapping mini-flow losses yields a gradient with a competing (k−1)‖v_t‖² regularization term that prevents overfitting, while stratified time sampling across sub-intervals ensures balanced training for highly irregular time gaps. The monotonic cubic Hermite spline choice (over natural cubic splines) guarantees per-dimension monotonicity, avoiding overshoot artifacts that plague natural splines when neighboring intervals differ greatly in length.

## Results

On the S-shaped Gaussians with equidistant time points (T₁, holding out t₅=0.83), the Triplet model achieves W₁=1.83 and W₂²=3.86, substantially outperforming MIOFlow (W₁=8.16, W₂²=66.91) and the Pairwise variant (W₁=2.36, W₂²=5.87). On the more challenging irregular time points T₂, the Triplet model further improves to W₁=1.62 on S-shaped and W₁=3.79 on α-shaped, while MIOFlow degrades to W₁=9.42 and W₁=5.04 respectively. On the high-dimensional CITEseq and Multiome gene expression datasets (50–1000 dimensions), the Triplet model consistently achieves lower W₁ and W₂² than the Pairwise model (e.g., Multiome PCA-100: Triplet W₁=70.39 vs. Pair W₁=70.72; Multiome HiVar-1000: Triplet W₂²=3160.84 vs. Pair W₂²=3166.01), confirming the benefit of overlapping windows in high-dimensional, noisy settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole external baseline is MIOFlow (Huguet et al., 2022), a manifold-interpolating optimal transport method that operates in a GAE-learned 2D embedding space. MMSFM (both Pairwise and Triplet variants) consistently and substantially outperforms MIOFlow on all three synthetic datasets: on S-shaped T₁, Triplet reduces W₁ from 8.16 to 1.83 (4.5× improvement) and W₂² from 66.91 to 3.86 (17× improvement); on α-shaped T₂, Triplet achieves W₁=3.79 versus MIOFlow's 5.04, while Pairwise actually underperforms MIOFlow (W₁=8.08), highlighting the importance of the overlapping-window design for irregular time points. On DynGen, both MMSFM variants beat MIOFlow on W₁ and W₂², with Triplet achieving the best MMD scores (0.22 vs. 0.53 for MIOFlow). On the real CITEseq and Multiome datasets (where MIOFlow is not evaluated), the Triplet model marginally but consistently outperforms the Pairwise model across all dimensionality settings (PCA-50, PCA-100, HiVar-1000), with the largest gap on Multiome MMD(M) (621.32 vs. 656.86 for PCA-100). The authors note that MIOFlow produces idiosyncratic trajectories between observed time points due to out-of-distribution reconstruction from the GAE, whereas MMSFM generates smoother, more biologically plausible flows in the ambient space.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the simulation-free score and flow matching framework of Tong et al. (2024b) and the conditional flow matching formulation of Lipman et al. (2023) from the pairwise source-target setting to the multi-marginal case with irregular time points. The key architectural departure is the use of overlapping rolling windows (k=2 triplets) with monotonic cubic Hermite transport splines (from Chewi et al., 2021) to construct conditional probability paths, rather than solving the full multi-marginal optimal transport problem or relying on dimensionality reduction as in MIOFlow or TrajectoryNet. The authors note a concurrent work by Rohbeck et al. (2025) that arrives at a similar ordered MMOT decomposition but uses natural cubic splines over all time points (k=M-1) rather than local rolling windows, and explicitly contrasts the monotonicity and overshoot properties of their spline choice.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method addresses a practically important gap in quantitative biology: modeling high-dimensional cellular dynamics from destructive single-cell snapshot measurements at non-uniform time points without the information loss inherent in dimensionality reduction. However, the results on highly irregular time points (T3) reveal a significant limitation—both Pairwise and Triplet models struggle, with the Triplet model actually failing catastrophically on the α-shaped dataset (W2² = 2.51×10⁶), suggesting the rolling-window approach has a hard boundary on how irregular the timing can be. The bifurcation handling failure on DynGen (particles jumping between branches due to mini-batch OT lacking consistency constraints) is acknowledged as an open problem. For broader DL practitioners, the technique of overlapping mini-flows with stratified time sampling is a useful pattern for any multi-marginal generative modeling task, but the primary impact remains in the single-cell genomics and trajectory inference community.
