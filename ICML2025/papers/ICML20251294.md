## Problem

Diffusion-based planners for offline RL can produce physically infeasible trajectories because the MSE-trained return estimator underestimates the true energy-guided distribution, causing intermediate samples to drift off the data manifold during reverse diffusion. The paper formalizes this as a guidance gap and proves a lower bound scaling as √d, showing the problem worsens with dimensionality and planning horizon, which limits deployment in safety-critical settings.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251294/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

At test time, LoMAP consumes the current noisy trajectory sample at each reverse-diffusion step, the trained noise-prediction network ε_θ, the trained return estimator J_φ, and the full offline trajectory dataset (used for k-NN retrieval via a Faiss IVF index). It outputs a projected trajectory sample constrained to a local low-rank subspace, which is then fed into the next diffusion step; the final denoised trajectory yields the planned action sequence. No additional parameters are trained—the offline dataset is queried but never modified.

## Method

LoMAP is a training-free, plug-and-play projection module inserted after each reward-guided update in the reverse diffusion loop. At step i−1, it first denoises the current sample via Tweedie's formula to obtain a clean surrogate τ̂⁰⁽ⁱ⁻¹⁾, then retrieves k nearest offline trajectories by cosine similarity. These neighbors are forward-diffused to timestep i−1, and a rank-r PCA (retaining components explaining ≥99% variance) yields an orthonormal basis U spanning the local tangent space. The guided sample is projected as UUᵀτⁱ⁻¹, pulling it back onto the feasible manifold. The paper also derives a theoretical lower bound on the guidance gap (Proposition 3.2) showing the MSE-based guidance underestimates the true exponential-weighted return by at least c√d/√(1−αᵢ), providing the formal justification for why projection is necessary.

## Results

On D4RL MuJoCo locomotion tasks, Diffuser^P (Diffuser + LoMAP) achieves an average normalized return of 82.8, outperforming Diffuser (77.5), RGG (81.2), and TAT (81.3), with the largest gain on Hopper-Medium (93.7 vs. 74.3 for RGG). On AntMaze, the hierarchical variant HD^P achieves 86.7 average success rate, nearly doubling HD (55.3) and substantially exceeding Diffuser^P (34.2) and Diffuser (13.3). On Maze2D, Diffuser^P reaches 136.3 (single-task) and 138.9 (multi-task) average return, surpassing Diffuser (119.5 / 129.4) and RGG (125.3 / 136.4). LoMAP also reduces artifact ratios in Maze2D-Large from 0.66 (Diffuser) to 0.38 at 30 sampled plans.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Diffuser^P outperforms the base Diffuser (Janner et al., 2022) across all 19 evaluated environments, with the largest absolute gains on Hopper-Medium (+19.4) and AntMaze-Medium-Play (+34.0). Against the trajectory-refinement baselines RGG (Lee et al., 2023b) and TAT (Feng et al., 2024), Diffuser^P wins on 4 of 6 Maze2D tasks and on Hopper-Medium (93.7 vs. 82.5/82.6), though it trails slightly on Walker2d-Medium (79.9 vs. 81.7/81.0). On AntMaze, HD^P (hierarchical Diffuser + LoMAP) dominates all baselines, achieving 86.7 average vs. HD's 55.3, RGG's 18.2, and DD's 3.0. Model-free baselines (IQL, CQL) and model-based baselines (MOPO, MOReL) are consistently outperformed, with MOPO/MOReL particularly weak on locomotion (42.1/72.9 average). Sequence-modeling baselines DT and TT trail Diffuser^P by 8.1 and 3.9 points on average. In the artifact-ratio comparison (Table 6), LoMAP achieves 0.35–0.38 infeasible-plan rate at 10–30 plans, beating stochastic sampling (Wang et al., 2024; 0.42–0.47), constrained gradient guidance (Lee et al., 2024b; 0.49–0.61), and inpainting optimization (Hao et al., 2024; 0.43–0.49).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Diffuser (Janner et al., 2022) for trajectory-level diffusion planning and Hierarchical Diffuser (Chen et al., 2024) for multi-level planning, positioning LoMAP as a training-free, plug-and-play projection module inserted into the reverse diffusion loop. It departs from prior trajectory-refinement approaches (RGG, TAT) that detect and correct infeasible plans post-hoc, instead intervening at every denoising step via local PCA-based manifold projection. Conceptually, it adapts projection ideas from image-generation inverse problems (MCG, DSG, MPGD) but replaces learned autoencoders with dataset-driven local subspace estimation, making it applicable without additional training infrastructure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The √d lower bound on the guidance gap provides a principled, dimensionality-aware explanation for why diffusion planners become unreliable in high-dimensional, long-horizon settings, moving the discussion beyond empirical observation. As a training-free module, LoMAP lowers the barrier to reliable diffusion planning and could be adopted broadly across existing diffusion-based planners without retraining. Key open questions include whether cosine-similarity-based neighbor retrieval remains effective in very high-dimensional or pixel-based observation spaces, whether the inherent restriction to the offline data manifold limits exploration of novel behaviors, and how the method extends to tasks requiring trajectory stitching or truly out-of-distribution generalization.
