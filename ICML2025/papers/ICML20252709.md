## Problem

Existing incremental object detection methods treat the detector as a monolithic unit, applying replay or distillation uniformly without identifying which internal component actually drives catastrophic forgetting. This paper asks whether forgetting is distributed across all modules or concentrated in a specific part, and finds it is predominantly localized to the RoI Head classifier while the RPN and regressor retain robustness across stages.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images (PASCAL VOC 2007 at ~500×375, MS COCO 2017 at variable resolution) and outputs bounding boxes with class labels, following the standard two-stage Faster R-CNN pipeline. Uniquely, the detector operates in a class-incremental setting where new classes are introduced sequentially without access to prior training data, and the model must maintain detection accuracy on all previously learned classes. Per-class coarse and fine-grained RoI feature prototypes (10 total per class) are stored in a lightweight buffer and replayed through the classification branch during subsequent training stages.

## Method

NSGP-RePRE targets the RoI Head classifier specifically, based on the empirical finding that it is the primary source of forgetting. RePRE stores two types of regional prototypes per class: a coarse prototype (mean of all RoI features for that class) representing the semantic center, and fine-grained prototypes selected via a density-aware hypersphere strategy that captures intra-class diversity; these are replayed through the classification branch with a cross-entropy loss. To prevent the evolving feature extractor from drifting RoI features away from stored prototypes, NSGP projects backbone and neck gradients into the null space of previous-stage inputs via SVD-based projection, ensuring updates are orthogonal to old feature directions. The overall loss combines current-stage classification, bounding-box regression, and prototype replay losses, with EWC applied to normalization layers.

## Results

On PASCAL VOC 2007 (single-step, mAP@0.5), NSGP-RePRE achieves 76.0% on all 20 classes and 72.7% average in the 19-1 setting, surpassing the previous SOTA BPF (74.1%/69.9%) by 1.9%/2.8%. On MS COCO 2017 (40-40), it reaches 35.4 AP / 55.3 AP50 / 38.6 AP75, exceeding BPF (34.4/54.3/37.3) by 1.0/1.0/1.3 points. In the challenging 70-10 COCO setting, NSGP-RePRE attains 36.5 AP, approaching the joint-training upper bound of 36.7 AP. Across multi-step VOC settings (e.g., 5-5 with 4 tasks), it achieves 65.7% mAP@0.5 on all 20 classes, outperforming BPF (62.5%) by 3.2%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NSGP-RePRE outperforms the previous state-of-the-art BPF (Mo et al., 2024) across all four VOC single-step settings: by 1.9% on 1-20 mAP and 2.8% on Avg in 19-1, and by 1.1%/1.1% in 10-10. Against the strongest replay-based competitor ABR (Yuyang et al., 2023), the gains are larger—4.4% in the initial class set and 3.3% on all 20 classes in 19-1. On MS COCO 40-40, NSGP-RePRE leads BPF by 1.0 AP and 1.3 AP75, while in 70-10 it edges out BPF by 0.3 AP but trails by 0.8 AP50 (56.0 vs 56.8). All other baselines (ORE, OW-DETR, ILOD-Meta, FasterILOD, PPAS, MVC, PROB, PseudoRM, MMA) are consistently outperformed by 3–10+ points on 1-20 mAP across every setting. In multi-step VOC (5-5, 4 tasks), NSGP-RePRE achieves 65.7% on 1-20, beating BPF (62.5%) by 3.2% and ABR (58.4%) by 7.3%, confirming robustness under longer incremental sequences.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in incremental object detection (IOD) that treats the detector monolithically, instead performing a component-level dissection of Faster R-CNN to localize catastrophic forgetting. Methodologically, it combines null-space gradient projection (Wang et al., 2021; Lu et al., 2024) for feature anti-drifting with a novel prototype-replay strategy targeting only the RoI Head classifier, while borrowing the pseudo-labeling strategy from BPF (Mo et al., 2024) to handle missing annotations. Unlike prior replay-based IOD methods (ABR, RODEO) that store full instances or compressed features, RePRE stores only a small number of coarse and fine-grained RoI feature prototypes per class, reducing storage while specifically addressing the identified forgetting locus.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that catastrophic forgetting in two-stage detectors is concentrated in the RoI Head classifier—while the RPN and regressor remain largely robust—provides a principled diagnostic that could simplify IOD method design and bridge incremental classification and detection research. However, the analysis is confined to Faster R-CNN on PASCAL VOC, leaving open whether the same component-level forgetting pattern holds for one-stage detectors (e.g., YOLO) or transformer-based detectors (e.g., DETR), where the architectural decomposition differs fundamentally. The modest performance gains (1–4% over BPF) and the reliance on pseudo-labeling as a baseline strategy also limit the generality of the conclusions.
