## Problem

Existing trajectory representation learning methods suffer from three limitations: they model trajectories from a single perspective (either free-space or road-network view), they are task-specific requiring separate models per downstream application, and none support online model updating despite the continuously evolving nature of urban trajectory data. GTR addresses all three gaps simultaneously with a unified pre-train-and-fine-tune framework.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252487/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes raw GPS trajectories that are first mapped onto a road network via map-matching (producing a road-network-constrained sequence of segments) and simultaneously projected onto a spatial grid partitioned by POI type labels from OpenStreetMap (producing a grid-constrained sequence). The output is a single low-dimensional trajectory embedding (768-d) that, after task-specific fine-tuning, supports six downstream tasks: similarity search, simplification, imputation, travel time estimation, classification, and generation. The ST-MoE gating mechanism uses a task ID as an additional input to dynamically reweight spatial versus temporal features at inference time.

## Method

GTR's architecture comprises three components. The Multi-View Encoder (MVE) produces four embedding streams: road embeddings via multi-head GAT over road segment features, grid embeddings augmented with POI type vectors, sinusoidal position embeddings, and multi-granularity time embeddings (minute/week/year), which are summed into a spatial representation Z_S. The Spatio-Temporal Fusion Pre-training (STP) module introduces a Spatio-Temporal Mixture of Experts (ST-MoE) with separate spatial and temporal experts (each a multi-head attention + residual block), whose outputs are combined via a task-ID-conditioned softmax gate to produce a fused representation; pre-training uses span masking (masking 30% of consecutive road segments) with cross-entropy loss plus a triplet loss where hard negatives are generated by a VAE. The Online Frozen-Hot Updating (OFU) strategy freezes the lower half of Transformer encoder layers to preserve historical trajectory knowledge while continuing to pre-train the upper half on streaming new data, with convergence guaranteed via a Lyapunov stability argument.

## Results

GTR achieves near-perfect trajectory similarity search with MR=1.0028 and HR@1=0.9974 on Porto (vs. next-best START at MR=1.0476, HR@1=0.9722) and MR=1.0130, HR@1=0.9906 on Beijing. On travel time estimation, GTR reduces MAE to 0.015 on Porto (a 96% improvement over START's 0.375) and 4.01 on Beijing. Across all six downstream tasks, GTR outperforms 15 baselines, with reported improvements of 15–60% on imputation, 10–90% on TTE, 37–81% on generation, and 4–8% on similarity search.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GTR outperforms all 15 baselines on every task it is evaluated on, with no losses reported. On trajectory similarity search, GTR (MR=1.0028, HR@1=0.9974 on Porto) surpasses the closest competitor START (MR=1.0476, HR@1=0.9722) by ~4% in HR@1, while JGRM degrades severely on Porto (HR@1=0.2326) and PIM/Toast struggle with large MR values (27.0/14.7). For travel time estimation, GTR's Porto MAE of 0.015 is an order of magnitude below START (0.375) and LightPath (0.681), and on Beijing GTR (MAE=4.01) beats LightPath (4.42) and START (5.50). On trajectory imputation, GTR (MAP=0.603 on Porto) leads MtrajRec (0.409) by 47% and Trajbert (0.378) by 60%, while Bi-STDDP and AttnMove fall further behind. For trajectory generation, GTR (DTW=0.0106 on Porto) outperforms ControlTraj (0.0358) by 70% and TS-TrajGen (0.2161) by 95%, with SeqGAN trailing by 97%. On classification, GTR (AUC=0.905 on Porto) edges out START (0.864) and JGRM (0.788), and on simplification GTR achieves the lowest PED compared to S3 and EB-OTS on both datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

GTR extends the BERT-style pre-train/fine-tune paradigm (Devlin et al., 2019) to trajectory representation learning, departing from prior single-view methods (Trembr, START, LightPath, JGRM) that model trajectories exclusively from either a free-space or road-network perspective. It introduces a Multi-View Encoder that jointly encodes road-network topology via GAT and free-space grid semantics via POI-augmented embeddings, then fuses them through a Spatio-Temporal Mixture of Experts (ST-MoE) module inspired by the broader MoE literature. The Online Frozen-Hot Updating (OFU) strategy, grounded in incremental learning theory with Lyapunov stability analysis, addresses a gap absent from all prior trajectory representation methods, which are strictly offline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GTR is the first trajectory representation framework to simultaneously support all six mainstream downstream tasks (similarity, simplification, imputation, TTE, classification, generation) and to enable online model updating, addressing practical deployment needs in dynamic urban environments. However, the substantial training cost (179 min vs. 5–118 min for baselines) and large parameter footprint (863 MB) limit immediate scalability, and the OFU strategy's theoretical grounding (Lyapunov analysis) remains somewhat informal. Open questions include whether the multi-view encoding and ST-MoE gains persist under stricter compute-matched comparisons, and the authors' stated future direction of integrating LLMs suggests the framework may evolve toward more general spatio-temporal reasoning.
