## Problem

Neural Theorem Provers (NTPs) suffer from poorly structured embedding spaces due to sparse gradient updates during soft unification, where only a small fraction of retrieved embeddings receive gradients per training step. While Knowledge Graph Embedding (KGE) methods learn well-structured representations through smooth triplet-level optimization, they lack the interpretability of symbolic reasoning. This paper addresses the gap by systematically integrating KGEs into the NTP framework to regularize the embedding space and reduce computational cost.

## Input / Output

The method operates on knowledge graph triplets (s, r, o) with predefined rule templates that define multi-hop proof paths (e.g., transitivity, symmetry). During training, each positive triplet is corrupted to generate negative samples (subject, object, or both replaced), and the model is trained with a combined NTP/KGE objective. During evaluation, given a query with one missing entity, the model steps through proof paths using top-k retrieval (via FAISS IndexFlatL2) or translational KGE operations, producing a ranking over all |E| entities with MRR and HITS@k metrics under the filtered setting.

## Method

The paper proposes four variants (CTP1–CTP4) that inject KGEs into specific modules of the Conditional Theorem Prover: (1) CTP1 adds KGE as an auxiliary loss term; (2) CTP2 blends the KGE score function into the unification score at every proof step via φ_mixed = λφ_NTP + (1−λ)φ_KGE; (3) CTP3 replaces top-k retrieval with translational KGE operations (e.g., TransE) to directly compute the next entity; (4) CTP4 replaces only the final ranking step with KGE scoring. CTP2 achieves the most consistent improvements because it regularizes each latent subject along the proof path, and a gradient analysis shows its cross-term structure reduces gradient noise variance by a factor of 4 compared to CTP1's independent self-terms. On large-scale datasets, CTP4 reduces evaluation time by over 1000× (942× on FB122, 1452× on WN18RR) with a mild accuracy trade-off, while CTP2 achieves SOTA MRR on Countries, Nations, UMLS, FB122, and CoDExS.

## Results

CTP2 (KGE score injected into NTP's unification) achieves the best overall performance among the four proposed variants, reaching 0.98 MRR on FB122 Test-II (vs. 0.94 for CTP, 0.92 for GNTP) and 0.68 MRR on FB122 Test-ALL. On WN18RR, CTP2 achieves 0.51 MRR, outperforming CTP (0.44) and GNTP (0.38) but trailing NBFNet (0.55) by 0.04 MRR. On CoDExS, CTP2 reaches 0.48 MRR, a substantial improvement over CTP (0.32) and GNTP (0.29). CTP4, which replaces the final proof step with KGE scoring, reduces evaluation time by 942× on FB122 and 1452× on WN18RR with a mild accuracy trade-off (0.61 vs. 0.68 MRR on FB122 Test-ALL).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CTP2 outperforms all NTP-based baselines (NTP, GNTP, CTP) by a large margin across nearly all datasets: on FB122 Test-II it gains +0.04 MRR over CTP and +0.06 over GNTP; on WN18RR it gains +0.07 over CTP and +0.13 over GNTP; on CoDExS it gains +0.16 over CTP. Against KGE baselines, CTP2 surpasses COMPLEX (0.41→0.51 MRR on WN18RR) and DistMult (0.46→0.51) on WN18RR, and DistMult (0.63→0.68) and COMPLEX (0.64→0.68) on FB122 Test-ALL. The only method that beats CTP2 is NBFNet on WN18RR (0.55 vs. 0.51 MRR), but NBFNet is 13× slower in inference (113 ms vs. 8.2 ms per sample). On small datasets (Kinship, Nations, UMLS), CTP2 achieves SOTA MRR of 0.71, 0.79, and 0.85 respectively, outperforming NEURALLP, MINERVA, and NBFNet. CTP4 trades some accuracy for extreme speed: it achieves 0.61 MRR on FB122 Test-ALL (vs. 0.68 for CTP2) but reduces evaluation time by over 1000×, making it practical for very large KGs where CTP's top-k retrieval becomes infeasible.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Neural Theorem Prover (NTP) lineage—specifically the Conditional Theorem Prover (CTP) framework—by systematically integrating Knowledge Graph Embedding (KGE) methods into the soft unification process. While the original NTP paper briefly mentioned KGE as an auxiliary loss, no subsequent NTP work (GNTP, CTP, DeepSoftLog) explored this direction. The paper departs from prior NTP optimization work (which focused on sparse gradients and differentiable semirings) by reframing the problem from an embedding-space perspective, arguing that NTPs' poorly structured embeddings—caused by sparse pairwise unification—are the root cause of optimization difficulty, and that KGEs' well-defined triplet scoring naturally regularizes this space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: CTP4 achieves over 1000× evaluation speedup on WN18RR, making NTP-based reasoning feasible on large-scale KGs where it was previously prohibitively slow. The embedding-space diagnosis (sparse pairwise scores, lack of global structure) provides a new lens for understanding why NTPs are hard to train, complementing the existing sparse-gradient narrative. However, the sparse gradient problem remains unresolved, and on large datasets the integrated embeddings still lack the clear global structure of pure KGE training, suggesting the integration is a partial fix. Open questions include whether the approach can leverage pre-trained foundation-model embeddings for zero-shot reasoning, and whether the accuracy-efficiency trade-off in CTP3/CTP4 can be further narrowed.
