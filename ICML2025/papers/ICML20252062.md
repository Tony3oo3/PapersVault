## Problem

Despite the proliferation of DPA loss variants (DPO, CPO, ORPO, SimPO, DPOP, etc.), there is no conceptual or technical framework for reasoning about their underlying semantics: what each loss actually constrains about model behavior, how losses relate to one another, or how to systematically derive new ones. This paper asks whether, given an existing DPA loss, one can derive a symbolic program that provably characterizes its semantics and can be compiled back into exactly that loss.

## Input / Output

The method takes a DPA loss function expressed as a core loss equation ρ_θ = log(ρ_θ^t / ρ_θ^b), where the numerator and denominator are disjoint multilinear polynomials over model prediction probabilities (e.g., P_θ(y_w|x), P_ref(y_l|x)). It strips away optimization details (convex function f, scaling β, length normalization) and produces a preference structure (P, P_C, P_A) — a triple of propositional formulas over weighted atomic predictions — whose weighted model counting ratio exactly recovers ρ_θ. The output is both a human-readable semantic description (e.g., 'if the loser is valid then the winner must be valid') and a mechanically compilable specification that reproduces the original loss under a generalized semantic loss.

## Method

The paper introduces a preference structure, a tuple (P, P_C, P_A) of propositional formulas that encodes winner/loser semantics via a core formula P, conditioning constraints P_C (restricting which truth assignments are counted), and additive constraints P_A (forcing certain assignments to always be counted). A key construction (Proposition 2) shows that any pair of formulas P_w and P_l can be expressed as a preference structure using the implication form P := P_l → P_w, P_C := P_w ∨ P_l, P_A := P_w ∧ P_l. The decompilation algorithm (Algorithm 1) compositionally translates each term in ρ_θ^t and ρ_θ^b into propositional logic via simple rules (multiplication → conjunction, subtraction from 1 → negation, addition → disjunction), then applies the implication construction to yield the preference structure. The generalized semantic loss ℓ_sl( P̄, θ, D) = E[f(log WMC(P̄_f;θ) / WMC(¬P̄_f;θ))] matches the DPA loss structure exactly, and the paper proves (Theorem 1) that the resulting preference structures correctly characterize all standard DPA losses. This reveals a lattice structure over the loss space via propositional entailment, with 4^(2^n) definable losses for n predictions, and enables systematic derivation of new losses by modifying conditioning constraints.

## Results

In a small-scale feasibility study using Qwen-0.5B tuned on UltraFeedback, the newly derived loss ℓ_cCPO achieves a 52.0% win-rate against the ℓ_CPO baseline, indicating comparable generation quality. In contrast, the unconstrained losses ℓ_unCPO (46.0%) and ℓ_cFUNL (46.1%) perform notably worse, consistent with the paper's theoretical prediction that less constrained losses are more susceptible to spurious alignment shortcuts (e.g., driving both winner and loser probabilities toward zero). Performance varies substantially across dataset subsets: ℓ_cCPO reaches 57.2% on flan and 53.1% on ultrachat, while ℓ_qFUNL drops to 34.7% on false-qa, suggesting that different semantic regions of the loss lattice suit different task types.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's sole empirical baseline is ℓ_CPO (Contrastive Preference Optimization, Xu et al. 2024), used as a reference for win-rate comparisons on UltraFeedback. The derived loss ℓ_cCPO matches ℓ_CPO in aggregate (52.0% win-rate, i.e., slightly above the 50% tie threshold), demonstrating that the formalize-and-refine procedure can produce empirically competitive losses. The unconstrained losses ℓ_unCPO and ℓ_cFUNL lose to ℓ_CPO by 4–6 percentage points in aggregate, validating the paper's theoretical claim that losses with fewer conditioning constraints are more prone to spurious behavior (driving both predictions to zero or one). Notably, ℓ_qFUNL shows extreme variance across subsets (34.7% on false-qa vs. 57.9% on flan), suggesting that a one-size-fits-all loss is suboptimal and that different semantic regions of the loss lattice may be better suited to different task types. The paper positions these results as a feasibility demonstration rather than a comprehensive empirical comparison, with the primary contribution being the theoretical formalization and the identification of ~4.3 billion definable DPA losses for future exploration.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the semantic loss framework of Xu et al. (2018) by introducing preference structures—a triple (P, P_C, P_A) of propositional formulas—that generalizes standard semantic loss to capture pairwise preference semantics. It departs from prior DPA analyses (Azar et al., 2024; Tang et al., 2024; Hu et al., 2024), which focus on optimization-theoretic properties, by instead performing a decompilation: translating known DPA losses (DPO, CPO, ORPO, SimPO, etc.) into symbolic logical formulas via weighted model counting. The approach draws on probabilistic logic programming (Manhaeve et al., 2018; Ahmed et al., 2022) but inverts the typical compilation direction, deriving logic from losses rather than losses from logic.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework reveals that DPA losses, despite looking algebraically distinct, share a common logical core (the implication 'if loser is valid then winner is valid') and differ primarily in conditioning constraints, providing a principled lens for understanding why certain losses succeed and others exhibit spurious behavior (e.g., driving both winner and loser probabilities to zero). The doubly exponential loss space (~4.3 billion for DPO) with a lattice structure offers a systematic search space for new losses, though the empirical validation is limited to a 0.5B model on a single dataset with modest win-rate differences. Open questions include whether semantic constrainedness predicts loss quality at scale, whether the lattice structure generalizes to online DPA variants, and whether the framework can guide loss selection for specific task types.
