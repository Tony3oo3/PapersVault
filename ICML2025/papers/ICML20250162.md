## Problem

State-space models (SSMs) consistently underperform Transformers on tasks requiring in-context retrieval, such as selecting the correct letter label in MMLU, but prior work treated this gap as a broad architectural limitation or studied it in simplified theoretical settings. This paper asks whether the Transformer-SSM retrieval gap can be traced to a small subset of specific heads rather than the entire model, and whether both architectures implement the same underlying retrieval mechanism.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250162/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The analysis operates on pretrained language models (Llama-3.1-8B, Falcon-Mamba-7B, Llamba-8B, Zamba-2-7B) and evaluates them on standard benchmarks (MMLU, GSM8K, BBH, ARC-Challenge) plus a synthetic key-value retrieval task where the model memorizes a dictionary of key-value pairs and must recall the value for a queried key. A notable detail is that the KV-retrieval prompt format matters: ending the query without a trailing space (e.g., "The value of the key 'scallops' is") produces sharp head-level retrieval signals with as few as 20–30 pairs, whereas adding a trailing space distributes the retrieval load across many heads and requires much longer contexts to observe the same effects.

## Method

The paper identifies a two-stage Gather-and-Aggregate (G&A) mechanism shared by both Transformers and SSMs: a Gather Head compresses each input segment (e.g., a multiple-choice option) into a single representative token, and a later Aggregate Head selectively attends to those summaries to extract the answer. They localize this mechanism by progressively pruning layers to find a minimal model where MMLU collapses, then ablating individual heads within the two critical layers to identify the specific Gather and Aggregate Heads (e.g., L16H22 and L17H24 in Llama-3.1-8B). Validation uses a synthetic KV-retrieval task and runtime masking that constrains heads to only attend within their hypothesized role, confirming the G&A interpretation across all four architectures. The key finding distinguishing SSMs is that their Aggregate Heads produce smoother, less sharply localized attention patterns due to the continuous hidden-state recurrence, requiring more heads to match the performance of fewer, stronger Transformer heads. Hybrid replacement experiments—swapping a single SSM layer (L17) with its attention counterpart in a distilled Llamba-8B—boost MMLU from 33% to 50%, confirming that the bottleneck is specifically the Aggregate Head's inability to perform sharp, selective aggregation in SSMs.

## Results

Disabling a single critical Gather or Aggregate Head in Llama-3.1-8B drops MMLU accuracy from 66.3% to 24.4%, while knowledge-task accuracy remains stable at ~39%. In Llamba-8B (Mamba-2 SSM), removing two critical heads drops MMLU from 58.9% to 29.8%, and in Falcon-Mamba-7B (Mamba-1), removing four channels drops MMLU from 52.3% to 25.0%. Across broader ablations (Table 4), Llama-8B loses 43% MMLU accuracy after removing 30 G&A heads (68.1%→38.7%), while Llamba-8B retains 96% of its MMLU score after the same number of removals (60.7%→58.1%), revealing that SSMs distribute retrieval across many weaker heads rather than concentrating it in a few strong ones.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three architectural families: Transformer (Llama-3.1-8B), pure SSM (Llamba-8B, Falcon-Mamba-7B), and hybrid (Zamba2-7B). On MMLU, the Transformer shows the sharpest retrieval bottleneck: a single head (L17H24) accounts for the difference between 66.3% and 24.4% accuracy, while knowledge tasks remain at ~39% regardless. The Mamba-2 SSM (Llamba-8B) requires two heads to reach 58.9% MMLU and degrades gradually—retaining 96% of MMLU after 30 head removals—indicating lower per-head expressiveness and redundancy. The Mamba-1 SSM (Falcon-Mamba-7B) needs four channels for 52.3% MMLU, further confirming weaker per-head capacity. The hybrid Zamba2-7B delegates aggregation to its shared attention layers: ablating just nine attention heads drops MMLU from 65.1% to 37.7% (−42%), while knowledge stays at 67.0%, showing that SSM layers alone cannot sustain strong aggregation. On retrieval-heavy benchmarks (GSM8K, BBH, LAMBADA), Transformers and hybrids suffer large drops with G&A removal, while pure SSMs show minimal change—confirming the gap is architectural and localized to a few heads rather than a general modeling deficiency.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the mechanistic interpretability line of work on retrieval heads—Lieberum et al. (2023) identified Content Gatherer and Correct Letter heads in Chinchilla, and Wu et al. (2024) showed fewer than 5% of heads are critical for retrieval—by demonstrating that the same two-stage Gather-and-Aggregate mechanism emerges in SSM-based models despite their lack of explicit attention. It builds on the MOHAWK distillation framework (Bick et al., 2024) to create Llamba-8B as a controlled SSM testbed, enabling direct comparison with its Transformer teacher. The key departure from prior work is the claim that the Transformer-SSM performance gap is not a fundamental architectural limitation but is localized to a small subset of heads implementing G&A, reframing the gap as a targeted functional difference rather than a broad modeling inadequacy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that the Transformer-SSM retrieval gap concentrates in just a few heads provides a practical recipe for hybrid model design: place attention layers where G&A heads naturally emerge (mid-network when training from scratch, or retain them at specific layers when distilling). This also reframes MMLU as primarily a retrieval benchmark rather than a knowledge benchmark, with implications for how practitioners interpret model scores. Open questions include whether G&A is part of a broader retrieval process involving other head types (e.g., Amplification Heads), how the mechanism scales to in-context learning and copying tasks, and whether optimal attention placement in hybrids can be further refined.
