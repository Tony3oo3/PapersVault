## Problem

Existing algorithms for Monitored MDPs (Mon-MDPs), where reward observability is governed by a separate monitor process, suffer from three critical limitations: they explore uniformly without exploiting the two-component (environment + monitor) structure, they cannot leverage a known monitor model, and they provide only asymptotic guarantees that fail for 'unsolvable' Mon-MDPs where some rewards are permanently unobservable. No prior work offers finite-sample complexity bounds or a principled way to handle the tension between exploring to discover observable rewards and acting pessimistically toward permanently hidden ones.

## Input / Output

At each timestep the agent observes a joint state (environment state s^e, monitor state s^m) and a proxy reward R̂^e that is either the true environment reward or the symbol ⊥ (unobserved), plus the monitor reward R^m. The agent selects a joint action (a^e, a^m) from the product action space. The output is a policy over joint actions that is minimax-optimal: in solvable Mon-MDPs this coincides with the standard optimal policy, while in unsolvable Mon-MDPs it is the optimal policy of the worst-case Mon-MDP where all never-observable rewards are set to their minimum. The algorithm operates on tabular (finite) state and action spaces with bounded rewards in [-1, 1].

## Method

Monitored MBIE-EB extends MBIE-EB with three innovations. First, it constructs separate confidence-interval bonuses for the environment mean reward, monitor mean reward, and joint transition dynamics, exploiting the Mon-MDP product structure. Second, for environment state-actions whose reward has never been observed (N(s^e,a^e)=0), it replaces the optimistic bonus with a pessimistic floor r_min^e, ensuring the agent avoids permanently hidden negative rewards. Third, to counteract the excessive pessimism that would prevent the agent from ever discovering stochastically observable rewards, it interleaves a second MBIE-EB instance whose reward model uses a KL-UCB upper-confidence bound on the probability of observing the reward, driving targeted exploration of unobserved state-actions. The algorithm switches between 'observe episodes' (KL-UCB-guided) and 'optimize episodes' (pessimistic-optimistic hybrid) according to a schedule κ*(k), and the paper proves the first polynomial sample-complexity bound for Mon-MDPs, with dependence on ρ^{-1} (the minimum non-zero reward-observation probability) shown to be essentially tight.

## Results

Across 48 benchmark environments (spanning 9 grid-world domains paired with 8 monitor types), Monitored MBIE-EB outperforms Directed-E² in 43 of 48 benchmarks and performs on par in the remaining 5. In the unsolvable Bottleneck environment with a 5% Button Monitor, Monitored MBIE-EB converges to the minimax-optimal policy while Directed-E² fails to learn to avoid never-observable (⊥) states. When the monitor model is provided in advance, Monitored MBIE-EB requires approximately 30% fewer steps to reach the minimax-optimal policy. The theoretical sample complexity bound scales as Õ(|S||A|H / (ε³(1−γ)⁵ρ)), where ρ is the minimum non-zero probability of observing an environment reward.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is Directed-E² (Parisi et al., 2024a), the prior state-of-the-art for Mon-MDPs. Monitored MBIE-EB wins in 43 of 48 benchmarks and ties in 5, with the largest gaps appearing in hard-exploration tasks (River Swim) and unsolvable Mon-MDPs (Bottleneck with non-ergodic monitors). In unsolvable settings, Directed-E² with its recommended random initialization never converges to the minimax-optimal policy because it retains its initial (random) estimate for never-observable rewards; only a pessimistic initialization (r₀ = −10) recovers minimax-optimality asymptotically, and even then its performance degrades as the monitor state-action space grows (e.g., N-Experts with N=4 vs. Ask with N=1). Monitored MBIE-EB, by contrast, is robust to the monitor space size and does not require special initialization. The paper also shows that Directed-E²'s dependence on ρ⁻¹ is empirically more severe than Monitored MBIE-EB's, as learning slows dramatically for Directed-E² when reward observability probability drops to 5%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends MBIE-EB (Strehl & Littman, 2008), a model-based interval estimation algorithm for classical MDPs, to the recently introduced Mon-MDP framework (Parisi et al., 2024b) where reward observability is governed by a separate Markovian monitor. It departs from the prior Mon-MDP algorithm Directed-E² (Parisi et al., 2024a) in three key ways: exploiting the joint environment-monitor structure with component-specific confidence bonuses, replacing optimism with pessimism for never-observable rewards to achieve minimax-optimality, and introducing a KL-UCB-driven observe episode to balance exploration against premature pessimism. The paper also provides the first finite-sample complexity bound for Mon-MDPs, a gap left by the asymptotic-only guarantees of prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first algorithm with polynomial sample complexity for Mon-MDPs that handles both solvable and unsolvable instances, and the first to leverage a known monitor model for accelerated learning. The ρ^{-1} dependence in the bound is shown to be essentially tight via a lower bound, giving practitioners a clear picture of how observability difficulty scales. Limitations include reliance on explicit state-action counts (restricting to enumerable spaces), the heuristic observe/optimize scheduling, and the need to solve MDPs via value iteration each episode. Open questions include whether a single unified model can simultaneously optimize for exploration and minimax-optimality, and whether pseudocount-based methods can extend the approach to continuous or high-dimensional spaces.
