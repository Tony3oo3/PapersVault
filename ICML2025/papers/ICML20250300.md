## Problem

Prior counterfactual identification results establish identifiability only for specific counterfactual effects or outcomes under constrained structures or mechanisms, leaving the strongest form—complete L3-identifiability, where all SCMs satisfying the assumptions agree on every counterfactual statement—largely unaddressed. This paper tackles that gap by introducing exogenous isomorphism as a tractable, model-based sufficient condition for L3-identifiability, and by characterizing exactly which structural assumptions (bijectivity, triangular monotonicity, Markovianity) are needed to guarantee it.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an observational dataset of endogenous variable samples drawn from P_V, a known total causal order over the variables, and arbitrary counterfactual queries specifying interventions on subsets of variables. It outputs a fully parameterized neural TM-SCM whose solution mapping is a bijective triangular monotonic map, enabling evaluation of any L3 quantity (conjunctions of potential outcomes under multiple interventions) via the change-of-variables formula. Unlike prior approaches that identify a single counterfactual effect, the learned model answers all counterfactual queries consistently, with the causal order and TM-SCM structure serving as hard architectural constraints rather than soft regularizers.

## Method

The core theoretical contribution is exogenous isomorphism (EI), an equivalence relation between SCMs requiring component-wise bijective maps on exogenous variables that preserve both the exogenous distribution and each causal mechanism; Theorem 3.2 proves EI implies L3-identifiability for arbitrary recursive SCMs. For Bijective SCMs, EI-identifiability reduces to fixing the counterfactual transport (Theorem 4.6), and for the more practical TM-SCM subclass, Corollary 5.4 shows that Markovianity plus a known causal order plus the observational distribution suffice, because triangular monotonicity forces all counterfactual transports to be TMI mappings that coincide with the (almost surely unique) KR transport. The neural implementation instantiates four prototype architectures—DNME (diagonal Jacobian), TNME (lower-triangular Jacobian), CMSM (composed normalizing-flow solution map), and TVSM (neural ODE with triangular velocity field)—all trained by maximum likelihood via the change-of-variables formula, with MAF-based exogenous distributions to satisfy Markovianity without constraining their form.

## Results

On ER-DIAG-50 and ER-TRIL-50, the four neural TM-SCM prototypes achieve CTF_RMSE of 0.37–0.53 (ER-DIAG-50) and 0.42–0.55 (ER-TRIL-50), with CMSM performing best (0.37±0.05 and 0.42±0.12). Ablating the causal order assumption (w/o O) causes the most severe degradation, e.g., TNME jumps from 0.47 to 11.24 on ER-DIAG-50, confirming the necessity of the correct causal ordering. Violating Markovianity (w/o M) or triangular monotonicity (w/o T) also consistently increases CTF_RMSE, validating each assumption in Corollary 5.4.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not compare against external baselines; instead, it ablates each theoretical assumption in Corollary 5.4 to demonstrate its necessity. The causal order assumption (A_≤) is the most critical: reversing it (w/o O) causes catastrophic failure for TNME (CTF_RMSE 0.47→11.24 on ER-DIAG-50) and substantial degradation for CMSM (0.37→2.64) and TVSM (0.46→0.79). The Markovianity assumption (A_M) is the second most important, with w/o M increasing CTF_RMSE by roughly 30–100% across methods (e.g., CMSM 0.37→1.69 on ER-DIAG-50). The triangular monotonicity assumption (A_TM-SCM), testable only for CMSM and TVSM, also matters: w/o T raises CTF_RMSE from 0.37 to 0.64 (CMSM) and from 0.46 to 0.67 (TVSM) on ER-DIAG-50. Among the four prototypes, CMSM (composed normalizing-flow solution mapping) consistently achieves the lowest CTF_RMSE, while DNME (diagonal noise mechanism) is the weakest, suggesting that richer solution-mapping parameterizations better approximate the true TM structure.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself within the Pearl Causal Hierarchy framework (Bareinboim et al., 2022) and recursive SCM foundations (Bongers et al., 2016), introducing exogenous isomorphism (EI) as a new equivalence relation that sits strictly between full model recovery (=identifiability) and counterfactual equivalence (full exogenous distribution recovery). The TM-SCM identifiability result (Corollary 5.4) explicitly unifies and generalizes prior counterfactual identifiability theorems from Lu et al. (2020), Nasr-Esfahany et al. (2023), and Scetbon et al. (2024), showing them to be special cases. On the practical side, the four neural TM-SCM prototypes (DNME, TNME, CMSM, TVSM) extend scalar-case models like LSNM, FiP, CausalNF, and CFM to the vector-valued setting, leveraging KR transport (Santambrogio 2015) and TMI mapping theory (Jaini et al., 2019) as the theoretical bridge.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the strongest available identifiability guarantee (full L3, i.e., all counterfactual statements simultaneously) for a concrete and implementable class of models, giving practitioners a clear checklist of assumptions (TM-SCM structure, known causal order, Markovianity, observational distribution) under which neural SCMs yield theoretically consistent counterfactual answers. A key limitation is that the TM-SCM requirement (triangular monotonic mechanisms) is a strong structural constraint that may not hold in many real-world systems, and verifying it in practice remains challenging. Open questions include whether the EI framework extends beyond TM-SCMs to more general bijective SCMs with unknown transport, whether the assumptions can be relaxed (e.g., partially known causal order, non-Markovian exogenous variables), and how to learn or verify the TM structure from data without already knowing it.
