## Problem

Protein structure tokenization (PST) methods encode 3D structures into discrete or continuous representations, but their capabilities and limitations remain poorly understood due to the absence of a unified evaluation framework that examines fine-grained local substructures rather than only global structures. Additionally, leading VQ-VAE-based PSTs suffer from severe codebook underutilization (e.g., up to 70% of ESM3's 4096 codes remain inactive), a manifestation of codebook collapse that limits representational capacity.

## Input / Output

The method consumes protein backbone 3D coordinates (L × 4 × 3 for N, Cα, C, O atoms) and residue sequences, which are converted into per-residue SE(3) frames with 16 nearest-neighbor frames and relative positional encodings to capture local backbone geometry. Outputs include discrete structural tokens (codebook indices from a K=512 × D=1024 codebook), their corresponding latent vectors as discrete structural representations, and reconstructed 3D coordinates. The benchmark evaluates these representations across 12 supervised tasks (24 test splits) using remote-homologous data splits to assess out-of-distribution generalization.

## Method

AminoAseed is a VQ-VAE-based PST built on ESM3's local frame paradigm, introducing two key techniques to address codebook collapse. First, codebook reparameterization replaces the standard learnable codebook with Q = Linear(C), where C is a fixed randomly-initialized orthogonal basis; this ensures the entire codebook receives gradient updates through the linear layer rather than only the subset of codes selected during quantization, preventing the distribution shift that causes collapse. Second, a Pareto-optimal codebook configuration strategy uses scaling experiments to balance codebook size K and dimension D under a fixed capacity constraint (K×D), identifying K=512, D=1024 as optimal for maximizing token diversity while minimizing redundancy. The encoder uses geometric self-attention layers for rotation and translation invariance, the decoder uses bidirectional transformer blocks, and training integrates commitment/quantization losses with five ESM3 reconstruction losses (geometric distance/direction, binned classification, and inverse-folding cross-entropy). The paper also introduces StructTokenBench, a four-perspective evaluation framework (Downstream Effectiveness, Sensitivity, Distinctiveness, Codebook Utilization Efficiency) spanning 10 datasets and 17 tasks, which reveals that no single PST dominates all perspectives and that reconstruction quality does not consistently correlate with codebook quality.

## Results

AminoAseed achieves a +6.31% average relative improvement over ESM3 across all 24 supervised task splits (72.43% avg AUROC for functional sites, 38.08% avg Spearman's ρ for physicochemical properties, 46.01% avg Macro F1 for structural properties). On sensitivity, AminoAseed outperforms ESM3 by +12.83% average relative gain (e.g., 75.89% Spearman's ρ on Fold Switching vs. 62.23% for ESM3). For codebook utilization, AminoAseed achieves a 124.03% relative improvement in utilization rate over ESM3 (64.45% UR on CASP14 vs. 27.60%), while maintaining the same codebook capacity (K×D). IF-based PSTs (ProteinMPNN, MIF) still lead on functional and physicochemical tasks but lag significantly on structural property prediction and sensitivity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AminoAseed outperforms ESM3 (the leading VQ-VAE-based PST) by +4.74% on functional site prediction, −0.27% (comparable) on physicochemical properties, and +27.31% on structural property prediction, with a +6.31% overall average gain. Against FoldSeek (K=20, D=2), AminoAseed achieves substantially higher downstream performance (72.43% vs. 51.90% avg AUROC) while maintaining competitive codebook utilization (64.45% vs. 99.00% UR). ProTokens shows moderate downstream effectiveness (65.37% avg AUROC) and the best sensitivity among existing VQ-VAE methods, but AminoAseed surpasses it on all four benchmark perspectives. IF-based PSTs (ProteinMPNN, MIF) lead on functional and physicochemical tasks (75.92–79.82% avg AUROC, 52.73–54.41% avg ρ) but are outperformed by AminoAseed on structural property prediction (46.01% vs. 39.94–43.55% Macro F1) and sensitivity (65.39% vs. 50.57–51.41% avg Spearman's ρ). The ablated VanillaVQ (same architecture without AminoAseed's codebook reparameterization) lags ESM3 by −5.61% overall, confirming that the codebook strategy—not the encoder architecture—drives the improvement.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the VQ-VAE-based protein structure tokenization line (FoldSeek, ProTokens, ESM3) and the inverse-folding-based line (ProteinMPNN, MIF), positioning itself as the first unified evaluation framework (StructTokenBench) for these methods. It departs from prior PST work by shifting focus from global structure reconstruction to fine-grained local substructure quality, and by identifying codebook collapse as the central bottleneck rather than representation format. The proposed AminoAseed method reparameterizes the codebook as a learnable linear transformation over a fixed orthogonal basis (Q = Linear(C)), a targeted fix for the gradient-update asymmetry that causes codebook collapse in standard VQ-VAEs, and pairs it with a data-driven Pareto-optimal codebook size/dimension trade-off under a fixed capacity constraint.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

StructTokenBench fills a critical gap by providing the first multi-perspective evaluation (downstream effectiveness, sensitivity, distinctiveness, codebook utilization) for PST methods, revealing that no single tokenizer dominates and that reconstruction quality—a metric ESM3 and others rely on—does not correlate with codebook quality. The finding that ~70% of ESM3's 4096 codes are inactive during inference, and that a simple reparameterization recovers 124% utilization, suggests that much of the apparent performance gap between IF-based and VQ-VAE-based PSTs is an optimization artifact rather than a fundamental limitation of discrete representations. Open questions include whether the codebook reparameterization generalizes to all-atom or multi-chain structures, whether the Pareto-optimal codebook configuration holds at larger scales, and whether the sub-exponential scaling behavior of VQ-VAE encoders can be overcome with architectural changes beyond simple capacity increases.
