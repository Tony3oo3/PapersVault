## Problem

Rotary Position Encoding (RoPE) relies on fixed, block-diagonal 2D rotation matrices designed for 1D sequential data, making it suboptimal for capturing spatial relationships in 2D images and 3D videos where distant pixels may have strong correlations. The sparse, handcrafted basis functions in RoPE constrain the model's ability to learn complex multi-dimensional spatial dependencies, and the exponential decay in frequencies is poorly suited to grid-structured visual data.

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard ViT patch tokenizations: 2D images (32×32 for CIFAR-100, 224×224 for ImageNet) and 3D video clips (8×224×224 for UCF101), where each patch receives an n-dimensional position vector (n=2 or 3). A synthetic spatial reasoning task additionally uses 108×108 images with a 9×9 grid of arrows and letters. The output is a standard class label, and the method requires no architectural changes beyond modifying the attention inner product with the learned rotation matrices.

## Method

LieRE replaces RoPE's fixed 2D block rotations with learned dense skew-symmetric matrices (Lie algebra elements) that are mapped to high-dimensional rotation matrices via the matrix exponential. Specifically, it learns a basis of skew-symmetric matrices {A_i} for i ∈ [n], computes R(p) = exp(Σ p_i A_i) for each n-dimensional position p, and applies R(p_i) to both keys and queries so that the attention inner product naturally encodes relative positions through R(p_i)^T R(p_j). The block-diagonal structure of the basis matrices controls capacity: block size 2 recovers RoPE-Mixed, block size 8 is the empirical sweet spot, and block size 64 yields a fully dense basis. This adds only 580k parameters (0.68%) to a ViT-B model, and the key insight is that learning in the Lie algebra (skew-symmetric space) sidesteps the difficulty of learning directly on the rotation matrix manifold while the exponential map guarantees valid orthogonal rotations.

## Results

On CIFAR-100, LieRE₈ achieves 70.3% top-1 accuracy, outperforming absolute positional encodings (63.9%), VisionLLaMA RoPE (65.5%), and RoPE-Mixed (68.8%). On ImageNet-1k, LieRE₈ reaches 69.6% top-1 accuracy versus 68.8% for RoPE-Mixed and 66.1% for absolute encodings. On the 3D video classification benchmark UCF101, LieRE₈ achieves 47.0% top-1 accuracy, a 15.1% relative improvement over absolute encodings (40.9%) and a 1.5% absolute gain over RoPE-Mixed (46.3%). All improvements over RoPE-Mixed are statistically significant (p < 0.01 on all three datasets).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LieRE₈ consistently outperforms all three baselines across all three benchmarks. Against the DeiT absolute positional encoding baseline, LieRE₈ achieves a 10.0% relative improvement on CIFAR-100 (70.3% vs. 63.9%), a 5.3% relative gain on ImageNet-1k (69.6% vs. 66.1%), and a 15.1% relative gain on UCF101 (47.0% vs. 40.9%). Against VisionLLaMA RoPE, LieRE₈ leads by 4.8 points on CIFAR-100, 4.2 points on ImageNet-1k, and 2.0 points on UCF101. Against the closest competitor RoPE-Mixed, LieRE₈ gains 1.5 points on CIFAR-100 (70.3% vs. 68.8%), 0.8 points on ImageNet-1k (69.6% vs. 68.8%), and 0.7 points on UCF101 (47.0% vs. 46.3%), with all differences statistically significant (p-values ranging from 1.3×10⁻⁵ to 7.1×10⁻⁴). Notably, LieRE₆₄ (fully dense basis) underperforms LieRE₈ on UCF101 (44.7% vs. 47.0%), suggesting that the 8×8 block-diagonal structure provides a beneficial inductive bias for 3D data.

## Relation to Prior Work

LieRE generalizes Rotary Position Encoding (RoPE) and its vision adaptations (RoPE-Mixed, VisionLLaMA) by replacing their fixed, block-diagonal 2D rotation matrices with learned dense skew-symmetric matrices mapped to the full rotation group via the matrix exponential. Conceptually, this moves from the commutative subgroup of block-diagonal rotations (where exp(U−V) = exp(U)exp(V)⁻¹ holds exactly) to the full Lie group, trading that algebraic convenience for greater representational capacity in encoding multi-dimensional spatial relationships. The paper also connects to the broader equivariant networks literature, noting that LieRE's rotation-based encoding is composable with equivariance constraints, and to the RoPE context-extension line of work, though it addresses a different axis of generalization (dimensionality rather than sequence length).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LieRE offers a clean, minimally invasive (0.68% parameter overhead for ViT-B) drop-in replacement for RoPE in vision transformers that consistently improves accuracy across 2D and 3D benchmarks and notably generalizes better to unseen resolutions, a practical concern for deployment. The synthetic spatial reasoning diagnostic—where GPT-4, Claude Sonnet 3.5, and Gemini Pro 1.5 all fail a simple arrow-direction task that LieRE-based ViTs solve near-perfectly—highlights that relative positional encoding is a specific bottleneck for spatial understanding in current models. Open questions include whether the approach extends to SE(3) pose encoding for robotics, whether the matrix exponential can be replaced by cheaper approximations without accuracy loss, and whether the 8×8 block-size sweet spot observed here holds at larger scales or in 1D language settings where LieRE provably reduces to RoPE.
