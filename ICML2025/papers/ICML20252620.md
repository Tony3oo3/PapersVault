## Problem

Existing cascade ranking training methods either optimize each stage in isolation or use interaction-aware paradigms that fail to align individual stage objectives with the global end-to-end recall goal. No prior approach simultaneously addresses both the misalignment between stage-level losses and the system-level top-k recall target, and the inability of independently trained stages to learn effective bidirectional collaboration patterns.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252620/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes full-stage training samples constructed by downsampling items from all cascade stages (e.g., retrieval, pre-ranking, ranking) for each impression, with labels determined jointly by stage order and within-stage rank. The retrieval and ranking models (DSSM and DIN respectively) take user and item features as input and produce scalar scores; the cascade system then sequentially filters a large candidate inventory down to a final top-k exposed set. Training uses a two-stage setup where the retrieval model selects top-30 and the ranking model selects top-20, with 10 ground-truth items per impression.

## Method

LCRON formulates cascade ranking training as optimizing the joint survival probability of ground-truth items through all stages, using differentiable sorting (NeuralSort) to produce soft permutation matrices that yield differentiable top-k selection probabilities. The core loss L_e2e is a cross-entropy on the product of per-stage selection probabilities (a provable lower bound of the true survival probability), directly aligning training with the global recall objective while enabling bidirectional collaboration across stages. An auxiliary per-stage loss L_single, applied over the full soft permutation matrix (not just top-m rows as in ARF), tightens the theoretical bound by enforcing consistency between stage outputs and provides additional supervision when survival probabilities are near zero. The two loss types are combined via Uncertainty Weighted Loss (UWL) with learnable scalars to reduce hyperparameter tuning, and the entire multi-stage system is trained end-to-end as a unified network with parameter-isolated stage models.

## Results

On the RecFlow public benchmark (two-stage cascade ranking), LCRON achieves a Joint Recall@10@20 of 0.8732±0.0005, outperforming all baselines including FS-LambdaLoss (0.8666), ARF-v2 (0.8678), and RankFlow (0.8647). In the online A/B test on Kuaishou's advertising system, LCRON delivers a 4.10% increase in advertising revenue and 1.60% increase in user conversions over the FS-LambdaLoss baseline, compared to +1.66% and +0.65% for ARF-v2. LCRON also demonstrates superior streaming performance, with a widening gap over baselines in the later phase of evaluation, and scales to three-stage cascade ranking (Joint Recall@10@20 = 0.7390 vs. 0.7332 for ARF-v2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LCRON achieves the best Joint Recall@10@20 (0.8732) against all six baselines, with the closest competitor being ARF-v2 (0.8678, +0.54% gap) and FS-LambdaLoss (0.8666, +0.66% gap). LCRON also leads on Ranking Recall@10@20 (0.8729) across all methods. However, LCRON does not dominate every individual-stage metric: FS-LambdaLoss edges it on Ranking NDCG@10 (0.7306 vs. 0.7291) and Retrieval NDCG@10 (0.7190 vs. 0.7151), while BCE slightly leads on Retrieval Recall@10@30 (0.9706 vs. 0.9700). The paper argues this trade-off is expected and desirable, as LCRON optimizes the joint end-to-end objective rather than per-stage metrics in isolation. In the online A/B test, LCRON's 4.10% revenue lift substantially exceeds ARF-v2's 1.66%, confirming that the joint-optimization advantage translates to real business impact. FS-RankNet performs notably worse than FS-LambdaLoss under the same full-stage sample organization (0.7881 vs. 0.8666 Joint Recall), highlighting that the choice of LTR loss within the FS-LTR framework is critical.

## Relation to Prior Work

LCRON builds directly on the interaction-aware cascade ranking training line (ICC → RankFlow → FS-LTR) and the differentiable-sorting-based Recall optimization of ARF, but is the first to simultaneously address both objective misalignment (each stage's loss being stricter than the global top-k recall goal) and the absence of bidirectional stage collaboration. Its technical foundation rests on NeuralSort/SoftSort soft permutation matrices and UWL for loss balancing, while its key departure is the derivation of a lower bound on the joint survival probability (L_e2e) and the auxiliary single-stage loss (L_single) that tightens this bound by enforcing cross-stage consistency.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a theoretically grounded, end-to-end training recipe for multi-stage cascade ranking that is already deployed at Kuaishou's advertising platform, yielding a 4.1% revenue lift over the prior best method. Key open questions include: (1) the inability to use the true cascade quotas (q_i) in L_e2e due to gradient conflicts in the soft permutation matrix, which limits physical interpretability; (2) whether UWL's Gaussian assumption holds broadly across model capacities and data complexities; and (3) whether O(n) differentiable top-k operators could replace the current O(n²) sorting-based formulation. The finding that joint performance can improve without dominating any individual stage metric also suggests that stage-level optimization is a red herring for cascade systems.
