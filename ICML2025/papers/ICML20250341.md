## Problem

Existing time series forecasting (TSF) foundation models follow two roads—repurposing LLMs (text-based) or training on large-scale time series corpora (TS-based)—but both suffer from severe modality gaps or in-domain heterogeneity. This paper asks whether a visual masked autoencoder pre-trained on ImageNet can serve as a zero-shot TSF foundation model without any time-series-specific training, exploiting the intrinsic similarities between pixel arrays and temporal sequences.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 1D univariate lookback window of length L and produces a forecast of length H. The input is segmented by periodicity P into a 2D matrix of shape P × ⌊L/P⌋, instance-normalized with a scaling constant r=0.4, rendered as a 3-channel grayscale image, and bilinearly resampled to a fixed 224×224 size matching MAE's pre-training resolution. The forecast is extracted by reversing the pipeline: resizing the reconstructed image back, averaging channels, de-normalizing, and flattening. Multivariate forecasting uses channel independence, processing each variable separately.

## Method

VISIONTS reformulates TSF as a patch-level image reconstruction task by aligning the lookback window with visible patches and the forecast horizon with masked patches in a pre-trained MAE (ViT-Base). The key alignment step resizes the segmented time-series image so that the number of visible patches n = ⌊c·N·L/(L+H)⌋ (with c=0.4) matches the ratio of context to prediction length, while the remaining N×(N−n) patches are masked for MAE to reconstruct. The c=0.4 factor further reduces visible width to approximate MAE's 75% masking ratio during pre-training, improving zero-shot transfer. No time-series-specific training is required for zero-shot use; for full-shot, only layer normalization parameters are fine-tuned for a single epoch while freezing all other MAE weights. The approach achieves O(1) inference cost with respect to context length because the input is always encoded into a fixed-size image.

## Results

On the long-term TSF benchmark (6 datasets, averaged over prediction lengths {96, 192, 336, 720}), VISIONTS achieves an average MSE of 0.309 and MAE of 0.345 in the zero-shot setting, outperforming MOIRAI_S (MSE 0.327) by ~6% relative reduction and matching MOIRAI_B (MSE 0.310). It wins 7 out of 14 dataset-metric combinations and surpasses all few-shot text-based baselines (TimeLLM, GPT4TS) and no-pretrain baselines (DLinear, PatchTST, TimesNet, Autoformer, Informer) by 8–84% relative MSE reduction. On the GIFT-Eval leaderboard (23 datasets, 144K time series), VISIONTS ranks first in normalized MASE among zero-shot foundation models. On the 29-dataset Monash benchmark, VISIONTS surpasses all individually trained models and LLMTime, placing second only to MOIRAI which was pre-trained on those datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VISIONTS (zero-shot, image-pretrained MAE) outperforms MOIRAI_S by ~6% relative MSE reduction on average and is comparable to MOIRAI_B (0.309 vs. 0.310 avg MSE), while MOIRAI_L actually underperforms both. Against text-based few-shot baselines, VISIONTS achieves 8–84% relative MSE reduction over TimeLLM (0.336) and GPT4TS (0.360), and 25–84% over no-pretrain models like DLinear (0.407), PatchTST (0.378), TimesNet (0.491), Autoformer (0.678), and Informer (1.904). VISIONTS wins 7 of 14 individual dataset-metric cells, losing primarily on Electricity and Weather where MOIRAI_B/L and TimeLLM/GPT4TS edge it out. On the GIFT-Eval leaderboard, VISIONTS ranks first in normalized MASE among zero-shot models, and on Monash it surpasses all 15 individually trained baselines (Naive, SES, Theta, TBATS, ETS, ARIMA, PR, CatBoost, FFNN, DeepAR, N-BEATS, WaveNet, Transformer) plus LLMTime, placing second only to MOIRAI which was pre-trained on those same datasets. The paper also shows VISIONTS outperforms TimesFM (avg MAE 0.30 vs. 0.36) and LLMTime (0.30 vs. 0.45) in zero-shot MAE, and that replacing the MAE backbone with LaMa yields performance comparable to MOIRAI, confirming the gain comes from image-time-series similarity rather than MAE-specific architecture.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself as a 'third road' to TSF foundation models, distinct from text-based approaches (TimeLLM, GPT4TS) that repurpose LLMs and TS-based approaches (MOIRAI, TimesFM) that train on large time-series corpora. It builds directly on MAE (He et al., 2022), a visual masked autoencoder pre-trained on ImageNet, and draws conceptual inspiration from prompt tuning in NLP (BERT's [mask] token) to reformulate forecasting as patch-level image reconstruction. While prior work has encoded time series as images for CNN-based classification or forecasting (Wang & Oates, 2015; Li et al., 2020), this paper is the first to show that a pre-trained visual foundation model, without any time-series-specific adaptation, can match or surpass purpose-built TSF foundation models in zero-shot settings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that images and time series occupy overlapping representation spaces in a visual encoder (shown via t-SNE) suggests a deeper structural similarity between the two modalities, opening a new axis for cross-modal research between CV and TSF. Key limitations remain: VISIONTS cannot capture inter-variable interactions (due to limited image channels) or perform distributional forecasting, and the zero-shot gains over MOIRAI_Base are modest. Future directions include leveraging diffusion models or more advanced inpainting architectures, and developing model modifications to handle multivariate and probabilistic forecasting within the visual framework.
