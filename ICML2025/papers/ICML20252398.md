## Problem

Unstructured sparse training methods like RigL achieve near-dense accuracy but fail to deliver practical speedups on modern GPUs due to irregular memory access patterns. Existing structured sparsity approaches are hardware-friendly yet suffer significant accuracy degradation at high sparsity levels, and many break their hardware-friendly patterns during backpropagation when weight matrices are transposed. The paper addresses this gap by introducing a structured sparsity pattern that is both transposition-invariant and dynamically optimizable.

## Input / Output

The method trains ViTs (Base, Large, Huge), MLP-Mixers, and GPT-2 (Small, Medium) from scratch on RGB images (224×224 for ImageNet-1K, 32×32 for CIFAR-10/100) and text sequences (WikiText-103), producing class labels or next-token predictions respectively. The key structural constraint is that each linear layer's weight matrix is parameterized as a sum of K permutation-diagonal products, where K is determined by the target sparsity level (60%–99.99%), and the active diagonals are dynamically selected per training step via a differentiable TopK gate.

## Method

DynaDiag represents each weight matrix W ∈ R^{M×N} as W_K = Σ_{j=1}^{K} P_j diag(V_j), where P_j are permutation matrices encoding diagonal positions (offsets) and V_j are learnable value vectors. A differentiable softmax-based TopK function with cosine-annealed temperature selects the K most important diagonals from a learnable importance vector α, enabling end-to-end optimization of both diagonal positions and values. The diagonal pattern is provably transposition-invariant (Appendix A), so the same BCSR conversion accelerates both forward and backward passes. Custom CUDA kernels convert the diagonal pattern to Block Compressed Sparse Row format and exploit Tensor Core mma.m16n8k16 operations, yielding up to 3.13× inference and 1.59× training speedups at 90% sparsity on ViT-Base. An ℓ₁ regularization on α and a compute-fraction-based per-layer sparsity budget complete the training recipe.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K, DynaDiag achieves 76.91% Top-1 accuracy on ViT-B/16 at 90% sparsity, the best among all structured DST methods (vs. SRigL 75.90%, PixelatedBFly 73.89%, DSB 72.89%), and is statistically indistinguishable from unstructured RigL (77.24%). At 90% sparsity on ViT-L/16, DynaDiag reaches 77.74%, outperforming the next-best structured method (SRigL) by 2.28%. The method delivers up to 3.13× inference speedup and 1.59× training speedup over dense equivalents at 90% sparsity on GPU. On WikiText-103, DynaDiag achieves 56.33 PPL on GPT-2-Small at 90% sparsity, beating SRigL (62.55) and PixelatedBFly (66.44) while remaining within 2.57 PPL of RigL (53.76).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DynaDiag consistently outperforms all structured DST baselines (SRigL, PixelatedBFly, DSB, DiagHeur) across every sparsity level and model size, with the gap widening at higher sparsities—e.g., at 90% on ViT-L/16, DynaDiag (77.74%) beats SRigL (75.46%) by 2.28% and PixelatedBFly (75.12%) by 2.62%. Against unstructured methods (RigL, SET, CHT, CHTs, MEST), DynaDiag is statistically indistinguishable at 60–80% sparsity on ViT-B/16 but trails RigL by ~0.3–0.7% at 90–95%; however, at extreme sparsity (99.99%), DynaDiag surpasses RigL. On language modeling (GPT-2-Small, WikiText-103), DynaDiag beats SRigL by 6.22 PPL at 90% sparsity and PixelatedBFly by 10.11 PPL, while remaining within 2.57 PPL of RigL. The only method that outperforms DynaDiag on PPL is the pruning-based Wanda (53.07 vs. 56.33 at 90%), but Wanda requires dense pre-training plus fine-tuning, making it computationally more expensive than DynaDiag's sparse-to-sparse training. In terms of hardware speedup, DynaDiag achieves 3.13× inference and 1.59× training speedup at 90% sparsity, whereas SRigL only accelerates inference (not training) and DSB/PixelatedBFly offer comparable or lower speedups with significantly worse accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

DynaDiag extends the Dynamic Sparse Training (DST) lineage initiated by SET, RigL, and Top-KAST, but departs from prior structured DST methods (SRigL, Pixelated Butterfly, DSB) in two key ways: it introduces a diagonal sparsity pattern inspired by small-world networks that preserves its structure under transposition (unlike N:M patterns that break in backpropagation), and it uses a differentiable TopK mechanism to dynamically select which diagonals are active, enabling true sparse-to-sparse training. The GPU acceleration strategy—converting diagonal matrices to BCSR format for both forward and backward passes—builds on the SmaT library's Jaccard-based blocking but adds a diagonal-proximity term to exploit the known structure. The work also connects to the CHT/CHTs line of brain-inspired sparse training by demonstrating that small-world connectivity properties emerge naturally in the learned diagonal patterns.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first structured DST method to deliver meaningful training speedup (1.59×) alongside inference speedup (3.13×) at 90% sparsity while matching unstructured methods like RigL in accuracy, addressing a long-standing gap where structured sparsity either sacrificed accuracy or failed to accelerate backpropagation. The practical impact is significant for deploying large ViTs and LLMs on resource-constrained hardware, as the method reduces both parameter count and compute without a dense-training prerequisite. Key limitations include scalability challenges with CNNs (per-channel diagonal search overhead), untested performance beyond 99.99% sparsity, and the observation that surpassing RigL at ≥80% sparsity requires a small LoRA-FA fine-tuning step that adds ~1.67% parameters, partially eroding the sparsity benefit. Open questions include whether Triton-based kernel optimizations can close the remaining gap to theoretical speedups, and whether the small-world diagonal pattern generalizes to architectures with non-square weight matrices or multi-head attention patterns.
