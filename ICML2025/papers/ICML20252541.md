## Problem

Existing LLM-based Lean4 theorem proving methods follow two incompatible paradigms: tree-search (which provides per-step FL verification but lacks high-level NL planning) and whole-proof generation (which leverages NL reasoning but receives no intermediate verifier feedback). No prior framework effectively balances natural-language reasoning with formal-language verifiability, and the extreme scarcity of NL-FL aligned Long CoT annotation data has prevented models from developing deep, structured formal reasoning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252541/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The prover model receives a natural-language theorem statement and a Lean4 formal statement, and produces a complete Lean4 proof wrapped in a structured <Thought>/<Output> Long CoT format where the thought section contains NL analysis, tactic planning, and code drafting. The corrector model additionally receives the incorrect proof and Lean4 executor error messages, which are embedded within its Long CoT to guide re-analysis and revised proof generation. The iterative loop continues until the Lean4 verifier accepts the proof or a maximum retry limit is reached, with each correction round costing roughly half the compute of a full proof generation.

## Method

MA-LoT separates the cognitive workload into a prover (whole-proof generation with emergent NL planning in Long CoT) and a corrector (error analysis and proof revision based on Lean4 feedback in Long CoT), both instantiated from the same LoT-Solver model. The key training innovation is LoT-Transfer Learning (LoT-TL), a three-stage pipeline that first trains general NL Long CoT reasoning on OpenO1-SFT-Pro, then fine-tunes on Lean4 SFT data (LoT-ProveData) and correction data (LoT-CorrectionData) while the system prompt explicitly instructs the model to solve WITHOUT Long CoT, using a placeholder sentence to occupy the CoT slot. This causes Lean4-specific Long CoT reasoning to emerge as a latent capability that activates only when the inference-time system prompt switches to 'WITH Long CoT', eliminating the need for any explicitly annotated formal Long CoT data. The curriculum learning strategy and the structured interaction between LLM and Lean4 verifier within the Long CoT loop enable the model to perform self-reflection, re-plan proof strategies, and iteratively refine proofs based on formal verification signals.

## Results

On the MiniF2F-Test benchmark, MA-LoT (Godel-Prover base) achieves 61.07% accuracy with a 16+8×2 sampling budget, while the cumulative result across all LoT models reaches 63.93%. The DeepSeek-Prover-based MA-LoT variant achieves 54.51% on MiniF2F-Test and 64.34% on MiniF2F-Valid. The LoT-Solver whole-proof generation alone (pass@128) reaches 52.05% on MiniF2F-Test, and the Long CoT efficiency study shows MA-LoT outperforms the base model by 4.93% despite using a smaller effective sampling budget.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MA-LoT (Godel-Prover base, 61.07%) outperforms all 13 baselines on MiniF2F-Test. Among tree-search methods, it surpasses the strongest baseline InternLM2.5-StepProver (50.7%) by 10.37%, Lean-STaR (46.3%) by 14.77%, and Expert Iteration (36.6%) by 24.47%. Among whole-proof generation methods, it beats the closest competitors STP-Lean (56.15%) by 4.92% and Godel-Prover (55.33%) by 5.74%, and substantially outperforms DeepSeek-Prover-v1.5-RL (48.36%) by 12.71%, TheoremLlama (35.66%) by 25.41%, and DeepSeek-V3 (33.61%) by 27.46%. Notably, the 7B MA-LoT model also surpasses the 685B DeepSeek-V3 by 27.46% and the >1T GPT-4-Turbo by 38.12%, demonstrating that the model-collaboration Long CoT framework provides a significant advantage over raw model scale. On the ProofNet benchmark, MA-LoT (Godel) achieves 15.47% versus Godel-Prover's 12.15%, confirming the method's effectiveness beyond MiniF2F.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously competing paradigms in LLM-based Lean4 theorem proving—tree-search (step-by-step tactic generation with executor feedback) and whole-proof generation (single-pass NL-guided proof writing)—into a model-collaboration framework where a prover handles high-level NL planning and a corrector performs fine-grained FL error analysis, both operating within a Long Chain-of-Thought structure inherited from OpenAI's O1. The LoT-Transfer Learning pipeline is the key methodological departure: rather than requiring expensive Long CoT annotations for formal reasoning, it uses system-prompt toggling (WITH/WITHOUT Long CoT) during SFT to make formal Long CoT an emergent capability, building on TheoremLlama's curriculum learning and DeepSeek-Prover/Godel-Prover as base models. This positions the work as a synthesis that explicitly addresses the NL-reasoning-vs-FL-verifiability tension that neither prior branch resolves alone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 61.07% MiniF2F-Test result (vs. 55.33% for Godel-Prover and 50.70% for InternLM-Step-Prover) demonstrates that structured model collaboration with Long CoT can outperform both single-model strategies, and the LoT-TL pipeline offers a general recipe for injecting domain-specific Long CoT into LLMs without RL or bespoke annotation—potentially applicable beyond Lean4. However, evaluation is limited to MiniF2F (244 problems) and a small-scale ProofNet check, leaving open whether the gains persist on harder or larger benchmarks. The 'emergent' Long CoT claim rests on a single prompt toggle and modest ablation deltas (~2.5%), so the mechanism by which formal reasoning truly emerges remains under-explained, and the framework's overhead (33% more tokens, 70% more GPU hours) may limit practical adoption at scale.
