## Problem

Existing LM-based diagnosis prediction methods capture semantic similarity of medical concepts but fail to encode their hierarchical structures (e.g., ontology parent-child relations and EHR-driven visit-to-concept containment). This limits prediction accuracy, particularly in few-shot settings where structural priors are most valuable for disambiguating related diagnoses.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252325/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a patient's sequence of EHR visits (each containing a set of ICD diagnoses and CCS codes), a predefined medical ontology hierarchy graph with directed parent-child edges, and pre-computed BioBERT embeddings indexed by clinical concept names. It produces multi-label CCS code predictions for the next visit via a volume-based similarity score between the patient's box and each candidate CCS code box, normalized to probabilities through softmax. All box embeddings are 16-dimensional hyperrectangles parameterized by a center and a non-negative offset vector.

## Method

BoxLM represents every medical entity (diagnosis, CCS code, visit, patient) as a box embedding (center + offset) in a 16-dimensional space. The structure-semantic fusion module sets box centers from BioBERT embeddings passed through an MLP, while box offsets are learned via relation-aware graph convolutional networks over the ontology hierarchy, encoding parent-child inclusion as relative box sizes. Visit boxes aggregate their constituent concept boxes using attention-weighted centers and element-wise maximum offsets, and patient boxes are built by an evolve-and-memorize mechanism: the center is a temporally-weighted sum of historical visit centers (weights from a learned MLP on time intervals), while the offset is the element-wise maximum across all visits. Diagnosis prediction computes the expected intersection volume between the patient box and each CCS code box using Gumbel-approximated MaxGumbel/MinGumbel corner distributions and a Bessel-function-based volume formula, which avoids gradient vanishing for disjoint boxes. Training uses binary cross-entropy loss over the log-volume similarity scores for multi-label CCS code classification.

## Results

On MIMIC-III (5% training data), BoxLM achieves 43.88% Visit-Level P@10 and 31.74% Code-Level Acc@10, outperforming the second-best method BoxCare (38.21% P@10, 28.12% Acc@10) by 14.84% and 12.87% respectively. On the larger MIMIC-IV dataset, BoxLM reaches 42.04% P@10 and 29.94% Acc@10, improving over BoxCare by 19.67% and 17.04%. Across all four metrics on both datasets, BoxLM consistently leads, with relative improvements of 11–20% over the runner-up. The advantage is particularly pronounced in few-shot settings (1–5% training data), where BoxLM with only 15% data surpasses BoxCare trained on 100% data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BoxLM wins across all 13 baselines on every metric for both datasets. Against the strongest hierarchy-aware baseline BoxCare (which also uses box embeddings but only for ontology hierarchies), BoxLM gains an average of 16.70% at the visit level and 13.96% at the code level, attributed to the additional EHR-driven hierarchy modeling and PLM semantic fusion. Among temporal-aware methods, BoxLM surpasses TRANS by up to 46.48% in P@10 on MIMIC-IV (42.04% vs. 28.70%) and RETAIN by 25.73% in P@10 on MIMIC-IV (42.04% vs. 33.47%), demonstrating that box-based temporal modeling via the evolve-and-memorize mechanism outperforms RNN/transformer sequence approaches. Against semantic-aware baselines, BoxLM achieves an average 28.76% improvement over methods like BioBERT (18.81% P@10 on MIMIC-IV) and VecoCare (29.17% P@10), confirming that vector-only representations are insufficient for capturing hierarchical inclusion relations. The gap widens on MIMIC-IV (37,917 unique diagnoses vs. 3,874 on MIMIC-III), where structural complexity amplifies the benefit of box embeddings. In few-shot settings (1% training data), BoxLM already outperforms BoxCare trained on 100% data, highlighting its data efficiency.

## Relation to Prior Work

BoxLM extends BoxCare (Lv et al., 2024), which first applied box embeddings to model ontology-driven disease hierarchies for diagnosis prediction, by adding EHR-driven visit hierarchies and fusing PLM-based semantic embeddings (BioBERT) into the box centers. The Gumbel-distribution-based intersection volume computation is directly inherited from GumbelBox (Dasgupta et al., 2020), adapted here for patient-to-CCS-code matching. Conceptually, the paper unifies three previously separate threads in EHR diagnosis prediction—temporal modeling (RETAIN, TRANS), hierarchy-aware representation (KAME, HiTANet, BoxCare), and semantic-aware PLM encoding (BioBERT, VecoCare)—into a single geometric framework where structure and semantics coexist in the same box embedding space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in the few-shot regime: BoxLM with 15% training data outperforms BoxCare with 100% data, which is directly relevant to privacy-constrained clinical deployments where patient-specific EHR data is scarce. The box-embedding formulation also offers geometric interpretability (inclusion, intersection) that vector-based methods lack, and the Consistency@k metric validates that predictions respect clinical hierarchy. Open questions include whether the frozen BioBERT embeddings can be replaced by fine-tuned or larger medical LLMs, whether the approach generalizes to tasks beyond CCS-code prediction (medication, imaging), and how it scales to the full ICD-10 taxonomy rather than the 285–842 CCS codes used here.
