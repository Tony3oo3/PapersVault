## Problem

Autonomous agents typically model human partners as noisy-rational actors, but humans systematically exhibit risk-sensitive biases (risk-seeking or risk-averse) that violate rationality axioms, degrading team coordination in risky settings. Existing approaches to personalized risk-sensitive modeling either require costly human data-priors or rely on tabular methods infeasible for complex tasks, leaving a gap for zero-shot adaptation to unknown partner risk-sensitivity in high-dimensional multi-agent environments.

## Input / Output

The agent consumes a lossless joint state encoding (agent positions, orientations, held objects, all counter objects, and pot statuses; 41–68 dimensions depending on layout) plus the partner's last 10 observed actions, and selects from a discrete set of three pre-trained CPT-conditioned candidate policies (risk-seeking, risk-neutral, risk-averse). It outputs its own action drawn from the inferred best-matching candidate policy and a Bayesian belief distribution over which candidate policy the partner is following. Unusually, the replay memory stores the full set of possible next states with their transition probabilities rather than a single observed transition, enabling direct CPT computation over the prospect distribution at update time.

## Method

The RS-ToM framework has three components: (1) Cumulative Prospect Theory replaces the standard expected-value TD-target, treating the set of possible next-state rewards {τᵢ, pᵢ} as a prospect distribution evaluated via CPT's utility and probability weighting functions; (2) a Deep MARSRL algorithm based on DDQN that uses a level-k Quantal Response Equilibrium to solve the single-stage common-payoff game at each transition, combined with a novel replay memory storing all transition prospects (s, a, r, s′) to amortize the expensive CPT computation, plus curriculum learning and reward shaping for sparse rewards and self-play for symmetric roles; (3) a Bayesian inference step that updates a belief over the space of pre-trained candidate policies using the partner's last 10 observations with decaying likelihood weights, after which the agent discretely adopts the most probable candidate policy. The paper proves convergence by showing the CPT-modified Bellman operator is a contraction under a conservative upper-bound on the loss-aversion parameter ℓ, and introduces the Risky Overcooked benchmark (extending Overcooked with probabilistic puddle states) to evaluate coordination under two framings: risk/rely and risk/detour.

## Results

Across both Risky Overcooked layouts (RCR and RMP), RS-ToM performs nearly identically to the Oracle baseline (which knows the partner's true risk-sensitivity), while significantly outperforming the noisy-rational (RAT) assumption. Team performance (Σr) improves by +77% in RCR and +189% in RMP over RAT, with the largest single gain being +273% in RMP with a Seeking partner. Coordination fluency (C-ACT) improves by 104% on average, and partner predictability (P_H) more than doubles (+121%) with RS-ToM. Risk-taking alignment (N_risk) matches Oracle values, whereas RAT systematically misaligns (decreasing risk with Seeking partners and increasing it with Averse partners).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares RS-ToM against two baselines: an Oracle (ground-truth partner risk-sensitivity known) and a noisy-rational (RAT) agent that assumes the partner is rational. RS-ToM matches or nearly matches Oracle on all four metrics across all four experimental conditions (e.g., Σr of 150 vs. 151 in RCR-Seeking; P_H of 0.76 vs. 0.76 in RCR-Seeking), confirming that the Bayesian inference step correctly identifies the partner's risk-sensitivity within a few observations. Against RAT, RS-ToM wins decisively on every metric: team reward improves by +53% to +273% depending on layout and partner type, coordination fluency by +44% to +150%, and predictability by +81% to +188%. The one exception is N_risk in RCR with a Seeking partner, where RAT (26.96) slightly exceeds RS-ToM (25.93) and Oracle (25.60), attributed to discoordination in a confined space requiring backtracking into puddles. All main effects and interaction effects in the 2×2 ANOVA were significant (p<0.02), with only five non-significant post-hoc comparisons noted.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of CPT-based risk-sensitive reinforcement learning (Danis et al., 2023; Ramasubramanian et al., 2021; Prashanth et al., 2016) from tabular, low-dimensional settings to deep multi-agent RL in complex tasks, and departs from prior personalized risk-sensitive models (Kwon et al., 2020; Sun et al., 2019; Cheng et al., 2023) by eliminating the need for costly human data-priors through a forward approach of pre-training candidate policies over a CPT parameter space. The multi-agent solution leverages QRE (McKelvey & Palfrey, 1995) as an efficient equilibrium approximation, and the deep learning backbone follows DDQN (van Hasselt et al., 2016). The Risky Overcooked benchmark extends the Overcooked environment (Carroll et al., 2019) by introducing explicit environmental risk (puddle states) that elicit divergent risk-sensitive strategies, filling a gap in standardized risky-coordination platforms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The RS-ToM framework addresses a practical limitation in human-autonomy teaming: the standard noisy-rationality assumption fails when humans exhibit risk-sensitive biases, and prior personalization methods require expensive data collection. The near-Oracle performance of a simple 3-candidate Bayesian inference over 10 observations suggests that even lightweight online adaptation can substantially close the coordination gap. Key limitations include reliance on a discrete set of candidate policies (limiting resolution for continuous-control tasks), validation only with artificial partners, and the assumption that risk-sensitivity is stable within an interaction. Open questions include whether the framework generalizes to context-dependent shifts in individual risk-sensitivity, how it scales to more than three candidate policies or continuous action spaces, and whether the simulated gains translate to perceived trustworthiness and team performance with real humans.
