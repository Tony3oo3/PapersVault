## Problem

Deep learning-based MRI reconstruction methods have largely overlooked noise propagation analysis, despite its critical role in quantifying solution fidelity and guiding algorithm design. While classical parallel imaging (SENSE, GRAPPA) maintained rigorous noise characterizations via g-factors, the nonlinear complexity of DL networks makes analytical noise propagation intractable, leaving a gap in spatially resolved uncertainty quantification for accelerated MRI.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250474/cropped_page5_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a trained DL reconstruction network (any differentiable architecture), the linear imaging operator A (encoding coil sensitivities, Fourier encoding, and undersampling mask), and a k-space noise covariance matrix Σ_k estimated from the outermost 5% of k-space points. It produces a voxel-wise noise variance map of dimension n (matching the image size, e.g., 320×256 for knee or 384×384 for brain), where each entry quantifies the variance of the reconstructed voxel due to acquisition noise. The framework is agnostic to the specific network architecture and undersampling pattern, requiring only that the network be differentiable and the forward operator be known.

## Method

The paper derives that the image-domain noise covariance is Σ_x = J_f A^H Σ_k A J_f^H, where J_f is the network Jacobian—too large to store explicitly. To circumvent this, they prove an unbiased diagonal estimator (Theorem 3.1): for a random vector v with E[v]=0 and E[vv^H]=I, the quantity (Σv)⊙v* has expectation equal to diag(Σ). Combined with a Cholesky factorization Σ_k = σ_k σ_k^H, this reduces the problem to computing J_f applied to random vectors (via Jacobian-vector products) rather than forming the full Jacobian. The algorithm generates S complex random-phase (unit-magnitude, uniform-phase) probing vectors, transforms them through σ_k and A^H, applies J_f via JVPs, takes the Hadamard product with the conjugate, and averages to obtain the variance map. Complex random-phase vectors are shown to yield strictly lower estimator variance than complex Gaussian vectors, and the approach reduces computation from ~54s (Monte Carlo) to ~1.3s per slice while cutting memory from ~3.5GB to ~1MB.

## Results

The proposed Jacobian sketching method achieves near-perfect agreement with the empirical Monte Carlo reference (N=3,000 trials) across six DL reconstruction architectures on both knee and brain datasets at R=8, α=1, with average PCC of 99.8% and average NRMSE of 0.8%. The best-performing methods (E2E-VarNet, SSDU, VORTEX, N2R) achieve PCC ≥ 99.9% and NRMSE ≤ 0.7% on both datasets, while U-Net (lacking iterative data-consistency) shows slightly higher NRMSE of 1.7–1.8%. Computationally, the proposed method reduces per-slice runtime from 54.0 s (empirical) to 1.3 s and memory from 3,513 MB to 1.1 MB, while the naive row-by-row Jacobian approach takes 880.2 s.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares the proposed Jacobian sketching estimator against two baselines: (1) an empirical Monte Carlo reference using N=3,000 noisy reconstructions per slice, and (2) a naive per-voxel variance calculation that explicitly computes each row of the network Jacobian. Against the empirical reference, the proposed method matches with 99.8% average PCC and 0.8% average NRMSE across all six architectures and both datasets, with no statistically significant difference in noise distributions (Wilcoxon p>0.05). The only notable deviation is U-Net, which yields 1.7–1.8% NRMSE due to its single-pass nonlinear denoising lacking iterative data-consistency, making the first-order Taylor approximation less accurate. Against the naive row-by-row Jacobian approach, the proposed method achieves a 677× speedup (1.3 s vs. 880.2 s per slice) with identical memory usage (1.1 MB), while the empirical baseline requires 3,513 MB of storage. The method also generalizes robustly across acceleration factors R∈{4,8,12,16,24}, undersampling patterns (1D/2D uniform, random, Poisson-disc), and noise levels α up to 50×, maintaining close agreement with empirical references in all clinically relevant regimes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes Jacobian sketching—a technique previously applied in ML for optimization (stochastic quasi-gradients) and continual learning (catastrophic forgetting mitigation)—toward a fundamentally different objective: efficient noise propagation analysis in nonlinear multi-coil MRI reconstructions. Theoretically, it extends the classical stochastic diagonal estimation framework of Hutchinson (1990) and Bekas et al. (2007) to the complex-valued, multi-coil setting, proving an unbiased estimator for the diagonal of the image covariance matrix via random-phase vectors. It departs from the dominant MC-simulation paradigm in MRI noise analysis and from architecture-specific analytical solutions (e.g., Dawood et al. 2024 for k-space interpolation networks) by providing a model-agnostic, first-principles derivation that handles both MRI physics and DL nonlinearities simultaneously.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper reestablishes spatially resolved noise quantification as a practical, routine component of DL-MRI pipelines—something that has been largely overlooked in favor of global metrics like PSNR/SSIM. The 40× speedup and 3000× memory reduction over MC make voxel-wise variance maps feasible for large 3D/4D volumes and clinical deployment, enabling downstream applications like adaptive acquisition, ROI-specific quality assessment, and noise-aware segmentation. Key limitations include the first-order Taylor approximation (which degrades at extreme noise levels beyond ~50× scaling) and the restriction to diagonal (voxel-wise) variance, leaving off-diagonal cross-covariance structure unexplored. The framework is explicitly noted as generalizable to CT, PET, and broader image-to-image tasks, but these extensions remain future work.
