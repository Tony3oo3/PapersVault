## Problem

Existing AI-based peer review systems produce homogenized, text-only feedback that lacks domain-specific depth and ignores visual elements like figures and layout. This limits their usefulness for researchers seeking actionable, diverse critiques before submission, as a single LLM prompt cannot replicate the varied expertise and multi-perspective evaluation that real human reviewers bring.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251641/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system consumes the paper's title, abstract, and introduction as plain text, a 3×3 thumbnail grid of the full document (chosen to minimize visual token cost while preserving layout information), and a pre-built shared memory pool of 28,372 historical papers with 43,954 clustered keywords. It outputs a structured JSON containing a strengths list, a weaknesses list, and an overall evaluation with a 0–10 score and a binary Accept/Reject recommendation, mirroring the standardized format of the Reviews-STD dataset.

## Method

Agent Reviewers is a training-free, prompt-engineered multi-agent pipeline with four roles: a meta-reviewer extracts k keywords from the paper and retrieves top-k₁ keywords plus top-k₂ papers from a RAG-based Shared Memory Pool (SMP) to initialize k domain-specific reviewers; a multimodal reviewer evaluates the 3×3 thumbnail for layout, figure quality, and visual presentation; the domain-specific reviewers produce initial reviews, then revise them in a discussion phase where each reads the others' feedback; and an Area Chair agent synthesizes all revised reviews into the final decision. The SMP is built via a two-stage keyword aggregation pipeline (NLTK stemming followed by DBSCAN clustering on all-mpnet-base-v2 embeddings of keyword descriptions) and is updated after each review. The key design insight is that equipping each reviewer with distinct, retrieved domain knowledge from historical papers—rather than relying on a single LLM's internal knowledge—combined with multimodal assessment and iterative peer discussion, yields substantially more diverse and accurate reviews than single-agent or text-only baselines.

## Results

On 300 ICLR 2024 papers, Agent Reviewers achieves a total F1-score of 0.462 and Jaccard of 0.315 for strengths-weaknesses alignment, outperforming the second-best method (AI Scientist: F1 0.426, Jaccard 0.285) by 8.5% and 10.5% respectively. For acceptance decision prediction, Agent Reviewers attains F1-score 0.566, MCC 0.220, Balanced Accuracy 0.613, and G-mean 0.612, surpassing the next-best method (AgentReview Top-4) by 35.7%, 78.9%, 11.7%, and 18.8% respectively. The system also operates at a cost of $0.65 per 100 papers, substantially lower than AgentReview ($7.53) while delivering superior quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against AgentReview (Jin et al., 2024b), Agent Reviewers wins decisively on every metric: F1-score for strengths-weaknesses improves from 0.340 to 0.462 (+36%), Jaccard from 0.215 to 0.315 (+47%), and decision F1 from 0.417 to 0.566 (+36%), while reducing cost by 91% ($0.65 vs. $7.53 per 100 papers). Against AI Scientist (Lu et al., 2024), Agent Reviewers leads on strengths-weaknesses quality (F1 0.462 vs. 0.426; Jaccard 0.315 vs. 0.285) and dominates on decision prediction (F1 0.566 vs. 0.049; MCC 0.220 vs. 0.123), though AI Scientist is slightly cheaper ($3.59 vs. $0.65). Against LLM Review (Liang et al., 2024), Agent Reviewers achieves higher strengths-weaknesses F1 (0.462 vs. 0.420) and Jaccard (0.315 vs. 0.275); LLM Review does not offer an acceptance decision, so no decision-level comparison is possible, and it is the cheapest method ($0.13) but provides the least structured output.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper synthesizes three previously separate research threads—LLM-based automated peer review (Reviewer2, SEA, AgentReview, AI Scientist), multi-agent LLM collaboration (AgentCoder, Generative Agents), and visual paper assessment ('paper gestalt' by Von Bearnensquash and Huang)—into a unified multi-agent architecture. Unlike prior single-agent or prompt-only review systems, it introduces a RAG-based shared memory pool that initializes domain-specific reviewers with retrieved historical paper metadata, and a dedicated multimodal agent that evaluates 3×3 paper thumbnails. The key conceptual departure is treating reviewer expertise as externally retrievable domain knowledge rather than relying solely on the LLM's internal parameters, and integrating visual layout assessment as a first-class input rather than an afterthought.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Reviews-STD dataset (38,312 papers, 144,027 standardized reviews) is the largest standardized peer-review resource to date and provides a practical benchmark for the community. The shared memory pool concept—where reviewer agents are grounded in retrieved domain-specific paper histories—offers a training-free mechanism to reduce the homogenization problem that plagues current LLM reviewers, and the ablation showing a 38% relative drop in decision accuracy without SMP underscores its importance. Open questions include generalization beyond AI conferences, whether the simple keyword-based retrieval can be replaced by richer RAG or online search, and whether fine-tuning individual agents (meta-reviewer, area chair) could yield further gains. The system's low cost ($0.65/100 papers) makes it a practical pre-submission feedback tool, though it explicitly does not replace human reviewers.
