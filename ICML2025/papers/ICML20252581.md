## Problem

Existing demonstration selection methods for in-context learning rely on fixed similarity-based or heuristic strategies that prioritize relevance while neglecting diversity in label coverage, producing biased demonstration sets that overfit to narrow patterns and generalize poorly in few-shot settings. The paper addresses the gap of jointly and dynamically optimizing both relevance and diversity through an adaptive, task-aware selection policy rather than static or purely similarity-driven criteria.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252581/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Given a test input text and a knowledge base of candidate demonstrations (ranging from ~9K to ~20K entries across the four classification datasets), the method sequentially selects k demonstrations to construct a prompt for a frozen LLM. The internal state representation encodes the input via TF-IDF vectors, aggregates embeddings of previously selected examples, tracks one-hot prediction history, and monitors normalized label diversity (|L(E_t)|/k). The output is the selected demonstration set paired with the LLM's predicted class label, with an optional Chain-of-Thought reasoning chain in the RDES/C variant.

## Method

RDES formulates demonstration selection as a finite-horizon Markov Decision Process where an RL agent sequentially picks examples from the knowledge base, guided by a reward function that combines prediction accuracy (indicator of correct label) and diversity gain (change in normalized label coverage, weighted by an exponentially annealing coefficient λ that prioritizes early exploration). Two RL algorithms are deployed: tabular Q-learning with ε-greedy exploration for smaller discretizable state spaces, and a PPO-based actor-critic with neural policy and value networks for high-dimensional settings, using a clipped surrogate objective with GAE advantage estimates and an entropy bonus. The state embedding concatenates TF-IDF features of the input, aggregated embeddings of selected demonstrations, one-hot prediction history, and the current diversity score, giving the agent sufficient context for informed sequential selection. An RDES/C variant integrates Chain-of-Thought prompting by marginalizing over reasoning chains, further boosting predictive accuracy on both classification and complex reasoning benchmarks.

## Results

On four text classification benchmarks (BANKING77, CLINC150, HWU64, LIU54) evaluated across 14 LLMs, RDES/C (Q-learning + CoT) achieves average accuracies of 0.838, 0.902, 0.872, and 0.824 on closed-source models, and 0.845, 0.731, 0.853, and 0.786 on open-source models. The peak single-model result is 0.961 accuracy on CLINC150 with Doubao-pro-4k. On challenging reasoning benchmarks (BigBenchHard, GSM-8K, SST5), RDES/C achieves 0.99 on boolean expressions and 0.73 on GSM-8K with DeepSeek-R1-32B, while RDES/PPO reaches 1.00 on boolean expressions and 0.94 on GSM-8K with Qwen-2.5-72B.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RDES/C (Q-learning + CoT) outperforms all ten baselines on every closed-source model average: it beats the strongest baseline ADA by +27.3 pp on BANKING77 (0.838 vs 0.565), +12.2 pp on CLINC150 (0.902 vs 0.780), +14.2 pp on HWU64 (0.872 vs 0.730), and +23.4 pp on LIU54 (0.824 vs 0.590). Among prompt engineering baselines, CoT and KP are the strongest (averaging 0.513 and 0.497 on HWU64 respectively), yet both trail RDES/C by 35+ points. Among demonstration selection baselines, ADA is consistently the best (0.565–0.780 across datasets), followed by FSC and AES, while RDS and AES are weakest on several datasets. On open-source models, RDES/C wins on BANKING77 (0.845 vs ADA 0.752), HWU64 (0.853 vs 0.734), and LIU54 (0.786 vs 0.779), but RDES/B (without CoT) edges out on CLINC150 (0.800 vs ADA 0.763 and RDES/C 0.731), suggesting CoT integration is not universally beneficial for smaller open-source models. On the challenging reasoning benchmarks, RDES/C achieves the highest accuracy on BigBenchHard boolean expressions (0.99) and GSM-8K (0.73) with DeepSeek-R1-32B, while ADA leads on GSM-8K (0.98) and BigBenchHard web-of-lies (0.83) with Qwen-2.5-72B, indicating that the PPO variant (RDES/PPO, 0.94 on GSM-8K) and ADA remain competitive on the most complex tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

RDES extends the RL-based demonstration selection line initiated by Zhang et al. (2022, AES) and Scarlatos & Lan (2023, RetICL), both of which framed example selection as a sequential decision problem but optimized primarily for relevance or uncertainty. The key conceptual departure is the explicit incorporation of a label-distribution diversity score into the reward function, creating a dual relevance-diversity objective that prior RL methods did not jointly optimize. RDES also introduces a PPO-based variant for high-dimensional state spaces and integrates Chain-of-Thought prompting as a complementary reasoning layer, positioning the work at the intersection of ICL, demonstration selection, and RL-based post-training without modifying model weights.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The consistent 15–25 point accuracy gains over the strongest baselines (ADA, AES) across 14 LLMs and 7 datasets demonstrate that explicitly balancing diversity with relevance in demonstration selection is a practical and effective lever for improving ICL in data-limited settings. However, the computational cost of RL training (requiring numerous LLM calls per training step) limits accessibility, and the diversity metric (normalized label coverage) is relatively coarse, leaving open whether richer diversity measures or adaptive CoT integration could yield further gains. Extending RDES beyond classification to generation and open-ended QA, and analyzing sample efficiency, are natural next steps that the authors identify but do not address.
