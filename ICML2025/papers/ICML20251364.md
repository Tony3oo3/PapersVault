## Problem

Online imitation learning methods that rely on explicit reward estimation (GAIL, SQIL, CFIL) suffer from training instability due to min-max optimization in the reward-policy space, and they struggle to scale to tasks with high-dimensional observations, actions, and complex dynamics. This paper asks whether a world model can be made entirely reward-free by decoding rewards from a learned Q-function via the inverse Bellman operator, thereby eliminating the separate reward model and its associated adversarial training instability.

## Input / Output

The method consumes state observation vectors (e.g., 15–223 dimensional) or 64×64×9 RGB frame stacks, together with a small set of expert demonstrations containing only state-action pairs (no reward labels). At inference, it outputs a single control action per step via MPPI planning over a 3-step horizon, and can additionally decode a dense reward signal r(z,a) = Q(z,a) − γV^π(z′) for inverse-RL evaluation. Expert data is drawn from 100–1000 trajectories (500 steps each) sampled from a pre-trained TD-MPC2 policy, while behavioral data is collected online during training.

## Method

IQ-MPC couples a decoder-free latent world model (encoder + latent dynamics + 5-ensemble Q-networks + policy prior, ~4.3M parameters, all MLPs with LayerNorm and Mish) with MPPI-based planning. The key architectural insight is that the inverse soft-Q objective (with χ² regularization) establishes a bijection between Q-space and reward-space via the inverse Bellman operator, so rewards are decoded as r = Q(z,a) − γV^π(z′) rather than learned by a separate reward head. Training jointly minimizes a latent consistency loss (stop-gradient L2 between rolled-out and encoded next states), the reformulated inverse soft-Q critic loss (using an initial-distribution value term for stability), a max-entropy policy prior loss, and an optional Wasserstein-1 gradient penalty to balance critic discriminative power. At inference, MPPI iterates 6 times over 512 candidate trajectories (24 from the policy prior, 488 from a Gaussian), scoring each with decoded rewards and terminal value, then executes the first action of the top-weighted sample.

## Results

On MyoSuite dexterous-hand manipulation, IQ-MPC achieves 87% success rate on Key Turn, 96% on Object Hold, and 73% on Pen Twirl, while all three baselines (IQL+SAC, CFIL+SAC, HyPE) score near 0% on Object Hold and Pen Twirl. On ManiSkill2, IQ-MPC reaches 79% on Pick Cube and 89% on Lift Cube, outperforming IQL+SAC (61% and 85%) and CFIL+SAC (0% and 1%). On DMControl locomotion tasks, IQ-MPC achieves stable expert-level performance across Hopper, Walker, Cheetah, Quadruped, Humanoid, and Dog, outperforming all baselines except being comparable to HyPE on Quadruped Run and Cheetah Run.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IQ-MPC decisively outperforms all three baselines on the MyoSuite dexterous-hand tasks, where IQL+SAC and CFIL+SAC achieve 0% success on Object Hold and Pen Twirl, and HyPE reaches only 13% on Object Hold. On ManiSkill2, IQ-MPC beats IQL+SAC by 18 points on Pick Cube (79% vs 61%) and by 4 points on Lift Cube (89% vs 85%), while CFIL+SAC fails entirely (0% and 1%). On DMControl locomotion, IQ-MPC outperforms IQL+SAC and CFIL+SAC across all six environments (Hopper, Walker, Cheetah, Quadruped, Humanoid, Dog) and is comparable to HyPE only on Quadruped Run and Cheetah Run, while surpassing HyPE on the remaining tasks. The most striking gap is on high-dimensional tasks (Humanoid, Dog) where baselines exhibit training instability and IQ-MPC maintains stable expert-level performance throughout training.

## Relation to Prior Work

IQ-MPC sits at the intersection of three established lines of work: inverse soft-Q learning (Garg et al., 2021), which reformulates GAIL's min-max objective in Q-policy space to avoid reward-space instability; decoder-free world models à la TD-MPC2 (Hansen et al., 2023), which learn latent dynamics without pixel reconstruction; and MPPI (Williams et al., 2015), a gradient-free planning algorithm. The key departure from prior model-based IL methods (CMIL, Ditto, V-MAIL) is the elimination of a separate reward model: rewards are decoded directly from the learned Q-function via the inverse Bellman operator, making the world model entirely reward-free. This contrasts with adversarial or flow-based reward estimation (GAIL, CFIL) and with model-based IL approaches that still train a dedicated reward head alongside the dynamics model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The reward-free formulation is practically appealing because it removes a fragile component (reward model training) from the IL pipeline while still enabling inverse RL reward recovery, as validated by high Pearson correlations with ground-truth rewards. The large performance gaps on MyoSuite dexterous-hand tasks (e.g., 0.96 vs. 0.00–0.13 success rate for baselines on Object Hold) suggest that latent-space planning is particularly well-suited to high-dimensional, contact-rich manipulation where model-free methods struggle. Limitations include the computational overhead of iterative MPPI planning at inference, a design primarily validated in deterministic environments (though some robustness to transition noise is shown), and dependence on a moderate number of expert demonstrations. Open questions include whether the approach scales to truly stochastic or partially observable real-world settings and whether the planning cost can be reduced without sacrificing the stability gains over direct policy execution.
