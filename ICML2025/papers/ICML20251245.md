## Problem

Image autoregressive models (IARs) now match or surpass diffusion models in image quality and generation speed, yet their privacy risks remain entirely unexplored. Existing membership inference attacks (MIAs) designed for diffusion models or LLMs fail when naively applied to IARs, leaving it unclear how much training data these models leak and whether their architectural properties make them inherently more or less vulnerable than their diffusion counterparts.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251245/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The MIA consumes a tokenized image (VQ-GAN tokens at 256×256 resolution) along with its class label, computing the per-token difference between conditional and unconditional predictions p(x|c) − p(x|c_null) as the membership signal. For dataset inference, per-sample MIA scores are aggregated via MinMaxScaler normalization and summation, then tested with Welch's t-test to determine whether a suspect set was used in training. The extraction attack takes a set of candidate images, identifies memorized ones via full-sequence prediction distance, generates completions from token prefixes, and outputs verbatim training images assessed by SSCD similarity (threshold τ=0.75).

## Method

The paper's core MIA exploits the fact that IARs combine LLM-like per-token prediction with DM-like conditional training: it computes the classifier-free guidance difference p(x|c) − p(x|c_null) and feeds this into LLM-style per-token metrics (MIN-K% PROB, Zlib, CAMIA, etc.), achieving 86.38% TPR@FPR=1% on VAR-d30 versus 6.38% for the strongest DM attack (CLiD). For MAR, which outputs continuous tokens via a small diffusion module rather than discrete logits, three targeted modifications amplify the signal: increasing the mask ratio from 0.86 to 0.95, fixing the diffusion timestep to t=500, and averaging over 64 noise samples instead of 4. The DI framework replaces the linear classifier stage (used in prior LLM/DM DI) with simple MinMaxScaler normalization and feature summation, since IAR MIA scores are consistently higher for members, reducing the required sample count to as few as 6. The extraction attack identifies candidates by feeding full token sequences and measuring prediction distance, then generates completions from prefixes using greedy sampling, requiring only 5000 generations to recover 698 training images from VAR-d30.

## Results

The paper's novel MIA for IARs achieves a remarkably high 86.38% TPR@FPR=1% on VAR-d30 (2.1B params), compared to only 6.38% for the strongest DM-specific MIA (CLiD) on SiT-XL/2. For Dataset Inference, the method requires as few as 6 samples to detect dataset membership for VAR-d30, versus at least 200 for comparable DMs. The data extraction attack recovers 698 verbatim training images from VAR-d30, 36 from RAR-XXL, and 5 from MAR-H, using only 5,000 generations per model. Across all 11 IARs evaluated, the paper's MIAs improve TPR@FPR=1% by up to 69.69 percentage points over naive LLM-MIA baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest DM-specific MIA (CLiD, Zhai et al. 2024), which achieves 6.38% TPR@FPR=1% on SiT-XL/2, the paper's IAR MIA reaches 86.38% on VAR-d30 — an order-of-magnitude gap that the authors attribute to IARs exposing per-token p(x) at the output. Compared to the LLM DI baseline (Maini et al., 2024), the paper's optimized DI procedure reduces required samples by up to 70% for smaller models (e.g., VAR-d16: 2000→600) and further to 200 with their tailored MIAs, while for VAR-d30 the total reduction is from 20 to 6 samples. In the unified Loss Attack comparison (Table 7), all IARs show TPR@FPR=1% above random guessing (1.43–5.73%) while all DMs remain at or below 1.08%, confirming the leakage gap is not an artifact of attack design. For data extraction, the paper's prefix-based method recovers 698 images from VAR-d30 with only 5,000 generations, versus 50 images requiring 175M generations for DMs (Carlini et al., 2023). Among IARs themselves, MAR models are consistently the most private (lowest MIA scores, fewest extracted images), which the authors link to MAR's use of a diffusion module for token sampling rather than direct categorical logits.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper synthesizes membership inference techniques from two distinct lineages—LLM-based per-token logit attacks (MIN-K% PROB, CAMIA, SURP, zlib) and DM-based conditional likelihood discrepancy (CLiD)—into novel IAR-specific MIAs that exploit classifier-free guidance and per-token autoregressive structure. It extends the Dataset Inference framework (Maini et al., 2021; 2024) by eliminating the linear classifier stage and replacing it with normalized feature summation, and adapts the prefix-completion extraction paradigm from LLMs (Carlini et al., 2021) to the tokenized image setting. The key departure from prior work is the demonstration that neither LLM nor DM MIAs transfer effectively to IARs, necessitating architecture-specific redesigns that achieve up to 69% improvement over naive baselines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As IARs (VAR, RAR, MAR) rapidly approach or surpass DMs in FID and generation speed, this work reveals a dramatic and previously unquantified privacy gap: 86.38% vs. 6.38% TPR@FPR=1% for MIA, 6 vs. 200+ samples for DI, and 698 vs. 50 extracted images. The unified Loss Attack experiment (Table 7) strengthens the claim that the gap is architectural rather than an artifact of attack design, though the analysis is limited to ImageNet-1k class-conditional models and does not cover the largest text-to-image IARs (Infinity, HART) trained on billion-scale datasets. Open questions include whether diffusion-based token sampling (as in MAR) can close the privacy gap at scale, whether output perturbation defenses can be made practical without sacrificing quality, and how these findings generalize to text-conditional and multi-modal IARs.
