## Problem

Continuous diffusion on protein language model (pLM) representations remains largely underexplored for protein sequence generation, with prior attempts limited to specific encoders or narrow conditional tasks. While discrete diffusion and autoregressive models have been widely applied, the potential of operating diffusion in the rich continuous latent spaces produced by modern pLMs (which capture both sequence and structural semantics) has not been systematically investigated across encoder architectures and generation tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251762/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

DiMA takes Gaussian noise as its primary input and iteratively denoises it in the continuous latent space of a frozen pLM encoder (ESM-2, CHEAP, or SaProt), producing amino acid sequences via a fine-tuned decoder. For conditional tasks, additional inputs include 3D motif coordinates (scaffolding), target fold structures (fold-conditioned generation), or discrete family labels (family-specific design), injected via cross-attention or embedding layers. Sequence length is not predicted by the model but sampled from the empirical training distribution at inference time, and the encoder is entirely discarded after training, leaving only the 35M-parameter denoiser for generation.

## Method

DiMA is a 35M-parameter, 12-layer transformer denoiser (16 heads, hidden size 320) trained to reconstruct normalized pLM embeddings from Gaussian-corrupted versions using an MSE loss. Three key design choices distinguish it from standard image diffusion: (1) a tan-10 noise schedule (α_t = 1/(1 + d²tan²(πt/2)), d=10) that ensures reconstruction difficulty increases linearly with diffusion time, avoiding the trivial low-noise regime that plagues linear/cosine schedules on protein encodings; (2) a modified self-conditioning mechanism where the previous-step z₀ prediction is linearly projected and added to every transformer block input rather than concatenated; and (3) long skip connections between shallow and deep blocks to stabilize training near t=0. The framework is encoder-agnostic—only a linear projection adapts the denoiser to different embedding dimensions—enabling the same architecture to operate on ESM-2 (8M–3B), CHEAP (dual sequence+structure decoding), and SaProt (structure-token-augmented) representations without architectural changes.

## Results

On SwissProt (470k sequences), DiMA-35M trained on ESM-8M encodings achieves the best distribution similarity (FD-seq = 0.34, vs. dataset reference 0.13) and near-dataset structural quality (pLDDT = 83.3 vs. 80.7) among all 35M-parameter generative models, while maintaining moderate diversity (CD₀.₅ = 0.611) and novelty (35.7). On the larger AFDBv4-90 dataset (2.2M sequences), DiMA with ESM-2 3B encoder reaches pLDDT 83.4 and FD-seq 0.314, matching or exceeding much larger pretrained models (ProGen2, DPLM, ProLLAMA) with two orders of magnitude fewer parameters. For fold-conditioned generation on CATH S40, DiMA achieves a mean TM-score of 0.93 with 100% success rate, compared to RFDiffusion's 0.48 and 41% success rate.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiMA decisively outperforms all autoregressive baselines (RITA, SeqDesign, nanoGPT, ProGen2, ProLLAMA, ProtGPT2) on distribution similarity (FD-seq 0.34 vs. 1.19–3.53) and structural quality (pLDDT 83.3 vs. 30–61), though autoregressive models retain higher diversity (CD₀.₅ ~0.9–1.0) and novelty. Against discrete diffusion, DiMA matches DPLM on pLDDT (83.3 vs. 84.0) but dramatically outperforms it on diversity (CD₀.₅ 0.611 vs. 0.494) and novelty (35.7 vs. 11.5), while D3PM and EvoDiff-OADM are far weaker on both quality and distribution matching. Flow-based DFM (Multiflow) and score-based Walk-Jump are substantially outperformed on all quality and distribution metrics (pLDDT 37.8/32.4 vs. 83.3). For conditional tasks, DiMA (19/24 solved) outperforms EvoDiff, DPLM, and DPLM2 on motif scaffolding, and achieves 100% fold-conditioned success (TM-score 0.93) versus RFDiffusion's 41% (TM-score 0.48). Against large pretrained models (ProGen2 151M, ProLLAMA 7B, DPLM 640M, Chroma, PLAID-100M), DiMA-35M achieves comparable or superior quality-diversity trade-offs with 2–200× fewer parameters, and matches structure-trained Multiflow while using only sequence input.

## Relation to Prior Work

DiMA extends the latent diffusion paradigm from NLP (where Gaussian diffusion operates on text embeddings) to protein sequence generation, building directly on the self-conditioning mechanism from Chen et al. (2022) and the tan-based noise schedule from Hoogeboom et al. (2023). It departs from prior continuous diffusion attempts for proteins (Lee et al. 2023, Zhang et al. 2023a), which were restricted to specific encoders or conditional settings, by establishing an encoder-agnostic framework validated across ESM-2, CHEAP, and SaProt representations spanning 8M to 3B parameters. The work also positions continuous latent diffusion as a practical alternative to the dominant discrete diffusion (DPLM, EvoDiff) and autoregressive (ProGen2, RITA) paradigms in protein generation, arguing that pLM continuous encodings capture richer structural information than discrete token spaces.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework's encoder-agnostic design means it automatically benefits from future advances in protein representation learning without architectural changes, and the 35M-parameter denoiser matching 7B-parameter autoregressive models offers a compelling efficiency story for resource-constrained labs. Key open questions include whether the quality-diversity trade-off (more diffusion steps → higher pLDDT but lower novelty) can be decoupled, whether the approach extends to full-atom or multi-state protein generation, and whether the fold-conditioned results (TM-score 0.93) generalize beyond the CATH S40 benchmark. The paper's reliance on ESMFold for all structural evaluation also means structural quality is bounded by ESMFold's own limitations.
