## Problem

Existing protein language models generate sequences conditioned on a single functional constraint from one modality (e.g., only GO terms or only EC numbers), forcing users into iterative filtering or multi-step optimization pipelines to satisfy multiple constraints simultaneously. This approach becomes impractical when training data is limited and struggles to achieve high success rates for multifunctional protein design, such as enzymes with multiple catalytic activities.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252902/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

CFP-GEN accepts any composable subset of three condition types: (1) functional annotations (GO terms, IPR domains, EC numbers) encoded as one-hot vectors through dedicated embedding layers, (2) sequence motifs of 10–30 residues extracted from IPR domain boundaries, and (3) 3D backbone atomic coordinates (N, CA, C, O) from PDB or AlphaFold DB structures. The output is a de novo protein sequence of 200–400 residues generated via 100 discrete diffusion sampling steps. Notably, sequence motifs are not fixed during generation but are dynamically updated by the RCFE module, allowing the model to refine functional sites rather than merely preserving them.

## Method

CFP-GEN is built on DPLM's discrete diffusion framework (absorbing-state <mask> noise schedule) and introduces two novel conditioning modules integrated into pretrained ESM transformer blocks. The AGFM module sums annotation embeddings from GO/IPR/EC layers, passes them through a single MLP to produce scale (γ), shift (β), and gate (α) parameters, and applies feature de-normalization before self-attention and FFN in every block—requiring training of only the embedding layers and one MLP. The RCFE module adopts a ControlNet-inspired dual-branch design: a frozen main branch processes the noised sequence plus annotations, while a trainable copy of the first half of ESM blocks encodes the sequence motif, with zero-initialized linear layers ensuring gradual information injection. Structural conditioning is added by injecting GVP-Transformer-encoded backbone coordinates via a cross-attention layer at the final block, reusing DPLM's pretrained adapter without additional training. Training proceeds in two progressive stages (AGFM, then RCFE) with condition dropout (p=0.5) to enforce composability, and the total model size is 1.48B parameters.

## Results

On GO-term-conditioned generation, CFP-GEN with full multimodal conditioning (GO + IPR + Motif) achieves a macro F1 of 0.550 and AUC of 0.795, surpassing the positive control (natural proteins) at 0.522 and 0.775 respectively. For IPR domain prediction, the same configuration reaches a macro F1 of 0.975 and AUC of 0.992, approaching the perfect positive control. In functional protein inverse folding, CFP-GEN (SFT) achieves 76.39% AAR, improving over DPLM (66.94%) by 9.45 percentage points while maintaining comparable structural fidelity (scTM 0.889 vs. 0.883). For EC-number-conditioned enzyme design, CFP-GEN with all conditions matches natural enzyme functionality (macro F1 0.925 vs. 0.927 positive control) and outperforms ZymCTRL by 15.1% in macro F1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CFP-GEN consistently outperforms all single-condition baselines across all three annotation types. Against DPLM (the base model), CFP-GEN improves macro F1 from 0.189 to 0.550 on GO terms (+191%), from 0.422 to 0.975 on IPR domains (+131%), and from 0.186 to 0.925 on EC numbers (+397%). Against ProGen2, the strongest single-condition baseline, CFP-GEN gains +19.5% macro F1 on GO (0.550 vs. 0.355), +39.1% on IPR (0.975 vs. 0.584), and +53.8% on EC (0.925 vs. 0.387). Against ESM3 on IPR prediction, CFP-GEN improves macro F1 from 0.565 to 0.975 (+73%), confirming the paper's claim of a 30% F1 improvement. Against ZymCTRL on EC numbers, CFP-GEN achieves 0.925 vs. 0.774 macro F1 (+19.5%) and 0.924 vs. 0.562 MRR (+64%). In inverse folding (Table 2), CFP-GEN (SFT) leads all baselines in AAR (76.39% vs. DPLM's 66.94%, LM-DESIGN's 63.81%, ESM-IF's 57.39%, ProteinMPNN's 45.28%) and MRR (0.882 vs. 0.721 for DPLM), while maintaining competitive structural metrics (scTM 0.889, pLDDT 83.53). Notably, with full multimodal conditioning, CFP-GEN exceeds the positive control (natural proteins) on GO macro F1 (0.550 vs. 0.522) and AUC (0.795 vs. 0.775), indicating it can generate proteins with functionality surpassing natural sequences.

## Relation to Prior Work

CFP-GEN builds directly on DPLM's discrete diffusion framework and ESM2 transformer architecture, extending them with two new conditioning modules: AGFM (for composable functional annotations) and RCFE (for sequence motifs, inspired by ControlNet's dual-branch design adapted to protein transformers). It departs from the single-condition paradigm of prior controllable PLMs—ProGen2 (GO terms), ProteoGAN (GO terms), ZymCTRL (EC numbers), and ESM3 (limited IPR vocabulary)—by jointly integrating multiple annotation types, sequence motifs, and 3D backbone coordinates within a unified diffusion process. The GVP-Transformer structure encoder is reused from DPLM without retraining, positioning CFP-GEN as a modular extension rather than a from-scratch architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ability to simultaneously satisfy multiple functional constraints (e.g., GO + IPR + EC + sequence motif + structure) addresses a core practical bottleneck in protein engineering, where iterative filtering across modalities is fragile and data-hungry. The demonstrated success in multi-catalytic enzyme design—where a single protein must exhibit multiple distinct catalytic activities—has direct relevance to industrial biocatalysis and synthetic biology. Key limitations remain: the model covers only a subset of GO/IPR/EC labels, lacks physicochemical property conditioning (hydrophobicity, charge), and does not perform sequence-structure co-design, all of which are necessary for broader bio-manufacturing applications.
