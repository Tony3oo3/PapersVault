## Problem

Score-based graph generative models (SGGMs) have strong empirical performance in drug discovery and protein design, but their convergence behavior remains theoretically unexplored. Unlike standard score-based generative models governed by a single SDE, SGGMs involve a system of coupled SDEs for graph structure and node features, making existing convergence analyses inapplicable. This paper provides the first non-asymptotic convergence analysis for SGGMs across three generation paradigms.

## Input / Output

The model takes standard Gaussian noise (zero mean, unit variance) as input and produces a graph G = (X, A) where X ∈ R^{N×F} is the node feature matrix and A ∈ R^{N×N} is the adjacency matrix. The analysis covers three paradigms: joint generation of both X and A, feature generation with a fixed A*, and structure generation with a fixed X*. Convergence is measured via KL divergence between the true data distribution and the generated distribution, with bounds depending on graph size N, feature dimension F, diffusion length T, and number of sampling steps M.

## Method

The paper formulates SGGMs as a system of two coupled Ornstein-Uhlenbeck SDEs (one for node features, one for graph structure) and derives non-asymptotic KL divergence convergence bounds under both Euler-Maruyama and exponential integrator discretization schemes. The score functions are modeled with GNNs (vanilla GCN) whose functional form F(A_t X_t) captures the interdependency between structure and features. The convergence bound decomposes into three error sources: distance to the convergent Gaussian distribution (scaling as (H + dim)·e^{-T}), score estimation error (T·ε²), and discretization error. A key finding is the non-isotropic scaling: graph size N enters quadratically (N²) while feature dimension F enters only linearly (NF), and the spectral norm of the adjacency matrix σ_A (bounded by max degree) amplifies error for heterogeneous degree distributions. The analysis yields explicit hyperparameter guidance: T = O(log((H+dim)/ε²)) and M = O(dim·L²·T²/ε²) suffice to make the overall error bounded by the score estimation error alone.

## Results

The paper derives non-asymptotic KL-divergence convergence bounds for three graph generation paradigms. Under the exponential integrator scheme with uniform steps, the joint generation bound takes the form KL(P(X₀)‖P(X̂_T)) ≲ (H_X + NF)e^{−T} + Tε_X² + NFL²T²/M, and KL(P(A₀)‖P(Â_T)) ≲ (H_A + N²)e^{−T} + Tε_A² + N²LT²/M (Corollary 4.7). A key finding is the non-isotropic scaling: the bound grows quadratically in graph size N but only linearly in feature dimension F, and the norm of the fixed structure σ_A (bounded by max degree) directly inflates the error in the feature-generation paradigm. Empirically, on synthetic graphs (T=100, M=500, one-layer GCN), SGGMs show measurably worse performance on power-law graphs than regular graphs, normalization reduces generative error, and performance degrades faster with increasing N than with increasing F.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical convergence-analysis paper and does not compare its method against competing generative models or baselines. The empirical study (Section 5) uses synthetic graph models (Barabási–Albert power-law graphs and regular graphs with Gaussian node features) solely to validate the three theoretical predictions: (1) regular graphs yield lower generative error than power-law graphs, (2) applying normalization to the feature matrix tightens the convergence bound, and (3) increasing graph size N causes steeper performance degradation than increasing feature dimension F. All three predictions are confirmed qualitatively in Figure 1, but no numerical comparison against alternative sampling schemes (e.g., Euler-Maruyama vs. exponential integrator) or competing graph generative models (e.g., DIFUSSE, DiGress) is provided. The paper notes that the exponential integrator is theoretically preferred over Euler-Maruyama due to smaller discretization error terms, consistent with prior SGM analyses (Chen et al., 2023a), but does not include a head-to-head empirical benchmark.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the convergence analysis framework for score-based generative models (SGMs) to the graph domain, where the generative process is governed by a system of coupled SDEs (one for graph structure, one for node features) rather than a single SDE. It builds directly on the SGGM formulation of Jo et al. (2022) and the SGM convergence methodology of Chen et al. (2023a), but departs from both by addressing the interdependency between graph structure and node features that makes standard SGM analyses inapplicable. The key conceptual departure is that existing convergence results assume independence between data elements, whereas SGGMs require handling entangled dynamics where changes in structure affect features and vice versa.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first non-asymptotic convergence analysis for SGGMs, providing practitioners with theoretical guidance on hyperparameter selection (diffusion length T, sampling steps M) and revealing that graph size degrades convergence quadratically while feature dimensionality only degrades it linearly. The connection between degree distribution heterogeneity and convergence bounds offers a new lens on why SGGMs struggle with power-law graphs. However, the analysis relies on strong assumptions (Lipschitz score functions, specific GCN functional form, static learning outcome) and empirical validation is limited to synthetic graphs with a single-layer GCN, leaving open questions about whether these insights transfer to real-world molecular or protein generation tasks and to more complex GNN architectures.
