## Problem

Standard heterogeneous GNNs applied to relational entity graphs treat primary-foreign key edges as semantic relations, ignoring the database's characteristic topology where many-to-many relationships are decomposed into bridge and hub nodes via junction tables. This leads to redundant multi-hop message passing, over-smoothed representations, and imbalanced signal aggregation that limits predictive fidelity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250314/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a relational database (multiple tables linked by primary-foreign key relationships) and a seed entity with a timestamp, from which a temporally consistent subgraph is sampled via temporal neighbor sampling (only nodes with timestamps ≤ t are included). Outputs are task-specific: binary labels for entity classification, continuous values for entity regression, or ranked top-K entity lists for recommendation, all produced by task-specific prediction heads (MLP or two-tower/ID-GNN) applied to the final node embeddings.

## Method

RELGNN introduces atomic routes—simple paths automatically derived from primary-foreign key schema relationships that enable complete single-hop information exchange between source and destination node-types, bypassing intermediate bridge or hub nodes. Composite message passing fuses the intermediate and source node embeddings via a learned linear combination (FUSE), then applies multi-head attention where the destination node embedding serves as the query and the fused embeddings serve as keys and values, completing the full information exchange in one step rather than two. Each atomic route has its own dedicated weight matrices, and the destination node aggregates messages from all its related atomic routes via summation. This design eliminates the redundancy of re-aggregating destination information through bridge/hub nodes and prevents irrelevant signals from other node-types connected to the intermediate node from contaminating the message.

## Results

RELGNN is evaluated on all 30 tasks in RELBENCH across 7 relational databases, spanning entity classification (ROC-AUC), entity regression (MAE), and recommendation (MAP@K). RELGNN surpasses all baselines on 27 of 30 tasks, with the largest gains on datasets with complex primary-foreign key structures: a 25% relative improvement on site-success regression (rel-trial), 13% on driver-top3 classification (rel-f1), and 11% on user-item-review recommendation (rel-amazon). On the remaining 3 tasks, RELGNN performs comparably to the heterogeneous GraphSAGE baseline. Ablation studies confirm that the gains stem primarily from atomic routes rather than the attention mechanism alone.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary RDL baseline is heterogeneous GraphSAGE (Hetero-GNN) from the original RELBENCH paper. RELGNN outperforms Hetero-GNN on 24 of 30 tasks, with the largest relative gains (10–25%) concentrated on datasets with many bridge/hub nodes (rel-f1, rel-trial, rel-avito, rel-event), while gains are minimal (0–2%) on simpler schemas (rel-amazon, rel-hm, rel-stack). LightGBM, the non-RDL tabular baseline, is consistently outperformed by both GNN methods across all 30 tasks, confirming the value of relational structure. In ablation studies, GAT and GIN backbones are also outperformed by RELGNN on the majority of tasks, with GAT showing the largest degradation (e.g., 60.03 vs. 85.69 on driver-top3), suggesting that the atomic-route message passing design is more critical than the specific aggregator choice. RELGNN ties or slightly underperforms Hetero-GNN on only 3 tasks (item-churn, user-visits, and post-votes), all on datasets with simpler primary-foreign key topologies or self-loop structures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Relational Deep Learning (RDL) paradigm introduced by Fey et al. (2024), which represents relational databases as temporal heterogeneous graphs and applies GNNs for end-to-end prediction. It departs from the standard heterogeneous GNN architectures (R-GCN, HGT, GraphSAGE) used in RELBENCH by arguing that primary-foreign key edges carry no intrinsic semantic meaning—unlike the edge types in general heterogeneous graphs—so treating them as ordinary semantic relations misrepresents information flow. The proposed atomic routes are conceptually analogous to meta-paths in heterogeneous graph learning but are automatically derived from the database schema rather than manually designed, and the composite message passing mechanism is a novel architectural primitive that collapses two-hop bridge/hub traversal into a single step.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a concrete structural mismatch—bridge and hub nodes in relational entity graphs cause redundant aggregation and signal imbalance under standard message passing—and shows that addressing it yields consistent gains (4–25%) across 27 of 30 RELBENCH tasks, making RELGNN the new reference baseline for RDL. Open questions include handling self-loop tables (rel-stack), improving prediction heads for unbounded regression targets, and whether the atomic-route framework extends to dynamically evolving schemas. The extensibility of the composite message passing template (swappable AGGR, FUSE, and attention variants) positions it as a practical building block for future RDL architectures.
