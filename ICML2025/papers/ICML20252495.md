## Problem

Existing molecular conformation generation methods designed for small molecules or proteins fail to scale to polymers due to their unique structural characteristics—greater conformational flexibility, absence of strong directional intramolecular interactions, and significantly larger chemical space. Compounding this, the severe scarcity of polymer conformation datasets has left this important area largely unexplored, with no tailored generative method or standardized benchmark available.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252495/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 2D polymer graph (encoded as a SMILES string with polymerization sites marked by '*' symbols) as input and produces a complete 3D polymer conformation as atomic coordinates. The output is structured hierarchically: the polymer conformation is decomposed into a series of repeating unit conformations (each containing N/Nu + 2 atoms due to two overlapping atoms from neighboring units) plus their corresponding SO(3) orientation transformations. Typical systems contain approximately 2,000 atoms with 20–100+ repeating units per chain.

## Method

PolyConf employs a two-phase hierarchical generative framework. In Phase 1, a masked autoregressive model (MAR) with a torsional diffusion procedure generates repeating unit conformations in random order: a multi-modal encoder (2D MolCLR graph encoder + 3D Uni-Mol SE(3)-invariant encoder) produces embeddings, a Transformer-based MAR encoder/decoder predicts representations for masked units, and a diffusion denoiser in torsion-angle space generates each unit's 3D coordinates conditioned on the decoded representations. In Phase 2, an SO(3) diffusion model generates the rotation matrices for each repeating unit, with translations derived analytically from overlapping-atom alignment, enabling assembly of the full chain. The key insight is that polymers can be decomposed into local repeating unit conformations plus orientation transformations, and the random-order MAR generation captures inter-unit dependencies without imposing a fixed sequential order, while the SO(3) diffusion handles the continuous rotation space natively.

## Results

On the PolyBench benchmark, PolyConf achieves S-MAT-R (structure matching, recall) of 35.021 Å (mean) and 24.279 Å (median), and E-MAT-R (energy matching, recall) of 0.933 (mean) and 0.359 (median), outperforming the best baseline TorsionalDiff (53.210 Å and 2.605 respectively) by at least 25% across all four structure and energy metrics. In terms of efficiency, PolyConf generates polymer conformations in 0.40 minutes on average, faster than TorsionalDiff (0.45 min), MCF (1.12 min), and GeoDiff (3.54 min). Under the scalability test (doubling repeating units to ~4,000 atoms), PolyConf maintains its lead with E-MAT-R of 1.259 vs. TorsionalDiff's 5.219, a >50% improvement in energy metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PolyConf outperforms all four baselines across every structure and energy metric. Against TorsionalDiff (the strongest baseline, which also receives a biased advantage by requiring an initial polymer structure as input), PolyConf improves S-MAT-R from 53.210 to 35.021 Å (34% reduction) and E-MAT-R from 2.605 to 0.933 (64% reduction). GeoDiff and ET-Flow, both designed for small molecules, degrade severely on polymers with S-MAT-R values around 93–94 Å, roughly 2.7× worse than PolyConf. MCF performs worst overall, with S-MAT-R exceeding 248 Å and energy metrics exceeding 10¹⁰, indicating complete failure to generate physically meaningful polymer conformations. Notably, PolyConf also achieves the fastest inference time (0.40 min) among all methods, demonstrating that the hierarchical decomposition (autoregressive unit generation + SO(3) rotation diffusion) is both more accurate and more efficient than treating the entire polymer as a single large molecule.

## Relation to Prior Work

PolyConf combines three established generative modeling components—masked autoregressive modeling with diffusion (Li et al., 2024), torsion-angle-space diffusion (Jing et al., 2022), and SO(3) diffusion (Yim et al., 2023)—into a hierarchical two-phase framework specifically designed for the polymer domain. The key conceptual departure from prior work is the decomposition of polymer conformations into repeating unit conformations plus orientation transformations, which exploits the repetitive monomer structure absent in small molecules and the lack of a unified backbone scaffold that distinguishes proteins. This positions the paper as the first to argue that neither small-molecule methods (which cannot scale to ~2000-atom systems) nor protein methods (which rely on MSA, backbone priors, and dihedral constraints inapplicable to polymers) can be directly transferred, necessitating a purpose-built architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By introducing both the first tailored method and the first benchmark (PolyBench, 50k+ polymer conformations from MD simulations) for polymer conformation generation, this paper establishes the foundational infrastructure for an entirely new subfield at the intersection of generative modeling and polymer science. Open questions include extending to multi-monomer copolymers, 2D topological polymer architectures, and whether flow-based or other generative paradigms could further improve quality. Limitations include evaluation on a single monomer type (PET), force-field-based (not DFT) ground truth, and a relatively modest dataset size, leaving the generalizability of the hierarchical decomposition to chemically diverse polymer families untested.
