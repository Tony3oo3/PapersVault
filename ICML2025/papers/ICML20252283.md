## Problem

Existing model merging methods such as Task Arithmetic and its magnitude-based variants (Ties-Merging, PCB Merging) accumulate task vectors to combine multiple finetuned models, but this direct aggregation causes knowledge conflicts where dominant high-magnitude components from one task overwrite critical information from others. Prior trimming strategies focus on removing small-magnitude parameters, overlooking that high-magnitude components are often the primary source of inter-task interference, leading to skewed merged models and degraded multi-task performance.

## Input / Output

The method consumes the pretrained model weights, K task vectors (each being the parameter difference between a finetuned model and the pretrained model), and a small set of unlabeled exemplars per task (typically 2–3 images or text samples). A single forward pass through each task's finetuned model collects layer-wise input features, which are then used to compute per-layer projection bases or binary masks. The output is a single merged multi-task model whose weights are the pretrained parameters plus the sum of all trimmed task vectors scaled by a hyperparameter α, with no additional training required.

## Method

CAT Merging formulates a dual-objective optimization at each layer: minimize the inter-task feature perturbation (conflict) while preserving intra-task representational integrity, weighted by a trade-off parameter λ. For linear weight matrices, it constructs a removal basis B_k from the top-c eigenvectors of a task-specific matrix derived from the collected input features, then projects out the components of other tasks' vectors aligned with B_k. For normalization scale and shift parameters, it applies a binary mask selecting the top-c dimensions of a conflict score vector. The theoretical grounding is an upper bound on knowledge conflict (Theorem 4.4) showing that the total loss increase is bounded by a weighted sum of layer-wise perturbations, justifying the layer-by-layer trimming strategy. The entire procedure is training-free, requiring only a lightweight forward pass with a handful of unlabeled exemplars to compute the trimming operations before the final vector summation.

## Results

On eight vision classification tasks, CAT Merging achieves 78.3% average accuracy with ViT-B/32 and 89.6% with ViT-L/14, outperforming the strongest training-free baseline PCB Merging by 2.5% and 2.0% respectively. On eight GLUE NLP tasks with RoBERTa, CAT Merging reaches 62.56% average score, beating PCB Merging (59.59%) and Task Arithmetic (58.34%). On six vision-language tasks with BLIP, CAT Merging attains the best result on 5 of 6 benchmarks, including 44.07% accuracy on OKVQA and 19.69% on TextVQA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CAT Merging outperforms all training-free baselines across every evaluated domain. Against the closest competitor PCB Merging (NeurIPS 2024), CAT Merging gains +2.5% on ViT-B/32 (78.3 vs 75.8), +2.0% on ViT-L/14 (89.6 vs 87.6), +3.0% on GLUE NLP (62.56 vs 59.59), and wins 5/6 vision-language tasks. Against Task Arithmetic (ICLR 2023), the improvement is +9.2% on ViT-B/32 and +5.1% on ViT-L/14, confirming that conflict-aware trimming substantially mitigates the knowledge conflict that plagues naive vector addition. Ties-Merging (NeurIPS 2023) and TATR (arXiv 2025) trail by 5.9% and 5.5% respectively on ViT-B/32, while Fisher Merging (NeurIPS 2022) and RegMean (ICLR 2023) lag by 10.0% and 6.5%. Against test-time training methods, CAT Merging is competitive: it slightly trails LW AdaMerging++ (81.1%) and Surgery Merging (80.9%) on ViT-B/32 but matches or exceeds them on ViT-L/14 (89.6 vs 91.0 and 89.0), all without any additional training or labeled data.

## Relation to Prior Work

CAT Merging builds directly on the Task Arithmetic framework (Ilharco et al., 2023), which defines task vectors as parameter differences between finetuned and pretrained models and merges them via simple addition. It departs from prior trimming methods (Ties-Merging, PCB Merging, Twin-Merging) that remove low-magnitude components under the assumption that high-magnitude parameters encode important task-specific knowledge. Instead, CAT Merging identifies high-magnitude, conflict-prone components as the primary source of inter-task interference and removes them via parameter-specific operations: eigenvector-based projection for linear weights and binary masking for normalization scale/shift parameters. The theoretical contribution—a Lipschitz-based upper bound on knowledge conflict (Theorem 4.4)—provides a principled layer-wise decomposition that motivates the local trimming strategy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CAT Merging offers a practical, training-free solution to multi-task model merging that requires only 2–3 unlabeled exemplars per task, making it deployable in settings where retraining is infeasible. The consistent 2–2.5% improvements over strong baselines (PCB Merging, TATR) across vision, NLP, and vision-language tasks, combined with low computational overhead (~46s for ViT-B/32), make it a competitive default for model merging pipelines. Open questions include scalability to very large task counts (the pairwise conflict analysis is O(K²)), generalization to non-transformer architectures, and whether the layer-wise conflict decomposition holds under more aggressive finetuning regimes where task vectors are less localized.
