## Problem

Computer Vision has not achieved the zero-shot task generalization that NLP enjoys, despite adopting similar architectural milestones. The authors hypothesize that the root cause is CV's reliance on discrete, terminological task definitions (e.g., 'semantic segmentation') that are merely symbolic labels conveying no true task objective, preventing models from genuinely understanding what a task requires and thus blocking generalization to novel tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252164/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts a 448×448 RGB input image paired with a free-form natural-language explanatory instruction that describes the desired transformation (e.g., 'overlay the stream area with a bright blue color' rather than 'semantic segmentation'), and autoregressively generates a 448×448 output image. The explanatory instructions are unconstrained in phrasing and can describe transformations in either direction (image-to-output or output-to-image), and at inference time entirely unseen instructions can be supplied to trigger both instruction-level and task-level zero-shot behavior.

## Method

The core contribution is the Explanatory Instruction paradigm: instead of fixed task labels, each vision task is defined by a detailed linguistic description of the transformation between input and output images. The authors construct the DECVT dataset (12 million image→instruction→output triplets, organized into terminological-based and explanatory-based components) using GPT-4o-generated and manually crafted descriptions. A vanilla token-based autoregressive VLM (decoder-only transformer with VQ-VAE image tokens, BPE text tokens, RMSNorm, SwiGLU, RoPE, and Uni-Rep for flexible resolution) is initialized from Lumina-mGPT-7B-768 and fine-tuned via next-token prediction with z-loss regularization. By omitting specific tasks (e.g., Canny-to-Image, Depth-to-Image) from training, the model demonstrates task-level zero-shot generalization, confirming that understanding task objectives through explanatory language—rather than memorizing terminological labels—enables genuine generalization to unseen vision tasks.

## Results

On the task-level zero-shot Canny-to-Image task (MultiGen-20M), the model achieves F1=20.69, FID=26.93, and CLIP-Score=27.16 without any training data for Canny tasks. For instruction-level zero-shot HED-to-Image, it reaches SSIM=0.6561, FID=15.91, and CLIP-Score=29.59. On task-level zero-shot Inpainting and Outpainting (Places), the model obtains FID=15.16/LPIPS=0.48 and FID=15.15/IS=17.20 respectively. On dense prediction and low-level tasks (Table 2), the model achieves mIoU=42.12 on Semantic Segmentation (ADE20K), RMSE=0.553 on Depth Estimation (NYU-Depth V2), and PSNR=14.48/SSIM=0.332 on Low-light Enhancement (LOLv2), all under unseen instructions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's method is not competitive with task-specific models: ControlNet-SD1.5 achieves F1=34.65 vs. 20.69 on Canny-to-Image, and MaskGIT reaches FID=7.80 vs. 15.15 on Outpainting. Among vision generalist baselines, PixWizard and OmniGen also outperform the proposed method on most generation metrics (e.g., PixWizard F1=35.46 vs. 20.69 on Canny-to-Image; OmniGen SSIM=0.8237 vs. 0.6561 on HED-to-Image). However, the key advantage is zero-shot generalization: the model achieves these results on tasks (Canny-to-Image, Inpainting, Outpainting) and instructions it has never seen during training, whereas baselines like Lumina-mGPT require fixed terminological instructions and fail when the format changes. Compared to the base model Lumina-mGPT, the fine-tuned model shows dramatic improvements across all tasks (e.g., Canny-to-Image F1: 20.69 vs. 10.09; HED-to-Image SSIM: 0.6561 vs. 0.1834; Inpainting FID: 15.16 vs. 42.69). On dense prediction tasks (Table 2), the method trails task-specific models substantially (e.g., Depth Estimation RMSE 0.553 vs. DepthAnything's 0.206; Deraining PSNR 16.72 vs. PromptIR's 36.37) but still outperforms Lumina-mGPT on Surface Normal Estimation (MAE 27.21 vs. 28.49) and is the only generalist to report results on all seven tasks simultaneously.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the token-based autoregressive VLM paradigm established by Chameleon and Lumina-mGPT, using Lumina-mGPT-7B as its pretrained initialization. It departs conceptually from prior vision generalist models (OmniGen, PixWizard, Lumina-mGPT) that rely on fixed or open-style terminological instructions (e.g., 'semantic segmentation', 'pose map') by proposing Explanatory Instructions—detailed natural-language descriptions of the transformation between input and output images. The key lineage shift is from treating vision tasks as discrete, symbolically-defined categories to treating them as continuous, linguistically-described transformations, drawing an explicit parallel to how NLP's instruction-following paradigm enabled zero-shot task generalization in language models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper introduces a compelling conceptual reframing: that terminological task definitions are a fundamental barrier to zero-shot generalization in CV, and that explanatory instructions can overcome this. However, the quantitative results reveal substantial performance gaps versus task-specific baselines (e.g., F1 of 20.69 vs. 34.65 for Canny-to-Image; mIoU of 42.12 vs. 60.80 for segmentation), and task-level zero-shot capability is limited to generation and low-level tasks while failing for inverse tasks like Image-to-Canny. The identified root cause—misalignment between the image tokenizer and text modality during pretraining—points to a concrete but unresolved engineering challenge. The DECVT dataset (12M triplets) and the explanatory instruction framework may prove useful as building blocks for future unified vision models, but the current results suggest the paradigm shift from terminological to explanatory instructions is necessary but not yet sufficient for competitive performance.
