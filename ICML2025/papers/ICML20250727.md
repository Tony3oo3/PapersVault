## Problem

Fairness audits of ML models are vulnerable to fairwashing: platforms can alter their responses during an audit to appear fair while maintaining biased behavior for real users. Existing robustness results assume the auditor's prior (e.g., hypothesis class or public dataset) is known to the platform, making manipulation trivially possible. This paper asks whether a *private* auditor prior—built from independently collected labeled data—can provably prevent such manipulations, and quantifies the maximum unfairness a platform can still conceal.

## Input / Output

The auditor sends a batch of n audit queries (input–protected-attribute pairs) to the black-box platform and receives binary predictions; separately, the auditor holds a private labeled dataset D_a = (X_a, A_a, Y_a) of size n that defines the prior. The method outputs a binary detection decision (platform is fair and honest vs. flagged as malicious), a scalar detection score (count of prediction mismatches against D_a), and a closed-form detection probability P_uf that depends on the ratio δ/τ (unfairness of the prior relative to the risk threshold) and the audit budget n. The concealable unfairness Δ_μ is reported as the maximum Demographic Parity gap the platform can hide while keeping its detection score below the threshold.

## Method

The core mechanism is geometric: the dataset prior H_a is a ball of radius τ centered on the ground-truth labels Y_a in the labeled dataset, and the set of fair models F is a hyperplane in the same space. The optimal manipulation is the orthogonal projection of the platform's true model h_p onto F, flipping the minimum number of labels to achieve Demographic Parity. Detection reduces to checking whether this projected model h_m* falls outside the prior ball, which the auditor evaluates via a simple mismatch count on D_a. The detection probability P_uf is derived in closed form using Wallis' integrals as a volume ratio, showing it is zero when the prior is perfectly fair (δ=0) and one when the ball is tangent to F (τ=δ). Empirically, four standard fairness-repair methods (ROC Mitigation, Optimal Label Transport, Linear Relaxation, Threshold Manipulation) are repurposed as manipulation strategies on ACSEmployment (GBDT, Logistic Regression) and CelebA (LeNet), revealing that concealable unfairness converges to zero only for low-uncertainty tasks and remains nonzero for harder ones even at large audit budgets.

## Results

Across both tabular (ACSEmployment) and vision (CelebA) datasets, platforms can conceal 10–20 percentage points of Demographic Parity gap while remaining undetected, with manipulation strategies nearly able to fully hide the original model's unfairness. The detection score for honest models ranges from ~0.1 to ~0.2 depending on the dataset and model, establishing the practical threshold for flagging manipulations. Theoretically, the detection probability P_uf depends on the ratio δ/τ (unfairness of the prior over the risk threshold) and the audit budget n, with P_uf = 0 when the prior is perfectly fair (δ = 0) and P_uf = 1 when the prior ball is tangent to the fair hyperplane (τ = δ). Empirically, for CelebA Smiling (low aleatoric uncertainty), concealable unfairness converges to zero as the audit budget grows to 5,000, while for all other configurations it remains nonzero even at high budgets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper repurposes four classical fairness repair methods as manipulation strategies and compares their effectiveness at concealing unfairness while staying below the auditor's detection threshold. Linear Relaxation (LinR, Lohaus et al., 2020) and ROC Mitigation (Kamiran et al., 2012) emerge as the most effective manipulation strategies, allowing the platform to conceal the largest DP gap across both ACSEmployment and CelebA. Optimal Label Transport (OT-L, Jiang et al., 2019) and Threshold Manipulation (ThreshOpt, Hardt et al., 2016) are less effective, concealing smaller amounts of unfairness under the same detection budget. The comparison is not about which method the auditor prefers but rather which manipulation strategy a malicious platform would choose: LinR and ROC dominate because they achieve the highest concealable unfairness for a given detection score. Notably, the optimal manipulation (orthogonal projection onto the fair hyperplane) is mathematically equivalent to the best fairness repair method applied on the audit set, meaning the platform's best attack is indistinguishable from a legitimate fairness intervention.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the manipulation-proof auditing line initiated by Yan & Zhang (2022), Godinot et al. (2024), and Shamsabadi et al. (2022), which studied auditor priors defined via hypothesis class knowledge or side-channel access. The key departure is the introduction of a *dataset prior*—a private labeled dataset collected independently by the auditor—replacing model-level priors with a data-level one, and showing (Theorem 3.2) that any public prior is trivially exploitable. The geometric analysis via Wallis integrals and the repurposing of standard fairness repair methods (ROC, OT-L, LinR, ThreshOpt) as manipulation strategies are novel framings that connect the auditing literature to the fairness repair literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides regulators with a concrete, computable framework for designing manipulation-resistant audits and quantifies the fundamental limit: even with a private dataset prior and arbitrarily large audit budgets, some unfairness (10–20 DP points) can remain concealable on harder tasks. This negative answer to RQ2 is practically important—it means no single audit can guarantee full detection, motivating continuous or adaptive auditing mechanisms. Open questions include calibration of the risk threshold τ across tasks, extension beyond Demographic Parity to other fairness metrics, and whether the geometric analysis generalizes to multi-class or multi-group settings.
