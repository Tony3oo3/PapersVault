## Problem

Preference alignment (RLHF/DPO) causes LLMs to become severely miscalibrated, exhibiting overconfidence that degrades reliability in high-stakes applications. The paper identifies the root cause as preference collapse generalizing to multiple-choice settings, and asks how to restore calibration without sacrificing the alignment benefits or model accuracy.

## Input / Output

The method consumes multiple-choice questions with four options (A–D) formatted with a system prompt enforcing single-letter responses, and is fine-tuned on domain-specific QA datasets (MMLU, MedMCQA, OpenBookQA) using QLoRA. It produces a calibrated probability distribution over the four answer choices, where confidence for option j is the normalized token probability π_θ(y=j|x) / Σ π_θ(y=j'|x). The fine-tuning loss operates on both the answer token (CFT) and the full question tokens (RCFT), with the EM-based ECE regularization stratifying samples into M confidence bins.

## Method

The paper introduces a theoretical framework defining calibratable and non-calibratable regimes via upper/lower bounds on Expected Calibration Error relative to a target probabilistic generative model, separated by a critical accuracy threshold. In the calibratable regime, Calibration-Aware Fine-Tuning (CFT) simply applies standard SFT loss on domain-specific QA data, which alleviates overconfidence on incorrect answers without explicit ECE regularization. In the non-calibratable regime (reached by stronger fine-tuning with L_SFT2 that also trains on question tokens), Regularized CFT (RCFT) adds an EM-algorithm-based ECE regularization: the E-step stratifies samples into M confidence bins using max-confidence, the M-step computes per-bin accuracy q_m, and a rank-preserving tanh mapping (Equation 6) constructs the estimated generative distribution p(x) to minimize MSE divergence against the model's confidence. This directly optimizes the ECE constraint in the Lagrangian reformulation of the calibration objective, balancing accuracy and calibration via a tunable weight λ.

## Results

CFT reduces in-domain conf-ECE from 14.22%–20.10% (DPO/RLHF) to 2.39%–6.51% across four models (Llama3.1-8B-Tulu, Vicuna-7B, Olmo2-7B, Mistral-7B), while preserving or improving out-domain accuracy (e.g., Vicuna-7B out-domain accuracy rises from 0.5233 to 0.6172). RCFT further boosts in-domain accuracy to 0.6015–0.8510 (vs. 0.4344–0.6331 for DPO/RLHF) at the cost of slightly higher conf-ECE (0.0474–0.0989), operating in the non-calibratable regime. On out-domain evaluation, CFT achieves the lowest conf-ECE for Olmo2-7B (0.0225) and Mistral-7B (0.0424), outperforming both Temperature Scaling and Label Smoothing.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CFT dominates Temperature Scaling on in-domain conf-ECE for all four models (e.g., 0.0239 vs. 0.1126 on Llama3.1-8B-Tulu; 0.0379 vs. 0.0598 on Vicuna-7B) and also wins on out-domain conf-ECE for Olmo2-7B (0.0225 vs. 0.1160) and Mistral-7B (0.0424 vs. 0.0991), while TS cannot improve accuracy at all. CFT also outperforms Label Smoothing on in-domain conf-ECE across all models (e.g., 0.0651 vs. 0.1874 on Mistral-7B) and on out-domain cw-ECE for Llama3.1-8B-Tulu (0.0375 vs. 0.0639) and Olmo2-7B (0.0637 vs. 0.1298). RCFT achieves the highest in-domain accuracy among all methods for every model (0.8341, 0.6015, 0.8510, 0.8297), exceeding DPO/RLHF by 18–31 percentage points, but at the cost of higher conf-ECE than CFT (e.g., 0.0897 vs. 0.0239 on Llama3.1-8B-Tulu). The DPO/RLHF-aligned models consistently show the worst calibration (conf-ECE 0.1422–0.2010), confirming the preference-collapse-induced overconfidence identified in the paper's analysis. Win-rate evaluations (AlpacaEval, Arena-Hard, UltraFeedback) confirm that both CFT and RCFT preserve alignment quality, with CFT achieving the best AlpacaEval score for Llama3.1 (22.6%) and Mistral-7B (26.8%), and RCFT achieving the best Arena-Hard score for Olmo2-7B (20.2%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of LLM calibration and preference alignment, building on the well-studied calibration literature (temperature scaling, label smoothing, Dirichlet calibration) and the growing body of work on LLM miscalibration (Jiang et al., 2021; Xiao et al., 2022; Chen et al., 2022). Its key conceptual departure is identifying preference collapse—a known pathology of DPO/RLHF—as the mechanism that generalizes to miscalibration in multiple-choice settings, and proposing post-hoc fine-tuning (CFT/RCFT) rather than modifying the alignment pipeline itself. The theoretical contribution of calibratable vs. non-calibratable regimes, bounded by a target calibration error (TCE), is novel and provides a principled framework for when calibration can be restored without sacrificing accuracy. The EM-based ECE regularization for the non-calibratable regime draws on classical EM theory but applies it in a new context for LLM confidence calibration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical relevance is significant: in many deployment scenarios only the final aligned model is available, making post-hoc calibration essential, and the demonstrated ECE reductions (14–20% → 2–6%) are substantial for high-stakes applications. However, the scope is limited to multiple-choice tasks and 7–8B models, the theoretical constant C in the lower bound (Theorem 4.5) remains undetermined, and extension to free-form generation is left open. The calibratable/non-calibratable regime framework raises an important open question about what determines the critical accuracy threshold for a given architecture, which the authors acknowledge requires deeper analysis of transformer properties. The finding that RCFT can simultaneously boost accuracy by ~20–30% while maintaining reasonable calibration suggests that the alignment-fine-tuning trade-off is less rigid than previously assumed.
