## Problem

Existing safe policy optimization methods for Constrained MDPs face a fundamental trade-off: trust region methods like CPO guarantee constraint satisfaction only in the infinite-sample limit and oscillate around the constraint boundary with high overshoot, while penalty and barrier methods introduce a bias in the optimal solution. The paper asks whether one can reshape the trust region geometry itself so that every update is provably safe by construction, without sacrificing reward performance or introducing regularization error.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251775/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes trajectory samples from the current policy to estimate the cost advantage (via GAE-λ) and the state-averaged KL divergence, alongside a learned cost value function for computing the constraint margin δ_b = b − V_c(π_k). Unlike standard TRPO, the trust region constraint is a single quadratic form in parameter space that incorporates the cost geometry, rather than a separate linear constraint. If the policy leaves the safe set due to estimation error, a hysteresis-based recovery step minimizes cost with a relaxed threshold b_H < b before resuming normal updates.

## Method

C-TRPO modifies the Bregman mirror function underlying TRPO's trust region by adding a barrier term β·φ(b − c^T d) where φ has infinite curvature at the constraint boundary (e.g., φ(x) = x log x), yielding a Constrained KL Divergence whose sublevel sets are contained entirely within the safe occupancy set. In practice, the exact divergence is replaced by a surrogate that depends on the estimated cost advantage rather than the unknown cost value of the proposal policy, reducing the inner-loop optimization to a single quadratic constraint (versus CPO's quadratic-plus-linear constraint). The resulting update is equivalent to a natural policy gradient step with a modified Fisher-information matrix G_C = G_K + β·φ''(δ_b)·∇V_c∇V_c^T, and the continuous-time limit (C-NPG) is proven to maintain invariance of the safe set and converge to the Bregman projection of the initial policy onto the optimal safe policy set.

## Results

On 8 Safety Gymnasium tasks (4 Navigation, 4 Locomotion) evaluated over 10M environment steps with 5 seeds, C-TRPO achieves competitive expected reward (normalized to unconstrained PPO) while remaining safe at the final iterate, unlike CPO and CUP which violate constraints. C-TRPO's cost regret (cumulative constraint violations during training, normalized by CPO's) is notably lower than high-return methods like CPO, CUP, and FOCOPS, and comparable to the more conservative PCPO. The method uses φ(x)=x·log(x) as the barrier generator with β=1 and hysteresis threshold b_H=0.8b across all experiments.

## Baselines & Comparisons

C-TRPO outperforms CPO, CUP, and FOCOPS on cost regret (cumulative constraint violations during training) while matching their reward levels, and unlike these methods it remains safe at the final iterate. Compared to TRPO-Lag, which is also safe at convergence, C-TRPO achieves lower cost regret because TRPO-Lag oscillates more around the constraint threshold during training. Against PCPO, C-TRPO achieves comparable cost regret but with higher expected reward, since PCPO is more conservative and sacrifices performance. P3O achieves the lowest cost regret of all methods but at the cost of significantly lower expected reward due to maintaining a wide margin from the constraint boundary. The paper attributes C-TRPO's improved constraint satisfaction over CPO to the modified gradient preconditioner deflecting trajectories away from the constraint surface, reducing oscillation frequency and overshoot, combined with the hysteresis-based recovery mechanism that smooths updates near the boundary.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends TRPO's trust region framework by modifying the underlying Bregman divergence geometry (originally the Kakade divergence induced by negative conditional entropy, per Neu et al. 2017) to incorporate a barrier term for cost constraints, yielding a trust region that is provably contained in the safe policy set. It generalizes CPO (recovered as the β→0 limit) while avoiding CPO's separate linear constraint and the oscillation/overshoot it causes near the boundary. The approach draws on barrier methods from convex optimization and safe control, but departs from penalty-based methods (IPO, P3O) by not altering the objective function, thus preserving the set of optimal solutions without regularization bias. The natural gradient interpretation (C-NPG) connects the method to Kakade's original NPG and the Hessian gradient flow literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

C-TRPO provides a clean geometric principle—reshape the trust region so it only contains safe policies—that yields provable safety during training (invariance of the safe set) and global convergence to the optimal safe policy, properties that penalty and Lagrangian methods lack due to inherent bias. The practical overhead is minimal (one additional cost advantage estimate beyond TRPO), making it a drop-in upgrade for existing TRPO pipelines. Open questions include finite-sample regret bounds, the sensitivity to noisy cost advantage estimation, and whether the average-cost CMDP formulation can be extended to trajectory-wise or state-wise safety constraints. The method's performance is tightly coupled to divergence estimation accuracy, which remains a practical bottleneck.
