## Problem

Existing speculative decoding methods are constrained by a dualistic draft-target paradigm where the inherent capacity gap between a single small drafter and a large target model caps acceptance lengths and speedups. The field also lacks a unified theoretical framework that rigorously characterizes how multiple models interact, when adding a model is beneficial, and how to guarantee stable inference performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252513/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system consumes a text prompt (context x_{≤t}) and a chain of n models ordered by capacity (target M₁, intermediate M₂, lightweight draft M₃), along with hyperparameters K (draft block length) and μ (threshold for triggering target-model verification). It produces a sequence of generated tokens that exactly match the target model's output distribution (lossless), with tokens accepted in blocks whose average length reaches 9–11 tokens per target forward pass—substantially higher than the ~4 tokens typical of dualistic methods.

## Method

The paper introduces a polybasic speculative decoding framework where multiple draft models coordinate in a staged verification pipeline: the lightweight drafter (e.g., EAGLE2) proposes K tokens, an intermediate model (e.g., 4-bit quantized target) performs fast first-stage verification, and the full-capacity target model verifies accumulated blocks only after a threshold μ is reached. The theoretical core consists of three results: (1) a closed-form expression for optimal inference time T = Σ(N/Lᵢ)·Tᵢ + β·(N/L_{n-1})·Tₙ that decomposes cost across model pairs; (2) a model-insertion efficiency theorem giving necessary and sufficient conditions (comparing T_new/Tᵢ against acceptance-length improvement factors) under which adding a model between Mᵢ and Mᵢ₊₁ reduces total inference time; and (3) a variance bound σ² = α[1−(n²−1)αⁿ]−(n²−1)αⁿ⁺¹ / (1−α)² showing that speculative sampling with high acceptance probability p=1−α stabilizes acceptance lengths across the multi-model chain. The framework is validated empirically on Vicuna-7B, LLaMA2-Chat-7B, LLaMA3-8B, and Qwen2-7B, achieving 3.3×–4.4× speedups over autoregressive baselines while preserving output fidelity.

## Results

On SpecBench, the polybasic framework achieves overall speedup ratios of 3.48× (Vicuna-7B), 3.31× (LLaMA3-8B), 3.66× (LLaMA2-Chat-7B), and 3.28× (Qwen2-7B), with peak speedups reaching 4.43× on mathematical reasoning (Vicuna-7B) and 4.10× on multi-turn conversation (LLaMA2-Chat-7B). Average acceptance lengths range from 9.32 to 9.88 tokens across models, roughly 2–2.5× higher than the dualistic EAGLE2 baseline (3.51–4.34 tokens). The method preserves the target model's output distribution (lossless) across all evaluated tasks including translation, summarization, QA, math, and RAG.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is EAGLE2 (dynamic draft trees), a state-of-the-art dualistic speculative decoding method. The polybasic framework outperforms EAGLE2 on every model and every task: overall speedup improves by 0.87× (LLaMA3-8B: 3.31× vs 2.44×), 0.96× (Vicuna-7B: 3.48× vs 2.61×), 0.96× (LLaMA2-Chat-7B: 3.66× vs 2.70×), and 1.34× (Qwen2-7B: 3.28× vs 1.94×). Acceptance lengths are consistently 2–2.5× higher (e.g., 9.88 vs 4.34 on Vicuna-7B), confirming the theoretical prediction that multi-tier verification increases tokens accepted per forward pass. The second comparison is Cascade Speculative Drafting (CS Drafting, Chen et al. 2023b), reproduced on FLAN-T5 models to validate Theorem 3.2: inserting a mid-size model between FLAN-T5-XXL and FLAN-T5-small improves speedup from 3.19× to 3.88×, satisfying the model-insertion efficiency condition (T_new/T_i = 0.403 < 0.461). A negative control (inserting Vicuna-1B, which violates the condition) degrades speedup from 2.61× to 1.08×, confirming the theorem's predictive power.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the speculative decoding paradigm from the standard dualistic draft-target architecture (Leviathan et al., 2023) to a general n-model 'polybasic' framework, where multiple draft models of varying capacity form a verification chain. While prior multi-level approaches such as Cascade Speculative Drafting (Chen et al., 2023b) and TRIFORCE (Sun et al., 2024) introduced hierarchical drafting, they relied on empirical heuristics without a unified theoretical account. The key departure here is the provision of a formal model-insertion criterion (Theorem 3.2) and a variance analysis for acceptance lengths (Theorem 3.3) that together give practitioners a principled basis for deciding which models to include and how to configure verification stages, rather than relying on trial-and-error system design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical framework fills a notable gap in the speculative decoding literature by providing actionable conditions for system design: practitioners can now test whether a candidate intermediate model will improve or degrade throughput before committing to implementation. The dramatic degradation observed in the non-compliant insertion case (speedup collapsing from 2.61× to 1.08×) underscores that naively adding models is counterproductive, a finding that has practical implications for system architects. Open questions remain around scaling to four or more models with off-the-shelf components, integrating KV-cache optimization to mitigate long-context overhead, and extending the framework to distributed or trillion-parameter settings. The 3–4× speedups with 9–10 token acceptance lengths (roughly double EAGLE2's ~4) are meaningful for latency-sensitive deployments, though the gains are somewhat task-dependent, with summarization and RAG showing more modest improvements.
