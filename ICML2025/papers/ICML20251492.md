## Problem

GNNs on large graphs are bottlenecked by graph-scale matrix multiplications, and existing sparsification methods either modify the graph in a static, layer-agnostic way or require costly additional training to identify sparse substructures. Moreover, the theoretical impact of sparsification on multi-layer GNN learning remains inadequately characterized, with prior bounds limited to single-layer approximation or representation error.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251492/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

UNIFEWS operates on a graph (adjacency/edge list), node feature matrix X ∈ R^{n×f}, and a GNN model with diffusion matrix T and weight matrices W ∈ R^{f×f}. Sparsification is applied on-the-fly during matrix multiplication: individual entries of T and W are zeroed based on magnitude thresholds δ_a and δ_w, producing a progressively sparser graph and weight set across layers without additional memory allocation. The output is approximate node representations H̃_{(L)} and class predictions, with the sparsified graph and weights as byproducts that reduce downstream FLOPs.

## Method

UNIFEWS unifies graph edge pruning and weight pruning in a single entry-wise framework: during each GNN layer's matrix multiplication, propagation messages τ[u,v] = T[u,v]·p[v] with magnitude below threshold δ_a are discarded (equivalent to edge removal), and weight entries W[j,i] with |W[j,i]| < δ_w/‖P[:,j]‖ are zeroed. Sparsity increases progressively across layers—edges pruned at layer l remain absent at layer l+1—so deeper layers operate on strictly smaller graphs. Theoretically, the paper shows this entry-wise pruning constitutes a spectral sparsifier: under scale-free degree and Gaussian embedding assumptions, the sparsified Laplacian is ε-similar to the original with ε = O(η_a(1−η_a)^{−t}), and the multi-layer approximation error is bounded by O(ε‖H_{(L)}‖_F + δ_w). This bridges graph Laplacian smoothing optimization with GNN sparsification, providing the first precision bound that characterizes the effect of joint sparsification across iterative or decoupled GNN layers.

## Results

On the billion-edge papers100m dataset, UNIFEWS achieves 99.8× speedup over the SGC backbone (19,212s → 192.4s) and 85.7× over APPNP (15,305s → 178.5s) at 50% graph sparsity, while maintaining or improving accuracy (63.1% vs 63.3% for SGC; 62.8% vs 60.9% for APPNP). On medium-scale arxiv, UNIFEWS delivers 2.6–2.8× propagation speedup with ~2× FLOPs reduction. Across all datasets, UNIFEWS achieves 10–20× matrix operation reduction with comparable or better accuracy, including a +5.3% accuracy gain on citeseer (SGC) and +4.5% on products (APPNP) due to over-smoothing mitigation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UNIFEWS decisively outperforms both decoupled propagation personalization baselines: NDLS suffers OOM on arxiv and is 13,620× slower on cora (1,362s vs 0.10s) while dropping accuracy by 5.5 points; NIGCN is 5× slower on products (1,026s vs 203.1s) and loses 9.4 points on papers100m (53.7% vs 63.1%). For iterative GNNs (GCN/GAT), UNIFEWS outperforms all four joint compression baselines (GLT, GEBT, CGP, DSpar) on most dataset–backbone combinations at sparsity below 80%, with GEBT hitting OOM on pubmed and CGP/DSpar showing particularly poor utility on GAT due to its variable attention connections. At high sparsity (>80%), UNIFEWS's skip-connection design preserves node identity and retains accuracy no worse than the trivial transformation, whereas competitors experience significant accuracy drops. Random entry-wise sparsification (RAN) is competitive only at low sparsity ratios, confirming that UNIFEWS's magnitude-based, entry-wise thresholding is critical for maintaining effectiveness at high compression levels.

## Relation to Prior Work

UNIFEWS builds on the graph Laplacian smoothing optimization framework (Ma et al., CIKM 2021; Zhu et al., WWW 2021) to reinterpret GNN message-passing as iterative spectral graph smoothing, then introduces entry-wise sparsification as a spectral sparsifier with bounded approximation error. It departs from prior graph sparsification methods (NeuralSparse, DSpar, FastGAT) that apply one-time, layer-agnostic edge removal, and from joint compression approaches (GLT, GEBT, CGP) that require full-scale training to identify sparse substructures. The key conceptual departure is unifying graph-edge and weight-matrix pruning into a single on-the-fly entry-wise operation applicable to both iterative (GCN, GAT) and decoupled (SGC, APPNP) architectures, with progressively increasing sparsity across layers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

UNIFEWS offers a practically significant efficiency gain—up to 100× speedup on billion-edge graphs—while maintaining or improving accuracy, with a simple implementation that requires no additional training phase. The multi-layer approximation bound (Propositions 4.3–4.4) provides the first theoretical characterization of how sparsification errors compound across GNN layers, filling a gap in prior work that only analyzed single-layer or layer-agnostic approximation. A noted limitation is the assumption of graph homophily: the magnitude-based pruning criterion may remove informative edges in heterophilous graphs, where large-magnitude messages between dissimilar nodes are actually important. Open questions include whether the progressive sparsity schedule can be adapted per-node or per-community, and whether the framework extends naturally to heterophilous or dynamic graph settings.
