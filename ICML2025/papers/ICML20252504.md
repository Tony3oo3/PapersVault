## Problem

Designing optimal dynamic pricing strategies is challenging when historical data are limited, as is common when launching new products or entering new markets. While abundant data may exist from related products or markets, no prior work has explored transfer learning for nonparametric contextual dynamic pricing, and no minimax lower bound existed under the standard Lipschitz condition for general dimension d ≥ 1.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252504/cropped_page6_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes a pre-collected source dataset of (covariate, price, revenue) triplets drawn from a distribution P_X that differs from the target covariate distribution Q_X (covariate shift), while both domains share the same unknown Lipschitz reward function f. At each target time step, the algorithm observes a covariate X_t ∈ [0,1]^d and outputs a price p_t ∈ [0,1]; the smallest exploration radius r̃ (computed from n_Q, n_P, γ, κ) controls the finest partition granularity. The theoretical output is a cumulative regret bound of order n_Q {n_Q + (κn_P)^((d+3)/(d+3+γ))}^{-1/(d+3)} up to logarithmic factors, matching a minimax lower bound.

## Method

TLDP is a UCB-type algorithm that adaptively partitions the joint covariate-price space [0,1]^d × [0,1] using contextual zooming, with the key innovation that source data provides a 'head start' for partitioning: each ball is initialized with source-data counts and cumulative revenues, and the refinement threshold T_B^Q is set to zero when sufficient source observations already occupy the ball. At each step, the algorithm selects the ball maximizing a revenue potential index I_t(B) that balances the empirical mean reward, ball radius, a UCB confidence term, and ℓ_∞-distance to neighboring balls, then samples a price uniformly within the selected ball's domain. The confidence width and partitioning threshold both incorporate the adjusted source sample size (κn_P)^((d+3)/(d+3+γ)), where γ (transfer exponent) quantifies covariate distribution overlap and κ (exploration coefficient) quantifies price-space coverage in the source data. This yields a phase transition: when the adjusted source size dominates n_Q, regret scales as n_Q r̃ (source-dominated), otherwise it reduces to the target-only rate n_Q^{(d+2)/(d+3)}.

## Results

On the auto loan dataset (East South Central as target, ~8K observations), TLDP with full source data (n_P = n) achieves a mean cumulative regret of 50.23 (Mountain) and 48.72 (Pacific), substantially outperforming ABE (70.81) and ExUCB (64.91). Without source data (n_P = 0), TLDP incurs slightly higher regret (71.95) than both baselines, but adding even 25% of source data drops regret to 55.63/51.61, and regret decreases monotonically with more source data. Theoretically, TLDP achieves a minimax-optimal regret bound of order n_Q {n_Q + (κn_P)^{(d+3)/(d+3+γ)}}^{-1/(d+3)} (up to log factors), with a matching lower bound established for the first time under a Lipschitz reward function.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TLDP with full source data (n_P = n) outperforms both baselines by a wide margin: 50.23 vs. 70.81 (ABE) and 64.91 (ExUCB) on the Mountain source, and 48.72 vs. 70.81 and 64.91 on the Pacific source — a 29–31% reduction in cumulative regret over ABE and 24–25% over ExUCB. Without source data (n_P = 0), TLDP is slightly worse than both baselines (71.95 vs. 70.81 for ABE and 64.91 for ExUCB), confirming that the transfer learning component is essential for its advantage. In simulation experiments, TLDP consistently outperforms all alternatives under the covariate shift setting (Scenarios 1 and 2), with regret growing sublinearly in n_Q as predicted by theory. ExUCB performs relatively well in Scenario 1 (linear reward structure) but degrades in Scenario 2 (fully nonparametric reward), while ABE shows low variance but higher regret. TLDP also demonstrates robustness under moderate posterior drift (Scenario 3), where reward functions differ between source and target, maintaining competitive performance despite model misspecification.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the contextual zooming framework of Slivkins (2011) for adaptive partitioning in metric-space bandits to the continuous action space of dynamic pricing, and adapts the transfer learning under covariate shift paradigm from Cai et al. (2024a) (discrete-action contextual MAB) to the continuous-price setting. The key methodological departure is handling the joint covariate-price space (dimension d+1) with a UCB index that balances estimated reward, ball radius, uncertainty, and inter-ball distance, rather than the simpler discrete-arm indices used in prior MAB transfer learning. The nonparametric dynamic pricing baselines (ABE from Chen & Gallego 2020, ExUCB from Luo et al. 2022) address the target-only setting with additional structural assumptions (local strong concavity, semi-parametric revenue form), whereas TLDP operates under a more general Lipschitz condition on the full reward function.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to establish both a regret upper bound and a matching minimax lower bound for transfer learning in nonparametric contextual dynamic pricing, and as a byproduct provides the first minimax lower bound under a pure Lipschitz condition for general dimension d ≥ 1 in the target-only setting. The phase transition between source-dominated and target-dominated regimes (governed by the transfer exponent γ and exploration coefficient κ) gives practitioners a clear diagnostic for when transfer learning will help. Key open questions include adaptively estimating γ and κ from data (currently assumed known), extending to multiple source domains with heterogeneous transfer parameters, and developing theory for the posterior drift setting where reward functions differ across domains.
