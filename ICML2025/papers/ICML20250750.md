## Problem

Post-hoc unlearning of memorized sequences from LLMs is fundamentally limited because standard training induces mechanistic entanglement: the same neurons that support general language capabilities also store memorized content, making clean removal impossible without degrading model performance. The paper shows this entanglement is not incidental but is an implicit bias of gradient descent, and that even naive forced-localization approaches (gradient masking) fail due to co-adaptation between segregated components.

## Input / Output

The method consumes pretraining text sequences alongside per-document sequence IDs (computed as a hash of document tokens), which are interleaved into the token stream at odd positions during tokenization to enable seamless streaming. At inference, the model produces standard next-token predictions; to remove memorization of a specific sequence, the corresponding memorization sink neurons are simply dropped from the forward pass. The sequence ID determines a deterministic mask over sink neurons, so the same document always activates the same subset of memorization neurons across all its repetitions.

## Method

MemSinks partitions each transformer MLP hidden layer into shared generalization neurons (fraction g, active for all sequences) and memorization sink neurons (fraction 1−g, selectively activated per sequence). A sequence ID deterministically selects which subset of sink neurons is active for a given document, so different sequences activate different sink neurons, reducing gradient interference and shielding each sequence's memorization from forgetting by other examples. This exploits the natural training-dynamics difference: generalization signals are consistently reinforced across sequences, while memorization signals are cyclically learned and forgotten due to interference; by isolating memorization in protected sink neurons, it accumulates there rather than diffusing into shared components. The paper provides theoretical proofs that standard gradient flow prefers entangled memorization solutions (Theorem 3.1) and that gradient masking fails due to co-adaptation (Theorem 4.1), then empirically validates MemSinks at 360M and 1.7B parameter scale on SlimPajama+TinyStories, showing it closes over 50% of the memorization loss gap while matching standard training validation loss.

## Results

On the small-scale TinyStories setting (GPT-2-Medium, ~344M params), MemSinks achieves validation loss comparable to standard training while dropping memorization neurons increases the loss on repeated sequences to roughly 66% of a non-memorizing model, closing over 50% of the memorization gap. At larger scale (SmolLM 360M and 1.7B on 1B and 2B tokens of SlimPajama + TinyStories), MemSinks mitigates memorization by at least 50% of the train-validation loss gap while matching or outperforming the deduplication baseline on validation loss. The benefits of MemSinks scale with model size, with larger models achieving a better unlearning–degradation tradeoff.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the post-hoc localization methods of Chang et al. (2024b) (Hard-Concrete pruning and Integrated Gradients), MemSinks achieves a strictly better unlearning–model-degradation tradeoff on natural repeated sequences (TS-Repetition), where both post-hoc methods struggle to remove memorization without significant validation loss increase. Integrated Gradients is particularly weak on natural sequences while being effective on atypical canaries, confirming the mechanistic entanglement hypothesis. Against the gradient-masking (forced localization) approach of Cloud et al. (2024), MemSinks wins on both axes: gradient-masked training achieves significantly worse validation loss than standard training even before any removal, and dropping the memorization neurons further degrades performance due to co-adaptation, whereas MemSinks maintains validation loss comparable to standard training and enables clean removal. In the large-scale SlimPajama+TinyStories setting, MemSinks also outperforms the deduplication baseline (which sees TinyStories only once) on validation loss, showing it preserves the generalization benefits of repeated data while isolating the memorization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant post-hoc unlearning paradigm (fine-tuning, neuron pruning, integrated gradients) by arguing that mechanistic entanglement between memorization and generalization makes such approaches fundamentally limited for natural sequences. It builds conceptually on the localization framework of Maini et al. (2023) and the gradient-routing idea of Cloud et al. (2024), but demonstrates that rigid gradient partitioning fails due to co-adaptation between components. The MemSinks method repurposes the selective-activation mechanism familiar from mixture-of-experts architectures, but with a key distinction: activation is deterministically tied to sequence identity rather than learned, and the goal is isolation of memorization rather than capacity expansion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first proof-of-concept at billion-parameter/billion-token scale that simultaneous generalization and clean memorization isolation is achievable, closing over 50% of the memorization loss gap while matching standard training validation loss. However, the approach critically depends on sequence ID metadata (hashes of document tokens) to assign dropout masks, and robustness beyond 10% ID noise degrades significantly. Open questions include efficient generation of sequence IDs at web-scale, verification against adversarial extraction, and whether the benefits persist at frontier model scales where memorization dynamics may differ.
