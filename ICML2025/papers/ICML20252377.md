## Problem

Fair spectral clustering (Fair SC) enforces group fairness by adding a linear constraint F^T H = 0 to the standard spectral clustering eigenvalue problem, but existing solvers (o-FSC, s-FSC) rely on eigendecomposition of a modified n×n Laplacian, which scales cubically and limits applicability to small datasets. No prior work had integrated the fairness constraints into a difference-of-convex (DC) optimization framework, leaving a gap between the theoretical DC structure of spectral clustering and the practical need for scalable fair clustering.

## Input / Output

The method takes a positive definite affinity matrix K ∈ R^{n×n} (or a graph adjacency matrix W), a group indicator matrix F ∈ R^{n×h} encoding h demographic groups, and a target number of clusters k. It produces a fair spectral embedding H ∈ R^{n×k} whose rows are fed into k-means to yield discrete cluster labels. Notably, the algorithm never explicitly forms M² = (D^{-1/2}KD^{-1/2})²; it only performs matrix-vector products with M, and the fairness constraint is enforced on the augmented variable Y = MH rather than on H directly, which empirically preserves the same group balance.

## Method

The core contribution is casting Fair SC as a DC minimization min_H g(H) + h(Y) − f(MH) s.t. MH = Y, where g encodes the Stiefel manifold constraint, h encodes the fairness constraint, and f is a quadratic. A novel variable augmentation couples the ADMM auxiliary variable Y with MH (rather than H), which is the key design choice that makes the H-subproblem amenable to DC dualization. The resulting dual problem is solved with L-BFGS, requiring only the SVD of a small k×k matrix V^T M² V per iteration (O(k³)) instead of an n×n eigendecomposition. The Y-subproblem is solved in closed form via a nullspace parameterization Y = QZ. The overall per-iteration cost is O(n²k) dominated by matrix multiplications with M, yielding 4–54× speedups over prior eigendecomposition-based methods while achieving comparable clustering cost and balance.

## Results

On the m-SBM synthetic benchmark (k=50, h=5), the proposed method achieves a balance of 1.0 (perfect fairness) while running in 9.19s at n=10000, compared to 495.70s for o-FSC and 107.94s for s-FSC — a ~54× speedup over o-FSC and ~12× over s-FSC. On real-world datasets with k=25, the method achieves 4–8× speedups over the state-of-the-art s-FSC (e.g., 4.59s vs 19.08s on LastFMNet; 25.85s vs 166.92s on 4area) while maintaining comparable clustering cost and balance. The method also outperforms alternative fair SC formulations (FFSC, UFSC) by 7–12× in runtime with better spectral clustering cost and tighter fairness/orthogonality constraint satisfaction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against o-FSC (Kleindessner et al., 2019), the proposed method is 4–54× faster across all tested settings while achieving identical balance (1.0 on synthetic, comparable on real data); o-FSC times out (>500s) on Census and 4area. Against s-FSC (Wang et al., 2023), the speedup is 4–12× with clustering cost and balance within noise of the exact eigendecomposition solution (e.g., cost 1.086 vs 1.057 on LastFMNet; balance 0.0093 vs 0.0105). Against FFSC (Feng et al., 2024), the proposed method is 7–12× faster and achieves substantially better spectral clustering cost (e.g., 1.09 vs 1067.91 on LastFMNet) and tighter fairness constraint violation (0.000014 vs 4.032). Against UFSC (Zhang & Wang, 2024), the proposed method is faster on smaller datasets (LastFM: 4.59s vs 16.36s; Thyroid: 7.38s vs 95.08s) and is the only method that completes on Census and 4area, where UFSC times out. Overall, the proposed method wins on runtime across all four baselines while matching or exceeding them on clustering quality and fairness metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Fair SC formulation of Kleindessner et al. (2019), which added a group-fairness linear constraint F^T H = 0 to the spectral clustering trace-maximization problem, and on Wang et al. (2023), who accelerated the required eigendecomposition via the Arnoldi method. The key departure is algorithmic: rather than solving the eigenvalue problem (O(n³)), the authors recast the problem in the difference-of-convex (DC) framework and design a novel variable augmentation that couples the fairness constraint to MH rather than H, enabling an ADMM-type algorithm whose subproblems are solved by gradient-based optimization (L-BFGS on the DC dual) and small SVDs. The DC dualization technique itself is adapted from Tonin et al. (2023) on kernel PCA, but the integration of fairness constraints within the DC-ADMM loop is new.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The primary barrier to deploying fair spectral clustering on real-world datasets has been the cubic-cost eigendecomposition; this paper removes that bottleneck, achieving 4–54× speedups while preserving comparable balance and clustering cost, making fair SC practical for datasets with tens of thousands of points. Convergence guarantees remain limited to first-order conditions under regularity assumptions (via Magnusson et al., 2016), and the method optimizes with M² rather than M, a modification whose theoretical equivalence to the original problem is not formally established. Open directions include extending the framework to other constraint types (must-link/cannot-link), more general fairness metrics, and kernel-based out-of-sample settings, as the authors note.
