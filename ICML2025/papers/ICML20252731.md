## Problem

Existing safe robot learning methods (BarrierNet, dQP, dMPC) are inefficient due to batch QP solving during training, can only implement a single safety enforcement layer, and produce unstable control signals under noisy inputs—making them unsuitable for building larger safe foundation models. The paper addresses how to merge multiple safety-critical learning models into a scalable architecture that retains formal safety guarantees while improving robustness and training efficiency.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252731/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For 2D robot and manipulation tasks, the input is the system state vector concatenated with the goal; for vision-based driving, the input is a front-view RGB image of shape 3×45×155. In the attention variant (ABNet-att), different heads receive cropped sub-images focusing on specific regions (left lane boundary, right lane boundary, lane center, etc.). The output is a safe control vector (steering rate + acceleration for driving, angular speed + acceleration for the 2D robot, or two joint angular accelerations for the manipulator), structured as a convex combination of per-head explicit-Barrier outputs with non-negative trainable weights summing to one.

## Method

The core contribution is the explicit-Barrier, which derives a closed-form solution to the differentiable CBF QP by reducing the constraint set to two safety specifications (via log-sum-exp or min functions), yielding an analytic expression with gate functions that explicitly encode the barrier—eliminating the need for iterative QP solvers during training. The ABNet architecture stacks multiple such heads (each potentially attending to different observation features), connected via shared penalty parameters (cross-connections) that ensure a common HOCBF structure across heads. Heads are fused via a convex combination with trainable weights, and the paper proves (Theorem 3.1) that this fusion preserves forward invariance of the safe set using Nagumo's theorem. Training supports both incremental mode (train heads independently, then fuse) and one-shot mode (end-to-end backpropagation), enabling scalable construction of larger safe models by simply adding heads.

## Results

On the VISTA vision-based autonomous driving benchmark (Table 3), ABNet achieves 0% crash rate and 100% obstacle passing rate with a positive safety margin (SAFETY = 1.455), while all safety-guaranteed baselines (DFB, BNet) still crash in 3–4% of runs and pass only 33–39% of obstacles due to excessive deceleration. ABNet also produces the lowest control uncertainty (u1 = 0.168, u2 = 0.316) among all models. On the 2D obstacle avoidance task (Table 1), ABNet-100 achieves SAFETY = 10.122 with u2 uncertainty of 0.013, compared to BNet's SAFETY = 5.045 and u2 uncertainty of 0.047.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the vanilla end-to-end baselines (V-E2E, E2Es-MCD, E2Es-DR), ABNet trades a small drop in passing rate (100% vs. 91–94%) for a dramatic safety improvement: all E2E variants violate safety constraints severely (SAFETY ≈ −60) and crash in 6–9% of runs, while ABNet achieves 0% crash with a positive safety margin. Against the safety-guaranteed baselines DFB and BNet, ABNet is decisively superior: DFB and BNet still crash in 4% and 3% of runs respectively and pass only 39% and 33% of obstacles (because they learn excessive deceleration rather than steering around obstacles), whereas ABNet passes 100% with 0% crash. BNet-UP (policy merging with uncertainty propagation) achieves the lowest crash rate among non-ABNet methods (2%) but still has a negative safety margin (−23.252) and only 35% passing rate. On the 2D obstacle avoidance and robot manipulation tasks, ABNet-100 consistently achieves the lowest control uncertainty (e.g., u2 = 0.013 in 2D, 0.004 in manipulation) and the least conservativeness among safety-guaranteed methods, while E2Es-MCD achieves the best MSE but violates safety. The paper also demonstrates that under 50% input noise (Table 4), ABNet maintains 0% crash while BNet's crash rate jumps to 23% and BNet-UP to 24%, confirming that standard model-merging approaches degrade safety under perturbation.

## Relation to Prior Work

ABNet directly extends BarrierNet (Xiao et al., 2023) by deriving a closed-form explicit solution to the differentiable CBF QP, eliminating the need for batch QP solving during training that plagues the implicit formulation. The multi-head architecture draws on mixture-of-experts ideas but departs from standard MoE by using a convex combination of safe controllers whose weights provably preserve the CBF/HOCBF safety invariance (via Nagumo's theorem). The HOCBF constraint structure (Xiao & Belta, 2021) and adaptive CBF framework (Xiao et al., 2021) provide the theoretical backbone for the safety proofs, while the VISTA simulator (Amini et al., 2022) serves as the driving evaluation platform. The key conceptual departure from prior safe-learning work is that safety is made explicit in the model's closed-form output rather than implicit in a solved optimization, enabling both interpretability and efficient incremental scaling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a critical gap: existing safe-learning methods (BarrierNet, DFB) are computationally expensive, hard to train stably, and do not scale to larger models, limiting their use in building safe foundation models for robotics. The demonstrated 100% obstacle passing rate in vision-based driving (vs. 33–39% for BNet/DFB) and 0% crash rate under 50% input noise highlight practical deployment advantages. However, limitations remain: all heads must share the same safety constraints, safety specifications must be known a priori, and merging occurs only in output space rather than parameter space. Open questions include whether the explicit-barrier approach extends to contact-rich manipulation, whether safety specifications can be learned from data within the ABNet framework, and whether the convex-combination fusion generalizes to heterogeneous constraint sets.
