## Problem

Both dominant self-supervised pre-training paradigms for vision—Masked Image Modeling (MIM) and Contrastive Learning (CL)—struggle with efficient visual representation. MIM prioritizes pixel-level reconstruction over high-level semantic concepts, while CL suffers from conceptual redundancy where hand-crafted augmentations produce views that differ primarily at the pixel level rather than the semantic concept level. The paper asks how to bridge efficient visual representation with effective conceptual pre-training without the overhead of reconstruction or complex augmentation pipelines.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252857/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB images at 224×224 resolution, which are split into 16×16 patches and randomly masked at a low global ratio (30–40%). The visible patches are then divided into two non-overlapping groups, each receiving its own [CLS] token. The output is a pair of D-dimensional [CLS] feature vectors per image (one per group), which serve as the visual representation for downstream tasks such as classification, detection, and segmentation. No auxiliary decoder, linear head, or reconstruction target is produced.

## Method

OCL generates contrastive views by randomly masking image patches and splitting the remaining visible patches into two non-overlapping groups, each processed independently by a ViT encoder with its own [CLS] token. The key insight is that this token-level occlusion creates views with fine-grained semantic concept differences (rather than pixel-level differences from traditional augmentations), effectively diminishing the conceptual redundancy inherent in images. A T-distributed spherical (T-SP) similarity metric with a concentration parameter κ is applied to the [CLS] tokens, and a symmetric cross-entropy loss (inspired by CLIP) drives the model to maximize intra-image agreement while separating inter-image tokens within a mini-batch. The approach requires no hand-crafted data augmentations, no auxiliary modules (no momentum encoder, no MLP head, no decoder), and no pixel reconstruction, making it substantially more efficient than both MIM and conventional CL baselines.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K with ViT-B/16, OCL achieves 74.2% top-1 linear probing and 83.4% top-1 fine-tuning accuracy using only 12.0 G FLOPs and no hand-crafted data augmentation, compared to 17.5 G FLOPs for MAE and 74.7 G for MoCo v3. Scaling to ViT-L/16, OCL reaches 77.9% linear probing and 85.8% fine-tuning accuracy, completing pre-training in 133 hours on 4 A100 GPUs. On COCO object detection (ViT-B/16), OCL attains 51.5 AP^box and 45.5 AP^mask, and on ADE20K semantic segmentation, 46.1 mIoU (ViT-B/16) and 53.2 mIoU (ViT-L/16). For domain generalization, OCL scores 42.2/52.3/37.6 on ImageNet-A/R/Sketch with only 800 epochs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On ImageNet-1K fine-tuning (ViT-B/16), OCL (83.4%) trails the strongest MIM+CL hybrids—ConMIM (85.3%) and MixMAE (84.6%)—by 1.9 and 1.2 points respectively, but outperforms pure MIM (MAE 83.6%, BEiT 83.2%) and pure CL (MoCo v3 83.2%, DINO 82.8%) baselines. On linear probing, OCL (74.2%) substantially surpasses MAE (68.0%) and MixMAE (61.2%) but falls short of iBOT (79.5%) and DINO (78.2%), which rely on student-teacher architectures with 74.7 G FLOPs versus OCL's 12.0 G. The efficiency advantage is the paper's central claim: OCL uses 6.2× fewer FLOPs than MoCo v3/DINO and 23% fewer than MixMAE, while requiring no hand-crafted augmentations or auxiliary modules. On COCO detection (ViT-B/16), OCL gains +3.6 AP^box over MoCo-v3 (51.5 vs 47.9) without view augmentations, and on ADE20K segmentation achieves 53.2 mIoU with ViT-L/16, competitive with MixMAE (53.8) at 800 vs 600 epochs. For domain generalization, OCL (42.2/52.3/37.6 on IN-A/R/S) is within 1.6/0.2/0.7 of SiameseIM (43.8/52.5/38.3) while using half the training epochs (800 vs 1600), and matches or exceeds iBOT (42.4/50.9/36.9) on IN-R and IN-S.

## Relation to Prior Work

OCL recombines two established pre-training paradigms—masked image modeling and contrastive learning—in a way that departs from both. It borrows the random patch-masking mechanism from MAE but repurposes it not for pixel-level reconstruction (as in MIM) nor for generating augmented views (as in SimCLR/MoCo v3/DINO), but for creating semantically distinct intra-image views that feed a contrastive objective. The symmetric cross-entropy loss structure follows CLIP, and the T-distributed spherical similarity metric is adopted from prior work on intra-class feature regularization. Unlike hybrid MIM+CL methods (iBOT, SiameseIM, ConMIM) that combine both objectives with auxiliary teacher-student modules, OCL eliminates reconstruction entirely and relies solely on contrastive alignment between masked views, requiring no hand-crafted augmentations or additional network branches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical appeal of OCL lies in its minimalism: no auxiliary encoders, no momentum updates, no hand-crafted view augmentations, and no reconstruction decoder—yet it achieves competitive ImageNet-1K accuracy with substantially lower FLOPs (12.0G vs. 15.6G for MixMAE) and shorter pre-training time. The ablation showing that an MLP projection head is not only unnecessary but slightly detrimental to linear probing accuracy challenges a common design assumption in contrastive learning. However, downstream results on COCO detection and ADE20K segmentation are competitive rather than state-of-the-art, and the method still depends on large effective batch sizes (2,048–9,600), limiting its accessibility. Open questions include whether the approach scales to billion-parameter models, whether the masking ratio and T-SP concentration parameters transfer across domains, and whether the efficiency advantage persists at larger resolutions or with more diverse pre-training corpora.
