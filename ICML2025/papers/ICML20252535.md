## Problem

Existing temporal causal inference methods focus on forward prediction of future trajectories, but decision-makers often need the inverse problem: identifying which intervention sequence will guide a system to a specific target outcome. Directly applying counterfactual estimation methods (e.g., CRN, RMSN) to this task fails because counterfactuals are unobservable, so prediction errors compound over the horizon and cannot be assessed, leading to misleading intervention selections where the predicted-best sequence is not the true-best.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a patient's historical trajectory (time-varying covariates, past treatments, past outcomes, and static features) up to time t, a desired target outcome Y_target at horizon t+τ, and the prediction length τ. It produces an optimal intervention sequence a*_{t,τ} (continuous in [0,1]^d or binary {0,1}^d) obtained by gradient-based optimization treating interventions as learnable parameters, and ELBO scores that rank candidate sequences by their conditional likelihood of achieving the target. The intervention sequence is planned in full advance (all τ steps simultaneously) rather than decided sequentially.

## Method

VCIP introduces latent state variables Z_s at each timestep to encode the system's temporal evolution, with LSTM-based generative and inference networks parameterizing Gaussian conditionals for state transitions and outcome decoding. The key theoretical contribution (Theorem 4.1) uses the g-formula to prove that the interventional log-likelihood can be approximated by an observational ELBO plus a confounding adjustment term, enabling reliable training from observational data without access to the true interventional distribution. The training objective (Eq. 19) combines KL divergence between inference and generative models, a reconstruction term for the target outcome, and a λ-weighted term that bridges interventional and observational likelihoods via intervention probability models (Beta for continuous, Bernoulli for binary). At inference time, model parameters are frozen and the intervention sequence is optimized end-to-end via gradient descent on the ELBO, avoiding the error accumulation inherent in predicting and then comparing counterfactual outcomes to the target.

## Results

On the tumor simulation dataset (γ=4, strong confounding), VCIP achieves the lowest target distance across all prediction horizons, from 0.29 at τ=1 to 1.09 at τ=12, outperforming the best baseline RMSN (0.30 and 1.47 respectively) by 3.3% and 25.9%. Under distinct intervention strategies between training and test, VCIP's advantage grows to 21.6% at τ=1 (0.20 vs. RMSN's 0.37) and 25.7% at τ=12 (1.36 vs. 1.83). On the real-world MIMIC-III dataset, VCIP achieves the highest Ground Truth Ranking Position (GRP) across all prediction horizons (τ=2,4,6,8), while baselines degrade with longer horizons due to compounding prediction errors.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VCIP consistently outperforms all four baselines (RMSN, CRN, CT, ACTIN) on target distance across every prediction horizon and confounding level tested. The closest competitor is RMSN, which VCIP beats by a narrow 3.3% margin at τ=1 (0.29 vs. 0.30) but by 25.9% at τ=12 (1.09 vs. 1.47), demonstrating that the advantage grows substantially with prediction horizon. CRN and ACTIN trail RMSN by 10–20% at short horizons and by 30–50% at long horizons, while CT (the Transformer-based method) performs worst overall, reaching 2.14 at τ=12 versus VCIP's 1.09. Under the more challenging setting of distinct intervention strategies between training and test (Table 2), VCIP still leads all baselines, with a 21.6% improvement over RMSN at τ=1 and 25.7% at τ=12. On the MIMIC-III dataset, VCIP achieves the highest GRP at every horizon (τ=2,4,6,8), whereas all counterfactual-estimation baselines show performance degradation as τ increases due to accumulated prediction errors.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the temporal counterfactual estimation lineage (RMSN, CRN, CT, ACTIN) by reframing the objective: rather than predicting potential outcomes under candidate interventions and comparing them to a target, it directly models the conditional probability of achieving a target outcome via variational inference. The g-formula serves as the theoretical bridge connecting the intractable interventional likelihood to a tractable observational ELBO, a connection that prior counterfactual estimation methods use for bias correction but not for planning. The work also positions itself against Dynamic Treatment Regimes, explicitly distinguishing individual-level target achievement from population-level policy optimization, and borrows the latent-state sequential architecture (LSTM-based generative/inference models) common to variational autoencoder frameworks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important gap in personalized healthcare: clinicians need to plan multi-step intervention sequences for individual patients to hit specific physiological targets (e.g., blood glucose ranges), a task that neither counterfactual estimation nor DTRs directly solve. Key open questions include the reliance on sequential ignorability (unobserved confounding remains unaddressed), the computational cost of variational inference combined with gradient-based sequence optimization limiting real-time deployment, and whether the approach generalizes beyond the two datasets tested. The finding that VCIP's ranking accuracy improves with longer prediction horizons while all baselines degrade suggests a fundamental advantage of likelihood-based over point-estimation approaches for sequential planning, though broader validation across clinical domains is needed.
