## Problem

Standard noise distributions (Gaussian, Laplace) are not optimal across all composition regimes in differential privacy: Cactus is optimal for large compositions, Staircase for single composition, but no unified framework exists for the moderate composition regime (roughly 10–40 compositions) where real-world applications like census data release operate. This paper addresses the gap by proposing a single optimization framework that produces noise distributions tailored to any fixed number of compositions and sensitivity, outperforming all existing mechanisms in the moderate regime.

## Input / Output

The algorithm receives user-specified privacy parameter δ, number of compositions N_c, noise standard deviation σ, query sensitivity s, and a discrete/continuous flag, then outputs a finite vector of bin probabilities defining a symmetric piecewise-constant PDF (continuous) or PMF (discrete) with geometric tails beyond bin N. The Rényi order α is not user-specified but is automatically co-optimized via Newton steps on the moments accountant formula, starting from the Gaussian-optimal α = √(2 log(1/δ)/N_c)·(σ/s) + 1. The cost constraint is enforced as a variance (MSE) upper bound C = σ², and the final (ε,δ) guarantee is computed post-hoc using the Connect-the-Dots accountant.

## Method

The core formulation is a minimax problem: minimize over the noise distribution P_Z the maximum Rényi divergence D_α(P_Z ‖ T_t P_Z) over all shifts t in [−s,s], subject to an expected-cost constraint. The infinite-dimensional search is reduced to a finite-dimensional convex problem by restricting to symmetric piecewise-constant PDFs (or PMFs) with geometric tails, parameterized by N+1 bin probabilities. Theorem 3.1 proves the objective is convex in P_Z and that symmetry suffices, while Theorem 3.6 shows the inner maximization over t reduces to a finite set of bin-aligned shifts. The resulting convex problem is solved by preconditioned gradient descent with transformation matrix M = diag(p)^{−1}, which keeps the probability vector strictly feasible (avoiding infinite Rényi divergence from support mismatches), combined with backtracking line search and projection onto the cost/normalization constraint manifold. Every T iterations, α is updated via a Newton step on the moments accountant, so the framework automatically recovers Cactus (α→1) and Staircase (α→∞) as limiting cases while finding genuinely new optimal distributions in between.

## Results

For 10 compositions, sensitivity s=1, δ=10⁻⁶, and noise standard deviation σ=8, the optimized RDP noise achieves ε=1.62, compared to ε=1.76 for Laplace/Staircase and ε=1.74 for Gaussian/Cactus — a minimum 6.89% improvement in the privacy parameter. On three medical datasets (Breast Cancer, Diabetes, UCI Heart Disease) with δ=10⁻⁶ and 10 queries, replacing Gaussian noise with the optimized RDP noise yields 8–12% MSE improvement across all tested ε values (0.62–1.05). The framework also recovers the Cactus distribution (α→1, large composition) and Staircase distribution (α→∞, single composition) as special cases, confirming consistency with known optimal mechanisms in their respective regimes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The optimized RDP noise consistently outperforms all four baselines (Gaussian, Laplace, Staircase, Cactus) in the moderate composition regime (roughly 10–40 compositions). Against the Gaussian mechanism (Abadi et al., 2016), the RDP noise achieves a 6.89% lower ε at the same σ and δ in the headline comparison (Figure 1), and 8–12% MSE improvement on three medical datasets (Table 1). Against the Laplace mechanism (Dwork et al., 2006), the RDP noise achieves ε=1.62 versus ε=1.76 for Laplace/Staircase at 10 compositions, σ=8, δ=10⁻⁶. Against the Staircase mechanism (Geng et al., 2015), which is provably optimal only for single composition and pure DP, the RDP noise still wins in the moderate regime while recovering Staircase exactly as α→∞. Against the Cactus mechanism (Alghamdi et al., 2022), optimal for large compositions, the RDP noise achieves ε=1.62 versus ε=1.74 at 10 compositions, and recovers Cactus exactly as α→1. Figure 4 further shows that no single baseline dominates across all (σ, compositions) settings, whereas the RDP noise provides a robust default that avoids the risk of mis-selecting among existing mechanisms.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies and extends the line of work on optimal DP noise mechanisms—Staircase (Geng et al., 2015) for single-composition pure DP, Cactus (Alghamdi et al., 2022) for the large-composition regime, and Schrödinger (Alghamdi et al., 2023) for vanishing sensitivity—into a single convex optimization framework parameterized by the Rényi order α. The finite-dimensional piecewise-constant parameterization with geometric tails directly borrows from the Cactus mechanism construction, while the moments accountant (Abadi et al., 2016) provides the bridge from RDP to (ε,δ)-DP. The key departure is that prior work identified optimal distributions only at the extremes of α (α→1 or α→∞), leaving the moderate composition regime (roughly 10–40 compositions) unaddressed; this paper fills that gap by optimizing at intermediate α values and showing the resulting distribution is distinct from all known mechanisms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is that practitioners in the moderate composition regime—common in census-style repeated queries or small-batch DP-SGD—no longer need to guess among Gaussian, Laplace, Staircase, and Cactus; the framework produces a distribution that is consistently 7–12% better in ε or MSE than the best standard alternative. The fact that no single known mechanism dominates across all (σ, N_c, δ) combinations is itself a useful negative result for mechanism selection. Open questions include whether the geometric-tail assumption remains tight for very skewed optimal distributions, how the optimization scales when N (number of bins) must grow for very small σ, and whether the approach extends naturally to multi-dimensional queries or non-quadratic cost functions.
