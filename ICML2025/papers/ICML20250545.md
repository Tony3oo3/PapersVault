## Problem

In bilevel optimization, solving the lower-level problem and computing Hessian inverse-vector products for hypergradient estimation are typically treated as separate tasks, despite both relying on the same Hessian ∇²₂₂g. Prior work (SHINE) recognized this shared structure but provided only asymptotic analysis without convergence rates or computational complexity bounds, leaving the practical and theoretical benefits of this intrinsic hypergradient structure unrealized.

## Input / Output

The framework takes an upper-level objective f, a lower-level objective g (strongly convex in y with parameter μ), and initial iterates (x⁰, y⁰, u⁰) that must lie within a predefined neighborhood of the true lower-level solution and Hessian-inverse solution (BOX 1/BOX 2). It produces the upper-level solution x* and, at each iteration, an approximated hypergradient d_x^k = ∇₁f(x^k, y^k) − ∇²₁₂g(x^k, y^k)u^k. The key structural constraint is that the Hessian ∇²₂₂g(x^k, y^k) is computed once per outer iteration and reused for both the lower-level Newton direction and the Hessian inverse-vector product, enabling potential parallel evaluation of the two quadratic subproblems.

## Method

NBO (Newton-based framework for Bilevel Optimization) exploits the shared Hessian by reformulating the inexact Newton step for the lower-level problem and the Hessian inverse-vector product as two strongly convex quadratic subproblems with the same Hessian H_k = ∇²₂₂g(x^k, y^k). The inexact Newton direction v^k approximates [∇²₂₂g]⁻¹∇₂g (the lower-level Newton step), while u^k approximates [∇²₂₂g]⁻¹∇₂f (the Hessian inverse-vector product), both computed via T steps of gradient descent or conjugate gradient on their respective quadratics. The approximated hypergradient is then d_x^k = ∇₁f(x^k, y^k) − ∇²₁₂g(x^k, y^k)u^k, and x is updated by a gradient step. The Lyapunov-based convergence analysis shows that T = Θ(κ) inner iterations yield a quadratic decay in the lower-level error, leading to O(κ³/ε) gradient complexity for NBO-GD—improving over AmIGO and AID-BiO by a factor of κ log κ—and O(κ⁹ε⁻²) sample complexity for the stochastic variant NSBO-SGD.

## Results

In the deterministic setting, NBO-GD achieves O(κ³ε⁻¹) gradient computations and O(κ⁴ε⁻¹) Hessian-vector products, improving the gradient complexity over AmIGO-GD and AID-BiO (both O(κ⁴log κ ε⁻¹)) by a factor of κ log κ. NBO-CG further reduces Hessian-vector products to O(κ³·⁵log κ ε⁻¹). In the stochastic setting, NSBO-SGD achieves O(κ⁹ε⁻²) sample complexity, improving over AmIGO's O(κ⁹log κ ε⁻²). Empirically, NSBO-SGD (with T=1) converges fastest on IJCNN1 and Covtype for hyperparameter optimization, and reaches the lowest test error fastest on MNIST and FashionMNIST for data hyper-cleaning, outperforming AmIGO (Q=10), SOBA, SABA, StoBiO, SHINE, F²SA, and MA-SABA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NBO-GD improves the gradient complexity over AmIGO-GD and AID-BiO by a factor of κ log κ (from O(κ⁴log κ ε⁻¹) to O(κ³ε⁻¹)), and over No-loop AID by κ³ (from O(κ⁶ε⁻¹) to O(κ³ε⁻¹)). NBO-CG matches AmIGO-CG's Hessian-vector product complexity of O(κ³·⁵log κ ε⁻¹) while reducing gradient computations from O(κ⁴log κ ε⁻¹) to O(κ³ε⁻¹). In the stochastic setting, NSBO-SGD improves over AmIGO by a factor of log κ (O(κ⁹ε⁻²) vs. O(κ⁹log κ ε⁻²)) and over ALSET by removing the log(κ/ε) factor. Empirically, NSBO-SGD with T=1 outperforms AmIGO with Q=10 on both IJCNN1 and Covtype, and is the fastest method to reach the lowest test error on MNIST and FashionMNIST, beating SOBA, SABA, StoBiO, SHINE, F²SA, and MA-SABA. The variance-reduced variant NSBO-SAGA outperforms SABA, and MA-NSBO-SAGA outperforms MA-SABA on both hyperparameter optimization and data hyper-cleaning. On the non-strongly convex toy example, BAMM+NBO significantly outperforms standalone BAMM.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the AID-based hypergradient estimation framework (Ghadimi & Wang 2018; Ji et al. 2021; Arbel & Mairal 2022a; Dagréou et al. 2022) and the structural insight from SHINE (Ramzi et al. 2022) that solving the lower-level problem and computing Hessian inverse-vector products share the same Hessian. The key departure is that SHINE lacked convergence rate and complexity guarantees due to its reliance on quasi-Newton recursion and complex refinement strategies, whereas this paper replaces that machinery with a clean inexact Newton formulation (NBO) that admits a full Lyapunov-based analysis. When T=0, NBO-GD reduces exactly to the single-loop SOBA framework of Dagréou et al. (2022), positioning NBO as a principled generalization that adds curvature exploitation on top of the existing gradient-based baseline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first convergence rate and computational complexity guarantees for exploiting the shared-Hessian structure in hypergradient computation, yielding an O(κ³/ε) gradient complexity that improves upon AID-BiO and AmIGO by a factor of κ log κ in the deterministic setting. Practically, the finding that a single inexact Newton step (T=1) matches or exceeds 10 gradient descent steps in AmIGO suggests curvature information is a more efficient use of inner-loop budget than additional gradient evaluations. Open questions include extending the framework to parallel/distributed settings, incorporating variance-reduction and momentum more deeply into the Newton subproblem, and handling non-strongly convex lower-level objectives without reformulation.
