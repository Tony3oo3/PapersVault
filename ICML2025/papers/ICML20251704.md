## Problem

Multi-modal long context learning (MLoC) significantly boosts MLLM performance by embedding many demonstration examples into the input, but the resulting extremely long contexts incur prohibitive computational and memory costs. Existing KV-cache compression methods (SnapKV, PyramidKV, H2O) rely on fixed per-layer retention ratios designed for text-only or single-image settings, and fail to preserve performance when applied to multi-modal long contexts at high compression ratios.

## Input / Output

The method consumes N multi-modal demonstration examples (each an image-question-answer triple; images at 224×224 for ImageNet100 or 448×448 for other benchmarks, videos as 8-frame sequences) concatenated into a long context, plus a test query. It produces a compact task-specific KV memory (a pruned subset of the full KV cache) that replaces the original context during inference, reducing average context length by ~77% (e.g., 11338→2600 tokens with 20 examples) while the MLLM then generates a standard text answer conditioned on this compressed memory.

## Method

EMLoC operates in two stages without modifying any model weights. First, chunk-wise compression partitions the long context into K chunks (~1.6k tokens each) processed sequentially, where each chunk's KV cache is extracted conditioned on the previously compressed memory, enabling divide-and-conquer on resource-constrained GPUs. Second, within each chunk, layer-wise adaptive pruning proceeds top-down: at each layer, a greedy search over an ascending retention ratio set selects the minimum number of tokens to retain such that the Jensen-Shannon divergence between the original and pruned output probability distributions (measured over answer tokens as the observation window) stays below a local threshold δ. Importance scores are computed by accumulating attention weights from answer tokens onto each context token per layer. The key insight is that layer importance is task-specific and dynamic—certain early layers (e.g., 4, 8, 14 in Qwen2-VL) are critical regardless of position—contradicting the fixed pyramid heuristic. A theoretical analysis establishes that the global JS divergence is bounded linearly by (K−1)√δ + ε, and empirically the bound is much tighter in practice, allowing users to increase K freely to reduce memory without degrading quality.

## Results

On ImageNet100 with 200 examples, EMLoC achieves 63.7% top-1 accuracy while compressing the context to 22.4% of the original length (3,643 tokens vs. 16,264 for vanilla MLoC), surpassing MLoC's 62.6% with a 77% context reduction. Across six benchmarks with 20 examples, EMLoC (Qwen2-VL) reaches 63.6 on ImageNet100, 42.2 on MME-RW, and 58.7 on OK-VQA, matching or exceeding MLoC with full memory while using only ~25% of the context length. In the fine-tuning comparison (Table 4), EMLoC achieves a 54.9% average across ImageNet100, MME-RW, and OK-VQA, edging out LoRA (54.7%) and full fine-tuning (52.4%) without any parameter updates.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the direct MLoC baseline (same Qwen2-VL model, 20 examples), EMLoC wins on ImageNet100 (63.6 vs. 62.6), MME-RW (42.2 vs. 41.1), ScreenSpot (18.3 vs. 18.2), and OK-VQA (58.7 vs. 58.6), ties on IllusionVQA (40.9), and trails slightly on YouCook2 (102.0 vs. 108.8), all while using only 2,600 average context tokens versus 11,338. Compared to other ICL methods, EMLoC outperforms RICES (Flamingo) on MME-RW (42.2 vs. 40.5) and OK-VQA (58.7 vs. 58.5) but falls 0.8 points behind on ImageNet100 (63.7 vs. 64.5); it decisively beats MTV (32.7 on ImageNet100). Against fine-tuning methods, EMLoC's 54.9% three-benchmark average surpasses LoRA (54.7%), full fine-tuning (52.4%), E2PT (47.8%), VPT (45.6%), and M2PT (37.5%) without any training. In the pruning-strategy ablation (Table 5), EMLoC at 22.4% retention achieves 63.7 on ImageNet100, far exceeding SnapKV/H2O (47.6), PyramidKV (49.3), FastGen (49.3 at 36% retention), and PyramidInfer (55.6 at 24.6% retention), confirming that adaptive JS-divergence-constrained pruning preserves task-critical information that static heuristics discard.

## Relation to Prior Work

EMLoC sits at the intersection of multi-modal in-context learning (pioneered by Flamingo and extended by RICES/MTV) and training-free KV-cache compression (SnapKV, PyramidKV, H2O, StreamingLLM). It departs from prior compression methods by replacing their fixed or heuristically-determined per-layer retention ratios with a dynamic, JS-divergence-constrained adaptive pruning that determines token budgets per layer based on task-specific demonstrations. The chunk-wise divide-and-conquer strategy is novel in the multi-modal setting, enabling processing on resource-constrained hardware, and the paper is the first to jointly integrate compression and pruning for multi-modal long-context learning rather than treating them as separate concerns.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 77% context reduction without performance loss (and in some cases with slight gains) makes many-shot multi-modal ICL practically deployable on consumer hardware, which is significant given that prior work showed open-source MLLMs struggle with long contexts. The finding that compression can *improve* accuracy by removing irrelevant visual noise challenges the assumption that more context is always better. Key limitations include evaluation on a single base model (Qwen2-VL), failure on high-disparity tasks (MedXpertQA), and the 144s adaptation cost that, while small, is non-trivial for rapidly changing tasks. Open questions include whether the JS-divergence constraint generalizes to other MLLM architectures and whether the non-pyramidal layer importance pattern holds across model families.
