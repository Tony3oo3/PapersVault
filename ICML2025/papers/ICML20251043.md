## Problem

In information-theoretic multi-objective Bayesian optimization, the predictive distribution conditioned on the true Pareto-frontier p(f(x)|F*) is a truncated normal, but F* is unknown in continuous domains and only a finite subset F*_S is available. All existing methods approximate this using over-truncation based on F*_S, which systematically overestimates the truncation effect and has no principled way to correct the bias. The paper asks whether a variational lower bound framework can optimally balance over- and under-truncation to minimize the mutual information approximation error.

## Input / Output

The method consumes GP posterior means and variances for L independent objective functions at n observed points, along with the input domain X ⊆ R^d. It produces the next evaluation point x* and an optimized mixture weight λ ∈ (0,1] via a joint (d+1)-dimensional maximization of the lower bound. The Pareto-frontier subset F*_S is obtained by running NSGA-II on K=10 sampled function paths generated via random feature maps, and the cell decomposition for computing normalization constants Z_O and Z_U is performed once per BO iteration using the QHV algorithm.

## Method

PFEV constructs a variational distribution q_λ as a mixture of two truncated normals: PFTN-U (under-truncation, removing only the region that dominates F*_S) and PFTN-O (over-truncation, keeping only the region dominated by F*_S). The mixture weight λ is optimized by maximizing the resulting lower bound on MI(f_x; F*), which Remark 3.1 shows is equivalent to minimizing the expected KL divergence between the true conditional p(f_x|F*) and q_λ. A MAP-based Monte Carlo estimator (Eq. 8) incorporates the prior knowledge that the probability of falling in the over-truncated region is approximately Z_O/Z_U, reducing estimation variance compared to the naive indicator-based MC (Eq. 6). The key empirical finding is that the gap between over- and under-truncation becomes substantial in output dimensions L ≥ 3, where the two truncated regions differ significantly in volume, making the variational balance particularly impactful.

## Results

The paper evaluates PFEV using Relative Hyper-Volume (RHV) over 100 BO iterations (averaged over 10 runs) on GP-derived synthetic functions (d∈{2,3}, L∈{2,3,4,5,6}), six benchmark problems, and four LightGBM hyper-parameter optimization tasks. PFEV achieves superior or comparable RHV to all baselines across all settings, with the performance gap over over-truncation-based methods (PFES, {PF}²ES, JES) becoming clearly visible for output dimension L≥3. In the hyper-parameter optimization experiments (Abalone, Waveform, Pageblocks, Gesturephase), PFEV attains the highest or near-highest hypervolume of per-class test accuracies among all compared methods. The MAP-based MC estimator (Eq. 8) consistently outperforms the naive MC estimator (Eq. 6) in both GP-derived and benchmark settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PFEV outperforms or matches all eight baselines on RHV across the GP-derived, benchmark, and hyper-parameter optimization experiments. Against the closest information-theoretic competitors—PFES (Suzuki et al., 2020), {PF}²ES (Qing et al., 2022), and JES (Tu et al., 2022)—PFEV shows comparable performance at L=2 but a clear and consistent advantage for L≥3, which the authors attribute to the growing discrepancy between over- and under-truncation in higher-dimensional output spaces. Against the hypervolume-based EHVI (Emmerich, 2005), PFEV generally achieves higher RHV, particularly in higher-dimensional output settings where EHVI was limited to L≤4 due to computational cost. In the boxplot comparison (Fig. 4) that additionally includes ParEGO (Knowles, 2006), MOBO-RS (Paria et al., 2020), and MESMO (Belakaria et al., 2019), PFEV occupies the top of the distribution at the 100th iteration for most (d,L) configurations. In the decoupled-setting experiment, PFEV's extension outperforms the hypervolume knowledge gradient baseline (HVKG, Daulton et al., 2023) on (d,L)=(3,3) GP-derived functions across three length scales. In the parallel-querying experiment (Q=2,3), PFEV again shows sufficiently high performance relative to ParEGO, EHVI, MOBO-RS, and JES.

## Relation to Prior Work

PFEV extends the Pareto-frontier entropy search (PFES) line of work, which approximates the conditional distribution p(f_x | F*) using a truncated normal (PFTN) based on over-truncation by the observed Pareto subset. The key departure is the introduction of a complementary under-truncation approximation and their combination into a mixture variational distribution, whose weight is optimized via the variational lower bound maximization framework originally developed for constrained BO (Takeno et al., 2022). This makes PFEV the first method to provide a general MI lower bound for continuous-space MOBO, whereas prior information-theoretic MOBO methods (PFES, JES, {PF}²ES) either use entropy-difference decompositions without guaranteed positivity or offer lower bounds only under restrictive conditions (e.g., L=2 with |F*_S|→∞).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The over-truncation bias in existing information-theoretic MOBO methods becomes increasingly problematic as the number of objectives grows (L≥3), where the geometric gap between over- and under-truncated regions widens substantially. PFEV's variational mixture provides a principled, non-heuristic alternative to ad hoc corrections like the shifting parameter in {PF}²ES, and the MAP-based MC estimator offers a practical variance reduction for the small sample sizes (K=10) typical in information-theoretic BO. Open questions include theoretical guarantees on MI approximation quality and convergence, whether more flexible variational families could tighten the bound, and the development of gradient-based acquisition maximization to replace the derivative-free DIRECT optimizer.
