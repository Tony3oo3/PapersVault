## Problem

Existing image restoration bridge models (e.g., IR-SDE, GOUB) must be trained from scratch for each specific degradation type, incurring high computational cost and limiting generalization. While pretrained generative diffusion models share the same target distribution (high-quality images) as restoration bridges, their intermediate state distributions differ fundamentally—generative models start from pure noise while restoration bridges start from degraded images—preventing direct reuse of the pretrained denoising network within a restoration bridge.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single degraded RGB image (rain, haze, snow, raindrops, low-light, or masked inpainting) at 512×512 resolution as input. The degraded image serves a dual role: it initializes the restoration bridge trajectory and is fed as a spatial conditioning signal to ControlNet, while an empty text prompt is used for the text encoder. The output is a restored high-quality RGB image at the same resolution, produced by iteratively applying forward and reverse transitions between the bridge and the pretrained Stable Diffusion v1-5 latent space over a configurable number of inference steps (25–100).

## Method

The core contribution is a transition equation (Proposition 3.1) that expresses the state of one diffusion process as a linear combination of the state of another process sharing the same endpoint distribution, derived by exploiting the conditional independence of the two processes given a common sample x₀. Both generative diffusion (DDPM) and restoration bridges (IR-SDE, GOUB) are unified under the reparameterized form x_t = f_t·x₀ + b_t + σ_t·ε, enabling the transition to be computed in closed form with coefficients α, β, γ, and a controllable noise term σ. The IRBridge framework alternates a forward transition (converting the bridge state to a DDPM trajectory state without requiring an x₀ estimate, by choosing σ at its minimum valid value so β=0) and a reverse transition (converting back using the estimated x₀ with σ=0 for a deterministic mapping), with the pretrained Stable Diffusion denoiser estimating x₀ at each step. A critical timestep analysis determines valid parameter ranges and guides the selection of the bridge hyperparameter λ, while task-specific timestep scheduling strategies (e.g., higher j for severe degradations like low-light) are empirically tuned to balance detail preservation against degradation correction.

## Results

On six image restoration tasks at 512×512 resolution, IRBridge-GOUB achieves the best FID across all tasks, with particularly large gains over bridge models trained from scratch (e.g., FID 8.64 vs. 18.14 for GOUB on deraining; FID 10.38 vs. 22.89 for DiffUIR on desnowing; FID 3.37 vs. 4.30 for GOUB on inpainting). IRBridge also leads on SSIM and LPIPS in most settings (e.g., SSIM 0.9639 and LPIPS 0.027 on deraining), though it occasionally trails on PSNR (e.g., 30.39 vs. 31.96 for GOUB on deraining). On real-world datasets (RealRain-1K, RTTS, RealSnow), IRBridge achieves the best no-reference IQA scores (MUSIQ 52.08, BRISQUE 41.32, NIQE 5.00 on RTTS), outperforming the recent DCPT method.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against bridge models trained from scratch (GOUB, IR-SDE, RDDM, DiffUIR, ResShift), IRBridge-GOUB consistently achieves the best FID across all six tasks (e.g., 8.64 vs. 18.14 for GOUB on deraining; 10.38 vs. 22.89 for DiffUIR on desnowing), while also leading on SSIM and LPIPS in most cases; however, it trails GOUB on PSNR for deraining (30.39 vs. 31.96) and inpainting (30.42 vs. 28.98 is a win, but LPIPS 0.067 vs. 0.037 is a loss). Against regression-based methods (WGWS-Net, PromptIR), IRBridge is competitive on PSNR/SSIM and clearly wins on FID (e.g., desnowing FID 10.38 vs. 35.87 for WGWS-Net), and even surpasses WGWS-Net on PSNR for desnowing (25.87 vs. 20.34). Against methods that also leverage pretrained generative priors but follow the standard noise-to-image diffusion paradigm (GDP, TAO, DDRM), IRBridge wins on every reported metric, with the largest gaps on FID (e.g., low-light FID 36.11 vs. 48.67 for GDP; inpainting FID 3.37 vs. 37.02 for DDRM). On real-world no-reference evaluation (Table 4), IRBridge outperforms all from-scratch bridge baselines and the recent DCPT method on MUSIQ, BRISQUE, and NIQE across RealRain-1K, RealSnow, and RTTS, confirming superior generalization to unseen degradation distributions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the image restoration bridge paradigm (IR-SDE, GOUB) and pretrained generative diffusion models (Stable Diffusion), but departs from both lines of work in a key way: prior bridge models require training a dedicated score network from scratch for each degradation type, while prior generative-prior methods (GDP, TAO, DDRM) follow the standard noise-to-data diffusion path and require explicit degradation modeling. The central contribution is a transition equation (Proposition 3.1) that algebraically bridges two independent diffusion processes sharing the same endpoint distribution, enabling a pretrained denoising network to operate directly on restoration-bridge states. ControlNet is used as a lightweight conditional interface, but the core novelty is the theoretical construction of the forward/reverse transition and the critical-timestep analysis that determines valid operating regimes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

IRBridge eliminates the per-degradation training bottleneck of bridge models by reusing large-scale pretrained generative priors, yielding substantial FID improvements (e.g., 8.64 vs. 18.14 on deraining) and better generalization to unseen scenes and real-world degradations. The transition equation is general—applicable to any pair of diffusion processes with matching endpoint distributions—opening the door to image editing, translation, and other tasks. Key open questions include the empirical nature of timestep scheduling (no principled optimization yet), the coarse ControlNet conditioning, and VAE-induced high-frequency loss; the authors note that task-specific guidance modules and Schrödinger-bridge coefficient optimization are promising but unexplored directions.
