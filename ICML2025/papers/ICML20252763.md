## Problem

Blind speech separation from microphone arrays is a blind inverse problem where the array geometry, room impulse responses, and speech sources are all unknown. Existing unsupervised methods (IVA, spatial clustering, UNSSOR) do not fully exploit speech source priors and suffer from frequency permutation and spatial aliasing, while supervised methods are constrained to fixed array geometries and produce deterministic, potentially blurred outputs. The core technical obstacle is that in the multi-channel array setting, the likelihood required by diffusion posterior sampling (DPS) is intractable because the mixing operator depends on unknown relative RIRs.

## Input / Output

ArrayDPS takes C-channel audio mixtures and the number of sources K as input, requiring no knowledge of microphone positions, array geometry, or source locations. It is generative: it samples from the posterior p(s|x), producing multiple plausible separation results per mixture. The primary output is separated source images at a designated reference channel (channel 1), obtained by convolving the separated virtual sources with FCP-estimated relative RIRs. The virtual sources themselves are an intermediate representation that the diffusion process directly samples, and the estimated relative RIRs are also returned as a byproduct.

## Method

ArrayDPS extends DPS to the multi-channel array setting by making the intractable likelihood tractable at each diffusion step. Given the current noisy source estimate, a pre-trained single-speaker diffusion denoiser produces a clean estimate, which is then used with Forward Convolutive Prediction (FCP) to solve a weighted least-squares problem that yields maximum-likelihood relative RIR estimates (proven equivalent to ML estimation in Theorem B.1). These estimated RIRs transform virtual sources to all microphone channels, making the Gaussian-noise log-likelihood computable as a reconstruction error. Because the entire pipeline (denoiser → STFT → FCP → ISTFT) is differentiable, the likelihood score is obtained by back-propagating the reconstruction loss to the noisy source, and the posterior score is the sum of the prior score (via Tweedie's formula) and this likelihood score. A second-order Heun stochastic sampler (from EDM) integrates the posterior ODE, with IVA used to initialize both the starting diffusion noise and the early-stage relative RIRs for stability, and an empirical reference-channel guidance term added in early steps to improve convergence.

## Results

On the 3-channel SMS-WSJ fixed-array benchmark, ArrayDPS-A (default) achieves 15.8 dB SDR and 15.0 dB SI-SDR, outperforming the best unsupervised baseline UNSSOR (15.4 dB SDR, 14.4 dB SI-SDR) by 0.4 dB SDR. With maximum-likelihood sample selection (ArrayDPS-A-ML), the method reaches 16.9 dB SDR and 16.2 dB SI-SDR, slightly exceeding the supervised TF-GridNet-SMS (16.8 dB SDR, 16.3 dB SI-SDR) on SDR. On the ad-hoc 4-channel Spatialized WSJ0-2Mix dataset, ArrayDPS-A-ML achieves 16.6 dB SDR and 15.8 dB SI-SDR, surpassing the supervised TF-GridNet-Spatial (15.8 dB SDR, 15.1 dB SI-SDR) by 0.8 dB SDR, demonstrating strong generalization to unseen array geometries.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against unsupervised baselines, ArrayDPS-A consistently outperforms Spatial Clustering (9.5 dB SDR) and IVA (13.4 dB SDR) by 2.4–6.3 dB SDR, and beats the state-of-the-art unsupervised method UNSSOR (15.4 dB SDR) by 0.4 dB SDR and 0.6 dB SI-SDR. With ML sample selection, ArrayDPS-A-ML (16.9 dB SDR) surpasses UNSSOR by 1.5 dB SDR and 0.29 PESQ. Against the supervised TF-GridNet-SMS baseline (16.8 dB SDR), ArrayDPS-A-ML matches or slightly exceeds it on SDR (16.9 vs. 16.8) but trails on PESQ (3.49 vs. 3.91) and eSTOI (0.884 vs. 0.924). Critically, on the ad-hoc array setting (Spatialized WSJ0-2Mix), ArrayDPS-A-ML (16.6 dB SDR) outperforms TF-GridNet-Spatial (15.8 dB SDR) by 0.8 dB SDR, while the supervised method suffers a 2.1 dB SDR drop from its fixed-array performance (16.8→14.7), highlighting ArrayDPS's array-agnostic advantage.

## Relation to Prior Work

ArrayDPS extends Diffusion Posterior Sampling (DPS) from the single-channel setting—where the likelihood p(x|s) is tractable—to the multi-channel array setting where the likelihood is intractable due to unknown array geometry and room impulse responses. The key technical bridge is Forward Convolutive Prediction (FCP), borrowed from reverberant speech separation literature, which provides a maximum-likelihood estimator for the relative RIRs at each diffusion step, making the intractable likelihood tractable. IVA is repurposed not as a separation method but as an initialization mechanism to stabilize early diffusion sampling. The paper is explicitly positioned as the first application of DPS to multi-channel array inverse problems, departing from prior DPS-for-separation work (Yu et al. 2023; Mariani et al. 2024) that only handled single-channel cases with known mixing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By closing the gap between unsupervised and supervised multi-channel speech separation (matching TF-GridNet on SDR/SI-SDR without any paired training data or array geometry knowledge), ArrayDPS offers a practical path to deployment on arbitrary microphone configurations where supervised models fail to generalize. The generative formulation naturally handles the multi-modal posterior, avoiding the blurring that plagues deterministic supervised networks. Open questions include computational cost (iterative diffusion sampling with FCP at each step), scalability beyond 2–3 speakers in real-time settings, and whether the approach extends to other multi-channel inverse problems (e.g., music, environmental sound). The method's reliance on a strong single-speaker diffusion prior also raises questions about performance when the prior's domain coverage is limited.
