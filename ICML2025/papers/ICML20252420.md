## Problem

While Armijo line-search (GD-LS) is the default step-size strategy in practice and empirically converges faster than fixed 1/L-step gradient descent, existing theory only guarantees constant-factor improvements over GD(1/L). This paper asks whether GD-LS can provably achieve a strictly faster *rate* of convergence, and shows it can for objectives whose local smoothness shrinks as the function value decreases.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252420/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a twice-differentiable objective satisfying an (L0, L1) non-uniform smoothness condition (where local smoothness is L0 + L1·f(θ)), an initial point θ0, and line-search hyperparameters (η_max, c, β). It outputs iterates θ_T with a sub-optimality guarantee f(θ_T) − f* ≤ ε, where the iteration count depends on f* (the minimum function value) rather than solely on the global smoothness constant L. The key unusual aspect is that the step-size is adaptively chosen per-iteration via backtracking, and the convergence rate improves as f* → 0, a regime where GD(1/L) remains sublinear.

## Method

The paper introduces an (L0, L1) non-uniform smoothness condition where the Hessian norm is bounded by L0 + L1·f(θ), combined with a gradient-norm bound ‖∇f(θ)‖ ≤ νf(θ) + ω. The key technical lemma shows that the Armijo step-size at iteration t is lower-bounded by 1/(λ0 + λ1·f(θ_t)), meaning GD-LS automatically uses larger steps as the loss decreases. A meta-theorem then converts this into a convergence rate of O((f*/ε)·ln(1/ε)) for convex losses with L0 = 0 (logistic regression, cross-entropy), improving over the O(1/ε) rate of GD(1/L), and achieving linear convergence on separable data. For non-convex objectives satisfying gradient domination (softmax policy gradient in RL, GLMs with logistic link), GD-LS matches the linear rates of specialized algorithms like natural policy gradient without requiring problem-dependent constants. The analysis extends to stochastic gradient descent with a stochastic line-search under the interpolation assumption.

## Results

The paper's main quantitative results are convergence rate guarantees. For convex losses with L₀=0 and ω=0 (logistic regression, multi-class cross-entropy), GD-LS achieves O((f*/ε)·ln(1/ε)) iterations, which reduces to O(ln(1/ε)) when f*=Θ(ε), compared to the O(1/ε) sublinear rate of GD(1/L). For logistic regression on linearly separable data with margin γ, GD-LS achieves a linear rate of O((ln(1/ε))²/γ²) iterations, an exponential improvement over the Ω(1/ε) lower bound for any constant-step-size method. For the softmax policy gradient on K-armed bandits, GD-LS achieves O(K²·ln(1/ε)) linear convergence, matching natural policy gradient and outperforming the Ω(1/ε) rate of GD(1/L).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GD-LS provably outperforms GD(1/L) in every setting studied: it achieves linear (logarithmic) convergence where GD(1/L) is stuck at sublinear O(1/ε) for separable logistic regression, MAB policy gradient, and GLM with logistic link. Compared to normalized GD (NGD) of Axiotis & Sviridenko (2023), GD-LS matches the same O((f*/ε)·ln(1/ε)) rate for logistic regression but does not require knowledge of the problem-dependent constant L₁, making it directly implementable. For the MAB setting, GD-LS matches the linear rate of natural policy gradient (Kakade & Langford, 2002) and specialized line-search methods (Lu et al., 2024) without requiring f* or problem-specific step-size schedules. Against Polyak step-size methods (Vankov et al., 2024; Gorbunov et al., 2024), GD-LS achieves comparable rates for the last-iterate without needing f*, though the Polyak bound is slightly tighter in ε-dependence for general (Lc,Lg)-smooth functions. In the stochastic setting, SGD-SLS achieves the same per-iteration cost as standard SGD (O(1) vs O(n) for GD-LS), and its gradient complexity O(n·[ln(n/ε)]²) is smaller than SARAH's O((n+1/ε)·ln(1/ε)) for general smooth convex losses.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the convergence analysis of GD with Armijo line-search beyond the standard uniform smoothness setting. Prior work (Scheinberg et al. 2014, Lu & Mei 2023, Fox & Schmidt 2024) showed GD-LS achieves constant-factor improvements over GD(1/L) under local smoothness, while Zhang et al. (2019) introduced non-uniform smoothness tied to gradient norm to explain gradient clipping. Here, the authors propose an alternative non-uniform smoothness condition tied to the function value (L₀ + L₁·f(θ)), show it is implied by Zhang et al.'s condition, and use it to prove that GD-LS achieves a strictly faster *rate* (not just constants) for convex and non-convex objectives. The stochastic analysis builds on Vaswani et al. (2019b)'s stochastic line-search under the interpolation assumption.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical implication is that GD-LS — already the default step-size strategy in most ML frameworks — provably outperforms constant step-size GD in convergence rate for a broad class of problems (logistic regression, multi-class classification, softmax policy gradient, GLMs) without requiring any problem-dependent constants, matching rates of specialized algorithms like natural policy gradient or normalized GD. Open questions include whether the advantage extends to broader non-convex landscapes, whether other adaptive schemes (AdaGrad, Adam) enjoy similar provable benefits, and how to efficiently implement the sampling strategy in Theorem 6 to remove the n-dependence in the stochastic setting. The empirical validation is limited to small-scale experiments, so the practical magnitude of the speedup on large-scale deep learning remains to be established.
