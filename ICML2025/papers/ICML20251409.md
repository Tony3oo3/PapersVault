## Problem

Existing robotic manipulation pre-training paradigms rely on text instructions to specify task targets, but the paper reveals that current robotic data lacks the diversity needed to train policies to effectively align text and vision (which would require millions of image-text pairs). As a result, text-instructed policies fail to focus on the correct target object. The paper asks whether vision-based instructions can serve as a more comprehensible alternative at the current data scale.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251409/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

During pre-training, the policy receives the current observation frame I₁, a future observation frame I_T, and sparse point flows (tens of tracked points describing intermediate motion) between them; each frame may contain multiple camera views at the same timestamp. At inference, the future frame is replaced by a cropped image of the target object (obtained via YOLOv10-small), and point flows are absent. The policy outputs a sequence of T=20 actions along with per-step Laplacian uncertainty values σ_t that are learned unsupervised and modulate the regression loss.

## Method

VIP pre-trains a policy to predict actions from purely visual information: a current image, a future image, and sparse point flows generated by CoTracker via a two-stage sampling strategy (uniform sampling → tracking → removal of static flows → resampling around moving flows). Point flows are progressively masked with increasing probability α_n = min(1.25·n/N, 1) over N pre-training iterations, so the policy gradually learns to rely on visual observations alone. The VIRT architecture uses 12 Transformer encoders initialized from DINOv2 (with random pixel masking at ratio τ=0.5) and 3 Transformer decoders with T learnable queries to produce actions and uncertainties. The training loss combines a Laplacian uncertainty-weighted regression term that down-weights ambiguous action segments. Pre-training runs for 120K iterations on 1.7B DROID data, followed by 8K iterations of fine-tuning where the future image is swapped for a cropped target-object region.

## Results

On six real-robot and simulated manipulation tasks, VIRT with VIP pre-training achieves the highest success rate across all tasks: 0.42 (Pour Blueberries), 0.71 (Open Lid), 0.37 (Clean Table), 0.92 (Move Box), 0.74 (Transport Box), and 0.68 (Stack Boxes). The pre-training paradigm consistently boosts every policy architecture by 0.05–0.12 in success rate. VIRT runs at 39.22 Hz inference speed on a single RTX 4090, slightly slower than ACT (43.48 Hz) but substantially faster than Diffusion Policy (27.32 Hz).

## Baselines & Comparisons

VIRT with VIP pre-training outperforms all three baselines on every task. Against ACT (the closest competitor), VIRT gains +0.08 on Pour Blueberries (0.42 vs 0.34), +0.12 on Open Lid (0.71 vs 0.59), +0.07 on Clean Table (0.37 vs 0.30), +0.02 on Move Box (0.92 vs 0.90), +0.09 on Transport Box (0.74 vs 0.65), and +0.10 on Stack Boxes (0.68 vs 0.58). Against Diffusion Policy, the margins are larger, e.g., +0.14 on Pour Blueberries and +0.12 on Open Lid. ConvMLP fails entirely on the three real-robot tasks (0.00 success rate) regardless of pre-training, confirming that a simple MLP head cannot handle multi-step manipulation. The only metric where VIRT trails is inference speed: ACT runs at 43.48 Hz versus VIRT's 39.22 Hz, a ~10% difference attributed to VIRT's heavier Transformer encoder stack initialized from DINOv2.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant text-instruction paradigm in robotic pre-training (RT-1, OpenVLA, Open X-Embodiment) by proposing vision-instructed pre-training, where future images and sparse point flows replace natural language as the task specification. The sparse point flow component is built directly on CoTracker (Karaev et al., 2024) for point tracking, while the encoder initialization draws on DINOv2's self-supervised visual representations. The progressive masking of point flows during training echoes the masked-image-modeling philosophy, and the Laplacian uncertainty loss follows Kendall & Gal's heteroscedastic regression framework. Conceptually, the paper positions itself at the intersection of visual instruction tuning (Liu et al., 2024) and robotic manipulation, arguing that the cross-domain alignment cost of text-vision-action is prohibitive at current robotic data scales.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical insight that vision instructions outperform text instructions at current robotic data diversity scales challenges the field's heavy investment in language-conditioned policies and offers a simpler, more effective alternative for near-term deployment. The use of cropped object images as inference-time instructions enables dynamic task re-specification (e.g., changing manipulation order) without retraining, which is valuable for multi-object scenarios. However, the approach still depends on an external detection/segmentation model (YOLOv10) at inference, and the world-model-based future image prediction failed in real scenarios, leaving the question of how to generate richer vision instructions without ground-truth future frames open. Whether the advantage of vision over text instructions persists as robotic data diversity grows to the millions-of-pairs scale remains an important open question.
