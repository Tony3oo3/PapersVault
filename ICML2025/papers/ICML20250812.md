## Problem

Existing methods that fine-tune quantized diffusion models with LoRA (e.g., QLoRA, QA-LoRA, IR-QLoRA) still produce FP16 merged weights at inference, necessitating an additional post-training quantization (PTQ) step that causes significant performance degradation at low bit-widths (e.g., 4-bit). The paper addresses this by making the low-rank adaptation parameters themselves integer-type, so that the merged downstream weights are naturally quantized without any extra PTQ.

## Input / Output

The method consumes pre-trained diffusion model weights already in integer format (INT4 or INT8, per-channel quantized) along with task-specific conditioning: text prompts for subject-driven generation, control signals (segmentation maps at 512×512, facial landmarks, or Canny edges) for controllable generation, or a single style image for style transfer. The output is a generated image plus merged weights that are directly in the target integer bit-width (INT4 or INT8), eliminating the separate PTQ step entirely. For the NLP validation, it takes Llama3-8B quantized weights and produces quantized merged weights evaluated on GSM8K math questions.

## Method

IntLoRA introduces three key mechanisms to enable integer-arithmetic LoRA. First, Adaptation-Quantization Separation (AQS) splits the adaptation into a zero-initialized trainable AB (preserving LoRA's gradient behavior) and a task-agnostic auxiliary matrix R (providing a nonzero, quantization-friendly distribution) via a stop-gradient formulation. Second, Multiplicative Low-rank Adaptation (MLA) algebraically rewrites the additive merge W + AB as a Hadamard product of a 'pre-training term' (already integer) and an 'adaptation term' (to be quantized independently), removing the need for a shared quantizer. Third, Variance Matching Control (VMC) scales R by a per-channel variance ratio r^α to balance quantization error against information retention in the pre-trained weights. Two deployment variants result: IntLoRA_MUL uses uniform affine quantization on the adaptation term and merges via integer multiplication, while IntLoRA_SHIFT uses log2-quantization and merges via hardware-friendly bit-shifting of the quantized pre-trained weights.

## Results

On subject-driven generation (Dreambooth), IntLoRA_MUL achieves 0.4498 DINO and 0.6882 CLIP-I at W8A8, outperforming QLoRA (0.4153 DINO) and IR-QLoRA (0.4070 DINO) while remaining close to the FP16 LoRA upper bound (0.4828 DINO). At the more challenging W4A8 setting, IntLoRA_MUL reaches 0.4242 DINO and 0.6913 CLIP-I, whereas QLoRA collapses to 0.2136 DINO. On controllable generation (4-bit S2I), IntLoRA_MUL achieves 30.87 FID versus 71.75 for QLoRA and 35.83 for IR-QLoRA. On the NLP task (GSM8K with Llama3-8B), IntLoRA_MUL attains 64.23% accuracy at W8A8, matching FP16 LoRA (64.24%) and exceeding QLoRA (64.06%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

IntLoRA_MUL consistently outperforms all quantized baselines across both 8-bit and 4-bit settings. At W8A8, it surpasses IR-QLoRA by 0.0428 DINO and QLoRA by 0.0345 DINO on subject-driven generation, while at W4A8 the gap widens dramatically: IntLoRA_MUL (0.4242 DINO) beats QLoRA (0.2136) by over 0.2 DINO, reflecting QLoRA's severe degradation under additional PTQ at low bit-width. On controllable generation at 4-bit, IntLoRA_MUL achieves 30.87 FID on S2I versus 71.75 (QLoRA), 31.51 (QA-LoRA), and 35.83 (IR-QLoRA), with similar margins on L2F (33.62 vs. 117.37/43.09/39.63) and C2I (16.32 vs. 62.49/16.73/18.30). Against the FP16 LoRA upper bound, IntLoRA_MUL retains ~93% of the DINO score at W8A8 and ~88% at W4A8 without requiring any post-training quantization step. On the NLP task, IntLoRA_MUL (64.23%) essentially matches FP16 LoRA (64.24%) and edges out QLoRA (64.06%) and QA-LoRA (63.53%). In the diffusion quantization comparison (Table 6), IntLoRA_MUL also outperforms EfficientDM on all four metrics (IS 199.20 vs. 178.20, FID 10.43 vs. 13.42, sFID 24.02 vs. 26.67, precision 0.79 vs. 0.70) while requiring only quantized weights during calibration rather than FP16 weights.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the QLoRA line of work (Dettmers et al., 2024; Xu et al., 2023b; Qin et al., 2024), which quantizes pretrained weights to reduce training memory but still requires post-training quantization (PTQ) on the merged FP16 weights at inference. IntLoRA departs from this by making the low-rank adaptation parameters themselves integer-type, so the merged weights are natively quantized without any additional PTQ step. The key technical departure from vanilla LoRA is the reformulation from additive (W + AB) to multiplicative (W ⊙ V) weight merging, which decouples the quantizers of pretrained and adaptation weights. The paper also positions itself against EfficientDM, which achieves inference-efficient quantized weights but at the cost of requiring FP16 weights during training, making it unsuitable for consumer-level GPUs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the PTQ step from the deployment pipeline, IntLoRA addresses a practical bottleneck in deploying fine-tuned diffusion models at low bit-widths, where PTQ causes significant and avoidable performance degradation. The method is both training- and inference-efficient, filling a gap that prior methods (QLoRA: training-efficient only; EfficientDM: inference-efficient only) leave open. Limitations include the continued reliance on STE for gradient propagation (quantized weights remain FP16 during tuning), the need to tune the smoothing factor α per task, and the focus on LoRA specifically rather than other PEFT methods. Open questions include whether integer-type gradient propagation could further reduce training costs, and whether the framework extends to other PEFT paradigms like adapters or prompt tuning.
