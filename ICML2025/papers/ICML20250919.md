## Problem

Sampling from discrete distributions known up to normalization (e.g., Boltzmann distributions on spin lattices) remains challenging: MCMC suffers from slow mixing in multimodal regimes, and while learned transport methods have advanced rapidly for continuous spaces, no comparable framework existed for discrete state spaces. Existing discrete approaches either rely on autoregressive models with O(d) sampling cost or lack the variance-reduction guarantees of annealed importance sampling and sequential Monte Carlo.

## Input / Output

The method takes an unnormalized potential U_1(x) defined on a discrete state space S = T^d (e.g., a 15×15 Ising lattice with d=225 binary spins) and a temperature schedule U_t(x) interpolating from uniform to the target. It produces samples from ρ_1 via Euler integration of a learned CTMC, along with self-normalized importance weights A_t that correct for residual training error and a one-dimensional free energy network F_t^φ. The rate matrix is restricted to nearest-neighbor jumps on the token hypercube, and the locally equivariant parameterization ensures the KFE residual is computable in a single network forward pass rather than |N(x)| evaluations.

## Method

LEAPS learns a time-dependent CTMC rate matrix Q_t^θ that transports a uniform initial distribution to the target Boltzmann distribution, trained via a physics-informed neural network (PINN) loss that minimizes the variance of Radon-Nikodym importance weights derived for reverse-time CTMCs. The key theoretical result (Theorem 5.2) shows that a 'proactive' importance sampling update—accumulating the KFE violation operator K_t ρ_t along trajectories—exactly reproduces the path-space RND, yielding valid unbiased estimators with zero variance if and only if the CTMC marginals match ρ_t. To make this scalable, the paper introduces locally equivariant networks (MLPs, attention, and convolutional layers) where the flux from state x to neighbor y is the negative of the flux from y to x, enabling the KFE residual to be computed in one forward pass. The PINN objective uses an auxiliary free energy network to bound the log-variance divergence, and the algorithm optionally incorporates MCMC mixing steps and SMC resampling when the effective sample size drops below a threshold.

## Results

On the 15×15 Ising model at critical temperature, LEAPS achieves an effective sample size (ESS) of ~68% in just 100 integration steps, whereas annealed importance sampling (AIS) requires approximately 10^6 steps to reach a comparable ESS. On the Potts model at critical temperature, LEAPS attains an ESS of ~20% in 100 steps, while AIS remains below this level even at 10^6 steps. On the DISCS benchmark, LEAPS outperforms standard MCMC samplers in ESS even after accounting for the number of function evaluations (NFEs), with the caveat that LEAPS function calls are neural network evaluations while MCMC calls are energy evaluations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LEAPS is compared against two families of baselines: (1) annealed importance sampling (AIS) and (2) MCMC samplers via the DISCS benchmark. Against AIS, LEAPS achieves a dramatic efficiency gain: on the Ising model at critical temperature, LEAPS reaches ~68% ESS in 100 steps while AIS needs roughly 10^6 steps for a similar ESS; on the Potts model, LEAPS achieves ~20% ESS in 100 steps while AIS remains below this even at 10^6 steps. Against MCMC samplers on the DISCS benchmark, LEAPS shows significantly higher ESS on both the Ising and Potts models, even after normalizing for the number of function evaluations. The paper notes a caveat in the MCMC comparison: LEAPS function calls are neural network forward passes while MCMC calls are energy evaluations, and ESS is measured differently for MCMC chains, so the comparison is not perfectly apples-to-apples. Overall, LEAPS wins decisively on both comparisons in terms of sample efficiency (ESS per step), with the AIS comparison being the most striking (4 orders of magnitude fewer steps needed).

## Relation to Prior Work

LEAPS extends the non-equilibrium transport sampling paradigm (NETS, Vargas et al., Albergo & Vanden-Eijnden) from continuous to discrete state spaces, where no prior transport-based sampler existed. It unifies annealed importance sampling and sequential Monte Carlo as special cases (Q_t^θ = 0) while adding a learned CTMC transport that offsets importance weight variance. The theoretical core—a Radon-Nikodym derivative for reverse-time CTMCs—generalizes the Jarzynski equality to discrete spaces, and the locally equivariant network architecture (MLPs, attention, convolutions) is a new inductive bias not present in prior discrete diffusion or PINN-based samplers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 4-order-of-magnitude efficiency gain over AIS (100 vs ~10^6 steps) and the principled variance-minimizing training objective make discrete sampling via learned transport practically viable for the first time. The locally equivariant network design is a reusable architectural contribution that could transfer to other discrete-state problems beyond statistical physics. However, experiments are confined to Ising and Potts models on 15×15 lattices, and the MCMC comparisons carry caveats about differing function-call semantics. Open questions include scalability to larger or non-lattice state spaces, integration with guidance/reward fine-tuning, and whether more expressive locally equivariant architectures close remaining gaps on harder distributions.
