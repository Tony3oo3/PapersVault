## Problem

Sparse autoencoders decompose LLM activations into millions of interpretable features, but manually interpreting each one is infeasible, and existing automated scoring methods (notably simulation scoring) are expensive, focus exclusively on activating examples while ignoring the vast majority of non-activating contexts, and fail to capture 'output features' whose behavior is better explained by their causal effect on model outputs than by input correlations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251933/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes SAE weights (16k, 131k, or 262k features trained on Gemma 2 9B or Llama 3.1 8B residual streams or MLP outputs), a 10M-token RedPajama-v2 corpus for activation collection, and a subject LLM for intervention experiments. For each feature it produces a short natural-language interpretation (generated by showing 40 stratified-sampled 32-token activating examples to Llama 3.1 70B Instruct) plus five quality scores computed over 100 activating and 100 non-activating contexts, with the intervention score additionally requiring paired clean and intervened generations from the subject model.

## Method

The paper introduces a three-stage automated pipeline: collecting SAE activations over a broad corpus, generating interpretations via an LLM explainer shown stratified-sampled activating examples, and scoring those interpretations with five new methods—detection, fuzzing, surprisal, embedding, and intervention scoring—that reframe interpretation quality as binary classification between activating and non-activating contexts rather than regression on activation magnitudes. Detection and fuzzing ask a scorer LLM to label whole sequences or individual tokens, surprisal measures the information value of an interpretation via cross-entropy reduction, and embedding uses a 400M-parameter encoder to compute cosine-similarity AUROC. Intervention scoring, the most novel contribution, measures how well an interpretation predicts the counterfactual effect of additively intervening on a feature by comparing the scorer's surprisal about the interpretation when conditioned on intervened versus clean generations, normalized to a fixed KL-divergence strength. These methods are 5–30× cheaper than simulation scoring, and the authors show that stratified sampling across activation quantiles (rather than top-only sampling) yields interpretations that generalize better across the full activation distribution.

## Results

The paper introduces five new scoring methods (fuzzing, detection, surprisal, embedding, intervention) that are substantially cheaper than the standard simulation scoring: fuzzing and detection cost ~$600–$680 per 100k features with Llama 70b, versus $3.6k for all-at-once simulation and $18.7k for token-by-token simulation (a 5×–30× reduction). Fuzzing achieves the highest Spearman correlation with simulation (0.73–0.75) and the best correlation with human judgments (0.69 vs. 0.60 for simulation). SAE features with 131k dimensions score 0.76 fuzzing / 0.74 detection on Gemma 2 9B, significantly outperforming top-32 neurons (0.62 / 0.59) and top-256 neurons (0.59 / 0.57). Intervention scoring reveals that features with low correlational scores can still be highly interpretable in terms of their causal effect on model output.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is simulation scoring from Bills et al. (2023), the standard method for evaluating neuron/feature interpretations. Fuzzing and detection are 5× cheaper than all-at-once simulation and 30× cheaper than token-by-token simulation while maintaining strong agreement: fuzzing correlates with simulation at 0.73–0.75 Spearman (the highest of all new methods), and achieves a 0.69 Spearman correlation with human raters versus 0.60 for simulation itself. Detection correlates lower with simulation (0.44–0.46) but captures context-level discrimination that simulation misses. The paper also shows that SAE features (131k, Gemma 2 9B) substantially outperform raw neurons on both fuzzing (0.76 vs. 0.62 for top-32) and detection (0.74 vs. 0.59), confirming that SAEs produce more interpretable units. Finally, intervention scoring identifies a class of 'output features' that have low fuzzing/simulation scores but high intervention scores, demonstrating that correlational baselines systematically miss causally meaningful features.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the automated interpretability pipeline introduced by Bills et al. (2023) for GPT-2 neurons to the much larger scale of SAE features (up to 262k per layer), following the SAE framework established by Bricken et al. (2023) and Templeton et al. (2024). The key departure is in evaluation: rather than relying solely on simulation scoring (the standard from Bills et al.), the authors introduce five cheaper scoring methods—fuzzing, detection, surprisal, embedding, and intervention scoring—shifting the evaluation paradigm from correlating predicted activation magnitudes to binary classification of activating vs. non-activating contexts. The intervention scoring method further departs from the purely correlational framing of prior work by evaluating causal effects of feature ablation on model output, drawing on ideas from Patchscopes and SelfIE.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: the open-source delphi pipeline and released interpretations for Gemma 2 9B and Llama 3.1 8B SAEs (via Neuronpedia) give the community a concrete, reproducible resource for feature-level interpretability at scale. The intervention scoring result—that features with low correlational scores can be well-explained by their downstream output effects—reveals a systematic blind spot in existing auto-interpretability methods and suggests that a meaningful fraction of SAE features are 'output features' that current pipelines misclassify as uninterpretable. Open questions include how to handle long-range features (beyond 32-token contexts), how to incorporate interpretation length into scoring, and how to use adversarial or similar-feature examples to test interpretation precision, particularly as SAEs scale and features become sparser.
