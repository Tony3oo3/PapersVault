## Problem

Large language models struggle with structurally novel tasks that require non-trivial reasoning, planning, or abstraction, even when given a small number of in-context examples. While in-context learning (ICL) works well for tasks within the pre-training distribution, it fails on out-of-distribution benchmarks like ARC and BIG-Bench Hard. This paper investigates whether temporarily updating model parameters at inference time (test-time training) can close this gap without requiring task-specific pre-training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250039/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For ARC, each task provides 2–7 demonstration pairs of 2D grids (up to 30×30, up to 10 colors) and 1–3 test inputs; the model must produce an exact-match output grid, scored with pass@2. For BIG-Bench Hard, each of 27 tasks provides 10 text input-output demonstration pairs and the model produces a text answer via greedy decoding. A key detail is that the demonstrations are not merely prepended to the prompt: they are decomposed into leave-one-out synthetic ICL tasks and, for ARC, further expanded via invertible geometric transformations (rotations, flips, transposes, translations, resolution changes) to form the TTT training set.

## Method

The TTT pipeline has three stages. First, a test-time training dataset is constructed by creating leave-one-out in-context tasks from the K demonstrations (each example is held out as a synthetic test while the rest serve as in-context prompts), with optional augmentation via invertible transformations for structured inputs like ARC grids. Second, task-specific LoRA adapters (rank 128 for ARC, rank 64 for BBH) are trained for a small number of gradient steps using a loss computed over both the synthetic test outputs and the in-context demonstration outputs, which the ablations show is critical. Third, an augmented inference strategy generates multiple prediction candidates by applying different geometric transformations to the input and permuting demonstration order, then aggregates them via a two-stage hierarchical voting scheme (intra-transformation top-3 selection followed by global top-2 selection). The key insight is that framing the few-shot examples as synthetic ICL tasks—rather than direct input-output pairs—yields substantially better TTT performance, and that the in-context formatting of the training data is what allows the pre-trained ICL capabilities to be leveraged during the gradient updates.

## Results

On the ARC validation set (pass@2), the TTT pipeline applied to the authors' fine-tuned 8B Llama 3 model improves accuracy from 18.3% to 47.1%, and when applied to BARC's neural model reaches 53.0%. Ensembling TTT with BARC's program-synthesis component yields 61.9%, matching average human performance (60.2%). On BIG-Bench Hard (10-shot), TTT achieves 57.8% accuracy, surpassing standard in-context learning (50.5%) by 7.3 percentage points and zero-shot (40.9%) by 16.9 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against BARC (Li et al., 2025), the paper's TTT pipeline substantially outperforms BARC's fully neural component: applying TTT to BARC's neural model raises accuracy from 39.3% to 53.0% (+13.7 pp), and the full TTT + BARC ensemble reaches 61.9% versus BARC's own 54.4% ensemble (+7.5 pp). Against frontier LLMs used as zero-shot or few-shot baselines, the TTT system dramatically outperforms GPT-4o (9.0%), Claude 3.5 Sonnet (21.0%), OpenAI o1-preview (21.0%), and DeepSeek R1 (20.5%) on ARC, though OpenAI o3-preview (82.8%) still exceeds the paper's best result. On BIG-Bench Hard, TTT (57.8%) beats standard 10-shot ICL (50.5%) by 7.3 pp and direct I/O fine-tuning (51.5%) by 6.3 pp, with the largest gains on structurally regular tasks like Dyck Languages and Ruin Names (20–50 pp improvements over ICL).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the test-time training (TTT) paradigm from Sun et al. (2020), which used unsupervised self-supervision on inputs alone, to the few-shot supervised setting by constructing synthetic in-context learning tasks via leave-one-out over demonstration pairs. It builds on the in-context learning framework of Brown et al. (2020) by augmenting it with explicit gradient-based LoRA updates rather than relying solely on implicit adaptation, and draws on Meta-ICL (Min et al., 2022a) for its shared-adapter variant. The key departure from prior TTT work is the use of supervised input-output signals from demonstrations to form the training objective, combined with an augmented inference strategy (geometric transformations + hierarchical voting) that has no direct analogue in the vision TTT literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a relatively simple test-time fine-tuning procedure (LoRA on leave-one-out ICL tasks) can close a large gap on tasks where LLMs are notoriously weak, reaching near-human performance on ARC when ensembled with program synthesis. This suggests that the bottleneck on novel reasoning tasks may be less about model capacity and more about the absence of a mechanism to explicitly adapt parameters to the specific task structure at inference time. Open questions include why TTT helps structured/pattern-based tasks (Dyck Languages, Hyperbaton) but not sequential-computation tasks (Boolean Expressions), whether the gains hold under stronger distribution shift (the semi-private ARC eval shows a notable decline to 47.5%), and whether the approach scales to settings where no invertible transformations or task-specific structure is available.
