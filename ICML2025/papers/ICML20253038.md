## Problem

Koopman operator theory provides a principled linear representation of nonlinear dynamics for time-series forecasting, but the operator is infinite-dimensional and prior Koopman-based forecasters (Koopa, KNF) rely on complex hierarchical architectures to approximate it. Meanwhile, linear RNNs have shown strong sequence-modeling performance but lack a theoretical grounding in dynamical systems theory. The paper asks whether a structured Koopman operator approximation can be made exactly equivalent to a linear RNN update, enabling a simple, efficient, and theoretically grounded forecasting architecture.

## Input / Output

The model takes a multivariate time series of length L (lookback window, set to L=2T for most benchmarks) and produces T future time steps. Each variate is processed independently (channel-independence), and the input is first decomposed into N frequency-specific branches via learnable FFT gating, then tokenized into non-overlapping patches of length P=L/6 to reduce sequence length before feeding the RNN. Instance normalization is applied to inputs and denormalization to outputs.

## Method

The key theoretical contribution is showing that when the dynamic state is represented as a collection of time-delayed observations, a block-diagonal structured Koopman operator (where each block is a power of a shared transition matrix W) is exactly equivalent to a linear RNN state update h_k = W h_{k-1} + g(y_k). SKOLR exploits this by splitting the input into N frequency-specific subspaces via learnable sigmoid-gated FFT filtering, encoding each branch with a 1–2 layer MLP (serving as Koopman measurement functions), and evolving each branch through an independent linear RNN (the structured Koopman operator). A mirrored MLP decoder inverts the measurement functions to produce predictions, and the entire pipeline is trained end-to-end with MSE loss. The block-diagonal structure yields N-fold parameter and compute savings over an unstructured operator of equivalent dimension, while the frequency decomposition allows each branch to specialize on different temporal scales.

## Results

On eight long-term forecasting benchmarks (ECL, Traffic, Weather, ETTm1, ETTm2, ETTh1, ETTh2, ILI) with L=2T and T∈{48,96,144,192}, SKOLR achieves the best average rank across all settings, with MSE values such as 0.132 on ECL (T=96), 0.368 on Traffic (T=96), 0.289 on ETTm1 (T=96), and 1.537 on ILI (T=48). On the M4 short-term forecasting benchmark, SKOLR achieves the best average OWA of 0.843, outperforming all 10 baselines. On nonlinear dynamical systems, SKOLR reduces MSE by up to 97% vs. Koopa on Pendulum (0.0001 vs. 0.0039) and 10.9% on Lorenz'63 (0.974 vs. 1.094).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SKOLR achieves the best average rank across all 8 datasets and 4 horizons, winning or tying for first place in the majority of settings. Against the closest Koopman-based competitor Koopa, SKOLR wins on ECL (0.132 vs. 0.134 MSE at T=96), Traffic (0.368 vs. 0.401), ETTm1 (0.289 vs. 0.294), and ILI (1.537 vs. 1.768 at T=48), while Koopa edges out SKOLR on ETTh2 (0.297 vs. 0.299 at T=96) and Weather at T=48 (0.126 vs. 0.131). Against transformer-based models, SKOLR consistently outperforms iTransformer, PatchTST, and Autoformer across nearly all datasets, with the largest margins on ETTm1 (0.289 vs. 0.302 for iTransformer at T=96) and ILI (1.537 vs. 1.667). Against TCN-based models (TimesNet, MICN) and the linear baseline DLinear, SKOLR wins on most datasets, with particularly large gaps on ILI (1.537 vs. 2.852 for TimesNet at T=48) and ETTm1 (0.289 vs. 0.329 for TimesNet). The KNF baseline performs poorly on ETT datasets (MSE >0.89), while SKOLR maintains competitive accuracy with only ~0.43M parameters and 1.7 MiB GPU memory on ETTh1, compared to 10–250M parameters for transformer and TCN baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper establishes an explicit mathematical equivalence between structured Koopman operator approximations (in the EDMD tradition of Li et al., 2017) and linear RNN state updates (Stolzenburg et al., 2018), building on the multiresolution DMD idea (Kutz et al., 2016) to decompose the operator into parallel branch-specific subspaces. While Orvieto et al. (2023) hinted at a connection between Koopman operators and linear RNNs, this work derives the equivalence formally and uses it as the architectural blueprint for SKOLR. The approach departs from prior Koopman-based forecasters (Koopa, KNF, K-Forecast) that rely on hierarchical architectures or complex spectral decompositions, replacing them with a simple parallel linear RNN stack whose block-diagonal structure directly encodes the Koopman operator.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The clean Koopman-to-linear-RNN equivalence provides a principled justification for why a very simple architecture (sigmoid frequency gating + 1–2 layer MLPs + linear RNN) can match or beat transformer-based forecasters, and the 85% parameter reduction at fixed Koopman dimension demonstrates that structured decomposition is far more parameter-efficient than a monolithic operator. Open questions include whether the approach extends to non-autonomous or partially observed systems with richer observation models, whether alternative spectral representations (beyond FFT-based soft gating) could improve expressivity, and whether the recursive prediction error accumulation can be further mitigated for very long horizons. The efficiency story (3.3 MiB GPU memory, 4× faster training than the best competitor) makes this practically relevant for resource-constrained deployment.
