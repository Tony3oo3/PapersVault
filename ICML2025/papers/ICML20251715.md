## Problem

Olympiad-level math reasoning for LLMs is bottlenecked by two issues: (1) the scarcity of large-scale, high-quality training data, since creating valid competition-level problems with correct solutions is expensive and expert-dependent, and (2) the saturation and contamination of existing benchmarks (MATH, GSM8K, OlympiadBench), which makes it impossible to distinguish genuine reasoning ability from memorized training data. The paper asks whether the unstructured Art of Problem Solving (AoPS) forum can be automatically mined to produce both a scalable training corpus and a continuously evolving, contamination-resistant evaluation set.

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes 1,076,712 raw AoPS forum topics (unstructured multi-post discussions containing math problems, informal solutions, and irrelevant chatter) and produces two artifacts: AoPS-Instruct, a 647K-pair SFT dataset of question + step-by-step rewritten solution pairs (posts before Jan 2024), and LiveAoPSBench, a 3,863-question evaluation set drawn exclusively from 2024 posts. Each solution is reformatted by Qwen 2.5 72B into a numbered step-by-step explanation with a boxed final answer in \boxed{} format, and the evaluation set additionally requires that two independent LLM rewrites (Llama 3.1 70B and Qwen 2.5 72B) agree on the final answer via string, numeric, or SymPy symbolic equivalence checks.

## Method

The core contribution is a fully automated five-stage curation pipeline: (1) Qwen 2.5 14B classifies each topic as math-relevant or not (reducing 1.08M topics to 478K), (2) Llama 3.1 70B extracts the question from the first post and identifies all valid solution posts in the thread, (3) Qwen 2.5 72B rewrites each concise community solution into a detailed step-by-step explanation (ablations show this rewriting is critical—fine-tuning on unrewritten solutions degrades benchmark performance), and (4) 10-gram exact-match decontamination removes overlap with standard benchmark test sets. For the evaluation set, the pipeline is extended with a stricter 8-gram filter, a heuristic that excludes proof-based questions lacking a concrete boxed answer, and a dual-LLM cross-check that discards any question where the two rewriters produce inconsistent final answers. The key design choice for contamination resistance is timestamp-based partitioning: the benchmark is built only from posts after a configurable cutoff date and is automatically regenerated as new forum posts appear, so the test set is by construction unseen to any model trained before that date.

## Results

Fine-tuning on AoPS-Instruct (647K QA pairs) consistently improves math reasoning across four model families. For Mathstral-7B, AoPS-Instruct raises AoPS24 accuracy from 15.40 (no SFT) to 23.60, and the Numina+AoPS-Instruct combination reaches 24.90; on MATH, scores go from 56.30 to 60.80. For DeepSeek-Math-7B, AoPS-Instruct lifts AoPS24 from 11.7 to 19.0 and OlympiadBench from 14.5 to 24.3. On LiveAoPSBench, all evaluated LLMs show a significant performance decline when moving from 2023 to 2024 questions (ranging from 2.4% to 23.6% drop), suggesting prior high scores on older benchmarks may reflect data contamination rather than true reasoning ability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Numina (Li et al., 2024), AoPS-Instruct alone outperforms Numina alone on every model and every benchmark. For example, on Mathstral-7B, AoPS-Instruct achieves 23.60 on AoPS24 versus Numina's 16.60, and 27.10 on OlympiadBench versus 23.40; the combined Numina+AoPS-Instruct mixture further improves to 24.90 and 29.60 respectively. On Llama-3.2-1B, AoPS-Instruct yields 10.00 on AoPS24 versus Numina's 8.00, a 25% relative gain. Against DART-Math-Hard (Tong et al., 2024, 585K samples), AoPS-Instruct (647K samples) wins on all four benchmarks when fine-tuning DeepSeek-Math-7B: 19.0 vs 14.4 on LiveAoPSBench, 24.3 vs 21.8 on OlympiadBench, 17.8 vs 15.4 on OmniMath, and 58.8 vs 52.5 on MATH. The paper also demonstrates that the performance gains are not merely due to increased compute, as a controlled-budget ablation (6 epochs on single datasets vs 3 epochs on the combined set) confirms the mixture advantage persists.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the timestamp-based evolving benchmark strategy from LiveCodeBench (Jain et al., 2024) to the math domain, partitioning AoPS forum posts by date to create a contamination-resistant evaluation set. For training data construction, it extends the Numina pipeline (Li et al., 2024), which also sources from AoPS but only from contest pages (~30K questions), to the full forum (1.07M posts), and adopts a similar LLM-based solution rewriting approach. The n-gram decontamination follows DeepSeekMath's 10-gram matching, while the dual-LLM cross-check verification (Llama 3.1 70B + Qwen 2.5 72B) for answer consistency is a novel quality-control step not present in prior math dataset construction work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 652K Olympiad-level QA pairs address a genuine data scarcity bottleneck for competition-level math reasoning, and the evolving, timestamped LiveAoPSBench offers a practical path toward contamination-resistant evaluation as static benchmarks like MATH and GSM8K saturate. Key limitations remain: the dataset excludes visual content (critical for geometry), cannot evaluate proof-based questions, and relies on community-generated solutions of variable quality. The observed 2.4–23.6% performance decline over 18 months reinforces that current LLM math scores on older benchmarks may overstate true reasoning ability, motivating continued investment in up-to-date evaluation infrastructure.
