## Problem

Traditional protein fitness prediction relies on a two-stage workflow: first retrieving homologs via computationally expensive Multiple Sequence Alignments (MSA), then training models on those alignments. This paradigm is slow, struggles with divergent sequences and complex indel patterns, and critically, retrieval operates independently of the downstream modeling objective. No prior work had achieved end-to-end joint training of a retrieval mechanism and a protein sequence model, leaving retrieval decisions fixed by alignment heuristics rather than learned for the specific task.

## Input / Output

At inference, a single query protein sequence is encoded by the retriever into a 480-dimensional embedding and searched against a pre-built Faiss index of ~62 million UniRef50 sequence embeddings (IVFPQ96x8, ~12.6 GB). The output is a fitness score defined as the log-likelihood ratio between the mutated and wild-type sequence under the reader model conditioned on retrieved homologs, ensembled across 15 forward passes varying conditioning set size (6k, 12k, 24k tokens) and sampling parameters, with sequences scored in both N-to-C and C-to-N directions. Retrieved sequences are filtered to retain only those with ≥15% sequence identity to the query.

## Method

Protriever comprises three components: a Retriever (ESM-2 35M transformer encoder with average pooling producing 480-dim embeddings), a Faiss-based vector index over UniRef50, and a Reader (PoET, a decoder transformer that processes concatenated query + retrieved homologs). Training is two-phase: first, the retriever is pretrained via Dense Passage Retrieval (DPR) contrastive loss on BLAST-identified homolog pairs from UniRef50; second, the full architecture is trained end-to-end using the EMDR loss, which treats retrieved sequences as latent variables and backpropagates the reader's autoregressive reconstruction performance through the retriever's relevance scores (with stop-gradient on the reader). At inference, vector similarity search via Faiss replaces MSA construction entirely, yielding ~4.6 ms per query versus 16.9 s (MMseqs2) or 2501 s (JackHMMER), while a re-ranking step with diversity-maximizing sampling across k-means clusters of retrieved sequences and ensembling over multiple conditioning set configurations produces the final fitness estimate.

## Results

On the ProteinGym substitution DMS benchmark (217 assays), Protriever achieves state-of-the-art performance among sequence-based models with a Spearman correlation of 0.479, AUC of 0.762, MCC of 0.374, top-K recall of 0.229, and NDCG of 0.788, outperforming the previous best sequence-only model PoET (0.470 Spearman) and all alignment-based and hybrid baselines. In terms of retrieval speed, Protriever completes homology search in 0.0046 s per query, making it approximately 133× faster than GPU-accelerated MMseqs2 (0.613 s) and over 500,000× faster than JackHMMER (2501 s), while simultaneously achieving higher downstream fitness prediction accuracy than all retrieval baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Protriever outperforms all baselines across every reported metric on ProteinGym. Against the closest competitor PoET (the previous best sequence-based model), Protriever gains +0.009 Spearman (0.479 vs. 0.470), +0.003 AUC, and +0.006 MCC. Compared to the strongest hybrid model TranceptEVE L, Protriever leads by +0.021 Spearman and +0.008 AUC. Against alignment-based methods, Protriever surpasses GEMME by +0.020 Spearman and EVE by +0.040 Spearman. Among unconditional protein language models, Protriever exceeds ESM-1v by +0.072 Spearman and ESM2 by +0.074 Spearman. In the retrieval speed comparison (Table 3), Protriever achieves a higher Spearman (0.479) than JackHMMER (0.459) while being ~544,000× faster (0.0046 s vs. 2501 s per query), and outperforms both MMseqs2 (0.463) and MMseqs2-GPU (0.462) in accuracy while being 133× and 3,665× faster respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

Protriever extends the retrieval-augmented generation paradigm from NLP (DPR, RAG, EMDR) to protein sequence modeling, using PoET as the reader backbone and ESM-2 as the retriever initialization. It departs from prior protein fitness prediction methods (EVE, MSA Transformer, Tranception, PoET) that rely on static, task-independent homology sets constructed via alignment tools like JackHMMER or MMseqs2, by making the retrieval process differentiable and jointly optimized with the downstream sequence modeling objective. The key conceptual shift is that the retriever learns which homologs are most informative for reconstruction rather than simply retrieving the most sequence-similar ones, drawing on the EMDR loss from open-domain QA to treat retrieved sequences as latent variables.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The two-orders-of-magnitude speedup over GPU-accelerated MMseqs2 (4.6 ms vs. 613 ms per query) makes large-scale proteome-wide fitness prediction practically feasible, and the portable vector index design allows dynamic database updates without model retraining—addressing a key limitation of both MSA-based and pLM approaches. The finding that JackHMMER's higher-sensitivity retrieval (more similar sequences) actually yields worse fitness prediction than Protriever's learned retrieval challenges the assumption that sequence similarity directly proxies evolutionary informativeness. Open questions include whether the learned retrieval representations capture structural or functional relationships beyond sequence identity, how the approach scales to databases beyond UniRef50, and whether structure-aware retrievers (e.g., TM-Vec) could further improve performance for tasks where 3D context matters.
