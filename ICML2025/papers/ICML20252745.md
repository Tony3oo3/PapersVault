## Problem

CLIP and similar contrastive language-image pre-training models struggle with fine-grained visual understanding due to three limitations: the 77-token text encoder cap prevents processing detailed descriptions, whole-image alignment prevents extracting region-specific representations, and the absence of hard fine-grained negative samples hinders discrimination of subtle attribute differences within the same object category.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252745/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts RGB images and text captions of variable length (short captions up to 77 tokens, long captions up to 248 tokens via linearly interpolated position embeddings), as well as bounding box coordinates for region-level alignment. It outputs image-level [CLS] embeddings, text [CLS] embeddings, and region-level embeddings obtained by average-pooling RoIAlign-extracted tokens within each bounding box. Cosine similarity between these embeddings yields the final image-text or region-text alignment score.

## Method

FG-CLIP employs a two-stage contrastive training paradigm on an extended CLIP dual-encoder (ViT-B/16 or ViT-L/14). Stage 1 performs global contrastive learning (InfoNCE) on 1.6 billion image pairs recaptioned with detailed long descriptions by CogVLM2-19B, alongside original short captions. Stage 2 introduces two additional losses: regional contrastive learning that aligns RoIAlign-extracted region features with 40 million region-specific captions across 12 million images, and hard fine-grained negative samples learning that uses 10 million attribute-modified negatives (generated by Llama-3.1-70B) to force the model to distinguish subtle semantic differences. The combined objective is L = L_global + 0.1·L_regional + 0.5·L_hard, where the hard negative loss treats each region's positive caption against M−1 negative captions in a softmax denominator.

## Results

On the FG-OVD fine-grained understanding benchmark, FG-CLIP (ViT-L/14) achieves 48.4% accuracy on the hardest subset, surpassing CLIP (15.4%), FineCLIP (22.8%), and Long-CLIP (9.6%) by large margins. On bounding box classification, FG-CLIP (ViT-L/14) reaches 63.2% Top-1 on COCO, 38.3% on LVIS, and 23.8% on Open Images, outperforming all baselines. For open-vocabulary detection on OV-COCO, F-ViT+FG-CLIP (ViT-L/14) achieves 41.2 AP50-novel, the best among all compared methods. On long-caption retrieval (DCI), FG-CLIP (ViT-L/14) scores 66.7 I2T / 66.1 T2I, substantially ahead of CLIP (37.2/36.4) and Long-CLIP (44.2/52.5).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FG-CLIP consistently outperforms CLIP across all evaluated tasks: on FG-OVD hard it improves from 15.4% to 48.4% (ViT-L/14), on COCO bounding box classification from 33.8% to 63.2%, and on DCI long-caption retrieval from 37.2 to 66.7 I2T. Against FineCLIP, the closest fine-grained competitor, FG-CLIP gains +25.6 points on FG-OVD hard (48.4 vs 22.8) and +8.7 points on COCO BBox classification (63.2 vs 54.5), demonstrating that the combination of regional contrastive learning and hard negative mining is more effective than FineCLIP's self-distillation approach. Long-CLIP, which extends token length but lacks region-level alignment, actually underperforms CLIP on fine-grained tasks (9.6 vs 15.4 on FG-OVD hard), confirming that longer text alone is insufficient without region-specific supervision. For open-vocabulary detection, F-ViT+FG-CLIP (ViT-L/14) achieves 41.2 AP50-novel, beating F-ViT+CLIPSelf (38.4) and F-ViT+FineCLIP (40.0). When used as the visual backbone for LLaVA-v1.5-7B, FG-CLIP improves RefCOCO testB from 67.9 to 74.9 (+7.0) and GQA from 61.9 to 62.9, while remaining competitive on ImageNet-1K zero-shot classification (69.0 vs EVA-CLIP's 74.7, the only task where a baseline leads).

## Relation to Prior Work

FG-CLIP extends the CLIP dual-encoder contrastive learning framework along the fine-grained understanding axis, building on Long-CLIP (longer text sequences), RegionCLIP (region-text alignment), and FineCLIP (self-distilled region-based training). It departs from prior work in three key ways: (1) scaling long-caption recaptioning to 1.6 billion pairs via CogVLM2-19B, far exceeding the 1–2.5 million scale of Long-CLIP and FineCLIP; (2) replacing category-label region alignment with 40 million bounding boxes paired with detailed natural-language region captions; and (3) introducing 10 million LLM-generated hard fine-grained negative samples that modify subtle attributes while preserving object identity—a component absent from all prior CLIP variants. The two-stage training paradigm (global contrastive learning followed by regional + hard-negative learning) integrates these contributions into a unified objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The FineHARD dataset (12M images, 40M region boxes, 10M hard negatives) and the hard-negative mining pipeline via Llama-3.1-70B provide a reusable resource for fine-grained multimodal research, and the released models/code lower the barrier for downstream open-vocabulary detection and LMM backbones. A notable limitation is the reliance on Yolo-World for automatic grounding, which may introduce systematic bias toward common object categories, and the 1.6B recaptioning cost (160×910B NPUs for 30 days) limits reproducibility. Open questions include whether the hard-negative strategy generalizes beyond attribute-level perturbations (e.g., spatial relationships, count changes), whether the approach extends to video or 3D grounding, and whether the gains persist when the grounding model is replaced with a weaker or domain-specific detector.
