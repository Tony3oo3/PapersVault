## Problem

Existing surrogate losses for multi-expert learning-to-defer are Bayes-consistent but fail to satisfy realizable H-consistency, so they cannot guarantee recovery of a zero-loss solution even when one exists in the hypothesis class. For two-stage deferral, prior realizable H-consistency results only hold for constant costs, not for the practically important classification-error-based costs. This paper asks whether surrogate losses can be constructed that simultaneously achieve realizable H-consistency, H-consistency bounds, and Bayes-consistency for both single-stage and two-stage multi-expert deferral.

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100/SVHN, 64×64 for Tiny ImageNet) and produces a score vector over an augmented label space of n + n_e entries; the argmax determines whether the system predicts a class label directly or defers to one of n_e experts. The deferral cost c_j(x,y) is typically the expert's 0/1 classification error, making the loss instance- and label-dependent. In the two-stage variant, the output is restricted to selecting among n_e pre-trained experts rather than also predicting a class, and the first-stage predictor is itself one of the experts.

## Method

The key technical insight is an alternative algebraic formulation of the multi-expert deferral loss (Lemma 3.1) that decomposes it into terms amenable to smooth surrogation. For single-stage deferral, indicator functions in this decomposition are replaced with comp-sum losses, yielding a new family L_Ψ that is realizable H-consistent under mild conditions (H closed under scaling, Ψ with appropriate boundary behavior) and admits a linear H-consistency bound when Ψ(u)=1−u (the MAE surrogate). For two-stage deferral, a cost-sensitive margin-based surrogate is proposed for two experts and extended to multiple experts via a comp-sum construction, achieving realizable R-consistency and R-consistency bounds for classification-error costs. The paper further derives enhanced H-consistency bounds under Tsybakov noise assumptions for both scenarios, interpolating between realizable guarantees and worst-case bounds.

## Results

In the single-stage setting, the proposed surrogate loss L_Ψ (with Ψ(u)=1−u) achieves the best system accuracy (SA) across all four datasets: 92.21% on CIFAR-10, 65.39% on CIFAR-100, 96.08% on SVHN, and 51.13% on Tiny ImageNet, outperforming both baselines. In the two-stage setting, L_Ψ (with Ψ(u)=−log(u)) similarly leads on all datasets: 93.34% (CIFAR-10), 68.70% (CIFAR-100), 96.47% (SVHN), and 46.62% (Tiny ImageNet). In the realizable Mixture-of-Gaussians experiment, the proposed loss achieves close to 100% system accuracy while both baselines fail to converge to a near-zero-error solution, empirically confirming realizable H-consistency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In the single-stage setting, the proposed L_Ψ (Ψ(u)=1−u) outperforms both Verma et al. (2023) and Mao et al. (2024a) on system accuracy across all four datasets, with margins of 0.23–0.52 percentage points (e.g., 92.21% vs. 91.85% and 91.98% on CIFAR-10; 65.39% vs. 64.13% and 64.93% on CIFAR-100). In the two-stage setting, L_Ψ (Ψ(u)=−log(u)) beats Mao et al. (2023a) on all datasets by 0.17–0.81 percentage points (e.g., 93.34% vs. 92.92% on CIFAR-10; 46.62% vs. 45.81% on Tiny ImageNet). The most striking comparison is in the realizable Mixture-of-Gaussians experiment, where the proposed loss reaches near-100% system accuracy while both baselines fail to find a near-zero-error solution, directly validating the realizable H-consistency guarantee that the baselines lack. Qualitatively, the proposed method defers significantly more and distributes deferrals more evenly across experts (e.g., 30.45%/30.76% for EXP1/EXP2 on CIFAR-10 vs. 11.23%/25.89% for Verma et al.), reflecting the different optimization landscape induced by the realizable-consistent surrogate.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the single-expert realizable H-consistent surrogate losses of Mao et al. (2024i) to the multi-expert setting, building on the comp-sum loss framework (Mao et al., 2023f) and the two-stage multi-expert formulation of Mao et al. (2023a). The key departure is from prior multi-expert surrogate losses (Verma et al., 2023; Mao et al., 2024a), which achieved Bayes-consistency and H-consistency bounds but were shown to lack realizable H-consistency even in the single-expert case. The paper also extends Tsybakov noise assumptions from standard classification to the deferral setting, providing enhanced non-asymptotic bounds under low-noise conditions for both single-stage and two-stage scenarios.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work closes a significant theoretical gap in learning-to-defer by providing the first surrogate losses for multi-expert deferral that simultaneously achieve realizable H-consistency, H-consistency bounds, and Bayes-consistency in both single-stage and two-stage settings. The practical relevance is substantial for LLM routing, medical triage, and other expert-assignment problems where one needs provable guarantees that the learned deferral policy is optimal in the realizable case. However, the empirical evaluation uses simplified expert models with non-overlapping domains, and the authors acknowledge that real-world scenarios with overlapping expert expertise, unseen experts at test time, and heterogeneous data remain open challenges for future work.
