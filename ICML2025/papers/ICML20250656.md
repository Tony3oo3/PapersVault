## Problem

Guidance for flow matching models—steering generated samples toward an energy-weighted target distribution—remains largely unexplored because flow matching's general design space (arbitrary source distributions, dependent couplings, flexible conditional paths) makes its guidance fundamentally different from the well-studied diffusion model case. Most existing guidance methods only apply to the special subset of flow matching with Gaussian sources and simple affine paths, where it reduces to classical diffusion guidance, leaving the broader design space without theoretically justified guidance techniques.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained flow matching vector field v_θ(x_t, t), an energy function J(x) (or, for inverse problems, a measurement y and known degradation operator H with noise scale σ_y), and produces guided samples from the weighted distribution p'(x) = (1/Z)p(x)e^{-J(x)} by adding a guidance vector field g_t to the original v_t during ODE integration. For image inverse problems, inputs are 256×256 CelebA-HQ images with box inpainting masks, 4× downsampling, or Gaussian blur, and outputs are reconstructed images evaluated via FID, LPIPS, PSNR, and SSIM. For offline RL, the energy is the negative return R, and outputs are action sequences scored by a learned critic.

## Method

The paper derives a unified guidance framework (Theorem 3.1) expressing the exact guidance vector field g_t as an integral over the intractable posterior p(z|x_t) weighted by the energy ratio e^{-J(x_1)}/Z_t, valid for arbitrary source distributions, couplings, and conditional paths. From this framework, they derive a family of practical methods: a training-free Monte Carlo guidance g^MC using self-normalized importance sampling (asymptotically exact but high-variance in high dimensions); approximate gradient-based guidance g^cov-A and g^cov-G obtained via Taylor expansion around the conditional mean, where g^cov-G uses a Jacobian trick to recover classical DPS/LGD under the uncoupled affine Gaussian path assumption; a derivative-free Gaussian-approximation guidance g^sim-MC and its inverse-problem specialization g^sim-inv-A; and training-based exact guidance g_φ learned via guidance matching losses (GM, VGM, RGM, MRGM) that train a network to fit g_t directly. The key theoretical result is that under the uncoupled affine Gaussian path assumption, the general guidance reduces exactly to ∇_{x_t} log Z_t, showing all classical diffusion guidance methods are special cases of the proposed framework.

## Results

On D4RL Locomotion (offline RL), the Monte Carlo guidance g^MC achieves the highest average normalized score of 81.0 under OT-CFM and 79.4 under CFM, outperforming the unguided baseline (55.9/54.3) and the Diffuser baseline (77.5). On CelebA-HQ image inverse problems (inpainting, super-resolution, deblurring at σ_y=0.05), ΠGDM achieves the best FID in 8 out of 12 metrics, while the proposed g^sim-A (derivative-free) achieves FID of 10.67/31.78/11.83 under OT-CFM, competitive with ΠGDM (12.56/9.83/12.95) without requiring backpropagation through the model. On 2D synthetic datasets with non-Gaussian source distributions, g^MC and the training-based g_φ produce guided samples that almost exactly match the ground-truth J-weighted distribution, while exact diffusion guidance (CEG) yields a largely distorted distribution.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Diffuser (Janner et al., 2022), the proposed g^MC guidance under OT-CFM achieves an average score of 81.0 vs. Diffuser's 77.5, and under CFM achieves 79.4, consistently outperforming the diffusion-based planner across all nine Locomotion tasks. Against ΠGDM (Song et al., 2023a) on CelebA-HQ inverse problems, the proposed g^cov-G matches or slightly trails ΠGDM (e.g., FID 15.42 vs. 9.83 on super-resolution under OT-CFM), while the derivative-free g^sim-A achieves a lower average FID than ΠGDM by 1.5 on inpainting and deblurring without any backpropagation cost. Against CEG (Lu et al., 2023), which is exact only under the uncoupled affine Gaussian path assumption, the paper shows on synthetic non-Gaussian datasets that CEG produces a largely distorted generated distribution, whereas g^MC and g_φ recover the correct J-weighted target. The training-based g_φ, while theoretically exact, underperforms g^MC by 10.4 points on average in D4RL due to unstable joint training of the two networks (g_φ and Z_φZ). Overall, g^MC is the best method in 7 out of 9 D4RL tasks, and g^cov-G outperforms g^cov-A by an average of 8.0 points, confirming that the Jacobian-based covariance estimation provides meaningful guidance improvement.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the flow matching framework (Lipman et al., 2023; Tong et al., 2024) and the energy-guided sampling paradigm from diffusion models (Dhariwal & Nichol, 2021; Song et al., 2023a,b; Chung et al., 2023), but departs fundamentally by deriving guidance for arbitrary source distributions and dependent couplings rather than the Gaussian-source, independent-coupling setting that reduces flow matching to diffusion. It subsumes classical diffusion guidance methods (DPS, LGD, ΠGDM, IGDM) as special cases under the uncoupled affine Gaussian path assumption, and extends the contrastive energy guidance of Lu et al. (2023) and the guided flows of Zheng et al. (2023) to the general flow matching design space. The Jacobian trick (Proposition 3.4) connecting the conditional covariance to the VF Jacobian under Gaussian paths provides a new theoretical bridge between flow matching and score-based methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first unified theoretical framework for guidance in general flow matching, providing practitioners with a principled decision tree for choosing among training-free (g^MC, g^cov-A, g^sim-MC) and training-based (g_φ) methods depending on dimensionality, energy smoothness, and whether the source is Gaussian. The key open limitation is the P≈1 approximation, which breaks down for strongly dependent couplings with rapidly varying energy functions—a gap the authors explicitly defer to future work. Practically, the asymptotically exact g^MC does not scale to high-dimensional image tasks due to MC variance, and the training-based g_φ suffers from unstable joint training, leaving a gap between theoretical exactness and practical deployability in the most important applications.
