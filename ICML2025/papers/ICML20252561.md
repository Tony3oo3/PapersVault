## Problem

Existing GCL approaches for protein representation learning rely solely on 2D topological augmentation that ignores the biological significance of functional communities, while 3D structure-based protein graph augmentation remains unexplored due to the fact that traditional coordinate perturbations disrupt peptide planes and homology modeling tools produce low-diversity or structurally inaccurate views.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252561/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes protein graphs built from Cα coordinates, residue-type embeddings, side-chain torsion angles (χ1–χ4), and DSSP secondary structure assignments, with edge features constructed via a rotation-invariant relative spatial encoding scheme. The hierarchical EdgeGCN encoder (four pooling levels) produces a single graph embedding per view, which is used both for contrastive learning (cosine similarity between original and augmented views) and for downstream prediction of fold class, enzyme reaction class, GO terms, EC numbers, and ligand binding affinity.

## Method

The paper proposes two biology-aware augmentation strategies integrated into a unified GCL framework. FCI formulates edge dropping/adding as a spectral optimization over the normalized Laplacian: it maximizes spectral change for dropped edges (weighted by 1/W where W is side-chain chemical similarity computed from sin/cos of the first four torsion angles) to remove cross-community edges while preserving high-similarity intra-community edges, and minimizes spectral change for added edges. 3-PSI introduces two 3D invariance-preserving perturbations: (1) small dihedral angle rotations (Δφ, Δψ) constrained within the Ramachandran region to preserve peptide plane integrity, and (2) controlled rotations of DSSP-identified α-helices and β-sheets around axes defined by cross products of adjacent structure normals, using Rodrigues rotation matrices with small random angles. The combined framework trains a shared hierarchical EdgeGCN encoder with a loss that sums the classification loss and GCL losses from both FCI and 3-PSI augmented views, weighted by λ.

## Results

On the four protein-related tasks (EC, GO, FOLD, Reaction), the best combined augmentation (3-PSIAlpha + FCI) achieves EC F_max of 0.885, GO-CC F_max of 0.477, FOLD average accuracy of 80.0%, and Reaction accuracy of 89.0%. The 3-PSIDiag + FCI variant achieves slightly higher GO-BP (0.461), GO-MF (0.662), GO-CC (0.484), and FOLD Superfamily (81.3%). On the PDBbind ligand binding affinity task, 3-PSIAlpha + FCI achieves Pearson correlation of 0.753 and Spearman of 0.749 at 60% sequence identity, outperforming Holoprot-Superpixel (0.724/0.715).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed 3-PSIAlpha + FCI outperforms the strongest protein-specific baseline CDConv (Fan et al., 2023) by +1.5% on EC (0.885 vs 0.870), +2.5% on GO-CC (0.477 vs 0.475), +3.0% on FOLD average (80.0 vs 77.0), and +0.4% on Reaction (89.0 vs 88.6). Against the best 2D topology-based GCL method CI-GCL (Tan et al., 2024), the combined approach gains +1.5% on EC, +5.4% on GO-CC (0.477 vs 0.453), +1.3% on FOLD average, and +2.4% on Reaction. Compared to the best 3D structure-based GCL baseline (Trad. 3D Aug. from ProNet), the improvement is most pronounced on GO-CC (+13.8% relative, 0.477 vs 0.420) and Reaction (+2.5%, 89.0 vs 86.5). The 3-PSIDiag + FCI variant achieves the best GO-BP (0.461), GO-MF (0.662), GO-CC (0.484), and FOLD Superfamily (81.3) scores across all methods. On the PDBbind ligand binding affinity task, 3-PSIAlpha + FCI surpasses Holoprot-Superpixel (Somnath et al., 2021) on all three metrics at both 30% and 60% sequence identity thresholds (e.g., Pearson 0.753 vs 0.724 at 60%). The paper reports that FCI alone surpasses the best 2D topology-based GCL by 4.4% relative gain on GO-CC, while 3-PSIDiag alone achieves 11.4% relative improvement on GO-CC over the best 3D baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the community-invariant graph contrastive learning framework of Tan et al. (2024) by incorporating residue-level chemical similarity (side-chain torsion angles from Wang et al., 2023b) as edge weights into the spectral augmentation objective, moving beyond purely topological community preservation. It also builds on the hierarchical EdgeGCN architecture and relative spatial encoding of Fan et al. (2023) as the shared backbone. The key departure is the introduction of 3D structure-based graph augmentation (dihedral angle rotation constrained by Ramachandran regions, and secondary-structure rotation via Rodrigues matrices), which the authors identify as entirely unexplored in prior protein GCL work, contrasting with existing approaches that rely solely on 2D topology or on homology-modeling tools that produce insufficiently diverse views.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper fills a genuine gap by showing that 3D structure-aware augmentation can be done without the structural disruption caused by naive coordinate perturbations or the limited diversity of homology-modeling tools, offering a principled alternative for protein GCL. However, the absolute performance gains over strong baselines (CDConv, CI-GCL) are modest (typically 1–2 points in F_max or accuracy), and the method is tightly coupled to protein-specific structural knowledge (DSSP assignments, Ramachandran constraints), limiting immediate transfer to other molecular domains. Open questions include whether the invariance-based augmentation principles generalize to other biological molecules or to larger-scale protein language models, and whether the spectral decomposition cost becomes prohibitive for very large protein complexes.
