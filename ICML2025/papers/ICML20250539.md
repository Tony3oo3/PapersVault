## Problem

Existing theoretical analyses of weak-to-strong (W2S) generalization attribute the student's advantage to absolute model capacity differences (e.g., robustness to perturbation or ability to fit the target function), yet W2S empirically occurs even when both teacher and student have sufficient capacity for the downstream task. This paper asks what drives W2S beyond absolute capacity, specifically through the lens of intrinsic dimension and student-teacher feature correlation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250539/cropped_page6_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on pretrained features φ_w(x), φ_s(x) ∈ R^d from weak and strong models, where relevant features concentrate in low-dimensional subspaces of dimensions d_w, d_s ≪ d. A small labeled set of size n finetunes the weak teacher, which then generates N pseudo-labels over an unlabeled set to finetune the strong student. The key structural parameter is the correlation dimension d_{s∧w} = ‖V_s^T V_w‖_F², measuring the overlap between the two feature subspaces via canonical angles, which governs how much of the teacher's variance is inherited versus reduced.

## Method

The paper casts finetuning as ridgeless regression over subgaussian features and provides an exact variance characterization for the W2S model under Gaussian features. The variance decomposes into two terms: in the overlapped subspace V_s ∩ V_w, the student inherits the weak teacher's variance (scaling as d_{s∧w}/n), while in the discrepancy subspace V_w \ V_s, the variance is reduced by a factor of d_s/N. This reveals that feature discrepancy between teacher and student is a virtue: the weak teacher's noise-induced error acts as independent label noise for the student in the discrepancy subspace, whose generalization error vanishes at rate O(d_s/N). The analysis further characterizes the scaling of performance gap recovery (PGR) and outperforming ratio (OPR) with respect to sample sizes n and N, showing non-monotonic behavior when approximation errors are non-negligible.

## Results

In the synthetic regression setting (d=20,000, d_s=100, d_w=200), W2S outperforms both the weak teacher and the strong SFT baseline when variance dominates (σ²=0.01 ≫ ρ_s+ρ_w) and the correlation dimension is low (d_{s∧w}=10), with the advantage growing as N increases. When the correlation dimension is high (d_{s∧w}=90), W2S still beats the weak teacher but fails to outperform the strong SFT baseline. On UTKFace age estimation (CLIP-B32 student vs. ResNet18–152 teachers), PGR and OPR are highest for weak teachers with the lowest d_{s∧w}/d_w ratio, and both metrics scale inversely with labeled sample size n while showing diminishing returns in pseudo-label size N.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares the W2S model against three references: the weak teacher f_w (finetuned on n noisy labels), the strong SFT baseline f_s (strong model finetuned on the same n labels), and the strong ceiling f_c (strong model finetuned on N+n labels). W2S consistently outperforms the weak teacher in the variance-dominated regime (σ² ≫ ρ_s+ρ_w), with the gap widening as the correlation dimension d_{s∧w} decreases; however, when variance is not dominant (σ² ≈ ρ_s+ρ_w), W2S can fail to beat the weak teacher. Against the strong SFT baseline, W2S wins only when d_{s∧w} is sufficiently small relative to d_s and d_w (e.g., d_{s∧w}=10 with N ≳ (d_w/d_{s∧w}−1)·d_s), while it loses when d_{s∧w} is large (e.g., d_{s∧w}=90). On UTKFace, the ResNet18 teacher (lowest d_w, lowest d_{s∧w}/d_w) yields the best PGR and OPR, while ResNet152 (highest d_w, higher d_{s∧w}/d_w) yields the worst, confirming that larger student-teacher feature discrepancy drives better W2S generalization. Injecting additional label noise (increasing σ²) monotonically improves both PGR and OPR, empirically validating that variance reduction is the key mechanism behind W2S.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the W2S generalization framework introduced by Burns et al. (2024) and the observation that finetuning operates in low intrinsic dimensions (Aghajanyan et al., 2021), casting the problem as ridgeless regression in the kernel regime (Jacot et al., 2018). While Ildiz et al. (2024) also analyze W2S in a ridgeless regression setting, this paper departs fundamentally by focusing on variance reduction rather than bias/approximation, introducing the correlation dimension d_{s∧w} as a new measure of student-teacher feature alignment. The key conceptual departure is treating model capacity not as an absolute property but as a relative one, where the *discrepancy* between student and teacher feature subspaces becomes the driving force for W2S generalization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 'discrepancy is virtue' finding provides practitioners with a concrete criterion for selecting weak teachers in W2S pipelines: lower correlation dimension and lower intrinsic dimension of the student relative to the teacher improve W2S performance. The non-monotonic scaling of PGR and OPR with labeled sample size n (Corollary 3.6) has direct practical implications for data budget allocation. However, the analysis is confined to the variance-dominated regime with both models having sufficient capacity, and the exact characterization relies on Gaussian features; the bias-dominated regime and the transition between regimes remain open, as does extending the theory beyond linear probing to full finetuning dynamics.
