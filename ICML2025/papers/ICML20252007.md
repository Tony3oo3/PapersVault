## Problem

Diffusion-based graph generative models tightly entangle training and sampling, so adjusting noise schedules, rate matrices, or step sizes requires costly retraining. This one-size-fits-all coupling prevents dataset-specific optimization and limits sampling efficiency. DeFoG addresses this by introducing a discrete flow matching formulation that fully decouples the two stages, enabling flexible, training-free sampling optimization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252007/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

At sampling time, DeFoG takes a target number of nodes (drawn from the training set distribution) and produces a complete undirected graph where every node pair carries an edge label from a discrete set (including an explicit 'non-existing' edge category) and each node carries a label from a discrete set. The denoising proceeds via independent Euler steps over a continuous time interval [0,1] with variable step sizes controlled by a distortion function, and the network is conditioned on the current noisy graph, RRWP structural features, and the continuous time value t. A hyperparameter λ in the training loss allows differential weighting of node versus edge prediction to accommodate varying topologies.

## Method

DeFoG formulates graph generation as a discrete flow matching problem: noising follows a straight linear interpolation from the one-hot clean graph to a predefined initial distribution, while denoising is governed by a CTMC whose rate matrix is computed from the network's prediction of the clean graph's marginal distributions. The key architectural insight is that training only requires the network to predict p_{1|t}(·|G_t) via cross-entropy loss, while the rate matrix used at sampling time (R_t*) is chosen independently, enabling post-hoc tuning of step sizes (sample distortion), training time distribution (train distortion), target guidance strength (ω), and stochasticity level (η) without retraining. The paper provides theoretical guarantees: Corollary 1 bounds the rate matrix estimation error by the training loss, and Corollary 2 bounds the total variation distance between the generated and ground-truth distributions, ensuring faithful replication. The backbone is a permutation-equivariant graph transformer augmented with Relative Random Walk Probability (RRWP) features to overcome expressivity limitations of standard GNNs.

## Results

On synthetic graph datasets, DeFoG achieves near-saturated V.U.N. scores of 99.5% (Planar), 96.5% (Tree), and 90.0% (SBM), with average ratios of 1.6, 1.6, and 4.9 respectively. On molecular generation, DeFoG reaches 99.0% validity on Guacamol and 92.8% validity on MOSES (with 99.9% uniqueness), surpassing the previous SOTA of 90.5% on MOSES. Notably, with only 5–10% of the sampling steps used by diffusion baselines, DeFoG still achieves 95.0% V.U.N. on Planar, 86.5% on SBM, and 91.7% validity on Guacamol, demonstrating substantial efficiency gains.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DeFoG outperforms all autoregressive baselines (GraphRNN, GRAN, BiGG, GraphGen) and the GAN baseline (SPECTRE) by large margins across all synthetic datasets, where those methods often produce 0–25% valid graphs. Against the discrete-time diffusion baseline DiGress, DeFoG wins on Planar (99.5 vs 77.5 V.U.N.), SBM (90.0 vs 60.0), and matches on Tree (96.5 vs 90.0). Among continuous-time diffusion models, DeFoG surpasses Cometh on SBM (90.0 vs 75.0 V.U.N.) and MOSES validity (92.8% vs 90.5%), and matches Cometh on Planar (99.5 vs 99.5); it also beats DisCo on both Planar (99.5 vs 83.6) and SBM (90.0 vs 66.2). The only baseline that outperforms DeFoG is HSpectre on the Tree dataset (100.0 vs 96.5 V.U.N.), which leverages a local expansion procedure particularly suited to hierarchical tree structures. On Guacamol, DeFoG achieves 99.0% validity, marginally exceeding Cometh (98.9%) and substantially outperforming DiGress (85.2%) and DisCo (86.6%). With only 5–10% of the sampling steps, DeFoG still surpasses most diffusion baselines at full step counts, e.g., 95.0% V.U.N. on Planar with 50 steps vs. DiGress's 77.5% with 500 steps.

## Relation to Prior Work

DeFoG adapts the Discrete Flow Matching (DFM) formulation of Campbell et al. (2024) to graph generation, replacing the CTMC-based noising process used in continuous-time discrete diffusion models (DisCo, Cometh) with a simple linear interpolation. This single change is what enables full training-sampling decoupling: in prior continuous-time discrete diffusion graph models, the noising rate matrix must be fixed during training, implicitly constraining the denoising rate matrix at sampling time. DeFoG instead prescribes the noising trajectory directly and selects the denoising rate matrix exclusively at sampling, opening a design space (time distortion, target guidance, stochasticity via detailed balance) that was inaccessible to prior methods. The architecture itself is borrowed from DiGress's graph transformer with RRWP features, so the contribution is in the generative formulation and sampling design rather than the network.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: DeFoG matches or exceeds SOTA diffusion models with only 5–10% of their sampling steps, which matters for deployment in iterative or resource-constrained settings (e.g., molecular screening, digital pathology). The theoretical guarantees (bounded rate-matrix estimation error and total-variation deviation) provide a principled justification for the decoupled framework rather than relying purely on empirical validation. Open questions include whether the current simple per-strategy hyperparameter sweep can be replaced by more principled or learned sampling strategies, how the approach scales to much larger graphs, and whether the sampling design space can be further exploited with purely inference-time techniques (e.g., distillation, learned step schedules).
