## Problem

Existing learning-based feature caching for Diffusion Transformers (e.g., Learning-to-Cache) suffers from two training-inference discrepancies: (1) Prior Timestep Disregard, where training samples a single timestep in isolation and ignores how earlier caching decisions shape the cache state and input at the current step, and (2) Objective Mismatch, where training minimizes per-step noise prediction error rather than the final image quality that inference actually targets. These gaps cause error accumulation and severe object distortion during multi-step denoising.

## Input / Output

The method trains only a lightweight Router matrix (T×N learnable values, one per timestep and Transformer block) on top of a frozen pre-trained DiT; no training images are required (image-free). At inference, the input is a text prompt (T2I) or class label (class-conditional) plus Gaussian noise, and the output is a generated image at resolutions from 256×256 up to 2048×2048. The Router is applied identically across 4 samplers (DDIM, DPM-Solver++, IDDPM, SA-Solver) and 8 model variants, with the only runtime overhead being a cache buffer consuming less than 6% extra memory.

## Method

HarmoniCa resolves both discrepancies with two coupled techniques. First, Step-Wise Denoising Training (SDT) runs the full T-step denoising trajectory during each training iteration: a student model (with caching) and a teacher model (without caching) both denoise from x_T to x_0, and the student is trained to mimic the teacher's per-step output via a differentiable soft combination o_i = r_{t,i}·b_i(h_i,cs) + (1−r_{t,i})·c_i, so the cache state at each step naturally reflects all prior steps' compute-vs-cache decisions. Second, the Image Error Proxy-Guided Objective (IEPO) replaces the uniform per-step MSE loss with a weighted loss λ^(t)·L_MSE^(t) + β·Σr_{t,i}, where the proxy λ^(t) = ‖x_0 − x_0^(t)‖_F² quantifies how much disabling caching at timestep t degrades the final image; λ^(t) is updated every C iterations via gradient-free generation passes that compare full-caching vs. timestep-t-disabled outputs. This lets the Router learn to cache aggressively at timesteps where the final image is insensitive and compute fresh features where it matters, achieving up to 2.07× theoretical speedup with improved FID over the non-accelerated model.

## Results

On DiT-XL/2 256×256 (ImageNet, 20-step DDIM), HarmoniCa achieves IS 206.57, FID 4.88, and sFID 5.91 with a 1.44× speedup (0.456s), outperforming Learning-to-Cache (IS 201.37, FID 5.34, 1.41×) and the 14-step DDIM baseline (IS 201.83, FID 5.77, 1.41×). On PIXART-α 256×256 (MS-COCO, 20-step DPM-Solver++), HarmoniCa reaches FID 26.33 and CLIP 31.13 with a 1.60× speedup, surpassing the non-accelerated model's FID of 27.68 and FORA's FID of 27.42. At 1024×1024 resolution, HarmoniCa achieves FID 23.02 (vs. 25.01 non-accelerated) with a 1.63× speedup, and at 2048×2048 (PIXART-Σ) it delivers a 1.56× speedup with improved CLIP score (31.51 vs. 31.19). Training requires no images and only 1.63 hours (vs. 2.15h for Learning-to-Cache), a 25% reduction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Learning-to-Cache (the prior SOTA for learnable DiT caching), HarmoniCa wins on every metric at all tested timesteps: at T=20 it improves IS by +5.2, FID by −0.46, and sFID by −0.45 while achieving a higher CUR (37.50% vs. 35.60%) and lower latency (0.456s vs. 0.468s); the gap widens at T=10 where HarmoniCa gains +6.74 IS and −1.24 FID. Against FORA on PIXART-α, HarmoniCa achieves lower FID (26.33 vs. 27.42 at 256×256; 23.73 vs. 25.16 at 100-step IDDPM) with a higher speedup (1.60× vs. 1.32×; 1.69× vs. 1.65×). Against Δ-DiT on PIXART-α 1024×1024, HarmoniCa reduces FID by 3.35 (32.53 vs. 35.88) and increases speedup from 1.49× to 1.73×. Against quantization baselines (EfficientDM, PTQ4DiT) and pruning (Diff-Pruning) on DiT-XL/2, HarmoniCa achieves substantially better FID (4.88 vs. 6.10, 71.82, and 8.22 respectively) at comparable or higher speedup ratios (1.44×). Against fewer-step samplers (DDIM, DPM-Solver++, IDDPM, SA-Solver), HarmoniCa consistently matches or exceeds the quality of the non-accelerated model while delivering 1.44×–1.69× speedup, whereas reducing steps alone degrades FID significantly (e.g., 20→15 step DPM-Solver++ increases FID from 27.68 to 31.68).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends Learning-to-Cache (Ma et al., 2024a), which first introduced a learnable router for block-wise feature caching in Diffusion Transformers. The key departure is a diagnosis of two specific training-inference discrepancies in that method—Prior Timestep Disregard (training ignores the cumulative effect of earlier caching decisions) and Objective Mismatch (per-step noise MSE vs. final image quality)—and addresses them via a student-teacher step-wise denoising training paradigm (SDT) and an image-error-proxy-guided loss (IEPO). The work also positions itself against heuristic caching strategies (FORA, Δ-DiT) and U-Net-specific methods (DeepCache, Faster Diffusion), arguing that adaptive, learned caching for DiTs requires proper alignment between training dynamics and the multi-step inference trajectory.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Feature caching is a practical, hardware-agnostic acceleration path for DiT models whose multi-step denoising makes inference prohibitively expensive at high resolutions; HarmoniCa demonstrates that fixing training-inference alignment yields consistent gains across 8 models, 4 samplers, and resolutions up to 2048×2048, with the notable practical advantage of requiring no training images (image-free), which removes a major barrier for models trained on proprietary datasets. Open questions include extending to finer (token-level) caching granularity, video/audio/3D generation, sampler-agnostic routers, and autonomous selection of the speed-quality trade-off coefficient β. The paper acknowledges that the Router is sampler-specific and that block-wise granularity is a limitation, leaving room for more generalizable caching formulations.
