## Problem

Magnitude-based pruning, which intuitively should outperform random pruning in model merging by retaining critical weights, paradoxically underperforms methods like DARE. The paper identifies two root causes: high parameter overlap between task vectors (causing sign and magnitude conflicts during merging) and unbalanced weight distribution (where retained weights cluster in specific regions, amplified by global rescaling). No existing sparsification method addresses both issues simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252840/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes task vectors (element-wise differences between fine-tuned model weights and a shared base model), the base model parameters, an n:m sparsity configuration (e.g., 32:128 meaning retain 32 of every 128 consecutive weights), and per-task scaling coefficients λ. It produces the final merged model weights and per-task binary sparse masks. A key structural constraint is that the n:m block size m must divide the weight matrix dimensions, and the sequential CA step means the mask from the first-pruned task vector directly constrains which weights the second task vector can retain.

## Method

CABS combines two complementary strategies. Conflict-Aware Sparsification (CA) applies sequential pruning: the first task vector is pruned via n:m block pruning, then its binary mask is inverted and element-wise multiplied with the second task vector before the second pruning pass, guaranteeing zero overlap between retained parameters (formally, (M_A)_ij · (M_B)_ij = 0 for all i,j). Balanced Sparsification (BS) repurposes n:m structured pruning—originally designed for inference acceleration—to enforce uniform weight retention across every block of m consecutive weights, preventing the localized concentration that magnitude pruning produces. The theoretical analysis proves that non-overlapping masks yield orthogonal task vectors (zero Frobenius inner product), which eliminates cross-terms in the merged weight update ‖ΔW‖²_F = ‖λ_A τ_A‖²_F + ‖λ_B τ_B‖²_F, enabling independent per-task scaling without interference. When total sparsity exceeds 100% (making zero overlap infeasible), a fallback algorithm selectively admits additional weights from the overlapping region to meet the target sparsity while minimizing residual overlap.

## Results

On the LLM Leaderboard (Mistral-7B, sparsity=0.75), CABS achieves an average score of 76.50, surpassing the 'ideal model' upper bound (76.30) and the best baseline (76.02). On the 4-task GLUE benchmark (RoBERTa-Base, sparsity=0.90), CABS reaches 81.70 average accuracy, outperforming the SOTA (TIES-Merging + DARE at 79.88) by 1.82 points and the vanilla Task Arithmetic baseline (79.55) by 2.15 points. On the 6-task GLUE set, CABS achieves 69.62, a +3.06 improvement over Task Arithmetic. CABS also dominates the top four positions among ≤8B models on the Open LLM Leaderboard 2 (Qwen2.5-7B backbone).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CABS consistently outperforms all six baselines across both small and large model settings. Against Task Arithmetic (Ilharco et al., 2022), CABS gains +2.15 on 4-task GLUE (81.70 vs. 79.55) and +0.48 on the LLM Leaderboard (76.50 vs. 76.02). Against DARE (Yu et al., 2024), CABS improves by +1.94 on 4-task GLUE (81.70 vs. 79.76) and +0.73 on the LLM Leaderboard (76.50 vs. 75.77). Against TIES-Merging (Yadav et al., 2024), CABS leads by +4.27 on 4-task GLUE (81.70 vs. 77.43) and +0.65 on the LLM Leaderboard (76.50 vs. 75.85). Magnitude pruning (Zhu & Gupta, 2018) actually degrades performance relative to Task Arithmetic on 4-task GLUE (78.53 vs. 79.55), while CABS improves it substantially. On the 2-task ablation (Table 5), CABS's Balanced Sparsification component outperforms both SparseGPT (Frantar & Alistarh, 2023; 80.34) and Wanda (Sun et al., 2023; 80.40) by roughly 1 point, confirming that advanced one-shot pruning methods are less suited to task-vector sparsification than the balanced n:m strategy.

## Relation to Prior Work

CABS builds directly on the task-vector merging paradigm initiated by Task Arithmetic (Ilharco et al., 2022) and the conflict-resolution strategies of TIES-Merging (Yadav et al., 2024) and DARE (Yu et al., 2024). Its key departure is a diagnostic: the paper identifies two specific failure modes of magnitude-based pruning in the merging context—high parameter overlap and unbalanced weight distribution—that explain why DARE's random pruning outperforms it, and then addresses each with a targeted mechanism (sequential masking for overlap, n:m block pruning for balance). The n:m pruning component is repurposed from its original role in structured compression (Zhou et al., 2021) to serve as a distribution-balancing tool for task vectors, a use case not explored in prior merging or pruning literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CABS provides a simple, low-overhead recipe that consistently outperforms existing sparsification-based merging methods across architectures (Mistral-7B, RoBERTa, GPT-2, Qwen2.5) and sparsity levels, and notably exceeds the per-task 'ideal model' upper bound on Mistral-7B—suggesting merging can yield generalization benefits beyond any single fine-tuned model. However, the method remains limited to same-architecture merging, requires manual λ tuning, and introduces two additional hyperparameters (pruning order, n:m ratio). Open questions include extending the approach to heterogeneous architectures and closing the gap in multi-task settings where merged models still underperform dedicated single-task fine-tunes.
