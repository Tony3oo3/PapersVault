## Problem

In latent variable LiNGAM models, causal effects are generally non-identifiable from the covariance matrix alone due to unobserved confounding. Existing cumulant-based methods either require one proxy variable per latent confounder and forbid a causal edge from the proxy to the treatment, or cannot handle underspecified instrumental variable settings where fewer instruments exist than treatments. This paper proves that a single proxy (or instrument) suffices for identification in both cases, provided higher-order cumulants are exploited.

## Input / Output

The method requires an i.i.d. sample from the observed variables, the known causal graph structure (DAG), and a bound l on the number of latent confounders. It computes k-statistics (unbiased cumulant estimators) up to order k(l) = (l+2) + ⌈(-3+√(8l+17))/2⌉, which grows with the number of latent variables. The output is a point estimate of the total causal effect from treatment to outcome; in the underspecified IV setting, it returns one estimate per treatment.

## Method

The identification strategy builds on a polynomial p_{V,l}(b) whose roots are the causal effects from a latent confounder to an observed variable (Schkoda et al., 2024). For the proxy variable graph, the polynomial is applied to three variable pairs [Z,T], [Z,Y], [T,Y]; a linear system (Lemma 3.3) recovers exogenous cumulants that disambiguate the permutation of roots, and the unique entry in the [T,Y] root vector not matching a ratio of [Z,T] to [Z,Y] entries yields the causal effect. When an edge from proxy to treatment exists, the method iterates over candidate pairs [b_{T,Z}, b_{Y,Z}] or [b_{T,L_i}, b_{Y,L_i}], subtracts the proxy's contribution to reduce to the no-edge case, and selects the candidate minimizing a sample-level consistency residual. For underspecified IVs, the instrument is regressed out of treatments and outcome via covariance-based backdoor adjustment, then the polynomial identification is applied to each [T^{I,i}, Y^{I}] pair, with the TSLS constraint b_{Y,I} = Σ b_{T^i,I}·b_{Y,T^i} selecting the correct root combination. Estimation replaces population cumulants with k-statistics and solves polynomial root-finding and permutation-alignment subproblems on finite samples.

## Results

The paper's primary evaluation metric is the relative absolute error |b̂_{Y,T} − b*_{Y,T}| / |b*_{Y,T}|, reported as median over 100 simulations with interquartile range. On the Card-Krueger minimum wage dataset, the proposed Cumulant method estimates the causal effect at 2.68 (graph G₁), 2.71 (graph G₂), and 8.26 (graph G₃), with the first two matching the Cross-Moment and Difference-in-Differences estimates of 2.68. The headline theoretical and empirical finding is that the Cumulant method is the only approach that yields a consistent estimator for graphs with multiple latent confounders (G₂), a causal edge from proxy to treatment (G₃), and the underspecified instrumental variable setting (Fig. 4), where all baselines fail to converge as sample size grows.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Cross-Moment (Kivva et al., 2023), the Cumulant method loses on graph G₁ (no edge from proxy to treatment) because Cross-Moment achieves consistency using only third-order cumulants, whereas Cumulant requires fourth-order cumulants that are harder to estimate in finite samples. However, on G₂ (multiple latent confounders) and G₃ (edge from proxy to treatment), Cross-Moment is provably inconsistent and its relative error does not decay with sample size, while Cumulant converges to the true value. Against GRICA (Tramontano et al., 2024b), Cumulant is consistently superior in the asymptotic regime for G₂, G₃, and the underspecified IV graph; GRICA shows better small-sample performance due to lower variance of its ℓ₁-optimization but is not asymptotically unbiased. Against ReLVLiNGAM (Schkoda et al., 2024), Cumulant outperforms on G₃ because ReLVLiNGAM jointly performs causal discovery and effect estimation, increasing its complexity and error. Against Min Norm (Ailer et al., 2023) on the underspecified instrumental variable graph (Fig. 4), Cumulant is the only method that consistently estimates both treatment effects with a single instrument, while Min Norm introduces arbitrary bias from the minimum-norm projection.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the algebraic identification framework of Schkoda et al. (2024), which provides a polynomial whose roots encode causal effects in a two-observed-variable, l-latent-variable graph, and on the canonical lvLiNGAM formulation of Salehkaleybar et al. (2020). It extends the proxy-variable identification result of Kivva et al. (2023) in two key ways: allowing a causal edge from the proxy to the treatment and recovering the effect for any number of latent confounders using a single proxy (rather than one per confounder). For the underspecified instrumental variable setting, it departs from the covariance-based TSLS tradition (which requires at least one instrument per treatment) by exploiting higher-order cumulants to identify multiple treatment effects from a single instrument, and provides the first consistent estimators for the identifiable effects characterized by Tramontano et al. (2024b), whose OICA-based approach lacked a consistent estimation procedure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper closes a practical gap: prior characterizations of identifiable causal effects in lvLiNGAM (Tramontano et al., 2024b) were not accompanied by consistent estimators, and existing moment-based methods (Kivva et al., 2023) fail when the proxy influences the treatment or when multiple latent confounders are present. The negative result (Theorem 3.6) establishing that third-order cumulants alone are insufficient for the proxy-to-treatment case sharpens the theoretical boundary of what is achievable. Open questions include whether the non-Gaussianity assumption can be relaxed, whether the methods extend to more complex graph topologies beyond the two specific settings studied, and how the high variance of k-statistics at moderate sample sizes can be mitigated without sacrificing consistency.
