## Problem

Symbolic regression methods fail to scale to high-dimensional datasets (p=102–459) because the expression search space expands double-exponentially with the number of input features. Standard variable selection approaches (e.g., random forest) are inadequate for SR pre-screening because SR demands a near-zero false negative rate—excluding even one relevant variable makes recovery of the true function impossible—while existing nonparametric methods like BART-G.SE exhibit insufficient true positive rates under noisy or low-sample-size conditions.

## Input / Output

The framework consumes a tabular dataset (y ∈ R^n, X ∈ R^{n×p}) with many irrelevant features plus a set of mathematical operations, and produces a reduced feature subset Ŝ (a superset of the true relevant set S₀) followed by a symbolic expression f̂(X_Ŝ). The reduced dataset is then handed to any of 19 contemporary SR methods unchanged, so no modifications to the SR algorithms themselves are required. The symbolic solution criterion requires the recovered expression to match the ground truth up to an additive or multiplicative constant, and the pre-screening must retain all relevant features while keeping the false positive rate as low as feasible.

## Method

PAN+SR integrates a novel BART-based nonparametric variable selection step (PAN) before expression synthesis. The method fits K=20 independent BART models, computes the variable inclusion proportion (VIP) ranking of each feature in every run, and averages these rankings across runs. Relevant features are expected to cluster at low average rankings (mean ≈ (1+p₀)/2) while irrelevant features cluster at high rankings (mean ≈ (p₀+1+p)/2); agglomerative hierarchical clustering with Euclidean distance and average linkage on the average rankings then separates these two clusters without requiring knowledge of the sparsity level p₀ or any tunable threshold. Features in the low-mean cluster are retained and passed to the SR module. This ranking-based clustering approach is tuning-free, avoids model misspecification inherent in parametric methods, and achieves near-100% true positive rate across sample sizes and signal-to-noise ratios, consistently outperforming BART permutation tests and random forest in both TPR and downstream SR accuracy.

## Results

On 35 high-dimensional PMLB black-box datasets, PAN+SR improves test-set R² for 18 out of 19 SR methods, with the largest gains in lower-performing methods (BSR, AIFeynman, ITEA). On 100 extended Feynman ground-truth problems (n=1000, SNR=∞, p=51p₀), PAN+SR improves both R² and symbolic solution rate for all 19 methods; uDSR's solution rate nearly doubles from 36.6% to 71.8%, and AIFeynman goes from complete failure (out-of-memory) to 2nd overall in solution rate. The PAN pre-screening overhead is minimal (~74 s on black-box, ~325 s on ground-truth), while delivering up to 5–6× speedups in downstream SR (e.g., AIFeynman: 71,250 s → 13,997 s; uDSR: 9,573 s → 1,596 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PAN+SR is evaluated against 19 contemporary SR methods (gplearn, AFP, AFP_FE, EHC, EPLEX, FEAT, FFX, GP-GOMEA, ITEA, MRGP, Operon, PySR, SBP-GP, BSR, DSR, uDSR, DySymNet, TPSR, AIFeynman 2.0) on both black-box and ground-truth problems. On the 35 PMLB black-box datasets, PAN+SR improves R² for 18/19 methods; the one exception is negligible. On the 100 extended Feynman ground-truth problems, all 19 methods benefit, with the most dramatic gains in AIFeynman (from OOM failure to 2nd in solution rate) and uDSR (solution rate 36.6%→71.8%, ~6× speedup). For the variable-selection component, the proposed VIP Rank method outperforms BART-G.SE, BART-Local, BART-G.MAX, and Random Forest (PySR's built-in) in TPR across all n and SNR settings, achieving near-100% TPR where G.SE drops below 95% under noise. In the head-to-head R² comparison (Table 3), VIP+Operon consistently beats GSE+Operon and standalone Operon by 2–5 R² points at every setting, with the gap widening at lower SNR (e.g., 0.892 vs 0.860 vs 0.846 at n=500, SNR=20). The method also reduces model complexity in several cases, improving interpretability alongside accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the BART framework (Chipman et al., 2010) for nonparametric variable selection and the PAN strategy (Ye et al., 2024) for ab initio feature screening, but departs from the established BART-G.SE permutation-test approach (Bleich et al., 2014) by replacing raw VIP thresholds with a ranking-based clustering criterion. The key conceptual shift is that SR pre-screening demands near-zero false negatives rather than controlled false discovery rates, motivating a fundamentally different selection philosophy. The paper also extends the SRBench platform (La Cava et al., 2021) with high-dimensional ground-truth problems, filling a gap where prior benchmarks contained at most 9 features.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PAN+SR provides a practical, plug-in pre-screening step that enables 19 existing SR methods to handle high-dimensional inputs (p up to 459) without algorithmic modification, addressing a scalability bottleneck that has limited SR adoption in modern scientific applications. A sobering open question is the sharp performance cliff at low SNR: solution rates for most methods drop to 0% at SNR=10, and the authors note this limitation persists even when only relevant features are provided, suggesting the bottleneck lies in the SR algorithms themselves rather than variable selection. Future work on iterative variable selection and noise-robust SR methods is needed to close this gap.
