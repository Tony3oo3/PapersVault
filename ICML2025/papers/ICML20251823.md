## Problem

Generative models for limit order book (LOB) data lack a standardized, quantitative evaluation paradigm; prior work relied on qualitative comparisons of stylized facts, making rigorous model comparison infeasible. Additionally, cross-entropy (the standard pretraining metric for autoregressive models) fails to capture the 'autoregressive trap,' where small per-token errors accumulate during open-loop sampling and cause distributional derailment over long sequences.

## Input / Output

The benchmark consumes real and generated LOB data in LOBSTER format (CSV files), with files separated by real data, generated data, and conditioning data. It produces a multi-dimensional scorecard: unconditional and conditional L1/Wasserstein-1 distances across ~12 scoring functions (spread, order book imbalance, inter-arrival times, volumes, OFI, etc.), error-divergence curves conditional on prediction horizon, market impact response function scores (ΔR) for six event types, a discriminator ROC score, and downstream mid-price prediction F1-scores. All scores are aggregated via mean, median, and inter-quartile mean with bootstrapped 99% confidence intervals.

## Method

The framework maps high-dimensional LOB sequences to 1D score distributions via pluggable aggregator functions Φ, bins them using the Freedman-Diaconis rule, and compares real vs. generated distributions via L1 (total variation) and Wasserstein-1 (earth mover's) distances. Conditional evaluation bins a second aggregator Φ₂ into 10 deciles and computes weighted average distributional distances across those buckets, enabling analysis of e.g. spread conditioned on time-of-day or error accumulation conditioned on prediction step. Market impact is quantified through Eisler-style response functions R_π(l) for six event types (MO/LO/CA × mid-price-affecting or not), scored by mean absolute difference ΔR. A Conv1D+attention discriminator trained on order-book state changes provides an adversarial worst-case scoring function. The benchmark is applied to five models—LOBS5 (35M-param S5 state-space), Cont et al. (2010) parametric baseline, Coletta CGAN, RWKV4, and RWKV6 (170M-param)—on GOOG and INTC data, revealing LOBS5 as state-of-the-art while identifying common failure modes like error snowballing and discriminator-detectable artifacts.

## Results

The LOBS5 autoregressive state-space model (35M parameters) achieves the lowest overall distributional error scores across all evaluated metrics on both GOOG and INTC, outperforming the Cont et al. (2010) parametric baseline, the Coletta CGAN, and RWKV4/6 models. On the market impact response function metric, LOBS5 achieves ΔR = 2.45 (mean L1 distance between real and generated tick-normalized mid-price response curves), compared to ΔR = 126 for RWKV-6. A trained discriminator network achieves an ROC of 0.83 on LOBS5-generated GOOG data, while the baseline model's generated data is nearly perfectly separable (ROC ≈ 1). LOBS5 message cross-entropy after 100 training epochs is 17.80 for GOOG and 19.14 for INTC.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Cont et al. (2010) parametric baseline, LOBS5 wins on nearly all distributional scores (L1 and Wasserstein-1), particularly on time and volume metrics where the baseline performs poorly, though the baseline does reasonably on discrete LOB depth and level scores. LOBS5 also decisively outperforms the baseline on impact response functions, which the baseline cannot faithfully reproduce. Against the Coletta CGAN, LOBS5 achieves lower overall scores; Coletta beats the baseline on Wasserstein but not L1, and its generated data markedly degrades downstream mid-price prediction F1-scores. Against RWKV4 and RWKV6 (170M parameters each), LOBS5 (35M parameters) achieves substantially lower distributional errors and far slower error divergence with prediction horizon; RWKV models show the fastest divergence across most scores and produce volatile data with incorrect order levels and mismatched book volumes. Notably, LOBS5 achieves these results with roughly 5× fewer parameters than the RWKV models, and the RWKV models' impact response curves diverge so rapidly (ΔR = 126 for RWKV-6 vs. 2.45 for LOBS5) that they cannot be meaningfully compared on that metric.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the autoregressive LOB generation paradigm introduced by Nagy et al. (2023) (LOBS5) and the parametric stochastic baseline of Cont et al. (2010), extending the evaluation of such models from individual stylized-fact checks (Vyetrenko et al., 2021; Paulin, 2019) and qualitative comparisons to a unified, quantitative distributional framework. It departs from the standard cross-entropy evaluation used for autoregressive models by explicitly targeting the sampling regime, motivated by the 'autoregressive trap' where small per-token errors accumulate into distributional derailment over long unrolls. The conditional evaluation design (scoring distributions binned by a conditioning variable) generalizes the conditional generation evaluation of Coletta et al. (2023) into a systematic, weighted multi-bucket protocol.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LOB-Bench provides the first comprehensive, open-source distributional benchmark for message-level LOB generative models, filling a gap where prior evaluation relied on ad-hoc qualitative comparisons of stylized facts. The identification of model derailment (error accumulation over unroll steps) as a prevalent failure mode gives the community a concrete diagnostic to target, and the discriminator-based adversarial score sets a high bar that current models (even the best, LOBS5, at ROC 0.83) have not yet met. Open questions include whether generated data can eventually aid downstream prediction tasks (currently it hurts performance, especially for weaker models), whether the framework transfers cleanly to other high-dimensional financial time series, and whether the square-root impact law can be quantitatively validated at scale using generative counterfactuals.
