## Problem

Adversarial training produces markedly different robustness-generalization trade-offs across architectures—vision models gain robustness at the cost of generalization, encoder-based LLMs show limited robustness gains with preserved generalization, and decoder-based LLMs benefit from multi-layer perturbations—yet no prior work has systematically explained why these trends differ. Additionally, the high computational cost of multi-step PGD in standard AT limits its practical deployment, especially for encoder-based models that remain critical in ML pipelines.

## Input / Output

SMAAT operates on the same inputs as standard AT (text sequences for enc-LLMs, RGB images for vision models, or text queries with document corpora for RAG) but generates adversarial perturbations in the latent space of an intermediate layer rather than in input space. For enc-LLMs, the perturbation is applied to the [CLS] embedding at the last layer before the classifier head, meaning the PGD loop only backpropagates through the classification head rather than the full transformer stack. Outputs remain standard: class labels for classification, binary harmful/safe labels for safety filtering, and ranked passage lists for retrieval.

## Method

SMAAT (Scalable Manifold Aware Adversarial Training) leverages the manifold conjecture by identifying the layer with the lowest intrinsic dimensionality (ID) via twoNN estimation and applying P-step PGD adversarial perturbations at that layer instead of the input layer. The key insight is that lower ID at a layer means a higher proportion of generated adversarial examples fall off the data manifold, which by the manifold conjecture yields greater robustness gains. For encoder-based LLMs (BERT, RoBERTa), the ID monotonically decreases across layers, so the optimal perturbation layer is consistently the last layer, reducing the PGD backpropagation chain to just the classifier head and cutting GPU time by 25–33%. The algorithm freezes all layers before l*, computes a forward pass to obtain the intermediate representation, then iteratively updates the perturbation δ_{l*} via sign-gradient PGD within an ε-ball before updating only the parameters from layer l* onward. This avoids full-network backward passes during the inner maximization while empirically producing higher robustness than input-space AT across sentiment classification, safety filtering, and RAG retrieval tasks.

## Results

On sentiment and topic classification (AGNEWS, IMDB, YELP), SMAAT achieves average robust accuracy (AR) of 73.5%, 67.4%, and 75.9% for BERT, and 76.9%, 72.9%, and 82.6% for RoBERTa, while maintaining clean accuracy (CA) within 0.5% of standard training. For safety filtering against GCG attacks, SMAAT achieves 97–100% robust accuracy on AdvBench (vs. 0% for standard and FreeLB++). In RAG retrieval on Natural Questions, SMAAT achieves 99.5% robust recall@10 under N=10 poisoning (vs. 46.0% standard, 75.2% FreeLB++). Training time is 5.2 min/epoch for SMAAT vs. 15.6 min/epoch for FreeLB++ (~3× speedup).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SMAAT outperforms all five baselines on average robust accuracy across every dataset and model. Against the closest competitor TMD (manifold-based projection), SMAAT gains 15.1 points on BERT/AGNEWS (73.5 vs. 58.4), 28.5 on BERT/IMDB (67.4 vs. 38.9), and 40.5 on BERT/YELP (75.9 vs. 35.4). FreeLB++ (embedding-space AT with gradient accumulation) achieves the best clean accuracy in 4 of 6 settings (e.g., 95.1 on BERT/AGNEWS vs. SMAAT's 94.6), consistent with its first-layer perturbations producing more on-manifold AEs, but its robustness lags SMAAT by 12–57 points. SAFER (certified randomized smoothing) and ASCC (input-space convex combination) are both dominated by SMAAT on both robustness and generalization. RSMI reports strong PWWS-specific robustness (e.g., 81.9 on RoBERTa/AGNEWS) but does not report results for TextFooler or BERT-Attack, limiting direct comparison. On runtime, SMAAT (5.2 min/epoch) is ~3× faster than FreeLB++ (15.6) and ~5× faster than ASCC (25.7), while matching standard training speed (5.1 min/epoch).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper synthesizes the manifold conjecture (Ethayarajh 2019; Shamir et al. 2021; Gilmer et al. 2018) with layer-wise intrinsic dimensionality (ID) estimation to explain why adversarial training produces different robustness-generalization tradeoffs across vision models, encoder-based LLMs, and decoder-based LLMs. It builds directly on Latent Adversarial Training (Sheshadri et al. 2024; Casper et al. 2024), which showed that perturbing intermediate layers of dec-LLMs improves robustness, and on YOPO (Zhang et al. 2019a) and TMD (Minh & Luu 2022), which implicitly identified critical layers (first for vision, last for enc-LLMs) without a unifying principle. The key departure is using twoNN-based ID as an explicit, measurable criterion for selecting which layer to perturb, rather than relying on architecture-specific heuristics or full-network PGD.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ID-based explanation provides the first principled account of why AT reduces generalization in vision models but preserves or improves it in enc-LLMs, resolving a long-standing puzzle in the adversarial robustness literature. Practically, SMAAT's last-layer perturbation strategy for enc-LLMs cuts GPU time by 25–33% while delivering 6–29% robustness gains over strong baselines, making adversarial training more deployable for safety filters and RAG retrievers. Open questions include whether the relaxed manifold conjecture (extending on/off-manifold reasoning to intermediate layers) can be formalized theoretically, whether joint control of the on/off-manifold ratio via multi-layer perturbation can simultaneously optimize robustness and generalization, and whether the ID trends generalize to newer architectures (e.g., mixture-of-experts, state-space models).
