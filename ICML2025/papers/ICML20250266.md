## Problem

Dominant image-to-image retrieval models (CNNs, ViTs) are biased toward low-level visual features like color rather than semantic content, and existing scene graph retrieval approaches rely on supervised GNNs trained with caption-based similarity labels from SBERT models, which exhibit significant disagreement (≥40% in top-1 rankings) across different SBERT variants. This paper addresses both gaps by proposing an unsupervised scene graph retrieval framework and advocating for Graph Edit Distance as a deterministic, model-independent ground-truth evaluation metric.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250266/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a scene graph represented as a 768-dimensional feature matrix (Sentence-Transformer embeddings over 189 object and predicate classes) and a binary adjacency matrix as input, and produces a ranked list of candidate scene graphs via cosine similarity between sum-pooled latent node embeddings. Retrieval operates in an inductive setting where both query and candidate test graphs are unseen during training, and for unannotated datasets (e.g., Flickr30K), scene graphs are generated on-the-fly via PSGTR and captions via BLIP-Captioner.

## Method

SCENIR is an unsupervised Graph Autoencoder with three key architectural choices: (1) a split encoder with two fully independent 3-layer GNN branches (GNN_μ and GNN_σ) rather than the shared-layer design of VGAE, allowing mean and variance parameters to learn structural and uncertainty aspects separately; (2) a dual-branch 2-layer MLP decoder that reconstructs both the adjacency matrix (Edge Decoder) and node feature matrix (Feature Decoder) instead of the traditional parameter-free inner-product decoder, capturing richer semantic relations; and (3) an adversarial discriminator (2-layer MLP) that regularizes the latent space toward a Gaussian prior. Training is entirely unsupervised with a combined loss of feature/edge reconstruction, adversarial, and KL-divergence terms, requiring no similarity labels or caption annotations. At inference, sum-pooling over the GNN_μ latent embeddings yields a global graph representation, and cosine similarity ranks candidates—achieving SotA retrieval while running in ~8 minutes versus ~50 minutes for supervised baselines.

## Results

On the PSG test set (1K queries, 999 candidates each), SCENIR-GIN achieves NDCG@1 of 31.39, NDCG@3 of 28.77, MAP@3 of 54.16, and MRR of 59.01, outperforming the best supervised GNN (IRSGS-GIN with RoBERTa captions: NDCG@1 29.64, MRR 59.16) and all Vision/VL models by 15–18 points in NDCG. On the unannotated Flickr30K setting, SCENIR maintains its lead with NDCG@3 of 22.75 and MRR of 56.20. In the counterfactual retrieval task, SCENIR achieves NDCG(B)@1 of 9.7 and MRR(B) of 14.99, surpassing the dedicated GC framework (7.0 / 10.67) in an inductive setting. Total pipeline runtime is ~8 minutes versus ~50 min (IRSGS) and ~3 hr (GC).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SCENIR-GIN (unsupervised) outperforms the strongest supervised GNN, IRSGS-GIN with RoBERTa captions, by 1.75 points in NDCG@1 (31.39 vs. 29.64) and 0.81 in NDCG@3, while matching its MRR (59.01 vs. 59.16) — all without any similarity labels or caption embeddings. Against Vision models (best: Efficient-ViT at NDCG@1 13.47) and VL models (best: ALBEF at 15.77), SCENIR-GIN leads by roughly 18 and 16 NDCG@1 points respectively, confirming that pixel-level and caption-level features are substantially less informative than explicit scene graph structure for image-to-image retrieval. Among unsupervised GAEs, SCENIR-GIN improves over VGAE-GIN by 4.12 NDCG@1 and over ARVGA-GIN by 6.30, attributable to its split encoder, dual-branch MLP decoder, and adversarial regularization. In the counterfactual retrieval task (Table 4), SCENIR beats the purpose-built GC framework (Dimitriou et al., 2024) by 2.7 NDCG(B)@1 and 4.3 MRR(B) points in a more challenging inductive setting. On unannotated Flickr30K images (Table 3), SCENIR retains its advantage over IRSGS (22.75 vs. 21.02 NDCG@3) and VGAE (17.92), demonstrating generalizability when scene graphs must be auto-generated. Computationally, SCENIR's linear-complexity pipeline (~8 min total) is ~6× faster than IRSGS (~50 min) and ~22× faster than GC (~3 hr), since it avoids quadratic pre-computation of pairwise similarity labels.

## Relation to Prior Work

SCENIR synthesizes three distinct GAE lineages—VGAE's variational encoder-decoder, ARVGA's adversarial latent regularization, and GFA's node-feature reconstruction—into a unified architecture with split GNN encoders and parallel MLP decoders. It departs sharply from the dominant supervised paradigm in scene graph retrieval (IRSGS, Maheshwari et al., Wang et al. 2023b), which relies on SBERT-embedded caption similarities as training labels, by eliminating all external supervision in favor of self-supervised reconstruction. The paper also repositions the evaluation landscape by substituting the inconsistent caption-matching ground truth with approximate Graph Edit Distance, a shift not previously made in image-to-image retrieval benchmarks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that an unsupervised GAE can match or exceed supervised GNNs in scene graph retrieval while running ~6× faster, SCENIR lowers the practical barrier to deploying semantic-aware retrieval on unannotated data (validated via PSGTR + BLIP on Flickr30K). The ≥40% top-1 disagreement among SBERT models for caption similarity is a concrete, reproducible argument that caption-based supervision injects systematic noise into GNN training—a finding that should prompt re-examination of existing supervised scene graph pipelines. Open questions include sensitivity to scene graph generation quality at scale, whether the GED-based evaluation generalizes beyond the PSG/Visual Genome annotation style, and whether the unsupervised advantage persists when the supervised baseline is given truly reliable (non-caption) labels.
