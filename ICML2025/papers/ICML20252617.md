## Problem

Existing self-supervised graph learning methods, particularly graph contrastive learning, implicitly treat each node as its own equivalence class, ignoring the rich structural (automorphic) and attribute-based equivalences that are prevalent in real-world graphs. This paper identifies that GCL is a degenerate form of equivalence constraint and asks how to explicitly enforce node equivalence relations during representation learning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252617/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a graph's adjacency matrix and node feature matrix X ∈ R^{n×d}, and additionally computes two equivalence-class partitions: one from PageRank scores (approximating automorphic orbits in O(m) time) and one from Euclidean-distance thresholding on node attributes. The output is a set of node embeddings z_v ∈ R^q (and pooled graph embeddings for graph-level tasks) that are constrained to be similar within fused equivalence classes and dissimilar across them, evaluated via linear probes for node/graph classification.

## Method

GALE is a dual-branch self-supervised framework: a GNN encoder produces node representations while a separate branch induces fused equivalence-class partitions by intersecting automorphic orbits (approximated via PageRank scores, leveraging the lemma that automorphically equivalent nodes share PageRank) with attribute-similarity groups (nodes within Euclidean distance ε). The training objective is an equivalence loss with two terms — an intra-class term maximizing inner-product similarity among nodes in the same fused class, and an inter-class term minimizing similarity across different classes. The paper further provides theoretical analysis showing that MPNNs naturally preserve intra-class similarity but suffer over-smoothing that erodes inter-class separation, that most positional encodings in graph transformers fail to respect automorphic equivalence, and that standard graph contrastive learning corresponds to the trivial case where every equivalence class is a singleton.

## Results

On node classification (primary task), GALE achieves the best unsupervised accuracy across all 8 benchmarks, reaching 85.36% on Cora, 85.06% on PubMed, and 95.91% on Coauthor-Physics. GALE surpasses the strongest unsupervised baseline CCA-SSG by 1.19% on Cora, 3.15% on PubMed, and 4.48% on Wiki-CS. Notably, GALE also exceeds the supervised GCN baseline on 7 out of 8 datasets (e.g., +5.9% on PubMed, +4.1% on Cora), though it trails GCN on Wiki-CS (82.15% vs. 93.02%). The approximate variant GALE-APR (using PageRank instead of exact automorphisms) remains highly competitive, within 0.5% of GALE on most datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GALE outperforms all 13 unsupervised baselines (MVGRL, GRACE, GCA, BGRL, SUGRL, CCA-SSG, DGI, GMI, VGAE, DeepWalk, Node2Vec, etc.) on every one of the 8 node-classification datasets, with the largest margins on PubMed (+3.15% over SUGRL) and Wiki-CS (+4.48% over CCA-SSG). Against the supervised GCN, GALE wins on 7/8 datasets (e.g., +5.90% on PubMed, +4.07% on Cora, +3.85% on CiteSeer) but falls short on Wiki-CS (82.15% vs. 93.02%), likely due to the dense citation structure where supervised signal is highly informative. On graph-level classification (Table 5), GALE achieves the best unsupervised accuracy on PROTEINS (81.15%), MUTAG (91.12%), COLLAB (75.95%), and IMDB-B (77.66%), surpassing the strongest GCL baselines (Sega, SimGRACE, RGCL) by 1–5% on these datasets. The approximate variant GALE-APR, which replaces exact Nauty automorphism computation with O(m) PageRank, stays within ~0.5% of GALE on most datasets, confirming that the linear-time approximation preserves most of the equivalence signal. Ablation results (Table 7) show that removing either automorphic or attribute equivalence degrades performance by 1–10% (e.g., CiteSeer drops from 74.58% to 64.72% without feature equivalence), validating the necessity of the fused partition.

## Relation to Prior Work

GALE builds directly on the PageRank–automorphism relationship established by Ghorbani et al. (2021), using it as the theoretical justification for approximating NP-hard exact automorphism detection with a linear-time PageRank-based partition. Conceptually, the paper positions itself as a generalization of graph contrastive learning (GCL), arguing that GCL's contrastive loss is a degenerate equivalence constraint where each node forms its own singleton class. It also draws on classical algebraic graph theory (automorphism groups, orbits) and recent work on AE-aware GNNs (GRAPE, Autobahn), but departs from those by framing equivalence as a self-supervised regularization signal rather than an architectural inductive bias, and by jointly incorporating node attributes alongside structural symmetry.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The reframing of GCL as a trivial-equivalence special case provides a unifying lens that could guide future self-supervised graph learning beyond pairwise node alignment. The empirical finding that most common positional encodings (LapPE, SignNet) substantially violate automorphic equivalence (VI > 7) while RWSE and HKdiagSE are far better is a useful diagnostic for Graph Transformer practitioners. However, the method is limited to static, homogeneous graphs, and the PageRank approximation, while near-perfect on tested benchmarks, may degrade on graphs with many near-automorphic but non-automorphic nodes. Extending the framework to dynamic or heterogeneous graphs remains an open direction.
