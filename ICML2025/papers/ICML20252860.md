## Problem

Existing 3DGS compression methods fall into two camps: generative approaches that embed primitives into neural networks (achieving high compression ratios but with encoding times exceeding 1 minute and non-standardized formats), and traditional point cloud encoders applied directly to vanilla 3DGS (fast and standardized but producing bitstreams an order of magnitude too large). No prior work bridges these two paradigms to simultaneously achieve compact representation, standard output, and sub-second coding latency.

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes multi-view training images to first generate an explicit 3DGS, then compresses the resulting primitive data (integer xyz positions, quantized color/rotation latent features, opacity, and scaling) into a standard GPCC bitstream. The output is split into an octree-coded geometry stream for positions and a RAHT-coded attribute stream for all other channels, with a few-kB metadata payload (de-quantization coefficients and two small MLP decoder weights) transmitted alongside. A key constraint is that all primitive positions must be unique integers, which the generation step enforces before the point cloud encoder is invoked.

## Method

HybridGS is a two-stage framework. In the first stage, a dual-channel sparse representation is learned during 3DGS training: color and rotation attributes are projected into low-dimensional latent features (k_c and k_r channels) and reconstructed by a single-hidden-layer MLP decoder (a generalized, trainable PCA), while a Robust Quantizer (RQ) is integrated into the rendering loop so that quantization distortion is minimized during generation rather than applied as post-processing. For positions, a Learnable Quantizer-based Method (LQM) decomposes integer coordinates into inner products of a fixed basis vector and a trainable coding vector with entries in {-1,0,1}, producing unique integer positions that can be rendered directly without de-quantization. Progressive pruning and adaptive bit-depth reduction provide two interpretable rate-control knobs. In the second stage, the explicit compact 3DGS is fed to GPCC v23 in lossless mode—positions via octree, attributes via RAHT—yielding a standard, decoder-compatible bitstream with encoding and decoding times under 2 seconds.

## Results

On five scenes across four datasets, HybridGS (k_c=3, k_r=2) achieves PSNR of 29.89/21.26/24.08/29.52/39.25 dB at High Rate (12.15/4.19/22.88/6.43/1.04 MB) and 29.49/20.96/23.53/29.23/37.65 dB at Low Rate (5.88/2.03/11.01/3.14/0.51 MB) for playroom/train/bicycle/room/dance respectively, representing a 45–80× size reduction over vanilla 3DGS. Encoding and decoding times are 0.26–1.67 s, compared to 7.82–132 s for SOTA baselines. Increasing latent dimensionality to k_c=6 improves PSNR by ~0.2–0.5 dB at the cost of ~30% larger bitstreams.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against generative compression methods, HybridGS achieves comparable PSNR at similar or smaller sizes: on 'playroom', HybridGS HR matches C3DGS at 29.89 dB while using 12.15 MB vs. 21.66 MB, and on 'dance' it outperforms CompGS(MM) across all rate points (e.g., 39.25 dB at 1.04 MB vs. 37.43 dB at 2.84 MB). However, HAC still achieves higher PSNR at very low bitrates (e.g., 30.84 dB at 6.86 MB on 'playroom' vs. HybridGS's 29.89 dB at 12.15 MB). Against traditional compression methods (GGSC, HGSC), HybridGS produces dramatically smaller bitstreams at equal or better quality: on 'playroom', CompGS(MM) achieves 28.98 dB at 4.89 MB while HGSC requires 96.82 MB for 28.83 dB, and HybridGS LR reaches 29.49 dB at only 5.88 MB. The most striking advantage is coding speed: HybridGS encodes/decodes in 0.26–1.67 s versus 7.82–132 s for HAC, CompGS(MM), and HGSC, making it the only method approaching real-time operation. HybridGS is better than LightGaussian on all scenes except 'room', where LightGaussian's aggressive pruning (p=0.9) yields slightly higher PSNR at comparable size.

## Relation to Prior Work

HybridGS sits at the intersection of two established 3DGS compression paradigms: generative compression (Scaffold-GS, HAC, CompGS, LightGaussian) that embeds primitives into neural networks for compact representation, and traditional compression (GGSC, HGSC) that applies standard point cloud encoders to explicit data. The paper departs from both by using generative techniques (dual-channel sparse representation with learnable latent features, Robust Quantizer, and progressive pruning) only to produce a compact *explicit* 3DGS file, which is then fed into a canonical GPCC encoder for standard bitstream output. The Learnable Quantizer-based Method (LQM) for primitive position is a novel contribution that generates integer coordinates directly during training, eliminating the need for de-quantization before rendering and satisfying the uniqueness constraint required by learning-based point cloud encoders like SparseConv.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 50–100× reduction in encoding/decoding time (1–2 s vs. 36–132 s for SOTA generative methods) while maintaining comparable PSNR makes 3DGS compression viable for real-time streaming and practical deployment, addressing a key barrier identified by MPEG WG4/WG7. The full compatibility with standard point cloud encoders means future improvements in GPCC or AI-PCC can be directly inherited without retraining. However, the optimal compression efficiency remains lower than end-to-end RD-optimized generative methods, and open questions include adaptive selection of latent feature dimension and BD, smooth latent dimension reduction without feature-space collapse, and whether 3D-space loss functions (e.g., Chamfer distance) could further improve point cloud encoder efficiency on 3DGS's distinctive geometry.
