## Problem

The unified framework of Negahban et al. (2009) for high-dimensional M-estimators with decomposable regularizers requires convexity of the loss function to guarantee that estimation errors lie in a restricted cone and to derive convergence rates. However, many practically important high-dimensional models—corrected linear regression with noisy or missing data, mixture models—have inherently nonconvex loss functions, leaving a gap in the theoretical guarantees. This paper asks whether the restricted-set containment and convergence rate results survive when the convexity assumption is dropped.

## Input / Output

The framework takes n i.i.d. samples, a differentiable (possibly nonconvex) loss function L_n, a decomposable norm-based regularizer Φ (e.g., ℓ₁, group Lasso, nuclear norm), and a regularization parameter λ_n. Crucially, the results apply to any stationary point θ̂ satisfying the first-order necessary conditions rather than requiring a global optimum, making the guarantees directly relevant to local optima produced by practical algorithms. The output is an estimation error bound ‖θ̂ − θ*‖² ≤ ε_n²(M̄, M^⊥) whose rate depends on the sparsity level s, sub-Gaussian parameters, and sample size n.

## Method

The core technical contribution replaces the convexity assumption with a weaker restricted strong convexity (RSC) condition on the gradient difference: ⟨∇L_n(θ*+Δ) − ∇L_n(θ*), Δ⟩ ≥ κ/2‖Δ‖² − τ_n²Φ²(Δ) on a ball, which does not require the loss itself to be convex. Theorem 3.1 shows that the error cone containment from Wainwright (2019) holds for any stationary point using only decomposability of the regularizer and a dual norm bound, with no convexity needed. Theorem 3.3 then combines this cone containment with the weaker RSC condition to recover the same convergence rate ‖θ̂ − θ*‖² ≤ ε_n²(M̄, M^⊥) for any stationary point, by bounding the regularizer contribution via Hölder's inequality and solving a resulting quadratic inequality in ‖Δ‖. The framework is instantiated for two nonconvex models: corrected linear regression (where the estimated covariance Γ̂ is not positive semidefinite) and an ℓ₁-penalized Lasso with a joint variance parameter σ (where log(σ) introduces nonconvexity), in both cases proving that the RSC condition and dual norm bound hold with high probability under sub-Gaussian assumptions.

## Results

The paper establishes two main theoretical guarantees for nonconvex M-estimators with decomposable regularizers. Theorem 3.1 shows that for any stationary point θ̂, the error Δ̂ = θ̂ − θ* lies in the restricted cone C_{θ*}(M, Ṁ⊥) defined by Φ(Δ̂_{Ṁ⊥}) ≤ 3Φ(Δ̂_{Ṁ}) + 4Φ(θ*_{M⊥}), extending the convex-case result of Wainwright (2019). Theorem 3.3 recovers the convergence rate ||θ̂ − θ*||² ≤ ε_n²(Ṁ, M⊥) = 9(λ_n²/κ²)Ψ²(Ṁ) + (8/κ){λ_n Φ(θ*_{M⊥}) + 16τ_n² Φ²(θ*_{M⊥})} under a weaker RSC condition (10) that does not require convexity. Applied to corrected linear regression (Theorem 4.2), the bound is ||θ̂ − θ*||₂ ≤ c₀B√s(σ_x² + σ_zσ_ε + σ_wσ_x + σ_z² + ||Σ_x||₂)√(log d) / (λ_min(Σ_x)√n), and for the ℓ₁-penalized Lasso with variance estimation (Theorem 4.6), ||(θ̂−θ*, σ̂−σ*)||₂ ≤ c₀B√s(σ_x² + σ_xσ* + ||Σ_x||₂)√(log d) / (λ_min(Σ_x)√n), both holding with probability at least 1 − O(exp(−c log d)).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no experimental baselines or empirical comparisons. The primary comparison is conceptual: the paper extends the convex framework of Negahban et al. (2009) and Wainwright (2019) to nonconvex loss functions while retaining decomposable regularizers. The key distinction from Loh & Wainwright (2015) is that their nonconvex theory targets nonconvex regularizers (SCAD, MCP) and uses ℓ₁/ℓ₂ norms for error measurement, whereas this paper handles decomposable regularizers (ℓ₁, group Lasso, nuclear norm) under arbitrary norms. For the corrected linear regression application, the resulting bound (Theorem 4.2) matches the rate previously established by Loh & Wainwright (2012, Theorem 1) and Loh & Wainwright (2015, Corollary 1), confirming that the nonconvex loss does not degrade the convergence rate. The ℓ₁-penalized Lasso with joint variance estimation (Theorem 4.6) is a new application not covered by prior work, extending the theory to the nonconvex log(σ) + ||y−Xθ||²/(2n) loss.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the convex-loss framework of Negahban et al. (2009) and Wainwright (2019) for regularized M-estimators with decomposable regularizers to the nonconvex-loss regime. The closest prior work, Loh & Wainwright (2015), addresses nonconvexity but through a different lens: nonconvex *regularizers* (SCAD, MCP) rather than nonconvex *loss functions* paired with decomposable regularizers, making their results inapplicable to the setting studied here. The paper's key technical move is showing that the restricted-set confinement (Proposition 2.2) and convergence-rate guarantees (Theorem 2.7) from the convex theory carry over to any stationary point under a weaker RSC condition (10) that only requires a second-order inequality on the gradient difference, rather than the full first-order Taylor error bound (4).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result removes a convexity assumption that was treated as foundational in the high-dimensional M-estimation literature, broadening the class of models (mixture models, corrected linear regression with noisy/missing data, variance-estimated Lasso) for which clean estimation-error guarantees are available. The practical impact is that practitioners working with nonconvex losses can now invoke the same restricted-set and rate machinery that was previously limited to convex settings, provided the weaker RSC and dual-norm conditions hold. Open questions include whether the stationary-point guarantee can be tightened to exclude spurious local minima, whether the analysis extends to non-norm regularizers, and whether the two illustrative examples generalize to broader nonconvex families such as deep network losses.
