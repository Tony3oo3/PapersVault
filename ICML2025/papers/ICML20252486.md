## Problem

MAG listing—enumerating all maximal ancestral graphs in a Markov equivalence class—is essential for downstream causal tasks like intervention design, but the only existing non-exhaustive method (MAGLIST) incurs exponential delay by enumerating all circles at a vertex simultaneously. Moreover, it remains unclear which orientation rules are sound and complete for incorporating background knowledge in the presence of latent variables, a long-standing open problem in causal discovery.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252486/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a PAG (partial ancestral graph) with d vertices and m edges as input, where circles denote uncertain edge orientations. It outputs all and only the MAGs consistent with the PAG, with a guaranteed polynomial delay of O(m³d⁴) between consecutive outputs. The key operational detail is that the algorithm transforms one circle at a time (singleton BK) rather than all circles at a vertex simultaneously, which is what enables the polynomial delay guarantee.

## Method

The paper introduces three novel orientation rules (R14–R16) that are proven sound and locally complete for incorporating singleton BK into any PMG compatible with local transformation, where local completeness ensures all identifiable marks at the target vertex are resolved. Building on these rules, MAGLIST-POLY uses two nested recursive loops: an outer loop selects a vertex with remaining circles, and an inner loop enumerates each circle at that vertex one by one as either an arrowhead or tail, applying the full rule set R1–R16 after each single transformation to propagate orientations and prune the search space. This one-circle-at-a-time strategy eliminates the O(2^d) enumeration bottleneck of MAGLIST, yielding the first polynomial-delay MAG listing algorithm. Additionally, two counterexamples demonstrate that even the proposed rules are incomplete for general BK, motivating two further rules (R17–R18) whose soundness is established.

## Results

The proposed MAGLIST-POLY achieves a polynomial delay bound of O(m³d⁴) per MAG output (Theorem 3), in contrast to the exponential delay O(2^d/d) incurred by MAGLIST. In experiments across d ∈ {6,8,10,12,14} vertices and ρ ∈ {0.1,0.15,0.2,0.25,0.3} densities (100 Erdős–Rényi simulations per setting), MAGLIST-POLY consistently outperforms both MAGLIST and BRUTEFORCE in runtime while returning identical sets of MAGs when d < 10. On the real phosphoproteomics dataset (Wang et al., 2017; 7,466 measurements), the maximal entropy criterion supported by MAG listing identifies 98 marks in 2 interventions versus 81 by random selection, demonstrating practical utility for interventional variable selection.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares MAGLIST-POLY against two baselines from Wang et al. (2024a): MAGLIST (recursive enumeration of all circles at a vertex simultaneously) and BRUTEFORCE (exhaustive enumeration of all circle orientations). On correctness, all three methods return identical sets of MAGs when d < 10, confirming that MAGLIST-POLY outputs all and only the MAGs in the MEC. On efficiency, MAGLIST-POLY consistently achieves lower running time than both baselines across all tested (d, ρ) combinations; the advantage grows with d, as MAGLIST's per-vertex enumeration of 2^d local structures leads to exponential delay while MAGLIST-POLY transforms one circle at a time with polynomial overhead. For d ≥ 10, MAGLIST and BRUTEFORCE frequently exceed the 3600-second time limit, whereas MAGLIST-POLY completes more configurations within the same budget. The real-data experiment further shows that the MAG listing capability (enabled by the polynomial-delay guarantee) translates into a 22% improvement in marks identified (98 vs. 81) when using the maximal entropy criterion over random intervention selection.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the MAG listing line initiated by Wang et al. (2024a, MAGLIST), which was the first method to avoid exhaustive search but suffered from exponential delay due to simultaneous enumeration of all circles at a vertex. The key departure is the introduction of singleton background knowledge (BK) and three novel orientation rules (R14–R16) that are sound and locally complete for incorporating such BK, enabling one-circle-at-a-time enumeration with polynomial delay. The rule set builds on Zhang's (2008) foundational R1–R10, Wang et al.'s (2024b) R11–R12, and Venkateswaran & Perkovic's (2024) R13, but the paper demonstrates that even this expanded set is incomplete for general BK, motivating two additional rules (R17–R18).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The first polynomial-delay MAG listing algorithm removes a practical bottleneck for downstream tasks like interventional variable selection via maximal entropy criterion, where all MAGs in an MEC must be enumerated. More broadly, the counterexamples showing incompleteness of all existing orientation rules (including the new ones) for general BK underscore that the long-standing open problem of sound and complete causal identification from observational data plus BK remains unresolved. The paper also highlights that MAG counting (determining the size of an MEC with latent variables) is an open problem with no known efficient algorithm, unlike its DAG counterpart.
