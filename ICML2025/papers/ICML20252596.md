## Problem

Foundation Models encode rich high-level semantic knowledge but lack environment-specific dynamics, while World Models capture physical dynamics but cannot interpret open-ended tasks specified in text or video. This paper asks whether these complementary strengths can be integrated to enable reward-free, multi-task embodied decision-making from visual observations, where prior methods like GenRL rely on rigid step-by-step visual alignment that fails under domain gaps and complex observations.

## Input / Output

The agent receives a reward-free offline dataset of image-action pairs (64×64 for DMC/Kitchen, 128×128 for Minecraft) and a task prompt in the form of free-form text or an 8-frame video (the maximum InternVideo2 can process). It outputs a goal-conditioned policy that produces action sequences conditioned on a single mapped goal state in the world model's latent space. Notably, no ground-truth rewards or task labels are available during training; all supervisory signals are derived from the VLM embedding, the learned WM, and the temporal distance predictor.

## Method

FOUNDER comprises three pretrained components: (1) a DreamerV3-style RSSM world model trained on offline data that preserves both deterministic and stochastic states (unlike GenRL, which drops the deterministic component, limiting it to single-frame visual alignment); (2) a mapping function Q_ψ that grounds VLM representations into WM state space via a variational-inference-like objective combining KL divergence alignment to ground-truth WM states with an autoencoder reconstruction loss, enabling multi-modal prompts to be translated into actionable goal states; and (3) a temporal distance predictor D_θ trained on state pairs from the same trajectory (positive) and different trajectories (negative) to estimate timesteps between states. During behavior learning, the VLM embedding of the task prompt is mapped to a goal state z_g, and a Dreamer-style actor-critic policy is trained in imagination using the negative predicted temporal distance to z_g as the reward, replacing the cosine-similarity reward that causes reward hacking in dynamic tasks.

## Results

On the DMC and Franka Kitchen benchmarks (19 tasks total), FOUNDER achieves an overall normalized score of 0.81, substantially outperforming GenRL (0.60), WM-CLIP (0.57), GenRL-TempD (0.59), and its own ablation FOUNDER w/o TempD (0.52). FOUNDER ranks among the highest in 14 out of 19 tasks, with particularly large gains on dynamic locomotion tasks (e.g., Cheetah Flip: 0.97 vs. −0.04 for GenRL) and cross-domain video tasks (best on 11/12 cross-embodiment tasks). In Minecraft, FOUNDER matches or surpasses the oracle MineCLIP-IQL baseline on 3 of 5 tasks while using only a VLM and offline data without internet-scale pre-training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GenRL (the closest prior method), FOUNDER achieves a 0.81 vs. 0.60 overall score, with dramatic gains on dynamic tasks (Cheetah Flip: 0.97 vs. −0.04; Cheetah Run: 0.81 vs. 0.68) and cross-embodiment video tasks (best on 11/12 vs. GenRL's complete failure on Cheetah-based domains). WM-CLIP, which aligns in VLM space rather than WM state space, scores 0.57 overall and is outperformed by FOUNDER on 14/19 tasks, confirming the importance of grounding in the WM state space. Model-free baselines TD3 and HILP (Table 5) are consistently outperformed by FOUNDER across all 8 evaluated Cheetah/Kitchen tasks, with FOUNDER reaching 0.97 on Cheetah Flip where TD3 scores −0.03 and HILP scores 0.20. In Minecraft, FOUNDER matches or surpasses the oracle MineCLIP-IQL (which uses internet-scale pre-trained MineCLIP rewards) on 3 of 5 tasks, while consistently exceeding GenRL's upper confidence interval on the three tasks where it wins. The learned reward function also shows the best consistency with ground-truth rewards (Corr=0.54, Regret=0.07, Precision=1.0) compared to all baselines in Table 2.

## Relation to Prior Work

FOUNDER builds directly on the DreamerV3 world model architecture (RSSM backbone, actor-critic policy learning) and the temporal distance prediction concept from LEXA, while departing fundamentally from GenRL, the closest prior method. Where GenRL performs step-by-step seq2seq visual alignment between FM representations and WM states (treating the WM encoder as a visual tokenizer without deterministic states), FOUNDER learns a single variational mapping from FM embeddings to full WM states (preserving both deterministic and stochastic components) and operates within a goal-conditioned RL paradigm rather than trajectory matching. The key conceptual move is reframing FM-generated task embeddings as 'external observations' in a POMDP sense, to be decoded into physical states within the learned environment simulator.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FOUNDER provides a general, reward-free framework for open-ended embodied task solving that bridges high-level semantic understanding (VLMs) with low-level dynamics (world models), addressing a practical gap where tasks specified in human-intuitive formats (text, video) cannot be directly converted to reward functions. The strong cross-domain generalization results—being the only method to succeed in Cheetah cross-embodiment tasks—suggest the grounding-in-state-space approach captures deeper task semantics than visual alignment. Key limitations include dependence on offline data coverage (unseen prompt elements cannot be grounded), focus on short-horizon tasks without decomposition, and the need for a pre-trained VLM. Open questions include whether FM reasoning capabilities can be integrated for long-horizon task decomposition and whether real-world video can be incorporated into WM learning to reduce the domain gap.
