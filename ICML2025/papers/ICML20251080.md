## Problem

Existing spatio-temporal forecasting models (STGNNs, TSFMs) require large training datasets to learn complex spatial and temporal correlations, making them ineffective in data-scarce scenarios. Moreover, they normalize time series into purely numerical vectors, discarding real-world contextual knowledge such as geographical information, urban contexts, and human behavioral patterns that are critical for accurate multivariate forecasting. While LLMs offer strong few-shot reasoning and contextual understanding, prior LLM-based forecasting methods either fine-tune on numerical inputs alone or use generic task prompts, failing to exploit spatial correlations among channels or the LLM's capacity for domain-aware reasoning.

## Input / Output

The method consumes multivariate time series (19 parking lots at 15-min intervals for Nottingham; 19 electricity clients at hourly intervals for ECL), node-specific textual documents (e.g., parking rates, location, capacity, user reviews), and STGNN-generated numerical prediction tokens. During fine-tuning, prompts are structured with six components: task instruction, node description, node pattern summary, historical input sequence, STGNN prediction token, and ground-truth future token; at inference the future token is omitted. The model outputs exactly T numerical predictions enclosed in square brackets (T=8 for Nottingham, T=12 for ECL), representing context-aware future values that respect real-world constraints such as capacity limits.

## Method

FSTLLM comprises three modules: (1) an LLM-Enhanced Graph Construction module that encodes node-specific textual documents with LLaMA-2-7B, projects embeddings via an FFN, and computes pairwise spatial correlations through a graph attention layer regularized by α-Entmax (α=2.0) to produce a sparse adjacency matrix; (2) an STGNN backbone (GTS with graph diffusion convolution replacing standard matrix multiplication in GRU gates) that performs joint spatio-temporal modeling and outputs numerical prediction tokens; (3) a Domain Knowledge Injection module that fine-tunes LLaMA-2-7B via QLoRA (4-bit quantization, LoRA rank 64, lr 2e-4, 2 epochs) using prompts enriched with domain-specific knowledge, node-level temporal patterns, historical inputs, and the STGNN's initial predictions. The key distinction from prior LLM-based forecasting is the dual use of LLMs—first to construct a semantically meaningful spatial graph from textual context, then to perform human-like reasoning over structured spatio-temporal information and numerical predictions—enabling robust few-shot forecasting without updating the STGNN backbone parameters.

## Results

On the Nottingham parking-lot dataset, FSTLLM achieves the best result in 22 out of 24 horizon–metric evaluations, with an average MAE of 21.0, RMSE of 82.3, and MAPE of 20.7%, outperforming the strongest baseline GTS (MAE 25.9, MAPE 24.7%) by roughly 19% in MAE. On the ECL electricity dataset, FSTLLM attains the best result in 25 out of 36 evaluations, with an average MAE of 2294, RMSE of 4669, and MAPE of 16.2%, reducing MAPE by over 50% relative to GPT4TS (33.8%) and iTransformer (37.9%). At the longest horizon (120 min / 12 h), FSTLLM still leads all baselines, e.g. MAPE 39.09% vs. 49.03% (GTS) on Nottingham and 20.37% vs. 23.80% (GTS) on ECL.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FSTLLM outperforms all 13 baselines on average across both datasets. On Nottingham, it beats the closest STGNN competitor GTS by 19% in MAE (21.0 vs. 25.9) and 16% in MAPE (20.7% vs. 24.7%), and surpasses GraphWaveNet (MAE 26.2) and DCRNN (MAE 27.4) by even wider margins. On ECL, FSTLLM leads GraphWaveNet (MAE 2859) by 20% and GPT4TS (MAE 2964) by 23%, while LLM-based baselines TimeLLM (MAE 5595) and PatchTST (MAE 5808) degrade severely under the few-shot setting. Two adaptive-graph STGNNs (AGCRN, MTGNN) fail to converge entirely due to insufficient training data, underscoring the data-scarcity challenge. In the plug-and-play integration study, wrapping GPT4TS and iTransformer predictions with FSTLLM's Domain Knowledge Injection module reduces their MAE from 27.6→21.8 and 28.4→22.3 respectively, confirming that the framework can augment existing architectures without retraining them.

## Relation to Prior Work

FSTLLM builds directly on the GTS (Shang et al., 2021) STGNN backbone, replacing standard GRU matrix multiplications with graph diffusion convolution, while departing from prior LLM-based time series methods (GPT4TS, Time-LLM, LLM4TS) that feed only normalized numerical sequences or generic task prompts into LLMs. The key conceptual departure is a dual use of LLMs: first, to encode node-specific textual documents into embeddings for context-aware adjacency matrix construction (replacing data-driven or cosine-similarity graph learning), and second, to perform domain-knowledge-injected prediction refinement via structured prompts that include node descriptions, temporal patterns, and STGNN numerical tokens. This positions FSTLLM as a bridge between the STGNN literature (which models spatial-temporal dependencies but ignores external context) and the TSFM literature (which leverages LLMs but neglects spatial correlations among channels).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses a practically important gap: many real-world spatio-temporal forecasting scenarios (parking, traffic, energy) have limited historical data at deployment, and existing STGNNs and TSFMs degrade sharply under such constraints. The plug-and-play integration study—showing that wrapping GPT4TS or iTransformer predictions with FSTLLM's Domain Knowledge Injection module yields consistent improvements without retraining those models—suggests a broadly applicable augmentation strategy. However, significant open questions remain: experiments cover only two small-scale datasets (19 nodes each), the ECL evaluation uses just 19 of 320 clients due to single-GPU hardware limits, and the paper does not address whether the LLM component provides genuine reasoning gains over simpler calibration layers (a concern raised by Tan et al., 2024 for related methods). Scalability to larger graphs, longer sequences, and diverse domains remains untested.
