## Problem

Naively enabling bidirectional attention in causal LLMs to improve text embedding performance causes severe degradation in generative quality, yet the underlying mechanism remains unexplained. This paper identifies that bidirectional fine-tuning increases 'subsequent dependence' (attention weight on future tokens), which corrupts the unidirectional generation distribution, and shows that the FFN layer is the least affected component during such training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251357/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts standard text sequences formatted with instruction prompts and produces two types of output depending on the task: autoregressive token sequences for generation, and a single embedding vector (pooled from the [EOS] token position) for text similarity tasks. During inference, a lightweight gating layer routes each token to either the unidirectional or bidirectional FFN expert, adding negligible computational overhead (e.g., 505.82 → 505.86 GFLOPs for the 0.5B model).

## Method

UBMoE-LLM constructs a two-expert Mixture-of-Experts at the FFN level: one expert is the original instruction-tuned unidirectional FFN (preserving generation), and the other is an FFN trained with bidirectional attention and SimCSE-style contrastive learning (enhancing embeddings), with all non-FFN parameters frozen during that training to minimize subsequent dependence. A gating network, trained with cross-entropy loss plus a negative-entropy regularization term (following Mistral 7B's MoE formulation), selects which expert processes each token. The gate is fine-tuned on a small subset of tulu-v2-sft-mixture data (tokens < 512, 1000 steps) while all other parameters remain frozen. The key insight is that restricting bidirectional contrastive updates to the FFN alone yields near-full-parameter embedding performance while keeping subsequent dependence lowest, because the attention distribution in early layers remains unchanged, implicitly separating the two modeling capabilities.

## Results

On MTEB STS tasks, fine-tuning only the FFN layer with bidirectional contrastive learning achieves average scores comparable to full-parameter fine-tuning (e.g., 70.48 vs. 53.31 on Qwen1.5-4B; 71.35 vs. 51.23 on Qwen1.5-7B), confirming the FFN is the least affected module by bidirectional training. On generation benchmarks, UBMoE-LLM maintains performance close to the base model while consistently improving TruthfulQA scores (e.g., 53.7→55.4 on 7B, 44.5→47.6 on 4B), demonstrating reduced hallucination. The attention-dependence analysis shows bidirectional training increases subsequent dependence (DP-A) by 4–12 points across all scales, correlating with MMLU drops of 6.7–18.8 points (e.g., 60.2→58.12 on 7B, 32.5→25.8 on 0.5B).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares UBMoE-LLM against the base unidirectional model and MNTP-LLM (a Wikipedia-trained baseline). On Qwen2.5-7B, UBMoE-LLM achieves 69.64 average (MMLU 70.86, Winogrande 75.42, TruthfulQA 62.65) versus MNTP-LLM's 68.93 (70.06, 74.21, 62.54) and the base model's 70.72 (73.86, 73.60, 64.72), showing UBMoE-LLM outperforms MNTP-LLM by ~0.7 points while staying within ~1 point of the base model. On the 0.5B scale, UBMoE-LLM (47.94 avg) beats MNTP-LLM (45.79) by over 2 points. For embedding, FFN-only bidirectional fine-tuning matches or exceeds full-parameter fine-tuning on MTEB STS (e.g., 70.48 vs. 53.31 on 4B; 71.35 vs. 51.23 on 7B), while ATT-only fine-tuning achieves slightly higher STS scores (71.40 on 4B, 72.57 on 7B) but introduces more subsequent dependence. The gating layer ablation confirms its contribution: removing it drops STS average by 1.1–1.7 points across scales.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LLM2Vec line of work, which demonstrated that enabling bidirectional attention in causal LLMs improves text embeddings but severely degrades generation. Rather than accepting this trade-off or using local bidirectional attention (Prefix-I/Prefix-H), the paper introduces a diagnostic framework—attention dependence metrics—that identifies *why* generation degrades (increased subsequent token dependence) and *where* in the architecture the damage is minimal (the FFN layer). Building on SimCSE's contrastive objective for the bidirectional expert and Mistral's MoE gating mechanism for routing, the paper proposes UBMoE-LLM, which parallelizes a bidirectionally-trained FFN with the original unidirectional FFN, a design not explored in prior unified-model work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The generation-embedding trade-off is a central obstacle for building truly unified LLMs, and the attention dependence metric offers a reusable diagnostic for quantifying how much a given training procedure disrupts autoregressive behavior. However, the practical gains are modest: UBMoE-LLM sometimes slightly degrades MMLU and Winogrande while improving TruthfulQA, and the bidirectional expert's lack of instruction alignment limits its generative contribution. Open questions include whether the FFN-robustness finding generalizes to non-Transformer architectures or to more than two experts, and whether the gate can be learned end-to-end without the small-data fine-tuning stage.
