## Problem

Existing Chinese scene text retrieval methods inherit English paradigms that crop detected text regions and enforce single-granularity alignment, which fails for Chinese because characters lack word boundaries, query terms can span arbitrary character combinations, and real-world layouts include vertical, cross-line, and partial arrangements. The prior CSVTR benchmark predominantly contains horizontal, independently-appearing queries, masking these layout challenges. This paper identifies the gap between cropped-region, single-granularity approaches and the diverse layout reality of Chinese scene text.

## Input / Output

At training, the model consumes triplets of (RGB scene image at 640×640, single-channel grayscale segmentation map highlighting one text region, corresponding Chinese text string); at inference, PaddleOCR detects text regions to generate segmentation maps, and each image produces multiple visual feature vectors (one per detected region) whose maximum cosine similarity with the query's text embedding determines the final retrieval score. The segmentation map is the key unusual input: it is a spatially-aligned mask that guides CLIP's attention without cropping, preserving all pixels outside the highlighted region.

## Method

CSTR-CLIP is a two-stage fine-tuning of Chinese CLIP (ResNet-50 visual encoder) that replaces the cropped-region paradigm with full-image feature extraction guided by a learned segmentation map. Stage 1 introduces a single-channel ConvTextpos layer that adds a text-position embedding to the RGB embedding before the CLIP image encoder, trained with InfoNCE loss on (image, segmentation map, text) triplets to teach OCR and region-focused perception. Stage 2 fuses intermediate features from a frozen original CLIP encoder at every layer via 1×1 convolutional fusion layers to recover global context lost in Stage 1, and applies Random Alignment Granularity Processing (RAGP) to randomly expand text by merging with spatially-adjacent text, mask characters from both ends, and splice adjacent segmentation maps—breaking the strict one-region-to-one-text correspondence and forcing the model to learn flexible multi-granularity matching across diverse layouts.

## Results

On the CSVTR benchmark, CSTR-CLIP achieves 88.57% mAP at 21.5 FPS, improving retrieval accuracy by 18.82 percentage points over the previous best method (Luo et al., 69.75%) and by 7.83 points over the CLIP-backbone variant (80.74%). On the newly proposed DL-CSVTR benchmark, CSTR-CLIP achieves 84.44% mAP on vertical layouts, 65.56% on cross-line layouts, and 61.85% on partial layouts, substantially outperforming all baselines across all three layout types. The model also delivers faster inference (21.5 FPS) than the strongest cropped-region baselines (10.5–12.3 FPS).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CSTR-CLIP outperforms all five baselines on both CSVTR and DL-CSVTR. On CSVTR, it surpasses the strongest cropped-region baseline Luo et al.† (80.74% mAP) by 7.83 points and TDSL† (78.36%) by 10.21 points, while also running roughly twice as fast (21.5 vs. 10.5 FPS). The original CLIP model (57.32% mAP) is notably weaker than CSTR-CLIP despite having the same backbone, confirming that the two-stage training with segmentation-map guidance and multi-granularity alignment is essential. On DL-CSVTR, the advantage widens dramatically: for cross-line layouts, CSTR-CLIP reaches 65.56% mAP versus 54.43% for CLIP and 40.46% for TDSL†; for partial layouts, it achieves 61.85% versus 37.31% for CLIP and 24.88% for TDSL†. Cropped-region methods (TDSL†, Luo et al.†) degrade sharply on cross-line and partial layouts, validating the paper's core claim that retaining full-image context is critical for diverse Chinese text layouts.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant cropped-region paradigm in scene text retrieval (TDSL, VSTR, Luo et al.), which inherits the English assumption of clear word boundaries and relies on detector-cropped text patches for feature extraction. Instead, it builds on CLIP's full-image contrastive pre-training (Radford et al., 2021) and its Chinese variant (Yang et al., 2023), repurposing the vision-language model's global perceptual capacity for text retrieval by introducing segmentation-map-guided regional attention (via ConvTextpos) and a two-stage curriculum from single- to multi-granularity alignment. The conceptual shift is from local text-region matching to global-semantics-enhanced layout pattern learning, motivated by the observation that Chinese text lacks word-level separations and exhibits diverse real-world layouts (vertical, cross-line, partial) that cropped regions cannot faithfully represent.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The DL-CSVTR benchmark exposes a concrete evaluation gap: prior Chinese scene text retrieval benchmarks (CSVTR) are dominated by horizontal, independently-appearing query terms, masking the difficulty of diverse layouts that are prevalent in real Chinese street scenes. The finding that raw CLIP outperforms cropped-region methods on cross-line and partial layouts challenges the field's assumption that tighter visual focus always helps text retrieval. Limitations include the benchmark's modest size (2,070 images, 89 queries), reliance on PaddleOCR at inference (introducing detection-error sensitivity), and evaluation restricted to Chinese; generalization to other non-Latin scripts with similar layout diversity remains an open question.
