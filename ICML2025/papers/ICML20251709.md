## Problem

Current protein structure predictors like AlphaFold3 are trained on static crystallographic models and output a single conformation, effectively marginalizing the conformational heterogeneity that experimental techniques (X-ray crystallography, NMR, cryo-EM) inherently report as ensemble-averaged measurements. This limits their ability to capture multi-modal backbone distributions, altloc regions, and solution-state dynamics that are critical for understanding protein function.

## Input / Output

The method takes an amino acid sequence plus one or more experimental observations—crystallographic electron density maps (volumetric, in e⁻/Å³ units, applied to local residue regions), NOE-derived distance restraints (pairs of lower/upper bounds on ensemble-averaged interatomic distances, applied globally), or fixed reference atom coordinates for substructure conditioning—and produces a non-i.i.d. ensemble of 3D all-atom structures. For crystallography, the final ensemble is pruned to at most 5 conformers via matching pursuit; for NMR, the full ensemble (16–128 members) is retained. The non-i.i.d. nature means ensemble members are jointly sampled rather than independently, which is essential for representing multi-modal conformational distributions.

## Method

The core contribution is a three-stage pipeline that casts protein ensemble generation as posterior inference under AlphaFold3's diffusion-based structural prior. First, Guided AlphaFold3 modifies AlphaFold3's variance-preserving SDE by adding a guidance score term η∇_X log p(y|X¹,…,Xⁿ,a) that is non-separable across ensemble members, enabling the model to jointly sample conformations whose ensemble-averaged properties (e.g., mean electron density, mean interatomic distances) match experimental observations—something standard i.i.d. sampling cannot achieve. Second, an AMBER force-field relaxation step removes geometric artifacts (broken bonds, steric clashes) introduced during guided sampling. Third, a matching-pursuit algorithm greedily selects a subset of relaxed structures that maximizes the experimental log-likelihood while preserving conformational diversity. The key architectural insight is that the likelihood term is conditioned on the entire ensemble (making it inseparable), which naturally encodes the ensemble-averaged nature of crystallographic and NMR observables.

## Results

On NMR structure determination, NOE-guided AlphaFold3 reduces NOE restraint violations from 9.7–42.6% (unguided AlphaFold3) to 5.3–36.5%, outperforming PDB-deposited NMR structures (1.0–31.4%) in half of the 12 evaluated proteins. On the ubiquitin benchmark, non-i.i.d. NOE guidance achieves r=0.72 correlation with experimental N-H S² order parameters, versus r=0.47 for unguided AlphaFold3 and r=0.52 for i.i.d. guidance. For crystallographic altloc regions, density-guided AlphaFold3 matches or exceeds PDB cosine similarity in 7 of 12 cases (e.g., 4OLE: 0.882 vs. PDB 0.880), while unguided AlphaFold3 drops to 0.447–0.839. Runtime is reduced from days (restrained MD) to minutes (e.g., ~14 min for a 32-conformation NMR ensemble).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against unguided AlphaFold3, the guided method consistently wins: in NMR, violations drop by 15–30 percentage points (e.g., 2K52: 27.6%→14.3%; 2KRS: 13.8%→10.3%), and in crystallography, cosine similarity improves by 0.1–0.3 (e.g., 5G51: 0.447→0.749). Against AlphaFlow and ESMFlow (Jing et al., 2024), guided AlphaFold3 outperforms both on NMR restraint satisfaction (e.g., 2K52: 14.3% vs. 37.6% AlphaFlow, 35.8% ESMFlow) and on crystallographic density fitting (e.g., 4OLE: 0.882 vs. 0.792 ESMFlow, 0.793 AlphaFlow; 5G51: 0.749 vs. 0.486 ESMFlow, 0.513 AlphaFlow). Against Guided Chroma (Maddipatla et al., 2024), the proposed method achieves higher density cosine similarity in 10 of 12 altloc cases (e.g., 4OLE: 0.882 vs. 0.863; 2Q3G: 0.737 vs. 0.743 is the one near-tie). In half of the NMR cases, guided AlphaFold3 even surpasses the PDB-deposited NMR structures in restraint compliance, and in 7 of 12 crystallographic altloc cases it matches or exceeds the PDB model's density fit.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes AlphaFold3's diffusion-based structure module as a sequence-conditioned generative prior for solving inverse problems in protein structure space, extending the guided-diffusion framework of Weiss et al. (2023) from molecular design to structural biology. It builds directly on the authors' prior work (Maddipatla et al., 2024) that used Chroma for density-guided ensemble generation, but replaces Chroma's hierarchical formulation with AlphaFold3's atom-transformer diffusion model, enabling longer-range conformational modeling and stronger sequence conditioning. The non-i.i.d. joint ensemble sampling distinguishes this from prior ensemble generators (AlphaFlow, ESMFlow) that produce independent samples, and the three-stage pipeline (guided diffusion → AMBER relaxation → matching pursuit) is a novel combination not present in prior NMR or crystallographic fitting methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dramatic speedup for NMR structure determination (from days of MD to minutes) and the consistent recovery of conformational heterogeneity that AlphaFold3's single-structure training objective erases have direct practical impact on structural biology workflows. The finding that guided ensembles sometimes outperform PDB-deposited structures in density faithfulness or NOE restraint compliance suggests that human-biased modeling in the PDB can be systematically improved. Open questions include generalization to protein complexes and ligand interactions, handling cryo-EM conformational heterogeneity, and whether the fixed B-factor and local density guidance limitations can be addressed with more principled noise models.
