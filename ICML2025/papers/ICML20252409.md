## Problem

Popular distribution-free calibration methods such as histogram binning and isotonic regression provide only asymptotic calibration guarantees, with finite-sample performance that can degrade substantially. Venn and Venn-Abers calibration, which offer finite-sample marginal calibration via set-valued predictions, were previously restricted to binary classification. This paper extends the Venn framework to a broad class of prediction problems defined by generic loss functions and introduces Venn multicalibration for finite-sample calibration across subpopulations.

## Input / Output

The method takes a fixed trained model f, an independent calibration dataset of n i.i.d. (X, Y) pairs, a new context x, and a loss function ℓ (e.g., squared error or quantile loss). Rather than producing a single calibrated prediction, it outputs a set of calibrated point predictions f_{n,x}(x) = {f_n^{(x,y)}(x) : y ∈ Y}, guaranteed to contain at least one marginally perfectly ℓ-calibrated prediction in finite samples. For quantile loss, the set induces a conformal prediction interval with quantile-conditional coverage; for multicalibration, the set contains a perfectly multicalibrated prediction with respect to a specified function class G.

## Method

The core mechanism iterates over all possible imputed outcomes y ∈ Y: for each y, it augments the calibration set with (x, y), applies a point calibrator A_ℓ (e.g., isotonic regression for Venn-Abers, or offset loss minimization over G for multicalibration) to obtain a perfectly in-sample calibrated model, and records the prediction at x. The collection of these predictions across all y forms the Venn prediction set. The key theoretical insight is that the oracle prediction (using the true Y_{n+1}) is necessarily contained in this set, and by exchangeability of the data, this oracle prediction is marginally perfectly calibrated, yielding a finite-sample guarantee without distributional assumptions. For Venn-Abers calibration, isotonic regression's stability ensures the prediction set shrinks to a point as n grows, with conditional ℓ² calibration error O_p(n^{-2/3}). The framework unifies conformal prediction as a special case: Venn quantile calibration recovers a novel CP procedure with quantile-conditional coverage, and multicalibrated conformal prediction (Gibbs et al., 2023) emerges as a special case of Venn multicalibration.

## Results

On six real-world datasets (Bike, Bio, STAR, MEPS, Concrete, Community), Venn-Abers (VA) conformal quantile calibration achieves the lowest conditional calibration error (CCE) across all datasets, ranging from 0.017 (Bio) to 0.035 (Concrete), while maintaining the target 90% marginal coverage. VA's CCE is 30–50% lower than CQR (e.g., 0.019 vs. 0.031 on Bike; 0.017 vs. 0.020 on Bio) and 2–5× lower than Marginal CP (e.g., 0.019 vs. 0.10 on Bike). Average interval widths for VA are comparable to CQR and Mondrian CP, typically within 2–5% of the narrowest calibrated baselines. In the Venn multicalibration experiment (Table 2), the oracle Venn-calibrated prediction achieves lower multicalibration error than the point-calibrated model on 5 of 6 datasets, with the largest gains on Community (0.0055 vs. 0.0209) and STAR (0.0078 vs. 0.0113).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Venn-Abers (VA) consistently outperforms CQR (Romano et al., 2019) on conditional calibration error across all six datasets, with CCE reductions of 30–50% (e.g., 0.019 vs. 0.031 on Bike, 0.017 vs. 0.020 on Bio, 0.028 vs. 0.031 on Community), at the cost of slightly wider intervals (e.g., 100 vs. 98 on Bike). Against Marginal CP (Vovk et al., 2005), VA achieves dramatically lower CCE—roughly 2–5× smaller (0.019 vs. 0.10 on Bike, 0.017 vs. 0.053 on Bio, 0.018 vs. 0.052 on MEPS)—while also producing narrower intervals (100 vs. 140 on Bike). Compared to Mondrian CP / VM (Romano et al., 2020), VA achieves lower CCE on 5 of 6 datasets; the closest competitor is VM(10 bin) on Bike (0.022 vs. 0.019) and Bio (0.020 vs. 0.017), but VA still wins on CCE. The key advantage of VA over VM is data-adaptive binning via isotonic regression, which avoids the need to pre-specify the number of bins and yields tighter calibration in finite samples. All calibrated methods achieve the nominal 90% marginal coverage, confirming the distribution-free guarantee, while uncalibrated intervals fall well short (71–86% coverage).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper generalizes Vovk's Venn calibration (originally for binary classification) and Venn-Abers predictors (using isotonic regression) to arbitrary loss functions, unifying them under a single set-valued calibration framework. It further shows that standard conformal prediction, Mondrian CP, and the multicalibrated CP of Gibbs et al. (2023) are all special cases of Venn multicalibration with the quantile loss, thereby subsuming three previously separate lines of work. The theoretical machinery builds on the property-elicitation view of calibration from Noarov and Roth (2023) and the distribution-free isotonic calibration guarantees of van der Laan et al. (2023, 2024).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides finite-sample marginal calibration guarantees (not merely asymptotic) while producing set-valued predictions that explicitly capture epistemic uncertainty in the calibration step — a practical advantage in small-sample or safety-critical settings where point calibrators can overfit. The unification of CP, Mondrian CP, and multicalibrated CP as special cases of Venn multicalibration offers a cleaner theoretical lens and a single algorithmic template for constructing calibrated prediction sets across subpopulations. Open questions include the computational cost for continuous outcome spaces (requiring discretization), the tradeoff between set width and informativeness in high-dimensional settings, and whether the conditional calibration rate O_p(n^{-2/3}) for isotonic Venn-Abers is minimax-optimal.
