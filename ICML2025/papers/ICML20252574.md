## Problem

Tree-search-based reasoning methods (e.g., Tree-of-Thoughts) dramatically improve LLM reasoning by exploring multiple intermediate thoughts, but they incur inference latency that increases by several orders of magnitude because thought generation dominates over 91% of total runtime. Existing acceleration approaches like standard speculative decoding operate only at the token level, failing to exploit the inherent structure of TSB reasoning where thoughts are fundamental units, resulting in suboptimal speedup.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252574/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a math problem as input and operates within a tree-search framework (beam search or MCTS) configured with a large model (e.g., Qwen2.5-72B-Instruct), a small draft model (e.g., Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct), and a process reward model (PRM) for thought evaluation. It produces a final reasoning solution (answer) along with the full reasoning path of intermediate thoughts. The framework is plug-and-play: it replaces the standard node expansion module in any TSB algorithm without modifying the search algorithm or prompting strategy, and the EMA weight θ (set to 0.9) controls the trade-off between historical and current quality estimates.

## Method

SpecSearch proposes a bi-level speculative thought generator following a draft-evaluate-reject-correct paradigm. At the coarse-grained thought level, a small model drafts N reasoning thoughts in parallel, a PRM scores them, and a quality-preserving rejection mechanism filters out thoughts below a dynamic step-wise threshold estimated via EMA over the large model's historical outputs. At the fine-grained token level, rejected thoughts are corrected using lossless token-level speculative decoding with the large model, ensuring corrected thoughts match the large model's distribution. The key insight is that the PRM (already present in TSB frameworks) serves as the thought-level verifier, avoiding the intractability of modeling thought distributions directly. A theoretical guarantee (Theorem 4.3) shows that if the threshold meets or exceeds the large model's quality at each step, the generator preserves undegraded reasoning quality, with probability bounds (Theorems 4.5–4.6) quantifying how this holds across K reasoning steps under a descending-quality assumption.

## Results

On MATH-100 with Qwen models, SpecSearch achieves 87.00% reasoning accuracy with 82.35s average latency, yielding a 3.35× speedup over autoregressive decoding (AR) and 1.72× over speculative sampling (SpS). On GSM8K-100 with Qwen, SpecSearch reaches 96.00% accuracy at 48.18s latency (2.87× vs AR, 1.44× vs SpS). With Llama models, SpecSearch not only accelerates inference (1.99× vs AR on GSM8K-100) but also improves accuracy over both baselines (88.00% vs 87.00% AR and 86.00% SpS). Across all settings, SpecSearch achieves up to 2.12× speedup over SpS while maintaining comparable or superior reasoning quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SpecSearch is compared against two baselines: AR (autoregressive decoding with the large model, i.e., standard Tree-of-Thoughts) and SpS (state-of-the-art lossless speculative sampling at the token level). On speed, SpecSearch consistently outperforms both baselines across all model-dataset combinations, achieving up to 3.35× speedup over AR and 1.72× over SpS on MATH-100 with Qwen. On reasoning accuracy, SpecSearch matches AR on Qwen MATH (87.00% vs 87.00%) and slightly trails on Qwen GSM8K (96.00% vs 97.00%), but surpasses both baselines on Llama MATH (64.00% vs 62.00% AR, 61.00% SpS) and Llama GSM8K (88.00% vs 87.00% AR, 86.00% SpS). Against SpS specifically, SpecSearch achieves a consistent 1.04×–1.72× additional speedup while maintaining or improving accuracy, demonstrating that thought-level speculation captures structural advantages that token-level speculation alone cannot exploit.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper generalizes the speculative decoding paradigm (Leviathan et al., 2023) from token-level to a bi-level (thought + token) formulation specifically for tree-search-based reasoning frameworks like Tree-of-Thoughts (Yao et al., 2023). Prior acceleration work on tree-search reasoning (Gao et al., 2024; SEED, Wang et al., 2024e) applied standard speculative decoding only at the token level, neglecting the structural role of thoughts as fundamental reasoning units. SpecSearch departs from these by treating full reasoning thoughts as high-level speculative tasks and tokens as low-level ones, and introduces a quality-preserving rejection mechanism using a process reward model and EMA-based threshold estimation rather than strict token-level alignment. The experimental setup builds on the OpenR framework (Wang et al., 2024a) for beam search and MCTS reasoning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 2–3× speedup for tree-search reasoning is practically significant for real-time deployment of slow-thinking LLMs, and the quality-preserving rejection mechanism provides a general recipe for safely offloading computation to smaller models in structured reasoning pipelines. The theoretical guarantee (Theorems 4.3–4.6) is a notable contribution, though it relies on strong assumptions (Gaussian quality distributions, monotonically descending quality across steps) that may not hold in all domains. Open questions include whether the approach extends to non-mathematical reasoning (code, science, multi-step planning), how it behaves with larger model-size gaps or different PRM architectures, and whether the EMA threshold estimation can be replaced by more adaptive or learned estimators for domains where quality does not monotonically decrease.
