## Problem

While synthetic face datasets have been proposed to address privacy and ethical concerns of large-scale real datasets, it remains unclear whether generative models can produce identities diverse enough to train competitive face recognition models. Diffusion-based methods achieve strong performance but are shown to memorize and leak training data, while existing GAN-based synthetic datasets underperform significantly. This paper addresses both gaps by introducing a physics-inspired sampling method that generates diverse, privacy-preserving synthetic identities from GAN latent spaces.

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes random Gaussian vectors in an auxiliary space Z, a pretrained StyleGAN2 generator (with its mapping network f and synthesis network g), a pretrained ArcFace-based IResNet50 face recognition model trained on MS-Celeb-1M (used only for computing embedding distances during optimization), and 7 covariates latent direction vectors extracted from CMU Multi-PIE via SVM fitting. The output is a structured synthetic dataset of N_id identities (10k–30k) each with N_var intra-class variations (16–64), totaling up to 1.95 million face images, organized for direct use in training face recognition models.

## Method

The core contribution is three complementary algorithms inspired by granular mechanics and Brownian dynamics. The Langevin algorithm treats synthetic identities as soft spherical particles in the GAN latent space, subject to a quadratic spring-like repulsive force in embedding space (active only when pairwise angular distances fall below a threshold d₀^E), a quadratic pull-back force toward the average latent w_avg, viscous damping, and a random Brownian force; the system is evolved via the overdamped Langevin SDE with an adaptive time-step. The Dispersion algorithm generates intra-class variations by applying analogous physics per identity, but with repulsive granular forces in latent space (to spread variations) and an attractive quadratic loss in embedding space (to preserve identity). DisCo extends Dispersion by initializing variations with random linear combinations of 7 covariates latent directions (pose, illumination, expressions), which forces greater intra-class diversity from the start. The key design choice is the granular contact potential, which naturally limits interactions to nearby particles and avoids the dimensionality-dependent power-law scaling of forces like gravity or electrostatics, making it well-suited for high-dimensional latent spaces.

## Results

The best configuration (Langevin-DisCo, 30k identities, 1.95M images) achieves 98.97% on LFW, 81.52% on CP-LFW, 93.95% on CA-LFW, 83.77% on CFP-FP, and 93.32% on AgeDB-30, surpassing all GAN-based synthetic datasets by a wide margin. Compared to the strongest diffusion-based baseline (DCFace-1.2M), the method matches or exceeds performance on LFW (98.97 vs 98.90), CA-LFW (93.95 vs 92.80), and AgeDB (93.32 vs 91.52), while trailing on CP-LFW (81.52 vs 84.97) and CFP-FP (83.77 vs 89.04). A 10k-identity variant still reaches 97.07% LFW and 89.05% CA-LFW, outperforming all GAN-based methods by 3–14 points. A gap of roughly 1–11 points remains relative to real-data training (MS-Celeb-1M: 99.82% LFW, 92.83% CP-LFW).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against all GAN-based synthetic datasets, Langevin-DisCo (30k) dominates: it beats the best GAN baseline GANDiffFace by +4.6 pts on LFW, +5.4 on CP-LFW, +14.1 on CA-LFW, +4.8 on CFP-FP, and +23.5 on AgeDB. Against diffusion-based methods, the paper's 30k configuration outperforms DCFace-1.2M on LFW (+0.07), CA-LFW (+1.15), and AgeDB (+1.80), but trails on CP-LFW (−3.45) and CFP-FP (−5.27); it outperforms IDiff-Face on every benchmark by 0.8–11.2 points. Against real-data training (MS-Celeb-1M), a gap of 0.85 pts on LFW but up to 11.3 pts on CP-LFW remains, indicating that synthetic data still does not fully replicate the diversity of web-crawled collections. The 10k-identity DisCo variant already surpasses all GAN-based methods and approaches DCFace-0.5M, suggesting strong data efficiency. The authors note that diffusion-based baselines suffer from identity leakage (memorization of training images), a problem their GAN-based pipeline avoids, making the comparison partially unfair in favor of their method on privacy-sensitive use cases.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of synthetic face dataset generation for FR training (SynFace, SFace, DCFace, IDiff-Face) by replacing ad-hoc latent sampling strategies with physics-inspired stochastic dynamics. It directly builds on Colbois et al. (2021) for the Covariates latent-direction method, which is integrated into the DisCo algorithm, and draws on granular mechanics (DEM) and the Langevin equation to formulate identity sampling as a soft-particle packing problem in GAN latent space. The granular contact loss function is novel in this context, though repulsive/potential-field losses have appeared in GAN training (Wang et al. 2018, Unterthiner et al. 2017) and in the unification of GANs with diffusion as interacting particle models (Franceschi et al. 2023). The key departure from prior work is the deliberate choice of GANs over diffusion models to avoid training-data memorization, combined with an additional repulsive loss against the generator's training set embeddings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a GAN-based synthetic dataset pipeline can nearly match diffusion-based methods (90.31 average vs. DCFace's 91.52 at 1.2M scale) while avoiding the identity leakage that plagues diffusion models, making it a more privacy-responsible option for FR training. The physics-inspired parameterization (repulsion thresholds, pull-back coefficients, stochastic force amplitude) gives practitioners interpretable knobs to tune dataset diversity and quality for specific deployment needs. Open questions include whether the gap to real-data performance (97.82 on LFW for MS-Celeb-1M vs. 98.97 for the best synthetic) can be closed, whether the approach generalizes beyond face recognition, and how to address residual demographic biases inherited from the pretrained StyleGAN and FR backbone. The unexpected finding that increasing training-set repulsion slightly improves FR accuracy, and the sharp performance collapse at N_var=128, suggest non-trivial interactions between dataset diversity and model capacity that remain unexplained.
