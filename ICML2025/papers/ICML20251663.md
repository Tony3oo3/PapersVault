## Problem

Existing model calibration methods rely exclusively on one-hot labeled data, which implicitly assumes uniform certainty across all samples and provides no ground-truth uncertainty for the model to learn from. While Mixup-based augmentation attempts to introduce soft labels, it produces low-fidelity pixel-interpolated images that deviate from real-world distributions and fail to improve calibration. The paper addresses the unexplored problem of generating high-fidelity, semantically mixed training samples with accurate confidence annotations for calibration-aware learning.

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 64×64 for Tiny-ImageNet, 224×224 for ImageNet) and produces calibrated class probabilities whose confidence scores reflect the true likelihood of correct prediction. During training, the pipeline additionally consumes pairs of class-conditioned one-hot samples and generates synthetic intermediate images with soft class posterior labels via a diffusion model; the final output is a softmax probability vector where the predicted confidence is aligned with empirical accuracy, measured by ECE and AECE.

## Method

CSM generates calibration-aware augmentations by conditioning a pretrained EDM diffusion model on the same Gaussian latent noise x^(T) but varying soft class posteriors q̃^λ = λq^(yi) + (1−λ)q^(yj), producing a correlated set of images that smoothly transition between two classes while preserving spatial completeness. A calibrated reannotation step then refines the mixing coefficient using CLIP's visual encoder: the encoded feature is projected onto the 1-D affine space spanned by class prototypes Pi and Pj to obtain λ_E, which is mapped through a temperature-scaled sigmoid to yield a bias-free λ. Theoretically, the paper proves that L2 loss is the unique confidence-balanced objective for soft-labeled data (Proposition 3.4), while CE and Focal losses systematically bias predictions toward sharper or softer labels respectively. The overall training objective combines standard CE loss on original one-hot samples with L2 loss on the CSM-augmented soft-labeled samples, enabling balanced confidence learning without sacrificing classification accuracy.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (ResNet-50), CSM achieves 95.79% accuracy with ECE of 0.54 and AECE of 0.33, substantially outperforming the best baseline FCL (95.44% ACC, 0.76 ECE) and RankMixup (94.88% ACC, 2.59 ECE). On CIFAR-100, CSM reaches 78.84% accuracy with ECE 1.29, compared to CE's 13.59 ECE and RankMixup's 3.46 ECE. On Tiny-ImageNet, CSM attains 66.99% accuracy with ECE 1.29, while FCL drops to 7.51 ECE. On ImageNet with ResNet-50, CSM achieves 79.87% top-1 accuracy and 1.32 ECE, and with Swin-Transformer-V2 it reaches 81.08% accuracy and 1.49 ECE.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CSM outperforms all 15 compared baselines on ECE across every dataset. Against regularization-based methods, CSM reduces ECE from 3.75 (CE) to 0.54 on CIFAR-10 and from 13.59 (CE) to 1.29 on CIFAR-100; the closest competitor FCL achieves 0.76 ECE on CIFAR-10 but degrades to 7.51 on Tiny-ImageNet, while CSM maintains 1.29. Among Mixup-based methods, RankMixup is the strongest (2.59 ECE on CIFAR-10, 3.46 on CIFAR-100), yet CSM still halves its ECE on both datasets while also achieving higher accuracy (95.79% vs. 94.88% on CIFAR-10). On ImageNet, CSM achieves the best accuracy (79.87% with ResNet-50, 81.08% with Swin-V2) and competitive ECE (1.32/1.49), surpassing CALS (1.46/1.61 ECE) and RankMixup (3.93 ECE). For OOD detection (AUROC), CSM leads on CIFAR-10→CIFAR-100 (91.82% vs. 87.82% for RankMixup) but is slightly behind RankMixup on CIFAR-10→Tiny-ImageNet (91.05% vs. 88.94%) and CIFAR-100→Tiny-ImageNet (80.45% vs. 80.67%), indicating near-parity on cross-domain OOD tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of data-aware calibration (Mixup, RankMixup, RegMixup) and diffusion-based generative augmentation (EDM, MagicMix, Diff-Mix), but departs from both lines in key ways. Rather than pixel-level interpolation as in Mixup, CSM performs semantic mixing at the diffusion conditioning level, generating high-fidelity samples that preserve object completeness. It further introduces a calibrated reannotation step using CLIP class prototypes to correct the misalignment between diffusion generation labels and true class posteriors, and provides a theoretical argument that L2 loss—unlike CE or Focal—is 'confidence-balanced' for correlated soft-labeled pairs, a property absent from prior calibration loss analyses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a fundamental data-level gap in calibration: one-hot labels encode zero uncertainty, yet calibration requires the model to learn graded confidence. By generating soft-labeled samples with controlled semantic transitions, CSM provides a principled data-driven alternative to post-hoc methods that are sensitive to distribution shift. The confidence-balanced loss theory is reusable beyond this specific pipeline. Open questions include scalability to very large or open-vocabulary settings, the sensitivity to the choice of diffusion backbone and CLIP encoder, and whether the approach extends naturally to detection or segmentation tasks where spatial uncertainty structure differs.
