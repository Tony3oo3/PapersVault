## Problem

Existing graph sparsification algorithms preserve cut values between a vertex set S and its complement V\S, but bipartite-like cluster detection requires preserving the cut value w(A,B) between two specific disjoint vertex sets A and B. Moreover, most sparsification techniques are limited to undirected graphs and rely on Laplacian solvers, making them impractical for online or local settings. This paper asks whether one can construct a sparse subgraph in nearly-linear time, using only random sampling, that preserves the structure of k bipartite-like clusters in both undirected and directed graphs.

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes a weighted graph (undirected or directed) with m edges and a parameter k specifying the number of bipartite-like clusters, and can be run online with degree oracles while a local clustering algorithm explores the graph from a seed vertex. It outputs a sparse subgraph G* with O(n·log³n / (2−λ_{n−k})) edges whose edge weights are rescaled by 1/p_e, such that the k-way dual Cheeger constant satisfies ρ̄_{G*}(k) = Ω(ρ̄_G(k)) with high probability. The sparsifier is designed to be plugged directly into existing local algorithms (e.g., LOCBIPARTDC, EVOCUTDIRECTED) as a subroutine to reduce their running time without degrading output quality.

## Method

For undirected graphs, each edge {u,v} is sampled independently with probability p_e = p_u(v) + p_v(u) − p_u(v)·p_v(u), where p_u(v) = min{w(u,v)·C·log³n / (d(u)·(2−λ_{n−k})), 1}; sampled edges receive rescaled weight w/p_e. Preservation of inter-cluster cut values is shown via Chebyshev's inequality on the second moment, while preservation of the number of clusters (i.e., the top n−k eigenspaces of the signless Laplacian J_G) is established using the Matrix Chernoff bound on a carefully constructed random matrix sum. For directed graphs, the key technical contribution is a novel 'semi-double cover' reduction that maps a digraph to an undirected bipartite graph where each directed bipartite-like cluster (A_i, B_i) corresponds to a low-conductance set A_{i,1} ∪ B_{i,2}; the undirected sparsifier is then applied, and a 'reverse semi-double cover' recovers a directed sparsifier. The entire pipeline runs in Õ(m) time, uses only random sampling (no Laplacian solvers), and is fully online-compatible with local exploration.

## Results

On the Dyadic Militarised Interstate Disputes Dataset (undirected), the proposed sparsification reduces runtime from ~0.034s to ~0.004s (≈8× speedup) while maintaining a similar bipartiteness ratio (e.g., 0.292 vs. 0.285 for USA). On the US Migration Dataset (directed), runtime drops from 20.66s to 13.43s (≈1.5× speedup) for Maricopa County while matching the flow-ratio (0.414 vs. 0.417). Synthetic experiments (Figures 3–5) confirm that the speedup grows with graph size (500–2,500 vertices per partition) while the bipartiteness/flow-ratio remains comparable to the unsparsified baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against the local algorithms LOCBIPARTDC (MS) and EVOCUTDIRECTED (ECD) from Macgregor & Sun (2021a), which are the state-of-the-art local methods for finding bipartite-like clusters in undirected and directed graphs, respectively. On undirected graphs (Table 1), the proposed sparsification achieves an approximately 8× runtime speedup (e.g., 0.034s → 0.0044s for USA) while preserving the bipartiteness ratio within a small margin (0.292 vs. 0.285), though one seed (Lithuania) shows a larger quality drop (0.303 → 0.165). On directed graphs (Table 2), the speedup is more modest at roughly 1.5–1.7× (e.g., 20.66s → 13.43s for Maricopa County), but the flow-ratio is matched or slightly improved (0.414 → 0.417; 0.546 → 0.621). Synthetic experiments (Figures 3–5) show that the runtime advantage widens as the number of vertices increases from 500 to 2,500 per partition, and for directed graphs the speedup grows with the edge probability η (0.7 → 0.9). Overall, the method consistently wins on runtime while tying or slightly improving on cluster quality metrics across both graph types.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the graph sparsification literature (Spielman & Teng, Batson et al., Lee & Sun) by introducing a fundamentally different preservation objective: instead of preserving cut values w(S, V\S) for all subsets, it preserves the cut values w(A_i, B_i) between specific pairs of vertex sets that form bipartite-like clusters. The algorithmic design builds directly on Sun & Zanetti (2019), whose edge-sampling framework and cluster-preserving sparsifier (Lemma 9) are reused as a subroutine, but the sampling probabilities are redefined to target the dual Cheeger constant rather than the standard Cheeger constant. A key departure is the extension to directed graphs via the novel semi-double cover reduction, since most prior sparsification results are restricted to undirected graphs and rely on Laplacian solvers that do not generalize to the directed setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is a direct, plug-in speedup for existing local algorithms that detect bipartite-like clusters (e.g., Macgregor & Sun 2021a), with experiments showing roughly 7–8× runtime reduction on undirected graphs and 1.5–2× on directed graphs while preserving output quality. The semi-double cover provides a clean, reusable reduction from directed to undirected sparsification that may find applications beyond this specific clustering task. Limitations include the requirement to know k in advance, the assumption that ρ̄_G(k) is bounded below (≥ 1/log n for undirected, = 1−o(1/k) for directed), and the fact that the speedup on directed graphs is more modest. Open questions include whether the approach extends to hypergraphs or to settings where k is unknown, and whether the polylogarithmic factors in the sparsifier size can be tightened.
