## Problem

Current text-to-image models suffer from poor controllability because multimodal concepts are entangled, causing text modifications to produce unintended image changes. The theoretical foundations for learning atomic, disentangled concepts from multimodal distributions remain underexplored: prior identifiability results are either block-wise rather than component-wise, rely on restrictive semi-parametric assumptions, or cannot handle discrete variables like text. This paper establishes component-wise identifiability of atomic textual and visual concepts under flexible nonparametric conditions that accommodate both continuous and discrete modalities.

## Input / Output

For generation, the model takes a text prompt as input and produces a single RGB image; for editing, it takes a source image plus a target text prompt and produces a modified image. The text prompt is first encoded into sequential embeddings by a pre-trained encoder, then compressed into 64 fixed-length textual concept tokens via a Perceiver Resampler. The source image (when editing) is encoded via SigLIP into a continuous embedding from which exogenous noise is recovered. The diffusion transformer is conditioned not on raw text embeddings but on learned visual concept representations, which is the key architectural departure from standard T2I pipelines.

## Method

ConceptAligner decomposes the T2I pipeline into four modules: a text network (Perceiver Resampler) extracting 64 disentangled textual concept tokens z^T, an image network (VAE-style encoder) recovering exogenous noise ε from the image, a concept network that combines z^T (modulated by a learnable sparse binary mask m) with ε to produce visual concepts z^I, and a diffusion transformer conditioned on z^I for image synthesis. The sparse mask m enforces a bipartite graph between textual and visual concepts, directly implementing the identifiability condition that each textual concept influences a distinct subset of visual concepts. Training combines a standard diffusion denoising loss, a KL divergence loss regularizing ε toward N(0,I), and an L1 sparsity penalty on m. The model is built on SANA's linear diffusion transformer with LoRA (rank 256), using only 64 text tokens at inference versus SANA's 300, making it faster than the base model.

## Results

On the EMU-Edit dataset, ConceptAligner achieves the best CLIP-I (0.903), LPIPS (0.357), and DINO (0.835) scores, outperforming the strongest baseline FLUX-D (CLIP-I 0.872, LPIPS 0.452, DINO 0.721) by 3.1% in CLIP-I, 21% in LPIPS reduction, and 15.7% in DINO. On PIE-Bench, it similarly leads with CLIP-I 0.879, LPIPS 0.488, and DINO 0.651. In the disentanglement analysis (Table 3), ConceptAligner achieves the highest subject consistency (0.946) and prompt consistency (0.814), surpassing SANA-Finetune (0.934 / 0.731) by 1.2% and 11.1% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ConceptAligner outperforms all 11 baselines on the primary metrics (CLIP-I, LPIPS, DINO) across both EMU-Edit and PIE-Bench. Against the strongest T2I baselines, it beats FLUX-D by 3.1% CLIP-I and 15.7% DINO on EMU-Edit, and SANA-Finetune by 3.9% CLIP-I and 4.8% DINO on PIE-Bench. On real-world image editing (Table 7), ConceptAligner achieves the best CLIP-I (0.917) and DINO (0.782), surpassing PnpInv (0.892 / 0.720) and RF-Inversion (0.906 / 0.737), while also matching or beating Plug-and-Play (0.887 / 0.705) and Fireflow-FLUX (0.891 / 0.725). The only metric where ConceptAligner does not lead is CLIP-T (text-image similarity), where SD3.5-M (0.321) and SANA-Finetune (0.320) on EMU-Edit, and SD3.5-L (0.340) on PIE-Bench, score slightly higher—suggesting a minor trade-off between prompt fidelity and edit precision. In the disentanglement evaluation, ConceptAligner's 0.814 prompt consistency substantially exceeds SANA (0.615) and SANA-Finetune (0.731), confirming that the learned atomic concepts enable more targeted edits without collateral changes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends multimodal causal representation learning by achieving component-wise identifiability of atomic concepts under fully nonparametric conditions that accommodate both continuous (image) and discrete (text) latent variables. It builds on the nonlinear ICA identification framework of Kong et al. (2022) for the visual modality and the mixture-oracle discrete identification results of Kivva et al. (2021) for the textual modality, unifying them into a two-stage identification procedure. The practical model, ConceptAligner, is implemented on top of SANA's linear diffusion transformer, replacing standard text-embedding conditioning with learned visual concepts produced by a Perceiver Resampler concept network with a learnable sparse mask. This departs from prior multimodal identifiability results (Daunhawer et al., von Kügelgen et al., Yao et al.) that only guarantee block-wise identifiability, and from Morioka & Hyvarinen / Sun et al. that require semi-parametric assumptions or exclude discrete variables.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identifiability guarantees provide a principled foundation for atomic-level controllability in text-to-image generation, addressing a persistent practical failure mode where text edits cause unintended image changes. A key limitation is that Condition 4.2-iv (sufficient variability) requires training captions to be diverse and informative enough to span the visual concept space, which may fail on datasets with poor caption quality. Open questions include whether the sparse bipartite connectivity assumption (Condition 4.3-iv) holds naturally in very complex scenes with many interacting concepts, and whether the identification theory extends to more than two modalities or to autoregressive text generation. The practical model's speed advantage (0.48s vs. 0.58s for SANA) despite additional networks suggests the compact concept representation may be broadly useful.
