## Problem

Fast adversarial training (FAT) methods use single-step attacks for efficiency but suffer from compromised robustness due to insufficient exploration of the adversarial space. Existing remedies either require stronger (multi-step) inner maximization, defeating the speed advantage, or add complex regularizers. This paper asks whether robustness can be improved in FAT simply by replacing the AT loss function, without altering the perturbation generation procedure.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252909/cropped_page6_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 64×64 for Tiny ImageNet) and outputs class labels over C classes. A key design detail is that the QUB loss operates entirely in the C-dimensional logit space rather than the high-dimensional input space: it uses f(x), f(x+δ), and ∇_f L(f(x)), all of which are C-vectors, avoiding the need to store or compute input-sized tensors (c×H×W) for the regularization terms. The perturbation δ is generated by any existing single-step or multi-step attack method and is consumed only to produce the perturbed logit f(x+δ).

## Method

The paper derives a quadratic upper bound (QUB) on the cross-entropy AT loss by exploiting the convexity of cross-entropy with respect to logits and bounding the Hessian norm: ‖H‖₂ ≤ 1/2. The resulting QUB loss has three terms — the clean-sample loss, a linear term (f(x+δ)−f(x))ᵀ∇_f L(f(x)) that penalizes logit changes aligned with the loss gradient (flattening the landscape), and a quadratic term ¼‖f(x+δ)−f(x)‖₂² that limits overall logit displacement. The QUB loss directly replaces the AT loss in the outer minimization of any existing FAT or multi-step AT method, requiring no changes to the inner maximization. Two training schedules are proposed: QUB-static (use QUB throughout) and QUB-decreasing (linearly interpolate from QUB to AT loss over training epochs to avoid over-regularization that degrades clean accuracy). Empirically, QUB-decreasing improves robust accuracy across most baselines (e.g., +2.35 PGD10 for FGSM-GA on CIFAR-10) while preserving standard accuracy, and loss-landscape analyses (dominant Hessian eigenvalues, adversarial sparsity) confirm that the bound flattens the loss surface around inputs.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with ResNet-18 (ε=8/255), applying QUB-static to existing FAT methods consistently improves robust accuracy: e.g., FGSM-CKPT PGD-10 rises from 41.19% to 45.41%, FGSM-GA from 49.89% to 52.24%, and PGD-AT from 52.99% to 54.58%. The QUB-decreasing variant (linearly transitioning from QUB to AT loss) better preserves standard accuracy while still gaining robustness, e.g., FGSM-PGI(MEP) achieves 53.95% PGD-10 with 81.56% SA versus 53.43%/81.48% baseline. The sole exception is FGSM-RS, where QUB degrades PGD-10 from 47.28% to 42.96% (static) due to catastrophic overfitting amplification. Training time increases modestly (e.g., Free-AT: 0.30h → 0.56h), keeping FAT methods well below multi-step baselines like TRADES (3.50h).

## Baselines & Comparisons

QUB-static improves PGD-10 robust accuracy over every baseline except FGSM-RS: FGSM-CKPT gains +4.22 pp (41.19→45.41), FGSM-GA +2.35 pp (49.89→52.24), N-FGSM +2.07 pp (49.12→51.19), ELLE-A +2.29 pp (47.91→50.20), Free-AT +1.40 pp (45.32→46.72), FGSM-UAP +0.87 pp (53.38→54.25), FGSM-PGI(MEP) +0.56 pp (53.43→53.99), and PGD-AT +1.59 pp (52.99→54.58). The QUB-decreasing variant recovers most of the standard-accuracy loss (e.g., FGSM-PGI(MEP) SA 81.56% vs. 80.45% for static) while retaining comparable robustness. FGSM-RS is the clear outlier: QUB-static drops PGD-10 by 4.32 pp (47.28→42.96) because the method's catastrophic overfitting produces misleading perturbations that QUB inadvertently smooths. Against multi-step baselines, QUB-augmented FAT methods remain competitive: FGSM-UAP+QUB-static (54.25% PGD-10) matches TRADES (54.25%) while training 6× faster (1.49h vs. 3.50h). On CIFAR-100 and Tiny ImageNet (Appendix F), the same pattern holds—QUB-decreasing consistently lifts PGD-10 by 1–10 pp across methods, with the largest gains on FGSM-CKPT (e.g., CIFAR-100: 12.68→24.97%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the adversarial training framework of Madry et al. (2018) and the fast adversarial training (FAT) paradigm of Shafahi et al. (2019), but departs from both by modifying the loss function rather than the attack procedure. Unlike prior FAT methods that add regularizers (FGSM-GA, ELLE, NuAT) or improve perturbation initialization (FGSM-PGI, FGSM-UAP), the QUB loss is derived as a provable quadratic upper bound on the cross-entropy loss via its convexity in the logit space, making it a principled drop-in replacement for the AT loss in any existing method. The decomposition of the bound into clean-loss, loss-increment, and logit-stability terms connects it conceptually to TRADES-style objectives, but the derivation is purely from first-order and second-order Taylor analysis rather than from an explicit trade-off formulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The QUB loss offers a low-cost, theoretically grounded way to improve robustness in FAT with minimal implementation overhead, and the consistent gains across many baselines and datasets make it a practical addition to the adversarial training toolkit. However, the improvements are modest (typically 1–3% in robust accuracy), the bound quality degrades with larger perturbation budgets, and the FGSM-RS failure case reveals that QUB can amplify the weaknesses of low-quality perturbation methods rather than compensate for them. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger architectures and datasets (e.g., ImageNet), whether the linear decreasing schedule is optimal, and whether the Hessian-norm bound of 1/2 can be tightened or made input-dependent for a tighter, more adaptive bound.
