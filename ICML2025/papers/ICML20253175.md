## Problem

MoE models require large VRAM to hold all expert weights, and existing offloading techniques that move experts to CPU memory shift the decoding bottleneck from memory-bound to I/O-bound, as PCIe bandwidth is orders of magnitude lower than GPU memory bandwidth. This makes on-the-fly (single-batch, latency-sensitive) inference impractical because expert loading overhead cannot be hidden by computation. The paper asks how to hide this I/O overhead within model computation while keeping generation quality degradation minimal.

## Input / Output

The system takes a text prompt and a MoE model (e.g., Mixtral-8×7B) whose expert weights reside in CPU DRAM, with only non-expert weights and a small expert cache in GPU VRAM (as low as 11GB). It produces generated text tokens at up to 91% of the speed of a fully GPU-resident quantized model. A key operational detail is that the current layer's hidden state is consumed by two lightweight predictors to prefetch the next layer's activated compressed experts, decoupling the routing and up-projection computation from the DRAM fetch.

## Method

FloE applies a hybrid compression to each expert's three projection matrices: contextual activation sparsity (magnitude-based pruning of low-salient channels) on the gate and down projections, and INT2 Half-Quadratic Quantization on the up projection, which is empirically and theoretically shown to be the least sensitive to both sparsification and quantization. To enable pipelining of DRAM-to-VRAM transfer with GPU computation, FloE introduces two predictors that exploit the high cosine similarity (>0.95) between consecutive layers' hidden states: a learning-based inter-expert predictor (small MLP) forecasts which experts the next layer will activate, and a parameter-free intra-expert predictor reuses the next layer's up projection with the current hidden state to precompute the sparsity mask. These predictors, combined with a compact asynchronous transfer scheme (co-located gate/down weight layout, AVX-512 SIMD, multi-threaded pinned-memory transfers) and a Triton-based sparse GEMV kernel, achieve 9.3× per-expert compression and 48.7× end-to-end speedup over DeepSpeed-MII on a single RTX 3090 with only 4.4%–7.6% average downstream accuracy loss.

## Results

On Mixtral-8×7B with a single GeForce RTX 3090 (24GB VRAM), FloE achieves a 48.7× end-to-end inference speedup over DeepSpeed-MII, 2.60× over Mixtral-Offloading, and 3.14× over Fiddler, while reaching 91% of the fully-GPU-resident Mixtral-GPU (HQQ INT2) speed. The system compresses expert parameters by 9.3× per expert and enables deployment on just 11GB VRAM (8.5× memory reduction). On downstream tasks (7 benchmarks), FloE-Wup at 80% sparsity achieves 0.693 average accuracy vs. 0.657 for CATS-80% and CHESS-80%, and at 90% sparsity FloE-Wup-90% (0.656) outperforms CATS-90% (0.559) and CHESS-90% (0.568) by 9.8%. The full FloE system (sparsity + INT2 quantization) incurs only 4.4%–7.6% average performance degradation relative to the FP16 baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FloE dominates all offloading-based baselines on speed: it is 48.7× faster than DeepSpeed-MII (which uses FP16 ZeRO-Infinity offloading), 2.60× faster than Mixtral-Offloading (which adds expert prediction and INT3 quantization), and 3.14× faster than Fiddler (CPU-GPU co-execution). Against the fully-GPU-resident Mixtral-GPU (HQQ INT2) lower-bound reference, FloE achieves 91% of its throughput (up to 95% for longer outputs), confirming that the on-the-fly overhead is nearly hidden. On accuracy, FloE-Wup (sparsity on up-projection only) outperforms CATS and CHESS at both 80% and 90% sparsity by 2.8% and 9.8% respectively, validating that up-projection sparsity is the least destructive pruning target. The full FloE system (sparsity + INT2 quantization on up-projection) still exceeds HQQ INT3 (0.676 vs. 0.665 at 80%) and dramatically surpasses uniform HQQ INT2 (0.676 vs. 0.311), demonstrating that the hybrid compression strategy avoids the catastrophic accuracy collapse of uniform ultra-low-bit quantization. On harder tasks (GSM8K, HumanEval, MMLU), FloE-80% retains 93% of the base model's average score while outperforming Mistral-7B and Llama-3.2-3B.

## Relation to Prior Work

FloE extends the MoE expert-offloading line (DeepSpeed-MII, Mixtral-Offloading, Fiddler) by shifting the compression target from the expert as a monolithic unit to its internal projection matrices, exploiting intra-expert activation sparsity (building on CATS, Deja Vu, TEAL) and selective ultra-low-bit quantization (HQQ). It departs from prior offloading systems that rely on inter-expert prediction alone by introducing a dual-predictor scheme—inter-expert (learning-based routing prediction) and intra-expert (parameter-free up-projection reuse)—that enables pipelined transfer-computation overlap for on-the-fly inference. The sparse GEMV kernel is directly adapted from CATS's Triton-based implementation, and the compact asynchronous transfer builds on standard AVX-512/SIMD techniques applied to the non-contiguous sparse weight layout.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FloE makes Mixtral-8×7B deployable on an 11GB consumer GPU with near-full-precision generation speed, a practically significant milestone for local MoE inference. The key empirical finding—that the up projection is simultaneously the least sensitive to both activation sparsity and ultra-low-bit quantization—provides a principled, architecture-aware compression recipe that could inform future MoE design. Limitations include evaluation primarily on Mixtral-8×7B (SwiGLU-based), a 4.4–7.6% average accuracy drop that may be unacceptable for some applications, and the approach's dependence on the specific SwiGLU structure (SiLU + Hadamard product) for the sparsity mechanism. Open questions include generalization to non-SwiGLU MoE architectures, larger MoE models with more experts per layer, and whether the predictor accuracy degrades under distribution shift.
