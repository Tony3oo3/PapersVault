## Problem

Existing multi-agent coordination frameworks such as Mixture of Agents (MoA) and Mixture of Experts (MoE) rely on static knowledge assumptions, making them unable to adapt to evolving expert capabilities or emerging task concepts. Moreover, MoA's computational cost scales linearly with the number of agents, and both frameworks lack semantic understanding of task-expert relationships, leading to redundant or conflicting expert contributions. The paper addresses how to dynamically select and coordinate specialized LLM experts in a way that is both semantically informed and computationally efficient.

## Input / Output

The system receives a free-form text instruction, which is parsed via NLP into a concept requirement vector over a predefined set of 12 knowledge concepts. Each of 24 experts (built from 6 open-source LLMs with tailored specialization prompts) is represented by a capability vector over the same concept space, and a knowledge graph encodes dependency edges between concepts. The output is a dynamically selected subset of top-3 experts (routed from top-2 concepts) whose individual responses are synthesized by a designated aggregator (Qwen2-72B-Instruct) into a single final text response, with semantic conflict detection and weighted fusion applied during aggregation.

## Method

KABB integrates three mechanisms into a closed-loop multi-agent pipeline. First, a knowledge distance metric Dist(S,t) combines five dimensions—task difficulty (log-scaled), semantic mismatch (1 − Jaccard overlap), dependency complexity (graph edge count normalized by expert pool size), historical effectiveness, and team synergy—weighted by learnable parameters summing to 1. Second, a dual adaptation mechanism updates Beta distribution parameters (α, β) for each expert subset using exponential time decay (γ^Δt = e^{−κΔt}) on historical counts plus an immediate feedback term and a knowledge-matching reward δ·KM(S,t), while simultaneously evolving the knowledge graph's concept relationships and synergy coefficients based on task outcomes. Third, a knowledge-aware Thompson Sampling strategy constructs a confidence score that multiplies the Beta expectation by an exponential knowledge-distance penalty, a time-decay factor, and a synergy term raised to a power η, enabling efficient top-k expert selection without exhaustive enumeration. Theoretical analysis provides an ε-approximate regret bound of O(√(T log T)) and proves pseudo-metric properties of the distance function, while an information-theoretic argument shows the knowledge graph reduces exploration complexity from O(K) to Õ(√(K log|C|)).

## Results

On AlpacaEval 2.0, KABB achieves a length-controlled (LC) win rate of 77.9%, a 9.8-point improvement over MoA (68.1%) while using only 2 experts versus MoA's 6 proposers. On MT-Bench, KABB attains a state-of-the-art average score of 9.65, with the KABB w/o Deepseek variant (9.47) exceeding GPT-4 Turbo (9.31). On reasoning benchmarks, KABB leads with 84.2% on BBH and 59.8% on MATH, surpassing MoA by +2.4% and +2.5% respectively. On Arena-Hard, KABB (74.8%) is competitive but trails GPT-4 Turbo (82.0%) and Deepseek-R1 (92.3%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

KABB decisively outperforms MoA (Wang et al., ICLR 2025) on AlpacaEval 2.0 LC win rate (77.9% vs. 68.1%) while using only 2 experts versus MoA's 6 proposers, and also leads on BBH (84.2% vs. 81.8%) and MATH (59.8% vs. 57.3%). Against proprietary GPT-4 variants, KABB w/o Deepseek (62.4% LC) surpasses GPT-4 Omni (57.5%), GPT-4 Turbo (55.0%), and GPT-4 Preview (50.0%), and on MT-Bench the KABB w/o Deepseek variant (9.47) exceeds GPT-4 Turbo (9.31). Among open-source individual models, KABB substantially outperforms Qwen2-72B-Instruct (38.1%), LLaMa-3-70B-Instruct (34.4%), Gemma-2-27B (44.9%), and WizardLM-2-8x22B (51.3%) on AlpacaEval 2.0. The KABB-Single-LLaMa3 variant (34.7%) marginally beats standalone LLaMa-3-70B-Instruct (34.4%), confirming that even a single-model KABB configuration benefits from the framework. On Arena-Hard, KABB (74.8%) trails GPT-4 Turbo (82.0%) and Deepseek-R1 (92.3%), and the KABB w/o Deepseek variant drops to 62.8%, highlighting the critical contribution of Deepseek models in the ensemble. Overall, KABB achieves the best cost-performance trade-off, requiring only 1/7 the cost of MoA for comparable LC win rates.

## Relation to Prior Work

KABB sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) Mixture-of-Agents/Experts frameworks (MoA, MoE) that coordinate multiple LLMs but assume static expert capabilities and uniform participation; (2) Multi-Armed Bandit algorithms (Thompson Sampling, UCB) that balance exploration and exploitation but rely solely on historical reward signals without semantic structure; and (3) knowledge graph representations that encode entity relationships but have not been integrated into sequential decision-making for agent selection. The paper's core departure is injecting a knowledge distance metric—combining Jaccard concept overlap, dependency path complexity, historical performance, and team synergy—directly into the Beta-distribution parameter updates and Thompson Sampling confidence scores, thereby making the bandit 'knowledge-aware' rather than purely reward-driven.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in the cost-performance trade-off: KABB with 2 selected experts outperforms MoA with 6 proposers by ~10% LC win rate at roughly 1/7 the inference cost, positioning it on a Pareto frontier that is attractive for deployment-constrained settings. However, several open questions remain: the framework's reliance on a predefined concept set (12 concepts, 24 experts) limits generalization to truly novel domains; the Arena-Hard results (74.8% vs. 92.3% for DeepSeek-R1) reveal that multi-agent coordination does not help on well-defined, deterministic problem-solving tasks where agent interference is detrimental; and the noted conciseness trade-off on FLASK-Hard suggests the aggregation step may over-elaborate. Future work on adaptive concept discovery, handling open-ended expert pools, and tighter theoretical guarantees (the convergence proof is a sketch) would strengthen the contribution.
