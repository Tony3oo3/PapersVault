## Problem

Diffusion-model-based super-resolution achieves strong perceptual quality but remains too memory- and compute-intensive for edge deployment. While binarization (1-bit quantization) and one-step distillation each reduce cost independently, naively combining them causes catastrophic model collapse, and the frequent dimension/resolution changes in UNet architectures make standard skip-connection auxiliary branches infeasible. No prior work has successfully unified both compression strategies into a single binarized one-step diffusion SR model.

## Input / Output

The model takes a single low-quality RGB image (generated via the RealESRGAN degradation pipeline) and produces a 4× super-resolved high-quality image in a single forward pass. During training, inputs are center-cropped to 64×64 patches; at inference the model operates on full images. All convolution and linear layers except the first two and last two are binarized to 1-bit weights and activations, with the first/last layers kept at full precision to avoid severe head/tail degradation.

## Method

BiMaCoSR decomposes each linear/convolutional layer into three branches: a Binarized Matrix Branch (BMB) performing XNOR-based 1-bit computation for high-frequency texture, a Low Rank Matrix Branch (LRMB) using rank-r (r=8) factorization initialized with the top-r SVD components to carry low-frequency information, and a Sparse Matrix Branch (SMB) storing only the top-k absolute-valued elements in COO format to absorb extreme outliers and provide high-rank representations. The branches are decoupled during initialization by subtracting the LRMB and SMB contributions from the weight matrix before binarization, so each branch handles a distinct frequency/rank regime. Built on the SinSR one-step diffusion backbone with ResShift as the distillation teacher, the model is trained with only a distillation loss for 100K iterations, achieving a 23.8× compression ratio and 27.4× speedup over the full-precision counterpart while matching or exceeding FP one-step quality on perceptual metrics.

## Results

On RealSR, BiMaCoSR (1/1 bits) achieves PSNR 26.84 dB, SSIM 0.7698, LPIPS 0.3375, FID 86.09, and CLIP-IQA+ 0.4800, outperforming all binarization baselines on every metric and even surpassing the full-precision SinSR on PSNR (+0.33 dB) and LPIPS (−0.026). On DRealSR, BiMaCoSR reaches PSNR 29.33 dB and LPIPS 0.3400, again leading all 1-bit methods. In terms of efficiency, BiMaCoSR requires only 1.83G FLOPs and 4.98M parameters (1 inference step), yielding a 27.4× compression ratio and 23.8× speedup over the full-precision SinSR backbone (50.23G FLOPs, 118.59M params).

## Baselines & Comparisons

BiMaCoSR decisively outperforms all five binarization baselines (XNOR, ReActNet, BBCU, ReSTE, BiDM) on every metric across all three datasets; for example, on RealSR it gains +0.24 dB PSNR over the strongest binarization method ReActNet and reduces LPIPS from 0.3834 to 0.3375. Against the full-precision SinSR backbone, BiMaCoSR actually surpasses it on distortion metrics (PSNR 26.84 vs 26.51, LPIPS 0.3375 vs 0.3635) while trailing on perceptual metrics (MUSIQ 49.01 vs 57.87, FID 86.09 vs 56.36, CLIP-IQA+ 0.4800 vs 0.5736), indicating that the 1-bit model preserves structural fidelity but loses some texture richness. Compared to the multi-step ResShift teacher (15 steps, 753.45G FLOPs), BiMaCoSR achieves comparable PSNR (26.84 vs 25.45) with a 27.4× FLOPs reduction. Among binarization methods, BiDM performs worst overall (PSNR 25.07, LPIPS 0.5042 on RealSR) and also has the highest parameter count (18.69M) and FLOPs (11.60G) among 1-bit methods, while BiMaCoSR uses fewer parameters (4.98M) and far fewer FLOPs (1.83G) than all other binarization baselines (5.83G). The gap between BiMaCoSR and the FP model is smallest on PSNR/SSIM but largest on FID and CLIP-IQA+, confirming that extreme quantization primarily degrades perceptual texture generation rather than structural accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two compression threads for diffusion-based super-resolution: binarization (BiDM, BI-DiffSR) and one-step distillation (SinSR, OSEDiff). Prior binarization work on diffusion models kept the multi-step inference loop, while prior one-step methods retained full-precision weights; BiMaCoSR is the first to combine both, using SinSR as its one-step backbone and XNOR-Net's sign-based convolution as the binarization primitive. The key architectural departure is replacing the naive skip-connection auxiliary branch with two purpose-built sub-branches—LRMB (inspired by LoRA, SVD-initialized to carry low-frequency content) and SMB (sparse matrix absorbing extreme values to restore high-rank information)—motivated by UNet's frequent dimension changes that break standard skip connections.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

A 23.8× parameter compression and 27.4× FLOP reduction while matching or exceeding the full-precision one-step model on distortion metrics (PSNR, SSIM) makes diffusion-based SR genuinely deployable on resource-constrained edge devices, a gap that has blocked practical adoption. The clean frequency-decoupling framework (low-freq → LRMB, high-freq → BMB, outliers → SMB) is a reusable design principle that could transfer to other binarized generative tasks. Limitations include evaluation on a single backbone (SinSR), a single upscale factor (×4), and retaining the first/last convolution layers at full precision; the authors themselves flag pruning + binarization as the next step for further compression, and generalization to other diffusion architectures (e.g., DiT-based) remains untested.
