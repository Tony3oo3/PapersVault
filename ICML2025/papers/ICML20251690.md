## Problem

Existing random defense methods inject randomness into the data (e.g., additive noise), making their hyperparameters data-dependent and difficult to generalize across datasets. Additionally, the trade-off between natural accuracy and adversarial robustness introduced by the randomness is largely unexplored in the random defense literature. This paper addresses both gaps by moving randomness from the data input into the network structure itself.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251690/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 224×224 for ImageNet) and outputs class labels, operating within a standard classification pipeline. The key unusual aspect is that during each forward pass, a new random deformed kernel is independently sampled for every DCS layer, meaning the same input traverses a different computational path each time. The DCS layer replaces a single standard convolutional layer (typically the second conv layer) while preserving the output feature map dimensions via matched stride and padding.

## Method

DCS replaces the fixed offsets in deformable convolution with randomly sampled binary masks: at each forward pass, n points are uniformly sampled from a k×k boundary to form the deformed kernel, creating a random space of inference paths. The paper derives two data-independent bounds on the receptive field n: an upper bound (Lemma 1) ensuring gradient cosine similarity between attacked and inference paths stays below ε_c (improving robustness), and a lower bound (Lemma 2) ensuring the L1 output inconsistency stays below ε_l (preserving natural accuracy). The intersection of these bounds yields a dataset-agnostic constraint on n. On top of this, Gradient-Selective Adversarial Training (GSAT) further reduces attack transferability by identifying the points selected by the attacked path (X^s) during PGD, masking them to zero, and re-sampling kernels before the weight update, effectively removing the most transferable gradient sources from training.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with WRN-34, DCS achieves 75.84% robust accuracy under PGD-20 and 75.46% under AutoAttack, with 90.58% natural accuracy. On CIFAR-100 with WRN-34, DCS reaches 48.05% PGD-20 and 52.36% AA robust accuracy with 66.69% natural accuracy. On ImageNet with ResNet-50, DCS attains 52.38% PGD and 66.79% AA robust accuracy. DCS also demonstrates strong black-box robustness, maintaining 80.53% (SQUARE) and 84.95% (FGSM) on CIFAR-10 with ResNet-18.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DCS outperforms all AT-based baselines by large margins: it exceeds Overfit (Rice et al., 2020) by 20.8% on CIFAR-10 PGD (75.84 vs 55.06) and 22.5% on ImageNet PGD (52.38 vs 39.85), and surpasses DWQ (Fu et al., 2021) by 23.7% on CIFAR-10 PGD. Among random-noise defenses, DCS beats AdditiveNoise (Li et al., 2019) by 13.5% on CIFAR-10 PGD and 17.0% on AA, and outperforms DF (Chen & Lee, 2024) by 26.2% on CIFAR-10 AA. DCS also surpasses the certified defense Cert-RA (Chiang et al., 2020) by 7.2% on CIFAR-10 PGD. The closest competitor is LWTA (Panousis et al., 2021), which achieves 81.87% on CIFAR-10 PGD (6.0% higher than DCS) but DCS edges it out on AA (75.46 vs 74.71). On ImageNet, DCS achieves the best AA robustness (66.79%) among all reported methods, exceeding MeanSparse (Amini et al., 2024) by 7.2% and DF by 26.2%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends deformable convolutional networks (Dai et al., 2017; Zhu et al., 2018) by replacing their learnable spatial offsets with randomly sampled binary masks, creating a structurally stochastic convolution layer (DCS). It departs from the dominant random-defense paradigm of injecting additive noise into data or feature maps (Xie et al., 2017; Li et al., 2019; Carbone et al., 2021) by localizing randomness entirely within the network architecture, making the defense hyperparameter (receptive field n) data-independent. The adversarial training component (GSAT) builds on PGD-based AT (Madry et al., 2017) but selectively masks gradient-contributing points identified during the attack phase, a mechanism not present in prior random-structure defenses like LWTA (Panousis et al., 2021) or random normalization (Dong et al., 2022).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first data-independent theoretical bounds (Lemma 1 and Lemma 2) that simultaneously constrain the random-defense hyperparameter for both robustness (gradient similarity) and clean accuracy (output consistency), addressing a trade-off that prior random defenses have largely ignored. Practically, DCS achieves competitive robustness across CIFAR-10/100 and ImageNet with modest training overhead (~78% more), and the framework is architecture-agnostic for any CNN. However, the method is inherently tied to convolutional operations (the ViT experiment only replaces a patch-embedding conv), the theoretical bounds are loose in practice, and the approach cannot reduce gradient similarity to zero due to the accuracy trade-off. Open questions include whether the data-independent bounds transfer to non-convolutional backbones and whether the GSAT masking strategy generalizes beyond the specific layer positions tested.
