## Problem

Existing Riemannian stochastic variance reduction methods (R-SVRG, R-SRG, R-SPIDER, R-SRM) each require separate theoretical analyses, and no hybrid estimator combining SVRG-type and SARAH-type components has been developed for the Riemannian setting. The paper asks whether a single unified formulation and convergence framework can encompass all these methods under retraction and vector transport, while also achieving the optimal IFO complexity lower bound.

## Input / Output

The algorithms accept a smooth non-convex objective on a Riemannian manifold (Grassmann or SPD) in either finite-sum form (n component losses) or online form, along with an initial manifold point and hyperparameters (step size η, momentum β, batch sizes b and b′). They output a randomly selected iterate x_ζ from the trajectory satisfying E[‖grad f(x_ζ)‖] ≤ ε, with IFO complexity O(min{n^{1/2}ε^{-2}, ε^{-3}}) for finite-sum and O(ε^{-3}) for online settings. All operations use retraction and vector transport rather than the more expensive exponential map and parallel transport.

## Method

The paper introduces the SRVRG estimator, which fuses a SVRG-type correction term u_k^s with a SARAH-type recursive difference (1−β)(w_k − w_{k−1}) under a momentum coefficient β, enabling larger β values for faster variance decay. This is generalized into the Stochastic Recursive Gradient Estimator (SRGE), a single template v_k = (1−β)v_{k−1} + βu_k + (1−β)(w_k − w_{k−1}) that recovers SGD, SVRG, SARAH, STORM, Hybrid-SGD, SVRRM, and SRVRG as special cases (Table 1). Extending SRGE to Riemannian manifolds via vector transport yields the SERENA framework, which also subsumes R-SGD, R-SVRG, R-SRG, R-SRM, and R-PAGE. A unified variance upper bound (Theorem 5.1) under retraction and vector transport then yields convergence guarantees and IFO complexities for all member algorithms in one pass, with R-SRVRG and R-SVRRM matching the known lower bound.

## Results

The proposed R-SRVRG and R-SVRRM achieve an IFO complexity of O(min{n^{1/2}ε^{-2}, ε^{-3}}) in the finite-sum setting and O(ε^{-3}) in the online setting, both matching the known lower bounds for stochastic non-convex optimization. R-Hybrid-SGD achieves O(min{n, ε^{-1}} + ε^{-3}) for finite-sum and O(ε^{-3}) for online. Experimentally, across six tasks (PCA on MNIST and ijcnn1, LRMC on MovieLens-1M and Jester, RC on Extended Yale B and Kylberg), R-SRVRG and R-SVRRM consistently achieve the lowest optimality gap or test MSE at a given IFO budget, outperforming all compared Riemannian methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against eight Riemannian baselines: R-SD, R-CG (deterministic), R-SGD, R-SRM, R-SPIDER, R-AbaSRG, R-AbaSVRG, and R-PAGE. In all six experimental settings (PCA on MNIST/ijcnn1, LRMC on MovieLens-1M/Jester, RC on YaleB/Kylberg), the proposed R-SRVRG and R-SVRRM (dashed lines) achieve the fastest convergence in optimality gap or test MSE, clearly outperforming R-SPIDER, R-AbaSRG, R-AbaSVRG, R-PAGE, R-SGD, and R-SRM. R-Hybrid-SGD also outperforms R-SRM, confirming the benefit of the hybrid estimator design. In the theoretical comparison (Table 2), R-SRVRG and R-SVRRM improve upon R-SVRG's O(n^{2/3}ε^{-2}) finite-sum complexity to O(min{n^{1/2}ε^{-2}, ε^{-3}}), and match the best-known O(ε^{-3}) online complexity of R-SPIDER and R-AbaSRG while providing a unified framework that subsumes all these methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies the landscape of stochastic variance reduction estimators—SVRG, SARAH, STORM, Hybrid-SGD, and SVRRM—under a single general form called the Stochastic Recursive Gradient Estimator (SRGE), then extends this to Riemannian manifolds as the SERENA framework. The key conceptual departure from prior work is the integration of an SVRG-type estimator with a SARAH-type recursive term within a single hybrid estimator (SRVRG), which had not been previously constructed in either Euclidean or Riemannian settings. The theoretical analysis is positioned as more general than prior Riemannian VR analyses by working under retraction and vector transport rather than the more restrictive exponential map and parallel transport, and it subsumes convergence results for R-SVRG, R-SRG, R-SRM, R-SPIDER, and R-PAGE within one theorem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified SERENA framework provides a single theoretical lens for analyzing a family of Riemannian stochastic algorithms, reducing the need for ad hoc Lyapunov constructions and making it straightforward to design new estimators by choosing parameters in the general form. Achieving the optimal O(min{n^{1/2}ε^{-2}, ε^{-3}}) IFO complexity for finite-sum and O(ε^{-3}) for online settings confirms that the proposed R-SRVRG and R-SVRRM are computationally optimal. However, the framework is currently limited to smooth objectives, and the authors identify extending to non-smooth problems and incorporating second-order information (e.g., R-SQN-VR, R-SVRC) as important open directions. For practitioners working on manifold-constrained problems (PCA, matrix completion, SPD geometry), the practical gain is modest but consistent across tasks.
