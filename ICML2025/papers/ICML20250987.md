## Problem

Existing universal adversarial perturbation (UAP) methods for CLIP have not achieved 'super transferability'—a single perturbation that simultaneously transfers across data, domains, models, and tasks. Prior ensemble-based transfer attacks rely on fixed surrogate sets that become computationally prohibitive to scale, leaving open whether super transferability is achievable and how it scales with the number of surrogate models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250987/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a surrogate dataset (default: ImageNet at 224×224) and a search space of N diverse pre-trained CLIP encoders (16, 32, or 64), and produces a single L∞-bounded perturbation vector (ε=12/255) that is added to any input image. For targeted attacks, the adversary additionally specifies a target text description. The output is one universal perturbation (or targeted UAP) applicable to all samples, datasets, victim encoders, and downstream tasks without per-sample optimization.

## Method

X-Transfer's core innovation is an efficient surrogate scaling strategy that, at each PGD optimization step, dynamically selects only k ≪ N surrogate encoders from a large search space using a UCB multi-armed bandit rule. Each encoder is treated as an arm whose reward is its current adversarial loss: encoders that are harder to fool receive higher selection priority, while already-fooled encoders are explored less. The adversarial objective is a generic embedding-space similarity loss (minimizing cosine similarity between clean and perturbed image embeddings for non-targeted, or maximizing similarity to a target text embedding for targeted) that is agnostic to architecture, embedding dimension, and pre-training loss, enabling heterogeneous CLIP encoders to be ensembled without architectural assumptions. This reduces per-step computation to roughly k/N of standard full-ensemble scaling while achieving comparable or superior transferability, and the super transferability scales with the total search space size N rather than the per-step selection count k.

## Results

On zero-shot classification averaged over 9 black-box CLIP victim encoders, X-Transfer (Large, N=64) achieves 75.6% average ASR, substantially outperforming the best baseline C-PGC (ViT-B/16-Flicker) at 51.6% and Meta-UAP (Ensemble) at 50.8%. On image-text retrieval (MSCOCO), X-Transfer Large reaches 71.8% TR@1 and 65.8% IR@1, compared to 43.8% and 35.7% for C-PGC. On large VLMs (Table 2), X-Transfer Large achieves up to 64.1% ASR on BLIP2 image captioning (MSCOCO) and 53.7% on BLIP2 VQA (OK-VQA), far exceeding the best baselines (ETU at 8.5% and 15.1% respectively). The super transferability scales with search space size: Base (N=16) → 69.2%, Mid (N=32) → 73.6%, Large (N=64) → 75.6% average ASR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

X-Transfer Large (N=64) outperforms all six baselines by a wide margin on every metric. Against the strongest CLIP-specific baseline C-PGC (ViT-B/16-Flicker, 51.6% avg ASR), X-Transfer Large gains +24.0 points; against Meta-UAP Ensemble (50.8%), the gap is +24.8 points. Even the vanilla single-surrogate version of X-Transfer (48.4% avg ASR) already matches or exceeds most baselines, indicating the generic embedding-space objective is well-suited for CLIP. On image-text retrieval, X-Transfer Large (71.8% TR@1) nearly doubles the best baseline C-PGC (43.8% TR@1). On large VLMs (Table 2), X-Transfer Large dominates across all four models: e.g., on BLIP2/MSCOCO captioning it reaches 64.1% ASR vs. 13.0% for C-PGC and 8.5% for ETU. Notably, CLIP-specific baselines (AdvCLIP, ETU, C-PGC) show no consistent advantage over image-classifier baselines (GD-UAP, TRM-UAP, Meta-UAP), suggesting that the ensemble scaling strategy—not the CLIP-specific loss—is the key driver of super transferability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the universal adversarial perturbation (UAP) literature from image classifiers to CLIP-based vision-language models, building on the foundational UAP formulation of Moosavi-Dezfooli et al. (2017) and the ensemble-based transfer attack paradigm of Liu et al. (2017) and Dong et al. (2018). Prior CLIP-specific UAP methods (AdvCLIP, ETU, C-PGC) rely on single or fixed surrogate encoders, while standard ensemble scaling becomes computationally prohibitive as the number of surrogates grows. X-Transfer departs from both by introducing an efficient surrogate scaling strategy based on UCB multi-armed bandit selection, dynamically choosing a small subset of encoders from a large search space at each optimisation step, and by framing the goal as simultaneous cross-data, cross-domain, cross-model, and cross-task transferability ('super transferability') under a strict black-box setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Because CLIP encoders form the visual backbone of widely deployed VLMs (LLaVA, BLIP2, MiniGPT-4, OpenFlamingo), the demonstration that a single L∞-bounded perturbation can degrade performance across multiple VLMs and tasks (captioning, VQA, retrieval, classification) exposes a concrete and realistic safety threat that adversaries could exploit using only publicly available open-source encoders. The paper shows that adversarial fine-tuning does not provide robustness against alternative perturbation types (L2, patches), leaving the defense landscape largely open. The introduction of X-TransferBench as an open-source collection of UAPs and TUAPs provides a much-needed standardised resource for evaluating defenses. Open questions include whether the observed semantic structure in non-targeted UAPs can be leveraged for detection, and whether any practical defense can simultaneously resist cross-model and cross-task transfer.
