## Problem

Existing randomized response (RR) mechanisms for label differential privacy optimize privacy solely for the binary response variable while ignoring the influence of covariates, and they do not address optimality or statistical inference for binary response models. This paper fills that gap by deriving optimal LabelDP RR mechanisms that incorporate covariate structure and enable both precise estimation and valid private confidence intervals.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20253005/cropped_page3_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes n i.i.d. observations consisting of d-dimensional non-sensitive covariates X_i and sensitive binary labels Y_i^*, along with user-specified privacy budgets (ε, δ). It outputs a closed-form optimal 2×2 RR design matrix P (specifying flip probabilities p00 and p11), maximum likelihood estimates β̂ of the regression coefficients, and asymptotically valid (1−α)-confidence intervals for each coefficient. The RR design matrix is determined by comparing the trace of the Fisher Information Matrix at a small set of candidate boundary points of the LabelDP feasible region, requiring no direct access to private labels during selection.

## Method

The paper embeds the RR mechanism directly into a generalized linear model by rewriting the conditional mean as E(Y_i|X_i) = 1 − p00 + (p00 + p11 − 1)·G(β*ᵀX_i), so that MLE on the privatized data requires no separate debiasing step. Optimality is defined via T-optimality: maximizing the trace of the Fisher Information Matrix tr[I(β; p00, p11)] over the LabelDP feasible region R (for ε-LabelDP) or R′ (for (ε,δ)-LabelDP). The key theoretical result is that the trace is monotonically increasing in both p00 and p11 within the feasible region, forcing the optimum onto the boundary, which yields closed-form design matrices: P_ε = diag(e^ε/(e^ε+1), e^ε/(e^ε+1)) for pure ε-LabelDP, and a three-case selection among boundary points for (ε,δ)-LabelDP. Asymptotic normality of β̂ is established under standard regularity conditions, enabling construction of private confidence intervals with nominal coverage 1−α that preserve the LabelDP guarantee throughout.

## Results

Across simulation scenarios (d=4, n=10⁵, B=500 replications) with ε ∈ {0.05, 0.06, 0.07, 0.08, 0.09, 0.1, 0.5, 0.7, 1} and δ ∈ {0, 10⁻⁵}, the proposed ORRbR method achieves log MSE of approximately 0 at ε=0.05 (δ=0) and drops to approximately −15 at ε=0.7 (δ=0) under Scenario I with logistic regression. The paper reports a threefold improvement in coverage probability within the 95% confidence interval compared to the traditional RRbR baseline, closely matching the non-private NP method. On the real-world plagiarism dataset (n=474), ORRbR consistently achieves lower MSE than RRbR across all tested ε levels, with MSE decreasing further as ε increases.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two baselines: NP (non-private RR binary response from Fox et al., 2018) and RRbR (traditional optimal DP RR mechanism from Holohan et al., 2017 applied solely to the response variable). As expected, NP achieves the smallest MSE and most accurate coverage probability since it imposes no privacy constraint. The proposed ORRbR method outperforms RRbR on both key metrics—MSE and coverage probability—across all scenarios (independent and dependent covariate structures), both link functions (logistic and probit), and both privacy settings (ε-LabelDP and (ε,δ)-LabelDP). The advantage of ORRbR over RRbR stems from incorporating covariate information X through Fisher Information maximization, whereas RRbR ignores covariates. Under very small ε (e.g., 0.05), ORRbR shows higher coverage than NP due to the conservative noise introduced by stringent privacy, but as ε increases, all methods converge toward NP-level accuracy. On the real plagiarism dataset, ORRbR achieves lower prediction MSE than RRbR at every ε level tested, confirming the simulation findings in a practical setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the optimal randomized response (RR) mechanism line initiated by Warner (1965) and refined by Holohan et al. (2017), who minimized estimator variance for the binary response alone. The key departure is incorporating covariate information through the T-optimality criterion (maximizing the trace of the Fisher Information Matrix, borrowed from optimal experimental design à la Pukelsheim, 2006), which prior LabelDP RR work ignored. The paper also extends the privacy framework from pure ε-LabelDP to (ε,δ)-LabelDP and adds private confidence interval construction, going beyond the point-estimation focus of Holohan et al. (2017) and the non-optimal inference of Fox et al. (2018).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first closed-form optimal RR design matrices under both ε- and (ε,δ)-LabelDP for binary response models with covariates, along with asymptotically valid private confidence intervals—filling a gap between privacy-preserving estimation and formal statistical inference. The T-optimality criterion yields O(d) computational complexity versus O(d³) for D-optimality, making the approach practical. Open questions include extension to multiclass k-RR mechanisms, compatibility with Rényi or Gaussian DP, and whether the covariate-aware optimality criterion generalizes to nonparametric or high-dimensional settings. The parametric GLM assumption and binary-response restriction limit immediate applicability to more complex private learning tasks.
