## Problem

Standard LLM leaderboards (e.g., Chatbot Arena) aggregate millions of human preference votes into a single averaged ranking, obscuring the fact that different models excel on different prompts. Stratifying the data by category to recover prompt-specific rankings is impractical—fine-grained categories like SQL table joins would require millions of additional votes to collect. The paper asks whether one can produce a reliable leaderboard for any individual prompt or arbitrary set of prompts from a single large preference dataset.

## Input / Output

The model takes a natural-language prompt z and a two-hot encoding x of the model pair (a length-M vector with +1 at the 'B' position and −1 at the 'A' position) and outputs an M-dimensional vector of Bradley-Terry coefficients θ̂(z), from which pairwise win rates are computed as σ(xᵀθ̂(z)). For aggregation over a distribution of prompts Q (e.g., a user's chat history), the method simulates the data-generating process using the learned θ̂ and fits a new BT model to the synthetic votes, yielding a single leaderboard for Q without requiring additional human labels. The grounded Rao-Kupper variant additionally outputs a tie coefficient and a 'both bad' probability, giving an absolute quality signal rather than purely comparative rankings.

## Method

P2L trains a pre-trained instruction-tuned LLM (SmolLM2 or Qwen2.5, 135M–7B parameters) by replacing the language-model head with a randomly initialized linear layer that outputs M coefficients, then fine-tunes all parameters via SGD on 1.5M Chatbot Arena preference pairs using binary cross-entropy loss for one epoch. The key insight is that P2L is a strict generalization of marginal BT regression: choosing the function class Θ to be constant functions recovers the standard averaged leaderboard, while using an LLM as the function approximator captures prompt-conditional heterogeneity in model performance. The paper proves (Theorem 1) that under the BT model, the two natural definitions of an 'optimal router' (maximize win rate vs. maximize BT coefficient) are equivalent and reduce to a linear program, enabling cost-constrained routing with a known Pareto frontier. The framework extends to the Prompt-to-Regression (P2R) setting, accommodating ties via a Rao-Kupper model and 'both bad' votes via a novel grounded Rao-Kupper model that introduces a fictitious zero-coefficient model as a quality anchor.

## Results

On LiveBench, P2L-7B achieves an overall score of 59.3, surpassing the best static model (claude-3-5-sonnet-20240620 at 59.2) and outperforming all other models including GPT-4o (52.8) and Llama-3.1-70B (42.4). On Chatbot Arena, the P2L-1.5B router reached the #1 spot with a score of 1389, exceeding the best static model Gemini-exp-1206 (1364) by 25 points overall and by 51 points in worst-case category performance. For preference prediction accuracy (grounded Rao-Kupper 4-class), P2L-7B achieves 47.88% versus 37.40% for marginal BT regression and 25.00% for random guessing.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On LiveBench, P2L-7B (59.3) narrowly edges out claude-3-5-sonnet-20240620 (59.2) and claude-3-5-sonnet-20241022 (59.0), while substantially outperforming gpt-4o-2024-05-13 (52.8, +6.5), qwen2.5-72b-instruct (52.6, +6.7), gemini-1.5-pro-001 (44.2, +15.1), and llama-3.1-70b-instruct (42.4, +16.9). Even the smaller P2L-1.5B (58.4) and P2L-0.5B (57.0) surpass all static models except the two Claude 3.5 Sonnet variants. On Chatbot Arena, the P2L-1.5B router achieved a score of 1389 versus 1364 for the best static model Gemini-exp-1206, a 25-point margin that expanded to 51 points in worst-case category performance. Against the marginal BT regression baseline, P2L-7B improves preference prediction accuracy from 37.40% to 47.88% (+10.5 points), and the validation loss gap between P2L and marginal BT is several times larger than the gap between marginal BT and a constant predictor. The paper also shows that P2L's aggregated leaderboards converge to near-optimal accuracy within 100–250 prompts, whereas marginal regression requires thousands of prompts for a stable ranking.

## Relation to Prior Work

P2L is explicitly positioned as a subclass of varying-coefficient models (Hastie & Tibshirani, 1993), but departs fundamentally by parameterizing the coefficient function with a foundation model (LLM) and training end-to-end via backpropagation, whereas prior varying-coefficient methods rely on bespoke fitting procedures and simpler statistical learners. The statistical backbone draws on the Bradley-Terry (1952) and Rao-Kupper (1967) paired-comparison models, extended here with a novel 'grounded Rao-Kupper' model inspired by Plackett-Luce to handle the 'Tie (both bad)' category. On the routing side, P2L generalizes prior LLM routing work (RouteLLM, AutoMix, RouterDC) that trains binary or small-set classifiers into a parametric function mapping prompts to full M-dimensional leaderboards, enabling cost-aware optimization via a clean linear program (Theorem 1). The meta-learning framing (training a model to output statistical model parameters) connects to the meta-learning literature but with an extremely simple learner (a BT regression) rather than a deep network.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

P2L addresses a practical bottleneck in LLM evaluation: stratified BT regression requires thousands of votes per category, making fine-grained (per-prompt or per-use-case) leaderboards infeasible, while P2L converges to near-optimal aggregated leaderboards within 100–250 prompts. The #1 Chatbot Arena deployment (25 points above the best static model) and the LiveBench OOD result (surpassing a static oracle that has access to ground-truth labels) demonstrate that preference-trained routing generalizes well, with implications for cost-efficient inference at scale. Open questions include the sensitivity of the routing optimality guarantee to violations of the BT transitivity assumption, whether the observed power-law scaling of prompt-specific evaluation quality persists as model and data scale further, and how the method extends to multi-turn or agentic settings where the 'prompt' is a conversation history rather than a single query.
