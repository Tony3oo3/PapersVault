## Problem

Offline reinforcement learning policies suffer from distribution shift between the static offline dataset and the dynamic online environment, leading to out-of-distribution (OOD) state-action pairs that degrade performance. Existing conservative and uncertainty-based methods remain locked to the offline dataset and cannot adapt at test time, while online RL requires reward signals that are often unavailable or impractical to obtain in real-world deployments.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252801/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes unlabeled state observations from the online test environment (no rewards required) along with a frozen pre-trained offline policy. For discrete control, the policy outputs a categorical distribution over a finite action set; for continuous control, it outputs the mean and variance of a Gaussian over the action space. The only parameters updated are the layer normalization weights and biases, keeping the rest of the network frozen to preserve pre-training knowledge while enabling lightweight adaptation.

## Method

TARL constructs task-specific unsupervised test-time objectives: for discrete control, it minimizes the Shannon entropy of predicted action probabilities (with a threshold filter to retain only high-confidence samples); for continuous control, it minimizes the differential entropy of the policy's Gaussian output (selecting top-k lowest-variance samples from a replay buffer). A KL divergence term between the frozen original policy and the test-time updated policy acts as a debiasing regularizer, preventing overfitting and error accumulation during iterative adaptation. The combined loss L^t = L_ent^t + λ·L_kl^t is optimized by updating only layer normalization parameters, making the adaptation computationally cheap while preserving the conservative structure of the offline-trained policy. This plug-and-play mechanism can be attached to any existing offline RL algorithm (CQL, IQL, QR-DQN, REM) without modifying their training procedures.

## Results

On the Atari discrete control benchmark, TARL consistently improves all three baselines: CQL(TARL) reaches 4602.67 on Qbert (vs. 4334.64 for CQL), QR-DQN(TARL) jumps from 503.38 to 819.24 on Asterix, and REM(TARL) gains from 914.38 to 920.18 on Qbert. On the D4RL continuous control benchmark, the headline result is a 13.6% relative episode return increase on hopper-expert-v2 (CQL: 99.72 → CQL(TARL): 113.34), with additional gains on walker2d-fully-replay-v2 (95.31 → 97.99) and IQL on Walker2d-medium-v2 (79.92 → 82.43). All improvements are achieved by updating only layer normalization parameters with unsupervised entropy minimization and KL-divergence regularization, without requiring environmental rewards.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TARL is evaluated as a plug-in module on top of four offline RL baselines: REM, QR-DQN, CQL (Atari), and IQL (D4RL). On Atari, TARL wins on every task for every baseline: the largest gain is QR-DQN on Asterix (503.38 → 819.24, +62.7%), followed by CQL on Qbert (4334.64 → 4602.67, +6.2%) and Pong (1.02 → 2.08, +104%). On D4RL, CQL(TARL) outperforms CQL on all three tasks, with the standout 13.6% relative improvement on hopper-expert-v2 (99.72 → 113.34) and a 2.8% gain on walker2d-fully-replay-v2 (95.31 → 97.99). IQL(TARL) similarly beats IQL on both Walker2d tasks (medium: 79.92 → 82.43; expert: 110.31 → 110.49). The paper does not report any case where TARL degrades performance relative to the base algorithm, though gains on expert-level D4RL tasks are marginal (0.18–0.32 points), suggesting the method is most impactful when the offline-online distribution shift is pronounced.

## Relation to Prior Work

TARL repurposes the entropy-minimization test-time adaptation framework from vision (TENT, Wang et al. 2020) and transplants it into the offline RL setting, where the 'prediction' being sharpened is the policy's action distribution rather than a class label. It departs from the dominant conservative-estimation lineage (CQL, IQL, QR-DQN) that treats the offline dataset as a fixed, closed source of information, by introducing a lightweight test-time feedback loop that requires no reward signal. The KL-divergence debiasing term and the restriction of updates to layer-normalization parameters are the paper's specific contributions on top of the TTA template, addressing the overfitting and error-accumulation risks that naive entropy minimization would introduce in an iterative RL deployment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical appeal is clear: a practitioner deploying a CQL or IQL policy in a real environment can plug in TARL with a few hyperparameters and a replay buffer, gaining a modest but consistent return improvement without any online reward infrastructure. However, the evaluation is narrow (five Atari games, three D4RL locomotion tasks), several improvements are small in absolute terms (e.g., Pong: 1.02 → 2.08), and the method's reliance on hand-tuned entropy thresholds and KL weights limits its plug-and-play generality. Open questions include whether the layer-norm-only update strategy transfers to model-based offline RL, multi-agent settings, or high-dimensional continuous control beyond locomotion, and whether the unsupervised entropy objective remains informative when the test distribution is adversarially shifted rather than merely shifted.
