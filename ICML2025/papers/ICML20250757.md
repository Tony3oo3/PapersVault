## Problem

Multimodal contrastive pretraining (e.g., CLIP) has transformed medical imaging by leveraging paired text reports to guide representation learning, yet this approach remains entirely unexplored for functional brain data. EEG clinical phenotyping is bottlenecked by scarce annotations and low signal-to-noise that hinder EEG-only self-supervised methods, while the long, multi-paragraph format of clinical EEG reports introduces unique alignment challenges absent in image-caption settings.

## Input / Output

The model consumes EEG time series cropped into non-overlapping segments (5–60 s, 20-channel TCP montage at 100 Hz) paired with clinical report text segmented by heading into four content clusters (clinical history, record description, medication, interpretation) or summarized into a single sentence by a local Llama-3 8B model. At inference, the frozen EEG encoder produces a 256-dimensional embedding per crop; pathology classification uses either a linear probe on these embeddings or zero-shot cosine similarity against 21 text prompts (e.g., 'The EEG is normal'), while retrieval tasks rank candidate EEG recordings or reports by cross-modal embedding similarity. No text is required at test time for classification, matching standard clinical EEG workflow.

## Method

The core contribution is sub-unit alignment: rather than aligning an entire EEG recording to an entire report, the method crops EEG into multiple segments and segments reports into paragraphs, then aligns these sub-units in a shared latent space. Two alignment strategies are explored—ELM_{e,l} (CLIP-style bidirectional InfoNCE with learned projectors on both modalities) and ELM_l (projecting EEG into a frozen MedCPT language model's output space with a composite alignment-plus-orthogonality loss). The key architectural extension is MIL-InfoNCE, which relaxes strict one-to-one pairing by sampling up to N positive EEG crops per text segment and M positive text segments per EEG crop, averaging their log-probabilities in the InfoNCE numerator to model P(e|l) and P(l|e) distributions. This bidirectional multiple-positive formulation, combined with a small 0.93M-parameter residual CNN EEG encoder and a frozen 768-dim language encoder, yields state-of-the-art pathology detection with up to 9.7% AUROC gains over EEG-only SSL at 1% labels and enables zero-shot classification and cross-modal retrieval for the first time in clinical EEG.

## Results

On TUAB abnormality classification, ELM-MIL (bidirectional) achieves 84.31% balanced accuracy zero-shot (no labels required) and 87.11% at 100% labels, outperforming the best EEG-only baseline (Temporal Shuffling: 84.10% at 100%) and the supervised reference (84.13%). At the critical 1% label setting, ELM-MIL reaches 83.10% BAcc and 91.54% AUROC, yielding improvements of ~8.7% BAcc and ~9.7% AUROC over the strongest EEG-only method. On the out-of-distribution NMT dataset, ELM-MIL maintains a ≥3.8% AUROC advantage at 1% labels. With only 0.93M trainable parameters and no finetuning, ELM-MIL also surpasses the 369M-parameter LaBraM-Huge foundation model by +1.84% BAcc on crop-level TUAB classification.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ELM-MIL (bidirectional) outperforms all six EEG-only SSL baselines (BYOL, VICReg, ContraWR, RP, TS, CPC) across every label budget on TUAB, with the largest margins at 1% labels where it gains ~8.7% BAcc and ~9.7% AUROC over the best EEG-only method (TS: 74.99% BAcc / 82.51% AUROC vs. ELM-MIL's 83.10% / 91.54%). ELM-MIL also surpasses the fully supervised baseline at 1% and 10% labels (83.10% vs. 71.36% BAcc at 1%), demonstrating that multimodal pretraining extracts more clinically relevant structure than direct label-driven training in low-data regimes. On the out-of-distribution NMT dataset, ELM-MIL leads all EEG-only methods by at least 3.8% AUROC at 1% labels (69.49% vs. 65.72% for ContraWR). In the cross-method comparison on crop-level TUAB (Table 4), ELM-MIL with 0.93M parameters and no finetuning achieves 84.42% BAcc, exceeding LaBraM-Huge (369M params, finetuned) by +1.84% BAcc while trailing by only −0.18% AUROC. On seizure detection (TUSZ) and 6-class event classification (TUEV), ELM-MIL again leads all EEG-only baselines at 100% labels (91.51% vs. 89.77% AUROC on TUSZ; 87.69% vs. 86.28% on TUEV), though the gap narrows compared to the recording-level task.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper transplants the CLIP-style contrastive image-text alignment paradigm (Radford et al., 2021) into the EEG domain, adapting it to the fundamentally different structure of long time-series signals and multi-paragraph clinical reports through sub-unit cropping and text segmentation. It builds on M-FLAG (Liu et al., 2023a) for the frozen-language-encoder variant with orthogonal regularization, and extends the MIL-NCE loss from video-text alignment (Miech et al., 2020) to accommodate multiple positive EEG-text pairs, relaxing the strict one-to-one alignment assumption. Conceptually, it departs from prior EEG self-supervised learning (BYOL, VICReg, ContraWR, temporal-ordering methods) by introducing an external text modality as the alignment signal rather than relying on data augmentations or temporal structure alone, and from prior medical vision-language work (which targeted ECG, X-ray, CT, and structural MRI) by being the first to apply this framework to functional brain data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a 0.93M-parameter EEG encoder pretrained with clinical reports can match or exceed 369M-parameter EEG foundation models (LaBraM-Huge) on pathology detection without finetuning, while enabling entirely new capabilities: zero-shot classification via text prompts and bidirectional EEG-report retrieval. The label-efficiency gains (up to 9.7% AUROC at 1% labels) are directly relevant to clinical settings where annotated data is scarce. Key open questions include whether the approach scales with larger paired EEG-report corpora (currently limited to ~15K recordings from a single hospital), how to handle the class imbalance and heterogeneity of real-world reports at scale, and whether the learned representations transfer across recording protocols and populations beyond the TUEG/NMT validation. The finding that even semantically irrelevant (shuffled) reports provide substantial benefit through the sub-unit alignment structure suggests the pretext task itself—encoding between-subject invariance—is independently useful, opening a path toward synthetic or metadata-based text generation for pretraining.
