## Problem

Geo-distributed LLM training faces two compounding challenges: inter-region links are orders of magnitude slower (0.1–10 Gbps, millisecond latency) than intra-region links, and accelerators across regions are inherently heterogeneous. Existing local-SGD methods either stall on the slowest worker (DiLoCo's synchronous design) or leave workers idle waiting for global-server round-trips (Async-Local-SGD), and no prior work provides a convergence guarantee for hierarchical asynchronous optimization with momentum.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard text corpora (e.g., the deduplicated Pile) and a model architecture (Pythia, Llama, Qwen at 70M–410M scale), but uniquely also takes the network topology—per-region bandwidths, worker operating speeds, and server placements—as first-class inputs that shape the optimization schedule. Evaluation is performed via a custom trace-driven simulator that replays model-update events on real H100 GPUs, using profiled per-step computation times and analytical α-β / ring-all-reduce communication models, producing wall-clock training time and token counts to reach a target validation loss, plus downstream benchmark accuracies (MMLU, HellaSwag, LAMBADA, WinoGrande, etc.).

## Method

HALoS introduces a three-tier hierarchy—Workers → Local Parameter Servers (LPS) → Global Parameter Server (GPS)—that is fully asynchronous at every level. Each worker performs H local gradient steps and pushes the accumulated parameter difference δ to its LPS; the LPS applies a momentum-based update (β_l) and, after K such updates, sends the accumulated Δ to the GPS, which applies its own momentum update (β_g) and returns the global model. The key mechanism is *global model merging*: upon receiving the GPS model, the LPS blends it with its current local model via a weighted average (θ ← (1−α)θ + αΘ), so local progress made during the inter-region round-trip is not discarded. This design hides slow inter-region latency behind continuous local computation, reduces GPS load by batching K updates, and mitigates straggler effects. The paper provides the first convergence proof for hierarchical asynchronous SGD with momentum under non-convex objectives, showing that the global momentum β_g must be kept moderate (~0.5) while local momentum β_l can be aggressive (~0.9), and that the bound decomposes into a hierarchical-variance term and an asynchronous-staleness term.

## Results

HALoS achieves up to 7.5× faster convergence than DiLoCo and up to 2.1× faster than Async-Local-SGD in geo-distributed LLM training (Pythia-70M/160M/410M on the Pile dataset), while also reducing token consumption by 18% versus Async-Local-SGD. Compared to fully synchronous SGD, HALoS delivers a 68.6× speedup in wall-clock training time while matching or exceeding accuracy on MMLU, HellaSwag, LAMBADA, and WinoGrande benchmarks. The method generalizes across model families (Llama-70M, Qwen-70M) with 2.1×–2.3× speedups over Async-Local-SGD using identical hyperparameters, and outperforms the strongest synchronous model-parallelism baseline (Hetero-Aware DP+PP) by 8.26×.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DiLoCo (Douillard et al., 2023), HALoS achieves 7.1–7.5× faster wall-clock convergence across Pythia-70M/160M/410M, primarily because DiLoCo's strictly synchronous design stalls on the slowest worker (47.5% of runtime lost to straggler waits). Even with dynamic local updates added (DiLoCo+DynUpd), HALoS still leads by 3.8–3.9×. Against Async-Local-SGD (Liu et al., 2024), the strongest baseline, HALoS wins by 1.8–2.1× in time and reduces token consumption by 18% (12.3B vs. 14.0B for Pythia-70M), as its hierarchical LPS layer hides inter-region communication delays that otherwise idle workers. Against FedAH (Wang & Wang, 2022), a semi-synchronous hierarchical FL method, HALoS is 3.3× faster in time-to-loss and requires only 12.3B tokens versus 38.1B. Against Hetero-Aware DP+PP (Li et al., 2022), a synchronous model-parallelism baseline with heterogeneity-aware placement, HALoS converges 8.26× faster by avoiding pipeline bubbles and cross-region synchronization stalls inherent to synchronous parallelism.

## Relation to Prior Work

HALoS extends the Local SGD lineage (Stich 2018, Zhang 2015) through a hierarchical parameter-server architecture, building directly on DiLoCo's momentum-based local SGD for geo-distributed LLM training and Async-Local-SGD's asynchronous gradient collection with momentum correction. Its key departure is introducing local parameter servers (LPSs) that accumulate updates before communicating with a global parameter server (GPS), combined with a weighted global model merging step—making the system fully asynchronous at both hierarchy levels, unlike semi-synchronous hierarchical FL methods (FedAH, Timely-AHFL) that require workers to wait for global broadcasts. The paper also provides the first convergence proof for hierarchical asynchronous distributed optimization with momentum, filling a theoretical gap left by prior async FL and local SGD analyses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As GPU scarcity and power constraints make single-datacenter training increasingly infeasible, geo-distributed LLM training is becoming a practical necessity; HALoS's 68.6× speedup over synchronous SGD while matching model quality on standard benchmarks demonstrates that hierarchical asynchrony can make this setting viable. The theoretical insight that global momentum must be substantially more conservative (β_g ≈ 0.5) than local momentum (β_l ≈ 0.9) to avoid averaging conflicting cross-region gradients is a practical guideline not available from prior work. Key limitations include the reliance on trace-driven simulations rather than real geo-distributed deployments, the i.i.d. data assumption in the main convergence proof, and the need for careful worker grouping (consistent grouping) to avoid divergence on heterogeneous clusters—open questions that will matter for real-world adoption.
