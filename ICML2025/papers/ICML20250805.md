## Problem

Existing mathematical reasoning benchmarks for LLMs are approaching saturation (GSM8K ~97%, MATH ~90%) and are increasingly compromised by training-set contamination, making them unable to discriminate between state-of-the-art models. Additionally, standard evaluation relies solely on final boxed answers, which cannot assess reasoning quality, penalize spurious derivations, or handle proof-based problems. The paper addresses both gaps by introducing a harder, contamination-resilient benchmark and a lightweight step-level reasoning metric.

## Input / Output

The benchmark consumes LaTeX-formatted Putnam competition problems (with Asymptote vector graphics for figures) and evaluates LLMs by extracting \boxed{} answers, comparing them to ground truth via the MATH equivalence function. The variation system takes Python scripts that programmatically perturb variables, constants, and phrasing to generate an unbounded stream of unseen, equally difficult instances from 100 seed problems. TFA additionally requires access to model log-probabilities (available only for open-source models), conditioning each token prediction on the full ground-truth solution prefix rather than the model's own prior outputs.

## Method

The paper curates 522 Putnam problems (1938–2023) across 11 mathematical domains, applying 'modified boxing' to 42.3% of problems by appending a trivial computational step that converts proof-based or multi-answer questions into single boxable outputs while preserving the core reasoning. For contamination resistance, 100 problems are paired with Python scripts that perform variable renames and constant substitutions (plus rephrasing), yielding an infinite supply of unseen instances; five random snapshots are drawn per evaluation to compute mean accuracy with 95% confidence intervals. The key metric, Teacher-Forced Accuracy (TFA), conditions the model on the ground-truth solution tokens and measures the fraction of correctly predicted next tokens, requiring only a single forward pass per step with no reward model or additional annotation. TFA is validated against all 18 ROSCOE metrics and BPC on the MATH dataset, achieving the highest average correlation with boxed accuracy (0.66 ± 0.02 vs. 0.53 ± 0.05 for the best ROSCOE metric). A LoRA fine-tuning experiment confirms the variation mechanism's effectiveness: after training on original problems, accuracy jumps to 80% on originals but only 33% on variations, demonstrating that models memorize rather than reason.

## Results

On Putnam-AXIOM Original (522 problems), the strongest model o1-preview achieves 41.94% boxed accuracy (219/522), while GPT-4o reaches 19.35% and most open-source models score below 12%. On the paired Putnam-AXIOM Variation set (100 problems), o1-preview drops to 43% (from 51% on corresponding originals), GPT-4o drops to 22% (from 30%), and DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-32B drops from 33% to 24%, with non-overlapping 95% CIs for several models confirming statistically significant contamination effects. For the proposed Teacher-Forced Accuracy (TFA) metric, TFA achieves 0.66±0.02 average correlation with boxed accuracy on MATH, outperforming all 18 ROSCOE metrics (best: Informativeness Chain at 0.53±0.05) and BPC (0.54±0.02).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary quantitative comparison is between TFA and the 18 ROSCOE metrics (Golovneva et al., 2023) plus BPC (Huang et al., 2024b) as proxy reasoning metrics, evaluated by their correlation with boxed accuracy across 15 open-source models on the MATH dataset. TFA (0.66±0.02) significantly outperforms the best ROSCOE metric, Informativeness Chain (0.53±0.05), by 0.13 in average correlation, and also beats BPC (0.54±0.02) by 0.12. TFA dominates across all seven MATH subcategories (e.g., 0.718 on Algebra vs. 0.616 for Informativeness Chain), while ROSCOE metrics show high variance across categories (e.g., Perplexity Step ranges from 0.081 to 0.644). By category, TFA (0.66) exceeds the global ROSCOE average (0.200±0.028) by more than 3×, and outperforms each individual ROSCOE category (grammar: 0.132, embedding: 0.288, consistency: 0.190, perplexity: 0.189). The paper also positions Putnam-AXIOM against existing benchmarks (MATH, GSM8K, ARB, OlympiadBench) qualitatively, noting that Putnam-AXIOM avoids their saturation and contamination issues while enabling fully automated evaluation without human grading.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the functional-variation approach of Srivastava et al. (2024) from the MATH dataset to the substantially harder Putnam competition, adapting the constant/variable perturbation protocol to university-level problems. It positions itself against a family of saturating math benchmarks (MATH, GSM8K, ARB, OlympiadBench) and differentiates from PutnamBench (Tsoukalas et al., 2024), which targets formal theorem proving in Lean/Isabelle/Coq, by focusing on natural-language problems with automatable boxed answers. On the evaluation-metric side, the paper introduces Teacher-Forced Accuracy (TFA) as a lightweight step-level proxy and benchmarks it against the full 18-metric ROSCOE suite (Golovneva et al., 2023) and the bits-per-character compression metric (Huang et al., 2024b).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark provides a contamination-resilient, fully automatable testbed for university-level mathematical reasoning at a difficulty level where even o1-preview scores below 42%, addressing the ceiling-effect problem that has rendered MATH and GSM8K uninformative for frontier models. TFA offers a practical, single-forward-pass step-level metric that outperforms all ROSCOE metrics in correlation with boxed accuracy, potentially reducing reliance on expensive human proof grading. Key limitations include the variation coverage (only 100 of 522 problems), TFA's dependence on a single reference solution style (penalizing valid but differently-structured proofs), and the exclusion of non-modifiable proof-based problems. Open questions include scaling the variation engine to the full corpus, establishing human-grader correlation for TFA, and extending evaluation to formal proof targets.
