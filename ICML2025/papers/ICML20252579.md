## Problem

RTL-level timing prediction suffers from a fundamental information gap: it lacks the physical RC parameters, parasitic effects, and interconnect delays that layout-level static timing analysis captures, yet layout-level STA is too slow and too late in the design flow for rapid iteration. Existing RTL models (e.g., RTL-Timer) achieve only ~60% accuracy on layout-level timing, and no prior work has systematically transferred high-fidelity physical characteristics from a layout-aware model into an efficient RTL-level predictor.

## Input / Output

The student model consumes an RTL Simple Operator Graph (SOG) with 16-dimensional node features (12-dim one-hot cell type, fan-in/fan-out counts, depth-per-input/output) and produces per-register arrival time, circuit-level WNS, and TNS. The teacher model consumes a layout netlist graph with 96-dimensional node features (78-dim one-hot gate type, drive strength, fanout capacitance/resistance, input/output slew, and 4-arc delay values) and is trained on sign-off STA labels. The student's 128-dim node embeddings are projected to 512-dim via a two-layer MLP to match the teacher's embedding space for distillation alignment.

## Method

RTLDistil employs a dual GAT architecture: a high-capacity teacher (512-dim, ≥3 rounds of asynchronous forward-reverse propagation) trained on layout netlist features, and a lightweight student (128-dim, 2 rounds) operating on the RTL SOG. The forward-reverse propagation strategy mimics STA delay accumulation: forward passes propagate along depth-per-input (DPI) edges to accumulate path delays, while reverse passes propagate along depth-per-output (DPO) edges to capture surrounding circuitry context. Knowledge is transferred via a three-level distillation loss—node-level (per-DFF embedding alignment), subgraph-level (mean-pooled fan-in cone embeddings), and global-level (whole-circuit mean-pooled embeddings)—each using smooth L1 loss between teacher and MLP-projected student representations. The total training objective combines supervised AT regression with weighted distillation terms (α, β, γ), and the student is first distilled with frozen teacher embeddings before downstream fine-tuning on timing labels.

## Results

On the RTLDistil dataset of 2004 RTL designs, RTLDistil achieves a PCC of 0.9227 and MAPE of 16.87% for Arrival Time (AT), 0.9066 PCC and 31.37% MAPE for Worst Negative Slack (WNS), and 0.9586 PCC and 37.95% MAPE for Total Negative Slack (TNS). The layout-level Teacher Model establishes an upper bound with 0.9797 PCC and 11.20% MAPE on AT, while the student model without distillation drops to 0.8787 PCC and 22.04% MAPE, confirming the value of knowledge transfer. The full RTLDistil pipeline (distillation + fine-tuning) closes most of the gap to the teacher while operating on lightweight RTL features with only 2 propagation rounds.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RTLDistil outperforms both SOTA baselines across all nine metric–task combinations. Against RTL-Timer (the stronger baseline), RTLDistil improves AT PCC from 0.8782 to 0.9227 (+5.1%) and reduces AT MAPE from 23.39% to 16.87% (−6.5 pp); for WNS it gains +2.5 pp PCC and −9.2 pp MAPE; for TNS it gains +11.4 pp PCC and −2.4 pp MAPE. Against MasterRTL, the improvements are dramatic: AT PCC jumps from 0.3498 to 0.9227 and AT MAPE drops from 81.20% to 16.87%, while TNS R² goes from −0.2967 (worse than a constant predictor) to 0.9181. The ablation in Table 3 further shows that removing distillation degrades AT MAPE from 16.87% to 22.04%, and removing fine-tuning from 16.87% to 17.77%, confirming that both the multi-granularity KD and the downstream fine-tuning contribute meaningfully over the RTL-only student.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the teacher-student knowledge distillation paradigm (Hinton, 2015) to the EDA domain, specifically to bridge the abstraction gap between RTL-level and layout-level timing prediction. It builds on prior GNN-based RTL timing predictors (MasterRTL, RTL-Timer) that operate purely on logical abstractions, and on layout-stage GNN timing models that require full physical features. The key departure is the cross-stage distillation framework: a layout-aware teacher GNN (96-dim physical features, 3 propagation rounds) transfers knowledge to a lightweight RTL student GNN (16-dim logical features, 2 propagation rounds) via multi-granularity feature alignment at node, subgraph (fan-in cone), and global levels, rather than relying solely on output-level soft targets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RTLDistil addresses a practical bottleneck in industrial chip design: catching timing violations at the RTL stage before expensive layout iterations. The demonstrated improvement (AT MAPE from 23.39% to 16.87%, TNS PCC from 0.8451 to 0.9586 over prior SOTA) is meaningful for enabling the EDA 'left-shift' paradigm. However, the framework is evaluated on a single process node with specific Synopsys/Cadence tool configurations, and the 2004-design dataset, while diverse, may not capture the full complexity of large SoC designs with multi-clock domains. Open questions include scalability to billion-gate designs, robustness across process nodes, and whether the distillation gap (teacher PCC 0.9797 vs. student 0.9227) can be further closed without increasing student complexity.
