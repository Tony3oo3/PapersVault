## Problem

Multi-View Reinforcement Learning (MVRL) struggles to learn compact, task-relevant representations from redundant high-dimensional multi-view observations, and existing fusion methods (cross-attention, VAE-based, contrastive) lack explicit mechanisms to focus on causally relevant features. Additionally, bisimulation-based representation learning assumes complete, high-quality observations, making it vulnerable to missing or noisy views in real-world deployment. The paper addresses both gaps simultaneously by integrating bisimulation metric learning with a masking-based reconstruction auxiliary task within a unified MVRL framework.

## Input / Output

The method consumes K synchronized RGB images (K=3 for Meta-World and PyBullet Ant; K=3 for CARLA; K=4 for DMControl where temporal frames proxy views), each encoded by a shared CNN into a d-dimensional embedding. The output is a single fused latent state vector (128-dim for Meta-World/PyBullet, 50-dim for CARLA/DMControl) produced by a learnable [state] token after L=2 self-attention layers, which is L2-normalized and fed directly into an actor-critic policy (PPO or SAC). During training, the fused representation is also consumed by an ensemble of K=5 deterministic latent dynamics models and a SimSiam-style prediction head for the reconstruction auxiliary loss.

## Method

MFSC fuses multi-view observations via a Self-Attention Fusion Module that prepends a learnable [state] embedding to per-view CNN embeddings (analogous to BERT/ViT class tokens) and processes them through L layers of multi-head self-attention with residual connections. The fusion is trained with a bisimulation metric loss (Eq. 8) that minimizes the squared difference between the learned cosine-distance representation and a target computed from reward differences and predicted next-state distances via an ensemble of deterministic latent dynamics models, thereby extracting task-relevant features. A multiview-based masking and latent reconstruction auxiliary task (Eq. 9) randomly masks 80% of pixels across views and uses a SimSiam-style asymmetric architecture (stop-gradient on the target branch) to reconstruct the unmasked latent embeddings, introducing a learnable mask token that enables graceful degradation under missing views. The overall objective is L_mfsc = L_fus + λ·L_rec (λ=1), optimized jointly with the policy. Theoretically, the paper extends the π-bisimulation metric to the multi-view setting and proves a value-function error bound of 2ε/((1−γ)(1−c)) for the induced latent MDP (Lemma 4.2).

## Results

On Meta-World (5 robotic manipulation tasks), MFSC consistently outperforms all MVRL baselines (Keypoint3D, LookCloser, F2C, MVD) with a 97.93% average performance improvement over the RAD baseline. On DeepMind Control Suite (single-view setting), MFSC achieves 832±29 (Cartpole Swingup), 679±33 (Cheetah Run), 977±7 (Finger Spin), and 909±40 (Walker Walk), matching or exceeding Dreamer-v3 and TD-MPC2 on most tasks. On CARLA driving, MFSC attains the highest episode return (168.01±9.62) and distance (183.30±67.39 m) among all compared methods. Under missing-view and noisy-view conditions, MFSC maintains performance comparable to the previous SOTA (F2C) while RAD and LookCloser suffer notable degradation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On Meta-World, MFSC outperforms all five MVRL baselines (Keypoint3D, LookCloser, F2C, MVD, and RAD) across all five tasks, with the largest gains over Vanilla PPO (which shows almost no learning) and RAD (which struggles on complex tasks like Open Window and Close Box). On PyBullet Ant, MFSC matches Keypoint3D in the standard setting and achieves the highest performance in the harder 'Ant No Color' variant where color cues are removed, while contrastive-learning and pixel-reconstruction baselines degrade significantly. On DMControl (single-view), MFSC is slightly behind TACO (e.g., 679 vs. 915 on Cheetah Run) but surpasses SAC, CURL, and DrQ-v2 on all four tasks and is competitive with Dreamer-v3 and TD-MPC2. On CARLA, MFSC achieves the best episode return (168.01 vs. 152.73 for SAC) and distance (183.30 m vs. 179.60 m for SAC), though it trails SAC on success rate (13% vs. 21%) and crash intensity. Under missing-view conditions, MFSC's degradation is limited and comparable to F2C, while LookCloser drops more sharply; under noisy-view interference, MFSC preserves performance whereas RAD and LookCloser show notable decline, confirming the robustness benefit of bisimulation-guided fusion.

## Relation to Prior Work

MFSC is the first to integrate bisimulation metric learning (from SimSR and MICo) into the multi-view reinforcement learning framework, providing a principled mechanism to extract task-relevant representations from redundant multi-view observations where prior MVRL methods (Keypoint3D, LookCloser, F2C, MVD) relied on cross-attention, information-theoretic bottlenecks, or contrastive learning without explicit task-relevance objectives. The self-attention fusion module with a learnable [state] token draws directly from BERT/ViT design, while the masking-and-latent-reconstruction auxiliary task adapts the SimSiam asymmetric architecture to the multi-view setting. A key departure from prior MVRL work is the use of latent-space reconstruction rather than pixel-level reconstruction, and the introduction of a learnable mask token that naturally handles missing views at inference time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical relevance is clear for robotic manipulation and autonomous driving where multiple cameras are standard but views can be occluded, missing, or corrupted by irrelevant content. The honest CARLA results—where highly independent views make simple concatenation competitive—temper the generalizability claim and highlight that MFSC's advantage is most pronounced when views share redundant information. A key open question is whether the masking-based approach can truly recover state when a critical view is absent, as the authors acknowledge that incomplete cross-view complementarity limits reconstruction fidelity; integrating state-space models for temporal reasoning is suggested as a natural next step.
