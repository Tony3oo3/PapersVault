## Problem

Processing long token sequences in time series transformers and state-space models remains computationally expensive despite subquadratic attention and SSM advances. Token merging, which reduces token count by combining similar tokens, has proven effective in computer vision but had never been explored for time series. Moreover, existing global merging has quadratic similarity-computation complexity, making it incompatible with long sequences and state-space models, and no causal merging scheme existed to enable token merging in decoder architectures.

## Input / Output

The method operates on tokenized sequences: multivariate time series (m timestamps × n variates, e.g., m=192 for transformers, m=512 for Chronos), univariate time series converted to a discrete vocabulary (Chronos), or genomic nucleotide sequences (m=16000 for Hyena/Mamba). Merging is applied at inference time without retraining (or optionally during training), reducing the token count passed to subsequent layers. Output is forecasted time series values (MSE-reported) or class labels for genomic classification, with the final decoder step unmerging tokens to restore the required output dimensionality.

## Method

The core contribution is local token merging, which restricts cosine-similarity computation to a local neighborhood of size k around each token, reducing complexity from O(t²) to O(t·k) — linear when k=1. This locality constraint preserves temporal order and causality; the k=1 special case (causal merging) is the first viable merging scheme for transformer decoders, where merged tokens are unmerged at the final layer by cloning into adjacent positions. A dynamic merging variant adaptively sets the merge count per batch element via a similarity threshold, avoiding forced merging of dissimilar tokens. The algorithm is applied after self-attention in encoders and after the Hyena/Mamba operator in state-space models, with a theoretical speed-up bound of 3L·4^(L-1)/(4^L - 1) for L-layer models. The paper further identifies that local merging acts as an adaptive low-pass filter, with spectral entropy and total harmonic distortion of the input signal reliably predicting quality gains without downstream evaluation.

## Results

On the Chronos foundation model in zero-shot forecasting, local merging achieves up to 54.76× inference speedup (5400%) while improving MSE by up to 9% on the Traffic dataset. Across five pretrained time series transformer architectures (Transformer, Autoformer, FEDformer, Informer, Non-stationary Transformer) on five datasets, local merging increases throughput by up to 3.80× with MSE changes typically within ±1%. On state-space models (HyenaDNA, Mamba) with 16,000-token genomic sequences, local merging achieves up to 4.09× acceleration while maintaining or improving accuracy, outperforming global merging on both speed and quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is global token merging from Bolya et al. (2023), which computes cosine similarity across all token pairs with quadratic complexity. On HyenaDNA, local merging (k=1, linear complexity) achieves 3.62× acceleration at 74.0% accuracy versus global merging's 2.93× at 69.4% accuracy in the fastest setting, and 1.68× at 80.6% versus 1.15× at 80.2% in the best-quality setting—winning on both speed and accuracy. On Mamba, local merging reaches 4.09× acceleration at 74.4% accuracy compared to global merging's 2.81× at 74.0%, again dominating on speed while matching accuracy. The similarity computation overhead also favors local merging: it adds only 14% execution time per Hyena block versus 68% for global merging. Across all five pretrained transformer architectures, local merging finds Pareto-optimal points (speedup with no accuracy loss) in 17 of 25 settings, with the remaining cases showing at most 9% MSE degradation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the token merging framework introduced by Bolya et al. (2023) for Vision Transformers into the time series domain, where sequences are typically much longer and exhibit temporal locality. The key methodological departure is the introduction of a locality constraint k that reduces merging complexity from quadratic to linear, making it compatible with state-space models and long-sequence processing. The authors further introduce causal merging (k=1) as the first viable token merging scheme for transformer decoders, and dynamic merging as an adaptive alternative to fixed-rate merging. The work is positioned as orthogonal to sparse attention and token skipping, since it reduces the number of tokens processed by all subsequent layers (including MLPs) rather than only the attention mechanism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: local merging accelerates already-trained foundation models like Chronos by up to 54× without any fine-tuning, which is critical as time series foundation models grow in size and computational cost. The finding that token merging acts as an adaptive low-pass filter—improving accuracy on high-spectral-entropy datasets while compressing—challenges the assumption that compression must sacrifice quality, and the identification of spectral entropy and THD as pre-evaluation predictors of merging benefit is actionable for practitioners. Limitations include the fixed two-set partitioning of tokens (restricting merging flexibility), the absence of ablations across all hyperparameter combinations, and the fact that accuracy gains are dataset-specific rather than universal. Open questions include whether more flexible merging schemes (beyond the two-set constraint) could further improve results, and whether the spectral-entropy predictor generalizes to unseen domains.
