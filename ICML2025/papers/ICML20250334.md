## Problem

STGNNs achieve robust spatial-temporal forecasting but at high computational cost, while efficient MLP-based methods lack the architectural mechanisms to resist noise perturbation. A key unaddressed issue is the 'dual noise effect': under the sliding-window mechanism, the same noisy sequence can serve as both input and target across overlapping windows, yet standard Information Bottleneck formulations assume a Z-X-Y Markov chain that implicitly ignores noise in the target. The paper asks whether a simple MLP can be made robust to this bilateral noise while remaining computationally efficient.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a historical window X^h ∈ R^{P×N×C} (P past time slots, N sensors/series, C features) which is augmented with learnable spatial-temporal prompts (spatial embedding, time-of-day, day-of-week, and dynamic transitional embeddings) to produce the effective input X ∈ R^{P×N×5C'}. It also requires a pre-trained teacher model f_T (default: STGCN) and an optional adjacency matrix A to compute per-series noise impact indicators. The output is a future window Y^S ∈ R^{F×N×C} of F predicted time slots, evaluated with MAE, RMSE, and MAPE.

## Method

The core contribution is the RSTIB principle, which lifts the standard Z-X-Y Markov assumption to only X-Z-Y, thereby introducing an explicit I(Z;Y|X) term that captures noise conveyed by the target; the resulting objective is min −I(Z,Y) + β₁·I(Z;X,Y) − β₂·I(X;Y;Z). RSTIB-MLP instantiates this with a 3-layer MLP encoder using VAE-style reparameterization for the input X̃, target Ỹ, and latent Z, yielding three closed-form KL-divergence regularization terms (input, target, and representation) against unit-Gaussian priors. A knowledge distillation training regime computes per-series noise impact indicators α̂_i from a frozen teacher model's prediction errors, then dynamically scales the regularization weights as (1+α̂_i) so that noisier series receive stronger compression, achieving a better balance of informative terms without fixed hyperparameter tuning.

## Results

On PEMS04, RSTIB-MLP achieves the best MAE at every noise level: 18.46 (clean), 23.64 (10%), 27.15 (30%), and 27.16 (50%), outperforming the next-best method (TrendGCN) by up to 0.74 MAE at 50% noise. Across all six datasets (PEMS04/07/08, LargeST(SD), Weather2K-R, Electricity), RSTIB-MLP consistently achieves the lowest MAE, RMSE, and MAPE under both clean and noisy evaluations. The robustness advantage grows with noise intensity: at 50% noise on PEMS08, RSTIB-MLP records 24.37 MAE versus 26.90 for TrendGCN and 27.76 for STID, a gap of over 2.5 MAE.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the MLP baseline STID, RSTIB-MLP is dramatically more robust: at 50% noise on PEMS04, STID degrades to 36.22 MAE while RSTIB-MLP holds at 27.16, a 9-point gap that widens further on PEMS07 (32.38 vs 30.94) and PEMS08 (27.76 vs 24.37). Versus STGNN-based methods (GWN, STExplainer, TrendGCN), RSTIB-MLP matches or exceeds their clean-data accuracy while being substantially faster to train (Fig. 5), and pulls clearly ahead under noise—e.g., on PEMS08 at 50% noise, RSTIB-MLP (24.37) beats TrendGCN (26.90) and STExplainer (27.17). Among dedicated robustness methods, RSTIB-MLP outperforms the IB-based STExplainer and STGKD, the adversarial-training TrendGCN, the frequency-domain FreTS, the BiTGraph TCN variant, the curriculum-dropout STC-Dropout, and the Neural-ODE STG-NCDE across all six datasets and all noise levels tested. The paper attributes this consistent advantage to explicitly modeling the 'dual noise effect' (noise in both input and target via the lifted Markov assumption) combined with the noise-impact-indicator-guided knowledge distillation training regime, whereas prior methods typically address only one noisy end or rely on heavier architectures that sacrifice efficiency.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Information Bottleneck (IB) principle from its standard Z-X-Y Markov formulation (DVIB, Alemi et al. 2017) to an X-Z-Y setting, following the theoretical relaxation shown by Wieczorek & Roth (2020), and applies it specifically to spatial-temporal forecasting where the sliding window mechanism creates a 'dual noise effect' on both input and target. It generalizes the Robust Graph Information Bottleneck (RGIB, Zhou et al. 2023) from graph-structured link prediction to continuous multivariate time series, instantiating the principle on pure MLPs rather than GNNs. The knowledge distillation training regime with per-series noise impact indicators departs from fixed-β IB training by dynamically adapting regularization strength based on a teacher model's per-series prediction quality.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The RSTIB principle provides a theoretically grounded, architecture-agnostic framework for robust spatial-temporal representation learning that could be instantiated on model types beyond MLPs, addressing a gap where prior robust STF methods (adversarial training, GIB, curriculum dropout) either impair efficiency or lack a unifying principle. The practical finding that a 3-layer MLP with IB regularization can match or exceed STGNN-based robust methods at substantially lower computational cost is valuable for deployment-constrained settings. Limitations include the dependence on a pre-trained teacher model for the noise impact indicator, the assumption of AWGN, and only marginal improvements on clean data, suggesting the regularization terms may be suboptimally balanced in low-noise regimes. Open questions include whether RSTIB generalizes to other noise models (e.g., missing values, outliers) and whether the principle can be instantiated on attention-based or hybrid architectures.
