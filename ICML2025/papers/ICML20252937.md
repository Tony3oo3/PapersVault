## Problem

Hierarchical semi-implicit variational inference (HSIVI) enables multi-step diffusion distillation with diverse samples, but its sequential simulation across discrete time steps causes slow convergence. Meanwhile, one-step distillation methods like SiD lack sample diversity, and their fused loss exhibits pathological behavior (becoming negative) when the balancing hyperparameter α exceeds 1. The paper seeks a continuous-time framework that retains the diversity benefits of multi-step generation while enabling simulation-free, efficient training.

## Input / Output

CoSIM takes Gaussian noise (and a class label for conditional generation) as input and produces RGB images at 32×32 (CIFAR-10), 64×64, or 512×512 (ImageNet). Generation is inherently multi-step: the continuous transition kernel is applied iteratively over a sequence of time points (typically 2–6 NFE), with fresh Gaussian noise injected at each step. The method requires a pretrained score network (e.g., VP-EDM or EDM2) as a frozen teacher, and the generator and auxiliary function are initialized from this teacher with only a 4% parameter increase for the dual time embedding.

## Method

CoSIM replaces HSIVI's discrete sequential layers with a continuous transition kernel q_trans(x_s|x_t; s,t) parameterized as a consistency function x_s = a(s)G_φ(x_t,t) + σ(s)ε, and constructs the mixing distribution via a single push-forward through the forward diffusion process, eliminating sequential simulation. Training uses a two-stage alternating optimization over a scaled Fisher divergence D_α with a regularization term that shifts the auxiliary function f_ψ toward the pretrained score network, which Theorem 3.1 proves preserves the Nash equilibrium for the generator while resolving SiD's pathological loss behavior. Proposition 3.2 establishes that the optimal G_φ is itself a consistency function mapping p(x_t;t) back to p(x_0;0), and Proposition 3.8 provides a multistep Wasserstein error bound showing that iterative application of the kernel reduces the one-step approximation error geometrically with the number of steps.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (32×32), CoSIM achieves FID 1.97 with only 4 NFE, matching the VP-EDM teacher (FID 1.97 at 35 NFE), and sets a new SOTA FD-DINOv2 of 113.51 at 4 NFE, substantially outperforming the previous best PFGM++ (141.65). On ImageNet (512×512), CoSIM-L achieves FD-DINOv2 41.79 with 4 NFE, surpassing the much larger EDM2-XXL teacher (42.84 at 63 NFE) and all one-step distillation baselines. On ImageNet (64×64), CoSIM reaches FID 1.46 and FD-DINOv2 58.66 with 4 NFE, outperforming SiD (FID 1.52, FD-DINOv2 79.15 at 1 NFE).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoSIM decisively outperforms SiD on FD-DINOv2 across all benchmarks: 113.51 vs. 148.17 on CIFAR-10, 58.66 vs. 79.15 on ImageNet 64×64, and 41.79 vs. 56.25 (SiD-L) on ImageNet 512×512, while matching or exceeding SiD's FID with only 2–4 NFE. On CIFAR-10, CoSIM matches the VP-EDM teacher's FID (1.97) with 4 NFE versus 35 NFE, and surpasses all consistency model variants (CD-LPIPS, iCT, sCT, CTM, ECT) on FD-DINOv2 by large margins (e.g., 113.51 vs. 197.59 for CTM). On ImageNet 512×512, CoSIM-L with 4 NFE (FD-DINOv2 41.79) beats the 1.5B-parameter EDM2-XXL teacher (42.84 at 63 NFE) and all one-step distillation methods (SiD-L: 56.25, sCD-L: 2.04 FID). CoSIM loses to CTM on FID for CIFAR-10 (1.97 vs. 1.84 at 4 NFE), which the authors attribute to CTM's much larger 324M parameter budget versus CoSIM's 56M. On ImageNet 64×64, Moment Matching (Salimans et al., 2024) achieves a slightly better FID (1.24 at 8 NFE) than CoSIM (1.46 at 4 NFE), though CoSIM uses fewer NFE and a smaller network. CoSIM also substantially outperforms its direct predecessor HSIVI-SM (FID 1.97 vs. 4.17 on CIFAR-10, 1.46 vs. 15.49 on ImageNet 64×64), validating the continuous-time extension.

## Relation to Prior Work

CoSIM extends hierarchical semi-implicit variational inference (HSIVI, Yu et al. 2023) from a discrete, sequential multi-layer structure to a continuous-time framework, replacing the fixed sequence of conditional layers with a single continuous transition kernel trained via a joint time schedule. It adopts the score-based (Fisher divergence) training objective from SiD (Zhou et al. 2024b) but reformulates the fused SiD loss into a two-stage alternating optimization that resolves the pathological negative-loss behavior of SiD when α > 1/2. The parameterization of the transition kernel borrows directly from consistency models (Song et al. 2023), and the paper proves (Proposition 3.2) that the optimal transition kernel is itself a consistency function, unifying the semi-implicit variational inference and consistency distillation perspectives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CoSIM provides a principled continuous-time framework for multi-step diffusion distillation that avoids the slow sequential simulation of HSIVI while retaining the sample diversity benefits of stochastic multi-step generation. The state-of-the-art FD-DINOv2 results (e.g., CoSIM-L at 41.79 with 4 NFE beating EDM2-XXL at 42.84 with 63 NFE) are notable, particularly given that FD-DINOv2 is argued to better reflect human perception than FID. Key limitations include high memory consumption from maintaining three models simultaneously, the constraint that the generator architecture must closely match the pretrained score network (limiting one-step quality relative to SiD/sCD), and the fact that FID results are competitive but not always superior to the best one-step methods. Open questions include whether the generator can be decoupled from the teacher architecture to enable larger models, and whether the continuous framework extends naturally to conditional or text-to-image settings beyond class-conditional ImageNet.
