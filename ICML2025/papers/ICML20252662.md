## Problem

Unlabeled offline trajectory data can be used either to pretrain low-level skills or as off-policy data for online RL, but prior methods use it for only one of these purposes, discarding the data after skill extraction. It has been unclear how to effectively combine both uses of the same offline data to accelerate online exploration in long-horizon, sparse-reward tasks where the agent must actively discover reward signals.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252662/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes unlabeled offline trajectory segments (states and actions, with all reward and termination information removed) and online environment interactions (state observations, actions, and sparse rewards). It produces a frozen low-level skill policy π_θ(a|s,z) parameterized by a continuous latent z, and a learned high-level policy π_ψ(z|s) that selects skill latents every H=4 environment steps. The offline data is consumed twice: first as VAE training data for skill extraction, then as pseudo-labeled high-level transitions (state, skill latent, UCB reward, next state) mixed with online replay data for the high-level agent. For the visual-antmaze domain, observations include 64×64 RGB images alongside proprioceptive state.

## Method

SUPE operates in two phases. Offline, a trajectory-segment VAE with a GRU encoder, state-dependent Gaussian prior, and Gaussian skill policy is trained to reconstruct actions from short trajectory segments, yielding a frozen low-level skill policy and a trajectory encoder that maps any segment to a skill latent estimate. Online, the key innovation is pseudo-labeling offline trajectory segments with (1) a high-level action label from the VAE trajectory encoder and (2) an optimistic UCB reward estimate computed from a reward model plus an RND-based novelty bonus, transforming unlabeled data into high-level off-policy transitions. These pseudo-labeled offline transitions are combined with online replay data in a balanced manner (128 offline + 128 online samples per batch) to train a high-level SAC agent using the RLPD algorithm. The high-level policy uses a tanh-squashed parameterization with entropy regularization rather than the KL-to-prior constraint used in prior skill-based methods, which the authors show performs better in practice.

## Results

SUPE is evaluated on 42 long-horizon, sparse-reward tasks across 8 domains (antmaze, kitchen, humanoidmaze, antsoccer, cube-single, cube-double, scene, visual-antmaze), using interquartile mean (IQM) normalized return as the primary metric. SUPE matches or outperforms all baselines on 6 of 8 domains, with SUPE (HILP) performing best on antsoccer and scene. On antmaze, SUPE achieves the best first-goal time across all 12 goal locations (e.g., 19±1.8k steps aggregated on medium vs. 40±2.0k for EXPLORE and 130±7.4k for DBC+JSRL). On humanoidmaze, SUPE is the only method to achieve nonzero return on the large and giant mazes, where all baselines fail completely.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SUPE outperforms EXPLORE (Li et al., 2024) on all 8 domains, with the gap widening on harder tasks: EXPLORE struggles on kitchen and achieves near-zero return on humanoidmaze-large/giant, while SUPE is the only method to solve those mazes. Against DBC+JSRL (Uchendu et al., 2023), SUPE achieves substantially lower first-goal times on antmaze (25k vs. 160k steps aggregated) and higher normalized return across all domains, indicating that structured skill composition is far more effective than diffusion-policy jump-starting for exploration. The skill-based baselines that discard offline data during online learning (TRAJECTORY SKILLS, HILP SKILLS) consistently underperform their SUPE counterparts, confirming that using offline data twice (pretraining + online) is critical. SUPE (HILP), a novel baseline combining HILP skills with offline-data pseudo-labeling, outperforms SUPE on antsoccer and scene, suggesting different skill representations suit different task types. Overall, no prior method that uses offline data for only one purpose (pretraining or online) matches the performance of methods that leverage it for both.

## Relation to Prior Work

SUPE sits at the intersection of two prior lines of work: trajectory-segment VAE skill pretraining (OPAL, SPiRL) and optimistic offline-data relabeling for exploration (EXPLORE). Prior skill-based methods (Ajay et al., Pertsch et al.) extract low-level skills from unlabeled trajectories but discard the offline data during online learning, while EXPLORE uses offline data for online exploration without skill pretraining. SUPE's key departure is combining both uses of the same unlabeled dataset—pretraining skills offline and pseudo-labeling trajectory segments with UCB rewards as additional off-policy data for the high-level policy—which no prior method does simultaneously. The high-level agent uses RLPD (Ball et al.) and the UCB estimation borrows directly from EXPLORE, making the contribution primarily one of architectural composition rather than new algorithmic primitives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that the 'entanglement problem' in RL pretraining—where general environment knowledge is mixed with task-specific behaviors—can be addressed by hierarchical decomposition that exploits offline data at both the skill level and the composition level. A key open limitation is that skills remain frozen during online learning, which can hinder performance when pretrained skills are poor or need adaptation; the authors note this is especially problematic on very noisy offline data (the 'Explore' dataset ablation). The reliance on RND for maintaining the UCB bound also raises questions about scalability to higher-dimensional observation spaces beyond the single visual-antmaze test. The practical takeaway—that a simple pseudo-labeling step transforms unlabeled trajectories into task-relevant off-policy examples—is broadly applicable to any hierarchical RL setting with prior data.
