## Problem

Binary spike activations in SNNs suffer from limited information capacity, causing significant accuracy degradation compared to full-precision ANNs. Prior work that increases spike precision (ternary, integer, or real-valued spikes) restores accuracy but sacrifices the multiplication-free and event-driven computational advantages that make SNNs energy-efficient. The paper identifies an asymmetry from ANN quantization research—activations are more sensitive to quantization than weights—and asks whether reversing the bit allocation (real-valued activations, binary weights) can recover accuracy while retaining SNN efficiency.

## Input / Output

For static datasets (CIFAR-10/100 at 32×32, ImageNet at 224×224), RGB images are rate-encoded into binary spike inputs at the first layer; for the neuromorphic CIFAR10-DVS dataset, native event streams are fed directly. The network output is the temporal average of per-timestep logits across T timesteps, passed through softmax to yield a class probability vector. The membrane potential decay constant τ is fixed at 0.25, and the firing threshold V_th is 0 for static inputs (no timing information) or 0.25 for event-based inputs.

## Method

ReverB-SNN replaces the standard binary spike output with a real-valued spike (the membrane potential value itself when it exceeds threshold) while binarizing all weights to {−1, +1} via a sign function. Because a real-valued spike multiplied by a binary weight reduces to addition (o×1 or o×−1), the network retains both the event-driven activation pattern and the multiplication-free inference property of vanilla SNNs. A learnable variant extends weights to {−α, +α} with a channel-wise scalar α trained jointly via STBP; during inference, a re-parameterization step folds α into the previous layer's spike output (O_new = α·O), restoring normalized binary weights and preserving addition-only computation. Weight binarization is made differentiable through a Straight-Through Estimator surrogate gradient, and the full model is trained end-to-end with spatial-temporal backpropagation and cross-entropy loss.

## Results

On ImageNet with ResNet34 and only 4 timesteps, the learnable binary weight variant of ReverB-SNN achieves 70.91% top-1 accuracy, surpassing the previous best SNN (Ternary spike, 70.12%) by 0.79% and the next-best (Multi-hierarchical, 69.73%) by 1.18%. On CIFAR-10 with ResNet19 and 12 timesteps, ReverB-SNN reaches 96.39% (learnable variant: 96.62%), outperforming RecDis-SNN (95.53%) and Real Spike (95.51%). On CIFAR10-DVS with ResNet19 and 10 timesteps, the learnable variant achieves 80.50%, exceeding SSCL (80.00%) and Spikformer (78.90%). Energy cost increases by only 0.52% over vanilla SNN while delivering these accuracy gains.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReverB-SNN consistently outperforms all 25 compared baselines across every dataset. On ImageNet (the headline result), the learnable variant at 70.91% beats the closest competitor Ternary spike (70.12%) by 0.79% and Multi-hierarchical (69.73%) by 1.18%, while using the same 4 timesteps; it also surpasses methods requiring more timesteps such as DSR (67.74% at 50 timesteps) and RecDis-SNN (67.33% at 6 timesteps). On CIFAR-10, ReverB-SNN with ResNet19 at 12 timesteps (96.39%) exceeds RecDis-SNN (95.53% at 24 timesteps) and Real Spike (95.51% at 24 timesteps) by roughly 0.9–1.0% while halving the timesteps. On CIFAR-100, the method achieves 78.46% (ResNet19, 12T), surpassing TET (74.47% at 24T) and LTL (76.08% at 31T) by 2.4–4.0%. On the neuromorphic CIFAR10-DVS dataset, the learnable variant reaches 80.50% (ResNet19, 10T), edging out SSCL (80.00%) and Spikformer (78.90%), and substantially outperforming Dspike (75.40%) and Real Spike (72.85%). No baseline is matched or beaten by ReverB-SNN in any reported setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes a well-known insight from ANN quantization literature—that activation quantization degrades accuracy more than weight quantization (Gong et al., 2019; Qin et al., 2024)—and applies it to SNNs by inverting the standard precision allocation: real-valued spikes replace binary spikes, while real-valued weights are binarized to {−1, +1}. It builds directly on the authors' own prior work on real-valued spikes (Guo et al., 2022d, 'Real Spike'), which achieved higher accuracy but lost the multiplication-free property; here, binary weights restore that property. The learnable-amplitude variant and its re-parameterization trick (folding α into the previous layer's output at inference) draw on the re-parameterization paradigm popularized in efficient CNN design, adapted to the spiking setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for neuromorphic and edge deployment: a 3.22% accuracy gain on ImageNet with only 4 timesteps and a mere 0.52% energy overhead makes this a near-free upgrade for existing SNN pipelines. The re-parameterization technique elegantly decouples training expressiveness from inference efficiency, a pattern that could generalize to other binary-parameter SNN variants. Open questions include whether the approach extends beyond LIF neurons (e.g., to IAF or adaptive neurons), whether it composes with other information-loss-reduction techniques (ternary spikes, multi-threshold), and whether the channel-wise α factor is sufficient or whether finer-grained (e.g., per-filter or per-timestep) amplitudes would yield further gains.
