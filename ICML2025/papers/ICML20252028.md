## Problem

TabPFNv2, the leading tabular foundation model using in-context learning, relies on alternating column-wise and row-wise self-attention that scales as O(n²m), making it computationally prohibitive beyond ~10K training samples. This paper asks whether ICL for tabular data can be effectively scaled to much larger tables (hundreds of thousands of samples) while retaining competitive accuracy, and whether the quadratic cost of self-attention can be structurally reduced rather than merely patched with prompt engineering.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252028/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

TabICL consumes a raw tabular table of n rows and m features (up to 500K × 500 in practice) together with the training-set labels, which serve as the ICL context. Labels are injected only at the final ICL stage (late fusion), so the row-embedding stage is label-independent and can be shared across sub-classifiers in hierarchical classification for >10 classes. The model outputs class probabilities for the entire test set in a single forward pass, with predictions ensembled over 32 random column permutations and two preprocessing variants (z-normalization with/without power transformation).

## Method

TabICL uses a two-stage architecture that collapses the column dimension before ICL. First, a shared Set Transformer (TF_col) processes each column as a permutation-invariant set of cell values via induced self-attention (ISAB with k=128 inducing vectors), producing per-cell distribution-aware weights and biases that encode statistical properties like skewness and kurtosis; this reduces column-attention cost to O(nkm). Second, a 3-layer transformer (TF_row) with rotary positional encoding (RoPE, base=100000) models inter-feature interactions per row and aggregates them into four [CLS] tokens (512-dim embedding), where RoPE breaks the symmetry that causes representation collapse among identically distributed features. A final 12-layer transformer (TF_icl) then performs ICL over the compact row embeddings plus one-hot labels, yielding overall complexity O(m²n + n²) versus O(m²n + n²m) for TabPFNv2. Pretraining uses curriculum learning (1K→60K samples over three stages) on ~82M synthetic datasets generated by SCMs enriched with tree-based (XGBoost) causal models and 19 diverse activation functions including Gaussian-process random functions.

## Results

On the TALENT benchmark (171 classification datasets with ≤10 classes), TabICL achieves the best median relative accuracy over a tuned MLP baseline, with a geometric mean training+inference time of 1.1 seconds per 1K samples on an A100 GPU—compared to ~3 minutes for CatBoost and ~7 minutes for RealMLP/ModernNCA. TabICL is 1.5× faster than TabPFNv2 on small datasets and 3–10× faster on large ones (e.g., ~20 s vs. ~100 s on 10K samples × 100 features). On the 53 datasets with >10K samples, TabICL surpasses both TabPFNv2 and CatBoost in accuracy, while handling up to 500K samples and 500 features within ~20 GB of GPU memory.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TabICL is statistically on par with TabPFNv2 in accuracy across all 171 TALENT datasets (critical difference diagram shows no significant gap), but is systematically faster: 1.5× on small tables and 3–10× on large ones, with an average ~5× speedup for datasets above 10K samples. On the 53 large datasets (>10K samples), TabICL surpasses both TabPFNv2 and CatBoost, demonstrating that ICL remains competitive in the large-sample regime where tree-based models traditionally dominate. Against other deep learning baselines (ModernNCA, RealMLP, TabR, TabM), TabICL achieves a wide margin in both accuracy and log-loss, while being orders of magnitude faster since it requires no hyperparameter tuning. On datasets with >10 classes, TabICL (via hierarchical classification) achieves the second-best mean normalized accuracy, whereas TabPFNv2 cannot natively handle more than 10 classes. TabICL also slightly outperforms TabPFNv2 on datasets with a high ratio of categorical features, despite TabPFNv2 having a more sophisticated categorical prior.

## Relation to Prior Work

TabICL extends the prior-data fitted network (PFN) paradigm introduced by TabPFN (Hollmann et al., 2022) and its successor TabPFNv2 (Hollmann et al., 2025), which perform tabular classification via in-context learning in a single forward pass. The key architectural departure is a two-stage design: a Set Transformer (Lee et al., 2019)-based column-wise embedding that collapses the feature dimension into fixed-size row vectors, followed by a standard transformer for ICL—replacing TabPFNv2's alternating column/row attention that operates on the full table without dimension collapse. RoPE (Su et al., 2023) is repurposed from NLP to break symmetry between identically distributed features, addressing a representation collapse problem also observed in TabPFNv2.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By scaling ICL from ~10K to 500K samples while being up to 10× faster than TabPFNv2, TabICL demonstrates that implicit learning in a single forward pass can compete with—and on large datasets surpass—gradient-boosted trees that require extensive hyperparameter tuning. This challenges the long-standing assumption that GBDTs dominate in the large-data regime and opens the door to foundation-model-style tabular prediction on industrial-scale tables. Open questions include whether ICL retains its edge at millions of samples, how to handle regression and missing values natively, and whether the RoPE-induced column-order sensitivity can be fully resolved without sacrificing the collapse fix.
