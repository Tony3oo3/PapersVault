## Problem

Existing LLM-based decision-making methods face a fundamental decoupling: autoregressive policies (e.g., ReAct) lack long-term planning, while planning-based methods (e.g., RAP, LATS) over-rely on potentially inaccurate simulated rollouts for action evaluation. Neither approach effectively reconciles the LLM's inherent prior knowledge with error-corrected planning insights, leading to suboptimal decisions in multi-step, sparse-reward environments.

## Input / Output

At each time step, LAC receives a natural language task goal and the full observation-action history as text, then samples n candidate actions from the LLM prior policy. The critic computes Q-values by extracting log-probabilities of special positive/negative tokens (e.g., 'GOOD'/'BAD') conditioned on the goal, history, candidate action, and a predicted future trajectory from a forward world model. The output is a single selected action (argmax of the updated policy) and a reweighted action distribution over the n candidates, with no gradient computation required at inference time.

## Method

LAC introduces two key mechanisms. First, a Q-function estimator that computes action values as the log-odds ratio of LLM token probabilities for positive vs. negative outcome tokens (Q = log P(y_w)/P(y_l)), augmented by multi-step trajectory rollouts from a prompted forward model and structured reflections on past actions that serve as lightweight Chain-of-Thought. Second, a gradient-free policy update derived from a KL-constrained maximization objective, yielding the closed-form solution π_new(a) ∝ π_LLM(a) · exp(α · Q_LLM(a)), which reweights the prior policy by the critic's evaluations without any backpropagation. The hyperparameter α interpolates between pure prior-policy behavior (α=0) and pure critic-driven selection (α→∞), and the method requires only ~18 fine-tuning trajectories to improve the reflection and forward-model components via next-token prediction loss.

## Results

On ALFWorld (134 tasks), LAC with fine-tuning achieves 79–84% success rate across 7B/8B models (CodeLlama-7B: 79%, Gemma-7B: 84%, Llama-3-8B: 78%, Mistral-7B: 79%), substantially outperforming ReAct with fine-tuning (38–73%). On WebShop (100 tasks), LAC achieves 32–46% success rate and 0.58–0.72 average reward, consistently surpassing all baselines. On BabyAI-Text (300 tasks), LAC outperforms all baselines across all six task types. Notably, LAC with 7B/8B open-source models surpasses ReAct with GPT-4 on most ALFWorld and BabyAI-Text settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LAC outperforms all five primary LLM-based baselines (ReAct, RAP, ICPI, RAFA, LATS) across all three main benchmarks (ALFWorld, BabyAI-Text, WebShop) and all four base models tested. Against ReAct, the gap is most dramatic: on ALFWorld with fine-tuning, LAC achieves 79–84% vs. ReAct's 38–73%, and LAC with 7B/8B models even surpasses ReAct with GPT-4 on most settings. Against RAP and LATS (tree-search/planning methods), LAC wins on both success rate and total computational cost, since LAC's higher success rate means fewer steps per task despite higher per-step inference cost. Against ICPI (policy iteration via LLMs), LAC is superior, particularly in sparse-reward settings where ICPI struggles. Against RAFA (sub-goal completion evaluation), LAC consistently achieves higher success rates. In the additional ALFWorld comparison (Appendix A.5), LAC also outperforms Conventional Decision Transformer, which performs similarly to ReAct due to insufficient training data (only 18 trajectories).

## Relation to Prior Work

LAC sits at the intersection of two LLM decision-making paradigms: direct policy generation (ReAct, Reflexion) and planning-based evaluation (RAP, LATS, ICPI). It departs from both by framing the problem as a principled actor-critic loop where the critic extracts Q-values from the LLM's internal token logits (GOOD/BAD probabilities) augmented by forward-model rollouts, and the policy is updated via a closed-form KL-constrained solution inherited from reward-weighted regression (Peters & Schaal, 2007) and DPO (Rafailov et al., 2024). Unlike prior LLM actor-critic attempts (Zhang et al., 2023) that the authors characterize as 'simplistic combinations,' LAC provides a theoretical derivation for the policy update and a gradient-free implementation that avoids backpropagation through the LLM.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that 7B/8B open-source models with LAC can match or exceed GPT-4+ReAct on complex multi-step tasks, reducing reliance on proprietary APIs for agentic decision-making. The gradient-free closed-form update is attractive for deployment since it avoids the high computational cost of gradient-based actor-critic training on LLMs. Key open questions include: whether the single-step rollout (no tree search) limits performance in longer-horizon tasks, how the method scales to larger or reasoning LLMs (the authors note preliminary failures with DeepSeek-R1), and whether the ad-hoc treatment of continuous rewards (WebShop) can be made more principled. The reflection component's advantage over CoT, while empirically supported, remains somewhat opaque in mechanism.
