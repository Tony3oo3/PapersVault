## Problem

Probabilistic verification of neural networks—formally proving statements about a network's output distribution under an input probability distribution—lacks a fast, general, sound, and complete algorithm. Existing approaches such as FAIRSQUARE rely on expensive SMT solving for input-space refinement, while SPACESCANNER and others sacrifice soundness or restrict to specific problem subclasses, resulting in solving times of tens of minutes or more on standard benchmarks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250176/cropped_page4_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a deterministic neural network, a hyperrectangular input space (potentially unbounded), an input probability distribution that admits exact hyperrectangle probability computation (e.g., univariate distributions, mixture models, Bayesian Networks), and a satisfaction function built from linear functions, multiplication, division, and monotone functions. It produces a sequence of iteratively tightening lower and upper bounds on each probability in the verification problem, and ultimately a definitive verdict (satisfied or violated) for the overall probabilistic property. The bounds are guaranteed sound at every iteration, unlike probably-sound or sampling-based alternatives.

## Method

PV (Probabilistic Verification) is a massively parallel branch-and-bound algorithm that iteratively refines probability bounds by partitioning the input space into hyperrectangles. Its core subroutine, PROBBOUNDS, selects a batch of branches by probability mass (SELECTPROB), applies a bound propagation technique (CROWN) to determine which branches are certainly satisfied or violated, prunes those branches, and splits inconclusive ones using BABSB or LONGESTEDGE heuristics. Probability bounds are updated by exactly integrating the input density over the pruned satisfied and violated regions. The outer PV loop runs PROBBOUNDS in parallel for each probability in the verification problem and applies bound propagation to the satisfaction function to decide when the overall property is proven or disproven. This design replaces the expensive SMT-based refinement of FAIRSQUARE with computationally cheap input splitting and linear-relaxation bound propagation from non-probabilistic verification, yielding soundness and (under mild conditions) completeness while reducing solving times from tens of minutes to tens of seconds.

## Results

On the ACAS Xu safety benchmark, PROBBOUNDS tightens probability bounds to a margin below 0.7% within one hour, while PROVE_SLR requires 4h35m–12h21m to compute exact violation rates. On the ACAS Xu robustness benchmark (125 instances), PROBBOUNDS achieves a mean runtime of 22s (median 6s) versus 33 minutes per instance for SPACESCANNER. On the FairSquare fairness benchmark, PV solves four additional instances within a 15-minute timeout, with a median runtime of 4s compared to 44s for FAIRSQUARE. On the new MiniACSIncome benchmark, PV solves 7 of 8 instances (up to 7 input dimensions) within one hour.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PV outperforms all five compared baselines across every benchmark. Against FAIRSQUARE (Albarghouthi et al., 2017), PV solves four more fairness instances within the 15-minute timeout and achieves a median runtime of 4s versus 44s (mean 109s, max 657s). Against SPACESCANNER (Converse et al., 2020), PROBBOUNDS achieves a mean runtime of 22s on the ACAS Xu robustness benchmark versus 33 minutes per instance, a roughly 90× speedup. Against PROVE_SLR (Marzari et al., 2023b), PROBBOUNDS reaches a <0.7% bound margin within one hour while PROVE_SLR requires 4–12 hours to compute exact violation rates. Against ε-PROVE (Marzari et al., 2024), PROBBOUNDS produces tighter *sound* bounds within 10s in 2 of 3 ACAS Xu cases, whereas ε-PROVE needs at least 57s and only provides a *probably sound* (99.9% confidence) upper bound. Against PREIMGAPPROX (Zhang et al., 2024), PV proves the VCAS correctness property in 0.13s versus 16.42s, and PV's result is sound while PREIMGAPPROX's is an unsound sampling approximation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the massively parallel branch-and-bound framework from non-probabilistic neural network verification (Bunel et al., 2020; Xu et al., 2021) and its core bound-propagation engine CROWN (Zhang et al., 2018) to the probabilistic setting, where prior sound algorithms relied on fundamentally different machinery: FAIRSQUARE used SMT solving to refine input-space partitions, SPACESCANNER used concolic execution with polytope splitting, and PROVE_SLR computed exact probabilities via exhaustive B&B. The key conceptual departure is that PV treats probability bounding as an iterative refinement problem—successively splitting hyperrectangles and accumulating pruned-region probabilities—rather than attempting to compute the exact probability in one pass, which is what makes the cheap bound-propagation step sufficient. This positions PV as strictly more general than FAIRSQUARE (fairness-only) and PROVE_SLR (#DNN-only) while remaining sound, unlike SPACESCANNER and PREIMGAPPROX.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dramatic speedup (orders of magnitude over existing sound verifiers) makes probabilistic verification of neural networks practically usable in safety-critical and fairness-auditing contexts where previously it was infeasible within reasonable time budgets. The MiniACSIncome benchmark, with its Bayesian Network input distributions up to 8 dimensions, provides a much harder testbed than prior benchmarks and will be useful for future algorithmic comparisons. Limitations include the requirement for hyperrectangular input spaces (mitigable via linear transformations for multivariate normals) and the mild completeness assumption that the satisfaction boundary has zero probability mass. Open questions include how the approach scales to very high-dimensional continuous inputs and whether the hyperrectangle restriction can be lifted without sacrificing the efficient probability computation that underpins the algorithm.
