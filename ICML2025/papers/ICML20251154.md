## Problem

Existing regret bounds for Bayesian Optimization from Human Feedback (BOHF) scale as Õ(Γ(T)κ²√T) or Õ((Γ(T)T)^(3/4)), which can become vacuous (linear in T) for common kernels like Matérn and depend on κ, the curvature of the sigmoid link function that can exceed 22,000 for bounded utilities. This paper closes the gap by proving Õ(√(Γ(T)T)) regret, matching the order-optimal bounds of conventional BO with scalar feedback, and showing that preferential samples are as sample-efficient as scalar-valued samples.

## Input / Output

At each step the algorithm selects a pair of actions (x, x') from a continuous or large discrete space and receives a single binary preference label y ∈ {0,1} indicating which action is preferred, modeled via the Bradley-Terry-Luce sigmoid on the utility difference. The known kernel k defines a derived 'dueling kernel' on action pairs, and the algorithm outputs a near-optimal action x̂_T after T preference queries, with the guarantee that P(x* ≻ x̂_T) − 1/2 ≤ ε for a sample complexity matching conventional BO lower bounds (e.g., Õ(d log(1/δ)/ε²) for linear kernels).

## Method

MR-LPF (Multi-Round Learning from Preference-based Feedback) partitions the T-step horizon into R ≈ ⌈log₂log₂(T)⌉+1 rounds of geometrically increasing length. Within each round, action pairs are selected purely by maximum kernel-based posterior variance (independent of observed labels), and at the end of each round, upper confidence bounds on preference probabilities eliminate actions that cannot be optimal, shrinking the candidate set M_r. This multi-round structure is the key insight: because query points within a round depend only on variance (not on labels), the observation points are independent of the observation values, enabling a tighter confidence interval that removes the √Γ(T) factor present in prior analyses. After the first round, the elimination rule guarantees that all remaining pairs have μ(h(x,x')) ∈ [1/4, 3/4], bounding the effective curvature κ_r to a constant (6) for all subsequent rounds and eliminating the κ² dependency entirely. The prediction uses a regularized negative log-likelihood loss solved via the representer theorem on the dueling kernel, yielding the final regret bound Õ(√(Γ(T)T)).

## Results

The paper establishes a regret bound of $\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(\sqrt{\Gamma(T)T})$ for the BOHF problem, matching the order-optimal bounds of conventional Bayesian Optimization and eliminating the $\kappa^2$ dependency present in prior work. Experimentally, MR-LPF consistently achieves lower cumulative regret than MaxMinLCB across all test functions (SE kernel, Matérn kernels with $\nu=2.5$ and $\nu=1.5$, Ackley function, and the Yelp Open Dataset), with sharp regret drops at round boundaries reflecting effective action elimination. The derived sample complexities match the $\Omega(1/\epsilon^2)$ lower bounds for conventional BO with scalar feedback, showing that the same number of preferential samples suffices as scalar-valued samples.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MaxMinLCB (Pásztor et al., 2024), MR-LPF achieves a strictly tighter theoretical regret bound, removing both the $\kappa^2$ factor and the extra $\sqrt{\Gamma(T)}$ factor, which is critical for kernels with polynomially decaying eigenvalues (e.g., Matérn) where the prior bound becomes vacuous (linear in $T$). Experimentally, MR-LPF outperforms MaxMinLCB on all six synthetic test functions and on the Yelp Open Dataset, with the gap widening as $T$ increases due to the multi-round elimination structure. Against POP-BO (Xu et al., 2024), MR-LPF improves the regret bound by a factor of $\mathcal{O}((\Gamma(T)T)^{1/4})$, though no direct experimental comparison is provided. The key algorithmic distinction is that MR-LPF selects pairs based solely on kernel variance within each round (independent of observation values), enabling tighter confidence intervals and the elimination of the $\kappa$ dependency, whereas both baselines select points based on statistics that depend on prior observations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the BOHF framework established by Pásztor et al. (2024) and Xu et al. (2024), which introduced the Bradley-Terry-Luce preference feedback model for kernel-based optimization. The key methodological departure is the adoption of a multi-round elimination structure inspired by the BPE algorithm from conventional BO (Li & Scarlett, 2022), adapted to the preference-based setting where observation points within each round are selected purely by variance (independent of feedback values). This structural choice enables tighter confidence intervals that eliminate the κ² curvature factor and reduce the information-gain dependence from Γ(T) to √Γ(T), matching the order-optimal bounds of conventional BO.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central result—that binary pairwise preference samples are as sample-efficient as scalar-valued samples for identifying near-optimal actions—removes a perceived fundamental cost of the reduced feedback model, with direct implications for applications like LLM prompt optimization where only comparative human judgments are available. The paper acknowledges that the comparison to Scarlett et al. (2017) lower bounds is informal (Gaussian vs. Gumbel noise), leaving open whether the bounds are truly tight. Practical limitations include the reliance on RKHS membership, the need for a known kernel, and the fact that the multi-round structure requires careful tuning of round sizes; extending to other link functions, non-stationary settings, or batched feedback remains open.
