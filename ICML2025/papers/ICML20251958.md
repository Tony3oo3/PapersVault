## Problem

In continual learning, the total dataset size is unknown at training time, making cross-validation-based model sizing infeasible. Existing sparse GP methods either fix the number of inducing points (risking under- or over-capacity), add a fixed count per batch, or rely on thresholds that must be tuned to dataset-specific properties such as noise level. This paper asks 'how big is big enough?' for variational GP approximations in streaming settings, seeking a principled, dataset-agnostic criterion that adapts model capacity without prior knowledge of data characteristics.

## Input / Output

The method consumes data in sequential batches (X_n, y_n) without storing previous batches, receiving only the current batch, the previous inducing point set Z_o, and kernel hyperparameters θ_n. It outputs an updated inducing point set Z_n (old points kept fixed, new points selected exclusively from the current batch) and predictive mean and variance for future queries. A single user-defined threshold δ (recommended 0.035) controls the accuracy–complexity trade-off and must be set before training begins, with no access to dataset size, noise level, or input-space coverage.

## Method

VIPS (Vegas Inducing Point Selection) extends the online sparse GP framework of Bui et al. (2017) by deriving an online log-marginal-likelihood upper bound, following the batch-case strategy of Titsias (2014). At each batch, it iteratively adds inducing points via the greedy variance criterion (maximizing marginal variance in the conditional prior) until the gap between this upper bound and the online ELBO falls below a threshold α = δ(L* − L_noise), where L_noise is the log-likelihood under a Gaussian model with the running observation mean and variance. This noise-relative threshold is scale-invariant, making it robust across datasets with different sizes and noise levels. The method provides a theoretical guarantee bounding the KL divergence between the approximate posterior and the best achievable variational posterior by α. A single value of δ achieves near-optimal performance across six UCI benchmarks and a real-world magnetic field mapping task without dataset-specific tuning, unlike competing methods OIPS and Conditional Variance.

## Results

At the operating point achieving a 10% RMSE threshold relative to a full-batch GP, VIPS requires the fewest inducing points on 5 of 6 UCI datasets: 134 on Skillcraft (vs. 174 for OIPS, 739 for CV), 1904 on Kin8nm (vs. 6458 for OIPS, 6316 for CV), 291 on Elevators (vs. 298 for OIPS, 3133 for CV), and 650 on Bike (vs. 1964 for OIPS, MAX.7000 for CV). On the Naval dataset, CV uses slightly fewer points (49 vs. 57), while OIPS fails to meet the accuracy threshold entirely. A single hyperparameter δ=0.035 achieves this performance across all datasets without per-dataset tuning, and VIPS also reduces training time compared to even an oracle fixed-size model on the Naval dataset.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VIPS outperforms both baselines on the majority of datasets by requiring substantially fewer inducing points while maintaining the same 10% RMSE accuracy threshold. Against Conditional Variance (CV), VIPS reduces model size by 70% on Kin8nm (1904 vs. 6316), 79% on Elevators (291 vs. 3133), and avoids CV's failure to converge within the 7000-point limit on Bike (650 vs. MAX.7000). Against OIPS, VIPS uses 71% fewer points on Kin8nm (1904 vs. 6458) and 67% fewer on Bike (650 vs. 1964), while OIPS fails entirely on the near-noiseless Naval dataset across all tested hyperparameter values. The critical practical advantage is that VIPS achieves this with a single pre-set hyperparameter (δ=0.035) that works uniformly across all six datasets, whereas both CV and OIPS require dataset-specific tuning of their thresholds—a requirement incompatible with the continual learning setting where data characteristics are unknown in advance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the streaming sparse GP framework of Bui et al. (2017), which provides the online ELBO for continual learning, and the variational inducing variable approximation of Titsias (2009). It departs from prior adaptive capacity methods (OIPS, VAR-GP, Conditional Variance) by replacing their dataset-dependent thresholds with a noise-relative KL-gap criterion derived from an online LML upper bound (following Titsias, 2014). The key conceptual departure is framing model size selection as a compression problem: the threshold δ controls how much of the information gain over a noise model must be captured, yielding a single hyperparameter that transfers across datasets without re-tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in eliminating the need for dataset-specific hyperparameter tuning in continual learning, where cross-validation is infeasible because data cannot be stored. The demonstrated cross-dataset consistency of a single δ value (recommended δ=0.035) is a meaningful step toward truly black-box continual learning. However, the method is validated only for single-layer GPs (equivalently, single-layer neural networks); extending the KL-gap stopping criterion to deep networks with multiple adaptive layers remains an open and non-trivial challenge. The paper also does not address non-Gaussian likelihoods or classification, limiting immediate applicability.
