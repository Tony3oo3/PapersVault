## Problem

Continual LLM unlearning—where a model must sequentially forget multiple pieces of data over time—suffers from severe utility degradation that is far worse than one-time unlearning. Two compounding mechanisms drive this: accumulative decline, where each task's parameter changes compound over successive tasks, and cascading degradation, where preceding tasks partially erase memory of subsequent task data, causing fixed-intensity unlearning to over-erase and further damage utility. This practical setting remains largely unexplored in the LLM unlearning literature.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sequence of disjoint unlearning datasets (text passages or QA pairs), a retain set for utility monitoring, and the current LLM parameters at each step. During training of task t, only D_u^t and the retain set D_r are accessible; data from other tasks is unavailable. The output is the updated parameter vector θ^t = θ^{t-1} − λ(θ_ft^t − θ^{t-1}), where θ_ft^t is the intermediate fine-tuned model. A cumulative binary mask M is maintained across all tasks to track which parameters have been substantially modified, feeding into both the gradient sparsity and adaptive learning rate modules.

## Method

ALKN operates within the task vector framework: it fine-tunes the current model on the unlearning data to reinforce target knowledge, then subtracts the parameter offset (scaled by λ) to negate that knowledge. Three modules modify the fine-tuning process. (1) Entropic Soft Labels replace one-hot cross-entropy targets with soft labels derived from the model's own current predictions, so partially forgotten data receives a gentler optimization signal, directly countering cascading degradation. (2) Dynamic Gradient Sparsity applies a learnable binary mask to gradients, updated each step by aligning unlearning and utility gradient directions (G_u ⊙ G_r) while penalizing overlap with previously activated parameters (−μM), encouraging each task to localize distinct parameter subsets and reducing accumulative decline. (3) Adaptive Parameter Modulation assigns a lower learning rate α_l to parameters already in the cumulative mask M^{t-1} and a higher rate α_h to others, preventing re-unlearning of features already removed by earlier tasks. The mask threshold η is annealed from 100% to 30% activated parameters over training, and the overall procedure is summarized in Algorithm 1.

## Results

On the TOFU dataset (Phi-1.5, 5 continual unlearning tasks), ALKN retains a Model Utility (MU) of 0.4534 after all five tasks, a drop of only 0.06 from the original 0.5237, while achieving F-Rouge of 0.3314 (below the 0.35 threshold for effective unlearning). In contrast, GA+RT collapses to MU 0.0812 and NPO+RT to 0.1841 by Task 5. On the WHP dataset (Llama-2-7B, 3 tasks), ALKN achieves the best utility (R-Rouge 0.4586, T-Rouge 0.5617) while maintaining competitive unlearning (F-Rouge 0.1790, MIA 0.2729), whereas NPO+RT and NPO+KL completely lose utility (R-Rouge and T-Rouge both 0.0).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ALKN outperforms all baselines on final model utility (MU) after 5 continual tasks on TOFU: 0.4534 vs. 0.3938 (EUL, the closest competitor), 0.3817 (NPO), 0.3359 (DPO+RT), and 0.3013 (WAGLE). On unlearning effectiveness (F-Rouge), ALKN (0.3314) is competitive with NPO+RT (0.3234) and TV (0.3028), while EUL (0.5058) and NPO (0.4033) show severe under-unlearning. On the WHP dataset, ALKN achieves the best utility metrics (R-Rouge 0.4586, T-Rouge 0.5617) while matching or exceeding baselines on unlearning: its F-Rouge of 0.1790 is lower (better) than WAGLE (0.2946) and SO-NPO (0.3080), and its MIA of 0.2729 is comparable to NPO+RT (0.2607) and NPO+KL (0.2452). The key differentiator is that gradient-ascent-based methods (GA+RT, NPO+RT) suffer catastrophic utility collapse (MU < 0.2) by Task 5, while ALKN's task-vector foundation with adaptive masking limits the total utility drop to under 0.06. WAGLE and EUL preserve utility better than GA/NPO variants but fail to achieve sufficient unlearning (F-Rouge > 0.40), indicating they under-unlearn rather than over-unlearn.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the task vector framework (Ilharco et al., 2023) for LLM unlearning, which fine-tunes to reinforce target knowledge and then subtracts the parameter offset to negate it. It departs from prior single-task unlearning methods (GA, NPO, WAGLE, EUL, SKU) by explicitly addressing the sequential multi-task setting, identifying two distinct failure modes—accumulative decline and cascading degradation—that are absent from one-shot unlearning. The dynamic gradient sparsity module draws on gradient masking ideas from von Oswald et al. (2021) for meta/continual learning, while the TRAVIS evaluation corpus repurposes membership inference attack techniques (Shi et al., 2024a) to construct a utility-sensitive test set.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Continual unlearning is a practically critical scenario (repeated GDPR deletion requests, iterative copyright takedowns) that prior work largely ignored, and this paper provides both a formal characterization of why naive sequential application of existing methods fails and a working solution that retains >95% utility across five tasks. The TRAVIS dataset is a useful and more sensitive evaluation tool than domain-specific retain sets. Open questions include whether the three-module approach scales to larger models (tested only on 7B-class), how it behaves when unlearning tasks are highly heterogeneous rather than homogeneous, and whether the dynamic mask can be made more computationally efficient for very large architectures.
