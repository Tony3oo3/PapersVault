## Problem

Existing two-stage Learning-to-Defer methods handle classification and regression independently, yet many real-world tasks—object detection, electronic health record analysis—require a single agent to jointly handle both components, making independent deferral decisions meaningless or suboptimal. No prior framework provides a unified deferral mechanism with theoretical guarantees for multi-task settings where classification and regression outputs are interdependent.

## Input / Output

The system consumes a raw input x (an RGB image for object detection or a tabular EHR record), the fixed outputs of a pre-trained multi-task model g(x) = (h(w(x)), f(w(x))) producing a class score vector and a regression value, and the fixed outputs of J pre-trained experts m_j(x) = (m_j^h(x), m_j^f(x)). The trained rejector r(x) outputs a single agent index j ∈ {0, …, J} indicating which agent (the model or one of the experts) should handle the query, with the final prediction being the selected agent's joint classification-and-regression output. The rejector is trained post-hoc via gradient descent on the surrogate deferral loss, with the model and experts held frozen.

## Method

The paper introduces a surrogate deferral loss Φ_def^ν(r, g, m, z) = Σ_j τ_j(g(x), m(x), z) · Φ_01^ν(r, x, j), where Φ_01^ν is a cross-entropy multiclass surrogate parameterized by ν ≥ 0 and τ_j is the aggregated cost of selecting agent j over all others. This construction is proven to be both Bayes-consistent and (G, R)-consistent, with explicit consistency bounds scaled by the L1-norm of the expected aggregated cost vector ‖τ̄‖₁. The paper further derives a closed-form characterization of the minimizability gap for the multi-expert two-stage regime, recovering Shannon entropy for ν = 1 and L1−L∞ norm differences for ν = 2, and generalizes prior single-expert results. An encoder-aware decomposition (Corollary 4.11) separates the end-to-end excess risk into rejector optimization error, head sub-optimality, and representation gap, aligning the theory with standard multi-task architectures that share a backbone w across classification and regression heads.

## Results

On the MIMIC-IV EHR task, the proposed method achieves 70.0% accuracy and 1.28 smooth L1 loss, balancing both classification and regression while allocating 66% of queries to the model. The classification-focused baseline (Mao et al., 2023a) reaches 71.3% accuracy but suffers a high sL1 of 1.45, while the regression-focused baseline (Mao et al., 2024h) achieves the best sL1 of 1.18 but drops to 50.7% accuracy. On Pascal VOC object detection, the system adaptively allocates queries across three Faster R-CNN agents of varying complexity, with allocation shifting from Expert 2 to Expert 1 as the cost differential increases (β₂ ≥ 0.2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two existing two-stage L2D methods on the MIMIC-IV EHR task: a classification-focused rejector (Mao et al., 2023a) and a regression-focused rejector (Mao et al., 2024h). Against the classification-focused baseline, the proposed method trades a small accuracy drop (70.0% vs. 71.3%) for a substantial regression improvement (sL1 1.28 vs. 1.45) and a higher model allocation rate (66% vs. 60%). Against the regression-focused baseline, the proposed method dramatically outperforms on accuracy (70.0% vs. 50.7%) at a modest sL1 cost (1.28 vs. 1.18), while allocating far more queries to the model (66% vs. 38%). The key finding is that neither task-specific baseline can handle the heterogeneous expert expertise across classification and regression clusters, whereas the unified multi-task deferral achieves a balanced trade-off. On Pascal VOC object detection, no direct numerical baseline comparison is provided since existing L2D methods cannot handle the interdependent classification-regression structure of object detection, making the proposed approach the first to address this setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the two-stage Learning-to-Defer (L2D) framework from Mao et al. (2023a) for classification and Mao et al. (2024h) for regression into a unified multi-task setting where a single rejector must jointly allocate decisions for both classification and regression outputs. The theoretical machinery draws on the H-consistency bounds of Awasthi et al. (2022) and the minimizability gap analysis of Mao et al. (2024i), generalizing both to the multi-expert, multi-task regime. The key conceptual departure is that prior L2D methods treat classification and regression as independent deferral problems, whereas this paper formalizes the case where the two tasks are interdependent (e.g., object detection) and must be deferred to a single agent simultaneously.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first L2D framework that handles joint classification-regression deferral, addressing a gap that becomes critical in applications like object detection and medical triage where the two outputs are inseparable. The encoder-aware consistency bounds (Corollary 4.11) explicitly decompose excess risk into rejector error, head sub-optimality, and representation gap, providing a principled justification for the two-stage pipeline. Limitations include the reliance on a pre-trained (frozen) multi-task model, the somewhat artificial expert specialization in the EHR experiment, and evaluation on only two datasets. Open questions include extending to one-stage multi-task L2D, handling more than two task types, and validating on larger-scale detection benchmarks beyond Pascal VOC.
