## Problem

Standard CVaR policy gradient methods discard all trajectories above the VaR threshold, leading to severe sample inefficiency (e.g., 95% of trajectories wasted at α=0.05) and a 'blindness to success' where the policy cannot learn from high-performing runs. This paper asks whether the CVaR objective can be reformulated to retain all trajectories while preserving optimality.

## Input / Output

The policy operates on an augmented state space S⁺ = (S, Σr) that includes the cumulative discounted reward up to the current timestep, making the static CVaR objective Markovian. Rather than using raw episode returns, the method adjusts per-timestep rewards so they sum to the capped return (r_t^a = min(R_t, C) − min(R_{t−1}, C)), enabling standard reward-to-go credit assignment within PPO. The cap C is a scalar updated online via an exponential moving average of the batch VaR estimate.

## Method

The key reformulation replaces trajectory discarding with return capping: all trajectories are retained, but any return exceeding a threshold C is truncated to C. Proposition 4.1 proves that if C = VaR_α(π*), then maximizing E[min(R(τ), C)] is equivalent to maximizing CVaR_α, so the optimal policy is unchanged. Since the true VaR of the optimal policy is unknown, the cap is approximated via a low-variance update rule C_k = C_{k−1} + η(VaR_α(π_{θ_{k−1}}) − C_{k−1}), with a minimum cap C^M (set to the CVaR of a conservative baseline) to prevent vanishing gradients. Training uses PPO with the adjusted per-timestep rewards, allowing all N trajectories to contribute to each gradient update rather than only the bottom α-proportion.

## Results

Across five risk-aware RL environments, Return Capping consistently achieves higher CVaR than standard CVaR-PG and CVaR-PPO baselines, with the most dramatic gains in sample efficiency. In the Discrete Guarded Maze (CVaR α=0.2), Return Capping converges to the CVaR-optimal policy in approximately half as many environment steps as CVaR-PPO. In the Continuous Guarded Maze (CVaR α=0.05), all 6 Return Capping seeds converge to the CVaR-optimal policy, compared to only 2/6 for CVaR-PPO and 0/6 for CVaR-PG. In the Autonomous Vehicle (CVaR α=0.05), 2 of 8 CVaR-PPO seeds fail to converge to goal-reaching policies, whereas Return Capping converges reliably across all seeds.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Expected Value PPO baseline (Schulman et al.), Return Capping achieves substantially higher CVaR in all environments, as expected since the risk-neutral policy is suboptimal for the CVaR objective; however, the Expected Value PPO serves primarily as a reference for the risk-neutral optimum rather than a competitive CVaR method. Against the standard CVaR-PG baseline (Tamar et al.), Return Capping wins decisively: CVaR-PG converges to an overly conservative 'bet nothing' policy in the Betting Game, fails to reach the goal in all 6 seeds of the Continuous Guarded Maze, and cannot discover any successful policy in Lunar Lander. Against the MIX baseline (Luo et al.), tested only in Lunar Lander, Return Capping clearly outperforms: the MIX baseline converges to the risk-neutral policy rather than the CVaR-optimal one, illustrating that mixing a risk-neutral policy can interfere with learning the risk-averse policy when the two are in direct opposition. The paper's own CVaR-PPO baseline (not in the original compares_to list) is the closest competitor, matching Return Capping in the Autonomous Vehicle but showing lower convergence reliability (2/8 seed failures) and slower convergence in the Discrete Guarded Maze.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reformulates the CVaR policy gradient objective from Tamar et al. (2015) by replacing trajectory discarding with return capping, proving that the two objectives are equivalent when the cap equals VaR_α(π*). It departs from the closest prior work—Greenberg et al. (2022), which requires a Context-MDP formulation, and Luo et al. (2024), which relies on policy mixing—by offering a simpler, model-agnostic modification that works within standard PPO. The paper also introduces a CVaR-PPO baseline (applying the PPO clip loss only to the worst-α trajectories), which it shows outperforms the original CVaR-PG in several environments.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Return Capping addresses a practical bottleneck in risk-averse RL: when α is small, standard CVaR-PG discards most trajectories, wasting samples and blinding the policy to successful runs. The clean equivalence proof makes the method theoretically grounded, and the empirical gains across four environments are consistent. However, the minimum cap hyperparameter (C^M) introduces sensitivity—particularly in the Guarded Maze, where setting C^M to the theoretically optimal VaR actually degrades performance by trapping the policy in a risk-neutral local optimum—raising open questions about robust cap initialization. The experiments are limited to relatively simple environments, and the authors note that combining Return Capping with other CVaR methods (annealing, distributional RL, policy mixing) remains unexplored.
