## Problem

The KV cache in transformer LLMs grows linearly with sequence length, quickly exceeding GPU VRAM and becoming the primary bottleneck for long-sequence inference. Existing post-training compression methods (eviction, merging, quantization) cause irreversible information loss that degrades subsequent decoding steps, while naive CPU offloading introduces prohibitive CPU-GPU transfer latency. The paper addresses how to drastically reduce VRAM usage without information forgetting and without adding inference latency.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251097/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard text prompt and, during prefilling, offloads the full 16-bit KV cache layer-by-layer to CPU RAM while retaining only a low-bit (1- or 2-bit) quantized copy in VRAM. At each decoding step, the model simultaneously processes two tokens—an output token and a speculative token—against a hybrid KV cache consisting of the low-bit copy plus a small set of top-k 16-bit KV pairs prefetched from CPU. The output is the standard autoregressive text token, with the speculative token's attention scores used internally to drive the next prefetch, so no additional output is exposed to the user.

## Method

SpeCache is a training-free inference method that decodes two tokens in parallel at each step: an output token computed against prefetched top-k 16-bit KV pairs (ensuring accuracy) and a speculative token computed against the low-bit KV cache in VRAM (predicting which KV pairs the next step will need). The speculative token's attention scores identify the top-k most relevant KV pairs for the following step, which are then prefetched from CPU RAM in parallel with the computation of subsequent transformer layers, eliminating the CPU-GPU transfer latency. Because LLM decoding is memory-IO bound, processing two tokens simultaneously shares model weights and KV cache reads without meaningful additional latency. The paper also modifies KIVI's 1-bit quantization by re-deriving the zero-point and scaling factor under a uniform-distribution assumption to minimize cumulative quantization error, enabling stable generation at 1-bit precision. Together, these mechanisms allow SpeCache to retain only ~10% of the original KV cache in VRAM while matching full-precision performance on LongBench and Needle-in-a-Haystack.

## Results

On LongBench, SPECACHE with 1-bit KV cache (0.10× size) achieves 40.4 average on Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.2 and 41.9 on LLaMA-3-8B-Instruct, within 1–2% of the full 16-bit KV cache (42.3 and 42.7 respectively), while retaining only ~10% of the original KV cache in VRAM. On the Needle-in-a-Haystack benchmark, 1-bit + SPECACHE recovers retrieval accuracy to near 16-bit levels, whereas 1-bit KIVI alone causes severe degradation. In throughput experiments, SPECACHE increases maximum batch size by up to 12× (1-bit, 32k context), yielding 4.6× higher decoding throughput compared to the full KV cache on a single A6000 GPU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SPECACHE consistently outperforms all three eviction/offloading baselines (H2O, StreamLLM, InfLLM) at equal or higher compression ratios. At 0.25× KV cache size on Mistral-7B, SPECACHE (0.16×, 42.0 avg) beats H2O (38.3), InfLLM (33.0), and StreamLLM (28.9) by 3.7, 9.0, and 13.1 points respectively. At the more aggressive compression level (~0.10–0.13×), the gap widens further: SPECACHE (0.10×, 40.4) surpasses H2O (0.13×, 36.1) by 4.3 points, while InfLLM and StreamLLM drop to 29.0 and 25.1. On LLaMA-3-8B, SPECACHE at 0.11× (41.9) exceeds H2O at 0.13× (40.1) by 1.8 points and is within 0.8 of the full 16-bit baseline (42.7), whereas H2O lags by 2.6. The key advantage is that SPECACHE's speculative prefetching avoids the irreversible information loss inherent in greedy eviction (H2O, StreamLLM) and the retrieval overhead of segment-based offloading (InfLLM), enabling near-lossless performance at 10× compression.

## Relation to Prior Work

SPECACHE sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: KV cache quantization (building directly on KIVI's asymmetric 2-bit scheme, which the authors extend to 1-bit with a modified zero-point and scaling factor) and CPU-GPU offloading (addressing the latency bottleneck identified in FlexGen, InfLLM, and ShadowKV). It departs from greedy eviction methods (H2O, Scissorhands, StreamLLM) by exploiting the query-dependent sparsity of attention—showing that different queries attend to different key subsets—rather than committing to a single static eviction policy. The speculative token mechanism repurposes the parallel-decoding idea from speculative decoding (Leviathan et al., 2023) not for token verification but for one-step-ahead KV pair prediction, enabling prefetching to overlap with computation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a 4.6× throughput gain and 12× batch-size increase at 32k context make long-sequence inference on a single GPU substantially more feasible, and the method is entirely training-free. However, the approach assumes ample CPU RAM to store the full 16-bit KV cache, which limits applicability to extremely long contexts or memory-constrained edge devices. The parallelism is implemented via PyTorch's multi-stream mechanism and Tensor.copy_(), which the authors acknowledge is not theoretically optimal; custom low-level operators could improve efficiency further. Open questions include how the method behaves in multi-GPU or distributed settings, whether the speculative token's accuracy degrades over very long generations, and whether the 1-bit KIVI modification generalizes to architectures beyond the tested LLaMA/Mistral family.
