## Problem

Existing scientific equation discovery benchmarks (SRBench, SRSD) rely on well-known textbook equations that LLMs can trivially recite from memorized variable names and problem descriptions, inflating performance metrics and obscuring genuine discovery capability. This paper addresses the need for a rigorous evaluation framework that prevents memorization while still leveraging the scientific priors embedded in LLMs, enabling fair assessment of data-driven reasoning and hypothesis generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252198/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each discovery task provides a natural-language scientific problem description, named input/output variables with physical meanings, and a numerical dataset (5000 samples for LSR-Synth; filtered subsets for LSR-Transform). The method must produce a symbolic equation hypothesis in any of three representations—equation string, expression tree, or executable Python program—which is then evaluated on both in-domain and held-out out-of-domain (OOD) test sets. The benchmark spans 239 problems across four scientific domains (chemistry, biology, physics, material science) with complexity constrained to match the original Feynman distribution to isolate semantic reasoning from syntactic difficulty.

## Method

LLM-SRBench introduces two complementary problem categories. LSR-Transform (111 problems) systematically transforms the 100 Feynman physics equations by selecting a pivot input variable, switching it with the original target, and symbolically solving for the new target using SymPy; transformations are filtered for analytical solvability, valid data domains, and expression-tree complexity within the original Feynman range, then re-described by GPT-4o to produce unfamiliar mathematical forms of known physics. LSR-Synth (128 problems) combines well-known domain-specific terms (e.g., first-order kinetics, logistic growth) with novel synthetic terms (e.g., kA²/(1+βA⁴), sin(√A(t))) generated by GPT-4o, validated for numerical solvability via SciPy's solve_ivp, assessed for novelty by an LLM judge, and cross-checked by domain experts. Evaluation employs a dual protocol: data fidelity via Acc_τ and NMSE on both ID and OOD splits, and symbolic accuracy via GPT-4o as an equivalence evaluator (validated at 94.6% agreement with human raters on 130 samples), which handles algebraic equivalence across diverse hypothesis representations.

## Results

On LLM-SRBench, the best-performing system (LLM-SR with GPT-4o-mini) achieves only 31.53% symbolic accuracy on LSR-Transform and 20.24% on LSR-Synth Material Science, underscoring the difficulty of the benchmark. LaSR with GPT-4o-mini leads in numeric precision on LSR-Transform (50.45% Acc₀.₁, NMSE 0.0011), while LLM-SR dominates symbolic accuracy. Direct Prompting (DataBlind) baseline achieves near-zero symbolic accuracy (≤7.21%), confirming that LLMs cannot solve these problems through memorization alone. GPT-4o-mini and Llama-3.1-8B consistently outperform GPT-3.5-turbo across all methods and datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LLM-SR (Shojaee et al., 2024b) achieves the highest symbolic accuracy on LSR-Transform (31.53% with GPT-4o-mini) and leads on LSR-Synth Biology (25.30% with Llama-3.1-8B), but LaSR (Grayeli et al., 2024) surpasses it in numeric precision on LSR-Transform (50.45% vs. 39.64% Acc₀.₁) and in symbolic accuracy on LSR-Synth Material Science (28.12% vs. 20.24%). SGA (Ma et al., 2024) consistently underperforms both LLM-SR and LaSR on symbolic accuracy across all domains, though it achieves the best Acc₀.₁ on LSR-Synth Material Science (36.11% with GPT-4o-mini). The non-LLM baseline PySR (Cranmer, 2023) achieves competitive numeric accuracy (56.76% Acc₀.₁ on LSR-Transform) but drops to 0% symbolic accuracy on all non-physics LSR-Synth domains, highlighting that data-driven search alone cannot recover scientifically meaningful equations without domain knowledge. Overall, no single method dominates across all metrics and domains, suggesting complementary strengths: LLM-SR excels at symbolic discovery while LaSR excels at numeric fitting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the standard symbolic regression benchmarks (SRBench/Feynman, SRSD) and recent LLM-based equation discovery methods (LLM-SR, LaSR, SGA), but departs from them by identifying a critical evaluation flaw: existing benchmarks rely on well-known textbook equations that LLMs can solve through memorization rather than genuine data-driven discovery. The LSR-Transform category draws on findings about LLM reasoning fragility under unfamiliar representations (Mirzadeh et al., 2024; Xie et al., 2024), while LSR-Synth systematically expands the handful of anti-memorization problems introduced in LLM-SR (Shojaee et al., 2024b) into a comprehensive multi-domain dataset. The evaluation protocol also introduces a novel LLM-as-judge symbolic accuracy metric and explicit OOD generalization assessment, both absent from prior symbolic regression benchmarks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals that the best current LLM-based method achieves only 31.5% symbolic accuracy, exposing a substantial gap between LLMs' apparent scientific knowledge and their actual discovery capabilities. The complexity-matched analysis showing LSR-Transform is harder than Feynman at identical expression-tree sizes suggests the bottleneck is semantic reasoning over unfamiliar forms rather than handling longer expressions—a distinction that should guide future method design. Open questions include whether the low performance reflects fundamental LLM limitations or simply insufficient search budget, whether OOD evaluation should become standard practice in symbolic regression, and how to assess scientific plausibility and interpretability beyond mathematical correctness. The finding that PySR achieves competitive numerical accuracy but near-zero symbolic accuracy on non-physics domains further motivates LLM-based approaches while clarifying their specific advantage.
