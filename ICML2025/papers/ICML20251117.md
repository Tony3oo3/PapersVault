## Problem

Existing diffusion models for 3D atomic systems are highly specialized to each domain—molecules, crystals, or biomolecules—each requiring distinct multi-modal generative processes on complex product manifolds of categorical and continuous data. Despite sharing the same underlying physics, no unified formulation exists that can jointly generate both periodic materials and non-periodic molecular systems within a single model.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251117/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the model takes only a binary class label (periodic vs. non-periodic) and Gaussian noise, producing either a 3D molecule (atom types + Cartesian coordinates, assembled via RDKit) or a crystal (atom types + fractional coordinates + lattice vectors, assembled via PyMatGen). During training, the unified input representation encodes each system as a set of N atoms with categorical types, continuous 3D coordinates (molecules) or fractional coordinates in [0,1)³ plus a 3×3 lattice matrix (crystals), with null values for inapplicable fields. The VAE compresses this into per-atom latent vectors of dimension d=8, and the DiT operates on these latents with a class-conditional flow matching objective.

## Method

ADiT is a two-stage latent diffusion framework that shifts the complexity of multi-modal atomic representations into a VAE, enabling a simple continuous diffusion process in latent space. Stage 1 trains a VAE with standard (non-equivariant) Transformers as encoder and decoder to jointly reconstruct both molecules and crystals into a shared latent space, using domain-specific loss weights (fractional coordinates + lattice for crystals; Cartesian coordinates for molecules) and learning symmetries via random rotation/translation augmentation. Stage 2 trains a class-conditional Diffusion Transformer (DiT) to perform conditional flow matching over the VAE latents, using classifier-free guidance with the periodic/non-periodic label and self-conditioning for stable sampling. The key architectural choice is using standard Transformers with minimal inductive biases rather than equivariant networks, which yields an order-of-magnitude speedup in inference and enables predictable scaling up to 450M parameters. Joint training on both domains yields higher validity than domain-specific training, demonstrating effective transfer learning between periodic and non-periodic systems.

## Results

On MP20 crystal generation, jointly trained ADiT achieves 91.92% overall validity, 15.4% DFT stable rate, and 5.3% S.U.N. (stable, unique, novel) rate for 10,000 sampled crystals, outperforming the best prior method FlowLLM (90.81% validity, 13.9% stable, 4.7% S.U.N.) and FlowMM (80.30% validity, 4.6% stable, 2.8% S.U.N.). On QM9 molecule generation, ADiT achieves 97.43% validity and 96.92% uniqueness, matching Equivariant Diffusion (97.50% validity) while substantially outperforming GeoLDM (93.80%) and Symphony (83.50%). On GEOM-DRUGS (up to 180 atoms), ADiT reaches 95.3% validity, exceeding SemlaFlow (93.9%) and EQGAT-diff (94.6%), though trailing SemlaFlow slightly on PoseBusters validity (85.3% vs. 87.5%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On MP20, jointly trained ADiT outperforms all crystal-generation baselines across every reported metric: it exceeds FlowLLM (the strongest prior method) by +1.1% overall validity, +1.5% stable rate, and +0.6% S.U.N. rate, and surpasses FlowMM by +11.6% overall validity and +10.8% stable rate. On QM9, ADiT essentially ties Equivariant Diffusion on validity (97.43% vs. 97.50%) while matching or exceeding it on PoseBusters physical-realism checks (e.g., 99.98% double-bond flatness vs. 98.58%), and clearly beats GeoLDM (+3.6% validity) and Symphony (+13.9% validity). On GEOM-DRUGS, ADiT achieves the highest validity (95.3%) among all three methods but trails SemlaFlow by 2.2 points on the aggregate PoseBusters validity score (85.3% vs. 87.5%), while substantially outperforming EQGAT-diff (59.7%). Joint training on both QM9 and MP20 consistently improves generation quality over dataset-specific ADiT variants (e.g., 91.92% vs. 90.13% crystal validity; 97.43% vs. 96.02% molecule validity), demonstrating effective transfer learning between periodic and non-periodic systems.

## Relation to Prior Work

ADiT adapts the latent diffusion paradigm from image generation (Rombach et al., 2022; Peebles & Xie, 2023) to 3D atomic systems, replacing the multi-modal product-manifold diffusion used by equivariant models (CDVAE, DiffCSP, FlowMM, Equivariant Diffusion) with a single diffusion process in a VAE latent space. The key conceptual departure is that both the autoencoder and denoiser use standard Transformers with minimal inductive biases, learning symmetries via data augmentation rather than architectural equivariance. The unification of periodic (crystal) and non-periodic (molecule) generation in one model, with a shared latent space and binary class conditioning, has no direct precedent in the generative chemistry literature, though it draws on the broader latent diffusion and classifier-free guidance toolkits.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ADiT demonstrates that a single model can generate both molecules and crystals at state-of-the-art quality, with joint training yielding mutual improvements — a concrete step toward foundation models for generative chemistry. The 10× inference speedup over equivariant baselines and predictable scaling to 450M parameters make the architecture practically attractive for large-scale sampling. Key limitations include reliance on small training sets (~130K samples), unconditional generation only, and unvalidated performance on very large systems (biomolecules, thousands of atoms). Open questions include whether conditional generation (target properties, motifs, infilling) can be added to enable practical inverse design, and whether the unified framework extends to hybrid systems like protein-ligand complexes.
