## Problem

All prior sublinear-time sparse Fourier transform (SFT) algorithms for continuous signals rely on randomization to handle both frequency collisions and time-varying noise. The discrete deterministic SFT of Li & Nakos (2020) cannot be directly extended to continuous signals because discretization causes a sparsity blow-up and the continuous noise function cannot be controlled by finite deterministic sampling. This paper addresses the open question of whether a deterministic sublinear-time SFT algorithm exists for continuous k-sparse signals with a frequency gap.

## Input / Output

The algorithm observes the noisy continuous signal x(t) = x*(t) + g(t) at a carefully chosen set of O(k² log k log²(F/η)) time points within [0,T], where T ≥ Õ(1/η). The noise g(t) must satisfy a (C,ξ)-noise condition bounding its pointwise maximum by its L² energy plus a g-dependent parameter ξ; bi-Lipschitz functions satisfy this with ξ = 0. The output is an O(k)-sparse vector ẑ over the frequency grid F = {i·η : i·η ∈ [-F,F]} such that |v_j − ẑ_{f_j}| ≤ O(N) for all true frequencies and |ẑ_f| ≤ O(N) for all non-active frequencies, where N captures the noise level and light-hitter energy.

## Method

The core contribution is a de-randomization procedure that finds a fixed sequence of d = O(k log(F/η)) hashing parameters (σ_r, b_r) guaranteeing no bad events (frequency collisions in the same bucket) across all pairs in F. This adapts the pessimistic estimator framework of Li & Nakos (2020) to the continuous setting, where the offset function o_{f,σ,b}(f') involves a continuous convolution structure rather than discrete modular arithmetic. The key subroutine HASHTOBINS is extended to continuous signals using a flat filter G with B = Θ(k) buckets, and its error is controlled by the (C,ξ)-noise assumption rather than random sampling. The main algorithm (Algorithm 4) iteratively calls a SUBLINEAR procedure that runs HASHTOBINS over the de-randomized hash sequence, uses a one-sparse recovery subroutine to locate heavy-hitter frequencies via phase-difference intervals, and applies a geometrically decaying threshold ν^(t) over O(log(F/η)) rounds, achieving Õ(k²·polylog(F/η)) samples and time.

## Results

The paper presents the first deterministic sublinear-time sparse Fourier transform algorithm for continuous k-sparse signals with frequency gap η. The algorithm requires Õ(k² · polylog(F/η)) samples and runs in Õ(k² · polylog(F/η)) time, recovering all frequencies f_i and amplitudes v_i with error bounded by O(N), where N captures the noise level and signal energy. This matches the Ω(k²) deterministic lower bound (Ganguly 2008; Foucart et al. 2010), making the sample complexity nearly optimal for any deterministic approach. The algorithm handles arbitrary noise satisfying a mild (C,ξ)-noise condition (e.g., bi-Lipschitz functions), which is significantly more general than the noise-free or i.i.d. noise assumptions in prior work.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical experiments or dataset evaluations, so there are no head-to-head numerical comparisons. The key comparison is in asymptotic complexity: the prior best continuous SFT algorithm (Price & Song 2015) is randomized and achieves Õ(k · polylog(F/η)) time, while this work introduces the first deterministic algorithm at the cost of an additional factor of k in both sample and time complexity (Õ(k² · polylog(F/η))). This k² overhead is provably necessary for deterministic methods by the Ω(k²) lower bound of Ganguly (2008) and Foucart et al. (2010). The discrete deterministic algorithm of Li & Nakos (2020) achieves Õ(k · polylog(n)) but cannot be directly applied to continuous signals due to sparsity blow-up from discretization and the presence of continuous noise. The main algorithmic contribution is adapting the pessimistic-estimator de-randomization framework from the discrete setting to handle continuous hashing parameters and the (C,ξ)-noise model, which is a non-trivial extension since the continuous noise function cannot be bounded pointwise without additional assumptions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the deterministic sparse Fourier transform framework of Li & Nakos (2020) from the discrete to the continuous setting, adapting their pessimistic-estimator de-randomization strategy to a fundamentally different hashing and filtering scheme where the noise is a continuous function rather than a finite set of light-hitter frequencies. The continuous signal model, flat filter construction, and noise-handling framework are inherited from Price & Song (2015) and Jin et al. (2023), while the recursive single-entry reduction structure follows Indyk & Kapralov (2014) and Kapralov (2017). The key departure is that the discrete de-randomization cannot be directly transplanted: the continuous noise function spans the entire frequency domain, the time-sampling points are non-uniform relative to the active frequencies, and the hashing parameters must be drawn from a different sample range, requiring a new analysis of the offset distribution and a new noise model ((C,ξ)-noise) to make the deterministic approach viable.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first sublinear-time deterministic sparse FT algorithm for continuous signals, closing a gap where only randomized methods existed. The Õ(k²) sample complexity matches the known deterministic lower bound, making the result information-theoretically optimal. However, the (C,ξ)-noise assumption, while mild (satisfied by bi-Lipschitz functions), is a necessary concession that has no discrete analogue and may not hold for all practical noise models. Open questions include whether the k² dependence can be improved with additional structural assumptions, whether the approach extends to off-grid frequencies or higher dimensions, and whether the deterministic guarantee can be achieved without the frequency-gap assumption.
