## Problem

Existing LLM-generated text detectors are designed for offline batch classification with fixed thresholds, but practical deployment requires detecting LLM sources in streaming settings where texts arrive sequentially. Naively adapting offline detectors to online use causes the false positive rate to converge to 1 as the number of observed texts grows, since any single misclassification triggers a false alarm. The paper addresses the need for an online detection algorithm with provable statistical guarantees: a controlled type-I error rate, asymptotic power one, and a bounded expected detection time.

## Input / Output

At each round t, the algorithm observes one text y_t from an unknown source and simultaneously samples a human-written text x_t from a pre-prepared pool (e.g., XSum news articles). Both texts are scored by a pluggable score function φ(·) (e.g., DetectGPT, Fast-DetectGPT, Likelihood, RoBERTa), and the score difference g_t = φ(x_t) − φ(y_t) drives the wealth update. The algorithm outputs a binary LLM/human declaration at a data-dependent stopping time τ (when wealth W_t ≥ 1/α), or at a fixed time budget T using a randomized Ville's inequality check. The user must specify a significance level α and, in the non-oracle scenario, an upper bound d_t on |g_t| estimated from the first 10 samples.

## Method

The paper frames online LLM detection as a sequential two-sample hypothesis test (H₀: μ_x = μ_y vs. H₁: μ_x ≠ μ_y) solved via testing by betting. The wealth process W_t = W_{t-1}(1 − g_t θ_t) is maintained as a nonnegative supermartingale under H₀, so Ville's inequality guarantees a valid level-α test at all times. The key mechanism is updating the bet fraction θ_t via Online Newton Step (ONS) on the exp-concave loss ℓ_t(θ) = −ln(1 − g_t θ), which minimizes regret and thus accelerates wealth growth under H₁. The main novelty over prior betting-based tests (Shekhar & Ramdas 2023; Chugg et al. 2023) is relaxing the requirement for a known global bound on |g_t|: instead, the algorithm only needs a per-round hint d_{t+1} ≥ |g_{t+1}|, and the decision space K_{t+1} = [−1/(2d_{t+1}), 1/(2d_{t+1})] is set adaptively to preserve non-negativity. A composite hypothesis extension (|μ_x − μ_y| ≤ ε vs. > ε) is handled by running two one-sided tests in parallel, each at level α/2, to tolerate small mean differences between texts written by different humans.

## Results

Under the oracle scenario (Scenario 1), the best-performing configurations (Fast-DetectGPT, DetectGPT, and Likelihood score functions) detect LLM-generated sources at an average rejection time of approximately t=100 out of a T=500 budget, while consistently maintaining false positive rates (FPR) below the chosen significance level α across 20 values of α (ranging from 0.005 to 0.1) over 1000 repeated runs. Under the more realistic Scenario 2, where d_t and ε must be estimated from the first 10 samples, the best configurations (Fast-DetectGPT with Neo-2.7, and Likelihood with Gemma-2B) achieve detection around t=300 with only slight degradation. The algorithm is a provable level-α sequential test with asymptotic power one, and the expected detection time is bounded by O(d_*²/Δ² · ln(d_*/(αΔ)) + d_*⁴/Δ⁴).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two baselines that adapt the fixed-time permutation test (Good, 2013) to sequential testing: one using a constant significance level α per batch, and another using α/2^i for the i-th batch to control cumulative type-I error via the union bound. Both baselines reject H₀ almost immediately after the first batch (rejection time ≈ batch size k), producing nearly vertical lines in the FPR-vs-rejection-time plots, because the score difference between XSum human texts and LLM-generated news is large enough to trigger rejection on the first batch of 25–500 samples. However, the baselines fail to maintain a valid level-α test: with small batch sizes, the increased variability in text scores causes the observed mean difference to exceed ε under H₀, inflating the FPR above α. The proposed betting-based method achieves a critical balance the baselines cannot: it detects LLMs within ~100–300 rounds while strictly keeping FPR below α across all tested significance levels, without requiring prior knowledge of the score distribution or a fixed batch size.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper applies the sequential hypothesis testing by betting framework (Shafer, 2021; Shekhar & Ramdas, 2023) to the problem of online LLM-generated text detection, building on the Online Newton Step optimization from Hazan et al. (2007) and the composite hypothesis treatment from Chugg et al. (2023). The key methodological departure from prior betting-based testing work is relaxing the assumption that a bound on the score difference g_t is known a priori, instead allowing time-varying bounds d_t that can be estimated from initial samples. On the detection side, the paper repurposes existing offline score functions (DetectGPT, Fast-DetectGPT, Likelihood, etc.) as the 'coin outcome' in the betting game, rather than proposing new detection metrics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap: real-world content (social media, news sites, public comment platforms) arrives in a streaming fashion, yet all existing LLM detectors are designed for offline batch classification and cannot control false positive rates in an online setting. The formal demonstration that naive online adaptation of any offline detector yields FPR → 1 as the number of texts grows (1−δ^T) underscores the need for the sequential framework. Open questions include designing score functions tailored to the sequential setting, improving d_t and ε estimation beyond the conservative first-10-samples approach, and studying the trade-off between delaying test start and obtaining more reliable parameter estimates. The method's dependence on a 'good' score function with sufficiently separated means remains a practical limitation.
