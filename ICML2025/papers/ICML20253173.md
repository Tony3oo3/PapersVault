## Problem

Large multimodal models (LMMs) struggle to recognize novel concepts (e.g., newly discovered species) and confusing/low-resource concepts because they rely on pre-trained knowledge and limited visual detail capture. Existing visual data augmentation methods (cropping, flipping, image-editing) are ineffective for novel concepts where real images are extremely scarce and for mis-represented concepts where existing images do not accurately depict the target. The core gap is a lack of a method that can generate targeted, high-quality synthetic training data specifically designed to teach LMMs the distinguishing features of concepts they currently confuse.

## Input / Output

The method takes a target concept name, a confusable concept name (automatically identified via LMM zero-shot misclassification), and as few as 1–5 real images of each concept. It produces a ranked list of up to 5 contrastive visual features (short phrases under 8 words each) and a set of synthetic 512×512 images generated by a text-to-image model conditioned on those features. Generated images are retained only if the target LMM's zero-shot verification confirms all extracted features are visibly present (satisfaction rate = 1.0). The final augmented images are used for LoRA fine-tuning of the LMM, and the feature list can additionally be appended to inference prompts for in-context recognition.

## Method

CoDA is a four-stage contrastive visual data augmentation pipeline. First, it identifies the confusable concept via LMM zero-shot inference, then extracts contrastive textual features (via GPT-4o-mini with chain-of-thought) and visual features (via VLM on real images) that distinguish the target from the confusable concept. Second, features are automatically filtered by two CLIP-based criteria: Discriminability (feature must be more associated with target than confusable images, threshold >0.6) and Generability (feature must be reliably rendered by the T2I model, top-5 selected). Third, the surviving features are passed to a text-to-image generative model (Stable Diffusion 3.5 Large Turbo or Recraft V3) to produce synthetic images. Fourth, each generated image is verified by the target LMM itself, which must confirm all features are present; images failing verification are discarded. The key insight is that contrastive feature extraction—describing what the target has that the confusable concept lacks—is far more tractable and effective than exhaustive description, and that text-to-image generation (rather than image editing) is necessary to produce diverse, structurally complete synthetic instances for concepts with no or misleading real images.

## Results

In the 1-shot (5 real + 1 synthetic) setting, CoDA achieves 73.0% accuracy on NovelSpecies (Visual features), 82.7% on SUN (T+V), and 17.8% absolute gain over all-real on iNaturalist. These represent 11.8%, 10.0%, and 17.8% absolute gains over the all-real baseline, respectively. CoDA outperforms the SOTA feature-based augmentation method ARMADA by 12.3% on NovelSpecies (73.0 vs. 60.7), 6.8% on SUN (82.7 vs. 75.9), and 6.0% on iNaturalist. Even the ablated CoDA without contrastive guidance (71.8% NovelSpecies, 82.7% SUN) still exceeds all traditional baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoDA consistently outperforms all four baselines (All Real, Cropping, Flipping, ARMADA) across both NovelSpecies and SUN in the 1-shot setting. On NovelSpecies, CoDA (Visual) reaches 73.0%, surpassing ARMADA (60.7%) by 12.3 points and Cropping (60.4%) by 12.6 points; even Flipping (60.7%) and All Real (61.2%) are far below. On SUN, CoDA (T+V) achieves 82.7%, beating ARMADA (75.9%) by 6.8 points and Cropping (78.3%) by 4.4 points. Notably, the ablated CoDA without contrastive guidance (70.3% NovelSpecies, 82.7% SUN) already exceeds every traditional baseline, confirming that text-to-image generative augmentation is fundamentally superior to cropping/flipping/image-editing approaches. The contrastive feature extraction and discriminability filtering provide an additional 2–3 point boost over the non-contrastive variant on NovelSpecies (73.0 vs. 70.3 for T+V), highlighting the value of targeting the specific confusable concept.

## Relation to Prior Work

CoDA departs from the dominant paradigm of visual data augmentation for VLMs, which has relied on editing or transforming existing real images (cropping, style transfer, ARMADA's image-editing backbone). The key conceptual shift is using text-to-image generative models to create entirely new synthetic images guided by contrastive features—attributes that distinguish a target concept from the specific confusable concept the LMM mistakes it for—rather than modifying real images that may be scarce or mis-representative. This positions CoDA as the first widely successful method to leverage T2I generation for visual data augmentation in LMM updating, complementing prior feature-based augmentation (ARMADA) and traditional manipulations. The paper also introduces NovelSpecies, a benchmark that sidesteps the data-leakage problem inherent in existing datasets by restricting to species discovered after any model's knowledge cutoff.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The contrastive framing—teaching a model what to look for by contrasting with what it already confuses the concept with—is a practical and elegant solution to the novel-concept recognition gap in LMMs, and the modular design means improvements in T2I models directly translate to better augmentation. The NovelSpecies benchmark (2240 images, 64 species) provides a clean, updatable testbed for evaluating true novel-concept recognition, addressing a real evaluation gap. Open questions include whether the approach scales to multi-concept confusion hierarchies, whether it extends to video or 3D, and whether the current reliance on a single confusable concept per target limits its effectiveness for concepts with many near-duplicates. The method's effectiveness is also inherently bounded by the instruction-following quality of the T2I backbone, as acknowledged by the authors.
