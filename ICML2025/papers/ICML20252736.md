## Problem

Scaling linear diffusion transformers for text-to-image generation from 1.6B to 4.8B parameters traditionally requires training from scratch, incurring prohibitive compute costs. The paper asks how to efficiently scale both training-time and inference-time compute for linear DiTs, enabling high-quality generation under diverse resource budgets without simply increasing model size.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252736/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts free-form text prompts (including long, detailed descriptions exceeding 77 tokens) and generates 512×512 RGB images using 20 denoising steps. During inference-time scaling, the system generates up to 2048 candidate images per prompt and a trained 2B VLM (SaVILA) performs pairwise tournament comparisons to select the best-matching image, adding approximately 2N×4,518G FLOPs of VLM computation on top of N×49,140G FLOPs of SANA generation.

## Method

SANA-1.5 introduces three complementary scaling strategies. For training efficiency, a model growth strategy expands a pre-trained 20-block DiT to 60 blocks using partial preservation initialization (retaining original layers, zero-initializing output projections of new layers as identity mappings) combined with QK RMS normalization for stability, reducing training time by 60% versus from-scratch training; this is paired with a CAME-8bit optimizer that applies block-wise 8-bit quantization to first-order moments while preserving 32-bit second-order statistics, cutting optimizer memory by ~8×. For deployment flexibility, a depth pruning technique computes block importance via input-output cosine similarity across diffusion timesteps, removes low-importance blocks, and recovers quality with only 100 fine-tuning steps on a single GPU. For inference-time scaling, a tournament-style best-of-N selection uses a fine-tuned NVILA-2B VLM (SaVILA) to compare image pairs against the prompt, boosting GenEval from 0.81 to 0.96 by trading compute for model capacity.

## Results

SANA-1.5 4.8B (post-trained) achieves a GenEval score of 0.81, outperforming Playground v3 (24B, 0.76) and FLUX-dev (12B, 0.67) while running 5.5× faster (4.2s vs 23.0s latency on A100). With inference-time scaling (best-of-2048 sampling via SaVILA), GenEval improves to 0.96, establishing a new state-of-the-art on the GenEval benchmark. The model also achieves FID 5.42 (pre-trained) / 5.99 (post-trained), CLIP score 29.23, and DPG 84.7, with a throughput of 0.26 samples/s. The model growth strategy from 1.6B to 4.8B reduces training time by 60% compared to training from scratch.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SANA-1.5 4.8B (Ours) outperforms all 11 baselines on GenEval, the paper's primary text-image alignment metric, achieving 0.81 versus Playground v3's 0.76 (24B), FLUX-dev's 0.67 (12B), SD3-medium's 0.62 (2.0B), and DALL-E 3's 0.67. On FID, the pre-trained 4.8B model achieves 5.42, beating SDXL (6.63), PixArt-σ (6.15), and FLUX-dev (10.15), though SANA-1.0 1.6B (5.76) and FLUX-schnell (7.94) remain competitive. On DPG-Bench, Playground v3 (87.0) and FLUX-schnell (84.8) slightly edge out SANA-1.5 (84.7), while SANA-1.5 surpasses all other baselines. In terms of efficiency, SANA-1.5 4.8B achieves 5.5× lower latency than FLUX-dev (4.2s vs 23.0s) and 3.6× lower than Playground v3 (4.2s vs 15.0s), with 6.5× higher throughput than FLUX-dev (0.26 vs 0.04 samples/s). With inference-time scaling (best-of-2048), SANA-1.5 reaches GenEval 0.96, surpassing Playground v3 by 20% absolute and SD1.5+scaling (0.87) by 9%, demonstrating that a 4.8B model with compute-at-inference can match or exceed 24B models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends SANA-1.0's linear diffusion transformer with a three-part efficient scaling framework: a model growth strategy (inspired by Net2Net-style identity initialization) that expands a 20-block model to 60 blocks while preserving learned knowledge, a depth pruning technique adapted from Minitron's block importance analysis for LLMs, and an inference-time scaling approach drawing on the 'Large Language Monkeys' repeated-sampling paradigm for LLMs. The CAME-8bit optimizer combines CAME's matrix factorization of second-order moments with Dettmers et al.'s block-wise 8-bit quantization, creating a hybrid precision scheme specific to diffusion transformer training. Conceptually, the paper departs from the prevailing 'train larger from scratch' paradigm by demonstrating that strategic initialization, selective compression, and inference-time compute can collectively match or exceed much larger models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: CAME-8bit enables fine-tuning a 4.8B diffusion model on a single RTX 4090, and the grow-then-prune pipeline provides a flexible path to deploy models at arbitrary sizes with only 100 steps of fine-tuning. The inference-time scaling result (4.8B + 2048 samples achieving 0.96 GenEval vs. 24B Playground v3's 0.76) challenges the assumption that parameter count must scale with quality, though the N× compute requirement limits real-world applicability. Open questions include whether the VLM-based selection generalizes beyond GenEval-style compositional prompts, whether the log-linear scaling pattern holds at even larger N, and whether the block importance analysis transfers across architectures or resolutions.
