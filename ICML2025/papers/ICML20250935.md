## Problem

High-dimensional neural imaging modalities (widefield calcium, functional ultrasound) contain complex local and global spatiotemporal patterns mixed with prevalent behaviorally irrelevant dynamics, yet existing dynamical models rely on preprocessing steps (PCA, LocaNMF, ROI-based reduction) that can discard spatiotemporal structure and behaviorally relevant information. No prior dynamical latent-state model learns directly from raw neural images while disentangling behavior-predictive dynamics from other ongoing neural processes, and functional ultrasound imaging had no dynamical model at all.

## Input / Output

SBIND consumes raw 1×128×128 neural image frames (downsampled from 540×640) at 30 Hz (WFCI 1), 15 Hz (WFCI 2), or 2 Hz (fUSI), along with simultaneously recorded behavior that can be continuous (14-dim from video ROIs), categorical (4 binary sensor traces), or intermittently recorded (target direction available only during fixation). It produces one-step-ahead neural image predictions, decoded behavior (MSE for continuous, F1/accuracy for categorical), and two 3D latent volumes (n₁×32×32 behaviorally relevant + n₂×32×32 residual) that together reconstruct the full neural image. A single inference step takes ~18 ms, faster than the effective sampling rates of both imaging modalities.

## Method

SBIND is a two-ConvRNN architecture where each ConvRNN's recurrence function f_A(·) = GlobalAttn(A*(·)) combines a 3×3 convolutional layer (capturing local spatial dependencies) with multi-head self-attention applied to 4×4 patches of the latent volume (capturing global, long-range spatial dependencies across brain regions). The two-phase sequential learning first trains ConvRNN1 to minimize behavior decoding error, yielding behaviorally relevant latents X^(1), then freezes those parameters and trains ConvRNN2 to predict the residual neural images (observed minus X^(1)-predicted), yielding latents X^(2) that capture other neural dynamics. Encoders and decoders are shallow convolutional networks (3 conv layers downsampling to 32×32, 2 transposed conv layers upsampling back), and the neural reconstruction loss combines L1, L2, and gradient-difference terms to preserve local image structure. This design directly models raw image data with spatial inductive biases rather than flattened or preprocessed features, and the sequential disentanglement cleanly separates behavior-predictive from residual dynamics without a combined loss.

## Results

On WFCI 1, SBIND achieves 0.4955±0.0254 MSE for one-step-ahead behavior decoding and 0.0414±0.0029 MSE for neural image prediction, outperforming the best DPAD variant (0.5877 MSE behavior, 0.0543 MSE neural) by ~16% and ~24% respectively. On WFCI 2, SBIND reaches 0.4569 F1-score for categorical behavior decoding, surpassing the best DPAD (0.3613) by ~26%. On the fUSI 8-directional task, SBIND achieves 0.3521±0.0201 behavior accuracy and 0.3919±0.0107 neural MSE, beating the best DPAD (0.2864 accuracy, 0.7109 MSE) by ~23% and ~45% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SBIND outperforms all five baselines across every dataset and metric. Against DPAD (Sani et al., 2024), the closest competitor, SBIND reduces behavior MSE by 16–26% and neural MSE by 24–45% on WFCI, and improves fUSI 8-directional accuracy from 0.2864 to 0.3521 while halving neural MSE (0.7109→0.3919). CEBRA (Schneider et al., 2023) performs substantially worse on neural prediction (MSE 0.44–0.70 vs. SBIND's 0.04–0.16 on WFCI) because it does not model residual neural dynamics, relying solely on behavior-guided embeddings. The PCA+LDA pipeline from Griggs et al. (2024) achieves 0.7001 accuracy on fUSI 2-directional but provides no neural prediction; SBIND matches its accuracy (0.7300) while also achieving 0.4725 neural MSE. Adapted STNDT and TNDM (originally for spiking data) also underperform SBIND on both neural and behavioral metrics, confirming that the ConvRNN + self-attention architecture with image priors is critical for raw neural imaging data.

## Relation to Prior Work

SBIND extends the line of neural-behavioral dynamical models (PSID, DPAD, CEBRA, TNDM) that jointly learn latent dynamics from neural and behavioral data, but departs from them by incorporating spatial priors (convolutional layers and self-attention) directly into the recurrence to model raw neural image data, rather than relying on preprocessing steps like PCA or LocaNMF that discard spatiotemporal structure. The two-phase sequential learning for disentanglement builds on DPAD's prioritized optimization strategy, while the combination of ConvRNN with patch-based self-attention in the recurrence function draws from vision Transformer architectures (ViT) adapted to a recurrent setting. The paper also introduces the first dynamical latent state model for functional ultrasound imaging, a modality previously only analyzed with PCA+LDA pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SBIND provides a versatile, preprocessing-free tool for modeling neural imaging data that is practically important for enabling real-time, non-invasive brain-computer interfaces, particularly via fUSI where inference speed (17.9 ms) exceeds typical sampling rates. The ability to handle diverse behavior distributions (continuous, categorical, intermittently recorded) broadens applicability across experimental paradigms. Key limitations include single-session/animal training (no multi-session generalization yet) and the relatively small dataset sizes (248–412 trials for WFCI, 13 sessions for fUSI), leaving open whether the architecture scales to larger, more heterogeneous datasets. Future directions include multi-session learning, adaptive tracking of time-varying dynamics, and extension to other imaging modalities.
