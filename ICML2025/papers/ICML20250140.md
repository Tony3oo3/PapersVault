## Problem

Second-order optimization of neural networks is bottlenecked by the O(p²) cost of constructing and inverting curvature matrices, and existing block-diagonal or Kronecker-factored approximations lack theoretical guarantees for nonlinear architectures. This paper asks whether the discrete symmetries inherent to neural network architectures (e.g., permutation invariance of neurons within a layer) can be exploited to derive the exact structure of the global curvature at dramatically reduced computational cost.

## Input / Output

The method consumes the gradient of the loss with respect to all MLP parameters (weights and biases) from a single model, together with the network's architecture (layer widths, depth, and activation function type, which determines the applicable symmetry group: orthogonal, signed-permutation, or permutation). It produces a second-order preconditioned parameter update where the preconditioning matrix is the inverse square root of the gradient covariance, computed entirely through O(d²+L²) scalar and matrix factors rather than the full O(p²) matrix. Crucially, the factors are estimated by averaging gradient outer products over symmetric parameter pairs within a single model, eliminating the need for an ensemble of differently-initialized models.

## Method

The core contribution is a theoretical framework proving that the global curvature (gradient covariance averaged over the parameter distribution) of an MLP is fully determined by O(d²+L²) independent factors rather than O(p²) elements, with the specific structure governed by the activation function's symmetry group (orthogonal for linear, signed-permutation for Tanh, permutation for ReLU). The practical algorithm proceeds in three steps: (1) estimate the factors from a single model's gradient by averaging over symmetric parameter pairs, (2) compute the inverse-square-root factors by inverting a small surrogate matrix (with minimal layer widths) that shares the same Kronecker-factored structure, and (3) apply the preconditioned update using efficient Kronecker-product matrix-vector products. A supporting theorem (Theorem 2.2) proves that distributional invariance under the symmetry group is preserved throughout training under only local invertibility of the update rule, extending prior results requiring global invertibility. The approach is validated on two-layer MLPs with synthetic data, where the proposed SymO optimizer converges faster than GD, Adam, KFAC, and Shampoo at comparable per-iteration cost.

## Results

On a two-layer MLP (d₀=100, d₁=70, d₂=40; 9,800 parameters) trained on synthetic Gaussian data with full-batch optimization, SymO converges to lower test loss faster than all four baselines (GD, Adam, KFAC, Shampoo) for both linear and Tanh activations, with nearly identical per-iteration wall-clock time. The factor estimation quality (Table 1) shows that single-model estimates of curvature factors correlate at 0.90–0.97 with a 10,000-model ground truth for Φ₁ at width d₁=100–10,000, and at 0.52–0.56 for Φ₂, improving with layer width. The theoretical structure of the global curvature is confirmed empirically: for Tanh activations the covariance becomes block-diagonal beyond two layers, while ReLU activations retain inter-layer correlations at all depths.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares SymO against four baselines—gradient descent (GD), Adam, KFAC (Kronecker-factored approximate curvature), and Shampoo—on a two-layer MLP with synthetic data. SymO outperforms all four baselines in convergence speed (lower test loss at every iteration) for both linear and Tanh activations, as shown in Figures 5 and 6, while maintaining nearly identical per-iteration computational cost. Against GD and Adam (first-order methods), the advantage is most pronounced, reflecting the benefit of second-order preconditioning. Against KFAC and Shampoo (second-order methods), SymO still converges faster, which the authors attribute to its use of the exact global curvature structure (Kronecker-factored but not block-diagonal) rather than the block-diagonal approximation used by KFAC. The block-diagonal terms of SymO's update are noted to be similar to Shampoo's, suggesting SymO's advantage comes from the additional cross-layer Kronecker-factored terms that Shampoo omits.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the global curvature framework of Titsias (2024) by imposing exact structural constraints derived from the discrete permutation and sign-flip symmetries inherent to MLP architectures. It departs from the dominant line of Kronecker-factored approximations (KFAC, Shampoo) by showing that the true global curvature is not block-diagonal but exhibits a Kronecker-factored structure with O(d²+L²) free parameters, partially justifying those approximations while revealing their limitations. Theorem 2.2 generalizes the equivariant-flow invariance result of Köhler et al. (2019) from globally invertible to merely locally invertible update maps. Crucially, this is the first work to leverage discrete symmetries (as opposed to the continuous symmetries studied by Neyshabur et al., Meng et al., Zhao et al.) to compute the full curvature matrix exactly.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework reduces the curvature computation from O(p²) to O(d²+L²) parameters, making exact second-order optimization potentially feasible for much larger models than previously possible. However, empirical validation is limited to tiny two- and three-layer MLPs on synthetic data with full-batch training, leaving open whether the global curvature structure reliably approximates local curvature in practice and whether the speedup persists at scale. The authors note the framework should extend to residual, convolutional, recurrent, and transformer architectures, and that it could improve Bayesian posterior estimation, but these remain future work. A key open question is whether the symmetry-derived structure captures the most informative directions in the loss landscape for real-world training.
