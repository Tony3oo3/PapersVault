## Problem

Reinforcement learning for LLM reasoning has yielded only modest gains and lacks the scalability of earlier training stages, while existing test-time scaling methods rely on repeated sampling with external verifiers rather than the model itself benefiting from longer generation. The paper asks how to scale RL training so that a single longer reasoning trajectory directly improves performance—enabling genuine inference scaling without auxiliary verification signals.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250940/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a text math problem as input and produces a single long chain-of-thought response (up to 16,384 tokens for the 32B variant) that interleaves trial-and-error attempts, self-verification, and reflection before concluding with a final numerical answer. For inference scaling analysis, the response is truncated at varying token budgets and a separate summarization model extracts the answer from the partial reasoning, allowing performance to be measured as a function of thinking length without any external verifier.

## Method

T1 combines three components: (1) SFT initialization on synthesized CoT data that explicitly incorporates incorrect attempts, error diagnosis, and verification steps—broadening the exploration space beyond correct-only trajectories; (2) scaled RL training that oversamples K=64 responses per prompt at high temperature (τ≈1.2), adds a token-level entropy bonus to the policy gradient loss, applies on-policy KL normalization (subtracting the mean KL across a prompt's responses) with an EMA-updated reference model to prevent over-regularization, and imposes a −1 penalty on repetitive, overlong, or garbled outputs to stabilize training; (3) a truncation-based inference scaling protocol where responses are cut at increasing token budgets and a summarization model extracts the answer, revealing that more RL training steps activate stronger inference scaling trends—e.g., T1 (Qwen2.5-32B) gains 66% relative accuracy on AIME by extending thinking from 30% to 100% of its full generation budget.

## Results

T1 (Qwen2.5-32B) achieves 92.4% on MATH500, 50.6% on AIME2024, 49.6% on Omni-MATH-500, and 56.1% on GPQA (Pass@1 accuracy). The RL-scaled T1 model outperforms its SFT-only counterpart by over 10% on MATH500 and Omni-MATH-500 and over 20% on AIME. On the hardest benchmark (AIME2024), T1 (Qwen2.5-32B) surpasses QwQ-32B-preview (50.6% vs. 50.0%) and o1-preview (50.6% vs. 44.6%), while also demonstrating consistent inference scaling where longer thinking budgets yield higher accuracy without external verifiers.

## Baselines & Comparisons

T1 (Qwen2.5-32B) outperforms QwQ-32B-preview on MATH500 (92.4 vs. 90.6), AIME (50.6 vs. 50.0), and Omni-MATH-500 (49.6 vs. 46.6), but trails slightly on GPQA (56.1 vs. 58.2). Against o1-preview, T1 leads on MATH500 (92.4 vs. 85.5) and AIME (50.6 vs. 44.6) but falls well behind on GPQA (56.1 vs. 72.3), reflecting that o1-preview benefits from broader science-domain training. T1 (Qwen2.5-32B) substantially surpasses GPT-4o on all math benchmarks (e.g., AIME: 50.6 vs. 9.3) and Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct across all four benchmarks. The 14B variant also shows strong gains: T1 (Qwen2.5-14B) improves AIME from 13.7% (base) to 30.5% and MATH500 from 78.9% to 87.4%, demonstrating that RL scaling is effective even at smaller model scales. On GPQA (an out-of-domain science benchmark), T1 shows modest OOD improvements over its base models but does not match the performance of larger proprietary models like o1-preview or Claude-3.5-sonnet.

## Relation to Prior Work

T1 extends the RLHF framework of Ouyang et al. (2022) by modifying the objective with a token-level entropy bonus, on-policy KL normalization with an EMA-updated reference model, and explicit penalties for degenerate outputs, while adopting RLOO's leave-one-out reward normalization (Ahmadian et al., 2024) as the optimization backbone. It departs from prior CoT-based SFT approaches (e.g., STaR, rejection sampling) that emphasize only correct reasoning steps by synthesizing training data that explicitly incorporates trial-and-error and self-verification. For test-time scaling, T1 contrasts with the repeated-sampling-plus-verifier paradigm (Brown et al., 2024; Snell et al., 2024) by demonstrating that a single longer generation—enabled by scaled RL—exhibits inference scaling without external verification signals.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a concrete, reproducible recipe (oversampling at K=64, high-temperature sampling, entropy bonus, on-policy KL normalization, and pattern penalties) that makes RL scaling practical for open-source models, and its truncation-based inference scaling measurement offers a simple diagnostic tool for the community. A key open question is whether the observed inference scaling trends persist at larger model scales or on non-mathematical reasoning tasks, and whether the specific hyperparameter choices (e.g., temperature 1.2, KL coefficient 2e-4) transfer across architectures. The finding that under-trained RL models show minimal inference scaling gains—only fully trained models exhibit the expected trend—suggests that RL training and inference scaling are coupled in a non-trivial way that remains to be theoretically understood.
