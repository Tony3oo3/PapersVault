## Problem

Existing keypoint-guided image generation methods rely on skeleton images as control signals, which suffer from class binding confusion (keypoints lack category identity) and instance binding confusion (overlapping instances cannot be disambiguated), making multi-class, multi-instance generation of humans and animals infeasible. Additionally, no large-scale dataset provides joint keypoint annotations for both humans and animals co-existing in the same image with the quality and diversity needed for modern generative models.

## Input / Output

The model takes a global text prompt and per-instance conditions consisting of 2D keypoint coordinates with per-point visibility flags, axis-aligned bounding boxes (top-left and bottom-right corners), and a class name string for each instance. It produces a 1024×1024 photorealistic RGB image in which each specified instance appears at the correct pose, position, and category. During training, bounding boxes are dropped with 50% probability, keypoints with 15%, and the text prompt with 10%, enabling flexible partial-conditioning at inference.

## Method

UNIMC builds on the PIXART-α DiT backbone and introduces two new modules trained from scratch while keeping the backbone frozen. The unified keypoint encoder converts each instance's keypoint coordinates and bounding box via Fourier positional encoding, concatenates them with a T5 text embedding of the class name, and projects through an MLP into a compact token; a learnable mask token replaces invisible or absent points, and per-instance keypoint and bounding-box tokens are grouped into a combined token that is class-aware. The timestep-aware keypoint modulator inserts a self-attention layer into each of the 28 DiT blocks, where the concatenated keypoint tokens G are appended to the backbone tokens V, processed through LayerNorm and multi-head self-attention, then modulated by a shared AdaLN-style timestep adapter plus block-wise learnable scale and shift, with a zero-initialized residual connection preserving the pretrained backbone. Training uses AdamW at 2e-5 for 8K steps at 1024×1024 on 8 A800 GPUs with batch size 256, and the accompanying HAIG-2.9M dataset (786K images, 2.9M instances across 31 species) is annotated with YOLO-World bounding boxes, DWPose/ViTPose++H keypoints, and GPT-4o/CogVLM2 captions.

## Results

On the HAIG-2.9M test set, UNIMC achieves FID 23.63 and KID 7.57, matching the quality of the uncontrolled PixArt-α backbone (FID 23.50, KID 7.52) while adding keypoint-level control. UNIMC attains 93.55% human and 91.71% animal class accuracy, and 30.01 / 28.38 human / animal pose AP, substantially outperforming both ControlNet and GLIGEN on all control metrics. The CLIP text-image alignment score of 32.28 is the highest among all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UNIMC dominates both keypoint-guided baselines on every control metric. Against ControlNet (skeleton-image conditioning), UNIMC improves animal class accuracy from 52.03% to 91.71% (+39.7 pp) and animal pose AP from 20.29 to 28.38 (+8.1), while also slightly surpassing it on human class accuracy (93.55% vs 90.67%) and human pose AP (30.01 vs 29.01). Against GLIGEN (keypoint-coordinate conditioning), the gap is even larger: animal class accuracy jumps from 9.90% to 91.71% and animal pose AP from 3.96 to 28.38, reflecting GLIGEN's near-inability to generate correct animal poses. On image quality, UNIMC (FID 23.63) is comparable to the uncontrolled PixArt-α (FID 23.50) and clearly better than ControlNet (26.72) and GLIGEN (31.01), showing that the compact token-based conditioning does not sacrifice generation fidelity. In the human preference study, UNIMC received 87/100 votes for class-and-keypoint control accuracy versus 9 for ControlNet and 4 for GLIGEN, confirming the quantitative advantage in perceived controllability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the keypoint-guided image generation line of work (ControlNet, HumanSD, GLIGEN) by replacing skeleton-image conditions with compact per-instance tokens (keypoint coordinates, bounding box, class name) encoded via a unified keypoint encoder that maps keypoints from 31 species into a shared representation space. The architecture builds directly on PIXART-α's DiT backbone, inserting self-attention-based timestep-aware keypoint modulators into its blocks, while the conditioning formulation inherits GLIGEN's coordinate-based paradigm but generalizes it to multi-class, multi-instance settings. The key conceptual departure from prior work is addressing 'class binding confusion' and 'instance binding confusion' that arise when skeleton images are used as conditions for overlapping or multi-species scenes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

HAIG-2.9M is the first large-scale dataset with joint human and animal keypoint, bounding box, and caption annotations (786K images, 2.9M instances, 31 classes), filling a critical gap for training and evaluating multi-class keypoint-guided generation. The unified keypoint encoder and timestep-aware modulator provide a practical recipe for adding keypoint-level control to DiT-based generators without species-specific encoders. However, the dataset suffers from a long-tail class distribution (e.g., only 178 rhino instances vs. 2.16M person instances), is limited to 31 predefined classes, and the method does not yet handle arbitrary non-rigid objects, leaving open the question of truly open-vocabulary keypoint-guided generation.
