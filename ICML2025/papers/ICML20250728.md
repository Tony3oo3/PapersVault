## Problem

Existing geometric and topological analyses of LLM internal representations (e.g., intrinsic dimension, per-layer persistent homology) provide only static, layer-by-layer snapshots and cannot track how topological features are born, persist, and die as prompts are dynamically rearranged across the model's depth. This paper addresses that gap by treating the sequence of layer representations as a discrete dynamical system and applying zigzag persistence to characterize the full evolutionary trajectory of p-dimensional holes in representation space.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained decoder-only transformer (Llama2-7B, Llama3-8B, Mistral 7B, Pythia 6.9B, all with 32 hidden layers) and a set of text prompts (reduced to 8,000 from SST, Pile, MATH, or Code datasets), extracting only the last-token embedding at each normalization layer to form a point cloud in R^d. It produces zigzag persistence diagrams, effective persistence images (a smoothed grid of size N_layers × N_layers per homology dimension), and two scalar descriptors—Births' Relative Frequency B_p(ℓ) and Inter-Layer Persistence Z̄_p(ℓ)—computed with a tunable power-weight exponent α that shifts emphasis between short-lived and long-lived topological features. As a downstream application, the inter-layer persistence plateau yields a set of layers to prune, producing a thinned model evaluated on MMLU, HellaSwag, and Winogrande.

## Method

At each layer ℓ_i, the method constructs a k-NN graph (k_NN=4) over the last-token embeddings and expands it to a simplicial complex K_{ℓ_i} up to dimension m=4 by filling simplices whose boundaries are complete. Intersection layers K_{ℓ_i} ∩ K_{ℓ_{i+1}} are computed between adjacent layers, yielding a zigzag filtration Φ with alternating forward and backward inclusions indexed by {0, 1, …, 2(N_layers−1)}. The zigzag persistence diagram Pers_p(Φ) is computed using the FastZigZag algorithm, and an 'effective persistence image' is derived by collapsing intersection-layer indices so that birth and death are reported only at model-layer positions. From this image, two interpretable descriptors are extracted: Births' Relative Frequency, which counts the rate of new p-dimensional holes at each layer with a power-weight ω(ℓ,ℓ_i)=|ℓ−ℓ_i|^α, and Inter-Layer Persistence, which measures the fraction of holes alive at layer ℓ_1 that remain alive at layer ℓ_2 and throughout all intermediate layers. The key distinction from prior work is the use of a k-NN-based (rather than Rips-based) filtration within the zigzag framework, which avoids the need for a continuous scale parameter and makes the layer index itself the sole time axis, directly encoding how the model rearranges prompt positions in representation space.

## Results

The paper identifies four distinct phases of prompt processing across transformer layers using zigzag persistence descriptors: (1) rapid rearrangement in early layers, (2) stable long-lived topological relations in middle layers, (3) refinement with short-lived adjustments in middle-to-late layers, and (4) final rearrangement in the last 2–3 layers. For layer pruning, the zigzag-based criterion (pruning layers within 10% of the maximum inter-layer persistence plateau) removes 8–11 layers depending on the model. On the Mistral 7B model, the method achieves 53.17% MMLU accuracy after pruning 10 layers, substantially outperforming the similarity-based baselines (38.20%), while on Llama 3 8B it matches the baselines exactly (53.44% MMLU, 41.60% HellaSwag, 70.00% Winogrande) after pruning 9 layers.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its zigzag-based layer pruning against two similarity-based methods: angular distance (Gromov et al., 2024) and Block-Influence Bi-score (Men et al., 2024), which happen to select identical layers at a fixed pruning budget and are reported jointly as 'Other works.' The zigzag method wins on Mistral 7B across all three benchmarks (MMLU 53.17 vs. 38.20, a 14.97-point margin; HellaSwag 36.67 vs. 34.45; Winogrande 66.50 vs. 63.76) and on Llama 2 for HellaSwag (44.71 vs. 42.78) and Winogrande (68.67 vs. 67.72). It ties exactly with the baselines on Llama 3 across all three benchmarks (53.44, 41.60, 70.00). The method loses on Llama 2 MMLU (37.38 vs. 43.95, a 6.57-point gap) and on Pythia for HellaSwag (31.43 vs. 34.96) and Winogrande (55.71 vs. 58.09). Overall, the paper characterizes the results as 'comparable,' with the zigzag criterion selecting different layers than the similarity methods in most cases (e.g., layers 20–27 for Llama 2 vs. 21–28 for the baselines) yet achieving competitive or superior downstream accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the standard persistent homology approach to neural network interpretability (Naitzat et al., 2020; Rieck et al., 2023), which treats each layer as an independent static snapshot and aggregates results post-hoc, by instead framing the sequence of layers as a discrete dynamical system and applying zigzag persistence (Carlsson & de Silva, 2010) to track the full birth-death trajectory of topological features across depth. The k-NN-based filtration, previously studied only in the context of standard persistent homology (Le & Taylor, 2024), is here adapted to the zigzag setting for the first time, and the fast zigzag algorithm of Dey & Hou (2022) makes the computation tractable at LLM scale. For the downstream layer-pruning task, the paper positions itself alongside similarity-based methods (Gromov et al., 2024; Men et al., 2024) but derives its pruning criterion from a topological plateau in inter-layer persistence rather than from angular or block-influence similarity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a system-level, dynamic characterization of how LLMs reorganize prompt representations across depth, complementing static geometric measures like intrinsic dimension (Valeriani et al., 2023; Cheng et al., 2024) and providing a principled, topology-driven criterion for layer pruning that matches state-of-the-art similarity-based methods. Key limitations include the reliance on the last token as a proxy for the full sequence, the restriction to static pre-trained models, and the sensitivity of results to the k_NN and maximum-simplex-dimension hyperparameters, whose optimal selection remains an open question. Extending the framework to track representation evolution during training, to multi-token point clouds, or to incorporate a scale parameter alongside the layer index (as in multidimensional persistence) are natural next steps that could sharpen both interpretability and pruning decisions.
