## Problem

Estimating the likelihood function of ancestral graphs (which include bidirected edges arising from unobserved latent variables) from empirical data is fundamentally difficult: unlike Bayesian networks, the c-component or head-and-tail factorization contributions cannot be directly estimated from the empirical distribution, blocking the use of standard search-and-score causal discovery. This paper derives an explicit likelihood decomposition in terms of multivariate cross-information over 'ac-connected' vertex subsets and uses it to implement an efficient greedy search-and-score algorithm that handles both continuous and categorical data with latent confounders.

## Input / Output

The method consumes N iid samples from a multivariate observational dataset with categorical or continuous (or mixed-type) variables and outputs an ancestral graph containing directed edges (causal effects), bidirected edges (latent common causes), and undirected edges (unresolved orientations). The algorithm operates in two passes over the graph: a node-level pass that primes orientations and prunes false-positive edges, followed by an edge-level pass that refines orientations. For continuous variables, conditional entropies are estimated as differences of mutual information via the optimization procedure of Cabeli et al. (2020), while for categorical variables the fNML complexity is computed in closed form.

## Method

The core theoretical result (Theorem 1) decomposes the cross-entropy of an ancestral graph into an inclusion-exclusion sum of multivariate cross-information terms I(C) over all ac-connected subsets C of vertices, where ac-connected means every pair in C is linked by a collider path confined to the ancestor set of C. Proposition 3 then shows this decomposition can be estimated by substituting the empirical distribution p for the model distribution q in each retained I(C) term, since the model's conditional independences are assumed to hold by construction. The practical algorithm, MIIC_search&score, starts from MIIC's hybrid constraint-and-information prediction and proceeds in two steps: (1) a node score (fNML-regularized conditional entropy) is minimized over each node's candidate parents/spouses to prime edge orientations and remove unprimed edges; (2) an edge orientation score (negative conditional mutual information plus symmetrized fNML complexity, Table 1) is minimized over each edge's three possible orientations (X→Y, Y→X, X↔Y), restricted to ac-connected subsets containing at most two-collider paths, with orientation changes accepted only if they avoid directed or almost-directed cycles. The symmetrized fNML terms enforce Markov equivalence by ensuring all three orientation scores coincide when the two endpoint nodes share identical parent/spouse sets.

## Results

On continuous 50-node datasets (average degree 5) with 0–20% hidden variables, MIIC_search&score outperforms MIIC in edge precision with little to no decrease in recall, and clearly outperforms M3HC, GFCI, and DAG-GNN on non-linear models, though it is outperformed by GFCI and DAG-GNN on linear Gaussian models. On categorical benchmarks (Alarm, Insurance, Barley, Mildew) with up to 20% hidden variables, MIIC_search&score outperforms FCI on both precision and recall for small datasets (N ≤ 10,000) on complex models (Barley, Mildew), while reaching similar performance to FCI at larger sample sizes or on simpler models (Alarm, Insurance). The method's scalability is competitive, with the two-step search-and-score scheme being essentially linear in the number of nodes (step 1) and edges (step 2) for fixed degree.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MIIC (Verny et al., 2017) serves as the starting-point baseline: MIIC_search&score consistently improves edge precision over MIIC with little to no recall loss across all tested datasets, demonstrating the benefit of extending MIIC's unshielded triple scores to higher-order local information contributions. M3HC (Triantafillou & Tsamardinos, 2016) and GFCI (Ogarrio et al., 2016) outperform MIIC_search&score on linear Gaussian models due to their intrinsic linearity assumptions, but MIIC_search&score clearly surpasses both on non-linear continuous models. DAG-GNN (Yu et al., 2019), a deep learning method using continuous constrained optimization, similarly outperforms MIIC_search&score on linear Gaussian data but is outperformed on non-linear couplings. FCI (via the causal-learn package, Zheng et al., 2024) is outperformed by MIIC_search&score on both precision and recall for small-sample complex categorical models (Barley, Mildew at N ≤ 10,000), while FCI catches up at larger sample sizes due to its asymptotic consistency; FCI also had convergence difficulties on bootstrapped complex datasets, limiting direct comparison at large N.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the MIIC hybrid causal discovery framework (Verny et al., 2017), which relies on unshielded triple scores from 3-point conditional information, by adding a two-step search-and-score refinement based on a novel likelihood decomposition of ancestral graphs. Theoretically, it departs from the standard head-and-tail factorization (Richardson, 2009) and c-component factorization (Tian & Pearl, 2002) by showing that the likelihood decomposes over ac-connected subsets via multivariate cross-information, a result that coincides with the parametrizing sets of Hu & Evans (2020) but is derived independently and shown to be empirically estimable. Practically, it positions itself against existing search-and-score methods for ancestral graphs (M3HC, GFCI, Claassen & Bucur, Rantanen et al., Hu & Evans 2024), which have only been demonstrated on linear Gaussian data, by providing the first such method validated on non-linear continuous and categorical datasets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, distribution-free search-and-score method for ancestral graphs that outperforms state-of-the-art causal discovery on complex non-Gaussian and categorical data, filling a gap where all prior score-based methods for latent-variable models were limited to linear Gaussian assumptions. Key limitations include the restriction to ac-connected subsets with at most two-collider paths (which may miss contributions from longer collider chains) and the restriction to MAGs without undirected edges. Open questions include extending the local scores to higher-order collider paths, incorporating undirected edges, and clarifying the relationship between this information-theoretic score and existing BIC-based scores for discrete or stratified Gaussian models (Bellot et al., 2024).
