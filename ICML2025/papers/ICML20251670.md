## Problem

Existing multi-scale time series methods assume all variables share a consistent sampling rate and original scale, so fixed downsampling or segmentation produces meaningful views for every variable. In Irregular Multivariate Time Series (IMTS), variables have distinct sampling rates and uneven intervals, making uniform downsampling either meaningless for sparse variables or insufficient for dense ones, and producing mixed-granularity views that single-scale extractors cannot handle. The paper tackles how to extract multi-scale temporal and inter-variable dependencies in IMTS where variables operate at fundamentally different granularities and observations are asynchronous.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a set of M irregular observations, each a (timestamp, value, variable-indicator) tuple, over a historical window, plus a set of forecasting queries specifying (timestamp, variable) pairs for which values must be predicted. Because observations form an unordered set rather than a fixed-length grid, the model must handle variable counts per patch that range from zero to many, and the forecasting decoder receives per-variable node embeddings concatenated with continuous time embeddings of query timestamps. For classification, the same encoder produces per-variable embeddings that are pooled into a single vector for a binary prediction.

## Method

Hi-Patch encodes each observation as a graph node (sum of continuous time embedding, variable embedding, and value embedding), then partitions nodes into non-overlapping time patches of span P. Within each patch, a fully connected graph attention network (GAT) with three separate parameter sets for three edge types—same variable at different times, different variables at the same time, and different variables at different times—captures local temporal and asynchronous inter-variable dependencies. Nodes of the same variable within a patch are then aggregated via multi-time attention into a single patch-level node, preserving irregularity by skipping variables with no observations. A stack of ⌈log₂N⌉ inter-patch graph layers follows, where each layer connects nodes in adjacent patches at progressively coarser scales (P, 2P, 4P, …) and re-aggregates pairs of same-variable nodes, so that fine-grained features of densely sampled variables are extracted in lower layers while coarse-grained features of all variables emerge in upper layers. The final per-variable node embeddings feed task-specific decoders (MLP for forecasting, softmax for classification).

## Results

On the IMTS forecasting task, Hi-Patch achieves the best MSE and MAE across all four datasets: Human Activity (MSE 2.57×10⁻³, MAE 3.11×10⁻²), USHCN (MSE 4.94×10⁻¹, MAE 2.96×10⁻¹), PhysioNet (MSE 4.86×10⁻³, MAE 3.62×10⁻²), and MIMIC-III (MSE 1.75×10⁻², MAE 7.24×10⁻²). Across all 8 datasets (4 forecasting + 4 classification), Hi-Patch achieves the best performance in 62 out of 72 metrics. On regular MTS forecasting (ETTm1, ETTm2, Exchange, Weather), Hi-Patch attains an average rank of 2.25, outperforming iTransformer on Exchange (MSE 0.085 vs 0.086) and Weather (MSE 0.158 vs 0.174).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Hi-Patch outperforms all 17 IMTS and regular MTS baselines on the default forecasting horizon across all four IMTS datasets, with the closest competitors being Warpformer (e.g., Human Activity MSE 2.61 vs 2.57) and GraFITi (MIMIC-III MSE 1.76 vs 1.75). Regular MTS methods (iTransformer, TimesNet, PatchTST, etc.) perform substantially worse on IMTS, with iTransformer showing particularly poor results on PhysioNet (MSE 53.55 vs Hi-Patch's 4.86), confirming their inability to handle irregular sampling. Among IMTS-specific baselines, tPatchGNN and GraFITi are the strongest single-scale competitors, but Hi-Patch's hierarchical multi-scale design consistently yields 3–10% lower MSE. On the classification task, Hi-Patch achieves the best AUROC on P19 (92.1%), P12 (87.1%), PhysioNet (86.8%), and MIMIC-III (84.1%), surpassing the next-best methods (Warpformer, StraTS, DuETT) by 1–5 points. On regular MTS forecasting (ETTm1, ETTm2, Exchange, Weather), Hi-Patch ranks 2nd–3rd overall but beats iTransformer on Exchange and Weather, demonstrating generality beyond its IMTS design.

## Relation to Prior Work

Hi-Patch sits at the intersection of three research threads: patch-based time series representation (PatchTST), multi-time attention for irregular timestamps (mTAND), and graph neural networks for inter-variable dependencies (tPatchGNN, Raindrop). It departs from existing multi-scale MTS methods (Pathformer, TimeMixer, MSGNet) that assume uniform sampling and use fixed downsampling, which breaks down when variables have heterogeneous sampling rates. It also extends single-scale IMTS methods (Warpformer, tPatchGNN, GraFITi) by introducing a hierarchical bottom-up architecture where intra-patch graphs capture fine-grained local dependencies and stacked inter-patch graphs progressively extract coarser global features, with multi-time attention aggregation preserving irregularity at each level.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine gap: no prior method handles multi-scale feature extraction for IMTS where variables have fundamentally different origin scales, making fixed downsampling infeasible. The hierarchical design naturally resolves the 'mixed granularity' problem at each scale level, and the alignment of optimal patch sizes with domain-specific cycles (circadian rhythms, seasonal climate patterns) adds interpretability. However, the quadratic complexity of the intra-patch graph layer limits scalability to densely observed data, and the authors acknowledge that adaptive scale selection per sample remains future work. The method's competitive (though not dominant) performance on regular MTS benchmarks suggests the hierarchical patch-GNN paradigm has broader applicability beyond irregular series.
