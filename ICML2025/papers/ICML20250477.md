## Problem

Standard MCTS propagates point-estimate value backups through the tree, which become unreliable in highly stochastic or partially observable environments where variance in transitions causes over- or under-estimation that compounds upward to the root. Prior distributional approaches (e.g., L2-Wasserstein barycenters in TD learning, Bayesian MCTS with Dirichlet-Normal-Gamma posteriors) either lack explicit convergence guarantees or do not unify flexible backup operators with principled exploration strategies within a single MCTS framework.

## Input / Output

The algorithm receives an initial state s_0 in a finite MDP or POMDP, a playout (rollout) policy π_0, and a planning horizon H, then incrementally builds a search tree over H steps. Each node in the tree stores a full Gaussian distribution N(m, σ²) rather than a scalar value, so the output is a search tree with per-node mean and standard deviation estimates, from which the algorithm extracts the optimal action a* at the root and the estimated value V̄(s_0). The power-mean parameter p = 1 − α (derived from the α-divergence) is tuned per environment via grid search over {1, 2, 4, 8, 15, 100, 150}.

## Method

Wasserstein MCTS models every V-node and Q-node as a Gaussian and defines the V-node as the L1-Wasserstein barycenter of its Q-children, where the Wasserstein cost is an α-divergence D_{f_α}. This yields closed-form power-mean backup operators: the V-node mean is (E_{a~π}[m(s,a)^p])^{1/p} and the V-node standard deviation is (E_{a~π}[σ(s,a)^p])^{1/p}, with p = 1 − α interpolating between average-like (p→1) and max-like (p→∞) updates. Q-nodes are updated via a Bellman-style empirical backup that aggregates child V-node means and variances weighted by visit counts. Action selection uses either an optimistic UCT bonus scaled by the empirical standard deviation (W-MCTS-OS) or Thompson sampling that draws θ_i ~ N(m(s,a_i), σ²(s,a_i)) and picks argmax θ_i (W-MCTS-TS). The paper proves O(n^{-1/2}) convergence of the root value estimate by reducing each tree node to a non-stationary multi-armed bandit, establishing concentration of the power-mean backup via Thompson sampling (Theorem 2), and propagating the bound upward by induction over tree depth (Proposition 3).

## Results

On POMDP benchmarks (Table 2), W-MCTS-TS achieves the best returns across all four environments: -5.90 on Tag, -8.75 on LaserTag, 20.29 on Rocksample(15,15), and 315.45 on Pocman. The paper reports up to 80% improvement over DNG on FrozenLake, 55.31% improvement over POMCP on LaserTag, and 65.90% improvement over POMCP on Rocksample(15,15). On Pocman (Table 1), W-MCTS-TS with p=100 reaches 77.70 discounted reward at 65536 rollouts, compared to 72.2 for D2NG and 28.5 for UCT. The theoretical analysis establishes O(n^{-1/2}) convergence of the root-node value estimate to the optimal value.

## Baselines & Comparisons

W-MCTS (both Thompson sampling and optimistic selection variants) consistently outperforms UCT across all nine environments, with particularly large gaps on stochastic MDPs like FrozenLake and NChain where UCT's fixed exploration bonus fails under high variance. Against Power-UCT, W-MCTS achieves faster convergence and higher final returns on NChain, RiverSwim, and SixArms, while the two methods reach comparable performance on Taxi and FrozenLake. Compared to DNG (Bayesian MCTS with Dirichlet-Normal-Gamma posteriors), W-MCTS-TS achieves up to 80% improvement on FrozenLake and dominates on all five stochastic MDPs in Figure 1. On POMDPs, W-MCTS-TS surpasses D2NG on all three Rocksample variants and matches or exceeds it on Pocman (77.70 vs. 72.2 at 65536 rollouts). Against POMCP, W-MCTS-TS delivers the largest relative gains: 55.31% on LaserTag (-8.75 vs. -19.58) and 65.90% on Rocksample(15,15) (20.29 vs. 12.23). Finally, W-MCTS-TS outperforms both AB-DESPOT and AR-DESPOT on Tag, LaserTag, and Rocksample, while on Pocman it exceeds AR-DESPOT (315.45 vs. 307.96) and AB-DESPOT (315.45 vs. 290.34) by a wider margin.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Wasserstein barycenter framework from Metelli et al. (2019), which used L2-Wasserstein distances for uncertainty propagation in temporal-difference learning, by transplanting it into the MCTS setting with an L1-Wasserstein formulation coupled with α-divergences. The resulting backup operator is shown to coincide with the power-mean operator from Dam et al. (2019), unifying average-like and max-like value backups under a single parameter. Unlike prior Bayesian MCTS methods (DNG, D2NG) that propagate point estimates or fixed-variance posteriors, W-MCTS dynamically updates both mean and variance at every node via the barycenter, and unlike DESPOT or POMCP, it does not rely on particle-based belief tracking. The paper also departs from prior distributional MCTS work by providing explicit O(n^{-1/2}) convergence guarantees, which were absent in earlier approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The principled coupling of L1-Wasserstein geometry with α-divergences offers a single-parameter knob (p = 1−α) to interpolate between risk-averse and optimistic backups, which is practically useful for tuning MCTS in domains with varying stochasticity. The O(n^{-1/2}) convergence guarantee for a distributional MCTS with Thompson sampling is a meaningful theoretical contribution, as prior distributional planning methods lacked such explicit rates. However, the evaluation is confined to small, well-known benchmarks (4×4 FrozenLake, 7×6 Taxi, 15×35 Rocksample), and the Gaussian node assumption may limit applicability to domains with multimodal value distributions. Open questions include whether the approach scales to large-state-space problems, whether the α parameter can be adapted online, and how the method interacts with learned value networks as in AlphaZero-style settings.
