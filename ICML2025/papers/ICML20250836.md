## Problem

Neural-network-based no-reference image quality assessment (IQA) metrics are vulnerable to adversarial perturbations that can manipulate search engine rankings, benchmark comparisons, and content quality assessments, yet no comprehensive benchmark exists for evaluating defense mechanisms specifically for IQA tasks. Unlike object classification, IQA defenses face a unique triple constraint: they must restore the original quality score, preserve the metric's correlation with subjective evaluations, and maintain the perceptual quality of the defended image—making direct transfer of classification defense results unreliable.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250836/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark processes single RGB images at resolutions from 299×299 (NIPS 2017) to 512×512 (AGIQA-3K) and 512×384 (KonIQ-10k, KADID-10k), in both clean and adversarially perturbed forms, across 9 NR IQA metrics spanning CNN, transformer, and custom architectures. Purification defenses output a purified image alongside the restored quality score, while certified methods output a quality class or score with a certified radius (classification-based) or certified delta (regression-based). Results are reported across 14 attacks in both adaptive and non-adaptive settings using metrics including SROCC, D_score, R_score, PSNR, SSIM, and certified robustness bounds.

## Method

The paper introduces a systematic benchmarking framework evaluating 30 defense strategies—17 purification methods (compression, spatial transforms, blurring, denoising, diffusion-based), 2 adversarial training approaches, and 6 certified methods (randomized smoothing variants and median smoothing)—against 14 white-box and black-box attacks across 9 NR IQA metrics. A key methodological contribution is the D_score and D_score^(D) metrics, which quantify how well a purification restores the IQA score of an attacked image relative to the original, normalized by the metric's dynamic range, complementing the existing R_score robustness measure. For certified methods applied to the regression IQA task, the paper discretizes quality scores into 10 ordered classes to enable classification-based smoothing and incorporates a denoising step before model inference to maintain SROCC. The benchmark further includes a large-scale subjective study with 60,000+ pairwise comparisons from 2,700+ participants, revealing that objective metrics like PSNR and SSIM can misrepresent perceived quality of defended images and that compression-based defenses (DiffJPEG, JPEG) are most effective because adversarial perturbations manifest as high-frequency noise distinguishable from natural image detail.

## Results

Among 17 purification defenses, DiffJPEG achieves the best overall balance with SROCC_adv of 0.608 (non-adaptive) / 0.549 (adaptive) and R_score of 1.07 / 0.71, while DISCO and DiffPure lead in D_score^(D) (3.51 and 2.01 non-adaptive) but at the cost of lower SROCC. Certified methods deliver the strongest correlations—DMS (regression-based) reaches SROCC_adv of 0.822 and DP (classification-based) 0.815—but require 4–82× more inference time than the slowest purification. Adversarial training (APGD-SSIM-2) achieves SROCC_adv of 0.763 with zero inference overhead, outperforming all purification methods in correlation but offering no image purification (D_score^(D) = 17.11).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among compression-based purifications, DiffJPEG (Reich et al., 2024) and JPEG (Guo et al., 2018) are the strongest, achieving SROCC_adv of 0.608 and 0.605 respectively—far above Color Quantization (Xu et al., 2018) at 0.532—by removing high-frequency adversarial noise while preserving structure. DISCO (Ho & Vasconcelos, 2022) and DiffPure (Nie et al., 2022) achieve the lowest D_score^(D) (3.51 and 2.01 non-adaptive), indicating superior score restoration, but DiffPure's SROCC_adv drops to 0.485 due to diffusion-induced artifacts, while DISCO's R_score of 1.14 is the highest among purifications. MPRNet (Zamir et al., 2021) and Real-ESRGAN (Wang et al., 2021) show average robustness (SROCC_adv 0.535 and 0.503) but Real-ESRGAN wins the subjective quality study, surpassing even the undefended baseline. For adversarial training, APGD-SSIM-2 (Chistyakova et al., 2024) achieves SROCC_adv of 0.763 and R_score of 1.23, outperforming the gradient-norm method NT (Liu et al., 2024) at 0.649/0.81. Among certified defenses, DMS (Chiang et al., 2020) achieves the best SROCC_adv of 0.822, followed by DP (Chen et al., 2022b) at 0.815 and DDRS (Carlini et al., 2022) at 0.792, all substantially outperforming plain RS (Cohen et al., 2019) at 0.706; however, their inference times (2,830–82,130 ms) are 4–100× slower than the fastest purification (0.05 ms for Flip). FCN (Gushchin et al., 2024), designed specifically for color attacks, underperforms general-purpose defenses (SROCC_adv 0.478) and is outclassed by DiffJPEG and DISCO across all metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the well-established adversarial defense literature from image classification to the IQA domain, where defenses must simultaneously restore quality scores, preserve correlation with subjective ratings, and maintain perceptual image quality. It builds on the certified regression-to-classification reduction of Hammoudeh & Lowd (2023) to apply randomized smoothing and its variants to IQA metrics, and incorporates IQA-specific adversarial training (Chistyakova et al., 2024) and gradient norm regularization (Liu et al., 2024). Unlike prior IQA robustness studies (Antsiferova et al., 2024) that only characterized vulnerabilities, this work is the first to systematically evaluate 30 defense strategies against 14 attacks in both adaptive and non-adaptive settings, filling a gap left by classification-focused benchmarks like RobustBench.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This benchmark provides the first actionable framework for practitioners deploying IQA metrics in search engines, compression pipelines, and public benchmarks, where adversarial manipulation of quality scores is a real threat. Key open questions include the transferability of defenses across attack types, handling of multi-parameter and boundary attacks, and whether task-specific adaptations of diffusion-based purification can close the gap with compression methods. The 60,000+ response subjective study and the released adversarial image dataset (5,200 images with MOS) create resources for future adversarial training and detection research that did not previously exist.
