## Problem

Standard RL for LLMs treats each token as an action, resulting in an excessively large action space (128K+ for Llama-3) that hampers exploration efficiency and makes training computationally expensive. Moreover, token-level RL requires updating the full model, which risks degrading pre-trained knowledge (alignment tax). The paper asks how to construct a compact, well-structured latent action space for LLMs that enables more efficient and controllable RL while preserving base model capabilities.

## Input / Output

At inference, the system takes a token sequence context x_{1:t} as input. The policy model (2B params) first selects a discrete latent action a_t from a codebook of N=64 actions, then the language world model (8B base + merge-MLPs) consumes both the context embedding and the latent action to produce the next token x_{t+1}. During training, the inverse dynamics model additionally requires the next token x_{t+1} as future-conditioned input to extract ground-truth latent actions, but this module is discarded at inference. The latent action space is 64-dimensional discrete, versus the 128K+ token vocabulary, reducing the decision space by over three orders of magnitude.

## Method

CoLA decomposes language generation into a bi-level structure: a high-level policy that selects discrete latent actions and a low-level world model that generates tokens conditioned on those actions. The latent action space is learned unsupervised via an inverse dynamics model that maps (current context, next token) to a codebook entry using a novel direct action assignment mechanism with Gumbel-Softmax sampling, avoiding the codebook collapse that plagues standard VQ-VAE. The world model is a pre-trained LLM (Llama-3.1-8B) augmented with lightweight merge-MLP modules that concatenate the token embedding with the latent action before feeding into the transformer layers. Training proceeds in stages: (1) joint pre-training of inverse dynamics + merge module on 200B tokens, (2) behavior cloning to initialize the policy, (3) RL that updates only the 2B policy model while freezing the 8B world model, and (4) Fine-Tuning under Action Guidance (FTA) for task-specific adaptation. This separation means RL optimizes a small policy over a compact 64-action space rather than the full 8B model over 128K tokens, yielding 2× faster format learning, 42.4 on MATH500 (vs. 38.2 baseline), 68.2 with MCTS-Q, and robustness to reward hacking even at KL=0.

## Results

On math500, CoLA with RL achieves 42.4, surpassing the Llama-3.1-8B token-level baseline (38.2), and reaches 68.2 with the MCTS-Q search variant (vs. 65.4 for MCTS on CoLA and 63.0 for MCTS-Q on the baseline). On agentic tasks, CoLA-RL improves over Base-RL by +9.3 on AlfWorld (77.9 vs. 68.6 seen) and +10.4 on ScienceWorld (28.4 vs. 18.0 seen), with consistent gains on unseen splits. In the Countdown Game, CoLA acquires the required thinking format 2× faster (step 10 vs. step 20). Under KL=0.00 RLHF, CoLA maintains alignment across all four preference types while the baseline completely degenerates into reward-hacking behavior.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline throughout the paper is Llama-3.1-8B with standard token-level actions (one-token-one-action formulation). CoLA wins on every major comparison: on math500, CoLA-RL scores 42.4 vs. 38.2 for the baseline (+4.2), and with MCTS-Q search the gap widens to 68.2 vs. 63.0. On AlfWorld, CoLA-RL outperforms Base-RL by 9.3 points (seen) and 3.0 points (unseen); on ScienceWorld the gains are 10.4 (seen) and 6.2 (unseen), whereas Base-RL actually degrades on unseen ScienceWorld (−1.9). In the Countdown Game, CoLA learns the required thinking format in half the training steps. Most strikingly, under KL=0.00 RLHF the baseline completely fails (reward hacking into degenerate clarification prompts), while CoLA maintains coherent, task-relevant outputs across all four preference categories, demonstrating that the smaller latent action space provides inherent robustness against reward model sub-optimality.

## Relation to Prior Work

CoLA draws directly from the 'reinforcement learning from observations' literature (Torabi et al., 2019; Sun et al., 2019; Kidambi et al., 2021), which learns latent actions and transition dynamics when only observation sequences are available, and adapts this to the LLM setting where token sequences serve as observations. The inverse dynamics architecture for extracting latent actions follows Tian et al. (2024), while the discrete codebook design is inspired by VQ-VAE (van den Oord et al., 2017), though the authors replace distance-based projection with a Gumbel-Softmax direct assignment to avoid codebook collapse. The key departure from standard LLM RL (Rafailov et al., 2023; Li et al., 2024b) is the replacement of the one-token-one-action formulation (128K+ action space) with a compact 64-action latent space, creating a bi-level structure where a small policy model controls a frozen world model rather than tuning the full LLM at the token level.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The bi-level decomposition offers a practical path to reducing alignment tax: by freezing the world model during RL and only training the 2B policy over 64 latent actions, the method avoids the poly-semantic parameter interference that degrades general capabilities during token-level RL. The KL=0 robustness result (baseline degenerates into clarifying-question loops while CoLA maintains quality) suggests that smaller action spaces may inherently constrain reward hacking, which could simplify RLHF pipelines. However, the work is validated only on Llama-3.1-8B with limited compute (200G tokens), and the authors acknowledge the need for broader comparisons across base models and scales. Open questions include whether the 64-action codebook is sufficient for all domains, how the approach scales to larger models, and whether the interpretability of latent actions (semantic groupings visible in word clouds) can be leveraged for more targeted control.
