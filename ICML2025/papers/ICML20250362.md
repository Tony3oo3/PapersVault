## Problem

Existing protein foundation models (AlphaFold2, ESMFold, Chroma, etc.) are designed exclusively for single-chain proteins, leaving multi-chain protein complexes—where inter-chain interactions at the atom level are critical for biological function—largely unmodeled. Prior attempts to handle multi-chain data rely on poly-G pseudo-sequences to stitch chains together, which constrains structural connectivity to head-to-tail linking and fails to capture natural complex formation. APM addresses this by natively modeling multi-chain proteins with all-atom-level inter-chain interaction information.

## Input / Output

For unconditional generation, APM takes only chain length specifications and produces a full multi-chain complex with amino acid types, SE(3) backbone frames, and four sidechain torsion angles per residue (rather than explicit atomic coordinates). For conditional tasks (folding, inverse-folding, chain-by-chain generation), it accepts a protein sequence, backbone structure, or a partially generated chain as input. For downstream design tasks, a target antigen or receptor structure conditions the generation. The sidechain representation uses torsion angles (χ ∈ [0,2π)⁴) parameterized per residue, which is more compact than full atomic coordinates while still encoding inter-chain interaction geometry.

## Method

APM is a three-module architecture built on AlphaFold2-style IPA + Transformer Encoder blocks: (1) a Seq&BB Module performing joint sequence-backbone generation via SE(3) flow matching (geodesic interpolation on SO(3) for rotations, linear interpolation for translations) combined with discrete flow matching for amino acid sequences; (2) a Sidechain Module that predicts torsion angles given the generated sequence and backbone; (3) a Refine Module that corrects sequence and backbone using all-atom information from predicted sidechains. A key design choice is decoupling the noising schedules for sequence (t_S) and structure (t_T) to preserve their mutual dependency, and a 50% probability of switching between joint generation and folding/inverse-folding tasks during training. The Sidechain and Refine modules activate only after t ≥ 0.8, and training proceeds in two phases: separate pre-training followed by iterative joint training (2-2-8 gradient steps per cycle). A consistency loss enforces smooth generative trajectories so that Refine Module corrections accumulate coherently across sampling steps.

## Results

On the RAbD antibody CDR-H3 co-design benchmark, APM (SFT) achieves the best performance across all four metrics: 41.20% AAR, 2.08 Å RMSD, 137.74 total energy, and 91.64 binding energy (ΔG), substantially outperforming dyMEAN (612.75 ΔG) and AbDPO (116.06 ΔG). In peptide design on the LNR test set, APM (SFT) achieves the best binding energy (−19.90 ΔG), highest proportion of negative ΔG (69.34%), and the only non-trivial DockQ ≥ 0.8 rate (11.29%). For multi-chain complex generation (50-100 residues), APM achieves −72.44 ΔG_RSC and −112.65 ΔG_RAA, compared to Chroma's +133.64 and −83.96, demonstrating dramatically stronger inter-chain binding. In unconditional single-chain generation at length 100, APM achieves 0.96 scTM and 1.80 scRMSD, outperforming all baselines including ProteinGenerator (0.91/3.75) and MultiFlow* (0.86/4.73).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Chroma (Ingraham et al., 2023) in multi-chain complex generation, APM achieves dramatically stronger binding: at 50-100 residues, APM reaches −72.44 ΔG_RSC versus Chroma's +133.64, and at 100-100, −91.61 versus +89.47, indicating Chroma's generated complexes are essentially non-binding while APM's are tightly bound. In antibody CDR-H3 design, APM (SFT) outperforms all four baselines (dyMEAN, DiffAb, AbDPO, AbDPO++) on every metric, with the largest gap in binding energy (91.64 vs. 116.06 for AbDPO, the closest competitor). In peptide design, APM (SFT) surpasses PPFlow, DiffPP, PepGLAD, and RFDiffusion in functionality (−19.90 vs. −8.56 to −23.27 ΔG) and is the only method achieving non-zero DockQ ≥ 0.8 (11.29% vs. 0.00–0.90%). For single-chain unconditional generation, APM outperforms ProteinGenerator and ProtPardelle at all tested lengths (100–300) in both scTM and scRMSD, while remaining competitive with MultiFlow* and ESM3. In multi-chain folding, APM (12.6 RMSD) outperforms Boltz-1 without MSA (17.86 RMSD) but trails Boltz-1 with MSA (5.40 RMSD), reflecting the extreme difficulty of multi-chain structure prediction.

## Relation to Prior Work

APM builds directly on AlphaFold2's IPA + Transformer Encoder architecture as its structural trunk, and adopts SE(3) flow matching (Yim et al., 2023; Lipman et al., 2023) for backbone generation and Discrete Flow Matching (Campbell et al., 2024) for sequence generation, with ESMFold's learnable-weight PLM integration for sequence understanding. The key conceptual departure is from prior multi-chain approaches (AlphaFold2, ESMFold, Linker-Tuning) that concatenate chains via poly-G pseudo sequences, which constrains structural connectivity to head-to-tail linking; APM instead natively models independent chains with inter-chain attention. The three-module decomposition (Seq&BB → Sidechain → Refine) with decoupled noising and a consistency loss is a novel architectural choice motivated by the incompatibility of sidechain packing supervision with flow-matching inputs, and the all-atom representation via sidechain torsion angles (rather than full atomic coordinates) is a deliberate efficiency trade-off distinct from AlphaFold3-style direct coordinate modeling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Multi-chain protein complex design at the all-atom level was largely unaddressed by prior generative models, and APM provides the first unified framework that handles generation, folding, inverse-folding, and functional design (antibodies, peptides) for multi-chain systems in a single model. The zero-shot capability—generating binding-capable antibodies and peptides without task-specific fine-tuning—suggests that multi-chain training genuinely captures inter-chain interaction physics, though the generated structures diverge from natural antibody/peptide patterns. Key limitations remain: multi-chain folding accuracy is still poor (RMSD ~12.6), the Refine Module's correction capacity is restricted, performance degrades beyond two chains, and the downstream task coverage is limited to antibodies and peptides. Open questions include whether the torsion-angle representation scales to non-protein biomolecules and whether the iterative three-module training can be simplified.
