## Problem

Existing uncertainty quantification methods (deep ensembles, Monte Carlo dropout, early-exit ensembles) impose memory and compute overheads that are prohibitive for KB-sized, milliwatt-scale tinyML devices deployed in remote environments without true labels. Prior early-exit ensemble approaches like EE-ensemble add expensive fully-connected layers at each exit to balance learning capacities, making them impractical for ultra-low-power microcontrollers. Additionally, larger models become overconfident on corrupted-in-distribution (CID) data, undermining their ability to detect accuracy drops in the field.

## Input / Output

The method accepts standard classification inputs: grayscale 28×28 images (MNIST), RGB 32×32 images (CIFAR-10), RGB 64×64 images (Tiny-ImageNet), or 49×10 mel-spectrograms (SpeechCommands audio). At inference, the model consists only of the base network plus K lightweight output blocks (each a single depthwise-convolution layer); all early-exits and the original output block are removed. The final output is the element-wise mean of K predictive probability vectors, from which a scalar confidence score (max probability) is extracted for monitoring.

## Method

QUTE appends K lightweight classification heads at the final exit of a base CNN and adds K early-exit blocks along the network that are used exclusively during training via an 'early-view assistance' mechanism. Before each training batch, the weights of each early-exit's learning layer h_θk are copied into the corresponding output block's layer h_φk; after the batch, h_φk is updated by backpropagation but immediately overwritten before the next batch, so the output blocks iteratively absorb early-exit features without diverging. A weighted loss with incrementally increasing coefficients (w_EV_k = w_EV_{k-1} + 0.5) promotes diversity across heads, and the base network is frozen for the last 10% of epochs to isolate EV-exit training. At inference, all early-exits and the overconfident original output block are discarded, leaving only the base network and K single-depthwise-CONV heads that produce the ensemble prediction in a single forward pass, achieving 59% smaller models and 31% lower latency than the closest prior work on microcontrollers.

## Results

QUTE achieves the best NLL on all tiny/medium models: 0.199 on MNIST (vs. 0.266 for EE-ensemble, 0.227 for DEEP), 0.202 on SpeechCmd, and 0.428 on CIFAR-10, while being 59% smaller than EE-ensemble on average. On accuracy-drop detection (AUPRC), QUTE outperforms all baselines, reaching 0.63 on MNIST-C and 0.61 on SpeechCmd-C. On MCU deployment, QUTE delivers a 31% average latency reduction over EE-ensemble and 47% over DEEP on the Big-MCU, and is the only method that fits on the Small-MCU (256 KB flash) for CIFAR-10. On the larger TinyImageNet/MobileNetV2 benchmark, QUTE+ (with extra output layers) achieves NLL 2.757, approaching EE-ensemble's 2.534 while remaining far more compact.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against **EE-ensemble** (the closest prior work), QUTE achieves substantially lower NLL on all tiny/medium models (0.199 vs 0.266 on MNIST, 0.202 vs 0.226 on SpeechCmd) with 59% fewer parameters and 31% lower MCU latency; EE-ensemble retains an edge only on TinyImageNet (NLL 2.534 vs 3.700) where its larger early-exit FC layers help. Against **DEEP**, QUTE wins on NLL for MNIST (0.199 vs 0.227) and SpeechCmd (0.202 vs 0.205) while DEEP leads on TinyImageNet (3.440 vs 3.700); QUTE also requires a single forward pass versus DEEP's K sequential passes, halving MCU latency. Against **MCD**, QUTE dominates on NLL across all four datasets (e.g., 0.199 vs 0.382 on MNIST), as MCD's inference-time dropout reduces effective model capacity. Against **HYDRA**, QUTE outperforms on NLL for every dataset and avoids HYDRA's collapse on CIFAR-10 (NLL 0.632, below BASE's 0.523). Against **G-ODIN**, QUTE achieves higher AUPRC on all corrupted datasets (0.63 vs 0.48 on MNIST-C, 0.61 vs 0.37 on SpeechCmd-C), confirming that general OOD detectors are unreliable for non-semantic CID shifts. Against **PostNets**, QUTE achieves lower NLL on both MNIST (0.199 vs 0.286) and CIFAR-10 (0.428 vs 0.462) with no additional memory overhead.

## Relation to Prior Work

QUTE sits at the intersection of early-exit networks and ensemble-based uncertainty quantification, building directly on the T-RecX early-exit architecture (Ghanathe & Wilton, 2023) and the EE-ensemble framework (Qendro et al., 2021). The key departure from EE-ensemble is architectural: rather than using early-exit outputs (with their expensive additional FC layers) as ensemble members, QUTE distills early-exit knowledge into lightweight classification heads at the final exit via a weight-transfer mechanism, then discards the early-exits entirely at inference. This contrasts with HYDRA's ensemble distillation approach, which compresses multiple independently-trained networks into a single multi-headed model, and with Deep Ensembles, which requires K full forward passes. The paper positions itself as the first early-exit ensemble architecture specifically optimized for the KB-scale, milliwatt constraints of tinyML deployment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

QUTE addresses a practical gap: most UQ methods are simply infeasible on ultra-low-power MCUs (EE-ensemble exceeds Small-MCU memory by 270KB on CIFAR10), leaving field-deployed tinyML models without any monitoring capability. The finding that smaller models are paradoxically less overconfident on corrupted-in-distribution data (Table 1) provides a useful design principle for edge deployment, though it is demonstrated primarily on a single architecture family. Open questions include whether the ensemble-size limit (bounded by network depth) suffices for safety-critical applications, whether the method transfers to transformer-based edge models (discussed but not tested), and whether improved early-exit calibration techniques could further enhance the EV-assistance mechanism.
