## Problem

Hypergraph isomorphism testing and the expressivity of hypergraph neural networks (HNNs) have been limited to the 1-dimensional generalized Weisfeiler-Leman test (1-GWL), with no systematic way to increase expressivity to higher dimensions. While the graph domain has well-understood k-WL hierarchies and corresponding GNNs with provable expressivity bounds up to 3-WL, the hypergraph counterpart lacks any higher-dimensional generalization, leaving most HNNs (UniGNN, AllSetTransformer, ED-HNN) capped at 1-GWL's distinguishing power.

## Input / Output

The k-GWL algorithm takes two hypergraphs (vertex sets, hyperedge sets, optional vertex feature vectors) and outputs an isomorphism/non-isomorphism verdict by iteratively coloring k-tuples of vertices. The k-HNN model takes a single hypergraph and produces a hypergraph-level embedding via a permutation-invariant sum readout over all k-set embeddings, which is then fed to a classifier. A key implementation detail is that k-HNNs use set-based k-tuples (k-sets, ignoring order and repetition) with local neighborhood pruning—retaining only neighbors where the replaced vertex shares a hyperedge with the original—to keep the k-tuple hypergraph tractable on GPU memory.

## Method

The paper proposes k-GWL, which generalizes k-WL to hypergraphs by (1) initializing k-tuple colors via the isomorphism type of the induced sub-hypergraph (with singleton hyperedges deleted to ensure consistency with k-WL on simple graphs), (2) constructing a k-tuple hypergraph where hyperedges encode neighborhood relations among k-tuples, and (3) applying 1-GWL two-stage color refinement on this k-tuple hypergraph. Two variants are defined: k-GFWL (folklore, n hyperedges per k-tuple, one per replacement vertex) and k-GOWL (oblivious, k hyperedges per k-tuple, one per position). The paper proves the complete GWL expressivity hierarchy (1-GOWL ≅ 1-GFWL < 2-GOWL < 3-GOWL ≅ 2-GFWL < …), shows k-GWL degenerates to k-WL on simple graphs, and establishes that (k+1)-GOWL ≅ k-GFWL for k ≥ 2. Building on k-GWL, the k-HNN architecture uses a base HNN (a UniGNN variant with residual connections) operating on the pruned k-set hypergraph, with initial features combining isomorphism-type encodings and base-HNN vertex embeddings, achieving provable expressivity upper-bounded by k-GWL.

## Results

The proposed k-HNN models (2-FHNN and 2-OHNN) achieve the best or near-best accuracy across all six hypergraph classification datasets. The headline result is on Steam-Player, where 2-FHNN reaches 67.53% accuracy, outperforming the runner-up AllSetTransformer (61.87%) by approximately 6 percentage points. On IMDB-WRI-GENRE, 3-OHNN (with vertex sampling) achieves 57.50%, surpassing the best 2-HNN variant (50.08% for 2-OHNN) and AllSetTransformer (54.09%). Across the four IMDB subsets, 2-FHNN leads on three (IMDB-DIR-FORM: 68.11%, IMDB-WRI-FORM: 55.36%, IMDB-WRI-GENRE: 45.44%) while 2-OHNN leads on IMDB-DIR-GENRE (79.75%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against six hypergraph deep learning baselines: MLP, HyperGCN, HNHN, UniGCNII, AllSetTransformer, ED-HNN, and HIC (1-GWL subtree kernel). The strongest baseline is AllSetTransformer, which 2-FHNN outperforms by 5.66% on Steam-Player (67.53 vs 61.87) and by 1.35% on IMDB-DIR-FORM (68.11 vs 66.76). On Twitter-Friend, 2-OHNN edges out AllSetTransformer by 0.09% (64.12 vs 64.03), while 2-FHNN trails slightly (62.37 vs 64.03), showing the two k-HNN variants have complementary strengths. ED-HNN and HIC are consistently the weakest baselines, with 2-FHNN outperforming ED-HNN by up to 9.2% (Steam-Player: 67.53 vs 58.35) and HIC by up to 9.2% (Steam-Player: 67.53 vs 58.35). On IMDB-WRI-GENRE, 3-OHNN with vertex sampling achieves 57.50%, beating AllSetTransformer (54.09%) by 3.41% and 2-OHNN (50.08%) by 7.42%, suggesting higher k captures structure that 2-HNNs miss on this particular dataset. Overall, 2-FHNN wins on 4 of 6 datasets and 2-OHNN wins on 2, with 3-OHNN taking IMDB-WRI-GENRE.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper generalizes the k-dimensional Weisfeiler-Leman (k-WL) graph isomorphism test (Immerman & Lander, 1990) to hypergraphs, producing k-GWL with folklore (k-GFWL) and oblivious (k-GOWL) variants that mirror the k-FWL/k-OWL distinction from the graph literature (Maron et al., 2019; Morris et al., 2019). It extends the 1-GWL hypergraph isomorphism test (Feng et al., 2024; Huang & Yang, 2021), which was the only known hypergraph isomorphism algorithm, to arbitrary dimension k, and builds k-HNNs on top of a UniGNN-style base HNN (Huang & Yang, 2021) to achieve expressivity bounded by k-GWL rather than 1-GWL. The sub-hypergraph extraction step (deleting singleton hyperedges) is a key technical addition that ensures k-GWL degenerates to k-WL on simple graphs, unifying the two isomorphism frameworks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes the first complete expressivity hierarchy for hypergraph isomorphism tests, filling a gap where only 1-GWL existed. A notable structural difference from the graph WL hierarchy is that 2-GOWL is strictly more powerful than 1-GWL (unlike 2-OWL ≅ 1-WL in graphs), meaning even the simplest dimensional lift yields a genuine gain for hypergraphs. Open questions include computational efficiency for large k (the k-tuple hypergraph grows as O(n^k)), whether efficient sampling or structural encodings can mitigate this, and characterizing the expressivity of hypergraph transformers. The 6% accuracy gain on Steam-Player and consistent improvements across six datasets demonstrate practical value, though the 3-HNN experiments rely on vertex sampling due to memory constraints.
