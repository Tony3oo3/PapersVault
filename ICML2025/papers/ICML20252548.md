## Problem

Existing time series foundation models scale up pre-training data and model size but overlook two critical design aspects: (1) how to derive unified representations from heterogeneous multi-domain data whose temporal patterns are composed of superimposed frequency components with varying significance across domains, and (2) how to capture and adaptively transfer domain-specific information rather than relying solely on generalized representations for downstream tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252548/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes univariate time series of fixed length 512 in a channel-independent manner, partitioned into 8 non-overlapping patches of size 64 plus 3 register tokens that encode domain-specific context. It produces multi-horizon forecasts at lengths {96, 192, 336, 720} via four dedicated prediction heads. The entire model is lightweight at 7.4M parameters with 3 encoder and 3 decoder Transformer layers (D=256, 16 heads), achieving inference times roughly one-tenth of the next-fastest foundation model.

## Method

ROSE introduces two key mechanisms. First, Decomposed Frequency Learning transforms each input via rFFT into the frequency domain, applies K_f=4 independently sampled high- or low-frequency masks (determined by random thresholds τ and Bernoulli variables), transforms back via irFFT, and trains the model to reconstruct the original sequence—disentangling coupled temporal patterns into frequency-specific views that yield unified representations. Second, the TS-Register maintains H=128 learnable cluster-center vectors that absorb domain-specific information during pre-training through a nearest-neighbor clustering loss; at fine-tuning time, the Top-K (k=3) nearest register vectors are selected for the target domain and adjusted via a learnable low-rank matrix A = u·v^T to inject dataset-specific information. Pre-training co-optimizes reconstruction, supervised prediction, and register clustering losses, while fine-tuning freezes the register and trains only the low-rank adjustment and prediction head.

## Results

In the full-shot setting, ROSE achieves the best average MSE across all seven benchmarks (ETTh1: 0.391, ETTh2: 0.331, ETTm1: 0.341, ETTm2: 0.246, Weather: 0.217, Electricity: 0.155, Traffic: 0.390), yielding a 15% average MSE reduction over the strongest baselines. Remarkably, ROSE fine-tuned on only 10% of downstream data (e.g., ETTh1 MSE 0.397, ETTm2 MSE 0.250) still outperforms baselines trained on full data by over 12% average MSE. In the zero-shot setting, ROSE achieves a 15% average MSE reduction over five foundation models, with a 43% relative improvement over Moment and 9% over Timer, while using only 7.4M parameters (~10× fewer than the next smallest foundation model).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ROSE outperforms all six specific/LLM-based baselines (iTransformer, PatchTST, TimesNet, DLinear, GPT4TS, S2IP-LLM) on every dataset in the full-shot setting, with the largest margins over TimesNet (e.g., 0.391 vs. 0.582 MSE on ETTh1) and DLinear on ETTh2 (0.331 vs. 0.508). In the 10% few-shot setting, ROSE's advantage widens dramatically: DLinear degrades to MSE 1.355 on ETTh1 and 1.569 on ETTh2, while ROSE(10%) maintains 0.397 and 0.335 respectively. Among foundation models in zero-shot, ROSE (7.4M params) beats Timer (67.4M) by 9% average MSE, MOIRAI (311M) by 6%, and Moment (385M) by 43%, while also being ~10× faster at inference (0.652s vs. 5.989s for Timer). PatchTST is the closest competitor in full-shot (matching ROSE on ETTh2 at 0.331 MSE), but falls behind in few-shot and zero-shot. The paper also demonstrates that multi-frequency masking transfers to other architectures, improving Autoformer by 6.3% and TimesNet by 3.7% average MSE, confirming the pre-training task's generality beyond ROSE's specific architecture.

## Relation to Prior Work

ROSE builds on PatchTST's patch-token and channel-independent formulation for time series encoding, and extends the single-pass frequency masking of Zhang et al. (2022) into a multi-threshold, multi-frequency decomposition scheme. Conceptually, it departs from the dominant scaling paradigm of recent time series foundation models (MOIRAI, Timer, Chronos, TimesFM, MOMENT), which pursue generalization primarily through larger pre-training corpora and parameter counts, by instead investing in pre-training task design (decomposed frequency learning) and a domain-aware transfer mechanism (TS-Register with Top-K selection and low-rank adaptation). The register-token idea borrows from vision transformer registers but repurposes them to cluster and store domain-specific embeddings learned during multi-domain pre-training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a concrete counter-narrative to the 'bigger is better' assumption in time series foundation models: a 7.4M-parameter model with 0.89B pre-training points matches or beats 46M–385M-parameter competitors on zero-shot forecasting, suggesting that pre-training task design and adaptive transfer may matter more than raw scale for this task. However, the evaluation is confined to seven relatively small benchmarks (ETT, Weather, Traffic, Electricity) and the pre-training corpus is an order of magnitude smaller than competitors', leaving open whether the advantage persists at truly large scale or on more diverse, high-dimensional domains. The authors note future work on expanding pre-training data and extending ROSE to classification and other time series tasks, but the current scope limits broader conclusions about the generalizability of the TS-Register mechanism.
