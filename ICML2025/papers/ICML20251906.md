## Problem

Prior mechanistic interpretability work on LLM safety has identified a single refusal direction in activation space, conflating multiple distinct safety features into one aggregate vector. This limits understanding of how safety fine-tuning actually shapes model behavior and obscures the specific vulnerabilities that jailbreak attacks exploit. The paper asks what multiple orthogonal directions jointly control safety-aligned behavior and how they interact to produce refusal.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251906/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes pre- and post-safety-fine-tuning hidden states from Llama 3.1 8B Instruct at the first generated token position across all 32 transformer layers, paired with harmful prompts (STRONG REJECT samples wrapped in various jailbreak templates) and benign prompts (OR-Bench). It produces an affine map S(x)=Wx+b approximating the representation shift, whose SVD yields orthogonal component directions; PLRP then attributes each direction to specific input tokens and to directions in earlier layers. The final output includes a trigger-removal attack that rephrases jailbreak prompts to avoid identified trigger tokens, evaluated by Strong Reject Score and refusal accuracy.

## Method

The paper defines the Safety Residual Space as the linear span of activation shifts induced by safety fine-tuning, approximated by a least-squares affine map S(x)=Wx+b fitted on training data. SVD of W−I extracts orthogonal principal components, with the first (dominant) component shown to predict refusal behavior and subsequent (non-dominant) components capturing interpretable features such as hypothetical narrative framing, role-playing patterns, and PAIR-attack-specific cues. To interpret these directions without requiring contrastive probe pairs, the authors introduce Partial Layer-wise Relevance Propagation (PLRP), which projects layer activations onto the span of extracted directions, decomposes the resulting norm into relevance scores, and back-propagates them to input tokens or earlier-layer directions using the epsilon rule. Intervention experiments (subtracting direction vectors from hidden states) confirm that non-dominant directions causally modulate the dominant direction's activation, and a Trigger Removal Attack that iteratively rephrases jailbreak prompts to eliminate PLRP-identified trigger tokens bypasses safety alignment even after 80-shot fine-tuning, retaining ~40% attack success while other methods drop to near zero.

## Results

After safety fine-tuning (SSFT) on Llama 3.1 8B Instruct, the Trigger Removal Attack maintains a 42% attack pass rate at 80-shot exposure and 30% at 160-shot, while all baseline jailbreak methods drop to near zero (PAIR: 0.12→0.05, FLIP: 0.03, GPTFUZZ: 0.03, ReNellm: 0.00). The dominant direction (L25-C1) achieves high refusal prediction accuracy in later layers, matching or exceeding the single-direction probe vector from Arditi et al. (2024). Suppressing non-dominant components reduces jailbreak refusal from 96.7% to 14.4% while preserving 80% refusal on plainly harmful prompts, confirming that indirect feature directions causally shape safety behavior.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The Trigger Removal Attack substantially outperforms all baseline jailbreak methods in resilience to safety fine-tuning. At 80-shot exposure, PAIR (Chao et al., 2023) drops from 0.82 to 0.12, FLIP (Liu et al., 2024) from 0.78 to 0.03, and ReNellm (Ding et al., 2023) to 0.00, while Trigger Removal retains 0.42 effectiveness. GPTFUZZ (Yu et al., 2023) is already weak pre-training (0.02) and remains negligible. In the mechanistic comparison, the dominant direction extracted from the safety residual space matches or exceeds the single-direction refusal probe vector (Arditi et al., 2024) in later layers, while the probe vector performs better in early layers—suggesting the probe aggregates multiple orthogonal safety feature directions. The paper also shows that removing the specific non-dominant direction L14-C6 selectively ablates PAIR refusal without affecting other attack types, a granularity of control not achievable with the single-direction probe.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the single-direction safety probe paradigm (Arditi et al., 2024) by introducing the Safety Residual Space — the linear span of activation shifts induced by safety fine-tuning — and decomposing it via SVD into orthogonal feature directions. It builds on the Linear Representation Hypothesis (Park et al., 2023) for the theoretical grounding of feature directions, and extends Layer-wise Relevance Propagation (Bach et al., 2015; Achtibat et al., 2024) into a new Partial LRP (PLRP) method that attributes direction activations back to input tokens or earlier-layer directions. The key conceptual departure is from the 'one direction governs refusal' view to a multi-dimensional picture where non-dominant directions (e.g., hypothetical narrative, role-playing) causally modulate the dominant refusal direction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a mechanistic account of why safety fine-tuning is vulnerable: non-dominant directions capture jailbreak-specific surface patterns (spurious correlations) rather than general harmfulness, and removing trigger tokens associated with these directions can bypass learned safety even after extensive fine-tuning (~40% attack success at 80-shot vs. near-zero for other attacks). This suggests current safety training may be overfitting to specific prompt structures rather than learning robust safety concepts. Open questions include whether the multi-dimensional structure generalizes to larger models, more diverse alignment data, and non-refusal safety behaviors, and whether targeted interventions in activation space or data augmentation can prevent the spurious-correlation learning the paper identifies.
