## Problem

State-of-the-art LLM unlearning methods, including NPO, remain vulnerable to relearning attacks where fine-tuning on just 20–60 forget samples can fully reverse the unlearning effect. No prior work had established a robust optimization foundation for LLM unlearning analogous to adversarial training's min-max framework, leaving the optimization landscape of the forget loss unaddressed as a source of fragility.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250608/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pretrained LLM (e.g., Zephyr-7B-beta, LLaMA-3 8B), a forget set defining the scope of unlearning (e.g., WMDP biosecurity texts, Harry Potter passages, BBC news articles), and a retain set for utility preservation (e.g., Wikitext). It outputs unlearned model parameters whose forget-loss landscape is explicitly smoothed, making the model resistant to both weight-space relearning attacks (fine-tuning on small forget subsets) and input-space jailbreaking attacks (adversarial GCG prompts). The unlearning procedure runs for a fixed number of steps (e.g., 125 for WMDP, 10 epochs for MUSE) with a perturbation radius ρ=0.01, adding only ~2× the forward/backward cost of vanilla NPO.

## Method

The paper formulates robust unlearning as a min-max optimization where the outer minimization performs unlearning (forget + retain losses) and the inner maximization identifies worst-case weight perturbations δ (bounded by ‖δ‖₂ ≤ ρ) that maximize the forget loss, directly mirroring adversarial training but in weight space rather than input space. This formulation is shown to be equivalent to applying SAM to the forget loss, and the paper further proves via Taylor expansion that SAM's gradient implicitly involves the Hessian-gradient product Hv, linking it to curvature regularization. Beyond SAM, the authors extend the smoothness framework to randomized smoothing (Gaussian weight perturbations), gradient penalty (‖∇ℓ_f‖₂ regularization), explicit curvature regularization (penalizing gradient change along v), and weight averaging across training checkpoints. The practical algorithm (Algorithm A1) alternates between computing the closed-form SAM perturbation direction and a standard gradient descent step, requiring no second-order computation at runtime.

## Results

On WMDP (Zephyr-7B-beta), NPO+SAM achieves a UE of 0.70 under a relearning attack with 20 forget samples (1 epoch), compared to 0.57 for vanilla NPO, while maintaining identical UE (0.74) in the absence of attacks. On LLaMA-3 8B, NPO+SAM matches TAR's UE (0.70) under a 60-sample relearning attack but runs in 11.5 minutes versus TAR's 7,441.9 minutes (~647× speedup), and significantly outperforms RMU-LAT (0.70 vs. 0.44 UE). On MUSE News, NPO+SAM reduces the post-attack VerbMem degradation on the forget set to 51.47 versus 56.57 for NPO, and on MUSE Books reduces it to 58.38 versus 67.52.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against vanilla NPO, NPO+SAM consistently improves robustness across all relearning settings: at N=20, UE rises from 0.57 to 0.70; at M=3, from 0.37 to 0.59, while UE without attack remains unchanged at 0.74. Against TAR (Tamirisa et al., 2024), NPO+SAM matches the UE of 0.70 under a 60-sample relearning attack on LLaMA-3 8B but completes training in 11.5 minutes versus TAR's 7,441.9 minutes, a ~647× runtime advantage. Against RMU-LAT (Sheshadri et al., 2024), NPO+SAM substantially outperforms on robustness (0.70 vs. 0.44 UE under attack) while also achieving higher UT (0.51 vs. 0.56 is a slight loss, but the robustness gap is decisive). Against GradDiff, adding SAM improves UE at N=20 from 0.45 to 0.65, confirming the generality of the smoothness-based defense. Against RMU, SAM yields only a modest gain (0.39→0.42 at N=20) because RMU updates only a subset of layers (5–7) while relearning attacks target the full model, creating a parameter-update mismatch.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper draws a direct analogy between adversarial training (Madry et al., 2018) for input-level robustness and robust LLM unlearning against weight-level relearning attacks, showing that the resulting min-max formulation naturally yields sharpness-aware minimization (SAM, Foret et al., 2021) applied to the forget loss. While prior robust unlearning work (TAR via meta-learning, RMU-LAT via latent adversarial training) addressed relearning attacks through more complex frameworks, this paper identifies smoothness optimization as the underlying principle and extends it beyond SAM to randomized smoothing, gradient penalty, curvature regularization, and weight averaging. The primary unlearning baseline is NPO (Zhang et al., 2024a), and the paper positions its contribution as revealing an overlooked optimization foundation rather than proposing a new unlearning objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: NPO+SAM achieves robustness comparable to TAR (7441 min runtime) at a fraction of the cost (11.5 min), making robust unlearning far more accessible. The dual defense against both relearning attacks (weight perturbations) and jailbreaking attacks (input perturbations) broadens the technique's applicability, and the identification of 'shallow unlearning alignment' as a failure mode provides a diagnostic lens for future work. Open questions include whether these gains persist at larger model scales (tested up to 8B), against more sophisticated multi-step relearning attacks, and whether the smoothness benefit is specific to preference-optimization-based unlearning or universal across all unlearning paradigms.
