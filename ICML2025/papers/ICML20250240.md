## Problem

3D vision-language grounding suffers from a six-order-of-magnitude data gap: 2D models train on billions of labeled images while 3D models access only thousands of annotated scenes, and all existing 3D VLG methods require ground-truth 3D masks or boxes during training. This creates a fundamental scaling barrier that prevents 3D grounding from leveraging the vast 2D data driving progress in 2D understanding. The paper asks whether differentiable rendering can bridge this gap, enabling training of powerful 3D grounding models using only 2D supervision from foundation models.

## Input / Output

The model takes a sparse RGB point cloud (|P|×6, XYZ+RGB, voxel-downsampled to 5 cm yielding ~50k points from RGB-D sensor scans) and a frozen CLIP text embedding as input. It outputs 256 3D mask candidates over N Gaussian primitives (M ∈ R^{256×N}), a correspondence matrix C ∈ R^{256×|Q|} mapping each mask to text tokens, and a full 3D Gaussian Splatting representation G ∈ R^{N×(m+12+512)} encoding positions, covariances, colors, and features. During training, the predicted 3D masks are rendered into 512×512 2D views for supervision; at inference, the 3D masks are used directly as outputs.

## Method

LIFT-GS predicts 3D Gaussian Splatting representations feed-forwardly from a point cloud via a sparse convolutional UNet encoder and MLP head, then uses a Mask-Former-style 8-layer transformer decoder (with 256 learnable mask proposal tokens and cross-attention over both Gaussian and language tokens) to produce mask candidates and text-mask correspondences. The key training mechanism is render-supervised distillation: predicted 3D masks are differentially rendered into 2D views and supervised with pseudo-labels generated by SAM (segmentation masks) and CLIP (image embeddings as proxy language queries), requiring no 3D annotations. Training combines an MDETR-style grounding loss (Hungarian matching, cross-entropy for correspondence, Focal+Dice for masks) with optional per-pixel photometric (L1+SSIM) and CLIP-style contrastive feature losses. The pipeline is two-stage: large-scale pretraining on unlabeled 3D data with 2D pseudo-labels, followed by fine-tuning with 3D annotations, where pretraining effectively multiplies fine-tuning data efficiency by ~2×. Unlike prior dual-encoder approaches that compute masks via dot-product similarity (inheriting bag-of-words limitations), LIFT-GS's decoder architecture jointly processes modalities through learned attention, enabling proper handling of relational and multi-object language.

## Results

On open-vocabulary 3D instance segmentation (ScanNet200), LIFT-GS achieves 25.7% mAP, surpassing the prior SOTA PQ3D (20.2%) by 5.5 points. On 3D referential grounding with realistic sensor point clouds, LIFT-GS achieves 50.9% Acc@25 and 36.5% Acc@50 on SR3D, 43.7% and 29.7% on NR3D, and 49.7% and 36.4% on ScanRefer, yielding 10–30% relative improvements over its scratch-trained counterpart. Pretraining with 2D pseudo-labels effectively multiplies fine-tuning data by ~2×, meaning a pretrained model with 50% of fine-tuning data matches a from-scratch model using 100%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LIFT-GS outperforms all single-stage baselines on sensor point clouds: it beats BUTD-DETR (Jain et al., 2021) by 7.6 points on SR3D Acc@25 (50.9 vs. 43.3) and by 11.3 points on NR3D Acc@50 (29.7 vs. 19.4). Against the two-stage 3D-VisTA (Zhu et al., 2023c), which uses Mask3D bounding box proposals from mesh point clouds, LIFT-GS achieves higher Acc@25 on SR3D (50.9 vs. 47.2) but lower Acc@50 (36.5 vs. 43.2), reflecting the advantage of 3D-VisTA's explicit box proposals at stricter IoU thresholds. On open-vocabulary segmentation, LIFT-GS surpasses PQ3D (Zhu et al., 2024) by 5.5 mAP points (25.7 vs. 20.2) and OpenMask3D (Takmaz et al., 2023) by 10.3 points. In the pretraining comparison (Table 3), LIFT-GS outperforms PonderV2 (Zhu et al., 2023b) retrained with the same SAM-CLIP pseudo-labels by 2.1 points on Acc@0.25 (47.5 vs. 45.4), demonstrating the benefit of the multimodal decoder architecture. Against 3D pseudo-labels from ConceptFusion (Jatavallabhula et al., 2023a) in Table 8, LIFT-GS's 2D-rendered features (51.8 Acc@0.25) exceed the lifted 3D pseudo-labels (50.1), showing that differentiable rendering learns better 3D feature aggregation than hand-crafted voxel pooling. Older baselines LanguageRefer (Roh et al., 2021) and SAT-2D (Yang et al., 2021) are substantially outperformed, with LIFT-GS exceeding their SR3D Acc@25 scores (39.5 and 35.4 on MeshPC) by over 10 points even under the harder sensor point cloud setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

LIFT-GS extends the render-supervised learning paradigm introduced by PonderV2 (which only trained 3D encoders with CLIP losses on rendered pixels) to enable end-to-end training of full encoder-decoder architectures for 3D vision-language grounding. It departs from the dominant dual-encoder (dot-product) formulation inherited from CLIP-style methods by adopting a transformer decoder with joint attention (following MDETR and UniVLG), and replaces per-scene optimization-based lifting (ConceptFusion, LERF) with feed-forward prediction of 3D Gaussian representations. The pseudo-labeling strategy using SAM for masks and CLIP/LLaMA for language queries positions the work at the intersection of foundation model distillation and differentiable rendering, a combination not previously applied to 3D VLG.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The consistent 2× fine-tuning data multiplication from 2D pretraining, observed without diminishing returns across data scales, provides strong empirical evidence that 3D VLG operates in a severely data-scarce regime and that render-supervised distillation is a scalable path forward. The approach is architecture-agnostic and directly benefits from ongoing improvements in 2D foundation models, making it a practical and future-proof training recipe. Key limitations remain: LIFT-GS still trails two-stage methods (3D-VisTA) at IoU 0.5 on referential grounding, CLIP-based pseudo-labels are imperfect for zero-shot use, and the method requires posed multi-view images during training. Open questions include whether improved VLM captioning or 2D grounding models can close the zero-shot gap, and whether the 2× multiplier holds as 3D annotation data grows.
