## Problem

Training large audio-language models (LALMs) requires extensive audio-language QA pairs, making data collection prohibitively expensive and training resource-intensive. Existing text-only captioning frameworks that avoid this cost are restricted to a single audio type (sound) and a single task (captioning), and their modality-gap bridging strategies—either pure noise injection or pure memory-bank retrieval—are individually insufficient, being either too stochastic or discarding audio-specific information.

## Input / Output

At inference, MATS accepts a 7-second audio clip (44.1 kHz, converted to 64-bin log-Mel spectrograms with 320 ms hop and 1024 ms window) paired with a free-form text instruction, and produces a text response whose format depends on the task: a class label for classification, a natural-language caption for captioning, or a free-form answer for open-ended QA. Unusually, during training the audio encoder is never invoked; instead, CLAP's language encoder processes text descriptions of audio content, so the model learns to map the shared contrastive embedding space into LLM latent space without ever seeing raw audio during the optimization phase.

## Method

MATS freezes CLAP's HTSAT audio encoder and GPT-2 language encoder, and trains only an 8-layer Transformer mapper (with learnable query embeddings) plus LoRA adapters on the LLM (GPT-2 125M or LLaMA-7B). During training, CLAP's language encoder encodes text descriptions, zero-mean Gaussian noise (σ=0.015) is injected to partially bridge the audio-language modality gap, and the noisy embedding is mapped into LLM space for autoregressive next-token prediction. At inference, the Santa mechanism replaces the noisy language embedding with the actual audio embedding from CLAP's audio encoder, then blends it with a weighted sum of the top-L (L=32) noise-augmented language embeddings retrieved from the closest k-means cluster (K=100) of a pre-built memory bank, using a balancing coefficient λ=0.3 and softmax-normalized dot-product weights with temperature τ=0.1. A theoretical analysis (Theorem 3.1) formalizes why reducing the CLAP audio-language discrepancy distance directly tightens the generalization error bound, motivating the combined noise-plus-memory design over either strategy alone.

## Results

On zero-shot audio classification, MATS-LLaMA achieves 88% ACC on ESC-50, 43% ACC on DCASE, 47% ACC on TUT, and 73% ACC on BJO, setting new state-of-the-art in the zero-shot setting across FSD50K, DCASE, TUT, VGG, and BJO benchmarks. On the MMAU open-ended benchmark, MATS-LLaMA (7B) achieves 44.5% average accuracy, surpassing the larger SALMONN (13B) by 11.7% and ranking second only to Qwen2-Audio-Instruction (8.4B). On audio captioning, MATS-LLaMA attains 0.735 CIDEr on AudioCaps and 18.7 ROUGH-L on MusicCaps, outperforming the dedicated music captioning model LP-MusicCaps by 9.9% on ROUGH-L despite using text-only supervision.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MATS-LLaMA (7B, text-only) outperforms SALMONN (13B, audio-supervised) by 11.7% on MMAU average accuracy (44.5% vs. 32.8%) and by a wide margin on zero-shot classification (e.g., DCASE 43% vs. 18%, TUT 47% vs. 8%). On zero-shot audio classification, MATS-LLaMA achieves SOTA on DCASE (43% vs. 38% for GAMA), TUT (47% vs. 33% for LTU), and BJO (73% vs. 70% for LTU/GAMA), while matching or exceeding Qwen-Audio Chat (8.4B) on GTZAN (63% vs. 42%) and BJO (73% vs. 51%). On ClothoAQA, MATS-LLaMA surpasses Qwen-Audio by 8% in ACC (66% vs. 58%) but trails Audio Flamingo on B-ACC (76% vs. 87%), which uses audio supervision and a sliding-window feature extractor. On AIR-Bench Chat, MATS-LLaMA (6.43 Sound, 5.76 Music) is competitive with Qwen-Audio (6.95, 5.52) but falls short of Qwen2-Audio (6.99, 6.79), attributed to CLAP's limited fine-grained audio information extraction. On MusicCaps captioning, MATS-GPT2 surpasses the dedicated LP-MusicCaps model by 9.9% on ROUGH-L (22.9 vs. 13.0), demonstrating that text-only supervision can outperform audio-supervised specialized models on music tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

MATS extends the Pengi architecture (CLAP encoder + transformer mapper + LLM) by replacing audio-language pair supervision with text-only training, leveraging CLAP's shared contrastive embedding space as a bridge. The training-time noise injection is adapted from NoAudio-Captioning (Deshmukh et al., 2024), while the inference-time memory bank concept draws from DeCap (Li et al., 2023) in the vision domain. The key departure from prior text-only audio captioning methods (NoAudio-Captioning, PromptAAC, DRCap) is the generalization from single-task, single-audio-type captioning to multi-task (classification, captioning, QA) and multi-type (sound + music) audio understanding, combined with the Santa mechanism that integrates both noise and memory-based approaches rather than relying on either alone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: eliminating the need for large-scale audio-language pair annotation dramatically reduces the cost of training audio LLMs, making the field more accessible. The theoretical analysis (Theorem 3.1) provides a principled explanation for why the CLAP modality gap is the critical bottleneck, guiding future work on contrastive audio-language pretraining. However, the approach inherits CLAP's limitations in fine-grained audio understanding (e.g., counting events, precise temporal details), and the zero-shot performance, while competitive, still trails supervised models on some benchmarks. Open questions include whether the text-only paradigm transfers to other modalities (video, speech) and whether improved audio encoders would close the remaining gap.
