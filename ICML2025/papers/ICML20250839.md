## Problem

Flow matching and variational flow matching have driven major advances in generative modeling for images and text, but their application to tabular data with mixed continuous and discrete features remains limited. Existing variational flow matching lacks a principled, unified mechanism to handle heterogeneous column types (continuous, categorical, binary) within a single training objective, and the connection between the variational formulation and standard flow matching loss functions (MSE, cross-entropy) was not previously established.

## Input / Output

The method takes standard Gaussian noise samples in R^D as input and produces synthetic table rows where numerical features are real-valued and categorical features are one-hot vectors on the probability simplex. Each column is independently embedded to dimension 4 via a linear transformation, processed through a two-layer transformer with positional markers, then passed through a five-layer timing-conditioned MLP and a final transformer with projection back to the original feature dimensions. Inference requires only 25 ODE integration steps with a single function evaluation per step, avoiding the doubled evaluations used by baselines like TabSyn and TabDiff.

## Method

EF-VFM extends Variational Flow Matching by parameterizing the variational posterior q_t(x_1|x) with exponential family distributions chosen per column type (Gaussian for continuous, categorical for discrete, Bernoulli for binary). The key theoretical result is that the VFM log-likelihood loss reduces to a sufficient statistics matching objective: the gradient depends only on the difference between empirical and predicted sufficient statistics, yielding MSE for continuous columns and cross-entropy for categorical columns in a unified framework. Under linear conditional velocity fields, only the first moment needs to be matched, further simplifying training. The paper additionally proves that the VFM objective is equivalent to minimizing a Bregman divergence induced by the conjugate dual of the log-partition function, which explains why the conditional flow matching trick (replacing marginal with conditional trajectories) works and provides a principled generalization to arbitrary exponential families. TabbyFlow implements this with a transformer-based architecture that processes all columns in a single pass, achieving state-of-the-art results on six tabular benchmarks.

## Results

TabbyFlow achieves the best average Shape error of 1.08% across six tabular benchmarks, outperforming the next-best diffusion-based methods TabDiff (1.17%) and TabSyn (1.35%). On α-Precision (fidelity), TabbyFlow leads with an average of 98.69, narrowly ahead of TabSyn (98.67) and TabDiff (98.59). For Wasserstein distance, TabbyFlow achieves the lowest train/test values (1.7/2.1) compared to TabDiff (2.4/2.9) and TabSyn (2.2/3.0). Notably, TabbyFlow reaches these results with only 25 inference steps and a single function evaluation per step, whereas TabSyn and TabDiff use a Karras-inspired correction requiring twice as many evaluations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GAN-based CTGAN and VAE-based TVAE/GOGGLE, TabbyFlow dominates across all metrics by a wide margin (e.g., Shape error 1.08% vs. 15.99–17.91%), confirming that flow-matching-based methods substantially outperform earlier paradigms for tabular generation. Compared to the diffusion-based TabDDPM, TabbyFlow wins on Shape (1.08 vs. 16.93 average, largely due to TabDDPM's 78.75% failure on News), Trend, α-Precision, and C2ST. Against the strongest baselines TabSyn and TabDiff, TabbyFlow achieves the best average Shape (1.08 vs. 1.35 and 1.17), Trend (1.77 vs. 2.33 and 1.80), α-Precision (98.69 vs. 98.67 and 98.59), and Wasserstein distance (2.1 test vs. 3.0 and 2.9). TabbyFlow is slightly behind TabDiff on β-Recall (49.35 vs. 50.37) and marginally behind TabSyn on MLE (0.854 vs. 0.858), but matches or exceeds them on DCR and C2ST. Overall, TabbyFlow achieves state-of-the-art or near-SOTA results with a simpler single-pass transformer architecture and fewer inference steps than the VAE-diffusion hybrids it outperforms.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Variational Flow Matching (VFM) to tabular data by parameterizing the variational posterior with exponential family distributions, yielding a moment-matching objective that unifies Gaussian (continuous) and categorical (discrete) column types under a single framework. It departs from prior discrete flow matching work (Gat et al., 2024; Stark et al., 2024), which required simplex constraints and sequential sampling, by treating each column independently through its natural sufficient statistics. The key theoretical departure is showing that the VFM objective is equivalent to minimizing a Bregman divergence induced by the conjugate dual of the log-partition function, which in turn explains why the conditional flow matching trick (replacing the unknown marginal velocity with a conditional one) works: it follows from the affine-invariance property of Bregman divergences under expectation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Bregman divergence connection provides a principled, unified explanation for why different column types naturally induce different loss functions (MSE for Gaussian, cross-entropy for categorical), and suggests that choosing appropriate Bregman divergences is a natural route to extending flow matching to new data types. However, the current implementation only matches the first sufficient statistic (the mean), leaving open the question of whether matching higher-order statistics (variance, higher moments) would improve expressiveness. Empirically, TabbyFlow achieves SOTA on most fidelity metrics with a simpler architecture and fewer inference steps (25, single function evaluation) than TabSyn/TabDiff, but trails slightly on MLE, suggesting a trade-off between architectural simplicity and downstream utility. The information geometry / natural gradient direction mentioned in the paper remains unexplored and could be a meaningful next step.
