## Problem

Fine-grained open-set detection (FGOD) requires assigning unseen samples to the correct parent node in a class hierarchy, but existing approaches are fundamentally limited: local top-down detectors accumulate errors across hierarchy levels, and global detectors ignore fine-grained hierarchical dependencies. The paper provides theoretical proofs that both local (path-probability) and global detector formulations lead to sub-optimal training, as they fail to decrease probabilities of all non-ground-truth nodes simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251993/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images and a pre-defined class hierarchy (a tree of closed-set classes) as input. The hierarchy is augmented with an openset child node at every non-leaf level, so the output is an assignment to a specific node in this augmented tree—either a closed-set leaf (e.g., 'California Gull') or an openset class at a particular level (e.g., 'Openset Sparrow' vs. 'Openset Bird'). Node probabilities for all hierarchy nodes are also produced, enabling hierarchical precision and recall evaluation beyond simple top-1 accuracy.

## Method

The core contribution is a state-based node representation that encodes the entire hierarchy as a graph with subsumption edges (parent→child) and exclusion edges (mutually exclusive siblings), then defines legal global states as binary assignments to all nodes satisfying hierarchical constraints. Each legal state corresponds to exactly one leaf in the augmented hierarchy, and state probabilities are computed as products of exponentiated logits over active nodes, from which node probabilities are derived by marginalization over states. Training uses a leave-one-out strategy where one node is removed at a time and its samples become openset, with a cross-entropy loss over state probabilities. The paper proves (Theorem 3.4) that this formulation guarantees all non-ground-truth logits decrease during training, unlike path-probability (Theorem 3.1) or global detector (Corollary 3.5) formulations. The backbone is ViT-B/16 with prompt tuning, producing logits for all hierarchy nodes simultaneously in a single forward pass.

## Results

State-FGOD achieves the best openset accuracy (OA) and AUC across all three primary datasets: CUB (OA 64.47%, AUC 51.04), Tiny ImageNet (OA 24.71%, AUC 20.03), and AWA2 (OA 53.32%, AUC 48.13). The AUC improvements over the strongest baseline are +5.45 on CUB (vs. E-HND at 45.59), +2.45 on Tiny ImageNet (vs. Relabel at 17.58), and +2.82 on AWA2 (vs. LOO at 45.31). On hierarchical precision/recall (CUB), State-FGOD achieves the best harmonic mean for both closed-set (92.42) and openset (91.73) classes, outperforming E-HND (91.47/88.84) and TD (90.37/88.67).

## Baselines & Comparisons

State-FGOD consistently outperforms all nine baselines (3 local, 6 global) on every dataset and metric. On CUB, it beats the strongest global baseline E-HND by +10.04 OA and +5.45 AUC, and the best local method MSP by +40.24 OA. On Tiny ImageNet (12 hierarchy levels, where error accumulation is most severe), it surpasses Relabel (the best baseline) by +2.69 OA and +2.45 AUC, while local methods drop to single-digit OA (7.52–11.56). On AWA2, it exceeds LOO by +4.49 OA and +2.82 AUC. Global detectors broadly outperform local ones (e.g., E-HND OA 54.43 vs. MSP 24.23 on CUB), confirming that top-down local decisions accumulate errors, especially in deep hierarchies. The ablation in Table 4 further shows that adding state representation to existing global methods (DARTS, Relabel, LOO) yields +4.36 to +7.78 AUC gains, isolating the contribution of the state formulation itself.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the hierarchical novelty detection framework of Lee et al. (CVPR 2018) and the authors' own E-HND (ICML 2024) by replacing the evidential loss with a state-based node representation that simultaneously captures parent→child, ancestor→descendant, and sibling→sibling dependencies. The state formulation is adapted from Deng et al. (ECCV 2014) and Chen et al. (CVPR 2022), which used it for hierarchical classification, but here it is repurposed for fine-grained open-set detection where openset nodes are added at every non-leaf level. The key departure is a rigorous theoretical analysis (Theorems 3.1, 3.4, Corollary 3.5) proving that both the intuitive local-detector-with-path-probability approach and the standard global multi-class detector are provably sub-optimal, motivating the state-based formulation as the minimal fix that guarantees all non-ground-truth logits decrease during training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first principled theoretical justification for why hierarchical open-set detection is harder than it appears, and the state-based representation offers a clean, general-purpose mechanism that can be plugged into existing global detectors (demonstrated by +4–8 AUC gains when added to DARTS, Relabel, and LOO). A key limitation is the requirement of a pre-existing class hierarchy; when one is unavailable, the authors note that Generalized Category Discovery methods must be applied first, making the pipeline two-stage. Open questions include whether the state formulation scales to very deep hierarchies (the state matrix grows with the number of leaf nodes) and whether the approach extends to settings where the hierarchy itself is uncertain or partially known.
