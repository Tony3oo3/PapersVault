## Problem

GPVAEs replace the factorised Gaussian prior of standard VAEs with a GP prior to model latent correlations, but exact GP inference incurs O(N³) cost, making large-scale training infeasible. Existing scalable GPVAE variants either restrict to narrow kernel families (low-rank, specific Matérn forms) or rely on inducing points that require large numbers for rapidly varying data and are difficult to optimise. The paper addresses how to achieve scalable GPVAE inference with flexible kernel choices while avoiding both rigid kernel assumptions and large inducing point sets.

## Input / Output

The model consumes structured observations y_n (e.g., 28×28 MNIST frames, 14-dimensional MuJoCo sensor readings, 4-channel reservoir properties) paired with auxiliary coordinates x_n (timestamps, 2D/3D spatial positions) that parameterise the GP kernel over the latent space. Notably, the encoder processes only y_n in a standard VAE architecture, while x_n enters exclusively through the kernel in the GP prior, decoupling the observation model from the correlation structure. Outputs include latent representations, imputed values for missing entries, generated frames at unobserved timestamps, and posterior predictive distributions obtained by Monte Carlo sampling from a Gaussian latent posterior conditioned on H nearest neighbours.

## Method

The paper introduces two neighbour-driven GP prior approximations that impose local sparsity in the latent space, inspired by Nearest Neighbour GP (NNGP) methods from geostatistics. Hierarchical Prior Approximation (HPA) adds a binary selection vector w that zeros out non-neighbour interactions in the covariance matrix (D_w K_XX D_w), so that for each data point only its H nearest neighbours in X contribute to the KL term, reducing it to H×H sub-matrix operations. Sparse Precision Approximation (SPA) chain-factorises the GP prior via Vecchia's approximation, where each latent variable depends conditionally only on its H nearest neighbours among previously ordered points, yielding a sparse precision matrix. Both variants use a standard diagonal-Gaussian VAE encoder (no auxiliary input), support arbitrary kernels, and train in mini-batches with pre-computed nearest-neighbour structure via Faiss, achieving O(N_b H³) per-batch complexity. The key insight is transplanting NNGP locality principles into the latent space of GPVAEs, effectively treating each data point as its own inducing point with a local neighbourhood, rather than relying on a separate set of pseudo-locations.

## Results

On Rotated MNIST missing-frame generation (100-frame sequences, 60% dropout), GPVAE-SPA-H10 achieves the best NLL of 69.54 and RMSE of 5.41, outperforming SVGPVAE-M30 (77.79/5.57) and MGPVAE (72.22/5.50). On MuJoCo Hopper conditional generation, GPVAE-HPA-H10 attains NLL −2.335 and RMSE 0.0222, substantially beating SVGPVAE-M30 (−1.071/0.0391) and MGPVAE (−1.708/0.0398). On the large-scale SPE10 geostatistical dataset (N≈142,000), both GPVAE-HPA-H20 and GPVAE-SPA-H20 achieve NLL ≈0.735 and RMSE 0.638, surpassing VNNGP-H20 (1.052/0.683) and SVGPVAE-M100 (2.025/0.979) while training in ~3–4 s/epoch.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against standard VAE and HI-VAE, both proposed models achieve substantially lower NLL and RMSE on every task, confirming that the factorised Gaussian prior cannot capture latent correlations. Compared to SVGPVAE (inducing-point based), GPVAE-SPA-H10 improves NLL from 77.79 to 69.54 on Rotated MNIST missing frames and from −1.07 to −1.72 on MuJoCo, even when SVGPVAE uses 3× more inducing points (M=30 vs H=10). Against MGPVAE, the proposed methods win on MuJoCo (NLL −2.34 vs −1.71; RMSE 0.022 vs 0.040) and Rotated MNIST missing frames (NLL 69.54 vs 72.22), while MGPVAE edges ahead slightly on corrupted-frame imputation (NLL 0.090 vs 0.095). On the large-scale SPE10 dataset (N≈142k), GPVAE-SPA-H20 outperforms VNNGP-H20 (NLL 0.736 vs 1.052) with 15× fewer trainable parameters, and beats SGPBAE-M20 (NLL 0.736 vs 1.335) while training ~400× faster (3.4 s vs 1586 s per epoch). On the Jura dataset, GPVAE-SPA-H10 achieves the best RMSE (0.584) and NLL (0.939), surpassing SVGPVAE-M30 (0.650/0.981) and Exact GP (0.724/1.305). On the Moving Ball task, both models match the full-batch GPVAE-Casale baseline (RMSE ≈1.1–1.4) using only 10 nearest neighbours, whereas SVGPVAE requires nearly half the trajectory length to reach comparable quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper transplants two NNGP approximation strategies—the hierarchical prior (Tran et al., 2021b) and the sparse precision/Vecchia factorisation (Wu et al., 2022)—from the observation-space GP regression setting into the latent space of GPVAEs (Casale et al., 2018). While prior scalable GPVAE work (SVGPVAE, MGPVAE, SGPBAE) has relied on inducing points or restrictive kernel families (Matérn state-space, low-rank), this paper shows that local neighbourhood structure in the auxiliary input space (timestamps, spatial coordinates) is sufficient to approximate the dense latent GP prior with arbitrary kernels. The standard VAE encoder/decoder architecture (Kingma & Welling, 2014) is retained, making the contribution a targeted modification of the prior rather than a new generative framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: GPVAE inference scales from O(N³) to O(N_b H³), enabling structured latent modelling at N~10⁵ (SPE10), a regime where prior GPVAE methods were infeasible. The consistent outperformance of neighbour-based approximations over inducing-point baselines across five diverse datasets suggests that local correlation structure in the latent space is a more efficient inductive bias than global pseudo-point summaries, particularly for data with rapid local variation. Open questions include whether non-Euclidean or correlation-based distance metrics (Kang & Katzfuss, 2023) further improve performance in high-dimensional auxiliary spaces, and whether the neighbour-driven idea extends to non-Gaussian latent priors or multi-scale hierarchical structures.
