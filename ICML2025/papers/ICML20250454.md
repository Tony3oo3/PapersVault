## Problem

Existing machine unlearning methods for text-to-image diffusion models rely on fine-tuning with negative gradients or parameter modifications, which offer limited transparency into what is actually being changed and often merely mask targeted concepts rather than truly removing them. This makes them highly vulnerable to adversarial prompts that can re-trigger the supposedly unlearned content. The paper addresses the need for an interpretable, robust unlearning approach that explicitly identifies and removes the internal features responsible for a concept.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250454/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard text prompt as input and produces a generated RGB image as output, operating as a drop-in inference-time intervention on a pre-trained Stable Diffusion model. Internally, the SAE processes d-dimensional activation vectors extracted from spatial positions in cross-attention feature maps (shape h×w×d) at each denoising timestep, with the SAE trained on activations from all 50 denoising steps. The intervention adds only a 1.92% inference overhead and requires no modification to the base model's weights, functioning purely by encoding, ablating, and decoding activations between two adjacent U-Net blocks.

## Method

SAeUron trains a single BatchTopK sparse autoencoder (with TopK activation, k=32, expansion factor 16) on activations from a specific cross-attention block (up.1.1 for objects, up.1.2 for styles) across all denoising timesteps of Stable Diffusion. A score function then identifies concept-specific features by comparing normalized average activations on concept-containing versus non-concept data, selecting the top-τ_c features that strongly activate for the target while remaining weak elsewhere. During inference, each activation vector is encoded through the SAE, the selected features are ablated by scaling them with a negative multiplier γ_c (normalized by their mean activation on concept samples), and the modified representation is decoded back, preserving the reconstruction error term. The sparsity of SAE features ensures that ablating a small number of concept-specific latents has minimal collateral impact on other concepts, enabling simultaneous unlearning of multiple concepts with a single SAE and robustness to adversarial prompt attacks.

## Results

On the UnlearnCanvas benchmark, SAeUron achieves the best overall average score of 94.03%, with state-of-the-art style unlearning retention (IRA 99.10%, CRA 99.40%) and the best object unlearning CRA (95.58%). For nudity removal on I2P, SAeUron detects only 18 inappropriate images (vs. 28 for the next-best AdvUn and 743 for base SD v1.4) while maintaining competitive CLIPScore (30.89) and FID (14.37). SAeUron also requires the least memory (2.8 GB) and storage (0.2 GB) among all compared methods, and shows the smallest degradation under adversarial attack (Post-ASR 1.40% vs. 7.04–97.89% for other methods).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAeUron achieves the best overall average (94.03%) on UnlearnCanvas, outperforming SalUn (92.43%) and EDiff (82.41%). On style unlearning, SAeUron dominates retention metrics (IRA 99.10%, CRA 99.40%) while ESD leads in unlearning accuracy (UA 98.58% vs. 95.80%) but suffers much lower retention (IRA 80.97%, CRA 93.96%); UCE and FMN show similarly poor retention (CRA 47.71% and 46.60% respectively). On object unlearning, SAeUron trails ESD (UA 92.15%) and UCE (UA 94.31%) in unlearning accuracy but achieves the best CRA (95.58%) and near-best IRA (95.47% vs. SalUn's 96.35%), whereas ESD and UCE collapse on retention (CRA 44.23% and 34.67%). For nudity removal on I2P, SAeUron detects only 18 inappropriate images, beating AdvUn (28), CPE (40), MACE (111), and ESD-u (123) while maintaining FID 14.37 comparable to base SD (14.04). Under adversarial attack (UnlearnDiffAtk), SAeUron shows the smallest Post-ASR (1.40%) versus Erasediff (2.11%), SHS (7.04%), and FMN (97.89%), confirming that fine-tuning-based methods like ESD, UCE, and SPM merely mask rather than truly unlearn concepts. SAeUron also requires the lowest memory (2.8 GB) and storage (0.2 GB), compared to 17.8–31.2 GB memory and 0–4.3 GB storage for other methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper revisits the original machine unlearning formulation of Cao & Yang (2015)—blocking summation weights to remove concepts—and adapts it to text-to-image diffusion models via sparse autoencoders trained on internal activations. It departs sharply from the dominant fine-tuning paradigm (ESD, FMN, CA, SalUn, etc.) by performing unlearning entirely at inference time: a single BatchTopK SAE (building on Bricken et al., 2023; Bussmann et al., 2024; Gao et al., 2025) decomposes cross-attention activations, and concept-specific features are ablated before decoding back. Unlike prior SAE applications to diffusion models (Surkov et al., 2024; Kim et al., 2024), which train separate SAEs per timestep or on distilled models, SAeUron trains one SAE across all denoising steps of a standard Stable Diffusion model and uses a score-based selection to identify the small set of features tied to a target concept.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical contribution is interpretability: practitioners can inspect, annotate (e.g., via GPT-4o), and audit exactly which visual features are being removed, a transparency that fine-tuning-based unlearning cannot offer. The robustness finding—that inference-time feature ablation resists adversarial prompt attacks while fine-tuning methods collapse—suggests that 'masking' a concept in weights is fundamentally weaker than 'removing' its activation pathway, a distinction with direct safety implications for deployed models. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to abstract or compositional concepts (the paper shows it struggles with hate/violence), whether it scales to video diffusion, and how to protect the blocking mechanism when users have white-box access to the model.
