## Problem

Despite the widespread practical value of activation sparsity in LLMs (inference acceleration, interpretability, training efficiency), there is no comprehensive quantitative study of how to accurately measure it, how it scales with architecture and training data, or how to deliberately build sparser models. Existing sparsity metrics lack generalizability across activation functions, and the scaling laws governing sparsity dynamics remain uncharacterized.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251661/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes pre-trained LLM checkpoints (0.1B–2.4B, μP Transformer architecture with gated FFNs) and a small validation set matching the pre-training distribution. It produces a per-layer sparsity ratio (fraction of weakly-contributed neurons identified via CETT-PPL-1%) and, as a validation artifact, a fully trained 2.4B ReLU-activated model with 93.52% activation sparsity. The sparsity measurement operates on the L2 norm of individual neuron outputs within each FFN, requiring forward passes over the validation set to binary-search the layer-wise threshold.

## Method

The core metric, CETT-PPL-1%, extends the CETT algorithm (which adaptively sets per-layer thresholds so that the cumulative L2 norm error from skipping weakly-contributed neurons is uniform across layers) by binary-searching the shared CETT hyperparameter until the validation PPL rises by exactly 1% over the dense model. The paper then derives scaling laws: ReLU-activated models follow a decreasing logspace power-law A_ReLU(D) = exp(−cD^α + b) + A₀ between activation ratio and pre-training data D, while SiLU models follow an increasing vanilla power-law A_SiLU(D) = −c/D^α + A₀, both converging to a scale-insensitive limit. Key architectural findings show ReLU outperforms SiLU in sparsity with comparable task performance, a small width-depth ratio (below a bottleneck point) linearly increases sparsity, and smaller models converge faster to the limit due to fewer neuron-specialization combinations. These principles are validated by training a 2.4B ReLU model on ~800B tokens with a small width-depth ratio, achieving 93.52% sparsity and 4.1× decoding speedup via PowerInfer over dense llama.cpp inference.

## Results

The 2.4B ReLU-activated model trained on ~800B tokens achieves a limit activation sparsity of 93.52% (activation ratio 6.48%), following a decreasing logspace power-law with training data. Using PowerInfer to exploit this sparsity yields a 4.1× decoding speedup (41.79 vs. 10.23 tokens/s) over dense llama.cpp inference. The proposed CETT-PPL-1% metric reduces the measured activation ratio by 61% relative to the common zero-threshold ReLU metric on the 0.8B model, while maintaining downstream task scores within ~0.5 points of the dense setting. Across 0.1B–1.2B scales, ReLU models converge to ~93–94% sparsity versus ~60–62% for SiLU, with the limit being nearly scale-insensitive.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among the four neuron-recognition methods compared (Straightforward ReLU, Top-k, FAT-ε, and CETT), CETT consistently achieves the lowest validation PPL at any given target sparsity ratio and the highest downstream task scores, as shown in the Pareto curves of Figures 2 and 3. Specifically, CETT-PPL-1% on the 0.8B ReLU model reduces the activation ratio by 61% relative to the zero-threshold Straightforward ReLU metric while keeping C.R. and R.C. scores within 1 point of Dense. ReLU activation dominates SiLU on sparsity: at the 1.2B scale, ReLU reaches a limit activation ratio of ~7.8% (92.2% sparsity) versus ~38.2% (61.8% sparsity) for SiLU, yet both achieve comparable task scores (e.g., 60.0 vs. 59.6 avg. C.R. at 1.2B Dense). For practical acceleration, PowerInfer exploiting the 93.52% sparsity of the 2.4B model delivers 41.79 tokens/s versus 10.23 tokens/s for dense llama.cpp, a 4.1× speedup. In Appendix B, a 0.1B MoE model is shown to have a significantly worse PPL–activation Pareto frontier than a vanilla decoder-only Transformer of the same scale, supporting the paper's focus on intrinsic activation sparsity over architectural sparsity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the CETT layer-wise threshold search algorithm (Zhang et al., 2024a) by introducing a PPL-increase-tolerance constraint (CETT-PPL-1%) to create a performance-friendly sparsity metric, and positions itself as the first comprehensive quantitative study of activation sparsity's measurement, architectural dependencies, and scaling behavior in LLMs. It builds on the activation sparsity phenomenon documented in prior work (Li et al., 2022; Zhang et al., 2022; Song et al., 2025) and the inference-acceleration literature (PowerInfer, Deja Vu), but departs from MoE-based sparsity by focusing exclusively on intrinsic sparsity in vanilla decoder-only Transformers. The neuron-specialization framing connects to interpretability work (Zhang et al., 2023; Gao et al., 2024), and the scaling-law analysis parallels prior work on parameter-sparse and fine-grained MoE scaling (Frantar et al., 2023; Krajewski et al., 2024).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides actionable design guidance for LLM developers: use ReLU activation, a small width-depth ratio, and more training data to achieve high intrinsic sparsity with negligible performance loss, demonstrated by a 4.1× decoding speedup on a 2.4B model. The scale insensitivity of limit sparsity (nearly identical across 0.1B–2.4B) suggests sparsity-based acceleration strategies transfer across model sizes, which is practically valuable. Open questions include whether these scaling laws hold at 7B+ scales, how data mixing policies affect sparsity dynamics, and whether the width-depth ratio findings account for FLOPS trade-offs (acknowledged as a limitation). The sensitivity of the CETT-PPL-p% metric to validation data distribution also limits generalizability across deployment domains.
