## Problem

Multimodal understanding and generation tasks—captioning, text-to-image synthesis, style transfer—typically require specialized models trained on task-specific paired data. Prior training-free approaches (e.g., ZeroCap, MeaCap) are limited to a single modality and task, relying on gradient-based token-level optimization that cannot generalize to generation or editing. This paper asks whether a single, task-agnostic, training-free framework can endow a text-only LLM with emergent multimodal capabilities across understanding and generation tasks.

## Input / Output

The LLM GENERATOR never directly observes the multimodal test sample; it only receives a textual task description plus a ranked list of previously generated candidates with their scalar scores from the SCORER. For captioning tasks, optimization is bootstrapped with a large initial candidate set (~30K for image/video from ImageNet class labels, ~50K for audio from AudioSet labels) to ensure diversity. For generation and editing tasks, no initial set is needed—the LLM rewrites or expands the user's prompt, and the SCORER (e.g., PickScore, Gram matrix distance) evaluates the resulting generated image against the target. Convergence is typically reached in 10–20 iterations, with the top-50 candidates retained per step.

## Method

MILS (Multimodal Iterative LLM Solver) is a gradient-free, test-time optimization loop with two swappable modules: a GENERATOR (an LLM, e.g., Llama 3.1 8B) that proposes candidate text outputs, and a SCORER (an off-the-shelf multimodal model such as SigLIP, ViCLIP, ImageBind, PickScore, or VGG19 Gram-matrix distance) that assigns a scalar similarity score to each candidate relative to the test sample. Each iteration, the SCORER returns the top-K scored candidates as structured feedback, and the GENERATOR uses its multi-step reasoning to produce a new, improved candidate set—combining, rephrasing, or introducing novel elements from the scored list. By simply swapping the GENERATOR (e.g., chaining an LLM to a T2I model or an image editor) and the SCORER, the identical loop handles image/video/audio captioning, prompt rewriting for T2I, style transfer, and cross-modal arithmetic (inverting embeddings into text, combining, and regenerating). The approach requires no task-specific training data, no gradient computation through the multimodal model, and no fine-tuning of the LLM.

## Results

On MSCOCO zero-shot image captioning, MILS achieves BLEU4 of 8.0, CIDEr of 33.3, METEOR of 15.0, and SPICE of 9.6, matching or exceeding all prior zero-shot baselines on BLEU4 and remaining competitive on the semantics-aware METEOR and SPICE metrics. On MSR-VTT video captioning, MILS (CIDEr 2.3, METEOR 14.4) outperforms the HowTo100M-trained baseline on both metrics and beats the VideoCC3M-trained baseline on METEOR. On Clotho audio captioning, MILS outperforms ZerAuCap on METEOR (12.4 vs 9.4) and SPICE (7.6 vs 5.3). For text-to-image generation on DrawBench, human annotators consistently prefer MILS-enhanced generations over base model outputs on both quality and faithfulness axes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MILS clearly outperforms ZeroCap, ConZIC, and CLIPRe on all four captioning metrics, with the largest margin over ZeroCap (e.g., BLEU4 8.0 vs 2.6, SPICE 9.6 vs 5.5). Against the strongest baseline MeaCap, MILS wins on BLEU4 (8.0 vs 7.1) but trails on CIDEr (33.3 vs 42.5), METEOR (15.0 vs 16.6), and SPICE (9.6 vs 11.8); however, the paper argues METEOR and SPICE are more meaningful for emergent zero-shot methods since they capture semantic similarity rather than exact word overlap. Against the authors' own re-run of MeaCap (MeaCap*), MILS wins on every metric. For video captioning, MILS beats the HowTo100M-trained model on both CIDEr and METEOR, and beats the VideoCC3M-trained model on METEOR (14.4 vs 11.3) despite using no training data. For audio captioning, MILS surpasses ZerAuCap on METEOR (12.4 vs 9.4) and SPICE (7.6 vs 5.3) while being slightly lower on BLEU4 (2.7 vs 2.9) and ROUGEL (23.1 vs 25.4).

## Relation to Prior Work

MILS repurposes the test-time reasoning paradigm of LLMs (Chain-of-Thought, O1-style iterative refinement) for multimodal tasks by framing them as a gradient-free optimization loop: an LLM GENERATOR proposes candidate outputs, and an off-the-shelf multimodal embedding model (CLIP, SigLIP, ImageBind, ViCLIP) acts as a SCORER whose feedback is fed back to the LLM. This departs from prior zero-shot captioning methods (ZeroCap, MeaCap, ConZIC) that compute gradients through CLIP to guide next-token prediction, and from LLM-as-optimizer work (Yang et al. 2023, Liu et al. 2024) that was not evaluated on visual tasks or shown to yield emergent zero-shot capabilities. The key conceptual move is that the multimodal model is used purely as a black-box scorer rather than a differentiable generator, enabling seamless generalization across modalities and tasks by simply swapping the GENERATOR-SCORER pair.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that no task-specific training data or fine-tuning is needed to give a text-only LLM competitive multimodal understanding (captioning images, videos, audio) and generation (T2I, style transfer) capabilities, which could reduce the data curation and training pipeline burden for new multimodal applications. However, performance is bounded by the GENERATOR's ability to produce diverse candidates and the SCORER's discriminative power, and the iterative optimization is computationally expensive (10 steps × 50 candidates per sample). Open questions include whether the approach extends to 3D/spatial tasks, whether convergence can be accelerated with longer-context or more efficient LLMs, and whether the gradient-free formulation can match gradient-based methods on fine-grained tasks where the scoring signal is noisy.
