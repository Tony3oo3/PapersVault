## Problem

Training Latent SDEs for time series and sequence modeling relies on adjoint sensitivity methods that require costly numerical integration of SDEs and backpropagation through their solutions, making them computationally expensive, difficult to parallelize, and numerically unstable. This simulation-based bottleneck has prevented Latent SDEs from scaling to high-dimensional problems like video, despite their expressiveness for stochastic dynamics.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250113/cropped_page5_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes sequences of observations (irregularly or regularly sampled time series, 50-dimensional motion capture data, or 32×32 image sequences) and produces a trained Latent SDE model capable of generating new trajectories, estimating latent states at any time step, and forecasting future observations. A key practical detail is that during training, each iteration requires only a single evaluation of the drift and diffusion terms (O(1) complexity), whereas adjoint sensitivity requires L sequential simulation steps. At inference, the reparameterization function F_φ provides direct access to posterior marginals q_φ(z_t|X) without integrating the conditional SDE, enabling simulation-free conditional sampling and interpolation.

## Method

SDE Matching establishes that conventional diffusion models are a special case of Latent SDEs with a single observation, then generalizes the simulation-free training property of diffusion models to the full Latent SDE setting. The core mechanism is parameterizing the posterior process through a reparameterization function F_φ(ε, t, X) that implicitly defines marginal distributions q_φ(z_t|X) at every time step, enabling direct Monte Carlo sampling of latent states without numerical integration. The corresponding conditional SDE is then derived analytically via the ODE-to-SDE correspondence (Song et al., 2021b), preserving the prior process diffusion term g_θ to keep the variational bound finite. Training optimizes the same variational bound as adjoint sensitivity (prior KL + diffusion loss + reconstruction loss) but estimates each term by sampling a single time step and a single latent state, achieving O(1) memory and time per iteration. The prior process (drift h_θ, diffusion g_θ, observation model) remains fully flexible and is trained jointly with the posterior reparameterization parameters.

## Results

On the CMU Motion Capture dataset (50-dim, 300 observations), SDE Matching achieves a test MSE of 4.50±0.32, closely matching the adjoint-sensitivity Latent SDE (4.03±0.2) while requiring only a single SDE evaluation per iteration instead of ~100 simulation steps. On the 3D stochastic Lorenz attractor, SDE Matching delivers a combined ~50× speedup (5× per-iteration × 10× faster convergence) and maintains stable gradient norms (log₁₀ ≈ 4.92) across time horizons T∈{1,2,5,10}, whereas adjoint sensitivity gradients grow exponentially (log₁₀ from 6.26 to 8.82). On the high-dimensional moving-pendulum video task (32×32 frames), SDE Matching learns the dynamics in ~20 minutes on a single GPU, while the adjoint method requires ~30 hours and still fails to capture the system's dynamics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SDE Matching (4.50±0.32) is within 0.47 MSE of the adjoint-sensitivity Latent SDE (4.03±0.2) on motion capture, a small gap given the ~50× computational speedup and the fact that the adjoint method becomes infeasible at higher dimensions (30 h vs 20 min on the video task). Against the other simulation-free method ARCTA (7.62±0.93), SDE Matching improves MSE by over 3 points, which the authors attribute to ARCTA's state-independent volatility and its reliance on ~100 latent samples per batch versus SDE Matching's single sample. All ODE-based baselines (Neural ODE 22.49, Latent ODE 5.98, ODE²VAE-KL 8.09) are substantially outperformed, confirming the advantage of stochastic dynamics for this task. On the stochastic Lotka-Volterra system, SDE Matching with state-dependent volatility accurately captures trajectory structure while the state-independent variant (the only option in ARCTA) fails, highlighting a key expressiveness advantage. The paper also shows that SDE Matching's gradient norms remain stable across time horizons up to T=10, whereas adjoint sensitivity gradients grow exponentially, making SDE Matching more robust for long-sequence modeling.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate lines of work: Latent SDEs trained via adjoint sensitivity (Li et al., 2020) and simulation-free score/flow matching for diffusion models (Song et al., 2021b; Lipman et al., 2023). The key conceptual move is showing that conventional diffusion models are a special case of Latent SDEs with a single observation, then generalizing the simulation-free training principle to arbitrary Latent SDEs by parameterizing posterior marginals through an invertible reparameterization function F_φ. It subsumes the prior simulation-free approach ARCTA (Course & Nair, 2023), which is restricted to Gaussian marginals and state-independent diffusion, as a special case while supporting state-dependent volatility and more flexible posterior distributions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SDE Matching's O(1) memory and time complexity per iteration (versus O(L) for adjoint sensitivity) makes Latent SDEs viable for high-dimensional sequential data like video and audio, where numerical integration was previously infeasible—the 20-minute vs. 30-hour video experiment illustrates this gap concretely. The gradient stability across time horizons (constant vs. exponential growth) addresses a practical pain point for long-sequence modeling. Key limitations remain: the reparameterization function F_φ must be invertible and provide efficient score computation, constraining posterior flexibility, and the diffusion term g_θ must have structure (diagonal, scalar-times-matrix, or state-independent) for tractable drift evaluation. Open directions include more expressive F_φ classes, simulation-free training of Latent ODEs, and connections to reinforcement learning.
