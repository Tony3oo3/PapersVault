## Problem

Current causal fairness frameworks require users to supply the correct causal model (causal graph) to identify and mitigate algorithmic bias, a prerequisite that is often infeasible in complex real-world settings where causal structures are unknown or difficult to identify. An incorrectly assumed causal graph can invalidate fairness metrics and produce 'fairwashing,' giving a false sense of security. This paper asks whether a model can learn to remove the causal effect of a protected attribute from predictions using only observational data, without any user-specified causal structure.

## Input / Output

At inference, FairPFN takes a single observational tabular dataset (features, a binary protected attribute, and a binary outcome) and an identifier specifying which column is the protected attribute; no causal graph, counterfactual data, or noise terms are required. The dataset is split into a training context and a validation/inference set, which are both passed through the transformer in a single forward pass (in-context learning), producing a causally fair binary prediction for each validation sample. The model is a frozen foundation model—no per-dataset fine-tuning is needed—and operates on small-to-medium tabular problems consistent with the PFN paradigm.

## Method

FairPFN is a transformer pre-trained on approximately 1.5 million synthetic datasets generated from sparse MLPs that encode Structural Causal Models with a binary exogenous protected attribute. During pre-training, each synthetic dataset is generated twice: once with the protected attribute's outgoing edges intact (biased data) and once with those edges dropped (fair data); the transformer receives the biased training split as in-context examples and is trained via binary cross-entropy to predict the fair validation outcomes, thereby learning the mapping from biased features to causally fair targets. At inference, the transformer approximates a modified Posterior Predictive Distribution by integrating over plausible SCM hypotheses for the observed data, effectively averaging over causal explanations without committing to a single graph. This Bayesian-style integration reduces the risk of fairwashing from an incorrect causal model and provides regularization benefits analogous to those observed in TabPFN. The key architectural difference from TabPFN is the synthetic data prior, which explicitly models protected attributes as binary exogenous causes and trains the model to suppress their causal influence rather than exploit it for prediction.

## Results

On two real-world datasets (Law School Admissions and Adult Census Income), FairPFN achieves a prediction Average Treatment Effect (ATE) below 0.01 while maintaining predictive accuracy comparable to the Unfair baseline. On 600 synthetic causal case studies, FairPFN attains Pareto-optimal fairness-accuracy performance in 40% of datasets using only observational data, and achieves an average rank of 1.88 out of 4 on causal effect removal. For counterfactual fairness, FairPFN achieves maximum absolute error divergences of <0.05 (Law School) and <0.2 (Adult Census Income), significantly lower than all baselines without causal access.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FairPFN is compared against CFP (Kusner et al., 2017), which requires access to fair unobservables and noise terms from the true causal model, and EGR (Agarwal et al., 2018), which optimizes group fairness via exponentiated gradient reduction. On real-world data, FairPFN achieves a prediction ATE of 0.02±0.04, substantially outperforming CFP (0.07±0.05) and EGR (0.03±0.40) while using only observational data. On synthetic benchmarks, FairPFN achieves an average rank of 1.88/4 on causal effect removal, with CFP (which has causal access) performing comparably but requiring knowledge of the true SCM. EGR performs similarly to Random in counterfactual divergence on real-world data, confirming that group-fairness optimization does not guarantee individual-level counterfactual fairness. FairPFN's predictions are closest to the Cntf. Avg. oracle target (mean error 0.02±0.04 on real-world, 0.00±0.06 on synthetic), outperforming both CFP and EGR in alignment with this counterfactual-averaging benchmark.

## Relation to Prior Work

FairPFN applies the Prior-data Fitted Network (PFN) paradigm—established by Müller et al. (ICLR 2022) and instantiated in TabPFN (Hollmann et al., ICLR 2023)—to the causal fairness problem, repurposing the synthetic-data pre-training recipe to learn removal of protected-attribute causal effects. It departs from the dominant causal fairness frameworks of Kusner et al. (2017) and Plecko & Bareinboim (2024), which require the user to supply a full or partial causal graph, by instead encoding the causal prior entirely in the pre-training distribution. The paper follows the exogenous-protected-attribute modeling of Kusner et al. (2017) rather than the more general Standard Fairness Model, and positions itself as a complement to (not replacement for) the CFA Fairness Cookbook metrics used in evaluation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical contribution is eliminating the need for a user-specified causal graph, which is a major barrier to deploying causal fairness in real settings where causal structure is unknown or contested. However, the current scope is narrow: a single binary exogenous protected attribute, binary classification, and the PFN constraint to small datasets (≤~10k samples) limit immediate applicability to large-scale production systems. Open questions include extending to multiple and non-binary protected attributes, handling endogenous or confounded protected attributes (intersectionality), and whether the approach generalizes to regression or time-series settings. The paper also raises the important question of whether PFNs can serve as a general-purpose tool for causal ML tasks beyond fairness.
