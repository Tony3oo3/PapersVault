## Problem

Most convergence guarantees for shuffling-type gradient methods require Lipschitz smoothness, an assumption empirically violated in important models such as deep language models, phase retrieval, and distributionally robust optimization. This paper provides concrete counterexamples demonstrating the prevalence of non-Lipschitz objectives and then establishes convergence rates for shuffling-type gradient methods under the weaker ℓ-smoothness condition, where the Hessian norm is bounded by a subquadratic function of the gradient norm.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes a finite-sum objective F(w) = (1/n)Σᵢ f(w;i) whose components are ℓ-smooth (with a shared subquadratic ℓ of degree p < 2), an initial point w₀, and a non-increasing stepsize sequence {ηₜ}. It outputs a sequence of epoch-end iterates {w̃ₜ} with the guarantee that the average squared gradient norm over T epochs is ≤ ε² (nonconvex) or the function gap F(w)−F* ≤ ε (convex cases), requiring O(n^{(p+1)/2} ε⁻³) total gradient evaluations for random reshuffling in the nonconvex setting. Experiments apply the method to synthetic ℓ-smooth functions, phase retrieval (m=3000, d=100), DRO on 2000 life-expectancy samples with 34 features, and CIFAR-10 classification via ResNet-18.

## Method

The core technical contribution is a two-part proof under ℓ-smoothness: (1) an induction argument showing that bounded gradient norms at epoch start propagate to bounded norms throughout the epoch, thereby guaranteeing local Lipschitz smoothness; and (2) a stopping-time analysis bounding the cumulative gradient norm summation, where the critical difficulty is conditioning on the event that Lipschitz smoothness holds (t < τ) while simultaneously estimating shuffling-specific variance terms that require unconditional expectations. This tension is resolved by constructing a supermartingale over the reshuffling variance and applying the optional stopping theorem to decouple the two conditioning requirements. The stepsize is set to η = O((n^{1−p}/T)^{1/3}) for random reshuffling, yielding O(n^{(p+1)/2} ε⁻³) gradient evaluations in the nonconvex case and matching the Lipschitz-smooth rate of Nguyen et al. (2021) when p = 0. The framework covers both random reshuffling and arbitrary shuffling schemes across nonconvex, strongly convex, and non-strongly convex settings, with the strongly convex and non-strongly convex arbitrary-scheme results additionally removing the bounded-variance assumption.

## Results

The paper establishes convergence rates for shuffling-type gradient methods under ℓ-smoothness (a generalization of Lipschitz smoothness). For the nonconvex case with random reshuffling, the total complexity is O(n^((p+1)/2) ε^(-3)) gradient evaluations, and O(n^(p/2+1) ε^(-3)) for arbitrary shuffling, where p ∈ [0,2) is the degree of the ℓ function. In the strongly convex case, the rate improves to Õ(n^((p+1)/2) ε^(-1/2)) for random reshuffling, and Õ(n ε^(-1/2)) for arbitrary shuffling without bounded variance. Empirically, Algorithm 1 with all three shuffling schemes (fixed, shuffle-once, uniform) converges faster than SGD on synthetic convex/strongly-convex problems, phase retrieval, DRO, and CIFAR-10 (ResNet-18, batch size 200, step size 10⁻³).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares Algorithm 1 (shuffling-type gradient) against standard SGD across five experimental settings. On synthetic convex (x_i⁴ + kx_i) and strongly convex (exp-based) objectives in ℝ⁵⁰, all three shuffling schemes of Algorithm 1 converge faster than SGD. On phase retrieval (m=3000, d=100, Gaussian data), Algorithm 1 with shuffle-once and uniform-shuffling outperforms SGD, while fixed-shuffling shows comparable performance. On DRO (life expectancy regression, 2000 samples, 34 features), Algorithm 1 with fixed shuffling converges faster than SGD. On CIFAR-10 with ResNet-18, Algorithm 1 with fixed-shuffling outperforms SGD on both training loss and test accuracy, while shuffle-once and uniform-shuffling outperform SGD on training data but show less consistent gains on test data. The theoretical rates match those of Nguyen et al. (2021) in the degenerate Lipschitz case (p=0), confirming that the ℓ-smoothness framework does not sacrifice convergence speed when the stronger assumption holds.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the convergence analysis of shuffling-type gradient methods—previously established under Lipschitz smoothness by Nguyen et al. (2021), Gurbüzbalaban et al. (2021), and Safran and Shamir (2020)—to the more general ℓ-smoothness framework introduced by Li et al. (2023a). It leverages the variance-of-reshuffling lemma from Mishchenko et al. (2020) and the arbitrary-shuffling bounds from Nguyen et al. (2021) as building blocks, while introducing a novel stopping-time argument to handle the circular dependency between gradient norm bounds and local Lipschitz smoothness. The paper positions itself as filling a gap: prior shuffling analyses all require Lipschitz smoothness, which fails for important models like LSTMs, Transformers, DRO, and phase retrieval.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result provides the first convergence guarantees for shuffling-type gradient methods under ℓ-smoothness, matching the best-known Lipschitz-smooth rates in the degenerate case (p=0) and thus showing the relaxation is essentially free. This broadens the theoretical justification for a technique that is standard practice in deep learning but previously lacked guarantees in many realistic settings. Open questions include whether the polynomial dependency on δ can be reduced to logarithmic, whether single-shuffling rates under ℓ-smoothness can match the O(1/(nT²)) result of Ahn et al. (2020), and how these methods interact with variance reduction. The constant G′ in the no-variance results (Theorems 4.9, 4.12) can be large and hard to verify, limiting practical tightness.
