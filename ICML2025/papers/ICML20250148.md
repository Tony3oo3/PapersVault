## Problem

When finetuning a pretrained language model on a small target-domain dataset, the model overfits and forgets generic pretraining knowledge, but it remains unclear how much pretraining data to inject into the finetuning mixture to mitigate both effects, and how this interacts with model scale and target data availability. Prior work on forgetting during finetuning (e.g., Kalajdzievski, 2024) did not study pretraining data injection, and no scaling law existed to predict the post-finetuning pretraining loss as a function of model size, finetuning data volume, and injection fraction.

## Input / Output

The scaling laws take three scalar inputs per configuration: model parameter count N (41M–1.27B), number of unique finetuning tokens D_ft (300K–30M), and pretraining data injection fraction p ∈ {0%, 0.1%, 0.5%, 1%, 5%}. They produce two predicted losses: the finetuning validation loss (target-domain performance) and the pretraining loss (forgetting measure). Notably, the forgetting law places D_ft in the numerator—more finetuning data increases forgetting because it extends training toward the bottom of the U-curve—while the finetuning loss is empirically nearly independent of p, so it is modeled without that variable.

## Method

The paper trains GPT-2-style transformers at five scales on RedPajama V2 (100 tokens/parameter), then finetunes on 12 domains from The Pile across five data sizes and five injection fractions (1,500 runs total), reporting losses at the bottom of the validation U-curve. For forgetting, they propose a modified multiplicative law L_pt = L_pt^0 + A·D_ft^β / ((1+Bp)N)^α, where the (1+Bp) factor models a fraction p of effective capacity being reallocated to the pretraining task at B× efficiency (B ≫ 1 because pretrained features are already aligned). For finetuning loss, they adopt the multiplicative form L_ft = A/(N^α · D_ft^β) + E from Zhang et al. (2024), which outperforms the additive alternative. Coefficients are fitted per domain via Huber loss in log-space with L-BFGS-B, yielding bootstrapped MREs of 0.40% (forgetting) and 0.89% (finetuning) across domains. A key practical finding is that injecting as little as p = 1% of pretraining data into the finetuning mixture prevents measurable forgetting at negligible cost to target-domain performance.

## Results

The paper derives two scaling laws: a multiplicative finetuning loss law (L_ft = A/N^α / D_ft^β + E) with a bootstrapped MRE of 0.89% across 12 domains, and a novel forgetting law (L_pt = L_pt^0 + A·D_ft^β / ((1+Bp)N)^α) with a bootstrapped MRE of 0.40%. A key practical finding is that injecting as little as p=1% pretraining data into the finetuning mixture prevents measurable forgetting on the pretraining set, while having negligible impact on finetuning validation loss. In extrapolation experiments, coefficients fitted on models up to Medium (334M) and 3,000K finetuning tokens predict Large and XL models at 9,000K–30,000K tokens with MRE below 2.01% (finetuning) and 0.83% (forgetting), enabling substantial compute savings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is primarily an empirical scaling-law study rather than a method comparison, so there are no external baseline methods to benchmark against. Internally, the authors validate their proposed multiplicative forgetting law against additive alternatives: the multiplicative form achieves 0.40% MRE versus >0.82% for additive laws with the same degrees of freedom, and omitting the base pretraining loss L_pt^0 inflates MRE to 1.05%. For the finetuning loss, the multiplicative law (0.89% MRE) outperforms the additive form (1.36% MRE), consistent with Zhang et al. (2024). On the practical mitigation side, pretraining data injection at p=1% clearly outperforms Anchored AdamW (a parameter-space regularization tying θ_t to θ_0) on both pretraining loss and finetuning loss on the Github domain, suggesting data diversity is more effective than simple weight-space anchoring. The forgetting coefficient B varies dramatically across domains (from 2 for Openwebtext 2 to 9,847 for Dm mathematics), quantifying how much pretraining data injection helps: domains far from the pretraining distribution benefit most.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the multiplicative finetuning scaling laws of Zhang et al. (2024) by deriving a companion scaling law for forgetting that explicitly incorporates the fraction of pretraining data injected into the finetuning mixture. It departs from Kalajdzievski (2024), who characterized forgetting with a single Llama2 model and measured cross-domain loss, by using five model scales and measuring forgetting directly via pretraining loss across 12 domains. The pretraining data injection mechanism builds on Ibrahim et al. (2024) and Liu et al. (2022), but is applied to the data-scarce finetuning regime (multiple epochs, U-curve) rather than the infinite-data-stream continual pretraining setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical takeaway—that injecting just 1% pretraining data into the finetuning mixture prevents forgetting across all tested domains and scales—gives practitioners a simple, low-cost regularization strategy. The forgetting scaling law enables extrapolation from small models (334M, 3000K tokens) to large ones (1.27B, 30000K tokens) with under 1% error, potentially saving substantial compute in model selection. Open questions include whether the law holds for parameter-efficient finetuning (LoRA), for models beyond 1.3B parameters, and for architectures other than GPT-2-style transformers; the paper explicitly defers PEFT to future work.
