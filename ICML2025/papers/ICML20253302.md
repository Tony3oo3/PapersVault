## Problem

Deep neural network phenomena such as emergence, neural collapse, grokking, and lazy/rich regime transitions are typically attributed to nonlinear activations, dataset complexity, or architectural details, yet no unified dynamical principle explains them. This position paper argues that the layerwise (product) structure of parameters — even in the absence of nonlinearities — is the primary driver of these phenomena, and that the community has underexploited exactly solvable layerwise linear models as a theoretical foundation before tackling the full nonlinear case.

## Input / Output

The analytical framework takes d-dimensional zero-mean Gaussian input features, a realizable linear target function with per-feature scales S_i, and specific initialization conditions (small equal initialization, λ-balanced initialization, or NTK-scale initialization) as its mathematical inputs. It produces closed-form gradient-flow solutions — sigmoidal saturation curves for individual modes, rank-c feature collapse predictions, and a weight-to-target ratio (Σ_0/S) that classifies whether dynamics will be lazy or rich. These analytical predictions are then validated against empirical training curves of 2-layer ReLU MLPs, ResNets on CIFAR-10, and transformers on modular arithmetic, showing that the linear-model solutions qualitatively and quantitatively match nonlinear DNN behavior.

## Method

The paper introduces the dynamical feedback principle: in a layerwise linear model, the magnitude of one layer's parameters multiplicatively scales the gradient of the other layer, creating a rich-get-richer amplification absent in single-layer linear models. This principle yields sigmoidal (delayed) saturation of each mode rather than exponential saturation, which the paper shows explains stage-like training, emergence (via sequential skill acquisition under power-law skill frequencies), and scaling laws. For linear neural networks, the same feedback produces greedy low-rank dynamics that explain neural collapse as a rank-c feature collapse enforced by the conserved quantity W_1^T W_1 − W_2 W_2^T ≈ 0. The paper further formalizes layer imbalance via the λ-balanced condition (W_2 W_2^T − W_1^T W_1 = λI) to show how initialization controls the lazy/rich transition, and introduces the weight-to-target ratio Σ_0/S as a unified parameter that subsumes both NTK initialization and target downscaling as mechanisms for entering the lazy regime, thereby explaining grokking as a transition from lazy overfitting to rich generalization.

## Results

This is a position paper without a single main results table; its key contributions are theoretical. The central quantitative result is the exact sigmoidal saturation solution (Eq. 9) for diagonal linear neural networks, which contrasts with the immediate exponential saturation (Eq. 10) of linear models, explaining stage-like training and emergence. In Section 7, a toy 2-layer linear network (d=c=1, p-wide) is solved exactly, showing that the weight-to-target ratio Σ₀/S governs the lazy-to-rich transition: small Σ₀/S yields greedy (rich) dynamics while large Σ₀/S (via NTK initialization or target downscaling) yields lazy dynamics. Empirically, a 4-layer tanh MLP trained on 1000 MNIST samples with large weight initialization exhibits grokking (delayed generalization), and all four techniques that reduce Σ₀/S (weight downscaling, target upscaling, input downscaling, output downscaling) eliminate the generalization delay (Figure 8). The theoretical layerwise model (Eq. 11) also quantitatively matches empirical emergence curves of a 2-layer ReLU network on the multitask sparse parity problem across time, data, and parameter scaling (Figure 4).

## Baselines & Comparisons

As a position paper, this work does not benchmark against competing methods in the traditional sense; the `compares_to` list is empty. The primary contrast is between linear models (Eq. 7, exponential saturation) and layerwise linear models (Eq. 6, sigmoidal saturation), demonstrating that the product-of-parameters structure alone—without any non-linearity—produces the amplifying feedback responsible for emergence, neural collapse, and greedy low-rank dynamics. The theoretical predictions are validated against empirical DNNs: the sigmoidal model (Eq. 11) reproduces the emergent skill-learning curves of a 2-layer ReLU network on multitask sparse parity (Figure 4), and the weight-to-target ratio framework (Section 7) predicts and empirically confirms that reducing Σ₀/S eliminates grokking in a 4-layer tanh MLP on MNIST (Figure 8). The λ-balanced condition (Eq. 14) is compared against the NTK/target-downscaling limits (Figure 6), showing two distinct routes to lazy dynamics: layer imbalance (Section 6) and large weight-to-target ratio (Section 7). No method is 'beaten' or 'matched' in a competitive sense; rather, the paper unifies previously separate explanations (emergence, NC, lazy/rich, grokking) under a single dynamical feedback principle.

## Relation to Prior Work

This position paper unifies four previously separate lines of analysis on layerwise linear models—Saxe et al.'s (2014) exact solutions for deep linear networks, Nam et al.'s (2024) sigmoidal dynamics for emergence and scaling laws, Mixon et al.'s (2020) unconstrained feature model for neural collapse, and Dominé et al. (2025) / Kunin et al. (2024) λ-balanced dynamics for lazy/rich regimes and grokking—under a single 'dynamical feedback principle.' Rather than introducing new experiments, the paper's contribution is conceptual: it argues that the product-of-parameters structure (not nonlinearity) is the common mechanistic driver across all four phenomena, and it formalizes this through a toy model (Section 7) that unifies NTK initialization and target downscaling under a weight-to-target ratio framework. The paper departs from the broader DNN theory literature by explicitly prioritizing solvability and layerwise structure over architectural fidelity, positioning linear models as the 'spherical cow' of neural network dynamics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unification is genuinely useful: it gives theorists a single diagnostic (does the phenomenon arise from amplifying feedback or its absence?) to triage whether a new DNN phenomenon should be attacked with layerwise linear models first, potentially saving significant analytical effort. However, the paper is a position statement with no new empirical results, and its scope is limited to two-layer models and four phenomena; the authors themselves acknowledge that some phenomena may require nonlinearity and that the limits of this approach remain unexplored. Key open questions include whether the feedback principle extends to deeper networks with skip connections, how batch normalization interacts with the rich regime, and whether perturbation theory can systematically incorporate ReLU nonlinearity into the solvable linear framework.
