## Problem

Expectation-based constrained RL methods guarantee only that the mean cumulative cost stays below a threshold, leaving the tail of the cost distribution uncontrolled. This means rare but catastrophic extreme events (e.g., black swan collisions) can still cause frequent constraint violations even when the expected cost is satisfied. Existing probabilistic alternatives (Gaussian CVaR in WCSAC, quantile regression in QCPO) either mis-model the tail decay rate or ignore the high variance and scarcity of extreme samples during training.

## Input / Output

The agent receives continuous state observations (12-dim for Point, 24-dim for Car in Safety Gym; robot proprioceptive states in Safety MuJoCo) and a scalar constraint threshold d (set to 25 in experiments). It outputs a stochastic action distribution parameterized by a 2-layer, 64-unit tanh network. Beyond the policy, the method internally maintains two fitted GPDs (one for cost excesses, one for reward excesses) with shape ξ and scale σ, and computes per-sample prioritization scores p = ω_r + ω_c that modulate replay frequency from the experience buffer.

## Method

EVO replaces the standard expected-cost constraint J_C(π) ≤ d with an extreme quantile constraint q_μ + q^H_{ν/(1-μ)} ≤ d, where q_μ is set to the expected cumulative cost (the safety boundary) and q^H_{ν/(1-μ)} is a quantile of the Generalized Pareto Distribution fitted to cost excesses above q_μ (the risk boundary). This decomposes the cost CDF into a body and a GPD-modeled tail, so the constraint explicitly accounts for tail extremes. During experience replay, an extreme prioritization mechanism assigns each sample a score equal to the sum of its quantile levels under the reward and cost GPDs, upweighting rare high-impact samples. To combat the high variance of GPD fitting on sparse extreme sets, off-policy importance resampling reweights stored samples by the ratio π(a|s)/π_0(a|s), augmenting the effective extreme sample size. The resulting constrained objective is solved within a CPO-style trust region using first-order reward/cost expansions and a second-order KL approximation, yielding a closed-form policy update via conjugate gradient when the constraint is violated.

## Results

EVO achieves the best normalized performance ratio (Average Return / Constraint Violation Rate) across 7 of 8 environments, ranging from 0.213 (SafetySwimmerVelocity) to 0.275 (SafetyPointGoal1). EVO maintains zero constraint violations throughout training in all Safety MuJoCo tasks and rapidly converges to the feasible domain in Safety Gymnasium tasks. The GPD fitting overhead is minimal, adding only ~8 seconds to a ~12-hour training run. Ablation studies confirm that removing the EVT-based constraint objective, extreme prioritization, or off-policy importance resampling each degrade performance or increase variance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CPO (expectation-based trust region), EVO achieves a higher ratio in all 8 environments (e.g., 0.275 vs 0.175 on SafetyPointGoal1, 0.250 vs 0.132 on SafetyAntVelocity), and critically, CPO violates the constraint threshold in SafetyPointGoal1-v0 while EVO maintains zero violations. Against WCSAC (Gaussian CVaR-based), EVO dominates across all environments; WCSAC exhibits significant constraint violations in SafetyCarCircle1-v0 and SafetyHalfCheetahVelocity-v1 due to its inability to capture tail behavior. Against Saute and Simmer (state augmentation methods emphasizing zero violations), EVO wins on 7 of 8 environments, with the sole exception being SafetySwimmerVelocity where Saute edges out EVO by 0.001 (0.214 vs 0.213). Against RCPO (multi-timescale Lagrangian, evaluated in the appendix), EVO consistently maintains constraint satisfaction while RCPO frequently violates constraints, and PPO-Lagrangian shows significant oscillations during training.

## Relation to Prior Work

EVO builds directly on CPO's trust-region framework for constrained policy optimization, replacing the expectation-based cost constraint with an EVT-derived extreme quantile constraint. It departs from prior probability-based methods (WCSAC, QCPO) that approximate the cost distribution with a Gaussian or use raw quantile regression, instead leveraging the Generalized Pareto Distribution to explicitly model tail extremes. The extreme prioritization and off-policy importance resampling mechanisms are novel additions that address the sample scarcity and high variance inherent in extreme events, extending the EVT-for-RL line of work (e.g., EVAC) from return modeling to constraint satisfaction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

EVO provides a principled, theoretically grounded approach to reducing constraint violation probability in safe RL, with a zero-violation guarantee at a specified quantile level that expectation-based methods cannot offer. The minimal computational overhead (GPD fitting takes seconds) makes it practical for deployment. However, experiments are limited to Safety Gymnasium and Safety MuJoCo environments, which are relatively low-dimensional; open questions include whether the EVT tail assumptions hold in high-dimensional continuous control or real-world safety-critical domains, and how the method behaves when the extreme/normal value gap is subtle (where GPD fitting degrades). The adaptive exploitation range ν and the reliance on sufficient peak samples for stable GPD estimation remain practical considerations for complex environments.
