## Problem

Formal verification of neural network robustness is intractable for large networks (limited to a few hundred parameters), while sampling-based adversarial methods scale but provide no formal guarantees. This paper bridges the gap by certifying a probabilistic relaxation of global robustness—called Probably Approximately Global (PAG) robustness—that is both scalable to large networks and backed by high-probability theoretical guarantees.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250172/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a trained classifier, an iid sample from the data distribution (or a noisy approximation thereof), and a pluggable local robustness oracle (e.g., PGD for adversarial search or LiRPA/Marabou for formal verification). It produces a single step-function map M(κ) from confidence to robustness radius, which simultaneously certifies all (ρ, κ) pairs: for any new point with confidence κ, its robustness is lower-bounded by M(κ) with probability at least 1−δ. The sample size depends only on ε, δ, and the VC-dimension of the quality space (d=2), not on input dimensionality, number of classes, or network architecture.

## Method

The key insight is to project each input point into a 2D 'quality space' Q via the map q(x) = (rob_f(x), conf_f(x)), where counterexamples to ρ-κ-robustness form axis-aligned half-plane intersections with VC-dimension 2. An iid sample is then treated as an ε-net over this range space: if no sampled point falls in the counterexample region R(ρ,κ), the region's probability mass is provably below ε. A second bound via a Chernoff-based quantile estimate (Lemma 4.3) ensures the confidence threshold κ is well-supported by the sample, preventing vacuous guarantees. The two bounds are combined with a union bound (Theorem 4.4) to yield the PAG certificate, and the robustness lower-bound map M(κ) is constructed in O(|N| log |N|) by sorting the sample. The framework is oracle-agnostic, supporting both exact formal verifiers and heuristic adversarial search, and extends to distribution shift via total variation distance (Theorem 4.6).

## Results

Across 230 experiments on 40 neural networks (MNIST and CIFAR-10), the PAG robustness guarantees hold in 211/230 (91.7%) of trials, with the worst-case empirical violation p̂ remaining below the theoretical bound ε/p_min. The method scales to the 10M-parameter VGG11_BN network, with runtimes ranging from 0.2 min (PGD oracle, FeedForward on MNIST) to 308 min (PGD oracle, VGG11_BN with TRADES on CIFAR-10). The residual violation rate of ~5/180 ≈ 0.028 in cases where guarantees appear to fail is close to the chosen confidence parameter δ = 0.01, suggesting the method is resilient to the imperfect sampling (Gaussian noise approximation) used in practice.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not perform direct head-to-head benchmark comparisons against competing robustness verification methods, as it introduces a new certification framework rather than a new classifier. Instead, it positions itself qualitatively against two existing paradigms: formal verification methods (e.g., Marabou, LiRPA) that provide exact local certificates but are intractable for networks beyond a few hundred parameters, and adversarial methods (e.g., PGD) that scale to large networks but offer no global guarantees. The paper demonstrates that its ε-net-based approach can use either paradigm as a local robustness oracle while providing a global probabilistic certificate, and empirically validates that the resulting guarantees transfer to unseen test data. The key empirical finding is that the method successfully differentiates between standard-trained and TRADES-trained networks (e.g., VGG11_BN on CIFAR-10 shows p̂ = 0.00 for standard training vs. p̂ = 17.00·10⁻³ for adversarial training under PGD), confirming that the certification captures meaningful qualitative robustness differences.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the confidence-based global robustness certification framework of Athavale et al. (2024) from a formal-verification setting (limited to networks with hundreds of parameters) to a probabilistic one by leveraging ε-net coverage in a 2D 'quality space' of (robustness, confidence) pairs. The key conceptual move is reducing the verification problem to a range space with VC-dimension 2, making the required sample size independent of input dimensionality, number of classes, and the specific classifier. The approach is oracle-agnostic, unifying formal methods (LiRPA, Marabou) and adversarial methods (PGD) as interchangeable local robustness oracles under a single sampling-based certification procedure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work bridges the scalability gap between formal verification (exact but intractable beyond small networks) and adversarial testing (scalable but guarantee-free) by providing high-probability global robustness certificates for networks with up to 10M parameters. The dimensionality-independent sample complexity is a clean theoretical contribution, and the empirical validation (211/230 trials consistent with guarantees, violation rate ≈ δ) confirms practical viability. Limitations include the reliance on iid sampling (approximated via Gaussian noise in experiments), the conditional nature of guarantees (only for confidence ≤ κ_max), and the dependence on oracle quality. Open questions include whether incorporating additional properties (predicted class, input features) can tighten bounds, and whether the approach extends to other safety-critical verification tasks beyond classification robustness.
