## Problem

Autoregressive visual generation models using next-token prediction must sequentially decode thousands of visual tokens, requiring hundreds to thousands of forward passes and resulting in high latency. Existing parallel decoding methods (Medusa, Jacobi, speculative decoding) are designed for LLMs and do not exploit the strong spatial locality inherent in visual content, where spatially distant tokens contribute minimally to attention. No training-free parallel decoding framework tailored specifically to the raster-order structure of AR visual generation had been developed.

## Input / Output

ZipAR is a training-free, plug-and-play decoding framework applied to pre-trained AR visual generation models (LlamaGen, Lumina-mGPT, Emu3-Gen) that take either a class label or a text prompt as input and produce RGB images at resolutions ranging from 256×256 to 1024×1024. During decoding, the model operates on discrete visual tokens (from a VQ-VAE) arranged in row-major raster order; ZipAR modifies only the decoding schedule, not the model weights or the token vocabulary. The output is the full 2D grid of visual tokens, rearranged and passed through the VQ-VAE decoder to reconstruct the image.

## Method

ZipAR exploits spatial locality by initiating decoding of the next row before the current row is fully complete: once a local window of s tokens in row i has been generated, token x_{i+1,0} in the next row can be decoded in parallel, since tokens beyond s columns in the previous row contribute negligibly to attention. An adaptive window size assignment scheme dynamically adjusts s per token using a rejection-sampling criterion analogous to speculative decoding: a token is first proposed with a smaller window, then verified against a larger window, and accepted only if the probability ratio exceeds a uniform random threshold; otherwise the window is expanded and the token resampled. All parallel tokens are produced by the original model head (no auxiliary heads or draft models), making the method entirely training-free. For models without explicit end-of-row tokens, a temporary placeholder value is assigned to the last token of the incomplete row to satisfy the autoregressive input requirement. Across LlamaGen-XL, Lumina-mGPT-7B, and Emu3-Gen, ZipAR reduces forward passes by 42–91% with minimal degradation in FID, CLIP score, or human-preference metrics.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256 class-conditional generation, ZipAR-16 on LlamaGen-L reduces forward passes from 576 to 422 (−26.7%) while improving FID from 3.16 to 3.14; on LlamaGen-XL, ZipAR-12 achieves FID 3.67 in 331 steps (−41.8%). On MS-COCO text-to-image, ZipAR-7 on LlamaGen-XL-512 cuts steps from 1024 to 324 (−68.4%) with CLIP score 0.285 vs. 0.287 for NTP. On Emu3-Gen, ZipAR achieves up to 91% forward-step reduction with minimal quality degradation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole external baseline is SJD (Speculative Jacobi Decoding, Teng et al., 2024). On LlamaGen-XL (ImageNet), ZipAR-12 matches SJD's step count (331 vs. 335) while achieving a substantially lower FID (3.67 vs. 3.87), and ZipAR-16 achieves near-NTP FID (2.87 vs. 2.83) at 423 steps. On LlamaGen-L, ZipAR-16 uses more steps than SJD (422 vs. 367) but yields a markedly better FID (3.14 vs. 3.85), indicating SJD degrades quality more at aggressive compression. On MS-COCO text-to-image (LlamaGen-XL-512), ZipAR-7 dramatically outperforms SJD in efficiency (324 vs. 635 steps, a 49% reduction) while also achieving a higher CLIP score (0.285 vs. 0.283). On Lumina-mGPT-7B-768, ZipAR-11 reduces steps by 75% (588 vs. 2352 NTP) with CLIP 0.312, comparable to SJD's 0.313 at 1054 steps. Overall, ZipAR consistently matches or exceeds SJD in image quality at equal or fewer forward passes, with the largest gains at higher resolutions.

## Relation to Prior Work

ZipAR repurposes the rejection-sampling acceptance criterion from speculative decoding (Leviathan et al., 2023; Chen et al., 2023) for a fundamentally different purpose: instead of verifying draft-model tokens, it verifies whether a smaller spatial window provides sufficient context for generating a visual token. It departs from prior parallel decoding methods for LLMs (Medusa, Jacobi/SJD) that predict tokens in sequence or random order, by exploiting the spatial locality of visual content to decode tokens from different raster rows simultaneously. The paper explicitly positions itself as the first parallel decoding framework tailored to AR visual generation, arguing that none of the LLM-focused approaches incorporate visual inductive biases.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As a training-free, plug-and-play method, ZipAR offers immediate practical value to practitioners who have already trained AR visual generation models and cannot afford retraining. The up-to-91% forward-step reduction (with modest quality tradeoffs at aggressive settings) could significantly lower inference costs for high-resolution image and video generation. The paper notes ZipAR is orthogonal to Medusa and Jacobi-style methods, suggesting combinatorial acceleration is possible but unexplored. Open questions include whether the spatial locality assumption holds for video tokens, whether it transfers to next-scale prediction models like VAR, and how it interacts with other efficiency techniques at scale.
