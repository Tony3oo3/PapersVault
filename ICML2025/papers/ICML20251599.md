## Problem

Single-domain generalization (SDG) in medical imaging remains challenging because models trained on one center or device struggle to generalize to unseen domains, and existing SDG methods (data augmentation, adversarial training, regularization) offer limited generalization at high computational cost. While vision foundation models (VFMs) like SAM and DINOv2 exhibit strong cross-domain robustness, their large parameter counts make them impractical for deployment. The paper asks whether the generalization capabilities of VFMs can be efficiently transferred to lightweight specialized models during training, so that only the small model is needed at inference.

## Input / Output

The method takes 2D fundus images (256×256, cropped from 800×800 ROIs centered on the optic disc) or 3D prostate MRI volumes (384×384 axial slices) as input and produces pixel-wise segmentation masks for optic disc/cup or prostate structures. During training, the VFM (SAM, DINOv2, or MedSAM) processes the same input alongside the lightweight U-Net/ResNet34 specialized model, with VFM features reprogrammed via residual blocks to match the specialized model's channel dimensions. At inference, only the 22.52M-parameter specialized model runs, achieving 147.73 fps versus 43.91 fps for the full DAPSAM pipeline.

## Method

TinyMIG transfers VFM generalization to a lightweight model through two synergistic mechanisms applied during training. For global feature distribution, the Universal Feature Distribution Modulation (UFDM) module uses AdaIN-style statistics mixing—sampling Beta/Bernoulli coefficients to blend the VFM's per-channel mean/variance with random uniform statistics—then a Cross-Distribution Consistency Loss (CDCL) enforces bidirectional KL-divergence consistency between original and modulated segmentation logits. For local fine-grained content, the Frequency-based Discriminative Content Enhancement (FDCE) module applies 2D FFT to decompose features into amplitude and phase, applies learnable sigmoid filters to selectively enhance domain-invariant spectral components, and uses SE blocks on the phase spectrum for adaptive channel refinement; an L2 alignment loss then matches the enhanced features between VFM and specialized model across multiple scales. The VFM itself is adapted via per-layer adapters (PEFT) and a Feature Reprogramming Module of residual blocks for dimension alignment, while co-training with an auxiliary segmentation loss keeps the VFM task-relevant. The overall loss combines VFM segmentation, specialized model segmentation (original and UFDM-stylized), LRA alignment, and CDCL terms with empirically tuned weights [1, 1, 0.5, 0.5, 0.5].

## Results

On the fundus OD/OC segmentation task (5 domains), TinyMIG achieves an average DSC of 90.09% for optic disc and 79.84% for optic cup, surpassing the strongest baseline DAPSAM by +1.82% and +4.85% respectively. On the prostate MRI segmentation task (6 domains), TinyMIG reaches 83.09% DSC and 3.06 ASD, outperforming DAPSAM by +1.79% DSC and −0.33 ASD. Critically, TinyMIG achieves these gains with only 22.52M parameters and 22.64 GFLOPs at inference, compared to DAPSAM's 98.45M parameters and 58.61 GFLOPs, yielding 3.4× faster throughput (147.73 vs. 43.91 fps).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TinyMIG outperforms all 14 baselines on both the fundus and prostate tasks. The closest competitor, DAPSAM (MICCAI 2024), is surpassed by +1.82% OD DSC and +4.85% OC DSC on fundus, and by +1.79% DSC / −0.33 ASD on prostate, while TinyMIG uses 4.4× fewer parameters and 2.6× fewer FLOPs. Among traditional SDG methods, TriD (MICCAI 2023) and SLAug (AAAI 2023) are the strongest non-foundation-model baselines, achieving 81.12%/69.23% and 82.23%/68.28% OD/OC DSC respectively, both trailing TinyMIG by 8–11 points. Foundation-model fine-tuning approaches (Rein+SAM, Rein+DINOv2) achieve 79.36–80.07% on prostate but are outperformed by TinyMIG+MedSAM (83.09%) while requiring 3–4× more compute. The ERM (U-Net) baseline, which performs standard empirical risk minimization without any domain generalization strategy, lags by 13–21 DSC points, confirming the substantial benefit of the proposed generalization transfer framework.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two active research threads: single-source domain generalization (SDG) for medical imaging (MixStyle, DSU, MaxStyle, TriD) and the use of vision foundation models (SAM, DINOv2, MedSAM) for medical segmentation (DAPSAM, Rein). It departs from prior VFM-based medical imaging work by treating the foundation model purely as a training-time teacher whose feature statistics and frequency-domain content are distilled into a lightweight student, rather than fine-tuning or prompting the VFM at inference. The UFDM module repurposes AdaIN-style normalization with Beta-sampled mixing coefficients (borrowed from MixStyle's stochastic style interpolation), while the FDCE module extends Fourier-based domain generalization (Xu et al. 2021, Liu et al. 2024) with learnable amplitude/phase filters and SE-based channel refinement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for clinical deployment: a 22.5M-parameter U-Net with ResNet34 encoder outperforms a 98.5M-parameter fine-tuned SAM (DAPSAM) by 1.8–4.9% DSC while running 3.4× faster (147.7 vs 43.9 fps), eliminating the inference-time dependency on a large foundation model. However, the approach still requires access to a VFM during training, and the evaluation is limited to two segmentation tasks (2D fundus, 3D prostate) with a single student architecture. Open questions include whether the VFM-as-style-prior paradigm transfers to classification or detection tasks, whether it generalizes to more complex 3D volumetric tasks beyond axial-plane prostate MRI, and how sensitive the method is to the choice of VFM when the VFM was not pre-trained on medical data.
