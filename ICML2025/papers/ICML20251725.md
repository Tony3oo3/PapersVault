## Problem

Grokking — the sharp, delayed transition to perfect test accuracy on modular arithmetic — has been observed exclusively in neural networks trained with gradient-based optimization, leading to the assumption that it is an artifact of those specific mechanisms. This paper asks whether grokking is actually a general property of feature learning, independent of both the model class (neural vs. non-neural) and the optimization procedure (gradient descent vs. iterative kernel fitting), and whether standard progress measures (training loss, test loss, accuracy) can predict the transition.

## Input / Output

Inputs are one-hot encoded pairs (a, b) ∈ Z_p × Z_p concatenated into a 2p-dimensional vector, and outputs are one-hot encoded results in R^p for operations {addition, subtraction, multiplication, division} modulo a prime p (typically p=61). Training uses a random subset (e.g., 50–80%) of the p² (or p(p−1) for division) discrete input-label pairs. A notable property is that RFM achieves numerically zero training loss at every single iteration, and neural networks reach 100% training accuracy within a few epochs, yet test accuracy remains at 0% for many iterations before the sharp grokking transition.

## Method

The paper demonstrates grokking in Recursive Feature Machines (RFM), a non-neural, backpropagation-free algorithm that iteratively (i) solves ridgeless kernel regression with a Mahalanobis kernel, (ii) computes the Average Gradient Outer Product (AGOP) of the resulting predictor, and (iii) updates the feature matrix via a matrix power of the AGOP. Both RFM and one-hidden-layer fully-connected networks with quadratic activations are shown to learn block-circulant feature matrices whose sub-blocks are circulant (or Hankel) matrices, with the multiplication/division cases revealing this structure only after reordering coordinates by the discrete logarithm of a generator of Z_p*. The paper introduces two a posteriori progress measures — circulant deviation (variance along wrapped diagonals) and AGOP alignment (cosine similarity to the final AGOP) — that track gradual, near-linear feature improvement during the plateau where loss and accuracy are flat. Theoretically, the paper proves that kernel machines equipped with block-circulant Mahalanobis matrices and trained on all discrete data implement the Fourier Multiplication Algorithm (FMA), the same algorithm previously identified in grokked neural networks, thereby unifying the solution across model classes.

## Results

RFM with a quadratic kernel on modular arithmetic (p=61) achieves 100% test accuracy after approximately 10–15 iterations, while maintaining numerically zero training loss at every iteration. One-hidden-layer neural networks (quadratic activation, 50% training fraction, p=61) similarly grok, transitioning from 0% to 100% test accuracy after an initial plateau. Training neural networks on inputs transformed by random block-circulant matrices at 17.5% training fraction reaches 100% test accuracy within several hundred epochs, whereas untransformed inputs fail to reach 100% even after 3000 epochs. The Pearson correlation between the Neural Feature Matrix (W₁ᵀW₁) and the square root of the AGOP ranges from 0.924 (Mul) to 0.955 (Add) across the four modular arithmetic tasks, confirming the Neural Feature Ansatz. Theorem 5.1 provides an exact proof that kernel predictors with circulant Mahalanobis matrices implement the Fourier Multiplication Algorithm for modular subtraction (and, by extension, addition, multiplication, and division).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not present a traditional benchmark comparison table; instead, it makes several targeted comparisons. First, standard kernel machines trained on random block-circulant features outperform RFM that must learn such features iteratively through AGOP (Fig. 4), demonstrating that the circulant structure itself—not the specific learned values—is what enables generalization. Second, neural networks trained on random circulant-transformed inputs dramatically outperform those trained on standard one-hot encodings: at 17.5% training fraction, transformed inputs reach 100% test accuracy within hundreds of epochs while untransformed inputs do not reach 100% within 3000 epochs (Fig. 7). Third, the paper shows that both RFM (a non-neural, non-gradient-based kernel method) and fully-connected neural networks converge to the same block-circulant feature structure and the same out-of-domain solution (the Fourier Multiplication Algorithm), despite using fundamentally different optimization and model classes. Finally, the introduced progress measures (circulant deviation and AGOP alignment) reveal gradual, near-linear improvement during the phase where standard metrics (training loss, test loss, test accuracy) remain flat, directly contradicting the 'mirage' hypothesis of Schaeffer et al. (2023) that sharp accuracy transitions are merely artifacts of discontinuous evaluation metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the RFM/AGOP framework of Radhakrishnan et al. (2024a) to the modular arithmetic domain, demonstrating that the same feature-learning mechanism that explains neural network feature acquisition also drives grokking in a purely non-neural, non-gradient-based setting. It departs from the dominant narrative in the grokking literature (Power et al., 2022; Gromov, 2023; Nanda et al., 2023), which has focused almost exclusively on neural networks trained with gradient descent, by showing that sharp emergence arises from feature learning alone, independent of the predictor class or optimization method. The theoretical connection to the Fourier Multiplication Algorithm (FMA) aligns with prior mechanistic interpretability work (Nanda et al., 2023; Varma et al., 2023; Morwani et al., 2024) but provides a clean kernel-method proof that block-circulant features implement FMA, and directly challenges the 'mirage' argument of Schaeffer et al. (2023) by showing that no continuous a priori metric predicts the transition.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By isolating feature learning as the sole driver of grokking in a minimal non-neural model, this work provides a tractable testbed for studying emergence without the confounds of deep architecture, gradient dynamics, or weight-magnitude effects that complicate neural network analyses. The finding that the 'lazy-to-rich' two-phase transition proposed by Kumar et al. (2024) and Lyu et al. (2023) is absent in RFM (features evolve gradually from iteration one) narrows the space of viable explanations for grokking. Open questions include whether the convergence to circulant features reflects a general inductive bias toward Fourier-based solutions for group-structured tasks, and whether the a posteriori progress measures (circulant deviation, AGOP alignment) can be made predictive without access to a fully trained model. The practical implication is that pre-training data with random circulant transforms dramatically accelerates learning, offering a simple data-augmentation strategy for algebraic tasks.
