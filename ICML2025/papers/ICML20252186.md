## Problem

Federated Graph Learning faces rapidly growing computational and communication costs as graph datasets scale and GNN depth increases, yet existing sparsification methods address only one domain (graph structure or model parameters) and rely on single criteria that cause 'structure information overfitting' or iterative retraining that conflicts with efficiency goals. No prior work provides a unified framework that simultaneously sparsifies both the graph and parameter spaces while accounting for cross-client data heterogeneity and parameter importance variability.

## Input / Output

Each client contributes a private subgraph with node feature vectors (dimension D) and an adjacency matrix; the server aggregates across K clients per round. The method outputs node-level classification labels (single-label for Cora/Pubmed/OGBN-Arxiv/OGBN-Products, multi-label binary for OGBN-Proteins) alongside sparsified one-hop subgraphs per node and pruned weight matrices at multiple sparsity levels. A distinctive aspect is that the GSyE gate matrix (W_gate) is uploaded alongside backbone parameters and used server-side to compute Optimal Transport distances between clients for structure-aware aggregation weighting.

## Method

EAGLES unifies two sparsification mechanisms. For parameters, it learns per-row dynamic thresholds via a Straight-Through Estimator to generate binary masks in a single forward pass, producing subnetworks at all sparsity levels simultaneously; client masks are bit-compressed and aligned via consensus (element-wise AND) to ensure unbiased knowledge sharing, with a dynamic rollback strategy that resets to the best subnetwork within ±3% accuracy at each 10% pruning milestone. For graphs, multiple Graph Sparsification Experts (GSEs) each score edges in a node's one-hop subgraph using distinct criteria (Jaccard, spectral, Katz, cosine similarity), and a Graph Synergy Expert (GSyE) applies a hard concrete distribution to differentiably gate which expert's sparsified subgraph to retain per node, balancing expert importance variance and load variance in its loss. The GSyE's learned gate matrix encodes per-client structural context, which the server uses to compute pairwise Optimal Transport distances, converting them into similarity-weighted personalized aggregation coefficients that bias knowledge transfer toward structurally similar clients.

## Results

On Ogbn-Proteins with DeeperGCN, EAGLES achieves 82.10% ROC-AUC (↑0.56 over FedAvg) while reducing training FLOPS by 82% (0.18×) and communication bytes by 80% (0.20×). On Ogbn-Arxiv with GraphSAGE, it reaches 65.37% Top-1 Accuracy (↑1.38) at 0.32× FLOPS and 0.48× communication. On Pubmed with GCN, EAGLES attains 86.97% accuracy (↑1.32) at 0.48× FLOPS and 0.37× communication. EAGLES is the only method that simultaneously improves accuracy, reduces training FLOPS, and lowers communication bytes across all three datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EAGLES outperforms all 14 baselines on every metric across all three datasets, making it the only method that simultaneously improves accuracy, reduces training FLOPS, and lowers communication bytes. Against the strongest accuracy-focused baselines (FedTAD at 65.03% on Ogbn-Arxiv, FedSSP at 86.32% on Pubmed), EAGLES achieves higher accuracy (65.37%, 86.97%) while those methods incur 27–35× higher FLOPS. Against the most efficient baselines (FedDIP at 0.38× FLOPS on Ogbn-Arxiv, ACE-GLT at 0.36× FLOPS), EAGLES matches or exceeds their efficiency (0.32×) while delivering 1.52% and 1.33% higher accuracy respectively. Pruning-only methods (PruneFL, FedTiny, FedDIP) reduce FLOPS but sacrifice 2–5% accuracy and fail to reduce communication bytes (PruneFL) or only modestly (FedTiny at 0.59×). Graph sparsification methods (DSpar, ACE-GLT) reduce FLOPS but leave communication unchanged at 1×, whereas EAGLES cuts communication by 52–80% through consensus-based parameter sparsification.

## Relation to Prior Work

EAGLES unifies two previously separate lines of work in federated graph learning: graph-structure sparsification (e.g., DSpar's degree-based edge pruning) and model-parameter pruning (e.g., PruneFL, FedTiny, FedDIP), which prior methods have addressed in isolation. The graph-level component draws on Mixture-of-Experts gating (hard concrete distribution) to combine multiple sparsification criteria, while the parameter-level component adapts lottery-ticket-style dynamic pruning with a consensus mask alignment mechanism specific to federated settings. The OT-based structural similarity metric for personalized aggregation extends the topology-aware averaging idea from FedGTA but grounds it in a more principled transport-distance formulation. The paper explicitly positions against centralized dual-sparsification (ACE-GLT) and knowledge-distillation-based FGL (FedTAD, FedSSP), arguing that neither adequately handles client heterogeneity or avoids additional computational overhead.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As graph datasets in federated settings continue to scale (OGBN-Products has 2.4M nodes), the computational and communication costs of GNN message passing become a practical bottleneck that existing FGL methods largely ignore. EAGLES demonstrates that aggressive joint sparsification (80%+ parameter reduction, 30% graph reduction) can maintain or even improve accuracy, which is encouraging for resource-constrained deployments. However, the method requires a pretraining phase to identify lottery-ticket subnetworks, partially offsetting the claimed efficiency gains, and the OGBN-Products results show only modest improvements (~0.3–1.1% accuracy) at the largest scale, raising questions about whether the multi-expert overhead scales favorably. Open questions include whether the consensus mask alignment degrades under extreme client heterogeneity and whether the OT computation cost becomes prohibitive with hundreds of clients.
