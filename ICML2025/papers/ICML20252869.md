## Problem

Offline model-based optimization (MBO) methods that maximize a forward surrogate reward provably collapse to low-diversity candidate sets, concentrating all generated designs near a single global optimum (Lemma 3.1). While prior work has addressed out-of-distribution exploitation of the surrogate, no existing method explicitly incorporates design diversity as an optimization objective in a way that is both task-agnostic and compatible with arbitrary backbone optimizers.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252869/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a static offline dataset of (design, oracle-score) pairs, a pre-trained forward surrogate r_θ (a 2-layer MLP with 2048 hidden units), and an initialized 1-Lipschitz source critic c* (2-layer MLP with 512 hidden units). For discrete search spaces (DNA sequences, molecules), a Transformer-based VAE with 256-dim latent space is co-trained with the surrogate to enable continuous optimization. The output is a set of k=128 candidate designs ranked by the penalized MBO objective, evaluated on both Best@128 (oracle quality) and Pairwise Diversity (normalized Levenshtein or Euclidean distance). The method is optimizer-agnostic, demonstrated with gradient ascent, Adam, CMA-ES, CoSyNE, BO-qEI, and BO-qUCB, with fixed hyperparameters β=1.0, τ=1.0, W_0=0 across all tasks.

## Method

DynAMO reformulates diversity as a KL-divergence distribution-matching objective: the generated design distribution q^π should match a τ-weighted reference distribution p_D^τ(x) ∝ exp(τr(x)) over the offline dataset, where τ controls the optimality–diversity trade-off (τ→∞ recovers pure reward maximization; τ→0 yields uniform sampling). A Wasserstein-GAN-style source critic constraint (E_{p_D^τ}[c*] − E_{q^π}[c*] ≤ W_0) prevents the optimizer from exploiting out-of-distribution surrogate overestimation. The key theoretical contribution is showing (Lemma 3.3) that the combined objective is equivalent to −H(q^π) − (1+β)D_KL(q^π‖p_D^τ), and then (Lemma 3.4) deriving an explicit concave lower bound g_ℓ(λ) on the Lagrangian dual function via the Fenchel conjugate of the KL f-divergence, which admits a closed-form convex solution for the Lagrange multiplier λ. This makes the constrained problem solvable with any standard optimizer by simply modifying the per-sample scoring function to the Lagrangian L(x;λ), requiring no changes to the optimizer itself.

## Results

Across six offline MBO tasks (TFBind8, UTR, ChEMBL, Superconductor, D'Kitty, Molecule) and six backbone optimizers, DynAMO achieves the best Pairwise Diversity (PD) Rank of 1.2 (Grad., Adam, CoSyNE) and 1.3 (BO-qEI), with PD Optimality Gaps as high as 74.2 (BO-qEI) and 59.4 (BO-qUCB). On design quality (Best@128), DynAMO attains an average Rank of 2.3–3.5 and Best@128 Optimality Gaps of 12.3–20.7, placing it within the top 2 methods for 5 of 6 optimizers. No other baseline exceeds a PD Optimality Gap of 16.8 (GAMBO-CMA-ES) or 51.4 (COMs⁺-BO-qUCB), confirming DynAMO's consistent diversity advantage without sacrificing quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DynAMO dominates all four baselines on Pairwise Diversity across every optimizer: its PD Rank is 1.2–1.8 versus 2.2–7.8 for the next-best method (COMs⁺ or ROMO), and its PD Optimality Gap reaches 74.2 (BO-qEI) versus at most 51.4 (COMs⁺-BO-qUCB) or 16.8 (GAMBO-CMA-ES) for any baseline. On Best@128 quality, DynAMO is competitive: it achieves the best Best@128 Optimality Gap for 5 of 6 optimizers (e.g., 20.7 for BO-qEI vs. 21.0 for RoMA⁻, 20.8 for GAMBO) and the best Best@128 Rank for CoSyNE (2.3) and is within 0.3 of the best for Grad. and Adam (2.8 vs. 2.5 for COMs⁺). COMs⁺ (Trabucco et al., 2021) is the strongest quality baseline but requires a specialized forward surrogate and shows poor diversity (PD Rank 2.8–7.8). RoMA (Yu et al., 2021) and ROMO (Chen et al., 2023) are mid-tier on both metrics, while GAMBO (Yao et al., 2024), the closest prior method, is outperformed by DynAMO on PD by 2–5 rank positions and by 11–57 points on PD Optimality Gap across optimizers.

## Relation to Prior Work

DynAMO builds directly on GAMBO (Yao et al., 2024), which introduced adversarial source critic regularization to prevent out-of-distribution exploitation of forward surrogate models in offline MBO. The key departure is the repurposing of distribution matching—previously a tool in imitation learning and offline RL (Kostrikov et al., 2020; Ke et al., 2021)—as an explicit diversity objective in generative design, combined with a τ-weighted reference distribution that interpolates between uniform (maximum diversity) and reward-weighted (maximum optimality) sampling. Unlike prior diversity methods in Bayesian optimization (Maus et al., 2023; Hernandez-García et al., 2024) that gate sampling via thresholds or are tied to a specific acquisition framework, DynAMO modifies the MBO objective itself and is agnostic to the backbone optimizer (gradient, evolutionary, or Bayesian). The Lagrangian duality analysis with a closed-form Fenchel-conjugate lower bound on the dual function (Lemma 3.4) provides a tractable solution that prior work approximated via grid search or hand-tuned hyperparameters.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important gap: offline MBO methods routinely collapse to a single mode of the design space, yet real-world applications (drug design, materials discovery, robotics) benefit from diverse candidate sets that capture multiple 'modes of goodness' and allow exploration of secondary objectives like toxicity or manufacturing cost. The plug-and-play nature of DynAMO—requiring only a pre-trained surrogate and a source critic, with no changes to the optimizer—makes it broadly applicable. Key limitations include suboptimal Median@128 performance (the method sacrifices median quality for diversity and best-case quality), evaluation restricted to well-studied Design-Bench tasks and one-shot batched evaluation, and unexplored regimes of noisy objectives, ultra-high-dimensional spaces, and small offline datasets. Extending DynAMO to active learning settings and leveraging domain-specific foundation models as surrogates are natural next steps.
