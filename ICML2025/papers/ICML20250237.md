## Problem

Graph-level anomaly detection (GLAD) must identify entire graphs deviating from normal patterns, but anomalous samples are typically scarce or unavailable during training. Existing approaches rely on strong distributional assumptions (e.g., hypersphere embeddings in DeepSVDD-based methods), require hand-crafted anomaly scores, or depend on limited labeled anomalies, making them brittle across diverse real-world graph domains.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250237/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a set of normal graphs, each specified by a node attribute matrix X_i ∈ R^{n_i×d} and a binary adjacency matrix A_i ∈ {0,1}^{n_i×n_i}, and produces a continuous anomaly score per graph that thresholds into a normal/anomalous binary decision. Training is fully unsupervised: no anomalous labels are required, and the classifier is trained solely on the normal graph set plus its own generated pseudo-anomalous counterparts. The framework is evaluated in one-class, large-scale imbalanced, and multi-class GLAD settings across 12 TUDataset benchmarks.

## Method

SDM jointly trains a generator g_φ that produces pseudo-anomalous graphs from normal inputs and a discriminator/classifier f_θ that separates normal from pseudo-anomalous graphs, so that the learned decision boundary encloses the normal distribution compactly. Three variants are proposed: SDM-ATI uses an MLP generator fed with random Gaussian noise in a GAN-style min-max game with a GIN discriminator; SDM-ATII replaces the noise input with a GIN-based VGAE encoder that perturbs the latent representation of each normal graph (via reparameterization), adding a discrepancy loss and KL-divergence regularizer to ensure generated graphs stay close to but distinguishable from normal ones; SDM-NAT removes the adversarial min-max entirely, directly training a GIN classifier with binary cross-entropy on normal graphs (label 1) versus VGAE-generated pseudo-anomalies (label 0). A volume-ratio argument in d-dimensional space motivates why most randomly perturbed pseudo-anomalies fall outside the low-intrinsic-dimensionality normal manifold, guaranteeing they interpolate toward real anomalies and yield a reliable boundary.

## Results

On the one-class GLAD benchmark (Table 1), SDM-NAT achieves the best AUC in all 14 dataset-class combinations, with headline results of 95.91% AUC / 92.42% F1 on PROTEINS (class 0), 99.23% AUC / 97.59% F1 on DD (class 1), and 99.94% AUC / 99.75% F1 on IMDB-BINARY (class 0). On the large-scale imbalanced molecular datasets (Table 3), SDM-NAT reaches 94.26–97.09% AUC, surpassing the best supervised baseline iGAD (83.22–86.04% AUC) by over 10 points. SDM-NAT also exhibits the lowest standard deviation (≤5% in 14/14 cases for both AUC and F1), confirming superior training stability over the adversarial variants SDM-ATI and SDM-ATII.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SDM-NAT dominates all 14 one-class GLAD evaluations, outperforming the strongest GNN-based baselines by large margins: on PROTEINS (class 0) it gains +26.4 AUC over SIGNET (69.51→95.91), +22.7 over MUSE (73.17→95.91), and +29.9 over DO2HSC (66.04→95.91). Graph kernel methods (SP, WL, NH, RW) are consistently weakest, often scoring below 70 AUC and suffering severe 'performance flip' across classes (e.g., WL drops from 81.57 to 19.22 on DD). On the large-scale imbalanced molecular datasets, SDM-NAT (94.26–97.09 AUC) surpasses the best supervised method iGAD (83.22–86.04 AUC) by 8–11 points, demonstrating that the unsupervised pseudo-anomalous generation strategy is more effective than leveraging limited labeled anomalies. Among the three SDM variants, SDM-NAT consistently outperforms the adversarial variants SDM-ATI and SDM-ATII in both accuracy and stability (std ≤5% in 14/14 cases vs. 10/14 and 11/14 respectively), confirming that removing the min-max game eliminates GAN training instability without sacrificing detection quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant DeepSVDD-based paradigm for graph-level anomaly detection (OCGIN, OCGTL, DO2HSC), which assumes a hyperspherical embedding distribution, and from reconstruction/knowledge-distillation approaches (GLocalKD, SIGNET, MUSE) that require explicitly defined anomaly scores. Instead, SDM repurposes the GAN generator-discriminator framework and VGAE variational inference to synthesize pseudo-anomalous graphs from normal ones, then trains a GIN-based classifier to separate the two. The non-adversarial variant (SDM-NAT) further breaks from the GAN lineage by replacing the min-max game with a straightforward supervised classification objective over generated negatives, drawing on the perturbation-learning idea from Cai & Fan (2022) for image anomaly detection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a practical, fully unsupervised route to graph-level anomaly detection that avoids strong geometric assumptions (hypersphere) and dataset-specific anomaly score definitions, and it consistently outperforms both unsupervised and semi-supervised baselines across 12 benchmarks including large-scale imbalanced molecular data. A key open question is how the method behaves when a small number of real anomalous graphs are available during training, which the authors acknowledge as a limitation. The reliance on TUDataset-scale benchmarks (≤40K graphs, small node counts) leaves open whether the pseudo-anomalous generation strategy scales to industrial-scale graphs with heterogeneous node types and very large node counts.
