## Problem

Existing diffusion-based offline MARL methods (e.g., MADIFF, DOM2) apply diffusion independently to each agent's historical trajectory, ignoring the inter-agent coordination structure. This limits robustness: while such policies can adapt to individual agent attribute changes (e.g., speed shifts), they fail when the coordination topology itself changes (e.g., an agent becomes unavailable), because no mechanism exists to restructure how agents interact.

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-agent local observations from offline trajectory datasets and constructs a sparse k-nearest-neighbor coordination graph at each timestep, where nodes carry continuous d-dimensional action vectors (one-hot for discrete spaces) and edges carry binary adjacency indicators. At inference, each agent receives only its local observation; the Q-function selects an initial action from a candidate set, other agents' actions are replaced with Gaussian noise, and the denoising process jointly recovers both the coordination graph structure and the final per-agent action. The output is a set of decentralized single-agent actions plus an evolved coordination graph that reflects which agents are actively coordinating.

## Method

MCGD introduces the first graph diffusion framework for offline multi-agent coordination, combining two distinct diffusion processes on a single graph: (1) categorical diffusion on discrete edges, where a transition matrix Q_βk = exp((C−D)βk) is derived from cosine similarity between agent observations, modeling how coordination links form and dissolve; and (2) anisotropic Gaussian diffusion on continuous nodes, where the forward noise covariance Σ_i is computed from the actions of agent i's neighbors, making the noise direction-dependent on the local coordination context. A graph transformer network f_θ jointly denoises both edge and node attributes, trained with cross-entropy loss on edges and an MSE-plus-Q-loss objective on nodes. During sampling, each agent first selects a Q-guided action from a finite candidate set, then iteratively denoises the coordination graph over K steps, with the categorical posterior sampling the evolving edge structure at each iteration. The framework is proven to satisfy symmetry, additivity, locality, and convergence properties for the edge transition matrix, and the anisotropic forward process admits a closed-form marginal distribution.

## Results

On the Expert/Good datasets across three benchmarks, MCGD achieves the best episodic return in all 10 tasks. In MPE (Expert), MCGD scores 93.8 (Spread), 109.6 (Tag), and 110.9 (World), improving over the best diffusion-based baseline (MADIFF/DOM2) by 5.7–11.5%. In MAMuJoCo (Good), MCGD reaches 3917.4 (2halfcheetah), 2782.7 (2ant), and 2609.2 (4ant), a 6.5–12.2% gain. In SMAC (Good), MCGD attains 22.1 (3m), 20.7 (2s3z), 18.9 (5m6m), and 20.1 (8m), a 4.7–12.8% improvement. Under shifted environments (Table 2), MCGD's advantage widens to up to 14.2% in coordination-structure-shift settings, confirming superior robustness.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MCGD outperforms all six baselines across every task in all three benchmarks. The two strongest competitors are the diffusion-based methods DOM2 (Li et al., 2023) and MADIFF (Zhu et al., 2023), which rank second and third in most tasks; MCGD improves over them by 5.7–12.2% in MPE/MAMuJoCo and 4.7–12.8% in SMAC. The conservatism-based offline MARL methods (MA-ICQ, MA-CQL, OMAR) and the generative-behavior-modeling method MA-SfBC are consistently weaker, with MA-CQL showing the largest gaps (e.g., 57.7 vs. 110.9 on MPE World, 11.3 vs. 20.1 on SMAC 8m). In shifted environments (Table 2), the gap widens further: MCGD gains up to 14.2% over the best baseline under coordination-structure shifts (e.g., 65.3 vs. 57.2 on MPE Spread), while attribute-shift gaps are more moderate (5.9–13.5%). The ablation confirms that both categorical diffusion (structure diversity) and anisotropic diffusion (action diversity) contribute, with the fixed-graph variant MCGD-CD degrading notably on complex SMAC maps.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends diffusion-based offline MARL (MADIFF, DOM2) by replacing independent per-agent diffusion with a unified graph diffusion framework that jointly models inter-agent coordination structure (discrete edges via categorical diffusion) and single-agent actions (continuous nodes via anisotropic diffusion). It draws on the graph diffusion literature (categorical diffusion for discrete structures, anisotropic Gaussian noise for continuous attributes) and adapts these to the multi-agent coordination setting, where the transition matrix is derived from observation similarity rather than being fixed. The key departure from prior diffusion-based MARL is the explicit modeling of coordination topology as a learnable, dynamic graph rather than treating agents as independent diffusion processes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ability to adapt coordination structure when an agent becomes unavailable (e.g., a robot disconnecting) addresses a practical failure mode that prior offline MARL methods cannot handle, making this relevant for real-world multi-robot deployment. However, all evaluations are on simulation benchmarks (MPE, MAMuJoCo, SMAC) with no real-world validation, and the authors acknowledge that deployment in multi-robot hunting scenarios is still in progress. Open questions include scalability to very large agent groups, whether the k-nearest-neighbor graph construction generalizes to non-spatial coordination, and how the method performs under partial observability with noisy or delayed observations.
