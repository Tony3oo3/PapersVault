## Problem

Predicting the exact future time steps at which anomalies will occur in time series (Anomaly Prediction) remains unsolved. Existing approaches either only flag whether an anomaly is imminent without localizing it, or naively combine a forecasting model with an anomaly detector, which fails because forecasting models trained solely on normal signals suppress the abnormality in their predictions, making downstream detection unreliable.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251922/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a multivariate time series window X_in ∈ R^{L_in×C} (L_in=100; C ranges from 1 for MBA to 123 for WADI) and produces binary anomaly labels O ∈ R^{L_out} for the forecasted future signal, where L_out ∈ {100, 200, 400}. Evaluation uses F1-score with a tolerance window t (default t=50) around each predicted anomaly step rather than the standard Point Adjustment, since precise temporal localization is the goal. At inference, only the shared backbone is retained: the input is first forecasted, then the forecast is reconstructed, and per-step anomaly scores are computed from the reconstruction discrepancy.

## Method

A2P unifies forecasting and anomaly detection in a single shared transformer backbone with separate embedding and projection heads. Two pre-trained modules are frozen during main training: (1) an Anomaly-Aware Forecasting Network that uses cross-attention over anomaly-injected signals to output per-step anomaly probabilities, which in main training weight the forecasting MSE so the model focuses on abnormal regions; and (2) an Anomaly Prompt Pool (APP) of M learnable prompt–key pairs, where a signal-adaptive feature extractor selects the top-N prompts via cosine similarity and concatenates them to the input embedding to synthesize anomalous features. A novel Divergence Loss (KL divergence between prompted and unprompted attention features, plus a key-retrieval term) trains the APP to push synthetic anomalies away from normal representations. Main training optimizes a weighted forecasting loss (anomaly-probability-modulated MSE) and a reconstruction loss that forces the model to recover the original normal signal from both the prompted and unprompted embeddings.

## Results

On the Anomaly Prediction task (L_in=100, L_out=100), A2P achieves an average F1-score of 46.84 across four real-world datasets (MBA, Exathlon, SMD, WADI), outperforming the best baseline combination (FITS+DCDetector at 41.55) by 5.3 points. The advantage grows with longer prediction horizons: at L_out=200, A2P reaches 53.08 average F1 (vs. 40.94 for the best baseline), and at L_out=400, it achieves 58.89 (vs. 41.18). On the MBA dataset specifically, A2P scores 67.55 F1 at L_out=100, surpassing the next-best (FITS+DC at 61.39) by over 6 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

A2P outperforms all 15 forecasting+AD baseline combinations (5 forecasting models × 3 AD models) across every dataset and prediction horizon. Among forecasting models, PatchTST, GPT2, and iTransformer paired with AnomalyTransformer yield the strongest baselines (39.6–41.4 avg F1 at L_out=100), while FITS+DCDetector is the single best combination at 41.55; A2P exceeds all of them by 5.3+ points. Among anomaly detection models, AnomalyTransformer consistently produces the best pairing with each forecaster, whereas CAD and DCDetector are more variable and often significantly weaker (e.g., GPT2+CAD drops to 17.77 avg F1). In the extended baselines (Table 12), TranAD and BeatGAN as AD components reach up to 45.04 avg F1 (MICN+BeatGAN), still trailing A2P by 1.8 points, while DiffusionAD is the weakest additional baseline (max 31.04). The gap between A2P and the best baseline widens substantially at longer horizons (L_out=400: 58.89 vs. 41.18, a 17.7-point margin), indicating that A2P's anomaly-aware forecasting and synthetic prompting become increasingly critical as prediction length grows.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of time series forecasting and anomaly detection, departing from both lines of work by introducing a unified shared-backbone architecture that performs both tasks simultaneously. It builds on the anomaly injection scheme from CARLA (Darban et al., 2025) for synthetic anomaly generation and adopts the association-discrepancy scoring from AnomalyTransformer (Xu et al., 2022) at test time. Conceptually, it departs from prior anomaly prediction attempts (Jhin et al., 2023; You et al., 2024) by being the first to actually solve the task of pinpointing specific future anomaly time points, rather than merely detecting whether an anomaly will occur or formulating the problem without a solution. The prompt-learning mechanism (Anomaly Prompt Pool) draws inspiration from prompt tuning in NLP but repurposes it for signal-level anomaly synthesis in the embedding space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first working method for Anomaly Prediction, a task with clear real-world value in medical monitoring, industrial maintenance, and infrastructure management where knowing *when* a failure will occur is more actionable than knowing *that* it will occur. The empirical demonstration that naively chaining SOTA forecasting with SOTA anomaly detection fails (due to anomaly smoothing in predictions) is a useful cautionary result for practitioners. Limitations include evaluation on only four primary datasets (some with very few anomaly segments, e.g., WADI has 27), reliance on synthetic anomaly injection for pre-training, and the relatively modest scale of experiments (5 epochs, 3-layer backbone). Open questions include whether the approach scales to very long prediction horizons, whether the prompt pool generalizes across domains without retraining, and how performance degrades when anomaly types in the test distribution differ from those seen during pre-training.
