## Problem

Medical LVLMs suffer from modality misalignment, where models prioritize learned textual knowledge over visual input, producing hallucinations that contradict medical images. Existing preference optimization approaches for Med-LVLMs reuse general LVLM data generation pipelines that ignore clinical relevance, resulting in easily distinguishable preference pairs that provide weak alignment signals.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20253226/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives a medical image (chest X-ray) paired with a clinical query and autoregressively generates a text response—either a short VQA answer or a full radiology report. During preference data construction, the visual input is additionally augmented with localized Gaussian noise applied only to lesion regions identified by MedKLIP heatmaps, creating a second input variant (x*) that pairs with the same ground-truth text to form a visual-level preference pair. Training uses LoRA fine-tuning on LLaVA-Med-1.5 7B with a batch size of 4 over 3 epochs.

## Method

MMedPO constructs multimodal preference data via two complementary strategies: (1) text-level dispreference by sampling hallucinated responses from the target Med-LVLM or GPT-4o and selecting the most clinically inaccurate one, and (2) visual-level dispreference by applying localized diffusion-style noise to lesion regions detected by MedKLIP, disrupting the model's understanding of critical areas. Each preference sample is then assigned a clinical relevance score: for text-level pairs, a multi-agent debate among multiple Med-LLMs (LLaMA3-Med42-7B/70B, BioMistral-7B) reaches consensus on the dispreferred response's clinical significance; for visual-level pairs, the confidence score from the visual tool's lesion heatmap serves as the relevance indicator. These scores are normalized (z-scored and clipped to [0.75, 1.25]) and used as per-sample weights in a modified DPO loss, so that clinically more relevant preference pairs contribute more strongly to the gradient, yielding a weighted DPO objective that prioritizes high-value alignment signals.

## Results

On the Med-VQA tasks, MMedPO achieves 53.99/73.08 (Open/Closed) on SLAKE and 36.36/66.54 (Open/Closed) on VQA-RAD, outperforming the best baseline (FiSAO) by 15.8% and 10.3% on open-ended and closed-ended questions respectively. On report generation, MMedPO reaches 23.49 BLEU and 29.52 ROUGE-L on IU-Xray, and 12.85 BLEU and 11.13 ROUGE-L on MIMIC-CXR, surpassing the best baseline by 61.9% and 26.0% on IU-Xray and MIMIC-CXR respectively. Overall, MMedPO delivers average improvements of 14.2% on Med-VQA and 51.7% on report generation over the strongest prior preference optimization methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MMedPO consistently outperforms all seven baselines across all four datasets and both task types. On Med-VQA, the closest competitor is FiSAO (52.69 SLAKE Open, 32.70 VQA-RAD Open), which MMedPO surpasses by 1.3 and 3.7 points respectively; on closed-ended questions, MMedPO leads FiSAO by 2.6 points on SLAKE and 2.5 on VQA-RAD. For report generation, MMedPO achieves 23.49 BLEU on IU-Xray versus FiSAO's 21.06 and POVID's 20.80, and 12.85 BLEU on MIMIC-CXR versus FiSAO's 11.32 and POVID's 11.21. The medical-specific baseline STLLaVA-Med underperforms significantly (16.11 BLEU on IU-Xray, 11.11 on MIMIC-CXR), confirming that generic preference data curation without clinical relevance scoring is insufficient. When combined with SFT, MMedPO maintains its lead, achieving 55.23/75.24 on SLAKE and 24.00 BLEU on IU-Xray, outperforming SFT+FiSAO (52.80/70.82, 23.57 BLEU) and SFT+DPO (53.50/69.47, 23.07 BLEU).

## Relation to Prior Work

MMedPO extends Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) to the medical vision-language domain by introducing a weighted variant where each preference pair is scaled by a quantified clinical relevance score. It departs from prior medical preference optimization methods (STLLaVA-Med, Banerjee et al., Hein et al.) that directly reuse natural-image preference data pipelines, by proposing two domain-specific curation strategies—hallucination injection via GPT-4o/target Med-LVLMs and localized lesion-noising guided by MedKLIP—and a multi-agent Med-LLM collaboration mechanism for scoring. The general LVLM alignment line (POVID, FiSAO, SIMA) informs the optimization framework, but MMedPO's key conceptual departure is treating clinical relevance as a first-class weighting signal rather than an implicit property of the data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Reducing hallucinations in medical VLMs is clinically critical, as factually incorrect radiology reports or VQA answers can contribute to misdiagnosis. MMedPO's compatibility with multiple backbones (LLaVA-Med-1.5, LLaVA-Med++) and its modest compute requirements (LoRA, ~2–3 hours on 4 GPUs) make it practically deployable. However, the approach depends on GPT-4o for data curation and MedKLIP for lesion localization, both of which may introduce domain-specific biases; generalization beyond chest X-rays to other modalities (MRI, CT, pathology) remains untested. An open question is whether the multi-agent scoring overhead can be reduced without sacrificing the ~3.6% gain it provides over single-LLM scoring.
