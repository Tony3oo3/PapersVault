## Problem

Model merging produces representations that diverge from those of individual expert models (representation bias), causing significant performance degradation. The existing deterministic Surgery module, while helpful, has limited representational capacity to model the uncertainty inherent in parameter interference across merged models, and requires a separate task-specific module per task, limiting practical applicability.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252629/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes the last-layer feature embedding (e.g., 512-d for ViT-B/32, 768-d for ViT-L/14, or 768-d for BERT) produced by a merged model's encoder and outputs a sampled bias vector of the same dimensionality, which is subtracted from the merged representation before passing to a task-specific classifier. Training is self-supervised: it requires only unlabeled test/validation samples from each task (no labels needed), using the individual expert model's features as alignment targets. At inference, the mean μ of the generated Gaussian is used as the bias correction, eliminating the need for task ID at test time in the one-to-all setting.

## Method

ProbSurgery replaces the deterministic three-layer MLP in Surgery with a probabilistic module that parameterizes a Gaussian distribution N(μ, σ²) over the representation bias, where μ and σ are produced by a lightweight MLP with architecture {h₁, h₂, h₁}. A bias sample ξ̂ = μ + ε·σ is drawn via the reparameterization trick and subtracted from the merged feature to produce the corrected representation. The training objective combines an alignment loss (L1 distance between corrected merged features and individual model features) with a KL divergence regularization toward N(0, I), balancing fit and simplicity. A PAC-Bayes analysis proves the probabilistic formulation yields a strictly tighter generalization error bound than the deterministic baseline. Two extensions—Direct Feature Alignment (DFA) and Proxy Distribution Alignment (PDA)—enable a single ProbSurgery module to handle all merged tasks simultaneously, outperforming multiple task-specific Surgery modules while using fewer parameters.

## Results

On eight vision tasks (ViT-B/32 backbone), ProbSurgery consistently improves all four base merging methods: Weight Averaging from 80.0% to 86.7% avg accuracy (+6.7%), Task Arithmetic from 80.9% to 87.0% (+6.1%), Ties-Merging from 83.1% to 88.0% (+4.9%), and LW AdaMerging from 87.5% to 88.8% (+1.3%). On five NLP tasks (BERT backbone), ProbSurgery achieves 76.0% avg accuracy under Weight Averaging, surpassing the dedicated multi-task learning method KD4MTL (75.5%). In the one-to-all setting, a single ProbSurgery module with PDA reaches 82.4% avg accuracy, outperforming eight independent Surgery modules (80.9%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ProbSurgery outperforms Surgery (Yang et al., 2024a) across all four base merging methods on vision tasks, with the largest gain of +6.7% under Weight Averaging (86.7% vs. 80.0%) and +6.1% under Task Arithmetic (87.0% vs. 80.9%). Compared to the strongest merging baseline EMR-Merging (88.7% avg), ProbSurgery with LW AdaMerging achieves 88.8%, slightly surpassing it. On NLP tasks, ProbSurgery with Weight Averaging reaches 76.0% avg accuracy, exceeding both Surgery (74.3%) and the dedicated multi-task learning method KD4MTL (75.5%). In the one-to-all setting, a single ProbSurgery module with PDA (82.4%) outperforms eight independent Surgery modules (80.9%), demonstrating superior parameter efficiency. ProbSurgery also shows stronger generalization: under domain shift, it achieves +5.4% improvement on other-domain data versus only +0.7% for Surgery, and on OOD detection it boosts AUROC from 69.5% to 76.1% under Weight Averaging.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends Surgery (Yang et al., ICML 2024), which introduced a deterministic task-specific MLP to correct representation bias in merged models, by reformulating the bias as a Gaussian distribution parameterized by an amortized MLP and sampled via the reparameterization trick. The theoretical grounding draws on PAC-Bayes bounds (McAllester, 2003) to show that the probabilistic formulation yields a strictly tighter generalization error bound than the deterministic counterpart. Within the broader model merging landscape (Task Arithmetic, Ties-Merging, AdaMerging, EMR-Merging), ProbSurgery occupies the post-merging calibration stage, positioning itself as a drop-in enhancement rather than a new merging strategy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The one-to-all finding—that a single ProbSurgery module outperforms eight task-specific Surgery modules—is practically significant for deployment, eliminating the need for task ID at inference and reducing parameter overhead as task count grows. However, the approach still requires access to individual expert model representations during the self-supervised calibration phase, limiting its applicability when only the merged model is available. Open questions include whether the Gaussian assumption captures the full structure of multi-model interference, whether the method scales to dozens or hundreds of merged tasks, and whether the PAC-Bayes bound's advantage translates to meaningful gains in very high-dimensional or non-convex settings.
