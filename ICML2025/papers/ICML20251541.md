## Problem

Existing jailbreak attacks on LLMs suffer from three key limitations: white-box methods require model weights and intensive optimization, iterative black-box methods (e.g., PAIR, ReNeLLM) demand many queries and high token costs, and other black-box methods rely on complex auxiliary tasks like ciphering or coding that LLMs frequently fail to execute. This paper seeks a universal, stealthy, and simple black-box jailbreak that operates in a single query without external perturbation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251541/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single harmful text prompt (e.g., 'How to make a bomb?') and produces a disguised prompt by flipping its characters or words, then appends a guidance instruction telling the LLM to reverse the flip and comply. The entire attack is delivered as one query to the target LLM's API, with no iterative refinement. The perturbation is constructed solely from the prompt's own characters—no external noise, random tokens, or auxiliary encodings are introduced—making the disguised prompt both stealthy (high perplexity to guard models) and recoverable by the target LLM.

## Method

FlipAttack exploits the autoregressive left-to-right processing bias of LLMs: introducing perturbation at the left side of a sentence degrades understanding far more than at the right side (verified via perplexity experiments showing ~10× higher PPL for left-side noise). The Attack Disguise Module iteratively moves each character to the left of the remaining sequence, generalized into four flipping modes (Flip Word Order, Flip Characters in Word, Flip Characters in Sentence, and Fool Model Mode which misleads the LLM about the recovery rule). The Flipping Guidance Module then instructs the LLM to reverse the flip and execute the original harmful intent, using progressively stronger scaffolding—vanilla instruction, chain-of-thought step-by-step recovery, LangGPT role-playing structure, and task-oriented few-shot demonstrations built by splitting the original prompt in half. Because the flipping task is trivially simple compared to ciphers or code, even weaker LLMs can recover the prompt, while the flipped text achieves the highest perplexity among all tested obfuscation methods, effectively bypassing guard models with ~98% average bypass rate.

## Results

FlipAttack achieves a 78.97% average attack success rate (ASR-GPT) across 8 LLMs, surpassing the runner-up black-box method ReNeLLM (56.64%) by 22.33 percentage points. Notably, it reaches 94.04% ASR on GPT-4 Turbo, 86.73% on GPT-4, and 88.08% on Claude 3.5 Sonnet, all with a single query. Against 5 guard models (LLaMA Guard 7B/2/3, WildGuard 7B, OpenAI Moderation), FlipAttack achieves a 98.08% average bypass rate. The method requires only 311 input tokens per attack, compared to 5,685 tokens for ReNeLLM and >24 GPU hours for white-box methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FlipAttack decisively outperforms all 15 baselines on average ASR-GPT (78.97% vs. 56.64% for the runner-up ReNeLLM). Among white-box methods, GCG (7.40%), MAC (6.20%), and COLD-Attack (5.60%) are nearly ineffective on commercial LLMs due to poor transferability, while AutoDAN (37.04%) performs moderately but still trails FlipAttack by over 40 points. Among black-box iterative methods, ReNeLLM (56.64%) is the strongest competitor but requires multiple queries (5,685 tokens), whereas PAIR (20.79%) and TAP (29.58%) are substantially weaker. Among single-query black-box methods, CodeChameleon (56.60%) is the closest rival, matching FlipAttack on GPT-4 Turbo (92.64% vs. 94.04%) but falling far behind on Claude 3.5 Sonnet (20.77% vs. 88.08%) and GPT-4 (22.27% vs. 86.73%). GPTFuzzer (39.12%) and DeepInception (23.30%) are mid-tier, while SelfCipher (5.22%), ArtPrompt (5.44%), and PromptAttack (2.16%) are largely ineffective, confirming that complex auxiliary tasks (ciphers, art words) hinder attack reliability.

## Relation to Prior Work

FlipAttack carves new ground by exploiting a mechanistic property of autoregressive LLMs—left-to-right reading bias—to construct a single-query black-box jailbreak, departing from all three dominant attack paradigms: white-box gradient-based optimization (GCG, AutoDAN), iterative multi-query refinement (PAIR, ReNeLLM), and complex auxiliary-task encoding (SelfCipher, CodeChameleon). Rather than introducing external perturbations or requiring model internals, it repurposes the prompt's own characters as perturbation through simple flipping, a conceptual departure from prior methods that rely on ciphers, code, or art words to obscure harmful content. The left-side perturbation insight, validated via perplexity experiments, provides a principled rationale absent in prior black-box attacks that treat the prompt as an opaque string.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~98% guard-model bypass rate and 79% average ASR with a single query highlight a significant practical vulnerability in current LLM safety pipelines, particularly for commercial APIs where iterative attacks are costly. However, the paper's own defense experiments show that simple countermeasures (system prompts, perplexity thresholds) are largely ineffective, leaving open the question of what architectural or training-level changes could robustly defend against this class of perturbation-based attacks. Limitations include reduced performance on physical-harm categories and weaker LLMs (LLaMA 3.1 405B at 27.5%), and the few-shot demonstration component risks reintroducing detectable harmful tokens, suggesting the method's stealthiness is not absolute.
