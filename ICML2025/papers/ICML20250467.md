## Problem

The ConfDiff classification method (Wang et al., 2024) uses a contrastive loss structure that forces pairwise instances to be predicted in opposite directions, which is valid when confidence differences are large but introduces unreliable supervision when they are small—instances with small |c| may belong to the same class yet are still pushed apart. This paper identifies and addresses this gap by analyzing how different confidence difference magnitudes reflect varying levels of supervisory signal reliability.

## Input / Output

The method consumes pairwise unlabeled instances (x_i, x_i') drawn i.i.d. from p(x) along with a scalar confidence difference c_i = p(y_i'=+1|x_i') − p(y_i=+1|x_i), which is generated by a pre-trained logistic regression classifier on a small labeled subset. The output is a binary classifier g: R^d → {−1, +1}. Experiments span grayscale 28×28 images (MNIST, K-MNIST, F-MNIST), RGB 3×32×32 images (CIFAR-10), and low-dimensional tabular data (Optdigits: 62 features; Pendigits: 16 features), with multi-class labels collapsed into positive/negative groups.

## Method

CRCR (Consistency Risk and Consistency Regularization) partitions the pairwise dataset by a threshold θ into D^S (|c| > θ, reliable) and D^C (|c| ≤ θ, less reliable). For D^S, the original ConfDiff risk estimator is retained to preserve strong contrastive supervision. For D^C, a consistency regularization term α · (1/log(|c_i| + ε)) · ‖g(x_i) − g(x_i')‖₂ is added, encouraging similar model outputs for pairs whose confidence difference is too small to reliably indicate different classes. The theoretical analysis shows that the ConfDiff loss decomposes into contrastive terms with weights (1/2 − c) and (1/2 + c) that inherently push predictions apart, plus a regularization term; the error bound is derived via Rademacher complexity with convergence rate O(max(1/√n₁, 1/√n₂)). An empirical risk correction (absolute value or ReLU) is applied to each loss component to prevent overfitting from negative unbiased risk estimates.

## Results

On the balanced setting (π=0.5, no noise), CRCR-ABS achieves the best accuracy across all six datasets: 96.2% on MNIST, 84.8% on K-MNIST, 95.5% on F-MNIST, 86.9% on CIFAR-10, 75.3% on Pendigits, and 82.3% on Optdigits. The method outperforms the strongest ConfDiff baseline (ConfDiff-ABS) by 1.8–7.8 percentage points and the strongest Pcomp baseline (Pcomp-Teacher) by 8.0–36.7 percentage points. Under 100% artificial noise, CRCR-ABS maintains 96.0% on MNIST and 85.1% on CIFAR-10, while ConfDiff-ABS drops to 93.4% and 83.5% respectively, demonstrating superior noise robustness.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CRCR-ABS consistently outperforms both Pcomp and ConfDiff baselines across nearly all dataset–noise configurations. Against the strongest ConfDiff variant (ConfDiff-ABS), CRCR-ABS gains 1.8 pp on MNIST (96.2% vs 94.4%), 2.3 pp on K-MNIST (84.8% vs 82.5%), 0.3 pp on F-MNIST (95.5% vs 95.2%), 2.1 pp on CIFAR-10 (86.9% vs 84.8%), 7.8 pp on Pendigits (75.3% vs 67.5%), and 2.4 pp on Optdigits (82.3% vs 79.9%) under no noise. Against the strongest Pcomp variant (Pcomp-Teacher), CRCR-ABS leads by 8.0 pp on MNIST, 14.0 pp on K-MNIST, 6.8 pp on F-MNIST, 5.7 pp on CIFAR-10, 25.7 pp on Pendigits, and 31.6 pp on Optdigits. The sole exception is Pcomp-Unbiased on Pendigits at m=0 (77.5% vs 75.3%), which the authors attribute to Pcomp's simpler pairwise comparison signal being less affected by the posterior-probability reconstruction step. Under 100% noise, CRCR-ABS maintains a clear lead over ConfDiff-ABS on all four image datasets (e.g., 96.0% vs 93.4% on MNIST) while ConfDiff-ReLU and Pcomp-ReLU degrade to near-random performance (~80%) on imbalanced settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the confidence-difference (ConfDiff) classification framework introduced by Wang et al. (2024), which itself extends the pairwise comparison (Pcomp) paradigm of Feng et al. (2021) by incorporating the magnitude of posterior probability differences rather than just binary ordering. The key departure is a structural analysis of the ConfDiff loss revealing that its contrastive weighting (1/2 ± c) forces pairwise instances toward opposite predictions regardless of whether they truly belong to different classes, a problem that becomes acute when |c| is small. The proposed fix—partitioning by confidence-difference magnitude and applying consistency regularization to the unreliable subset—borrows the consistency-regularization principle from semi-supervised learning and the risk-correction (ABS/ReLU) machinery from the unbiased risk estimation literature in PU learning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a concrete failure mode in ConfDiff classification (noisy supervision from small confidence differences) and provides a principled, theoretically grounded remedy with Rademacher-complexity error bounds, which is valuable for the growing body of pairwise weakly supervised learning. However, the practical impact is bounded: the method still requires knowledge of the class prior π, a manually tuned threshold θ, and confidence differences that are themselves estimated from a pre-trained classifier, introducing a circular dependency. Open questions include whether the partitioning strategy generalizes to multi-class settings, how sensitive performance is to miscalibrated confidence differences in truly noisy annotation regimes, and whether the consistency regularization could be replaced by learned, instance-adaptive weighting rather than a hard threshold.
