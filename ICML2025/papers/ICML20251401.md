## Problem

Existing joint advertising auction mechanisms (VCG, JAMA, JRegNet) establish incentive compatibility only along individual bidder dimensions and overlook the bundle structure that actually determines slot allocation, leaving revenue on the table. Moreover, RegretNet-style approaches like JRegNet suffer from poor generalization in large bipartite settings and often underperform VCG. This paper identifies the exact optimal mechanism for single-slot joint advertising and proposes a bundle-aware neural architecture for the multi-slot case.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a bipartite graph G=(R,S,E) where each edge e=(r,s) is a bundle, along with per-bidder bid vectors scaled by the slot CTR vector λ to form CPC feature vectors X_r = b_r·λ ∈ R^m_{≥0} and X_s = b_s·λ ∈ R^m_{≥0}. It produces a doubly stochastic allocation matrix A ∈ [0,1]^{n×m} (each bundle to at most one slot, each slot to at most one bundle) and per-bidder payments p_r^e, p_s^e that are guaranteed non-negative and bounded by the allocated CTR-weighted bid, ensuring ex-post individual rationality by construction.

## Method

BundleNet fuses retailer and supplier node features into edge-level representations via two operations: Divided Bids (concatenation [X_r, X_s] ∈ R^{2m}) and Stacked Bids (sum X_r + X_s ∈ R^m). The Allocation Network applies an MLP to Stacked Bids, then projects through two weight matrices and applies row-wise and column-wise softmax before taking the element-wise minimum to produce a doubly stochastic allocation matrix. The Payment Network applies a sigmoid-activated MLP to Divided Bids to yield normalized payment fractions, which are scaled by the CTR-weighted allocated bid to guarantee IR. The key theoretical contribution is a bundle-level IC constraint (rgt^e) that sums the retailer and supplier regret terms per bundle; Lemma 5.1 proves this encloses the individual-bidder IC constraints, so driving bundle regret to zero ensures full incentive compatibility. Training uses an augmented Lagrangian loss with adversarial updates of misreport values v'_r, v'_s to maximize the regret penalty, alternating with gradient descent on network parameters.

## Results

In the single-slot setting (Table 1), BundleNet closely approximates the theoretical optimal mechanism, achieving revenue of 0.8802 vs. the optimal 0.8819 under U(0,1) with 5 bundles, while JRegNet reaches only 0.7882. In the multi-slot setting (Table 2, 5 slots, CTRs = (1, 0.8, 0.6, 0.4, 0.2)), BundleNet achieves revenue of 2.6495 with 12 bundles under U(0,1), substantially outperforming JRegNet (1.9973) and RVCG (2.5868). IC violation remains below 0.001 across all settings for BundleNet. JRegNet's revenue plateaus around 2.0 as the number of bundles grows beyond 9, while BundleNet continues to scale.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against RVCG (Revised VCG from Ma et al., 2024), BundleNet consistently achieves higher revenue across all single- and multi-slot settings. In the multi-slot U(0,1) setting with 12 bundles, BundleNet earns 2.6495 vs. RVCG's 2.5868, and the gap widens at smaller scales (e.g., 1.4982 vs. 0.8420 at 5×5). Against JRegNet (Zhang et al., 2024), BundleNet shows a clear and growing advantage in the multi-slot setting: at 12×5 bundles, BundleNet achieves 2.6495 while JRegNet plateaus at 1.9973 (a 33% revenue gap). In the single-slot setting, JRegNet occasionally exceeds BundleNet in raw revenue (e.g., 0.5622 vs. 0.5286 at U_2), but BundleNet is consistently closer to the theoretical optimum (0.5247), indicating JRegNet over-optimizes revenue at the cost of deviating from the true optimal allocation. Under the truncated lognormal and normal distributions (Tables 4–5), BundleNet maintains its lead over JRegNet in multi-slot settings, with JRegNet's revenue declining as bundle count increases beyond 9.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Myerson's optimal auction theory to the joint advertising setting, where ad slots are allocated to bundles of (retailer, supplier) pairs rather than individual bidders, yielding a closed-form optimal mechanism for the single-slot case (Theorem 4.3). For the multi-slot case, it builds on the RegretNet/differentiable economics framework (Dütting et al., 2024) and the closest prior work JRegNet (Zhang et al., 2024), but departs from both by replacing individual-bidder IC constraints with bundle-level IC constraints and introducing a graph-based feature fusion architecture that explicitly encodes the bipartite retailer-supplier structure. The key conceptual shift is treating the bundle as the unit of incentive compatibility rather than the individual bidder, which the authors show (Lemma 5.1) yields a valid relaxation that encloses the original feasible region.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Joint advertising is a commercially significant and growing scenario (e.g., Facebook Collaborative Ads), and this paper provides the first theoretical characterization of the revenue-optimal mechanism in the single-slot case, giving a clear benchmark for future work. The bundle-level IC constraint is a clean and generalizable idea that could inform other multi-parameter auction settings where bidders form coalitions. However, all experiments are on synthetic data with small numbers of bidders (≤12 bundles), and scalability to real-world ad auctions with thousands of participants remains untested; the paper also does not address dynamic or repeated auction settings, which are more representative of actual platform operation.
