## Problem

Existing machine-learning surrogate models for multi-physics simulation are typically trained for a single prediction task (e.g., forward prediction of the solution), require retraining from scratch for each new task, and lack uncertainty quantification. This paper addresses the need for a single probabilistic framework that can perform forward prediction, inverse inference, joint data generation, and function completion across multiple coupled functions without retraining.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251618/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes M coupled functions (e.g., permeability, pressure, source term) sampled on regular meshes (64×64 in experiments), a diffusion time step t, and a binary mask specifying which function values are fixed (conditioned) versus to be generated. It outputs predictive samples of the target functions at the specified locations; by drawing multiple samples, it naturally yields confidence intervals and posterior distributions. The same trained model handles all task configurations by simply changing which functions are masked as conditioned at inference time.

## Method

ACM-FD extends DDPM to functional space by replacing white Gaussian noise with zero-mean Gaussian process noise, so diffusion and denoising operate on function values rather than pixel vectors. A random-mask, zero-regularized denoising loss trains a single FNO-based network to predict the true GP noise for unmasked (to-be-generated) entries while predicting exactly zero for masked (conditioned) entries, preventing the network from perturbing fixed inputs during generation. To make GP sampling tractable at mesh sizes up to 128×128, a multiplicative Square-Exponential kernel induces a Kronecker-product structure in the covariance matrix, allowing Cholesky decomposition to be performed on small per-dimension local kernels and the sample to be computed via a Tucker tensor product in O(m̄·Σm_d) time instead of O(m̄³). This single trained model then supports arbitrary conditional sampling, forward/inverse prediction, joint generation, and spatial completion through the same iterative denoising procedure.

## Results

Across 24 prediction tasks on four multi-physics systems (Darcy Flow, Convection Diffusion, Diffusion Reaction, Torus Fluid), ACM-FD achieves relative L2 errors ranging from 1.32e-02 to 6.06e-02, consistently outperforming task-specific neural operators (FNO, GNOT, DON, PODDON) and the multi-task Simformer. For unconditional generation, ACM-FD achieves equation errors of 0.0576 (D-F), 0.114 (C-D), and 0.0273 (T-F), comparable to a dedicated multi-function diffusion model (MFD) and an order of magnitude better than β-VAE (0.265, 0.282, 0.737). For function completion, ACM-FD reduces relative L2 error by 4–25× compared to MFD-Inpaint and 8–60× compared to classical interpolation. For uncertainty quantification, ACM-FD achieves empirical coverage probabilities within 0.02–0.07 of the target confidence level, outperforming Simformer by 0.05–0.25 in most tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against FNO (task-specific, retrained per task), ACM-FD wins on 22 of 24 tasks; FNO only edges out ACM-FD on the D-R task f1,u1→f2 (1.07e-02 vs 1.44e-02) and most T-F tasks (e.g., w0,w5→w1: 1.28e-02 vs 2.73e-02), though ACM-FD remains within 2× in those cases. Against GNOT, ACM-FD wins on all 24 tasks, with particularly large margins on D-F (e.g., a,f→u: 1.75e-02 vs 6.09e-01) and D-R (e.g., f2,u2→f1: 4.10e-02 vs 6.53e-01). Against DON, ACM-FD wins on all 24 tasks with margins of 1.2×–5×. Against Simformer (the closest multi-task competitor using random masking without zero-regularization), ACM-FD wins on all 24 prediction tasks and all UQ tasks, with ECP gaps of 0.05–0.25. Against PODDON (Appendix Table 5), ACM-FD wins on all 24 tasks, with the largest gaps on D-R (e.g., f2,u2→f1: 4.10e-02 vs 5.09e-01). For generation quality, ACM-FD matches MFD (dedicated unconditional diffusion) on equation error and MRPD, while both substantially outperform β-VAE by an order of magnitude on equation error.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends DDPM to functional space by replacing white noise with Gaussian process samples, building on the GP-as-noise idea from Kerrigan et al. (2023) but scaling it to multi-functional settings. It departs from prior diffusion-based function generation work (Lim et al., 2023; Zhang & Wonka, 2024), which targets single-function unconditional generation, by introducing a random-mask, zero-regularized denoising loss that enables arbitrary conditioning on any subset of functions. The denoising backbone is an FNO, and the Kronecker-product/Tucker-product sampling trick is borrowed from tensor algebra to make GP sampling tractable at realistic mesh sizes. Compared to Simformer (Gloeckler et al., 2024), which also uses random masking for flexible conditional generation, ACM-FD adds explicit zero-regularization on conditioned components and operates in the DDPM (rather than score-based) framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical value is a single trained model that replaces the need for separate task-specific neural operators across forward prediction, inverse inference, joint simulation, function completion, and uncertainty quantification — a meaningful simplification for multi-physics workflows. Limitations include the high training cost (~20K epochs vs. ~1K for FNO), evaluation on relatively low-dimensional PDEs (up to 7 functions on 64×64 meshes), and uncertainty calibration that, while superior to Simformer, still under-covers nominal confidence levels (e.g., 0.833 empirical vs. 0.90 nominal). Open questions include whether the approach scales to higher-dimensional or 3D systems, whether the GP noise assumption is sufficient for highly oscillatory or multi-scale fields, and how the method performs with limited training data.
