## Problem

Existing diffusion-based social recommendation models (e.g., RecDiff) assume isotropic Gaussian noise when denoising social graph embeddings, but real social data exhibits strong anisotropic directional structure. This mismatch causes representation degradation and unstable training, limiting denoising quality and recommendation accuracy. The paper asks whether a generative framework that natively handles anisotropic noise can improve social recommendation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250995/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a user-item interaction matrix R ∈ R^{|U|×|V|} and a user-user social adjacency matrix S ∈ R^{|U|×|U|}, both binary. LightGCN encodes each into d-dimensional node embeddings; the flow-matching module then operates specifically on the social embeddings E_s, producing a denoised user embedding that is combined with the collaborative item embedding via inner product to yield a scalar interaction score r̂_{u,v}. At inference, a single ODE integration step (rather than multi-step SDE sampling) reconstructs the clean embedding from a sampled noise vector.

## Method

RecFlow integrates a LightGCN encoder for both the collaborative bipartite graph and the social graph, then applies conditional flow matching on the social embeddings to learn a continuous velocity field v_θ(x_t, t) = FC²(E_s ‖ E_t) that maps noisy interpolations x_t = tz + (1−t)E_s back to clean representations. Unlike DDPM's iterative SDE denoising, the reverse process is a single ODE integration x_0 = x_t + ∫_t^0 v_θ(x_τ, τ) dτ, which preserves anisotropic directional structure in the social graph. Training jointly minimizes a BPR pairwise ranking loss on predicted interaction scores and a conditional flow-matching regression loss on the velocity field, weighted by λ_1. The key insight is that flow matching's linear interpolation path and deterministic ODE trajectory naturally align with the anisotropic vector-field structure of social data, whereas DDPM's isotropic Gaussian perturbation blurs directional information and requires many discrete steps for convergence.

## Results

RecFlow achieves the best Recall and NDCG across all three datasets: 0.725/0.438 on Ciao, 0.618/0.341 on Yelp, and 0.486/0.341 on Epinions. Compared to the closest diffusion-based competitor RecDiff, RecFlow improves Recall by 1.8% (Ciao), 3.5% (Yelp), and 5.6% (Epinions), and NDCG by 4.5% (Ciao), 10.7% (Yelp), and 1.5% (Epinions). The gains are most pronounced on Yelp, where the NDCG improvement over RecDiff reaches 10.7%, suggesting flow matching is particularly effective on larger, denser social graphs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RecFlow outperforms all 11 baselines on every metric across all three datasets. The closest competitor is RecDiff (the prior diffusion-based SR model), which RecFlow surpasses by 1.8–5.6% in Recall and 1.5–10.7% in NDCG, with the largest NDCG gap (10.7%) on Yelp. Among GNN-based social recommenders, MHCN is the strongest baseline (0.621/0.378 on Ciao, 0.567/0.292 on Yelp, 0.438/0.321 on Epinions), yet RecFlow still leads by 10.2–15.5% in Recall and 15.6–16.8% in NDCG. Traditional matrix-factorization methods (TrustMF, PMF) and early GNN approaches (DiffNet, GraphRec, DGRec, NGCF) trail RecFlow by 20–60% in Recall, confirming that the flow-matching denoising mechanism provides a substantial advantage over both hand-crafted denoising (DSL, GDMSR) and self-supervised augmentation (MHCN, SMIN, KCGN). Notably, KCGN performs anomalously poorly on Epinions (Recall 0.220, NDCG 0.146), suggesting it does not scale well to larger social graphs, while RecFlow maintains consistent gains across all dataset sizes.

## Relation to Prior Work

RecFlow builds directly on the Flow Matching framework of Lipman et al. (2022), which learns continuous velocity fields via ODEs rather than the stochastic SDEs of DDPM. It departs from the recent line of diffusion-based social recommenders (RecDiff, DiffRec, DiffuASR, CGSoRec) by replacing their isotropic Gaussian noise assumption with a conditional flow-matching trajectory that preserves anisotropic graph structure. The graph encoding backbone (LightGCN) and the BPR training paradigm are standard in the social recommendation literature, so the novelty is concentrated in the generative denoising module and its conditional formulation on social embeddings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a concrete mismatch—social graphs exhibit directional (anisotropic) noise that isotropic DDPM blurs—and shows that a flow-matching velocity field can respect that structure, yielding faster convergence and modest but consistent gains over the strongest diffusion baseline (RecDiff). However, the anisotropy evidence rests largely on a 2-D PCA visualization, and the velocity field estimator is only two fully-connected layers, leaving open whether the gains stem from the ODE formulation itself or from the conditional social-embedding guidance. Scalability beyond the three medium-sized datasets (≤100K users) and a more rigorous isolation of the anisotropy contribution remain important open questions.
