## Problem

Hybrid macro placement methods that combine constructive and adjustment strategies, such as WireMask-EA, suffer from three practical limitations: sequential adjustment-then-reconstruction incurs unnecessary full-layout rebuilding overhead, random swap mutations waste computational budget on unpromising configurations, and mask-based position evaluation scales quadratically with grid resolution. EGPlace addresses these by unifying adjustment and construction into a single guided mutation operator and reducing mask computation to linear time per module.

## Input / Output

The method takes a circuit netlist represented as a hypergraph (modules as nodes, nets as hyperedges connecting pins) and a rectangular chip placement region discretized into a B×B grid (224×224 bins in experiments). It produces 2D bottom-left-corner coordinates for each macro module, optimized under a weighted-sum objective combining Half Perimeter Wire Length (HPWL), RUDY congestion, and overlap rate, with user-tunable weights λ₁ and λ₂ to trade off congestion and overlap against wirelength.

## Method

EGPlace employs a population-based evolutionary search backbone (fitness-proportional selection, mutation-only, bounded population) whose core is a two-phase greedy repositioning-guided mutation operator. In the selection phase, each module receives a score combining its contribution to wirelength (average pin-to-net-center distance), congestion (count of pins on high-RUDY nets), and normalized overlap area; modules are then removed with probability proportional to their scores, targeting the worst-placed components. In the repositioning phase, a greedy strategy places the selected modules in an order determined by size and connectivity, choosing positions that minimize a combined wire mask (HPWL increment) and a novel continuous overlap mask (exact overlap area rather than binary overlap indicator). The key efficiency gain comes from an O(iB) overlap mask algorithm that exploits the piecewise-linear structure of module overlap along each axis and combines x- and y-direction results via outer product, reducing per-module cost from O(iB²) to O(iB) compared to naive bin-by-bin evaluation.

## Results

On the ISPD2005 benchmark (6 circuits), EGPlace achieves the best average rank (1.8) among all 11 methods, reducing HPWL by 10.8% over WireMask-EA and 9.3% over EfficientPlace while running 7.8× and 2.8× faster, respectively. On the large-scale bigblue4 circuit (1,024 macros), EGPlace achieves 61.90×10⁵ HPWL versus 86.86×10⁵ for EfficientPlace (28.7% reduction) and 82.96×10⁵ for WireMask-EA. On the Ariane RISC-V CPU benchmark (78.4% area utilization), EGPlace achieves 7.91×10⁵ HPWL and 1.24% overlap rate, substantially outperforming EfficientPlace (12.47×10⁵ HPWL, 3.37% overlap) and causing DreamPlace to fail legalization entirely.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EGPlace outperforms all analytical and simulated-annealing baselines (SP-SA, NTUPlace3, RePlace, DreamPlace) by large margins across all six ISPD2005 circuits, with HPWL reductions exceeding 50% in most cases. Against RL-based constructive methods (GraphPlace, DeepPR, MaskPlace, Chipformer), EGPlace achieves the best HPWL on all six circuits, with particularly large gains on adaptec2 (37.39 vs. 75.16 for MaskPlace, 67.10 for Chipformer). Against the two closest competitors—WireMask-EA and EfficientPlace—EGPlace wins on 3 of 6 circuits (adaptec1, adaptec2, adaptec4, bigblue3) but loses on adaptec3 and bigblue1, yielding a 10.8% and 9.3% average HPWL advantage respectively while running 7.8× and 2.8× faster. On the ICCAD2015 benchmark, EGPlace achieves 7.4% HPWL reduction over EfficientPlace and 17.4% over WireMask-EA. In mixed-size placement (EGPlace + DreamPlace 4.1.0), results are slightly inferior to DreamPlace 4.1.0 alone, which the authors acknowledge as a limitation and future work direction.

## Relation to Prior Work

EGPlace extends the hybrid evolutionary-greedy paradigm introduced by WireMask-EA (Shi et al., 2023), which combined a (1+1)-EA backbone with random swap mutations and a separate greedy reconstruction phase. The key conceptual departure is unifying adjustment and construction into a single guided mutation operator: modules are selected for repositioning based on quality-impact scores rather than random swaps, and greedy placement is applied only to the removed subset rather than rebuilding the entire layout. The efficient overlap mask computation builds on the wire-mask caching idea from MaskPlace (Lai et al., 2022) but extends it to exact (non-binary) overlap quantification, enabling better handling of high-density benchmarks where complete overlap elimination is infeasible.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The simultaneous 9–11% HPWL improvement and 2.8–7.8× speedup over the two strongest baselines (EfficientPlace and WireMask-EA) makes EGPlace a practically attractive option for industrial macro placement, where both quality and runtime are critical. The overlap mask's ability to reduce overlap from 3.27% to 1.24% on the high-density Ariane benchmark (where DreamPlace fails legalization entirely) addresses a real gap for designs with large area utilization. However, the method does not directly optimize PPA objectives and its mixed-size placement results remain slightly inferior to DreamPlace 4.1.0 alone, leaving open the question of whether integrating learned masks (LaMPlace) or regulation masks (MaskRegulate) can close that gap without sacrificing the efficiency gains.
