## Problem

The standard Soft Actor-Critic algorithm for Maximum Entropy RL relies on Gaussian policies, whose unimodality limits exploration in complex multi-goal environments where the optimal policy is inherently multimodal. While diffusion models can capture such multimodal distributions, integrating them into the SAC framework requires solving two non-trivial challenges: training the diffusion policy to approximate exp(Q/β) without access to samples from that target distribution, and computing the intractable log-probability of the diffusion policy needed for the soft Bellman backup.

## Input / Output

The method takes a continuous state vector from the environment and outputs a continuous action sampled from a diffusion policy conditioned on that state. Actions are generated by solving the probability flow ODE with the trained noise prediction network over 20 diffusion steps. For bounded action spaces, the forward noising uses a truncated Gaussian to keep all intermediate samples within valid action bounds. At inference, M=10 action candidates are sampled and the one with the highest Q-value is selected.

## Method

MaxEntDP replaces the Gaussian actor in SAC with a diffusion model, addressing two key challenges. For policy improvement, the paper derives a Q-weighted Noise Estimation (QNE) method: by decomposing the reverse transition distribution p(a₀|a_t) as a Gaussian weighted by exp(Q(a₀)/β), the score function is estimated via weighted importance sampling, yielding a training target that is a softmax-weighted sum of noise samples with weights softmax(Q/β) — requiring no gradient of Q. For policy evaluation, the log-probability of the diffusion policy is approximated via numerical integration of the exact log-likelihood formula (Kong et al., 2023), changing the integration variable to σ(α_t)∈(0,1) for stability and estimating the noise prediction error at each of T timesteps with N Monte Carlo samples. The full algorithm alternates between soft Bellman error minimization (using the approximated log-prob) and QNE-based noise prediction network updates, with a temperature parameter β controlling exploration strength per task.

## Results

On six Mujoco benchmarks (Ant, HalfCheetah, Hopper, Humanoid, Swimmer, Walker2d), MaxEntDP surpasses or matches all baselines (SAC, MEow, TD3) with substantially smaller evaluation variance across five runs. On the DeepMind Control Suite (3 high-dimensional tasks), MaxEntDP outperforms SAC, MEow, and TD3. Training time on HalfCheetah-v3 is 3.9 h (2-layer MLP) or 5.5 h (3-layer MLP), competitive with SAC (4.6 h) and QSM (1.9 h) but faster than MEow (11 h) and DACER (22.6–55.6 h).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SAC (Gaussian policy, ICML 2018), MEow (energy-based normalizing flow, NeurIPS 2024), and TD3 (deterministic, ICML 2018), MaxEntDP surpasses or matches on all six Mujoco tasks and all three DeepMind Control Suite tasks, with notably smaller evaluation variance, indicating more stable convergence. Against QSM (ICML 2024) and iDEM (ICML 2024), which both rely on Q-function gradients to estimate the score function, MaxEntDP's Q-weighted Noise Estimation exhibits significantly lower variance in target noise estimation (Figure 3b), leading to steadier learning curves without the post-peak fluctuations seen in QSM and iDEM. Among the four diffusion-based online RL baselines (QSM, DIPO, QVPO, DACER) in Figure 4, no single competitor dominates all tasks; MaxEntDP outperforms or matches the best-performing method on every individual task, demonstrating consistent sample efficiency and stability. On the AntMaze multimodal benchmark, MaxEntDP learns diverse behavior modes and achieves broader state coverage after 1 M interactions, whereas SAC converges to a single mode, confirming the advantage of the expressive diffusion policy for multi-goal exploration.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) framework by replacing its standard Gaussian policy with a diffusion model, motivated by the unimodality limitation of Gaussians in multi-goal tasks. The key methodological departure is the Q-weighted Noise Estimation (QNE) approach, which trains the diffusion policy to approximate exp(Q/β) via weighted importance sampling over noise samples, avoiding the gradient-of-Q computation required by prior methods like QSM and iDEM. The probability approximation via numerical integration over σ(α_t) (rather than α_t) is a practical contribution that makes the soft Bellman backup tractable for diffusion policies. The work sits at the intersection of MaxEnt RL (SAC, MEow) and diffusion-based generative modeling (DDPM, score-based SDEs), and explicitly positions itself against both gradient-based score matching (QSM, iDEM) and other diffusion-based online RL methods (DIPO, QVPO, DACER).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a fundamental gap in MaxEnt RL: how to use expressive multimodal policies that can capture all behavioral modes rather than collapsing to a single mode. The QNE technique and the numerical-integration-based log-probability estimator are broadly applicable beyond RL to any setting requiring diffusion models to fit an energy function or compute exact likelihoods. A key limitation is that the temperature β must be manually tuned per task, and the method requires solving a probability flow ODE at each sampling step, adding inference cost. Open questions include automatic temperature adaptation, whether the approach extends to high-dimensional or partially observable settings, and whether faster samplers (e.g., consistency models) can reduce the ODE-solving overhead without sacrificing the multimodal coverage that motivates the diffusion policy.
