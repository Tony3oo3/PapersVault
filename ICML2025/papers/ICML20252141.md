## Problem

Solving parametric PDEs requires models to generalize across varying coefficients, boundary conditions, and forcing terms, but existing data-driven approaches rely on gradient-based meta-learning or fine-tuning at inference, adding significant computational overhead. No prior work has successfully applied the in-context learning paradigm of large language models to parametric PDE solving, where a model adapts to new dynamics purely by conditioning on example trajectories without any parameter updates.

## Input / Output

Zebra takes n context trajectories (sharing the same PDE parameters but differing in initial conditions) plus a single query initial-condition snapshot as input. Each spatial frame is compressed by a VQ-VAE into a grid of discrete tokens (32 tokens per frame in 1D at 256 resolution; 256 or 128 tokens per frame in 2D at 64×64), and the full sequence is flattened with special boundary tokens. The model autoregressively generates the remaining tokens, which are detokenized back to physical space to yield a predicted trajectory; by varying the sampling temperature, it can also produce multiple samples for uncertainty quantification or generate entirely new trajectories conditioned only on context examples.

## Method

Zebra employs a two-stage pretraining pipeline: first, a VQ-VAE with a learned codebook (size 256 for 1D, 2048 for 2D) compresses each spatial frame into discrete tokens; second, a Llama-based causal transformer is trained with next-token prediction on sequences that concatenate multiple trajectories from the same environment, delimited by <bot>/<eot> and <bos>/<eos> tokens. The key insight is that this in-context pretraining strategy—where the model sees several trajectories sharing identical dynamics but different initial conditions—teaches the transformer to infer the underlying PDE parameters from context and unroll new trajectories from a query initial condition, entirely without gradient updates at inference. The generative (discrete-distribution) formulation, rather than a deterministic MSE objective, proves critical for stable autoregressive rollouts and enables uncertainty quantification. An optional acceleration module appends a [DYN] token to extract a dynamics embedding from the transformer, which conditions a lightweight UNet for frame-wise prediction, reducing inference time by 30×–150× while matching or exceeding the original token-wise accuracy.

## Results

In the one-shot in-distribution adaptation setting (Table 1), Zebra achieves the lowest relative L2 error on 5 out of 7 datasets, with particularly strong gains on the challenging 2D problems: 0.207 on Wave 2D and 0.119 on Vorticity 2D, compared to 0.777 and 0.678 for CODA respectively. On 1D datasets, Zebra is competitive with CODA on Advection (0.00794 vs. 0.00687) and Combined (0.00965 vs. 0.00857), while substantially outperforming it on Burgers (0.115 vs. 0.767) and Wave boundary (0.245 vs. 1.020). CAPE diverges entirely on both 2D datasets, and Zebra also outperforms the ViT-in-context baseline across all seven scenarios.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CODA (Kirchmeyer et al., ICML 2022), Zebra achieves substantially lower relative L2 on the four most challenging datasets: Burgers (0.115 vs. 0.767), Wave boundary (0.245 vs. 1.020), Wave 2D (0.207 vs. 0.777), and Vorticity 2D (0.119 vs. 0.678). On the simpler 1D datasets Advection and Combined, CODA retains a slight edge (0.00687 vs. 0.00794 and 0.00857 vs. 0.00965), but the gap is small. Against CAPE (Takamoto et al., ICML 2023), Zebra wins on every dataset where CAPE does not diverge, and CAPE fails entirely on both 2D benchmarks. Zebra also outperforms the two ViT-based baselines across all seven datasets, with the largest margins on Wave boundary (0.245 vs. 0.472 for ViT-in-context) and Vorticity 2D (0.119 vs. 0.173). In the out-of-distribution setting (Table 2), Zebra further extends its lead, achieving 0.15 on Wide Forcing Heat versus 1.03 for CODA and 0.47 for CAPE, and 0.32 on the far Vorticity regime where CODA diverges.

## Relation to Prior Work

Zebra repurposes the VQ-VAE + autoregressive transformer paradigm from image/video generation (VQGAN, Magvit) for physical dynamics modeling, combined with an in-context pretraining strategy borrowed from LLMs. It departs from the dominant gradient-based meta-learning line (CODA, CAPE, LEADS) by eliminating inference-time gradient updates entirely, and from prior in-context PDE work (Yang et al., 2023) by using a generative stochastic model on quantized tokens rather than a deterministic encoder-decoder on scattered points. The paper explicitly positions itself as the first successful application of causal generative modeling with quantized representations to physical systems, and avoids the temporal compression used in video generation in favor of spatial-only VQ-VAE encoding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The demonstration that in-context learning can match or exceed gradient-based adaptation for parametric PDEs—while enabling uncertainty quantification and trajectory generation—offers a conceptually simpler and faster inference pipeline. However, the framework is validated only on seven synthetic PDE datasets at modest resolutions (256 for 1D, 64×64 for 2D), and the authors acknowledge significant limitations: reconstruction fidelity for high-frequency phenomena, inability to handle irregular grids, heavy data requirements, and quadratic attention complexity that will be prohibitive in 3D. Open questions include whether the ICL advantage persists at larger scales, on real-world PDEs, and whether the VQ-VAE bottleneck can be replaced without losing the generative benefits.
