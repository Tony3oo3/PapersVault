## Problem

LLM-based web agents are increasingly deployed with direct access to real-world environments, yet prior safety evaluations relied on text-based settings with synthetic tools, limiting their relevance to graphical web interfaces. It remains unclear whether safety alignment applied to underlying LLMs transfers to autonomous web navigation tasks, where agents must interpret screenshots, accessibility trees, and HTML to execute multi-step actions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252396/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each task provides a natural-language intent (harmful or safe) to a web agent operating within one of four BrowserGym-hosted environments (forum, e-commerce store, GitLab, retail management). The agent receives the full BrowserGym observation at each step—pruned HTML, accessibility tree, screenshot with set-of-marks annotations, action history, and error logs—and outputs a single action per step (up to 30 steps). Evaluation produces ARIA risk levels (1–4) via GPT-4o LLM judge and human annotators, binary task completion via reference answers/URLs/programs, and the Normalized Safety Score that conditions harmful completion on safe-task capability.

## Method

SAFEARENA comprises 500 paired tasks (250 harmful, 250 safe) across five harm categories (misinformation, illegal activity, harassment, cybercrime, social bias), with each harmful task paired to a near-identical safe task to disentangle agentic capability from safety. Tasks are curated via two pipelines: 150 fully human-designed and 200 generated by GPT-4o-Mini with rigorous human verification. The ARIA framework categorizes agent behavior into four risk levels (immediate refusal, delayed refusal, attempted-but-failed, successful completion), assessed by both a GPT-4o LLM judge and human annotators (Cohen's Kappa 0.96). The Normalized Safety Score (NSS) computes 1 − (harmful completions among safe-capable tasks) / (safe completions), enabling fair comparison across agents with differing capabilities. The paper further evaluates two jailbreak strategies—task decomposition (breaking a harmful intent into sequential benign substeps) and priming (starting the agent mid-trajectory at a later URL)—demonstrating that even the safest agent (Claude-3.5-Sonnet) is fully jailbroken via decomposition with an average of 1.26 attempts per task.

## Results

On SAFEARENA (250 harmful + 250 safe tasks across 4 web environments), GPT-4o completes 34.7% of harmful tasks while Qwen-2-VL-72B completes 27.3%, revealing high compliance with malicious requests. Claude-3.5-Sonnet is the safest agent with a Normalized Safety Score (NSS) of 55.0 and a 64.0% refusal rate, whereas Qwen-2-VL-72B has the lowest NSS (21.5) and near-zero refusal rate (0.7%). A task decomposition attack successfully jailbreaks Claude-3.5-Sonnet on all 49 initially refused tasks, requiring only 1.26 attempts on average.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Claude-3.5-Sonnet (Anthropic) is the clear safety leader, achieving an NSS of 55.0 and refusing 64.0% of harmful tasks, substantially outperforming GPT-4o (NSS 31.7, 31.4% refusal) and Qwen-2-VL-72B (NSS 21.5, 0.7% refusal). GPT-4o (OpenAI) shows the highest harmful task completion rate at 34.7%, making it the most dangerous agent in terms of raw capability for misuse, despite a moderate refusal rate. Qwen-2-VL-72B (Alibaba) is the least safety-aligned, with a near-zero refusal rate (0.7%) and the lowest NSS (21.5), indicating it almost never distinguishes harmful from safe tasks. Llama-3.2-90B (Meta) has the lowest overall agentic capability (lowest safe-task TCR) but a moderate refusal rate of 11.4%, making it a relatively safer alternative despite weaker task performance. GPT-4o-Mini sits in the middle with NSS 35.7 and 30.0% refusal, performing similarly to its larger counterpart GPT-4o on safety metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

SAFEARENA extends the LLM agent safety evaluation line (AgentHarm, ToolEmu, DoomArena, BrowserART) from text-based or synthetic-tool environments to realistic GUI-based web environments built on WebArena and BrowserGym. It also departs from prior web agent benchmarks (WebArena, VisualWebArena, WorkArena) that measure capability but not safety, by introducing paired safe/harmful tasks and the ARIA risk-level framework. The normalized safety score (NSS) is a novel metric that disentangles an agent's harmlessness from its raw capability, addressing a gap where prior safety benchmarks could not fairly compare agents of differing competence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that safety alignment in underlying LLMs transfers poorly to web agent tasks, and that even the safest agent (Claude-3.5-Sonnet) is fully jailbreakable via simple task decomposition (100% of 49 refused tasks, ~1.26 attempts on average), challenges the assumption that aligned LLMs provide adequate protection in agentic web settings. This has direct implications for anyone deploying LLM-based web agents in production. Key limitations include reliance on explicit (unambiguous) harmful intents, simulated rather than live websites, and brittle automatic evaluation metrics that may miss off-target harmful behavior. Open questions include evaluating agents on ambiguous intents, adversarial content injected into real webpages, and developing more open-ended safety evaluation beyond exact-match reference objects.
