## Problem

When fine-tuning foundation models on long-tailed downstream data, existing methods focus exclusively on the data imbalance in the downstream task while ignoring the parameter imbalance inherited from the foundation model's imbalanced pre-training data. This paper demonstrates that parameter imbalance plays a more critical role than data imbalance during fine-tuning and, crucially, cannot be mitigated by standard re-balancing techniques such as logit adjustment, which only correct data imbalance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250338/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard RGB image as input and produces a class label from a C-way classification. Unusually, the inference pipeline requires M fine-tuned foundation models (e.g., CLIP, OpenCLIP, MetaCLIP, each fine-tuned with Adaptformer or VPT on the same downstream data) to process the same image, and the final prediction is an ensemble of their individually logit-adjusted outputs. The method operates on ViT-B/16 backbones and is evaluated on datasets with 100–8,142 classes.

## Method

The paper frames long-tailed learning under foundation models through a causal lens, identifying the 'incomplete semantic factor'—the partial semantic information a parameter-imbalanced model captures (e.g., only a dog's head rather than its full body)—as a confounder that creates a backdoor path X←C→Y, inducing spurious correlations between inputs and labels. To eliminate this confounding, the method applies a backdoor adjustment: instead of estimating P(Y|X), it estimates P(Y|do(X)) by ensembling the logit-adjusted outputs of M diverse foundation models, each representing a different incomplete semantic factor, with uniform weighting 1/M over the factors. In practice, M=3 models (CLIP, OpenCLIP, MetaCLIP) are fine-tuned with Logit Adjustment to handle data imbalance, and their predictions are fused at inference to simultaneously correct both data and parameter imbalance. This approach achieves 1.5–2.0% average gains over state-of-the-art methods on ImageNet-LT, Places365-LT, and iNaturalist2018 without requiring additional training data.

## Results

The proposed backdoor adjustment method achieves 53.01% overall accuracy on Places365-LT, 79.57% on ImageNet-LT, and 82.01% on iNaturalist2018, using ViT-B/16 with Adaptformer (0.54M trainable parameters, 10–20 epochs). The method delivers the most balanced performance across D-Many, D-Medium, and D-Few groups, with the largest gains on tail classes (e.g., +3.49% on D-Few for ImageNet-LT vs. LIFT). The average improvement over the strongest baseline (LIFT) is approximately 1.5–2.9% per dataset.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all 8 baselines across all three datasets. Against the strongest PEFT-based competitor LIFT (Shi et al., 2024), it gains +1.51% on Places365-LT (53.01 vs. 51.5), +2.57% on ImageNet-LT (79.57 vs. 77.0), and +2.91% on iNaturalist2018 (82.01 vs. 79.1). Against VL-LTR (Tian et al., 2022), which uses extra internet-collected class descriptions, the method achieves +2.91% on Places365-LT and +2.37% on ImageNet-LT without any additional data. On iNaturalist2018, it surpasses RAC (Long et al., 2022), which also leverages retrieval-augmented extra data, by +1.81% (82.01 vs. 80.2). The method also beats GML (Suh & Seo, 2023) by +1.57% on ImageNet-LT (79.57 vs. 78.0), and substantially outperforms full-fine-tuning baselines like LiVT (Xu et al., 2023b) by +12.21% on Places365-LT and +18.67% on ImageNet-LT, while using only 0.54M trainable parameters versus 85.80M. Notably, the method achieves the most balanced per-group performance (e.g., 52.52/53.62/52.54 on Places365-LT), whereas VL-LTR achieves the highest D-Many score (54.2) but suffers significant drops on D-Medium and D-Few.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the long-tailed learning literature with foundation models (LIFT, LPT, VL-LTR) by identifying a previously overlooked source of bias: parameter imbalance inherited from imbalanced pre-training data. It builds on Logit Adjustment (LA) and Generalized Logit Adjustment (GLA) for handling data imbalance, but demonstrates these techniques are fundamentally insufficient for parameter imbalance. The methodological core draws on Pearl's backdoor criterion from causal inference, applying it to the 'incomplete semantic factor' as a confounder, and operationalizes the adjustment by ensembling multiple foundation models (CLIP, OpenCLIP, MetaCLIP) with distinct pre-training imbalances.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that parameter imbalance dominates over data imbalance in PEFT-based fine-tuning, and that it cannot be corrected by logit adjustment, challenges a common assumption in the long-tailed learning community and redirects attention to the provenance of foundation model biases. The backdoor adjustment approach is simple to implement (ensemble M models with LA) but incurs M× computational cost at inference, limiting its practicality for large-scale deployment. Open questions include whether the incomplete semantic factor can be identified without requiring multiple pre-trained models, whether the approach extends to detection/segmentation, and whether the 3× FLOPs overhead can be reduced through model distillation or selective ensembling.
