## Problem

No computationally efficient algorithm was known for online linear classification under Massart noise, where labels are flipped with probability at most η per example. Prior mistake-bound guarantees (Ben-David et al., 2009) relied on combinatorial algorithms exponential in dimension, and the paper asks whether polynomial-time online learners can achieve the near-optimal ηT + o(T) mistake bound. The paper further extends the question to a k-arm contextual bandit setting with monotone (linearly sorted) rewards, a regime between fully realizable and fully agnostic bandit models.

## Input / Output

In the classification setting, the learner receives a sequence of context vectors x^(t) ∈ R^d with ||x^(t)||_2 ≤ 1 and γ-margin (|w*·x^(t)| ≥ γ for some unit w*), along with noisy labels y^(t) ∈ {±1} satisfying Pr[y^(t) ≠ sign(w*·x^(t))] ≤ η, and outputs a predicted label ŷ^(t) = sign(w^(t)·x^(t)). In the bandit setting, the learner receives k context vectors per round, selects one arm, and observes only the scalar reward of the chosen arm (not the full reward vector), with rewards satisfying a monotone-margin condition: if w*·x_i > w*·x_j then E[r_i − r_j] ≥ Δ.

## Method

The algorithm performs online gradient descent on a margin-reweighted Leaky-ReLU loss ℓ^(t)(w) = C_Δ̃(w·x^(t); y^(t)) / max(|w^(t)·x^(t)|, τ), where C_Δ̃(t;y) = (1/2)(Δ̃|t| − yt) and Δ̃ = 1 − 2η − ε. The reweighting by the current hypothesis's margin is the key technical ingredient: without it, the adversary can present examples near the current decision boundary, causing gradient descent to stall; with it, the optimal vector w* provably accumulates negative loss on expectation (exploiting the Massart condition E[y·sign(w*·x)] ≥ Δ), while the learner's loss is bounded via standard OCO regret. For the k-arm bandit, the learner explores a random arm with probability q to construct an unbiased estimate of the full reward vector, then applies the same reweighted Leaky-ReLU loss to pairwise reward differences (r_α − r_j) with context differences (x_α − x_j), reducing the bandit problem to the online Massart classification subroutine.

## Results

The paper's main result (Theorem 1.9) shows that under a γ-margin assumption, there exists a poly(d)-time-per-round online algorithm for linear classification with Massart noise achieving a mistake bound of M(T) = ηT + O(T^{3/4}/γ). For the k-arm contextual bandit extension (Theorem 1.11), the algorithm achieves expected reward at least (k−1)/k · ΔT − O(T^{5/6}(kΔM²)^{1/3}/γ) above the random-action baseline. The 2-arm special case (Corollary 1.12) recovers the classification result: expected reward ≥ T/2 + (Δ/2)T − o(T), which translates to M(T) ≤ ηT + o(T) mistakes. The ηT term is shown to be essentially tight for computationally efficient (statistical query) algorithms.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no experimental baselines or datasets. The key theoretical comparisons are: (1) Against agnostic online learning (Ben-David et al., 2009), which gives Õ(√T·LD(H)) regret, this paper's ηT + o(T) mistake bound is strictly better whenever η is bounded away from 1/2, since √T grows faster than ηT for small η. (2) Against the random-guessing benchmark (T/2 mistakes), the algorithm improves to ηT + o(T), a meaningful gain when η < 1/2. (3) Against the prior online Massart algorithm of Ben-David et al. (2009), which achieves a similar mistake bound but requires exponential-in-d runtime (via Littlestone's Standard Optimal Algorithm), this paper provides the first poly(d)-time-per-round algorithm. (4) The offline error guarantee matches the best known results of Diakonikolas et al. (2019) and Chen et al. (2020) for Massart halfspace learning, and the ηT lower bound is supported by SQ hardness results (Diakonikolas & Kane, 2020; Nasser & Tiegel, 2022) showing no polynomial-time algorithm can beat η/polylog(1/(1−2η)) error.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the online Massart classification framework of Ben-David et al. (2009), which established the problem but only provided information-theoretically optimal yet computationally intractable (exponential-time) algorithms. It adapts the Leaky-ReLU loss from the offline Massart learning literature (Diakonikolas et al., 2019; Chen et al., 2020) and introduces a margin-based reweighting that makes online gradient descent viable where plain OGD would stall near the decision boundary. The k-arm bandit model (Massart Bandits) is a new semi-parametric formulation that deliberately sits between the realizability-based contextual bandits (Filippi et al., 2010; Abbasi-Yadkori et al., 2011) and the agnostic-classification-based approaches (Langford & Zhang, 2007; Dudik et al., 2011), avoiding both strong parametric assumptions and computational intractability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first computationally efficient algorithm for online Massart linear classification, resolving a fundamental open question and matching (up to polylog factors) the SQ lower bounds that preclude better guarantees for any polynomial-time method. The reweighted Leaky-ReLU technique has already proven valuable beyond this paper, enabling sample-optimal offline Massart classifiers in follow-up work (Chandrasekaran et al., 2024; Diakonikolas & Zarifis, 2024). A key open question is whether the (1−1/k)ΔT reward gap is optimal for k>2 arms, since the gap between random play and best-arm play can be much larger than Δ, suggesting room for improved bandit algorithms under the same model.
