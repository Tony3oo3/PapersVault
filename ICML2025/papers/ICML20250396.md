## Problem

Despite the widespread use of Adam and AdaGrad in training large language models where gradient noise is empirically heavy-tailed, no high-probability convergence guarantees with polylogarithmic dependence on the confidence level δ existed for these methods under such noise. The paper shows that without gradient clipping, Adam/AdaGrad (and their delayed variants) provably suffer from inverse-power dependence on δ in the worst case, and that clipping is necessary to recover the desired polylogarithmic behavior.

## Input / Output

The methods consume a stochastic first-order oracle returning noisy gradients ∇f_ξ(x) whose noise satisfies a bounded α-th moment condition (α ∈ (1,2], allowing unbounded variance), an initial point x_0, and a user-specified clipping level λ. The output is an iterate (or averaged iterate) with a high-probability guarantee: the optimization error or gradient norm is bounded by ε with probability at least 1−δ, where the iteration complexity depends on δ only through a polylogarithmic factor ln(K/δ). The analysis covers both norm-based (scalar stepsize) and coordinate-wise (vector stepsize) variants, with and without momentum delay.

## Method

The paper first constructs a 1D Huber-loss counterexample showing that a single large noise realization at the first (or last, for delayed variants) iteration inflates the adaptive denominator b_t, causing the effective stepsize to shrink and the high-probability complexity to degrade to inverse-power in δ. To fix this, the authors propose Clip-Adam-Norm and Clip-AdaGrad-Norm, which apply the clipping operator clip(∇f_ξ(x_t), λ) in two distinct places: (i) inside the momentum buffer m_t to prevent oversized steps, and (ii) inside the computation of the scaling factor b_t to prevent it from growing too rapidly. The key analytical insight is that clipping in b_t decouples the heavy-tailed noise from the adaptive stepsize, enabling a Bernstein-type concentration argument that yields the polylogarithmic δ-dependence. The resulting high-probability complexities for smooth convex and non-convex problems match (up to log factors) the best-known bounds for Clip-SGD, and are optimal up to logarithmic factors in the non-convex case.

## Results

The main theoretical result is that Clip-Adam(D)/Clip-M-AdaGrad(D)-Norm achieves high-probability convergence with polylogarithmic dependence on the confidence level δ (specifically A = ln(4(K+1)/δ)), yielding complexities of Õ(max{LR²/((1−β₁)³ε), (σR/((1−β₁)^{3/2}ε))^{α/(α−1)}}) in the convex case and Õ(max{(LΔ/((1−β₁)³ε))^{(3α−2)/(2α−1)}, (σ√(LΔ)/((1−β₁)^{3/2}ε))^{(3α−2)/(2α−2)}}) in the non-convex case. In contrast, unclipped Adam/AdaGrad and their delayed variants provably require T = Ω(poly(ε^{−1/2}, δ^{−1/2}, δ^{−1/3})) iterations, i.e., inverse-power dependence on δ. Empirically, on ALBERT Base v2 fine-tuning of CoLa (heavy-tailed noise), Clip-Adam produces substantially narrower 5th–95th percentile validation-loss bands than Adam, while on RTE (milder tails) the two perform comparably.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares four families of unclipped adaptive methods (Adam, M-AdaGrad, AdamD, M-AdaGradD) against their clipped counterparts (Clip-Adam, Clip-M-AdaGrad, Clip-AdamD, Clip-M-AdaGradD) on both synthetic and real-world tasks. On the 1-D quadratic problem with additive heavy-tailed noise (p(t) ∝ (1+|t|)^{−2.5}), all clipped AdaGrad variants produce visibly tighter 10th–90th percentile error bands than their unclipped versions across multiple stepsizes, confirming the theoretical prediction that clipping eliminates the inverse-power δ-dependence. On ALBERT Base v2 fine-tuning of CoLa, where gradient noise is significantly heavy-tailed (ρ_mR and ρ_eR metrics well above 1), Clip-Adam achieves a much narrower 5th–95th percentile validation-loss band than Adam, while on RTE (milder tails) the two are comparable. The delayed variants (AdamD, M-AdaGradD) show the same pattern: clipping is beneficial when noise is heavy-tailed and neutral otherwise. Theoretically, the unclipped methods are shown to have Ω(δ^{−1/2}) or Ω(δ^{−1/3}) dependence on the confidence level, whereas the clipped methods match the polylogarithmic δ-dependence of Clip-SGD up to logarithmic factors, making them the first Adam/AdaGrad-type methods with such guarantees under heavy-tailed noise without requiring sub-Gaussianity or bounded gradients.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two well-developed threads: (1) high-probability convergence of Clip-SGD under heavy-tailed noise (Gorbunov et al., 2020; Sadiev et al., 2023; Nguyen et al., 2023), and (2) convergence analysis of adaptive methods like Adam and AdaGrad (Kingma & Ba, 2014; Duchi et al., 2011; Li & Orabona, 2020). The key departure is showing that despite the structural similarity between Adam and Clip-SGD (Adam as Clip-SGD with momentum and iteration-dependent clipping), the adaptive stepsize mechanism alone does NOT provide the same high-probability protection under heavy-tailed noise. The paper extends the Clip-SGD high-probability framework to clipped adaptive methods, building on the noise model from Zhang et al. (2020) and the delayed-variant analysis from Li & Orabona (2020).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to establish that unclipped Adam/AdaGrad have fundamentally poor high-probability convergence (inverse-power in δ) under heavy-tailed noise, and the first to provide polylogarithmic-in-δ guarantees for their clipped counterparts. For LLM training where heavy-tailed gradient noise is empirically confirmed, this gives theoretical justification for the widely-used but previously unexplained practice of combining Adam with gradient clipping. Open questions include whether Assumption 1.4 (bounded objective) can be removed for non-delayed methods, whether the second term in the convex-case bound is optimal, and whether the negative results extend to arbitrary initializations and stepsize choices beyond the mild conditions stated.
