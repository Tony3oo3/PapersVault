## Problem

Existing classifiers assume class concepts are precisely defined with crisp boundaries, contradicting the cognitive science consensus that real-world concepts are inherently ambiguous. The authors' prior FLM captures concept fuzziness via exemplar theory but separates concept representation from the human knowledge system, limiting its ability to form a profound, knowledge-grounded understanding of concepts and their interrelations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250449/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 2048-dimensional feature vectors (extracted by a pre-trained backbone network) as sample input, along with class knowledge in flexible forms—domain-expert description vectors, pre-trained language model embeddings, or WordNet hypernym trees—unified as a fuzzy similarity relation (FSR) on the class space. Output is a class label produced by exemplar-based prediction: the learned FSR selects top-n exemplars per class, and a test sample is assigned to the class whose exemplars yield the highest mean predicted similarity. The intermediate learned FSR on the sample space also serves as an interpretable concept representation.

## Method

HC-HFLM mines a class FSR T from human knowledge (description vectors, WordNet, or human-annotated similarity), optionally calibrating it against data-estimated similarities via a convex quadratic program to reconcile scale and ranking differences. A novel Hierarchical Alignment (HA) loss then guides learning of a sample FSR f((·,·);Θ) by penalizing predicted pairwise similarities that fall outside an interval bounded by adjacent values in the sorted set of class-knowledge similarities, thereby preserving the rank structure of class relations. The authors develop an FSR-based quotient space theory (extending the classical FER-based framework) and prove that zero HA loss aligns the hierarchical partition structures derived from data and from class knowledge, guaranteeing that human knowledge is structurally integrated into the learned representation. Training proceeds in two stages: pre-training the FLM backbone with the original fuzziness-permissible loss, then fine-tuning with the HA loss via SGD. Prediction reuses the FLM exemplar mechanism, but the knowledge-guided FSR yields more semantically grounded exemplar selection and classification.

## Results

On six public datasets spanning coarse-grained (APY, ImageNet1K), medium-grained (AWA1, AWA2), and fine-grained (FLO, CUB) classification, CK1-HFLM (using class description vectors) achieves the best test accuracy across all six datasets, ranging from 90.23% on APY to 95.06% on FLO. The most significant gain over the FLM baseline appears on the fine-grained CUB dataset, where CK1-HFLM reaches 68.78% versus FLM's 66.19% (+2.59 pp). CK2-HFLM (using WordNet) achieves comparable or slightly better results on ImageNet1K (76.20% vs. 76.16%) but is unavailable on FLO and CUB due to missing WordNet coverage.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HC-HFLM consistently outperforms all five baselines (KNN, DT, SVM, NB, CEC) and its direct predecessor FLM on every dataset where it is evaluated. Compared to FLM, CK1-HFLM gains +0.81 pp on APY, +1.15 pp on AWA1, +0.80 pp on AWA2, +1.01 pp on FLO, and +2.59 pp on CUB, with the largest improvement on the fine-grained CUB benchmark where class knowledge is most informative. CK2-HFLM (WordNet-based) slightly edges out CK1-HFLM on ImageNet1K (76.20% vs. 76.16%) because the ImageNet class space is a subset of WordNet words, making WordNet's hypernym structure more directly applicable. On APY, AWA1, and AWA2, the domain-expert-constructed class description vectors (CK1) outperform WordNet (CK2) by 0.02–0.23 pp, suggesting task-specific knowledge is more effective than general lexical knowledge for these tasks. Classical classifiers (KNN, DT, SVM, NB) are substantially outperformed by all deep-network-based methods, with DT being the weakest baseline (e.g., 21.64% on CUB vs. 68.78% for CK1-HFLM).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Fuzzy Learning Machine (FLM, Cui & Liang 2022), which models concepts via exemplar theory and fuzzy similarity relations, by incorporating knowledge theory (Murphy & Medin 1985) to integrate class relations from human knowledge systems (WordNet, class description vectors, LLM embeddings) into the learning process. Theoretically, it generalizes the FER-based quotient space framework of Zhang & Zhang (2014) to the FSR setting, developing transitive-closure properties and hierarchical structure theorems for fuzzy similarity relations. The key departure from FLM is the hierarchical alignment (HA) loss, which uses a knowledge-derived class FSR to guide sample-level FSR learning, going beyond purely data-driven exemplar-based concept representation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled mechanism for injecting structured class knowledge into fuzzy classifiers with theoretical guarantees (alignment of data and knowledge hierarchical structures in quotient space), which is relevant for interpretable and knowledge-augmented classification. However, the empirical gains over FLM are modest (0.5–2.6% accuracy), and the method's practical utility depends on the availability and quality of class-level knowledge, which limits its applicability to open-world or novel-class scenarios. Open questions include scalability to very large class spaces (the calibration step is O(|Y|⁴) before reduction), whether the HA loss generalizes beyond pairwise similarity learning, and how the method performs when knowledge quality is poor or contradictory.
