## Problem

Dynamic regret minimization for curved losses (squared, logistic) in non-stationary online learning has remained underexplored relative to the convex case. Existing results for exp-concave losses achieve O~(d + d^{10/3} T^{1/3} P_T^{2/3}) regret via technically involved KKT-condition analysis, leaving a large gap in dimensionality dependence compared to the known lower bound of Ω(d^{1/3} T^{1/3} P_T^{2/3}). This paper asks whether a simpler analytical framework based on mixability can close this gap.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sequence of feature-label pairs (x_t, y_t) or general loss functions f_t, parameterized by a mixability coefficient η and a fixed-share parameter μ (set to 1/T). Rather than tracking a single model parameter, it maintains a probability distribution P_t over the parameter space; predictions z_t are constructed to satisfy the mixability inequality, yielding a non-positive mixability gap. For squared and least-squares losses the prediction has a closed-form expression with O(t) per-round cost, while for logistic loss the required expectation E[σ(w^T x_t)] must be evaluated via sampling, giving polynomial per-round cost.

## Method

The core algorithm is an exponential-weight method with fixed-share updates over the continuous parameter space: at each round the distribution P_t is reweighted by exp(−ηf_t(u)) and then blended with a reference Gaussian via the fixed-share step. The key analytical insight is a three-term regret decomposition—mixability gap (≤ 0 by construction), mixability regret, and comparator gap—where the latter two are bounded using a time-varying Gaussian comparator Q_t = N(u_t, σ²I_d) whose variance σ is tuned to balance path-length and smoothness terms, entirely avoiding the KKT analysis of prior work. This yields O~(d + d T^{1/3} P_T^{2/3}) dynamic regret, improving the d^{10/3} dependence to d. The algorithm is proven equivalent to a follow-the-leading-history (FLH) two-layer ensemble in which base-learners run over different time intervals and a meta-learner aggregates via Hedge over the mix loss. For general OCO with exp-concave losses, a projection step onto a set of bounded Gaussian mixtures and a quadratic surrogate loss are added to guarantee proper learning and remove the smoothness assumption.

## Results

The paper establishes a dynamic regret bound of Õ(d + dT^{1/3}P_T^{2/3}) for mixable losses via a fixed-share exponential-weight method, improving the dimensionality dependence from Õ(d^{10/3}T^{1/3}P_T^{2/3}) in prior exp-concavity-based analyses. For the 1-dimensional squared loss, the bound Õ(1 + T^{1/3}P_T^{2/3}) matches the best-known result of Baby & Wang (2021) under proper learning. For least-squares regression and logistic regression, the method achieves Õ(d + dT^{1/3}P_T^{2/3}), reducing the d-dependence by a factor of d^{7/3} compared to prior work. The general OCO extension (Theorem 3) achieves the same Õ(d + dT^{1/3}P_T^{2/3}) bound under proper learning for arbitrary exp-concave losses over bounded convex domains, without requiring smoothness.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Baby & Wang (2021) for the 1-dimensional squared loss, the paper's Corollary 1 matches the Õ(1 + T^{1/3}P_T^{2/3}) bound under proper learning, but achieves it without the KKT-based analysis. For least-squares regression, Corollary 2 improves upon Baby & Wang (2022b) by reducing the dimensionality dependence from d^{10/3} to d, at the cost of using improper learning (predictions may fall outside the linear function class). Similarly, for logistic regression, Corollary 3 improves upon Baby et al. (2023) with the same d^{10/3} → d reduction, though the time complexity becomes poly(T) rather than O(T) due to the lack of a closed-form prediction. For the general exp-concave OCO setting, Theorem 3 improves upon Baby & Wang (2022a) from Õ(d + d^{10/3}T^{1/3}P_T^{2/3}) to Õ(d + dT^{1/3}P_T^{2/3}) while additionally guaranteeing proper learning over arbitrary bounded convex domains, a property not available in the prior general result. The lower bound of Ω(max{d^{1/3}T^{1/3}P_T^{2/3}, d log T}) for d-dimensional squared loss indicates the d-dependence is still not fully optimal (d vs. d^{1/3}), but the gap is substantially narrowed from d^{10/3}.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on dynamic regret minimization for exp-concave (curved) losses initiated by Baby & Wang (2021; 2022a,b) and Baby et al. (2023), which relied on KKT-condition analysis of offline optimal reference sequences to achieve O~(d + d^{10/3} T^{1/3} P_T^{2/3}) regret. The key departure is replacing that KKT-based machinery with a mixability-based analysis of an exponential-weight fixed-share algorithm, drawing on Vovk's (2001) mixability concept and the FLH-type equivalence (Hazan & Seshadhri, 2009; Adamskiy et al., 2016) to obtain O~(d + d T^{1/3} P_T^{2/3}) regret. The analytical framework also connects to prior fixed-share analyses for convex losses (Cesa-Bianchi et al., 2012b) and exponential-weight methods in OCO (van der Hoeven et al., 2018), but extends them to time-varying Gaussian comparators in continuous spaces.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The reduction in dimensionality dependence from d^{10/3} to d closes a significant gap toward the known lower bound of Ω(d^{1/3} T^{1/3} P_T^{2/3}) and provides a simpler, more extensible analytical template that avoids the technically intricate KKT conditions. However, the main results (Sections 3–4) require improper learning and a smoothness assumption, while the proper-learning extension (Section 5) introduces a computationally challenging projection step. Open questions include whether the geometric-covering speedup (O(log T) per round) available to KKT-based FLH methods can be ported to the EW framework, how to handle the logistic loss computation efficiently, and whether the mixability framework can yield problem-dependent or parameter-free bounds analogous to those in the convex setting.
