## Problem

While generative model evaluation has received extensive attention, the reliability of the fidelity and diversity metrics themselves has been underexamined. Previous works each identified a small number of failure cases and proposed fixes, but it remained unclear whether those fixes introduced new problems or whether the fixed metrics still suffered from other known failure modes. This paper asks whether any current metric is reliable enough for practical use, and arrives at the position that all current generative fidelity and diversity metrics are flawed.

## Input / Output

The evaluation framework takes pairs of real and synthetic datasets (1000 samples each by default) and an embedding function—either a pretrained neural network for images or a simple one-hot/normalization pipeline for tabular data—and produces a binary pass/fail verdict for each of 12 metrics (6 fidelity, 6 diversity) across 15 sanity checks. Passing criteria are precisely defined numerical thresholds (e.g., metric value within 0.05 of expected value at specific points, or curve shape matching a bell/monotone pattern) and are checked programmatically over 10 repeats. The final output includes per-metric pass/fail tables, a practical usage checklist for practitioners, and a discussion of which metrics to prefer or avoid for specific evaluation needs.

## Method

The paper proposes six desiderata (purpose, hyperparameters, data efficiency, bounds, invariance, computation) and distills known failure cases from the literature into 15 sanity checks using simple artificial distributions—Gaussian mean/std shifts, Gaussian mixtures for mode collapse/dropping/invention, uniform hypercubes with varying sample sizes, hypersphere surfaces, sphere-vs-torus geometry, and two novel tabular-specific checks (Pareto tail and discrete-vs-continuous). Each check isolates a single potential failure mode and has a known correct metric value (typically 0 or 1 at extremes), enabling unambiguous pass/fail determination. All 12 metrics from six recent papers are evaluated against every check, revealing that no metric passes all checks and that the hardest checks (one-disjoint-dimension, hypercube convergence, discrete-vs-continuous) are failed by every metric. The authors conjecture that the common thread of failures is reliance on Euclidean geometry, and suggest that future metrics must incorporate topological or non-Euclidean features to overcome these limitations.

## Results

Across 15 sanity checks and 5 desiderata (D1b–D5), all six fidelity metrics and all six diversity metrics fail a large number of checks. The best-performing fidelity metric (P-Prec) still fails 18 out of 35 criteria, while the worst (C-Prec) fails 23 out of 35. For diversity metrics, the best (C-Rec) fails 19 out of 35, and the worst (IBR) fails 24 out of 35. No metric passes all checks, and every metric fails at least one check in each desiderata category except D6 (computation), which all metrics trivially satisfy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates six fidelity/diversity metric pairs against 15 sanity checks. P-Prec/P-Rec (Park & Kim, 2023) achieves the highest overall pass rate among fidelity metrics (17/35) and performs well on mode-related checks (mode collapse, dropping, invention) and outlier robustness, but fails all D4 (bounds) checks except two and fails the one-disjoint-dimension check. Density/Coverage (Naeem et al., 2020) has the best D4 (bounds) performance among fidelity metrics (9/14) and passes the scaling invariance check, but fails simultaneous mode dropping and the hypersphere surface check. I-Prec/I-Rec (Kynkäänniemi et al., 2019) passes mode-related checks but is the most vulnerable to outliers (fails Gaussian mean + outlier) and fails the scaling invariance check. IAP/IBR (Alaa et al., 2022) benefits from its neural network embedding on the one-disjoint-dimension check (the only metric close to passing) but fails mode dropping/invention and standard deviation checks. C-Prec/C-Rec (Cheema & Urner, 2023) has the lowest overall pass rate (12/35 for fidelity) and fails the sphere-vs-torus check. symPrec/symRec (Khayatkhoei & Abdalmageed, 2023) uniquely passes the hypersphere surface check but fails many basic D1b checks including mode dropping and invention. No single metric dominates across all checks, confirming the paper's position that all current metrics are flawed.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the desiderata-based evaluation tradition of Borji (2019; 2022) and Xu et al. (2018) by applying it specifically to fidelity and diversity metrics rather than demanding a single metric handle multiple qualities simultaneously. It builds on the iterative fix-and-break lineage from Sajjadi et al. (2018) through Kynkäänniemi et al. (2019), Naeem et al. (2020), Alaa et al. (2022), Cheema & Urner (2023), Khayatkhoei & Abdalmageed (2023), and Park & Kim (2023), where each work identified specific failure cases and proposed targeted fixes. The key departure is that instead of proposing yet another fix, the paper takes the position that no current metric is reliable, and formalizes this through programmatically checkable sanity checks with precise passing criteria, including novel tabular-data checks absent from prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides practitioners with a concrete checklist for when specific metrics can and cannot be trusted, which is immediately actionable for anyone evaluating synthetic data in production. The open question it leaves is what metric architecture could actually satisfy the desiderata simultaneously—the authors conjecture that going beyond Euclidean geometry (e.g., topological features) is necessary, but no solution is proposed. A key limitation is that all sanity checks use simple artificial distributions, so passing them is necessary but not sufficient for real-world reliability. The benchmark of sanity checks itself becomes a useful standard that future metric proposals should be evaluated against.
