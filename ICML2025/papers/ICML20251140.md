## Problem

Whether scaling video generation models can lead to the discovery of fundamental physical laws purely from visual observations remains unverified. While video generation is widely considered a promising path toward world models, it is unclear whether these models learn principled physical rules or merely memorize and interpolate training data. This paper provides the first systematic evaluation of video generation models' physical law discovery across in-distribution, out-of-distribution, and combinatorial generalization scenarios.

## Input / Output

The model conditions on 1 or 3 initial frames from a 2D physics simulation (128×128 or 256×256 resolution, 32 total frames), which are zero-padded to full video length and accompanied by a binary mask indicating which frames serve as conditions. The output is the remaining 29 or 31 frames, generated by denoising in the VAE latent space. The VAE compresses videos spatiotemporally, and the DiT operates on flattened spacetime patches with 3D RoPE positional embeddings, treating the entire spatio-temporal sequence uniformly without separating spatial and temporal dimensions.

## Method

The paper constructs a 2D simulation testbed using Box2D and PHYRE to generate deterministic videos governed by classical mechanics (uniform motion, elastic collision, parabolic motion, and multi-object free-fall with collisions), then trains diffusion-based video generation models from scratch using a (2+1)D VAE (extended from SD1.5-VAE with causal 3D blocks) for compression and a DiT architecture with 3D RoPE for denoising, scaling from 22M to 456M parameters and 30K to 6M training samples. The central finding is that ID generalization approaches ground-truth accuracy with scaling, but OOD generalization fails regardless of data or model size, revealing that models exhibit 'case-based' generalization—mimicking the closest training example—rather than abstracting universal physical rules. Through controlled pairwise experiments, they discover an attribute prioritization hierarchy (color > size > velocity > shape) that governs how the model retrieves training examples, which they hypothesize reflects the magnitude of pixel-space variation each attribute change induces. For combinatorial generalization, increasing the diversity of object combinations in training data (from 6 to 60 templates out of 70 possible) reduces physically implausible outputs from 67% to 10%, suggesting that scaling combination diversity is more effective than scaling data volume or model size alone.

## Results

On in-distribution (ID) tasks, the DiT-L model achieves a velocity error of 0.012 with 3M training samples, nearly matching the ground-truth parsing error of 0.010. On out-of-distribution (OOD) tasks, the same model's error jumps to 0.427 (an order of magnitude higher), and scaling data or model size does not reduce this gap. For combinatorial generalization on the PHYRE simulator, increasing training templates from 6 to 60 (0.6M to 6M videos) reduces the human-judged abnormal rate from 67% to 10%, while FVD on out-of-template videos improves from 22.1 to 18.7.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper fine-tunes Stable Video Diffusion (SVD) on the uniform motion task as the sole external baseline. The paper's DiT-B (trained from scratch) outperforms SVD-Finetune on both ID (velocity error 0.0138 vs. 0.0505) and OOD (0.3583 vs. 0.9081), indicating that the large gap between SVD's internet-video pretraining distribution and the simplified kinematic testbed harms its performance. However, both models exhibit the same qualitative failure: OOD errors remain an order of magnitude above ID errors, confirming that the inability to extrapolate physical laws is not an artifact of the from-scratch training protocol but a fundamental limitation of the diffusion-based video generation paradigm. The SVD VAE reconstruction error (0.0107) is comparable to the ground-truth parsing error (0.0104), ruling out VAE compression as the source of OOD failure.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adopts the Sora-style VAE + DiT architecture for video generation but repurposes it as a controlled probe for physical law discovery rather than a quality benchmark. It connects two previously separate threads: the world model literature (Dreamer, Genie, GAIA-1) that assumes video generation can learn environment dynamics, and the LLM generalization literature (Hu et al., 2024) that distinguishes case-based from rule-based reasoning. The evaluation framework—categorizing generalization into ID, OOD, and combinatorial settings—borrows from the OOD generalization literature in vision (Schott et al., 2021) but applies it to a novel domain of deterministic physical simulation using Box2D and PHYRE, enabling quantitative velocity-error metrics that prior video-physical-reasoning benchmarks (VideoPhy, CLEVRER) could not provide.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central finding—that scaling data (30K→6M) and model size (22M→456M) consistently improves ID and combinatorial generalization but fails to reduce OOD error by even a factor of two—directly challenges the implicit assumption in Sora's framing that sufficient scaling will yield world models that obey physical laws. The mechanistic discovery that models perform 'case-based' retrieval with a fixed attribute hierarchy (color > size > velocity > shape) rather than abstracting universal rules provides a concrete explanation for why current video generators struggle with object consistency and physical plausibility. The actionable insight that combinatorial diversity in training data (6→60 templates) reduces abnormal rates from 67% to 10% offers a practical scaling direction distinct from raw data volume. Open questions include whether incorporating explicit physical priors, structured latent states, or hybrid symbolic-neural architectures can bridge the OOD gap, and whether these findings transfer to 3D and real-world video.
