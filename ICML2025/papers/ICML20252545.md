## Problem

Existing neural adaptation methods for brain-computer interfaces (e.g., NoMAD, Cycle-GAN, ERDiff) rely on prior distributional assumptions or adversarial training that break down when only a handful of target trials are available, causing decoding performance to collapse below 5 trials. This limits practical BCI deployment where minimal recalibration is needed over extended chronic implantation periods.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252545/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-channel extracellular spike recordings from motor cortex, processed into short-term context windows of w time points (50 ms firing-rate bins) per channel, with the source domain providing ~200 labeled trials and the target domain supplying only a few unlabeled trials (typically ≤5 at r=0.02). The output is a one-step Euler-sampled latent state z(1) that feeds a fixed linear decoder (η*) to produce 2D cursor velocity predictions; the straight-line flow path design means decoding requires a single forward pass through the velocity field rather than iterative ODE integration.

## Method

FDA is a two-phase framework: (1) supervised pre-training uses conditional flow matching, where a transformer extracts conditional features from context windows and a residual-MLP velocity field learns to flow from Gaussian noise toward behavior-labeled latent states along a linear interpolation path; (2) unsupervised fine-tuning freezes the flow network and updates only the conditional feature extractor via either source-free log-likelihood maximization (FDA-MLA, exploiting the explicit change-of-variables formula to reduce the objective to minimizing the log-determinant of the velocity Jacobian) or MMD alignment (FDA-MMD). The key theoretical contribution is a Lyapunov-based stability proof (Theorem 3.1) showing that Lipschitz-continuous activations and regularized scale coefficients guarantee latent state deviations decay geometrically, keeping representations within an invariant bounded set. This stability, combined with the absence of Gaussian prior assumptions, is what enables robust adaptation with fewer than five target trials.

## Results

On the CO-M and RT-M motor cortex datasets with r=0.02 (fewer than 5 target trials), FDA-MMD achieves average R² of 45.59±5.15% and 42.08±6.31% across target sessions, respectively, while FDA-MLA (source-free) reaches 44.33% and 41.73%. Baselines without alignment (LSTM, CEBRA) frequently produce negative R² scores, and ERDiff often suffers gradient explosions in few-trial settings. The paper reports an overall ~15% R² improvement over the best existing alignment methods (NoMAD, Cycle-GAN) in the few-trial regime, with FDA's advantage diminishing only when r increases to ~0.3 (~60 trials).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FDA (both MLA and MMD variants) consistently outperforms all five baselines across the vast majority of target sessions at r=0.02. Against NoMAD (Karpowicz et al., 2022), FDA-MMD achieves 45.59% vs. NoMAD's highly variable and often negative R² on CO-M (e.g., −37.13% on Day29, 15.32% on Day8), with FDA winning on 10 of 11 CO-M sessions. Against Cycle-GAN (Ma et al., 2023), the gap is even larger: Cycle-GAN typically scores 0–16% on CO-M target sessions while FDA-MMD reaches 27–57%, and Cycle-GAN's adversarial training is noted to be unstable with few trials. ERDiff (Wang et al., 2023b) frequently produces negative R² (e.g., −47.41% on CO-M Day14) due to gradient explosions in few-trial fine-tuning, while FDA maintains positive scores. Unaligned baselines LSTM and CEBRA yield predominantly negative R², confirming the necessity of alignment. In the zero-shot (no fine-tuning) setting, FDA still achieves 16.23% (CO-M) and 38.15% (RT-M) R², far exceeding NoMAD (−121.47%, −74.06%) and Cycle-GAN (−126.84%, −3.42%) without alignment, attributed to the stable latent dynamics. NDT-2 (Ye et al., 2024) achieves high intra-session R² (89–94%) but degrades to −34.70% (CO-C) and −52.31% (CO-M) in zero-shot inter-session transfer, whereas FDA without alignment remains positive.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes conditional flow matching (Lipman et al., 2023; Liu et al., 2023b) — previously used for image generation and distribution transfer — as a mechanism for learning neural representations in brain-computer interfaces, departing from the dominant paradigms of VAE-based latent alignment (NoMAD, ERDiff) and adversarial signal alignment (Cycle-GAN). The velocity field parameterization follows the residual MLP design with scale coefficients from SIT (Ma et al., 2024), but the key conceptual departure is the theoretical stability guarantee via Lyapunov exponents and the source-free alignment through direct log-likelihood maximization, which avoids the Gaussian posterior assumptions that cause negative transfer in few-trial regimes. Unlike prior flow-based domain adaptation (Gong et al., 2019; Liu et al., 2023a) that maps source to target distributions, FDA learns a single conditional flow from noise to behavior-aligned representations, enabling one-step sampling and simplified likelihood computation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is substantial for chronic BCI deployment: achieving reliable decoding with fewer than 5 target trials dramatically reduces recalibration burden, a major barrier to long-term clinical use. The Lyapunov stability proof provides a principled explanation for why flow-based representations generalize across sessions without alignment, suggesting that dynamical stability in latent spaces is a sufficient condition for cross-session transfer. However, all experiments use non-human primate motor cortex data from the same subject and task, leaving cross-subject, cross-task, and clinical human data as critical open questions. The method's applicability to higher-density recording modalities (e.g., Neuropixels) and its integration with self-supervised pre-training frameworks (e.g., NDT-2) remain unexplored.
