## Problem

The iterative Linear Quadratic Regulator (iLQR) is a powerful numerical controller but has remained underexplored as a differentiable module because naively backpropagating through hundreds of iLQR iterations couples the forward and backward passes, causing memory and compute costs that scale linearly with iteration count. Prior work (DiffMPC) partially addressed this by differentiating only through the final LQR layer, but treated the converged trajectory as a constant rather than a function of the learnable parameters, yielding inexact gradients. This paper closes both gaps by deriving an exact analytical gradient for iLQR via implicit differentiation at its fixed point.

## Input / Output

The method takes a parameterized nonlinear system (dynamics f_t(x,u,θ) and cost g_t(x,u,θ)) along with an initial trajectory, and produces the converged optimal trajectory τ*(θ) together with its exact Jacobian ∂τ*/∂θ. In the visual control setting, the input is a stack of four compressed RGB frames (to capture velocity) fed through a convolutional encoder, and the output is a sequence of predicted future frames generated by a decoder conditioned on the iLQR-stepped state. The analytical gradient is computed in O(1) time with respect to the number of iLQR iterations, requiring only the final-layer Jacobians of the LQR subproblem.

## Method

DiLQR recasts the converged iLQR trajectory as a fixed point of a composite operator (X*, U*) = (F(X*,U*,θ), G(X*,U*,θ)) and applies implicit differentiation to this identity, yielding a closed-form analytical solution (Proposition 4.1) for the sensitivity matrices ∇_θ X* and ∇_θ U* that depends only on the Jacobians of F and G evaluated at the fixed point. Unlike DiffMPC, which treats the input trajectory as θ-independent, DiLQR accounts for the full θ-dependence of X*(θ) and U*(θ), producing exact gradients. A forward algorithm propagates ∂D_t/∂θ across time steps by reusing the previous step's ∇_θ x_{t-1}, avoiding redundant backpropagation and achieving dozens-of-times speedup over torch.autograd.jacobian. Block-diagonal sparsity in ∂D/∂X is exploited to avoid instantiating T² submatrices, and the computation of ∂H/∂D terms is parallelized via batches of binary loss functions. The resulting backward pass has constant cost regardless of iLQR iteration count, enabling up to 128× speedup over auto-differentiation and 10⁶× improvement in imitation loss over standard neural network policies.

## Results

DiLQR achieves up to 128× backward-pass speedup over AutoDiff (0.067 s vs. 8.57 s at horizon 10, 300 iterations) while maintaining a minimum 21× speedup even at 50 iterations. In imitation learning, DiLQR.dx outperforms the NN baseline by 10⁶× (pendulum) and 10⁴× (cartpole) in imitation loss. For model recovery, DiLQR reduces cost-function model loss by 32% and dynamics model loss by 41% relative to DiffMPC, while also producing far fewer non-physical parameter values (2.76% vs. 16.85% negative-value ratio at train=100).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DiffMPC (Amos et al., 2018), DiLQR wins on every reported metric: it reduces cost-function model loss by 32%, achieves 41% lower dynamics model loss (train=50), and produces dramatically fewer non-physical parameters (2.76% vs. 16.85% bad-value ratio at train=100). The paper attributes this to DiLQR's exact analytical gradient via implicit differentiation, whereas DiffMPC treats the fixed-point input as a constant. Against SafePDP (Jin et al., 2021) and IDOC (Xu et al., 2024a), DiLQR shows a clear training-loss advantage once expert trajectories are generated by iLQR (rather than an oracle IPOPT solver) and initial dynamics parameters are matched; under those aligned conditions DiLQR reaches significantly lower final loss on the Cartpole task. DiLQR also outperforms the NN and SysId baselines in imitation loss, with the dx mode achieving orders-of-magnitude improvement over NN, while the dcost mode is slightly weaker than SysId (which has access to state estimates) but still competitive.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the differentiable MPC line initiated by DiffMPC (Amos et al., 2018), which first provided analytical gradients for LQR but approximated the fixed-point input as a constant. By adopting the implicit-differentiation-at-fixed-point principle from Deep Equilibrium Models (Bai et al., 2019) and applying it to the iLQR recursion, DiLQR treats the optimal trajectory as an explicit function of the learnable parameters, yielding exact analytical gradients rather than the one-step approximation. It also departs from Pontryagin-based differentiable programming (Jin et al., 2020, 2021) and IDOC (Xu et al., 2024a), which assume a broad range of forward-pass solutions and do not align backward-pass gradients with the actual forward solution, and introduces a forward algorithm for computing ∂D/∂θ that exploits temporal recurrence to avoid redundant backpropagation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The O(1) backward-pass complexity (independent of iLQR iteration count) is a practical enabler for embedding differentiable controllers in larger end-to-end pipelines, and the demonstrated 128× speedup and substantially lower model loss over DiffMPC make this a drop-in upgrade for existing differentiable-control stacks. However, the method requires iLQR to converge to a fixed point and access to second-order dynamics derivatives, limiting applicability to systems where these conditions hold. Open questions include whether the approach extends to non-convergent or stochastic iLQR variants, how it scales to high-dimensional multi-contact robotics, and whether integration with RL (actor-critic) or perceptual frameworks like DiffTORI can close the gap to fully data-driven control.
