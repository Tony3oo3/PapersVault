## Problem

Domain generalization methods that seek flatter minima (e.g., SWAD, DiWA, EoA) typically rely on weight averaging or ensembling of full-precision models, which inflates memory footprint and computational cost. The intersection of model quantization and domain generalization had not been explored, despite quantization inherently introducing structured noise into weights. This paper asks whether quantization-aware training can serve as a free implicit regularizer that finds flatter minima for DG while simultaneously reducing model size.

## Input / Output

The method accepts standard RGB images (or text for the WILDS Amazon dataset) and outputs class labels, with no unusual input format constraints. The key operational detail is that after 2000 training steps, all layers except the last are quantized to 7-bit precision using per-channel scaling factors, reducing the model to ~0.22× its full-precision size. For the EoQ ensemble, predictions from 5 independently trained quantized models are combined by averaging their softmax outputs before argmax.

## Method

QT-DoG applies Quantization-Aware Training (using LSQ) to a standard ERM training loop, where the quantization step size introduces bounded uniform noise into weights that acts as an implicit regularizer. The paper provides a second-order Taylor expansion argument showing that this noise disproportionately penalizes sharp minima (large Hessian eigenvalues) while leaving flat minima nearly unaffected, thereby steering optimization toward flatter, more generalizable solutions. Empirically, 7-bit precision is optimal, yielding a 4.6× smaller model with improved OOD accuracy. The EoQ variant trains 5 diverse quantized models from random initializations and combines them via bagging, achieving state-of-the-art DG accuracy at only 1.1× the memory of a single full-precision model—roughly 6× less memory than prior ensemble methods.

## Results

On the DomainBed benchmark (5 datasets), EoQ (Ensemble of Quantization, 5 quantized models at 1.1× size) achieves 68.4% average out-of-domain accuracy, outperforming the state-of-the-art EoA (68.0%, 6× size) and DiWA (68.0%, 60 models) while using ~75% less memory. The single-model QT-DoG (0.22× size) reaches 66.2% average, competitive with SWAD (66.9%, 1× size) and MIRO (65.9%, 1× size) at 4.5× smaller model size. The largest gain is on Terra Incognita, where EoQ achieves 53.2% vs. 51.9% for DiWA and 52.3% for EoA. On WILDS, QT-DoG improves in-domain accuracy from 71.9% to 79.2% (Amazon) and 93.2% to 96.4% (Camelyon17) over ERM.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EoQ (68.4% avg) outperforms both EoA (68.0%, 6 full-precision models at 6× size) and DiWA (68.0%, 60 models) by 0.4% while requiring only 1.1× memory and 5 models, a 12× reduction in training compute over DiWA. The single-model QT-DoG (66.2% avg, 0.22× size) trails SWAD (66.9%) by 0.7% but is 4.5× smaller, and beats ERM (63.8%) by 2.4% and Fishr (65.7%) by 0.5%. On Terra Incognita specifically, EoQ achieves 53.2% vs. 52.3% (EoA) and 51.9% (DiWA), the largest per-dataset margin. QT-DoG also combines additively with other DG methods: CORAL+QT-DoG improves PACS from 85.5% to 86.9% and TerraInc from 47.1% to 50.6%, while MixStyle+QT-DoG lifts TerraInc from 44.0% to 47.7%. On WILDS, QT-DoG improves in-domain accuracy by 7.3% (Amazon) and 3.2% (Camelyon17) over ERM, and out-of-domain by 2.1% and 8.1% respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate research threads: domain generalization methods that explicitly seek flatter minima (SWAD, SAM, SWA, DiWA, EoA) and quantization-aware training (QAT) traditionally used for model compression. Building on the ERM baseline from Gulrajani & Lopez-Paz (2021), it repurposes QAT as an implicit regularizer by showing that quantization-induced weight noise drives optimization toward flatter minima, drawing on the classical noise-regularization literature (An, 1996; Murray & Edwards, 1992) and the flat-minima/generalization connection (Hochreiter & Schmidhuber, 1997). The authors position this as the first explicit exploration of the quantization–DG intersection, and the EoQ ensemble extends the bagging idea to quantized models to match or exceed full-precision ensembling methods like EoA and DiWA at a fraction of the memory cost.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical appeal is that QT-DoG simultaneously improves OOD generalization and reduces model size by ~4.6×, a combination most DG methods do not offer (they typically increase compute or model count). EoQ achieves SOTA on DomainBed while using roughly 12× less training compute than DiWA and 6× less memory than EoA, making it attractive for deployment-constrained settings. Open questions remain: the optimal bit-width (7-bit here) may not transfer across datasets or architectures, the Taylor-expansion analysis of flatness is suggestive rather than rigorous, and whether mixed-precision or layer-selective quantization could further sharpen the effect is unexplored. The method's reliance on mid-training quantization timing (2000 steps) also introduces a hyperparameter that may need per-dataset tuning.
