## Problem

Layer pruning methods for LLM inference apply a fixed skip/compute decision uniformly across all tokens at a given layer, failing to exploit token-level variation in computational need. The paper asks whether the internal dynamics of token hidden states—specifically their relationship to the attention sink token—can reveal which tokens still require updates at each layer, enabling dynamic, training-free token selection.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252192/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a token sequence and, at each selected layer, reads the normalized hidden states (post-RMSNorm, pre-attention) of all tokens to compute their inner product with the sink token (position 0). It outputs a per-layer binary selection mask identifying the top-K most orthogonal tokens for full computation; unselected tokens bypass query, attention, and FFN updates but still contribute key and value vectors to the selected tokens' attention. The final output is the generated text sequence, with the selection mask serving as an intermediate control signal.

## Method

OrthoRank is motivated by the empirical finding that, after the attention sink layer, the normalized hidden state of the sink token remains nearly static across layers while other tokens' states progressively align toward it. Token importance is defined as the gradient magnitude of cosine similarity with the sink token, which simplifies (under near-equal norm assumptions) to 1 − cos²(h̄₀, h̄ᵢ), meaning tokens most orthogonal to the sink are most important to update. In practice, selection reduces to ranking tokens by |h̄₀ᵀh̄ᵢ| and computing only the K smallest (most orthogonal). This token selection is applied selectively at layers identified by an SLEB-style iterative layer-pruning procedure, so that only a subset of layers perform partial-token computation while the rest are fully pruned or fully computed. No additional modules, routers, or fine-tuning are required; the method is directly applicable to pretrained models.

## Results

At 20% sparsity on C4 perplexity, OrthoRank reduces the degradation from dense models substantially: Llama-2-13B drops from 6.73 (dense) to 8.74 (vs. SLEB's 9.42), Llama-3-8B from 9.45 to 14.95 (vs. SLEB's 16.49), and Mistral-7B from 8.38 to 11.54 (vs. SLEB's 12.39). On zero-shot accuracy (mean of PIQA, WinoGrande, HellaSwag, ARC-C, ARC-E), Llama-2-13B at 20% sparsity reaches 66.99% with OrthoRank versus 62.97% for SLEB alone. Throughput improvement is 1.18× at 20% sparsity, comparable to SLEB's proportional scaling.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OrthoRank is compared against two layer-pruning baselines: SLEB (iterative layer pruning, Song et al. 2024) and Shortened LLaMA (one-shot pruning without fine-tuning, Kim et al. 2024). Against SLEB, OrthoRank consistently achieves lower perplexity and higher zero-shot accuracy at both 10% and 20% sparsity across all seven tested models (Llama-2 7B/13B/70B, Llama-3 8B, Llama-3.1 70B, Mistral-7B, Mixtral-8×7B); the largest gap appears on Llama-3-8B at 20% sparsity (14.95 vs. 16.49 PPL; 60.84% vs. 58.41% zero-shot accuracy). Against Shortened LLaMA without fine-tuning (evaluated only at 10% sparsity due to severe degradation at 20%), OrthoRank again wins on perplexity for every model (e.g., 7.39 vs. 7.93 on Llama-2-13B) and on zero-shot accuracy (e.g., 69.71% vs. 69.72% on Llama-2-13B, a near-tie, but 70.77% vs. 69.87% on Llama-3-8B). On LongBench, OrthoRank also outperforms SLEB at matched sparsity, and on TruthfulQA it improves both MC1 and BLEU scores over SLEB across all models. Throughput is comparable to SLEB (both scale roughly linearly with sparsity), and OrthoRank avoids the 0.71× throughput penalty that attention-based token selection incurs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the attention sink phenomenon (Xiao et al., 2024b) from the attention map into the hidden-state space, discovering that the sink token's normalized hidden states remain nearly static while other tokens progressively align with it across layers. It then repurposes this geometric observation as a training-free token importance criterion, departing from both layer pruning (SLEB, Shortened LLaMA) which operates at layer granularity, and early-exit/mixture-of-depths methods which require trained routers or classifiers. The key conceptual departure is combining layer-level sparsity (which layers to modify) with token-level sparsity (which tokens to compute within those layers) using a simple inner-product-based orthogonality score, avoiding the attention computation overhead that attention-based selection incurs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that the sink token acts as a nearly fixed anchor in normalized hidden-state space while other tokens converge toward it provides a new interpretability lens on LLM internals that goes beyond attention distributions. Practically, OrthoRank offers a drop-in, training-free token selection mechanism that achieves modest but consistent perplexity and accuracy improvements over layer pruning at matched sparsity, with the added benefit of compatibility with fused-kernel implementations like FlashAttention. Open questions include whether the orthogonality criterion generalizes to non-autoregressive or non-decoder architectures, whether the fixed 33% token ratio can be made adaptive per-layer or per-segment, and whether the method extends to models where the attention sink is distributed across multiple delimiter tokens rather than concentrated on the first position.
