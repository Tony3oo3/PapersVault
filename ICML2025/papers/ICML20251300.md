## Problem

The design space of text-to-audio (TTA) models involves many correlated factors—training data, architecture, training objective, and sampling strategy—yet no systematic study has isolated the contribution of each. Existing TTA datasets are small and caption quality is inconsistent, making it difficult to assess whether improvements come from data scale, model capacity, or algorithmic choices. This paper conducts a large-scale controlled empirical sweep over diffusion and flow matching TTA models to elucidate which design decisions matter most.

## Input / Output

The model takes a natural language caption as input, encoded by a T5-base text encoder with a maximum sequence length of 512 tokens to accommodate longer synthetic captions. It produces a 10-second stereo audio waveform at 44.1 kHz sampling rate. Generation occurs in the latent space of a 156M-parameter Audio-VAE (64-dimensional latent at 21.5 Hz frame rate), which is frozen after training and used only for the final decode from latent to waveform.

## Method

ETTA follows the latent diffusion model paradigm: a VAE compresses audio into a compact latent space, and a 1.44B-parameter Diffusion Transformer (24 layers, 24 heads, width 1536) generates conditioned on T5 text embeddings. Three key technical contributions differentiate it from the Stable Audio Open baseline: (1) AF-Synthetic, a 1.35M-caption dataset generated by Audio Flamingo with CLAP-similarity filtering (≥0.45) and keyword-based quality rejection, which yields the largest single improvement; (2) ETTA-DiT, an improved DiT implementation that applies AdaLN to all sub-layers (self-attention, cross-attention, feed-forward) with FP32 enforcement, zero-initializes the final projection to match the VAE latent mean, uses RoPE with base 16384, tanh-approximated GELU, and 0.1 dropout; and (3) OT-CFM training with logit-normal timestep sampling (t ~ σ(N(0,1))) instead of v-prediction diffusion, which provides better training stability and robustness at fewer sampling steps. At inference, Euler sampling with NFE=100 and CFG scale 3.5 is selected via Pareto curve analysis balancing quality metrics (KL, IS, CLAP) against diversity (FD).

## Results

On AudioCaps, ETTA achieves FD_P=13.12, KL_S=1.22, IS_P=14.36, and CL_M=0.43, substantially outperforming its base model Stable Audio Open (FD_P=38.27, KL_S=2.23, IS_P=12.09, CL_M=0.34). On MusicCaps, ETTA reaches FD_P=10.06, KL_S=0.84, and CL_M=0.53, setting new state-of-the-art among publicly-trained models. After fine-tuning on AudioCaps for 100k steps (ETTA-FT-AC-100k), the model narrows the gap to proprietary Audiobox Sound (FD_P=10.10 vs. 8.30) while maintaining competitive IS_P (14.29 vs. 12.70). Subjective evaluation confirms ETTA's advantage, with OVL=3.43 and REL=3.68 on AudioCaps matching or exceeding all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ETTA substantially outperforms its base model Stable Audio Open across all metrics on both benchmarks (e.g., FD_P drops from 38.27 to 13.12 on AudioCaps, and from 36.42 to 10.06 on MusicCaps). Against public-data baselines, ETTA dominates: it beats AudioLDM2-large by 13+ points on FD_P (13.12 vs. 26.18) and nearly doubles IS_P (14.36 vs. 8.55) on AudioCaps, and outperforms TANGO-AF by 12.6 points on FD_P (10.06 vs. 22.69) on MusicCaps. ETTA also surpasses GenAU-L (FD_P 13.12 vs. 16.51) and Make-An-Audio 2 (FD_P 13.12 vs. 11.75, where MAA2 is slightly better on FD_P but ETTA leads on IS_P 14.36 vs. 11.16 and CL_M 0.43 vs. –). On MusicCaps, ETTA's KL_P=1.04 and IS_P=3.32 exceed proprietary music-specialist models FluxMusic (KL_P=1.25, IS_P=2.98) and MusicGen-medium (KL_P=1.22), despite using only public data. The fine-tuned variant ETTA-FT-AC-100k approaches proprietary Audiobox Sound on FD_P (10.10 vs. 8.30) while exceeding it on IS_P (14.29 vs. 12.70), though it degrades on MusicCaps (FD_P rises to 13.49), indicating a trade-off when specializing to one domain.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself as the first systematic empirical study of the design space for diffusion/flow-matching-based text-to-audio models, building directly on Stable Audio Open (Evans et al., 2024c) as its base architecture and the DiT (Peebles & Xie, 2023) transformer backbone. It extends the synthetic-caption approach pioneered by DALL-E 3 (Betker et al., 2023) and AF-AudioSet (Kong et al., 2024b) to audio, scaling it to 1.35M high-quality captions via Audio Flamingo. Rather than proposing a single novel architecture, the paper isolates and ablates each design axis—data, DiT implementation details, training objective (v-diffusion vs. OT-CFM), and sampling strategy—under a controlled protocol, filling a gap the authors identify in prior TTA work where conclusions across experiments are hard to draw.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ETTA establishes a strong public-data SOTA for text-to-audio and text-to-music generation, narrowing the gap with proprietary-data models like Audiobox and Jen-1, and the AF-Synthetic dataset (1.35M CLAP-filtered captions) is a reusable resource for the community. The key practical takeaway—that caption quality matters more than raw quantity, and that DiT implementation details (AdaLN placement, zero-init final layer, RoPE in FP32) significantly affect convergence—gives practitioners concrete levers. Open questions remain around data augmentation (caption rephrasing, audio re-mixing), better evaluation metrics that capture both accuracy and diversity in a human-aligned way, and whether the observed scaling trends (depth/width help, convFF kernel size does not) persist at much larger scales.
