## Problem

Federated full-parameter tuning of LLMs faces a fundamental tension: first-order methods (e.g., FedAvg) achieve fast convergence but require O(d) communication per round, which is prohibitive for billion-parameter models, while zeroth-order methods with shared randomness (e.g., FedKSeed) reduce communication to O(K) but suffer from high per-round computational cost and many more communication rounds to converge. No existing approach simultaneously achieves the computational efficiency of first-order optimization, the communication efficiency of shared randomness, and competitive model accuracy.

## Input / Output

Each client holds a pre-trained LLM (e.g., LLaMA-3B, LLaMA2-7B/13B) and a local dataset, and per round transmits only a single random seed plus K projected coordinates (K=4096, K≪d) to the server—roughly 10^6× fewer parameters than FedAvg. The random bases (d×K matrices) are never transmitted; they are regenerated locally from the shared seed using a truncated normal distribution with bounded 2-norm. The full d-dimensional update is reconstructed block-wise (L blocks) to keep storage at O(max{K_l, d_l}) rather than O(Kd), and the final output is the updated global model parameters redistributed to all clients.

## Method

Ferret is the first first-order federated learning method with shared randomness, operating in three steps per round: (1) each client reconstructs other clients' local updates from K projected coordinates and regenerated random bases, then averages them for global aggregation; (2) each client performs T iterations of standard first-order optimization (SGD or Adam) on its local data; (3) the resulting local update Δ is projected into K coordinates via γ = (ρK)^{-1} V^T Δ, where V is a d×K matrix of random bases sampled from a truncated normal distribution, and only the seed and γ are transmitted. The key theoretical insight is that the reconstruction Δ̃ = (ρK)^{-1} VV^T Δ is unbiased (Theorem 1) and avoids the constant bias term βε/2 inherent in zeroth-order finite-difference estimators, yielding a reconstruction error of Õ(d/K) that does not accumulate over T local iterations. A block-wise variant partitions d into L blocks with per-block bases, reducing computational complexity from O(Kd) to O(Σ K_l d_l) and enabling adaptive allocation of K_l ∝ √(‖Δ_l‖/ρ_l) to minimize total reconstruction error.

## Results

On Natural Instructions with LLaMA-3B, Ferret achieves 30.03% Rouge-L, outperforming FedKSeed (29.77%) and FedZO (29.46%), though slightly below FedAvg (32.11%). On Dolly-15K with LLaMA-3B, Ferret achieves the best score of 34.57% Rouge-L, marginally beating FedKSeed (34.56%). In terms of scalability, Ferret achieves a 6.0× reduction in per-round computational cost versus FedKSeed and a 10⁶× reduction in communication overhead versus FedAvg, while converging in only 2 rounds compared to FedKSeed's 40 rounds (a 20× reduction).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Ferret consistently outperforms all PEFT-based baselines (FedPTuning, FedPrompt, FedIT, FedIT-SGD) by 2–20+ Rouge-L points across Natural Instructions and Dolly-15K, confirming the advantage of full-parameter tuning over parameter-efficient approaches. Against the zeroth-order baseline FedKSeed (Qin et al., ICML 2024), Ferret achieves higher Rouge-L on Natural Instructions (24.99 vs. 22.33 on DataJuicer-1.3B; 30.03 vs. 29.77 on LLaMA-3B) and ties on Dolly-15K LLaMA-3B (34.57 vs. 34.56), while requiring 6.0× less computation per round and 20× fewer communication rounds. Compared to FedZO (Fang et al., 2022), Ferret wins on Natural Instructions across both model sizes and is competitive on Dolly-15K, with substantially lower computational cost (first-order vs. zeroth-order updates). Against FedAvg (McMahan et al., 2017), Ferret slightly underperforms on Rouge-L for complex tasks (e.g., 30.03 vs. 32.11 on NI/LLaMA-3B; 12.10 vs. 15.41 on CodeAlpaca/LLaMA2-7B), likely due to reconstruction error, but achieves a 10⁶× reduction in per-round communication (7.8×10³ vs. 6.0×10⁹ parameters) and comparable GPU memory usage (19.1 GB vs. 19.1 GB on LLaMA-3B). Overall, Ferret strikes the best balance among all baselines: it matches or exceeds FedKSeed in accuracy while being far more computationally efficient, and it approaches FedAvg's accuracy at a fraction of the communication cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

Ferret sits at the intersection of two established lines of federated learning research: first-order FL methods (FedAvg, FedProx) that offer computational efficiency and fast convergence but incur O(d) communication, and zeroth-order FL with shared randomness (FedZO, FedKSeed) that reduce communication to O(K) but suffer from biased gradient estimation and higher per-round compute. The key departure is showing that shared randomness need not be coupled with zeroth-order optimization; instead, random projection of first-order updates (drawing on random matrix theory à la Vershynin) achieves unbiased reconstruction with O(K) communication. The block-wise reconstruction further borrows from structured sparsity and low-rank approximation techniques (e.g., SparseGPT, BAdam) to reduce both compute and memory.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Ferret addresses a practically critical bottleneck: federated tuning of billion-parameter LLMs is currently infeasible with standard first-order FL due to O(d) communication, while existing communication-efficient alternatives (ZOO-based) are computationally expensive and converge slowly. The 10^6× communication reduction with competitive accuracy makes federated full-parameter tuning of LLMs a realistic deployment option. However, the paper explicitly defers privacy analysis to future work, and Ferret still slightly underperforms FedAvg on complex reasoning tasks (GSM8K, CodeAlpaca) due to reconstruction error, leaving open whether adaptive or higher-fidelity projection schemes can close this gap. The heterogeneous (non-IID) convergence analysis is also left to future work.
