## Problem

Standard variational inference via ELBO maximization yields approximate Bayes posteriors that are overconfident and poorly calibrated, a deficiency compounded under model misspecification. Fractional posteriors—where the likelihood is raised to a power γ < 1—offer improved robustness and calibration, but lacked a principled variational objective that naturally produces them as optimal solutions while simultaneously providing a lower bound on the marginal likelihood.

## Input / Output

The method takes a generative model (prior and likelihood), a variational family (explicit parametric or semi-implicit hierarchical with a mixing variable), and a scalar γ ∈ (0,1), and produces an approximate fractional posterior together with a lower bound on the log-evidence. For VAE experiments the input is 28×28 grayscale images (MNIST, Fashion-MNIST) and the output includes trained encoder-decoder networks whose decoders can generate images from prior samples alone. Unlike ELBO, the bound requires more than one Monte Carlo sample to be effective, and the semi-implicit variant additionally requires sampling from both the mixing distribution and the conditional posterior.

## Method

The paper derives a one-parameter lower bound L_γ on the log-evidence by applying Hölder's inequality, producing a data-fitting term (log-integral of the likelihood raised to (1−γ) under the variational density) minus a Rényi divergence regularization term. The optimal solution is exactly the fractional posterior p(D|z)^γ p(z)/Z, and the bound recovers the conventional ELBO in the limit γ→1. Two extensions are provided: a hierarchical bound L_γ^h that enables semi-implicit neural-network posteriors without requiring the marginalizing density to be known explicitly, and a joint bound L_γ^bh that simultaneously learns an approximate Bayes posterior r and a fractional posterior q within the same semi-implicit family. For VAEs, smaller γ values yield higher evidence bounds and train decoders that are better aligned with the prior, producing lower FID scores (67.8 vs. 83.5) for pure generative sampling on Fashion-MNIST.

## Results

On MNIST (VAE), the proposed L_γ bound with γ=0.1 achieves a test evidence of 1677.2±3.4 (explicit posterior) and 1678.7±3.1 (semi-implicit), substantially exceeding the ELBO (γ=1.0) values of 1583.2±14.6 and 1609.6±20.6 respectively. In the calibration study on a 2-component GMM, ELBO posteriors are overconfident (empirical coverage κ=0.928 vs. target 0.95), while fractional posteriors with γ≤0.8 achieve proper calibration (κ≥0.95). On Fashion-MNIST, VAE decoders trained with fractional posteriors (γ=10⁻³) achieve FID 67.8, outperforming both the ELBO-trained decoder (FID 83.5) and β-VAE at the same γ (FID 334.7).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the standard ELBO (γ=1.0), the proposed L_γ bound with smaller γ consistently yields higher evidence bounds on MNIST: L_γ at γ=0.1 improves test evidence from 1583.2 to 1677.2 (explicit) and from 1609.6 to 1678.7 (semi-implicit), a gain of roughly 90–95 nats. In the GMM calibration study, ELBO posteriors are overconfident (κ=0.928, below the 0.95 target), while fractional posteriors with γ≤0.8 achieve proper or conservative calibration (κ≥0.95), with the regression-calibrated R_κ strategy reaching κ=0.957. Against β-VAE on Fashion-MNIST, the proposed method is dramatically more stable and effective: at γ=10⁻³, the proposed method achieves FID 67.8 while β-VAE collapses to FID 334.7; at γ=10⁻⁵, the proposed method gives FID 68.8 versus β-VAE's 342.3. The joint bound L_γ^bh does not outperform L_γ^h in evidence, but it produces fractional posteriors significantly closer to the prior (lower Rényi divergence), confirming the constraining effect of the jointly-learned Bayes posterior r.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper derives a new one-parameter variational lower bound L_γ on the log-evidence via Hölder's inequality, positioning it as a direct generalization of the ELBO (recovered as γ→1) that has fractional/Gibbs posteriors as its optimal solution—unlike the ELBO or variational Rényi bound, both of which yield the Bayes posterior. It departs from β-VAE, which also produces fractional posteriors but through a weighted KL objective that is provably not tighter than ELBO; here the bound can be tighter. The hierarchical extension builds on semi-implicit variational inference (Yin & Zhou, 2018), but the authors show that the Rényi-divergence structure of L_γ prevents the degeneracy of the mixing distribution that plagues the ELBO-based semi-implicit construction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, lower-bound-based route to approximate fractional posteriors that simultaneously improves posterior calibration (demonstrated on GMMs) and generative quality (FID on Fashion-MNIST), without requiring PAC-Bayes machinery or likelihood modification. Key open questions include: how to select γ in practice (the authors acknowledge no universal recipe), whether the approach helps under genuine model misspecification (not directly tested), and scalability of the Monte Carlo estimates which require N_s > 1. The Rényi divergence is finite in fewer cases than KL, and the mixture-model derivation still requires ELBO for cluster assignments, limiting full generality.
