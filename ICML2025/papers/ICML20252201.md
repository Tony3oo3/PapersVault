## Problem

Existing adaptive zeroth-order optimizers like ZO-AdaMM underutilize moment information: they treat the first moment as mere velocity accumulation, ignoring its inherent variance-reduction effect on noisy ZO gradient estimates, and they compute the second moment directly from raw high-variance gradient estimates. This leads to suboptimal convergence, particularly in high-dimensional settings where ZO gradient variance is large. No prior work had analyzed the variance-reduction role of first moments in ZO optimization or refined the second moment using variance-reduced estimates.

## Input / Output

The method requires only function evaluations f(θ;ξ) (no gradient access), with gradients approximated via K random-direction finite-difference perturbations of smoothing parameter μ. The sole algorithmic change from ZO-AdaMM is replacing g_t² with m_t² in the second moment update—a one-line modification. Per-iteration computational cost is O(Kd) and memory is O(d), identical to ZO-AdaMM, making it a drop-in replacement for existing adaptive ZO pipelines.

## Method

R-AdaZO introduces two refinements to adaptive ZO optimization. First, it formally identifies and proves the variance-reduction effect of the first moment estimate m_t on ZO gradient estimates, showing the variance is reduced by a factor of (1−β₁)/(1+β₁) compared to the raw gradient estimate. Second, it refines the second moment update from v_t = β₂v_{t-1} + (1−β₂)g_t² to v_t = β₂v_{t-1} + (1−β₂)m_t², using the variance-reduced first moment to produce a more stable and accurate approximation of the variance-free ideal, enabling larger effective step sizes. The paper provides the first variance-aware convergence framework for adaptive ZO methods via Hölder's inequality decomposition, proving R-AdaZO achieves a speedup of O(((1+β₁)/(1−β₁))^{1/4}) over ZO-AdaMM when gradient estimation variance dominates, with the same O(1/√T) rate.

## Results

On the black-box adversarial attack task (MNIST), R-AdaZO requires only 2.9±0.8×10³ iterations to achieve a successful attack, a 5.4× speedup over ZO-RMSProp (15.6±3.2×10³) and ZO-AdaMM (15.5±4.1×10³). For memory-efficient LLM fine-tuning on SST-2, R-AdaZO achieves a 4.29× speedup on OPT-1.3B and 3.75× speedup on OPT-13B compared to ZO-RMSProp and ZO-AdaMM to reach the same training loss. On four synthetic functions (Quadratic, Cubic, Levy, Rosenbrock) with d=10⁴, R-AdaZO consistently converges significantly faster than all baselines while maintaining competitive optimality gaps.

## Baselines & Comparisons

R-AdaZO decisively outperforms both ZO-AdaMM and ZO-RMSProp across all three experimental settings. On the adversarial attack task, R-AdaZO achieves a 5.4× reduction in iterations (2.9k vs. ~15.5k) with a much tighter standard deviation (0.8k vs. 3.2–4.1k), indicating both faster and more stable convergence. On LLM fine-tuning (OPT-1.3B and OPT-13B on SST-2 and Copa), R-AdaZO converges 3.75–4.29× faster to the same training loss. Notably, ZO-AdaMM and ZO-RMSProp exhibit nearly identical convergence curves in all experiments, suggesting that the first moment (momentum) alone contributes minimally to adaptive ZO optimization gains; the critical improvement comes from R-AdaZO's refined second moment estimate (using m_t² instead of g_t²), which better captures the optimization geometry by reducing the impact of high-variance ZO gradient estimates.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on ZO-AdaMM (Chen et al., 2019), which first brought adaptive momentum methods (Adam-like) into the zeroth-order optimization setting. The key conceptual departure is recognizing that the first moment estimate in ZO optimization inherently performs variance reduction on the noisy gradient estimates—a property previously established only for first-order methods—and then exploiting this variance-reduced signal to refine the second moment update (replacing g_t² with m_t²). The paper also introduces the first variance-aware convergence framework for adaptive ZO methods, extending the Hölder-inequality-based decomposition used in first-order adaptive optimizer analyses (Zhang et al., 2024a; Wang et al., 2024a) to the ZO setting where gradient variance is substantially larger.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The variance-aware convergence framework is of independent interest, as it explicitly incorporates the impact of second moment estimates (measured by V) into convergence bounds for adaptive ZO methods, a gap in prior analyses that only bounded the normalized gradient norm. Practically, the one-line modification yields consistent 4–5× speedups in black-box adversarial attacks and memory-efficient LLM fine-tuning, making it immediately deployable. Open questions include whether the variance-aware framework can be tightened to remove the loose bound on the refined second moment (which the authors acknowledge limits the theoretical speedup characterization), whether the approach extends to other ZO gradient estimators beyond the spherical finite-difference scheme, and whether the benefits persist in extremely high-dimensional or highly non-smooth settings.
