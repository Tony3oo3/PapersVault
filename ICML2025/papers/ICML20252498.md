## Problem

Previous circuit analysis of fine-tuning (Prakash et al., 2024; Chhabra et al., 2024) focused on tasks where pre-trained models already perform near-perfectly, so fine-tuning merely adds a few attention heads. This paper addresses the more practical and underexplored setting where pre-trained LLMs perform poorly on a task and require substantial fine-tuning on domain-specific data, asking how circuits actually restructure under such large performance shifts.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252498/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained transformer (Pythia-1.4B, GPT-Neo-2.7B, or OPT-6.7B) and a mathematical task dataset (80% for fine-tuning, 20% held out for circuit discovery and evaluation), then produces both a fine-tuned model with non-uniform LoRA rank allocation and a structural circuit diff (added/removed nodes and edges per layer). Circuit discovery uses EAP-IG with m=5 integrated-gradient interpolation steps, selecting the top 5% of edges to ensure >70% faithfulness. A novel robustness metric (Jaccard edge similarity under 10–90% dataset perturbation) is also computed to validate circuit stability independent of prediction accuracy.

## Method

The core contribution is CircuitLoRA, which exploits the empirical finding that fine-tuning restructures circuit edges 2–4× more than nodes. In Phase 1, EAP-IG circuits are extracted from the pre-trained and fine-tuned models; edge-score differences are aggregated per layer to identify the top-5 Critical Layers. In Phase 2, higher-rank LoRA modules (r_c) with larger scaling factors are assigned to Critical Layers while standard-rank modules (r_o) are applied elsewhere, yielding ~2.46% average accuracy gain over uniform LoRA at matched parameter budgets. The paper further shows that for two-step compositional tasks (e.g., (61−45)×45), the Union Circuit—formed by edge-union of subtask circuits—approximates the true Compositional Circuit (89.2% faithfulness at 5% edges) and can drive CircuitLoRA without re-extracting a dedicated compositional circuit, enabling single-phase fine-tuning.

## Results

CircuitLoRA (r_o=8, r_c=32) at 0.72% parameter ratio achieves 82.70% on Add/Sub(300), 96.00% on Mul/Div, 92.20% on Sequence, 92.60% on LCM, and 99.40% on Function, outperforming LoRA (r_o=16) at 0.88% parameters by 4.3–8.8 points across tasks. At the higher budget (r_o=16, r_c=64, 1.42% params), CircuitLoRA reaches 83.10%, 97.00%, 94.60%, 93.00%, and 99.50% respectively, surpassing LoRA (r_o=32) at 1.75% parameters. The paper reports an average improvement of 2.46% over standard LoRA with similar parameter sizes. On the compositional two-step task, CircuitLoRA with the Union Circuit achieves 65.50% accuracy, exceeding LoRA (r_o=32) at 64.70%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against standard LoRA, CircuitLoRA consistently wins at comparable or lower parameter budgets: at the 0.72% vs. 0.88% parameter comparison, CircuitLoRA (r_o=8, r_c=32) beats LoRA (r_o=16) by 4.3 points on Add/Sub(300), 8.8 points on Sequence, and 2.1 points on LCM, while using ~18% fewer parameters. At the higher budget, CircuitLoRA (r_o=16, r_c=64) at 1.42% parameters outperforms LoRA (r_o=32) at 1.75% on all five tasks (e.g., 83.10% vs. 80.50% on Add/Sub(300), 94.60% vs. 92.70% on Sequence). Against AdaLoRA (Zhang et al., 2023), CircuitLoRA (r_o=16, r_c=64) at 1.42% parameters dominates on every task—achieving 83.10% vs. 76.70% on Add/Sub(300), 97.00% vs. 92.75% on Mul/Div, and 94.60% vs. 90.10% on Sequence—while using a smaller parameter budget (1.42% vs. 1.75%). The RandomLoRA control (same parameter allocation but random layer selection) confirms the gains are attributable to circuit-informed layer identification rather than simply increasing rank in some layers.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the circuit-analysis-during-fine-tuning line of work (Prakash et al., 2024; Chhabra et al., 2024) by deliberately targeting tasks where pre-trained models perform poorly, rather than tasks where models already achieve near-ceiling accuracy. It builds on EAP-IG (Hanna et al., 2024) for circuit discovery and LoRA (Hu et al., 2021) as the base PEFT method, but departs from prior findings that fine-tuning primarily adds new attention heads or components; here, the authors show edges are restructured far more than nodes are added or removed. The CircuitLoRA method also positions itself against adaptive rank-allocation approaches like AdaLoRA (Zhang et al., 2023), but uses circuit-level edge-change signals rather than gradient-based importance to decide where to allocate rank.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that fine-tuning predominantly reorganizes circuit edges rather than introducing new nodes offers a concrete mechanistic picture of how PEFT methods reshape model computation, and the resulting CircuitLoRA provides a practical recipe for allocating adapter rank based on interpretability signals. However, the scope is narrow: all experiments use arithmetic tasks on models up to 6.7B parameters, and the compositional-task analysis is limited to two-step operations, leaving open whether the edge-dominance pattern and union-circuit approximation generalize to non-mathematical tasks, multi-step reasoning, or larger models. The modest 2.46% average improvement over standard LoRA, while consistent, suggests the practical gains from circuit-aware allocation may be task-dependent and could shrink as base models improve.
