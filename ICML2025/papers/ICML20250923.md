## Problem

Existing diffusion transformer architectures (DiT) are designed for fixed-channel image data and do not naturally handle the variable number of physical channels, multi-scale structure, and high-resolution demands of PDE simulation data. No prior transformer backbone efficiently generalizes across diverse PDE types with different channel counts, resolutions, and boundary conditions while scaling to high-resolution grids, limiting its use as a foundation model for physics.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250923/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes sequences of 2D spatiotemporal PDE snapshots (256×256 in experiments, generated at 2048×2048) with 1 or 2 physical channels, plus conditioning vectors encoding PDE type, simulation hyperparameters (viscosity, domain extent, etc.), and in diffusion mode the flow-matching time t. In supervised mode it outputs a single predicted next snapshot; in diffusion mode it outputs samples from the full posterior by integrating a learned velocity field via explicit Euler steps. The separate-channel variant embeds each physical channel as an independent token set, so the token count scales linearly with the number of channels rather than compressing them into a fixed-size patch.

## Method

PDE-Transformer augments the DiT backbone with three key modifications: (1) a U-shaped multi-scale architecture using PixelShuffle/PixelUnshuffle for token down- and upsampling between transformer stages, (2) shifted window multi-head self-attention (window size w=8) to avoid quadratic compute scaling at high resolutions, and (3) a separate-channel (SC) representation where each physical channel is embedded independently and channels interact only through an additional channel-wise axial attention operation, preserving a constant expansion rate E(p) regardless of channel count. Conditioning is injected via adaLN-Zero blocks that receive embeddings for PDE type, per-channel type labels, simulation parameters, and diffusion time, all with 10% dropout to support both conditional and unconditional use. For probabilistic tasks, the model is trained with flow matching (rectified flow) to learn a velocity field mapping noise to the posterior, enabling multi-step sampling. The architecture also uses RMSNorm on Q/K, log-spaced relative positions within windows, and EMA-based gradient clipping for stable training.

## Results

On the 16-PDE pre-training benchmark, PDE-S achieves the best long-horizon accuracy (nRMSE₁₀ = 0.36) while training 2.4× faster than UDiT-S (7h 42m vs. 18h 30m) and using fewer parameters (33.2M vs. 58.9M). On three challenging downstream tasks from the Well repository, pre-trained PDE-S delivers an average 42% nRMSE improvement over the second-best baseline (FNO), with the largest gains on RBC (0.104 vs. 0.269) and Shear Flow (0.125 vs. 0.274). At the larger B configuration, pre-trained PDE-B achieves a 75% accumulated nRMSE reduction over the pre-trained Poseidon scOT-B model, which becomes unstable for long rollouts.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PDE-Transformer outperforms all six pre-training baselines on nRMSE₁₀: it beats UDiT-S (0.36 vs. 0.39), scOT-S (0.36 vs. 0.59), DiT-S (0.36 vs. 0.78), FactFormer (0.36 vs. 0.65), and UNet (0.36 vs. 0.68), while also training fastest at 7h 42m. On downstream tasks, pre-trained PDE-S dominates: it achieves 0.104 nRMSE on RBC vs. 0.269 for FNO and 0.520 for OFormer, and 0.125 on Shear Flow vs. 0.274 for FNO and 0.780 for OFormer; scOT-S and Galerkin Transformer are limited to square domains and cannot be evaluated on RBC or Shear Flow. The separate-channel (SC) representation amplifies the pre-training benefit, yielding 2.7×–4.4× larger improvements over the mixed-channel variant on downstream tasks. At the B scale, pre-trained PDE-B (178.97M params) substantially outperforms the comparable pre-trained Poseidon scOT-B (152.70M params), which diverges to oscillatory states during long rollouts while PDE-B maintains stable, low-error trajectories.

## Relation to Prior Work

PDE-Transformer is explicitly built on the DiT (Peebles & Xie, 2023) diffusion transformer backbone, but departs from it in several key ways: it operates on raw data rather than a learned latent space, replaces global self-attention with shifted window attention (borrowed from Swin Transformer), and introduces a U-shaped multi-scale architecture with token down/upsampling that DiT deliberately omitted. The paper positions itself as a purpose-built alternative to scOT/Poseidon (Herde et al., 2024), arguing that a diffusion transformer designed from the ground up for PDEs outperforms a neural operator transformer adapted for the task. The separate channel (SC) representation with axial attention over the channel dimension is a novel architectural contribution not present in prior PDE transformer work, motivated by the need to maintain consistent token information density across PDEs with different numbers of physical quantities.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, open-source backbone for building foundation models in physical sciences, demonstrating that pre-training on 16 idealized PDE types transfers meaningfully to complex downstream tasks (active matter, RBC, shear flow) with 42% average nRMSE improvement over the best baseline. The finding that the SC representation yields 2.7–4.4× greater transfer benefit than the MC variant is a useful design principle for multi-PDE pre-training. However, the model is currently limited to 2D regular grids and autoregressive prediction; extending to 3D, unstructured meshes, data assimilation, and inverse problems remains open. The 75% nRMSE reduction over Poseidon's scOT-B on long rollouts is a large gap, though partly attributable to scOT-B's instability rather than a fundamental architectural superiority.
