## Problem

Fine-tuning pre-trained diffusion models for human preferences has been largely empirical, with RL-based methods like DPOK and DDPO lacking convergence guarantees and failing to exploit the known linear dynamics structure of diffusion processes. This paper is the first to provide a convergence guarantee for fine-tuning diffusion models by formulating the problem as a stochastic control task with KL regularization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250852/cropped_page7_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained DDPM (Stable Diffusion v1.5) with its score functions, a known reward function (ImageReward) evaluated on generated samples, and text prompts as input, and produces a sequence of fine-tuned score functions (controls) that drive the diffusion process to generate preference-aligned images. Fine-tuning is performed via LoRA adapters with 10 trajectories of 50 transitions each, and the reward is assumed known (estimated from a small human-feedback dataset).

## Method

The paper formulates diffusion fine-tuning as a discrete-time stochastic control problem where the fine-tuned score replaces the pre-trained score in the DDPM dynamics, and a KL divergence between the fine-tuned and pre-trained transition densities penalizes deviation from the pre-trained model. A key simplification (Lemma 2.5) shows this KL term reduces to a squared loss between the control and the pre-trained score, making the one-step Bellman optimization strongly concave when the regularization coefficient β is sufficiently large. The proposed PI-FT algorithm iteratively updates the control via the gradient of the value function and computes the value function via the Bellman equation, achieving global linear convergence at rate O((1−λ)^M). The proof relies on a coupled backward induction showing that Lipschitz and gradient-Lipschitz regularity of both the value function and control are preserved throughout all iterations, without assuming regularity a priori. A parametric extension with linear basis functions enables practical implementation with guaranteed convergence to the unique optimal policy.

## Results

PI-FT is evaluated on Stable Diffusion v1.5 fine-tuned with LoRA and ImageReward across four prompts testing color, composition, counting, and location. PI-FT consistently achieves higher ImageReward scores than both the pre-trained Stable Diffusion and DPOK across all four prompts, with scores averaged over 50 samples per model. The comparison is computationally fair: DPOK uses 10,000 gradient steps (10 per sampling step × 1000 sampling steps) while PI-FT uses 1,000 gradient steps. Varying the KL coefficient β ∈ {0.01, 0.1, 1.0} shows that smaller β yields higher ImageReward scores but larger KL divergence, while larger β keeps the fine-tuned model closer to the pre-trained one with faster convergence (gradient norm decays linearly in all cases).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares PI-FT against two baselines: the pre-trained Stable Diffusion v1.5 model and DPOK (Fan et al., 2024). PI-FT outperforms both baselines on ImageReward across all four evaluation prompts (color, composition, count, location), as shown in Figure 1. Against DPOK specifically, PI-FT achieves higher ImageReward scores while using only 1,000 gradient steps versus DPOK's 10,000 gradient steps, indicating substantially better sample efficiency. Visually (Figure 3), PI-FT more accurately captures object counts (e.g., generating four wolves) and spatial placements (e.g., dog on the moon), avoids errors like miscoloring the rabbit, and produces more natural textures compared to both baselines. The pre-trained Stable Diffusion serves as the lower bound, confirming that fine-tuning with PI-FT provides meaningful improvement over the unmodified model.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant RL-based fine-tuning approaches (DPOK, DDPO, DRaFT) that treat diffusion model alignment as a generic MDP solved with PPO or REINFORCE, instead formulating fine-tuning as a discrete-time stochastic control problem with known linear dynamics and KL regularization over transition densities. It also distinguishes itself from continuous-time control/RL treatments (Berner et al. 2024, Tang 2024, Zhao et al. 2024a) by working in the discrete-time DDPM setting that matches practical implementation, and from prior RL theory by handling continuous state-action spaces with general (non-quadratic) cost functions. The key conceptual departure is applying KL divergence to path-wise transition dynamics rather than terminal distributions, which the authors show introduces the one-step concavity needed for convergence analysis.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to provide a global linear convergence guarantee for fine-tuning diffusion models, filling a significant theoretical gap in a field that has been largely empirical. The regularity preservation result—that the algorithm inherently maintains Lipschitz and gradient-Lipschitz properties throughout iterations rather than assuming them—enables the analysis to be self-contained and extends naturally to parametric (neural network) implementations. Key limitations include the assumption of a known reward function, the restriction to DDPM's specific linear-Gaussian dynamics, and the fact that the parametric extension's convergence proof relies on a realizability assumption. Open questions include whether the theoretical guarantees hold under unknown rewards, whether the framework extends to score-based SDE formulations, and how the β trade-off (convergence speed vs. fidelity to pre-trained model) behaves at scale.
