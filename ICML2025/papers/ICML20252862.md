## Problem

Existing multimodal RAG methods for LVLMs address factual hallucination but leave instruction misalignment largely unaddressed: retrieved Q&A pairs are formatted in rigid, formulaic ways that prevent LVLMs from capturing underlying reasoning patterns, and the order of retrieved examples is not optimized, leading to erratic or suboptimal in-context learning. The paper asks whether multimodal RAG can be integrated with in-context learning to elevate LVLM responses from merely 'known' to 'better understood' through reasoning-aware context.

## Input / Output

The method takes a user query (one RGB image plus a text question) and an external VQA knowledge base of (image, question, answer, reasoning-context) tuples, and outputs a predicted VQA answer. Retrieval operates on hybrid token-level embeddings (text via frozen BERT-base, image via ViT-L + 2-layer MLP from PreFLMR), concatenating all token vectors before computing max-pooling similarity. The final prompt is a K-shot sequence (K=3) of re-ranked examples with their generated reasoning contexts, concatenated with the user's image and question; for non-reasoning datasets (VizWiz, VSR-MC) the reasoning context is omitted since answers are single words or short phrases.

## Method

RCTS is a training-free multimodal RAG framework with three components. First, a self-consistent evaluation mechanism automatically generates reasoning contexts: for each knowledge-base Q&A pair, N_c candidate chain-of-thought explanations are produced, each is used to re-predict the answer N_p times, and the candidate whose predicted answers most consistently match the ground truth is retained. Second, hybrid-modal retrieval concatenates text and image token embeddings and selects Top-N (N=20) candidates via max-pooling similarity. Third, a Monte Carlo Tree Search with Heuristic Rewards (MCTS-HR) re-ranks the N candidates down to K=3 by formulating sequential example selection as a tree-search problem: each node expansion samples an unused Q&A pair as an action, and at maximum depth a K-shot simulation generates a response scored by a composite reward combining a self-consistency term (re-predicting the answer from the generated reasoning context) and a mutual term (checking whether the context also helps answer N_m other knowledge-base questions correctly). UCT-guided exploration and reward backpropagation refine the tree over P=10 rollouts, yielding an optimally ordered few-shot prompt for the LVLM.

## Results

On the reasoning VQA benchmark (Table 2), RCTS achieves 91.44% on ScienceQA, 53.33% on MMMU, and 28.95% on MathV using Qwen2-VL (7B), surpassing Vanilla-RAG by +4.2%, +3.33%, and +4.28% respectively. On the smaller Qwen2-VL (2B), gains are even larger: +11.81% over Zero-Shot and +7.05% over Vanilla-RAG on ScienceQA. On non-reasoning datasets (Table 3), RCTS improves over Vanilla-RAG by +1.61% on VizWiz and +3.05% on VSR-MC, confirming generalizability beyond complex reasoning tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RCTS is compared against two baselines: ICL with random retrieval (Han et al., 2023) and Vanilla-RAG with top retrieval (Lin et al., 2024b, PreFLMR). RCTS consistently outperforms both across all five datasets and all three LVLMs. Against Vanilla-RAG, the largest gains appear on ScienceQA (+7.05% for Qwen2-VL 2B, +4.2% for Qwen2-VL 7B, +3.9% for InternVL-2 8B) and MathV (+4.28% for Qwen2-VL 7B), where reasoning context and tree-search re-ranking most directly benefit complex multi-step reasoning. Against ICL (random retrieval), RCTS yields an average ~3% improvement, confirming that semantic-aware retrieval plus re-ranking substantially beats random example selection. Notably, Vanilla-RAG sometimes underperforms Zero-Shot (e.g., InternVL-2 on ScienceQA drops from 93.00 to 92.78), whereas RCTS never degrades below Zero-Shot, demonstrating that the MCTS-HR re-ranking effectively filters out harmful retrieved examples.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends multimodal RAG (PreFLMR, EchoSight) and in-context learning for LVLMs by repurposing retrieval not for factual grounding but for reasoning-example augmentation. It draws on Auto-CoT for automatic reasoning context generation and adapts MCTS-based LLM reasoning (MCTS-Self-Refine, Mutual Reasoning) to the multimodal re-ranking setting, replacing the LM-as-reward-function with a lighter heuristic reward (self-consistency + mutual answer prediction). The key conceptual departure from prior multimodal RAG is treating retrieved VQA pairs as sequential actions in a tree search rather than a fixed top-K set, and enriching each pair with a self-consistently validated reasoning chain before retrieval.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RCTS offers a training-free, plug-and-play framework that can be attached to any LVLM supporting multi-image input, making it practically accessible without fine-tuning. However, its effectiveness is bounded by knowledge-base coverage (the authors explicitly note failure when no semantically similar samples exist) and incurs non-trivial computational overhead from multiple LVLM calls per rollout. Open questions include whether the MCTS re-ranking generalizes to open-domain VQA where the knowledge base is encyclopedic rather than example-based, and whether the heuristic reward can be replaced by learned signals to reduce inference cost.
