## Problem

Adapter-based model editing methods (GRACE, MELO) rely on distance functions over the model's internal representation space to decide when to trigger an edit, but this space exhibits a strong lexical bias: prompts sharing surface tokens but differing in meaning are closer to an edit than true paraphrases. This forces a hand-tuned trade-off between generalizing to unseen paraphrases and avoiding misfires on lexically similar but irrelevant prompts, and no prior work had identified or addressed this vulnerability in the model editing setting.

## Input / Output

At inference, a user text prompt is passed through the frozen LLM up to a single target layer (layer 2), where the feed-forward output is projected by the learned network and compared via Euclidean distance against all keys in the codebook. If the nearest key's distance falls below that edit's learned threshold, the stored edited string is returned immediately (halting further generation); otherwise the prompt falls through to the base model for standard generation. The codebook stores one entry per edit (projected representation as key, edited text plus threshold as value), so retrieval is a single nearest-neighbor lookup with a per-entry radius check.

## Method

PENME introduces a 2-layer MLP projection network (with ReLU and batch normalization) trained via a contrastive loss that pulls edit–paraphrase pairs together while pushing edit–irrelevant-prompt and edit–unrelated-edit pairs beyond a margin, explicitly disentangling lexical from semantic similarity in the representation space. The projected representations feed a key-value codebook where each edit occupies a single entry, and a per-edit scoping threshold is set data-drivenly as the maximum observed paraphrase distance plus a tunable offset τ. The projector is applied at only one layer (chosen via a lexical-bias analysis showing minimal bias at early layers), keeping the adapter lightweight and the base model entirely frozen. This design yields one codebook entry per edit (enabling efficient add/remove/update), faster retrieval than GRACE/MELO's multi-entry clusters, and state-of-the-art scores on both generalization and locality across T5-small, GPT2-XL, and Llama-2-7b on Counterfact and zsRE.

## Results

PENME achieves perfect Edit Success (ES = 1.000) across all three models (T5-small, Llama-2-7b, GPT2-XL) on both Counterfact and zsRE. On the more challenging Counterfact dataset, PENME attains a composite Score of 0.925 (Llama-2-7b), 0.907 (GPT2-XL), and 0.865 (T5-small), with Locality and Paraphrase Generalization both exceeding 0.80. On zsRE, scores reach 0.984 (Llama-2-7b) and 0.966 (GPT2-XL), with Locality and Generalization above 0.94. The key differentiator is the Paraphrase Generalization metric, where PENME scores 0.808–0.906 on Counterfact versus 0.002–0.140 for GRACE and 0.020–0.037 for MELO.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GRACE, PENME achieves a dramatic advantage on Paraphrase Generalization: on Counterfact, PENME scores 0.808–0.906 versus GRACE's 0.002–0.140, a gap of over 80 percentage points, while GRACE retains a slight Locality edge on Llama-2-7b (0.997 vs. 0.869). Against MELO, PENME similarly dominates on Generalization (0.808–0.906 vs. 0.020–0.037 on Counterfact), though MELO achieves perfect Locality (1.000) on GPT2-XL Counterfact at the cost of near-zero generalization. Against SERAC, PENME wins on all three metrics for every model–dataset combination where both are evaluated, with composite scores of 0.865–0.925 versus SERAC's 0.184–0.675 on Counterfact. Against MEMIT, PENME substantially outperforms on Edit Success and Locality for Llama-2-7b (ES 1.000 vs. 0.147; Loc 0.869 vs. 0.149), though MEMIT achieves perfect Paraphrase Generalization (1.000) on Llama-2-7b Counterfact at the expense of very low ES and Loc. Overall, PENME is the only method that simultaneously achieves high ES, Loc, and Para across all models and both datasets, with composite scores above 0.86 on every configuration.

## Relation to Prior Work

PENME extends the weight-preserving adapter-based model editing line initiated by GRACE (Hartvigsen et al., 2023) and MELO (Yu et al., 2024), which use key-value codebooks with distance-based scoping to trigger edits without modifying base model weights. The key departure is the insertion of a contrastively-trained projection network that learns a disentangled representation space, directly targeting the lexical bias that the paper identifies as the primary failure mode of these scoping mechanisms. While lexical bias has been documented in NLI and other NLP tasks, this is the first work to diagnose it as the critical bottleneck in adapter-based model editing and to propose a learned projection as the remedy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clear diagnostic (lexical bias causes misfires on lexically-overlapping but semantically irrelevant prompts) that explains why prior adapter methods face an intractable generalization-locality trade-off, and shows that resolving it via a small projection network enables both metrics to be high simultaneously. Practical limitations include sensitivity of the contrastive training to hyperparameters (margin, edit-pairing threshold), model-specific threshold tuning, and evaluation only on models up to 7B parameters. Open questions include whether a projector pretrained on large-scale data can serve as a plug-and-play cross-lingual component, and whether the projector can be updated incrementally as new edits are added.
