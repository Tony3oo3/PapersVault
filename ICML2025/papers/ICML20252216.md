## Problem

Existing LLM serving systems that disaggregate prefill from decode do not account for the encoding stage required by Large Multimodal Models, which adds significant compute and memory overhead and inflates token counts. Current systems bundle encoding and prefill on the same GPUs, causing interference between stages, restricting available memory for high-resolution inputs, and degrading SLO attainment (TTFT, TPOT) under multimodal workloads.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252216/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system accepts a text prompt (typically ~22 tokens) alongside multimodal data: multiple images at resolutions up to 4032×3024 (converted into patches processed by a vision encoder), video represented by 8–64 uniformly sampled frames, or audio files (up to 24 per request). The multimodal encoder produces high-dimensional token embeddings that are concatenated with the text prompt for prefill, and the system autoregressively generates output text tokens. A key constraint is that the number of images per request can reach 80 in memory-benchmarking experiments, and the maximum context length of the underlying LLM caps the total token budget.

## Method

EPD Disaggregation assigns the encoding, prefill, and decode stages to dedicated GPU instances operating in data-parallel mode, with asynchronous token transfer between stages via NVLink/InfiniBand and a paged MM cache managed by an MMBlockManager. A key mechanism, Intra-Request Parallelism (IRP), shards a single request's image patches across multiple encoding workers so they are processed and transferred concurrently, reducing first-token latency. A Bayesian black-box optimizer searches over per-stage batch sizes, parallelization configs (TP/PP/IRP), and scheduling strategies to maximize goodput minus GPU cost, using a DistServe-derived simulator. Finally, dynamic role switching monitors queuing statistics across stages and migrates an entire instance (offload → reconfigure model/cache → onload) in under 0.7 seconds to rebalance the pipeline when workload characteristics shift online.

## Results

EPD Disaggregation achieves up to 71% lower TTFT and 90–100% higher SLO attainment compared to non-disaggregated baselines across MiniCPM-V 2.6, InternVL2-8B, and InternVL2-26B. On the Video-MME benchmark, EPD reduces mean TTFT from 3.11 s (vLLM) and 3.08 s (DistServe) to 1.00 s at 64 video frames. Memory efficiency gains include up to 15× lower peak memory on encoding workers, 22× larger encoding batch sizes, 10× more images per request, and 2.2× larger KV cache allocation on prefill nodes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EPD is compared against two baselines: DistServe (PD disaggregation, where encoding and prefill share GPUs) and vLLM (fully monolithic, all stages on the same GPUs). EPD decisively outperforms both on every reported metric. Against DistServe, EPD reduces TTFT by up to 71.9% (MiniCPM-V 2.6), 32.8% (InternVL2-8B), and 44.9% (InternVL2-26B), and achieves 90–100% higher SLO attainment at equivalent request rates. Against vLLM, EPD cuts TTFT by up to 69.2% on Video-MME and 35.2% on Huawei Ascend NPUs. In memory benchmarks, EPD supports up to 22× larger encoding batch sizes and 10× more images per request versus DistServe, while DistServe frequently hits OOM at high resolutions where EPD still operates. On the NextQA real-world video QA dataset, EPD is the only system that reaches the 90% SLO attainment threshold at low request rates, whereas both baselines remain below 10%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the PD (prefill-decode) disaggregation paradigm established by DistServe, SplitWise, and DéjàVu to the multimodal setting by introducing a third disaggregated stage: encoding. Prior LMM serving systems (vLLM, SARATHI, Orca) bundle the multimodal encoder with the LLM on the same GPUs, and even PD-disaggregated systems like DistServe keep encoding coupled to prefill. The key conceptual departure is treating the encoding stage as an independent, parallelizable pipeline stage with its own resource allocation, parallelization strategy (Intra-Request Parallelism), and cache management (MM cache), rather than a sub-step of prefill.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As LMMs with high-resolution and multi-image/video inputs become standard in production (autonomous driving, video QA, healthcare), the encoding bottleneck directly limits SLO attainment and throughput. The demonstrated 15× memory reduction and 22× batch size gains make previously infeasible configurations (e.g., 10+ 4K images per request on a 26B model) practical. Open questions include whether heterogeneous hardware (high-memory GPUs for prefill, high-compute units for encoding) can further improve cost-efficiency, how to reduce visual token transfer overhead via compression, and whether the framework extends to privacy-aware edge-cloud inference where encoding stays local. The paper acknowledges that disaggregation is not universally optimal—throughput-focused, SLO-relaxed workloads may still favor aggregated serving.
