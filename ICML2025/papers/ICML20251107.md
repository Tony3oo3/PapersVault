## Problem

Existing in-context learning demonstration selectors (similarity, diversity, clustering, uncertainty) must repeatedly retrieve from the full training dataset for every query, incurring high computational overhead especially at high shot counts. Moreover, these methods evaluate demonstrations in isolation using generic metrics that ignore the specific LLM's capabilities and knowledge, meaning a 'good' demonstration for one model may be poor for another. The paper asks whether a one-time, LLM-aware pre-selection of a representative subset can replace the full training data for downstream selection.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251107/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a training dataset of input-output pairs and a frozen LLM as input; the LLM is queried via one-shot inference to evaluate sufficiency/necessity relationships between pairs of demonstrations. It produces a pre-selected subset D_FEEDER (typically reducing data size by >20%) that is computed once and is independent of the test set, so it can be reused across all queries. In the fine-tuning setting, the output additionally includes a tuned LLM obtained by training on the fixed D_FEEDER subset. The subset is LLM-specific: different base models yield different D_FEEDER subsets for the same training data.

## Method

FEEDER introduces two LLM-conditioned metrics borrowed from causal reasoning: sufficiency (plugging in demonstration (x_n, y_n) corrects the LLM's answer to x_m) and necessity (unplugging a plugged-in demonstration causes the LLM to produce an incorrect output), formalized via plug/unplug operations analogous to Pearl's do-operator. A tree-based approximation algorithm builds a hierarchy bottom-up: at each level, pairs of node-sets are checked for mutual sufficiency via one-shot LLM inference, and the redundant set is pruned (keeping the smaller if both are sufficient, keeping the sufficient one if only one is, or merging if neither is). The algorithm runs in O(K log|D_TRAIN|) per round, with K=1 (single-shot inference) and R=1 (single run) already yielding strong results in practice. For fine-tuning, the paper wraps pre-selection and LLM training in a bi-level optimization: the outer loop selects D_FEEDER with a frozen LLM, the inner loop fine-tunes the LLM on the fixed subset, and the two can be iterated for joint refinement.

## Results

On text classification (SST-2, 10-shot), FEEDER pre-selection with a simple similarity selector substantially outperforms the full training pool: GPT-3(6B) improves from 77.6% to 86.4% and Llama-2(7B) from 76.3% to 87.1%, while retaining only ~50% of the original training data. In the fine-tuning (bi-level optimization) setting, GPT-2(0.8B) on SUBJ (1-shot) jumps from 67.8% (D_TRAIN+Random) to 93.0% (D_FEEDER+Random), a 25.2-point gain. On the harder GSM8K reasoning task with Qwen-2.5(32B) at 10 shots, D_FEEDER+Similarity reaches 91.23% versus 90.41% for D_TRAIN+Similarity. Across all evaluated LLMs (335M–32B parameters) and datasets, FEEDER consistently reduces the selection pool by over 20% while matching or exceeding baseline accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the similarity-based selector (Sorensen et al., 2022), FEEDER consistently improves accuracy: on SST-2 at 10 shots, D_FEEDER+Similarity beats D_TRAIN+Similarity by +8.8 pts (GPT-3) and +10.8 pts (Llama-2), and by +0.82 pts on GSM8K with Qwen-2.5(32B). Against the diversity/MMR selector (Ye et al., 2022), the headline result is that D_FEEDER+Similarity outperforms D_TRAIN+Diversity by +9.9 pts (GPT-3, SST-2, 10-shot) and +10.0 pts (Llama-2, SST-2, 10-shot), showing that a well-pre-selected pool with a simple metric can surpass a full pool with a more complex one. Against the uncertainty-based selector (Köksal et al., 2022), D_FEEDER+Uncertainty matches or slightly exceeds D_TRAIN+Uncertainty on GSM8K (e.g., 22.10 vs 20.66 for Gemma-2 at 10 shots) and SMCALFlow (30.01 vs 31.00, a small loss). The clustering-based (Zhou et al., 2023) and latent-variable (Wang et al., 2024) selectors are evaluated in the appendices; the paper reports that D_FEEDER+Similarity is competitive with or superior to these more sophisticated methods, reinforcing the claim that pre-selection quality matters more than selector complexity. In the fine-tuning setting, FEEDER's advantage is most dramatic: GPT-2(0.8B) fine-tuned on D_FEEDER achieves 93.0% on SUBJ (1-shot) versus 67.8% for D_TRAIN, a 25.2-point gain that no baseline selector replicates.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes Pearl's causal notions of probability of sufficiency and probability of necessity, originally defined for binary variables, into a demonstration-selection framework where 'plugging in' and 'unplugging' data points serve as analogues of the do-operator. It departs from the existing ICL demonstration-selection literature (similarity, diversity, uncertainty, clustering, and latent-variable selectors) by inserting a model-specific pre-selection stage that operates before any per-query selector, drawing conceptual inspiration from coreset selection but grounding the selection criteria in the frozen LLM's own inference behavior rather than generic geometric or statistical metrics. The tree-based approximation algorithm and the bi-level optimization loop connecting pre-selection to fine-tuning are novel methodological contributions not present in prior demonstration-selection or coreset-selection work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FEEDER offers a practical data-reduction lever (≥20% smaller selection pool) that composes with any downstream selector, which is attractive for settings where retrieval over large corpora is a bottleneck. However, the approach inherits the LLM's own biases and knowledge gaps—sufficiency and necessity are evaluated by the same model being served, so a weak LLM may produce a poorly representative subset. Open questions include whether the transitivity assumption on set-level sufficiency holds reliably at scale, how the method behaves when the LLM is updated (requiring re-pre-selection), and whether the gains persist on tasks where the LLM's parametric knowledge dominates over in-context evidence. The paper also notes potential amplification of existing biases in model weights as a concern.
