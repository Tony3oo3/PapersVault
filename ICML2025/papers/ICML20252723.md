## Problem

Existing drag-based editing methods for diffusion models (e.g., DragDiffusion) directly optimize DDIM-inverted latent features, which suffers from limited representational capacity for motion supervision and high computational cost from large spatial search regions for point tracking. This leads to imprecise deformations and slow convergence, requiring many optimization steps to achieve acceptable results.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252723/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a source RGB image, user-specified pairs of 2D source (handle) and target pixel coordinates, and an optional binary foreground mask. The source image is first DDIM-inverted to timestep t=35 in the Stable Diffusion 1.5 latent space, and a reconstruction LoRA is pre-trained on the image (80 steps, ~48s) to anchor the model to the source appearance. The output is a single edited image where foreground content has been deformed toward the target points while background regions (outside the mask) remain unchanged.

## Method

DragLoRA replaces direct latent feature optimization with online optimization of LoRA adapter parameters integrated into all UNet attention layers, initialized from reconstruction fine-tuning weights. A Dual-Objective Optimization (DOO) scheme combines the standard drag loss with a Delta Denoising Score (DDS) loss that penalizes divergence between the LoRA-enhanced and original UNet noise predictions on perturbed features, preventing the adapter from drifting away from pretrained priors. An Input Latent Feature Adaptation (ILFA) step cyclically denoises and re-noises the input latent within the foreground mask at each iteration, propagating accumulated deformation into the feature space and enabling handle movement even without gradient updates. An Adaptive Switching Scheme (ASS) dynamically toggles between full DOO+ILFA (when point tracking confidence is low) and ILFA-only (when tracking is confident), skipping backpropagation when unnecessary. An Efficient Point Tracking (EPT) strategy further reduces the search region using distance-closer or angle-closer geometric constraints, cutting redundant candidate evaluations.

## Results

On DragBench, DragLoRA achieves the best editing accuracy (MD = 23.77) among all 15 compared methods, surpassing the previous best GoodDrag (MD = 24.26) while also being the fastest optimization-based method at 29.84 s per image (vs. GoodDrag's 56.97 s and FreeDrag's 51.42 s). The lighter DragLoRA-Fast variant further reduces time to 22.89 s with a modest accuracy drop (MD = 27.55). In the Drag-Back evaluation, DragLoRA attains the lowest LPIPS (1.33 × 10) and a competitive CLIP score (0.96), confirming that edits are both accurate and reversible.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DragLoRA achieves the lowest MD (23.77) across all 15 baselines, beating the closest competitor GoodDrag (24.26) by ~0.5 px and GDrag (26.49) by ~2.7 px, while also cutting runtime roughly in half compared to GoodDrag (29.84 s vs. 56.97 s) and FreeDrag (29.84 s vs. 51.42 s). Against the original DragDiffusion pipeline, DragLoRA improves MD by 8.36 px (32.13 → 23.77) and M-MD by 8.01 px (30.71 → 22.70) at comparable speed. The main trade-off is 1-LPIPS fidelity: DragLoRA scores 0.87, trailing StableDrag, EasyDrag, and GDrag (all 0.91) by 0.04, suggesting slightly more aggressive deformation at the cost of minor background perturbation. Among encoder-based methods (InstantDrag, LightningDrag), DragLoRA wins decisively on MD (23.77 vs. 31.51 and 29.10) but is ~15–20× slower due to per-image online optimization. In the Drag-Back test, DragLoRA records the best LPIPS (1.33) among the four tested methods, edging out GoodDrag (1.37) and DragDiffusion (1.39), while GoodDrag marginally leads on CLIP (0.97 vs. 0.96).

## Relation to Prior Work

DragLoRA builds directly on the DragDiffusion pipeline (DDIM inversion at t=35, motion supervision via feature alignment, and iterative point tracking) but replaces the core optimization target: instead of updating the latent feature z_35, it online-optimizes LoRA adapter parameters integrated into the UNet. The Delta Denoising Score loss from Hertz et al. (2023) is repurposed with a reversed gradient sign as a regularization term to prevent LoRA drift from the pretrained model, rather than as an editing direction. The Input Latent Feature Adaptation (cyclic denoise-renoise) and the adaptive switching between DOO+ILFA and ILFA-only modes are novel mechanisms not present in prior drag methods, which typically apply a fixed optimization loop for all steps.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The shift from latent-feature optimization to online model adaptation offers a more expressive optimization space for fine-grained deformations while preserving pretrained priors, and the adaptive scheme (skipping gradient updates when tracking is confident) yields a practical ~2× speedup over GoodDrag with slightly better accuracy. However, the method is still tied to SD 1.5 and a single timestep (t=35), and the authors acknowledge that challenging cases (e.g., occluded objects) remain unresolved. Open questions include whether the online LoRA paradigm generalizes to region-based or reference-guided drag, whether it transfers to newer diffusion backbones (SDXL, DiT), and whether the meta-learning-like generalization across steps can be formalized and leveraged for multi-image or video editing.
