## Problem

Current multimodal VAEs aggregate single-modality (expert) distributions using Product of Experts or Mixture of Experts, both of which assume independence between experts—an overoptimistic assumption since modalities are different views of the same object. This independence assumption leads to miscalibrated joint posteriors (PoE underestimates variance; MoE cannot be sharper than any expert) and, in MoE-based models, introduces harmful modality sub-sampling. Additionally, existing ELBO formulations weight all modality subsets equally during optimization, ignoring that subsets with more modalities or lower uncertainty should contribute more.

## Input / Output

The model accepts any subset of M modalities (e.g., MNIST digit images, SVHN house images, text strings, bird images, face images, or text descriptions) and must produce a valid latent representation even when modalities are missing at test time. It outputs latent representations z, generated modalities for all subsets, log-likelihood estimates of the full multimodal data, and linear classification accuracy. A key structural detail is that the model learns K = 2^M − 1 variational distributions (one per non-empty subset) and a set of learned weights π_k that modulate each subset's contribution to the overall ELBO, rather than treating all subsets symmetrically.

## Method

CoDE-VAE introduces a principled Bayesian consensus method (CoDE) that models stochastic dependence between expert distributions through their estimation errors. Each expert provides a point estimate μ_j^d and uncertainty σ_j^d per dimension; the joint error vector is assumed multivariate Gaussian with a block-diagonal covariance Σ_k whose off-diagonal blocks Σ^d encode inter-expert correlation via a single parameter ρ (cross-validated over [0, 0.2, 0.4, 0.6, 0.8]). Under a flat prior, the consensus (joint posterior) distribution is derived in closed form as N(A_k^{-1}B_k, A_k^{-1}) where A_k = u^T Σ_k^{-1} u and B_k = u^T Σ_k^{-1} μ_k, subsuming PoE when ρ = 0. The ELBO is extended with a categorical indicator ξ over subsets, yielding learnable weights π_k that are optimized via entropy maximization so that more informative (lower-trace covariance) and larger-cardinality subsets contribute more to training. Unlike MoE-based approaches, CoDE-VAE does not rely on modality sub-sampling, which eliminates the generative quality gap that grows with the number of modalities.

## Results

On MNIST-SVHN-Text, CoDE-VAE achieves the highest conditional coherence (0.82±0.022) and unconditional coherence (0.51±0.015) among all compared models, while maintaining competitive FID scores (79.11±2.73 conditional, 71.40±1.93 unconditional). On CUB, CoDE-VAE attains the best conditional coherence (0.75±0.050) with a conditional FID of 175.97±0.30, close to MMVAE+ (164.94). On PolyMNIST, CoDE-VAE is the only model that achieves unconditional FID scores comparable to unimodal VAEs when trained with 4–5 modalities, effectively closing the generative quality gap that grows with modality count in sub-sampling-based methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoDE-VAE outperforms all six baselines on generative coherence across all three main datasets: it leads on conditional coherence (0.82 vs. 0.79 for MoPoE-VAE, 0.74 for mmJSD) and unconditional coherence (0.51 vs. 0.46 for MoPoE-VAE, 0.34 for MMVAE) on MNIST-SVHN-Text, and achieves the highest conditional coherence on CUB (0.75 vs. 0.72 for MMVAE+). In terms of generative quality (FID), CoDE-VAE does not match the best FID scores of MVTCAE (38.34) or MVAE (41.09) on MNIST-SVHN-Text, but it substantially outperforms MoE-based methods (MMVAE at 153.96, mmJSD at 148.47) and is competitive with MMVAE+ (54.69) while achieving far higher coherence. On CUB, CoDE-VAE's conditional FID (175.97) is slightly worse than MMVAE+ (164.94) but much better than MMVAE (232.20), mmJSD (262.80), and MoPoE-VAE (265.55). The key differentiator is the generative quality gap on PolyMNIST: only CoDE-VAE (along with MVAE and MVTCAE) avoids the increasing FID gap as modalities grow, and CoDE-VAE uniquely reaches unimodal-VAE-level unconditional FID at 4–5 modalities. Ablation experiments confirm that both modeling expert dependence (ρ > 0) and learning per-subset ELBO weights (π_k) contribute to the gains over the independent-equal-weight baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Winkler's (1981) consensus of experts framework to multivariate data with stochastic dependence, applying it for the first time to approximate joint variational posteriors in multimodal VAEs. It departs from the dominant PoE and MoE aggregation methods, which assume independence between single-modality expert distributions, by modeling dependence through a correlation parameter ρ in the covariance of expert estimation errors. The ELBO formulation builds on Sutter et al.'s (2021) generalized multimodal ELBO (sum over all subset ELBOs) but replaces equal weighting with learned coefficients π_k, and avoids the modality sub-sampling inherent in MoE-based approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that CoDE-VAE closes the generative quality gap as modalities increase (reaching unimodal-VAE-level FID at 4–5 modalities) addresses a known failure mode of sub-sampling-based methods that has persisted across the multimodal VAE literature. The method is a principled Bayesian drop-in replacement for PoE, applicable to any existing multimodal VAE architecture. Open questions include whether the cross-validated ρ generalizes to high-dimensional or highly heterogeneous modalities, whether the O(2^M − 1) complexity remains tractable beyond 5–6 modalities, and whether the learned π_k weights transfer across datasets or must be re-optimized per task.
