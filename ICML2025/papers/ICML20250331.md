## Problem

Existing red-teaming methods for Vision-Language Models are either manual and inefficient, limited to specific jailbreak input types, or lack effective feedback from the target model's responses to iteratively improve test cases. This paper addresses the unexplored question of how to conduct comprehensive, automated red-teaming for VLMs that simultaneously maximizes attack success rate, test case diversity, and stealthiness across multiple harmful categories.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250331/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a harmful category label (one of six: harassment, hate, illegal activity, self-harm, sexual, violence) and grey-box access to a target VLM. It produces a paired multimodal test case consisting of a textual prompt (two 'tips' embedded in a game-style template asking the VLM to describe an activity step-by-step) and a corresponding image generated by Stable Diffusion 3 Medium from a model-generated caption. The output also includes a structured safety evaluation report from two moderation models (Llama Guard 2 and MD Judge) that classifies whether the VLM's response constitutes a safety violation and provides granular failure analysis for refinement.

## Method

TRUST-VLM is a three-phase iterative framework built on in-context learning with a 70B red-teaming LLM (Llama-3.1-70B-Instruct). In the generation phase, 8 ICL demonstrations guide the model to simultaneously produce a harmful activity, two subtle textual tips, and an image caption, all anchored to the same activity to ensure cross-modal alignment; the tips are embedded in a 'game' template that implicitly requests step-by-step instructions rather than directly asking for harmful content. In the evaluation phase, the multimodal test case is fed to the target VLM and the response is assessed by Llama Guard 2 and MD Judge. The key differentiator is the refinement phase: a BART-Large-MNLI zero-shot classifier categorizes both the VLM's response (e.g., refusal, deflection, safety guidance) and the moderation analysis into predefined labels, which are then mapped through a feedback table into specific rephrasing instructions (e.g., 'rephrase to avoid triggering rejection mechanisms') that are concatenated into the ICL prompt for the next iteration. This feedback loop typically converges in fewer than 7 refinement steps, achieving 95% fault detection on LLaVA-v1.5-13B and 82% on GPT-4o while maintaining low input toxicity.

## Results

On LLaVA-v1.5-13B, TRUST-VLM achieves a Fault Detection Rate (FDR) of 95%, substantially outperforming JailBreakV-28K (72%) and RTVLM (24%). It also attains the highest textual diversity (0.88) and textual alignment (0.76) among all compared methods, while maintaining lower visual toxicity (51%) than both baselines. On the commercial model GPT-4o, TRUST-VLM achieves an average FDR of 82.04% across six harmful categories, with the highest per-category FDR of 96% on illegal activity and 95.83% on violence.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the benchmark-based baselines, TRUST-VLM dominates on FDR: 95% vs. 72% (JailBreakV-28K) and 95% vs. 24% (RTVLM), while also achieving superior textual diversity (0.88 vs. 0.81 and 0.55) and textual alignment (0.76 vs. 0.39 and 0.25). It slightly trails RTVLM on visual diversity (0.50 vs. 0.52) and JailBreakV-28K on textual toxicity (11.67% vs. 5.33%), but outperforms both on visual toxicity. Compared to the automatic red-teaming method Arondight, TRUST-VLM achieves higher FDR in all three overlapping categories (98% vs. 82% on illegal activity, 98% vs. 35% on adult content, 100% vs. 92% on violent content) and higher prompt diversity (0.88 vs. 0.58). Against the SOTA jailbreak attack HADES, TRUST-VLM trades a marginal 1% FDR drop (99% vs. 100%) for dramatically lower toxicity (12% vs. 83% textual; 51% vs. 99% visual) and substantially better textual alignment (0.76 vs. 0.45), making its test cases far more realistic and harder to filter.

## Relation to Prior Work

TRUST-VLM extends the red-teaming paradigm from LLMs (Ganguli et al., 2022; Perez et al., 2022) to vision-language models, building on the nascent VLM red-teaming efforts of Arondight (Liu et al., 2024) and RTVLM (Li et al., 2024c). Its key departure is a closed-loop feedback mechanism: a BART-based classifier interprets the target VLM's refusal patterns and moderation analysis, then feeds structured instructions back into an in-context-learning prompt to iteratively refine failed test cases—something Arondight explicitly lacks. The method also repurposes ICL (Brown et al., 2020) and a text-to-image model (Stable Diffusion 3) to jointly generate semantically aligned adversarial text and image inputs, moving beyond single-modality jailbreak attacks like HADES.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers model developers a practical, automated pipeline for pre-deployment safety auditing of VLMs, with the 95% FDR on LLaVA and 82% on GPT-4o demonstrating that even heavily filtered commercial models remain vulnerable to subtle, low-toxicity multimodal prompts. The ablation revealing that fewer ICL examples increase FDR but sharply raise toxicity and reduce diversity highlights a fundamental stealth-vs-effectiveness trade-off that the field has not previously characterized. Open questions include reducing the computational cost of iterative refinement, extending to more harmful categories and modalities (video, audio), and whether the discovered vulnerabilities generalize across model families or are architecture-specific.
