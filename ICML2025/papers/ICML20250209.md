## Problem

Empirical work (Merullo et al., 2024) revealed that LLMs solve single-token factual-recall ICL tasks via Word2Vec-style vector arithmetic (task vector + query vector ≈ answer vector), and that QA training data is critical for enabling this mechanism. However, no theoretical framework explains how a non-linear residual transformer trained by gradient descent with cross-entropy loss naturally produces this vector arithmetic behavior, nor why QA data succeeds where ICL-style training fails. This paper provides the first optimization-theoretic account that explicitly incorporates the residual stream, layer normalization, and softmax attention.

## Input / Output

The transformer receives a prompt T = [x₁, y₁, …, x_J, y_J, x_query] ∈ R^{d×(2J+1)} (or a QA-ICL variant with M-token QA prefixes replacing word demonstrations) and produces a single predicted answer token via argmax over a dictionary of 7K+K' normalized embedding vectors. Unlike prior theoretical work that uses a structured 'up-word-down-label' embedding to decouple attention and MLP, here the full prompt is fed directly into a non-linear residual transformer, and the intermediate output h_θ,0(T) (the task vector before residual addition) is the key quantity whose alignment with the high-level concept a_{k*} determines correctness.

## Method

The paper develops a two-phase optimization analysis for a single-layer non-linear residual transformer (softmax attention + MLP + l₂ layer-norm + residual connection) trained by GD with L₂-regularized cross-entropy loss under a hierarchical concept data model where K orthogonal high-level task vectors a_k are orthogonal to K orthogonal low-level concept vectors b_k. The key insight is a training-distribution separation: QA data (where the query x^QA contains no b_k component) allows the value matrix W_V to align cleanly with task vectors, while ICL data induces co-occurrence asymmetries that cause W_V to memorize low-level b_k features, producing a contaminated hybrid vector that disrupts the arithmetic y ≈ a_{k*} + x_query. The proof introduces six continuous-time ODE surrogates to bound the discrete gradient dynamics of attention projections (W_Q a_k)ᵀ(W_K a_k) and MLP projections a_kᵀW_V a_k across two phases (linear then decelerating growth), ultimately establishing 0-1 test loss convergence for QA-trained models and Θ(1) irreducible error for ICL-trained models, plus OOD guarantees for unseen dictionaries and multi-concept prompts.

## Results

The paper's primary contributions are theoretical: Theorem 3.2 proves that a residual transformer trained via GD on QA data retrieves the high-level task vector (cosine similarity Θ(1) with a_{k*}, o(1) with all other directions), while Proposition 3.3 establishes that the 0-1 test loss converges to ≤ε for QA-trained models but remains Θ(1) for ICL-trained models. Proposition 3.4 further proves OOD generalization to unseen dictionaries (conic combinations of learned task vectors) and distribution shifts (multi-concept prompts with |K|<4). Simulations (K=2, d=3000, M=30, η=5, q_V=10⁻⁵) confirm the theory: the QA-trained model reaches near-zero test loss within 2000 epochs, whereas ICL-trained and QA-ICL-trained models plateau at a constant ~0.2 test error even after 5000 epochs, with W_V overfitting to low-level features b_k.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper with no external method baselines; the central empirical comparison is between three training paradigms under identical architecture and hyperparameters. Training on Word-Label ICL data (P_T) or QA-ICL data (P_QA^T) both fail to converge, maintaining a constant test error of approximately 0.2 regardless of training duration (5000 epochs), because the value matrix W_V memorizes low-level concept vectors b_k through unintended co-occurrence asymmetries between demonstration pairs and query words. In contrast, training on QA sentence data (P_QA) achieves near-zero test loss within 2000 epochs, since the QA prefix x_QA contains only the task vector a_k and irrelevant tokens (no b_k components), preventing harmful low-level feature memorization. The paper positions this as a theoretical explanation for the empirical finding by Allen-Zhu & Li (2024) and Zhang et al. (2025a) that QA data is essential for factual-recall capability, and contrasts with prior ICL theory (Zhang et al., 2024; Kim & Suzuki, 2024; Chen et al., 2024a) that assumes training and testing on the same ICL distribution and overlooks the residual stream.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper provides the first optimization-theoretic explanation for the Word2Vec-style vector arithmetic mechanism empirically discovered by Merullo et al. (2024), grounding it in a hierarchical concept data model inspired by Park et al. (2025)'s observation of orthogonal high/low-level concept geometry in LLMs. It departs sharply from prior ICL theory (Zhang et al. 2024, Kim & Suzuki 2024, Chen et al. 2024a, Bu et al. 2024a), which relies on simplified 'up-word-down-label' embeddings, linearized attention, or non-standard losses, by analyzing a realistic residual transformer with softmax attention, layer normalization, and cross-entropy loss. A key conceptual departure is the demonstration that the standard theoretical assumption of training and testing on ICL data is actually harmful, whereas training on QA data (as empirically advocated by Allen-Zhu & Li 2024) is what enables proper task vector retrieval.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper resolves a long-standing gap by explaining *why* QA data is essential for factual-recall ICL and *how* gradient descent on it produces the task vector arithmetic mechanism, providing theoretical grounding for a widely observed but previously unexplained phenomenon. The OOD generalization results (dictionary shift, multi-concept distribution shift) offer a principled account of why transformers outperform static Word2Vec embeddings in compositional settings. However, the framework is confined to single-token factual recall with an idealized orthogonal concept geometry, and does not address how task vectors emerge in deeper layers, how polysemous vocabulary evolves during training, or how multi-token reasoning extends these mechanisms—leaving significant open questions for connecting this theory to real LLM behavior.
