## Problem

Message-passing GNNs and hand-crafted structural features dominate link prediction, yet the potential of visual perception to comprehend graph topology has been entirely overlooked. The paper asks whether rendering graph subgraphs as images and extracting visual structural features can provide discriminative power that MPNNs and heuristic SFs cannot, and how such vision-based awareness can be fused into existing MPNN architectures.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252639/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a graph (adjacency + node features) and a set of queried link pairs. For each query, the k-hop subgraph (k≤3) is rendered as a 2D image via Graphviz with the queried link masked and node labels ablated; this image is fed to a pretrained ResNet50 to produce a fixed-dimension visual structural feature vector. The output is a scalar link-existence probability per pair. In the efficient E-GVN variant, visualization is node-centered (one image per node rather than per link), and the vision encoder is frozen with a lightweight adapter, reducing preprocessing cost from O(|E|) to O(|V|).

## Method

GVN integrates vision into MPNNs in three steps: (1) scope-decoupled subgraph visualization, where the k-hop neighborhood around the queried link is rendered as a consistent-style image independent of MPNN depth; (2) adaptive extraction of visual structural features (VSFs) via a finetunable ResNet50 encoder; (3) compatible feature integration (default: cross-attention) that fuses VSFs with MPNN node representations without modifying the message-passing pipeline. The key insight is that VSFs can distinguish links involving isomorphic nodes (which 1-WL-equivalent MPNNs cannot), capture fine-grained substructures like triangles, and adaptively encode a superset of common-neighbor and path-based SFs depending on graph density. E-GVN improves scalability by switching to node-centered visualization, freezing the vision encoder with a learnable adapter, and injecting VSFs into node attributes before message passing so visual information propagates through all MPNN layers.

## Results

On the Planetoid datasets, GVN_GCN achieves HR@100 of 81.13 (Cora), 83.93 (Citeseer), and 73.17 (PubMed), representing relative improvements of 21.5%, 25.1%, and 38.0% over the GCN baseline. The efficient variant E-GVN_GCN extends these gains to large-scale OGB graphs, achieving HR@50 of 62.14 on ogbl-collab (+38.9% over GCN), HR@100 of 32.15 on ogbl-ppa (+72.2%), MRR of 86.10 on ogbl-citation2 (+1.6%), and HR@20 of 60.21 on ogbl-ddi (+62.4%). When built on the SOTA SF-enhanced NCNC backbone, E-GVN_NCNC achieves the best scores across all seven datasets, e.g., 91.47 HR@100 on Cora, 94.44 on Citeseer, 84.02 on PubMed, 68.14 HR@50 on ogbl-collab, 63.45 HR@100 on ogbl-ppa, 90.72 MRR on ogbl-citation2, and 87.31 HR@20 on ogbl-ddi.

## Baselines & Comparisons

All heuristic baselines (CN, AA, RA) are substantially outperformed by every GVN/E-GVN configuration across all seven datasets. Compared to the basic MPNNs, GVN_GCN improves GCN by 21–38% relative on Planetoid graphs and by 39–72% on OGB large-scale graphs; SAGE is similarly surpassed. Against SF-enhanced MPNNs, E-GVN_NCNC consistently beats NCNC (the SOTA baseline) on all seven datasets, e.g., +1.82 on Cora HR@100, +0.97 on Citeseer, +2.73 on PubMed, +1.53 on Collab HR@50, +2.03 on PPA HR@100, +1.60 on Citation2 MRR, and +3.20 on DDI HR@20. E-GVN_NCNC also surpasses BUDDY, Neo-GNN, SEAL, and NBFNet on every dataset where they are reported. Notably, GVN_GCN (link-centered) outperforms E-GVN_GCN (node-centered) on small graphs, but E-GVN_NCNC surpasses GVN_NCNC on all datasets, attributed to pre-MPNN integration allowing visual information to propagate through message passing. The paper reports that E-GVN achieves 39 best scores and GVN 7 best scores out of 49 total metric-dataset combinations in the broader evaluation (Table 4), versus only 3 for NCNC.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the SF-enhanced MPNN line of work (SEAL, Neo-GNN, BUDDY, NCNC) by replacing or augmenting hand-crafted structural features with visual structural features (VSFs) extracted from rendered subgraph images via a standard CNN (ResNet50). It builds directly on GCN/GraphSAGE as the message-passing backbone and on the decoupled-scope principle from prior GNN work, but departs fundamentally from the numerical/structural-feature paradigm by introducing a visual modality into the pipeline. The connection to multimodal learning (VQA, vision-language models) is noted, but the paper is the first to apply image-based structural perception specifically to MPNN-based link prediction, positioning itself as a new orthogonal axis of enhancement rather than a replacement for existing SFs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The orthogonal compatibility with existing SOTA methods (NCNC, BUDDY) makes the framework a drop-in enhancement rather than a competing architecture, which is practically valuable. The E-GVN variant addresses the scalability bottleneck of per-link visualization, making the approach viable for large-scale graphs (ogbl-citation2 with 2.9M nodes). Open questions include whether the visual modality generalizes to other graph tasks (node classification, heterogeneous/temporal graphs), whether more powerful vision encoders (e.g., foundation models) could yield larger gains, and whether the modest 1–3% improvement over NCNC on SOTA benchmarks represents the ceiling or whether richer visual representations could close the gap further. A key limitation is that GVN (link-centered) is not scalable, and the pre-processing cost of graph visualization remains a practical overhead.
