## Problem

Despite advances in ML for conditional average treatment effect (CATE) estimation, practical adoption remains limited because building, tuning, and validating CATE pipelines is highly complex: there is no ground truth CATE for validation, confounding induces covariate shift, and metalearners require multiple sub-models with independent hyperparameters. No existing AutoML solution addresses these unique counterfactual challenges, leaving practitioners without an accessible, end-to-end tool for automated CATE estimation.

## Input / Output

AutoCATE consumes observational tabular data as tuples (X, t, y) with covariates, a binary treatment indicator, and a scalar outcome, and produces CATE predictions τ̂(x) for new instances. The pipeline is configured via a scikit-learn-style API (fit/predict) with initialization arguments controlling the number of optimization trials, search spaces, ensemble strategy, and holdout ratio. The output can be a single retrained pipeline or an ensemble of top-k pipelines with learned weights, and the framework supports both regression and classification outcomes.

## Method

AutoCATE formalizes CATE pipeline search as a counterfactual Combined Algorithm Selection and Hyperparameter (CASH) optimization and solves it in three stages. Stage 1 (Evaluation) automatically tunes the ML pipelines underlying a chosen risk measure (e.g., T-risk, DR-risk) by searching over preprocessors, baselearners, and hyperparameters to construct reliable pseudo-outcome proxies for the unobserved CATE. Stage 2 (Estimation) searches over combinations of preprocessors, metalearners (S, T, Lo, X, DR, R, RA, Z, U, F), baselearners, and their hyperparameters to generate candidate CATE estimation pipelines. Stage 3 (Ensembling) uses the Stage-1 proxy risk to select and combine pipelines via top-k selection, constrained-optimization stacking, softmax weighting, or Pareto-optimal selection, with multi-objective strategies (average score, Euclidean distance, average rank) when multiple risk measures are combined. The entire search is implemented with Optuna (random search in experiments) over a space of 2,187+ pipeline configurations, and the framework normalizes risk scores against an ATE baseline to handle differing scales across objectives.

## Results

AutoCATE is evaluated on four semi-synthetic benchmarks (IHDP, ACIC, Twins, News) using √PEHE (lower is better). With its optimized configuration (T-risk, BestMeta-BestBase, 50 trials), AutoCATE achieves the best √PEHE on IHDP (1.25 ± 0.18, Top 1) and News (2.29 ± 0.06, Top 5), outperforming the strongest baseline (T-Learner with gradient boosting) by 33% and 5% respectively. On ACIC, AutoCATE (1.45 ± 0.10, Top 5) is competitive with T-GB (1.38 ± 0.09), and on Twins it achieves the best result (.314 ± .00, Top 5). The Top 5 ensemble consistently matches or improves upon Top 1 selection across all datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper benchmarks AutoCATE against S-Learner and T-Learner (Künzel et al., 2019) paired with random forests, gradient boosting, and linear regression, all tuned via the μ-risk (error in predicting observed outcomes). AutoCATE's Top 1 configuration decisively outperforms all baselines on IHDP (1.25 vs. 1.86 for the best baseline T-GB, a 33% improvement) and News (2.33 vs. 2.40 for T-GB). On ACIC, the T-Learner with gradient boosting (1.38) slightly edges out AutoCATE Top 5 (1.45), though AutoCATE Top 1 (1.52) is within one standard error. On Twins, all methods perform similarly (range .314–.335), with AutoCATE Top 5 achieving the best result. The gains are attributed to three factors: a larger search space (multiple metalearners, baselearners, and preprocessing), model selection aligned with CATE estimation via the T-risk rather than the μ-risk, and the Top 5 ensemble for improved robustness.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the CASH (Combined Algorithm Selection and Hyperparameter) optimization paradigm from AutoML to the counterfactual setting, formalizing CATE pipeline search as a 'counterfactual CASH' problem. It builds on the metalearner framework (Kunzel et al., 2019) and the growing literature on risk measures for CATE validation (Curth & van der Schaar, 2023; Mahajan et al., 2023; Schuler et al., 2018), but departs from both by integrating evaluation, estimation, and ensembling into a single automated search rather than treating them as separate design decisions. Unlike prior AutoML systems (AutoGluon, FLAML, H2O), which assume observed ground truth, AutoCATE must navigate the absence of true CATE labels and covariate shift from confounding, requiring pseudo-outcome-based proxy risks and multi-objective optimization across risk measures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AutoCATE lowers the barrier to CATE estimation for non-ML practitioners (clinicians, marketers) by reducing the pipeline to four lines of code, and its empirical analysis provides the first systematic comparison of metalearners, risk measures, and ensembling strategies under a unified protocol. Key open questions remain: robustness to violations of unconfoundedness and overlap (the paper only partially addresses this with synthetic experiments), validation on fully real-world data (most benchmarks are semi-synthetic), and the optimal allocation of compute across the three stages. The finding that no single risk measure or metalearner dominates across settings reinforces that automated, data-driven selection is necessary rather than optional.
