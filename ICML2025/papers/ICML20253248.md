## Problem

When a trained RL agent encounters a sudden, permanent change in environment dynamics at inference time (a novelty), its converged policy becomes unreliable and can cause catastrophic failures. Existing RL novelty detection methods like RIQN require manually tuned thresholds and drift parameters that demand a priori knowledge of the anomaly's scale, contradicting the fundamental assumption that novelties are unanticipatable and unencountered during training. This paper addresses the gap of detecting such distributional shifts without any user-specified hyperparameters.

## Input / Output

The method consumes the image observation x_t, recurrent hidden state h_t, and action a_t from a pre-trained DreamerV2 world model operating on RGB frames (64×64 for Atari, 64×64 for MiniGrid, 84×84 for DMC). It produces a binary novelty flag and a continuous KL-divergence novelty score computed entirely within the latent space of the RSSM, requiring no access to the raw pixel reconstruction or any external replay buffer. The detection is threshold-free: the bound is self-calibrating from the world model's own learned distributions, so no per-environment tuning is needed.

## Method

The core contribution is a threshold-free KL divergence bound (Eq. 4) derived from the DreamerV2 loss function that flags novelty when the inequality KL[p_φ(z_t|h_t,x_t)‖p_φ(z_t|h_t)] ≤ KL[p_φ(z_t|h_t,x_t)‖p_φ(z_t|h_0)] − KL[p_φ(z_t|h_t,x_t)‖p_φ(z_t|h_0,x_t)] is violated. The intuition, grounded in Bayesian surprise, is that under nominal conditions the posterior given the full history should be closer to the prior than to a history-dropped prediction, but a novel observation disrupts this relationship by making the model over-rely on the current image x_t rather than the learned dynamics in h_t. Two propositions handle detection: if the right-hand side becomes negative, the non-negativity of KL divergence immediately flags novelty; otherwise the bound defines a decision boundary in latent space measuring robustness to partial input destruction. The method is architecture-agnostic in principle (demonstrated on RNN-based DreamerV2, transformer-based IRIS, and diffusion-based DIAMOND world models) and operates as a single-step check per transition without ensemble sampling or CUSUM accumulation.

## Results

The proposed KL bound method achieves false positive rates ≤10⁻² across all tested environments (Atari, DMC, MiniGrid), compared to RIQN's rates ranging from 5% to 68%. On Atari, the KL bound attains ADE ≤10⁻² and AUC ≥0.99 on Freeway novelties (InvisibleCars, ColorCars, FrozenCars), and ADE of 9.9 with AUC 0.787 on Kangaroo-FloorSwap, while RIQN achieves ADE of 166.3 and AUC 0.541 on the same task. The KL bound also provides a 10²× real-time inference speedup over RIQN on Atari and 10³× on DMC, due to eliminating per-feature CUSUM computation over ensemble samples.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against RIQN (Danesh & Fern, 2021), the primary state-of-the-art RL novelty detection method, and PP-Mare (a reconstruction-error ablation of their own world model). The KL bound decisively outperforms RIQN on false positive rate (≤10⁻² vs. 5–68% across environments) and on ADE/AUC in most Atari and MiniGrid settings (e.g., ADE 9.9 vs. 166.3 on Kangaroo-FloorSwap; AUC 0.787 vs. 0.541). RIQN achieves lower ADE in several DMC sub-tasks (e.g., 6.20 vs. 51.9 on Cartpole-3D-Balance/LowPerturb), but this comes at the cost of 68% false positive rates, which the authors argue is unacceptable for safety-critical deployment. PP-Mare, the reconstruction-error ablation, generally trails the KL bound on AUC (e.g., 0.281 vs. 0.787 on Kangaroo-FloorSwap) because pixel-level reconstruction error is not optimized for semantic novelty. The KL bound also runs 10²–10³× faster in real time than RIQN, since it avoids computing per-feature anomaly scores across an ensemble and CUSUM accumulation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the DreamerV2 world model architecture (Hafner et al., 2021), repurposing the KL divergence term already present in its VAE/RSSM training objective as a parameter-free novelty detection bound. Conceptually, it draws on Bayesian surprise (Itti & Baldi, 2005) and the robustness-to-partial-input-destruction framework from denoising autoencoders (Vincent et al., 2008; Srivastava et al., 2014) to formalize when a world model's posterior should deviate from its prior. It departs from the existing RL novelty detection line (primarily RIQN, Danesh & Fern, 2021) by eliminating the need for ensemble-based anomaly scoring and CUSUM threshold tuning, instead exploiting the internal structure of a single trained world model. The PP-Mare ablation connects to standard reconstruction-error anomaly detection but adapted to the prior/posterior split inherent in RSSM-based world models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The parameter-free nature of the KL bound is practically significant because novelty detection, by definition, cannot assume prior knowledge of what anomalies will look like—yet most existing methods (RIQN, reconstruction-error thresholds) require environment-specific hyperparameter tuning that implicitly encodes such knowledge. The consistent ≤10⁻² false positive rate across MiniGrid, Atari, and DMC without any tuning, combined with 10²–10³× inference speedup over RIQN, makes this approach attractive for real-time deployment on robotic or safety-critical agents. Key limitations include sensitivity to exploration effects (the policy restricting the world model's learned transition space causes spurious novelty flags) and dependence on world model architectures that maintain explicit latent state distributions (the KL bound cannot be directly applied to diffusion-based world models). Open questions include whether alternative divergences (Jensen-Shannon, Wasserstein) provide stronger guarantees, how the method scales with latent dimensionality, and how to respond to detected novelties (adaptation vs. halting).
