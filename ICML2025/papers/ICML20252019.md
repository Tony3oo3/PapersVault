## Problem

Fine-tuning Large Graph Models (LGMs) on full downstream datasets for Graph Continual Learning is computationally prohibitive, and existing GNTK-based dataset distillation methods are restricted to graph-level tasks, failing to generalize to node-level and edge-level tasks due to inconsistent input structures. Additionally, the O(n²d) complexity of standard GNTK computation limits scalability to large graphs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252019/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a graph corpus (adjacency matrix and node feature matrix) at any task level (graph, node, or edge) along with a validation set drawn from the same distribution. All task levels are unified via a subgraph anchoring strategy: a query subgraph is constructed around the target (node, edge pair, or whole graph) using k-hop neighborhoods. The output is a compact synthetic dataset of N_syn selected subgraphs (as few as 1 per class) used to fine-tune a pretrained LGM, producing class labels or edge predictions.

## Method

LIGHTGNTK replaces the full-rank Laplacian in GNTK computation with a low-rank approximation obtained by Bernoulli sampling over the eigenvalue spectrum (sampling probability p = r/n), reducing per-graph complexity from O(n²d) to O(nrd) while preserving both local and global structural information. A unified subgraph anchoring strategy converts node-, edge-, and graph-level tasks into a common subgraph representation, enabling a single distillation pipeline across all task types. The distillation criterion selects training subgraphs whose minimum GNTK gradient similarity to the validation set is lowest, ensuring the synthetic set best supports generalization. Theoretical guarantees (Theorems 4.1–4.2) bound the gradient and kernel approximation error in terms of the sampling probability p and the Lipschitz smoothness of the GNN. The LGM is pretrained via masked signal reconstruction and link prediction before supervised fine-tuning on the distilled set.

## Results

On graph classification, LIGHTGNTK achieves the best or near-best performance across all 7 datasets at every distillation ratio (1, 10, 50 samples/class). For example, on NCI109 with 50 graphs/class, LIGHTGNTK reaches 70.4% ACC vs. 66.8% for KIDD and 62.9% for DosCond; on ogbg-molhiv with 50 graphs/class, it achieves 70.8 ROC-AUC vs. 69.3 for KIDD and 62.0 for DosCond. On node classification (Cora, 10 nodes/class), LIGHTGNTK attains 76.2% ACC, surpassing GNTK (75.5%) and DosCond (74.2%). Computationally, LIGHTGNTK reduces distillation time by 6–27% over full GNTK (e.g., 683s vs. 921s on ogbg-molhiv) while maintaining or improving accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LIGHTGNTK consistently outperforms the coreset baselines (Random, Herding, K-Center) across all 13 datasets and all three task levels, with margins of 5–15 points in the most extreme low-data settings (e.g., 62.0 vs. 57.4 on NCI1 with 1 graph/class). Against the gradient-matching method DosCond, LIGHTGNTK wins on the majority of configurations, with the largest gaps on ogbg-molhiv (70.8 vs. 62.0 ROC-AUC at 50 graphs/class) and PubMed (70.7 vs. 61.0 ACC at 10 nodes/class). The closest competitor is KIDD (the prior GNTK-based distillation method), which is only evaluated on graph-level tasks; LIGHTGNTK edges out KIDD on NCI109 (70.4 vs. 66.8 at 50/class) and ogbg-molbace (76.8 vs. 76.6 at 50/class), while KIDD slightly leads on NCI109 at 50/class (70.4 vs. 65.6) and ogbg-molbace at 10/class (74.8 vs. 70.8). On node and edge-level tasks where KIDD is not applicable, LIGHTGNTK dominates all baselines, e.g., 29.1 vs. 24.0 Hits@50 on ogbn-arxiv (50 nodes/class) and 19.5 vs. 17.2 Hits@50 on ogbl-collab (10 links/class). Overall, LIGHTGNTK is the only method that unifies all three task levels and achieves competitive or best performance in nearly every setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the GNTK-based dataset distillation line initiated by Du et al. (2019) and operationalized for graph classification by KIDD (Xu et al., 2023b), which used kernel ridge regression to match GNTK Gram matrices. The key departures are: (1) replacing the full-rank Laplacian with a Bernoulli-sampled low-rank approximation to reduce GNTK computation from O(n²d) to O(nrd), and (2) introducing a unified subgraph-anchoring formulation that generalizes distillation from graph-level tasks (the scope of KIDD) to node-level and edge-level tasks. The theoretical grounding draws on NTK training dynamics (Jacot et al., 2020) and spectral graph theory, with new approximation-error bounds (Theorems 4.1–4.2) that KIDD did not provide.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck for Large Graph Models: fine-tuning on full downstream datasets is as expensive as pretraining, so a compact synthetic set is needed. The unified subgraph framework is a clean conceptual contribution that removes the structural mismatch between task levels. However, the efficiency gains are modest (6–27% speedup over full GNTK), and performance advantages over KIDD and DosCond are inconsistent across datasets and sample budgets (e.g., KIDD outperforms LIGHTGNTK on NCI109 at 50 samples/class). Open questions include behavior under distribution shift between pretrain and fine-tune domains, scalability to truly large graphs (ogbn-arxiv results are far from full-dataset performance), and whether the Bernoulli sampling advantage over simpler top-r selection is robust beyond the small margins observed in ablations.
