## Problem

Existing differential privacy guarantees for MCMC algorithms either require strong convexity of the target, suffer from bad dependence on the state-space radius or stepsize, or rely on composition bounds whose privacy decays with the number of iterations. This paper addresses the open problem of obtaining uniform-in-time DP guarantees for Langevin-based MCMC in a non-convex setting on an unbounded domain, and further clarifies the fundamental relationship between the privacy of the posterior distribution and the privacy of any MCMC algorithm targeting it.

## Input / Output

The method takes a dataset D (defining the posterior π_D), an initial state x_0 ∈ ℝ^d, a step size γ, and a number of iterations n, and produces either the final chain state x_n, the full trajectory (x_1, …, x_n), or a noisy Monte Carlo estimator, together with explicit (ε, δ)-DP or (α, ε)-Rényi-DP parameters. A key structural feature is that the final-state privacy bound is uniform in n (independent of the number of iterations), while the trajectory bound scales as O(n + √(n log(1/δ))) for (ε,δ)-DP and O(αn) for Rényi-DP, improving on standard composition theorems.

## Method

The core technique is a change-of-measure argument via Girsanov's theorem: for two adjacent datasets, one constructs a measure Q under which the law of the chain under D equals the law under D′, then bounds the Radon-Nikodym derivative dP/dQ to obtain DP guarantees. For the final state (uniform-in-time result), a perturbation trick is used: an auxiliary process Z_t follows the D-dynamics on [0, T−1] but is steered on [T−1, T] to coincide with X_T^{D′}, making the RN derivative depend only on a bounded one-unit-interval perturbation rather than the full trajectory. For the full trajectory, the RN derivative is bounded directly using the assumed uniform drift discrepancy |f_D − f_{D′}| ≤ c. Applied to ULA and stochastic gradient Langevin dynamics under a convexity-outside-a-ball assumption (U_D = V_D + K with ∇V_D bounded and K strongly convex with L-Lipschitz gradient), this yields dimension-independent, uniform-in-time privacy bounds in a non-convex setting, generalising prior strongly-convex results.

## Results

The paper establishes uniform-in-time (ε,δ)-DP and (α,ε)-Rényi-DP guarantees for the final state of ULA and noisy SGD in a non-convex setting, with ε_δ = C/4 + √(C log(1/δ)) independent of the number of iterations n (Theorems 5.2, 5.6). For the full trajectory, the (ε,δ)-DP bound scales as ε = O(n + √(n log(1/δ))) and the Rényi-DP bound as ε = O(αn), improving on standard composition bounds. In the strongly convex regime (Theorem 5.7), the privacy parameter for noisy SGD scales as C_7 = c²β/s² · (2(L+1)/(a−γL²/2)+1)², showing explicit improvement with stochastic gradient batch size s. All bounds are dimension-independent and do not depend on the radius of the (unbounded) state space or blow up as the step-size γ→0.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's main theoretical contribution is extending uniform-in-time DP guarantees from the strongly convex setting of Chourasia et al. (2021) to a non-convex setting (Assumption 5.1: convex outside a ball with bounded non-convex gradient), while matching their bounds in the strongly convex regime. Compared to Altschuler & Talwar (2022), whose bounds depend on the radius of the state space and blow up as the step-size γ→0, the present results are dimension-independent on an unbounded domain and remain finite as γ→0. For trajectory privacy, the paper's (ε,δ)-DP bound of ε = O(n + √(n log(1/δ))) improves on the advanced composition theorem of Kairouz et al. (2015), which gives ε = O(n + √(n log(e + ε√n/δ))), and matches the Rényi composition bound of O(αn). The perturbation trick (Proposition 4.5) is the key technical novelty enabling uniform-in-time bounds without convexity, by constructing an auxiliary process that only differs from the target on the final time interval [T−1, T].

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on DP guarantees for MCMC and Langevin-based algorithms, building most directly on Chourasia et al. (2021), which obtained uniform-in-time Rényi-DP bounds for gradient descent-type algorithms under strong convexity. The key departure is removing the convexity assumption via a novel Girsanov's theorem combined with a perturbation trick that controls the Radon-Nikodym derivative on an unbounded, non-convex domain. The paper also positions itself against prior approaches (Altschuler & Talwar 2022, Asoodeh & Diaz 2023) that suffered from bad dependence on state-space radius or stepsize, and against composition-based methods (Kairouz et al. 2015) whose privacy decays with the number of iterations. A distinct conceptual contribution is the explicit connection between the DP of the posterior distribution and the DP of any MCMC algorithm targeting it, which prior work largely overlooked by focusing on one-step transition noise.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper resolves the open problem of uniform-in-time DP guarantees in a non-convex setting for ULA and SGLD, providing concrete, dimension-independent bounds that do not blow up as the stepsize goes to zero. The finding that the posterior itself must be differentially private for the MCMC output to be both private and accurate (Propositions 3.3–3.4) is a practical design principle: one cannot simply add noise to MCMC steps and expect end-to-end privacy without also designing a private Bayesian model. Open questions remain on how dataset size m and mini-batch size s affect privacy in the non-convex noisy SGD regime, and on extending the framework to more complex settings such as Bayesian federated learning.
