## Problem

Autoregressive LLM decoding is inherently sequential, leading to poor hardware utilization (<20% MFU at inference vs. 40–60% at training). Existing asynchronous decoding methods (SoT, APAR) exploit semantic independence but rely on hand-crafted regex heuristics tied to fixed syntactic patterns (lists, paragraphs), making them rigid, imprecise, and non-scalable. No prior work trains LLMs to autonomously identify and express parallelization opportunities in their own responses.

## Input / Output

The system takes a standard text instruction prompt and produces a coherent decoded text response, identical in form to sequential decoding output. Internally, the model emits PASTA-LANG control tags (<promise/> with topic and token-count attributes, <async>…</async> blocks, and <sync/> synchronization markers) that the interpreter consumes to spawn and merge parallel decoding threads. The model predicts each async block's length in multiples of 10 tokens to assign estimated position IDs before the block is fully decoded, and the interpreter uses an interleaved KV-cache layout with per-thread attention masks to prevent cross-thread interference.

## Method

PASTA introduces a learning-based asynchronous decoding system with three components: (1) PASTA-LANG, an XML-like annotation language where <promise/> tags mark semantically independent chunks for parallel generation and <sync/> tags enforce synchronization; (2) an interpreter that launches parallel decoding threads on a single contiguous KV-cache pool with interleaved layout and directed attention masks, avoiding the memory waste of naive batched decoding; and (3) a two-stage finetuning procedure on Gemma-7B. Stage 1 (Pasta-SFT) fine-tunes on 100K Gemini-1.5-Flash-annotated SlimOrca responses with three modifications to standard next-token prediction: attention masking to enforce independence, position-ID prediction via the tokens attribute, and skip-past-promise training targets. Stage 2 applies BoNBoN preference optimization where each sampled response is scored as speedup + λ × quality (confidence-weighted LLM-judge win-loss ratio), directly optimizing the quality–latency Pareto frontier. The quality weight λ acts as a tunable knob, and repeated preference-optimization rounds continue improving the frontier without observed saturation.

## Results

On AlpacaEval (805 prompts), PASTA models achieve geometric mean speedups ranging from 1.21× to 1.93× over sequential decoding, with corresponding length-controlled win-rate changes of +2.2% to −7.1% relative to the sequential baseline. The best-quality model (Pasta-BoN-∞) reaches 52.3% win rate at 1.21× speedup, while the best-speedup model (Pasta-BoN-1) reaches 1.93× speedup at 42.9% win rate. The middle model (Pasta-BoN-2) achieves 1.62× speedup with only a ~5% win-rate drop, Pareto-dominating all prior asynchronous decoding methods. Realized speedup closely matches theoretical speedup, confirming the interpreter efficiently exploits the learned parallelism.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PASTA Pareto-dominates both prior asynchronous decoding baselines (APAR and Skeleton-of-Thought/SoT) on the quality–speedup frontier. Against APAR, Pasta-BoN-2 achieves a comparable speedup (1.62× vs. 1.6×) but a 4.3 percentage-point higher win rate (~45.5% vs. 41.2%), while Pasta-BoN-1 matches APAR's win rate (42.9% vs. 41.2%) yet delivers a 20.6% higher speedup (1.93× vs. 1.6×). Against SoT, no speedup is observed when applied to the Baseline-SFT model; even with the stronger Gemma-IT model, SoT achieves only 1.61× speedup at a 12% win-rate drop, whereas Pasta-BoN-2 achieves 1.62× speedup with just a 5% drop. The advantage over APAR is attributed to PASTA-LANG's flexibility in placing asynchronous annotations at any position, avoiding the false positives inherent in regex-based syntactic heuristics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of asynchronous LLM decoding initiated by SoT (Ning et al., 2024) and APAR (Liu et al., 2024), which identify parallelizable chunks via hand-crafted regex-based syntactic heuristics (lists, paragraphs, bullet points). PASTA departs from this by introducing a learned annotation language (PASTA-LANG) and training the LLM itself to express semantic independence, replacing fixed patterns with a flexible, learnable mechanism. The preference optimization stage builds directly on BoNBoN (Gui et al., 2024), adapting it to jointly optimize for both response quality and decoding speedup rather than quality alone. The systems design draws on approximate parallelization from the compiler literature (Udupa et al., 2011; Misailovic et al., 2012), where breakable dependencies are annotated and exploited at runtime, but here the LLM autonomously produces the annotations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LLM inference latency is a major practical bottleneck, and PASTA offers a scalable path: unlike heuristic-based methods that require manual engineering for each new pattern, PASTA converts additional training compute directly into better quality-speedup tradeoffs with no observed saturation after two preference optimization rounds. However, evaluation is limited to Gemma 7B on a single benchmark (AlpacaEval, 805 prompts), leaving open whether the approach generalizes to larger models, multi-turn conversations, or domains with less naturally parallelizable structure. The KV-cache interleaving design, while elegant, assumes fixed batch size and sequence length for compilation, which may limit deployment flexibility. A key open question is whether the learned annotations remain effective as model scale increases and whether the quality-speedup frontier continues to improve with more iterations beyond the two tested.
