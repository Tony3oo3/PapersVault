## Problem

Existing test-time adaptation methods for 3D human pose estimation rely on 2D projection loss and temporal smoothing, which suffer from depth ambiguity (many 3D poses share the same 2D projection) and over-smoothing that collapses predictions toward average poses. When 2D keypoints are missing due to occlusion or truncation, adaptation becomes entirely unguided. No prior TTA work incorporates high-level motion semantics to constrain the 3D solution space to a semantically meaningful subset.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 224×224 RGB video frames segmented into 60-frame clips, OpenPose 2D estimates with per-keypoint visibility flags (confidence threshold 0.3), and a single text label per segment retrieved from the BABEL motion dictionary via GPT-4o. It outputs adapted SMPL parameters (θ, β, t) and iteratively refined 2D pseudo-labels. A key constraint is that all frames within a segment must share the same text label; segments with mixed motions are discarded to avoid semantic misalignment.

## Method

The core mechanism is a semantics-aware motion prior built on MotionCLIP: predicted SMPL rotations are converted to 6D format, their global rotation is replaced with that of a retrieved exemplar motion (to isolate text-relevant local poses), and the resulting motion feature is aligned with the text label's CLIP embedding via a cosine-similarity loss (L_align). A complementary 2D pose update module refines existing 2D estimates via EMA (α=0.9) and fills in missing keypoints using the model's own 2D projection when the motion-text similarity exceeds a threshold (σ=0.75), providing supervision for occluded or truncated body parts. The overall per-epoch objective combines 2D projection loss, the motion-text alignment loss, and CycleAdapt's motion-denoising smoothing loss, with only the HMR network updated while the motion encoder and CLIP model remain frozen. Weighted sampling prioritizes segments whose predicted motion deviates most from the text label, focusing adaptation where it is needed most.

## Results

On 3DPW, the method achieves 76.4 mm MPJPE and 47.2 mm PA-MPJPE, outperforming the strongest baseline CycleAdapt (87.7 / 53.8) by 12.9% and 12.3% respectively. On 3DHP, it reaches 101.3 mm MPJPE and 65.1 mm PA-MPJPE, improving over CycleAdapt (110.3 / 74.4) by 8.2% and 12.5%. With the stronger HMR2.0 backbone on EgoBody, the method further reduces PA-MPJPE from 60.4 (CycleAdapt) to 56.1 mm, confirming gains transfer to state-of-the-art backbones.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all five baselines on every reported metric across both 3DPW and 3DHP. Against the closest competitor CycleAdapt (Nam et al., ICCV 2023), it reduces MPJPE by 12.9% on 3DPW (87.7→76.4) and by 8.2% on 3DHP (110.3→101.3), with PA-MPJPE improvements of 12.3% and 12.5% respectively. Earlier TTA methods that rely on ground-truth 2D labels (BOA, DynaBOA, DAPA) are substantially worse: BOA and DynaBOA score 137.6 and 135.1 MPJPE on 3DPW, while DAPA reaches 108.0, all far above the proposed 76.4. The pre-trained HMR without any adaptation (230.3 MPJPE on 3DPW) underscores the magnitude of the domain gap the method addresses. On the harder EgoBody dataset with HMR2.0, the method still beats CycleAdapt by 4.3 mm PA-MPJPE (60.4→56.1), showing the semantic prior generalizes beyond the original HMR backbone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the test-time adaptation (TTA) line for 3D human pose estimation initiated by BOA, DynaBOA, DAPA, and most directly CycleAdapt, which relies on 2D projection loss and temporal smoothing (motion denoising) to refine predictions. The key departure is the introduction of a semantics-aware motion prior: by projecting predicted motion and a VLM-generated text label into MotionCLIP's shared embedding space, the authors constrain the 2D-to-3D solution space to a semantically coherent subset, addressing the over-smoothing and unguided-prediction failures that purely geometric TTA methods exhibit. The 2D pose completion mechanism (filling missing keypoints from text-aligned motion predictions) further departs from prior TTA, which simply discards unreliable 2D evidence rather than synthesizing replacements.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and addresses a concrete failure mode in TTA for 3D pose estimation—temporal smoothing collapsing predictions to average poses and leaving occluded/truncated body parts entirely unguided—yielding 12%+ PA-MPJPE improvements over the strongest baseline. However, the approach is constrained by MotionCLIP's fixed motion vocabulary (limiting generalization to novel actions), a 3.6% VLM hallucination rate, and added runtime (~33 ms/frame, mostly from GPT-4o). Open questions include whether the method handles motion blur, whether step-frequency and timing under occlusion can be recovered more faithfully, and whether more flexible motion-language models (beyond MotionCLIP) would further close the gap.
