## Problem

Progressive sharpening—the increase of loss sharpness during gradient descent before saturating at the edge of stability—is widely observed but its underlying mechanisms remain poorly understood. In particular, how dataset properties, network depth, optimizer stochasticity, and step size jointly govern the degree of progressive sharpening has lacked a unified theoretical explanation grounded in a tractable model.

## Input / Output

The minimalist model takes a data matrix X ∈ R^{N×d}, a label vector y ∈ R^N (assumed in col(X) for zero-loss attainability), and parameters θ = (u, v_1, …, v_{D-1}) where u ∈ R^d is the first-layer weight and each v_i ∈ R is a scalar. It produces closed-form sharpness bounds at initialization and convergence parameterized by the dataset difficulty Q = Σ d_i²/σ_i² and the layer imbalance C(θ) = ‖Π_W u‖² − v_1², along with a predicted sharpness quantity Ŝ_D = (σ_1²/N) Q^{(D-1)/D} that can be computed from the data alone without running training.

## Method

The paper introduces a deep linear network with a single neuron per layer (width 1, identity activation) as a minimalist testbed that reproduces progressive sharpening, edge-of-stability oscillations, and their dependence on dataset size, depth, batch size, and learning rate. The key theoretical tool is the dataset difficulty Q, which captures the total 'distance to travel' for fitting the data, and the layer imbalance C(θ), which is shown to be exactly conserved under gradient flow (D=2) or to preserve balancedness (D>2), enabling sharpness bounds at convergence to be determined entirely from initialization. For discrete optimizers, a theorem characterizes the per-step increase in C(θ) for GD and SGD, showing that SGD (especially with small batch B and large step size η) increases C more than GD, which via the sharpness–imbalance relationship explains why SGD induces less progressive sharpening. The predicted sharpness Ŝ_D correlates strongly (r ≈ 0.99) with empirical post-training sharpness even for wider, nonlinear networks, validating the minimalist model as a predictive tool beyond its theoretical scope.

## Results

The paper's headline empirical result is that the predicted sharpness $\hat{S}_D = \frac{\sigma_1^2}{N} Q^{(D-1)/D}$ correlates strongly with actual post-training sharpness: correlation 0.99 for a 5-layer identity-activation linear network on CIFAR-10 (N=100) and 0.81 for a 4-layer tanh network (width 1024) on SVHN (N=300). Dataset difficulty Q grows substantially with dataset size (e.g., CIFAR-10: Q≈0.22 at N=100, Q≈44.44 at N=1000; SVHN: Q≈1.16 at N=100, Q≈859.4 at N=1000), and the predicted sharpness $\hat{S}_D$ increases with both Q and depth D when Q>1. The minimalist model (single-neuron-per-layer deep linear network) successfully replicates progressive sharpening, edge-of-stability oscillation around 2/η, and the attenuated oscillation decay observed in a Transformer trained on SST-2.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical analysis paper without direct method-vs-method comparisons; the `compares_to` list is empty. Instead, the paper validates its theory in two ways: (1) the minimalist model's sharpness dynamics are compared qualitatively against Cohen et al. (2021) observations (Phenomenon 1) and a Transformer on SST-2, showing matching progressive-sharpening trends and edge-of-stability oscillation patterns; (2) the predicted sharpness $\hat{S}_D$ is compared numerically against empirically measured post-training sharpness $S(\theta(\infty))$ across 50 random 2-label subsets, yielding correlations of 0.99 (linear) and 0.81 (tanh). The paper also contrasts its results with prior minimalist-model analyses (Zhu et al. 2023, Kreisler et al. 2023, Kalra et al. 2025), noting that those models fail to reproduce the non-monotonic loss spikes and attenuated oscillation at the edge of stability, whereas the proposed single-neuron-per-layer model captures both. For GD vs. SGD, Theorem 5.9 shows that smaller batch size B and larger step size η increase layer imbalance C more, leading to lower final sharpness—consistent with the empirical observation that SGD with small batches exhibits less progressive sharpening than GD.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Cohen et al. (2021), whose controlled experiments formalized progressive sharpening and the edge of stability (EoS) and identified key factors (dataset size, depth, batch size, learning rate) that the authors summarize as Phenomenon 1. It departs from prior minimalist-model analyses (Zhu et al. 2023, Kreisler et al. 2023, Kalra et al. 2025) that study scalar or single-data-point settings by considering general training data, and from Marion & Chizat (2024) who provide only upper bounds on sharpness in deep linear networks, by deriving both upper and lower bounds tied to a new dataset-difficulty quantity Q. The self-stabilization mechanism of Damian et al. (2023) motivates the EoS analysis but assumes progressive sharpening occurs; this paper instead explains *why* and *how much* sharpening occurs via layer imbalance dynamics under GD/SGD.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The minimalist model (single neuron per layer) provides a rare simple testbed that simultaneously reproduces progressive sharpening, EoS oscillations, and non-monotonic loss spikes—behaviors that other minimalist models fail to capture—making it a useful platform for future theoretical work. The dataset difficulty Q and predicted sharpness Ŝ_D offer a quantitative, architecture-independent tool that empirically predicts post-training sharpness with high correlation (0.99) even for nonlinear, wide networks far outside the theoretical setup. Open questions include why EoS oscillation amplitude decays over time (which appears tied to machine precision), how to extend the analysis to nonlinear activations beyond the preliminary Appendix E, and whether the layer-imbalance mechanism generalizes to wider or more complex architectures.
