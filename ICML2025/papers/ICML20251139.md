## Problem

While LLMs finetuned on reasoning datasets often reach near-perfect training accuracy, their test performance varies dramatically depending on hyperparameters like learning rate, making it unclear which aspects of the training process drive genuine generalization versus superficial memorization. Prior generalization metrics (gradient variance, distance from initialization, ATC) have not been shown to reliably predict test accuracy in LLM reasoning finetuning, leaving practitioners without a practical signal to guide training decisions.

## Input / Output

The method consumes the full training trajectory of a finetuned LLM: at each checkpoint, it evaluates the model's sampled responses on all training queries and computes both answer accuracy and the perplexity of the target solution trace under the model. A fixed perplexity threshold p (calibrated per model-task pair via a small sweep) defines when an example is 'memorized.' The output is a per-example scalar (pre-memorization train accuracy) that, when averaged over the dataset, predicts test accuracy with R² ≥ 0.9, and at the individual level predicts robustness to prompt perturbations and guides which examples to prioritize for new data collection.

## Method

The paper introduces pre-memorization train accuracy, a metric that captures the best accuracy a model achieves on a training example before it begins verbatim-copying the target solution trace (i.e., before the trace's perplexity under the model drops below threshold p). Formally, a masked accuracy is computed at each epoch (zeroed out once memorization is detected), and the pre-memorization accuracy is the maximum of these masked values, further capped by the model's accuracy at the current epoch to handle rare accuracy-decrease cases. The key empirical finding is that the dataset-average of this metric tracks test accuracy almost perfectly across Llama3 8B, Gemma2 9B, GSM8K, and MATH under varied learning rates, epochs, and dataset sizes. At the per-example level, low pre-memorization accuracy identifies examples where the model's predictions are fragile to small prompt perturbations (e.g., prepending 'First' or 'We know that'), indicating the model has learned a brittle verbatim mapping rather than a robust solution. Leveraging this signal, the paper proposes an iterative data curation strategy that prioritizes collecting new examples (via GPT rephrasing) from the subset with the lowest pre-memorization accuracy, achieving 1.5–2× sample efficiency gains over i.i.d. sampling and standard difficulty-based curation baselines.

## Results

Pre-memorization train accuracy predicts test accuracy with R² of 0.94 (GSM8K/Llama3 8B), 0.95 (MATH/Llama3 8B), 0.97 (GSM8K/Gemma2 9B), and 0.88 (MATH/Gemma2 9B) across varying learning rates, epochs, and dataset sizes. In data curation experiments, prioritizing examples with low pre-memorization accuracy achieves 2× sample efficiency over i.i.d. scaling on GSM8K and 1.5× on MATH levels 1–3. The metric also outperforms prior generalization predictors, achieving a correlation of 0.98 with the generalization gap versus −0.72 (gradient variance), 0.59 (distance from initialization), and −0.04 (ATC).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against prior generalization metrics, pre-memorization train accuracy (correlation 0.98) substantially outperforms gradient variance (−0.72), distance from initialization (0.59), and ATC (−0.04), all evaluated on GSM8K with Llama3 8B. In data curation, the proposed approach (prioritizing low pre-memorization accuracy examples) achieves 2× sample efficiency over i.i.d. sampling on GSM8K and 1.5× on MATH levels 1–3, outperforming both the IFD metric (Li et al., 2023) and heuristic difficulty scores (line count for GSM8K, dataset-provided levels for MATH). The performance gap over baselines widens with dataset size, suggesting the advantage grows as models become more capable. The method also wins on per-example robustness: examples with high pre-memorization accuracy retain correct answers under prompt perturbations (e.g., prepending 'First' or 'We know that'), while low pre-memorization examples degrade significantly.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the classical memorization literature (Feldman & Zhang, Arpit et al.) that relies on computationally expensive leave-one-out gaps or worst-case generalization bounds, instead proposing a direct, empirically measurable metric grounded in perplexity-based trace matching. It also contrasts with prior generalization-prediction metrics (gradient variance, distance from initialization, ATC) that the authors show fail to correlate well with test accuracy in LLM reasoning finetuning. On the data curation side, the work aligns with model-based selection strategies (Li et al. 2023 IFD, Mekala et al. 2024) but replaces perplexity-ratio or heuristic difficulty scores with a learning-dynamics-derived criterion, carving new ground by tying curation decisions to the temporal structure of memorization during training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The pre-memorization accuracy metric offers a cheap, interpretable, and highly predictive (R² ≥ 0.9) proxy for test performance that requires no held-out data or expensive influence-function computations, making it practical for guiding training decisions in real time. The 1.5–2× sample-efficiency gain in data curation demonstrates concrete utility, and the per-example robustness signal opens a path toward targeted interventions on fragile training examples. Key limitations include evaluation restricted to 8B–9B models and math-reasoning tasks, manually constructed perturbation preambles, and a single memorization threshold p that must be recalibrated per model/task pair; whether the phenomenon persists at larger scales or for non-mathematical reasoning remains open.
