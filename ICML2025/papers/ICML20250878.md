## Problem

Existing backdoor defenses in federated learning rely on individual client-to-server distance metrics, predefined scaling thresholds, or external proxy datasets, all of which can be bypassed by adaptive attackers and degrade when the number of malicious clients changes. These approaches ignore inter-client behavioral communication and cannot dynamically adapt to varying attack scales without manual rule tuning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250878/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes local model parameters (from CNN or ResNet-18 backbones) and the global model parameters at each communication round, along with each client's local private dataset (potentially poisoned with trigger-labeled samples). It produces reweighted aggregation weights computed via softmax over negative normalized cosine-similarity margin contributions, a purified global model, and locally aligned gradient directions. Data heterogeneity is simulated via Dirichlet label skew (β=0.5), and the method operates on 28×28 grayscale (MNIST, FashionMNIST) and 32×32 RGB (CIFAR-10/100) images.

## Method

SPMC introduces two coupled modules inspired by the Shapley Value from cooperative game theory. On the server side, Margin Contribution Aggregation (MCAgg) computes each client's margin contribution as the cosine similarity between its parameters and the coalition model (the weighted average of all other clients), then reweights aggregation via softmax of the negative normalized contributions, penalizing clients whose update direction deviates from the collaborative norm. On the client side, Local Gradient Alignment (LGAlign) performs non-proxy knowledge distillation using the coalition model as a virtual teacher: it computes a KL-divergence-based general-knowledge gradient and, when the local cross-entropy gradient conflicts with it (dot product < 0), projects the local gradient onto the orthogonal complement of the general-knowledge direction to prevent overfitting to poisoned samples. This eliminates the need for predefined thresholds, external clean datasets, or fixed scaling rules, making the defense adaptive to varying numbers of attackers.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with γ=0.2, SPMC achieves a trade-off score V=76.05 (A=66.78, R=85.32), outperforming the strongest baseline FoolsGold (V=74.15) and the predefined-scale method DnC (V=70.36). On the more challenging CIFAR-100 with γ=0.2, SPMC reaches V=57.20, exceeding the best counterpart by 10.43 percentage points. Under the higher attack ratio γ=0.3 on CIFAR-10, SPMC attains V=72.98 (R=80.14), while most baselines degrade substantially (e.g., Bulyan drops to V=45.00, CRFL to V=47.47). The method maintains competitive clean accuracy (A) while substantially boosting backdoor failure rate (R) across all four datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SPMC outperforms all 10 baselines on the V (trade-off) metric across every dataset and attack ratio. Against the strongest 'no predefined scale' baseline FoolsGold, SPMC gains +1.90 V on CIFAR-10 (γ=0.2) and +25.92 V on CIFAR-100 (γ=0.2), where FoolsGold's clean accuracy collapses to 24.92. Predefined-scale methods like DnC show competitive R at γ=0.2 (80.93 on CIFAR-10) but degrade sharply at γ=0.3 (R drops to 63.35), while SPMC maintains R=80.14. Bulyan and RSA are the most fragile: Bulyan's clean accuracy crashes to 10.61 on CIFAR-10 (γ=0.2) and RSA fails entirely on CIFAR-100. RFA achieves very high backdoor removal (R≈0.18 on FashionMNIST) but at the cost of low clean accuracy, yielding the lowest V among non-collapsing methods. CRFL shows the worst clean accuracy on CIFAR-100 (26.50 at γ=0.2), indicating its clipping strategy overly suppresses legitimate updates. Overall, SPMC is the only method that simultaneously maintains high clean accuracy (within ~3% of FedAvg) and achieves strong backdoor failure rates (>60% R) across all settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in federated backdoor defense, which relies on individual client-to-server distance metrics (FoolsGold, DnC), statistical outlier detection (Bulyan, RFA), or predefined threshold rules (CRFL, RLR). Its conceptual foundation is the Shapley Value from cooperative game theory (Ghorbani et al., 2020), repurposed here as an efficient approximation (Eq. 4) that measures each client's marginal contribution relative to the coalition of all other clients, rather than relative to the global model alone. The client-side self-purification module further draws on knowledge distillation (Hinton et al., 2015) and gradient alignment (Rame et al., 2022), but applies KL divergence against a coalition model rather than a teacher network, eliminating the need for proxy data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SPMC addresses a genuine gap: existing defenses degrade when the number of attackers changes because they rely on fixed thresholds or individual distance heuristics, whereas the margin-contribution formulation adapts automatically to the coalition composition. However, the evaluation is limited to a single BadNets-style attack with a fixed 2×6 trigger, four relatively small image datasets, and Dirichlet heterogeneity only; generalization to adaptive attacks, larger-scale deployments, or non-Dirichlet data distributions remains untested. An open question is whether the coalition model approximation (Eq. 5), which assumes the global model is a simple weighted average of all clients, holds under more complex aggregation strategies or asynchronous FL settings.
