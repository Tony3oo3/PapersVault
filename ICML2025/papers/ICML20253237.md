## Problem

Existing RL-based alpha discovery methods (e.g., AlphaGen) treat the search for synergistic formulaic alphas as a standard MDP, ignoring two critical properties: the reward function is non-stationary because it changes whenever the alpha pool is updated, and rewards are sparse because most generated alphas contribute zero incremental IC. These unaddressed issues cause inefficient exploration and unstable training, particularly in large, complex market datasets.

## Input / Output

The agent operates on RPN token sequences (capped at 20 tokens) built from a fixed vocabulary of time-series operators (e.g., TsRank, EMA), cross-sectional operators (e.g., Rank, Corr), and raw features (OHLCV, constants, time deltas). Each completed token sequence is parsed into a formulaic alpha, evaluated against a pool of up to P alphas via a linear meta-alpha, and scored by the change in cross-sectional IC against 20-day forward returns. The final output is a set of interpretable formulaic alphas with linear combination weights that produce a predictive signal for N stocks.

## Method

AlphaQCM formulates alpha discovery as a non-stationary, reward-sparse MDP and solves it with a dual-network DRL architecture: an IQN-based quantile network learns the distribution of discounted cumulative rewards, while a separate DQN-based Q network estimates the mean Q-function. The key insight is that the Quantiled Conditional Moments (QCM) method, grounded in the Cornish-Fisher expansion, recovers an unbiased variance estimate from the (potentially biased) quantiles via OLS regression of quantiles on Gaussian polynomial features. This unbiased variance is injected as an exploration bonus in the action-selection rule (argmax over Q̂ + λ√ĥ), steering the agent toward high-uncertainty states that yield the most informative experiences. The combination of IQN's random quantile sampling, prioritized experience replay, and the QCM variance bonus allows the agent to efficiently navigate the dynamic reward landscape without requiring the quantile estimates themselves to be unbiased.

## Results

AlphaQCM achieves out-of-sample Information Coefficient (IC) values of 8.49%, 9.55%, and 9.16% on the CSI 300, CSI 500, and full Market datasets, respectively, consistently outperforming all baselines. The advantage over the closest competitor AlphaGen (8.13%, 8.08%, 6.04%) widens substantially as the number of stocks increases, with a 3.12-percentage-point gap on the Market dataset. On the S&P 500 dataset, AlphaQCM achieves 8.46% IC versus 7.48% for AlphaGen, confirming cross-market generalizability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Alpha101 (Kakushadze, 2016), AlphaQCM achieves roughly 2.5× the IC on all three Chinese datasets (8.49% vs. 3.44% on CSI 300; 9.55% vs. 4.38% on CSI 500; 9.16% vs. 3.15% on Market), demonstrating that RL-discovered formulaic alphas substantially surpass human-designed ones. Against AlphaGen (Yu et al., KDD 2023), the closest RL-based competitor, AlphaQCM wins on every dataset: 8.49% vs. 8.13% (CSI 300), 9.55% vs. 8.08% (CSI 500), and 9.16% vs. 6.04% (Market). The performance gap over AlphaGen grows with system complexity, suggesting that AlphaGen's PPO-based approach struggles more with non-stationarity and reward-sparsity as the stock universe expands. On the S&P 500, AlphaQCM also leads AlphaGen (8.46% vs. 7.48%), though all methods show lower IC than on CSI 500, likely reflecting the greater efficiency of the U.S. market.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the AlphaGen framework (Yu et al., 2023), which first cast formulaic alpha discovery as a sequential decision-making problem solvable by RL (specifically PPO), by replacing the policy-gradient backbone with a distributional RL architecture (IQN for quantiles + DQN for Q-values) and introducing the QCM method (Zhang & Zhu, 2023) to derive an unbiased variance estimate from potentially biased quantiles. The key conceptual departure from AlphaGen is the explicit treatment of non-stationarity (reward function shifts as the alpha pool updates) and reward-sparsity (most generated alphas yield zero reward), which AlphaGen left unaddressed. The QCM variance, computed via Cornish-Fisher expansion and OLS regression on quantile estimates, serves as a principled exploration bonus that remains unbiased even when the underlying quantile estimates are biased due to non-stationarity—a property not shared by vanilla quantile-based variance estimators.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that addressing non-stationarity and reward-sparsity through distributional RL with QCM-based exploration yields substantially better alpha discovery, particularly as market complexity grows (IC of 9.16% vs. 6.04% for AlphaGen on the full Market dataset). The theoretical guarantee that QCM variance remains unbiased under biased quantiles provides a principled foundation for exploration in dynamic environments beyond finance. Open questions include whether the linear meta-alpha combination is optimal, whether the approach scales to even larger or more heterogeneous markets, and whether the QCM exploration bonus generalizes to other non-stationary, reward-sparse RL problems (e.g., robotics, recommendation). A noted limitation is that Q-function estimates remain biased under non-stationarity; the method mitigates but does not eliminate this issue.
