## Problem

Deep learning phenomena such as double descent, grokking, and the lottery ticket hypothesis have generated enormous research interest, yet the community largely treats them as isolated puzzles requiring bespoke, case-by-case explanations. This position paper argues that many of these phenomena are edge cases with limited practical relevance, and that the prevailing norm of developing narrow ad hoc hypotheses to explain them in isolation is an inefficient use of research effort that may not advance the broader field of deep learning.

## Input / Output

The paper takes as its analytical subject the existing literature on three prominent deep learning phenomena (double descent, grokking, and the lottery ticket hypothesis), examining their practical relevance and the nature of the explanations developed for them. It produces a conceptual framework for distinguishing narrow ad hoc hypotheses from broad explanatory theories, a self-evaluation checklist for researchers at each stage of the research process, and practical recommendations organized into three categories: identification and cataloging, prioritizing utility, and following scientific principles.

## Method

The paper's core analytical contribution is a two-axis framework: a pragmatic axis grounded in sociotechnical pragmatism that evaluates explanations by their downstream utility rather than mere descriptive accuracy, and a scientific axis advocating for rigorous methodology including falsifiability, preregistration, and reconciliation among competing explanations. To concretely illustrate the distinction between narrow ad hoc hypotheses and broad explanatory theories, the authors construct a deliberately absurd example showing that both double descent and grokking can be 'explained' by the number of prime numbers in network parameters—a technically true but practically useless explanation that captures the pathology of post-hoc fitting. The framework is then applied to three case studies, demonstrating that while each phenomenon is largely absent from practical settings, it has nonetheless catalyzed methodological developments with broader implications (e.g., layer-wise learning rate heuristics from double descent, stable softmax alternatives from grokking, and sparsification and quantization techniques from lottery tickets). Practical recommendations are grounded in a self-evaluation checklist spanning early-stage problem identification, continuous reassessment of utility during the research process, and concluding considerations on cataloging and pathways to practical utility.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This position paper does not compare methods against baselines in the traditional experimental sense, as it proposes no new method. Instead, it qualitatively contrasts two research strategies: (1) narrow ad hoc hypotheses (isolated, overfit explanations for a specific phenomenon) versus (2) broad explanatory theories (general principles refined through the phenomenon). The paper argues the latter is more valuable, using double descent, grokking, and the lottery ticket hypothesis as case studies. For each phenomenon, it demonstrates practical irrelevance (e.g., double descent disappears under standard regularization; grokking is limited to small algorithmic datasets; lottery tickets cannot be identified a priori) while highlighting broader value (e.g., double descent motivated reevaluation of the bias-variance tradeoff; grokking inspired progress measures and numerical stability work; lottery tickets influenced sparsity, quantization, and parameter-efficient fine-tuning). The paper also includes a deliberately absurd 'prime network parameters' example (Figure 4) to illustrate the futility of narrow ad hoc explanations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This position paper does not build on a specific method but rather reframes the conceptual landscape around deep learning phenomena research. It draws on the original introductions of double descent (Belkin et al., 2019), grokking (Power et al., 2022), and the lottery ticket hypothesis (Frankle & Carbin, 2019), while grounding its methodological critique in Popper's philosophy of falsifiability, the concept of ad hoc hypotheses (Leplin, 1975), and the sociotechnical pragmatism framework of Watson et al. (2024). It departs from the prevailing community norm of treating each phenomenon as an isolated puzzle requiring a bespoke resolution, instead advocating for a unified scientific-pragmatic lens that prioritizes broad explanatory theories over narrow descriptions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper challenges a research direction that has generated thousands of citations, dedicated workshops at every major conference, and hundreds of main-track papers, arguing that much of this effort may be misaligned with practical progress. Its practical recommendations (cataloging phenomena, explicitly connecting findings to broader theories, applying preregistration and replication) are actionable but their adoption depends on community and venue-level norm shifts. Key open questions include how to operationalize the ad-hoc-vs-broad distinction in peer review, how to measure the 'utility' of an explanation beyond the phenomenon it describes, and whether the pragmatic framing adequately captures long-term foundational contributions that lack immediate downstream impact.
