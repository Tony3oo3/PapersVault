## Problem

Conventional conditional diffusion models for time series forecasting use a fixed, non-learnable forward process (linear Gaussian noising) and an isotropic Gaussian prior N(0,I), which limits the model's capacity to capture complex temporal patterns and makes the reverse denoising process particularly difficult for high-dimensional multivariate series. This paper addresses both limitations by making the forward process learnable through nonlinear data transformations and by introducing a learnable condition that produces a data-dependent prior.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252065/cropped_page2_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a lookback window of historical multivariate time series (length L ∈ {96, 192, 336, 720, 1440}, d variables) and produces a forecast window of H future steps. The condition c is derived from the historical data via a single dense layer, and the nonlinear transformation T_φ is implemented as two dense layers with tanh activation—one operating along the feature dimension and one along the forecast window dimension. The encoder uses DiT blocks with adaptive layer normalization, while embeddings and the decoder use TiDE-style MLP modules.

## Method

CN-Diff introduces a nonlinear, time-dependent transformation T_φ(x, t) into the forward noising process, replacing the fixed linear scaling with a learnable operator that produces a non-Markovian latent sequence. A learnable condition c is embedded directly in the forward process (not just the reverse), yielding a condition-dependent prior N(c, I) at the terminal step rather than the standard N(0, I). Because the forward process becomes non-Markovian, the reverse process is formulated as a non-Markovian generative chain, and the variational loss must include prior and reconstruction terms that depend on the transformation parameters φ (unlike DDPM where these are constant). The diffusion term is computed as a KL divergence between the true posterior q_φ(x^{t-1}|x^t, x, c) and the approximate reverse p_θ(x^{t-1}|x^t, c) parameterized by the predicted clean data x̂_θ, making the model predict both the original data and the transformed data T_φ(x, t) at each step.

## Results

CN-Diff achieves an average rank of 1.7 across nine real-world multivariate time series datasets, ranking first on 6 of 9 (NorPool 0.531, Caiso 0.094, Electricity 0.145, Weather 0.296, Exchange 0.016, ETTh1 0.405 MSE) and second on Traffic (0.374) and ETTm1 (0.340) with narrow margins. The only dataset where CN-Diff does not rank in the top 2 is Wind (MSE 0.988, rank 5), where the multi-resolution mr-Diff model (0.881) excels due to its multi-scale strategy. In univariate forecasting, CN-Diff ranks first on 4 of 9 datasets and second on 2, again achieving the best average rank overall. Computational analysis shows CN-Diff is the fastest diffusion-based method, with training times of 0.21–0.39 ms and inference times of 6.2–9.1 ms on ETTh1, roughly 2–3× faster than mr-Diff and TimeDiff.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CN-Diff outperforms all five diffusion-based baselines (mr-Diff, TimeDiff, TimeGrad, CSDI, SSSD) on average, with the closest competitor being mr-Diff (avg rank 3.0 vs. 1.7), which only wins on the highly volatile Wind dataset (MSE 0.881 vs. 0.988). Against non-diffusion methods, CN-Diff dominates transformer-based models (Informer, Autoformer, PatchTST, FedFormer, Pyraformer, Scaleformer, Transformer) and basis-expansion methods (FiLM, NBeats, Depts, N-Hits), achieving rank 1 on 6 datasets where these methods typically rank 5th or lower. Among the strongest non-diffusion baselines, NLlinear (rank 5.2) and SCINet (rank 6.4) are the closest, but CN-Diff still leads by a clear margin on complex multivariate datasets like Electricity (0.145 vs. 0.147/0.171) and NorPool (0.531 vs. 0.707/0.613). The ablation study confirms that the nonlinear transformation T_φ contributes the largest single improvement (54–73% MSE reduction over plain DDPM), while the forward-process condition adds a further 8–21% gain that scales with feature dimensionality. On the additional CSI-300 stock dataset, CN-Diff achieves MSE 0.020 vs. 0.028 (PatchTST) and 0.040 (FiLM), confirming generalizability beyond the nine primary benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

CN-Diff extends the DDPM/DDIM diffusion framework by making the forward process learnable through two modifications: a nonlinear time-dependent data transformation T_φ and a learnable condition c embedded in the forward (not just reverse) process. This departs from all prior conditional diffusion time series models (TimeDiff, TimeGrad, CSDI, mr-Diff), which keep the forward process as a fixed linear Gaussian noising chain and apply conditioning only during denoising. The resulting non-Markovian forward process necessitates a non-Markovian reverse process, and the condition-dependent prior N(c, I) replaces the standard isotropic N(0, I), reducing the gap between the true negative log-likelihood and its variational approximation. Architecturally, the denoising network uses DiT blocks with adaptive layer normalization and TiDE-style MLPs, but the core contribution is in the diffusion formulation rather than the network design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical takeaway is that learnable forward-process modifications (nonlinear transform + condition) yield large gains (54–92% MSE reduction in ablations) while simultaneously making CN-Diff the fastest diffusion-based time series forecaster in both training and inference, suggesting that formulation-level improvements can outperform architectural complexity. The finding that the condition's benefit scales with feature dimension (8% for dim 7 → 21% for dim 862) points to a clear regime where isotropic Gaussian priors become inadequate. Open questions include whether multi-resolution CN-Diff can close the gap on highly volatile data (Wind, rank 5), whether the approach extends to imputation and anomaly detection as the authors suggest, and whether the non-Markovian reverse process can be further simplified for faster sampling.
