## Problem

Applying State Space Models to graph learning faces a fundamental challenge: transforming a static graph into a sequence without sacrificing permutation equivariance. Existing graph SSM approaches either order nodes heuristically (breaking equivariance) or treat only pairwise edge interactions as sequences, failing to exploit the long-range sequence-processing capacity of SSMs. Meanwhile, standard MPNNs suffer from oversquashing, which degrades long-range information propagation.

## Input / Output

The method takes a static graph G=(V,E) with node features f ∈ R^{n×c} and an adjacency matrix. The key design choice is that the static graph is expanded into a sequence of L graphs by stacking node features L times and applying position-specific MLPs to embed them into d channels, yielding an initial state sequence F^(0) ∈ R^{L×n×d} and a residual sequence Δ^(0). The output is the final state matrix from the last GRAMA block, fed through a readout MLP for node-level (O(n·d·o)) or graph-level (O(d·o)) predictions.

## Method

GRAMA processes the graph sequence via a neural ARMA(p,q) recurrence where autoregressive coefficients weight previous node-feature states and moving-average coefficients weight residuals, with the current-step residual computed by a GNN backbone (GCN, GatedGCN, or GPS) applied without nonlinearity to keep each recurrence linear. The ARMA coefficients are learned adaptively through a selective attention mechanism over the state and residual sequences (inspired by Mamba's input-dependent selection), with scores normalized to [−1,1] under a sum-to-one constraint to ensure stability. The paper proves GRAMA is exactly equivalent to a linear SSM, derives stability conditions via the spectral radius of the state matrix, and shows that initializing eigenvalues near the unit circle biases the model toward long-range propagation. Multiple GRAMA blocks are stacked with elementwise nonlinearities between them, and the sequence length L, number of recurrences R, and number of blocks S are hyperparameters (typically L=R=p=q≤50, S≤4).

## Results

On the Long-Range Graph Benchmark (LRGB), GRAMA_GCN achieves 70.93 AP on Peptides-func and 0.2439 MAE on Peptides-struct, outperforming all MPNN, DE-GNN, and Graph SSM baselines. On the synthetic Graph Property Prediction tasks, GRAMA_GPS achieves the best log₁₀(MSE) across all three tasks (Diameter: −0.87, SSSP: −3.93, Eccentricity: −1.30), reducing error by over 1.2 points vs. SWAN and 1.7 points vs. A-DGN on Eccentricity. GRAMA consistently boosts its backbone: GCN gains ~11.6 AP and GatedGCN gains ~11.9 AP on Peptides-func over their unmodified versions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GRAMA_GCN (70.93 AP) outperforms all standard MPNNs by large margins: GCN (59.30), GatedGCN (58.64), GAT, GraphSAGE, GIN, and GCNII (Table 1), with gains of 11–13 AP over the weakest baselines. Against DE-GNNs, GRAMA beats GRAND (57.89), GraphCON (60.22), A-DGN (59.75), and SWAN (67.51) by 3.4–13.0 AP on Peptides-func. Among Graph SSMs, GRAMA_GCN edges out GMN (70.71) by 0.22 AP and Graph-Mamba (67.39) by 3.54 AP, while also achieving the best MAE (0.2439) on Peptides-struct. The only method that narrowly beats GRAMA_GCN on Peptides-func is DRew-GCN+LapPE (71.50 AP, +0.57), though GRAMA_GCN still achieves a lower MAE (0.2439 vs. 0.2536). Compared to Graph Transformers (GPS+LapPE: 65.35, Transformer+LapPE: 63.26, SAN+LapPE: 63.84), GRAMA gains 4.5–7.7 AP without requiring positional encodings. On the synthetic Graph Property Prediction tasks, GRAMA_GPS dominates all baselines, improving over SWAN by 1.2+ log₁₀(MSE) points on Eccentricity and over A-DGN by 1.7+ points.

## Relation to Prior Work

GRAMA builds on the classical equivalence between ARMA models and linear SSMs (Whittle, 1951; Hamilton, 1994) and adapts the selective coefficient mechanism from Mamba (Gu et al., 2023) to the graph setting. It departs from prior Graph SSMs (Graph-Mamba, GMN) that sacrifice permutation equivariance by imposing node orderings, and from Bianchi et al. (2021) which applies ARMA via spectral convolution on a static graph. The key conceptual departure is transforming a static graph into a *sequence of graphs* (stacking node features L times) rather than a graph into a single node sequence, enabling any GNN backbone to operate as a drop-in residual module within the ARMA recurrence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GRAMA provides a theoretically grounded, permutation-equivariant mechanism for long-range propagation that can be attached to virtually any GNN backbone, addressing oversquashing without the quadratic cost of full attention. The open question of whether GRAMA improves expressiveness beyond the 1-WL bound of its backbone remains unresolved, and the computational overhead of L stacked MLPs and S recurrent blocks, while modest, may become non-trivial at very large scale. The sequence-of-graphs abstraction is a clean reframing that could inspire further work on coupling other sequential models (e.g., xLSTM, Griffin) with graph backbones.
