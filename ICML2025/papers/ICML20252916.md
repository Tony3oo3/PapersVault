## Problem

The tree-Wasserstein distance (TWD) approximates the 1-Wasserstein distance in linear time, but existing methods suffer from suboptimal tree structures: unsupervised approaches (QuadTree, ClusterTree, qTWD, cTWD, sliced variants) either fix the tree topology or rely on random initialization, while the supervised UltraTree requires expensive precomputed Wasserstein labels. No prior method simultaneously optimizes both the ultrametric tree structure and edge weights in an unsupervised manner, leaving a persistent gap between W_T and W_1.

## Input / Output

The method takes a cost matrix D ∈ R^{n×n} of pairwise Euclidean distances between n word embeddings (d=300) as input, and produces a binary ultrametric tree T* with 2n−1 nodes and associated edge weights. Unlike QuadTree or ClusterTree, UltraTWD operates solely on the distance matrix D rather than the raw embedding coordinates, eliminating the need for spatial partitioning. The resulting tree enables O(n) computation of W_T(μ,ν) for any pair of bag-of-words distributions over the vocabulary.

## Method

UltraTWD reframes TWD optimization as an ultrametric nearness problem: find the ultrametric D_T closest to the cost matrix D under either the l∞ or Frobenius norm, subject to the strong triangle inequality d_ij^T ≤ max{d_ik^T, d_jk^T}. Three algorithms solve this: (1) UltraTWD-MST applies Prim's MST plus a closed-form l∞-nearest ultrametric formula (Theorem 5) in O(n²); (2) UltraTWD-IP uses Halpern-Lions-Wittmann-Bauschke iterative projection, enforcing one triplet constraint at a time via a closed-form projection onto each Ω_ijk subset, typically converging in a single O(n³) iteration; (3) UltraTWD-GD parameterizes D_T through 2n−1 node heights and alternates gradient descent on ‖f(H_T)−D‖_F² with periodic MST recomputation to update the tree topology, achieving O(mn²) with m=8 iterations. The key distinction from prior work is that all three methods are fully unsupervised, require no training data or precomputed Wasserstein labels, and jointly refine both tree structure and edge weights through the ultrametric constraint.

## Results

UltraTWD-IP achieves the lowest approximation error (RE-W) across all four datasets: 0.014 (BBCSport), 0.033 (Reuters), 0.036 (Ohsumed), and 0.026 (Recipe), substantially outperforming the best unsupervised baseline Sliced-cTWD (0.079–0.116) and even the supervised UltraTree (0.022–0.041). UltraTWD-GD is competitive, achieving the best RE-W on Ohsumed (0.017) and Reuters (0.030). In document retrieval, UltraTWD-IP attains the highest precision (0.788–0.885), exceeding the best baseline by 2–4 percentage points. Tree learning time for UltraTWD-GD is 15–72 seconds, up to 43× faster than UltraTree while also being more accurate.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the entropy-based Sinkhorn distance, UltraTWD-IP reduces RE-W by 4–18× (e.g., 0.014 vs. 0.258 on BBCSport) while also achieving far higher retrieval precision (0.885 vs. 0.534). Compared to tree-construction baselines (QuadTree, ClusterTree), UltraTWD-IP lowers RE-W by roughly 5–50×, since those methods fix edge weights by tree depth and cannot optimize structure. Against weight-optimized methods (qTWD, cTWD) and their sliced variants, UltraTWD-IP achieves 2–3× lower RE-W (e.g., 0.033 vs. 0.079 on Reuters) because it jointly optimizes both tree topology and edge weights rather than adjusting weights on a fixed random tree. Most notably, UltraTWD-IP surpasses the supervised UltraTree method on 3 of 4 datasets in RE-W (0.014 vs. 0.022 on BBCSport; 0.033 vs. 0.041 on Reuters; 0.026 vs. 0.027 on Recipe) without requiring any training data or precomputed W₁ labels, and it is up to 43× faster in tree learning. UltraTWD-GD is slightly less accurate than IP but more scalable (O(mn²) vs. O(n³)), making it preferable for very large vocabularies.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the tree-Wasserstein distance (TWD) framework of Le et al. (2019) and the supervised ultrametric tree learning of UltraTree (Chen et al., 2024) by formulating the problem as an unsupervised ultrametric nearness problem. It departs from prior unsupervised TWD methods (QuadTree, ClusterTree, qTWD, cTWD, and their sliced variants) that fix the tree structure and only optimize edge weights, by simultaneously optimizing both structure and weights through three algorithms: a closed-form MST-based solution (leveraging Chepoi & Fichet's l∞-nearest ultrametric theorem), an iterative projection method (HLWB), and a gradient descent approach adapted from UltraTree but with an unsupervised objective. The key conceptual shift is replacing the supervised regression loss ‖W_T^train − W_1^train‖² with the direct distance-matrix alignment objective ‖D_T − D‖², eliminating the need for expensive precomputed Wasserstein labels.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

UltraTWD provides the first unsupervised framework that jointly optimizes tree structure and edge weights, achieving lower approximation error than the supervised UltraTree baseline without requiring any training data or precomputed W1 labels. This is practically significant for text analysis pipelines where computing W1 ground truth is prohibitively expensive (O(n³ log n) per pair). However, the non-convex optimization lacks convergence guarantees, the IP method's O(n³) complexity limits scalability to very large vocabularies, and the ultrametric restriction (a subset of tree metrics) may sacrifice expressiveness compared to general tree metrics. Open questions include whether the gap between ultrametric and general tree metric approximations matters in practice, and whether the methods extend naturally to non-Euclidean cost matrices or multimodal settings.
