## Problem

Multi-objective test-time alignment of frozen LLMs requires balancing multiple, potentially conflicting preference dimensions (e.g., helpfulness, harmlessness, humor) at inference. The state-of-the-art method GenARM trains a separate autoregressive reward model (ARM) per dimension, which (i) multiplies inference cost by k and (ii) causes misalignment between the combined guided generation and the user-specified preference vector because the ARMs are trained independently without awareness of each other.

## Input / Output

At inference, PARM takes a text prompt and a preference vector α ∈ Δ_{k-1} (a point on the (k-1)-simplex specifying weights for each preference dimension) and produces a single aligned text response from the frozen base LLM. The preference vector is consumed by a lightweight linear layer that generates the r₂×r₂ conditioning matrix W₂(α) inside PBLoRA, so the same trained PARM can be steered to any α without retraining. Notably, the PARM can be substantially smaller than the base LLM it guides (e.g., a 1.1B PARM guides a 7B LLM, or a 7B PARM guides a 65B LLM), enabling weak-to-strong alignment.

## Method

PARM replaces k independently trained ARMs with a single unified ARM whose parameters θ(α) are conditioned on the preference vector via Preference-Aware Bilinear Low-Rank Adaptation (PBLoRA). PBLoRA augments standard LoRA's BA product with an intermediate r×r matrix W(α), yielding the bilinear form BWA; a theoretical result shows this spans an r²-dimensional subspace versus only r for BA, making it strictly more expressive. The bilinear form is decomposed into a preference-agnostic term B₁W₁A₁ (shared across all α) and a preference-aware term B₂W₂(α)A₂ (specific to each α), where W₂(α) is generated by a linear layer f_φ(α). Training minimizes the linear scalarization Σᵢ αᵢ ℓ(π_{θ(α)}, Dᵢ) with α sampled from the simplex at each step, so a single model approximates the entire Pareto set. At inference, the single PARM computes token-level rewards r(x,y,α) = Σₜ log π_{θ(α)}(yₜ|x,y_{<t}), and the frozen base LLM's next-token distribution is reweighted by (π_{θ(α)})^{1/β}, requiring only one forward pass through the reward model per token regardless of the number of preference dimensions.

## Results

On the safety alignment task (7B base model), PARM achieves HV=113.38 and MIP=2.59, outperforming GenARM (HV=99.34, MIP=0.80) by 14.1% in HV and 223.8% in MIP. On the helpful assistant task (1.1B PARM guiding 7B base), PARM reaches HV=82.12 and MIP=1.42 while using only 1.53M learnable parameters and 38.96s inference time, compared to GenARM's 44.38 HV, 0.93 MIP, 4.59M parameters, and 48.39s. In the weak-to-strong setting (7B PARM guiding 65B base), PARM achieves HV=121.73 and MIP=3.46, a 91.2% MIP improvement over GenARM.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PARM consistently outperforms all three baselines across every experimental setting. Against Rewarded Soups (RS) and MOD on the safety alignment task, PARM achieves substantially higher HV (113.38 vs. 69.79 and 89.96) and MIP (2.59 vs. 1.40 and 2.15), reflecting both better Pareto front coverage and tighter alignment with specified preference vectors. Against GenARM—the closest competitor—PARM wins on both HV and MIP in all three settings: 14.1% higher HV and 223.8% higher MIP on safety alignment (7B), 6.1% higher HV and 91.2% higher MIP on weak-to-strong (65B), and 85.0% higher HV and 52.7% higher MIP on helpful assistant. Crucially, PARM also reduces inference time by ~19% (38.96s vs. 48.39s) and uses only 1/3 the learnable parameters (1.53M vs. 4.59M) compared to GenARM, since it requires a single unified ARM rather than k separate ones. Against MOD-w2s on the helpful assistant task, PARM improves HV by 90.7% and MIP by 54.3% while being 34% faster, demonstrating that the unified preference-conditioned approach is both more effective and more efficient than maintaining multiple specialized models.

## Relation to Prior Work

PARM directly extends GenARM's autoregressive reward model framework from single-objective to multi-objective test-time alignment, replacing k independently trained ARMs with a single unified ARM conditioned on a preference vector. The conditioning mechanism, PBLoRA, is a bilinear extension of LoRA that inserts a preference-dependent weight matrix W(α) between the low-rank factors, and subsumes SVD-LoRA (used in Panacea) as a special case. Unlike prior multi-objective alignment methods (Rewarded Soups, MOD, CLP) that require training or merging multiple base LLMs, PARM keeps the base model entirely frozen and trains only a small reward model, positioning it at the intersection of parameter-efficient fine-tuning and multi-objective optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a single 7B PARM can guide a frozen 65B LLM with better alignment quality than methods requiring training multiple 65B models, making multi-objective alignment accessible under tight compute budgets. The 223.8% MIP improvement over GenARM on safety alignment suggests that jointly training across preference dimensions (rather than combining independently trained models) is critical for faithful preference vector adherence. Open questions include how the method scales beyond 2–3 preference dimensions, whether the simplex-based preference vector is sufficient for more complex preference structures, and whether the gains persist at larger model scales (e.g., 70B+ base models) and with more diverse task distributions.
