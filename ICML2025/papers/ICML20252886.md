## Problem

Large Neighborhood Search (LNS) for large-scale MILP problems depends critically on neighborhood selection, yet existing strategies are either hand-crafted (requiring domain expertise and suffering cold-start issues) or ML-based (RL converges slowly, imitation learning demands expensive labeled data). LLM-based evolutionary approaches like FunSearch and EOH use fixed evolutionary strategies, limiting solution diversity and causing premature convergence. The paper addresses the gap of an adaptive, scalable framework that automatically discovers effective neighborhood selection strategies from scant small-scale training data while generalizing to million-variable MILPs.

## Input / Output

The method consumes small-scale MILP training instances (tens of thousands of variables and constraints, roughly 1% of test size) along with their structural information (variable bounds, constraint matrices, objective coefficients) to evolve heuristic strategies. The LLM receives strategy-thought-fitness tuples from parent populations plus evolutionary prompt strategies as context for generating offspring. The output is an evolved heuristic (natural-language thought paired with executable Python code) that scores decision variables for neighborhood selection, which is then applied via ALNS to large-scale test instances (up to 10^6 variables and 3×10^6 constraints) to produce optimized solutions and objective values.

## Method

LLM-LNS introduces a dual-layer self-evolutionary LLM agent: the inner layer evolves heuristic strategies (thought + code) under fixed prompt strategies to drive convergence, while the outer layer evolves the evolutionary prompt strategies themselves to maintain diversity and escape local optima when stagnation is detected (top-l individuals unchanged for t generations). A key mechanism is Differential Memory for Directional Evolution, where fitness values of parent strategies are fed back into the LLM via a meta-prompt combining a learning directive (emphasize traits of higher-fitness strategies) and an evolutionary operation prompt (crossover, mutation, or hybrid), enabling the LLM to act as both generator and optimizer. The evolved heuristic is integrated into an Adaptive LNS that scores all decision variables, selects the top-k to form the neighborhood, solves the subproblem with Gurobi, and dynamically expands or contracts k based on improvement thresholds and time limits. Unlike FunSearch (code-only evolution) and EOH (fixed prompts), LLM-LNS adds both prompt evolution and differential memory, achieving superior convergence and diversity with the same GPT-4o-mini model and comparable iteration budgets.

## Results

On large-scale MILP benchmarks (up to 2M variables), LLM-LNS achieves the best objective value on all 8 instances, e.g., 158,878.9 on SC2 (Min) vs. 320,240.4 for Gurobi and 385,708.4 for SCIP, and 363,749.5 on MIKS2 (Max) vs. 329,642.4 for Gurobi. On Online Bin Packing, the evolved heuristic achieves 1.63% average excess bin fraction, outperforming EOH (2.09%) and FunSearch (2.31%). On TSP, it reaches a 0.08% average gap to best-known solutions, beating EOH (0.16%) and LEHD (1.51%). Primal integral analysis confirms >20% improvement over the closest competitor on SC2 and MVC2.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LLM-LNS outperforms all 16 baselines across every evaluated benchmark. On large-scale MILP, it beats the closest competitor Light-MILPopt on all 8 instances (e.g., 158,878.9 vs. 160,015.5 on SC2; 363,749.5 vs. 362,265.1 on MIKS2) and dramatically surpasses Gurobi and SCIP on the 2M-variable instances (SC2: 158,878.9 vs. 320,240.4 and 385,708.4 respectively). CL-LNS fails to produce results on 6 of 8 large-scale instances, and GNN&GBDT cannot solve either MIKS instance. On heuristic generation, LLM-LNS's dual-layer agent beats EOH by 0.46 percentage points on Online Bin Packing (1.63% vs. 2.09% avg excess bins) and by 0.08 percentage points on TSP (0.08% vs. 0.16% avg gap to best-known), while FunSearch trails further at 2.31% on bin packing. Traditional solvers (Gurobi, SCIP) and hand-crafted LNS (Random-LNS, ACP) show significant degradation as problem size scales from 100K to 2M variables, whereas LLM-LNS maintains consistent superiority, confirming its scalability advantage.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LLM-plus-evolutionary-algorithm paradigm established by FunSearch (code-level evolution only) and EOH (thought+code evolution with fixed strategies) by introducing a dual-layer self-evolutionary structure: an inner layer that evolves heuristic strategies (thoughts and code) for convergence, and an outer layer that evolves the evolutionary prompt strategies themselves to maintain diversity. It further departs from both by incorporating differential memory—feeding fitness values of parent strategies back into the LLM to guide crossover and mutation directionally—rather than relying on undirected random recombination. On the optimization side, it builds on the LNS destroy-and-repair framework (Song et al., 2020) and the Adaptive Constraint Partitioning approach (Ye et al., 2023a), replacing hand-crafted or ML-learned neighborhood selection with LLM-generated strategies trained on small-scale instances.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical contribution is demonstrating that a small amount of small-scale training data (tens of thousands of variables) suffices to evolve neighborhood-selection heuristics that generalize to instances with millions of variables, outperforming both commercial solvers (Gurobi, SCIP) and ML-based LNS methods (CL-LNS, GNN&GBDT, Light-MILPopt) under time-limited settings. This reduces the expert-knowledge bottleneck in LNS design and offers a more deployable alternative to neural-network-based approaches that require extensive training data and infrastructure. Open questions include the cost and latency of LLM API calls at scale, sensitivity to the specific LLM used (only GPT-4o-mini is tested), and whether the dual-layer mechanism generalizes beyond the four MILP problem families evaluated. The reliance on Gurobi as the sub-solver within the LNS loop also means the method's advantage is in neighborhood selection rather than in the underlying optimization engine.
