## Problem

State-of-the-art flow-matching and diffusion models for molecular conformer generation require extensive computational resources for both training and inference. Training objectives like conditional optimal transport or Kabsch-aligned flow necessitate explicit rotational alignment between the noise prior and data, leading to slower convergence, while sampling demands hundreds to thousands of ODE steps per conformer—making these models impractical for virtual screening over billion-compound libraries. The paper addresses both bottlenecks simultaneously: a more efficient training objective that analytically handles SO(3) symmetry, and a trajectory-straightening procedure that enables one-step generation.

## Input / Output

The model takes a featurized 2D molecular graph as conditioning (atom types, bond types, degree, formal charge, valence, hybridization, chirality, ring membership, plus a 32-dim graph Laplacian positional encoding) and produces an ensemble of 3D conformer coordinates as an N×3 matrix per molecule. Generation starts from standard Gaussian noise and follows a learned vector field via ODE integration; after reflow+distillation, a single forward pass (one Euler step) suffices to produce a conformer. The model is trained on CREST-generated conformers from GEOM-QM9 (small molecules) and GEOM-Drugs (drug-like molecules), with a post-hoc RDKit-based chirality correction applied to molecules containing stereocenters.

## Method

The core training contribution is the SO(3)-Averaged Flow objective, which replaces explicit rotational alignment (Kabsch) or random rotation assignment (conditional OT) by analytically integrating the conditional vector field over the full SO(3) group using the Haar measure. The key insight is that the SO(3) integral of an exponential of a trace term admits a closed-form solution (via the signed SVD of a 3×3 matrix, following Mohlin et al. 2020), so the averaged target velocity u_t(x_t) can be computed in closed form as a derivative of a log-partition function, introducing only a small computational overhead. For equivariant architectures (modified NequIP, ~4.7M params), a linear interpolant is used; for non-equivariant architectures (pairwise-bias DiT, ~52–64M params), the interpolant must be obtained by simulating the ground-truth Averaged Flow ODE with 20 Euler steps. For fast inference, the paper applies reflow (fine-tuning on self-generated noise–conformer pairs with an exponential t-distribution biased toward early timesteps) followed by distillation (equivalent to reflow at t=0), which straightens the probability flow trajectories and enables high-quality one-step or two-step conformer generation without iterative ODE solving.

## Results

On GEOM-Drugs (δ=0.75Å), AvgFlow_DiT-L (64M params, 100 ODE steps) achieves COV-P of 75.7% and AMR-P of 0.516Å, outperforming MCF-L (242M, 1000 steps) in precision metrics while using ~10× fewer steps. In one-step generation, AvgFlow_DiT-D (52M) achieves COV-R of 76.8% and COV-P of 61.0%, dramatically surpassing MCF-L (27.2% COV-R, 8.9% COV-P) and ET-Flow (27.6% COV-R, 25.7% COV-P). On GEOM-QM9 (δ=0.5Å), AvgFlow_DiT (52M) sets a new state-of-the-art across all four metrics, with COV-R of 96.0% and AMR-P of 0.088Å.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MCF (Wang et al., 2024), AvgFlow_DiT-L (64M) outperforms all three MCF variants (13M–242M) in precision metrics (COV-P 75.7% vs. 66.8% for MCF-L) while using only 100 ODE steps versus 1000. In one-step generation, AvgFlow_DiT-D achieves 76.8% COV-R and 61.0% COV-P, compared to a mere 27.2% and 8.9% for MCF-L — a gap of nearly 50 percentage points in recall. Against ET-Flow (Hassan et al., 2024), AvgFlow_DiT matches ET-Flow-SS in full simulation (COV-R 82.0% vs. 79.6%) and surpasses it in recall, while in one-step generation AvgFlow_DiT-D (76.8% COV-R) outperforms ET-Flow (27.6% COV-R) by a wide margin. Against Torsional Diffusion (Jing et al., 2022), AvgFlow_DiT-D in one step exceeds Tor. Diff. with 20 steps on all metrics (76.8% vs. 72.7% COV-R), despite Tor. Diff. starting from RDKit-generated conformers. Against cheminformatics tools (RDKit, OMEGA) and GeoMol, all AvgFlow variants dominate across every metric, with the gap being most pronounced on the harder GEOM-Drugs dataset where RDKit achieves only 38.4% COV-R versus 76.8% for AvgFlow_DiT-D.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the flow-matching framework (Lipman et al., 2023) and the rectified flow reflow/distillation technique (Liu et al., 2022), applying both to molecular conformer generation. Its core departure from prior work is the SO(3)-Averaged Flow objective, which analytically integrates the probability flow over all rotations of the data distribution using a closed-form SO(3) integral (from Mohlin et al., 2020), rather than requiring explicit Kabsch alignment (as in ET-Flow) or random rotation assignment (as in conditional OT). The method is demonstrated to be architecture-agnostic, working with both an equivariant NequIP GNN and a non-equivariant DiT transformer, and the reflow+distillation pipeline is borrowed from rectified flow work in image and point-cloud generation but is the first application to molecular conformer generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The one-step generation quality of AvgFlow_DiT-D (outperforming 20-step torsional diffusion and 1000-step MCF-S in precision metrics) pushes the quality-speed trade-off to a point where flow-based conformer generation becomes viable for industrial-scale virtual screening of billion-compound libraries. The SO(3)-Averaged Flow objective is a clean, generalizable technique that could extend to other rotationally-invariant generative tasks such as protein structure generation. Open questions include whether the closed-form solution generalizes to other symmetry groups (e.g., SE(3) for translation-invariant tasks), whether the approach maintains its advantage at much larger molecular sizes, and whether the reflow+distillation pipeline can be further compressed without quality loss.
