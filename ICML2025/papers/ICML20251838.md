## Problem

Previous work on MPNN generalization linked embedding distances to task-agnostic structural graph distances, but it remained unclear what distance a well-performing MPNN actually learns in practice. This paper shows that trained MPNNs align with the task-relevant functional distance (based on target labels) rather than structural distances, and that this alignment correlates with predictive performance. The open question is how to interpret the learned metric structure: which subgraphs determine the relative positions of embeddings in MPNN space?

## Input / Output

The method takes a trained MPNN (GCN or GIN with mean or sum pooling) and an attributed graph dataset as input. It first runs the L-iteration WL test on all graphs to build a WILT (a rooted tree whose nodes are WL colors and whose edges connect consecutive-iteration colors of the same node). The output is a set of non-negative edge weights on the WILT, from which one can read off which WL subgraphs (colors) most strongly influence the MPNN embedding distance. The WILTing distance itself is computable in O(|V_G| + |V_H|) time per graph pair using sparse WILT embeddings, making it practical for large datasets.

## Method

The core contribution is the WILTing distance: an optimal transport (Wasserstein) distance between multisets of WL colors, where the ground metric is the shortest-path length on a weighted Weisfeiler Leman Labeling Tree (WILT). A key technical result (Proposition 5.3) shows this OT distance is equivalent to a weighted L1 (Manhattan) distance between WILT embedding vectors, enabling linear-time computation. Two normalizations handle graphs of unequal size: size normalization (dividing by node count, generalizing the WWL kernel) and dummy node normalization (padding with isolated nodes, generalizing the WLOA kernel). Edge weights are learned by minimizing MSE between d_WILT and d_MPNP via gradient descent (Algorithm 1), with an L1 regularization option to sparsify. The expressiveness analysis (Theorem 5.4) proves that size-normalized WILT is a tighter upper bound for mean-pooled MPNNs while dummy-node-normalized WILT is appropriate for sum-pooled MPNNs, explaining the empirical preference for each normalization.

## Results

The WILTing distance (d_WILT) approximates the MPNN embedding distance (d_MPNP) with RMSE ×10⁻² of 0.82–9.15 across GCN models on Mutagenicity, ENZYMES, and Lipophilicity, substantially outperforming the fixed-weight baselines d_WWL (9.25–14.97) and d_WLOA (5.98–23.71). Size-normalized d_WILT best approximates mean-pooled MPNNs (e.g., 1.74 on Mutagenicity), while dummy-node-normalized d_WILT best approximates sum-pooled MPNNs (e.g., 0.82 on Mutagenicity). The alignment between d_MPNP and the task-relevant functional distance d_func (measured by ALI_k) shows strong positive Spearman correlation with predictive performance (SRC 0.61–0.89 for classification, −0.55 to −0.65 for regression), whereas alignment with structural distances shows no consistent correlation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares d_WILT against two fixed-weight WL-based distances that are special cases of d_WILT: d_WWL (Togninalli et al., 2019) and d_WLOA (Kriege et al., 2016). d_WILT with learned edge weights consistently outperforms both by a large margin — e.g., on Mutagenicity with sum pooling, d_WILT (dummy) achieves RMSE 0.82 vs. d_WLOA's 5.98 and d_WWL's 12.25 (×10⁻²). The size-normalized variant d_WILT (size) is the best match for mean-pooled MPNNs, while the dummy-node variant d_WILT (dummy) is best for sum-pooled MPNNs, consistent with the theoretical expressiveness analysis. For structural distances (d_GED from Sanfeliu & Fu, 1983, and d_TMD from Chuang & Jegelka, 2022), the paper shows that alignment between d_MPNP and these task-agnostic structural pseudometrics does not improve with training and does not correlate with predictive performance (SRC values in Table 4 are weak and inconsistent across datasets), in stark contrast to the strong correlation observed with the task-relevant functional distance d_func.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the line of work analyzing MPNN generalization through structural graph distances (Chuang & Jegelka, 2022; Böker et al., 2024; Franks et al., 2024), showing that alignment with task-agnostic structural pseudometrics does not explain MPNN performance, while alignment with task-relevant functional distances does. Methodologically, it builds on the Weisfeiler-Leman framework and optimal transport on trees (Le et al., 2019) to propose WILT, a trainable graph distance that provably generalizes both the Wasserstein WL kernel (Togninalli et al., 2019) and the WL optimal assignment kernel (Kriege et al., 2016). In the GNN interpretability literature, it extends from instance-level explanations (Liu et al., 2022a; Yuan et al., 2022) to a global-level interpretation of the embedding space geometry via distillation into an interpretable tree structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The WILT framework provides a principled, linear-time-computable tool for interpreting which subgraphs drive MPNN embedding distances, with the identified subgraphs matching known toxicophores in the mutagenicity domain. Key limitations include testing only GCN and GIN with fixed hyperparameters, analyzing only final-layer embeddings, and restricting to 1-WL colors; extending to higher-order WL variants, internal layers, and node-level tasks on large graphs remains open. The finding that structural distance alignment is irrelevant to performance challenges the theoretical framing of several recent generalization bound papers and redirects attention toward task-specific geometry.
