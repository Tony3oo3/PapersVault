## Problem

Autoregressive and recurrent neural networks achieve strong predictive performance but lack a principled framework for quantifying epistemic uncertainty, making them unreliable in scientific applications such as PDE solving, molecular generation, and weather forecasting where data distribution shifts and overfitting are common. No existing method provides a general, scalable way to convert an arbitrary autoregressive or recurrent model into its Bayesian counterpart while maintaining accuracy and producing well-calibrated uncertainty estimates.

## Input / Output

For PDE solving, BARNN takes a spatial field at time t−1 (e.g., a 1D velocity profile) and produces the next time-step solution as a Gaussian predictive distribution (mean + per-point standard deviation), unrolled autoregressively over hundreds of steps. For molecule generation, it consumes SMILES tokens sequentially through an LSTM and outputs the next-token predictive distribution. In both settings, a lightweight encoder E_ψ (fewer than 50K parameters) maps the previous state (or hidden state) to per-layer dropout coefficients α_t that modulate the static network weights Ω, so the only additional input beyond the base model is the current state or hidden representation.

## Method

BARNN constructs a joint distribution p(y_{0:T}, ω_{1:T}) where network weights ω_t evolve in time alongside observable states y_t, and trains via a variational lower bound that decomposes into a per-timestep expected log-likelihood minus a KL divergence between a variational posterior q(ω_t | y_{0:t−1}) and a prior p(ω_t). The key mechanism is a reparameterization ω_t = α_t · Ω · (1 + ε) that extends Variational Dropout: Ω are static deterministic weights, α_t are time-dependent variational coefficients produced by a small encoder conditioned on previous states, and ε is Gaussian noise, enabling the local reparametrization trick for efficient sampling in large networks. The prior is a Temporal Variational Mixture of Posteriors (tVAMP), where at each timestep the prior is the batch-aggregated posterior, making the KL term independent of Ω and yielding a closed-form regularization that acts as Bayesian weight regularization during training. This framework is architecture-agnostic—applied to a Fourier Neural Operator for PDEs and an LSTM for SMILES generation—with the only modification being the addition of the encoder and the dropout-coefficient reparameterization, and it reduces to standard log-likelihood training in the limit of peaked, time-independent posteriors.

## Results

On PDE modelling (Burgers, Kuramoto-Sivashinsky, KdV), BARNN achieves the best NLL across all three equations (−2.51, −0.87, −0.94) and the best ECE (0.05, 0.04, 0.10) over 320-step rollouts, outperforming Dropout, ARD, Input Perturbation, and PDE-Refiner. On molecule generation (ChEMBL, 1.9M SMILES), BARNN-LSTM achieves 95.09% validity, 88.63% diversity, 95.41% novelty, and 95.06% uniqueness, surpassing both SMILES LSTM and standard LSTM. BARNN also attains the lowest Wasserstein distance for all six molecular property descriptors, with a particularly large gap on molecular weight (W₂ = 2.53 vs. 10.4 for SMILES LSTM).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against PDE-Refiner (Lippe et al., 2024), BARNN dominates decisively: Refiner diverges (breaks) on NLL for all three PDEs and has the worst ECE (0.23–0.34), while BARNN maintains stable, well-calibrated predictions. Against ARD-Dropout (Kharitonov et al., 2018), BARNN wins on NLL for all three PDEs (e.g., −2.51 vs. 5.00 on Burgers) and on ECE (0.05 vs. 0.26 on Burgers); ARD also breaks on KdV. Against Input Perturbation (Lam et al., 2022), BARNN achieves lower NLL on all three PDEs (−2.51 vs. −2.01 on Burgers, −0.94 vs. −0.37 on KdV) and lower ECE on Burgers (0.05 vs. 0.16) and KdV (0.10 vs. 0.11), with a near-tie on KS (0.04 vs. 0.05). Against Monte Carlo Dropout (Gal & Ghahramani, 2016), BARNN wins on NLL for Burgers (−2.51 vs. 0.06) and KdV (−0.94 vs. 0.40) and on ECE for Burgers (0.05 vs. 0.17) and KdV (0.10 vs. 0.14), though Dropout edges ahead on KS NLL (−0.48 vs. −0.87). For molecule generation, BARNN outperforms SMILES LSTM (Segler et al., 2018) by ~0.5% on validity, ~1.2% on novelty, and ~0.5% on uniqueness, while matching diversity; against standard LSTM (Schmidhuber et al., 1997), the gaps are larger (~2% validity, ~2.6% novelty, ~2.9% uniqueness). BARNN also reduces ring-closure errors by 30% over SMILES LSTM and 50% over LSTM, demonstrating superior long-range dependency modelling.

## Relation to Prior Work

BARNN extends Variational Dropout (Kingma et al., 2015; Gal & Ghahramani, 2016) by making network weights evolve jointly with observable states over time, rather than remaining static, and introduces a temporal VAMP prior (tVAMP) as a time-dependent generalization of the VAMP prior from Tomczak & Welling (2018). The variational lower bound it derives connects directly to the VAE ELBO (Kingma & Welling, 2014), positioning Bayesian autoregressive training as a form of weight-regularized likelihood optimization. The framework is architecture-agnostic, applying the same reparameterization and KL machinery to both Fourier Neural Operators for PDE solving and LSTMs for molecular generation, building on the autoregressive PDE solver and SMILES-LSTM literatures respectively.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

BARNN offers a drop-in, general recipe for adding calibrated epistemic uncertainty to any autoregressive or recurrent model, which is critical for scientific applications (PDE solving, drug discovery) where overconfident predictions are dangerous. A key practical insight is that RMSE alone can mask severely miscalibrated uncertainty (e.g., PDE-Refiner achieves comparable RMSE but diverges in NLL), urging the community to adopt NLL/ECE alongside pointwise error. Open questions include scaling to truly large over-parameterized models (LLMs), incorporating domain-specific priors, and disentangling epistemic from aleatory uncertainty over long autoregressive rollouts. The framework's reliance on a small encoder network (<50K params) for dropout coefficients makes it lightweight, but its validation is limited to ~1M-parameter PDE solvers and ~25M-parameter LSTMs, leaving open whether the approach retains its calibration advantages at LLM scale.
