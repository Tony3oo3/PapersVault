## Problem

Combining multiple heuristics for Metrical Task Systems typically requires full feedback (querying all heuristics each step), which is costly when heuristics rely on heavy prediction models. In the bandit-feedback setting where only one heuristic can be queried per step, the cost of a heuristic at time t cannot be estimated unless it was also queried at time t−1, creating a memory-bounded observation problem that prevents direct application of classical online learning methods. This paper tackles this gap by designing an algorithm with sublinear regret in the best heuristic's cost and proving a matching lower bound.

## Input / Output

The algorithm receives an MTS instance (metric space (M,d) with diameter D, starting point s_0, and online cost functions c_t) plus ℓ heuristics accessible via m-delayed bandit queries: at each step only one heuristic can be queried, and its state is revealed only if it has been queried continuously for the preceding m−1 steps as well. The algorithm retains full access to the input instance (cost functions), which is essential since c_t may be unbounded or non-Lipschitz. The output is a sequence of states s_1,…,s_T with expected cost bounded by OPT_{≤0} + O(OPT_{≤0}^{2/3}) for the static benchmark, or OPT_{≤k} + Õ(k^{1/3} OPT_{≤k}^{2/3}) for a benchmark switching at most k times.

## Method

The algorithm alternates between exploitation steps (following the heuristic sampled from an internal HEDGE/SHARE distribution) and exploration steps (querying a uniformly random heuristic for m consecutive steps to observe its cost, then updating the internal distribution). Three key mechanisms distinguish it from classical MAB: (1) improper greedy steps are taken during the m-step bootstrapping windows when no heuristic state is known, (2) MTS-style rounding via Earth Mover's Distance ensures bounded switching costs between heuristics rather than independent resampling, and (3) exploration steps are correlated across time due to the m≥2 constraint, preventing unbiased loss estimation. The analysis exploits a stability property of the internal algorithm (‖x_{t−1}−x_t‖₁ ≤ η·g_{t−1}^T x_{t−1}) to charge costs incurred during exploration blocks back to the perceived losses, yielding the O((Dℓ ln ℓ)^{1/3} m^{2/3} OPT_{≤0}^{2/3}) regret bound. The lower bound adapts the Dekel et al. (2013) construction for Bandits with Switching Costs to MTS by encoding each bandit arm as a heuristic in a 3-state metric component, circumventing the algorithm's one-step lookahead and ability to take improper actions.

## Results

The main result (Theorem 1.1) shows an algorithm achieving expected regret O(OPT_{≤0}^{2/3}) with m-delayed bandit access to ℓ heuristics on MTS with diameter D, where the full dependence is O((Dℓ ln ℓ)^{1/3} m^{2/3} OPT_{≤0}^{2/3}). A matching lower bound (Theorem 1.3) of Ω̃(OPT_{≤0}^{2/3}) is proven even for m=2, establishing tightness up to logarithmic factors. For the dynamic benchmark allowing k switches (Theorem 1.2), the regret is Õ(k^{1/3} OPT_{≤k}^{2/3}), and the competitive ratio converges to 1 as OPT_{≤k} grows. The algorithm also adapts to the Arora et al. (2012) memory-bounded adversary setting, achieving O((mℓ ln ℓ)^{1/3} T^{2/3}) policy regret.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines; comparisons are to prior theoretical results. Against Antoniadis et al. (2023b), who study a strictly easier bandit setting where the queried predictor also reports its moving cost, this paper works in the more natural setting where only the state is revealed, yet still achieves sublinear regret (vs. their (1+ε)-competitive ratio). The paper's upper bounds therefore also apply to the Antoniadis et al. setting. Against the full-feedback combining-heuristics literature (Blum & Burch 2000; Wei 2020), which achieves (1+ε)-competitiveness via HEDGE, this paper shows that bandit feedback fundamentally changes the problem: the m-delayed access prevents unbiased loss estimation and forces the algorithm to take 'improper' MTS steps. The lower bound (Theorem 1.3) is derived from the Dekel et al. (2013) construction for Bandits with Switching Costs, but requires additional technical work to handle the algorithm's ability to take arbitrary MTS actions and its 1-step look-ahead, both of which are indispensable in the MTS model. The dependence on D, ℓ, and k in the upper bound is shown to be (almost) optimal via a scaling argument and a k-segment construction (Appendix B.1).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Combining Heuristics framework for Metrical Task Systems from the full-feedback setting (Blum & Burch, 2000; Wei, 2020) to a bandit-feedback variant where only one heuristic can be queried per step and its cost is only observable after m consecutive queries. The m-delayed access structure is directly inspired by Arora et al. (2012)'s memory-bounded adversary setting, while the lower bound adapts the Dekel et al. (2013) construction for Bandits with Switching Costs. The paper departs from Antoniadis et al. (2023b), who studied a strictly easier bandit setting where the queried predictor also reports its moving cost, by working in the more natural setting where such costs are not revealed, and by proving tight regret bounds rather than competitive ratios.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The tight O(OPT^{2/3}) regret bound (matching up to log factors) establishes the fundamental limits of combining heuristics under restricted information access, which is practically relevant when heuristics rely on expensive ML models that cannot all be queried simultaneously. The result also provides a robustification tool: by including a worst-case competitive algorithm among the heuristics, the combined algorithm inherits worst-case guarantees while asymptotically matching the best heuristic on easy instances. Open questions include whether the m-delayed structure can be relaxed, whether the log-factor gap between upper and lower bounds can be closed, and whether the approach extends to settings where the metric space is unbounded or the cost functions satisfy additional structure.
