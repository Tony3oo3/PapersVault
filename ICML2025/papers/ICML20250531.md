## Problem

Existing federated learning quantization methods either compress only the uplink (gradient quantization like QSGD) while still downloading full-precision models, or operate at a fixed INT8 precision that cannot be safely reduced to INT4 without model collapse. There is no framework that simultaneously reduces both uplink and downlink communication and client-side computation by dynamically allocating mixed low-bit precisions (below INT8) across the training trajectory.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250531/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

Each client receives a pre-trained DQN agent (1.92K parameters, trained on 10% of CIFAR-10 with ResNet-20) and a local non-IID data shard. The agent observes a 4-dimensional state (training progress ratio, current bit-widths, quantization losses, last-change timestamps) every 5 epochs and outputs one of four discrete actions (hold, or increment weight/activation/gradient bit-width by 2 bits). The final output is a model with INT4 weights and temporally varying activation/gradient precisions, communicated in low-bit format between clients and server, reducing communication by 8× versus full precision.

## Method

LBI-FL employs a Deep Q-Network agent to perform temporally dynamic bit-width allocation over INT4/INT6/INT8 for weights, activations, and gradients, with the constraint that bit-widths can only increase during training (initialized at INT4). The reward function is a product of three terms: a quantization index (inverse compression ratio vs. INT8), an accuracy index (penalty relative to a stored INT8 reference accuracy), and a time-step multiplier that emphasizes late-training decisions. To accelerate convergence of the RL agent, the authors use behavior cloning with hand-crafted teacher samples (gradient→INT8 early, activation→INT8 late, weight stays INT4) regularized via KL divergence between the agent policy and teacher policy. Theoretically, the paper proves that FL with gradient quantization achieves an O(1/√T) convergence rate equivalent to standard FedAvg under bounded quantization noise, and empirically validates this with convergence-epoch comparisons across LeNet, ResNet-18, ViT-S, and MobileNetV2 on CIFAR-10/100.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, LBI-FL achieves 84.16% top-1 accuracy with ResNet-18 (vs. 84.23% for UI8) while reducing BitOPs by 54.06% (3.28G vs. 7.14G). Across all five architectures (LeNet, ResNet-18/50, ViT-S, MobileNetV2), LBI-FL maintains less than 1.5% accuracy loss relative to UI8 in most cases, with an average BitOPs reduction of ~51%. On CIFAR-100, the accuracy gap versus UI8 stays under 1% for ResNet-18 (53.48% vs. 53.57%) and MobileNetV2 (71.61% vs. 72.37%), with BitOPs reductions of 53.81% and 51.40% respectively. Communication cost is reduced by 87.5% compared to full-precision FL since weights are kept at INT4 throughout training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is UI8 (Zhu et al., 2020), a unified INT8 training method that quantizes weights, activations, and gradients to 8-bit integers. LBI-FL matches or slightly underperforms UI8 on accuracy: on CIFAR-10, the gap is only 0.07% for ResNet-18 (84.16% vs. 84.23%), 0.63% for MobileNetV2 (89.02% vs. 89.65%), and 1.12% for LeNet (81.79% vs. 82.91%), while actually exceeding UI8 by 0.70% on ViT-S (72.55% vs. 71.85%). On CIFAR-100, LBI-FL trails UI8 by at most 1.0% (ViT-S: 48.32% vs. 49.32%) and even surpasses it on LeNet (47.41% vs. 46.91%). The critical advantage is computational: LBI-FL reduces BitOPs by 47–54% relative to UI8 across all architectures, and cuts communication cost by 50% (INT4 weights vs. INT8) while maintaining comparable accuracy. Compared to the full-precision FP32 baseline, LBI-FL sacrifices 1–2% accuracy but reduces BitOPs by over 96% and communication by 87.5%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on quantized federated learning (QSGD, SignSGD) that compressed only gradient uplinks, by simultaneously quantizing weights, activations, and gradients below INT8 to reduce both uplink and downlink communication. It departs from prior low-bit training methods (Zhu et al., 2020; Zhao et al., 2021) that operated at INT8 and collapsed at INT4, by introducing a reinforcement-learning agent (DQN) for temporally dynamic bit-width allocation that replaces the fixed-threshold switching of Zhang et al. (2020). The theoretical analysis builds on the convergence framework of Yang et al. (2021) for partial-participation FL, extending it to accommodate gradient quantization noise while preserving the O(1/√T) rate of FedAvg.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 8× communication reduction versus full-precision FL and >50% BitOPs reduction versus INT8 training with <2% accuracy loss make this practically relevant for bandwidth-constrained and compute-limited client deployments. The tiny RL agent (1.92K parameters) generalizing across architectures (LeNet to ViT-S) and datasets (CIFAR-10 to CIFAR-100) without retraining is a useful property, though evidence is limited to two small-scale image datasets. Open questions include whether the one-directional bit-width policy (only increasing from INT4) is suboptimal, whether the approach extends to larger datasets (ImageNet) or non-convolutional architectures beyond ViT-S, and whether the convergence constant Φ grows problematically with the number of clients or heterogeneity level.
