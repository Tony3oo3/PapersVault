## Problem

Existing medical VQA benchmarks focus on basic visual perception tasks (e.g., organ identification) that are clinically irrelevant, leaving a gap in evaluating whether LVLMs can handle the complex, professional-level reasoning questions that radiologists and neonatologists actually need answered—such as predicting two-year neurocognitive outcomes from neonatal brain MRI. Current state-of-the-art LVLMs perform at or below random chance on such tasks, revealing a fundamental lack of domain-specific medical knowledge and structured clinical reasoning capability.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 3D neonatal MRI volumes (ADC maps) unrolled into sequential 2D slice sequences, augmented with two derived visual inputs: Z_ADC lesion probability maps (voxel-wise ADC values normalized against a healthy neonatal atlas, thresholded at −2 to flag abnormal regions) and patient-specific brain anatomy maps (62 ROIs warped from standard space via DRAMMS). Outputs span six structured tasks: a 4-level lesion severity grade with continuous percentage, a set of affected ROI labels, a set of atypical injury region labels, a 4-level MRI injury score (simplified from NICHD-NRN's 6 levels), a binary two-year neurocognitive outcome (normal/adverse), and a free-text MRI interpretation summary following a radiologist-recommended template.

## Method

The Clinical Graph of Thought (CGoT) model is a zero-shot prompting framework that decomposes the complex outcome-prediction problem into a sequential graph of six clinical sub-tasks, each mimicking a specific specialist's role (radiologist for lesion grading/anatomy, neonatologist for injury scoring, multi-specialist for outcome prediction). At each node, the LVLM receives three types of visual knowledge (raw ADC slices, Z_ADC lesion probability overlays, and color-coded ROI anatomy maps) plus two types of textual clinical knowledge: meta knowledge (lesion definitions, ROI lists, NICHD-NRN scoring criteria, MRI-to-outcome relationships) and individual knowledge (the model's own answers from preceding tasks in the graph). This graph-of-thought structure ensures each task builds on prior clinical findings, replicating the sequential diagnostic workflow a neonatologist follows when interpreting a radiologist's report. Ablations confirm that both the graph structure and the clinical knowledge inputs are essential, with the MRI Injury Score task contributing the largest single-task impact (~21% accuracy drop when removed), and the full pipeline achieving ~15% absolute gain over direct LVLM prompting on the primary neurocognitive outcome task.

## Results

On the HIE-Reasoning benchmark, all baseline LVLMs perform at or near random chance across the six clinical reasoning tasks. CGoT-Gemini-1.5-Flash achieves the strongest overall performance, reaching 71.73% inter-class accuracy on Neurocognitive Outcome Prediction (the primary task), 62.41% accuracy on Lesion Grading, and 53.68% ROUGE-L on MRI Interpretation Summary. CGoT-GPT4o-Series also improves substantially, reaching 61.11% on Neurocognitive Outcome and 56.25% on Lesion Grading. The headline gain is approximately 15 percentage points absolute on the neurocognitive outcome task (56.60% → 71.73% for Gemini; 52.16% → 61.11% for GPT-4o).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates five baseline LVLMs (GPT-4o-Series, Gemini-1.5-Flash, MiniGPT4-Med-7B, LLaVA-Med-7B, Med-Flamingo-7B) in zero-shot mode and then applies CGoT to the two strongest general-purpose models. CGoT-Gemini-1.5-Flash outperforms the Gemini baseline by +15.13 pp on Neurocognitive Outcome (56.60% → 71.73%), +37.6 pp on Lesion Grading accuracy (24.81% → 62.41%), and +13.4 pp on Lesion Anatomy F1 (30.17% → 43.57%). CGoT-GPT4o-Series similarly beats the GPT-4o baseline by +8.95 pp on Neurocognitive Outcome (52.16% → 61.11%) and +22.4 pp on Lesion Grading (33.83% → 56.25%). The three medical-specific LVLMs (MiniGPT4-Med, LLaVA-Med, Med-Flamingo) are only evaluated as zero-shot baselines and perform at or below random chance on most tasks; Med-Flamingo fails entirely on Lesion Anatomy and Rare Locations due to hallucination. No CGoT variant is tested on the medical LVLMs, so the paper does not directly compare CGoT against them. Overall, CGoT wins on every task it is evaluated against, with the largest absolute gains on the clinically critical Neurocognitive Outcome and Lesion Grading tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the medical VQA benchmarking line (VQA-Med, OmniMedVQA, VQA-RAD, SLAKE, PathVQA) by shifting from general pattern-recognition questions to professional-level clinical reasoning that requires disease-specific knowledge and future outcome prediction. Methodologically, it adapts graph-of-thought and chain-of-thought prompting—previously applied to text-based reasoning (Sun et al., 2023; Wei et al., 2022)—to the medical imaging domain, injecting structured clinical knowledge (NICHD-NRN scoring criteria, ADC/ZADC visual features, 62-ROI brain anatomy) as both visual and textual inputs to LVLMs. The CGoT framework departs from prior prompt-engineering approaches by encoding a multi-specialist diagnostic pipeline (radiologist → neonatologist → neurologist) as a sequential reasoning graph rather than a flat chain.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark is the first to combine clinical visual perception with professional-level medical knowledge and future neurocognitive outcome prediction, filling a gap where existing medical VQA datasets only test basic organ/modality recognition. The finding that even GPT-4o and Gemini perform at or below random chance on these tasks underscores a significant capability gap in current LVLMs for real clinical decision support. However, the small sample size (133 patients, single disease, single institution) and the modest absolute performance of CGoT (71.73% on outcome prediction) leave open questions about generalizability across populations, imaging sites, and other neonatal or adult neurological conditions. The approach's reliance on pre-computed ZADC lesion maps and DRAMMS-registered ROIs also raises practical deployment questions about pipeline complexity in real clinical settings.
