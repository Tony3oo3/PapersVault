## Problem

Existing learning-augmented algorithms for one-max-search face a fundamental trade-off: the Pareto-optimal algorithm of Sun et al. (2021) is brittle, collapsing to its worst-case guarantee under arbitrarily small prediction errors, while smooth alternatives sacrifice Pareto-optimality or rely on randomization. The paper asks whether a deterministic algorithm can simultaneously achieve the best possible consistency-robustness trade-off and smooth performance degradation as prediction error grows.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250137/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The algorithm receives a sequence of prices (p_i)_{i=1}^n in [1, θ] revealed sequentially, along with a prediction y ∈ [1, θ] of the maximum price p*, and must irrevocably accept the first price exceeding a threshold Φ(y). The theoretical output is a smoothness guarantee of the form ALG(p,y)/p* ≥ max(r, (1/rθ)·E(p*,y)^s), where E = min{p*/y, y/p*} is a scale-invariant, symmetric multiplicative error measure. In the stochastic extension, the input distributions F* (for P*) and G (for Y) and their coupling π* determine the expected performance bound.

## Method

The paper first characterizes the full class P_r of thresholds yielding Pareto-optimal consistency-robustness pairs, then defines a one-parameter family Φ_r^ρ (ρ ∈ [0,1]) that interpolates between the brittle Sun et al. threshold (ρ=0) and the smoothest Pareto-optimal threshold (ρ=1), where the threshold's maximal slope controls the smoothness exponent. Using the multiplicative error E, the authors prove a smoothness bound with exponent s_ρ = max(1, (ln θ)/(ρ ln(rθ)) − 2) and show via worst-case instances that no Pareto-optimal algorithm can improve this exponent for r ≤ θ^{-2/3}, establishing a triple Pareto front over consistency, robustness, and smoothness. For the stochastic setting, the analysis is cast as a Kantorovich optimal transport problem over couplings of (P*, Y), whose dual provides practical lower bounds on expected performance as a function of the marginal distributions and their coupling.

## Results

The paper's main results are theoretical. The proposed algorithm A_r^1 achieves a smoothness guarantee of ALG(p,y)/p* ≥ max(r, (1/rθ)·E(p*,y)^s) where s = max(1, ln θ/ln(rθ) − 2) under multiplicative error E, and this exponent is proven optimal among all Pareto-optimal algorithms for r ≤ θ^(−2/3) (Theorem 3.3). For the additive error η, A_r^1 achieves the optimal smoothness coefficient β* = (1−r²θ)/(rθ)·max(1/(1−r), 1/(rθ−1)) for all r ∈ [θ^(−1), θ^(−1/2)] (Theorem 3.4), attaining the triple Pareto front of consistency, robustness, and smoothness. On Bitcoin data (θ≈28, λ=0.5), A_r^1 degrades gradually as prediction error α increases, while A_r^0 (Sun et al.) collapses quickly to the robustness floor r.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is A_r^0 from Sun et al. (2021), the previously known Pareto-optimal algorithm. Both algorithms achieve identical consistency (1/rθ) and robustness (r), but A_r^0 suffers from a discontinuity in its threshold at y = 1/r, causing performance to collapse to the robustness floor even under arbitrarily small prediction errors. The proposed A_r^1 (ρ=1) eliminates this discontinuity by smoothly interpolating the threshold over [1/r, θ], yielding a continuous degradation governed by the exponent s. On synthetic worst-case instances (θ=5, λ=0.5), Figure 3 shows A_r^0's ratio drops sharply with minimal prediction perturbation, while A_r^1's ratio decreases much more gradually, matching the theoretical bound. On real Bitcoin data (θ≈28, 10-week windows, 2020–2024), Figure 4 confirms this: as the prediction error parameter α increases from 0 to 1, A_r^0 rapidly degrades to r while A_r^1 maintains a smoother, higher ratio throughout. The paper also notes that the randomized algorithm of Benomar & Perchet (2025) achieves smoothness but at the cost of deviating from the Pareto front, whereas A_r^1 achieves both properties simultaneously without randomization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends the Pareto-optimal threshold algorithm of Sun et al. (2021) for learning-augmented one-max-search, which achieved the optimal consistency-robustness trade-off but was shown to be brittle (Benomar & Perchet, 2025). The authors characterize the full class of Pareto-optimal thresholds and identify a smooth subfamily within it, resolving the previously perceived incompatibility between Pareto-optimality and smoothness that had motivated randomized or non-Pareto-optimal alternatives. The stochastic analysis section introduces a novel use of optimal transport (Kantorovich duality) to bound expected performance under coupled price and prediction distributions, a tool not previously applied to competitive analysis.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that a deterministic algorithm can simultaneously achieve the triple Pareto front of consistency, robustness, and smoothness is a clean theoretical resolution to a gap that prior work addressed only through randomization or by sacrificing one objective. The optimal transport framework opens a new analytical lens for stochastic competitive analysis, with the authors noting that the geometry of the induced OT problems is largely unexplored. Open questions include extending the framework to k-search (multi-unit), bounded prediction error settings, and whether the OT-based bounds can be tightened beyond the current dual-potential approach.
