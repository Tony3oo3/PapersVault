## Problem

Existing molecular property prediction methods—GNNs, Transformers, and equivariant message-passing networks—rely on pairwise or triplet-level approximations to capture many-body interactions such as bond angles and torsional potentials. Attention-based approaches that enable direct atom communication are typically limited to three-body (triplet) interactions, and none explicitly model four-body (quartet) interactions while preserving E(3) equivariance at practical computational cost.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252043/cropped_page3_idx4.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a 3D molecular structure specified by atomic types and Cartesian coordinates, which are embedded into scalar features (R^N×D), vector features (R^N×L×D via spherical harmonics expansion), and edge features (R^E×D) using exponential-normal RBFs with cosine cutoffs. It outputs the total molecular energy and per-atom forces, where forces are computed as the exact negative gradient of the predicted energy with respect to input coordinates, guaranteeing thermodynamic consistency. Training uses MSE loss with separate energy and force loss weights tuned per dataset.

## Method

MABNet introduces a four-body direct communication attention module embedded within an E(3)-equivariant message-passing (EquiMP) architecture derived from ViSNet. The key mechanism constructs two relationship tensors—node-node (pairwise) and node-edge (triplet)—which serve as asymmetric query and key/value components in a gated softmax attention over atomic quartets (i,j,k,l) corresponding to dihedral angles. This explicitly encodes torsional features via spherical harmonics while retaining vector-valued operations for SE(3) equivariance. To avoid the O(N⁴) combinatorial explosion, the method employs spatial sparsity (cutoff radius) and quantum-inspired edge pruning, reducing complexity to O(N²·|E|) with |E| ≤ 6N. The full model stacks 9 EquiMP layers, each combining the many-body attention update with equivariant scalar-vector interactions and vector-rejection-based edge updates, trained end-to-end with Adam and a linear warmup + decay schedule.

## Results

On MD22, MABNet achieves the lowest energy MAE in 4 of 5 molecules (e.g., 0.0534 kcal/mol on Ac-Ala3-NHMe, 0.0618 on DHA) and the lowest force MAE in 3 of 5, outperforming the strongest baseline ViSNet-LSRM by an average ~20% across both metrics. On SPICE, MABNet attains the best energy and force MAE in all 8 categories, with particularly large gains on dipeptides (7.7 meV/Å force MAE vs. 23.1 for TensorNet, a 67% reduction) and solvated amino acids (31.9 vs. 66.4 meV/Å, a 52% reduction). On MD17 (small molecules), MABNet matches or slightly exceeds ViSNet and Equiformer on energy but shows no significant advantage, consistent with the limited role of higher-order interactions in small systems.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MABNet outperforms all 12 baselines on the majority of metrics across MD22 and SPICE. Against the strongest competitors ViSNet-LSRM and Equiformer-LSRM, MABNet wins on 7 of 10 MD22 metrics but trails on AT-AT-CG-CG energy (0.1862 vs. 0.1135 kcal/mol), which the authors attribute to the dominance of pairwise interactions in that symmetric nanotube system. On SPICE, MABNet dominates all categories: it reduces dipeptide force MAE from 23.1 (TensorNet) to 7.7 meV/Å and solvated amino acid force MAE from 66.4 (TensorNet) to 31.9 meV/Å, while also beating SchNet, DimeNet++, and ET by wide margins. On MD17 (small molecules), MABNet achieves the best energy MAE in 6 of 7 systems (e.g., 0.039 meV on Ethanol vs. 0.051 for ViSNet/Equiformer) but is comparable to or slightly behind ViSNet on force MAE, confirming that four-body attention provides diminishing returns when higher-order interactions are less pronounced. The ablation further shows that removing equivariance from the 3-body attention degrades solvated amino acid force MAE by 23% (59.4 vs. 48.4 meV/Å), and extending to 4-body attention with 6N edges reduces it further to 31.9 meV/Å.

## Relation to Prior Work

MABNet builds directly on ViSNet's equivariant vector-scalar interactive message passing (EquiMP) architecture for its backbone, but introduces a novel four-body direct-communication attention module that replaces the implicit many-body approximation inherent in sequential message passing. It departs from prior explicit higher-order approaches—triplet graph transformers (TGT), axial attention (GEM-2), and AlphaFold's triangular updates—by simultaneously achieving E(3) equivariance and modeling four-body (dihedral) interactions, a combination none of these prior methods provide. The paper positions itself against the broader family of equivariant GNNs (SchNet, PaiNN, Equiformer, MACE) and hypergraph-based methods (SE3Set) that approximate multi-body effects through iterative aggregation rather than direct quartet communication.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The explicit four-body attention mechanism offers a physically grounded path toward capturing torsional and polarization effects that pairwise or triplet approximations systematically miss, with SOTA results on MD22 and SPICE validating this approach at scale. However, the method underperforms on highly symmetric systems dominated by pairwise interactions (e.g., the 370-atom carbon nanotube), and force predictions occasionally trail baselines, suggesting the four-body inductive bias is not universally beneficial. Open questions include whether the framework extends cleanly to five-body and higher-order terms, how adaptive quantum-inspired pruning will scale to thousands of atoms, and whether the ~2× training speed reduction relative to ViSNet becomes prohibitive for production MD simulations.
