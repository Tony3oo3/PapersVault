## Problem

Many widely used time series forecasting models (e.g., iTransformer, RMLP) are channel-unidentifiable: they produce identical outputs for identical inputs regardless of which channel the input belongs to, thereby discarding channel-specific characteristics. Even channel-identifiable models can benefit from stronger channel differentiation. The paper identifies this gap and asks how to systematically enhance channel identifiability across diverse TS backbones with minimal architectural changes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251317/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates as a plug-in normalization layer inside the encoder of existing TS backbones, consuming D-dimensional channel tokens (z ∈ R^{B×C×D}) and emitting normalized outputs with channel-specific affine transformations. It is applied to multivariate time series with a fixed lookback window (L=96) and produces forecasts over multiple horizons (H ∈ {96,192,336,720} for ETT/Exchange/Weather/ECL/Solar; H ∈ {12,24,48,96} for PEMS traffic data). The method is orthogonal to input/output normalization (e.g., RevIN) and can be stacked with other CID techniques such as channel identifiers or C-LoRA.

## Method

Channel Normalization (CN) replaces the shared affine parameters (α, β) in Layer Normalization with per-channel parameters (α_c, β_c), so each channel receives a distinct scaling and shift after normalization, enabling the model to differentiate channels even when inputs are identical. Adaptive CN (ACN) extends this by introducing local parameters that are dynamically weighted by a channel-similarity matrix (cosine similarity normalized by softmax with temperature τ); the effective parameters are the element-wise product of the global CN parameters and the input-adaptive local parameters, allowing the normalization to capture time-varying channel characteristics. Prototypical CN (PCN) replaces per-channel parameters with a fixed set of K learnable prototypes, where each channel's effective parameters are a weighted combination of prototypes determined by channel-prototype similarity computed in a projected data space, enabling a single model to handle datasets with unknown or varying numbers of channels (e.g., TS foundation models). The paper provides a theoretical justification via Gaussian entropy analysis showing H(Z_None) ≤ H(Z_LN) ≤ H(Z_CN), and empirically demonstrates that the channel entropy gain correlates with MSE improvement (ρ = 0.724) across 12 datasets.

## Results

Across 12 datasets and 4 forecast horizons (H ∈ {96, 192, 336, 720}), CN and ACN consistently reduce average MSE for all four backbones. For non-CID models, iTransformer improves from 0.275 to 0.244 MSE (+CN, 11.3%) and 0.241 (+ACN, 12.4%), while RMLP improves from 0.297 to 0.262 (+ACN, 11.8%). For CID models, S-Mamba improves from 0.253 to 0.240 (+ACN, 5.1%) and TSMixer from 0.266 to 0.243 (+ACN, 8.6%). PCN applied to the UniTS foundation model achieves 7.7% MSE reduction (supervised) and 5.2% (prompt-tuning) on 20 forecasting tasks, while also improving 18 classification tasks by 3.0–5.5% accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CN/ACN are applied as plug-in modifications to four backbone architectures (iTransformer, RMLP, S-Mamba, TSMixer), yielding consistent MSE reductions of 4–12% depending on the backbone's CID and data-dependency properties. Against prior CID-enhancing methods, ACN outperforms the Channel identifier (Chi et al., 2024) and C-LoRA (Nie et al., 2024) across all four backbones in Table 6; for example, on iTransformer the average MSE is 0.241 (ACN) vs. 0.248 (Channel identifier) and 0.254 (C-LoRA), and on RMLP it is 0.262 (ACN) vs. 0.284 (Channel identifier) and 0.282 (C-LoRA). Notably, the naive baselines C-token and C-project even degrade CID models (e.g., TSMixer drops from 0.266 to 0.310 with C-token, a −16.5% change). CN/ACN also outperform Instance Normalization (Ulyanov, 2016) on iTransformer (0.241 vs. 0.246 average MSE). For the TSFM setting, PCN improves UniTS (Gao et al., 2024) by 7.7% MSE on supervised forecasting (0.433 vs. 0.469) and 5.2% on prompt-tuning forecasting (0.453 vs. 0.478), while also enabling zero-shot inference on unseen datasets (4.2% average MSE reduction on Solar, River, Hospital).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly modifies Layer Normalization by replacing its shared affine parameters with channel-specific ones, creating Channel Normalization (CN). It positions itself against prior CID-enhancing methods—InjectTST's channel identifiers, C-LoRA's channel-aware low-rank adaptation, and CCM's channel-cluster identity—which the authors argue either require architectural modifications or yield limited gains. The adaptive (ACN) and prototypical (PCN) variants extend the core idea by introducing input-dependent local parameters and learnable prototypes, respectively, drawing on attention-style weighting mechanisms. The theoretical grounding borrows from Gaussian entropy analysis and MMSE bounds established in prior representation-learning literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that a minimal normalization change (channel-specific vs. shared affine parameters) yields 11–12% average MSE reductions across diverse backbones, the paper elevates channel identifiability from an implicit architectural property to an explicit, addressable design criterion. The PCN variant is particularly impactful for the emerging TS foundation model paradigm, where a single model must handle datasets with unknown or varying channel counts. Open questions include automatically determining the number of prototypes K, whether the approach transfers to other sequence tasks (e.g., language, audio), and whether the gains persist under distribution shift beyond what RevIN-style input normalization already handles.
