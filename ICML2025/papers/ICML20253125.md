## Problem

Standard neural network pruning methods, when applied to long-tailed datasets, risk removing parameters critical for tail-class recognition, use static criteria that conflict with the dynamic evolution of parameter importance during training, and rely on single evaluation metrics that fail to capture nuanced parameter roles across imbalanced classes. This paper asks how to design a pruning strategy that adaptively protects tail-class parameters while achieving aggressive model compression.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20253125/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images from long-tailed datasets (CIFAR-100-LT at 32×32 with IR=50/100, ImageNet-LT at 224×224, iNaturalist 2018) paired with class labels following a power-law distribution. It produces a pruned CNN backbone (ResNet-32 or ResNet-50) retaining approximately 30% of original parameters, along with per-class (head/medium/tail) classification accuracy. The pruning process begins after epoch 100 and operates on parameter groups (convolutional kernels), with the final mask applied to the backbone while the classifier head is preserved.

## Method

LTAP combines three key mechanisms: (1) a multi-criteria importance scoring framework that integrates magnitude-based scores (absolute and normalized L2 norms) with gradient-based scores (cosine alignment, first-order Taylor impact, second-order Taylor stability) into a weighted composite score per parameter group; (2) an LT-Vote dynamic weight adjustment mechanism that maintains a learnable criteria-weight matrix D updated by class-specific accuracy changes, so that criteria contributing to tail-class improvement receive higher weights over training; and (3) a progressive multi-stage pruning schedule that distributes the total sparsity ratio across P stages, re-evaluating importance at each stage. The theoretical foundation (Theorems 1–4) establishes that tail classes require higher overparameterization (γ_c ≥ Ω(N₁/N_c · 1/log N_c)), motivating a tail-biased pruning objective that weights the loss by class imbalance ratio to preferentially retain tail-class parameters.

## Results

On CIFAR-100-LT (IR=50), BS+LTAP achieves 47.8% overall accuracy and 34.1% tail accuracy while reducing FLOPs to 22.6% of the baseline, yielding a C/F ratio of 4.1—nearly double that of BS+RReg (2.2) and ~6× that of BS+ATO (0.7). On ImageNet-LT, BS+LTAP reaches 48.1% overall accuracy at 30.6% FLOPs (C/F=2.6), and on iNaturalist 2018 it achieves 60.5% overall accuracy at 30.6% FLOPs (C/F=2.9). Across all three datasets and all three long-tailed baselines (BS, LDAM-DRW, DBLP), LTAP consistently attains the highest C/F ratio while preserving or improving tail-class accuracy relative to both ATO and RReg.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LTAP is evaluated in combination with three long-tailed learning baselines (BS, LDAM-DRW, DBLP) and compared against two SOTA pruning methods (ATO and RReg) on three datasets. Against ATO, LTAP wins decisively on every dataset and every long-tailed backbone: on CIFAR-100-LT IR=50, BS+LTAP achieves 47.8% overall accuracy vs. 32.9% for BS+ATO, while using only 22.6% FLOPs versus ATO's 84.7%. Against RReg, LTAP also dominates: BS+LTAP reaches 34.1% tail accuracy vs. 23.8% for BS+RReg at a lower FLOPs budget (22.6% vs. 35.1%), and the C/F ratio is 4.1 vs. 2.2. On ImageNet-LT, BS+LTAP achieves 30.1% tail accuracy versus 18.2% (RReg) and 17.8% (ATO), with a C/F of 2.6 versus 1.3 and 1.1 respectively. On iNaturalist 2018, LTAP maintains near-uniform accuracy across head/medium/tail classes (59–61%) while ATO and RReg drop to 30–45%, confirming that traditional pruning disproportionately harms tail classes. The only case where LTAP does not lead in raw tail accuracy is LDAM-DRW+LTAP on CIFAR-100-LT IR=50 (23.3% tail vs. 23.7% for RReg), but LTAP still wins on overall accuracy (44.8% vs. 42.4%) and C/F (3.6 vs. 2.4).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two well-established research threads—long-tailed classification (BS, LDAM-DRW, DBLP) and neural network pruning (ATO, RReg)—but identifies a gap: standard pruning methods are agnostic to class imbalance and can inadvertently remove parameters critical for tail classes. Rather than building on a single prior method, LTAP repurposes the multi-criteria importance scoring framework from general pruning (magnitude, gradient, Taylor terms) and injects a class-aware dynamic weighting mechanism (LT-Vote) that redistributes criterion weights based on per-class accuracy changes during training. The theoretical section provides a principled justification—tail classes require higher overparameterization—linking the practical pruning heuristic back to sample complexity bounds, though the connection between the theoretical allocation strategy and the actual algorithmic implementation remains somewhat loose.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical relevance is clear: deploying accurate classifiers on imbalanced real-world data (e-commerce, speech, NLP) under tight compute budgets is a common engineering need, and LTAP offers a drop-in pruning layer on top of existing long-tailed training pipelines. However, several open questions remain: the theoretical results rely on a strong assumption of logarithmic performance-parameter scaling, the method is validated only on ResNet-32/50 backbones, and the C/F metric (relative accuracy ratio divided by relative FLOPs ratio) is sensitive to baseline choice. Whether the tail-biased protection generalizes to transformer architectures, larger imbalance ratios beyond IR=100, or non-visual modalities is untested. The dynamic weight update rule (Eq. 6) is also quite simple—essentially a binary indicator of accuracy improvement—raising questions about robustness in noisy training regimes.
