## Problem

Gradient-based molecule optimization struggles with the continuous-discrete nature of molecular data: standard gradient guidance is designed for continuous Gaussian variables and cannot be directly applied to discrete atom types, while guiding only continuous coordinates (as in TAGMol) creates inconsistencies between modalities and fails to optimize topology-dependent properties like synthesizability. This paper addresses both challenges by formulating guidance in a continuous, differentiable parameter space derived through Bayesian inference.

## Input / Output

The method takes a protein pocket (3D atomic coordinates and K-dimensional type features), a pretrained differentiable energy function (a 9-layer equivariant network with kNN graphs, k=32, trained via squared loss on Vina Score, QED, and SA), and optionally a reference molecule whose scaffold or R-groups are fixed for constrained tasks. It outputs an optimized 3D molecule (N atom coordinates in R^{N×3} and N discrete atom types) sampled over 200 BFN steps with guidance scale 50. In constrained settings (R-group optimization, scaffold hopping), specific substructures are enforced via infilling, and the molecule size is restricted to match the reference for fair comparison.

## Method

MolJO builds on the MolCRAFT BFN backbone and performs gradient guidance over the Bayesian posterior θ = [θ^x, θ^v] rather than directly on noisy latents, enabling a unified product-of-experts formulation π(θ_i|θ_{i-1}) ∝ p_φ(θ_i|θ_{i-1}) · exp[−E(θ_i, t_i)]. For continuous coordinates, guidance shifts the Gaussian mean of θ^x by an uncertainty-adjusted gradient; for discrete types, guidance operates on the Gaussian latent y^v and, through the Bayesian update function, reweights the Categorical distribution of θ^v without leaving the probability simplex. A novel backward correction strategy maintains a sliding window of k past steps, replacing the original clean-sample estimates in the Bayesian update with the current optimized estimate, which aligns gradients across steps and provides a flexible exploration-exploitation trade-off. The full pipeline preserves SE(3)-equivariance when both the BFN network Φ and the energy function E are equivariant, and requires no oracle simulations—only differentiable property predictors.

## Results

On CrossDocked2020, MolJO achieves a Success Rate of 51.3%, Vina Dock of -9.05, and SA of 0.78, representing a 4× improvement in Success Rate over the gradient-based baseline TAGMol (11.1%) and a 2× higher 'Me-Better' ratio (39.8% vs. <17%) compared to other 3D baselines. When equipped with top-of-N oracle selection (G+O variant), MolJO further reaches 70.3% Success Rate and Vina Dock of -10.50. In constrained optimization (R-group redesign, growing, scaffold hopping), MolJO consistently outperforms all baselines, e.g., achieving 29.0% Success Rate in R-group redesign vs. 22.7% for MolCRAFT and 19.2% for TAGMol.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MolJO substantially outperforms the gradient-based baseline TAGMol, achieving 51.3% Success Rate vs. 11.1% (4× improvement) and SA of 0.78 vs. 0.56, demonstrating that joint continuous-discrete gradient guidance is critical—TAGMol's coordinate-only guidance improves affinity but sacrifices synthesizability. Against the BFN backbone MolCRAFT (no guidance), MolJO improves Vina Dock from -7.67 to -9.05 and Success Rate from 26.8% to 51.3%, validating the gradient guidance mechanism. Compared to oracle-based methods RGA (46.2%) and DecompOpt (39.4%), MolJO achieves a higher Success Rate without any docking simulation calls, and maintains higher diversity (0.66 vs. 0.41 and 0.63). Among all generative baselines, MolJO dominates on Vina Dock (-9.05 vs. best generative IPDiff at -8.57) while also achieving the best SA (0.78), whereas IPDiff's superior affinity comes at the cost of low SA (0.59). In constrained tasks (Table 2), MolJO leads all baselines across R-group redesign (29.0% SR), growing (32.7% SR), and scaffold hopping (23.6% SR), with diffusion-based baselines (TargetDiff, TAGMol) falling below 20% Success Rate and <70% connected-molecule validity in the hardest scaffold hopping setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

MolJO builds directly on Bayesian Flow Networks (Graves et al., 2023) and the MolCRAFT backbone (Qu et al., 2024), repurposing the continuous parameter space θ as the natural substrate for gradient guidance. It departs from prior gradient-guided molecular optimization (TAGMol, EEGSDE, DiGress) by operating on the Bayesian posterior rather than noisy latents, which resolves the continuous-discrete mismatch: discrete atom types are guided through their Gaussian-distributed latent y while remaining on the probability simplex. The backward correction strategy is a novel sampling mechanism that unifies and generalizes prior BFN sampling variants (k=1 point estimate, k=n full-history) into a sliding-window framework with explicit exploration-exploitation trade-off.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides the first principled, end-to-end differentiable framework for joint gradient guidance over both continuous coordinates and discrete atom types in structure-based molecule optimization, eliminating the need for expensive oracle simulations while maintaining SE(3)-equivariance. The extension to constrained tasks (R-group optimization, scaffold hopping) demonstrates practical relevance for lead optimization pipelines. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond CrossDocked2020 to larger or more diverse protein sets, whether the trained property regressors (Vina, QED, SA) can be replaced by more accurate but less differentiable oracles, and how the backward correction window size k should be adapted per task or per protein.
