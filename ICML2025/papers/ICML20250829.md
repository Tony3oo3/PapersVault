## Problem

Self-attention in Graph Transformers inherently acts as a low-pass filter, preserving only low-frequency signals and failing to capture high-frequency components critical for heterophilic graphs. Existing remedies—polynomial filters (FeTA, PolyFormer) with fixed bases, or Specformer's spectrum-adaptive but first-order-only filter—remain limited in expressiveness and cannot simultaneously adapt to both spectral order and graph spectrum. The paper asks whether a GT can efficiently learn filters that are adaptive in both dimensions.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a graph's adjacency matrix and node feature matrix, with the normalized Laplacian's spectral decomposition (eigenvalues in [0,2] and eigenvectors) precomputed offline. For large graphs (e.g., Penn94 with 41K nodes), truncated decomposition retains only the smallest and largest eigenvalues to maintain scalability. Output is node class probabilities via softmax for node classification, or graph-level predictions via max-pooling readout for graph classification/regression tasks.

## Method

GrokFormer introduces a Graph Fourier KAN spectral filter that parameterizes learnable activation functions as Fourier series over K-order graph Laplacian spectrum: h(λ) = Σ_k α_k Σ_m (cos(mλ^k)·a_km + sin(mλ^k)·b_km). The learnable order coefficients α_k adaptively weight contributions from different spectral orders (1st through Kth), while the Fourier coefficients a_km and b_km adaptively shape the frequency response at each order, making the filter simultaneously order- and spectrum-adaptive. This spectral convolution (U diag(h(λ)) U^T X) is combined with efficient multi-head self-attention in a standard Transformer encoder, with both branches synthesized via summation and layer normalization. The Fourier series parameterization is chosen over KAN's B-splines for orthogonality, guaranteed convergence, and linear O(KNM) filter learning complexity, and the paper proves that polynomial filters and Specformer's filter are special cases of this formulation.

## Results

On 11 node classification datasets, GrokFormer achieves the best accuracy on all datasets, with particularly strong gains on heterophilic graphs: 91.39% on Squirrel, 75.58% on Chameleon, and 94.59% on Texas, outperforming the closest spectral GT baseline Specformer by 3.8–9.8 percentage points. On homophilic datasets, GrokFormer reaches 89.57% (Cora), 98.08% (Physics), and 83.59% (Penn94). On 5 graph classification benchmarks, GrokFormer achieves the best result on 4 out of 5 datasets, with an 11.9% relative improvement over Specformer on PTC-MR and a 5.6% average improvement across all graph-level tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GrokFormer outperforms the closest spectral GT competitor Specformer on all 11 node classification datasets, with the largest margins on heterophilic graphs: +15.4 pp on Chameleon (75.58 vs. 60.17), +1.8 pp on Squirrel (91.39 vs. 89.57), and +5.6 pp on Texas (94.59 vs. 89.02). Among polynomial spectral GNNs, JacobiConv is the strongest baseline on heterophilic data (74.20 on Chameleon, 57.38 on Squirrel), yet GrokFormer still leads by 1.4–33.0 pp, demonstrating the advantage of spectrum-adaptive Fourier bases over fixed polynomial bases. On homophilic datasets, GrokFormer edges out JacobiConv and SGFormer by 0.6–1.6 pp (e.g., 89.57 vs. 88.98 on Cora, 98.08 vs. 97.96 on Physics). Spatial GNNs (GCN, GAT, H2GCN, HopGNN) and vanilla GTs (Transformer, GraphGPS, NodeFormer) are consistently outperformed, with GraphGPS and NodeFormer failing to run on large-scale Penn94 (OOM). On graph classification, GrokFormer beats Specformer by an average of 5.6% across 5 TU benchmarks and achieves an 11.9% relative improvement on PTC-MR, while also surpassing kernel methods (GK, WL, DGK) by up to 34.9%.

## Relation to Prior Work

GrokFormer sits at the intersection of two recent lines of work: Kolmogorov-Arnold Networks (KANs), which replace fixed weight matrices with learnable activation functions, and Specformer, which learns spectrum-adaptive filter bases via self-attention over eigenvalues. The key conceptual departure is replacing KAN's B-spline parameterization with Fourier series and extending Specformer's first-order-only spectral modeling to arbitrary K-th order, thereby unifying order adaptivity (as in polynomial GNNs like BernNet and JacobiConv) with spectrum adaptivity in a single filter. The paper formally proves that both polynomial filters and Specformer's filter are special cases of the proposed GrokFormer filter, positioning it as a strict generalization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first graph Transformer with a filter that is simultaneously adaptive in both spectral order and graph spectrum, addressing a gap where prior methods could only adapt to one dimension. The practical impact is demonstrated by consistent SOTA improvements across 11 node-classification and 5 graph-classification datasets, plus a favorable efficiency profile (lower memory and time than both Specformer and PolyFormer). A key limitation is the reliance on full or truncated spectral decomposition (O(N³) offline), which the authors acknowledge as a barrier to very large graphs; the stated future direction of learning expressive filters without spectral decomposition remains open. The method's generality also raises the question of whether the added complexity (K×M Fourier coefficients per layer) is justified over simpler spectrum-adaptive approaches on homophilic graphs where low-pass filters suffice.
