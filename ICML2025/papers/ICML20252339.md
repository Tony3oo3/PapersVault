## Problem

Long-context models can accurately locate salient information within long sequences but produce misaligned generations (hallucinations, instruction unfollowing), and existing remedies—scaling SFT data or post-training on long instructions—fall short in either effectiveness or efficiency. The paper identifies that SFT's cross-entropy loss is dominated by the long context portion, leaving the prediction signal under-optimized, and that the open-source community lacks both a principled preference-alignment objective and an efficient data synthesis pipeline for long-context scenarios.

## Input / Output

At inference, the model receives a long context (up to 256K tokens) paired with a question or instruction and generates a free-form text response. During training, each sample is a triplet {question, context, prediction} where the context is split into 16 equal-length chunks of 512 tokens; the actual GPU sequence length is kept short (4K–8K) while positional indices are synthetically expanded to simulate up to 64K–256K context, so a single 8×A800 node can process the data without OOM. The preference pair consists of one chosen response (generated from relevant chunks) and M=2 rejected responses (generated from irrelevant or partially relevant chunks) that share the same assembled context.

## Method

LOGO combines a reference-free multi-rejected-sample preference optimization loss with a positional-indices-synthesis data pipeline. The loss extends SimPO by averaging the log-probabilities of M rejected samples inside the sigmoid, encouraging the model to push down a broader set of misaligned outputs simultaneously, and adds a λ-weighted SFT regularization term to preserve language-modeling quality. For data synthesis, an NER-based evaluator (spaCy) scores each 512-token chunk by entity overlap with the question; chunks above a threshold δ form the 'critical' set used to generate the chosen response, while chunks below δ (or a mix) generate the rejected responses. Positional indices are then synthesized by sampling per-chunk biases from a uniform distribution so that relative distances are uniformly covered across the target context length, allowing short physical sequences to stand in for long ones. The entire pipeline trains with only ~0.3B tokens on a single 8×A800 machine for 16 hours via LoRA, and the authors provide a generalization-error bound (via McDiarmid's inequality) and gradient-level convergence analysis for the multi-rejected objective.

## Results

On LongBench (average across S-Doc QA, M-Doc QA, Summarization, Few-shot, and Synthetic subtasks), LOGO achieves 47.0 for Llama-3-8B-Instruct-80K, approaching GPT-4's 48.5 while using only 0.3B tokens of training data on a single 8×A800 GPU for 16 hours. For short-context models, LOGO-64K on Llama-3-8B-Instruct-8K reaches 43.9 average, outperforming the 8K baseline (40.7) and all context-scaling baselines. On short-context tasks (MMLU, TruthfulQA), LOGO preserves or slightly improves backbone performance, whereas SFT-based long-context alignment causes measurable degradation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LOGO outperforms all seven baselines across every model and setting. Against training-free YaRN (40.9 avg), LOGO-64K achieves 43.9 on Llama-3-8B-Instruct, a 3.0-point gain; against SFT-based PoSE (37.7), the margin widens to 6.2 points. Among long-context alignment methods, LOGO-80K (47.0) surpasses SimPO (44.5) by 2.5 points and LongLoRA (42.8) by 4.2 points on Llama-3-8B-Instruct-80K. For Llama-2-7B-64K, LOGO-64K (36.1) beats LongAlign (33.9) by 2.2 points, and for Mistral-7B-32K, LOGO-32K (40.1) edges out FILM-32K (38.8) by 1.3 points. The largest gains appear on information-intensive subtasks (Summarization and Synthetic), where LOGO improves by 5+ points over SFT baselines, confirming that preference optimization specifically addresses the generation misalignment that SFT alone cannot resolve.

## Relation to Prior Work

LOGO extends the SimPO reference-free preference optimization framework by introducing multiple rejected samples (M > 1) to broaden the dis-preferred response space, addressing the absence of reliable long-context evaluators. It combines this with the positional indices synthesis technique from PoSE (Zhu et al., 2023) to simulate long-sequence training within short-sequence memory budgets. Conceptually, the paper departs from the dominant SFT-based long-context alignment paradigm (LongAlign, FILM, data-engineering approaches) by arguing that cross-entropy loss on long contexts is insufficient for calibrating generation quality, and that explicit preference signals are needed to push models away from hallucinations and instruction-unfollowing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical efficiency is the standout contribution: 0.3B tokens, 16 hours, and a single 8×A800 machine suffice to bring an open-source 8B model close to GPT-4 on LongBench, making long-context alignment accessible to resource-constrained labs. A key open limitation is the reliance on NER-based entity overlap as the automatic evaluator for data synthesis, which the authors acknowledge is imperfect and would benefit from stronger LLM-based or human-annotated evaluation. Whether the multi-rejected-sample preference objective and positional synthesis strategy transfer cleanly to even longer contexts (beyond 256K) or to non-English/multilingual settings remains to be validated.
