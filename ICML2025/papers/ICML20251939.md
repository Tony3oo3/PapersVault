## Problem

While power-law scaling relationships are well-characterized for large language models, embodied AI tasks like world modeling and behavior cloning have only demonstrated that 'bigger is better' through limited ablations, without precise power-law coefficients or understanding of what factors (tokenizer, architecture, task) govern the optimal trade-off between model and dataset size. This paper asks whether the same types of scaling laws found in LLMs emerge in these embodied pre-training tasks, and how the coefficients are shaped by design choices.

## Input / Output

The models consume sequences of RGB image observations (128×128 or 180×300 pixels) paired with discretized controller actions (binary buttons plus 11-bucket joystick values for Bleeding Edge; 500-bucket continuous values for RT-1). Images are either tokenized into 256 or 540 discrete VQGAN codes per frame, or compressed into a single 1024-dim continuous embedding via a 0.6M-parameter ConvNeXt CNN. The primary outputs are not predictions per se but fitted power-law coefficients (exponents a and b in N_optimal ∝ C^a, D_optimal ∝ C^b) and compute-optimal loss curves, derived from cross-entropy pre-training loss measured in the infinite data regime.

## Method

The paper trains families of GPT-2-style causal transformers across model sizes (2M–894M parameters) and dataset sizes for two tasks (world modeling predicting next image tokens, behavior cloning predicting next action tokens) under two architectures (tokenized VQGAN inputs vs. single CNN embedding). Scaling coefficients are extracted via two methods: a Frontier fit on the efficient frontier of loss-vs-FLOPs curves, and a Parametric fit to the form L(N,D) = Nc/N^α + Dc/D^β + E when curves do not saturate. The key finding is that the tokenizer's compression rate shifts the optimal model-size exponent (0.49 for 256 tokens/image to 0.62 for 540), while the sparse, super-classed nature of BC supervision (3% of tokens supervised vs. 97% for WM) combined with the 556× compute overhead of the tokenized architecture shifts BC coefficients heavily toward data (N_optimal ∝ C^0.32). Tiny-scale Shakespeare character-level experiments with sparse and super-classed losses confirm these mechanisms, and an 894M-parameter extrapolation test validates the derived scaling laws.

## Results

The paper establishes that power-law scaling relationships analogous to LLMs emerge in world modeling (WM) and behavior cloning (BC) pre-training. The key quantitative finding is in the compute-optimal scaling coefficients: WM-Token-256 yields N_optimal ∝ C^0.49 and D_optimal ∝ C^0.51 (matching LLM Chinchilla ratios), while WM-Token-540 shifts to N_optimal ∝ C^0.62 and D_optimal ∝ C^0.37, showing tokenizer compression rate directly affects the optimal model-to-data trade-off. For BC, the tokenized architecture (BC-Token-540) heavily favors data over model size (N_optimal ∝ C^0.32, D_optimal ∝ C^0.68), whereas the CNN architecture (BC-CNN) reverses this to favor model size (N_optimal ∝ C^0.66, D_optimal ∝ C^0.34). Pre-training loss is validated as a proxy for downstream performance, correlating with online return at r > −0.94 and with world-model generation quality (FVD) at r = 0.83.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not benchmark a single method against external baselines; instead it compares scaling-law coefficients across internal configurations and against the LLM literature. The WM-Token-256 coefficients (a≈0.49, b≈0.51) closely match the Chinchilla-optimal ratio from Hoffmann et al. (2022) for LLMs, confirming that the same power-law structure transfers to embodied world modeling. Increasing tokenizer compression (256→540 tokens per image) shifts the WM coefficient from 0.49 to 0.62, a finding not observed in LLM scaling (where tokenizer choice was reported as irrelevant by Henighan et al., 2020). For BC, the tokenized architecture produces coefficients (a=0.32, b=0.68) that heavily favor data over model size, while the CNN architecture (a=0.66, b=0.34) aligns more closely with Tuyls et al. (2023) who found similar model-size-favoring coefficients for LSTM-based BC. The RT-1 robotics dataset and small-scale language experiments (character-level: a=0.66 vs. GPT-2: a=0.44) independently confirm that lower-compression tokenizers consistently increase the optimal model-size coefficient across domains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LLM scaling laws framework (Kaplan et al. 2020; Hoffmann et al. 2022) to embodied AI, specifically world modeling and behavior cloning, building on Tuyls et al. (2023) who first investigated scaling for BC with small LSTM models. It departs from prior embodied AI scaling work by studying pre-training loss in the infinite data regime rather than downstream task performance, and by using transformer architectures on large-scale human behavior datasets (Bleeding Edge, RT-1) rather than small fixed-policy environments. A key conceptual departure from Henighan et al. (2020) is the finding that tokenizer compression rate *does* affect optimal model size coefficients in world modeling, contradicting their video generation results.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This provides the first concrete, actionable scaling guidance for embodied AI practitioners: how to split compute between model size and data depends on both the task (WM vs BC) and the architecture (tokenized vs CNN), with coefficients ranging from 0.32 to 0.66 for N_optimal. The tokenizer compression effect has direct implications for world model design choices (e.g., VQGAN resolution). Key limitations include restriction to generative pre-training objectives (no representation learning or reward-centric models), two specific datasets, and no accounting for inference compute or fine-tuning effects. Open questions include whether these laws hold for alternative objectives, larger-scale robotics, and whether the pre-training loss proxy remains valid in more complex real-world settings.
