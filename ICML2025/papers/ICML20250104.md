## Problem

In the era of generative AI producing vast libraries of de novo molecules, identifying truly drug-like candidates remains an open problem. Existing drug-likeness predictors rely on ambiguous negative sets or purely structural features, while approved drugs are extremely scattered in chemical space (fewer than two drugs per scaffold on average) and the full compound space (~10^60 compounds) has no representative negative distribution, making supervised and PU-learning approaches fundamentally ill-posed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250104/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 1024-dimensional Morgan Fingerprints as structural input for all compounds, and 300-dimensional DREAMwalk biomedical knowledge graph embeddings as a second modality available only for approved drugs. The trained structural encoder projects non-drugs (which lack biomedical annotations) into the unified knowledge-enriched space. Output is a continuous drug-likeness score (Euclidean distance from the boundary center in latent space) and a binary drug-like/non-drug-like label determined by whether the compound falls inside the learned hyperspherical boundary of radius r around center c.

## Method

BOUNDR.E operates in two stages. First, a knowledge-integrated multi-modal alignment maps molecular fingerprint space and biomedical knowledge graph space into a unified embedding space U using a softened CLIP loss weighted by ATC-code semantic similarity (replacing hard one-hot targets with soft labels) combined with three forms of geodesic mixup (S-Mix, K-Mix, KS-Mix) to close the modality gap on the hypersphere. Second, an EM-like iterative optimization constructs a hyperspherical one-class boundary: the E-step updates the boundary center and radius to enclose ~100% of drug embeddings, identifying out-of-boundary compounds as pseudo-negatives; the M-step updates the embedding encoder via a distance-based loss that contracts drugs toward the center while pushing pseudo-negative compounds toward the compound-space boundary. A theoretical guarantee (Theorem 1) proves that decreasing the boundary loss monotonically reduces the number of non-drugs inside the boundary, and convergence is monitored via the in-boundary compound ratio rather than raw loss to avoid numerical instability.

## Results

On the time-based split (10-fold CV), BOUNDR.E achieves F1=0.846, ICR=0.009, AUROC=0.978, and Average Precision=0.908, representing a ~10% F1 improvement over the previous state-of-the-art DeepDL (F1=0.740) and a 6× reduction in in-boundary compound ratio (ICR 0.009 vs. 0.054). On the scaffold-based split, BOUNDR.E maintains F1=0.655 with ICR=0.063, still outperforming all baselines. In zero-shot toxic compound filtering, BOUNDR.E achieves the lowest false-positive rates across withdrawn (0.51), hepatotoxic (0.54), cardiotoxic (0.20), and carcinogenic (0.19) categories.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BOUNDR.E outperforms all 10 baselines on F1 and ICR under the time-based split. Against the strongest baseline DeepDL (F1=0.740, ICR=0.054), BOUNDR.E gains +10.6 F1 points and reduces ICR by 6× (0.009 vs. 0.054), though DeepDL retains a higher IDR (0.888 vs. 0.799), indicating BOUNDR.E's boundary is tighter at the cost of slightly lower recall. Among supervised GNN methods, D-GCAN (F1=0.669, ICR=0.160) and DrugMetric (F1=0.170, ICR=0.760) are substantially outperformed, with DrugMetric's GMM-based approach failing to produce probability scores for AUROC/AP. Classical fingerprint-based classifiers (FP-XGB F1=0.692, FP-SVM F1=0.665) and PU-learning methods (FP-nnPU F1=0.608, FP-PU with NN F1=0.634) all trail BOUNDR.E by 15–25 F1 points. One-class baselines (FP-OCSVM F1=0.090, FP-DeepSVDD F1=0.166) perform worst overall, confirming the paper's argument that static one-class boundaries are insufficient for the dispersed drug distribution in chemical space.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant binary-classification and PU-learning formulations of drug-likeness prediction (DGCAN, DeepDL, nnPU) by reframing the problem as one-class hyperspherical boundary construction around approved drugs, eliminating the need for a representative negative set in the unbounded chemical space. It further extends multi-modal contrastive alignment (CLIP, Geodesic Mixup) into the biochemical domain by integrating DREAMwalk knowledge-graph embeddings with Morgan fingerprints via a softened CLIP loss weighted by ATC semantic similarity. The EM-like iterative co-optimization of boundary parameters and the embedding encoder is the paper's core architectural novelty, distinguishing it from static one-class classifiers (OC-SVM, DeepSVDD) that the authors show are fundamentally inadequate for this dispersed drug distribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work addresses a practical bottleneck in AI-driven drug discovery: generative models produce vast compound libraries, but no reliable data-driven filter exists to separate viable drug candidates from non-drugs without relying on ambiguous negative sets. The zero-shot toxic compound filtering (without explicit toxicity labels) and the in silico screening pipeline (reducing 10k generated compounds to ~3% for wet-lab validation) demonstrate immediate practical utility. Key open questions include whether the EM-like optimization can be guaranteed to reach global optima, how the model generalizes to novel modalities (PROTACs, biologics) or rare-disease compounds lacking precedent, and whether the biomedical knowledge alignment remains effective as the approved-drug corpus expands into new therapeutic areas.
