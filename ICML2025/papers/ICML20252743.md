## Problem

Existing domain reweighting methods for LLM pretraining (DoReMi, DoGE) are computationally expensive, couple weight computation to proxy model optimization (requiring costly retraining when domains change), and do not extend to the fine-tuning stage. No prior method simultaneously achieves efficient weight computation, seamless adaptation to new data, and applicability across both pretraining and fine-tuning.

## Input / Output

The method consumes text samples from k domains, a small proxy model (82M decoder-only transformer) trained with uniform weights, and a regularization parameter λ. It produces a domain weight vector α on the probability simplex via softmax-normalized KRLS scores. A key structural detail is the phase-dependent inversion: pretraining uses inverse KRLS (S_λ^{-1}) to upweight high-density domains, while fine-tuning uses KRLS (S_λ) directly to upweight unique domains. For fine-tuning, no separate proxy is needed—the pretrained model itself supplies embeddings, making the pipeline a single forward pass plus an O(k³) matrix inversion.

## Method

CHAMELEON is a data-centric framework that decouples domain weight computation from proxy model optimization. It extracts domain embeddings by averaging L-th layer hidden states of a uniformly-trained proxy model, constructs a domain affinity matrix Ω_D = XX^T using a linear kernel, and computes Kernel Ridge Leverage Scores S_λ(D_i) = [Ω_D(Ω_D + kλI)^{-1}]_{ii} to quantify each domain's representational uniqueness. The theoretical grounding links inverse KRLS to Christoffel functions (local data density in embedding space), justifying why high-density domains should be upweighted during pretraining for generalization, while high-KRLS (unique) domains should be upweighted during fine-tuning for specialization. Because the proxy's training process is unaltered, new domains can be scored by simply running inference through the existing proxy to obtain their embeddings and recomputing KRLS—requiring only ~1% of the FLOPs of retraining a proxy from scratch.

## Results

On SlimPajama with a 684M model, CHAMELEON achieves an average test perplexity of 22.31 and 39.6% average downstream accuracy across 13 benchmarks, matching DoGE (22.32 PPL, 39.4% acc) while requiring only 1.36×10¹⁷ FLOPs versus 6.68×10¹⁷ for DoGE and 1.34×10¹⁸ for DoReMi. At 1.2B scale, CHAMELEON's advantage widens: 15.03 average PPL (vs. DoGE 16.26) and 41.5% average downstream accuracy (vs. DoGE 40.4%). When transferring to the Pile dataset (17 domains) without retraining the proxy, CHAMELEON achieves 19.94 average PPL at only 4.62×10¹⁵ extra FLOPs (1% of retraining a new proxy), outperforming DoReMi (21.45) and DoGE (22.79). For finetuning, CHAMELEON improves perplexity on 7/7 domains on both Wiki40b (9.43→8.58 avg) and The Stack (22.02→17.18 avg) over uniform weights.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DoReMi, CHAMELEON improves average PPL from 23.34 to 22.31 and downstream accuracy from 38.4% to 39.6%, while reducing FLOPs by 10× (1.36×10¹⁷ vs. 1.34×10¹⁸). Against DoGE, CHAMELEON matches PPL (22.31 vs. 22.32) and slightly edges out downstream accuracy (39.6% vs. 39.4%) at 5× lower compute; at 1.2B scale the gap widens to 1.23 PPL and 1.1% accuracy in CHAMELEON's favor. Against RegMix (a task-aware method that knows the target downstream tasks), CHAMELEON achieves lower PPL (22.31 vs. 24.36) and higher downstream accuracy (39.6% vs. 39.3%) without any task-specific knowledge. On the Pile transfer experiment, CHAMELEON beats the Human baseline (19.94 vs. 23.05 avg PPL) and DoReMi (19.94 vs. 21.45) at only 1% of the retraining FLOPs required by DoReMi (4.62×10¹⁵ vs. 1.34×10¹⁸). Against Data Mixing Laws, CHAMELEON achieves 22.31 vs. 24.40 avg PPL and 39.6% vs. 39.0% downstream accuracy at 394× lower FLOPs.

## Relation to Prior Work

CHAMELEON departs from the dominant paradigm in domain reweighting (DoReMi, DoGE) where weights are derived from the proxy model's optimization trajectory (Group DRO losses or gradient tracking). Instead, it adopts a data-centric perspective, computing weights from the intrinsic geometry of domain embeddings via Kernel Ridge Leverage Scores, a tool previously used in kernel sampling and density estimation but not for LM data mixing. The method also extends the domain reweighting task beyond pretraining to fine-tuning, a stage that prior offline methods (DoReMi, DoGE, RegMix) do not address, and introduces the ability to incorporate new domains without retraining the proxy model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 10× reduction in data-mixing compute (1.36×10¹⁷ vs 1.34×10¹⁸ FLOPs for DoReMi) makes principled domain reweighting accessible to academic labs and smaller-scale training pipelines, and the no-retraining adaptation to new domains addresses a real practical pain point in dynamic LLM training. The phase-specific use of KRLS (inverse for pretraining density, direct for finetuning uniqueness) provides a clean theoretical rationale grounded in Christoffel functions. Open questions include whether the approach extends to online/dynamic reweighting during training, how it scales to hundreds of domains, and whether task-specific modifications to the KRLS identity matrix (mentioned as future work) can match task-aware methods like RegMix.
