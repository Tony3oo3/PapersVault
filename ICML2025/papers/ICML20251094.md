## Problem

Current MLLMs perform poorly on compositional condition questions in tabular understanding (e.g., 'What is the math score of the student with the highest total score?'), with even GPT-4o achieving only 47% accuracy. The paper identifies two root causes: the ViT patch division strategy severs row and column relationships causing positional misalignment, and the model systematically overlooks conditions specified in the question.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251094/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a table image at 384×384 resolution and a free-form text question as input, producing a free-form text answer. The table image is consumed by a SigLIP ViT encoder that generates three types of patch tokens (square, row, and column), while the question is embedded via the LLM's word embedding table. No structured table representation is required; the model operates end-to-end on the raw image.

## Method

COCOTAB augments the standard ViT square-patch division (P×P) with full-width row patches (P×W) and full-height column patches (H×P), each projected through separate MLPs to preserve intra-row and intra-column structural relationships that are otherwise severed by uniform patching. A cross-modal attention module then uses question embeddings as queries and visual tokens as keys/values to produce question-conditioned visual tokens, explicitly aligning each condition in the question with the relevant table region. Training proceeds in two stages: Stage 1 learns the three patch-to-text projections and modality alignment with frozen ViT and LLM weights, while Stage 2 performs instruction tuning on TQA data with frozen ViT but trainable projector and LLM. The backbone combines SigLIP-ViT (so400m-patch14-384) as the vision encoder with Qwen2-Instruct as the LLM, adding only ~7% extra tokens (54 row/column patches to the original 729) for minimal computational overhead.

## Results

COCOTAB achieves state-of-the-art performance among open-source MLLMs on all four MMTU sub-tasks: IE 0.68, RC 0.50, CC 0.43, and CR 0.38, substantially outperforming the strongest open-source baseline DocOwl (0.65/0.45/0.26/0.07). On external benchmarks, COCOTAB reaches 0.45 on WTQ, 0.74 on TabFact, and 0.34 on ComTQA, again leading all open-source competitors. The largest relative gains appear on compositional condition (CC) and calculation/reasoning (CR) tasks, where COCOTAB improves over LLaVA-7B by 3.6× and 6.3× respectively. Against closed-source models, COCOTAB approaches Qwen2-VL on CC (0.43 vs 0.38) and CR (0.38 vs 0.38) but still trails GPT-4o (0.61 CC, 0.85 CR).

## Baselines & Comparisons

COCOTAB outperforms all 10 open-source baselines across every benchmark and sub-task. The most dramatic wins are on CC and CR: COCOTAB scores 0.43 on CC versus 0.28 (Monkey/TextMonkey) and 0.26 (DocOwl), and 0.38 on CR versus 0.08 (LLaVA-13B) and 0.07 (DocOwl), representing 50–500% relative improvements. On IE and RC, gains are more modest (e.g., 0.68 vs 0.65 for DocOwl on IE; 0.50 vs 0.45 for DocOwl on RC), confirming that the row/column patches and conditional tokens primarily benefit structural reasoning rather than simple cell reading. Against structured table-parsing methods (TAPEX, TaPas), COCOTAB is competitive on IE/RC but significantly superior on CC (0.43 vs 0.33/0.04) and CR (0.38 vs 0.19/0.19), demonstrating that end-to-end image processing with structural patches preserves spatial relationships lost in text conversion. Compared to closed-source GPT-4o, COCOTAB still lags substantially (0.43 vs 0.61 on CC; 0.38 vs 0.85 on CR), though it matches or exceeds Qwen2-VL on CC and CR, suggesting the architectural improvements close part of the open-to-closed gap.

## Relation to Prior Work

COCOTAB builds directly on the LLaVA architecture (ViT vision encoder + MLP projector + LLM) but departs from its uniform patch-based image processing by augmenting the visual encoder with dedicated row and column patches that preserve intra-row and intra-column structural integrity. It also introduces a cross-modal attention mechanism (conditional tokens) between question embeddings and visual tokens, a design more closely related to Q-Former-style alignment in BLIP-2/InstructBLIP than to LLaVA's simple concatenation. The paper positions itself against document-specialized MLLMs (DocOwl, Monkey, TextMonkey, Donut) and general-purpose models (GPT-4o, Qwen-VL), arguing that the ViT patch division strategy is the root cause of misalignment errors in tabular understanding rather than the LLM's reasoning capacity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The diagnostic finding that the vision encoder's patch division—not the LLM's reasoning—is the primary bottleneck for compositional condition tasks in tabular understanding is a useful reframing for the field, and the MMTU benchmark (8,921 QA pairs across 4 capability categories and 10+ domains) fills a gap in systematic evaluation. However, COCOTAB still trails closed-source models (GPT-4o, Qwen2-VL) by a wide margin on CC and CR tasks, and the paper explicitly acknowledges that the CR (calculation/reasoning) dimension remains largely unaddressed. The 7% token overhead for row/column patches makes the technique practical, but the overall contribution is a targeted architectural fix rather than a paradigm shift, and the persistent open-source/closed-source gap suggests that scale and data volume remain dominant factors.
