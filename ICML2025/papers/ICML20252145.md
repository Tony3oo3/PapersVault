## Problem

Existing remote sensing LMMs lack pixel-level grounding, relying on coarse bounding-box coordinates that cannot capture fine-grained spatial semantics, and they operate at low resolutions (224–504 px) that fail to resolve small objects and intricate patterns in high-resolution aerial imagery. Additionally, no granular, RS-domain-specific grounded conversation dataset exists to train models for interleaved mask-and-text generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252145/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

GeoPixel accepts high-resolution RS images at any aspect ratio up to 4K, which are dynamically partitioned into a grid of local patches (up to 9 patches of 560×560) plus a single global 560×560 view, alongside a text prompt or referring expression. The model outputs a natural-language description in which specific noun phrases are interleaved with [SEG] tokens; each [SEG] token's embedding is decoded into a binary segmentation mask (instance-level for large objects, semantic-level for clusters or small objects), and bounding boxes can be post-processed from these masks for detection evaluation.

## Method

GeoPixel combines five modules: an adaptive image divider that up-scales and splits the input into k₁×k₂ non-overlapping patches plus a global view; a frozen CLIP ViT-L/14 vision encoder (base resolution 560) that produces patch and global features concatenated with a separator token; an InternLM2-7B LLM with Partial LoRA applied selectively to visual token projections for efficient cross-modal alignment; a frozen SAM-2 grounding vision encoder (Hiera-based MAE); and a lightweight trainable pixel decoder that fuses SAM-2 image features with the projected [SEG] token embedding (via a two-layer MLP text projector) to generate segmentation masks. The [SEG] token is added to the LLM vocabulary so the model learns when and where to emit it within the text stream, enabling multi-target grounding in a single forward pass. Training uses LoRA (α=8) fine-tuning on the newly curated GeoPixelD dataset (53,816 grounded phrases, 600,817 masks), which is built via a semi-automated pipeline combining set-of-marks prompting with spatial and categorical priors, hierarchical 3×3/2×2/1×2 grid-based localization, and LLM paraphrasing with strict phrase-preservation constraints.

## Results

On the novel RS-GCG task (GeoPixelD benchmark), GeoPixel achieves 21.6 CIDEr, 24.0 METEOR, 77.50 CLAIR, 19.0 AP50, 52.3 mIoU, and 38.8 Recall overall, outperforming the strongest baseline GLaMM-FT (15.7 CIDEr, 46.4 mIoU, 32.8 Recall) by 6.5 CIDEr and 5.9 mIoU. On RRSIS-D referring segmentation, GeoPixel-FT reaches 83.33 P@0.5 and 67.30 mIoU on the test set, surpassing the previous best RMSIN (74.26 P@0.5, 64.20 mIoU) by 9.07 and 3.10 points respectively. On VRSBench referring expression detection, GeoPixel-FT achieves 67.70 Acc@0.5 and 40.83 Acc@0.7 overall, nearly doubling the performance of GeoChat (49.8 Acc@0.5, 19.9 Acc@0.7) and GeoPix (49.8, 20.0).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the primary RS-GCG task, GeoPixel decisively beats all four pixel-grounding baselines: it surpasses GLaMM-FT (the strongest competitor) by 6.5 CIDEr, 5.9 mIoU, and 6.0 Recall, while LISA† and PixelLM† trail further behind at 42.7 and 42.4 mIoU respectively; zero-shot GLaMM is essentially non-functional (0.1 CIDEr, 7.1 Recall). On RRSIS-D referring segmentation, GeoPixel-FT outperforms all nine specialist baselines, with the largest gap over RMSIN (previous SOTA) at +9.07 P@0.5 and +3.10 mIoU on the test set, and a more modest +5.73 P@0.5 over LAVT. On VRSBench detection, GeoPixel-FT achieves 67.70 Acc@0.5 overall, substantially exceeding GeoChat (49.8) and GeoPix (49.8) by ~18 points, while MiniGPT-v2, LLaVA-1.5, and Mini-Gemini all fall below 42 Acc@0.5. The OBB-based comparison (Table 5) further confirms GeoPixel-FT's advantage over GeoChat (58.00 vs 24.3 Acc@0.5 overall), underscoring that pixel-level grounding translates into superior localization even when evaluated with coarser box metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

GeoPixel builds directly on the IXC-2.5 architecture for its adaptive image partitioning strategy and base LLM (InternLM2 7B), adopts SAM-2's Hiera encoder as a frozen grounding vision encoder, and leverages Partial LoRA from InternLM-XComposer2 for efficient cross-modal alignment. It departs from existing RS-LMMs (GeoChat, SkyEyeGPT, EarthGPT) that rely on bounding-box coordinates for grounding, and from general-domain pixel-grounding models (LISA, PixelLM, GLaMM) that operate at low resolution and lack RS-specific spatial reasoning. The key conceptual departure is combining 4K-capable adaptive partitioning with a [SEG]-token-to-SAM-2 decoder pipeline specifically tailored to the top-down perspective, scale variation, and dense object layouts of remote sensing imagery.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GeoPixel is the first end-to-end RS-LMM with pixel-level grounding, and the accompanying GeoPixelD dataset (53,816 grounded phrases, 600,817 masks) and 5,427-expression benchmark fill a critical gap in RS-specific grounded conversation data. The large OBB accuracy gap over GeoChat (58.0 vs 24.3 Acc@0.5) underscores that pixel-level alignment substantially outperforms box-based grounding in RS, a finding that may reshape how the community approaches spatial localization. Open questions include handling multimodal RS inputs (SAR, infrared), resolving the counterintuitive finding that mixed instance/semantic annotations degrade recall, and extending to dynamic or video RS data.
