## Problem

Conventional regression losses (MSE, MAE) minimize pointwise prediction errors but are blind to pairwise relationships among samples, failing to preserve rank structure. Existing pairwise methods (RankSim, RNC, ConR) address this by operating in latent feature space, incurring quadratic computational cost and lacking theoretical justification for why pairwise similarity in feature space should improve regression. The paper asks whether pairwise label differences can be captured directly in label space with linear complexity and a clear theoretical link to gradient learning.

## Input / Output

The method accepts tabular feature vectors (8–966 dimensions across six benchmark datasets) or 224×224 face images (AgeDB) and produces a single continuous regression prediction per sample (or 15 predictions for the multi-target IC50 task). The pairwise losses are computed within each mini-batch over the batch of predictions and their corresponding ground-truth labels, requiring no additional data beyond the standard (x, y) pairs. The approach is model-agnostic, applied atop FFNNs of varying depth for tabular data and ResNet18 for image regression.

## Method

GAR combines three losses: a conventional MAE loss for pointwise fitting, a pairwise difference loss L_diff^MSE proven equivalent to the variance of prediction errors (enforcing consistent error magnitudes), and a normalized pairwise difference loss L_diffnorm^{p=2} proven equivalent to 1 minus the Pearson correlation coefficient between predictions and ground truths (enforcing rank preservation). Both pairwise losses are defined over all N² pairs in a batch but reduce to O(N) computations via algebraic identities, matching the cost of standard regression losses. The three components are aggregated via a Distributionally Robust Optimization formulation with KL divergence, yielding a single hyperparameter α that smoothly interpolates between geometric mean (α→∞), arithmetic mean (α=1), and max (α→0) of the sub-losses. Theorem 4 establishes that matching pairwise prediction differences to pairwise label differences is equivalent to matching all-order gradients of the ground truth function, providing the theoretical justification for the 'gradient aligned' name.

## Results

GAR achieves the best Pearson and Spearman correlation coefficients across all eight real-world regression tasks, confirming that the pairwise label-difference losses effectively capture rank-preserving structure. On AgeDB (Scratch), GAR sets a new SOTA with 6.286 MAE and 0.846 R² (previous SOTA: 6.40 MAE, 0.830 R²); on AgeDB (RNC Linear Probe), GAR achieves 6.069 GAE and 0.854 R² (previous SOTA: 6.14 GAE, 0.850 R²). On the challenging IC50 drug-sensitivity task, GAR reaches 0.302 Pearson correlation versus 0.202 for the best competitor (ConR), a 50% relative improvement. Running-time experiments confirm GAR is as fast as conventional MAE/MSE losses (linear complexity), while latent-space pairwise methods (RankSim, RNC, ConR) are 3–10× slower in loss computation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GAR outperforms all five baseline families on Pearson and Spearman correlation across every dataset, which is the paper's central claim about rank preservation. Against RankSim (Gong et al., 2022), GAR wins decisively on every metric—RankSim's Pearson drops to 0.125 on Parkinson and 0.673 on Concrete, while GAR maintains 0.92+—and RankSim is also 4–10× slower in loss computation. Against RNC (Zha et al., 2023), GAR matches or beats RNC on MAE/RMSE (e.g., 6.257 vs 6.238 MAE on Super Conductivity) while achieving higher Pearson (0.949 vs 0.947) and running at comparable speed; RNC additionally requires a two-stage pre-training + fine-tuning pipeline. Against ConR (Keramati et al., 2023), GAR achieves a 50% relative Pearson gain on IC50 (0.302 vs 0.202) and wins on all other datasets' correlation metrics, while ConR's latent-space contrastive loss is ~5× slower in loss time. Against Huber and Focal losses, GAR is competitive on MAE (within 1–3%) but consistently superior on rank-based metrics (Pearson/Spearman), demonstrating that the pairwise losses capture structure invisible to pointwise robust losses.

## Relation to Prior Work

GAR departs from recent pairwise regression methods (RankSim, RNC, ConR) that impose label-similarity constraints in the latent feature space with quadratic batch complexity, by instead operating directly in label space on pairwise prediction differences. This shift enables two key equivalences: the pairwise MSE loss reduces to the variance of prediction errors, and the normalized pairwise loss reduces to 1 minus the Pearson correlation coefficient, both computable in linear time. For aggregating the three component losses (conventional MAE, pairwise MSE, pairwise normalized), the paper adapts a DRO formulation from Zhu et al. (ICML 2023) that trades off between geometric mean, arithmetic mean, and max via a single hyperparameter α, avoiding the numerical instabilities of naive geometric-mean aggregation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical result (Theorem 4) that matching all pairwise label differences is equivalent to matching all-order gradients of the ground truth function provides a principled justification for why pairwise losses improve rank preservation — a connection prior latent-space methods lacked. Practically, achieving pairwise-like benefits at the same O(N) cost as standard MAE/MSE removes the main adoption barrier of prior pairwise methods. However, the scope is explicitly limited to clean data without noise, outliers, or distributional shifts, and the empirical gains in MAE/RMSE are modest (often <5%) while Pearson/Spearman improvements are more consistent; robustness under realistic noisy settings remains an open question that could limit real-world adoption.
