## Problem

Sharpness-Aware Minimization (SAM) seeks flat minima by optimizing the worst-case loss over a parameter neighborhood, but its min-max formulation is computationally difficult in highly non-convex landscapes and ignores potentially many other sharp regions near the solution. This paper asks whether a smoothed, distributional reweighting of all neighborhood losses—rather than focusing on a single worst-case perturbation—can yield flatter minima that are both easier to optimize and better at generalizing.

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB images (32×32 CIFAR-100, 64×64 Tiny-ImageNet, DTD textures) or text sequences (GLUE tasks) and produces class labels or regression scores as usual. The unusual aspect is in training: each optimization step requires s=3 or 5 sampled parameter perturbations ε drawn from a tilted distribution e^{tL(θ+ε)} constrained to an L2 ball of radius ρ, making each iteration more expensive than a single SAM gradient-ascent step but cheaper than exhaustive neighborhood search.

## Method

TSAM replaces SAM's max operator with a LogSumExp (exponential tilting) operator parameterized by t≥0, yielding the objective (1/t)log E_ε[e^{tL(θ+ε)}]. The resulting gradient is a tilted weighted average of ∇L(θ+ε) where weights are proportional to e^{tL(θ+ε)}, so high-loss perturbations receive exponentially more influence. As t→∞ the objective recovers SAM; as t→0 it reduces to average-perturbed loss, giving a smooth interpolation with bounded smoothness O(t) that is easier to optimize than SAM's unbounded smoothness. To estimate the tilted gradient efficiently, the authors adapt a one-step Euler discretization of Hamiltonian dynamics (Algorithm 3): each perturbation ε is generated by a single gradient-ascent step along ∇L(θ+ε) with a random initialization, and s such perturbations are aggregated with tilted weights e^{(t−δ)L(θ+ε_j)}. Theoretical results show TSAM provably favors flatter solutions as t increases (measured by t-weighted variance of neighborhood losses) and admits a generalization bound tighter than ERM's for moderate t.

## Results

TSAM achieves the best test accuracy across all image classification benchmarks: 77.78% on CIFAR-100 (ResNet-18), 80.85% on CIFAR-100 (WideResNet-16-8), 68.82% on DTD (ViT), and 73.55% on Tiny-ImageNet (ResNet-18), consistently outperforming SAM by 1.0–2.4 points. On the noisy CIFAR-100 setting, TSAM reaches 69.98% (ResNet-18) and 70.26% (WideResNet), a 1.0–2.2 point gain over SAM. On the GLUE text benchmark, TSAM achieves the best or tied-best score on 7 of 8 tasks (e.g., SST-2: 91.86, QQP: 88.77, QNLI: 87.81), with the only loss being a 0.24-point margin on MRPC (85.05 vs. 85.29 for SAM).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TSAM outperforms vanilla SAM (Foret et al., 2020) on every image dataset: +1.26 pts on CIFAR-100 ResNet-18 (77.78 vs. 76.52), +2.41 pts on CIFAR-100 WideResNet (80.85 vs. 78.44), +0.95 pts on DTD ViT (68.82 vs. 67.87), and +0.98 pts on Noisy CIFAR-100 ResNet-18 (69.98 vs. 69.00). Against PGN (Zhao et al., 2022), TSAM wins on all image tasks with margins of 0.33–6.28 pts, with the largest gap on Noisy CIFAR-100 WideResNet (70.26 vs. 64.02). Against RSAM (Liu et al., 2022b), TSAM leads on all image benchmarks by 0.21–4.33 pts (e.g., 77.78 vs. 77.35 on CIFAR-100 ResNet-18; 70.26 vs. 65.93 on Noisy CIFAR-100 WideResNet). On GLUE, TSAM ties or beats SAM on 7 of 8 tasks, with the sole exception being MRPC where SAM edges out TSAM by 0.24 pts (85.29 vs. 85.05); TSAM also outperforms PGN and RSAM on most GLUE subtasks. The gains are most pronounced under distribution shift (noisy labels, OOD DTD), consistent with the flatness-based generalization theory.

## Relation to Prior Work

TSAM is a direct smoothed generalization of SAM's min-max objective, replacing the hard max over perturbations with a LogSumExp (exponential tilting) operator that reweights all neighborhood parameters by their loss values. The smoothing technique itself is classical (Kort & Bertsekas, 1972), and the tilted-risk framework has been applied to data-space reweighting for fairness and robustness (Li et al., 2023; Robey et al., 2022), but this paper is the first to apply exponential tilting to parameter-space perturbations for sharpness-aware optimization. For solving the resulting tilted integral, the authors adapt a single-step Euler discretization of Hamiltonian dynamics, reducing the sampling to a normalized gradient step that requires only 3–5 perturbation evaluations per update.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TSAM offers a principled middle ground between ERM (t=0) and SAM (t→∞) with provably bounded smoothness, making it easier to optimize while still explicitly favoring flatter minima as t increases. The consistent 0.5–1% test accuracy gains across CNNs, ViTs, and BERT on both in-distribution and distribution-shift settings suggest the tilted reweighting captures information that the single worst-case perturbation in SAM misses. Open questions remain: the rigorous link between flatness and generalization is still unresolved, the optimal t must be tuned per task, and the single-step HMC approximation (N=1) lacks a consistency guarantee for the tilted integral, leaving room for more principled samplers at scale.
