## Problem

Multi-preference optimization enriches LLM alignment by contrasting sets of responses rather than single pairs, but on-policy self-play generation produces dozens of candidates per query, making it computationally infeasible to include all in the training objective. Naive approaches either overemphasize frequently generated mediocre responses or suffer from memory blow-ups due to near-duplicate candidates. The paper addresses how to actively select a small, informative subset that covers reward extremes and distinct semantic regions for effective preference training.

## Input / Output

Given a text prompt, the method samples N candidate responses (e.g., 32) from the current policy at a fixed temperature, scores each with a reward model (e.g., Skywork-Reward-LM), and embeds them into a d-dimensional semantic space. From this pool, it selects one positive (highest-reward response) and K negatives (K << N) via an active selection subroutine, then trains the policy using a group-contrastive REFA loss over this 1-vs-K set. The output is an updated policy parameter vector θ and the curated response subset, which is iterated on-policy: the refined policy generates a fresh batch for the next round of selection.

## Method

AMPO unifies on-policy generation, a reference-free group-contrastive objective (REFA), and active subset selection into a single pipeline. The key mechanism is a 1-vs-K scheme: the single highest-reward response is the positive, while K negatives are chosen to balance reward extremity, generation probability, and embedding-space diversity. Three selection strategies are proposed: BottomK (lowest-rated), Coreset (k-means clustering with one worst-rated response per cluster), and Opt-Select (solving a weighted K-medoids MIP or local-search approximation that minimizes a Lipschitz-weighted coverage cost). Under a mild L-Lipschitz constraint on the policy, the paper proves that minimizing this coverage cost is equivalent to maximizing expected reward, yielding a 5-approximation guarantee for the local-search variant and an additive L·d_max bound for the coreset variant.

## Results

On Llama-3-Instruct (8B), AMPO-Coreset achieves the best AlpacaEval 2 length-controlled win rate of 52.4% and raw win rate of 52.1%, while AMPO-Opt-Select reaches 51.6% LC and 51.2% WR. On Arena-Hard, AMPO-Coreset attains 47.8% win rate (vs. 34.9% for SIMPO), and on MT-Bench the AMPO variants score 8.0–8.1 (GPT-4 judge). On Mistral-Instruct (7B), AMPO-Opt-Select leads with 33.1% LC and 37.8% WR on AlpacaEval 2, and AMPO-Coreset achieves the best Arena-Hard score of 22.6%. All three AMPO variants consistently outperform the strongest baseline (SIMPO) by 3–13 points across benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AMPO outperforms all nine baselines across both model sizes and all four evaluation metrics. Against the strongest baseline SIMPO, AMPO-Coreset gains +4.8 LC points (52.4 vs. 47.6) and +7.4 WR points (52.1 vs. 44.7) on Llama-3-8B AlpacaEval 2, and +12.9 Arena-Hard points (47.8 vs. 34.9). On Mistral-7B, AMPO-Opt-Select surpasses SIMPO by +3.0 LC (33.1 vs. 30.1) and +5.5 WR (37.8 vs. 32.3). Among the DPO-family baselines, R-DPO is the closest competitor on Llama-3-8B (41.1 LC, 33.1 Arena-Hard), but AMPO-Coreset still leads by 11.3 LC and 14.7 Arena-Hard points. On MT-Bench, AMPO variants (8.0–8.1) match or slightly exceed the best baselines (IPO at 8.3, KTO at 8.2), indicating that the gains on AlpacaEval and Arena-Hard do not come at the cost of general conversational quality. The consistent advantage across all three AMPO variants (BottomK, Coreset, Opt-Select) over every baseline confirms that the active selection mechanism, rather than the specific selection strategy, drives the improvement.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on REFA (Gupta et al., 2024a), a reference-free group-contrastive objective for multi-preference optimization, by addressing the practical bottleneck that on-policy generation produces far more candidate responses than can be feasibly included in a single training step. It departs from prior multi-preference methods (InfoNCA, MPO) that either use all responses or rely on simple reward-threshold filtering, instead importing active learning and coreset construction principles (k-means, weighted k-medoids, facility location) to select a small, coverage-maximizing subset. The theoretical framing connects the selection problem to a Lipschitz-constrained expected reward maximization, linking it to classical combinatorial optimization (MIP, min-knapsack) in a way not previously explored in the LLM alignment literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AMPO addresses a genuine scaling bottleneck in on-policy multi-preference alignment: as models generate dozens of candidates per query, naive inclusion becomes infeasible, yet random or reward-only selection wastes the diversity signal. The theoretical guarantee that coverage-based negative selection is optimal under a Lipschitz constraint provides a principled justification beyond heuristic clustering, and the empirical SOTA on AlpacaEval (52.4 LC vs. SIMPO's 47.6) with Llama-3-8B validates the approach. Open questions include whether the Lipschitz assumption holds for real LLM policies, how the method scales to much larger candidate pools (N >> 32), and whether the coreset/MIP selection generalizes to settings where the reward model is noisy or misaligned with task-specific evaluation criteria.
