## Problem

LLMs struggle to satisfy precise biophysical constraints (e.g., protein stability, motif placement) in black-box sequence optimization, yet existing synthetic benchmarks are either trivially solvable or already compromised by train-test leakage into pretrained weights. This makes it impossible to rigorously compare LLM-based optimizers against specialized solvers like LaMBO-2 under realistic query budgets. The paper addresses both gaps by introducing Ehrlich functions (procedurally generated, provably solvable test functions with adjustable difficulty) and a bilevel optimization framework that teaches LLMs to solve them from online feedback.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a seed polypeptide sequence (a discrete token string of length L over a vocabulary of size |V|), a black-box scoring function f that returns a scalar reward (or −∞ for infeasible sequences), and a cumulative set of oracle-labeled (sequence, score) pairs collected over iterations. The LLM policy π_θ(y|x) takes a prompt sequence x and generates refined candidate sequences y, which are ranked by policy likelihood and filtered to j=2000 candidates per round for oracle evaluation. The final output is the argmax sequence over all evaluated candidates, with performance measured by minimum simple regret (1 − f(x)) across optimization rounds.

## Method

LLOME alternates between an outer loop that fine-tunes the LLM policy on oracle-labeled data and an inner loop that generates and selects new candidates via iterative refinement without oracle access. The key training objective is the MargE loss, derived from a Forward-Reverse KL (FReKL) formulation: D_KL(π_θ ∥ π*) + λ·D_KL(π_ref ∥ π_θ), where π* is the Boltzmann-optimal policy and the reverse KL regularizer maintains exploratory coverage to prevent mode collapse. The reward function is a margin reward r_margin(x,y) = max(f(y) − f(x), 0), whose zero-clipping is critical to ensure prompt-dependent local refinement rather than prompt-independent global ranking. The FReKL objective satisfies the Strong Interpolation Criteria (SIC), smoothly interpolating between the optimal and reference policies, which empirically prevents the premature collapse observed with DPO and the early plateau observed with REINFORCE. A dataset matching augmentation (from PropEn) pairs each sequence with nearby known improvements to enrich the training signal.

## Results

On medium-difficulty Ehrlich functions, LLOME-MARGE achieves the lowest minimum simple regret on f₂ (Ehr(32,32)-4-4-4), while LaMBO-2 achieves the lowest on f₃ (Ehr(32,128)-4-4-4). On the easy (f₁) and difficult (f₄) variants, all methods (LLOME-MARGE, LLOME-SFT, LLOME-DPO, LaMBO-2, GA) perform comparably, either converging quickly or making minimal progress. LLOME-DPO consistently underperforms, failing to improve diversity or feasibility of outputs and sometimes collapsing prematurely. The multi-stage pipeline LLOME-SFT₁-MARGE₉ is the sole configuration to reach the optimal solution (regret = 0) on f₂ within 10 training rounds.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is against LaMBO-2 (Gruver et al., 2024), a 314K-parameter discrete diffusion solver purpose-built for constrained protein sequence design. On f₂, LLOME-MARGE (using Pythia 2.8B, ~2.8B parameters) achieves lower minimum regret than LaMBO-2, while on f₃ LaMBO-2 edges out LLOME-MARGE. On the easy (f₁) and difficult (f₄) tasks, both methods perform comparably. LaMBO-2 produces more diverse and feasible candidate sequences (Fig. 4) but requires custom hyperparameter tuning per test function, whereas LLOME-MARGE transfers a single configuration across all four functions without retuning. The paper notes that LaMBO-2's 314K parameters represent ~0.01% of the LLM's parameter count, making it far more computationally efficient for easy problems. Among LLOME variants, LLOME-MARGE and LLOME-SFT significantly outperform LLOME-DPO in both regret and reward, with DPO exhibiting mode collapse and likelihood-reward miscalibration.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the bilevel optimization paradigm for LLMs introduced by Ma et al. (2024), but departs critically by operating in a purely black-box setting without gradient access to the objective. The training objective (MargE/FReKL loss) is derived from the generalized variational Bayes framework (Knoblauch et al., 2019) and the maximum-entropy/Boltzmann policy principle, positioning it as a principled alternative to DPO (Rafailov et al., 2023) and RLHF-PPO that satisfies the Strong Interpolation Criteria. The dataset matching strategy is borrowed from PropEn (Tagasovska et al., 2024), and the overall problem setup is motivated by the specialized discrete-diffusion solver LaMBO-2 (Gruver et al., 2024), which the paper uses as its primary specialized baseline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Ehrlich functions provide a much-needed accessible, non-leaked benchmark for discrete sequence BBO that captures non-additive mutational effects, filling a gap between trivial additive models and expensive wet-lab validation. The finding that DPO-trained LLMs suffer mode collapse and infeasibility drift in constrained optimization settings is a practical warning for the growing community applying preference learning to structured generation. However, all results are on synthetic functions with a single 2.8B model (Pythia), so whether LLOME-MargE's competitive performance transfers to real biophysical objectives or scales with model size remains open. The paper also leaves unresolved whether the SIC requirement is truly necessary or merely sufficient for good optimization behavior.
