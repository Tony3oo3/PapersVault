## Problem

QAOA has been applied to correlation clustering, but existing multi-level formulations require qudits (multi-level quantum systems) rather than simple qubits, limiting hardware compatibility and expressiveness. Additionally, the standard expectation-value-based cost computation is sensitive to the 'unreliable tail' of high-cost states that inflate the objective. This paper addresses both limitations by introducing a sub-problem decomposition and a nucleus-sampling-based cost evaluation.

## Input / Output

The method takes an undirected graph G=(V,E) with n nodes and binary edge costs c∈{+1,−1}^E, along with a nucleus sampling threshold t∈(0,1] that controls the fraction of probability mass retained when computing the expected cost. It produces a node-to-cluster partition and the corresponding expected cost. Notably, only n qubits (one per node) are required and are reused across all sub-problems via intermediate measurements, in contrast to the multi-level QAOA formulation which assigns a qudit to each node.

## Method

SQAOA generalizes QAOA by (1) replacing the full expectation value with a nucleus-sampled cost that truncates the distribution to the smallest set of states whose cumulative probability exceeds threshold t, and (2) decomposing correlation clustering into l=n−1 sequential max-cut sub-problems, each solved by a QAOA circuit of depth p on the remaining unassigned nodes. After each sub-problem, qubits are measured and the next sub-problem is evaluated only on the nucleus states from the previous step, enabling qubit reuse. The phase-separation operator includes both pairwise edge-cost terms and a cost-independent individual-node term with distinct weights w, which is essential for the theoretical universality proof. Via a Lie algebra argument, the paper shows that for p→∞ the ansatz can construct any basis state in each sub-problem, guaranteeing that optimal correlation clustering solutions are reachable with certainty.

## Results

On Erdős-Rényi graphs with n=3,4,5 nodes and ansatz depth p=1, SQAOA with nucleus sampling threshold t=0.1 achieves near-optimal approximation ratios of 1.00±0.00 (n=3), 1.00±0.02 (n=4), and 1.00±0.02 (n=5), matching the multi-level QAOA baseline at the same threshold. Critically, SQAOA with t=0.1 reduces runtime by an order of magnitude compared to QAOA with t=0.1 (e.g., 10±5 s vs. 109±32 s for n=5), while using only one qubit per node rather than qudits. Both methods substantially exceed the classical Swamy bound of 0.7666 even without nucleus sampling (t=1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is the multi-level QAOA formulation of Weggemans et al. (2022), which encodes each node as a qudit whose state indicates its cluster. With nucleus sampling (t=0.1), SQAOA matches QAOA's approximation ratios (both reach ~1.00) while reducing runtime by roughly 10× at n=5 (10 s vs. 109 s) and using only n qubits instead of n qudits. Without nucleus sampling (t=1), QAOA achieves slightly better approximation ratios (e.g., 0.92 vs. 0.85 at n=5), but SQAOA still outperforms the Swamy bound of 0.7666. The key advantage of SQAOA is its runtime scaling: with t=0.1, SQAOA's runtime grows much more slowly than QAOA's as n increases, since the nucleus size remains nearly constant while QAOA's cost function becomes increasingly discontinuous. SQAOA also requires only one qubit per node, making it more hardware-efficient than the qudit-based multi-level approach.

## Relation to Prior Work

SQAOA generalizes the Quantum Alternating Operator Ansatz (QAOA) by incorporating two ideas from outside the quantum optimization literature: nucleus sampling from LLM decoding (Holtzman et al., 2020) to compute the expected cost, and a sequential decomposition of correlation clustering into dependent max-cut sub-problems. This departs from the multi-level QAOA formulation of Weggemans et al. (2022), which encodes cluster assignments via qudits, by using only one qubit per node and solving the problem iteratively. Unlike prior QAOA sub-problem approaches (Tomesh et al., 2022; Esposito & Danzig, 2024) that split into independent instances of the same problem, SQAOA solves dependent sub-problems that differ from the original, with each sub-problem's solution conditioning the next.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a theoretical guarantee (via a Lie algebra universality argument) that SQAOA recovers optimal solutions with certainty as depth p→∞, and experimentally demonstrates better approximation ratios and runtimes than the multi-level QAOA baseline while using only n qubits. However, experiments are limited to simulations on graphs with at most 10 nodes, no lower bound on the approximation ratio is established, and the permutation-dependent γ₂ parameter must be relearned per instance. Open questions include whether the sub-problem decomposition generalizes to other multi-label combinatorial problems (e.g., Maximum k-Colorable Subgraph) and whether exploiting different operators per sub-problem would further improve performance.
