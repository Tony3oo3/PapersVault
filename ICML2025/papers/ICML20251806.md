## Problem

Automated hypothesis generation from text data—identifying natural language concepts that predict a target variable—is hindered by a fundamental tension: efficient statistical learning requires numerical representations (e.g., neurons), but interpreting those representations is difficult. Existing LLM-based approaches either cannot scale beyond a few examples due to context window limits or produce hard-to-interpret polysemantic neurons from fine-tuned models, leaving a gap between scalable feature learning and human-interpretable output.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a dataset of texts paired with a target variable (binary or continuous), using pre-trained OpenAI text-embedding-3-small vectors (1536-dim) as the numerical input to the SAE. It outputs H natural language hypotheses, each specifying a concept (e.g., 'mentions being surprised or shocked') with a direction of effect and statistical significance, evaluated via LLM annotation on a heldout set. The pipeline is fully decoupled: SAE training is unsupervised, feature selection is supervised via Lasso, and only the final interpretation step requires LLM calls, making compute scale with H rather than dataset size.

## Method

HYPOTHESAES trains a k-sparse autoencoder (with TopK masking and an auxiliary loss for dead neurons) on text embeddings to produce monosemantic, interpretable neurons, then selects the H most predictive neurons via L1-regularized regression with binary search on the penalty parameter. For each selected neuron, it prompts GPT-4o with 10 high-activating and 10 weakly-activating texts to generate candidate natural language concepts, selecting the highest-fidelity interpretation (measured by F1 between LLM concept annotations and binarized neuron activations). A key theoretical result—a triangle inequality (Proposition 3.1)—formally justifies this decoupling by showing that the separation score of an interpretation is bounded below by the neuron's separation score minus the interpretation's fidelity loss, meaning high-predictiveness neurons with high-fidelity labels yield predictive hypotheses. This architecture leverages the sparsity of natural language (only a few concepts are active per text) to produce features that are simultaneously interpretable and expressive, outperforming both classical topic models and fully LLM-driven baselines at 10–50× lower cost.

## Results

On three synthetic datasets (WIKI-5, WIKI-15, BILLS), HYPOTHESAES achieves the highest F1 similarity (0.58, 0.47, 0.67), surface similarity (0.70, 0.61, 0.78), and overall AUC (0.84, 0.84, 0.89) across all three metrics, beating all baselines on 8 of 9 dataset-metric pairs. On three real-world datasets, HYPOTHESAES produces 15/20 significant hypotheses on each task (45/60 total), compared to at most 12 for BERTOPIC, 11 for NLPARAM, and 12 for HYPOGENIC. Overall predictive performance (AUC or R²) is best for HYPOTHESAES on 8 of 9 comparisons, with the sole exception being HYPOGENIC's 2% higher R² on YELP (0.78 vs. 0.76). HYPOTHESAES is 10–50× cheaper and 30–50× faster than the LLM-based baselines NLPARAM and HYPOGENIC.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against BERTOPIC (neural topic model), HYPOTHESAES wins on F1, surface similarity, and AUC for all three synthetic tasks, and produces 3–15× more significant hypotheses on real-world tasks (e.g., 15 vs. 1 on HEADLINES); BERTOPIC's only edge is a slightly higher surface similarity on BILLS (0.88 vs. 0.78). Against NLPARAM (bottleneck + LLM labeling), HYPOTHESAES dominates across nearly all metrics: +0.36 mean F1 on synthetic tasks, and 2–7 more significant hypotheses per real-world dataset (e.g., 15 vs. 5 on HEADLINES, 15 vs. 8 on CONGRESS). Against HYPOGENIC (direct LLM prompting with bandit selection), HYPOTHESAES wins on all synthetic metrics and on significant-hypothesis count for HEADLINES (15 vs. 3) and CONGRESS (15 vs. 5), but HYPOGENIC edges out on YELP R² (0.78 vs. 0.76) and ties on YELP significant count (12 vs. 15). The paper notes that HYPOGENIC's top YELP hypotheses (e.g., 'expresses disappointment with the overall dining experience') are near-tautological restatements of the rating task, whereas HYPOTHESAES yields more specific, actionable insights (e.g., 'mentions food poisoning or symptoms of foodborne illness'). In terms of cost, HYPOTHESAES runs in ~18 minutes at $1.29 on CONGRESS, versus 905 minutes at $69.92 for NLPARAM and 575 minutes at $11.02 for HYPOGENIC.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes sparse autoencoders—previously developed for mechanistic interpretability of LLM internals (Cunningham et al., 2023; Bricken et al., 2023; Templeton et al., 2024)—by training them on pre-trained text embeddings rather than model activations, shifting the goal from steering LLM behavior to generating interpretable features for hypothesis generation. It departs from recent LLM-based hypothesis generation methods (Zhou et al., 2024; Zhong et al., 2024; Batista & Ross, 2024) by decoupling the pipeline into three stages: unsupervised feature generation (SAE), supervised feature selection (Lasso), and LLM-based interpretation, rather than relying on the LLM for both proposal and scoring. The theoretical triangle inequality (Proposition 3.1) provides a formal bridge between neuron predictiveness and hypothesis predictiveness, a connection not previously made explicit in the hypothesis generation literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method offers a practical, scalable path to automated hypothesis generation that is 10–50× cheaper than LLM-based alternatives while producing more significant and novel hypotheses, making it accessible for social science applications where compute budgets are limited. The clean decomposition of performance loss (embedding → SAE → selected neurons → interpreted hypotheses) gives practitioners a diagnostic tool for identifying where to invest improvements. Open questions include whether the approach extends to non-text modalities (images, proteins), whether larger SAEs or more data can close the remaining gap between SAE representations and raw embeddings (notable on HEADLINES), and whether the binary annotation step can be replaced with continuous representations to preserve more signal.
