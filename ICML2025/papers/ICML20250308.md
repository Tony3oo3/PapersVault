## Problem

Existing methods for steering LLMs between textual reasoning and code generation (e.g., OpenAI Code Interpreter, AutoGen) fail to effectively decide when symbolic computing is needed, leaving code-based capabilities underutilized. The optimal code/text choice depends on task type, complexity, and model capabilities in ways that are hard to judge a priori, and LLMs frequently produce text-like code that hardcodes answers rather than performing efficient symbolic computation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250308/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

CodeSteer operates as a multi-turn guidance loop (max 5 turns) where CodeSteerLLM receives the TaskLLM's current answer, feedback from a Self-answer Checker (code-based verification), a Symbolic Checker's AST-derived complexity score, and the full guidance/answer history. It outputs a structured guidance prompt—wrapped in <<>> for code-directed turns or <<<>>> for text-directed turns—or signals finalization of the answer. The guidance is not a direct answer but an instruction that shapes the TaskLLM's next generation, reducing the small model's burden to steering rather than solving.

## Method

CodeSteer fine-tunes Llama-3.1-8B as a steering model via two stages: (1) multi-turn SFT on 12k GPT-4o-synthesized guidance/generation trajectories with data augmentation that doubles the loss weight of the final two turns to counteract gradient cancellation in early turns, and (2) multi-turn DPO on 5.5k preference pairs sampled from a tree structure (branching factor 2–3) using a custom recursive scoring function that rewards correct answers in the fewest turns and penalizes incorrect ones, with a threshold of score difference >2 and a positive-score constraint to prevent reward hacking. Two auxiliary checkers—Self-answer Checker (re-queries TaskLLM to write verification code) and Symbolic Checker (rule-based AST analysis detecting loops, itertools, numeric operations, and string-based expression handling)—provide structured feedback that both improves CodeSteerLLM's guidance quality and increases dataset synthesis efficiency. The framework's key design insight is that iterative 'executing and exploring' with dynamic code/text switching outperforms single-step method selection, and that a small specialized model can effectively steer a much larger TaskLLM without needing to solve tasks itself.

## Results

On SymBench (37 symbolic tasks), augmenting GPT-4o with CodeSteer raises the Average Normalized Score from 53.3 (Only Question) to 86.4, outperforming OpenAI o1 (82.7), DeepSeek R1 (76.8), and o1-preview (74.8). On seen tasks, CodeSteer achieves 88.1 vs. o1's 83.8; on unseen tasks, 81.3 vs. o1's 79.4, indicating no overfitting. The best training-based baseline (Code/Text Choice) reaches only 77.9, and the best training-free baseline (Symbolic Agent) reaches 74.8. CodeSteer also generalizes to other LLMs, providing an average 41.8-point boost on Claude-3-5-Sonnet, Mistral-Large, and GPT-3.5.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CodeSteer (GPT-4o + CodeSteer, 86.4) outperforms all nine baselines and all three CoT LLMs. Against the strongest CoT LLM, o1 (82.7), CodeSteer leads by 3.7 points overall and by 4.3 points on seen tasks, while maintaining a 1.9-point advantage on unseen tasks. The best training-based baseline, Code/Text Choice (77.9), trails CodeSteer by 8.5 points, and the best training-free baseline, Symbolic Agent (74.8), trails by 11.6 points. OpenAI's Code Interpreter (70.5) and AutoGen (69.9) are both substantially outperformed. Notably, CodeSteer is particularly effective on tasks where symbolic computing is critical (e.g., Standard Sudoku: 100 vs. o1's 0; String Deletion: 93 vs. o1's 60), while on tasks where textual reasoning suffices (e.g., Web of Lies: 98 vs. o1's 100), it remains competitive. The generalizability test (Table 2) shows CodeSteer trained on GPT-4o data transfers to Claude-3-5-Sonnet (29.1→92.0), Mistral-Large (31.0→59.8), and GPT-3.5 (8.6→42.3), confirming the steering mechanism is model-agnostic.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the code/text steering problem identified by Chen et al. (2025), who showed that existing approaches (GPT-4o Code Interpreter, AutoGen) fail to effectively decide when to use code versus text. It builds on the DPO framework (Rafailov et al., 2024) but introduces a novel multi-turn tree-structured sampling scheme with a custom recursive scoring function to handle gradient cancellation across turns. The symbolic computing paradigm draws from PAL, Program of Thoughts, and Chain of Code, but the key departure is framing code generation as a *guided* process where a small fine-tuned model (Llama-3.1-8B) steers a larger TaskLLM through iterative multi-turn interaction rather than relying on the TaskLLM's own code/text decisions. The benchmark SymBench redevelops and extends tasks from Chen et al. (2025) and Gui et al. (2024) with publicly released generation code and adjustable complexity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a small 8B model can effectively guide a much larger model to outperform purpose-built reasoning models (o1, DeepSeek R1) on symbolic tasks, suggesting that structured code/text guidance is a more efficient path to symbolic reasoning than purely textual chain-of-thought exploration. The generalizability finding—CodeSteer trained for GPT-4o transfers to Claude, Mistral, and GPT-3.5 with a 41.8 average boost—implies the guidance mechanism captures task-agnostic principles rather than model-specific quirks. Key limitations remain: CodeSteer is not robust to varying task complexity (performance drops at medium difficulty), generated code can be suboptimal leading to timeouts, and the approach depends on the TaskLLM having sufficient base capability. Open questions include whether the multi-turn guidance paradigm scales to open-ended or multi-modal tasks, and whether the 8B guidance model can be further compressed without performance loss.
