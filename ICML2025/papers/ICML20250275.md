## Problem

Existing matrix completion methods that leverage side information (e.g., row/column space descriptors) assume the side information is perfect, meaning it fully spans the target matrix's row and column spaces. In practice, side information is often incomplete—user features in recommendation systems or label descriptors in multi-label learning only partially capture the underlying structure. Prior approaches for imperfect side information either handle small perturbations or decompose the matrix into components while ignoring the global low-rank property, leaving a gap in how to systematically exploit partially informative side information.

## Input / Output

The method takes a partially observed rank-r matrix R ∈ R^{m×n} (with observed entries at positions Ω) and two incomplete side information matrices A ∈ R^{m×r_A} and B ∈ R^{n×r_B} whose column spaces only partially span col(R) and row(R), respectively. The completeness level is quantified as (rank(A)+rank(B))/(2·rank(R)), and the method produces the full completed matrix X. The key structural assumption is that A and B are semi-orthogonal and their column spaces are disjoint from the unknown supplementary components, so the target decomposes into four orthogonal projection blocks.

## Method

The core insight is that when the target matrix is decomposed into four orthogonal projections (P_AB, P_AB⊥, P_A⊥B, P_A⊥B⊥), the first three are inherently low-rank because they contain known side-information factors, but the orthogonal complement projection P_A⊥B⊥(X) has no such guarantee and its maximum possible rank far exceeds the others. The OCMC model therefore adds a nuclear norm penalty on P_A⊥B⊥(X) to the standard nuclear norm objective: min_X ‖X‖_* + λ‖P_A⊥B⊥(X)‖_* subject to P_Ω(X)=P_Ω(R), where λ scales with side-information completeness. Using PAC learning theory and Rademacher complexity bounds, the paper proves the sample complexity is O(P² log N / ε²), where P decreases linearly with completeness, yielding a quadratic reduction in required observations. The convex problem is solved by a linearized ADMM algorithm that approximates quadratic terms with second-order Taylor expansions, yielding closed-form singular-value-shrinkage updates for both X and Y at each iteration with O(1/k) convergence rate.

## Results

On MovieLens-100k, OCMC achieves the lowest RMSE at every training proportion, ranging from 1.1503 (10% training) to 0.8625 (90% training), outperforming the next-best method (DirtyIMC) by 0.1548 and 0.0077 respectively. On the Yahoo.com 'Arts' multi-label dataset, OCMC attains the highest Average Precision at 4 out of 5 sampling rates (0.7206 at 30% up to 0.9596 at 90%), with FNNM as the closest competitor. Theoretically, the sample complexity scales as O(P² log N / ε²), where P decreases linearly with side-information completeness, yielding a quadratic reduction in required observations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OCMC dominates all five baselines on MovieLens-100k across every sampling rate, with the largest margin over SVT (which uses no side information) reaching 1.23 RMSE at 10% training. Maxide, which assumes perfect side information, degrades severely under incompleteness (RMSE stuck near 1.43–1.45 regardless of sampling rate), confirming the paper's motivation. DirtyIMC is the strongest competitor, leveraging a decomposition approach for noisy side information, but OCMC still leads by 0.008–0.155 RMSE. On Yahoo.com 'Arts', OCMC wins at 30%–90% sampling (AP 0.7206–0.9596) but trails FPC at the extreme 10% setting (0.5249 vs. 0.3402), suggesting FPC's fixed-point iteration has an edge under very sparse observations. FNNM (feature + nuclear norm) is the second-best method on both datasets but consistently lags OCMC by 0.03–0.05 AP on Yahoo and 0.03–0.07 RMSE on MovieLens.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends inductive matrix completion (IMC) from the perfect-side-information setting (Xu et al., 2013; Jain & Dhillon, 2013) to the incomplete case, where the available side information only partially spans the target matrix's row and column spaces. It departs from prior incomplete-side-information approaches (DirtyIMC, FNNM) that decompose the matrix into independent components and optimize each separately, instead imposing a low-rank constraint on the orthogonal complement projection while preserving the global nuclear norm structure. The PAC learning framework with Rademacher complexity analysis provides a distribution-free sample complexity guarantee, contrasting with the uniform-sampling assumptions in earlier convex relaxation analyses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The quadratic reduction in sample complexity with increasing side-information completeness offers a clean quantitative characterization of how much side information is 'worth' in matrix completion, which is practically relevant since real-world features (user demographics, item genres) are almost never perfectly informative. The linearized ADMM solver with O(1/k) convergence makes the approach computationally tractable. Limitations include the need to tune the completeness-dependent parameter λ (or P) based on knowledge of the target matrix's nuclear norm, the semi-orthogonality assumption on side information matrices, and the somewhat loose theoretical constants. The authors note future work on integrating the OCMC framework into neural network-based approaches, suggesting the current convex formulation may not scale to very large or non-stationary settings.
