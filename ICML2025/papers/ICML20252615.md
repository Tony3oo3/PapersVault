## Problem

Long-term dynamics forecasting in complex environments is hindered by noisy, irregularly sampled data and the infeasibility of obtaining complete governing equations from first principles. Existing physics-enhanced methods either assume fully known physics, rely on finite-difference derivatives (limiting them to noise-free regular data), or use NODE-based architectures whose heavy dependence on initial conditions causes error accumulation in long-horizon extrapolation. The paper asks how to seamlessly incorporate partially known physics into a model that can dynamically refine predictions from subsequent observations while generalizing to unseen temporal domains.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a noisy, irregularly sampled observation sequence [x(t_0), …, x(t_n)] at non-uniform time intervals (e.g., drone data at ~1010 Hz with per-sample rates ranging 573–1916 Hz; COVID-19 daily records with 10% missing entries) together with a control input sequence [u(t_0), …, u(t_{n+l})] extending l steps beyond the observation window. It produces two types of output: interpolated observations within the observed window and extrapolated trajectories for t ∈ (t_n, t_{n+l}], evaluated over horizons of 60–200 timesteps. Because the model retains continuous-time parameters and applies bilinear (Tustin) discretization per time step, it natively handles arbitrary sampling intervals without resampling.

## Method

Phy-SSM embeds partially known physics into a structured state space model (S5-based) via a three-step Phy-SSM unit: (1) multi-layer structured SSMs with HiPPO memory learn the unknown continuous dynamics matrices A_unk(t) and B_unk(t) from the latent state and control input; (2) a binary knowledge mask is applied via Hadamard product to hard-constrain which matrix entries are learnable, leaving known physics entries fixed; (3) the combined continuous matrix A = A_knw + A_unk is discretized per time step using the bilinear method to produce the next latent state. The overall framework is VAE-like: a sequential SSM encoder estimates the posterior over latent states, the Phy-SSM unit generates a physics-consistent prior, and a decoder maps latent states back to observations. A physics state regularization term (L_reg) penalizes the Euclidean distance between posterior and prior latent samples, guiding the encoder to produce states that respect system dynamics and improving extrapolation. State augmentation (e.g., appending sin(θ), cos(θ)) linearizes nonlinear dynamics so that partial physics can be encoded directly as fixed matrix entries, and a uniqueness theorem guarantees that the known/unknown decomposition is well-posed under the training objective.

## Results

On the drone state prediction task (high-frequency, irregularly sampled at ~1010 Hz), Phy-SSM achieves the best interpolation MAE of 1.002×10⁻¹ and extrapolation MAE of 2.733×10⁻¹, outperforming the next-best baselines (S5 at 1.059×10⁻¹ for interpolation; GOKU at 3.456×10⁻¹ for extrapolation). On COVID-19 forecasting with 10% missing records, Phy-SSM achieves an extrapolation MAE of 1.998×10⁻¹, a 2.6× improvement over the best baseline (S5 at 5.212×10⁻¹). For out-of-domain vehicle motion prediction (5–6 s horizon), Phy-SSM achieves ADE 6.206 and FDE 7.197, beating the best baseline (SDVAE at ADE 7.050) by ~12%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Phy-SSM outperforms all 11 baselines across every dataset and task. Among continuous-time models, S5 is the closest competitor on interpolation (MAE 1.059 vs. 1.002 on drone) but degrades catastrophically on extrapolation (MAE 8.426 vs. 2.733), confirming that purely data-driven SSMs lack physics-consistent inductive bias. ContiFormer achieves reasonable interpolation (MAE 1.446) but its NODE-based architecture accumulates error over long horizons (extrapolation MAE 4.059). Among physics-enhanced baselines, GOKU is the strongest on drone extrapolation (MAE 3.456) but still trails Phy-SSM by 21%, while PI-VAE and ODE2VAE remain competitive but consistently behind. SDVAE is the best baseline on out-of-domain vehicle prediction (ADE 7.050) yet Phy-SSM still leads by 12%. The data-driven vehicle-specific methods (Wayformer, AutoBot, G2LTraj) perform competitively in-domain (Wayformer ADE 1.899 vs. Phy-SSM 1.884) but collapse out-of-domain (Wayformer ADE 8.842 vs. Phy-SSM 6.206), underscoring the generalization advantage of embedding partial physics knowledge.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two active research threads: structured state space models (S4, S5, Mamba) for long-range sequential modeling, and partially-known physics-enhanced machine learning (GOKU, Phy-Taylor, SINDy Autoencoders). Prior partially-known physics methods relied on neural ODEs or finite-difference operators, which either depend heavily on initial conditions (NODEs) or require noise-free, regularly sampled data. Phy-SSM departs from both by using SSMs' HiPPO memory to capture long-range sequence correlations while a knowledge-mask mechanism enforces known physics as hard constraints on a decomposed state matrix, and a physics state regularization term aligns latent states with system dynamics—neither of which has a direct analogue in prior PEML or SSM literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work addresses a practically important gap: real-world dynamical systems (autonomous vehicles, drones, epidemiology) rarely admit fully known governing equations, and sensor data is typically noisy and irregularly sampled. The strong extrapolation gains (e.g., ~2.6× MAE reduction over S5 on COVID-19) suggest that SSM memory mechanisms are a natural complement to partial physics in ways that NODEs are not. Open questions include how to automate the knowledge-mask design (currently task-specific), whether the approach extends to stochastic or high-dimensional systems, and whether the bilinear discretization is optimal for all sampling regimes. The theoretical uniqueness guarantee, while clean, relies on assumptions (bounded approximation error, unique minimizer) that may not hold in practice.
