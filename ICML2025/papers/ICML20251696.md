## Problem

Existing density-based mode-seeking methods (Quick Shift, DPC, and variants) identify modes using only local characteristics such as density and distance to higher-density neighbors, requiring case-by-case threshold tuning or manual intervention. The paper shows that whether a point should be a mode depends on the global structure of the dataset — the same local configuration can yield different correct mode assignments depending on the broader data distribution — yet no prior method captures this global context automatically.

## Input / Output

TANGO takes a dataset of n points in R^d and a single hyperparameter k (neighborhood size, searched from 2 to 100) as input, and produces cluster labels as output. The number of target clusters must be specified for the final spectral clustering step. For image segmentation, each pixel is encoded as a 5-dimensional vector (2 spatial coordinates + 3 RGB channels), and the method clusters 154,401 such vectors per image. The algorithm is fully unsupervised in its mode-detection phase, requiring no ground-truth labels or manual threshold selection.

## Method

TANGO introduces 'typicality,' a global-view measure T_i = ρ_i + Σ_j B_ji T_j that quantifies a point's confidence as a mode by accumulating weighted contributions from all descendant points in the density-ascending dependency tree. The dependency matrix B uses rank-based weights (position of the leader in the similarity-sorted neighbor list) to avoid bias toward dense regions, and typicality is computed in O(n) time via forward substitution after sorting by density. Modes are identified by breaking the dependency from x_i to its leader x_j whenever T(x_i) ≥ T(x_j), eliminating the need for human-set thresholds. The resulting mode-centered sub-clusters are then aggregated using spectral clustering with Normalized Cut, where inter-sub-cluster similarity is an improved path-based measure (PBSim) that weights edges by point densities and is computed in O(nk log(nk)) via a variant of Kruskal's algorithm.

## Results

TANGO achieves the best ARI on all 16 real-world datasets, with particularly large margins on banknote (96.53% ARI vs. 95.39% for CPF), Leukemia (83.72% vs. 73.71% for LDP-SC), and MNIST-AE (83.40% vs. 73.80% for USPEC). On the more challenging datasets (ionosphereEW, ver2, waveform), TANGO still leads by 10–20 ARI points over the next-best method. Statistical significance is confirmed via Friedman's test (p = 2.9×10⁻¹⁶) and Nemenyi's post-hoc test, where TANGO's mean rank difference from every other algorithm exceeds the critical difference threshold of 2.30.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TANGO outperforms all 10 baselines on every dataset in at least one metric (ARI, NMI, or ACC), and leads in ARI across all 16 datasets. The closest competitor is USPEC (Huang et al., 2020), a scalable spectral clustering method, which ranks second on several datasets (e.g., wdbc 81.76 vs. 85.01 ARI; Leukemia 73.71 vs. 83.72) but falls 10–30 ARI points behind TANGO on high-dimensional or complex-structure data (semeion, ver2, MNIST-AE). Density-peak-based methods (LDP-SC, DCDP-ASC, LDP-MST, NDP-Kmeans, DEMOS) consistently underperform, with DCDP-ASC and LDP-MST often producing near-random or negative ARI scores (e.g., DCDP-ASC: 0.24 on wdbc, −5.82 on Leukemia), highlighting the insufficiency of purely local density features. Mode-seeking methods CPF (Tobin & Zhang, 2024) and QKSPP (Jiang et al., 2018) are competitive on some datasets (CPF reaches 95.39 ARI on banknote) but lack the global aggregation step that gives TANGO its edge. DPC-DBFN (Lotfi et al., 2020) and LDP-SC (Long et al., 2022) are the strongest density-peak baselines but still trail TANGO by 5–20 ARI points on most datasets, confirming that the typicality-driven mode detection and path-based graph-cut aggregation provide meaningful gains over local-only approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

TANGO builds directly on the density-ascending dependency framework established by Quick Shift and DPC, where each point links to its nearest higher-density neighbor, and on spectral clustering (Ng et al., 2001) for final partitioning. The key departure is the introduction of 'typicality'—a recursive, global-view measure analogous to PageRank centrality that quantifies a point's confidence as a mode by aggregating weighted contributions from all descendant points in the dependency tree. This replaces the ad-hoc threshold-based mode-breaking used in Quick Shift, DPC, and their variants (QKSPP, CPF, VDPC) with a parameter-free comparison between a point's typicality and its leader's, eliminating the need for case-by-case human intervention. The improved path-based similarity for sub-cluster aggregation extends prior connectivity-kernel ideas (Fischer et al., 2003; Chang & Yeung, 2008) by incorporating density-weighted edge costs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The elimination of threshold parameters for mode detection addresses a persistent practical pain point in density-based clustering, making the method more deployable across diverse datasets without expert tuning. The theoretical guarantees (unique typicality solution, O(n) computation, power-law tail behavior) provide a solid foundation, and the consistent outperformance over 10 baselines across 16 datasets with statistical significance testing strengthens the empirical case. However, the acknowledged limitation—degraded performance when low-density points surround high-density points with extreme density discrepancy—suggests the hierarchical dependency assumption may not capture all data geometries. Open questions include automatic selection of the neighborhood size k and whether alternative dependency structures could handle the identified failure mode.
