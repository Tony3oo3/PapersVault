## Problem

Existing Time Series Foundation Models (TSFMs) achieve generalization through massive parameter counts (67M–700M) and fixed-length tokenization, which cannot consistently represent intrinsic periodic patterns across datasets with different sampling rates and cycle lengths. This scale-dependent tokenization disrupts periodic structure, forcing models to compensate with more parameters and increasing computational cost, while limiting zero-shot transfer to unseen scales.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252547/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a multivariate look-back window X_t ∈ R^{C×L} (C channels, L timesteps) along with the cycle length P (number of data points per intrinsic period), which is either known from the sampling rate or estimated via FFT. It outputs parallel predictions for forecast horizons F ∈ {96, 192, 336, 720} in a channel-independent manner. Unlike fixed-patch models, the effective patch size equals P, so the number of tokens N = ⌊L/P⌋ adapts to each dataset's scale, and the decoder produces K = ⌈F/P⌉ tokens that are projected back to F raw values.

## Method

LightGTS introduces two key mechanisms built on the inductive bias of scale-invariant intrinsic periods. First, Periodical Tokenization segments the input into non-overlapping patches of length P (one full cycle), then embeds them via a Flex Projection Layer that dynamically resizes a reference weight matrix θ* using the closed-form transformation δ⁻¹(A)⁺θ* (derived via Moore-Penrose pseudoinverse under a Revin-normalization assumption) to preserve token consistency across varying patch sizes without retraining. Second, Periodical Parallel Decoding (PPD) is a non-autoregressive scheme that replicates the last encoder token K times as decoder input, applies exponential positional reweighting ω(τ)=1/e^τ, and feeds all K tokens simultaneously through a Transformer decoder with RoPE, avoiding cumulative error and enabling parallel inference. The full architecture is a lightweight encoder-decoder Transformer (3 encoder + 3 decoder layers, d=256) with only ~4M parameters, pre-trained on 32 multi-source datasets spanning frequencies from milliseconds to months using MSE loss.

## Results

In zero-shot forecasting across 9 benchmarks, LightGTS-mini (4M params) achieves an average MSE reduction of over 30% compared to the strongest baselines, with particularly large gains on Solar (0.191 vs. 0.771 for Timer) and ETTm1 (0.327 vs. 0.768 for Timer). Even the 1M-parameter LightGTS-tiny variant outperforms most baselines, achieving a 27% average MSE reduction. In full-shot setting, LightGTS-mini achieves a 7% average MSE reduction over the best task-specific baselines (e.g., 0.156 vs. 0.160 on Electricity, 0.179 vs. 0.200 on Solar). Efficiency-wise, LightGTS uses only 4M parameters and 213M MACs, making it 10–100× smaller than competing foundation models (67M–700M params) while matching or exceeding their accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LightGTS-mini (4M params) outperforms all five zero-shot foundation model baselines on 8 out of 9 datasets in MSE, with the sole exception being Electricity where MOIRAI (300M) achieves 0.188 vs. LightGTS's 0.213. The margins are substantial: LightGTS-mini beats Timer (67M) by 27% average MSE, MOIRAI (300M) by 28%, Chronos (700M) by 27%, and Time-MoE (453M) by 17%. In the full-shot setting, LightGTS-mini (4M) surpasses all six task-specific baselines (PDF, iTransformer, Pathformer, FITS, TimeMixer, PatchTST) on 8 of 9 datasets, with Exchange being the closest contest (0.322 vs. iTransformer's 0.321). Notably, LightGTS's zero-shot performance on five datasets (ETTm1, ETTm2, ETTh1, ETTh2, Solar) already exceeds the full-shot performance of all task-specific baselines, demonstrating strong transferability from multi-source pre-training. The efficiency gap is dramatic: LightGTS uses 4M parameters and 213M MACs versus 67M–700M parameters and 52G–92T MACs for the foundation model baselines, while inference time (0.01s) matches the smallest baseline PatchTST (6.3M params).

## Relation to Prior Work

LightGTS departs from the dominant paradigm in Time Series Foundation Models (TSFMs) that relies on fixed patch tokenization (Timer, TimesFM) or point-level tokenization (Chronos) combined with large parameter counts for generalization. Instead, it repurposes the well-known inductive bias of periodicity—exploited in task-specific models like PDF and TimeMixer—into a general-purpose tokenization strategy (periodical patching) and a non-autoregressive decoding scheme (periodical parallel decoding). The Flex Projection Layer, grounded in a Moore-Penrose pseudoinverse analysis under Revin normalization, is a novel mechanism for handling variable patch sizes that has no direct precedent in prior TSFM literature. The architecture itself is a standard Transformer encoder-decoder with RoPE, so the contribution is in the tokenization and decoding design rather than in the backbone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a 4M-parameter model that matches or exceeds 200M–700M-parameter TSFMs in zero-shot forecasting enables deployment on resource-constrained devices and reduces pre-training and inference costs by orders of magnitude. The periodical tokenization is demonstrated as a plug-in that improves Timer by ~19% without retraining, suggesting broad applicability to existing patch-based TSFMs. Open questions include how the method performs on strongly non-periodic or multi-scale data (e.g., Exchange shows minimal benefit), whether the theoretical assumptions (Gaussian patch distribution, Revin normalization) hold in practice for all domains, and whether the zero-shot advantage persists as competing TSFMs adopt similar periodicity-aware designs.
