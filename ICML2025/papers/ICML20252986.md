## Problem

Existing unsupervised anomaly detection methods, whether reconstruction-based or embedding-based, derive anomaly scores through image- or feature-level matching that is inherently noisy yet largely overlooked. This matching noise blurs boundaries between normal and anomalous regions, degrading detection accuracy especially for subtle, low-contrast, or small-area anomalies, and rendering simple thresholding of pixel- or image-level logits ineffective.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252986/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB input image (resized to 256×256 or 224×224 depending on the baseline) and N normal templates—either multi-step diffusion reconstructions or randomly sampled normal training images—and extracts multi-layer features (L=4 layers) via a frozen DINO ViT-B/8 or EfficientNet-B4 encoder. It produces a pixel-level anomaly score map at feature resolution H'×W' (upsampled for visualization) and an image-level score computed as the mean of the top 250 pixel values. As a plug-in, the final output is a weighted combination λ·M + (1−λ)·M_baseline with the original method's score map.

## Method

CostFilter-AD reformulates UAD as a three-step pipeline: (1) multi-layer feature extraction from input and templates, (2) construction of a global anomaly cost volume via cosine similarity matching across all spatial positions of all templates (converted to cost as 1−similarity), and (3) filtering of this volume with a 3D U-Net. The filtering network is guided by a dual-stream attention mechanism (RCSA module) that concatenates input image features (spatial guidance to preserve anomaly edges) and an initial anomaly map from min-pooling (matching guidance to focus on relevant channels) as residual channel-spatial attention queries at each decoder layer. A class-aware adaptor dynamically modulates the focal loss γ parameter using soft classification logits to prioritize challenging samples across multiple anomaly classes. The method is trained with a composite loss (focal + cross-entropy + soft IoU + SSIM) on synthetic anomalies and operates as a generic post-processing plug-in compatible with both reconstruction-based (GLAD, HVQ-Trans) and embedding-based (AnomalDF) UAD methods without modifying their core architectures.

## Results

On MVTec-AD (multi-class, 15 categories), CostFilter-AD integrated with GLAD achieves 98.7% image AUROC / 98.2% pixel AUROC, with HVQ-Trans+Ours reaching 99.0/98.0 and AnomalDF+Ours reaching 98.5/98.8, all surpassing their respective baselines by 1.0–1.7% (image) and 0.7–0.9% (pixel). On VisA (12 categories), AnomalDF+Ours attains the best mean of 94.3/99.2, outperforming the strongest external baseline MambaAD (94.3/98.5) on pixel AUROC. Across four additional benchmarks (MVTec-AD, VisA, MPDD, BTAD) with seven metrics, the plug-in consistently improves UniAD and Dinomaly, e.g., Dinomaly+Ours reaches 99.7/98.4 on MVTec-AD and 95.5/98.1 on BTAD.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CostFilter-AD is a plug-in that improves three primary baselines: GLAD (diffusion-based, ECCV'24) gains +1.2%/+0.9% image/pixel AUROC on MVTec-AD and +3.1%/+0.7% on VisA; HVQ-Trans (transformer-based, NeurIPS'23) gains +1.0%/+0.7% on MVTec-AD and +2.1%/+0.1% on VisA; AnomalDF (embedding-based, WACV'25) gains +1.7%/+0.7% on MVTec-AD and +3.8%/+1.7% on VisA. Against external multi-class baselines, AnomalDF+Ours (98.5/98.8) surpasses MambaAD (98.6/97.7) on pixel AUROC and VPDM (98.4/97.8) on both metrics on MVTec-AD. On VisA, AnomalDF+Ours (94.3/99.2) outperforms MambaAD (94.3/98.5) and VPDM (94.2/98.9) on pixel AUROC. The plug-in also improves UniAD (NeurIPS'22) from 97.5→99.0 image AUROC and 96.9→97.5 pixel AUROC on MVTec-AD, and Dinomaly (ICCV'25) from 99.6→99.7 and 98.3→98.4. Single-class methods like PatchCore (CVPR'22, 96.4/95.7 mean) and OmniAL (CVPR'23, 97.2/98.3) are substantially outperformed by the multi-class +Ours configurations, confirming the scalability advantage of the unified model with cost filtering.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes cost volume filtering—a well-established technique from stereo matching, depth estimation, and optical flow—into the unsupervised anomaly detection (UAD) setting, where it had not previously been applied. Conceptually, it reframes UAD as a three-step matching pipeline (feature extraction, cost volume construction, cost volume filtering) rather than treating anomaly score computation as a byproduct of reconstruction or embedding quality. The filtering network's dual-stream attention mechanism is directly inspired by CBAM (Woo et al., 2018), adapted with residual connections and 3D convolutions to handle the volumetric matching dimension. The method is positioned as a generic post-processing plug-in that integrates with both reconstruction-based (GLAD, HVQ-Trans) and embedding-based (AnomalyDINO) UAD methods, departing from prior work that focuses exclusively on improving the reconstruction or feature extraction stage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies matching noise as a critical but systematically overlooked factor in UAD, providing a principled mechanism (cost volume filtering) to address it across method families. As a plug-in requiring only ~43M additional parameters and modest inference overhead, it offers a practical path to improve existing UAD pipelines without retraining the base model. However, the gains are modest (1–2% AUROC), and the failure case analysis reveals that the method still struggles when anomaly-relevant signals are absent from the cost volume (e.g., very low-contrast or tiny anomalies). Open questions include whether the approach extends to video or 3D anomaly detection, whether the matching cost volume formulation can be made more efficient for real-time deployment, and whether the 'identical shortcut' problem can be fundamentally eliminated rather than merely filtered.
