## Problem

Existing world models for model-based RL (e.g., TD-MPC2, Dreamer) train their dynamics models at a single fixed time step Δt, causing severe performance degradation when deployed at different observation rates due to compounding errors, overfitting to one temporal resolution, and inefficient sampling of multi-scale dynamics. The paper asks how to train a world model that captures task dynamics across a continuous range of Δt values without increasing the number of training samples or steps.

## Input / Output

The model receives a raw observation vector o_t, an action a_t, and a continuous-valued time step size Δt (e.g., ranging from 1 ms to 50 ms for Meta-World tasks). It outputs a predicted next latent state z_{t+Δt}, a predicted reward, a Q-value estimate, and a control action via an MPPI planner. Unusually, Δt is not merely a scheduling parameter but an explicit conditioning input to the dynamics, reward, value, and policy networks; the dynamics model internally integrates using a log-scaled time step τ(Δt) = max(0, log₁₀(Δt) + 5) to handle the wide numerical range of Δt values stably.

## Method

TAWM reformulates the latent dynamics model as a derivative function d(z_t, a_t, Δt) advanced via Euler or RK4 integration with the log-scaled step τ(Δt), enforcing the intrinsic property that z_{t+Δt}|_{Δt=0} = z_t. All dynamics-dependent components (dynamics, reward, value, policy) are conditioned on Δt, while the observation encoder remains Δt-independent. During training, Δt is sampled per-episode from a log-uniform or uniform distribution over a predefined range, exposing the model to multiple temporal resolutions simultaneously. The theoretical motivation draws on the Nyquist-Shannon sampling theorem: since real-world systems comprise subsystems at different characteristic frequencies, varying Δt during training avoids both undersampling high-frequency components and oversampling low-frequency ones, yielding better dynamics learning at the same sample budget. The approach is architecture-agnostic and is demonstrated on the TD-MPC2 backbone with an MPPI planner across Meta-World manipulation tasks and PDE-control environments (Burgers, Allen-Cahn, Wave).

## Results

On Meta-World tasks (9 tasks, success rate metric), TAWM matches or exceeds the TD-MPC2 baseline at the default Δt=2.5 ms and significantly outperforms it at all larger evaluation Δt values (5–50 ms). Notably, TAWM maintains ≈90% success on Faucet-Open at Δt=50 ms, while the fixed-Δt baseline degrades sharply; baselines trained exclusively at low observation rates (Δt≥10 ms) achieve zero success on every task. On PDE-control tasks (Burgers, Allen-Cahn, Wave; episode reward metric), TAWM with RK4 integration outperforms the fixed-Δt baseline across all evaluation time steps. TAWM also converges at least as quickly as the baseline at the default Δt, requiring no additional training steps or samples (1.5 M steps for Meta-World, 750 k–1 M steps for PDE tasks).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary baseline (TD-MPC2 trained at a fixed Δt=2.5 ms), TAWM wins on every evaluation Δt above the default: at Δt=2.5 ms it matches or slightly exceeds the baseline (e.g., both TAWM variants reach 100% success on Basketball vs. a lower baseline rate), and at Δt≥5 ms the gap widens substantially. Baselines retrained at other fixed Δt values (5, 10, 20, 50 ms) are all dominated by TAWM; those trained at Δt≥10 ms fail to converge entirely (0% success). Against MTS3 (NeurIPS 2023), TAWM consistently outperforms across all tested slow-dynamics hyperparameter H settings (H=5, 10, 11, 15), with the advantage growing at larger evaluation Δt. MTS3 suffers rapid degradation as Δt increases due to compounding errors from its multi-step roll-out strategy, whereas TAWM's single-step prediction conditioned on Δt preserves accuracy (≈90% at Δt=50 ms on Faucet-Open). TAWM also uses fewer training steps (1.5 M) than MTS3's offline data (4 M transitions) while achieving superior control performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on TD-MPC2 (Hansen et al., 2024), adapting its latent-space world model architecture by conditioning the dynamics, reward, value, and policy components on the time-step size Δt. The closest prior work is MTS3 (Shaj Kumar et al., 2023), which also addresses multi-temporal-scale dynamics but only at two discrete levels (Δt and HΔt) and relies on multi-step rollouts that compound errors. TAWM departs from both by treating Δt as a continuous input and training with a log-uniform mixture of step sizes, grounded in the Nyquist-Shannon sampling theorem applied to multi-scale dynamical subsystems. The Euler/RK4 reformulation of the dynamics model as a learned derivative function is a methodological departure from the direct state-transition mapping used in prior world models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that a single time-aware world model can operate across a range of observation rates without retraining, directly addressing the sim-to-real gap where deployment frequencies differ from training frequencies. The mixture-of-Δt training strategy is simple, model-agnostic, and does not increase sample complexity, making it broadly applicable to existing world-model architectures. Key limitations include the empirical (non-systematic) determination of Δt_max, the deterministic dynamics model that may be insufficient at large Δt or long horizons, and the restriction to relatively low-dimensional control tasks. Open questions include whether automatic identification of the highest dynamical frequency is feasible and whether the approach extends to high-dimensional or stochastic real-world systems.
