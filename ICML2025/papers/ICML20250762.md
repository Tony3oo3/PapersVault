## Problem

Existing role-based MARL methods derive emergent roles exclusively from an agent's past observation-action history, ignoring how those roles should shape future behavior. This causes agents with similar initial observations to adopt identical roles and exhibit redundant behaviors (e.g., both drones targeting the same fire), undermining coordination. The paper's central insight is that an agent's role should be linked to its expected future trajectories so that different roles naturally produce distinct, complementary behaviors.

## Input / Output

Each agent receives local observations and its own observation-action history τ_i^t (encoded into a 128-dim trajectory embedding via an MLP+GRU encoder), while the global state is used only during centralized training for the QMIX mixing network. The method outputs decentralized ε-greedy actions per agent, 64-dimensional role embeddings z_i^t learned via contrastive clustering, and per-agent intrinsic rewards (policy + dynamics components) that are added to the task reward in the TD loss. The dynamics intrinsic reward is computed by running one-step imagination through paired role-conditioned and role-agnostic DreamerV3 RSSM models over the local observation space.

## Method

R3DM maximizes a mutual information objective between an agent's role, its observed trajectory, and its expected future trajectory, decomposed via a theorem into two tractable components. The first component is optimized through the ACORM contrastive learning framework (K-means clustering of trajectory embeddings, bilinear score functions, MOCO-updated key network) to derive intermediate role embeddings from past behavior. The second component is optimized via two intrinsic rewards: a policy reward measuring KL divergence between role-conditioned SoftMax(Q_i) and the role-agnostic average action distribution, and a dynamics reward computed as the log-likelihood difference between a role-conditioned DreamerV3 RSSM and a role-agnostic RSSM for predicting next observations. These intrinsic rewards are scaled by hyperparameters α and β₃ and added to the QMIX TD loss, so that the mixing network and individual Q-networks are trained to simultaneously maximize task reward and role-specific future behavioral diversity.

## Results

R3DM achieves up to 20% improvement in final test win rates over state-of-the-art baselines on challenging SMAC and SMACv2 environments. On SMAC, the most significant gains appear on super-hard maps (3s5z_vs_3s6z, Corridor, 6h_vs_8z), where R3DM also demonstrates faster convergence (better sample efficiency). On SMACv2, R3DM outperforms all baselines in test win rates on zerg_5_vs_5, protoss_10_vs_11, and zerg_10_vs_11, while matching ACORM's win rate on the 5_vs_5 suite but achieving higher cumulative rewards, indicating more efficient winning strategies.

## Baselines & Comparisons

R3DM outperforms all six baselines (QMIX, ACORM, CIA, GoMARL, CDS, EMC) across the majority of evaluated environments. Against QMIX, R3DM's role learning mechanism yields consistently higher win rates and cumulative rewards, confirming that shared-parameter CTDE alone is insufficient for diverse coordination. Against ACORM (the method R3DM builds upon), R3DM achieves superior win rates on super-hard SMAC maps (3s5z_vs_3s6z, Corridor, 6h_vs_8z) and better cumulative rewards on SMACv2 5_vs_5 maps, attributed to intrinsic rewards promoting role-specific future behavior beyond what contrastive learning alone provides. Against CDS, R3DM clearly wins because CDS's agent-identity-based diversity leads to fragmented, uncoordinated behaviors with lower cumulative rewards. Against EMC, R3DM outperforms particularly on asymmetric 10_vs_11 SMACv2 environments where EMC's exploration emphasis hinders learning progress. Against GoMARL and CIA, R3DM achieves higher win rates, with the dynamics-model-based intrinsic rewards providing a more principled mechanism for role differentiation than grouping or credit-assignment-based contrastive approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

R3DM builds directly on ACORM (Hu et al., 2024), which uses contrastive learning to derive role embeddings from agents' observation-action histories, and on DreamerV3's RSSM architecture for world modeling. The key conceptual departure from prior role-based MARL methods (ROMA, RODE, CIA, GoMARL) is that roles are no longer derived solely from past trajectories; instead, R3DM introduces a mutual information objective linking roles to *expected future* trajectories, optimized through intrinsic rewards computed via a learned dynamics model. This shifts the role paradigm from 'roles describe what an agent has done' to 'roles determine what an agent will do next,' a distinction the paper argues is critical for avoiding redundant behaviors in coordination tasks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The insight that roles must shape future behavior rather than merely summarize past experience addresses a fundamental limitation in role-based MARL: agents with similar initial observations converge to identical roles and redundant actions. By integrating a DreamerV3-style RSSM to compute role-conditioned intrinsic rewards, R3DM opens a path toward model-based RL in cooperative MARL, a largely unexplored combination. However, the approach requires the number of roles to be set a priori, and the dynamics model only captures local observation dynamics without accounting for other agents' actions or roles, limiting its applicability to highly interactive settings. The 20% win-rate improvement on SMAC/SMACv2 is encouraging but confined to micro-management benchmarks, leaving open whether the approach generalizes to continuous-action or larger-scale multi-agent domains.
