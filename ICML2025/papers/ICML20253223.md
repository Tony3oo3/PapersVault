## Problem

Conventional uplift and Qini curves only count individuals with positive outcomes (Y=1) in their cumulative gain calculations, systematically undervaluing persuadables with negative outcomes (PE^CN) while overvaluing sleeping dogs with negative outcomes (SD^TN). This imbalance allows biased models that simply rank treated-positive individuals at the top to achieve higher curve scores than unbiased models that correctly differentiate between principal strata, leading to suboptimal model selection even in randomized controlled trials.

## Input / Output

The method operates on tabular RCT data with q-dimensional baseline covariates X, binary treatment assignment T ∈ {0,1}, and binary outcome Y ∈ {0,1}. It produces individual CATE estimates τ̂(x) used to rank all units in descending order, and the PUC evaluates this ranking by assigning equal weight to both positive and negative outcome individuals across treatment and control groups. The PUL loss consumes a binary proxy label g(t_i, y_i) = I(y_i = t_i) that is 1 for TP and CN samples and 0 for TN and CP samples, training the CATE head via sigmoid-transformed BCE.

## Method

The paper proposes the Principled Uplift Curve (PUC) with value function V_PUC(k,S) = R_S^T + NR_S^C − R_S^C − NR_S^T, which incorporates cumulative negative-outcome counts (NR_S^T, NR_S^C) alongside the standard positive-outcome counts, ensuring equal contribution from positive and negative outcomes within each stratum. From the PUC, the Principled Uplift Loss (PUL) is derived as a BCE loss between the helper function g(t_i,y_i)=I(y_i=t_i) and σ(τ̂(x_i)), directly encouraging the model to assign higher CATE to persuadables (TP+CN) than to sleeping dogs (TN+CP). PTONet implements this as a three-headed neural network variant of DragonNet: a shared representation feeds a treatment reconstruction head, an outcome prediction head, and a CATE head, with the total loss combining treatment loss, outcome loss, balance (propensity) loss, targeted regularizer, and the PUL term weighted by β. Proposition 4.1 proves the PUC differentiates persuadables from sleeping dogs while assigning equal value to treatment and control individuals within each stratum, and experiments show PUC achieves perfect Kendall correlation (1.0) with the true gain curve, whereas all conventional curves fall short.

## Results

On synthetic data, PTONet achieves the best PEHE (0.883±0.13), PUC AUUQC (0.948±0.15), and AUTGC (0.961±0.11), approaching the true CATE upper bound of 1.000. On the real-world Criteo dataset, PTONet attains the highest PUC score (0.191±0.01), outperforming the next-best EUEN (0.186±0.01) and DragonNet (0.137±0.05). On the high-dimensional Lazada dataset (83 covariates), PTONet again leads on PUC (0.0108±0.001) versus DragonNet (0.0082±0.001). The PUC metric itself achieves a perfect Kendall correlation of 1.000 with AUTGC, whereas all conventional curves (SUC, SQC, JUC, JQC) fall below 0.82.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PTONet outperforms all eight baselines on both PUC and AUTGC across all three datasets. On synthetic data, the gap is most pronounced against TARNet (PUC 0.948 vs. 0.431) and CFRNet (PUC 0.948 vs. 0.384), while the closest competitor is DragonNet (PUC 0.948 vs. 0.636; AUTGC 0.961 vs. 0.739). On Criteo, PTONet leads on PUC (0.191) over EUEN (0.186) and DragonNet (0.137), though it trails DESCN (0.556) and DragonNet (0.518) on the conventional SUC metric—illustrating the paper's central claim that conventional curves can reward biased rankings. On Lazada, PTONet achieves the best PUC (0.0108) while DESCN leads on SUC (0.1160), again confirming the divergence between balanced and unbalanced evaluation. The S-Learner augmented with the PUL loss (S-Learner PU) nearly matches PTONet (PUC 0.943 vs. 0.948), demonstrating that the Principled Uplift Loss is the primary driver of performance gains rather than the specific network architecture.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on DragonNet (Shi et al., 2019) as its architectural backbone and on Devriendt et al. (2020), which introduced uplift helper functions to guide learning-to-rank for uplift modeling. The key departure is a critical re-examination of the standard evaluation metrics (SUC, SQC, JUC, JQC): the authors show these curves systematically undervalue individuals with negative outcomes (Y=0), creating an imbalance that allows biased models to outscore unbiased ones. By introducing the Principled Uplift Curve (PUC) and its corresponding loss (PUL), the paper extends the Devriendt-style helper-function paradigm with a theoretically grounded, balanced objective that treats positive and negative outcomes symmetrically across treatment and control groups.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that conventional uplift/Qini curves can assign a higher AUUQC to a biased model than to an unbiased one challenges a foundational assumption in uplift model evaluation and has direct implications for model selection in marketing, healthcare, and finance. However, the current formulation is restricted to binary treatment and binary outcome in RCT settings, cannot distinguish sure things from lost causes, and relies on the unconfoundedness assumption. Open questions include whether PUC generalizes to multi-valued or continuous treatments, observational data with unobserved confounders, and whether the balanced evaluation principle can be extended to identify all four principal strata rather than just separating persuadables from sleeping dogs.
