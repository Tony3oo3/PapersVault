## Problem

Four-dimensional variational data assimilation (4D-Var) is the standard approach for estimating states of nonlinear dynamical systems from partial observations, but it suffers from high computational cost in complex systems and lacks theoretical guarantees when deep learning models are integrated to handle nonlinear dynamics or imperfect observation mappings. Existing ML-hybrid approaches either remain non-convex (latent 4D-Var, AD-based 4D-Var) or lack consistency guarantees between the learned space and the original state space, leaving a gap for a method that is simultaneously convex, theoretically grounded, and scalable.

## Input / Output

The method consumes partial, noisy observations (only 20–25% of state dimensions observed through a nonlinear arctan mapping), a window of m consecutive historical observations to resolve the underdetermined inverse problem (n_s > n_o), and a background state with associated covariance. It produces assimilated state estimates over the 4D-Var window and multi-step forecasts, all decoded from a learned fixed-dimensional feature space (d_s) back to the original state space via a trained inverse feature map. In the NWP experiments, inputs are 5 atmospheric variables on 64×32 or 240×121 grids at 6-hour intervals, with observations sampled at 6–15% spatial coverage including dynamic satellite track locations.

## Method

Tensor-Var embeds the 4D-Var problem into a Reproducing Kernel Hilbert Space where the kernel conditional mean embedding (CME) operator C_{S+|S} provides the best linear approximation of the nonlinear dynamical model, and a tensor-product CME operator C_{S|OH} (combining current observation and history features via φ_O ⊗ φ_H) serves as a linear inverse observation model that resolves incomplete observations. This transforms the original non-convex 4D-Var cost function into a convex quadratic program with linear dynamic constraints, solvable by interior-point methods with O(log(1/ε)) iteration complexity versus O(1/ε²) for L-BFGS-based baselines. For scalability, the infinite-dimensional kernel features are replaced by learned deep features (neural networks) trained jointly with the CME operators and an inverse feature map for the preimage problem, using a two-stage supervised procedure on trajectory data. Theoretical consistency between original and feature-space solutions is established via Kazantzis-Kravaris/Luenberger (KKL) observer theory, proving that the kernel feature map satisfies the state-transformation conditions for a linear observer with exponential convergence to the unique 4D-Var solution.

## Results

On the four chaotic system benchmarks (Lorenz-96 at 40/80 dims, Kuramoto-Sivashinsky at 128/256 dims), Tensor-Var achieves the lowest NRMSE in every setting, with the most dramatic gain on KS-256 where it reaches 4.31% NRMSE versus 8.87% for the best ML-hybrid baseline (Frerix et al.) and 10.23% for 4D-Var with adjoint — a >2× error reduction. Iteration counts drop from 83 (4D-Var) to 23 (Tensor-Var) on KS-256, and total evaluation time falls from 99.7 s to 21.4 s. On the global NWP task (ERA5, 64×32 grid, 15% observations), Tensor-Var consistently achieves the lowest mean and standard deviation of NRMSE across all five atmospheric variables (z500, t850, q700, u850, v850) and the lowest evaluation time among all baselines including Latent 3D/4D-Var and Fengwu 4D-Var.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against standard 4D-Var (with and without adjoint), Tensor-Var reduces NRMSE by 2–4× on all four chaotic benchmarks (e.g., 4.31% vs. 10.23% on KS-256) while cutting iteration counts by ~3.5× (23 vs. 83) and total evaluation time by ~4.7× (21.4 s vs. 99.7 s). Against the ML-hybrid Frerix et al. (2021) baseline, which learns an inverse observation operator but still solves non-convex 4D-Var in the original space, Tensor-Var achieves roughly 2× lower NRMSE on KS-256 (4.31% vs. 8.87%) and fewer iterations (23 vs. 31). On the global NWP task (ERA5, 64×32), Tensor-Var outperforms both Latent 3D-Var and Latent 4D-Var (Cheng et al., 2024) in NRMSE across all five atmospheric variables and runs faster because its deep features are used only for mapping rather than gradient-based AD during optimization. Against Fengwu 4D-Var (Xiao et al., 2024), which embeds a pretrained neural weather model into 4D-Var via AD, Tensor-Var achieves lower NRMSE with lower variance, particularly in the satellite-observation experiment at 240×121 resolution with only 6.37% spatial coverage, where it maintains the lowest mean and standard deviation over a 7-day assimilation horizon.

## Relation to Prior Work

Tensor-Var sits at the intersection of kernel conditional mean embedding (CME) theory from RKHS (Fukumizu et al., 2004; Song et al., 2009) and the classical 4D-Var formulation (Asch et al., 2016), repurposing CME operators as linear surrogates for nonlinear dynamical and observation models. It departs from latent-space DA (Peyron et al., 2021; Cheng et al., 2024), which performs variational optimization in a learned low-dimensional space without theoretical consistency guarantees, and from ML-hybrid 4D-Var approaches (Frerix et al., 2021; Xiao et al., 2024) that retain non-convex optimization. The key conceptual departure is invoking KKL observer theory (Tran & Bernard, 2023) to prove that the kernel feature map constitutes a valid state transformation yielding a linear system, thereby converting 4D-Var into a convex quadratic program with guaranteed solution consistency between original and feature spaces.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By establishing that 4D-Var can be solved as a convex QP in a learned feature space with linear convergence, Tensor-Var offers a principled alternative to the costly iterative nonlinear solvers (L-BFGS, adjoint methods) that dominate operational NWP, achieving 10–20× speedups while improving accuracy across chaotic benchmarks and global weather prediction. The KKL-based theoretical guarantee that feature-space and original-space solutions are consistent addresses a long-standing gap in latent DA methods. Key limitations remain: the framework requires access to true system states during training (not feasible in operational settings where only observations are available), and the simplified empirical error covariance may underestimate correlations. Future work on learning dynamics purely from observations and refining the covariance structure within the feature space would be needed for full operational deployment.
