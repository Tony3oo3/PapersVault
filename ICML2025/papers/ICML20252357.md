## Problem

Food systems drive a third of human-caused greenhouse gas emissions, yet LLMs' potential to accelerate sustainable food development and adoption remains unexplored. When prompted to revise a menu to cut emissions by 75% while maintaining patron satisfaction, LLMs produce entirely plant-based menus that significantly reduce satisfaction, revealing a fundamental weakness in multi-constraint optimization. The paper asks what LLMs can and cannot do across design and prediction tasks in sustainable food, and how to remedy their optimization shortcomings.

## Input / Output

The method consumes recipe titles and descriptions (from a 36-item reference menu plus LLM-generated plant-based candidates), per-item GHG emissions data (from Poore & Nemecek), animal welfare data (animals killed per kg from Faunalytics), and constraint parameters (75% emissions reduction, welfare non-degradation). The LLM outputs a scalar preference estimate p̂(u_i) ∈ [0,1] for each of the 56 candidate recipes, and the IQP solver outputs a binary selection vector x ∈ {0,1}^56 identifying the 36 recipes for the final menu. The framework is general: the ground set can be any collection of items, and the LLM's role is to supply the subjective scoring function that is difficult to formalize mathematically.

## Method

The paper proposes an LLM-Guided Combinatorial Optimization framework that decomposes the menu design problem into two stages: (1) an LLM generates candidate plant-based recipes and estimates expected preference scores p̂(u_i) for every item in the ground set U = C ∪ O (20 LLM-generated + 36 original recipes), and (2) an integer quadratic programming (IQP) solver selects the optimal 36-item subset by maximizing expected satisfaction plus a λ-weighted negative pairwise similarity (diversity) term, subject to expected emissions and animal welfare constraints weighted by the predicted preference distribution. The key insight is that LLMs supply the hard-to-formalize subjective preference estimates while the optimizer handles the combinatorial constraint satisfaction that LLMs fail at. The authors prove (Proposition 6.1) that the suboptimality gap is bounded by 2Kε, where K is the number of selected items and ε is the maximum item-level preference estimation error, providing a theoretical guarantee that coarse-grained LLM predictions suffice. In experiments with 800 participants, o1-preview+IQP achieves a 79% emissions reduction while maintaining satisfaction within 1% of the original menu.

## Results

On the experimental design task, o1-preview achieves a mean estimated 45% time saved for expert food scientists, compared to 22% for a fellow human expert (p=0.0002), and is significantly better on specificity (p=0.003). On the menu design task, the proposed o1-preview+IQP approach reduces GHG emissions of food choices by 79% (from 44.91 to 8.70 kg CO₂ eq) while maintaining patron satisfaction with their set of choices (8.44 vs. 8.40, p=1.37e-18). For preference prediction, no LLM meaningfully outperforms the simple nutritional baseline on sensory profile prediction (best: o1-preview at 0.64 vs. 0.67 baseline), but GPT-4o reaches 64% pairwise accuracy on Food.com recipe ratings (vs. 50% random), and Claude 3.5 Sonnet reaches 73% on menu-design order frequency prediction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is the original 36-item menu from Banerjee et al. (2023), which yields 44.91 kg CO₂ eq emissions and 8.40/10 satisfaction. The proposed o1-preview+IQP method dramatically outperforms this on emissions (8.70 vs. 44.91, a 79% reduction) while statistically maintaining satisfaction (8.44 vs. 8.40, p=0.93). It also substantially outperforms the 'Vegetarian Subset' baseline (19.87 emissions, 6.82 satisfaction) and the 'Remove Beef' baseline (21.65 emissions, 7.74 satisfaction) on both emissions and satisfaction. Critically, o1-preview alone (without IQP) generates entirely plant-based menus that achieve comparable emissions (9.64) but significantly lower satisfaction (6.51 vs. 8.44), demonstrating that the combinatorial optimization component is essential for balancing sustainability with patron preferences. On the experimental design task, o1-preview outperforms expert human food scientists on specificity (p=0.003) and estimated time saved (45% vs. 22%, p=0.0002), though the difference in accuracy is not statistically significant (p=0.121).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the predict-then-optimize framework (Elmachtoub & Grigas, 2022; Bertsimas & Kallus, 2020) to combinatorial optimization where the scoring function involves subjective human preferences that cannot be easily formalized mathematically. Unlike prior LLM+optimization work (OptiMUS, LLMOpt, SatLM, LogicLM) where the optimization problem is fully specified and the LLM translates natural language into solver code, here the LLM itself provides the preference estimates p(u_i) that parameterize the objective, while a standard IQP solver handles constraint satisfaction. The paper also positions itself as the first systematic LLM evaluation in the sustainable food domain, drawing on the typology of LLM evaluations in law (LegalBench) and chemistry (Guo et al., 2023) but applying it to food science tasks grounded in expert collaboration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The LLM+IQP framework is general and applicable to any domain where one must optimize over items with a subjective, hard-to-formalize quality (education curricula, fitness regimens, travel planning), making it potentially broadly adoptable. However, the 79% emissions reduction is demonstrated in a controlled hypothetical restaurant with a single 36-item menu and no actual food delivery, and the sensory profile prediction results show LLMs do not beat a simple nutritional baseline for holistic taste measures, limiting near-term applicability to product development. The identified omnivore bias in preference prediction raises concerns for deploying these tools to support plant-based food adoption without additional calibration. Open questions include whether LLM-generated experimental designs actually improve products when implemented, and whether the framework generalizes to more complex, multi-restaurant or supply-chain settings.
