## Problem

Existing deep learning methods for quantum state characterization (e.g., GQNQ, NQS) produce implicit vector representations that act as black boxes—they can predict properties but cannot be experimentally reconstructed into executable quantum circuits. Meanwhile, variational quantum methods that do produce circuits suffer from barren plateaus and local minima when optimizing parameters via gradient descent, limiting scalability to large systems. This paper asks whether one can learn explicit, hardware-executable circuit representations from local measurements alone, without gradient-based circuit optimization.

## Input / Output

The input is a real-valued matrix of shape (N−1)×9 containing expectation values of all 9 two-qubit Pauli operators (XX, XY, XZ, YX, YY, YZ, ZX, ZY, ZZ) measured on each neighboring qubit pair; the measurement operator labels are discarded, so the network must implicitly learn which value corresponds to which observable. The output is an ordered sequence of (gate_type, continuous_parameter) tokens that constitute an explicit quantum circuit; executing this circuit on |0⟩^⊗N yields a surrogate state whose properties can be read out by direct measurement. The circuit depth is bounded by a maximum of T steps (100–200 in experiments), and the framework supports zero-shot transfer across system sizes via the aggregation block.

## Method

QCrep frames circuit construction as a sequential decision problem solved by a PPO-trained agent whose policy is a Transformer encoder (two layers, 128-dim embeddings, 4 heads) followed by a 3-layer MLP. At each step the agent selects a layer of single- or two-qubit gates from a task-aware action space (e.g., {H, CZ, R_z(φ)} for IQP states) and applies them to evolve the target state toward |0⟩^⊗N; the learned inverse circuit V_s† then serves as the explicit representation. A key architectural choice is the Transformer's self-attention over the (N−1) measurement sites, which captures long-range entanglement correlations, combined with global average pooling to produce a size-agnostic feature vector enabling zero-shot transfer. The reward is the average 1-local fidelity (probability of all measured qubits being in |0⟩), which avoids the barren-plateau pathology of global fidelity; a theoretical proposition bounds global fidelity from below as 1−(N−n+1)ε given local fidelity ≥ 1−ε. Because the agent selects discrete gate types and continuous angles via a Gaussian policy rather than backpropagating through the circuit, no gradient with respect to circuit parameters is ever computed, sidestepping the barren plateau problem entirely.

## Results

On 10-qubit IQP circuits, QCrep achieves a global fidelity of 0.9851±0.0208 and near-zero RMSE on Rényi entropy (3.07e-07) and two-point correlations (1.09e-08), substantially outperforming all baselines. On 10-qubit Ising-evolved states, the global fidelity reaches 0.9979±0.0012, and on 10-qubit Heisenberg XXZ ground states, all three property RMSEs (Rényi entropy, correlations, spin-Z) drop to 0.0000. The framework scales to 50-qubit systems and demonstrates zero-shot transfer to 100-qubit systems, with circuit depths as low as 2 (IQP) to 28 (Heisenberg) gates.

## Baselines & Comparisons

QCrep outperforms all four baselines (TQS, VQE, QAOA, QAS) on every metric across all four state families tested. Against TQS, the advantage is most dramatic: on IQP circuits, QCrep's global fidelity (0.9851) exceeds TQS (0.6894) by nearly 30 percentage points, and Rényi entropy RMSE drops from 0.5765 to 3.07e-07. VQE is the strongest gradient-based competitor on IQP (0.9174 fidelity) but collapses on Ising-evolved states (0.2795 fidelity) and Heisenberg ground states (0.4765), likely due to barren plateaus. QAOA is competitive on Ising ground states (0.9614 vs. 0.9691 fidelity) and ties QCrep on Heisenberg spin-Z (both 0.0000), but still trails on global fidelity and other properties. QAS consistently underperforms, with the lowest global fidelity in every table (0.4694–0.8032), suggesting that architecture search without gradient-free parameter optimization struggles to converge on these tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground in quantum state characterization by introducing 'explicit circuit representations'—sequences of quantum gates with parameters—as an alternative to the implicit vector representations produced by prior ML-based methods (GQNQ, NQS, TQS). It departs from earlier RL-for-quantum work (Wauters et al. 2020, Yao et al. 2021, Ostaszewski et al. 2021) by eliminating gradient-based parameter optimization entirely, and from variational methods (VQE, QAOA) by avoiding the barren plateau problem through a local fidelity reward. The Transformer-based measurement feature aggregation block and the theoretical bound connecting n-local fidelity to global fidelity (Proposition 2.1) are novel components that bridge the gap between local measurements and global state reconstruction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The explicit circuit representation opens the 'black box' of implicit quantum state representations, enabling experimental reconstruction of surrogate states and downstream tasks like Hamiltonian learning with a simple linear model. Zero-shot transfer across system sizes (trained on 50 qubits, applied to 10–100 qubits) and the linear recoverability of Hamiltonian parameters from circuit representations are practically significant for quantum device calibration and characterization. Key limitations include restriction to 1D Hamiltonians, shallow circuits, and classical MPS-based simulation only; open questions include extension to 2D systems, critical many-body behavior, deeper circuits, quantum process learning, and validation on real noisy hardware.
