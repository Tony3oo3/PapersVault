## Problem

Equivariant neural networks that hard-code geometric symmetries improve parameter efficiency but typically require more FLOPs per parameter than standard networks, making them computationally expensive at scale. This paper addresses the gap by showing that equivariant architectures can be designed to maintain a comparable FLOPs-per-parameter ratio to their non-equivariant counterparts, removing the computational penalty that has limited their practical adoption.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250182/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 224×224 RGB images and produces a single class label over 1,000 ImageNet-1K categories. A key structural constraint is that the network is guaranteed flopping-invariant: the prediction for any image is identical to that of its horizontal mirror. This is enforced by splitting all intermediate features into flopping-invariant and (-1)-equivariant channels, with only the invariant channels fed to the final classification head.

## Method

The core mechanism is to parametrize all feature spaces in terms of irreducible representations of the flopping group D₂, splitting channels into invariant and (-1)-equivariant components. This forces every linear layer into a block-diagonal form with two (d/2)×(c/2) blocks instead of one d×c block, halving the FLOPs while preserving equivariance. The approach is instantiated across three modern architectures—ResMLP, ViT (DeiT III), and isotropic ConvNeXt—requiring only minimal modifications: equivariant PatchEmbed with symmetric/antisymmetric filters, equivariant GELU via a Fourier-domain transform, equivariant attention using invariant dot products, and (for ConvNeXt) alternating symmetric/antisymmetric depthwise convolutions. For ResMLP, the decomposition extends to the patch dimension as well, pairing spatially mirrored patches to form invariant and equivariant quantities. The paper demonstrates that as model size scales, the equivariant variants achieve comparable or improved Top-1 accuracy at half the FLOPs and higher throughput than their non-equivariant baselines.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K, the flopping-equivariant models achieve comparable or improved top-1 accuracy at roughly half the FLOPs of their non-equivariant counterparts. The hybrid H(ViT-H) achieves the best overall accuracy of 85.0% (vs. 84.6% for ViT-H) with 127.6 GFLOPs vs. 168.0 GFLOPs. E(ResMLP-L24) beats its baseline by 1.1 points (81.5% vs. 80.4%) while using only 31.7 GFLOPs vs. 63.3 GFLOPs. Throughput improvements of 30–60% are observed for larger models, e.g., E(ResMLP-L24) reaches 1756 im/s vs. 1107 im/s for ResMLP-L24.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ResMLP baselines, E(ResMLP-L24) outperforms its non-equivariant counterpart by 1.1 points (81.5% vs. 80.4%) at half the FLOPs, while E(ResMLP-B24) nearly ties (80.8% vs. 81.0%); however, smaller ResMLP variants lose 4.5–4.6 points, suggesting the regularization in the original training recipes is too aggressive for the reduced parameter count. Against DeiT III ViT baselines, the hybrid H(ViT-H) achieves the best accuracy of all models (85.0% vs. 84.6%), and H(ViT-L) also beats its baseline (84.5% vs. 84.2%); the fully equivariant E(ViT-H) and E(ViT-L) trail by only 0.2 and 0.8 points respectively, while E(ViT-S) failed to converge. Against ConvNeXt baselines, E(ConvNeXt-L(iso.)) exactly matches the baseline at 82.6% with half the FLOPs and higher throughput (1527 vs. 1284 im/s), but E(ConvNeXt-B(iso.)) and E(ConvNeXt-S(iso.)) lose 0.7 and 2.9 points respectively. Across all architectures, the equivariant models consistently achieve roughly half the FLOPs and parameters, with throughput gains of 30–60% for larger models, confirming the paper's central claim that equivariance need not increase computational cost per parameter.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the steerable CNN framework of Cohen & Welling (2017) and Weiler & Cesa (2019), which decompose feature spaces into irreducible representations of a symmetry group to enforce equivariance. The key departure is computational: prior equivariant ConvNets implemented equivariant convolutions via standard convolution routines, yielding no FLOP savings despite reduced parameters. Here, by restricting to the two-element flopping group D2 and exploiting the resulting block-diagonal structure of linear layers, the authors achieve half the FLOPs per parameter compared to non-equivariant networks. The approach is applied to three modern architectures (ViT/DeiT III, ConvNeXt, ResMLP) rather than the ConvNets typical of prior equivariant work, and the ConvNeXt variant specifically leverages the depthwise-then-1×1-convolution structure to realize the block-diagonal savings in practice.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper challenges the widely held assumption (reinforced by Klee et al. 2023 and Weiler & Cesa 2019) that equivariant architectures necessarily trade parameter efficiency for higher computational cost per parameter, showing that for the flopping group the two can be simultaneously optimized. This opens a practical path to incorporating geometric symmetry into modern vision backbones without a compute penalty, which is relevant as equivariant networks are increasingly considered for scaling. Limitations include the restriction to the minimal D2 group (no rotations), the absence of hyperparameter tuning for the equivariant variants, and the observation that small models actually suffer in throughput. Open questions include extending to larger groups like D8, optimizing the invariant-to-equivariant feature ratio per layer, and developing custom GPU kernels for symmetric/antisymmetric convolutions.
