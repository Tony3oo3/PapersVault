## Problem

While data curation is well-studied for pretraining and supervised fine-tuning, the selection of high-quality prompts for preference optimization (DPO/RLHF) remains underexplored. Existing filtering methods rely on prompt-only heuristics or LLM-as-judge scores, overlooking the signal contained in the paired model responses. The paper asks whether the quality of rejected responses and the reward gap between chosen and rejected responses can serve as effective, response-based indicators of prompt quality.

## Input / Output

The method consumes preference pairs consisting of a prompt x, a chosen response y_w, and a rejected response y_l, each annotated with a reward score (from ArmoRM, LLM-as-a-Judge, or human annotators). It outputs a filtered subset of prompts (e.g., 4,538 out of 20,000 WildChat prompts) that are then used for DPO training. In the Self-RIP variant, the output is additionally a set of synthetic prompts generated via few-shot sampling from the RIP-curated seed pool, which are themselves re-filtered by RIP before training.

## Method

RIP filters prompts using three response-derived metrics: (1) rejected response reward r(y_l|x), (2) rejected response length len(y_l), and (3) reward gap r(y_w|x) − r(y_l|x). Prompts are retained if the rejected reward and length exceed lower-bound thresholds and the reward gap falls below an upper-bound threshold, with thresholds set at the 50th percentile of each metric's distribution. The underlying hypothesis is that low-quality prompts (ambiguous, nonsensical, unsafe) produce low-quality rejected responses and high response variance, making these metrics effective proxies for prompt quality. For synthetic data generation (Self-RIP), RIP-curated prompts serve as 8-shot few-shot examples to guide the seed model in generating new prompts, which are then re-filtered by RIP to ensure quality. This two-stage filtering (seed selection + post-filtering) outperforms either component alone and surpasses standard Self-Instruct using unfiltered prompts.

## Results

On WildChat-20k with Llama 3.1-8B-Instruct, RIP (filtering to 4,538 prompts) achieves 57.8% AlpacaEval2 LC Win Rate, 43.1 Arena-Hard, and 45.6 WildBench, outperforming the no-filtering baseline (48.4%, 37.9, 41.5) by 9.4, 5.2, and 4.1 points respectively. Scaling to Llama 3.3-70B-Instruct, RIP boosts Arena-Hard from 70.5 to 82.9 and AlpacaEval2 LC Win Rate from 54.3% to 67.7%, moving the model from 18th to 6th place on the Arena-Hard leaderboard. Self-RIP (synthetic prompts generated from RIP-curated few-shots) further improves AlpacaEval2 LC Win Rate to 60.2%, surpassing even the best human-written prompt results.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RIP outperforms all six alternative filtering baselines across all three benchmarks (AlpacaEval2, Arena-Hard, WildBench). The closest competitor is Jaccard Similarity (which also uses both chosen and rejected responses), achieving 49.0/42.6/43.7 vs. RIP's 57.8/43.1/45.6 — a gap of 8.8 points on AlpacaEval2 LC Win Rate. Prompt-only methods (LLM-as-Prompt-Judge, InsTag) and prompt+chosen-response methods (IFD, perplexity) all underperform even the no-filtering baseline on AlpacaEval2, confirming that filtering based solely on prompt or chosen-response features does not transfer well to the pairwise preference setting. RIP also beats the no-filtering DPO baseline by 9.4 points on AlpacaEval2 LC Win Rate while using only 23% of the training data (4,538 vs. 20,000 prompts). For synthetic data generation, Self-RIP (60.2% LC Win Rate) outperforms standard Self-Instruct (49.1%) by 11.1 points, and the ablation shows both RIP-curated few-shots and RIP post-filtering contribute independently to this gain.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Self-Instruct framework (Wang et al., 2023) for synthetic prompt generation and the DPO training paradigm (Rafailov et al., 2023), but departs from prior data curation work by shifting the filtering signal from the prompt or chosen response to the *rejected* response quality and the reward gap between the pair. While prior methods (InsTag, IFD, perplexity, LLM-as-prompt-judge) evaluate prompts in isolation or via the chosen response, RIP's key conceptual move is to treat the rejected response as a diagnostic of prompt quality: low-quality prompts produce low-quality rejected responses and high-variance response distributions. The paper also extends the best-vs-worst pairing method (Pace et al., 2024) by showing that filtering prompts based on pair characteristics outperforms alternative pairing strategies (best-vs-random, best-vs-bottom-K%).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a simple three-metric filter (rejected reward, rejected length, reward gap) applied offline can improve model alignment by 9–15 points on standard benchmarks while discarding 77% of training data, making it an attractive drop-in addition to existing DPO/SimPO pipelines. The demonstrated weak-to-strong generalizability (8B model filtering for 70B model) suggests the method is computationally efficient for large-scale training. Open questions include whether the approach extends beyond preference optimization to other RL training schemes, how to incorporate explicit safety criteria into the reward signal, and whether the filtering thresholds transfer across domains or require re-tuning per dataset. The finding that standard SFT data-selection methods actively degrade preference-optimization performance is a useful caution for practitioners mixing curation strategies across training phases.
