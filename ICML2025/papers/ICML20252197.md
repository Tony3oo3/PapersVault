## Problem

Coreset construction for regularized logistic regression in vertical federated learning (VFL) was entirely unexplored, and the existing VFL coreset for regularized linear regression (Huang et al., 2022) depended on a data-dependent condition-number parameter that an adversary could make arbitrarily large. More fundamentally, no prior VFL coreset method captured the reduction in model complexity induced by the regularization parameter λ, so coreset sizes did not shrink as regularization increased.

## Input / Output

Each client j ∈ [T] holds a local feature submatrix X^(j) ∈ R^(n×d_j) (with Σd_j = d for VRLog, Σd_j = d+1 for VRLR where the last client also holds the response vector y), and all clients share the label/response vector y and the scalar regularization parameter λ. The algorithm outputs a weighted coreset S_w = (S, w) where S ⊆ Z is a sampled subset of expected size m and w: S → [1,∞) assigns weights inversely proportional to sampling probability; the guarantee is that for every model vector q ∈ R^d, the regularized loss on S_w is within (1±ε) of the full-data loss with probability ≥ 0.99.

## Method

For VRLog, each client computes ℓ₁ Lewis weights on a locally augmented matrix Ẑ^(j) = (Z^(j); λI_{d_j}), where the appended λI rows encode the regularization penalty; the first n Lewis weights serve as local importance scores. For VRLR, each client computes ℓ₂ Lewis weights (leverage scores) on an augmented matrix that appends √λ·I rows. In both cases, the augmented construction causes the total sensitivity to equal the ℓ₁ or ℓ₂ statistical dimension sd(Z^(j), λ, p) = Σ 1/(1+λ/σ_i²), which decreases monotonically with λ, directly shrinking the coreset size. The local scores are fed into the unified VFL sampling framework (Algorithm 3 from Huang et al., 2022): clients report score sums, the server samples a subset of clients, each selected client samples points proportionally to its local scores, and the server assigns global weights. For VRLR, this approach eliminates the (σ_max/σ_min)² dependence present in prior work, yielding a coreset of size Õ(T·Σ_j sd(Z^(j),λ,2)·log d / ε²) that depends only on the number of clients T and the per-client statistical dimensions.

## Results

On the Credit Card dataset (VRLog), AugLewis achieves the best F1 score of 0.9343 (test, 500 samples) and 0.9331 (test, 2500 samples), outperforming Lewis (0.9230/0.9315), HLSZ (0.8712/0.9078), and Uniform (0.8185/0.8731). Training on the coreset is approximately 80–100× faster than training on the full dataset. For VRLR, the proposed leverage-score-based sampling (Algorithm 2) consistently achieves lower test RMSE and smaller model-parameter distance compared to both uniform sampling and the Huang et al. (2022) leverage-score method across Blog Feedback, Financial, Wave Energy, and Year Prediction datasets. The coreset size provably decreases as the regularization parameter λ increases, with the total sensitivity bounded by the ℓ₁/ℓ₂ statistical dimension of the data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against HLSZ (Huang et al., 2022), the proposed AugLewis method achieves a substantial F1 improvement of ~6.3 points at 500 samples (0.9343 vs. 0.8712) and ~2.5 points at 2500 samples (0.9331 vs. 0.9078) on the Credit Card dataset; the gap is even more dramatic on KDD Cup (0.8772 vs. 0.4702 at 50 samples). Against Lewis (Mai et al., 2021, unregularized ℓ₁ Lewis weights), AugLewis wins by ~1.1 F1 points at 500 samples (0.9343 vs. 0.9230) and by ~0.2 points at 2500 samples, while also providing a tighter theoretical coreset size that shrinks with λ. Against SqLev (Munteanu et al., 2018), AugLewis is competitive in balanced accuracy and F1, though SqLev occasionally shows slightly better model closeness; however, SqLev's coreset size scales as O(√n) with no regularization-aware guarantee, making it less reliable at scale. For VRLR, the proposed method (Algorithm 2) clearly outperforms both uniform sampling and the Huang et al. (2022) leverage-score baseline in test RMSE and model-parameter distance across all tested datasets (Blog Feedback, Financial, Wave Energy, Year Prediction, UJIIndoorLoc), with the advantage growing as the number of clients increases.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the VFL coreset framework of Huang et al. (NeurIPS 2022), which provided a unified importance-sampling algorithm for regularized linear regression and k-means in vertically partitioned settings. The authors introduce a new coreset construction for regularized logistic regression in VFL by leveraging ℓ₁ Lewis weights (building on Mai et al., NeurIPS 2021) applied to an augmented matrix that incorporates the regularization term, and they improve the ridge regression coreset size by eliminating a dependence on the squared condition number of the data. The overall pipeline follows the standard sensitivity-sampling framework of Feldman & Langberg (STOC 2011), with Lewis weight computation via the iterative algorithm of Cohen & Peng (STOC 2015).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

In VFL, communication cost scales linearly with dataset size, so smaller coresets directly reduce the dominant overhead of multi-client gradient synchronization. The theoretical result that coreset size decreases monotonically with the regularization parameter λ provides a principled knob for practitioners to trade off model complexity against communication budget. However, the coreset size still carries a factor of T (number of clients) and, for VRLog, a μ-complexity term that can be data-dependent and large; eliminating these factors or extending the approach to non-convex losses and deeper models remain open. The practical 80–100× training speedup on tabular datasets is encouraging but the evaluation is limited to linear models and moderate-scale data.
