## Problem

Despite the apparent simplicity of counting token occurrences in a sequence, even 8B-parameter language models fail to solve the histogram task robustly in-weights. The specific roles of individual transformer components—attention mechanism, feed-forward layers, softmax normalization, and special tokens—in determining whether and how a model can count remain poorly understood. This paper asks how architectural design choices shape the solution space for counting in minimal transformer blocks.

## Input / Output

The model receives a sequence of L tokens drawn from an alphabet of size T (with L ≤ T) and produces a same-length output sequence where each position contains the total count of its corresponding input token across the entire sequence. The task is inherently permutation-equivariant, so no positional embeddings are used. The output is a discrete categorical prediction over {1, ..., C} where C ≤ L, making the problem fundamentally about mapping continuous embedding-space information to a small set of integer classes.

## Method

The paper analyzes 1-layer transformer blocks combining a token-mixing mechanism (linear, dot-product, or dot-product with BOS token, each optionally with softmax) followed by a single-hidden-layer ReLU feed-forward network. The key theoretical contribution is identifying two distinct counting algorithms—relation-based counting (RC), which uses dot-product attention for pairwise token comparisons and requires only p=1 hidden neurons, and inventory-based counting (IC), which memorizes the full alphabet in feed-forward weights and requires p≥T hidden neurons—and providing explicit weight constructions proving 100% accuracy in specific (d, p) regimes. For the non-orthogonal embedding regime (d < T), they derive minimum dimension bounds via the Welch bound on mutual coherence, and show that softmax non-linearity can reduce the required dimension to d = ⌈log₂(T+1)⌉+2 by suppressing cross-token noise. Empirical training and mechanistic introspection of learned attention matrices and feed-forward activations confirm the predicted learning regimes and strategy selection.

## Results

For the histogram task with T=32 tokens and L=10 sequence length, the paper identifies distinct feasibility regimes: bos and bos+sftm achieve 100% accuracy with d≥T and p=1 via relation-based counting (RC), while dot achieves comparable performance without a BOS token. In contrast, dot+sftm, lin, and lin+sftm require p≥T (inventory-based counting, IC) to reach 100% accuracy in the d≥T regime. When d<T, the softmax operator dramatically reduces the required embedding dimension: bos+sftm and dot+sftm can solve the task with d≈7 (vs. d≥29–30 without softmax), as the softmax nonlinearly suppresses noise from non-orthogonal embeddings. Even large LLMs (Mistral-7B, Llama-3.1-8B) fail to count robustly in-weights, with positional queries predominantly predicting count 3.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against external baselines but instead contrasts six architectural variants of a 1-layer transformer (lin, lin+sftm, dot, dot+sftm, bos, bos+sftm) on the same histogram task. The key finding is that the choice of mixing mechanism and the presence of softmax fundamentally determine which counting strategy is feasible: dot-product mixing without softmax (dot, bos) enables memory-efficient RC with p=1, while softmax-normalized dot-product (dot+sftm) blocks RC and forces the more parameter-hungry IC requiring p≥T. Linear mixing (lin, lin+sftm) always requires IC regardless of softmax. The BOS token is shown to be sufficient but not necessary for RC, as dot achieves the same p=1 regime via tagged embeddings. In the d<T regime, softmax models (bos+sftm, dot+sftm) are substantially more robust, achieving near-perfect accuracy at d≈7 versus d≥29–30 for non-softmax counterparts, highlighting the softmax's role in suppressing entanglement noise from non-orthogonal embeddings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the histogram task framework introduced by Weiss et al. (2021) via RASP(-L), which showed that single-layer transformers with one attention head can solve counting. While RASP predicted a BOS token was necessary, this paper provides explicit weight constructions showing it is not, and identifies two distinct algorithmic strategies (relation-based and inventory-based counting) that emerge depending on the mixing mechanism and feed-forward capacity. The analysis draws on compressed sensing (Welch bounds, mutual coherence) to characterize the d < T regime, and connects to mechanistic interpretability work on feed-forward layers as memorization modules (Geva et al., 2021; Meng et al., 2022) and superposition (Elhage et al., 2022). Concurrent work by Yehudai et al. (2024) independently studies counting bounds but does not examine architectural variations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a seemingly trivial aggregation task reveals deep architectural dependencies: the softmax operator, often viewed as purely beneficial, actually prevents relation-based counting in standard dot-product attention, while simultaneously enabling lower-dimensional solutions through noise suppression. The finding that 8B-parameter LLMs (Mistral-7B, Llama-3.1-8B) fail to reliably count 10 items in a list underscores that scale alone does not guarantee algorithmic competence. Open questions include which strategy is learned when both RC and IC are feasible (evidence suggests superposition), whether these phenomena persist in deeper autoregressive architectures with causal masks, and how the required numerical precision scales with sequence length in finite-precision implementations.
