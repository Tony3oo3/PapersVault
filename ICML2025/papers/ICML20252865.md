## Problem

Existing environment-centered graph OOD methods treat spurious subgraphs as the entirety of environment variables, limiting the inference space to the scale of those subgraphs. This positive inference paradigm fails under complex environment shifts (e.g., when spurious substructures are scaled or diversified), because the true space of environment changes encompasses far more than intra-sample spurious patterns. The paper asks how to broaden the environment inference space so that invariant subgraph extraction remains robust under severe and unseen distribution shifts.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard graph inputs (node feature matrix X ∈ R^{N×d}, adjacency matrix A ∈ R^{N×N}) along with class labels during training. Beyond the predicted class label, NeGo outputs a causal subgraph via a subgraph extractor operating on environment-enhanced node representations. A distinctive aspect is the use of class-specific learnable prompt tokens (L vectors of dimension d, one per class) that are shared across all samples and jointly optimized, and the environment embedding A_N ∈ R^{L×d} is inferred at the graph level but must be propagated to individual nodes through cross-attention before subgraph extraction.

## Method

NeGo introduces a negative inference paradigm for graph OOD generalization with two coupled components. First, a negative prompter (a cross-attention Transformer decoder) takes the GNN-encoded graph embedding Z_G and class-specific learnable prompt tokens P, producing environment embeddings A_N that are trained via a negative loss to match extra-class samples and mismatch intra-class samples, thereby modeling a far broader environment space than spurious subgraphs alone. Second, an environment-enhanced invariant learning module propagates A_N to node level through an attention-based residual connection (softmax(Z^Q(A^K)^T/√d)·A^V + Z), then feeds the enriched node representations into a subgraph extractor to isolate the causal subgraph G_C for prediction. The overall training objective is L = L_nega + λ·L_posi, where L_nega is a KL divergence pulling the inferred environment toward the extra-class distribution and L_posi combines causal subgraph extraction with label prediction. Theoretically, the paper proves that this negative inference objective (max I(E; G_C | G̅^Y)) encompasses a strictly broader environment cognitive space than positive inference, with its upper bound being the ground-truth environment distribution, and that causal subgraph extraction is guaranteed under both FIIF and PIIF assumptions.

## Results

On the four chemical OOD benchmarks (Table 1), NeGo achieves the best ROC-AUC on 7 out of 8 evaluation settings, reaching 75.21% (ID val) and 75.87% (OOD val) on GOOD-HIV-scaffold, 73.20%/73.94% on DrugOOD-assay, and 66.91% on DrugOOD-size OOD val. On sentiment datasets (Table 2), NeGo attains 84.16% accuracy on GOOD-SST2 OOD val and 61.25% on GOOD-Twitter OOD val, outperforming all baselines. On synthetic datasets (Table 8), NeGo leads on GOOD-Motif-size (72.65%) and GOOD-CMNIST-color (53.28%), though LECI edges ahead on GOOD-Motif-basis (84.56% vs. 83.96%) and GOOD-CMNIST-covariate (83.20% vs. 82.43%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

NeGo outperforms the two strongest environment-centered baselines, LECI and GALA, on 7 of 8 chemical dataset splits (Table 1), with the largest margin on GOOD-HIV-scaffold OOD val (+1.44% over LECI) and DrugOOD-size OOD val (+0.42% over LECI). On sentiment graphs (Table 2), NeGo leads all baselines on both GOOD-SST2 OOD val (84.16% vs. LECI's 83.44%) and GOOD-Twitter OOD val (61.25% vs. GALA's 60.45%). On synthetic datasets (Table 8), NeGo wins on GOOD-Motif-size (72.65% vs. LECI 71.43%) and GOOD-CMNIST-color (53.28% vs. GALA 52.68%), but trails LECI on GOOD-Motif-basis (83.96% vs. 84.56%) and GOOD-CMNIST-covariate (82.43% vs. 83.20%). Among the earlier invariant-learning baselines (DIR, GSAT, CAL, CIGA, GIL), NeGo consistently achieves the highest scores across all six real-world datasets, with gaps of 3–10 points over the weakest methods (DIR, CIGA). The general-purpose OOD baselines (ERM, IRM, V-Rex, IB-IRM) are uniformly outperformed by 5–15 points, confirming that environment-aware modeling is critical for graph OOD generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

NeGo builds directly on the environment-centered invariant learning lineage (GIL, CIGA, LECI, GALA), adopting their FIIF/PIIF structural causal model framework and the environment mixture assumption from Li et al. (2022). Its key conceptual departure is reframing environment inference from a 'positive' paradigm (extracting spurious subgraphs as the environment space) to a 'negative' paradigm (modeling all extra-class samples as the environment), which the authors argue broadens the inference space beyond the dimension of spurious subgraphs. The mechanism for implementing this negative inference is borrowed from prompt learning in NLP (Brown et al., 2020; Gao et al., 2020), repurposing learnable prompt tokens as class-specific environment probes — a first application of prompt learning to graph OOD generalization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a concrete and well-motivated limitation in existing environment-centered OOD methods (narrow inference space) and provides both a theoretical argument (Proposition 4.3) and empirical evidence (performance collapse under doubled spurious substructures) for why it matters. However, the practical gains over strong baselines like LECI and GALA are modest (typically 1–2% ROC-AUC), and the method's reliance on class-level extra-class samples limits its applicability to binary classification settings, as the authors themselves acknowledge. Open questions include whether the negative inference paradigm extends to multi-task or cross-dataset transfer, and whether the prompt-based environment inference can be made class-agnostic.
