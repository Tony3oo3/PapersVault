## Problem

Bilevel optimization with blackbox, noisy, constrained, and derivative-free functions remains largely unsolved: existing bilevel Bayesian optimization methods rely on nested frameworks that repeatedly solve the lower-level problem at each upper-level query, which is sample-inefficient and typically requires gradient information. No prior method simultaneously optimizes both levels without repeated lower-level optimization while providing theoretical regret guarantees for general bilevel problems with constraints.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250395/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

BILBO takes discretized upper-level domain X ⊂ R^{d_x} and lower-level domain Z ⊂ R^{d_z} (uniform grid), initial noisy observations for all functions in F = {F, f} ∪ C_up ∪ C_lo, and iteratively queries exactly one function per iteration in a decoupled setting. It outputs the estimated optimal bilevel solution (x̂_T, ẑ_T) selected as the point minimizing the maximum estimated regret across all functions, along with GP surrogate models for each function. The search space is reduced at each iteration to the intersection of two trusted sets, and an infeasibility declaration is issued if the trusted feasible set becomes empty.

## Method

BILBO constructs two confidence-bound-based trusted sets: S_t^+ (points whose upper confidence bounds on all constraints are non-negative, bounding constraint regret) and P_t^+ (points whose upper confidence bound on the lower-level objective f is at least the lower confidence bound at the estimated optimal lower-level solution z̄_t(x), bounding lower-level suboptimality). The query point is selected as argmax of the upper confidence bound of F over S_t^+ ∩ P_t^+, and the function to query is chosen by maximizing the estimated regret across all functions, where the lower-level objective's estimated regret includes an additional uncertainty term σ_f(x_t, z̄_t(x_t)) that captures uncertainty in the estimated lower-level solution. A conditional reassignment step then redirects the lower-level query to z̄_t(x_t) when its GP uncertainty exceeds that of the originally selected z_t, effectively replacing repeated lower-level optimization with targeted exploration of the lower-level objective. This yields a sublinear cumulative regret bound of O(√(T|F|β_T max_h C_h γ_{h,T})) and a simple regret bound of O(√(|F|β_T max_h C_h γ_{h,T}/T)) for common kernels.

## Results

BILBO is evaluated on 4 synthetic and 2 real-world bilevel problems, with performance measured by instantaneous regret (sum of per-function regrets) against query count, averaged over 5 runs. On BraninHoo+GoldsteinPrice, BILBO converges to the optimal bilevel solution within ~150 queries, substantially outperforming both baselines. On SMD6, BILBO achieves the smallest regret after approximately 250 steps, and on SMD12 (with 8 functions and active constraints), BILBO converges faster than TrustedRand, demonstrating the effectiveness of its decoupled function query strategy. On the real-world Energy problem, BILBO's regret decreases fastest, though it exhibits an empirical asymptotic regret floor due to a discontinuity in the lower-level objective that the GP surrogate cannot fully capture.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BILBO is compared against two baselines: **TrustedRand**, which randomly samples query points from the same trusted sets (isolating the contribution of BILBO's informed query selection), and **Nested**, which uses SLSQP for lower-level optimization and UCB-BO for the upper level (representing existing nested bilevel BO approaches like Kieffer et al. 2017 and Dogan & Prestwich 2023). BILBO outperforms both baselines on all six problems. Against TrustedRand, BILBO's advantage comes from its function query strategy (selecting the most uncertain function) and conditional reassignment of the lower-level query, which are especially impactful on SMD12 where 8 functions make the decoupled setting crucial for sample efficiency. Against Nested, BILBO's advantage is most pronounced on multimodal problems (BraninHoo+GoldsteinPrice) and problems with multiple lower-level optimal solutions (SMD6), where Nested's SLSQP solver converges to suboptimal lower-level solutions. Nested cannot handle constraints and is therefore excluded from SMD12 and Chemical experiments. In wall-clock time, BILBO is ~1.5× slower per iteration than TrustedRand and ~26× slower than Nested, but its superior regret reduction more than compensates, as Nested's regret plateaus quickly due to suboptimal lower-level estimates.

## Relation to Prior Work

BILBO extends the GP-UCB framework of Srinivas et al. (2010) and the confidence-bound trusted sets from constrained BO (Nguyen et al., 2023; Xu et al., 2023) to the bilevel setting, where the trusted set concept is generalized to simultaneously bound constraint violations and lower-level suboptimality. It departs fundamentally from the dominant nested bilevel BO paradigm (Kieffer et al., 2017; Dogan & Prestwich, 2023; Fu et al., 2024), which applies BO only at the upper level and solves the lower level separately—typically requiring gradients—by instead optimizing both levels simultaneously through a single query per iteration with conditional reassignment. The method also subsumes the constrained BO trusted-set machinery, extending its regret bounds from the query point to all points in the trusted set to facilitate composition with the lower-level trusted set.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

BILBO provides the first bilevel BO algorithm with sublinear regret guarantees for derivative-free, noisy, constrained black-box functions, filling a gap where prior methods either required lower-level gradients or lacked theoretical analysis. The conditional reassignment mechanism offers a principled alternative to repeated lower-level optimization, which is sample-inefficient and inapplicable to discrete or non-smooth lower-level problems. Key limitations include reliance on discretized search spaces (limiting scalability to high dimensions), and the energy experiment revealed that sharp discontinuities in the lower-level objective can produce asymptotic regret, suggesting the need for explicit constraint modeling of such features. Open questions include extending to continuous domains, adaptive discretization, and directly modeling F(x, z*(x)) to reduce surrogate model dimensionality.
