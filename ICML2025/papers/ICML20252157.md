## Problem

Existing implicit neural representation (INR) methods for flow field reconstruction rely on an impractical grid-independence assumption that fails for real simulation data, where attribute values at the same location differ across mesh resolutions and numerical accuracies. Additionally, no standardized large-scale benchmark exists for high-fidelity flow reconstruction, and current INR frameworks struggle to resolve fine-scale structures and spatiotemporal dynamics under severe temporal-spatial data imbalance, compounded by spectral bias that suppresses high-frequency flow features.

## Input / Output

The method consumes spatiotemporal coordinates (augmented with neighboring point coordinates for stencil-based spatial encoding) and a low-fidelity flow field that is first upscaled via bicubic interpolation to match the target resolution. The network outputs the residual error field δF = F^H − F^B, which is added to the interpolated low-fidelity field to yield the reconstructed high-fidelity field. The HFR-Bench dataset spans 2D and 3D unsteady flows with upscaling factors up to 64× and numerical-precision improvements up to 7th-order WENO, on both uniform Cartesian and non-uniform structured meshes.

## Method

PEINR decouples temporal and spatial processing through physical encoding: temporal inputs are mapped via a Gaussian RBF kernel followed by Kernel PCA to produce translation-invariant, nonlinear temporal features that address the temporal-spatial dimension disparity, while spatial coordinates are expanded to include nearest-neighbor points (4 in 2D, 9 in 3D) to mirror stencil-based CFD discretization. These encoded features are fused by TransSTF, a transformer-based spatiotemporal fuser combining multi-head attention for long-range temporal dependencies, ResuMLP blocks with SIREN activations for gradient propagation, and a spectral block (FFT-based frequency gating) to counteract spectral bias and recover high-frequency flow structures. The network is trained end-to-end with MSE loss under supervised learning, learning the continuous mapping from encoded spatiotemporal coordinates to the error field, with the final high-fidelity reconstruction obtained by adding the predicted error to the bicubic-interpolated low-fidelity input.

## Results

PEINR achieves substantial improvements over both baselines across all five flow problems. On the 2D FFS dataset (α=16, β=1), PEINR reaches PSNR 74.15, SSIM 99.84, and DD 4.61, compared to CoordNet (PSNR 42.71, DD 98.46) and NIF (PSNR 35.09, DD 2991.72). On the 3D SV dataset (α=64, β=1.4), PEINR attains PSNR 45.26 and DD 4.21, outperforming CoordNet (PSNR 27.34, DD 21.96) and NIF (PSNR 27.47, DD 20.75) by roughly 18 dB in PSNR. The only metric where PEINR is slightly inferior is PSNR on the RM dataset (44.22 vs. CoordNet's 45.34), attributed to PEINR's deliberate preservation of sharp discontinuities at the cost of a mean-squared-error-based metric.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PEINR outperforms both NIF (Pan et al., JMLR 2023) and CoordNet (Han & Wang, IEEE TVCG 2023) on the majority of metrics across all five flow problems. The most dramatic gains appear on FFS (PSNR 74.15 vs. 42.71 for CoordNet, a 31 dB improvement) and Cylinder (PSNR 40.04 vs. 21.80, an 18 dB improvement), where the non-uniform mesh and complex vortex structures challenge grid-based baselines. On the 3D SV dataset with the largest upscaling factor (α=64), PEINR achieves PSNR 45.26 versus ~27.4 for both baselines, confirming that the transformer-based spatiotemporal fuser and spectral block effectively handle high-dimensional reconstruction. The sole exception is PSNR on the RM dataset, where CoordNet edges out PEINR by 1.1 dB (45.34 vs. 44.22); however, PEINR's DD is 8.7× lower (2.51 vs. 21.80), indicating far superior preservation of shock discontinuities. NIF consistently lags behind both PEINR and CoordNet on every dataset and metric, with particularly poor DD values (e.g., 2991.72 on FFS), reflecting its inability to capture fine-scale turbulent structures.

## Relation to Prior Work

PEINR extends the INR-based flow field reconstruction line (NIF, CoordNet) by incorporating two key architectural components: ResuMLP from Han & Wang (2022) for gradient-stable feature extraction, and the spectral block from SpectFormer (Patro et al., 2023) to counteract spectral bias. It departs from prior INR methods in two conceptual ways: (1) it rejects the grid-independence assumption that underlies existing INR flow reconstruction, instead learning the error field between low- and high-fidelity simulations, and (2) it introduces physics-motivated encoding—stencil-based spatial neighborhood expansion and Gaussian RBF + Kernel PCA temporal encoding—that explicitly mirrors CFD discretization logic. The paper also positions itself as the first to jointly enhance both grid resolution and numerical precision (WENO order), whereas prior INR methods addressed only resolution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 5.4 TB HFR-Bench dataset is the most practically significant contribution, providing the first standardized large-scale benchmark for high-fidelity flow field reconstruction across uniform and non-uniform meshes, multiple WENO orders, and 2D/3D problems—filling a gap the authors identify as a barrier to broader INR adoption in CFD. The demonstration that the grid-independence assumption fails in real simulation data (Figure 1) is an important corrective for the ML-for-CFD community. However, the method remains tailored to specific canonical flow scenarios, and the authors acknowledge that generalization to out-of-distribution flows (e.g., different geometries, Reynolds numbers) is an open question they plan to address via meta-learning. The spectral block's contribution, while validated in ablation, is borrowed from vision and its transferability to other scientific INR tasks remains untested.
