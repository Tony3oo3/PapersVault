## Problem

Existing LLM-based automatic heuristic design (AHD) methods rely on population-based evolutionary computation that discards underperforming heuristics each iteration, preventing them from being further refined and causing premature convergence to local optima. This paper asks whether a tree-structured search can more comprehensively explore the heuristic space by preserving and developing temporarily inferior heuristics alongside top performers.

## Input / Output

The method takes a task description, a predefined general framework (e.g., step-by-step construction, ACO, GLS, or BO), an evaluation dataset D, and access to a pre-trained LLM (GPT-3.5-turbo or GPT-4o-mini). It produces a single Python heuristic function with a linguistic description, designed to operate within the given framework. The search is bounded by a total evaluation budget T (typically 1,000 heuristic evaluations on D, each with a 60-second per-instance time limit), and the LLM is called twice per action: once for code generation and once for a short thought-alignment description.

## Method

MCTS-AHD replaces the population with an MCTS tree where each node stores a complete Python heuristic function and its description, and the root is a virtual node. Node selection uses a normalized UCT score (Eq. 5) with a linearly decaying exploration factor λ (exploration-decay, Eq. 7) to balance broad early exploration against late-stage convergence. Expansion employs six LLM-prompted actions—initialization (i1), two mutations (m1 for new mechanisms, m2 for parameter changes), two crossovers (e1 from multiple subtrees, e2 from a parent plus an elite reference), and a novel tree-path reasoning action (s1) that analyzes the full evolution history along a root-to-leaf path. Progressive widening (α=0.5) gradually adds new children to non-leaf nodes as visit counts grow, enabling re-exploration with updated elite references. Backpropagation propagates the maximum child quality and summed visit counts upward, and a thought-alignment step generates a concise description after each code generation to mitigate LLM hallucination between code and prose.

## Results

On TSP (step-by-step construction, GPT-4o-mini), MCTS-AHD achieves optimality gaps of 9.69%, 11.79%, and 13.71% for N=50, 100, and 200 nodes respectively, outperforming EoH (12.67%, 14.49%, 16.68%) and Funsearch (12.00%, 13.93%, 15.54%). On KP, MCTS-AHD reaches gaps as low as 0.04% (N=200), compared to 0.09% for both EoH and Funsearch. Under the ACO framework, MCTS-AHD achieves 0.00% gap on TSP N=50 and N=100, and 0.00% on MKP N=200, surpassing DeepACO (0.71% and 1.20% respectively) and all LLM-based baselines. For BO CAF design, MCTS-AHD dramatically outperforms EoH on Rosenbrock (1.68% vs 56.78%) and Powell (1.27% vs 70.89%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MCTS-AHD consistently outperforms all LLM-based AHD baselines (Funsearch, EoH, ReEvo, HSEvo) on TSP across all three scales under both step-by-step construction and ACO frameworks, with the largest margin on TSP N=200 (13.71% vs 16.68% for EoH, a 3-point improvement). On KP, MCTS-AHD achieves the best gap in all three scales with GPT-4o-mini (0.04–0.11% vs 0.09–0.24% for baselines). Under the ACO framework, MCTS-AHD matches or beats DeepACO on TSP (0.00% vs 0.71% at N=50) and MKP (0.00% vs 1.20% at N=200), but trails slightly on CVRP N=100 (5.70% vs EoH's 5.02%) and offline BPP N=500 (0.48% vs DeepACO's 0.00%). For online BPP, MCTS-AHD achieves the best average gap (0.89%) over Funsearch (0.99%), EoH (1.17%), ReEvo (1.71%), and HSEvo (1.13%). In BO CAF design, MCTS-AHD wins on 6 of 12 synthetic instances versus EoH, with dramatic margins on Rosenbrock (1.68% vs 56.78%) and Powell (1.27% vs 70.89%), while EoH retains an edge on a few instances like Hartmann (0.01% vs 0.04%). Overall, MCTS-AHD demonstrates clear superiority on the majority of test sets, particularly where the heuristic space is complex enough to benefit from tree-structured exploration.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the population-based evolutionary computation paradigm that underpins existing LLM-based AHD methods (Funsearch, EoH, ReEvo, HSEvo) by replacing the fixed-size population with a Monte Carlo Tree Search structure over all generated heuristics. It builds directly on the UCT selection mechanism (Kocsis & Szepesvári, 2006) and the progressive widening technique (Coulom, 2007) from the MCTS literature, adapting them to the heuristic-design setting where node quality is measured by task performance rather than game value. The LLM-based action set (mutation, crossover, and a novel tree-path reasoning action) extends the prompting strategies introduced by EoH, while the thought-alignment process addresses a known hallucination issue in LLM code-description generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that preserving temporarily underperforming heuristics in a tree structure (rather than discarding them as population methods do) enables more comprehensive exploration of the heuristic space and avoids premature convergence, yielding consistently better heuristics across six NP-hard CO problems and a Bayesian Optimization task. The tree-path reasoning action (s1) is a genuinely novel use of the MCTS structure to provide LLMs with organized evolutionary history as context. A stated limitation is that MCTS-AHD's convergence speed could be improved, and the authors suggest MCTS-Population hybrid methods as future work; the broader open question is whether tree-based search can scale to even larger heuristic spaces or multi-objective settings without prohibitive evaluation budgets.
