## Problem

Adaptive optimizers like Adam treat neural networks as black boxes, adapting the geometry of the problem on-the-fly from gradients without exploiting the known structure of the architecture. This paper asks whether one can instead adapt a priori by choosing a norm that matches the natural geometry of the network, and shows that linear minimization oracles (LMOs) over norm-balls—classically a tool for constrained optimization—can be applied to unconstrained problems to yield a unified, theoretically grounded optimizer family.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard model parameters and stochastic gradients (one set of weights and one set of gradients, the latter stored in half-precision, requiring no additional moment buffers as in Adam). The LMO is computed independently per layer using operator norms (spectral, sign, or column-norm), so the update is dense but layer-local. The output is the updated parameter set; for the constrained SCION variant, parameters are additionally guaranteed to remain within a prescribed norm ball, providing explicit norm control useful for robustness and generalization.

## Method

The core algorithm, uSCG (unconstrained Stochastic Conditional Gradient), replaces the standard gradient step with x^{k+1} = x^k + γ·lmo(d^k), where d^k is a momentum-averaged stochastic gradient and lmo is the linear minimization oracle over a chosen norm ball. Because the LMO is scale-invariant, every update has the same magnitude regardless of gradient size, making the stepsize independent of the Lipschitz constant and yielding O(n^{-1/4}) convergence under nonconvexity. For deep networks, the paper selects operator norms per layer—spectral norm (√(d_out/d_in)·UV^⊤ via Newton-Schultz) for hidden layers and sign (elementwise ±1) for input/output layers—which automatically satisfies the spectral scaling condition for width-invariant hyperparameter transfer. This framework unifies Muon, SignSGD, Normalized SGD, and Shampoo as special cases (Table 1), and the constrained variant SCION adds explicit norm-ball feasibility, connecting weight decay to a radius rescaling.

## Results

On a 3B-parameter GPT model trained on FineWeb, UNCONSTRAINED SCION achieves the lowest validation loss of 2.882, outperforming SCION (2.890), Muon (2.909), and Adam (3.024). On ViT/ImageNet, SCION requires 30% fewer epochs and delivers a >40% wallclock speedup due to a larger critical batch size. At batch size 6144 on the 124M NanoGPT model, SCION matches Muon's validation loss with 25% less wallclock time, and its optimal stepsize transfers across model widths from 64M to 1B parameters without retuning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SCION consistently outperforms both baselines across all evaluated settings. Against Adam, UNCONSTRAINED SCION achieves a 0.142 lower validation loss on the 3B GPT model (2.882 vs. 3.024), and Adam's optimal learning rate does not transfer across model widths, requiring retuning at each size. Against Muon, SCION achieves a 0.027 lower validation loss on the 3B model (2.882 vs. 2.909) and a 0.004 lower loss on the 124M model (2.984 vs. 2.988), while also eliminating Muon's reliance on AdamW for the first and last layers. A key practical advantage is that SCION's optimal stepsize is invariant to model width (64M→1B), whereas both Adam and Muon require width-dependent retuning. On large-batch training (batch 6144), SCION is notably more robust than Muon, matching its validation loss with 25% less wallclock time.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies a family of norm-based optimizers—Muon, SignSGD, Normalized SGD, and Shampoo—under a single linear minimization oracle (LMO) framework, showing they are all special cases of a new unconstrained Stochastic Conditional Gradient (uSCG) method. It departs from the classical use of LMOs in constrained optimization (Frank-Wolfe/CG) by demonstrating they are equally effective for unconstrained problems, and builds on the operator norm perspective of Large et al. (2024) and the spectral scaling rule of Yang et al. (2023) to propose specific norm choices per layer that enable zero-shot hyperparameter transfer. The connection to steepest descent via the sharp operator is made explicit, clarifying that uSCG is a normalized variant that avoids computing the dual norm and is agnostic to the Lipschitz constant.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: SCION eliminates the need for Adam entirely, requiring only one set of weights and one half-precision gradient (no second-moment state), which is critical for memory-constrained large-scale training. The demonstrated hyperparameter transfer across model widths (64M to 3B) addresses a major pain point in scaling experiments, and the 30% epoch reduction on ViT/ImageNet with >40% wallclock speedup suggests real efficiency gains. Open questions include whether the specific operator norm choices generalize to architectures beyond MLPs and transformers (e.g., Mamba, state-space models), whether the theoretical O(n^{-1/4}) rates are tight in practice, and whether the method's advantage over Muon persists at even larger scales (10B+) where the current 3B comparison is the largest evidence point.
