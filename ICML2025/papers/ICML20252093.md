## Problem

Auditing multiclass classifiers for fairness under multiclass equalized odds requires quantifying the deviation from fairness, but the natural generalization of the Disparate Conditional Prediction (DCP) measure to multiclass settings leads to a non-convex, non-smooth constrained optimization problem with no known efficient exact solution. This paper provides the first practical bounding methods for multiclass DCP, enabling interpretable fairness auditing even when the classifier is proprietary or individual-level data is unavailable.

## Input / Output

The method operates in two regimes: (1) when conditional confusion matrices (k×k per sub-population) are known, it takes these matrices along with sub-population weights w_a and true-label proportions π_a to produce upper and lower bounds on DCP; (2) when only population-level frequencies (true and predicted label proportions per sub-population) are available, it produces bounds on minDCP, the best-case DCP over all confusion matrices consistent with those frequencies. The output is interpretable as a fraction of the population receiving disparate conditional predictions, making it directly comparable across classifiers with different numbers of classes, protected attribute values, or degrees of class imbalance.

## Method

The core technique is a sequential linear programming (SLP) approach that handles the non-smooth max-of-concave-η structure in the DCP objective. The η function is split into two smooth components (η₁ = 1 − b/α and η₂ = 1 − (1−b)/(1−α)), and at each iteration a first-order Taylor linearization around the current iterate converts the problem into a standard LP with highly sparse constraint matrices. For the no-confusion-matrix regime, the optimization jointly over the baseline matrix and all sub-population confusion matrices, with the coupling constraint M_a^T π_a = p̂_a introducing cross-label dependencies handled within the same LP framework. A greedy initialization procedure exploits the monotonicity of DCP under label merging to build the baseline confusion matrix label-by-label, solving each step as a one-dimensional concave minimization, which empirically outperforms simple averaging initialization.

## Results

For multiclass classifiers with known confusion matrices, the proposed Greedy+LM local optimization method achieves upper bounds on DCP that are within a factor of 1.00–2.85 of the analytical lower bound across 17 US Census classifiers and 4 Natality classifiers, with most ratios at or near 1.00 (exact). For the Natality data set (high-error regime, 22–31% classifier error), the best ratio is between 1.00 and 1.58. When confusion matrices are unavailable, the minDCP local optimizer produces values consistently below the true DCP range in all but one case, confirming it as a valid best-case lower estimate. DCP values themselves range from 0.01% to 8.29% (lower bounds) and up to 23.61% (upper bounds) depending on the classifier and protected attribute.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares four upper-bound estimation strategies: (1) Average (weighted mean of sub-population confusion matrices), (2) Greedy (label-by-label binary optimization), (3) Average+LM, and (4) Greedy+LM (the proposed method). Greedy+LM dominates in 16 out of 17 US Census decision-tree classifiers and all 4 Natality classifiers, yielding the tightest upper bound in every case except one. The Average method is consistently the worst, producing upper bounds 5–20× the lower bound (e.g., 84.54% vs. 8.29% lower bound for the 8-label classifier), while Greedy+LM typically stays within 1.00–1.5× of the lower bound. The Greedy initialization alone (without local minimization) is competitive but slightly looser than Greedy+LM in most cases. For the minDCP setting (no confusion matrices), the local optimizer's output is below the true DCP range in all but one experiment, validating it as a meaningful best-case estimate rather than an overestimate.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends the Disparate Conditional Prediction (DCP) measure of Sabato & Yom-Tov (2020) from binary to multiclass classifiers, generalizing the binary DCP formula (Eq. 3) to a multiclass formulation (Eq. 5) involving a max over predicted labels. It builds on the multiclass equalized odds framework established by Putzel & Lee (2022) and Alghamdi et al. (2022), but departs from the difference- or ratio-based deviation measures used in those works by retaining DCP's interpretable 'fraction of population' semantics. The optimization methodology draws on sequential linear programming (Nocedal & Wright, 2006) and the Charalambous-Conn reformulation for handling max terms, applied here to a non-convex, non-smooth objective that had no known efficient solution in the multiclass setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This provides the first practical tool for auditing multiclass classifiers for equalized-odds fairness without access to individual-level data or the classifier itself, which is critical for proprietary systems (e.g., health insurance, hiring). The DCP measure's consistent interpretability as a population fraction—unlike difference or ratio measures that suffer from artifacts with rare labels or varying class counts—makes it suitable for cross-classifier and cross-domain comparison. Key limitations include the reliance on local optimization (no global optimality guarantee), the potential for loose bounds in highly non-convex regimes, and the assumption that population-level frequencies are accurately estimated. Open questions include whether the bound gap can be further tightened, how the methods scale to very large numbers of classes or subpopulations, and whether the approach extends to other fairness criteria beyond equalized odds.
