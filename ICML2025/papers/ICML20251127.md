## Problem

SAM's class- and task-agnostic design introduces a unique source of uncertainty—task ambiguity—that existing UQ methods (ensembles, BNNs, test-time augmentation) neither model nor can address post-hoc. Current approaches either require expensive retraining, are computationally prohibitive for foundation models, or conflate distinct uncertainty sources, making it impossible to determine whether a prediction failure stems from an under-parameterized model, an insufficient prompt, or an inherently ambiguous task.

## Input / Output

USAM consumes SAM's 256-dimensional mask token and 256-dimensional IoU token (concatenated to 512 dimensions) extracted from the mask decoder's latent representation, requiring no additional forward passes or augmentations at inference. It outputs four scalar uncertainty scores per image: predictive uncertainty (1 − expected IoU), epistemic/model uncertainty (expected IoU gap between Large and Tiny SAM), prompt uncertainty (expected IoU gap between a single-coordinate and a refined multi-point prompt), and task uncertainty (expected IoU gap between the SamScore-selected mask and the ground-truth-best mask). The Bayesian baseline additionally requires 5 image augmentations, 8 sampled prompts, 3 mask proposals, and 4 model sizes per evaluation.

## Method

The paper first derives a Bayesian entropy formulation (Eq. 2) that decomposes SAM's predictive uncertainty into epistemic model uncertainty, aleatoric prompt uncertainty, and a newly defined aleatoric task uncertainty, approximated via Monte Carlo sampling over model sizes, prompts, augmentations, and mask proposals. To make this practical, USAM trains six lightweight 3-layer MLPs (512 hidden units, sigmoid activation) post-hoc on SAM's concatenated mask and IoU tokens, each minimizing MSE against a specific IoU gap target (e.g., Tiny-vs-Large IoU for model uncertainty, single-vs-refined prompt IoU for prompt uncertainty, SamScore-vs-best-mask IoU for task uncertainty). A second set of MLPs (denoted Δ*) directly regresses the gap without first predicting the individual IoUs, reducing computation further. The key insight is that SAM's latent tokens already encode sufficient signal to deterministically estimate all three uncertainty sources, eliminating the need for expensive sampling while matching or exceeding the Bayesian approximation's accuracy across five datasets.

## Results

USAM achieves state-of-the-art uncertainty quantification across all five datasets. For overall segmentation uncertainty (Table 4), USAM_L reaches 91.59% (DAVIS), 92.60% (ADE20k), 92.78% (MOSE), 90.01% (COCO), and 89.37% (SA-V) relative AUC, outperforming the best Bayesian baseline H_Xp (87.16/83.79/86.23/79.13/83.23) and SAM's built-in SamScore (83.36/75.40/89.48/79.60/80.80). The most dramatic gain appears in task uncertainty (Table 3), where USAM Δ_A scores ~94% relative AUC across all datasets versus 58–76% for all competing methods. USAM adds negligible runtime overhead (e.g., 0.441 s vs. 0.437 s for SAM-Large alone on an RTX 3050 Ti), compared to 2.187 s for MC-based Bayesian sampling with |T|=5 augmentations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

USAM consistently outperforms SAM's built-in SamScore across all four uncertainty tasks and all five datasets. For overall segmentation uncertainty, USAM_L exceeds SamScore by 8.2–18.2 percentage points in relative AUC (e.g., 92.60% vs. 75.40% on ADE20k). Against the best Bayesian entropy baseline H_Xp, USAM_L leads by 4.4–13.2 points (e.g., 92.78% vs. 86.23% on MOSE). In the task uncertainty setting, USAM Δ_A achieves ~94% relative AUC while the best competing method (mask entropy H_Std) reaches only 68–76%, a gap of 18–26 points. The Bayesian predictive entropy H_Y performs worst overall (38–57% relative AUC), confirming that aggregating all uncertainty sources without decomposition is counterproductive. USAM also matches or slightly exceeds the targeted Bayesian entropies (H_Θ for model uncertainty, H_Xp for prompt uncertainty) while being 5–15× faster at inference, making it the preferred practical choice.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the standard Bayesian UQ decomposition (aleatoric + epistemic) to the SAM setting by introducing two new uncertainty sources specific to class-agnostic, prompt-based segmentation: aleatoric prompt uncertainty (ambiguity in the user's coordinate input) and aleatoric task uncertainty (ambiguity in which object the prompt refers to). The Bayesian entropy approximation draws on established MC sampling techniques (ensembles, test-time augmentation, multi-mask proposals), while the practical USAM method follows the deterministic post-hoc estimation paradigm of DEUP and Compensation Learning, but operates on SAM's 256-dim mask and IoU tokens rather than raw features. The key conceptual departure is the formalization of Equation (2), which explicitly models the prompt encoder and decoder as separate uncertainty sources within the predictive distribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

USAM provides a near-zero-overhead UQ method (Table 7: 0.441s vs. 2.187s for MC sampling on Large SAM) that tells users not just *how* uncertain a prediction is, but *why*—insufficient model capacity, inadequate prompting, or genuinely ambiguous task—enabling targeted interventions (switch to larger model, refine prompt, request supervision). A notable limitation is that training the MLPs requires ground-truth masks, and performance on the small-scale DAVIS dataset is weaker, suggesting data diversity matters. Open questions include whether integrating UQ objectives directly into SAM's pretraining (rather than post-hoc) would yield further gains, and whether the decomposition generalizes to SAM 2 or other prompt-based foundation models.
