## Problem

Estimating density ratios via probability paths requires learning the time score ∂_t log p_t(x), but the existing Time Score Matching (TSM) objective is computationally expensive due to double automatic differentiation (first in t, then in θ) and can yield inaccurate estimates in high dimensions. Moreover, prior work lacked theoretical guarantees on the resulting density ratio estimator, and the weighting function used (Stein score normalization) was not directly aligned with the time score being learned.

## Input / Output

The method consumes i.i.d. samples from two densities p0 (typically a known Gaussian) and p1 (the target, e.g., image data), along with a conditioning variable z (e.g., a raw data sample x1) that is available during training but marginalized out at inference. The output is a neural network s_θ(x,t) parameterizing the time score; integrating its predictions over t∈[0,1] yields log(p1/p0), which combined with log p0 gives the target density. The network can also be used for sampling via annealed MCMC or probability flow ODE by differentiating the learned time score to obtain Stein scores.

## Method

The key insight is to augment the state space with a conditioning variable z so that the intractable marginal path p_t(x) decomposes into a mixture of tractable conditional paths p_t(x|z) (e.g., Gaussians), making the conditional time score ∂_t log p_t(x|z) available in closed form. The CTSM objective (Eq. 8) regresses the network on this conditional time score, and Theorem 1 proves it is equivalent to the original TSM objective up to a constant, eliminating the need for double backpropagation. A vectorized variant (CTSM-v) further decomposes the time score into per-dimension autoregressive terms, enabling a joint multi-dimensional regression that empirically improves accuracy and is essential for trainability on image data. The paper introduces a time score normalization weighting function (inverse variance of the conditional time score) that stabilizes training, and provides a general error bound (Theorem 4) decomposing the KL divergence into discretization error and time-score estimation error, with a finite-sample rate (Proposition 5) that decreases as O(1/N).

## Results

On MNIST energy-based modeling, CTSM-v achieves 1.26 BPD in latent space (vs. TSM's 1.30) at 58 ms/step (vs. TSM's 347 ms/step), and 1.03 BPD in ambient pixel space where TSM training is unstable, at 142 ms/step (vs. TSM's 1103 ms/step). On synthetic Gaussians with D=10, CTSM-v with time score normalization achieves MSE 1.62 compared to TSM with Stein score normalization at MSE 7.34. For mutual information estimation at dimensionality 320, CTSM-v converges to the correct value orders of magnitude faster in terms of optimization steps, while each step is also consistently faster than TSM.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is TSM (Choi et al., 2022), the Time Score Matching objective that this paper directly reformulates. CTSM-v wins on both accuracy and speed across all experiments. On MNIST ambient pixel space, TSM training is completely unstable while CTSM-v achieves 1.03 BPD — surpassing the best previously reported results using pre-trained flows. In latent space, CTSM-v improves BPD from 1.30 to 1.26 while being ~6× faster per step. On synthetic Gaussians at D=10, CTSM-v with time score normalization reduces MSE from 7.34 (TSM+Stein) to 1.62, a 4.5× improvement. For mutual information estimation, CTSM-v converges to the correct value in far fewer optimization steps and each step is 2–3× faster than TSM, with the speed advantage growing in higher dimensions. The non-vectorized CTSM variant is essentially not trainable on MNIST, highlighting the importance of the vectorization step.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the probability-path framework for density ratio estimation introduced by Rhodes et al. (2020) and refined by Choi et al. (2022) into the Time Score Matching (TSM) objective. The key conceptual move is to import the conditioning-variable decomposition from denoising score matching (Vincent, 2011) and flow matching (Lipman et al., 2023) to replace the intractable marginal time score with a tractable conditional one, yielding the CTSM and CTSM-v objectives. The paper also generalizes a theorem that has appeared in specific forms in score matching and flow matching literature to arbitrary functions, and provides the first theoretical error bounds for density ratio estimation via probability paths, addressing a gap left by Rhodes et al. (2020) and Choi et al. (2022).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: CTSM-v achieves orders-of-magnitude speedups over TSM in high dimensions while matching or exceeding accuracy, and the non-vectorized CTSM is essentially untrainable on MNIST, highlighting that the vectorization is not a minor add-on but a critical design choice. The theoretical guarantees (Theorem 4, Proposition 5) give practitioners concrete error bounds depending on discretization steps, sample size, and design choices, which were previously absent. Open questions include finding probability paths that minimize the estimation error (the edge case where conditional and marginal scores coincide), rigorously studying optimal weighting functions, and extending the framework to multi-class classification in the infinitesimal limit.
