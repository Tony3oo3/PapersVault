## Problem

Schneider & Zimmert (2023) proposed an algorithm for cross-learning contextual bandits with adversarial losses and unknown context distributions that achieves near-optimal Õ(√(KT)) regret, but only in expectation. It remained open whether this expected bound is a fundamental limitation of the algorithm or an artifact of the analysis, and whether a high-probability regret guarantee is achievable. This paper resolves the question by showing the algorithm does in fact achieve Õ(√(KT log(1/δ))) regret with probability at least 1−δ.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250991/cropped_page7_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

In each round, the learner observes a context c_t sampled i.i.d. from an unknown distribution ν over [C], selects an arm a_t ∈ [K], and receives the loss ℓ_{t,c}(a_t) for every context c ∈ [C] simultaneously (the cross-learning structure), not just for the current context c_t. Losses are chosen adversarially in [0,1]. The algorithm's parameters (η, γ, L) are set as functions of K, T, and the target confidence δ, and the output is a per-round arm action together with the theoretical guarantee that regret against any fixed policy π is Õ(√(KT log(1/δ))) with probability ≥ 1−δ.

## Method

The paper re-analyzes the epoch-based EXP3/FTRL algorithm of Schneider & Zimmert (2023), whose key design is to estimate importance weights 1/f̂_e(a) from a dedicated sub-epoch so that the estimator is independent of the estimand. The central new insight is that successive epochs are only weakly dependent (each epoch's frequency estimator uses only the previous epoch's samples), so although the per-epoch bias 1/f̂_e(a) − 1/f_e(a) cannot be tightly concentrated, the cumulative bias across all epochs can be bounded via a refined application of Freedman's inequality over an epoch-level filtration. Because the per-epoch bias random variable is not almost-surely bounded, the authors introduce a surrogate sequence (multiplying by an indicator F_e that holds with high probability) to which standard martingale concentration applies, then show the surrogate equals the original sum with high probability. A rearranged regret decomposition—specifically isolating a bias_5 term that was absorbed into bias_2 in the original analysis—is essential to make the high-probability argument close.

## Results

The main theoretical result (Theorem 1) establishes that the algorithm of Schneider & Zimmert (2023) achieves a high-probability regret bound of $\tilde{O}(\sqrt{KT\log(1/\delta)})$ with probability at least $1-\delta$ for any policy $\pi$, improving upon the previously known expected-regret-only guarantee. In the experimental evaluation (Section 5), the paper considers a synthetic adversarial contextual bandit with $T=10^4$ rounds, $K=9$ arms, and $C=1000$ contexts sampled uniformly, with losses drawn from a Bernoulli distribution parameterized by $|\cos(a+c)t\cdot\sin a|$. On this instance, Algorithm 1 consistently achieves significantly lower average cumulative loss than the OBPC baseline and exhibits smaller variance across 10 runs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares Algorithm 1 (the epoch-based EXP3-type algorithm from Schneider & Zimmert, 2023, now analyzed with high-probability guarantees) against the One-Bandit-per-Context (OBPC) baseline from Lattimore & Szepesvári (2020), which maintains $C$ independent EXP3 instances—one per context—and does not share information across contexts. On the synthetic adversarial instance with $T=10^4$, $K=9$, $C=1000$, Algorithm 1 consistently achieves a significantly lower average cumulative loss than OBPC throughout the time horizon (Figure 1). Algorithm 1 is also more robust, showing smaller standard deviation across 10 runs. The OBPC baseline, while minimax-optimal for vanilla adversarial contextual bandits, cannot exploit the cross-learning structure (observing losses for the played arm across all contexts), which is precisely the advantage Algorithm 1 leverages through its importance-weighted loss estimation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper provides a new high-probability analysis of the epoch-based EXP3-type algorithm introduced by Schneider & Zimmert (2023) for cross-learning contextual bandits with unknown context distributions. The original analysis by Schneider & Zimmert deliberately sidestepped high-probability bounds and proved only expected regret, leaving open whether the algorithm was inherently limited to expected guarantees. The key technical departure is exploiting the weak dependency structure between epochs (which was overlooked in prior analyses) and introducing a surrogate sequence to handle unbounded random variables in martingale concentration, enabling the use of refined Freedman and Azuma-Hoeffding inequalities across epochs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

High-probability regret bounds are strictly stronger than expected regret bounds and are the standard for robust online learning guarantees; this result closes a gap by showing the known algorithm is as strong as previously suspected. The technique of leveraging weak inter-epoch dependency with surrogate sequences is a general analytical tool that the authors explicitly suggest could extend to other multi-epoch algorithms in online learning. The experimental validation is minimal (a single synthetic instance), so the practical impact rests entirely on the theoretical contribution. An open question the authors raise is whether this epoch-dependency technique applies to other multi-epoch algorithms beyond cross-learning bandits.
