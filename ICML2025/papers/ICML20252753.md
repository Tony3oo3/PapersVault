## Problem

Feature evolvable learning lacks a formal characterization of how relationships between old and new feature spaces correlate with classifier performance, and existing methods either require storing training data or fix kernel choices without theoretical justification. This paper addresses both gaps by introducing a principled discrepancy measure and a one-pass algorithm that avoids storing any training instances.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252753/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a three-stage data stream: first, instances from the old feature space X^[1] (previous stage); second, paired instances (x^[1], x^[2]) from both spaces (evolving stage, length T_e); and third, instances from the new space X^[2] only (current stage). All instances are processed in a single pass with no storage of the full or partial training set. The output is a binary classifier h_t(x^[2]) that adaptively combines two online kernel models via a sensitivity-weighted ensemble, producing a prediction in {-1, +1}.

## Method

The core contribution is the Kernel Ortho-Mapping (KOM) discrepancy, which measures the gap between two feature spaces by minimizing the Frobenius distance between their square-root Gram matrices over orthogonal transformations, yielding a closed-form expression involving the nuclear norm of the product of square-root Gram matrices. During the evolving stage, the new kernel K^[2] is learned by minimizing the KOM discrepancy via random Fourier features, reducing the problem to a convex optimization over a probability simplex solved by mirror descent in O(1/√T_M) time. In the current stage, two online models are maintained: one using the learned kernel K^[2] (capturing feature relationships) and one using an ideal kernel K^l aligned with label information, combined with adaptive exponential weighting. The previous model is transferred to the new space via an orthogonal transformation derived from the SVD of the cross-feature matrix, providing a theoretically grounded initialization that tightens the regret bound.

## Results

On 20 tabular datasets, OPFES achieves the lowest cumulative error rate (CER) in 15–20 out of 20 runs depending on the baseline, with a win/tie/loss record of 15/5/0 vs. align-FESL, 14/3/3 vs. rff-FESL, and 20/0/0 vs. all kernel and linear baselines (ker-FESL, ker-ROGD, lin-FESL, lin-ROGD, OCDS). Representative CERs include 0.0654 on pol (vs. 0.0807 for rff-ROGD and 0.1655 for lin-ROGD), 0.1354 on letter (vs. 0.2311 for rff-ROGD), and 0.1602 on miniboone (vs. 0.2804 for align-FESL). OPFES is the only method that simultaneously achieves the best CER and the fastest convergence in the current stage across all datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OPFES dominates all kernel-based and linear-based baselines (ker-FESL, ker-ROGD, lin-FESL, lin-ROGD, OCDS) with a perfect 20/0/0 win record on CER, with margins of 5–20 percentage points (e.g., 0.1354 vs. 0.3139 on letter for ker-FESL). Against the closest random-features baselines, OPFES wins 17/1/2 vs. rff-ROGD and 14/3/3 vs. rff-FESL, losing only on the three class-imbalanced datasets (acoustic, runwalk, higgs) where the ideal-kernel component degrades. Against align-FESL (kernel alignment), OPFES wins 15/5/0, confirming that the KOM discrepancy captures feature relationships more effectively than cosine similarity between Gram matrices (Lemma 3.5). The largest absolute gains appear on high-dimensional or imbalanced datasets: miniboone (0.1602 vs. 0.2804 for align-FESL, a 12-point drop) and letter (0.1354 vs. 0.3373 for ker-ROGD, a 20-point drop). Ablation studies confirm that removing the KOM discrepancy or the previous-model initialization causes the largest performance drops (e.g., pol CER jumps from 0.0654 to 0.1290 without KOM, and to 0.1418 without initialization).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the feature evolvable learning line initiated by Hou et al. (2017; 2021; 2022), which studies streaming classification when old features vanish and new ones emerge. Prior methods characterized feature-space relationships via kernel alignment (Cristianini et al., 2001) or ℓ₂ distance (Romero et al., 2015), and typically required storing partial or full training data. The paper departs by introducing the KOM discrepancy—a rotational-invariance-aware kernel distance with a closed-form solution via nuclear norm—and showing it subsumes both prior characterizations (Lemmas 3.5–3.6), while enabling a truly one-pass algorithm using random Fourier features and mirror descent on a simplex.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The KOM discrepancy provides the first principled, theoretically grounded metric linking feature-space relationships to classifier performance gaps in feature evolvable learning, filling a gap the authors identify as a fundamental open problem. The one-pass constraint (no data storage) makes the approach applicable to truly memory-constrained streaming deployments. Limitations include focus on binary classification and shift-invariant kernels, and the ablation reveals the ideal-kernel (label) component contributes only marginal gains, suggesting the feature-relationship signal dominates. The authors note extending KOM to deep learning via neural tangent kernels as a natural next step.
