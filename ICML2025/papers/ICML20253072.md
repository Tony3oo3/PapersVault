## Problem

MLLM-based autonomous driving systems treat numerical control signals as text with standard cross-entropy loss, which fails to penalize predictions proportionally to their numerical distance from ground truth. Additionally, these purely data-driven approaches lack explicit safety verification against traffic rules, allowing the model to hallucinate unsafe or illegal actions, and they underutilize structured environmental knowledge when retrieving relevant past driving experiences.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20253072/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The system consumes a driving video (8 frames for BDD-X) or six multi-view camera images (DriveLM), concatenated with seven historical control signal values (speed, curvature, acceleration, course) formatted as text, and a sequence of three conversational queries (action, justification, control). Outputs are generated autoregressively as text: a high-level action phrase, a justification sentence, and numeric control signals (speed in m/s, course in degrees) formatted to fixed five-digit strings (e.g., '08.100'). For DriveLM, low-level output extends to 2D trajectory waypoints over the next three seconds. The MLN can overwrite the high-level action before the MLLM generates the final response.

## Method

SafeAuto integrates three components into a Video-LLaVA (Vicuna 1.57B) backbone. First, a Position-Dependent Cross-Entropy (PDCE) loss replaces one-hot digit targets with Gaussian soft distributions centered on each ground-truth digit, weighted by the cumulative probability of all preceding digits under their respective Gaussians—this makes the loss decrease monotonically with numerical proximity while preserving autoregressive token generation. Second, a Markov Logic Network encodes traffic rules from the California Driver Handbook as weighted first-order logic formulas over observed environmental predicates (from YOLOv8 detection) and MLLM action predicates (mapped via GPT4o); inference over the limited set of possible action worlds verifies or overrides the MLLM's suggested action. Third, a Multimodal RAG module trains four-layer MLP projectors on video embeddings (LanguageBind), control signal vectors, and binary environmental predicate vectors to produce a unified embedding whose pairwise similarity matrix matches the text-embedding similarity matrix (CLIP-style contrastive objective), enabling retrieval of top-K similar driving scenarios as in-context examples during both training and inference.

## Results

On BDD-X, SafeAuto achieves a speed RMSE of 0.65 and course RMSE of 3.85, reducing RMSE by 5.8% and 14.1% respectively over the SOTA baseline RAGDriver (0.69 / 4.48). For high-level action prediction on BDD-X, SafeAuto scores 337.4 CIDEr (vs. 260.8 for RAGDriver), a 29.4% improvement. On DriveLM, SafeAuto reaches 74.60% high-level behavior accuracy (vs. 61.60% for DriveLM-Agent) and an ADE of 0.84 for motion prediction, a 44.4% reduction over DriveLM-Agent (1.51), approaching the purely low-level UniAD-Full (0.80).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SafeAuto outperforms all BDD-X baselines on low-level control prediction: it beats RAGDriver (the closest competitor) by 5.8% in speed RMSE (0.65 vs. 0.69) and 14.1% in course RMSE (3.85 vs. 4.48), and also surpasses DriveGPT4, TimeLLM, and ADAPT by wide margins. On BDD-X high-level action, SafeAuto leads RAGDriver by 29.4% in CIDEr (337.4 vs. 260.8) and 11.6% in BLEU-4, though it trails RAGDriver slightly on justification CIDEr (96.0 vs. 109.1). On DriveLM, SafeAuto dominates DriveLM-Agent across all metrics: +13.0% high-level accuracy (74.60 vs. 61.60), +16.2% speed accuracy (81.61 vs. 65.40), and a 44.4% ADE reduction (0.84 vs. 1.51). SafeAuto's ADE of 0.84 is also competitive with UniAD-Full (0.80), a system trained purely for low-level prediction, while UniAD-Single (1.80) and BLIP-RT-2 (2.63) are substantially worse. TimeLLM, evaluated only on BDD-X low-level, is outperformed by SafeAuto on both speed (0.65 vs. 1.17 RMSE) and course (3.85 vs. 4.10 RMSE).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the MLLM-based autonomous driving line (DriveGPT4, DriveLM-Agent, Time-LLM) by addressing two gaps those methods leave open: (1) the tension between autoregressive text generation and numerical regression accuracy, solved via a novel Position-Dependent Cross-Entropy loss that mimics MSE behavior while preserving token-level generation; and (2) the absence of explicit safety verification, addressed by encoding traffic rules from the California Driver Handbook into a Markov Logic Network that post-verifies and overrides MLLM actions. The Multimodal RAG component builds on RAGDriver's retrieval idea but replaces triplet-loss metric learning with a CLIP-style ranking objective over a joint embedding of video, control signals, and binary environmental predicates extracted by the MLN pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work is practically important because it provides a plug-and-play safety layer (MLN post-verification) that can be attached to any MLLM-based driving system, directly addressing the hallucination risk that makes purely data-driven approaches unsafe for deployment. The finding that binary environmental predicates are essential for effective multimodal retrieval—without them, RAG actually degrades performance below the base model—suggests a broader principle that structured knowledge can denoise unstructured retrieval. Open questions include whether the MLN approach scales to richer rule sets beyond ~35 formulas, whether the GPT4o-dependent predicate extraction is robust enough for real-time use, and whether the PDCE loss generalizes to other numerical-prediction domains beyond driving.
