## Problem

Existing incomplete multi-view clustering (IMC) methods encode cluster representations using either perspective-shared or perspective-specific determinants, but not both concurrently. This single-paradigm limitation prevents sufficient exploitation of interrelations among data features, restricting clustering performance especially under high missing ratios.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252917/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of incomplete multi-view data matrices {X_r ∈ R^{d_r × n}} with heterogeneous feature dimensions d_r and per-view indicator vectors w_r specifying which of the n total samples are observed on each view (n_r ≤ n). It produces a common clustering embedding E ∈ R^{n×k} from which final cluster assignments are obtained via spectral clustering, along with per-view sample cluster matrices E_r and adaptive view weights a_r that automatically balance contributions across views.

## Method

BACDL bifurcates feature clusters into a perspective-shared component C (common across all views) and perspective-specific components C_r (per view), coupling them via view guidance matrices P_r to form [P_r C | C_r] and thereby resolving cross-view dimension inconsistency. A mutual exclusion (alienation) term ⟨P_r C, C_r⟩ explicitly pushes the two determinants apart to strengthen discrimination. Distribution learning assigns C the role of learning marginal distributions and a unified association matrix D the role of learning conditional distributions, bridging all views through a single shared D. Per-view sample clusters E_r are reordered via orthogonal rotation matrices F_r and mapped to a common E with adaptive weights b_r to form the final clustering embedding. The entire optimization is solved by a nine-step multiplicative updating rule with proven monotonic convergence and overall O(n) time and space complexity.

## Results

On SYNTHREED (3 views, 3 clusters), BACDL achieves the best PUR/ACC/FSC across all missing ratios, e.g., 42.83/42.83/35.24 at 50% missingness, outperforming the next-best IMVCI (39.77/40.68/32.29) by ~3 PUR points. On BGFEA at 50% missingness, BACDL scores 22.64 PUR / 22.40 ACC / 20.16 FSC, edging out LBIMV (22.18/20.73/16.16). On the large-scale YOUFOURV dataset (38,654 samples, 4 views), BACDL is the only method alongside GIMC, PIMVC, USETL, and LBIMV that completes within memory limits, while 7 of 11 baselines (LRTL, TCIMC, AGCIM, LSIMV, IMVCI, HCCGL, UIMC) fail entirely due to complexity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BACDL achieves the best PUR, ACC, and FSC on SYNTHREED at all three missing ratios, with a clear margin of ~2–3 PUR points over the strongest baselines (IMVCI, HCCGL, LBIMV). On BGFEA, BACDL leads in PUR and FSC at all ratios (e.g., 22.64 PUR vs. LBIMV's 22.18 at 50%), though PIMVC edges it slightly on PUR at 20% (22.58 vs. 22.08). On DEOLOG, GIMC surpasses BACDL in PUR at 50% (32.64 vs. 31.84), and BACDL records one sub-optimal FSC at 80% missingness; on AWTEN, BACDL is second-best in most settings. On FLOEVEN and YALTHREE, BACDL trails several baselines in FSC, which the authors attribute to generating cluster labels from the learned spectrum rather than directly from raw data. A key practical advantage is scalability: 7 of 11 baselines (LRTL, TCIMC, AGCIM, LSIMV, IMVCI, HCCGL, UIMC) cannot run on the 38,654-sample YOUFOURV dataset, while BACDL completes with only 3.37 GB memory—the lowest among all methods that succeed.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Non-negative Matrix Tri-factorization (NMTF) framework of Ding et al. (2006) to the incomplete multi-view clustering setting, introducing a 'bifurcate then alienate' paradigm that simultaneously exploits perspective-shared and perspective-specific determinants within a single factorization. Prior IMC methods (LRTL, TCIMC, AGCIM, PIMVC, UIMC, etc.) encode cluster representations using only one type of determinant—either shared across views or specific to individual views—limiting their ability to capture heterogeneous interrelations. BACDL departs from this single-paradigm approach by coupling view guidance matrices into the shared determinant via distribution learning to resolve dimension inconsistency, then applying mutual exclusion to alienate the two determinant types, all while maintaining a unified association matrix across views.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The linear time and space overhead (Theorem 6) is a practically significant advantage: on the largest benchmark (YOUFOURV, 38,654 samples), seven of eleven baselines simply cannot execute, while BACDL completes with 3.37 GB memory. The convergence guarantee (Theorem 5) and explicit lower bound (Theorem 4) provide theoretical grounding that many competing IMC methods lack. Limitations include modest performance gaps on some datasets (FLOEVEN, YALTHREE) attributed to spectral label generation rather than direct data clustering, and the method remains confined to the NMTF family—whether the dual-determinant principle transfers to deep or graph-based IMC frameworks remains an open question.
