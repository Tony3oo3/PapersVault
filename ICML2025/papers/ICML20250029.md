## Problem

Laplacian-based representations in RL have traditionally been learned under a fixed (typically uniform random) policy in a pretraining phase, but this fixed representation may be suboptimal for the evolving policies encountered during training. While Klissarov & Machado (2023) demonstrated empirically that updating the representation online alongside the policy improves exploration and reward, no theoretical convergence guarantees existed for this online setting. This paper asks whether Laplacian representations can be learned online with provable convergence to the true eigenvectors while the policy is simultaneously being updated.

## Input / Output

The encoder is a fully connected neural network (3 hidden layers of 256 units) that maps (x,y) state coordinates to a d-dimensional vector (d=11 in experiments). The AGDO loss is estimated by sampling transition pairs (s, s') from a replay buffer, which in the online setting contains samples collected under different policies, introducing distributional bias. The output is a d-dimensional Laplacian eigenvector embedding per state, constrained to lie in the set C^(t) = {u : ⟨u_i, u_i⟩ ≤ 2/ρ_min} to ensure Lipschitz continuity of the gradient. The current policy π_t defines the transition matrix P^π, stationary distribution ρ^π, and hence the time-varying Laplacian L^(t) and inner product space H^(t).

## Method

The paper introduces the Asymmetric Graph Drawing Objective (AGDO), which breaks the rotational symmetry of the standard graph drawing objective using a stop-gradient operator (treating earlier vectors u_k as constants when computing gradients for u_j, j>k), eliminating the dual variables required by ALLO. The online formulation is a sequence of time-varying AGDO losses indexed by the policy update step t, optimized via projected gradient descent with constant learning rate η = 1/α. The key theoretical results are: (1) for a fixed policy, the only stable equilibrium under gradient descent is the identity permutation of the d-smallest eigenvectors (Theorem 4.4), and (2) under a bounded policy drift assumption (satisfied by PPO with scheduled clipping), online PGD achieves ergodic convergence at rate O(f(T)/T) where f is sublinear (Theorem 4.7). The drift in the loss function between consecutive steps is bounded by a quantity proportional to the policy drift δ_π^(t), the condition number of the Markov chain, and the number of states |S|.

## Results

The primary metric is average cosine similarity between the true Laplacian eigenvector representation and the learned representation, reported across 5 seeds with 95% confidence intervals. In the fixed uniform policy setting (Figure 2), AGDO and ALLO achieve nearly identical cosine similarity across all four grid-world environments (GridRoom-1, GridRoom-4, GridMaze-9, GridMaze-11). In the online PPO setting (Figure 3), both methods show upward-trending similarity as training progresses, with slightly lower early-stage accuracy on larger environments (GridRoom-1, GridRoom-4), consistent with the theoretical drift bound scaling with |S|. The ablation in Figure 5a confirms that tighter policy drift bounds (lower PPO clipping) yield higher representation accuracy, while DQN's ε-greedy policy updates produce substantially lower accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AGDO is compared against ALLO (Gomez et al., 2023) in both fixed-policy and online-PPO settings, achieving nearly identical cosine similarity to the true Laplacian representation across all environments and seeds, confirming that the simplified objective (eliminating dual variables) does not sacrifice accuracy. Against VPG (Sutton et al., 1999, equivalent to PPO without clipping), AGDO shows lower representation accuracy, validating the bounded-drift assumption (Assumption 4.2) as necessary for convergence. Against DQN (Mnih et al., 2015), the learned representation accuracy is substantially lower because ε-greedy policy updates can cause drastic probability shifts (from ε/|A| to 1−ε) in individual states, violating the bounded drift condition. Overall, AGDO ties ALLO on the primary metric while offering a simpler single-objective formulation without dual variable updates, and the ablation confirms that on-policy methods with controlled drift (PPO with scheduled clipping) are the most compatible RL algorithms for online Laplacian representation learning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Laplacian-based representation learning framework from Wu et al. (2018) and Gomez et al. (2023) from the fixed-policy setting to the online setting where the policy is simultaneously being updated. The key methodological contribution is the Asymmetric Graph Drawing Objective (AGDO), which simplifies the Augmented Lagrangian Laplacian Objective (ALLO) by eliminating dual variables while preserving the same equilibrium structure. The paper also builds on the empirical observation by Klissarov & Machado (2023) that online representation learning improves exploration, but provides the first theoretical convergence analysis for this online setting via projected gradient descent under bounded policy drift.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work fills a notable theoretical gap by establishing that online Laplacian representation learning converges to the true eigenvectors under mild assumptions, giving practitioners confidence in simultaneously updating representations and policies. The empirical finding that bounded policy drift (e.g., via PPO clipping) is essential for representation accuracy, while off-policy methods like DQN degrade performance, provides practical guidance for algorithm compatibility. Limitations include evaluation only on small grid-world environments with finite state spaces, and the bounded drift assumption may not hold in all practical RL settings. Open questions include applicability to non-stationary environments, multi-agent scenarios, and integration with linear value function approximations or options learning.
