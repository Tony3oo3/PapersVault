## Problem

Vanilla diffusion and flow matching models approximate the denoising distribution as a single Gaussian, which forces them to use many small sampling steps to avoid discretization error and produces oversaturated colors under classifier-free guidance due to unbounded mean extrapolation. The single-Gaussian assumption also fails to capture the inherently multi-modal structure of the velocity distribution, degrading few-step generation quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250374/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a Gaussian noise sample x_T and an optional class label c as input, and produces a synthetic 256×256 RGB image (or 2D coordinate samples in toy experiments). The network output is structured as K per-pixel weight maps, K per-pixel mean maps, and a single shared standard deviation s predicted by a small MLP conditioned only on time and class, yielding a pixel-wise factorized Gaussian mixture denoising distribution. For ImageNet experiments, the model operates in latent space using a DiT-XL/2 backbone with only 0.2% additional parameters over the vanilla flow-matching baseline for K=8.

## Method

GMFlow replaces the single-Gaussian mean velocity prediction with a full K-component Gaussian mixture over the flow velocity u, trained by minimizing KL divergence between the predicted and ground-truth velocity distributions (which reduces to the standard L2 loss when K=1, s=1). For inference, the paper derives analytic GM-SDE and GM-ODE solvers: the SDE solver computes the reverse transition distribution as a GM via convolution of the predicted x₀-GM with the forward transition kernel, while the ODE solver analytically converts the current GM prediction to intermediate times τ<t without additional network evaluations, enabling sub-step integration along the curved trajectory. A novel probabilistic guidance scheme reweights the conditional GM PDF by a Gaussian mask that shifts the mean toward the condition while preserving the original variance bounds, avoiding the OOD extrapolation that causes CFG oversaturation. For high-dimensional images, pixel-wise factorization prevents mode collapse, and a spectral sampling pipeline (FFT + Knothe-Rosenblatt transport with a learnable power spectrum) restores spatial correlations lost by independent per-pixel sampling.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256, GMFlow (K=8) achieves a Precision of 0.942 with only 6 NFE using GM-ODE 2, and a state-of-the-art Precision of 0.950 at 32 NFE using GM-SDE 2, outperforming all vanilla flow matching baselines (DDPM, Euler, DPM++, UniPC, DEIS, SA-Solver) across all step counts. For FID, GMFlow significantly outperforms baselines in few-step settings (NFE≤8) and remains competitive at NFE≥32. On CIFAR-10, GMFlow (K=2) achieves FID 9.11 at 10 NFE, substantially better than GMS (13.80), SN-DDPM (16.33), and DDPM small (34.76). GMFlow also reduces over-saturation, achieving Saturation values closest to real data (−0.019 to −0.024) versus +0.032 to +0.064 for baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GMFlow consistently outperforms all vanilla flow matching baselines on ImageNet 256×256 in Precision across all NFEs: at 32 NFE, GM-SDE 2 (0.950) beats DDPM small (0.944), DPM++2M SDE (0.938), Euler (0.934), and UniPC (0.931) by 0.6–1.9 points. In few-step sampling (NFE≤8), GMFlow also achieves lower FID than all baselines, with the gap widening as NFE decreases. On CIFAR-10, GMFlow (K=2) achieves FID 9.11 at 10 NFE, outperforming GMS (13.80), SN-DDPM (16.33), and DDPM small (34.76) by 4.7–25.7 FID points, though GMS slightly edges out GMFlow at 100 NFE (3.46 vs. 3.76). A key differentiator is saturation: GMFlow's probabilistic guidance keeps Saturation near or below real-data levels (−0.019 to −0.024), while all CFG-based baselines exhibit positive over-saturation (+0.032 to +0.064), confirming that GMFlow's reweighting approach avoids the OOD extrapolation problem inherent to vanilla CFG.

## Relation to Prior Work

GMFlow generalizes the standard diffusion/flow matching framework, where the denoising distribution is approximated as a single Gaussian and the network regresses its mean (or reparameterized velocity). It subsumes prior variance-learning approaches (Nichol & Dhariwal; Bao et al.) as the K=1 special case, and extends the moment-matching idea of GMS (Guo et al., 2023) from inference-time bimodal fitting to a full learned GM parameterization trained with a KL divergence loss. The key conceptual departure is treating the velocity distribution as multi-modal from the outset, which enables analytic derivation of reverse transition distributions and a reweighting-based guidance scheme that replaces CFG's unbounded mean extrapolation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses two persistent practical pain points in flow matching: the need for many sampling steps due to discretization error, and over-saturation under high CFG scales. By enabling analytic few-step solvers (GM-SDE/ODE) and a bounded probabilistic guidance mechanism, GMFlow offers a drop-in improvement (only 0.2% parameter overhead) that practitioners can adopt without architectural changes. The pixel-wise factorization required for high-dimensional data is acknowledged as a limitation that may underutilize the GM's expressiveness, and the authors point toward posterior sampling with GMFlow priors as a promising future direction. Whether the approach extends cleanly to video generation or benefits from full (non-isotropic) covariances remains open.
