## Problem

Most improved Naive Bayes algorithms focus on nominal attributes, leaving numerical attributes largely unaddressed beyond Gaussian NB. Additionally, none of the existing structure-, probability-, attribute-, or instance-oriented improvements exploit correlations among instances, treating each instance in isolation. ICGNB targets both gaps by introducing instance-level correlation structure into a numerical-attribute NB framework.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251376/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a z-score-normalized numerical attribute matrix X ∈ R^{n×m} and a class label vector c, and outputs a predicted class label per test instance. The VGAE is trained on the training set to produce an n×m embedding matrix Z, which is concatenated with X to form a 2m-dimensional augmented attribute space. At classification time, a single test instance's original m attributes are augmented with its m-dimensional embedding vector, and the weighted GNB formula over all 2m attributes yields the predicted label.

## Method

ICGNB is a three-stage pipeline. First, it constructs a sparse Instance Correlation Graph (ICG) by computing pairwise Euclidean distances and selecting edges via a Kruskal-style greedy procedure (equivalent to a minimum spanning tree) plus self-loops, yielding an n×n adjacency matrix G. Second, a variational graph auto-encoder (VGAE) with three graph-convolution layers (m, 2m, m neurons) encodes G and X into an embedding matrix Z, trained by maximizing the ELBO with a mixture-of-two-Gaussians vector prior; the variational prior p(Z) is set to independent zero-mean unit-variance Gaussians so that generated attributes are approximately independent and Gaussian, aligning with GNB's assumptions. Third, the original m attributes are augmented with the m new attributes (total 2m), and a weight vector w is optimized by gradient descent to maximize the conditional log-likelihood of the weighted GNB, which then classifies via argmax over π_c ∏ θ_{a_j|c}^{w_j}.

## Results

ICGNB achieves an average classification accuracy of 78.87% across 24 real-world numerical datasets, outperforming all five competitors. The largest margins are over GNB (71.84%, +7.03 pp) and CFWNB (73.03%, +5.84 pp), while the closest competitor is WANBIA (77.84%, +1.03 pp). ICGNB wins on 20 of 24 datasets against GNB, 22 of 24 against AE-NBC, and 19 of 24 against AG-NBC, with Wilcoxon signed-ranks tests confirming statistical significance at α=0.05 against all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against WANBIA (attribute weighting for nominal NB, adapted to GNB), ICGNB wins on 17/24 datasets and loses on 7, with a 1.03 pp average advantage; the losses are concentrated on datasets like balance, movement_libras, and wine where the original attributes already carry strong signal. Against CFWNB (correlation-based feature weighting), ICGNB wins on 18/24 and ties on 1, with a 5.84 pp average gap, showing the largest improvement on yeast (56.53% vs 18.22%) and texture (96.84% vs 78.35%). Against AG-NBC (attribute grouping for numerical NB), ICGNB wins on 19/24 and loses on 5, with a 1.20 pp average edge; the losses occur on balance, banana, and movement_libras where AG-NBC's grouping strategy happens to suit the data structure. Against AE-NBC (auto-encoding attribute generation), ICGNB wins on 22/24 and loses on only 2, with a 2.95 pp average advantage, confirming that instance-correlation-based generation outperforms instance-independent auto-encoding. Against the GNB baseline, ICGNB wins on 20/24 datasets with a 7.03 pp average improvement, with the most dramatic gains on yeast (56.53% vs 14.41%) and glass (63.02% vs 47.21%). Wilcoxon signed-ranks tests confirm ICGNB's superiority over all five baselines at α=0.05.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two well-studied lines of work: improved Naive Bayes classifiers and graph-based representation learning. It builds directly on VGAE (Kipf & Welling, 2016) but repurposes it not for graph classification or link prediction, but as an attribute generator that feeds into a Gaussian Naive Bayes classifier. The key conceptual departure from prior NB improvements (WANBIA, CFWNB, AG-NBC, AE-NBC) is the introduction of instance-level correlations via a minimum spanning tree over the instance space, a dimension that the authors argue has been entirely overlooked by all four categories of NB improvements (structure-, probability-, attribute-, and instance-oriented). The ICG construction is essentially an MST, and the VGAE is trained with a standard ELBO objective, so the individual components are standard; the novelty lies in their specific composition for the NB pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a genuine and well-articulated gap: no prior NB improvement leverages correlations *among* instances, and almost none handles numerical attributes beyond GNB. The ablation and synthetic-dataset experiments (validating independence, Gaussianity, and sensitivity) provide a thorough internal validation. However, the ICG is constructed without class labels (unsupervised MST), which the authors acknowledge as a limitation, and the greedy MST construction carries O(n²m) cost that may limit scalability. The practical impact is moderate: the method is a clean, interpretable pipeline for tabular classification with numerical features, but the absolute gains over the strongest baselines (WANBIA, AG-NBC) are modest on many datasets, and the broader DL community is unlikely to adopt a VGAE-augmented Naive Bayes as a general-purpose classifier.
