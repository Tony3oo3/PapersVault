## Problem

Lee and Valiant's (2022) 1-D mean estimator achieves the optimal sub-Gaussian error constant √2 in the standard finite-variance i.i.d. setting, but lacked proven robustness guarantees under adversarial data corruption or heavy-tailed distributions with infinite variance. This paper asks whether an estimator can simultaneously enjoy optimal-constant performance in the standard setting and the breadth of robustness guarantees (strong contamination, low moments, neighborhood optimality) previously available only for classic estimators like median-of-means and trimmed mean.

## Input / Output

The estimator takes n i.i.d. samples from an unknown distribution and a single confidence parameter δ, and outputs a point estimate μ̂ of the distribution mean. Notably, the estimator does not require knowledge of the corruption fraction η, the moment order z, or the variance σ²; the δ parameter serves a dual role, implicitly setting the maximum tolerated corruption level via the constraint η ≤ (1/24n)log(1/δ). The estimator is computed in quasilinear time via sorting, or linear time with a more subtle algorithm, and is affine-invariant (shift-and-scale equivariant).

## Method

The paper proves four properties of the unmodified Lee-Valiant estimator: (A) robustness to strong contamination with O(σ√η) additive error, (B) optimal low-moment error for z∈(1,2) matching Devroye et al.'s lower bounds, (C) neighborhood optimality in the sense of Dang et al. (2023), and (D) asymptotic normality and efficiency. The robustness proof decomposes the corruption-induced error into two parts—change in the influence parameter α and direct sample corruption—bounding each via Cauchy-Schwarz, Bernstein's inequality on truncated second moments, and a lower bound on α derived from empirical quantile arguments. The low-moment analysis extends Lee and Valiant's Chernoff-bound framework by replacing the variance constraint with a z-th moment constraint in the max-min linear programming game, then constructing feasible dual solutions that converge to the √2 constant as z→2. The neighborhood optimality result is general: any estimator satisfying a sub-Gaussian + robust error bound automatically achieves the distribution-specific Pareto-optimal error rate, since samples from D can be viewed as corrupted samples from a trimmed version D*.

## Results

The paper proves four main theoretical guarantees for the Lee-Valiant estimator without modifying its structure. Under η-fraction adversarial corruption (Theorem 2.2), the error is σ·((√2+o(1))√(log(1/δ)/n) + 222√η), where the √2 constant is optimal and the O(σ√η) robustness term matches the known lower bound. For distributions with finite z-th moment, z∈(1,2) (Theorem 2.4), the error is (M_z)^(1/z)·(1+o(1))·(c_z·log(1/δ)/n)^(1-1/z) with c_z = 2(5.6)^(1/(z-1))−1, matching Devroye et al.'s lower bounds up to constants. The estimator is also shown to be neighborhood optimal (Proposition 2.6) and asymptotically normal and efficient (Theorem 2.8), with √n(μ̂−μ) → N(0,σ²) in distribution.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper positions the Lee-Valiant estimator against median-of-means and trimmed mean across four dimensions. In the standard i.i.d. finite-variance setting, Lee-Valiant achieves the optimal √2 leading constant, while median-of-means has a larger constant and asymptotic variance (π/2)σ² instead of the efficient σ². Under adversarial corruption, both Lee-Valiant and median-of-means achieve the optimal O(σ√η) rate, but trimmed mean's current analysis (Oliveira et al., 2025) requires precise knowledge of η to match the optimal rate, whereas Lee-Valiant adapts to the corruption level automatically. For low-moment distributions (z∈(1,2)), Lee-Valiant's multiplicative constant c_z = 2(5.6)^(1/(z-1))−1 strictly dominates median-of-means' constant 8√3(12)^(1/(z-1))−1 across all z. Finally, for neighborhood optimality, the paper shows that Lee-Valiant (and in fact all sub-Gaussian + robust estimators) achieves the distribution-specific optimal error rate, extending a result previously known only for median-of-means.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the analysis of Lee and Valiant's (2022) optimal-constant sub-Gaussian mean estimator, which itself sits in a lineage from Catoni (2012) and Devroye et al. (2016). The key departure is proving that this estimator, designed specifically for tight constants in the clean i.i.d. setting, also achieves optimal robustness under adversarial corruption and heavy-tailed (finite z-th moment) distributions without any modification to its structure or parameters. Additionally, the paper builds on Dang et al. (2023)'s neighborhood optimality framework, showing that their result for median-of-means generalizes to all simultaneously sub-Gaussian and robust estimators, resolving an open question from that work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is a clear, unified recommendation: Lee-Valiant's estimator dominates median-of-means and trimmed mean across all standard robustness and performance criteria while maintaining the smallest possible estimation error in the i.i.d. setting, removing the need for practitioners to choose between accuracy and robustness. The general theorem that robustness implies neighborhood optimality (Proposition 2.6) is a conceptual contribution that simplifies the landscape of beyond-worst-case analysis for mean estimators. Open questions include whether the large constants in the robustness terms (222, 135) can be tightened, and whether analogous results extend to higher-dimensional mean estimation.
