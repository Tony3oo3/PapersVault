## Problem

In extreme classification with millions of labels, textual metadata can significantly improve accuracy, but early-fusion of metadata incurs high inference latency and is fragile to noisy retrievals, while late-fusion methods (e.g., OAK) are efficient and robust yet cannot match early-fusion accuracy even when given ground-truth metadata. The paper addresses how to transfer the representational quality of a metadata-rich early-fusion oracle to a low-latency late-fusion disciple without any additional inference cost.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251982/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At training time, the method consumes short-text queries and labels along with their ground-truth textual metadata (e.g., Wikipedia categories, hyperlinked article titles, product categories) on both the query and label sides. At inference time, only predicted (potentially noisy) metadata is available, retrieved from a memory bank via the disciple's own encoder. The output is a ranked set of relevant labels from an extremely large label space (312K–501K labels), scored by cosine similarity between the enriched query embedding and precomputed label embeddings.

## Method

MOGIC is a two-phase oracle-guided training framework. Phase 1 trains an early-fusion oracle (DistilBERT, LLaMA-2, or Phi-2) by concatenating query/label text with their ground-truth metadata and optimizing a triplet loss, producing high-quality embeddings that capture metadata context. Phase 2 freezes the oracle and trains a late-fusion disciple (OAK, NGAME, or DEXA) with a composite loss: the disciple's own task-specific loss plus two novel guidance terms — an Alignment loss (cross triplet margins between oracle query embeddings and disciple label embeddings, and vice versa) and a Matching loss (L2 distance between oracle and disciple embeddings for both queries and labels). The key insight is that the oracle's superior early-fusion representations are distilled into the disciple's late-fusion architecture via gradient regularization, so the disciple inherits metadata-aware representations while retaining low inference latency and robustness to noisy or missing metadata at test time.

## Results

MOGIC(OAK) achieves state-of-the-art P@1 of 34.62 on LF-WikiSeeAlsoTitles-320K, 47.28 on LF-WikiTitles-500K, and 47.01 on LF-AmazonTitles-131K, outperforming the OAK baseline by 1–2% in P@1 and 2–3% in propensity-scored precision (PSP@5) across all six benchmark datasets. The framework incurs no additional inference-time cost since it only modifies the training procedure via oracle-guided regularization. MOGIC also outperforms graph-based baselines (GraphFormers, GraphSAGE) by approximately 8% in P@1, even though those baselines are given ground-truth metadata at inference time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MOGIC(OAK) consistently outperforms OAK by 0.6–2.5% in P@1 and 1.5–2.4% in PSP@5 across all three title-based datasets, confirming that oracle-guided regularization adds meaningful signal on top of the existing late-fusion architecture. Against DEXA, MOGIC(OAK) wins by 1.7% P@1 on LF-WikiSeeAlsoTitles-320K and 0.6% on LF-AmazonTitles-131K, though DEXA narrowly edges out MOGIC by 0.13 P@1 on LF-WikiTitles-500K (47.41 vs. 47.28), indicating the gain is dataset-dependent. MOGIC(OAK) dominates NGAME by 2.0–8.2% P@1 across datasets, and surpasses graph-based methods GraphFormers and GraphSAGE by roughly 8% and 11% P@1 respectively on LF-WikiSeeAlsoTitles-320K, despite those baselines having access to ground-truth metadata at inference. The paper also demonstrates that MOGIC is a plug-and-play framework: when applied to NGAME and DEXA as disciples, it still yields 1–2% P@1 and 2–3% PSP improvements over their unregularized counterparts.

## Relation to Prior Work

MOGIC builds directly on OAK (Mohan et al., 2024), which introduced late-stage embedding-based metadata fusion for extreme classification, and on the broader RAG literature (Lewis et al., 2020; Guu et al., 2020) that demonstrated the benefits of early-fusion of textual context. The key departure is the two-phase oracle-guided training: an early-fusion oracle with access to ground-truth metadata is used to regularize a late-fusion disciple via novel Alignment and Matching losses, effectively performing knowledge distillation from an early-concatenation model to a two-tower architecture. This contrasts with standard knowledge distillation in that the oracle has privileged information (ground-truth metadata linkages) unavailable to the disciple, and the oracle can be the same size as the disciple (65M DistilBERT vs. 7B LLaMA-2).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MOGIC offers a practical, plug-and-play regularization framework that improves any existing XC model (OAK, NGAME, DEXA) by 1-2% at zero additional inference cost, making it immediately deployable in latency-sensitive production systems. The robustness inversion—where the late-fusion disciple outperforms the early-fusion oracle under noisy or missing metadata—highlights a general principle that oracle-guided training can transfer not just accuracy but also architectural robustness. Open questions include whether the framework extends to non-textual metadata (embeddings, structured data), whether the oracle can be replaced by a retrieval-based pseudo-oracle to eliminate the need for ground-truth metadata linkages at training time, and how the approach scales to the 1.3M+ label regime where metadata sparsity becomes more acute.
