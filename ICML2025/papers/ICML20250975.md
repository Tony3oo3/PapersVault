## Problem

Existing multivariate time series forecasting methods face a fundamental trade-off in dependency modeling: channel-independent (CI) strategies ignore inter-channel relationships, channel-dependent (CD) strategies fuse all dependencies indiscriminately and introduce noise, and channel-clustering (CC) strategies use coarse-grained, channel-wise grouping that cannot capture time-varying interactions at fine-grained temporal resolution. The paper addresses the need for an adaptive mechanism that selects which types of dependencies (temporal, spatial, spatial-temporal) are relevant for each specific time segment rather than applying a single strategy globally.

## Input / Output

The model takes a multivariate time series X ∈ R^{C×L} (C channels, look-back length L, typically 96 or searched from {192,336,512,720}) and produces future predictions Y ∈ R^{C×T} for horizons T ∈ {96,192,336,720} (long-term) or T ∈ {12,24,48} (short-term). The input is segmented into non-overlapping patches of length P (dataset-specific, ranging from 2 to 96), yielding C×N patch tokens that serve as graph nodes. The patch length P scales proportionally with L to maintain constant memory usage across different look-back horizons.

## Method

TimeFilter is a three-module GNN framework. First, the Spatial-Temporal Construction module embeds patches, computes multi-head projection distances, builds a k-NN graph, and decomposes it into patch-specific ego graphs each partitioned into temporal, spatial, and spatial-temporal subgraph regions. Second, the Patch-Specific Filtration module applies a Mixture-of-Experts router with three filter experts (Temporal, Spatial, Spa.-tem.) using noisy gating to compute per-patch confidence scores, then employs a Top-p dynamic allocation (rather than fixed Top-K) to select a variable number of filters per patch, retaining only the corresponding edges in each ego graph. Third, the Adaptive Graph Learning module restores the filtered ego graphs into a global graph, performs additive GNN message passing over O layers, and maps to predictions via a residual linear projection. Training combines MSE loss with an entropy-based dynamic loss (encouraging expert diversity) and an expert importance loss (preventing collapse to a single filter), all optimized with Adam.

## Results

On long-term forecasting (L=96, T∈{96,192,336,720}), TimeFilter achieves the best MSE and MAE across all 9 datasets, reducing MSE by 4.48% and MAE by 2.23% over the second-best method Leddam. Under the scaling-law optimal horizon setting (L searched from {192,336,512,720}), TimeFilter reduces MSE by 5.34% and 6.89% over DUET and CCM respectively. On short-term forecasting (PEMS, T∈{12,24,48}), TimeFilter achieves the best results on all four PEMS datasets, with a 13.54% MSE reduction on PEMS08 over DUET.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TimeFilter achieves the best MSE and MAE on every dataset in both long-term and short-term forecasting, with no losses to any baseline. Against the strongest competitor Leddam (a CD method), TimeFilter reduces MSE by 4.48% and MAE by 2.23% on average across long-term datasets, with a Wilcoxon test p-value of 4.66×10⁻¹⁰ confirming statistical significance at the 99% confidence level. Against the channel-clustering baselines DUET and CCM, TimeFilter reduces MSE by 5.34% and 6.89% respectively under the optimal-horizon setting, demonstrating that fine-grained patch-level filtration outperforms coarse channel-wise clustering. On short-term PEMS forecasting, TimeFilter leads all 12 baselines on all four datasets; the margin is largest on PEMS08 (13.54% MSE reduction over DUET) and PEMS07 (5.33% MSE reduction over SOFTS). CI methods (PatchTST, DLinear) consistently underperform CD and CC methods, while GNN-based baselines (CrossGNN, MSGNet) show high variance across datasets, particularly struggling on Traffic and Solar-Energy.

## Relation to Prior Work

TimeFilter sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) the CI-vs-CD debate in multivariate time series forecasting, (2) channel clustering methods (CCM, DUET) that attempt to reconcile the two by grouping similar channels, and (3) GNN-based dependency modeling (CrossGNN, MSGNet). The key conceptual departure is shifting from coarse-grained, channel-wise clustering to fine-grained, patch-wise dependency selection, where each temporal segment of each channel independently decides which type of relationship (temporal, spatial, or spatial-temporal) to retain. The dynamic expert allocation mechanism is directly adapted from MoE-Dynamic (Huang et al., 2024), replacing fixed top-k routing with a cumulative-probability top-p criterion to allow variable numbers of active filters per patch.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a well-recognized tension in the field: CI methods discard potentially useful inter-channel information while CD methods introduce noise from irrelevant dependencies. By operating at the patch level with adaptive filtering, TimeFilter offers a principled middle ground that outperforms both extremes and the intermediate channel-clustering approaches. However, the practical impact is tempered by the fact that the graph construction step (k-NN over C×N patches) scales quadratically with the number of patches, which could be prohibitive for very high-dimensional or long-sequence settings beyond what is tested here. An open question is whether the patch-level granularity provides diminishing returns compared to simpler region-based filtering, as the ablation margins on some datasets (e.g., Traffic) are quite small.
