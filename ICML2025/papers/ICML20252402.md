## Problem

Existing high-performance minority sample generators for diffusion models rely on computationally expensive guidance—either separately trained classifiers or backpropagation through the diffusion network—to steer generation toward low-density regions. This paper asks whether competitive minority generation can be achieved without any such guidance, eliminating the associated computational overhead entirely.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252402/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method requires only a pretrained diffusion score network and modifies the sampling procedure at inference time—no retraining, external classifiers, or gradient computations are needed. It draws the initial noise from a γ²-scaled Gaussian (rather than standard N(0,I)) and optionally accepts a class label for conditional generation, producing minority images at the pretrained model's native resolution (64×64 or 256×256). The only tunable hyperparameters are the boosting scale γ and the skip amount Δ_t, and the method is restricted to stochastic (SDE) samplers rather than deterministic ODE-based ones.

## Method

Boost-and-Skip introduces two minimal modifications to the standard stochastic reverse diffusion process: (i) variance-boosted initialization, where the starting noise is drawn from N(0, γ²I) with γ > 1, and (ii) timestep skipping, where the reverse process begins at T_skip = T − Δ_skip rather than T. The key insight is that these modifications are synergistic: boosting alone is ineffective because the near-zero terminal SNR α(T) ≈ 0 attenuates the boosted noise, while skipping alone yields only marginal gains; together, skipping preserves a non-negligible α(T_skip) so the boosted initialization can amplify probability mass in low-density regions. The paper provides theoretical grounding via a Gaussian analysis showing the combined modifications increase the variance of the generated distribution, and via stochastic contraction theory proving that the spurious error from the boosted initialization decays exponentially as sampling progresses. This contraction mechanism is why the method works with SDE samplers but fails with deterministic PF-ODE samplers, which propagate rather than rectify the initial distribution mismatch.

## Results

On CelebA 64×64, Boost-and-Skip achieves cFID 19.79 and sFID 6.87, closely matching the state-of-the-art guided method Um & Ye (2024b) (cFID 19.34, sFID 8.85) while achieving higher recall (0.51 vs. 0.47). On ImageNet 256×256, it reaches cFID 10.04 and sFID 4.33, essentially tying Um & Ye (2024b) at cFID 9.98 and sFID 4.35. Critically, on ImageNet 64×64, Boost-and-Skip requires only 0.98s inference time and 85.73 MB peak memory versus 2.80s and 386.75 MB for Um & Ye (2024b) — a 65% reduction in wall-clock time and 4.5× lower memory — with no additional training or external classifiers needed.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest guided baseline Um & Ye (2024b), Boost-and-Skip achieves near-parity on ImageNet 256×256 (cFID 10.04 vs. 9.98) and CelebA 64×64 (cFID 19.79 vs. 19.34), while substantially outperforming it on sFID (6.87 vs. 8.85 on CelebA) and recall (0.51 vs. 0.47). On ImageNet 64×64, B&S is slightly behind Um & Ye on cFID (12.68 vs. 11.24) but matches on sFID (3.18 vs. 3.17) and recall (0.61 vs. 0.62). Against classifier-guided methods Sehwag et al. (2022) and Um et al. (2023), B&S wins on cFID across all four datasets (e.g., 19.79 vs. 28.25 on CelebA; 10.04 vs. 10.93 on ImageNet 256×256) and on sFID in most settings. Temperature sampling, the natural guidance-free alternative, is consistently outperformed by B&S on cFID by 10–25 points across datasets. The key differentiator is computational cost: B&S runs at 0.98s/sample with 85.73 MB memory (identical to standard ADM), whereas Um & Ye (2024b) requires 2.80s and 386.75 MB, and classifier-guided methods additionally need >16 days of extra training for external classifiers.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant guided-sampling paradigm for minority generation (Sehwag et al., 2022; Um et al., 2023; Um & Ye, 2024b), which relies on classifier guidance or backpropagation-based self-guidance to steer diffusion trajectories toward low-density regions. Instead, it builds on the standard VP-SDE/DDPM reverse process (Ho et al., 2020; Song et al., 2020) and introduces two minimal modifications—variance-boosted initialization and timestep skipping—grounded in stochastic contraction theory (Pham, 2008; Chung et al., 2022). The paper explicitly contrasts its approach with temperature sampling (shown to be provably ineffective via Proposition 3.1) and the GAN truncation trick (shown to be insufficient alone), positioning Boost-and-Skip as a guidance-free alternative that exploits the contracting nature of stochastic generation to rectify initial distributional errors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: Boost-and-Skip achieves competitive minority generation quality (cFID 12.68 vs. 11.24 for the SOTA on ImageNet 64×64) while requiring 65% less wall-clock time and 4.5× lower peak memory than the current state-of-the-art, making minority generation accessible without expensive guidance infrastructure. A key limitation is that the method requires stochastic (SDE) sampling; it fails with deterministic PF-ODE samplers because the contraction mechanism that rectifies initial errors is absent. The paper also reveals an interesting dual-use: setting γ<1.0 flips the mechanism to enhance quality at the expense of diversity, suggesting Boost-and-Skip serves as a general quality-diversity control knob. Open questions include extending the approach to ODE-based samplers and to conditional/text-to-image settings where the interaction with classifier-free guidance remains unexplored.
