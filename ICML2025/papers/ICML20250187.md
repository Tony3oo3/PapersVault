## Problem

While data contamination of LLM benchmarks is well-documented, the precise conditions under which contamination actually invalidates evaluations remain unclear. It is unknown whether a single exposure to a benchmark question during millions of gradient steps necessarily impacts final performance, or whether the model can 'forget' contaminated examples. This paper challenges the common assumption that any contamination renders evaluations invalid by quantifying when overfitting occurs and when it is erased by continued training.

## Input / Output

The experimental pipeline takes a pre-training corpus (FineWeb-Edu 100BT split for GPT-3 models, Dolma for OLMo) and inserts de-duplicated benchmark questions from seven suites at random positions with controlled repetition rates (4, 12, 36, or 144 times). A 10,000-question holdout set is never added to training, and near-duplicate questions are filtered via length-normalized Levenshtein distance to prevent cross-contamination side effects. The primary output is the accuracy difference (in percentage points) between contaminated and holdout questions as a function of model size, training tokens, and repetition count, along with forgetting curves tracking this difference over subsequent gradient steps. For large-scale runs that cannot be experimentally probed, the method outputs a cumulative weight decay estimate derived solely from the learning rate schedule and weight decay parameter.

## Method

The paper trains GPT-3-architecture transformers (124M–1.6B parameters) from scratch and performs continual pre-training of OLMo-1B/7B, inserting benchmark questions at controlled positions and repetition rates to measure overfitting and subsequent forgetting. Three scaling experiments isolate the effects of model parameters, training tokens, and repetition count, revealing that contamination overfitting increases with model size and repetitions but vanishes when training extends beyond ~5× Chinchilla-optimal tokens. A key mechanistic finding is that exposure to a continuous stream of novel data drives forgetting, whereas multi-epoch training on a fixed dataset stabilizes overfitting at a nonzero level. Theoretically, the paper derives that the cumulative weight decay of AdamW (the product of (1 − γ_t λ) over all steps) bounds the influence of any past gradient update on final weights, and empirically shows that forgetting occurs faster than this bound. This allows estimating the degree of forgetting in large-scale runs (OLMo-7B, Llama 3 405B) purely from published optimizer hyperparameters, indicating that the first 10–40% of training data is likely forgotten in these models.

## Results

Under Chinchilla-optimal training, contamination causes substantial overfitting: a 124M model trained on 2.5B tokens shows a 54.2 pp accuracy gap between holdout (42.2%) and 144×-contaminated questions (96.5%), while a 1.6B model shows a 43.3 pp gap (52.1% vs. 95.4%). However, scaling training data to 15× Chinchilla eliminates the effect of 12× contamination entirely (accuracy difference within confidence interval of holdout). Continual pre-training of OLMo-7B confirms that 4× contamination (causing a 17 pp accuracy increase) is reduced by 87% after just 13% of remaining training time, and the forgetting curves of OLMo-1B and OLMo-7B align when rescaled by the 5.9× parameter ratio.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is an empirical study rather than a method comparison, so there are no external baselines. The key internal comparisons are: (1) scaling model size from 124M to 1.6B at fixed 7B tokens increases the 4× contamination gap from 5 pp to 20 pp, confirming that larger models overfit more; (2) scaling training tokens from 2× to 15× Chinchilla at fixed 124M parameters reduces the 12× contamination gap from 18 pp to within the confidence interval of zero, demonstrating that data scale can erase contamination effects; (3) under Chinchilla scaling (joint model+data scaling), moderate contamination (4×) still yields a 15 pp gap for the 774M model, showing that Chinchilla-optimal training does not protect against contamination; (4) for OLMo-7B, 4× contamination produces a 17 pp accuracy increase that decays by 87% after 13% of remaining pre-training, with WinoGrande and ARC-Easy returning to clean levels while HellaSwag and PiQA retain ~2 pp residual overfitting; (5) the cumulative weight decay analysis shows that for OLMo-7B, gradients from the first 40% of training decay to zero by the end, and for Llama 3 405B, the first 10% is likely forgotten, implying that early-training contamination is naturally erased in large-scale runs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established research threads: data contamination in LLM evaluation (GPT-3's n-gram detection, Jiang et al. 2024 showing contamination causes overfitting, Oren et al. 2024 on detection) and forgetting dynamics in neural network training (Tirumala et al. 2022, Jagielski et al. 2023). It departs from prior contamination work by introducing the dimension of continued training as a forgetting mechanism, and from prior forgetting work by showing that complete forgetting (rather than convergence to a stable baseline) is possible when the model is exposed to a continuous stream of novel data. The connection to AdamW's weight decay as a mechanistic driver of forgetting, formalized via the cumulative weight decay bound, is a novel theoretical contribution that repurposes an optimizer hyperparameter typically discussed only in terms of generalization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work challenges the widely-held assumption in the LLM evaluation community that any detected contamination invalidates benchmark results, showing instead that the impact depends critically on the joint scaling of model size, training tokens, and repetition count. The practical implication is significant: for the >5× Chinchilla training regime characteristic of modern LLMs (Llama 3, Gemma, OLMo), even 144× contamination of individual benchmark questions can be completely forgotten. Key limitations include restriction to exact contamination of benchmark questions (not paraphrased or uniquely identifiable text), validation at 7B parameters only via continual pre-training rather than full training, and the heuristic (not proven) nature of the cumulative weight decay analysis for large-scale runs. Open questions include whether the forgetting dynamics differ for privacy-sensitive memorized content, whether non-exact contamination follows the same scaling, and whether the results hold at 405B+ parameter scale.
