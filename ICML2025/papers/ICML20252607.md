## Problem

Most graph neural networks rely on message passing, which is fundamentally bounded by the 1-WL isomorphism test and struggles to explicitly capture recurring substructure patterns like triangles, k-cliques, and rings. While recent work has begun tokenizing graphs into substructure patterns, existing approaches lack a universal tokenizer adaptable to node-, link-, and graph-level tasks, still depend on message passing for pattern encoding (inheriting its expressiveness limits), and lack a mechanism to identify which patterns are most relevant to downstream tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252607/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

GPM takes a graph with node feature vectors, optional edge features, and optional positional embeddings (Laplacian eigenvectors or random-walk structural embeddings) as input. Rather than operating on the graph directly, it first tokenizes each instance (node, edge, or graph) into k random-walk patterns, where each pattern is a pair of a semantic path (node sequence) and an anonymous path (relative-position sequence). The output is a single prediction per instance—node class, link score, graph class, or regression value—produced by mean-pooling or class-token readout over the transformer-encoded pattern embeddings.

## Method

GPM is a three-stage architecture that entirely bypasses message passing. First, a random-walk tokenizer samples k patterns per instance, where each pattern is uniquely defined by the combination of its semantic path (the actual node sequence) and anonymous path (the sequence of relative first-visit positions, encoding topology without node identity); a proposition proves this pair captures all inductive biases of the corresponding substructure. Second, a pattern encoder processes the two paths separately—semantic paths via a transformer (or GRU/mean) over projected node features, and anonymous paths via a GRU over loop-based adjacency vectors that encode both identity and connectivity—then combines them as a weighted sum. Third, a transformer encoder with multi-head self-attention (and an optional class token for interpretability) identifies the dominant patterns relevant to the downstream task. Theoretically, under the reconstruction conjecture, GPM can distinguish all non-isomorphic graphs and surpasses k-WL expressiveness for any k; empirically it achieves perfect fitting on TREE_NEIGHBOR_MATCHING up to radius 7 where message passing exhibits over-squashing at radius 4.

## Results

On node classification across 9 datasets, GPM achieves the best accuracy on 8 out of 9, with headline results of 82.62% on Products (2.4M nodes), 92.28% on Computer, and 89.41% on Flickr-S (low homophily 0.24). On link prediction, GPM reaches 92.85 Hits@20 on Cora and 49.70 Hits@50 on ogbl-Collab, outperforming all baselines. On graph classification/regression, GPM achieves 82.67% on IMDB-B, 51.87% on Reddit-M5K, and 0.064 MAE on ZINC, consistently leading across all six graph-level benchmarks. In the expressiveness analysis, GPM achieves 100% accuracy on CSL, EXP, and SR25 datasets where 3-WL-based methods (e.g., 3-GNN, GIN-AK+) score only 6.7% on SR25.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GPM outperforms all message-passing GNNs (GCN, GAT, APPNP, GPR-GNN) by 3–10 points across node classification datasets, with the largest gap on low-homophily graphs (e.g., +11.3 points over GCN on Flickr-S). Among graph transformers, GPM beats GOAT (82.00→82.62 on Products), NodeFormer (72.93→82.62 on Products), and GCFormer (91.63→92.28 on Computer), but is narrowly outperformed by NAGphormer on CoraFull (71.23 vs. 71.51), the only node-classification loss. On link prediction, GPM surpasses the strongest baseline LLP by 2.9 points on Cora (92.85 vs. 89.95) and 0.6 points on ogbl-Collab (49.70 vs. 49.10). On graph-level tasks, GPM leads RUM by 1.57 points on IMDB-B and SAN by 1.11 points on Reddit-M5K, while on ZINC regression it achieves 0.064 MAE versus GPS's 0.070. In OOD generalization, GPM beats StruRW by 4.7 points on A→D (74.91 vs. 70.19) but trails SSReg by 6.4 points on D→A (59.57 vs. 65.93), though GPM+SSReg recovers to 67.30.

## Relation to Prior Work

GPM builds directly on the anonymous walk framework of Ivanov & Burnaev (2018), repurposing anonymous paths as structural tokens rather than as embedding features. It departs from the dominant message-passing paradigm (GCN, GAT, GNN-AK, higher-order WL GNNs) and from prior 'graph patterns as tokens' methods (GraphViT, NAGphormer, SAT) that still rely on message passing as the pattern encoder. The key conceptual departure is treating each random walk's semantic path and anonymous path as a joint token, encoded by sequential models (transformer/GRU) and aggregated by a transformer, thereby eliminating message passing entirely from the pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GPM offers a theoretically grounded alternative to message passing with provable expressiveness beyond 1-WL (and empirically beyond 3-WL), while retaining practical advantages in scalability, distributed training efficiency, and interpretability via class-token attention. The main limitation is that performance depends heavily on the quality of randomly sampled patterns, and the current random sampling strategy may be suboptimal for specific downstream tasks; developing adaptive or task-aware sampling remains an open problem. Future extensions to unsupervised learning, LLM integration, and heterogeneous graphs are identified but not yet explored.
