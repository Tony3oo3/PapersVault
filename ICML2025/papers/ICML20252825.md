## Problem

Inferring unobserved human mental states (intentions, desires) from irregularly spaced action sequences is critical for AI-human collaboration, but existing approaches either treat actions as i.i.d. sequences ignoring temporal irregularity, or rely on opaque neural models that cannot capture the bidirectional, logic-structured dependencies between actions and latent mental events. The paper addresses the joint challenge of modeling irregular temporal dynamics, inferring entire latent mental event sequences, and discovering interpretable rules that link intentions to actions—all while remaining data-efficient.

## Input / Output

The model consumes an observed action event sequence as (timestamp, event-type) pairs, optionally augmented with prior temporal logic rule templates that seed the rule space. Time is discretized into grids of configurable resolution (e.g., 0.5s for synthetic, 0.25–2.5s for real-world data) to parameterize the DT-RP hazard functions. Outputs include a sampled latent mental event sequence (timestamps and types of inferred intentions), a predicted next (or next-k) action with both type and continuous-time estimate, and a refined set of temporal logic rules with learned weights. A backtracking mechanism iteratively regenerates action predictions whenever a newly sampled mental event falls between the last observed action and the proposed next action, ensuring temporal coherence.

## Method

The framework couples a logic-informed temporal point process (TLPP) decoder with a discrete-time renewal process (DT-RP) encoder, trained via amortized variational EM. The TLPP decoder defines intensity functions as base rates plus weighted contributions from temporal logic rules (Horn clauses with Before/After/Equal constraints and exponential decay), where the discrete rule set is expanded during training via a column-generation sub-problem that proposes rules with the most negative expected reduced cost. The DT-RP encoder uses a neural hazard architecture: cross-attention aligns discrete time grids with action embeddings, type-specific emission and timing MLPs produce per-grid hazard probabilities, and an autoregressive RNN maintains a shared mental-history state that resets upon event occurrence. The E-step optimizes the encoder via REINFORCE with a baseline plus a closed-form Bernoulli entropy term; the M-step alternates continuous parameter optimization (master problem) with discrete rule expansion (sub-problem). This staged EM design avoids the instability of joint VAE-style updates and accommodates the non-differentiable rule-discovery step.

## Results

On the two synthetic datasets, the proposed method achieves ER% of 41.72 (Syn Data-1) and 46.85 (Syn Data-2), with MAE of 2.32 and 2.52 respectively, outperforming the best baseline (PromptTPP) by 1.75 and 0.66 ER% points. On real-world datasets, the method achieves the best ER% and MAE across all four: Hand-Me-That (75.28% ER, 1.26 MAE), Car-Following (32.72% ER, 1.80 MAE), MultiTHUMOS (57.20% ER, 6.76 MAE), and EPIC-Kitchen-100 (40.26% ER, 32.19 MAE). The method also correctly recovers most ground-truth temporal logic rules on synthetic data and discovers novel rules with real-world significance (e.g., Shot ← Dribble ∧ GoodShootingOpportunity on MultiTHUMOS).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all 11 baselines on every dataset for both ER% and MAE. Among neural TPP baselines, PromptTPP and HYPRO are the strongest competitors: on Hand-Me-That, HYPRO achieves 75.88% ER vs. 75.28% for Ours (0.6 pp gap), but Ours leads by 0.44 in MAE (1.26 vs. 1.70). Among rule-based baselines, STLR is closest on Car-Following ER (32.75% vs. 32.72%), yet Ours still wins on MAE (1.80 vs. 2.47). Generative model baselines (AVAE, GNTPP, VEPP, STVAE) consistently underperform, with GNTPP and VEPP showing the largest gaps (e.g., 85.38% ER on Hand-Me-That vs. 75.28% for Ours). The logic-based TELLER baseline, which shares the column-generation mechanism, is outperformed by 3.5–4.4 ER% points on synthetic data, demonstrating the benefit of the amortized variational EM and DT-RP encoder over TELLER's purely rule-search approach. On the multi-step prediction task (next 3 actions on Syn Data-2), Ours matches or slightly beats HYPRO in both ER% and MAE, confirming that the rule-based decoder mitigates cascading errors without sacrificing accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies three previously separate research threads: (1) logic-informed temporal point processes (TELLER, CLNN, STLR) that encode domain knowledge as temporal logic rules in intensity functions, (2) variational inference for latent state recovery in event sequences (AVAE, VEPP, STVAE), and (3) column generation for data-driven rule discovery (Barnhart et al., 1998; Li et al., 2021). The key departure is the amortized variational EM framework that jointly optimizes continuous TPP parameters, discrete logic rule sets, and a neural DT-RP encoder in a staged (non-reparameterized) manner, rather than the end-to-end differentiable updates used in VAE-style TPP models. The backtracking prediction mechanism, which checks for intervening mental state shifts between observed and predicted actions, has no direct analogue in prior TPP prediction literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses a practically important gap: inferring unobserved cognitive states (intentions, desires) from irregularly spaced behavioral data while maintaining interpretability through learned logic rules. The data-efficiency gain from logic priors is meaningful for behavioral domains where labeled data is scarce, and the ability to discover novel rules (e.g., basketball dribble→shooting opportunity→shot) without explicit prior templates broadens applicability. Open questions include scalability to very large rule spaces, sensitivity to the discrete-time grid resolution in continuous-time settings, and whether the non-differentiable column generation step can be replaced by more efficient alternatives. The reliance on hand-crafted rule templates for real-world datasets, even if optional, limits fully autonomous deployment.
