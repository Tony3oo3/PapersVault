## Problem

As synthetic data proliferates across the web, future language models will inevitably be trained on mixtures of synthetic and human-produced data, yet the impact of this direct mixing (non-iterative model collapse) on pre-training was unexplored. The paper identifies that synthetic data suffers from coverage narrowing and n-gram over-concentration, and that existing data-selection methods like DSIR cannot correct this distributional shift, leaving open the question of how to synthesize data that improves rather than degrades model performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20253208/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a human-produced text sequence and a frozen prior language model (Llama-3-8B), performing a single non-autoregressive forward pass to compute per-token conditional probabilities. Tokens whose probability exceeds a threshold p (default 0.99) are resampled via top-k (k=8) from the prior's conditional distribution; all other tokens are retained unchanged. The entire editing pipeline runs on a single 4090 GPU using vLLM, producing a semi-synthetic sequence of identical length that preserves the source distribution's long tail while introducing controlled local variation.

## Method

ToEdit replaces the standard autoregressive synthesis pipeline with a token-level editing operation: for each position i, if P(x_i | x_1,...,x_{i-1}) ≥ p, the token is resampled from the prior; otherwise it is kept. This targets the 'easy-to-learn' high-probability tokens (which constitute ~27% of the U-shaped probability distribution) while preserving the challenging low-probability tokens that carry the long-tail diversity of human data. Theoretically, the paper proves via a linear-model framework with idempotent diagonal editing matrices M_i that the test error is bounded by 2σ²d/(T−d−1) for all iterations n, in contrast to the unbounded linear growth σ²d·n/(T−d−1) of pure iterative synthesis. The key mechanism is that selective editing (rather than full regeneration) maintains the coverage of the initial human data distribution, preventing the variance collapse that drives model degradation. Experiments across pre-training from scratch (GPT-2, OLMo-1B), continual pre-training (OLMo-1B, Llama-3-8B on biomedicine/finance/math), and supervised fine-tuning (Llama-3-8B) confirm consistent downstream improvements without increasing data volume.

## Results

On continual pre-training (CPT), ToEdit yields the largest gains: Biomedicine OLMo-1B improves from 36.63 to 40.89 average (+4.26), Finance OLMo-1B from 46.22 to 50.21 (+3.99), and Llama-3-8B Biomedicine from 54.13 to 56.48 (+2.35). In pre-training from scratch, OLMo-1B average rises from 32.75 to 33.11, and in SFT, FLAN v2 improves from 70.18 to 70.65. The non-iterative model collapse finding shows PPL on 22 Pile subdomains degrades from 20.99 (100% human) to 51.93 (100% synthetic), with even 25% synthetic data causing measurable harm (22.06).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Cosmopedia (pure synthetic data), the paper demonstrates that mixing even 25% synthetic data degrades PPL from 20.99 to 22.06 on 22 Pile subdomains, and 100% synthetic data causes a 2.5× PPL increase (51.93), confirming non-iterative model collapse. Against DSIR (importance sampling for data selection), the paper shows that importance-weighted filtering of synthetic data cannot correct the distributional shift: selected synthetic samples still fluctuate around the original synthetic-data performance level (Figure 4A), and t-SNE embeddings reveal persistent misalignment with human data even at boundary regions (Figure 4B). ToEdit consistently outperforms both the unedited base model and standard CPT across all three training stages (pre-training, CPT, SFT) and both model architectures (OLMo-1B, Llama-3-8B), with the largest gains in domain-specific CPT settings (up to +4.26 on Biomedicine OLMo-1B). The method also outperforms the rephrasing approach (Maini et al., 2024) in preserving distributional coverage, as it operates at the token level rather than replacing entire samples.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the theoretical framework of iterative model collapse established by Dohmatob et al. (2024a) and Gerstgrasser et al. (2024) to a more practical 'non-iterative' setting, where synthetic and human data are simply mixed in a single training run rather than iteratively regenerated. Methodologically, it departs from both pure synthetic data generation (Cosmopedia, Phi-1/2) and data selection (DSIR, importance sampling) by proposing token-level editing (ToEdit), which sits between these two: it modifies human-produced data using a prior model's confidence scores rather than generating new sequences or filtering existing ones. The approach is conceptually related to Maini et al.'s (2024) rephrasing-the-web semi-synthetic data strategy but operates at a finer granularity (individual tokens vs. full-sentence reformatting) and requires only a single forward pass rather than autoregressive generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The demonstration that even a single non-iterative mixture of synthetic and human data degrades pre-training performance is practically critical for next-generation LLM training, where synthetic data will inevitably contaminate training corpora. The theoretical proof that token-level editing bounds test error (unlike unbounded error growth in pure iterative collapse) provides a clean guarantee, though the linear-regression framework is a significant simplification of real transformer training. The negative result that importance sampling (DSIR) cannot correct the distributional shift of synthetic data is a useful caution against relying on post-hoc data selection. Open questions include whether the modest empirical gains (typically 0.3–2 points) persist at larger scales, whether the linear-theory bound translates to transformer architectures, and whether the single-forward-pass editing can be made more coherent for longer sequences.
