## Problem

Prompt tuning for vision-language models faces two intertwined limitations: frozen visual encoders produce misaligned features that cause confusion between similar classes, limiting task-specific specialization, and existing methods treat specialization and generalization as competing objectives, forcing a trade-off. No prior approach simultaneously improves both without adding extra network parameters or sacrificing one for the other.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250928/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a set of class labels as input, processing them through a frozen CLIP encoder (ViT-B/16 or ViT-L/14) to produce visual and textual embeddings. At inference, the output is a classification probability vector computed from a mixture model that combines predictions from K+1 prompts: one specialized learnable prompt per class domain and one generalized hand-crafted prompt (e.g., 'a photo of a [CLASS]'). The mixture weights are confidence-aware scalars (π_i^in for in-class, π_i^out for out-class) that modulate each prompt's contribution to the final softmax over class similarities.

## Method

CoCoA-Mix introduces a mixture model over K+1 prompts and proves (Theorem 3.2) that its expected error is upper-bounded by the weighted sum of individual prompt errors, then decomposes this bound into specialization and generalization terms (Lemma 3.3). For specialization, CoA-loss (1 − p̂_t(y)) is added to standard cross-entropy, which analytically increases gradient magnitude for confusing samples where the correct and incorrect class probabilities both approach 0.5, thereby sharpening decision boundaries between easily confused classes. For generalization without trade-offs, CoA-weights optimize π_i^in via cross-entropy of the mixture model (increasing weight when the specialized prompt outperforms others on in-class data) and π_i^out via an entropy-margin loss that forces the specialized prediction to be less confident than the generalized hand-crafted prompt on out-of-domain classes. The entire framework requires only two additional scalar parameters per prompt (π_i^in, π_i^out) beyond the learnable prompt tokens, avoiding the extra network components used by methods like MaPLe or DePT.

## Results

On the base-to-new generalization benchmark (4-shot, 11 datasets), CoCoA-Mix achieves an average harmonic mean of 77.03%, surpassing the best prior method KgCoOp (76.38%) and zero-shot CLIP (66.82%) by 0.65 and 10.21 points respectively. In cross-dataset transfer (trained on ImageNet, tested on 10 datasets), CoCoA-Mix reaches a harmonic mean of 66.07%, outperforming CLIP (63.97%) by 3.28 points. On the FSCIL benchmark (CIFAR-100, 5-shot 5-way), CoCoA-Mix achieves the highest mean accuracy of 83.5% with the lowest performance drop (PD=7.4) across 9 sessions, beating FSPT-FSCIL (81.4%, PD=7.5) and L2P (78.2%, PD=24.9).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoCoA-Mix outperforms all six prompt-tuning baselines on the average harmonic mean across 11 datasets. The largest gains are over CoOp (+5.70 H) and DePT (+5.25 H), both of which sacrifice generalization for specialization; CoCoA-Mix matches DePT's Base accuracy (79.31 vs 79.20) while improving New accuracy by 8.74 points. Against the strongest generalization-oriented baselines, KgCoOp and ProGrad, CoCoA-Mix leads by 0.65 and 1.97 H points respectively, with the advantage driven primarily by New-class accuracy (75.10 vs 74.62 and 72.19). In FSCIL, CoCoA-Mix achieves the best mean (83.5%) and lowest forgetting (PD=7.4), surpassing FSPT-FSCIL by 2.1% mean and L2P by 5.3% mean while reducing the performance drop from 24.9 to 7.4. In cross-dataset transfer, CoCoA-Mix attains the highest source accuracy (70.85%) and target accuracy (65.27%), beating KgCoOp by 0.90 H and MaPLe by 0.81 H, while using only 0.26% of MaPLe's parameters.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the textual prompt tuning paradigm initiated by CoOp (Zhou et al., 2022c), which optimizes learnable prompts with frozen CLIP encoders using standard cross-entropy. It departs from prior regularization-based methods (ProGrad, KgCoOp) that constrain prompts to hand-crafted templates, and from capacity-increasing methods (MaPLe, DePT) that add network parameters, by instead introducing a confusion-aware loss (CoA-loss) that directly targets decision-boundary confusion and a confidence-aware mixture model (CoA-weights) that reweights individual prompt predictions. The key conceptual departure is the mathematical framework (Theorem 3.2, Lemma 3.3) showing that a weighted mixture of specialized and generalized prompts can simultaneously reduce both specialization and generalization error, challenging the prevailing assumption that these objectives are in tension.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, parameter-efficient alternative to the growing family of prompt tuning methods that either sacrifice generalization for specialization or add substantial model capacity. The theoretical decomposition of error into specialization and generalization components offers a reusable analytical lens for future prompt tuning research. However, the method is limited to textual prompt tuning and relies on an assumption that visual embeddings remain consistent across domains, which constrains its effectiveness under significant style shifts (as reflected in the weaker cross-dataset transfer gains). Future work on visual prompt optimization to handle style variation, and on extending the mixture framework to multi-modal or multi-task settings, would address these limitations.
