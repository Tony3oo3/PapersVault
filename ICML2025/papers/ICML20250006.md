## Problem

Most RL research for LLMs focuses on single-step optimization (e.g., RLHF), leaving multi-turn goal-directed interaction—such as multi-turn dialogue, games, and information gathering—largely unbenchmarked. There is no established protocol for head-to-head comparison of RL algorithms on the same multi-turn language tasks, and multi-turn evaluation requires interactive simulators rather than static test sets, making algorithm development and iteration difficult.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250006/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The agent's state is the full token history of the conversation or game (a POMDP where observations are individual tokens), and the action is the next generated token. Offline datasets range from 1.24k trajectories (Maze) to 1M (Wordle), with average trajectory lengths from 4.82 (Wordle) to 46.7 (Chess) agent responses. Evaluation is conducted by running the trained policy against LLM-based simulators (for dialogue) or scripted game engines (for text games), producing a normalized reward score where 50 corresponds to the dataset average, 0 to the minimum, and 100 to the maximum possible return.

## Method

LMRL-Gym introduces 8 tasks split into 3 Interactive Dialogue tasks (20 Questions, Guess My City, Car Dealer) that simulate real-world goal-oriented conversations, and 5 RL Capability text games (Maze, Text-Nav, Wordle, Chess, Endgames) designed to isolate specific RL capabilities: strategic decision-making, credit assignment, partial observability, trajectory stitching, and complex language handling. Data generation uses GPT-3.5 to seed initial conversations, then fine-tuned FLAN-T5-XL (or GPT2-XL for Car Dealer) models generate 100K+ trajectories by interacting with each other; text-game data is produced by diluting near-optimal engine play with suboptimal actions. The research framework implements BC, Filtered BC, MC Returns, ILQL (offline value-based), and Online PPO, all initialized from GPT-2 weights fine-tuned via behavioral cloning, with the value functions used to perturb base-model logits for policy extraction. A key design choice is including both fully observed and partially observed variants of Maze and Text-Nav, and deliberately restricting dataset trajectories to suboptimal regions to force trajectory stitching.

## Results

Across all 8 LMRL-Gym tasks, offline RL methods (MC Returns and ILQL) consistently outperform both behavioral cloning (BC) and filtered BC, confirming that RL can extract more optimal policies than the best behaviors in the dataset. On RL Capability tasks, ILQL achieves the highest normalized scores on FO Maze (99.9), FO Text-Nav (91.8), and Wordle (97.7), while online PPO excels on Endgames (77.5) and PO Text-Nav (85.5). On Interactive Dialogue tasks, the simpler MC Returns method outperforms ILQL (e.g., 88.0 vs. 75.0 on Guess My City), and GPT-4 few-shot prompting dominates (95.7 on 20Qs, 92.3 on Guess My City) but fails catastrophically on RL Capability tasks (0 on Chess and Endgames, 15.4 on Wordle).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ILQL (Snell et al., 2022a), the paper's MC Returns baseline wins on all three Interactive Dialogue tasks (20Qs: 87.1 vs. 82.9; Guess: 88.0 vs. 75.0; Car: 57.2 vs. 46.3), suggesting that full TD-learning does not scale well to complex open-ended language. ILQL, however, dominates on most RL Capability tasks (FO Maze: 99.9 vs. 75.0; FO Text-Nav: 91.8 vs. 71.9; Wordle: 97.7 vs. 94.9), likely due to its trajectory stitching capability. Against online PPO (Schulman et al., 2017), ILQL wins on FO Maze (99.9 vs. 79.7) and Wordle (97.7 vs. 84.2), while PPO wins on Endgames (77.5 vs. 45.8) and PO Text-Nav (85.5 vs. 83.7), indicating that online interaction helps in partially observable and adversarial settings. Against GPT-4 few-shot prompting (OpenAI, 2023), the RL-finetuned GPT-2 models dramatically outperform on all RL Capability tasks (e.g., ILQL 97.7 vs. GPT-4 15.4 on Wordle; PPO 77.5 vs. GPT-4 0 on Endgames), but GPT-4 dominates on Interactive Dialogue tasks (95.7 vs. 87.1 on 20Qs; 92.3 vs. 88.0 on Guess), likely because these tasks are more in-distribution for GPT-4's pretraining. Overall, no single method wins across all tasks, highlighting significant room for algorithmic improvement in both offline and online multi-turn RL for language models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the RLHF and offline RL literature for LLMs (PPO, ILQL, behavioral cloning) but departs from the dominant single-step bandit framing by targeting multi-turn, goal-directed interaction. It draws on text-game environments (TextWorld, Stockfish) and LLM-as-simulator approaches (Park et al. 2023, Hausknecht et al. 2019) for data generation, but uniquely combines both interactive dialogue tasks and capability-isolating text games under a unified offline/online RL evaluation protocol. Prior multi-turn dialogue RL work (Lewis et al. 2017, Verma et al. 2022, Jaques et al. 2020) lacked head-to-head algorithm comparison on shared tasks, which this benchmark explicitly addresses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LMRL-Gym fills a clear gap by providing the first systematic, reproducible benchmark for multi-turn RL with LLMs, complete with offline datasets, LLM simulators, and a research framework. The finding that ILQL's trajectory-stitching advantage on text games reverses into underperformance on complex dialogue (where simpler MC Returns or even filtered BC can win) highlights an open problem in scaling TD-based offline RL to realistic language settings. Limitations include reliance on small GPT-2-scale models (≤1.5B params) and synthetic LLM-generated data, leaving open whether conclusions transfer to frontier-scale models and human-interactive evaluation.
