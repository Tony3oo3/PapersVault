## Problem

Conservation laws in gradient flow training dynamics are well understood for shallow ReLU and linear networks, but remain largely unexplored for the practical architectures that dominate modern deep learning—convolutional ResNets and Transformers. This paper asks: what quantities are exactly invariant during training of these architectures, are the identified laws complete, and do they persist under discrete SGD optimization?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251736/cropped_page8_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The analysis takes a parametric neural network g(θ,·) (a convolutive ResNet block, a single-head attention layer, a cross-entropy classification head, or a composition thereof), a loss function ℓ satisfying a subspace-invariance assumption, and the associated gradient flow ODE or SGD update rule. It produces explicit closed-form conservation laws (e.g., ‖u‖²−‖v‖² per hidden channel for convolutions, QQᵀ−KKᵀ and VVᵀ−OOᵀ for attention), completeness proofs via Lie algebra dimension counting, and for SGD an error bound |h(θ_k)−h(θ_0)| ≤ (C_h C_L/2)·Στ_i² showing approximate conservation with O(step-size²) drift.

## Method

The core technique is a reparameterization framework: by writing g(θ,x)=f(φ(θ),x) for a suitable φ, the conservation problem reduces to computing the trace of the Lie algebra generated by vector fields ∂φ(θ)ᵀP_ℓ(φ(θ))e_i, whose dimension k yields exactly D−k independent conservation laws via Frobenius' theorem. Key structural results include: (i) a structure theorem showing weight-decay conservation laws are simply the no-WD laws composed with exponential rescaling; (ii) the surprising fact that residual blocks share identical conservation laws with their non-residual counterparts since ∂_θg=∂_θg̃; (iii) for deeper networks, a 'block law' theorem proving that conservation laws depending only on one residual block's parameters exactly match those of the block in isolation. For discrete dynamics, a Taylor-expansion argument under bounded-gradient and bounded-Hessian assumptions yields the O(τ²) SGD conservation error, validated numerically on ResNet-18/CIFAR-10 and a Transformer/IMDb.

## Results

The paper establishes complete characterizations of conservation laws for modern architectures: for multi-channel convolutional ReLU blocks, there are exactly c₁ independent conservation laws given by h_j(θ) = Σ_k‖u_{k,j}‖² − Σ_i‖v_{j,i}‖² (Theorem 3.6); for single-head attention layers, the conserved quantities are QQᵀ − KKᵀ and VVᵀ − OOᵀ (Corollary 3.9); and for cross-entropy classification layers, the m independent laws are column sums of the weight matrix (Proposition 3.11). For deeper ResNets and Transformers, Theorem 4.6 proves that block-level conservation laws in the global network exactly match those of the isolated block, while Theorem 4.7 shows no additional laws arise from parameter subsets overlapping two residual blocks. Under discrete SGD, Proposition 5.1 provides an error bound |h(θ_k) − h(θ_0)| ≤ (C_h C_L / 2) Σ τ_i², yielding O(τ²k) drift for constant step size and O(τ₀²) bounded error for decaying step sizes, confirmed numerically on ResNet-18/CIFAR-10 and a Transformer/IMDb setup.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper without a standard method-vs-baseline comparison table. The key extensions over prior work are: (1) Marcotte et al. 2023 established completeness of conservation laws for shallow ReLU/linear networks under Euclidean gradient flow; this paper extends the framework to multi-channel convolutions (generalizing Du et al. 2018's single-channel result), attention layers, and cross-entropy layers, proving completeness in each case. (2) Marcotte et al. 2024 studied non-Euclidean and momentum dynamics; this paper's Structure Theorem (Theorem 2.1) cleanly separates the weight-decay case from the non-regularized case, showing conserved functions with WD reduce to H(θ·exp(∫λ)) of the WD-free laws. (3) The novel contribution of Proposition 4.3 and Theorem 4.6 provides a generic reduction showing that block-level conservation laws in deep residual networks are identical to those of the isolated block, a result not available in prior work. Numerically, the O(τ²) SGD error bound is validated on ResNet-18 (CIFAR-10) and a Transformer (IMDb), with the conservation error slope matching the theoretical Cτ² prediction across learning rates from 10⁻³ to 5×10⁻³.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the conservation-law framework established by Marcotte et al. (2023, 2024) for shallow ReLU and linear networks to the building blocks of modern deep architectures: multi-channel convolutions (generalizing Du et al., 2018 from single-channel), single-head attention layers, and cross-entropy classification heads. A key structural result is that residual (skip) connections do not alter the conservation structure of a block, and that conservation laws for a deep network depending only on a subset of parameters reduce exactly to those of the corresponding shallow block in isolation. The paper further bridges the continuous-to-discrete gap by showing gradient-flow conservation laws are approximately preserved under SGD with an O(τ²) error bound.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The block-decomposition theorem (Theorem 4.6) provides a practical recipe for analyzing conservation laws in arbitrarily deep ResNets and Transformers without re-deriving them from scratch, which is valuable for understanding implicit bias and designing balancedness-enforcing optimizers. However, the analysis omits normalization layers, max-pooling, and multi-head attention completeness, limiting direct applicability to production architectures. The SGD preservation result, while theoretically clean, relies on bounded-gradient assumptions that are hard to verify in practice for deep networks, and the numerical experiments use only ResNet-18 on CIFAR-10 and a small transformer on IMDb, leaving open whether the O(τ²) scaling holds at scale.
