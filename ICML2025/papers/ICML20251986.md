## Problem

Existing graph counterfactual explainers rely on forward perturbation to cross the oracle's decision boundary, which requires oracle access during training, risks overshooting the boundary, and discards structural information by starting from uninformative initializations. No prior method has reformulated counterfactual generation as a backtracking (inverse) process, and style transfer—separating global structure from local content—has never been defined or applied to graphs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251986/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Given an input graph G = (X, A) and a black-box oracle Φ, GIST first selects an overshot intermediary graph G^ε from the dataset such that Φ(G) ≠ Φ(G^ε). The method then produces a counterfactual graph G* = (X*, A*) whose normalized Laplacian spectrum interpolates between G's (style) and G^ε's (content), controlled by a scalar α ∈ [0,1]. The output is a discrete adjacency matrix obtained by thresholding Gumbel-Softmax-sampled edge probabilities, paired with reconstructed node features.

## Method

GIST reformulates counterfactual generation as spectral style transfer via backtracking: rather than forwardly crossing Φ's boundary, it starts from an already-overshot graph G^ε and learns a reverse transformation toward G's global spectral structure while preserving G^ε's local node/edge content. The architecture uses transformer-based convolutional layers to produce node embeddings from G^ε, an MLP to predict pairwise edge probabilities, and Gumbel-Softmax relaxation for differentiable discrete edge sampling. Training minimizes a weighted combination of a spectral style loss (L1 distance between eigenvalues of the normalized Laplacians of G and G*) and a content preservation loss (L1 node-feature reconstruction plus BCE edge reconstruction against G^ε). The paper provides theoretical guarantees—eigenvalue linear interpolation under commuting Laplacians, connectedness preservation, spectral gap bounds via Weyl's inequalities, and a Frobenius norm bound showing G* is spectrally sandwiched between G and G^ε—validated empirically with sub-10⁻² errors on 8 benchmark datasets.

## Results

GIST achieves an average +7.6% improvement in validity over the second-best method (CF-GNNExp.) and a +45.5% gain in fidelity across 8 graph classification benchmarks. On validity, GIST reaches 0.969 (AIDS), 0.956 (BBBP), 0.970 (ENZYMES), and 1.000 (BAShapes), outperforming all baselines on 4/8 datasets and tying on 4/8. On fidelity, GIST reports 0.957 (AIDS), 0.809 (BBBP), and 0.968 (BAShapes), with the largest margin over RSGG-CE on COLORS-3 (+37.4%). The interpolation factor α=0.9 is used by default, and ablation studies confirm that GIST's produced counterfactuals align with the theoretical spectral convex combination to within 2.051×10⁻³ average eigenvalue error.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GIST outperforms CF-GNNExp. (the strongest forward-perturbation baseline) on 6/8 datasets in validity, with the largest gaps on PROTEINS (0.791 vs. 0.378, +109%) and ENZYMES (0.970 vs. 0.910, +6.6%), while tying on BZR (0.810) and MSRC21 (0.965). Against RSGG-CE, GIST dominates on 5/8 datasets (e.g., AIDS: 0.969 vs. 0.128; BBBP: 0.956 vs. 0.404) and ties on BAShapes (1.000) and COLORS-3 (0.884). CLEAR and CF² are consistently far behind GIST across all datasets, with validity often below 0.5. In terms of fidelity, GIST achieves the highest scores on 7/8 datasets, with a notable +37.4% improvement over RSGG-CE on COLORS-3 (0.202 vs. 0.147) and +100% over CF-GNNExp. on BAShapes (0.968 vs. 0.484). The iRand random baseline confirms that the gains are not trivial: GIST's validity exceeds iRand by orders of magnitude on most datasets (e.g., 0.969 vs. 0.013 on AIDS).

## Relation to Prior Work

GIST departs from the dominant forward-perturbation paradigm in Graph Counterfactual Explainability (GCE), where methods like CF-GNNExp, CF², CLEAR, and RSGG-CE all learn to cross the oracle's decision boundary from the input side. Instead, it repurposes the content-style separation principle from neural style transfer (Gatys et al., 2016) to the graph domain, defining 'style' as the Laplacian spectrum and 'content' as node features and edge structure, then inverts the generation direction: first overshoot the boundary to an intermediary graph G^ε, then backtrack toward the input while interpolating between G's spectral style and G^ε's local content. The architecture combines transformer-based graph convolutional layers with Gumbel-Softmax differentiable edge sampling, and the theoretical analysis (spectral gap bounds, connectivity preservation under convex Laplacian combinations) provides guarantees absent in prior GCE work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The backtracking reframing offers a principled way to control the overshooting factor via the interpolation parameter α, addressing a key instability in forward methods that can oscillate or overshoot the decision boundary. However, the paper acknowledges a critical open gap: in multi-class settings, backtracking only guarantees Φ(G*) ≠ Φ(G) but not Φ(G*) = Φ(G^ε), meaning the counterfactual may land in an unintended class. The simple random overshooting algorithm and the commuting-Laplacian assumption in the theoretical proofs limit generality, and the α=0.5 ablation reveals that balanced content-style weighting degrades performance substantially. Future work on spectrally-informed overshooting, multi-class class maintenance guarantees, and global (model-level) counterfactuality would strengthen the framework's practical applicability.
