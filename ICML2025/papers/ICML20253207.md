## Problem

Token-level reward models have been shown to substantially improve PPO-based LLM alignment by providing dense, fine-grained feedback, but extending this guidance to Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) is nontrivial because DPO is formulated as a sequence-level bandit problem where the reward is implicitly expressed through the policy being optimized. The central technical challenge is deriving a computable DPO loss that incorporates token-level reward signals while eliminating the intractable partition function that arises from the token-level reward decomposition.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a human preference dataset of (prompt, preferred response, dispreferred response) triples, a frozen reference policy, and per-token reward values r̂([x, y^{<t}], y^t) computed from a pre-trained DPO model's implicit token-level reward (β log π_θ/π_ref at each token position). The shaping functions f_w and f_l are applied independently to each token in the win and lose responses respectively, producing a per-token weighted log-ratio that feeds into a sigmoid preference loss. The output is a fine-tuned language model policy that exhibits token-level granularity in its deviation from the reference policy, with preferred tokens receiving stronger reinforcement and dispreferred tokens receiving stronger suppression.

## Method

TGDPO decomposes sequence-level PPO into a sequence of token-level PPO problems via an upper-bounding argument (Theorem 4.1), then modifies each token-level problem by replacing the uniform KL penalty β with a reward-dependent penalty β·f(r̂(s_t, a_t)), yielding a closed-form optimal token-level policy and a corresponding token-level reward representation (Theorem 4.3). Substituting this reward into the Bradley-Terry preference model produces a per-instance loss containing partition functions, which are then provably eliminable because they are independent of the policy being optimized (Theorem 4.4), yielding the final computable loss L_TGDPO. In practice, the token-level reward r̂ is taken from a pre-trained DPO model's implicit reward, and the shaping functions are set as f_w = 1 + α·r̂ for win-response tokens and f_l = 1 − α·r̂ for lose-response tokens, so that preferred tokens in the win response receive amplified gradient updates while dispreferred tokens receive attenuated ones (and vice versa in the lose response). This framework recovers DPO, SimPO, R-DPO, and D²PO as special cases by appropriate choices of f, and empirically achieves up to 7.5-point win-rate gains on MT-Bench while maintaining stable performance at loss convergence—unlike DPO and SimPO which degrade significantly.

## Results

TGDPO consistently outperforms DPO and SimPO across all three benchmarks (AlpacaEval 2, Arena-Hard, MT-Bench) and all four model settings. The headline gains over the best baseline are up to 7.5 points on MT-Bench win rate (Llama3.2-3B: 36.9% vs. DPO's 29.4%), 6.2 points on AlpacaEval 2 win rate (Llama3.2-3B: 35.8% vs. DPO's 29.6%), and 4.3 points on Arena-Hard win rate (Gemma2-2B: 30.7% vs. DPO's 26.4%). On the Llama3-8B-Instruct PairRM setting, TGDPO achieves 43.9% AlpacaEval 2, 34.3% Arena-Hard, and 41.9% MT-Bench win rate, surpassing DPO (41.7%, 30.4%, 37.5%) and SimPO (39.8%, 28.7%, 32.5%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TGDPO outperforms DPO on all benchmarks across all four model settings. On Llama3-8B-Instruct PairRM, TGDPO beats DPO by 2.2 points on AlpacaEval 2 (43.9% vs. 41.7%), 3.9 points on Arena-Hard (34.3% vs. 30.4%), and 4.4 points on MT-Bench win rate (41.9% vs. 37.5%). The margin over SimPO is even larger, with TGDPO leading by 4.1 points on AlpacaEval 2 and 9.4 points on MT-Bench win rate in the same setting. On the smaller Llama3.2-3B model, TGDPO's advantage over DPO widens to 6.2 points on AlpacaEval 2 and 7.5 points on MT-Bench win rate. TDPO (an additional baseline in Appendix B.1) performs very close to DPO, confirming that TGDPO's gains come from explicit token-level reward guidance rather than merely token-level KL regularization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DPO framework (Rafailov et al., 2023) by incorporating token-level reward guidance, a technique previously shown beneficial only for PPO-based RLHF (Yang et al., 2023; Yin et al., 2025; Zhong et al., 2024). It departs from TDPO (Zeng et al., 2024), which provides a token-level MDP interpretation of DPO but does not use external token-level rewards to shape the loss, and from sequence-level variants like SimPO and R-DPO that ignore token-level structure entirely. The key conceptual move is decomposing sequence-level PPO into a sequence of token-level PPO subproblems via an upper-bounding argument, then deriving a closed-form optimal token-level policy whose induced reward, combined with the Bradley-Terry model and a new partition-function elimination theorem, yields a computable DPO loss with per-token reward weighting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical finding that TGDPO maintains strong performance at loss convergence while DPO and SimPO degrade substantially (SimPO's AlpacaEval 2 win rate drops to 4.6%) addresses a well-known pain point in preference optimization where practitioners must carefully tune early-stopping to avoid degenerate policies. The demonstrated robustness to the quality of the token-level reward signal (nearly identical performance whether using rewards from a DPO model trained with β=0.1 or β=0.01) reduces the practical burden of obtaining high-quality token-level rewards. Open questions include whether the framework extends to safety, honesty, and fairness alignment objectives, whether the simple linear shaping function f(r) = 1 ± αr is optimal, and whether the convergence advantage persists at larger model scales or with different reward model architectures.
