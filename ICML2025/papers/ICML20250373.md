## Problem

Consistency models enable fast one-step or multistep sampling from diffusion models, but their theoretical foundations remain poorly understood. In particular, the self-consistency property only holds approximately under the training distribution in practice, the starting distribution (Gaussian noise) mismatches the true forward-process marginal, and it is unclear why multistep sampling yields diminishing returns. Previous theoretical works required the ground-truth consistency function to be Lipschitz, an assumption that can fail exponentially for multimodal distributions.

## Input / Output

The method takes a Gaussian noise sample from N(0, σ_T²I), a trained consistency function estimate f̂(·,·) satisfying approximate self-consistency (Assumption 1), a noise schedule {α_t, σ_t²} parameterizing the forward SDE, and a discrete sampling time schedule {t_i}_{i=1:N} as input. It produces data samples from the target distribution P_data along with dimension-free upper bounds on W2 distance (requiring bounded support or light-tail conditions) and TV distance (requiring log-smoothness of the data density plus an additional Gaussian perturbation step). The error bounds scale polynomially in problem parameters and depend on the consistency loss ε_cm, the time-step size Δτ, and the forward-process convergence rate α_t²/σ_t².

## Method

The core technical contribution is a three-part error decomposition for multistep consistency sampling that avoids Lipschitz assumptions on the consistency function. First, the W2 error is split into a starting-distribution mismatch term (controlled by KL divergence via Pinsker's inequality and the data-processing inequality) and a consistency-function inaccuracy term (controlled by the consistency loss ε_cm). Second, the KL divergence between the true and estimated intermediate distributions is tracked via a recursive formula derived from the chain rule of KL divergence, where each induction step adds an error proportional to α_{t_{i+1}}²/σ_{t_{i+1}}² · t_i² · ε_cm²/Δτ². Third, the pointwise evaluation error ‖f̂(x,τ_k) − f⋆(x,τ_k)‖ is bounded by a stepwise decomposition along the PF-ODE trajectory, yielding τ_k · ε_cm/Δτ via Minkowski's inequality. For TV guarantees, an additional Gaussian smoothing step N(0,σ_ε²I) is applied, with the smoothing bias bounded by 2dLσ_ε under log-smoothness. Two case studies (OU process and variance-exploding SDE) demonstrate that two-step sampling reduces W2 error by roughly half, and that a geometric time schedule is optimal for the VE SDE.

## Results

The main theoretical result (Theorem 2) establishes that for a consistency model with ε_cm²-accurate self-consistency and target distribution with bounded support of radius R, one-step sampling yields W₂ error of O(ε_cm/Δτ · log(R³Δτ²/ε_cm²)) while two-step sampling reduces this to O(ε_cm/Δτ · log(R²Δτ/ε_cm)), roughly halving the error when ε_cm ≪ Δτ. For the variance-exploding SDE case study (Corollary 2), a geometrically-spaced multistep schedule achieves W₂ error of Õ(R√(ε_cm/Δτ)), improving over the single-step rate of O(R(ε_cm/Δτ)^{1/3}). The simulation (Figure 2) confirms that the proposed two-step schedule matches the best baseline performance with significantly fewer function evaluations, and that excessive sampling steps degrade quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against competing generative methods on standard benchmarks; instead, it provides theoretical guarantees under weaker assumptions than prior work. Compared to Lyu et al. (2023), Li et al. (2024b), and Dou et al. (2024), which all require the ground-truth consistency function to be Lipschitz (a condition shown to be exponentially large for multimodal distributions in Appendix A), this paper only assumes bounded support or a light-tail condition on the data distribution. In the simulation study (Figure 2), the proposed two-step sampling schedule for the OU process achieves W₂ error comparable to the best of two baseline schedules (evenly-spaced steps and halving schedule) while requiring far fewer consistency function evaluations. Both baselines show degraded performance when too many sampling steps are used, consistent with the theoretical prediction that additional steps accumulate KL divergence without proportionally reducing the final-step error.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the consistency model framework of Song et al. (2023) and the SDE-based forward process formulation of Song et al. (2021), providing the first convergence analysis of multistep consistency sampling under minimal data assumptions. It departs from prior theoretical works (Lyu et al. 2023; Li et al. 2024b; Dou et al. 2024) that required the ground truth consistency function to be Lipschitz — an assumption this paper shows can demand an exponentially large constant (Appendix A) — by instead relying on bounded support or light-tail conditions on the data distribution and the data-processing inequality. Additionally, while prior analyses were restricted to variance-preserving SDEs, this paper handles a general family of forward processes including variance-exploding SDEs, and provides the first theoretical characterization of when multistep sampling helps versus hurts sample quality.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a rigorous explanation for the empirically observed phenomenon that two-step consistency sampling yields most of the quality gain while additional steps offer diminishing or even negative returns, giving practitioners a principled basis for choosing sampling schedules. The exponential lower bound on the Lipschitz constant of the ground truth consistency function (for bimodal distributions) clarifies why prior theoretical approaches were fundamentally limited and motivates the data-processing-inequality-based analysis used here. Open questions include establishing matching lower bounds for multistep sampling and deriving end-to-end guarantees that incorporate training error (optimization, statistical) rather than assuming a fixed consistency loss. The TV distance result requires an additional Gaussian smoothing step, which introduces a bias-variance trade-off (2dLσ_ε) that may be non-trivial in high dimensions.
