## Problem

Robotic foundation models have not achieved the pre-training success seen in NLP and vision, largely due to the scarcity of large-scale diverse robotic data and the lack of representations that effectively model the physical world. Existing approaches either rely on costly robotic annotations or use high-level VLM representations (e.g., language decoders pre-trained on captioning/VQA) that are misaligned with the low-level spatial reasoning required for precise manipulation.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a language instruction, multi-view RGB images (128×128, typically front and wrist cameras), and a context window of C=16 (or 32) previous timesteps of either 3D point coordinates (g×g grid, n=g² points with x,y,z in camera frame) or robot proprioceptive states. During pre-training it outputs the next timestep's 3D point tracks; during control it outputs a 16-step sequence of end-effector Cartesian position (x,y,z), Euler-angle rotation (θx,θy,θz), and a binary gripper command, of which only the first step is executed. The key design choice is that the point-coordinate and robot-state representations share a geometric structure up to a linear transformation, allowing the same autoregressive architecture to switch between them across training stages.

## Method

ARM4R is a causal Transformer (ViT-Base) trained for next-token prediction over a sequence of language, observation, and prediction tokens, where the observation token is formed by attention-pooling a frozen CrossMAE-pretrained ViT image embedding with a 2-layer MLP encoding of 3D point coordinates (or robot states). The core insight is that 3D point tracks in a robotic scene evolve through linear transformations of the robot's joint states (via SE(3) twist exponentials), so a model pre-trained to predict 3D point trajectories from human video can transfer directly to predicting robot states. Training proceeds in three stages: (1) pre-training on ~75K Epic-Kitchens100 egocentric videos using SpatialTracker-generated pseudo 3D point tracks, (2) fine-tuning on ~1–2K robotic scene videos for the same 3D point tracking task to bridge the camera-dynamics and embodiment gap, and (3) fine-tuning for control by swapping the point MLPs for state MLPs and predicting end-effector trajectories with L1 loss. The language encoder is a frozen CLIP text encoder with a learnable projection, and the image encoder is frozen throughout, keeping the trainable parameters in the causal Transformer and the small MLP heads.

## Results

On RLBench (12 simulation tasks), ARM4R achieves the best average success rate of 59.47%, surpassing PerAct (55.33%), LLARVA (48.33%), and ManiGaussian (48.00%). On the real Kinova Gen3 robot (13 tasks), ARM4R achieves an average success rate of 83.1%, dramatically outperforming OpenVLA (37.2%), π₀-FAST (21.2%), LLARVA (18.3%), and ATM (6.4%). In the cross-robot generalization experiment, adding human video pre-training plus Kinova fine-tuning improves average performance on the Franka Emika Panda by 19.6% over training from scratch.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ARM4R outperforms all nine baselines across both simulation and real-robot evaluations. On RLBench, ARM4R (59.47%) beats PerAct (55.33%) by 4.1 points, LLARVA (48.33%) by 11.1 points, ManiGaussian (48.00%) by 11.5 points, and C2FARM-BC (23.83%) by 35.6 points. On the real Kinova robot, the gaps are far larger: ARM4R (83.1%) surpasses OpenVLA (37.2%) by 45.9 points, π₀-FAST (21.2%) by 61.9 points, LLARVA (18.3%) by 64.8 points, and ATM (6.4%) by 76.7 points. Notably, ARM4R is pre-trained exclusively on human video data (Epic-Kitchens100), while OpenVLA and π₀-FAST are pre-trained on the much larger OpenX robotic dataset, suggesting that the 4D point-tracking representation is more effective than scale of robotic pre-training data. In the pre-training comparison (Table 3), ARM4R also outperforms MVP (75.0/18.75/81.25), RPT (87.5/31.25/93.75), Octo (56.25/12.5/37.5), and ATM (7.11/2.0/6.67) on the three Kinova cube tasks, confirming that 4D point-tracking pre-training is superior to both vision-encoder pre-training and 2D point-tracking pre-training.

## Relation to Prior Work

ARM4R departs from the dominant VLA paradigm (OpenVLA, LLARVA, RT-2, π₀-FAST) that relies on high-level language decoders pretrained on captioning or VQA, instead adopting a low-level 4D representation (3D point tracks across time) as the pre-training objective. It extends the 2D point-tracking line of work (ATM, Track2Act, RoboPoint) by lifting 2D observations into 3D via monocular depth estimation (building on SpatialTracker) and tracking points across time, thereby adding geometric structure that aligns with robot kinematics. The three-stage training recipe (human video pre-training → robotic scene fine-tuning → control fine-tuning) is a novel pipeline that bridges the embodiment gap between egocentric human videos and stationary-camera robotic setups.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical implication is that effective robotic pre-training can be achieved using freely available human video data (Epic-Kitchens100) rather than expensive large-scale robotic datasets, provided the representation captures 3D spatial dynamics. This opens a path toward scalable, data-efficient robotics without requiring orders-of-magnitude more robot demonstrations. However, limitations remain: tracking in camera coordinates couples object and camera motion, the fixed uniform grid lacks adaptivity to task-relevant regions, and the single-viewpoint design limits occlusion robustness. Future work on world-coordinate tracking (e.g., via MonST3R or MegaSAM), multi-view fusion, and selective point tracking could address these gaps and broaden applicability.
