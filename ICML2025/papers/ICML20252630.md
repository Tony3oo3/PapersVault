## Problem

Standard LLM unlearning evaluation pipelines randomly sample data for removal, capturing only average-case privacy risk and systematically underestimating leakage for minority populations whose rare PII (e.g., uncommon phone area codes) is unlikely to be selected. Because minority data points tend to be more strongly memorized, they are also harder to unlearn, yet current protocols never test this worst case. The paper demonstrates that minorities suffer at least 20% higher privacy leakage across six unlearning methods, three MIA variants, three datasets, and two model scales.

## Input / Output

The evaluation takes a fine-tuned LLM (GPT-2 117M or Llama-2 7B with LoRA), a forget set of 100 PII-containing samples (1% of the training set), and an unlearning algorithm constrained to a matched 10-Complexity-Unit budget. The primary output is the maximum PrivLeak (PL) score across three settings—Random, Canary, and Minority—along with worst-case perplexity, yielding a single minority-aware privacy risk estimate per method. The canary construction is a key design detail: only the PII field (area code, email domain, or judgment year) is swapped to the least-frequent value while all other email content remains byte-identical, isolating the minority effect from any confounding content differences.

## Method

The paper introduces a minority-aware LLM unlearning evaluation protocol that augments the standard random-sampling pipeline with two additional settings: a Canary setting where PII in the forget set is replaced with the dataset's least-frequent PII (keeping all other content identical to isolate the minority effect), and a Minority setting where the forget set is directly constructed from samples containing the rarest PII. They benchmark six unlearning methods—Random Labels, EUk/CFk, Gradient Ascent, NegGrad+, SCRUB, and Langevin Unlearning—under this protocol using three MIA variants (lossMIA, zlibMIA, Min-K%) and the PrivLeak metric, all under a matched 10-Complexity-Unit computational budget to ensure fair comparison. Experiments span three PII types (phone area codes, email domains, judgment years) across the Enron and ECHR datasets at two model scales. A central empirical finding is that Langevin Unlearning, the only method incorporating noise via DP-SGD, achieves the most favorable privacy-utility trade-off, while gradient ascent proves unstable and highly sensitive to forget set size and iteration count.

## Results

Across six unlearning methods, three MIA variants (lossMIA, zlibMIA, Min-K%), three datasets (Enron-Phone, Enron-Email, ECHR-Year), and two LLM scales (GPT-2 117M, Llama-2 7B), minority data (Canary/Minority settings) suffers at least 20% higher privacy leakage (PL) than the standard Random evaluation in nearly all cases. For GPT-2 on Enron-Phone, the No Unlearn baseline shows PL of 0.190 (Random) vs. 0.340 (Minority, 79%↑) under lossMIA; for Llama-2 7B, the gap is even more extreme at 0.060 (Random) vs. 0.242 (Canary, 303%↑). Among unlearning methods, Langevin Unlearning achieves the best privacy-utility trade-off (e.g., PL of 0.093 Random / 0.157 Minority for GPT-2 lossMIA with perplexity ~13.88), while Gradient Ascent is unstable and degrades utility severely on Enron-Email (perplexity up to 27.3).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper benchmarks six unlearning methods under the proposed minority-aware evaluation. Langevin Unlearning (Chien et al., 2024a,b) achieves the best overall privacy-utility trade-off: on GPT-2 Enron-Phone it reaches PL of 0.093 (Random) / 0.157 (Minority) under lossMIA while maintaining perplexity at ~13.88, outperforming SCRUB (Kurmanji et al., 2024) which shows higher PL (0.167/0.321) at similar perplexity (~12.96). Gradient Ascent (Golatkar et al., 2020a; Graves et al., 2021) is the most unstable: it produces negative PL (over-forgetting) on Llama-2 (e.g., −0.562 Random, −0.464 Minority for lossMIA) and degrades perplexity to 27.3 on Enron-Email, making it unreliable in practice. EUk/CFk (Goel et al., 2022) show the most dramatic underestimation gaps (EUk: 362%↑ for Minority on GPT-2 lossMIA), indicating that partial-layer retraining is particularly ineffective for minority data. Random Labels (Golatkar et al., 2020a; Yao et al., 2024) and NegGrad+ (Kurmanji et al., 2024) exhibit moderate but consistent underestimation (40–80%↑) and are more stable than GA but less effective than Langevin. SCRUB (Kurmanji et al., 2024) is stable but consistently shows 10–20% higher PL than Langevin across settings, confirming that noise incorporation (DP-SGD) provides a meaningful privacy advantage over KL-regularized approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the canary-injection methodology from the privacy auditing literature (Jagielski et al., 2020; Steinke et al., 2024) to the machine unlearning evaluation setting, where it had not previously been applied. It builds directly on the PrivLeak metric and MIA-based evaluation framework from MUSE (Shi et al., 2024b), but departs from the standard random-sampling protocol by introducing a minority-aware evaluation that considers worst-case privacy leakage across Random, Canary, and Minority settings. The unlearning methods benchmarked (Gradient Ascent, SCRUB, NegGrad+, Langevin Unlearning, EUk/CFk, Random Labels) are all established heuristics; the paper's conceptual contribution lies in the evaluation protocol rather than in new unlearning algorithms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that minority data suffers at least 20% higher privacy leakage across all tested methods, datasets, and model scales reveals a systematic blind spot in how the field currently assesses unlearning efficacy, with direct implications for GDPR 'right to be forgotten' compliance. The proposed minority-aware evaluation protocol is lightweight (reporting max PL across three settings) and can be readily adopted as a standard addition to unlearning benchmarks. Open questions include whether the canary-based PII proxy generalizes to non-PII minority attributes (e.g., dialect, rare entities), whether the gap widens at larger model scales beyond 7B, and whether noise-based methods like Langevin Unlearning can close the minority-specific leakage gap with further tuning.
