## Problem

Greedy Task Allocation (GTA), used in state-of-the-art parallel SGD methods like Rennala SGD and Ringmaster ASGD, minimizes iteration completion time but wastes resources by running far more than B tasks per round (e.g., 990 unnecessary tasks with 1000 workers and B=10). The open question is how to achieve near-optimal completion time without prior knowledge of heterogeneous, random worker computation time distributions, while guaranteeing that no more than B tasks are completed per round.

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes an allocation budget B (number of tasks per round), an upper bound α on the maximum Orlicz norm of worker computation times (or η = max_i α_i/μ_i for ATA-Empirical), and sequentially observes computation times only for workers it has allocated tasks to (partial/semi-bandit feedback). It outputs a per-round allocation vector a_k ∈ ℕ^n with ||a_k||_1 = B specifying how many tasks each of the n workers performs. The action space is combinatorial with cardinality C(n+B-1, B), and the algorithm does not require knowledge of the total number of rounds K.

## Method

ATA reformulates task allocation as a stochastic combinatorial multi-armed bandit problem with a non-linear, discontinuous proxy loss ℓ(a, μ) = max_i a_i μ_i, which is shown to bound the expected computation time within a factor of (1 + 4η ln B). Each round, ATA computes lower confidence bounds on worker means using sub-exponential concentration inequalities (parameterized by the Orlicz norm α), then selects the allocation minimizing ℓ(a, s_k) via a Recursive Allocation Selection (RAS) subroutine running in O(n ln(min{B,n}) + min{B,n}²) time. ATA-Empirical improves upon this with a novel data-dependent concentration inequality that adapts to each arm's individual Orlicz norm rather than the global maximum, yielding tighter confidence intervals. The theoretical guarantee shows total computation time is within a (1 + 4η ln B) multiplicative factor of the oracle-optimal allocation plus an O(ln K) additive term, recovering standard MAB regret bounds in the B=1 special case.

## Results

On the convex quadratic benchmark with B=23, ATA and ATA-Empirical achieve total worker time ratios of 12.45× and 14.1× over GTA at n=459 workers, closely matching OFTA's 27.3× (oracle) while requiring no prior knowledge of computation times. In terms of runtime, ATA and ATA-Empirical are 6.36× and 5.51× slower than GTA at n=459, compared to OFTA's constant 2.17× overhead. On CIFAR-100 with a 160k-parameter CNN trained via Adam (step size 8×10⁻⁵), both ATA variants converge to OFTA-level total worker time after an initial exploration phase, while GTA's resource waste grows unboundedly with n.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares ATA and ATA-Empirical against three baselines: GTA-SGD (Rennala SGD, the greedy approach), OFTA (oracle optimal fixed allocation), and UTA (uniform random allocation). GTA is fastest in wall-clock runtime but becomes arbitrarily wasteful in total worker time as n grows (its total worker time ratio over OFTA reaches 27.3× at n=459), since it keeps all workers busy and discards excess gradients. ATA and ATA-Empirical match OFTA closely in total worker time (within a small constant factor) without any prior knowledge of computation time distributions, confirming the theoretical guarantee of Theorem 4.2. The runtime overhead of ATA/ATA-Empirical over GTA grows with n (from 1.73× at n=17 to 6.36× at n=459) due to the exploration phase, whereas OFTA's overhead remains constant at ~2.17×. On CIFAR-100, both ATA variants converge to OFTA-level efficiency after initial exploration, while GTA's resource waste continues to scale with the number of workers.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Lower Confidence Bound (LCB) framework from Auer (2002) and the greedy combinatorial bandit approach of Chen et al. (2013), extending them to a novel setting with a non-continuous loss function and a combinatorial action space of size C(n+B-1, B). It directly addresses the resource wastefulness inherent in Greedy Task Allocation (GTA) used by recent optimal-time-complexity methods like Rennala SGD (Tyurin & Richtárik, 2024) and Ringmaster ASGD (Maranjyan et al., 2025b), which the authors identify as the first step toward reducing asynchrony overhead. Unlike federated learning approaches that adjust local steps based on prior knowledge of worker speeds, ATA learns computation time distributions adaptively online, and unlike prior combinatorial bandit work, it handles a discontinuous loss function without smoothness or Lipschitz assumptions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, drop-in replacement for GTA that eliminates arbitrary resource waste in all asynchronous and parallel SGD variants, with provable logarithmic regret and a constant multiplicative gap to the oracle-optimal allocation. The practical impact is significant for large-scale distributed training where worker heterogeneity is common and compute costs are substantial. Open questions include whether the multiplicative factor (1+4η ln B) can be tightened to 1, how the method performs under non-stationary computation time distributions, and whether the exploration overhead becomes prohibitive in very large-scale deployments with thousands of workers. The reliance on an upper bound on the Orlicz norm (or η for ATA-Empirical) as prior knowledge is a mild but non-trivial practical requirement.
