## Problem

While LLMs have demonstrated strong chemical knowledge, their ability to navigate the highly constrained, multi-step retrosynthesis planning problem—where only valid reaction templates and commercially available precursors are permitted—remains largely unexplored. Prior LLM-based retrosynthesis work either fine-tunes the base model or uses LLMs as single-step predictors within search algorithms, leaving open whether LLMs can directly generate and optimize complete multi-step synthetic pathways without any parameter updates.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252494/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a target molecule in SMILES format, a fixed set of reaction templates (SMARTS strings from USPTO), and a canonicalized set of ~23M purchasable building blocks from eMolecules. It outputs a multi-step retrosynthetic route encoded as a linear sequence of decision steps, where each step specifies the current molecule set, a rational explanation, the selected product, the reaction template, the predicted reactants, and the updated molecule set. For synthesizable molecular design, the input additionally includes a property oracle function, and the output is a property-optimized molecule guaranteed to have a valid synthesis pathway (SC score ≤ 3.5).

## Method

The core contribution is LLM-Syn-Planner, an evolutionary search algorithm that prompts an LLM (GPT-4o or DeepSeek-V3, no fine-tuning) to generate entire multi-step retrosynthetic pathways rather than single reaction steps. Routes are encoded in a linear sequential format (not a tree) to reduce exponential complexity, and each step includes a 'Rational' reasoning field to elicit chain-of-thought. An evolutionary loop—Initialize (with RAG using Morgan-fingerprint Tanimoto similarity to retrieve reference routes), Evaluate (three-level feedback: molecule validity/availability, reaction existence/validity via database matching, route connectivity), Select (top-n by partial SC-score reward at the first invalid step), and Mutate (LLM edits the partial route from the first invalid step with feedback)—iterates until a valid route is found or the model-call budget is exhausted. For synthesizable molecular design, LLM-Syn-Designer wraps MolLEO's LLM-based genetic operators with SC-score filtering and uses LLM-Syn-Planner as the synthesis pathway validator in each evolutionary round.

## Results

On retrosynthesis planning, LLM-Syn-Planner (GPT-4o) achieves solve rates of 91.0% (USPTO-Easy), 64.7% (USPTO-190), 93.3% (Pistachio Reachable), and 72.0% (Pistachio Hard) at N=100 model calls, and reaches 100%, 92.1%, 98.0%, and 87.0% respectively at N=500. The DeepSeek-V3 variant performs comparably, reaching 93.0%, 62.1%, 96.7%, and 74.0% at N=100. In contrast, using LLMs as single-step predictors within MCTS or Retro* yields dramatically lower solve rates (e.g., 0.0% on Pistachio Hard), while LLM-Syn-Planner's evolutionary full-route optimization closes the gap with specialized single-step models. For synthesizable molecular design, LLM-Syn-Designer achieves competitive or superior fitness to Graph-GA, REINVENT, MolLEO, and MARS while enforcing synthesizability constraints.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest single-step model baseline, RootAligned (Zhong et al., 2022), LLM-Syn-Planner trails on USPTO-Easy (91.0% vs. 98.0% MCTS / 99.0% Retro*) and USPTO-190 (64.7% vs. 79.4%/86.8%), but is competitive on Pistachio Hard (72.0%/74.0% vs. 83.0%/78.0%). Against Graph2Edits (Zhong et al., 2023), LLM-Syn-Planner clearly wins on USPTO-190 (64.7% vs. 42.7%/51.1%) and Pistachio Hard (72.0%/74.0% vs. 26.0%/71.0%), and is comparable on USPTO-Easy and Pistachio Reachable. Against LocalRetro (Chen & Jung, 2021), LLM-Syn-Planner wins on USPTO-190 (64.7% vs. 44.3%/51.0%) and Pistachio Hard (72.0%/74.0% vs. 52.0%/63.0%), but is slightly behind on USPTO-Easy (91.0% vs. 92.5%/95.5%). The most striking comparison is LLM-Syn-Planner versus LLM-as-single-step-predictor: the evolutionary full-route approach improves solve rates by 36–72 percentage points over MCTS/Retro* integration (e.g., 72.0% vs. 0.0% on Pistachio Hard). For synthesizable molecular design (Figure 3), LLM-Syn-Designer matches or exceeds Graph-GA, REINVENT, MolLEO, and MARS in top-1 expected property while uniquely guaranteeing synthesizability of the output molecules.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of coupling LLMs (or specialized models) as single-step reaction predictors with search algorithms like MCTS or Retro*, showing that this approach underperforms significantly. Instead, it reformulates retrosynthesis as a full-route generation problem where the LLM proposes complete multi-step pathways, optimized via an evolutionary algorithm with partial SC-score rewards and retrieval-augmented initialization. The route representation draws on the tree-search framework of Segler et al. (2018) but converts it to a linear sequential format more amenable to LLM generation, and the synthesizable design component builds directly on MolLEO (Wang et al., 2024) by integrating LLM-Syn-Planner as a synthesizability filter. Unlike prior LLM retrosynthesis efforts (Nguyen-Van et al., 2024; Yang et al., 2024) that fine-tune or instruction-tune the base model, this work operates entirely zero-shot.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that zero-shot LLMs can match or exceed specialized single-step models on retrosynthesis when the problem is reframed as full-route generation with evolutionary optimization offers a practical, retraining-free alternative for synthesis planning that adapts immediately to new reaction databases or design goals. Key limitations include persistent failure under sparse rewards (e.g., shooting setups), dependence on a fixed template set that inherits reactivity/selectivity issues, and the high inference cost of large LLMs. Open questions include how to integrate explicit search when LLMs cannot generate any viable path, and whether the evolutionary + LLM paradigm generalizes to more flexible constraints like material-limited or multi-objective synthesis planning.
