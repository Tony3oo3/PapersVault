## Problem

Designing differentially private algorithms for binary linear classification that adapt to large-margin structure without requiring prior knowledge of the margin value or which points are outliers remains an open challenge. Prior work either assumed known geometric margin and full linear separability (Nguyen et al., 2020) or relied on data-independent confidence margins with weaker O(1/√n) rates (Bassily et al., 2022). The fundamental obstacle is that margin is highly unstable under single-point changes, rendering standard DP techniques such as smoothed sensitivity or propose-test-release inapplicable.

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes a labeled binary dataset S = {(x_i, y_i)}_{i=1}^n with x_i ∈ B^d(1) and y_i ∈ {±1}, plus privacy parameters (ε, δ), and requires no knowledge of the data margin or outlier structure. It outputs a linear classifier w_out ∈ R^d that is (ε, δ)-DP, with a zero-one risk bound of Õ(1/(nγ²ε) + |S_out|/(γn)) that simultaneously adapts to the best margin subset S \ S_out over all possible outlier removals. The margin grid is constructed data-independently as a logarithmic grid {1/n, 2/n, 4/n, …, 1}, and the full algorithm runs in polynomial time.

## Method

The core technical contribution is an inlier-outlier analysis of noisy gradient descent that decomposes the empirical hinge loss into a zero inlier component (when the hinge parameter c ≤ γ) and a bounded outlier component proportional to |S_out|/c, yielding a dimension-free convergence bound that avoids the √d term typical of private gradient methods. The main algorithm M* constructs a data-independent logarithmic grid of candidate margins, builds data-oblivious Johnson-Lindenstrauss projection matrices with dimension Õ(1/γ²) for each candidate (preserving margin without consuming privacy budget), and privately selects the best margin via a hyperparameter tuning procedure minimizing empirical zero-one loss. The base optimization subroutine projects data into the lower-dimensional space, clips to a bounded ball, and runs full-batch noisy gradient descent with hinge loss ℓ_{γ/3}, using the normalized max-margin separator of the inlier set as the reference point to enable the inlier-outlier decomposition. Privacy is managed through Gaussian DP composition across the grid with a final conversion to (ε, δ)-DP, and an advanced variant replaces brute-force selection with a truncated negative binomial sampling scheme that scales the privacy budget only logarithmically with the number of hyperparameter configurations.

## Results

The main theoretical result (Theorem 4.1) achieves an empirical and population zero-one risk bound of Õ(1/(nγ²ε) + |S_out|/(γn)) for any subset S_out whose removal yields a γ-margin separable remainder, without requiring prior knowledge of γ or S_out. In the realizable case (S_out = ∅), this recovers the 1/(nγ²ε) rate of Nguyen et al. (2020) but adaptively, without needing the margin as input. In the agnostic case with |S_out| = o(√n), the bound achieves a √n improvement over Bassily et al. (2022, Thm. 3.2), which has a 1/(√n·γ·√ε) dependence. A motivating CIFAR-10 experiment (Figure 2) shows that after removing ~0.1% of points, both ViT and ResNet-50 features yield positive geometric margins, with ViT features achieving larger margins more quickly.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Nguyen et al. (2020), the proposed method matches the 1/(nγ²ε) realizable-case rate but removes the requirement that γ be known in advance, making the algorithm fully adaptive. Against Bassily et al. (2022, Thm. 3.2), the paper achieves a √n improvement in the agnostic case: when the number of outliers |S_out| is o(√n), the proposed bound scales as 1/(nγ²ε) + |S_out|/(γn) rather than 1/(√n·γ·√ε) + min_w L̃_S^γ(w). Against Bassily et al. (2022, Thm. 3.1), the proposed method is polynomial-time (vs. exponential-time for Thm. 3.1) and avoids the bounded parameter space assumption (‖w‖ ≤ C). The key advantage is adaptivity: the algorithm simultaneously competes with the best S_out ⊂ S that minimizes the bound without explicitly searching over 2^n subsets, incurring no log(2^n) statistical penalty. The CIFAR-10 margin analysis (Figure 2) provides empirical motivation but is not a head-to-head accuracy comparison.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on differentially private learning of large-margin halfspaces initiated by Nguyen et al. (2020), which required known geometric margin and linear separability, and further developed by Bassily et al. (2022), which used a data-independent confidence margin but achieved worse rates in the agnostic case. The key departure is an inlier-outlier analysis of noisy gradient descent that allows the algorithm to adapt to the margin γ and outlier set S_out without either being specified as input, using a data-independent doubling grid over margin values combined with private hyperparameter selection. The optimization core (JL projection + noisy gradient descent) follows Bassily et al. (2014), while the advanced private tuning subroutine adapts the truncated negative binomial scheme from Papernot & Steinke (2022).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper resolves a fundamental tension in DP learning: margin is highly unstable under single-point perturbations (one point can shift margin from positive to zero), yet large margins are known to drive generalization. The adaptive mechanism avoids the log(2^n) statistical cost that naive adaptivity to all subsets would incur, achieving a √n improvement over prior agnostic-case bounds when outliers are few. An open question is whether the 1/γ factor in the outlier term |S_out|/(γn) can be eliminated, which is unlikely without additional noise-model assumptions given NP-hardness of proper agnostic halfspace learning. The empirical connection to neural collapse and pre-trained features (ViT vs. ResNet-50 margin analysis on CIFAR-10) suggests practical relevance for private fine-tuning, though the experimental validation is limited to a single margin plot.
