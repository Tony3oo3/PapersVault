## Problem

Prior label error detection methods are predominantly unimodal (relying only on image representations) or require training a task-specific classifier, and no prior work has proposed or rigorously compared methods for detecting errors in datasets with natural language labels such as image captions. Additionally, the impact of noisy captioning data on downstream model performance had not been concretely assessed. This paper addresses both gaps by proposing a training-free, multimodal neighborhood-based approach and evaluating its effect on downstream captioning and classification.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252983/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a dataset of image-caption pairs (or image-class-label pairs for classification datasets, where the label name serves as the text modality) along with a pretrained contrastive multimodal encoder (e.g., OpenAI CLIP ViT-B/32 or BiomedCLIP ViT-B/16 for medical images). It produces a single continuous mislabel score per sample, where higher scores indicate greater likelihood of a label error. The score is computed using the training/reference split to retrieve nearest neighbors, and the method operates without any task-specific training or fine-tuning of the encoder.

## Method

LEMON computes a mislabel score as a weighted linear combination of three terms: (1) the direct cosine distance between image and text embeddings (d_mm), (2) s_n, which retrieves k nearest neighbors of the query image in image space and averages the text-space distance between their corresponding captions and the query caption, weighted by exponential decay terms that downweight distant neighbors and neighbors whose own image-text pairs are poorly matched, and (3) s_m, the analogous term using text-space neighbors and image-space distances. The key insight is that the neighborhood geometry in the joint embedding space of contrastively pretrained models provides complementary signal beyond pairwise image-text similarity: a correctly labeled sample should have neighbors in both modalities whose cross-modal counterparts are also close. The paper provides a theoretical guarantee (Theorem 4.1) showing that under mild assumptions about distance distributions and a paraphrase function, the neighborhood score yields better-than-random AUROC for distinguishing mislabeled from correctly labeled samples.

## Results

On the four captioning datasets, LEMON_OPT achieves AUROC of 94.5 (Flickr30k), 95.6 (MSCOCO), 86.0 (MMIMDB), and 70.4 (MIMIC-CXR), outperforming the CLIP similarity baseline by up to 6.3 AUROC points (MIMIC-CXR: 70.4 vs. 64.1). On the four classification datasets, LEMON_OPT reaches AUROC of 98.1 (CIFAR-10), 90.8 (CIFAR-100), 90.0 (miniImageNet), and 73.1 (StanfordCars), beating the best training-free baseline (Deep k-NN) by up to 6.8 AUROC points on miniImageNet. Filtering the top 40% of predicted mislabels with LEMON_OPT improves downstream captioning BLEU-4 by ~3.6 points on MSCOCO (36.0 → 39.6) and ~0.5 points on Flickr30k (31.6 → 32.1) over noisy training, approaching clean-data performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LEMON_OPT outperforms the strongest training-free baseline (CLIP Similarity) on 3 of 4 captioning datasets (MSCOCO: 95.6 vs. 93.8; MMIMDB: 86.0 vs. 85.1; MIMIC-CXR: 70.4 vs. 64.1) but is marginally behind on Flickr30k (94.5 vs. 94.8). Against the supervised oracle CapFilt, LEMON_OPT is outperformed on Flickr30k (94.5 vs. 98.6) and MSCOCO (95.6 vs. 99.3) but substantially exceeds it on MMIMDB (86.0 vs. 82.7) and MIMIC-CXR (70.4 vs. 49.7), showing the oracle does not transfer across domains. On classification datasets, LEMON_OPT beats all training-free baselines including Deep k-NN (by up to 6.8 AUROC on miniImageNet: 90.0 vs. 83.2) and SimiFeat, and is competitive with the training-dependent AUM and Datamap methods, outperforming them on miniImageNet (90.0 vs. 83.1/85.1) and StanfordCars (73.1 vs. 70.4/72.2). LEMON_FIX with fixed hyperparameters (k=30, β=γ=5) achieves within ~1.6 AUROC of LEMON_OPT on average, confirming robustness without a labeled validation set. The VDC baseline (using multimodal LLMs) is competitive on Flickr30k and MSCOCO but degrades sharply on MIMIC-CXR (50.8 AUROC), where LEMON_OPT leads by nearly 20 points.

## Relation to Prior Work

LEMON extends the unimodal k-nearest-neighbor label error detection line (Deep k-NN, SimiFeat) to the multimodal setting by retrieving neighbors in both image and text embedding spaces of a contrastively pretrained model like CLIP, then combining cross-modal distance signals. It is explicitly a generalization: setting β=γ=0 recovers CLIP similarity, and a specific parameter regime recovers Deep k-NN. The key conceptual departure from prior work is that most label error detection methods are either unimodal (image-only) or require training a task-specific classifier, whereas LEMON is training-free and exploits the joint geometry of the multimodal embedding space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As vision-language models are increasingly pretrained on tens of millions of web-scraped image-caption pairs, reliable automated filtering of mislabeled instances is practically critical, yet no prior work had systematically compared detection methods for captioning datasets or measured downstream impact. The paper's finding that pretraining CLIP from scratch on noisy in-domain data (MIMIC-CXR) can outperform a large externally pretrained model (BiomedCLIP) suggests domain alignment may matter more than pretraining scale for detection tasks. Open questions include how the method handles ambiguous or partially correct labels (e.g., blurry images), whether filtering introduces systematic biases against rare or atypical examples, and whether the approach transfers to other multimodal pairings beyond image-text.
