## Problem

LLMs struggle on ARC-AGI abstract reasoning tasks despite possessing latent reasoning capacities, with prior open-source approaches plateauing below 63% while closed-source systems achieve higher scores at prohibitive cost (~$17/task). The paper identifies that the core bottleneck is not model capacity but the inability to reliably express reasoning under a single problem representation, and asks whether exploiting valid semantic-preserving transformations (augmentations) can unlock this latent ability at minimal inference cost.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250678/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each task provides k input-output grid pairs (grids up to 30×30 over 10 colors) and a single test input grid; the model must produce the correct output grid within two guesses. The tokenization uses a reduced 64-token vocabulary (one token per cell, no digit-grouping merges) with a short pre-prompt alphabet, and the method internally generates 16 augmented versions of each task (D8 symmetries × 2 with randomized color permutations and example orderings) for both candidate generation and scoring.

## Method

The approach fine-tunes an 8B-parameter LLM (NeMo-Minitron) via LoRA on RE-ARC augmented data, then performs per-task test-time training on the task's own examples. At inference, a depth-first search with a probability threshold T=9% systematically enumerates high-probability candidate solutions across 16 augmentations, pruning partial paths whose cumulative log-probability falls below T. Each candidate is then re-scored under all 16 augmentations, and the final selection uses a Product-of-Experts geometric mean of per-augmentation likelihoods, which the paper proves (via a KL-divergence bound) to be superior in expectation to any single augmentation. This dual generator-scorer role for a single model, combined with augmentation-aware DFS, achieves 71.6% two-guess accuracy at ~$0.02/task.

## Results

The method achieves 71.6% two-guess accuracy (286.5/400 tasks) on the ARC-AGI public evaluation set, setting a new state-of-the-art among open-source approaches and surpassing the average human performance of 60.2%. On ConceptARC, the same pipeline reaches 73.3% two-guess accuracy, and on Sudoku 3M it achieves 53% top-1 accuracy (vs. <3% for prior SOTA LLMs). The full inference cost is approximately $0.02 per task on a single RTX 4090, compared to $17 per task for the higher-scoring closed-source GPT o3.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method (71.6%) outperforms all open-source baselines: TTT (53.5%, +18.1 pts), BARC (56.75%, +14.9 pts), and the combined TTT+BARC pipeline (62.8%, +8.8 pts). It also surpasses the average human performance of 60.2% by 11.4 points. Among closed-source systems, it beats o1-preview (21%), GPT-4o (42%), and Sonnet 3.5 with evolutionary test-time compute (58.5%), but falls short of GPT o3 (82.8%) by 11.2 points. However, the cost disparity is extreme: the proposed method costs ~$0.02 per task versus $17 per task for o3, a factor of ~850×. The method also generalizes beyond ARC, reaching 73.3% on ConceptARC and 53% on Sudoku (vs. <3% for prior LLM baselines), confirming that the gains are not dataset-specific.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the test-time training (TTT) paradigm of Akyürek et al. (2024) and the induction/transduction framework of BARC (Li et al., 2025), extending both with a depth-first search over the LLM's probability tree for candidate generation and a product-of-experts (PoE) scoring step that re-evaluates each candidate under multiple semantic-preserving augmentations. The PoE component draws on Hinton's (1999, 2002) classical ensemble theory, here instantiated with geometric-mean pooling over augmentation-induced likelihoods and backed by a KL-divergence bound (Theorem 4.1). The data pipeline relies on RE-ARC (Hodel, 2024) for augmentation-aware training, deliberately avoiding other ARC-like datasets to prevent conceptual leakage into the evaluation set.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 71.6% public-eval score at roughly $0.02 per task (vs. $17 for OpenAI o3) demonstrates that structured inference-time search and multi-perspective scoring can close most of the gap to closed-source systems using a single 8B open-weight model, making high-level abstract reasoning accessible on consumer hardware. The generalization to Sudoku (53% vs. <3% for prior LLM baselines) and ConceptARC (73.3%) suggests the augmentation-as-ensemble principle is not ARC-specific. Open questions include whether text-based or stylistic augmentations can deliver similar PoE gains for natural-language reasoning, whether the DFS threshold T can be adapted automatically per task, and whether the approach transfers to domains where valid semantic-preserving transformations are harder to define.
