## Problem

Cardinality sketches of size k can be broken by adaptive adversaries after only Õ(k²) queries — the so-called quadratic barrier — because queries become correlated with the sketch's internal randomness. In realistic workloads, however, individual keys participate in far fewer queries than the total number t, suggesting the barrier should depend on per-key participation r rather than t. The paper asks whether a robust composable sketch can be designed whose guarantees depend on r instead of t, enabling an exponential number of total queries as long as r = Õ(k²).

## Input / Output

The method takes a bottom-k sketch S (a set of at most k key-priority pairs (i, ρᵢ) for a query set V ⊆ [n]) and returns a (1±α)-accurate estimate of |V|. The Basic Robust Estimator is stateless and requires only the sketch plus parameters k, r, n, α, β. The Tracking Robust Estimator additionally maintains a server-side dictionary C of per-key participation counts that is updated after each query; deactivated keys (those exceeding r participations) are excluded from future estimates, and the guarantee degrades smoothly based on the fraction of deactivated entries in the sketch.

## Method

The core contribution is a fine-grained adaptive data analysis (ADA) framework that reformulates the Hassidim et al. robustness wrapper from a differential-privacy reduction to an ADA reduction, enabling per-key granularity. The sketch's random priority vector ρ is treated as a dataset, and cardinality estimation is expressed as a sequence of linear threshold queries h_{V,τ}(i, ρᵢ) = 1[i∈V ∧ ρᵢ < τ] over ρ. A fine-grained Sparse Vector Technique with per-key charging (Algorithm 1) bounds privacy loss by the per-key participation r rather than total queries, yielding a generalization error of O(α⁻¹√r · log^{3/2}(mn/β)). The Basic Robust Estimator performs a geometric binary search over τ with Laplace noise, guaranteeing (1±α) accuracy when every key appears in at most r query sketches; the Tracking Robust Estimator extends this by deactivating keys after r participations, maintaining accuracy as long as at most αk/4 sketch entries are deactivated per query.

## Results

The paper's main theoretical result shows that a bottom-k cardinality sketch of size k = O(α⁻²√r log^{3/2}(n/β)) can handle an exponential number of adaptive queries provided each key participates in at most r = Õ(k²) query sketches, shifting the quadratic barrier from total queries t to per-key participation r. Empirically, on synthetic query sets (support size 10⁶, query size 5×10³), the fine-grained estimators achieve gains of two orders of magnitude (≈100×) over the baseline for uniformly sampled queries, 40× for Pareto-distributed queries with α=2, and 12× for the highly skewed Pareto case with α=1.5. The tracking estimator (TRobustEst) provides smooth degradation, maintaining (1+α)-accuracy as long as at most αk/4 sketch entries are deactivated per query.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is the generic robustness wrapper of Hassidim et al. (2020), which uses differential privacy to support t adaptive queries at the cost of √t independent sketch copies, yielding a space complexity of k ≈ √t/α² (equivalently, t = Õ(k²) queries). The proposed fine-grained estimators (RobustEst and TRobustEst) outperform this baseline by a factor of 12× to 100× in the number of guaranteed adaptive queries for the same sketch size k, depending on the query distribution. For uniform query sets, the gain reaches two orders of magnitude even without key tracking (using only RobustEst). For Pareto-distributed queries where a small fraction of heavy-hitter keys appear in many sketches, the tracking estimator TRobustEst is necessary and still achieves 12×–40× gains. The key insight is that in realistic settings, per-key participation r is much smaller than total queries t, and the fine-grained analysis exploits this gap to provide substantially stronger guarantees.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reformulates the robustness wrapper of Hassidim et al. (2020), which previously reduced robust sketch design to differential privacy, into a reduction to adaptive data analysis (ADA), enabling a fine-grained per-key analysis that the original DP-based wrapper could not express. The fine-grained Sparse Vector Technique (Algorithm 1) is built directly on the target-charging technique of Cohen & Lyu (2023a), and the overall framework generalizes the robust CountSketch analysis of Cohen et al. (2022a). The paper positions itself against the matching upper/lower bounds establishing the quadratic barrier (Hassidim et al. 2020 upper; Cohen et al. 2024 lower), showing that the barrier is really a per-key participation barrier rather than a total-query barrier.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result is practically significant for systems that reuse cardinality sketches across many adaptive queries (e.g., BigQuery, distributed analytics), where the 12×–100× gain in guaranteed query count directly translates to longer sketch lifetimes before re-sampling is needed. The tracking estimator's smooth degradation (continuing to work as long as <α/4 of sketch entries are deactivated) is a useful design pattern for robust data structures more broadly. Open questions include extending the fine-grained ADA framework beyond linear queries to other MinHash variants (k-partition/PCSA), to ℓ₂ norm estimation with the AMS sketch, and to broader sublinear statistics like capping statistics.
