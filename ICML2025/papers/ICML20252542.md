## Problem

Pre-Contrast MRI images are essential for quantitative analysis, baseline evaluation, and comparative assessment in contrast-enhanced MRI workflows, yet they are frequently unavailable or severely degraded due to motion, aliasing, or acquisition constraints. Direct image-to-image synthesis from Post-Contrast to Pre-Contrast fails because contrast agent uptake introduces non-linear, spatially varying intensity changes that a single network cannot simultaneously model for both structural and contrast components. No prior method has produced Pre-Contrast syntheses of sufficient fidelity to be clinically interchangeable with standard-of-care acquisitions.

## Input / Output

The primary input is a Post-Contrast 3D T1-weighted MPRAGE volume (mean-normalized and affine-registered to the Pre-Contrast reference); the SPHERE-Art variant additionally accepts a corrupted Pre-Contrast volume to recover contrast information lost to artifacts. The model outputs a synthesized Pre-Contrast volume and an intermediate soft uptake mask M ∈ [0,1] that encodes per-pixel contrast agent concentration. All processing operates on 3D volumes from paired Pre/Low-Dose/Post-Contrast MPRAGE acquisitions at 3T, with the mask serving as a physics-grounded conditioning signal for the reconstruction stage rather than a standalone output.

## Method

SPHERE decomposes the intractable direct mapping S_pre = f(S_post) into two cascaded stages grounded in the SPGR steady-state signal equation: (1) a segmentation stage (NestedUNet + hyperintensity prior) predicts the uptake mask M from S_post, where the prior applies learnable soft-thresholding H = σ(u(γ·A(S_post) − β₀)) to enforce that enhanced regions correspond to hyperintense pixels; (2) an inpainting+ stage (NestedUNet with GAN training) reconstructs S_pre from (S_post, M) using the closed-form physics relation (Eq. 11) that links pre- and post-contrast signals through the exponential T1-shortening term e^{−TR·r₁·β·M}. The key insight is that the composite dependency S_pre = f_inpaint+(S_post, f_seg(S_post)) creates multiplicative optimization complexity in direct learning, which the staged design reduces to additive complexity by decoupling gradient conflicts between contrast-enhanced and non-enhanced regions. Training combines L1, SSIM, VGG perceptual, and adversarial losses for reconstruction with BCE and physics-based penalty terms (R_hyper, R_psyn) for the segmentation stage, all using NestedUNet backbones.

## Results

On Site A (13 test cases), SPHERE achieves PSNR 36.82 dB, SSIM 0.9026, CNR 0.0628, and LPIPS 0.0313, outperforming the strongest baseline BICEPS (35.25 dB, 0.8735, 0.0751, 0.0397) by 1.57 dB in PSNR. On Site B (10 test cases), SPHERE reaches PSNR 35.36 dB, SSIM 0.9263, CNR 0.0699, and LPIPS 0.0330, again surpassing BICEPS (34.50 dB, 0.9122, 0.0772, 0.0381). SPHERE also leads on all four contrast-specific metrics (GMSD, CFS, IRC, EIS) on both sites, with EIS reaching 0.5364 (Site A) and 0.4433 (Site B) versus 0.4253 and 0.3619 for BICEPS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SPHERE outperforms all eight baselines on every metric across both sites. The closest competitor is BICEPS (Xue et al., 2022), the only prior method specifically designed for Pre-to-Post contrast synthesis; SPHERE improves PSNR by 1.57 dB (Site A) and 0.86 dB (Site B) over BICEPS, while also reducing LPIPS by ~21% and CNR by ~16%. Among general-purpose image restoration models, UNet and Att-UNet are the strongest baselines (PSNR ~34.6–34.8 on Site A), but they still trail SPHERE by over 2 dB. Transformer- and state-space-based methods (SwinIR, UKAN, MambaIR) perform notably worse, with UKAN dropping to 24.14 dB PSNR on Site B, confirming the paper's argument that direct image-to-image learning is ill-suited for this task due to coupled contrast-structure dependencies. For the artifact-removal variant (SPHERE-Art), the method achieves 37.12 dB PSNR versus 33.58 dB for the best UNet-family baseline (R2-AttUNet), a 3.5 dB gap that underscores the benefit of the physics-grounded dual-stage design.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the direct image-to-image translation paradigm (UNet, SwinIR, MambaIR, UKAN) and the closest prior work, BICEPS (Xue et al., 2022), which attempted bidirectional Post-to-Pre synthesis via feature disentanglement but produced clinically unusable results. The key conceptual departure is grounding the synthesis in the SPGR signal equation, which motivates a two-stage decomposition: first learning a contrast uptake mask via segmentation with a hyperintensity prior, then performing a physics-constrained 'inpainting+' reconstruction. Rather than building on a specific prior architecture, the paper repurposes NestedUNet as a uniform backbone for all components, arguing that the physics-informed task decomposition—not architectural sophistication—is what enables clinical-quality synthesis.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

If validated at scale, this approach could eliminate the Pre-Contrast acquisition step in contrast-enhanced brain MRI, reducing scan time, cost, and patient risk while enabling downstream tasks (dose simulation, tumor assessment) that currently require both image types. The clinical reader study showing Syn-Pre scoring comparably to (and on some metrics above) SOC-Pre is a strong practical signal. However, open questions remain: the method is validated on only 23 test cases across two sites, assumes a simplified binary uptake mask that may not capture spatial heterogeneity of gadolinium concentration, and the Pre-to-Post direction (discussed in Appendix E.4) remains unsolved. Generalization to other contrast agents, sequences, or anatomies beyond brain/spine/breast is untested.
