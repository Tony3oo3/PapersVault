## Problem

Vision-language models face prohibitive computational costs when processing long videos: a one-minute clip at 24 FPS generates over 1 million visual tokens, far exceeding LLM context limits. Existing token pruning risks losing critical temporal dependencies, while feature merging dilutes semantic information, leaving the trade-off between efficiency and information fidelity unresolved for hour-scale videos.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250383/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

ViLAMP takes a video sampled at 1 FPS with frames at 384×384 resolution and a text query, producing a text answer (multiple-choice or free-form). The keyframe count K is fixed at 32 regardless of video length, so a 10K-frame video yields 32 full-resolution frames (each with 729 patch tokens) plus 9,968 single-token compressed non-keyframes, reducing total visual tokens from ~7.3M to ~33K. Frame-level relevance scoring uses a frozen CLIP-ViT-B-32 encoder, while patch-level merging uses the VLM's own SigLIP-so400m visual encoder.

## Method

ViLAMP implements a 'differential distillation' principle D(v) = R(v,Q) − T(v,C(v)) at two hierarchical levels. At the frame level, Differential Keyframe Selection (DKS) greedily ranks frames by CLIP query-relevance cosine similarity and selects K=32 keyframes that also satisfy a temporal-distinctiveness threshold (τ=0.85), achieving O(max(NK, N log N)) complexity. At the patch level, Differential Feature Merging (DFM) compresses each non-keyframe to a single token via softmax-weighted pooling, where per-patch weights are proportional to query relevance minus λ·cosine similarity to the spatially corresponding keyframe patch (λ=1, α=10⁻²). The architecture builds on LLaVA-OneVision (SigLIP-so400m + 2-layer MLP + Qwen2-7B) with a two-stream connector: separate MLPs project full keyframe patch embeddings and compressed non-keyframe tokens before concatenation in temporal order. Training uses a three-stage supervised pipeline (7.4M pretrain → 1.3M short-video QA → 500K long-video QA) with a standard language modeling objective, end-to-end optimizing the DFM pooling parameters.

## Results

ViLAMP (7B, 1 FPS) achieves 72.6% on MLVU (outperforming GPT-4o's 64.6%), 70.2% on EgoSchema, 61.2% on LongVideoBench, 45.2% on LVBench, and 67.5%/73.5% (non-subtitled/subtitled) on Video-MME Overall. On the challenging Video-MME Long subset (avg. 2,386 s), ViLAMP reaches 57.8%/68.1%, a +3.0%/+4.8% absolute gain over the previous best similar-sized model (NVILA). On the VideoNIAH benchmark at 10K frames, ViLAMP maintains 58.15% average accuracy versus 47.25% for VideoChat-Flash, while consuming ~50% less GPU memory on a single A100.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ViLAMP outperforms all similar-sized (7–9B) open-source video-language models across all five main benchmarks. On MLVU, it surpasses GPT-4o (72.6 vs. 64.6) and the closest open-source competitor LLaVA-Video (72.6 vs. 70.8). On EgoSchema, it leads LongVU (70.2 vs. 67.6) and LLaVA-Video (70.2 vs. 65.6). On the Video-MME Long subset, it improves over NVILA by 3.0% (non-sub) and 4.8% (sub), and on LVBench it nearly doubles LLaMA-VID (45.2 vs. 23.9). On the VideoNIAH ultra-long benchmark (10K frames), ViLAMP achieves 12.82% higher average accuracy than VideoChat-Flash while using ~50% less GPU memory. In zero-shot action recognition, ViLAMP beats AdaFrame on ActivityNet (78.6 vs. 71.5 mAP) and MGSampler on Sth-V2 (86.9 vs. 60.1 Acc.) and UCF-101 (97.6 vs. 95.2 Acc.).

## Relation to Prior Work

ViLAMP builds directly on the LLaVA-OneVision architecture (SigLIP encoder + Qwen2-7B LLM + MLP connector) and positions itself against two established lines of long-video compression: token pruning (uniform sampling, content-aware selection in Goldfish, LongVU) and feature merging (Q-Former, mean-pooling, similarity-guided merging). Its key departure is the 'differential distillation' principle, which unifies query relevance and temporal distinctiveness into a single saliency score applied hierarchically at both frame and patch levels, rather than treating selection and compression as separate heuristics. The 'mixed precision' framing—full tokens for keyframes, single compressed tokens for non-keyframes—draws an explicit analogy to mixed-precision training, providing a principled resource-allocation rationale absent in prior pruning or merging methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ability to process 10K frames (~2.7 hours) on a single A100 with stable accuracy (only ~12.8% drop from 2K to 10K frames) addresses a critical practical bottleneck for real-world long-video applications like continuous robot learning and hour-scale video analysis. The VideoNIAH benchmark fills a gap in evaluation methodology by testing both localization and comprehension of needle clips within very long haystacks, going beyond prior single-frame NIAH adaptations. Open questions include whether the CLIP-based frame-level scoring (separate from the VLM's own encoder) remains optimal at extreme scales, whether the greedy keyframe selection generalizes to videos with highly non-stationary content, and whether the approach extends naturally to audio-visual or multi-camera settings.
