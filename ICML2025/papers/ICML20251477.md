## Problem

Existing binaural audio rendering methods face a dual challenge: they cannot simultaneously achieve high-fidelity rendering (accurately modeling binaural cues, room reverb, and stochastic ambient noise) and support causal, streaming inference with low latency. Regression-based approaches fail to generate acoustic effects absent from the input, while generative approaches rely on non-causal architectures and multi-step inference that preclude real-time continuous synthesis.

## Input / Output

The model takes a mono audio signal at 48 kHz (32768 samples ≈ 683 ms per chunk), along with 7-dimensional speaker and listener poses (3D position + 4D quaternion rotation) sampled over time. Internally, the mono signal is converted to a complex STFT spectrogram (256×257), Gaussian noise is added, and the channel is duplicated to form the initial noisy two-channel spectrogram. The output is a binaural two-channel audio signal at 48 kHz, produced by applying streaming ISTFT to the predicted binaural spectrogram after iterative denoising.

## Method

BinauralFlow reframes binaural rendering as a conditional flow matching generative task rather than a regression problem, using an optimal-transport linear interpolation path from a noisy version of the mono spectrogram to the target binaural spectrogram. The backbone is a fully causal U-Net with one-side-padded 3×3 convolutions, frame-local GroupNorm, and causal 4×4 down/up-sampling, ensuring each output frame depends only on past information. Conditioning on time step, speaker/listener poses (encoded via Random Gaussian Fourier Embedding + MLP), and the mono spectrogram guides the vector field prediction. For streaming inference, a continuous pipeline combines streaming STFT/ISTFT with a buffer bank (storing per-timestep convolution buffers to avoid overwriting history across denoising steps), a midpoint ODE solver, and an early skip schedule that reduces inference to 6 function evaluations while preserving quality.

## Results

BinauralFlow achieves the best quantitative performance across all three metrics: L2 error of 1.00 (×10⁻⁵), magnitude L2 error of 0.0071, and phase angular error of 1.33, using only 6 function evaluations (NFE) with an inference speed of 163 ms on a single 4090 GPU. In the perceptual study, BinauralFlow reaches a 42% confusion rate in the A-B test (upper bound 50%), an environment score of 68.4±23.4, and a spatialization score of 83.1±18.9, approaching ground-truth levels (87.4 and 89.9 respectively). The model is 4.7× faster than SGMSE (163 ms vs. 770 ms) while using 5× fewer inference steps (6 vs. 30).

## Baselines & Comparisons

BinauralFlow outperforms all seven baselines on L2 and magnitude error. Against the closest generative competitor SGMSE (30 NFE), BinauralFlow reduces L2 error from 1.55 to 1.00 (35% improvement) and magnitude error from 0.0076 to 0.0071, while also improving phase error from 1.43 to 1.33, all with 5× fewer inference steps. Against the prior SOTA for binaural synthesis, BinauralGrad (6 NFE), BinauralFlow cuts L2 error from 2.93 to 1.00 (66% reduction) and magnitude error from 0.0143 to 0.0071 (50% reduction), while matching its phase error of 1.33. All three regression-based baselines (2.5D Visual Sound, WaveNet, WarpNet) cluster tightly around L2≈2.78–2.79 and are substantially outperformed. The DSP baseline SoundSpaces 2.0 is the weakest overall (L2=4.91). In the perceptual A-B test, BinauralFlow's 42% confusion rate far exceeds SGMSE (21%), BinauralGrad (3%), and SoundSpaces (12%), approaching the 50% random-guess ceiling. Against the simplified flow matching formulation (Tong et al.), BinauralFlow reduces L2 from 1.86 to 1.00 and magnitude from 0.0101 to 0.0071, validating the use of full-magnitude Gaussian noise and mono-audio conditioning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the binaural audio synthesis line from regression-based neural renderers (WarpNet, 2.5D Visual Sound) and diffusion-based generative models (BinauralGrad, SGMSE) by recasting the problem as conditional flow matching, which reduces inference steps from 30 to 6 while improving quality. It departs from prior generative audio work by introducing a fully causal U-Net with per-layer buffers and a continuous inference pipeline (streaming STFT/ISTFT, buffer bank, midpoint solver, early skip schedule), addressing the streaming constraint that no prior binaural synthesis method handles. The conditional flow matching formulation also differs from simplified flow matching (Tong et al., 2023) by using normal-magnitude Gaussian noise and the mono input as a generation condition, which the authors show prevents model collapse.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

BinauralFlow is the first binaural speech synthesis method to simultaneously achieve near-indistinguishable rendering quality (42% ABX confusion rate) and causal streaming inference, making it directly applicable to real-time VR/AR/gaming scenarios where prior methods fail. The causal U-Net with buffer bank and early skip schedule is a reusable architectural pattern for any U-Net-based generative model requiring streaming. Limitations include evaluation in a single room with 10 hours of data, a single listener, and speech-only content; open questions include personalization (HRTF variability), multi-source scenes, and whether the pretraining strategy with artificial binaural heads generalizes to diverse acoustic environments.
