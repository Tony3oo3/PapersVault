## Problem

Quantum Hamiltonian Descent (QHD) leverages quantum tunneling to escape local minima in continuous optimization, but it relies solely on function-value oracles (zeroth-order), resulting in slower convergence than classical gradient methods and limited robustness in highly nonconvex landscapes. No prior quantum Hamiltonian-based approach incorporates gradient information to accelerate convergence while retaining the tunneling advantage.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251328/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes a continuous objective function f and its gradient ∇f through quantum oracles O_f and O_∇f (mapping |x⟩|z⟩ to |x⟩|f(x)+z⟩ and |x⟩|∇f(x)+z⟩ respectively), an initial quantum state |Ψ₀⟩, and scalar Hamiltonian parameters α, β, γ. The output is a classical point ξ ∈ ℝ^d obtained by measuring the position observable of the final quantum state after K discrete-time evolution steps, with gate complexity per iteration scaling as Õ(d).

## Method

The paper constructs a new time-dependent Lagrangian that augments the Bregman Lagrangian with explicit gradient terms (∇f·Ẋ and ‖∇f‖²), motivated by the Lyapunov structure of high-resolution ODEs. Canonical quantization of the resulting Hamiltonian yields a quantum operator containing non-commuting momentum and gradient-multiplication operators, whose Schrödinger dynamics are proven to converge at O(t⁻²) in both function value and gradient norm via Lyapunov functions built from quantum operator expectations. For discrete-time implementation, the Hamiltonian is split into three components (Laplacian, anti-commutator cross-term, and potential), where the cross-term is simulated via QSVT with Õ(αdhL) first-oracle queries per step, while the other two are fast-forwardable. The original QHD is recovered as the special case α=β=γ=0, and numerical experiments on convex and nonconvex benchmarks show gradient-based QHD outperforms QHD, NAG, and SGDM by up to two orders of magnitude in final objective value.

## Results

Gradient-based QHD achieves an O(t^{-2}) convergence rate in both function values (Theorem 1) and gradient norms (Theorem 4) for convex, continuously differentiable objectives. On the non-convex Cube-Wave function, gradient-based QHD attains a final objective value nearly one order of magnitude lower than QHD and two orders of magnitude lower than SGDM and NAG. Across all tested non-convex benchmarks (Styblinski-Tang, Michalewicz, Cube-Wave, Rastrigin), gradient-based QHD consistently achieves higher success probability and faster convergence than all three baselines, with the paper claiming overall improvements of at least an order of magnitude over existing methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares gradient-based QHD against three baselines: QHD (the original zeroth-order quantum method), SGDM (stochastic gradient descent with momentum), and NAG (Nesterov's accelerated gradient). Against QHD, gradient-based QHD consistently outperforms on both function value convergence and success probability across all five test problems, with the gap being most dramatic on the Cube-Wave function (~10× lower final objective). Against SGDM and NAG, gradient-based QHD also wins on all non-convex benchmarks, achieving roughly two orders of magnitude lower final objective values on Cube-Wave. On the convex test function (19), gradient-based QHD outperforms all three baselines in both function value and gradient norm convergence, while standard QHD is slower than NAG. The key differentiator is that gradient-based QHD's solution distribution becomes much more concentrated around the global minimizer as iterations progress, whereas QHD shows no clear advantage over classical methods and SGDM/NAG struggle with the non-convex landscapes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Quantum Hamiltonian Descent (QHD) framework of Leng et al. (2023a), which quantized the Bregman Lagrangian of Nesterov's accelerated gradient descent via Feynman path integrals to produce a quantum zeroth-order optimizer. The key departure is incorporating gradient information into the Hamiltonian, motivated by the high-resolution ODE framework of Shi et al. (2022) where the Lyapunov function naturally couples kinetic energy with gradient terms. The derivation uses canonical quantization rather than path integrals, yielding a quantum first-order method whose Hamiltonian reduces to the original QHD when gradient parameters are set to zero. The convergence analysis introduces quantum-operator Lyapunov functions, a technique not previously applied in this setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first convergence guarantees (O(t^{-2}) in both function value and gradient norm) for a gradient-augmented quantum Hamiltonian dynamics, and demonstrates via 2D numerical experiments that the gradient-based variant substantially outperforms both the zeroth-order QHD and classical baselines (NAG, SGDM) on non-convex benchmarks. However, practical impact is currently limited: all experiments are in 2D due to exponential state-space growth, the discrete-time convergence is left open, and the method requires a quantum first-order oracle whose construction is non-trivial. Open questions include whether the quantum advantage persists in high dimensions, whether Jordan's gradient estimation can replace the first-order oracle without prohibitive overhead, and whether the product-formula step size can be rigorously bounded.
