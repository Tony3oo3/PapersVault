## Problem

In the standard online strategic classification model, decision-makers fully reveal their classifier before agents manipulate their features. This paper asks whether withholding the classifier can protect against manipulation, and answers negatively: when agents instead best-respond to a γ-weighted average of historical classifiers, the decision-maker incurs (1−γ)^(−1) or k_in times more mistakes than under full transparency.

## Input / Output

The decision-maker receives a sequence of agents (x_t, y_t) where x_t is a node in a manipulation graph G and y_t ∈ {0,1}; the agent manipulates from x_t to a neighbor v_t ∈ N_out[x_t] based on either the revealed classifier h_t or a γ-weighted average of all previously implemented classifiers. The output is a binary prediction h_t(v_t) on the post-manipulation feature vector, with theoretical mistake bound guarantees expressed in terms of the Littlestone dimension of the hypothesis class, the maximum in-degree k_in and out-degree k_out of the manipulation graph, and the discount factor γ.

## Method

The core contribution is a two-level reduction framework. For the intermediate Revealed-Arb setting (arbitrary tie-breaking), Algorithm 1 maintains a weighted expert ensemble where each expert runs a classical online learning algorithm (e.g., SOA); upon a false negative, it enumerates all in-neighbors of the observed v_t that could not have received a positive label under h_t to identify candidate true feature vectors, then splits experts to cover all possible optimal trajectories. For the main γ-Weighted setting, Algorithm 3 reduces to Revealed-Arb by committing to a classifier for Φ = ⌈ln(1/3)/ln(γ)⌉+1 consecutive rounds, ensuring the γ-weighted historical average is within 1/3 of the current classifier so agents effectively best-respond to h_t with arbitrary tie-breaking. A separate conservative Algorithm 2 achieves O(|H|) mistakes by predicting positive whenever any surviving hypothesis labels the node positive, eliminating one hypothesis per false positive. Matching lower bounds are established via adversarial constructions on layered graphs (for the k_in factor) and replicated star graphs (for the (1−γ)^(−1) factor), showing both multiplicative gaps are unavoidable for deterministic algorithms.

## Results

The paper establishes that in the γ-Weighted setting (where agents best-respond to a discounted average of past classifiers), the decision-maker's mistake bound is O(min{d(1−γ)⁻¹ · k_in · k_out · ln(k_in·k_out), |H|}), with matching lower bounds showing both the (1−γ)⁻¹ and k_in factors are unavoidable in different regimes. In the intermediate Revealed-Arb setting (arbitrary tie-breaking), the mistake bound is O(k_in · k_out · ln(k_in·k_out) · d) with a matching Ω(k_in · k_out · d) lower bound. Compared to the standard Revealed-Std setting with bound O(k_out · Ldim(H)), hiding the classifier introduces a multiplicative gap of Õ((1−γ)⁻¹ · k_in).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is against the standard Revealed-Std setting established by Ahmadi et al. (2023) and Cohen et al. (2024a), which achieves a tight O(k_out · Ldim(H)) mistake bound under the assumption that agents remain at their original feature when all neighbors are labeled negative. The key finding is that this bound degrades by a factor of k_in when the standard tie-breaking assumption is removed (Revealed-Arb), and by an additional (1−γ)⁻¹ factor when agents cannot observe the current classifier (γ-Weighted). The lower bound construction in Theorem 4.4 shows that even with k_in = k_out = 2 (a star graph), the (1−γ)⁻¹ blow-up is unavoidable for any deterministic algorithm, meaning the decision-maker cannot simply absorb the cost of hidden classifiers. The paper also notes that for mean-based learning agents (e.g., Multiplicative Weights, ε-Greedy), the situation can be strictly worse, with infinite mistakes possible even in the strategic realizable setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the online strategic classification framework of Ahmadi et al. (2023) and Cohen et al. (2024a), which assume full classifier transparency and standard tie-breaking (agents stay put when all neighbors are labeled negative). The key departure is a two-step relaxation: first removing the standard tie-breaking assumption (Revealed-Arb setting), then replacing the current classifier with a γ-weighted average of historical classifiers (γ-Weighted setting). The manipulation graph model from Zhang & Conitzer (2021) underlies the entire analysis, and the reduction to classical online learning via weighted expert algorithms (building on the SOA/Littlestone dimension framework) is the core methodological tool, adapted to handle the information loss from unobserved true feature vectors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central finding—that withholding classifiers backfires by inflating the mistake bound by a factor of (1−γ)^{-1} or k_in—challenges the intuitive belief that hiding decision rules protects against manipulation. This has direct implications for institutions (universities, lenders) that currently publish only past cutoffs rather than current rules. Open questions include closing the logarithmic gap between upper and lower bounds, extending to randomized or agnostic settings, and developing meaningful performance guarantees for learning agents whose action spaces are non-static and potentially infinite. The Section 5 result that mean-based learning algorithms (Multiplicative Weights, ε-Greedy) can cause infinite mistakes even in the realizable setting is a significant negative result that highlights the difficulty of modeling adaptive agents in strategic classification.
