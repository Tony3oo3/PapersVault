## Problem

In high-stakes domains like medicine, decision makers need to balance safety and utility when acting on uncertain predictions, but it was unclear what the correct notion of uncertainty quantification is for risk-averse agents who optimize their value at risk rather than expected utility. While conformal prediction produces prediction sets with coverage guarantees, the literature lacked a principled connection between these sets and optimal action policies, leaving open which decision processes make prediction sets the right interface and how to design them to best serve risk-averse utility.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a feature vector x, a pre-trained model's probability vector f_x over the label set (e.g., softmax outputs from Inception_v3 or a neural network), a user-defined utility function u(a,y) that encodes domain-specific priorities (such as a clinical treatment matrix), and a miscoverage level α. It produces a prediction set C(x) ⊆ Y, an action a(x) selected by the max-min rule over the set, and a utility certificate ν(x) that guarantees the realized utility is at least ν(x) with probability ≥ 1−α. The utility function is entirely user-specified and arbitrary, and the prediction sets are constructed via a single scalar parameter β calibrated on finite samples, making the method agnostic to the underlying predictive model.

## Method

The core contribution is a decision-theoretic framework proving that prediction sets are a sufficient statistic for risk-averse agents optimizing their value at risk. The paper establishes three results: (1) the max-min policy—choosing the action that maximizes worst-case utility over the prediction set—is minimax optimal over all distributions consistent with marginal coverage (Proposition 2.2); (2) the optimal action policy and utility certificate from the original risk-averse optimization (RA-DPO) are exactly equivalent to applying max-min over a suitably designed prediction set (Theorem 2.3); and (3) the optimal prediction sets admit a one-dimensional characterization via a scalar β, where the set for x is {y : u(a(x,β), y) ≥ θ(x,β)} with θ(x,t) defined as the maximum over actions of the (1−t)-quantile of utility under the conditional label distribution (Theorem 3.2). The RAC algorithm instantiates this with finite-sample calibration: it estimates θ and the assignment function g(x,β) = argmax_s {θ(x,s) + βs} using the model's probability vector, then finds β by solving a scalar equation ensuring marginal coverage ≥ 1−α on calibration data, yielding distribution-free safety guarantees under exchangeability (Theorem 4.1).

## Results

On the COVID-19 Radiography Database (4-class chest X-ray), RAC achieves the highest worst-case utility (average realized max-min value) at every miscoverage level α, and drives the critical-error rate (e.g., failing to act on pneumonia or COVID-19) below 10% at α=0.02, compared to over 60% for the best-response baseline, while incurring less than a 5% drop in average realized utility. On MovieLens (5-class rating prediction), RAC at α=0.05 reduces critical mistakes (recommending movies rated 1 or 2) by 75% relative to best-response, with only a 15% reduction in average utility. Across both tasks, RAC dominates all conformal-prediction baselines (Sadinle, Romano, Cortes-Gomez scoring rules) on both worst-case utility and average realized utility at every α, while all prediction-set methods meet their target miscoverage.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RAC is compared against four baselines across both experiments. Against the Calibration + Best-Response baseline (Noarov et al., 2023), RAC sacrifices a small amount of average utility (under 5% in medical, 15% in MovieLens) but dramatically reduces critical errors (from >60% to <10% in medical; 75% reduction in MovieLens), making it the clear winner on the safety-utility trade-off. Against the three conformal-prediction + max-min baselines—Sadinle et al. (2019) score-1 (1−f_x(y)), Romano et al. (2020) score-2 (adaptive cumulative probability), and Cortes-Gomez et al. (2024) score-3 (greedy max-min-tailored scoring)—RAC achieves strictly higher worst-case utility (average max-min value) and higher average realized utility at every α level on both datasets. The paper explicitly states that its framework 'yields significantly more effective action policies' compared to Cortes-Gomez et al. (2024), the most recent prior work in this direction. All prediction-set-based methods (including RAC) satisfy their nominal miscoverage targets, confirming valid distribution-free guarantees; the differentiation is purely in the quality of the resulting action policies and utility certificates.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the conformal prediction framework (Vovk et al., 2005) for distribution-free prediction sets and extends it into the decision-theoretic domain. It departs from prior risk-control-over-prediction-sets work (Angelopoulos et al., 2022; Cortes-Gomez et al., 2024) by explicitly connecting set design to optimal action policies for risk-averse agents, rather than treating risk as a standalone set property. It also departs from classical economic risk-aversion theory (Bernoulli, von Neumann–Morgenstern, Pratt) by grounding the analysis in data-driven, distribution-free finite-sample guarantees rather than assumed utility functions or known distributions. A key technical departure from standard conformal prediction is the finding that optimal risk-averse prediction sets need not be nested or score-thresholded, unlike minimum-size sets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first decision-theoretic justification for when prediction sets (as opposed to calibrated probability forecasts) are the correct uncertainty language, specifically for agents optimizing Value-at-Risk. The RAC algorithm is practical—requiring only a black-box model and a utility matrix—and offers distribution-free safety guarantees, making it directly applicable to high-stakes domains like medical triage. Key limitations include the restriction to marginal (not group-conditional) guarantees and a single risk measure (VaR rather than CVaR or other coherent measures). Open questions include extending to conditional validity, richer action spaces, and settings where the utility function itself is learned or partially unknown.
