## Problem

Differentially private optimization for nonsmooth nonconvex (NSNC) objectives had only one prior result (Zhang et al., 2024) with a sample complexity whose non-private term scaled linearly in dimension d. This paper closes that gap by showing the non-private term can be sublinear in d, improving the single-pass sample complexity by a factor of Ω(√d), and further improving the multi-pass ERM complexity to sublinear dimension dependence. Additionally, no prior result established that Goldstein-stationarity generalizes from the empirical loss to the population loss in the NSNC setting.

## Input / Output

The algorithms consume a dataset of n points with L-Lipschitz component functions f(x;ξ), and are parameterized by privacy budget (ε,δ), Goldstein accuracy parameters (α,β), and a loss gap Φ. Most algorithms are zero-order, requiring only function evaluations of f(·;ξ) rather than gradients, though a first-order variant is also provided. The output is a point x^out satisfying E‖∂̄_{2α}F(x^out)‖ ≤ β, where ∂̄_{2α}F denotes the minimum-norm element of the Goldstein 2α-subdifferential of the population (or empirical) objective.

## Method

The base algorithm is the O2NC (Online-to-Non-Convex conversion) framework, which reduces finding Goldstein-stationary points to controlling the variance and second moment of a stochastic gradient oracle. The key technical insight is that the zero-order gradient estimator, when averaged over m random directions, has a high-probability sensitivity over neighboring minibatches of O(L/B) rather than the worst-case O(Ld/B), because subGaussian concentration ensures the estimator is close to the true smoothed gradient. This reduced effective sensitivity allows substantially less noise to be added for privacy, yielding the improved sample complexity. For single-pass algorithms, the tree mechanism is used to privatize cumulative gradient sums with O(log Σ) noise; for multi-pass ERM, the Gaussian mechanism with advanced composition handles data reuse. The paper further proves a generalization result showing that any (α,β̂)-stationary point of the empirical loss is an (α, β̂ + Õ(√(d/n)))-stationary point of the population loss, and provides a first-order variant that reduces oracle complexity by a factor of Ω(d²) over the zero-order case.

## Results

The paper's headline result is an Ω(√d) improvement in sample complexity for single-pass private NSNC optimization: the new algorithm requires n = Õ(√d/(αβ³) + d/(εαβ²)) samples, compared to the previous best of Õ(d/(αβ³) + d^{3/2}/(εαβ²)) by Zhang et al. (2024). The multi-pass ERM algorithm further reduces the sample complexity to Õ(d^{3/4}/(εα^{1/2}β^{3/2})), achieving the first sublinear dimension-dependent private ERM for nonsmooth nonconvex objectives. A first-order (gradient-based) variant maintains the same sample complexity while reducing oracle calls by a factor of Õ(d²) over the zero-order version.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole prior baseline is Zhang et al. (2024), the only existing result for private NSNC optimization. The paper's single-pass algorithm (Theorem 3.1) improves upon Zhang et al. by a factor of Ω(√d) in the non-private term (reducing dimension dependence from d to √d) and by a factor of √d in the private term (reducing d^{3/2} to d). Notably, the sublinear √d dimension dependence in the non-private term was previously (and erroneously) claimed impossible by Zhang et al. The multi-pass ERM result (Theorem 4.1) is entirely new — no prior work achieved private ERM for NSNC objectives with sublinear dimension dependence. The first-order variant (Theorem 6.1) matches the zero-order sample complexity while reducing oracle complexity by Õ(d²), a larger gap than the Θ(d) ratio seen in non-private settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the O2NC (Online-to-Non-Convex conversion) framework of Cutkosky et al. (2023) for finding Goldstein-stationary points, and on the sole prior DP result for nonsmooth nonconvex optimization by Zhang et al. (2024), which used a zero-order single-pass algorithm with sample complexity d/(αβ³) + d^{3/2}/(εαβ²). The key technical departure is a subGaussian concentration argument showing that the worst-case sensitivity of the zero-order gradient estimator (Ld/B) can be reduced to L/B with high probability, enabling substantially less noise in the tree mechanism. The paper further extends the setting to multi-pass ERM with Gaussian mechanism and advanced composition, and introduces a new generalization result from empirical to population Goldstein-stationarity that had no prior counterpart in the literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result corrects an impossibility claim in Zhang et al. (2024) by demonstrating that sublinear (√d) dimension dependence in the non-private sample complexity term is achievable, and provides the first private ERM algorithm with sublinear dimension dependence for NSNC objectives. Open questions include establishing tight lower bounds for DP nonconvex optimization (current upper and lower bounds do not match even in the smooth case), resolving the gap between approximate-DP and Rényi-DP guarantees inherent to the concentration-based sensitivity reduction, and determining whether the computationally inefficient Lasry-Lions smoothing approach in Appendix C can be made practical. The practical impact is significant for privacy-preserving training of models with nonsmooth components (ReLU, MaxPool), where data efficiency is a critical bottleneck.
