## Problem

In Learning from Label Proportions (LLP), a learner only observes the fraction of positive labels within each bag of examples rather than individual labels, yet must still predict at the individual-example level. Prior theoretical results for LLP under square loss had suboptimal dependence on bag size k: Busa-Fekete et al. (2023) achieved only slow rates with O(k²/β²) sample complexity, while Li et al. (2024) obtained fast rates but with a widely suboptimal O(k³/β) dependence. This paper closes the gap by proving essentially optimal sample complexity bounds of O(k/β²) (non-realizable) and O(k/β) (realizable), and establishes a matching lower bound in the realizable case.

## Input / Output

The training data consists of m bags, each containing k feature vectors drawn i.i.d. from a fixed distribution, with only the fraction of positive labels (α_j) revealed per bag rather than individual binary labels. Feature vectors are observed individually, but the label signal is compressed to a single scalar per bag, so larger k means less total label information for the same number of examples. The output is a hypothesis h: X → [0,1] that assigns a probability prediction to each individual example, and the goal is to minimize population square loss at the individual-example level despite training on aggregate signals.

## Method

The core technical contribution is a variance-reduced bag-level square loss that centers the loss by subtracting marginal expectations (E[h(x)] and p = E[h*(x)]) and applies a clipping indicator to control tail variance. For ERM, the clipped bag-level loss ℓ^c(h, z_j) = (1/k)(kα̃_j - Ẽ_j(h))² · G_j(h) + (E[h(x)] - p)² is shown to be an unbiased estimator of the individual-example square loss whose variance grows only as O(γ log(k/β) + k²θ) rather than O(k), enabling fast rates via a bias-variance tradeoff with θ = β/(16k²). For SGD on norm-bounded linear predictors, a similar truncated loss with a centroid-based clipping threshold θ = √(8/k · log(...)) is used, and an online-to-batch argument yields the same linear-in-k sample complexity. The key insight is that the centering step removes the O(k) variance inflation inherent in prior debiasing approaches, while the clipping introduces only O(kθ) bias that can be made smaller than the target regret β.

## Results

Across MNIST, CIFAR-10, Higgs, and UCI Adult, the proposed clipped bag-level square loss (OURS) achieves the highest individual-example test accuracy at every bag size (k = 2^0 to 2^9) and every number of training epochs, outperforming all four baselines. The advantage is most pronounced at large bag sizes (k ≥ 2^6) and few training epochs, where competing methods degrade sharply due to high variance. On CIFAR-10 at k=2^6, all tested methods exceed 0.80 accuracy (vs. ~0.65 reported for Dulac-Arnold et al.), and on Higgs at k=2^6 all methods exceed 0.65 (vs. <0.60 for Dulac-Arnold et al.). A controlled variance experiment (Figure 2) confirms that OURS maintains near-constant loss-estimate variance (~0.034) across bag sizes, while LI ET AL. and EASYLLP exhibit variance growing linearly with k.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares five aggregate losses: OURS (clipped debiased square loss), LI ET AL. (unbiased but high-variance debiased square loss from Li et al., 2024), EASYLLP (unbiased debiased cross-entropy from Busa-Fekete et al., 2023), and two non-unbiased proportion-matching baselines (VANILLASQ and VANILLACE, the latter from Yu et al., 2013). OURS wins against all four baselines at every bag size and training duration on all four datasets. The gap is small at low bag sizes (k ≤ 2^3) where all methods perform comparably, but widens dramatically at k ≥ 2^6: LI ET AL. and EASYLLP degrade substantially because their loss-estimate variance scales linearly with k, while OURS's variance remains essentially constant. VANILLASQ and VANILLACE, being biased estimators, also fall behind at large k. The advantage of OURS is particularly striking when training is restricted to few epochs (e.g., 1–5), suggesting faster convergence. The paper also notes that the Dulac-Arnold et al. (2019) label-generation method underperforms all tested baselines by 5–15% accuracy on CIFAR-10 and Higgs at comparable bag sizes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LLP theoretical framework established by Busa-Fekete et al. (2023) and Li et al. (2024), which introduced debiasing techniques to convert aggregate label proportions into individual-level loss estimates. While Busa-Fekete et al. achieved O(k²/β²) sample complexity for general bounded losses and Li et al. obtained fast rates for square loss at the cost of O(k³/β), this paper applies a clipping-based variance reduction to the bag-level square loss, reducing the bag-size dependence to the optimal O(k) in both realizable and non-realizable regimes. The approach departs from prior work by treating the clipping threshold as a bias-variance tradeoff parameter, a technique borrowed from fast-rate ERM analyses (Massart, Mendelson) but applied here in the novel context of aggregate label supervision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The essentially optimal linear dependence on bag size k has direct practical implications for privacy-preserving ad conversion reporting (Apple SKAN, Google Privacy Sandbox), where larger aggregation levels are mandated and the sample efficiency per bag becomes the binding constraint. The paper's empirical validation across four datasets and multiple architectures confirms that the variance advantage translates to substantially better accuracy at large bag sizes and fewer training epochs. Key limitations include restriction to square loss and binary classification, with the authors noting that extension to log loss (common in DNN training) and multiclass settings remains open, as does handling distribution drift over time.
