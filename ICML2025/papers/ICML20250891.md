## Problem

Long-term autoregressive prediction of high-dimensional chaotic systems (e.g., turbulent flows with O(10^5)-dimensional phase spaces) is intractable with existing ergodicity-preserving methods that rely on optimal transport, which suffer from the curse of dimensionality when matching multimodal distributions. No transformer architecture has been specifically designed to handle the spatial mixing properties and extreme-value statistics of large-scale chaotic dynamics while guaranteeing long-term statistical consistency.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250891/cropped_page3_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a single snapshot of a physical field on a uniform grid—either a 2D vorticity field (256×256 or 128×128) for Kolmogorov Flow or a 3-channel 2D velocity field (192×192) extracted from a 3D turbulent channel flow simulation—and autoregressively produces the next state, rolled out over 1000+ steps for long-term evaluation. The forward map operates in a learned L² feature space of dimension d (256 in practice), with a decoding CNN projecting back to physical variables under periodic boundary conditions. Evaluation spans both short-term relative L² error (τ=5, 25 steps) and long-term statistics including energy spectrum matching, mixing rate, TKE, and KL divergence on principal components.

## Method

The architecture replaces standard scaled-dot-product attention with an A3M (Axial Mean-Max-Min) factorized attention that applies separate attention heads with mean, max, and min pooling along each spatial axis, reducing complexity from O(N²) to O(S₁+…+Sₘ) while capturing both statistical moments and extreme values critical for turbulence. Spatial correlations are encoded via random Fourier feature positional encoding that approximates a Gaussian kernel over grid distances, with bandwidth σ controlling the interaction length scale. The key theoretical contribution is a unitary constraint on the forward operator G (G*G = I) derived from the Von Neumann mean ergodic theorem, ensuring long-term statistics are preserved in L² space without explicit distribution matching. This constraint is enforced through Hutchinson's stochastic trace estimator (sampling k random unit vectors), yielding O(kd²) complexity versus O(d³) for Frobenius-norm alternatives, and the operator is parameterized in SO(d) as a real embedding of the circle group U(1)^{d/2}.

## Results

On the 256×256 Kolmogorov Flow (KF256), the proposed transformer achieves the lowest relative L² error of 0.86 at τ=5 and 1.27 at τ=25, with long-term metrics of ME-APE 0.10, ME-LRw 0.15, Δλ 0.08, and KLD 0.29. On the more challenging 3-channel 192×192 Turbulent Channel Flow (TCF), it achieves relative L² of 0.03 (τ=5) and 0.13 (τ=25), with ME-APE 0.09, ME-LRw 0.09, Δλ 0.08, and KLD 1.97. The method outperforms the best baseline by 23.1–30.8% across long-term statistical metrics on both datasets, with the largest gains on TCF (30.8% on ME-APE, 27.3% on mixing rate error).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against four baselines spanning operator-based and transformer-based paradigms: MNO (FNO with forward-invariance constraint), UNO (U-shaped FNO), MWT (multiwavelet operator), and FactFormer (factorized attention transformer). On KF256, the proposed method beats the closest competitor FactFormer by 6.5% on short-term Rel-L² (0.86 vs 0.92 at τ=5) and by 23.1% on ME-APE (0.10 vs 0.13), 21.0% on ME-LRw, and 20.0% on mixing rate error. On TCF, the margins widen substantially: the method outperforms MWT (the best short-term baseline) by 25% on Rel-L² at τ=5 (0.03 vs 0.04) and beats FactFormer by 30.8% on ME-APE (0.09 vs 0.13) and 27.3% on Δλ (0.08 vs 0.23). MNO and UNO, which rely on integer Fourier spectra, show notably worse long-term statistics (ME-APE 0.84–0.86, KLD 3.73–4.89 on TCF) and exhibit periodic correlation patterns inconsistent with chaotic mixing. FactFormer is the strongest transformer baseline but still lags on all long-term metrics, particularly on the 3D TCF where its Δλ error (0.23) is nearly 3× that of the proposed method (0.08).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the factorized attention framework of FactFormer (Li et al., 2024) by introducing A3M (Axial Mean-Max-Min) pooling to capture both statistical moments and extreme values in physical fields, and by replacing standard positional encoding with random Fourier features (Rahimi & Recht, 2007; Tancik et al., 2020) that encode spatial distance via a Gaussian kernel. Conceptually, it departs from the dominant line of work on preserving ergodicity in chaotic system prediction—optimal transport-based distribution matching (Jiang et al., 2024; Schiff et al., 2024) and dissipativity constraints (MNO, Li et al., 2022)—by instead enforcing unitarity on the forward operator in L² space, grounded in the Von Neumann mean ergodic theorem. The unitary constraint is made scalable via Hutchinson's stochastic trace estimator, avoiding the O(d³) cost of Frobenius-norm regularization that limits prior operator-based approaches to moderate dimensions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine scalability bottleneck: distribution-matching methods for ergodicity preservation become computationally intractable in the high-dimensional, non-Gaussian phase spaces typical of turbulent flows (O(10⁵) dimensions). By shifting the ergodicity constraint to L²-space unitarity, the method sidesteps the curse of dimensionality while retaining theoretical guarantees. The introduction of a 140k-snapshot 3D turbulent channel flow dataset and a processed 185k Kolmogorov Flow dataset fills a practical gap, as existing 3D DNS datasets require ~100 TB of storage and are impractical for ML research. Key limitations include restriction to ergodic systems (non-ergodic cases remain open) and reliance on uniform grid structures, with mesh-free or graph-based extensions identified as future work.
