## Problem

Existing neural multi-task VRP solvers perform all feature transformations in flat Euclidean space, treating every node uniformly. However, Ollivier-Ricci curvature analysis reveals that VRP instances contain nodes with both negative (contractive) and positive (expansive) curvature, indicating mixed non-Euclidean geometric structures that Euclidean embeddings cannot faithfully represent, leading to suboptimal solution quality and limited cross-task generalization.

## Input / Output

The solver takes a VRP instance as a fully connected undirected graph with 2D node coordinates concatenated with task-specific attributes (capacity, time windows, duration limits, backhaul flags) and autoregressively decodes a feasible route sequence via attention with hard masks for infeasible actions. During inference, greedy rollout with 8× instance augmentation (rotations and clips) is applied to select the best trajectory. Although all problem instances are grounded in Euclidean coordinates, intermediate feature representations are processed in mixed-curvature product manifolds, and the final output is the total route length (minimized) along with the corresponding route assignment.

## Method

The proposed mixed-curvature module partitions the feature space of dimension D into C=8 subspaces (each 16-dimensional), each assigned a learnable curvature parameter κ that can be negative (hyperbolic/Poincaré ball), zero (Euclidean), or positive (hyperspherical). Linear transformations are performed by projecting features from the mixed-curvature manifold into the flat tangent space via the logarithmic map, applying a standard Euclidean weight matrix, and mapping back via the exponential map (Eq. 10), making the curvature parameters differentiable and jointly optimizable. This module is inserted into both the embedding layer (with separate mixed-curvature projections for depot and customer nodes) and every encoder layer. To address curvature mismatch between consecutive layers—where shallow layers capture local tree-like structures (negative curvature) while deeper layers aggregate global information (positive curvature)—a Mix-up mechanism interpolates the current Euclidean representation with the log-mapped mixed-curvature features from the previous layer using learnable weights α and β. The full model is trained with REINFORCE policy gradient using negative route length as reward, with all curvatures initialized at zero and optimized alongside other parameters.

## Results

On 6 in-distribution VRP tasks (CVRP, OVRP, VRPB, VRPTW, VRPL, OVRPTW) with 1,000 test instances each, Mixed-MVMoE achieves the best gap on 5/6 tasks at n=50 and 6/6 tasks at n=100. Headline improvements include CVRP n=100 gap of 1.599% (vs. 1.846% for POMO-MTL and 1.653% for MVMoE) and VRPTW n=100 gap of 4.732% (vs. 5.313% for POMO-MTL). On 10 zero-shot unseen tasks, Mixed-MVMoE achieves state-of-the-art performance across all 10 tasks at both node scales, with gaps ranging from 0.227% (OVRPL n=100) to 9.749% (OVRPBTW n=100). Inference time increases modestly (~7–11%) over the base architectures.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against traditional solvers, Mixed-MVMoE trades a small gap increase (e.g., 0.865% vs. 0.000% for HGS on CVRP n=50) for a ~1000× speedup (5 s vs. 4.6 min per instance). Among neural baselines, Mixed-MVMoE consistently outperforms POMO-MTL on all 6 seen tasks at both scales, with the largest gains on VRPTW n=100 (4.732% vs. 5.313%, a 0.58 pp reduction) and OVRP n=100 (2.946% vs. 3.458%). Compared to MVMoE (the strongest prior neural baseline), Mixed-MVMoE improves the gap on 5/6 tasks at n=50 and 6/6 at n=100, with typical improvements of 0.03–0.15 pp. On the 10 zero-shot unseen tasks (Table 2), Mixed-MVMoE achieves the best gap on all 10 tasks at both n=50 and n=100, outperforming MVMoE by 0.05–0.35 pp and POMO-MTL by 0.1–0.5 pp. The mixed-curvature module also improves the lighter MVMoE-L backbone on 6/6 seen tasks, confirming architecture-agnostic benefits.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the multi-task VRP solver lineage (POMO-MTL, MVMoE, RouteFinder) by replacing their Euclidean feature transformations with mixed-curvature operations on product manifolds, drawing on the geometric deep learning literature (Gu et al., 2018; Ganea et al., 2018; Cho et al., 2023) that has previously applied hyperbolic and hyperspherical spaces to NLP, vision, and graph learning but never to combinatorial routing. The key departure from prior multi-task VRP work is the introduction of learnable per-subspace curvatures and a Mix-up-style interpolation between layers to handle the curvature mismatch that arises as receptive fields grow with depth. Unlike prior mixed-curvature methods that fix curvature or use it for representation learning in classification tasks, here curvature is a jointly optimized parameter within a REINFORCE-trained routing policy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that VRP instances carry non-Euclidean geometric structure (quantified via Ollivier-Ricci curvature) that flat-space solvers systematically distort, and that explicitly modeling this structure yields consistent (if modest) gains across 24 VRP variants and three backbone architectures. Limitations include the added computational overhead from repeated Exp/Log map operations (~7–11% extra inference time), the absence of evaluation on very large-scale instances (~10,000 nodes), and the fixed 16-dimension allocation per curvature subspace. Open questions include whether adaptive or learned subspace partitioning (e.g., via NAS) could further improve the trade-off between geometric diversity and per-subspace expressiveness, and whether the curvature-aware pre-training paradigm transfers to other combinatorial optimization domains beyond routing.
