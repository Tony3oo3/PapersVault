## Problem

Existing multi-disciplinary reasoning benchmarks such as MMLU and MMMU are approaching saturation for frontier models (o1 achieves 92.3% and 78.2% respectively), and no single benchmark simultaneously satisfies four critical properties: comprehensiveness across disciplines, sufficient difficulty to discriminate advanced models, multimodal coverage, and multilingual (English-Chinese) evaluation. This paper addresses the gap by constructing a graduate-level benchmark that matches MMLU's breadth while reaching the difficulty of mathematical olympiad problems.

## Input / Output

For LLM evaluation (RBench-T), input is a text-only single-choice question in English or Chinese; for MLLM evaluation (RBench-M), input is a text question accompanied by one or more images, also in English or Chinese. All questions are uniformly formatted as 6-option single-choice items (A–E plus 'All other answers are incorrect'), with options manually adjusted to ensure sufficient numerical gaps to avoid approximation errors. The output is a Top-1 accuracy score, reported separately per language and per discipline, enabling cross-lingual consistency analysis and per-subject performance breakdowns across 108 (text) and 83 (multimodal) subjects.

## Method

RBench is constructed through a six-step pipeline starting from 100+ graduate and undergraduate courses across 19 departments at Tsinghua University. Fifty-one domain experts collect and initially filter questions, retaining reasoning-based items while discarding knowledge-recall and proof-based questions, yielding 10,270 raw items. Three successive filtering rounds then apply: (1) expert screening for reasoning depth, (2) model-based screening using o1's reasoning-token count (questions requiring fewer than 2,000 reasoning tokens are removed to enforce a difficulty floor), and (3) manual review for completeness, ambiguity, duplication, and subject balance (capped at 50 questions per subject). All surviving questions are converted to 6-option single-choice format via GPT-4o with manual verification, and each question is translated into both English and Chinese with review by three bilingual experts. The final benchmark comprises 1,094 text questions spanning 108 subjects and 665 multimodal questions spanning 83 subjects, all with verified auto-gradable answers.

## Results

On RBench-T (1,094 text questions across 108 subjects), the top model o1-20241217 achieves 69.0% top-1 accuracy (English) and 70.1% (Chinese), while GPT-4o scores 53.6% and DeepSeek-R1 scores 61.2%. On RBench-M (665 multimodal questions across 83 subjects), o1 drops to 53.2% (English) and 55.0% (Chinese), and GPT-4o falls to 33.4%, revealing a substantial multimodal reasoning gap. Open-source models lag significantly: Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct reaches only 49.5% on RBench-T and 20.0% on RBench-M. The o1 thinking time on RBench-T is 98.2s per question (7.3× MMLU's 13.5s), confirming substantially higher reasoning demands.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RBench is positioned against two established benchmarks: MMLU (text) and MMMU (multimodal). On difficulty, expert pairwise voting shows RBench-T questions require more reasoning than MMLU in 85.94% of comparisons (vs. 10.62% for MMLU), and RBench-M over MMMU in 76.88% of comparisons. o1's own judgment agrees: 76.67% for RBench-T over MMLU and 83.33% for RBench-M over MMMU. In terms of saturation, o1 scores 92.3% on MMLU but only 69.0% on RBench-T, and 78.2% on MMMU but only 53.2% on RBench-M, confirming RBench remains far from saturated. The o1 thinking time is 7.3× longer on RBench-T (98.2s vs. 13.5s) and 4.5× longer on RBench-M (91.7s vs. 20.3s), further validating the higher reasoning complexity. RBench additionally covers both English and Chinese, a dimension neither MMLU nor MMMU addresses.

## Relation to Prior Work

RBench carves new ground by simultaneously satisfying four properties—comprehensiveness, difficulty, multimodality, and multilingualism—that no prior benchmark achieves together. It extends the multi-disciplinary evaluation paradigm of MMLU (Hendrycks et al., 2021) and the multimodal reasoning evaluation of MMMU (Yue et al., 2024a) by raising difficulty to graduate/olympiad level (sourced from 100+ Tsinghua University courses) and adding English-Chinese cross-lingual testing. Unlike math-focused benchmarks such as AIME, FrontierMath, and Omni-Math, RBench avoids subject bias by spanning 108 text subjects and 83 multimodal subjects across 19 departments. Conceptually, it draws on the system-I/system-II distinction (Kahneman, 2011; Wei et al., 2022) to explicitly target slow, deliberate reasoning rather than knowledge retrieval.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

With MMLU and MMMU approaching saturation (o1 at 92.3% and 78.2%), the field urgently needs a harder, more comprehensive benchmark to guide model development; RBench fills this gap with o1 achieving only 69.0% (text) and 53.2% (multimodal). The finding that multimodal reasoning lags text reasoning by 15–20 points across all models highlights a critical open problem: how to transfer linguistic reasoning intelligence to the visual domain. The observation that explicit CoT prompting is ineffective for reasoning models like o1-mini (zero delta) challenges the assumption that CoT is universally beneficial and suggests future prompting strategies must account for a model's internal reasoning architecture. Open questions include whether the cross-lingual consistency (>70%) will hold as models scale, and whether discipline-specific weaknesses (30.4%–68.3% range for GPT-4o) can be addressed without sacrificing performance elsewhere.
