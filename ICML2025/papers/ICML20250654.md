## Problem

Existing post-training quantization methods for diffusion transformers are designed for image generation and degrade significantly when applied to video generation, where the higher token count (spatial × temporal) amplifies quantization information loss and where inter-frame temporal correlations are ignored by standard MSE-based calibration. The paper identifies two specific gaps: quantization error estimation that only considers the feature dimension misses per-token variation in information loss, and frame-wise MSE optimization fails to preserve the spatiotemporal coherence that defines video quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250654/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained video DiT (Open-Sora or Latte) and a text prompt, applies channel-wise weight quantization and dynamic token-wise activation quantization at target bit-widths (W3A6, W3A8, W4A6, W4A8, W6A6, W8A8), and produces generated videos. Calibration is performed post-training using 10 Open-Sora prompts with 50 uniformly sampled diffusion steps, fine-tuning only the TQE parameters (α, β, M) and quantization scales over 5k–15k iterations depending on bit-width, without modifying the base model weights.

## Method

Q-VDiT combines two components. First, the Token-aware Quantization Estimator (TQE) approximates the quantization error Δ using two low-rank vectors α ∈ R^{d_in} and β ∈ R^{d_out} (reducing overhead from d_out×d_in to d_out+d_in parameters), justified by a proof that quantization error carries strictly lower information entropy than the original weights. A per-frame scaling matrix M, initialized from the ratio of activation-quantization dissimilarity to token salience, modulates the error estimate across temporal tokens to account for uneven information loss. Second, Temporal Maintenance Distillation (TMD) computes pairwise cosine-similarity distributions between frame token sequences in both the FP and quantized models, then minimizes the KL divergence between these distributions so that each frame's gradient is jointly guided by all other frames. The total loss is L_task + γ·L_temporal, where L_task is the standard MSE between FP and quantized outputs and L_temporal enforces temporal coherence, with γ=100 used in practice.

## Results

On the VBench benchmark under the hardest W3A6 setting, Q-VDiT achieves a Scene Consistency of 23.40, nearly doubling the best prior method (EfficientDM at 12.04) and surpassing the closest competitor ViDiT-Q (11.99) by ~2×. Under W3A8, Q-VDiT reaches an Overall Consistency of 22.39 versus 18.53 for ViDiT-Q, and under W4A6 it achieves 25.02 Overall Consistency (vs. 24.53 for ViDiT-Q), approaching the FP16 baseline of 26.21. On the OpenSORA multi-aspects evaluation, Q-VDiT improves VQA-Technical from 29.58 (EfficientDM) to 59.10 under W3A6, and achieves near-lossless performance under W4A6 (CLIPSIM 0.1784 vs. 0.1797 FP).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Q-VDiT outperforms all eight baselines across every bit-width setting (W4A6, W3A8, W3A6) and all eight VBench dimensions. The most dramatic gap appears under W3A6: Q-VDiT's Scene Consistency of 23.40 nearly doubles EfficientDM (12.04) and ViDiT-Q (11.99), while its Overall Consistency of 22.58 exceeds ViDiT-Q (18.38) by 4.2 points. Under W3A8, Q-VDiT leads ViDiT-Q by 3.86 points in Overall Consistency (22.39 vs. 18.53) and by 9.88 in Scene Consistency (25.07 vs. 15.19). Under the milder W4A6 setting, Q-VDiT still wins on all metrics, with the closest competitor ViDiT-Q trailing by 0.49 in Overall Consistency (25.02 vs. 24.53) and 5.96 in Scene Consistency (38.66 vs. 32.70). LLM-oriented baselines (SmoothQuant, Quarot) and image-focused DiT methods (Q-DiT, PTQ4DiT) degrade severely under 3-bit weight quantization, with Scene Consistency dropping below 8, whereas Q-VDiT maintains 23.40. On the OpenSORA multi-aspects evaluation, Q-VDiT improves VQA-Technical from 29.58 (EfficientDM, best prior) to 59.10 under W3A6, and under W4A6 achieves near-lossless CLIPSIM (0.1784 vs. 0.1797 FP16).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of post-training quantization work for diffusion transformers (Q-DiT, PTQ4DiT, SVDQuant, ViDiT-Q) from image generation to video generation, identifying that the additional temporal dimension fundamentally changes the quantization landscape. The Token-aware Quantization Estimator (TQE) is conceptually a rank-1 LoRA module (building on Hu et al., 2021 and SVDQuant's LoRunner kernel) repurposed to compensate for quantization error across both token and feature dimensions, while the Temporal Maintenance Distillation (TMD) introduces a KL-divergence-based distillation loss over inter-frame similarity distributions—a departure from the standard MSE-based output alignment used in prior DiT quantization methods. The theoretical grounding (Theorem 3.2) that quantization error carries strictly lower information entropy than the original weight provides a principled justification for the low-rank error estimation approach.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As video-generation DiTs (Open-Sora, Latte, and successors) grow toward billions of parameters, efficient quantization becomes critical for edge deployment, and this paper demonstrates that image-focused quantization methods fail catastrophically on video tasks (e.g., scene consistency dropping to near zero at W3A6). The near-doubling of scene consistency at W3A6 and the ability to exceed FP quality at W4A8 suggest that video-specific quantization is not merely an incremental improvement but a necessary design consideration. However, the evaluation is limited to two specific architectures (Open-Sora and Latte) with relatively small calibration sets, and it remains open whether the TMD temporal distillation generalizes to longer videos, autoregressive video models, or substantially larger DiT backbones. The approach also assumes a fixed number of frames during calibration, which may not transfer to variable-length generation.
