## Problem

Existing LLM self-improvement mechanisms suffer from a feedback bottleneck: reward models compress evaluation into scalar signals that lack actionable guidance, while verification tools produce low-level execution traces that don't translate to high-level fixes. This paper asks whether a critic model can be trained end-to-end to generate textual feedback that maximizes the correction performance of a fixed generator, without any human-annotated critique supervision.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252735/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The critic receives a natural-language programming problem and a candidate code solution as input; during training it also conditions on sandbox execution feedback (pass/fail per test case, error messages), but at test time it sees only the problem and solution. The output is a structured critique with three fixed components — an analysis of the solution's strengths and weaknesses, concrete improvement suggestions, and a binary correctness judgment (correct/incorrect) — which the generator consumes to produce a revised solution. The process is iterative: critique-revision rounds repeat until the judgment signals correctness, and the critic was trained on single-turn tasks but generalizes to multi-turn settings.

## Method

CTRL is a two-stage training pipeline that decouples the critic from the generator. Stage I synthesizes high-quality critiques by prompting a base model (Qwen2.5-Coder-32B) with execution-feedback hints (mapped from sandbox results: positive feedback for passing, restart suggestion for total failure, exact error details for partial failure), then performs supervised finetuning with the hints removed to internalize the strategy. Stage II refines the critic via Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), where for each problem-solution pair a group of G critiques is sampled, each drives the generator to produce a revised solution, and group-relative advantages (normalized by group mean/std) provide low-variance credit assignment without a value network. The training objective directly maximizes the expected correctness of the revised solution, and a KL regularization term prevents over-optimization. The key design choice is using GRPO over PPO because the double summation over both solution and critique spaces makes value-network-based credit assignment unstable, whereas GRPO's Monte Carlo group sampling naturally handles the high-variance, large-action-space setting.

## Results

On CodeContests with Qwen2.5-Coder as generator, CTRL achieves 11.76% Pass@1 (single-turn) and 16.24% (×5 iterations), a 106.1% relative improvement over zero-shot (7.88%), while maintaining a low regression rate (Δ↓ = 0.85%). When paired with the stronger GPT-4o generator, CTRL achieves 25.45% Pass@1 (×5) on CodeContests, a 23.5% relative improvement over GPT-4o's zero-shot (20.61%). Across all three code benchmarks, CTRL's average Pass@1 reaches 45.83% with GPT-4o as generator (vs. 43.53% zero-shot) and 42.77% with Qwen2.5-Coder (vs. 38.75% zero-shot). On JudgeBench, the CTRL critic achieves 64.3% accuracy as a generative reward model, competitive with Claude 3.5-Sonnet.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares CTRL against two main baselines: self-critique (the generator critiques its own output, following the Self-Refine paradigm) and GPT-4o as an external critic. On CodeContests with Qwen2.5-Coder as generator, CTRL (11.76% single-turn, 16.24% ×5) outperforms both self-critique (8.36%, 9.21%) and GPT-4o critic (10.67%, 12.48%), with the gap widening substantially in multi-turn settings where baselines suffer from compounding errors. Notably, CTRL also reduces the regression rate (Δ↓) from 1.82% (self-critique) and 2.06% (GPT-4o) to just 0.85% in single-turn, and maintains 0.85% even at ×5 iterations versus 2.42% for both baselines. When the generator is upgraded to GPT-4o, the weaker CTRL critic (trained on Qwen2.5-Coder) still outperforms both GPT-4o self-critique (20.97%) and Qwen2.5-Coder critic (20.24%) in single-turn, and achieves 25.45% at ×5 versus 20.61% for GPT-4o self-critique — demonstrating a weak-to-strong generalization effect. On LiveCodeBench, CTRL's gains are more modest (33.21% vs. 32.32% for GPT-4o critic), and on MBPP+ the differences are small due to the easier task, but CTRL consistently maintains the lowest regression rates across all benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the self-improvement literature (Reflexion, Self-Refine) but departs fundamentally by decoupling the critic from the generator and training it via reinforcement learning rather than relying on the model's innate self-evaluation. Methodologically, it adopts GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) for variance-reduced policy optimization, addressing the credit-assignment instability observed with PPO in this setting. The two-stage pipeline—execution-guided critique synthesis for SFT followed by RL—repurposes the insight from Ni et al. (2024) that LLMs can reason over execution traces, but channels this into a dedicated critic model rather than a one-shot revision. Conceptually, the work positions itself at the intersection of generative reward models and scalable oversight, unifying discrimination and actionable critiquing in a single textual output without human-annotated critique supervision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The demonstration that a 32B critic trained against itself can effectively guide GPT-4o provides concrete evidence for weak-to-strong generalization in the critic role, with implications for scalable oversight where human or oracle supervision is unavailable. The Markov-chain analysis formalizes why critiquing ability (not just discrimination) is the bottleneck for iterative refinement, offering a principled target for future training. Key limitations include the reliance on execution feedback (restricting applicability to verifiable domains like code), a notable timeout trade-off on LiveCodeBench, and still-low absolute pass rates on hard benchmarks. Open questions include extending the framework to domains without sandbox verification, training explicitly for multi-turn revision, and whether the weak-to-strong effect persists as the generator-critic capability gap widens further.
