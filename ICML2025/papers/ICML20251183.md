## Problem

Backpropagation-free PTQ methods like GPTQ assume layer-wise independence and use naive nearest-rounding for weight assignment, ignoring inter-layer dependencies within the attention module that are critical for Transformer performance. Gradient-based alternatives (AdaRound, BRECQ, OmniQuant) can model these dependencies but require impractical optimization loops for billion-parameter LLMs. No existing backpropagation-free method optimizes quantized weights while accounting for how quantizing one projection (e.g., query) affects the attention weights and thus other projections (e.g., key, value).

## Input / Output

BOA consumes pre-trained LLM weights (Q, K, V, and output projections per attention head) along with 128 calibration sequences of length 2048 sampled from WikiText2. It produces uniform integer weights at 2-, 3-, or 4-bit precision, with quantization parameters (scale, zero-point) set per head. For weight-activation quantization, input activations and KV caches are additionally quantized using dynamic per-token Min-Max rounding, yielding configurations denoted WxAyKVz (e.g., W2A4KV4). The method operates on a single NVIDIA H100 GPU and requires no gradient computation or model retraining.

## Method

BOA replaces GPTQ's layer-wise Hessian (which depends only on the layer input) with attention-aware Hessians derived from the attention reconstruction error, so that quantizing W_Q accounts for its effect on attention weights A_h and thus on W_K, W_V, and W_out. Specifically, the Hessian for W_{Q,h} takes the form 2XX^T ⊗ K_h^T K_h (after Hessian relaxation that avoids the expensive softmax Jacobian), coupling the query projection to the key projection through the attention mechanism. To keep computation tractable, BOA exploits Kronecker-product structure to compute inverse Hessians and Cholesky decompositions in O(d³) rather than O(d³d_h³), and introduces a refined row-update formula (Proposition 3.1) that compensates quantization error across rows within a head. Finally, rows belonging to different attention heads are quantized simultaneously (head-wise joint quantization), reducing sequential quantization steps by a factor of H and cutting processing time by over 40× on 30B models.

## Results

On weight-only INT2 quantization of LLaMA2-13B with QuaRot transformation, BOA achieves a WikiText-2 PPL of 8.237 and 56.92% average zero-shot accuracy, compared to 9.593 PPL and 49.51% for QuaRot-GPTQ, 12.92 PPL for OmniQuant, and 9.415 PPL for AffineQuant. On LLaMA2-7B INT2, BOA reduces PPL from 22.05 (QuaRot-GPTQ) to 10.42 and improves average accuracy by 12 percentage points (51.00% vs. 38.98%). For weight-activation quantization (W2A4KV4) on LLaMA2-13B, BOA achieves 8.974 PPL and 52.50% average accuracy, outperforming SpinQuant-GPTQ (15.54 PPL, 40.02%) by 12.5 percentage points in accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BOA consistently outperforms GPTQ across all model sizes and bit-widths, with the largest gains at INT2: 12%p average accuracy improvement on LLaMA2-7B (51.00% vs. 38.98%) and 7.4%p on LLaMA2-13B (56.92% vs. 49.51%) under QuaRot transformation. Against gradient-based transformation methods (OmniQuant, AffineQuant, DuQuant+LWC), BOA achieves 10–20%p accuracy improvements at INT2 while being 2–7× faster in processing time (e.g., 0.96 h vs. 1.83 h for OmniQuant on LLaMA-7B). Compared to QuaRot-GPTQ (the strongest Hessian-based baseline), BOA reduces PPL by 53% on LLaMA2-7B INT2 (10.42 vs. 22.05) and by 14% on LLaMA2-13B INT2 (8.237 vs. 9.593). For weight-activation quantization (W2A4KV4), BOA with SpinQuant transformation achieves 10–12.5%p accuracy gains over SpinQuant-GPTQ on LLaMA2 models, while OmniQuant and AffineQuant either diverge (NaN) or remain far behind. BOA also surpasses DuQuant and DuQuant+LWC by 10–15%p at INT2, confirming that the attention-aware Hessian provides a more effective weight-rounding strategy than permutation-based outlier distribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

BOA is a direct extension of GPTQ's Hessian-based, backpropagation-free quantization framework, replacing GPTQ's layer-wise reconstruction error (which assumes inter-layer independence) with an attention reconstruction error that captures dependencies among Q, K, V, and output projections within the multi-head attention module. It departs from gradient-based PTQ methods (AdaRound, BRECQ, OmniQuant, AffineQuant) by remaining one-shot, and from transformation-based methods (QuaRot, SpinQuant, DuQuant) by optimizing the actual integer weight assignment rather than relying on nearest rounding. The paper positions itself as the first backpropagation-free method to explicitly model inter-layer dependencies, and it composes with transformation methods (QuaRot for weight-only, SpinQuant for weight-activation) to achieve state-of-the-art results.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

BOA provides a practical, one-shot quantization pipeline that significantly outperforms GPTQ at low bit-widths (INT2/INT3) while remaining far faster than gradient-based alternatives, making it attractive for deploying LLMs on edge devices with limited compute. The head-wise simultaneous quantization trick (40× speedup) and the Kronecker-product-based efficient inverse/Cholesky computation address the main practical objection to using richer Hessians. Open questions include whether the head-independence assumption can be relaxed, whether the Hessian relaxation (dropping the softmax Jacobian) can be tightened for further gains, and whether the approach extends naturally to architectures beyond standard multi-head attention (e.g., sliding-window or linear attention).
