## Problem

Existing geometric-algebra-based equivariant neural networks such as CGENN suffer from overparameterization, leading to overfitting on small training datasets and inefficient training times. This is particularly problematic in natural science applications where datasets are typically small. The paper addresses this gap by introducing a parameter-light architecture that respects the fundamental algebraic structures of geometric algebras, balancing expressiveness with parameter efficiency.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250662/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Inputs are multivectors (elements of geometric algebras Cl_{p,q,r}) encoding geometric quantities at multiple grades simultaneously—scalars, vectors, bivectors, trivectors, and higher-grade objects. The network processes these by decomposing them into four subspaces defined by the grade involution and reversion (quaternion types 0–3), operating in groups with a step size of 4 rather than per individual grade. Outputs are multivectors that can be projected onto Cl^0 for scalar predictions (invariant) or Cl^1 for vector predictions (equivariant), with the full multivector structure preserved internally.

## Method

GLGENN introduces generalized Lipschitz groups that preserve the four Z₂×Z₂-graded subspaces of a geometric algebra under the twisted adjoint representation, and proves that equivariance under these groups implies equivariance under the corresponding pseudo-orthogonal group. The architecture's key weight-sharing mechanism treats the four quaternion-type subspaces uniformly: Cℓ-linear layers apply the same linear map across all four subspaces, Cℓ-geometric product layers compute second-order interactions parameterized by 4³ coefficients per pair, and Cℓ-normalization layers stabilize products using norm functions. Conjugation operation layers with discrete ±1 coefficients provide additional equivariant nonlinearity. This unified treatment across the four subspaces reduces the parameter count by more than a factor of two compared to CGENN while maintaining full pseudo-orthogonal equivariance, as the layers are provably equivariant under the generalized Lipschitz group action.

## Results

On the O(5,0)-Convex Hull Volume Estimation task, GLGENN outperforms CGENN with K=256 points (MSE 2918 vs. 3385) and K=512 points (MSE 4872 vs. 7212), while using roughly half the trainable parameters (791K vs. 1.72M and 922K vs. 1.75M respectively). On the O(5,0)-Regression task, GLGENN achieves MSE 0.0011 with 30K training samples, matching CGENN's 0.0003 within a small margin while using only ~0.6K GA-layer parameters versus CGENN's ~1.8K. Across all experiments, GLGENN consistently uses 2–3× fewer optimizable parameters than CGENN, reducing overfitting risk on small datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CGENN (Ruhe et al., 2023), GLGENN outperforms on the convex hull tasks with K=256 (MSE 2918 vs. 3385) and K=512 (MSE 4872 vs. 7212) at 2¹⁴ training samples, while using roughly half the parameters. On the O(5,0)-Regression task, CGENN achieves slightly lower MSE at the largest dataset size (0.0003 vs. 0.0011), but GLGENN reaches comparable performance with ~3× fewer GA-layer parameters (0.6K vs. 1.8K) and less training time. Against the equivariant MLP baselines from Finzi et al. (2021) (EMLP-O(5) and EMLP-SO(5)), GLGENN substantially outperforms on the regression task, achieving MSE 0.0011 vs. 0.0273–0.0279 at 30K samples. Against Geometric Vector Perceptrons (GAP, Jing et al., 2021), GLGENN dominates on the convex hull task, where GAP produces negative or near-zero MSE values indicating complete failure to learn the volume function, while GLGENN achieves meaningful predictions across all dataset sizes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on CGENN (Ruhe et al., 2023), which first introduced Clifford Group Equivariant Neural Networks using the Lipschitz group and twisted adjoint representation in geometric algebras. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of *generalized* Lipschitz groups that preserve the four subspaces defined by grade involution and reversion (quaternion types) rather than the n+1 individual grade subspaces, enabling a weight-sharing parametrization that reduces trainable parameters by roughly a factor of two. Theoretically, the work extends the spin group and Lipschitz group literature (Benn & Tucker, Lounesto, Atiyah-Bott-Shapiro) by proving equivalences between these generalized groups and norm-function-defined groups, and by establishing that equivariance under the generalized Lipschitz group implies equivariance under the corresponding pseudo-orthogonal group. The architecture also sits within the broader equivariant neural network lineage (Cohen & Welling 2016; Finzi et al. 2021; Thomas et al. 2018) and the GA-in-neural-networks line (Pearson & Bisset 1994; Brandstetter et al. 2023; Brehmer et al. 2023).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Parameter-light equivariant architectures are practically important in natural science applications (molecular modeling, particle physics, fluid dynamics) where training datasets are often small and overfitting is a persistent risk; GLGENN's ~2× parameter reduction with matched or improved performance directly addresses this. The theoretical contribution—generalized Lipschitz groups and their relation to pseudo-orthogonal groups—provides a cleaner algebraic framework that may simplify the design of future GA-based equivariant layers. Limitations include experiments restricted to non-degenerate GAs (Cl_{p,q}) and relatively simple benchmark tasks; the paper explicitly defers degenerate GA experiments and more complex real-world applications to future work. An open question is whether the weight-sharing structure generalizes well to higher-dimensional GAs (n > 4) where the four quaternion-type subspaces contain multiple grades, and how the approach compares to irrep-based equivariant methods at scale.
