## Problem

Existing VLA models (Octo, OpenVLA, RT-2) fine-tune pre-trained vision-language encoders on robotic datasets, which degrades the rich semantic alignments learned from large-scale image-text pre-training and causes significant performance drops on unseen objects and environments. The paper asks whether a VLA can instead preserve these pre-trained alignments by keeping the VLM frozen and selectively extracting only task-relevant visual features guided by the language instruction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251000/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes multi-view 224×224 RGB images, a natural-language instruction, and a 10-dimensional proprioceptive state (absolute end-effector xyz, a 6DoF rotation vector from the first two rows of the SO(3) matrix, and continuous gripper position). It outputs a 10-dimensional delta end-effector pose (relative translation + 6DoF rotation vector + absolute gripper position) for the next 12 timesteps, executed via temporal ensembling with a receding-horizon action window of 8 steps. The policy transformer operates over a context window of T=12 past timesteps, and the entire CLIP backbone is frozen at inference, enabling 50 Hz control on a single GPU.

## Method

OTTER's core mechanism is a parameter-free text-aware visual feature extraction that replaces direct feature passing. It uses the attention output X_attn from CLIP's last vision attention block (motivated by ClearCLIP's finding that this is cleaner than the full output X_out), computes per-token cosine similarities between normalized text features and visual patch features, and applies a temperature-weighted softmax (Eq. 4) to produce a selection distribution over spatial patches for each language token. The resulting text-aware visual tokens are compressed via learnable cross-attention pooling (4 queries, 8 heads, 2 blocks) into a single token per camera, concatenated with a pooled text token and an MLP-encoded embodiment feature, and fed into a 4-layer, 8-head causal transformer (hidden dim 512) with an FFN action head. Critically, the entire CLIP model is frozen—only the temperature τ is learnable—decoupling task-relevant feature selection from action planning and preserving zero-shot generalization. The model is trained via imitation learning on robot demonstration datasets, optionally pre-trained on Open X-Embodiment before fine-tuning.

## Results

On the multi-primitive zero-shot generalization benchmark (150 unseen trials across pouring, drawer, poking, and pick-and-place), OTTER-OXE-L achieves a 77%±3.3% mean success rate, with per-primitive rates of 77% (pouring), 75% (drawer), 93% (poking), and 75% (pick-and-place). The base OTTER model (trained from scratch on 1,185 demonstrations) reaches 67%±3.8%, while OTTER-OXE (pre-trained on Open X-Embodiment) reaches 70%±3.6%. In simulation on LIBERO, OTTER achieves 84%±1.1% on in-distribution tasks and 61%±1.1% on unseen tasks, compared to 48%±1.0% for Finetuned OpenVLA and 26%±1.1% for Finetuned Octo on unseen tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OTTER decisively outperforms both Finetuned Octo (4% mean) and Finetuned OpenVLA (0.6% mean) across all four manipulation primitives, with the gap being most dramatic on pouring (63–77% vs. 0%) and drawer manipulation (50–75% vs. 0%). Against π₀-Fast-Droid, which achieves 61% on pick-and-place (its dominant training distribution) but 0% on the other three primitives, OTTER maintains 60–93% across all primitives, yielding a 67–77% mean versus 29%. Even after fine-tuning π₀-Fast-Droid on the multi-primitive dataset, it still fails on pouring (0%) and lags OTTER by 32–42 percentage points in mean success rate. The results indicate that direct feature-passing architectures (Octo, OpenVLA, DFP-OTTER) cannot learn generalizable vision-language associations from small robotic datasets, whereas OTTER's frozen CLIP with text-aware feature extraction preserves pre-trained semantic alignment and transfers effectively to unseen objects and environments.

## Relation to Prior Work

OTTER departs from the dominant VLA paradigm (RT-2, OpenVLA, Octo) that fine-tunes pre-trained vision-language models or trains vision encoders from scratch on robot data. Instead, it keeps CLIP entirely frozen and borrows ClearCLIP's insight that the last attention block's output carries cleaner text-aligned semantics, using cosine similarity between per-token text features and visual patch features to selectively extract task-relevant visual tokens before feeding them to a small policy transformer. This positions OTTER as a parameter-free fusion mechanism that leverages pre-trained alignment rather than relearning it, contrasting with FiLM-style conditioning (RT-1) that learns vision-language coupling from robot data alone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper challenges a core assumption in VLA research—that fine-tuning a pre-trained VLM on robot data is necessary for good performance—by showing that freezing the VLM and extracting text-aware features yields substantially better zero-shot generalization (62% vs. 9–12% on unseen tasks). The technique is lightweight (only a learnable temperature parameter) and composes well with scaling axes (larger CLIP, larger policy, more robot data), suggesting a practical path for data-efficient robot learning. Open questions include whether the approach extends to non-SE(3) morphologies (multi-finger hands), long-horizon tasks, and whether the frozen-encoder strategy generalizes beyond CLIP to other VLM families.
