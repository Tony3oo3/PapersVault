## Problem

Popular optimizers such as SGD, AdamW, and Lion rely on steepest descent in either ℓ2 or ℓ∞ norms, leaving a critical gap in handling the non-Euclidean geometry observed in modern deep network training. The paper asks how to develop and analyze optimizers under general ℓp norms with p ∈ (2,∞), which can better capture the anisotropic curvature structure of large neural network objectives than either purely Euclidean or purely sign-based updates.

## Input / Output

STACEY is an optimizer that consumes stochastic gradient estimates of a differentiable loss function and produces updated model parameters, with a tunable exponent p controlling the norm geometry of the descent direction. In practice it is applied to standard supervised image classification (CIFAR-10 at 32×32, ImageNet at 224×224) and self-supervised LLM pretraining (C4 text sequences), where the optimal p varies by task: p≈2 for CIFAR/ResNet-18 and p≈3 for LLaMA-100M pretraining. The per-iteration memory overhead is 2d (one momentum vector plus one dual vector), matching Adam's footprint.

## Method

STACEY combines two iterate sequences via a convex interpolation: a primal sequence following the ℓp steepest descent direction s_i(x) = x^(i)/(|x^(i)|^((p−2)/(p−1)) + ε), and a dual sequence following a mirror descent step (ℓ2-based in STACEY(p,2), ℓp-based in STACEY(p,p)). The final iterate is θ_{t+1} = τ·z_{t+1} + (1−τ)·y_{t+1} − η_t·λ·θ_t, where τ controls the primal-dual coupling that produces acceleration, distinct from momentum which is applied separately to both sequences. Theoretically, the paper establishes an O(ε^(−4)) convergence rate for stochastic ℓp descent under ℓp-smoothness, bounded variance, and bounded gradient assumptions, strictly generalizing the p=∞ (signSGD) case. The key technical challenge is that the coordinate-wise re-scaling introduces bias (E[s(g̃)] ≠ s(g)), requiring novel Taylor-expansion and Markov-inequality arguments to bound the bias terms rather than relying on standard unbiased-gradient cancellations.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (ResNet-18, 200 epochs), STACEY(p,p) achieves 93.79% test accuracy and 0.0021 training NLL, outperforming SGD+Momentum (92.76%, 0.0352), Adam (92.66%, 0.0083), AdamW (92.12%, 0.0195), and Lion (92.15%, 0.0245). On ImageNet (ResNet-50, 60 epochs), STACEY(p,p) reaches 69.87% top-1 accuracy versus 68.81% (SGD), 69.31% (AdamW), and 69.62% (Lion). On LLM pretraining (llama-100m, C4, 30k steps), STACEY(p,p) achieves 4.4962 training loss, substantially below AdamW (5.4268), Adam (6.2485), SGD (6.3920), and Lion (6.7794).

## Baselines & Comparisons

STACEY consistently outperforms all four baselines across all three tasks. On CIFAR-10, STACEY(p,p) achieves 93.79% test accuracy at 200 epochs, beating SGD+Momentum by 1.03 points, Adam by 1.13 points, AdamW by 1.67 points, and Lion by 1.64 points, while also reducing training NLL by 4–17×. On ImageNet, STACEY(p,p) edges out Lion (69.87% vs. 69.62%) and AdamW (69.87% vs. 69.31%) at 60 epochs, with a clear margin over SGD (68.81%). On LLM pretraining, the advantage is most dramatic: STACEY(p,p) reaches 4.4962 training loss at 30k steps, a full 0.93 points below AdamW (5.4268) and nearly 1.9 points below SGD (6.3920), while Lion performs worst (6.7794) with high variance. Notably, the best ℓp-norm differs by task: p≈2 is optimal for CIFAR-10, while p≈3 is optimal for LLM pretraining, underscoring the value of the flexible norm parameter.

## Relation to Prior Work

STACEY sits at the intersection of two well-studied lines: non-Euclidean acceleration (Allen-Zhu & Orecchia's linear coupling, Nesterov's smooth minimization, Nemirovskii & Nesterov's ℓp-smooth convex rates) and sign-based optimizers (signSGD, Lion), which are ℓ∞ steepest descent. The paper's key positioning is filling the gap between the two known extremes—ℓ2 (SGD/AdamW) and ℓ∞ (signSGD/Lion)—by providing the first general ℓp stochastic descent guarantees for 2 < p < ∞ in the non-convex setting, strictly generalizing signSGD's analysis. The acceleration mechanism is a primal-dual interpolation (coupling an ℓp steepest descent step with a mirror descent step), which the authors explicitly distinguish from momentum and from Lion-K's single steepest-descent update, showing the two are not iterate-equivalent.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled framework for choosing the norm geometry of an optimizer based on the Hessian structure of the objective, with the empirical finding that the optimal p is task-dependent (p≈2 for CIFAR image classification, p≈3 for LLM pretraining) suggesting that a one-size-fits-all ℓ2 or ℓ∞ approach is suboptimal. Open questions include how to automatically select or adapt p during training (the authors note parameter-free approaches could help), whether the acceleration exponent trade-off (decreasing from 2 toward 1 as p grows) can be mitigated, and whether the gains persist at much larger scales (the LLM experiments use only a 100M-parameter model). The 2d memory overhead matching Adam makes the method practical, but the modest accuracy margins (e.g., ~1% on CIFAR, ~0.3% on ImageNet) suggest the benefit may be most pronounced in settings with strongly anisotropic curvature.
