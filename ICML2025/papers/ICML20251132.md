## Problem

HE-based GCN inference suffers from a fundamental conflict: the irregular sparsity of graph adjacency matrices demands per-node variable aggregation patterns, while CKKS's SIMD structure requires uniform, fully-packed ciphertext operations. Existing methods either exploit sparsity (CryptoGCN) or SIMD packing (Penguin) but not both simultaneously, resulting in redundant rotations and multiplications that limit practical speedups.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251132/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The client encrypts the node feature matrix X ∈ R^{N×F} using CKKS and sends the ciphertexts to an honest-but-curious server, which holds the plaintext adjacency matrix A and trained weight matrices W. The server returns encrypted node embeddings that the client decrypts locally. A key structural detail is that the column-wise packing parameter t (number of feature columns per ciphertext) and the node ordering within each ciphertext are jointly optimized offline via the NOO algorithm, so the client's initial encryption order is determined by back-propagating the optimal aggregation-friendly arrangement from the final layer.

## Method

FicGCN combines three mechanisms to exploit GCN sparsity under CKKS constraints. First, a latency-aware packing scheme selects the optimal column-packing parameter t by minimizing an objective function that trades off PMult/Add counts against rotation overhead, with the optimal t depending on whether ciphertext slots M exceed or fall short of N·F'. Second, Sparse Intra-Ciphertext Aggregation (SpIntra-CA) replaces per-node extraction with bit-decomposed rotations: each node's required shift is decomposed into binary bits, and rotations are executed from the lowest bit upward so that a single rotation serves all nodes whose corresponding bit is set, reducing the ideal-case rotation count to log(M); Aggregation Order Optimization (AOO) minimizes each node's shift distance, and Ciphertext Processing Order Optimization (CPOO) merges consecutive sparse rotations into larger steps. Third, Node Order Optimization (NOO) performs BFS-based region partitioning on sibling-node connectivity, interleaves regions as sub-rings within the ciphertext to preserve rotational consistency, and greedily places nodes within each region to minimize slot conflicts. A layer-wise scheduler selects between Inter-CA (PMult/Add-dominated, used for the first layer where client-side encryption eliminates inter-ciphertext overhead) and SpIntra-CA (Rot-dominated, used for subsequent layers) via a closed-form latency comparison.

## Results

FicGCN achieves end-to-end inference latencies of 64.12s (Cora), 79.98s (Citeseer), 7733s (Corafull), and 1373.82s (NTU-cross-View), with 100% ciphertext slot utilization across all datasets. The headline speedups over the strongest prior method are 2.01× on Cora (vs. Penguin), 1.78× on Citeseer (vs. Penguin), 4.10× on Corafull (vs. CryptoGCN), and 1.26× on NTU (vs. CryptoGCN). Compared to the earliest baseline Gazelle, FicGCN delivers 21.6×, 36.2×, and >120× speedups on Cora, Citeseer, and Corafull respectively. The advantage grows with graph scale, with the largest gains observed on Corafull (19,793 nodes, 8,710 features).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FicGCN outperforms all three baselines on every dataset tested. Against Gazelle (the earliest HE-GCN framework), FicGCN achieves 21.6×, 36.2×, and >120× latency reductions on Cora, Citeseer, and Corafull respectively, primarily because Gazelle does not exploit graph sparsity and suffers from low slot utilization (35–81%). Against Penguin (NeurIPS 2024), which uses efficient block-wise packing but ignores sparsity, FicGCN wins by 2.01× on Cora and 1.78× on Citeseer, with the gap widening on Corafull where Penguin's fixed packing becomes a bottleneck (35,565s vs. 7,733s). Against CryptoGCN (NeurIPS 2022), which exploits sparsity but suffers from low slot utilization (66–90%) and redundant ciphertexts, FicGCN achieves a 4.10× speedup on Corafull and 1.26× on NTU. The paper attributes its advantage to two factors: SpIntra-CA resolves the sparsity-vs-SIMD trade-off that limits both Penguin and CryptoGCN, and the NOO reordering algorithm reduces rotation counts by up to 66% compared to unoptimized SpIntra-CA.

## Relation to Prior Work

FicGCN extends the HE+GCN inference lineage from Gazelle (diagonal encoding) through CryptoGCN (sparsity-aware aggregation) and Penguin (block-wise packing) by co-optimizing all three dimensions simultaneously: sparsity exploitation, ciphertext slot utilization, and node ordering. Its key conceptual departure is the SpIntra-CA technique, which performs neighbor aggregation within a single ciphertext via bit-decomposed rotations rather than constructing multiple inter-ciphertext copies as in CryptoGCN, and the NOO algorithm that reorders nodes to exploit CKKS's ring structure for conflict minimization. The paper positions itself as resolving the fundamental tension between GCN's irregular sparsity and CKKS's SIMD computation pattern, a conflict that prior work addressed only partially.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 4.1× speedup on Corafull (19,793 nodes) is practically significant for deploying HE-based GCN services in privacy-sensitive domains like healthcare and finance, where prior methods required over 30,000 seconds. However, the speedup is highly scale-dependent (only 1.26× on the 25-node NTU dataset), and the evaluation is limited to 2-layer GAE models on a single CPU thread, leaving open whether the approach scales to deeper architectures or benefits from multi-threaded/parallel HE libraries. The NOO preprocessing, while shown to be negligible at 1M nodes (Pokec), introduces an offline dependency on graph structure that complicates dynamic or streaming graph scenarios.
