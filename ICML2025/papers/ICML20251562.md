## Problem

While network quantization (PTQ and QAT) is known to preserve overall model accuracy, it can disproportionately degrade accuracy for underrepresented demographic groups, yet the underlying mechanisms remain unexplained. This paper provides the first theoretical analysis identifying which factors drive disparate accuracy loss across groups and why QAT exacerbates unfairness more severely than PTQ despite its superior overall accuracy at low bit-widths.

## Input / Output

The method takes pre-trained CNN classifiers (ResNet50, ResNet18, VGG19) trained on image datasets—48×48 face images (UTK-Face, FER2013), 32×32 RGB images (CIFAR-10), and 28×28 grayscale digits (MNIST)—and quantizes their weights from 32-bit floating point to int16, int8, or int4 using signed symmetric uniform quantization. Beyond standard per-class accuracy, the output is a per-group accuracy breakdown and the fairness metric φ(D), defined as the maximum absolute gap in excessive loss (quantized loss minus full-precision loss) across all pairs of protected attribute subgroups.

## Method

The core contribution is a second-order Taylor expansion analysis bounding the excessive loss G(a) for each protected group. For PTQ, the bound decomposes into a term proportional to the group's gradient norm and a term proportional to the trace of the group's Hessian matrix, both scaled by the quantization step size s_max. Two lemmas then prove that both the gradient norm and Hessian trace are inversely proportional to group size, establishing class imbalance as the root cause of disparate degradation. For QAT, the analysis reveals additional cross-terms coupling gradient norms with Hessian traces (via the learning rate η), explaining why QAT's fairness deterioration is strictly worse than PTQ's. As a mitigation, the paper applies geometric transformations (rotation, zoom, flip) and random erasing (randomly sized square patches) to augment underrepresented groups until all groups reach equal size, empirically reducing φ(D) by an order of magnitude at int4.

## Results

On the imbalanced UTK-Face dataset with ResNet50, the fairness metric φ(D) for PTQ rises from 2.4% at int16 to 18.8% at int4, while QAT rises more severely from 3.3% to 25.4%. On the balanced CIFAR-10 dataset, φ(D) remains minimal (0.9%→1.3% for PTQ, 1.1%→1.5% for QAT), confirming that class imbalance drives the unfairness. Applying geometric transformations (GT) or random erasing (RE) as data augmentation reduces φ(D) at int4 on UTK-Face from 18.8%/25.4% (no augmentation) to 2.6%/2.7% (GT) and 1.7%/2.0% (RE) for PTQ/QAT respectively, demonstrating a >85% reduction in unfairness.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's central comparison is between PTQ and QAT in terms of fairness preservation. QAT consistently exhibits worse fairness than PTQ across all imbalanced datasets: on UTK-Face (ResNet50), QAT's φ(D) at int4 is 25.4% versus PTQ's 18.8%, and on FER2013 (VGG19) the gap widens to 65.7% vs 50.7%. The theoretical explanation is that QAT's excessive loss bound includes additional interaction terms between gradient norms and Hessian traces that are absent in PTQ. On balanced datasets (CIFAR-10, MNIST), both methods show negligible unfairness (φ(D) < 1.5%), confirming that class imbalance—not the quantization method itself—is the root cause. For mitigation, both GT and RE data augmentation dramatically outperform the no-augmentation baseline, reducing int4 φ(D) on UTK-Face by over 85% for both PTQ and QAT. Among augmentation strategies, RE with random patch sizes (n sampled from {3,...,20}) slightly outperforms GT, while fixed patch sizes (n=3 or n=20) are nearly ineffective, matching the no-augmentation baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the empirical observations of disparate fairness impacts from model compression (Hooker et al. 2019, 2020; Jha et al. 2019; Joseph et al. 2020) into a rigorous theoretical framework specifically for quantization, building on the analogous pruning analysis by Tran et al. (2022). It departs from prior work by identifying gradient norms and Hessian traces as the two responsible factors for quantization-induced unfairness, and by providing the first theoretical comparison showing why QAT exacerbates unfairness more than PTQ through additional interaction terms. The mitigation strategy repurposes standard data augmentation techniques (geometric transformations, random erasing) from the class-imbalance and fairness literature (Sharma et al. 2020; Mroueh et al. 2021) into the quantization context.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that QAT—typically preferred over PTQ for accuracy preservation at low bit-widths—actually produces worse fairness is a critical practical insight for deploying quantized models on resource-constrained devices (mobile phones, access control systems) where fairness and privacy are paramount. The simple data augmentation mitigation (reducing φ(D) from 25.4% to 2.0–2.7% at int4) offers an immediately actionable remedy. Limitations include scope restricted to CNN classification on image tasks, reliance on upper-bound theoretical analysis, and the assumption of symmetric quantization; the authors note future work on detection tasks, NLP, and solutions beyond data augmentation.
