## Problem

Recent work showed that simple Bayesian optimization methods perform surprisingly well on high-dimensional real-world benchmarks, contradicting the long-held belief that GP-based BO is limited to ~20 dimensions. However, the underlying reasons for these performance gains were not thoroughly identified. This paper investigates the specific challenges (vanishing gradients in GP fitting and acquisition function optimization) that cause HDBO failures and explains why recent methods succeed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251915/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on a d-dimensional continuous search space [0,1]^d (tested up to 6392 dimensions) with an expensive black-box objective function, consuming sequential point evaluations under a fixed budget. The GP surrogate uses a 5/2-ARD-Matern kernel with d+1 hyperparameters, and the acquisition function is LogEI maximized via multi-start L-BFGS-B from 512 scrambled Sobol candidates. The key implementation detail is that the initial length scale for MLE is set to √d/10 (rather than the GPyTorch default of ln 2), and RAASP sampling perturbs the top 5% observations by replacing each dimension with probability min(1, 20/d) using a truncated Gaussian with σ=10⁻³.

## Method

The paper identifies that vanishing gradients of the GP marginal log-likelihood with respect to length scales are the primary cause of HDBO failures: when initialized with short length scales (e.g., ln 2), the MLL gradient drops below machine precision in high dimensions, leaving length scales unfitted. The proposed MSR method addresses this by initializing the MLE optimizer at √d/10, which is long enough to avoid vanishing gradients while remaining short enough to allow the optimizer to find the true length scale. Additionally, MSR incorporates RAASP sampling during acquisition function maximization, which generates local candidates around the incumbent and automatically reverts to local search behavior when the AF surface is flat due to vanishing gradients. The paper demonstrates that plain MLE with this initialization (no MAP hyperprior) achieves state-of-the-art performance, and that RAASP sampling reduces the variance of length scale estimates compared to MLE without it. Empirically, MSR matches or exceeds the dimensionality-scaled prior (DSP) method on benchmarks ranging from 60 to 6392 dimensions.

## Results

MSR (MLE Scaled with RAASP) achieves state-of-the-art performance across four real-world HDBO benchmarks, matching or exceeding the SOTA DSP method. MSR ranks first on 124d Mopta08 and second on 888d Ant and 6392d Humanoid, while DSP ranks first on Lasso-DNA and Humanoid. The constant-length-scale MLE baseline (ℓ=ln2) fails entirely on the high-dimensional Ant and Humanoid benchmarks due to vanishing gradients of the GP marginal log-likelihood, while MSR's √d-scaled initialization avoids this failure mode. On synthetic 100-dimensional benchmarks (Levy, Schwefel, Griewank), no single algorithm dominates: TuRBO wins on Levy, CMA-ES on Schwefel, and DSP on Griewank.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DSP (Hvarfner et al., 2024), the current SOTA using MAP with a dimensionality-scaled log-normal prior, MSR matches or slightly outperforms: MSR wins on 124d Mopta08 (rank 1 vs. 2) and 888d Ant (rank 2 vs. 4), while DSP wins on 180d Lasso-DNA (rank 1 vs. 4) and 6392d Humanoid (rank 1 vs. 2). Against Bounce (Papenmeier et al., 2023), MSR consistently outperforms it on all four benchmarks where both are evaluated (ranks 1–2 vs. 3–5). Against SAASBO (Eriksson & Jankowiak, 2021), Bounce itself surpasses SAASBO, especially on Ant, and SAASBO is excluded from the main ranking due to its prohibitive runtime (terminated at 72 hours). Against TuRBO (Eriksson et al., 2019), the comparison is limited to synthetic 100d benchmarks where no method dominates: TuRBO wins on Levy, DSP on Griewank, and CMA-ES on Schwefel, illustrating that exploration-exploitation trade-offs are problem-dependent. The ablation MLE(ℓ=ln2) without RAASP sampling collapses to random-search-like behavior on Ant and Humanoid, confirming that both the √d-scaled initialization and RAASP sampling are necessary for reliable high-dimensional performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper is a diagnostic empirical study that builds on the recent wave of work showing simple GP-based BO methods (Hvarfner et al., 2024; Xu & Zhe, 2024) perform well in high dimensions, but asks *why* they succeed. It draws on TuRBO's RAASP sampling mechanism (Eriksson et al., 2019) and the dimensionality-scaled log-normal prior of DSP (Hvarfner et al., 2024), but departs from the MAP-based fitting paradigm by demonstrating that plain MLE with a √d-scaled length scale initialization (MSR) is sufficient for state-of-the-art performance. The paper positions itself against the broader HDBO literature (SAASBO, Bounce, COMBO, BODi) by arguing that the key differentiator is not architectural complexity but numerical stability of the GP fitting and acquisition function optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of vanishing gradients in GP MLE as a previously overlooked root cause of HDBO failures provides a concrete, actionable fix (scale initial length scales by √d) that practitioners can adopt without specifying arbitrary hyperpriors. The finding that popular benchmarks like Mopta08 and Lasso-DNA have substantially lower effective dimensionality than their nominal input count raises important questions about whether current HDBO evaluations truly stress-test algorithms. Open questions include whether the findings extend to non-GP surrogates (random forests, Bayesian neural networks), whether the local-search behavior that drives performance at ~1000+ dimensions can be made more principled, and how to design benchmarks that avoid the 'simple structure' confound identified here.
