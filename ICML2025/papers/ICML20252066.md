## Problem

Parallel Tempering (PT) is the state-of-the-art MCMC method for sampling from unnormalized densities in terms of target evaluation efficiency, but it produces only dependent samples and must be rerun from scratch whenever new samples are needed. Existing diffusion-based neural samplers amortize sampling but require orders of magnitude more target evaluations than PT to reach comparable sample quality. The paper asks how to integrate PT's temperature-exchange mechanism into neural samplers to combine the efficiency of PT with the amortization and independence of learned samplers.

## Input / Output

The method requires only access to the unnormalized density function p̃(x) (no normalization constant) and a geometrically spaced temperature schedule from a maximum temperature down to the target temperature T₁=1. It outputs a trained diffusion model (parameterized as a denoiser network following Karras et al. 2022) at the target temperature that generates independent samples via the reverse SDE or PF-ODE. For particle systems (LJ-55, Alanine Dipeptide), the network is an EGNN operating on Cartesian coordinates; for non-particle targets (MoG-40, MW-32), a 5-layer MLP is used.

## Method

PTSD introduces a temperature guidance mechanism that approximates the score at a lower temperature T₀ by a first-order Taylor expansion around a higher temperature T₁, using finite differences between two trained diffusion models at T₁ and T₂ (Eq. 11). This is structurally identical to classifier-free guidance, contrasting a 'better' lower-temperature model with a 'worse' higher-temperature one. The training pipeline proceeds sequentially: (1) run PT at the two highest temperatures to collect buffers and fit initial diffusion models; (2) generate approximate lower-temperature samples via the PF-ODE with temperature guidance; (3) refine these samples using truncated importance resampling (clipping weights at a quantile to control variance) and local PT between adjacent temperature pairs; (4) initialize the next lower-temperature model from the current one and fine-tune on the refined buffer. This 'bootstrapping across temperatures' replaces PT's sample swapping with weight sharing, transferring global distributional knowledge more efficiently while the IS and local PT correct the approximation error at each step.

## Results

On MoG-40 (d=2), PTSD achieves the best TVD (0.08), MMD (0.02), and W2 (1.93) among all neural samplers. On MW-32 (d=32), PTSD again leads with TVD 0.14, MMD 0.04, and W2 4.99. The headline efficiency gain is dramatic: PTSD requires only 1.0×10⁶ target evaluations on MoG-40 and 5.3×10⁶ on MW-32, compared to 10⁹–10¹⁰ for competing methods like iDEM, BNEM, and DDS, representing orders-of-magnitude improvement in target density evaluation efficiency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PTSD outperforms all six neural sampler baselines (FAB, CMCD, DDS, iDEM, BNEM, DiKL) on both MoG-40 and MW-32 across all three metrics (TVD, MMD, W2), while requiring 3–4 orders of magnitude fewer target evaluations (e.g., 1.0×10⁶ vs. iDEM's 5.0×10¹⁰ on MoG-40). On LJ-55, BNEM achieves the best TVD (0.18) and MMD (0.01), with PTSD trailing at 0.79 and 0.19 respectively, though PTSD is competitive on W2 (1.81 vs. BNEM's 1.76); the authors attribute BNEM's advantage to its explicit energy regression and target smoothing, which better handles LJ-55's inhibitory regions. Compared to the PT+DM baseline (running PT then fitting a diffusion model post hoc), PTSD matches or slightly exceeds sample quality on MoG-40 and MW-32 while using fewer energy evaluations (5.3×10⁶ vs. 8.0×10⁶ on MW-32), and on LJ-55 PTSD achieves comparable W2 (1.81 vs. 1.82) with fewer evaluations (1.5×10⁵ vs. 1.65×10⁵). The ablation (Table 4) confirms that removing temperature guidance degrades W2 from 4.99 to 24.59 on MW-32, and removing truncated IS degrades it to 5.84, validating both components.

## Relation to Prior Work

PTSD positions itself in the methodological gap between pure neural samplers (DDS, iDEM, BNEM, CMCD, FAB, DiKL) that self-bootstrap without data but are inefficient in target evaluations, and the PT+DM pipeline that relies entirely on MCMC-generated data. The core novelty is a 'temperature guidance' mechanism that directly adapts the classifier-free guidance formula (Ho & Salimans 2021; Karras et al. 2024) to the temperature axis: contrasting a lower-temperature diffusion model with a higher-temperature one to extrapolate scores to intermediate temperatures. This is combined with truncated importance resampling (Ionides 2008) and local parallel tempering refinement to correct approximation errors, creating a sequential pipeline that progressively descends through temperatures while fine-tuning diffusion models at each level.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The orders-of-magnitude reduction in target density evaluations (e.g., 5.3×10⁶ vs. 10⁹–10¹⁰ for baselines on MW-32) makes neural samplers practically competitive with PT for the first time, while retaining the amortization benefit of generating uncorrelated samples on demand. Key limitations include non-parallelizable sequential execution, sensitivity to temperature schedule and network quality, and still being slower than PT in wall-clock time. The paper opens a promising direction toward amortizing PTSD across multiple related targets (e.g., conformer generation), where the progressive temperature descent could share information across targets, potentially reducing the cost of sampling from many related distributions simultaneously.
