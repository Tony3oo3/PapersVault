## Problem

Deep Binary Neural Networks (DBNNs) deployed on resource-constrained edge devices are vulnerable to unpredictable environmental noise (e.g., patient movement artifacts, sensor noise) during inference, yet existing robustness methods target full-precision models on GPU servers, introduce exponential computational overhead, and fail to account for the specific vulnerabilities introduced by binary weights and scaling factors. No prior work provides a tight quantitative upper bound on noise perturbation for DBNNs or a low-overhead training strategy tailored to their binarized architecture.

## Input / Output

The method accepts heterogeneous inputs—32×32 RGB images (CIFAR-10/100), 224×224 images (ImageNet), brain tumor MRI scans, and 20 Hz tri-axial accelerometer time-series signals—and produces a single class label. Environmental noise is injected at inference time as random additive perturbations at randomly selected pixel/point locations, with dataset-specific epsilon ranges (e.g., ε∈[1/255, 4/255] for images, ε∈[0.4, 0.8] for bio-electrical signals, ε∈[0.05, 0.1] for MRI), simulating the unpredictable noise encountered on edge devices.

## Method

The paper identifies that DBNN robustness vulnerability originates from the interaction between binary weights and their associated scaling factors (α, β), then derives a closed-form upper bound on output discrepancy under L∞-norm input perturbations using the L_{1,∞}-norm of transposed binary weight matrices across all layers. The key training innovation is an auxiliary robustness loss L_p = δ·∏_j α‖W_b^j‖_{1,∞} added to the standard cross-entropy objective, where δ is a constraint coefficient balancing the two terms. This L_{1,∞}-norm constraint yields a provably tighter bound than the spectral-norm-based LCR method (via a quantitative ratio analysis showing the LCR bound grows exponentially with depth due to its γ>1 hyperparameter), while requiring only row/column max operations—no retention matrices or approximation steps—thus adding zero extra training overhead. The framework is architecture-agnostic, plug-and-play across ResNet18/34 backbones and diverse BNN variants (BiRealNet, ReActNet, DoReFa-Net, IRNet, AdaBin, CycleBNN), and is validated on five classification tasks with up to 5.4% robustness improvement.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with environmental noise (ε=[1/255, 4/255]), the proposed L1,∞-norm constraint improves robustness test accuracy across all four BNN baselines: IRNet+our reaches 93.64% (vs. 91.41% for IRNet, +2.23%) with ResNet18, and Bireal+our reaches 90.13% (vs. 87.80%, +2.33%) with ResNet34. On CIFAR-100, IRNet+our achieves 70.04% (vs. 65.24%, +4.80%) with ResNet18. On the Brain Tumor MRI dataset, CycleBNN+our achieves 85.46% (vs. 83.36%, +2.10%) with ResNet18, and on the bio-electricity series, AdaBinConv+our reaches 93.72% (vs. 93.13%, +0.59%). Training time on ImageNet is reduced by 16% compared to LCR (56:08 vs. 66:37 per epoch).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed L1,∞-norm constraint consistently outperforms all four standard BNN baselines (DoReFa-Net, BiRealNet, ReActNet, IRNet) on both CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100 under environmental noise, with gains ranging from +0.43% (IRNet+ours on CIFAR-100/ResNet34) to +4.80% (IRNet+ours on CIFAR-100/ResNet18). Against the SOTA robustness method LCR (Shang et al., 2022), the proposed approach achieves 58.23% vs. 57.27% on ImageNet (BiRealNet/ResNet18) and 90.13% vs. 88.48% on CIFAR-10 (BiRealNet/ResNet34), while also reducing training time by 16%. Compared to alternative norm constraints (L1, L2 from Li et al. 2019, L∞ from Kanai et al. 2020), the L1,∞ constraint yields the highest robustness accuracy on both CIFAR-10 (90.13% vs. 88.15–88.48%) and ImageNet (58.23% vs. 57.27–58.07%). On the Brain Tumor MRI dataset, CycleBNN+ours outperforms CycleBNN by +2.10% (ResNet18) and +6.45% (ResNet34), and on the bio-electricity series, all three binary convolution modules (IRConv, BirealConv, AdaBinConv) show improvements of +0.59% to +1.15% with the proposed constraint.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Lipschitz-continuity-based robustness framework (Gouk 2021, Miyato 2018) from full-precision CNNs to deep binary neural networks (DBNNs), departing from the closest prior work LCR (Shang 2022) which used spectral norm approximations via retention matrices to handle the extreme sparsity of binary weights. The key conceptual departure is identifying the scaling factors (α, β) in binarization as a distinct vulnerability source alongside binary weights, and proposing an L1,∞-norm constraint that avoids the 20% training overhead of LCR's approximation strategy. The paper also positions itself as orthogonal to formal verification of quantized networks, focusing instead on a training-time regularization that improves empirical robustness against unpredictable environmental noise rather than certifying a specific robustness radius.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

For practitioners deploying BNNs on edge devices in safety-critical settings (medical monitoring, bio-electrical signal classification), this provides a low-overhead training modification that modestly improves noise robustness without architectural changes. However, the practical gains are limited (typically 1–5% accuracy under noise), and the threat model is narrow: random environmental noise at fixed SNR, not adaptive adversarial attacks. Open questions include whether the L1,∞-norm constraint generalizes to other quantization schemes (ternary, 2-bit), whether it composes well with other regularization techniques, and whether the theoretical tightness advantage translates to meaningful certified robustness guarantees for deeper architectures beyond ResNet34.
