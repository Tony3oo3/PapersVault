## Problem

Multimodal large model development has historically followed isolated model-centric or data-centric paths, with no systematic methodology for co-developing data recipes and model architectures in a feedback-driven loop. The absence of a cost-effective sandbox infrastructure forces researchers into heuristic, single-perspective exploration, where improvements in one domain cannot directly inform the other, leading to suboptimal performance and wasted compute.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250349/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The sandbox consumes raw multimodal datasets (e.g., 1.2M image-text pairs for I2T, 1.2M video-text pairs for T2V, 10.9M pairs for CLIP pre-training) and applies 40+ Data-Juicer filtering operators to produce equal-size data pools (typically 40k–200k samples) segmented into low/mid/high statistical tiers. It outputs optimized data recipes (specific OP sequences with thresholds), trained reference models evaluated across 70+ benchmark metrics (VBench, MMBench, MME, TextVQA, DataComp's 40 subsets), and transferable insights validated at scales from 1B to 26B parameters. A key constraint is that all small-pool experiments maintain identical compute budgets to their random-sampled baselines, ensuring fair attribution of performance changes to data interventions.

## Method

The core contribution is a 'Probe-Analyze-Refine' workflow executed within a three-tier sandbox (end-to-end workflows → behavior hooks → factory classes) that orchestrates Data-Juicer's 100+ operators alongside SOTA training/evaluation libraries. Phase 1 (Probe) applies each of N filter OPs independently to create equal-size data pools, trains reference models on each pool plus a random baseline at matched compute, and ranks OPs by model performance deltas. Phase 2 (Analyze) combines top-ranked OPs sequentially into multi-OP recipes, testing whether combinations complement or counteract individual effects, with two strategies: progressive impact ordering and Pearson-correlation-based clustering. Phase 3 (Refine) constructs pyramid-shaped data pools from OP combinations to explore the quality-diversity-compute trade-off, comparing repetitive training on high-quality top pools against non-repetitive training with progressively added lower-quality pools at matched sample counts. The theoretical cost analysis shows that m small-pool experiments at ratio r of full compute yield total cost (1+mr)×T_full, which is feasible when r < 0.01 and m is in the dozens, versus M full-scale heuristic iterations.

## Results

On the VBench leaderboard (16 metrics, 946 prompts), Data-Juicer's T2V model achieves a Board Average of 82.53% and Uniform Average of 81.26%, topping the leaderboard as of Sep 23, 2024. This represents a +1.53% Board Avg and +2.59% Uniform Avg improvement over the base T2V-Turbo model (81.01% / 78.67%), achieved with at least 22× less compute than VEnhancer. For image-to-text generation, the optimal data recipe derived from small pools yields +2.12% average performance on MGM-2B while using only 159k distinct instances (×4 repetition) versus 1226k in the baseline — i.e., half the compute FLOPs. Scaling experiments with InternVL-2.0 show consistent performance advantages maintained as model scale increases from 1B to 26B parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Data-Juicer's T2V model (82.53% Board Avg, 81.26% Uniform Avg) outperforms all listed competitors on the VBench leaderboard. It beats Gen-3 (RunwayML) by +0.21% Board Avg and +1.62% Uniform Avg, VEnhancer by +0.56% Board Avg and +1.26% Uniform Avg (while using ≥22× less compute: 7.68α vs ≥167.3α EFLOPs), Kling by +0.68% Board Avg and +1.72% Uniform Avg, and CogVideoX-5B by +0.92% Board Avg and +1.36% Uniform Avg (with ~1500× less training data: 228k vs 35M samples). Over its own base model T2V-Turbo (81.01% / 78.67%), the data-driven improvements yield +1.53% Board Avg and +2.59% Uniform Avg gains. For image-to-text, the derived recipe achieves +2.12% average benchmark improvement over the full-data MGM-2B baseline while training on only half the distinct instances and half the total compute.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Data-Juicer, an open-source multimodal data processing system with 100+ operators, and positions itself as a missing intermediary layer between data-centric curation pipelines (e.g., DataComp, MetaClip) and model-centric training frameworks (e.g., Mini-Gemini, EasyAnimate, CLIP). It departs from prior work by treating data and model development as a coupled, feedback-driven optimization problem rather than two isolated tracks, introducing a 'Probe-Analyze-Refine' workflow that systematically links operator-level data statistics to downstream model benchmark metrics. The layered sandbox architecture (workflows → behavior hooks → capability factories) is novel in its integration of SOTA model training libraries with data processing tools under a unified, configuration-driven interface.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first open-source infrastructure for systematic data-model co-development, lowering the barrier for researchers to iterate on both data recipes and model configurations with controlled compute costs. Its finding that stacking more high-performing data operators does not monotonically improve model performance challenges a widespread heuristic in the field and suggests that recipe design requires empirical, feedback-driven exploration rather than intuitive accumulation. Open questions include the theoretical characterization of the trade-off between sandbox experiment cost and insight transferability to full-scale production, and whether the methodology generalizes to reinforcement fine-tuning and other emerging training paradigms. The 22× compute reduction relative to VEnhancer while achieving VBench Top-1 is practically significant, though the result relies on a specific distillation setup that may not transfer trivially to all architectures.
