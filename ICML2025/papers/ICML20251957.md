## Problem

Existing data-driven PDE discovery methods (SINDy, PDEFIND, WeakSINDy) are limited to linear combinations of fixed basis functions, struggle with complex nonlinear PDEs, and degrade sharply under noise. No prior approach combines a differentiable PDE solver with flexible neural network parameterization to discover governing equations that go beyond generalized linear forms while remaining robust to noisy spatiotemporal data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251957/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes spatiotemporal data u_data(t, x) sampled on a Cartesian grid (e.g., 32×32×32 mini-batches for 2D spatial + time dimensions) and produces a discovered PDE expression built from elementary basis functions (polynomials up to degree 3–4) with learned scalar coefficients, along with learned Dirichlet boundary conditions and grid step sizes. The PDE expression inputs are transformed through a neural network before being fed into the basis functions, so the final output is a concise, interpretable PDE in the original data coordinates rather than in the network's hidden space.

## Method

The core contribution is NeuRLP-PDE, a differentiable solver that reduces linear PDE solution to a relaxed constrained optimization problem with three constraint types: PDE equation constraints at every grid point, Taylor-expansion smoothness constraints linking adjacent grid values, and initial/boundary conditions. The resulting large sparse linear system is solved by a GPU-parallel, batch-parallel FGMRES iterative solver preconditioned with a multigrid V-cycle (Gauss-Seidel relaxation, linear interpolation restriction/prolongation), keeping memory bounded even for 128×128 grids. This solver is embedded as a module in a discovery architecture where a mechanistic encoder (10-layer 2D ResNet) parameterizes the PDE input, and a two-layer MLP generates polynomial basis coefficients; an L1 sparsity loss enforces conciseness while L1 data-fidelity and L1 consistency losses (between the network-transformed input and raw data) ensure the discovered equation is correct in the original coordinates. Because derivatives are computed internally by the solver rather than applied directly to data, the method avoids amplifying noise through finite differences and can represent arbitrary differentiable expressions (e.g., real exponents in the porous medium equation) that fixed-basis methods cannot.

## Results

On the harder 2D Ginzburg-Landau reaction-diffusion dataset (256×256 spatial grid), MechNN-PDE maintains high TPR up to 40% noise and retains TPR of 0.6 at 80% noise, while both PDEFIND and WeakSINDy fail to recover any significant non-zero terms even on clean data. On the easier reaction-diffusion case (64×64 grid), all three methods achieve TPR = 1 on clean data, and MechNN-PDE matches WeakSINDy in TPR and E∞ error up to 80% noise. For the incompressible Navier-Stokes equation (ν = 0.001), MechNN-PDE recovers the governing equation while WeakSINDy is unable to discover the system. On 1D inviscid Burger's equation, MechNN-PDE achieves TPR = 1 with E∞ = 0.043 (clean) and 0.002 (10% noise), comparable to WeakSINDy (E∞ = 0.001 and 0.012 respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares MechNN-PDE against PDEFIND (Rudy et al., 2017) and WeakSINDy (Reinbold et al., 2020) on 1D and 2D PDE discovery tasks. On the harder 2D reaction-diffusion dataset, MechNN-PDE decisively outperforms both baselines: PDEFIND and WeakSINDy cannot identify any correct terms even on noise-free data (TPR ≈ 0), while MechNN-PDE maintains TPR near 1 up to 40% noise. On the easier 2D reaction-diffusion case, MechNN-PDE ties with WeakSINDy in TPR and E∞ across most noise levels, with WeakSINDy showing slightly better E∞ at 80% noise; PDEFIND degrades sharply, dropping to TPR 0–0.2. For the incompressible Navier-Stokes equation, MechNN-PDE successfully recovers the governing PDE while WeakSINDy fails entirely. On 1D Burger's equations, both methods are on par, with WeakSINDy achieving marginally lower E∞ on clean data (0.001 vs. 0.043) but higher error at 10% noise (0.012 vs. 0.002). Overall, MechNN-PDE's advantage is most pronounced on complex 2D nonlinear PDEs where basis-function-based methods struggle.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Mechanistic Neural Networks (MechNN, Pervez et al. 2024) from ODE discovery to PDE discovery, developing a new differentiable PDE solver (NeuRLP-PDE) that reduces PDE solution to a relaxed constrained optimization following Young (1961) and differentiates through it using the OptNet framework (Amos & Kolter, 2017). The solver-in-network architecture contrasts with physics-informed neural networks (which supervise via physics-aware losses) and universal differential equations (which embed networks inside classical solvers). For PDE discovery specifically, the paper positions against sparse identification methods—PDEFIND and WeakSINDy—which are limited to linear combinations of fixed basis functions and struggle with noise and complex nonlinear dynamics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ability to discover PDEs beyond linear combinations of fixed basis functions (demonstrated on the porous medium equation with a real exponent) and to handle complex 2D dynamics where standard sparse identification baselines fail entirely on clean data (harder reaction-diffusion, low-viscosity Navier-Stokes) broadens the class of discoverable equations. The sparse multigrid-preconditioned differentiable solver is a practical contribution enabling GPU-parallel batch solving of PDEs at resolutions where dense methods are infeasible. Key open questions include scalability to higher spatial dimensions, the identifiability conditions under which discovery is possible, and whether the accuracy-speed trade-off of the current multigrid solver can be improved.
