## Problem

Mainstream CNN and Transformer pansharpening methods rigidly model pixel relationships on regular Euclidean grids, which is suboptimal for remote sensing scenes containing irregular ground objects. While graph structures offer greater topological flexibility, no prior work has constructed a customized graph that explicitly encodes pansharpening-specific spatial-spectral relationship priors (PAN spatial, LR-MS intra-spectral, and cross-spectral relationships) and learns a unified representation across them.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251691/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a panchromatic image (128×128×1) and a low-resolution multispectral image (32×32×4) and produces a high-resolution multispectral image (128×128×4). Both inputs are divided into N overlapping patches; PAN patches become one node type while each LR-MS patch is further split into four spectral-band feature vectors forming a second node type, yielding a graph of 5n nodes. Edge construction uses k-nearest-neighbor with cosine similarity to form three distinct edge types, and the final node representations are decoded back into the spatial image domain.

## Method

HetSSNet constructs an attributed multiplex heterogeneous graph (HetSS-Graph) with two node types and three edge types that explicitly encode pansharpening-specific relationship priors. A basic relationship pattern generation module decouples the graph by edge type and applies XNOR and bitwise-AND operations with logical variables to enumerate all 2³−1 = 7 basic spatial-spectral relationship pattern matrices. A relationship pattern aggregation module then learns node representations from two perspectives: local-wise aggregation weights the pattern matrices with learnable coefficients and feeds the result into a multi-layer GCN, while global-wise aggregation computes inter-node similarity from concatenated pattern-degree vectors and applies a second GCN. A contrastive loss aligns the local and global node representations, and the final output is the average of both, trained jointly with an L₁ reconstruction loss.

## Results

HetSSNet achieves the best or near-best results across all five metrics (PSNR, SSIM, SAM, ERGAS, SCC) on all three datasets. On GaoFen-2, it reaches PSNR 49.541, SSIM 0.994, SAM 0.015, ERGAS 0.955, and SCC 0.990, outperforming the strongest competitor WINet (PSNR 49.405) by 0.136 dB. On WorldView-3, HetSSNet attains PSNR 40.623 and SSIM 0.905, surpassing LGTEUN (PSNR 40.124) and MDCUN (PSNR 39.929). On QuickBird, it achieves the lowest SAM (0.029) and highest SSIM (0.931), with a notable margin over CNN/Transformer-based methods that struggle with irregular ground objects.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HetSSNet outperforms all 17 baselines on nearly every metric across all three datasets. Against the strongest CNN/Transformer-based competitors, it leads LGTEUN by 0.5 dB PSNR on WorldView-3 (40.623 vs. 40.124) and WINet by 0.14 dB PSNR on GaoFen-2 (49.541 vs. 49.405), while also achieving the lowest SAM (0.015 vs. 0.016) and highest SCC (0.990 vs. 0.977). The graph-based GPCNet, which uses two independent GCNs in Euclidean space, lags significantly behind HetSSNet on QuickBird (PSNR 33.972 vs. 37.228) and GaoFen-2 (PSNR 40.926 vs. 49.541), confirming that rigid Euclidean graph modeling breaks the spatial-spectral balance. CNN and Transformer methods (DCFNet, HyperTransformer) show particularly poor QuickBird results (PSNR ~34.4–34.7), attributed to their inability to handle irregular ground objects. Traditional methods (SFIM, BROVEY, GS) are uniformly 5–12 dB below the best deep learning methods in PSNR, validating the superiority of learned representations. On full-resolution real-world scenes (no-reference metrics), HetSSNet also achieves the best QNR (0.860 on GaoFen-2) and lowest D_s (0.107), confirming generalization beyond the reduced-resolution training protocol.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant CNN and Transformer paradigms for pansharpening (e.g., DCFNet, MDCUN, HyperTransformer, CTINN), which rigidly model pixel relationships on regular Euclidean grids, by instead constructing a spatial-spectral heterogeneous graph in non-Euclidean space. It also differentiates itself from GPCNet (Yan et al., 2022), the closest prior graph-based pansharpening method, which applies standard GCNs to model spatial and spectral relationships independently in Euclidean space. The GCN layer design follows the simplified formulation from MHGCN (Yu et al., 2022), while the multiplex heterogeneous graph structure and the basic relationship pattern generation module (extracting all 2^R−1 edge-type combinations) are novel contributions specific to pansharpening.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first systematic attempt to model pansharpening-specific relationship priors (PAN spatial, LR-MS intra-spectral, cross-spectral) within a unified non-Euclidean graph, supported by empirical distribution analyses (Appendix B) that justify the three edge types. However, the performance gains over strong baselines like WINet and MDCUN are modest (≈0.1–0.3 dB PSNR), and the method is limited to 4-band MS imagery and three standard datasets. Open questions include scalability to hyperspectral (many-band) settings, the fixed k-NN graph construction's sensitivity to scene content, and whether the non-Euclidean framing offers advantages beyond what well-designed CNN/Transformer architectures already capture.
