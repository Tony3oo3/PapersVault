## Problem

VLM-based multi-agent systems are vulnerable to infectious jailbreak attacks where a single compromised agent propagates adversarial examples through RAG-based memory retrieval, potentially compromising the entire system. Existing defenses target individual models and require deployment on every agent, which is infeasible for large-scale systems where the defender may only control a small subset of agents. No mechanism exists to provide system-wide immunity by modifying only a few edge agents.

## Input / Output

The COWPOX agent receives adversarial image samples (virus) passed via pairwise chat, the corresponding VLM output text, and the agent's chat history as input. It produces a cure sample — a benign image of the same format as the virus that scores higher than the virus in the RAG retrieval process — and a binary malicious/benign detection flag from the output analysis module. The cure sample is then stored in the agent's album and naturally propagates through normal chat, replacing the virus in other agents' memory banks.

## Method

COWPOX consists of two modules deployed on a small set of defender-controlled agents: an Output Analysis Module that uses an LLM inspector with a diverse template pool to flag suspicious responses, and a Cure Generation Module that produces a neutralizing sample. Two cure strategies are proposed: Strategy 1 directly optimizes the virus sample to maximize its RAG score for malicious queries (exploiting catastrophic forgetting to erase the adversarial target), while Strategy 2 optimizes a benign base image to simultaneously maximize RAG score for malicious queries and preserve RAG score for benign queries. The core insight is converting the virus's positive RAG feedback loop into a negative one: the cure scores slightly higher than the virus, so RAG retrieves the cure instead, halting propagation and enabling recovery. A new three-state (susceptible/infected/cured) transmission model is derived, and a sufficient condition (ε ≥ η, i.e., the cure spreads at least as fast as the virus) is proven to guarantee full system recovery given enough chat rounds.

## Results

With only 4 COWPOX agents (~3% of the 128-agent system), both Strategy 1 and Strategy 2 reduce the current infectious rate from 93–100% (no defense) to 0–3% by round 50, and cap the cumulative infectious rate at 80–92% (vs. 100% without defense). The peak current infectious rate drops from ~99% to 55–78%, and the system reaches a current rate below 10% by round 35–40, whereas the undefended system never recovers. Strategy 1 (optimizing the virus sample directly) consistently outperforms Strategy 2 (optimizing a benign base sample) by 1–5 percentage points on cumulative infection and by 1–3 rounds on recovery time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against other defense methods; the primary comparison is COWPOX (Strategy 1 and Strategy 2) versus no defense (W/O.) under identical attack conditions. Across all four attack configurations (border h=6, h=8; pixel ε=8/255, ε=16/255), COWPOX reduces the steady-state current infectious rate from 93–100% to 0–3%, a reduction of over 90 percentage points. The cumulative infectious rate is capped at 80–92% with COWPOX versus 100% without, meaning roughly 8–20% of agents are never infected thanks to the immune barrier formed by the cure sample. Strategy 1 marginally outperforms Strategy 2 on most metrics (e.g., 90.63% vs. 89.84% cumulative at round 32 for border h=6; 55.47% vs. 54.69% peak current rate for pixel ε=8/255), attributed to the virus sample already having a high RAG score that requires fewer optimization epochs to suppress. The output analysis module (the detection component) achieves 89.1% accuracy with 3-turn evaluation (FPR 7.9%, FNR 3.0%), which the authors note is sufficient because COWPOX agents encounter the virus multiple times over 64 rounds.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper is a direct response to AgentSmith (Gu et al., 2024), which introduced the first infectious jailbreak attack against VLM-based multi-agent systems by crafting adversarial examples that exploit the RAG retrieval mechanism to propagate malicious content across agents. While prior defenses (Bianchi et al., 2024; Bai et al., 2022; Deng et al.) target individual model safety through alignment or prompt filtering, COWPOX departs by operating at the system level: it inverts the attacker's own adversarial optimization to generate a 'cure' sample that outcompetes the virus in RAG scoring, requiring deployment on only a small subset of agents. The theoretical analysis extends the SIR-like transmission model from Gu et al. (2024) with a new cured-agent compartment, yielding a provable recovery condition (ε ≥ η) that has no analogue in single-agent adversarial defense literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As multi-agent VLM systems are deployed in embodied AI, virtual assistants, and software development pipelines, the demonstrated ability of a single compromised agent to infect millions of others in a few rounds represents a critical and previously unaddressed security gap. COWPOX provides the first principled, theoretically grounded countermeasure, but its applicability is currently confined to the specific AgentSmith experimental environment (LLaVA-1.5 + CLIP RAG, pairwise chat protocol). Open questions include generalization to heterogeneous agent architectures with different operational procedures, resistance to more sophisticated adaptive attacks that jointly optimize RAG score and malicious output, and whether the cure-induced RAG dominance causes long-term degradation of conversational diversity in production systems.
