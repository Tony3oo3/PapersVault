## Problem

The empirical Gram matrices of natural datasets exhibit power-law eigenvalue decay, but the origin of this behavior, its quantitative link to feature correlations, and whether these matrices obey universal random matrix statistics were entirely unknown. This gap blocks rigorous analytical treatment of real-world data in neural network theory, which has relied on uncorrelated Gaussian assumptions that miss the structured long-range correlations present in natural images.

## Input / Output

The method consumes centered and normalized image datasets (MNIST, FashionMNIST, CIFAR-10, Tiny-ImageNet, CelebA downsampled to 109×89 grayscale) as a data matrix X ∈ R^{d×M}, and for synthetic baselines constructs Correlated Gaussian Datasets (CGDs) parameterized by a Toeplitz covariance T_ij = I_ij + c|i-j|^α with tunable α and c. Beyond the eigenvalue spectrum and fitted exponent α, the outputs include full RMT diagnostics (unfolded level spacing, r-statistics, spectral form factor), the generalized Marčenko-Pastur spectral density, Shannon entropy of the bulk, and the convergence threshold M_crit at which the Gram matrix becomes ergodic.

## Method

The paper analogizes datasets to physical systems and applies quantum-chaos RMT diagnostics to the empirical Gram matrix Σ_M = XX^T/M. The central construction is the Correlated Gaussian Dataset (CGD): a Wishart matrix whose population covariance is a Toeplitz matrix with power-law correlations T_ij = I_ij + c|i-j|^α, where α controls correlation strength and c sets the scale. Via the discrete Laplace transform of the Toeplitz structure, they derive the bulk spectrum λ_i ∝ i^{-1-α}, showing that α directly encodes the correlation decay/growth in the underlying data. They verify GOE universality across all dataset types using level spacing distributions, r-statistics (⟨r⟩ ≈ 0.536), the spectral form factor, and higher-order r^(n) statistics, and demonstrate that the effective convergence threshold M_crit ~ d—far below typical dataset sizes—coincides with entropy saturation and power-law exponent convergence, establishing the CGD as a faithful, analytically tractable proxy for real-world Gram matrices.

## Results

Across all real-world vision datasets (MNIST, FashionMNIST, CIFAR-10, Tiny-ImageNet, CelebA, ImageNet), the bulk eigenvalues of the empirical Gram matrix follow a power-law λ_i ∝ i^{-(1+α)} with α ≤ 1/2, while uncorrelated Gaussian data (UGD) exhibits α ≈ -1 (no power-law). All dataset types—real-world, correlated Gaussian (CGD), and UGD—converge to the GOE universality class, with r-statistics matching the theoretical value ⟨r⟩_GOE = 4 − 2√3 ≈ 0.536 at M = 50,000 samples. The effective convergence threshold M_crit scales as ~d (number of features), far below typical training set sizes, and the Shannon entropy of the eigenvalue spectrum correlates with both the power-law exponent and the RMT convergence, being substantially lower for strongly correlated data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's central comparison is between three data models: real-world image datasets, Correlated Gaussian Datasets (CGDs) with Toeplitz covariance T_ij = I_ij + c|i−j|^α, and Uncorrelated Gaussian Data (UGD). The CGD model successfully reproduces both the power-law bulk spectrum (matching α to within fitting precision) and the full GOE local statistics (level spacing, r-statistics, SFF) of real-world data, as quantified by low KL-divergence between spectral densities. UGD, while also falling in the GOE universality class for local statistics, fails to capture the power-law eigenvalue decay (its spectral density follows the standard Marčenko-Pastur law rather than a power-law), making it an inadequate proxy for natural data correlations. Higher-order r^{(n)} statistics reveal that deviations from GOE emerge only at n ≥ 6, indicating that the CGD approximation is accurate up to moderate correlation lengths. The convergence analysis shows that both real-world and CGD data reach the RMT regime at M_crit ~ d, with the entropy, α-exponent, and r-statistics all saturating at the same sample size, confirming the CGD as a faithful analytical surrogate for real-world Gram matrices.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the neural scaling laws program initiated by Kaplan et al. (2020) and Maloney et al. (2022), which identified power-law eigenvalue decay in data covariance matrices as a key ingredient in scaling behavior. It draws on the RMT toolkit from quantum chaos—level spacing distributions, r-statistics, and spectral form factors—previously applied to financial markets and nuclear physics, and repurposes these diagnostics for empirical Gram matrices of image datasets. The correlated Gaussian dataset (CGD) model builds on the Toeplitz matrix framework of Gray (2006) and the generalized Marčenko-Pastur law of Silverstein and Bai (1995), providing a simple Wishart ensemble whose bulk statistics match real-world data. The paper departs from prior RMT-in-ML work (Pennington & Worah, Liao & Mahoney) by focusing on the full spectral statistics and universality class rather than just spectral density, and by explicitly connecting convergence thresholds to entropy and chaos diagnostics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By establishing that real-world data Gram matrices fall into the GOE universality class and can be approximated by a simple Wishart matrix with a single-parameter Toeplitz covariance, the paper provides an analytically tractable model that could replace ad hoc Gaussian assumptions in neural scaling law derivations. The finding that the RMT regime is reached at M_crit ~ d—far below typical training set sizes—has practical implications for understanding when data statistics stabilize during training. Open questions include the origin of the observed chaotic (GOE) statistics in natural data, the role of eigenvector localization in learning tasks, and whether the framework extends to language, audio, or other modalities. The teacher-student toy example in Appendix F demonstrates concrete utility but remains a linear model, leaving the interplay between bulk spectrum, outlier eigenvalues, and eigenvector structure in nonlinear networks as a significant open problem.
