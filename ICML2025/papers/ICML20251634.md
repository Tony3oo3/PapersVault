## Problem

Diffusion-based planners achieve state-of-the-art performance in offline reinforcement learning but require 0.1–1+ seconds per decision even on a GPU, making them impractical for real-time deployment. The paper asks whether the cognitive science principle of habitization—where slow, deliberate goal-directed behavior is gradually converted into fast, automatic habitual behavior through practice—can be formalized to transform a powerful but slow diffusion planner into a lightweight, high-frequency decision-making model without sacrificing performance.

## Input / Output

At inference, the habitized model (Habitual Inference) takes only the current proprioceptive state as input and produces a single action by sampling N=5 latent candidates from a learned prior distribution, decoding each into an action, and selecting the best via a learned critic. All components are lightweight 3-layer MLPs with 256 hidden dimensions, enabling 800–1500 Hz decision frequency on a laptop CPU (Apple M2 Max), orders of magnitude faster than the source diffusion planner. During training, the posterior encoder additionally consumes the diffusion planner's selected action a* to encode goal-directed behavior, but this is discarded at inference time.

## Method

Habi frames the diffusion planner's policy as a posterior distribution q(z|s, a*) and a lightweight habitual policy as a prior p(z|s) over a shared 256-dim latent variable z, then trains them by maximizing the ELBO: a reconstruction loss (decoder recovers the planner's action from z^q) plus an adaptive β_KL-weighted KL divergence that aligns the prior's decision space with the posterior's. A separate critic MLP(z, a) is trained on the full batch of candidate actions (including suboptimal ones) to score decision quality, enabling best-of-N selection at inference. The adaptive β_KL mechanism (inspired by SAC) dynamically adjusts the KL weight during training to balance reconstruction fidelity against distributional alignment without task-specific tuning. At inference, only the prior encoder, decoder, and critic are needed—no diffusion sampling loop—yielding a single forward pass per candidate that achieves comparable or superior performance to the source diffusion planner at 100–1000× higher frequency.

## Results

On the D4RL benchmark, Habitual Inference (HI) achieves an average performance of 89.2 on MuJoCo locomotion tasks, 82.3 on Franka Kitchen, 80.3 on AntMaze, and 164.5 on Maze2D, matching or exceeding the strongest diffusion planners (DV: 83.5, 83.8, 83.2, 163.6) and diffusion policies (DQL: 90.5, 60.3, 80.5, 159.8). Critically, HI runs at 1329.7 Hz on MuJoCo, 385.7 Hz on Kitchen, 908.3 Hz on AntMaze, and 1532.6 Hz on Maze2D using only a laptop CPU (Apple M2), representing a 50–100× speedup over diffusion planners (0.03–27 Hz) and a 5–10× speedup over the fastest accelerated baselines (DL-R2: 206.6 Hz, DTQL: 142.7 Hz on MuJoCo).

## Baselines & Comparisons

HI outperforms all diffusion planners (Diffuser 77.5, AdaptDiffuser 83.3, DD 81.8, DV 83.5) on MuJoCo average performance (89.2) while running 50–100× faster (1329.7 Hz vs. 0.03–16.7 Hz). Against diffusion policies, HI beats IDQL (84.1) and DQL (90.5) on Kitchen (82.3 vs. 66.6/60.3) and AntMaze (80.3 vs. 59.8/80.5), though DQL retains a slight edge on MuJoCo (90.5 vs. 89.2). Among accelerated methods, HI surpasses DiffuserLite (DL-R1: 84.0, DL-R2: 83.8) and DTQL (88.7) on MuJoCo average, and leads on Kitchen (82.3 vs. 52.0/45.2/67.3) and AntMaze (80.3 vs. 54.7/67.1/67.0). On Maze2D, HI (164.5) edges out DV (163.6) and DQL (159.8). The one notable loss is Hopper-Medium-Expert, where HI (92.4) trails DQL (111.4) and DD (111.8), though this is a single task among 24 evaluated. In Table 5, HI (92.7 shared-task avg) is competitive with flow-based FTB (85.5) and consistency-based CPQL (92.8), confirming it is not inferior to orthogonal acceleration approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

Habi adapts the Bayesian behavior framework of Han et al. (2024), which modeled the habitual/goal-directed interplay via ELBO in online RL on simple T-maze tasks, to the offline RL setting with state-of-the-art diffusion planners as the goal-directed (posterior) component. The variational inference machinery draws directly from VAE/β-VAE (Kingma & Welling 2014; Higgins et al. 2017), with an adaptive β-KL schedule borrowed from SAC (Haarnoja et al. 2018). Unlike prior acceleration approaches (DiffuserLite, DTQL, consistency policies) that modify the diffusion sampling process itself, Habi sidesteps the diffusion model entirely at inference time, training a lightweight MLP-based prior that approximates the planner's final decisions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The orders-of-magnitude speedup (800–1500+ Hz on a laptop CPU vs. 0.03–7.5 Hz for diffusion planners) while matching or exceeding planner performance makes probabilistic diffusion-based decision-making practically viable for real-time embodied AI applications. The framework's generality—applicable to any pretrained diffusion planner or policy without architectural modification—lowers the barrier to adoption. Key limitations include restriction to state-based (proprioceptive) offline RL on D4RL; open questions include extension to vision-based tasks, online RL, and real-world robotic deployment where the critic's Q-values may be harder to obtain.
