## Problem

Conventional pipelines for obtaining quantized fine-tuned LLMs apply supervised fine-tuning (SFT) first and then post-training quantization (PTQ), which fails to exploit the synergy between the two processes and often degrades performance severely at 4-bit. Quantization-aware training (QAT) avoids this but requires retraining on a large corpus, and existing QAT methods struggle with 4-bit activation quantization due to weight and activation outliers. The paper asks whether one can perform quantization-aware SFT in a single training phase that simultaneously adapts the model to a downstream task and selects effective rotation configurations to suppress outliers.

## Input / Output

The method takes pre-trained LLM weights (Pythia, Qwen2.5, or Llama 3.1) and a curated SFT dataset (e.g., Reddit TL;DR or Tulu 3 mixture) as input, and produces a 4-bit quantized fine-tuned model with per-layer rotation matrix assignments (each layer independently set to either identity or a random Walsh-Hadamard matrix). Quantization uses asymmetric uniform quantization with per-token groups for activations and per-channel groups for weights, applied to weights, activations, and KV caches simultaneously (W4A4KV4). The rotation matrices are binary (Walsh-Hadamard with random sign flips), enabling efficient GPU implementation via fast Hadamard CUDA kernels with negligible overhead.

## Method

RoSTE formulates QA-SFT as a bilevel optimization problem: the upper level optimizes weight matrices via STE-based gradient descent on the SFT loss of the quantized-and-rotated model, while the lower level selects per-layer rotation matrices from {I, H} to minimize a surrogate quantization error (sum of weight and activation quantization errors over a calibration set). The algorithm alternates between T steps of STE-based QAT (with rotation-aware gradients) and a low-complexity layer-wise combinatorial search over rotation configurations, updated lazily after each outer iteration. A key theoretical result (Theorem 4.3) shows that the prediction error of the converged quantized model is directly proportional to the weight quantization error, which justifies using quantization error as the surrogate loss for rotation selection. Proposition 4.4 further shows that random Walsh-Hadamard rotation reduces the worst-case quantization error from O(d·max_i w_i²) to O(‖w‖²), explaining why rotation is effective for outlier-prone weight vectors.

## Results

On the TL;DR summarization task (Exp.1), RoSTE achieves 23.66 average ROUGE on Pythia 6.9B and 25.10 on Qwen2.5 7B under W4A4KV4 quantization, recovering 95.7% and 97.0% of the full-precision SFT performance (24.72 and 25.87 respectively). On the Tulu 3 multi-task benchmark (Exp.2), RoSTE achieves 31.69 average accuracy on Llama 3.1 8B under W4A4KV4, a +2.56 improvement over the best PTQ baseline (SpinQuant at 29.13). The ablation in Table 3 confirms that adaptive rotation (23.07) outperforms both no rotation (22.37) and complete rotation (13.09) on Pythia 1B.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RoSTE consistently outperforms all PTQ baselines by large margins: on Pythia 6.9B W4A4KV4, it beats SpinQuant (6.22) by +17.44 ROUGE, QuaRot (7.46) by +16.20, GPTQ (5.73) by +17.93, and RTN (5.14) by +18.52. Against the closest QAT baseline STE (no rotation), RoSTE gains +3.01 ROUGE on Pythia 6.9B, +1.78 on Qwen2.5 7B, and +14.55 average accuracy on Llama 3.1 8B, demonstrating that adaptive Walsh-Hadamard rotation is critical for 4-bit activation quantization. On Llama 3.1 8B (Exp.2), RoSTE (31.69) surpasses SpinQuant (29.13) by +2.56 and QuaRot (28.46) by +3.23, while STE without rotation collapses to 17.14. In additional experiments (Tables 7–8), RoSTE also outperforms LLM-QAT (13.19 on Pythia 6.9B W4A4KV4), QLoRA r=64 (20.20), and DuQuant (which degrades to 0.00 on Qwen2.5 7B W4A4KV4), confirming that the rotation-aware QAT approach is superior to both data-free QAT and low-rank adaptation strategies under aggressive 4-bit quantization.

## Relation to Prior Work

RoSTE sits at the intersection of three established threads: rotation-based post-training quantization (QuaRot, SpinQuant, QuIP#), quantization-aware training via straight-through estimators (LLM-QAT), and supervised fine-tuning of LLMs. The key departure is unifying these into a single QA-SFT phase rather than the conventional two-step pipeline (SFT followed by PTQ), and formulating the joint optimization of weights and rotation matrices as a bilevel problem where the lower level selects per-layer rotation (Hadamard vs. identity) based on quantization error. The theoretical analysis extends prior STE convergence results by showing the prediction error of the quantized model is directly governed by the weight quantization error, providing a principled justification for the rotation-based surrogate loss.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RoSTE demonstrates that 4-bit quantized fine-tuned LLMs can recover most of the full-precision SFT performance (within ~1 ROUGE point on summarization) at a training cost comparable to standard SFT, making low-bit deployment of task-specific models practical. The ablation revealing that indiscriminate rotation across all layers severely degrades performance (−9.98 ROUGE) while adaptive per-layer selection is critical challenges the assumption that rotation is universally beneficial. Open questions include whether the one-shot rotation configuration (K=1) is sufficient for larger datasets or more aggressive bit-widths, whether the bilevel formulation can be tightened beyond the current heuristic binary search, and whether the simplified linear-theory analysis extends to the full transformer architecture.
