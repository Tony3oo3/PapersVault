## Problem

Existing generative INRs model the joint distribution of flattened INR parameters or a single flat latent, ignoring the inherent hierarchical structure of INR layers where earlier layers capture coarse features and later layers refine fine details. This misalignment entangles data semantics, limits controllability over generated content, and impairs generalizability to unseen data. The paper asks: how can we align INR's layer-wise expanded representational capacity with the hierarchical abstraction of data semantics to enable precise, layer-specific control during generation?

## Input / Output

At inference, the HCDM takes Gaussian noise and iteratively samples layer-wise latents h¹ through h^L (each of dimension H) via a conditional chain, where each layer's sampling is conditioned on a low-dimensional binary vector derived from all preceding latents. The resulting stacked latent vector modulates the LoE INR, which maps input coordinates (pixel positions for 64×64 images, 3D grid coordinates for 64³ voxels, or time-joint indices for 200×165 motion grids) to signal values. For hierarchical control, users can fix early-layer latents to lock coarse semantics (e.g., face shape, object category) while the remaining layers are still sampled to vary finer details (e.g., expression, texture).

## Method

CHINR is a two-stage framework. In Stage-1, each INR layer is restructured as a Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) layer with K shared expert weights and a data-specific latent that, through a gating module, computes a convex combination of experts; cascading L such layers forms a Layers-of-Experts (LoE) network trained via meta-learning (CAVIA-style interleaved updates) or auto-decoding. In Stage-2, a Hierarchical Conditional Diffusion Model (HCDM) factorizes the latent distribution as p(h¹)∏p(h^l|h^{<l}), where each conditional p(h^l|h^{<l}) is learned by a UNet-based denoiser conditioned on a binary vector formed by compressing and binarizing the concatenated preceding latents. The binary condition is deliberately low-dimensional to prevent the model from memorizing one-to-one mappings while preserving inter-layer dependencies. This design aligns INR's progressive frequency-basis expansion (analyzed via Bessel-function reformulation of SIREN) with data semantic hierarchy, enabling coarse-to-fine generation where early-layer routing determines global structure and later-layer routing controls fine details.

## Results

CHINR achieves the best reconstruction PSNR across all three main datasets: 34.9 dB on CelebA-HQ, 22.3 dB on ShapeNet, and 26.3 dB on SRN-Cars. For generation quality, it matches mNIF's FID of 13.4 on CelebA-HQ while improving to 77.8 on SRN-Cars (vs. 79.3 for mNIF), and achieves the highest Coverage of 0.441 on ShapeNet. On the AMASS motion dataset, CHINR reduces reconstruction MSE to 0.011 versus mNIF's 0.015. The paper further shows CHINR generalizes better than mNIF, with generated samples having a mean minimum L2 distance of 15.971 to training images (vs. 6.243 for mNIF), indicating less memorization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CHINR outperforms all four baselines (Functa, GEM, GASP, mNIF) on reconstruction PSNR across every dataset, with the largest gains over Functa (e.g., +8.7 dB on CelebA-HQ) and GASP (e.g., +5.6 dB on CelebA-HQ). Against the strongest baseline mNIF, CHINR improves PSNR by 0.4 dB on CelebA-HQ, 0.9 dB on ShapeNet, and 0.3 dB on SRN-Cars, while also achieving higher SSIM and Coverage. On generation FID, CHINR ties mNIF on CelebA-HQ (13.4) but improves on SRN-Cars (77.8 vs. 79.3); the authors attribute mNIF's competitive FID to memorization, evidenced by a much lower mean L2 distance to training images (6.243 vs. 15.971). On ShapeNet, CHINR achieves the best Coverage (0.441 vs. 0.435 for mNIF) and Accuracy (0.988 vs. 0.975). On AMASS, CHINR reduces motion reconstruction MSE by 27% over mNIF (0.011 vs. 0.015).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the generative INR line of work (Functa, GASP, mNIF) that models the distribution of INR parameters via latent vectors, but departs from the flat joint-distribution formulation by explicitly modeling conditional dependencies across layers. The LoE architecture draws on Mixture-of-Experts to give each INR layer a shared expert bank plus a data-specific latent, while the HCDM builds on standard DDPM with a UNet denoiser but introduces a hierarchical conditional chain with binary-condition compression. The meta-learning training procedure follows CAVIA, and the SIREN-based INR with its Fourier/Bessel frequency analysis provides the theoretical motivation for why layers correspond to semantic granularity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CHINR is the first framework to offer layer-wise, coarse-to-fine control over generated content in the INR setting, which is practically valuable for applications requiring semantic editing (e.g., changing facial expression without altering face shape). The finding that mNIF substantially memorizes training data (min L2 distance 6.2 vs 16.0) while CHINR generalizes better highlights a real limitation of flat-latent generative INRs. Key open questions include scalability to larger and more diverse datasets (the shared expert network may not capture all patterns), whether the learned layer-to-semantic mapping is stable across domains, and how to guide the hierarchy with explicit attribute supervision for more interpretable control.
