## Problem

Existing diffusion and flow-based protein backbone generators suffer from a fundamental trade-off: achieving high designability (the fraction of generated backbones that can be realized by folding a corresponding amino acid sequence) requires many sampling steps, leading to long inference times (e.g., 66–113 s for a 300-residue backbone). Moreover, all baselines degrade severely on long-chain generation (N > 500), and matrix-based rotation interpolation introduces numerical instability near extreme rotation angles that propagates during training.

## Input / Output

The method takes, for each of N residues, a 3D Gaussian noise vector (translation) and a random SO(3) rotation sampled from the isotropic Gaussian on SO(3) (uniform axis on S² with angle density f(φ) = (1−cos φ)/π · Σ(2l+1)e^{−l(l+1)ε²} sin((l+½)φ)/sin(φ/2)), and produces N SE(3) frames — each a pair (x ∈ ℝ³, q ∈ S³) — that rigidly transform idealized heavy-atom coordinates [N*, Cα*, C*, O*] into the generated backbone. The oxygen coordinate is imputed from planar backbone geometry, so only the Cα–C bond rotation angle is left unparameterized. Generation is unconditional and operates on backbones of length 60–512 (PDB) or 60–128 (SCOPe).

## Method

ReQFlow decouples each residue's frame into a translation flow in ℝ³ (linear interpolation) and a rotation flow in SO(3) constructed via SLERP in exponential format (q_t = q₀ ⊗ exp(t log(q₀⁻¹ ⊗ q₁))), which guarantees numerical stability at both φ→0 and φ→π where matrix geodesic and additive-format SLERP fail. An SE(3)-equivariant network (FrameFlow architecture) predicts the endpoint transformation T_{θ,1} from the interpolated state T_t, trained by minimizing MSE on translation velocity and angular velocity plus an auxiliary physical-violation loss. The key acceleration step is flow rectification: the trained QFlow model generates 7,653 noise–backbone pairs, which are filtered (scRMSD ≤ 2 Å, TM-score ≥ 0.9 for long chains, <50% loops) and used to retrain the model, straightening sampling paths in both ℝ³ and SO(3). The paper proves that this rectification preserves marginal distributions (Theorem 3.1) and reduces convex transport cost (Theorem 3.2) on the quaternion manifold. At inference, an Euler solver with an exponential step-size scheduler (γ=10) updates rotations, enabling high-quality generation in as few as 20 steps (Fraction 0.872) or 50 steps (Fraction 0.912), achieving 37× and 63× speedups over RFDiffusion and Genie2 respectively.

## Results

On PDB (N=300), ReQFlow achieves the highest designability Fraction of 0.972 at 500 steps with the lowest scRMSD (1.071±0.482 Å), outperforming all baselines. At just 50 steps, ReQFlow retains a Fraction of 0.912 in 1.78 s, which is 37× faster than RFDiffusion (66.23 s) and 63× faster than Genie2 (112.93 s). Even at 20 steps (0.72 s), ReQFlow maintains a Fraction of 0.872, whereas most baselines degrade sharply under reduced sampling. On long-chain generation (N>500, beyond training range), ReQFlow sustains a Fraction of 0.70 at N=500 while all baselines drop below 0.30.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReQFlow outperforms RFDiffusion (Fraction 0.972 vs 0.904) and Genie2 (0.972 vs 0.908) on designability while being 37× and 63× faster respectively at N=300. Against FoldFlow₂, the strongest fast baseline, ReQFlow at 500 steps achieves a higher Fraction (0.972 vs 0.952) with notably lower scRMSD variance (0.482 vs 1.308 Å), and at 50 steps ReQFlow (0.912) still exceeds FoldFlow₂ at 20 steps (0.644). ReQFlow decisively beats FrameDiff (0.972 vs 0.564) and all FoldFlow variants (best: 0.852 for FoldFlow-OT). Compared to FrameFlow (0.872 at 500 steps), ReQFlow gains +0.100 Fraction and reduces scRMSD from 1.380 to 1.071 Å. The ablation on SCOPe further shows that rectification is universally beneficial: ReFrameFlow improves over FrameFlow (0.924 vs 0.851 at 500 steps), and ReQFlow outperforms ReFrameFlow (0.947 vs 0.924), confirming the advantage of quaternion-based SLERP over matrix geodesic interpolation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the SE(3)-equivariant flow matching framework of FrameFlow (Yim et al., 2023a) for protein backbone generation, replacing its rotation-matrix geodesic interpolation with quaternion-based SLERP in exponential format (Sola, 2017). It then applies the rectified flow technique (Liu, 2022) to straighten the sampling paths, extending that method from Euclidean space to the SO(3) rotation manifold via unit quaternions. The key conceptual departure from prior flow-based protein generators (FrameFlow, FoldFlow, Genie2) is the quaternion representation, which the authors argue provides superior numerical stability near both 0 and π rotation angles, and the rectification step, which they prove preserves marginal distributions and reduces transport cost on S³.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 37× and 63× speedups over RFDiffusion and Genie2 while matching or exceeding designability make large-scale protein backbone generation practically feasible, and the demonstrated robustness to long-chain generation (N > 500, beyond training data) addresses a critical failure mode of all prior methods. The rectification technique is shown to be model-agnostic (ReFrameFlow also benefits), suggesting broad applicability to any flow model on SO(3). Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger model architectures (the paper uses the same ~17M parameter FrameFlow backbone), whether it extends to conditional generation and side-chain/full-atom modeling, and whether the data-filtering requirement for effective rectification generalizes to other domains.
