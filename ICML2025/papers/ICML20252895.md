## Problem

Vision Transformers partition images into non-overlapping patches and rely on weak positional embeddings for continuity, yet the role of this continuity in cross-domain generalization has been unexplored. The paper identifies a previously overlooked phenomenon: disrupting image token continuity (via patch shuffling, positional embedding removal, or frequency-domain manipulation) causes substantial performance drops on the source domain but only marginal drops on distant target domains, raising the question of what continuity actually contributes to ViT's transferability under large domain gaps.

## Input / Output

During source-domain training, RGB images from miniImageNet (224×224) are split into 16×16 patches and subjected to spatial and frequency-domain continuity disruptions before being fed into a DINO-pretrained ViT-S. During target-domain evaluation, the model operates in a k-way n-shot paradigm (5-way 1-shot or 5-shot), receiving a small support set of target-domain images (from CropDiseases, EuroSAT, ISIC2018, or ChestX-ray8) to compute class prototypes, then classifying query images via nearest-prototype distance or fine-tuning-based classification.

## Method

The proposed ReCIT method disrupts image token continuity in two stages during source-domain training to force the model to rely on smaller, more transferable spatial patterns. First, a warm-up spatial-domain disruption randomly divides each image into a varying number of patches and shuffles them, gradually increasing difficulty. Second, a balanced frequency-domain disruption clusters patches by cosine similarity of their mean pixel values, models each cluster's amplitude distribution as a Gaussian, samples from these distributions, and reassigns amplitudes to patches using randomly weighted proportions—ensuring that even visually homogeneous regions (e.g., dark water in a jellyfish image) receive diverse amplitude assignments. This balanced reassignment eliminates style biases while preserving some local continuity, encouraging the model to extract domain-agnostic small-scale features that transfer effectively to distant target domains.

## Results

ReCIT achieves the best average 5-way accuracy across all four target domains (CropDiseases, EuroSAT, ISIC2018, ChestX-ray8) in every evaluation setting. In the standard 1-shot no-fine-tuning setting, ReCIT reaches 54.43% average accuracy, surpassing the closest competitor AttnTemp (54.12%) by 0.31 points. In the 5-shot no-fine-tuning setting, ReCIT attains 66.76% average, and in the transductive 5-shot fine-tuning setting it reaches 68.32%, consistently leading all baselines. The largest per-domain gains appear on ISIC2018 (skin lesions), where ReCIT improves over AttnTemp by up to 2.26 points (5-shot transductive: 55.60 vs. 55.22).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReCIT outperforms all 13 compared baselines across every evaluation setting (1-shot/5-shot, with/without fine-tuning, transductive/non-transductive). The closest competitor is AttnTemp (Zou et al., NeurIPS-24), which ReCIT surpasses by 0.14–0.55 points in average accuracy depending on the setting; the gap is largest in the 1-shot transductive setting (+0.55). Among the other ViT-based baselines, CD-CLS (NeurIPS-24) and DAMIM (AAAI-25) trail ReCIT by 0.75–1.04 points on average in the 1-shot no-FT setting. The CNN-based ResNet10 methods (GNN+FT, MN+AFA, GNN+ATA, LDP-net, RDC, ConFeSS) are substantially weaker, with average accuracies 7–15 points below ReCIT, reflecting the advantage of the ViT backbone. Notably, ReCIT achieves the best per-domain score on CropDiseases and EuroSAT in all settings, while on ISIC2018 (the hardest domain) it leads AttnTemp by 0.21–2.26 points. The source-domain (miniImageNet) accuracy drops slightly (97.78 → 96.33), confirming the intended trade-off of sacrificing some source-domain performance for improved cross-domain transferability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper operates within the well-established Cross-Domain Few-Shot Learning (CDFSL) task, building on the ViT architecture and DINO pretraining paradigm used by recent CDFSL methods (MEM-FS, StyleAdv, FLoR, DAMIM, CD-CLS, AttnTemp). Its conceptual departure is the first consideration of image token continuity as a factor in cross-domain generalization: it repurposes patch shuffling and Fourier amplitude-phase decomposition—techniques previously explored for robustness or style transfer—into a principled continuity-disruption strategy that encourages learning of smaller, more transferable spatial patterns. The CKA-based domain similarity measurement follows prior work by Oh et al. (2022) and Davari et al. (2022), but is applied here to validate the continuity-disruption hypothesis rather than to diagnose domain shift.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that disrupting token continuity asymmetrically degrades source-domain performance while barely affecting target-domain performance offers a concrete mechanistic explanation for why large spatial patterns fail to transfer under extreme domain gaps, and provides a simple, training-time-only augmentation strategy that requires no architectural changes. However, the practical gains over strong baselines like AttnTemp are modest (~0.5–1% average), the interpretation that 'only small patterns within patches transfer' remains intuitive rather than rigorously proven (e.g., no probing of which specific pattern scales are extracted), and evaluation is limited to four target domains and a single ViT-S backbone. Open questions include whether the continuity-disruption principle generalizes to CNNs, larger ViT variants, or domains with smaller gaps, and whether the balanced frequency-domain disruption can be further formalized beyond the ad-hoc Gaussian sampling.
