## Problem

Existing visual prompting methods for Vision Transformers apply the same set of learnable prompts to all tokens uniformly, ignoring the fundamentally different roles of CLS tokens (global information aggregation) and image tokens (local feature extraction), as well as the diverse discriminative information carried by different image patches. This uniform prompting causes different tokens to attend to similar regions and extract biased, indistinguishable features, limiting the representational capacity of the ViT backbone.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251615/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard RGB image, which is partitioned into N uniform patches and embedded into D-dimensional tokens alongside a CLS token. TCPA is a plug-and-play module inserted into the self-attention layers of a frozen pretrained ViT-B/16 backbone; it introduces only CLS prompt pools (size N_c=10) and image prompt pools (size N_i=20) as additional learnable parameters. The output is a class label produced by a trainable linear head applied to the final CLS token, with the entire pretrained encoder kept frozen during fine-tuning.

## Method

TCPA introduces two coordinated mechanisms. First (R-TCPA), prompts are disentangled into CLS Prompts and Image Prompts that are fed into separate attention computations: CLS prompts interact exclusively with the CLS token while image prompts interact exclusively with image tokens, with the other token's output discarded in each pass. Second (T-TCPA), each prompt type is expanded into a pool of multiple prompts paired with learnable indicator vectors; for each token, cosine distance to all indicators is computed, the top-K nearest prompts are selected via a binary mask, and this mask is applied element-wise to the attention map to restrict each token's attention to only its matched prompts. This enables a single attention computation to simulate multiple targeted token-prompt interactions without additional feed-forward cost. Training minimizes cross-entropy loss plus regularization terms that encourage the prompt matching to align with token-prompt affinity, while the ViT backbone remains frozen.

## Results

On the HTA benchmark (10 datasets), TCPA consistently improves all prompting baselines: VFPT+TCPA achieves 90.4% average accuracy (vs. 89.2% for VFPT alone), surpassing full fine-tuning (85.8%). On the VTAB benchmark, VPT+TCPA reaches 79.7% (Natural), 84.3% (Specialized), and 56.2% (Structured), outperforming VPT by 1.2%, 1.9%, and 1.2% respectively. The largest per-dataset gains appear on fine-grained tasks (e.g., +3.1% on Stanford Dogs for VP+TCPA, +3.2% on GTSRB for DAMVP+TCPA). Additional training time is minimal, e.g., only 0.05 s/epoch over VPT on CUB.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TCPA is a plug-and-play module that consistently improves every prompting baseline it is applied to. On the HTA benchmark, VP+TCPA gains +2.0% average over VP (85.4 vs. 83.4), VPT+TCPA gains +1.5% (87.0 vs. 85.5), DAMVP+TCPA gains +1.4% (89.9 vs. 88.5), AutoVP+TCPA gains +1.8% (87.2 vs. 85.4), and VFPT+TCPA gains +1.2% (90.4 vs. 89.2). The strongest prompting method, VFPT+TCPA, surpasses full fine-tuning (85.8%) by 4.6% in average accuracy while training far fewer parameters. Among non-prompting baselines, Bias (87.1%) and AdaptFormer (85.8%) are the closest competitors, but both are outperformed by every TCPA-enhanced prompting method. On VTAB, the largest relative gains appear in the Specialized group (e.g., +2.5% for VP+TCPA, +2.1% for DAMVP+TCPA and AutoVP+TCPA), suggesting TCPA is particularly effective for domain-specific tasks. Partial-tuning baselines (Linear 75.7%, Partial 84.6%, MLP 79.9%) and extra-module baselines (Sidetune 81.0%, Adapter 80.8%) are all substantially below the prompting methods, confirming the advantage of the prompt-learning paradigm that TCPA builds upon.

## Relation to Prior Work

TCPA extends the visual prompt tuning paradigm initiated by VPT (Jia et al., 2022) and input-level prompting (Bahng et al., 2022), which both apply a single shared set of prompts uniformly across all tokens in a ViT. The key departure is a two-level disentanglement: first separating CLS prompts from image prompts to respect their distinct roles (global aggregation vs. local extraction), then expanding each into a prompt pool with a cosine-distance matching function that assigns token-specific prompts via attention masking. This positions TCPA as a plug-and-play module that can be layered on top of existing methods (VPT, VP, DAMVP, AutoVP, VFPT) rather than a standalone approach, building directly on the low-rank attention analysis from Wang et al. (2020) and Kim et al. (2024) to motivate why uniform prompts limit representational capacity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play design and minimal computational overhead (e.g., 2.41s vs 2.36s per epoch for VPT) make TCPA a practical drop-in improvement for the visual prompting community, with consistent 1–3% gains across five base methods and two major benchmarks (HTA, VTAB). However, the absolute improvements are modest, the method is validated only on ViT-B/16 with ImageNet-21k supervision, and it remains unclear whether the prompt-pool matching mechanism scales meaningfully to larger backbones (ViT-L/H) or self-supervised pretraining regimes. An open question is whether the token-prompt assignment learned via cosine distance generalizes across domains or requires retraining per task, and whether the low-rank attention argument fully explains the observed gains or whether the benefit is primarily from increased parameter count in the prompt pools.
