## Problem

Prior theoretical analyses of Weak-to-Strong Generalization either assumed high-quality strong model representations, attributed performance gains solely to underfitting, or studied benign overfitting under restrictive data-distribution assumptions. No unified framework existed to characterize how the interaction between weak and strong models' internal representations jointly determines which supervision errors are corrected versus replicated, leaving the conditions for effective W2SG unclear across the full spectrum of behaviors.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252807/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes the internal representations of both weak and strong models (last-layer embeddings for fixed-representation setups, or layer-wise normalized activation maps for full-model finetuning) along with the finetuning data, and produces a label-agnostic scalar metric ‖P_s(I−P_w)‖_op that predicts W2SG performance without ground truth labels. Principal representations are approximated via PCA (or top-variance coordinate selection for high-dimensional activation maps), and the effective regularization β_eff is shared across all weak models, requiring only four hyperparameters total per experimental setting.

## Method

The paper shows that W2SG performance is governed by the overlap between the complement of the weak model's principal kernel span and the strong model's principal kernel span, formalized as P_s(I−P_w) where P_w and P_s are scaled projections onto the spans of principal kernels computed from the finetuning data. The main theorem (Thm. 3.8) establishes PredGap = ‖P_s(I−P_w)(1/√n)ŷ‖² ± o(1) under a general (δ,γ̂,γ̃)-decomposability assumption, meaning only weak supervision errors expressible through the strong model's principal representations are replicated at test time. This yields a label-free upper bound PredGap ≤ C‖P_s(I−P_w)‖_op² + o(1), validated across 150 molecular transformers, 22 embedding models, and 28 LLMs with strong Spearman correlation to actual W2SG error even in full-model finetuning. The framework also derives a general condition for benign overfitting: when the strong model's non-principal kernel is highly isotropic, overfitting to weak supervision errors outside the principal kernel span is harmless, explaining why large networks can memorize noisy labels yet still generalize.

## Results

The main theoretical result (Thm. 3.8) characterizes PredGap as ||P_s(I−P_w)(1/√n)ŷ||² ± o(1), where P_w and P_s are scaled projections onto the spans of the weak and strong models' principal kernels. Empirically, the label-agnostic metric ||P_s(I−P_w)||_op exhibits strong Spearman correlation with Err_w2s across all three experimental setups: 150 small transformers on molecular tasks (Exp. I), 22 embedding models on NLP classification (Exp. II), and 28 LLMs finetuned end-to-end on three NLP tasks (Exp. III). In the toy benign-overfitting example (Corollary 4.3), with signal strength η=0.6, the W2S model achieves Err_w2s ≈ 0.16 versus the weak model's Err_w ≈ 0.4, despite nearly perfect overfitting on the finetuning data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not perform a head-to-head comparison against competing W2SG methods; instead, it validates its proposed representation-based metric against simpler heuristics. The key comparison is between ||P_s(I−P_w)||_op and model size (or activation map dimension) as predictors of W2SG performance. In Exp. III (Fig. 5), for models with activation map dimensions ≤ 8000, both the raw dimension and the dimension of approximated principal representations correlate poorly with Err_w2s, while ||P_s(I−P_w)||_op maintains a strong correlation. Similarly, in Exp. I, smaller weak models (hidden size 64) can outperform larger ones (hidden size 128) due to differences in pretraining epochs, yet the proposed metric still captures the correct performance ordering. This demonstrates that the kernel-overlap metric provides a more fine-grained and robust predictor of W2SG performance than model capacity or representation dimensionality alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the representation-based analysis of W2SG initiated by Charikar et al. (2024), which characterized performance gains via disagreement between finetuned models but assumed high-quality strong-model representations. It also generalizes the benign overfitting results of Wu & Sahai (2024) from specific bi-level ensemble assumptions to a broader condition governed by the overlap of principal kernel spaces. Unlike Lang et al. (2024) and Shin et al. (2024), which attribute W2SG solely to underfitting of weak supervision, this work provides a unified framework where both underfitting and overfitting regimes are captured by a single geometric quantity: the overlap between the complement of the weak model's principal kernel span and the strong model's principal kernel span.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The label-agnostic metric ‖P_s(I−P_w)‖_op offers a practical tool for predicting W2SG performance without access to finetuning labels, validated across 150 transformers and 52 LLMs on 8 datasets. However, the practical computation of P for real models relies on heuristics (PCA on embeddings, top-variance coordinate selection on activation maps) that are acknowledged as proof-of-concept, and the theoretical analysis assumes linear finetuning on fixed representations—conditions that do not strictly hold for full LLM finetuning. Open questions include whether more principled representation definitions (e.g., NTK-based) would tighten the empirical correlation, and whether the metric can guide practical decisions like weak-model selection or pretraining recipe design.
