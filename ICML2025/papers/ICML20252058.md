## Problem

Achieving optimal Differential Privacy guarantees in Federated Learning under partial-participation—where only a subset of machines join each round—remains an open challenge. Prior methods either required full-participation or incurred super-linear computational complexity (O(n^{3/2}) or O(n^{9/8})), failing to match the linear cost of standard training. The paper asks whether one can simultaneously achieve optimal population-loss bounds under DP and maintain linear computational complexity in the total number of samples.

## Input / Output

Each participating machine draws exactly one fresh sample per round it participates and computes two gradient estimates (at the current and previous query points), so total gradient computations remain linear in n = mT across T rounds with m machines per round. The parameter server aggregates noisy gradient corrections from the m participating machines each round and outputs a single model parameter x_T after T rounds. Privacy is guaranteed per-machine as ρ²/2-zCDP, with each machine's noise variance scaled by its cumulative participation count N_{t,i}, so machines that join more rounds inject proportionally more noise.

## Method

The core contribution is a noise-cancellation mechanism layered on the μ²-SGD double-momentum update. Rather than injecting independent noise at every round, each machine computes a gradient correction s_{t,i} = α_t∇f(x_t; z_{t,i}) − α_{t-1}∇f(x_{t-1}; z_{t,i}), then adds fresh Gaussian noise y_{t,i} while subtracting its previously stored noise Y_{t-1,i}, yielding a private correction s̃_{t,i} = s_{t,i} + y_{t,i} − Y_{t-1,i}. When the server sums these corrections over rounds, the noise terms telescope so the effective server-side noise is simply (1/m)Σ_i Y_{t,i}—the last noise from each machine—rather than an accumulation over all T rounds. The noise variance is set as σ²_{t,i} = 4S²(1+log T)·N_{t,i}/ρ², tying each machine's noise to its participation history to balance privacy and utility. This achieves optimal excess loss O(1/√n + √(Md)/(εn)) for the untrusted-server case and O(1/√n + √d/(εn)) for the trusted-server case, both matching known lower bounds, while preserving linear computational complexity in n.

## Results

On MNIST logistic regression (n=60,000 samples, m=50, M=100), the proposed DP-μ²-FL method achieves 63.7% test accuracy at ρ=8 and 66.5% at ρ=12, while maintaining a constant runtime of 13 seconds regardless of privacy level. The theoretical excess loss bound is O(1/√n + √(Md)/(εn)), matching the known lower bound for the untrusted-server case, with linear O(n) computational complexity. At the highest privacy level (ρ=12), the method achieves 66.5% accuracy in 13 seconds, compared to 66.7% in 730 seconds for the prior state-of-the-art (Lowy & Razaviyayn, 2023).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two baselines: Noisy SGD (Abadi et al., 2016) and the ISRL-DP method of Lowy & Razaviyayn (2023). Against Noisy SGD, the proposed method consistently wins on accuracy by 4.8–8.7 percentage points across all privacy levels (e.g., 63.7% vs. 58.9% at ρ=8), at only a 4-second runtime cost (13 s vs. 9 s). Against Lowy & Razaviyayn (2023), the proposed method achieves nearly identical accuracy (within 0.2–6.1 pp, with the gap narrowing at higher ρ) but is dramatically faster: 13 seconds versus 64–730 seconds, a speedup of 5× to 56×. At ρ=12, the prior work edges out the proposed method by 0.2 pp (66.7% vs. 66.5%), but at ρ=4 the proposed method leads by 6.2 pp (53.8% vs. 47.6%). The key practical advantage is that the proposed method's runtime is invariant to both privacy level and number of participating machines, whereas Lowy & Razaviyayn's runtime scales super-linearly with both.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DP-μ²-FL framework of Reshef & Levy (2024) from full-participation to the more practical partial-participation setting, building on the μ²-SGD variance-reduction mechanism (Levy, 2023) and the Anytime-GD weighted-averaging scheme (Cutkosky, 2019). The key departure from prior work (Lowy & Razaviyayn, 2023; Gao et al., 2024) is the introduction of a correlated noise-cancellation mechanism that replaces independent per-round noise injection, enabling optimal convergence rates with linear computational complexity rather than the super-linear n^{3/2} or n^{9/8} complexity of prior methods. The paper also departs from the standard independent-noise approach in DP-SGD by tailoring each machine's noise magnitude to its cumulative participation count, a design choice motivated by the untrusted-server privacy requirement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work closes a notable gap in the DP-FL literature by showing that optimal population-loss guarantees and linear computational complexity can coexist under partial participation with an untrusted server, a setting that reflects real-world FL deployments where device availability is inconsistent. The noise-cancellation technique is a clean conceptual contribution that could inform future work on correlated-noise mechanisms in other distributed privacy settings. However, the empirical validation is limited to logistic regression on MNIST with modest accuracy (60–66%), and the theoretical guarantees are restricted to convex objectives, leaving open whether the approach extends to non-convex federated learning or deeper architectures. The practical significance will depend on whether the linear-complexity advantage translates to meaningful speedups in large-scale, multi-round FL systems beyond the toy experiments presented.
