## Problem

Decoding-time LLM alignment and even basic decoding algorithms (greedy, beam search) lack foundational theoretical understanding, and how to sample-efficiently learn user preferences from feedback during inference remains unaddressed. This paper shows that without structural assumptions on the sequence utility function, any algorithm suffers exponential-in-depth regret, making the problem fundamentally intractable. The paper introduces a natural structural assumption (DDMC) under which sublinear regret becomes achievable, providing the first provable guarantees for tokenized bandit formulations of LLM decoding and alignment.

## Input / Output

At each round, the decision maker receives a user query x_t and must irrevocably select tokens one-by-one from a vocabulary of size n until an EOS token is emitted, with a hard cap of L tokens. The reward r_t(y_t) = u_t(y_t) + η_t is observed only after the full sequence is finalized, where u_t is a sequence function (linear in an embedding for TLB, arbitrary for TMAB) and η_t is bounded noise. In the alignment application, the utility is a convex combination of a known frozen-LLM probability mapped through v(·) and a latent linear function ⟨θ, e(x_t, y)⟩, so the algorithm must learn both θ and the mixing weight γ online.

## Method

The core contribution is the DDMC assumption: for two same-length sequences y, z and any token τ, appending τ to both can only shrink their utility gap, |u(y:τ)−u(z:τ)| ≤ |u(y)−u(z)|. For TLB, the EOFUL algorithm combines greedy decoding with LinUCB-style confidence balls over the embedding space, achieving regret Õ(L√T) via a novel analysis that introduces level-k regret, a fictitious extension that recursively propagates regret upward, and a sum-of-squares argument. For TMAB (no linear structure, fixed context), GreedyETC uses an exploration-then-commit strategy at each depth level, achieving Õ(nLT^{2/3}) by taking union bounds only along the algorithm's selected path rather than over all n^L sequences. As a corollary, in the static (known-utility) setting, greedy decoding is provably (1−ε)-optimal under DDMC, where ε is the single-level deviation parameter, explaining its empirical effectiveness.

## Results

The paper establishes that without structural assumptions, TLB suffers worst-case regret Ω(T(1−1/2^(L−2))) and TMAB suffers Ω(min(√(n^L)·T), T)), showing exponential hardness in sequence length L. Under the DDMC assumption, EOFUL achieves regret Õ(L√T) for TLB and GreedyETC achieves Õ(LT^(2/3)) for TMAB, both linear in L and sublinear in T. Empirically, EOFUL achieves sublinear regret on synthetic LLM alignment data (L=30, top-15 tokens, γ=0.8), and the DDMC assumption is validated on TruthfulQA, HH-RLHF, AdvBench, and just-eval-instruct, showing decreasing utility gaps as common suffix tokens increase. Assumption 3.6 (subsequence norm bound) is verified with a universal upper bound of 1.25 on the ratio ‖y^(1:l)‖_Σ / ‖y‖_Σ across all token positions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares EOFUL against three baselines on synthetic LLM alignment data: (i) a theoretical regret upper bound (scaled by 0.1), (ii) WrongTheta, which uses a fixed incorrectly-estimated θ′=(−0.5,…,−0.5), and (iii) Misaligned greedy, which performs greedy decoding solely on the frozen LLM's probability. In early rounds (t=1–2), EOFUL underperforms all baselines due to cold-start (e.g., utility −26.64 vs. optimal greedy's −1.36), but by later rounds (t=9999–10000), EOFUL's utility (−1.57 or 28.34) surpasses both Misaligned greedy (−11.06 or 2.68) and even the optimal greedy (−2.99 or 22.87) in some cases. WrongTheta produces garbled, incoherent outputs in later rounds (e.g., utility 56.36 with corrupted text), confirming that a static wrong estimator cannot adapt. The paper notes that EOFUL's early-round degradation motivates practical hybrid strategies (e.g., running standard alignment in early rounds while EOFUL learns in the background), and that top-k truncation (k=15) creates a trade-off between exploration freedom and filtering out cold-start errors.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper introduces tokenized variants of linear and multi-armed bandit problems (TLB, TMAB) specifically for LLM decoding, building directly on the LinUCB confidence-ball machinery (Abbasi-Yadkori et al., 2011; Li et al., 2010) for its EOFUL algorithm. It positions itself in contrast to the MDP-based framing of decoding-time alignment (Josifoski et al., 2022; Huang et al., 2024; Mudgal et al., 2023) by treating token selection as an irrevocable sequential bandit problem, and establishes a formal reduction to the bandit tree search (BTS) problem of Coquelin & Munos (2007). The novel DDMC assumption is explicitly positioned as complementary to (not implied by) the submodularity assumptions widely used in sequence function maximization (Mitrovic et al., 2019; Alaei et al., 2021), differing in that it governs diminishing *distance* between same-length sequences rather than diminishing *returns* of nested sets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical implication is Theorem 5.1, which proves greedy decoding is (1−ε)-optimal under DDMC, providing the first theoretical justification for the empirically observed 'unreasonable effectiveness' of greedy decoding in many LLM tasks. However, the result is conditional on DDMC (validated only in an average sense on four datasets) and the TLB algorithm requires a Mahalanobis-norm structural assumption (3.6) that depends on query similarity. Open questions include: characterizing regimes where sampling-based methods provably outperform greedy, extending DDMC validation beyond average-case to worst-case guarantees, and addressing the cold-start problem where EOFUL produces grammatically incoherent outputs in early rounds. The exponential lower bound without DDMC (Propositions 3.3, 4.1) underscores that any practical decoding theory must impose some structure on the sequence function.
