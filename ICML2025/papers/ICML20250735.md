## Problem

Multi-task and meta-reinforcement learning methods struggle to generalize when tasks are highly diverse, suffering from negative transfer or requiring large numbers of training tasks. Existing task-clustering approaches for personalized RL lack performance guarantees and still perform poorly with few training tasks. No prior method provides both provable coverage guarantees and practical algorithms for learning a small set of policies that collectively handle a diverse, potentially unknown task distribution.

## Input / Output

The method takes task parameter vectors θ ∈ R^d (or, for non-parametric tasks like Meta-World, 50-dimensional embeddings extracted from the penultimate layer of Phi-3 Mini-128k Instruct applied to natural-language task descriptions) and produces a committee of K policies. For zero-shot evaluation, the best-performing policy in the committee is selected per task; for few-shot adaptation, all K policies are evaluated over N randomly initialized episodes on the new task and the one with the highest empirical return is chosen. The clustering step itself requires only the task embeddings (no on-policy interaction), completing in roughly 1 second, while the dominant computational cost is the K parallel RL training runs.

## Method

PACMAN reformulates the clustering objective as a parameter-coverage problem: find K representative points C = {θ_1,…,θ_K} such that a randomly drawn task parameter θ is within ε in ℓ∞-distance of at least one representative with high probability, rather than minimizing aggregate distance as in k-means. Two algorithms solve this: the Greedy Intersection Algorithm (GIA), which builds per-dimension intersection trees and selects the hypercube covering the most points, yielding a (1−1/e) approximation guarantee for constant dimension d; and a gradient-based method that optimizes a differentiable ReLU-softmax proxy for the non-convex coverage objective, applicable to high-dimensional embeddings. Each cluster's tasks are then used to train one policy via any standard RL algorithm (e.g., VariBAD for MuJoCo, MOORE for Meta-World), requiring only K independent and parallelizable RL runs. The framework provides a sample-efficient generalization bound (Theorem 3.7) under Lipschitz reward assumptions and a few-shot adaptation bound (Theorem 3.8) whose sample complexity is linear in K and independent of state or action space dimension.

## Results

On Meta-World few-shot adaptation (12K updates), PACMAN achieves a 0.60±0.02 success rate, outperforming the best baseline CARE (0.40±0.06) by over 36%. On MuJoCo few-shot, PACMAN achieves -54.03±1.34 on HalfCheetahVel (a 2.5× improvement over the best baseline VariBAD at -137.99) and 2086.50±0.89 on HumanoidDir (22% over VariBAD's 1706.38). In zero-shot MTRL training and test evaluations on Meta-World, PACMAN shows approximately 25% improvement over the best MTRL baseline after 500K steps, and consistently outperforms all baselines in both MuJoCo environments.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PACMAN outperforms all 11 baselines across both MuJoCo and Meta-World in every evaluation setting. On Meta-World few-shot (the most challenging setting with only 30 training tasks and 20 diverse test tasks), PACMAN (0.60 at 12K updates) beats the strongest MTRL baselines CARE (0.40), CMTA (0.34), and MOORE (0.44) by 36–76%, and dominates all meta-RL baselines (MAML 0.01, PEARL 0.27, RL2 0.02, VariBAD 0.027, AMAGO 0.093) by wide margins. On MuJoCo few-shot, PACMAN achieves -54.03 on HalfCheetahVel, a 2.5× improvement over VariBAD (-137.99), the only competitive baseline, while EM (-325.29), AMAGO (-279.13), and RL2 (-314.37) are far behind. In zero-shot MTRL training and test on Meta-World, PACMAN surpasses all MTRL baselines (CMTA, MOORE, CARE, Soft, Multi-task SAC) by ~25% after 500K steps, while meta-RL baselines underperform MTRL methods in this setting due to the small training set. The EM-based personalized RL baseline (Ivanov & Ben-Porat, 2024) is only applicable to MuJoCo (shared dynamics) and is substantially outperformed by PACMAN in few-shot (-54 vs. -325 on HalfCheetah). Notably, the meta-RL baselines that typically excel in standard few-shot benchmarks (MAML, RL2, VariBAD) perform poorly here because the evaluation uses far fewer training tasks (30) and more diverse test tasks (20) than typical, highlighting PACMAN's advantage in high-diversity, low-data regimes.

## Relation to Prior Work

PACMAN extends the personalized RL line of work (Ivanov & Ben-Porat, 2024; Ackermann et al., 2021), which clusters tasks and trains distinct policies per cluster, by reformulating the objective as a flexible coverage problem over an unknown task distribution rather than insisting on full coverage. This reformulation, combined with an explicit parametric task representation, enables provable approximation and sample complexity guarantees that prior EM-style approaches lack. The paper also departs from standard MTRL and meta-RL methods (MAML, PEARL, VariBAD, AMAGO, MOORE) that rely on shared parameters and task conditioning, which struggle under high task diversity, and borrows the LLM-based task embedding idea from Bing et al. (2023) but applies it to higher-level goal descriptions rather than subtask plans.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first policy-committee approach with provable performance guarantees (sample-efficient generalization and few-shot adaptation bounds independent of state/action space dimension), which is valuable for safety-critical or resource-constrained settings where guarantees matter. The practical finding that meta-RL methods underperform MTRL baselines in a challenging few-shot regime (30 train / 20 test tasks, short adaptation horizon) is a useful caution for practitioners. Key limitations include the requirement for parametric task representations (or LLM embeddings, whose generalizability beyond Meta-World is unclear) and the single environment-specific hyperparameter ε; open questions include whether the coverage formulation extends to settings with varying transition dynamics and how to obtain reliable task embeddings for arbitrary non-parametric domains.
