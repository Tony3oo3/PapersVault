## Problem

MLLMs struggle with emotion classification during inference due to two issues: confusion between semantically similar emotion categories (e.g., amusement vs. excitement) and visual redundancy that obscures key emotional cues. Existing remedies rely on fine-tuning or manual image annotation, making them resource-intensive and hard to scale. This paper seeks a training-free, annotation-free solution that operates purely at inference time.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an RGB image and a text instruction (a multiple-choice emotion query) and outputs a single emotion category label. It is applied to LLaVA-7b and VILA-8b across datasets with 6–25 emotion categories. Internally, the pipeline runs two sequential inference passes: the first produces a coarse positive/negative label plus attention maps and logits, and the second produces the final fine-grained category after visual token pruning and prompt refinement.

## Method

SEPM combines two training-free mechanisms. First, Confidence-Guided Coarse-to-Fine Inference (CCI) splits classification into a coarse stage (positive vs. negative) and a fine stage (specific category within the chosen group); a confidence score computed from the variance of the two option logits determines whether to restrict the fine-stage options or retain all categories with an ambiguity-aware prompt. Second, Focus-on-Emotion Visual Augmentation (VTA) prepends a 'Please focus on emotion' prompt, extracts the attention map from the coarse-stage inference, computes per-visual-token relevance to that prompt, and drops the lowest-relevance tokens (controlled by a drop rate β) before the fine-stage inference. The entire pipeline requires no parameter updates, no additional encoders, and no manual image annotation.

## Results

SEPM achieves an average accuracy of 40.04% on LLaVA-7b (β=0.1) and 38.37% on VILA-8b (β=0.1), improving over zero-shot baselines by +6.00 and +4.54 points respectively. The largest gain appears on WebEmo-7, where SEPM(β=0.1) on LLaVA-7b reaches 42.39% accuracy versus 25.56% for zero-shot (+16.83). Zero-shot-CoT actually degrades performance relative to plain zero-shot on most datasets (e.g., 33.04% vs. 34.04% average on LLaVA-7b), while SparseVLM yields only a marginal +0.55 average improvement over zero-shot.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SEPM outperforms both baselines across all five dataset splits on LLaVA-7b. Against Zero-shot-CoT (Kojima et al., NeurIPS 2022), SEPM(β=0.1) gains +7.00 average accuracy (40.04% vs. 33.04%), with the most dramatic improvement on WebEmo-7 (+19.46 points); notably, Zero-shot-CoT is actually worse than plain zero-shot on most splits, indicating that generic chain-of-thought prompting is counterproductive for emotion classification. Against SparseVLM (Zhang et al., 2024), which also drops visual tokens but uses query-relevance rather than emotion-specific relevance, SEPM(β=0.1) improves average accuracy by +5.45 points (40.04% vs. 34.59%), with the gap widening to +15.75 on WebEmo-7. On VILA-8b, SEPM(β=0.1) beats Zero-shot-CoT by +5.73 average points (38.37% vs. 32.64%), though SEPM(β=0.2) underperforms on WebEmo-7 (39.10% vs. 41.90% zero-shot), suggesting that aggressive token dropping can be harmful when the base model already attends well to emotional cues.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of training-free MLLM inference and emotion recognition, departing from fine-tuning-based approaches (EmoVIT, Emotion-LLaMA) that require costly emotion-specific training. Conceptually, it repurposes two existing ideas—coarse-to-fine classification decomposition and attention-based visual token pruning (as in SparseVLM)—but recombines them with an emotion-specific Focus-on-Emotion prompt and a confidence-gated mechanism that is absent from prior token-dropping methods. Unlike visual prompting approaches like Set-of-Vision that require manual face-region annotation, SEPM operates entirely at inference time without any data modification, carving a distinct niche in the training-free MLLM literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free, annotation-free nature of SEPM makes it immediately deployable on any MLLM for emotion tasks, which is practically valuable given the high cost of emotion-specific fine-tuning. However, the method is validated on only two MLLM architectures (LLaVA-7b, VILA-8b) and four relatively small emotion datasets, leaving open whether the gains transfer to larger or more diverse models. The paper itself flags an important open question (RQ4): MLLMs exhibit category-specific biases under different prompts, and selectively enhancing accuracy for underperforming emotion categories remains unaddressed. Broader applicability beyond emotion classification to other fine-grained, semantically-confusable classification tasks is a natural but unexplored extension.
