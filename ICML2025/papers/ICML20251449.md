## Problem

Clipped SGD is widely used for privacy preservation and training stability, but its behavior under decision-dependent data distributions (performative prediction) was entirely unexplored. The clipping operator introduces non-smooth, non-gradient dynamics that interact with distribution shifts in a way not captured by prior analyses of either clipped SGD in i.i.d. settings or unclipped SGD in performative settings. This paper identifies a bias amplification phenomenon where the asymptotic clipping bias is multiplicatively worsened by the sensitivity of the data distribution to model changes.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes single-sample stochastic gradients drawn from a decision-dependent distribution D(θ_t) that shifts at every iteration via greedy deployment, along with a clipping threshold c, a step size schedule {γ_t}, and a Gaussian DP noise level σ_DP. The output is a sequence of model parameters θ_t that converge (in expectation) to the performative stable solution θ_PS or a biased neighborhood thereof, with explicit bounds on the asymptotic clipping bias of order Θ(1/(μ−Lβ)²) for PCSGD and O(1/√T) for the non-convex case. A key structural constraint is that the data distribution itself is a function of the current iterate, making the gradient estimates non-i.i.d. and the dynamics non-gradient.

## Method

The paper first analyzes Projected Clipped SGD (PCSGD) by constructing a Lyapunov function that accounts for both the non-smooth clipping operator and the Wasserstein-bounded distribution shift, yielding tight upper and lower bounds on the asymptotic clipping bias of Θ(1/(μ−Lβ)²) for strongly convex losses. The lower bound is established via a constructed counterexample with Bernoulli-distributed data, proving the bias is unavoidable and demonstrating the amplification effect as β approaches μ/L. As a remedy, the paper adapts DiceSGD (Zhang et al., 2024) to the performative setting, where a doubly-clipping mechanism on both the gradient and an accumulated error-feedback vector eliminates the asymptotic bias, achieving O(1/t) convergence to θ_PS under strong convexity and O(1/√T) stationarity for non-convex losses. The analysis further derives optimal step size and clipping threshold selections that balance model efficacy against (ε,δ)-differential privacy guarantees, revealing a tradeoff where tighter privacy budgets increase the irreducible bias floor.

## Results

The paper establishes that PCSGD converges to a neighborhood of the performative stable (PS) solution with an irreducible asymptotic clipping bias of Θ(1/(μ−Lβ)²) for strongly convex losses (Theorems 3–4), where β is the distribution-shift sensitivity. For non-convex losses, PCSGD achieves O(1/√T) convergence to a biased stationary PS solution (Theorem 5). DiceSGD, equipped with dual clipping and error feedback, removes the clipping bias entirely: under strong convexity it converges to θ_PS at rate O(1/t) (Theorem 7), and for non-convex losses it achieves O(1/√T + bβ) stationarity (Theorem 8). Numerically, on the GiveMeSomeCredit dataset (m=15,776, d=10), DiceSGD's performative stability gap ‖θ_t − θ_PS‖² decays at O(1/t) while PCSGD plateaus at a non-zero steady state; increasing β from 0.001 to 0.1 degrades PCSGD's true positive rate and increases its asymptotic bias.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three algorithms: plain SGD with DP noise (Abadi et al., 2016), PCSGD (projected clipped SGD), and DiceSGD (Zhang et al., 2024, adapted to performative prediction). On the synthetic quadratic problem, plain SGD with DP noise shows the slowest convergence due to accumulated noise, PCSGD converges but plateaus at a non-zero gap from θ_PS (the clipping bias), while DiceSGD converges to θ_PS at the optimal O(1/t) rate with no residual bias. On the GiveMeSomeCredit logistic regression task, DiceSGD's performative stability gap decays monotonically to zero, whereas PCSGD reaches a steady-state offset after a transient phase; the gap between the two widens as the distribution-shift sensitivity β increases from 0.001 to 0.1. In terms of test performance, increasing β degrades PCSGD's true positive rate more severely than DiceSGD's, confirming the theoretical bias amplification effect. The optimal step size γ* (Corollary 2), which accounts for β, consistently yields smaller PCSGD bias than a non-optimal step size that ignores distribution shift, validating the tradeoff between privacy budget ε and model efficacy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two previously separate research threads: performative prediction (Perdomo et al., 2020; Drusvyatskiy & Xiao, 2023), which studies SGD under decision-dependent distributions, and clipped SGD analysis (Koloskova et al., 2023; Zhang et al., 2024), which characterizes the asymptotic bias introduced by gradient clipping under i.i.d. sampling. Prior performative prediction work assumed smooth drift terms and did not account for the non-smooth clipping operator, while prior clipped SGD work assumed fixed distributions and did not model distribution shift. The paper explicitly identifies this gap as an open problem and provides the first convergence analysis of PCSGD under performative dynamics, then adapts the DiceSGD error-feedback mechanism (Zhang et al., 2024) to the performative setting to eliminate the clipping bias.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The bias amplification effect—where clipping bias scales as Θ(1/(μ−Lβ)²) and worsens as distribution sensitivity β approaches μ/L—is a new phenomenon unique to performative prediction that has no analogue in standard i.i.d. clipped SGD. This has direct practical implications for DP-SGD deployments in settings where models influence their training data (e.g., strategic classification, recommendation systems). The DiceSGD remedy demonstrates that error feedback can restore unbiased convergence even under distribution shift, offering a concrete algorithmic fix. Open questions include tight lower bounds for the non-convex case, the practical difficulty of tuning step sizes when problem parameters (μ, L, β) are unknown, and whether the multiplicative sensitivity of DiceSGD's bias to DP noise and clipping thresholds limits its practical advantage over PCSGD.
