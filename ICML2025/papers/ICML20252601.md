## Problem

Different active learning (AL) strategies (uncertainty-based vs. diversity-based) perform inconsistently across datasets and tasks, yet no existing method can automatically and efficiently select the optimal query strategy in a differentiable manner. Prior approaches like ALBL and SelectAL rely on non-differentiable bandit or subset-removal heuristics that are computationally expensive and fail to fully capture real-world task complexities.

## Input / Output

AutoAL takes an initial labeled seed set L and a large unlabeled pool U of RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR/SVHN, 64×64 for TinyImageNet, variable for MedMNIST) and iteratively outputs a batch of b most informative samples for labeling plus a trained ResNet-18 classifier. Notably, FitNet and SearchNet are trained exclusively on the labeled pool (split into training/validation halves), requiring no access to unlabeled labels; the unlabeled pool is only scored by the optimized SearchNet at selection time.

## Method

AutoAL introduces a bi-level optimization framework with two co-trained networks: FitNet (lower level) models the labeled data distribution via cross-entropy minimization, while SearchNet (upper level) learns per-sample informativeness scores that aggregate K candidate AL strategies (e.g., BALD, KMeans, Margin Sampling). The key differentiability trick relaxes the discrete strategy-selection problem into a continuous space by applying a sigmoid over strategy mixing weights, so the combined score S̄(x_j) = Σ_κ [λ·σ(Θ_κ^(j))]·Ŝ_κ(x_j, Ω_S) is fully back-propagatable. A Gaussian Mixture Model fuses the individual strategy scores, and a loss-prediction module (à la Yoo & Kweon) regularizes SearchNet to select exactly b samples per round. SearchNet simulates AL selection by treating the training half as the 'labeled pool' and the validation half as the 'unlabeled pool', enabling end-to-end gradient-based strategy optimization without querying the actual unlabeled data during training.

## Results

The main accuracy results are presented as labeled-data-vs-accuracy curves (Figure 2) across seven datasets, where AutoAL consistently achieves the highest accuracy at every AL round compared to all 14 baselines, with notably smaller standard deviations across three repeated runs. The runtime analysis (Table 2) shows AutoAL's total cost ranges from 2.95× (TissueMNIST) to 6.54× (OrganCMNIST) relative to Entropy Sampling, with the AL strategy score computation being the dominant time cost while SearchNet/FitNet updates are nearly negligible. On challenging datasets like CIFAR-100 (100 classes), TinyImageNet (200 classes), and OrganCMNIST (small pool of 12,975 images), AutoAL maintains its advantage, and its accuracy curves are smoother than baselines, indicating fewer harmful selections.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AutoAL outperforms all 14 baselines across all seven datasets in terms of final and per-round accuracy, as shown in Figure 2. Among the simple uncertainty-based baselines (Maximum Entropy, Margin Sampling, Least Confidence), AutoAL consistently leads, with Margin Sampling notably underperforming on SVHN and Least Confidence struggling on OrganCMNIST. Against diversity-based methods (KMeans, CoreSet), AutoAL wins on most datasets, though KMeans is competitive in early AL rounds. Among deep AL methods (BADGE, LPL, VAAL, ENSvarR, DDU), AutoAL achieves the highest accuracy with smoother curves and lower variance; VAAL and KMeans yield the worst performance on OrganCMNIST and CIFAR-10 respectively. Compared to the closest selective-AL baseline ALBL (Hsu & Lin, 2015), AutoAL achieves better accuracy with lower computational cost, since its differentiable gradient-based optimization avoids the multi-armed bandit overhead. The runtime table confirms that while AutoAL's total cost is 3–6.5× a single strategy, the SearchNet/FitNet update step is nearly free, and reducing the candidate pool to 3 strategies brings the total cost down to ~1.3× while retaining most of the accuracy gain.

## Relation to Prior Work

AutoAL extends the bilevel optimization formulation introduced by Sener & Savarese (2017) for CoreSet selection, repurposing it not for coreset construction but for differentiable AL strategy search. It departs from prior strategy-selection methods (ALBL's multi-armed bandit, SelectAL's subset-removal estimation, Zhang et al.'s noisy-reward bandit) by relaxing the discrete strategy choice into a continuous space via sigmoid-weighted mixing, enabling gradient-based optimization. The loss prediction module from Yoo & Kweon (2019) and the Gaussian Mixture score aggregation from Zhao et al. (2020) are incorporated as auxiliary components within the SearchNet architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AutoAL provides a principled, data-driven mechanism for automatically selecting among competing AL strategies without manual tuning, which is practically valuable in real-world annotation pipelines where the optimal strategy depends on unknown data characteristics. However, the framework is validated only on image classification with ResNet-18 and a fixed set of seven candidate strategies, leaving open whether the bilevel formulation scales to more complex tasks (segmentation, structured prediction) or larger candidate pools. The ~4× runtime overhead over a single strategy also limits its applicability in compute-constrained settings, and the paper does not explore whether the learned strategy weights transfer across domains or require retraining per dataset.
