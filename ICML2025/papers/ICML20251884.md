## Problem

Standard denoising diffusion models (DDMs) sample noise from a continuous Gaussian distribution at each reverse step, yielding an unbounded representation space that is highly redundant. The paper asks whether this space can be replaced by a discrete, finite set of pre-defined noise vectors without sacrificing generative quality, and whether such a discrete representation can simultaneously serve as a compact bit-stream for lossy image compression and compressed conditional generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251884/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

For image compression, the method takes an RGB image (512×512 or 768×768) and produces a reconstructed image along with a compressed bit-stream consisting of a sequence of (T−1) codebook indices, each requiring log₂(K) bits. For compressed conditional generation (e.g., face restoration), the input is a degraded image and the output is a restored image plus its bit-stream; for text-conditional generation, the input is a text prompt. In latent-space variants (e.g., Stable Diffusion 2.1), the image is first encoded into the VAE latent domain, all operations occur there, and the result is decoded back to pixel space. The bit-stream length is (T−1)·log₂(K) bits in the base scheme, or (T−1)(log₂(K)·M + C(M−1)) bits when matching pursuit refinement is used for higher rates.

## Method

DDCM replaces the continuous Gaussian noise z_i in the DDPM reverse sampling equation with a selection from a pre-defined codebook C_i of K fixed iid Gaussian vectors, constructed once and held constant for the model's lifetime. For unconditional generation, indices are drawn uniformly at random; for compression, each index k_i is chosen to maximize the inner product between the codebook entry and the residual x_0 − x̂_{0|i}, steering the denoising trajectory toward the target image. For general compressed conditional generation, indices minimize a loss L(y, x_i, C_i, k), and the paper proves (Proposition 6.1) that with the posterior-score loss L_P, the process converges to a probability-flow ODE over p_0(x_0|y) as K→∞. Higher bit rates are achieved via a matching-pursuit refinement that expresses each selected noise as a convex combination of M codebook entries with C quantized scalar coefficients. The entire scheme is training-free and compatible with any pre-trained DDM (pixel-space ImageNet or latent-space Stable Diffusion 2.1), with bit rate controlled by codebook size K and the number of active timesteps T.

## Results

On Kodak24 (512×512), DDCM achieves FID=24.2 at 0.095 BPP, outperforming PerCo (SD) (FID=26.4 at 0.127 BPP) and PSC-P (FID=35.3 at 0.095 BPP) while also attaining lower LPIPS (0.138 vs. 0.145 and 0.266) and higher PSNR (23.6 vs. 22.3 and 21.7). On ImageNet 256×256, DDCM reaches FID=36.9 at 0.122 BPP versus PSC-P's FID=44.6 at 0.155 BPP. For zero-shot image restoration (colorization, 4× SR), DDCM achieves lower FID than both DPS and DDNM while maintaining competitive PSNR. For real-world face restoration on WIDER-Test, DDCM optimizes NIQE, CLIP-IQA+, and TOPIQ-FACE effectively and generalizes well on FD_DINOv2, outperforming PMRF, DifFace, and BFRffusion on perceptual quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DDCM dominates at low-to-moderate bit rates: at ~0.095 BPP on Kodak24, it achieves FID=24.2 vs. PSC-P (35.3), PerCo (SD) (26.4 at 0.127 BPP), and ILLM (28.6 at 0.159 BPP), while also beating them on LPIPS. Against BPG and HiFiC, DDCM achieves substantially lower FID at comparable BPP (e.g., FID=24.2 vs. BPG's 114.3 at 0.088 BPP), though BPG and HiFiC retain higher PSNR at their operating points. At the highest bit rates (>0.5 BPP), DDCM underperforms ILLM and CRDR-R on PSNR due to the latent-space VAE encoder-decoder distortion bound, though it still matches or beats them on FID. For image restoration, DDCM achieves lower FID than DPS and DDNM (and their compressed versions) on colorization and 4× SR, at a slight PSNR cost consistent with the perception-distortion tradeoff. For face restoration on WIDER-Test, DDCM outperforms PMRF, DifFace, and BFRffusion on all perceptual quality metrics (NIQE, CLIP-IQA+, TOPIQ-FACE, FD_DINOv2) while showing slightly worse distortion. The method also outperforms IPIC on FID at low BPP on ImageNet (FID=36.9 at 0.122 BPP vs. IPIC's 38.4 at 0.148 BPP), though IPIC edges ahead at the highest BPP (FID=20.7 vs. 22.9 at ~1.0 BPP).

## Relation to Prior Work

DDCM directly modifies the DDPM reverse sampling equation by replacing continuous Gaussian noise draws with selections from fixed, pre-sampled codebooks of iid Gaussian vectors, requiring no retraining of the underlying diffusion model. The theoretical grounding draws on score-based SDE formulations (Song et al., 2020), showing that a specific noise-selection loss converges to a probability flow ODE over the posterior as codebook size grows. For higher bit rates, the paper borrows the matching pursuit framework (Mallat & Zhang, 1993) to refine each selected noise as a convex combination of multiple codebook entries. Conceptually, the work departs from prior zero-shot diffusion compression methods (PSC, Theis et al.) that require thousands of denoising operations or complex communication schemes, and from GAN-based compressed generation (Kang et al., Rajesh et al.) by offering a training-free, DDM-compatible discrete representation space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that extremely small codebooks (K=64, sometimes K=16) preserve DDPM-level generative quality challenges the implicit assumption that the continuous Gaussian noise space is necessary, and opens the door to interpreting diffusion sampling as an autoregressive 'language' over discrete image tokens. The compressed conditional generation framework unifies image compression, restoration, and editing under a single index-selection paradigm, though the paper acknowledges the lack of a theoretical explanation for why simple inner-product selection works so well. Key limitations include the VAE distortion ceiling for latent-space models at high bit rates, the absence of entropy coding on the selected indices, and the fact that codebooks are fixed rather than learned. The tokenization perspective suggests promising future directions, such as training a transformer to predict codebook index sequences from text, effectively creating a new image tokenization scheme.
