## Problem

Despite the growing need for natural-language interaction with time-series data in domains like industrial monitoring and medical diagnostics, no standardized benchmark or architecture exists for Time-Series Question Answering. Existing cross-modal work focuses on vision-language, while time-series–text approaches treat language as an auxiliary feature rather than enabling direct, open-ended QA over raw temporal signals whose semantics are governed by multi-scale dependencies and continuous dynamics.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252509/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes multivariate time-series segments (33 sensor channels × 600 time steps per cycle, from NCMAPSS aero-engine data) and a natural-language question as input, producing a natural-language answer. Understanding and Decision-Making tasks yield open-ended responses evaluated by BLEU/Rouge-L, while Perception and Reasoning tasks produce multiple-choice answers evaluated by Accuracy/F1. The time-series is consumed via a PatchTST encoder (patch length 60, stride 60), and the LLM (Qwen2.5 0.5B/3B/7B) remains fully frozen throughout training.

## Method

ITFormer is a lightweight 2-layer, 8-head connector that bridges any time-series encoder with a frozen LLM using four mechanisms: (1) Time Token Position Encoding adds sinusoidal, learnable-channel, and rotary segment-level positional signals to encoder outputs; (2) Learnable Instruct Tokens are prepended to the tokenized query and refined via self-attention to produce condensed task-specific instructions; (3) Instruct Time Attention performs two-stage cross-modal fusion—first aggregating channel dimensions guided by the instruct tokens, then attending over the temporal dimension—to yield a compact fused representation; (4) Time Token as Language replaces placeholder tokens in the query embedding with the fused temporal tokens, making them directly consumable by the frozen LLM decoder. Training uses supervised fine-tuning with cross-entropy loss, updating only the alignment module (~0.07% of total parameters) while keeping both the time-series encoder and LLM frozen.

## Results

On EngineMT-QA, ITFormer-7B achieves the best performance across all four tasks: Understanding (Rouge-L 58.04, BLEU 38.23), Perception (Accuracy 65.07, F1 68.36), Reasoning (Accuracy 88.69, F1 88.69), and Decision-Making (Rouge-L 56.62, BLEU 38.68). Even the smallest variant, ITFormer-0.5B, surpasses all baselines on Reasoning (80.18% accuracy vs. 51.64% for the best baseline InstructBlip). On the domain-agnostic TimeSeriesExam benchmark, ITFormer with transfer learning from EngineMT-QA reaches 0.86 on Pattern Recognition, 0.89 on Anomaly Detection, and 0.83 on Causality Analysis, outperforming GPT-4o, Gemini-Pro, and MCAN-VQA across all five sub-tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ITFormer-7B dominates all seven baseline groups across every metric on EngineMT-QA. Against multimodal APIs, the gap is stark: ChatGPT-4o scores only 3.30 BLEU on Decision-Making and 39.53 F1 on Reasoning, while ITFormer-7B reaches 38.68 BLEU and 88.69 F1 respectively. Among vision-text models adapted with a PatchTST encoder, MCAN-VQA is the strongest baseline (48.52 Rouge-L on Understanding, 54.78% on Reasoning), yet ITFormer-7B still leads by 9.52 Rouge-L points on Understanding and 33.91 accuracy points on Reasoning. Time-series-text models (Time-LLM, AutoTime) underperform most severely on generative tasks (Decision BLEU: 11.51 and 9.80 vs. 38.68 for ITFormer-7B), reflecting their limited generative capacity. Notably, even ITFormer-0.5B already surpasses InstructBlip and CoCa on Reasoning (80.18% vs. 51.64% and 50.21% accuracy), demonstrating that the lightweight alignment architecture (<1% trainable parameters) is the primary driver of performance rather than LLM scale alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of time-series analysis and LLM-based question answering, a space the authors argue has been largely unexplored. Prior time-series-text work (Time-LLM, AutoTime, ChatTime) treats natural language as an auxiliary signal to improve forecasting or classification, whereas ITFormer inverts this by treating time-series as the primary modality to be queried and interpreted through natural language. The architecture repurposes the frozen-LLM-plus-lightweight-adapter paradigm popularized in vision-language work (InstructBLIP, BLIP-2) by replacing the vision encoder with a time-series encoder (PatchTST) and introducing domain-specific alignment mechanisms (TPE, LIT, ITA, TAL) tailored to the hierarchical structure of multivariate temporal signals.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The introduction of a standardized Time-Series QA benchmark (EngineMT-QA) and a modular framework that connects arbitrary time-series encoders to arbitrary frozen LLMs with <1% trainable parameters provides a practical template for building interactive, language-driven interfaces over temporal data in industrial, medical, and scientific settings. However, the dataset is domain-specific (aero engines from NCMAPSS) and relatively modest in scale (11k pairs), limiting immediate generalizability; the paper's own generalization experiments on TimeSeriesExam are encouraging but involve only five canonical sub-tasks. Open questions include whether the approach scales to irregularly-sampled or streaming time series, how performance degrades with more complex multi-step reasoning, and whether the frozen-LLM assumption holds as time-series-specific LLMs mature.
