## Problem

Generating privacy-preserving synthetic text data via DP finetuning of billion-scale LLMs is computationally prohibitive for resource-constrained (e.g., on-device) settings, while prompt-based alternatives like Private Evolution require domain-specific prompt engineering, rely on LLM creativity, and only exploit private data through embedding-space nearest-neighbor selection, limiting their ability to capture fine-grained word-level distributions and constraining synthetic dataset size.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252327/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a private corpus of text documents (e.g., medical abstracts, chat dialogues, reviews) and produces an arbitrary number of synthetic text documents without additional privacy cost, leveraging the post-processing property of DP. The private data is consumed in two forms: a differentially private topic histogram (1,300 bins with Gaussian noise) capturing high-level distributional proportions, and a DP-finetuned 140M generator that retains fine-grained textual patterns. Unlike PE-based methods whose synthetic size is bounded by DP-NN noise tolerance, CTCL can generate 400K+ examples in a single pass.

## Method

CTCL combines two pre-trained components: a 140M-parameter BART-base conditional generator (pre-trained on 430M condition-document pairs from SlimPajama, where conditions are multi-aspect descriptions extracted by Gemma-2-2B) and a universal topic model (HDBSCAN clustering of 6M Wikipedia document embeddings yielding 1,300 topics, each annotated with 10 KeyBERT keywords). To adapt to a private domain, the topic model assigns each private document to a topic and constructs a DP histogram of topic proportions, while the generator is DP-finetuned (DP-Adam, 2000 steps, batch 4096) on (10-keywords, document) pairs so that the conditioning format aligns with pretraining. At generation time, the DP-finetuned generator is sampled proportionally per topic according to the DP histogram, using the topic's 10 keywords as the conditioning input. The key design insight is that pretraining on diverse free-form conditions makes the small generator robust to DP training noise, and the separation of distributional (histogram) and textual (finetuning) information lets a 140M model match or exceed billion-scale DP-finetuned baselines under strict privacy budgets.

## Results

On the primary generative task (PubMed, evaluated by next-word prediction accuracy of downstream BERT models), CTCL achieves 35.9% (BERTMini) and 38.1% (BERTSmall) at ε=4, substantially outperforming BARTBase DP finetuning (30.5%/32.4%) and AUG-PE+GPT-3.5 (30.3%/32.5%), while approaching the GPT2XL-1.5B upper bound (37.7%/40.5%). Under the strictest privacy constraint ε=1, CTCL retains 34.5%/36.7%, compared to 26.7%/28.5% for BARTBase and 30.1%/32.4% for AUG-PE. On classification tasks, CTCL achieves 46.2% accuracy on OpenReview at ε=1, far exceeding BARTBase (25.7%) and AUG-PE (43.1%), and 67.7% on Yelp at ε=1, competitive with the GPT2XL-1.5B upper bound (68.2%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against vanilla DP finetuning (Yue et al., 2023), CTCL wins decisively on all generative tasks: on PubMed at ε=4, CTCL scores 35.9% vs. 30.5% for BARTBase and 35.0% for GPT2Small; on Multi-Session Chat at ε=1, the gap widens to 27.6% vs. 10.8% (BARTBase) and 15.1% (GPT2Small). The post-generation resampling variant (Yu et al., 2024) narrows the gap slightly (e.g., 35.3% vs. 35.9% on PubMed at ε=4) but still trails CTCL, especially under tight budgets. Against the Private Evolution family, CTCL achieves a consistent 5–6 point advantage on generative tasks (e.g., 35.9% vs. 30.3% for AUG-PE+GPT-3.5 on PubMed at ε=4) and a 3-point lead on OpenReview classification at ε=1 (46.2% vs. 43.1%); notably, PE methods show almost no improvement as ε increases, while CTCL benefits from larger budgets. On classification tasks (Yelp, OpenReview), CTCL matches or slightly exceeds the best baselines at ε=∞ but pulls ahead under privacy constraints, particularly on OpenReview where BARTBase collapses to 25.7% at ε=1 while CTCL holds at 46.2%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of two existing lines of work: DP finetuning of language models for synthetic data (Yue et al. 2023; Yu et al. 2024; Kurakin et al. 2023), which typically relies on billion-scale backbones, and LLM API prompting frameworks like Private Evolution (Lin et al. 2024; Xie et al. 2024), which avoid finetuning but require domain-specific prompts and API access. CTCL departs from both by showing that a 140M-parameter BART-base, when pretrained on 430M condition-document pairs and conditioned on topic keywords from a universal HDBSCAN-based topic model, can match or exceed billion-scale DP finetuning while consuming only a small fraction of the privacy budget for the topic histogram. The conditional generation design draws on metadata-conditioning ideas, while the DP histogram for distributional alignment follows the post-generation resampling concept of Yu et al. (2024), but repurposes it as a sampling guide rather than a filtering step.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that CTCL makes DP synthetic data generation feasible in resource-constrained settings (e.g., on-device federated learning) where finetuning billion-scale LLMs is infeasible, and removes the need for domain experts to craft prompts. A key limitation acknowledged by the authors is that the 140M generator's outputs still lack the fluency and coherence of billion-scale LLMs, and the paper explicitly flags leveraging LLMs in resource-constrained settings as an open problem. The finding that PE-based methods do not benefit from increased privacy budgets (their performance is essentially flat across ε values) raises a broader question about whether the DP-NN selection mechanism is fundamentally capacity-limited, independent of the underlying LLM quality. Future work on multi-modal extension (e.g., diffusion models for images) is noted but not explored.
