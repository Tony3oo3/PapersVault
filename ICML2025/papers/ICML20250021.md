## Problem

Federated learning suffers from model inconsistency when clients have non-IID data distributions and heterogeneous computational capabilities (varying learning rates and epoch counts). Prior methods like FedProx, FedNova, and FedExp address one challenge or the other but lack a unified framework that synchronizes asynchronous client updates while accounting for data heterogeneity in the aggregation step.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250021/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each client provides its local dataset (partitioned via Dirichlet distribution), a precomputed constant sensitivity model G̃_th^i derived from averaged Hessian over a data subset, and its final local state x_i(T_i) along with its total simulation time T_i = Σ Δt_i^k after e_i epochs. The central agent produces updated global model parameters x_c and flow variables I_L^i at each communication round, with the adaptive step-size Δ_t selected to keep the Backward Euler local truncation error below a tolerance δ. The sensitivity model is computed once before training and not updated during the run.

## Method

FedECADO reformulates federated learning as a continuous-time ODE (gradient flow) and models it as an equivalent circuit where node voltages are state variables and gradients are current sources, with inductors coupling the central agent to each client. Two key mechanisms address heterogeneity: (1) an aggregate sensitivity model based on Thevenin impedance (G_th^i = 1/Δt + p_i·H̄^i) that scales each client's first-order response by its relative dataset size p_i, improving Gauss-Seidel convergence and capturing non-IID effects; and (2) a multi-rate integration scheme that uses a linear interpolation/extrapolation operator Γ to estimate client states at synchronized intermediate time points τ, then solves the coupled central-agent ODEs via Backward Euler with adaptive step-size selection driven by local truncation error bounds. The paper proves the consensus step is a contraction mapping in a weighted β-norm, guaranteeing convergence to a stationary point regardless of the ratio of client step-sizes.

## Results

On the most challenging combined setting (ResNet-34 on CIFAR-100 with non-IID Dirichlet data and heterogeneous learning rates across 100 clients), FedECADO achieves 81.3% (±4.8) mean classification accuracy after 200 epochs, outperforming all baselines by 8.9–20.0 percentage points. On VGG-11/CIFAR-10 with non-IID Dirichlet data, FedECADO reaches 57.8% (±3.6) accuracy, a 7% improvement over FedNova and 13% over FedProx. Under heterogeneous computation (random learning rates and epochs) with IID data, FedECADO achieves 72.2% (±3.8), demonstrating that the multi-rate integration alone contributes meaningful gains. Across all four datasets (CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, TinyImageNet, Sentiment140) and model architectures (VGG-11, ResNet-18, ResNet-34, LSTM), FedECADO consistently attains the highest accuracy with competitive or lower variance than baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedECADO outperforms all five federated learning baselines across every experimental setting. On ResNet-34/CIFAR-100 (Table 3), FedECADO (81.3%) surpasses FedRS (72.3%) by 9.0 points, FedProx (70.6%) by 10.7 points, FedNova (69.5%) by 11.8 points, FedExp (68.6%) by 12.7 points, and FedDecorr (61.3%) by 20.0 points. On VGG-11/CIFAR-10 with non-IID data (Table 1), the margins are 8.9 points over FedNova, 13.5 over FedProx, 12.5 over FedExp, 12.5 over FedDecorr, and 11.7 over FedRS. Under heterogeneous computation with IID data (Table 2), FedECADO (72.2%) leads FedRS (69.3%) by 2.9 points, FedNova (67.6%) by 4.6 points, and FedProx (61.4%) by 10.8 points, confirming the multi-rate integration's independent contribution. FedECADO also achieves lower standard deviation than most baselines (e.g., 4.8 vs. 10.2 for FedExp on CIFAR-100), indicating more stable convergence. Compared to the original ECADO (Agarwal and Pileggi, 2023), which assumed full client participation and uniform step sizes, FedECADO achieves higher classification accuracies by addressing intermittent participation and heterogeneous computation through the aggregate sensitivity model and multi-rate integration.

## Relation to Prior Work

FedECADO extends the equivalent circuit approach to distributed optimization (ECADO, Agarwal and Pileggi 2023) from homogeneous, fully-participating settings to the federated learning regime, where clients are intermittent, computationally heterogeneous, and operate on non-IID data. Two new mechanisms are introduced: an aggregate sensitivity model (analogous to Thevenin impedance in circuit theory) that weights client updates by relative dataset size, and a multi-rate integration scheme with a linear interpolation/extrapolation operator that synchronizes asynchronous client updates on a common continuous-time axis. The paper positions itself against the standard heterogeneous-FL baselines (FedProx, FedNova, FedExp, FedDecorr, FedRS), which address non-IID data or heterogeneous computation through discrete-time corrections (proximal penalties, gradient rescaling, extrapolation) rather than through a unified continuous-time dynamical system formulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The circuit-simulation lens offers a principled, physically interpretable framework for reasoning about federated learning heterogeneity, and the multi-rate integration with adaptive step-size selection provides a convergence guarantee (contraction mapping) that is not typically available in heuristic aggregation schemes. However, the aggregate sensitivity model requires periodic Hessian estimation (even if averaged over a subset), which may be prohibitive for very large models or memory-constrained edge devices. Open questions include whether the continuous-time framing yields practical advantages at scale (thousands of clients, ImageNet-class datasets), how sensitive performance is to the quality of the Hessian approximation, and whether the added algorithmic complexity (LU factorization, backtracking line search, interpolation) is justified over simpler discrete-time corrections in production federated systems.
