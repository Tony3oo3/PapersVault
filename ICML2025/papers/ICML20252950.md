## Problem

Existing MoE routing strategies for diffusion transformers (token-choice and expert-choice) impose fixed degrees of freedom in expert-token allocation, failing to adaptively assign more experts to spatially complex regions or temporally demanding denoising timesteps. This rigidity limits the scalability benefits of MoE in diffusion models, where generation difficulty varies significantly across both image regions and noise levels.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252950/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a class label and a noisy latent (Gaussian noise mixed with the target at a sampled timestep) as input, and outputs a 256×256 RGB image on ImageNet. A key operational detail is the training-inference asymmetry: during training, each sample in a batch is assigned a random timestep, while at inference all samples share a uniform timestep. This mismatch is handled by an EMA-updated learnable threshold that replaces the batch-dependent k-th value selection at inference time.

## Method

Race-DiT replaces the MLP in DiT with MoE blocks and introduces Expert Race, a routing strategy that performs a single global top-K selection across the entire B×L×E gating score tensor (setting D_A=1 in the unified routing formulation), allowing any token to be assigned to any number of experts and any expert to process any number of tokens. To stabilize this high-flexibility routing, the method employs identity gating (instead of softmax) to preserve cross-token score ordering, an EMA-based learnable threshold to bridge the training-inference distribution gap, a router similarity loss that minimizes off-diagonal elements of expert co-selection correlation matrices to prevent mode collapse, and per-layer regularization that adds auxiliary prediction heads at each layer to counteract the AdaLN-induced weakening of shallow-layer gradients. The architecture uses fine-grained expert segmentation where each expert's hidden dimension is divided by the top-k activation count, keeping activated parameters constant while expanding the candidate pool.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256, RaceDiT-XL/2-4in32 achieves FID 2.06, IS 318.64, Precision 0.83, and Recall 0.60 with only 0.707B activated parameters (2.788B total) trained for 1.7M×1024 samples. Under the identical training protocol, it outperforms the vanilla DiT-XL/2* baseline (FID 3.02, IS 261.49, Recall 0.51) by approximately 0.96 FID points. The MoE variant also surpasses the XL-Dense model (FID 6.31) with less than half the total parameters, demonstrating strong scaling efficiency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the same-protocol DiT-XL/2* baseline (1.7M×1024 samples), RaceDiT-XL/2-4in32 wins on every metric: FID 2.06 vs 3.02, IS 318.64 vs 261.49, Precision 0.83 vs 0.81, and Recall 0.60 vs 0.51. Compared to DiT-MoE-XL/2 (the strongest MoE baseline), RaceDiT achieves a higher FID (2.06 vs 1.72) but uses less than half the activated parameters (0.707B vs 1.530B) and roughly 4× fewer training samples (1.7M vs 7.0M iterations). Against SiT-XL, RaceDiT matches the FID (2.06) while achieving substantially higher IS (318.64 vs 277.50) and better Recall (0.60 vs 0.59) with fewer total training samples. RaceDiT also outperforms DiT-XL/2 (FID 2.06 vs 2.27, IS 318.64 vs 278.24), MaskDiT (FID 2.06 vs 2.50), and MDT (FID 2.06 vs 2.15) on both FID and IS, establishing it as the most parameter-efficient method in the comparison.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the DiT architecture (Peebles & Xie, 2023) by replacing its MLP blocks with MoE layers, and generalizes the Expert-Choice routing paradigm (Zhou et al., 2022) into a unified top-K selection framework that spans batch, sequence, and expert dimensions simultaneously. The MoE layer design and sparse gating paradigm follow Switch Transformers (Fedus et al., 2022), but the key departure is the 'Expert Race' strategy, which performs a single global top-K selection rather than per-row selections, along with a router similarity loss that extends conventional balance loss from individual experts to expert-pair co-selection patterns. The per-layer regularization addresses a previously unacknowledged training pathology in adaLN-based DiT where shallow-layer contributions are progressively suppressed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that routing flexibility is a first-order factor in MoE performance for diffusion models, with a clean monotonic improvement from token-choice → expert-choice → expert-race, providing a principled design axis for future MoE-diffusion work. The identity-gating finding (outperforming softmax) and the per-layer regularization (FID 11.56→8.95) are practical insights likely to transfer to other diffusion architectures. Limitations include evaluation only on ImageNet 256×256 class-conditional generation, inference-time computational variance from dynamic expert activation, and the EMA threshold mechanism that couples training and inference behavior. Open questions include whether Expert Race scales to text-to-image, video, or 3D generation, and whether the routing flexibility advantage persists at much larger model scales or with different noise schedules.
