## Problem

Existing anchor-based multi-view subspace clustering methods fail to guarantee the robustness of learned consensus anchors, do not model the specific local distribution of clusters within affine subspaces, and cannot simultaneously learn robust consensus anchors and the common cluster structure shared across views. This prevents mutual enhancement between anchor learning and partitioning, yielding less discriminative anchors and cluster indicators.

## Input / Output

The method takes v multi-view data matrices {X^p ∈ R^{d_p × n}} along with hyperparameters λ (Frobenius penalty), β (partition coupling), and the number of clusters k; the anchor count l is tuned over {2k, 3k, …, 7k}. It directly outputs a binary cluster assignment matrix F ∈ R^{k×n} (one-hot per column) and a consensus anchor graph S ∈ R^{l×n}, eliminating the need for a post-processing step such as spectral clustering. The shared dimension d across views is also a tunable parameter.

## Method

RCSC formulates a joint optimization over view-specific orthogonal projections U^p, a shared orthogonal anchor matrix A, an affinity matrix S, a centroid matrix G, and a binary assignment F, with the reconstruction term ‖X^p − U^p A S‖_F² regularized by a Frobenius penalty on S and coupled to partitioning via ‖S − GF‖_F². Three constraints on S—non-negativity, column-sum-to-one (affine), and the Frobenius penalty—collectively restrict each data point's representation to a specific local distribution in the affine subspace, guaranteeing robustness of the learned consensus anchors. A key theoretical result (Theorem 1) proves that under independent subspace assumptions and orthogonal constraints, the optimal S is block-diagonal and unique, while Theorem 2 establishes a grouping effect: highly correlated basis vectors receive nearly identical coefficients, encouraging anchors to cluster together. The problem is solved by alternating minimization: U^p, A, and G are updated via SVD of their respective cross-product matrices, S is updated column-wise by solving a quadratic program, F is assigned by nearest-centroid matching, and view weights α_p are computed in closed form via a Cauchy–Schwarz argument. The overall complexity is near-linear in n since l ≪ n and k ≪ n.

## Results

RCSC achieves the best ACC on all eight multi-view datasets, with headline values of 65.20% on ORL, 85.60% on Mfeat, 67.40% on Caltech101-20, and 27.80% on YouTubeFace. On NMI, RCSC leads on six of eight datasets (e.g., 90.00% on ORL, 85.32% on Mfeat) but trails FPMVS on Caltech101-20 (52.40% vs. 63.30%) and BMVC on Caltech101-all (31.30% vs. 42.50%). On F1-score, RCSC is best on all eight datasets, reaching 65.00% on ORL and 18.50% on YouTubeFace. Notably, several baselines (AMGL, SFMC) are infeasible (out of memory) on the three largest datasets (NUSWIDEOBJ, AWA, YouTubeFace), while RCSC remains computationally tractable.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RCSC outperforms all eight baselines on ACC across every dataset, with the largest margins on large-scale data: +2.50% over OMSC on NUSWIDEOBJ (23.50% vs. 21.00%), +1.50% over OMSC on AWA (10.50% vs. 9.00%), and +1.30% over OMSC on YouTubeFace (27.80% vs. 26.50%). On NMI, RCSC leads on six of eight datasets but is notably outperformed by FPMVS on Caltech101-20 (52.40% vs. 63.30%) and by BMVC on Caltech101-all (31.30% vs. 42.50%), suggesting the Frobenius-norm regularization may over-smooth the affinity on these particular benchmarks. On F1-score, RCSC is best on all eight datasets, confirming consistent gains. The scalability advantage is clear: AMGL and SFMC are infeasible (N/A) on NUSWIDEOBJ, AWA, and YouTubeFace, while RCSC completes within comparable runtime to the most efficient anchor-based method MSGL. Among anchor-based competitors (LMVSC, MSGL, FPMVS, EOMSC-CA), RCSC consistently achieves the highest ACC, validating the joint learning of robust consensus anchors and cluster structure in a unified framework.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the consensus anchor framework for multi-view subspace clustering introduced by Wang et al. (IEEE TIP 2022), which uses view-specific projection matrices and latent consensus anchors to build a unified anchor graph, and the nonnegative-orthogonal factorization mechanism from Yang et al. (IEEE TIP 2021) for direct cluster assignment. The key departure is the simultaneous imposition of Frobenius norm, non-negativity, and affine constraints on the shared affinity matrix, combined with a theoretical guarantee that the resulting anchor graph is block-diagonal under independent subspace assumptions. Unlike prior anchor-based methods that either fix anchors via K-means or learn them separately from cluster structure, RCSC jointly optimizes robust anchor learning, partition, and graph construction in a single alternating framework, enabling mutual enhancement between the two.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The near-linear computational complexity makes RCSC practical for large-scale multi-view datasets where n×n graph methods fail (as evidenced by multiple 'N/A' entries for baselines on NUSWIDEOBJ, AWA, and YouTubeFace). The theoretical results—block-diagonal structure under independent subspaces and the grouping effect bounding anchor coefficient differences by basis correlation—provide interpretable guarantees that are rare in anchor-based clustering. However, the independent subspace assumption is strong and unlikely to hold exactly in practice, and the performance gains over the strongest baselines (OMSC, EOMSC-CA) are modest (1–3% on most datasets), leaving open whether the added theoretical machinery translates to substantially better results on noisier or more heterogeneous real-world data.
