## Problem

Prediction-powered inference (PPI) has been largely restricted to Z-estimation problems such as means, quantiles, and regression coefficients, leaving many important inference tasks—change-point detection, causal discovery, anytime-valid risk monitoring—out of reach. This paper closes that gap by applying PPI to e-values, a more general inferential object that natively supports sequential, anytime-valid, and post-hoc inference, thereby extending PPI to essentially any procedure that can be framed in terms of e-values.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a data stream where cheap covariates X_i are always available and expensive labels Y_i are observed only with probability π_i(X_i) (controlled by the analyst, e.g., 0.5%–10% budget). A predictive model μ_i: X → Y and a base e-value component function e_i(·) with known bounds [a_i, b_i] are required; the model and collection rule may be updated adaptively at each step. Outputs include prediction-powered e-values E_n^ppi, from which one derives confidence sequences, hypothesis test decisions, change-point locations, or causal graph edges depending on the downstream algorithm.

## Method

The core construction replaces each e-value component e_i(Y_i) with a debiased surrogate e_i^ppi = e_i(μ_i(X_i)) + [e_i(Y_i) − e_i(μ_i(X_i))]·ξ_i/π_i(X_i), where ξ_i ~ Bern(π_i(X_i)) indicates whether the expensive label was collected. Conditional on the past, E[e_i^ppi | F_{i-1}] = E[e_i(Y_i) | F_{i-1}], so the product E_n^ppi = ∏e_i^ppi remains a valid e-value (and test supermartingale / e-process) under the null, proven via backward induction. A boundedness constraint π_i(X_i) ≥ 1 − a_i/b_i ensures non-negativity; in practice this is satisfied by truncating the betting sequences (e.g., λ_i) that define the base e-values. The sequential structure allows the predictive model μ and collection rule π to be updated at every step without invalidating the inference, and the method extends modularly to confidence sequences (by indexing e-values over a parameter space) and to arbitrary e-value-powered algorithms (by simple substitution of base e-values with their PPI counterparts, preserving validity under a mild assumption).

## Results

Across four case studies, the prediction-powered e-value method achieves substantially tighter valid inferences with 100x–200x reductions in expensive data collection. In the causal discovery experiment (6-node DAG, 3 costly variables, 10% labelling budget), the method reduces average structural Hamming distance from 12.5 (only labelled data) to 6.7, approaching the best-possible 6.4 achievable with full data. In the diabetes prevalence estimation (BRFSS 2014, 1% labelling budget), the method yields the tightest valid nonasymptotic confidence sequences among all baselines. In online risk monitoring (Covertype), the method detects distribution drift significantly faster than the only-labelled-samples baseline while maintaining anytime-validity, whereas naive imputation produces a false alarm before the null is actually violated.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against vanilla prediction-powered inference (Angelopoulos et al., 2023a), the proposed method produces tighter confidence intervals on the diabetes prevalence task despite being nonasymptotic, attributed to its ability to update the predictive model without requiring a separate data split for training. Against active prediction-powered inference (Zrnic & Candes, 2024), performance is comparable in terms of interval width, but the e-value-based approach provides strictly stronger guarantees: anytime-validity under arbitrary optional stopping and post-hoc validity, which the CLT-based confidence intervals of the baseline do not offer. Against the only-labelled-samples baseline, the method dominates across all four case studies: tighter confidence sequences, faster drift detection in online monitoring, successful change-point detection (where the baseline detects none), and recovery of over half the causal edges (where the baseline recovers zero). Against naive imputation, the method is the only valid approach: imputation's confidence interval misses the true diabetes prevalence, and its risk monitor triggers a false alarm before the null hypothesis is actually falsified.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the prediction-powered inference (PPI) framework of Angelopoulos et al. (2023) and the active data-collection variant of Zrnic & Candès (2024) by transplanting the AIPW debiasing mechanism from estimators to e-value components. Prior PPI was essentially restricted to Z-estimation problems (means, quantiles, regression coefficients), whereas here the authors show that any inference procedure expressible via e-values—sequential testing, confidence sequences, change-point detection, causal discovery—admits a valid prediction-powered counterpart. The theoretical backbone draws on the e-value and test-supermartingale literature (Howard et al., 2018; Waudby-Smith & Ramdas, 2020) and the AIPW estimator (Robins et al., 1994), with the key insight that the inverse-probability-weighting correction applied component-wise preserves the supermartingale and e-process structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The modular 'swap-in PPI e-values' recipe dramatically broadens the scope of prediction-powered inference from a narrow class of Z-estimation tasks to essentially the entire e-value-based inference toolkit, including anytime-valid sequential monitoring and post-hoc significance selection. A notable practical advantage is that the predictive model and data-collection policy can be updated online during inference, avoiding the data-split overhead of prior PPI methods. Open questions include the tightness of the power guarantees (Theorem 2.2 gives a growth-rate bound, but translating this to actual confidence-interval width remains case-specific), and the causal-discovery application relies on asymptotic p-values calibrated via PToE, a compromise that limits the nonasymptotic guarantees in that setting.
