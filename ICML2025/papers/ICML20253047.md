## Problem

Statistical adversarial data detection (SADD) methods effectively identify adversarial examples via distributional discrepancy but discard detected adversarial inputs, losing their semantic information. Meanwhile, denoiser-based defenses preserve data but cannot distinguish adversarial from clean examples beforehand, degrading clean accuracy. The paper asks whether one can leverage SADD's detection power while preserving all input data, and provides a theoretical bound showing that minimizing distributional discrepancy directly reduces adversarial risk without the extra constant present in standard domain adaptation.

## Input / Output

The method consumes batches of RGB images (minimum ~100 samples for stable detection) alongside a held-out clean validation batch that serves as the reference distribution for MMD-OPT comparison. At inference, each test batch is first screened against the validation batch; if the MMD-OPT score falls below a threshold, the batch is classified directly, otherwise it is passed through a denoiser before classification. The output is a standard class label per image, but the decision to denoise or not is made at the batch level rather than per-sample.

## Method

DAD builds on an optimized MMD statistic (MMD-OPT) whose kernel is trained to maximize test power between clean and adversarial distributions (Algorithm 1). This single statistic plays two roles: during training, it guides a DUNet denoiser by minimizing the MMD-OPT between clean samples and denoised adversarial samples plus a cross-entropy term (Algorithm 2); at inference, it acts as a batch-level discriminator comparing the test batch against a clean validation reference. The two-pronged inference then routes detected clean batches directly to a pre-trained classifier and detected adversarial batches through the denoiser before classification. Theoretically, the paper proves (Theorem 1) that adversarial risk is bounded by clean risk plus L1 distributional divergence with no additional constant, because the ground-truth labeling functions for clean and adversarial domains are identical under the threat model assumptions.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (WRN-28-10, ℓ∞ ε=8/255), DAD achieves 94.16% clean accuracy and 67.53% robust accuracy, outperforming the best AT baseline (Gowal et al. 2021: 87.51%/63.38%) and the best AP baseline (Lee & Kim 2023: 90.16%/55.82%). Under ℓ2 (ε=0.5), DAD reaches 94.16%/84.38%, surpassing the strongest AT method (Augustin et al. 2020: 93.96%/78.79%) and AP method (Lee & Kim 2023: 90.16%/83.59%). On ImageNet-1K (RN-50, ℓ∞ ε=4/255), DAD achieves 78.61% clean and 53.85% robust accuracy, improving over the best AP baseline (Lee & Kim 2023: 70.74%/42.15%) by 7.87% clean and 11.70% robust accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DAD outperforms all 21 baselines on both clean and robust accuracy across every main comparison setting. On CIFAR-10 (ℓ∞, WRN-28-10), DAD improves robust accuracy by 4.15% over the best AT method (Gowal et al. 2021: 63.38%) and by 11.71% over the best AP method (Lee & Kim 2023: 55.82%), while also achieving the highest clean accuracy (94.16% vs. 88.62% and 90.16% respectively). Under ℓ2 (ε=0.5), DAD's 84.38% robust accuracy surpasses the best AT baseline (Augustin et al. 2020: 78.79%) by 5.59% and the best AP baseline (Lee & Kim 2023: 83.59%) by 0.79%. On ImageNet-1K (ℓ∞, RN-50), the advantage is even more pronounced: DAD's 53.85% robust accuracy exceeds the best AP method (Lee & Kim 2023: 42.15%) by 11.70% and the best AT method (Salman et al. 2020a: 34.96%) by 18.89%, while clean accuracy improves by at least 7.13% over AP baselines. The paper also shows DAD generalizes to unseen transfer attacks (Table 3), maintaining 80–93% robust accuracy across WRN-70-16, RN-18, RN-50, and Swin-T under both PGD+EOT ℓ∞ and C&W ℓ2 attacks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of statistical adversarial data detection (SADD) and adversarial purification (AP). It builds directly on Gao et al. (2021) for the MMD-based detection of adversarial examples, on Liu et al. (2020) for the test-power-optimized MMD kernel (MMD-OPT), and on Liao et al. (2018) for the DUNet denoiser architecture. The key departure is twofold: theoretically, the authors show that adversarial classification is a special domain adaptation problem where the labelling functions coincide, yielding a risk bound without the extra constant C that plagues standard domain adaptation; methodologically, MMD-OPT is repurposed to serve simultaneously as a training signal for the denoiser and as an inference-time discriminator, avoiding both the data-discarding limitation of SADD and the density-estimation burden of diffusion-based AP.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The clean theoretical bound (Theorem 1) provides a principled justification for distributional-discrepancy-based defenses and explains why SADD methods are effective against adaptive attacks. Practically, DAD's 'discrimination is easier than data generation' insight makes it more scalable than diffusion-based purification, as evidenced by the 7+ point clean accuracy and 11+ point robust accuracy gains over AP baselines on ImageNet-1K. Key limitations include the batch-wise processing requirement (batch size ≥ 100 for stability), sensitivity to the MMD-OPT threshold, and the fact that mixed batches with high AE proportions still degrade performance. Open questions include whether more robust statistics can reduce the minimum viable batch size and whether the approach extends to non-image modalities or certified robustness guarantees.
