## Problem

Single-intervention physical reasoning tasks require agents to iteratively choose actions, observe outcomes, and refine their strategy, but existing methods either score actions with a dynamics model without learning from past attempts or use simple priors (e.g., SSUP's Gaussian mixture) that fail to encode how actions are causally related through their effects on the environment. This limits sample efficiency, as the agent cannot leverage the fact that two actions producing similar physical consequences should have similar scores.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 2D puzzle scene image with initial bounding boxes of all dynamic objects and the action object, plus scores from previously executed actions. The dynamics model (RPIN) consumes the image and bounding boxes to predict bounding boxes for the next n_pred=20 timesteps. The action space is hybrid continuous/discrete: x_pos and y_pos are continuous coordinates, while action_obj is discrete (one of three tool types in Virtual Tools, or a ball radius in [2,32] in PHYRE), yielding ~1M–2.5M possible actions per puzzle. The output is the next action to execute, proposed iteratively until the puzzle is solved.

## Method

Causal-PIK frames puzzle solving as Bayesian optimization over a hybrid action space, replacing the standard RBF kernel with a Physics-Informed Kernel (PIK) that encodes causal similarity between actions. The PIK first uses a trained dynamics model (RPIN) to predict the immediate state change of each dynamic object after the first collision event, then computes per-object similarity as the product of cosine and magnitude similarity of these state-change vectors, averaged over all objects and rescaled by an exponential term to accentuate differences. This kernel is proven to be symmetric and non-negative, satisfying GP kernel validity. Action selection uses a UCB acquisition function evaluated over 500 Sobol-sampled candidates, with the top 5 refined via probabilistic simulation before execution. The objective function captures 'almost' reaching the goal by using the closest distance to the goal state at any timestep, normalized against a counterfactual no-action baseline, which also helps the kernel distinguish causal effects from environmental confounds.

## Results

On the Virtual Tools benchmark (max 10 attempts), Causal-PIK achieves an AUCCESS of 65.0±25.0, outperforming the prior state-of-the-art SSUP (58.0±27.0) by 7 points and the human baseline (53.25±23) by ~12 points. On PHYRE-1B Cross (max 100 attempts, full 2.5M action space), Causal-PIK reaches 41.6±9.33 AUCCESS, surpassing the only other full-action-space method Harter et al. (30.24±8.9) by over 11 points and matching methods that use a 10K reduced action space (Ahmed et al. 41.9, RPIN 42.2). The RBF-kernel ablation drops to 42.0 on Virtual Tools and 27.7 on PHYRE, confirming the Physics-Informed Kernel is critical for performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On Virtual Tools, Causal-PIK (65.0 AUCCESS) beats the state-of-the-art SSUP (58.0) by 7 points and the human baseline (53.25) by ~12 points, while the RBF-kernel ablation (42.0) falls well below both, isolating the contribution of the Physics-Informed Kernel. On PHYRE, Causal-PIK (41.6) substantially outperforms the only comparable full-action-space baseline Harter et al. (30.24) by over 11 points, and also beats naive baselines RAND (13.0), MEM (18.5), and DQN (36.8). Notably, Causal-PIK matches or approaches methods that operate on a 10K reduced action space (Ahmed et al. 41.9, RPIN 42.2) while searching over the full 2.5M-action space, a problem the authors argue is far more realistic. When restricted to 10 attempts on PHYRE, Causal-PIK (24.8) underperforms humans (36.6), but with 100 attempts it surpasses them. Per-puzzle correlation analysis shows Causal-PIK aligns most closely with human difficulty judgments on PHYRE (r=0.73), suggesting it captures similar physical intuitions about which dynamics are easy versus hard to reason about.

## Relation to Prior Work

Causal-PIK builds on the SSUP framework (Allen et al., 2020) for initialization and probabilistic simulation-based action approximation, and on Region Proposal Interaction Networks (Qi et al., 2021) as the dynamics model architecture. Its key departure from prior PHYRE/Virtual Tools methods (Girdhar et al., Ahmed et al., Qi et al.) is that instead of using a dynamics model to directly score and rank actions, it feeds dynamics predictions into a custom Physics-Informed Kernel that encodes causal similarity between actions, which then drives a Gaussian Process surrogate within a Bayesian optimization loop. This repurposes the standard BO kernel — typically an RBF assuming geometric proximity implies similarity — into a causality-aware metric that clusters actions by their predicted physical effects on the environment rather than their coordinate distance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Physics-Informed Kernel offers a general principle for injecting domain causal structure into Bayesian optimization, potentially applicable beyond 2D physics puzzles to any setting where actions have interpretable causal effects. The demonstrated robustness to noisy dynamics predictions (only 4 AUCCESS points lost when L2 error increases ~5×) suggests viability for sim-to-real transfer where perfect dynamics models are unavailable. Key open questions include cross-task knowledge transfer, scaling beyond the 3D action space, and whether the causal-similarity kernel formulation generalizes to multi-intervention or continuous-control settings. The method's narrow scope (single-intervention, 2D, small puzzle sets) limits immediate broader impact but establishes a clear template for physics-informed search.
