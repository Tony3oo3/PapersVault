## Problem

Current Video Large Language Models rely on a single visual encoder for all visual processing, which inherently limits the breadth of visual information they can capture. Different encoder families excel at different aspects—spatial understanding, temporal dynamics, or vision-language grounding—yet no prior VideoLLM effectively combines multiple specialized encoders into a unified representation. The paper asks whether and how multiple heterogeneous visual encoders can be integrated to produce a comprehensive video representation that outperforms any single-encoder baseline.

## Input / Output

The method accepts a video clip (16 uniformly sampled frames at 224×224; ViViT uses 32 frames but produces a 16-frame output) and a text query, treating images as single-frame videos. Each frame is independently processed by four parallel encoders, and the resulting features are spatio-temporally aligned to a common shape before fusion. The output is a free-form text response generated by LLaMA-2 7B, with the unified visual embedding concatenated to the tokenized text prompt in a PrefixLM-style setup.

## Method

MERV integrates four specialized encoders—DINOv2 (spatial), ViViT (temporal), SigLIP (image-language contrastive), and LanguageBind (video-language contrastive)—through a three-stage pipeline: (1) spatio-temporal alignment via adaptive 2D average pooling to a shared spatial resolution and matched temporal length, (2) projection to a common embedding dimension via per-encoder linear layers, and (3) cross-attention fusion where a single learnable query attends to sequence-averaged keys from all encoders and full-length values, producing an additive mixture weighted by visual content. The fused embedding is concatenated with tokenized text and fed to a frozen LLaMA-2 7B. Training uses FSDP to parallelize the four visual encoders, keeping step time comparable to single-encoder baselines, and the default recipe (MERV frozen) trains only the projectors and fusion module on Stage-2 instruction data for one epoch, achieving results in 43% of the time of the full two-stage Video-LLaVA recipe.

## Results

MERV (frozen) achieves up to 4.62% higher accuracy than Video-LLaVA on TVQA (42.28% vs. 37.66%), 3.79% on ActivityNet-QA (50.87% vs. 47.08%), and 3.48% on NExT-QA (63.09% vs. 59.61%), while using the same training data mixture. MERV (full) further improves Perception Test to 48.41%, surpassing SeViLA's 46.2% by 2.2%. The method introduces minimal extra parameters (7,686M total vs. ~7,147M for single-encoder Video-LLaVA) and trains in under 24 hours on 8 L40 GPUs, with step time comparable to single-encoder baselines due to parallelized visual encoding via FSDP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Video-LLaVA (the primary same-data-mix baseline), MERV (frozen) wins on 7 of 8 benchmarks, with the largest gains on TVQA (+4.62%), ActivityNet-QA (+3.79%), and NExT-QA (+3.48%), but loses on VLEP (−2.55%). MERV (full) narrows the VLEP gap to −1.14% and achieves the best Perception Test score (48.41%), surpassing both Video-LLaVA (+4.19%) and SeViLA (+2.2%). Compared to LLaMA-VID-7B (different training mix), MERV (frozen) leads on Perception Test (+4.57%), ActivityNet-QA (+4.42%), and TVQA (+4.85%), though LLaMA-VID-7B edges ahead on VLEP. Against older single-encoder models (Video-Chat, Video-LLaMA, Video-ChatGPT, LLaMA-Adapter), MERV dominates across all reported benchmarks by wide margins (e.g., 70.97% vs. 56.3% on MSVD-QA vs. Video-Chat). The only consistent loss is on VLEP, where the multi-encoder fusion appears to slightly underperform the single LanguageBind encoder, suggesting the video-language contrastive signal is particularly well-suited to that specific future-event prediction task.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LLaVA/Video-LLaVA paradigm (Liu et al., 2023; Lin et al., 2024) by replacing the single vision encoder with a multi-encoder architecture that fuses features from spatial (DINOv2), temporal (ViViT), image-language (SigLIP), and video-language (LanguageBind) experts. It departs from prior multi-encoder image VLMs like SPHINX-X, BRAVE, and Cambrian-1 by introducing spatio-temporal alignment and a cross-attention mixer specifically designed for video's temporal dimension, and by demonstrating that parallelized encoding makes the multi-encoder overhead negligible. The work is positioned as a direct extension of Video-LLaVA, using the same training data mixture and LLM backbone to isolate the contribution of multi-encoder fusion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that video understanding can be improved by combining complementary encoder specializations rather than relying on a single backbone, opening a path to scaling video LLMs by adding domain-specific encoders with minimal computational cost. The finding that a parameter-free 2D average pool outperforms learned projectors like the attentive resampler challenges the assumption that sophisticated fusion modules are necessary. Key limitations include dependence on the LLM's own capabilities, the fixed set of four encoders, and the fact that FSDP's generic sharding prevents optimal parallelization. Open questions include whether this approach scales to more encoders or better LLMs, and whether the cross-attention fusion remains optimal at larger scales.
