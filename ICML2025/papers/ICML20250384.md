## Problem

Current text watermarking algorithms embed statistical signals in high-entropy tokens to preserve text quality, but this design choice creates an exploitable vulnerability that existing paraphrasing attacks fail to target effectively. Prior model-based attacks (DIPPER, GPT Paraphraser) perform untargeted rewrites that are inefficient, costly, and already fail against newer robust watermarks like SIR, leaving a gap in rigorous black-box robustness evaluation.

## Input / Output

The method takes a watermarked token sequence and a base language model (M_attack, distinct from the watermarking model) as input, and produces a de-watermarked paraphrase as output. No access to the watermark algorithm, secret key, or detector is required (strict black-box setting). The attack operates on token-level self-information scores computed by M_attack, masks tokens above a percentile threshold with placeholders, and reconstructs the text via a fill-in-the-blank prompt. The entire pipeline runs on mobile-level 3B-parameter models (Llama-3-3B) with ~7 GB VRAM, costing approximately $0.88 per million tokens.

## Method

SIRA exploits the fact that watermarking algorithms must embed in high-entropy tokens (to avoid degrading quality), and these tokens exhibit high self-information under any language model. The attack proceeds in two steps: (1) compute the conditional self-information I(y_t) = -log P(y_t | y_0,...,y_{t-1}; M_attack) for each token, mask all tokens whose self-information exceeds the ε-percentile threshold (default ε=0.3) with placeholders, and simultaneously generate a reference paraphrase of the full watermarked text; (2) prompt M_attack to complete the masked template while incorporating all information from the reference text, using greedy decoding. The key distinction from prior paraphrasing attacks is that masking is targeted at potential green-list tokens rather than random, transforming the problem into a structured fill-in-the-blank task that systematically removes watermark-carrying tokens. The paper also provides theoretical bounds (Theorem H.4) showing that under small-δ watermarking constraints, watermarked tokens remain detectable in the high self-information region, guaranteeing near-complete removal with an appropriately chosen threshold.

## Results

SIRA achieves near-100% attack success rates across all seven tested watermarking algorithms (KGW-1, Unigram, UPV, EWD, DIP, SIR, EXP) using the SIRA-Large variant (Llama-3-70B), with scores of 100%, 100%, 99.6%, 100%, 100%, 98.8%, and 99.8% respectively. Even the most lightweight SIRA-Tiny (Llama-3-3B) outperforms all previous paraphrasing baselines on every algorithm, achieving 75.0%–99.8% attack success rates. The method costs only $0.88 per million tokens (SIRA-Small via AWS Bedrock) and runs on a single A100 GPU for the Tiny and Small variants, compared to $20 per million tokens for GPT-4o-based paraphrasing.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SIRA dominates all four baseline categories across the seven watermarking algorithms. Against Word Delete (Welbl et al., 2020) and Synonym Substitution (Yu et al., 2010), SIRA-Tiny already achieves 3–50× higher attack success rates (e.g., 96.4% vs. 22.4% on KGW-1; 87.6% vs. 17.4% on Unigram). Against the strongest model-based baselines, SIRA-Tiny (Llama-3-3B) outperforms DIPPER-2 (Krishna et al., 2024) by 42.0 points on Unigram (87.6% vs. 45.6%), 22.6 points on UPV (84.4% vs. 61.8%), 11.4 points on SIR (75.0% vs. 63.6%), and 8.4 points on EXP (90.6% vs. 82.2%), while matching or exceeding GPT Paraphraser (Liu et al., 2024) on all algorithms except KGW-1 and DIP where both reach 100%/99.8%. SIRA-Large (Llama-3-70B) achieves 100% or near-100% on every algorithm, surpassing the best baseline by up to 36.4 points (Unigram: 100% vs. 63.6% for DIPPER-2). Notably, SIRA-Tiny requires only 7.06 GB VRAM and 5.3 seconds per attack, versus 44.56 GB and 14.7 seconds for DIPPER, making it both more effective and substantially cheaper.

## Relation to Prior Work

SIRA builds on the established text watermarking literature (KGW family, Christ family) and prior paraphrasing-based attacks (DIPPER, GPT Paraphraser), but departs from them by introducing a *targeted* rather than untargeted rewriting strategy. The key conceptual move is repurposing self-information—a standard information-theoretic quantity in NLP—as a proxy for identifying green-list tokens without any knowledge of the watermark algorithm, key, or detector. This transforms the paraphrasing task into a fill-in-the-blank problem, exploiting the fact that high-entropy tokens (required for watermark imperceptibility) are also high-self-information tokens. The paper also provides theoretical bounds (Appendix H) connecting the small probability perturbation δ used by watermarking to the residual self-information of watermarked tokens, grounding the empirical observation in information theory.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper reveals that a core design principle of current watermarking—embedding patterns in high-entropy tokens to preserve text quality—creates a universal, exploitable signal that requires no access to the watermarking system. This finding will likely force the watermarking community to reconsider whether imperceptibility and robustness can be simultaneously achieved under the current paradigm. Open questions include: (1) whether watermarking schemes that avoid high-entropy token selection (e.g., embedding in low-entropy or structural positions) can resist SIRA, (2) how the attack transfers when the watermarking and attack models are architecturally very different, and (3) whether the theoretical bounds hold in practice for much larger watermarking models than the Opt-1.3B tested here. The low cost ($0.88/M tokens) and small-model requirement (3B) make this a practical threat, not just a theoretical one.
