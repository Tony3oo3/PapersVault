## Problem

Existing defenses such as SmoothVLM counter vision attacks on LVLMs by cropping images and majority-voting across responses, but cropping distorts image semantics and significantly degrades response quality on clean inputs. The paper addresses the core dilemma that partial images can disrupt adversarial cues yet also lose critical visual information, asking how to leverage partial-image understanding to guide full-image responses at inference time without harming clean-input performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20253191/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image (potentially containing adversarial noise, typographic cues, or jailbreak content) and a text question as input. Internally, it generates three partial image crops—center-cropped (half-size), random-cropped (1/4 to 1/2 size from random locations), and adaptively-cropped (main objects extracted via an LVLM)—which are processed by Part-Perc models to produce supervisory text descriptions. The final output is a text response to the original question, optionally passed through a plug-and-play LLM safety checker (LS-DPS) that rewrites harmful content into benign responses for jailbreak scenarios.

## Method

DPS is a black-box, training-free defense inspired by the weak-to-strong learning phenomenon, where partial-image responses serve as 'weak' supervision for the 'strong' full-image model. In Step 1, three Part-Perc models independently describe and answer the question on their respective cropped views; in Step 2, these responses are summarized and injected as supervisory prompts into the Full-Perc model, which re-analyzes the full image and produces the final answer. The mechanism exploits an observed confidence asymmetry: vision attacks reduce the LVLM's confidence, making it susceptible to the supervisory prompts and triggering self-correction, whereas clean images maintain high confidence, causing the model to disregard the partial supervision and preserve its original response. A theoretical analysis formalizes this by showing that the crop-induced text T_c acts as a semantic-consistency prior φ(T, T_c) that reweights the output distribution, increasing P(T_clean) relative to P(T_target). For jailbreak defense, LS-DPS appends an LLM-based safety checker that evaluates and rewrites the final response, achieving an average ASR of 0.05 on Qwen-VL-Plus across three jailbreak datasets.

## Results

DPS reduces the average Attack Success Rate (ASR) by 76.3% across six attack datasets on three LVLMs (Qwen-VL-Plus, GPT-4o-Mini, Gemini-1.5-Flash). On misleading defense, DPS achieves average ASR of 0.31 (Qwen-VL-Plus), 0.39 (GPT-4o-Mini), and 0.39 (Gemini-1.5-Flash), outperforming the best baseline by 2.5×, 1.95×, and 1.90× respectively. For jailbreak defense, LS-DPS achieves average ASR of 0.05 on Qwen-VL-Plus, 0.04 on GPT-4o-Mini, and 0.05 on Gemini-1.5-Flash. On standard performance (MM-Vet), DPS maintains scores close to vanilla (e.g., 54.8 vs. 57.3 for Qwen-VL-Plus), while SmoothVLM degrades significantly (47.3).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DPS outperforms all seven baselines on misleading defense across all three models. MLLM-Protector (Pi et al., 2024) and ECSO (Gou et al., 2025), designed specifically for safety scenarios, are largely ineffective against misleading attacks (avg. ASR 0.98 and 0.89 on Qwen-VL-Plus). SmoothVLM (Sun et al., 2024) achieves the best baseline on GPT-4o-Mini (0.76) but degrades standard MM-Vet performance from 69.3 to 52.4, whereas DPS maintains 66.8. On jailbreak defense, LS-DPS (avg. ASR 0.05 on Qwen-VL-Plus) substantially outperforms MLLM-Protector (0.16) and ECSO (0.15). Against adversarial purification methods (Guo et al., ICLR 2018; Lee et al., ICCV 2023), DPS achieves lower ASR on both misleading (0.25 vs. 0.33 for DAP on RTA-100) and jailbreak (0.50 vs. 0.56 for DAP on MM-Safety) tasks. Compared to MirrorCheck (Fares et al., 2024), DPS achieves comparable ASR (0.10 vs. 0.20 on MM-Safety) but dramatically better standard performance (0.68 vs. 0.26 MM-Vet score).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on SmoothVLM (Sun et al., 2024), which defends against vision attacks by majority-voting over responses to randomly modified (e.g., cropped) images. DPS departs from this aggregation paradigm by instead using partial-image responses as supervisory prompts that guide the full-image model to re-examine its answer, drawing an analogy to weak-to-strong learning where a less-informed agent guides a more-informed one. The interaction framework also borrows from multi-agent debate literature, and the LLM-Secured variant (LS-DPS) composes with existing LLM-based safety checkers like MLLM-Protector and ECSO. Conceptually, the key shift is from treating modified-image outputs as votes to be aggregated toward treating them as diagnostic signals that exploit the observed confidence drop in attacked images.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DPS offers a practical, black-box, training-free defense that requires no knowledge of attack specifics and no model retraining, making it deployable across commercial LVLM APIs. The empirical observation that vision attacks reduce model confidence (making outputs more susceptible to prompt interference) provides a useful mechanistic explanation for why the supervision works. However, the method incurs 5–6 API calls per query, and its effectiveness collapses when cropping fails to disrupt the attack (e.g., attacks embedded in the central region). Open questions include whether integration with segmentation models (e.g., SAM) or textual smoothing can address this failure mode, and whether more sophisticated multi-agent interaction (e.g., persuasive debate, explored preliminarily) can further close the gap.
