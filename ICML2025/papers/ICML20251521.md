## Problem

Unsupervised protein-ligand binding energy prediction is critical for ligands like antibodies where labeled affinity data is extremely scarce (only 566 samples in SAbDab). Existing unsupervised approaches such as DSMBind rely on approximations in their SE(3) denoising score matching formulation and are memory-intensive during training, limiting their practicality for co-training with other models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251521/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes the 3D structure of a protein-ligand complex as input, represented by atom types (one-hot over 95 types) and 3D coordinates for both protein and ligand atoms. It outputs a single scalar binding energy per complex. Crucially, training requires no binding affinity labels—only unlabeled complex structures—while evaluation measures Pearson correlation between predicted and ground-truth affinity on held-out labeled sets.

## Method

CEBind formulates unsupervised training via the law of conservation of energy: random forces are sampled per ligand atom, applied rigidly to the ligand (decomposed into center-of-mass translation, rigid rotation via inertia matrix, and side-chain rotation about α-carbons), and the energy model must predict that the energy gap between perturbed and unperturbed complexes equals the work of the outer forces (½kΔX²). A second loss performs denoising score matching on per-atom forces, matching the energy gradient with respect to coordinates to the inner force. The architecture combines a protein encoder (atom embedding + ESM residue embeddings + positions), a ligand encoder (GNN or atom-only embedding + positions), and an energy-based module (equivariant GNN + SRU interaction modeling) that sums pairwise atom energies within a distance threshold. A key efficiency gain is that the entire rigid-dynamics perturbation is computed in the dataloader without requiring backpropagation, reducing peak GPU memory from 6.43 GB (DSMBind) to 4.89 GB and speeding up training by ~24%.

## Results

On the protein-small molecule dataset, CEBind achieves a Pearson correlation (r_p) of 0.652±0.005, outperforming the previous best unsupervised method DSMBind (0.644±0.003) and the best physics-based method MM/GBSA (0.647). On the antibody-antigen dataset, CEBind achieves r_p = 0.374±0.009, surpassing all prior approaches including the best supervised model FANN_transfer (0.350±0.033) and the best physics-based method AP_PISA (0.323). CEBind also demonstrates superior training efficiency, requiring 4.89 GB peak GPU memory versus 6.43 GB for DSMBind, and 0.0116 s per batch versus 0.0152 s.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CEBind outperforms all unsupervised baselines on both datasets: on protein-small molecule it beats DSMBind by 0.008 (0.652 vs. 0.644), Gauss DSM by 0.016, and Contrastive by 0.027; on antibody-antigen it beats DSMBind by 0.009 (0.374 vs. 0.365), Gauss DSM by 0.014, and Contrastive by 0.066. On the antibody-antigen task, CEBind also surpasses the best supervised model FANN_transfer (0.350) and all physics-based methods (AP_PISA 0.323, ZRANK 0.318, PYDOCK_vina 0.248, CP_PIE 0.234), which is notable given the extreme data scarcity (only 566 labeled samples, all held out for testing). On protein-small molecule, supervised methods IGN (0.837) and PLANET (0.811) still outperform CEBind, as expected given the availability of >19,000 labeled training samples. CEBind also outperforms the ESM-based unsupervised baselines (ESM-1v and ESM-IF, both at r_p=0.024) by a large margin on antibody-antigen, confirming that structural energy modeling is far more informative than sequence embeddings alone for binding affinity. The atom-only variant CEBind_atom (0.648 on protein-small molecule, 0.346 on antibody-antigen) remains competitive, and cross-validation from antibody-antigen to protein-small molecule (0.646) still exceeds all prior unsupervised methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

CEBind builds directly on DSMBind (Jin et al., 2024), which first formulated the unsupervised protein-ligand binding energy prediction task using SE(3) denoising score matching on the whole ligand as a rigid body. CEBind departs from this by sampling forces per-atom and applying them through rigid dynamics (translation, rotation, and side-chain rotation), then enforcing the conservation of energy as the primary training signal rather than matching aggregate rotation and translation. The energy model architecture (equivariant GNN plus protein-ligand interaction module) and the force score matching loss are inherited from DSMBind, while the key methodological shift is making the rigid-dynamics computation gradient-free and precomputable in the data loader, reducing GPU memory from 6.43 GB to 4.89 GB.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The memory and speed gains make CEBind more practical for co-training with generative or design models, and the per-atom physics formulation provides a cleaner, more interpretable training signal than DSMBind's whole-body rotation matching. The cross-dataset generalization result (CEBind_atom trained on antibody-antigen achieving r_p=0.646 on protein-small molecule, surpassing all prior unsupervised methods) supports the emerging view, shared with AlphaFold3, that a universal biomolecular interaction model is feasible. Open questions include whether the simplified linear-spring inner-force model can be replaced by more accurate force fields without breaking the gradient-free pipeline, and whether the modest performance gap to supervised methods on protein-small molecule can be closed with larger-scale pre-training.
