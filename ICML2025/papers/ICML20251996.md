## Problem

Existing Federated Graph Learning methods rely on multi-round personalized optimization, making them unable to efficiently capture diverse local knowledge and generalize across heterogeneous graph structures when communication is restricted to a single round. Additionally, extreme structural bias across clients causes the global model to overfit to current data distributions, leading to catastrophic forgetting of previously acquired topological knowledge. No prior work addresses both generalization and topology retention simultaneously in the one-shot FGL setting.

## Input / Output

Each client holds a local subgraph with F-dimensional node feature vectors and an adjacency matrix; the proxy model consumes random Gaussian noise concatenated with one-hot node labels to generate pseudo features, from which a KNN-based pseudo adjacency matrix is constructed. The server aggregates proxy model parameters (uploaded exactly once per client) and generates integrated pseudo-graph knowledge sets to train a two-layer GCN global model. The final output is per-node class labels for the downstream node classification task, evaluated in a generalization setting where one global model is tested on all clients' test data.

## Method

GHOST comprises two modules. First, the Dual-Level Aligned Proxy Model (DLA) trains a per-client proxy (3 hidden linear layers + projection head) via three losses: a Dispersion Loss (negative cosine similarity to prevent mode collapse), a Divergence Loss (KL divergence between softmax feature distributions), and a Fused Gromov-Wasserstein Loss that jointly aligns feature and structural geometry between the local graph and the generated pseudo graph. Second, the Topology-Conscious Knowledge Retention (TCKR) module computes a Coherence Factor measuring alignment of connected-node hidden representations, then derives a Topology-Consistency Criterion (the gradient of coherence w.r.t. each weight, inspired by synaptic intelligence) to identify parameters critical for structural knowledge. During sequential global training over knowledge sets from different clients, a Knowledge Retention Loss (element-wise product of the criterion with parameter drift) stabilizes these critical weights, while an L2 regularization on the criterion preserves model plasticity for new knowledge integration.

## Results

GHOST achieves the best node classification accuracy on all seven benchmark datasets, with particularly large margins on Cora (50.41%, +19.80 over FedAvg), Amazon-Photo (37.22%, +14.10), and Coauthor-CS (29.91%, +7.41). On the largest dataset Ogbn-Arxiv (169K nodes), GHOST reaches 17.24% accuracy, outperforming the next-best baseline FedAvg (14.58%) by 2.66 points. The method also demonstrates strong resilience to data heterogeneity, consistently outperforming baselines across Dirichlet α values from 0.05 to 0.25.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GHOST outperforms all 10 baselines across all 7 datasets. Among traditional FL methods, FedProx is the strongest (e.g., 35.73% on CiteSeer) but still trails GHOST by 2.02 points; FedNova and FedRCL collapse severely under high heterogeneity (α=0.05), dropping below 20% on most datasets. Traditional FGL methods (FedPub, FedTAD, FedGTA) perform moderately on small graphs but degrade sharply on large-scale ones—FedTAD even runs out of memory on Ogbn-Arxiv, while FedGTA scores only 1.15% there. Among one-shot FL baselines, FedCVAE is the closest competitor (31.62% on Amz-Photo, 13.71% on Ogbn-Arxiv), yet GHOST still leads by 5.60 and 3.53 points respectively. DENSE and FedSD2C perform poorly on graph tasks, confirming that vision-oriented one-shot methods do not transfer well to graph-structured data.

## Relation to Prior Work

GHOST sits at the intersection of three research threads: one-shot federated learning (DENSE, FedCVAE, FedSD2C), federated graph learning (FedPub, FedTAD, FedGTA), and continual-learning-inspired parameter importance (synaptic intelligence, EWC). It departs from prior one-shot FL by extending the paradigm from vision to graph-structured data and from personalized to generalized aggregation, and from prior FGL by eliminating multi-round iterative communication in favor of a single proxy-model upload. The Dual-Level Alignment loss repurposes Gromov-Wasserstein distance (previously used in graph matching and domain adaptation) as a structural alignment objective for a generative proxy, while the Topology-Consistency Criterion adapts the synaptic-intelligence gradient-importance idea to identify topology-critical GNN parameters.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The one-shot constraint targets a real deployment bottleneck in edge-computing and bandwidth-limited networks, and the paper demonstrates that a single communication round can suffice for generalized graph learning if local knowledge is captured through well-aligned proxy models. However, the approach inherits a fundamental limitation of proxy-based methods: sensitivity to noise in local data, which the authors acknowledge but do not resolve. Open questions include whether the framework extends beyond node classification (e.g., link prediction, graph classification), how it scales to heterogeneous proxy architectures across clients, and whether the topology-consistency criterion remains informative for very deep or heterogeneous GNN backbones.
