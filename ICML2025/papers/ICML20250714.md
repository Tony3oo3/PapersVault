## Problem

Existing concept erasure methods (ESD, UCE, EAP, MACE) were designed for the Stable Diffusion v1.4 paradigm (DDPM/DDIM + U-Net with explicit cross-attention and CLIP text encoders) and fail to transfer to newer flow-matching, transformer-based architectures like Flux. Flux's MMDiT dual-stream blocks lack explicit cross-attention layers, and its T5 text encoder produces sentence-level embeddings that are unreliable for word-level semantic similarity, making direct adaptation of prior erasure techniques infeasible and leaving a significant gap in robust concept removal for the latest T2I models.

## Input / Output

The method takes a target concept to erase (e.g., 'nude', 'soccer', a celebrity name) and the frozen Flux.1 [dev] model weights, then trains a lightweight LoRA adapter on the add_q_proj and add_k_proj parameters within the dual-stream blocks. The output is a LoRA weight matrix (Δθ) that, when loaded onto Flux, produces concept-erased images at 512×512 resolution using a 28-step flow-matching Euler sampler. The LoRA can be composed with other concept-erased LoRAs via normalized linear summation to erase multiple concepts simultaneously without degrading generation quality on unrelated prompts.

## Method

EraseAnything formulates concept erasure as a bi-level optimization over a single LoRA adapter. The lower level combines the ESD negative-guidance loss with an attention-map regularizer that suppresses activations at the token-index columns of the target word within the dual-stream attention matrix (W_attn), where text and pixel embeddings are concatenated to form implicit cross-attention. The upper level preserves irrelevant concepts via a flow-matching reconstruction loss on reference images plus a novel Reverse Self-Contrastive Loss (RSC) that inverts the standard InfoNCE objective: instead of pulling the target concept's attention features toward its synonym, it pushes them toward dynamically sampled irrelevant concepts (generated heuristically by GPT-4o) while repelling them from the synonym, effectively obfuscating the erased concept within a cloud of unrelated semantics. Prompt shuffling at each iteration prevents overfitting to fixed token positions, and the entire procedure is robust to black-box attacks (misspellings, repeated keywords) that defeat deterministic attention-map zeroing.

## Results

On the I2P nudity-erasure benchmark (4,703 prompts), EraseAnything detects 199 total explicit images (second-lowest after UCE's 173), while achieving the best FID (21.75) and CLIP score (30.24) among all erasure methods—nearly matching the unmodified Flux.1[dev] baseline (FID 21.32, CLIP 30.87). On the broader Entity/Abstraction/Relationship erasure evaluation (Table 3), EraseAnything consistently outperforms CA across all three categories in erasure efficacy (Acc_e), irrelevant-concept retention (Acc_ir), and synonym robustness (Acc_g). Under adversarial attacks (Ring-A-Bell-Nudity), EraseAnything achieves the lowest attack success rate of 2.46% on original prompts and 8.77% under MU-Attack (step 0), compared to 3.16% and 15.44% for CA respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EraseAnything outperforms all six baselines on the combined erasure-and-preservation objective. Against UCE (Gandikota et al., 2024), which achieves the lowest nudity count (173 vs. 199), EraseAnything dramatically wins on generation quality: FID 21.75 vs. 30.71 and CLIP 30.24 vs. 24.56, indicating UCE severely degrades general image synthesis. Against ESD (Gandikota et al., 2023), EraseAnything reduces detected nudity from 506 to 199 while improving FID (21.75 vs. 23.08) and CLIP (30.24 vs. 28.44). Against CA (Kumari et al., 2023), both model-based and noise-based variants are dominated: EraseAnything cuts nudity by ~42–49% and improves FID by ~0.9–1.3 points. Against MACE (Lu et al., 2024), EAP (Bui et al., 2024), and Meta-Unlearning (Gao et al., 2024), EraseAnything achieves the best FID and CLIP while reducing nudity counts by 22%, 48%, and 62% respectively. On the adversarial robustness front (Table 4), EraseAnything achieves the lowest ASR (2.46%) on clean prompts and remains most robust under MU-Attack (8.77% vs. 15.44% for CA at step 0), confirming that its learning-based approach resists the keyword-obfuscation attacks that defeat attention-map-only methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the concept erasure (CE) line of work from U-Net-based Stable Diffusion to the newer rectified-flow transformer architecture (Flux/SD3), which lacks explicit cross-attention layers that prior methods (ESD, UCE, MACE, EAP) fundamentally depend on. It builds directly on ESD's negative-guidance loss as the lower-level erasure objective and on InfoNCE-style contrastive learning, inverting the numerator/denominator to create a Reverse Self-Contrastive Loss that pushes the target concept's attention features away from synonyms and toward irrelevant concepts. The bi-level optimization framing and the use of LLMs (GPT-4o) for irrelevant-concept selection represent departures from prior CE work, which relied on CLIP word-level embeddings for adversarial prompt construction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As Flux and SD3 become the dominant open-source T2I architectures, the inability of existing CE methods to transfer is a practical gap that this paper addresses with a working solution. However, the performance margins are modest (EraseAnything achieves 199 vs. UCE's 173 total nudity detections, trading some erasure strength for significantly better FID/CLIP preservation), and the method is validated primarily on a single model (Flux [dev]) with limited scaling experiments. Open questions include fine-grained control over erasure intensity, efficient combination of 100+ concept-erased LoRAs, and whether the bi-level optimization framework generalizes to other flow-matching architectures beyond Flux.
