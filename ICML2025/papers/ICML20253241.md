## Problem

Vanilla LoRA adapters converge to suboptimal minima near their initialization due to 'lazy' gradient dynamics, and exhibit pronounced layer-wise imbalance where higher-layer adapters receive the bulk of gradient signal while lower-layer adapters remain under-utilized. This uneven optimization restricts single-task generalization and hampers downstream operations such as adapter merging and pruning, yet existing LoRA variants address neither issue explicitly.

## Input / Output

CoTo operates as a training-time modification to any LoRA-based fine-tuning pipeline, taking a frozen pre-trained foundation model (ViT-B/16, LLaMA-2-7B/13B, LLaMA-3-8B, or SDXL) and a small set of low-rank adapter matrices (rank r=2 to 128) as its only trainable parameters. The method produces standard task outputs—class labels for image classification, text answers for commonsense or mathematical reasoning, or generated images for diffusion models—without altering the model architecture or inference procedure. At inference time, all adapters are active and the model behaves identically to standard LoRA.

## Method

CoTo introduces a progressive activation schedule in which each adapter's activation probability p(t) increases linearly from 0 to 1 over the first 75% of training steps, after which all adapters remain fully active. At each training step, every adapter is independently sampled from a Bernoulli(p(t)) distribution; when deactivated, the adapter's low-rank matrices are skipped entirely, reducing both forward and backward computation. Theoretically, the paper shows via Jensen's inequality that CoTo's objective upper-bounds a binomially weighted sum of subnetwork losses, which promotes layer-wise dropout stability and linear mode connectivity between independently trained solutions. A cooperative-game formulation using Shapley values further quantifies each adapter's marginal contribution, revealing that CoTo shifts contribution from the top 4/12 layers (69% in vanilla LoRA) to a near-uniform distribution (±3% deviation). The method requires no architectural changes and integrates as a lightweight callback with existing LoRA variants including DoRA, HiRA, PiSSA, rsLoRA, LoRA+, and LoRA-Pro.

## Results

On 11 image classification tasks (ViT-B/16, rank r=2), CoTo improves average accuracy by +0.53% for LoRA (82.95→83.48%), +0.48% for DoRA (83.45→83.93%), and +0.36% for HiRA (83.98→84.34%). On commonsense reasoning with LLaMA-2-7B (rank r=32), gains are more pronounced: LoRA improves from 77.61% to 80.63% (+3.02%), and with LLaMA-3-8B from 80.79% to 85.02% (+4.23%). On mathematical reasoning (GSM8K, LLaMA-2-7B, rank r=8), vanilla LoRA jumps from 42.08% to 55.85% (+13.77%), and LoRA-Pro from 54.23% to 57.16%. For multi-task merging on LLaMA-2-7B, linear weight fusion accuracy rises from 47.17% to 58.53% (+11.36%), and CoTo also reduces training time by 7–24% depending on the LoRA variant.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoTo consistently outperforms its corresponding base method across all evaluated LoRA variants and tasks: LoRA-CoTo beats LoRA by +0.53% (image classification), +3.02% (LLaMA-2-7B commonsense), and +13.77% (GSM8K math); DoRA-CoTo beats DoRA by +0.48% and +0.95%; HiRA-CoTo beats HiRA by +0.36% and +0.52%. Against alternative initialization/optimization baselines on GSM8K (Table 3), CoTo applied to LoRA-Pro (57.16%) surpasses PiSSA (50.16%), rsLoRA (56.99%), and LoRA+ (54.36%), while also improving each of those methods when CoTo is added on top. For multi-task merging (Table 4), CoTo-trained adapters improve linear weight fusion by +11.36% on LLaMA-2-7B and +16.98% on LLaMA-2-13B, and LoRA-LEGO merging by +4.98% and +1.30% respectively, indicating substantially better linear mode connectivity. CoTo also reduces wall-clock training time by 7.20% (LoRA), 23.69% (DoRA), and 24.21% (HiRA) without sacrificing accuracy, making it strictly superior in both performance and efficiency compared to all baselines tested.

## Relation to Prior Work

CoTo builds directly on LoRA (Hu et al., 2022) and its variants (DoRA, HiRA, LoRA-Pro) by introducing a progressive stochastic activation schedule at the adapter level, rather than modifying architecture, initialization, or gradient computation. It draws conceptual inspiration from stochastic regularization (Dropout, Stochastic Depth, LayerDrop) but departs from prior LoRA dropout work (Wang et al., 2024a; Lin et al., 2024) by operating at the layer level with a time-varying probability, explicitly targeting the layer-wise gradient imbalance problem. The theoretical framing via Jensen's inequality and Shapley-value cooperative games connects PEFT training dynamics to the model-merging (LMC) and pruning literatures, positioning CoTo as a bridge between training strategy and downstream composability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CoTo addresses a persistent and underappreciated problem—layer-wise adapter imbalance—that simultaneously degrades single-task generalization, multi-task merging, and pruning robustness, and does so with a mechanism that is trivially implementable (a few lines of code) and orthogonal to existing LoRA variants. The open question the authors highlight is how CoTo's progressive schedule interacts with adaptive-rank schemes (AdaLoRA, ALoRA) and weight-decomposed updates (DoRA), suggesting a combinatorial design space that remains unexplored. A practical limitation is that the 75/25 phase split, while ablated, is a heuristic rather than a principled choice, and the method's benefits may diminish for very shallow networks or extremely low-rank settings where fewer adapters are available to deactivate.
