## Problem

Existing preference alignment losses derived from Plackett-Luce (PL) and Bradley-Terry (BT) models fail to robustly reject harmful content: PL inadvertently ranks harmful responses as 'preferred' relative to worse alternatives, while BT only rejects the single most dispreferred response in a pair. Additionally, both treat all dispreferred responses equally, ignoring their varying informativeness, and PL incurs high computational costs by requiring forward and backward passes through all n ranked responses per prompt.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a text prompt paired with a ranked set of n candidate responses (one most preferred, n−1 dispreferred) along with scalar reward estimates for each response, and produces a fine-tuned LLM that generates aligned text responses. Training uses LoRA (rank 8, α=16) on Llama3-8B with 2048-token sequences, and at each iteration only a single dispreferred response is sampled via Monte Carlo rather than processing all n responses, reducing per-prompt computation from O(n) to O(1).

## Method

HPS replaces the recursive PL sub-losses with a single-sample objective that ranks the most preferred response above all dispreferred ones, then applies importance sampling with a distribution q(x,y) ∝ exp(γ·r_est(x,y)) to select one 'hard' dispreferred response per training step, where γ controls the emphasis on responses with high estimated rewards (i.e., those closest to the preferred response in reward space). This single-sample Monte Carlo strategy reduces GPU memory and compute by ~62% compared to full PL while provably improving sample complexity from O(n²/√m) to O(n/√m). Theoretically, optimizing the HPS loss is shown to maximize the reward margin between the preferred response and the hardest dispreferred response, ensuring stronger rejection of harmful content. The framework is plug-and-play, integrating with any implicit reward parameterization (DPO, IPO, SPPO, NCA, EXO) by simply replacing the preference model component.

## Results

On HH-RLHF, DPO-HPS achieves BLEU 0.232 and Reward 0.430 (comparable to DPO-BT's 0.230/0.431) while dramatically improving reward margins: RM_DPO of 2.723 vs. DPO-PL's −0.795 and DPO-BT's 0.349, and RM_R-DPO of 2.040 vs. −1.448 and −0.455 respectively. On PKU-SafeRLHF, DPO-HPS maintains comparable BLEU (0.306) and Reward (0.407) while achieving the best RM_DPO (−5.359) among DPO variants. In transfer learning (HH-RLHF→PKU-Safety), DPO-HPS achieves RM_DPO of 5.725 vs. DPO-BT's 1.346, an 83% average reward margin improvement over SoTAs. HPS also reduces fine-tuning time by 61.76% compared to PL-based methods (64.4h vs. 168.4h).

## Baselines & Comparisons

HPS is integrated into five implicit reward frameworks (DPO, EXO, IPO, SPPO, NCA) and compared against their PL and BT variants. On BLEU and Reward, HPS matches or is within 0.001 of all baselines, confirming it does not degrade generation quality. The decisive advantage is in reward margins: DPO-HPS achieves RM_DPO of 2.723 on HH-RLHF, a 442% improvement over DPO-PL (−0.795) and 780% over DPO-BT (0.349). PL-based baselines consistently show the worst margins (e.g., IPO-PL: −5.199, SPPO-PL: −7.630, NCA-PL: −5.760 on HH-RLHF), while HPS variants bring all of them to near-zero or positive margins. In transfer learning, DPO-HPS achieves RM_DPO of 5.725 on PKU-Safety vs. DPO-BT's 1.346 and DPO-PL's 0.144, and SPPO-HPS jumps from −13.442 (BT) to −5.273, a 61% margin improvement. Human evaluation (Table 3) confirms DPO-HPS scores highest quality (3.93/5) vs. DPO-BT (3.82) and DPO-PL (3.69), and Qwen-2.5-72B win-rate evaluation shows DPO-HPS winning ~64% against SFT and ~59% against DPO-BT on HH-RLHF.

## Relation to Prior Work

HPS builds directly on the DPO implicit reward parameterization (Rafailov et al., 2024) and the Plackett-Luce ranking model (Plackett, 1975), but departs from the PL loss's recursive sub-loss structure by replacing it with a single-sample Monte Carlo importance sampling objective. The 'hard negative' concept is borrowed from supervised and contrastive learning (FaceNet, LIFT, Robinson et al., 2020), but is adapted to the RLHF setting where autoregressive generation makes sentence-embedding-based hard negative mining impractical; instead, hardness is defined via reward-space proximity. The framework is designed to be a drop-in replacement for the PL component in existing alignment methods (DPO, EXO, IPO, SPPO, NCA), positioning it as a loss-level modification rather than a new alignment paradigm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 61.76% reduction in fine-tuning time (from 168.4h to 64.4h) makes PL-style listwise alignment practically feasible for longer response sets, and the sample complexity improvement from O(n²/√m) to O(n/√m) is theoretically meaningful for data-limited settings. However, experiments are confined to Llama3-8B with LoRA (rank 8), and the large reward-margin improvements (89% over DPO) are measured on a paper-defined metric whose correlation with real-world safety remains unvalidated beyond a small user study (30 prompts, 20 raters). Open questions include whether the single-sample approximation degrades for very large n, whether the approach transfers to larger models or non-English settings, and whether the reward margin metric genuinely predicts harmful output rates in deployment.
