## Problem

The schedule-free method (Defazio et al., 2024) demonstrated impressive empirical success in training neural networks without learning rate scheduling, but its theoretical guarantees were confined to convex optimization. This paper closes that gap by proving schedule-free SGD achieves optimal iteration complexity for nonsmooth, nonconvex optimization, and provides a theoretical explanation for why the interpolation parameter κ_t should be chosen close to 1—a phenomenon unexplained by prior convex analysis.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250031/cropped_page6_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a G-Lipschitz differentiable loss function F: R^d → R accessed through a stochastic gradient oracle with bounded variance σ², and produces a (λ, ε)-stationary point (an approximate Goldstein stationary point) in O(λ^{1/2} ε^{-7/2}) oracle calls, which is the known optimal rate. The output is a random exponential moving average iterate ŷ_τ where τ is a carefully designed random index that assigns the final iterate T a probability mass scaled by 1/(1−β), effectively favoring later iterates while maintaining the averaging structure needed for the nonconvex guarantee.

## Method

The paper introduces a general online-to-nonconvex conversion framework (Algorithm 1) that takes an online learner and produces a nonconvex optimization algorithm, with the key design freedom being the arbitrary choice of iterates x_t at each step, governed by an iterate stability factor C_x. The framework converts discounted regret bounds from a β-OMD online learner into nonconvex guarantees via exponential moving averages and a carefully designed random index. Three special cases are explored: x_t = w_{t-1} recovers prior conversions (SGD with momentum, Adam), x_t = x_0 yields a novel multi-epoch anchoring scheme, and x_t = x_{t-1} + δ_t/ζ with β-OMD directly recovers schedule-free SGD. The equivalence is established by introducing extrapolated z-iterates that follow a base SGD trajectory, with κ_t drawn uniformly from [ζ, 1] and step size γ = η/(1−ζ). The analysis yields ζ* = 1 − Θ(ε²/(G+σ)²), providing the first theoretical justification for choosing κ_t close to 1 in practice.

## Results

The paper establishes that schedule-free SGD achieves the optimal iteration complexity of O(λ^{1/2} ε^{-7/2}) stochastic gradient oracle calls for finding a (λ, ε)-stationary point of a G-Lipschitz, nonconvex, nonsmooth function. Specifically, Corollary 5.1 shows that T ≥ 49(G+σ)²ε⁻² · max{20Δ_F λ^{1/2} ε^{-3/2}, 1} iterations suffice to guarantee E‖∇F(ȳ_τ)‖^{[λ]} ≤ 5ε. The analysis also reveals that κ_t should be chosen close to 1 (specifically κ_t ∈ [1−Θ(ε²/(G+σ)²), 1]), providing a theoretical explanation for the empirical observation in Defazio et al. (2024) that κ_t ≈ 0.98 is critical for performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines; comparisons are against prior theoretical results. The paper extends Cutkosky et al. (2023), who established the first optimal algorithm for stochastic nonsmooth nonconvex optimization via online-to-nonconvex conversion, by showing their framework (Option I) is a special case of a more general scheme. Compared to Zhang and Cutkosky (2024), who achieved the same optimal rate with random scaling and momentum, the paper's use of a carefully designed random index τ yields a slightly tighter constant (O(1) vs. O((G+σ)ε^{-1}) in the second argument of the max). Most significantly, the paper extends Defazio et al. (2024), whose schedule-free method was only analyzed for convex losses, by proving schedule-free SGD is also optimal for nonsmooth nonconvex optimization. The paper further provides a theoretical justification for choosing κ_t near 1 in schedule-free SGD—a parameter choice that Defazio et al.'s convex theory permits to be arbitrary in [0,1] but which empirically proved critical, leaving a gap that this work closes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the online-to-nonconvex conversion framework of Cutkosky et al. (2023) by introducing a more general scheme (Algorithm 1) that subsumes the prior conversion as a special case while enabling new conversion paths. It directly builds on Defazio et al. (2024), who introduced schedule-free SGD and proved its optimality only in the convex setting via online-to-batch conversion; here the authors show that one of their novel conversion schemes (Algorithm 5) is exactly schedule-free SGD, thereby establishing its optimality for the far harder nonsmooth nonconvex problem. The framework also naturally recovers SGD with momentum (via OMD) and Adam (via FTRL), positioning schedule-free SGD as the third practical optimizer explainable through this unified lens.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result resolves a theoretical gap: convex analysis of schedule-free SGD permitted κ_t to be chosen arbitrarily in [0,1], yet empirically κ_t near 1 (e.g., 0.98) was critical; the nonconvex analysis shows κ_t must be close to 1 for optimal rates, providing the first theoretical justification for this empirical observation. The general framework is a versatile tool for designing and analyzing new optimizers, but the paper is purely theoretical with no experiments, and it only covers schedule-free SGD (not the more practically important schedule-free Adam). Key open questions include whether a single parameter choice can be simultaneously optimal for both convex and nonconvex objectives, and why schedule-free methods genuinely eliminate the need for learning-rate decay schedules—their most notable empirical advantage remains unexplained.
