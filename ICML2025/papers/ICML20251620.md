## Problem

Min-ε segmented regression with exactly k segments has an optimal O(n²) dynamic programming solution that is intractable for large datasets, while existing O(n log n) heuristics (Acharya et al., 2016) produce MSE errors 2–5× higher than optimal and either require knowledge of the noise variance or create more than k segments, which hampers qualitative breakpoint analysis. The paper addresses the gap between scalability and statistical efficiency: achieving near-optimal accuracy with exactly k segments at O(n log n) time without any assumptions on the noise distribution.

## Input / Output

The method consumes n ordered noisy samples (x_i, y_i) along with a target segment count k and polynomial degree d (where each segment requires at least d samples for a unique OLS fit). It outputs exactly k piecewise regression segments with their breakpoint positions and per-segment OLS parameter vectors β; the resulting piecewise function is not required to be continuous at breakpoints. The algorithm requires no input about the noise variance σ, distinguishing it from Acharya et al.'s σ-based variant.

## Method

The core contribution is a greedy merging algorithm that achieves constant-time (O(d²)) segment merging by precomputing a (d+1)×(d+1) symmetric matrix C per segment, which encodes the Gram matrix A = XᵀX, the cross-product B = Xᵀy, and the sum of squared responses. Merging two segments reduces to adding their C matrices, and the resulting RSS is computed as the grand sum of the Hadamard product D ⊙ C, where D is derived from the OLS parameters β — both operations are independent of the number of samples. The algorithm initializes ⌊n/d⌋ segments, uses a priority queue to iteratively merge the adjacent pair with the smallest additional RSS until exactly k segments remain, then performs a post-optimization pass that shifts each breakpoint by up to a quarter of its adjacent segment's size (processed from largest to smallest segment) using Sherman-Morrison rank-1 updates for O(d²) per-sample adjustments. This yields O(n log n) time and O(n) space while matching the optimal DP solution's MSE within ~3% on average.

## Results

On a real-world dataset of 70,607 CPU-usage samples, the proposed method matches the optimal dynamic program (DP) exactly (MSE = 1.82×10⁴) while running in 0.01 s versus DP's 2,938 s (~293,000× speedup). On synthetic piecewise-linear data, the relative MSE is 1.03 (only 3% above the optimal DP), compared to 495× for Acharya's k-segment heuristic and 3.43× for its 2k variant. The paper reports up to 1,000× lower MSE and ~100× less runtime than Acharya et al. (2016) on datasets exceeding 10⁴ samples.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two baselines from Acharya et al. (2016): the exact dynamic program (DP, O(n²)) and the O(n log n) heuristic in its k, 2k, and 4k segment variants. Against DP, the proposed method matches it exactly on the real bwa/sarek dataset (identical MSE of 1.82×10⁴) and is only 3% worse in relative MSE on synthetic data, while being ~293,000× faster on the 70k-sample dataset. Against Acharya's k-segment heuristic, the proposed method achieves a relative MSE of 1.03 versus 495.13 (a ~480× improvement) and runs ~30× faster. Against Acharya's 2k and 4k variants, the proposed method still wins on both MSE (1.03 vs. 3.43 and 4.95) and runtime, while using exactly k segments—preserving the qualitative interpretability of breakpoint positions that the 2k/4k variants sacrifice. Notably, on the real dataset, Acharya's 2k and 4k variants actually perform *worse* than its k variant (12.2% and 9.18% relative error vs. 2.61%), demonstrating that more segments do not guarantee better results in practice.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the greedy segment-merging framework introduced by Acharya et al. (2016), which reduced min-ε segmented regression from O(n²) (dynamic programming, Bai & Perron 1998) to O(n log n) but at the cost of requiring 2k or 4k segments for acceptable accuracy and knowledge of the noise variance. The key departure is a constant-time merging mechanism: by precomputing a (d+1)×(d+1) symmetric matrix C per segment (built from the Vandermonde Gram matrix and cross-products with y), two segments can be merged and their combined RSS computed in O(d²) time independent of segment size, using a Hadamard-product formulation with the OLS parameter matrix D. The placement-optimization step further leverages the Sherman-Morrison rank-1 update to adjust breakpoints in O(d²) per sample shift, a technique the paper notes was implemented suboptimally in Acharya et al.'s code.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method delivers a practical sweet spot: exactly k segments (enabling qualitative breakpoint interpretation), no noise-distribution assumptions, O(n log n) time, and an average MSE only 3% above the optimal dynamic program—while running ~100× faster on datasets over 10⁴ samples. On the real bwa CPU-usage dataset it reproduces the DP solution exactly in 10 ms versus 49 minutes. Open questions include extension to multidimensional breakpoints (rectangular segments, as in Diakonikolas et al. 2020), adaptation to enforce continuity at breakpoints, and whether the constant-time merging trick generalizes to non-polynomial basis functions. The paper also notes that for very small n with abundant compute, the exact DP remains marginally more accurate, so the heuristic's advantage is most pronounced at scale.
