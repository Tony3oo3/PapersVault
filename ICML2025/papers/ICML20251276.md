## Problem

Online MDPs with aggregate bandit feedback—where the agent observes only the total loss over an entire trajectory rather than per-step losses—had no policy optimization algorithms and only suboptimal regret bounds from reduction-based approaches (e.g., to distorted linear bandits). This paper introduces the first Policy Optimization algorithms for this setting, achieving a near-optimal regret bound under known dynamics and a significant improvement over prior work in the unknown dynamics case.

## Input / Output

The agent observes the full trajectory (states and actions at each step) but receives only the scalar sum of all step losses as feedback, rather than individual per-step losses. In the known dynamics case the transition function is given; in the unknown dynamics case it must be learned from experience via Bernstein-style confidence sets. The output is a sequence of policies (state-to-action distributions per time step) accompanied by high-probability regret guarantees.

## Method

The core technical contribution is the U-function, defined as the expected total trajectory cost conditioned on visiting a specific state-action pair at a given time. Unlike the standard Q-function (cost-to-go), the U-function can be unbiasedly estimated using only the aggregated trajectory loss, making it the natural quantity for this feedback model. The Value Difference Lemma is extended to decompose regret with respect to U-functions rather than Q-functions, enabling a per-state-action bandit interpretation. The algorithm applies Multiplicative Weight Updates with respect to U-function estimates, augmented with a bonus function that cancels the distribution mismatch between the current policy's occupancy and the optimal policy's occupancy. For unknown dynamics, upper and lower confidence bounds on the occupancy measure replace exact values, and the bonus is computed optimistically over the transition confidence set.

## Results

Under known dynamics, Algorithm 1 achieves the first near-optimal regret bound of $\tilde{\Theta}(H^2\sqrt{SAK})$, matching the newly established lower bound of $\Omega(H^2\sqrt{SAK})$ up to logarithmic factors. Under unknown dynamics, Algorithm 2 achieves regret of $\tilde{O}(H^3 S\sqrt{AK})$, improving the previous best adversarial bound by a factor of $H^2 S^5 A^2$. Notably, the unknown-dynamics regret matches the state-of-the-art for policy optimization with the more informative semi-bandit feedback (Luo et al., 2021). Both algorithms provide high-probability guarantees with closed-form updates, avoiding the convex optimization required by prior reductions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the reduction to efficient linear bandits (Hazan & Karnin, 2016), Algorithm 1 improves the known-dynamics regret from $\tilde{O}(H^2 SA\sqrt{K})$ to the optimal $\tilde{O}(H^2\sqrt{SAK})$, a factor of $\sqrt{SA}$ improvement, while also providing a high-probability guarantee instead of an expected one. Against Cohen et al. (2021b) (reduction to Distorted Bandit Online Linear Optimization), Algorithm 2 improves the unknown-dynamics adversarial regret from $\tilde{O}(H^5 S^6 A^{5/2}\sqrt{K})$ to $\tilde{O}(H^3 S\sqrt{AK})$, a dramatic factor of $H^2 S^5 A^2$ improvement. Against Efroni et al. (2021) and Cassel et al. (2024), which handle stochastic i.i.d. losses, Algorithm 2 achieves $\tilde{O}(H^3 S\sqrt{AK})$ versus their $\tilde{O}(H^{7/2} S^2 A^{3/2}\sqrt{K})$, an improvement of $SA\sqrt{H}$ even though the paper's setting is strictly harder (adversarial losses). The paper also establishes the first lower bound $\Omega(H^2\sqrt{SAK})$ specific to aggregate bandit feedback, confirming optimality in the known-dynamics case.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Policy Optimization framework for adversarial MDPs (Luo et al., 2021; Shani et al., 2020) and the value difference lemma (Kakade & Langford, 2002), but introduces a key conceptual departure: the U-function, which replaces the Q-function in the regret decomposition. While Cohen et al. (2021b) previously addressed online MDPs with aggregate bandit feedback via a reduction to Distorted Linear Bandits (DBOLO), this paper shows that a direct PO approach using the U-function yields substantially tighter bounds and a closed-form update. The analysis closely follows the structure of Luo et al. (2021) for semi-bandit feedback, adapted to the less informative aggregate feedback setting where only the total trajectory loss is observed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The U-function provides a natural and elegant tool for value estimation under aggregate bandit feedback, and its introduction opens a path toward extending PO to function-approximation settings (though the U-function is not linear, making this non-trivial). The unknown-dynamics regret bound matching the semi-bandit PO bound is a strong theoretical result, but a gap of H√S remains from the lower bound, and the exact optimal rate for unknown dynamics is still open. For practitioners, the closed-form update and improved computational efficiency over DBOLO-based reductions make this approach more deployable in settings like multi-turn dialogue or robotic manipulation where only end-of-trajectory feedback is available.
