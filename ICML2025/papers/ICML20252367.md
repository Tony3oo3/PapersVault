## Problem

Existing neural operators for PDEs face a fundamental trade-off: transformer-based methods (GNOT, Transolver, ONO) capture rich kernel integral interactions but incur quadratic attention complexity that limits scalability to high-resolution grids, while frequency-based methods (FNO) scale well but suffer from spectral decay and limited expressiveness for complex dynamics. State-space models (SSMs) like Mamba offer linear-time sequence processing with strong long-range dependency modeling, yet their application to PDE operator learning remained largely unexplored in scientific machine learning.

## Input / Output

LaMO accepts PDE inputs in three geometric formats: regular grids (Darcy at 85×85, Navier-Stokes at 64×64 with 10 temporal frames), structured meshes (Airfoil at 221×51, Pipe at 129×129, Plasticity at 101×31), and point clouds (Elasticity with 972 points). The input is first lifted to a higher-dimensional embedding, then compressed into a fixed number of latent tokens (M ≪ N physical tokens) via adaptive Perceiver-style weights, and the output is decoded back to the original spatial resolution. For time-dependent problems like Navier-Stokes and Plasticity, the output spans multiple future timesteps (10 and 20 frames respectively), while steady-state problems produce a single solution field.

## Method

LaMO replaces the self-attention mechanism in neural operators with bidirectional selective state-space models (Mamba) operating in a compressed latent space. A Perceiver-inspired encoder projects N physical tokens into M latent tokens using input-adaptive softmax weights, then L stacked latent blocks apply multihead bidirectional SSMs (with ZOH discretization and input-dependent B, C, Δ parameters) followed by channel mixing, before a decoder projects back to physical space. For regular grids, a four-directional scan (top-left→bottom-right, bottom-right→top-left, top-right→bottom-left, bottom-left→top-right) replaces the standard forward/backward pass to capture spatial continuity. The paper proves (Theorem 3.4) that the latent SSM layer is equivalent to a learnable kernel integral operator, and that ZOH discretization generalizes the Euler method, establishing a rigorous connection between SSM dynamics and PDE solution operators. This yields linear O(NMD) complexity with M held constant, enabling 3× fewer parameters and 1.8× faster inference than transformer baselines while achieving 32.3% average relative L2 error reduction across six PDE benchmarks.

## Results

LaMO achieves the lowest mean relative ℓ₂ error across all six PDE benchmarks, with an average 32.3% improvement over the second-best baseline (Transolver). The largest gains appear on time-dependent PDEs: Navier-Stokes (0.0460 vs. 0.0957, a 51.9% reduction) and Plasticity (0.0007 vs. 0.0013, a 46.1% reduction). On regular-grid Darcy flow, LaMO reaches 0.0039 relative ℓ₂ error, outperforming the next-best Transolver (0.0059) by 33.9%. LaMO also uses 3× fewer parameters and is 1.8× faster per epoch than Transolver.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LaMO outperforms all 17 baselines on every one of the six benchmarks. The closest competitor is Transolver (the prior SOTA transformer-based operator), which LaMO beats by 21.8% on Elasticity (0.0050 vs. 0.0064), 51.9% on Navier-Stokes (0.0460 vs. 0.0957), 33.9% on Darcy (0.0039 vs. 0.0059), 46.1% on Plasticity (0.0007 vs. 0.0013), 22.6% on Airfoil (0.0041 vs. 0.0053), and 17.4% on Pipe (0.0038 vs. 0.0046). Among frequency-based operators, LSM is the strongest (e.g., 0.0065 on Darcy, 0.0025 on Plasticity) but is still substantially outperformed by LaMO. Transformer-based methods like GNOT (0.0086 on Elasticity) and ONO (0.0076 on Darcy) trail LaMO by wide margins, particularly on time-dependent and turbulent flow tasks where SSM's long-range dependency modeling provides a clear advantage. Classic CNN baselines (U-Net, ResNet, Swin) and DeepONet are consistently the weakest, with errors 2–10× higher than LaMO across all benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

LaMO sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) the selective state-space model (Mamba, Gu & Dao 2023), whose input-dependent S6 parameters and ZOH discretization form the core sequence-processing mechanism; (2) the Perceiver-style latent token encoder/decoder (Jaegle et al. 2021), which compresses physical tokens into a small latent set for linear-complexity processing; and (3) the kernel-integral neural operator framework (Kovachki et al. 2021), which formalizes PDE solution operators as learnable integral transforms. The key departure from prior work is replacing the quadratic-complexity attention mechanism in transformer-based operators (GNOT, ONO, Transolver) with bidirectional SSMs operating on latent tokens, and providing a theoretical proof (Theorem 3.4) that the SSM recurrence is equivalent to a Monte Carlo approximation of a learnable kernel integral—thereby unifying the SSM and neural-operator literatures that had previously developed in parallel.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 32.3% average improvement over the prior SOTA (Transolver) across six diverse PDE benchmarks, combined with linear scaling in mesh size and 3× fewer parameters, makes LaMO a practical candidate for large-scale PDE surrogates where transformer-based operators become prohibitive. The data-efficiency result (competitive performance with only 40% of training data) is particularly relevant for scientific applications where generating ground-truth solutions is expensive. Open questions remain: the paper is limited to 2D PDEs, pretraining/foundation-model paradigms are explicitly left unexplored, and it is unclear whether the multidirectional scan strategy generalizes to 3D or higher-dimensional domains without combinatorial blowup in scan directions.
