## Problem

Protein language models (pLMs) are widely used for structure and function prediction, yet the specific features they learn from millions of protein sequences remain opaque. Prior analyses suggested pLMs store common motifs and pairwise contacts, but no method existed to systematically extract and characterize individual interpretable features from pLM representations. This paper bridges mechanistic interpretability (established for LLMs via sparse autoencoders) to mechanistic biology, asking what pLMs actually represent and whether those representations can reveal known or novel biological mechanisms.

## Input / Output

The SAEs consume ESM-2 (650M, 33 layers) residual stream activations at intermediate layers, trained on 1M random UniRef50 sequences of ≤1022 residues. The SAE outputs a sparse TopK encoding where only the k largest latent dimensions are non-zero; these latents are visualized via InterProt as activation overlays on aligned sequences and 3D structures. For downstream evaluation, mean-pooled SAE latent vectors feed linear probes that produce residue-level secondary structure labels, protein-level subcellular localization classes, thermostability regression scores, and binary CHO cell expression predictions. Family specificity is assessed by thresholding a single latent's activation to binary-classify Swiss-Prot sequences (clustered at 30% identity) into InterPro families.

## Method

The paper trains TopK SAEs (rather than the ReLU SAEs used in concurrent work InterPLM) on ESM-2 residual streams, choosing TopK for explicit L0-norm control and improved reconstruction at a given sparsity level. Two systematic classification schemes are introduced: an activation-pattern taxonomy (point, periodic, short/medium/long motif, whole) based on rule-based criteria over top-activating sequences, and a family-specificity metric where a latent is labeled family-specific if it achieves F1 > 0.7 in binary-classifying InterPro family membership. The key interpretability contribution is interpretable probing: linear probes trained on SAE latents for four downstream tasks, where the largest-magnitude coefficients are manually inspected in InterProt to identify latents corresponding to known biological determinants (e.g., bipartite NLS motifs, signal peptide cleavage sites, hydrophobic core residues for thermostability). A blinded human evaluation study with seven protein biology raters confirms SAE latents are rated interpretable ~80% of the time versus a much lower rate for raw ESM neurons. The paper further shows that SAE hyperparameters (sparsity k, expansion factor) and ESM layer choice systematically shift the proportion of family-specific features, with middle layers containing the most.

## Results

In a blinded human evaluation, approximately 80% of SAE latents were rated as interpretable, substantially outperforming ESM neuron baselines (Figure 4). For downstream linear probing, SAE embeddings achieve performance comparable to ESM embeddings across all four tasks (secondary structure, subcellular localization, thermostability, and CHO cell expression), with SAE consistently outperforming ESM on secondary structure prediction in most layers (Figure 5). Family-specific latents peak in early-to-mid ESM layers and increase with higher sparsity (lower k) and larger expansion factors. The SAE coefficient distribution shows higher variance (2.03e-3 vs. 3.24e-4) and greater negative skew than ESM, reflecting more monosemantic, individually predictive latents.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is ESM-2 (650M) residual stream embeddings, compared directly against SAE latent embeddings via linear probes on four downstream tasks. SAE probes match ESM probes in overall performance across subcellular localization, thermostability, and CHO cell expression, but consistently outperform ESM on secondary structure prediction in most layers, attributed to the abundance of SAE latents corresponding to secondary structure elements. In the human interpretability study, SAE latents receive a 'yes' rating at roughly 80%, far exceeding the ESM neuron baseline. The paper also contrasts its TopK SAE architecture with the ReLU-based SAEs used in the concurrent InterPLM work (Simon & Zou, 2024), noting that TopK SAEs allow explicit L0 sparsity control and improved reconstruction at a given sparsity level. Overall, SAEs are competitive or superior to raw ESM embeddings for probing while providing substantially greater interpretability of the learned features.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper transplants the sparse autoencoder (SAE) framework from LLM mechanistic interpretability (Gao et al., 2024) to protein language models, specifically ESM-2. It is concurrent with Simon & Zou (2024, InterPLM), which also trained SAEs on ESM-2 but used ReLU-based architectures and LLM-assisted annotation; this work instead employs TopK SAEs for explicit L0 sparsity control and introduces systematic classification of latents by activation pattern and family specificity. The broader pLM interpretability literature (attention maps resembling contact maps, influence functions revealing homology dependence, categorical Jacobian analyses) provides the motivation, but those methods require supervision or task-specific analysis, whereas SAEs offer an unsupervised, feature-level decomposition.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes a practical pipeline (InterProt visualizer + linear probing on SAE latents) that can surface known biological determinants—nuclear localization signals, signal peptide cleavage sites, thermostability-relevant amino acid compositions—directly from model internals, offering a template for generating testable hypotheses about unknown mechanisms. Key limitations include restriction to a single 650M-parameter model, reliance on mean-pooling for protein-level tasks, and interpretation bounded by current biological knowledge. Open questions include whether SAE features scale with model size, whether steering family-specific latents can be harnessed for protein engineering, and whether task-specific training data yields more functionally relevant features.
