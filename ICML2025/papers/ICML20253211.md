## Problem

Existing diffusion-based graph representation methods (e.g., DDM) adopt full-spectrum MSE reconstruction objectives designed for generation, which forces the model to encode high-frequency details that are irrelevant or even detrimental to downstream recognition tasks. The paper identifies and formalizes this generation-recognition misalignment: minimizing MSE over all frequency components allocates encoder capacity to superfluous information, reducing the mutual information available for label-relevant signals.

## Input / Output

The method takes a node feature matrix X ∈ R^{N×d} and a graph adjacency matrix A ∈ R^{N×N} as input. Because the adjacency is extremely sparse (edges grow linearly while non-edges grow quadratically), the method does not regress individual edge entries; instead, it distills global low-frequency Laplacian information via a learnable encoder and feeds it as conditioning to the denoiser. The reconstruction target is solely the clean node feature matrix. Outputs are node embeddings (concatenated activations from U-Net upsampling layers) for node classification, or graph-level embeddings for graph classification, extracted without any labeled supervision.

## Method

SDMG introduces two learnable low-frequency encoders: a topology encoder (MLP) that approximates the first q eigenvectors of the normalized Laplacian by minimizing ‖L̂ − AAᵀ‖_F² (grounded in Eckart-Young-Mirsky), and a feature encoder (GAT) that acts as a low-pass filter on node features. These produce a conditioning vector C concatenated into a U-Net denoising decoder that reconstructs the clean feature matrix from a noised version. The core novelty is the Multi-Scale Smoothing (MSS) loss, which replaces pointwise MSE with cosine similarity between original and reconstructed features aggregated over k-hop neighborhoods (H^(k) = A_norm^k X), effectively applying a low-pass filter g(λ̂_i) = (1−λ̂_i)^k that preferentially penalizes low-frequency reconstruction errors while tolerating high-frequency discrepancies. The U-Net uses GAT layers with element-wise fusion for graph classification and MLPs with additive fusion for node classification, and a masking strategy (inspired by Soft Diffusion) can be applied to node features to encourage holistic representation learning.

## Results

On node classification, SDMG (w/mask) achieves 84.3% on Cora, 73.9% on CiteSeer, 91.6% on Computer, and 94.7% on Photo, outperforming the prior diffusion-based method DDM (83.1%, 72.1%, 89.8%, 93.8%) and the strongest self-supervised baseline GraphACL (84.2%, 73.6%, 89.8%, 93.3%) on four of six datasets. On graph classification, SDMG achieves the best accuracy on four of five benchmarks, including 76.03% on IMDB-B, 82.23% on COLLAB, and 91.58% on MUTAG, surpassing DDM and all contrastive/generative baselines. The masked variant (SDMG w/mask) consistently edges out the unmasked version, suggesting that random feature masking encourages more holistic representation learning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SDMG (w/mask) outperforms the closest self-supervised competitor GraphACL by 0.1% on Cora, 0.3% on CiteSeer, 1.8% on Computer, and 1.4% on Photo, while trailing it by 2.0% on PubMed (80.0 vs 82.0) and matching GAT's supervised result on OGBN-Arxiv (72.1). Against the primary diffusion-based baseline DDM, SDMG (w/mask) gains 1.2% on Cora, 1.8% on CiteSeer, 1.8% on Computer, and 0.9% on Photo, confirming that the low-frequency encoders and MSS loss address DDM's overfitting to high-frequency noise. On graph classification (Table 2), SDMG beats DDM on all five datasets (e.g., +2.0% on IMDB-B, +1.5% on COLLAB, +1.4% on MUTAG) and surpasses the best self-supervised method GraphMAE on IMDB-B (+0.5%) and COLLAB (+1.9%), though it falls short on PROTEINS (73.17 vs 75.30). Random-walk methods (Node2Vec, DeepWalk, Graph2Vec) are consistently 5–20% behind, underscoring the advantage of learned spectral-aware representations over purely topological embeddings. Overall, SDMG achieves state-of-the-art or near-state-of-the-art results across 10 of 11 benchmarks, with the sole notable gap being PubMed where GraphACL's contrastive objective appears better suited to that dataset's spectral characteristics.

## Relation to Prior Work

SDMG extends the diffusion-based graph representation learning paradigm introduced by DDM (Yang et al., 2024), which first applied denoising diffusion to node feature spaces with a U-Net decoder. The key departure is identifying and addressing a fundamental misalignment: DDM's full-spectrum MSE reconstruction objective, inherited from generative diffusion (Ho et al., 2020; Dhariwal & Nichol, 2021), overemphasizes high-frequency details that are detrimental to discriminative representations. SDMG introduces two architectural modifications—learnable low-frequency encoders (GAT for features, MLP for topology) and a Multi-Scale Smoothing (MSS) loss that replaces pointwise MSE with multi-hop cosine similarity—thereby reorienting the diffusion objective from faithful generation toward recognition-oriented representation learning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that reconstructing only the lowest ~5–20% of Laplacian eigenmodes suffices for strong node representations, while full-spectrum reconstruction actively degrades performance, challenges the core assumption in generative SSL that higher reconstruction fidelity yields better embeddings. The theoretical result (Theorem 3.1) formalizes this tension via information-bottleneck arguments, showing that MSE minimization and mutual-information maximization are partially misaligned when the signal decomposes into relevant and superfluous components. Open questions include whether the low-frequency bias generalizes to tasks requiring fine-grained high-frequency cues (anomaly detection, fine community boundaries), how the approach scales to very large or dynamic graphs where even approximate spectral encoders become costly, and whether adaptive, task-aware frequency selection can be learned end-to-end.
