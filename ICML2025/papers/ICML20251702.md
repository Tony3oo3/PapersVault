## Problem

The g-BH algorithm for OOD detection relies on empirical p-values computed from a calibrated set, yet the impact of calibrated set size on detection performance has not been systematically studied. This paper reveals that small calibrated sets significantly degrade the g-BH algorithm's true positive rate, and provides both a theoretical characterization of this degradation and a practical remedy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251702/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a test image and a calibrated set of in-distribution examples (drawn from CIFAR-10, 32×32 RGB) as input, and outputs a binary ID/OOD decision per test sample. The calibrated set size m is a critical hyperparameter: the paper shows that reducing m from 500 to 200 can increase the probability of TPR ≤ 0.9 from <0.01 to 0.32. In the eg-BH variant, the training set is partitioned into L subsets, each serving as a separate calibrated set paired with a score function trained on the complementary data.

## Method

The paper first proves that the conditional expectation of TPR given the calibrated set follows a Beta(m−β+1, β) distribution, where β = ⌊f⁻¹(α)(m+1)⌋−1, establishing that smaller m increases variance and lowers expected TPR. To mitigate this, the proposed eg-BH algorithm partitions the training set into L parts, trains L separate score functions (each on the complement of one part), computes L empirical p-values per test sample, and integrates them via a weighted power-mean: p̄ = ((κ+1)Σw_j p̂_j^κ)^(1/κ). Theorem 5.1 guarantees this integrated value is a valid p-value for any κ > 0, and Theorem 5.2 shows it can be exact (uniform under H₀) when the maximum weight w* ≤ min{1/2, 1/(1+κ), κ/(1+κ)}, preserving the FDR control of the g-BH procedure without requiring structural dependence among p-values.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 as ID data with L=5 (small calibrated set), eg-BH achieves an average F1-score of 83.81% and TPR of 84.61% using the Energy score (ResNet18), compared to 69.45% and 73.59% for g-BH, while maintaining a comparable FPR (25.88% vs. 27.06%). With the MSP score, eg-BH reaches 87.67% average F1 and 89.64% TPR versus 80.84% and 83.39% for g-BH. The largest gains appear on TinyImageNet (F1: 59.34%→73.25%) and Place365 (F1: 56.07%→78.55%) under the Energy score, where the calibrated set is most insufficient for g-BH.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is the g-BH algorithm (Ma et al., ICML 2024), which uses a single empirical p-value from one calibrated subset. eg-BH consistently outperforms g-BH on all six OOD datasets under both the Energy and MSP score functions. The average F1 improvement is 14.36 points (69.45%→83.81%) with Energy and 6.83 points (80.84%→87.67%) with MSP, while FPR remains comparable or slightly lower. The advantage is most pronounced on harder OOD distributions: TinyImageNet gains 13.91 F1 points and Place365 gains 22.48 F1 points under Energy, reflecting that g-BH degrades most severely when the calibrated set is too small to capture distributional structure. The paper further shows (Figure 3) that the performance gap widens as L increases (i.e., the calibrated set shrinks), confirming that eg-BH's ensemble strategy is specifically designed to mitigate the small-calibration limitation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Ma et al. (2024), which first framed OOD detection as a multiple hypothesis testing problem and proposed the g-BH algorithm with FDR guarantees. The current work identifies an underexplored dependency of g-BH on the calibrated set size, provides a beta-distribution characterization of the conditional TPR, and extends the p-value integration framework from Kolmogorov & Castelnuovo (1930) to construct a valid ensemble p-value. The methodological contribution (eg-BH) departs from the single-calibrated-set paradigm of g-BH by training multiple score functions on complementary data splits and integrating their empirical p-values via a power-mean construction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical result that TPR conditional on the calibrated set follows a Beta(m−β+1, β) distribution gives practitioners a concrete, quantifiable understanding of how calibration set size affects detection reliability, which was previously unaddressed. The eg-BH algorithm offers a practical remedy for the common scenario where training data is limited and reserving a large calibration set causes underfitting. Open questions include whether the ensemble approach extends to settings with correlated p-values across splits, how the choice of κ and weights w_i should be tuned in practice, and whether the FDR guarantee of the integrated p-values holds under the PRDS condition assumed by the original g-BH framework.
