## Problem

Designing procurement auction mechanisms that approximately maximize welfare (auctioneer value minus seller costs) under a submodular value function while simultaneously satisfying incentive compatibility, individual rationality, and non-negative auctioneer surplus (NAS) remains an open challenge. The underlying optimization problem—regularized submodular maximization—is computationally hard, and existing mechanisms either lack approximation guarantees, are computationally infeasible (e.g., VCG), or fail to preserve NAS when converting optimization algorithms into truthful mechanisms.

## Input / Output

The mechanism receives a bid profile b (each seller's reported cost) and operates on a known monotone submodular value function f: 2^N → R_{≥0} with f(∅)=0. It outputs a winning seller set S* and a payment vector p to those sellers. In the offline setting all bids are submitted simultaneously; in the online setting sellers arrive sequentially and the auctioneer must make irrevocable accept/reject decisions. The payment rule is constructed via a critical-bid procedure: for each winner, the mechanism re-runs the underlying algorithm with that seller's bid set to infinity and extracts the supremum bid at which the seller would still be selected across all intermediate rounds.

## Method

The paper's core contribution is a general framework that converts any submodular optimization meta-algorithm A=(G) satisfying three mild assumptions (score non-increasing in own bid, negative score when bid exceeds marginal contribution, score independent of other sellers' bids) into a sealed-bid mechanism that is IC, IR, NAS, and preserves the algorithm's bi-criteria welfare guarantee. The payment rule uses a critical-bid construction: for each winner i, the mechanism re-runs A with b_i=∞, records all intermediate solution sets, and sets p_i to the maximum over rounds of the supremum bid at which i would still be selected with positive score. On the optimization side, the paper improves the distorted greedy analysis to show (1−e^{−β}, β+o(1)) bi-criteria approximation simultaneously for all β∈[0,1], matching the Pareto frontier up to o(1). The framework extends to the online setting via posted-price mechanisms and to descending auctions, where a cost-scaled greedy demand oracle yields (1/2,1)-approximation even under adversarial price-decrement schedules.

## Results

The paper's main theoretical contribution is showing that the distorted greedy algorithm achieves a (1−e^{−β}, β+o(1)) bi-criteria approximation simultaneously for all β∈[0,1], improving upon the previously known (1−1/e, 1) guarantee and matching the Pareto frontier up to o(1) terms. Empirically, on a coverage problem from the SNAP wiki-Vote graph (~7000 sets, 2800 vertices), greedy-based mechanisms achieve polynomial running time while VCG and Descending Auction (DA) with an exact oracle exhibit super-polynomial (MIP-based) complexity. In welfare at n=4000, the ordering among approximation algorithms is Greedy-margin > Greedy-rate > Cost-scaled Greedy > Distorted Greedy, and direct (sealed-bid) implementations consistently outperform their DA variants. DA with an optimal oracle, when computationally feasible, achieves the highest welfare but at the greatest running-time cost.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares four submodular optimization algorithms as mechanism backbones. Greedy-margin (Kleinberg et al., 2004) has no worst-case guarantee but empirically achieves the highest welfare among all greedy variants at n=4000. Greedy-rate (Feige et al., 2013) provides a parametrized guarantee and ranks second in empirical welfare. Distorted Greedy (Harshaw et al., 2019) receives the paper's improved analysis—achieving (1−e^{−β}, β+o(1)) for all β∈[0,1]—yet empirically performs worst among the greedy methods and has the slowest polynomial running time due to the absence of a diminishing-return structure. Cost-scaled Greedy (Nikolakaki et al., 2021) provides a (1/2, 1) guarantee in the online/adversarial setting and is the only algorithm that fits the descending auction framework with provable guarantees; it ranks third in offline welfare. Across all comparisons, direct sealed-bid implementations outperform their descending-auction counterparts, and DA with an exact oracle (when computationally feasible) outperforms all approximation-based mechanisms in welfare.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two research threads: regularized submodular maximization (Sviridenko et al. 2017; Harshaw et al. 2019) and procurement auction mechanism design (Nisan & Ronen 1999; Singer 2010; Cary et al. 2008). It builds directly on the distorted greedy algorithm's (1−1/e, 1) bi-criteria guarantee, improving the analysis to (1−e^{−β}, β+o(1)) for all β∈[0,1] simultaneously (matching Feldman's 2021 tightness bound), and on the cost-scaled greedy algorithm for online submodular optimization (Nikolakaki et al. 2021). The key conceptual departure is a general framework that converts any submodular optimization algorithm satisfying mild scoring-function assumptions into a sealed-bid or posted-price mechanism that is IC, IR, and NAS while preserving the algorithm's approximation guarantee—something prior work on budget-feasible mechanisms (Singer 2010; Balkanski et al. 2022) did not achieve for the regularized objective with the NAS property.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a practical recipe for turning off-the-shelf submodular optimization algorithms into deployable procurement mechanisms, with the NAS property ensuring the auctioneer never runs at a loss—a critical requirement for real-world adoption. The most striking open question is the gap between the (1/2, 1) guarantee for descending auctions and the (1−1/e, 1) guarantee for sealed-bid mechanisms; the paper shows this gap is not merely a technical artifact but is tied to a long-standing open problem in online submodular optimization. A further open direction is replacing the cost term in the objective with the actual payment (i.e., maximizing auctioneer surplus rather than welfare), which would be a more natural objective for the mechanism designer but is substantially harder.
