## Problem

VAR's scale-wise autoregressive image generation relies on a complex, non-uniform scale sequence {1,2,3,4,5,6,8,10,13,16} and a tightly coupled multi-scale residual VQGAN discrete tokenizer, which together limit generalization to other resolutions and prevent independent updates to the tokenizer. This paper asks whether a simpler scale design and a decoupled continuous tokenizer can match or exceed VAR's generation quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252057/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single ImageNet class label as input, encoded as a class token [C] that conditions the autoregressive Transformer. It produces a 256×256 or 512×512 RGB image by iteratively predicting five coarse-to-fine latent scales (token maps of sizes 1×1, 2×2, 4×4, 8×8, 16×16) and decoding the finest scale with a VAE decoder. Unlike VAR, the latent representations are continuous (from any off-the-shelf VAE) rather than discrete VQGAN tokens, and the scale sequence is simply {1,2,4,8,16} with each scale doubling the previous.

## Method

FlowAR replaces VAR's multi-scale residual VQGAN with any off-the-shelf continuous VAE, constructing coarse-scale token maps by directly downsampling the finest-scale latent. A scale-wise autoregressive Transformer (with KV cache) takes the class token and upsampled previous-scale latents as input and outputs continuous semantic representations ŝⁱ for each scale. A scale-wise flow matching model then predicts the velocity field that transforms Gaussian noise into the target latent at each scale, conditioned on ŝⁱ via the proposed Spatial-adaLN, which injects semantics position-by-position into the scale, shift, and gate parameters of adaptive layer normalization in every Transformer block. The training objective is a sum of L2 velocity-prediction losses across all five scales, and inference iterates the autoregressive-then-flow-matching pipeline from coarsest to finest scale before VAE decoding.

## Results

On ImageNet-256, FlowAR-H (1.9B params) achieves a state-of-the-art FID of 1.65, IS of 296.5, Precision of 0.83, and Recall of 0.60, outperforming all prior GAN, diffusion, flow matching, and autoregressive methods. The smaller FlowAR-L (589M params) reaches FID 1.90, surpassing the much larger VAR-d30 (2B params, FID 1.97) and LlamaGen-3B (3.1B params, FID 2.18). On ImageNet-512, FlowAR-L (590M params) achieves FID 2.43 and IS 295.1, beating VAR-d36 (2.3B params, FID 2.63) by 0.2 FID while using only 25.6% of its parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FlowAR consistently outperforms all 15 baselines on FID across both ImageNet-256 and ImageNet-512. Against the closest competitor VAR (scale-wise AR), FlowAR-L (589M) beats VAR-d20 (600M) by 1.05 FID (1.90 vs 2.95), and FlowAR-H (1.9B) beats VAR-d30 (2B) by 0.32 FID (1.65 vs 1.97). On ImageNet-512, FlowAR-L (590M) surpasses VAR-d36 (2.3B) by 0.2 FID (2.43 vs 2.63) using only 25.6% of the parameters. Against token-wise AR methods, FlowAR-H (1.9B, FID 1.65) outperforms LlamaGen-3B (3.1B, FID 2.18) and ViT-VQGAN (1.7B, FID 4.17). Against diffusion and flow matching methods, FlowAR-H beats DiT (675M, FID 2.27), SiT (675M, FID 2.06), and StyleGAN (166M, FID 2.30). The only metric where FlowAR does not lead is Inception Score, where VAR-d30 achieves a higher IS (323.1 vs 296.5), though FlowAR-H leads on FID, Precision, and Recall.

## Relation to Prior Work

FlowAR extends VAR's scale-wise autoregressive paradigm (next-scale prediction) by replacing its tightly coupled multi-scale VQGAN discrete tokenizer with any off-the-shelf continuous VAE, and by simplifying the scale sequence from a rigid {1,2,3,4,5,6,8,10,13,16} to a uniform {1,2,4,8,16} obtained by direct downsampling. The per-scale distribution is modeled via flow matching (Lipman et al., 2022) rather than categorical cross-entropy, with the autoregressive Transformer generating conditioning semantics injected through a novel Spatial-adaLN mechanism. This positions FlowAR at the intersection of the VAR line of work and the flow-matching/diffusion Transformer line (SiT, SD3), unifying sequential scale prediction with continuous generative modeling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical contribution is decoupling the tokenizer from the generator: any modern VAE can be swapped in without retraining a bespoke multi-scale tokenizer, which lowers the barrier to adopting scale-wise AR and to benefiting from future VAE improvements. The paper demonstrates this modularity empirically (DC-AE, SD-VAE, MAR-VAE all work) and shows that VAR's complex scale design is not merely a design choice but a genuine constraint (VAR fails to train under simplified scales). Open questions include whether the approach extends to text-to-image or video generation, whether the flow matching component can be replaced by faster single-step methods, and how the method scales beyond ImageNet-class-conditional settings to open-ended generation.
