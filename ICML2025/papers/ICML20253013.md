## Problem

Existing HDR novel view synthesis methods require multiple-exposure LDR images per view, which are vulnerable to motion artifacts, ghosting, and high capture/storage costs, and are impractical in dynamic or mobile-capture scenarios. This paper introduces the single-exposure HDR-NVS problem for the first time, where only one LDR image per view is available during training, making the HDR reconstruction fundamentally underdetermined.

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of single-exposure LDR images (one fixed exposure time per view, e.g., 18 training views) along with their camera poses, and produces HDR novel view renderings at arbitrary viewpoints. It operates as a meta-algorithm: the underlying 3D representation (NeRF or 3DGS) is first trained in LDR space, then lifted to HDR via a dedicated color converter, so the same framework can be plugged into different 3D scene models without modification. At inference, only the trained HDR 3D model is needed to render either HDR or LDR images from a novel camera pose.

## Method

Mono-HDR-3D first learns a standard LDR 3D scene model from single-exposure images, then lifts it to HDR via a camera-imaging-aware LDR-to-HDR Color Converter (L2H-CC) whose architecture mirrors the inverse camera response formula (Eq. 6), with three sub-modules simulating the scaling, correction, and noise terms using ReLU-constrained MLPs and a residual connection. A second module, the HDR-to-LDR Color Converter (H2L-CC), closes the loop by converting rendered HDR images back to LDR, providing self-supervised photometric supervision even when no HDR ground truth is available. The overall loss combines the underlying 3D model's LDR loss, an optional HDR ground-truth loss in the μ-law compressed domain, and the H2L closed-loop loss, with the L2H-CC and H2L-CC trained jointly. This design is motivated by the physical LDR image formation model (Eq. 3–5), ensuring the color conversion respects the sensor's linear gain, offset, and saturation behavior rather than learning an unconstrained mapping.

## Results

On the synthetic HDR-GS benchmark, Mono-HDR-GS achieves 38.57 dB PSNR and 0.975 SSIM for HDR novel view synthesis, outperforming HDR-GS (35.30 dB, 0.965) by +3.27 dB PSNR. For the NeRF-based variant, Mono-HDR-NeRF reaches 32.86 dB PSNR on HDR rendering versus only 13.76 dB for HDR-NeRF, a gap of +19.1 dB, since HDR-NeRF largely fails to converge under single-exposure training. On LDR rendering, Mono-HDR-GS attains 41.68 dB PSNR / 0.983 SSIM, surpassing HDR-GS (39.48 / 0.977). On real scenes (LDR only, no HDR ground truth), Mono-HDR-GS edges out HDR-GS with 35.81 vs. 35.34 dB PSNR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two multi-exposure HDR-NVS baselines: HDR-NeRF (Huang et al., CVPR 2022) and HDR-GS (Cai et al., NeurIPS 2024), both originally designed for multi-exposure LDR training but evaluated here under the single-exposure setting. Mono-HDR-GS decisively outperforms HDR-GS on all synthetic metrics: +3.27 dB PSNR (38.57 vs. 35.30) and +0.010 SSIM for HDR rendering, and +2.20 dB PSNR for LDR rendering, while maintaining comparable inference speed (137 vs. 147 fps). The gap is far more dramatic for the NeRF variant: Mono-HDR-NeRF achieves 32.86 dB HDR PSNR versus 13.76 dB for HDR-NeRF (+19.1 dB), because HDR-NeRF fails to converge without multi-exposure supervision, often producing entirely black or white outputs. On real scenes where no HDR ground truth exists, Mono-HDR-GS slightly leads HDR-GS (35.81 vs. 35.34 dB PSNR), while Mono-HDR-NeRF essentially ties HDR-NeRF (32.52 vs. 32.50 dB), indicating the advantage is most pronounced in the synthetic HDR evaluation where the full dynamic range can be measured.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the HDR-NVS line initiated by HDR-NeRF (Huang et al., CVPR 2022) and HDR-GS (Cai et al., NeurIPS 2024), both of which require multi-exposure LDR training images to recover HDR radiance. The key departure is the single-exposure constraint: rather than relying on multiple exposure levels per view, Mono-HDR-3D learns an LDR 3D scene model first and then lifts it to HDR via a camera-imaging-aware LDR-to-HDR Color Converter (L2H-CC), with a complementary HDR-to-LDR converter (H2L-CC) forming a closed loop for unsupervised optimization. The framework is designed as a meta-algorithm that wraps around existing 3D representations (NeRF, 3DGS), inheriting their geometric modeling while adding the color-space bridging logic grounded in the physical LDR image formation equation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the multi-exposure requirement, this work removes practical barriers (motion artifacts, ghosting, precise alignment, high capture cost) that have limited HDR-NVS deployment in dynamic scenes and on mobile devices, making HDR scene reconstruction accessible from ordinary single-shot photography. The closed-loop H2L-CC design is particularly notable for enabling HDR model optimization even without HDR ground-truth data, broadening applicability to real-world settings where HDR references are unavailable. However, evaluation is limited to 8 synthetic and 4 real scenes, and the real-data results show only marginal gains over baselines, leaving open questions about scalability to larger, more complex scenes and generalization across diverse camera models and exposure conditions.
