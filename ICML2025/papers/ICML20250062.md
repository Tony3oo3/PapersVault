## Problem

Existing online conformal prediction methods like ACI rely on bang-bang controllers that only guarantee long-run average coverage, even when data exhibits exploitable temporal structure. They provide no per-time-step conditional coverage guarantee, meaning individual confidence sets may be arbitrarily miscalibrated at any given moment. This paper asks whether one can simultaneously maintain adversarial long-run coverage and achieve time-conditional coverage (correct coverage at every step, conditioned on the past) for stochastic data with dependence.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a stream of conformal scores S_t = |Ŷ_t − Y_t| generated by a base forecaster (AR, Theta, Prophet, or Transformer) applied to time-series labels, along with a feature map Φ_t that constructs conformal covariates Z_t from past scores (e.g., Z_t = [S_{t−1}, …, S_{t−p}, w] for LQT of order p with bias weight w). It outputs a time-varying threshold q_t(θ) = Z_t^T θ that defines the confidence set C_t(X_t) = {y : s_t(X_t, y) ≤ q_t}. The parameter vector θ ∈ Θ is updated online via projected gradient descent, so the threshold adapts to the evolving score distribution without retraining the base forecaster.

## Method

The core algorithm (Algorithm 1) performs batched projected online gradient descent on the (1−α)-quantile loss ℓ_quantile(S_t − q_t(θ)), whose conditional expectation is minimized by the true conditional quantile q_t*. Unlike ACI's single scalar with fixed-magnitude bang-bang updates, the method uses a vector parameter θ and a parametric conformal predictor (e.g., LQT: q_t(θ) = θ^T[S_{t−1}, …, S_{t−p}, 1]) so that the threshold can track time-varying quantiles driven by autoregressive structure in the scores. Theoretically, the paper proves three guarantees: (i) adversarial long-run coverage via boundedness of the bias term, (ii) almost-sure convergence to the conditional quantile under well-specification with finite-sample O(t^{−c/2}) rates derived from a new strong-convexity lemma for batched conditional losses, and (iii) convergence to the best-in-class minimizer under mis-specification with β-mixing data, extending ergodic mirror descent to last-iterate strongly convex settings. Empirically, the method's advantage over ACI, SQT, PI, and PID baselines scales with the first-lag autocorrelation of the scores, with a pre-registered ERCOT electricity experiment showing >10% smaller confidence sets and >30% lower quantile loss across all 78 pre-registered hypotheses.

## Results

On the pre-registered ERCOT electricity demand dataset (autocorrelation 0.94), LQT(fixed) achieves a quantile loss of 29.10 and average set size of 691.5, representing over a 30% improvement in quantile loss and over 10% reduction in set size compared to the best baseline (PID(T): 42.34 quantile loss, 778.8 set size). On the larger Elec2 dataset, LQT(fixed) achieves 0.005 quantile loss and 0.16 set size versus 0.011–0.015 for baselines. On low-autocorrelation datasets (e.g., MSFT AR, autocorrelation 0.19), all methods perform comparably (quantile loss 0.088–0.091). All 78 pre-registered hypothesis tests comparing LQT(fixed) to each baseline were rejected at the 0.05 significance level.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ACI (Gibbs & Candès, 2021), LQT(fixed) achieves a 62% reduction in quantile loss on ERCOT (29.10 vs. 76.63) and a 67% reduction on Elec2 (0.005 vs. 0.015), with set sizes reduced by 38% and 35% respectively. Against the Conformal PID control family (Angelopoulos et al., 2023), LQT(fixed) outperforms PID(T) and PID(A) by 31% in quantile loss on ERCOT (29.10 vs. 42.34) and by 11–13% on MSFT Prophet (0.104 vs. 0.134). On low-autocorrelation datasets (MSFT AR, MSFT Theta), the proposed methods are on par with or slightly behind the best baselines (e.g., PID(T) achieves 0.128 vs. LQT's 0.139 on MSFT Theta quantile loss), confirming that the advantage is driven by the data's linear dependence. The paper notes that LQT is also substantially faster than PID methods (0.18s vs. 234.76s on Elec2), making it practical for large-scale deployment. All 78 pre-registered hypothesis tests (using Lobato's dependent-data test) comparing LQT(fixed) to each baseline across quantile loss, absolute loss, and set size were rejected at the 0.05 level on the ERCOT data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reframes online conformal prediction as an online optimization problem over a quantile loss, departing from the bang-bang controller paradigm of ACI (Gibbs & Candès, 2021) and its control-theory extensions (conformal PID, BCI). While prior work like decaying-step-size ACI (Angelopoulos et al., 2024) considered stochastic guarantees, it was limited to i.i.d. data; here the authors extend to general dependent stochastic processes (e.g., AR models) by leveraging the structure of the quantile loss and developing new convergence results for SGD with dependent data that do not require stationarity or mixing. The LQT algorithm's use of autoregressive score features as conformal covariates is a natural but previously unexploited connection between time-series modeling and conformal calibration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The time-conditional coverage guarantee (valid at every time step, not just on average) is a meaningful strengthening for practical deployment where a single miscalibrated prediction can be costly. The pre-registered ERCOT experiment with 78/78 significant hypotheses provides unusually strong empirical evidence, and the clear diagnostic (first-lag autocorrelation of scores) tells practitioners when to expect gains. Limitations include the focus on linear AR models for the conformal predictor, the requirement of well-specification for full conditional guarantees, and the fact that gains vanish for low-dependence data. Open questions include extending to richer nonlinear feature maps, covariate-conditional coverage, and whether the SGD convergence results generalize to weakly convex losses.
