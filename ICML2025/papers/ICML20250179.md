## Problem

De novo molecular structure generation from mass spectra remains a major bottleneck in metabolomics, with prior methods achieving near-zero structural accuracy. Existing approaches—autoregressive language models and fingerprint-to-SMILES decoders—fail to capture the permutation-invariant nature of spectra and molecules, do not enforce chemical formula constraints as a physical prior, and are limited by the scarcity of labeled spectrum-structure pairs (tens of thousands) relative to the virtually infinite supply of fingerprint-structure pairs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250179/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an MS/MS mass spectrum (a set of m/z–intensity peaks) and a known chemical formula (heavy-atom composition, typically inferred by off-the-shelf tools like SIRIUS or MIST-CF) as input. Because the formula fixes the number and types of heavy atoms, only the adjacency matrix with one-hot encoded bond types (no bond, single, double, triple, aromatic) needs to be generated. The output is a ranked list of k candidate molecular graphs, obtained by sampling 100 molecules per spectrum, filtering out invalid or disconnected structures, and ranking by frequency.

## Method

DiffMS is a formula-restricted encoder-decoder model. The encoder is the MIST formula transformer, which embeds each spectral peak with its SIRIUS-predicted formula assignment and applies a set transformer that implicitly models pairwise neutral losses; it is pretrained to predict Morgan fingerprints from spectra. The decoder is a discrete graph diffusion model (built on DiGress) using a Graph Transformer that denoises the adjacency matrix conditioned on fixed node features (atom types from the formula) and the spectrum embedding, employing a cosine noise schedule and a marginal-distribution prior over bond types. A key design choice is the pretraining-finetuning framework: the diffusion decoder is pretrained on 2.8M fingerprint-structure pairs from virtual chemical libraries (DSSTox, HMDB, COCONUT, MOSES) to learn strong structural conditioning, while the encoder is pretrained on spectrum-to-fingerprint prediction; the full model is then finetuned end-to-end on labeled spectrum-structure data. This leverages the virtually unlimited fingerprint-structure data to bridge the gap to the small spectrum-structure dataset, and discrete diffusion naturally accommodates the one-to-many mapping from spectrum to structure.

## Results

On NPLIB1, DiffMS achieves 8.34% top-1 accuracy and 15.44% top-10 accuracy, outperforming all baselines including MIST+MSNovelist (5.40% / 11.04%) and MADGEN (2.10% / 2.39%). On the more challenging MassSpecGym benchmark, DiffMS achieves 2.30% top-1 accuracy and 4.25% top-10 accuracy, more than doubling the next-best method MADGEN (1.31% / 1.54%) and being the only method alongside MADGEN to generate any correct structures. DiffMS also achieves 100% molecular validity and the highest Tanimoto similarity (0.35 top-1, 0.47 top-10 on NPLIB1; 0.28 top-1, 0.39 top-10 on MassSpecGym) among all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiffMS outperforms all five baseline families on both benchmarks across every metric. Against Spec2Mol (spectrum-to-SMILES CNN), DiffMS achieves 8.34% vs. 0% top-1 accuracy on NPLIB1 and 2.30% vs. 0% on MassSpecGym, with substantially lower MCES (11.95 vs. 27.82 on NPLIB1). Against MADGEN (scaffold-conditioned diffusion), DiffMS more than doubles top-1 accuracy on MassSpecGym (2.30% vs. 1.31%) and improves MCES from 27.47 to 18.45, while also leading on NPLIB1 (8.34% vs. 2.10%). Against MIST+MSNovelist (fingerprint-to-SMILES LSTM), DiffMS gains 2.94 points in top-1 accuracy on NPLIB1 (8.34% vs. 5.40%) and is the only method to produce correct structures on MassSpecGym (2.30% vs. 0%). Against MIST+Neuraldecipher (fingerprint-to-SMILES via CDDD), DiffMS leads by 6.02 points in top-1 accuracy on NPLIB1 (8.34% vs. 2.32%) and again is the sole method with non-zero accuracy on MassSpecGym. The SMILES and SELFIES Transformer baselines from the MassSpecGym paper achieve 0% accuracy on all metrics, highlighting the difficulty of the out-of-distribution setting that DiffMS partially overcomes.

## Relation to Prior Work

DiffMS builds on DiGress (Vignac et al., 2023) for discrete graph diffusion and the MIST formula transformer (Goldman et al., 2023b) for spectrum encoding, but departs from prior de novo generation methods (MSNovelist, Spec2Mol, MS2Mol) that rely on autoregressive SMILES decoding or intermediate scaffold/fingerprint representations. The key conceptual departure is formulating generation as a formula-constrained (heavy-atom-constrained) adjacency-matrix denoising problem, which natively enforces the known chemical composition and handles the one-to-many spectrum-to-structure mapping. A novel pretraining-finetuning framework exploits the virtually infinite supply of fingerprint-structure pairs to train the diffusion decoder, addressing the data scarcity of spectrum-structure pairs (tens of thousands).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Mass spectrometry structure elucidation remains a bottleneck in metabolomics and chemical discovery, with the majority of observed spectra still classified as 'metabolite dark matter.' DiffMS demonstrates that formula-constrained discrete diffusion with large-scale fingerprint pretraining can generate structurally close matches at rates far exceeding prior methods, and the pretraining scaling curve (nearly 4× top-1 accuracy from 0 to 2.8M pretraining molecules) suggests a clear path to further improvement as virtual chemical libraries grow. However, absolute accuracy remains low (8.34% top-1 on NPLIB1, 2.30% on MassSpecGym), and the reliance on externally predicted formulae introduces a compounding error source. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to larger molecular weight ranges, multi-adduct spectra, or settings where formula annotation is unreliable.
