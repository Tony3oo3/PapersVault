## Problem

While standard Transformers have well-established universal approximation and approximation rate results, the weight-tying constraint in Looped Transformers prevents direct extension of these findings. The approximation rate of Looped Transformers for sequence-to-sequence functions was completely unknown, and it was unclear whether the fixed-size recursive architecture could even compute the contextual mappings required for universal approximation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252781/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on fixed-length sequences of N token embeddings in R^d (input X ∈ [0,1]^{d×N}) and produces output sequences of the same shape, targeting permutation-equivariant continuous functions. The Looped Transformer consists of a single fixed-size Transformer block (two attention heads, head size 1, FFN width 49d+25) applied r times, sandwiched between two affine linear maps L1: R^d → R^{17d+9} and L2: R^{17d+9} → R^d, so the parameter count is O(d) and independent of both sequence length and desired accuracy.

## Method

The paper establishes the approximation rate of Looped Transformers by defining three novel moduli of continuity—sequence, contextual, and token continuity—and showing the error decomposes into terms governed by each. The constructive proof maps inputs through three sub-steps (token-wise quantization, contextual mapping via inner products in a δ^{-d}-base system, and sequential function-value mapping) all concatenated into a single Transformer block, requiring r = δ^{-1} + 2δ^{-(N+1)d} + N loops. A key limitation emerges: the weight-tied feed-forward network in Step 3 can only bound per-coordinate error by the maximum difference between adjacent contextual token embeddings, introducing additional dependence on token and contextual continuity absent in standard Transformers. To eliminate this, the authors propose Timestep-Modulated Looped Transformers (TMLT), which condition time-dependent scaling vectors on sinusoidal timestep encodings processed through a two-layer SiLU MLP, generating per-loop parameters for RMSNorm gains and residual scaling (γ1(t), γ2(t), α1(t), α2(t)). This hypernetwork-style modulation enables exact memorization of target embeddings (Theorem 4.2) and empirically yields consistent gains on reasoning tasks (Sudoku, Countdown, Edit Distance, LCS) and language modeling (WikiText-103) over both standard and unmodulated Looped Transformers.

## Results

On reasoning tasks, increasing the number of loops in Looped Transformers consistently improves performance over standard Transformers. For example, on Sudoku (L=6), Looped TF with r=32 loops achieves 87.9% accuracy versus 0.0% for the standard 6-layer Transformer, and adding timestep encoding further boosts this to 90.2%. The most dramatic gain appears on Edit Distance (n=60, L=12), where the standard Transformer scores 41.4%, Looped TF with r=100 reaches 47.7%, and TMLT with r=100 achieves 88.3%. On in-context learning (MSE↓), TMLT with r=12 loops achieves 1.7e-03, substantially outperforming both the standard 12-layer Transformer (8.6e-03) and the plain Looped TF (1.4e-02).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is the standard (non-looped) Transformer with matched parameter budgets (L=6 or L=12 layers). Looped Transformers with sufficient loops (r≥16 for L=6, r≥50 for L=12) substantially outperform the standard Transformer on all reasoning tasks: e.g., 87.9% vs. 0.0% on Sudoku, 98.2% vs. 39.8% on LCS(100), and 90.4% vs. 54.2% on ED(40). However, at low loop counts (r=4–8), Looped TF often underperforms the standard Transformer (e.g., 28.3% vs. 53.8% on Countdown at r=4), indicating that the looped architecture requires enough iterations to realize its advantage. Timestep-Modulated Looped Transformers (TMLT) consistently match or exceed plain Looped TF at the same loop count, with the largest relative gain on ED(60) where TMLT r=100 reaches 88.3% versus 47.7% for Looped TF and 41.4% for the standard Transformer. On language modeling (WikiText-103 perplexity), TMLT r=24 achieves 19.6 test perplexity, slightly better than the standard 12-layer Transformer (20.5) and the plain Looped TF (20.6). On in-context learning, TMLT r=12 achieves 1.7e-03 MSE, a 5× improvement over the standard Transformer (8.6e-03) and an 8× improvement over plain Looped TF (1.4e-02).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the approximation rate analysis of Transformers (Yun et al., 2020; Takakura & Suzuki, 2023; Jiang & Li, 2024) and weight-tied ReLU networks (Zhang et al., 2023) to the Looped Transformer architecture, where a single fixed Transformer layer is iterated. The proof strategy directly follows Zhang et al. (2023) for looped ReLU networks, but the authors must overcome the additional challenge of contextual mappings under weight-tying constraints, which prevents direct application of prior Transformer universality results. The paper departs from prior work by identifying that the looped architecture introduces two new types of modulus of continuity (contextual and token) that do not appear in standard Transformers or looped ReLU networks, revealing a fundamental expressivity gap inherent to weight-tying.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first theoretical characterization of the function approximation capabilities of Looped Transformers, filling a notable gap given the architecture's growing popularity for parameter-efficient reasoning. The identification of the inherent limitation (additional continuity dependencies) and the proposed Timestep-Modulated Looped Transformer (TMLT) provide both a diagnostic tool and a practical remedy. Open questions include extending the analysis to multi-layer looped architectures, variable-length inputs, and characterizing optimal memorization capacity; the authors also note that estimation performance and training stability remain unaddressed, which are critical for real-world deployment.
