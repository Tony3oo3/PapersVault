## Problem

Existing RL-based fine-tuning of diffusion models (e.g., DDPO, DPOK) relies on discrete-time MDP formulations that introduce discretization errors, overfit to specific inference timesteps, and become intractable for higher-order or blackbox solvers. This paper asks whether a principled continuous-time RL framework can eliminate these limitations by treating the score function itself as the control action in the backward SDE, rather than discretizing time first and then applying standard RL.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20253109/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pretrained diffusion model (e.g., Stable Diffusion v1.5) whose score function serves as the initial policy, a text prompt as the conditioning variable c, and a reward model (ImageReward) as the terminal reward. The output is a fine-tuned score function (U-Net with LoRA adapters) that, when plugged into the backward SDE, generates images better aligned with the prompt. The value network is parameterized using x-prediction via Tweedie's formula to predict denoised samples from noisy states, sharing parameters with the policy network and the reward model backbone (BLIP + MLP).

## Method

The core reformulation treats the score function s_θ(t,x,c) as a Gaussian control action a_t in the backward SDE, converting diffusion fine-tuning into a continuous-time stochastic control problem with state-independent diffusion coefficients. A key theoretical result (Theorem 3.1) shows the KL divergence between fine-tuned and pretrained terminal distributions decomposes into a tractable running reward proportional to ‖μ_θ − μ_θpre‖², enabling standard RL optimization without solving PDEs. The value network exploits diffusion model structure by using x-prediction (Tweedie's formula) as a 'reward mean predictor' combined with a residual corrector, reducing MSE from 2.63 to 0.29 compared to naive architectures. For policy optimization, the paper derives a continuous-time PPO with a finite-difference approximation of the advantage rate function, avoiding prior time discretization and yielding policies that are empirically robust to inference-time step choices (25, 50, or 100 steps).

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (32×32) with T=10 generation steps, the continuous-time RL method achieves consistently lower FID scores than DxMI throughout training and converges significantly faster (Figure 3). On Stable Diffusion v1.5 fine-tuning with ImageReward, the continuous-time RL policy achieves a more stable and higher average reward than the discrete-time DDPO baseline under the same 50-step discretization (Figure 9). A key robustness result shows that policies trained by continuous-time RL produce nearly identical reward curves at 25, 50, and 100 inference steps (curves overlap after 20 epochs), whereas DDPO exhibits an 'overfitting' phenomenon where reward improvement at 100 steps (<0.016) is far smaller than at the training discretization of 50 steps (0.046).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares four baselines across two experimental settings. On CIFAR-10, continuous-time RL outperforms DxMI (Yoon et al., 2024) on FID, converging faster and reaching lower FID scores throughout training. On Stable Diffusion v1.5 with ImageReward, continuous-time RL outperforms DDPO (Black et al., 2023) in both stability and efficiency of reward improvement under the same 50-step discretization. It also outperforms two non-RL discrete-time fine-tuning methods, DRaFT (Clark et al., 2023) and AlignProp (Prabhudesai et al., 2023), on the same ImageReward metric (Figure 10). A distinguishing advantage over all discrete-time baselines is robustness to inference-time discretization: the continuous-time policy's reward curves at 25, 50, and 100 steps nearly overlap, while DDPO shows significant degradation when evaluated at a different discretization than used during training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reframes the well-studied task of RLHF fine-tuning for diffusion models (previously handled via discrete-time MDPs in DDPO and DPOK) as a continuous-time stochastic control problem, where the score function serves as the control action. It builds directly on the SDE-based diffusion model formalism of Song et al. (2021b) and the continuous-time RL theory of Wang et al. (2020) and Jia & Zhou (2022b), while adapting the PPO clipping mechanism of Schulman et al. (2017) to the continuous-time setting. The key conceptual departure from prior discrete-time RL approaches is that the KL regularization is shown (via Girsanov's theorem) to decompose into a tractable running reward, and the policy gradient can be computed without any a priori time discretization. Concurrent work by Uehara et al. (2024) and Tang (2024) also formulates diffusion alignment as continuous-time control, but this paper's specific parameterization of score-as-action and its value network design leveraging x-prediction (Tweedie's formula) are distinct contributions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The continuous-time formulation offers a principled escape from the discretization-sensitivity problem observed in discrete-time RL fine-tuning (where models overfit to the training timestep), and the demonstrated robustness across 25/50/100 inference steps is practically valuable for deployment. The value network architecture that shares parameters with the policy via x-prediction is a reusable design pattern that could benefit other diffusion-model alignment tasks. Open questions include extending the theory to PDE-based policy evaluation (HJB equations, temporal difference rates), handling higher-order solvers like Heun's method, and validating the approach on newer architectures such as rectified-flow-based Stable Diffusion v3. The theoretical bounds (Theorem 4.4) rely on bounded reward and KL assumptions that may not hold for all reward models in practice.
