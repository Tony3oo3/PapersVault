## Problem

Existing neural network verification methods assume a predefined input domain and check whether outputs satisfy a specification within it, but they do not explicitly identify the structure of the verifiable input space itself. Precisely characterizing the region C where no adversarial examples exist is challenging because C is typically non-convex, discontinuous, and high-dimensional, and this task remains largely unexplored in the literature.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250284/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a trained ReLU network, an initial center x₀, input bounds [l, u], and a verification specification (e.g., f(x) ≤ y_max for power flow or f(x)_j − f(x)_l > 0 for classification), and produces verifiable balls defined by a center and an ℓ_p-norm radius (p ∈ {1, 2, ∞}). LEVIS-α returns a single large ball guaranteed to touch at least two boundaries of C, while LEVIS-β returns a union of many such balls that collectively cover the full verifiable space. The ℓ_∞ norm is preferred in practice because its cube geometry minimizes gaps between adjacent balls during coverage.

## Method

The core mechanism formulates two MIP problems: one that locates the nearest adversarial point to a given center (defining the maximum verifiable ball radius), and another that finds adversarial points along a user-specified direction φ(θ) = d·cos(θ) + q·sin(θ). For scalability, the paper replaces the full Big-M MIP with a complementarity-constraint (CC) NLP that eliminates most integer variables, then solves a reduced MIP only over the small bi-active neuron set I₀, achieving up to 7.8× speedup with an optimality gap below 0.004. LEVIS-α iteratively averages d pairs of collinear/orthogonal boundary adversarial points to converge to a large boundary-touching ball (Theorem 4.1 guarantees convergence for convex bounded C), while LEVIS-β uses a queue-based search that places new centers at surface points m = c − γ(c−b)/‖c−b‖·r outside the known union, resampling across discontinuities and parallelizing over angles θ and shifts Δ to cover the full verifiable space.

## Results

The proposed CC-based hybrid NLP-MIP solver achieves a 7.82× speedup over the MIP_CROWN baseline on MNIST (1.34s vs. 10.48s) and a 2.93× speedup on CIFAR-10 (5.96s vs. 17.47s), while maintaining an optimality gap below 0.004. In terms of verifiable ball quality, the exact solver produces radii up to 10× larger than the Lipschitz-based lower bound (e.g., 47.28 vs. 4.80 for p=1 on DC-OPF). LEVIS-α yields final verifiable ball radii 3–9× larger than both the Exact Fixed Center and Lipschitz Lower Bound baselines on the DC-OPF task.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the MIP_CROWN baseline (Wang et al., 2021), the proposed complementarity-constrained hybrid solver wins decisively on runtime: 7.82× faster on MNIST and 2.93× faster on CIFAR-10, with a negligible optimality gap (≤0.004), confirming that the reduced MIP formulation preserves global optimality in practice. Against the Lipschitz-based lower bound (Fazlyab et al., 2021), the exact solver produces substantially tighter (larger) verifiable radii across all norms: 16.24 vs. 4.52 (p=∞), 47.28 vs. 4.80 (p=1), and 28.12 vs. 12.88 (p=2) on DC-OPF, demonstrating that solving the optimization directly avoids the conservatism inherent in Lipschitz-based estimates. LEVIS-α further outperforms both the Exact Fixed Center method and the Lipschitz Lower Bound by 3–9× in final ball radius on DC-OPF, validating the iterative center-refinement strategy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the MIP-based ReLU encoding of Tjeng et al. (2019) and the complementarity-constraint relaxation of Yang et al. (2022), combining them with the two-step hybrid NLP-MIP strategy of Kazi et al. (2024) into a unified search framework. The key conceptual departure from prior verification work (Gowal et al., Wang et al., Kuvshinov & Gümmermann) is inverting the problem: rather than checking whether a *given* input domain satisfies an output specification, LEVIS identifies the *largest* input region that is guaranteed verifiable. Prior input-space analyses (Peck et al. 2017 for adversarial vulnerability, Kotha et al. 2024 for convex-hull over-approximation) either targeted vulnerability discovery or produced over-approximations; LEVIS provides exact, adversarial-free balls with a convergence guarantee for bounded convex regions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

For safety-critical deployments (power-grid control, autonomous systems), knowing *where* a network is reliable is as important as knowing *whether* it is reliable in a prescribed region; LEVIS gives practitioners a principled, exact map of the safe input space rather than a single yes/no verdict. The convergence theorem (Theorem 4.1) is restricted to convex bounded regions, and the authors acknowledge that non-convex or disconnected verifiable spaces can cause oscillation, leaving a gap for the most realistic high-dimensional settings. Open questions include scaling LEVIS-β to very high-dimensional inputs (beyond 784/3072), adapting the framework to neural-network-based controllers with stability guarantees, and whether the CC relaxation's optimality gap remains negligible for deeper or wider architectures.
