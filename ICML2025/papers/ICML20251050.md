## Problem

Explicit regularization (e.g., weight decay) and implicit bias from overparameterization are both well-studied in isolation, yet in practice they act in tandem and their interplay remains poorly understood. The paper asks whether explicit regularization can be used to deliberately control the shape, position, and range of the implicit bias, and what lasting effects it has on the geometry of training dynamics even after being removed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251050/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework takes a reparameterized model x = g(w) with w on a smooth manifold M, a loss f, and an explicit regularizer h(w) with a time-varying strength α_t. It produces a time-dependent Legendre function R_a (parameterized by a_t = −∫₀ᵗ α_s ds) that characterizes the evolving implicit bias, together with convergence and optimality guarantees. The output is a geometric characterization of how the Riemannian metric of training dynamics deforms over time, applicable to quadratic reparameterizations underlying matrix sensing, attention, and LoRA.

## Method

The core result (Theorem 3.6) shows that a commuting reparameterization g paired with an explicit regularizer h induces a time-dependent mirror flow governed by a parameterized Legendre function R_a, where the parameter a_t = −∫₀ᵗ α_s ds encodes the cumulative regularization. Geometrically, explicit regularization deforms the Riemannian metric of the training dynamics, and this deformation persists after regularization is switched off—a lasting effect that creates a novel link between explicit and implicit regularization. The paper identifies three distinct effects: type of bias (shape change, e.g., L₂ → L₁), positional bias (global minimum shifts toward zero), and range shrinking (attainable values of the Legendre function contract). Convergence is established via a contracting Bregman function property (Theorem 3.10), and optimality for underdetermined linear regression is proven (Theorem 3.11). The practical implication is that turning off weight decay mid-training exploits the accumulated geometric deformation to reach sparser solutions, demonstrated on matrix sensing, LoRA fine-tuning of GPT-2, and ViT on ImageNet.

## Results

On ImageNet with a pretrained ViT, turning off weight decay (wd=0.2) at epoch 150 improves validation accuracy from 71.08% to 73.66% (+2.58 pp), and on CIFAR-10 from 52.38% to 56.70% (+4.32 pp). In the matrix sensing experiment, turning off weight decay at T=2500 recovers the sparse ground truth (reconstruction error 1.7×10⁻⁴, nuclear norm 1.00), while constant weight decay (α=0.01) fails to recover it (reconstruction error 3.9×10⁻², nuclear norm 0.93). For LoRA fine-tuning of GPT-2 on Tiny Shakespeare, turning off weight decay at iteration 200 reduces validation loss from 2.96 to 2.95. The nuclear-to-Frobenius norm ratio decreases during weight decay (indicating a shift toward L₁/nuclear-norm bias) and then stabilizes after turn-off, creating a 'window of opportunity' for improved generalization at higher sparsity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against external methods but instead contrasts regularization strategies within the same architecture. Constant weight decay (no turn-off) consistently underperforms the proposed dynamic strategy: on ImageNet, wd=0.2 without turn-off yields 71.08% vs. 73.66% with turn-off, and on CIFAR-10, 52.38% vs. 56.70%. In matrix sensing, a linear reparameterization with L₁ regularization achieves high sparsity but cannot recover the sparse ground truth (reconstruction error remains high), whereas the quadratic reparameterization with turn-off weight decay recovers it exactly (reconstruction error 1.7×10⁻⁴). Among dynamic schedules (linear decay, cosine decay, constant-then-turn-off), all achieve the same nuclear norm of 1.00 and near-zero reconstruction error, confirming the theoretical prediction that the accumulated regularization effect is schedule-invariant. The key finding is that the benefit of turn-off is most pronounced at higher weight decay strengths (wd=0.2), where the constant schedule is most constrained, while at low strengths (wd=0.01–0.02) the gap narrows.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the mirror flow framework of Li et al. (2022), which characterizes implicit bias as a time-independent Legendre function determined by reparameterization, by incorporating explicit regularization to produce a time-dependent Legendre function. It builds directly on Jacobs & Burkholz (2025), who first recognized that dynamic regularization can induce time-dependent mirror flows for sparsification, and on the Bregman/Riemannian geometry foundations of Alvarez et al. (2004). The key departure from prior work is the systematic characterization of three distinct geometric effects (positional bias, type of bias, range shrinking) that explicit regularization imposes on the training dynamics, unifying previously separate analyses of implicit bias (Woodworth et al., 2020; Gunasekar et al., 2017) and explicit regularization (Dai et al., 2021; Khodak et al., 2021) within a single framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical takeaway—that turning off weight decay mid-training exploits the accumulated geometric effect to improve generalization—is simple, broadly applicable, and demonstrated across matrix sensing, LoRA fine-tuning of GPT-2, and ViT on ImageNet. However, the theoretical framework has clear limitations: it does not apply to linear (non-overparameterized) parametrizations or to reparameterizations of depth greater than 2 with weight decay, restricting its direct applicability to deep networks. Open questions include whether the framework can be extended to deeper architectures, how it interacts with SGD noise and large learning rates (which also induce sparsity), and whether the turn-off strategy generalizes to other dynamic regularization schedules beyond the constant-then-zero pattern studied here.
