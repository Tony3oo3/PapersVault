## Problem

Existing approaches that enable LLMs to 'think more' (e.g., Chain-of-Thought prompting) generate discrete token sequences just-in-time during decoding, making them difficult to optimize end-to-end and introducing significant latency. This paper asks whether a frozen LLM can be augmented with an offline, differentiable coprocessor that distills additional computation into the model's KV-cache, enabling latent-space deliberation without modifying the base model or requiring sequential token generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251570/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The coprocessor receives the full KV-cache (keys and values across all layers) produced by the frozen LLM when processing an input prompt, along with a set of trainable soft-token embeddings that serve as abstract prompts. It outputs a configurable number of latent embeddings (N_L, ranging from 4 to 64 in experiments) in a single forward pass, which are appended to the original KV-cache at the augmentation position. The augmented cache is then consumed by the unchanged frozen LLM for standard autoregressive text generation, meaning the output is ordinary text tokens but conditioned on the enriched cache.

## Method

A coprocessor with the same transformer architecture as the frozen LLM (initialized from its pretrained weights) processes the KV-cache and trainable soft-token embeddings to produce latent embeddings in one parallel forward pass. Training uses standard language-modeling loss on pretraining data with a multi-position augmentation strategy: at randomly selected positions within a sequence, the coprocessor generates N_L latent embeddings and is trained to predict N_A future tokens (16 in the main experiments) in a teacher-forced manner, effectively learning latent-space interpolation between known context and future text. A carefully constructed attention mask ensures each group of latent embeddings and its ahead tokens only attends to original tokens preceding its insertion point, enabling all augmentation positions to be trained in a single forward pass. Because the base LLM remains frozen throughout, the coprocessor can operate offline and asynchronously, and the model functions normally if the coprocessor is unavailable. The approach is fully differentiable, avoiding the need for reinforcement learning or discrete token-level reasoning traces.

## Results

Using a frozen Gemma-2 2B model with a trained coprocessor (no task-specific fine-tuning), cache augmentation with 64 latent embeddings yields a +10.05% absolute improvement on GSM8K (21.38% → 31.43%) and +4.70% on MMLU (52.00% → 56.70%). Perplexity on the validation set drops by up to 3.94% at position 1 and 1.20% at position 32 after augmentation, with gains scaling monotonically with the number of latent embeddings (8→64). Across 17 public benchmarks, the method improves or matches the baseline on 15 of 17, with the largest gains on reasoning-intensive tasks (GSM8K, MMLU, ARC-c, MATH, DROP).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Pause Token baseline (Goyal et al., 2023), which uses fixed, input-independent trainable embeddings, the proposed cache augmentation method wins decisively on both metrics: validation perplexity of 10.60 vs. 11.63 (Pause Token) and GSM8K 8-shot accuracy of 26.76% vs. 22.37%, with both methods using 32 embeddings and identical training data. Against zero-shot Chain-of-Thought prompting (Kojima et al., 2022, 'Let's think step by step'), cache augmentation with 16 latents achieves 24.72% on GSM8K vs. 23.20% for CoT, and with 32 latents reaches 26.76%, a 3.56-point margin over CoT. The key architectural distinction is that Pause Token embeddings are static and do not condition on the input, whereas the coprocessor generates context-dependent latent embeddings from the kv-cache in a single forward pass. Relative to the frozen Gemma-2 2B baseline itself, the method improves on 15 of 17 benchmarks with 64 latents, with the two exceptions (SIQA at −0.05 with 16 latents, recovering to +0.20 with 64) showing only marginal dips. The paper also notes that zero-shot CoT was closer to baseline on MATH and HumanEval, suggesting the latent-space approach is most advantageous on tasks where sequential token-by-token reasoning is costly.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of chain-of-thought reasoning, KV-cache manipulation, and latent-space computation. It departs from CoT methods (Wei et al., 2022; Kojima et al., 2022) by replacing discrete token generation with continuous latent embeddings, and from Pause Token (Goyal et al., 2023) by replacing fixed, input-agnostic embeddings with context-dependent ones generated by a dedicated coprocessor. The KV-cache compression literature (Mu et al., 2024; Ge et al., 2024) provides the architectural inspiration, but the motivation is inverted: rather than compressing the cache, the coprocessor augments it with additional representational capacity. The frozen-LLM constraint distinguishes this from COCONUT (Hao et al., 2024), which requires fine-tuning the base model and generates latents sequentially.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The frozen-LLM-plus-coprocessor paradigm enables asynchronous, offline deliberation that does not block decoding, opening a path toward models that 'bank' computation independently of user queries. However, the coprocessor is currently the same size as the base LLM, effectively doubling inference compute, and the latent reasoning trace is opaque, raising interpretability concerns. Key open questions include whether the approach scales to larger models, whether a much smaller coprocessor can suffice (requiring cross-size KV-cache adaptation), and whether sequential latent generation within a single deliberation step yields further gains. The method's reliance on general pretraining data without task-specific fine-tuning is promising but untested at scale.
