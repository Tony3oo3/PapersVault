## Problem

Existing activation sparsification methods for non-ReLU LLMs face a fundamental trade-off: ReLU-induction approaches require costly recovery training, while magnitude-based pruning (CATS, TEAL) relies on offline-calibrated thresholds that produce inconsistent actual sparsity and unstable inference speed-ups. The paper asks whether activation sparsity can be exploited quickly, effectively, and consistently for LLM inference without additional training or empirical magnitude thresholds.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251603/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

LaRoSA takes a pre-trained LLM and a small calibration set (16 sequences of 2048 tokens) to compute per-layer PCA rotation matrices Q_l offline (~12 min on 8×A100 for a 70B model), then at inference time consumes a text token sequence and a target model-level sparsity level p. The rotation matrices are absorbed into the corresponding weight matrices so no extra online computation is introduced; a residual adapter Q_l^T Q_{l+1} is inserted in the residual stream to maintain layer-specific rotations. The output is generated text with guaranteed consistent sparsity per activation (via Top-K) and a corresponding wall-clock speed-up from a fused Triton GEMV kernel.

## Method

LaRoSA applies a layerwise orthogonal rotation Q_l (derived from PCA on each layer's input activation covariance) to transform activations into a principal-component space where low-magnitude entries are more reliably prunable, then uses a Top-K function on the rotated activations to enforce exact per-activation sparsity rather than a fixed magnitude threshold. A residual adapter Q_l^T Q_{l+1} is inserted between layers to decouple the rotation of each layer, and Q_l is absorbed into the downstream weight matrices (W_q, W_k, W_v, W_up, W_gate, W_down) so the rotation adds zero online FLOPs. A grid-search procedure tunes per-activation sparsity coefficients α_1–α_4 (constrained by block dimension ratios) to optimally allocate sparsity across h_1, h_2, h_3, h_4 while preserving the overall model-level sparsity. A custom Triton GEMV kernel fuses the Top-K selection with selective column loading of weight matrices stored in column-major format, translating the activation sparsity into consistent end-to-end token-generation speed-up (up to 1.9× at 75% sparsity on A100).

## Results

On LLaMA2-7B at 40% model-level sparsity, LaRoSA achieves 66.15% zero-shot accuracy (only 0.54% below the dense model's 66.69%) and 5.64 perplexity (0.17 above dense), while delivering a consistent 1.30× wall-clock speed-up. At 25% sparsity on LLaMA3-70B, LaRoSA incurs merely 0.09 PPL loss (2.94 vs. 2.85 dense). At 75% sparsity, LaRoSA achieves up to 1.90× token generation speed-up on LLaMA3-70B (A100) and 1.92× on Qwen2.5-72B (H20), with only moderate accuracy degradation (e.g., 73.81% zero-shot on LLaMA3-70B vs. 76.29% dense).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LaRoSA consistently outperforms both TEAL and CATS across all models and sparsity levels. Against TEAL, LaRoSA gains 1.23% zero-shot accuracy at 40% sparsity on LLaMA2-7B (66.15% vs. 64.92%) and 2.27% on LLaMA3-8B (67.19% vs. 64.92% at 50%); the gap widens at higher sparsity, reaching 3.01% on LLaMA3-70B at 50% (73.81% vs. 70.80%). Against CATS, LaRoSA's advantage is dramatic at ≥40% sparsity: on LLaMA2-7B at 40%, LaRoSA scores 66.15% while CATS collapses to 49.55% (a 16.60% gap), and CATS's perplexity explodes to 45.46 vs. LaRoSA's 5.64. A key structural advantage is sparsity consistency: LaRoSA's Top-K mechanism guarantees exactly the target sparsity (e.g., 50.0% ± 0.0%), whereas TEAL's calibrated thresholds yield only 48.8% actual sparsity with ±4.7% per-activation variance, leading to unstable inference speed-ups. On perplexity, LaRoSA at 50% sparsity on LLaMA2-7B (5.87) even outperforms TEAL at 25% (6.31), demonstrating superior sparsity efficiency.

## Relation to Prior Work

LaRoSA sits at the intersection of two research threads: rotation-based model compression (SliceGPT, QuaRot, SpinQuant) and training-free activation sparsification (CATS, TEAL). It repurposes the computational invariance theorem from SliceGPT to justify layerwise orthogonal rotations, but applies PCA-derived rotations specifically to make activations more amenable to Top-K sparsification rather than to improve quantizability. The method departs from TEAL and CATS by replacing their empirical magnitude-threshold pruning with a deterministic Top-K selection in the rotated space, and from ReLU-based sparsity methods (DejaVu, Mirzadeh et al.) by requiring no retraining. The Triton GEMV kernel builds directly on the infrastructure from DejaVu and TEAL.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LaRoSA offers a practical, training-free path to consistent inference speedup (1.3–1.9×) across diverse LLM architectures without the calibration fragility or sparsity instability that plagues magnitude-threshold methods. Its demonstrated compatibility with 4-bit weight quantization suggests a composable compression stack, though the paper does not explore joint sparsity-quantization kernels. Open questions include whether the PCA calibration step (requiring a forward pass over 16 sequences) can be further reduced, whether the approach extends to MoE or multimodal architectures, and whether the modest additional TFLOPs from residual adapters become a bottleneck at very high sparsity or on memory-constrained hardware.
