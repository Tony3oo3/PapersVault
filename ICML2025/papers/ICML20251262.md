## Problem

Simultaneous speech-to-speech translation requires producing translated speech in real-time, chunk by chunk, without waiting for the full source utterance. Prior systems (Seamless, StreamSpeech) rely on complex, non-batchable inference policies to decide when to emit translations, and no large-scale dataset of causally aligned speech pairs with voice preservation exists to train such models. The fundamental challenge is determining, per word, how much source context is needed before a correct translation can be produced.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a monophonic 24 kHz source speech waveform, encoded into discrete tokens at 12.5 Hz by the causal Mimi codec (up to 16 RVQ codebooks of cardinality 2048). It produces two outputs simultaneously: a translated speech waveform (decoded from target audio tokens) and a translated text stream (the 'Inner Monologue'), both causally aligned so that each target word is emitted only after sufficient source context has been observed. The text stream provides word-level timestamps in the target language, and the audio output preserves the source speaker's voice identity.

## Method

Hibiki is a decoder-only multistream RQ-Transformer (2.2B Temporal + 449M Depth parameters) that jointly models source audio tokens, target audio tokens, and a text token stream over both the time axis (Temporal Transformer) and the quantizer axis (Depth Transformer), with a 2-step acoustic delay on non-semantic codebooks. The key training innovation is 'contextual alignment': the per-word delay for each target word is estimated by finding the source word index that maximizes the log-likelihood increment of that target word under MADLAD-3B, then silences are inserted (or alignment-aware TTS is used) to enforce this causality in synthetic parallel data. Voice transfer is handled via conditional training—each example is labeled with a discrete speaker-similarity score, and at inference time classifier-free guidance (γ=3.0) between 'very_good' and 'very_bad' conditions amplifies voice fidelity without sacrificing translation quality. Inference requires only vanilla temperature sampling at a constant frame rate, enabling batched streaming and on-device deployment without any external decision policy.

## Results

On the short-form CVSS-CFR-EN test set, Hibiki achieves 39.2 BLEU and 35.5 ASR-BLEU, outperforming Seamless (37.0 / 33.8) and StreamSpeech (26.4 / 25.4), while also achieving the highest speaker similarity (0.41 vs. 0.30 for Seamless). On the long-form Audio-NTREX dataset, Hibiki reaches 27.5 BLEU and 34.9 ASR-COMET versus Seamless's 25.4 BLEU and 27.3 ASR-COMET; StreamSpeech fails entirely (0.1 BLEU). In human evaluation, Hibiki scores 3.78/5 on quality and 3.73/5 on naturalness, approaching ground-truth human interpretation (4.18/4.12), while Seamless scores only 2.22 and 2.18 respectively. Hibiki also outperforms all offline baselines in Table 1, reaching 30.5 ASR-BLEU versus 28.5 for the offline StreamSpeech variant.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Seamless, the primary simultaneous S2ST baseline, Hibiki wins on all translation quality metrics (BLEU, ASR-BLEU, ASR-COMET) on both short-form and long-form sets, and on speaker similarity (0.41 vs. 0.30 short-form; 0.48 vs. 0.43 long-form), at the cost of roughly 0.7–1.1 s additional LAAL latency. Against StreamSpeech, Hibiki dominates decisively: on short-form, 39.2 vs. 26.4 BLEU, and on long-form StreamSpeech produces unintelligible output (0.1 BLEU). Against offline baselines in Table 1, Hibiki's 30.5 ASR-BLEU exceeds the best offline model (StreamSpeech offline at 28.5) and Unity (27.8), despite operating in the harder simultaneous setting. In human evaluation (Table 3), Hibiki's quality (3.78) and naturalness (3.73) MOS scores are substantially higher than Seamless (2.22, 2.18) and approach ground-truth human interpretation (4.18, 4.12), making it the first model to deliver an interpretation experience close to professional human interpreters. The distilled Hibiki-M (1.8B) remains competitive with Seamless on short-form (37.5 vs. 37.0 BLEU) and slightly trails on long-form speaker similarity (0.39 vs. 0.43), while enabling real-time on-device inference on an iPhone 16 Pro.

## Relation to Prior Work

Hibiki repurposes the multistream RQ-Transformer architecture from Moshi (Defossez et al., 2024), originally designed for full-duplex spoken dialogue, into a simultaneous speech translation system. It departs from prior simultaneous S2ST systems (Seamless, StreamSpeech) that rely on complex, non-batchable inference policies (decision thresholds, chunk-based control flow) by instead encoding the 'when to translate' decision into the training data via a novel contextual alignment method based on MT perplexity, enabling inference with simple temperature sampling. The voice transfer mechanism also departs from the standard practice of filtering training data by speaker similarity, instead using conditional training with classifier-free guidance to steer voice fidelity at inference time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Hibiki is the first simultaneous S2ST system to approach professional human interpreter quality in human evaluations (3.78 vs. 4.18 MOS), and its simple inference paradigm enables batched GPU processing and real-time on-device deployment—capabilities that prior simultaneous S2ST systems lacked due to their complex control flow. Key limitations include the single Fr-En language pair, the quality/latency trade-off being fixed at training time rather than controllable at inference, and translation quality being bounded by the MADLAD-3B pseudo-targets. Open questions include whether the contextual alignment approach generalizes to more language pairs and spontaneous speech, and whether the quality/latency trade-off can be made inference-time steerable without retraining.
