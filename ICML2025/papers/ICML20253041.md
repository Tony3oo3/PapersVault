## Problem

Prior feature learning theory for neural networks relies heavily on isotropic covariates x~N(0,I), under which SGD provably learns useful features. When this assumption is violated, SGD becomes fundamentally suboptimal—its convergence rate depends on the condition number of the task matrix, and in single-index models it learns a biased direction. The paper asks: what practical family of optimizers provably overcomes these deficiencies, and why do Kronecker-Factored preconditioners (KFAC, Shampoo) succeed where SGD and Adam fail?

## Input / Output

The method operates on two-layer feedforward networks f_{F,G}(x) = Fσ(Gx) with weight matrices F ∈ R^{d_y×d_h} and G ∈ R^{d_h×d_x}, trained on labeled pairs (x_i, y_i) drawn from potentially anisotropic Gaussian distributions. The preconditioners are computed directly from the data batch: Q_G = empirical covariance of x, Q_F = empirical covariance of the post-activation features z = σ(Gx), and P_G = F^T F (or identity). The output is the updated weight matrices after alternating layer-wise preconditioned descent steps, with the learned representation G's row-space serving as the feature subspace for downstream transfer.

## Method

The paper derives a stylized KFAC update from first principles by analyzing exactly where SGD fails in two prototypical feature learning problems. For linear representation learning, the G-update becomes G_+ = G − η_G (F^T F)^{-1} ∇_G L · Σ̂_x^{-1}, which pre- and post-multiplies the gradient to eliminate both the condition-number dependence on F* and the anisotropy-induced bias, yielding the first condition-number-free local convergence rate. For single-index learning, the same right-preconditioner (Q_G + λ_G I)^{-1} transforms the one-step SGD direction β_SGD = X^T y/n into the ridge regression estimator β_KFAC = (Q_G + λ_G I)^{-1} X^T y/n, which provably recovers the true target direction β* even under strong anisotropy. The Kronecker-factored structure—pre-multiplying by P^{-1} and post-multiplying by Q^{-1} in matrix form—emerges as the minimal correction needed, and the paper shows that standard alternatives (Adam's diagonal preconditioning, batch normalization) only mildly mitigate or even destroy the shared representation structure.

## Results

In linear representation learning, the paper proves that SGD's convergence rate is fundamentally bottlenecked by the condition number of F_* (Proposition 3.5), while the proposed stylized KFAC achieves a condition-number-free contraction rate of (1 − 0.9η_G) per step (Theorem 3.6). In single-index learning, one-step SGD learns a biased direction β_SGD = X^T y/n whose correlation with the true direction β_* equals d_x^{-1}Tr(Σ_x)/√(d_x^{-1}Tr(Σ_x²)) — equal to 1 only when Σ_x is isotropic — whereas one-step KFAC recovers the ridge regression estimator and achieves correlation → 1 as λ_G → 0 and n ≫ d_x (Lemma 3.12). Numerically, KFAC outperforms SGD, Adam, NGD, AMGD, and DFW in both subspace distance and transfer loss (Figure 1), and maintains high direction correlation under increasing anisotropy while SGD degrades (Figure 2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

KFAC clearly outperforms all five baselines on the key generalization metrics. Against SGD and AMGD (Collins et al., 2021), KFAC achieves substantially lower subspace distance and transfer loss in Figure 1, while SGD/AMGD converge in training loss but negligibly in subspace distance. Against DFW (Zhang et al., 2024b), which applies only the right-preconditioner Q_G = Σ̂_x, KFAC further improves by also applying the left-preconditioner P_G = F^T F, eliminating the condition-number dependence that DFW retains. Against Adam (Kingma & Ba, 2015), the paper shows that entry-wise diagonal preconditioning only mildly mitigates the anisotropy issues and does not recover the correct representation. Against NGD (Zhang et al., 2019), full second-order methods actually underperform KFAC in both convergence rate and stability, confirming the finding of Benzing (2022). Finally, batch-norm (Ioffe & Szegedy, 2015) is shown to be counterproductive: while it enables AMGD's training loss to converge by whitening covariates, it causes the learned representation to converge to rowsp(G_* Σ_x^{1/2}) rather than rowsp(G_*), destroying the shared structure needed for transfer (Figure 3).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate research threads: Kronecker-Factored preconditioning (KFAC, Shampoo), which has been motivated almost exclusively from an approximation-theoretic perspective (approximating the Fisher Information or Gauss-Newton matrix), and feature learning theory, which has studied how SGD learns useful representations in simplified two-layer models under the near-universal assumption of isotropic covariates x~N(0,I). The paper departs from both: it shows that relaxing isotropy makes SGD provably suboptimal for feature learning, and that the natural first-principles fix is precisely the Kronecker-Factored preconditioner, providing a non-approximation-theoretic justification for KFAC. It builds directly on Collins et al. (2021) and Zhang et al. (2024b) for the linear representation learning setting, subsuming their algorithms as special cases while removing the condition-number dependence in the convergence rate.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first condition-number-free local convergence guarantee for linear representation learning and the first provable necessity result for layer-wise preconditioning in feature learning, elevating KFAC/Shampoo from empirically successful heuristics to provably optimal solutions in these settings. The finding that data whitening (batch-norm) can destroy the shared representation structure needed for transfer learning is a practical warning for practitioners. Key limitations include the restriction to two-layer networks, linear or single-index targets, and the alternating (non-simultaneous) update scheme; extending these results to deeper architectures, non-linear multi-task settings, and practical Shampoo variants with damping and momentum remains open.
