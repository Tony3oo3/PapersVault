## Problem

Multi-view diffusion models (e.g., SyncDreamer, MVD-Fusion, VideoMV) generate multiple views of an object in a single inference but remain plagued by the Janus Problem, content drifting, and geometric artifacts due to incomplete 3D training data and the lack of explicit geometric constraints in their cross-view attention mechanisms. Existing remedies either require costly fine-tuning or rely on implicit learned correspondences that are prone to local optima, leaving a gap for an efficient, training-free rectification approach that enforces 3D consistency at inference time.

## Input / Output

MIRROR is a plug-and-play post-processing module that accepts the output of any pre-trained multi-view diffusion model (16–24 views at fixed elevation, generated from either a single RGB image or a text prompt) and produces a rectified set of multi-view consistent images. Internally it consumes monocular depth maps (from Depth-Anything-V2) estimated on the Stage-1 clean images to drive point correspondence, and operates entirely in the VQGAN latent space during a second DDIM denoising pass, requiring no additional training data or model parameters.

## Method

MIRROR employs a two-stage inference strategy: Stage 1 generates a multi-view image set with the base model and extracts depth maps; Stage 2 applies DDIM Inversion to re-noise the clean latents and then performs guided denoising where, at each step, a Trajectory Tracking Module (TTM) computes pixel-wise correspondences of 3D physical points across adjacent views via a depth-guided azimuthal rotation operator, and a Feature Rectification Module (FRM) fuses 3×3 block features from neighboring views using a dual-anchor (positive/negative) cosine-similarity weighting to produce a per-pixel gradient map that corrects the noise prediction. A key theoretical contribution shows that omitting the UNet Jacobian in the gradient computation introduces an error bounded by a constant that vanishes as t→0, yielding a ~2× speedup without quality loss. The entire pipeline is training-free and model-agnostic, requiring only the base model's forward pass and a monocular depth estimator.

## Results

MIRROR consistently improves all four metrics (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, CLIP Score) across every baseline model tested. The most dramatic gains appear on VideoMV text-based generation, where PSNR rises from 21.67 to 24.82 (+3.15 dB), SSIM from 0.814 to 0.898, LPIPS drops from 0.206 to 0.115, and CLIP Score jumps from 26.96 to 32.78. On image-based tasks (GSO dataset), MIRROR adds roughly 0.6–1.25 dB PSNR over each baseline (e.g., SyncDreamer 18.75→19.37, MVD-Fusion 18.90→20.15, VideoMV 17.64→18.26). Inference time increases only 2.6–2.9× over the baseline (vs. 4.5–5.7× if the UNet Jacobian were retained), keeping the full two-stage rectification within a few minutes on a single L40S GPU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MIRROR outperforms every baseline it is applied to across all four metrics, with no case of degradation. Against SyncDreamer (Liu et al., 2023), MIRROR improves PSNR by +0.62 dB, SSIM by +0.055, LPIPS by −0.026, and CLIP Score by +1.68 points. Against MVD-Fusion (Hu et al., CVPR 2024), the gains are slightly larger: +1.25 dB PSNR, +0.016 SSIM, −0.004 LPIPS, and +2.86 CLIP Score. The largest absolute improvement is on VideoMV text-based generation, where MIRROR nearly halves LPIPS (0.206→0.115) and boosts CLIP Score by nearly 6 points, indicating that the rectification is most impactful when the baseline suffers from severe Janus artifacts and content drift. MIRROR also improves 3D reconstruction quality (Chamfer Distance and Volume IoU via NeuS), e.g., reducing CD from 0.042 to 0.039 for SyncDreamer and from 0.046 to 0.028 for VideoMV text-based, confirming that the multi-view consistency gains translate to better downstream 3D geometry.

## Relation to Prior Work

MIRROR positions itself as a training-free post-hoc rectification layer atop existing multi-view diffusion models (MVDream, SyncDreamer, MVD-Fusion, VideoMV), departing from prior consistency-improvement strategies that modify network architectures (cross-view attention, epipolar geometry in attention) or require fine-tuning. Conceptually, it repurposes the gradient-guidance paradigm from classifier-free diffusion (Dhariwal & Nichol, 2021) but applies it to an explicit geometric consistency loss computed via depth-guided point-to-point trajectory tracking, rather than to a learned classifier or text condition. This contrasts with epipolar-correspondence methods (Ye et al., Kant et al., Zhou & Tulsiani) that establish line-based correspondences, and with CoSER (Li et al., 2024a) which injects spatial information into attention but lacks depth, by using a more precise and computationally cheaper point-to-point tracking strategy with a dual-anchor feature fusion mechanism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MIRROR offers a practical, drop-in solution to the persistent Janus Problem and content drifting that affect all current multi-view diffusion models, without requiring retraining or architectural changes—making it immediately applicable to any new multi-view generator as they emerge. The theoretical guarantee that omitting the UNet Jacobian introduces negligible error (converging to zero as t→0) is practically significant, halving inference overhead and making the method deployable in real-time pipelines. Key limitations include the fixed-elevation constraint (limiting free-viewpoint applications), object-level scope (no scene-level generation), and dependence on monocular depth estimation quality. Open questions include extending trajectory tracking to arbitrary camera trajectories, handling dynamic/deformable objects, and whether the approach generalizes to scene-level 3D generation with inter-object relationships.
