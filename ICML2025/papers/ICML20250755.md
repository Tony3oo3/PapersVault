## Problem

Prior online multicalibration algorithms obtain ℓ₁ guarantees indirectly by first proving rates in weaker norms (ℓ∞ or ℓ₂) and then transferring, incurring additional loss and yielding rates significantly weaker than the O(T^{-1/3}) bound for standard online calibration. Additionally, most existing algorithms have runtime linear in the hypothesis class size |H|, making them intractable for large or infinite group families. The paper asks whether a direct ℓ₁ approach can match the near-optimal T^{-1/3} rate and whether oracle-efficient variants are possible.

## Input / Output

The learner receives a context vector x_t ∈ X each round and must output a probability prediction p_t drawn from a discretized grid P = {0, 1/m, …, 1} (with m set to T^{1/3} for the improved rate). The hypothesis class H consists of B-bounded real-valued functions h: X → R defining the groups over which calibration is required. For the oracle-efficient variant, the algorithm additionally requires a single call per round to an offline oracle that approximately solves a max over h ∈ H and θ ∈ {±1}^M of a weighted sum of product rewards, and assumes H is binary-valued with either transductive or small-separator structure on the context sequence.

## Method

The key technical contribution is a novel reduction of online ℓ₁-multicalibration to Online Linear-Product Optimization (OLPO), an online learning problem whose rewards involve products of the chosen hypothesis value and a reward vector. To achieve the Ō(T^{-1/3}) rate, the paper linearizes OLPO into Lin-OLPO—a standard online linear optimization problem over a mixed-norm unit ball in R^{|H|×M}—and solves it by running |H| parallel online gradient descent instances (one per hypothesis) aggregated via multiplicative weights update, yielding regret √(T log|H|) + √(TM). For oracle efficiency, the authors instead apply the Generalized Follow-the-Perturbed-Leader (GFTPL) framework directly to OLPO, restricting decisions to the Boolean hypercube {±1}^M so that the perturbation matrix Γ is both δ-admissible and implementable via a single offline oracle call per round, achieving Ō(T^{-1/4}) regret without enumerating H. A halfspace oracle (extending Abernethy et al., 2011) converts the OLPO/Lin-OLPO solution into a valid prediction distribution, and a 1-Lipschitz property of the multicalibration error in the L∞ metric extends finite-class bounds to infinite classes via β-covering arguments.

## Results

For a finite hypothesis class H, the paper achieves an online ℓ₁-multicalibration error of Õ(T^{-1/3}) (Theorem 1.2), nearly matching the best known rate for online calibration. For infinite H, the error is bounded by Õ(T^{-1/3}) + β in terms of the β-covering number (Theorem 1.4), yielding O(Bd^{1/2} T^{-1/3} log(BT)) for bounded linear functions in d dimensions (Corollary 1.5). The oracle-efficient variant requires only a single offline oracle call per round and achieves Õ(T^{-1/4}) (Theorem 1.7), under transductive or small-separator assumptions with binary-valued H.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper improves upon Gupta et al. (2022), who obtained Õ(T^{-1/4}) for online ℓ₁-multicalibration by first solving the weaker ℓ∞-multicalibration problem and transferring via norm inequalities; this paper's direct ℓ₁ reduction to OLPO yields the tighter Õ(T^{-1/3}) rate for finite H. For the oracle-efficient setting, Garg et al. (2024) achieved Õ(T^{-1/8}) using an online regression oracle, while this paper improves the rate to Õ(T^{-1/4}) with a simpler offline optimization oracle (one call per round). The authors note that Noarov et al. (2025) can also derive a similar Õ(T^{-1/3}) bound for finite H, but through a fundamentally different framework requiring a small-loss regret bound, whereas this paper's approach only needs a worst-case regret bound and avoids min-max optimization. The Õ(T^{-1/3}) rate for finite H nearly matches the O(T^{-1/3}) bound for plain online calibration (Foster & Vohra, 1998; Dagan et al., 2025), closing the gap that previously existed between calibration and multicalibration. The oracle-efficient rate of Õ(T^{-1/4}) is a 4× improvement in the exponent over Garg et al.'s Õ(T^{-1/8}), though it still lags the non-oracle Õ(T^{-1/3}) due to the linear dependence on the discretization parameter m in the GFTPL regret bound.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from prior online multicalibration work (Gupta et al. 2022; Garg et al. 2024) that obtained ℓ₁ guarantees indirectly by first proving ℓ∞ or ℓ₂ rates and then transferring, incurring additional loss. The key conceptual departure is a novel reduction of online ℓ₁-multicalibration to an intermediate problem—online linear-product optimization (OLPO)—which allows a direct ℓ₁ analysis. For oracle efficiency, the authors adapt the generalized Follow-the-Perturbed-Leader framework (Dudik et al. 2020) directly to OLPO rather than to the intractable linearized Lin-OLPO space, and replace Garg et al.'s online regression oracle with a simpler offline optimization oracle.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Ō(T^{-1/3}) rate nearly matches the best known bound for online calibration itself, closing a significant gap that prior indirect approaches left open, and has direct implications for online omniprediction (where ℓ₁-multicalibration is the core subroutine). The oracle-efficient Ō(T^{-1/4}) result is a meaningful improvement over the prior Ō(T^{-1/8}) bound and uses a strictly weaker oracle assumption. Open questions include whether the oracle-efficient rate can be closed to T^{-1/3} (the gap stems from a linear-in-m dependence in the GFTPL regret analysis), whether the binary-valued and transductive/small-separator assumptions can be relaxed, and whether the OLPO reduction extends to other calibration variants (e.g., moment multicalibration).
