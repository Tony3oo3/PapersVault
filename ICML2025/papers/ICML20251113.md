## Problem

Generic neural PDE surrogates, including PINNs, enforce conservation laws only as soft penalties in the loss, which can lead to suboptimal mass and momentum conservation and stiff optimization in nonlinear regimes. No prior work has exploited divergence-free symmetric tensors (DFSTs) as an architectural inductive bias that guarantees both mass and momentum conservation simultaneously at the network level.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251113/cropped_page5_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The network takes spatial-temporal coordinates (t, x₁, …, xₙ) as input and produces an (n+1)×(n+1) divergence-free symmetric tensor field S_θ(x). Internally, an MLP outputs m(m+1)/2 scalar coefficients whose Hessians are computed via Taylor-mode automatic differentiation; the tensor is assembled as a fixed linear combination of Riemann-like basis tensors weighted by these Hessians. Physical fields (density, momentum, pressure, stress) are then extracted from the block structure of S_θ, and for MHD a separate scalar-potential network yields the magnetic field via curl.

## Method

The key insight is that every divergence-free symmetric tensor on a flat manifold can be written as the double divergence of a Riemann-like (0,4)-tensor (Theorem 2.1), so an MLP that outputs the scalar coefficients c_ij(x) of such a tensor, combined with automatic-differentiation-computed Hessians, yields a network whose output is divergence-free and symmetric to machine precision by construction. The RTNN architecture is: S_θ(x) = Σ_{i≤j} T^(i,j)_{acbd} ∂^c∂^d c_ij(x;θ), where {T^(i,j)} is a fixed non-trainable basis of Riemann-like tensors. A universal approximation theorem guarantees that RTNNs can approximate any C² DFST to arbitrary precision. Training uses L-BFGS with loss terms for boundary/initial conditions, a zero-deviatoric (or viscous-stress-matching) constraint on the stress tensor, and energy or induction residuals; for incompressible problems, exact incompressibility is enforced by zeroing coefficients of basis 2-forms containing the time covector e_0*.

## Results

Across five benchmark problems (2D Euler vortex, 3D Beltrami flow, NACA airfoil, cylinder wake, and incompressible MHD), RTNN consistently achieves the lowest median relative L² error among all methods. On the Euler vortex, RTNN reaches 9.92e-05 relative L² error, two orders of magnitude below PINN (3.82e-02) and four orders below NCL (3.87e-01). On the Navier-Stokes suite, RTNN improves over PINN by roughly 4–17× (e.g., 5.70e-03 vs 2.99e-02 on cylinder wake; 1.44e-02 vs 2.48e-01 on airfoil) and over NCL by 4–11×. For MHD, RTNN reduces velocity error to 2.34e-02 versus 1.61e-01 (Curl-SPINN) and 1.82e-01 (SPINN), while also improving magnetic field accuracy to 1.04e-01.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RTNN outperforms all three baselines on every benchmark. Against standard PINN (Raissi et al., 2019), which enforces PDE residuals as soft loss penalties, RTNN achieves 4–17× lower relative L² error across the Navier-Stokes suite and 2 orders of magnitude lower error on the Euler vortex, while training in comparable or slightly longer wall time. Against Neural Conservation Laws (Richter-Powell et al., 2022), which enforces only mass conservation via divergence-free vector fields, RTNN improves accuracy by 4–11× on Navier-Stokes problems and by 4 orders of magnitude on the Euler vortex, while also being roughly 2× faster to train. Against SPINN (Cho et al., 2023) and its Curl-SPINN variant on the MHD problem, RTNN reduces velocity error by ~7× (2.34e-02 vs 1.61e-01/1.82e-01) and magnetic field error by ~1.5–2.8×, at roughly 2× the wall time of SPINN. The consistent advantage across all problems and both self-supervised (PINN-style) and data-supervised (airfoil) settings indicates that the architectural enforcement of both mass and momentum conservation provides a substantially stronger inductive bias than partial or soft constraint approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Serre's mathematical theory of divergence-free symmetric tensors (DFSTs) in continuum mechanics, translating the abstract representation theorem (that every DFST arises as the double divergence of a Riemann-like (0,4)-tensor) into a concrete neural architecture. It departs from the PINN paradigm (Raissi et al., 2019) and Neural Conservation Laws (Richter-Powell et al., 2022), which enforce conservation via soft loss penalties or divergence-free vector fields (mass only), by hard-coding both mass and momentum conservation into the network output structure. The approach is conceptually related to Hamiltonian and Lagrangian neural networks (Greydanus et al., 2019; Cranmer et al., 2020), which embed energy conservation architecturally, but extends the idea to the full flux-divergence structure of conservative PDEs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By enforcing both mass and momentum conservation to machine precision through architecture rather than optimization, RTNNs eliminate a major failure mode of PINNs (inconsistent conservation under stiff optimization) and provide a principled inductive bias that consistently yields 2–4 orders of magnitude lower error across Euler, Navier-Stokes, and MHD benchmarks. The universal approximation theorem guarantees no expressivity loss from the constraint. Open questions include whether the approach scales to higher-dimensional or non-flat domains, how it integrates with operator-learning frameworks for parametric surrogates, and whether the Hessian-based construction can be made more computationally efficient for large-scale problems.
