## Problem

Existing time series foundation models like MOIRAI impose human-defined frequency-level specialization via separate projection layers per frequency, but frequency is an unreliable grouping criterion—series of different frequencies can share patterns while same-frequency series diverge—and nonstationarity means distributions shift even within short windows. This paper asks whether model specialization can be achieved in a fully data-driven, token-level manner without any human-imposed data groupings.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a raw time series context window (length up to 8192 steps), segments it into non-overlapping patches of size 16, applies a causal normalizer computed over a masked portion (ratio r=0.3) of the sequence to handle nonstationarity, and projects all patches through a single residual MLP into D-dimensional tokens. Output is a mixture distribution parameterized by the Transformer's final hidden state passed through a single output projection, from which both point forecasts and probabilistic forecasts (via CRPS, PICP, QICE) are derived. Unlike MOIRAI's masked-filling approach, MOIRAI-MOE generates predictions autoregressively, one patch at a time.

## Method

MOIRAI-MOE replaces MOIRAI's frequency-specific input/output projection layers with a single projection layer and delegates pattern specialization to sparse Mixture-of-Experts layers (M=32 experts, K=2 activated) that replace every FFN in a decoder-only Transformer. The key architectural novelty is a cluster-centroid gating function: a pretrained dense MOIRAI model is run over the LOTSA corpus, its per-layer attention outputs are clustered via mini-batch k-means, and the resulting centroids serve as fixed reference points for computing token-to-expert affinity via Euclidean distance, replacing the standard randomly initialized linear gate. The pretraining objective is switched from masked filling to next-token prediction, which enables parallel learning of multiple context lengths in a single update and yields ~2× pretraining efficiency. This design achieves token-level specialization in a data-driven way: similar tokens (regardless of frequency) are routed to the same experts, while dissimilar tokens within the same frequency are separated, as confirmed by T-SNE visualizations and expert allocation analyses showing progressive denoising and frequency-invariant representations in deeper layers.

## Results

On the zero-shot evaluation across 10 datasets, MOIRAI-MOE_B achieves the best overall CRPS (0.478 avg, 0.439 non-leak) and MASE (0.651 avg, 0.611 non-leak), outperforming all baselines. MOIRAI-MOE_S (11M activated params) surpasses its dense counterpart MOIRAI_S (14M) by 17% on in-distribution Monash evaluation and delivers 3–14% CRPS and 8–16% MASE improvements over all MOIRAI sizes in zero-shot settings. Compared to Chronos_L (710M params), MOIRAI-MOE_S achieves superior results with up to 65× fewer activated parameters. In-distribution, MOIRAI-MOE_S also beats the larger MOIRAI_B and MOIRAI_L by 8% and 7% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MOIRAI-MOE dominates all 11 baseline families. Against its direct predecessor MOIRAI (S/B/L), MOIRAI-MOE_S achieves 3–14% lower CRPS and 8–16% lower MASE in zero-shot, while using fewer activated parameters (11M vs. 14M/91M/310M). Versus Chronos (S/B/L), MOIRAI-MOE_B matches or beats Chronos_L (710M) on CRPS (0.478 vs. 0.500) with only 86M activated parameters (~8× fewer), and MOIRAI-MOE_S beats all Chronos sizes with up to 65× fewer activated params. Against TimesFM (200M), MOIRAI-MOE_B achieves lower CRPS (0.478 vs. 0.488) and MASE (0.651 vs. 0.689). The concurrent Time-MoE (B/L) is clearly outperformed: MOIRAI-MOE_S beats Time-MoE_L by 9% in CRPS (0.497 vs. 0.589) and 1% in MASE (0.670 vs. 0.678), attributed to cluster-based gating and patch-based tokenization. Full-shot baselines (iTransformer, TiDE, PatchTST, MoLE-DLinear) are all surpassed by MOIRAI-MOE in zero-shot, with iTransformer being the closest competitor (CRPS 0.540 vs. 0.497 for MOIRAI-MOE_S). TTM and Timer, which lack specialization modules, show the largest gaps (CRPS 0.758/0.797 vs. 0.497), confirming the value of token-level expert routing.

## Relation to Prior Work

MOIRAI-MOE builds directly on MOIRAI (Woo et al., 2024), replacing its human-imposed frequency-level projection layers with a single projection layer and sparse mixture-of-experts FFNs within a decoder-only Transformer. The MoE architecture follows the standard top-K gating paradigm from Switch Transformers (Fedus et al., 2022) and GShard (Lepikhin et al., 2021), but introduces a novel cluster-centroid-based gating function inspired by sparse upcycling (Komatsuzaki et al., 2022) that leverages a pretrained dense model's representations to guide expert allocation. The paper also departs from the concurrent Time-MoE (Shi et al., 2024) by adopting patch-based tokenization over point-wise tokenization and by proposing and analyzing a new gating mechanism rather than relying on standard linear gating.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first comprehensive study of MoE inner workings in time series foundation models, revealing that the model progressively denoises representations across layers and achieves frequency-invariant hidden states at deep layers—insights that inform future architecture design. The finding that token-level data-driven specialization substantially outperforms frequency-based grouping challenges the prevailing assumption in the field that frequency is a useful grouping criterion. Open questions include whether the cluster-based gating generalizes to other modalities, how to efficiently prune underutilized experts at inference, and whether the progressive denoising pattern observed here holds at larger scales (MOIRAI-MOE_L was not trained due to compute constraints).
