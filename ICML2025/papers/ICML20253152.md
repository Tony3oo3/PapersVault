## Problem

Conditional Sequence Modeling methods for offline RL face a fundamental tension: Decision Transformer's full attention captures long-term dependencies but ignores local Markov structure, while Decision Convformer's fixed convolution excels on local features but fails on non-standard Markov tasks requiring long-range credit assignment. No existing approach dynamically adapts its receptive field to the specific Markovian character of each task, and suboptimal trajectory segments remain unfiltered, degrading both attention quality and computational efficiency.

## Input / Output

The model consumes trajectory sequences of (return-to-go, state, action) triplets with a context window of K=20 timesteps (S=60 tokens total), processed autoregressively. During training the full sequence is available for token selection, but during autoregressive sampling only past tokens exist, necessitating a separate auxiliary predictor that decides token selection from a single token's embedding. The output is a predicted action vector at each state position, trained with MSE loss against ground-truth actions from the offline dataset.

## Method

Decision Mixer introduces a plug-and-play 'mixer layer' that dynamically selects which tokens participate in attention at each layer. A lightweight router assigns a scalar weight to every token, while a hypernetwork (MLP over the full sequence) determines the selection threshold k; the top-k weighted tokens are concatenated in original order and passed through attention + FFN, while unselected tokens bypass via a residual path, with router weights placed on the gradient path for learnable selection. This mechanism simultaneously acts as a trajectory-stitching operator (concatenating high-quality segments from suboptimal data) and a computational saver (halving sequence length reduces attention FLOPs to 25%). To handle the train-test mismatch in autoregressive sampling, a per-layer auxiliary predictor is trained in parallel with cross-entropy loss to predict binary selection labels from individual tokens. The overall architecture alternates three mixer layers with three standard transformer layers to stabilize training, and the entire design is orthogonal to existing CSM improvements, demonstrated by plug-and-play integration into QT and ODT.

## Results

On the D4RL benchmark (normalized scores, where 100 = expert), DM achieves the best average across all five domains: Gym 84.7, Adroit 51.0, Kitchen 70.2, Maze2D 91.1, and AntMaze 86.7. The most dramatic gains appear on non-standard Markov tasks: Maze2D improves from 57.6 (DC) to 91.1, and AntMaze from 75.0 (RvS) to 86.7. Computationally, DM reduces FLOPs by 47% and memory by 37.2% relative to DT while using a comparable parameter count (43.5M vs. 43.3M).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DM outperforms all 20 baselines on average across every domain. Against the closest CSM competitors, DM leads CGDT (82.4) and LSDT (82.0) by 2.3 and 2.7 points on Gym, and decisively beats DC (57.6) by 33.5 points on Maze2D, confirming that dynamic token selection handles non-standard Markov dependencies far better than fixed-range convolution. On Adroit, DM (51.0) surpasses the previous best GDT (38.9) by 12.1 points, with the largest single-task gain on pen-human (125.4 vs. 92.5). On AntMaze, DM (86.7) exceeds RvS (75.0) by 11.7 points, particularly on antmaze-umaze-diverse where DM reaches 100.0 while most baselines score below 70. On Kitchen, DM (70.2) beats GDT (58.6) by 11.6 points. The only domain where DM's margin is modest is Gym (84.7 vs. 82.4 for CGDT), where standard Markov properties make all methods competitive; DM still wins on 7 of 9 Gym tasks individually.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Decision Transformer (DT) paradigm for offline RL via conditional sequence modeling, while drawing conceptual inspiration from Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) routing and conditional computation literature (early exit, token dropping). It positions itself as resolving the tension between DT's strength in long-term dependency modeling and Decision Convformer's (DC) strength in local Markov feature capture, arguing that neither fixed attention nor fixed convolution ranges can adapt to the varying Markov structure across tasks. The dynamic token selection mechanism—combining a per-token router, a sequence-level hypernetwork for threshold k, and a token-level auxiliary predictor for autoregressive inference—is the core architectural departure from both DT and DC, and is explicitly framed as orthogonal and plug-and-play relative to prior CSM improvements like Q-value regularization (QT) and online fine-tuning (ODT).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DM offers a practically important efficiency gain (~47% FLOP reduction) alongside consistent SOTA improvements across five D4RL domains, suggesting a viable path toward scaling offline RL foundation models without proportional compute increases. The plug-and-play demonstration (QDM, ODM) indicates the architecture can be layered onto existing methods without architectural conflict, broadening its utility. However, the paper acknowledges limited robustness to incomplete or noisy data, and the scaling-law behavior in RL remains underexplored—only three model sizes are tested for generalization. An open question is whether the dynamic selection mechanism's benefits persist as context length grows substantially or in continuous-control settings with higher-dimensional state spaces beyond D4RL.
