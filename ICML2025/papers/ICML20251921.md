## Problem

Existing neural networks for the functional ANOVA model (NAM, NBM) produce unstable estimates of individual components because the ANOVA decomposition is not unique—multiple decompositions exist for the same function, and gradient descent may converge to different ones across runs. This unidentifiability makes the resulting feature-level interpretations unreliable, undermining the core purpose of interpretable AI.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251921/cropped_page7_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a numerical feature vector (tabular data) or a set of concept predictions from a pretrained CNN (for image data such as CELEBA) as input, with each feature transformed via marginal ranks to approximate a uniform marginal distribution. It outputs a scalar prediction, the individual low-dimensional ANOVA component functions (1D main effects and 2D second-order interactions), l1-norm-based feature importance scores, and SHAP values computed in closed form from the components via the sum-to-zero condition (ANOVA-SHAP).

## Method

ANOVA-TPNN replaces each tensor product basis function with a specially designed basis neural network φ_j(x|θ) built from a sigmoid activation with a data-dependent correction term c_j(b,γ) that enforces the sum-to-zero condition by construction, guaranteeing identifiability of every ANOVA component. For higher-order interactions, TPNN forms tensor products of these basis neural networks, but unlike classical tensor product basis expansions, the number of learnable parameters grows linearly (not exponentially) in the interaction order |S| because each basis neural network carries its own small parameter set rather than a full coefficient tensor. A universal approximation theorem proves that TPNN can approximate any L-Lipschitz function satisfying the sum-to-zero condition to arbitrary precision by increasing the number of basis neural networks K_S. Training proceeds via standard Adam optimization on the empirical risk, and the sigmoid-based basis functions provide robustness to input outliers compared to spline-based alternatives.

## Results

ANOVA-TPNN achieves substantially lower (better) stability scores than NAM and NBM across all nine benchmark datasets, e.g., 0.012 vs. 0.045 (NAM) and 0.039 (NBM) on CALHOUSING GA¹M, and 0.035 vs. 0.071/0.075 on CALHOUSING GA²M. On component selection, ANOVA-T²PNN reaches AUROC 1.000, 0.943, and 0.956 on three synthetic functions, far exceeding NAM (0.330, 0.311, 0.381) and NBM (0.522, 0.481, 0.477). In terms of prediction accuracy, ANOVA-T²PNN attains an average rank of 3.11 across nine datasets (tied with XGB), confirming that the interpretability gains come without sacrificing predictive performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against NAM (Agarwal et al., 2021), ANOVA-TPNN achieves 2–4× lower stability scores (e.g., 0.012 vs. 0.045 on CALHOUSING GA¹M) and dramatically better component-selection AUROC (1.000 vs. 0.330 on f⁽¹⁾), while matching or exceeding NAM's prediction accuracy on most datasets. Against NBM (Radenovic et al., 2022), ANOVA-TPNN again wins decisively on stability (e.g., 0.035 vs. 0.075 on CALHOUSING GA²M) and component selection (AUROC 0.943 vs. 0.481 on f⁽²⁾), with comparable RMSE/AUROC. Against NODE-GAM (Chang et al., 2021), ANOVA-T²PNN is competitive on prediction (rank 3.11 vs. 5.56) and superior on stability. Against the black-box baselines XGB and DNN, ANOVA-T²PNN ties XGB on average prediction rank (3.11) but provides full component-level interpretability that neither black-box model offers. Against Spline-GAM (Serven & Brummitt, 2018), ANOVA-TPNN outperforms on both RMSE (0.512 vs. 1.326 on CALHOUSING GA²M) and stability (0.035 vs. 0.052), with the paper attributing the gap to Spline-GAM's vulnerability to input outliers due to linear extrapolation outside the training domain.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the functional ANOVA decomposition framework (Hoeffding, Sobol) and the recent wave of neural network approaches to it—NAM (Agarwal et al., 2021), NBM (Radenovic et al., 2022), and NODE-GAM (Chang et al., 2021)—while drawing structural inspiration from classical tensor product basis expansions (Wood, 2006; Rügamer, 2024). The key departure is that existing neural ANOVA models suffer from unidentifiability (multiple valid decompositions of the same function), causing unstable component estimation; ANOVA-TPNN resolves this by structurally embedding the sum-to-zero identifiability condition (Hooker, 2007) into the basis neural network design, so that each component is uniquely determined. The tensor product structure is retained from basis expansion but with data-adaptive neural basis functions, avoiding the exponential parameter growth that limits traditional tensor product splines to low-dimensional settings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Stable component estimation is critical for reliable XAI: if the estimated main effects and interactions shift substantially with different random seeds or data splits, the resulting interpretations are untrustworthy. ANOVA-TPNN provides a principled fix with a universal approximation guarantee, a fast ANOVA-SHAP computation (Proposition 3.2), and easy monotone constraint enforcement—features that make it practically attractive for regulated domains like credit scoring. The main open limitation is computational cost for high-order interactions in high-dimensional settings; the authors acknowledge that a simultaneous selection-and-estimation tool for signal components would be needed to scale beyond second-order interactions. The connection between sum-to-zero ANOVA and SHAP also opens a path toward cheaper global attribution methods.
