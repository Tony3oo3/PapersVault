## Problem

Offline-to-online RL agents pre-trained on fixed datasets often underperform during online fine-tuning due to inaccurate value estimation under distribution shift, sometimes performing worse than training from scratch. Prior remedies (e.g., Cal-QL, SO2) attempt to correct this by modifying the single value function shared across both phases, but this paper argues that the fundamental issue is the reliance on one value function for both offline and online regimes, and that a dedicated value function trained specifically for online adaptation is needed.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an offline dataset B_off, an offline pre-trained agent (policy π^off and value function Q^off-pt), and online environment interactions. During the Online Pre-Training phase, N_τ = 25k transitions are collected by the frozen offline policy into a separate online buffer B_on, which is then jointly used with B_off to train the new value function. The final output is a refined policy and two value functions (Q^off-pt and Q^on-pt) whose weighted combination guides policy improvement, with the weighting coefficient κ scheduled linearly over the 275k fine-tuning steps.

## Method

OPT introduces a third learning phase, Online Pre-Training, between standard offline pre-training and online fine-tuning. In this phase, a newly initialized value function Q^on-pt is trained using a meta-adaptation objective (adapted from OEMA) that combines a TD loss on the offline dataset with a meta-gradient term evaluated on the online buffer, encouraging Q^on-pt to adapt rapidly to evolving policy samples without overfitting to the fixed offline policy. During online fine-tuning, policy improvement uses a convex combination (1−κ)Q^off-pt + κQ^on-pt, where κ is scheduled to increase from a small initial value to 0.9, progressively shifting reliance from the offline-trained value function to the online-adapted one. Balanced replay prioritizes recent online samples to accelerate Q^on-pt's adaptation. The approach is backbone-agnostic and has been validated on TD3+BC, SPOT, IQL, and RLPD across MuJoCo, Antmaze, and Adroit domains.

## Results

On the D4RL MuJoCo domain (9 environments), TD3+OPT achieves a total normalized score of 939.1, surpassing the strongest baseline Off2On (860.9) by 9.1% and the from-scratch TD3 baseline (752.4) by 24.8%. On Antmaze (6 environments), SPOT+OPT reaches 565.3, outperforming Cal-QL (503.4) and SPOT alone (465.7) by 12.3% and 21.3% respectively. On Adroit (4 environments), SPOT+OPT achieves 309.1 versus SPOT's 200.2 (+54.4%) and Cal-QL's near-zero score (-0.23). Across all three domains, OPT delivers an average ~30% improvement over the backbone algorithms.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OPT (TD3+BC backbone) achieves the highest total MuJoCo score of 939.1, beating Off2On (860.9, +9.1%), TD3 from-scratch (752.4, +24.8%), OEMA (694.6, +35.2%), PEX (588.8, +59.5%), ACA (738.5, +27.2%), FamO2O (504.4, +86.2%), and Cal-QL (586.8, +60.0%). On Antmaze, SPOT+OPT (565.3) outperforms Cal-QL (503.4, +12.3%) and SPOT alone (465.7, +21.3%), with particularly large gains on umaze-diverse (97.7 vs. 75.2 for Cal-QL) and large-diverse (90.1 vs. 72.9). On Adroit, SPOT+OPT (309.1) dramatically surpasses both SPOT (200.2) and Cal-QL (-0.23), which essentially fails on cloned datasets. When integrated with RLPD, OPT improves the MuJoCo total from 887.6 (RLPD Off-to-On) to 918.7 (+3.5%), and when applied to IQL, it yields a ~25% average improvement. Against the most recent baselines BOORL (852.1 at 300k) and SO2 (818.5 at 300k), OPT (933.9) maintains a clear lead on aggregate MuJoCo performance while avoiding the ensemble-based computational overhead those methods require.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the offline-to-online RL line of work (Off2On, PEX, ACA, FamO2O, Cal-QL) by departing from the shared assumption that a single value function must serve both offline and online phases. Prior methods (Cal-QL, SO2) attempt to correct or constrain the offline-trained value function during fine-tuning, whereas OPT introduces an entirely new value function (Q^on-pt) trained via a meta-adaptation objective adapted from OEMA (Guo et al. 2023), originally designed for policy learning. The backbone algorithms (TD3+BC, SPOT) provide the actor-critic structure, while the dual-value-function policy improvement with scheduled κ weighting is the paper's core architectural contribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

OPT provides a modular, plug-in solution to the well-documented 'counter-intuitive trend' where offline pre-training actively harms online fine-tuning, achieving ~30% average improvement across D4RL with minimal architectural changes to existing algorithms. Its applicability to diverse backbones (TD3, SPOT, IQL, RLPD) positions it as a general enhancement module rather than a standalone algorithm. Open questions include whether the per-environment κ scheduling can be automated, whether the approach scales to longer online budgets or continuous deployment settings, and whether the meta-adaptation objective is truly optimal compared to simpler alternatives. The ~50% increase in wall-clock training time and the need for 25k environment steps before fine-tuning begin are practical costs that limit immediate deployment in latency-sensitive settings.
