## Problem

Referring MLLMs frequently generate incorrect responses influenced by misleading regions adjacent to or visually similar to the target region, rather than being fully tailored to the input visual prompt. Existing contrastive decoding methods for MLLMs are not specifically designed for the referring setting, and manual or random sampling of contrastive prompts lacks robustness and optimality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20253212/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives an RGB image, a visual prompt in one of four forms (bounding box, mask, scribble, or point), and a free-form text instruction, and produces a natural-language response (classification answer, region description, or reasoning output). The prompt extraction network internally generates multiple contrastive point prompts from misleading regions, which are used during contrastive decoding but are not part of the user-facing input. The distilled single-execution model requires only one MLLM forward pass per input, adding just 2.1% parameters and 4.3% inference time over the Ferret baseline.

## Method

CPCF contrasts the MLLM's output from the original visual prompt r with outputs from automatically extracted contrastive prompts r̂ sampled from misleading regions, amplifying target-region information while suppressing erroneous signals. A prompt extraction network (initialized from BLIP2's Q-Former) generates 64 candidate contrastive points, guided by semantic similarity and relative distance maps computed via CLIP features, with Mean Shift clustering to select final prompts. The network is trained via a self-training loop on unlabeled Open Images data: RAG-based in-context question generation produces instruction pairs, and DPO preference optimization (using chain-of-thought answers as preferred over direct answers) tunes the prompt extraction network. Finally, a distillation step transfers the multi-execution contrastive decoder into a single-execution student model using adapter modules whose tokens are generated by an AGN encoding regional differences, optimized with both KL-divergence distillation loss and a Stable Diffusion inpainting loss that forces the adapter to capture what is present in the target region but absent in contrastive regions.

## Results

CPCF-7B achieves 78.37% accuracy on Referring Object Classification (ROC-Box), surpassing Ferret-7B (71.71%) by 6.66 points and Ferret-13B (72.83%) by 5.54 points. On Ferret-Bench, CPCF-7B scores 83.5 on Referring Captioning and 82.9 on Referring Reasoning, outperforming Ferret-v2-7B (79.9 / 81.7) by 3.6 and 1.2 points respectively. The 13B variant further improves to 79.09% ROC-Box and 84.0 / 83.2 on Ferret-Bench, setting new state-of-the-art across all four evaluated tasks (ROC, RTC, RD, and Ferret-Bench) and all four prompt types (box, mask, scribble, point).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CPCF-7B outperforms all 11 baselines across every task and prompt type. Against the primary baseline Ferret-7B, CPCF-7B gains +6.66 on ROC-Box, +7.16 on ROC-Mask, +6.39 on ROC-Scribble, and +8.60 on ROC-Point, with similar margins on RTC and RD. Against the strongest prior referring MLLM, Ferret-v2-7B, CPCF-7B leads by +3.6 on Referring Captioning and +1.2 on Referring Reasoning on Ferret-Bench. Among contrastive decoding baselines (Table 3), CPCF-7B surpasses Ferret+CRG (75.40) by +2.97, Ferret+VCD (74.90) by +3.47, and Ferret+ICD (74.55) by +3.82 on ROC-Box, confirming that the automatic prompt extraction and task-specific contrastive design outperform perturbation- or instruction-based contrastive strategies. Even the 7B CPCF model exceeds the 13B Ferret baseline (72.83) by over 5 points on ROC-Box, and CPCF-13B further extends the lead to 79.09. On the specialized misleading-region evaluations (Tables 9–10), CPCF-7B achieves 91.3% accuracy on adjacent-region errors and 83.9% on similar-region errors, compared to 73.9% and 67.0% for Ferret-7B, respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Ferret (You et al., 2023) as its base referring MLLM architecture and adapts the contrastive decoding mechanism from Li et al. (2022) to the referring setting by contrasting outputs from different visual prompts rather than different models or perturbed images. The most closely related work is CRG (Wan et al., 2025), which also applies contrastive decoding to referring tasks but relies on perturbing image regions; CPCF instead uses an automatic prompt extraction network (initialized from BLIP2's Q-Former) to identify contrastive prompts from misleading regions, avoiding semantic disruption. The self-training pipeline (RAG-based question generation + DPO) and the inpainting-based distillation loss are novel contributions that have no direct precedent in the referring MLLM literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CPCF addresses a concrete and frequently observed failure mode in referring MLLMs—confusion with adjacent or visually similar regions—with a principled mechanism (cross-prompt contrastive decoding) and makes it practical through distillation to single-execution inference (~74% reduction in inference time). The error-specific evaluations (91.3% vs. 76.2% on adjacent-region errors) provide strong evidence that the method targets the right failure mode rather than merely improving general accuracy. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond the Ferret/Vicuna architecture, whether the inpainting-based distillation loss is essential or a proxy for simpler regional-difference encoding, and how the method scales to dense multi-prompt or video referring scenarios.
