## Problem

Standard language model pre-training treats all documents from heterogeneous web sources identically, overlooking contextual signals that aid comprehension and impeding reliable surfacing of appropriate behaviors (e.g., factuality vs. humor) for downstream tasks. While metadata conditioning has been explored in narrow settings (style control, robustness, synthetic memorization), its general-purpose utility in realistic-scale LM pre-training—and whether a model can still perform well without metadata at inference—remained unexplored.

## Input / Output

During training, each document is prepended with its source URL's absolute domain name (e.g., 'en.wikipedia.org') as a metadata prefix, with cross-entropy loss computed only over document tokens, not the metadata or template. The final 10% of training (cooldown) uses standard documents without any prefix. At inference, the model accepts plain text (unconditional) or text with an optional real or fabricated URL prepended (conditional), enabling steering of generation style, factuality, or safety without requiring the model to have seen that specific URL during training.

## Method

MeCo (Metadata Conditioning then Cooldown) is a two-stage pre-training procedure: the first 90% of training prepends each document's source URL domain to the text, while the final 10% cooldown trains on standard documents without metadata, inheriting the learning rate schedule and optimizer states from the conditioning stage. The cooldown is critical—training solely on metadata-augmented data degrades unconditional inference performance by ~5 points. Ablations reveal that metadata primarily functions as a grouping signal: hashed URLs (with no semantic content) achieve identical performance to real URLs, and model-generated topics work comparably, indicating the model benefits from document co-clustering rather than URL semantics. At inference, prepending a real or fabricated URL (e.g., 'en.wikipedia.org' to reduce toxicity, 'factquizmaster.com' to boost knowledge recall) activates conditional inference, yielding up to 6% absolute gains on commonsense tasks and several-fold reductions in harmful generations. The method adds zero computational overhead, requires no data selection or scoring, and is compatible with Llama-architecture transformers across 600M–8B parameters.

## Results

On 10 downstream tasks (MMLU, ARC-e, ARC-c, CSQA, HSwag, OBQA, PIQA, SIQA, WinoGrande, TruthfulQA) evaluated with 5-shot OLMES, MeCo achieves 56.7 average accuracy for a 1.6B model trained on 160B DCLM tokens, matching the 240B-token standard baseline (56.7) while using 33% less data. MeCo also outperforms the fastText data-selection baseline (56.0) by 0.7 points without incurring any additional computational overhead. Gains are consistent across model scales (600M–8B) and corpora (C4, RefinedWeb, DCLM), with the largest improvements observed on OpenBookQA (+5.2) and TruthfulQA (+3.3) over standard pre-training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MeCo is compared against three baselines in the main experiment (1.6B, DCLM). Against standard pre-training (55.7 avg), MeCo wins by +1.0 points, with the largest per-task gains on OBQA (+5.2) and TruQA (+3.3). Against the fastText data-selection baseline from Li et al. (2024) (56.0 avg), MeCo wins by +0.7 points while requiring no additional classifier inference over the corpus. Most notably, MeCo (160B tokens) ties the 240B-token standard baseline (56.7 avg) exactly, achieving equivalent downstream performance with 33% less training data and compute. The paper also notes that validation perplexity does not correlate with downstream performance: the 240B baseline has lower PPL (12.9 vs. 13.3) yet identical average task accuracy, reinforcing that MeCo's gains stem from improved inductive bias rather than better language modeling.

## Relation to Prior Work

MeCo extends the conditional language model paradigm introduced by CTRL (Keskar et al., 2019), which prepended source-domain control codes to documents for steerable generation, and the synthetic knowledge-memorization experiments of Allen-Zhu & Li (2024), which showed that prepending a special token to high-quality data improves recall. The key departures are: (1) demonstrating the approach at modern pre-training scale (600M–8B parameters, 80B–240B tokens) with real URLs rather than synthetic tokens, and (2) introducing a cooldown phase that lets the model operate without metadata at inference—a practical requirement absent from prior conditional-training work. The paper also positions itself against data-selection methods (e.g., DCLM's fastText classifier) and domain-mixture optimization (DoReMi), showing that a zero-overhead URL prefix matches or exceeds their gains without any additional compute.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

A 33% reduction in pre-training data with zero computational overhead is a practically significant efficiency gain that is trivially adoptable by any team with URL-annotated corpora, and the conditional-inference steering capability (e.g., prepending wikipedia.org to reduce toxicity) opens a new axis of controllability without fine-tuning. However, the study is limited to single runs on English corpora, provides no mechanistic explanation for why metadata conditioning accelerates learning, and does not examine interaction with post-training (RLHF, instruction tuning). Open questions include whether the benefit persists at much larger scales (100B+), whether the grouping signal can be replaced by cheaper proxies at inference time, and how metadata conditioning interacts with data-mixture optimization methods.
