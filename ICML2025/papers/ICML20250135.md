## Problem

Pre-trained univariate time series foundation models (e.g., Moment, Chronos, TimesFM) are trained for single-channel forecasting due to tractability constraints, yet real-world applications demand multivariate forecasting with reliable uncertainty quantification. Existing approaches either require expensive full fine-tuning or process channels independently, ignoring inter-feature dependencies. The paper asks: how can frozen univariate FMs be adapted to multivariate probabilistic forecasting without retraining the backbone?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20250135/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a multivariate time series context window of length L=512 with D features (7–21 depending on dataset) and produces a probabilistic forecast over horizon H (24–192 steps) for all D channels simultaneously. The frozen FM operates per-channel in a learned D′-dimensional latent space (D′ ≤ D), and the decoder maps predictions back to the original D-dimensional feature space. Uncertainty is captured through quantile estimates from the stochastic latent representation, with calibration evaluated via reliability diagrams.

## Method

AdaPTS inserts a trainable feature-space adapter (encoder φ and decoder φ⁻¹) around a frozen univariate FM: the encoder projects the D-channel input into a D′-dimensional latent space, the FM forecasts each latent channel independently, and the decoder reconstructs the D-channel prediction. The paper provides a closed-form optimal solution for linear adapters under a linear FM (Proposition 3.3), proving that the identity transformation is generally suboptimal. For probabilistic forecasting, the adapter is extended to VAE adapters (with β-VAE KL regularization controlling latent disentanglement) and Monte Carlo dropout adapters, which introduce stochasticity in the latent space to yield predictive distributions. Training optimizes only the adapter parameters via supervised MSE, unsupervised reconstruction, or fine-tuning modes, while the FM backbone remains frozen, enabling modular plug-and-play adaptation across different foundation models.

## Results

On the four multivariate forecasting benchmarks (ETTh1, Illness, Weather, ExchangeRate) with Moment as the frozen backbone, AdaPTS adapters improve MSE in 5 out of 8 tasks, match in 2, and degrade in 1. The largest gains are a 15% MSE reduction on Illness (H=24, from 2.902 to 2.461 with the VAE adapter) and a 12% reduction on Weather (H=96, from 0.177 to 0.156 with dropoutLinearAE). On ETTh1 (H=96), dropoutLinearAE achieves the best MSE of 0.395 versus the 0.411 baseline (8% improvement). The VAE adapter also achieves the best calibration (lowest ECE) at β=4.0 and log σ²=3.0 on Illness.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the vanilla Moment baseline (each channel forecast independently), AdaPTS adapters win on 5 of 8 dataset-horizon combinations, tie on 2 (ETTh1 H=192, ExchangeRate H=96), and lose on 1 (ExchangeRate H=192, where VAE degrades to 0.607 vs. 0.210). The PCA adapter, used as a simpler non-learned baseline, is consistently outperformed by the learned adapters (LinearAE, dropoutLAE, LinearVAE, VAE) on every task, confirming that the learned feature-space transformation captures channel interactions that PCA misses. When extended beyond Moment, the VAE adapter yields a 12.35% MSE improvement on TTM (Illness H=24), while dropoutLAE gives a more modest 3.64% improvement on TimesFM, suggesting adapter effectiveness depends on the underlying foundation model architecture. The paper notes these cross-model results are preliminary and require broader evaluation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the adapter paradigm from time series classification (Feofanov et al., 2024; Benechehab et al., 2025) to multivariate forecasting, replacing simple PCA rotations with learned encoder-decoder pairs that are jointly optimized against a frozen foundation model. The probabilistic variants draw directly on the VAE ELBO (Kingma & Welling, 2013), β-VAE regularization (Higgins et al., 2017), and Monte Carlo dropout as approximate variational inference (Gal & Ghahramani, 2016), while the theoretical grounding for partially stochastic networks follows Sharma et al. (2023). A key departure from prior adapter work is the explicit fitting of an inverse transformation (decoder) to map FM predictions back to the original feature space, and the provision of a closed-form optimal solution in the linear case (Proposition 3.3) that motivates gradient-based optimization for the general case.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AdaPTS offers a practical, modular recipe for leveraging existing univariate time series FMs on multivariate tasks without expensive fine-tuning, with the added benefit of probabilistic outputs for uncertainty quantification. However, the empirical gains are mixed (5 of 8 tasks improved, 1 degraded), the evaluation is limited to four datasets and primarily the Moment model, and calibration degrades noticeably at longer horizons. Open questions include whether the adapter approach generalizes robustly across diverse FMs and very high-dimensional settings, whether MCMC-based inference would meaningfully improve calibration over the VI/dropout approximations used here, and whether the theoretical insights from the linear case transfer to the non-linear regime where the closed-form solution is unavailable.
