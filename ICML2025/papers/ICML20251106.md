## Problem

Efficiently ranking N items using multiple noisy oracles (e.g., human annotators evaluating LLMs on different tasks) remains challenging when oracles have heterogeneous accuracy and no single oracle is best for all pairs. Under the weak stochastic transitivity (WST) condition, no multi-oracle ranking algorithm with tight sample complexity existed, and the lower bound conjectured by Lou et al. (2022) remained unproven. Under strong stochastic transitivity (SST), the existing multi-oracle algorithm of Saad et al. (2023) carried an unnecessary log(N) factor in its sample complexity.

## Input / Output

The algorithm receives N items, M oracles that return binary pairwise preference feedback (Bernoulli with parameter p^u_{i,j} for oracle u), and a target confidence level δ. All oracles must agree on the direction of preference for every pair (consistency assumption), though their accuracy levels differ. The output is a δ-correct total ordering of the N items, with the guarantee that the returned ranking matches the ground truth with probability at least 1−δ. The key input structure is that each oracle has its own comparison probability matrix, and the algorithm adaptively decides which oracle to query for each pair.

## Method

RMO-WST employs a bi-level design: at the high level, Probe-Max iteratively identifies and removes the maximal item from a partial-order-preserving directed graph, using transitive closure under WST to infer additional orderings without queries; at the low level, the Compare/Try-Compare subroutine adaptively allocates comparison budgets across oracles by maintaining an active set and eliminating oracles whose estimated accuracy falls below the group median. The hardness factor H_{i,j} = M/Σ_u(Δ^u_{i,j})² captures the benefit of oracle selection over uniform averaging, yielding a sample complexity of Õ(N Σ H_{σ⁻¹(i),σ⁻¹(i-1)}) that matches a new lower bound. For the SST setting, RMO-SST replaces the Attempt-to-Compare subroutine in the Iterative-Insertion-Ranking framework with the same Compare routine, removing an extra log(N) factor to achieve Õ(Σ H_i log N) and matching the known lower bound.

## Results

Under the WST condition, RMO-WST achieves a sample complexity of $\widetilde{O}\left(N \sum_{i=2}^{N} H_{\sigma^{-1}(i),\sigma^{-1}(i-1)}\right)$, where $H_{i,j}$ is the multi-oracle hardness factor, and the paper proves a matching lower bound of $\widetilde{\Omega}\left(N^2 \min_i H_{\sigma^{-1}(i),\sigma^{-1}(i-1)}\right)$, resolving an open conjecture by Lou et al. (2022). Under the SST condition, RMO-SST achieves $\widetilde{O}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N} H_i \log(N)\right)$, improving over the prior best (Saad et al., 2023) by a factor of $\log N$ and matching the known lower bound. In empirical experiments with $N \in \{2,4,8,16,32,64\}$ items and $M \in \{2,4,6\}$ oracles (one high-accuracy, rest noisy), RMO-WST consistently requires fewer comparisons than the Probe-Max baseline, with the advantage widening as the fraction of noisy oracles increases.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Probe-Max (Lou et al., 2022), RMO-WST strictly improves the sample complexity by replacing the single-oracle hardness $\bar{H}_{i,j}$ with the multi-oracle hardness $H_{i,j}$ (where $H_{i,j} \leq \bar{H}_{i,j}$ by Cauchy-Schwarz), and empirically outperforms the baseline across all tested $N$ and $M$ values, with the gap widening from $M=2$ to $M=6$ as more noisy oracles are present. Against Binary-Search (Saad et al., 2023), RMO-SST removes an extra $\log N$ factor in the sample complexity (going from $\log^2(N)$ to $\log(N)$) by using a more sophisticated insertion procedure, while maintaining the multi-oracle setting. Against IIR (Ren et al., 2019), RMO-SST extends the single-oracle algorithm to the multi-oracle setting and matches the theoretical lower bound of $\Omega\left(\sum_{i\in[N]}H_i(\log\log(H_i)+\log(N/\delta))\right)$. The paper also establishes the first multi-oracle lower bound under WST, confirming that RMO-WST is optimal in its $N$-dependence.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends two parallel lines of active ranking research: the single-oracle WST ranking of Lou et al. (2022, Probe-Max/Probe-Rank) and the multi-oracle SST ranking of Saad et al. (2023, Binary-Search). The core algorithmic contribution is a Compare subroutine that adapts Saad et al.'s oracle-elimination strategy from numerical (sub-Gaussian) feedback to binary Bernoulli preference feedback, then plugs it into both the Probe-Max framework (for WST) and the IIR insertion-sort framework of Ren et al. (2019) (for SST). The paper also proves a lower bound for multi-oracle WST ranking that was explicitly conjectured but left open by Lou et al. (2022), closing a gap in the theoretical landscape.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The proven lower bound resolves an open problem and confirms that the RMO-WST sample complexity is tight in N, giving practitioners a clear picture of the fundamental cost of ranking under the weaker WST assumption. The log N improvement over Saad et al. (2023) under SST is a clean but incremental theoretical gain. The practical motivation—ranking LLMs across heterogeneous evaluation tasks where no single oracle dominates—is timely, though the experiments are limited to a single high-accuracy oracle among many noisy ones and the monotonicity assumption (all oracles agree on the true ranking) may not hold in real annotation settings. Open questions include whether an instance-dependent lower bound exists for WST, and whether a unified algorithm can achieve best-of-both-worlds guarantees under both WST and SST simultaneously.
