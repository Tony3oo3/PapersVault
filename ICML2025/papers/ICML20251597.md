## Problem

Existing feature-level adversarial attacks (FIA, RPA, NAA, DANAA, SFVA, BFA) perform multiple perturbations in pixel space, yet the paper proves via the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality that their net effect is mathematically equivalent to a single-step perturbation of intermediate features along the feature importance direction. This inefficiency in disrupting shared critical features fundamentally limits the transferability of the resulting adversarial examples across models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20251597/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard RGB image (224×224 from the 1000-image ImageNet-NIPS benchmark), its ground-truth label, and a white-box surrogate model (e.g., Inception-v3, ResNet-152) as input. It outputs a single adversarial image constrained within an ℓ∞ ball of radius ε=16 around the original. Unusually, the optimization variable is not the pixel image but the intermediate-layer feature map; the final adversarial example is reconstructed from perturbed features via a feature inversion procedure rather than by directly adding a pixel-space perturbation.

## Method

P2FA shifts the perturbation space from pixels to features and iteratively perturbs the k-th layer feature map T=3 times along dynamically updated feature importance directions (computed from BFA's fitted gradient), using momentum (μ=1.0) to stabilize the update direction and prevent local convergence. At each iteration t, the momentum accumulator is updated as g_{t+1}=μ·g_t+W_t, the feature map is shifted as f̃_k=f_k(x_t^{adv})+λ·g_{t+1}/‖g_{t+1}‖₂ with step size λ=10⁵, and the perturbed features are mapped back to pixel space via the guided feature inversion algorithm of Du et al. (2018). The key theoretical insight is that the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality shows the optimal solution to the feature-level attack objective ⟨W, f_k(x^{adv})⟩ is achieved by a single feature-space step f_k(x)+s·W, meaning all prior multi-step pixel-space attacks collapse to this one-step equivalent; P2FA breaks this collapse by performing genuinely multiple distinct feature-space perturbations with re-computed importance at each step.

## Results

On the ImageNet-NIPS dataset, P2FA achieves an average attack success rate of 95.7% (Inc-v3 surrogate), 94.9% (Inc-v4), 92.4% (IncRes-v2), and 96.3% (Res-152) against seven normally trained target models, outperforming the strongest baseline BFA (93.6%, 93.1%, 89.5%, 94.8%) by 2.1% on average. When combined with input transformations PIM and DIM, PIDI-P2FA reaches 99.1% average (Res-152 surrogate) versus 95.8% for PIDI-BFA. Against defense models and vision transformers, PIDI-P2FA achieves 62.6% and 74.9% average success rates respectively, surpassing all baselines by over 7% and 15%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

P2FA consistently outperforms all six feature-level baselines (FIA, RPA, NAA, DANAA, SFVA, BFA) across every surrogate model and every target model in Table 2. The closest competitor is BFA, which P2FA surpasses by 2.1% on average (e.g., 95.7% vs. 93.6% with Inc-v3 surrogate; 96.3% vs. 94.8% with Res-152 surrogate). The gap widens substantially for weaker baselines: NAA lags P2FA by 15.1% (80.6% vs. 95.7%) and FIA by 11.4% (84.3% vs. 95.7%) under the Inc-v3 surrogate. When combined with input transformations PIM+DIM, PIDI-P2FA reaches 99.1% average (Res-152 surrogate) versus 95.8% for PIDI-BFA, a 3.3% gap. Against defense models and vision transformers, PIDI-P2FA achieves 62.6% and 74.9% average success rates, exceeding the best baseline (PIDI-BFA) by over 7% and 15% respectively, indicating the advantage is most pronounced on harder targets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the lineage of feature-level adversarial attacks (FIA, RPA, NAA, DANAA, SFVA, BFA) that improve transferability by targeting intermediate-layer features rather than output logits. Its key conceptual departure is a theoretical proof (via Cauchy-Schwarz) that all these existing attacks, despite performing multiple pixel-space perturbations, are mathematically equivalent to a single-step perturbation along the feature-importance direction in feature space. P2FA then shifts the optimization variable from pixel space to feature space, performing multiple perturbations directly on feature maps with dynamic feature importance and momentum, before inverting back to pixels via feature inversion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical equivalence result reframes how the community should think about feature-level attacks: the apparent benefit of multi-step pixel-space optimization is illusory, and the real lever is multi-step perturbation in feature space. This could redirect future work away from ever-more-sophisticated pixel-space schedules toward feature-space optimization. Limitations include evaluation on only 1000 ImageNet-NIPS images, reliance on a specific feature-inversion algorithm (Du et al., 2018) that may introduce artifacts, and the fact that the method's gains, while consistent, are moderate for normally trained CNNs (~2% over BFA) though more substantial for Vision Transformers (~15%). An open question is whether the feature-space perturbation paradigm extends naturally to generative models or non-visual modalities where feature inversion is less well-defined.
