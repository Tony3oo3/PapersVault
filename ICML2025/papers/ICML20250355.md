## Problem

Adversarial-based fair GNNs that use learnable masks to remove sensitive attribute information suffer from severe training instability: accuracy curves fluctuate dramatically during convergence, making optimal model selection largely accidental. Existing Lipschitz constraints for GNNs are separable (per-layer) and only bound the encoder, ignoring the uncertainty introduced by the mask generator, so they cannot control the joint stability of the full adversarial framework.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an attributed graph G=(V,E,X) with K nodes, node feature matrix X∈R^{K×N₀}, and binary sensitive attribute labels (e.g., gender, race, age). A Gumbel-Softmax mask generator produces μ=10 stochastic fair feature views by element-wise multiplying X with learned per-channel keep probabilities. The 2-layer GraphSAGE encoder then maps each fair view to node embeddings, from which a classifier predicts node labels and a discriminator predicts sensitive attributes. The final output is a set of fair node embeddings whose predictions are statistically independent of the sensitive attribute, evaluated via ΔDP and ΔEO fairness metrics alongside accuracy and AUC.

## Method

SFG derives a tight, non-separable upper Lipschitz bound (Theorem 3.1) that jointly captures the spectral norm of the product of all encoder layer weights and the mask generator weights, expressed via the maximal frequency response h_max(λ) of the graph filter from spectral graph theory. To enforce this bound, a stochastic projected subgradient algorithm (accelerated DFB) operates in a block-coordinate manner across layers, projecting each layer's weight matrix onto the intersection of the nonnegative cone and a spectral ball of radius τ, thereby converting the non-convex joint constraint into a sequence of convex subproblems. Additionally, SFG replaces standard ERM with a multi-view DRO objective over a chi-squared divergence ball surrounding each generated fair view distribution, which upweights large losses and effectively drops overfitting nodes that cause accuracy collapse during fairness chasing. The combination of the Lipschitz constraint (controlling overall weight fluctuation) and DRO (mitigating extreme per-epoch anomalies) yields stable training while preserving both utility and group fairness.

## Results

On the three real-world datasets, SFG achieves the best accuracy on Bail (89.49%) and Credit (80.34%), and competitive accuracy on German (70.32%, second only to FairGNN's 71.32%). For fairness, SFG attains the lowest ΔEO on Bail (1.11) and German (0.54), and the lowest ΔDP on Bail among adversarial-based methods (3.55 vs. FairVGNN's 4.39). The key differentiator is training stability: SFG's accuracy curve in the convergence phase shows dramatically reduced fluctuation amplitude and frequency compared to FairVGNN, with the worst-case accuracy on Bail improving substantially due to the DRO module dropping overfitting nodes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SFG (built on FairVGNN) achieves the best accuracy on Bail (89.49% vs. FairVGNN's 89.24%) and Credit (80.34% vs. 80.13%), and the best AUC on Bail (93.98% vs. 91.99%), while on German it trails FairGNN on accuracy (70.32% vs. 71.32%) and FairSAD on AUC (69.38% vs. 72.20%). For fairness, SFG attains the best ΔEO on Bail (1.11 vs. FairVGNN's 2.49) and German (0.54 vs. 3.06), but FairSAD dominates ΔDP across all datasets (0.00/2.08/2.39 vs. SFG's 1.36/3.55/5.44) due to its disentangled encoder with multiplicatively increasing parameters. Compared to the non-adversarial baselines (GCN, NIFTY, FairGNN), SFG consistently achieves lower ΔDP and ΔEO, confirming the advantage of adversarial masking. The paper's primary empirical claim is stability rather than raw metric superiority: SFG's training accuracy curve shows 43% smaller weight fluctuation range and 14% smaller relative weight change between epochs compared to FairVGNN, and the DRO module specifically eliminates the worst-case accuracy dips caused by overfitting nodes during fairness chasing.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the adversarial-based fair GNN framework of FairVGNN (Wang et al., 2022) by addressing a practical but underexplored issue: training instability caused by the uncertainty of the learned mask generator. It departs from prior Lipschitz-constraint work (Scaman & Virmaux, 2018; Zhao et al., 2021; Jia et al., 2024) that used separable per-layer bounds or Jacobian approximations, instead deriving a tight non-separable bound via spectral graph theory that jointly covers the generator and encoder. The optimization leverages the accelerated DFB algorithm from Neacsu et al. (2024) in a block-coordinate fashion, and adds a chi-squared DRO objective (building on Duchi & Namkoong, 2021) to handle distribution shifts from generated fair views.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Training instability in adversarial fair GNNs makes model selection unreliable and undermines the fairness-utility trade-off, a practical concern for any deployment of fair graph models. The tight non-separable Lipschitz bound is a clean theoretical contribution that bridges spatial and spectral GNN perspectives, but its applicability is limited by the non-negativity assumption on weights and the restriction to GraphSAGE with mean aggregation. Open questions include whether the approach scales to deeper architectures, heterogeneous GNNs, or larger graphs, and whether the restrictive non-negativity constraint can be relaxed without losing the tight bound. The DRO component's dependence on the Lipschitz constraint (neither works well alone) suggests the two mechanisms address complementary failure modes, but the interaction is not fully characterized theoretically.
