## Problem

SE(3) equivariant GNNs for Hamiltonian prediction rely heavily on Clebsch-Gordan tensor product operations whose cost scales quadratically with the number of atoms and with the sixth power of the maximum angular momentum order L. This makes them prohibitively expensive for large molecular systems and for larger DFT basis sets (e.g., Def2-TZVP requiring L=6 versus Def2-SVP at L=4), limiting scalability to molecules with hundreds of atoms.

## Input / Output

The model takes atomic numbers and 3D Cartesian coordinates as input and outputs the full Kohn-Sham Hamiltonian matrix. Rather than predicting H directly, SPHNet predicts the residual ΔH = H_ref − H_init (where H_init is a cheap PySCF Minao initial guess) and adds H_init back, which stabilizes training since ΔH has roughly an order-of-magnitude smaller scale. The Hamiltonian matrix dimensions depend on the basis set: Def2-SVP (L_max=4) for MD17/QH9 and Def2-TZVP (L_max=6) for PubChemQH, where the latter yields ~15× more orbitals and ~200× more matrix elements per molecule.

## Method

SPHNet introduces two adaptive sparse gates into the equivariant architecture: a Sparse Pair Gate that selects a subset of atom pairs (i,j) to eliminate redundant quadratic interactions, and a Sparse Tensor Product Gate that prunes low-importance cross-order combinations (ℓ₁,ℓ₂,ℓ₃) within each tensor product, reducing the O(L⁶) per-operation cost. Both gates are governed by a Three-phase Sparsity Scheduler that first randomly samples combinations (unbiased exploration), then adaptively selects top-weighted combinations via a learnable score vector, and finally freezes the selection for efficient static inference. The network architecture uses four Vectorial Node Interaction Blocks (ℓ≤1, no tensor products) to build low-order features, two Spherical Node Interaction Blocks to elevate to the required L_max, two Pair Construction blocks (diagonal and non-diagonal), and an Expansion block that maps pair irreps to the Hamiltonian via tensor expansion with atom-type filtering. Sparsity rates are tuned per dataset scale (10% for MD17, 40% for QH9, 70% for PubChemQH), yielding up to 7× speedup and 75% memory reduction over QHNet while improving Hamiltonian MAE.

## Results

On the PubChemQH dataset (50k molecules, 40–100 atoms, Def2-TZVP basis), SPHNet achieves a Hamiltonian MAE of 97.31 × 10⁻⁶ Eₕ, outperforming QHNet (123.74) and WANet (99.98), while training 7.1× faster (3.12 vs. 0.44 samples/sec) and using only 5.62 GB/sample of GPU memory (vs. 22.5 GB for QHNet). On QH9 (134k small molecules, Def2-SVP), SPHNet reduces Hamiltonian MAE from 76.31 to 45.48 × 10⁻⁶ Eₕ on the stable-iid split with a 4.0× speedup. On the smaller MD17 trajectory dataset, SPHNet achieves accuracy comparable to QHNet (e.g., H MAE of 19.36 vs. 20.12 on uracil), confirming that sparsification does not degrade performance on small systems.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against QHNet (Yu et al., 2023), SPHNet wins decisively on every metric: lower Hamiltonian MAE (97.31 vs. 123.74 on PubChemQH; 45.48 vs. 76.31 on QH9 stable-iid), lower occupied-energy MAE, higher cosine similarity, 7.1× faster training, and 75% less GPU memory. Against WANet (Li et al., 2025), SPHNet achieves a better Hamiltonian MAE (97.31 vs. 99.98) and is 2.9× faster (3.12 vs. 1.09 samples/sec), though WANet retains an edge on occupied-energy MAE (1.17 vs. 2.16 Eₕ) and cosine similarity (3.13 vs. 2.97 × 10⁻²). On the MD17 trajectory dataset, SPHNet is comparable to QHNet (e.g., H MAE 19.36 vs. 20.12 on uracil; 21.02 vs. 20.91 on ethanol) and substantially outperforms SchNOrb (Schütt et al., 2019) and PhiSNet (Unke et al., 2021), which report H MAE values of 187–228 and 15–21 × 10⁻⁶ Eₕ respectively. The paper also demonstrates that the sparse gates are transferable: applying them to QHNet alone yields a 3.3× speedup with only a ~5 × 10⁻⁶ Eₕ increase in H MAE, confirming the generality of the sparsification strategy across SE(3)-equivariant architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

SPHNet extends the QHNet architecture (Yu et al., 2023) by introducing adaptive sparsity into the tensor product operations that dominate SE(3) equivariant network computation. It also incorporates the long-short range message-passing mechanism from Li et al. (2024) for node feature extraction. The key conceptual departure from prior Hamiltonian prediction work (PhiSNet, QHNet, WANet) is the application of learned, three-phase sparsification gates to both the pair-selection and cross-order-combination dimensions of tensor products, drawing on the broader network sparsification literature (Lottery Ticket Hypothesis, iterative pruning) but adapting it specifically to the Clebsch-Gordan tensor product structure of equivariant networks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 7x speedup and 75% memory reduction on large-molecule systems (PubChemQH, ~100 atoms, L_max=6) make Hamiltonian prediction practically feasible for molecular systems that were previously intractable for equivariant GNNs, and the technique generalizes to other SE(3) equivariant architectures as demonstrated on QHNet. An open question is whether the sparsity rates can be pushed further or whether the three-phase scheduler's fixed stage is truly necessary for downstream property accuracy beyond Hamiltonian MAE. The paper also leaves open whether these sparsification strategies transfer to periodic systems (DeepH-type tasks) or to equivariant networks for force/energy prediction where the tensor product structure differs.
