## Problem

While upper bounds on sample complexity for first-order stochastic non-convex optimization under Markovian sampling have been established (e.g., O(τ ε^{-4})), the best known lower bound was only Ω(τ ε^{-1}), leaving a substantial gap. This paper closes that gap by proving an Ω(τ ε^{-4}) lower bound for countable-state stationary Markov chains and an Ω(τ ε^{-2}) lower bound for finite-state chains, and demonstrates near min-max optimality by proposing an algorithm that matches the finite-state bound.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252295/cropped_page9_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The analysis considers smooth non-convex objectives F: R^d → R with bounded initial gap Δ and L-smoothness, accessed exclusively through a stochastic gradient oracle that returns samples from a stationary Markov chain with hitting time at most τ. The algorithm class is restricted to zero-respecting first-order methods with batch size M, meaning each iterate's support is contained in the span of previously observed gradient coordinates. The output is an ε-approximate critical point satisfying ‖∇F(x)‖ ≤ ε, and the paper's main deliverables are algorithm-independent sample complexity lower bounds and the MaC-SAGE algorithm with a matching upper bound.

## Method

The lower bound proof constructs 'hard' functions via a progress function prog_α(x) that tracks the largest nonzero coordinate, ensuring the gradient norm exceeds ε unless all d coordinates are nonzero. For countable-state chains, key states are split into substates sampled with probability q, making progress conditions hold only probabilistically and yielding Ω(τ ε^{-4}) after setting d = Ω(ε^{-2}) and q = O(ε^{-2}). For finite-state chains, deterministic transitions requiring Ω(τ) steps between critical states give Ω(τ ε^{-2}). The proposed MaC-SAGE algorithm adapts SAG by maintaining a running empirical estimate y_t of the stationary distribution Π to reweight per-state gradients, so that the composite gradient G_t = Σ_i y_t^i ∇f_i(x_{a_i(t)}) approximates ∇F(x_t) with variance O(σ² τ_mix / t), achieving a sample complexity of Õ(max{τ, τ_mix} ε^{-2}) that nearly matches the lower bound.

## Results

For countable-state stationary Markov chains, the paper establishes an Ω(τσ²/ε² + τσ²/ε⁴ · min{c₁σ², c₂LΔ}) sample complexity lower bound for any zero-respecting first-order method, which simplifies to Ω(τLΔ/ε² + τσ²LΔ/ε⁴) when σ² ≻ LΔ, matching the best-known upper bound O(τLΔ/ε² + τσ²LΔ/ε⁴) from Beznosikov et al. (2024). For finite-state Markov chains, a tighter Ω(τ/ε² · min{c₃σ², c₄LΔ}) lower bound is proven. The proposed MaC-SAGE algorithm achieves Õ(max{τ, τ_mix} · ε⁻²) sample complexity for finite-state chains, nearly matching the new lower bound and demonstrating near min-max optimality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's main contribution is improving the non-convex Markovian lower bound from Ω(τε⁻¹) (Even, 2023) to Ω(τε⁻⁴), which closes the gap to the best-known upper bound O(τε⁻⁴) established by Dorfman & Levy (2022) and Beznosikov et al. (2024). For the i.i.d. special case (τ=1), the new bound recovers the Ω(ε⁻⁴) result of Arjevani et al. (2023), confirming consistency. In the finite-state setting, the new Ω(τε⁻²) lower bound is not directly comparable to the countable-state Ω(τε⁻⁴) bound since it operates under a stricter function class (pointwise L-smoothness of each f(·;s)) and oracle class. The proposed MaC-SAGE algorithm, inspired by SAG (Schmidt et al., 2017), achieves Õ(max{τ, τ_mix} · ε⁻²) sample complexity, nearly matching the finite-state lower bound and establishing near min-max optimality. No empirical baselines are compared, as the paper is purely theoretical.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the lower-bound construction framework of Arjevani et al. (2023) from i.i.d. sampling to Markovian sampling, adapting the hard-function and progress-function technique to account for temporal correlations in gradient estimates. For the algorithmic side, MaC-SAGE is a direct generalization of the SAG variance-reduction algorithm (Schmidt et al., 2017), replacing the known uniform weights with an online estimator of the Markov chain's stationary distribution. The paper positions itself as filling a clear gap: prior work (Even 2023; Beznosikov et al. 2024; Dorfman & Levy 2022) established upper bounds for non-convex Markovian optimization but left lower bounds open, with only a loose Ω(τε⁻¹) result from Even (2023).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By matching the best-known upper bound of O(τε⁻⁴) for countable-state Markov chains and showing near min-max optimality for finite-state chains via MaC-SAGE, the paper provides a complete complexity picture that was previously missing. The connection to temporal-difference learning makes these bounds directly relevant to understanding fundamental limits in reinforcement learning with linear function approximation. An open question is whether the gap between the ε⁻⁴ (countable) and ε⁻² (finite) lower bounds reflects a genuine structural difference or an artifact of the function/oracle class restrictions, and whether tighter finite-state bounds or improved algorithms can close the remaining logarithmic factors.
