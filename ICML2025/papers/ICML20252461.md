## Problem

Existing temporal action localization methods identify action boundaries (event-level) but cannot pinpoint the precise frame-level moments of audible actions such as collisions within a video. This gap is critical for video dubbing applications, where professionals must manually mark exact collision frames for sound insertion. No dedicated dataset or method existed for this fine-grained audible action timing task.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20252461/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes silent video frames (resized to 112×112, 64 frames sampled per training clip) and pre-computed optical flow from GMFlow; no audio signal is used at any stage. It outputs per-frame binary probabilities indicating whether each frame contains an audible action, along with a spatial motion localization map as a side output. The Audible623 dataset provides frame-level keyframe annotations (binary labels per frame) across 623 videos averaging 250 frames and 9.2 seconds in duration.

## Method

TA²Net introduces inflectional flow—the second derivative of optical flow (i.e., acceleration)—as a kinematic prior to detect sudden velocity changes indicative of collisions, complementing standard motion flow. Three parallel ResNet50 encoders extract features from image frames, motion flow, and inflectional flow, which are then fused via a cross-kinematics aggregation module that uses attention to selectively pop-up motion and velocity-inflection information from image context features. A self-supervised spatial auxiliary training strategy applies inter-video contrastive learning (pushing motion-region and non-motion-region features apart) and intra-video temporal smoothing to produce a discriminative spatial motion map without requiring spatial labels. The fused spatio-temporal features pass through a 3D convolution and a transformer with self-attention before a fully connected layer yields frame-level predictions, trained with a combined cross-entropy, focal, contrastive, and smoothing loss.

## Results

On the Audible623 dataset, TA²Net achieves an F1 of 0.616, Precision of 0.656, Recall of 0.648, NME of 3.462, and PME of 0.744, outperforming all 11 baselines on F1, NME, and PME simultaneously. The closest competitor, TransRAC, reaches F1 0.553 but has an NME of 12.176 (3.5× worse) and PME of 0.988. On zero-shot repetitive counting, TA²Net sets new benchmarks with MAE 0.588 / OBO 0.185 on UCFRep and MAE 0.549 / OBO 0.346 on CountixAV, surpassing purpose-built counting methods without any counting-specific training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TA²Net wins on F1, NME, and PME against all 11 baselines simultaneously. Among temporal action localization methods (BMN, ActionFormer, TriDet), all three underperform substantially (F1 0.294–0.413) because they are designed for coarse-grained action boundary detection rather than frame-level collision timing. TransRAC is the strongest single competitor (F1 0.553, Precision 0.614), but its NME of 12.176 is 3.5× worse than TA²Net's 3.462, reflecting its inability to pinpoint exact collision frames. STG-NF achieves the highest recall (0.679) but at the cost of very low precision (0.229) and does not report NME/PME, making it unsuitable for frame-level evaluation. Video recognition methods (TimeSformer, X3D, Omnivore, TSM, Hiera) all score below F1 0.424, with Hiera showing the worst NME (20.748) due to its reliance on long temporal context. On the zero-shot counting task (Table 3), TA²Net outperforms both RepNet and TransRAC on CountixAV (MAE 0.549 vs. 0.749/0.686; OBO 0.346 vs. 0.231/0.255) and matches or beats them on UCFRep (MAE 0.588 vs. 0.915/0.594), despite never being trained on counting data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by introducing audible action temporal localization as a distinct task, explicitly differentiating it from temporal action localization (which targets event-level boundaries) and video action recognition (which targets semantic categories). The core methodological contribution is the 'inflectional flow' concept—using the second derivative of optical flow (i.e., acceleration) as a kinematic prior for collision detection—which repurposes classical kinematics in a deep learning pipeline. The architecture combines ResNet-based encoders with a cross-kinematics attention aggregation module and a self-supervised spatial contrastive learning strategy, drawing on ideas from attention mechanisms and contrastive learning but applying them to a novel kinematic signal. No single prior method is directly extended; instead, the paper synthesizes optical flow estimation (GMFlow), attention-based feature fusion, and contrastive learning into a unified framework for a previously unaddressed problem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical motivation—automating the dubbing of silent videos by identifying precise collision frames—is concrete and addresses a real production bottleneck in video editing. The zero-shot generalization to repetitive action counting (competitive MAE/OBO on UCFRep and CountixAV without any counting-specific training) suggests inflectional flow captures a broadly useful signal beyond audible collisions. However, the dataset is small (623 videos, ~9s average), the method's reliance on optical flow accuracy limits robustness to large viewpoint changes (acknowledged as a limitation), and the comparison baselines are all repurposed from different tasks, making the performance gap partly a function of task mismatch rather than pure methodological superiority. Open questions include whether the approach scales to longer videos, multi-object collisions, or scenarios with significant camera motion.
