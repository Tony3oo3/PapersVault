## Problem

Self-interpretable GNNs trained with different random seeds produce inconsistent and sometimes inaccurate explanations, undermining user trust in high-stakes applications. Prior work attributed this inconsistency to training instability or spurious correlations, but neither fully explains the phenomenon. This paper identifies redundancy—caused by overly relaxed conciseness constraints that allow models to retain irrelevant edges—as the root cause, and shows that existing remedies (SWA, contrastive learning, embedding alignment) fail to eliminate it.

## Input / Output

The method operates on graphs G = (V, E, X, A) with node feature matrices and binary adjacency matrices, using GIN or GCN backbones to produce per-edge importance scores in [0,1] via a 3-layer MLP explainer. The Explanation Ensemble (EE) aggregates these scores across n independently trained models (n=1 to 5 in experiments) by simple averaging, yielding a single ensemble explanation per graph. A threshold of 0.5 discretizes soft weights into present/absent edges for computing Structural Hamming Distance, while continuous scores are used for ROC-AUC evaluation against ground-truth substructures.

## Method

The paper's core contribution is a systematic diagnosis of redundancy in self-interpretable GNNs plus a simple mitigation strategy called Explanation Ensemble (EE). The diagnosis shows that global conciseness hyperparameters (e.g., sparsity coefficients β) are inherently suboptimal across varying graph sizes, causing models to consistently over-include irrelevant edges; Proposition 4.1 formalizes that when the budget K exceeds the true explanation size, multiple valid explanations exist, and Proposition 4.2 shows that in multi-explanation settings (e.g., multiple benzene rings), no single edge is strictly necessary, making contrastive-learning-based redundancy removal inapplicable. EE addresses this by averaging edge importance scores from n independently trained models: Proposition 5.1 (via Chebyshev's inequality) proves that ensemble inconsistency shrinks as 1/n relative to individual model inconsistency, and Proposition 5.2 (via Hoeffding's inequality) proves that the probability of correctly ranking irrelevant edges below relevant ones increases exponentially with n. The strategy is tuning-free, requires no architectural or loss-function changes, and consistently reduces SHD while improving AUC across all 48 experimental configurations (4 datasets × 4 GNN types × 3 metrics).

## Results

Across 4 self-interpretable GNN types, 4 datasets, and 3 metrics (48 total settings), Explanation Ensemble (EE) is the only strategy that consistently improves explanation quality: it reduces SHD and improves AUC in all 48 cases, and boosts classification ACC in 46/48 cases. By contrast, SWA reduces SHD in only ~44% of settings and EA actually degrades AUC by up to 3% on BA-2MOTIFS. The largest EE gains appear on BENZENE (e.g., Type I AUC improves from 83.50% to 90.90%, a +7.40 pp gain) and MUTAGENICITY (Type II SHD drops from 19.57 to 9.12).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SWA (Madhyastha & Jain, CoNLL 2019), EE wins decisively: SWA reduces SHD in only 7 of 16 tested settings and can actually increase SHD (e.g., Type I on BA-2MOTIFS goes from 4.75 to 12.07), while EE reduces SHD in all 48 settings. Against Embedding Alignment (Zhao et al., ACM TIST 2023), EE also dominates: EA degrades AUC by up to 3 pp on BA-2MOTIFS across all four GNN types and increases SHD on 3MR (e.g., Type I: 5.59→9.79), whereas EE improves both metrics uniformly. Against Contrastive Learning (Deng & Shen, AAAI 2024), the comparison is dataset-dependent: CL helps on BA-2MOTIFS (single-explanation) by reducing SHD from 4.75 to 4.27 and raising AUC from 99.30% to 99.67%, but on BENZENE (multi-explanation) CL degrades all metrics (SHD 6.11→10.89, AUC 83.50%→81.97%), confirming that its sufficiency/necessity assumption breaks down when multiple valid explanations coexist. Overall, EE is the only baseline that improves every metric in every setting without requiring additional loss terms or training-regime changes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on GNN explanation consistency (Zhao et al., 2023a;b) from post-hoc explainers to self-interpretable GNNs, where explanations are learned jointly with predictions. It departs from prior diagnoses by systematically ruling out training instability (via SWA) and spurious correlations (via embedding alignment) as primary causes of inconsistency in self-interpretable settings, then identifies redundancy—stemming from weak conciseness constraints—as the root cause. The proposed Explanation Ensemble (EE) repurposes the classical ensemble-averaging idea from statistics as a tuning-free inference-time aggregation of edge importance scores, distinct from training-time ensembling approaches like MGR in NLP.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that redundancy, not spurious correlations or training instability, drives explanation inconsistency in self-interpretable GNNs reframes how the community should think about explanation trustworthiness in high-stakes deployments. EE provides a practical, zero-tuning mitigation that consistently improves all 48 evaluated settings, but its linear scaling cost and incompatibility with faithfulness metrics limit its applicability. The central open question is whether redundancy can be eliminated proactively during training (e.g., via adaptive per-instance conciseness constraints) rather than addressed reactively through ensembling, which the authors explicitly call out as future work.
