## Problem

Current Neuro-Symbolic (NeSy) systems depend heavily on labeled data, and removing labels causes three compounding failures: loss of symbolic information through label leakage, an exponentially larger solution space, and a proliferation of shortcut solutions that satisfy the rules but are semantically wrong. This paper asks whether unsupervised NeSy can be made feasible by replacing label-based logical reasoning with a label-free verification process that only requires unlabeled data and a rule-conformance check.

## Input / Output

The system takes unlabeled images (handwritten digits for addition/sort, characters for match, chess pieces for chess) processed by a LeNet backbone, plus a lightweight Python verification function V_KB that returns True/False on whether a candidate symbolic assignment satisfies the domain rules. The output is the highest-scoring symbolic label sequence that passes verification, optionally refined by test-time correction (TTC) to guarantee rule conformance at inference. Notably, no labels of any kind are used during training, and the verification function replaces an entire logic program (Prolog, ASP, etc.) with a simple predicate.

## Method

Verification Learning (VL) reframes the NeSy training objective as a Constraint Optimization Problem (COP): find the symbolic assignment S that maximizes a score (e.g., neural confidence or consistency distance) subject to V_KB(S) = True. To solve this without exhaustive search, the paper introduces Dynamic Combinatorial Sorting (DCS), a heap-based algorithm that generates candidate solutions in strict descending score order by exploiting the fact that under independence (or more generally, monotonicity), the (i+1)-th best solution is always a one-symbol modification of one of the first i solutions. This reduces the COP to a complexity comparable to CSP, with the first verified-valid solution guaranteed to be globally optimal. A distribution alignment step (Eq. 3) rescales the model's output probabilities toward the natural symbol distribution to prevent collapse onto trivial shortcuts (e.g., all-zeros in addition). The theoretical analysis uses symmetry group decomposition of V_KB to characterize which tasks are solvable without labels: the worst-case error is bounded by the probability mass on non-fixed-point orbits, and the average error by orbit sizes, proving that rules can substitute for infinite labels when symbols are distinguishable under the rule structure.

## Results

On the Addition task (bases 2–9), VL with test-time correction and non-independent scoring (VL≠^TTC) achieves 99.95–100.00% symbol recognition accuracy, compared to 21–56% for the best supervised baselines. On Sort (sequence lengths 4–8), VL≠^TTC reaches 99.25–99.90%, versus 45–97% for DeepProblog. On Chess (2–6 piece types), VL⊥ achieves 95.15–100.00%, while A3BL drops from 100% to 34.35% as complexity increases. In terms of efficiency, VL completes Addition training in ~80–120 seconds, compared to 2,778–42,391 seconds for DeepProblog and 1,133–38,526 seconds for NeurASP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VL dominates all five baselines across all four tasks. On Addition, VL≠^TTC achieves 99.95–100% for bases 2–9, while DeepProblog (21–54%), DeepStochlog (21–56%), NeurASP (8–54%), Ground ABL (20–98%), and A3BL (20–100%) all degrade sharply as the base increases. On Sort, VL≠^TTC reaches 99.25–99.90% across all sequence lengths, outperforming DeepProblog (which drops to 45.97% at length 8) and A3BL (29.75–97.28%). On Match, VL≠^TTC achieves 68–100% versus 10–19% for all baselines, a gap of over 50 percentage points. On Chess, VL⊥ maintains 95–100% accuracy across 2–6 piece types, while A3BL collapses from 100% to 34.35% and Ground ABL from 49.90% to 33.95%. In terms of training time, VL (~80–120 s) is 20–350× faster than DeepProblog and NeurASP, and marginally faster than Ground ABL (86–160 s) and A3BL (161–203 s) on Addition.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the abductive learning (ABL) line of work (Dai et al., 2019; He et al., 2024; Jia et al., 2025) by removing the supervision signal Y entirely, replacing the standard NeSy reasoning step (KB, S ⊨ Y) with a label-free verification function V_KB(S). It builds directly on Jia et al. (2025) for the Dynamic Combinatorial Sorting algorithm and generalizes its independence assumption to the weaker monotonicity condition. Conceptually, it departs from the probabilistic-reasoning NeSy family (DeepProblog, DeepStochlog, NeurASP) by arguing that verification is strictly more general, halting, and computationally tractable than logical reasoning, and it introduces a symmetry-group-theoretic framework to characterize when rules can substitute for labels.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that fully unsupervised NeSy is feasible for a class of tasks (those where all symbols are fixed points of the verification function's symmetry group), providing a clean theoretical criterion for when rules can replace infinite labels. The DCS algorithm's reduction of COP to near-CSP complexity under monotonicity is a practical algorithmic contribution, and the distribution-alignment trick for shortcut mitigation is a simple but effective regularizer. Open questions include whether the approach scales to tasks violating monotonicity, to real-world (non-synthetic) data, and to tasks with partial symmetry (like chess, where one-way inclusions arise); the current evidence is confined to four synthetic tasks with a LeNet backbone and 10 epochs.
